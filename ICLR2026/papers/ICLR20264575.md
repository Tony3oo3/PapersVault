## Problem

Prototypical self-supervised learning methods suffer from partial prototype collapse, where most initialized prototypes converge to a small number of redundant representations, undermining their purpose as diverse learning targets. While this phenomenon was documented for the DINO family, its underlying mechanism remained unexplained and it was unclear whether it was universal across all prototypical SSL frameworks. This paper identifies joint optimization of encoders and prototypes under a shared loss as the root cause, showing it induces a form of shortcut learning that drives prototypes toward redundant representations early in training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes J stochastic augmentations of an RGB image, which are mapped through a student encoder and an EMA-updated teacher encoder to latent representations in R^D. The prototypes are structured as the means of a Gaussian Mixture Model with diagonal covariances, updated via an online EM procedure on the teacher's latent embeddings rather than through gradient descent on the SSL loss. The output is a set of K diverse prototype vectors (GMM means) that serve as assignment targets, alongside the learned encoder representations used for downstream k-NN, linear probing, and transfer evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully decoupled training procedure that breaks the joint optimization of encoders and prototypes by alternating two separate objectives: (i) updating prototypes by solving an independent unsupervised estimation problem on the current latent features, and (ii) updating the encoder for fixed prototypes by minimizing the consistency loss. Prototypes are modeled as the means of an online Gaussian Mixture Model updated via an EM-style procedure, incorporating responsibility-weighted forgetting to handle sparse updates at large prototype counts, deterministic annealing to modulate assignment sharpness during training, and a split-resurrect mechanism to prevent dominant components from monopolizing mass. A theoretical analysis shows that at decoupled fixed points, collapsed prototypes occur on a measure-zero set whenever components have distinct responsibilities, whereas joint gradient updates can admit collapse as a stationary point. This decoupling eliminates partial prototype collapse across all tested thresholds without explicit regularization, and yields improved k-NN accuracy and robustness to long-tailed distributions in several settings.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, CARP+Decoupling achieves 69.1% k-NN accuracy with ResNet-50 (400 epochs), a 1.4-point improvement over CARP (67.7%) and 1.6 points over DINO (67.5%). With ViT-S/16 at 300 epochs, the decoupled variant reaches 74.1% k-NN versus 73.6% for CARP and 72.8% for DINO; at 800 epochs it reaches 75.3%, surpassing DINO (74.5%) and DINO-vMF (74.7%). On the long-tailed iNaturalist-2018 benchmark, CARP+Decoupling improves overall accuracy by 3.0 points over CARP (48.9% vs. 45.9%), with gains across head (+3.1), medium (+2.4), and tail (+3.4) class splits. The decoupled method also reduces peak GPU memory by up to 1.5 GB at batch size 2048 while adding only 0.2 hours to training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINO (Caron et al., 2021), CARP+Decoupling consistently wins on k-NN across all backbone/epoch configurations tested (e.g., 69.1% vs. 67.5% on RN-50; 75.3% vs. 74.5% on ViT-S/16 at 800 epochs), while linear accuracy remains comparable. Compared to CARP (Silva & Ramírez Rivera, 2023), the decoupled variant improves k-NN by 1.4–1.5 points and iNaturalist overall accuracy by 3.0 points, confirming that eliminating prototype collapse yields stronger representations. Against CARP+KP (Govindarajan et al., 2024), the decoupled method achieves higher k-NN (74.1% vs. 73.7% on ViT-S/16) and avoids the slight accuracy drop that KP introduces under long-tailed conditions. Versus SWaV (Caron et al., 2020) and DeepCluster-v2 (Caron et al., 2018), CARP+Decoupling leads by 4.1 and 2.5 k-NN points respectively on RN-50. On iNaturalist-2018, CARP+Decoupling outperforms all baselines including DINO+KP (48.9% vs. 49.0% for DINO+KP, but DINO+Decoupling drops to 36.2% due to DINO's sensitivity to full prototype diversity). For transfer learning (Table 4), CARP+Decoupling achieves 79.18% average (RN-50) and 79.66% (ViT-S/16), marginally exceeding CARP (79.06% / 79.46%) and matching CARP+KP (79.67%), indicating that the k-NN gains do not always translate to linear-probe transfer metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototypical self-supervised learning lineage (DINO, SWaV, iBOT, CARP) by identifying joint optimization of encoders and prototypes as the root cause of partial prototype collapse, rather than treating collapse as a symptom to be regularized. It builds directly on CARP as the primary framework and repurposes online EM / Gaussian Mixture Model machinery (Neal & Hinton; Sato & Ishii) augmented with responsibility-weighted forgetting (Celaya & Agostini) and deterministic annealing (Ueda & Nakano) to decouple prototype estimation from encoder learning. Conceptually, it goes beyond CAPI's partial decoupling (which separates only the teacher's prototypes) by fully isolating both student and teacher prototype updates from the consistency loss, and it contrasts with Govindarajan et al.'s KoLeo-Prototype regularization, which adds an explicit diversity penalty rather than restructuring the optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic diagnosis linking joint optimization to prototype collapse across multiple prototypical SSL families, supported by a clean theoretical argument that decoupled fixed points do not admit collapse almost surely. However, the practical impact is architecture-dependent: while CARP+Decoupling yields consistent gains in k-NN accuracy and long-tailed robustness, DINO is extremely sensitive to full decoupling (requiring Sinkhorn normalization, temperature scheduling, and reduced prototype counts to recover performance), and transfer learning benefits are negligible. Open questions include which specific architectural components (random partitioning, unnormalized outputs, entropy regularization) confer CARP's robustness to full decoupling, whether the approach extends to dense/patch-level objectives, and how to adapt prototype update rates to the encoder's changing representation space without additional hyperparameters.
