## Problem

Existing systems for psychological and social behavior understanding are built as single-task specialists on heterogeneous datasets with inconsistent input formats, labeling schemes, and evaluation protocols, making it infeasible to train a unified foundation model. No prior benchmark standardizes the full spectrum of behavioral dimensions (affective, cognitive, pathology, social) into a consistent training interface, leaving cross-task transfer and generalization largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261509/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample is a unified prompt referencing available modalities (video, audio, transcript) with an optional behavioral descriptor stream (MediaPipe facial landmarks/body pose keypoints, OpenSMILE ComParE 2016 acoustic features). Outputs are standardized into two forms: discrete label sets for classification tasks (sentiment, emotion, humor, sarcasm, depression, anxiety, PTSD) or free-text responses for open-ended inference tasks (social reasoning, intent, non-verbal communication). The benchmark provides both representations so models can use either a classifier head or a pure decoder, and 83.6% of samples include video data with 29.2% of clips exceeding 20 seconds.

## Method

HUMAN BEHAVIOR ATLAS unifies 13 datasets (101,964 samples) into a common prompt-target schema with task-specific standardized metrics (binary weighted F1 for sentiment, mean per-class weighted accuracy for emotion, LLM-judge accuracy for free-text tasks). Three model variants are trained on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B: (1) SFT uses LoRA fine-tuning with per-task classifier heads on the penultimate hidden state plus a shared decoder head, jointly optimized with cross-entropy and teacher-forcing losses; (2) BAM freezes the SFT backbone and attaches a lightweight feed-forward adapter that pools temporal behavioral descriptors via mean and standard deviation, then adds a learnable-scaled residual update (α·z_f) to h_penult, enabling non-invasive integration of feature-based cues without destabilizing backbone representations; (3) RL replaces all classifier heads with a single generative decoder and optimizes via GRPO, computing group-normalized advantages from sampled responses with a KL penalty against the SFT reference policy.

## Results

On the unified HUMAN BEHAVIOR ATLAS benchmark (101,964 samples across 13 datasets and 10 behavioral tasks), OMNISAPIENS-7B SFT and BAM outperform all general multimodal LLM baselines on 8 of 10 tasks, while OMNISAPIENS-7B RL does so on 7 of 10. Headline numbers include SFT achieving 1.00 weighted F1 on PTSD (PTSD-in-the-Wild), 0.91 on ANX (MMPsy), and 0.79 on SEN (CH-SIMSv2); BAM reaching 0.80 on SAR (MUStARD) and 0.79 on SEN; and RL attaining 0.49 LLM-Judge accuracy on INT (IntentQA) and 0.30 on SOC (Social-IQ 2.0). In transfer experiments, pretraining on HUMAN BEHAVIOR ATLAS yields +18.3% on held-out MOSEI (SEN), +29.4% on DAIC-WOZ (DEP), and +39.1% on the novel-task MUStARD (SAR) over the same-architecture Qwen 2.5-Omni-7B baseline after only 1 epoch of fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen 2.5-Omni-7B (the shared backbone), OMNISAPIENS-7B SFT wins on 8 of 10 tasks (EMO 0.63 vs 0.58, PTSD 1.00 vs 0.76, ANX 0.91 vs 0.79, SEN 0.77 vs 0.67, NVC 0.12 vs 0.07), losing only on HUM (0.53 vs 0.54) and SAR (0.62 vs 0.66). OMNISAPIENS-7B BAM also wins 8/10, adding HUM (0.64 vs 0.54) and SAR (0.80 vs 0.66) but dropping INT (0.18 vs 0.25) and SOC (0.20 vs 0.25). OMNISAPIENS-7B RL wins 7/10, with its largest margins on INT (0.49 vs 0.25) and SOC (0.30 vs 0.25), but underperforms on SEN (0.40 vs 0.67) and EMO (0.57 vs 0.58). Against HumanOmniV2-7B (the closest human-data-focused baseline), all three OMNISAPIENS variants lead on 7–8 of 10 tasks, with the most dramatic gaps on ANX (0.91–0.92 vs 0.53) and SAR (0.62–0.80 vs 0.40). Against Gemma-3-4B and Qwen 2.5-VL-7B, the OMNISAPIENS models dominate on pathology tasks (PTSD, ANX, DEP) and NVC, while the baselines remain competitive only on HUM and SEN. The pattern is consistent: SFT/BAM excel on structured classification, RL excels on open-ended generation, and no single baseline matches the OMNISAPIENS family across the full task spectrum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long-standing affective computing tradition (Picard 2000; Breazeal 2000) and the multimodal multitask pretraining paradigm (PaLI, BLIP, Kosmos) into the domain of psychological and social behavior understanding. It departs from the prevailing single-task specialization approach by unifying 13 heterogeneous datasets across four behavioral dimensions (affective, cognitive, pathology, social) into a single standardized benchmark, and repurposes GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from mathematical reasoning to behavioral classification and generation. The Behavioral Adapter Module (BAM) introduces a novel residual-style mechanism that bridges the traditionally mutually exclusive feature-based and end-to-end modeling paradigms in behavioral signal processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As one of the first large-scale unified benchmarks (101k samples, 10 tasks, 4 dimensions) for psychological and social behavior foundation models, HUMAN BEHAVIOR ATLAS provides both a methodological template (taxonomy, schema unification, standardized evaluation) and an empirical testbed that the field has lacked. The finding that multi-task behavioral pretraining enables meaningful transfer even to unseen behavioral tasks (e.g., +39% on sarcasm detection) suggests that diverse behavioral supervision yields complementary, non-conflicting features. Open questions include whether pragmatic recognition capacity is emergent and scales with model size, which novel behavioral phenomena remain difficult to transfer to, and whether the LLM-judge evaluation protocol for free-text tasks is sufficiently reliable at scale.
