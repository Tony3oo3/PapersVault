## Problem

Existing visual reasoning models commit to a single chain-of-thought format—either text-based reasoning or visually-grounded reasoning with bounding boxes—each excelling in different domains but failing in others. No single mode dominates across all tasks, yet no prior work integrates multiple modes into one model with the ability to adaptively select the appropriate mode per question. This paper asks whether a unified model can learn both modes and develop context-sensitive mode selection to achieve general visual reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262768/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed via Qwen2.5-VL's dynamic-resolution patchification) and a text question as input. It produces a single autoregressive sequence that begins with a mode prefix token (<text> or <ground>), followed by a chain-of-thought reasoning process in the corresponding format (pure natural language for text mode; natural language interleaved with bounding-box coordinates in the form 'object[x1,y1,x2,y2]' for grounded mode), and concludes with a structured answer. During inference, a lightweight mode-switching fallback re-prompts in the other mode if the model fails to emit an answer within a token budget, but no external information is used.

## Method

The core contribution is the Mixture-of-Visual-Thoughts (MoVT) paradigm, which unifies text-based and visually-grounded reasoning in one LVLM by assigning each mode a unique prefix token, so that mode selection and reasoning collapse into a single autoregressive generation. Training follows the two-stage AdaVaR framework: (1) a supervised cold-start SFT phase mixes 1:1 expert trajectories from both modes to teach the model both reasoning styles simultaneously; (2) an RL phase using the proposed AdaGRPO algorithm induces adaptive mode selection. AdaGRPO modifies standard GRPO in three ways: prefix-guided exploration forces n rollouts per mode per sample to prevent mode collapse; a mode-relative advantage—computed as the probability (via Gaussian CDF) that one mode's reward distribution stochastically dominates the other's—is assigned to the prefix tokens while rollout-level advantages drive reasoning-token updates; and a curriculum schedule progresses from easy binary-mixture data to harder diverse data to refine fine-grained selection. This design lets the model discover, through online reward comparison, which mode is better for each specific question rather than relying on a fixed or pre-estimated assignment.

## Results

AdaVaR-7B achieves the best average accuracy of 55.82% across 8 diverse benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, MathVerse, WeMath, MMStar, V*, POPE, SpatialScore), surpassing GPT-4o (53.20%) and the strongest single-mode 7B baseline OVR-7B (55.76%). AdaVaR-3B reaches 50.84% average, matching the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-7B (50.90%) while outperforming all 3B-scale reasoning baselines by 2–5 points. Critically, AdaVaR is the only model that improves over its Qwen2.5-VL base on every single benchmark, whereas text-based models (e.g., OVR-7B) gain on math but drop on V* and POPE, and grounded models (e.g., DeepEyes, Chain-of-Focus) gain on V* but lose on math.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against text-based 7B models, AdaVaR-7B (55.82) edges out OVR-7B (55.76) by 0.06 and VLAA-Thinker-7B (54.20) by 1.62, while also maintaining V* (83.4 vs. 62.5/78.5) and POPE (89.0 vs. 83.2/86.2) scores that text-based models sacrifice. Against grounded 7B models, AdaVaR-7B leads DeepEyes (53.72) by 2.10, Chain-of-Focus (50.94) by 4.88, and ViGoRL-7B (50.53) by 5.29, while also matching or exceeding their V* scores (83.4 vs. 90.1/88.0/86.4) on math benchmarks where grounded models degrade. In the 3B group, AdaVaR-3B (50.84) outperforms LMM-R1 (48.80) by 2.04, VLAA-Thinker-3B (46.70) by 4.14, GRIT-3B (45.80) by 5.04, and ViGoRL-3B (45.70) by 5.14. The most striking comparison is AdaVaR-7B vs. GPT-4o: AdaVaR-7B wins on 6 of 8 benchmarks (MathVista 74.4 vs. 63.8, WeMath 44.8 vs. 42.9, MMStar 63.0 vs. 64.7 is the one loss, V* 83.4 vs. 66.0, POPE 89.0 vs. 86.9, SpatialScore 20.4 vs. 30.6 is the other loss) for a 2.62-point average advantage. The paper's central empirical claim—that no single-mode model improves on its base across all benchmarks while AdaVaR does—is supported: every text-based or grounded baseline shows at least one benchmark where it falls below Qwen2.5-VL, whereas AdaVaR improves on all eight.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeekMath and the RL-based reasoning paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1, extending both to the multi-modal setting. It departs from prior visual reasoning work—which has focused exclusively on either text-based or visually-grounded reasoning in isolation—by unifying both modes within a single model and introducing AdaGRPO, a modified RL algorithm with prefix-guided exploration and mode-relative advantage estimation. The two-stage SFT-then-RL training paradigm follows the cold-start pattern popularized by R1-OneVision and Orsta, but applies it to a novel multi-mode setting where the RL objective explicitly optimizes mode selection rather than just reasoning quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that no single reasoning mode dominates across visual reasoning tasks and that adaptive mode selection via RL yields consistent improvements over all single-mode baselines, with AdaVaR-7B surpassing GPT-4o in average accuracy. Open questions include whether the paradigm scales beyond two modes (the authors discuss width and depth extensions but do not test them), whether the learned mode selection generalizes to unseen task distributions, and whether iterative SFT+RL cycles could further close the gap to the theoretical upper bound. The framework is limited to English-language scenarios and Qwen2.5-VL base models, and the mode-switching mechanism at inference (triggered by repetitive logic) adds a practical complexity that single-mode models do not face.
