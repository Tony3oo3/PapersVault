## Problem

Existing RL methods for video reasoning rely on coarse sequence-level rewards or single-factor token selection (e.g., entropy alone), failing to model fine-grained dependencies between visual inputs, temporal dynamics, and linguistic outputs. This leads to optimization noise from irrelevant tokens, underutilization of visual evidence, and limited interpretability of which reasoning steps the model actually learns from.

## Input / Output

The model consumes up to 16 video frames (max resolution 128×28×28) during training and up to 64 frames (256×28×28) at inference, paired with a text prompt, and generates a free-form text reasoning response. During the RL training loop, each rollout additionally requires two counterfactual forward passes—one with visual features zeroed and one with frames randomly shuffled—to compute per-token attribution scores before the policy update is applied.

## Method

Video-KTR modifies the GRPO objective by multiplying each token's loss term with a binary mask that restricts gradient updates to a union of top-20% tokens identified by three complementary attribution signals: (1) visual-aware tokens, scored by the absolute log-probability shift when all visual features are masked to zero; (2) temporal-aware tokens, scored by the log-probability shift when input frames are randomly shuffled; and (3) high-entropy tokens, scored by predictive uncertainty over the vocabulary. The union of these three sets (yielding ~40% of tokens) receives full policy-gradient updates while the remainder is masked out, concentrating learning on semantically informative, modality-sensitive content. Built on a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone initialized from the Video-R1 SFT checkpoint, the framework requires only one extra forward pass per rollout for attribution scoring, adding minimal computational overhead (~5% FLOPs) over vanilla GRPO.

## Results

Video-KTR (7B, 64 frames) achieves 42.7% on Video-Holmes, surpassing GPT-4o (42.0%) and all open-source baselines, while remaining competitive on general video understanding (73.5% TempCompass, 62.5% VideoMME). On knowledge-intensive reasoning benchmarks, it reaches 53.1% on VideoMMMU and 66.6% on MMVU(mc). Performance scales consistently with input frames (16→32→64), with Video-Holmes accuracy rising from 40.7% to 42.7%. The method introduces only ~5% additional training overhead (5.2h vs 4.7h for vanilla GRPO) on 32 H100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-source 7B models, Video-KTR achieves the highest Video-Holmes score (42.7%), outperforming Video-RTS (40.7%) by 2.0 points, Video-R1/T-GRPO (36.5%) by 6.2 points, and TW-GRPO (32.9%) by 9.8 points. It also leads on VideoMMMU (53.1% vs. 52.7% for Video-RTS) and MMVU (66.6% vs. 66.4% for Video-RTS), while matching or exceeding TempCompass scores of all open-source baselines (73.5% vs. 73.3% for TW-GRPO). Against proprietary models, Video-KTR surpasses GPT-4o on Video-Holmes (42.7% vs. 42.0%) but trails GPT-5 (46.7%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (45.0%) by 4.0 and 2.3 points respectively; on VideoMMMU and MMVU the gap to proprietary models is larger (e.g., 53.1% vs. 84.6% for GPT-5). On VideoMME, Video-KTR (62.5%) is slightly below Video-RTS (63.0%) and well below GPT-4o (71.9%), indicating that the token-level RL strategy prioritizes reasoning accuracy over broad video comprehension. The controlled comparison in Figure 3a (same data and recipe) confirms that Video-KTR's gains over vanilla GRPO, T-GRPO, and TW-GRPO are attributable to the token attribution mechanism rather than data or training differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

Video-KTR extends the GRPO policy-optimization framework (Shao et al., 2024) by introducing a binary token-level mask that selectively reinforces only modality-critical tokens, departing from the uniform token treatment in vanilla GRPO and the sequence-level temporal penalties in T-GRPO (Feng et al., 2025). It repurposes the entropy-based token selection from Wang et al. (2025b) as one of three complementary attribution signals, adding two novel modality-specific signals—counterfactual visual masking and frame-shuffling perturbation—that had not previously been integrated into RL credit assignment for video. The paper positions itself as the first to unify multiple modality-aware token attribution strategies within a single RL objective for Video-MLLMs, contrasting with prior work that either applies coarse global temporal constraints or relies on a single entropy heuristic without explicit visual-temporal modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from sequence-level to token-level credit assignment in video RL is practically important because it offers a drop-in modification to existing GRPO pipelines with minimal computational overhead (~5% extra FLOPs), making it accessible to practitioners already training video MLLMs. However, the framework remains limited to vision-language modeling without audio or motion signals, and its validation is concentrated on reasoning benchmarks rather than broader video tasks like captioning or temporal grounding. Open questions include whether the attribution signals remain stable under challenging conditions (occlusion, rapid motion, OCR noise) and whether the 20% update ratio generalizes across model scales and video lengths beyond the 7B/64-frame regime tested here.
