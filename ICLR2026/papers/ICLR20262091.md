## Problem

Despite tokenizers being fundamental to modern visual generative models, it remains unclear what properties make them effective for downstream generation. Current tokenizers are trained as standard VAEs optimizing pixel-level reconstruction, yet this objective is misaligned with the actual task generative models perform—reconstructing clean signals from corrupted inputs (denoising). The paper asks whether explicitly aligning tokenizer training with this shared denoising objective can produce better latent representations for generation.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer encodes 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images into a 16×16 grid of 16-dimensional continuous latent embeddings (256 tokens total for 256×256), using a ViT encoder with patch size 16. During training, these latents are corrupted via interpolative Gaussian noise and/or random patch masking before the decoder reconstructs the original image. Downstream, the frozen tokenizer feeds these latents to generative models (DiT, SiT, MAR, etc.) that condition on class labels or CLIP text embeddings to produce full-resolution images; at inference, all corruption is disabled (τ=0, m=0).

## Method

l-DeTok trains a ViT-based encoder-decoder tokenizer to reconstruct clean images from deliberately corrupted latent embeddings, directly mirroring the denoising objective of downstream generative models. Two complementary corruption strategies are applied: (1) interpolative latent noise, where each latent is replaced by x′=(1−τ)x+τ·γ·N(0,I) with τ~U(0,1) and γ=3.0, which unlike additive noise ensures the original signal is fully suppressed at high τ; and (2) random patch masking with ratio m sampled from a slightly biased uniform distribution U(−0.1, 0.7), inspired by MAE. The decoder reconstructs the original image from these deconstructed latents using a combined loss of pixel MSE, KL regularization, perceptual losses (VGG/ConvNeXt), and adversarial GAN loss. The key design insight is that stronger corruption during tokenizer training yields more robust, downstream-aligned latents, and that this principle is architecture-agnostic (works with CNNs, 1D tokenizers, and VQ tokenizers) and paradigm-agnostic (benefits both autoregressive and non-autoregressive generative models without any semantics distillation from pretrained encoders).

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditioned generation, simply swapping the tokenizer to l-DeTok improves MAR-B FID from 2.31 to 1.55 (matching the 2.3× larger MAR-H) and MAR-L FID from 1.78 to 1.35 (surpassing MAR-H). On ImageNet 512×512, MAR-L + l-DeTok achieves FID 1.61 and IS 315.7, outperforming SiT-XL/2+REPA (FID 2.08). Across six generative models (Table 4), l-DeTok without any semantics distillation achieves the best FID on all three autoregressive models (MAR 2.43, RandomAR 5.22, RasterAR 4.46) and remains competitive on non-autoregressive models (SiT 5.13, DiT 6.58, LightningDiT 3.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest non-distilled tokenizer (MAR-VAE), l-DeTok reduces FID by ~34% on MAR (3.71→2.43), ~56% on RandomAR (11.78→5.22), and ~44% on RasterAR (7.99→4.46), while also improving non-AR models (SiT 6.26→5.13, LightningDiT 3.98→3.63). Against semantics-distilled tokenizers (VA-VAE, MAETok), l-DeTok dramatically outperforms on all AR models—where those methods degrade severely (e.g., VA-VAE MAR FID 16.66 vs. l-DeTok 2.43)—revealing that distillation-based tokenizers do not generalize across generative paradigms. On non-AR models, l-DeTok without distillation is competitive with or slightly behind VA-VAE (SiT 5.13 vs. 4.33), but adding a lightweight DINOv2 distillation loss to l-DeTok yields the best non-AR FID (SiT 3.40, LightningDiT 2.18), surpassing all prior distilled tokenizers. At the system level, MAR-B + l-DeTok (208M params) matches MAR-H (943M params) at FID 1.55, and MAR-L + l-DeTok (479M) beats MAR-H at FID 1.35 vs. 1.55, all without relying on external pretrained encoders. On MS-COCO text-to-image, l-DeTok achieves FID 4.97 (MAR-B) and 4.31 (SiT-B) with CLIP scores of 24.82 and 24.61, substantially outperforming MAR-VAE (FID 12.49) and SD-VAE (FID 19.75) while eliminating the 'spot artifacts' observed with other tokenizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the training of visual tokenizers by unifying the objectives of diffusion models (removing Gaussian noise) and autoregressive models (filling masked positions) under a single 'denoising' principle, then directly incorporating that principle into tokenizer training. It builds on standard VAE-based tokenizers (Kingma & Welling, 2014) and borrows the masking strategy from MAE (He et al., 2022), but departs from both the pixel-reconstruction-only objective of conventional tokenizers and the semantics-distillation approaches (VA-VAE, MAETok) that require external pretrained encoders like DINOv2 or CLIP. The key conceptual departure is treating the tokenizer's latent space as something that should be robust to the same corruption patterns the downstream generative model must reverse, rather than merely a compact, high-fidelity encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a simple, self-contained recipe—add interpolative latent noise and random masking during tokenizer training—that consistently improves generation quality across six generative models, multiple resolutions, and both AR and non-AR paradigms, without requiring any external pretrained semantic encoders. This is particularly important for domains (video, audio, 3D) where strong pretrained teachers like DINOv2 may not exist. A notable open question is whether the gains persist at web-scale (10⁷–10¹⁰ samples) and in video generation, and the authors acknowledge a training/inference discrepancy (decoder trained on noisy latents but used on clean ones) that decoder fine-tuning only partially resolves. The finding that semantics-distilled tokenizers fail to generalize from non-AR to AR models challenges a previously unexamined assumption in the field.
