## Problem

Existing lossless watermarking schemes for diffusion models (Gaussian Shading, PRC Watermark) require per-image cryptographic keys or heavyweight pseudorandom error-correcting codes with belief-propagation decoding, incurring substantial storage, management, and computational overhead. This paper asks whether lossless, undetectable watermarking can be achieved without any encryption, while maintaining robustness under adversarial attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263921/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512-bit binary watermark (encoding user metadata such as user ID and timestamp) and a text prompt as input. The watermark is repeated N=31 times and concatenated with 512 random padding bits to form a 16384-bit vector matching the 4×64×64 latent dimensionality of Stable Diffusion. The output is a 512×512 RGB watermarked image; extraction inverts the image through the VAE encoder and ODE solver to recover the original 512-bit watermark via majority voting across the N repeated copies. No per-image keys or nonces are required—the fixed signature (embedding matrix T, rotation matrix C) is shared across all generations.

## Method

Spherical Watermark embeds watermarks through three reversible modules operating entirely in the latent Gaussian prior, requiring no modification to the pretrained diffusion model. The binary embedding module mixes N repeated copies of the watermark with random padding via a sparse invertible matrix T (constructed so each bit is XORed with a disjoint subset of paddings), producing a 3-wise independent bitstream. The spherical mapping module then converts this bitstream to a {±1} vector, normalizes it onto the unit sphere, applies a fixed orthogonal rotation C (from QR decomposition of a random Gaussian matrix), and scales by a radius r where r² ~ χ²(l_x), yielding a vector whose distribution is provably a spherical 3-design and empirically indistinguishable from N(0, I). The diffusion integration module feeds this watermarked noise as z_T into Stable Diffusion's ODE solver (DPM-Solver++ or DDIM) to generate the image. The key theoretical result is that the spherical 3-design property, combined with the chi-square radial scaling, guarantees the watermarked noise matches the standard Gaussian prior up to third-order moments, enabling lossless embedding without any cryptographic primitives.

## Results

On the COCO dataset with SD v2.1, Spherical Watermark achieves 99.99% clean ACC and 100.00% clean TPR, matching the best baselines. Under adversarial attacks (WEvade), it maintains 98.12% ACC and 99.83% TPR, outperforming PRC Watermark (97.69% ACC, 95.38% TPR) and Gaussian Shading (88.06% ACC, 99.23% TPR), while all lossy methods collapse to ~49–52% ACC. In terms of undetectability, the FID of watermarked images (48.12 on COCO/SD v1.5) is statistically identical to the unwatermarked original (48.13), and latent-level classifiers achieve only ~50% accuracy (chance level). Extraction is approximately four orders of magnitude faster than PRC Watermark due to the elimination of belief-propagation decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two lossless baselines, Spherical Watermark outperforms PRC Watermark on both post-processing ACC (95.02 vs. 93.52) and adversarial TPR (99.83 vs. 95.38), with a particularly large gap under adversarial attacks. Compared to Gaussian Shading, the method trades a small amount of post-processing ACC (95.02 vs. 98.43) for a substantial gain in adversarial ACC (98.12 vs. 88.06) and TPR (99.83 vs. 99.23), reflecting the robustness advantage of the orthogonal rotation over element-wise truncated sampling. All three lossy baselines (DwtDct, DwtDctSvd, RivaGan) and Tree-Ring suffer catastrophic degradation under adversarial attacks, dropping to 49–52% ACC (near random), while Spherical Watermark retains 98.12%. On undetectability (FID), only Spherical Watermark and PRC Watermark match the original distribution; Gaussian Shading with fixed keys inflates FID by ~2.6 points on COCO/SD v1.5, and Tree-Ring by ~1.2 points. In computational efficiency, Spherical Watermark's extraction is roughly 10,000× faster than PRC Watermark, eliminating the belief-propagation decoding bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the cryptographic lineage of lossless diffusion watermarking (Gaussian Shading's stream cipher, PRC Watermark's pseudorandom error-correcting codes) by replacing all encryption machinery with a purely geometric construction: projecting binary bits onto the unit sphere, applying a fixed orthogonal rotation, and scaling by a chi-square-distributed radius. The theoretical backbone draws on spherical t-design theory from combinatorial design, repurposing it to prove that the resulting noise is statistically indistinguishable from a standard Gaussian prior up to third-order moments. The binary embedding module (XOR-mixing watermark bits with random padding via a structured sparse matrix) is a lightweight substitute for the repetition-and-cipher pipelines in prior lossless schemes, and the overall framework requires no per-image key storage, a practical departure from both Gaussian Shading and PRC Watermark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of all cryptographic overhead (no per-image keys, no belief-propagation decoding) makes lossless watermarking substantially more deployable in production API settings, with extraction roughly four orders of magnitude faster than PRC Watermark. The theoretical argument that losslessness inherently resists detector-driven adversarial attacks (Appendix E) elevates the design principle from a quality metric to a security guarantee, which could reshape how future watermarking schemes are evaluated. Limitations include the guarantee being only up to third-order moments (higher-order deviations from the true Gaussian prior are unaddressed), vulnerability to strong inversion-breaking attacks targeting the VAE or ODE solver, and a scope limited to origin tracing rather than post-hoc editing or forgery detection. Open questions include whether the spherical t-design framework extends to higher-order designs for tighter distributional matching, and whether the approach generalizes cleanly to non-Gaussian-prior generative models.
