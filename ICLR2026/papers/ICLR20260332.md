## Problem

The dominant mechanistic explanation for in-context learning attributes ICL to Induction Heads that copy label tokens from demonstrations, but this framework cannot explain why ICL accuracy remains well above zero-shot in unseen-label scenarios where the correct label is absent from demonstrations. The paper asks what additional mechanism supports ICL beyond label copying, and proposes that ICL fundamentally operates by removing task-irrelevant information from the query's non-selective hidden state representations rather than by injecting new information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260332/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes zero-shot queries (a single input with no demonstrations) or few-shot ICL prompts (k input-label pairs concatenated with a query) fed into frozen LMs (Llama 3.2-1B, Llama 3-8B, Llama 3-13B Instruct, Qwen 2.5-3B/7B). Outputs include predicted class labels via open-end greedy decoding (hard matching across the full vocabulary, not restricted to label candidates), identified Denoising Heads (specific attention head indices per layer), and two geometric metrics—eccentricity and covariance flux—measured on last-token hidden states at each layer. The low-rank filter injection experiment additionally produces a trained W_enc W_dec pair (rank as low as 2) that defines the Task-Verbalization Subspace.

## Method

The paper proposes a three-step framework for tracing task-oriented information removal. Step 1 injects a trainable low-rank filter (W_enc W_dec, rank r) into the residual stream of the last token at layer l in zero-shot inputs, freezing all other LM parameters and training only the filter toward ground-truth outputs; preserving just 0.7% of dimensions boosts accuracy from near-zero to a remarkable level, confirming that task-relevant information resides in a low-rank subspace (TVS) while the rest is noise. Step 2 measures two geometric metrics on few-shot hidden states—eccentricity (covariance loaded ratio on the first principal direction, quantifying information compression) and covariance flux (ratio of nuclear norms of the TVS-projected vs. original covariance matrices, quantifying correctness of removal)—showing that demonstrations autonomously drive hidden states toward the independently computed TVS in middle-to-late layers. Step 3 identifies Denoising Heads by ablating each attention head and re-measuring the metrics, then verifies causality by zeroing all DHs: accuracy drops sharply in seen-label settings and collapses to near zero in unseen-label settings, confirming DHs as the primary mechanism complementing Induction Heads. The DHs are shown to be independent from Induction Heads, exhibit local re-encoding attention patterns confined to query tokens, and function as a hybrid of in-context and in-weight learning.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative finding is that ablating Denoising Heads (DHs) causes ICL accuracy to collapse in unseen-label scenarios: on Llama 3.2-1B, 8-shot unseen-label accuracy drops from 14.54% to 1.06% (vs. 10.65% for random-head ablation), and on Llama 3-13B Instruct from 29.59% to 10.35%. In seen-label and random-sample settings, DH ablation still degrades accuracy substantially (e.g., 73.40%→56.52% on Llama 3.2-1B) but less catastrophically, since induction heads provide a complementary copying mechanism. The filter-injection experiment shows that a low-rank TVS preserving only ~0.7% of hidden-state dimensions boosts zero-shot open-end accuracy from near-zero to competitive levels (e.g., ~88% on SST-2 with rank-2 filter). Eccentricity of hidden states reaches ~60% in middle-to-late layers with 8-shot demonstrations, confirming strong anisotropic information compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability paper rather than a method-vs-benchmark comparison, so the key 'comparison' is DH ablation versus random-head (RH) ablation as a control. Across all five models tested, DH ablation consistently produces significantly larger accuracy drops than RH ablation (all P-values < 0.05 except Qwen 2.5-3B Instruct in seen/random settings), confirming the causal role of denoising heads. The effect is most dramatic in the unseen-label scenario, where DH ablation reduces accuracy to near-chance (1.06% on Llama 3.2-1B) while RH ablation retains 10.65%, demonstrating that DHs are the primary accuracy source when induction heads cannot copy a label from context. The paper also contrasts its information-removal mechanism against the induction-head framework, showing that DHs are largely non-overlapping with induction heads (Fig. 9) and operate via a local re-encoding attention pattern rather than prefix-suffix copying. Additionally, the filter-injection baseline (zero-shot without filter) achieves near-zero open-end accuracy, while the rank-2 TVS filter recovers ~88% on SST-2, establishing the information-removal hypothesis before generalizing to few-shot ICL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability line of work on Induction Heads (Elhage et al., 2021; Cho et al., 2025a) by identifying a complementary mechanism—Denoising Heads—that performs task-oriented information removal rather than label copying, thereby explaining the unseen-label scenario where induction heads fail. It builds on the hidden-state geometry and task-vector framework (Yang et al., 2025; Hendel et al., 2023) by formalizing the Task-Verbalization Subspace (TVS) as a low-rank information bottleneck and introducing geometric metrics (eccentricity, covariance flux) to trace its dynamics. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant 'copying' or 'implicit gradient descent' narratives by reframing ICL as selective removal of task-irrelevant information from superposed non-selective representations, a perspective that unifies the role of demonstrations as filters rather than information sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Denoising Heads and the information-removal mechanism provides a causal explanation for ICL accuracy in unseen-label scenarios—a gap that induction-head-based accounts leave unaddressed—and yields a practical, plug-and-play accuracy boost via DH amplification. The TVS framework and its geometric metrics offer a generalizable evaluation methodology applicable beyond ICL to any task where hidden states encode superposed information. Key open questions include whether the linear low-rank bottleneck assumption holds for nonlinearly separable features (e.g., generative tasks on manifolds), how to efficiently localize DHs without costly ablation-remeasurement, and whether the ICL/IWL hybrid nature of DHs (some task-specific, some shared) can be further disentangled to clarify the boundary between in-context and in-weight learning.
