## Problem

KernelSHAP, the dominant model-agnostic Shapley value estimator, approximates the cooperative game as a linear function of features, inherently missing non-linear feature interactions. Additionally, paired (antithetic) sampling is a widely adopted heuristic that substantially improves KernelSHAP accuracy, yet the theoretical reason for its effectiveness remained unexplained beyond restricted special cases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264616/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PolySHAP takes a black-box model f, a target instance x, a sampling budget m, and an interaction frontier I specifying which feature subsets to include as polynomial terms. The game ν is defined via a user-chosen perturbation strategy (baseline, marginal, or conditional imputation), making the method agnostic to the explanation game. It outputs per-feature Shapley value estimates plus the baseline prediction ν(∅); the Shapley values are recovered from the fitted polynomial coefficients via a closed-form mapping that distributes each interaction coefficient equally among its constituent features.

## Method

PolySHAP replaces KernelSHAP's linear regression with a polynomial regression over a chosen interaction frontier I (e.g., all pairs for 2-PolySHAP, all triples for 3-PolySHAP), solving a weighted least-squares problem over m sampled coalitions with an efficiency constraint (coefficients sum to ν(D)). The Shapley values of the fitted polynomial are extracted in O(d·d') time via a simple formula: each feature's value equals its own coefficient plus the sum of interaction coefficients containing that feature, divided by the interaction size. A key theoretical contribution proves that KernelSHAP with paired sampling (sampling S and its complement D\S) produces exactly the same Shapley value estimates as 2-PolySHAP without ever fitting a degree-2 polynomial, providing the first general theoretical justification for paired sampling's empirical success. The method also supports leverage score sampling for provable approximation guarantees and partial interaction frontiers to manage combinatorial growth in high dimensions.

## Results

Using paired sampling (the standard practical setting), 3-PolySHAP achieves MSE of 4.0×10⁻⁹ on Housing (d=8), 4.3×10⁻⁷ on Forest (d=13), and 1.9×10⁻⁹ on Adult (d=14), compared to 5.5×10⁻⁶, 4.9×10⁻³, and 1.2×10⁻⁷ for KernelSHAP (1-PolySHAP) respectively — improvements of 2–4 orders of magnitude. The key theoretical result (Theorem 5.1) proves that paired KernelSHAP produces exactly the same Shapley value estimates as 2-PolySHAP without ever fitting a degree-2 polynomial, providing the first strong justification for the paired sampling heuristic. In high-dimensional settings (d≥60), gains from 3-PolySHAP are more modest (e.g., 3.2×10⁻⁷ vs 5.4×10⁻⁷ on IL60) because the combinatorial explosion limits the number of order-3 interactions that can be included within a fixed budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Permutation Sampling, all PolySHAP variants (1–4) substantially outperform across all 15 games, with 3-PolySHAP achieving 2–4 orders of magnitude lower MSE on low-to-mid-dimensional tabular games (e.g., 4.0×10⁻⁹ vs 1.7×10⁻⁴ on Housing). Against KernelSHAP (1-PolySHAP), the improvement from 2-PolySHAP is zero under paired sampling due to the proven equivalence (Theorem 5.1), but 3-PolySHAP delivers dramatic gains: 1375× lower MSE on Housing, 11,395× on Forest, and 63× on Adult. Against RegressionMSR with XGBoost, PolySHAP achieves comparable or better performance on most games, with the notable exception that RegressionMSR degrades significantly on CG60 (correlated groups) where the tree-based backbone cannot approximate the game well. Against SVARM, MSR, and Unbiased KernelSHAP, PolySHAP consistently outperforms, as these direct Monte Carlo methods do not reuse game evaluations across all Shapley value estimates. In high-dimensional settings (d≥60), the advantage of 3-PolySHAP over baselines narrows because the budget can only accommodate a small fraction of order-3 interactions, though partial frontiers (3-PolySHAP 50%, 3-PolySHAP log) still provide measurable gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolySHAP extends KernelSHAP (Lundberg & Lee, 2017) by replacing its linear game approximation with a polynomial regression that explicitly models higher-order feature interactions, generalizing the Faith-SHAP interaction index (Tsai et al., 2023) to arbitrary interaction frontiers. It leverages leverage score sampling (Musco & Witter, 2025) for provably accurate estimation and provides a simpler, more transparent alternative to k-ADD-SHAP (Pelegrina et al., 2023) while proving convergence for all k. The key theoretical departure is Theorem 5.1, which shows paired KernelSHAP is exactly equivalent to 2-PolySHAP, generalizing and explaining a recent special-case result by Mayer & Wüthrich (2025) and providing the first general theoretical justification for the paired sampling heuristic used across state-of-the-art Shapley estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paired-sampling equivalence (Theorem 5.1) resolves a long-standing practical mystery: why sampling subsets in complementary pairs dramatically improves KernelSHAP accuracy for all games, not just those with bounded interaction degree. This insight could guide the design of future sampling strategies and explain the empirical success of antithetic sampling in other Monte Carlo contexts. Open questions include proving the conjectured equivalence between paired k-PolySHAP and (k+1)-PolySHAP for odd k > 1, developing structured interaction frontiers that detect important interactions rather than enumerating them combinatorially, and understanding scalability in very high-dimensional settings where only a small fraction of order-3 terms can be included.
