## Problem

Existing video relighting methods face a fundamental trade-off between lighting fidelity and temporal consistency, while camera-controlled video generation methods lack any illumination editing capability. No prior framework jointly controls both camera trajectory and illumination from a single monocular video, and the paired multi-view, multi-illumination training data required for such joint control is unavailable in real-world settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264632/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular source video (384×672, 49 frames), a user-specified camera trajectory (per-frame rotation and translation), and an illumination condition in one of three forms: a text prompt, an HDR environment map, or a reference image from a different scene. It outputs a target video re-rendered under the specified trajectory and lighting, preserving the original scene's geometry, motion, and identity. The framework also supports independent usage modes—camera-only redirection (by replacing the relit frame with the original) or relighting-only (by setting projected views equal to the source video).

## Method

Light-X decouples geometry and illumination through a dual point-cloud conditioning scheme: dynamic point clouds from depth-estimated source frames are projected along the target camera trajectory to provide geometry/motion cues, while a single IC-Light-relit frame is back-projected into the same geometry and re-projected to provide fine-grained illumination cues. These six projected signals (views and masks for both branches) are encoded by a VAE, concatenated with noise, and fed into DiT blocks; a Q-Former extracts global illumination tokens from the relit frame, which are injected via a dedicated Light-DiT cross-attention layer to prevent illumination fading over time. To overcome the absence of paired training data, Light-Syn synthesizes training pairs by degrading in-the-wild videos (via relighting, novel-view synthesis, or both) and applying inverse geometric mappings to recover aligned conditioning cues, yielding 18k samples from static, dynamic, and AI-generated sources. Soft-weighted illumination masks (α=0.25 for reference images, α=0.50 for HDR maps) act as domain indicators enabling a single model to generalize across diverse lighting conditions.

## Results

On the joint camera-illumination control task (Table 1), Light-X achieves FID 101.06, Aesthetic 0.623, Motion Preservation 2.007, and CLIP 0.989, outperforming all baselines (best competitor TL-Free: FID 122.73, Motion Pres. 3.356) while running 1.8–3× faster (1.83 min vs. 3.25–5.50 min). Against real in-the-wild video references (Table 2), Light-X attains PSNR 13.96, SSIM 0.582, LPIPS 0.378, and FVD 45.91, the best across all metrics. For text-conditioned video relighting (Table 3), Light-X reaches FID 83.65, Aesthetic 0.645, and Motion Preservation 1.137, surpassing IC-Light (Motion Pres. 3.293) and LAV (FID 112.45) in both fidelity and temporal smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Light-X outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated metric. Against the camera-control baselines (TrajectoryCrafter, ReCamMaster, Free4D), Light-X achieves substantially lower Motion Preservation error (2.007 vs. 3.157–7.259 in combined baselines) and lower FID (101.06 vs. 152.03–163.56), showing that its disentangled point-cloud conditioning yields more geometrically coherent novel views. Against the relighting baselines (IC-Light, Light-A-Video, RelightVid, TC-Light), Light-X improves FID by 25–60 points (83.65 vs. 106.05–144.32 for text-conditioned relighting) and reduces Motion Preservation error by 30–70% (1.137 vs. 1.657–3.777), confirming superior temporal consistency. IC-Light+AnyV2V achieves the best user-study scores (97.6% RQ, 98.4% IP) but at the cost of higher FID (106.05) and Motion Preservation (3.777) than Light-X (77.97 FID, 1.452 Motion Pres. on 16 frames). On the iPhone multi-view dataset, Light-X achieves the lowest FID (155.36) and best Motion Preservation (3.316) among all methods, and matches or exceeds TrajectoryCrafter on novel-view synthesis (PSNR 15.60 vs. 14.62, LPIPS 0.352 vs. 0.380). For HDR-map-conditioned relighting, Light-X surpasses DiffusionRenderer by 5.1 dB in PSNR (16.98 vs. 11.88) and 0.24 in LPIPS (0.250 vs. 0.493). The only setting where a baseline shows a marginal advantage is the 16-frame user study for IC-Light+AnyV2V on identity preservation, where its higher IP score (98.4%) slightly exceeds Light-X's, though Light-X still leads on FID and motion smoothness.

## Relation to Prior Work

Light-X sits at the intersection of two previously independent research lines: video relighting (IC-Light, Light-A-Video, RelightVid) and camera-controlled video generation (TrajectoryCrafter, ReCamMaster, Free4D). It builds directly on TrajectoryCrafter's dynamic point-cloud formulation for viewpoint control and IC-Light's single-image relighting as an illumination prior, but departs from both by explicitly disentangling geometry/motion from lighting in a shared geometric space. The key methodological departure is the Light-Syn degradation-based data pipeline, which synthesizes paired multi-view, multi-illumination training data from in-the-wild monocular footage via inverse geometric mapping—a strategy absent from prior work that either requires real multi-view captures or ignores the data bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly control camera trajectory and illumination from a single monocular video, unifying two capabilities that were previously treated as separate problems. The Light-Syn pipeline offers a practical, generalizable solution to the data scarcity problem that plagues any joint geometry-lighting learning task. Key limitations remain: reliance on single-image relighting priors (IC-Light) caps illumination quality, point-cloud-based geometry degrades under large camera motions (>60°) and inaccurate depth, and the multi-step diffusion process is computationally expensive. Open questions include whether stronger video backbones (Wan2.2), progressive point-cloud expansion, and autoregressive techniques (Diffusion Forcing, Self Forcing) can extend the approach to longer sequences and wider viewpoint ranges.
