## Problem

Existing non-uniform quantization methods for LLMs (AQLM, VPTQ, GPTVQ) rely on fixed codebook sizes, Hessian-based optimization, or layer-wise fine-tuning, imposing significant computational overhead. More critically, the outlier patterns in non-uniform quantization differ fundamentally from uniform quantization—errors are smaller and more dispersed across channels rather than concentrated in specific outlier channels—making standard amplitude-based compensation strategies ineffective. The paper addresses both the need for a flexible, layer-wise non-uniform quantization framework and the unique block-level outlier structure that emerges under non-uniform quantization.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained LLM weight matrices (e.g., LLaMA3, Qwen3, Gemma2 at 8B–70B scale) and a small set of calibration activation samples (e.g., 50 WikiText-2 sequences) as input. It produces, for each linear sublayer, a set of r sequential codebooks (each with c cluster centers of dimension d, partitioned into g groups with learnable scaling), corresponding quantization indices, plus an outlier-specific codebook and indices identified via a sliding-window threshold. Additionally, OCP outputs compensation parameters at three granularities: per-sublayer weight deltas, per-Transformer-block attention adjustments, or a global KL-divergence parameter set. The framework supports arbitrary per-layer bit-widths, so the overall model compression ratio is a weighted combination of layer-specific settings determined by a constrained grid search.

## Method

NuBitQ decomposes each weight matrix into subvectors of dimension d, partitions them into g groups with learnable scaling, and encodes each subvector via r sequential codebooks (first encodes the original, subsequent ones encode residuals) built with beam-search k-means; the compression ratio is controlled by the (r, c, d) triple, enabling flexible bit-widths from 4-bit down to 2-bit without Hessian computation. The key insight driving OCP is that non-uniform quantization errors manifest as block-level outliers rather than channel-level ones, so the paper derives a logarithmic outlier score β_i per Transformer block that combines three factors: the Frobenius norm of the weight perturbation, the trace of the input activation covariance, and the product of squared Frobenius norms of all downstream layer weights—avoiding explicit Hessian computation while capturing perturbation propagation. Based on β_i, OCP applies three hierarchical compensation strategies selected by thresholds: MSE minimization of output error at the linear-sublayer level, attention-score-matrix deviation minimization at the Transformer-block level, and KL-divergence minimization of the full output distribution at the whole-model level. A sliding-window mechanism identifies individual outlier elements whose deviation from local statistics exceeds a threshold, clusters them into a separate outlier codebook, and the final reconstructed weights combine compensated normal quantized entries with outlier-specific reconstructions.

## Results

At 4-bit quantization, NuBitQ achieves the lowest perplexity across all tested models, e.g., 5.79 WikiText2 PPL on LLaMA3-8B (vs. 5.81 GPTVQ, 6.04 AQLM, 6.10 VPTQ) and 10.68 on Gemma2-9B (vs. 10.70 GPTVQ, 10.91 AQLM). With the OCP module at 3-bit, NuBitQ+OCP reaches 5.66 WikiText2 PPL on LLaMA3-8B—nearly matching the FP16 baseline of 5.57—while the best baseline (GPTVQ+OCP) only reaches 6.12. At 2-bit, the gap widens dramatically: NuBitQ+OCP achieves 6.42 WikiText2 PPL versus 9.14 (GPTVQ+OCP) and 8.92 (VPTQ+OCP). On task accuracy (Table 2, LLaMA3-8B), NuBitQ+OCP at 3-bit attains 62.07% average MMLU knowledge accuracy, essentially matching FP16 (62.18%), while GPTVQ+OCP drops to 48.81%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NuBitQ outperforms all three baselines (AQLM, VPTQ, GPTVQ) at 4-bit across every model and metric, with the largest margins on Qwen3-8B and Gemma2-9B where VPTQ catastrophically fails (PPL > 22 vs. ~8.8 for NuBitQ). GPTVQ is the strongest baseline, but NuBitQ still edges it out by 0.02–0.12 PPL at 4-bit. The advantage grows substantially at lower bit-widths with OCP: at 3-bit on LLaMA3-8B, NuBitQ+OCP (5.66 Wiki PPL) beats GPTVQ+OCP (6.12) by 0.46 and AQLM+OCP (6.93) by 1.27; at 2-bit the gap widens to 2.72 over GPTVQ+OCP (6.42 vs. 9.14). On task accuracy (Table 2, LLaMA3-8B), NuBitQ+OCP at 3-bit achieves 62.07% average MMLU accuracy—nearly lossless vs. FP16 (62.18%)—while GPTVQ+OCP drops to 48.81% and AQLM+OCP to 31.58%. VPTQ is particularly weak on Qwen3 and Gemma2 models (PPL in the thousands at 2-bit), likely because its Hessian-based optimization was not enabled for those architectures, whereas NuBitQ's design avoids Hessian computation entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

NuBitQ extends the additive vector-quantization paradigm established by AQLM, VPTQ, and GPTVQ by introducing flexible per-layer parameter combinations (number of codebooks r, cluster centers c, subvector dimension d, groups g) that enable arbitrary bit-width allocation without fixed codebook sizes. Its key departure is the Outlier Compensation Plugin (OCP), which addresses a gap identified in prior work: non-uniform quantization produces block-level, input-dependent outliers rather than the channel-level outliers that uniform-quantization methods (LLM.int8, AWQ, SmoothQuant) target. Unlike GPTVQ and BCQ, which rely on expensive Hessian-guided optimization or layer-wise fine-tuning, OCP uses a trace-based approximation of perturbation propagation that avoids second-order matrix computation entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play OCP module is practically significant because it can be grafted onto existing non-uniform quantization methods (demonstrated with AQLM, VPTQ, GPTVQ) to recover performance at ultra-low bit-widths without retraining, lowering the barrier to 2-bit deployment. However, the 4-bit gains over GPTVQ are modest (e.g., 5.79 vs. 5.81 WikiText2 PPL on LLaMA3-8B), and the parameter selection still relies on manual grid search rather than an automated procedure. Open questions include whether the outlier score generalizes to multimodal or non-Transformer architectures, whether the block-level outlier characterization holds under different calibration data, and whether the three-level compensation hierarchy can be further automated to reduce the threshold-tuning burden.
