## Problem

Vector-quantized networks (VQNs) used as discrete tokenizers for image generation suffer from three fundamental optimization challenges introduced by the straight-through estimator: estimation bias, one-step-behind updates, and sparse codebook gradients. These issues cause codebook collapse—where only a small fraction of code vectors receive meaningful updates—making it impossible to achieve full codebook usage when scaling to large codebooks (e.g., 262k entries) or high vector channels (e.g., 256), which in turn limits reconstruction quality and downstream generative performance.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes 256×256 RGB images and produces a 16×16 grid of discrete tokens (at 16× downsampling) or a 32×32 grid (at 8×), with codebook sizes ranging from 16k to 262k and vector channels from 4 to 512. The VQBridge projector operates only during training to jointly optimize the codebook; at inference it is discarded entirely, so the deployed model retains the original VQN architecture with zero additional latency. When paired with an autoregressive generator (LlamaGen), the discrete token sequence is decoded into a 256×256 class-conditional image.

## Method

FVQ introduces VQBridge, a compress–process–recover projector that maps the original codebook to a jointly optimized version. It first applies 1D patchification to group K code vectors into p compressed tokens via a shared linear projection, then applies N=2 ViT blocks with global self-attention to model dense cross-code interactions, and finally unpatchifies back to the original codebook size and dimensionality. Combined with learning-rate annealing (warmup followed by linear decay to 1%), this design ensures that gradients from frequently used codes propagate through attention weights to all codes, preventing the low-rank, usage-sparse updates that cause collapse in pointwise MLP or linear projectors. The approach achieves 100% codebook usage across all tested configurations—including a 262k-codebook with 256 channels—while adding only ~3M parameters (~4% overhead) and no inference cost, yielding state-of-the-art reconstruction (rFID 0.88) and, when plugged into LlamaGen-XL, a FID of 2.07 that surpasses both VAR and DiT.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 reconstruction, FVQ achieves rFID=1.30 (40 epochs) and rFID=1.17 (120 epochs) with 100% codebook usage, substantially outperforming VQGAN (rFID=4.98, 5.9% usage) and LlamaGen (rFID=2.19, 97% usage). Scaling to a 262k codebook with 120 epochs yields rFID=0.88, the best discrete tokenizer result reported. For class-conditional ImageNet generation, FVQ-XL (LlamaGen-XL, 775M) achieves FID=2.07, surpassing VAR-d20 (600M, FID=2.57) and DiT-XL/2 (675M, FID=2.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FVQ decisively outperforms all discrete tokenizer baselines on reconstruction: it beats VQGAN (rFID 1.30 vs. 4.98 at 16k/256ch, with 100% vs. 5.9% usage), LlamaGen (1.30 vs. 2.19), VQGAN-LC (1.30 vs. 3.01), and IBQ (1.30 vs. 1.55), while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.13). In the generation setting, FVQ-XL (775M, FID=2.07) surpasses the larger VAR-d20 (600M, FID=2.57) by 0.5 FID and DiT-XL/2 (675M, FID=2.27) by 0.2 FID, and even outperforms VAR-d24 (1.0B, FID=2.09) and L-DiT-3B (3.0B, FID=2.10) with fewer parameters. FVQ-L (343M, FID=2.39) also beats LlamaGen-L (3.81) and IBQ-B (2.88) at the same parameter scale. The method generalizes to multi-code VQ: FVQ(RQ-VAE) at 10 epochs (rFID=2.98) surpasses vanilla RQ-VAE at 50 epochs (3.20), and FVQ(VAR) achieves rFID=0.80 vs. VAR's 1.00. The only continuous tokenizer that matches or beats FVQ is VAVAE (rFID=0.28), which uses a fundamentally different (non-discrete) architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear reparameterization framework for VQ codebook optimization introduced by Huh et al. (ICML 2023) and Zhu et al. (2024), which showed that jointly transforming the codebook through a mapping function f(·) mitigates STE-induced optimization challenges. The key departure is the finding that a linear (or even 5-layer MLP) projector is insufficient for scaling: it collapses under learning-rate annealing and fails at large codebook sizes. VQBridge replaces the pointwise mapping with a ViT-based compress-process-recover pipeline (inspired by DiT's architecture) that introduces dense cross-code interaction via self-attention, enabling 100% codebook usage at scales (262k entries, 256-dim vectors) where prior projectors fail. The downstream generation experiments plug FVQ into LlamaGen's autoregressive framework, building on VQGAN's encoder/decoder architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a vanilla autoregressive model (LlamaGen) equipped with FVQ surpasses both the more sophisticated VAR (FID 2.07 vs. 2.57) and the diffusion-based DiT-XL/2 (2.07 vs. 2.27) shifts the narrative for AR image generation: the tokenizer, not the generator architecture, was the binding constraint. VQBridge adds only ~3M parameters and zero inference cost, making it a drop-in improvement for any VQ-based tokenizer. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger unlabeled datasets (OpenImages, LAION-COCO), whether it enables a unified tokenizer aligned with CLIP's 512-dim embeddings for joint generation and understanding, and whether substantially larger codebooks require correspondingly larger generators to avoid optimization difficulty.
