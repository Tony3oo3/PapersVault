## Problem

Behavior cloning policies suffer from compounding errors during closed-loop deployment: small deviations accumulate, driving the agent into out-of-distribution states where the learned policy oscillates arbitrarily. Existing remedies (simulators, interactive experts, explicit smoothness regularizers with tuned hyperparameters) go beyond the pure BC setting. The paper asks whether variance can be reduced using only the original expert demonstration dataset, with no additional data, supervision, or task-specific structure.

## Input / Output

At inference, DARP takes a query state (low-dimensional state vector or high-dimensional R3M image embedding) and the full expert demonstration dataset. The query state is used to retrieve k nearest neighbors via a history-aware distance metric (accumulated Euclidean distances over a lookback window with exponential decay), and the predictor f_θ receives tuples of (neighbor state, neighbor action, difference vector s_i* − s_q) rather than the query state alone. Output is either a single action (via averaging) or parameters of a richer action distribution (GMM, diffusion score) produced by a permutation-invariant aggregator g_ψ over the k candidate actions.

## Method

DARP reparameterizes imitation learning from direct state-to-action mapping to a retrieval-based, difference-aware architecture: for each query state, k neighbors are retrieved from the expert dataset, and a network f_θ predicts a candidate action for each neighbor conditioned on (s_i*, a_i*, Δs_i = s_i* − s_q), with candidates aggregated by a permutation-invariant function g_ψ (simple average, Set Transformer, or DeepSets). Training uses the standard BC objective with no additional regularization term or hyperparameter λ. The key theoretical result (Theorem 2) shows that the neighbor aggregation step implicitly applies the fixed spectral filter φ(μ) = 1 − μ on the k-NN graph Laplacian, making DARP equivalent to Laplacian smoothing (manifold regularization) with effective λ ≈ 1, thereby suppressing high-frequency variance without explicit tuning. The difference-vector parameterization is critical: ablations show it lets the network learn how expert actions vary under local perturbations, and divergence analysis confirms that at the point where BC fails, the query state is out-of-distribution but the difference vectors remain in-distribution, enabling DARP to recover.

## Results

DARP achieves 15–46% improvements over standard behavior cloning across all tested domains. On MuJoCo locomotion tasks, DARP scores 3545 (Hopper), 4383 (Ant), 4894 (Walker), and 5515 (HalfCheetah), outperforming BC by 53%, 84%, 84%, and 419% respectively. On Robosuite manipulation (low-dim state), DARP reaches 72/63/62% success on Stack/Threading/Peg vs. BC's 47/37/46%. With high-dimensional R3M visual features, DARP further widens the gap to 75/76/52% vs. BC's 44/38/17%, and on the multimodal Push-T task, DARP with a GMM head scores 70±8 vs. BC's 48±8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DARP outperforms all five baselines across every domain tested. Against standard BC (Pomerleau, 1991), DARP wins on all tasks: 53–419% score gains on MuJoCo, 15–25 percentage-point gains on Robosuite/RoboCasa manipulation, and 22% improvement on Push-T. Against REGENT (Sridhar et al., 2025), a transformer-based in-context retrieval method, DARP achieves substantially higher scores on all four MuJoCo tasks (e.g., 3545 vs. 1819 on Hopper, 5515 vs. 170 on HalfCheetah). Against LWR (Pari et al., 2022), a locally weighted regression baseline, DARP wins on all MuJoCo tasks (e.g., 4383 vs. 847 on Ant). Against R&P (Sridhar et al., 2025), which simply retrieves the nearest-neighbor action, DARP dominates across the board. Against CCIL (Ke et al., 2024), an explicit smoothness-constrained method, DARP achieves higher relative improvement on 3 of 4 MuJoCo tasks (53.2% vs. 32.6% on Hopper, 84.5% vs. 12.6% on Ant, 418.7% vs. 5.4% on HalfCheetah) and ties on Walker (84.1%). DARP also composes with Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2024), where DARP+Diffusion outperforms standalone Diffusion by 30–28 percentage points on Robosuite tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DARP synthesizes two previously separate threads in imitation learning: local/non-parametric methods (VINN, LWR, nearest-neighbor policies) that adapt predictions to dataset structure, and global parametric behavior cloning that compresses demonstrations into a single function. Its key conceptual departure is from explicit manifold regularization (Laplacian smoothing as an added loss term with a tunable λ), showing instead that a specific architectural choice—conditioning a parametric predictor on (neighbor state, neighbor action, difference vector) tuples and aggregating via a permutation-invariant function—implicitly applies the same spectral low-pass filter without any additional hyperparameter. This positions DARP between pure non-parametric retrieval (which blurs multimodal actions) and in-context learning methods like REGENT (which target adaptation to new tasks rather than stability on the original task), offering a parameter-free variance-reduction mechanism that requires no assumptions beyond standard BC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DARP provides a drop-in, assumption-free improvement over behavior cloning that is both theoretically grounded (equivalence to Laplacian smoothing) and empirically consistent (15–46% gains across MuJoCo, Robosuite, RoboCasa, and vision-based settings), making it a practical tool for the robotics community where BC is the dominant training paradigm. The divergence analysis—that at the point of BC failure the absolute state is out-of-distribution but the difference vector to a neighbor remains in-distribution—offers a clear mechanistic explanation for why the reparameterization helps, which could inform future retrieval-based or local methods in sequential decision-making. Open questions include scalability to very large demonstration corpora (retrieval cost grows with dataset size), whether the spectral analysis extends to non-Euclidean or learned metric spaces, and how DARP interacts with online or corrective learning settings where the dataset itself evolves.
