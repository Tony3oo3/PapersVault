## Problem

Existing trajectory data preparation (TDP) methods assume centralized data access and are designed for single tasks, making them inapplicable under strict privacy regulations that vertically partition trajectories across regional silos and unable to generalize across the 10+ heterogeneous TDP tasks (imputation, map matching, anomaly detection, etc.). No prior work systematically addresses federated TDP under vertical partitioning with a unified, task-agnostic model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265007/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatio-temporal points (longitude, latitude, timestamp) stored in vertically partitioned regional silos, where each client holds only the sub-trajectory segments within its geographic boundary. For cross-client tasks, raw points are first encoded into 32-dimensional embeddings by a local TPA before transmission; the server-side LLM operates on these embeddings plus structured prompts of the form (Task, Data, Information, Format), where Information optionally incorporates road network segments or weather tokens from public sources. Outputs are task-specific: reconstructed/cleaned trajectories, classification labels (anomaly, travel mode, user identity), spatio-temporal point sequences, road segment IDs, or trajectory segment boundaries.

## Method

FedTDP couples a server-side LLM with client-side SLMs through three modules. (1) A Trajectory Privacy AutoEncoder (TPA), a 3-layer MLP with 256 hidden / 32 embedding dimensions, encodes local sub-trajectories into embeddings for cross-client transmission; its parameters are aggregated via a decentralized secret-sharing protocol (pairwise keys with signed masking) that provably cancels all secret terms, preventing embedding and gradient inversion attacks without adding noise. (2) A Trajectory Knowledge Enhancer (TKE) bridges text-oriented LLMs to trajectory semantics via four mechanisms: trajectory-aware prompt engineering, offsite-tuning (dispatching the LLM's last-layer adapter to the client SLM for joint fine-tuning), LoRA sparse-tuning (selecting only the top-m layers by normalized parameter change rate each round to reduce trainable parameters), and bidirectional KL-divergence distillation (inverse KL aligns SLM→LLM output space; forward KL lets the LLM learn trajectory knowledge from the SLM's access to raw data). (3) Federated Parallel Optimization (FPO) decomposes training via split learning (clients train TPA+SLM, server trains LLM), applies alternating optimization to freeze embeddings/results between rounds to cut communication, and runs multiple loss objectives in parallel on each side, reducing training runtime by 11–14× versus LLM-based baselines.

## Results

FedTDP achieves the best F1 score across all 10 TDP tasks on 6 datasets. On the seen GeoLife benchmark, it reaches 82.89% F1 on Anomaly Detection, 82.89% on Trajectory Imputation, and 82.69% on Travel Mode Identification, surpassing the strongest spatio-temporal LLM baseline (UrbanGPT) by 1.7–13.2 percentage points. On unseen datasets, gains are larger: 68.78% F1 on Porto AD (vs. 53.54% best baseline), 62.44% on Porto TI (vs. 43.13%), and 61.33% on Tencent MM (vs. 49.58%). The paper reports overall improvements of ≥18.38% over single-task methods, ≥32.26% over LLM-based tabular preparation methods, and 4.84%–45.22% over LLM-based spatio-temporal analysis methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedTDP is compared against 13 baselines in three categories. Against the 7 single-task S-TDP methods (ATROM, Kamel, GraphMM, AttnTUL, Estimator, S3, LightTR), FedTDP wins on every task by at least 18.38% F1, demonstrating that task-specific models cannot capture shared spatio-temporal knowledge. Against the 3 LLM-based tabular data preparation methods (FM4DP, MELD, TableGPT), FedTDP leads by at least 32.26%, as these methods treat trajectories as unordered rows and ignore sequential/spatial dependencies. Against the 3 LLM-based spatio-temporal analysis methods (PromptGAT, UniST, UrbanGPT), FedTDP achieves the largest margins on unseen datasets—e.g., 15.24 points on Porto AD (68.78 vs. 53.54) and 11.75 points on Tencent MM (61.33 vs. 49.58)—while on seen GeoLife tasks the gap is smaller (1.7–13.2 points), with UrbanGPT being the closest competitor. On TSim (where lower SED is better), FedTDP achieves 1.39 on GeoLife and 2.11 on T-Drive, outperforming all baselines including UniST (1.57/3.40) and UrbanGPT (1.69/3.19). No baseline outperforms FedTDP on any task-dataset combination in the main results table.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) trajectory data preparation (TDP), where prior methods (ATROM, Kamel, GraphMM, etc.) are single-task and assume centralized data access; (2) LLM-based data preparation, where FM4DP, MELD, and TableGPT handle tabular data but ignore spatio-temporal structure; and (3) federated learning, where most prior work addresses horizontal partitioning (different users at different institutions) rather than the vertical partitioning of trajectories across geographic regions. The paper directly builds on offsite-tuning (Xiao et al., 2023) for the adapter-foundation split between server LLM and client SLM, and repurposes Shamir secret sharing for secure TPA model aggregation. Its key departure is framing F-TDP as a unified, multi-task, vertically-partitioned federated problem and coupling it with a TDP-oriented LLM via the Trajectory Knowledge Enhancer, rather than training separate task-specific models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: privacy regulations (PIPL, FGDC) and platform architectures (e.g., Uber Movement) already enforce vertical trajectory partitioning, yet no prior work systematically handles TDP under this constraint. The unified multi-task LLM approach could reduce the engineering burden of maintaining separate models for each TDP task across regions. However, the privacy guarantee relies on keeping the TPA encoder/decoder private (a threat-model assumption rather than a cryptographic guarantee), the secret-sharing scheme requires pairwise key exchange that may not scale to many clients, and the framework's complexity (TPA + SLM + LLM + TKE + FPO) raises deployment questions. Open questions include whether the LLM-based approach truly outperforms simpler federated sequence models for individual TDP tasks, and how the framework behaves with heterogeneous client data distributions or dynamic client participation.
