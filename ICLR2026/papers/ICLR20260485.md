## Problem

Existing methods for enforcing hard constraints in probabilistic deep learning models face a fundamental trade-off: approaches that provide uncertainty quantification (e.g., ProbConserv, HierE2E) either apply constraints only as post-processing at inference time or require expensive sampling during training, and none can handle general nonlinear constraints end-to-end. This paper asks whether one can learn a full probabilistic model (mean and covariance) under arbitrary hard constraints in a single differentiable, sampling-free training loop.

## Input / Output

The framework is backbone-agnostic: for PDEs it takes spatial-temporal parameter fields (e.g., initial conditions, PDE coefficients) and outputs a constrained multivariate distribution over the solution field; for hierarchical forecasting it takes historical time-series observations and outputs constrained distribution parameters over all hierarchy levels. During training the DPPL operates directly on the (μ, Σ) parameter pair (sampling-free), while at inference it projects individual samples through a constrained least-squares solve to guarantee strict per-sample feasibility. The weight matrix Q in the projection (oblique with Q=Σ⁻¹ vs. orthogonal with Q=I) controls how uncertainty structure is preserved under the constraint.

## Method

ProbHardE2E introduces a Differentiable Probabilistic Projection Layer (DPPL) that acts as a corrector step after an unconstrained probabilistic backbone (e.g., VarianceNO, DeepVAR). The key mechanism is a two-mode design: during training, the DPPL projects the distribution parameters (μ, Σ) onto the constraint manifold using a first-order delta-method approximation (Theorem 3.1), yielding closed-form updated parameters (μ̂, Σ̂) without any sampling; during inference, it projects each sample via a constrained least-squares problem with a symmetric positive semi-definite weight Q. The DPPL handles three constraint classes—linear equality (closed-form oblique projection), nonlinear equality (implicit differentiation through KKT conditions), and convex inequality (differentiable convex optimization solvers)—all with backpropagatable gradients. Training optimizes the CRPS (a strictly proper scoring rule) using its closed-form expression for location-scale distributions, which is distribution-agnostic and avoids the bias of NLL-based objectives, yielding up to 3–5× speedup over sampling-based baselines.

## Results

On four PDE datasets (Heat, PME, Advection, Stefan), ProbHardE2E-Or achieves the best CRPS (×10⁻³) of 0.271, 1.798, 2.94, and 7.52 (Ob variant) respectively, with zero constraint error across all datasets. On five hierarchical time-series benchmarks, ProbHardE2E achieves state-of-the-art CRPS (×10⁻³) on LABOUR (28.6), TOURISM-L (155.2), and WIKI (212.1). The paper reports up to 15× lower MSE and 2.5× improved CRPS over baselines in the nonlinear constraint setting, and a 3–5× reduction in training time from the sampling-free closed-form CRPS formulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ProbConserv (Hansen et al., 2023), which applies oblique projection only at inference, ProbHardE2E-Or reduces CRPS by 31% on Heat (0.271 vs 0.392), 11% on Advection (2.94 vs 3.88), and 4% on Stefan (7.52 vs 7.85), while also achieving zero constraint error (ProbConserv has CE > 0 on all PDEs). Against HierE2E (Rangapuram et al., 2021) on time series, ProbHardE2E-Or wins on LABOUR (28.6 vs 50.5), TOURISM (82.4 vs 103.1), TOURISM-L (155.2 vs 161.3), and WIKI (212.1 vs 216.5), but loses on TRAFFIC (60.6 vs 41.8). Against the unconstrained VarianceNO backbone (Mouli et al., 2024), ProbHardE2E improves CRPS on all four PDE datasets (e.g., 2.94 vs 3.98 on Advection) while enforcing zero constraint error versus VarianceNO's large violations. Against classical statistical baselines (ARIMA-NaiveBU, ETS-NaiveBU, PERMBU-MINT), ProbHardE2E achieves the best CRPS on LABOUR, TOURISM-L, and WIKI, and is competitive on TOURISM (82.4 vs PERMBU-MINT's 77.1) and TRAFFIC. The oblique projection variant (Q=Σ⁻¹) outperforms the orthogonal variant (Q=I) on the hard Stefan problem (7.52 vs 14.15 CRPS) and TOURISM-L, while the orthogonal variant is better on easier PDEs and most time-series datasets, indicating that covariance-weighted corrections better preserve uncertainty structure around sharp features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: differentiable optimization layers (Amos & Kolter, 2017; Agrawal et al., 2019), hard-constraint enforcement in neural networks (DC3, HardNet), and probabilistic forecasting/PDE solving with conservation laws (HierE2E, CLOVER, ProbConserv). The key departure is the Differentiable Probabilistic Projection Layer (DPPL), which extends projection-based constraint enforcement from point estimates to full distribution parameters via a first-order Delta Method, enabling end-to-end training with UQ under nonlinear constraints. Prior probabilistic methods (HierE2E, CLOVER) required expensive sampling during training and handled only linear constraints, while PDE-focused methods (ProbConserv, HardC) applied constraints only as post-processing at inference. ProbHardE2E also shifts the training objective from NLL to the distribution-agnostic CRPS, a choice well-motivated in forecasting but uncommon in SciML.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a single, model-agnostic architecture for constrained probabilistic learning that spans PDE solving and hierarchical forecasting, with a 3–5× training speedup from the sampling-free closed-form CRPS and the ability to handle nonlinear equality and convex inequality constraints that prior UQ methods could not. Practical limitations remain: the distributional assumption is restricted to location-scale families, inequality constraints must be convex, and the covariance is diagonal in experiments. Open questions include extending to general non-convex nonlinear inequalities, richer (low-rank or dense) covariance structures, and local (per-cell) conservation à la finite volume methods, which the authors identify as natural next steps.
