## Problem

Despite the widespread adoption of DPO for LLM alignment, no systematic, per-sample analysis of open-source DPO datasets exists: it remains unclear which individual preference pairs are high-quality, whether preference orders are coherent, or how to combine multiple corpora into an effective mixture. Existing comparisons are limited to small subsets under heterogeneous training setups, and most datasets lack rich annotations that would enable principled, data-driven curation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw DPO preference pairs (instruction prompt + chosen completion + rejected completion) from five open-source datasets totaling approximately 440k samples. It produces two outputs: (1) per-sample annotations covering 12-class task category, query difficulty, input quality, and an independent preference reward score computed by the FsfairX reward model, and (2) UltraMix, a 190k-sample curated DPO mixture that is 30% smaller than TuluDPO while outperforming it across 14 benchmarks on 8 models of varying scale.

## Method

The pipeline uses the Magpie framework with Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct as a judge to assign task category, difficulty, and input quality to every preference pair, augmented with a novel preference reward signal from the FsfairX reward model that independently scores chosen and rejected completions. This reveals that 20–30% of pairs in popular datasets have misaligned preference orders and that prompt quality strongly correlates with reward margins. The curation recipe applies a multi-signal filter—retaining only samples with high input quality, non-trivial difficulty, and reward-coherent pairs—followed by percentile-based reward thresholds and deduplication, then strategically boosts math/code samples and recovers instruction-following tasks with relaxed quality constraints. The key finding is that no single signal suffices: effective curation requires the principled combination of quality, reward coherence, and task-awareness, grounded theoretically in DPO's margin-based optimization where incoherent pairs generate conflicting gradients that degrade the implicit reward model.

## Results

UltraMix-190k, the final curated DPO mixture (190k samples, 30% smaller than TuluDPO's 272k), achieves an overall average of 56.04 on Llama-3.1-8B-TuluSFT and 62.05 on Qwen-2.5-7B-TuluSFT, outperforming TuluDPO (53.96 and 59.56 respectively) across 14 benchmarks. Notable gains include TruthfulQA (63.32 vs 56.78 for Llama), MATH (23.56 vs 22.66 for Llama; 49.55 vs 43.13 for Qwen), and IFEval (81.13 vs 80.35 for Llama). Across six additional open models of varying scales (1B–8B), UltraMix-190k consistently leads all original DPO datasets, e.g., improving OLMo-2-1B-SFT from 37.63 (TuluDPO) to 38.74 (UltraMix-190k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TuluDPO (the strongest individual DPO dataset), UltraMix-190k wins on overall average for both Llama (+2.08) and Qwen (+2.49), with the largest gains on TruthfulQA (+6.54 for Llama), MATH (+6.42 for Qwen), and BBH (+4.50 for Llama). Compared to ORPO, UltraFeedback, and HelpSteer, UltraMix-190k leads by 3.95–5.60 points on overall average for Llama and 3.53–5.03 points for Qwen, confirming that no single open DPO dataset matches the curated mixture. Code-Preference-Pairs is competitive on code benchmarks (70.68 vs 69.05 HumanEval for Llama) but falls well behind UltraMix-190k on math, reasoning, and instruction following. Against RIP-style prompt filtering (Yu et al., 2025), UltraMix-190k achieves 56.04 vs 54.00 (Llama) and 62.05 vs 59.56 (Qwen) overall, demonstrating that combining quality, reward-coherence, and task-aware filtering outperforms prompt-quality-only curation. Across six additional models (Apertus-8B, OLMo-2-7B, SmolLM-3-3B, Instella-3B, SmolLM-2-1.7B, OLMo-2-1B), UltraMix-190k consistently ranks first, with margins of 0.4–2.1 points over TuluDPO depending on model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) by treating the preference data itself as the primary optimization target, building on the Magpie annotation pipeline (Xu et al., 2025) and augmenting it with an independent reward-model signal (FsfairX) to validate preference ordering at scale. It departs from prior monolithic dataset comparisons (Ivison et al., 2024) and single-signal filtering approaches (RIP, Yu et al., 2025) by introducing a multi-signal, per-sample curation recipe combining input quality, reward coherence, and task-category balance. The work also extends the authors' own prior SFT data-quality analysis (Djuhera et al., 2025) to the preference-optimization setting, where the Bradley-Terry margin structure makes reward coherence a first-order concern rather than a secondary quality metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 20–30% of preference pairs in widely-used 'curated' DPO datasets have reward-inconsistent ordering challenges the assumption that popular open corpora are reliable as-is, and provides a concrete, reproducible mechanism (independent reward-model scoring) for detecting such noise. UltraMix demonstrates that principled multi-signal curation can reduce data volume by 30% while improving performance, offering a practical template for future dataset construction. Open questions include whether the curation recipe generalizes to PPO-based alignment or to newly released datasets, and whether the reward-model signal (FsfairX) remains reliable as base models and training distributions shift.
