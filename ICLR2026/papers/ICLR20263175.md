## Problem

Existing 2D-to-3D ultrasound reconstruction methods rely on implicit NeRF-style fields (computationally heavy), voxel grids (memory-limited), or camera-style light-transport assumptions (ray accumulation with transmittance) that fundamentally mismatch ultrasound physics, where waves propagate into tissue and return to the probe rather than reflecting off surfaces. No Gaussian Splatting approach tailored to ultrasound 2D-to-3D reconstruction existed, as the standard projection-and-occlusion paradigm of 3DGS does not apply to plane-based echo sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263175/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D ultrasound slices (160×160 pixels at 0.6×0.6 mm² resolution) paired with their 6-DoF probe poses — either ground-truth poses from volumetric 3D scans or poses estimated by a separate pose-regression network for sensorless freehand cinesweeps (~100 frames per video). It outputs a continuous 3D Gaussian representation (100K–2M anisotropic Gaussians) from which novel-view slices can be rendered at arbitrary orientations and resolutions, enabling retrospective multiplanar review of the reconstructed 160×160×160 volume.

## Method

UltraGauss replaces camera-style projection rendering with probe-plane intersection rendering: instead of projecting 3D Gaussians onto the image plane, it evaluates each anisotropic Gaussian's 3D Mahalanobis distance at the point where the pixel's ray intersects the probe plane, then aggregates weighted intensities in-plane (Eqs. 6–8). A triangular lower-triangular inverse-covariance (precision) parameterisation (Σ⁻¹ = LLᵀ + βI) guarantees positive-definiteness, enables O(1) inversion via forward substitution, and is 5.4× fewer FLOPs than the quaternion-based 3DGS formulation. Rasterisation is accelerated by closed-form χ² ellipsoidal bounds in probe coordinates that yield tight per-Gaussian 2D bounding boxes, coupled with a two-phase CUDA pipeline that first rejects Gaussians whose 3D bounding boxes do not intersect the probe plane, then rasterises only accepted Gaussians onto their 2D intersection windows with atomic accumulation. A lightweight Beer-Lambert attenuation term (computed via warp–cumsum–unwarp along the beam direction) models acoustic shadowing at negligible cost. The model is trained end-to-end with Adam, using standard densification/sparsification heuristics adapted for plane-intersection rasterisation, reaching 0.99 SSIM within ~20 minutes on a single RTX-A4000.

## Results

On the INTERGROWTH-21st 3D fetal brain volumes, UltraGauss-2M achieves a mean SSIM of 0.995 at convergence, exceeding the best baseline by ≥0.20 SSIM even at the 5-minute mark. On freehand cinesweep videos (Dataset B), UltraGauss reaches an average held-out test SSIM of 0.914 (std 0.012) versus 0.768 for RapidVol, 0.747 for ImplicitVol, and 0.531 for UltraNerf. UltraGauss is 6.94× faster than UltraNerf, and a clinician survey found 70% of expert sonographers rated UltraGauss reconstructions as more realistic than ground-truth 3D scans after just 4 minutes of training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UltraGauss decisively outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and training budget. Against RapidVol (hybrid implicit-explicit), UltraGauss gains +0.146 average test SSIM on cinesweeps (0.914 vs. 0.768) and shows markedly lower variance (0.012 vs. 0.028). Against ImplicitVol (fully implicit NeRF-like), the gap is +0.167 SSIM (0.914 vs. 0.747). Against UltraNerf (physics-informed ray tracing), UltraGauss leads by +0.383 SSIM (0.914 vs. 0.531) while being 6.94× faster, since UltraNerf requires precise probe geometry and parallel-ray assumptions that break down with curvilinear probes or sensorless acquisition. On the 3D scan benchmark (Dataset A), UltraGauss-2M reaches 0.995 SSIM at convergence versus ~0.75–0.97 for baselines, with at least 10× lower variance across gestational ages. In the clinician reader study, all 10 expert sonographers preferred UltraGauss over RapidVol and ImplicitVol under matched short training budgets (t≤20 min), and no clinician rated UltraGauss worse than alternatives at convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

UltraGauss extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) to ultrasound by replacing the core projection-and-depth-compositing paradigm with probe-plane intersection rendering, arguing that accumulated transmittance (central to both NeRF and camera GS) fundamentally mismatches wave-based echo acquisition. It departs from prior ultrasound reconstruction methods—NeRF-based (UltraNerf, ImplicitVol) and hybrid implicit-explicit (RapidVol)—which all inherit light-transport assumptions, and introduces a triangular inverse-covariance parameterisation that avoids the numerical instabilities of quaternion-based 3D covariance inversion. The Beer-Lambert shadow model provides a lightweight alternative to full physics-based wave simulation, and the two-phase CUDA rasterisation with χ² ellipsoidal bounds is a novel engineering contribution specific to plane-intersection geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first Gaussian Splatting framework tailored to ultrasound 2D-to-3D reconstruction, UltraGauss opens a practical path to minute-level volumetric reconstruction on a single GPU without external tracking hardware, which is directly relevant to scaling 3D assessment across resource-limited clinical settings. Key limitations include sensitivity to pose estimation error (~1.5% threshold), quality degradation below ~25% scan coverage, and inability to capture multi-scattering speckle that physics-based methods like UltraNerf can model. Open questions include whether joint pose-reconstruction optimisation can reduce the pose-accuracy requirement, whether richer acoustic effects (beyond Beer-Lambert) can be incorporated without sacrificing speed, and whether the approach generalises beyond fetal brain to other anatomies and probe types.
