## Problem

Gradient-regularized value learning methods such as MAGE improve sample efficiency by backpropagating through a learned world model to estimate action-gradients of the Q-function, but they treat these gradients deterministically and degrade in stochastic or noisy environments. The paper asks whether modeling the full distribution over both returns and their action-gradients—rather than just their expectations—can preserve the benefits of gradient-aware training under irreducible environmental uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The critic is a generative network that takes a state-action pair (s,a) and a noise vector ξ, and outputs a sample of the joint Sobolev return: a scalar discounted return Z and its action-gradient ∇_a Z, forming a (1+|A|)-dimensional vector. The cVAE world model takes (s,a) and a latent ε to produce a stochastic next-state-reward pair (ŝ', r̂), which is then differentiated with respect to (s,a) via reparameterization to supply the gradient terms in the Bellman backup. The policy network maps state s to a deterministic action a, and the actor is updated by ascending the critic's estimated expected return gradient.

## Method

The core contribution is a Sobolev Bellman operator that jointly bootstraps the distribution over returns and their action-gradients, expressed as a random affine pushforward: Z^{S_a}(s,a) = b(s,a) + L^{Sob}(s,a)[Z^{S_a}(s',a')], where the gradient component is derived by chain-rule differentiation of the Bellman target through the transition and policy. A reparameterized Sobolev critic generates samples of (Z, ∇_a Z) pairs, and a cVAE world model provides differentiable one-step transitions, relaxing the need for a known differentiable environment. Training minimizes the Max-sliced Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MSMMD) between predicted and bootstrapped Sobolev-return distributions, with TQC-style truncation to control overestimation bias. The paper proves the Sobolev operator is a strict contraction under both Wasserstein and MSMMD metrics (requiring γκ < 1, where κ depends on Jacobian bounds), establishing a fundamental smoothness-horizon trade-off for gradient-aware RL.

## Results

On a 2D point-mass toy task with N bonus locations, DSDPG (both MSMMD and MMD Sobolev variants) consistently outperforms all baselines as N increases, while deterministic Sobolev (MAGE) shows no clear advantage over gradient-agnostic critics. On six MuJoCo environments, DSDPG matches all baselines in the noise-free setting but outperforms every competitor in 3 of 6 environments under both multiplicative observation noise and Gaussian dynamics noise, with the largest gains on Ant-v2 and Humanoid-v2. Under multiplicative noise, MAGE suffers severe drops on Walker2d-v2 and Humanoid-v2, whereas DSDPG remains robust. The provably contractive MSMMD metric shows a mild advantage over plain MMD in the toy setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TD3-Huber (Fujimoto et al., 2018), DSDPG matches performance in the noise-free MuJoCo setting but clearly outperforms it under both multiplicative observation noise and Gaussian dynamics noise in 3 of 6 environments. Against MAGE (D'Oro & Jaskowski, 2020), the deterministic Sobolev baseline, DSDPG shows the most dramatic advantage: MAGE suffers severe performance drops on Walker2d-v2 and Humanoid-v2 under noise and exhibits larger variance on InvertedDoublePendulum-v2, while DSDPG maintains stable, competitive performance. Against IQN (Dabney et al., 2018a), a distributional baseline without gradient information, DSDPG matches in the noise-free case and outperforms under stochastic perturbations. Against standard MMD training (Nguyen et al., 2020), which uses MMD for distributional matching but without the Sobolev gradient component, DSDPG again matches in the noise-free setting and gains under noise, confirming that the gradient-aware distributional structure (not just distributional matching per se) drives the robustness improvement. Overall, the key finding is that the benefit of DSDPG is concentrated in noisy/stochastic environments, where deterministic gradient methods degrade and distributional gradient methods retain their sample-efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads—gradient-regularized value learning (Sobolev training, MAGE), distributional RL (C51, IQN), and differentiable world models (SVG)—into a unified framework that models the joint distribution over returns and their action-gradients. It departs from MAGE (D'Oro & Jaskowski, 2020) by making gradient modeling distributional rather than deterministic, and from standard distributional RL by incorporating gradient information directly into the bootstrapped Bellman quantity rather than using it only as an auxiliary regularizer. The cVAE world model extends SVG (Heess et al., 2015) with a more expressive generative architecture, and the MSMMD metric lifts the max-sliced divergence framework (Deshpande et al., 2019) into the RL setting to obtain a tractable, provably contractive training signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first contraction proofs for gradient-aware RL (Sobolev Temporal Difference) provide a theoretical foundation that was previously absent, and the smoothness trade-off (γκ < 1) reveals a fundamental tension between environment dynamics and effective horizon that practitioners can use to diagnose when gradient-based methods will struggle. Empirically, distributional gradient modeling shows robustness gains over deterministic Sobolev methods in noisy MuJoCo environments, though the improvements are moderate (3 of 6 tasks) and the computational cost of sampling multiple Sobolev returns per update remains high. Open questions include whether the complete Sobolev operator (jointly bootstrapping state- and action-gradients) can be made tractable, whether more efficient inductive biases can reduce the sampling overhead, and whether the framework transfers to other domains requiring aleatoric uncertainty in gradient modeling (PINNs, neural volume rendering).
