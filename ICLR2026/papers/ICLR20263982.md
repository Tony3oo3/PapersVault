## Problem

Dominant MSE loss in time series forecasting implicitly assumes a single-mode Gaussian with fixed variance, making it unable to capture multi-modal futures where identical pasts diverge into multiple possible outcomes. Existing probabilistic alternatives (Gaussian, Student-T, predefined mixture distributions) still rely on manually specified parametric forms that limit expressiveness for complex, real-world distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263982/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a past time series window of length T, divided into patches of length P (default 12), which a patch-based backbone encodes into l = τ/P latent tokens for the future horizon. At inference, it produces up to M=10 multi-mode predictions (each a full τ-length series) with associated probabilities via an evolving variational GMM, a deterministic point forecast obtained in a single forward pass through an anchor input, and N=100 probabilistic samples drawn over K_infer=20 diffusion steps. The loss is backbone-agnostic: it only requires the backbone to output per-patch latent tokens, making it applicable to decoder-only Transformers, RNNs, and masked autoencoders alike.

## Method

MMPD is a plug-and-play training loss that conditions a diffusion process on latent tokens from any patch-based backbone, rather than being a standalone generative model. The denoising network is a lightweight Patch Consistent MLP (a single AdaLN-MLP block) that, unlike independent per-patch MLPs, conditions on adjacent noisy patches to model the joint distribution and avoid discontinuous samples. Deterministic forecasting is integrated by adding an anchor term at a specific diffusion step where the noisy input is zero, reducing the diffusion objective to a scaled regression target with no extra architecture. For multi-mode extraction, an evolving variational GMM is fitted at each reverse diffusion step with Dirichlet and Gamma priors derived from the forward process, enabling adaptive mode selection and mitigating GMM initialization difficulty. The joint training objective balances the diffusion loss (weight λ=0.99) with the deterministic anchor term, and the entire loss adds negligible FLOPs over standard MSE since the backbone dominates computation.

## Results

On the primary diverse-forecasting metric (Top-3 MSE, averaged over 4 horizons), MMPD achieves rank 1 across all 8 datasets, with values ranging from 0.147 (ECL) to 0.396 (ETTh1), consistently outperforming the next-best Mix loss (rank 1.875) and MSE loss (rank 3.5). For deterministic forecasting (MSE), MMPD (rank 1.75) matches or slightly beats the MSE loss baseline (rank 1.875), e.g., 0.337 vs. 0.350 on ETTm1 and 0.264 vs. 0.270 on ETTm2. For probabilistic forecasting (CRPS), MMPD (rank 2) is on par with the best baseline Student-T (rank 2.25), e.g., 0.199 vs. 0.201 on WTH and 0.203 vs. 0.224 on Dynamic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMPD decisively outperforms all deterministic losses (MSE, MAE) and parametric distribution losses (Gaussian, Student-T) on diverse forecasting (Top-3 MSE/MAE), achieving rank 1 on all 8 datasets versus ranks 3.5–5.25 for MSE/MAE and 4.25–4.75 for Gaussian/Student-T. Against the closest diverse-forecasting competitor Mix (Woo et al., 2024), MMPD wins on Top-3 MSE by 0.02–0.06 per dataset (e.g., 0.269 vs. 0.289 on ETTm1, 0.193 vs. 0.209 on WTH), because Mix's mixture components are predefined while MMPD learns them adaptively. On deterministic MSE, MMPD (rank 1.75) slightly edges out the MSE loss baseline (rank 1.875), confirming that the diffusion-deterministic joint objective (Eq. 8) preserves point-forecast accuracy. On probabilistic CRPS, MMPD (rank 2) is essentially tied with Student-T (rank 2.25), while Mix suffers from infinity values due to its log-normal component generating outliers. Against standalone TS diffusion models (CSDI, TSDiff, MG-TSD, Diffusion-TS, D3U) on ETTh1 (T=96, τ=24), MMPD achieves the best Top-3 MSE (0.186 vs. 0.225–0.287), best MSE (0.298 vs. 0.338–0.351), best CRPS (0.254 vs. 0.265–0.306), and fastest inference (0.075 s vs. 0.217–1.014 s per instance).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the diffusion process (Ho et al., 2020) not as a standalone generative model but as a trainable loss function for time series forecasting, drawing on the AdaLN-MLP denoising architecture from DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2023). It departs from the dominant MSE/MAE regression paradigm and from parametric distribution losses (Gaussian, Student-T, Mix) by modeling the future distribution implicitly through conditional diffusion, and from standalone TS diffusion models (CSDI, TSDiff, MG-TSD) by decoupling the diffusion into a lightweight plug-and-play loss that leverages an upstream backbone. The multi-mode inference via an evolving variational GMM distributed across diffusion steps is a novel algorithmic contribution that goes beyond post-hoc clustering of final samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMPD addresses a fundamental expressiveness limitation of MSE-based training: the single-mode Gaussian assumption that prevents models from representing genuinely multi-modal futures, which is critical for risk-aware applications like trading. The plug-and-play design (applicable to any patch-based backbone with minimal changes) and the efficient lightweight denoiser make it practically adoptable, while the deterministic forecasting integration ensures no sacrifice in point-prediction accuracy. Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to non-patch-based or foundation-model architectures at scale, how the multi-mode inference cost scales with very long horizons, and whether the adaptive mode count can be further principled beyond the Dirichlet decay heuristic.
