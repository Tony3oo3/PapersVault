## Problem

Conventional Mixture-of-Experts architectures for LM-based embodied agents fix their routing functions after training, making adaptation to unseen or evolving domains require costly end-to-end retraining. In-context learning approaches merely shift the computational burden to inference with inflated context windows rather than providing true modularity. The paper asks whether the routing mechanism itself can be reconfigured at test time to enable rapid, data-efficient adaptation without retraining the full system.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural language task instruction and an environment observation encoded as a directed graph whose nodes are entities (agents, objects, rooms) and edges are relational triples (spatial containment, proximity, adjacency, agent-object interactions). At each of the 16 base-model layers, the router produces a sparse top-K mixture weight vector over N world-model adapters, and the final output is the next action token. In the real-world setup, RGB-D perception combined with a VLM generates the scene-graph input, introducing detection noise relative to the ground-truth text observations used in simulation.

## Method

TMoW extends the MoE paradigm by making the routing function itself trainable at test time. A multi-granular prototype-based router, built on a hybrid MPNN (GCN at layers 0, 4, 8, 12; MLP elsewhere) with a context-aware edge matrix that cross-attends observation hidden states against instruction embeddings, computes per-layer cosine-similarity scores between the current observation embedding and pre-extracted domain prototypes, enabling layer-wise mixture of LoRA adapters on Llama-3.2-1B. Test-time prototype refinement iteratively updates each prototype via a similarity-gated interpolation toward a weighted sum of all prototypes (weights from prototype-prototype similarity), expanding prototype coverage toward unseen-domain features without any gradient updates to the adapters. For fundamentally novel domains, distilled mixture-based augmentation initializes a new adapter as the router-weighted sum of existing adapters, fine-tunes it on few-shot demonstrations with a teacher-forcing loss, and registers its prototype directly into the routing set, enabling seamless incremental expansion.

## Results

On zero-shot adaptation (Table 1), TMoW achieves 83.61% SR on VirtualHome, 72.05% on ALFWorld, and 71.89% on RLBench for seen domains, and 80.16%, 68.83%, and 62.75% respectively for unseen domains, consistently outperforming all baselines. The headline gain is a 27.21% average SR improvement over SayCanPay (the strongest baseline) on unseen domains, with a 14.81% reduction in Pending Steps. In few-shot expansion (Table 2), TMoW reaches 82.59% average SR in VirtualHome with only 1–5 demonstrations, a 25.66% gain over SayCanPay. In real-world Franka Research 3 experiments, TMoW achieves 91.46% SR on seen domains and 74.64% on unseen domains, surpassing the best baseline (FLARE) by 34.36% and 38.60% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TMoW outperforms all five baselines across every environment and domain split. Against SayCanPay (the strongest baseline), TMoW gains +19.47% SR on VirtualHome unseen (80.16% vs. 49.53%), +26.79% on ALFWorld unseen (68.83% vs. 42.04%), and +24.21% on RLBench unseen (62.75% vs. 38.54%), while also reducing Pending Steps by 3–5 steps. FLARE, the second-strongest baseline, is surpassed by +40.09% SR on ALFWorld unseen (68.83% vs. 28.74% average of seen/unseen) and by +34.36% on real-world seen domains. LLM-Planner and LLM+FT show the largest degradation on unseen domains (e.g., LLM-Planner drops from 50.98% to 36.05% on VirtualHome), whereas TMoW's test-time prototype refinement limits the seen-to-unseen drop to only 3.45% SR. ZSP, the non-adaptation reference, remains far below all adapted methods (≤11% SR), confirming that some form of domain-specific knowledge is essential. In few-shot expansion, TMoW's distilled mixture augmentation yields 82.59% average SR in VirtualHome with 1–5 shots, beating SayCanPay (56.93%) by 25.66% and FLARE (42.29%) by 40.30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

TMoW extends the parameter-efficient MoE architecture of MixLoRA (Li et al., 2024) by making the routing function itself test-time adaptable rather than fixed after training, which is the core departure from conventional MoE. The multi-granular prototype router builds on MPNN/GCN message passing (Gilmer et al., 2017) with a novel context-aware edge matrix that conditions neighbor aggregation on the task instruction, while the world model formulation follows the sequence-modeling approach of Janner et al. (2021). Compared to prior embodied agents (SayCan, LLM-Planner, FLARE, SayCanPay) that rely on monolithic LLMs or in-context learning for domain shifts, TMoW introduces a modular, graph-based routing mechanism that reconfigures expert mixtures at test time and supports incremental expansion via distilled few-shot augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying embodied agents: the inability to adapt to new environments without costly retraining, which is especially problematic in real-world settings where tasks and scenes evolve continuously. The multi-granular routing insight—that domains share local object affordances but diverge at global scene structure—provides a principled mechanism for leveraging partial cross-domain knowledge. However, performance remains bounded by the underlying LLM's planning capability, and the approach may struggle in highly non-stationary multi-agent settings where environment dynamics shift continuously. Open questions include the safety and interpretability of test-time routing decisions, scalability to many more domains, and whether the prototype refinement mechanism generalizes beyond the relatively small number of world models tested here.
