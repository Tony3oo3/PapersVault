## Problem

Existing probability-level LLM ensemble methods that aggregate next-token distributions at every token position suffer severe performance degradation in long-form generation (e.g., CoT reasoning) when models use heterogeneous tokenizers, because tokenization mismatches introduce OOV-like tokens that corrupt probability distributions. The paper identifies that the critical unaddressed question is *when* to ensemble, not just *how*, and shows that two factors—tokenization mismatch and consensus in next-token probability distributions—determine the optimal ensembling positions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and k LLMs with heterogeneous tokenizers (e.g., Internlm3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, EXAONE-3.5-7.8B), designating the best-performing model as drafter and the rest as verifiers. The drafter generates lookahead sequences of n=5 tokens per step, which the verifiers process in a single parallel forward pass rather than autoregressively. Output is a greedy-decoded text response (CoT chain, max 2048 tokens) in the same format as individual model outputs, but with ensembled tokens replacing drafter tokens only at verified positions.

## Method

SAFE iterates a Generate-Verify-Ensemble cycle inspired by speculative decoding: the drafter produces n tokens, then verifiers check two conditions in one forward pass before ensembling is triggered. The first check (OOV-like token verification) ensures the preceding token's boundary aligns with every verifier's tokenization scheme, preventing distribution corruption. The second check (ensemble distribution verification) skips ensembling if all verifiers unanimously agree on the token or its average probability across all models exceeds 0.5, avoiding unnecessary alignment operations. When ensembling is triggered, a probability sharpening strategy (heuristic subword consolidation with threshold λ, or geometric-mean aggregation) concentrates diffused probability mass to enable confident token selection. A KV cache pruning mechanism maintains cache consistency after token replacement, and the overall design restricts autoregressive generation to the single drafter, yielding inference speed comparable to individual models.

## Results

On the two-model ensembling (Internlm3-8B + Qwen2.5-7B) with CoT, UniTE+SAFE achieves an average accuracy of 84.20% across five benchmarks, outperforming the best individual model (Internlm3 at 82.87%) and the UniTE baseline (75.12%). The most dramatic improvement is on MATH500, where UniTE drops to 59.6% (vs. 74.8% for the best individual model) but UniTE+SAFE recovers to 77.4% (+2.6 over the best individual). The three-model UniTE+SAFE ensemble achieves the highest average of 84.59%. Critically, SAFE reduces the ensemble frequency (E/T) from 100% (UniTE) to 12–17%, meaning ensembling is triggered on fewer than 1 in 5 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UniTE (Yao et al., 2025), SAFE delivers the most dramatic gains: UniTE ensembles at every token (E/T = 100%) and suffers severe degradation under CoT, dropping to 59.6% on MATH500 (−15.2 below the best individual model) and 75.12% average. Adding SAFE reduces E/T to ~12–17% and lifts MATH500 to 77.4% (+2.6 over the best individual) and average to 84.20% (+1.33), effectively reversing the CoT degradation. Against GaC (Yu et al., 2024), which already ensembles selectively (only when the main LLM's probability < 0.5), SAFE provides more modest but consistent improvements: MATH500 rises from 74.2% to 76.0% and ARC-C from 90.61% to 91.13% for the Internlm3+Qwen2.5 pair, while further reducing E/T from 8.43% to 5.23%. The paper also shows that on models with near-identical tokenizers (Qwen2-7B + Llama-3.1-8B, >99% tokenization agreement), UniTE+SAFE still yields a +9.6% gain on MATH500 over the best individual model, confirming that the consensus-based verification (not just OOV-like prevention) contributes to accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the speculative decoding drafter-verifier paradigm (Leviathan et al., 2023) for LLM ensembling, where one model drafts a token sequence and the remaining models verify rather than autoregressively generate. It builds directly on two probability-level ensemble methods—UniTE (Yao et al., 2025) and GaC (Yu et al., 2024)—which focus on *how* to aggregate next-token distributions across heterogeneous vocabularies, but assume ensembling at every token or use a simple probability threshold. The key conceptual departure is identifying *when* to ensemble as the critical, previously unaddressed question for long-form generation, introducing the OOV-like token concept to formalize how tokenization mismatch corrupts probability distributions and destabilizes the ensemble.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard every-token ensembling actively degrades CoT performance (e.g., UniTE drops 15 points on MATH500) challenges the default assumption that more ensembling is always better, and provides a concrete diagnostic (OOV-like tokens) for why. SAFE's plug-and-play design makes it immediately applicable to existing ensemble pipelines, and the KV cache management resolves a practical bottleneck that prior ensemble methods left as future work. Limitations include evaluation only on 7B-scale non-reasoning models with greedy decoding; extending to reasoning models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, o3) and sampling-based decoding remains open, as does understanding whether the OOV-like token phenomenon persists at larger scales where tokenizers may converge.
