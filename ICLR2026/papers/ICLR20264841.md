## Problem

JEPA-based video representation learning (V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2) relies on EMA-updated dynamic teachers with stop-gradient to prevent representation collapse, which couples teacher and student architectures, makes the training loss uninformative for model selection, and introduces hyperparameter sensitivity. This paper asks whether such elaborate collapse-prevention machinery is actually necessary, or whether a simple frozen teacher can provide equally effective (or better) prediction targets for the student.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264841/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16-frame video clips at 224×224 spatial resolution (tubelet size 2, patch size 16×16×2) with V-JEPA-style multi-block masking applied to both stages. The student encoder produces video representation embeddings evaluated under a frozen-backbone protocol with attentive probing (multi-clip, multi-view for K400/SSv2; single-clip for ablations), while the predictor outputs latent-space predictions on masked spatio-temporal regions that are matched against the frozen teacher's embeddings via L1 loss. Downstream outputs include classification scores on K400, SSv2, COIN, Jester, Diving-48, ImageNet-1K, and surprise-based scores on intuitive physics benchmarks.

## Method

SALT (Static-teacher Asymmetric Latent Training) decomposes video representation learning into two decoupled stages. Stage 1 (V-Pixel) trains a teacher encoder with a pixel-reconstruction objective under V-JEPA's multi-block masking scheme, producing a frozen checkpoint. Stage 2 freezes this teacher and jointly optimizes a student encoder and a lightweight predictor (ViT with width 384, depth 12) to predict the teacher's latent embeddings on masked regions using the standard JEPA L1 objective—without EMA, stop-gradient, or any explicit regularizer. The key architectural asymmetry is that the teacher can be the same size or smaller than the student (e.g., a 300M ViT-L teacher supervises 600M–2B students), and the student's training loss is highly predictive of downstream accuracy (R² ≈ 0.95–0.98), enabling interpretable model selection. Empirically, compute is best allocated overwhelmingly to the student (e.g., 40k teacher steps + 200k student steps), and even small, sub-optimal teachers yield students that outperform same-size EMA-based V-JEPA 2 encoders.

## Results

Under frozen-backbone evaluation on the V-3.6M dataset, SALT ViT-L achieves 74.9% SSv2 and 85.4% K400 top-1 accuracy with only 1.2×10²¹ total FLOPs, outperforming V-JEPA 2 ViT-L (68.2% SSv2, 83.8% K400) at 1.4×10²¹ FLOPs. Scaling to ViT-g, SALT reaches 76.2% SSv2 and 86.8% K400 at 1.9×10²¹ FLOPs, surpassing V-JEPA 2 ViT-g (75.3% SSv2, 86.6% K400) which requires 5.3×10²¹ FLOPs. The student's training loss correlates strongly with downstream accuracy (R²=0.951–0.984), enabling interpretable model selection without proxy metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SALT decisively outperforms V-JEPA 2 on SSv2 across all model sizes when trained on the same V-3.6M dataset: SALT ViT-L gains +6.7 points (74.9 vs 68.2) and SALT ViT-H gains +2.0 points (75.4 vs 73.4), while using fewer total FLOPs (1.2 vs 1.4 and 1.5 vs 2.6 respectively). Against V-JEPA 2 trained on the larger VM-22M dataset, SALT ViT-g (76.2% SSv2 at 1.9 FLOPs) still exceeds V-JEPA 2 ViT-g (75.3% at 5.3 FLOPs), demonstrating a ~2.8× compute advantage. On K400, SALT is competitive with V-JEPA 2 (85.4–87.2% vs 85.1–86.6%) but trails PEcoreG (88.5%) and InternVideo2 (87.9%), which leverage much larger pretraining data (MetaCLIP-5B, IV-25.5M). SALT substantially outperforms reconstruction-based baselines VideoMAEv2 (56.1% SSv2) and MVD (66.5% SSv2), as well as image-only encoders DINOv2 (50.7%) and SigLIP2 (49.9%) on the motion-sensitive SSv2 benchmark. Notably, SALT uses a single frozen ViT-L (300M) teacher for all student sizes up to ViT-G (2B), whereas V-JEPA 2 requires an EMA teacher of the same size as the student, making SALT's compute savings grow with model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

SALT builds directly on the V-JEPA/V-JEPA 2 masked-latent-prediction framework (Bardes et al., 2024; Assran et al., 2025) and the VideoMAE pixel-reconstruction objective (Tong et al., 2022), but departs from the JEPA family's core design by replacing the EMA-updated, stop-gradient teacher with a fully frozen encoder trained in a separate Stage 1. This is conceptually closer to prior two-stage distillation work (MVD, InternVideo2, VideoPrism) that uses pretrained frozen teachers, but SALT's key distinction is that the teacher is a small, cheaply-trained pixel-reconstruction model rather than a large vision-language or same-size encoder, and the student is evaluated under frozen-backbone probing rather than fine-tuning. The paper also connects to the broader BYOL/SimSiam/Asym-Siam literature on asymmetry in self-distillation, arguing that the elaborate EMA collapse-prevention machinery is unnecessary when the teacher is static.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 300M-parameter teacher trained for only 40k steps can supervise 1B–2B students that outperform same-size EMA-based V-JEPA 2 encoders challenges a core assumption in the JEPA family and has direct practical implications: compute budgets should be overwhelmingly allocated to the student, and model selection becomes interpretable via the student's training loss (R²≈0.95 with downstream accuracy). Open questions include what property of a teacher makes it 'good' (neither loss nor embedding rank is predictive), whether the weak-teacher/strong-student effect persists at much larger data scales (the paper trains on 3.6M clips vs. billions for V-JEPA 2's original data), and whether a principled theoretical characterization of teacher quality can be developed. Performance plateaus at larger model sizes in the current setting, suggesting data limits that larger pretraining corpora may resolve.
