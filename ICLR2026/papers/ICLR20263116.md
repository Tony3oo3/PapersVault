## Problem

Scaling diffusion transformers to generate minute-long multi-shot videos is fundamentally blocked by the quadratic cost of self-attention, which makes both memory and computation intractable at ~180k-token sequence lengths. Prior approaches either compress history into fixed-size representations (losing detail) or impose static sparse patterns that cannot adapt to which past events matter at each step, limiting long-range identity and narrative coherence. The paper reframes long-context video generation as an internal information retrieval problem and proposes a learnable sparse routing mechanism to solve it.

## Input / Output

The model takes a global scene caption (structured as '[Character] [Environment] [Story]' with named character IDs) plus per-shot captions describing local action and camera framing, and produces a 480p, 12 FPS multi-shot video of up to 8 shots (~64 seconds, ~180k tokens after VAE compression). Text and video tokens are interleaved in a single sequence with 3D RoPE positioning, and a special '[SHOT CUT]' token marks true shot transitions. The output preserves cross-shot subject identity, background geometry, and scene layout across hundreds to thousands of frames.

## Method

MoC replaces dense self-attention in the DiT backbone with a parameter-free, per-head top-k routing mechanism: the token stream is partitioned into content-aligned chunks along frames, shots, and caption boundaries, each chunk's keys are mean-pooled into a single descriptor, and every query token selects the top-k most relevant chunks via dot-product scoring. Two mandatory anchors are always included—cross-modal links to all text tokens (acting as attention sinks) and intra-shot local windows—while a causal mask enforces a directed acyclic interaction graph to prevent pathological loop closures. Training uses progressive sparsification (chunk size decreases from 10240 to 1280 tokens) with context drop-off/drop-in regularization, and the selected key-value pairs are processed in a single Flash-Attention var-len call. Built on the 3B-parameter LCT MMDiT and fine-tuned with its original recipe, MoC prunes 85% of token pairs, reduces attention FLOPs by >7×, and achieves a 2.2× end-to-end speedup while matching or exceeding dense-attention quality on VBench metrics.

## Results

On 8-shot, 64-second multi-shot scenes (~180k tokens), MoC achieves 85% attention sparsity while reducing FLOPs from 1.7×10¹³ to 2.3×10¹² (>7× reduction) and delivering a 2.2× end-to-end speedup. Quality metrics largely improve over the dense LCT baseline: Dynamic Degree rises from 0.4583 to 0.5625, Motion Smoothness from 0.9859 to 0.9920, and Subject Consistency from 0.9378 to 0.9421. The only slight regression is Image Quality (0.5140 → 0.5003), while Aesthetic Quality, Background Consistency, and all other metrics are matched or improved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is LCT (Guo et al., 2025), a dense-attention long-context video generator that expands a 3B MMDiT to an 8-shot scene window. MoC outperforms LCT on five of six VBench metrics—Subject Consistency (+0.004), Background Consistency (+0.001), Motion Smoothness (+0.006), Dynamic Degree (+0.104), and Aesthetic Quality (+0.002)—while incurring only a small drop in Image Quality (−0.014). The headline advantage is computational: MoC prunes 85% of token pairs, cutting attention FLOPs by over 7× and yielding a measured 2.2× speedup on minute-scale scenes. A secondary comparison against the native 3B MMDiT (Esser et al., 2024) on short single-shot videos (6.3k tokens, 83% sparsity) shows MoC matching or exceeding the dense baseline on all VBench metrics (e.g., Dynamic Degree 0.7500 vs. 0.6875), confirming that the routing benefit is not limited to long sequences. Overall, MoC consistently wins or ties on quality while delivering substantial efficiency gains, with the sole trade-off being a marginal Image Quality decrease in the multi-shot setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LCT (Guo et al., 2025), a long-context video generator that expands a 3B MMDiT's attention window to multi-shot scenes using dense self-attention with interleaved 3D RoPE. The key departure is replacing that dense attention with a learned, parameter-free sparse routing mechanism (Mixture of Contexts) that treats long-video generation as an internal retrieval problem, where each query dynamically selects a few content-aligned chunks via top-k mean-pooled key matching. This contrasts with prior long-video approaches that either compress history into fixed-size latents (FramePack, TTTVideo), impose static sparse patterns (Radial Attention, STA), or rely on external memory banks with heuristic retrieval (WORLDMEM, Context-as-Memory). The paper also draws on the Mixture-of-Experts literature for its per-head distributed routing and context drop-in/drop-out regularization, and on Flash-Attention's variable-length kernel for efficient heterogeneous chunk processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoC demonstrates that minute-scale multi-shot video generation can be achieved at near short-video computational cost (7× FLOPs reduction, 2.2× speedup) without degrading—and in some metrics improving—perceptual quality, which is a significant practical unlock for AIGC video pipelines. The zero-shot result (applying the sparse kernel to a pretrained dense model with >75% sparsity and retaining reasonable coherence) suggests the mean-pooled descriptor is a robust, architecture-agnostic retrieval signal, potentially enabling efficient inference for existing video models without retraining. Key open questions include whether the approach scales beyond the ~180k-token / 64-second regime tested here, whether hardware-software co-design (block-sparse kernels, fused routing+attention operators) can close the remaining gap between FLOPs savings and wall-clock speedup, and whether the learned routing generalizes across architectures and resolutions without retraining.
