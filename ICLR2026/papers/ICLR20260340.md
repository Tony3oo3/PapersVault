## Problem

Existing long-context reasoning benchmarks for LLMs confound intrinsic task complexity, distractor interference, and sequence length, making it impossible to attribute performance failures to specific cognitive load dimensions. No prior benchmark operationalizes Cognitive Load Theory's three load types (intrinsic, extraneous, germane) with independent, factorial control over each dimension, leaving the precise cause of degradation under long contexts ambiguous.

## Input / Output

Each puzzle instance consists of an initial state description (people with d attribute categories), N sequential natural-language update statements (a controlled mix of PoI-relevant 'needles' and distractor 'hays'), and a final query asking for one attribute value of a randomly selected person of interest. The model produces a free-form reasoning trace that must conclude with a single sentence naming the person and the queried attribute; evaluation applies a three-tier graduated exact-match (valid PoI sentence → PoI sentence → last sentence) with synonym tolerance and alternative-value exclusion, all within a 32K token context budget. Puzzles are generated entirely via deterministic string templates without LLM involvement, ensuring full reproducibility and contamination resistance.

## Method

CogniLoad generates puzzles through a deterministic state-tracking algorithm that maintains a full attribute matrix S_t(p,c) across N sequential steps, parameterized by three independently tunable variables: intrinsic difficulty d (controls number of people, attribute categories, value-domain cardinality, and per-statement condition/update counts), task length N (total sequential statements), and needle-to-hay ratio ρ (fraction of statements targeting the PoI). Validation constraints at each generation step ensure hay statements never affect the PoI and that state diversity is preserved, preventing degenerate or trivially solvable puzzles. For quantitative analysis, a per-model binomial GLM with logit link models accuracy as a function of d, log₁₀(N), ρ, and ρ², from which interpretable 50%-accuracy capacity thresholds (ECL₅₀, NT₅₀, ID₅₀) are derived by solving the logit equation for each parameter. Pairwise and three-way interaction terms are additionally fitted to probe whether load dimensions compound super-additively, revealing that difficulty and length interact negatively in 17/22 models while frontier models show near-separable responses.

## Results

Across 22 SotA reasoning LLMs evaluated on 14,000 CogniLoad puzzles each, task length (N) emerges as the dominant performance constraint: at N=250, only gpt-5 (76%) and o3 (68%) maintain above 50% accuracy, while most models drop to 20–30%. Intrinsic difficulty (d) causes monotonic decline, with 12 of 22 models falling below 50% by d=5, though frontier models remain robust (gpt-5: 82%, o3: 80% at d=10). Distractor density (ρ) produces a characteristic U-shaped response with worst performance around ρ∈[25,50]%, and the regression-derived capacity thresholds (ECL₅₀, NT₅₀, ID₅₀) reveal a clear three-tier stratification: frontier models (ECL₅₀>300), mid-capacity models (ECL₅₀≈30–100), and low-capacity models that never reach 50% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogniLoad evaluates 22 models rather than proposing a single method against baselines, so the key comparison is the stratification of model capabilities revealed by the benchmark. Frontier models gpt-5 and o3 dominate across all three load dimensions: gpt-5 achieves 100% accuracy at (d=1, N=20) and retains 76% at N=250, while o3 reaches 96% at d=1 and 68% at N=250, with ECL₅₀ values of 382.8 and 356.9 respectively—far exceeding all other models. DeepSeek-R1-0528 (ECL₅₀=104.6, ID₅₀=7.51) and its distilled variants form a mid-tier: DS-Llama-70B reaches 89% at N=20 but drops to 27% at N=250, while DS-Qwen-7B and DS-Qwen-1.5B fail to sustain 50% accuracy beyond moderate loads. A notable finding is that gemini-2.5-pro, despite a high baseline (β₀=22.5), suffers the steepest length penalty (β_N=−9.15) due to context-budget overflows (268/350 errors at N=250), making its reported accuracy a lower bound. The U-shaped ρ response further differentiates models: gpt-5 and o3 show near-ρ-invariance (no NT₅₀ threshold), while mid-tier models like DS-Llama-70B (NT₅₀=0.53) and Qwen3-32B (NT₅₀=0.94) require substantially higher signal-to-noise ratios to maintain 50% accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogniLoad carves new ground by grounding LLM reasoning evaluation in Cognitive Load Theory (CLT), a framework from cognitive science that has not previously been applied to LLM benchmarking. It departs from long-context benchmarks (LongBench, BABILong, RULER) that vary sequence length without controlling intrinsic difficulty or distractor density, from logic-reasoning suites (LogicBench, ZebraLogic, BBH) that probe complexity within minimal context, and from needle-in-a-haystack designs that isolate distractors but involve trivial per-step reasoning. The key conceptual move is treating the three CLT load types—intrinsic, extraneous, and germane—as independently tunable axes (d, ρ, N) in a single synthetic puzzle generator, enabling factorial disentanglement that no prior benchmark provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By isolating the three load dimensions, CogniLoad shifts the question from 'does the model fail on long contexts?' to 'which specific cognitive load dimension is the binding constraint for this model?', yielding actionable diagnostic fingerprints (ECL50, NT50, ID50) for model developers. The U-shaped distractor response and the super-additive d×N interaction reveal that failure modes compound in non-obvious ways, suggesting targeted interventions (e.g., reducing distractor density helps most when tasks are simultaneously long and complex). Limitations include restriction to sequential deductive reasoning without domain knowledge, reliance on exact-match accuracy, and the acknowledged gap between CLT's biological working-memory framing and LLM computational constraints; extending to inductive/abductive reasoning, richer step-wise metrics, and bridging the CLT analogy with mechanistic interpretability remain open.
