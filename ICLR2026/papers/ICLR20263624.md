## Problem

Most Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) rely on channel-independent modeling and neglect inter-channel correlations, or only capture a single aspect of correlation (e.g., TTM's static MLP mixing, UniTS/Moirai's uniform attention). No existing lightweight plugin is designed specifically for downstream fine-tuning of TSFMs that simultaneously models dynamic, heterogeneous (positive/negative), and partial correlations without requiring re-pre-training or incurring O(N²) inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263624/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CoRA operates as a plug-and-play adapter during fine-tuning, consuming three signals from the backbone TSFM: the raw multivariate input X_t ∈ R^{N×L}, the initial prediction Ŷ_t from the TSFM, and the intermediate latent representation X̃_t ∈ R^{P×N×d} (P patches, d hidden dim). It produces an enhanced forecast by gating the original prediction with a new prediction derived from the adapter's fused representations. At inference, only the O(N) channel-aware projection layers remain active, so the adapter adds negligible latency even for high-channel-count datasets (e.g., Electricity with N=321).

## Method

CoRA decomposes the learnable correlation matrix into a low-rank product Q_t V Q_t^T (Theorem 1 proves functional equivalence to additive decomposition), where Q_t ∈ R^{N×M} is a Time-Varying component estimated via learnable K-order time-aware polynomials with a shared basis q, and V ∈ R^{M×M} is a Time-Invariant component from global learnable vectors; a Pearson correlation matrix R is added as a rule-based prior. A channel-aware projection module (inspired by Squeeze-and-Excitation) maps backbone representations into separate positive and negative latent spaces, with adaptive per-channel weights computed from cross-channel context. Within each space, a Heterogeneous-Partial Contrastive Loss uses the DCE correlation matrix to define positive/negative channel pairs, pulling strongly correlated channels together and pushing weakly correlated ones apart, thereby learning partial correlations without extra inference cost. The final prediction is a per-channel gated convex combination of the TSFM's original output and the adapter's fused prediction, with the gate β ∈ [0,1]^N learned end-to-end.

## Results

In the 5% few-shot fine-tuning setting, CoRA consistently reduces MSE across all 10 datasets and all 6 TSFM backbones (GPT4TS, CALF, UniTime, Moment, Timer, TTM). Improvements range from ~1% (e.g., GPT4TS on ETTh2: 0.374→0.369) to ~10% (e.g., TTM on Traffic: 0.485→0.436; TTM on Solar: 0.219→0.197). The largest absolute gains appear on high-channel-count datasets (Electricity N=321, Traffic N=862) and on backbones that originally use channel-independent modeling. CoRA also outperforms existing correlation plugins LIFT and C-LoRA in the same few-shot setting, where those plugins degrade due to limited training samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoRA is evaluated as a plug-and-play adapter on six TSFM backbones: three LLM-based (GPT4TS, CALF, UniTime) and three pre-trained (Moment, Timer, TTM). CoRA improves every backbone on every dataset, with the most substantial relative gains on TTM (e.g., Traffic MSE drops from 0.485 to 0.436, a 10.1% reduction; Solar from 0.219 to 0.197, a 10.0% reduction) and on channel-independent models like Timer (ETTm1: 0.443→0.426). Against the two existing correlation plugins, LIFT (Zhao & Shen, ICLR 2024) and C-LoRA (Nie et al., CIKM 2024), CoRA clearly outperforms both in the 5% few-shot setting (Figure 4), where LIFT and C-LoRA degrade because they were not designed for TSFM fine-tuning and struggle with limited samples. Notably, TTM's channel-independent variant fine-tuned with CoRA outperforms TTM's native channel-dependent variant fine-tuned without CoRA, demonstrating that explicit multi-type correlation modeling (DCorr + HCorr + PCorr) is more effective than TTM's fixed MLP-based channel mixing.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoRA sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) like GPT4TS, Moment, Timer, and TTM, which predominantly adopt channel-independent architectures and neglect inter-channel dependencies; and (2) multivariate correlation modeling methods (TSMixer, iTransformer, Crossformer, EnhanceNet) that capture channel interactions but are either end-to-end models or plugins not designed for TSFM fine-tuning. The paper departs from prior correlation plugins (LIFT, CCM, C-LoRA) by being specifically architected as a lightweight adapter for downstream TSFM fine-tuning, and from prior dynamic correlation methods (EnhanceNet, Ada-MsHyper) by decomposing the correlation matrix into low-rank Time-Varying and Time-Invariant components with learnable polynomials rather than full-rank additive decomposition. The channel-aware projection draws on Squeeze-and-Excitation, while the contrastive learning for partial correlation extends CCM's clustering idea into a dual positive/negative space without requiring separate pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As TSFMs become the default paradigm for time series forecasting, the observation that they systematically underperform on multivariate tasks due to ignored channel correlations is practically significant; CoRA offers a drop-in solution that requires only a few fine-tuning samples and adds O(N) inference overhead. However, the improvements are modest (typically 1–5% MSE reduction), the method is validated only on 10 forecasting benchmarks, and it does not address whether the same adapter design transfers to other TSFM tasks (classification, anomaly detection, imputation). An open question is whether the polynomial-based dynamic correlation estimation generalizes to highly non-stationary or irregularly sampled series, and whether the contrastive learning component remains effective as the number of channels grows beyond the 862 (Traffic) tested here.
