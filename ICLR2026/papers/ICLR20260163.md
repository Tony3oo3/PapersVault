## Problem

Existing OOD generalization methods for bioactivity prediction (invariant learning, data augmentation, graph-based approaches) all require full access to source data, making them infeasible in real-world settings where source data is restricted by confidentiality, privacy, or intellectual property constraints. This paper introduces the first source-data-absent test-time adaptation setting for bioactivity prediction, where only a pretrained source model and unlabeled target samples are available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260163/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pocket-ligand complex graph as input, where nodes encode atomic and chemical features with 3D coordinates, and edges represent both covalent and non-covalent bonds. At test time, only unlabeled target samples are accessible—no source data, intermediate features, or labels are available. The output is a continuous bioactivity value (IC50, EC50, Ki, or Kd). For datasets like DrugOOD that provide only SMILES and protein sequences, molecular docking via Vina-GPU with FPocket-defined binding sites is required to generate the 3D complex structures.

## Method

TAB adapts a pretrained GNN backbone (DTIGN) at test time using a self-supervised objective combining two complementary components. First, uncertainty-weighted consistency learning: each sample is augmented by randomly masking atom and edge features (which predominantly removes non-binding-site content, naturally directing attention to the binding region), and the model minimizes the cosine distance between original and augmented representations, weighted by MC-dropout variance so that high-confidence samples dominate the gradient signal. Second, a MoCo-style contrastive objective with a momentum encoder and FIFO memory queue pulls together two augmented views of the same complex while pushing apart views from different complexes, enhancing discriminability and preventing the feature collapse that consistency alone can cause. The two losses are combined with equal weights (α=β=1), and the online encoder is updated via gradient descent on the target data while the momentum encoder follows an EMA update, requiring no source data or labels throughout adaptation.

## Results

On the DTIGN benchmark (8 protein targets, scaffold OOD), TAB achieves an average RMSE of 1.157, Pearson's R of 0.448, and Kendall's Tau of 0.312, improving R by 8.2% and Tau by 5.8% over the best baseline (ERM: R=0.414, Tau=0.295). On SIU 0.6 (protein OOD), TAB attains the highest Pearson's R (0.393 for Kd, 0.141 for Ki) and Spearman's rho (0.419 for Kd, 0.175 for Ki) across all methods. On DrugOOD (assay and protein OOD), TAB achieves the best correlation metrics: R=0.388 and Tau=0.230 on assay, and R=0.144 and Tau=0.073 on protein, while also delivering the lowest RMSE (1.319) on the protein subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAB outperforms all nine baselines on the DTIGN average across all three metrics, with the strongest competitor being ERM (RMSE 1.209, R 0.414, τ 0.295) and SR-GNN (RMSE 1.251, R 0.363, τ 0.268). On SIU 0.6, TAB dominates both Kd and Ki tasks; the nearest competitor is SR-GNN on Kd (R 0.384 vs. TAB's 0.393) and ERM on Ki (R 0.123 vs. TAB's 0.141), while IRM and GroupDro are inapplicable due to the absence of domain partitioning. On DrugOOD assay, TAB's RMSE (1.552) is slightly higher than ERM's (1.506), but TAB substantially surpasses all methods on correlation metrics (R 0.388 vs. next-best CIA-LRA 0.265; τ 0.230 vs. next-best CIA-LRA 0.170). On DrugOOD protein, TAB achieves the best RMSE (1.319 vs. DANN's 1.327) and the highest R (0.144 vs. EERM's 0.110). Notably, invariant-learning methods (IRM, GroupDro, CIA-LRA) and graph-OOD methods (EERM, SR-GNN) that require source data access consistently underperform the simple ERM baseline on DTIGN, whereas TAB—operating without any source data—achieves the best results across all three benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAB sits at the intersection of test-time adaptation (TTA) from computer vision (TTT, SHOT, MEMO) and out-of-domain generalization for bioactivity prediction (IRM, GroupDro, EERM, SR-GNN). The key departure is that all prior OOD methods for protein-ligand affinity prediction require full access to source data—either to construct training environments, generate augmentations, or analyze graph structures—whereas TAB operates in a source-data-absent setting where only a pretrained model is available. The method repurposes MoCo-style contrastive learning (momentum encoder, memory queue) and consistency-based self-supervision, but grounds both in domain-specific insights: masking atom/edge features to suppress privileged substructure bias and using MC dropout uncertainty to weight consistency toward confident predictions. The backbone is DTIGN, a geometric interaction GNN, positioning TAB as a plug-in adaptation layer rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The source-data-absent framing addresses a genuine practical constraint in pharmaceutical settings where training data is proprietary or confidential, making prior OOD methods inapplicable. A key open question is whether the approach can extend to sequence-only inputs without requiring 3D pocket-ligand structures (currently necessitating docking via Vina-GPU when experimental structures are unavailable). The ablation finding that either consistency or contrastive learning alone can underperform the ERM baseline highlights a non-trivial complementarity that may inform future self-supervised adaptation designs beyond bioactivity. Broader applicability to other molecular property prediction tasks under data-restricted settings remains to be explored.
