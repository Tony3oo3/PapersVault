## Problem

Existing speech-to-speech models apply a uniform autoregressive objective to both text and audio tokens, overlooking that text follows strong target-target causal dependencies while audio is primarily driven by source-target dependencies on the conditioning text. This mismatch imposes unnecessary sequential constraints on audio generation and amplifies error propagation through the AR chain. The paper asks whether a unified model combining AR text generation with NAR audio diffusion can resolve this fundamental asymmetry within a single Transformer.

## Input / Output

The model accepts spoken audio (or text instructions paired with audio) and produces interleaved sequences of text tokens and discrete audio codes, which a separate neural codec decoder converts to continuous waveforms. Audio is discretized via the GLM-4-Voice tokenizer into a unified vocabulary shared with text tokens and special control symbols (SOA, EOA, EOS, [M]). At inference, the model dynamically alternates between AR text decoding and NAR block-wise audio diffusion based on control tokens, enabling streaming generation with low first-span latency and content-aware variable-length audio spans.

## Method

TtT integrates AR text generation with NAR audio diffusion in a single Transformer initialized from Qwen2.5, using a modality-aware attention mechanism that enforces strict causal attention for text spans while allowing bidirectional attention within each audio span. Text is trained with standard causal cross-entropy, while audio is modeled via absorbing discrete diffusion whose denoising objective is proven equivalent to an any-order AR loss; a partial-order factorization over token positions yields a joint distribution where the combined objective L_AR + L_AO is shown to upper-bound the negative log-likelihood of the target joint distribution. Three training strategies—BANOM (stochastically skipping diffusion noise so text sees clean audio), PPM (preserving all preceding audio spans unmasked), and SST (randomly truncating the final audio span to break positional bias in EOA prediction)—bridge the gap between partially-masked training and clean-conditioned inference. At inference, block-wise diffusion synthesizes each audio span in parallel through iterative denoising, while AR decoding handles text, with the model switching modes via control tokens until EOS is generated.

## Results

Pretrain+TtT (3B) achieves WER of 5.78 on AISHELL-1 and 6.80 on AISHELL-2, substantially outperforming the pure AR baseline Qwen2.5-3B (72.01 and 54.94) and the pure NAR baseline (160.58 and 212.27). On Audio-QA, Pretrain+TtT scores 26.73 on AlpacaEval and 40.07 on LLaMAQuestions, compared to 14.42 and 10.00 for the AR baseline. On URO-Bench, Pretrain+TtT achieves the best scores among all efficient (≤3B) models across both basic and pro difficulty levels, with 57.63 on basic understanding and 46.10 on pro oral conversation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among efficient (≤3B) models, Pretrain+TtT dominates: it outperforms Mini-Omni (0.5B) by large margins on all Audio-QA and ASR metrics (e.g., WER 5.78 vs. 442.06 on AISHELL-1), and surpasses SLAM-Omni (0.5B) on Audio-QA (26.73 vs. 17.47 on AlpacaEval) and ASR, though SLAM-Omni reports higher AAC scores (54.52 vs. 11.55 on Clotho) using a separate 7B Vicuna model. Against 7B+ models, Pretrain+TtT beats SpeechGPT on all six ASR benchmarks (e.g., WER 5.78 vs. 111.81 on AISHELL-1) and on AlpacaEval (26.73 vs. 10.00), and surpasses Moshi on WebQuestions (21.43 vs. 16.85) and AlpacaEval (26.73 vs. 25.63). However, Pretrain+TtT trails Kimi-Audio (7B) on TriviaQA (11.07 vs. 43.51), WebQuestions (21.43 vs. 43.20), and AAC (11.55 vs. 55.92 on Clotho), and falls behind VITA-Audio (7B) and GLM-4-Voice (9B) on most Audio-QA metrics (e.g., LLaMAQuestions: 40.07 vs. 54.30 and 62.67). On URO-Bench, Pretrain+TtT achieves the best scores among all ≤3B models and outperforms Moshi and SpeechGPT across all task categories, while remaining competitive with VITA-Audio and LLaMA-Omni on pro-level oral conversation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the theoretical equivalence between absorbing discrete diffusion and any-order autoregressive (AO-ARM) modeling established by Ou et al. (2024), which itself extends the D3PM framework of Austin et al. (2021). It departs from the prevailing S2S paradigm (Moshi, GLM-4-Voice, VITA-Audio, Mini-Omni) that applies a uniform autoregressive objective to both text and audio tokens within a single Transformer. The key conceptual departure is identifying and formalizing the asymmetry in dependency structures—text follows target-target causal dependencies while audio follows source-target dependencies—and using this to justify a hybrid AR-for-text / NAR-diffusion-for-audio architecture with modality-aware attention, rather than treating both modalities identically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid AR-NAR framework offers a principled way to combine the strengths of sequential text generation with parallel audio synthesis in a single model, with a clean theoretical guarantee (upper bound on joint NLL) that the tractable training objective aligns with the desired distribution. The three training strategies (BANOM, PPM, SST) address practical train-test discrepancies that are likely to matter for any future hybrid generation system. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 3B parameters, whether the specific audio tokenizer/decoder (inherited from GLM-4-Voice) is a bottleneck, and whether the modality-aware attention design generalizes to additional modalities beyond text and audio. The paper's reliance on an external LLM-as-judge for evaluation and the substitution of the original URO-Bench judge model also leave some evaluation questions open.
