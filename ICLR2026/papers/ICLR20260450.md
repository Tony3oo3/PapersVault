## Problem

Current fMRI foundation models rely on reconstruction-centric objectives (masked signal prediction) applied to small brain regions, producing representations that are sensitive to BOLD noise and temporal fluctuations and require extensive task-specific fine-tuning. The paper argues this low-level focus is misaligned with predicting stable, high-level phenotypes, and that a shift from signal reconstruction to semantic abstraction is needed for a truly transferable brain dynamics foundation model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260450/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes parcellated fMRI BOLD time series (457 ROIs from Schaefer-400, Tian-III, and Buckner-7 atlases) that are bandpass-filtered (0.01–0.1 Hz), z-scored per ROI per scan, and downsampled to 2 s resolution; two temporal views of length 100 timepoints are sampled per recording with light corruption augmentations. The output is a compact 9×5×768 token tensor (networks × temporal patches × embedding dim) plus a [CLS] token, from which a frozen linear probe or fine-tuned head predicts downstream labels (age, sex, clinical diagnosis, cognitive scores).

## Method

Brain-Semantoks introduces a semantic tokenizer that aggregates ROIs within nine functional networks (Yeo 7 + subcortical + cerebellar) into robust tokens via a dual-branch multiscale convolutional filter bank (standard short-kernel convolutions plus structured depthwise-separable convolutions with decaying multi-scale kernels) over temporal patches of length 20, yielding a short, semantically meaningful sequence for the transformer. Pretraining uses a student-teacher self-distillation framework with slice masking (entire network rows or contiguous temporal columns are masked in the student's input) and three hierarchical losses: a bidirectional [CLS] cross-view consistency loss, a within-view masked-token distillation loss, and a Teacher-guided Temporal Regularizer (TTR) that constrains the student to match the teacher's time-averaged network embeddings during the first 5% of training before decaying to zero. A coding-rate regularizer (SimDINO-style) prevents representational collapse, and the EMA-updated teacher encoder provides the transferable representations for downstream linear probing.

## Results

Under a rigorous linear probing protocol (frozen pretrained weights, single linear layer), Brain-Semantoks achieves the highest balanced accuracy on 8 of 9 downstream tasks, with particularly large gains on out-of-distribution clinical datasets: 65.13% on ABIDE (ASD), 69.26% on SRPBS-SZ (schizophrenia), and 62.60% on SRPBS-MDD, compared to 53.84%/55.72%/57.61% for BrainLM and 52.92%/57.63%/52.72% for Brain-JEPA. The only task where Brain-Semantoks does not lead is HBN Age (39.16% vs. BrainLM's 43.89%). With full fine-tuning, Brain-Semantoks further surpasses all supervised baselines (FC, BNT, BolT, BrainMass) on 8 of 12 tasks, including a 52.95% average across six external datasets versus 50.68% for the best supervised baseline (FC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two prior fMRI foundation models, Brain-Semantoks substantially outperforms both BrainLM (Caro et al., 2023) and Brain-JEPA (Dong et al., 2024) under linear probing, winning 8 of 9 tasks with the largest margins on clinical OOD tasks (e.g., +13.5 pp on SRPBS-SZ over BrainLM, +11.5 pp on ABIDE over Brain-JEPA). Against supervised baselines, Brain-Semantoks with only a linear probe surpasses functional connectivity + SVM (FC), Brain Network Transformer (BNT, Kan et al., 2022), BolT (Bedel et al., 2023), and BrainMass (Yang et al., 2024) on 8 of 12 tasks; the exceptions are HBN CELF and HBN WISC where FC and BolT tie or narrowly lead. With full fine-tuning, Brain-Semantoks extends its lead, achieving 52.95% average accuracy across six external datasets versus 50.68% (FC), 50.11% (BolT), 48.43% (BrainMass), 47.48% (BrainLM), and 47.08% (Brain-JEPA). Notably, the paper's standardized evaluation protocol (8-crop averaging + batch normalization) significantly boosts BrainLM's linear probing scores relative to previously reported numbers, yet Brain-Semantoks still leads by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the reconstruction-centric paradigm that dominates current fMRI foundation models (BrainLM's masked signal prediction, Brain-JEPA's latent-space prediction) by reframing the pretraining objective as learning abstract, temporally stable representations via self-distillation. It builds directly on the DINO/BYOL student-teacher architecture and iBOT's masked-token distillation, adapting them to the low signal-to-noise regime of BOLD time series. The key architectural novelty is a neuroscience-grounded semantic tokenizer that aggregates ROIs into functional-network tokens before the transformer, and a Teacher-guided Temporal Regularizer (TTR) curriculum that stabilizes training on noisy data—both absent from prior fMRI or vision self-distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that abstract semantic representations outperform signal reconstruction for downstream phenotype prediction—and that this advantage scales reliably with unlabeled data without domain adaptation—the paper provides a concrete recipe for building transferable fMRI foundation models. The first detailed scaling analysis for fMRI FMs and the built-in interpretability via slice-masking (revealing, e.g., cerebellar over default-mode importance for MDD) open new avenues for both methodological and neuroscientific inquiry. Open questions include whether functional-network groupings can be learned from data rather than fixed a priori, how to integrate task-based fMRI more deeply, and which specific distribution shifts (scanner, TR, cohort) most degrade transfer.
