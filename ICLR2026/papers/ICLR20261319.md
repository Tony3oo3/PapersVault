## Problem

Existing multi-scale methods for irregular multivariate time series (IMTS) rely on resampling to construct coarse-grained series, which disrupts the original sampling pattern information (e.g., dense-to-sparse monitoring in clinical data) that encodes valuable temporal and variable dependencies. Capturing multi-scale dependencies while preserving these irregular sampling patterns remains an open challenge, as most multi-scale approaches either assume regular sampling or alter timestamps during scale construction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261319/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an IMTS sample S (a set of observation tuples with timestamps, values, and variable indicators) zero-padded to a fixed length L¹×V with a corresponding binary mask, along with forecast queries Q specifying target timestamps and variables in the forecast window. It outputs predicted values Z for the query positions. A key constraint is that splitting is performed by real-world time periods (e.g., 24h, 12h, 6h) rather than by observation count, so subsamples at different scales may contain varying numbers of actual observations while maintaining consistent temporal coverage.

## Method

ReIMTS recursively partitions an IMTS sample top-down into subsamples with progressively shorter time periods (e.g., 48h → 24h → 12h), preserving all original timestamps and zero-padding structure at every level. At each scale, a pluggable IMTS backbone encoder (e.g., GraFITi, mTAN, GRU-D) learns latent representations capturing dependencies within that time window. An irregularity-aware representation fusion (IARF) mechanism then adaptively combines global representations from the upper scale with local representations at the lower scale: a lightweight feed-forward scoring layer produces weights α, and the binary mask is applied to temporal/observation representations to distinguish real observations from padding before fusion. The decoder at the finest scale concatenates fused representations across all levels and predicts forecast values via MSE loss computed only on query positions. The architecture is plug-and-play, requiring no modification to the backbone's internal structure, and supports three representation types (temporal, variable, observation) with appropriate splitting or duplication to align shapes across scales.

## Results

ReIMTS (with GraFITi backbone) achieves the best MSE across all five IMTS datasets: 4.07 on MIMIC-III, 1.79 on MIMIC-IV, 2.83 on PhysioNet'12, 0.42 on Human Activity, and 1.66 on USHCN (all ×10⁻¹). When applied as a plug-and-play module, ReIMTS delivers an average 27.1% MSE reduction across six existing IMTS backbones, with the largest gain being 62.3% for PrimeNet. ReIMTS also runs the fastest and uses the least GPU memory among multi-scale IMTS methods (Warpformer, HD-TTS, Hi-Patch) while achieving the lowest MSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReIMTS outperforms all 26 baselines on at least four of five datasets, with the most notable margin on MIMIC-IV where it achieves 1.79 MSE versus 2.36 for HD-TTS and 2.42 for Warpformer (a ~24–25% reduction). On MIMIC-III, ReIMTS (4.07) edges out Warpformer (4.09) and HD-TTS (4.17), while on Human Activity it achieves the best result (0.42) ahead of tPatchGNN (0.44) and Hi-Patch (0.48). On PhysioNet'12 and USHCN, ReIMTS ties with HD-TTS (2.83 and 1.66 respectively) but still surpasses all other baselines. Among regular-sampling models, the strongest (PatchTST, TimeMixer, Crossformer) are consistently outperformed by ReIMTS on all five datasets, confirming the advantage of irregularity-aware multi-scale modeling. As a plug-and-play module, ReIMTS improves every tested backbone: PrimeNet by 62.3%, GRU-D by 25.8%, mTAN by 24.3%, TimeCHEAT by 12.1%, GraFITi by 6.3%, and Raindrop by 4.5% in average MSE reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReIMTS carves new ground at the intersection of multi-scale time series modeling and irregular multivariate time series (IMTS) forecasting. Existing multi-scale IMTS methods (Warpformer, Hi-Patch, HD-TTS) obtain coarse-grained series via resampling, which the authors argue disrupts informative sampling patterns (e.g., dense-to-sparse clinical monitoring). In contrast, ReIMTS recursively splits samples by time periods while preserving original timestamps, and introduces an irregularity-aware fusion mechanism to combine global-to-local representations. The method is positioned as a plug-and-play wrapper over existing encoder-decoder IMTS backbones (GraFITi, mTAN, GRU-D, Raindrop, PrimeNet, TimeCHEAT), rather than a standalone architecture, distinguishing it from both regular multi-scale transformers and single-scale patch-based IMTS methods like tPatchGNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and consistent 27.1% average improvement across six diverse backbones and five datasets make ReIMTS a practically useful tool for the IMTS forecasting community, particularly in healthcare where sampling density patterns carry clinical meaning. The efficiency results (fastest training and lowest GPU memory among multi-scale IMTS methods) further lower the barrier to adoption. Open questions remain regarding compatibility with ODE-based models (needing theoretical justification) and diffusion-based models (which predict noisy latent representations), and the authors note that the method's potential with a wider range of backbones is still unexplored.
