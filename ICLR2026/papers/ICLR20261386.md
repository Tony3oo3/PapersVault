## Problem

Exact posterior sampling with score-based generative models is provably intractable in KL divergence under standard cryptographic hardness assumptions (Gupta et al., 2024), yet practical algorithms for tasks like super-resolution and inpainting work well empirically. No prior work provides polynomial-time guarantees for any notion of approximate posterior sampling. This paper asks: under what relaxed notion of 'close to the posterior' can we achieve tractable sampling, and what algorithm achieves it?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained score network for the unconditional prior p(x) (providing access to ∇log p_t(x) at various noise levels t), a measurement y, and a known measurement operator A defining the log-likelihood R_y(x) = ‖A(x) − y‖². It is entirely training-free at inference time: no additional network training or conditioning is needed. The output is a single sample x whose distribution simultaneously satisfies a KL bound to the posterior of a noised prior μ_τ (giving global correctness on mode weights) and a Fisher Divergence bound to the true posterior μ_0 (giving local correctness within each mode).

## Method

The algorithm, Annealed Langevin Monte Carlo (ALMC), has two phases. First, a Warm Start runs standard LMC toward μ_∞ = γe^{−R}/Z (the posterior when the prior is fully noised to a Gaussian), which is tractable because the target is strongly log-concave. Second, an Annealing phase tracks the path t ↦ μ_t ∝ p_t e^{−R} from a high-noise time T_ws down to 0, where p_t is the OU-smoothed prior, using a slowed traversal rate 1/κ so the iterates can follow the moving target. The key technical contribution is bounding the action (Wasserstein velocity) of this path via bounds on ∂_t log μ_t, which are polynomial for t > 0 but blow up as t → 0. This yields a KL guarantee up to τ = κ^{−3/16} (Theorem 4.4) and a Fisher Divergence guarantee against the true posterior μ_0 for the final iterate (Theorem 4.5), with only sub-Gaussianity, Lipschitz score, and smooth convex likelihood assumed.

## Results

The paper establishes the first polynomial-time guarantees for approximate posterior sampling. The main [KL + FI] result shows that an early-stopped Annealed Langevin Monte Carlo (ALMC) algorithm with runtime Õ(κ^{5/4}) produces an iterate that simultaneously satisfies KL(μ_τ ‖ ρ_{τκ^{5/4}}) ≤ O(dκ^{-1/2}) (implying TV ≤ O(√(dκ^{-1/2}))) against the posterior of a noised prior, and FI(ρ_{τκ^{5/4}}, μ_0) ≤ O(dκ^{-1/16}) against the true posterior. The KL guarantee provides a 'global' density-level correctness (e.g., correct mode weights in multimodal settings), while the FI guarantee provides 'local' correctness within each mode. These results hold under minimal assumptions: sub-Gaussian prior with Lipschitz score, and smooth convex log-likelihood.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or comparison tables. The paper positions its contribution against prior hardness results: Gupta et al. (2024) showed posterior sampling is intractable in KL under cryptographic one-way function assumptions, and Bruna & Han (2024) reduced it to an ill-conditioned Ising model (hard unless NP=RP). The ALMC framework bypasses these lower bounds by settling for a weaker but tractable notion of approximation: KL closeness to a noised posterior plus FI closeness to the true posterior. The paper also contrasts with empirical methods (Chung et al. 2022, Rout et al. 2023) that are provably biased, and with Split-Gibbs variants (Wu et al. 2024) that only achieve average FI guarantees. A key distinction from prior annealed LMC work (Cordero-Encinar et al. 2025, Guo et al. 2024) is that the posterior path {μ_t} exhibits 'discontinuities' absent in the prior path {p_t}, making the KL tracking intractable beyond a certain noise level τ = Õ(κ^{-3/16}), which is precisely where the FI guarantee takes over.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Annealed Langevin Monte Carlo framework (Guo et al. 2024; Cordero-Encinar et al. 2025), which was previously applied to sampling from a prior, to the harder problem of posterior sampling where a likelihood term is added. It builds on the Fisher Divergence convergence results of Balasubramanian et al. (2022) for non-log-concave sampling, repurposing FI as a 'local' correctness guarantee that complements a 'global' KL guarantee to a noised posterior. The paper explicitly positions itself against the cryptographic hardness results of Gupta et al. (2024) and the Ising-model reduction of Bruna & Han (2024), arguing that exact posterior sampling in KL is the wrong target and that a combined KL+FI notion of approximation is both achievable in polynomial time and meaningful in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These are the first polynomial-time guarantees for any notion of approximate posterior sampling, resolving a gap that has persisted despite widespread empirical success of diffusion-based inverse problem solvers. The key open question is whether the specific annealed path {μ_t} is optimal or whether other paths (e.g., Split-Gibbs, DDPM-aligned) can yield tighter or more practical guarantees. Limitations include the requirement of Lipschitz scores, sub-Gaussian priors, and smooth convex likelihoods, and the guarantee is to a *noised* posterior rather than the exact one, leaving a gap at very low noise levels. The mode-collapse analysis (Section 4.1) clarifies why FI alone is insufficient for multimodal posteriors, which has implications for understanding failure modes of existing diffusion-based inverse solvers.
