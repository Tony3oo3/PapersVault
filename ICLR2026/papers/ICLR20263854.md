## Problem

Existing hard negative generation methods for cross-modal hashing rely solely on local anchor-negative pair correlations during interpolation, ignoring the global geometric structure of the embedding space. This causes synthesized negatives to intrude into unrelated semantic regions, weakening discriminative boundaries in the Hamming co-space. The paper is the first to introduce global-sense hard negative generation into deep cross-modal hashing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263854/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes image-text pairs as input, with images resized to 224×224 and normalized via CLIP statistics, and texts tokenized to a maximum of 77 tokens using the CLIP tokenizer. A dual CLIP ViT-B/32 Transformer backbone (12 blocks, 8 heads) extracts features from both modalities, which are then passed through hash layers (MLP + tanh) to produce binary hash codes of 16, 32, 64, or 128 bits in a shared Hamming space. During training, the framework additionally synthesizes channel-wise interpolated hard negative embeddings that are fed back into the triplet loss without requiring a separate generator network.

## Method

DGHDGH combines two key modules: Relevance Global Propagation (RGP) and Discriminative Global-sense Synthesis (DGS). RGP constructs three parallel structured graphs (image, text, cross-modal) where nodes store hash embeddings and edges encode pairwise relevance, then applies a graph transformer (2 blocks, 4 heads) with asynchronous alternating updates—nodes use Masked Multi-head Self-Attention attending only to negative samples, while edges use Cross-Attention to integrate neighboring node information—so that after iterative propagation, edges encode global inter-class correlations. DGS converts each anchor-negative edge into a channel-wise interpolation vector via Sigmoid(FC(edge)), then performs difficulty-adaptive interpolation where a dynamic scaling factor τ = e^(−1/l_avg) progressively tightens the interpolation interval as training loss decreases, ensuring generated negatives grow harder over epochs. The overall training alternates between a generation optimization loss (combining interpolation similarity, semantic preservation via a frozen classifier, and coefficient diversity) and a hash learning loss (triplet loss over both real and synthetic negatives), with shared-parameter classification layers maintaining semantic consistency across modalities.

## Results

DGHDGH achieves state-of-the-art mAP@all across all three benchmarks and both retrieval directions. On MIRFLICKR-25K (128 bits), it reaches 87.75% (I2T) and 85.74% (T2I), outperforming the strongest baseline DDBH by 1.25% and 1.41% respectively. On NUS-WIDE (128 bits), it attains 73.76% (I2T) and 74.64% (T2I), and on MS COCO (128 bits) it reaches 79.19% (I2T) and 79.59% (T2I). The method also leads in Fisher ratio and P@H≤2, confirming improved discriminative structure in the learned Hamming co-space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGHDGH outperforms all 17 baselines across every dataset, bit length, and retrieval direction. Among modern deep hashing methods, the closest competitor is DDBH (TCSVT'25), which DGHDGH surpasses by 0.16–1.41% mAP (e.g., 87.75 vs. 86.50 on MIRFLICKR-25K I2T 128-bit; 79.59 vs. 77.07 on MS COCO T2I 128-bit). DHaPH (TKDE'24) and DNpH (TMM'24) trail by 2–8% on MS COCO, where the larger category count (80) amplifies the benefit of global-sense hard negatives. Classical pre-CLIP methods (DCMH, CMHH, AGAH, DADH, SCAHN) show substantially lower performance even after backbone replacement to CLIP ViT-B/32, with gaps of 5–20% mAP, confirming that the gains stem from the learning framework rather than the feature extractor. Against informative-learning strategies (DWS mining, HashGAN, HDML, DAS), DGHDGH consistently leads on MIRFLICKR-25K, demonstrating that global-aware generation produces more discriminative negatives than local interpolation or GAN-based synthesis. Under noisy labels (up to 80% corruption), DGHDGH degrades gracefully (e.g., 84.16% at 128-bit I2T with 80% noise vs. 80.29% for DDBH), attributed to the graph propagation acting as a natural denoising filter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting hard negative generation (HNG) from the metric learning literature into the cross-modal hashing setting, a combination the authors argue has been absent from prior work. Conceptually, it departs from local-interpolation HNG methods (HDML, DAS, Mixup) that operate on single anchor-negative pairs by introducing a graph-based Relevance Global Propagation module that captures inter-class geometry across the entire batch before synthesis. The channel-wise adaptive interpolation with a self-paced difficulty schedule extends the single-coefficient interpolation paradigm, while the overall architecture (dual CLIP-based transformers + graph transformer) is assembled from standard components rather than building on a specific prior hashing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that globally-aware, semantically consistent hard negatives sharpen Hamming-space boundaries without requiring an extra generator network, the paper offers a practical plug-and-play module that can be grafted onto existing cross-modal hashing pipelines (validated on DHaPH and DDBH). The noise-robustness finding—that graph propagation acts as a natural denoising filter and label-free synthetic negatives avoid amplifying corrupted supervision—suggests a broader principle for robust representation learning. Open questions include whether the graph-based global propagation scales to very large batches or more than two modalities, and whether the theoretical distinction from Laplacian smoothing (Appendix B) holds empirically at greater network depth.
