## Problem

Bongard Problems require identifying fine-grained abstract visual concepts from just six examples per side, but existing real-world BP datasets either represent only coarse-grained concepts (Bongard HOI, Bongard-OpenWorld) or are too small for robust evaluation (Bongard-RWR, 60 manually constructed instances). This paper addresses the scalability gap by introducing a generative pipeline that produces thousands of real-world-like images faithfully depicting the fine-grained abstract concepts from classical synthetic BPs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264579/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each BP instance consists of two sides of P=6 generated 512×512 images plus one test image per side, with the underlying concept expressed as a natural-language pair (C_L, C_R). Evaluation spans six formulations: binary side classification (I1S/I2S) on raw images, text-based side classification (D1S/D2S) using InternVL2.5 captions as intermediate representations, multiclass concept selection (CS) from K∈{2,4,8,16} candidate concepts, and free-form concept generation (CG) scored with BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr, and BERTScore. All VLMs are evaluated zero-shot at temperature 0.5 with structured JSON output enforced via Outlines decoding.

## Method

The dataset construction pipeline starts from each Bongard-RWR matrix: Pixtral-12B produces paired positive/negative captions per image aligned to the side's concept, a T2T model augments each positive caption into N=15 diverse descriptions, and Flux.1-dev renders 512×512 candidate images using the positive caption as prompt and the negative caption as a negative prompt. Human reviewers then filter ~30% of generated images that fail to faithfully reflect the intended concept. Matrices are assembled by greedily selecting M=10 subsets per side that minimize maximum pairwise ViT-L/14 cosine similarity, with an image-removal step across iterations to maximize cross-matrix diversity, yielding M²=100 BPs per source (5400 total from 54 source BPs). Evaluation compares four open VLMs (InternVL2.5 78B, Qwen2-VL 72B, LLaVA-Next 110B, MiniCPM-o 2.6 8B), the text-only DeepSeek-R1 70B, and a non-parametric Similarity Classifier baseline that assigns test inputs to the side with lower maximum embedding distance, revealing that VLMs perform at or near chance on image-based side classification while the SC baseline outperforms them.

## Results

On Bongard-RWR+, all four VLMs perform at or near chance on the image-based side-classification tasks (I1S: 0.48–0.50; I2S: 0.39–0.50), while the non-parametric Similarity Classifier (SC) baseline outperforms every VLM on both (0.52 I1S, 0.54 I2S). In the Concept Selection task, InternVL2.5 78B achieves the best accuracy at 91% for K=2 but drops to 57% for K=16, whereas MiniCPM-o 2.6 8B and LLaVA-Next 110B reach only 19% at K=16. Adding an intermediate captioning step (D1S/D2S) yields modest gains (e.g., InternVL2.5: 0.57 D1S vs. 0.50 I1S), and DeepSeek-R1 70B (text-only) matches or exceeds the VLMs in the text-based settings (0.57 D1S, 0.56 D2S). In the free-form Concept Generation task, all models score very low across BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr, and BERTScore, with no clear winner.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InternVL2.5 78B is the strongest VLM overall, leading in CS (91% at K=2, 57% at K=16) and I1S (0.50), but it is outperformed by the simple SC baseline on both image-based tasks (SC: 0.52 I1S, 0.54 I2S vs. IVL2.5: 0.50, 0.39). Qwen2-VL 72B is competitive, achieving the best D1S score (0.58) and matching InternVL2.5 on I1S (0.49), while LLaVA-Next 110B and MiniCPM-o 2.6 8B trail significantly on CS (both ~19% at K=16) despite LLaVA-Next being 14× larger. DeepSeek-R1 70B, a text-only reasoning model, matches or exceeds the VLMs in text-based settings (0.57 D1S, 0.56 D2S), confirming that the bottleneck is visual perception rather than language reasoning. Supervised baselines (WReN, SNAIL, MLP) all perform at chance level (0.47–0.51) on Bongard-RWR+/TVT, indicating the task is challenging even for trained models. Notably, MiniCPM-o 2.6 8B produces higher-quality captions than the much larger LLaVA-Next 110B, yielding better downstream D1S accuracy, suggesting that image-to-text capability does not scale monotonically with parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Bongard-RWR (Małkiński et al., 2025), a manually constructed 60-instance dataset linking synthetic Bongard Problem concepts to real-world images, by replacing manual curation with a semi-automated VLM pipeline (Pixtral-12B for image-to-text, Flux.1-dev for text-to-image) that scales the dataset to 5400 instances while preserving the original abstract concepts. It positions itself at the intersection of two prior BP lines: synthetic-concept datasets (Bongard-LOGO) and real-world-image datasets (Bongard HOI, Bongard-OpenWorld), uniquely combining abstract fine-grained concepts with realistic imagery. The generative pipeline for dataset construction draws on T2I models applied to data augmentation and synthetic data generation, but repurposes them specifically for preserving abstract relational structure across diverse visual instantiations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90× scale-up of Bongard-RWR enables statistically robust evaluation that was previously impossible, and the comprehensive ablations (model size, color, image count, diversity, noise) provide a diagnostic framework for characterizing VLM reasoning limitations. The central finding—that a non-parametric ViT embedding classifier outperforms all 78B–110B VLMs on side-assignment tasks—reveals a fundamental gap in multi-image abstract visual reasoning that is unlikely to close with incremental VLM improvements alone. Open questions include whether fully automated generation (removing the 30% human filtering) preserves dataset quality, whether the pipeline generalizes to the 6 Bongard-RWR concepts that could not be rendered, and whether future multimodal reasoning models (analogous to DeepSeek-R1 in text) can bridge the fine-grained concept identification gap.
