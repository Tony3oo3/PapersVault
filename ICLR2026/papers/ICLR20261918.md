## Problem

Existing vector quantization methods face a fundamental trade-off: data-dependent approaches (e.g., product quantization, Hessian-based tuning) achieve good distortion but require heavy preprocessing and are unsuitable for online/streaming settings like KV cache compression, while data-oblivious methods lack sharp distortion-rate guarantees relative to bit-width. No prior online quantizer achieves near-optimal distortion for both MSE and unbiased inner product estimation across all bit-widths and dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method takes arbitrary d-dimensional Euclidean vectors (no distributional assumptions, worst-case setting) and produces B-bit binary codes where B = b·d for a chosen bit-width b. The MSE variant outputs per-coordinate centroid indices; the inner-product variant additionally stores a d-bit sign vector (from QJL on the residual) and the residual L2 norm as a scalar. Dequantization reconstructs an approximate vector, and the inner-product variant guarantees an unbiased estimator E[⟨y, x̃⟩] = ⟨y, x⟩. In practice, rotation matrices are implemented as randomized Hadamard transforms for O(d log d) complexity, and the quantizer is fully data-oblivious—no calibration or training data is needed.

## Method

TURBOQUANT applies a random rotation to the input vector, which induces a Beta distribution on each coordinate that converges to N(0, 1/d) in high dimensions, with distinct coordinates becoming nearly independent. This near-independence reduces the vector quantization problem to independent per-coordinate scalar quantization, solved optimally via the Lloyd-Max algorithm (continuous 1D k-means) with precomputed codebooks. For MSE optimization, this yields distortion ≤ (√3π/2)·4^{-b}. For unbiased inner product estimation, a two-stage design first applies the MSE quantizer at b−1 bits, then applies a 1-bit Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss (QJL) transform to the residual, giving distortion ≤ (√3π²‖y‖²/d)·4^{-b} with zero bias. The approach is provably within a constant factor (~2.7) of the Shannon information-theoretic lower bound, and is fully online, data-oblivious, and accelerator-friendly via Hadamard transforms and LUT-based SIMD operations.

## Results

On LongBench-V1 with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, TURBOQUANT at 3.5 bits per channel achieves an average score of 50.06, matching the full-precision (16-bit) baseline exactly, while at 2.5 bits it scores 49.74 (vs. 50.06 full precision) — a marginal degradation at over 4.5× compression. On the Needle-in-a-Haystack test, TURBOQUANT achieves the same recall as the uncompressed model at 4× KV cache compression, outperforming KIVI, PolarQuant, SnapKV, and PyramidKV. In nearest neighbor search on DBpedia Entities (1536-d and 3072-d OpenAI3 embeddings) and GloVe (200-d), TURBOQUANT attains the highest recall@k in almost all bit-width settings compared to Product Quantization and RabitQ, while reducing indexing time to near zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KIVI (Liu et al., 2024), TURBOQUANT at 2.5 bits (avg 49.74) substantially outperforms KIVI at 3 bits (avg 48.50) on LongBench-V1, while KIVI at 5 bits (50.16) marginally edges out TURBOQUANT at 3.5 bits (50.06) — meaning TURBOQUANT matches KIVI's quality at roughly half the bit-width. Against PolarQuant (Han et al., 2025), TURBOQUANT at 2.5 bits (49.74) nearly ties PolarQuant at 3.9 bits (49.78) while using ~36% fewer bits. In the Needle-in-a-Haystack test, TURBOQUANT matches full-precision recall at 4× compression, outperforming both token-level methods (SnapKV, PyramidKV) and scalar quantization (KIVI) that degrade significantly at equivalent compression ratios. In nearest neighbor search, TURBOQUANT achieves the highest recall@k across nearly all settings on DBpedia Entities (1536-d, 3072-d) and GloVe (200-d), beating both Product Quantization (FAISS) and RabitQ; the only exceptions are a few GloVe 2-bit configurations where RabitQ slightly leads. Notably, TURBOQUANT requires zero indexing/training time (data-oblivious), whereas Product Quantization requires k-means codebook construction and RabitQ lacks vectorized GPU support, making TURBOQUANT the fastest in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

TURBOQUANT builds on the random-rotation quantization lineage (QuaRot, QuIP, HIGGS, EDEN) and the Lloyd-Max scalar quantization framework, but its core novelty is theoretical: it derives the exact Beta-distribution coordinate structure induced by random rotation and provides the first formal proof of near-optimality for this rotated-coordinate scalar quantization rule. The inner-product variant composes the MSE-optimal quantizer with the QJL 1-bit transform (Zandieh et al., 2024), a departure from prior work that either optimizes MSE or inner products separately. Unlike data-dependent methods (GPTQ, AWQ, product quantization) that require calibration or k-means preprocessing, TURBOQUANT is fully data-oblivious and online, positioning it as a lightweight alternative for streaming settings like KV cache compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a notable gap in vector quantization theory by proving that a simple data-oblivious scheme (random rotation + per-coordinate Lloyd-Max quantization) achieves distortion within a factor of ~2.7 of the Shannon information-theoretic lower bound across all bit-widths, with the gap shrinking to ~1.45 at b=1. Practically, this translates to quality-neutral KV cache compression at 3.5 bits per channel and near-zero indexing time for nearest-neighbor search, addressing two of the most pressing bottlenecks in LLM deployment. Open questions include whether the 2.7× constant can be further tightened, whether the approach extends naturally to other distortion measures (e.g., cosine similarity, L1), and whether the two-tier outlier-aware bit allocation strategy generalizes beyond the specific channel-count heuristics used in experiments.
