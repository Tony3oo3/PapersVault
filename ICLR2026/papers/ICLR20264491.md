## Problem

Test-Time Scaling (TTS) via Best-of-N evaluation has proven effective for LLMs but remains largely unexplored for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Two critical gaps exist: (1) no effective multimodal critic model is available to score reasoning steps, as existing open-source MLLMs tend to assign uniformly positive judgments and fail to distinguish correct from incorrect steps; and (2) no benchmark exists to evaluate the step-level judgment ability of multimodal critic models in a controlled, policy-model-independent setting.

## Input / Output

Each training sample is an image paired with a question and a step-by-step solution (averaging 5.6 steps, 22.6 words per step), formulated as a multi-turn chat where the first turn contains the image, question, and step 0, and each subsequent turn appends one new step. The model outputs a binary correctness token (+ or −) per step; during inference, all step scores are computed in a single forward pass by treating '+' as a placeholder and reading its generation probability, then averaged into a final response score for Best-of-N selection. The dataset contains ~400K samples and 2M steps, with only ~10% incorrect steps, creating a significant class imbalance.

## Method

VisualPRM400K is constructed by collecting image-question pairs from MMPR v1.1, sampling 4 solutions per pair using InternVL2.5 models (capped at 12 steps), and annotating each step's correctness via Monte Carlo sampling: 16 continuations are generated from each step, and the step is labeled correct if the expected accuracy mc_i > 0. VisualPRM (8B parameters) is trained as a value-based PRM in a multi-turn chat format, predicting step correctness at each turn conditioned on the image, question, and all prior steps, with supervision applied to all steps (not just up to the first error). At inference, the step score is the weighted sum of generation probabilities over the discretized output tokens {+, −} with weights {1, 0}, and the response score is the mean of all step scores. The paper also introduces VisualProcessBench (2,866 samples, 26,950 human-annotated step labels from 5 reasoning benchmarks and 5 source MLLMs), which requires models to detect all erroneous steps rather than only the first, and uses macro F1 to mitigate the correct/incorrect class imbalance.

## Results

Under Best-of-8 evaluation, VisualPRM improves the overall reasoning score across seven multimodal benchmarks by +8.0 (MiniCPM-V2.6-8B), +3.7 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), +8.4 (InternVL2.5-8B), +8.9 (InternVL2.5-26B), +6.3 (InternVL2.5-38B), and +5.9 (InternVL2.5-78B) points over their respective pass@1 baselines. On VisualProcessBench, VisualPRM achieves a macro F1 of 62.0, outperforming GPT-4o (60.3) and GPT-4o-Mini (57.9) while matching Gemini-2.0-Flash (62.3), despite using only 8B parameters. In BoN ablations, PRM consistently outperforms both Self-Consistency and Outcome Reward Models, with the gap widening from 2.4 points at N=8 to 3.1 points at N=128.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary models, VisualPRM paired with InternVL2.5-78B reaches 51.9 overall, surpassing GPT-4o (47.9) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (50.5) but trailing Gemini-2.0-Flash (53.4) on the seven reasoning benchmarks. On VisualProcessBench, VisualPRM (62.0 macro F1) outperforms GPT-4o (60.3) and GPT-4o-Mini (57.9) while essentially matching Gemini-2.0-Flash (62.3), all with only 8B parameters. Compared to Self-Consistency as a critic strategy, VisualPRM yields +2.4 points at BoN=8 and +3.1 points at BoN=128 for InternVL2.5-8B, with the gap widening as N increases. Against Outcome Reward Models, VisualPRM achieves +1.5 points at BoN=8 and +4.3 points at BoN=128, and unlike ORM, PRM performance continues to improve monotonically with larger N. As an MLLM-as-judger baseline, InternVL2.5-8B and InternVL2.5-78B provide only marginal BoN gains (+0.4 and +1.4 points respectively) due to their tendency to label most steps as correct, whereas VisualPRM achieves +8.4 and +5.9 points on the same policy models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the text-only process reward model (PRM) lineage—MathShepherd's Monte Carlo data pipeline, PRM800K's human-annotated process supervision, and OmegaPRM's automated step scoring—into the multimodal setting, where no prior dataset or model existed. The data construction adapts MathShepherd's expected-accuracy estimation to image-conditioned reasoning, while the benchmark (VisualProcessBench) generalizes ProcessBench and PRM800K's step-detection evaluation from text to multimodal reasoning. The broader Test-Time Scaling framework (Snell et al., 2024) and the MMPR multimodal preference dataset provide the evaluation protocol and question source, respectively, but the core contribution is the first multimodal instantiation of the PRM paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first large-scale multimodal process supervision dataset (~400K samples) and the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating multimodal step-level judgment, this work fills a critical infrastructure gap that will likely become a standard resource for MLLM reasoning research. The finding that even 78B open-source MLLMs perform near-randomly as step-level critics (F1 ≈ 50 on VisualProcessBench) while a purpose-trained 8B PRM outperforms GPT-4o on the same benchmark underscores that general-purpose MLLMs are not adequate substitutes for dedicated critic models. Open questions include whether the automatic Monte Carlo pipeline's inherent noise (only ~10% incorrect steps) limits PRM quality, whether more sophisticated multimodal PRM architectures (beyond multi-turn chat with binary/ternary outputs) can close the remaining gap to proprietary models, and how process rewards integrate with online RL for MLLMs beyond the preliminary GRPO experiment.
