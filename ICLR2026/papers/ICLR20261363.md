## Problem

Existing feedforward 3D reconstruction methods impose restrictive assumptions—accurate camera poses, known intrinsics, or a fixed small number of views—making them unsuitable for real-world use where any combination of these may be missing. A single model that handles arbitrary view counts, posed or unposed, and calibrated or uncalibrated inputs simultaneously remains an open challenge, compounded by the entanglement between pose estimation and geometry learning and the inherent scale ambiguity of SfM-derived training data.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an arbitrary number (2–32+) of unposed, uncalibrated RGB images at 224×224 or 280×518 resolution, optionally augmented with ground-truth camera poses and/or intrinsics. It outputs a global 3D Gaussian scene (per-pixel Gaussians with opacity-based pruning removing 20–70% of them), per-view camera poses (3D translation + 9D rotation representation converted via SVD), and per-view focal length. Local per-view Gaussians are aggregated into the global frame using either predicted or supplied poses, enabling seamless switching between pose-free and pose-dependent inference.

## Method

YoNoSplat uses a DINOv2 ViT encoder followed by a decoder of alternating per-frame self-attention and global concatenated self-attention blocks (VGGT-style), with separate Gaussian heads (centers and parameters, 2× upsampled) and a camera pose head (MLP → pooling → MLP → 12D vector). The key training innovation is a mix-forcing curriculum: training begins with pure teacher-forcing (ground-truth poses for Gaussian aggregation) to establish geometric stability, then linearly increases the probability of using the model's own predicted poses, balancing stability against exposure bias. Scale ambiguity is resolved by normalizing camera translations by the maximum pairwise camera distance and by an Intrinsic Condition Embedding (ICE) module that predicts focal length from an intrinsic token in the encoder, converts it to camera rays, encodes the result, and adds it to image features for conditioning. The model is initialized from π³ weights and trained with a multi-task loss combining rendering (MSE + LPIPS), intrinsic L2, pairwise relative pose, and opacity L1 regularization.

## Results

On DL3DV with 6 input views in the most challenging pose-free, intrinsic-free setting, YoNoSplat achieves 24.53 PSNR / 0.804 SSIM / 0.142 LPIPS, surpassing the pose-dependent DepthSplat (23.42 PSNR) and the pose-free NoPoSplat (22.77 PSNR). At 24 views, the gap widens: YoNoSplat reaches 22.17 PSNR vs. DepthSplat's 20.09 and NoPoSplat's 17.86. With optional post-optimization, YoNoSplat further improves to 27.53 PSNR (6v) and 25.86 PSNR (24v), substantially outperforming the optimization-based InstantSplat (21.68 / 18.49 PSNR). On pose estimation (DL3DV), the 518×280 model achieves 0.844 AUC@5°, 0.922 AUC@10°, and 0.961 AUC@20°, outperforming π³ (0.795/0.897/0.949) and MASt3R (0.778/0.883/0.941).

## Baselines & Comparisons

YoNoSplat's most striking result is that in the fully pose-free, intrinsic-free setting (no ground-truth poses or intrinsics), it outperforms the pose-dependent DepthSplat by 1.11 dB PSNR at 6 views and 2.09 dB at 24 views on DL3DV, demonstrating that its learned priors compensate for missing camera information. Against the closest pose-free competitor NoPoSplat, YoNoSplat leads by 1.77 dB PSNR at 6 views and by a dramatic 4.31 dB at 24 views, where NoPoSplat's canonical-space prediction degrades severely. AnySplat, the concurrent unconstrained-view method, trails YoNoSplat by 5.50 dB PSNR at 6 views in the no-prior setting. On cross-dataset generalization (trained on DL3DV, tested on ScanNet++), YoNoSplat outperforms AnySplat—which was trained directly on ScanNet++—by 2.83 dB PSNR at 32 views and 2.30 dB at 128 views. For pose estimation, YoNoSplat (518×280) achieves 0.844 AUC@5° on DL3DV, surpassing π³ (0.795), MASt3R (0.778), and VGGT (0.700), and maintains a lead in zero-shot transfer to RealEstate10K (0.78 vs. 0.705 for π³). With optional post-optimization, YoNoSplat extends its lead over the optimization-based InstantSplat to 5.86 dB PSNR at 6 views.

## Relation to Prior Work

YoNoSplat builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (Kerbl et al., 2023) and the local-global attention architecture from VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a), while initializing its backbone and pose head from π³ (Wang et al., 2025c). It departs from prior pose-free methods (NoPoSplat, Flare) that predict Gaussians in a canonical space and are limited to 2–4 views, instead adopting a local-to-global aggregation paradigm that scales to arbitrary view counts. The key conceptual departure is the mix-forcing training strategy, which resolves the pose-geometry entanglement that plagues both self-forcing (unstable) and teacher-forcing (exposure bias) approaches in prior joint prediction settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

YoNoSplat is the first feedforward model to achieve SOTA in both pose-free and pose-dependent settings simultaneously across arbitrary view counts, with a 2.69-second inference time for 100 views — making real-time, unconstrained 3D reconstruction practically viable. The mix-forcing curriculum is a general training technique applicable to any joint prediction problem where one output conditions another. Open questions remain: GPU memory still caps the maximum number of views (motivating incremental reconstruction), drastic illumination changes break photometric consistency, and the significant gains from optional post-optimization suggest the feedforward model has not yet fully converged on pose and geometry.
