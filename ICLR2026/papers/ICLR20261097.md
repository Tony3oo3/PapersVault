## Problem

Existing entropy-constrained reinforcement learning methods either add entropy bonus terms directly to the training objective (distorting the primary optimization landscape) or rely on ad-hoc, domain-specific heuristics (e.g., clip-higher, forking-token filtering) that lack theoretical guarantees. No universally applicable mechanism exists that enforces a minimum policy entropy without modifying the loss function, leaving a gap between principled entropy control and practical non-invasiveness across continuous control, image classification, and LLM alignment.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in activation layer at the final output of any policy network: for continuous control it consumes raw Gaussian mean and pre-activation standard deviation parameters (state-vector input → bounded continuous action); for image classification it consumes pre-softmax logits (RGB image → class probability vector); for LLMs it consumes token logits after sampling but before the policy gradient update (text prompt → text response). A notable design choice in the LLM setting is that the activation is applied post-sampling during the update step, leaving the inference/sampling policy unchanged while still regularizing the learned policy.

## Method

ERA enforces a minimum entropy constraint architecturally by designing a non-linear activation function g(·) applied to the network's final output, completely decoupling entropy regularization from the loss function. For continuous control with bounded Gaussian policies, g(·) redistributes the standard deviation across dimensions via a softmax-weighted allocation that provably guarantees H(π) ≥ H₀ while respecting [σ_min, σ_max] bounds, with a learnable compensation parameter δ̂ for the bounding bias. For discrete classification, g(·) maps logits through an inverse Lambert W function (approximated numerically) to enforce a per-class entropy floor, offering input-dependent regularization that subsumes label smoothing. For LLMs, a specialized adaptive variant conditionally scales logits by a factor k (sharpening when entropy is below ω_low, flattening when above ω_high) only for positively-advantaged tokens, with matching advantage rescaling to balance gradients—effectively acting as an adaptive KL regularizer that maintains entropy within [ω_low, ω_high] without requiring off-policy importance sampling.

## Results

On LLM mathematical reasoning (Qwen2.5-Math-7B, GRPO training), ERA achieves a 51.0% average across six benchmarks (AIME'24, AIME'25, AMC'23, MATH500, Minerva, OlympiadBench), a +11.6% improvement over GRPO (45.7%), with particularly dramatic gains of +4.7% on AIME'24 (36.0 vs. 34.4) and +70.7% on AIME'25 (21.0 vs. 12.3). In continuous control, ERA improves SAC by over 30% on HumanoidBench and accelerates learning across DMC and MuJoCo Gym. For image classification, ERA boosts ResNet-50 ImageNet top-1 accuracy by 0.69% (74.75→75.44%) and CIFAR-10 by 0.21% (93.61→93.82%) without data augmentation, with all gains achieved at <7% computational overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERA outperforms all 13 baselines across all three domains. In LLM reasoning, ERA (51.0% avg) beats the strongest entropy-control baselines KL-Cov (46.6%) and Clip-Cov (44.1%) by 4.4 and 6.9 points respectively, and surpasses the GRPO baseline (45.7%) by 5.3 points; the gap is most pronounced on AIME'25 where ERA scores 21.0% vs. 12.3% for GRPO and 13.1% for KL-Cov. Among classical RL methods, ERA also dominates Oat-Zero (43.1%), PRIME-Zero (40.7%), and SimpleRL-Zero (39.0%). In continuous control, ERA consistently improves SAC, PPO, OBAC, TD-MPC2, and FastSAC across HumanoidBench, DMC, and MuJoCo Gym, with >30% gains over SAC on HumanoidBench and >25% on high-dimensional DMC tasks (Humanoid, Dog). For image classification, ERA adds +0.69% top-1 on ImageNet and +0.21% on CIFAR-10 over the standard ResNet-50 baseline, complementing (rather than replacing) existing label smoothing. ERA also generalizes to GSPO (+6.9% avg on Qwen2.5-Math-7B) and smaller models (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, +14.1% avg over GRPO), confirming it is not tied to a specific algorithm or model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

ERA departs from the dominant maximum entropy RL paradigm (SAC, PPO with entropy bonus) by moving the entropy constraint from the loss function into the network architecture via a specially designed output activation, thereby decoupling entropy enforcement from the primary reward objective. It generalizes earlier projection-based methods (Akrour et al., 2019; Otto et al., 2021), which imposed linear, dimension-uniform constraints, to non-linear, dimension-aware activations with provable entropy bounds. In the LLM setting, ERA contrasts with ad hoc entropy-collapse mitigations (KL-Cov, Clip-Cov, clip-higher, forking-token filtering) by providing a unified, theoretically grounded mechanism that is compatible with on-policy training and requires no importance-sampling ratio or KL penalty term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ERA offers a drop-in, non-invasive module that can be grafted onto existing algorithms (SAC, PPO, GRPO, TD-MPC2, FastSAC) without modifying their loss functions, which is practically attractive for practitioners who want entropy guarantees without re-deriving optimization dynamics. The cross-domain validation (continuous control, image classification, LLM reasoning) with theoretical proofs strengthens the case that output activation is a general-purpose tool for stochasticity control. However, the method is inherently tied to the MaxEnt objective, which the authors acknowledge is not universally beneficial; extending the framework to non-maximum-entropy settings (e.g., diffusion policies, flow-based models) remains an open and important direction.
