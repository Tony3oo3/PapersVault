## Problem

Most multi-objective RL methods assume a fixed or externally specified preference vector, yet in realistic non-stationary settings the relevant trade-offs among objectives shift with context (e.g., urgency, resource scarcity). This paper asks how an agent can infer its current preference weights online from only vector-valued rewards and partial observations, treating them as unobserved latent variables that drift with the environment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264981/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a short history window of H recent states (H=3 by default), which are either 3-channel pixel images (observation, time ratio, energy ratio) in the Maze environment or 6-dimensional symbolic vectors (position, queue length, energy, deadline, recent-cut flag, service rate) in Queue. It produces a discrete or continuous action and, simultaneously, a d-dimensional preference vector on the probability simplex obtained by softmax-ing a latent logit vector sampled from a diagonal Gaussian posterior; at decision time K=8 preference candidates are drawn and the envelope operator selects the one maximizing predicted scalarized value.

## Method

DPI decomposes decision-making into a Value Appraisal module (a GRU encoder over the history window that outputs mean and log-variance of a diagonal Gaussian posterior over latent preference logits z_t, mapped to the simplex via softmax) and an Action Selection module (a preference-conditioned PPO actor-critic that produces vector-valued returns and a policy conditioned on the selected preference). Preference inference is formulated as variational inference with a Boltzmann-rational likelihood p(e_t|z_t) ∝ exp(β⟨ω_t, G_t⟩) and a unit Gaussian prior, yielding an ELBO objective that balances fit to current vector returns against prior regularization. Training jointly optimizes the PPO actor-critic loss, the ELBO, a directional alignment loss (cosine similarity between predicted preferences and vector returns), and a self-consistency loss anchoring the encoder's mean prediction to the envelope-selected preference. The key distinction from prior envelope or preference-conditioned methods is that preferences are never given externally—they are inferred online from perceptual history, with the variational posterior providing both epistemic uncertainty and a mechanism for smooth, evidence-driven reweighting under distributional shifts.

## Results

On the Queue environment, DPI-PPO achieves a mean episodic return (MER) of 10.34 ± 0.02 and a success rate (SR) of 39.95 ± 2.75%, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline ENVELOPE (MER −3.54, SR 25.10%). On the Maze environment, DPI-PPO reaches MER 30.16 ± 1.22 and SR 59.04 ± 0.10%, exceeding ENVELOPE by +191.1% in MER while all other baselines (FIXED, HEURISTIC, ENVELOPE) achieve near-zero SR. In the continuous-control HalfCheetah setting, DPI-PPO attains MER 42.10 ± 6.25 and SR 81.00 ± 1.00%, surpassing ENVELOPE-PPO (MER 13.52, SR 53.28%) while remaining below the Dense Oracle upper bound (MER 66.95, SR 99.98%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPI-PPO decisively outperforms both compared baselines across all environments and metrics. Against ENVELOPE (Yang et al., 2019), which uses rule-based event-dependent preferences with an envelope Q-learning policy, DPI-PPO improves MER from −3.54 to 10.34 (+192%) and SR from 25.10% to 39.95% on Queue, and from 10.36 to 30.16 (+191%) with SR jumping from 0.01% to 59.04% on Maze. Against FIXED (Mossalam et al., 2016), which uses a static preference vector emphasizing task-completion objectives, DPI-PPO improves MER from −4.19 to 10.34 on Queue and from 16.15 to 30.16 on Maze, while SR increases from 10.05% to 39.95% (Queue) and from 1.12% to 59.04% (Maze). The gap is most dramatic on Maze, where FIXED achieves a moderate MER of 16.15 but a near-zero success rate (1.12%), confirming that no single fixed scalarization can handle the combinatorial diversity of event configurations, whereas DPI's online preference inference enables consistent task completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends preference-conditioned MORL (Van Moffaert & Nowe, 2014; Yang et al., 2019; Basaklar et al., 2023) by treating the preference vector as a latent variable inferred online via a variational Bayesian module, rather than a fixed or externally supplied input. The envelope operator from Yang et al. (2019) is repurposed from a post-hoc Pareto-front selection tool into an on-policy action-selection mechanism, and the Boltzmann-rational likelihood for preference evidence draws on free-energy formulations (Friston, 2010) and quantal response models. The cognitive framing—separating 'value appraisal' from 'action selection'—is grounded in appraisal theory and dual-process psychology, but the computational instantiation (GRU encoder → diagonal Gaussian posterior → softmax → PPO actor-critic) is a straightforward variational inference module trained jointly with a standard preference-conditioned policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: most MORL methods assume preferences are given, yet in realistic non-stationary settings an agent must infer what matters from noisy, evolving context. However, the evaluation is confined to small-scale simulated environments (a 2D grid maze, a toy queue, and a modified HalfCheetah), and a hand-crafted Dense Oracle with privileged event signals actually outperforms DPI on success rate in both Maze (62.9% vs 59.0%) and Queue (49.3% vs 40.0%), suggesting the learned inference does not yet fully match simple rule-based adaptation when event signals are available. Open questions include whether the variational preference module scales to high-dimensional, partially observable, or multi-agent settings, and whether the ELBO-based inference remains stable under longer horizons or more complex preference dynamics.
