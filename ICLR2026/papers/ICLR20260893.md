## Problem

Multi-agent option discovery is severely hampered by the exponential growth of the joint state space with the number of agents, making it infeasible to apply standard eigenoption methods directly. Existing multi-agent approaches (e.g., Kronecker product-based covering options) produce loosely coupled or fully independent behaviours that fail to capture strong inter-agent dependencies. This paper addresses the gap by asking: can we compress the joint state space into a compact representation centred on team alignment that preserves the relational information needed to discover strongly coordinated joint options?

## Input / Output

The method consumes 500,000 transitions sampled from a random joint policy, factorised into individual agent state vectors (e.g., x/y coordinates in LBF; x/y, orientation, and item inventory in Overcooked). It produces a set of joint options—each a tuple of initiation set, joint policy, and termination condition over macro-level histories—along with the intermediate Fermat state estimates and per-feature n-distance representations. A key structural constraint is that joint options require full team consensus (N votes) for initiation and a shared termination action, and the multi-dimensional n-distance output is one scalar per state feature dimension rather than a single aggregate value.

## Method

The core mechanism re-centres the joint state space around a learned Fermat state (the point minimising summed temporal distance to all agents), then encodes each joint state as a vector of per-feature n-distances to that Fermat state. The Fermat encoder and a successor-distance (temporal distance) module are trained jointly via a contrastive InfoNCE loss, with a stop-gradient on the distance parameters during Fermat training. To prevent the multi-dimensional distance from collapsing onto a single feature, a Conditional Mutual Information penalty—minimised adversarially via a discriminator following Dunion et al.—forces each distance component to carry no more information about other features than the features themselves already share. These disentangled relative representations serve as node encodings in a state-transition graph, on which ALLO approximates the first k graph Laplacian eigenvectors; each eigenvector (and its negation) then provides an intrinsic reward signal for training a separate joint option policy via IQL. The resulting options are integrated into a MacDec-POMDP with two modelling assumptions: an information-sharing channel among agents and a synchronisation mechanism ensuring sufficient consensus before a joint option executes.

## Results

The paper reports IQM scores (apples eaten per episode in LBF; successful deliveries per episode in Overcooked) with 95% confidence intervals across 10 seeds. IQL+IARO achieves consistent performance gains over vanilla IQL in all four scenarios, with the improvement being especially pronounced in Overcooked where IQL tends to get stuck in suboptimal solutions. Even a small set of options (as few as 2 in LBF, 10 in Overcooked) yields substantial performance boosts, with gains saturating at larger option counts (20 for LBF, 40 for Overcooked). The multi-dimensional n-distance variant outperforms the scalar variant in Overcooked's richer state space, while both perform similarly in LBF's simpler coordinate-based states.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against option-free baselines, IQL+IARO outperforms IQL, IPPO, and MAPPO across all evaluated scenarios, and also beats VDN in three of four scenarios (losing only in Overcooked's Forced Coordination, where VDN is known to perform well per Rutherford et al., 2024). Against other option discovery methods, IQL+IARO clearly outperforms both IQL+Kron (Kronecker product, Chen et al., 2022) and IQL+RJS (raw joint states); the paper notes that options from these two methods can actually degrade performance, particularly in LBF, because their first non-trivial eigenvectors drive agents toward the edges of the state space rather than promoting inter-agent alignment. The Kronecker product eigenvectors are completely unaffected by teammate positions, confirming the absence of inter-agent coupling in the discovered behaviours. The multi-dimensional n-distance representation (IQL+IARO-MultiDim) provides a clear advantage over the scalar variant (IQL+IARO-Scalar) in Overcooked, where agent states combine X/Y coordinates, orientations, and categorical inventory features, enabling selective alignment along specific feature subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the eigenoption discovery pipeline (Machado et al., 2017; ALLO, Gomez et al., 2024) to multi-agent settings by introducing a novel inter-agent relative state representation centered on the Fermat state, a point of maximal team alignment defined via n-metric theory (Kiss et al., 2018). It departs from the primary competing multi-agent approach—Kronecker product-based joint option discovery (Chen et al., 2022)—which the authors argue produces loosely coupled behaviours that merely synchronise independent single-agent options rather than capturing genuine inter-agent dependencies. The method also draws on temporal/successor distances (Myers et al., 2024) for state similarity and CMI-based disentanglement (Dunion et al., 2023) to prevent degenerate multi-dimensional distance predictions, and adapts the MacDec-POMDP framework (Amato et al., 2019) to support joint option execution with team-level consensus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in multi-agent RL: the exponential growth of the joint state space makes option discovery intractable, and existing methods sacrifice coordination quality. The Fermat-state-centered relative representation offers a principled, task-agnostic way to compress joint states while preserving the relational structure needed for coordinated behaviour, and the multi-dimensional n-distance with CMI disentanglement enables agents to selectively align on subsets of state features. Key limitations include the requirement for full team consensus on option initiation, the assumption of direct observation sharing (rather than a communication protocol), and the restriction to team-level (not subset-level) options. Open directions include richer topologies of coordinated behaviours, communication-based option triggering, and scaling to heterogeneous agent teams with more complex interaction structures.
