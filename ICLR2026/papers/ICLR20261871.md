## Problem

Existing provenance methods for generative vision models—watermarking, fingerprinting, and reconstruction-based approaches—either require modifications to the training or generation pipeline or show suboptimal performance on image autoregressive models (IARs). This leaves a critical gap: there is no post-hoc, model-agnostic method to attribute already-published, unmarked IAR-generated images to their source model, which is essential for combating misinformation, fraud, and model collapse from training on synthetic data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261871/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a suspect RGB image of arbitrary resolution and white-box access to a target IAR's autoencoder components (encoder E, decoder D, and quantizer Q with its codebook Z). It requires no text prompts, class labels, or generation metadata. The output is a provenance score that enables both binary detection (generated by this IAR vs. natural/from another model) and multi-model attribution across a set of candidate IARs. A one-time post-hoc finetuning of the inverse decoder (on ~10K–50K images generated by the target model) precedes inference, after which attribution runs in under 10 ms per image for most models.

## Method

The framework exploits the fact that IARs sample tokens from a fixed codebook, leaving generated images with feature representations that are consistently closer to codebook entries than natural images. The core signal, QuantLoss, is computed by inverting the image through a finetuned inverse decoder D^{-1} (initialized from the original encoder and trained on IAR-generated images with the codebook and decoder frozen), then measuring the L2 distance between the recovered feature map and its re-quantized version. For multi-scale IARs like VAR, a novel gradient-descent-based token search algorithm optimizes the multi-scale token combination to minimize reconstruction error, since the original greedy scalewise quantization cannot invert the summed feature map. A complementary EncLoss signal captures the information loss from projecting an image into the latent space and back, calibrated by a double-reconstruction ratio to normalize for image complexity. The final provenance score is the product of QuantLoss and calibrated EncLoss, achieving near-100% TPR@1%FPR across LlamaGen, RAR, Taming, VAR, Infinity, and VQ-Diffusion.

## Results

The proposed framework achieves near-perfect TPR@1%FPR (≥99.2%) across all six evaluated IARs (LlamaGen, RAR, Taming, VAR, Infinity, VQ-Diffusion) and all nine non-belonging sources (3 natural datasets + 6 generated datasets). The method operates in the latent space, achieving attribution in under 10 ms per image for most models (vs. 2.4–84.9 s for LatentTracer). Robustness to common post-processing (JPEG, resize, brightness, etc.) is maintained at 80–99% TPR@1%FPR with augmentation-aware finetuning, while baselines drop below 12%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: LatentTracer (Wang et al., ICML 2024) and AEDR (Wang et al., 2025). LatentTracer achieves competitive TPR@1%FPR on LlamaGen (89–99%) and VQ-Diffusion (93–99%) but collapses to near-zero on RAR (0–27%), VAR (0.1–15%), and Infinity (0–32%), making it unreliable for multi-scale and random-order IARs. AEDR shows moderate performance (15–92% TPR@1%FPR) but is consistently outperformed by the proposed method by 10–95 percentage points across nearly all model–dataset pairs. The proposed method achieves ≥99.2% TPR@1%FPR in every setting, a margin of 50+ points over the best baseline on the hardest models (RAR, VAR, Infinity). Additionally, under image post-processing attacks (Table 2), the proposed QuantLoss with augmentation-aware finetuning retains 80–99% TPR@1%FPR, while both LatentTracer and AEDR drop below 12%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reconstruction-based image provenance line (RONAN, LatentTracer, AEDR) by exploiting a model-specific artifact unique to vector-quantized autoregressive models: generated images' token representations are consistently closer to the codebook entries than natural images'. It adapts AEDR's double-reconstruction calibration ratio for the EncLoss signal but introduces two fundamentally new components — a finetuned decoder inversion (D⁻¹) that replaces the original encoder, and an optimized multi-scale token search for next-scale prediction models like VAR. Crucially, it departs from all prior provenance methods (watermarking, fingerprinting, and reconstruction-based) by being fully post-hoc, requiring no modification to the generation process or output, and operating on already-published unmarked content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As IARs (VAR, LlamaGen, Infinity, etc.) become widely accessible and their outputs visually indistinguishable from real images, reliable post-hoc provenance is essential for combating misinformation, fraud, and model collapse from training on synthetic data. The near-100% TPR@1%FPR across six diverse models with a simple two-loss framework (QuantLoss × EncLoss) and minimal finetuning overhead makes this highly deployable in practice. Key limitations include the requirement for white-box access to the model's encoder, decoder, and codebook, and the attribution is to the autoencoder rather than the autoregressive model, which may be insufficient when multiple AR models share the same tokenizer. Open questions include robustness to more aggressive adversarial perturbations, generalization to future IAR architectures that may abandon discrete codebooks, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation.
