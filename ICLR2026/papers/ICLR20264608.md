## Problem

Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks for VLMs lack a unified cognitive framework, focus predominantly on static scene perception rather than dynamic mental transformation, and are too small in scale to robustly evaluate or drive model development. This leaves a critical gap in assessing intrinsic-dynamic spatial reasoning—abilities like mental rotation and multi-step folding—that are fundamental to human spatial cognition but largely untested in current VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264608/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation item presents one or more rendered spatial images (isometric 3D views, 2D orthographic projections, unfolded nets, or multi-panel compositions) alongside a multiple-choice text question with four options (A–D). In the standard evaluation setting, the question image and all option images are merged into a single composite image fed to the VLM; a layout ablation also tests a separate-images variant where each panel is an independent input. The model outputs a single letter, scored by exact match, and results are reported as accuracy broken down by DISE quadrant (I-S, I-D, E-S, E-D) and by individual task type (e.g., 3D Rotation, Fold & Punch, 3D Combination).

## Method

The core contribution is a cognitively grounded 2×2 DISE taxonomy (Intrinsic/Extrinsic × Static/Dynamic) that organizes 10 spatial reasoning tasks drawn from classical psychometric tests into four quadrants, enabling systematic diagnosis of model weaknesses along structural dimensions. To overcome data scarcity, the authors build an automated Blender-based generation pipeline that produces verifiable 3D VQA pairs through seeded randomization (hashing question_id to a reproducible seed), programmatic shape construction, optimal-viewpoint rendering, and tiered distractor strategies (geometric variation, face rotation/swapping, incorrect orthographic views, component replacement). The resulting Spatial-DISE Bench (559 pairs across all 10 tasks and 4 quadrants) and Spatial-DISE-12K (12,355 synthetic 3D pairs) are used to evaluate 32 SOTA VLMs and to fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL-7B and SpaceOm via LoRA on all linear layers. A key empirical finding is that quadrant-specific fine-tuning produces a strong diagonal transfer pattern—training on one DISE quadrant primarily improves that quadrant—indicating that the four quadrants encode relatively distinct spatial competencies rather than factorizing cleanly along the Intrinsic/Extrinsic or Static/Dynamic axes.

## Results

Across 32 state-of-the-art VLMs evaluated on the Spatial-DISE Bench (559 VQA pairs), the average accuracy is only 28.4%, marginally above random chance (25%) and drastically below the human baseline of 76.8%. The best-performing model, Doubao-1.5-VL-thinking, achieves 42.0% overall accuracy, with its strongest quadrant being Extrinsic-Dynamic (61.9%) where it even surpasses the human baseline (61.1%). Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on Spatial-DISE-12K via LoRA yields the largest single-model gain, improving overall accuracy from 26.1% to 47.0% (+20.9 pp), with Intrinsic-Static jumping from 16.1% to 51.7%. Even after fine-tuning, the best model remains ~30 pp below human performance, confirming a persistent gap in cognitive spatial reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 32 evaluated VLMs fall substantially short of the human baseline (76.8%), with the best proprietary model (Doubao-1.5-VL-thinking, 42.0%) trailing by over 34 pp. Among proprietary models, Gemini 2.0 Flash (34.2%) and Claude 3.7 Sonnet (30.6%) outperform GPT-4o (28.1%) and GPT-4o-mini (25.6%), while open-source models like InternVL3-38B (32.4%) and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (27.2%) cluster in the 23–32% range. Spatial-specific models (SpaceThinker 32.6%, SpaceOm 25.9%, SpaceR 27.0%) do not consistently outperform general foundation models of similar scale, and reasoning-enhanced variants (VLM-R1 30.8%, LMM-R1 26.1%, LLaVA-CoT 24.0%) show only marginal gains over their base models. Fine-tuning on Spatial-DISE-12K produces the largest improvements: Qwen2.5-VL-7B-sft reaches 47.0% (+20.9 pp over base) and SpaceOm-sft reaches 41.3% (+15.4 pp), with the most pronounced gains in Intrinsic-Dynamic and Extrinsic-Dynamic quadrants. On external benchmarks (CVBench, SAT, SPACE, OmniSpatial, VSIBench_MCQ), fine-tuned models show selective transfer—Qwen2.5-VL-7B-sft improves most on OmniSpatial (+12.2 pp) and SPACE (+3.5 pp)—confirming that the learned spatial skills generalize primarily to structurally homologous tasks rather than broadly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the fragmented landscape of spatial reasoning benchmarks (SpatialRGPT, BLINK, VSR, SAT, SPACE, etc.) by introducing a unified 2×2 cognitive taxonomy (DISE: Intrinsic/Extrinsic × Static/Dynamic) grounded in Maier's (1996) and Uttal et al.'s (2013) cognitive science framework. Rather than proposing a new model architecture, it reorganizes the evaluation space around a principled taxonomy that exposes the systematic under-testing of dynamic spatial reasoning (mental rotation, folding, multi-step transformation) in prior work. The Blender-based synthetic generation pipeline extends the data-generation approach of SPARE3D and Spatial457 but adds verifiability through seeded randomization and tiered distractor strategies, while the 12K training set addresses a data scarcity that prior dynamic-reasoning benchmarks (SPACE, BSA) could not overcome.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a universal ~48-point gap between the best VLM (42%) and human performance (76.8%), with error analysis showing 72.5% of failures are reasoning errors (rule application, mental simulation) rather than perceptual errors—reframing the bottleneck as cognitive rather than visual. The counterintuitive finding that some models perform better on dynamic than static tasks, and that quadrant-specific fine-tuning produces a diagonal (non-factorized) transfer pattern, challenges the assumption that spatial skills decompose cleanly along intrinsic/extrinsic or static/dynamic axes. Open questions include whether the sim-to-real gap in the synthetic 12K dataset limits transfer to naturalistic scenes, whether interactive/process-oriented evaluation (beyond multiple-choice) would yield different conclusions, and whether the computational-strategy advantage observed in E-D tasks generalizes to genuinely novel 3D configurations.
