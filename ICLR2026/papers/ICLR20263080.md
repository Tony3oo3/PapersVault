## Problem

Existing fairness benchmarks (BBQ, StereoSet, UnQover) score each LLM in isolation with scalar bias scores or accuracy, providing no principled way to understand how biases align, diverge, or persist across model families, scales, and tuning strategies. Without relational analysis, it is impossible to determine whether fairness failures are structurally inherited across releases, whether swapping one model for another actually resolves bias, or whether tuning gains are superficial behavioral shifts rather than representational changes.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes bias-sensitive prompts in three distinct formats—BBQ's 3-option multiple choice (stereotype/anti-stereotype/unknown), UnQover's 2-option forced choice without abstention, and StereoSet's open-ended sentence completion—and evaluates 30 LLMs (base and instruction-tuned) across 1M+ prompts. For each model pair, it produces a 6-dimensional bias similarity signature vector and pairwise similarity matrices, with individual metrics (accuracy, s_AMB/s_DIS bias scores, UNK flip rates, cosine distances, CKA scores, histograms) computable independently. Most metrics are fully black-box compatible, though CKA requires access to internal layer activations, limiting it to open-weight models.

## Method

BSM reframes fairness as a relational property between models, asking 'which models behave similarly with respect to bias?' rather than 'is model M biased?' It integrates four complementary similarity categories into a unified 6-dimensional signature: scalar metrics (accuracy on disambiguated questions, directional bias scores s_AMB and s_DIS), distributional comparisons (histograms and cosine distance on raw count vectors without normalization), behavioral measures (UNK flip rates quantifying the proportion of biased base-model responses converted to 'Unknown' by the tuned counterpart), and representational similarity via Centered Kernel Alignment on layer-wise Gram matrices. The pipeline standardizes all model outputs into categorical labels, abstention markers, and embeddings, then applies pairwise similarity functions to construct matrices analyzable locally (within-family, e.g., base vs. tuned) or globally (cross-vendor, open vs. proprietary). A key design principle is modularity: each metric operates independently, enabling lightweight single-metric audits or full multi-dimensional ecosystem mapping. The framework explicitly distinguishes fairness-through-caution (appropriate abstention on ambiguous items) from fairness-through-representation (directional neutrality in committed answers), a separation invisible to any single scalar score.

## Results

Across 30 LLMs evaluated on 1M+ prompts from BBQ, UnQover, and StereoSet, instruction tuning primarily drives abstention rather than altering internal representations: CKA diagonal scores between base and tuned models exceed 0.94 (e.g., LLaMA 2 7B: 0.991, Gemma 3 12B: 0.972), with full CKA remaining above 0.85. LLaMA 3.1 8B shows the largest bias reduction (s_AMB: 18.59 → 1.38; s_DIS: 31.37 → 4.78), while small models like LLaMA 3.2 3B paradoxically worsen after tuning (s_AMB: 11.95 → 15.71; s_DIS: 17.67 → 30.97). Gemma 2 9B-It achieves the highest abstention uptake (73.1% UNK flip in gender), and GPT-5 Mini reaches near-perfect neutrality (s_AMB = 0.21, s_DIS = 1.10) with the highest accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against baselines; instead, it uses BSM as an auditing framework to compare 30 LLMs across four families. The key cross-model comparisons reveal: (1) Open-weight Gemma 3 12B-It and 27B-It achieve near-neutral bias scores (s_AMB ≈ 0.07–0.15) comparable to proprietary GPT-4o Mini (0.47) and GPT-5 Mini (0.21), while abstaining less (utility advantage). (2) Within-family, instruction tuning reduces bias for mid-to-large models (LLaMA 3.1 8B: s_AMB drops 17.2 points) but increases it for small models (LLaMA 3.2 3B: +3.76; Gemma 3 4B: +9.72). (3) Gemma family converges on abstention-heavy mitigation (UNK flips >50% for 9B-It, 12B-It), while LLaMA 3.1 shifts toward this strategy from earlier generations that preserved base tendencies. (4) Proprietary Gemini models over-refuse (very low accuracy, heavy abstention), suppressing utility despite moderate bias scores, whereas GPT-5 Mini achieves the best accuracy-bias balance. (5) CKA analysis confirms that all these behavioral shifts occur atop nearly identical internal representations (diagonal CKA > 0.94), meaning tuning modifies decision policies rather than underlying feature geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate literatures: LLM bias assessment (BBQ, StereoSet, UnQover), which evaluates models in isolation via scalar scores, and model similarity analysis (CKA, prediction overlap, adversarial transferability), which compares architectures without centering fairness. BSM departs from both by reframing bias as a *relational* property between models, integrating scalar, distributional, behavioral (UNK flip rates), and representational (CKA) signals into a unified pairwise similarity space. It directly builds on Kornblith et al.'s CKA for representational comparison and Parrish et al.'s BBQ bias score for directional measurement, but the key conceptual move is treating these as components of a cross-model bias signature rather than per-model diagnostics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that instruction tuning preserves internal representations (CKA > 0.94) while primarily enforcing abstention challenges the assumption that alignment training structurally debiases models, pointing toward the need for representation-level interventions. The framework provides a practical auditing workflow for procurement, regression testing, and lineage screening that goes beyond single-model scorecards. Open questions include whether the abstention-heavy convergence is robust to adversarial prompting, how findings generalize to multilingual and code settings, and whether targeted debiasing (counterfactual augmentation, feature-level retraining) would produce different similarity dynamics than the natural tuning effects observed here.
