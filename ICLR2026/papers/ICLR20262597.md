## Problem

CLIP-based synthetic image detectors inherit text-image alignment semantics that are irrelevant to authenticity, causing features to cluster by object class rather than the real/synthetic boundary and hindering cross-generator generalization. Additionally, no principled continual learning strategy exists for adapting detectors to newly emerging 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendered-fake domains without catastrophic forgetting of prior diffusion and GAN detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262597/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector consumes RGB images at a minimum resolution of 224×224 (datasets typically span 256×256 to 512×512) and produces a single binary real/synthetic probability via a sigmoid on a scalar logit. Internally, the input image is encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14, whose 24 intermediate layer outputs plus the final layer are concatenated into a 19,200-dimensional vector that serves as the bottleneck input x; the compressed 64-dimensional latent z is then classified by a single linear layer.

## Method

The HSIC bottleneck concatenates all 24 intermediate CLIP ViT layer features with the final layer (19,200-dim), projects them through a single linear layer to a 64-dim latent z, and is trained with L_total = λ_x·HSIC(x,z) − λ_y·HSIC(y,z) + L_BCE, where Gaussian RBF kernels with median-heuristic bandwidth estimate the Hilbert-Schmidt independence criterion. Minimizing HSIC(x,z) suppresses input-anchored shortcuts (including text-alignment semantics), while maximizing HSIC(y,z) preserves label-relevant discriminative structure, yielding generator-invariant yet task-aligned representations. For domain-incremental adaptation to 3DGS domains, HSIC-Guided Replay (HGR) selects per-class exemplars by minimizing a hybrid score s_i(t) = (1−N(r_i)) + λ_kc·(1−N(d_i(t))), where r_i is the HSIC-inspired relevance (squared norm of the centered Gram matrix row) and d_i(t) is the k-center distance to the already-selected set, balancing information centrality with spatial coverage in a compact replay buffer.

## Results

On the cross-generator generalization benchmark (Table 1, trained on SDV1.4), the full method with intermediate CLIP ViT features achieves 93.86% mACC / 98.89% mAP averaged over 15 diffusion and GAN targets, outperforming the strongest baseline VIB-Net (85.83% / 95.53%) by over 8 points in accuracy. Under GAN-only training (Table 2, ProGAN source), the method reaches 91.07% / 98.25%, again leading all baselines. For domain-incremental adaptation to 3DGS head-avatar domains, HSIC-Guided Replay (HGR) attains 94.38% / 98.92% (SDV1.4 start) and 88.63% / 96.31% (ProGAN start), surpassing the non-continual oracle in the SDV1.4 setting and exceeding iCaRL and CBRS by 1.7–4.3 points in mACC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The strongest cross-generator baseline is VIB-Net (Zhang et al., CVPR 2025), which achieves 85.83% mACC on the diffusion-trained setting; the proposed HSIC bottleneck with intermediate features outperforms it by 8.03 points (93.86%) and by 8.10 points on the GAN-trained setting (91.07% vs. 82.97%). NPR (Tan et al., CVPR 2024) is the next-best non-CLIP baseline at 76.50% mACC (diffusion-trained) and 82.84% (GAN-trained), trailing the proposed method by 17.4 and 8.2 points respectively. Among CLIP-based detectors, RINE (Koutlis & Papadopoulos, ECCV 2024) reaches 64.59% (diffusion-trained) and 86.03% (GAN-trained), while UniFD (Ojha et al., CVPR 2023) scores 65.37% and 77.72%; both are surpassed by 28–29 and 13–14 points. For domain-incremental 3DGS adaptation, HGR (94.38% mACC, SDV1.4 start) exceeds the non-continual oracle (93.75%) and outperforms CBRS (92.66%) and iCaRL (92.40%) by 1.7 and 2.0 points; from a ProGAN start, HGR (88.63%) leads CBRS (86.18%) and iCaRL (84.33%) by 2.5 and 4.3 points, though the oracle (89.04%) retains a slight 0.4-point edge. On the DRCT-2M diffusion benchmark (Table 10), the intermediate-feature variant reaches ≈99.9% accuracy across all Stable Diffusion variants, surpassing DRCT (Chen et al., ICML 2024) by 1–16 points on harder targets such as SDXL and SDXL-Turbo.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the HSIC bottleneck from DualHSIC (Wang et al., 2023b), which applied layer-wise HSIC to ResNet intermediate features for continual learning, to the CLIP ViT setting by concatenating all 24 intermediate layers plus the final layer and compressing through a single linear head. For replay, it combines the k-center coreset selection of Sener & Savarese (2018) with an HSIC-inspired relevance score, creating a hybrid exemplar selection strategy (HGR) tailored to synthetic image detection. The paper positions itself against the CLIP-based detection line (UniFD, RINE, VIB-Net) by arguing that text-image alignment semantics in CLIP features are actively detrimental to authenticity discrimination, and against cross-generator detection methods (CNNSpot, LGrad, NPR, DIRE, DRCT) by showing that information-theoretic feature shaping on a frozen ViT backbone outperforms gradient-space or reconstruction-based cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first photorealistic 3DGS head-avatar benchmark (GHA, SA, GAGAvatar) and the first domain-incremental learning protocol for synthetic image detection, exposing a significant blind spot: existing CLIP-based detectors (UniFD, RINE) drop to near-chance (~50–58%) on 3DGS renders, while the proposed HGR achieves 94–98%. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full-body 3DGS, NeRF-rendered content, adversarial perturbations, and imbalanced real/synthetic splits, none of which are evaluated. The HSIC bottleneck is backbone-agnostic and lightweight (a single 19200→64 linear layer), making it a practical plug-in for any frozen ViT-based detector, though the paper does not test lighter backbones or in-the-wild deployment.
