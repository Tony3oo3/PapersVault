## Problem

Hybrid mechanistic neural ODEs (MNODEs) built on physiological models often contain dozens of latent states and interactions, causing training inefficiency and overfitting in data-scarce healthcare settings. Existing model reduction approaches either require deep domain expertise (e.g., quasi-steady-state approximations) or are purely data-driven and agnostic to mechanistic structure, leaving a gap for computationally efficient, structure-aware sparsification that preserves biological plausibility while improving predictive performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a past observable state history (e.g., 42 blood glucose readings at 5-minute intervals over 210 minutes), past and future exogenous inputs (carbohydrate intake, insulin delivery rate, heart rate, step count over the same window), and a mechanistic ODE directed graph (e.g., the UVA-Padova model with 20+ latent states). It produces a future observable state trajectory (12-step, 60-minute glucose prediction) along with a sparsified graph structure where redundant edges are pruned to zero weight. The input is consumed as a 5-dimensional time series with 54 discrete time steps, and the graph structure constrains which node features feed into each per-node MLP in the decoder.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-step Hybrid Graph Sparsification (HGS) pipeline applied to the MNODE architecture (LSTM encoder + graph-structured MLP neural ODE decoder solved via forward-Euler discretization). Step 1 collapses all maximal strongly connected components into super-nodes, converting the graph into a relaxed DAG that eliminates feedback loops responsible for training instability and exploding gradients. Step 2 augments the RDAG with partial transitive closure shortcuts along key input-to-observable-state pathways, giving the model flexibility to skip intermediate latent states while preserving mechanistic reachability constraints. Step 3 applies a combined L1 penalty on edge-specific weights and L2 penalty on MLP parameters during training, which the paper proves is equivalent to a first-layer group LASSO with a 2/3-power exponent—encouraging entire edge-weight groups to vanish simultaneously rather than individual parameters. This structure-aware, gradient-based approach is more computationally efficient than greedy or random search baselines and yields sparser, more robust models than regularization-only methods.

## Results

On the T1DEXI real-world dataset (342 exercise instances from 105 T1D patients), MNODE_HGS significantly outperforms all black-box sequence models (LSTM, BNODE, TCN, S4D, Transformer) across RMSE, Peak RMSE, MAPE, and Diagnostic Accuracy while using fewer parameters. Against alternative reduction methods (NeuralSparse, Exclusive Group LASSO, Elastic Net, Random Search, Greedy Search, Domain Knowledge), HGS consistently achieves the best predictive performance and the fewest Effective Number of Parameters (ENP), with the largest gains appearing on graphs with substantial redundancy. On synthetic data, HGS outperforms black-box models at n=100 training samples, though TCN surpasses HGS in RMSE at n=1000; however, HGS retains superior worst-case robustness (Peak RMSE) in both regimes. An ablation study confirms that removing any single step of the three-step HGS pipeline (MSCC merging, shortcut augmentation, L1+L2 regularization) leads to a marked performance drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against black-box sequence models (LSTM, BNODE, TCN, S4D, Transformer), MNODE_HGS wins on all reported metrics (RMSE, Peak RMSE, MAPE, Diagnostic Accuracy) on the T1DEXI dataset while using fewer parameters, demonstrating the benefit of mechanistic structure plus sparsification over purely data-driven approaches. Among reduction methods, HGS outperforms NeuralSparse (Zheng et al., 2020), Exclusive Group LASSO (Jiang et al., 2023), Elastic Net (Jiang et al., 2021), Random Search, and Greedy Search on both RMSE and Peak RMSE, with the advantage being most pronounced on the comprehensive (highly redundant) initial graph where regularization-only methods struggle to recover signal. HGS also beats the no-reduction MNODE (MNODE_NR) and the domain-knowledge-reduced MNODE (MNODE_DK from Zou et al., 2024), showing that automatic data-driven sparsification can surpass expert-curated reductions. On synthetic data with a refined initial graph, gains over other reduction methods are modest, but on the comprehensive graph with 6 redundant inputs, 3 latent nodes, and 3 latent cycles, HGS's advantage becomes large and statistically significant. The only case where HGS does not lead is at n=1000 synthetic samples where TCN achieves slightly lower RMSE, though HGS still wins on Peak RMSE (robustness) and ENP (sparsity).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mechanistic Neural ODE (MNODE) framework of Zou et al. (2024) by introducing an automatic, gradient-based model reduction pipeline (Hybrid Graph Sparsification) that combines classical graph-theoretic operations—collapsing strongly connected components into super-nodes and augmenting pathways with partial transitive-closure shortcuts—with L1/L2 regularization on edge weights. It departs from purely data-driven graph sparsification methods in the GNN literature (NeuralSparse, exclusive group LASSO, elastic net) by grounding the search space in mechanistic plausibility, and from traditional biochemistry reduction (quasi-steady-state, timescale separation) by being fully differentiable and data-adaptive. The regularization is shown to be equivalent to a first-layer group LASSO penalty (connecting to Cherepanova et al., 2023), providing a theoretical bridge between structured sparsity in tabular deep learning and mechanistic ODE model reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying hybrid neural ODEs in data-scarce healthcare settings: overly large mechanistic priors (e.g., 20+ latent states in the UVA-Padova glucose model) cause overfitting and training instability. The three-step HGS pipeline offers a principled, computationally efficient recipe that outperforms both unreduced MNODEs and alternative reduction strategies while producing interpretable, biologically plausible structure (e.g., eliminating glucagon feedback loops). Limitations include dependence on a correctly specified mechanistic prior, the explicit disclaimer that true causal support recovery is not the goal, and evaluation on a single real-world dataset (T1DEXI, 342 instances). Open questions include scalability to much larger mechanistic models, whether the SCC-merging step is always beneficial, and generalization beyond glucose dynamics to other physiological systems.
