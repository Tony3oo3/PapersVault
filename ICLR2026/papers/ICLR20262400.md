## Problem

Existing image composition methods struggle with physically plausible results under complex lighting (accurate shadows, water reflections) and diverse high-resolution inputs. Training-based approaches are constrained by low-quality synthetic triplet data that inherits inpainting artifacts, while prior training-free methods rely on fragile image inversion (which locks object poses) and brittle attention surgery. The paper asks whether modern T2I diffusion models like FLUX already encode the necessary physical and resolution priors, and proposes a framework to unleash them without inversion.

## Input / Output

The method takes one or more reference subject images, a background image at arbitrary resolution (landscape or portrait, e.g., 768×1088, 1152×768), and a user-provided binary insertion mask. A VLM (BLIP-3) captions the subject, and an inpainting model (Flux.1-Fill) generates an initial composite image that is encoded into VAE latent space and perturbed via one-step forward diffusion (flow matching) to a chosen timestep. The output is a full-resolution composed image where the subject is seamlessly integrated with correct lighting, shadows, and reflections, produced in 20 denoising steps starting from timestep 15.

## Method

SHINE is a training-free, model-agnostic framework built on three components. (1) Manifold-Steered Anchor (MSA) loss optimizes the noisy latent by minimizing the L2 distance between the adapter-augmented model's velocity prediction (conditioned on the subject latent) and a stop-gradient anchor from the base model, effectively projecting the latent onto the subject's data manifold while preserving background structure; the Jacobian term is omitted following SDS precedent. (2) Degradation-Suppression Guidance (DSG) constructs a negative velocity by applying a 2D Gaussian blur to Q_img within FLUX's joint self-attention (proven equivalent to blurring attention weights), then steers the denoising trajectory away from low-quality regions in a CFG-like manner. (3) Adaptive Background Blending (ABB) replaces the rigid user mask during early denoising steps with a semantically precise mask derived from binarized cross-attention maps of subject tokens, eliminating visible seams at boundaries. The entire pipeline avoids image inversion, instead initializing from a VLM-captioned inpainted image perturbed by one-step forward diffusion.

## Results

On DreamEditBench (220 pairs), SHINE-LoRA achieves the best subject identity scores: DINOv2 0.7452, IRF 0.7900, and DreamSim 0.3577 (lower is better), while also leading in image quality with ImageReward 0.5906 and VisionReward 3.6161. On the more challenging ComplexCompo (300 pairs, varied resolutions and complex lighting), SHINE-LoRA maintains its lead with DINOv2 0.7384, DreamSim 0.3542, ImageReward 0.4246, and HPSv3 9.8418. The adapter variant (SHINE-Adapter) is competitive, achieving DINOv2 0.7415 and IR 0.5709 on DreamEditBench, with slightly lower identity fidelity than the LoRA variant but comparable image quality. Both variants surpass all 12 baselines on human-preference-aligned metrics across both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHINE outperforms all 12 baselines on human-preference-aligned metrics (DreamSim, ImageReward, VisionReward) across both benchmarks. On DreamEditBench, SHINE-LoRA leads the strongest training-based baseline UniCombine by a margin of 0.012 in DINOv2 (0.7452 vs. 0.7332), 0.041 in DreamSim (0.3577 vs. 0.3984), and 0.134 in ImageReward (0.5906 vs. 0.4565). AnyDoor achieves competitive identity scores (DINOv2 0.7283) but produces noticeably lower image quality (IR 0.4511) due to its copy-paste behavior, while SHINE-LoRA achieves both higher identity and quality simultaneously. On the harder ComplexCompo benchmark, most baselines degrade substantially (e.g., UniCombine drops from DINOv2 0.7332 to 0.6552; DreamCom from 0.6749 to 0.5692), whereas SHINE-LoRA retains its lead with DINOv2 0.7384 and DreamSim 0.3542. Among training-free baselines, EEdit (the only other FLUX-based method) trails SHINE-LoRA by 0.123 in DINOv2 and 0.283 in DreamSim on ComplexCompo, confirming that inversion-based and attention-surgery approaches remain inferior to SHINE's manifold-steered optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHINE departs from the dominant training-free composition paradigm (TF-ICON, TALE, TIGIC, EEdit) that relies on image inversion and attention surgery, arguing that inversion locks objects into reference poses and is suboptimal for CFG-distilled models like FLUX. Instead, it repurposes pretrained customization adapters (IP-Adapter, InstantCharacter) as implicit manifold priors via a novel Manifold-Steered Anchor loss, and introduces a targeted self-attention manipulation (blurring Q_img) as a degradation signal. The approach also builds on the broader observation that flow-matching T2I models encode physical scene priors, positioning SHINE as a way to unlock those priors without the fine-tuning that prior composition models (AnyDoor, ObjectStitch, UniCombine) require on synthetic triplet data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that base models like FLUX already encode physically plausible composition priors (shadows, reflections, lighting) that are destroyed by fine-tuning on low-quality synthetic data—reframes the composition problem from 'learn to compose' to 'preserve what the base model already knows.' The ComplexCompo benchmark fills a real gap by evaluating diverse resolutions and challenging lighting conditions absent from prior 512×512 benchmarks. Open questions include whether the MSA loss generalizes to multi-object composition or video, whether the adapter dependency limits identity fidelity for novel subjects, and whether the color-inheritance limitation from inpainting prompts can be resolved without additional supervision.
