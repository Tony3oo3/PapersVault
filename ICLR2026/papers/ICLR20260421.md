## Problem

Existing vision-language navigation (VLN) models adopt tightly coupled end-to-end pipelines that map vision-language inputs directly to short-horizon discrete actions, producing fragmented motions, high execution latency from frequent VLM calls, and poor handling of dynamic obstacles. No prior work decouples high-level semantic reasoning from low-level real-time control in a dual-system architecture for long-horizon, cross-building VLN.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260421/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a sequence of egocentric RGB frames (single first-person view, no depth or odometry required) together with a natural-language instruction. System 2 outputs either discrete view-adjustment actions (Turn Left/Right 15°, Look Up/Down 15°, up to 4 consecutive) or a 2D pixel coordinate in the current image for the next waypoint, plus a STOP signal. System 1 then produces 32 dense continuous trajectory waypoints in world coordinates at 30 Hz, conditioned on both the frozen latent goal embedding from System 2 and the latest high-frequency RGB observation.

## Method

DualVLN decouples navigation into two asynchronously operating systems. System 2 is a fully fine-tuned Qwen-VL-2.5 (7B) that performs farthest pixel-goal grounding—projecting 3D ground-truth trajectories onto 2D egocentric views using depth-based visibility checks—and self-directed view adjustment, operating at 2 Hz. System 1 is a compact Diffusion Transformer (hidden dim 384, 12 layers) trained with flow matching to denoise 32-waypoint trajectories, conditioned on (a) four learnable latent queries extracted via prompt tuning from the frozen System 2 hidden states and (b) high-frequency RGB features encoded by a ViT (DepthAnythingV2-Small backbone) and compressed to 32 tokens via a Q-Former. Training is decoupled into two stages: first fine-tuning System 2 for pixel-goal grounding, then freezing it and jointly training the latent queries and the DiT policy, which preserves the VLM's generalization while enabling System 1 to learn adaptive, obstacle-aware local control from only a small fraction of trajectories.

## Results

On VLN-CE R2R Val-Unseen, DualVLN achieves NE=4.05, OS=70.7%, SR=64.3%, and SPL=58.5%, surpassing the best prior RGB-only method StreamVLN (NE=4.98, SR=56.9%, SPL=51.9%) by 7.4 points in SR and 6.6 points in SPL. On RxR Val-Unseen, DualVLN reaches SR=61.4% and nDTW=70.0%, outperforming StreamVLN (SR=52.9%, nDTW=61.9%). On the physically realistic VLN-PE benchmark (R2R Val-Unseen), DualVLN achieves SR=51.6% and SPL=42.49% zero-shot from VLN-CE training, dramatically exceeding NaVid (SR=22.42%) and all VLN-PE-trained baselines (best: RDP at SR=25.24%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualVLN outperforms all 20 baselines across every reported metric on both R2R and RxR Val-Unseen. Among RGB-only VLM-based methods, it surpasses StreamVLN (the strongest prior) by +7.4 SR and +6.6 SPL on R2R, and by +8.5 SR and +9.1 nDTW on RxR. Compared to multi-sensor methods (ETPNav*, GridMM*, CMA*), DualVLN achieves higher SR and SPL using only single RGB + depth, demonstrating that the dual-system design compensates for the lack of panoramic and odometry inputs. On VLN-PE, DualVLN zero-shot transfers from VLN-CE training and achieves SR=51.6%, more than doubling the best VLN-PE-trained baseline (RDP at SR=25.24%) and outperforming NaVid (SR=22.42%) by nearly 30 points. On the new Social-VLN benchmark, DualVLN beats StreamVLN in SR (37.2% vs. 31.4%) and SPL (35.8% vs. 29.1%) while maintaining a comparable Human Collision Rate (35.4% vs. 36.4%), confirming its advantage in dynamic obstacle avoidance. In the ablation comparing System 1 against SOTA point-goal planners (iPlanner, NavDP) on VLN-PE, the integrated diffusion policy achieves SR=63.62% vs. 58.72% (NavDP) and 47.07% (iPlanner) on Val-Unseen, validating the benefit of joint dual-system training over modular pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen-VL-2.5 as the VLM backbone for high-level planning, extending the pixel-goal grounding paradigm explored by RoboPoint and NaviMaster into a full navigation pipeline. It departs from prior end-to-end VLA approaches (NaVid, NaVILA, UniNaVid, StreamVLN) that map vision-language inputs directly to short-horizon discrete actions, instead decoupling reasoning from control into an asynchronous dual-system architecture. While dual-system designs have appeared in robotic manipulation (FigureAI Helix, Hi Robot, Bu et al. 2024), this is the first to apply the slow-fast paradigm to long-horizon, cross-building vision-language navigation, connecting the two systems through both explicit pixel goals and learnable latent queries extracted via prompt tuning from frozen VLM hidden states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled training strategy preserves the VLM's generalization while enabling a lightweight, data-efficient local policy (saturating at ~10% of training data) that outperforms SOTA point-goal navigators even with oracle depth, suggesting that joint dual-system training captures trajectory distributions better than modular pipelines. The introduction of Social-VLN fills a critical gap in evaluating dynamic obstacle avoidance and task recovery, though the ~27% performance drop on this benchmark indicates the problem remains substantially unsolved. Open questions include whether the latent-query mechanism generalizes beyond the 4-query design, how the system scales to longer-horizon multi-room instructions, and whether the asynchronous inference gap (2 Hz vs 30 Hz) becomes a bottleneck in highly dynamic scenes.
