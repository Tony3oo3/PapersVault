## Problem

Prior activation steering methods for hallucination reduction (e.g., Contrastive Activation Addition) require real-time monitoring and per-instance intervention during inference, introducing computational overhead that limits scalable deployment. While interpretability research has shown that LLMs encode linear representations of their own knowledge boundaries, no prior training method has amortized these steering benefits directly into model weights. CASAL addresses this gap by connecting interpretability with amortized optimization, baking the knowledge boundary into a single transformer layer during training so that no inference-time steering is needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260362/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

CASAL takes a text query (or an image paired with a text query for vision-language models) and produces either a factual short-form answer or an explicit abstention/refusal. During training, the method consumes only the residual stream activation at the last token position of the question at a single target layer L*, computing a local MSE loss rather than a full-sequence cross-entropy. The trained model is a drop-in replacement for the original: at inference it requires no additional modules, steering vectors, or runtime interventions—only a standard forward pass through the modified weights at layer L*.

## Method

CASAL proceeds in three stages. First, knowledge boundary probing samples k=10 completions per query and partitions the dataset into known (≥7 correct) and unknown (≥7 incorrect) subsets. Second, steering computes difference-in-means vectors from residual activations at a selected target layer L* to construct target activations that push unknown queries toward abstention and known queries toward confident answering. Third, CASAL trains a lightweight one-layer network initialized with the original layer's weight matrix W_original^{L*}, minimizing a contrastive MSE loss between current activations and the steering targets as the sole training objective (not auxiliary to cross-entropy). After training, the learned weights replace W_original^{L*} in the frozen model, embedding the knowledge boundary directly into the architecture. Because the loss is local to one layer and computed at a single token position, CASAL requires only ~30× fewer FLOPs per token and ~20× less data than LoRA-based SFT/DPO/GRPO baselines while reducing hallucination by 30–40%.

## Results

CASAL reduces hallucination rates by ~30–40% across TriviaQA, PopQA, and EntityQA while maintaining the lowest refusal rates on TriviaQA (7.29%) and EntityQA (6.84%) among all methods. On general capability benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, GPQA, MT-Bench), CASAL performs on par with the unmodified baseline (e.g., 68.04% MMLU vs. 68.01% baseline; 7.57 MT-Bench vs. 7.38 baseline). CASAL achieves comparable hallucination reduction to SFT/DPO/GRPO trained on 12,800 examples using only 640 training examples (~20× data efficiency) and requires over 30× fewer FLOPs per token than LoRA-based fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA-based SFT, DPO, and GRPO, CASAL achieves the lowest refusal rates on TriviaQA (7.29% vs. 10.01%, 14.37%, 17.77%) and EntityQA (6.84% vs. 11.08%, 17.66%, 16.67%) while preserving accuracy on known queries (95.34% TriviaQA, 89.90% EntityQA), whereas SFT degrades TriviaQA accuracy to 92.45% and GRPO to 91.67%. In the OOD setting (trained on TriviaQA, tested on EntityQA), CASAL reduces hallucination from 50.7% to 11.7% with 95.77% accuracy on known queries, outperforming SFT (23.13% hallucination, 80.77% accuracy), DPO (18.30%, 87.41%), and GRPO (12.83%, 85.24%). Compared to inference-time Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA), CASAL achieves comparable hallucination reduction on unknown queries but critically preserves accuracy on known queries (>80% across middle layers), whereas CAA degrades known-query accuracy from ~90% to ~10% by layer 30. CASAL also requires ~20× less training data (640 vs. 12,800 examples) and ~30× fewer FLOPs per token than LoRA-based baselines to reach equivalent hallucination reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

CASAL sits at the intersection of two research threads: inference-time activation steering (Contrastive Activation Addition, Rimsky et al. 2024; difference-in-means steering, Arditi et al. 2024) and amortized optimization (Kingma & Welling 2013; Gershman & Goodman 2014). Prior steering methods require per-instance intervention at inference, while prior representation-level fine-tuning work (RepE, ReFAT, ReFT) uses representation losses only as auxiliary signals alongside cross-entropy. CASAL departs from both by treating a local representation MSE loss as the sole training objective and by framing the steering problem as amortized optimization—training a single-layer subnetwork to internalize the steering solution into weights, eliminating inference-time intervention entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a method that trains only a sub-module of one transformer layer with a local loss achieves 30× compute and 20× data efficiency over LoRA-based SFT/DPO/GRPO while reducing hallucination by 30–40%, making it viable for data-scarce and compute-constrained deployment. The strong OOD generalization (TriviaQA→EntityQA: 50.7%→11.7% hallucination) and extension to MoE and vision-language models suggest the learned knowledge boundary is a transferable property rather than a dataset-specific artifact. Key open questions include whether CASAL generalizes to long-form generation, reasoning models, and larger-scale deployments, and whether the single-layer constraint is truly necessary or merely a design choice that could be relaxed for further gains.
