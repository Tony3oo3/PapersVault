## Problem

Despite the empirical success of PPO, TRPO, and actor-critic methods with expressive function approximators, theoretical guarantees for global convergence remain fragmented: existing analyses are largely confined to tabular or linear settings, or assume exact value/advantage estimates. A central open question is whether one can design a policy evaluation step with controlled statistical error in a rich function class and couple it with a policy improvement step that provably ascends toward the optimal policy, while explicitly specifying the per-iteration sample requirements for guaranteed improvement.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes one-step state-action transition quadruplets (s₀, a₀, s₁, a₁) sampled from the MDP under the current policy, along with a chosen RKHS kernel K on S×A. Unlike GAE-based PPO which requires full trajectory segments to compute advantage targets, the kernel TD critic learns Q from single-step TD errors, making it non-local in time. The output is a Q-function estimate fₜ in the RKHS and an updated policy of the form π ∝ exp{F}, where F is an accumulated sum of step-scaled Q-estimates; the convergence rate is expressed in terms of RKHS entropy, yielding explicit sample-complexity formulas for tabular, Sobolev, Gaussian, and NTK regimes.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage algorithm in an infinite-dimensional RKHS. For policy evaluation, it formulates the Bellman fixed-point problem as a Kernel Ridge Regression (KRR) over the entire RKHS and solves it via kernel gradient descent (RKHS-gradient updates) rather than inverting the n×n Gram matrix; this RKHS inner product acts as an implicit preconditioner, yielding geometric convergence at O(log n) iterations without cubic-time matrix inversions. For policy improvement, it derives a KL-regularized proximal update whose closed-form solution exponentiates the evaluated Q-function, recovering a Natural Policy Gradient (NPG) step that generalizes tabular, linear, and NTK-based NPG to arbitrary RKHS functions. The key theoretical contribution is a non-asymptotic error bound for the kernel TD critic whose rate depends on the RKHS entropy H(δ) ≤ Cδ^{-2β}|log δ|^{2κ}, and an explicit per-iteration sample-size rule n^{(k)} that ensures the overall policy sequence achieves the optimal O(k^{-1/2}) convergence rate for stochastic optimization across all four kernel regimes.

## Results

On CartPole-v1, the proposed NPG with one-step TD critic consistently achieves the maximum reward of 500 across all 10 seeds, while clip-PPO converges more slowly with high variance and only some seeds reach the optimum. The theoretically predicted step-size schedule Δ_k = k^{-1/2} yields stable convergence on all three benchmarks (CartPole-v1, Acrobot-v1, HalfCheetah-v5), whereas Δ_k = k^{-0.1}/k^{-0.2} diverges and Δ_k = k^{-1.5} stagnates. In terms of throughput, the NPG method processes more state-action pairs per second than clip-PPO, owing to its lightweight one-step TD-error update versus PPO's recursive GAE computation over full trajectory segments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against clip-PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), the proposed NPG with one-step TD clearly wins on both sample efficiency and computational throughput on CartPole-v1: NPG reaches the maximum reward of 500 in all 10 seeds while clip-PPO shows high variance with only partial convergence, and NPG processes more state-action pairs per second due to its local one-step TD update versus PPO's non-local recursive GAE. Against discrete SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), NPG wins decisively—SAC fails to find the optimal solution on CartPole-v1. Against DQN (Mnih et al., 2015), NPG is more reliable: DQN is computationally efficient but unstable, converging to the optimum only in some seeds. The paper also notes that implicit PPO (the original PPO formulation analyzed in Appendix K) shows performance similar to the explicit NPG but with more stable iterations, analogous to the implicit-vs-explicit SGD distinction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends three parallel lines of work—TD learning (Sutton, 1988), natural policy gradient (Kakade, 2001), and PPO/TRPO (Schulman et al., 2015, 2017)—from finite-dimensional or overparameterized neural network settings to general infinite-dimensional RKHS. Where prior TD analyses (Cai et al., 2019; Bhandari et al., 2018) were restricted to linear or NTK regimes, and prior PPO/NPG guarantees (Liu et al., 2019; Agarwal et al., 2020) required tabular or linear function classes, this work unifies them under a single RKHS framework with entropy-based rates. A key departure is the explicit quantification of per-iteration sample requirements for the proximal policy update, addressing a gap in prior analyses where policy expectations were treated as exact or left unspecified. The kernel gradient descent update also serves as an implicit preconditioner, avoiding the cubic-time matrix inversions of standard kernel ridge regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By placing PPO and NPG on a firmer theoretical footing in infinite-dimensional function spaces, this work clarifies when kernel-proximal updates with TD critics yield global policy improvement and at what sample cost, unifying tabular, linear, Sobolev, Gaussian, and NTK regimes under a single entropy-based rate. The NTK connection means the guarantees directly inform wide neural network critics and actors in the lazy-training regime, bridging a gap between kernel theory and practical deep RL. Open questions include extending the analysis to L_infinity metrics (more natural for RL but harder due to the fixed-point KRR structure), generalizing to mirror descent beyond KL regularization, and developing practical estimators for the RKHS norm of the policy, which governs the sampling requirement but is intractable for deep networks.
