## Problem

Large VLMs struggle with information-intensive images (infographics, charts) that densely interleave textual annotations with fine-grained graphical elements, because existing zoom-in pipelines rely on attention or confidence signals that correlate weakly with true relevance in dense layouts, and multi-hop reasoning over dispersed evidence is highly error-sensitive. The paper asks whether a training-free, draft-then-verify paradigm can recover correct answers by synthesizing diverse reasoning paths from multiple small models, without the costly task-specific training that RL-based zoom methods require.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260194/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an image-question pair where the image is an information-intensive visual (infographic, chart, or high-resolution scene at up to 4K resolution) plus a natural-language question. For information-intensive benchmarks, a layout-preserving structured representation generated by PP-StructureV3 is rendered as an additional image and fed alongside the raw image to the verdict model. The output is a single concise text answer (a word, phrase, or option letter), produced in one verdict inference call that processes thousands of prefill tokens from the draft reasoning paths but generates only a few answer tokens.

## Method

Speculative Verdict (SV) repurposes the draft-then-verify paradigm of speculative decoding from inference acceleration to error correction in visual reasoning. In the draft stage, k=5 lightweight VLMs each produce a candidate answer; a consensus scoring mechanism computes pairwise NLL differences (normalized against each peer's own answer) to select m=3 experts with the strongest cross-model agreement, who then generate full chain-of-thought reasoning paths. In the verdict stage, a large VLM (GPT-4o or Qwen2.5-VL-72B) receives the original image, the question, and the concatenated reasoning paths in a single call, acting as a synthesizer that cross-checks grounding, resolves contradictions, and integrates complementary cues into the final answer rather than simply voting. The key design choices—consensus-based expert selection to filter noisy paths, full reasoning paths (not just answers) as verdict input, and a single prefill-heavy verdict call—yield 47–53% recovery of minority-correct cases and 6.6–12% gains over GPT-4o at 15–26% of o1's inference cost, all without any training.

## Results

SV with GPT-4o as verdict achieves 88.4 ANLS on InfographicVQA, 49.3% accuracy on ChartMuseum, 64.0% on ChartQAPro, and 71.4% on HR-Bench 4K, outperforming GPT-4o by +11.9, +6.6, +11.4, and +4.0 points respectively. With Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct as verdict, SV reaches 75.6% on HR-Bench 4K (surpassing the verdict itself by 2.5%). On cost-efficiency, SV matches or exceeds o1-level performance (88.3 vs. 77.8 on InfographicVQA, 63.4 vs. 58.8 on ChartQAPro) while requiring only 15–26% of o1's inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SV with GPT-4o verdict outperforms GPT-4o by +11.9 ANLS on InfographicVQA, +6.6% on ChartMuseum, and +11.4% on ChartQAPro, while also exceeding o1 on InfographicVQA (88.3 vs. 77.8) and ChartQAPro (63.4 vs. 58.8) at only 15–26% of o1's API cost. Against the strongest open-source draft expert GLM-4.1V-Thinking (9B), SV gains +3.6 ANLS on InfographicVQA and +6.7% on ChartQAPro; against MiMo-VL-RL (7B), gains are +4.9 and +6.7% respectively. SV substantially surpasses tool-driven baselines: DeepEyes is outperformed by +12.9 ANLS on InfographicVQA and +21.3% on ChartMuseum, while Pixel-Reasoner trails by +4.4 ANLS and +24.7% on ChartQAPro. On HR-Bench 4K, SV with Qwen2.5-VL-72B verdict (75.6%) exceeds DeepEyes (73.0%) and the verdict alone (73.1%). Finally, SV outperforms the LMM-as-a-Judge baseline LLaVA-Critic-7B by 4.9–11.9% across information-intensive benchmarks, confirming that synthesis-based verdict outperforms selection-based judging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the draft-then-verify paradigm of speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023), originally designed for LLM inference acceleration, into a training-free accuracy framework for information-intensive visual reasoning. Rather than verifying token-level continuations, SV uses multiple lightweight VLMs as draft experts to generate diverse chain-of-thought reasoning paths, and a large VLM as a verdict model to synthesize and correct them. It departs from tool-driven zoom-in pipelines (DeepEyes, Pixel-Reasoner, ViCrop) that rely on RL-trained or attention-based cropping, and from LMM-as-a-Judge selection (LLaVA-Critic) that picks a single best trajectory, by instead performing synthesis-based verification across complementary reasoning paths with a consensus-based expert selection mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SV offers a practical, training-free recipe that outperforms GPT-4o by 10%+ on information-intensive VQA while costing only 15–26% of o1's inference budget, making it attractive for practitioners who need strong chart/infographic reasoning without fine-tuning or proprietary model access. The 47–53% recovery rate on minority-correct cases where the verdict itself fails demonstrates that synthesis across diverse reasoning paths captures information no single model or majority vote can. Open questions include whether the single-call verdict design suffices for very long multi-hop chains, how the framework scales beyond 5–7B draft pools, and whether the residual failures (color matching, fine-grained extraction on very tall infographics) can be addressed without additional training.
