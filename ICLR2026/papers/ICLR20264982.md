## Problem

In sequential decision-making, environment dynamics dictate how semantic states evolve, but the corresponding latent transitions in a neural network's embedding space are left implicit, creating a potential misalignment between the two. This gap is especially acute in discrete-state MDPs (e.g., Atari, gridworlds) where the state space has no inherent continuity, so smoothness and structural regularity must be imposed on the latent space rather than inherited from the input.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes stacked RGB game frames (Atari) or grid observations (Minigrid), which are encoded by a Nature CNN into 512-dimensional latent vectors. The neural ODE flow operates on these extracted feature vectors rather than raw pixels, and a time-sampling scheme (either step index τᵢ=i or exponential decay τᵢ=γⁱ) assigns each state in a trajectory an integration time. The ODE is used exclusively as a training-time regularizer and is never invoked at inference; the agent still outputs a discrete action via its standard policy head.

## Method

FlowReg introduces a decoupled neural ODE (a two-layer MLP with tanh activation parameterizing the latent derivative f_φ) that defines a smooth continuous flow in the 512-dim latent space. During training, the agent's discrete latent trajectory H_θ = {h_θ(s₀), …, h_θ(s_{N-1})} is aligned to the ODE-sampled flow H_φ via a simple MSE loss (Eq. 11), which is added to the standard actor-critic objective with a weighting λ. Because ODE flows are unique and non-intersecting under Lipschitz continuity, this alignment imposes a diffeomorphic structure on the latent space, penalizing abrupt jumps and crossings that violate the flow. The flow model carries curvature/topological information while the semantic embedder carries task information, and the mutual alignment fuses both signals. The ODE is autonomous (time-invariant) due to the Markov property, and the regularization is applied at a configurable frequency relative to agent updates (e.g., every 10 or 20 steps) to balance overhead against benefit.

## Results

FlowReg consistently improves A2C performance across all 11 Atari environments. On Qbert, the best FlowReg configuration (Index, U-5) nearly doubles the baseline reward from 4374.30 to 8306.05; on River Raid it improves from 1862.27 to ~2947, and on Beamrider from 960.66 to ~1591. Latent path smoothness metrics show dramatic reductions: path length drops from 34.39 to 4.20 (Qbert) and acceleration energy from 4424.75 to 64.17, while simultaneously improving task reward. On Minigrid, flow-regularized PPO clearly outperforms baseline on FourRooms and Dynamic Obstacles, matching it on DoorKey.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the unregularized A2C baseline, which FlowReg outperforms on all 11 Atari environments, with the largest gain on Qbert (+89.9%). Against TACO (temporal contrastive learning), FlowReg achieves substantially higher rewards on Qbert (8306 vs. 2434) and Breakout (24.03 vs. 11.12), while nearly matching TACO on River Raid (2948 vs. 2943). Notably, TACO produces smoother latent paths than the baseline on all three environments yet degrades performance on two of them, whereas FlowReg achieves even greater smoothness (lower path length and acceleration energy) while simultaneously improving task reward. This demonstrates that FlowReg's ODE-flow alignment imposes a structurally compatible constraint on the latent space, unlike TACO's contrastive objective which can over-constrain and destroy task-relevant information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) not as an inference model but as a decoupled training-time regularizer, departing from prior work (Du et al., 2020; Alvarez et al., 2020) that uses ODEs to directly predict semantic trajectories. It also takes a broader view than the ResNet-as-continuous-depth literature (Lu et al., 2018; Haber & Ruthotto, 2017), which treats individual layers as ODE discretizations, by instead modeling the entire sequence of latent embeddings across an MDP trajectory as a single ODE flow. Compared to TACO (Zheng et al., 2023), which enforces temporal predictability via contrastive learning, FlowReg imposes a stricter structural constraint: ODE-flow uniqueness guarantees that latent paths are diffeomorphic and non-intersecting, not merely smooth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlowReg offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic way to inject global trajectory structure into latent representations of discrete-state MDPs, where continuity cannot be inherited from the state space. The key open limitation is that ODE flows cannot self-intersect, which may conflict with environments containing bottleneck states reachable from multiple starting points. The paper leaves open whether the technique transfers to off-policy algorithms (DQN), model-based methods (Dreamer), or non-RL paradigms like imitation learning. Practically, the modest runtime overhead (0.5–19%) for consistent performance gains across 11 Atari and 3 Minigrid environments makes it a low-cost add-on, though the A2C/PPO baselines are not state-of-the-art, limiting the absolute impact.
