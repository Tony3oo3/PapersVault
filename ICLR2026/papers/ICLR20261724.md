## Problem

Existing transformer-based video action anticipation methods rely on dot-product attention over pixel-level token representations, which lack the high-level semantics needed to model latent intentions behind upcoming actions. This causes the model to overfit to explicit visual cues in past frames, degrading generalization to unseen test sets where observations are partial and inherently uncertain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261724/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video frames (224×224) sequentially at dataset-specific sampling rates (1 fps for EK100/EK55, 0.5 fps for EGTEA Gaze+), processing one frame at a time through a frozen backbone (TSN or Swin-B) followed by a trainable encoder. At each timestep it outputs a softmax probability distribution over N_c action classes, predicting the action occurring τ_a seconds after the last observed frame. A FIFO queue of size S=16 bounds the temporal window of referenced history, and the model operates without any auxiliary modalities (no optical flow, audio, or text).

## Method

AGA replaces pixel-level queries and keys in standard dot-product attention with the model's own predicted action distributions: the query is an exponential moving average (α=0.8) of past predictions projected through an MLP, the keys are the S most recent predictions projected through a separate MLP, and the values are the corresponding frame embeddings. Multi-head attention with PreNorm-style RMSNorm aggregates semantically relevant past moments, and an Adaptive Gating module then componentwise fuses this history context with the current frame embedding via a sigmoid-gated MLP to balance past evidence against present visual cues. The final prediction is produced by a two-layer MLP with softmax over the gated output. This design explicitly models next-action prediction conditioned on previously predicted actions, and enables post-training forward analysis (which past actions the model attends to for a given query) and backward analysis (counterfactual optimization of past predictions to recover a target action), providing interpretable insight into learned action dependencies.

## Results

On EPIC-Kitchens-100 validation (RGB only), AGA with Swin-B achieves 18.8% MT5R overall action accuracy, 32.5% verb, and 38.7% noun, outperforming single-backbone baselines like AFFT-Swin-B (17.6%) and S-GEAR-ViT-B (18.3%). On the unseen EK100 test set, AGA reaches 16.9% action MT5R, surpassing AVT++ (16.7%), RAFTformer-2B (15.4%), and S-GEAR-4B (15.5%). On EK55, AGA (Swin-B) attains 16.3% Top-1 and 16.6% MT5R, and on EGTEA Gaze+ it reaches 45.4% Top-1 action accuracy, both leading among RGB-only methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EK100 validation with RGB only, AGA (Swin-B, 18.8% MT5R) outperforms AFFT-Swin-B (17.6%), S-GEAR-ViT-B (18.3%), and MeMViT-32x3 (17.7%) on overall action accuracy, and leads on unseen-class action (16.3% vs. 15.2%) and tail-class noun (35.0% vs. 31.0%). However, multi-backbone ensembles RAFTformer-2B (19.1%) and S-GEAR-2B (19.6%) marginally exceed AGA on overall action accuracy, though they do not report unseen/tail splits. On the EK100 test set, AGA (16.9%) surpasses all listed competition submissions including AVT++ (16.7%), RAFTformer-2B (15.4%), and S-GEAR-4B (15.5%), with a notably small validation-to-test gap indicating strong generalization. On EK55, AGA (Swin-B) achieves 16.3% Top-1 and 16.6% MT5R, beating DCR-TSM (16.1% Top-1) and S-GEAR-ViT-B (15.8% Top-1); S-GEAR with TSN (15.6%) is slightly lower but uses higher-resolution inputs. On EGTEA Gaze+, AGA (Swin-B) leads with 45.4% Top-1 action accuracy over AVT (43.0%) and TSN-AFFT (42.5%), and also achieves the best mean Top-1 recall across all three class types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant transformer-based anticipation paradigm (AVT, MemViT, RaftFormer) that applies standard dot-product attention over pixel-derived token representations. Instead, AGA repurposes the attention mechanism by using the model's own predicted action distributions as queries and keys, while retaining frame embeddings as values, thereby shifting the attention signal from low-level visual correlation to high-level semantic action dependency. The design also introduces an adaptive gating module to balance history context against current visual evidence, and builds on the EMA formulation for temporal smoothing of the query. Unlike prior work that keeps predictions separate from feature extraction (Abu Farha et al., Ke et al., Zhao & Wilde), AGA feeds predictions back into the attention mechanism to dynamically reweight visual tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that grounding attention in action semantics rather than pixel features yields consistent generalization from validation to unseen test sets, addressing a known overfitting weakness of anticipation models on partially observed inputs. The forward and backward analysis tools offer a rare degree of interpretability for anticipation models, enabling practitioners to inspect learned action dependencies and counterfactual reasoning paths. Limitations include the sequential (frame-by-frame) processing that increases inference latency relative to parallel transformers, the restriction to RGB-only input, and the need for careful tuning of the EMA smoothing factor. Open questions include whether the action-guided attention principle extends to multi-modal settings, longer anticipation horizons, or larger-scale benchmarks beyond the kitchen domain.
