## Problem

Predicting experimentally realizable 3D molecular crystal structures from 2D chemical graphs (crystal structure prediction) remains an open challenge because existing ML-based methods either enforce rigid-body assumptions that fail on flexible molecules or rely on explicit lattice parametrization and equivariant architectures that do not scale to the large, chemically diverse unit cells typical of organic crystals. Classical DFT-based search-and-rank pipelines, while more general, require generating and optimizing 1,000–100,000 structures per molecule at prohibitive computational cost (e.g., 46 million CPU core-hours for 8 targets in CSP7).

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 2D molecular graph (SMILES) as input, with no explicit unit-cell, space-group, or multiplicity (Z) specification. It outputs a finite, non-periodic block of 3D all-atom Cartesian coordinates that simultaneously encodes the intramolecular conformation and the intermolecular packing motif; periodicity is recovered implicitly from the learned local interaction patterns rather than from an explicit lattice vector. At inference, 30 independent samples are drawn per target (one per seed), and evaluation uses COMPACK alignment of 15-molecule clusters against experimental structures.

## Method

OXTAL is a 100M-parameter all-atom diffusion transformer that learns the conditional joint distribution over conformations and packing directly from Cartesian coordinates, deliberately discarding equivariant architectures and explicit lattice parametrization in favor of SE(3) data augmentation. The key training innovation is Stoichiometric Stochastic Shell Sampling (S⁴), a lattice-free cropping scheme that builds concentric molecular shells around a randomly chosen central molecule using a contact-radius distance metric, then fills shells up to a token budget while preserving the crystal's molecular stoichiometry—mirroring the local-to-global nature of nucleation and growth. The architecture adapts AlphaFold3's Pairformer trunk (4 blocks, 16 heads, 2 recycling rounds) for atom-level tokens, followed by a 70M-parameter diffusion transformer denoising head. Training on ~600K CSD structures uses a composite loss combining MSE, SmoothLDDT (pairwise distance fidelity), and a distogram head, with a theoretical guarantee (Proposition 1) that the boundary error from S⁴ cropping decays as O(T^{-1/3}) in the number of tokens.

## Results

On the rigid molecular CSP dataset (30 samples), OXTAL achieves a packing similarity rate (Pac_S) of 0.873, conformer recovery (Rec_C) of 0.960, and approximate solve rate (Sol_C) of 0.300, with near-zero collision rate (Col_S = 0.011). On the flexible dataset, OXTAL attains Pac_C = 0.900 and Sol_C = 0.220, the only ML method to approximately solve any flexible crystal. On the CCDC CSP Blind Tests 5–7, OXTAL (30 samples) outperforms the aggregate DFT submissions (464–868 samples) on packing similarity rate across all three tests (e.g., 0.667 vs. 0.323 on CSP5; 0.660 vs. 0.183 on CSP6; 0.483 vs. 0.058 on CSP7), while DFT retains a higher approximate solve rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OXTAL decisively outperforms both ML baselines (A-Transformer and AssembleFlow) across all metrics and all datasets. On the rigid dataset, OXTAL achieves Pac_S = 0.873 versus 0.015 (A-Transformer) and 0.001 (AssembleFlow); on the flexible dataset, OXTAL is the only method with non-zero approximate solves (Sol_C = 0.220 vs. 0 for both baselines). Against AlphaFold3 (zero-shot, rigid dataset only), OXTAL improves Pac_S from 0.089 to 0.873 and Rec_C from 0.480 to 0.960. Against the aggregate DFT submissions on the CCDC blind tests, OXTAL wins on packing similarity rate by a wide margin (e.g., 0.667 vs. 0.323 on CSP5, 0.660 vs. 0.183 on CSP6, 0.483 vs. 0.058 on CSP7) using only 30 samples versus 83–868 for DFT, but DFT retains a higher approximate solve rate (Sol_C: 0.544/0.496/0.421 vs. 0.167/0.200/0.125), indicating that OXTAL captures the correct packing motif more frequently per sample but has not yet matched DFT's ability to refine to the exact energy minimum.

## Relation to Prior Work

OXTAL adapts the AlphaFold3 architecture (Pairformer trunk, atom encoder, diffusion module) to molecular crystal structure prediction, but makes two key conceptual departures from both prior inorganic crystal generation models (which rely on equivariant architectures and explicit unit-cell parametrization) and classical CSP pipelines (search + DFT ranking). The introduction of S4 (Stoichiometric Stochastic Shell Sampling) removes lattice parametrization entirely, training on locally consistent molecular neighborhoods rather than full periodic cells, while SE(3) data augmentation replaces explicit equivariant inductive bias. This positions OXTAL as a bridge between the protein-structure-prediction paradigm and the crystallography community's generate-optimize-rank workflow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OXTAL's orders-of-magnitude cost reduction relative to DFT-based methods (46M CPU core-hours for 8 targets in CSP7 vs. a single GPU inference) makes dense posterior sampling and broad screening feasible for the first time, potentially transforming how crystal forms are explored in pharmaceuticals and materials. The ability to capture both thermodynamic and kinetic regularities (e.g., predicting distinct polymorphs from independent samples) suggests the model learns the experimentally realized distribution rather than just the energy landscape. Key open questions include integrating reliable ranking, local geometry relaxation, conditioning on crystallization context (solvent, temperature), and improving the still-modest approximate solve rate (~12–30%) to competitive levels with DFT.
