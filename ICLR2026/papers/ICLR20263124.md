## Problem

Under expert parallelism in Mixture of Experts (MoE) inference, imbalanced token-to-expert assignment causes a Straggler Effect where overloaded experts dictate overall latency while underloaded experts sit idle waiting at synchronization barriers. Training-time auxiliary balance losses fail to mitigate this imbalance at inference, and existing remedies like DeepSeek-V3's expert duplication incur significant additional resource overhead. The paper asks: how can we prevent extreme expert overloading and better utilize underloaded experts at inference time without extra computational cost?

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on standard MoE model inputs: text token sequences (batch size × sequence length × hidden dim) for language models, or image tokens concatenated with text tokens for multimodal models (e.g., 576 image tokens vs. 31 text tokens in MME). It produces the same model predictions as the unconstrained baseline but with reduced end-to-end latency. A key design constraint is that all capacity enforcement and expanded routing are restricted to local experts on the same GPU to avoid additional inter-device communication overhead.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary inference-time mechanisms. First, Capacity-Aware Token Drop enforces a per-expert capacity C = γN̄ (where N̄ is the expected token count per expert); when an expert's assigned tokens exceed C, it discards the lowest-scoring excess tokens using gating scores as an importance metric, which consistently outperforms order-based or random dropping. Second, Capacity-Aware Expanded Drop addresses the residual underutilization of low-load experts by expanding each token's candidate set to include m additional local experts on the same device (yielding up to k+m candidates), so that tokens dropped from overflowed experts can be absorbed by underloaded local experts while still respecting strict local capacity constraints. The approach is justified by the observation that gating scores decay gradually beyond the top-k, meaning rerouted tokens still reach reasonably relevant experts. A device-level variant relaxes the constraint from per-expert to per-device granularity, allowing tokens to flow between experts on the same GPU as long as the aggregate device load stays within bounds.

## Results

On Mixtral-8×7B-Instruct, Expanded Drop achieves a 74.5 average across 9 benchmarks (vs. 74.3 baseline), a 0.2% improvement, while delivering a 1.85× end-to-end inference speedup. On OLMoE-Instruct, Token Drop with γ=2.0 maintains a 62.3 average (vs. 63.5 baseline, a 1.2% drop) and achieves a 30% MoE-layer speedup. On Qwen1.5-MoE-Chat, Expanded Drop reaches 65.6 average (vs. 65.9 baseline), and on DeepSeek-V2-Lite-Chat it scores 68.9 (vs. 69.3 baseline). In multimodal settings (MolmoE), the Image-First strategy with Expanded Drop at γ=1.0 achieves 1362.1/297.1 on MME Perception/Cognition, surpassing the 1358.1/269.6 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three strategies: (1) no capacity constraint (Baseline), (2) Expert Drop (skipping the 10% lowest-load experts), and (3) the proposed Token Drop and Expanded Drop. Expert Drop consistently degrades performance by 1.0–4.0 points in average accuracy across all four models (e.g., 60.5 vs. 63.5 on OLMoE-Instruct, 72.2 vs. 74.3 on Mixtral), confirming that low-load experts are not redundant. Token Drop narrows the gap to within ~0.5–1.2 points of baseline while enabling substantial speedups (up to 1.87× end-to-end on Mixtral). Expanded Drop further closes or eliminates the gap, matching or slightly exceeding baseline on Mixtral (74.5 vs. 74.3) and DeepSeek-V2-Lite (68.9 vs. 69.3), while delivering a 1.85× speedup on Mixtral. On Qwen1.5-MoE-Chat, Expanded Drop (65.6) remains 0.3 points below baseline (65.9) but outperforms Token Drop (65.1) by 0.5 points, demonstrating the benefit of redistributing overflow tokens to underutilized experts rather than discarding them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the capacity-constrained Token Drop mechanism from Switch-Transformer (Fedus et al., 2022), which was originally designed as a training-time regularization, into the inference phase where it directly bounds expert load and latency. It departs from DeepSeek-V3's approach of duplicating high-load experts across devices (which incurs additional memory and communication overhead) by instead enforcing strict per-expert or per-device capacity limits at inference with no architectural or hardware changes. The novel Expanded Drop component further generalizes the top-k routing by allowing tokens to consider additional local experts on the same GPU, a mechanism not present in prior MoE inference work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE architectures (Mixtral, DeepSeek, Qwen) become the dominant scaling strategy for LLMs, inference-time load imbalance is a practical bottleneck that training-time balance losses cannot resolve. The paper's key practical contribution is a drop-in, retraining-free modification that yields up to 1.85× speedup with negligible performance loss, making it immediately deployable in production serving stacks. Limitations include reduced effectiveness when many experts share a single GPU (diminishing the straggler's relative impact) and the fact that the speedup is workload-dependent (more pronounced under high-throughput serving). Open questions include interaction with speculative decoding, KV-cache management, and whether the approach transfers to MoE models with dynamic expert selection or expert-choice routing.
