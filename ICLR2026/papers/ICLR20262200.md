## Problem

RLVR fine-tuning of LLMs consistently improves single-attempt accuracy (Pass@1) while degrading multi-attempt performance (Pass@k) and causing catastrophic forgetting on out-of-domain tasks. The community has largely attributed this to entropy collapse or reward design, while overlooking that the standard reverse-KL divergence's mode-seeking nature actively narrows the policy onto a single solution style. This paper identifies the choice of f-divergence as a neglected, fundamental axis for mitigating diversity collapse.

## Input / Output

For SQL tasks, the model receives a natural-language question paired with a database schema and generates an executable SQL query, scored by running the query and comparing the result set against ground truth via Cartesian product matching. For math tasks, the model receives a problem statement and produces a step-by-step solution with a boxed final answer, evaluated by the Qwen2.5 official evaluation tool. Training data is pre-partitioned into D_exp (queries the reference model fails on most attempts) and D_pef (queries solved correctly at a high threshold, e.g., 6/8 or 7/8 rollouts), with one additional verification sample drawn to reduce sampling bias.

## Method

DPH-RL reframes the KL divergence term from a passive policy constraint into an active diversity-preserving 'rehearsal mechanism.' During a pre-sampling stage, the reference policy's rollouts partition the dataset into D_exp (challenging queries) and D_pef (near-perfect queries). During online training, D_exp samples are optimized with a standard PPO-clip objective and no divergence penalty, granting full exploration freedom; D_pef samples are regularized with a mass-covering f-divergence (Forward-KL or JS-divergence) computed via a generator-based formulation that uses pre-sampled reference trajectories, eliminating the need for an online reference model. The total loss is L_DPH = L_exp + η·L_pef. The paper further proves an enhanced monotonic improvement guarantee (Theorem 1) extending TRPO's bound with a positive ε_f term that arises from the f-divergence regularization on expert data, rigorously showing that the method accelerates convergence on mastered tasks while gracefully reducing to the standard TRPO guarantee on exploration data.

## Results

On the BIRD SQL task (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), DPH-JS (η=0.2) achieves the best Greedy score of 62.8 and Pass@8 of 70.5, surpassing the base model's Pass@8 of 68.8 while GRPO (66.2) and DAPO (67.2) both fall below it. On the cross-domain Spider task, DPH-F achieves Pass@16 of 85.7 vs. 80.6 for GRPO and 76.7 for DAPO, while the base model scores 93.2. On mathematical reasoning (Qwen2.5-Math-7B), DPH-JS achieves the best average Pass@k of 70.74, outperforming GRPO (67.71), DAPO (67.97), and RKL (68.01), with a 5.35-point gain over the base model (65.39).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), DPH-RL consistently wins on Pass@k metrics: on BIRD, DPH-JS achieves Pass@8 of 70.5 vs. GRPO's 66.2 (+4.3), and on Spider, DPH-F achieves Pass@16 of 85.7 vs. GRPO's 80.6 (+5.1). GRPO's Pass@k actually falls below the base model (66.2 vs. 68.8 on BIRD), confirming the diversity collapse the paper targets. Against DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), DPH-RL also wins on Pass@k: DPH-JS scores 70.5 vs. 67.2 on BIRD Pass@8, and DPH-F scores 85.7 vs. 76.7 on Spider Pass@16 (+9.0). DAPO's cross-domain performance is notably unstable, dropping to 75.3 on Spider Pass@8. On out-of-domain math tasks (Table 2), DPH-F and DPH-JS surpass DAPO by 8.35% and 7.6% in average score, while RKL performs worst (48.45 avg vs. base 60.35), demonstrating that reverse-KL's mode-seeking behavior severely harms generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its base RL algorithm and extends TRPO's (Schulman et al., 2015) monotonic improvement theorem with an additional f-divergence bonus term. It departs from the dominant line of work on RLVR diversity collapse—which has focused on entropy control, Pass@k reward shaping, and hybrid SFT+RL training—by arguing that the f-divergence term itself, long treated as a mere policy constraint, is the overlooked lever. The paper also connects to f-PO/f-DPO (Wang et al., 2024; Han et al., 2025), which explored f-divergences in offline preference alignment, but transplants them into online RLVR with verifiable rewards and a data-partitioning strategy (D_exp vs. D_pef) that has no direct analogue in prior f-divergence work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that swapping reverse-KL for a mass-covering f-divergence (forward-KL or JS) in existing GRPO/DAPO pipelines is a low-cost, plug-and-play fix that simultaneously improves Pass@1, preserves Pass@k, and mitigates catastrophic forgetting on out-of-domain tasks—without requiring an online reference model in the Generator implementation. A key limitation, shared with GRPO, is dependence on the base model's capability: on very hard datasets where the base model rarely succeeds, the D_pef set becomes small and the rehearsal signal weakens. Open questions include how to handle this regime (learning from imperfect data without estimation bias) and whether the data-partitioning threshold (e.g., '8 of 8') generalizes cleanly across task difficulty distributions.
