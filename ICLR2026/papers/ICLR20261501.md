## Problem

Existing hallucination mitigation methods for LVLMs (e.g., OPERA, VCD) apply coarse-grained, uniform attention adjustments across all layers or modalities, overlooking the fine-grained, stage-specific attention dynamics that distinguish truthful tokens from hallucinatory ones. This paper identifies that real and hallucinated tokens follow distinct, model-specific attention patterns at different reasoning stages, and that hallucinations arise precisely when the model fails to reproduce these patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261501/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, AGE takes a standard RGB image and text instruction, plus a single pre-computed n-dimensional directional vector δ (where n equals the number of visual tokens from the image encoder). δ is constructed offline from only ~10 COCO samples by averaging the attention-vector difference between real and hallucinated tokens at the final decoder layer, with adaptive weighting based on each sample's CHAIR score. The output is a standard autoregressive text response, but with attention scores at designated layers (30–31 for visual, layer 20 for text in LLaVA) modified during decoding to steer generation toward truth-aligned attention patterns.

## Method

AGE is a training-free, decoding-time framework built on two stage-specific interventions derived from empirical analysis of attention disparities. First, Imitating Image Attention: a directional vector δ is computed as the weighted average of (real − hallucinated) visual attention vectors across M samples, then injected into visual attention at late-stage layers via a_vision + λ_v·δ, restoring visual grounding where the gap is most pronounced. Second, Imitating Text Attention: for models like LLaVA where middle-stage layers show stronger reliance on textual context, a self-multiplicative enhancement (a_text + λ_t·a_text) amplifies the model's focus on its own generated context. The interventions are model-adaptive—MiniGPT-4 and mPLUG-Owl2 require only the image-attention correction, while LLaVA additionally needs the text-attention boost—and are applied selectively at specific layers rather than uniformly, which ablations confirm is critical for avoiding degradation.

## Results

On COCO image captioning (Table 1), AGE achieves an average CHAIR_S of 17.15% and CHAIR_I of 6.35% across LLaVA-1.5-7B, MiniGPT-4-7B, and mPLUG-Owl2-7B, outperforming the best prior method (VISTA) by 2.85% on CHAIR_S and 0.10% on CHAIR_I, while maintaining a BLEU of 16.16 (vs. 15.21 for greedy baseline). On the POPE benchmark (Table 2), AGE reaches 73.86% average accuracy, surpassing the greedy baseline (56.77%) by 17.09% and OPERA (53.77%) by 20.09%, with a comparable F1 of 69.37. On MME hallucination subtasks, AGE outperforms VCD at both object and attribute levels on LLaVA-1.5-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGE outperforms all 12 compared baselines on the primary COCO captioning metric (CHAIR_S) across the three LVLMs. Against the closest competitor VISTA (C_S = 20.10), AGE achieves 17.15% (−2.85%), and against LURE (22.87%) and OPERA (24.42%), the margins are 5.72% and 7.27% respectively. On POPE, AGE's 73.86% average accuracy exceeds OPERA (53.77%) by 20.09% and VCD (57.11%) by 16.75%, while maintaining a comparable F1 (69.37 vs. 69.32 for greedy). In the extended Table 13 comparison (max token = 512), AGE (C_S = 35.6, C_I = 10.8) beats VTI (35.8/11.1), HALC (41.8/12.2), DeGF (46.0/14.2), AGLA (43.0/14.1), DeCo (37.8/11.1), and Nullu (54.6/14.1); the only near-tie is with VTI, where AGE edges ahead by 0.2% on C_S. Woodpecker achieves a slightly higher BLEU (16.26 vs. 16.16) but at the cost of substantially worse hallucination rates (C_S = 26.35 vs. 17.15), and it requires an auxiliary correction model, whereas AGE is training-free and uses only 10 calibration images.

## Relation to Prior Work

AGE departs from the dominant decoding-time hallucination mitigation paradigm (OPERA, VCD, DoLA) that applies uniform, direction-agnostic attention reweighting across all layers or modalities. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes the general idea of attention calibration by grounding it in a token-level, layer-wise empirical analysis: real and hallucinated tokens exhibit distinct, stage-specific attention patterns, and hallucinations arise when the model fails to reproduce these patterns. The directional vector δ, computed from the difference between real and hallucinated token attention, provides a corrective signal that prior coarse-grained methods lack, while the self-multiplicative text enhancement for LLaVA's middle stage addresses a model-specific need that global visual-attention boosting cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AGE offers a training-free, model-agnostic intervention that requires only 10 calibration samples and negligible latency overhead, making it immediately deployable across LVLMs without retraining or auxiliary models. The core insight—that hallucinations stem from stage-specific attention pattern failures rather than a uniform visual-attention deficit—reframes how the field thinks about the mechanism of hallucination and suggests that future methods should be stage-aware and direction-aware. Open questions include whether the offline δ computation can be replaced by reliable online estimation (the paper's online variant underperforms), whether the approach extends to video and audio modalities, and why scenes with many small objects remain failure cases, pointing toward the need for finer-grained visual grounding.
