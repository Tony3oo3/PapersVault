## Problem

While T2I models are increasingly used to generate synthetic training data, the systematic impact of prompt complexity (length, specificity, detail level) on the utility axes of synthetic data—quality, diversity, and consistency—remains unexplored. Prior evaluations treat prompts as fixed inputs without varying their complexity, leaving open whether models generalize well to prompts outside their training distribution and how inference-time interventions interact with prompt specificity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261699/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes an image-caption dataset (CC12M, ImageNet-1k, or DCI) and produces captions at K increasing complexity levels (e.g., 1-word to 8-word captions for CC12M, or progressively longer DCI sentences up to 100 words). For each complexity level, it generates N_gen synthetic images per prompt using a T2I model, where N_gen matches the cardinality of the smallest aligned real-image set across complexities, ensuring fair real-vs-synthetic comparison. Outputs include reference-free utility scores (Vendi diversity, aesthetic quality, DSG consistency) and reference-based distributional metrics (FDD, precision, density, coverage in DINOv2 feature space).

## Method

The paper contributes three interlocking components. First, a synthetic experiment on a four-Gaussian mixture with conditional U-Net diffusion models proves that generalizing to general prompts (an OR operator over fine-grained conditions) is fundamentally harder than generalizing to fine-grained prompts (an AND operator), because the OR case requires a likelihood weighting p_θ(x_t|c_f^i) that diffusion models do not learn, while the AND case reduces to a sum of score functions analogous to CFG. Second, a novel five-step benchmarking framework (captioning via Gemma-3, pairing via SigLIP embeddings, iterative alignment across complexities, uniform sampling, and generation) enables apples-to-apples comparison of real and synthetic data utility at each complexity level. Third, a large-scale empirical study evaluates six T2I models (LDMv1.5, LDM-XL, LDMv3.5M, LDMv3.5L, Flux-schnell, Infinity) under five inference-time interventions (CFG, CADS, interval guidance, APG, and prompt expansion via Gemma-3) across three datasets, revealing that increasing prompt complexity reduces diversity and consistency while improving distributional fidelity, that diversity plateaus rather than collapses at ~30 words, and that combining APG with prompt expansion yields the best utility trade-offs.

## Results

Across CC12M prompts of increasing complexity (1–8 words), diversity (Vendi score) drops from 9.23 to 6.48 for LDMv1.5+CFG and from 5.60 to 3.97 for LDMv3.5L+CFG, while prompt expansion recovers diversity to 15.07 and 14.67 respectively at the lowest complexity—surpassing real-data diversity. Aesthetic quality remains relatively stable for shorter prompts (LDMv3.5L+CFG: ~5.0–5.2) but prompt expansion consistently boosts it (up to 5.24 for LDMv3.5L). Consistency (DSG) declines monotonically with prompt length for all models (e.g., LDMv3.5L+CFG: 0.92→0.76), and reference-based FDD is lower for LDMv1.5 than LDMv3.5L, indicating the older model better preserves distributional fidelity despite worse reference-free quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDMv3.5L outperforms LDMv1.5 on reference-free quality (aesthetic ~5.0–5.2 vs. ~4.1–4.4) and consistency (DSG 0.92 vs. 0.90 at c1), but LDMv1.5 achieves substantially higher diversity (Vendi 9.23 vs. 5.60 with CFG) and lower FDD, indicating better distributional fidelity to real data. Among advanced guidance methods, Interval guidance yields the largest diversity boost over vanilla CFG (e.g., +6.3 Vendi for LDMv3.5L at c1), while APG provides the best balance of diversity and quality. Prompt expansion (using Gemma-3-12B) consistently outperforms all guidance-only interventions on both diversity and aesthetic score, reaching Vendi scores above real data at low complexities, but at the cost of ~0.07–0.10 DSG consistency drop. Combining APG with prompt expansion achieves the most favorable multi-axis trade-off, boosting diversity further while maintaining competitive quality and FDD. Flux-schnell performs comparably to LDMv3.5L with a small gap in diversity and consistency, while Infinity (autoregressive) shows the lowest diversity (~2 effective distinct images) but the highest consistency among all evaluated models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic data utility evaluation literature (Astolfi et al., 2024; Hall et al., 2024c; Lee et al., 2023) by introducing prompt complexity as a systematic evaluation axis, building on the compositional generation theory of Liu et al. (2022) and Du et al. (2023) to derive why generalizing to general (OR) prompts is fundamentally harder than to specific (AND) prompts in diffusion models. It positions itself against the growing body of inference-time interventions (CFG, APG, CADS, interval guidance, prompt expansion) by studying how these methods interact with prompt complexity rather than treating prompts as fixed inputs. The evaluation framework departs from prior fixed-prompt-set evaluations by constructing matched real-image sets across complexity levels, enabling direct real-vs-synthetic comparisons that were previously non-trivial.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that diversity plateaus rather than collapses with increasing prompt length suggests an inherent 'lower bound of diversity' in T2I models, with practical implications for synthetic data generation pipelines. The observation that the older LDMv1.5 achieves better distributional fidelity (FDD) than the newer LDMv3.5L despite inferior reference-free quality metrics challenges the assumption that model progress uniformly improves synthetic data utility, urging cautious use of T2I-generated data for downstream training. Open questions include how to handle very general prompts where the likelihood weighting (Equation 1) is intractable, and whether the diversity gap between synthetic and real data can be closed without sacrificing consistency or distributional fidelity.
