## Problem

Existing AI Scientist systems generate novel findings but lack goal-orientation, often producing naive recombinations of existing techniques that lack genuine scientific value under human evaluation. No prior system has demonstrated the ability to conduct sustained, goal-directed research over month-long timelines on modern, resource-intensive AI tasks, progressively surpassing strong human-designed SOTA baselines through autonomous methodological redesign rather than incremental engineering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261636/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a human-verified SOTA codebase, its evaluation benchmark script (e.g., Who&When, MBPP, RAID), and a task description, all pre-loaded into a sandboxed Docker environment. Over month-long runs on 16 H800 GPUs, it produces a structured funnel of outputs: ~5,000 generated hypotheses, ~1,100 experimentally validated implementations, 21 SOTA-surpassing Progress Findings, and 5 fully written research papers with LaTeX source, figures, and bibliography. Each finding record in the persistent Findings Memory stores the hypothesis, implementation details, evaluation metrics, and full execution logs for both successes and failures.

## Method

DeepScientist formalizes scientific discovery as a goal-driven Bayesian Optimization problem over a space of candidate research programs, using a persistent Findings Memory as the shared knowledge substrate. The core loop is a three-stage cycle: (1) Strategize & Hypothesize, where a ScientistAgent analyzes the Findings Memory to identify limitations and an ExplorerAgent generates new hypotheses, each scored by a surrogate LLM into a valuation vector V = ⟨v_u, v_q, v_e⟩ (utility, quality, exploration); (2) Implement & Verify, where a UCB acquisition function selects the highest-scoring hypothesis for resource-intensive validation by a sandboxed Claude-4-Opus coding agent that modifies the SOTA codebase and runs the benchmark; (3) Analyze & Report, triggered only on success, where specialized agents design ablation experiments, synthesize results, and write a full paper via MCP tools. The key architectural insight is that the Findings Memory—accumulating both successes and failures across thousands of cycles—enables the system to progressively deepen its search, turning each breakthrough into a new starting point for identifying the next limitation, rather than performing undirected trial-and-error around a single idea.

## Results

Across three frontier AI tasks, DeepScientist surpasses human SOTA by 183.7% (step-level accuracy on Who&When algorithm-generated), 1.9% (tokens/second on MBPP), and 7.9% (AUROC on RAID), while also halving inference latency for AI text detection (117ms → 60ms). The system generated over 5,000 unique ideas, experimentally validated ~1,100, and produced 21 Progress Findings that ultimately yielded 5 publishable papers. On the ALGOTUNE benchmark (154 algorithmic tasks), the lightweight Micronano-DeepScientist variant outperforms baselines on 77.9% of tasks with a 16.6× average speedup. In automated peer review, DeepScientist's papers achieve a 60% simulated acceptance rate—the only system to do so—versus 0% for all other AI Scientist systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Agent Failure Attribution, A2P dramatically outperforms the ICML 2025 Spotlight method 'All at Once' by 142.8% (handcraft) and 183.7% (algorithm-generated) step-level accuracy, reframing the task from pattern recognition to structured causal inference. On LLM Inference Acceleration, ACRA edges out the ACL 2025 Outstanding method Token Recycling by 1.9% throughput (193.90 vs. 190.25 tokens/sec on MBPP) by exploiting stable suffix patterns rather than local Markov transitions. On AI Text Detection, PA-TDT surpasses the ICLR 2024 method Binoculars by 7.9% AUROC (0.863 vs. 0.800) while nearly halving latency (60ms vs. 117ms), shifting from global distributional statistics to time-frequency signal analysis. In paper-quality comparisons against AI Scientist, AI Scientist-v2, CycleResearcher, Zochi, and AI-Researcher, DeepScientist achieves the highest overall rating (5.90/10) and the only non-zero accept rate (60%) under DeepReviewer, and consistently ranks first or second across five independent automated reviewer systems (o3-mini, Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-4o, GPT-5, CycleReviewer). Human expert review (3 ICLR-experienced reviewers) gives DeepScientist's papers an average rating of 5.00, closely matching the ICLR 2025 submission average of 5.08, with two papers scoring 5.67.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepScientist extends the automated scientific discovery line (AI Scientist, AI Scientist-V2, Zochi, AI-Researcher) by departing from their 'one-shot pipeline' or 'single-idea trial-and-error' paradigms and instead formalizing discovery as a goal-driven Bayesian Optimization problem over a persistent Findings Memory. Unlike prior systems that operate on synthetic or narrowly scoped problems without strong human baselines, DeepScientist targets real frontier AI tasks (ICML/ACL/ICLR SOTA methods) and uses a UCB acquisition function to balance exploitation and exploration across thousands of structured records. The three-stage iterative workflow (Strategize & Hypothesize, Implement & Verify, Analyze & Report) with a surrogate model for low-cost hypothesis valuation is the core architectural departure from prior AI Scientist systems, which lack a principled mechanism for learning from accumulated failures over long horizons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale empirical demonstration that an autonomous system can progressively surpass human SOTA on multiple complex AI tasks, with the striking two-week vs. three-years compression on AI text detection suggesting a potential paradigm shift in research throughput. However, the 0.4% idea-to-progress rate (21/5000+) and the finding that 60% of failures stem from implementation errors rather than flawed hypotheses reveal that the bottleneck has shifted from ideation to reliable execution and validation. Open questions include whether the near-linear compute scaling holds beyond 16 GPUs, whether the Bayesian Optimization framing generalizes to domains with slower feedback loops (e.g., wet-lab science), and how to close the gap between the system's strong ideation and its weaker experimental rigor as noted by human reviewers.
