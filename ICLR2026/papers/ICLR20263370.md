## Problem

Diffusion language models (DLMs) suffer from high inference latency due to the quadratic complexity of bidirectional attention over all query-key pairs. While sparse attention is well-established for autoregressive models, existing methods are incompatible with DLMs because attention patterns in DLMs are head-specific (not fixed), temporally stable across denoising steps, and critically sensitive in early steps—properties that fixed-pattern or AR-derived sparse methods fail to handle.

## Input / Output

At each denoising step, SparseD receives Q, K, V matrices (each l×d) per attention head for a masked sequence of length l (prompt tokens + [MASK] generation tokens), along with hyperparameters skip%, ρ%, and block_size. It produces the standard attention output (l×d) that feeds the denoised sequence, plus a per-head sparse mask M_S of size (l/block_size)×(l/block_size) boolean entries, computed once at step T×skip% and reused for all subsequent steps. The block-wise granularity (rather than token-level) is what enables hardware-friendly execution via FlexAttention.

## Method

SparseD combines three mechanisms grounded in empirical observations of DLM attention: (1) Skipping Sparse—full attention (FlashAttention) is applied during the first T×skip% denoising steps because early steps are critical for generation quality; (2) Sparse Reusing—at step T×skip%, attention scores are computed once per head, top-ρ% blocks are selected via average-pooled scores, and the resulting mask M_S is reused for all remaining T×(1−skip%) steps without recomputation; (3) Isolated Selection—important blocks are selected separately for prefill and generation tokens in the key dimension (each receiving ρ% of their respective blocks) to prevent the selection from being dominated by high-scoring prefill tokens. The block-wise selection (avgpool over block_size×block_size regions) keeps memory at O(l×block_size) rather than O(l²), and the final sparse attention is executed via FlexAttention with the precomputed mask.

## Results

SparseD achieves near-lossless accuracy on both evaluated DLMs: only a 0.04% average drop on Dream-7B-Instruct (72.38 vs. 72.42) and a 0.29% average improvement on LLaDA-1.5 (67.62 vs. 67.33) across MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, RULER-4k, and RULER-8k. On latency, SparseD delivers up to 1.50× speedup over FlashAttention at 64k context length with 1,024 denoising steps (1.23× and 1.25× at 128 steps on Dream-7B-Instruct and LLaDA-1.5, respectively). The speedup scales with the number of diffusion steps because sparse patterns are pre-computed once and reused across all subsequent steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original FlashAttention baseline, SparseD matches accuracy (≤0.04% drop) while achieving 1.23–1.50× speedup at 64k context, confirming lossless acceleration. Against AR-derived sparse methods (Slide Window and StreamingLLM), SparseD wins decisively: on Dream-7B-Instruct it scores 72.38 vs. 48.84 (Slide Window) and 50.17 (StreamingLLM) average, and on LLaDA-1.5 it scores 67.62 vs. 44.89 and 45.94, respectively—gaps of 20+ points driven primarily by catastrophic RULER-8k failures of the fixed-pattern methods (34–36% vs. 72.47%). Against cache-based DLM accelerators, SparseD shows clear advantages in long-context: on RULER-8k, SparseD achieves 72.47 vs. 55.08 (dKV-Cache) and 55.64 (Fast-dLLM) on Dream-7B-Instruct, and 62.44 vs. 57.11 and 47.76 on LLaDA-1.5, while cache-based methods suffer ~16% accuracy reduction. On short-context tasks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval), all methods perform comparably, with SparseD matching or slightly exceeding the original model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the sparse attention paradigm well-established for autoregressive (AR) models—where fixed patterns like sliding windows and attention sinks work well—and adapts it to diffusion language models (DLMs), which exhibit fundamentally different attention structures. The three empirical observations (head-specific patterns, temporal stability across denoising steps, and criticality of early steps) motivate a design that departs from AR sparse attention in three ways: per-head pattern selection instead of fixed patterns, one-time computation with reuse instead of per-step recomputation, and a skip-early-steps strategy. On the DLM efficiency side, the paper positions itself against cache-based methods (dKV-Cache, Fast-dLLM) that achieve speedups at the cost of accuracy degradation, whereas SparseD targets lossless acceleration through attention sparsification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As DLMs gain traction as an alternative to ARs, their quadratic attention cost becomes a practical bottleneck for long-context deployment; SparseD provides the first sparse attention method purpose-built for DLMs that preserves accuracy while delivering up to 1.50× speedup at 64k context. The paper identifies an open incompatibility between sparse attention and KV-cache acceleration (Table 7 shows a 13-point accuracy drop when combining them), leaving a clear gap for future work on unified DLM inference pipelines. System-level optimizations for head-specific sparse kernels and generalization to other DLM architectures (e.g., continuous-space or flow-matching variants) remain unexplored.
