## Problem

Existing LLM-based multi-agent embodied planners handle uncertainty in partially observable, decentralized environments primarily through repeated natural-language communication, incurring high token and time costs and disrupting human workflows. Although LLMs implicitly generate assumptions about uncertain environment states in their reasoning traces, these assumptions are invoked locally and never aggregated for global decision-making, preventing systematic reconciliation of competing hypotheses. The paper asks whether these latent assumptions can be extracted, structured, and jointly evaluated to enable rational action selection without heavy inter-agent communication.

## Input / Output

The Planner receives the common goal, current task progress, truncated message logs (K_message=3), recent action history (K_action=10), and an available action list annotated with physical distances to target objects. The Composer transforms the Planner's free-form reasoning trace into a binary decision tree of maximum depth D=3, where internal nodes are True/False assumption splits and leaves are concrete actions copied verbatim from the available action list (or a [send_message] action). The Evaluator outputs a normalized [0,1] score per leaf path combining scenario likelihood, conditional gain, and execution cost, from which the highest-scoring leaf determines the final action—either a physical interaction or a communication act.

## Method

PCE is a three-stage pipeline that converts fragmented LLM reasoning into structured uncertainty-aware planning. The Planner produces a candidate action with a chain-of-thought trace in which each action is grounded in a single implicit environmental assumption. The Composer semantically parses this trace to identify key uncertainties, then expands a binary decision tree top-down using a local ranking policy that prioritizes assumptions most reducing uncertainty and most influencing action choice; when no suitable assumptions remain, it generates new ones grounded in context entities, and expansion stops at depth D or when further splits would not change the action. The Evaluator scores each root-to-leaf path with three LLM-estimated quantities—scenario likelihood L(S), conditional gain G(a), and execution cost C(a) decomposed into movement distance and message length via indicator functions—then computes a final utility U(S,a) = L(S)·G(a) − λC(a) to rank paths. Crucially, communication is treated as an atomic action within the search space rather than the search mechanism itself, so the agent selects dialogue only when its expected utility exceeds that of acting alone, shifting the paradigm from communication-centric coordination to structured reasoning over the agent's own belief state.

## Results

On C-WAH, PCE achieves the lowest Total Steps across all three LLM backbones: 42.76 (GPT-4o mini), 49.60 (GPT-OSS:20B), and 59.20 (Gemma3:4B), outperforming the best baseline (REVECA) by 4.04, 4.26, and 3.36 steps respectively. On TDW-MAT, PCE attains the highest Total transport rate: 87.50% (GPT-4o mini), 81.25% (GPT-OSS:20B), and 70.83% (Gemma3:4B), exceeding the next-best baseline by 6.25, 7.92, and 3.33 percentage points. PCE also uses fewer communication actions (1.70–3.58 vs. 4.04–108.92 for baselines) while maintaining comparable or lower total token usage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCE outperforms all four communication-centric multi-agent baselines (CoELA, REVECA, CaPo, CoTS) on both C-WAH and TDW-MAT across all three LLM backbones. On C-WAH, PCE reduces Total Steps by 29–42% relative to CoELA and CaPo, and by 8–12% relative to REVECA and CoTS, while also cutting communication actions by 3–5×. On TDW-MAT, PCE achieves 6–36 percentage-point higher Total transport rates than the baselines, with the largest gap over CoELA (e.g., 87.50% vs. 62.50% with GPT-4o mini). Against reasoning-enhancement baselines (CoT, ToT, Self-Consistency) evaluated in the C-WAH ablation, PCE completes tasks in 42.76 steps versus 50.96–55.40 steps, while using roughly 3× fewer tokens than ToT and SC. Finally, PCE outperforms the MCTS-based Hierarchical Planner (MHP) from the Watch-and-Help challenge by 34% in Total Steps (42.76 vs. 64.90), demonstrating that semantic uncertainty reasoning surpasses purely topological search in partially observable settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant communication-centric paradigm in LLM-based multi-agent embodied planning (CoELA, REVECA, CaPo, CoTS), where uncertainty is resolved through repeated inter-agent dialogue. Conceptually, it repurposes tree-structured reasoning (à la Tree of Thoughts) but redirects the tree from reasoning steps to environmental assumptions, treating communication as an atomic action within the search space rather than the search mechanism itself. The DEC-POMDP formulation and modular agent architecture follow prior work, but the Planner-Composer-Evaluator pipeline that extracts, structures, and scores implicit assumptions from CoT traces is a new architectural contribution not present in any single prior system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured uncertainty handling is additive to and distinct from LLM scaling, challenging the implicit assumption that larger models or deeper reasoning chains will eventually resolve partial-observability challenges. This opens a practical path for deploying embodied agents in settings where communication is costly or disruptive (e.g., human-in-the-loop scenarios), as confirmed by the user study. Limitations include evaluation only on simulated household tasks with up to 4 agents, LLM-based (rather than truly probabilistic) scoring, and reliance on the quality of the Planner's initial reasoning trace. Open questions include scalability to larger heterogeneous teams, dynamic environments with rapidly changing assumptions, and whether the decision-tree structure generalizes to non-household domains.
