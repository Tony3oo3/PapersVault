## Problem

Existing speech-to-speech (S2S) models fail to adjust their responses based on paralinguistic cues such as emotion, sarcasm, age, and gender, producing tone-deaf replies that ignore how something is said. No benchmark directly evaluates S2S models' response speech for paralinguistic awareness, and the scarcity of high-quality, expressive paired demonstrations makes it difficult to train such capabilities via supervised fine-tuning alone.

## Input / Output

The S2S model receives a user speech waveform (with paralinguistic attributes like emotion, sarcasm, age, or gender) and generates a response speech waveform that should be appropriate in both content and speaking style. The automatic judge consumes both input and output waveforms, projecting them into text via Whisper-V3 transcription, PolyTone-trained captioners for discrete style labels, and AudioReasoner for natural-language tone descriptions, then outputs a single 1–5 Likert naturalness score. During RL training, only the raw speech prompts are used (all style labels are discarded), making the system applicable to unlabeled audio.

## Method

ParaS2S comprises a benchmark (ParaS2SBench) and an RL alignment framework (ParaS2SAlign). The benchmark pairs each query with two contrasting speaking styles over paralinguistically neutral content, forcing models to attend to vocal cues rather than text. The automatic judge decouples paralinguistic analysis from linguistic content via PolyTone training—fine-tuning captioners on utterances that share identical text but differ in style—then feeds extracted labels and tone descriptions to a text LLM for scoring, outperforming end-to-end ALLM judges by >15% in human correlation. For alignment, the pipeline judge is distilled into a compact reward model (Qwen2.5-Omni + LoRA) trained on 320k scored prompt-response pairs, then used to optimize Kimi-Audio via GRPO with KL regularization on unlabeled speech. A brief SFT warmup (10 hours of demonstrations) is essential to give the base model preliminary paralinguistic awareness so that RL can sample diverse, scored responses; the resulting model achieves >10% relative improvement over SFT while requiring five times fewer demonstrations.

## Results

On ParaS2SBench, Kimi-Audio + GRPO achieves an average score of 4.382 (synthetic 4.441, real 4.161), surpassing all existing S2S models and the Whisper-GPT-TTS pipeline baseline (3.176). This represents a >10% relative improvement over SFT alone (3.955) and a 68% improvement over the base Kimi-Audio model (2.350). In cost-controlled experiments, RL with only 20 hours of warm-up demonstrations matches the performance of pure SFT trained on 100 hours of data. The method also preserves general dialogue capabilities on VoiceBench, with the KL-regularized GRPO preventing catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kimi-Audio + GRPO (4.382) outperforms all seven compared S2S models by a wide margin. Against the strongest closed-source baseline, Gemini (3.552), the proposed method leads by 0.83 points; against GPT-4o voice mode (3.403), by 0.98 points. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5 Omni (3.369), LLaMa-Omni 2 (3.291), GLM-4-Voice (3.034), and Freeze-Omni (2.769) are all surpassed by 1.0–1.6 points. The base Kimi-Audio model (2.350) is improved by 2.03 points (68% relative gain) after SFT + GRPO. The only system that exceeds the proposed method is the oracle topline (4.725), which uses ground-truth style labels unavailable at inference time. Notably, all existing S2S models score near the Whisper-GPT-TTS pipeline baseline (3.176), confirming they are largely tone-deaf and fail to adapt responses to contrasting speaking styles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the paralinguistic-aware dialogue line (StyleTalk, ParalinGPT, VoxDialogue, GOAT-SLM) from speech-to-text evaluation to end-to-end speech-to-speech evaluation at the waveform level, and departs from prior S2S benchmarks (VoiceBench, Llama Questions) that focus on QA and instruction-following rather than speaking-style appropriateness. Methodologically, it builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for RL post-training and on the RLAIF paradigm, but is the first to apply scalable AI-feedback RL to S2S models for content-style alignment. A key conceptual departure from prior automatic-evaluation work (Chiang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2026) is the finding that end-to-end ALLMs hallucinate paralinguistic cues from spoken content, motivating the PolyTone decoupling strategy and a multi-stage pipeline judge instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a significant gap: SOTA S2S models (Qwen2.5 Omni, Kimi-Audio, GPT-4o Voice) perform no better than a naive Whisper-GPT-TTS pipeline on paralinguistic-aware dialogue, suggesting current training data and objectives do not capture input-output style dynamics. The demonstration that RL with an automatic judge achieves comparable performance to SFT with 5× more demonstrations addresses the critical data-scarcity bottleneck for expressive speech data. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-turn conversations, non-English languages, and finer-grained paralinguistic dimensions (prosody, speaking rate), and whether the pipeline judge's reliance on separate specialized components can be unified without reintroducing hallucination.
