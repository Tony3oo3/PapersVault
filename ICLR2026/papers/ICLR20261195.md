## Problem

Online learning in Stackelberg games with side information under bandit feedback (where only the follower's action is revealed, not its type) previously achieved only Õ(T^{2/3}) regret, a gap from the Õ(T^{1/2}) rate attainable under full feedback. This paper closes that gap, matching known lower bounds up to logarithmic factors, and further handles the case where follower utilities depend on the context—an assumption prior algorithms could not accommodate.

## Input / Output

Each round the leader observes a context vector z_t ∈ R^d and commits to a mixed strategy x_t over a finite action set A_l; the follower of unknown type f_t ∈ [K] best-responds, and the leader receives only their own realized scalar utility u(z_t, a_{l,t}, a_{f,t}) as bandit feedback. The key structural constraint is that the leader's expected utility is non-linear in x_t (due to the follower's best-response), so the algorithm operates in a derived 'utility space' U_t = {u(z_t, x) : x ∈ E_t} where E_t is a finite set of δ-approximate extreme points of contextual best-response regions, making the problem linear in the utility vector.

## Method

The core contribution is a reduction from contextual Stackelberg learning to linear contextual bandits in the leader's utility space: the leader's utility decomposes as u(z_t, x_t, b_{f_t}(z_t, x_t)) = ⟨u(z_t, x_t), 1_{f_t}⟩, which is linear in the K-dimensional utility vector u(z_t, x_t). Algorithm 1 restricts the leader to the finite set E_t of approximate extreme points (of size O((KA_f²)^{A_l} A_f^K)), maps them to utility vectors U_t, and delegates action selection to an off-the-shelf linear contextual bandit algorithm that recommends v_t ∈ U_t; the leader then inverts the mapping to recover the mixed strategy x_t inducing v_t. For stochastic follower types (adversarial contexts), the bandit algorithm is OFUL, which learns the unknown follower-type distribution p*; for adversarial follower types (stochastic contexts), it is a rescaled logdet-FTRL variant that handles adversarial losses. This yields Õ(T^{1/2}) regret in both settings, and the framework extends to unknown leader utilities (under a linearity assumption), second-price auctions with side information, and online Bayesian persuasion with public and private states.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is improving the regret bound for bandit learning in Stackelberg games with side information from the previously best-known Õ(T^{2/3}) to Õ(T^{1/2}), matching known lower bounds up to logarithmic factors. Specifically, Theorem 3.1 achieves O(K√T log T) regret (adversarial contexts, stochastic followers via OFUL) and Theorem 3.2 achieves O(K^{2.5}√T log T) regret (stochastic contexts, adversarial followers via logdet-FTRL). Extensions to unknown leader utilities add polynomial factors in d, A_l, A_f (Corollaries 3.5–3.6), and the same rates carry over to second-price auctions and Bayesian persuasion with side information (Corollaries 4.2–4.6). Empirically, Algorithm 1-OFUL significantly outperforms both a random baseline and Harris et al.'s Barycentric Explore-Then-Commit over T=2000 rounds in synthetic settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary theoretical comparison is against Harris et al. (2024), whose Barycentric Explore-Then-Commit algorithm achieves Õ(T^{2/3}) regret under bandit feedback. This paper's Algorithm 1 improves this to Õ(T^{1/2}), closing the gap to the known lower bound. Empirically (Figure 2a, T=2000 rounds, 5 follower types, 3 actions, d=3), Algorithm 1-OFUL achieves substantially lower cumulative regret than both Harris et al.'s Algorithm 3 and a random baseline, with the gap widening over time. In the more general setting where follower utilities depend on context (Figure 2b, T=2000 rounds, 4 follower types, 4 actions, d=4), Harris et al.'s algorithm is entirely inapplicable, and Algorithm 1-OFUL again significantly outperforms the random baseline. The paper also notes that its algorithm handles the case where follower utilities depend on side information, an assumption Harris et al. (2024) does not make, making the comparison strictly broader in scope.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Harris et al. (2024), which introduced online learning in Stackelberg games with side information and achieved Õ(T^{2/3}) regret under bandit feedback. The key conceptual departure is a reduction to linear contextual bandits in the leader's 'utility space': rather than optimizing over the non-linear strategy space, the algorithm operates on the K-dimensional vector of utilities the leader would receive against each follower type, where the reward becomes linear. This is conceptually related to Bernasconi et al. (2023), who used a similar utility-space reduction for non-contextual settings, but here the action set is time-varying and either contexts or follower types are adversarial, requiring two distinct linear contextual bandit algorithms (OFUL and logdet-FTRL). The paper also extends the framework to second-price auctions and Bayesian persuasion with side information, settings the authors claim to study for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Closing the gap from Õ(T^{2/3}) to Õ(T^{1/2}) regret (matching lower bounds up to logarithmic factors) resolves a key open question in online Stackelberg learning and provides a clean, reusable reduction template for other strategic settings with finite opponent types. The utility-space linearization is elegant and general, but the worst-case per-round runtime is exponential in problem size, and the unknown-utility extension does not carry over to the auction and persuasion applications. Open questions include whether smoothed analysis can yield practical polynomial runtimes, and whether the unknown-utility framework can be extended to the other application domains.
