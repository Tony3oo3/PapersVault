## Problem

While sub-billion-parameter models like Qwen3-0.6B have demonstrated that reasoning can emerge in small architectures, the prevailing assumption that such capabilities require pretraining on extremely large corpora (>10T tokens) remains largely unchallenged. This paper asks whether careful data curation and principled mixing can enable strong reasoning in sub-billion models using a fraction of the standard token budget, and how to identify which data sources and proportions are most effective without access to downstream benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts and generates text responses that include extended chain-of-thought reasoning traces, with a 32k-token context window for the reasoning SFT stage. For on-device deployment, models are quantized to 8-bit dynamic activations and 4-bit weights (group size 128, or 32 for the 140M variant), and are benchmarked on a Samsung Galaxy S22 via ExecuTorch. The training pipeline consumes curated open-source text corpora (~2T unique tokens resampled to 4.2T total) across pretraining, mid-training, and post-training stages, with the final model producing multi-step reasoning sequences incorporating reflection and self-validation.

## Method

The core contribution is a benchmark-free, self-evolving data optimization framework spanning three training stages. During pretraining, a leave-one-out ablation identifies each dataset's per-token contribution to code, math, and knowledge capabilities via NLL on hierarchically rejection-sampled capability-probing sets (~10K examples each). An extended AutoMixer influence-score computation then derives closed-form dataset-level mixing weights by aggregating self- and cross-capability influences across 10 evenly spaced checkpoints, weighted linearly by training step. During mid-training, a data-model co-evolution loop iteratively filters samples with non-positive influence scores and re-weights dataset proportions using the current model's influence estimates, converging when most samples reach zero or negative influence. The architecture follows MobileLLM/LLaMA3.2 with input-output embedding weight sharing and QK-norm, trained with Adam (β1=0.9, β2=0.95) and linear LR decay, using knowledge distillation from LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct during mid-training.

## Results

MobileLLM-R1-950M, trained on only 4.2T tokens (11.7% of Qwen3's 36T corpus), achieves 74.0 on MATH500, 67.5 on GSM8K, 15.5 on AIME'24, and 19.9 on LiveCodeBench-v6. Among fully open-source models, it attains 5× higher MATH accuracy than OLMo-2-1.48B (74.0 vs. 19.2) and 2× higher than SmolLM2-1.7B (74.0 vs. 19.2), while significantly outperforming both on code benchmarks despite having fewer parameters. On LiveCodeBench, MobileLLM-R1-950M (19.9) matches DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (19.9) and surpasses Qwen3-0.6B (14.9), while MobileLLM-R1-360M (5.1) outperforms even 1B+ models like SmolLM2-1.7B (4.4) and Gemma3-1B (2.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen3-0.6B (596M, trained on 36T tokens), MobileLLM-R1-950M (949M, 4.2T tokens) wins on MATH500 (74.0 vs. 73.0), AIME'24 (15.5 vs. 11.3), and LCBv6 (19.9 vs. 14.9), but trails on GSM8K (67.5 vs. 79.2) and AIME'25 (16.3 vs. 17.0). Against fully open-source baselines, MobileLLM-R1-950M dominates: it achieves 74.0 on MATH500 versus 19.2 for both OLMo-2-1.48B and SmolLM2-1.7B, 15.5 on AIME'24 versus 0.6 and 0.3 respectively, and 19.9 on LCBv6 versus 0.0 and 4.4. Against LLaMA3.2-1B-Instruct and Gemma-3-1B-it, MobileLLM-R1-950M leads on every benchmark (e.g., MATH500: 74.0 vs. 24.8 and 45.4; LCBv6: 19.9 vs. 4.1 and 2.0). Against the larger DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, MobileLLM-R1-950M matches on LCBv6 (19.9 vs. 19.9) but falls behind on MATH500 (74.0 vs. 83.2), GSM8K (67.5 vs. 77.3), and AIME'24 (15.5 vs. 29.1). The 360M variant also stands out, achieving 5.1 on LCBv6—surpassing all models above 1B except Qwen3-1.7B—while scoring 28.4 on MATH500 versus 3.4 for SmolLM2-360M-Instruct.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AutoMixer influence-function framework (Chang et al., 2025) from single-domain data mixing to a cross-capability self-influence formulation that jointly weights datasets by their contribution to code, math, and knowledge. Architecturally, it builds on MobileLLM (Liu et al., 2024) and LLaMA3.2, while methodologically it departs from the OLMo/SmolLM line of fully open-source pretraining by introducing a principled, benchmark-free data curation pipeline (leave-one-out ablation + hierarchical rejection sampling + influence-based mixing) rather than relying on heuristic mixture ratios. The post-training stage follows the established SFT-then-reasoning-SFT paradigm popularized by Tulu-3 and the OpenReasoning datasets, and the RL ablation positions the work against the DeepSeek-R1/Qwen3 distillation-from-large-models paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical contribution is a fully reproducible, open recipe (weights, data sources, mixing ratios, hyperparameters) for sub-billion reasoning models that matches Qwen3-0.6B with only 11.7% of its pretraining tokens, directly relevant to on-device deployment where memory and latency are binding constraints. The influence-based data-model co-evolution strategy for mid-training is a reusable technique that could transfer to other model scales and domains. Open questions include whether the specific data curation choices generalize beyond the tested architectures, whether the approach extends to multi-modal or agentic settings, and whether the SFT-over-RL finding for small models holds as model capacity increases. The paper also leaves open whether the observed data efficiency gap is primarily due to curation quality or to the specific two-phase curriculum design.
