## Problem

Existing Schrödinger Bridge Matching and flow matching frameworks model a single stochastic path between two endpoint distributions, making them inherently limited to unimodal transitions and susceptible to mode collapse when the true dynamics involve branched divergence from a common origin to multiple distinct target modes. This paper addresses the gap by formulating and solving the Branched Generalized Schrödinger Bridge problem, which captures population-level splitting into multiple terminal distributions with branch-specific growth and decay of mass.

## Input / Output

The method takes samples from an initial distribution π₀ and K+1 target distributions {π_{1,k}}, along with a data-dependent state cost V_t (LAND or RBF metric) that encodes the feasible data manifold. Target weights w_{1,k}^* are set as the population fraction in each terminal cluster. The output is a set of K+1 branch-specific drift networks and growth networks that, given a single initial sample, generate the full branched evolution via Euler integration, producing reconstructed target distributions and intermediate branched trajectories at any time t∈[0,1].

## Method

BranchSBM formulates the branched transport as a sum of Unbalanced Conditional Stochastic Optimal Control (CondSOC) problems, one per branch, where the primary branch (k=0) starts with weight 1 and K secondary branches start at weight 0, with mass redistributed via learned growth rates constrained to sum to zero. A four-stage training procedure first learns a neural path interpolant minimizing trajectory energy on the data manifold, then trains independent branch-specific drift fields via conditional flow matching, then learns growth networks by jointly minimizing branched energy, weight matching, and mass conservation losses, and finally performs joint drift-growth optimization with a nearest-neighbor reconstruction loss. The key theoretical contribution is proving that the Branched GSB problem decomposes into independent per-branch CondSOC objectives (Proposition 3.2), enabling tractable neural network parameterization of both the velocity and growth fields without requiring intermediate-time supervision.

## Results

On the LiDAR navigation task, BranchSBM reduces W1 from 0.975 to 0.239 and W2 from 1.285 to 0.309 compared to single-branch SBM. On mouse hematopoiesis, BranchSBM achieves W1 of 0.366 at the held-out intermediate time t1 (vs. 0.582 for single-branch SBM) and W1 of 0.210 at the terminal time t2 (vs. 0.940). On the Tahoe-100M perturbation task, BranchSBM reduces W1 from 5.124 to 1.076 (Clonidine, 50 PCs) and from 5.428 to 0.838 (Trametinib, 50 PCs), while also outperforming single-branch SBM at 100 and 150 PCs. On pancreatic β-cell differentiation, BranchSBM trained only on endpoints (t0, t7) produces intermediate trajectories competitive with DeepRUOT and CytoBridge, which are explicitly trained on all intermediate snapshots.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BranchSBM consistently outperforms single-branch SBM (Liu et al., 2023) across all four experimental settings by large margins: W1 improves by ~4× on LiDAR navigation (0.239 vs. 0.975), ~4.5× on mouse hematopoiesis terminal reconstruction (0.210 vs. 0.940), ~4.8× on Clonidine perturbation (1.076 vs. 5.124), and ~6.5× on Trametinib perturbation (0.838 vs. 5.428). The single-branch SBM baseline suffers from mode collapse, reconstructing only the dominant cluster and failing to reach secondary target modes. On pancreatic β-cell differentiation, BranchSBM (trained only on t0 and t7 endpoints) is competitive with DeepRUOT (Zhang et al., 2025c) and CytoBridge (Zhang et al., 2025d), which are explicitly trained on all seven intermediate snapshots: BranchSBM achieves lower W1 at t=2 (7.46 vs. 8.08) and t=7 (6.87 vs. 7.83) but higher W1 at t=1 (11.98 vs. 8.04), indicating that endpoint-only training trades early-time accuracy for late-time fidelity. Overall, BranchSBM wins decisively against single-branch SBM on every metric and dataset, and matches or exceeds snapshot-trained baselines on terminal reconstruction while requiring no intermediate supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Schrödinger Bridge Matching (SBM) and Generalized Schrödinger Bridge Matching (GSBM) frameworks, which model a single stochastic path between two endpoint distributions, to a branched setting where mass from a common source diverges into multiple terminal distributions via learned growth rates. It builds on the stochastic optimal control reformulation of SB (Chen et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2023a), the neural interpolant approach for energy-minimizing paths (Kapuśniak et al., 2024; Neklyudov et al., 2023), and unbalanced optimal transport for mass non-conservation (Chen et al., 2021b; Pariset et al., 2023). The key departure from prior work is the explicit formulation of a Branched Generalized Schrödinger Bridge problem as a sum of Unbalanced Conditional Stochastic Optimal Control objectives, which the authors note has not been previously formulated or solved, distinguishing it from Branching Brownian Motion (Baradat & Lavenant, 2021) that models particle proliferation rather than mass redistribution across trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BranchSBM addresses a concrete limitation of single-path SBM—mode collapse when target distributions are multi-modal—which is critical for biological applications like cell fate bifurcation and perturbation-induced heterogeneity where a homogeneous population splits into distinct fates. The multi-stage training (interpolant → drift → growth → joint) is practical and avoids requiring intermediate-time supervision, making it applicable when only endpoint snapshots are available. However, the pancreatic β-cell results (Table 2) show BranchSBM performing worse than DeepRUOT and CytoBridge at several intermediate time points, suggesting the method may struggle when many branches (K=11) must be learned simultaneously without explicit intermediate supervision. Open questions include how to determine the optimal number of branches, handling of mass destruction (currently modeled via an auxiliary branch), and whether the non-decreasing growth constraint on secondary branches limits expressiveness for systems with transient subpopulations.
