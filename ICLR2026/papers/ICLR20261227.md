## Problem

While distillation has resurged as a prominent pretraining technique in modern LLM families (Llama-3.2, Gemma), its effects on capabilities central to current LLM frontiers—specifically in-context learning and test-time scaling—remain largely unexplored. A fundamental open question is whether distillation gains are merely a proxy for the teacher's access to more data, or whether they reflect unique benefits that persist even when student and teacher are trained on identical data (the IsoData regime).

## Input / Output

The method consumes text sequences from a diverse pretraining corpus (DCLM, GitHub, math, code, scientific literature) alongside soft probability distributions produced by an 8B teacher model over the vocabulary at each token position. The output is a 1B-parameter pretrained language model. A key design detail is the weighted distillation loss (Eq. 2) that interpolates between ground-truth cross-entropy and teacher soft-label cross-entropy via a mixing coefficient α, and the token routing variant that selectively drops the distillation term on the lowest-entropy teacher predictions (e.g., 15% of positions) to preserve induction-head learning.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a systematic empirical and theoretical dissection of distilled pretraining (DPT) through the lens of modern LLM capabilities. Empirically, DPT yields models with markedly stronger test-time scaling (higher pass@k at matched pass@1, matching standard pretraining on 2× data) while simultaneously impairing in-context learning by weakening induction heads—the transformer circuits responsible for copying tokens from context. To isolate the mechanism, the authors build a bigram sandbox where each row of the transition matrix represents a prompt's next-token distribution: distillation accelerates learning of high-entropy rows (diverse completions like 'I work at') with sample complexity O(k log k) versus O(k² log k) for standard training, but provides no benefit—and can inject noise—on low-entropy rows (deterministic copying required by induction heads). This unified explanation accounts for both the diversity gain (better pass@k via richer probability estimation) and the ICL loss (softened supervision on one-hot targets). The paper further proposes token routing—skipping distillation on the 15% lowest-entropy tokens—as a practical mitigation that recovers ICL performance without sacrificing standard benchmark gains.

## Results

On 1B models trained on 125B tokens, distilled pretraining (DPT-90) achieves 26.99% GSM8K pass@16 and 17.40% MATH pass@16, matching or exceeding a standard-pretrained model trained on 2× the data (26.97% and 16.30% respectively), despite having a lower pass@1 (3.11% vs 4.40% on GSM8K). DPT-90 also attains the highest average benchmark score (31.24) across 14 standard tasks, outperforming both SPT (28.00) and SPT-2x (30.00). However, in the IsoData setting (teacher and student both trained on 1T tokens), DPT underperforms SPT on in-context learning tasks such as needle-in-a-haystack (babilong) and counterfactual QA, due to impaired induction head learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard next-token pretraining (SPT) on the same 125B tokens, with a harder baseline of SPT trained on 2× data (250B tokens, 'SPT-2x'). DPT-90 outperforms SPT-2x on pass@16 for GSM8K (26.99% vs 26.97%), MATH (17.40% vs 16.30%), and MBPP (40.88% vs 39.56%), despite using half the training data and having a lower pass@1 on GSM8K (3.11% vs 4.40%). On standard language modeling benchmarks (HellaSwag, TQA, COPA, ARC), DPT-90 also leads SPT-2x, achieving the best average score of 31.24. Against multi-token prediction (MTP, Gloeckle et al. 2024), DPT achieves higher pass@16 at any given pass@1 on GSM8K and MBPP, and matches MTP on MATH, in an IsoData setting where the teacher saw no extra data. However, DPT loses to SPT on in-context learning tasks (babilong needle-in-a-haystack, counterfactual QA) in the IsoData regime, where distillation's softening of low-entropy supervision impairs induction head formation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Hinton et al. (2015) soft-label distillation framework to the modern LLM pretraining setting, building on the bigram sandbox and induction head analysis from Bietti et al. (2023) to isolate the mechanism behind distillation's effects. While prior work (Gemma, Llama-3.2) demonstrated distillation's benefits on standard benchmarks, this paper departs by examining its impact on two underexplored modern paradigms—test-time scaling (pass@k) and in-context learning—and introduces the IsoData setting to disentangle data advantage from distillation-specific effects. It also positions distillation against multi-token prediction (Gloeckle et al., 2024) as an alternative diversity-enhancing pretraining strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs approach the data wall, understanding whether distillation provides intrinsic benefits beyond teacher data access is critical for future pretraining pipelines. The identified trade-off—distillation boosts generation diversity and test-time scaling while impairing induction-head-based in-context learning—gives practitioners a concrete design tension to navigate, and the proposed token-routing mitigation (skipping distillation on low-entropy tokens) is a simple, low-cost fix. Open questions include whether these findings hold at larger scales (the study uses 1B students), how to optimally combine distillation with multi-token prediction or future-aware pretraining, and whether distillation-specific data curation can further close the ICL gap.
