## Problem

Existing queueing bandit frameworks assume homogeneous job contexts or fix a context per queue, failing to capture the heterogeneity of individual jobs in modern service platforms (cloud scheduling, LLM inference, ride-hailing). When jobs carry distinct contextual features, two policies may process jobs in different orders, causing the remaining feature sets in the queue to diverge — a phenomenon the authors call queue state misalignment. This paper introduces contextual queueing bandits and provides the first provable decay rate for queue length regret under such heterogeneous contexts.

## Input / Output

At each discrete time step, the agent observes the set of contextual feature vectors of all remaining jobs in a single queue (each x ∈ ℝ^d with ‖x‖₂ ≤ 1), a possibly new arriving job's feature, and a set of K servers with unknown parameters θ_k* ∈ ℝ^d (‖θ_k*‖₂ ≤ S). The agent outputs a job-server pair (x_t, k_t); the service succeeds with probability μ(x_t^T θ_{k_t}^*) under a logistic model, yielding a binary departure indicator. Performance is measured by queue length regret R_T = E[Q(T) − Q*(T)], the expected difference in queue length at the horizon end between the learned policy and the oracle optimal policy.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a policy-switching queue decomposition combined with a coupling argument to handle queue state misalignment. Queue length regret is decomposed into a telescoping sum over consecutive policy-switching queues (which follow the learned policy up to round t then switch to optimal), and a shared-uniform coupling aligns arrivals and departures so the queue length difference ψ(t,T) ∈ {−1,0,1}. Each term is bounded by the product of an instantaneous departure-rate gap m_t and a long-term queue-state-difference term δ_t. For the stochastic setting, CQB-ε uses a pure-exploration phase (round-robin server assignment) followed by ε-greedy UCB; monotonicity of m_t (nonincreasing) and δ_t (nondecreasing) enables Chebyshev's sum inequality, yielding Õ(T^{−1/4}) regret. For adversarial contexts, CQB-Opt uses pure UCB and a counting version of the elliptical potential lemma to bound the number of 'bad rounds' where the uncertainty term is large, achieving O(log²T) regret via Cauchy-Schwarz.

## Results

Theoretically, CQB-ε achieves a decaying queue length regret bound of Õ(T^{-1/4}) under stochastic contexts, while CQB-Opt achieves O(log²T) under adversarial contexts. Empirically, on random instances (λ=0.7, ε=0.1, K=5, d=5), both algorithms reduce queue length toward the optimal level after an initial exploration phase, whereas the random policy exhibits linear queue growth. On three real-world datasets (MNIST, Heart Disease, In-Vehicle Coupon Recommendation), CQB-ε consistently achieves the lowest average queue length at T=5000 rounds. Performance degrades as K and d increase, and convergence accelerates with larger traffic slack ε, consistent with the theoretical dependence on ε^{-3} and d.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CQB-ε and CQB-Opt against six baselines: a random policy, the oracle optimal policy, CQB-ε-Opt (UCB with uniform random exploration), CQB-TS (Thompson Sampling variant), and Q-UCB / Q-ThS from Krishnasamy et al. (2021). The random policy shows linearly growing queue length, confirming instability without learning. Q-UCB and Q-ThS, which assume a single fixed context (first-in job) and do not handle heterogeneous job features, perform noticeably worse than the proposed contextual algorithms, particularly as the diversity of job features increases. CQB-ε and CQB-Opt both converge to near-optimal queue lengths, with CQB-ε (the two-phase algorithm with pure exploration followed by ε-greedy) achieving the best final queue length on all three real-world datasets. The CQB-TS variant is competitive but slightly behind CQB-ε, while CQB-ε-Opt (which lacks the structured pure-exploration phase) converges more slowly. The gap between the proposed methods and the optimal policy shrinks over time, validating the decaying regret guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the queueing bandits framework (Krishnasamy et al., 2016; 2021), which studies queue length regret for scheduling with unknown service rates, to the setting where individual jobs carry heterogeneous contextual features. It departs from the closest prior work, Kim & Oh (2024), which assigns a fixed context per queue and uses a proxy regret notion, by allowing per-job contexts and directly analyzing queue length regret. The estimation machinery (regularized MLE with confidence radius) is borrowed from logistic bandits (Faury et al., 2020), but the core analytical contribution—the policy-switching queues coupled with a sophisticated coupling argument to handle queue state misalignment—is entirely new and not reducible to standard bandit regret techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to establish a provably decaying queue length regret bound (Õ(T^{-1/4})) in a contextual queueing bandit setting, showing that the queue length gap vanishes as the horizon grows. The decomposition framework separating the short-term effect of a suboptimal job-server choice from its long-term impact on queue state differences is of independent interest and could inform analysis in other sequential decision-making problems with state-dependent action sets. Open questions include establishing matching lower bounds, extending to multiple queues, and incorporating operational constraints such as maximum waiting time. The adversarial-context result (O(log²T)) is more modest but addresses a practically important regime where contexts are not i.i.d.
