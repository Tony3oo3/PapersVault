## Problem

Synthesizability constraints have been successfully incorporated into 2D molecular graph generation via reaction templates, but extending these constraints to 3D geometry-based conditional generation remains largely unexplored. Existing 3D molecular generators produce spatially meaningful conformers without ensuring the molecules can actually be synthesized, while synthesizable molecule generators only output 2D graphs and cannot leverage conformational information critical for binding and property prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264939/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sampled building block count n (from a categorical distribution) and optionally conditioning signals such as pharmacophore types with 3D positions or fixed fragment identities with coordinates for inpainting tasks. It outputs a building block reaction graph where nodes encode building block identities and edges encode specific coupling reactions with reaction-center atom indices, plus 3D Cartesian coordinates for all atoms. The atom count is flexible: when a building block is still masked during generation, its coordinate slot is padded to a maximum M atoms, and the model learns to disregard padding, eliminating the need to pre-specify atom counts as in most 3D generators.

## Method

SYNCOGEN jointly samples from the distribution over building block reaction graphs and atomic coordinates using a unified time parameter that couples a discrete absorbing (masked) diffusion process on the graph (X, E) with a continuous flow-matching process on coordinates C. The backbone is an SE(3)-equivariant architecture adapted from SEMLAFLOW, with stride-pooled 2D convolutions collapsing atom-level features into building-block-level logits. A key technical contribution is the visibility-aware coordinate noising scheme: when building blocks are masked, their atom counts are unknown, so padding atoms are introduced and a visibility mask S_t is applied to both the noisy and ground-truth coordinates to maintain consistent atom counts within each training pair. Chemistry-aware training constraints—including compatibility masking that restricts building block logits to chemically valid reagents given already-denoised edges, edge count limits enforcing exactly n−1 edges for n blocks, and no-edge diagonals—provide the largest performance gains in ablations. The SYNSPACE dataset (1.2M building block reaction graphs with 7.5M conformers) is procedurally generated by iteratively coupling commercially available building blocks via high-yield reaction templates, ensuring every generated molecule has a tractable synthetic route by construction.

## Results

On unconditional 3D molecule generation (Table 1), SYNCOGEN achieves 96.7% RDKit validity, 50% AiZynthFinder solve rate, and 72% Syntheseus solve rate, substantially outperforming all atom-level baselines (best: 93.3% validity, 38% AiZyn, 48% Synth for SEMLAFLOW-SYNSPACE). It also produces the lowest median per-atom GFN-FF non-bonded energy (3.01 vs. 3.27–5.96 for baselines) and the best GFN2-xTB energy (−0.91 vs. −0.68 to −0.80), with a FCD of 2.91. In pharmacophore-conditioned generation across 10 targets, SYNCOGEN achieves the best docking score on 8/10 targets (best sample) and 7/10 (median), while in fragment linking it is the only method yielding synthesizable molecules (58–79% retrosynthesis solve rate vs. 0% for DiffLinker).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SYNCOGEN dominates all atom-and-coordinate baselines (SEMLAFLOW, EQGAT-diff, MiDi, JODO, FlowMol) on synthesizability metrics: its 50% AiZynthFinder and 72% Syntheseus solve rates exceed the best baseline (SEMLAFLOW-SYNSPACE at 27%/48%) by 23 and 24 percentage points, respectively, while also achieving the highest RDKit validity (96.7% vs. 93.3%) and lowest non-bonded interaction energies. The trade-off is modestly lower diversity (0.78 vs. 0.85–0.86) and novelty (93.9% vs. 99.4–99.8%), attributed to the constrained building-block vocabulary. Against the purpose-built fragment-linking model DiffLinker, SYNCOGEN is the only method producing synthesizable linkers (58–79% retrosynthesis solve rate vs. 0% for DiffLinker) with lower interaction energies and no disconnected fragments. In pharmacophore-conditioned generation, SYNCOGEN outperforms ShEPhERD (3D, no synthesis constraint) by 45% in RDKit validity and 25% in PoseBusters validity, and surpasses both SynFormer (2D synthesizable) and CGFlow-ZS (3D + synthesis) by 15–65% in retrosynthesis solve rates while achieving competitive or superior docking scores on 8/10 targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

SYNCOGEN unifies two previously separate research threads: synthesizable 2D molecule generation via building-block reaction graphs (GFlowNets, SynFormer, RGFN) and 3D all-atom molecular generation via equivariant diffusion/flow matching (SEMLAFLOW, EQGAT-diff, MiDi). It adapts the SEMLAFLOW SE(3)-equivariant backbone as its architecture and inherits the masked discrete diffusion machinery (zero-masking, carry-over unmasking) from MDLM, but departs from both by coupling a discrete absorbing process on reaction graphs and a continuous flow on coordinates under a single shared time parameter, rather than treating them as sequential or decoupled stages. The SYNSPACE dataset further distinguishes the work from synthon-based or RDKit-simulator-based approaches by providing explicit atom-mapped reaction graphs with pre-computed 3D conformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first unified, non-autoregressive framework that simultaneously generates 3D molecular geometry and an explicit synthetic route, addressing a critical practical bottleneck in generative drug design where most 3D generators produce molecules that cannot be made in a lab. The SYNSPACE dataset (1.2M molecules, 7.5M conformers) is a reusable resource for the community, and the amortized conditional generation (pharmacophore, fragment linking) without target-specific retraining or external reward models offers a practical path toward self-driving lab integration. Key limitations include the restricted reaction vocabulary (no macrocycles, no protecting groups, at most 4 sequential steps), the absence of experimental validation of generated molecules, and the fact that synthesizability is guaranteed only within the curated building-block space rather than in general chemical space.
