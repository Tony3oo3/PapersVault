## Problem

LLM alignment is brittle: benign fine-tuning can erode prior safety or capability, adversarial fine-tuning with as few as 10 samples destroys all capabilities in a handful of steps, and white-box jailbreaking remains trivial. These phenomena lack a unified geometric explanation that connects parameter-space geometry to both fine-tuning degradation and input-space vulnerability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an LLM's parameter vector θ ∈ ℝ^d, a benchmark dataset D with a 0-1 judgment oracle, and a perturbation variance σ². It produces (i) 2-D and 3-D loss landscape visualizations along random (most-case) and steepest (worst-case) directions, (ii) a closed-form lower bound on post-fine-tuning benchmark score via the strong randomized-smoothing theorem applied in parameter space, and (iii) a pre-trained model whose basin has been enlarged by the GO optimizer. Unusually, the 'loss' is a discrete task-success score (not NLL), and the smoothing noise is added to weights rather than inputs, making the certified radius a parameter-space norm ‖θ_sft − θ₀‖₂.

## Method

The paper first reveals that the most-case loss landscape (along δ ~ N(0,I)) of LLMs evaluated with 0-1 generative benchmarks forms a literal basin: performance is invariant within a radius σ and collapses outside it, with basin size growing with model scale. It then applies randomized smoothing to the parameter space—convolving the benchmark functional S_{f,D}(θ) with N(0,σ²I)—to derive a Lipschitz bound (Theorem 4.3) that certifies a lower bound on post-fine-tuning performance as a function of ‖θ_sft − θ₀‖₂/σ, unifying benign and adversarial fine-tuning under one guarantee. A heuristic embedding-geometry argument (Theorem 4.5) further links parameter-space basin size to input-space robustness against token-substitution jailbreaks. Finally, the GO optimizer enlarges the basin by minimizing the expected cross-entropy over Gaussian-perturbed parameters (L_train = −E_ε[log p(x|θ+ε)]), which empirically translates reduced Gaussian-noise degradation into reduced catastrophic forgetting during downstream SFT, outperforming SAM and Continuous Dropout baselines.

## Results

The paper discovers that LLM loss landscapes (measured via 0-1 generative benchmarks) exhibit basin-like structures: within the basin, benchmark scores remain literally unchanged (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B safety stays at 1.0 up to α=1×10⁻³), while outside, capabilities collapse to near zero. Hypothesis testing with the Clopper-Pearson bound confirms that over 90% of random directions form strict basins at σ=0.01 for Qwen2.5-7B on AdvBench. The proposed GO optimizer, when used for pre-training GPT2-127M, reduces catastrophic forgetting during Alpaca SFT: OWT NLL degrades by only 0.40 (2.97→3.37) versus 0.55 (2.94→3.49) for AdamW, and Lambada PPL drops to 80.1 vs. 94.1, while pre-training NLL remains within 0.03 of AdamW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed GO optimizer against two sharpness-aware baselines: SAM (Foret et al., ICLR 2021) and Continuous Dropout (Srivastava et al., JMLR 2014). On the primary metric of SFT degradation (Fig. 5c), GO achieves the lowest performance drop across all benchmarks, outperforming both SAM-ρ and CDrop-σ, which target worst-case sharpness or act as implicit proxies rather than explicitly optimizing average-case Gaussian resilience. During pre-training (Fig. 5a), GO is initially slower than AdamW but converges to comparable NLL (2.97 vs. 2.94), with the gap narrowing over training. On individual benchmarks after SFT, GO-SFT retains SST-2 at 57.1 vs. 51.7 for AdamW-SFT (+5.4 points) and WinoGrande at 51.1 vs. 49.8 (+1.3), while AdamW-SFT shows larger drops on ARC-E (44.0 vs. 42.8) and ARC-C (23.6 vs. 21.8). The paper also notes that GO introduces beneficial inductive biases beyond basin enlargement, outperforming AdamW on SST-2 (57.7 vs. 51.4) and ARC-C (18.9 vs. 18.2) even before fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes randomized smoothing (Cohen et al., 2019; Salman et al., 2019) from its standard input-space setting to the parameter space of LLMs, providing a certification framework for fine-tuning degradation that unifies benign and adversarial fine-tuning under one bound. It extends the loss landscape analysis of Peng et al. (2024) by switching from NLL-based metrics to 0-1 generative benchmarks, which reveals a discrete basin structure invisible in the smooth likelihood surface. The proposed GO optimizer is positioned as distinct from SAM (which targets worst-case sharpness) and Dropout (an implicit proxy), instead explicitly optimizing average-case Gaussian parameter-space resilience.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a unifying geometric explanation for three previously separate phenomena—benign fine-tuning forgetting, adversarial fine-tuning attacks, and input jailbreaking—via basin size, which could reshape how the field approaches alignment robustness. However, the GO optimizer is validated only on GPT2-127M, the theoretical bounds are explicitly loose, and the embedding-geometry argument connecting parameter-space basins to input-space jailbreaking is acknowledged as heuristic. Key open questions include whether basin enlargement via GO scales to frontier models, whether the over-parameterization explanation for basin formation holds at scale, and how the framework interacts with RLHF-based alignment stages.
