## Problem

Mask-based Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) fix tokens once unmasked, so early errors persist through the entire generation even when later context would reveal them. Existing remedies—random predictor-corrector remasking or modified noise schedules—lack a principled mechanism to identify which specific tokens are wrong, making them inefficient or unable to guarantee monotonic noise reduction. The paper asks: can a DLM be trained to self-reflectively detect and remask its own incorrect tokens during generation?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261937/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RemeDi takes a text prompt and produces a variable-length text response via block-wise generation (32-token blocks, each fully denoised before the next block begins, continuing until an EOS token appears). The unusual aspect is that at each denoising step within a block, the model may both unmask new positions and remask previously unmasked positions, so the intermediate state is not monotonically revealing—tokens can oscillate between masked and unmasked states across steps. A linear noise schedule controls the total number of unmasked tokens, ensuring it reaches L (fully unmasked) at the final step.

## Method

RemeDi extends a standard transformer (initialized from LLaDA-8B) into a dual-stream architecture: a Token Prediction Stream (TPS) predicts token probabilities for masked positions, and a smaller Unmasking Policy Stream (UPS) outputs per-token confidence scores that determine which positions to unmask or remask at each step. Training proceeds in two stages. First, Remask SFT constructs noisy inputs with two noise types—mask tokens and randomly replaced (incorrect) tokens—and trains the model with a combined diffusion loss (on mask positions) and a binary cross-entropy loss on confidence scores (positive for clean tokens, negative for incorrect tokens, soft labels from predicted probabilities for mask tokens). Second, Remask RL applies GRPO over full N-step generation trajectories, where the unmasking policy samples positions via a Plackett-Luce distribution over confidence scores and the token policy samples from TPS predictions, jointly optimizing toward verifiable or model-based rewards. The key design constraint is that the number of mask tokens must decrease monotonically, enforced by a noise schedule where the incorrect-token ratio is bounded relative to the mask ratio.

## Results

RemeDi (++RemaskRL) achieves state-of-the-art results among open-source DLMs across math, code, and general benchmarks: 89.1% on GSM8K, 52.9% on MATH, 73.2% on HumanEval, 59.4% on MBPP, 87.7% on ARC-C, 85.4% on IFEval, and 24.8% on AlpacaEval. After Remask SFT alone, RemeDi already surpasses all compared DLMs on GSM8K (86.3%), MATH (51.4%), HumanEval (71.3%), and MBPP (57.8%). The RL stage provides further gains, most notably a +12.3% improvement on AlpacaEval (12.5% → 24.8%) and +2.8% on GSM8K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RemeDi outperforms all compared DLMs on GSM8K (89.1% vs. LLaDOU's 88.1%), MATH (52.9% vs. LLaDA1.5's 42.6%), HumanEval (73.2% vs. LLaDOU's 59.1%), and MBPP (59.4% vs. LLaDOU's 51.6%), with the largest margin over LLaDA+ReMDM (predictor-corrector remasking) on HumanEval (+28.7%). On general tasks (Table 2), RemeDi leads all DLMs on ARC-C (87.7% vs. LLaDA's 83.9%), IFEval (85.4% vs. LLaDA1.5's 73.5%), and AlpacaEval (24.8% vs. LLaDA1.5's 13.9%). The one benchmark where RemeDi trails other DLMs is GPQA (29.5% vs. LLaDA1.5's 36.9% and Dream's 30.6%). Among AR models of comparable size, RemeDi matches or exceeds DeepSeekMath 7B on GSM8K (89.1% vs. 88.2%) and MATH (52.9% vs. 51.7%), and surpasses LLaMA3 8B on GSM8K, MATH, and HumanEval, though Gemma2 9B retains an edge on MBPP (74.9% vs. 59.4%). The ablation in Table 7 confirms that the learned remask policy substantially outperforms the ReMDM predictor-corrector applied to the same RemeDi model (e.g., 86.3% vs. 58.3% on GSM8K, 71.3% vs. 39.6% on HumanEval).

## Relation to Prior Work

RemeDi builds directly on LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025) as its backbone and adopts the variable-length block-wise generation paradigm from Block Diffusion (Arriola et al., 2025). It departs from prior mask-based DLMs, which fix tokens once unmasked, by introducing a learned remasking policy via a dual-stream architecture (Token Prediction Stream + Unmasking Policy Stream) that outputs per-token confidence scores. Unlike predictor-corrector samplers (ReMDM) that remask randomly at inference, or Seed Diffusion that resamples all tokens without a monotonicity guarantee, RemeDi trains the model to *selectively* identify and remask low-quality tokens through a two-stage pipeline of Remask SFT (with a novel incorrect-token noise schedule) and Remask RL (using GRPO over full generation trajectories).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental structural limitation of mask-based DLMs—the inability to self-correct—by making remasking a learned, confidence-driven operation rather than a random or uncontrolled process. The finding that learned selective remasking substantially outperforms both random remasking (predictor-corrector) and unrestricted resampling (Seed Diffusion, which actually degrades performance) suggests that *which* tokens to revise matters more than *whether* to revise, a principle that could inform future DLM architectures. Open questions include whether the dual-stream architecture is necessary at scale, how the approach interacts with other DLM improvements (e.g., continuous-time formulations), and whether the remasking mechanism transfers to non-text modalities.
