## Problem

Prior work on non-clashing teaching in graphs focused on balls of arbitrary radius, leaving the more general and equally expressive concept class of closed neighborhoods (radius-1 balls) largely unexplored. This paper addresses that gap by providing the first FPT algorithms for closed neighborhoods under structural parameters (treedepth, vertex cover), the first strong algorithmic lower bounds for the general (non-positive) NCTD of balls, and combinatorial upper bounds for planar and unit square graphs that dramatically improve upon the unbounded-by-cycle results for balls.

## Input / Output

The decision problems N-NCTD and N-NCTD+ take as input a graph G, a set B of closed neighborhoods N[v] in G, and a positive integer k, and ask whether the (positive) non-clashing teaching dimension of B is at most k. A key structural property is that this formulation is as general as any finite binary concept class: given C subset of 2^V, one constructs a graph where concept vertices form a clique and are adjacent to domain vertices exactly when the domain element belongs to the concept, making B = {N[x_C] : C in C}. The teaching map T assigns to each N[v] in B a set of at most k labeled vertices, and the non-clashing condition requires that for every pair of distinct concepts, some vertex in their combined teaching sets is consistent with exactly one of them.

## Method

The paper combines three technical pillars. First, 3-SAT reductions yield ETH-based lower bounds: no 2^{o(f(k)*|V|)} algorithm for N-NCTD (Theorem 2) and no 2^{o(f(k)*(|V|+|E|))} algorithm for N-NCTD+ (Theorem 3), the latter matching the 2^{O(|E|)} brute-force upper bound (Theorem 4). Second, for FPT algorithms, N-NCTD+ is solved parameterized by treedepth via a bottom-up pruning of the treedepth decomposition: a reduction rule deletes a connected component when more than (|X|+t)*2^{(|X|+t)^2}*2^{2t+|X|+1} automorphic components exist, using the pigeonhole principle to find three components with identical adjacencies, membership in B, and teaching-set structure, then re-routing teaching-set vertices through automorphic copies (Theorem 5). For N-NCTD parameterized by vertex cover, a kernelization exploits false-twin equivalence classes in the independent set: Reduction Rule 2 deletes surplus false twins whose closed neighborhoods are in B (bounded by 2^{2^{|X|}+|X|}+1 per class), and Reduction Rule 3 deletes false twins whose closed neighborhoods are absent from B, yielding a kernel of size 2^{|X|}*(2^{2^{|X|}+|X|}+2^{|X|}+2|X|)+|X| (Theorem 7). Third, combinatorial arguments give NCTD+ <= 7 and NCTD <= 5 for planar graphs (exploiting K_{3,3}-freeness to bound the number of vertices sharing three common neighbors) and NCTD+ <= 4 for unit square graphs (using the minimum enclosing rectangle of each closed neighborhood to select four extremal vertices).

## Results

The paper establishes tight algorithmic bounds for N-NCTD⁺: a 2^{O(|E(G)|)}-time algorithm (Theorem 4) matching the ETH lower bound of no 2^{o(f(k)·(|V(G)|+|E(G)|))} algorithm (Theorem 3). For N-NCTD, a new ETH lower bound of no 2^{o(f(k)·|V(G)|)} algorithm (Theorem 2) dramatically improves the previous 2^{o(f(k)·√|V(G)|)} bound. Combinatorially, the paper proves NCTD⁺(B) ≤ 7 and NCTD(B) ≤ 5 for planar graphs (Theorems 12–13), and NCTD⁺(B) ≤ 4 for unit square graphs (Theorem 14), all substantially improving prior bounds for balls in graphs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Ganian et al. (ICLR 2025) [1], this paper achieves three key improvements: (i) the FPT parameterization is extended from vertex integrity to the strictly more general treedepth for N-NCTD⁺ (Theorem 5); (ii) the first FPT algorithm for N-NCTD (allowing negative examples) parameterized by vertex cover is given (Theorem 7), a setting where no structural-parameter FPT was previously known; and (iii) the algorithmic upper and lower bounds for N-NCTD⁺ now match under ETH (2^{O(|E|)} vs. 2^{o(f(k)(|V|+|E|))}), whereas [1] had a gap. Compared to Chalopin et al. (COLT 2024) [2], the combinatorial bounds are dramatically improved: for planar graphs, NCTD⁺ drops from unbounded (witnessed by cycles) to ≤ 7, and NCTD drops from ≤ 615 to ≤ 5; for unit square graphs, NCTD⁺ drops from unbounded to ≤ 4. The closed-neighborhood concept class is strictly more general than balls (any finite binary concept class can be represented as closed neighborhoods), so these results subsume and strengthen the prior ball-based bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-clashing teaching framework (Kirkpatrick et al., 2019; Fallat et al., 2023) from the general setting of balls in graphs (Chalopin et al., 2024; Ganian et al., 2025) to the specific concept class of closed neighborhoods (balls of radius 1), showing that this restriction is actually maximally general—any finite binary concept class can be represented as closed neighborhoods in a graph. The algorithmic techniques build on the exact exponential and parameterized algorithms for NON-CLASH from Ganian et al. (2025), but the paper introduces new kernelization and treedepth-based FPT algorithms that were not available for the general balls setting. The combinatorial bounds for planar and unit square graphs leverage forbidden-minor and geometric arguments that go well beyond the cycle-based constructions used for general balls.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tight algorithmic bounds for N-NCTD+ (matching 2^O(|E|) upper and 2^o(f(k)(|V|+|E|))) lower) and the dramatic improvement in planar combinatorial bounds (≤7 vs. ≤615 for general balls) provide a much sharper picture of the complexity landscape for a maximally general concept class. The FPT algorithm parameterized by treedepth is notable because treedepth strictly generalizes vertex integrity (the best known parameter for balls), and the vertex-cover FPT for the negative variant fills a gap where no structural parameterization was previously known. Open questions include whether treewidth or planarity parameterizations exist for N-NCTD+, whether the planar bound of 5 is tight (potentially answering the Kirkpatrick et al. open question about NCTD > VCD), and whether W[1]-hardness holds for treewidth as expected.
