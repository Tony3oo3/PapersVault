## Problem

SAT solver branching heuristics rely on hand-crafted scoring functions that are rigid and difficult to adapt to specific instance distributions without extensive expert tuning. Prior ML-based approaches either require supervised labels (e.g., UNSAT-core or backbone membership) or invoke a GNN at every branching decision, creating a severe runtime bottleneck. This paper asks whether a GNN can be trained purely from the solver's own computational cost as a reward signal to produce effective, distribution-specific branching guidance in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263348/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a CNF formula and encodes it as a Literal-Clause Graph (vertices are literals and clauses; edges connect literals to their clauses and to their negation). A single GNN forward pass produces a variable parameterization W = (w, p) that assigns each variable a positive real weight w(x) drawn from a LogNormal distribution and a binary polarity p(x) drawn from a Bernoulli distribution. At test time, the mode of each distribution is used deterministically, and the resulting weights and polarities are reused at every branching decision throughout the entire search with negligible per-decision overhead.

## Method

The core mechanism is a generic multiplicative-weight injection into existing branching heuristics: the solver's variable score is scaled by a learned weight w(x)·Score(x), and the branching literal's sign is set by a learned polarity p(x), requiring no architectural changes to the base solver beyond a single multiplication. The GNN policy is trained as a single-step MDP where the formula is the state and the full variable parameterization is the action, with reward equal to the negative number of solver decisions. Training uses GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization), which samples M parameterizations per formula, runs the solver in-the-loop for each, computes group-relative advantages from the observed costs, and updates the GNN via clipped policy-gradient steps with a KL penalty. This one-shot design—contrasted with prior per-decision GNN invocation—allows the learned weights to influence every branching decision at essentially zero additional runtime cost (≈0.02–0.1 s GNN overhead per instance).

## Results

RLAF-trained GNN policies reduce mean solver runtime by up to 69% for Glucose on satisfiable 3SAT(400) instances (598.35s → 186.70s) and by 41% on unsatisfiable 3SAT(400) (1895.62s → 1112.71s). For the March solver, improvements reach ~64% on CRYPTO(10) UNSAT (467.38s → 169.22s) and ~37% on 3Col(600) UNSAT (313.57s → 197.12s). The GNN forward-pass overhead is negligible (0.02–0.12s), and policies trained on 200-variable instances generalize to 400-variable 3SAT, 600-vertex coloring, and 10-help-bit cryptographic instances. On SATLIB, RLAF achieves a ~3× higher MRIR than Graph-Q-SAT on 250-variable SAT instances (11.03 vs. 3.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unguided base solvers (Glucose, March), RLAF consistently reduces runtime on 14 of 15 test settings; the sole exception is March on UNSAT 3SAT(400), where the marginal decision-count improvement (245,064 → 242,499) is offset by the GNN forward-pass overhead, yielding a near-tie (27.49s vs. 27.51s). Against supervised UNSAT-core prediction (Selsam & Bjørner, 2019), RLAF achieves lower relative runtime on both 3COL and CRYPTO distributions for both solvers, while the core-based approach is inapplicable to 3SAT (cores contain all variables). Against supervised backbone prediction (Wang et al., 2024), RLAF outperforms on all distributions except satisfiable 3SAT(300), where backbone guidance is marginally better; on UNSAT 3SAT, backbone guidance actually increases March's runtime by ~10%. Against Graph-Q-SAT (Kurin et al., 2020) on SATLIB, RLAF-SATLIB achieves a ~3× higher MRIR on 250-variable SAT instances (11.03 vs. 3.91) but underperforms on 100-variable SAT (3.14 vs. 3.94) and on UNSAT splits, consistent with its training on mixed SAT/UNSAT data. The RLAF-Main policy (trained on 3SAT(250) with Glucose) transfers to MiniSAT and achieves the highest MRIR on 250-variable UNSAT (1.94 vs. 1.54 for Graph-Q-SAT).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learned SAT solver guidance (NeuroSAT, Graph-Q-SAT, Selsam & Bjørner, Wang et al.) by introducing a fundamentally different interface: instead of invoking a GNN at every branching decision or periodically resetting solver scores, a single forward pass assigns multiplicative variable weights and polarities that are injected into the solver's existing scoring function. The training paradigm departs from both supervised prediction of variable properties and per-decision Q-learning by framing the entire weight/polarity assignment as a single-step MDP optimized with GRPO, using solver cost as the sole reward. The GNN architecture and Literal-Clause Graph representation are inherited from NeuroSAT, while the RL algorithm is borrowed from the LLM fine-tuning literature (GRPO), creating a cross-domain methodological bridge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-shot multiplicative-weight mechanism is a practical design pattern that could transfer to other combinatorial solvers (MIP, CSP) where branching heuristics follow the same argmax-of-score pattern, and the demonstration that RL-trained policies outperform supervised baselines challenges the assumption that predicting known variable properties is the right training signal. Key limitations include the reliance on easy training instances due to single-machine compute constraints, the bounded expressivity of standard message-passing GNNs (color refinement), and the absence of integration with state-of-the-art CDCL solvers. Open questions include whether distributed training on harder instances yields meaningfully better policies, whether the approach scales to industrial problem sizes, and whether the cross-solver weight correlation reflects a universal structural signal or is an artifact of the specific problem classes tested.
