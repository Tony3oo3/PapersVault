## Problem

LoRA constrains weight updates to a low-rank subspace, limiting expressivity for tasks requiring high-rank adaptations. HiRA lifts this rank limitation via a Hadamard product with frozen pre-trained weights, but its expressivity remains tightly coupled to W₀, restricting the update to a subspace defined by the pre-trained model. The open question is whether one can achieve high-rank, fully decoupled updates while preserving LoRA's parameter and memory efficiency.

## Input / Output

ABBA takes a frozen pre-trained LLM (Llama-3.2 1B/3B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-2 9B) and inserts adapter modules into attention projections (Q, K, V, output) and feedforward layers, then fine-tunes on task-specific text data (commonsense reasoning, arithmetic word problems, code). At inference, the adapters are merged into the base weights (W' = W₀ + (B₁A₁)⊙(B₂A₂)), so the model produces standard text completions with zero runtime overhead. The trainable output is a set of four low-rank matrices per layer (B₁, A₁, B₂, A₂) totaling (r₁+r₂)(m+n) parameters, matched to LoRA's budget via r₁=r₂=r/2.

## Method

ABBA reparameterizes the weight update as ΔW = (α²/√(r₁r₂))·(B₁A₁)⊙(B₂A₂), where both low-rank factors are independently learnable, fully decoupling the update from the pre-trained weights W₀. A key technical contribution is an exact Khatri-Rao reformulation (Theorem 1) that rewrites the Hadamard product as a LoRA-like matrix-vector product ΔWx = B_kr(A_kr x), avoiding materialization of full m×n matrices and matching LoRA's memory efficiency. Initialization anchors one adapter pair via truncated SVD of W₀ (Eckart-Young-Mirsky optimal rank-r₁ approximation) while the other uses standard LoRA initialization (zeros for B, Kaiming for A), preventing the zero-gradient failure mode of naive LoRA-style init. A rank-stability theorem (Theorem 2) proves the scaling s ∈ Θ(1/√(r₁r₂)) is necessary and sufficient for well-conditioned forward and backward dynamics, and empirical reconstruction experiments confirm ABBA achieves lower error than LoRA at equal parameter budgets across diverse matrix types.

## Results

On commonsense reasoning (COMMONSENSE170K), ABBA at r=32 achieves 75.03% average accuracy on Llama-3.2 1B and 84.08% on Llama-3.2 3B, surpassing full fine-tuning (74.17% and 82.39% respectively) while using only 22.54M and 48.63M trainable parameters. On arithmetic reasoning, ABBA at r=32 reaches 66.26% GSM8K and 18.08% MATH on Mistral-7B, and 79.76% GSM8K and 39.18% MATH on Gemma-2 9B, again exceeding full fine-tuning (63.87%/17.65% and 79.23%/38.02%). Notably, ABBA at r=16 (half the parameter budget) already outperforms all other PEFT methods at full rank, e.g., 73.76% avg on Llama-3.2 1B vs. HiRA's 72.76% at the same 22.54M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABBA consistently outperforms all seven PEFT baselines (LoRA, rsLoRA, PiSSA, DoRA, LoRA-Pro, HiRA) across both model scales and both task families. On Llama-3.2 3B commonsense reasoning, ABBA (r=32) leads the strongest baseline HiRA by 1.52 points (84.08% vs. 82.56%) and LoRA by 2.78 points (84.08% vs. 81.30%). On Mistral-7B arithmetic, ABBA (r=32) beats HiRA by 3.11 points on GSM8K (66.26% vs. 63.15%) and 0.64 points on MATH (18.08% vs. 17.44%). A particularly striking result is that ABBA at r=16 (half the parameter budget of all other methods) already surpasses every full-rank baseline, e.g., 83.68% avg on Llama-3.2 3B vs. HiRA's 82.56% at 48.63M params. ABBA also exceeds full fine-tuning on most benchmarks, suggesting its structured parameterization provides implicit regularization. On the coding task (HumanEval, Llama-3.1 8B), ABBA achieves 49.39% Pass@1, outperforming MoRA (47.28%), HiRA (48.17%), and full FT (48.54%).

## Relation to Prior Work

ABBA directly extends the LoRA → HiRA lineage: LoRA models the weight update as a single low-rank product BA, while HiRA lifts the rank constraint by taking a Hadamard product with the frozen pretrained weights W₀. ABBA's key departure is to replace the frozen W₀ with a second independently learnable low-rank factor, so the update becomes (B₁A₁)⊙(B₂A₂), fully decoupling the update from the pretrained weights. The paper also draws on PiSSA-style truncated-SVD initialization for one adapter pair and introduces an exact Khatri-Rao factorization (Theorem 1) that rewrites the Hadamard product in a LoRA-like matmul form, preserving memory efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ABBA offers a practical, drop-in replacement for LoRA that consistently outperforms all tested PEFT baselines (including HiRA, DoRA, PiSSA, LoRA-Pro) across four model families and three task categories, often matching or exceeding full fine-tuning with 50× fewer trainable parameters. The Khatri-Rao reformulation means the method incurs only ~2–3% training overhead over LoRA and zero inference overhead after weight merging, making it immediately deployable. Open questions include why chained ABBA (4 adapter pairs) underperforms the 2-pair version despite higher theoretical expressivity, whether the advantage persists in higher-data regimes (the gap to full FT narrows at 40K samples), and whether the expressivity gains transfer to modalities beyond text.
