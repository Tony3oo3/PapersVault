## Problem

Co-designing robot morphology and neural control has stagnated for three decades because each morphological variant requires training a bespoke controller via reinforcement learning in non-differentiable simulation, a cost that compounds exponentially as designs evolve. Prior differentiable-simulation approaches still demanded per-morphology policy learning and could only produce rote behaviors like straight-line locomotion, leaving adaptive sensor-guided navigation and successful design recombination (crossover) unaddressed. Additionally, the paper identifies a previously unknown pathology—diversity collapse—where simultaneous co-optimization of a universal controller and an evolving design population converges to homogeneous designs that are easier to steer, undermining the very purpose of co-design.

## Input / Output

The universal controller is a fixed-size MLP (250-dim input: 245 photosensor readings + 5 CPG sinusoidal waves; 1648-dim output: spring actuation signals) that accommodates all morphologies in the 6×6×4 voxel morphospace via zero-masking of absent sensors and actuators, providing implicit morphological conditioning. Robot morphologies are encoded as binary voxel genotypes mapped deterministically to mass-spring phenotypes (up to 245 masses at voxel corners and 1648 axial/diagonal springs on a 10 cm³ cubic lattice), evaluated in a 3D differentiable simulator with bilinearly interpolated terrain height maps and inverse-square-root light decay. The evolutionary pipeline outputs populations of 8192 distinct morphologies, each with a unique phylogeny, along with the pretrained (620,912-parameter) and fine-tuned controller weights.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline: (1) morphological pretraining, where a universal MLP controller is trained end-to-end via gradient backpropagation through 1000 timesteps of differentiable mass-spring simulation (implemented in Taichi + PyTorch) across 10M+ randomly generated morphologies, terrains, and light positions, minimizing the ratio of final-to-initial distance to the light source; and (2) evolutionary design search using the pretrained controller as a frozen or periodically reset prior. In zero-shot evolution, the frozen controller evaluates non-differentiable genotype changes (bit-flip mutation and XOR-based crossover) without any retraining; in few-shot evolution, the controller is reset to pretrained weights each generation and fine-tuned for 60 steps on the current population, which the paper shows not only preserves but significantly increases morphological diversity while improving performance. The key insight is that gradient-based pretraining across body, world, and goal variations produces a morphology-agnostic controller that can be rapidly adapted, enabling the first convincing demonstration of successful crossover in evolved robots and revealing that generational fine-tuning is necessary to prevent diversity collapse.

## Results

Morphological pretraining of the universal MLP controller across 10M+ random morphologies achieves a 70% reduction in phototaxis loss (from 1.0 to ~0.3) in ~1400 steps (57 min on 8×H100 GPUs). Zero-shot evolution with the frozen pretrained controller reaches near-optimal test performance in 100 generations (17 min wall-clock), matching the simultaneous co-design baseline (Li et al., 2025) at generation 31 versus the baseline's generation 180. Few-shot evolution with 60 fine-tuning steps per generation matches the baseline at generation 6 while significantly increasing and sustaining population diversity (mean pairwise Hamming distance), whereas both zero-shot evolution and simultaneous co-design suffer diversity collapse. In the first generation of zero-shot evolution, 77% of crossover attempts produced offspring outperforming at least one parent, demonstrating successful recombination enabled by the pretrained controller.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the simultaneous co-design algorithm from Li et al. (2025), which jointly evolves a population of 64 morphologies and a universal controller from scratch using simulation gradients. The proposed few-shot evolution matches or exceeds this baseline's performance at generation 6 versus the baseline's generation 180—a 30× speedup in generations—while simultaneously increasing population diversity, whereas the baseline suffers severe diversity collapse (converging to a single species of similar designs). Zero-shot evolution (frozen controller) also beats the baseline at generation 31 but, like the baseline, exhibits diversity collapse, suggesting that without fine-tuning the evolutionary pressure favors cloning the most controller-compatible designs. The paper argues that the baseline's apparent crossover success is confounded because the randomly initialized controller improves over generations, making it unclear whether offspring gains come from morphology or controller learning; in contrast, zero-shot and few-shot evolution provide clean evidence of successful crossover since the pretrained controller is already strong at generation 0. On robustness, evolved designs from generation 31 retain functionality with ~25% motor failure and >50% sensor failure, and zero-shot evolution generalizes to out-of-distribution terrains (discrete platforms) and tasks (magnetotaxis) without any controller modification, significantly improving upon pretraining performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable mass-spring simulator of Strgar et al. (2024) from 2D to 3D with exteroception, and departs fundamentally from prior universal-controller work (Huang et al. 2020; Gupta et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2023b; Xiong et al. 2023; Bohlinger et al. 2024) by replacing non-differentiable RL with end-to-end gradient-based optimization through simulation, enabling large-scale pretraining across millions of morphologies. It also builds on the simultaneous co-design algorithm of Li et al. (2025) as a benchmark, but identifies a previously unknown pathology (diversity collapse) in that approach that is resolved by the proposed morphological pretraining paradigm. The conceptual lineage draws from the pretrain-then-fine-tune paradigm of NLP and computer vision, repurposed for discrete morphological search in robotics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of diversity collapse as an inherent pathology of simultaneous morphology-control co-design is a previously unknown finding that reframes how the field should approach joint optimization, and the demonstration that generational fine-tuning not only preserves but increases diversity without explicit pressure is a practical recipe for maintaining open-endedness. The method also convincingly demonstrates successful crossover (XOR recombination) in robot design, a capability long assumed to be unreliable, opening the door to more biologically faithful evolutionary search. Key limitations include restriction to a single material, percept, actuator type, and task (terrestrial phototaxis), absence of sim-to-real transfer, and reliance on a differentiable simulator; extending to multi-task, multi-material, manipulation, and non-terrestrial locomotion remains open.
