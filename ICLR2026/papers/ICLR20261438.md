## Problem

No single text embedding model consistently outperforms across domains and tasks, motivating ensemble methods that combine complementary strengths. However, existing ensemble approaches aggregate deterministic embeddings with fixed or uniform weights, treating all models as equally reliable regardless of their per-query uncertainty, which leads to suboptimal retrieval and poor confidence calibration when some models are mismatched to the target task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261438/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes K pre-trained deterministic embedding models (e.g., BGE, E5, GTE) and a text query or document at inference time. It produces, for each model, a probabilistic Gaussian embedding with a mean vector and diagonal covariance derived via last-layer Laplace Approximation; a set of query-adaptive ensemble coefficients (one per model, summing to 1); and a single scalar uncertainty-aware similarity score between query and candidate embeddings. The entire pipeline is post-hoc—no retraining or fine-tuning of the base models is required—and the similarity score is designed to approximate the squared 2-Wasserstein distance between the two Gaussian embeddings up to O(ε²) error.

## Method

UEC operates in three stages. First, each deterministic embedding model is converted into a probabilistic one by applying a diagonal Laplace Approximation to the final linear layer, yielding a Gaussian posterior over last-layer weights that propagates to a Gaussian embedding distribution with mean equal to the original embedding and covariance capturing epistemic uncertainty. Second, the K probabilistic embeddings are combined via a weighted Gaussian convolution where the coefficients are obtained in closed form as a temperature-controlled softmax over the negative trace of each model's per-query covariance, derived from an entropy-regularized convex optimization grounded in a squared-distance surrogate for contrastive objectives. Third, similarity between two ensembled Gaussian embeddings is computed by moment-matching the inner product to a Gaussian and applying a probit calibration that divides the mean dot product by a variance-dependent denominator, yielding a lightweight score provably equivalent (up to O(ε²)) to ranking by the squared 2-Wasserstein distance.

## Results

On the MMTEB retrieval benchmark (5 datasets), UEC achieves the highest average nDCG@10 of 79.58, Recall@100 of 90.69, and AUC@10 of 67.61, surpassing the strongest individual model GTE-MB (77.33 / 88.60 / 64.07) and the probabilistic baseline GroVE (77.48 / 89.34 / 65.16). On classification (5 datasets), UEC attains the best average accuracy (68.89%), F1 (61.04), and AUROC (73.02). On STS (10 datasets), UEC achieves the highest average Spearman correlation of 76.49, ranking first on 8 out of 10 datasets. The uncertainty metric AUC@10 shows the largest relative gains, with UEC outperforming the weighted ensemble by +4.48 points and Borda count by +32.76 points on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UEC consistently outperforms all baselines across retrieval, classification, and STS tasks. Against the strongest individual model GTE-MB, UEC gains +2.25 nDCG@10 and +3.54 AUC@10 on average in retrieval, and +2.16 accuracy and +0.93 AUROC in classification. The probabilistic embedding baseline GroVE (applied on GTE-MB) is the closest competitor, but UEC still leads by +2.10 nDCG@10 and +2.45 AUC@10 on retrieval, confirming that the ensemble convolution and uncertainty-aware similarity add value beyond single-model probabilistic embeddings. Among deterministic ensemble methods, the weighted ensemble is the strongest (78.49 avg nDCG@10), yet UEC improves by +1.09 nDCG@10 and +4.48 AUC@10, with the largest gap on the uncertainty metric. Task arithmetic (model merging) underperforms all representation-level ensembles, with UEC leading by +3.89 nDCG@10. Borda count, a rank-aggregation baseline, is dramatically outperformed on AUC@10 (67.61 vs. 34.85), highlighting that uncertainty-aware scoring is far superior to simple rank fusion for confidence calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) post-hoc Bayesian uncertainty via Laplace Approximation (MacKay 1992; Daxberger et al. 2021), which it repurposes to convert frozen deterministic embedding models into Gaussian-output probabilistic models without retraining; (2) probabilistic embedding representations (Vilnis & McCallum 2014; Sen2Pro 2023; GroVE 2025), which it extends from single-model uncertainty to multi-model ensemble settings; and (3) deterministic embedding ensemble methods (uniform averaging, task arithmetic, Borda count), which it replaces with an uncertainty-driven convex combination grounded in a surrogate contrastive loss (Wang & Isola 2020). The key conceptual departure is that prior probabilistic embedding work either requires fine-tuning (Sen2Pro) or is modality-specific (GroVE for vision-language), while prior ensemble work treats all models as equally reliable; UEC is the first to unify post-hoc probabilistic conversion, query-adaptive uncertainty-weighted coefficients, and an uncertainty-aware similarity metric (probit-calibrated, provably approximating squared 2-Wasserstein distance) in a single framework for text embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UEC offers a practical, drop-in upgrade for any existing embedding ensemble pipeline: no retraining, only 0.6% additional compute, and it consistently improves both retrieval accuracy and uncertainty calibration (AUC@10) across diverse benchmarks. The theoretical guarantee that the similarity estimator approximates the squared 2-Wasserstein distance up to O(ε²) error provides a principled justification for the lightweight scoring function, which is valuable for large-scale retrieval where exact distributional distances are infeasible. Key limitations include modeling only epistemic (not aleatoric) uncertainty, relying on diagonal Laplace approximation, and assuming all ensembled models share the same embedding dimensionality. Open directions the authors identify include extending to full covariance posteriors, incorporating bias/fairness-aware uncertainty, and generalizing to multimodal settings where heterogeneous uncertainty sources interact across vision, speech, and text.
