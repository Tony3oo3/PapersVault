## Problem

The internal mechanisms by which VLMs perform spatial and temporal reasoning remain largely opaque. While SoTA VLMs can answer simple spatial queries like 'Is the dog to the left or right of the cat?', it is unclear whether they rely on structured linear representations of spatial information or more complex nonlinear processes. This paper asks whether identifiable linear 'spatial IDs' exist in VLM activations and whether they causally mediate model beliefs about object locations in space and time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261407/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis operates on standard VLM architectures (LLaVA, LLaMA-VL, Qwen, InternVL, Gemma, and video variants) processing RGB images or video clips paired with text queries. Spatial IDs are extracted from intermediate-layer activations at object-word token positions by subtracting the position-averaged embedding across an m×m grid, yielding d-dimensional vectors encoding the object's grid location. Outputs include steering results (log-probability shifts for binary spatial/temporal answers), per-sample ID deviation metrics for failure diagnosis, and a spatial ID cosine-similarity loss for fine-tuning.

## Method

The core insight is that VLMs linearly bind spatial information to object word activations in intermediate layers, forming what the authors term spatial IDs. Extraction places objects on an m×m grid, computes text token embeddings at each position, and subtracts the mean to isolate the spatial component; canonical horizontal and vertical direction vectors are then derived from pairwise differences across grid-aligned coordinates. Causal verification uses targeted activation steering: adding or subtracting spatial IDs to object-word residuals at binding layers systematically shifts model beliefs (64.6% median belief swap vs. 29.5% for noise across 11 SoTA models). A theoretical sketch shows spatial IDs emerge as linear transformations of positional encodings (e.g., RoPE) written into text tokens via cross-modal attention, with a rank-3 linear fit explaining R²≳0.85 of variance. The framework extends to temporal IDs in video models and provides a diagnostic pipeline (ID deviation + image masking) to attribute failures to vision encoder, cross-modal integration, or linguistic reasoning stages, plus a spatial ID loss that yields a 6% accuracy gain over control fine-tuning.

## Results

The headline causal result is that steering object-word activations with extracted spatial IDs produces a median 64.6% binary belief swap on COCO-SPATIAL across 11 SoTA VLMs, versus only 29.5% for norm-matched noise vectors (43.6% above-chance influence). A low-rank linear fit from positional encodings to spatial IDs explains most variance (rank-3 yields R² ≳ 0.85), confirming the linearity hypothesis. In a fine-tuning experiment on Qwen2-2B, adding a spatial ID cosine-similarity loss at layer 11 reaches 91% COCO-Spatial validation accuracy at 3.2k steps, a 6-point gain over the control (85%) under identical training conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability paper rather than a method-vs-benchmark comparison, so traditional baseline matchups do not apply. The primary controlled comparison is spatial ID steering versus norm-matched random noise steering: spatial IDs achieve 64.6% median belief swap on COCO-SPATIAL while noise achieves only 29.5%, establishing a clear causal signal above chance. For model diagnostics, LLaVA-1.5 7B and LLaMA-3.2-VL 11B are contrasted: LLaVA's failures correlate with poor vision-encoder object detection (bounding-box masking sensitivity is significantly more negative for wrong answers), whereas LLaMA's failures are agnostic to image obfuscation, pointing to cross-modal integration as its bottleneck. In the fine-tuning comparison, the spatial ID loss ablation shows a 6-point accuracy gain (91% vs 85% at 3.2k steps) over standard language-modeling-only training, with the spatial ID loss decreasing monotonically (0.75→0.33) while the control's LM loss saturates immediately. Across the 11 VLMs analyzed (LLaVA, LLaMA, Qwen, InternVL, Gemma, LLaVA-Video, VideoLLaMA3, etc.), spatial IDs are consistently extractable and causally effective, with steerability positively correlated with zero-shot COCO-Spatial accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear binding ID mechanism identified in LLMs (Feng & Steinhardt, 2024) to the vision-language setting, showing that VLMs encode object locations by linearly binding spatial positional information to object-word token activations. Methodologically, it builds on activation patching and steering techniques from mechanistic interpretability (Meng et al., 2022; Zhang & Nanda, 2023) and the Linear Representation Hypothesis (Park et al., 2024), applying them to VLM internals. It departs from prior VLM interpretability work (Jiang et al., 2025b; Neo et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024a), which characterized attention patterns or modality-switching layers, by isolating a specific linear vector (the spatial ID) and demonstrating its causal role through targeted interventions. The theoretical sketch in §2.3 connects the mechanism to standard attention-based positional encoding (RoPE, learned 2D embeddings), grounding the empirical finding in a minimal analytical model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, testable mechanistic account of how VLMs perform spatial reasoning, moving beyond correlational attention analyses to causal evidence that a simple linear structure mediates model beliefs. The practical diagnostic framework (spatial ID deviation + image masking) offers a principled way to attribute VQA failures to specific architectural components (vision encoder vs. cross-modal integration vs. linguistic reasoning), which is directly useful for model engineering. The finding that depth and height are conflated in VLM representations (above/front and below/behind map to overlapping spatial ID locations) highlights a fundamental representational limitation that motivates better 3D-aware architectures. Key open questions include whether the mechanism persists in models beyond 14B parameters, how it generalizes to complex multi-object or compositional spatial queries, and whether the spatial ID loss can be scaled to improve real-world VLM performance beyond synthetic training data.
