## Problem

Existing unsupervised skill discovery methods struggle in complex, multi-entity environments: MI-based approaches favor simple static skills due to invariance properties, while DSD methods, though more dynamic, treat the state space holistically and lack explicit mechanisms to ensure skill coverage across all controllable factors. This leads to underrepresentation of harder-to-control or less immediately responsive elements in the learned skill latent space, limiting the expressiveness and composability of discovered skills.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263516/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires direct access to a factorized state vector decomposed into N entity-specific sub-vectors (e.g., 10 agent-station pairs in Multi-Particle, 7 objects in Kitchen), with each factor fed to its own embedding network. The skill latent vector is also factorized into N sub-vectors of dimension D=2, sampled from a standard normal prior at episode start. The skill-conditioned policy outputs a continuous action distribution, and the factorized embeddings φ_i(s^i) serve as the structured representation used for both intrinsic reward computation and downstream factor decoding.

## Method

SUSD factorizes the DSD optimization by assigning a dedicated embedding network φ_i and skill sub-vector z^i to each state factor, converting the holistic Wasserstein-based objective into a sum of factor-wise terms with a joint Lipschitz constraint. A curiosity-based weighting module trains a conditional density model q_θ(s'|s) on collected transitions, then extracts per-factor marginal Gaussians to compute factor-wise curiosity signals −log q_θ(s'_{t+1}^i|s_t) that scale each factor's intrinsic reward, dynamically emphasizing underexplored or harder-to-control components. Training uses dual gradient descent: the skill policy is optimized with SAC using the curiosity-weighted intrinsic reward R = Σ_i √(−log q_θ(s'_{t+1}^i|s_t)) · r_i, while each φ_i and its Lagrange multiplier λ are updated via separate gradient ascent objectives enforcing the Lipschitz constraint. This structured, adaptive approach yields a disentangled skill representation that supports fine-grained compositional control via HRL.

## Results

On the Multi-Particle environment, SUSD achieves a factor decoding MSE of 0.060, substantially outperforming METRA (0.147), CSD (0.313), and LSD (0.308). In the Kitchen environment, SUSD reaches 0.014 MSE versus 0.028 (METRA), 0.049 (CSD), and 0.038 (LSD). During skill learning in Kitchen, SUSD accidentally completes the Butter-in-Pot task with an average reward of 39.875±18.452 over 8 runs, while all DSD baselines (CSD, METRA, LSD) and DUSDi score 0. On unfactorized environments (Ant, HalfCheetah), SUSD achieves competitive zero-shot goal-reaching performance comparable to METRA and CSD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUSD outperforms all five baselines on the factorized environments (Multi-Particle, Kitchen, 2D-Gunner). Against the DSD baselines, SUSD achieves roughly 2.4× lower factor decoding MSE than METRA (0.060 vs 0.147 on MP) and 5× lower than CSD (0.060 vs 0.313) and LSD (0.060 vs 0.308). Against the MI-based DIAYN, SUSD shows substantially better downstream task returns on Multi-Particle and Kitchen, as DIAYN's invariance properties limit it to simple static skills. Against the factorized MI-based DUSDi, SUSD achieves significantly higher worst-agent state coverage in Multi-Particle and is the only method that accidentally completes Kitchen tasks (e.g., 39.875 reward on BiP vs 0.0 for DUSDi). On the unfactorized Ant and HalfCheetah environments, SUSD matches or slightly trails METRA and CSD on zero-shot goal reaching, confirming that the factorization inductive bias is most beneficial in structured, multi-entity settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SUSD extends the Distance-Maximizing Skill Discovery (DSD) lineage (LSD, CSD, METRA) by factorizing both the state embedding and skill latent space along environment-specific components, a structural inductive bias previously exploited only in MI-based methods (DUSDi, SkiLD). The curiosity-based factor weighting generalizes CSD's coarse-grained transition-level controllability signal to a per-factor level, and the reformulation of the Lipschitz constraint into the objective follows a lemma adapted from Kim et al. (2024). The key departure from prior DSD work is the explicit allocation of dedicated skill variables to each state factor, which prevents the underrepresentation of harder-to-control elements that plagues holistic DSD formulations in multi-object environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode of DSD methods in complex, multi-entity environments, and the factorized skill representation naturally supports compositional downstream task solving via HRL. However, the approach requires known or manually specified state factorization, limiting its applicability to settings where the compositional structure is not readily available (e.g., raw pixel observations without a disentangled encoder). Open questions include whether the factorization can be learned end-to-end, how the method behaves when the true factorization is partially incorrect (the over/under-factorization analysis in Appendix M hints at sensitivity), and whether the curiosity weighting mechanism generalizes to environments with more than ~10 factors or with strongly coupled dynamics.
