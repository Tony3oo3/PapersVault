## Problem

Outcome-only RL for long-horizon LLM agents suffers from 'inefficient exploration': the agent is rewarded for task success regardless of whether its reasoning path is coherent, leading to high rates of repetitive and invalid actions that undermine generalization to unseen tasks. SFT avoids this inefficiency but produces brittle policies that fail on novel task categories, creating a fundamental trade-off between efficiency and generalization that no existing training paradigm resolves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263899/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives the task description, current environment observation, recent action history, and a list of admissible actions, and must produce exactly one meta-reasoning tag (one of <planning>, <explore>, <reflection>, <monitor>) containing a single concise sentence, followed by one action enclosed in <action> tags. Actions must conform to strict environment-specific syntax (e.g., 'take [obj id] from [recep id]'), and any output that omits a valid tag-action pair incurs a format penalty during training.

## Method

RLVMR introduces four verifiable meta-reasoning tags that decompose the agent's reasoning into planning, exploration, reflection, and monitoring, each with a lightweight programmatic reward rule (e.g., exploration is rewarded only when the action targets a previously unvisited object or location; reflection is rewarded only when it follows a failure and leads to a corrective action). A brief cold-start SFT phase on 200 GPT-4-annotated trajectories bootstraps the tag syntax, after which the agent is trained end-to-end via GRPO-MR, a critic-free policy gradient that computes a composite step-level advantage by combining a trajectory-level relative advantage (normalized outcome reward across same-environment rollouts) with a tag-level relative advantage (normalized meta-reasoning reward within same-tag groups), weighted by a hyperparameter α. The final policy is optimized with a clipped surrogate objective and KL regularization against a reference policy, yielding dense process-level supervision that complements the sparse terminal success signal.

## Results

On ALFWorld, RLVMR with Qwen2.5-7B achieves 91.4% success on seen tasks (L0), 91.8% on unseen variants (L1), and a headline 83.6% on the hardest unseen split (L2: unseen task variants AND categories), surpassing the next-best method GiGPO by 16.4 percentage points. On ScienceWorld, the same model reaches 67.2% (L0), 77.8% (L1), and 43.0% (L2) success with a cumulative score of 49.1. The 1.5B model also sets new SOTA, reaching 56.3% on ALFWorld L2 and 34.4% on ScienceWorld L2, while Llama3.1-8B achieves 83.2% on ALFWorld L2 and 49.2% on ScienceWorld L2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLVMR outperforms all six baselines (ReAct, SFT, ETO, GLIDER, GRPO, GiGPO) across every model size and every evaluation split on both ALFWorld and ScienceWorld. The most striking margin is on ALFWorld L2 with Qwen2.5-7B, where RLVMR reaches 83.6% versus GiGPO's 67.2% (+16.4 pp) and GRPO's 52.3% (+31.3 pp), confirming that process-level meta-reasoning rewards substantially improve generalization to entirely unseen task categories. On ScienceWorld L2 (7B), RLVMR leads GiGPO by 7.8 pp (43.0% vs. 35.2%) and GRPO by 12.9 pp (43.0% vs. 30.1%), while also achieving the highest cumulative score (49.1 vs. 33.2 and 34.3 respectively). Even the 1.5B model benefits dramatically: RLVMR lifts ALFWorld L2 from 29.7% (GRPO) to 56.3% and ScienceWorld L2 from 13.7% (GRPO) to 34.4%, showing the gains are not merely a scaling artifact. GiGPO is the strongest baseline overall, particularly on seen tasks (e.g., 89.5% ALFWorld L0 for 7B), but RLVMR consistently extends its lead on the harder unseen splits, validating the paper's central claim that rewarding verifiable meta-reasoning behaviors addresses the inefficient exploration problem that outcome-only RL cannot resolve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ReAct framework by decomposing its monolithic reasoning step into four explicit meta-reasoning tags (planning, exploration, reflection, monitoring), drawing on metacognitive theory from cognitive science. It modifies GRPO's group relative advantage computation by adding a tag-conditioned, step-level meta-reasoning advantage that provides dense, verifiable process rewards alongside the sparse outcome signal. The work departs from prior RL-for-agents methods (ETO, GLIDER, GiGPO) that focus on credit assignment granularity or offline trajectory optimization, instead targeting the quality of the reasoning process itself through rule-based, programmatic rewards for specific cognitive behaviors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a concrete failure mode (inefficient exploration) in outcome-only RL for long-horizon agents, showing that even successful trajectories can contain illogical loops and that scaling model size exacerbates rather than fixes this problem. The verifiable meta-reasoning reward design is lightweight (programmatic rules, no learned reward model) and could be adapted to other sequential decision-making domains, though its current applicability is limited to environments where 'novel state transitions' and 'corrective actions' are programmatically detectable. Open questions include whether the hand-designed tag taxonomy generalizes to more open-ended or multimodal tasks, whether the reward rules can be learned rather than specified, and whether the approach transfers to real-world robotics or software engineering settings where verifiable process signals are harder to define.
