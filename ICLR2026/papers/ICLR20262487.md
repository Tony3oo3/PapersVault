## Problem

LLMs process every token through all transformer layers regardless of input difficulty, wasting computation on simple queries while offering no flexibility for harder reasoning tasks. Prior adaptive-depth methods either trade accuracy for speed, require architectural modifications and large-scale retraining, or depend on costly inference-time search that is impractical at deployment scale. Dr.LLM targets the gap of retrofitting frozen pretrained LLMs with dynamic depth control that simultaneously improves accuracy and reduces compute without altering base weights.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and produces a generated text response, but internally each of the L transformer blocks is gated by a lightweight router that decides whether to skip, execute once, or repeat the block. Routers operate on windowed mean-pooled hidden states (W=8 windows) from the previous layer and are executed once per input sequence rather than per token, making them fully compatible with KV caching and adding under 1% latency overhead. The per-layer routing decisions are discrete (skip/execute/repeat) and are made greedily at inference with no search.

## Method

Dr.LLM attaches a per-layer bottleneck MLP router (Linear-GELU-Linear, hidden dim 128) to each frozen transformer block, where each router reads windowed mean-pooled hidden states and outputs three logits for skip, execute, or repeat. Supervision is generated offline via a length-aware MCTS that explores edited execution paths (allowing at most two consecutive skips and one repeat per layer, capped at 2L total length) and retains only paths that preserve or improve task accuracy under a compute budget, yielding 4k training tuples. Routers are trained with focal loss (γ=2) combined with effective-number class-rebalancing weights to handle extreme imbalance (execute ~94%, skip ~7.6%, repeat ~2.3%), using teacher forcing to avoid inter-router dependency. Training updates only ~11M router parameters (0.14% of base weights) on a single A100 GPU in under 4 hours, and at inference the trained routers make greedy per-layer decisions with zero search overhead.

## Results

On in-domain ARC and DART across six models (LLaMA-3.2 3B/8B, Qwen-2.5 3B/7B), Dr.LLM improves accuracy in all cases with mean gains of +2.25%p (up to +4.0%p on DART for LLaMA-3B-Base) while saving 3–11 layers per query on average. On out-of-domain benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8k, AIME24, TruthfulQA, SQuADv2, GPQA Diamond, AGIEval, PIQA), routers generalize with only a 0.85%p average accuracy drop while retaining efficiency. Wall-clock generation time is reduced by 15.3% on 1k-token runs, with router overhead under 1% of total latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dr.LLM outperforms all four baselines on every benchmark in Table 5, with the largest margins on HumanEval (+16.3%p over FlexiDepth, +39.4%p over MindSkip) and GSM8k (+9.2%p over FlexiDepth, +21.3%p over ShortGPT). The closest competitor, FlexiDepth, still trails Dr.LLM by 7.7%p in average accuracy; notably, FlexiDepth requires training on 326k Tulu-v2 examples while Dr.LLM uses only 4k MCTS-derived examples on a single GPU. On MMLU the gap is minimal (+0.4–0.9%p), suggesting routing provides the least benefit for factual recall tasks. The paper emphasizes that these benchmarks are in-domain for the prior methods but out-of-domain for Dr.LLM, making the consistent wins particularly strong evidence of generalization. LayerSkip and ShortGPT perform catastrophically on reasoning (GSM8k: 0.4% and 53.6% respectively), confirming that naive early-exit or static pruning strategies are inadequate for multi-step tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dr.LLM builds directly on CoLa (Li et al., 2025), which uses MCTS to search for input-specific layer paths, but repositions the search entirely offline to generate a small supervision set (4k examples) from which lightweight per-layer routers are trained, eliminating costly inference-time search. It departs from prior retrofit routing methods (FlexiDepth, MindSkip) that require hundreds of thousands of training examples and typically sacrifice accuracy, and from early-exit approaches (LayerSkip) that cannot repeat layers or operate without auxiliary classifiers. The skip/execute/repeat action space, combined with focal loss under extreme class imbalance and windowed mean pooling for long-context stability, distinguishes the training recipe from both the MCTS-based and the supervised-routing lines of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a frozen LLM can be made budget-aware with <1% router overhead, full KV-cache compatibility, and no architectural changes, making it deployable in production inference pipelines. The structured routing patterns (early layers preserved, middle layers pruned, late layers repeated) offer an interpretable account of where transformer depth is actually needed, which could inform future architecture design. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether the 4k-example supervision budget suffices for more diverse task distributions, and whether the modest OOD drops (up to ~1.2%p) remain acceptable as routing is applied to increasingly specialized or safety-critical workloads.
