## Problem

Existing LDP quantile methods are either single-machine procedures that ignore client heterogeneity or provide only point estimates without valid inferential guarantees. In federated settings with heterogeneous distributions, quantile targets, and privacy budgets, no prior work delivers confidence intervals or hypothesis tests for a global quantile under local differential privacy. A further obstacle is that the quantile (check) loss is non-smooth, so standard SGD-based variance estimation via the Hessian is unavailable.

## Input / Output

Each of K clients holds i.i.d. samples from its own distribution P_k and applies a randomized-response LDP mechanism with a client-specific truthful response rate r_k (equivalently, privacy budget ε_k = log((1+r_k)/(1-r_k))) before transmitting a binary signal to the server. The server coordinates local SGD updates over a communication schedule {E_m} and client weights {p_k}, and outputs a Polyak-Ruppert averaged quantile estimate Q̂_T together with a self-normalized confidence interval [Q̂_T − v_{α/2,g}√V̂_T, Q̂_T + v_{α/2,g}√V̂_T] whose critical value is distribution-free, requiring no estimation of density values or asymptotic variance.

## Method

The key insight is a reduction (Theorem 2.1) showing that the LDP federated quantile problem with data {P_k} and levels {τ_k} is exactly equivalent to a non-DP federated quantile problem with modified distributions {P̃_k} (each mixing the original data with ±∞ mass) and shifted levels τ̃_k = r_k τ + (1−r_k)/2, so the same target Q* is recovered. On top of this reduction, the paper establishes asymptotic normality and a functional central limit theorem for the local-SGD estimator without the average-smoothness condition assumed in prior local-SGD theory, by proving the L² convergence rate E|q̄_{t_m} − Q*|² ≲ γ_m directly under the weaker Hölder-1/2 smoothness of the indicator-based gradient. For inference, a self-normalized statistic Q_T(1)/√V_T is constructed from the FCLT, yielding a pivotal limit distribution B(1)/√∫{B(r)−g(r)B(1)}²dr that is free of all nuisance parameters, so confidence intervals are obtained online (Algorithm 2) without consuming additional privacy budget.

## Results

On heterogeneous simulation settings (K=10 clients, t_T=10,000), the proposed C1 (parallel SGD) strategy achieves empirical coverage probabilities (ECP) of 0.950–0.990 with mean absolute errors (MAE) of 0.0025–0.0237 across quantile levels τ∈{0.3, 0.5, 0.8} and response rates r∈{0.25, 0.9, hetero}. On the real Government Salary Dataset (t_T=53,960 per client), the C1 strategy yields point estimates within ~1,500 USD of the full-sample quantile with CI half-widths of 1,138–2,255 USD, while the Log strategy widens CIs to 6,863–13,144 USD. The divide-and-conquer baseline collapses in heterogeneous settings, with ECP dropping to 0.049 (τ=0.8, r=0.9, Hete L) and MAE reaching 0.21, whereas the proposed method maintains ECP ≥ 0.990 in the same setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DP-SGD (Song et al., 2013), the proposed method consistently achieves lower MAE across all settings (e.g., 0.0165 vs. 0.0224 at τ=0.5, r=0.25, Hete L, t_T=10,000) while maintaining comparable or slightly higher ECP; DP-SGD's Laplace-noise injection inflates variance without the bias-correction structure of the randomized-response mechanism. Against the divide-and-conquer (DC) baseline, the proposed method wins decisively in heterogeneous settings: at τ=0.8, r=0.9, Hete L, DC achieves ECP of only 0.049 with MAE 0.2098, whereas C1 reaches ECP 0.990 with MAE 0.0042 — a gap of nearly 20× in MAE and 94 percentage points in coverage. Against the single-machine LDP method of Liu et al. (2023b) ("Single"), the proposed C1 strategy performs comparably in homogeneous settings (e.g., MAE 0.0025 vs. 0.0051 at τ=0.5, r=0.9) but handles client-level heterogeneity in quantile targets and response rates without the bias that plagues direct aggregation of independent local estimators. The Log communication strategy, while incurring higher MAE than C1, reduces communication rounds by a factor of ~log₂(T) and still maintains ECP ≥ 0.98 in most settings, offering a practical trade-off for bandwidth-constrained deployments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: federated local SGD (Li et al., 2022; Stich, 2019), local differential privacy mechanisms for quantile estimation (Liu et al., 2023b; Gillenwater et al., 2021), and self-normalized inference (Shao, 2015). It extends the single-machine LDP quantile inference of Liu et al. (2023b) to a federated, heterogeneous setting with client-specific privacy budgets and quantile targets, and extends the local SGD asymptotic theory of Li et al. (2022) to the non-smooth check loss by proving a functional CLT without the standard average-smoothness condition. The key conceptual move is Theorem 2.1, which shows the LDP randomized-response mechanism is equivalent to solving a non-private quantile problem under modified distributions and shifted quantile levels, thereby reducing the privacy analysis to a non-DP asymptotic framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: hospitals, financial institutions, and government agencies need valid quantile confidence intervals from distributed, privacy-sensitive data, and prior methods either ignored client heterogeneity or provided only point estimates. The self-normalized CI construction avoids estimating nuisance parameters (local densities, individual quantiles) that would otherwise require additional privacy budget or data splitting. Limitations include reliance on a central server (ruling out fully decentralized settings), conservative intervals due to the heavier-tailed self-normalized limit, and regularity assumptions that may not hold for arbitrary heavy-tailed distributions. Open directions include extending to asynchronous/partial participation, decentralized topologies, and understanding the fundamental privacy-accuracy tradeoff more sharply.
