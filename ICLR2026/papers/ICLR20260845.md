## Problem

LLMs achieve near-expert accuracy on abstract math benchmarks like AIME and MATH-500, yet this performance does not transfer to real-world settings where the mathematical core is embedded in narrative scenarios with indirect or layered conditions. This paper identifies and systematically measures this gap through contextual mathematical reasoning — the ability to formulate and solve a mathematical problem when it is conveyed through descriptive context rather than explicit symbolic form.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260845/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes AIME 2024, AIME 2025, and MATH-500 problems and transforms each into two controlled variants: Scenario Grounding (SG), which maps all mathematical components to concrete real-world entities while preserving the reasoning core, and Complexity Scaling (CS), which replaces explicit conditions with outputs of simple sub-problems (e.g., encoding '25 lights' as 'the total number of unique pairs is 300'). Models receive the narrative scenario as input and must produce a numerical answer; in the formulation analysis, they instead output only the abstract mathematical formulation stripped of narrative detail. Open-source models are evaluated with 16 samples per problem using recommended generation configurations, while proprietary models use single-pass greedy decoding.

## Method

ContextMATH is constructed via a structured LLM pipeline (o1-mini) that performs iterative scenario generation, self-verification, and revision, followed by independent review from three experts with competitive mathematics backgrounds to guarantee mathematical equivalence. The evaluation framework introduces three complementary formulation metrics — accuracy (correct translation rate), necessity (P(correct formulation | correct answer)), and sufficiency (P(correct answer | correct formulation)) — to disentangle the formulation bottleneck from the reasoning bottleneck. Training experiments compare end-to-end SFT on original problems, synthetic scenario data, or a mixture (using 50k validated contextual problems from DeepMath-103K) against a dedicated formulation-model pipeline, revealing that scenario-aware fine-tuning improves contextual performance while isolated formulation training is ineffective.

## Results

On ContextMATH, both open-source and proprietary LLMs show substantial accuracy drops when abstract math problems are embedded in narrative scenarios. On average, open-source models decline by 13 percentage points on Scenario Grounding (SG) and 34 points on Complexity Scaling (CS), while proprietary models drop by 13 and 20 points respectively. Even GPT-5 (estimated 1.8T parameters) drops from 90% to 66.7% on AIME 2025-CS, and DeepSeek-R1 falls from 86.7% to 53.3% on the same set. Formulation errors (incorrectly extracting the mathematical core from narrative) account for roughly 80% of all failures, with GPT-5 achieving only 81.4% average formulation accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This benchmark paper evaluates 61 models (46 open-source, 15 proprietary) rather than proposing a new method, so comparisons center on relative robustness to contextualization. GPT-5 is the most contextually robust proprietary model, dropping only 7–11% on AIME 2024 SG/CS, while DeepSeek-R1 (−25%/−29%) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (−12%/−8% on AIME 2024) show moderate degradation. Among open-source models, Qwen3-32B (81.2% → 67.9% SG, 57.1% CS on AIME 2024) and AReaL-boba-2-32B (81.5% → 65.4% SG, 58.3% CS) lead their size group, but all open-source models suffer far larger CS drops (average −34%) than proprietary ones (−20%). Smaller models are particularly vulnerable: Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B collapses from 11.5% to 0.4% on AIME 2024-CS (−96%), and even the 1.5B OpenMath-Nemotron drops 77% on CS. Scale mitigates but does not eliminate the gap—within the OpenMath-Nemotron family, CS drops shrink from 77% (1.5B) to 51% (32B), yet no open-source model exceeds 58.3% on AIME 2024-CS. Fine-tuning with scenario data (SFT_Mix) improves Qwen3-14B-Base from 11.0% to 52.5% on AIME 2024-SG, but even the best fine-tuned models solve under 40% of CS problems, confirming the gap persists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing established abstract math benchmarks (AIME, MATH-500) into contextual narrative variants, rather than building on a specific prior method. It positions itself against the dominant line of LLM math evaluation (GSM8K, MATH, AIME, OlympiadBench) that targets well-formulated problems, and against scenario-based evaluation in other domains (WebArena, SWE-bench) that does not focus on mathematics. A key conceptual departure is from Cheng et al. (2025), who found abstract formulation is not the primary bottleneck on simpler benchmarks like GSM8K; this paper demonstrates that formulation becomes the dominant failure mode once the mathematical core is embedded in rich, layered narratives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that near-perfect AIME scores do not translate to reliable real-world mathematical performance, with even GPT-5 dropping 26% on Complexity Scaling variants. The finding that ~80% of errors stem from incorrect problem formulation (rather than computation) reframes where improvement efforts should focus. The negative result that training a dedicated formulation model is ineffective (performance collapses from ~54% to ~21%) challenges the intuitive decomposition of contextual reasoning into separate formulation and solving stages. Open questions include whether the gap can be closed with larger-scale or differently structured training, and whether the ContextMATH construction approach generalizes to other domains beyond mathematics.
