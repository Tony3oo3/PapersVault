## Problem

Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has driven major gains in tasks with deterministic gold answers (code, math), but open-ended QA lacks such verifiable signals. Existing alternatives—RLHF reward models and LLM-as-a-Judge—are costly, opaque, vulnerable to reward hacking, and often fail to provide sufficiently discriminative or interpretable evaluation. The paper asks: can we construct explicit, question-specific rubrics from accessible web sources to serve as verifiable reward signals, thereby extending RLVR to the open-ended QA setting?

## Input / Output

For each open-ended question, the pipeline retrieves ranked web articles (N_w pages), distills them into compact meta-descriptions via Qwen2.5-7B, and constructs a case-wise rubric set R*(q) structured into two parts: Writing Quality (3 points: clarity/logical flow, engagement/stylistic flair) and Content Coverage (7 points: question-specific criteria like specific examples, theoretical grounding, variability). During RL training, the policy model samples N=8 candidate answers per question; an LLM judge scores each answer against the rubric items, and a deterministic parser normalizes the summed rubric scores into [0,1] as the reward. The final output is a post-trained LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-QuRL) that produces more vivid, specific, and well-structured open-ended answers.

## Method

QuRL extends RLVR to open-ended QA by replacing opaque scalar rewards with explicit, case-wise rubrics distilled from human-authored web content. The rubric construction pipeline has four stages: (1) question-based retrieval of web articles ranked by click-through rate, (2) meta-description extraction that retains argumentative content and stylistic features while filtering noise, (3) rubric generation guided by four design principles covering both content quality (core arguments, viewpoints) and writing quality (fluency, coherence, expressiveness), and (4) a two-criteria filter that discards rubric sets with insufficient score variance (σ < α·Δ, α=0.15) across 20 diverse model responses and consolidates semantically consistent rubrics across K=10 sampling runs. The filtered rubrics R*(q) then serve as structured reward references in GRPO: an LLM judge evaluates each sampled answer against rubric items, a parser extracts per-item scores normalized to [0,1], and GRPO computes relative advantages across the group of N=8 samples to update the policy. This design preserves RLVR's scalability and interpretability while capturing the fine-grained, question-specific evaluation criteria that fixed checklists or scalar reward models cannot express.

## Results

Qwen2.5-7B-QuRL achieves an average score of 59.3 across three benchmarks (HelloBench 56.4, QuRL-Test 62.4, LongBench-Write 59.2), representing a +17.0 point improvement over the supervised fine-tuning baseline (Qwen2.5-7B-SFT, avg 42.3) and +11.6 over the RLHF reward-model variant (avg 47.7). Notably, this 7B model matches DeepSeek-V3 (~700B parameters, avg 59.1) while trailing only proprietary frontier models like Gemini-2.5-Pro (70.4) and GPT-4o (64.7). In human-evaluation consistency analysis, QuRL rubrics achieve the highest Spearman's ρ (0.31, p < 10⁻⁵) with human judgments, outperforming HelloBench (0.20), InternLM2 reward model (0.22), and generic LLM-as-a-Judge (0.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary models, Qwen2.5-7B-QuRL (59.3 avg) trails Gemini-2.5-Pro (70.4) by 11.1 points and GPT-4o (64.7) by 5.4 points, but narrows the gap substantially compared to the base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (28.3). Among open-source models, QuRL surpasses DeepSeek-R1 (62.4) on HelloBench (56.4 vs. 32.8) and QuRL-Test (62.4 vs. 80.4—actually loses here), while essentially matching DeepSeek-V3 (59.1) on average despite being ~100× smaller. QuRL decisively outperforms same-size baselines: Qwen2.5-7B-SFT (42.3, +17.0 gap), Llama-3.1-8B (23.7, +35.6 gap), and LongWriter-9B-DPO (27.5, +31.8 gap). The RLHF reward-model variant (47.7) confirms that scalar reward signals are less effective than rubric-based verifiable rewards, with QuRL gaining an additional +11.6 points. On LongBench-Write specifically, DeepSeek-V3 (78.4) and DeepSeek-R1 (74.0) still lead, suggesting QuRL's rubric approach is most impactful on content-quality and style dimensions rather than pure length optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm (pioneered by DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI's o-series for verifiable domains like code and math) to open-ended QA, a setting previously dominated by RLHF with implicit scalar reward models. It departs from prior rubric-based evaluation work (Huang et al. 2025, Gunjal et al. 2025, Li et al. 2025) by automatically mining case-wise rubrics from human-authored web sources rather than constructing them via complex LLM-agent pipelines or generating them solely from questions. The GRPO algorithm from DeepSeek-R1 is adopted as the optimization backbone, with the key conceptual shift being that rubrics serve as explicit, interpretable, and verifiable reward signals rather than opaque parameterized reward functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QuRL offers a practical path to bringing the scalability and stability of RLVR into subjectively evaluated tasks, potentially reducing dependence on costly human annotation and fragile reward models. However, the human consistency correlation remains modest (Spearman's ρ = 0.31), suggesting that even well-constructed case-wise rubrics capture only a fraction of human judgment. Open questions include the safety and bias of web-derived rubrics, whether the approach generalizes beyond the single Qwen2.5-7B model family tested, and how to design more flexible scoring schemes that adapt to question-specific evaluation priorities.
