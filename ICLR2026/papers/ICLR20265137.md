## Problem

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and In-Context Learning (ICL) achieve task adaptation through fundamentally different internal mechanisms, as evidenced by their divergent activation patterns in self-attention layers. In data-scarce few-shot settings, SFT is prone to shortcut learning and produces miscalibrated responses, while ICL generalizes better but incurs higher inference cost. The paper asks whether ICL's information-rich internal computations can be leveraged to improve the quality of SFT.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265137/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a base decoder-only transformer LM, a small set of labeled input-output pairs (N ranging from 2 to 128), and ICL demonstrations constructed from the same training samples. It first requires a one-time activation collection pass where the model performs ICL inference to gather target activation tensors (L×G×d per sample), then produces LoRA-adapted weights (rank 8 on W_Q, W_K, W_O) that generate text responses at the same inference cost as standard SFT models. The pipeline is applied to both single-token outputs (classification labels, T/F, MCQ choices) and multi-token chain-of-thought responses (math, QA).

## Method

IA2 (ICL Activation Alignment) is a self-distillation priming step that enforces functional alignment between the SFT model and the base model's ICL behavior. For each training sample, the model's ICL-conditioned activations (with demonstrations in context) are collected as targets, and the model is trained via MSE loss to reproduce those same activations when processing only the query without demonstrations. This shifts the LoRA weights into a subspace that encodes ICL-like internal reasoning patterns, which is shown to be nearly orthogonal to the subspace reached by SFT-only training (~39% overlap between IA2→SFT and IA2-only, versus near-zero for SFT-only). After IA2 convergence, standard cross-entropy SFT on ground-truth target tokens is applied to align output behavior. The two-step IA2→SFT pipeline consistently outperforms SFT-only in both accuracy and expected calibration error across 12 benchmarks and two model families (Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.2-1B/3B), with the improvement being statistically significant at most sample sizes.

## Results

On 12 benchmarks across Qwen3-4B-Base and Llama-3.2 models in the few-shot (N=4) setting, IA2→SFT consistently outperforms standard SFT in both accuracy and calibration. Headline single-token results (N=4, Qwen3-4B-Base): on SST2, IA2→SFT achieves 90.4% accuracy (ECE 0.06) vs. SFT-only 65.2% (ECE 0.22) and ICL 85.4% (ECE 0.13); on FinS, 78.7% (ECE 0.16) vs. SFT 67.4% (ECE 0.31) and ICL 63.6% (ECE 0.12). For multi-token tasks, IA2→SFT also beats SFT-only on all datasets (e.g., GSM8K: 73.6% vs. 70.9%; HMathA: 55.3% vs. 50.4%), though it trails ICL on some math benchmarks. Paired t-tests confirm the accuracy improvement over SFT-only is statistically significant (p < 10⁻⁷) at N=4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ICL (Brown et al., 2020), IA2→SFT wins on accuracy in most single-token settings (e.g., SST2: 90.4% vs. 85.4%; FinS: 78.7% vs. 63.6%; SciQr: 91.7% vs. 87.7%) while achieving comparable or slightly worse ECE. However, on multi-token math tasks (GSM8K, HMathA), ICL still edges out IA2→SFT (76.4% vs. 73.6% on GSM8K), which the authors attribute to Qwen's STEM mid-training making ICL unusually sample-efficient. Against the Knowledge Distillation baseline (soft-label matching from Hinton et al., 2015), IA2-based training shows a clear advantage in the single-token case: on SST2 (N=8, Llama-3.2-1B), IA2→SFT reaches 64.7% accuracy vs. 55.9% for SFT with soft labels, while soft labels achieve better ECE (0.16 vs. 0.33). In the multi-token case (GSM8K, N=4, Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct), the gap narrows (IA2+SFT: 37.0% vs. soft-label SFT: 36.1%), suggesting soft labels are more competitive for longer sequences. Overall, IA2→SFT provides a more consistent improvement across both single- and multi-token tasks than either baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior context distillation work (Snell et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2024b) that compresses ICL into weights using only response-token signals, by instead using the model's own ICL activation patterns as a self-distillation target. It also challenges the functional equivalence implied by the 'ICL as internal gradient descent' theory (Von Oswald et al., 2023; Akyürek et al., 2022), showing empirically that ICL and SFT produce distinct activation patterns despite similar token-level outputs. The method is conceptually adjacent to activation steering (Postmus & Abreu, 2024; Stolfo et al., 2025) but aims for broad behavioral change rather than targeted intervention, and extends the ICL-vs-SFT comparison literature (Mosbach et al., 2023; Duan et al., 2024) from performance metrics to internal representation analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SFT-only weight updates are nearly orthogonal to IA2-aligned weights reveals a training signal fundamentally inaccessible to standard SFT, offering a practical priming step that improves both accuracy and calibration in data-scarce settings. However, the method is validated only on small models (1B–4B) with LoRA rank 8, and the authors acknowledge that selective layer alignment, prompt optimization, and interaction with RL-tuned models remain unexplored. A key open question is whether the activation-alignment signal scales to larger models where ICL and SFT may converge more naturally, and whether IA2 can mitigate the memorization and catastrophic forgetting issues recently documented in SFT (Chu et al., 2025; Shenfeld et al., 2025).
