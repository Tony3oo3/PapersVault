## Problem

Existing KGQA methods face a fundamental trade-off: retrieve-then-reason approaches extract static candidate paths that cannot adapt to question-specific semantics, while dynamic LLM-based path generation (CoT, MCTS) incurs prohibitive computational costs from repeated LLM calls and relies on static scorers that fail to capture the evolving semantics of multi-hop reasoning paths. Additionally, training a reliable path evaluator is hampered by the scarcity of high-quality supervision signals in dynamic search settings.

## Input / Output

DAMR takes a natural language question and a Freebase knowledge graph, from which a localized subgraph is extracted around the topic entity to constrain the search space. The LLM planner (GPT-4.1) is invoked only during the MCTS expansion phase to select top-k (default k=3) semantically relevant relations from the current entity's outgoing edges, while a lightweight 2-layer Transformer scorer (128-dim embeddings from Qwen3-Embedding-8B) handles all path evaluation locally without further LLM calls. The output is a set of answer entities accompanied by explicit multi-hop relation paths (e.g., Entity → base.rosetta.languoid.parent → Attic Group), providing interpretable reasoning traces.

## Method

DAMR integrates three tightly coupled components within an MCTS backbone. First, an LLM-based planner selects top-k semantically relevant relations at each expansion step, reducing the branching factor and limiting LLM invocations to only the expansion phase (7.1 calls on WebQSP vs. 30.9 for DoG). Second, a context-aware path evaluator uses a 2-layer Transformer with learnable positional encodings and cross-attention to jointly encode the question embedding and the relation sequence, producing a plausibility score via attention pooling and an MLP head trained with pairwise ranking loss. Third, a dynamic pseudo-path refinement mechanism periodically samples high-confidence path pairs from MCTS rollouts, assigns pseudo-labels based on empirically grounded search values (aggregated UCT statistics), and fine-tunes the evaluator online, enabling it to adapt to the evolving distribution of reasoning trajectories. The paper provides a theoretical analysis proving that this self-improving loop is a contraction mapping (via Banach's fixed-point theorem) under Lipschitz continuity conditions, guaranteeing convergence to a stable equilibrium.

## Results

DAMR achieves 94.0% Hits@1 and 81.7% F1 on WebQSP, and 78.0% Hits@1 and 75.1% F1 on CWQ, outperforming all 25 baselines across both datasets. The strongest prior method, DP (Ma et al., 2025b), reaches 87.5% Hits@1 on WebQSP and 75.8% on CWQ, yielding absolute gains of +6.5 and +2.2 Hits@1 points respectively for DAMR. In terms of efficiency, DAMR requires only 7.1 average LLM calls and 3,931 tokens per question on WebQSP, reducing LLM calls by over 50% and token consumption by 75% compared to the strongest baseline (DoG: 30.9 calls, 22,538 tokens).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAMR outperforms all 25 baselines on both WebQSP and CWQ. Against the strongest LLM+KG baseline DP (87.5/81.4 on WebQSP, 75.8/69.4 on CWQ), DAMR gains +6.5 Hits@1 on WebQSP and +5.7 F1 on CWQ. The second-strongest method FiDeLis (84.3/78.3 on WebQSP, 71.5/64.3 on CWQ) is surpassed by +9.7 Hits@1 on WebQSP and +10.8 F1 on CWQ. Among tree-search-based methods, DAMR improves over ToG (81.9/76.0) by +12.1 Hits@1 on WebQSP and over RwT (not in Table 1 but in efficiency comparison) while using far fewer LLM calls (7.1 vs. 15.1 for RwT, 23.2 for ToG, 30.9 for DoG). General-purpose LLMs without KG grounding (ChatGPT+CoT: 75.6 Hits@1 on WebQSP; Llama3-8B: 30.3) are substantially outperformed, confirming the benefit of structured KG reasoning. On the additional datasets (MetaQA, BioGraphletQA, WebQSP-Wiki), DAMR also leads ChatGPT, FiDeLis, and DoG, with 99.2% Hits@1 on MetaQA and 80.9% on BioGraphletQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAMR builds directly on the MCTS framework (Kocsis & Szepesvári, 2006) for structured search, but departs from prior LLM+KGQA methods (ToG, RoG, DP, RwT) that invoke LLMs at every reasoning step by restricting LLM usage to the expansion phase only, where it selects top-k semantically relevant relations. The context-aware path evaluator with cross-attention extends the static scoring functions used in prior MCTS-based KGQA work, while the dynamic pseudo-path refinement mechanism draws on self-training via pseudo-labeling from semi-supervised learning, repurposing it for online adaptation of a path scorer during search. The theoretical analysis (contraction mapping, variance reduction) is a notable addition absent from prior KGQA work that relies on MCTS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50%+ reduction in LLM calls and 75% reduction in token consumption relative to the strongest baseline makes LLM-guided KGQA substantially more practical and deployable, addressing a key bottleneck for real-world systems. The convergence guarantees for the self-improving scorer provide theoretical grounding that prior heuristic or RL-based approaches lack, though the assumptions (sub-Gaussian rollouts, Lipschitz constants) may not always hold in practice. Open questions include scalability to much larger knowledge graphs beyond Freebase, whether the GPT-4.1 dependency can be replaced by lighter local models without significant quality loss, and whether the pseudo-path mechanism generalizes to domains with sparser relational structure.
