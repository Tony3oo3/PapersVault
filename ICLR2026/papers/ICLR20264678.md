## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs on diverse dataset mixtures suffers from both global imbalance (uneven dataset sizes) and local heterogeneity (varying difficulty within each dataset). Existing dynamic balancing methods address only the global dimension, assuming each dataset is internally homogeneous, which limits their effectiveness when examples from the same source exhibit substantially different characteristics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes instruction-response pairs organized into N subsets, where each subset is further partitioned into M_i difficulty groups (default 4) using the SuperFiltering metric. The pre-trained LLM backbone is fine-tuned end-to-end, and the output is the converged model parameters θ alongside dynamically evolving global and local sampling probability distributions maintained by lightweight 2-layer fully connected actor networks that are discarded after training.

## Method

HBO formulates fine-tuning as a bilevel optimization where the outer problem minimizes the LLM's training loss and the inner problem adjusts hierarchical sampling probabilities. A Global Actor (ψ_global) balances sampling across N datasets using the L2 gradient norm on each subset as a reward signal, while Local Actors (ψ_local) balance sampling within each dataset across difficulty groups using the ratio of current-to-initial perplexity as a reward. Both actors are updated via REINFORCE policy gradient at fixed intervals (every 200 steps by default), with the global actor shifting focus toward subsets where the model still has large gradients and local actors cycling emphasis between easy and hard groups. This hierarchical structure enables simultaneous adaptation at both the inter-dataset and intra-dataset levels, incurring roughly 15% additional training overhead compared to static balanced sampling.

## Results

HBO achieves the best macro-average accuracy across all three model backbones in both multilingual (μ_ML) and multitask (μ_MT) settings. On Llama-3.1-8B, HBO reaches 48.07 μ_ML and 52.28 μ_MT, outperforming the strongest heuristic baseline (Prop. at 46.25 μ_ML) by +1.82 and the strongest dynamic baseline (MoS+ at 46.94 μ_ML) by +1.13. On Qwen2.5-7B, HBO attains 55.21 μ_ML and 60.37 μ_MT, surpassing the best baseline (MoS+ at 54.26 μ_ML) by +0.95. Gains are statistically significant (p < 0.05) on all three backbones, with the largest per-task improvement reaching +4.10 on MMMLU with Llama-3.1-8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HBO outperforms all six baseline families across every model backbone and both evaluation settings. Against the heuristic temperature-sampling baselines (Prop., Temp., Uni. from Arivazhagan et al.), HBO gains +1.82 μ_ML over Prop. on Llama-3.1-8B and +1.71 over Temp. on Qwen2.5-7B, confirming that static temperature tuning is insufficient. Among dynamic global-only methods, HBO beats MultiDDS (Wang et al., 2020) by +1.21 μ_ML on Llama-3.1-8B and MultiUAT (Wu et al., 2021) by +1.27 μ_ML on the same backbone, demonstrating that local (within-dataset) balancing adds substantial value beyond global rebalancing. Against MoS (Wu et al., 2024), the closest dynamic competitor, HBO achieves +1.63 μ_ML on Llama-3.1-8B and +1.22 on Qwen2.5-7B, with the gap widening on specific tasks (e.g., +4.10 on MMMLU). In the additional baselines comparison (Table 8), HBO surpasses DMT (Dong et al., 2024) by +2.64 μ_ML and EVIC (Liang et al., 2025) by +1.97 μ_ML on Llama-3.1-8B, further validating the hierarchical global-local design. Notably, HBO's advantage is consistent across all nine tasks and three backbones, with no task where HBO underperforms the best baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of dynamic data balancing for LLM fine-tuning (MultiDDS, MultiUAT, MoS) by introducing a hierarchical structure that addresses both cross-dataset (global) and within-dataset (local) heterogeneity simultaneously. The mathematical backbone is bilevel optimization (Colson et al., 2007), where the outer problem trains the LLM and the inner problem adjusts sampling probabilities, with the non-differentiability handled via the Reinforce policy gradient. The local actor's difficulty-based grouping leverages the SuperFiltering/IFD metric, and the two reward signals (gradient L2 norm globally, perplexity ratio locally) are drawn from prior observations about learning dynamics. The key conceptual departure from prior work is that existing dynamic methods assume internal homogeneity within each dataset, whereas HBO explicitly models and optimizes over intra-dataset difficulty variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HBO provides a practical, relatively lightweight (~15% overhead) drop-in replacement for static sampling in multi-dataset LLM fine-tuning, with consistent gains across three model families and nine tasks. The finding that discarding easy examples degrades performance (Table 5) challenges the common practice of filtering out 'low-information' training data, suggesting that diversity in difficulty is itself valuable. Open questions include whether the bilevel optimization and 4-group difficulty split generalize to much larger models (70B+), whether the method interacts well with RLHF or DPO stages, and whether the emergent cyclical sampling pattern observed in local actors reflects a fundamental curriculum structure or is an artifact of the specific reward design.
