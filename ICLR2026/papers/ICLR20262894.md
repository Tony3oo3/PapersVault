## Problem

In multi-LLM serving, KV cache eviction and query routing are tightly coupled yet have been treated independently: LRU-based eviction is fragile under dynamic arrivals (achieving only O(n) competitive ratio), while existing load-balancing heuristics are static and cannot adapt to evolving query patterns. No prior work provides a unified formal model capturing the joint dynamics of cache state evolution and queue load, leaving a gap between practical system designs and theoretical understanding.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a sequence of text queries arriving online in a fixed (potentially adversarial) order, each with a prompt of variable length (up to 8k tokens) and a generated response. Per-worker state includes a radix-tree KV cache of fixed capacity B_i tokens and a scalar queue load estimate updated with exponential decay every Δt time units. The method outputs a binary routing assignment (one worker per query), a leaf-token eviction decision when the cache is full, and a per-worker end-to-end latency estimate decomposed into service time, queue load, and a learned residual term.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary algorithms. RLT (Randomized Leaf Token eviction) adapts the classical marking algorithm to the radix-tree KV cache: tokens are marked as accessed, marks are cleared after B_i+1 unique tokens, and when eviction is needed, an unmarked leaf token is chosen uniformly at random, yielding a provable O(log n) competitive ratio versus O(n) for L-LRU. LBGR (Learning-Based Greedy Routing) estimates per-worker end-to-end latency as the sum of (i) service time computed from a global radix tree's cache-hit estimate, (ii) a queue load scalar updated per assignment and decayed exponentially every 20 ms, and (iii) a 4-dimensional online linear regression residual that absorbs unmodeled latency bias. Each query is greedily routed to the worker with the lowest predicted latency, and the residual model is updated online via squared-loss minimization upon observing realized latency. The combined LBGR+RLT system achieves up to 11.96× lower median latency and 14.06× lower TTFT over the state-of-the-art Cache-Aware+LRU baseline across four benchmarks.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (NVIDIA L40), LBGR+RLT achieves an 11.96× reduction in median latency and 14.06× reduction in median TTFT compared to the state-of-the-art Cache-Aware+LRU baseline, while also delivering 36.45% higher cache hit rate and 36.51% higher throughput on average across 4 benchmarks. On Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct (NVIDIA H200), the same method achieves 5.46× and 7.19× reductions in median latency and TTFT respectively. Under worst-case round-robin query arrivals on the Loogle benchmark, RLT alone achieves a 6.92× higher cache hit rate and 77.4% higher throughput than L-LRU, and LBGR+RLT reduces median latency and TTFT by 22.8× and 15.5× over Cache-Aware+LRU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines formed by combining routing strategies (Random, Round-Robin, Cache-Aware) with the L-LRU eviction policy from SGLang. LBGR+RLT dominates all baselines across every metric on all 4 benchmarks (GSP, ShareGPT, UltraChat, Loogle) and both model scales (8B and 70B). Against the strongest baseline, Cache-Aware+LRU (SGLang's state-of-the-art), LBGR+RLT achieves 11.96× lower median latency, 14.06× lower median TTFT, 2.03× lower P95 latency, and 2.62× lower P95 TTFT on average, while also improving hit rate by 36.45% and throughput by 36.51%. Against the weaker baselines (Random+LRU and Round-Robin+LRU), the gains are even more dramatic: 30.9× and 44.49× improvements in median latency and TTFT respectively. The ablation in Table 2 further shows that each component contributes independently: RLT alone (Cache-Aware+RLT) reduces median latency by 28.1% and TTFT by 42.7% over Cache-Aware+LRU, while LBGR alone (LBGR+LRU) reduces them by 77.4% and 88.2%, confirming that the two mechanisms address complementary failure modes (eviction fragility vs. static routing). The total added runtime overhead is only ~2 ms per query (0.58 ms for RLT eviction, 0.56 ms for LBGR routing), making the approach practical for deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the classical marking algorithm from competitive paging theory (Fiat et al., 1991) to the KV cache leaf-token eviction setting in RadixAttention, introducing RLT as a randomized replacement for L-LRU. It also builds on SGLang's RadixAttention infrastructure and its cache-aware routing heuristic, but departs from prior work by providing the first unified formal model that jointly captures the coupling between local cache eviction and global query routing, rather than treating them as independent heuristics. The LBGR routing algorithm extends static threshold-based or linear-score routing (as in SGLang, NVIDIA Dynamo, llm-d) with an online regression model that adaptively estimates end-to-end latency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(n) → O(log n) competitive ratio improvement for eviction, combined with the negligible ~2ms runtime overhead, makes RLT a drop-in replacement that is both theoretically optimal for randomized algorithms and practically deployable. The unified model formalizes a problem that has been addressed only heuristically in production systems, providing a foundation for future work on KV cache-aware scheduling. Key limitations include the absence of multi-modal evaluation, the restriction to at most 10 workers in a single domain, and the reliance on SGLang's global radix tree for cache state estimation, which may not generalize to all serving architectures. Open questions include whether these techniques transfer to geo-distributed deployments and whether the online regression model remains stable under highly non-stationary workloads.
