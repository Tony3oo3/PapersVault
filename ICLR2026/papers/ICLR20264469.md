## Problem

Existing blind super-resolution methods are fundamentally restricted to simple, low-pass downscaling kernels (e.g., anisotropic Gaussians, motion blur) and fail catastrophically on complex non-Gaussian degradations, performing even worse than bicubic interpolation. The paper identifies that kernel accuracy is often more critical than the SR algorithm itself, yet no prior method can recover arbitrary, unrestricted, image-specific downscaling kernels from a single LR image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264469/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image as input with no ground-truth HR image, no known kernel, and no external training data. It simultaneously produces a 4× super-resolved HR image and a 24×24 downscaling kernel (represented on a normalized coordinate grid via an INR). The kernel is assumed to be globally uniform (spatially invariant), and the method operates entirely zero-shot, training all components from scratch on the single input image.

## Method

KernelFusion operates in two phases. Phase 1 trains a patch-based diffusion model (PD) on the single LR image using a small fully-convolutional network (15×15 receptive field, v-parametrization, 64×64 crops) to capture the image's internal patch distribution. Phase 2 performs simultaneous SR and kernel estimation during the reverse diffusion process: a SIREN-based Implicit Neural Representation (INR) parameterizes the downscaling kernel (avoiding the smoothness bias of CNNs/MLPs), while a U-Net (DIP-style) applied twice at each timestep enforces global structure. A single MSE consistency loss couples the HR estimate and kernel by requiring that convolving the predicted HR image with the INR kernel and downsampling reproduces the observed LR input, enabling joint optimization of both networks from scratch at every diffusion timestep.

## Results

On the two non-Gaussian kernel datasets, KernelFusion achieves 27.191 dB PSNR / 0.719 SSIM on Blind144 and 26.426 dB PSNR / 0.720 SSIM on DIV2KFK, outperforming all SOTA Blind-SR methods by over 2 dB and even surpassing simple bicubic interpolation (24.865 / 24.101 dB) by ~2.3 dB. On the Gaussian DIV2KRK dataset, KernelFusion reaches 26.761 dB PSNR / 0.715 SSIM, which is competitive with but slightly below the best Gaussian-specialized methods (DRAT 27.953, RealDAN-spec 27.821, DCLS-SR 27.150). The paper also demonstrates that SOTA Blind-SR methods (DPSR, DCLS-SR, SwinIR, StableSR) perform *worse* than bicubic interpolation on non-Gaussian kernels, highlighting the out-of-distribution failure mode that KernelFusion addresses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the non-Gaussian datasets (Blind144, DIV2KFK), KernelFusion wins decisively over all 14 baselines by 2.3–4.1 dB PSNR. The closest competitors on Blind144 are DPSR (24.824) and DCLS-SR (24.808), both of which are actually *below* bicubic interpolation (24.865), confirming the out-of-distribution failure. On DIV2KFK, the gap is even wider: KernelFusion (26.426) leads DPSR (23.977) by 2.45 dB and DCLS-SR (23.886) by 2.54 dB. On the in-distribution Gaussian dataset DIV2KRK, KernelFusion (26.761) trails the top Gaussian-specialized methods—DRAT (27.953), RealDAN-spec (27.821), and DCLS-SR (27.150)—by 0.4–1.2 dB, but still outperforms diffusion-based methods like StableSR (25.262), OSEDiff (23.867), and DiffBIR (25.431). Kernel estimation comparisons (Fig. 4, Fig. 5) show that KernelGAN, IKR, MLMC, and DKP all produce kernels biased toward Gaussian or motion-blur shapes, while KernelFusion's SIREN-based INR recovers complex non-Gaussian structures (L-shapes, empty/filled squares, real camera-jitter kernels) with high fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

KernelFusion extends the cross-scale patch similarity principle from Michaeli & Irani (2013) and KernelGAN (Bell-Kligler et al., 2019) by embedding it within a zero-shot patch-diffusion framework (inspired by SinFuSion, Nikankin et al., 2023) that is trained on the single LR input image, eliminating any dependence on an external training distribution. The kernel estimation component departs from the CNN/MLP architectures used in prior explicit kernel methods (KernelGAN, MLMC, DKP) by employing a SIREN-based Implicit Neural Representation, which the authors argue avoids the implicit smoothness bias that limits prior methods to Gaussian and motion-line kernels. The overall pipeline fuses three ideas—patch diffusion for local statistics, a DIP-style U-Net for global structure, and an INR for unrestricted kernel recovery—into a single jointly-optimized reverse-diffusion process, whereas prior zero-shot methods (KernelGAN + ZSSR) required a separate two-step kernel-then-SR pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most important contribution is the empirical demonstration that all current SOTA blind-SR methods (DPSR, DCLS, SwinIR, StableSR, RealDAN) perform worse than plain bicubic interpolation on non-Gaussian downscaling kernels, exposing a fundamental out-of-distribution fragility in the field. KernelFusion establishes the feasibility of truly unrestricted, image-specific kernel recovery in a zero-shot setting, opening a new research direction. Key open questions include: (i) reducing the ~20-minute per-image runtime to practical levels, (ii) extending beyond the global-kernel assumption to handle spatially varying blur, and (iii) integrating external large-scale diffusion priors without reintroducing distributional bias. The method's reliance solely on internal image statistics means it forgoes rich external knowledge that could further improve reconstruction quality.
