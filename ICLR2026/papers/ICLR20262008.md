## Problem

Current RLVR methods for LLM reasoning suffer from premature convergence, entropy collapse, and calibration collapse, where model confidence decouples from correctness. Existing exploration strategies either inject undirected randomness (entropy bonuses, ε-greedy) or require auxiliary prediction modules and effective trajectory representations that are difficult to obtain for LLMs, leaving a gap in principled, lightweight exploration guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262008/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem as a text prompt and generates a chain-of-thought reasoning response whose final line must be formatted as 'Answer: $Answer'. Training uses a binary verifiable reward (r=1 for correct, r=-1 for incorrect) from a rule-based checker. The curiosity signals are computed internally: the actor's perplexity over its own generated tokens and the standard deviation across K bootstrapped critic heads, requiring no external modules or trajectory embeddings.

## Method

CDE introduces two intrinsic curiosity signals as exploration bonuses within the RLVR framework. For the actor, a PPL bonus (negative mean log-probability of the response) is added to the reward with an adaptive clipping mechanism (capped at |r|/κ) and an annealing schedule to prevent reward hacking while encouraging exploration of underexplored regions. For the critic, a multi-head bootstrapped architecture with K heads sharing a common LLM backbone is trained on resampled trajectory subsets; the standard deviation across heads serves as a critic-level exploration bonus added to the advantage. Theoretically, the PPL bonus provably penalizes overconfident errors and promotes diversity among correct responses (Theorem 3.1), while the multi-head critic bonus is shown to be a consistent estimator of the classical count-based exploration bonus in linear MDPs (Theorem 3.2). The framework integrates with both GRPO (PPL bonus only) and PPO (PPL + multi-head critic bonus), achieving ~+3 points on AIME benchmarks over standard RLVR.

## Results

On Qwen3-4B-Base, the PPL bonus improves GRPO's average accuracy from 48.2 to 50.6 (+2.4), with a notable +6.6 point gain on AIME24 Pass@16 (48.5 vs. 41.9). Multi-head PPO (K=4) lifts the PPO baseline from 46.5 to 48.5 average, and K=16 reaches 48.6, with up to +11.8 points on AIME25 Pass@16 (45.5 vs. 33.7). On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, the PPL bonus adds +2.1 points (32.3→34.4) for GRPO and +3.7 points (30.5→34.2) for PPO with 4 heads. The paper reports an approximate +3 point improvement over standard RLVR on AIME benchmarks overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), the PPL bonus consistently wins: +2.4 average on Qwen3-4B-Base (50.6 vs. 48.2) and +2.1 on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (34.4 vs. 32.3), with the largest single-metric gain being +6.6 points on AIME24 Pass@16. Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017), the multi-head critic (K=4) yields +2.0 average on Qwen3 (48.5 vs. 46.5) and +3.7 on Llama (34.2 vs. 30.5), with a striking +11.8 point improvement on AIME25 Pass@16 (45.5 vs. 33.7). Against i-MENTOR (Gao et al., 2025), the PPL-bonus GRPO outperforms i-MENTOR-GRPO by +1.5 average (50.6 vs. 49.1), and multi-head PPO (K=4) beats i-MENTOR-PPO by +2.2 (48.5 vs. 46.3). The entropy bonus baseline provides only marginal gains (+0.3 on Qwen3-GRPO, +0.4 on Qwen3-PPO), confirming that simple stochasticity is insufficient compared to the structured curiosity signals proposed here.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDE extends the RLVR training paradigm (GRPO, PPO) by injecting curiosity-driven exploration bonuses derived directly from the model's own signals—perplexity for the actor and multi-head critic variance for the critic—rather than relying on external counting mechanisms or auxiliary prediction networks. It departs from count-based exploration (UCB, LSVI-UCB) after demonstrating that SimHash-based pseudo-counts fail in practice due to embedding collapse of reasoning trajectories, and from prediction-based methods (ICM, RND, i-MENTOR) that require training additional modules. The theoretical contribution bridges these lineages: Theorem 3.1 shows the PPL bonus is a sample-specific refinement of entropy bonuses that penalizes confident errors, while Theorem 3.2 establishes equivalence between the multi-head critic variance and classical count-based bonuses under linear MDP assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'calibration collapse'—where standard GRPO training progressively decouples model confidence from correctness—is a practically important diagnostic that connects RLVR training dynamics to the broader LLM hallucination literature and motivates inference-time strategies like self-certainty Best-of-N. The method's lightweight design (only a final-layer dimension change for multi-head critics, negligible compute overhead) makes it easy to adopt in existing RLVR pipelines. Open questions include whether the linear MDP assumption in Theorem 3.2 holds for real LLM reasoning, whether the approach generalizes beyond mathematical reasoning to other verifiable tasks, and whether the calibration benefits persist at larger model scales where entropy collapse may manifest differently.
