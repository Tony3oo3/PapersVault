## Problem

Existing molecular editing methods rely on string-based (SMILES/SELFIES) or continuous latent representations that fail to explicitly encode the discrete, graph-structured topology of molecules, leading to poor scaffold preservation and imprecise localized edits. Additionally, prior approaches lack mechanisms for actively exploring novel editing strategies beyond fixed training data, and performing discrete graph edits while simultaneously satisfying natural language instructions and structural constraints remains technically unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264277/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source molecule as an attributed graph (atom nodes with feature vectors, bond edges with 6 bond-type labels) and a free-form natural language instruction (e.g., 'increase LogP of molecule SMILES'), serialized into a single unified token sequence of variable length with dynamic padding. The output is a complete target molecular graph specifying atom types (from a 51,933-token vocabulary) and a symmetrized bond-type matrix, generated iteratively over 40 denoising steps from a fully masked graph. Edge predictions are symmetrized as (e_{i,j}+e_{j,i})/2 to respect undirected bond constraints.

## Method

MolEditRL uses a two-stage training pipeline built on a RoBERTa-base transformer (12 layers, 768 hidden dim) augmented with a structure-aware attention mechanism that injects bond-level connectivity priors as learnable bias terms into attention scores—source adjacency at all layers, target adjacency at layer 0, and propagated attention from prior layers for deeper layers. Stage 1 pretrains the network via discrete denoising diffusion (2000-step mutual noise schedule) to reconstruct target molecules from progressively masked graphs conditioned on the source structure and instruction, using a cross-entropy loss over atom types, bond types, and instruction tokens. Stage 2 recasts the reverse denoising process as an MDP and applies KL-regularized policy gradient optimization (β=0.1) with x0-parameterization to reduce gradient variance, where rewards (1.0/0.2/0.0) are computed from RDKit and TDC property oracles at the final denoising step. Policy updates are applied at a fixed stride (t_s=50) over the 2000-step diffusion, and top-k=15 sampling is used at inference, yielding a 125M-parameter model that achieves 74% higher editing success rate than the best baseline with 98% fewer parameters.

## Results

On the MolEdit-Instruct benchmark, MolEditRL achieves an editing success rate (Accall, TS≥0.65) of 0.342 on single-property tasks and 0.632 on multi-property tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline DrugAssist (0.236 and 0.532 respectively) by 45% and 19%. MolEditRL also attains the lowest Fréchet ChemNet Distance (FCD) of 7.99 (single-property) and 7.54 (multi-property), compared to 9.42 and 9.68 for DrugAssist, indicating superior distributional fidelity. The model achieves 95.2% chemical validity while using 98% fewer parameters (125M vs. 7B+) than the LLM-based baselines, yielding a 74% improvement in editing success rate over leading methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MolEditRL outperforms all nine baselines across every accuracy metric and FCD on both single- and multi-property tasks. The strongest baseline, DrugAssist (Llama2-7B-based LLM), achieves 0.236 Acc_all (TS≥0.65) on single-property tasks versus MolEditRL's 0.342, a 45% relative gap; on multi-property tasks the gap widens to 0.532 vs. 0.632. SELFIES-based methods (BioT5, MolGen) guarantee 100% chemical validity but produce near-zero structural accuracy (≤0.032), confirming that syntactic correctness alone is insufficient for structure-preserving edits. Among instruction-tuned LLMs, GeLLM³ᴼ_M is the closest competitor on MCS≥0.6 (0.284 vs. 0.364) but still trails MolEditRL by 28% and has a 29% higher FCD. On the C-MuMOInstruct benchmark, MolEditRL (125M params) matches or exceeds GeLLM⁴ᴼ-C models (7B+ params) in 7 of 10 multi-property tasks, with the largest gains on challenging combinations like drd2+hia+mutagenicity+qed (0.286 vs. 0.191 total accuracy). In the oracle-efficiency comparison on the held-out HIA↑ task, MolEditRL achieves 0.466 Acc_all and FCD 7.964 with only 5,000 oracle queries, while GCPN and MolDQN score near zero on accuracy and exhibit FCD values above 13, demonstrating that the diffusion prior dramatically reduces the exploration burden compared to classical RL-based molecular optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MolEditRL sits at the intersection of two recent threads: discrete denoising diffusion for structured data (Austin et al., 2021) and RL-based fine-tuning of diffusion policies (Uehara et al., 2024), both adapted here to the molecular graph editing setting. It departs from the dominant SMILES/SELFIES-based editing paradigm (BioT5, DrugAssist, GeLLM, MolGen) by operating directly on attributed molecular graphs with a structure-aware attention bias that injects bond-level connectivity priors into transformer attention. The two-stage design—discrete diffusion pretraining followed by KL-regularized RL fine-tuning—mirrors the broader trend of using diffusion as a structure-preserving prior and RL for targeted property optimization, but is the first to apply this combination explicitly to instruction-conditioned molecular editing with graph-level structural constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 98% parameter reduction (125M vs. 7B+ baselines) while matching or exceeding their editing accuracy is practically significant for deployment in resource-constrained drug-discovery pipelines, and the MolEdit-Instruct dataset (3M examples, 10 properties) fills a real gap in the molecular editing benchmarking landscape. The demonstrated ability to generalize to entirely unseen properties (BBBP, HIA, hERG) via RL fine-tuning alone—without any pretraining data for those objectives—suggests a scalable path to handling new pharmacokinetic or safety constraints as they emerge. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D conformational editing, multi-step iterative editing workflows, and whether the discrete graph diffusion formulation scales to larger molecules or ring systems where the fixed-size graph padding becomes a bottleneck.
