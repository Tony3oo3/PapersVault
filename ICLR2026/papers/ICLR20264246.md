## Problem

Existing multi-precision LLM quantization methods (e.g., Any-Precision LLM) rely on non-uniform, clustering-based schemes that require centroid table lookups and bit-transposition at inference, introducing significant hardware overhead. Moreover, these methods suffer severe accuracy degradation at extremely low bit-widths (2-bit), restricting practical deployment to 3–4 bits. The paper addresses both limitations by seeking a multi-precision quantization format that supports direct bit-plane computation and maintains strong accuracy even at 2 bits.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full-precision weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n} and produces a set of shared binary bit-planes B_i ∈ {-1,+1}^{m×n} (one per bit level up to p_H) together with per-precision scale factor vectors {α_i^p}. At inference, only the first p bit-planes and the corresponding p scale factors are fetched from memory, so lower-precision requests consume proportionally less bandwidth. Weights are organized in groups of size g=128, and the binary representation uses 0→-1 mapping, making each bit-plane a pure binary tensor amenable to bit-parallel arithmetic.

## Method

AnyBCQ extends Binary-Coded Quantization to the multi-precision setting via a progressive precision expansion mechanism: the model is first quantized at a base precision p_L using greedy initialization followed by alternating least-squares/binary-search refinement, then each subsequent bit level freezes all previously assigned binary codes and introduces a new residual-derived bit-plane whose scale factors are optimized via block-wise reconstruction error minimization (BRECQ-style). Because binary codes are shared across precisions while scale factors are precision-specific, a single model supports inference at any p ∈ [p_L, p_H] with monotonic accuracy improvement. A co-designed CUDA kernel exploits the binary structure by performing LUT-based GEMM directly on each fetched bit-plane (reducing multiplication to add/subtract of activations), accumulating per-plane partial sums weighted by their scale factors, and returning the result after p planes—eliminating both bit-transposition and centroid lookups that plague non-uniform multi-precision kernels.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B, AnyBCQ (Multi-prec.) achieves 58.71% CSR average at 2-bit, substantially outperforming all baselines (ShiftAddLLM: 45.78%, Any-Precision LLM: 39.65%, AWQ: 35.60%) and closing the gap to FP16 (72.72%) to within 14 points. At 3-bit, AnyBCQ reaches 69.30% CSR average, matching ShiftAddLLM (69.33%) and surpassing Any-Precision LLM (66.45%) and AWQ (67.31%). At 4-bit, AnyBCQ attains 72.37% CSR average, within 0.35 points of FP16. In end-to-end decoding, AnyBCQ delivers up to 3.0× throughput over FP16 (245 vs. 105 tok/s at 2-bit on Llama-3.1-8B) and 7–17% higher tokens/sec than Any-Precision LLM across all models and bit-widths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AWQ (uniform quantization), AnyBCQ dominates at every precision: at 2-bit it achieves 58.71% CSR average versus AWQ's 35.60% (a 23-point gap), and at 4-bit it reaches 72.37% versus 71.39%. Against Any-Precision LLM (non-uniform multi-precision), AnyBCQ wins decisively at 2-bit (58.71% vs. 39.65% CSR; 35.32% vs. 24.66% MMLU) and at 3-bit (69.30% vs. 66.45%), but is marginally behind at 4-bit (72.37% vs. 72.57%), where the non-uniform scheme's greater representational flexibility pays off. Against ShiftAddLLM (BCQ-based fixed-precision), AnyBCQ's advantage is largest at 2-bit (58.71% vs. 45.78% CSR), essentially tied at 3-bit (69.30% vs. 69.33%), and slightly behind at 4-bit (72.37% vs. 72.56%). In throughput, AnyBCQ consistently exceeds Any-Precision LLM by 7–17% tokens/sec across Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and Phi-4-14B at all bit-widths, and achieves up to 3.0× speedup over FP16 cuBLAS in GEMV latency, confirming that the hardware efficiency of direct bit-plane computation translates into practical end-to-end gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyBCQ extends Binary-Coded Quantization (BCQ), originally formulated as a fixed-precision scheme via greedy-plus-alternating refinement (Xu et al., 2018), into the multi-precision setting through a progressive precision expansion mechanism that freezes previously assigned binary codes and adds residual-derived bit-planes. It departs from the dominant multi-precision approach of Any-Precision LLM (Park et al., 2024), which relies on clustering-based non-uniform quantization requiring centroid lookups and bit-transposition at inference, by replacing the non-uniform codebook with BCQ's binary bit-planes and per-precision scale factors. The optimization leverages BRECQ-style block-wise error reconstruction (Li et al., 2021) for scale refinement, and the inference kernel inherits the LUT-based binary GEMM design from LUT-GEMM (Park et al., 2022) and its accelerator extensions (FIGLUT, iFPU).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnyBCQ provides a practical path to deploying a single LLM that serves heterogeneous service-level objectives (2-bit for latency-critical, 4-bit for quality-critical) with 49% less memory than storing separate per-precision models, and with a kernel that avoids the bit-transpose and centroid-lookup overheads that bottleneck existing multi-precision systems. The significant 2-bit accuracy gain over both non-uniform and other BCQ baselines widens the practical operating range of multi-precision inference beyond the 3–4-bit window that prior methods were confined to. Key open questions include theoretical guarantees for the progressive expansion procedure, whether the shared-binary constraint's accuracy cost at 4-bit can be mitigated, and how the approach transfers to activation quantization or training-time settings.
