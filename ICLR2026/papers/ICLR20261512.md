## Problem

Existing text-to-SQL benchmarks evaluate models on single-turn, unambiguous queries, while real-world database interaction is iterative, stateful, and riddled with ambiguity, execution errors, and evolving user goals. Prior multi-turn benchmarks either freeze conversation history as static context (preventing models from shaping the dialogue) or restrict evaluation to read-only SELECT queries, ignoring the full CRUD spectrum of production database work. BIRD-INTERACT addresses this by restoring dynamic, multi-turn interaction with a live user simulator and full database state mutation.

## Input / Output

The evaluated system receives an underspecified natural-language sub-task, database schema metadata, and a hierarchical knowledge base (HKB) organized as a DAG, then engages in multi-turn dialogue with a function-driven user simulator to resolve injected ambiguities before submitting SQL. Each task comprises two state-dependent sub-tasks (an ambiguous priority query and a follow-up that may depend on modified database state), and correctness is verified by executable test scripts rather than string matching. The system is scored on per-sub-task success rate (online) and a normalized reward (offline) that weights the priority sub-task at 70% and the follow-up at 30%, with additional penalties for requiring a debugging retry.

## Method

The benchmark is constructed by converting single-turn LIVESQLBENCH tasks into interactive scenarios through two annotation strategies: (1) ambiguity injection across three sources—superficial user-query ambiguities (lexical, syntactic, semantic, schema-linking, intent, knowledge-linking), knowledge-base gaps (one-shot masking and multi-hop chain breaking), and environmental noise (NULLs, inconsistent fields)—each paired with a unique SQL-snippet clarification source; and (2) follow-up sub-task generation with a 5-category taxonomy enforcing state dependency between sub-tasks. A key technical contribution is the two-stage function-driven user simulator: an LLM first classifies each model question into one of three constrained symbolic actions (AMB for pre-annotated ambiguities, LOC for AST-based retrieval of relevant SQL fragments, UNA for rejecting inappropriate requests), and only then generates a natural-language response grounded in the annotated ground-truth SQL, preventing the ground-truth leakage and task drift observed in prior LLM simulators. Two evaluation settings are provided: c-Interact (protocol-guided multi-turn dialogue with a fixed clarification budget τ_clar = m_amb + λ_pat) and a-Interact (agentic ReAct-style planning over 9 discrete tools with a weighted action-cost budget B = 6 + 2·m_amb + 2·λ_pat), both supporting budget-constrained stress testing. The suite spans 900 tasks (600 FULL, 300 LITE) covering BI and DM use cases across 22 databases, unfolding up to 11,796 dynamic interactions.

## Results

On BIRD-INTERACT-FULL, even the strongest models achieve very low success rates: in c-Interact, Gemini-2.5-Pro leads with 25.00% priority-sub-task SR and 16.33% overall SR (20.92 normalized reward), while GPT-5 achieves only 14.50% priority SR and 8.67% overall SR (12.58 reward). In a-Interact, GPT-5 reverses its position to lead with 29.17% priority SR and 17.00% overall SR (25.52 reward), followed by Claude-Sonnet-4 at 27.83% and 12.67% (23.28 reward). No model exceeds 17% end-to-end task completion in either setting, and follow-up sub-tasks are consistently harder than priority sub-tasks across all models. Average per-task cost ranges from $0.04 (Gemini-2.5-Pro, c-Interact) to $0.60 (Claude-Sonnet-3.7, a-Interact).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, BIRD-INTERACT evaluates 7 frontier LLMs rather than proposing a new method, so the 'comparisons' are cross-model. A striking finding is the interaction-mode reversal: GPT-5 is the worst performer in c-Interact (14.50% priority SR, 8.67% overall SR) yet the best in a-Interact (29.17% priority SR, 17.00% overall SR, 25.52 reward), while Gemini-2.5-Pro leads c-Interact (25.00% priority SR, 16.33% overall SR) but ranks only 4th in a-Interact (20.33% priority SR, 10.33% overall SR). This suggests that constrained conversational protocols and open-ended agentic planning stress fundamentally different model capabilities. DM (data management) tasks are consistently easier than BI (business intelligence) tasks across all models and settings—for example, in a-Interact GPT-5 scores 58.42% on DM vs. 15.61% on BI priority sub-tasks. The Memory Grafting experiment further shows that GPT-5's c-Interact weakness is primarily a communication/clarification issue rather than a SQL-generation deficit: when given clarification histories from Qwen-3-Coder or O3-Mini, its performance improves substantially. Interaction Test-Time Scaling (ITS) experiments on BIRD-INTERACT-LITE confirm that models like Claude-3.7-Sonnet improve monotonically with more interaction turns, approaching the idealized single-turn (ambiguity-free) baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LIVESQLBENCH for its database infrastructure, hierarchical knowledge base, and single-turn task foundation, converting those static tasks into dynamic multi-turn interactive scenarios. It departs from prior multi-turn text-to-SQL benchmarks (CoSQL, SParC, CHASE) that rely on fixed, pre-recorded conversation transcripts shared across all models, instead introducing a function-driven user simulator that responds adaptively to each system's specific clarification requests. The a-Interact setting draws on the ReAct paradigm for agent-based tool use, while the c-Interact setting extends conversational evaluation with budget-constrained clarification protocols. The full-CRUD scope (INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE, ALTER TABLE) extends beyond the SELECT-only focus of nearly all prior text-to-SQL benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical gap: even GPT-5 solves fewer than 17% of tasks end-to-end, and the memory-grafting experiment reveals that the bottleneck for top models is interactive communication strategy rather than SQL generation ability—a finding that redirects research attention toward dialogue management and clarification planning. The Interaction Test-time Scaling law (monotonic improvement with more interaction turns) suggests that current models underutilize available interaction budget, pointing to a concrete training objective. Open questions include whether free-mode (unbudgeted) evaluation reveals qualitatively different strategies, whether post-trained local user simulators can match the function-driven approach at lower cost, and whether the framework generalizes beyond SQL to other structured-output generation tasks.
