## Problem

Current RAG pipelines rely on static, fixed-size chunking and flat retrieval, making retrieval quality highly sensitive to chunk size and unable to adapt to heterogeneous queries and document structures. This introduces noise from irrelevant chunks, suffers from 'lost-in-the-middle' effects, and scales poorly to large corpora. The paper tackles the need for a query-adaptive framework that dynamically selects chunk granularity while remaining computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

The system takes a user query, a reference corpus segmented into a multi-level chunk hierarchy (sentence through document level), and document metadata (first 1000 tokens). The planner outputs a discrete chunk-size range (level_min, level_max) from the set {sentence, paragraph, subsection, section, document} in JSON format, constrained to under 128 reasoning tokens (~1 s latency). The compression encoder maps a group of fine-grained chunk embeddings into a single high-level embedding in R^d, and the final output is a generated answer produced by a GPT-4o generator over the retrieved top-k chunks.

## Method

SMARTCHUNK introduces three coupled components: (1) a lightweight planner (Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct) that reasons over query intent and document metadata to predict the smallest and largest chunk levels needed, restricting the candidate retrieval set; (2) STITCH, a training framework that alternates between vanilla RL rollouts, hinted RL rollouts (conditioned on short hints extracted from expert traces), and imitation learning on persistently unsolved cases, using a GRPO-style clipped objective with multi-objective rewards (QA correctness, chunk-budget penalty, trace-length penalty, format reward, and pseudo-label alignment); and (3) a Chunk Compression Encoder that directly maps a set of fine-grained SBERT embeddings to a single high-level embedding via L2 regression against GPT-summarized ground-truth embeddings, eliminating repeated LLM summarization calls. The planner is trained on synthetic pseudo-labels generated by a four-stage pipeline (hierarchy construction, initial retrieval, pseudo-label assignment, and diverse multi-model reasoning trace generation spanning 1.5B–671B parameter scales), enabling stable multi-objective optimization without ground-truth chunk annotations.

## Results

On the four in-domain QA benchmarks (NarrativeQA, QASPER, QuALITY, Natural Questions), SMARTCHUNK achieves 56.4% QA accuracy and 82.9% retrieval recall (Recall@5), matching or exceeding the best multi-level baseline MAL RAG (56.1% QA, 84.2% recall) while reducing monetary cost from $0.301 to $0.078 per query (74% reduction) and latency from 4.14s to 3.62s. Compared to single-level chunking baselines, SMARTCHUNK delivers roughly 30% higher QA accuracy (0.564 vs. 0.285–0.363). On the out-of-domain NewsQA benchmark, SMARTCHUNK with only three few-shot examples reaches 90.6% F1, matching MAL RAG (90.7%) at 78% lower cost ($0.032 vs. $0.147).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-level chunking baselines (fixed-size sentence/512, late chunking), SMARTCHUNK wins decisively on QA accuracy (56.4% vs. 25.1–36.3%) and retrieval recall (82.9% vs. 51.7–66.1%), at a modest cost increase ($0.078 vs. $0.006–0.007). Among multi-level structured RAG methods, SMARTCHUNK edges out MAL RAG on QA accuracy (56.4% vs. 56.1%) and substantially reduces cost (74% cheaper: $0.078 vs. $0.301) and latency (3.62s vs. 4.14s); it also beats GRAG (54.7% QA, $0.269, 4.20s) and RAPTOR (52.6% QA, $0.398, 3.21s) on both accuracy and cost, though RAPTOR has slightly lower latency. On the out-of-domain NewsQA benchmark, SMARTCHUNK with three few-shot examples nearly matches MAL RAG on F1 (90.6% vs. 90.7%) and retrieval recall (96.7% vs. 97.1%) while using only 22% of the monetary cost ($0.032 vs. $0.147), demonstrating strong cross-domain transfer of the planner and compression encoder without any in-domain fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a lighter-weight alternative to tree- and graph-structured RAG methods (RAPTOR, MAL RAG, GraphRAG) that build full hierarchical chunk structures with expensive LLM summarization at every level. It builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for the planner's RL training objective, and on the broader RL-for-reasoning literature (DeepSeek-R1, DAPO) for the STITCH training loop. The key conceptual departure is replacing costly LLM-based summarization for high-level chunk embeddings with a lightweight compression encoder trained to match those embeddings, and replacing static or exhaustive hierarchy construction with a query-adaptive 1.5B planner that selects only the necessary chunk levels per query.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for RAG deployments: matching or slightly exceeding SOTA tree/graph RAG accuracy while cutting monetary cost by ~30% and reducing latency, using only a small 1.5B planner and a fine-tuned SBERT-based compressor. The out-of-domain result (matching MAL RAG with 3 few-shot examples at 25% cost on NewsQA) suggests the planner generalizes well, which is critical for real-world systems serving diverse document types. Open questions include scalability to very large corpora (the paper argues for it but tests only moderate-sized datasets), whether the compression encoder degrades with more aggressive compression ratios, and extension to multimodal or deep-research settings the authors mention as future work.
