## Problem

When AI4S surrogate models replace expensive PDE solvers as RL simulation environments, their deterministic discretization errors do not average out with more data, systematically biasing policy learning. No prior framework quantifies how the surrogate's grid resolution should be coordinated with the RL agent's discretization to minimize total computational cost while preserving PAC guarantees on value estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265081/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input the PDE system's dominant modal growth rate λ₁, spatial dimension d, boundary-actuation topology (determining scaling exponents α, β), and the discretization tuples for both the AI4S surrogate (H_w, S_w, A_w) and RL agent (H_r, S_r, A_r), along with target accuracy ε and confidence δ. It outputs a closed-form optimal resolution ratio K* (e.g., K* = (7/4·exp(1/λ₁))^{1/3} for tokamak control), the minimal computational cost N*(ε) as a function of system parameters, and a PAC sample complexity bound of the form O(H_r³ S_r A_r · log(1/δ)). Unlike standard RL analyses that output a learned policy, the output here is a resource-allocation prescription that prescribes how to split a fixed compute budget between surrogate fidelity and RL exploration.

## Method

The paper develops a unified ε-N framework built on three coupled analyses. First, a spectral separability argument (via Fréchet linearization and modal decomposition) establishes that the RL timestep must satisfy Δt ≲ 1/λ₁ to prevent observation uncertainty from exceeding grid-cell boundaries, making PAC guarantees achievable. Second, a ρ-K analysis derives the forward projection error rate ρ = 1 − 1/(λ₁/H_r + 1 + C₁/K^d), showing that in the high-resolution regime ρ = O(1/H_r + 1/K^d), where K is the spatial resolution ratio between RL and AI4S domains. Third, by imposing a computational balance condition H_r³ S_r A_r ∼ H_w S_w A_w and minimizing the resulting cost function Cost(K) = H_r³ K^{α+β} · (log(1/δ)/ε²) · (1/(1 − 1/H_r − 1/K^d))², the paper derives closed-form optimal K* values that depend on system-specific scaling exponents (α, β) determined by boundary vs. interior actuation topology. The framework is validated empirically on four systems—tokamak MHD control, turbulent airfoil regulation, teppanyaki heat diffusion, and cart-pole stabilization—using tabular RL, DQN, and PPO, confirming the predicted non-monotonic sensitivity to resolution and the N^{1.6} cost penalty for off-optimal parameter choices.

## Results

The ε-N framework yields closed-form optimal resolution ratios K* for four physical systems: (7/4)^(1/3)·exp(1/(3λ₁)) for tokamak plasma control, (5/3)^(1/2)·exp(1/(2λ₁)) for airfoil control, (7/3)^(1/2)·exp(1/(2λ₁)) for teppanyaki heat sequencing, and 2·exp(1/λ₁) for cart-pole. Empirically, tabular RL on cart-pole achieves its minimum at (K=1.5, log_{H_w}H_r=1/3) with ~4,500 samples, while DQN reaches its optimum at (K=2.0, log_{H_w}H_r=1/2) with only ~450 samples. Deviating from the optimal resolution ratio incurs a computational cost scaling of approximately N^1.6 relative to the optimal N to reach the same 1% value-function accuracy. On the PDE-governed teppanyaki task, PPO confirms the predicted optimum at (K*=6, log_{h_w}(h_r)=1/3), and configurations at K=8.0 fail to converge entirely, validating the learnability boundary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing AI4S-RL methods in the traditional sense; instead, it compares resolution configurations and RL algorithms under a unified ε-N cost framework. Tabular value iteration (the theoretical oracle) requires ~4,500 samples at its optimum, while DQN exploits state similarity to reduce this by an order of magnitude to ~450 samples at its own optimum. The worst tabular configuration (K=2.5, log_{H_w}H_r=2) needs ~46,000 samples—a 10-fold increase over the optimum—confirming the predicted N^1.6 penalty for off-optimal resolution. On the teppanyaki PDE task, PPO across 25 (K, log_{h_w}h_r) configurations shows the same non-monotonic cost landscape, with the theoretical K*=6 matching the empirical minimum total cost. The key finding is that optimal resolution ratios are algorithm-robust: tabular RL and DQN peak at nearby K values (1.5 vs. 2.0), and PPO on the PDE task confirms the same K* predicted by Theorem 3, indicating that the resolution trade-off is governed by system physics rather than the specific RL algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical PAC sample complexity bounds for tabular RL (Azar et al., 2017; Dann et al., 2017) to a setting where transition dynamics are corrupted by deterministic discretization errors from AI4S neural surrogates, rather than stochastic noise that averages out. It departs from robust RL frameworks (Derman et al., 2021; Agarwal & Zhang, 2022), which target irreducible distributional uncertainty, by instead exploiting the structured, resolution-dependent nature of numerical PDE errors to derive principled resolution-sample trade-offs. On the scientific computing side, it builds on neural operator error bounds (Kovachki et al., 2023) and classical PDE control theory (Pontryagin, 2018), but is the first to formally connect surrogate grid resolution to downstream RL policy quality through a unified ε-N framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled, closed-form guidance for co-designing AI4S surrogate resolution and RL discretization under finite compute budgets, revealing that 'finer is always better' is false and that optimal resolution ratios follow system-specific fractional scaling laws. Key limitations include the restriction to tabular RL for theoretical tractability (though DQN/PPO experiments suggest the trends transfer), the treatment of surrogates as fixed rather than adaptively refined, and the focus on deterministic discretization errors while ignoring neural approximation and generalization errors. Open questions include extending the bounds to function approximation settings, incorporating adaptive surrogate refinement within the RL loop, and validating the PDE-specific predictions (e.g., tokamak K* formula) on actual high-fidelity MHD simulations rather than the 2D heat equation used here.
