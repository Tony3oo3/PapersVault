## Problem

Existing sketch-to-image diffusion models treat the complete sketch as a static spatial constraint, ignoring the rich preference information encoded in the progressive drawing process. More critically, standard preference alignment methods (DPO, SPO) fail under dual text-and-sketch constraints because noise-only perturbations produce insufficiently diverse latent samples, yielding weak optimization gradients that cannot meaningfully improve aesthetic quality without sacrificing sketch fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264504/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a full sketch drawing sequence {s_1, …, s_N} (stroke-by-stroke from first mark to completed drawing) as input, and produces a single RGB image as output. It is fine-tuned via LoRA on ControlNetXL (SDXL-based) at 256×256 resolution, trained exclusively on the Sketchy dataset (12,500 images, 75,471 sketches across 125 categories). Notably, the model also operates with only the final sketch or even edge maps without a real stroke sequence, by constructing a pseudo initial sketch for the rollback mechanism.

## Method

SketchEvo introduces two complementary mechanisms. During training, Sequence-Guided Preference Optimization (SGPO) replaces SPO's noise-only candidate pool construction: at each denoising step, K sketches sampled from different completion stages of the drawing sequence serve as diverse conditional inputs, producing meaningfully divergent latent samples whose aesthetic score gaps provide strong DPO-style gradients (threshold 0.8 vs. SPO's 0.4). During inference, the Sequence-Guided Rollback (SGR) mechanism extends ZigZag's denoise-invert-denoise cycle by conditioning the forward denoising on the complete sketch s_N with text, while the inversion step uses an earlier, more abstract sketch s_n with text; this asymmetry amplifies the cumulative information gain from both textual semantics and structural sketch priors, transferring the learned aesthetic preferences into the final image while preserving fidelity to the user's drawing intent.

## Results

On the Sketchy dataset, SketchEvo achieves the best scores across all five evaluation metrics: ImageReward 1.18, HPS v2 30.08, PickScore 22.41, LPIPS-Sketch 0.15, and CLIP-Score 24.15. The closest competitor, ControlNet-SPO, trails by 0.57 in ImageReward, 2.39 in HPS v2, and 0.37 in PickScore. On unseen datasets (QuickDraw, AnimeDiffusion, FSCOCO), SketchEvo consistently leads in aesthetic scores (e.g., HPS v2 of 30.22, 31.57, and 30.31 respectively) while maintaining competitive sketch fidelity, demonstrating strong generalization from training solely on Sketchy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SketchEvo outperforms all eight baselines on every human preference metric (ImageReward, HPS v2, PickScore) and CLIP-Score. Against the strongest preference-aligned baseline, ControlNet-SPO, SketchEvo gains +0.57 ImageReward (1.18 vs 0.61), +2.39 HPS v2 (30.08 vs 27.69), and +0.37 PickScore (22.41 vs 22.04), while also improving LPIPS-Sketch from 0.17 to 0.15. Against the strongest structural baseline, ControlNet, SketchEvo achieves dramatically higher preference scores (ImageReward 1.18 vs 0.004; HPS v2 30.08 vs 24.08) at the cost of slightly higher LPIPS-Sketch (0.15 vs 0.11), reflecting the inherent trade-off between aesthetic quality and strict sketch reproduction. The ablation variant SGPO (without the SGR rollback mechanism) already surpasses ControlNet-SPO on preference metrics but degrades sketch fidelity (LPIPS-Sketch 0.20), confirming that the SGR mechanism is critical for recovering structural alignment. On generalization to unseen datasets, SketchEvo leads ControlNet-SPO by +0.46 ImageReward on QuickDraw, +0.99 on AnimeDiffusion, and +0.44 on FSCOCO, while matching or exceeding sketch similarity in most cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: sketch-conditioned controllable generation (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, VersaGen, ControlNet++) and preference alignment for diffusion models (DPO, D3PO, SPO). It departs from both in a specific way: rather than treating the final sketch as a static spatial constraint and relying on Gaussian noise perturbations to create diverse sample pairs for preference optimization, SketchEvo repurposes the temporal drawing sequence (first stroke through completion) as a structured source of semantic and structural diversity. The training component (SGPO) modifies SPO's candidate-pool construction, while the inference component (SGR) extends the ZigZag-style rollback mechanism to jointly account for text and sketch information gain, building on the classifier-free guidance framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap where existing sketch-to-image models produce structurally faithful but aesthetically flat outputs, especially for amateur sketches, by showing that drawing dynamics carry preference-relevant information that static sketches discard. The on-the-fly generation capability (producing coherent images from a few initial strokes) has direct implications for interactive creative tools. Limitations include reliance on having actual stroke sequences (though edge-map transferability is demonstrated), training on a single dataset (Sketchy), and a slight regression in sketch fidelity (LPIPS-sketch 0.15 vs. ControlNet's 0.11) that reflects the aesthetic-fidelity trade-off. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D sketching, and whether the sequence-guided diversity insight transfers to other conditional modalities (e.g., depth maps, pose skeletons) that also have natural construction sequences.
