## Problem

Supervised pathology segmentation models are restricted to the few pathology classes annotated in existing datasets, missing all other findings. Existing unsupervised visual anomaly segmentation (UVAS) methods adapted to medical CT suffer from domain shift (relying on ImageNet-pretrained encoders), require anomaly-free training data, or use hand-crafted positional encodings that lack anatomical relevance in unaligned CT scans. This paper addresses these gaps by introducing a fully self-supervised density-based UVAS framework with domain-specific features and learned contextual conditioning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D CT volumes preprocessed to 1.5×1.5×2.25 mm voxel spacing with intensities clipped to [-1000, 300] HU, rescaled to [0,1], and CLAHE-normalized. At inference, the volume is divided into overlapping 96×96×64 patches, and the three-model pipeline (descriptor, condition, density) is applied patch-wise with predictions averaged in overlapping regions to produce a full-volume voxel-level anomaly map. After distillation into a single 350M-parameter UNet, the output becomes a binary segmentation mask via a sigmoid head for supervised fine-tuning.

## Method

Screener is a three-stage density-based UVAS pipeline. First, a UNet-like descriptor model is pretrained via dense VICReg (or InfoNCE) self-supervised learning on overlapping 3D crops, producing full-resolution feature maps that are crop-equivariant and color-jitter-invariant. Second, a condition model trained identically but with an additional random masking augmentation learns voxel-wise contextual embeddings that are invariant to pathology presence while retaining anatomical and patient-level information. Third, a density model (Gaussian or Glow-based normalizing flow) learns the conditional distribution of descriptors given conditions, with anomaly scores computed as negative log-conditional densities via 1×1×1 (or 3×3×3) convolutions. The key insight is that masking-invariant conditioning simplifies the conditional density enough that a simple Gaussian model matches normalizing flows. Finally, the entire pipeline is distilled into a single regression UNet using MSE loss on anomaly score maps, enabling supervised fine-tuning with BCE+Dice loss for downstream segmentation.

## Results

In the fully unsupervised setting, Screener achieves voxel-level AUROC of 0.96 (LIDC), 0.87 (MIDRC), 0.90 (KiTS), and 0.93 (LiTS), substantially outperforming the best baseline (Patched Diffusion Model at 0.87/0.76/0.76/0.80) and the density-based MSFlow (0.71/0.67/0.63/0.63). In the low-shot fine-tuning setting (25 labeled cases per fold), Screener reaches Dice of 0.31 on LIDC (a 49% relative improvement over nnUNet from scratch at 0.21), matching supervised pretraining (0.29) and outperforming all other self-supervised baselines on LIDC. On KiTS and LiTS, Screener (0.43 and 0.48) is competitive with VoCo (0.49 and 0.49) but slightly behind.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the unsupervised setting, Screener decisively outperforms all six UVAS baselines on every dataset for both AUROC and Dice. The largest gaps are against reconstruction-based methods (Autoencoder, f-AnoGAN, Patched Diffusion), which overfit to pathologies in training data and fail to reconstruct fine-grained normal details, and against MSFlow, whose ImageNet-pretrained descriptors suffer severe domain shift on CT (AUROC drops to 0.63–0.71). Synthetics-based DRAEM and MOOD-Top1 generalize poorly to real medical anomalies, peaking at 0.83 AUROC on KiTS/LiTS versus Screener's 0.90/0.93. In the fine-tuning setting (25 labeled cases), Screener achieves the best LIDC Dice (0.31, +49% over nnUNet from scratch), surpassing supervised STU-Net pretraining (0.29) and all SSL baselines including VoCo (0.20), MAE (0.24), SwinUNETR (0.16), and DAE (0.15). On KiTS, Screener (0.43) trails VoCo (0.49) by 6 points, and on LiTS it is within 1 point of VoCo (0.48 vs 0.49), making VoCo the strongest competitor in the fine-tuning regime. 2D-only methods (GroundingDINO, LVM-Med) consistently underperform all 3D baselines, confirming the importance of volumetric context.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the density-based UVAS framework (CFlow-AD, MSFlow) by replacing hand-crafted sinusoidal positional encodings with learned, masking-invariant conditioning variables, and by substituting ImageNet-pretrained or supervised medical encoders with dense self-supervised features (VICReg/InfoNCE objectives adapted from VADER and VICRegL). The key conceptual departure is that dense SSL, previously used for natural-image feature learning, is repurposed here to produce domain-specific 3D CT descriptors that are more discriminative for pathology than supervised alternatives like STU-Net. Additionally, the paper introduces a novel use of UVAS as a self-supervised pretraining strategy: the three-model Screener pipeline is distilled into a single UNet, enabling supervised fine-tuning for downstream segmentation tasks—a framing not present in prior UVAS or medical SSL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Screener addresses a fundamental practical limitation in medical imaging: supervised models can only detect the few pathology classes annotated in existing datasets, while unlabeled CT volumes are abundant. The large-scale evaluation (30K training volumes, 1,820 test scans across four datasets) provides the first comprehensive benchmark of UVAS methods for 3D CT, and the finding that a simple Gaussian density model with learned conditioning matches normalizing flows offers a practical simplification for future work. Key open questions include the validity of the rarity assumption for common pathologies, generalizability beyond CT to other modalities, and how performance scales with model size and data volume. The honest negative result—that Screener-based pretraining does not improve over training from scratch when full labeled data are available—clarifies the regime where this approach is most valuable (low-data fine-tuning).
