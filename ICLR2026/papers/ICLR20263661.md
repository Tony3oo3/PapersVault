## Problem

Federated bilevel optimization is vulnerable to Byzantine attackers, but existing defenses are either limited to single-level problems or rely on sub-loop hypergradient estimation (e.g., BILANTINE) that incurs high communication and computation costs. The paper asks two fundamental questions: what is the algorithm-independent lower bound on asymptotic error induced by Byzantine attackers in bilevel optimization, and can a single-loop algorithm achieve this bound while remaining communication- and computation-efficient?

## Input / Output

Each honest client i holds local upper-level loss f_i(x,y) and lower-level loss g_i(x,y) sampled from heterogeneous distributions, and per round computes three stochastic gradient vectors (D_x, D_y, D_z) from locally sampled mini-batches. The server broadcasts the triple (x^k, y^k, z^k), applies a (δ,κ)-robust aggregation rule to the collected vectors, and updates all three variables simultaneously; the auxiliary variable z is projected onto an ℓ₂-ball of radius ρ after each update to maintain boundedness. The final output is a uniformly random iterate x̂ from the trajectory, guaranteed to satisfy E[‖∇Φ_H(x̂)‖²] ≤ ε.

## Method

BR-FedBi extends the SOBA single-loop framework to the Byzantine-resilient federated setting by introducing an auxiliary variable z that approximates the Hessian-inverse-vector product [∇²_yy g_H]^{-1}∇_y f_H, decomposing hypergradient estimation into three simultaneously updated subproblems (upper-level x, lower-level y, and auxiliary z) without any inner-loop iterations. Building on this, BR-FedBiM incorporates Polyak's heavy-ball momentum on the local gradient estimates, which reduces the variance-related asymptotic error from O(κσ²) to O(κ(1−β²)σ²), and by taking β→1 eliminates this term entirely, matching the derived lower bound Ω(δ(ζ_f²+ζ_g²)) for optimal Byzantine resilience. BR-FedBiP further integrates the PAGE probabilistic gradient estimator with a Bernoulli-sampled refresh probability p, achieving the optimal sample complexity O(ε^{-1.5}) under mean-squared smoothness. The paper also establishes the first algorithm-independent lower bound on asymptotic error for Byzantine-resilient bilevel optimization, showing that both upper- and lower-level heterogeneity (ζ_f and ζ_g) contribute to the irreducible error floor.

## Results

On the heterogeneous MNIST hyper-representation task, BR-FedBiP achieves a worst-case test accuracy of 82.58% (Trimmed Mean) and 81.36% (Median) across four Byzantine attacks (ALIE, BF, IPM, RN), compared to BILANTINE's worst-case of 41.84% and 46.02% respectively. On CIFAR-10 (homogeneous), BR-FedBiM attains a worst-case accuracy of 63.18% (TM) and 63.16% (Krum), while BILANTINE failed to converge entirely. In terms of efficiency, the single-loop algorithms require only 3 communication rounds per epoch versus 8 for the sub-loop BILANTINE, and complete 200 training epochs in ~3,140 s versus ~7,200 s for BILANTINE on MNIST.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is BILANTINE (Abbas et al., 2024), a sub-loop Byzantine-resilient bilevel algorithm. On heterogeneous MNIST, all three proposed single-loop variants (BR-FedBi, BR-FedBiM, BR-FedBiP) dramatically outperform BILANTINE: worst-case accuracy improves from 41.84–46.02% (BILANTINE, TM/Median) to 81.28–82.58% (proposed methods), a gap of over 35 percentage points. Under the RFA aggregator the gap is even more extreme (19.96% vs. 70.80–72.76%), and under CWMed BILANTINE drops to 51.02% while BR-FedBiM reaches 69.37%. On CIFAR-10 (homogeneous), BILANTINE failed to converge after extensive hyperparameter search, so no direct numerical comparison is possible; the proposed methods achieve worst-case accuracies of 62.09–63.18% depending on aggregator. In terms of wall-clock time, BR-FedBi completes 200 MNIST epochs in ~3,140 s versus ~7,200 s for BILANTINE, and requires only 3 communication rounds per epoch versus 8, confirming the single-loop design's efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SOBA single-loop bilevel framework (Dagréou et al., 2022) to the Byzantine-resilient federated setting, introducing an auxiliary variable z to approximate the Hessian-inverse-vector product without sub-loop iterations. It departs from the only prior Byzantine-resilient federated bilevel algorithm, BILANTINE (Abbas et al., 2024), which relies on sub-loop hypergradient computation requiring multiple communication rounds per iteration. The theoretical analysis builds on the (δ,κ)-robustness framework of Allouah et al. (2023) and the lower-bound methodology of Karimireddy et al. (2020a), extending both from single-level to bilevel optimization, while incorporating Polyak's momentum and the PAGE variance-reduction estimator as targeted modifications to achieve optimal resilience and sample complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first tight characterization of asymptotic error in Byzantine-resilient federated bilevel optimization, showing that both lower- and upper-level heterogeneity contribute to the irreducible error floor and that the breakdown point depends on heterogeneity coefficients rather than being fixed at 1/2. The single-loop design reduces per-epoch communication from 8 rounds (sub-loop) to 3, with wall-clock times roughly halved in experiments, making Byzantine-resilient bilevel training practically viable. A key open question, acknowledged by the authors, is whether the convergence guarantees extend to the milder (ζ,B)-dissimilarity assumption, which would cover more realistic non-i.i.d. data distributions. The empirical evaluation is limited to small-scale tasks (MNIST, CIFAR-10 with MLP/CNN), leaving open whether the theoretical advantages persist at larger scale or with more complex architectures.
