## Problem

Training-free subject-consistent generation (SCG) methods such as ConsiStory and StoryDiffusion enforce identity by sharing self-attention keys and values across a batch of images, which entangles spatial feature updates and severely collapses pose and layout diversity. No existing training-free approach simultaneously achieves strong subject consistency while preserving the pose diversity inherent to vanilla diffusion sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263862/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a reference image x_id generated from an identity prompt (e.g., 'a hyper-realistic digital painting of a fairy') and N attribute prompts specifying scene-specific descriptions, CoDi produces N target images at 1024×1024 resolution that share the reference subject's identity while exhibiting diverse poses and layouts. The method operates entirely in the latent space of a pre-trained SDXL backbone over 50 denoising steps, requiring no parameter updates; subject masks are extracted from cross-attention maps at the final timestep, and the OT transport plan is computed once per target image from the final-step features.

## Method

CoDi exploits the frequency decomposition of diffusion denoising—low-frequency pose/layout forms early, high-frequency identity details form later—via a two-stage strategy. In the early steps (first 10 of 50), Identity Transport (IT) independently aligns each target's masked subject features with the reference's via optimal transport: a cosine-similarity cost matrix and cross-attention-derived importance weights define the OT problem, solved by network simplex, so reference features are 'mosaicked' into the target's spatial layout, preserving pose while injecting identity. In the later steps (last 40 of 50), Identity Refinement (IR) applies selective cross-image attention where each target attends only to the top-α most salient reference tokens (saliency scored by the OT plan), avoiding the entangled multi-image attention that causes pose collapse. This decoupling—coarse pose-preserving transport early, fine-grained identity refinement late—lets CoDi match vanilla SDXL's pose diversity (0.0758 vs. 0.0772) while achieving the best subject consistency scores across CLIP-I, DINO-v2, and DreamSim.

## Results

On the ConsiStory+ benchmark, CoDi achieves the best subject consistency across all three metrics: CLIP-I 0.8809, DINO-v2 0.8514, and DreamSim 0.2136 (lower is better). It also attains the highest pose diversity score of 0.0758, nearly matching Vanilla SDXL (0.0772) while far exceeding ConsiStory (0.0621) and StoryDiffusion (0.0605). Prompt fidelity is competitive at 0.9041, ranking second only to ConsiStory (0.9148). In a user study with 39 participants, CoDi received 45.21% preference, surpassing the second-best method (StoryDiffusion at 21.20%) by over 24 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDi outperforms all four training-free baselines on every subject consistency metric (CLIP-I, DINO-v2, DreamSim), with the largest margin over Vanilla SDXL (e.g., DreamSim 0.2136 vs. 0.3139). Against ConsiStory and StoryDiffusion, CoDi achieves substantially higher pose diversity (0.0758 vs. 0.0621 and 0.0605 respectively), closing the gap to Vanilla SDXL (0.0772) while maintaining superior consistency. Compared to 1Prompt1Story, CoDi improves both consistency (DINO-v2 0.8514 vs. 0.8233) and pose diversity (0.0758 vs. 0.0662). The only metric where CoDi trails is prompt fidelity: it scores 0.9041 versus ConsiStory's 0.9148, though it remains comparable to Vanilla SDXL (0.9082) and StoryDiffusion (0.9038). In a qualitative comparison with the training-based method UNO (Appendix E), CoDi produces more diverse layouts and better prompt adherence while remaining training-free.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoDi departs from the dominant cross-image attention paradigm used by ConsiStory and StoryDiffusion, which share self-attention keys and values across a batch of images to enforce consistency. Instead, it repurposes optimal transport from the classical OT literature to align reference subject features to each target image independently in early denoising steps, motivated by the frequency decomposition of diffusion (low-frequency pose/layout forms early, high-frequency identity details form later). The two-stage IT+IR design is a novel architectural choice that combines this transport-based coarse alignment with a selective top-α cross-attention refinement in later steps, drawing on the saliency scores derived from the OT plan itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoDi resolves a long-standing trade-off in training-free SCG: prior attention-based methods achieve consistency by entangling spatial layouts across images, inevitably collapsing pose diversity. By transporting identity features per-image via OT, CoDi preserves each target's pose structure while still anchoring subject identity, and the user study (45% preference vs. 21% for the runner-up) confirms the perceptual benefit. Limitations include reliance on cross-attention quality for subject masks (rare but real failures), higher inference time (~155s vs. ~114s for ConsiStory), and evaluation restricted to human subjects on a single benchmark. Open questions include whether the OT formulation scales to very long story sequences, non-anthropomorphic subjects, or can be made more computationally efficient than the network simplex solver.
