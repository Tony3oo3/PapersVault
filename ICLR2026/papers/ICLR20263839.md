## Problem

Federated inference schemes (one-shot FL, edge ensembles, federated ensembles) combine predictions from multiple client-local models but have largely neglected robustness to corrupted or adversarial client outputs. This paper formalizes the problem of robust federated inference, provides the first theoretical robustness analysis showing that even simple averaging-based aggregators are vulnerable to basic attacks, and introduces a defense that achieves state-of-the-art robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263839/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The aggregator receives n probit vectors (each a probability distribution over K classes, i.e., points in the simplex Δ^K) produced by client-local classifiers on a shared input x, where up to f < n/2 clients may return arbitrarily corrupted simplex vectors of hidden identity. The output is a single class label in [K]. The system is evaluated across vision (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 with ResNet-8 and ViT-B/32) and text (AG-News with DistilBERT) modalities under heterogeneous data splits (Dirichlet α ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.8}).

## Method

The proposed DeepSet-TM aggregator uses a permutation-invariant DeepSet architecture (two-layer MLP ρ applied per-client, averaged, then mapped by two-layer MLP μ) trained via multi-step FGSM adversarial training where f random clients' probits are perturbed each iteration. The key insight is that DeepSet's order-invariance reduces the adversarial search from permutations P(n,f) to combinations C(n,f), making adversarial training tractable with only N ≪ C(n,f) sampled adversary subsets. At inference time, coordinate-wise trimmed mean (CWTM) robust averaging is inserted between ρ and μ, reducing sensitivity to corrupted probits without increasing training cost. The paper further derives formal robustness certificates (Theorems 1–2) bounding the robustness gap in terms of corruption fraction f/n, inter-client dissimilarity σ_x, and the margin of the aggregated probit, and introduces a new Strongest Inverted Attack (SIA) to stress-test defenses.

## Results

DeepSet-TM achieves worst-case accuracy of 48.2% on CIFAR-10, 49.6% on CIFAR-100, and 77.5% on AG-News (n=17, f=4, α=0.5), improving over the strongest static baseline (CWTM) by 4.7–22.2 percentage points. The advantage is most pronounced under the strongest attacks (SIA white-box and PGD-cw), where baselines drop 35–40 points on CIFAR-10/100 while DeepSet-TM limits the drop to 20–30 points. On AG-News, DeepSet-TM retains 83.2% accuracy under PGD-cw compared to 53–55% for all baselines. Scalability experiments (n=10/17/25, f=3/4/5) confirm consistent gains of 6.1–20.3 points over the best baseline as the system scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepSet-TM outperforms all three static robust aggregators (CWTM, GM, CWMed) on worst-case accuracy across all three datasets, with the largest margins on CIFAR-10 (+21.0 over CWTM, +22.8 over GM, +20.4 over CWMed) and AG-News (+22.2, +24.9, +24.3 respectively). On CIFAR-100, the gap is smaller but still consistent: +4.7 over CWTM, +10.6 over GM, and +7.9 over CWMed. Against COPUR (Liu et al., 2022), DeepSet-TM shows a dramatic advantage under the logit-flipping attack, where COPUR's accuracy on CIFAR-10 drops to near random chance (due to its logit-space sensitivity) while DeepSet-TM remains stable at 67.6%. DeepSet-TM achieves the highest accuracy in 14 out of 18 dataset-attack combinations, with only minor drops (≤2.3 points) in the remaining four cases. The scalability study further confirms that DeepSet-TM's advantage grows with system size, improving worst-case accuracy by 9.2%, 6.1%, and 20.3% over the best baseline at n=10, 17, and 25 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: permutation-invariant aggregation (DeepSet, Zaheer et al. 2017), Byzantine-robust averaging (Allouah et al. 2023, CWTM/CWMed), and adversarial training (Madry et al. 2018). Prior federated inference work (one-shot FL, edge ensembles, federated ensembles) optimized clean-setting accuracy but largely ignored adversarial corruptions, while prior robust aggregation methods (COPUR, robust voting) either operate in logit space, assume fixed Byzantine identities, or require repeated client interactions. The key departure is casting robust federated inference as an adversarial ML problem over the probit simplex, exploiting DeepSet's permutation invariance to reduce the adversarial search from P(n,f) to C(n,f), and composing adversarial training with test-time robust averaging—a combination not previously explored in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal robustness analysis of federated inference, providing both theoretical certificates (Theorems 1–2) and a practical defense (DeepSet-TM) that substantially outperforms prior methods. The finding that robust averaging alone is insufficient due to argmax discontinuity, and that test-time robust averaging can be layered on top of an adversarially trained DeepSet without increasing training cost, offers a clean and deployable recipe. Limitations include evaluation on only three datasets with up to 25 clients, reliance on Lipschitz assumptions that may be loose in practice, and the adversarial training overhead (N sampled adversary subsets per epoch). Open questions include scaling to hundreds of clients, applicability to generative or multi-task inference, and interaction with differential privacy constraints.
