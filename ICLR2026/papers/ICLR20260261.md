## Problem

Traffic flow modeling requires simultaneously capturing microscopic vehicle interactions and macroscopic flow dynamics, yet existing methods adopt either a purely microscopic or macroscopic perspective, failing to model the nonlinear bidirectional coupling between scales. A fundamental gap remains in jointly modeling vehicle trajectories and traffic flow density within a unified, computationally tractable framework that also avoids the need for historical trajectory data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260261/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only a single current-state snapshot: ego and surrounding vehicle states (2D position, velocity, lane ID, and size class) within a 3-lane, 90 m × 60 m perception window, organized into a 6-nearest-neighbor directed graph. It produces the ego vehicle's future position trajectory and the traffic density field evolution over a prediction horizon T_f, with density estimated via Gaussian KDE (25 m bandwidth) at each vehicle node. The history-free design means no prior frames or tracked trajectories are required, relying solely on the Koopman operator's Markovian property.

## Method

MMCKM lifts both macroscopic and microscopic dynamics into Koopman observation spaces where evolution becomes linear. On the macro side, the LWR advection-diffusion PDE is discretized onto a vehicle-centric Lagrangian graph (not a fixed Eulerian grid), yielding an antisymmetric advection operator (energy-preserving) and a PSD diffusion operator (nonnegative entropy production), with edge weights learned by GNNs; a Koopman encoder (GNN), linear evolver K_Z, and MLP decoder predict density evolution, regularized by a commutator penalty and spectral alignment loss that couples K_Z to the graph-PDE operators. On the micro side, a family of five scenario-specific Koopman evolvers (parameterized by spectral radius, oscillation frequency, and actuation bounds) is selected by an Intent Discriminator (MLP-based MoE), while a CrossAttention module serves as the Koopman actuation operator, injecting macroscopic flow state as a bounded control input into vehicle dynamics with ISS guarantees. The entire system is trained end-to-end under supervised losses, and at inference only GNN forward passes and linear matrix iterations are needed, giving O(kNd + Td²) complexity.

## Results

On NGSIM (RMSE, meters), the history-free MMCKM with 0.1 s operator interval achieves 0.33 m at 1 s and 1.63 m at 3 s, while the 1.0 s interval variant reaches 2.93 m at 5 s—both substantially outperforming the history-free CV baseline (0.64 m at 1 s, 5.62 m at 5 s). On HighD, the optimal operator interval of 0.4 s yields an ADE of 1.65 m, balancing dynamic fidelity against numerical stability. The method requires only a single current-state snapshot (no historical trajectories), yet matches or exceeds history-dependent SOTA methods at longer horizons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the history-free CV baseline, MMCKM wins at every horizon for both operator intervals: the 0.1 s variant cuts 1 s RMSE from 0.64 to 0.33 m (48 % reduction), and the 1.0 s variant reduces 5 s RMSE from 5.62 to 2.93 m (48 % reduction). Compared to history-dependent methods, MMCKM 0.1 s at 1 s (0.33 m) approaches BAT (0.27 m) and beats MS-STGCN (0.42 m) and Vit-Traj (0.39 m), while MMCKM 1.0 s at 5 s (2.93 m) edges out MS-STGCN (3.05 m) and is within 0.04 m of Vit-Traj (2.89 m). The key distinction is that all history-dependent baselines require 3–8 s of past trajectories, whereas MMCKM operates from a single current-state snapshot. At intermediate horizons (3–4 s), BAT and Vit-Traj still hold a modest advantage over MMCKM 0.1 s, but the 1.0 s interval variant narrows or closes that gap, suggesting that over extended horizons the history-free approach becomes competitive as reliance on initial conditions diminishes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the neural Koopman operator framework of Lusch et al. (2018) and the Koopman control extension of Proctor et al. (2018), repurposing both to jointly model microscopic vehicle dynamics and macroscopic LWR flow evolution within a single linear-operator architecture. Its key conceptual departure from prior traffic modeling is the vehicle-centric Lagrangian graph discretization of the advection-diffusion PDE, which replaces the standard Eulerian grid approach and preserves high-frequency microscopic perturbations that grid methods inherently average out. The bidirectional coupling—micro-to-macro via a learned diffusion term and macro-to-micro via a Koopman-control actuation operator—unifies two traditionally separate modeling paradigms (microscopic trajectory prediction and macroscopic flow simulation) under one history-free, Markovian framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The history-free (single-snapshot) prediction capability is practically significant for real-time autonomous driving deployment, eliminating the trajectory tracking and storage overhead of sequence-based methods while maintaining competitive accuracy. The physics-informed operator design (PSD diffusion, antisymmetric advection, spectral alignment) provides interpretable edge weights that quantify vehicle interaction intensity, a capability absent in grid-based or purely data-driven approaches. However, validation is limited to two highway datasets with KDE-estimated density labels rather than sensor-derived ground truth, and the macro-flow evaluation lacks cross-paper SOTA comparison. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to urban networks with heterogeneous topology, whether the diffusion term's benefit persists with true sensor density, and how the method scales to dense multi-lane scenarios beyond the 6-vehicle k-NN neighborhood.
