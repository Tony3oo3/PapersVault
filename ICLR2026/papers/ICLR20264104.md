## Problem

Existing adversarial scenario generation methods for autonomous driving are locked to a single fixed trade-off between adversariality and realism, producing behavior-specific models that cannot be steered at inference time. Adapting to a new preference (e.g., more extreme for stress testing vs. more realistic for data augmentation) requires expensive retraining, and naive linear scalarization of competing objectives is heuristic and unstable when soft and hard constraints interact. The paper asks whether adversarial scenario generation can be reframed as a multi-objective preference alignment problem that yields a continuous, steerable spectrum of policies without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method perturbs an existing logged trajectory from the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (370 filtered scenarios) rather than generating from scratch. The input context includes the road map geometry, historical states of all agents, and the ego vehicle's recorded future trajectory (9-second horizon at 10 Hz). At test time, a user-specified preference weight μ ∈ [0,1] selects the desired adversariality–realism trade-off; the model generates K candidate trajectories from which the best is selected by a real-time reward ranking. The backbone is DenseTNT, a goal-conditioned motion prediction model that outputs top-k trajectory candidates with confidence scores, modified to bypass non-differentiable NMS for end-to-end preference optimization.

## Method

SAGE introduces two key mechanisms. First, Hierarchical Group-based Preference Optimization (HGPO) decouples hard feasibility constraints (binary map compliance) from soft preference trade-offs (adversariality vs. realism), and constructs multiple preference pairs per scenario from a group of 32 sampled trajectories rather than a single winner-loser pair, improving data efficiency and enforcing absolute feasibility. Second, instead of training one model for a fixed trade-off, SAGE fine-tunes two expert policies (adversarial and realism) from the same pretrained DenseTNT backbone using HGPO with opposing reward weights, then constructs a continuous spectrum of policies at inference time by linearly interpolating their weights θ(λ) = (1−λ)θ_real + λθ_adv. The paper provides theoretical grounding via Linear Mode Connectivity, proving a bounded suboptimality gap for the interpolated model (Theorem 1) and showing that weight-space mixing outperforms output-space ensembling when the reward landscape is concave along the interpolation path (Proposition 1). In closed-loop adversarial training, SAGE integrates a dual-axis curriculum that anneals both the interpolation weight λ (scenario intensity) and the adversarial encounter frequency to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

Against the Replay policy, SAGE (w_adv=1.0) achieves a 76.15% attack success rate and 4.121 adversarial reward while maintaining the lowest behavioral realism penalty (1.429) and map compliance penalty (Cross Line: 1.084) among all high-ASR methods. In closed-loop adversarial training, the SAGE-trained TD3 agent achieves the highest reward (45.14±3.27), lowest cost (0.61±0.04), highest route completion (0.69±0.03), and lowest collision rate (0.31±0.02) compared to CAT, Replay, and Rule-based baselines. SAGE also demonstrates superior generalizability in cross-evaluation, achieving the highest route completion across all held-out adversarial test sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE (w_adv=1.0) achieves a 76.15% attack success rate, trailing CAT (94.85%) and Rule-based (100%) in raw adversariality, but dramatically outperforms them on realism: its behavioral penalty is 1.429 vs. CAT's 8.941 and Rule's 2.798, and its Cross Line map violation is 1.084 vs. CAT's 9.078 and Rule's 7.724 (an 85%+ reduction). Against optimization-based baselines AdvTrajOpt (70.46% ASR) and SEAL (63.93% ASR), SAGE achieves higher adversariality while maintaining substantially lower realism penalties (e.g., Cross Line 1.084 vs. AdvTrajOpt's 10.16 and SEAL's 11.612). GOOSE and KING achieve the lowest ASR (36.07% and 40.85%) with no clear advantage in realism. In closed-loop training (Table 3), the SAGE-trained agent outperforms CAT, Replay, and Rule-based generators on all six metrics, with the largest gaps in reward (+7.44 over CAT) and route completion (+0.11 over CAT). Cross-evaluation (Table 4) confirms SAGE's superior generalizability: it achieves the highest route completion in both CAT-generated (0.678) and Rule-generated (0.655) held-out scenarios, while CAT and Rule-based agents show significant performance drops out-of-distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGE reframes adversarial scenario generation as a multi-objective preference alignment problem, directly borrowing the DPO loss (Rafailov et al., 2023) and extending it via group-based sampling inspired by DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) into a hierarchical scheme (HGPO) that decouples hard feasibility constraints from soft preference trade-offs. The test-time steering mechanism—linear weight interpolation between two expert policies—adopts the multi-objective alignment paradigm from Rewarded Soups (Rame et al., 2023) and Model Soups (Wortsman et al., 2022), which had previously been applied only to LLM alignment. The paper departs from prior adversarial generation methods (CAT, KING, AdvTrajOpt, SEAL, GOOSE) that solve a fixed scalarized objective, by instead learning a continuous spectrum of policies that can be steered at inference without retraining, and provides the first theoretical grounding of this interpolation scheme in the motion-generation domain via Linear Mode Connectivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAGE addresses a practical pain point in AD safety testing: the inability to adjust the adversariality–realism trade-off at inference time without costly retraining, which is critical for both targeted stress testing and closed-loop adversarial training. The closed-loop results showing that SAGE-trained agents generalize better across diverse adversarial distributions (Table 4) suggest that the steerable curriculum prevents overfitting to a single attack style. However, the evaluation is confined to MetaDrive with WOMD scenarios and only two objectives; extending to richer objective sets (novelty, complexity), more diverse simulators, and automated curriculum scheduling remain open. The theoretical bounds (Theorem 1) are informative but rely on strong smoothness/concavity assumptions that are only empirically validated in a single backbone (DenseTNT).
