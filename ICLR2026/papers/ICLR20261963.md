## Problem

Scaling multi-agent Vision-Language Model (VLM) systems is economically unsustainable because existing coordination paradigms—Mixture-of-Agents and knowledge-based routers like KABB—rely on heuristic proxies (consensus, semantic similarity) that are provably cost-agnostic and collapse multi-dimensional uncertainty into scalar scores. This agnostic coordination leads to redundant computation, spiraling operational costs, and suboptimal performance, with no principled mechanism to balance accuracy against the economic cost of invoking additional agents.

## Input / Output

The system receives a standard RGB image paired with a text query and produces a final text answer, but its internal output is a structured uncertainty allocation: the initial epistemic uncertainty vector is decomposed into perceptual, semantic, and inferential components and dynamically redistributed across a pool of five heterogeneous VLMs (qwen2.5vl-72b, gemini-2.0-flash, qwen2.5vl-7b, gemma-3-27b, gpt-4o-mini) via a trading protocol. All models are accessed via API with greedy decoding (do_sample=False) for determinism, and the number of concurrent agents N varies from 1 to 9 across experiments.

## Method

Agora recasts multi-agent coordination as a decentralized micro-economy where epistemic uncertainty is 'minted' into a three-dimensional tradable asset (perceptual, semantic, inferential) and reallocated among agents through a profitability-driven trading protocol. A trade from agent i to j executes if and only if the cost delta ΔC = T_ij·[c_j(1−ξ_j) − c_i] < 0 and the receiver's capacity constraint is satisfied, making each trade a greedy descent step on the global cost function. A market-aware Broker, extending Thompson Sampling with a composite utility function that integrates expected net reward, task-distance decay, strategic uncertainty index, team synergy, and temporal decay, selects the initial handler agent. The algorithm then iterates the trading protocol until no further profitable trades exist, converging to a locally optimal cost-efficient equilibrium without requiring a central planner.

## Results

On five multimodal benchmarks, Agora achieves 79.2% on MMMU (+8.5% over the best individual baseline), 89.5% on MMBench_V11_Test (+1.1%), 44.3% on MathVision (+2.0%), 88.9% on InfoVQA (+1.6%), and 81.2% on CC-OCR (+1.4%). Against routing and multi-agent baselines on MMBench, Agora attains the highest accuracy (89.50%) at a relative cost of 1.00, while KABB-VLM and MOA reach 87.12% and 86.65% at 1.24× and 3.11× cost respectively. The cost-performance ratio analysis shows Agora maintains a superior Pareto frontier across agent pool sizes N=1–9, with accuracy peaking at 89.6% at N=8 and sub-linear cost growth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against individual VLMs (qwen2.5vl-72b, gemini-2.0-flash, qwen2.5vl-7b, gemma-3-27b, gpt-4o-mini), Agora wins on all five benchmarks, with the largest margin on MMMU (+8.5% over gemini-2.0-flash's 70.7%). Against external SOTA models, Agora surpasses InternVL3-78B on MMBench (89.5% vs 87.7%), InfoVQA (88.9% vs 84.1%), and CC-OCR (81.2% vs 80.3%), but trails gemini-2.5-pro-exp-03-25 on MMMU (79.2% vs 81.7%) and MathVision (44.3% vs 63.5%), where the specialized thinking model dominates. Among routing and multi-agent strategies on MMBench (N=6), Agora achieves the best accuracy (89.50%) at the lowest relative cost (1.00×); KABB-VLM and MOA reach 87.12% and 86.65% but at 1.24× and 3.11× cost with higher residual uncertainty (0.21, 0.25 vs Agora's 0.16). Cost-reducing routers (FrugalGPT, RouteLLM, EmbedLLM, HybridLLM) cut cost to 0.73–0.91× but sacrifice 8–9.6 accuracy points and yield higher final epistemic uncertainty (0.27–0.33). In the MAB strategy ablation on MMMU, Agora's Thompson Sampling broker outperforms KABB selector by 3.0% accuracy and all RL-based selectors (PPO, MCTS, A2C, DQN) by 4.5–6.0% accuracy, confirming the economic utility function's critical role.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multi-agent VLM coordination paradigms—aggregation-based (Mixture-of-Agents) and routing-based (KABB, FrugalGPT, RouteLLM)—by reframing coordination as a decentralized market where epistemic uncertainty is a structured, tradable asset. It draws on Thompson Sampling for the broker's agent selection, on comparative advantage and mechanism design from economics for the trading protocol, and on the epistemic/aleatoric uncertainty decomposition from Bayesian deep learning for the asset structure. The key conceptual departure is making the system explicitly cost-aware and uncertainty-structure-aware, which the authors formalize as a provable deficiency ('agnostic coordination') of all prior heuristic approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point—multi-agent VLM systems are expensive to operate—and offers a principled economic framework that reduces cost by ~3× while maintaining or improving accuracy. However, the accuracy gains over the best individual model in the pool are modest (1–2% on most benchmarks), and the framework's complexity (multi-dimensional uncertainty vectors, trading protocols, broker utility functions) raises questions about practical deployability. Open questions include whether the market metaphor scales beyond ~9 agents, how expertise vectors are calibrated in practice, and whether the theoretical guarantees (local equilibrium, convergence) hold under non-stationary task distributions.
