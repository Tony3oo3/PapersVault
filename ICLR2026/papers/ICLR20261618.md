## Problem

Score-based density ratio estimation is theoretically path-invariant—integrating the time score along any smooth interpolation between two densities recovers the exact log-ratio—yet in practice, estimator accuracy and stability vary dramatically with the chosen path schedule. The paper traces this paradox to an overlooked term in the training objective: the path variance of the ground-truth score, which prior methods implicitly treat as a constant when the path is fixed. This term is shown to be the dominant factor driving performance differences across paths, but no existing method explicitly optimizes it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. samples from two distributions p0 and p1 (e.g., a Gaussian base and a complex data distribution) and a query point x, and outputs the density ratio r(x) = p1(x)/p0(x) via numerical integration of the learned time score over [0,1]. Two interpolant regimes are supported: the deterministic interpolant (DI) requiring a Gaussian prior on p0, and the dequantified diffusion bridge interpolant (DDBI) for general p0. The path itself is a learned, low-dimensional object parameterized by K Kumaraswamy mixture components (typically K=5), with either an affine or spherical constraint coupling α(t) and β(t).

## Method

The key theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) proves that the gap between the ideal time-score-matching loss and the tractable sliced time-score-matching loss is exactly the path variance V = ∫₀¹ Var_{p_t}(∂_t log p_t) dt, establishing that minimizing overall estimation error requires jointly minimizing both the model loss and V. The paper derives closed-form, computable expressions for V under both DI and DDBI interpolants (Proposition 4.3), depending only on path coefficients and data moments. To make the functional optimization over path schedules tractable, the path α(t) is parameterized as 1 − F_φ(t) where F_φ is the CDF of a K-component Kumaraswamy Mixture Model, which inherently satisfies boundary conditions (α(0)=1, α(1)=0) and monotonicity. The KMM latent parameters φ are optimized via gradient descent on the analytical path variance, while the score network parameters θ are trained on the STSM loss, with an alternating scheme that refreshes a variance-based timestep sampler to stabilize gradient estimates.

## Results

On five real-world tabular density estimation benchmarks, MVP achieves state-of-the-art NLL across all datasets, with the largest gain on BSDS300 (−143.97 vs. −131.90 for the next-best VP path, a >12-point improvement). In the most challenging high-dimensional MI estimation setting (d=160, MI=40), MVP with an affine constraint achieves MSE of 1.02, outperforming the next-best baseline (Trigonometric, MSE 4.18) by over 4× and the Föllmer path (MSE 72.98) by more than 70×. On geometrically pathological f-divergence tasks (Additive Noise, Gamma-Exponential), MVP with a spherical constraint reduces MSE by 3–10× relative to the best fixed-path baseline across all correlation levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVP consistently outperforms all five fixed-path baselines (Linear, Cosine, Föllmer, Trigonometric, VP) on every evaluated benchmark. On tabular density estimation (Table 4), MVP with the spherical constraint achieves the best NLL on HEPMASS (18.01 vs. 18.06 for VP), MINIBOONE (17.81 vs. 18.25 for VP), and BSDS300 (−143.97 vs. −131.90 for VP), while the affine constraint wins on POWER (−0.81 vs. −0.04 for VP) and GAS (−6.95 vs. −6.02 for VP). In high-dimensional MI estimation at d=160, MI=40 (Table 3), MVP-affine achieves MSE 1.02, beating Trigonometric (4.18) by 4×, Linear (9.17) by 9×, VP (43.60) by 43×, and Föllmer (72.98) by 71×. On the pathological Additive Noise and Gamma-Exponential f-divergence tasks (Table 2), MVP-spherical reduces MSE by roughly 3–10× over the best fixed path (e.g., 0.0009 vs. 0.0210 for VP at correlation 0.4 on Additive Noise). The paper notes that the optimal constraint (affine vs. spherical) is task-dependent: affine suits high-discrepancy but geometrically simple distributions, while spherical is preferable for complex, high-dimensional, or non-Gaussian settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous score-based DRE framework of DRE-∞ (Choi et al., 2022) and its DDBI generalization (Chen et al., 2025c), which express the log-density ratio as a path integral of a time-dependent score along a smooth interpolation. The key departure is identifying that the path variance of the ground-truth score—a term previously dismissed as a path-dependent constant in the STSM loss decomposition—is actually the dominant factor driving the practical path-dependence paradox. By borrowing the principle from generative modeling that noise schedules should be learned rather than fixed (Kingma et al., 2021; Guo & Schwing, 2025), the paper introduces a Kumaraswamy Mixture Model parameterization to optimize this term directly, converting an infinite-dimensional functional search into a tractable low-dimensional problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MVP principle provides the first theoretically grounded, data-adaptive path selection for score-based DRE, eliminating the need for heuristic path choices that have been a persistent source of instability, especially under the density-chasm problem. The closed-form path variance expressions (Proposition 4.3) are a reusable technical contribution applicable to any score-based method with a known interpolant structure. Open questions include whether the MVP principle transfers to other score-based generative models (the authors suggest it should), whether the heuristic connection between path variance minimization and Lipschitz constant control can be made rigorous, and whether lighter parameterizations or OT-based formulations could reduce the computational overhead of path optimization.
