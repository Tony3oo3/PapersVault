## Problem

Existing rotation-sensitive representational metrics like the soft-matching distance force every unit in two neural populations to be matched, producing spurious correspondences when populations contain noisy, inactive, or genuinely non-corresponding units common in fMRI recordings and deep networks. This inflates distances, obscures true signal, and prevents reliable identification of which model or subject shares the most genuine correspondence with a reference population.

## Input / Output

The method takes two tuning-curve matrices X ∈ R^{M×Nx} and Y ∈ R^{M×Ny} (e.g., fMRI voxel responses to M natural scenes, or DNN layer activations to M images), mean-centers and unit-norm-scales them so that inner products equal Pearson correlations, and produces a partial transport plan T* whose near-zero row/column sums identify unmatched units, a scalar alignment correlation score d^corr, and a continuous ranking of every unit by its participation in the optimal matching. The regularization parameter s (fraction of mass to transport) is selected automatically via an L-curve elbow heuristic, removing the need for manual thresholding.

## Method

The core contribution replaces the equality marginal constraints of the standard soft-matching transportation polytope with inequality constraints (row sums ≤ 1/Nx, column sums ≤ 1/Ny) and a total-mass constraint sum(T) = s < 1, yielding a partial optimal transport problem solved via the Chapel et al. (2020) dummy-point augmentation. The cost matrix uses pairwise cosine distance, and the resulting transport plan T* partitions units into matched and unmatched subpopulations based on their marginal participation. An L-curve heuristic—computing transport cost ζ(s) over a grid of s values and selecting the point of maximal positive curvature via centered second finite differences—automatically determines how much mass to match without knowing the noise level a priori. For the practically relevant task of identifying the top or bottom X% most-aligned units, a single O(n³ log n) optimization at the chosen s yields rankings nearly identical to an O(n⁴ log n) brute-force ablation that iteratively removes each unit and re-solves the full transport problem.

## Results

On the Natural Scenes Dataset (NSD), partial soft-matching (ParSM) achieves higher cross-subject voxel alignment precision than standard soft-matching (SM) in 13 of 15 visual region pairs, with the largest gains for V1d+V2v (0.881→0.971) and V2d+V3v (0.833→0.971). In the model-identification simulation, ParSM correctly ranks the true model higher (s_partial = 0.715 vs. 0.645), whereas standard soft-matching inverts the ranking (0.339 vs. 0.415). For unit ranking in ResNet-18 layers, a single ParSM optimization at O(n³ log n) cost reproduces the alignment scores of brute-force ablation (O(n⁴ log n)) at every threshold, while correlation-based ordering degrades alignment scores dramatically.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard (fully balanced) soft-matching distance (Khosla & Williams, 2024), which forces all units into correspondence. On the NSD voxel-alignment task (Table 1), ParSM outperforms SM in 13 of 15 region pairs, with the most dramatic improvements where anatomical boundaries are ambiguous (V1d+V2v: 0.881→0.971; V2d+V3v: 0.833→0.971), while SM and noise-ceiling thresholding (ε=0.1, 0.3) remain close to each other. In the model-identification simulation, ParSM correctly selects the model sharing all 100 signal units (score 0.715) over the model sharing only 80 (0.645), whereas SM inverts this ranking (0.339 vs. 0.415) because forced matching of 60 noise units inflates the distance to the true model. For unit/voxel ranking in ResNet-18 and NSD, ParSM at a single L-curve-selected regularization reproduces the alignment scores of the O(n⁴ log n) brute-force ablation baseline at every removal threshold, while the cheaper correlation-based heuristic (pairwise Pearson from a single SM plan) fails catastrophically, removing units that are actually critical for alignment and producing sharply degraded scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the soft-matching distance (Khosla & Williams, 2024), an OT-based metric for rotation-sensitive neuron-level correspondence, by relaxing the strict mass-conservation constraint to a partial optimal transport formulation. The partial OT solver follows Chapel et al. (2020), who augmented the cost matrix with dummy nodes to discard unmatched mass, and the L-curve heuristic for selecting the matched-mass fraction is adapted from classical Tikhonov regularization (Cultrera & Callegaro, 2020). Conceptually, the paper positions itself against rotation-invariant metrics (CKA, RSA, Procrustes, CCA) that cannot resolve single-unit correspondence, while departing from the balanced soft-matching framework that forces all units into alignment regardless of correspondence quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap in representational comparison: biological recordings and DNNs routinely contain noisy, inactive, or architecture-specific units that standard soft-matching must force into spurious correspondences. The computational advantage—achieving near-brute-force rankings with a single O(n³ log n) optimization rather than O(n⁴ log n) iterative ablations—makes unit-level alignment feasible at realistic population sizes. Key open questions include the generality of the L-curve heuristic for selecting matched mass, the fact that partial OT violates the triangle inequality (limiting use in clustering or metric-space analyses), and whether the O(n³ log n) cost scales to very large populations. Recent partial Wasserstein variants preserving full metric properties (Raghvendra et al., 2024) offer a natural direction for future integration.
