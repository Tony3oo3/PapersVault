## Problem

Safety-aligned LLMs exhibit a brittle generalization gap where a simple tense shift (e.g., 'How to make a Molotov cocktail?' → 'How did people make a Molotov cocktail?') bypasses refusal mechanisms, yet the internal causal mechanisms responsible for this vulnerability remain poorly understood. Existing alignment methods (SFT, DPO, representation steering) operate at the output-distribution level and, when forced to close this specific gap, produce severe side effects such as catastrophic over-refusal or knowledge loss rather than a targeted repair.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen LLM's weights plus a small set of paired prompts (100 JBB-Behaviors jailbreak prompts reformulated into past- and present-tense variants, each with 20 reformulations judged by GPT-4.1). It first produces a sparse set of identified vulnerable attention heads per model (e.g., 11 heads for Llama-3.1-8B), then trains only d_head-dimensional scaling vectors for those heads (a few thousand parameters total), and finally outputs a full set of updated model weights after preventative fine-tuning. The scaling vectors are detached after training, so the final artifact is a standard LLM checkpoint with no additional inference-time overhead.

## Method

ASGUARD is a three-stage, mechanistically-informed pipeline. (1) Circuit construction: using EAP-IG edge attribution, the paper builds sparse circuits for 'False-to-True' (jailbreak succeeds in past tense but fails in present) and 'Always-False' (refused in both) prompt pairs, then takes the set difference to isolate attention heads that appear exclusively in successful jailbreak circuits. (2) Identify-then-Scale: a learnable channel-wise scaling vector s_j ∈ R^{d_head} is trained via cross-entropy on the jailbreak dataset to steer the output of each vulnerable head toward a predefined refusal, with all other model weights frozen; the scaling is equivalent to a diagonal pre-multiplication of the head's output projection and can be fused into W_O at zero inference cost. (3) Preventative fine-tuning: the trained scaling vectors are attached as fixed, non-trainable components while the full model is fine-tuned on a refusal dataset, implicitly regularizing the optimizer to discover alternative, non-vulnerable pathways for refusal; the vectors are then detached, leaving a model that retains the safer behavior without the intervention. This 'handicapped training' strategy, inspired by Chen et al. (2025), is the key differentiator from post-hoc activation steering: it forces the model to internalize a robust refusal mechanism rather than merely suppressing a single pathway.

## Results

ASGUARD reduces past-tense jailbreak Attack Success Rate (ASR) from 42%→8% on Llama-3.1-8B, 51%→8% on Qwen2.5-7B, 38%→19% on Gemma-2-9B, and 28%→9% on OLMo-2-7B, while preserving MMLU scores (68.2, 74.0, 72.2, 60.6 respectively). It achieves the highest Overall score (mean of ASR reduction in percentage points and R-Score) on all four models: 52.9, 58.8, 44.6, and 46.3. On additional attacks, ASGUARD reduces GCG ASR from 15%→1% and LogiBreak ASR from 30%→13% on Llama-3.1, confirming out-of-domain generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASGUARD achieves the best Overall score on all four models, outperforming the strongest competitor RepBend by 4.5 (Llama: 52.9 vs. 48.4), 18.2 (Qwen: 58.8 vs. 40.6), 2.7 (Gemma: 44.6 vs. 41.9), and 11.4 (OLMo: 46.3 vs. 34.9) points. SFT (30/70) can drive ASR to 0% on Qwen and Gemma but at catastrophic cost: over-refusal surges to 98.5% on Qwen and MMLU collapses from 72.2 to 43.1 on Gemma, yielding lower Overall scores than ASGUARD. DPO and RepE are nearly ineffective, reducing ASR by only 4 and 1 percentage points on Llama respectively. Circuit Breaker achieves 0% ASR on Llama but its R-Score collapses to 30.6 due to extreme over-refusal (OR-Bench-Hard 84.8%), making it the worst trade-off. RepBend is the closest baseline, achieving 11% ASR on Llama with R-Score 65.7, but ASGUARD matches or exceeds it on both ASR and R-Score across all models while fully preserving MMLU.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASGUARD sits at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability (circuit discovery via EAP-IG, following Nanda 2023 and Hanna et al. 2024) and activation engineering (channel-wise scaling vectors, extending Lee et al. 2025 and Stoehr et al. 2024). It departs from prior safety-alignment methods (SFT, DPO, RepBend, Circuit Breaker) by targeting a minimal, causally-identified set of attention heads rather than reshaping global output distributions, and introduces 'preventative fine-tuning'—a training regime inspired by Chen et al. (2025) where the model learns refusal while the vulnerability is temporarily suppressed by the scaling vectors. The specific vulnerability addressed (tense jailbreaking, Andriushchenko & Flammarion 2025) is a recent addition to the jailbreak landscape, and the paper positions its contribution as a surgical, interpretable repair rather than a broad alignment overhaul.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that mechanistic interpretability can be turned into a practical, parameter-efficient safety tool: identifying a handful of attention heads and training a small scaling vector (fusable into weights at zero inference cost) achieves meaningful ASR reduction without the catastrophic over-refusal or knowledge loss that plagues brute-force fine-tuning. A notable mechanistic finding is that tense-vulnerable heads are entirely distinct from previously identified Temporal Heads, suggesting LLMs encode linguistic tense for safety purposes differently than for factual recall. Key limitations include dependence on localizable causal circuits (unclear for MoE or distilled architectures), sensitivity in small models, and the fact that the method is designed for a specific, known vulnerability class—generalizing to compositional or novel attack surfaces remains open.
