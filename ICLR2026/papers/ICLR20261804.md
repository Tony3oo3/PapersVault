## Problem

Diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) theoretically enable parallel token prediction, yet existing open-source models still require decoding steps proportional to sequence length to maintain quality. The paper identifies the root cause as sequential certainty convergence: although all masked tokens are predicted in parallel at each step, their predictive confidence converges in a left-to-right order, preventing the model from reliably committing multiple tokens simultaneously. This bottleneck has not been addressed by prior acceleration methods, which focus on KV caching or sampling heuristics rather than reshaping the model's internal certainty dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method takes instruction prompts (math word problems, code tasks) as input and produces generated text responses. During training, the clean trajectory is perturbed via semi-autoregressive masking with a fixed 50% masking ratio and task-specific block sizes (32 tokens for LLaDA, 256 for Dream), creating a noisy input where only the active block contains partially masked tokens. At inference, the model uses an entropy-threshold semi-autoregressive remasking strategy, committing tokens whose predictive entropy falls below a task-specific threshold (0.45 or 0.5), reducing decoding steps from 256 to as few as 24–46 depending on the benchmark.

## Method

The core contribution is certainty-forcing distillation (CFD), a self-distillation strategy that reshapes the model's certainty convergence from sequential to parallel. A pretrained dLLM serves as both teacher (generating trajectories via semi-autoregressive remasking) and student (initialized as an identical copy, trained with LoRA). The training objective combines two losses applied only to masked tokens in the active block: a cross-entropy consistency loss that preserves trajectory alignment with the teacher, and a temperature-scaled entropy minimization loss applied exclusively to tokens the student already predicts correctly, explicitly pushing their output distributions toward peak confidence. The semi-autoregressive forward masking scheme (unmasked context blocks, partially masked active block, fully masked future blocks) ensures the training signal mirrors the actual decoding process. This dual objective forces more tokens to reach high certainty in parallel at each step, breaking the sequential propagation bottleneck without altering the generation trajectory.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, dParallel reduces decoding steps from 256 to 30 on GSM8K (8.5× speedup, 76.1% accuracy vs. 75.7% baseline) and to 24 on MBPP (10.5× speedup, 40.8% vs. 42.4%). On HumanEval, it achieves 8.2× speedup with 40.2% accuracy, surpassing the original model's 38.4%. On Dream-7B-Instruct, dParallel achieves 6.9× speedup on GSM8K (82.1% vs. 82.9%) and 8.8× on MBPP (56.2% vs. 58.8%), maintaining near-parity accuracy while dramatically cutting inference steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original LLaDA-8B-Instruct baseline, dParallel achieves 8.5–10.5× speedup while matching or exceeding accuracy (76.1% vs. 75.7% on GSM8K, 40.2% vs. 38.4% on HumanEval). Dual-Cache (Wu et al.) reduces per-step latency via KV caching but keeps 256 steps and degrades accuracy (e.g., 72.9% vs. 75.7% on GSM8K); dParallel dominates on both speed and accuracy. Conf-threshold Decoding (Wu et al.; Yu et al.) preserves accuracy well (75.5% on GSM8K) but only achieves 3.6× speedup, roughly half of dParallel's 8.5×. Consistency Distillation (Luo et al.) reduces steps to 64 but suffers large accuracy drops (69.9% on GSM8K, 19.5% on HumanEval), whereas dParallel at 30–33 steps maintains or improves accuracy. On Dream-7B-Instruct, dParallel similarly outperforms all baselines, achieving 6.9× speedup on GSM8K (82.1% vs. 82.9% baseline) and 8.8× on MBPP (56.2% vs. 58.8%), with the gap to baseline accuracy being smaller than for LLaDA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the masked diffusion language model (MDLM) framework (Shi et al., 2024) and applies its training strategy to two representative open-source dLLMs: LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025), a native dLLM, and Dream (Ye et al., 2025), an AR-initialized dLLM. It departs from prior acceleration efforts that either reduce per-step cost (KV caching, token dropping) or optimize sampling algorithms (confidence-threshold remasking, adaptive parallel decoding) by instead modifying the model's internal certainty dynamics through a new training objective. The key conceptual departure from consistency distillation (Luo et al., 2023) and diffusion forcing (Chen et al., 2024) is the identification of sequential certainty convergence as the true bottleneck and the use of entropy minimization over correctly-predicted tokens as a direct training signal, rather than relying solely on trajectory alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.5×–10.5× reduction in decoding steps while maintaining (or slightly improving) accuracy, achieved with only ~10 hours of LoRA training on 8 consumer-grade GPUs, makes highly parallel dLLM inference practically accessible. The diagnostic of sequential certainty convergence as the core bottleneck provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why naive step reduction fails, guiding future work on few-step dLLMs. Key limitations include reliance on the pretrained model's quality (the method cannot improve a weak base model), a small training set (~10k math prompts), and the observation that Dream required standard random masking instead of semi-autoregressive masking to avoid degeneration toward the AR prior. Open questions include whether certainty-forcing can be integrated into pretraining, whether it scales with larger and more diverse data, and whether the approach generalizes to multimodal or reasoning dLLMs.
