## Problem

Early action prediction requires classifying actions from only a fraction of a video, but prior methods either train a separate model for each observation ratio (requiring 9× more compute) or rely on auxiliary objectives like motion forecasting and graph-based future modelling that do not consistently improve classification. No existing framework trains a single, observation-ratio-agnostic model that generalizes across all test-time ratios while remaining computationally efficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264458/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, EAST receives the first ρ·T_d frames of an RGB video (224×224 crops, T=8 sampled frames) and outputs a single action class label via a linear head over mean-pooled decoder features. The model is entirely agnostic to ρ—it processes whatever present frames are available without needing the ratio as an explicit input. During training, the model additionally consumes the full video (observed + unobserved frames) to compute oracle encoder features for the compound loss, but this information is discarded at test time.

## Method

EAST's core training strategy randomly samples an observation ratio ρ ∈ {0.1,…,0.9} per training example, splits the video into temporally adjacent observed and unobserved segments, and feeds both through a shared VideoMAE-pretrained ViT-B/16 encoder. A 4-layer direct transformer decoder forecasts future features from spatially-pooled present tokens, and a compound loss jointly optimizes classification from both the forecasted decoder output and the oracle encoder features of the full clip, using a single shared linear classifier. To halve training memory, EAST applies difference masking: it ranks tubelets by L1 pixel distance between consecutive temporal patches at each spatial position and retains only the top 50% most-changing tokens, independently for observed and unobserved segments to prevent leakage. This yields a single model that outperforms per-ratio baselines across all observation ratios on NTU60, SSv2, and UCF101 while training on just two 20 GB GPUs.

## Results

EAST sets new state-of-the-art on three major early action prediction benchmarks: NTU60 (87.9% top-1 at ρ=0.9, surpassing TemPr by 10.1 pp on average), SSv2 (64.0% top-1 at ρ=0.9, surpassing TemPr by 7.7 pp on average), and UCF101 (96.5% top-1 at ρ=0.9 with MoViNet, surpassing TemPr by 3.9 pp on average). On SSsub21, EAST achieves 79.3% at ρ=0.9, a 22.7 pp average improvement over TemPr. A key efficiency result is that EAST trains a single model for all nine observation ratios (vs. per-ratio models), reducing training time by 9×, while token masking halves GPU memory usage (36.7 GB → 19.2 GB) with only 0.8 pp accuracy loss on NTU60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAST outperforms all 19 compared baselines across every dataset and observation ratio. The most direct competitor, TemPr (Stergiou & Damen, 2023), is surpassed by 6.8 pp average on NTU60 (with a peak gap of 19.2 pp at ρ=0.3), 28.3 pp on SSv2, 22.7 pp on SSsub21, and 3.9 pp on UCF101; critically, TemPr requires a separate model per observation ratio while EAST uses a single model. On UCF101, EAST (MoViNet) beats ERA (Foo et al., 2022) by 1.3 pp average and the original TemPr† results by 3.9 pp, while matching or exceeding the corrected TemPr‡ reproduction. Multi-modal baselines (DBDNet, TS, MSRNN) that use depth or skeletal keypoints are also outperformed by EAST using RGB alone on NTU60. On EK-100, EAST significantly outperforms TemPr at low observation ratios (e.g., 20.4% vs. 7.4% on All Action at ρ=0.1) but is limited by the weaker VideoMAE ViT-B encoder at high ρ, where TemPr's SlowFast backbone has higher full-video accuracy. The encoder-only EAST_E baseline (no decoder) already surpasses the previous SOTA on SSv2 by 5.4 pp average, confirming that the sampling strategy itself is the primary driver of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAST builds on VideoMAE's masked autoencoder pre-training as its encoder backbone and borrows the concept of token masking from the self-supervised video pre-training literature, but repurposes it for supervised early action prediction by masking temporally repetitive tubelets rather than random spatio-temporal cubes. It departs from prior early action prediction methods (TemPr, DBDNet, AA-GAN, DeepSCN) that rely on auxiliary objectives—motion forecasting, adversarial training, Kalman-filter residual propagation, or graph-based future modeling—by using a purely discriminative compound loss over forecasted and oracle features. The key conceptual departure is the randomized observation-ratio sampling strategy, which replaces the per-ratio model training paradigm used by TemPr and most prior work with a single unified model, and the finding that an encoder-only variant already surpasses previous state of the art.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-model-for-all-ratios approach has direct practical impact: it eliminates the need to train and deploy nine separate models, reducing training compute by 9× and simplifying deployment for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and surveillance. The token masking technique (halving memory with negligible accuracy loss) makes early action prediction trainable on modest hardware (2× 20 GB GPUs), broadening accessibility. Limitations include the lack of causal/streaming inference (the ViT encoder requires sliding-window evaluation), dependence on GPU for near-real-time operation, and the ViT-B/16 encoder capping performance at high observation ratios on EK-100 relative to SlowFast-based methods. Open questions include whether the sampling strategy extends to unsupervised or semi-supervised settings, and how to unify action anticipation (predicting what will happen) with early action prediction (classifying what is happening) in a single framework.
