## Problem

While knowledge distillation is widely used, the theoretical impact of teacher output probabilities on SGD-based student learning remains poorly understood. Specifically, it is unclear how the quality of a teacher's probability estimates (i.e., how well they approximate the true Bayes Class Probabilities) affects the student's convergence rate, gradient noise, and generalization. This paper fills that gap by providing rigorous SGD convergence analysis under both perfect and noisy BCP supervision, and uses the resulting insights to motivate a concrete design choice for teacher models.

## Input / Output

The student network receives standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) along with soft labels from a Bayesian teacher. These soft labels are K-dimensional probability vectors obtained by averaging S stochastic forward passes through the BNN teacher after applying softmax, rather than a single deterministic forward pass. The student outputs a class label, and the distillation loss combines the standard one-hot CE loss with a CE loss against the teacher's averaged soft labels, weighted by a distillation parameter λ.

## Method

The paper provides two complementary contributions. Theoretically, it proves that supervising a student with exact BCPs satisfies the interpolation property (zero per-sample gradient at the optimum), which eliminates the neighborhood term in SGD convergence bounds and doubles the admissible learning rate range compared to one-hot supervision. For noisy BCPs (modeling imperfect teachers), the gradient noise scales linearly with the noise variance ν, and KD is beneficial precisely when this noise is smaller than the inherent variance from one-hot labels. Practically, the paper uses Bayesian Neural Networks as teachers—either trained from scratch via variational inference or converted from pre-trained deterministic networks via Laplace approximation—so that their Monte Carlo-averaged outputs provide better-calibrated BCP estimates. Experiments on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet across 12 teacher-student architecture pairs show that Bayesian teachers yield up to +4.27% student accuracy improvement and up to 30% reduction in convergence noise compared to deterministic teachers.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, students distilled from Bayesian teachers (trained via variational inference) consistently achieve the highest test accuracy across all 12 teacher-student pairs, with gains of up to +4.27% over deterministic-teacher students (ResNet-50 → WRN-16-2: 73.63% vs. 69.36%). Laplace-approximation teachers also improve student accuracy in 11 of 12 pairs (up to +3.16%), despite the Laplace teachers themselves performing slightly worse than their deterministic counterparts. Bayesian students also exhibit up to 30% less convergence noise (measured by accuracy variance over the last 50 epochs) compared to deterministic-teacher students, consistent with the theoretical variance-reduction guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Bayesian teacher (Ours) outperforms all three baselines across every teacher-student pair on CIFAR-100. Against MCMI (Ye et al. 2024), which fine-tunes the deterministic teacher with a conditional mutual information loss, the Bayesian method gains +0.83% to +4.05% in student accuracy (e.g., 73.63% vs. 69.58% for ResNet-50→WRN-16-2), while MCMI itself sometimes degrades student performance below the deterministic baseline (e.g., 75.86% vs. 75.92% for ResNet-18→ResNet-18). Against TTDA (Gawlikowski et al. 2023), which uses test-time data augmentation to produce stochastic predictions, the Bayesian method gains +0.97% to +4.39%, and TTDA occasionally hurts the student (e.g., 69.24% vs. 69.36% for ResNet-50→WRN-16-2). Against MSE (Hamidi et al. 2024), which retrains the teacher with mean-squared-error loss, the Bayesian method gains +1.12% to +5.08%, while the MSE baseline consistently degrades student accuracy by 0.81%–2.70% below the deterministic baseline. The Laplace variant (also Ours) is competitive with MCMI and TTDA in most settings and clearly superior to MSE, but trails the full Bayesian (VI) teacher by 0.5–1.5% in student accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian perspective on knowledge distillation initiated by Menon et al. (2021), who showed that learning from accurate Bayes Class Probabilities (BCPs) reduces the variance of the student's objective and derived excess risk bounds. The key departure is a shift from analyzing statistical risk properties to characterizing SGD convergence dynamics: the authors prove that BCP supervision induces the interpolation property (vanishing gradient noise at the optimum), eliminating the neighborhood term in standard SGD convergence bounds. On the algorithmic side, the paper departs from prior calibration-focused KD methods (MCMI, TTDA, MSE-based teacher training) by advocating Bayesian deep learning models as teachers, leveraging their naturally calibrated probabilistic outputs rather than modifying loss functions or training objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous SGD convergence analysis for KD with probabilistic supervision, yielding a concrete design principle: teacher calibration (proximity to true BCPs) directly controls the student's convergence neighborhood and generalization. The practical recommendation to use Bayesian teachers (via VI or Laplace approximation) is immediately actionable and requires no changes to the student training pipeline. Open questions include finding optimal distillation weight λ as a function of teacher uncertainty, extending the analysis beyond PL/quasi-convexity assumptions that rarely hold for real overparameterized networks, and whether the benefits persist at ImageNet scale. The theoretical assumptions (AS1–AS4) are restrictive, though the authors note empirical generalization to Adam and non-convex settings.
