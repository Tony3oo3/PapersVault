## Problem

Soft Thinking replaces discrete token selection with feeding the full output probability distribution into the next decoding step, and prior work hypothesized this enables parallel exploration of multiple reasoning paths. However, the actual internal mechanism remained unexplored, and preliminary experiments revealed that vanilla Soft Thinking underperforms discrete token decoding. This paper investigates why, uncovering that LLMs behave as single-threaded reasoners dominated by the top-1 token in the soft input, creating a greedy feedback loop that suppresses alternative paths.

## Input / Output

At each reasoning step, the model's output probability distribution over the vocabulary (the Soft Token) is converted into a Soft Input via a weighted sum of token embeddings, with top-k or top-p truncation and renormalization to limit noise and computational cost. The reasoning process continues in this continuous mode until the top-1 token of the Soft Token is the end-of-thinking token, at which point the model switches to standard discrete decoding to produce a readable final answer. The Stochastic Soft Thinking variant additionally applies Gumbel-Softmax (with temperature τ) or Dirichlet sampling (with scaling γ) to the Soft Token before constructing the Soft Input.

## Method

The paper first uses three probing techniques—JS divergence between soft-input and single-token predictions, Logit Lens tracking of hidden-state reasoning paths, and ROUGE-L sequence similarity—to demonstrate the Greedy Pitfall: the model's forward pass acts as a pruner that progressively favors the top-1 token's reasoning path while diminishing others. To break this loop, Stochastic Soft Thinking introduces controlled randomness into the Soft Token construction. Two approaches are explored: Dirichlet sampling (sampling from Dir(γp)) and the Gumbel-Softmax trick (adding Gumbel noise to log-probabilities and applying temperature-scaled softmax). The Gumbel-Softmax approach is shown to be superior because it uniquely satisfies Luce's Choice Axiom, ensuring selection probabilities remain proportional to original utilities, and it provides a tunable trade-off between randomness (high JS divergence from the original distribution) and softness (high normalized entropy) via the temperature parameter, whereas Dirichlet sampling forces a rigid trade-off between these two properties.

## Results

Across three 32B reasoning LLMs (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, QwQ-32B, Skywork-OR1-32B) and eight benchmarks, Stochastic Soft Thinking with Gumbel-Softmax (τ=0.5) achieves average scores of 79.55, 83.63, and 84.62 respectively, outperforming discrete Token Sampling (78.50, 82.35, 82.99) by +1.05, +1.28, and +1.63 points. In stark contrast, vanilla Soft Thinking (without stochasticity) underperforms Token Sampling by 2.3–7.0 points on average, confirming the Greedy Pitfall. The Gumbel-Softmax variant is the only stochastic method that consistently surpasses discrete token decoding across all models and benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is vanilla Soft Thinking (Zhang et al., 2025), which feeds the full output probability distribution as a soft embedding without any stochastic perturbation. Stochastic Soft Thinking (Gumbel-Softmax, τ=0.5) decisively outperforms vanilla Soft Thinking on average across all three models: +8.05 (DeepSeek: 79.55 vs 71.50), +3.57 (QwQ: 83.63 vs 80.06), and +5.41 (Skywork: 84.62 vs 79.21). The Dirichlet variant also improves over vanilla Soft Thinking but by a smaller margin and fails to consistently beat discrete Token Sampling (e.g., 81.39 vs 82.35 on QwQ). Critically, only Gumbel-Softmax surpasses the Token Sampling baseline on all three models, with the largest gain on Skywork-OR1-32B (+1.63 avg) and notable improvements on GPQA-Diamond (+5.04 over Token Sampling) and LiveCodeBench (+1.79). The paper also reports Pass@k on MATH-500 with Qwen2.5 base models (0.5B–7B), where Stochastic Soft Thinking consistently exceeds discrete Token Thinking across all k values, suggesting stronger exploration for future RL integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Soft Thinking framework of Zhang et al. (2025), which proposed feeding full vocabulary probability distributions (Soft Tokens) into subsequent decoding steps to enable continuous-space reasoning. It departs from the theoretical claims of COCONUT (Hao et al., 2024) and related works (Zhu et al., 2025; Gozeten et al., 2025) that Soft Thinking inherently supports parallel exploration of multiple reasoning paths, instead providing empirical evidence that LLMs behave as single-threaded reasoners dominated by the top-1 token. The proposed Stochastic Soft Thinking mechanism repurposes the Gumbel-Softmax trick (Maddison et al., 2014; Kool et al., 2019) from discrete categorical sampling to continuous distribution perturbation, with a theoretical grounding in Luce's Choice Axiom that distinguishes it from the Dirichlet alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying the 'Greedy Pitfall,' this paper reframes the continuous reasoning literature: the failure of vanilla Soft Thinking is not a capacity limitation but a behavioral one, and the fix (controlled stochasticity) is training-free and immediately applicable. The Pass@k results suggest that Stochastic Soft Thinking could serve as a more effective rollout mechanism for RL-based reasoning training, though full RL integration is left as future work. A key open question is whether the single-threaded limitation is an inductive bias of the Transformer architecture and next-token prediction objective that can be overcome through multi-token prediction pre-training or novel architectures, or whether it is a fundamental constraint of autoregressive generation.
