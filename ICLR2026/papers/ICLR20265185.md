## Problem

Conventional multimodal fusion methods project raw features into unified spaces via concatenation or linear transformations, entangling modality-unique characteristics with shared semantics and causing semantic interference where unimodal details disrupt cross-modal relationships. Existing decoupling approaches (e.g., MISA, DMD) address this at a global level but overlook token-level local inconsistencies, while over-alignment risks erasing modality-specific traits. DecAlign targets this gap by explicitly separating heterogeneous and homogeneous components and applying tailored alignment strategies to each.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted unimodal features with heterogeneous temporal lengths and dimensions (768-d BERT/DeBERTa text, 35-d OpenFace/MA-Net visual, 74-d COVAREP/wav2vec audio), which are first normalized to a common temporal length T_s and feature dimension d_s via modality-specific 1D temporal convolutions. The output is a scalar sentiment intensity score (regression, evaluated by MAE and Pearson Corr) or a discrete sentiment/emotion class label (classification, evaluated by Acc-2/Acc-7, F1, WAcc, WAF1 depending on the dataset). The architecture is modality-agnostic in principle, operating on any number M of modalities with per-modality encoders.

## Method

DecAlign first decouples each modality's features into modality-unique (heterogeneous) and modality-common (homogeneous) streams using separate encoders, enforced orthogonal via a cosine-similarity decoupling loss. For heterogeneity alignment, it fits a Gaussian Mixture Model per modality to generate K category prototypes, then solves a multi-marginal optimal transport problem with entropy regularization to align prototype distributions across modalities, supplemented by a sample-to-prototype calibration term and a modality-specific transformer for fine-grained refinement. For homogeneity alignment, it matches latent-space statistics (mean, covariance, skewness) of the shared features across modalities and applies Maximum Mean Discrepancy in a Gaussian-kernel RKHS for non-parametric distribution correction. The aligned heterogeneous features (post-transformer) are concatenated with homogeneous features and passed through a fully connected layer for the downstream task, with the total loss combining task loss, decoupling loss, and weighted heterogeneity/homogeneity alignment losses.

## Results

DecAlign achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four benchmarks and all five metrics. On CMU-MOSI, it reaches MAE 0.735, Acc-2 85.75%, and F1 85.82%; on CMU-MOSEI, MAE 0.543, Acc-2 86.48%, and F1 86.07%. On IEMOCAP (six-class), it attains WAcc 73.35% and WAF1 73.43%, and on CH-SIMS, MAE 0.403 and F1 81.85%. All results are averaged over five independent runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DecAlign outperforms all 13 baselines on every metric across all four datasets. The closest competitors are DMD (graph-based knowledge distillation) and CGGM (classifier-guided gradient modulation). On CMU-MOSI, DecAlign surpasses DMD by 2.51 points in Acc-2 (85.75 vs. 83.24) and 2.27 in F1 (85.82 vs. 83.55), while on CMU-MOSEI the margins are 2.31 in Acc-2 and 2.19 in F1. On IEMOCAP, DecAlign leads CGGM by 1.10 in WAcc and 1.26 in WAF1. On CH-SIMS, the gap over CGGM is 1.73 in F1 (81.85 vs. 80.12) and 0.014 in MAE. Transformer-based baselines (MulT, Self-MM, PMR) and feature-decoupling methods (MISA, FDMER) trail by 3–5 points in Acc-2, indicating that neither cross-attention fusion nor simple disentanglement alone matches the hierarchical alignment strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DecAlign synthesizes two established research threads—feature decoupling (MISA, DMD, FDMER) and cross-modal alignment (contrastive learning, cross-attention transformers, knowledge distillation)—into a unified hierarchical framework. Its key conceptual departure is the explicit separation of alignment into a heterogeneity stream (GMM-based prototypes + multi-marginal optimal transport) and a homogeneity stream (latent moment matching + MMD), rather than treating cross-modal alignment as a monolithic objective. Prior decoupling methods like MISA and DMD focus on global, token-agnostic separation, while DecAlign introduces prototype-level, distribution-aware alignment that operates at a finer granularity, bridging the gap between local structural mismatches and global semantic consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled decomposition of the cross-modal alignment problem into two complementary sub-problems, offering a template that could be adapted to other multimodal settings beyond sentiment analysis. However, the evaluation is confined to four sentiment/emotion benchmarks with three modalities (text, audio, visual), leaving open whether the GMM prototype assumption and multi-marginal OT scale gracefully to more modalities, higher-dimensional feature spaces, or tasks with weaker cross-modal correspondence. The computational cost of fitting GMMs and solving multi-marginal transport at each training step also raises practical questions about efficiency at scale.
