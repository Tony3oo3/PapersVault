## Problem

Existing syntactic probing methods face a fundamental trade-off: trainable networks on hidden states (linear probes, MLPs) risk the probe itself learning the task rather than extracting structure from the model, while attention-based methods over-trust raw attention scores as dependency probabilities without principled filtering. No prior method provides a mathematically rigorous, parameter-free way to estimate the information-theoretic relationship between individual attention heads and specific dependency types, nor to reconstruct fully labeled dependency trees from attention alone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unmasked attention scores (reconstructed from cached Q/K/V to avoid softmax magnitude variation across sentence lengths) from all heads and layers of a decoder LLM, aligned to word level via last-sub-token scores, together with dependency tree annotations from Universal Dependencies 2.9. It produces labeled dependency trees decoded via the Eisner algorithm, per-head per-label binary mutual information values, and full posterior probability distributions f(L|A) for every head–dependency composition. The MI is computed per individual dependency type (binary formulation) rather than over all types jointly, enabling fine-grained head specialization analysis.

## Method

IPBP estimates the joint distribution between discrete dependency labels and continuous attention scores using Kernel Density Estimation on per-label attention score sets, then applies Bayes' theorem to obtain posterior distributions without any trainable parameters. For head importance, it computes binary mutual information per dependency type per head, normalized by the entropy of the binary indicator to yield adaptive [0,1] thresholds for selecting highly syntactical heads. Tree reconstruction pools posteriors across selected heads via MI-weighted geometric mean (logarithmic opinion pooling), constructs a valid multivariate probability space over all dependency types via independent voting, and decodes the final tree with the Eisner dynamic programming algorithm. The entire pipeline requires no network training—only KDE, Bayesian inference, and a fixed decoding procedure—making it transparent and immune to the probe-learning-the-task concern.

## Results

On the English UD 2.9 dataset using open_llama_7b, IPBP achieves 49.1 UAS and 30.6 LAS for labeled dependency tree reconstruction, outperforming all baselines. The enhanced variant IPBP+MIpos further improves to 49.9 UAS and 34.8 LAS. Across four LLMs (Meta-Llama-3-8B, Mistral-7B, vicuna-7b, open_llama_7b) and three languages (English, French, Spanish), UAS ranges from 39.9 to 50.4 and LAS from 21.9 to 30.9. In intrinsic head-selection evaluation (Spearman-R consistency), IPBP achieves the highest column-average score of 0.398, indicating the most consistent head importance rankings with other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPBP outperforms all four main baselines on UAS: 49.1 vs. Probeless (34.8, +14.3), IoU (38.3, +10.8), ElasticNet (41.9, +7.2), and V-Information (41.3, +7.8). On LAS, IPBP (30.6) beats Probeless (20.9), IoU (26.6), and V-Information (20.9) by 9.7–19.7 points, but is marginally behind ElasticNet (31.3) by 0.7 points; the IPBP+MIpos variant closes this gap with 34.8 LAS. Notably, the parameter-free IPBP surpasses the supervised ElasticNet and V-Information methods despite requiring no trainable networks, while V-Information—despite its information-theoretic framing—suffers from the long-tail distribution of dependency labels and achieves only 20.9 LAS. In the intrinsic Spearman-R evaluation (Table 3), IPBP shows the highest consistency with other head-selection methods (column average 0.398 vs. 0.309 for ElasticNet, 0.286 for IoU, 0.267 for Probeless), further validating its head importance estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant supervised-probing paradigm (Hewitt & Manning 2019; Pimentel et al. 2020b) that trains external networks on hidden states, and also from earlier parameter-free attention-based methods (Clark et al. 2019; Htut et al. 2019; Ravishankar et al. 2021) that naively treat attention scores as dependency probabilities. IPBP repurposes information-theoretic probing ideas (Pimentel et al. 2020b; Voita & Titov 2020) in a parameter-free setting by estimating mutual information and Bayesian posteriors over attention scores via kernel density estimation, then decoding labeled trees with the Eisner algorithm. The logarithmic opinion pooling (Heskes 1997) is adapted to balance contributions from multiple attention heads, and the mixed discrete-continuous MI formulation draws on Gao et al. (2017) while avoiding the dimensionality curse through a Bayesian decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free design directly addresses the long-standing concern that deep probing networks may learn the task independently of the probed model, and the fine-grained per-head, per-label MI estimates open a new analytical surface for mechanistic questions (e.g., layer-depth correlation, left/right dependency asymmetry) that prior unlabeled-tree probing could not support. However, absolute tree quality remains moderate (UAS ≈ 49, LAS ≈ 31 on 7B models), baseline comparisons are restricted to a single model (open_llama_7b) due to compute constraints, and the method still requires dependency-annotated training data for density estimation, limiting its applicability to truly unsupervised settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models or benefits from the richer attention patterns of MoE architectures, and whether the KDE-based density estimation can be replaced by more scalable alternatives.
