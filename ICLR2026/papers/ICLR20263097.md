## Problem

Existing LLM-based theorem provers require expert-written formal statements as input, yet real-world mathematical problems are expressed in natural language. The critical autoformalization step—translating informal problems into formal language—remains understudied: evaluation relies on binary compilation checks that miss nuanced semantic errors, and no prior autoformalizer has been trained with dynamic feedback from both syntactic and semantic correctness signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263097/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a natural language math problem statement (originally in Chinese from Gaokao exams, with English translations) and produces a formal Lean 4 theorem statement, which is then passed to a prover to generate a complete machine-verifiable Lean 4 proof. The autoformalization stage generates G=14 candidate formal statements per input, each validated by the Lean compiler (syntactic check up to `:= by sorry`) and LeanScorer (continuous semantic consistency scoring); the best candidate is forwarded to the proving stage with a fixed budget of 32 proof attempts per problem.

## Method

Mathesis-Autoformalizer is the first autoformalizer trained via online reinforcement learning, using GRPO with a composite binary reward that sums Lean compiler verification (syntactic validity) and an LLM-based semantic correctness judgment, initialized from the Kimina-Autoformalizer 7B base model with LoRA fine-tuning. This is followed by Hierarchical Preference Optimization (HPO), where a DPO stage uses prover-derived preferences—formalizations that lead to Lean-verified proofs are preferred over those that fail—aligning the autoformalizer with downstream proof success beyond syntax and semantics alone. For evaluation, LeanScorer decomposes NL statements into subtasks, assigns three-level consistency labels (Match, Minor Inconsistency, Major Inconsistency), and aggregates them via a customized Sugeno Fuzzy Integral to produce a continuous correctness score, achieving 0.92 F1 against human annotations. The Gaokao-Formal benchmark (495 problems from China's college entrance exams, 2008–2025) provides a challenging, contamination-free testbed spanning functions, sequences, analytic geometry, and comprehensive multi-domain questions.

## Results

Mathesis-Autoformalizer-HPO achieves 71% LC+LSC@6 on Gaokao-Formal, surpassing the previous best (Kimina-Autoformalizer at 49%) by 22 percentage points (45% relative gain), and 96% on MiniF2F-test. On the challenging Putnam benchmark, it reaches 30% LC+LSC@6 versus 10% for Kimina and 9% for Herald. In downstream proving (pass@32), pairing Mathesis-HPO with DeepSeek-Prover-V2 yields a 42% relative gain over Kimina-Autoformalizer on Gaokao-Formal, and up to 122% gain over Herald-Autoformalizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest open-source baseline Kimina-Autoformalizer (Wang et al., 2025), Mathesis-HPO improves LC+LSC@6 by 22 pp on Gaokao-Formal (71% vs. 49%), 5 pp on MiniF2F (96% vs. 91%), and 20 pp on Putnam (30% vs. 10%). Against Herald-Autoformalizer (Gao et al., 2025), gains are even larger: 44 pp on Gaokao-Formal (71% vs. 27%) and 27 pp on MiniF2F (96% vs. 69%). Among API models, Mathesis-HPO (a 7B model) outperforms all larger API baselines on Gaokao-Formal LC+LSC@6 (71% vs. 57% for DeepSeek-R1, the best API model) and MiniF2F (96% vs. 84% for DeepSeek-V3), though DeepSeek-R1 retains an edge on Putnam LC+LSC@6 (37% vs. 30%). For semantic evaluation (Table 2), LeanScorer achieves 0.92 F1, outperforming LLM-as-a-Judge (Goedel-Prover, Lin et al., 2025) by 7 pp and Re-informalization (Lean Workbook, Ying et al., 2024) by 27 pp. In downstream proving, replacing Herald with Mathesis-HPO boosts pass@32 by 86–122% across Goedel-Prover, Kimina-Prover, and DeepSeek-Prover-V2 on Gaokao-Formal, demonstrating that autoformalization quality is the dominant bottleneck over prover capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoformalization line of work (Wu et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2023; Kimina-Autoformalizer) by replacing static supervised fine-tuning with online reinforcement learning (GRPO) that directly optimizes for Lean compilability and semantic faithfulness, followed by DPO with prover-derived preferences (HPO). It departs from prior semantic evaluation approaches (LLM-as-a-Judge, Re-informalization) by introducing LeanScorer, which uses subtask decomposition and Sugeno Fuzzy Integral aggregation for continuous scoring rather than binary judgments. The Gaokao-Formal benchmark extends the evaluation landscape beyond formal-to-formal benchmarks (MiniF2F, PutnamBench, FIMO) by specifically targeting the difficulty of autoformalizing complex, multi-domain natural language problems that prior benchmarks either simplify or exclude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies autoformalization as the critical bottleneck in formal theorem proving from natural language, showing that autoformalizer quality improvements (86–122% gains) can exceed prover upgrades (55–95% gains) on challenging problems—a finding that redirects community attention toward the NL-to-FL translation step. The Gaokao-Formal benchmark and LeanScorer provide much-needed tools for rigorous evaluation of autoformalization quality beyond binary compilation checks. Key limitations include the ambiguity in defining formalization correctness (e.g., whether explicit domain declarations are required), the restriction to Lean 4, and the observation that the pipeline remains far from full automation for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the RL+HPO training paradigm transfers to other proof assistants (Isabelle, Coq) and how to resolve the inherent ambiguity in what constitutes a 'correct' formalization.
