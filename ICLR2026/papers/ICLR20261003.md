## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods assume the entire dataset is available upfront and distill it at once, but real datasets are collected incrementally over time. Applying conventional DD sequentially to arriving domain datasets causes catastrophic forgetting—old knowledge is overwritten by the newest domain—and linearly increases storage and training costs. This paper introduces Domain Incremental Dataset Distillation (DIDD), a new problem setting that requires continually distilling sequentially arriving datasets into a single fixed-size synthetic dataset while preserving knowledge from all prior domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of domain datasets {D^t}_{t=1}^T sharing a common label space (e.g., 10 classes for R-MNIST, 50 for Seq-CORe50, 7 for PACS), with images resized to 32×32 (or 128×128 for scalability tests) and a randomly initialized 3-layer ConvNet serving as the frozen feature extractor. The storage budget is fixed at IPC × C synthetic images throughout the entire sequence, so the output is always a single synthetic dataset of constant size regardless of how many domains have arrived. Evaluation trains a fresh model on the final synthetic dataset and measures average accuracy and average forgetting across all domains.

## Method

The core contribution is an asymmetric synthetic data update strategy that assigns distinct per-sample update rates to the stability loss (MMD between current and previous synthetic features) and the plasticity loss (MMD between current real and synthetic features), allowing each synthetic image to specialize in either preserving old knowledge or adapting to the new domain. These per-sample scale parameters α_i and β_i are learned via bi-level optimization within a meta-learning framework: a meta-update of the synthetic images is performed, a meta-loss combining both objectives is computed, and α_i, β_i are updated to minimize it. Penalty terms on the average magnitudes of α_i and β_i prevent the trivial solution of maximizing all rates indiscriminately, enforcing selectivity. A KKT-condition interpretation frames α_i and β_i as sample-wise Lagrange multipliers for stability and plasticity constraints, providing theoretical grounding for the asymmetric update rule.

## Results

On R-MNIST (IPC=1), the proposed method achieves 58.6% average accuracy with 21.0% forgetting, surpassing the Joint upper bounds of DSA (55.4%) and DC (53.7%) while maintaining a fixed-size synthetic dataset. On Seq-CORe50 (IPC=10), it reaches 55.4% accuracy and 38.8% forgetting, outperforming Joint(DSA) (33.2%) and Joint(DC) (34.7%). On PACS (IPC=20), the method recovers over 95% of the Joint(M3D) upper bound (52.1% vs. 54.5%), achieving 10.0% forgetting. Across all settings, the proposed method outperforms the best continual-learning baseline (MAS) by 13–23 percentage points in average accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all continual-learning baselines adapted to the DIDD setting. On R-MNIST (IPC=10), it achieves 62.7% average accuracy, exceeding MAS (45.6%), LF (42.6%), EWC (40.7%), Finetune (39.4%), and LwF (32.8%) by 17–30 percentage points. On PACS (IPC=10), the gap is 13–22 points over the best CL baseline (MAS/LwF at 35.0%). Notably, the method surpasses the Joint upper bounds of DSA and DC on R-MNIST (IPC=1: 58.6 vs. 55.4/53.7), Seq-CORe50 (IPC=10: 55.4 vs. 33.2/34.7), and PACS (IPC=10: 48.0 vs. 46.1/45.3), demonstrating that incremental distillation with asymmetric updates can exceed what is achievable by jointly distilling all domains at once with those methods. The method does not surpass Joint(M3D), which remains the strongest upper bound on Seq-CORe50 (76.3%) and R-MNIST IPC=10 (90.9%), but it narrows the gap substantially while operating under the more realistic incremental setting. EWC and LF perform nearly identically to Finetune, indicating that parameter-level regularization is ineffective when applied to synthetic data updates in this context.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of dataset distillation (DD) and domain incremental learning (DIL). On the DD side, it builds directly on distribution-matching methods (M3D, DSA, DC) that align feature distributions between synthetic and real data, but departs from their core assumption that the full dataset is available upfront and can be revisited repeatedly. On the DIL side, it repurposes the stability-plasticity trade-off and regularization-based continual learning ideas (EWC, MAS, LwF) but applies them to synthetic data updates rather than model parameters. The key methodological novelty is a bi-level meta-learning framework (inspired by MAML) that learns per-sample asymmetric update rates, combined with a KKT-condition interpretation that frames these rates as sample-wise Lagrange multipliers for stability and plasticity constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real datasets are collected incrementally, yet all prior DD methods assume full availability, forcing either linearly growing storage (per-domain distillation) or unrealistic joint access. By formalizing DIDD and showing that a fixed-size synthetic dataset can be maintained across domains with competitive accuracy, the work opens a path toward memory-efficient continual distillation pipelines. Key limitations include the restriction to domain-incremental (not class-incremental) settings, the higher per-domain distillation cost from bi-level optimization (~2.7× Finetune on PACS), and the fact that the method still trails Joint(M3D) — the true oracle with the same DD backbone — on longer sequences. Extending DIDD to class-incremental learning and reducing the meta-optimization overhead are natural next steps.
