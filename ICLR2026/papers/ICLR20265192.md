## Problem

Embodied cognition theory posits that intelligence arises from sensorimotor interaction rather than passive observation, yet it remains unclear whether modern VLMs—trained predominantly on disembodied data—exhibit signs of such cognition. Prior benchmarks examine isolated slices (spatial perception, linguistic planning, primitive object interactions) but lack a unified, objective evaluation that tightly couples egocentric perception with multi-step embodied interaction in everyday activities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265192/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For forward world modeling, the model receives one current egocentric RGB image (512×512), an ordered sequence of textual scene-graph-difference actions (e.g., 'Add Open(fridge)', 'Remove Inside(basket, cabinet)'), and a shuffled set of future observation images, and must output a Python list of indices that reorders the images chronologically. For inverse world modeling, the model receives the current image, an ordered sequence of observation images, and a shuffled set of action descriptions, and must output a permutation of action indices. Step lengths range from 3 to 10, and the online verifier accepts both exact index matches and semantically valid subset matches, reporting Task Accuracy (all-or-nothing) and Pairwise Accuracy (graded per-step credit).

## Method

ENACT frames embodied cognition evaluation as world modeling within a POMDP, where states are symbolic scene graphs extracted from the BEHAVIOR physics simulator and actions are visible scene-graph differences (11 predicate classes such as RightGrasping, OnTop, Open, Cooked). A scalable data pipeline segments raw robot trajectories into key-frames at abstract state changes (using a 40-frame temporal stability filter and cosine-similarity deduplication), then constructs length-L key-frame trajectories via dynamic programming over a DAG of valid transitions, enabling combinatorial sampling of up to C(M,L) trajectories per episode. The two tasks—forward (reorder shuffled observations given actions) and inverse (reorder shuffled actions given observations)—are cast as sequence-reordering VQA, deliberately avoiding photorealistic video prediction to isolate long-horizon interactive visual reasoning. Evaluation uses an online verifier that accepts any permutation consistent with the input constraints, and the benchmark comprises 8,972 QA pairs across 29 long-horizon home-scale activities.

## Results

On ENACT (8,972 QA pairs, step lengths L=3–10), the strongest proprietary models GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro achieve Pairwise Accuracy of 84.62% and 86.10% respectively on forward world modeling at L=3, dropping to 46.93% and 36.98% at L=10. Human annotators maintain 93–97% accuracy across all horizons, revealing a widening gap as interaction length increases. Inverse world modeling is consistently easier than forward for every model (e.g., GPT-5: 86.28% vs. 84.62% at L=3; 55.33% vs. 46.93% at L=10). Among open-weight models, InternVL3.5-241B-A28B and GLM-4.5V lead, reaching 75.79% and 74.30% forward accuracy at L=3, but still trail the best proprietary models by 10+ points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves the highest inverse accuracy at every step length (87.94% at L=3, 56.62% at L=10), while GPT-5 leads on forward at L=3 (84.62%) and maintains the best forward scores at L=7–10 (57.48% at L=7). GPT-5 mini is surprisingly competitive, achieving the best forward score at L=3 (87.50%) and L=7 (58.14%), but degrades faster than GPT-5 at longer horizons. Gemini 2.5 Flash and Flash-Lite show a clear quality gradient within the family, with Flash-Lite trailing Pro by ~18 points at L=3. Claude Sonnet 4 is the weakest proprietary model, scoring 65.65% forward at L=3 and only 20.16% at L=10. Among open-weight models, InternVL3.5-241B-A28B and GLM-4.5V are the strongest, with GLM-4.5V actually surpassing Claude Sonnet 4 on inverse at L=3 (80.59% vs. 73.25%) and at L=10 (37.93% vs. 28.49%). Qwen2.5-VL-72B is competitive at short horizons (78.15% forward at L=3) but degrades sharply. Cosmos-Reason1, trained on embodied data, shows notably more stable long-horizon performance than other ~7B models (24.88% forward at L=10 vs. 16–18% for Gemma-3-27b and Ovis2.5-9B). BAGEL is the weakest model overall, scoring only 30.24% forward at L=3, suggesting its unified multimodal pretraining does not transfer well to structured world-modeling reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ENACT carves new ground at the intersection of embodied cognition evaluation and world modeling. Prior embodied cognition benchmarks examine isolated slices (spatial perception, linguistic planning, primitive object interactions) or rely on subjective taxonomies, while prior world modeling work focuses on generative video prediction or latent dynamics. This paper departs from both by reframing world modeling as discrete sequence reordering over symbolic scene-graph actions from egocentric interaction, grounded in a POMDP with semi-MDP-style key-frame segmentation. The BEHAVIOR simulator provides the physics-based ground truth (continuous poses, contacts) that makes scalable, objective evaluation possible without conflating reasoning ability with image synthesis quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ENACT provides the first scalable, objective benchmark that tightly couples egocentric perception with multi-step embodied interaction, filling a gap the authors identify as 'missing' in the literature. The finding that the human-model gap widens dramatically with horizon length (GPT-5 drops from ~85% to ~47% pairwise accuracy while humans stay at ~95%) is practically critical for anyone deploying VLMs in long-horizon robotic manipulation. The anthropocentric biases (right-handedness, human-like camera intrinsics) raise important questions about generalization to diverse robotic embodiments. Open questions include whether training on embodied data (as with Cosmos-Reason1) can close the gap, whether the scene-graph abstraction is sufficient for richer physical reasoning, and whether the benchmark extends beyond home-scale activities to industrial or outdoor settings.
