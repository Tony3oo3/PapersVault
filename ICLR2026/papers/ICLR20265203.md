## Problem

Reactive Dance Generation (RDG) faces two unresolved challenges: existing methods produce synchronized but artistically sterile duets because they rely on holistic, high-level constraints that miss fine-grained spatial interactions (e.g., subtle whip-like boleo in Tango), and autoregressive sampling over long sequences causes error accumulation and temporal drift that breaks synchronization. No prior approach simultaneously addresses multi-scale spatial fidelity and long-term temporal coherence in a single framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a leader's motion as a holistic sequence of 22-joint SMPL global positions (N×22×3 at 30 fps) and per-frame 54-dimensional music features (MFCCs, deltas, chroma, onset/beat) as conditioning signals. The output is the reactor's motion decomposed into three asymmetric components: upper-body joint positions relative to the reactor's own root, lower-body joint positions relative to the reactor's root, and the leader-reactor root displacement vector. Each component is processed through a dedicated HFSQ stream, and the system generates coherent sequences exceeding 2000 frames (60+ seconds) within ~2 seconds of inference time.

## Method

ReactDance is a two-stage diffusion framework built on a Hierarchical Finite Scalar Quantization (HFSQ) autoencoder that groups motion features into parallel channels and applies cascaded residual FSQ quantization (R=2 stages), naturally disentangling coarse global posture (base layer) from fine-grained local dynamics (residual layer) while avoiding VQ-VAE codebook collapse. A Transformer-based DDPM denoiser operates on the continuous hierarchical latent set with independent loss weights per residual scale, conditioned on leader motion via cross-attention and music via FiLM. For long-sequence generation, Blockwise Local Context (BLC) replaces autoregressive sampling with parallel block synthesis using periodic causal masking and phase-aligned positional encodings, while Dense Sliding Window training (stride 4, window 240) teaches the decoder phase-agnostic boundary transitions that implicitly stitch blocks. Layer-Decoupled Classifier-Free Guidance (LDCFG) assigns independent guidance strengths to each HFSQ scale, enabling separate control over structural stability versus fine-grained interaction detail.

## Results

On the DD100 test set (average sequence length 2066 frames), ReactDance achieves the best solo motion quality with FID_k of 5.57 and FID_g of 7.63, substantially outperforming the nearest baseline Duolando (27.68 / 35.01). For interaction quality, ReactDance attains FID_cd of 14.17 and BED of 0.3863, while the closest competitor Duolando scores 17.49 and 0.3285 respectively. ReactDance also achieves the lowest inter-penetration rate (IPR 7.84%) and the fastest inference time (AITS 1.75 s per sequence), thanks to its parallel Blockwise Local Context sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReactDance outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics. Against the closest RDG-specific baseline Duolando (autoregressive VQ-VAE + RL), ReactDance reduces FID_k by ~5× (5.57 vs 27.68), FID_cd by ~20% (14.17 vs 17.49), IPR by >50% (7.84% vs 17.42%), and inference time by >60% (1.75 s vs 4.41 s). Against the gesture-generation baseline GestureLSM, ReactDance achieves a 2.6× lower FID_k and a 2.8× lower FID_cd, indicating far superior spatial interaction modeling. The music-driven baselines EDGE and TCDiff perform poorly on interaction metrics (FID_cd > 1400) because they lack explicit leader-reactor conditioning; TCDiff does marginally beat ReactDance on IPR (7.58 vs 7.84) and BAS (0.2270 vs 0.2031), but is far worse on all other metrics. InterGen, adapted from multi-human text-to-motion, shows the worst inference speed (5.22 s) and high interaction FID_cd (176.19), confirming that repurposing non-dance architectures for RDG is suboptimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReactDance builds on the diffusion-based motion generation paradigm (DDPM) and extends the classifier-free guidance framework by decomposing the single guidance scalar into per-layer weights (LDCFG). Its tokenizer departs from the VQ-VAE/RVQ-VAE lineage (used in MoMask, MotionGPT, Duolando) in favor of Finite Scalar Quantization, borrowing the grouped residual structure from audio codec work (HiFi-Codec) to create a spatial hierarchy that prior motion tokenizers lacked. For long-sequence generation, it moves away from autoregressive or iterative-inpainting strategies (EDGE, MotionStreamer) toward a non-autoregressive parallel block scheme whose coherence is enforced implicitly through dense sliding-window training rather than explicit stitching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles two persistent gaps in reactive dance generation—fine-grained spatial interaction fidelity and long-term temporal coherence—that prior duet models (Duolando, DuetGen, DanY) have not resolved, and does so with a unified hierarchical representation rather than ad-hoc constraints. The BLC sampling strategy and DSW training principle are potentially transferable to other long-sequence motion or video generation tasks where autoregressive drift is a bottleneck. Key limitations include the absence of explicit semantic labels across HFSQ scales (reducing interpretability), the omission of finger motions due to dataset noise, and evaluation confined to a single dataset (DD100) with adapted baselines, leaving open whether the hierarchical advantage generalizes to other motion domains or larger-scale training.
