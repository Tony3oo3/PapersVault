## Problem

LLMs are expected to serve as effective decision-making agents but consistently underperform in interactive settings due to sub-optimal exploration and an inability to act on their own knowledge. This paper systematically identifies three prevalent failure modes—greediness (premature commitment to high-reward actions leaving up to 55% of the action space unexplored), frequency bias (small models copying the most frequent action in context regardless of reward), and the knowing-doing gap (87% correct rationales but only 21% optimal actions)—and investigates whether reinforcement learning fine-tuning can mitigate them.

## Input / Output

The agent receives task-specific instructions describing the environment, available actions, and objective, concatenated with the most recent C interaction steps (state, action, reward tuples) as text within a 1792-token context window. It generates a CoT rationale followed by an action in the permissive format 'ACTION=X' within a 256-token generation budget (2048 for knowing-doing gap analysis), with the action extracted via regex matching on the last valid pattern and a −5 reward penalty applied if no valid action is found.

## Method

The paper proposes RLFT: fine-tuning pre-trained Gemma2 models (2B/9B/27B) using a PPO-style clipped surrogate objective with a KL constraint (β=0.05) to the reference policy, where rewards come from environment interaction plus a validity penalty, and advantages are estimated via Monte Carlo baselines for fixed-horizon bandits or a learned state-value head with GAE for stateful Tic-tac-toe. The key finding is that RLFT on self-generated CoT rationales increases action coverage by ~12%, counteracts frequency bias, and narrows the knowing-doing gap, but exploration remains sub-optimal compared to UCB. The paper further evaluates seven exploration mechanisms (try-all, ε-greedy, context randomization, context summary, self-correction, self-consistency, and an exploration bonus), finding that a simple +1 reward for untried actions and the try-all strategy yield the largest performance gains. Ablations confirm CoT is essential for RLFT effectiveness (without it, RLFT barely exceeds ICL+CoT) and that increasing the generation budget from 256 to 512 tokens substantially improves performance, approaching 9B-level results.

## Results

On Gaussian MABs (button scenario, σ=1), RLFT lowers cumulative regret for both Gemma2 2B and 9B, with 2B narrowing the gap to its larger counterparts and UCB. RLFT increases action coverage by +12% for 2B after 30K updates (from ~40% to ~52% on 10 arms), partially mitigating greediness. In Tic-tac-toe, RLFT elevates Gemma2 2B's average return from 0.15 to 0.75 against a random opponent and from −0.95 to 0.0 (draw) against optimal MCTS. The knowing-doing gap is quantified at 87% correct rationales but only 21% optimal actions (58% greedy), and RLFT reduces the frequent-action fraction from 70% to 35% for low-repetition contexts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UCB (Auer, 2002) on MABs, RLFT 2B and 9B reduce cumulative regret substantially compared to their ICL counterparts, with 2B closing much of the gap to 9B/27B and approaching UCB, though it does not fully match UCB within the 50-step horizon. Against LinUCB (Chu et al., 2011) on contextual bandits (MovieLens), similar regret reductions are observed for RLFT 2B. In Tic-tac-toe, RLFT 2B dramatically outperforms ICL 2B: average return improves from 0.15 to 0.75 against a random opponent and from −0.95 to 0.0 (a draw) against optimal MCTS, whereas frontier models in prior work barely beat random. SFT on UCB expert data (both behavior cloning and thought cloning) achieves regret comparable to UCB itself, confirming that expert supervision is effective but requires pre-collected trajectories, unlike RLFT which uses self-generated rationales. An exploration bonus (+1 reward for untried actions) during RLFT further increases action coverage from 50% to 70% and narrows the regret gap to UCB, highlighting that reward shaping is critical for eliciting exploration in LLM agents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PPO-style clipped surrogate objective (Schulman et al., 2017) as the RLFT training procedure, but its primary contribution is diagnostic rather than algorithmic. It extends the line of work by Krishnamurthy et al. (2024) and Nie et al. (2024) on LLM exploration in bandits, and by Paglieri et al. (2024) and Ruoss et al. (2024) on the knowing-doing gap, by systematically decomposing LLM decision-making failures into three independent modes (greediness, frequency bias, knowing-doing gap) and quantifying each across model scales. Unlike prior work that primarily reports sub-optimal performance, this paper isolates the causal mechanisms and then tests whether RLFT on self-generated CoT rationales addresses each mode specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic failure-mode decomposition provides actionable diagnostics for practitioners building LLM agents: greediness means restricting action space breadth early, frequency bias in small models motivates RL over pure imitation, and the knowing-doing gap (87% correct rationales but only 21% optimal actions) suggests that reasoning capability alone is insufficient without aligned action selection. Key open questions include whether findings transfer to frontier-scale models, whether RLFT generalizes to longer horizons and more complex stateful environments, and whether intrinsic reward mechanisms can close the remaining exploration gap. The paper explicitly positions itself as foundational analysis rather than a complete solution, leaving the design of robust agentic LLMs as an open problem.
