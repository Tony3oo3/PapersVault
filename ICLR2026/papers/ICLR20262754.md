## Problem

Models for quantifying cellular morphology are typically trained on a single microscopy imaging type with a fixed number of channels, making them non-reusable across biological studies with different technical specifications. The field lacks a large, heterogeneous, multi-channel microscopy dataset that could support pre-training channel-adaptive foundation models at scale, with prior resources being either small proof-of-concept collections or single-modality datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262754/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts multi-channel microscopy images with a variable number of channels (1 to 14+), each channel processed independently through a shared ViT-small backbone at 224×224 resolution (8-bit PNG, original spatial resolution preserved in the dataset). The bag-of-channels mechanism produces per-channel feature vectors that are concatenated into a single embedding, which is then used for downstream tasks including classification, retrieval, and cross-domain generalization without any fine-tuning.

## Method

The paper introduces CHAMMI-75, a curated dataset of 2.8M multi-channel images from 75 heterogeneous biological studies spanning 16 organisms, 223 cell lines, and 25 channel types, built through a multi-phase pipeline of data acquisition, metadata integration, and content-based curation (3D/temporal sampling, control balancing, and k-means clustering on intensity histograms). The top-performing model, MorphEm, uses a DINO-BoC (bag-of-channels) architecture where a single ViT-small backbone processes each channel independently and concatenates the resulting features, trained with DINO self-supervised learning over the full dataset. Ablation studies reveal that diversity in microscopy modality (fluorescence, bright-field, DIC, etc.) is the primary driver of generalization performance, outperforming the effect of channel count or cell line diversity. The paper also benchmarks multi-channel attention (MCA) alternatives and weakly supervised learning variants, finding that BoC outperforms MCA under SSL by up to 19% at a fraction of the compute cost.

## Results

MorphEm (ViT-small, DINO-BoC, trained on CHAMMI-75) achieves the best SSL performance on 3 of 7 benchmarks, most notably on the channel-generalization task CellPHIE (80.51 vs. 71.23 for SubCell, +13%) and the cross-domain task RBC-MC (68.34 vs. 59.10, +15%). On the standard in-domain benchmarks (CHAMMI, HPAv23, JUMP-CP1/2), the larger WSL-trained SubCell models retain the edge (e.g., 53.38 vs. 48.75 on CHAMMI; 69.33 vs. 58.87 on HPAv23). Among SSL-only methods, MorphEm outperforms DINOv2, OpenPhenom, and IDRCell on every benchmark, confirming that the diversity of CHAMMI-75 (25 channel types, 14 microscopy modalities, 16 organisms) is the primary driver of improved generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SubCell (Gupta et al., 2024; ViT-base, WSL on HPAv23), MorphEm loses on the four in-domain benchmarks (CHAMMI −4.6, HPAv23 −10.5, JUMP-CP1 −1.3, JUMP-CP2 −0.7) but wins decisively on the two generalization benchmarks: CellPHIE +9.3 (80.51 vs. 71.23) and RBC-MC +9.2 (68.34 vs. 59.10), and edges out SubCell on IDR-0017 (75.52 vs. 75.37). Against DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023; fixed-RGB SSL adapted via BoC), MorphEm leads on all seven benchmarks, with the largest gaps on HPAv23 (+5.1) and RBC-MC (+8.9). Against OpenPhenom (Kraus et al., 2024; channel-adaptive MAE trained only on Cell Painting), MorphEm wins on six of seven benchmarks, confirming that training exclusively on one imaging modality limits generalization. Against IDRCell (Bourriez et al., 2024; multi-channel model on the smaller IDRCell100k dataset), MorphEm outperforms on all benchmarks, suggesting that CHAMMI-75's 30× larger scale and broader modality coverage provide additional informative signal. In the WSL comparison (Table 2), ChA-MAEViT (Pham et al., 2025) and MCA-SupC (Khosla et al., 2020) both improve over the MCA-SSL baseline on HPAv23 and JUMP-CP1, but remain well below MorphEm's BoC-SSL results on the generalization tasks, indicating that weak supervision helps in-domain but does not substitute for data diversity in cross-domain settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DINO-BoC architecture (De Lorenci et al., 2025) for its core model (MorphEm), but its primary contribution is the data infrastructure that was missing from the channel-adaptive microscopy modeling line. Prior channel-adaptive models (OpenPhenom, CHADA-ViT, ChA-MAEViT) were limited to small proof-of-concept datasets or private data, and the closest public resource (IDRCell100k) contained only 100K images from 79 sources. CHAMMI-75 extends this effort by 30× in image count while introducing substantially greater biological and technical heterogeneity (16 organisms, 25 channel types, 14 microscopy modalities), and pairs it with three new benchmarks (CellPHIE, IDR-0017, RBC-MC) that test channel and cross-domain generalization in ways prior benchmarks did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CHAMMI-75 fills a critical data gap that has constrained the development of truly generalizable multi-channel microscopy models, and the ablation studies provide actionable guidance: modality diversity (not just data volume or channel count) is the dominant driver of generalization performance. The finding that a smaller SSL-trained BoC model outperforms a larger WSL-trained model (SubCell) on novel channel combinations and cross-domain tasks challenges the assumption that more supervision and scale always win for generalization. Limitations include noisy metadata, sparse coverage of some biological variables, and the fact that the best model is only ViT-small due to compute constraints, leaving open whether the benefits scale to ViT-large. The dataset and MorphEm model are publicly released, making this a likely standard resource for the field.
