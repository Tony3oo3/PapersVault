## Problem

Existing input-side defenses for large vision-language models rely on CLIP similarity to detect unsafe images and prepend safety prefixes to prompts, but both steps are fragile: CLIP scores overlap between safe and unsafe samples in complex scenes, and the attention assigned to safety prefixes decays across layers, allowing the model to override its initial refusal with transitional phrases like 'however.' The paper asks whether detection and decoding can be made robust without any retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard RGB image (split into M patches) and a user text prompt. It produces a sanitized image in which patches whose OT transport score falls below a threshold τ are zeroed out, and a safe autoregressive text response generated by the LVLM conditioned on the masked visual tokens plus a safety-aware prefix. No model parameters are modified; the entire pipeline runs at inference time on a single GPU.

## Method

GuardAlign combines two training-free modules. First, OT-enhanced safety detection encodes image patches and N textual variants of C predefined unsafe categories with CLIP, then computes a Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal-transport distance between the patch distribution (weighted by patch-level entropy) and each category distribution; patches with aggregated transport mass below τ are masked to remove harmful visual cues. Second, cross-modal attention calibration amplifies pre-softmax attention scores from instruction tokens to safety-prefix tokens in middle LLM layers via a Hadamard product with a positive-part mask scaled by γ, preventing the prefix signal from diluting during generation. A theoretical result proves that the OT-based patch classifier achieves lower or equal error than a cosine-similarity baseline, with equality only under uniform weights.

## Results

GuardAlign achieves the lowest Unsafe Response Rate (USR) across all six evaluated MLLMs and five safety benchmarks. On SPA-VL Harm with LLaVA-1.5-7B, it reduces USR from 46.04% (vanilla) to 10.31%, a 39% relative improvement over the strongest inference-time baseline ETA (16.98%). On FigStep Vanilla, GuardAlign reaches 3.40% USR versus ETA's 7.80% and ECSO's 37.40%. Crucially, GuardAlign preserves or improves general utility: VQAv2 accuracy rises from 78.51% to 79.21% (LLaVA-1.5-7B) and from 79.99% to 80.25% (LLaVA-1.5-13B), while inference overhead is modest (42 min vs. ETA's 2h 30min on SPA-VL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GuardAlign consistently outperforms both inference-time baselines across all seven MLLMs and all five safety benchmarks. Against ECSO (Gou et al., 2024), which converts images to text descriptions before prompting, GuardAlign achieves substantially lower USR — e.g., 10.31% vs. 23.40% on SPA-VL and 3.40% vs. 37.40% on FigStep Vanilla with LLaVA-1.5-7B — indicating that image-to-text transformation loses fine-grained safety signals. Against ETA (Ding et al., 2025), the strongest inference-time defense that prepends a safety prefix, GuardAlign narrows the gap most on SPA-VL (10.31% vs. 16.98%, a 39% relative reduction) and FigStep (3.40% vs. 7.80%), while matching or exceeding ETA on Adv. Image+Text (15.00% vs. 22.50%). Against the fine-tuned VLGuard baselines (Posthoc-LoRA and Mixed-LoRA; Zong et al., 2024), GuardAlign is training-free yet achieves higher VQAv2 accuracy (79.21% vs. 76.87%/79.18% on LLaVA-1.5-7B) and a 93.6% GPT-4 win-tie helpfulness rate versus 53.2%/55.7% for the fine-tuned methods, confirming that safety gains do not come at the cost of utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the input-side defense paradigm for LVLMs established by ECSO (Gou et al., ECCV 2024) and ETA (Ding et al., ICLR 2025), which detect unsafe images via CLIP and prepend safety prefixes to prompts. GuardAlign departs from this paradigm in two specific ways: it replaces global CLIP similarity with patch-level optimal transport (Sinkhorn) to measure distributional alignment between image regions and unsafe semantic anchors, and it introduces cross-modal attention calibration that amplifies prefix-token attention in middle layers to counteract the progressive decay of safety signals during autoregressive decoding. The method is entirely training-free, positioning it alongside inference-time defenses rather than fine-tuning approaches like Posthoc-LoRA or Mixed-LoRA from VLGuard (Zong et al., ICML 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GuardAlign offers a practical, training-free safety layer that can be applied to any LVLM at inference time with modest overhead (42 min vs. ETA's 2h30min on SPA-VL), making it attractive for deployment in high-risk settings where retraining is infeasible. The finding that safety enforcement can simultaneously improve general utility (e.g., VQAv2 78.51→79.21) challenges the common assumption that safety and helpfulness are in tension. Key open questions include whether the framework extends to reasoning-oriented multimodal models, video/audio modalities, and whether the OT-based detector remains effective against adaptive adversaries that specifically target patch-level transport patterns. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that OT-based classification error is bounded above by cosine similarity provides a principled justification, though the proof relies on simplifying assumptions (equal variance, single patch).
