## Problem

Transformer decoders face prohibitive KV cache memory at long sequence lengths, and existing cross-layer sharing methods (YOCO, CLA) consistently underperform within-layer approaches like GQA. The paper identifies the root cause: these methods treat keys and values as a monolithic unit, ignoring that they draw information from different source layers. This asymmetry in information flow has been previously overlooked in cross-layer cache design.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text token sequences (context length up to 128k in long-context experiments) and produces autoregressively generated tokens. The key structural difference is that top-half decoder layers do not maintain independent KV caches; instead, their keys and values are reconstructed on-the-fly from stored caches of the bottom and middle layers. The reconstruction weights are learnable d-dimensional vectors applied via Hadamard products, and the entire pipeline is compatible with RoPE by operating directly on post-RoPE key caches with symmetric 2D weight pairs.

## Method

FusedKV reconstructs top-half layer KV caches as a learnable weighted fusion of the bottom layer (layer 1) and middle layer (layer n) caches: K^i = a_{i,1} ⊙ K^1 + a_{i,n} ⊙ K^n, with analogous equations for values. This is motivated by a dense-fusion experiment revealing that value reconstruction is dominated by the bottom layer while key reconstruction draws from both bottom and middle layers. FusedKV-Lite simplifies this to direct asymmetric sharing (K^i = K^n, V^i = V^1), eliminating fusion I/O overhead at a small perplexity cost. A theoretical analysis proves that fusing post-RoPE keys preserves relative positional encoding provided the learnable weights are symmetric within each 2D RoPE subspace (w_{2j} = w_{2j+1}), avoiding RoPE recomputation at inference. The method halves KV cache memory while achieving lower validation perplexity than the full-cached vanilla Transformer across 332M–4B parameter models.

## Results

On 1.5B-parameter models, FusedKV achieves the lowest validation loss (2.221) and WikiText perplexity (13.33) while using only 50% KV cache memory, and attains the highest average downstream accuracy (55.82%) across 8 benchmarks, surpassing the full-cache vanilla baseline (54.55%). FusedKV-Lite, which avoids fusion I/O overhead, also outperforms vanilla with 55.30% average accuracy and 13.45 WikiText perplexity. At the 4B scale, FusedKV further reduces validation loss to 1.978 (vs. 2.002 vanilla) and improves average accuracy to 60.01% (vs. 59.07%). On the RULER long-context benchmark (128k context), FusedKV-Lite retains 42.31 average score versus only 17.32 for YOCO, demonstrating far superior long-range dependency preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLA (cross-layer adjacent sharing), FusedKV and FusedKV-Lite consistently win on every metric at all three scales; at 1.5B, FusedKV improves average accuracy by 1.91 points (55.82 vs. 53.91) and reduces WikiText perplexity by 0.86. Against YOCO (decoder-decoder cache reuse), FusedKV-Lite achieves 55.30% average accuracy versus YOCO's 54.19%, and the gap widens dramatically on RULER long-context (42.31 vs. 17.32 average), indicating YOCO's single-source reuse severely degrades long-range retrieval. Against GQA (within-layer head sharing), FusedKV outperforms on 6 of 8 downstream tasks at 1.5B (e.g., HellaSwag 61.88 vs. 60.16, ARC-E 74.20 vs. 73.91) while matching on ARC-C (44.20 vs. 44.28), and both methods use the same 50% cache budget. Against MLA (low-rank KV compression), the combined MLA+FusedKV reduces cache to 1/4× of MHA while achieving lower WikiText perplexity (20.08 vs. 21.03) and higher average accuracy (44.79 vs. 45.03 is a near-tie, with FusedKV winning on 5 of 8 tasks), confirming orthogonality with within-layer compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the cross-layer KV cache sharing framework introduced by YOCO (decoder-decoder reuse of a single intermediate layer's cache) and CLA (adjacent-layer sharing) by replacing their direct, symmetric cache reuse with a learnable, asymmetric reconstruction function. The key conceptual departure is the identification of a key-value asymmetry: values are best reconstructed from the bottom layer while keys draw more information from the middle layer, a distinction that prior cross-layer methods treated uniformly. The theoretical analysis establishing RoPE compatibility with symmetric fusion weights provides a principled justification for operating on post-RoPE caches, avoiding recomputation overhead that would otherwise make cross-layer fusion impractical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that cross-layer KV fusion can achieve *lower* perplexity than the full-cache vanilla model while halving cache memory is a notable reversal of the expected compression-quality trade-off, and it is consistent across 332M–4B parameters and verified under near-convergence training. The method's demonstrated compatibility with MLA, GQA, MoE, and Sliding Window Attention positions it as a broadly composable building block for efficient inference stacks. Open questions include whether the performance advantage persists at 70B+ scale, whether the interaction with aggressive pruning (which showed significant degradation) can be resolved through co-design, and whether the gradient-flow explanation for the perplexity gain generalizes beyond the tested architectures.
