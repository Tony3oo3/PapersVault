## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods rely on training-time objectives to prevent the learning of domain-specific features but lack any mechanism to correct such reliance once it emerges during training. Because domain-specific dependence can arise dynamically at different training stages, there is no adaptive, post-hoc procedure that identifies which samples introduce domain-specific bias and selectively removes the associated features while preserving domain-invariant knowledge.

## Input / Output

The IU module operates as a plug-in post-epoch intervention on top of any existing DG method, consuming the trained model's parameters, the full training set, and a validation set sampled from source domains (no target-domain access). Input images are standard 224×224 RGB patches; the module produces an updated model with reduced domain-specific feature reliance, which then maps unseen-domain RGB images to class labels. The unlearning score and IDV channel scores are recomputed at each invocation, making the module adaptive to the evolving model state.

## Method

IU comprises three components executed after each training epoch. First, an unlearning score U_x = G_x^α / C_x combines an influence-function-based complexity score (ℓ₂-norm of the parameter change from removing sample x) with a generalization score (aggregated influence on validation loss); samples below a MAD-derived threshold form the unlearning set D_u. Second, Inter-Domain Variance (IDV) identifies domain-specific channels by computing the variance of within-domain activation variances across source domains, making the metric domain-aware and domain-size agnostic—unlike prior Aggregated Variance approaches that conflate within-domain dispersion with cross-domain differences. Third, Domain-Specific Gradient Ascent (DSGA) selectively applies gradient ascent only on the parameters of identified domain-specific channels using only samples from D_u, thereby removing domain-specific features while leaving domain-invariant parameters untouched. A theoretical analysis via mutual information decomposition proves that DSGA strictly decreases the model's predictive dependence on domain-specific features while approximately preserving its dependence on domain-invariant features.

## Results

Across seven benchmarks (PACS, OfficeHome, VLCS, Terra Incognita, DomainNet, Digits-DG, NICO++), the IU module consistently improves leave-one-domain-out top-1 accuracy for all 16 DG baselines, with average gains of up to 3.0%. For the ERM baseline, IU lifts PACS accuracy from 83.0% to 85.7% and Terra Incognita from 41.7% to 44.2%, while the EMA-smoothed variant (IUE) further pushes PACS to 86.0% and Digits-DG to 82.9%. Even strong baselines such as UDIM (PACS: 84.7% → 87.3%) and VL2V (PACS: 85.5% → 87.9%) show consistent improvements, confirming the model-agnostic nature of the approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IU is a plug-and-play module that improves every one of the 16 DG baselines tested (ERM, MMD, IRM, DDAIG, MixStyle, DomainMix, EFDMix, RISE, SSPL, LFME, UDIM, VL2V, CrossGrad, MLDG, RSC, DomainDrop) across all seven benchmarks, with no baseline showing degradation. Gains are largest for weaker baselines: ERM improves by 2.7% on PACS and 2.5% on Digits-DG, while IRM gains 1.3% on PACS and 2.6% on NICO++. Even the strongest baselines benefit—UDIM improves from 84.7% to 87.3% on PACS (+2.6%) and VL2V from 85.5% to 87.9% (+2.4%)—demonstrating that post-epoch unlearning complements rather than merely compensates for existing DG strategies. The EMA-smoothed variant (IUE) consistently outperforms the standard IU by an additional 0.3–1.0% across benchmarks, confirming that stabilizing the unlearning score trajectory is important. Ablation results confirm that both the Unlearning Set Selection and Domain-Specific Channel Selection components are necessary: applying gradient ascent to all parameters (ERM_USS) or to all training samples on domain-specific channels (ERM_DSCS) degrades performance below the ERM baseline, while their combination (ERM_IU) yields the best results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes influence functions (Koh & Liang, 2017) — originally designed for interpretability — as a scoring mechanism to identify training samples that disproportionately increase model complexity without aiding generalization. It then borrows the concept of machine unlearning (Cao & Yang, 2015; Bourtoule et al., 2021), which has been applied to privacy and continual learning, and redirects it toward domain generalization, a setting where the goal is not to forget data for safety but to selectively erase domain-specific features that impair OOD performance. The Inter-Domain Variance (IDV) metric departs from the Aggregated Variance used in DomainDrop (Guo et al., 2023) and CLIPCEIL (Yu et al., 2024) by measuring cross-domain variability rather than pooled within-domain variance, addressing a conflation the authors demonstrate can mislabel domain-invariant channels as domain-specific. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as the first framework to explicitly remove domain-specific features *after* they have been learned, rather than relying solely on training-time objectives to prevent their acquisition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic, plug-and-play nature of IU means it can complement any existing DG method without architectural changes, and the consistent (if modest, 1–3%) gains across 16 baselines and 7 benchmarks validate the premise that post-epoch correction addresses a real gap in current DG pipelines. However, the dominant computational cost of influence-function-based unlearning set selection (up to ~354 min/epoch on DomainNet with ResNet-18) significantly limits practical adoption, and the paper's own accuracy-cost trade-off analysis shows that reducing invocation frequency to every 2–5 epochs recovers most of the cost savings at only a small accuracy penalty. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large multimodal models (acknowledged as future work), whether the first-order influence approximation remains reliable in very deep or transformer-based architectures, and whether the MAD-based thresholding generalizes to highly imbalanced or streaming domain settings beyond the fixed leave-one-domain-out protocol used here.
