## Problem

Existing perturbation prediction methods either operate as opaque black boxes or treat curated biological knowledge as static, unmodifiable constraints, leaving no mechanism to identify and resolve the pervasive inconsistencies between experimental data and knowledge bases (42–71% of data-derived regulatory relationships are missing from curated GRNs, while ≥14% directly conflict). This prevents models from producing transparent, biologically grounded predictions and blocks the systematic refinement of mechanistic understanding from large-scale perturbation datasets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a ternary perturbation vector (−1/0/1 per gene for knockout, none, overexpression) and a gene regulatory network encoded as Boolean adjacency matrices for activation and inhibition, which are compiled into transitive-closure matrices up to a configurable pathway depth k. It produces a ternary response vector per gene (decreased/unchanged/increased expression) and, through its refinement step, an updated real-valued GRN with corrected direct regulatory interactions. The symbolic prediction is computed as a single matrix–vector product (R₊⁽ᵏ⁾ − R₋⁽ᵏ⁾)ᵀx, making genome-scale queries computationally efficient, while the adaptor outputs a binary indicator vector that selects, per gene, whether to trust the neural or the symbolic prediction.

## Method

ALIGNED is an end-to-end neuro-symbolic framework built on the Abductive Learning paradigm, comprising a neural predictor (MLP or GNN), a symbolic reasoner (Boolean matrix transitive closure over the GRN), and a learned adaptor. The adaptor is trained with the REINFORCE algorithm (gradient-free, since its output is a discrete binary selection vector) to minimize a composite loss that penalizes inconsistency with the knowledge base, restricts over-reliance on symbolic predictions via an L₀ threshold, and weights per-gene trust using a precomputed reliability vector combining data–knowledge conflict counts and GO annotation density. Knowledge refinement then updates the GRN via proximal gradient descent on a real-valued relaxation of the Boolean matrix operations, using a smooth approximation ε(·) to make the non-differentiable transitive-closure computation amenable to backpropagation, with L₁ sparse regularization to enforce minimal modifications and prevent shortcut learning. Training alternates between alignment (joint update of neural and adaptor on labelled + unlabelled data) and refinement (KB update on neuro-symbolic predictions) for a fixed number of iterations, and a balanced consistency metric F₁_balance with a penalty exponent γ evaluates the trade-off between data and knowledge agreement.

## Results

On the primary Norman et al. (2019) benchmark, ALIGNED (GNN) with one alignment-refinement cycle (A-R) achieves a balanced consistency of 0.5714, substantially outperforming the best baseline (GEARS at 0.3731) by ~53%. Knowledge consistency jumps to 0.6555 (vs. 0.3175 for GEARS and 0.3016 for scFoundation) while data consistency is maintained at 0.5270, exceeding all baselines. On the E. coli genome (Table 1), ALIGNED (GNN) A-R-A-R reaches 0.4288 balanced consistency versus 0.3689 for the GNN-only baseline, demonstrating that knowledge refinement is particularly beneficial under limited data availability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALIGNED (GNN) A-R outperforms all five baselines on balanced consistency across all three datasets (Norman: 0.5714 vs. best baseline 0.3731; Dixit: 0.5082 vs. 0.3335; Adamson: 0.5641 vs. 0.4132). The dominant advantage is in knowledge consistency, where ALIGNED reaches ~0.65 compared to 0.26–0.38 for all baselines, while simultaneously matching or exceeding the best data-consistency scores (e.g., 0.5270 vs. GEARS 0.4755 on Norman). GEARS is the strongest data-driven baseline but remains far below ALIGNED on knowledge grounding. State (Adduri) performs poorly on data consistency (0.1440 on Norman) despite moderate knowledge scores, and scGPT/scFoundation, while competitive on data, show the weakest knowledge consistency (0.2580/0.3016). The linear baseline (Ahlmann-Eltze) is competitive on data but offers no knowledge alignment, confirming that ALIGNED's neuro-symbolic adaptor is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

ALIGNED extends the Abductive Learning (ABL) paradigm (Zhou, 2019) from image classification and knowledge refinement to the domain of genetic perturbation prediction, adapting its consistency-optimization loop to handle the specific challenge of data-knowledge inconsistency in gene regulatory networks. The symbolic component draws on Boolean matrix logic programming (Ai, 2025; Ioannidis & Wong, 1991) for efficient transitive-closure queries over GRNs, while the gradient-based knowledge refinement borrows the Boolean approximation trick from Ravanbakhsh et al. (2016) to make non-differentiable matrix operations amenable to proximal gradient descent. Unlike prior perturbation-prediction methods (GEARS, scGPT, scFoundation, State) that treat prior knowledge as a static inductive bias or ignore it entirely, ALIGNED positions knowledge as a dynamic, refinable component and introduces a REINFORCE-based adaptor that selects per-gene between neural and symbolic predictions based on their relative reliability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and underappreciated problem in computational biology: the pervasive inconsistency between experimental perturbation data and curated knowledge bases (42–71% of data-derived interactions missing, ≥14% conflicting), which prevents naive integration from improving predictions. By introducing the balanced consistency metric and demonstrating that the adaptor automatically down-weights symbolic predictions for poorly curated or sparsely represented genes, ALIGNED provides a principled path toward interpretable, self-improving biological models. Key limitations include the simplified Boolean path-composition model (no dose-response, no context-dependence), convergence guarantees that rely on assumptions not always met in practice, and evaluation limited to a small number of cell lines and organisms. Open questions include whether differentiable GRN models (e.g., ODE-based) can replace the Boolean approximation within the same ABL loop, and whether the framework generalizes to other structured priors such as protein-protein interaction or metabolic networks.
