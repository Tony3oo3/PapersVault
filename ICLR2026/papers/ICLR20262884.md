## Problem

Existing LVLMs rely on text-level self-correction (e.g., chain-of-thought) for error rectification, but this paradigm provides minimal benefit for vision-centric tasks like chart parsing, where the core bottleneck is visual perception rather than textual reasoning. On high-density charts without explicit numeric labels, even strong models like GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro produce data omissions, misaligned correspondences, and hallucinated points, and cannot identify their own errors through textual reflection alone.

## Input / Output

The model takes a chart image (resized so the longer side is 1036 px, a multiple of the 28×28 patch size) and, during the Refine Stage, an annotated version of that image with the model's own pixel-level markers drawn on it. The Refine Stage outputs a list of [x, y] pixel coordinates for each data point; the Decode Stage then consumes the original chart plus these verified coordinates and produces a structured JSON object containing series labels, axis titles, and per-point numerical values. The two-stage design means the model never attempts to read values and localize simultaneously.

## Method

ChartVSR is built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B (ViT encoder + Qwen2.5 LM + MLP merger) and decomposes chart parsing into a Refine Stage and a Decode Stage. In the Refine Stage, the model first predicts pixel-level localizations for all data points; these coordinates are rendered as markers on the original chart and fed back as a new visual input, enabling the model to inspect its own prior output and iteratively correct mislocalizations, omissions, or hallucinations until it confirms correctness or a round limit is reached. In the Decode Stage, the verified pixel coordinates serve as precise 'visual anchors' that guide the model to map each point to its chart-coordinate value and legend label, decoupling perception from interpretation. The model is fine-tuned end-to-end on ~800K samples generated by a custom data engine that combines Parameter-Agnostic Matplotlib templates with a Hybrid Configuration Generator to maximize stylistic diversity and avoid the implicit regularities of existing synthetic chart datasets.

## Results

On the newly introduced ChartP-Bench, ChartVSR (3B) achieves an average AP-Slight of 38.41, substantially outperforming the strongest closed-source model Gemini-2.5-Pro (34.07) and all chart-specific baselines (OneChart 5.22, DePlot 4.04, ChartVLM 1.81, ChartAst 1.55). On existing benchmarks, ChartVSR is competitive with the best prior method OneChart (0.2B), achieving AP-Slight of 83.69 vs. 78.89 on ChartQA-SE-Clean, 84.61 vs. 84.18 on PlotQA-SE, and 53.48 vs. 53.12 on ChartX-SE. The advantage of VSR is most pronounced on the Hard subset of ChartP-Bench (>18 data points), where ChartVSR scores 49.30 AP-Slight versus 46.11 for Gemini-2.5-Pro and only 1.98 for OneChart.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChartVSR (3B) decisively outperforms all chart-specific baselines on ChartP-Bench: it achieves 38.41 overall AP-Slight versus 5.22 for OneChart (0.2B), 4.04 for DePlot (1.3B), 1.81 for ChartVLM (7.3B), and 1.55 for ChartAst (13B) — a gap of over 30 points over the strongest prior chart model. Against closed-source LVLMs, ChartVSR surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro (34.07) by 4.3 points overall and by a wider margin on the Easy subset (39.83 vs. 27.54), while GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Flash score near zero on AP-Strict and remain far below ChartVSR on all metrics. On the three existing benchmarks (ChartQA-SE-Clean, PlotQA-SE, ChartX-SE), ChartVSR is roughly on par with OneChart, the strongest prior chart parser, with small gains of 1–5 points in AP-Slight and AP-High, confirming that the VSR paradigm's primary advantage emerges on visually dense, high-precision charts rather than simpler ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant text-based self-correction paradigm in LVLMs (exemplified by OpenAI o1 and Gemini-2.5-Pro) by arguing that textual reflection is insufficient for visually perception-heavy tasks like chart parsing. It builds on the chart-parsing line of work (DePlot, ChartVLM, OneChart) and the SCRM evaluation metric from StructChart, but introduces a fundamentally different mechanism: the model generates pixel-level localizations, renders them as visual markers, and feeds the annotated image back to itself for iterative correction. The base architecture is Qwen2.5-VL-3B, and the training data engine addresses known limitations of existing synthetic chart datasets (stylistic homogeneity, implicit regularities) identified in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VSR paradigm offers a general-purpose visual feedback mechanism that could extend beyond chart parsing to visual counting, grounding, and other perception-centric tasks, though this generality is currently demonstrated only qualitatively. A key open question highlighted by the per-round analysis is why multi-round refinement yields diminishing returns after the first round (error samples plateau at ~52), suggesting deeper perceptual limitations that simple iteration cannot overcome; the authors propose a bootstrapping strategy but do not validate it. The increased inference cost (3+ forward passes vs. 1) is a practical trade-off that limits deployment in latency-sensitive settings, and the modest average gain over the no-VSR ablation (~2 AP points) raises questions about the paradigm's cost-effectiveness relative to simply scaling model size or training data.
