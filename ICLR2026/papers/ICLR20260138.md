## Problem

Active Deep Probabilistic Subsampling (A-DPS) adapts sampling trajectories per instance but fails to exploit the rich prior knowledge embedded in the training dataset, and its reliance on sequential top-1 sampling inflates the effective Lipschitz constant of the loss, impeding optimization. This paper addresses both gaps by integrating a deterministic prior-informed sampling pattern with group-based (top-k) active sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260138/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts diverse input modalities—28×28 grayscale MNIST digits, 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10 images, 208×208 or 368×640 k-space volumes, and 51-band hyperspectral patches—and produces a binary subsampling mask (selecting M out of N elements) jointly with the downstream task prediction (class label, reconstructed image, or per-pixel segmentation map). The mask is generated in two phases: a fixed proportion (Ps) of samples is drawn deterministically from learned prior logits, and the remaining proportion (As) is acquired in one or more active group-sampling iterations where k samples are selected simultaneously per step rather than one at a time.

## Method

PGA-DPS augments A-DPS with two mechanisms: (1) a set of trainable logits (φ₁) that define a deterministic, dataset-prior sampling mask applied before any active iteration, and (2) replacement of A-DPS's sequential top-1 selection with DPS-top-k (Gumbel top-k trick), which selects k samples per active iteration using a single shared logit vector. The sampling network is an LSTM-based encoder that ingests the current task prediction (via analysis-by-synthesis) to produce context-aware logits for the next group. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) proves that the effective Lipschitz constant of the loss under top-k sampling is L_k, whereas under top-1 it is the product ∏L_r, so for typical neural-network Lipschitz constants ≥ 1, group sampling yields a strictly smoother loss landscape and more stable optimization. Training is fully end-to-end with Gumbel-Softmax relaxation (fixed τ=2), jointly optimizing the sampling logits, the LSTM sampling network, and the task model (MLP, CNN, unfolded proximal gradient, or residual U-Net) under a task-specific loss.

## Results

On MRI reconstruction (fastMRI knee, acceleration factor 8, M=26 lines), PGA-DPS achieves NMSE 0.0359, PSNR 25.9 dB, and SSIM 0.621, outperforming A-DPS (0.0398, 25.4, 0.576) and DPS (0.0408, 25.3, 0.571). On CIFAR-10 classification at 20% sampling, PGA-DPS reaches 82.7% accuracy versus 68.3% for A-DPS and 54.3% for DPS. On hyperspectral segmentation (AeroRIT, 5 bands), PGA-DPS attains mIOU 0.6752 and mDICE 0.7803, approaching the all-bands reference (0.7037, 0.8063) while using only ~10% of spectral bands.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGA-DPS consistently outperforms all baselines across every task and dataset. Against the two most direct learned baselines, DPS and A-DPS, PGA-DPS wins on all four benchmarks: on CIFAR-10 at 20% sampling it gains +14.4 points over DPS (82.7% vs 54.3%) and +14.4 over A-DPS (82.7% vs 68.3%); on MRI it reduces NMSE by ~10% relative to A-DPS (0.0359 vs 0.0398) and improves SSIM by 0.045. Against traditional MRI sampling strategies (low-pass, VDS, Greedy Prox., LOUPE), PGA-DPS achieves the best NMSE and SSIM, with a 1.4 dB PSNR advantage over the strongest traditional method (Greedy Prox.). Against RL-based active acquisition (Evaluator Policy, DS-DDQN) on the larger 368×640 fastMRI protocol, PGA-DPS leads by 0.3 dB PSNR and 0.045 SSIM over DS-DDQN. On hyperspectral segmentation, PGA-DPS surpasses SRL-SOA (mIOU 0.6752 vs 0.6160) and GSS (0.6752 vs 0.6220), and dramatically outperforms A-DPS (0.6752 vs 0.5181), which struggles with the multi-class segmentation objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPS → A-DPS lineage of joint sampling-mask and task-model optimization. DPS (Huijben et al., ICLR 2020) introduced Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization to make discrete sampling differentiable, and A-DPS (Van Gorp et al., ICML 2021) added iterative top-1 active sampling that adapts to each test instance. PGA-DPS departs from A-DPS in two ways: it prepends a deterministic prior-informed mask learned from the training distribution, and it replaces sequential top-1 selection with Gumbel-Top-k group sampling (Kool et al., ICML 2019), supported by a Lipschitz-constant argument showing that composing k task functions multiplies their constants while a single group function does not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Lipschitz analysis provides a clean theoretical explanation for why group sampling stabilizes optimization, and the prior-aware deterministic component offers a practical way to inject dataset-level knowledge without sacrificing instance adaptivity. The method generalizes across three tasks (classification, MRI reconstruction, HSI segmentation) and four datasets, consistently outperforming both fixed-pattern and active baselines. Key open questions include automatic selection of the (Ps, As) hyperparameters, which currently require task-specific tuning, and whether temperature annealing in the Gumbel-Softmax relaxation could yield further gains. The approach is most impactful in low-measurement regimes where acquisition cost dominates, such as clinical MRI or real-time hyperspectral sensing.
