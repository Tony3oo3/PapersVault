## Problem

Learned optimizers (LOs) meta-trained on small tasks fail to generalize to wider, deeper, or longer unseen tasks, a critical limitation that prevents their practical deployment. While Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) enables zero-shot hyperparameter transfer for hand-designed optimizers like Adam and SGD, it was unclear whether existing LO architectures are even compatible with μP, and no principled meta-training recipe existed to exploit this compatibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262139/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each inner step, the LO consumes per-parameter features built from the optimizee's gradient, momentum accumulators, a variance accumulator, and multiple adafactor accumulators, producing a scalar magnitude m and direction d for the update. The update is applied as w_t = w_{t-1} − α_W · λ₁ · d · exp(λ₂m), where α_W is a tensor-level learning rate (VeLO only) and λ₁, λ₂ are fixed small constants (0.001) that bias initial step sizes. Under μP, an additional 1/FAN_IN factor is applied to the update for hidden-layer weights, and output-layer pre-activations are scaled by 1/FAN_IN during the forward pass.

## Method

The paper derives a valid μP for two state-of-the-art LO architectures (small_fc_lopt and VeLO) by specifying width-dependent initialization (N(0, 1/FAN_IN) for hidden/input layers, N(0,1) for output), output-layer pre-activation multipliers (1/FAN_IN), and a 1/FAN_IN rescaling of the optimizer update for hidden layers, then proves via LLN alignment arguments that these choices satisfy the μP desiderata. A simple meta-training recipe meta-trains the LO on MLP ImageNet classification tasks at three widths (128, 512, 1024) for 1000 inner steps using evolution strategies, requiring only ~100 GPU hours. Empirically, the resulting μLOs smoothly generalize to widths up to 8× meta-training (8192 for MLPs, 4096 for transformers), outperforming per-task-tuned AdamW and μAdam on wide OOD tasks, and unexpectedly extend to 5× deeper networks and 25× longer training horizons where SP LOs diverge or stagnate.

## Results

Across 35 evaluation tasks (MLP, ViT, and Transformer architectures on CIFAR-10, ImageNet, and LM1B), μLO_M and μVeLO_M consistently achieve the best and second-best average rank (out of 6 optimizers) at all OOD widths (2048, 4096/3072, and maximum) and all training lengths (1k, 3k, 5k steps). At the most challenging setting (5k steps, XXL width), μLO_M achieves an average rank of 1.60 and μVeLO_M 2.00, compared to 2.60 for per-task-tuned μAdam, 3.80 for per-task-tuned AdamW, and 5.20–5.80 for SP learned optimizers that typically diverge. Remarkably, μLOs also generalize to networks 5× deeper and training horizons 25× longer than meta-training, where SP LOs diverge or fail to reduce loss, all at a meta-training cost of only 100 GPU hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against per-task-tuned AdamW (500+ hyperparameter configurations per task), μLO_M and μVeLO_M achieve substantially better average ranks at all OOD widths: at 5k steps and XXL width, the μLOs rank 1.60–2.00 versus 3.80 for AdamW, and at XL width μVeLO_M (1.40) even beats AdamW (2.40). Against per-task-tuned μAdam, the μLOs similarly dominate at larger widths (e.g., 5k/XXL: 1.60–2.00 vs. 2.60) and at longer training (5k steps), though μAdam is competitive at the smallest OOD width (Large) where the distribution shift is minimal. Against SP learned optimizer baselines (LO_M and VeLO_M meta-trained under standard parametrization on the same tasks), the μLOs show a dramatic gap: SP LOs diverge or fail to reduce loss at widths beyond 1024–2048, yielding ranks of 4.80–6.00, while μLOs maintain smooth, stable training with ranks below 2.50. The paper further shows that μLOs generalize to 5× deeper networks and 25× longer training horizons where SP LOs diverge, an advantage not shared by any hand-designed baseline comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: learned optimizers (Andrychowicz et al. 2016; Metz et al. 2019, 2022a,b) and the Maximal Update Parametrization (μP) for hyperparameter transfer (Yang et al. 2022; Yang & Littwin 2023). While μP was originally derived for hand-designed optimizers (Adam, SGD) and learned optimizers were meta-trained under standard parametrization, this work is the first to theoretically derive a valid μP for two state-of-the-art LO architectures (small_fc_lopt and VeLO) and to show that meta-training under this parametrization with a multi-width recipe resolves the width generalization failure documented in Metz et al. (2022b). The paper also positions itself relative to concurrent work on alternative parameterizations (CompleteP, Everett et al.'s layer-wise learning rates) that also admit hyperparameter transfer, leaving open which is optimal for meta-learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Learned optimizers have demonstrated the potential to outperform hand-designed optimizers at scale (VeLO, 4000 TPU-months), but their inability to generalize to wider, deeper, or longer tasks has been a fundamental barrier to practical adoption. This paper shows that a zero-extra-compute parametrization change plus a simple multi-width meta-training recipe substantially fixes width generalization, making LOs viable for large-model training at low meta-training cost (~100 GPU hours). The empirically observed (but theoretically unexplained) generalization to 5× deeper networks and 25× longer training horizons opens intriguing questions about whether μP's stabilizing effects on activations confer broader benefits beyond width. Key limitations include meta-training only on MLPs, evaluation capped at width 8192, and the absence of an oracle SP baseline tuned at every width; future work should explore other transferable parameterizations (CompleteP, layer-wise LRs) and broader meta-training distributions.
