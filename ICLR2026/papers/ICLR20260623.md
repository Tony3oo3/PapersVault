## Problem

While retrieval-augmented generation has proven effective for cultural understanding in text-only settings, its role in multimodal scenarios remains largely unexplored. Existing multimodal culture datasets primarily probe VLMs' memorized cultural knowledge rather than testing whether external, culturally grounded retrieval can improve real-world visual culture comprehension. There is no dedicated benchmark that simultaneously evaluates multimodal retrievers' ability to find culturally relevant documents and VLMs' ability to leverage such retrieved context in downstream reasoning.

## Input / Output

For the cVQA task, the model receives an image, a culture-specific question with four multiple-choice options, and the first 256 words of the top-1 retrieved Wikipedia document, producing a single letter answer. For cIC, the model receives an image plus the same retrieved document context and generates a one-sentence caption that must explicitly name the country and incorporate culturally relevant elements. The retrieval sub-task takes an image paired with a GPT-4o-generated cultural caption and outputs a ranking over 10 candidate Wikipedia documents, scored by MRR, P@k, and nDCG@k. A novel RegionScore metric additionally checks whether generated captions contain the country name or its demonyms, complementing standard captioning metrics that miss geographic specificity.

## Method

The paper introduces Culture-Aware Contrastive (CAC) learning, a supervised framework that fine-tunes CLIP or SigLIP encoders for culturally relevant document retrieval. The CAC loss combines three components: (1) a binary cross-entropy classification loss over cosine similarity scores between image and document embeddings, (2) a margin ranking loss that pushes relevant documents above irrelevant ones for the same image, and (3) an entropy-based diversity penalty that discourages near-duplicate text embeddings within the candidate set. The dataset is constructed by first generating culturally informative captions with GPT-4o, retrieving top-10 candidates via BM25 from 6M+ English Wikipedia articles, and then having human annotators decompose cultural relevance into three binary dimensions (country association, topic alignment, explicit visual representation) to produce training labels on a −3 to +3 scale. The benchmark pairs 1,868 images from eight countries across eleven categories with 11,396 human-ranked documents, enabling evaluation of seven retrievers and seventeen VLMs (2B–78B parameters) on both cVQA accuracy and cIC RegionScore.

## Results

On the multimodal retrieval task, Ravenea-CLIP (CLIP-L/14 fine-tuned with the proposed Culture-Aware Contrastive loss) achieves the best MRR of 82.17 and P@1 of 72.05, substantially outperforming the frozen CLIP-L/14 baseline (MRR 75.44, P@1 60.87) and all other retrievers. On downstream cVQA, Ravenea-CLIP improves the average accuracy from 67.1% (no RAG) to 73.3% (+6.2%), and on cIC RegionScore from 57.5% to 68.8% (+11.3%). Lightweight models benefit most: Qwen3-VL-2B gains +31.6% on cVQA, while larger models (≥27B) see only marginal improvements of 2–3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ravenea-CLIP decisively outperforms all five baselines on every retrieval metric. Against the frozen CLIP-L/14@224px (Radford et al., 2021), fine-tuning with the CAC loss yields a +6.73 MRR gain (75.44→82.17) and a +11.18 P@1 gain (60.87→72.05), confirming that culture-specific supervision substantially improves retrieval. The frozen SigLIP2-SO/14@384px (Tschannen et al., 2025) trails CLIP on all metrics, and its fine-tuned variant (Ravenea-SigLIP) improves only modestly (MRR 68.62→70.95), suggesting CLIP's architecture is better suited to the contrastive retrieval objective. Among the generative/reranking baselines, VisualBERT (Li et al., 2019), VL-T5 (Cho et al., 2021), and LLaVA-OV-7B (Li et al., 2025) all underperform even the frozen CLIP after fine-tuning (MRR 58–61 vs. 75.44), likely because their pretraining objectives are misaligned with similarity-based retrieval. On downstream tasks, Ravenea-CLIP achieves the highest average cVQA accuracy (73.3%) and cIC RegionScore (68.8%), beating the no-RAG baseline by +6.2% and +11.3% respectively, while the frozen CLIP retriever provides smaller gains (+4.8% cVQA, +7.6% cIC), validating the added value of the CAC training objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two largely separate research threads: retrieval-augmented generation for cultural understanding (which has been explored in text-only settings via World Values Survey retrieval and web-search-augmented QA) and multimodal cultural benchmarks (CVQA, CCUB, WorldCuisines) that probe VLMs' memorized cultural knowledge without external retrieval. RAVENEA bridges these by being the first benchmark that explicitly evaluates how retrieved cultural documents affect VLM performance on visual culture tasks. Methodologically, it extends the CLIP/SigLIP contrastive learning paradigm with a novel Culture-Aware Contrastive (CAC) loss that combines binary classification, margin ranking, and diversity-promoting objectives to train retrievers on culturally annotated image-document pairs, departing from standard InfoNCE-style contrastive training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAVENEA provides the first systematic testbed for diagnosing whether and how multimodal RAG improves cultural understanding, revealing that lightweight VLMs (≤8B) gain substantially while larger models plateau—suggesting retrieval is most valuable as a plug-and-play enhancement for resource-constrained deployments. The finding of model-specific cultural biases (e.g., consistent underperformance on Nigerian and Mexican contexts) highlights that no single VLM is culturally neutral, a concern with real implications for deployment in diverse settings. Key limitations include the restriction to eight countries and Wikipedia as the sole knowledge source, which may amplify existing digital-footprint biases; the authors identify GLAM-curated knowledge bases and expert human evaluation as necessary next steps for truly inclusive cultural AI.
