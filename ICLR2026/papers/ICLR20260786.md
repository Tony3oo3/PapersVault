## Problem

Despite feed-forward networks dominating the parameter budget and computational footprint of transformer-based LLMs, their high-dimensional latent-space dynamics remain poorly understood. Prior analyses relied on attention maps or piecewise-affine spline partitions, neither of which reveals how FFN nonlinearities redistribute variance across eigenmodes or capture the rich spectral structure governing information flow. This paper asks: how do FFN nonlinearities reshape the eigenspectrum, and how do architectural and optimizer choices modulate this process?

## Input / Output

The framework consumes FFN pre-activation tensors (output of the up-projection, before the nonlinearity) and post-activation tensors (after the nonlinearity, before the down-projection), collected via registered PyTorch hooks during training. Tokens are flattened across batch and sequence dimensions into N×D matrices (N = B×S), intentionally discarding sequence order so each token is treated as an independent sample. The output is four per-layer, per-logging-step scalar metrics—spectral entropy, participation ratio, eigenvalue early enrichment, and Jensen-Shannon divergence—that are scale-invariant and bounded, enabling cross-configuration comparison without normalization.

## Method

NerVE is a lightweight, online, memory-efficient eigenspectral tracking framework that computes unbiased covariance matrices from full-batch activations, performs eigendecomposition via torch.linalg.eigvalsh, and extracts four complementary, scale-invariant metrics: Spectral Entropy (variance uniformity), Participation Ratio (effective dimensionality), Eigenvalue Early Enrichment (top-heaviness of cumulative variance), and Jensen-Shannon divergence (distributional shift between pre- and post-activation spectra). The key insight is that FFN nonlinearities do not merely rescale activations but actively reinject variance into under-utilized eigenmodes, flattening the spectrum and reawakening inactive directions; the optimizer geometry (e.g., AdamW vs. Muon vs. Dion) modulates the extent of this reinjection, shifting the nonlinearity's role from repair (recovering spectral collapse) to refinement (stabilizing a well-conditioned spectrum). The framework is validated across GPT-2 and LLaMA models (71M–1.3B) trained from scratch, norm-free variants, FFN weight-geometry constraints, positional encodings, and multiple optimizers, and extends to non-transformer MLP-Mixer architectures, confirming cross-architecture generality.

## Results

NERVE is an interpretability framework rather than a new model, so its headline 'results' are spectral signatures that correlate with generalization. Pre-activation spectral entropy (SE_pre) correlates with validation loss at |r| ≥ 0.97 across all FFN widths (D=1d–8d), and post-activation participation ratio (PR_post) reaches |r| ≥ 0.93 at D ≥ 2d, confirming eigenspectrum metrics as forward-pass-only generalization proxies. In the norm-free setting, ReLU reduces the perplexity gap to the LayerNorm baseline by ~50% (PPL 2.988 vs. 3.223 for GELU), and among parametric FFN normalizations, Spectral Normalization achieves the best PPL (3.000) via sustained spectral flattening. Among optimizers, Muon attains the lowest perplexity with the smallest PR gains and lowest JS divergence, while RoPE sustains higher mid-to-deep PR and achieves PPL 15.20 vs. 16.78 for NoPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among parametric FFN normalizations applied to norm-free Leaky ReLU models, Spectral Normalization (SNorm, PPL 3.000) outperforms Weight Normalization (WNorm, 3.041) and Hyperspherical Normalization (HNorm, 3.122), with SNorm achieving the lowest EEE_post (≈0.45) and most sustained spectral flattening across depth. Among optimizers on GPT-2-350M, Muon achieves the best perplexity with uniformly small PR gains and lowest JS divergence, while AdamW shows the worst perplexity due to early-layer spectral collapse requiring aggressive but incomplete nonlinear repair; Dion falls in between. For LayerNorm placement on GPT-2-125M, PreLN (PPL 2.714) beats MixLN (2.808) and PostLN (2.830), with PreLN sustaining the highest normalized PR_post across the FFN width sweep (D=1d–8d). RoPE outperforms NoPE (PPL 15.20 vs. 16.78) by preventing mid-to-deep spectral collapse, maintaining higher PR in intermediate and deeper layers. In the norm-free regime, ReLU (PPL 2.988) substantially outperforms GELU (3.223) by aggressively reinjecting variance (PR gain >200×) to compensate for the absence of LayerNorm, whereas GELU exhibits spectral inertia (EEE_post → 1, JS → 0) in early layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior FFN analysis that focused on attention-map contextualization (Kobayashi et al., 2024) or piecewise-affine spline geometry (Balestriero et al., 2024) by directly tracking how nonlinearity restructures the eigenspectrum between pre- and post-activation within each FFN layer. It also distinguishes itself from spectral analyses of weights (RankMe, Diff-eRank), attention eigenspectra (Ruscio et al., 2025), or Hessian-based optimizer diagnostics (Zhang et al., 2024) by operating on activation covariance matrices and quantifying the dynamic, layer-wise, training-dependent variance redistribution. The four-metric framework (SE, PR, EEE, JS) repurposes concepts from quantum information theory (von Neumann entropy) and information geometry (Jensen-Shannon divergence) into a unified, online, memory-efficient diagnostic for high-dimensional latent space utilization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NerVE provides practitioners with a lightweight, forward-pass-only diagnostic that correlates strongly with generalization (|r| ≥ 0.97), enabling architectural and optimizer selection without training to convergence. The finding that optimizer geometry shifts FFN nonlinearity from 'repair' (AdamW) to 'refinement' (Muon) offers a concrete spectral mechanism for why certain optimizers generalize better, with practical implications for training large models. Key limitations include the computational cost of full eigendecomposition at very large FFN dimensions (mitigated by sub-sampling), the fact that spectral metrics do not directly predict downstream task quality, and that all experiments are at relatively small scales (71M–1.3B parameters), leaving open whether the observed spectral signatures persist at frontier model scale.
