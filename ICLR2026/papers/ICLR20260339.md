## Problem

Classical active inference models human behavior through prospective planning at every step, but lacks three ingredients central to human cognition: a mechanism to compress repeated successes into compact reusable rules, a principled switching mechanism between fast habitual action and costly deliberative planning, and an offline consolidation process to refine or prune such rules. This paper addresses these gaps by embedding symbolic, latent-grounded rules directly into the active inference framework, enabling the agent to act habitually in familiar contexts while retaining flexible planning for novel situations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a history of observations and past actions, with domain-specific representations: LLM-guided symbolic features (relative velocity, spacing, formation compactness) for NBA SportVU, driving regime statistics for Car-Following, Evidence2Vec embeddings of medical evidence for DDXPlus, and 128×128 grayscale frames for Atari-Berzerk. It produces a discrete action prediction (from 7, 18, or 225 classes depending on domain), a latent state estimate decomposed into a continuous world state S_t and a discrete mental state m_t, and a growing library of interpretable symbolic rules of the form (S_f*, m_f*, a_f, ρ_f) that map prototypical context-intention pairs to prescribed actions with confidence weights.

## Method

The framework splits the latent state into a continuous world state S_t and a discrete mental state m_t, then represents habits as anchored condition-action rules (S_f*, m_f*, a_f, ρ_f) that form a Gaussian mixture over latent contexts. A hybrid policy (Eq. 5) blends the rule-induced action prior with expected-free-energy planning: when a high-confidence rule fires (Gaussian kernel similarity above threshold and matching mental state), the agent acts habitually; otherwise it falls back on multi-step EFE minimization via beam search. Training uses a biologically inspired wake-sleep algorithm: the wake phase processes real trajectories to update the generative model and encoder while harvesting candidate rules from recurring (S, m, a) triplets that consistently reduce variational free energy, updating rule anchors as weighted centroids; the sleep phase performs generative replay to jointly minimize a composite objective (VFE + η·EFE + γ·KL for mental-state consistency), consolidating, refining, and pruning rules. A two-stage schedule first bootstraps the world model via blockwise VFE-only pretraining, then runs full wake-sleep cycles, yielding interpretable rules that mirror human-like habits while preserving planning flexibility.

## Results

The proposed rule-guided active inference framework achieves 97.00% Acc@1 on NBA SportVU, 96.77% on Car-Following, 79.63% on DDXPlus (225 actions), and 85.55% on Atari-Berzerk, outperforming all baselines across every dataset. Inference latency remains competitive: 35.92 ms (NBA), 10.44 ms (Car-Following), 159.45 ms (DDXPlus), and 92.63 ms (Atari), substantially lower than LLM-based methods (e.g., LaTee at 95,029 ms on DDXPlus) and comparable to or better than DreamerV2. The method also achieves strong High-Hit Action Ratios on rare/critical actions, confirming that mined rules specifically improve performance on low-frequency behaviors that dominate real-world action distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and every accuracy metric. Against the strongest baseline DreamerV2 (model-based RL), gains are +10.6 pp Acc@1 on NBA (97.00 vs 86.42), +8.3 pp on Car-Following (96.77 vs 88.43), +15.6 pp on DDXPlus (79.63 vs 64.05), and +9.2 pp on Atari (85.55 vs 76.33), while also reducing latency on three of four domains (e.g., 10.44 ms vs 38.57 ms on Car-Following). Against the best active-inference baseline DAI-MC, improvements are even larger: +14.7 pp on NBA, +12.2 pp on Car-Following, +22.4 pp on DDXPlus, and +18.7 pp on Atari, with latency reduced by 10–14× on DDXPlus (159 ms vs 2304 ms). LLM-based methods (LaTee, Qwen-0.5B) are dominated on both accuracy and latency by orders of magnitude (e.g., Qwen-0.5B achieves only 19.62% Acc@3 on DDXPlus vs 73.58% for the proposed method, at 125,842 ms vs 159 ms). Logic-based methods (RNNLogic, STLR) and the deep event model Re-Net trail by 10–60 pp in accuracy, confirming that static or post-hoc rules without joint world-model optimization cannot match the adaptive, free-energy-optimized rule library.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends deep active inference (DAI, DAI-MC) by embedding symbolic rules as compact habitual shortcuts within the AIF generative model, grounded in a split latent state (continuous world state + discrete mental state). The wake-sleep learning paradigm from Hinton et al. (1995) is repurposed to jointly consolidate and prune these rules alongside the world model, whereas prior AIF work (Fountas et al., 2020; Catal et al., 2020) treats habits only implicitly through amortized planning networks. The control-as-inference formulation (Levine, 2018; Toussaint, 2009) provides the preference encoding, and the rule library is shown to be equivalent to a lightweight Gaussian mixture over latent contexts with EM-style updates, connecting it to the options literature (Bacon et al., 2017) and neuro-symbolic methods (Li et al., 2023b; Xu et al., 2023) while differing in that rules are learned end-to-end under a free-energy objective rather than imposed post hoc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, biologically motivated account of how humans switch between deliberate planning and automatic habitual control, producing interpretable rules that mirror domain-specific strategies (e.g., 'brake when car ahead is close and mode is cautious'). Open questions include the domain specificity of mined rules, sensitivity to rule thresholds and the number of latent mental states, and whether the approach scales to hierarchical timescales or real-time deployment. The reliance on LLM-guided feature construction and mental-state labeling introduces a dependency that may limit fully autonomous operation, and the planning fallback (beam search over EFE) remains a computational bottleneck in large action spaces like DDXPlus.
