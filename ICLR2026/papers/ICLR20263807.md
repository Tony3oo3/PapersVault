## Problem

Current LLM safety alignment enforces universal standards that ignore individual user sensitivities, and even benign prompts can elicit content violating user-specific preferences (e.g., a user who wants to avoid violence or political content). Existing personalization approaches require expensive fine-tuning or structured preference data, while activation steering methods operate as ad-hoc heuristics without formal guarantees. The paper asks how to achieve personalized safety control that is efficient, data-light, and theoretically grounded.

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method takes a user text prompt and a set of safety preference categories (e.g., 'no violence,' 'no political ideologies') and produces a modified text response that suppresses undesired content while preserving helpfulness. The intervention operates on internal activations at a fixed middle transformer layer, applying an additive shift only when the prompt's activation falls within a quantile-based proximity threshold of the harmful region, ensuring benign queries pass through unmodified. Intervention directions are pre-computed offline from contrastive prompt pairs, so no training or reward model is needed at deployment.

## Method

The core method, Paired Contrast Mean Shift (PCMS), estimates a user-specific harm direction by averaging activation differences between harmful and harmless prompts matched on the same topic, yielding an unbiased and consistent estimator of the true harm-difference vector a*. A formal bias-variance decomposition proves PCMS is asymptotically optimal, while the alternative strategies (ILCS and UMS) suffer from instance-level noise or topic-distribution bias. The intervention subtracts α·d_int from the activation at a middle layer, where d_int = a*/||a*|| is shown (Proposition 1) to be the worst-case optimal direction maximizing guaranteed harm-score reduction across all admissible scoring functions. For multi-facet control, cosine-distance-based relevance scores dynamically weight multiple facet directions, and a quantile threshold gates intervention to avoid degrading utility on unrelated queries.

## Results

On the primary single-facet evaluation (LLaMA-3.1-8B, 100 prompts per category), PCMS achieves an average harmfulness score of 1.73 (scale 1–5, lower is better) while maintaining a utility score of 7.95 (scale 1–10, higher is better). This represents a 48% reduction in harmfulness over Direct Prompting (3.36) with only a 0.56-point utility drop. Across three models (LLaMA-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, DeepSeek-R1-Distilled-LLaMA3-8B), PCMS consistently reduces harmfulness by 0.63–2.17 points relative to DP while preserving utility within ~0.5 points. On GSM8K, the intervention introduces no statistically significant accuracy change (80.23% vs. 80.12% for LLaMA-3.1-8B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCMS dominates the safety–utility Pareto front: it reduces harmfulness by 1.63 points over Direct Prompting (3.36→1.73) and by 1.09–1.13 over ICL (2.86) and RAG (2.82), while retaining utility within 0.14–0.20 of those prompt-based baselines. Among activation-engineering baselines, UMS achieves the lowest harmfulness (1.43) but at a severe utility cost (5.80 vs. PCMS's 7.95), consistent with its theoretically identified topic-distribution bias. ILCS-local (2.51 harmfulness, 7.57 utility) and ILCS-global (2.95 harmfulness, 6.23 utility) both fall below PCMS on the Pareto front, reflecting their high variance (ILCS) or topic bias (ILCS-global). The RAG baseline (Lewis et al., 2020) achieves competitive utility (8.15) but offers only modest harmfulness reduction (2.82 vs. DP's 3.36), confirming that retrieval-based safety prompting is insufficient for personalized content control. Cross-model validation with Claude-3.7 as an independent judge and human evaluation (5 annotators) confirm the same ranking: PCMS harmfulness drops from 3.52 to 1.79 (human) while helpfulness stays at 7.67.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the activation engineering/steering paradigm (Turner et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023; Arditi et al., 2024) and the difference-in-means concept editing framework (Belrose, 2024), but reframes the problem from global behavioral control (refusal, sentiment) to personalized safety direction estimation. The key departure is providing a formal bias-variance decomposition across three estimation strategies (ILCS, UMS, PCMS) and a worst-case optimality guarantee (Proposition 1) that prior heuristic steering methods lack. The multi-facet weighted composition mechanism extends single-direction steering to handle intersecting user preferences, drawing on mixture-of-experts principles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretically grounded, training-free framework for user-specific safety control at inference time, addressing a gap where even benign prompts can violate individual sensitivities. The practical impact is significant: no retraining, no reward models, and applicability to any open-weight LLM. Key limitations include reliance on an additive activation model assumption, fixed single-layer intervention, and GPT-4o-based evaluation that may not fully capture subjective human perception. Open questions include optimal layer selection across models and facets, whether the additive structure holds for more complex harm types, and how to further minimize utility degradation in hard cases where prompts closely border restricted content.
