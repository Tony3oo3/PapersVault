## Problem

Multimodal editing large models (e.g., GPT-4o, Flux.1 Kontext, In-ContextEdit) suffer significant facial identity degradation under complex long-instruction real-person editing, producing random or distorted faces. Existing ID-preservation, fusion, restoration, and face-swapping methods each fail to simultaneously restore the original facial identity and preserve fine-grained edited element attributes (e.g., glasses frame style) due to Cross-source Distribution Bias and Cross-source Feature Contamination between the source and edited images.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source identity image I₁ and an edited image I₂ (e.g., a portrait with added glasses), along with text prompts identifying semantic tokens ("face", "glasses") and spatial masks delineating target regions. For degraded I₂, DiffBIR super-resolution is applied as a pre-processing step. The output is a single reconstructed image I₃ that preserves I₁'s facial identity (ID-Sim > 0.7) while retaining I₂'s edited element IP (color, pattern, texture). The framework is training-free and plug-and-play, operating as a post-processing module on outputs from any multimodal editing model with a single GPU (~4.2 s per image).

## Method

EditedID is a diffusion-inversion-based framework with three stages. (1) Adaptive Mixing: learnable per-timestep weights λ_t dynamically blend the DDIM-inverted latent trajectories of I₁ and I₂, optimized via gradient descent to minimize alignment loss, with constrained convergence (λ_t = 0.5) at t = T to produce a unified initialization free of abrupt transitions. (2) Hybrid Solver: a global-timestep scheduling strategy that invokes DDIM in early diffusion steps (preserving identity via deterministic paths) and DPM-Solver++ in late steps (restoring textural detail via high-order Taylor expansion), with pre-computed timestep sequences for both schedulers to eliminate discontinuities at the transition boundary. (3) Attentional Gating: mask-selective self-attention replacement (using spatial masks with weighted overlap fusion) preserves single-element structures, while token-selective cross-attention replacement (replacing attention maps for specific text tokens like "face" or "glasses") controls multi-element semantic interactions. The entire pipeline extends null-text inversion and prompt-to-prompt attention manipulation, requiring no model fine-tuning.

## Results

On the ID preservation benchmark (Table 1), EditedID achieves ID-Sim 0.73, CLIP-S 28.14, and I-Reward 1.82, outperforming all 9 baselines on every metric. The average gains over baselines are 0.27 in ID-Sim and 2.43 in CLIP-S. As a plug-and-play enhancement for multimodal editing large models (Table 2), EditedID boosts In-ContextEdit's ID-Sim from 0.56 to 0.72 (+0.16) and Doubao's from 0.63 to 0.75 (+0.12), both crossing the 0.7 identity-consistency threshold. Inference takes ~4.2 s per single-ID image on one GPU, roughly 6× faster than DiffFace.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EditedID wins on all three metrics against every baseline in Table 1. Against the strongest identity-preserving baseline DreamID (Ye et al., 2025), it gains +0.08 ID-Sim (0.73 vs 0.65), +2.03 CLIP-S, and +0.09 I-Reward. Against DynamicFace (Wang et al., 2025), the closest face-swapping competitor, it improves ID-Sim by +0.10 and I-Reward by +0.26. The largest margins are over IP-Adapter (+0.38 ID-Sim, +7.72 CLIP-S) and DiffBIR (+0.39 ID-Sim), confirming that identity-preserving adapters and blind restoration methods suffer most from Cross-source Distribution Bias. In Table 2, EditedID as a plug-in raises In-ContextEdit from 0.56 to 0.72 and Doubao from 0.63 to 0.75 in ID-Sim, both surpassing the 0.7 same-identity threshold, while all unmodified multimodal editing models (GPT-4o Plus 0.58, Flux.1 Kontext 0.55, Qwen-Image-Edit 0.52, BAGEL 0.50) remain below it. No baseline is matched or exceeded by EditedID on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

EditedID builds directly on the null-text inversion framework (Mokady et al., CVPR 2023) for real-image reconstruction trajectories and Prompt-to-Prompt (Hertz et al., 2022) for cross-attention-based feature transfer, extending both to a dual-source (original ID + edited element) setting. It departs from prior identity-preserving methods (IP-Adapter, FaceDancer, DiffSwap) by being entirely training-free and by explicitly addressing the simultaneous preservation of original facial identity AND edited element IP, which prior fusion-based approaches sacrifice. The alignment-disentanglement-entanglement paradigm draws conceptual inspiration from 3D facial compositing (MEGANE) but is instantiated through diffusion trajectory manipulation, hybrid DDIM/DPM-Solver++ sampling, and attentional gating rather than geometric re-simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-post-processing solution, EditedID offers a practical path to deploying multimodal editing large models in real-person scenarios without the data, compute, or fine-tuning costs that currently block adoption. The demonstrated compatibility with both academic (In-ContextEdit) and industrial (Doubao) models suggests it could serve as a general ID-consistency layer. However, the method is scoped to U-Net-based diffusion models and facial regions with explicit spatial masks, leaving open whether the trajectory-alignment and hybrid-solver principles transfer to diffusion-transformer architectures or non-facial identity attributes. The paper also raises the interesting but underexplored idea of using EditedID as a data augmentation tool to expand facial datasets, which could address the data-scarcity bottleneck for future ID-consistent editing models.
