## Problem

Existing multi-domain translation methods either require fully aligned tuples across all K domains (impractical as domain count grows) or only support translations between a central domain and each non-central domain, leaving cross non-central translations unaddressed. This paper formalizes Universal Multi-Domain Translation (UMDT), which seeks to enable translation between any pair of K domains using only K−1 paired datasets sharing a central domain, and proposes a unified framework to solve it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263893/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source-domain sample (RGB image, sketch, segmentation map, or depth map) together with integer source and target domain labels, and produces a translated sample in the target domain. Images are processed at 64×64×3 (Shoes-UMDT), 128×128×3 (Faces-UMDT-Pixel), or encoded to 32×32×4 latents via a pretrained VAE (Faces-UMDT-Latent, COCO-UMDT). Notably, for non-central↔non-central translations no direct paired training data exists; the model must infer the mapping either by routing through the central domain (indirect) or via a learned direct conditional distribution (direct).

## Method

Diffusion Router (DR) uses a single U-Net noise predictor ε_θ conditioned on both source and target domain labels, so one network handles all central↔non-central bidirectional mappings. Indirect non-central translation is achieved in two stages: denoise from source to central, then from central to target. For direct non-central translation, the paper derives a variational upper bound on the KL divergence between the indirect and direct path distributions, which reduces to a noise-matching loss (Eq. 10) that aligns ε_θ(x_t^j, t, x^i, j, i) with a frozen reference ε_ref(x_t^j, t, x^c, j, c). To sample x_t^j from the reference conditional p_ref(x_t^j|x^c) efficiently during training, Tweedie refinement (Eq. 12) iteratively refines an unconditional noisy sample in only a few steps. The final training loss combines this unpaired direct-translation term with the original paired loss (weighted by λ₂) to prevent catastrophic forgetting of previously learned central↔non-central mappings.

## Results

On Shoes-UMDT, iDR achieves FID of 1.66/5.15 (Edge↔Shoe), 0.53/1.60 (Gray.↔Shoe), and 1.85/5.48 (Edge↔Gray.), substantially outperforming UniDiffuser (2.98/11.94, 2.72/4.40, 4.81/12.26) and Rectified Flow (2.88/30.92, 3.75/43.38, 20.14/18.83). On Faces-UMDT-Latent, iDR reaches 9.07/23.88 (Ske.↔Face), 6.12/19.12 (Seg.↔Face), and 15.37/6.15 (Ske.↔Seg.), again the best across all baselines. The direct variant dDR incurs only marginal FID degradation on non-paired translations (e.g., 19.42/5.52 vs. 15.37/6.15 on Ske.↔Seg.) while halving the number of sampling steps required for cross-domain translation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iDR dominates all three baselines on every translation direction across all four benchmarks. StarGAN, a GAN-based method originally designed for unpaired translation, performs worst overall (e.g., FID 9.92/20.18 on Edge↔Shoe vs. iDR's 1.66/5.15), attributed to its outdated generator and mismatch with paired supervision. Rectified Flow, a deterministic flow-based method, degrades sharply on high-variance targets such as Seg.→Face (FID 81.44 vs. iDR's 19.12) because its single-trajectory formulation cannot capture the stochasticity of the conditional distribution. UniDiffuser, which models the joint distribution over all domains via a single transformer, suffers from substituting missing domains with Gaussian noise during training, yielding FID 55.46 on Ske.→Face versus iDR's 23.88. The direct variant dDR shows a small FID increase over iDR on non-paired translations (e.g., 19.42 vs. 15.37 on Ske.→Seg.) due to Monte Carlo approximation bias in the variational-bound objective, yet still outperforms all baselines by a wide margin while requiring only half the sampling steps of iDR for cross-domain translation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the U-Net architecture of Dhariwal & Nichol (2021), extending conditional diffusion to a multi-domain setting via a single noise predictor conditioned on source and target domain labels — the 'Diffusion Router' concept. It departs from UniDiffuser and One Diffusion, which require fully aligned tuples across all domains, and from StarGAN, which treats cross-domain translation as unpaired. The key conceptual departure is the UMDT formulation: enabling any-pair translation among K domains using only K−1 paired datasets with a central domain, plus a variational-bound objective and Tweedie refinement procedure to learn direct non-central mappings without paired supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UMDT addresses a practically important gap: real-world multi-modal data (image, text, audio, sketch, segmentation) rarely comes as fully aligned tuples, yet pairwise datasets with a pivot modality are abundant. The single-model router design avoids the linear model-size growth of prior MDT methods and the quadratic number of per-pair models, making it scalable to many domains. Limitations include the Monte Carlo approximation bias in the direct-translation objective (causing a modest dDR vs. iDR gap on unpaired translations) and the current restriction to star-shaped topologies in experiments, though the authors argue generalization to spanning trees. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly heterogeneous modalities (e.g., image↔audio) where the central-domain overlap assumption may be weaker, and whether the Tweedie refinement can be further accelerated for real-time applications.
