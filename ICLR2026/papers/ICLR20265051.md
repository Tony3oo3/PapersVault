## Problem

Multi-domain RL for LLMs suffers from cross-domain conflicts where improving one capability degrades another, yet existing gradient-balancing methods either require storing all per-domain gradients simultaneously (causing OOM at LLM scale) or ignore the geometric structure of the reward surface. The paper asks how to mitigate these conflicts in a way that is both consistent with the rollout-based, noisy-gradient nature of RL and scalable to large language models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text queries from four domains (math, code, scientific QA, creative writing) and generates G=8 candidate responses per query under the current policy. Each response is scored by a domain-specific reward function—rule-based symbolic verification for math, sandboxed unit-test execution for code, a 1.5B General-Verifier for science, and Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct as a pairwise judge for creative writing—with a mandatory think-tag format enforced across all domains. The trained policy outputs a single text response per query at inference time, evaluated on seven held-out benchmarks spanning the same four domains.

## Method

CGPO distills Newton's method into a lightweight sequential-update mechanism that avoids explicit Hessian computation. At each training step, domains are visited in a random permutation; each domain's GRPO gradient is computed at parameters already perturbed by prior domains' updates, so a first-order Taylor expansion reveals that the gradient shift encodes the cross-domain product H_j·g_i. After the full sequential pass, the resulting parameter change is interpolated with the original parameters via a mixing coefficient α (default 1.2), balancing curvature-informed exploration against stability. Theoretically, taking expectation over the random permutation symmetrizes the cross terms into ∇(g_i^T g_j), showing that CGPO implicitly maximizes inter-domain gradient inner products and steers parameters toward regions of high cross-domain consistency. The approach adds negligible overhead (~8% per-step time) since sequential updates are equivalent to splitting a mini-batch into smaller chunks, and no additional gradient storage is required.

## Results

CGPO achieves the highest average score across all seven benchmarks for both model scales: 50.42 on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and 59.59 on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, outperforming the strongest baselines (Omni-Thinker at 49.13/56.75 and FAMO at 49.01/57.26) by 1.3 and 2.3 points respectively. The largest per-domain gains appear in code generation (HumanEval 84.15, MBPP 72.00 on 7B) and creative writing (WritingBench 66.52 on 7B), while math and scientific QA show more modest but consistent improvements. Training reward curves confirm that CGPO accelerates reward improvement across all four domains, with the most pronounced advantage in creative writing where cross-domain conflicts are strongest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Omni-Thinker (a progressive curriculum learning method with a fixed domain order), CGPO wins on average by 1.29 points (3B) and 2.84 points (7B), with the largest margins on code generation (HumanEval +1.36, MBPP +3.40 on 7B) and creative writing (WritingBench +4.17 on 7B); Omni-Thinker slightly edges CGPO on MATH500 for 3B (65.65 vs 64.20) but trails on AMC and all other benchmarks. Against FAMO (a gradient-magnitude balancing method), CGPO leads by 1.41 points (3B) and 2.33 points (7B), again with the most substantial gains in code (MBPP +2.00 on 7B) and creative writing (WritingBench +2.90 on 7B); FAMO is competitive on math (MATH500 75.65 vs 75.55 on 7B) and SuperGPQA but cannot match CGPO's cross-domain coordination. Both baselines also trail CGPO on training reward curves, where CGPO consistently stays above them across all four domains, confirming that curvature-guided sequential updates outperform both difficulty-scheduled curricula and reactive gradient balancing for multi-domain RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGPO builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its per-domain policy gradient surrogate, and draws its core design principle from Newton's method: preconditioning gradients with curvature to correct directional deviations in complex landscapes. It departs from the dominant line of gradient-conflict mitigation (PCGrad, CAGrad, FAMO, MGDA), which intervenes reactively at gradient aggregation time and requires simultaneous storage of all domain gradients—impractical at LLM scale. The sequential-update mechanism itself has precedents in meta-learning (Reptile) and federated learning (FedAvg), but the paper's novelty lies in motivating this mechanism through a second-order (Hessian-gradient) lens and showing that randomized sequential passes implicitly induce cross-domain curvature interactions that align gradients in expectation, without ever computing a Hessian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in multi-domain RLHF: cross-domain conflicts that cause gains in one capability to erode another, and the memory infeasibility of standard gradient-surgery methods at LLM scale. CGPO's negligible overhead (Table 2) and lack of multi-gradient storage make it immediately deployable in existing RLHF pipelines (verl framework). However, the evaluation is limited to 3B/7B models, four domains, and a single epoch; the Taylor-expansion analysis is first-order and may not capture behavior in deeper training or with more domains. Open questions include whether the curvature-alignment interpretation holds empirically at scale (32B+), whether the approach extends to more than four domains or to pre-training, and whether the modest but consistent gains persist under longer training or different reward designs.
