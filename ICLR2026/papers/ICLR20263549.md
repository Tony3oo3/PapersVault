## Problem

Outcome-based RL for multi-turn search agents suffers from severe reward sparsity: a single terminal signal causes advantage collapse (zero gradients when all rollouts agree), obscures fine-grained credit assignment across turns, and wastes dense intermediate information. Existing process-reward alternatives rely on external reward models, manual step annotations, or high-variance Monte Carlo estimation, making them costly, biased, or unstable. The paper asks whether a simple, intrinsic, and computationally cheap turn-level reward can resolve all three issues simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a text question and iteratively invokes an external search tool (Google Search API) over up to 10 turns, where each intermediate turn comprises a reasoning step, a structured tool call, and the returned tool response. Tool-response tokens serve as conditioning context but are masked from the loss; only model-generated decision tokens (reasoning, tool-call, and answer tokens) are optimized. The final turn produces a rationale-then-answer sequence, and the ground-truth answer is appended in a fixed schema to compute teacher-forced log-probabilities for the information-gain reward.

## Method

IGPO defines each turn's reward as the marginal gain in length-normalized ground-truth log-probability between consecutive interaction histories, computed under teacher forcing with a stop-gradient operator. These information-gain rewards and the standard F1 outcome reward are separately z-normalized within each rollout group, then combined via discounted accumulation (γ=1.0) to yield turn-level discounted returns that replace GRPO's rollout-level advantages in a clipped surrogate objective with KL regularization. A vectorized implementation appends T copies of the formatted ground-truth target to the trajectory with a custom attention mask, computing all turn-level confidences in a single forward pass at <0.02% end-to-end overhead. A theoretical analysis links maximizing cumulative information-gain reward to tightening an upper bound on snowball error accumulation, providing a principled justification for the dense turn-level signal.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, IGPO achieves an average word-level F1 of 60.2 across seven QA benchmarks (4 in-domain, 3 out-of-domain), outperforming the strongest outcome-reward baseline DeepResearcher (53.9) by +6.3 points and the best step-reward baseline GiGPO (47.2) by +13.0 points. On the smaller Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, IGPO improves over standard GRPO by +16.6 points (32.3 → 48.9), demonstrating that dense turn-level supervision is especially beneficial when advantage collapse is severe. IGPO also adds less than 0.02% end-to-end training overhead compared to GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGPO outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset. Against prompt-based methods (CoT, CoT+RAG, Search-o1), IGPO gains 23.8–36.8 points in average F1, confirming the necessity of RL training. Among outcome-reward RL methods, IGPO surpasses DeepResearcher (53.9) by +6.3, Search-r1-base (49.2) by +11.0, and R1-searcher (49.0) by +11.2. Among step-reward RL methods, IGPO leads GiGPO (47.2) by +13.0, StepSearch-base (46.0) by +14.2, and ReasoningRAG (42.3) by +17.9. In the controlled RL-algorithm comparison (Table 2, same hyperparameters), IGPO (60.2) beats GRPO (51.9) by +8.3, GSPO (52.0) by +8.2, PPO (51.5) by +8.7, RLOO (49.7) by +10.5, and Reinforce++ (47.3) by +12.9. The largest relative gains appear on multi-hop and out-of-domain datasets (e.g., 2Wiki: 72.1 vs. 59.7 for DeepResearcher; Bamboogle: 77.0 vs. 71.0), suggesting that dense turn-level credit assignment is most impactful when trajectories are long and intermediate steps matter.

## Relation to Prior Work

IGPO builds directly on the GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024), retaining its clipped surrogate objective and group-relative normalization while replacing the single rollout-level advantage with turn-level discounted returns. It departs from outcome-reward RL methods (Search-R1, DeepResearcher, R1-Searcher) that supervise only the final answer, and from process-reward methods (StepSearch, ReasoningRAG, GiGPO) that rely on external annotations, MCTS, or Monte Carlo estimation. The key conceptual departure is deriving dense turn-level rewards from the model's own ground-truth confidence updates under teacher forcing, eliminating the need for any external reward model or costly simulation while remaining on-policy and annotation-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IGPO addresses a concrete and widely-observed training pathology (advantage collapse) in multi-turn agentic RL with a mechanism that adds less than 0.02% end-to-end overhead, making it highly practical for adoption. The finding that information-gain-only rewards (without outcome supervision) already match or exceed standard GRPO suggests the ground-truth confidence signal is remarkably informative, though this also raises the question of how the method behaves when ground truth is ambiguous or unavailable. The primary limitation is the hard dependency on ground-truth answers, which restricts applicability to open-ended or creative tasks; extending IGPO to settings without explicit supervision is identified as key future work.
