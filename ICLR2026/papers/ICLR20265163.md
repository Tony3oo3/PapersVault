## Problem

Existing time series QA datasets (e.g., Time-MQA) operate at the level of surface pattern matching rather than genuine multi-step reasoning, with overly simple questions, insufficient context, and ambiguous options that force models to guess. Pretrained LLMs lack temporal priors entirely, and no unified framework exists that defines which tasks truly demand reasoning over time series, how to construct high-quality reasoning data, or how to train a single model across diverse time series reasoning capabilities.

## Input / Output

The model consumes time series as tokenized text sequences (average ~860 tokens for the series, ~246 for context, totaling ~1,106 tokens) alongside auxiliary context such as event descriptions, pricing schedules, or task instructions. Outputs follow a strict schema: a chain-of-thought rationale enclosed in  tags followed by a final answer in <answer></answer> tags. For three of four tasks the answer is a single uppercase letter (multiple choice); for event-aware forecasting it is a numerical sequence of a fixed horizon length, where the model must also correctly count the number of output steps.

## Method

TIMEOMNI-1 is built on Qwen2.5-Instruct-7B and trained in two stages. Stage 1 (SFT) injects temporal priors by fine-tuning on ~2.3K human-guided chain-of-thought traces generated through a hierarchical annotation pipeline (LLM Analyzer → Human Reviewers → LLM Rewriter) that enforces structured decomposition templates per task. Stage 2 applies GRPO reinforcement learning with task-grounded outcome rewards: a binary exact-match reward for discrete tasks and an exponential-decay-of-MAE reward plus a horizon-length bonus for the forecasting task, combined with a format-compliance component (λ=0.1). A key finding is that RL alone on the base model causes performance collapse, confirming that SFT priors are a prerequisite for stable RL refinement. All four tasks (scenario understanding, causality discovery, event-aware forecasting, decision making) are jointly trained in a single model, and the paper provides the first evidence that joint training yields mutual gains across the three underlying capabilities (perception, extrapolation, decision-making), supporting a train-once, use-across-tasks paradigm.

## Results

TIMEOMNI-1 (built on Qwen2.5-Instruct-7B) achieves top-2 performance across all four reasoning tasks under both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) testbeds. The headline result is on Causality Discovery, where TIMEOMNI-1 reaches 69.3% ACC (ID) and 64.0% ACC (OOD), surpassing GPT-4.1 by 40.6% (ID) and 28.1% (OOD). On Decision Making, it achieves 47.9% (ID) and 58.9% (OOD) versus GPT-4.1's 25.5% and 27.8%, respectively. On Event-aware Forecasting, TIMEOMNI-1 reports 14.30 MAE (ID) with 93.8% success rate, slightly behind GPT-4.1's 13.79 MAE but with a substantially higher OOD success rate (82.3% vs. 76.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4.1, TIMEOMNI-1 delivers a decisive win on Causality Discovery (69.3% vs. 28.7% ID; 64.0% vs. 35.9% OOD) and Decision Making (47.9% vs. 25.5% ID; 58.9% vs. 27.8% OOD), while matching or slightly exceeding it on Scenario Understanding (90.7% vs. 85.5% ID). On Event-aware Forecasting, GPT-4.1 retains a narrow MAE edge in-distribution (13.79 vs. 14.30), but TIMEOMNI-1 achieves a higher OOD success rate (82.3% vs. 76.1%) and lower OOD MAE (145.53 vs. 170.78). Among time-series-specific baselines, Time-R1 (the closest TSRM competitor) trails TIMEOMNI-1 by 39.1 points on Causality Discovery ID and 20.1 points on Decision Making ID, while Time-MQA and ChatTS suffer from very low success rates (often below 50%) that reflect poor instruction-following. Compared to the Qwen2.5-7B base model, TIMEOMNI-1 improves Causality Discovery by 47.7 points (ID) and Decision Making by 22.4 points (ID), confirming that the two-stage SFT+RL pipeline injects genuine temporal reasoning rather than surface pattern matching. On general reasoning benchmarks (DROP, GPQA, ReClor), TIMEOMNI-1 improves average accuracy by 16.5% over the base model, showing that time-series specialization does not degrade general capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the R1-style SFT+RL paradigm (DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeekMath/GRPO) from math and code to time series, introducing task-grounded reward functions tailored to temporal reasoning. It departs from existing time series QA datasets (Time-MQA, ChatTS) that the authors argue reward surface pattern matching over genuine reasoning, and from time series foundation models (Time-MoE, Moirai) that remain confined to forecasting without multi-step reasoning. The key conceptual departure is formalizing time series reasoning as a progressive three-capability hierarchy (perception → extrapolation → decision-making) with four atomic tasks, rather than treating it as a collection of independent single-task benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TSR-SUITE (23K+ samples with 2.3K human-annotated CoT traces) is the first time series reasoning suite that supports both training and evaluation, addressing a critical data bottleneck that has kept the field fragmented across single-task, single-dataset pipelines. The finding that joint training across perception, extrapolation, and decision-making yields mutual gains supports a 'train-once, use-across-tasks' paradigm that could reduce the cost of building TSRMs. Open questions include whether the text-based tokenization strategy scales to high-dimensional or very long sequences (the authors acknowledge the absence of a ViT-analogous pretrained TS encoder), whether the 7B model's results transfer to larger backbones, and how the framework extends beyond the four tasks to broader temporal reasoning.
