## Problem

Existing training-free caching methods for Flow Matching inference reuse instantaneous velocity or feature information across timesteps, which causes severe trajectory drift and error accumulation at high acceleration ratios. No prior caching framework operates in the average-velocity domain to provide a more stable reconstruction signal, and fixed-interval or threshold-based cache scheduling rules degrade generation quality when the speedup exceeds ~3×.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262056/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MeanCache takes a text prompt and a pre-trained Flow Matching model (FLUX.1 at 1024×1024, Qwen-Image at 1664×928, or HunyuanVideo for video) and produces the corresponding generated image or video. The user specifies a budget B (number of full NFE steps, e.g., 10–15 out of 50), which directly controls the acceleration ratio. A one-time precomputation step samples 50 prompts from T2V-CompBench to build a stability map (multigraph) that guides cache placement; at inference time no additional data or retraining is required.

## Method

MeanCache reformulates feature caching in the average-velocity domain by deriving a start-point MeanFlow Identity (v(z_t,t) = u(z_t,t,s) − (s−t)·du/dt) and approximating the derivative term with a cached Jacobian-vector product (JVP) computed from a reference timestep r preceding t, yielding the estimator ũ(z_t,t,s) = v(z_t,t) + (s−t)·JVP_K. The JVP span K controls how far back the cached segment extends, trading approximation error against stability. To decide when to cache and what K to use, the paper constructs a multigraph over timesteps whose edge weights are the L1 deviation between true and JVP-approximated average velocities, then solves a Peak-Suppressed Shortest Path (minimizing Σ C(e)^γ subject to path length ≤ B) via dynamic programming. The peak-suppression exponent γ (γ≥1) penalizes error spikes concentrated on few edges, and the budget B directly sets the acceleration ratio. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires only a one-time stability-map precomputation.

## Results

On FLUX.1[dev] (1024×1024), MeanCache (B=10) achieves a 4.12× speedup (2.81 s latency) while maintaining an ImageReward of 0.993, CLIP Score of 31.323, and LPIPS of 0.272, whereas competing methods at similar speedups collapse to negative ImageReward scores. On Qwen-Image (1664×928), MeanCache (B=10) reaches 4.56× speedup with ImageReward 1.142 and LPIPS 0.236, closely matching the original 50-step quality (ImageReward 1.180). On HunyuanVideo, MeanCache (B=10) achieves 3.59× speedup with a VBench score of 80.08% (vs. 80.39% original) and SSIM of 0.732, outperforming all baselines at comparable or higher acceleration ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeanCache decisively outperforms all six baselines at high acceleration ratios. On FLUX.1[dev] at ~4× speedup, MeanCache (B=10) achieves ImageReward 0.993 and LPIPS 0.272, while TeaCache (l=1.5) drops to 0.717/0.504, DiCache (δ=2.0) to −0.652/0.586, and TaylorSeer (N=20,O=1) to −0.727/0.798 — all baselines suffer catastrophic quality collapse. On Qwen-Image at ~4.5× speedup, MeanCache (B=10) retains ImageReward 1.142 and CLIP Score 33.621 (nearly matching the original 1.180/33.626), whereas DBCache (r=1.5) collapses to −2.059 and DBCache+TaylorSeer to −0.227. On HunyuanVideo at 3.59×, MeanCache (B=10) achieves VBench 80.08% and SSIM 0.732, surpassing DiCache (δ=3.0) at 70.86%/0.490, TeaCache (l=0.39) at 79.75%/0.631, and TaylorSeer (N=7,O=1) at 79.76%/0.595. At moderate acceleration (~3×), MeanCache also leads on reconstruction metrics: on FLUX.1 it achieves LPIPS 0.142 and SSIM 0.870 vs. TaylorSeer's 0.415/0.663 and TeaCache's 0.338/0.721, confirming its advantage is not limited to extreme speedups.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeanCache builds directly on the MeanFlow identity (Geng et al., 2025), which provides a theoretical bridge between instantaneous and average velocity via Jacobian-vector products, and repurposes this identity as a practical caching mechanism rather than a training objective. It departs from the entire line of feature-caching methods (DeepCache, TeaCache, TaylorSeer, DiCache, DBCache, ToCa, DuCa) that operate in the instantaneous-velocity domain by reformulating the reuse problem in the average-velocity domain, where trajectories are inherently smoother. The trajectory-stability scheduling component draws on graph-based shortest-path modeling from ShortDF (Chen et al., 2025) and trajectory drift mitigation ideas from sequence processing (Lin et al., 2019), adapted here to a budget-constrained multigraph over denoising timesteps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MeanCache provides a training-free, drop-in acceleration strategy for commercial-scale flow matching models (FLUX.1, Qwen-Image, HunyuanVideo) that maintains generation quality at 3.5–4.5× speedup where all prior caching baselines collapse, making it practically relevant for interactive and resource-constrained deployment. The average-velocity perspective opens a new design axis for caching strategies beyond step-wise feature reuse, potentially inspiring further stability-driven acceleration methods. Limitations include the need for a precomputed stability map (50 prompts × 5 seeds) that may not transfer perfectly to unseen prompt distributions, and the JVP approximation quality is bounded by the cache span K, which constrains the maximum achievable speedup without quality loss.
