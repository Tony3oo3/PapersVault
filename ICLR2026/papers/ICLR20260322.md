## Problem

Standard reinforcement learning policies for navigation lack explicit geometric and physical structure, leading to poor sample efficiency, brittle generalization under distribution shift, and difficulty coordinating multi-scale temporal decisions (reactive avoidance vs. strategic planning). In simultaneous navigation and mapping (SNAM), most approaches build detailed global maps before navigating, whereas the practical goal of reaching a goal with minimal exploration while maintaining safety and path quality remains unaddressed, especially for deformable robots that must squeeze through narrow gaps.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only a local 2d×2d sensing window containing obstacle primitives, a goal position, and the robot's current state (center position, orientation, scale, and angular velocity) — no global map is ever constructed. Outputs are structured as energy gradients from a surrogate Hamiltonian: the navigator produces adaptive dual weights (β, λ, per-obstacle α_i) and friction coefficients that reshape the local energy landscape, while three sub-policies emit velocity commands, path waypoints, and deformation (scale) adjustments. The hyperelastic ring robot is represented by a periodic cubic B-spline with reduced coordinates (s, o, θ), and all forces are mapped to generalized coordinates via virtual work.

## Method

GRL-SNAM formulates navigation as dynamics on a generalized Hamiltonian manifold where H(q,p) = ½pᵀM⁻¹p + R(q;E), with the potential R decomposed into sensor cost, goal attraction, deformation energy, and IPC collision barriers, each governed by an independent sub-policy (sensor, frame, shape) operating at distinct timescales with disjoint parameters. A meta-policy g_ξ maps the current environment descriptor to nonnegative dual weights via a permutation-invariant set encoder, assembling a surrogate Hamiltonian whose port-Hamiltonian flow (with Rayleigh damping and port corrections) generates feedforward control actions. Offline, reference Hamiltonians are fitted from interaction trajectories; online, a secant Gauss-Newton update on observable residuals (clearance, goal distance, speed) performs stagewise Jacobian-based corrections to the active energy weights without retraining. The Legendre-Fenchel conjugate of the control penalty provides the kinetic term, linking the formulation directly to Pontryagin's optimal control, while symplectic integration preserves energy invariants for long-horizon stability.

## Results

On 2D deformable navigation tasks, GRL-SNAM achieves SPL = 0.95, Detour Ratio = 1.09, and Minimum Clearance = 0.26 m while mapping only 10.7% of the environment—matching CBF-level path quality (SPL = 0.96) with a comparable sensing budget. In the short-rollout point-agent setting, GRL-SNAM reaches 87.5% success with 0.3 m mean state error and 0.1 m mean goal distance, using only 500k gradient steps versus 3.2–4.1M environment steps for PPO/TRPO/SAC (which achieve 18–26% success). The method also generalizes to out-of-distribution layouts with near-100% success, where all baselines degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CBF baseline, GRL-SNAM achieves nearly identical path quality (SPL 0.95 vs. 0.96, Detour 1.09 vs. 1.04) with a slightly lower minimum clearance (0.26 m vs. 0.32 m) but a marginally smaller mapping ratio (10.7% vs. 11.2%), confirming that the Hamiltonian energy refinement extracts comparable navigation value per sensed unit. Against deep RL baselines (PPO, TRPO, SAC), GRL-SNAM dominates across all metrics: SPL of 0.95 versus 0.07–0.57, positive clearance (0.26 m) versus near-zero or negative clearance (−0.09 to 0.004 m), and a smaller mapping budget (10.7% vs. 14.3–14.7%). In the matched short-rollout point-agent experiment, GRL-SNAM achieves 87.5% success with 0.3 m mean state error, while PPO/TRPO/SAC reach only 26.1%/21.7%/18.4% success with 1.8–2.4 m state error, despite 6–8× more environment interaction steps. The paper also notes that GRL-SNAM uniquely dominates the safety–performance Pareto frontier, maintaining high SPL with positive clearance where all RL baselines either collide, stall, or take inefficient detours under the same sensing constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRL-SNAM extends the Differential Policy Optimization framework of Nguyen & Bajaj (2025) from low-dimensional control to simultaneous navigation and mapping, coupling it with Pontryagin's Maximum Principle and Legendre-Fenchel conjugacy to derive a reduced Hamiltonian whose gradients generate local control actions. It departs from standard Bellman-recursive RL (PPO, TRPO, SAC) by replacing value-function bootstrapping with direct energy-landscape learning, and from traditional SNAM methods (SGoLAM, CMP, CL-SLAM) by explicitly targeting minimal exploration rather than progressive global map construction. The modular architecture with three independent score functions (sensor, frame, shape) and stagewise secant Gauss-Newton adaptation distinguishes it from both monolithic RL policies and pre-programmed deformation strategies for soft robots (HAVEN, LCE-based gap navigation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that Hamiltonian structure can serve as a strong inductive bias for navigation, achieving near-CBF path quality (SPL 0.95) with minimal mapping (10.7%) and substantially outperforming deep RL baselines (87.5% vs. 18–26% success) under identical short-rollout sensing constraints. However, all evaluations are confined to 2D procedurally generated environments with a single hyperelastic ring robot, leaving open whether the approach scales to 3D, richer sensing modalities, or real-world deployment. The 'minimal exploration' framing is a useful reframing of the SNAM objective, but its practical significance depends on whether the Hamiltonian parameterization remains tractable as environment complexity and robot degrees of freedom grow.
