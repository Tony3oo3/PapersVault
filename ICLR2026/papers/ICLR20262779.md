## Problem

Agentic RAG systems trained with outcome-based RL rewards exhibit widespread suboptimal search behaviors: over-search (redundant retrievals of already-known information) and under-search (failing to retrieve when needed). Existing efficiency-oriented methods rely on coarse proxies such as length penalties or confidence thresholds, which either over-suppress necessary searches or fail to provide explicit, step-specific feedback on whether each individual retrieval decision was warranted.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262779/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language question and interacts with a Wikipedia-based retrieval environment (E5-base retriever returning top-3 passages per query). The output is a single  block containing a sequence of explicitly tagged <step> blocks—each either a search step (<reasoning> → <search> → <context> → <conclusion>) or a non-search step (<reasoning> → <conclusion>)—followed by a final <answer> tag. This enforced XML schema is what makes the trajectory deterministically parsable by rule-based methods, enabling per-step reward assignment without expensive LLM interpretation.

## Method

HiPRAG introduces a hierarchical process reward into the RL training loop for agentic RAG. For each search step, over-search is detected on-the-fly by re-generating an answer from the search query alone and using an external LLM judge (GPT-4.1 mini) to check semantic equivalence with the step's original conclusion; for each non-search step, under-search is detected by prompting an external verifier (GPT-5 mini) to assess factual and logical correctness of the reasoning and conclusion. The reward function R(T) = A(T)(1−λf) + λf·F(T) + λp·A(T)·F(T)·N_corr(T)/N(T) gates the process bonus (proportional to the fraction of optimal steps) behind both answer correctness and format correctness, so the agent is first incentivized to produce well-formed, correct outputs before receiving additional reward for search efficiency. This is trained via PPO or GRPO on Qwen2.5 (3B/7B) and Llama-3.2-3B models, reducing over-search rate from >27% to 2.3% while improving average CEM to 65.4% (3B) and 67.2% (7B) across seven QA benchmarks.

## Results

HiPRAG achieves average Cover Exact Match (CEM) scores of 65.4% (3B) and 67.2% (7B) across seven QA benchmarks, outperforming the strongest baseline β-GRPO (62.5%) by 2.9 and 4.7 points respectively. On the efficiency side, HiPRAG reduces the over-search rate from over 27% in prior baselines to just 2.3% (7B-GRPO) and lowers the under-search rate to as low as 29.0%, while also cutting the average number of search calls per question from 2.45 (Search-R1) and 2.15 (β-GRPO) down to 1.75. The 7B model leads on most individual benchmarks, e.g., 71.2% on NQ, 76.3% on TriviaQA, and 71.7% on 2WikiMultiHopQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiPRAG outperforms all seven baselines on average CEM. Against the strongest RL-based baselines, HiPRAG-7B (67.2%) beats β-GRPO (62.5%) by 4.7 points and R1-Searcher++ (62.1%) by 5.1 points; even the 3B model (65.4%) surpasses both. Compared to the prompt-based agentic RAG methods, HiPRAG-7B leads Search-o1 (43.9%) by 23.3 points and IRCoT (36.4%) by 30.8 points, while Standard RAG (45.3%) trails by 21.9 points. On efficiency, HiPRAG reduces the over-search rate from >27% in prior work to 2.3% (7B-GRPO) and cuts average search calls from 2.45 (Search-R1) to 1.75, a 29% reduction, without sacrificing accuracy. The gains are consistent across model families (Qwen2.5, Llama-3.2), RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO), and model types (base, instruct), with the 3B HiPRAG model (64.4% under GRPO) even surpassing the 7B R1-Searcher++ baseline (62.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

HiPRAG builds directly on the Search-R1 framework (Jin et al., 2025a,b), which established the RL paradigm for training LLMs to interleave reasoning with search, and extends its outcome+format reward with a hierarchical process bonus. It departs from prior efficiency-focused methods like R1-Searcher++ (length/retrieval-count penalties) and β-GRPO (confidence-based rewards) by replacing coarse trajectory-level signals with explicit, step-level detection of over-search and under-search via on-the-fly LLM judges. The structured, machine-parsable output format is a practical departure from the free-form  blocks used in Search-R1, enabling deterministic step isolation without expensive post-hoc NLP interpretation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic reduction in over-search rate (from >27% to 2.3%) while simultaneously improving accuracy demonstrates that fine-grained process supervision can correct search behavior without the over-suppression pitfalls of length penalties. The finding that a 3B model trained with HiPRAG outperforms 7B baselines suggests reward design can be more impactful than raw model scale for search-agent tasks. Key limitations include the dependency on proprietary LLM judges (GPT-4.1 mini, GPT-5 mini) for detection, which adds inference cost and a single point of failure, and the under-search rate remains non-trivial (~29–33%), leaving room for further improvement. Open questions include whether the structured format constraint generalizes to more complex multi-tool agentic settings and whether the detection accuracy degrades on more nuanced or domain-specific queries.
