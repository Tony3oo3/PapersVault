## Problem

Existing GNN-based 3D dynamics models restrict message passing to explicitly observed graph structures (e.g., distance-based adjacency), neglecting unobserved all-pair interactions that are critical for long-range physical effects like Van der Waals tails, crystallization, and protein folding. Modeling these latent interactions is non-trivial because the search space over potential pair structures is exponential in particle count, and preserving SE(3)-equivariance while accommodating such latent structure is not straightforward.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a geometric graph at time t=0 consisting of N particle 3D positions, velocities, node features (e.g., atomic types as one-hot vectors), and edge attributes encoding the observed interaction structure. It produces predicted 3D positions for all N particles at T future time steps simultaneously via a parallel decoder, supporting both single-step (S2S) and multi-step trajectory (S2T) prediction. The recurrent encoder processes one attention layer per time step, while the EGNN-based decoder generates all T predictions in parallel from the initial state, keeping inference cost independent of T.

## Method

PAINET derives its attention mechanism from minimizing a regularized energy function (Eqn. 3) that balances embedding fidelity against pairwise smoothness governed by a Landau-Ginzburg quadratic potential ρ_ij(h²) = a_ij·h² − b_ij·h⁴. Theorem 1 proves that a specific convex-combination update rule constitutes a rigorous descent step on this non-convex energy, yielding a closed-form attention where weights are the gradient of ρ_ij evaluated at current embedding distances. The attention uses adaptive pairwise mappings Φ and Ψ parameterized by particle-type lookup embeddings (Z·E_φ·Zᵀ), enabling type-specific long-range dependencies. A parallel EGNN decoder then integrates the latent embeddings with the observed graph structure to produce SE(3)-equivariant position predictions for all T time steps simultaneously, preserving equivariance throughout both encoding and decoding.

## Results

PAINET achieves the lowest prediction error across all 11 datasets and both evaluation tasks (S2S and S2T), yielding 4.7%–41.5% MSE reductions over the strongest baselines. On Motion Capture, PAINET attains 8.45×10⁻² F-MSE on Walk (S2S) and 0.86×10⁻¹ A-MSE on Walk (S2T), outperforming the best competitor GF-NODE by 40.5% and 31.2% respectively. On MD17, improvements range from 1.6% to 18.0% (notably 13.4% on benzene S2S and 18.0% on naphthalene S2T). On the large-scale Adk protein, PAINET achieves A-MSE 1.654 vs. 1.735 (HEGNN) and 1.762 (GF-NODE), with the gap widening at longer horizons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAINET outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and task. Against the most competitive recent methods, PAINET reduces F-MSE by 40.5% over GF-NODE on Walk (S2S: 8.45 vs. 15.7) and by 31.2% on Walk (S2T: 0.86 vs. 1.25 A-MSE). On MD17, PAINET beats EGNO by up to 13.4% (benzene S2S: 4.65 vs. 6.20) and GF-NODE by up to 18.0% (naphthalene S2T: 3.24 vs. 4.58). On the large-molecule MD22 benchmark, PAINET achieves the best F-MSE (3.00 vs. 3.11 for GF-NODE on stachyose) and A-MSE (2.40 vs. 2.54). On the Adk protein, PAINET's A-MSE of 1.654 surpasses HEGNN (1.735), GF-NODE (1.762), and EGNO (1.805), with the advantage growing at later time steps (e.g., t=5: 1.994 vs. 2.087/2.113/2.240). Classic baselines (EGNN, MPNN, RF, ClofNet) are substantially outperformed, with PAINET reducing Walk S2S F-MSE by over 67% relative to EGNN (8.45 vs. 26.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

PAINET extends the EGNN lineage (Satorras et al., 2021) and its recent successors—EGNO (temporal convolution), HEGNN (higher-order interactions), and GF-NODE (neural-ODE formulation)—by addressing their shared limitation: message passing restricted to a fixed observed adjacency graph. The key conceptual departure is formulating all-pair latent interactions as the minimization of a regularized energy function over particle embeddings, yielding a closed-form attention update (Theorem 1) whose pairwise weights are instantiated via a Landau-Ginzburg quadratic potential. This connects the architecture to classical physics (phase transitions, symmetry breaking) in a way that prior equivariant GNNs for dynamics did not, while the parallel EGNN decoder preserves SE(3)-equivariance without the sequential cost of recurrent decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling unobserved all-pair interactions—rather than relying solely on distance-based adjacency—yields consistent and sometimes large improvements (up to 41.5%) across motion capture, small-molecule MD, large-molecule MD, and protein dynamics, with comparable compute costs. The energy-minimization derivation provides a principled (rather than ad-hoc) justification for the attention mechanism, which could inform future designs for other relational learning tasks. Open questions include whether the quadratic Landau-Ginzburg potential is truly optimal versus other concave forms, whether the O(N²) all-pair attention remains tractable for systems with tens of thousands of particles, and whether the approach generalizes to continuous-time settings beyond discrete-step prediction.
