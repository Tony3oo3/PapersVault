## Problem

While soft labels in knowledge distillation are known to encode statistical structure in the data distribution, it remained unclear whether they also convey memorized information—specific facts the teacher has overfit—and whether a student trained on only a subset of these soft labels can recover non-trivial accuracy on held-out memorized data. Prior work addressed this question only from a privacy-attack perspective, leaving the benign distillation setting, where a student legitimately inherits the teacher's memorized knowledge, unexplored.

## Input / Output

The student receives input feature vectors x ∈ R^d paired with the teacher's soft labels (softmax of teacher logits at temperature τ) for only a fraction ρ of the teacher's memorized training set D_*^T. Evaluation is performed on the held-out remainder D_test^S (data the teacher memorized but the student never saw) and on validation data D_val, with the critical comparison being acc_test^S versus random guessing (1/c) and versus acc_val^S. For logistic regression the student is recovered in closed form via the pseudo-inverse of the input matrix applied to teacher logits; for non-linear models the student is trained via cross-entropy on soft labels using Adam.

## Method

The paper isolates memorization transfer in two controlled settings: small transformers on modular addition (where early stopping yields teachers at varying memorization/generalization stages) and fully connected networks on purely random i.i.d. Gaussian inputs with uniform labels (where generalization is impossible by design, so any teacher fit implies pure memorization). For multinomial logistic regression, the authors derive closed-form thresholds—teacher memorization capacity α_label^T ≈ 2 (Cover's theorem), identifiability threshold α_id^S(ρ) = 1/ρ, and student memorization capacity α_label^S(ρ)—that delineate distinct leakage regimes in the (α, ρ) plane, and show that temperature τ interpolates between fitting the teacher function (high τ) and recovering only class identity (low τ). For single-hidden-layer ReLU MLPs, the authors identify two functionally distinct student solutions: one that memorizes the soft labels and one that matches the teacher functionally, with a sudden accuracy jump marking the transition once the teacher becomes identifiable. Ablations show that the complete soft label vector is necessary for leakage—zeroing out even a single entry or removing top-k truncation destroys the student's ability to recover held-out memorized data.

## Results

In the 2D random-label setting (3 classes), an independent student trained on hard labels achieves only ~30% (chance) test accuracy, while a student trained on the teacher's soft labels reaches ~50% on held-out memorized data. In the modular addition task (p=113), students distilled from memorizing teachers at τ=10 achieve acc_test^S substantially above acc_val^S, reaching 100% on held-out teacher data while failing to generalize to the validation set. For multinomial logistic regression on random i.i.d. data, the teacher memorization capacity is α_label^T ≈ 1.96 (close to Cover's bound of 2), and the identifiability threshold α_id^S(ρ=0.8) ≈ 1.26, beyond which the student recovers the teacher functionally and achieves ≥99% accuracy on held-out memorized samples. In a GPT-2 classifier fine-tuned on 6,000 random token sequences, a student seeing only 80% of the teacher's data achieves 65.2% test accuracy versus ~0.1% random guessing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between an independent student (trained via cross-entropy on ground-truth class labels only, making it independent of the teacher) and a soft-label student (trained to match the teacher's softmax-transformed logits). Across all settings—2D random data, modular addition, logistic regression, ReLU MLPs, and GPT-2—the soft-label student consistently and substantially outperforms the independent student on held-out memorized data: ~50% vs ~30% (2D, 3 classes), ~66% vs ~6% (2D, 20 classes), and 65.2% vs 0.1% (GPT-2, 1000 classes). Temperature acts as a critical control: at low τ (e.g., 0.1), soft labels approximate one-hot labels and the student's behavior becomes independent of the teacher (reducing to the hard-label baseline), while at high τ (e.g., 10–20), the student can functionally recover the teacher and achieve near-perfect accuracy on held-out memorized data. A within-class shuffled soft-label baseline (preserving class identity but breaking teacher-specific input correlations) shows that the leakage depends on the full soft-label vector, not merely the argmax class, confirming that the 'dark knowledge' in off-diagonal logit entries carries the memorized information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear identifiability result of Phuong & Lampert (2019)—showing that distillation allows a student to functionally match a teacher—to multi-class and non-linear ReLU MLP settings, and crucially to a regime where the teacher has purely memorized data with no latent structure. It builds on the modular addition task and single-layer transformer from Nanda et al. (2023) to study structured-data memorization, and on Hinton et al.'s (2015) soft-label distillation framework as the mechanism under investigation. The key departure from prior distillation theory is isolating memorization as the sole source of information in soft labels, a controlled setting the authors note has not been previously studied in the distillation literature, and connecting this to the privacy literature on memorization leakage (Ma et al. 2024; Cloud et al. 2025) from a benign rather than adversarial perspective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that soft labels leak held-out memorized data even when no generalizable structure exists has direct privacy implications for distillation pipelines: a student trained on a fraction of a teacher's soft labels can recover information about data it never saw, up to perfect accuracy. The closed-form capacity and identifiability thresholds for logistic regression, and the discovery of two distinct leakage mechanisms in ReLU MLPs (soft-label memorization vs. teacher functional matching), provide a theoretical framework for understanding when and how this leakage occurs. Key limitations include the restriction to synthetic data and simple architectures (single-layer MLPs, small transformers, one GPT-2 instance), the absence of regularization or optimization-dynamics analysis, and the open question of how these regimes translate to deeper, more realistic models where memorized and generalized content coexist.
