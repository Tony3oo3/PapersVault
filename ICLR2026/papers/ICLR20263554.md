## Problem

Existing KG-augmented LLM frameworks rely on static integration mechanisms—either fixed reasoning paths or local-only retrieval—that cannot adapt to evolving context and retrieved evidence, causing error propagation and premature termination in multi-hop reasoning. Specifically, they suffer from inflexible reasoning (predefined depth/width parameters) and limited information use (ignoring global context such as prior steps and forward-looking relations), leaving intermediate hallucinations uncorrected until the full chain is complete.

## Input / Output

VoG takes a natural language question, a knowledge graph (Freebase or Wikidata), and explicitly mentioned topic entities as input. It produces a final answer entity extracted from a revised reasoning plan, where the plan is a sequence of steps each containing a thought, an action (Search or Finish), and a predicted observation. At each step, the system also generates KG feedback as a set of retrieved triplets that serve as the factual basis for verification and revision, with the plan length T adaptively determined by the plan agent rather than fixed a priori.

## Method

VoG employs three specialized LLM agents (plan, retrieve, verify) in an iterative loop: the plan agent generates a ReAct-style reasoning plan, the retrieve agent performs plan-guided stepwise KG retrieval via SPARQL queries with entropy-based adaptive sampling to control candidate set width, and the verify agent checks each step's predicted observation against retrieved triplets using both an LLM prompt and a DeBERTa NLI classifier. When a revision signal is triggered, a context-aware multi-armed bandit (modified UCB) selects among three context strategies—Local (immediate KG feedback), Lookahead (future relations), and Global (full plan history plus all past feedback)—guided by exploitation, exploration, and three KG-specific bonus terms (entropy-based, repetition-aware, and diversity penalties). The selected strategy's revised plan is scored by a composite reward combining five task-specific metrics (validation, quality, question alignment, thought coherence, efficiency) with a confidence-based reward measuring answer stability across candidate plans, integrated via an entropy-aware weighting that shifts emphasis from local quality to global consensus as answer uncertainty increases.

## Results

VoG achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three KGQA benchmarks with GPT-4: 77.6% EM on CWQ, 88.7% EM on WebQSP, and 72.3% EM on WebQuestions, outperforming the strongest agent-based baselines ToG and PoG by up to 14.4 EM points. With GPT-3.5, VoG reaches 64.7/83.2/63.0 EM, and even with the smaller Qwen2.5-7B backbone it attains 53.3/67.3/52.8 EM, consistently surpassing all baselines. VoG also reduces average token consumption by 20–43% compared to ToG and PoG while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest agent-based competitor PoG, VoG wins on all three datasets and all three backbones: with GPT-4 it gains +2.6 EM on CWQ (77.6 vs 75.0), +1.4 on WebQSP (88.7 vs 87.3), and +0.6 on WebQuestions (72.3 vs 71.7); with GPT-3.5 the margins are +1.5/+1.2/+1.3 EM. Against ToG, VoG's advantages are substantially larger, reaching +10.0 EM on CWQ and +14.4 EM on WebQuestions under GPT-4. VoG also outperforms all fine-tuned methods (e.g., DECAF at 70.4 EM on CWQ, KG-Agent at 72.2 EM) without any fine-tuning, and surpasses the best fine-tuned retriever GNN-RAG+RA (90.7 EM on WebQSP) only narrowly on WebQSP but clearly on CWQ (77.6 vs 68.7). On the cross-KG generalization benchmark QALD-10 (Wikidata), VoG achieves 57.4% accuracy versus ToG's 50.2% and the fine-tuned SPARQL-QA's 45.4%. On biomedical datasets, VoG substantially outperforms ToG (45.3% vs 17.6% on PubMedQA; 93.5% vs 18.0% on BioASQ) while using roughly 8× fewer tokens than GIVE.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoG extends the LLM-agent paradigm for KG reasoning (ReAct, ToG, PoG) by introducing a closed-loop of stepwise verification and adaptive plan revision, departing from prior frameworks that either fix the reasoning plan at generation time (RoG, KG-Agent) or perform only end-of-chain verification (KD-CoT). The adaptive context selection mechanism repurposes the classical UCB bandit algorithm with KG-specific priors (entropy, entity repetition, strategy diversity) to dynamically choose among Local, Lookahead, and Global revision contexts — a formulation not previously applied to KG reasoning. Unlike PoG, which corrects local retrieval errors, VoG revises the entire reasoning trajectory and explicitly models the trade-off between local factual grounding and global plan coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VoG offers a training-free, model-agnostic recipe for reducing hallucination and error propagation in multi-hop KG reasoning, with the practical advantage of lower token consumption than competing agent baselines while achieving higher accuracy. The framework's reliance on a sufficiently complete and clean KG remains a key limitation, as real-world knowledge bases are often noisy or sparse, and the biomedical generalization experiments (small 200-question set, sparse-KG setting) only provide preliminary evidence of cross-domain transfer. Open questions include whether the MAB-based context selector scales to very deep reasoning chains, how performance degrades under significant KG incompleteness, and whether the entropy-based reward integration generalizes beyond entity-based QA to more open-ended generation tasks.
