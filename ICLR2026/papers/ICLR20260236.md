## Problem

Existing video social reasoning datasets are limited in complexity, scale, and coverage of mental states, lacking multi-step causal chains that connect observable physical events to internal mental states (belief, intent, desire, emotion). No large-scale video dataset models the full causal dynamics of real-life social interactions, and current SOTA LVLMs show substantial deficiencies in consistent multi-step social reasoning that single-question accuracy metrics fail to expose.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260236/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes video clips (≤180 s, avg 65.8 s) paired with a multiple-choice question (5 options) and optionally Whisper-generated subtitles; models receive either raw video or 16 uniformly sampled frames. Beyond standard accuracy, the evaluation introduces Chain Consistency and Subchain Consistency metrics that require a model to answer *all* questions derived from a single causal chain (or subchain) correctly, penalizing fragmented reasoning. The training set R³-FDT comprises 2,812 movie clips (~129 s avg) with 41k QA pairs spanning four question types (Event Understanding, Mental State Estimation, Causal-Why, Causal-How/What) organized under 1,406 annotated subchains.

## Method

The core structural contribution is the mental-physical causal chain: a directed graph whose nodes are events or mental states (belief, intent, desire, emotion) and whose edges encode causal relations, with each subchain comprising one result node and one or more necessary reason nodes. R³-Bench is built through a five-stage human pipeline (crowdsourced video collection → expert verification → causal chain annotation → GPT-4o QA generation → expert QA verification), while R³-FDT uses an automated pipeline that aligns movie scripts and annotations (MovieNet, MovieQA, CondensedMovies) with Whisper-recognized dialogue, prompts GPT-4o to infer causal chains from purely textual descriptions, applies self-correction and Gemini 2.5 Flash hallucination detection, and generates distractors grounded in the causal structure. For model improvement, Qwen2-VL-7B is fine-tuned on a 13k subset via both SFT (10% open-ended) and GRPO reinforcement learning with a rule-based reward (format + accuracy), yielding +13.84% on R³-Bench-DX and +7.91% on R³-Bench-Hard (SFT) while generalizing to Social-IQ 2.0 and IntentQA.

## Results

On R³-Bench-Hard (video+subtitle), the best SOTA model Gemini 2.5 Pro (Frames) achieves 59.18% overall accuracy, still far below the human level of 80.06%. GPT-4o reaches 48.73%, while most open-source models (Idefics2-8B, PLLaVA-7B, Video-LLaVA) score below 20%, near random chance (20%). Fine-tuning Qwen2-VL-7B on R³-FDT via SFT yields 42.09% (+10.13% over the base model's 31.96%), surpassing Gemini 1.5 Pro (Frames) at 39.24%. On R³-Bench-DX, the 'high accuracy, low consistency' paradox is stark: GPT-4o scores 82.64% overall accuracy but only 25.36% chain consistency, while Gemini 2.5 Pro reaches 86.34% accuracy but 43.63% consistency, versus 60.47% for humans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 11+ LVLMs on R³-Bench-Hard and R³-Bench-DX. On R³-Bench-Hard, Gemini 2.5 Pro (59.18%) leads all models but trails humans (80.06%) by over 20 points; GPT-4o (48.73%) is second, and Gemini 1.5 Pro (Frames) (39.24%) is third. The fine-tuned Qwen2-VL-7B (SFT, 42.09%) surpasses Gemini 1.5 Pro (Frames) by ~3 points but remains well below GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Pro. On R³-Bench-DX, the fine-tuned models outperform Gemini 2.5 Pro in overall accuracy (per the paper's claim), and SFT yields +13.84% improvement over the base Qwen2-VL-7B. On external benchmarks, RLFT improves Social-IQ 2.0 by +6.89% and IntentQA by +7.59%, while SFT gains +3.87% and +4.78% respectively, confirming transferability. A critical finding across all baselines is the consistency gap: even the best models (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro) show chain consistency scores (25–44%) far below their individual-question accuracy (82–86%), exposing fragmented rather than holistic social reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of video social reasoning benchmarks (Social-IQ, IntentQA, MMToM-QA) by introducing multi-step mental-physical causal chains as the structural backbone for both evaluation and training. Prior work either evaluates social reasoning in text-only settings (Hi-ToM, SimpleToM, BigToM) or in video settings that target isolated mental states without modeling their causal interdependencies over time. The training pipeline repurposes existing movie datasets (MovieNet, MovieQA, CondensedMovies) and aligns them with Whisper-recognized dialogue to generate causal-chain-structured QA at scale, departing from the small, manually curated training sets (1k–6k samples) that have constrained prior social reasoning models. The base model Qwen2-VL-7B is fine-tuned via both SFT and GRPO, building on the standard LVLM training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that SOTA LVLMs achieve high per-question accuracy (82–86%) yet collapse to 25–37% chain consistency—reveals a hidden reasoning deficit that standard accuracy metrics mask, suggesting models may be exploiting heuristics rather than performing genuine multi-step causal inference. The R³-Bench (5.1k QA pairs with fine-grained causal annotations) and R³-FDT (41k QA pairs across 2.8k videos) together provide the first large-scale, causally structured resources for training and evaluating social reasoning in video. Open questions include whether the consistency metrics generalize beyond the specific causal-chain structure used here, whether the movie-domain training data transfers to open-world video, and whether the identified weaknesses (contradiction detection, pragmatic inference) can be addressed through architectural changes rather than data scaling alone.
