## Problem

Despite extensive scaling and chain-of-thought supervision, current models still fail to perform reasoning reliably, and existing approaches do not explain why reasoning is intrinsically difficult for machines. This paper identifies a structural gap: prior work treats reasoning as pattern matching or surface-level imitation of human explanations, without modeling the underlying latent structure of logical inference. The authors argue that reasoning difficulty stems from two underexplored properties — the latent space vastly exceeding the observation space, and dense interdependencies among latent variables — and that these require explicit architectural treatment rather than more parameters or data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single structured observation (a partially filled 9×9 Sudoku grid, a 30×30 maze layout, or a small grid-transformation example) and produces the corresponding solution (completed grid entries, optimal path, or transformed output grid). Critically, the input is injected only during the first M iterations of the recurrent loop (the reflection phase) and is then completely dropped; the remaining M×(N−1) iterations operate solely on the internal latent state. Outputs are read out through a prediction head at periodic alignment intervals (every M steps), with the final prediction taken at the last alignment point.

## Method

SR2 formulates reasoning as a causal selection mechanism where high-level logical rules act as constraints on observed inputs, then instantiates this as a flattened recurrent Transformer with a single shared block (3.4M parameters) unrolled over T = M×N iterations. The first M iterations perform reflective representation learning, iteratively updating the latent state via z^(t+1) = f(z^(t), x) to contract toward a constraint-satisfying representation. The subsequent M×(N−1) iterations perform dependency self-refinement by dropping the observation entirely (z^(t+1) = f(z^(t), 0)), forcing the model to resolve dense latent interdependencies autonomously. Periodic alignment injects supervision at every M-step boundary with gradient detachment to prevent vanishing gradients over the long recurrence. A convergence theorem (via Banach fixed-point) guarantees that under contraction assumptions, both phases converge to unique equilibria, and ablations confirm that each of the three modules is necessary for peak performance.

## Results

On Sudoku-Extreme, SR² achieves 66.63% pass@1 accuracy with only 3.4M parameters, surpassing HRM (55.0%, 27.3M params) by 11.6 percentage points. On Maze-Hard, SR² reaches 93.7% accuracy versus HRM's 74.5%, a 19.2-point gain while using 8× fewer parameters. On ARC-AGI, SR² attains 44.3% pass@2 on ARC-1 and 6.7% on ARC-2, outperforming HRM (40.3% and 5.0% respectively). The standard Transformer baseline fails entirely on these reasoning tasks (1.17% on Sudoku, 0% on Maze), underscoring the necessity of iterative latent refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SR² decisively outperforms all four baselines on every benchmark where they are directly compared. Against the standard Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017), SR² improves Sudoku-Extreme accuracy from 1.17% to 66.63% and Maze-Hard from 0% to 93.7%, demonstrating that a single forward pass is fundamentally insufficient for constraint-satisfaction reasoning. Compared to the Block Universal Transformer (Dehghani et al., 2018), which reuses a single block without input injection, SR² gains 66.63 points on Sudoku and 63.3 points on Maze, showing that reflective input injection is critical. Against Recurrent Depth (Geiping et al., 2025), which injects both input and latent state at every step, SR² achieves a 24.1-point gain on Sudoku (66.63% vs. 42.52%) and nearly doubles Maze accuracy (93.7% vs. 48.4%), highlighting the importance of the dedicated dependency self-refinement phase that removes the observation signal. Most notably, SR² surpasses the recent HRM (Wang et al., 2025) by 11.6 points on Sudoku-Extreme and 19.2 points on Maze-Hard while using only 1/8 of its parameters (3.4M vs. 27.3M), and also leads on both ARC-AGI splits (44.3% vs. 40.3% on ARC-1; 6.7% vs. 5.0% on ARC-2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the selection mechanism concept from causal inference (Pearl; Zheng et al., 2024) to provide a principled theoretical framing for reasoning tasks, arguing that high-level logical rules act as selection operators constraining observed inputs. Architecturally, it builds on the recurrent latent reasoning line—Universal Transformers, Recurrent Depth, and HRM—by combining a flattened single-layer recurrent Transformer (inspired by Deep Equilibrium Models) with two distinct iterative phases: a reflective phase that injects the observation, and a self-refinement phase that evolves the latent state autonomously. Unlike verbal self-correction methods (Reflexion, Self-Refine, STaR) that operate over token sequences, SR2 performs all correction implicitly through latent-space fixed-point iterations, and unlike HRM's hierarchical high/low-level split, it uses a single shared function for both phases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal selection-mechanism framing offers a concrete theoretical explanation for why reasoning is hard (latent space vastly exceeds observation space; latent variables are densely interdependent), which in turn motivates each architectural component rather than relying on ad hoc design. The 8× parameter efficiency with >10% accuracy gains over HRM on Sudoku and Maze is practically useful for resource-constrained settings. However, the evaluation is limited to tasks with well-defined logical constraints (Sudoku, Maze, ARC-AGI), and the authors acknowledge the selection-mechanism assumption may oversimplify richer forms of human reasoning; whether the approach transfers to open-domain or compositional generalization tasks remains an open question.
