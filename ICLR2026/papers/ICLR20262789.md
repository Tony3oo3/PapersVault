## Problem

Off-policy RL for LLMs under large rollout staleness (≥256 model updates) faces a fundamental trade-off: standard ε-clipping (GRPO) disproportionately masks high-entropy informative tokens, causing performance degradation, while removing the trust region entirely leads to training collapse. The paper uncovers a 'prosperity-before-collapse' phenomenon showing stale data is as informative as on-policy data, but no existing algorithm can exploit this information while maintaining stability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes stale rollouts (trajectories generated k model updates earlier, where each training step comprises 4 model updates), their associated prompts and rewards, and the current policy parameters. It produces updated policy weights via a masked policy gradient objective. A single batch-level threshold τ_M2 = 0.04 governs the masking and is shown to be insensitive to its value across all model scales (1.7B–32B) and staleness levels, requiring no per-setting tuning.

## Method

M2PO replaces PPO/GRPO's per-token ε-clipping with an adaptive batch-level masking strategy based on the second moment of log importance ratios (M2 = mean of (log r_i)²). Rather than clipping individual tokens whose ratios exceed a fixed [1−ε, 1+ε] band, M2PO sorts tokens by their per-token M2 values and iteratively masks the largest until the batch-level M2 of remaining trust-region tokens falls below τ_M2. This design is variance-sensitive (capturing instability from high-entropy tokens) and statistically stable (each per-token M2 estimate is non-negative, avoiding the cancellation issues of batch KL). Theorem 1 proves M2 upper-bounds the Pearson chi-square divergence between new and behavior policies, providing a principled trust-region guarantee. The result is a dramatic reduction in clipped tokens (from 1.22% to 0.06% under s=256 on Qwen-2.5-32B) while preserving the majority of informative gradient signal, enabling stable off-policy training that matches on-policy performance.

## Results

Across six model scales (1.7B–32B) and eight math reasoning benchmarks, M2PO with staleness s=256 achieves average accuracy on par with or exceeding on-policy GRPO (s=0) in every setting, e.g., 55.1% vs. 53.6% on Qwen3-Base-8B and 52.6% vs. 51.6% on Qwen2.5-32B. Compared to GRPO under the same staleness (s=256), M2PO improves average accuracy by up to 11.2% (36.6% vs. 30.4% on Qwen3-Base-1.7B). M2PO also reduces the average token clipping ratio by over an order of magnitude (from 1.22% to 0.06% on Qwen2.5-32B), and a single threshold τ_{M2}=0.04 works across all models and staleness levels without retuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against on-policy GRPO (s=0), M2PO with s=256 matches or exceeds the on-policy baseline in 5 of 6 model settings (e.g., 55.1% vs. 53.6% on Qwen3-Base-8B; 36.6% vs. 33.0% on Qwen3-Base-1.7B), and is within 0.5% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (48.8% vs. 49.3%). Against off-policy GRPO (s=256), M2PO improves average accuracy by 3.1–6.2 points across all models (e.g., 52.6% vs. 47.0% on Qwen2.5-32B; 36.6% vs. 30.4% on Qwen3-Base-1.7B). GSPO (s=256), the only other baseline that maintains training stability, is consistently outperformed by M2PO by 1.1–6.5 points in average accuracy. AREAL, TOPR, GPPO, and CISPO all suffer training collapse under s=256 (Figure 6a), making them non-competitive in this extreme-staleness regime. On the coding task (LiveCodeBench), M2PO with s=256 also outperforms GRPO at the same staleness and matches on-policy GRPO, confirming generality beyond math.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO/PPO trust-region framework for LLM RL by replacing fixed ε-clipping with an adaptive second-moment constraint on importance ratios. It builds directly on the line of trust-region strategies (GSPO's sequence-level clipping, AREAL's approximate policy, GPPO's gradient-preserving clipping, CISPO) but departs from all of them by operating at the batch level with a variance-sensitive statistic rather than per-token or per-sequence fixed bounds. The key conceptual departure is the empirical discovery that ε-clipping disproportionately masks high-entropy tokens under staleness, reframing the trust-region problem as one of selective outlier suppression rather than uniform ratio bounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M2PO's ability to match on-policy performance under 256-update staleness with a single insensitive threshold (τ=0.04) makes it a practical building block for asynchronous RL systems targeting complex, high-latency tasks (agentic coding, multi-step tool use) where on-policy training is infeasible. The prosperity-before-collapse finding reframes how the community should think about stale data: it is not inherently degraded but underutilized by current algorithms. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even longer-horizon agentic settings, whether the second-moment constraint interacts well with other off-policy corrections (e.g., TIS for engine mismatch) in production systems, and whether the single-threshold robustness holds under different reward structures beyond verifiable math/coding tasks.
