## Problem

Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) remains highly susceptible to illumination changes, motion artifacts, and limited temporal modeling, while Large Language Models—despite excelling at long-range dependency capture—struggle with the continuous, noise-sensitive nature of rPPG signals due to their discrete, text-centric architecture. No prior work has effectively bridged the representational gap between physiological signal processing and LLM-based reasoning for non-contact physiological measurement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263177/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 128-frame RGB facial video clips (resized to 128×128 pixels at 30 fps) along with three automatically generated text cue streams: visual scene descriptions produced by LLaVA from a central frame, a fixed task-description preamble, and statistical descriptors (min, max, median, trend, sign of cumulative difference, top-K spectral peaks) computed from the backbone's rPPG output. The model outputs a continuous rPPG waveform predicted via an LLM decoder, from which heart rate (bpm) and respiratory rate are derived; training uses MSE loss between predicted and ground-truth waveforms.

## Method

PhysLLM integrates a frozen PhysNet backbone with three trainable enhancement modules and a DeepSeek-1.5B LLM. The Dual-Domain Stationary (DDS) Algorithm stabilizes the raw rPPG signal by combining exponential-decay temporal smoothing with discrete wavelet transform (3-level) frequency-domain re-weighting, fused via a learnable parameter β. A Vision Aggregator fuses multi-scale backbone features using cross-attention (deep features as queries over shallow features) followed by self-attention. The key cross-modal bridge is Text Prototype Guidance (TPG), which projects both signal and visual features into LLM token space via a small set of learnable text prototypes (V′ ≪ V) processed through stacked self-attention and cross-attention blocks, avoiding the need to reprogram the full vocabulary. Three cue types (LLaVA-generated visual descriptions, task priors, and backbone-derived statistics) are compressed by modality-specific attention compressors and fused through adaptive learnable prompt tokens, enabling the LLM to condition its waveform prediction on scene-specific physiological context.

## Results

On the four benchmark rPPG datasets, PhysLLM achieves state-of-the-art heart rate estimation: MAE 0.21 bpm / RMSE 0.57 bpm on UBFC-rPPG, MAE 0.17 bpm / RMSE 0.35 bpm on PURE, MAE 6.48 bpm / RMSE 8.48 bpm on BUAA, and MAE 4.36 bpm / RMSE 10.76 bpm on MMPD. In cross-domain generalization (three-source training → single-target testing), PhysLLM achieves MAE 6.01 / RMSE 8.6 bpm on Others→BUAA and MAE 12.2 / RMSE 15.5 bpm on Others→MMPD, outperforming all baselines. The model also demonstrates superior robustness across skin tones (Types 3–6) and lighting conditions (LED, incandescent, natural) on MMPD, with the best MAE of 3.45 bpm under natural light.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysLLM outperforms all 14 baselines across the four datasets. On UBFC-rPPG, it ties Contrast-Phys+ on MAE (0.21 bpm) but reduces RMSE from 0.80 to 0.57 bpm, and beats RhythmFormer by 0.29 bpm MAE and 0.21 bpm RMSE. On PURE, PhysLLM achieves the best MAE (0.17 bpm), surpassing RhythmFormer (0.27) and Contrast-Phys+ (0.48) by 0.10 and 0.31 bpm respectively. On the more challenging BUAA dataset (low-light), PhysLLM reduces MAE from 6.89 (Green) and 8.46 (PhysFormer) to 6.48 bpm, and RMSE from 10.39 to 8.48 bpm. On MMPD (real-world, diverse skin tones), PhysLLM achieves MAE 4.36 bpm, beating RhythmFormer (4.69) and PhysNet (4.80), while dramatically outperforming PhysFormer (11.99) and EfficientPhys (13.47). In cross-domain settings (Tables 2–3), PhysLLM consistently achieves the lowest MAE/RMSE, e.g., 9.95/14.96 bpm on P+U→MMPD versus 10.5/16.7 for RhythmFormer, confirming superior generalization enabled by the LLM's cross-modal reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysLLM builds directly on PhysNet (Yu et al., 2019) as a fixed CNN backbone for rPPG signal and multi-scale feature extraction, and adapts the time-series reprogramming paradigm from Time-LLM (Jin et al., 2023) to align continuous physiological signals with LLM token spaces via the Text Prototype Guidance module. It departs from prior rPPG work (PhysFormer, RhythmFormer, EfficientPhys) by introducing an LLM as the central reasoning engine rather than a dedicated temporal transformer, and from prior LLM-for-time-series work by incorporating rPPG-specific visual features, wavelet-based signal stabilization (DDS), and automated cue generation via LLaVA. The framework positions itself as the first to establish interpretable cross-modal connections between physiological dynamics and contextual semantics in remote sensing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that LLM pre-trained knowledge (not merely transformer architecture) is essential for cross-domain generalization in rPPG, as shown by the significant performance drop when replacing DeepSeek with the non-LLM Sundial transformer. The strong cross-dataset results (e.g., 6.01 bpm MAE on Others→BUAA) suggest that multi-modal contextual reasoning can mitigate domain shift, a persistent challenge in rPPG. However, the 97.2M parameter / 424.3G MACs footprint is an order of magnitude above competing methods, raising open questions about edge deployment and whether knowledge distillation or parameter-efficient fine-tuning can preserve the cross-modal gains. The fixed PhysNet backbone also limits how much the framework benefits from advances in rPPG-specific encoders.
