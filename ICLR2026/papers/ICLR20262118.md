## Problem

Post-Training Quantization for video matting models remains largely unexplored, with existing PTQ methods (MSE, BRECQ, QDrop) suffering from unstable convergence on deep recurrent architectures, error accumulation that degrades temporal coherence (flickering/jitter), and catastrophic accuracy collapse at 4-bit precision. This paper presents the first systematic PTQ framework tailored to video matting, addressing both spatial accuracy loss and temporal inconsistency simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262118/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB video frames at 512×288 (VM dataset) or 512×512 (D646 dataset) and produces per-pixel alpha mattes in [0,1] for each frame. Calibration requires only 256 images (64 clips × 4 frames) from the VM dataset, with no retraining of the pre-trained model. The OFA component additionally consumes pre-computed RAFT optical flow fields between consecutive calibration frames to warp the previous frame's predicted matte into the current frame's coordinate system as a temporal prior.

## Method

PTQ4VM is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (BIQ) partitions the network into functionally closed blocks via dependency-aware topological partitioning, then sequentially optimizes per-block rounding and activation scales to minimize MSE between quantized and full-precision block outputs, achieving fast convergence while capturing local dependencies. Stage 2 introduces two complementary mechanisms: (a) Global Affine Calibration (GAC) learns per-layer scaling γ_i and shift β_i parameters applied to the initially quantized folded weights, plus per-layer activation scale factors, all jointly optimized end-to-end to minimize MSE between predicted and ground-truth alpha mattes—this compensates for cumulative statistical distortions caused by BN folding and quantization error propagation; (b) Optical Flow Assistance (OFA) warps the previous frame's predicted alpha matte using RAFT flow to create a motion-compensated prior, adding an L1 alignment loss that regularizes the calibration toward temporally coherent predictions. The entire pipeline requires no retraining and achieves near-FP32 quality at 4-bit with 8× FLOP savings.

## Results

On the VM video matting dataset, PTQ4VM at W8A8 achieves MAD 6.03, matching or slightly surpassing the FP32 RVM baseline (MAD 6.08) while reducing FLOPs from 4.57G to 1.14G (4× savings). At the challenging W4A4 setting, PTQ4VM achieves MAD 20.81 and MSE 11.17, reducing error by ~15% over QDrop (MAD 24.36) and by >87% over BRECQ (MAD 168.34). On the unseen D646 image matting dataset, the method similarly leads all PTQ baselines at every bit-width, confirming strong generalization. The 4-bit model enjoys 8× FLOP savings (0.57G vs 4.57G) while retaining near-full-precision temporal coherence (DTSSD 3.77 vs 1.36 for FP32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the FP32 RVM baseline, PTQ4VM at W8A8 matches full-precision quality (MAD 6.03 vs 6.08, Conn 0.41 vs 0.41) while cutting FLOPs by 4×, and at W4A4 retains usable matting quality (MAD 20.81) where the gap to FP32 is the expected degradation. Against BRECQ, the improvement is dramatic at low bits: at W4A4 on VM, PTQ4VM reduces MAD from 168.34 to 20.81 (an 87.6% reduction), and at W4A8 from 28.67 to 10.61 (63% reduction), indicating BRECQ's block reconstruction alone cannot handle cumulative error in recurrent video matting architectures. Against QDrop, the closest PTQ competitor, PTQ4VM consistently wins across all bit-widths: at W4A4 on VM, MAD improves from 24.36 to 20.81 (~15%), MSE from 18.02 to 11.17 (~38%), and DTSSD from 4.70 to 3.77 (~20%), with similar margins on D646. Against the naive MSE calibration, the advantage is overwhelming at 4-bit (MAD 20.81 vs 189.21), confirming that simple per-layer calibration is entirely inadequate for this task. On the generality validation (Table 3), PTQ4VM outperforms all PTQ baselines on both MODNet (pure CNN) and MatAnyone (Transformer), e.g., at W4A4 on MatAnyone achieving MAD 13.80 vs QDrop's 20.91 and BRECQ's 169.35, confirming the framework is architecture-agnostic. The FP32 references (DeepLabV3, BGMv2, MODNet) serve as upper-bound quality anchors rather than direct PTQ competitors, with RVM being the most efficient FP32 model and thus the natural quantization target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: post-training quantization (PTQ) for neural networks and video matting. It builds directly on general PTQ methods—BRECQ for block-wise reconstruction and QDrop for quantization-noise robustness—but is the first to systematically adapt and extend these for the specific challenges of video matting models (recurrent temporal structures, limited calibration data, temporal coherence requirements). The GAC component departs from prior PTQ practice by explicitly modeling the statistical distortion introduced by BN folding and non-linear reshaping of accumulated quantization error, while the OFA component repurposes optical flow (RAFT) as a calibration-time regularizer, exploiting the low-data nature of PTQ to make a computationally expensive prior feasible where it would not be during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a clear gap: video matting models like RVM are already efficient but still too heavy for many edge deployments, and no prior work had systematically addressed PTQ for this task. The GAC mechanism is architecturally agnostic and could benefit PTQ pipelines for other tasks where BN folding and non-linear accumulation cause distributional shifts. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 2-bit or mixed-precision settings, whether the OFA component generalizes beyond matting to other video understanding tasks, and whether the small 256-image calibration set is sufficient for more diverse or longer video sequences. The practical impact is significant for real-time video processing on mobile and embedded platforms.
