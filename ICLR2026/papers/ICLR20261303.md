## Problem

Cyclic learning methods (e.g., CycleGAN, cycle-consistent tracking) are designed as task-specific loss functions with no shared theoretical foundation, making cross-domain generalization difficult and leaving the role of each loss component (cycle consistency, adversarial/similarity) unexplained. Moreover, many approaches still depend on pseudo-labels or base trackers, limiting their applicability. This paper asks: can all cyclic learning tasks be unified under a single probabilistic framework that yields principled, task-agnostic training objectives?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261303/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For paired image translation, the method consumes two sets of unpaired images (domains X and Y, e.g., Cityscapes labels and photos) and produces bidirectional translations without any paired supervision or GAN discriminators. For self-cyclic visual tracking, the input is a full template frame with a random object bounding box and a full search frame (no cropping), and the output is a confidence-weighted bounding box in the search frame; detector-generated boxes (DETA) serve as the reference for the similarity term. The self-cyclic formulation conditions the same network g_θ on domain indicators (X, Y) to distinguish forward from backward mapping, avoiding the trivial identity solution.

## Method

The paper reformulates cycle consistency as variational inference by treating intermediate data points as latent variables and cross-domain mappings as conditional probability distributions, deriving an ELBO whose maximization decomposes into a cycle-consistency term (D_cyc) and a domain-similarity term (D_sim). Two optimization strategies follow: a VAE-style single-step loss (Eq. 12) that jointly trains both mappings end-to-end, and an EM-based alternating scheme (Algorithms 1–2) that fixes one mapping to generate pseudo-labels (E-step) then updates the other via cycle loss (M-step), eliminating the need for explicit KL divergence estimation. The framework unifies paired tasks (two networks f_φ, g_θ) and self-cyclic tasks (one network g_θ with domain conditioning) under the same ELBO. Applied to image translation, this yields CycleGN—a GAN-free EM-based alternative that matches CycleGAN's performance—and provides a theoretical decomposition showing CycleGAN's adversarial loss is a JS-divergence surrogate for D_sim. Applied to tracking, it produces CycleTrack (single-step) and CycleTrack-EM (EM-based), a ViT + FCOS tracker trained purely on forward-backward trajectory consistency without pseudo-labels, achieving state-of-the-art unsupervised results on LaSOT and TrackingNet.

## Results

On unsupervised visual tracking (detector-annotated), CycleTrack-EM achieves 56.5 AUC and 57.9 Precision on LaSOT, and 77.3 AUC and 74.4 Precision on TrackingNet, surpassing the best prior method (ULAST*-on) by 9.4 and 11.9 AUC points respectively. The single-step variant CycleTrack also leads all baselines with 51.0 AUC on LaSOT and 75.9 AUC on TrackingNet. On unpaired image translation (Cityscapes labels→photo), CycleGN matches CycleGAN's per-pixel accuracy (0.52) without any adversarial discriminator, while trailing slightly on per-class accuracy (0.14 vs. 0.17) and class IoU (0.10 vs. 0.11).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In unsupervised tracking, both CycleTrack and CycleTrack-EM decisively outperform all four baselines (ResPUL, LUDT+, USOT, ULAST) on every metric across LaSOT and TrackingNet; CycleTrack-EM leads ULAST*-on by 9.4 AUC on LaSOT and 11.9 AUC on TrackingNet, with similar margins in Precision. In strictly unsupervised settings (optical-flow annotations, Table 6), CycleTrack-EM again leads with 51.2 AUC on LaSOT and 69.1 AUC on TrackingNet, beating ULAST-on by 7.9 and 6.8 AUC respectively. For image translation on Cityscapes, CycleGN (GAN-free, EM-based) ties CycleGAN on per-pixel accuracy (0.52) but falls slightly behind on per-class accuracy (0.14 vs. 0.17) and class IoU (0.10 vs. 0.11); it clearly outperforms CoGAN (0.40), BiGAN/ALI (0.19), SimGAN (0.20), and feature-loss+GAN (0.06) on per-pixel accuracy. In the fully-supervised tracking comparison (Table 8), CycleTrack reaches 70.1 AUC on LaSOT, marginally exceeding GRM (69.9) and Mixformer-22k (69.2), while semi-supervised CycleTrack-EM achieves 63.6 AUC with only first-frame annotations, surpassing fully-supervised DiMP (56.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the previously fragmented landscape of cycle-consistent learning (CycleGAN for image translation, CyCO/SC-Tune for referring expressions, temporal cycle-consistency for video) under a single variational inference framework, treating intermediate data points as latent variables and reformulating cycle-consistency as ELBO maximization. It draws directly on the EM algorithm's E-step/M-step structure and the VAE's reconstruction-plus-KL decomposition, showing that CycleGAN's adversarial loss is a specific instantiation of the KL divergence term in the general formulation. The key departure from prior work is the extension from paired cyclic tasks (A→B, B→A with separate networks) to self-cyclic tasks (A→B, B→A with the same network), enabling application to unsupervised tracking where no second domain exists.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled theoretical justification for why cycle-consistency losses work, replacing ad-hoc task-specific designs with a general probabilistic recipe. The practical implication is that practitioners can choose between single-step (VAE-style) and EM-based training based on whether a reliable D_sim/KL surrogate is available, rather than defaulting to GANs. Key open questions remain: the EM method's susceptibility to converging to unintended domain pairs (X↔A, B↔Y instead of X↔Y), the difficulty of defining D_sim in non-image modalities, and whether the learned mapping is guaranteed to be the semantically intended one. The tracking results (56.5 AUC on LaSOT, 77.3 on TrackingNet) are strong SOTA, but the image translation results show CycleGN still trails CycleGAN slightly, suggesting the GAN discriminator remains a superior KL estimator in practice.
