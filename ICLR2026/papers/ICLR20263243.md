## Problem

Shrinking long protein sequences while preserving function is costly and typically requires extensive experimental campaigns. Existing generative models of natural protein sequences struggle to search the combinatorial space of all possible deletions and lack inductive biases for learning how to delete, while prior diffusion frameworks are limited to substitution mutations and cannot propose deletions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263243/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For protein shrinking, SCISOR takes a natural protein sequence X of length L and a target number of deletions M, then iteratively plans M deletions to produce a shorter subsequence. For mutation effect prediction, it outputs a deletion probability score q_θ(X^(-i) | X, M=1) for every residue position, with multi-deletion effects computed by summing over all deletion orderings. For unconditional generation, it takes only a desired length L and produces a full protein sequence by starting from a long random sequence and iteratively deleting. The de-noiser conditions on M via FiLM layers, allowing it to plan different deletion strategies depending on how many deletions remain.

## Method

SCISOR defines a novel insertion-only forward noising process (a pure birth process) that adds random amino acids to natural sequences, and trains a de-noiser to reverse it by planning deletions. The key theoretical contribution is proving that a formal stationary distribution is unnecessary (Theorem 4.1): as insertions grow, the noised sequence is well-approximated by a long random sequence, enabling a closed-form ELBO. The training objective is a schedule-conditioned loss (Theorem 4.2) whose gradient is Rao-Blackwellized by integrating over all insertion paths, computed efficiently via sequence alignment counts (Proposition 4.3). The de-noiser architecture fine-tunes ESM2 with FiLM conditioning layers on M, and long sequences are handled via random windowing with re-normalized predictions to maintain a valid ELBO lower bound.

## Results

On ProteinGym, SCISOR L achieves state-of-the-art Spearman correlations of 0.573 (single deletions) and 0.492 (multiple deletions), outperforming all baselines including PoET (0.551/0.488), Tranception (0.439/0.475), and ProGen2 (0.513/0.385). For protein shrinking on 200 UniProt sequences, SCISOR consistently produces shrunken proteins with higher pLDDT, better TM-score/RMSD structural preservation, and greater enrichment of annotated functional sites compared to Raygun and ProGen2 baselines across all shrinkage levels (1–50%). As a generative model, SCISOR achieves competitive perplexity with EvoDiff and DPLM on UniRef50, and its samples score comparably to autoregressive models on Fréchet protein distance and foldability (pLDDT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCISOR L outperforms all baselines on ProteinGym deletion effect prediction: it beats PoET (the strongest MSA-based baseline) by 0.022 on single deletions (0.573 vs. 0.551) and by 0.004 on multiple deletions (0.492 vs. 0.488), and surpasses Tranception by 0.134 on single deletions. On the protein shrinking task (Fig. 6), SCISOR consistently outperforms both Raygun and ProGen2 across all shrinkage levels (1–50%) on pLDDT, TM-score, RMSD, and functional site preservation; ProGen2 achieves slightly higher functional site preservation at 50% shrinkage but at the cost of substantially lower pLDDT, suggesting its samples are unlikely to fold. For generative model fit (Fig. 4), SCISOR is competitive with EvoDiff and DPLM in perplexity and produces samples with FPD and pLDDT scores comparable to autoregressive models, though it does not clearly dominate on these metrics. A more expensive ProGen2 baseline requiring O(L·M) forward passes (Fig. 9) still does not close the gap with SCISOR on shrinking quality. SCISOR M already achieves 0.520 on multiple deletions, exceeding all baselines except SCISOR L, indicating strong scaling behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCISOR extends discrete diffusion models (Campbell et al., 2022; Austin et al., 2021) by replacing the standard substitution-only noising with a pure birth process that adds random insertions, so the learned reverse process performs deletions. This is conceptually the inverse of TDDM (Campbell et al., 2023), which uses deletion-based noising to learn sequence expansion, and differs from Johnson et al. (2021) by operating in continuous time with a closed-form ELBO. The training objective builds on the schedule-conditioned loss from Amin et al. (2025) and introduces a Rao-Blackwellized gradient estimator computed via sequence alignments (Prop. 4.3), while the architecture fine-tunes ESM2 with FiLM conditioning on the deletion count. Among protein-specific methods, SCISOR departs from Raygun (Devkota et al., 2024), which shrinks proteins by decoding autoencoder embeddings at shorter lengths without enforcing substring similarity or learning deletion dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Protein shrinking is a practical bottleneck in bioengineering and drug delivery, and SCISOR provides the first diffusion-based method with an explicit inductive bias for planning deletions, achieving state-of-the-art deletion-effect prediction on ProteinGym (outperforming even MSA-informed models like PoET) and producing shrunken proteins that better preserve foldability and functional motifs than Raygun or ProGen2. Key limitations include a distribution shift between training on noisy sequences and inference on natural proteins (partially addressed by a toy experiment), the restriction to deletions only (no compensatory substitutions), and the absence of explicit functional guidance. Open directions include more realistic forward processes, classifier-guided sampling for specific functions, and extending the framework to joint substitution-insertion-deletion planning.
