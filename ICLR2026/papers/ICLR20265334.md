## Problem

2D Vision-Language Models struggle with 3D spatial understanding because they lack a global-local correspondence between the overall scene layout and individual video frames. Prior work addressed this by feeding 3D point clouds into LLMs, but aligning point-cloud, image, and text modalities remains complex. This paper asks whether a purely vision-based prompting strategy—inspired by how humans perceive 3D from visual cues alone—can close the gap without modifying VLM architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265334/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives 32 uniformly sampled egocentric video frames (512×490) concatenated with a single top-down BEV image, both annotated with consistent Spatial-Temporal Object (STO) markers that carry the same object IDs across views. The BEV is rendered from a BundleFusion point-cloud reconstruction, and markers are projected from Mask3D instance segmentation. After fine-tuning on ScanAlign, the model retains strong 3D understanding even when fed raw unannotated video frames without the BEV image or markers, producing free-form QA answers, per-object dense captions, and bounding-box localizations for grounding queries.

## Method

GPT4Scene introduces a visual prompting paradigm with two coupled components: (1) a Bird's-Eye View image rendered from a 3D reconstruction to supply holistic scene layout, and (2) Spatial-Temporal Object markers that assign consistent IDs to each segmented object across all sampled frames and the BEV, bridging local per-frame observations with global spatial context. The marked frames and BEV are concatenated as a single visual input to a standard VLM (no architectural changes). For smaller open-source models, the authors construct ScanAlign—165K instruction-tuning pairs reformatted from five ScanNet-based benchmarks (ScanQA, SQA3D, Scan2Cap, ScanRefer, Multi3DRef) into the (marked video, marked BEV, text) format—and fine-tune Qwen2-VL-7B / Qwen2.5-VL-8B / InternVL3-8B for one epoch. A key empirical finding is that models trained under this paradigm develop an intrinsic 3D spatial representation: at inference they continue to outperform baselines even when the explicit BEV image and STO-markers are removed, indicating the training signal internalizes the global-local mapping rather than merely relying on the markers as a crutch.

## Results

On ScanQA, Qwen2.5-VL-7B (GPT4Scene) achieves BLEU-1 of 49.2 and CIDEr of 105.7, while on SQA3D it reaches EM-1 of 63.5 and EM-R1 of 66.2, surpassing the previous SOTA Chat-scene (BLEU-1 43.2, CIDEr 87.7, EM-1 54.6) by substantial margins. On 3D visual grounding, Qwen2.5-VL-7B (GPT4Scene) attains Acc@0.25 of 65.6 on ScanRefer and all F1@0.25 of 67.3 on Multi3DRef, outperforming Chat-scene by 10.1 and 10.2 points respectively. On Scan2Cap dense captioning, the model scores BLEU-4 of 45.9 and ROUGE of 67.9 at IoU@0.25, setting a new SOTA. In zero-shot settings, GPT4Scene improves GPT-4o's ScanQA ROUGE from 34.2 to 39.3 (+5.1) and SQA3D EM-1 from 42.0 to 44.8 (+2.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT4Scene-finetuned models comprehensively outperform the previous SOTA Chat-scene across all three task categories: on ScanQA, Qwen2.5-VL-7B (GPT4Scene) improves CIDEr from 87.7 to 105.7 (+18.0) and BLEU-1 from 43.2 to 49.2 (+6.0); on SQA3D, EM-1 rises from 54.6 to 63.5 (+8.9); on ScanRefer grounding, Acc@0.25 jumps from 55.5 to 65.6 (+10.1); and on Multi3DRef, all F1@0.25 increases from 57.1 to 67.3 (+10.2). Against ROSS3D (the strongest 3D LLM baseline), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (GPT4Scene) matches BLEU-1 (49.2) on ScanQA and slightly trails on CIDEr (105.7 vs. 107.0), but wins on SQA3D EM-1 (63.5 vs. 63.0) and dominates on grounding (Acc@0.25: 65.6 vs. 61.1; Multi3DRef F1@0.25: 67.3 vs. 59.6). Compared to other vision-LLM baselines, GPT4Scene surpasses Video-3D-LLM on ScanQA CIDEr (105.7 vs. 102.1) and SQA3D EM-1 (63.5 vs. 58.6), and outperforms LLaVA-3D on all reported metrics. In zero-shot evaluation, GPT4Scene's prompting paradigm boosts GPT-4o by +5.1 ROUGE on ScanQA and +2.8 EM-1 on SQA3D, while smaller models like Qwen2-VL-2B show negligible or negative gains, indicating that the paradigm's benefits scale with model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPT4Scene departs from the dominant 3D point cloud LLM paradigm (3D-LLM, Chat-scene, LEO, LL3DA) by eliminating the need for point cloud inputs entirely, instead relying on a visual prompting strategy that concatenates a reconstructed Bird's-Eye View image with video frames annotated by Spatial-Temporal Object markers. It repurposes existing off-the-shelf VLMs (Qwen2-VL, InternVL, GPT-4o) without architectural modification, positioning itself as a training/inference paradigm rather than a new model. The ScanAlign dataset reformats annotations from established 3D benchmarks (ScanQA, SQA3D, Scan2Cap, ScanRefer, Multi3DRef) into a VLM-compatible visual format, bridging the gap between geometric 3D annotations and 2D visual inputs. Conceptually, it extends the object-identifier idea from Chat-scene/Chat-3D v2 from a textual token mechanism to a purely visual cross-view correspondence mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pure visual inputs (video + BEV) can match or exceed point-cloud-based methods across all major 3D scene understanding tasks, suggesting that 3D spatial reasoning may not require explicit geometric representations if the right visual context is provided. A notable open question is whether the STO-markers can be generated from video-only segmentation (as the authors acknowledge in the conclusion) rather than relying on ground-truth point cloud annotations, which would make the pipeline fully self-contained. The finding that fine-tuning with the GPT4Scene paradigm imparts residual 3D understanding even without BEV/markers at inference hints at a transferable spatial reasoning capability, though this is validated on a single model and benchmark. Limitations include dependence on 3D reconstruction quality (albeit robustly), small-object performance degradation, and evaluation restricted to ScanNet-based indoor scenes.
