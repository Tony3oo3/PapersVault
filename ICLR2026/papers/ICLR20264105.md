## Problem

Gradient-based jailbreak attacks on vision-language models overfit to a single white-box surrogate and fail to transfer to unseen black-box models, while manually designed attacks lack cross-target universality. Achieving both universality (one image works across many harmful queries) and transferability (the image generalises across different VLM architectures) simultaneously remains an open challenge, as prior optimisation-based methods produce brittle, model-specific adversarial patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264105/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method optimises a single 224×224 RGB adversarial image using a few-shot training corpus (SafeBench-Tiny, five harmful queries per topic) on one surrogate VLM. At inference, this one universal image is paired with an arbitrary harmful text query (augmented with a Targeted Prompt Guidance prefix instructing the model to begin with an affirming phrase) and fed to any target VLM, which then generates a harmful step-by-step text response. The same optimised image is evaluated across all jailbreak objectives and all victim models without retraining.

## Method

UltraBreak combines two complementary mechanisms to produce a universal, transferable jailbreak image. First, it constrains the visual optimisation space by applying randomised geometric transformations (translation, rotation, scaling) at each gradient step, projecting pixel values through an affine clip, and adding a total-variation regulariser, which together force the optimiser to discover robust, model-invariant patterns (e.g., letter-like shapes) rather than fragile pixel-level artifacts. Second, it replaces the standard cross-entropy token-matching loss with a semantic loss computed in the LLM's embedding space: the model's expected next-token embedding (softmax of logits multiplied by the embedding matrix) is aligned via cosine distance to a dynamically weighted sum of future target embeddings, where the weights are produced by a causal attention mechanism with a learnable temperature. This semantic objective smooths the loss landscape that the image-space constraints would otherwise make spiky, enabling stable optimisation and generalisation. Targeted Prompt Guidance further biases the surrogate toward affirmative, step-by-step outputs by prepending an instruction to begin with a chosen affirming phrase, strengthening the semantic supervision signal.

## Results

UltraBreak achieves black-box average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 71.05% on SafeBench, 52.19% on AdvBench, and 58.50% on MM-SafetyBench, substantially outperforming all gradient-based baselines across six open-source VLMs. On three closed-source commercial models (GPT-4.1-nano, Gemini-2.5-flash-lite, Claude-3-haiku), UltraBreak reaches 32.26% average ASR, more than doubling the best baseline (UMK at 14.59%). The method also attains the highest StrongREJECT quality scores (e.g., 0.79 on LLaVA-v1.6-mistral-7b-hf vs. 0.18 for no-attack), confirming that jailbroken responses contain actionable harmful content rather than superficial refusals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UltraBreak dominates all three baselines across every benchmark. On SafeBench, it outperforms VAJM by ~30 pp (71.05% vs. 41.46%) and UMK by ~51 pp (71.05% vs. 20.00%); it also edges out FigStep (71.05% vs. 66.54%), though FigStep is target-specific and cannot be evaluated on other datasets. The gap widens dramatically on AdvBench, where UltraBreak reaches 52.19% while VAJM and UMK stall at 5.61% and 5.19% respectively—a gap of over 46 pp. On MM-SafetyBench, UltraBreak (58.50%) again leads VAJM (28.16%) and UMK (21.82%) by 30+ pp. On closed-source commercial models, UltraBreak (32.26%) more than doubles UMK (14.59%) and nearly triples VAJM (11.48%), demonstrating that the advantage persists even against stronger, safety-hardened proprietary systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of optimisation-based VLM jailbreaks (VAJM, UMK) by replacing their token-level cross-entropy objective with a semantic embedding-space loss augmented by an attention mechanism, and by borrowing input-diversity transformations from the adversarial transferability literature (Xie et al., 2019) as constraints on the visual optimisation space. It also adopts Targeted Prompt Guidance from Wang et al. (2025a) to bias the surrogate toward affirmative outputs. The key departure is the joint use of vision-space regularisation (random transformations + TV loss) to enforce robustness and a relaxed semantic objective to smooth the resulting spiky loss landscape — a combination absent from prior VLM jailbreak methods that either overfit to a single surrogate or require target-specific images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single surrogate model suffices for strong cross-model transferability challenges the prevailing assumption (Qi et al., 2024; Bailey et al., 2024) that ensembles of surrogates are necessary, which has practical implications for the cost and accessibility of VLM red-teaming. The loss-landscape analysis provides a mechanistic explanation for why prior methods fail to transfer, offering a diagnostic tool for future attack/defence design. However, transferability to frontier-scale closed-source models remains modest (~32% ASR), and the approach has only been validated on 7B-scale surrogates, leaving open whether the method scales to much larger models or whether the semantic-loss trick generalises to text-only LLM jailbreaking.
