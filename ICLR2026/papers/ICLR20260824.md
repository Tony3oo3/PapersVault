## Problem

Despite LoRA's widespread empirical success in parameter-efficient fine-tuning, no prior work provided a theoretical explanation for its robustness. This paper establishes that LoRA can be self-stabilized in feature learning under appropriate hyper-parameters, but reveals that the standard non-zero initialization of A (required to avoid saddle-point halting) fundamentally compromises this self-stability, causing persistent feature-learning instability that degrades performance.

## Input / Output

The method operates on frozen pretrained LLM weight matrices (W_0) of specific attention projection layers (q_proj, v_proj, or extended sets like qkvogud) in Qwen-2 (0.5B–1.5B) and LLaMA-3.2 (1B–3B) models, introducing trainable low-rank matrices A ∈ R^{r×n} and B ∈ R^{m×r} with rank r=8. Inputs are text questions (multi-choice QA or math word problems) and outputs are text answers or chain-of-thought reasoning chains, evaluated by exact-match accuracy. The shrinkage mechanism modifies only the A matrix update rule in-place, requiring no additional memory beyond standard LoRA.

## Method

Stable-LoRA introduces a weight-shrinkage operation applied to matrix A during the earliest training steps: at each step before the stable condition is met, A is multiplied by (1−λ) (with 0<λ<1) in addition to the standard gradient update, i.e., A_{t+1} = (1−λ)A_t − ηg_A^t. The theoretical analysis shows that the non-zero A_0 causes γ[A_t Z] > γ[η]+1, making the δ_1 term in the output update dominate and breaking the Θ(1) scaling required for stable feature learning; this instability persists throughout training due to the inductive growth of A. Shrinkage stops once the practical stable condition ‖A‖_F/n ≤ ‖B‖_F/m is satisfied, at which point both δ_1 and δ_2 terms are guaranteed to scale as Θ(1). The approach is orthogonal to gradient optimizers (AdamW) and weight decay, adds no memory overhead since the shrinkage is in-place, and incurs only ~0.6% additional training time because the norm checks and scalar multiplications are applied only during initial steps.

## Results

On multi-choice QA tasks (HellaSwag, SocialIQa, OpenbookQA, ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge), Stable-LoRA achieves the highest average accuracy across all four model sizes: 64.01% (0.5B), 72.52% (1B), 81.95% (1.5B), and 84.03% (3B), outperforming AdamW by up to 2.07 points (0.5B) and 0.5 points (3B). On chain-of-thought math reasoning (GSM8K), Stable-LoRA also leads, reaching 31.84% at 5000 steps on the 1B model versus 31.08% for AdamW. Training overhead is negligible at only +0.6% time compared to AdamW, with zero additional memory usage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stable-LoRA consistently outperforms all four baselines (AdamW, LoRA+, Riemann Preconditioned Optimization, and LoRA-RITE) across every model size and dataset in the main QA comparison. The largest margin is against Riemann (Zhang & Pilanci, 2024), which underperforms AdamW on several tasks (e.g., 69.07% vs. 71.90% average on 1B), while Stable-LoRA leads by 3.45 points over Riemann on 1B. LoRA+ (Hayou et al., 2024b) and LoRA-RITE (Yen et al., 2025) are closer competitors, trailing Stable-LoRA by 0.5–2.1 points depending on model size, but neither matches its consistency across all five datasets. On the CoT math reasoning task (GSM8K), Stable-LoRA also beats AdamW at every checkpoint (e.g., 31.84% vs. 31.08% at 5000 steps on 1B). In a separate comparison (Table 11), Stable-LoRA outperforms DoRA (Liu et al., 2024) by 0.84 points (1B) and 0.64 points (3B) average accuracy on QA tasks, though the authors note Stable-LoRA cannot be directly applied to DoRA's modified parameterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA fine-tuning framework (Hu et al., 2022) by introducing a theoretical lens—stable feature learning with respect to model width—that connects LoRA's training dynamics to the broader initialization-stability literature (Xavier, He, μP). It departs from prior LoRA stability work (LoRA+, Riemannian preconditioned LoRA, LoRA-RITE) by identifying the root cause of instability as the standard non-zero A initialization itself, rather than the optimizer or learning-rate schedule. The proposed weight-shrinkage strategy is orthogonal to these approaches and operates as a lightweight patch to the gradient-descent loop rather than a replacement for the optimizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical explanation for LoRA's robust empirical effectiveness (self-stability under appropriate hyper-parameters) and identifies a concrete, previously overlooked limitation of the widely-adopted A₀≠0, B₀=0 initialization. The practical impact is notable given the method's near-zero overhead (no extra memory, 0.6% compute), making it immediately deployable in resource-constrained LoRA settings. However, validation is limited to text LLMs (Qwen-2, LLaMA-3.2) with AdamW and standard initialization; compatibility with alternative optimizers, cross-modal tasks, and different initialization schemes remains unexplored.
