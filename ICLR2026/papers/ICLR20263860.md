## Problem

Existing multimodal preference optimization methods (e.g., DPO) treat all response tokens uniformly during training, ignoring that different tokens have varying degrees of visual grounding and thus different contributions to hallucination. This uniform treatment prevents fine-grained correction of hallucinatory outputs, and many prior approaches rely on external detection models, GPT-4 APIs, or noise injection rather than exploiting the MLLM's own multimodal representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263860/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (encoded via CLIP+ViT into N_p visual patch tokens) and a text question as input, producing a free-form text response. During training, it operates on preference pairs (chosen/rejected responses) from the RLHF-V dataset (5,733 samples). Token-level rewards are pre-computed once from the model's internal cross-modal attention maps and CLIP semantic similarity, then reused across all training epochs, adding only ~38% per-sample overhead over standard DPO with negligible memory increase.

## Method

Cat-PO computes three hierarchical visual relevance scores per response token using only the MLLM's own representations: (1) global relevance as the sum of cross-modal attention weights from the token to all image patches, (2) local relevance as 1 minus the normalized Shannon entropy of the token's attention distribution over patches (capturing focus concentration), and (3) semantic relevance as the cosine similarity between the token embedding and its attention-weighted visual feature vector. These are fused into a unified score, smoothed via tanh, and converted into asymmetric token weights that up-weight visually-aligned tokens in chosen responses and up-weight misaligned tokens in rejected responses. The final loss combines a token-weighted DPO objective (weighted log-likelihood ratio between policy and reference) with a token-level, weight-modulated KL divergence regularizer, enabling fine-grained suppression of hallucinatory tokens while preserving generation quality.

## Results

On the LLaVA-v1.5-7B model, Cat-PO achieves an AMBER-Generation Score of 2.74 (vs. 2.14 for DPO and 2.47 for TPO), reduces the MM-Hal hallucination rate to 42.0% (vs. 58.3% for DPO and 51.0% for TPO), and attains 70.3 on LLaVA-Bench and 67.0 on SEED-Bench. On the 13B model, Cat-PO further improves to an AMBER-Generation Score of 2.85, MM-Hal rate of 42.0%, LLaVA-Bench 74.3, and SEED-Bench 69.2. The paper reports 7–15% relative improvements over state-of-the-art methods on AMBER-Generation and MM-Hal benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cat-PO outperforms all six baselines on the hallucination-focused metrics: it reduces the MM-Hal hallucination rate from 58.3% (DPO), 51.0% (TPO), and 56.0% (V-DPO) down to 42.0%, and improves the AMBER-Generation Score from 2.14 (DPO) and 2.47 (TPO) to 2.74 on the 7B model. On AMBER-Discrimination, Cat-PO achieves 85.3 F1, slightly edging out TPO (85.0) and DPO (82.1), though TPO retains a small edge in Accuracy (79.3 vs. 78.0). On general-capability benchmarks, Cat-PO leads on LLaVA-Bench (70.3 vs. 70.2 for TPO, 69.1 for DPO) and SEED-Bench (67.0 vs. 66.6 for TPO, 66.4 for DPO). The 13B results confirm the trend: Cat-PO achieves the best AMBER-Generation Score (2.85), lowest MM-Hal rate (42.0%), and highest LLaVA-Bench (74.3) and SEED-Bench (69.2) scores, surpassing both DPO and TPO across all reported metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cat-PO extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for multimodal hallucination mitigation by introducing per-token visual relevance weights computed from the model's own cross-modal attention, patch entropy, and CLIP-based semantic similarity. It departs from prior multimodal DPO variants (CSR, POVID, RLHF-V, V-DPO, TPO) that either treat all response tokens uniformly or rely on external tools (GPT-4V, visual detectors, noise injection) to construct preference signals. The key conceptual departure is a hierarchical, self-contained token-reward mechanism that fuses three complementary cross-modal signals without any external dependency, combined with a token-weighted KL regulariser added to the standard DPO objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete gap in multimodal preference optimization: the uniform treatment of all tokens ignores that visually critical tokens and hallucinated tokens contribute very differently to alignment. By showing that self-contained, attention-derived token weights can yield 7–15% improvements on hallucination benchmarks with negligible training overhead (+38% time, +0.07% memory), Cat-PO offers a practical, low-cost recipe for practitioners already using DPO on MLLMs. Open questions include whether the fixed fusion coefficient α generalizes across architectures and visual encoders, whether the approach extends to video or multi-image inputs, and whether the one-time pre-computation of rewards remains feasible at scale. The authors also note that systematic resource-consumption measurement and broader evaluation metrics remain future work.
