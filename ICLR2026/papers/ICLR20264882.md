## Problem

Existing GraphRAG methods optimize graph construction and graph retrieval in isolation, neglecting their interdependencies, which limits complex multi-hop reasoning performance especially under domain shifts. Additionally, standard evaluation is confounded by knowledge leaking—LLMs have already memorized most public benchmark content during pre-training, making it impossible to fairly measure retrieval quality.

## Input / Output

The system takes a document corpus, a natural language query, and a compact seed graph schema (defining targeted entity types, relation types, and attribute types) as input. The schema is not static: an adaptive agent continuously expands it with new entity/relation/attribute types at a 0.9 confidence threshold as it processes unseen documents. Output is a natural language answer grounded in a structured knowledge graph organized as a four-level hierarchical Knowledge Tree (Community → Keywords → Entity-Relation Triples → Attributes), evaluated under both open mode (parametric knowledge allowed) and reject mode (must abstain if retrieved evidence is insufficient). The authors also introduce AnonyRAG-CHS/ENG datasets where entities are replaced with anonymized tokens (e.g., [PERSON#277]) to force reliance on retrieved context rather than memorized knowledge.

## Method

Youtu-GraphRAG vertically unifies construction and retrieval through a shared graph schema that simultaneously constrains extraction and guides query decomposition. For construction, a schema-bounded LLM agent extracts only triples matching predefined entity/relation/attribute types, reducing open-ended noise; a dual-perception community detection algorithm then fuses topological connectivity (Jaccard overlap of incident relation types) with subgraph semantic similarity (cosine similarity of one-hop neighborhood embeddings) to iteratively merge communities into a four-level knowledge tree. For retrieval, a schema-guided decomposer transforms complex queries into parallel atomic sub-queries aligned to valid graph patterns, which are resolved through four parallel routes (entity matching, triple matching, community filtering, DFS path traversal up to depth 5). An iterative reasoning-and-reflection loop lets the agent inspect retrieved evidence, detect gaps, and issue follow-up sub-queries until convergence, creating a closed-loop that progressively refines the answer.

## Results

On the primary DeepSeek-V3-0324 backbone, Youtu-GraphRAG achieves top-20 accuracy of 86.50% (Open) / 81.20% (Reject) on HotpotQA, 85.50% / 77.60% on 2WikiMultiHopQA, and 53.60% / 47.50% on MuSiQue, outperforming the strongest baseline (HippoRAG2) by 4.7, 8.2, and 2.8 points respectively in Open mode. On the domain-specific G-Bench, it reaches 86.54% versus 79.37% for HippoRAG2, and on the novel anonymous datasets it scores 42.88% (AnonyRAG-CHS) and 43.26% (AnonyRAG-ENG). The method simultaneously reduces graph-construction token consumption by up to 33.60% while improving accuracy by up to 16.62% over state-of-the-art baselines, effectively shifting the Pareto frontier of performance versus cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Youtu-GraphRAG outperforms all eight baselines across every benchmark and both evaluation modes. Against the strongest competitor HippoRAG2, it gains +4.7 points on HotpotQA (Open), +8.2 on 2Wiki (Open), +2.8 on MuSiQue (Open), +7.2 on G-Bench, and +29.96 on AnonyRAG-CHS, while matching HippoRAG2 closely on AnonyRAG-ENG (43.26 vs 43.16). In Reject mode, the margins widen substantially: +6.3 on HotpotQA, +29.3 on 2Wiki, and +9.7 on MuSiQue over HippoRAG2, confirming superior retrieval fidelity when the model must abstain. Tree-based methods (RAPTOR, E²GraphRAG) and flat graph methods (LightRAG, GraphRAG, G-Retriever) trail by 5–30+ points, with E²GraphRAG and G-Retriever performing particularly poorly on multi-hop tasks. The anonymous datasets (AnonyRAG-CHS/ENG) are especially revealing: HippoRAG2 drops to 12.92% on AnonyRAG-CHS (vs 42.88% for Youtu-GraphRAG), exposing severe knowledge-leaking in baselines that rely on parametric memory rather than genuine retrieval. The lightweight variant without the iterative agent (Ours w/o Agent) still surpasses all baselines, indicating that the schema-guided construction and dual-perception community detection alone provide most of the gain, with the agentic reflection loop adding a further 2–5 points on complex reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two previously separate trajectories in GraphRAG: graph construction (GraphRAG, RAPTOR, E²GraphRAG) and graph retrieval (LightRAG, HippoRAG, GNN-RAG, GFM-RAG). Rather than improving either component in isolation, Youtu-GraphRAG introduces a shared graph schema as the binding mechanism that simultaneously constrains extraction during construction and guides query decomposition during retrieval, creating a vertically unified agentic pipeline. The dual-perception community detection (fusing topological Jaccard overlap with semantic cosine similarity) extends prior Louvain/Leiden-based approaches by incorporating subgraph-level semantics, while the iterative reasoning-reflection agent builds on the perception-reasoning-action paradigm from ToG/ToG 2.0 but grounds it in schema-aligned sub-queries rather than free-form graph traversal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of knowledge leaking as a confounding factor in GraphRAG evaluation, and the introduction of AnonyRAG with the Anonymity Reversion task, addresses a genuine methodological gap that affects fair comparison across all existing GraphRAG systems. The Pareto frontier movement (33.6% token savings alongside 16.62% accuracy gains) is practically significant for deployment cost, and the claim of domain transfer with minimal schema intervention, if it holds at scale, would reduce the engineering burden of adapting GraphRAG to new domains. Open questions include whether the schema-bounded approach degrades gracefully on truly open-domain corpora with no reasonable seed schema, how the four-level knowledge tree scales to corpora with millions of entities, and whether the dual-mode evaluation protocol (open vs. reject) becomes the standard for future GraphRAG benchmarks.
