## Problem

Generation-based offline RL methods (e.g., Decision Transformer, Decision Diffuser) produce trajectories that are locally plausible but globally incoherent in long-horizon sparse-reward tasks, due to inadequate modeling of multi-scale temporal dependencies. Existing hierarchical generation methods mitigate this with fixed two-layer architectures and separate policies per level, which limits the capture of multi-scale temporal abstractions and introduces joint-optimization difficulties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260424/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, MAGE takes the current state s₀ and a target return-to-go R₀ as conditioning signals. It autoregressively generates K discrete token maps ordered from coarsest to finest temporal resolution (e.g., K=8 scales), which are decoded via a shared codebook into a full trajectory of (R, s) pairs. An action is then extracted from the aggregated multi-scale latent representation Z through a latent inverse dynamics model, rather than from the decoded trajectory itself.

## Method

MAGE adapts VAR's next-scale autoregressive generation from the spatial to the temporal domain for offline RL. A Multi-scale Trajectory Autoencoder (MTAE) uses residual VQ-VAE quantization with a shared codebook to encode trajectories into K hierarchical discrete token maps, where coarse scales capture long-range structure and fine scales encode short-term dynamics. A multi-scale transformer then generates these token maps sequentially (coarse-to-fine), with each scale conditioned on all previously generated coarser maps plus (s₀, R₀), trained via cross-entropy loss. A condition-guided refinement module—lightweight adapters inserted into a frozen decoder—enforces that the decoded initial state matches s₀ through an MSE loss, using Gumbel-Softmax with a straight-through estimator to make the discrete codebook lookup differentiable end-to-end. Finally, a latent inverse dynamics model maps the summed multi-scale embeddings directly to an action, preserving dynamics-consistent information at the finest temporal scale.

## Results

On the Adroit benchmark (the most challenging sparse-reward, long-horizon setting), MAGE achieves a mean score of 66.9 across all 12 scenarios, substantially outperforming the next-best baselines IQL (53.4), DT (49.2), and HD (40.3). On Maze2D and Multi2D navigation, MAGE scores 153.3 and 155.0 respectively, surpassing HD (139.9/149.9) and DD (121.5/129.5). On Franka Kitchen, MAGE reaches 88.8 average, exceeding HD (72.5) and ADT (66.7). Inference speed is 27.3 ms/step, approximately 50× faster than HD and 80× faster than DD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGE achieves the best score on 11 of 12 Adroit scenarios, with the largest margins on Pen-Human (137.1 vs. IQL 78.4, a 75% relative improvement) and Pen-Cloned (108.4 vs. IQL 83.4). On Maze2D and Multi2D, MAGE outperforms all 8 baselines on every maze size, with single-task averages of 153.3 vs. HD's 139.9 and DD's 121.5. On AntMaze, MAGE wins 5 of 6 datasets (average 89.7 vs. ADT 78.1), losing only on Play U-maze to Diffusion-QL (92.2 vs. 93.4). On Franka Kitchen, MAGE leads by a wide margin (88.8 vs. HD 72.5, ADT 66.7). On Gym locomotion (dense reward), MAGE wins 7 of 9 tasks (average 89.8 vs. IQL 84.0), trailing HD on HalfCheetah-Medium (43.9 vs. 46.7) and Walker2d-Medium (83.5 vs. 84.0). Hierarchical baselines (ADT, HD, HDMI) consistently underperform MAGE, confirming that a fixed two-level hierarchy is insufficient compared to MAGE's multi-scale (K=8) autoregressive generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGE adapts the hierarchical autoregressive generation paradigm of VAR (Tian et al., 2024) from spatial image scales to temporal trajectory scales, combined with VQ-VAE-style discrete tokenization (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) for offline RL sequence modeling. It departs from flat generation-based offline RL methods (Decision Transformer, Decision Diffuser) that struggle with long-horizon sparse rewards, and from fixed two-level hierarchical methods (ADT, HDMI, HD) by replacing the rigid two-policy hierarchy with a single unified multi-scale autoregressive transformer that generates K levels of token maps from coarse to fine. The return-to-go conditioning and inverse dynamics model follow the Decision Transformer lineage, while the adapter-based conditional refinement draws on parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAGE addresses a persistent weakness of generation-based offline RL—poor global coherence in long-horizon sparse-reward tasks—by explicitly modeling multi-scale temporal dependencies, and does so with 27 ms inference that is 50–80× faster than diffusion-based alternatives, making it practical for real-time robotic control. The finding that a single unified policy across all temporal scales outperforms separate per-level policies simplifies the hierarchical RL design space. Open challenges remain for extremely sparse-reward settings (OGBench) and out-of-distribution generalization, and the optimal number of scales K is task-dependent, suggesting the framework's hyperparameter sensitivity could limit plug-and-play adoption.
