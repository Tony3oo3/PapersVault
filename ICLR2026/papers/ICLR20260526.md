## Problem

In episodic MDPs where transition kernels are adversarially chosen at each episode, prior regret bounds scale as Õ(√T + C^P), where the corruption term C^P can reach O(T) in fully adversarial settings, yielding linear regret. This paper answers the fundamental question of whether sublinear regret is achievable under fully adversarial transitions, and further establishes that the optimal policy must be history-dependent rather than Markovian—a structural property absent from prior adversarial MDP analyses.

## Input / Output

The learner interacts with a layered episodic MDP (K stages, |S| states per layer, |A| actions per layer) and receives only bandit feedback: the loss at visited state-action pairs and the next state, while the transition kernel P_t remains entirely hidden and may change arbitrarily across episodes. The output is a history-dependent policy π_t that maps full state-action histories h^k to action distributions, updated via an FTRL rule over the trajectory space of size (|S||A|)^K. The key constraint is that the policy class must be ε-greedy (min π(a|h) ≥ ε) to ensure unbiased importance sampling, and the regret guarantee is Õ(√((|S||A|)^K T)), which is exponential in the horizon K.

## Method

AD-FTRL avoids estimating adversarial transition kernels entirely by operating on trajectory-level occupancy measures q_{π,P_t}(τ) rather than state-action occupancies. The trajectory occupancy is decomposed into a policy-dependent factor Π_π(τ) = ∏π(a^k|h^k) and a transition-dependent factor F_t(τ), and the FTRL regularizer is designed solely on Π_π (a Shannon entropy term), sidestepping the unknown F_t and preventing variance inflation. An unbiased estimator for the trajectory loss is constructed via importance sampling with a clipped denominator (γ + Π_{π_t}(τ)), and the cumulative estimated loss vector Υ_t drives the policy update. The paper proves a matching lower bound of Ω(√((|S||A|)^K T)) by reducing the regret problem to a composite hypothesis testing task over the space of deterministic history-dependent policies, combining Assouad's lemma with Fano's method to handle the dependencies across trajectories that simpler two-point arguments cannot capture.

## Results

The AD-FTRL algorithm achieves a sublinear regret upper bound of Õ(√((|S||A|)^K · T)) under fully adversarial transition kernels and bandit feedback, where K is the horizon length, |S| is the number of states, and |A| is the number of actions. A matching lower bound of Ω(√((|S||A|)^K · T)) is established via a hard MDP instance and composite hypothesis testing (Assouad's Lemma + Fano's method), proving minimax optimality. This is the first sublinear regret guarantee in the fully adversarial transition setting, where prior corruption-based bounds (scaling as Õ(√T + C^P)) degrade to linear O(T) regret when C^P = O(T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against prior adversarial MDP results that rely on estimating an average transition kernel P̄ and incur a corruption penalty C^P. Jin et al. (2023) and Wei et al. (2022) achieve Õ(√(|S||A|·T) + C^P), which is sublinear only when C^P = o(T); under fully adversarial transitions (C^P = O(T)), their bounds become linear. AD-FTRL avoids this entirely by operating on trajectory-level occupancy measures via importance sampling, achieving sublinear regret without any C^P term. For lower bounds, Tian et al. (2021) established Ω(√(2^K · T)) for Markov games but did not characterize dependence on state or action space sizes; this paper's lower bound of Ω(√((|S||A|)^K · T)) is strictly tighter and explicitly captures both dimensions. The paper also notes that reducing the problem to a contextual bandit or multi-armed bandit is fundamentally suboptimal, as the state feedback within an episode violates the exogenous-context assumption of bandits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FTRL-based adversarial MDP framework (Rosenberg & Mansour, 2019; Zimin & Neu, 2013) and the bandit-feedback adversarial MDP setting (Jin et al., 2020) by replacing state-action occupancy with trajectory-level occupancy and importance sampling, thereby avoiding the need to estimate adversarial transition kernels. The key departure from prior works (Jin et al., 2023; Wei et al., 2022) is that those methods estimate the average transition kernel and incur a C^P corruption term that becomes linear in T under fully adversarial dynamics, whereas AD-FTRL operates directly in trajectory space to achieve sublinear regret. The paper also departs from the standard assumption of Markov policies by proving that history-dependent policies are necessary under adversarial transitions, a structural insight absent from prior adversarial MDP analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result establishing that sublinear regret is achievable under fully adversarial transition kernels, resolving a fundamental open question and proving minimax optimality via a matching lower bound constructed through composite hypothesis testing. The exponential dependence on horizon K (|S||A|)^K is a significant limitation, and the paper itself raises the open question of whether this rate can be improved. Practical applicability is further constrained by the exponential computational cost of the FTRL update over the trajectory space, though the paper notes a more efficient mirror-descent variant reduces this to O(|S||A|K). The result provides a clean theoretical characterization of the fundamental difficulty of adversarial RL but leaves the gap between theory and practice wide open.
