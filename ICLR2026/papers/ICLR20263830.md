## Problem

Data-level unlearning in LLMs aims to remove the influence of specific training datapoints without full retraining, but existing methods that operate solely on the final model checkpoint struggle to precisely estimate and reverse data influence, often resulting in under-forgetting, degraded utility, or poor alignment with an ideal model that was never trained on the target data. The paper asks whether intermediate model checkpoints—routinely stored during training for experimentation and fault tolerance—can be repurposed to yield more accurate estimates of data influence and thus enable more effective unlearning.

## Input / Output

MSA takes as input a trained model θ_D, an intermediate checkpoint C (weights θ_0) taken before the model was exposed to the forget set, and the forget set D_f; optionally a retain set D_r is also provided, from which a subset matching |D_f| is sampled to preserve O(|D_f|) scaling. The output is a single set of unlearned model weights θ_unlearn computed via a closed-form vector subtraction/addition in parameter space, requiring no iterative optimization on the target model itself. The checkpoint need not be immediately prior to the forget set—experiments show effectiveness even when the checkpoint is hundreds of billions of tokens earlier in training.

## Method

MSA (Model State Arithmetic) computes a forget vector by finetuning a pre-exposure checkpoint C on the forget set D_f, yielding θ_1, and taking θ_f = θ_1 − θ_0; optionally a retain vector θ_r = θ_2 − θ_0 is computed similarly from the retain set. The unlearned model is then obtained by the closed-form update θ_unlearn = θ_D − α·θ_f + β·θ_r, where α and β are scalars tuned on a validation set. The key insight is that deriving the forget vector from a checkpoint that has not yet seen the target data produces a cleaner, less entangled estimate of data influence than task-vector-style approaches that extract the vector from the target model itself, where the forget-set signal is already mixed with all other learned knowledge. The method is fully post-hoc, requires no modification to the original training objective or optimizer, and scales linearly in the forget set size, making it practical for LLM-scale models.

## Results

On the TOFU forget10 benchmark with OLMo-2-7B, MSA variants (MSA_3691B, MSA_3859B, MSA_last) achieve Acc_forget of 0.82–0.84, Acc_recover of 0.42–0.45, and Acc_retain of 0.82–0.85, while simultaneously reaching +100% (matching or exceeding the ideal model) on Extraction Strength, Model Utility, and ROUGE-L_f. Notably, even MSA_500B—using a checkpoint ~3.5T tokens before the unlearning target—achieves +100% on Extraction Strength (0.05), Model Utility (0.41), and ROUGE-L_f (0.34). On RESTOR, MSA_instruct recovers Llama-3.1-8B accuracy from 44.31% (target) to 63.95%, approaching the ideal model's 64.80%, while all baselines remain below 50%. On MUSE-Books, MSA_last achieves MIN-K% of 0.37 and Privacy Leakage of 14.67, substantially outperforming NPO (MIN-K% 1.00, PrivLeak −99.93) and SatImp (MIN-K% 1.00, PrivLeak −100.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSA is the only method that remains competitive across all six metrics simultaneously; every baseline fails on at least one. RMU achieves the highest Acc_forget (0.92) and lowest ROUGE-L_f (0.14) but catastrophically underperforms on Acc_recover (0.08 vs. MSA's 0.42–0.45), indicating it refuses to answer rather than genuinely unlearning. NPO is the closest overall competitor (Acc_forget 0.71, Acc_recover 0.30) but is consistently outperformed by MSA variants on all GPT-4o judge metrics. GradDiff and Task Vector are substantially weaker, with Acc_forget of only 0.45 and 0.53 respectively, and much higher Extraction Strength (0.17 and 0.24 vs. MSA's 0.06–0.08). SatImp performs worst overall (Acc_forget 0.28, Ext. Strength 0.40), while UNDIAL matches MSA on Extraction Strength but lags on Acc_forget (0.48 vs. 0.84) and Acc_recover (0.23 vs. 0.42). On MUSE-Books, MSA variants achieve MIN-K% scores of 0.14–0.37 (vs. 1.00 for NPO, SatImp, and UNDIAL), demonstrating that unlearned documents are no longer detectable via membership inference, whereas most baselines remain fully detectable.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSA extends the task-arithmetic framework of Ilharco et al. (2022) by computing the forget vector from a prior checkpoint rather than the target model itself, and is conceptually related to Unrolling SGD (Thudi et al., 2022), which also leverages pre-exposure model states for approximate unlearning. However, MSA departs from both by being fully post-hoc (requiring no training-time intervention or per-step update logging as in Amnesiac ML), operating at LLM scale, and repurposing checkpoints that practitioners already store for fault tolerance and experimentation. The paper positions MSA against a broad set of gradient-based and loss-reweighting unlearning baselines (NPO, GradDiff, RMU, SatImp, UNDIAL), arguing that all of these operate solely on the final model and thus produce noisier estimates of data influence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that MSA requires no new infrastructure—model developers already save intermediate checkpoints—making it a low-friction path toward regulatory compliance (e.g., GDPR Right to Be Forgotten) for LLMs. A key open question is how far back in training a checkpoint can be while still yielding effective unlearning; the paper shows hundreds of billions of tokens of separation still works, but performance degrades for very early checkpoints on some benchmarks. The paper also highlights that no single unlearning method dominates all evaluation criteria (forgetting efficacy vs. ideal-model recovery vs. utility preservation), suggesting the field still lacks a universally optimal approach and that practitioners must make tradeoff decisions based on their specific compliance or safety goals.
