## Problem

Differentiable MPC solvers rely on sequential Riccati recursions over the prediction horizon, making them difficult to parallelize on GPUs and limiting their use in large-batch learning applications such as reinforcement learning and imitation learning. Existing GPU-capable differentiable solvers (e.g., Theseus) do not exploit the time-induced sparse structure of optimal control problems, while OCP-specific solvers (mpc.pytorch, trajax) remain bottlenecked by their sequential-in-time design, yielding no meaningful GPU speedup.

## Input / Output

The solver accepts a parametric optimal control problem with separable quadratic costs, linearized equality dynamics constraints, and an initial condition, all parameterized by θ (which may be neural network weights, cost matrices, or physics parameters). It returns the optimal state-control trajectory and, via implicit differentiation, the gradient of any downstream scalar loss with respect to θ. The KKT matrix from the forward pass is cached and reused in the backward pass, so the backward pass requires only one additional PCG solve rather than recomputing the factorization. The entire pipeline is implemented in JAX and operates on batched problem instances in parallel on a single GPU.

## Method

DiffMPC replaces the sequential Riccati recursions of iLQR-based differentiable MPC with a preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) routine using a symmetric stair (tridiagonal) preconditioner to solve the KKT linear systems arising from an SQP formulation. In the forward pass, each SQP iteration forms a QP whose KKT matrix has a block-tridiagonal Schur complement S; PCG with the tridiagonal preconditioner Φ⁻¹ preserves this structure, enabling all matrix-vector products to be parallelized over time steps t. The backward pass reuses the same S and Φ⁻¹ from the forward pass, solving a single additional PCG system to compute the vector-Jacobian product via the implicit function theorem, avoiding any recomputation of the KKT factorization. A standard line search with a merit function is evaluated in parallel over predefined step sizes, and warm-starting of the PCG iterates across problem instances further reduces solve times in high-frequency MPC settings. This design yields 4–7× speedups over the fastest baselines (trajax, mpc.pytorch) on GPU for both forward and backward passes across benchmark RL and IL tasks.

## Results

On GPU, DiffMPC achieves a 4× speedup over the fastest baseline (trajax) on Problem 1 (0.22 s fwd / 0.32 s bwd vs. 0.95 s / 1.83 s), with speedups ranging from 4–7× across all six tested QP problems. On the nonlinear attitude stabilization task, DiffMPC is approximately 7.3× faster than trajax on the backward pass (69.3 ms vs. 505.0 ms). For the cart-pole imitation learning benchmark, DiffMPC trains approximately 2× faster in wall-clock time while maintaining convergence. In the drifting application, the learned policy achieves 100% success rate on randomized water-puddle trials versus 70% for the manually tuned baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffMPC is compared against three state-of-the-art differentiable solvers: Theseus (Pineda et al., 2022), mpc.pytorch (Amos et al., 2018), and trajax (Frostig et al., 2021). On the GPU, DiffMPC decisively outperforms all three baselines across every tested problem, with the largest gap against Theseus (which does not exploit OCP sparsity and is 15–20× slower) and a consistent 4–7× advantage over trajax (the fastest baseline). On the CPU, the picture is different: trajax is the fastest method, and DiffMPC falls between mpc.pytorch and trajax, confirming that the PCG-based approach is specifically designed for GPU parallelism. mpc.pytorch shows minimal GPU speedup over CPU due to its sequential Riccati recursions, which require large batch sizes to saturate GPU cores. In the imitation learning task, DiffMPC trains ~2× faster than trajax in wall-clock time while converging to the same loss. For the drifting application, the learned DiffMPC policy (100% success) substantially outperforms the manually tuned baseline MPC (70% success) under domain randomization with water puddles.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffMPC unifies two previously separate lines of work: GPU-accelerated MPC solvers (MPCGPU, Adabag et al. 2024) that exploit the sparse-in-time KKT structure via a preconditioned conjugate gradient routine, and differentiable MPC frameworks (Amos et al. 2018; Frostig et al. 2021) that compute parameter sensitivities via implicit differentiation. The key departure from prior differentiable MPC tools (mpc.pytorch, trajax) is replacing sequential Riccati recursions with a PCG solver using a symmetric stair preconditioner (Bu & Plancher, 2024), which exposes parallelism over time steps and enables warm-starting across problem instances. General-purpose differentiable solvers like Theseus are contrasted as insufficiently exploiting OCP structure, resulting in orders-of-magnitude slowdowns on MPC-specific problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making differentiable MPC genuinely GPU-parallel, DiffMPC removes a practical bottleneck that has limited the adoption of MPC-based policies in large-batch RL and IL settings, where the sequential nature of Riccati recursions made GPU speedups negligible. The Toyota Supra drifting demo demonstrates that the resulting speedups enable training regimes (large batches, long episodes, domain randomization) that are infeasible with CPU-bound solvers, yielding robust controllers for safety-critical driving. Open questions include handling inequality constraints differentiably (gradients are discontinuous at constraint boundaries), improving CPU performance, and developing robust initialization strategies to prevent solver divergence during training.
