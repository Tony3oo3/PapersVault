## Problem

Prior work treats human motion generation and camera trajectory generation as independent single-modality tasks, overlooking the fundamental cinematographic principle that camera framing and actor performance are tightly coupled. No existing method jointly generates both modalities from text while maintaining consistent on-screen framing, and any mismatch between the two can produce empty frames or incoherent compositions. This paper is the first to cast the task as text-conditioned joint generation of human motion and camera trajectories with explicit multimodal coherence enforcement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263344/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt describing both the desired human motion and camera behavior, and produces a paired sequence of F frames (up to 300) containing SMPL-based human motion (pelvis position/velocity, 22 body pose parameters in 6D, 22 joint positions) and camera trajectory (6D rotation, linear velocity, relative distance to human, horizontal/vertical FOV). The on-screen framing — the 2D NDC projection of 9 key joints (ankles, pelvis, spine, head, shoulders, wrists) into the camera view — is derived from the generated pair rather than directly generated, serving as the auxiliary coherence signal. The model operates in a shared latent space (128-dim human, 64-dim camera, 64-dim framing) learned by a joint autoencoder.

## Method

The core contribution is an auxiliary sampling scheme that enforces multimodal coherence at inference time without architectural modifications. A joint autoencoder learns a shared latent space for human motion x and camera trajectory y, together with a lightweight linear transform W that maps the concatenated latent u=[x,y]ᵀ to an on-screen framing latent z (N_z < N_x+N_y). During sampling, the joint latent u is decomposed via the Moore-Penrose projection P_∥ = W(WᵀW)⁻¹Wᵀ into a z-dependent component u_∥ (in the coimage of W) and an orthogonal complement u_⊥ (in ker W), which are independent by Cochran's theorem. The standard classifier-free guidance equation is then augmented with an additional term w_z · P_∥ · ε_θ, steering the denoising process toward regions of the joint distribution where the projected framing is coherent. This requires no extra training, no external pretrained models, and no conditioning on z at inference — the auxiliary modality is purely a sampling-time steering signal. The approach is architecture-agnostic, validated on both DiT and MAR backbones, and consistently improves framing quality (FD_framing, out-of-frame rate) and per-modality text alignment without degrading generation fidelity.

## Results

On the PulpMotion mixed subset, the proposed (x,y)+Aux method achieves the best framing quality across both architectures: FD_framing of 3.37 (DiT) and 6.42 (MAR), with out-of-frame rates of 16.76% and 33.65% respectively. It also attains the highest text-modality alignment scores: TMR-Score of 25.05 (DiT) and 24.46 (MAR) for human motion, and CLaTr-Score of 32.81 (DiT) and 45.96 (MAR) for camera trajectories. The method is architecture-agnostic, consistently improving framing over all baseline configurations (independent, joint, triplet) on both DiT and MAR backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against (x)+DIRECTOR (Courant et al., 2024), the proposed (x,y)+Aux method dramatically outperforms on framing: FD_framing drops from 22.21 to 3.37 (DiT) and out-of-frame rate falls from 60.56% to 16.76%, confirming that conditioning camera generation on separately generated human motion is insufficient for coherent framing. Against ReDi (Kouzelis et al., 2025), the proposed method wins on all framing metrics (FD_framing 3.37 vs 5.57 for DiT; 6.42 vs 6.96 for MAR) and on text-modality alignment (TMR-Score 25.05 vs 22.48 for DiT; 24.46 vs 20.84 for MAR; CLaTr-Score 32.81 vs 22.73 for DiT; 45.96 vs 42.60 for MAR). However, ReDi retains an edge on raw generation fidelity: FD_TMR is lower for ReDi (360.07 vs 431.54 on DiT; 275.58 vs 301.39 on MAR), and human coverage is higher (12.83 vs 8.91 on DiT; 18.41 vs 14.14 on MAR), suggesting the auxiliary guidance trades some distributional spread for tighter multimodal coherence. Overall, the paper's method establishes the best framing quality and text alignment across both DiT and MAR architectures, while the trade-off in Fréchet distance is modest relative to the large gains in coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDPM/CFG framework by introducing an auxiliary modality (on-screen framing) as a bridge between human motion and camera trajectory latents, using a learned linear transform and an orthogonal projection at sampling time. It departs from prior multimodal generation approaches (MMDiffusion, CoDi, UniDiffuser, ReDi) that enforce coherence through architectural modifications, paired-data training, or external pretrained models, instead achieving coherence with a model-agnostic sampling-time projection that requires no training adaptations. The joint autoencoder borrows from MARDM's ResNet-based design, and the dataset pipeline builds directly on the E.T. extraction pipeline (TRAM + CondensedMovies), extending it with VLM-based motion captions and a RePaint-based refinement step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PulpMotion dataset (193K samples, 314 hours, all four modalities with captions) fills a critical gap as the first large-scale joint human-camera dataset with rich motion captions, enabling proper evaluation of both modalities simultaneously. The auxiliary sampling technique is notably simple and model-agnostic (validated on both DiT and MAR), suggesting it could transfer to other multimodal generation tasks beyond human-camera. However, the method currently supports only character-level framing without fine-grained body-part or region control, and the framing improvements, while consistent, are moderate in absolute magnitude. Open questions include whether the linear-transform assumption generalizes to more complex inter-modality relationships and whether the approach scales to multi-character scenes.
