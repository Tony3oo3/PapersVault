## Problem

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for LLM reasoning suffers from training plateaus after thousands of optimization steps, where additional compute yields diminishing returns. This stems from sparse exploration during training: models rely on limited direct rollouts that miss critical reasoning paths, while structured search (e.g., MCTS) is restricted to inference time only. The paper asks whether embedding tree-based search directly into the RLVR training loop can overcome this exploration bottleneck more effectively than simply extending training duration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a mathematical problem as a text prompt (truncated to 2048 tokens) and produces a step-by-step reasoning trajectory ending in a final answer enclosed in \boxed{}. During MCTS expansion, each node generates 256-token reasoning steps with 8 candidate children per expansion, up to a maximum depth of 64 levels (16,384 total tokens). Training uses the DeepMath-103K dataset with a DeepScaleR-style 'think step by step' prompt template, and evaluation reports Pass1@1 accuracy with n=32 samples across six benchmarks (AIME 2024/2025, AMC 2023, MATH500, Minerva, OlympiadBench).

## Method

DeepSearch embeds a modified MCTS directly into the RLVR training loop, replacing root-to-leaf UCT traversals with a global frontier selection strategy that scores all leaf nodes simultaneously using a composite priority (tanh-transformed parent Q-value, policy entropy bonus, and square-root depth bonus). When no correct terminal is found, an entropy-based guidance rule selects the most confident (lowest average trajectory entropy) incorrect path for negative supervision, targeting systematic reasoning errors. Credit assignment uses an asymmetric q-value backup with depth-decayed rewards and a constrained update rule that preserves non-negative q-values for nodes ever observed on correct trajectories. The training objective (Tree-GRPO) extends GRPO with node-level q-value advantages normalized by the tree-wide terminal mean, combined with an adaptive replay buffer that progressively filters mastered problems and caches verified solutions to avoid redundant MCTS computation. This yields 62.95% average accuracy on 1.5B models using only 50 additional training steps and 330 GPU hours, versus 1,883 GPU hours for the best extended-training baseline.

## Results

DeepSearch-1.5B achieves 62.95% average Pass@1 accuracy across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME 2024/2025, AMC 2023, MATH500, Minerva, OlympiadBench), establishing a new state-of-the-art for 1.5B-scale reasoning models. It outperforms the previous best, Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B v2 (61.70%), by +1.25 points on average, with the largest gains on AIME 2024 (53.65% vs. 51.77%) and AMC 2023 (90.39% vs. 88.83%). Critically, these gains are achieved with only 50 additional training steps and 330 GPU hours, compared to 1,883.2 GPU hours for the most compute-intensive extended-training baseline—a 5.7× reduction in computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepSearch-1.5B wins on all six benchmarks against every baseline. Against the closest competitor, Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B v2 (61.70% avg), DeepSearch gains +1.25 points on average, with the largest per-benchmark improvements on AIME 2024 (+1.88), AIME 2025 (+2.50), and AMC 2023 (+1.56). Against DeepScaleR-1.5B (55.64%), the gap is +7.31 points, and against the Open-RS series (~49% avg), it is roughly +14 points. The base Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B models (29–36% avg) and the search-at-inference-only Oat-Zero variant (36.89%) are substantially outperformed, confirming that integrating MCTS into training rather than restricting it to inference yields the largest gains. In terms of efficiency, DeepSearch matches or exceeds the accuracy of extended-training baselines (61.78–62.08%) while using 5.7× fewer GPU hours (330 vs. 1,883.2) and 37× fewer additional training steps (50 vs. 1,875).

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepSearch extends the RLVR training paradigm (DAPO, GRPO) by embedding Monte Carlo Tree Search directly into the training loop, departing from prior work that restricts structured search to inference-time compute scaling (Tree of Thoughts, AlphaMath, RStar-Math). It builds on the UCT selection mechanism from classical MCTS but replaces root-to-leaf traversals with a novel global frontier selection strategy, and extends GRPO into Tree-GRPO with node-level q-value advantages derived from tree-structured backpropagation. The base model and training configuration follow ProRL/Nemotron (Liu et al., 2025a), positioning DeepSearch as a targeted algorithmic modification rather than a new training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that RLVR performance plateaus can be overcome by simply extending training steps, showing that structured exploration during training (scaling breadth) can outperform brute-force depth scaling with 5.7× fewer GPU hours. However, the evidence is limited to 1.5B-scale models on mathematical reasoning, and the MCTS hyperparameters (frontier scoring weights, depth bonus function, expansion width) are fixed heuristics selected offline rather than learned. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to domains with approximate or subjective verification, whether it scales to larger models where inference cost dominates, and whether the search components themselves can be made end-to-end trainable in an AlphaZero-style framework.
