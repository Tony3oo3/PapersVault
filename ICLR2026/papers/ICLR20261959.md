## Problem

Existing time series forecasting methods achieve multi-frequency decomposition through intricate model structures (e.g., explicit trend-seasonality modules, multi-scale mixing) or handcrafted frequency-domain rules, which increase computational complexity and rely on rigid assumptions. The paper asks whether mapping time series into higher-dimensional hypercomplex spaces (complex, quaternion, octonion, sedenion) can naturally induce frequency decomposition with simple MLP architectures, eliminating the need for complex structural priors.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×F} (historical window of length T with F features) and produces future values Ŷ ∈ R^{P×F} over prediction horizons P ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}. Before processing, the input undergoes mean normalization and multi-level patch embedding that segments the series into subsequences of lengths ⌊T/2^i⌋ for i = 0,…,l_p−1, linearly maps each patch to a shared encoding dimension d_e, and concatenates across levels. The model is variable-independent, processing each of the F features separately rather than fusing cross-channel information.

## Method

Numerion generalizes linear layers and the Tanh activation to hypercomplex spaces of arbitrary power-of-two dimension via the Cayley-Dickson algebra, forming the RHR-MLP: input is mapped into an n-dimensional hypercomplex space (HMAP), processed through L layers of hypercomplex linear transformations with a novel HNTanh activation (which normalizes by the p-norm, p=6, then applies tanh to the modulus while preserving phase), and mapped back to the real domain (LMAP). Five RHR-MLPs operate in parallel across Real(1D), Complex(2D), Quaternion(4D), Octonion(8D), and Sedenion(16D) spaces, each independently capturing distinct frequency bands—higher dimensions naturally suppress high-frequency content. A Multi-Hypercomplex Adaptive Fusion module learns per-space weights via a small MLP with softmax to produce the final prediction. Training uses standard real-valued gradient descent justified by GHR (Generalized Hamilton-Real) Calculus, which circumvents Liouville's Theorem by treating hypercomplex weights and their conjugates as independent variables, and hypercomplex multiplication is implemented efficiently via precomputed coefficient-selection and sign matrices that reduce the operation to a single matrix multiply.

## Results

Numerion achieves the best MAE across all nine benchmark datasets (averaged over prediction lengths {96, 192, 336, 720}), with headline values of 0.417 on ETTh1, 0.388 on ETTh2, 0.315 on ETTm2, and 0.267 on Electricity. It consistently ranks within the top two for MSE, with an overall average MAE improvement exceeding 4% over the second-best method on ETT datasets and over 3% MSE improvement. On high-dimensional datasets like Traffic (862 features), Numerion's variable-independent design yields slightly higher MSE (0.468) than variable-fusion models like iTransformer (0.428), but still leads on MAE (0.281 vs. 0.282).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Numerion outperforms all ten baselines on MAE across every dataset. Against the closest MLP-based competitors, it beats PatchMLP by 2–10% MAE (e.g., 0.417 vs. 0.437 on ETTh1, 0.262 vs. 0.292 on Solar Energy) and TimeMixer by 1–15% MAE (e.g., 0.388 vs. 0.409 on ETTh2, 0.267 vs. 0.273 on Electricity). Among Transformer-based methods, Numerion surpasses iTransformer by 4–15% MAE (0.417 vs. 0.467 on ETTh1), PatchTST by 3–13% MAE, and Crossformer by 15–44% MAE (0.399 vs. 0.707 on Exchange). On MSE, Numerion is top-2 everywhere except Electricity (iTransformer: 0.178 vs. 0.181) and Traffic (iTransformer: 0.428 vs. 0.468), where variable-fusion architectures retain a slight edge. DLinear, the simplest baseline, is outperformed by 4–38% MAE, while TimesNet and FEDformer trail by 5–25% MAE, confirming that the hypercomplex decomposition provides gains beyond what frequency-domain or 2D-variation modeling alone can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLP-based time series forecasting lineage (DLinear, N-BEATS, TimeMixer, PatchMLP) by replacing explicit decomposition modules with an algebraic mechanism: mapping inputs into successive Cayley-Dickson hypercomplex algebras (complex → quaternion → octonion → sedenion). It adopts the multi-level patch embedding from PatchTST (Nie et al., 2022) to handle multi-scale temporal features, and draws on the GHR calculus framework (Xu et al., 2015) to justify training hypercomplex parameters via real-valued gradient descent. The work also connects to a small but growing body of hypercomplex neural network research (Zhang et al., 2021; Comminiello et al., 2024; Lopez et al., 2024), but is the first to exploit the frequency-suppression property of higher-dimensional algebras as an implicit, learned decomposition for time series.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that increasing hypercomplex dimensionality naturally attenuates high-frequency components, yielding an emergent multi-frequency decomposition without handcrafted modules—offers a principled alternative to the trend-seasonality decomposition architectures that dominate the field. However, the practical impact is currently limited by significant computational overhead (simulating hypercomplex multiplication via real-valued matrix operations inflates parameters ~63×) and the absence of native framework/hardware support. Open questions include whether the frequency-suppression property has a clean spectral-theoretic explanation, whether it generalizes to non-stationary or multivariate-coupled settings, and whether dedicated hypercomplex hardware could close the efficiency gap and make the approach competitive with lightweight baselines like DLinear.
