## Problem

Existing RLVR methods for LLM reasoning rely on local token-level diversity (entropy bonuses, high-entropy branching) to encourage exploration, but these dithering strategies are insufficient for the vast, high-dimensional textual state space where reward sparsity creates 'reward deserts.' The paper identifies that global sequence-level diversity across a group of rollouts is the key driver of deep exploration and versatile reasoning, yet efficient mechanisms to explicitly optimize this diversity during RL training remain largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a math problem prompt and generates G=8 candidate reasoning chains per prompt during training, each structured as a Thought section (iterative analysis) followed by a Solution section with a boxed final answer. Diversity is computed pairwise across the group of rollouts using two lightweight metrics: Textual Diversity (inverted BLEU n-gram overlap) and Equational Diversity (ratio of unique LaTeX formulas not appearing in other group members). At inference, the model produces a single reasoning chain and answer, evaluated via Pass@1 or Pass@k across k independent samples.

## Method

DIVER builds on GRPO and introduces a potential-based intrinsic reward R_int(s,a,s') = γd(s') − d(s) where d(·) is the sequence-level diversity of a response within its rollout group. A key telescoping property collapses the per-step intrinsic reward over a full trajectory to simply γ^T·d(final response), eliminating the need to compute diversity at intermediate states. To preserve optimal policy invariance (Theorem 1), the shaping follows Ng et al.'s potential-based framework, guaranteeing that the transformed MDP shares the same optimal policy as the original. Two anti-hacking heuristics are applied: conditional shaping (diversity reward only for correct responses, gated by an indicator function) and balanced shaping (clipping the diversity reward to [0, σ] with a decaying λ). The augmented reward r'_i = r_i + λ·r_int^i·I(r_i) replaces the original binary reward in GRPO's group-normalized advantage computation, with no additional model parameters or forward passes required.

## Results

On six in-domain math benchmarks (AIME24/25, AMC, MATH-500, Minerva, OlympiadBench), DIVER-MIX achieves an average of 43.1, outperforming the best exploration baseline Entropy-RL (41.8) by +1.3 points and the strongest RLVR method OpenReasoner-Zero (41.0) by +2.1 points. On out-of-domain tasks (ARC-c, GPQA*, MMLU-Pro), DIVER-MIX reaches 58.8 average, surpassing Entropy-RL by +2.8 and OpenReasoner-Zero by +7.2 points. On Pass@32 (the metric most sensitive to exploration breadth), DIVER achieves 50.0 on AIME25, a +6.7 point improvement over Entropy-RL, and 69.0 on OlympiadBench (+2.7 over the best baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIVER consistently outperforms all six baselines on both in-domain and out-of-domain tasks. Against the closest competitor Entropy-RL (action-level token diversity), DIVER-MIX gains +1.3 points in-domain and +2.8 points out-of-domain, with the largest gap on GPQA* (+2.5) and OlympiadBench (+2.5). Compared to GRPO w/ Clip-higher (undirected exploration via relaxed clipping), DIVER improves by +2.4 in-domain and +3.6 out-of-domain, indicating that merely loosening clip constraints without strategic direction is insufficient. Versus Pass@k Training (within-group bootstrap sampling), DIVER leads by +1.6 in-domain and +3.5 out-of-domain, showing that explicit diversity optimization surpasses implicit multi-sample rewards. Against established RLVR methods, DIVER outperforms OpenReasoner-Zero by +2.1 in-domain and +7.2 out-of-domain, and PRIME-Zero by +2.4 in-domain and +17.4 out-of-domain. On Pass@32 (exploration breadth), DIVER's advantage is most pronounced: +6.7 on AIME25 over Entropy-RL and +2.7 on OlympiadBench over GRPO w/ Clip-higher, confirming that global sequence-level diversity unlocks higher reasoning capacity beyond what local token-level strategies achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIVER extends the intrinsic-reward exploration paradigm from classical RL (count-based bonuses, information-gain rewards) to LLM reasoning, building directly on GRPO as the base RLVR algorithm. It departs from concurrent work on local token-level diversity (Entropy-RL, Clip-higher, Pass@k Training) by shifting the diversity signal from individual token uncertainty to global sequence-level dissimilarity across a group of rollouts. The potential-based reward shaping mechanism (Ng et al., 1999) is repurposed here with an elegant telescoping property: the per-step intrinsic rewards collapse to a single term equal to the final response's diversity, avoiding costly intermediate computations while guaranteeing optimal policy invariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that global sequence-level diversity is positively correlated with reasoning capacity and generalization, offering a principled lever for RLVR practitioners who face reward sparsity in hard math problems. The conditional shaping heuristic (rewarding diversity only on correct responses) is a practical safeguard against reward hacking that could be adopted in other RLVR pipelines. Limitations include restriction to single-turn math reasoning, reliance on BLEU as a textual diversity proxy (validated but approximate), and the fact that gains, while consistent, are moderate (1–3 points in-domain, ~3 points out-of-domain) over already-strong baselines. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to multi-turn agent settings and whether more powerful diversity metrics (e.g., LLM-as-a-judge) would yield further gains.
