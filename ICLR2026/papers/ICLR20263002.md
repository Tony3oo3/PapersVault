## Problem

Existing model-based unlearning methods reduce the forget–retain trade-off to a scalarized single objective with fixed weights, producing unstable training dynamics, utility collapse under aggressive forgetting, and sharp performance degradation on hard-to-unlearn samples where forget and retain gradients are strongly entangled. This symmetric treatment fails to capture the asymmetric priority inherent in unlearning: forgetting must be enforced as a non-negotiable constraint before utility restoration is meaningful, yet scalarization cannot adapt its emphasis across optimization stages.

## Input / Output

OFMU operates on a pre-trained model (LLM or vision classifier) and consumes two disjoint data partitions—the forget set D_f and retain set D_r—via independent mini-batches of size B sampled at each inner and outer step, requiring no auxiliary data or reference models. The output is a single set of updated parameters θ^(K) after K outer iterations, directly deployable as the unlearned model. The framework is modality-agnostic, applied to LLaMA-2-7B and LLaMA-3.2-1B for text unlearning on TOFU/WMDP and to ResNet-style backbones for CIFAR-10/100 image classification.

## Method

OFMU formulates unlearning as a bilevel optimization problem: the inner loop maximizes a forgetting objective Φ(θ) = L_f(θ) − β·Sim(∇L_f, ∇L_r) via T steps of gradient ascent, while the outer loop minimizes a penalty objective F(θ) = L_r(θ) + ρ‖∇Φ(θ)‖² via gradient descent. The key mechanism is a similarity-aware penalty that explicitly decorrelates forget and retain gradients, preventing destructive interference that causes collateral utility loss on hard samples. A monotonically increasing penalty parameter ρ progressively enforces inner-objective stationarity, and the outer gradient is computed using only a Hessian-vector product (via Pearlmutter's trick) rather than a full Hessian, keeping per-iteration cost at Θ(Bd). The two-loop design avoids full inner convergence by using a small fixed T (5–10 steps), and the paper proves convergence to ε-stationary points of F in both convex and non-convex regimes with explicit dependence on K, T, ρ, and gradient noise variance.

## Results

On TOFU (LLaMA-2-7B), OFMU achieves FQ=0.44, MU=0.63, FTR=0.68 on forget01, and maintains MU=0.65 on forget05 where Gradient Ascent collapses to MU=0.00. On WMDP, OFMU reaches 72.8% unlearning efficacy on Biosecurity, 70.4% on Cybersecurity, and 74.6% MMLU utility accuracy, outperforming all baselines. On CIFAR-10 class-wise forgetting, OFMU achieves 81.51% UA, 93.51% RA, and 59.76 MIA-Efficacy; on the harder random forgetting setting, it reaches 7.71% UA, slightly exceeding retraining (6.79%) while all other approximate methods collapse below 1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gradient Ascent (GA) and GradDiff, OFMU decisively wins on utility preservation: GA collapses to MU=0.00 on forget05/forget10 while OFMU sustains MU=0.65/0.61, and OFMU's FTR (0.82/0.77) far exceeds GA's near-zero values. Against preference-based methods (NPO, SimNPO, IdkDPO), OFMU achieves higher FQ on forget05 (0.14 vs. 0.09/1.10e-106/4.00e-6) and forget10 (0.42 vs. 0.42/1.52e-198/5.40e-13) while maintaining comparable MU, indicating more reliable forgetting without overfitting. Against RMU, OFMU trades a small MU advantage (RMU: 0.62 vs. OFMU: 0.63 on forget01) for substantially better FQ on harder scenarios (0.14 vs. 9.61e-10 on forget05), showing RMU under-forgets. On CIFAR-10 class-wise forgetting, Influence Unlearning (IU) achieves higher UA (89.31% vs. 81.51%) but OFMU wins on MIA-Efficacy (59.76 vs. 55.22) and dramatically outperforms IU on random forgetting (7.71% vs. 0.62% UA). Fisher Forget (FF) is outperformed by OFMU on both UA (81.51% vs. 79.71%) and MIA (59.76 vs. 46.38) in class-wise CIFAR-10. On WMDP, OFMU leads all baselines by 1.5–5.5 points in unlearning efficacy, with the largest margins over NPO and SimNPO on Cybersecurity (70.4% vs. 64.9%/66.2%). Overall, OFMU consistently achieves the best or second-best score across all metrics, with its key advantage being stability on hard-to-unlearn samples where baselines exhibit sharp performance degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes bilevel optimization—a structural paradigm well-established in meta-learning, hyperparameter tuning, and adversarial robustness—for the machine unlearning problem, where prior model-based methods (GA, GradDiff, NPO, SimNPO, RMU) rely on scalarized weighted-sum objectives. The key conceptual departure is replacing the flat weighted combination of forget and retain losses with a hierarchical inner-maximization/outer-minimization structure, augmented by a similarity-aware cosine penalty that decorrelates the two gradient directions. The penalty-based single-level reformulation and two-loop algorithm draw on classical constrained-optimization techniques (penalty methods, Hessian-vector products via Pearlmutter's trick) rather than introducing new optimization primitives, but their application to unlearning with convergence guarantees in both convex and non-convex regimes is novel within this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in LLM deployment—regulatory unlearning requests—by showing that the instability and utility collapse plaguing scalarized unlearning methods stem from conflating two asymmetric objectives, and that a principled hierarchical structure mitigates this. Convergence guarantees (rare in the unlearning literature) and the low UDI–utility-drop coupling (0.29 vs. 0.71 for GA) provide evidence that the method generalizes to hard-to-forget samples where baselines fail. Open questions include continual/sequential unlearning, adaptive penalty schedules, scaling to frontier-scale models, and extension to multimodal settings, all of which the authors flag as future work.
