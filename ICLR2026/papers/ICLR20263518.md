## Problem

Existing preference alignment for text-to-3D generation relies on paired multi-view 2D image annotations to train reward models, which are expensive to collect and provide only view-dependent supervision. This 2D bias causes geometric artifacts such as Janus faces and structural incompleteness, because rewards derived from rendered images can favor assets that look plausible from some viewpoints while ignoring global 3D structural flaws. The paper addresses this by introducing the first 3D geometry-aware reward model trained on unpaired preference data, eliminating the need for costly paired comparisons.

## Input / Output

The generation pipeline takes a text prompt and produces a 3D asset (NeRF/SDF implicit field or explicit mesh) via SDS-based optimization over 20,000 steps with a coarse-to-fine rendering schedule (64×64 then 256×256). The RewardCS model consumes a 3D mesh partitioned into 256 non-overlapping patches of 64 triangular faces each (with 10-dimensional per-face geometric features) alongside a text prompt, and outputs a scalar reward score. The differentiable meshization step converts implicit fields to meshes via DMTet, and an adaptive mesh fusion algorithm reorganizes topology to match the 256×64 patch constraint while preserving geometric fidelity.

## Method

DreamCS comprises three components: (1) 3D-MeshPref, a 30,000+ sample unpaired 3D mesh dataset with LLM-scored and human-refined preference labels partitioned by a 3.5–4.0 threshold; (2) RewardCS, a reward model using MeshMAE (masked ViT autoencoder) for 3D geometry encoding and MeshCLIP for text encoding, fused via cross-attention with a learnable class token, trained with a combined MSE regression loss and a Cauchy-Schwarz divergence loss that separates preferred and dispreferred embedding distributions in RKHS without requiring paired comparisons; and (3) a progressive reward guidance scheme that integrates RewardCS into SDS optimization via differentiable DMTet meshization, where the reward weight α(t) increases linearly from α_min to α_max over training to balance early shape exploration with late-stage geometric refinement. The paper proves asymptotic equivalence between CS-divergence training on unpaired data and standard paired preference supervision at rate O(1/√m + 1/√n).

## Results

On the GPTEval3D benchmark (110 prompts), RewardCS consistently improves all text-to-3D backbones across CLIP score (CP), VisionReward (VR), and the proposed 3D Geometry-Asset Alignment Reward (GA). The strongest gains appear in VR and GA: Fantasia3D+RewardCS achieves the best VR (-1.01 vs. -3.03 baseline) and GA (3.33 vs. 2.95), while Magic3D+RewardCS reaches GA 3.22 and VR -1.58. In user studies, MVDream+RewardCS scores 3.72 on 3D Plausibility and 3.59 on Geometry-Texture Alignment (vs. 2.87 and 2.91 for vanilla MVDream), and reduces the Janus artifact proportion from 0.52 to 0.30.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RewardCS outperforms all 2D-based preference alignment methods (Reward3D/DreamReward and DreamDPO) on the 3D-specific GA metric across every backbone: e.g., on MVDream, RewardCS achieves GA 2.96 vs. 2.87 for both Reward3D and DreamDPO, and VR -2.11 vs. -3.12 (Reward3D) and -2.13 (DreamDPO). Against vanilla baselines, RewardCS yields the largest absolute gains on VR (e.g., Fantasia3D improves from -3.03 to -1.01, a 2.02-point gain) and GA (e.g., Magic3D from 2.65 to 3.22). In the Janus artifact user study, RewardCS reduces the proportion of flawed assets from 0.52 to 0.30 on MVDream and from 0.61 to 0.41 on DreamFusion, outperforming both Reward3D (0.44/0.53) and DreamDPO (0.43/0.50). In extended comparisons (Appendix F), RewardCS also beats MVControl (GA 2.96 vs. 2.86, Janus 0.31 vs. 0.40), ProlificDreamer+Reward3D (GA 3.04 vs. 2.89), DreamCraft3D+Reward3D (GA 3.13 vs. 2.90), and TRELLIS+Reward3D (GA 3.02 vs. 2.90), confirming consistent superiority over both 2D reward guidance and 3D controllable generation methods. The combined 2D+3D guidance (Reward3D+RewardCS) achieves the highest GA on DreamFusion (3.22) and MVDream (3.08), indicating the two signals are complementary rather than redundant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDS-based text-to-3D generation paradigm (DreamFusion, MVDream, Magic3D) by adding a 3D geometry-aware reward signal, departing from prior preference-alignment work (DreamReward, DreamDPO) that relies on paired 2D multi-view renderings and 2D reward models like ImageReward. The core methodological departure is the use of Cauchy-Schwarz divergence as a distributional training objective for the reward model, enabling learning from unpaired 3D mesh data rather than explicit preferred/dispreferred pairs. The encoder architecture repurposes MeshMAE (a masked ViT autoencoder for meshes) and MeshCLIP (a cross-modal text-mesh encoder) in a novel reward-model context, while the differentiable meshization via DMTet and adaptive mesh fusion are new engineering contributions to bridge implicit 3D fields and explicit mesh-based reward evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of 3D-MeshPref (30K+ unpaired 3D meshes with LLM-scored and human-refined preference labels) and the theoretical guarantee of asymptotic equivalence between paired and unpaired CS-divergence training provide a scalable pathway to 3D preference alignment without the prohibitive cost of paired annotation. The consistent reduction in Janus artifacts (e.g., 0.52→0.30 on MVDream) and improvements across diverse backbones (implicit NeRF, explicit mesh, multi-stage pipelines) demonstrate practical utility. However, the evaluation relies on open-source LLMs (MiniCPM-o) due to budget constraints, no standardized Janus-artifact benchmark exists, and performance remains bounded by the underlying text-to-3D backbone quality—open questions that limit the generality of the conclusions.
