## Problem

Graph neural networks require separate training on labeled data for each new graph, preventing universal generalization across datasets that differ in homophily levels, community structures, and feature distributions. This paper asks whether a single model can learn to perform node classification on arbitrary graphs without any graph-specific training, by learning posterior predictive distributions from synthetic graph priors alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262426/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, NodePFN takes a real-world graph (adjacency matrix + node feature matrix) with a predefined train/test split, where training nodes provide both features and labels as context while test nodes provide only features. Node features of varying dimensionality are zero-padded to a fixed maximum with a normalization factor, and 32 ensemble inputs with shuffled feature orderings and scalings are aggregated. The output is a calibrated posterior predictive distribution (softmax over classes) for each test node, produced in a single forward pass with no gradient updates or fine-tuning.

## Method

NodePFN extends the prior-data fitted network (PFN) paradigm to graphs by pre-training on ~250,000 synthetic graphs drawn from a 50/50 mixture of Erdős-Rényi networks and contextual stochastic block models with homophily sampled uniformly from 0.1 to 0.9, while feature-label dependencies are generated via structural causal models (random MLPs converted to DAGs). The architecture is a 12-layer dual-branch design: a context-query attention branch (self-attention among labeled train nodes, cross-attention from test nodes to train nodes) runs in parallel with a local GCN message-passing branch using the symmetrically normalized adjacency matrix, and the two branches are fused via residual connections with LayerNorm. Training minimizes cross-entropy over synthetic graphs with random train/test partitions, and at inference the frozen model processes any real graph in one forward pass, requiring no dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning beyond a small ensemble/smoothing search.

## Results

NodePFN achieves a 71.27% average accuracy across 23 real-world node classification benchmarks using a single pre-trained model, with an average ranking of 1.70 (best). On homophily graphs (13 datasets), it reaches 77.39% average accuracy, outperforming GCN (73.05%), GAT (74.39%), and the best GraphAny variant (71.45%). On heterophily graphs (10 datasets), NodePFN achieves 65.14% average accuracy, substantially exceeding GCN (58.84%), GAT (59.11%), and the best GraphAny variant (61.62%). The model requires no per-dataset training, performing inference in a single forward pass.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NodePFN outperforms all standard GNN baselines (GCN, GAT) by 4.6–6.4 percentage points in overall average accuracy, with the largest gaps on heterophily graphs (e.g., Wisconsin: 79.22% vs. GCN 37.25%, GAT 52.94%). Against GraphAny (Zhao et al., 2025), which requires dataset-specific training, NodePFN achieves 71.27% vs. 66.24% (best variant) overall, and notably maintains consistent performance across both homophily and heterophily regimes where GraphAny's accuracy varies significantly depending on its training source. Among training-free methods (Table 2), NodePFN dominates all baselines: SGC (78.20% on Cora), HGC (22.50%), LabelProp (60.30%), and TFGNN (60.03%) all fall well below NodePFN's 82.06% on Cora. Against TabPFN (the tabular PFN without graph structure), NodePFN improves average accuracy from 55.62% to 77.39% on homophily and from 53.47% to 65.14% on heterophily, confirming the necessity of explicit graph topology modeling. On heterophily-specific GNNs (Table 14), NodePFN wins on 7 of 9 datasets, surpassing H2GCN, GPRGNN, and FAGCN without any dataset-specific training. On GLBench (Table 15), NodePFN is competitive with LLM-based graph foundation models (OFA, LLaGA, GraphText) on Cora and WikiCS while requiring no text attributes or language model dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Prior-Data Fitted Network (PFN) paradigm from Müller et al. (2022) and its tabular instantiation TabPFN (Hollmann et al., 2023) to the graph domain, introducing the first PFN for node classification. It departs from standard GNNs (GCN, GAT) that require per-graph training, from LLM-based graph foundation models (OFA, GraphGPT) that depend on text attributes, and from inductive frameworks like GraphAny that still require training on specific source datasets. The key architectural departure is a dual-branch design combining context-query attention (inherited from PFN) with local GCN message passing, trained on synthetic graphs generated from contextual SBMs with controlled homophily and Erdős-Rényi networks, rather than on real data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single model pre-trained exclusively on ~250K synthetic graphs can achieve 71.27% average accuracy across 23 diverse real-world benchmarks—outperforming per-graph-trained GNNs and inductive baselines—validates that universal graph learning patterns are learnable from synthetic priors, establishing a new training paradigm for graph ML. The strong heterophily performance (65.14%) is particularly significant since GNNs traditionally degrade sharply in low-homophily regimes. Key limitations remain: fixed maximum class count (20) and feature dimension, quadratic attention complexity restricting scalability to large graphs, and the ~6 GPU-hour pre-training cost. Open questions include whether the approach scales to graphs with millions of nodes, whether it can handle open-vocabulary or multi-label settings, and how it might combine with semantic information from text-attributed graphs.
