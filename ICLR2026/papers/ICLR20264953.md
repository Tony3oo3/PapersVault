## Problem

Existing constrained sampling methods face a fundamental trade-off: projected Langevin enforces constraints exactly but traps dynamics in limited regions under non-convex constraints, while penalty methods encourage but never guarantee strict satisfaction. The paper proves via Lagrangian duality in Wasserstein space that the constrained sampling problem admits no strong duality, showing that standard dual and penalty-based approaches provably cannot sample from the target conditional distribution. No prior method achieves both strict constraint satisfaction and unbiased exploration simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a zero-shot plug-in on any pretrained generative model (diffusion, EBM, score-based), requiring only the score function and a projection operator onto the constraint set—no retraining or differentiable constraint model is needed. A key design feature is that when sampling occurs in a latent space (e.g., Fourier coefficients) while constraints are defined in physical space, CASAL performs the projection solely in physical space via a modified splitting constraint Ax = z, avoiding the need to invert the decoder or define a projection in latent space. Outputs are samples that lie exactly on the constraint set, with the dual variable correcting for finite-penalty bias.

## Method

CASAL splits the sample variable x into a pair (x, z) where z is constrained to C, then relaxes the coupling x = z to an expectation constraint with a quadratic penalty ρ‖x−z‖²/2, yielding a primal-dual saddle point problem in Wasserstein space where strong duality holds (unlike the original projection problem). The stochastic iterations alternate three updates: x follows a Langevin step biased toward z via a coupling term, z is projected onto C via a proximal step, and a dual variable μ integrates the residual x−z to correct bias—making CASAL a stochastic analog of proximal ADMM operating directly on coupled samples rather than in distribution space. The variational analysis in P₂(ℝᵈ) yields explicit mixing time rates of O(ln t / √t) for the time-averaged iterate, and the relaxation error is bounded by (1/ρ)·D(p_C‖p), recovering the exact conditional distribution as ρ→∞. The framework extends to time-dependent diffusion models by applying the split-augmented updates at each noise level as a drop-in replacement for standard Langevin steps, with ρ optionally annealed along the diffusion schedule.

## Results

On the Darcy flow PDE experiment, CASAL achieves 10% reconstruction error with 2×10⁻⁵% constraint violation, compared to 22% (projected diffusion) and 23% (penalty) reconstruction error. On Navier-Stokes, CASAL reaches 8% reconstruction error with 10⁻⁵% constraint violation, and on the 2D Poisson equation it achieves 5% reconstruction error with 2×10⁻⁷% constraint violation. In the energy-constrained field generation task, CASAL is the only method that both strictly satisfies the non-convex energy constraint and correctly samples the conditional distribution (matching the negative mode), while projected Langevin gets trapped in the wrong mode and penalty methods only enforce energy on average. In the Burgers data assimilation task, CASAL yields the lowest ℓ₂ estimation error while strictly preserving mass and energy conservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASAL outperforms all baselines across the three main tasks. Against projected Langevin/diffusion (Christopher et al., 2024; Zampini et al., 2025), CASAL wins on exploration quality: projected methods satisfy constraints exactly but trap samples in the wrong mode (field generation) or introduce high-frequency artifacts (data assimilation) and distorted paths (optimal control), while CASAL maintains correct sampling of the conditional distribution. Against soft penalty/guidance methods (Carvalho et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025), CASAL wins on constraint satisfaction: penalty methods show 20–25% constraint violation on PDE tasks and only enforce energy on average in field generation, whereas CASAL achieves ~10⁻⁵% violation. Against unconstrained diffusion, CASAL eliminates the drift in mass and energy that degrades long-horizon forecasts in data assimilation. Against 3D-Var (Gaussian prior), the diffusion-based methods (including CASAL) better regularize complex state structure, with CASAL further reducing error by enforcing physical invariants. In the optimal control feasibility problem, CASAL achieves significantly higher ADMM success rates than both penalty guidance (which allows obstacle penetration) and projected diffusion (which produces dynamically infeasible paths), as the splitting mechanism preserves both constraint satisfaction and trajectory plausibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the variational formulation of Langevin dynamics in Wasserstein space (Jordan et al., 1998) and the ADMM/variable-splitting framework from constrained optimization (Gabay & Mercier, 1976; Boyd et al., 2011), repurposing them for stochastic sampling. It departs from projected Langevin (Bubeck et al., 2015; Christopher et al., 2024), which is limited to convex constraints, and from penalty/guidance methods (Ho & Salimans, 2022; Fishman et al., 2023), which do not enforce constraints strictly. A key conceptual departure is the proof that standard Lagrangian duality fails for the natural constrained sampling formulation (Proposition 3.2), explaining why penalty methods fundamentally cannot achieve strict feasibility, and motivating the variable-splitting relaxation that restores strong duality. The algorithm is positioned as a stochastic analog of proximal ADMM in sample space, extending Chamon et al. (2024) from average-constraint enforcement to hard constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CASAL provides the first principled, zero-shot method that simultaneously enforces hard (potentially non-convex) physical constraints and preserves unbiased exploration in pretrained diffusion models, addressing a gap that projected and penalty-based approaches leave open. The duality-gap analysis offers a diagnostic tool explaining *why* prior methods fail, which is valuable beyond this specific algorithm. Limitations include the requirement of a (possibly approximate) projection operator, convergence guarantees that assume convex constraint sets, and a finite-ρ bias that is corrected only in expectation. Open directions flagged by the authors include a natively time-dependent constrained variational formulation for diffusion SDEs and scalable approximate/parallelized projections for very high-dimensional physical systems.
