## Problem

Existing EEG foundation models (LaBraM, EEGPT, CBraMod) are pre-trained exclusively on unimodal EEG data, so their representations are never optimized to align with the structure of acoustic stimuli, fundamentally limiting their transferability to auditory decoding tasks. The paper identifies that simply concatenating or linearly projecting modalities is insufficient—deep, fine-grained cross-modal alignment between neural activity and auditory content is required for robust and generalizable auditory perception decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263687/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes paired 2-second EEG epochs (bandpass-filtered 1–40 Hz, resampled to 200 Hz, per-channel z-score normalized) and 2-second audio waveforms (16 kHz mono, peak-normalized). The EEG encoder handles heterogeneous channel counts (8–255 channels across datasets) via channel-independent temporal patching with learnable 10-20 spatial embeddings, eliminating the need for bad-channel interpolation. The primary output is a pair of aligned embeddings (E_aligned, A_aligned) in a shared cosine-similarity space; lightweight task heads then map these to attended-speaker labels, emotion classes, or retrieved music pieces.

## Method

MindMix employs a two-stage training strategy. Stage 1 pre-trains a 12-layer Transformer EEG encoder on 3,500+ hours of diverse EEG data using a multi-task self-supervised objective that combines masked token prediction (with codebook quantization into discrete neural tokens) and concurrent Fourier spectrum reconstruction. Stage 2 aligns this encoder with a Wav2Vec 2.0 audio encoder via the proposed CALRA module on 100+ hours of paired EEG-audio data, optimized end-to-end with a directed EEG-to-audio InfoNCE contrastive loss. CALRA implements a 'refine-then-contrast' strategy with three synergistic components: a Type-specific Aligner that routes embeddings through stimulus-type-conditional transformations (speech vs. music), a Bi-directional Cross-Attention layer enabling each modality's global embedding to retrieve complementary context from the other, and a Shared Low-Rank Alignment that enforces bilinear fusion via an element-wise product in a 128-dimensional bottleneck before residual feedback. This multiplicative fusion captures fine-grained cross-modal dependencies that linear projections or concatenation-based baselines cannot model, while preserving the dual-stream architecture required for efficient cross-modal retrieval.

## Results

MindMix achieves near-perfect performance on Speech AAD (99.82% Balanced Accuracy on KUL, 99.93% on DTU, 100.00% on ESAA), substantially outperforming the strongest task-specific baseline DARNet (94.81% on KUL) and all unimodal EEG foundation models (LaBraM: 63.30%, EEGPT: 66.42%, CBraMod: 68.42% on KUL). On emotion analysis, MindMix reaches 88.78% Balanced Accuracy on HR-EEG4EMO, surpassing the best baseline DBPNet (82.74%) by over 6 points. For music retrieval on MAD-EEG, MindMix achieves 94.75% Duo Accuracy, edging out MusicAAD (94.25%) and significantly outperforming other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MindMix decisively outperforms all 10 baselines across every task. On Speech AAD (KUL), it achieves 99.82% Balanced Accuracy versus DARNet (94.81%), DBPNet (93.57%), and MusicAAD (93.18%)—the strongest task-specific models—while unimodal EEG foundation models (LaBraM 63.30%, EEGPT 66.42%, CBraMod 68.42%) fall 25–37 points behind, revealing that their non-auditory pre-training renders them poorly suited for auditory decoding. On emotion analysis (HR-EEG4EMO), MindMix (88.78%) surpasses DBPNet (82.74%) by 6.04 points and CBraMod (72.85%) by 15.93 points. On music retrieval (MAD-EEG), MindMix (94.75% Duo) narrowly beats MusicAAD (94.25%) but leads AADNet (88.24%) and DBPNet (78.49%) by large margins. A key finding is that unimodal foundation models consistently underperform task-specific SOTA models, whereas MindMix's multimodal alignment strategy provides a dramatic leap over both categories, validating that deep cross-modal coupling (not mere modality concatenation) is the critical factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

MindMix sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: unimodal EEG foundation models (LaBraM, EEGPT, CBraMod) that learn general-purpose neural representations via self-supervised objectives, and task-specific auditory decoding models (DBPNet, DARNet, MusicAAD) that achieve strong performance on narrow benchmarks but fail to generalize. The key departure is the introduction of explicit multimodal contrastive alignment between EEG and audio, drawing on the CLIP paradigm and low-rank bilinear fusion techniques from multimodal NLP/VQA (Fukui et al., Zadeh et al., Liu et al.), but applied here to the neural-acoustic domain for the first time. The EEG encoder's dual self-supervised objective (masked token prediction + Fourier spectrum reconstruction) is a novel combination, and the CALRA module's type-specific routing addresses stimulus heterogeneity in a way prior cross-modal alignment work has not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that unimodal EEG foundation models, despite their scale, are fundamentally misaligned for auditory decoding and actually underperform task-specific models—a finding that challenges the assumption that larger unimodal pre-training automatically transfers to all downstream tasks. The multimodal paradigm (reframing AAD as a neural-acoustic matching problem rather than binary classification) offers a structurally different and more robust approach, as evidenced by the between-trial evaluation where task-specific models collapse but MindMix maintains high accuracy. A key limitation is the scarcity of paired EEG-audio data (~100 hours), which precludes investigation of scaling laws and likely caps the model's ceiling; the field needs substantially larger paired corpora to fully realize this paradigm.
