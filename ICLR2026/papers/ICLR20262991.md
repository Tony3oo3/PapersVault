## Problem

RL-based safe control optimizes expected returns over a user-specified initial-condition distribution, which misaligns with the goal of worst-case safety: policies perform well on frequently sampled states but fail catastrophically on rare yet feasible ones. A natural fix is robust optimization over a parameter set, but whether that set is feasible (a safe policy exists for every parameter) is unknown a priori, and including infeasible parameters renders the robust problem degenerate. The paper addresses how to simultaneously identify the largest feasible parameter subset and learn a safe policy over it.

## Input / Output

The method takes the current system state s_k ∈ R^{n_s} (or RGB images for Lander tasks) and a parameter vector θ that jointly specifies the initial state s_0(θ), the dynamics f_θ, and the safety function h_θ defining the unsafe set. It produces a parameter-conditioned policy π_θ: S×Θ→A that outputs safe control actions, a feasibility classifier q_ψ(f=1|θ) trained via variational inference, and an expanding estimate of the feasible parameter set D_f. The reward is a negative indicator −1{h_θ(s_k)>0} with episode termination upon entering the unsafe set, making the objective a sum-over-time MDP compatible with standard on-policy RL.

## Method

Feasibility-Guided Exploration (FGE) interleaves three mechanisms each iteration. First, a feasibility classifier is trained by minimizing forward KL to a mixture distribution p_mix that upweights empirically safe parameters (reliable positives from D_f) and downweights noisy on-policy labels, yielding zero false positives and a controllable false-negative rate governed by the mixing coefficient α and the density ratio ζ. Second, the policy is updated via PPO on a rehearsal buffer of worst-case feasible parameters, where the adversary's best-response is computed by a learned policy-conditioned classifier rather than an adversarial policy, and the policy update follows a follow-the-regularized-leader (FTRL) scheme with quadratic regularization to ensure sublinear regret and stable convergence to the saddle point. Third, an explore distribution uses rejection sampling against the feasibility classifier to draw parameters currently estimated as infeasible, breaking the negative feedback loop where a weak policy keeps D_f small. The reset distribution is a mixture of base (uniform), explore, and rehearsal components, and newly discovered safe parameters are added to D_f after each episode, progressively expanding the feasible set estimate.

## Results

FGE achieves over 50% more coverage than the best existing baseline for challenging initial conditions across all benchmark environments. In the performance profile (Fig. 7a), FGE dominates all other methods at every safety-rate threshold, and in the IQM-over-all-tasks comparison (Fig. 7b), FGE attains the highest safety rate by rendering the most hard parameters safe without losing coverage on easy ones. On the harder environments, other methods underperform FGE by 40% to 90% in coverage gain, and with the exception of ToyLevels, almost all unique safe parameters are discovered exclusively by FGE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGE outperforms all eight baselines on safety rate and coverage gain across every environment. Compared to Domain Randomization (DR), FGE achieves the largest coverage gain relative to DR in all environments, while DR serves as the reference for parameter difficulty. RARL achieves the second-best coverage gain in three of five environments but consistently suffers the worst coverage loss due to training instability. VDS and PLR (curriculum learning methods) fail to concentrate samples efficiently on rare, difficult parameters — e.g., VDS crashes on the rare overtake condition in Dubins and fails in the Hopper gap region, whereas FGE allocates vastly more samples there via rejection sampling. Among UED methods, PAIRED's antagonist policy yields zero regret signal on hard-but-feasible parameters, FARR is computationally expensive and may return suboptimal solutions, ACCEL performs poorly because it requires maximum-length rollouts reducing total updates, and SFL assigns zero learnability to currently unsuccessful parameters, preventing exploration of difficult but feasible regions. FGE's feasibility classifier and explore distribution collectively enable it to surpass all these heuristics, with the next-best method varying by environment (RARL or PLR) but never closing the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis, robust reinforcement learning (RARL), and saddle-point optimization from online learning. It departs from standard RL's distributional objective by formulating a worst-case robust problem, and from RARL by not assuming the full parameter space is feasible—instead, it jointly discovers the feasible set and solves the robust control problem over it. The exploration mechanism draws on curriculum learning and unsupervised environment design (UED), but replaces their regret-based heuristics with a principled feasibility classifier trained via a mixture distribution that guarantees zero false positives. The saddle-point solver uses FTRL from online learning rather than the gradient-descent-ascent dynamics that RARL implicitly approximates, yielding improved stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mismatch between RL's expected-return objective and HJ's worst-case safety guarantee is a fundamental gap for safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, aerospace), and this paper provides a practical, well-motivated resolution that achieves over 50% more coverage than the best prior method across diverse environments. Key limitations include the assumption of deterministic dynamics (stochastic safety via chance constraints remains open) and the use of approximate best-response oracles that prevent full theoretical convergence guarantees. The finding that recent UED methods (PAIRED, FARR, ACCEL, SFL) all fail to efficiently concentrate samples on hard-but-feasible parameters, despite their regret-based motivations, suggests that the feasibility structure of safe control problems is not well-captured by generic regret approximations—a useful negative result for the UED community.
