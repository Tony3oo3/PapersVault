## Problem

Classical rate-distortion theory assumes reconstructions remain close to the observed input distribution, which is misaligned for restoration tasks where the encoder sees a degraded sample (noisy, low-resolution) but the decoder must produce output from a distinct clean target distribution. Moreover, standard formulations ignore downstream task utility such as classification accuracy. This paper unifies cross-domain optimal transport, compression rate, and classification constraints into a single information-theoretic framework, providing closed-form tradeoffs that were previously unavailable.

## Input / Output

The encoder receives a degraded image X drawn from p_X (e.g., a noisy or 4× downsampled image) along with a shared pseudorandom seed U that both encoder and decoder can reproduce. The decoder outputs a reconstruction Y drawn from the clean target distribution p_Y, which is unpaired with X—only unpaired noisy and clean samples are available during training. The compressed representation Z must satisfy a rate budget H(Z|U) ≤ R, and the reconstruction must preserve classification utility via H(S|Y) ≤ C, where S is the semantic label.

## Method

The core contribution is formulating cross-domain lossy compression as rate- and classification-constrained optimal transport: finding a coupling p_{X,Y} with fixed marginals that minimizes expected distortion subject to a rate constraint and a conditional-entropy bound on the label. With shared common randomness, the one-shot problem reduces to a deterministic transport plan (Theorem 1), yielding closed-form DRC/RDC functions for Bernoulli sources under Hamming distortion and Gaussian sources under MSE in the asymptotic regime. The framework is further extended to a DRPC setting incorporating KL and squared Wasserstein perception divergences, with extremality results showing jointly Gaussian reconstructions are optimal. For practical implementation, a stochastic autoencoder is trained end-to-end with universal quantization (realizing shared randomness), an entropy model for rate estimation, a WGAN discriminator to align the reconstruction distribution with p_Y via a Wasserstein-1 penalty, and a classifier head enforcing the cross-entropy upper bound on H(S|Y), all optimized under an unsupervised, unpaired training protocol.

## Results

The paper validates its theoretical DRC/DRPC tradeoffs through deep end-to-end compression experiments on super-resolution (MNIST, 4×), denoising (SVHN with σ=20, CIFAR-10, ImageNet, KODAK), and inpainting (SVHN). Results are presented as rate–distortion–accuracy tradeoff curves rather than single-point comparisons: at low rates (R=4), reconstructions are blurry and classification accuracy is degraded, while at higher rates (R=32), both MSE and perceptual quality improve substantially. The empirical curves closely match the closed-form theoretical predictions derived for Bernoulli and Gaussian sources, confirming the consistency between the constrained optimal transport framework and learned codec performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing compression or restoration methods; the `compares_to` set is empty. Instead, the empirical evaluation serves to validate the theoretical DRC/RDC/DRPC characterizations: the learned stochastic autoencoder (with WGAN discriminator, universal quantization, and classifier head) is trained to satisfy the rate, distortion, and classification constraints simultaneously, and the resulting tradeoff curves are checked against the closed-form expressions from Theorems 2, 4, and 7. The key empirical claim is qualitative agreement — that increasing rate monotonically reduces MSE and improves classification accuracy in a manner consistent with the piecewise-linear (Bernoulli) or analytic (Gaussian) theoretical predictions — rather than a head-to-head numerical comparison with prior codecs or denoisers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Liu et al. (2022), who formulated cross-domain lossy compression as entropy-constrained optimal transport with shared randomness, by adding a classification constraint H(S|Y) ≤ C borrowed from the RDC/RDPC line (Wang et al., 2025; Zhang, 2023). The perception-divergence extension (KL, squared Wasserstein) builds on the rate-distortion-perception coding theorem of Theis & Wagner (2021), while the asymptotic single-letter characterization relies on Saldi et al. (2015b). The key conceptual unification is that all three constraints—rate, classification, and perception—enter as additional constraints on a single transport plan, with shared randomness enabling a clean structural decoupling of the transport (matching p_Y) and compression (coding Y) steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first closed-form DRC/RDC/DRPC tradeoffs for cross-domain compression with classification, filling a gap where prior work treated cross-domain OT and task-aware compression in isolation. However, the theoretical results are for 1D Bernoulli and Gaussian sources, while the experiments validate on low-dimensional image tasks (MNIST, SVHN), leaving open how tightly these bounds govern high-dimensional natural images. A key limitation is that the WGAN discriminator approximates the OT marginal constraint rather than enforcing it exactly, and the entropy-model rate estimation is a proxy for the true H(Y|U). Future work could explore whether the closed-form structure extends to multivariate Gaussian or mixture models and whether the decoupling property holds under more realistic distortion measures.
