## Problem

Existing activation steering methods for LVLM hallucination mitigation, such as VTI, apply fixed steering vectors regardless of input variation, ignoring the fact that truthfulness directions vary significantly across semantic contexts. Moreover, prior work largely treats visual-level and text-level interventions separately, failing to exploit the finding that truthfulness and visual perception capabilities engage distinct subsets of attention heads. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a dynamic, multimodal steering approach that adapts interventions to the semantic content of each query.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes an RGB image and a text question/prompt, producing a standard text response (VQA answer or image caption). Unusually, the method requires a pre-constructed steering vector database built from AMBER and SEED datasets (clustered into 4 semantic groups), a YOLOv11 object detector to generate visual perception contrast pairs, and a sentence transformer for semantic similarity matching. The intervention is applied at the attention-head level within the LLM backbone during forward pass, with no modification to the vision encoder or connection layer.

## Method

DMAS is a training-free, three-stage approach. First, it constructs a semantic-based truthfulness steering vector database: within each of 4 semantic clusters, it contrasts attention-head activations between truthful and hallucinated responses, averages the differences, applies PCA for denoising, and stores the resulting vectors keyed by cluster embedding. Second, it computes a single visual perception steering vector by contrasting activations on clean versus diffusion-noised images (with YOLOv11-detected objects in the prompt), again applying PCA. Third, during inference, it dynamically retrieves the most semantically relevant truthfulness vector via cosine similarity with the input query, then applies both vectors additively to the top-K attention heads (selected by activation magnitude) using binary masks and scalar hyperparameters α and β to control intervention strength. The key distinction from prior work is the dynamic, context-aware retrieval of truthfulness vectors combined with simultaneous multimodal (truthfulness + visual perception) head-level intervention.

## Results

On MME, DMAS achieves a total score of 659.99 on LLaVAv1.5 (a +94.66 improvement over the 565.33 baseline) and 633.33 on QwenVL (+46 over baseline), outperforming the strongest baselines ICT (649.10) and VAF (628.33) respectively. On POPE (MSCOCO, averaged over random/popular/adversarial), DMAS improves LLaVAv1.5 F1 from 79.65 to 86.79 (+7.14%) and QwenVL F1 from 81.70 to 87.65 (+5.95%). On CHAIR (open-ended captioning), DMAS reduces sentence-level hallucination (CHAIR_S) from 51.0 to 30.8 (−20.2) and image-level hallucination (CHAIR_I) from 15.2 to 11.4 (−3.8), surpassing the prior best VTI (35.8 CHAIR_S) by 5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MME, DMAS outperforms all 8 baselines on both LLaVAv1.5 and QwenVL, beating the closest competitor ICT by 10.89 points (659.99 vs. 649.10) on LLaVAv1.5 and VAF by 5 points (633.33 vs. 628.33) on QwenVL. On POPE, DMAS achieves the best F1 in 3 of 4 model–dataset combinations (QwenVL-MSCOCO 87.65, LLaVAv1.5-GQA 85.63, QwenVL-GQA 84.32), though ICT narrowly edges it on LLaVAv1.5-MSCOCO (F1 87.12 vs. 86.79; Acc 87.35 vs. 86.81). On CHAIR, DMAS achieves the lowest CHAIR_S (30.8) among all methods, beating VTI (35.8) by 5 points and DECO (37.8) by 7 points, while VTI retains a marginal 0.3-point advantage on CHAIR_I (11.1 vs. 11.4). Decoding-based baselines (VCD, OPERA, ICD) consistently underperform activation-based methods (ICT, VTI, DMAS) on both discriminative and generation tasks, and training-free contrastive methods like AGLA and VAF trail DMAS by 5–20 points on MME total scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation steering paradigm from representation engineering (Zou et al., 2023) to the multimodal LVLM setting, building most directly on VTI (Liu et al., 2025b), which applies fixed steering vectors to both visual encoder and LLM hidden states. The key departure is making the truthfulness steering vector dynamic and semantically conditioned—retrieving from a cluster-based database via cosine similarity—rather than applying a single static vector regardless of input. Additionally, DMAS targets specific attention heads (top-K by activation difference) rather than intervening on all activations, and introduces a separate visual perception steering vector derived from noise-contrastive image pairs, addressing both truthfulness and visual grounding simultaneously in a training-free framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of DMAS makes it immediately deployable as a post-hoc fix for existing LVLMs without retraining or data curation, which is practically significant for practitioners working with frozen models. The finding that truthfulness and visual perception localize to different attention heads (layer 30 vs. 31 in LLaVA) and that truthfulness vectors are semantically heterogeneous provides useful interpretability insights for the field. However, open questions remain: the method's gains diminish on newer, stronger models (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3), the fixed 4-cluster granularity may not scale to more diverse semantic spaces, and the approach has not been validated on video or multi-image inputs. Whether the dynamic retrieval mechanism can be made adaptive (e.g., growing the cluster database) without degrading performance is an important direction.
