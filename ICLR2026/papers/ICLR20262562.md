## Problem

Diffusion models for medical image segmentation have been largely confined to 2D tasks; when adapted to 3D volumetric segmentation, they suffer from 'initial-stage collapse,' where reverse sampling from high-noise timesteps (near pure Gaussian) produces incoherent outputs due to the enlarged 3D manifold and vanishingly weak structural cues. Additionally, standard diffusion samplers treat each timestep in isolation, lacking any mechanism to accumulate structural evidence across the denoising trajectory.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D medical volume (CT or T1-weighted MRI) as the conditional image X_c and a noisy 3D segmentation mask x_t at timestep t as the denoising input, both processed as 96×96×96 patches. The conditional image is embedded with the current timestep information so the network is aware of the diffusion stage. The output is a 3D segmentation mask x_0 produced by iteratively denoising from pure Gaussian noise over T=1000 steps, with the cross-timestep state S_t updated at each reverse step to carry accumulated structural evidence.

## Method

Cross-Timestep combines two key mechanisms within a standard DDPM ε-prediction framework. First, the Adaptive Priori Decoding Strategy (APDS) runs a Priori Decoder exclusively on the conditional branch to produce a preliminary mask F_prior, which is injected into the main decoder via a Reverse Addition operation and a time-weighted fusion (ω_t) that peaks at high-noise timesteps and decays to zero as t→0, providing a structural scaffold that prevents initial-stage collapse without constraining later refinement. Second, the trans-temporal memory LSTM (tLSTM) — instantiated as Conv-tLSTM (3D convolutions in all gates, maintaining full 3D tensor cell and hidden states) or Linear-tGRU (linear layers for efficiency) — is modulated by a timestep embedding and carries low-frequency structure, residual statistics, and uncertainty-aware saliency across denoising steps so each step refines rather than re-discovers evidence. Two extensions, SC-tLSTM (stateful spatial-channel attention) and FFT-tLSTM (frequency-domain denoising with recurrent filtering), demonstrate the modularity and scalability of the recurrent controller.

## Results

On LNCTVSeg (440 CT volumes, 4 centers), Cross-Timestep achieves 83.7 Dice, 74.2 IoU, and 2.44 HD95, outperforming the strongest baseline Perspective+ (82.4/73.6/3.27) by 1.3 Dice and 0.8 HD95. On OAseg (160 T1-MRI volumes, 3 centers), it reaches 72.8 Dice, 65.4 IoU, and 6.24 HD95, surpassing the next-best Diff-UNet (71.5/64.2/6.88) by 1.3 Dice and 0.64 HD95. The method also reports a balanced computational profile: 33.4 h training time, 0.17 s inference, 453.5 GFLOPS, and 15,152 MiB GPU memory, comparable to Diff-UNet while delivering substantially higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cross-Timestep outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both datasets. On LNCTVSeg, the closest competitor is Perspective+ (82.4 Dice, 3.27 HD95), which Cross-Timestep surpasses by 1.3 Dice and 0.83 HD95; 3DUXNET (81.6 Dice) and Diff-UNet (81.7 Dice) trail by roughly 2.0 Dice. On OAseg, the gap widens: Diff-UNet (71.5 Dice, 6.88 HD95) is the nearest rival, beaten by 1.3 Dice and 0.64 HD95, while Perspective+ (69.6 Dice) and 3DUXNET (68.9 Dice) lag by 3.2 and 3.9 Dice respectively. Transformer-based baselines (SwinUNETR, UNETR, nnFormer) consistently underperform the diffusion-based and large-kernel CNN methods on both datasets, with UNETR posting the weakest OAseg Dice (67.1) and TransBTS the worst HD95 on OAseg (12.7). The diffusion-based Diff-UNet is the strongest single competitor on OAseg but still falls short of Cross-Timestep on all three metrics, confirming that the APDS + tLSTM combination provides a meaningful advantage over prior diffusion approaches that rely on post-hoc multi-step fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based medical segmentation from the well-studied 2D setting to 3D volumetric data, where prior diffusion work has focused almost exclusively on generative synthesis (3D MedDiffusion, HiDiff) or post-hoc fusion of independent per-step predictions (Diff-UNet). It departs from these approaches by identifying a specific failure mode—'initial-stage collapse'—and addressing it architecturally rather than through output-level fusion. The tLSTM modules repurpose classical recurrent architectures (Conv-LSTM, GRU) as cross-timestep state carriers within a DDPM denoiser, while APDS borrows the idea of conditional-branch decoding from U-Net-style architectures and injects it with a time-decaying weight. Conceptually, the paper sits at the intersection of diffusion models, recurrent state tracking, and conditional segmentation, but no single prior work combines these elements for 3D medical segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'initial-stage collapse' as a named, reproducible failure mode gives the community a concrete diagnostic for why naive 2D-to-3D diffusion transfers fail, and APDS offers a plug-in fix that could stabilize other 3D diffusion pipelines. The modular tLSTM units (Conv-tLSTM, Linear-tGRU, SC-tLSTM, FFT-tLSTM) are presented as reusable building blocks, suggesting broader applicability beyond NPC segmentation. However, evaluation is limited to two nasopharyngeal carcinoma datasets (CT and MRI), and the computational overhead (0.17 s inference, 15 GB GPU memory) is higher than non-diffusion baselines, raising questions about clinical deployability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other anatomies and pathologies, whether the specific ω_t weighting function is optimal, and whether the recurrent state can be replaced by lighter alternatives (e.g., state-space models) without losing the temporal coherence benefit.
