## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction for autonomous driving must handle two simultaneous challenges: sparse, minimally-overlapping surround-view cameras that break 2D cross-attention fusion, and complex scene dynamics that complicate temporal aggregation. Existing methods either fuse spatially in image space (suffering from limited view overlap), lack temporal propagation, or rely on costly per-scene optimization, leaving a gap for a unified, real-time framework that jointly addresses both issues.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-camera RGB frames (5 cameras at 350×518 for Waymo, 6 at 224×406 for nuScenes) along with known ego-pose, processed in a streaming fashion over 20-frame clips. It outputs a set of 3D Gaussian primitives (with per-Gaussian dynamic scores) defined in ego-centric coordinates within a 144m×144m×16m volume at 0.1m×0.1m×0.2m voxel resolution, which are rendered into novel views and also produce per-pixel dynamic segmentation masks. The geometry foundation model imposes a constraint that image dimensions be divisible by 14.

## Method

UniSplat constructs a 3D latent scaffold by feeding multi-view images through a frozen geometry transformer (π³) to obtain a metric-scale point map (with a LiDAR-supervised scale alignment branch) and a DINOv2 backbone for semantic features, then voxelizing the fused features into a sparse ego-centric grid. Spatio-temporal fusion operates directly in this 3D scaffold space: a sparse 3D U-Net (8× downsampling) integrates multi-view spatial context, while temporal fusion warps the previous frame's fused scaffold via ego-pose and performs sparse tensor addition with the current scaffold, refined by a second sparse 3D U-Net (2× downsampling). A dual-branch decoder then generates Gaussians from both point anchors (preserving fine geometric detail) and voxel centers (filling sparsely covered regions, 4 Gaussians per voxel), each assigned a learned dynamic score. A streaming memory bank retains only static Gaussians (score < 0.7) across frames, enabling progressive scene completion beyond current camera coverage while suppressing dynamic ghosting artifacts.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset (multi-view setting), UniSplat achieves 28.56 PSNR / 0.83 SSIM / 0.20 LPIPS for input-view reconstruction and 25.12 PSNR / 0.74 SSIM / 0.27 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. With optimal LiDAR-based scale alignment (UniSplat†), reconstruction improves to 29.58 PSNR and novel view synthesis to 25.98 PSNR. On nuScenes, UniSplat reaches 25.37 PSNR / 0.765 SSIM, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art Omni-Scene (24.27 PSNR) by +1.10 dB while also running faster (4.0 FPS vs. 2.5 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniSplat dominates all six baselines across both datasets. On Waymo (multi-view), it outperforms the strongest general-purpose baseline DepthSplat by +3.18 dB PSNR in reconstruction and +1.26 dB in novel view synthesis, and beats the driving-specific DriveRecon by a dramatic +7.80 dB in novel view synthesis (25.12 vs. 17.32 PSNR). Against EvolSplat (front-view only), UniSplat gains +5.58 dB in reconstruction PSNR while also providing novel view synthesis that EvolSplat does not support. On nuScenes, UniSplat surpasses Omni-Scene by +1.10 dB PSNR (25.37 vs. 24.27) and +0.029 SSIM, while also achieving higher throughput (4.0 vs. 2.5 FPS) with comparable memory usage (8.30 vs. 8.22 GB). The gap over MVSplat is +3.62 dB in reconstruction and +3.08 dB in novel view synthesis on Waymo, confirming that 3D scaffold-based fusion substantially outperforms 2D cost-volume or cross-attention fusion strategies in sparse, minimally-overlapping driving camera setups.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniSplat sits at the intersection of feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction (MVSplat, DepthSplat, PixelSplat) and driving-specific scene modeling (EvolSplat, Omni-Scene, DriveRecon), but departs from both by performing all fusion operations directly in a 3D latent scaffold space rather than in 2D image space via cross-attention or MVS cost volumes. It repurposes pretrained geometry foundation models (π³, DUSt3R lineage) and visual foundation models (DINOv2) as frozen feature extractors to initialize the scaffold, then applies sparse 3D U-Nets for spatial and temporal fusion—a streaming, ego-motion-warped accumulation of voxel features that replaces the raw-image temporal aggregation used by prior driving methods. The dual-branch decoder (point-anchored + voxel-generated Gaussians) and the dynamic-score-based streaming memory for scene completion are novel architectural contributions not present in any single prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The streaming memory mechanism that accumulates static Gaussians over time and enables rendering outside the original camera frustums is practically significant for autonomous driving simulation and long-horizon planning, where no single frame captures the full 360° scene. The dynamic-aware filtering also unlocks scene editing (removing, moving, inserting actors) with automatic background disocclusion recovery, a capability not available in prior feed-forward driving reconstruction. Limitations include reliance on a fixed dynamic threshold (τ_d = 0.7), occasional misclassification of slow-moving objects as static (causing ghosting), and the need for LiDAR references for optimal scale alignment. Open questions include whether the streaming scaffold degrades over very long sequences, how the method scales to higher-resolution or more camera configurations, and whether the implicit dynamic suppression observed in the scaffold generalizes to faster or more complex motion.
