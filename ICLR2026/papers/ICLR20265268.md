## Problem

Existing multi-track music generation models (e.g., MSDM, MSG-LD) treat the task as learning a joint distribution over all tracks, analogous to multivariate time series or video generation. This architecture overemphasizes inter-track discrepancies while neglecting the shared rhythmic backbone that must be stable within each track and synchronized across tracks, resulting in generated music with irregular beats and poor cross-track alignment. Furthermore, the standard evaluation metric FAD compresses audio into VGGish embeddings that discard temporal resolution, making it impossible to quantify rhythmic stability or synchronization.

## Input / Output

In unconditional mode, the model takes pure Gaussian noise in latent space (shape 4×8×64×64, where 4 is the number of tracks) and iteratively denoises it over 200 DDIM steps to produce four separate 16 kHz audio waveforms (bass, drums, guitar, piano) at 10.24 seconds each, decoded via a VAE decoder followed by a HiFi-GAN vocoder. In conditional mode, a subset of tracks is provided as known latents while the remaining tracks are generated. The latent space is obtained by STFT + Mel filter bank + a pre-trained VAE encoder, compressing raw waveforms into a C×T×F representation.

## Method

SyncTrack is a latent diffusion model (DDPM objective) built on a U-Net backbone that replaces the standard uniform blocks with two distinct module types: track-shared modules and track-specific modules. Each track-shared module contains a ResBlock, inner-track attention (standard 2D U-Net self-attention with shared parameters), a global cross-track attention that attends over all tracks across both temporal and frequency dimensions to enforce a consistent tempo, and a time-specific cross-track attention that attends only over other tracks at the same temporal index to align onsets and chordal structures. Each track-specific module injects a learnable instrument prior—constructed from one-hot track identifiers passed through positional encoding and a two-layer MLP—into the residual stream to capture timbre and pitch-range differences. The model is initialized from MusicLDM pre-trained weights and trained for 320K iterations (241M trainable parameters) on Slakh2100 four-track clips, with the key architectural insight being that rhythm is a shared property requiring cross-track attention while timbre is a per-track property requiring dedicated parameters.

## Results

On the Slakh2100 four-track (bass, drums, guitar, piano) unconditional generation task, SyncTrack achieves a mixture FAD of 1.26, outperforming MSG-LD (1.31), JEN-1 Composer (4.04), STEMGEN (4.3), and MSDM (6.55). On the proposed rhythmic metrics, SyncTrack attains a Cross-track Beat Synchronization (CBS) of 0.5206 (vs. 0.3861 for MSG-LD and 0.4694 for MSDM) and a Cross-track Beat Dispersion (CBD mean) of 0.2681 (vs. 0.3714 for MSG-LD), approaching the ground-truth values of 0.5740 and 0.2412 respectively. Subjective evaluation confirms the gap: SyncTrack receives an average mixture rating of 3.42/5 versus 1.57/5 for MSG-LD and 4.48/5 for ground truth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SyncTrack outperforms all four baselines on mixture FAD, with the largest margin over MSDM (1.26 vs. 6.55, a 80.8% reduction) and STEMGEN (1.26 vs. 4.30, 70.7% reduction). Against the closest competitor MSG-LD, SyncTrack achieves a modest FAD improvement (1.26 vs. 1.31, 3.8%) but a substantially larger gain on rhythmic quality: CBS improves by 34.8% (0.5206 vs. 0.3861) and CBD(mean) drops by 27.8% (0.2681 vs. 0.3714), indicating tighter cross-track beat alignment. On track-wise FAD, SyncTrack beats MSG-LD by 32–46% across all four instruments, with the largest improvement on piano (45.6%). In subjective listening tests, SyncTrack's average mixture score (3.42/5) is more than double that of MSG-LD (1.57/5), while MSDM and STEMGEN are not included in the subjective comparison due to lack of open-source code. The ablation study further confirms that the track-specific module contributes the largest single-track FAD reduction (up to 84.4%), while the two cross-track attention modules jointly drive the synchronization gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyncTrack builds on the Latent Diffusion Model framework (Rombach et al., 2022; Ho et al., 2020) and is initialized with MusicLDM pre-trained weights, but departs fundamentally from prior multi-track generation methods (MSDM, MSG-LD, StemGen, Jen-1 Composer) that treat the task as multivariable time-series or video generation with a single unified model. The key architectural departure is the explicit decomposition into track-shared modules (with global and time-specific cross-track attention for rhythm) and track-specific modules (with learnable instrument priors for timbre), reflecting the domain insight that rhythm is shared across tracks while timbre is track-specific. On the evaluation side, the paper extends the FAD-only paradigm by introducing three rhythm-specific metrics (IRS, CBS, CBD) that prior multi-track generation work had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proposed rhythmic metrics (IRS, CBS, CBD) fill a genuine evaluation gap: FAD's VGGish-based compression destroys the temporal resolution needed to assess beat stability and cross-track alignment, and no prior objective metric captured these properties for waveform multi-track generation. The track-shared/track-specific architectural separation is noted as structurally simple and potentially transferable to other latent-audio diffusion systems, broadening its impact beyond this specific model. Limitations include evaluation on only four fixed tracks (bass, drums, guitar, piano) and short 10.24-second clips; the authors acknowledge extending to longer-form and more diverse multi-track generation as future work. The modest FAD gap over MSG-LD (3.82%) suggests the primary gains are in rhythmic coherence rather than overall spectral fidelity, which may limit adoption by practitioners focused solely on FAD.
