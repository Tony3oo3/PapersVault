## Problem

Standard GRPO training for search agents relies on sparse, binary outcome-based rewards that treat all incorrect rollouts identically, discarding the rich entity information embedded during synthetic data generation. This makes it impossible to distinguish informative 'near-miss' samples—those with substantially correct intermediate reasoning but a flawed final answer—from complete failures, wasting valuable learning signals and forcing the model to re-learn steps it had already mastered.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260491/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a text question and iteratively generates thoughts and tool calls (search queries and webpage visits) in a ReAct-style multi-turn rollout, producing a final answer enclosed in <answer> tags. During training, the ground-truth entity set from the synthetic QA generation pipeline is retained and matched against the agent's thoughts via exact string matching (not observations), yielding a per-rollout entity match rate. The reward is computed per group of G=8 rollouts, with the entity match rate normalized within each group to a 0-to-1 scale before being used in the advantage calculation.

## Method

E-GRPO replaces GRPO's binary reward with a dense, entity-aware reward: correct rollouts receive 1, incorrect rollouts receive α·γ̂_i (the normalized entity match rate scaled by a hyperparameter α, default 0.3), and format/overlength errors receive 0. This creates a continuous reward spectrum that differentiates the quality of negative samples, so a near-miss that identifies most ground-truth entities receives a meaningfully higher reward than a complete failure. The empirical foundation is a strong positive correlation between entity match rate and final answer accuracy, validated across per-question and distributional analyses on SailorFog-QA. Training follows a two-stage procedure: cold-start SFT on 11K SailorFog-QA samples, followed by RL with a KL-free objective and clip-higher exploration (following DAPO), using only 1K synthetic samples per environment. The entity matching is confined to the agent's thoughts (not tool observations) to ensure the reward reflects genuine reasoning rather than passive information exposure, and exact string matching is used over LLM-based judging to avoid reward hacking and maintain computational efficiency.

## Results

On deep research benchmarks, Web-30B-E-GRPO achieves 48.5 on GAIA, 12.9 on BrowseComp, 26.4 on BrowseComp-ZH, and 46.7 on xbench-DeepSearch (Pass@1), consistently outperforming its GRPO counterpart (47.6, 12.3, 25.7, 45.3) and surpassing WebSailor-32B on BrowseComp and BrowseComp-ZH. On QA benchmarks in the Web environment, Local-7B-E-GRPO achieves a 67.8 average Pass@1, beating ASearcher-14B (65.6) and Search-R1-32B (62.5). The Pass@3 gains are particularly pronounced: E-GRPO improves GAIA Pass@3 from 44.7 (GRPO) to 51.5 (7B) and 65.1 (30B), reflecting its ability to explore diverse solution paths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-GRPO consistently outperforms the GRPO baseline across all 11 benchmarks and both model scales (7B and 30B), with the largest relative gains on BrowseComp (e.g., 9.3 vs. 6.3 for 7B, a 48% relative improvement) and GAIA Pass@3 (51.5 vs. 44.7 for 7B). Against external open-source agents, Web-30B-E-GRPO surpasses WebSailor-32B on BrowseComp (12.9 vs. 10.5) and BrowseComp-ZH (26.4 vs. 25.5), and WebDancer-QwQ on BrowseComp (12.9 vs. 3.8), BrowseComp-ZH (26.4 vs. 18.0), and xbench-DS (46.7 vs. 39.0), though it trails WebSailor-32B on GAIA (48.5 vs. 53.2) and xbench-DS (46.7 vs. 53.3). On QA benchmarks in the Web environment, Local-7B-E-GRPO (67.8 avg) beats ASearcher-14B (65.6), Simple-DS-QwQ (65.3), and Search-R1-32B (62.5) despite being a 7B model trained on a local corpus. Notably, Web-30B-E-GRPO surpasses Claude-4-Sonnet on BrowseComp (12.9 vs. 12.2) while remaining competitive on BrowseComp-ZH (26.4 vs. 29.1), though it still lags behind frontier models like OpenAI-o3 and DeepSeek-V3.1 on most deep research benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for search-agent training by replacing its binary outcome reward with a dense, entity-aware reward derived from ground-truth entities that are systematically generated but discarded during entity-centric synthetic data synthesis (ASearcher, SailorFog-QA). It departs from the broader family of process-supervision approaches (PRMs, tree-based search) that are infeasible for open-ended web search due to annotation cost and trajectory length, instead exploiting a signal already present in the training data at zero additional annotation cost. The ReAct paradigm and DAPO's KL-free, clip-higher modifications provide the operational substrate, but the core contribution is the reward reformulation that treats partially-correct 'near-miss' rollouts as distinct from complete failures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that E-GRPO offers a drop-in, computationally trivial (exact string matching) upgrade to any GRPO-based search-agent pipeline that already uses entity-centric synthetic data, yielding consistent 2–4 point accuracy gains and fewer tool calls across 11 benchmarks and two model scales. The empirical correlation between entity match rate and final-answer correctness (4-to-1 per-question margin) provides a principled justification rather than an ad-hoc heuristic. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-entity-centric data sources, whether the rigid exact-string matching (partially addressed by the 'Robust Matching' variant) limits applicability to diverse entity types, and whether dynamic or decaying α schedules could further improve long-horizon training.
