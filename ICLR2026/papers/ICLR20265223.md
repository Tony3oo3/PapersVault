## Problem

Current LLM safety evaluations rely on token-level backdoor defenses that detect rare lexical triggers, leaving a blind spot for concept-conditioned semantic divergences where common high-level cues (ideologies, public figures, framings) elicit unusually uniform, stance-like responses. Because such cues appear naturally in benign data, small biased fine-tuning or sampling dynamics can entrench systematic propaganda-like behaviors that evade existing audits. The paper formalizes this phenomenon and asks whether a practical black-box detection method can surface these meaning-level anomalies without access to training data or model internals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paraphrase-controlled prompts (360 unique prompts spanning 12 sensitive topics, each generated from an Entity A / Entity B pair with 10 template variants) and draws k=6 sampled responses per prompt at temperature T=0.7 from each of five LLMs, yielding 2,160 responses per model. It produces a per-model-prompt suspicion score in [0,100] that couples inverse semantic entropy (Confidence) with cross-model divergence (Divergence), and flags (ψ, A) concept-level pairs where entropy falls below θ_e=0.3 and the model disagrees with ≥60% of peers. Flagged cases are reported as triage signals ranked by suspicion score (default reporting threshold θ_d=85), not as correctness judgments.

## Method

RAVEN is a four-stage black-box audit pipeline. Stage I defines sensitive domains and entity-perspective pairs, generating 360 prompts via GPT-4o. Stage II queries five diverse LLMs (GPT-4o, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-2-7B, Mistral-7B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) with 6 samples per prompt. Stage III clusters each model's responses per prompt using bidirectional entailment (GPT-4o-mini as judge: two responses are equivalent iff each entails the other), then computes semantic entropy over the resulting cluster distribution. Stage IV computes a suspicion score S = α·Confidence + (1−α)·Divergence (α=0.4), where Confidence is 1 minus normalized entropy scaled to 0–100, and Divergence is a weighted combination of the fraction of peer models whose representative answers semantically differ and the average entailment-based divergence magnitude from disagreeing peers. The key insight is that coupling within-model uniformity (low entropy under paraphrase) with cross-model disagreement separates model-specific concept-conditioned anomalies from corpus-wide priors, operating at the meaning level where no rare token trigger need exist. A controlled LoRA fine-tuning study (3 epochs, lr=1e-3, 100 biased + 100 control Q&A pairs) validates that such divergences can be implanted without lexical triggers and are reliably detected by RAVEN.

## Results

RAVEN surfaces concept-conditioned semantic divergences in 9 of 12 sensitive topics across five LLM families (GPT-4o, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-2-7B, Mistral-7B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B), with 12 high-suspicion cases (S ≥ 85) reported. In the controlled LoRA implantation study, all four fine-tuned models exhibit a clear concept-conditioned negative stance shift (Δ = −1.0 to −1.8 on a 1–5 sentiment scale), and RAVEN flags them with mean suspicion scores of 86.5–91.7 and coverage ranging from 71.4% (Llama-3.1-LoRA) to 100% (Mistral-LoRA, Llama-2-LoRA). The strongest natural divergences appear in stance-based domains (climate, vaccination, gender, religion) and approach/justification domains (immigration, surveillance), while aspect-based and application/consequence domains (Amazon, McDonald's, AI) show no flags.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly omits head-to-head comparisons against token/syntax-trigger backdoor defenses (e.g., ONION, BadNets, instruction-backdoor sanitization), arguing that the threat-model mismatch—concept-conditioned cues vs. rare lexical triggers—renders such comparisons non-diagnostic. Instead, RAVEN's 'comparison' is cross-model: each LLM's responses are benchmarked against four peer models, and a flag requires the target model to disagree with at least 60% of peers while maintaining low semantic entropy (≤ 0.3). The closest methodological predecessor is Farquhar et al. (Nature 2024), which uses semantic entropy for hallucination detection; RAVEN extends this by coupling entropy with cross-model divergence to produce a security triage signal. No competing anomaly-detection or backdoor-detection method is evaluated under the same black-box, concept-level protocol, so the paper positions RAVEN as a complementary tool rather than a method that wins or loses against a specific baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAVEN extends the semantic-entropy framework of Farquhar et al. (Nature 2024), which used output clustering to detect hallucinations, by repurposing within-model entropy as a security signal and coupling it with cross-model disagreement to flag concept-conditioned anomalies. It deliberately departs from the token-level backdoor literature (Gu et al. 2017; Qi et al. 2021; Zhang et al. 2024; Yan et al. 2024), which targets rare lexical triggers, by operating at the meaning level where no rare string need exist. The paper also positions itself alongside recent semantic-backdoor work (Zhang et al. 2024; Yan et al. 2024) that identifies concept-level triggers but focuses on attack construction rather than black-box detection, and complements those efforts with a practical audit pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small biased fine-tuning set (as few as 25% biased samples) can implant a stable, concept-conditioned stance detectable only through meaning-level auditing, and that pretrained models already exhibit such divergences in 9 of 12 sensitive topics—gaps invisible to token-trigger defenses. This motivates concept-level checks in release triage and post-deployment monitoring as LLMs increasingly shape public discourse. Key limitations include reliance on a predefined domain set, a single entailment judge (GPT-4o-mini), and the absence of causal attribution; open questions include scaling to multi-turn dialogue, real-time monitoring, and integrating black-box flags with white-box mechanistic evidence.
