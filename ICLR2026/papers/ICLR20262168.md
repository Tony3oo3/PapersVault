## Problem

GUI visual grounding has relied almost exclusively on supervised fine-tuning of MLLMs, demanding millions of labeled examples and significant compute. As base models improve and rule-based reinforcement fine-tuning (GRPO) shows promise in other domains, it remains unclear whether RFT can match or exceed SFT for GUI-VG, and no prior work has systematically studied the optimal formulation of RFT objectives for this task. Notably, naive application of GRPO to GUI-VG actually underperforms the SFT baseline, revealing that careful design of reward functions, KL regularization, and training configurations is essential.

## Input / Output

The model receives a GUI screenshot (RGB image at arbitrary resolution) paired with a short textual instruction describing a target element (e.g., 'click the search button'). It produces a single (x, y) point coordinate intended to fall within the target element's bounding box. The model generates intermediate reasoning within <think> tags before emitting the coordinate in <answer> tags. Image resolution is deliberately withheld from the prompt during training but can be prepended at inference time to aid spatial calibration.

## Method

GUIRLVG applies GRPO to Qwen2.5-VL with LoRA (rank 64) on as few as 5.2K samples from ShowUI, guided by a step-by-step empirical ablation of each GRPO component. Three key design choices distinguish it from trivial RFT: (1) a Soft Reward Function that grants partial credit for format compliance and extracts numeric coordinates regardless of JSON style, (2) direct point prediction scored by a binary In-Bbox reward that aligns the training signal with the downstream action, and (3) a novel Adversarial KL Factor that dynamically scales the KL penalty by the ratio of each response's reward to its theoretical maximum (α_i = r_i/m), preventing over-optimization on high-reward samples. Additional findings include that β=1e-4 with the Adversarial KL Factor outperforms static KL, group size 6 with batch size 4 is optimal (larger groups degrade performance), and withholding resolution during training while providing it at test time yields the best accuracy. The final pipeline trains for 1,300 steps and achieves 88.7% on ScreenSpot, 91.9% on ScreenSpotV2, and 36.1% on ScreenSpot-Pro, surpassing SFT baselines trained on up to 13.58M samples.

## Results

On ScreenSpot, GuirlVG (7B, 5.2K training samples) achieves 88.7% average accuracy, surpassing the strongest SFT baseline OS-Atlas (7B, 13.58M samples) by +7.7%. On ScreenSpot v2, it reaches 91.9% average accuracy (+7.8% over OS-Atlas). On the more challenging ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark, GuirlVG achieves 36.1% average accuracy with 5.2K samples, an absolute gain of +17.2% over OS-Atlas (18.9%), demonstrating strong generalization to high-resolution professional GUIs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GuirlVG (5.2K samples) decisively outperforms all nine SFT baselines across all three benchmarks. The most striking comparison is against OS-Atlas (7B, 13.58M samples), the strongest prior method: GuirlVG exceeds it by +7.7% on ScreenSpot (88.7% vs 81.0%), +7.8% on ScreenSpot v2 (91.9% vs 84.1%), and +17.2% on ScreenSpot-Pro (36.1% vs 18.9%), despite using ~2,600× fewer training samples. Against SeeClick (53.4% on ScreenSpot), UGround (73.3%), and ShowUI (75.1%), the gains range from +13.6% to +35.3%. On ScreenSpot-Pro, where all prior methods struggle (best prior: OS-Atlas at 18.9%), GuirlVG more than doubles the accuracy. Even with only 2K training samples, GuirlVG already surpasses every baseline, including the 18B CogAgent (47.4%) and the 35M-sample AGG (66.6%). Notably, on the Mobile-Icon subset of ScreenSpot, GuirlVG exceeds OS-Atlas by +11.8% despite containing no mobile-specific training data, suggesting RFT enhances out-of-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1/DeepSeekMath) to GUI visual grounding, a task previously dominated by supervised fine-tuning methods (SeeClick, UGround, OS-Atlas, ShowUI). While recent concurrent works (UniVG-R1, GUI-R1, VLM-R1, Visual-RFT) have begun transferring RFT to visual grounding, this paper explicitly departs from their performance-driven framing to conduct the first controlled, component-by-component empirical study of RFT design choices for GUI-VG. Its key conceptual departure is the finding that naive RFT underperforms SFT, necessitating careful redesign of reward functions (Soft Reward Function), prediction format (point + In-Bbox), and KL regularization (Adversarial KL Factor) before RFT becomes competitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that large-scale SFT is the most effective post-training strategy for GUI-VG, showing that a carefully designed RFT pipeline with 5.2K samples can match or exceed SFT methods trained on 13.58M samples. The Adversarial KL Factor and the systematic ablation findings (e.g., group size 8 hurting performance, withholding resolution during training helping) provide actionable guidance for practitioners designing RFT pipelines beyond GUI-VG. Limitations include evaluation on a single backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) and a single training dataset (ShowUI), and the cross-method comparisons in Tables 8–10 involve different data scales and training protocols, making the 'fair' comparison primarily the 500-step ablation in Table 1. Open questions include whether these findings transfer to other MLLM architectures, multi-step GUI interaction tasks, and whether the data-efficiency advantage persists as base model capability grows.
