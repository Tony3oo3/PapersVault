## Problem

Heterogeneous multi-agent reinforcement learning faces a fundamental tension: trust-region and value-decomposition methods guarantee stability but restrict policies to unimodal Gaussians that cannot capture the multimodal coordination strategies heterogeneous agents require, while expressive generative policies like diffusion models lack optimization guarantees in non-stationary multi-agent settings. No prior framework provides monotone improvement guarantees for diffusion-based policies under sequential heterogeneous updates with intractable log-likelihoods.

## Input / Output

Each heterogeneous agent receives its own local state observation (which may differ in dimensionality and modality across agents) and produces an action sampled from a diffusion-based policy defined implicitly through a reverse SDE. Actions are generated by iterative denoising from Gaussian noise conditioned on the state, rather than direct sampling from a parametric distribution. Training follows the CTDE paradigm: a centralized critic observes the joint state for advantage estimation, while each agent's diffusion actor operates on its local observation at execution time.

## Method

HAQO unifies three components into a single per-agent objective optimized sequentially in random permutation order. First, sequential advantage-aware updates condition each agent's surrogate on already-updated peers' policies while retaining the baseline state distribution, yielding a telescoping return-difference decomposition with quadratic KL drift penalties that scale linearly (not exponentially) in agent count. Second, because diffusion policies have intractable log-likelihoods, the method replaces the classical likelihood-ratio surrogate with a Q-weighted denoising loss weighted by non-negative advantage transformations (qadv or qcut), which provably lower-bounds the policy improvement surrogate up to critic bias. Third, an entropy surrogate injects uniformly sampled actions into the denoising objective to enforce a positive spectral floor on action covariance, preventing mode collapse without explicit log-density computation. The combined objective (QV surrogate + entropy surrogate − drift penalty) is optimized per-agent with PPO-style clipping, and Theorem 5.4 establishes monotone joint return improvement under bounded rewards, regular policies, and bounded critic error.

## Results

On Multi-Agent MuJoCo, HAQO achieves the highest mean return on 5 of 6 environments: Ant-v2 4x2 (6014±201), Hopper-v2 3x1 (3884±81), Walker2d-v2 2x3 (5681±301), Walker2d-v2 6x1 (4789±293), and Humanoid-v2 17x1 (7013±311). The only environment where HAQO trails is HalfCheetah-v2 2x3 (6873±137 vs. HAPPO's 7024±103). In the GRF ablation, diffusion policies with HAQO achieve 97.27±1.07 on 3-vs-1-with-keeper versus 66.82±9.12 for Gaussian policies, demonstrating both higher returns and substantially lower variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAQO is compared against MAPPO (Chao et al., 2022) and the heterogeneous sequential-update methods HAPPO/HATRPO (Zhong et al., 2024) on Multi-Agent MuJoCo. Against MAPPO, HAQO wins on 5 of 6 environments, with the largest margin on Humanoid-v2 (7013 vs. 732, where MAPPO fails to learn meaningful locomotion) and Walker2d-v2 6x1 (4789 vs. 4693); it trails only on HalfCheetah-v2 2x3 (6873 vs. 6984). Against HAPPO/HATRPO, HAQO similarly wins on 5 of 6 environments, with notable gains on Walker2d-v2 6x1 (4789 vs. 4317, +472) and Humanoid-v2 (7013 vs. 6739, +274), while losing narrowly on HalfCheetah-v2 2x3 (6873 vs. 7024, −151). The paper attributes HAQO's advantage to diffusion-based multimodal policies capturing gait cycles and coordination patterns that unimodal Gaussian baselines collapse on, particularly in high-dimensional tasks like Humanoid where MAPPO and HAPPO produce unstable, homogeneous behaviors.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAQO sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the sequential-update trust-region framework for heterogeneous MARL from Zhong et al. (2024), which provides monotone improvement guarantees but assumes unimodal Gaussian policies; (2) the Q-weighted variational policy optimization (QVPO) of Ding et al. (2024), which connects diffusion-based denoising objectives to policy-gradient theory but only in single-agent settings; and (3) diffusion policies (Chi et al., 2023; Kuba et al., 2022a) that offer expressive multimodal action distributions. The paper's core departure is the non-trivial integration of all three: extending QVPO's variational lower bound to a multi-agent sequential-update setting with per-agent trust regions, and introducing an entropy surrogate that replaces the intractable log-likelihood of diffusion actors, thereby preserving monotone improvement guarantees without requiring closed-form policy densities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a genuine gap: prior MARL methods either guarantee stability (trust-region, value-decomposition) at the cost of unimodal policies, or offer expressive generative policies without optimization guarantees. By establishing monotone improvement bounds for diffusion-based heterogeneous agents, HAQO provides the first theoretical bridge between generative policy modeling and trust-region MARL. Limitations include reliance on bounded-reward and bounded-critic-bias assumptions that may be loose in practice, the computational overhead of diffusion denoising in multi-agent loops, and evaluation limited to cooperative settings. Open questions include scalability to large agent counts, applicability to competitive or mixed-motive games, and whether the sequential update order can be learned rather than randomized.
