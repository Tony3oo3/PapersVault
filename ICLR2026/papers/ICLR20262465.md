## Problem

Aligning AI with human values is a pressing unsolved problem, but the most highly-valued traits (kindness, conservatism, ecological ethics) are inherently subjective, meaning no ground-truth labels exist for evaluation. Existing LM ranking systems (LMArena, LitmusValues) either rely on human preferences or rate values within a single model, leaving no quantitative method to comparatively benchmark an ensemble of models against an arbitrary value system without objective labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: a population of N models (each a pair of a base LM and an optional prompted persona), a constitution consisting of a set of comparative judgment criteria, and a dataset of prompted scenarios (drawn from r/AskReddit, OASST, or AIRiskDilemmas). The process is double-blind: evaluees never see the constitution or know they are being judged, and judges never see evaluee identities. Output is a vector of Elo alignment scores per model, an N×N row-stochastic trust matrix, and learned d-dimensional latent embeddings (model dispositions v_j and judge lenses u_i) that can be visualized to reveal how models differ in their interpretation of the constitution.

## Method

Each model serves as both judge and evaluee: for a sampled scenario, two evaluees generate responses, a third model reflects on each response against the constitution, then issues a pairwise preference (win/loss/tie). To reduce order and primacy bias, both orderings are collected and inconsistent pairs are remapped to ties. Pairwise trits are aggregated by fitting a low-rank Bradley-Terry-Davidson model via maximum likelihood, learning vector-valued model dispositions v_j ∈ R^d and judge lenses u_i ∈ R^d (so the log-latent strength of model j as judged by model i is the inner product u_i^T v_j) plus per-judge tie propensities λ_i. The trust matrix entry T_ij is derived from a Davidson-Luce distribution over the learned strengths, encoding how much judge M_i trusts evaluee M_j's alignment. EigenTrust power iteration then extracts the left principal eigenvector t of T, which satisfies t = tT — a fixed-point equation that weights each judge's trust proportionally to its own alignment score, embodying the premise that better-aligned models are better judges. The resulting trust vector is converted to Elo ratings for interpretability.

## Results

On the GPQA benchmark (15 models, 448 questions), EigenBench recovers a ranking only 12 adjacent swaps from ground truth (Kendall-tau τ ≈ 0.77), a probability of ~1 in 200,000 for a random ordering, without ever being given the correct answers. In human validation, the average human–LM interjudge distance (0.3130) is nearly identical to the average human–human interjudge distance (0.3133), indicating LM judges approximate human consensus as well as humans approximate each other. For character training validation, fine-tuning Llama 3.1 8B on the Loving constitution raises its EigenBench Elo from 1426 to 1573 (fine-tuned) or 1579 (pre-prompted), a ~150-point improvement over the base model. In the prompted-dispositions experiment (5 LMs × 5 personas), 79% of variance in trust scores is explained by the persona and 21% by the underlying LM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against a competing value-alignment method head-to-head; instead it validates against three reference signals. (1) Against self-reported model surveys: EigenBench rankings diverge sharply from models' own 1–7 self-ratings (e.g., Grok 4 rates itself 7.0/7 on Universal Kindness yet ranks 6th on EigenBench, while Claude 4 Sonnet rates itself lowest at 6.13 yet ranks 3rd), consistent with Chiu et al. (2025) on stated-vs-revealed preferences. (2) Against human judges: the mean 1-norm distance between a human and an LM trust vector (0.3130) is essentially equal to the mean human–human distance (0.3133), so LM judges are no less consistent with the human consensus than humans are with each other. (3) Against GPQA ground truth: EigenBench achieves τ ≈ 0.77 (12 adjacent swaps out of 15 models) without access to correct answers, far exceeding the ~1-in-200,000 chance of a random ranking matching this closely. Robustness checks show scores are stable across scenario datasets (max ~100 Elo swing), constitution wording (max 16 Elo std-dev), and model-population changes (scores shift <15 Elo for existing models when new ones are added).

## Relation to Prior Work

EigenBench combines two established mathematical tools — the Bradley-Terry-Davidson model for aggregating pairwise comparisons with ties, and the EigenTrust algorithm for computing a consensus reputation vector — into a new pipeline for measuring subjective value alignment in LMs. It departs from prior LM ranking systems (LMArena, Prompt-to-Leaderboard, LitmusValues) in a key way: those systems rely on human preferences or objective labels, whereas EigenBench uses LM-to-LM peer judgments aggregated via a weighted eigenvector, specifically designed for traits where no ground truth exists. The conceptual inspiration traces to Scott Aaronson's Eigenmorality blog post and Kleinberg's PageRank, but the paper's technical contribution is deriving a trust matrix from natural-language critiques through a low-rank BTD fit with separate judge-lens and model-disposition embeddings, rather than from explicit link structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EigenBench addresses a genuine gap: there is no widely adopted quantitative method for benchmarking LMs on subjective value dimensions (kindness, conservatism, ecological ethics) where reasonable judges disagree on correct labels. The validation that LM peer-judgment consensus approximates human inter-judge agreement (0.3130 vs. 0.3133 average 1-norm distance) and that the method recovers GPQA rankings to within 12 adjacent swaps without ground-truth labels are both encouraging. Key limitations include the high API cost per comparison (two response calls, two reflections, one comparison per pair), the assumption that better-aligned models are better judges (which is untested for adversarial or misaligned populations), and the Greenbeard-effect vulnerability, which the paper shows is partially mitigated but not fully resolved. Open questions include whether active learning or hybrid human-LM teleportation can reduce data collection costs, and whether the method generalizes to long-horizon or multi-turn evaluation settings.
