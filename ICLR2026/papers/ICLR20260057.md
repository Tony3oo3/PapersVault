## Problem

Mutual information estimation remains difficult in high-dimensional and high-MI regimes, where classical non-parametric methods suffer from the curse of dimensionality and existing diffusion-based estimators like MINDE introduce a non-vanishing bias term by framing MI as a noise-to-data generative task. This paper identifies that the domain-transfer formulation of bridge matching, which operates on data-to-data trajectories, can yield an unbiased MI estimator with substantially lower variance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired samples (x0, x1) drawn from the joint distribution π(x0, x1) of arbitrary dimensionality (tested from 2D up to 1024-dim protein embeddings and 160-dim high-MI settings). A single neural network with a binary conditioning input s∈{0,1} approximates both the joint and independent drifts simultaneously. The output is a scalar MI estimate computed as a Monte Carlo average of the squared L2 distance between the two drift predictions at sampled bridge points, requiring no SDE simulation at inference time.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 4.1) shows that MI(X0;X1) equals the KL divergence between two reciprocal processes induced by the joint and product-of-marginals distributions, which by the Girsanov theorem decomposes into (1/2ε)∫E‖v_joint − v_ind‖²dt. Both drifts are learned via conditional bridge matching: a regression where the target is (x1−xt)/(1−t) with xt sampled from a Brownian bridge between x0 and x1. Training alternates between the joint loss (using true pairs) and the independent loss (using permuted pairs), with a single U-Net or MLP conditioned on a binary flag s to distinguish the two drifts. At estimation time, the MI is computed by sampling (x0, x1) from the joint, drawing bridge points xt, and averaging the squared difference of the two network outputs — a simulation-free procedure that is unbiased and exhibits 1–2 orders of magnitude lower variance than MINDE's noise-to-data trajectories.

## Results

On the image-based benchmark (16×16 and 32×32 Gaussian/Rectangle images), InfoBridge achieves a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.38 with an average standard deviation of 0.07, outperforming the previous best method MIENF (MAE 0.45) and the diffusion-based MINDE-C (MAE 0.56, std 0.43). On protein language model embeddings (ProtTrans5, 1024-dim), InfoBridge achieves MAE 0.04, while MINDE-C drastically overestimates MI with MAE 9.29. On the high-MI benchmark (d ∈ {20,40,80,160}, MI ∈ {10,20,40,80}), InfoBridge yields the most accurate estimates, while MINE, InfoNCE, and fDIME fail to capture high MI values entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoBridge outperforms all eight baselines on the image benchmark, achieving MAE 0.38 vs. MIENF (0.45), MINDE-C (0.56), MINE (0.92), KSG (1.15), NWJ (1.24), InfoNCE (1.44), and MINDE-J (1.66). The most striking advantage is in estimation stability: InfoBridge's average standard deviation (0.07) is 6–7× lower than MINDE-C (0.43) and MINDE-J (0.45), meaning its MI estimates are far more reproducible across seeds. On the protein embeddings benchmark, InfoBridge (MAE 0.04) is the only method that consistently estimates MI accurately, while MINDE-C overestimates by nearly two orders of magnitude (MAE 9.29). On the high-MI benchmark, MINE, InfoNCE, and fDIME all fail to track increasing MI values, and MINDE-C consistently underperforms InfoBridge. On the low-dimensional benchmark, InfoBridge matches MINDE in 83% of cases and is superior to classical (KSG, NWJ) and flow-based (NVF, JVF) methods, with the sole exception being the Cauchy distribution (no first moment), where a tail-shortening asinh transform is needed. The flow-based baselines NVF and JVF (Dahlke & Pacheco, 2025) are competitive on low-dimensional tasks but are not included in the image or high-MI comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Diffusion Bridge Matching framework (originally developed for data-to-data translation in generative modeling) for mutual information estimation, framing MI as a domain transfer problem between the joint and product-of-marginals distributions. The most direct prior work is MINDE (Franzese et al., 2024), which also uses diffusion models for MI but frames it as a noise-to-data generative task with standard backward diffusions, introducing an intractable bias term. The theoretical core draws on reciprocal processes, the Girsanov theorem, and the Doob h-transform from the Schrödinger Bridge literature, while the practical algorithm follows the conditional bridge matching regression procedure established by Zhou et al. (2024) and Shi et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is an unbiased MI estimator with 1–2 orders of magnitude lower Monte Carlo variance than MINDE, making reliable estimation feasible in high-dimensional and high-MI regimes where discriminative methods (MINE, InfoNCE) and flow-based methods (MIENF) degrade. The framework naturally extends to KL divergence, differential entropy, and interaction information estimation, broadening its utility beyond pairwise MI. A notable limitation is the finite first-moment requirement, which excludes heavy-tailed distributions (e.g., Cauchy) without auxiliary transforms. Open directions include alternative bridge SDEs (variance-preserving), time reweighting, and scaling to even higher-dimensional real-world data.
