## Problem

Existing deep-research agents rely on a mono-contextual paradigm that linearly accumulates all retrieved information and reasoning steps into a single expanding context window, causing context suffocation (shrinking reasoning space) and noise contamination (irreversible embedding of irrelevant information). This fundamentally limits sustained reasoning on long-horizon tasks, as performance degrades progressively with interaction depth and the approach is structurally infeasible beyond a few dozen interactions.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a research question and interacts with four tools (Google Search, Google Scholar, a web browser with goal-oriented summarization, and a Python interpreter) over multiple rounds. At each round, the agent's output is a structured triple of Think (internal reasoning), Report (an updated compressed memory of validated findings), and Action (a tool call or final answer). Critically, the workspace is reconstructed to O(1) size each round—containing only the question, the evolving report, and the immediate prior action/response—rather than growing linearly with trajectory length, enabling theoretically unbounded exploration within a fixed 40K-token context.

## Method

IterResearch formalizes deep research as an MDP where each state is a strategically reconstructed workspace (question, evolving report, immediate context) rather than an ever-growing history, and the transition function discards prior trajectory while preserving synthesized knowledge in the report. The agent's policy generates composite decisions separating internal thought (Think + Report update) from external action, leveraging the LLM's inherent compression ability for selective retention. Training employs Efficiency-Aware Policy Optimization (EAPO), which adapts geometric reward discounting (r_t = γ^(T-t) · R_T) to incentivize concise exploration over exhaustive searching, combined with adaptive downsampling to handle variable per-round sample counts in distributed training. EAPO is built on GSPO and applied after a two-stage pipeline: rejection-sampling fine-tuning on 110K synthesized trajectories to instill the iterative paradigm, followed by RL on 4,096 calibrated questions (20–60% success rate) with T_max=32, yet the agent extrapolates to 2048 interactions at inference.

## Results

IterResearch-30B-A3B achieves an average accuracy of 49.1% across six deep-research benchmarks (HLE 28.8%, BrowseComp 37.3%, BrowseComp-zh 45.2%, GAIA 72.8%, Xbench-DeepSearch 71.0%, SEAL-0 39.6%), representing an average +14.5pp improvement over the best open-source agents. On interaction scaling, accuracy on BrowseComp rises from 5.5% at 2 turns to 50.1% at 2048 turns using only a 40K context window. As a prompting strategy (no training), IterResearch improves frontier models by +12.7pp (o3) and +19.2pp (DeepSeek-V3.1) over ReAct on BrowseComp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IterResearch outperforms all open-source agents by a wide margin, with the largest gains on BrowseComp (+20.1pp over MiroThinker-32B) and SEAL-0 (+18.9pp over WebDancer). Against proprietary systems, IterResearch surpasses OpenAI DeepResearch on HLE (28.8 vs 26.6), BrowseComp-zh (45.2 vs 42.9), and GAIA (72.8 vs 67.4), but trails on BrowseComp (37.3 vs 51.5). It also beats Kimi-Researcher on HLE (28.8 vs 26.9), Xbench-DeepSearch (71.0 vs 69.0), and SEAL-0 (39.6 vs 36.0). Among direct-inference baselines, the gap is most dramatic: IterResearch exceeds o4-mini by +9.9pp on HLE and +39.5pp on GAIA. As a prompting strategy, IterResearch improves o3 by +12.7pp and DeepSeek-V3.1 by +19.2pp over ReAct on BrowseComp, confirming model-agnostic benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the mono-contextual paradigm adopted by all existing open-source deep-research agents (Search-o1, WebThinker, WebDancer, WebSailor, Asearcher, MiroThinker), which linearly accumulate all retrieved information into a single expanding context window. IterResearch instead formalizes deep research as an MDP with iterative workspace reconstruction, where each state is a bounded, strategically rebuilt workspace containing only the question, an evolving report (compressed memory), and the immediate prior interaction. For training, it builds on Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) and adapts geometric reward discounting from classical RL (Sutton & Barto) to create efficiency-aware incentives, while the evolving-report mechanism conceptually parallels memory systems in LLM agents (MemOS, MemoryR1) but integrates memory natively into the decision output rather than as a separate module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interaction scaling to 2048 steps within a fixed 40K context window demonstrates a practical capability that mono-contextual approaches cannot structurally achieve, potentially unlocking genuinely complex research tasks that require extensive exploration. The finding that the paradigm works as a zero-training prompting strategy for frontier models (o3, DeepSeek-V3.1) suggests the architectural insight is model-agnostic and broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond web-based research to other long-horizon domains (e.g., multi-step planning, scientific discovery), whether the evolving report quality degrades over very long trajectories, and whether the efficiency gains from EAPO transfer to settings with denser intermediate reward signals. The cross-paradigm knowledge transfer result also raises the question of whether iterative-paradigm trajectories could serve as a general data augmentation strategy for mono-contextual agents.
