## Problem

Existing dynamic 3D reconstruction methods rely on offline, per-scene optimization over the full video sequence, requiring calibrated camera poses and hours of iterative computation, making them impractical for real-time deployment under sparse observations and strict latency constraints. The central question is whether feed-forward, online reconstruction can match the quality of these optimization-based pipelines while operating on uncalibrated video streams of arbitrary length.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264181/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes uncalibrated RGB video frames (resized to 512×288) sequentially with no camera intrinsics or extrinsics required; a pseudo-depth map from Depth Anything V2 is computed internally per frame. It maintains a persistent canonical 3D Gaussian set in a shared orthographic space that absorbs camera motion and perspective effects, and outputs rendered RGB and depth maps at arbitrary intermediate times and novel viewpoints. The entire pipeline runs at 0.049 s per frame on a single A100 GPU, enabling continuous online processing of arbitrarily long streams.

## Method

StreamSplat is a two-stage feed-forward framework built on a Transformer-based Static Encoder (10 layers, 768-dim) that maps a single RGB-D frame into pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians in an orthographic canonical space, and a Dynamic Decoder (10 layers, conditioned on DINOv2 features) that predicts per-Gaussian deformation fields between consecutive frames. Three key mechanisms distinguish it: (1) probabilistic position sampling, where 3D offsets and velocities are drawn from a predicted truncated normal distribution rather than regressed deterministically, promoting spatial exploration and avoiding local minima inherent to single-pass 3DGS prediction; (2) a bidirectional deformation field that jointly estimates forward (previous→current) and backward (current→previous) 3D velocities, yielding robust cross-frame associations without requiring a variable number of Gaussians; and (3) adaptive Gaussian fusion via a time-dependent opacity deformation (a sigmoid-gated fade-out window) that implicitly soft-matches persistent Gaussians while naturally handling emerging and vanishing content, eliminating the need for hard one-to-one matching or iterative fusion. Training proceeds in two stages—static encoder first, then dynamic decoder with the encoder frozen—using photometric, depth, and mask losses over randomly sampled frame pairs.

## Results

On DAVIS, StreamSplat achieves 37.83 PSNR / 0.982 SSIM / 0.016 LPIPS on key-frame reconstruction and 23.66 PSNR / 0.684 SSIM / 0.193 LPIPS on middle-4-frame interpolation, outperforming all optimization-based 3DGS baselines (CoDeF: 19.40 PSNR; DGMarbles: 21.33 PSNR) while running at 0.049 s/frame (~1200× faster). On RE10K, it reaches 29.51 average PSNR, surpassing all dynamic baselines by 5.78 dB over CoDeF. On the 8-frame interval task, StreamSplat (22.10 PSNR) outperforms both 2D video interpolation methods (AMT: 21.09, RIFE: 20.48) and 4D reconstruction methods (DGMarbles: 19.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DAVIS key-frame reconstruction, StreamSplat (37.83 PSNR) approaches MonST3R (42.33 PSNR) but is ~20× faster and uniquely supports intermediate-frame synthesis; it substantially outperforms CoDeF (31.49), DGMarbles (28.38), and 4DGS (18.12). On the middle-4-frame interpolation task, StreamSplat (23.66 PSNR) beats CoDeF (19.40) by 4.26 dB and DGMarbles (21.33) by 2.33 dB. On the 8-frame interval task, StreamSplat (22.10 PSNR) surpasses all 2D interpolation baselines (AMT 21.09, RIFE 20.48, FILM 20.71) and 4D methods (DGMarbles 19.83, CoDeF 20.34). On RE10K, StreamSplat (29.51 avg PSNR) outperforms all dynamic baselines (CoDeF 21.77, DGMarbles 23.73) by a large margin, and slightly edges out static feed-forward methods (MVSplat 29.34, pixelSplat 28.99) on average, though static methods still lead on novel-view PSNR (28.31–28.65 vs. 24.68) due to StreamSplat's lack of camera pose input. On zero-shot DyCheck and NVIDIA benchmarks, StreamSplat (12.37 and 16.30 avg PSNR) significantly outperforms DGMarbles without camera (9.76 and 13.91) while approaching methods with ground-truth camera parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamSplat sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) 3D Gaussian Splatting as a scene representation (Kerbl et al., 2023), (2) per-Gaussian deformation fields for dynamic scenes (Luiten et al., 2024; Duan et al., 2024), and (3) feed-forward, pixel-aligned 3DGS prediction with probabilistic sampling (Charatan et al., 2024). The key departure is that all prior dynamic 3DGS methods (4DGS, DGMarbles, Splatter a Video) require calibrated poses and hours of per-scene iterative optimization, while feed-forward 3DGS methods (pixelSplat, NoPoSplat, StreamGS) handle only static scenes. StreamSplat is the first to unify these into a single feed-forward pipeline that operates online on uncalibrated video, using a bidirectional deformation field and adaptive opacity-based fusion to replace the iterative matching and refinement steps that offline methods rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1200× speedup (49 ms/frame) and support for arbitrarily long uncalibrated streams make dynamic 3D reconstruction practically deployable in AR/VR, robotics, and autonomous driving—settings where per-scene optimization is infeasible. Key limitations remain: reliance on an external monocular depth estimator (Depth Anything V2) introduces noise at depth discontinuities, the two-frame deformation window can lose information under fast motion or extended occlusions, and orthographic projection may leave residual distortions in close-range scenes with strong perspective effects. Open questions include whether internal depth refinement can replace the external prior, how to extend the deformation window beyond two frames without sacrificing efficiency, and whether the approach scales to large-scale outdoor scenes with complex multi-object dynamics.
