## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based traffic scene reconstruction methods optimize global visual similarity metrics (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS), but this does not guarantee that a perception module produces consistent detection outputs on reconstructed versus original images. Since ADS behavior is driven by object-level perception rather than global image fidelity, and since even perception errors in ground truth should be faithfully reproduced for meaningful system testing, the paper identifies a gap between visual reconstruction quality and perception stability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261787/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view traffic scene images (320×480 per camera) and LiDAR point clouds from the Waymo dataset as input to base 3DGS pipelines (S³ Gaussian, OmniRe, EMD). The output is a 3DGS scene whose rendered frames, when passed through a frozen detection model (YOLOv8n, Faster R-CNN, or RT-DETR), yield bounding boxes and class labels consistent with those obtained from the original ground-truth frames. The perception model is never updated; it serves solely as a frozen reference whose outputs define the stability target.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary loss terms integrated into the fine-tuning stage of existing 3DGS training. First, a perception-aligned loss computes CIoU-based bounding box regression error and a classification mismatch penalty between the frozen detector's outputs on ground-truth and rendered frames, directly penalizing perception inconsistency at every training iteration. Second, an object zone quality loss computes a separate SSIM term restricted to the bounding-box regions identified by the detector on ground-truth images, reinforcing reconstruction fidelity specifically where objects appear without requiring per-iteration detector inference. Both losses are added to the standard visual reconstruction objective with equal weighting (λ=1), and the perception model remains frozen throughout training. The object zone loss is computationally cheaper (negligible overhead vs. ~10% increase for the perception-aligned loss) and provides more stable improvements, while combining both yields the best overall perception stability across all tested base methods and detector architectures.

## Results

On the Waymo dataset, integrating both the perception-aligned loss and object zone quality loss into S3Gaussian raises YOLOv8 mAP from 0.550 to 0.700 and mean IoU from 0.803 to 0.872, while reducing missed detections from 1.5 to 0.0. The same combined losses improve EMD(S3G) mean IoU from 0.755 to 0.860 and OmniRe mAP from 0.489 to 0.609. Visual quality (SSIM) is maintained or slightly improved (e.g., S3Gaussian SSIM 0.924→0.941), and gains transfer to unseen detectors: Faster R-CNN mAP on S3Gaussian rises from 0.171 to 0.269, and RT-DETR mAP on OmniRe jumps from 0.519 to 0.675.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its two proposed losses (perception-aligned loss and object zone quality loss) against three state-of-the-art 3DGS baselines—S³Gaussian, OmniRe, and EMD (in both S³G and OmniRe variants)—all evaluated on Waymo scenes. The combined losses consistently outperform every baseline on all perception metrics: on S³Gaussian, YOLOv8 mAP improves by +0.150 (0.550→0.700) and mean IoU by +0.069 (0.803→0.872), with missed detections dropping from 1.5 to 0.0. The largest relative gain appears on EMD(S³G), where mean IoU jumps from 0.755 to 0.860 (+0.105), indicating that the object zone quality loss particularly addresses the edge-blurring weakness of LiDAR-initialized Gaussians. OmniRe, which already had zero missed detections, still sees mAP rise from 0.489 to 0.609 and mean IoU from 0.832 to 0.870. Crucially, improvements transfer to unseen detectors: Faster R-CNN mAP on S³Gaussian increases from 0.171 to 0.269, and RT-DETR mAP on OmniRe surges from 0.519 to 0.675, confirming that the gains reflect genuine reconstruction quality rather than overfitting to the training-time detector. Visual quality (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS) is preserved or marginally improved across all baselines, with fluctuations under 1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3DGS-based traffic scene reconstruction line (S3 Gaussian, OmniRe, EMD) but departs from their shared optimization paradigm of maximizing global visual similarity (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS). Rather than proposing a new reconstruction architecture, it introduces two plug-and-play loss terms—a perception-aligned loss using CIoU and class-consistency, and an object zone quality loss—that can be integrated into any existing 3DGS training pipeline. The conceptual departure is framing reconstruction quality through the lens of downstream perception stability rather than pixel-level fidelity, drawing on the perception robustness literature (Dong et al., Liu et al.) to motivate why object-region fidelity matters more than global image similarity for ADS testing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a practical gap: high visual similarity in reconstructed scenes does not reliably preserve perception module outputs, which undermines the core use case of reconstruction for ADS development and testing. The proposed losses are simple, architecture-agnostic, and the object zone quality loss in particular avoids per-iteration detector inference, making it easy to adopt. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D detection (the paper only evaluates 2D bounding boxes), whether adaptive loss weighting can better balance visual and perceptual objectives across diverse scene compositions, and whether the principle generalizes to other perception-critical domains (robotics, AR/VR) as the authors speculate but do not demonstrate.
