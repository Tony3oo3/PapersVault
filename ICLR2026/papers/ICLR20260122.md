## Problem

RL-trained LLM agents learn a fixed policy during training and struggle to actively explore or adapt from trial-and-error experiences at test time. Existing approaches either focus on single-turn reasoning or rely on offline data for imitation, leaving a gap for agents that can gather environment feedback across multiple attempts and leverage it for improved decision-making.

## Input / Output

At each step within an episode, the agent receives a text observation (grid state, board layout, or web page) plus the task instruction and produces a text action (direction, cell coordinate, or search query) wrapped in <action> tags. After each failed episode, the agent additionally generates a self-reflection in <remark> tags summarizing mistakes and an improved plan. This reflection, along with the full trajectory history, is concatenated into the inter-episode memory H^(n) that conditions the policy for the next episode, with no parameter updates between episodes.

## Method

LAMER formulates agent training as a Meta-RL problem over a multi-episode trial structure: each trial consists of N=3 sequential episodes on the same task, and the objective maximizes a discounted return G_t^(n) that sums within-episode returns plus cross-episode returns weighted by a trajectory discount factor γ_traj. This cross-episode credit assignment incentivizes the agent to explore in early episodes (gathering diverse information) and exploit in later ones. The inner-loop adaptation is implemented entirely in-context via self-reflection: after each episode, the agent is prompted to reflect on its trajectory and produce a concise improved plan, which is appended to the context for the next episode. Training uses standard policy gradient methods (GiGPO by default) with the cross-episode return for advantage estimation, and the reflection step itself is trained using the reward from the subsequent episode. The key distinction from standard RL is that episodes within a trial are sequentially dependent (conditioned on prior reflections) rather than independent, and the learned policy encodes an explore-then-exploit strategy that activates at test time without any gradient updates.

## Results

On the three primary environments, LAMER achieves pass@3 success rates of 55.9% (Sokoban), 74.4% (MineSweeper), and 89.1% (Webshop), outperforming the strongest RL baseline GiGPO by 11.8, 19.3, and 13.9 percentage points respectively. LAMER also demonstrates stronger test-time scaling: from pass@1 to pass@3 on Sokoban it gains 13.5%, far exceeding the <5% gains of RL and prompting baselines. On the ALFWorld out-of-distribution generalization task, LAMER reaches 81.0% on Cool and 50.2% on Pick2, surpassing the RL baseline by 22.9 and 14.2 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAMER outperforms all six baselines across every environment and attempt count. Against the strongest RL baseline GiGPO, LAMER gains +11.8 pp on Sokoban pass@3 (55.9 vs 44.1), +19.3 pp on MineSweeper pass@3 (74.4 vs 55.1), and +13.9 pp on Webshop pass@3 (89.1 vs 75.2). The gap widens with more attempts: on Sokoban, LAMER's pass@2 (52.0) already exceeds GiGPO's pass@3 (44.1), and on MineSweeper LAMER's pass@2 (66.4) surpasses GiGPO's pass@3 (55.1). Weaker RL baselines (PPO, RLOO, GRPO) are further behind, with GRPO reaching only 27.0% on Sokoban and 40.4% on MineSweeper at pass@3. Prompting baselines (Zero-shot, ReAct, Reflexion) are substantially outperformed, with ReAct achieving at most 12.5% on Sokoban and 10.9% on MineSweeper at pass@3. Notably, LAMER starts slightly below GiGPO on pass@1 for MineSweeper (44.1 vs 52.0) and Webshop (67.8 vs 73.4), but recovers and surpasses by pass@2, reflecting its learned exploration-then-exploitation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAMER combines two established ideas in a novel way for LLM agents: the cross-episode credit assignment from Meta-RL (Duan et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2016) and the verbal self-reflection mechanism from Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023). Unlike prior Meta-RL work that uses RNN-based inner loops or gradient-based adaptation (Finn et al., 2017), LAMER implements the inner loop entirely in-context via self-reflection, leveraging the LLM's in-context learning ability. It departs from recent test-time exploration approaches (Tajwar et al., 2025; Gandhi et al., 2024; Setlur et al., 2025) that either target single-turn reasoning or rely on offline imitation data, by formulating exploration as a cross-episode RL objective optimized online with environment feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first Meta-RL framework applied to LLM agent training, providing a principled mechanism to balance exploration and exploitation across multiple attempts at a task. The consistent 11–19% gains over strong RL baselines (GiGPO) across four diverse environments, combined with superior test-time scaling (pass@k) and out-of-distribution generalization, suggest that learned exploration strategies transfer to harder and novel tasks. Key limitations include the ~2× training time overhead from sequential episode generation, evaluation restricted to 4B–8B models, and text-only modality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether more efficient asynchronous sampling can close the training-time gap, and whether Meta-RL-trained agents can generalize to entirely novel environment types.
