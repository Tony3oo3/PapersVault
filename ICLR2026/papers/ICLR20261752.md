## Problem

Existing RLVR methods for autoregressive image generation, particularly GRPO, apply uniform policy optimization across all image tokens, implicitly assuming each token contributes equally to the training objective. However, different tokens play fundamentally distinct roles—early tokens establish global structure via causal attention, high-entropy-gradient tokens bridge spatial regions, and low-similarity tokens carry the diversity signal that drives group-based reward learning. The paper addresses the unexplored question of how to identify these critical tokens and implement effective token-wise optimization for them in AR visual generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261752/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated image via autoregressive next-token prediction over a VQ-VAE codebook, operating at 256×256 (LlamaGen) or 384×384 (Janus-Pro) resolution. During RL training, a group of G images is sampled per prompt, and the method selects only ~30% of the token sequence (10% each from three criteria) for gradient computation, while the remaining 70% of tokens are excluded from the policy update. The approach is applied to both standalone AR models (LlamaGen, Janus-Pro 1B/7B) and a scale-wise next-scale prediction model (Star).

## Method

GCPO modifies the GRPO objective in two ways: (1) it restricts policy gradient computation to a union of three critical-token subsets—initial tokens (causal dependency), tokens with the largest 2D entropy-gradient (spatial structure, computed via local averaging and central difference on the reshaped entropy map), and tokens with the lowest intra-group cosine similarity at each position (RLVR-focused diversity); and (2) it multiplies each critical token's advantage by a dynamic weight w_t defined as the cumulative mean of clipped confidence divergence (log-probability difference) between the policy and reference models up to position t. This dynamic weight naturally allocates stronger exploration to structure-related tokens while constraining early tokens to prevent global structural collapse. By optimizing only 30% of tokens with this token-wise weighting, GCPO outperforms standard GRPO on all 100% tokens across GenEval, T2I-CompBench, and human-preference benchmarks, with no additional computational overhead.

## Results

Using only 30% of image tokens, GCPO outperforms GRPO (which uses 100% of tokens) across all three base models. On GenEval, Janus-Pro-7B+GCPO achieves an overall score of 0.90 vs. 0.87 for GRPO, with the largest gain on Counting (+0.19, from 0.71 to 0.90). On T2I-CompBench, GCPO delivers up to 20% relative improvements on Shape (+0.117), Texture (+0.118), and Spatial (+0.134) over GRPO, while adding only ~1% training time overhead (14.89s vs. 14.75s per step for Janus-Pro-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCPO consistently outperforms GRPO (its direct RL baseline) on all three base models: Janus-Pro-7B improves from 0.87 to 0.90 overall on GenEval, Janus-Pro-1B from 0.84 to 0.85, and LlamaGen from 0.39 to 0.42, while using only 30% of tokens versus 100%. The most dramatic gain is on the Counting sub-task (+0.19 for Janus-Pro-7B), indicating that critical-token selection particularly benefits compositional reasoning. On T2I-CompBench, GCPO surpasses GRPO by up to 0.134 on Spatial and 0.118 on Texture for Janus-Pro-7B, demonstrating strong generalization beyond the GenEval-style training distribution. Against other AR+RL methods, Janus-Pro-7B+GCPO (0.90) outperforms T2I-R1 (0.79) and Focus-Diff (0.85) on GenEval overall. Among all open-source methods, GCPO achieves the highest GenEval score (0.90), surpassing GPT-4o (0.85) and all diffusion-based models (best: FLUX.1-dev at 0.66). The method adds negligible computational cost (~1% extra time per training step) relative to standard GRPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

GCPO directly extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) to autoregressive image generation by introducing token-level selectivity: rather than applying uniform policy optimization across all image tokens, it identifies a subset of 'critical tokens' (~30%) from three complementary perspectives—causal dependency of early tokens, entropy-gradient-based spatial structure, and intra-group token diversity—and optimizes only on those. The critical-token concept is borrowed from concurrent LLM reasoning work (Wang et al., 2025d,a) that identified high-entropy 'fork tokens' as driving reasoning, but the paper adapts this to the visually-specific setting where entropy alone is prompt-dependent and the entropy *gradient* (not entropy itself) serves as a more reliable structural proxy. A dynamic advantage weight based on policy-reference confidence divergence further differentiates GCPO from standard GRPO, which allocates uniform advantage across all tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 30% of carefully selected tokens outperform 100% of tokens under uniform GRPO is a practical and somewhat counterintuitive result for RLVR-based visual generation, suggesting that token-level heterogeneity in contribution is a first-order factor that prior methods have overlooked. The entropy-gradient-as-structure-proxy observation provides a lightweight, model-agnostic signal that could be reused in other AR or diffusion-based generation pipelines. Open questions include whether the 10%-per-type ratio generalizes to higher resolutions or video generation, whether the three selection criteria are truly independent at scale, and whether the approach transfers to non-VQ-VAE tokenizers (e.g., continuous or frequency-domain tokens). The method is also limited to relatively small models (≤7B) and 384×384 resolution in experiments.
