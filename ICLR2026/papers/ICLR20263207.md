## Problem

Current LLM decoding methods, including top-k, top-p, and temperature sampling, operate independently at the token level and fail to learn from previously generated responses. This causes autoregressive models to repeatedly exploit high-probability regions, producing structurally similar outputs that share common beginnings and only diverge in local details, leading to redundant generations and degeneration issues like repetition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263207/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a pretrained LLM, a set of text contexts, a number of decoding rounds T, and a reweighting parameter λ, the method produces T diverse responses per context. Unusually, the method performs inference-time fine-tuning of low-rank output head adapters between rounds, and the reweighted distribution is computed as a normalized ratio (p/q_t)^λ · p, where p is the original model's next-token distribution and q_t is the guide model's distribution. The parallel version integrates directly with LLM serving systems (vLLM, SGLang) by sorting duplicated requests by round number to synchronize sampling and adapter updates within the KV-cache-reuse pipeline.

## Method

OverRIDE iteratively fine-tunes a guide model M_qt on previously generated responses via cross-entropy loss, then reweights the original model's next-token distribution as p_t(x_i|x_<i) ∝ (p(x_i|x_<i)/q_t(x_i|x_<i))^λ · p(x_i|x_<i), suppressing tokens that lead to previously seen semantic patterns while preserving fidelity to the original model. The reweighting formula is derived as the closed-form solution to a constrained optimization that minimizes KL divergence from the original distribution while maximizing the expected log-likelihood ratio against the pattern distribution. For efficient parallel decoding, trainable parameters are restricted to LoRA-style low-rank adapters (rank r=16) on the output head only, enabling both p and q_t to be computed in a single forward pass from shared hidden states. A synchronized fine-tuning scheme updates the adapter for round t+1 immediately after sampling at round t, making the method compatible with the sequential request processing of serving systems. This design yields only 6.4% throughput loss for 72B models under parallel decoding on vLLM.

## Results

On HumanEval with Qwen-2.5-7B and Top-p (τ=0.6), OverRIDE improves PASS@5 from 78.4% to 81.6% and PASS@10 from 81.4% to 86.4%, while reducing pairwise CodeBLEU from 0.754 to 0.610. On MATH500, PASS@10 rises from 86.8% to 87.3% and cosine similarity drops from 0.950 to 0.936. On CCNews, MAUVE improves from 0.901 to 0.938 while similarity decreases from 0.726 to 0.678. Under parallel decoding on vLLM, OverRIDE incurs only a 6.4% throughput loss for 72B models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Contrastive Decoding (CD) on GPT2-XL/CCNews, OverRIDE achieves MAUVE 0.846 vs. CD's 0.752 and similarity 0.664 vs. 0.676, winning on both quality and diversity. Against SimCTG on Qwen-2.5-7B, OverRIDE substantially outperforms on HumanEval (PASS@10: 86.4% vs. 77.2%; CodeBLEU: 0.610 vs. 0.747) and MATH500 (PASS@10: 87.3% vs. 86.8%; similarity: 0.936 vs. 0.954), but trails on CCNews MAUVE (0.938 vs. 0.976) while still winning on similarity (0.678 vs. 0.756). On Mistral-7B, OverRIDE edges out SimCTG on HumanEval (PASS@10: 56.9% vs. 56.1%), MATH500 (PASS@10: 37.4% vs. 36.8%), and CCNews (MAUVE: 0.952 vs. 0.915; similarity: 0.703 vs. 0.758). Overall, OverRIDE wins or ties on the majority of metrics across both baselines, with its largest margins appearing on code generation tasks where diversity gains are most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

OverRIDE extends the guided-decoding paradigm (contrastive decoding, DoLa, SimCTG) by making the guidance dynamic and history-aware: instead of contrasting against a fixed amateur model or a different layer, it fine-tunes a lightweight guide model at inference time on previously generated responses. The output-head adapter architecture borrows directly from LoRA, but repurposes it for inference-time fine-tuning of a single head rather than training-time adaptation of the full model. The reweighting formula is derived as the closed-form solution to a KL-divergence-constrained optimization (Appendix D), providing a theoretical grounding that prior contrastive or unlikelihood-based diversity methods lack. Unlike unlikelihood training or SimCTG, which require extensive pre-training on large corpora, OverRIDE operates entirely at inference time with minimal overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OverRIDE addresses a practical bottleneck in LLM serving: when multiple diverse outputs are needed (ensembles, creative generation, code search), standard sampling methods produce structurally similar responses that share the same errors. The 6.4% throughput loss for 72B models under parallel decoding makes this approach deployable in production systems like vLLM. Open questions include the model-specific sensitivity of λ (requiring per-model tuning), the safety-alignment risk of pushing the model into low-probability regions, and whether the approach generalizes to multimodal or agentic settings where 'diversity' has different semantics. The finding that larger models benefit more from OverRIDE despite smaller adapter proportions suggests the method may scale favorably to frontier models.
