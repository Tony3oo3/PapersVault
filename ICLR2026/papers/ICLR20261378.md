## Problem

Existing multi-agent LLM orchestration methods rely on fixed topologies, pretrained graph generators, edge-level reinforcement learning, or external LLM judges to determine communication structure, all of which introduce substantial overhead and assume a static optimal topology per task or query. Because LLM agents are inherently stochastic, the same agent may produce correct or incorrect responses across runs, making any predetermined topology brittle. The paper asks whether communication structure can instead be determined on-the-fly from the agents' actual current responses, without any additional training or external supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and N independently generated LLM agent responses as input. Each response is mapped to a fixed-dimensional embedding (384-dim via all-MiniLM-L6-v2, a 22.7M-parameter sentence encoder separate from the agent LLMs) to compute pairwise cosine similarities and contribution scores. The output is a final text response selected (not generated) from the pool of existing agent responses as the one closest to a contribution-weighted embedding centroid, along with a per-round directed acyclic communication graph and per-agent Shapley-inspired contribution scores that are recomputed after each collaboration round.

## Method

SELFORG operates in iterative rounds: agents first answer independently, then their response embeddings are used to estimate contributions via a linear-time Shapley approximation (cosine similarity to the mean embedding, with a formal approximation bound and ranking-stability guarantee from Theorem 1). A directed acyclic graph is constructed by activating edges only between semantically aligned peers (cosine similarity ≥ τ) where the source has strictly higher contribution than the target; any remaining cycles are broken by removing the edge from the weaker to the stronger agent, ensuring information flows from high- to low-contribution nodes. In subsequent rounds, agents process in topological order, incorporating upstream peer responses into their prompts, and the graph is reformulated from the updated responses. The final answer is the existing response nearest to the contribution-weighted centroid. Theoretically, the paper proves that correct responses form tight embedding clusters while incorrect ones scatter (Assumption 1), so correct agents dominate contribution scores (Lemma 2) and the DAG preferentially routes information from them (Corollary 2), explaining why the framework amplifies correctness most in the weak-backend regime.

## Results

On Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct, SELFORG achieves a mean accuracy of 45.05% across seven reasoning benchmarks (MATH, GSM8K, AQUA-RAT, GSM-Hard, MMLU, MMLU-Pro, AIME), outperforming the best single-agent baseline (41.24%) by ~4 points and the strongest multi-agent baseline (DyLAN, 37.82%) by ~7 points. On larger backbones, SELFORG reaches 70.19% average on LLaMA-3.3-70B-Instruct and 68.95% on Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct, attaining the best average rank (1.25 and 1.38 respectively) across all methods. The gains are most pronounced in the weak-backend regime, where all multi-agent baselines cluster around 33–37% average accuracy while SELFORG maintains a clear margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SELFORG outperforms all six baselines on every benchmark in the weak-backend setting (Qwen-1.5B). Against CoT (Wei et al., 2022), SELFORG gains ~4 points in average accuracy (45.05% vs. 41.18%), with the largest per-benchmark margin on AQUA-RAT (+4.7 pts) and MMLU (+3.2 pts). Against DyLAN (Liu et al., 2024), which uses a dedicated LLM judge for scoring, SELFORG leads by ~7.2 points on average, with DyLAN collapsing on MMLU-Pro (15.40% vs. 31.60%). MacNet (Qian et al., 2025) with random topology trails SELFORG by ~8.5 points, and G-Designer (Zhang et al., 2025b), which trains a separate graph generator, lags by ~11.6 points—its learned topology fails to adapt to noisy weak-agent responses. AgentVerse (Chen et al., 2024) is outperformed by ~8.7 points on average, while AutoGen (Wu et al., 2024) collapses dramatically (18.93% vs. 45.05%), particularly on MATH (11.60% vs. 52.40%) and GSM-Hard (5.40% vs. 38.00%). On stronger backbones (LLaMA-70B, Qwen-72B), SELFORG still achieves the best average rank (1.25 and 1.38) with modest but consistent gains of 1.7–2.1 points over the best baseline, confirming that the advantage persists but diminishes as single-agent reliability increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

SELFORG departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM multi-agent orchestration, which relies on fixed topologies, pretrained graph generators (G-Designer, MAS-GPT), edge-level reinforcement learning (GPTSwarm, AgentPrune), or external LLM judges. Instead, it repurposes the Shapley value approximation from cooperative game theory (Shapley, 1953; Xu et al., 2021) as a lightweight, training-free contribution estimator over response embeddings, and uses the resulting scores to dynamically construct a per-instance directed acyclic communication graph. The key conceptual shift is from task- or query-level topology design to a state-driven, response-conditioned approach: because LLM agents are stochastic, the paper argues the communication structure should adapt to the concrete responses at each round rather than to a static task label.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is practically important because it is training-free, model-agnostic, and requires no external judge or pretrained generator, making it broadly deployable across diverse LLM backbones and task domains. The most significant finding is the large performance gap in the weak-backend regime (Qwen-1.5B: ~45% vs. ~33–37% for all multi-agent baselines), where prior orchestration methods effectively collapse. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large agent pools (N > 10), how it performs on open-ended generation tasks beyond multiple-choice or short-answer reasoning, and whether the diminishing returns observed at 72B scale indicate a fundamental ceiling for response-conditioned orchestration when individual agents are already highly reliable.
