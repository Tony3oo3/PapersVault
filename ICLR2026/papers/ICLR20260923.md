## Problem

In semi-supervised graph anomaly detection with only labeled normal nodes, existing generative methods synthesize pseudo-anomalies via feature interpolation or random noise perturbation but lack any quantitative mechanism to assess the anomaly degree of generated nodes, resulting in low-representativeness synthetic samples that fail to capture meaningful anomalous patterns. This paper addresses the gap by introducing topology-based metrics that principledly quantify node abnormality before selecting pseudo-anomalies for training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attributed graph G=(V, A, X) with a small set of labeled normal nodes V_l (sampled at rate ρ from normal nodes; all anomaly nodes remain unlabeled) and a K-hop neighborhood (K=2) for topological computation. It outputs a continuous anomaly score per node via a GNN-based scoring function f: V → R, where lower scores indicate normality, and a binary normal/anomalous classification. The pseudo-anomaly selection threshold τ (default 0.05) controls what fraction of labeled nodes are re-designated as pseudo-anomalies for training.

## Method

TAQ-GAD operates in two stages. First, the Topological Anomaly Quantification (TAQ) module scores each labeled normal node using two metrics: the Node Boundary Score (NBS), which measures the fraction of K-hop neighbors that are *not* labeled normal, and the Proxy Isolation Score (PIS), which combines local clustering density (edges among neighbors normalized by possible edges) with a logarithmic degree penalty; the top τ-fraction of highest-scoring nodes become pseudo-anomalies. Second, the Topological Anomaly Enhancement (TAE) module computes per-node risk scores from prediction uncertainty relative to class-averaged uncertainty, applies a neighborhood-guided label-flipping strategy for high-risk nodes, then generates virtual anomaly centroid nodes and probabilistically samples edges between nodes and centroids weighted by risk and posterior class probability, yielding an augmented graph G_new. Training uses a GNN encoder with a joint loss combining Frobenius-norm regularization on embeddings and binary cross-entropy over both ground-truth and refined pseudo-labels.

## Results

Under the semi-supervised setting (ρ=15% labeled normal nodes), TAQ-GAD achieves the best AUROC on all five benchmark datasets: Amazon (0.9474), T-Finance (0.8675), Reddit (0.6682), Elliptic (0.7453), and Photo (0.7107). It also attains the highest AUPRC across all datasets, with particularly large margins on Elliptic (0.3573 vs. 0.2425 for GGAD) and Photo (0.2073 vs. 0.1420). On the extremely imbalanced DGraph dataset, TAQ-GAD reaches AUROC 0.6623 and AUPRC 0.0162 at 0.5% label rate, nearly doubling GGAD's AUPRC (0.0083).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAQ-GAD outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset for both AUROC and AUPRC in the semi-supervised setting. The closest competitor is GGAD (the prior generative semi-supervised method), which TAQ-GAD surpasses on all five datasets: e.g., T-Finance AUROC 0.8675 vs. 0.8228 (+4.5 pts), Elliptic AUPRC 0.3573 vs. 0.2425 (+11.5 pts), and Photo AUROC 0.7107 vs. 0.6476 (+6.3 pts). Among non-generative semi-supervised baselines, CHRN is the strongest on Amazon (0.9346 AUROC) and Elliptic (0.7315 AUROC), but TAQ-GAD still leads by 1.3 and 1.4 points respectively. SpaceGNN is competitive on T-Finance AUROC (0.8326) but falls well behind TAQ-GAD (0.8675) and is weakest on Reddit (0.5010). Unsupervised methods (DOMINANT, AnomalyDAE, OCGNN, AEGIS, GAAN, TAM) are consistently inferior, confirming the value of even limited normal labels. On the highly imbalanced DGraph dataset, TAQ-GAD nearly doubles GGAD's AUPRC (0.0162 vs. 0.0083 at 0.5% label rate), highlighting its advantage under extreme class imbalance.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAQ-GAD directly extends the generative semi-supervised GAD line initiated by GGAD (Qiao et al., 2024), which synthesizes pseudo-anomaly nodes to address label scarcity. It departs from prior generative strategies—feature interpolation (GraphENS, AuGAN) and noise perturbation (DAGAD, GGAD)—by replacing stochastic or unguided sample construction with a quantitative topological selection mechanism (NBS and PIS scores) that explicitly measures how 'anomaly-like' a candidate node is in graph structure. The TAE module, which generates virtual anomaly centroid nodes and rewires topological connections, is a new architectural component not present in any prior generative GAD method, adding a structural augmentation layer on top of the pseudo-labeling pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important and underexplored setting—semi-supervised GAD where only normal labels are available—and provides a principled, topology-driven alternative to ad-hoc pseudo-anomaly generation. The consistent improvements over GGAD across six datasets and multiple label rates, particularly the roughly 2× AUPRC gain on the extremely imbalanced DGraph benchmark, suggest the topological quantification approach is robust to class imbalance. However, open questions remain: the method relies on a fixed K-hop neighborhood (K=2) and the theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1–2) are stated under homophily assumptions that may not hold in heterophilic or dynamic graphs; scalability to very large graphs and extension to temporal settings are acknowledged as future work.
