## Problem

Existing temporal causal discovery methods assume static or partially stationary causal structures, failing to capture fully dynamic causality where both instantaneous and lagged dependencies evolve over time. This gap is critical because real-world systems (e.g., patient disease trajectories, traffic flows) exhibit causal relationships that emerge, change, or vanish, and learning such structures is further complicated by the need for efficient, stable optimization and reliable acyclicity enforcement under rapidly changing graphs.

## Input / Output

The method takes N multivariate time series of length T with d variables each, processed through a sliding convolutional window of width K and step size S. It outputs a sequence of time-varying weighted adjacency matrices {W_t ∈ R^{d×dτ}} for t = τ+1 to T, where each matrix encodes both instantaneous (d×d) and lagged (d×d per lag) causal weights; after threshold-based pruning, these yield a sequence of sparse dynamic causal graphs. The reconstruction is performed window-by-window, with each W_t responsible for reconstructing K consecutive time steps, effectively augmenting the sample size per matrix.

## Method

DyCausal encodes causal structures at coarse-grained time steps using a CNN over sliding windows, then refines to every time step via linear interpolation (first-order Taylor approximation), reducing the number of matrices that must be explicitly learned from T to ⌊(T−K)/S⌋. A parallel decoding strategy splits the state vector into m sub-vectors decoded by independent MLPs, cutting parameter count from O(d³m²τ) to O(d²mτ + d²m²τ). The key theoretical contribution is a new acyclic constraint h_norm that scales W∘W by its matrix 1-norm before applying the log-determinant penalty, guaranteeing the argument always lies within the optimizable space (spectral radius < 1⁺) and eliminating the need for retraining or learning-rate rollback. Training uses a score-based objective combining reconstruction loss, L1 sparsity, and h_norm, solved via a central path approach with a decaying penalty coefficient μ. The framework extends naturally to nonlinear SEMs (m>1 hidden units) and ODE-based models by modifying the reconstruction equation.

## Results

On synthetic time series with fully dynamic causality (20 nodes, T=50), DyCausal achieves TPR 89.54%, F1 91.43%, and SHD 7.40 at the series endpoints (W1), substantially outperforming DYCAST (TPR 25.98%), DYNO (TPR 3.59%), and NTS-NO (TPR 3.89%). At 80 nodes, DyCausal maintains TPR 89.10% and F1 93.85% while DYNO and NTS-NO collapse to near-zero TPR (<1%). On the real-world CausalTime benchmark, DyCausal achieves the best Precision (87.41% on Traffic, 75.31% on AQI, 83.21% on Medical) and F1 (54.81%, 63.16%, 56.86%) across all three subsets, outperforming the next-best method CUTS+ by 7–38 percentage points in Precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyCausal dominates all baselines on fully dynamic causality: at the series endpoints (W₁, W₅₀) with 20 nodes, it achieves TPR ≈ 89% versus DYCAST's 26%, DYNO's 3.6%, and NTS-NO's 3.9%, reflecting that static methods (DYNO, NTS-NO) cannot track time-varying weights and DYCAST, limited to partially dynamic (instantaneous-only) causality, fails when lagged edges also change. At 80 nodes, DYCAST fails to converge entirely, while DYNO and NTS-NO drop to TPR < 1%; DyCausal retains TPR ≈ 89% thanks to its coarse-to-fine encoding that avoids per-timestep optimization. On the real-world CausalTime benchmark, DyCausal outperforms CUTS+ by 7.3 pp in Precision on Traffic (87.41 vs 80.10) and by 38.4 pp on Medical (83.21 vs 44.82), and beats JRNGC, NTS-NO, DYNO, SVAM, PCMCI, NGM, and TECDI on both Precision and F1 across all three subsets. On static-causality benchmarks (Table A4), DyCausal also achieves the lowest SHD and competitive TPR/F1 against DYNO, NTS-NO, PCMCI, SVAM, NGM, and TECDI, with significant runtime speedups attributed to the norm-scaled acyclic constraint h_norm.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyCausal extends the score-based temporal causal discovery framework established by DYNOTEARS and NTS-NOTEARS, which use vector autoregressive reconstruction with differentiable acyclic constraints to learn static causal graphs from time series. Its acyclic constraint h_norm is a direct modification of the log-determinant constraint from DAGMA, scaling W∘W by its matrix 1-norm to guarantee an always-differentiable optimizable space. The key departure is the coarse-to-fine encoding strategy: prior dynamic methods (DyCAST, Gao et al. 2023) model only partial dynamics (instantaneous or lagged edges), while DyCausal uses a sliding CNN window plus linear interpolation to recover fully time-varying causal matrices where all edges evolve simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fully dynamic causality is more faithful to real-world systems where causal relationships emerge, shift, and vanish (e.g., seasonal disease trajectories, traffic patterns), yet prior methods either assume stationarity or only partially model dynamics. The coarse-to-fine design offers a practical efficiency gain—computing causal matrices at coarse steps and interpolating between them—while the norm-scaled acyclic constraint eliminates the retraining and hyperparameter search that plagued h_log. Limitations include the assumption of regular (equally spaced) time series, smooth causality evolution, and shared causal structure across all series; extending to irregular sampling, non-smooth regime shifts, and dynamically entering/leaving variables remains open.
