## Problem

Recent time-series anomaly detection methods increasingly rely on heavy Transformer and Foundation model architectures that incur high computational costs and memory usage, yet fail to demonstrate significant performance gains over simpler methods under rigorous evaluation protocols. Meanwhile, representation-based approaches—particularly patch-level embedding methods—remain largely underexplored for time-series anomaly detection, leaving a gap between the architectural complexity of existing methods and the localized, short-range temporal patterns that anomalies actually disrupt.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261852/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PaAno consumes univariate or multivariate time-series sequences and extracts overlapping fixed-length patches (window size w=96) via a unit-stride sliding window, applying instance normalization to each patch to standardize channels to zero mean and unit variance. At inference, it produces a scalar anomaly score for every time step by averaging the patch-level scores of all w patches that contain that time step, where each patch-level score is the mean cosine distance to its k=3 nearest neighbors in a K-means-reduced memory bank of normal patch embeddings.

## Method

PaAno embeds each temporal patch into a 64-dimensional vector using a compact 4-layer 1D-CNN (kernel sizes [7,5,3,3], channels [128,256,128,64] with batch normalization and ReLU, followed by global average pooling). Training combines a triplet loss—where the positive is a randomly shifted version of the anchor within r=2 steps and the negative is the farthest patch in the minibatch by cosine distance—with a pretext loss that trains a classification head to predict whether two patches are temporally consecutive; the pretext loss is applied only during the first 20% of training with linear decay of its weight λ from 1 to 0. After training, only the patch encoder is retained, and a memory bank of all training patch embeddings is reduced to 10% of its original size via K-means coreset subsampling (selecting the vector closest to each cluster centroid). At inference, anomaly detection is purely retrieval-based: each query patch is compared against the reduced memory bank using cosine distance, requiring no model retraining and enabling online adaptation by simply updating the memory bank with recent normal embeddings.

## Results

On the TSB-AD benchmark, PaAno achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six evaluation measures on both univariate (TSB-AD-U) and multivariate (TSB-AD-M) time-series anomaly detection. On TSB-AD-U, PaAno scores 0.53 VUS-PR, 0.89 VUS-ROC, 0.49 Range-F1, 0.47 AUC-PR, 0.87 AUC-ROC, and 0.52 Point-F1, ranking first on every metric. On TSB-AD-M, it scores 0.43 VUS-PR, 0.79 VUS-ROC, 0.41 Range-F1, 0.38 AUC-PR, 0.76 AUC-ROC, and 0.43 Point-F1, again first on all measures. The model uses only 0.3M parameters and runs in 5.4s (univariate) / 9.7s (multivariate), substantially faster than Transformer-based baselines (48.9s–1220.8s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaAno outperforms all 48 baselines on every metric in both univariate and multivariate settings. On TSB-AD-U, the closest competitor is KAN-AD (VUS-PR 0.40 vs. PaAno's 0.53, a 13-point gap), while (Sub)-PCA achieves the second-best VUS-PR (0.42) but trails PaAno by 11 points; among Transformer methods, MOMENT (fine-tuned) is the strongest at 0.39 VUS-PR, still 14 points behind. On TSB-AD-M, KAN-AD again leads the baselines (VUS-PR 0.40 vs. 0.43), with PCA and DeepAnT/OmniAnomaly/DADA tied at 0.31; PaAno's margin is smaller here (3 points) but it still ranks first on all six measures. Transformer-based methods (AnomalyTransformer, DCdetector, OFA, TimesFM) consistently underperform, with VUS-PR scores between 0.09 and 0.30, despite having 4.8M–203.5M parameters. PaAno's 0.3M parameter count and 5.4s/9.7s run time make it 9–225× faster than the heaviest Transformer baselines while achieving superior accuracy, and it also beats the lightweight KAN-AD (<0.1M params) by a meaningful margin on VUS-PR in both settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

PaAno adapts the patch-based representation learning paradigm from visual anomaly detection (Yi & Yoon 2020; Defard et al. 2021; Roth et al. 2022) to the time-series domain, replacing 2D image patches with 1D temporal patches and a compact 1D-CNN encoder. It deliberately departs from the dominant trend of adopting Transformers and Foundation models for time-series anomaly detection, instead positioning itself within the underexplored representation-based paradigm (as opposed to forecasting- or reconstruction-based methods). The training objective combines triplet metric learning with a self-supervised pretext task (predicting temporal consecutiveness), drawing on both deep metric learning (Schroff et al. 2015) and the patch-level classification idea from Yi & Yoon (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PaAno provides strong empirical evidence that a 0.3M-parameter 1D-CNN can match or exceed 100M+ parameter Transformer and Foundation models on rigorous, threshold-independent evaluation, reinforcing the 'illusion of progress' critique and offering a practical, deployable alternative for real-time and resource-constrained settings. The method's robustness to hyperparameters and its simple online memory-bank update mechanism for non-stationary data make it attractive for industrial deployment. Open questions include whether the patch-based approach scales to very long-range dependencies (e.g., multi-day periodicities), how it performs under extreme distribution shift, and whether the representation-based paradigm can be extended to other sequential anomaly detection tasks beyond time series.
