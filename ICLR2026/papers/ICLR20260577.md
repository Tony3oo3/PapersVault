## Problem

Current agentic RL algorithms for LLM-based agents rely on trajectory-level rollout sampling, assigning rewards only to final outputs and neglecting the fine-grained, step-level exploration of multi-turn tool-call interactions. The paper identifies that LLMs exhibit sharply elevated token entropy in the first 10–50 tokens after each tool-call feedback, revealing latent tool-use behaviors that trajectory-level methods systematically under-explore.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260577/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the LLM receives a text question and iteratively generates tool calls (search queries, Python code, or web-browsing requests) within a multi-turn agentic loop, receiving real-time textual or numerical feedback from the tool environment after each invocation. The output is a trained policy that, at inference time, produces a final text answer (extracted from a \boxed{} marker) preceded by a multi-turn reasoning trace interleaved with tool invocations. Evaluation uses pass@1 with temperature 0.6 and top-p 0.95, with LLM-as-judge (Qwen2.5-72B) for accuracy on most benchmarks and F1 for knowledge-intensive QA.

## Method

ARPO introduces an entropy-based adaptive rollout mechanism that augments standard trajectory-level sampling with step-level branching: after each tool-call, the model monitors the normalized entropy change ΔH_t over the next k tokens, and if a computed branching probability P_t = α + β·ΔH_t exceeds a threshold τ, it spawns Z additional partial reasoning paths from that node, drawing from a reserved budget of M−N trajectories. This is paired with an Advantage Attribution Estimation that assigns shared (averaged) advantage values to tokens on the common prefix before branching and distinct advantage values to tokens on diverged branches; in practice, the paper adopts a soft variant where GRPO's importance-sampling ratio naturally aligns shared-token updates while differentiating branched-token updates. The theoretical grounding is a Generalized Policy Gradient (GPG) Theorem showing that for any differentiable Transformer policy, the gradient can be expressed over macro actions (partial-rollout segments) rather than individual tokens, making step-level branching a valid policy-gradient operation. Across 13 benchmarks, ARPO outperforms GRPO, DAPO, and REINFORCE++ while using only half the tool-call budget of trajectory-level baselines.

## Results

On 10 mathematical and knowledge-intensive reasoning benchmarks, ARPO achieves an average accuracy of 55.3% (Llama3.1-8B) and 58.3% (Qwen2.5-7B), outperforming the best trajectory-level RL baselines (GRPO: 51.1%/56.5%, DAPO: 50.4%/54.8%, REINFORCE++: 51.1%/54.9%) by 4.2–3.5 points. On deep search tasks, ARPO with Qwen3-14B reaches 43.7% on GAIA and 10.0% on HLE, surpassing GRPO (36.9% GAIA, 8.6% HLE) and even large closed-source models like GPT-4o (2.6% HLE) and DeepSeek-R1-671B (8.6% HLE). Notably, ARPO achieves these gains while using only half the tool-call budget of GRPO during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARPO consistently outperforms all three trajectory-level RL baselines (GRPO, DAPO, REINFORCE++) across all 10 reasoning benchmarks on both Llama3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B backbones, with average gains of 4.2 points over GRPO (55.3 vs 51.1 on Llama; 58.3 vs 56.5 on Qwen). On deep search, ARPO (Qwen3-14B) beats GRPO by 6.8 points on GAIA (43.7 vs 36.9) and 1.4 points on HLE (10.0 vs 8.6), while also surpassing workflow-driven agents like WebThinker (33.0 GAIA) and ReAct (32.0 GAIA). ARPO with only Qwen3-14B trained on 1K RL samples exceeds GPT-4o (2.6% HLE) and DeepSeek-R1-671B (8.6% HLE) on the HLE benchmark. Tool-integrated prompting (TIR) fails to improve over direct reasoning on Qwen2.5-7B (31.0 vs 32.0 avg), confirming that prompt engineering alone is insufficient. In terms of efficiency, ARPO achieves higher accuracy than GRPO while using only half the tool-call budget during training, and its rollout sampling produces 54 distinct clusters vs 48 for GRPO, indicating greater behavioral diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARPO extends the RLVR paradigm (GRPO, DAPO, REINFORCE++) from single-turn reasoning to multi-turn agentic tool-use settings, where prior work has predominantly relied on trajectory-level rollout sampling. It draws on entropy-based analyses of LLM reasoning (Wang et al. 2025b,c; Zheng et al. 2025b) but repurposes token entropy as a *branching criterion* at tool-call decision points rather than as a token-selection signal. The method also builds on agentic RL efforts like Tool-Star and RAGEN for reward design and tool environments, while departing from them by introducing step-level partial rollouts and a Generalized Policy Gradient (GPG) theorem that subsumes the standard policy gradient as a special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and actionable gap—trajectory-level RL underexplores high-entropy tool-call steps—and provides a practical mechanism (entropy-gated branching) that improves accuracy while halving tool-call budget, which is directly relevant to deployment costs in agentic systems. Open questions include whether the entropy signal generalizes to non-textual tool feedback (e.g., images, structured APIs), how the method scales to longer horizons with many tool types, and whether the GPG framework enables further algorithmic refinements beyond the current GRPO-based loss. The reliance on a single entropy threshold and the fixed partial-sampling budget also leave room for more adaptive or learned exploration policies.
