## Problem

Large reasoning models trained with RL post-training produce excessively long chains of thought, inflating inference cost and latency, and prior work has largely assumed an inherent tradeoff between response length and accuracy. This paper challenges that assumption by asking whether a principled, instance-aware mechanism exists that shortens reasoning up to a natural threshold without sacrificing correctness, and whether the stochastic shortest path structure of goal-reaching MDPs can be exploited directly through discounting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265046/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a math problem as a text prompt and emits a structured response partitioned into a reasoning span (delimited by explicit tags such as <reasoning>...</reasoning>) and a final answer segment. Only tokens within the reasoning span are subject to discounting; formatting and answer tokens carry zero discount. Evaluation reports Pass@1 (fraction of prompts where the first generated solution passes a binary verifier) and mean response length in tokens, with greedy decoding at inference.

## Method

The paper models verifier-based reasoning as a finite-horizon MDP with a deterministic binary terminal reward and proves, via Blackwell optimality, that within any restricted finite policy class the optimal policy for γ sufficiently close to 1 simultaneously maximizes success probability and minimizes expected trajectory length among success maximizers — a stochastic shortest path criterion with no accuracy-length tradeoff up to an instance-dependent threshold. The practical recipe applies GRPO with the environment correctness reward discounted by γ^K(τ) (where K(τ) counts reasoning tokens), leaves intrinsic formatting rewards undiscounted, and adds a KL penalty to a periodically refreshed reference policy to prevent collapse. The Blackwell discount factor γ_bw, shown to be strictly less than 1 for any finite policy class with an explicit upper bound for the greedy deployment class, characterizes how close to 1 the discount must be to preserve accuracy. Across GSM8K, MATH, AMC 2023, AIME 2025, MINERVA, and OLYMPIAD, discounted GRPO reduces mean response length by 13–37% while matching or exceeding undiscounted Pass@1.

## Results

On GSM8K, discounted GRPO reduces mean response length by 22% for Qwen2.5 7B-Instruct (217.60 → 170.08 tokens) and by 13% for Llama 3 8B-Instruct (125.43 → 108.67 tokens) while matching or slightly exceeding undiscounted Pass@1 (91.07 vs. 91.06; 81.07 vs. 80.87). On MATH, length reductions of ~22% are observed for both models with negligible accuracy change. On harder benchmarks (AMC 2023, AIME 2025, MINERVA, OLYMPIAD) with larger models (Phi-4, Qwen2.5 14B), discounted training achieves 30–40% shorter responses while matching or improving Pass@1, e.g., Phi-4 on AMC 2023 goes from 51.00 to 61.00 Pass@1 with length dropping from 1134 to 716 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between discounted GRPO (γ < 1) and standard undiscounted GRPO (γ = 1) as the baseline, with token budgets matched across methods. On GSM8K and MATH, discounted training matches or marginally exceeds undiscounted Pass@1 while reducing mean response length by 13–22%, confirming the theoretical prediction that Blackwell-optimal policies minimize length among accuracy maximizers. On the harder generalization benchmarks (AMC 2023, AIME 2025, MINERVA, OLYMPIAD), the pattern is even more pronounced: Phi-4 improves Pass@1 by 10 points on AMC 2023 (51→61) and 5.3 points on AIME 2025 (14→19.3) while cutting response length by 37–42%. The only cases where accuracy dips slightly are MATH for Qwen2.5 7B (64.80→64.55) and OLYMPIAD for both larger models (36.91→35.67; 35.13→34.76), suggesting the instance-dependent threshold predicted by the theory. The paper does not directly benchmark against other efficient-reasoning methods (length penalties, curated data, prompt control) in its main tables, positioning the comparison as a controlled ablation of the discounting mechanism itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of classical MDP theory (Blackwell optimality, stochastic shortest path) and modern LRM post-training via GRPO. It departs from the three dominant approaches to efficient reasoning—explicit length penalties, curated compressed traces, and prompt-based control—by proposing plain discounting of the environment reward as a principled alternative, grounded in the observation that near-γ=1 discounting in binary-reward MDPs implements the SSP criterion of maximizing success then minimizing path length. The theoretical machinery draws directly on Grand-Clément & Petrik's Blackwell discount factor framework and Peters et al.'s relative entropy policy search for the KL-to-moving-reference component, while the training algorithm is standard GRPO with a modified return.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central theoretical result—that within a restricted policy class, Blackwell optimal policies simultaneously maximize accuracy and minimize expected response length, with no fundamental tradeoff—challenges a widely held assumption in the LRM community and provides a clean justification for why discounting works. The practical recipe is notably simple (add a discount factor to the correctness reward, mask formatting tokens, match token budgets), making it easy to adopt. Open questions include whether shorter reasoning traces improve generalization (linking to compression-based generalization arguments), whether a two-stage pipeline (long traces for discovery, then compression) outperforms either alone, and whether the finite-policy-class theory extends cleanly to the infinite softmax training class in practice.
