## Problem

Existing benchmarks for evaluating long-term memory in LLMs suffer from three fundamental limitations: they extend conversation length by artificially concatenating short sessions from different users (producing incoherent dialogues with abrupt topic shifts), they cover narrow personal-life domains, and they test only simple recall rather than complex abilities like contradiction resolution, event ordering, and instruction following. Additionally, even LLMs with 1M-token context windows degrade substantially as dialogue length increases, and no prior method systematically addresses this degradation across extreme context lengths up to 10M tokens.

## Input / Output

The system takes a probing question (appended as the final turn) and a long user-assistant conversation ranging from 100K to 10M tokens across 19 domains as input, and produces a natural-language answer evaluated against human-validated rubric nuggets (atomic, self-contained criteria scored 0/0.5/1 by an LLM judge) for nine memory abilities, with event ordering scored via Kendall tau-b after LLM-based event alignment. The BEAM benchmark comprises 100 conversations and 2,000 validated questions, with two questions per ability per conversation. At 10M tokens, models that cannot natively support that length are evaluated on the largest recent dialogue segment fitting their window.

## Method

LIGHT is a cognitive-inspired inference framework combining three complementary memory systems without any model retraining. (1) Episodic memory: after each dialogue turn, Qwen2.5-32B-AWQ extracts key-value pairs and a summary, which are embedded via bge-small-en-v1.5 and stored in a FAISS vector database; at inference, the question is embedded and the top-k nearest dialogue segments are retrieved. (2) Working memory: the most recent z dialogue pairs are included verbatim. (3) Scratchpad: after each turn, Qwen2.5-32B-AWQ reasons over the current and preceding turns to record salient facts (semantic knowledge, autobiographical details, prospective memory, contextual metadata); when the scratchpad exceeds 30K tokens it is compressed to 15K by GPT-4.1-nano, and at inference it is filtered via semantic chunking plus binary relevance labeling by Qwen2.5-32B-AWQ. The backbone LLM then generates the answer conditioning jointly on the question, retrieved episodic segments, the working memory buffer, and the filtered scratchpad, achieving 3.5%–12.69% average improvement over the strongest baseline depending on the backbone model.

## Results

On the BEAM benchmark, LIGHT consistently outperforms both long-context (Vanilla) and RAG baselines across all four conversation lengths (100K–10M tokens) and all four backbone LLMs. At 100K tokens, LIGHT achieves average scores of 0.311 (Qwen2.5), 0.358 (Llama-4-Maverick), 0.294 (Gemini-2.0-flash), and 0.345 (GPT-4.1-nano), versus 0.240–0.280 for the best baseline. Gains grow with context length: at 10M tokens, LIGHT reaches 0.266 (Llama-4-Maverick) and 0.226 (GPT-4.1-nano) versus 0.104 and 0.109 for Vanilla, representing improvements of up to +155.7% over the long-context baseline. The largest relative gains appear in summarization (+160.6%), preference following (+76.5%), and multi-hop reasoning (+27.2%), while contradiction resolution remains the weakest ability across all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LIGHT against two baselines: (1) long-context LLMs (Vanilla), where the full conversation is fed into the model's context window, and (2) a RAG baseline that embeds each user-assistant turn pair and retrieves the top-5 most similar segments at inference. LIGHT wins against both baselines in nearly all model–length combinations. Against the Vanilla baseline, improvements range from +44.3% (GPT-4.1-nano at 100K) to +155.7% (Llama-4-Maverick at 10M), with the gap widening as context length increases. Against the RAG baseline, LIGHT still leads by 3.5%–12.69% on average, confirming that the episodic index + scratchpad + working memory architecture provides complementary information beyond simple dense retrieval. The sole exception is Gemini-2.0-flash at 10M tokens, where LIGHT (0.192) slightly trails RAG (0.216), likely due to model-specific retrieval behavior. The RAG baseline itself outperforms Vanilla at 500K and 1M tokens for most models, indicating that retrieval helps but is insufficient without the structured memory components that LIGHT adds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of long-context evaluation and cognitively-inspired memory architectures. It departs from existing long-term memory benchmarks (DialSim, LoCoMo, MemoryBank, LongMemEval, MemBench) that the authors argue rely on concatenated multi-user sessions with weak narrative coherence, narrow personal-life domains, and simple recall-oriented probes. The LIGHT method repurposes the RAG paradigm (Lewis et al., 2020) through a cognitive-science lens, combining episodic retrieval, a short-term working memory buffer, and an iteratively-built scratchpad—an architecture inspired by human episodic/working/semantic memory distinctions (Binder & Desai, 2011; Sridhar et al., 2023) rather than by any single prior LLM memory system. No direct methodological predecessor is identified; the contribution is a new benchmark plus a new multi-memory inference framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEAM is likely to become a standard reference for evaluating long-context conversational memory, given its scale (100 conversations up to 10M tokens, 2,000 validated questions, 10 abilities, 19 domains) and its human-validated nugget-based evaluation. The finding that contradiction resolution scores remain near zero (≤0.05) across all models and methods—including those with 1M-token windows—highlights a fundamental unsolved capability gap. Open questions include whether the scratchpad's iterative compression (30K→15K tokens) degrades fidelity at extreme lengths, whether the cognitive-science framing generalizes to non-English or multimodal settings, and whether the reliance on a separate LLM (Qwen2.5-32B) for indexing and scratchpad construction introduces a bottleneck that limits deployment practicality.
