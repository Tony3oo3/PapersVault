## Problem

While reinforcement learning has successfully extended chain-of-thought reasoning to vision-language models, this paper reveals a critical dual nature: longer reasoning improves logical inference but progressively degrades perceptual grounding, causing recognition failures on basic visual questions. The authors attribute this to 'visual forgetting,' where attention to visual tokens decays as the reasoning sequence grows, and show that over 50% of errors in existing reasoning VLMs are perception errors rather than logical ones, with a substantial fraction recoverable by simply stopping reasoning earlier.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text question as input and produces a free-form reasoning trajectory followed by a final answer. During training, GPT-5-generated visual claims (balanced sets of correct and incorrect factual statements about the image, independent of the question) are inserted at K random positions along the generated trajectory as anchors; at each anchor the model is constrained to output a single binary token (yes/no) judging the claim's validity, which is scored against the ground-truth label to produce the perception reward signal.

## Method

VAPO (Vision-Anchored Policy Optimization) extends GRPO by embedding a perception reward that explicitly incentivizes visually grounded reasoning. GPT-5 generates balanced, question-independent visual claims per training example; these claims are placed as anchors at K random positions along the model's reasoning trajectory, where the model is probed with a binary yes/no judgment on each claim. The per-anchor scores are aggregated into a perception reward using late-emphasis exponential weighting (β controls emphasis on later anchors, targeting the stage where perception degrades most). The final sequence reward combines accuracy, format, and a gated perception term (γ·1[acc=1]·R_perc) to prevent reward hacking via trivially short outputs. Training follows the standard GRPO objective with the modified reward, requiring only a single-token binary output per anchor, which adds minimal computational overhead (~30 min over vanilla GRPO).

## Results

VAPO-Thinker-7B achieves an average of 51.1% across six mathematical benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVista, MathVision, LogicVista, WeMath, Geometry3k), improving over the strongest open-source baseline Vision-R1-7B (49.1%) by 2.0%. On general-purpose benchmarks (MMMU, MMStar, HallusionBench, MMVet), VAPO-Thinker-7B reaches 63.1% average, surpassing the previous best (Vision-R1 at 59.9%) by 3.2%, establishing a new state of the art. The gains are most pronounced on vision-intensive tasks: MMStar improves from 61.4% to 63.0% and HallusionBench from 49.5% to 57.4% (+7.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-source 7B-scale reasoning models, VAPO-Thinker-7B (51.1% avg) outperforms Vision-R1-7B (49.1%) by 2.0%, VLAA-Thinker-7B (47.2%) by 3.9%, and R1-OneVision-7B (46.1%) by 5.0% on mathematical benchmarks. The advantage is substantially larger on general-purpose benchmarks, where VAPO (63.1%) surpasses Vision-R1 (59.9%) by 3.2%, with the biggest single-benchmark gain on HallusionBench (+7.9 points, 49.5%→57.4%), confirming that VAPO's perception reward is most effective on vision-intensive tasks. Against the base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B (40.1%), VAPO-Thinker-7B yields an 11-point improvement, demonstrating that the RL training with visual anchoring is the primary driver. On the 3B scale, VAPO-Thinker-3B (41.0%) remains competitive with 7B baselines like R1-OneVision-7B (46.1%) but trails the 7B VAPO model, indicating consistent scaling behavior. The paper also shows that even when baselines are augmented with inference-level remedies (visual replay or focus prompt), VAPO still leads by 1.5–2.1 points on average, confirming that training-time visual anchoring is more effective than test-time interventions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based reinforcement learning paradigm for training multimodal reasoning models (as used by R1-OneVision, VLAA-Thinker, and Vision-R1) by introducing a perception reward that explicitly penalizes visual forgetting during reasoning. It builds on the well-documented text bias in VLMs (Chen et al., 2024a; Fu et al., 2024) but reframes it as a dynamic, reasoning-length-dependent phenomenon rather than a static modality imbalance. The method departs from prior test-time remedies (contrastive decoding, attention reallocation) by addressing the root cause at training time through a policy gradient objective augmented with visual anchor scoring, and it challenges the test-time scaling assumption inherited from LLM reasoning research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of visual forgetting as the primary bottleneck in multimodal reasoning provides a concrete diagnostic for why longer reasoning chains degrade performance on perception-heavy tasks, and VAPO offers a low-overhead (~30 min extra training) fix that practitioners can adopt with minimal architectural changes. However, the method is currently limited to single-image settings, depends on an external LLM (GPT-5) for claim generation, and introduces several task-sensitive hyperparameters (K, β, γ) whose optimal values shift between logic-heavy and vision-intensive tasks. Open questions include whether visual forgetting generalizes to video/multi-image reasoning, whether self-supervised claim generation can fully replace the GPT dependency, and whether the phenomenon is architecture-specific or a fundamental property of autoregressive next-token prediction over long sequences.
