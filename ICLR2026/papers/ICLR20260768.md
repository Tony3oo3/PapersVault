## Problem

Existing multimodal misinformation detection benchmarks focus on surface-level cross-modal misalignment (out-of-context pairing, media manipulation) but fail to capture the deeper communicative strategy behind deceptive content. Prior intent-related work (EMU, MRF, NewsINT) is confined to unimodal settings and infers intent retrospectively from a reader's perspective, diverging from the creator's actual purpose. This paper asks whether misleading creator intent can be systematically modeled and detected in multimodal news, and whether current VLMs possess the reasoning capability to do so.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260768/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each instance is a triplet (I, T, A) consisting of a news image, an accompanying caption, and a trustworthy reference article drawn from the VisualNews corpus of verified reporting. The benchmark spans 12,000 instances across 10 news domains, with manipulations at two severity levels (subtle and significant) applied to either the text or the visual modality, and images synthesized via FLUX.1 [dev] or GPT-image-1. Outputs are structured as three complementary tasks: a binary misleading/non-misleading label, a three-way source attribution (image, text, or NA), and a multi-label creator desire inference selecting up to three areas from eight predefined societal influence categories.

## Method

The core contribution is an intent-guided multimodal news simulation framework that operationalizes creator intent along two dimensions grounded in strategic communication theory: desired influence (the societal sector targeted, selected from a predefined taxonomy of eight aspects) and execution plan (an open-ended description of how the creator aims to achieve the influence). GPT-4o simulates both malicious and trustworthy creators, generating intent-aligned captions for text modifications and textual descriptions that are rendered into images via FLUX.1 [dev] for visual modifications, producing both misleading and non-misleading variants for each of six intent categories anchored to verifiable news contexts. For evaluation, 14 VLMs are tested under two reasoning paradigms—implication-oriented (inferring downstream implications) and consistency-oriented (checking cross-modal and context alignment)—revealing that models rely on superficial cues like stylistic polish and image-text consistency rather than genuine intent reasoning. The authors further demonstrate that fine-tuning LLaVA-v1.6-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B on 6,000 DECEPTIONDECODED samples transfers substantial macro-F1 gains to three general MMD benchmarks (MMFakeBench, Fakeddit, FakeNewsNet), validating the framework as both a diagnostic benchmark and a data synthesis engine.

## Results

On DECEPTIONDECODED's misleading intent detection (Task 1), the best-performing VLMs under the consistency-oriented approach achieve 84–86% average accuracy on text-modified samples but only 64–69% on image-modified samples (e.g., Gemini-1.5-Pro: 84.4% text, 67.9% image; o4-mini: 85.0% text, 63.8% image). Smaller open-source models (LLaVA-v1.6-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) score at chance level (~33.3%), indicating a complete failure to detect misleading intent. Fine-tuning LLaVA-v1.6-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B on 6,000 DECEPTIONDECODED samples yields substantial transfer gains on general MMD benchmarks: +7.96 to +30.70 macro-F1 on MMFakeBench, +4.80 to +9.27 on Fakeddit, and +21.49 to +23.72 on FakeNewsNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 14 VLMs as baselines on DECEPTIONDECODED rather than proposing a new detection method. Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest image-modified accuracy (69.2% avg) while o4-mini leads on text-modified samples (85.0% avg); Gemini-1.5-Pro is the strongest on subtle text manipulation (80.5% M-Sub). All top-tier models show a consistent 15–20 point drop from text to image manipulation, revealing a shared blind spot for visually conveyed deception. Smaller open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LLaVA-v1.6-7B) perform at chance (33.3%), while mid-size models like Qwen2.5-VL-72B (75.5% text, 53.2% image) and GPT-4o-mini (53.5% text, 50.6% image) show partial but unreliable capability. The consistency-oriented prompting consistently outperforms the implication-oriented approach by 2–10 points across all models, suggesting that cross-modal inconsistency detection is a more tractable signal than inferring communicative goals. On the transfer evaluation, fine-tuning on DECEPTIONDECODED produces the largest gains on FakeNewsNet (+21.5 to +23.7 macro-F1), indicating that intent-focused training generalizes most strongly to real-world social media misinformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MMD paradigm of detecting cross-modal misalignment (out-of-context pairing, media manipulation) by reframing the problem around misleading creator intent, grounded in communication strategy theory (desired influence + execution plan). Prior intent-interpretation efforts (EMU, MRF, NewsINT) are limited to unimodal settings and define intent from a reader-perceived perspective, whereas DECEPTIONDECODED explicitly models creator-produced intent in a multimodal setting anchored to trustworthy news contexts from VisualNews. The intent-guided simulation framework repurposes GPT-4o for social simulation and FLUX.1 for image synthesis, but the core conceptual contribution is the shift from heuristic mismatch detection to implication-level intent reasoning with explicit ground-truth labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even frontier MLRMs (Claude-3.7-Sonnet, o4-mini) achieve only ~50–65% on subtle image-based manipulation detection and are systematically hijacked by spurious prompt-level authenticity cues, exposing a fundamental gap between surface-level consistency checking and genuine intent reasoning. The dual utility as both a diagnostic benchmark and a data synthesis engine (fine-tuning on 6k samples yields +8 to +31 macro-F1 on three real-world MMD benchmarks) makes it immediately actionable for building more robust detectors. Open questions include fully open-ended intent articulation, measuring downstream societal influence of misleading narratives, and the escalating risk as GPT-image-1 and Nano Banana produce near-perfect intent-aligned manipulations that bypass safety guardrails.
