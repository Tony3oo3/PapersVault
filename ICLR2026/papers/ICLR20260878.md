## Problem

Multimodal LLMs incur prohibitive inference costs because visual tokens dominate the input sequence, a problem that worsens sharply for high-resolution images and video. Existing token-reduction methods target only one pipeline stage (vision encoding or LLM decoding) and largely ignore textual alignment, causing loss of instruction-relevant visual details and performance degradation. No prior framework unifies compression across the full forward pass while explicitly preserving text-visual correspondence.

## Input / Output

VisionTrim consumes the standard MLLM input—an image (or 8 video frames) encoded by a CLIP-based ViT into N visual tokens (e.g., 576 for LLaVA-1.5, 2880 for LLaVA-NeXT) plus a text instruction—and outputs a compressed set of K+R visual tokens (e.g., 48+16=64) that replace the original sequence before or during LLM decoding. The modules are plug-and-play: they can be inserted between any two layers of the vision encoder or the LLM, and the K:R split (default 3:1) is configurable per model and resolution. At the LLM decoding stage, the [CLS] token is replaced by the first generated token's attention distribution as the global-importance signal.

## Method

VisionTrim comprises two complementary plug-and-play modules. The Dominant Vision Token Selection (DVTS) module scores each visual token by combining (a) global semantic importance from the [CLS] token's attention in the penultimate ViT layer and (b) local spatial continuity via a Local Token Affinity Measurement (LTAM) algorithm that uses a dual-kernel (feature-similarity + position-proximity) affinity over a 3×3 neighborhood; the two scores are fused with an adaptive variance-based weighting that automatically up-weights the more consistent signal. The Text-Guided Vision Complement (TGVC) module then takes the remaining (non-dominant) tokens, computes text-to-visual similarity to select R clustering centers, assigns all other tokens to their nearest center via a text-guided assignment score, and merges each cluster into a single complement token through weighted averaging. Both modules can be applied at the vision-encoding stage (pre-LLM) and again at the LLM decoding stage (between transformer layers), where the first generated token substitutes for [CLS] and cross-modal attention between visual and textual tokens replaces CLIP text features, enabling iterative refinement of the visual representation across the full forward pass.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B with an 88.9% visual token reduction (576→64 tokens), VisionTrim retains 98.8% of the original model's average performance across 10 benchmarks, substantially outperforming SparseVLM (86.7%), VisionZip (94.4%), PyramidDrop (90.8%), and VScan (96.8%). At a milder 77.8% reduction (128 tokens), VisionTrim retains 99.9% of original performance. On high-resolution LLaVA-NeXT-7B with 94.4% reduction (2880→160 tokens), VisionTrim achieves 94.0% average performance, surpassing VisionZip by 3.3%. On video (Video-LLaVA-7B, 93.4% reduction), VisionTrim retains 98.0% of original performance versus 86.5% for SparseVLM and 91.3% for VisionZip.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisionTrim consistently outperforms all five training-free baselines across every reduction ratio and model family tested. Against SparseVLM (ICML 2025), which prunes tokens solely during LLM decoding based on attention weights, VisionTrim achieves a 12.1-point average improvement at 88.9% reduction (98.8% vs. 86.7%) on LLaVA-1.5-7B, with the gap widening to 7.7 points on high-resolution LLaVA-NeXT (94.0% vs. 86.3% at 94.4% reduction). Against VisionZip (CVPR 2025), which performs global dominant token selection after vision encoding, VisionTrim leads by 4.4 points at 88.9% reduction and by 3.3 points at 94.4% reduction on LLaVA-NeXT. Against PyramidDrop (arXiv 2024), VisionTrim gains 8.0 points at 88.9% reduction on LLaVA-1.5. Against VScan (arXiv 2025), which uses a two-stage pruning approach, VisionTrim leads by 2.0 points at 88.9% reduction, with the advantage growing at higher compression ratios. Against FastV (ECCV 2024), tested on Qwen2-VL-7B, VisionTrim exceeds the vanilla model by 2.1 points on MMBench while FastV drops 2.9 points below vanilla. On video tasks (Video-LLaVA-7B), VisionTrim retains 98.0% of original performance at 93.4% token reduction, outperforming SparseVLM (86.5%) and VisionZip (91.3%) by 11.5 and 6.7 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisionTrim carves out a unified position in the visual token compression literature by operating across the entire MLLM pipeline rather than a single stage. Prior methods like VisionZip and FasterVLM prune tokens after vision encoding, while FastV and SparseVLM prune during LLM decoding; VScan attempts two-stage pruning but neglects the text query's role in the vision encoding stage. VisionTrim departs from these by introducing two complementary plug-and-play modules (DVTS for global-local visual importance, TGVC for text-guided token merging) that can be inserted at both stages, and by explicitly leveraging CLIP text-visual alignment to complement dominant tokens rather than relying solely on text-visual attention for selection (as in CrossGET or Turbo, which the authors argue causes hallucinations).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: at 88.9% token reduction, VisionTrim achieves 61.4% CUDA time reduction and 91.7% FLOPs reduction while retaining ~99% accuracy, making high-resolution and video MLLMs far more deployable in real-world settings. The training-free, plug-and-play design means it can be applied to any pre-trained MLLM without retraining, which is critical for the rapidly evolving MLLM ecosystem. Key limitations include the residual performance gap at extreme compression ratios (e.g., 82.8% at 1 token) and the acknowledged non-lossless nature of the approach. Open questions include whether truly lossless compression is achievable, how the method generalizes to future MLLM architectures with different tokenization schemes, and whether the text-guided complement can be made more robust to adversarial or misleading prompts.
