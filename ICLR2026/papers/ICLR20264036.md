## Problem

Existing video-to-4D generation methods either rely on fragile score distillation sampling, accumulate errors through multi-view generation and reconstruction, or produce non-mesh representations (Gaussian particles) with limited generalization. No prior work directly generates a feedforward sequence of 3D meshes from a monocular video while handling non-rigid motion, volume changes, and topological transitions without per-frame optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264036/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video as input and produces a sequence of 16 watertight 3D meshes (decoded from truncated SDFs via marching cubes), each represented by 1024 latent vectors. Generated geometry is in a canonical coordinate system, so a global pose registration step aligns the first frame to the input video and propagates the transform to all frames. A subsequent topology-consistent texturization pipeline (pairwise mesh registration + texture propagation from the first frame) yields a fully textured 4D mesh sequence.

## Method

ShapeGen4D extends a pretrained rectified-flow 3D shape diffusion transformer (Step1X-3D / Hunyuan3D) to the 4D setting through three key mechanisms. First, learnable spatiotemporal attention layers are inserted after each frozen base-model block, performing joint self-attention across shape and image hidden states from all frames with 1D RoPE frame-index embeddings. Second, the VAE encoder uses temporally-aligned query points: FPS is applied to the first frame's non-watertight mesh, and subsequent frames' query sets are obtained by warping through the known animation, ensuring each latent corresponds to the same physical surface point over time. Third, the same Gaussian noise is replicated across all frames during both training and inference, preventing the pose and scale flickering that arises because the base 3D model is viewpoint-agnostic and lacks explicit positional embeddings. Only the spatiotemporal layers are trained (zero-initialized output projections) on 14k animated Objaverse assets, while the base 3D model remains frozen to preserve its large-scale geometric priors.

## Results

On the held-out Objaverse test set, ShapeGen4D (Hunyuan3D-2.1 backbone) achieves the best geometric accuracy across all metrics: Chamfer distance 0.0827, IoU 0.4155, and F-Score 0.3971, substantially outperforming all baselines. The Step1X-3D variant also leads with Chamfer 0.1220, IoU 0.3276, and F-Score 0.2934 while running in just 3 minutes (vs. 30 min for V2M4 and 40 min for DreamMesh4D). On the Consistent4D rendering evaluation, ShapeGen4D outperforms Step1X-3D and GVFD on LPIPS, FVD, and DreamSim, though L4GM scores higher on rendering metrics due to its inherent alignment with input viewpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShapeGen4D outperforms all five baselines on geometric accuracy. Against GVFD, the improvement is dramatic: Chamfer drops from 0.3978 to 0.1220 (Step1X-3D) or 0.0827 (Hunyuan3D-2.1), and F-Score jumps from 0.0699 to 0.2934/0.3971, reflecting GVFD's poor generalization from limited 4D training data. Against V2M4, ShapeGen4D achieves slightly better Chamfer (0.1220 vs. 0.1233), IoU (0.3276 vs. 0.3023), and F-Score (0.2934 vs. 0.2814) while being 10× faster (3 min vs. 30 min) and fully feedforward. Against L4GM, ShapeGen4D wins on Chamfer (0.1220 vs. 0.1576) and F-Score (0.2934 vs. 0.1932), though L4GM retains an advantage on rendering metrics (LPIPS 0.0988 vs. 0.1359, FVD 302 vs. 796) because its predictions are inherently aligned with input viewpoints. Against DreamMesh4D, ShapeGen4D improves Chamfer from 0.2108 to 0.1220 and F-Score from 0.2422 to 0.2934. Against per-frame Step1X-3D, the spatiotemporal extensions yield consistent gains (Chamfer 0.1220 vs. 0.1356, IoU 0.3276 vs. 0.3033, F-Score 0.2934 vs. 0.2617) at the same 3-minute inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DShape2VecSet-based VAE and rectified-flow diffusion transformer architecture from Step1X-3D and Hunyuan3D to the 4D setting by inserting spatiotemporal attention layers, redesigning surface point sampling for temporal alignment, and sharing noise across frames. It departs from concurrent 4D generation approaches (L4GM, GVFD) that introduce new modalities such as Gaussian particle deformation fields, instead generating a sequence of 3D meshes—a representation already learned by the base 3D model—thereby inheriting its generalization from large-scale 3D datasets. Pose registration and texturization are borrowed from V2M4, and MultiDiffusion is used to extend generation beyond the 16-frame training horizon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully feedforward video-to-4D mesh generation framework that handles topology changes, volume variation, and non-rigid motion without per-frame optimization, offering a practical path to animatable 3D assets from monocular video. Key limitations remain: the model is viewpoint-agnostic (inheriting the base 3D model's pose ambiguity), requires post-hoc pose registration and texture propagation for fully usable assets, and local temporal jitter persists. Open questions include whether a spatiotemporal 3D VAE could further reduce flickering, and whether fine-tuning with viewpoint-aligned data could eliminate the need for separate pose registration.
