## Problem

Existing text-to-image (T2I) benchmarks for cultural evaluation focus on object-centric artifacts (landmarks, clothing, food) rather than social activities, where cultural meaning emerges from context, interactions, and spatial arrangements. Moreover, standard image-text alignment (ITA) metrics like CLIPScore correlate poorly with human judgments of cultural faithfulness and paradoxically reward hallucinated or exaggerated stereotypical elements, making them unreliable for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264622/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes T2I-generated images (photorealistic photos prompted as 'A photorealistic photo of {activity} in {country}'), LLM-generated reference descriptors organized across five cultural dimensions (background, attire, objects, interactions, spatial layout), and ~3,000 real reference images scraped from the web for exaggeration calibration. It produces per-dimension and aggregate scores (ALIGN, HAL, EXAG, FAITH), interpretable descriptor-level feedback identifying which specific cultural elements are aligned, missing, hallucinated, or exaggerated, and diversity metrics (DDIV, SDIV) measuring semantic variation across N generations per prompt.

## Method

AHEaD is a descriptor-based diagnostic framework that avoids using VLMs to directly score faithfulness (which inherits cultural biases) and instead decomposes evaluation into interpretable components. Reference descriptors are generated via a Proposer-Refiner pipeline where multiple LLMs (Gemini 2.5 Flash, GPT-4o) independently propose up to 10 mutually exclusive descriptors per dimension, and GPT-4o refines by removing duplicates and errors. Predicted descriptors are extracted from generated images using MLLMs (InternVL3-14B or Qwen2.5-VL-7B) for generic scene understanding only. A complete bipartite graph is constructed between predicted and reference descriptors with edges weighted by sentence-embedding similarity (all-MiniLM-L6-v2); ALIGN measures the fraction of reference descriptors whose best predicted match exceeds a calibrated threshold τ, HAL measures the fraction of predicted descriptors lacking any reference match, and EXAG quantifies over-representation of stereotypical elements by comparing ITA scores of generated images against a baseline computed from real images. The composite FAITH score (arithmetic mean of ALIGN, 1−HAL, 1−EXAG) achieves 27% higher Spearman correlation with human faithfulness judgments than MLLM-as-judge baselines, confirming that alignment alone is insufficient and that penalizing hallucination and exaggeration is necessary for accurate cultural evaluation.

## Results

FAITH (the composite of ALIGN, HAL, and EXAG) achieves Spearman correlation of 0.47 with human faithfulness judgments using InternVL3 backbone, and 0.42 using Qwen2.5-VL, substantially outperforming all ITA baselines (≤0.15) and MLLM-as-judge baselines (0.10–0.20) with the same backbones. This represents a 27% improvement over the best MLLM-as-judge baseline with the same backbone. Across all six T2I models evaluated, Global North countries consistently receive 4–8% higher ALIGN scores than Global South countries, with FAITH scores ranging from 0.52 (SD-3.5, GS) to 0.61 (GPT-Image-1/Gemini 2.5 Flash, GN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAITH decisively outperforms all five ITA baselines: CLIPScore (0.04), ImageReward (−0.08), PickScore (0.15), VQAScore (0.14), and CuRe (0.10) all achieve correlations below 0.15, while FAITH reaches 0.42–0.47. Against MLLM-as-judge baselines using the same backbone, FAITH improves correlation by +0.32 (Qwen2.5-VL: 0.10→0.42) and +0.27 (InternVL3: 0.20→0.47). The strongest baseline, GPT-4o-as-judge (0.48), is comparable to FAITH with InternVL3 (0.47), yet FAITH uses a substantially weaker and cheaper backbone. VIEScore with GPT-4o (0.35) also falls well short of FAITH. The paper further shows in Table 3 that ALIGN alone (0.39–0.41) is insufficient—adding HAL and EXAG is necessary to reach the full FAITH score, confirming that alignment without penalizing hallucination and exaggeration cannot capture cultural faithfulness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-generated descriptor paradigm from Menon & Vondrick (2023) — originally used for fine-grained object recognition — to the evaluation of cultural faithfulness in text-to-image generation. It departs from prior cultural T2I benchmarks (Kannen et al. 2024, Basu et al. 2023, Rege et al. 2025) that focus on object-centric artifacts (landmarks, clothing, food) by shifting to social activities, which are compositional and contextual. The Proposer-Refiner descriptor generation draws on self-consistency prompting (Wang et al. 2023), and the exaggeration metric adapts the open-set bias detection idea from D'Incà et al. (2024). Critically, the paper challenges the widespread use of VLM-based image-text alignment (CLIPScore, VQAScore) as a proxy for cultural faithfulness, showing these metrics actually reward stereotypical exaggeration rather than penalizing it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard ITA metrics correlate poorly (and sometimes positively with exaggeration) with human cultural judgments undermines a common evaluation shortcut in the T2I literature, suggesting that many prior cultural evaluation results may be unreliable. The CULTIVate benchmark and AHEaD framework provide the first scalable, interpretable, and automated pipeline for diagnosing cultural faithfulness in social activities, with descriptor-level feedback that enables targeted image editing. Limitations include reliance on LLM-generated reference descriptors (which may carry their own cultural biases), coverage of only 16 countries and 9 activity categories, and the fact that the GN/GS gap (4–8%) is reported but not addressed by any mitigation strategy. Open questions include whether the descriptor-based approach generalizes to more fine-grained cultural subgroups and whether the Proposer-Refiner can be made fully culture-specific without LLM bias.
