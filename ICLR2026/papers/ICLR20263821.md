## Problem

Existing RAG hallucination detectors either require large annotated datasets for fine-tuning, incur high inference costs by querying external LLM judges, or probe raw hidden states with limited accuracy due to polysemanticity. It remains unclear whether sparse autoencoder (SAE) features, which disentangle monosemantic concepts from LLM activations, can capture the specific dynamics of RAG hallucinations arising from the interplay between retrieved evidence and generated content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263821/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RAGLens consumes the hidden states of a frozen LLM at a chosen layer, computed by encoding the generated answer sequence conditioned on the user query and retrieved passages, so that SAE features implicitly capture interactions between output and context without explicitly encoding the passages. The SAE encoder maps each token's hidden state to a sparse K-dimensional vector, which is then max-pooled across tokens into a single instance-level representation. The detector outputs a binary hallucination label, per-feature contribution scores (via the GAM's additive shape functions), and token-level attributions that highlight specific spans likely to be ungrounded, enabling targeted post-hoc revision.

## Method

RAGLens extracts sparse features from a frozen LLM's mid-layer hidden states via a pretrained SAE encoder, applies channel-wise max pooling across generated tokens to form an instance representation, and selects the top K′ features by mutual information with the hallucination label. A Generalized Additive Model (implemented as an Explainable Boosting Machine) then maps the selected features to a hallucination prediction, with each feature contributing through a learned univariate shape function. The paper provides a theoretical justification for max pooling in the sparse-activation regime (showing mutual information scales linearly in sequence length and quadratically in the activation-rate gap between hallucinated and faithful tokens), and empirically demonstrates that mid-layer pre-activation SAE features with high MI, combined with GAM, outperform alternatives including MLP, XGBoost, logistic regression, and raw hidden-state baselines. The additive structure of the GAM yields transparent local and global explanations, and the identified features support causal interventions that steer the model toward faithful generation.

## Results

RAGLens achieves AUC scores above 0.84 across all four dataset-model combinations (RAGTruth and Dolly with Llama2-7B and Llama2-13B), with the highest being 0.8964 AUC and 0.8333 balanced accuracy on RAGTruth (Llama2-13B). On Dolly (Llama2-7B), RAGLens reaches 0.8764 AUC and 0.8070 macro F1, outperforming the strongest baseline ReDeEP (0.7949 AUC, 0.7833 F1) by over 8 points in AUC. The method also generalizes across domains: a detector trained on RAGTruth achieves 0.8019 AUC on AggreFact and 0.7637 AUC on TofuEval without retraining, far exceeding the CoT prompting baseline (~0.55–0.57 AUC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAGLens outperforms all 18 baselines across every dataset-model combination. The closest competitor is ReDeEP (Sun et al., 2025), another internal-representation method, which RAGLens surpasses by 9.5 AUC points on RAGTruth-7B (0.841 vs. 0.746) and 8.1 points on Dolly-7B (0.876 vs. 0.795). Among prompting-based detectors, RAGAS (0.729 AUC) and ChainPoll (0.674 AUC) on RAGTruth-7B are the strongest, yet both trail RAGLens by over 11 AUC points. Uncertainty-based methods (SelfCheckGPT, Perplexity, Energy, LN-Entropy) perform near or below chance on RAGTruth-7B (AUC 0.51–0.61), highlighting their inability to capture RAG-specific faithfulness signals. The fine-tuned Llama2-13B(LR) baseline, which requires task-specific training data, also underperforms RAGLens (e.g., 0.635 Acc vs. 0.758 Acc on RAGTruth-7B), confirming that SAE-based probing extracts more informative signals than supervised fine-tuning of the full model. On Llama2-13B, RAGLens extends its lead, reaching 0.896 AUC on RAGTruth versus 0.850 for the next-best method (ITI), demonstrating that larger models encode richer hallucination-related internal knowledge that SAEs can effectively disentangle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability (sparse autoencoders for disentangling LLM hidden states, following Huben et al., 2024) and RAG hallucination detection. Prior RAG hallucination detectors rely on prompt engineering, LLM-as-judge, fine-tuned classifiers, or raw hidden-state/attention signals, while earlier SAE-based hallucination work (Ferrando et al., 2025; Suresh et al., 2025) targets generic LLM hallucinations rather than the RAG-specific interplay between retrieved evidence and generated text. RAGLens departs from both lines by applying a systematic pipeline—max pooling, mutual-information-based feature selection, and a generalized additive model (via EBM, Nori et al., 2019)—to SAE features, and by providing the first theoretical justification (Theorem 1) for why max pooling is effective in the sparse-activation regime. The paper also contrasts SAE features against raw hidden states and Transcoder features, positioning SAEs as the preferred extractor for compact, interpretable hallucination signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAGLens offers a lightweight, interpretable alternative to costly LLM-as-judge pipelines, with the added benefit of token-level attributions that enable actionable post-hoc mitigation (reducing hallucination rates by 5–15 percentage points across judges). A key open limitation is that SAE features are not transferable across LLMs, requiring a per-model SAE, and causal intervention on SAE features is only practical when the feature fires before the hallucinated span. The finding that a simple additive model outperforms MLPs and XGBoost on SAE features suggests the hallucination signal is largely decomposable across features, but whether this holds as SAE architectures improve or as models scale remains to be tested. Broader implications include the potential to integrate SAE-based probes into production RAG systems as real-time faithfulness monitors without incurring additional LLM inference costs.
