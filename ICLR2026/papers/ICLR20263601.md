## Problem

Pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP face distributional shifts and novel tasks in real deployment, but existing continual learning methods are impractical for lifelong use: storage-based approaches (replay buffers, gradient memories) grow linearly with task count, and expansion-based methods (adapters, prompts) cause unbounded parameter growth. This paper asks whether a model with a fixed parameter budget can sequentially absorb new knowledge while preserving its zero-shot generalization, using only its intrinsic weight structure and no external storage.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone, applying adapters to all attention projection matrices (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o) in both the vision and text encoders. For each task in a sequence, it consumes the current model weights (no prior task data or gradients), learns a small intermediate matrix M (r×r, with r ≤ 128), and produces updated backbone weights via a single merge step. The model then accepts standard image inputs and text prompts (class name templates) to produce class labels, with the zero-shot transfer capability on unseen tasks preserved throughout the sequence.

## Method

NuSA-CL performs a three-stage cycle per task: (1) SVD of the current weight matrix W_{t-1} identifies an intrinsic null space by finding the smallest k such that the top-k singular values capture a ρ fraction (95–99%) of total spectral energy, leaving the remaining d−k low-energy directions as the null space; (2) a LoRA-like update ΔW = U_n M V_n^T is trained with U_n and V_n frozen from the SVD and only the small intermediate matrix M (initialized to zero) trainable, persistently constraining all updates to the null space throughout training; (3) the learned update is merged directly into the backbone (W_t ← W_{t-1} + ΔW_t), maintaining a fixed parameter budget. The key distinction from prior subspace methods (e.g., MiLoRA) is the persistent constraint—updates cannot deviate from the null space during training—and the data-agnostic derivation of the subspace from the weight spectrum alone, requiring no replay buffer, gradient memory, or auxiliary model. A theoretical interference bound (Lemma 1, Theorem 2) shows that the Frobenius inner product between existing weights and each update is bounded by σ_max^null · ‖M‖_F, providing a principled guarantee against catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the MTIL benchmark (11 sequential vision tasks), NuSA-CL achieves 68.6% Transfer, 75.1% Avg., and 82.8% Last accuracy using only 1.5M trainable parameters, zero additional storage, and 1.21 GPU-Hours. On the 50-step CIFAR-100 class-incremental benchmark, NuSA-CL reaches 71.85% Last accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline ZSCL (67.36%) by over 4.4 points. The method uses 40× fewer parameters than MoE-Adapters (1.5M vs. 59.8M), requires no replay buffer or gradient memory, and runs in under 2 GPU-Hours on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among storage-free PEFT methods, NuSA-CL decisively outperforms both LoRA (68.6 vs. 63.9 Transfer; 82.8 vs. 79.9 Last) and MiLoRA (68.6 vs. 62.8 Transfer; 82.8 vs. 77.4 Last) on all three MTIL metrics, confirming that persistent null-space constraints are more effective than unconstrained or minor-singular-value-initialized updates. Against the storage-based InfLoRA, NuSA-CL wins on Transfer (68.6 vs. 66.2) and Avg. (75.1 vs. 74.2) while trailing slightly on Last (82.8 vs. 83.6), but does so with zero external memory versus InfLoRA's 9 MB gradient projection store and 3.5× less training time (1.21 vs. 4.29 GPU-Hours). Compared to the storage-based SOTA methods (MoE-Adapters, DIKI, ZSCL), NuSA-CL sacrifices 1–2 points of Last accuracy but eliminates all additional storage, reduces parameter count by 40×, and cuts GPU-Hours by 3–39×. On the long-horizon 50-step CIFAR-100 CIL benchmark, NuSA-CL (71.85% Last) surpasses ZSCL (67.36%) by 4.5 points and iCaRL (59.07%) by 12.8 points, demonstrating that the advantage grows with sequence length. In the 5-shot MTIL stress test (Table 2), NuSA-CL leads all fixed-backbone PEFT methods on Transfer (68.1%), Avg. (70.3%), and Last (75.4%), outperforming InfLoRA by 1.3, 1.4, and 0.6 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

NuSA-CL builds directly on LoRA's low-rank factorization but departs from it in two critical ways: it derives the update subspace from the SVD of the current weights (rather than learning arbitrary projection matrices) and enforces a persistent constraint throughout training (rather than using the subspace only for initialization, as in MiLoRA or LoRA-Null). It also explicitly rejects the storage-based paradigm of methods like InfLoRA (gradient projection memory), MoE-Adapters (expanding router library), and ZSCL (knowledge distillation with reference data), instead deriving all constraints intrinsically from the model's own spectral structure. The dynamic per-task re-computation of the null space and the merge-into-backbone cycle distinguish it from both single-task SVD-guided fine-tuning and expansion-based PEFT approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a 1.5M-parameter, zero-storage method achieves performance within ~2% of storage-based SOTA while using 40× fewer parameters and no auxiliary memory, making it viable for on-device and resource-constrained continual deployment where parameter growth and replay buffers are infeasible. The spectral dynamics analysis (progressive effective-rank increase without saturation even after 50 tasks) provides mechanistic confidence in long-horizon viability. Open questions remain around null-space exhaustion under extreme task counts, SVD overhead for substantially larger backbones (beyond ViT-B/16), and sensitivity to highly correlated or adversarially ordered task sequences.
