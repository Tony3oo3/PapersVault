## Problem

Existing Locate-and-Edit (L&E) knowledge editing methods (MEMIT, AlphaEdit, etc.) are limited to editing only hundreds of facts before the model's general capabilities degrade and previously edited facts are forgotten. The paper identifies two root causes: the suboptimal capacity of the linear systems used for parameter updates, and the inadequacy of Wikipedia-sampled subsets in representing the full breadth of general knowledge. This paper asks whether a fundamentally different retrieval mechanism can scale editing to 100,000+ facts without any loss in general ability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of factual triples (s, r, o → ô) and a target LLM, then computes a key matrix K1 ∈ R^{d1×m} and residual matrix R1 ∈ R^{d2×m} (where m is the number of edited facts) to form a neural KV database. At inference, the model receives a standard text prompt; the hidden state at a target FFN layer serves as a query into the KV database, and the output is either the original model response (for general knowledge) or a response incorporating the retrieved residual (for edited facts). The additional parameter overhead is O((d1+d2)×m), amounting to ~150M parameters (2.2% of Llama-3-8B) for 10,000 facts, with only ~1.5% additional inference time.

## Method

The paper first provides a theoretical and empirical analysis showing that MEMIT and AlphaEdit can be reformulated as querying a linear KV database, where the updated parameter Δ acts as a weighted average over residual vectors R1 with weights ω = K1^T S k. Empirically, these weights are extremely sparse: one-hot for edited facts and zero for unrelated content. Building on this insight, NeuralDB replaces the linear perturbation Δ with a non-linear gated retrieval function g(k; K1, R1) = r_j · 1[cos(k, k_j) > γ], where j = argmax_i cos(k, k_i). This function returns the single most similar residual only when cosine similarity exceeds a threshold γ (typically 0.65), and returns zero otherwise, thereby preserving general knowledge without requiring Wikipedia sampling or null-space projection. The module is a plug-and-play addition to a single FFN layer, supports efficient add/delete/modify operations on the KV database, and scales to 100,000 facts with only marginal degradation in editing accuracy and no measurable loss in general capabilities across six benchmark tasks.

## Results

On Llama-3 8B editing 10,000 facts, NeuralDB achieves 99.2% efficacy and 85.9% generalization on CounterFact, and 95.9% efficacy and 91.0% generalization on ZsRE, while preserving fluency (631.0 vs. 635.2 pre-edited) and consistency (32.6 vs. 24.1 pre-edited). At 100,000 edits, efficacy remains at 95.5% and generalization at 90.2%, with MMLU actually improving from 56.2% to 56.9%. Across six general-ability benchmarks (MMLU, SciQ, CommonsenseQA, ARC Challenge, Lambada, WSC273), NeuralDB maintains near-identical scores to the pre-edited model at 10k edits, whereas MEMIT, RECT, and AlphaEdit all degrade sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuralDB decisively outperforms all L&E baselines at 10k edits. On Llama-3 8B CounterFact, NeuralDB retains 99.2% efficacy versus AlphaEdit's 75.8%, RECT's 60.0%, and MEMIT's 63.4%; on ZsRE the gap is even starker (95.9% vs. 90.5% AlphaEdit, 0.0% RECT, 0.1% MEMIT). At 2k edits, NeuralDB already leads on specificity (88.2% vs. 68.6% AlphaEdit, 52.0% MEMIT) and consistency (32.9 vs. 32.8 AlphaEdit, 6.5 MEMIT) on CounterFact. Against memory-based methods evaluated only at 2k (SERAC, GRACE, ROME, MEND), NeuralDB achieves substantially higher generalization (86.6% vs. 50–61%) and specificity (88.2% vs. 45–74%). In the 100k scaling experiment, NeuralDB degrades only marginally (efficacy 96.9→95.5%) while AlphaEdit's 10k results already show a 23-point drop from its 2k score. On MQuAKE multi-hop QA, NeuralDB achieves 12.40% accuracy versus AlphaEdit's 9.14% and MEMIT's 6.13%, and on KnowEdit Wiki Counterfact it reaches 96.58% rewrite accuracy and 73.15% paraphrase accuracy, surpassing AlphaEdit (73.05% / 50.81%) and MEMIT (69.22% / 48.67%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the Locate-and-Edit (L&E) paradigm—specifically MEMIT and AlphaEdit—as querying a linear Key-Value database, showing that their closed-form solutions reduce to weighted averages over a residual matrix with near one-hot weights at inference. Building on this theoretical unification, NeuralDB replaces the linear perturbation Δ with a non-linear gated retrieval module (cosine similarity + threshold) that directly retrieves the most relevant residual, eliminating the need for expensive Wikipedia sampling (K₀) and null-space projection. The approach departs from memory-based methods like GRACE and SERAC by anchoring keys to FFN-layer activations of the subject's last token rather than full hidden states, yielding substantially better key discrimination for rephrased queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuralDB scales knowledge editing to 100,000 facts (50× prior work) with only marginal degradation in editing accuracy and no measurable loss in general abilities across six benchmarks, addressing the core bottleneck that has limited L&E methods to hundreds of edits. The plug-and-play, single-layer design with linear memory growth and support for add/modify/delete operations makes it practically deployable for maintaining up-to-date LLMs without retraining. Open questions include whether the single-layer assumption holds for more complex (multi-hop, relational) edits, how the method interacts with sequential or conflicting edits over very long horizons, and whether the gating threshold γ can be made adaptive rather than fixed per model.
