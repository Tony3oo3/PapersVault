## Problem

Deep speech denoising (DNS) models are deployed in safety-critical applications (hearing aids, emergency communications, air-traffic-control radio), yet their robustness to imperceptible adversarial perturbations was largely unexplored. Prior work on attacking speech-enhancement networks used audible, norm-bounded perturbations in limited settings (two models, no reverb, narrow SNR range), leaving open whether psychoacoustically hidden noise could reliably destroy intelligibility across diverse, realistic conditions.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a clean speech waveform (16 kHz, 5–10 s) mixed with background noise at a chosen SNR and optionally convolved with a room impulse response (RIR), plus white-box gradient access to the target DNS model. It produces a time-domain perturbation δ whose STFT magnitude is clipped to stay below psychoacoustic masking thresholds (with a 12 dB safety offset), ensuring the perturbation is inaudible when superimposed on the input. For over-the-air attacks, the perturbation is additionally constrained so that its RIR-convolved version remains masked, requiring a non-trivial projection step.

## Method

The core method is a masking-aware projected gradient descent (PGD) loop that maximizes negative STOI (a differentiable intelligibility metric) over the perturbation, with a custom projection operator that clips STFT magnitudes bin-by-bin to the computed auditory masking threshold while preserving phase. The psychoacoustic model is enhanced beyond prior work by adding temporal pre-masking (20 ms window, 0.16 ms⁻¹ decay) and post-masking (100 ms window, 0.02 ms⁻¹ decay) combined via element-wise maximum, plus a uniform 12 dB threshold reduction to guarantee imperceptibility. For simulated over-the-air attacks where the RIR convolution makes the projection non-closed-form, the paper combines one step of Wiener deconvolution with iterative gradient descent on a constraint-violation function to find a feasible δ. The framework is applied to four open-source DNS models (Demucs, Full-SubNet+, FRCRN, MP-SENet) across SNR levels from 70 dB to 0 dB, with and without reverb, and with simulated or real recorded RIRs.

## Results

All four tested DNS models (Demucs, Full-SubNet+, FRCRN, MP-SENet) can be driven to output unintelligible audio by psychoacoustically hidden perturbations, with mean ΔSTOI (STOI improvement) flipping from positive to strongly negative: Demucs −1.08, FRCRN −0.99, FSN+ −0.49, MP-SENet −1.25. Attacks succeed across all tested settings, including near-clean 70 dB SNR with no reverb, and simulated over-the-air conditions (all models except FSN+). A human study with 15 audio experts confirmed near-zero word accuracy on attacked outputs while ABX discrimination accuracy (59%) was not significantly above the 50% chance level, supporting subjective imperceptibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new defense or attack method to benchmark against external baselines; instead it compares the adversarial robustness of four open-source DNS models under an identical attack pipeline. Full-SubNet+ (FSN+) is the most resilient (ΔSTOI −0.49 vs. −0.99 to −1.25 for the others), but this protection is attributed to exploding gradients (norms reaching 10³⁰) rather than architecture or parameter count, and is known to be circumventable. Demucs (33.5M params, time-domain) and FRCRN (10.3M params, TF-domain) show comparable susceptibility despite very different architectures, while the smallest model MP-SENet (2.3M params) is actually the most vulnerable. Naive cross-architecture transfer of imperceptible perturbations fails entirely (off-diagonal ΔSTOI ≈ 0 in the transfer matrix), and leave-one-out transfer across Demucs checkpoints produces only minor degradation, confirming that white-box gradient access is currently required for effective attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the psychoacoustic masking framework of Schönherr et al. (2018) and Qin et al. (2019) from ASR attacks to speech denoising models, adding temporal pre/post-masking and a 12 dB threshold reduction to ensure imperceptibility. It builds on the PGD optimization framework of Madry et al. (2018) by introducing a masking-threshold projection operator in STFT space, and on the MP3 psychoacoustic model of Lin & Abdulla (2015) for threshold computation. The closest prior work, Dong et al. (2023), first attacked speech-enhancement networks but used audible ∞-norm-bounded perturbations in limited settings; this paper departs by enforcing strict psychoacoustic hiding, covering near-clean to noisy reverberant conditions, and demonstrating simulated over-the-air attacks with RIR-aware projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that four open-source DNS models can be driven to output unintelligible gibberish by imperceptible noise—even at 70 dB SNR with no reverb and in simulated over-the-air settings—raises urgent safety concerns for deployment in hearing aids, emergency-responder communications, and air-traffic-control radio. The discovery that FSN+'s partial robustness stems from exploding gradients (a known brittle pseudo-defense) rather than architecture or model size challenges common assumptions about what confers adversarial robustness. Key open questions include whether token-based DNS models (e.g., SELM) are similarly vulnerable, whether streaming or universal perturbations can be made effective, and whether more sophisticated defenses beyond Gaussian noise (adversarial training, inference-time ensembling) can provide practical protection before these models are deployed in safety-critical contexts.
