## Problem

LLMs frequently produce confident but incorrect answers rather than abstaining, a weakness that is especially acute in temporal QA where facts evolve over time and questions may become unanswerable due to contradictory or outdated temporal evidence. Prior abstention methods (calibration, prompting, consistency-based reasoning) have not been systematically studied in the temporally grounded setting, leaving a gap in understanding how to jointly optimize temporal reasoning and reliable abstention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262483/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a time-sensitive question (e.g., 'Who was the spouse of Anna Karina from 1966 to 1967?') concatenated with one of several auxiliary evidence types: the full original context, a time-filtered sub-context extracted via GPT-4o-mini, or top-k temporal KG quadruples retrieved via Faiss or KeyBERT. The output is structured as  followed by <answer></answer>, where the answer is either a factual response or the literal string 'No Answer' to signal abstention. The CoT-SFT phase additionally trains the model to emit intermediate reasoning steps before the final answer.

## Method

The pipeline couples a CoT-SFT cold start with GRPO-based reinforcement learning. CoT supervision data (~2,119 Easy / ~1,045 Hard samples) is generated by GPT-o1 and filtered to retain only samples whose generated answer matches ground truth, forming a trusted reasoning set for one epoch of full-parameter SFT. RL then optimizes the SFT model via GRPO (group size 4, KL coefficient β=0.01) using a rule-based reward: a format reward (0.5) for correct /<answer> structure plus an answer reward that assigns 1.0 for correct abstention, ROUGE-L + Exact-Match for correct factual answers, and 0 for all other cases (hallucination or over-abstention). A key finding is that RL fails entirely without the CoT-SFT cold start, and that implicit information (original contexts, time sub-contexts, KGs) provides limited benefit for reasoning-with-abstention compared to explicit CoT supervision, with the 1.5B RL model surpassing GPT-4o by 3.46% and 5.80% EM on TimeQA-Easy and -Hard respectively.

## Results

The headline result is that Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, trained with a CoT-SFT cold start followed by GRPO reinforcement learning, achieves 43.41% Exact Match on TimeQA-Easy and 35.75% EM on TimeQA-Hard, surpassing GPT-4o (39.95% and 29.95% EM) by 3.46 and 5.80 percentage points respectively. The RL model also improves the True Positive rate on unanswerable questions by 20% over the pure SFT variant. In contrast, SFT alone yields only 1.81% EM (Easy) and 3.95% EM (Hard), demonstrating that supervised fine-tuning with implicit context signals is far less effective than RL with explicit CoT supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o (direct inference with original context), the 1.5B RL model wins on both splits: +3.46% EM on TimeQA-Easy (43.41 vs. 39.95) and +5.80% EM on TimeQA-Hard (35.75 vs. 29.95). Against the stronger reasoning model o4-mini, the RL 1.5B model is slightly behind (43.41 vs. 44.14% Easy EM; 35.75 vs. 37.12% Hard EM), indicating that the gap to the best closed-source reasoning model is narrow. Compared to SFT baselines, the RL approach is dramatically superior: the 1.5B RL model achieves 43.41% EM on Easy versus only 1.81% for 1.5B SFT and 3.70% for 7B SFT, showing that model scale under SFT cannot compensate for the lack of RL. The 0.5B RL model (32.48% Easy EM) also surpasses GPT-3.5 (30.18%), confirming that even very small models benefit substantially from the CoT-SFT + RL pipeline. On abstention specifically, the RL model improves True Positive rate by 20% over SFT on unanswerable questions, though it still exhibits overconfidence on TimeQA-Hard where false negatives increase sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm (GRPO from DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1) from math and coding to temporal question answering with abstention, a setting where prior abstention methods (calibration, prompting, consistency-based reasoning) have been largely unexplored. It repurposes Chain-of-Thought supervision (Wei et al., 2022) not merely as a prompting technique but as a necessary cold-start for RL, drawing on the DeepSeek-R1 training template and rule-based reward design. The key departure from prior abstention work is framing abstention as a teachable skill optimizable via RL rather than a property to be elicited through calibration or multi-model aggregation, and systematically comparing implicit reasoning cues (original context, temporal sub-contexts, knowledge graphs) against explicit CoT supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 1.5B model trained with CoT-SFT + RL surpasses GPT-4o on temporal QA with abstention suggests that targeted post-training can unlock capabilities in small models that scale alone does not provide, with practical implications for deploying reliable abstention in resource-constrained settings. However, the paper reveals that abstention ability fails to generalize to OOD domains (MMLU, HellaSwag, SQuAD-v2), and that SFT paradoxically induces overconfidence while balanced data distributions cause collapse into 'always abstain'—highlighting that robust, generalizable abstention remains an open problem. Key open questions include how to design rewards and data distributions that yield calibrated abstention without sacrificing answer accuracy, and whether the CoT-SFT + RL recipe transfers to other reasoning domains beyond temporal QA.
