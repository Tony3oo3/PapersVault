## Problem

Test-time alignment for LLMs faces a fundamental trade-off: guided decoding methods (ARGS, GenARM) require external 7B reward models that double inference cost, while representation engineering methods (CAA, BiPO, RE-Control) manipulate entangled hidden states where feature superposition causes unpredictable side effects on general capabilities. No prior work has explored the naturally disentangled logit/vocabulary space as an intervention point for preference alignment, leaving a gap between computational efficiency and context-dependent adaptive control.

## Input / Output

At each decoding step, PALC consumes the frozen base model's final-layer hidden state (h_t ∈ R^4096) and original logits (l_t ∈ R^32000) strictly as read-only context, producing a single calibration vector m_t ∈ R^32000 that is added to the logits scaled by a user-adjustable factor γ. The calibrated logits l'_t = l_t + γ·m_t feed into the standard softmax for token sampling, yielding an aligned text response. The only trainable component is a 9.2M-parameter bottleneck MLP (0.13% of the 7B base model), and γ can be modified at inference time without any retraining or model changes.

## Method

PALC introduces vocabulary-space intervention as a novel alignment paradigm: rather than modifying entangled hidden representations, it learns position-dependent calibration vectors directly in logit space where each dimension corresponds to a unique token, sidestepping the superposition problem inherent in hidden-state manipulation. The Calibration Module is a two-layer bottleneck MLP (W_down: R^4096→R^256 with ReLU, W_up: R^256→R^32000) that compresses hidden states through a low-dimensional subspace, forcing extraction of only essential preference signals. Training uses a simplified preference loss that directly optimizes the log-probability gap between preferred and rejected responses without a reference model or KL divergence term, cutting memory requirements by ~50% versus DPO. The bottleneck constraint (B=256) induces a power-law singular value decay (α≈1.02), empirically confirming that human preferences concentrate on a surprisingly low-dimensional manifold, while a single fixed scaling factor γ provides runtime control over alignment strength with optimal performance at γ=1.0.

## Results

On HH-RLHF, PALC achieves a 58.17% Win+½Tie rate against the base LLaMA-7B-SFT model, outperforming CAA (77.17%), RE-Control (61.67%), and ARGS (55.50%) while adding only 8% inference latency (1.08× vs. base). On MT-Bench, PALC reaches a Length-Controlled Win+Tie rate of 61.9% against the base model, surpassing the computation-heavy GenARM (58.7%) at a fraction of the cost. PALC trains only 9.2M parameters (0.13% of the 7B base model) with a single forward pass through a bottleneck MLP, compared to 7B-parameter reward models required by ARGS and GenARM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PALC decisively outperforms CAA (77.17% Win+½Tie) and RE-Control (61.67%), the two representation-engineering baselines, confirming that dynamic logit-space calibration captures context-dependent preferences better than static steering vectors or test-time gradient optimization. Against ARGS (55.50%), PALC achieves a modest win while eliminating the need for an external 7B reward model that causes 4.40× latency. PALC is roughly on par with BiPO (49.00% Win+½Tie), suggesting logit-space and activation-space interventions capture similar preference signals. PALC trails DPO (41.17%) and GenARM (44.33%), but these methods require full training infrastructure or dual 7B-model deployment with 3.17× latency, respectively. On MT-Bench, PALC's 61.9% LC Win+Tie surpasses GenARM's 58.7% while maintaining 1.08× latency versus GenARM's 3.17×, highlighting a strong performance-efficiency trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

PALC carves new ground by identifying the logit (vocabulary) space as a previously unexplored intervention point for test-time preference alignment, departing from both representation engineering methods (CAA, BiPO, RE-Control) that manipulate entangled hidden states and guided decoding methods (ARGS, GenARM) that rely on external reward models. Conceptually, it repurposes the standard bottleneck MLP architecture as a read-only context-to-calibration bridge, treating hidden states purely as input features rather than as the locus of intervention. The training objective is a simplified DPO loss that drops the reference-model KL term, justified by the frozen backbone's inherent constraint on distribution shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PALC's 0.13% parameter overhead and 8% latency cost make test-time preference alignment practical for resource-constrained deployments where dual-model architectures (ARGS, GenARM) or full fine-tuning (DPO) are infeasible. The ablation finding that preferences concentrate on a surprisingly low-dimensional manifold (B=256 optimal, catastrophic failure at B=4096) suggests a structural regularity in human preference geometry that could inform future alignment architectures. However, evaluation is limited to a single 7B model and the HH-RLHF dataset, and PALC underperforms DPO and GenARM on raw alignment quality, leaving open whether the efficiency-performance trade-off holds at larger scales or across diverse preference domains.
