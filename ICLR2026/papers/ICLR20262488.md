## Problem

Existing incomplete multi-view clustering methods that impute missing views in the feature space overlook the inter-view distribution bias introduced by missing data. This distribution shift between representations fused from complete views and those from incomplete views causes semantic misunderstanding and cross-view misalignment noise, ultimately degrading clustering accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262488/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N samples each described by V heterogeneous feature views (e.g., pixel, Fourier, GIST, PHOG, LBP, HSB, RGB, Colorsim, Haralick descriptors) where some views may be absent, along with a binary indicator matrix M ∈ {0,1}^{N×V} marking available views. It outputs K cluster assignments obtained by applying k-means to the final shift-corrected consensus representation S′ ∈ R^{N×d}. The method assumes no sample is missing in all views simultaneously.

## Method

DDR-IMVC first extracts view-specific embeddings via independent autoencoders, then fuses them into a common representation using an adaptive weight matrix computed from per-view cluster separability (variance). The common representation is split into unbiased (complete-view) and biased (missing-view) subsets; a multi-head attention mechanism computes affinity weights between biased and unbiased samples, and the unbiased representations are used to correct the distribution shift in the biased ones, yielding shift-corrected consensus representations. Missing-view embeddings are then imputed from these consensus representations. Training is governed by three losses: reconstruction, maximum mutual information between consensus and view-specific representations, and a dual contrastive loss combining inter-view InfoNCE with a robust truncated-series contrastive loss (balancing MAE and InfoNCE) between consensus and view representations to prevent cluster collapse from imputation noise.

## Results

On Scene-15 (missing rate 0.3), DDR-IMVC achieves 45.53% ACC, 45.99% NMI, and 28.05% ARI, outperforming the second-best method APADC by 3.73%, 2.89%, and 3.75% respectively. On ALOI-100 (missing rate 0.3), DDR-IMVC reaches 73.18% ACC and 85.82% NMI, while the strongest competitor GHICMC fails entirely due to out-of-memory errors. On HandWritten (missing rate 0.3), DDR-IMVC attains 96.15% ACC and 91.49% NMI, marginally surpassing GHICMC (96.11% ACC, 91.32% NMI). On LandUse-21 (missing rate 0.3), DDR-IMVC scores 28.02% ACC and 33.49% NMI, edging out ICMVC (25.77% ACC, 29.39% NMI).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDR-IMVC achieves the best or near-best results across all four datasets at missing rate 0.3. On Scene-15, it outperforms the strongest baseline APADC by 3.73% ACC (45.53 vs 41.80) and 3.75% ARI (28.05 vs 24.30), with a clear margin over all other methods. On ALOI-100, DDR-IMVC leads by a wide margin (73.18% ACC vs 68.52% for DIMVC, the next best), while GHICMC fails entirely due to out-of-memory errors at this scale. On HandWritten, DDR-IMVC narrowly beats GHICMC at low missing rates (96.15 vs 96.11 ACC at 0.3) but falls slightly behind at higher rates (94.34 vs 94.88 at 0.5; 90.86 vs 92.73 at 0.7), which the authors attribute to the dataset's simple inter-class structure favoring cascade-graph recovery. On LandUse-21, DDR-IMVC surpasses ICMVC (the prior best) by 2.25% ACC and 4.10% NMI. Overall, DDR-IMVC wins on 3 of 4 datasets unconditionally and shows the most stable degradation as missing rate increases from 0.1 to 0.7.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDR-IMVC sits at the intersection of two established IMVC paradigms: data-space imputation (GAN-based, prototype-matching) and feature-space consensus representation imputation. It departs from the latter line (Completer, DCP, ICMVC, GHICMC) by explicitly modeling the distribution shift between complete-view and incomplete-view samples in the shared embedding space, correcting it via multi-head attention that pulls biased representations toward unbiased ones. The robust contrastive loss draws on the power-series decomposition of InfoNCE (inspired by Yuan et al., 2024) but repurposes the truncated series as a tunable interpolation between MAE (noise-robust) and InfoNCE (discriminative), applied here to prevent cluster collapse during consensus-based imputation rather than prototype alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distribution-shift correction addresses a gap that prior consensus-imputation methods implicitly ignore, and the truncated-InfoNCE loss offers a principled, tunable alternative to standard contrastive objectives in noisy settings. However, the evaluation is limited to four relatively small datasets (≤10,800 samples), and the method still relies on k-means for final clustering, capping its scalability. Open questions include whether the attention-based correction generalizes to extreme missingness (e.g., >80%) or to settings where no complete samples exist, and whether the variance-based adaptive weighting remains reliable when view quality varies non-uniformly across the dataset.
