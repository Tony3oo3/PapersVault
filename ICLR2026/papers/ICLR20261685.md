## Problem

Current VLMs process visual features independently in a vision encoder and defer all cross-modal interaction to LLM decoding via a single MLP projector, resulting in weak and unidirectional vision-language fusion. This design fails to capture the bidirectional, query-aware integration observed in human perception, where linguistic context actively shapes visual attention at every processing stage. FLARE addresses this by embedding text guidance into vision encoding, introducing bidirectional feature exchange during decoding, and enforcing modality-space consistency through a dedicated loss.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed at fixed 384×384 resolution for FLARE-L yielding ~630 vision tokens, or dynamic multi-scale resolution for FLARE-X yielding ~1400 tokens) and a free-form text query. The text query is not merely concatenated with image tokens in the LLM; it is first projected into the vision encoder's feature space to guide visual encoding, and context-aware latent tokens (randomly sampled from {4, 16, 36, 64, 144} per batch) are prepended to the query to mediate cross-modal attention during decoding. Output is a standard text answer generated autoregressively by the LLM backbone (Phi-3.5-mini for 3B, LLaMA3.1-8B for 8B).

## Method

FLARE introduces four coupled mechanisms for full-pipeline vision-language integration. (1) Text-Guided Vision Encoding projects the query's LLM embeddings into the vision encoder's space via MLP_t2v and jointly processes them with image patches through all encoder layers (with visual-to-textual attention masked in the first half to preserve low-level visual features), then concatenates shallow and deep-layer features before projecting back to LLM space via MLP_v2t. (2) Context-Aware Alignment Decoding prepends spatially-organized latent tokens to each query and inserts semantic exchange layers every three decoding layers, where windowed attention lets latent tokens aggregate the most relevant visual features conditioned on the current textual context, enabling bidirectional interaction that the causal mask alone cannot provide. (3) Dual-Semantic Mapping Loss enforces bidirectional reconstruction: text tokens processed through the vision encoder are mapped back to text space and compared via cosine similarity to the original LLM embeddings, and vice versa for image tokens, with a weighting λ=0.1. (4) Text-Driven VQA Synthesis inverts the conventional data pipeline by first generating diverse QA pairs from high-quality captions using LLaMA3.1-70B, then producing matching images via FLUX-dev, yielding ~3.4M synthesized samples across 12 task categories. Training proceeds in three stages (foundational alignment → contextual fusion → instruction tuning) with all parameters unfrozen at each stage.

## Results

FLARE-X 8B (1400 vision tokens) achieves 83.6 on MMBench-EN, 89.1 on POPE, 62.8 on MM-Vet, and 91.2 on ScienceQA, matching or exceeding Qwen2.5VL 7B on roughly half of 18 benchmarks while using ~1/1000 of its training data. FLARE-L 3B (only 630 vision tokens) surpasses both Cambrian-1 8B and Florence-VL 8B across most benchmarks, e.g., 79.6 vs. 75.9/76.2 on MMBench-EN and 76.3 vs. 51.3/73.3 on MMVP. FLARE-L 8B (630 tokens) reaches performance on par with LLaVA-OneVision 7B (2400 tokens) on MMBench-EN (81.8 vs. 81.7) and MM-Vet (59.6 vs. 58.8) while requiring only one-fourth the vision tokens. In fair comparisons with identical LLaMA3.1-8B backbones and training data (Table 4), FLARE-X 8B leads all baselines on MMBench-EN (78.4), MM-Vet (51.2), TextVQA (78.4), and ChartQA (79.6) on the Cambrian-1 7M dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLARE-L 3B (630 tokens) decisively outperforms Cambrian-1 8B (576 tokens) on MMBench-EN (79.6 vs. 75.9), MM-Vet (59.1 vs. 48.0), AI2D (79.4 vs. 73.0), and MMVP (76.3 vs. 51.3), and similarly surpasses Florence-VL 8B on MMBench-EN (79.6 vs. 76.2) and MM-Vet (59.1 vs. 56.3), despite being a smaller model with fewer tokens. Against Qwen2.5VL 7B (1400 tokens), FLARE-X 8B wins on MMBench-EN (83.6 vs. 83.2), POPE (89.1 vs. 85.9), Seed-Image (78.7 vs. 77.0), SQA (91.2 vs. 89.0), and CVBench (81.5 vs. 81.1), but trails on MMBench-CN (78.3 vs. 82.8), MM-Vet (62.8 vs. 69.7), MME-Cognition (378.9 vs. 633.4), OCRBench (69.9 vs. 88.5), and MMMU (45.6 vs. 58.0), yielding a roughly 50/50 split. FLARE-L 8B (630 tokens) matches LLaVA-OneVision 7B (2400 tokens) on MMBench-EN (81.8 vs. 81.7) and slightly leads on MM-Vet (59.6 vs. 58.8), but falls behind on MME-Cognition (347.3 vs. 483.0), OCRBench (66.3 vs. 69.7), and SQA (89.7 vs. 96.6). In the controlled fair comparison (Table 4, same LLaMA3.1-8B backbone and training data), FLARE-X 8B leads all baselines on the Cambrian-1 7M dataset across MMBench-EN (78.4), MM-Vet (51.2), TextVQA (78.4), and ChartQA (79.6), while FLARE-L 8B matches Eagle 8B and LLaVA-NeXT 8B on MMBench-EN (77.8 vs. 76.6/76.4) using far fewer vision tokens and a single encoder versus four. Against MiniCPM-V-2.0 3B, FLARE-L 3B leads on every reported benchmark, e.g., MMBench-EN 79.6 vs. 69.1 and MM-Vet 59.1 vs. 41.0, while using only ~1/100 of MiniCPM-V's training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLARE extends the LLaVA/LLaVA-NeXT paradigm (vision encoder → MLP projector → LLM decoder) by injecting cross-modal interaction at every pipeline stage rather than deferring it to LLM decoding. It draws conceptual inspiration from Q-Former's text-guided visual projection (InstructBLIP) and entity-enhanced alignment (SEA), but unifies these into a single framework with bidirectional mapping losses and context-aware latent tokens during decoding. The text-driven VQA synthesis departs from the dominant image-first data construction (LLaVA, ShareGPT4V, Pixmo) by starting from high-quality text and generating matching images via diffusion, inverting the typical pipeline. Architecturally, FLARE achieves competitive results with a single SigLIP2 encoder where Cambrian-1 and Eagle rely on four encoders, and with far fewer vision tokens than LLaVA-NeXT's dynamic resolution approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical takeaway is that deep, multi-stage cross-modal integration can substitute for brute-force increases in vision tokens or encoder count, offering a more efficient path to strong VLM performance—relevant for deployment-constrained settings. The text-driven VQA synthesis pipeline provides a reusable recipe for generating diverse, text-rich training data that targets the specific fusion behaviors the architecture encourages. Limitations include the acknowledged quality gap between synthetic and real images (lower human-rated image quality in their own Table 13), the added architectural complexity (three interacting components with multiple hyperparameters like λ, latent token counts, and exchange layer placement), and the fact that fair comparisons controlling for data and backbone (Table 4) show more modest gains than the headline cross-model comparisons suggest. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 8B parameters, whether the synthetic data quality gap can be closed with better generative models, and whether the windowed-attention semantic exchange layers generalize to very long-context or multi-image settings.
