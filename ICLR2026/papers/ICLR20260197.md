## Problem

Diffusion-based LLMs (dLLMs) offer faster inference and greater interactivity through parallel decoding and bidirectional context modeling, but their safety alignment has never been systematically evaluated. The paper identifies that these two architectural properties create a fundamental blind spot: existing alignment mechanisms fail to prevent harmful completions when adversarial interleaved mask-text prompts exploit the model's obligation to generate contextually coherent content at masked positions, even when the harmful intent is fully visible in the unmasked text.

## Input / Output

The method takes a vanilla harmful prompt (e.g., from HarmBench, JailbreakBench, or StrongREJECT) and produces an interleaved mask-text jailbreak prompt where [MASK] tokens are inserted at strategic positions separated by short benign phrases (capped at 10 words each). Critically, no harmful content is rewritten, obfuscated, or hidden — the malicious instruction remains fully exposed in the fixed (unmasked) tokens, while the actionable sensitive content is forced to appear at masked positions. The dLLM then decodes the masked spans in parallel, yielding a harmful completion. The auxiliary LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct or GPT-4o) performs the prompt transformation via few-shot in-context learning with 2–4 mask segments per prompt.

## Method

DIJA exploits two intrinsic dLLM properties: bidirectional context modeling, which coerces the model to fill masked spans with content that preserves overall coherence with the surrounding harmful context, and parallel decoding, which eliminates the opportunity for dynamic rejection sampling or on-the-fly risk assessment that autoregressive models can perform. The framework uses an auxiliary LLM guided by diverse few-shot demonstrations to automatically construct interleaved mask-text prompts, applying three strategies — prompt diversification across harmful forms, varied masking patterns (block-wise, fine-grained, progressive), and context-sensitive benign separator insertion — to maximize attack coverage. Ablations confirm the interleaved structure itself (not LLM-based refinement) is the primary attack driver, with ASR-e dropping from 55.5% to 14.0% when masks are removed. As a preliminary defense, the authors propose Refusal-Aware Denoising Alignment, which fine-tunes the dLLM on ~10,000 adversarial prompt–refusal pairs to emit a standardized refusal when the interleaved mask-text pattern is detected, reducing ASR-e by up to 56 points on JailbreakBench with only modest degradation on general benchmarks.

## Results

DIJA achieves the highest keyword-based ASR (ASR-k) across all four evaluated dLLMs on all three benchmarks, reaching up to 100% on Dream-Instruct and LLaDA-1.5 (JailbreakBench). On the safest dLLM (Dream-Instruct), DIJA surpasses the strongest prior baseline ReNeLLM by 54 percentage points in evaluator-based ASR on HarmBench (57.5% vs. 6.5%), by 78.5 points on JailbreakBench (90.0% vs. 11.5%), and by 37.7 points in StrongREJECT Score. Using GPT-4o for prompt construction (DIJA*) yields marginal gains, e.g., 99.0–100% ASR-k on JailbreakBench. The preliminary Refusal-Aware Denoising Alignment reduces DIJA's ASR-e on LLaDA-Instruct from 55.5% to 32.5% on HarmBench while preserving general capability (e.g., GSM8K 78.5→78.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIJA decisively outperforms all four baselines (GCG, AIM, PAIR, ReNeLLM) on every evaluated dLLM and benchmark. Against GCG, DIJA more than doubles ASR-e on LLaDA-Instruct (55.5% vs. 24.3%) and Dream-Instruct (57.5% vs. 6.7%). AIM is essentially ineffective on aligned dLLMs (0–4.8% ASR-k), while DIJA reaches 96–99% ASR-k. PAIR, the strongest search-based baseline, is surpassed by DIJA by 32.6 points in ASR-k and 11.9 points in ASR-e on LLaDA-Instruct (HarmBench). The most critical comparison is against ReNeLLM (the strongest rewriting-based baseline): on the safest model Dream-Instruct, DIJA achieves 57.5% ASR-e versus ReNeLLM's 6.5% (a 54-point gap on HarmBench) and 90.0% vs. 11.5% on JailbreakBench (78.5-point gap), demonstrating that DIJA's exploitation of bidirectional masking is fundamentally more effective than prompt-rewriting strategies that were designed for autoregressive models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the jailbreak attack literature (GCG, PAIR, ReNeLLM, AIM) from autoregressive LLMs to the emerging class of diffusion-based LLMs (dLLMs), identifying that the architectural properties of bidirectional context modeling and parallel decoding create a fundamentally new attack surface. Conceptually, it generalizes prefilling attacks (which exploit prefix injection in left-to-right models) to arbitrary masked-span manipulation enabled by non-autoregressive decoding. For the defense component, it adapts the 'deep alignment' principle from Qi et al. (2024) — which showed that standard refusal training is shallow and vulnerable to prefilling — into a Refusal-Aware Denoising Alignment tailored to the dLLM denoising process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

dLLMs (LLaDA, Dream, MMaDA, DiffuCoder, Inception Labs' Mercury) are rapidly gaining adoption for code generation, structured output, and interactive editing, yet this work shows their safety alignment is architecturally incompatible with the very features that make them useful. The finding that perplexity-based defenses are fundamentally inapplicable to dLLMs (mask tokens cause artificial perplexity surges) closes off a common mitigation path. Key open questions include whether the preliminary Refusal-Aware Denoising Alignment generalizes across model families, whether multi-modal dLLMs inherit the same vulnerability, and whether disabling arbitrary mask placement in closed-source APIs is a sufficient long-term fix or merely a feature regression.
