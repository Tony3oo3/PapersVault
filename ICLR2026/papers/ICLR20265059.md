## Problem

Sequential model editing—applying multiple successive knowledge updates to LLMs—suffers from catastrophic forgetting and rapid performance collapse, yet the underlying geometric mechanism driving this degradation has remained unclear. This paper identifies hyperspherical uniformity of weight neuron distributions as the critical factor, demonstrating empirically and theoretically that fluctuations in Hyperspherical Energy (HE) during editing directly cause corruption of pretrained knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a pretrained LLM weight matrix W ∈ R^{d₁×d₀}, a batch of knowledge edit triples (subject, relation, object), and a perturbation matrix ΔW computed by any base editing method (e.g., MEMIT, AlphaEdit). It additionally requires an eigendecomposition of WᵀW to identify principal hyperspherical directions. The output is the updated weight matrix Ŵ = W + ΔW·P⊥, where P⊥ is a d×d projection matrix that attenuates perturbation components along the top-r principal directions. The projection is applied post-hoc as a single modular step, making it a plug-and-play wrapper around existing editing algorithms without modifying their internal optimization.

## Method

SPHERE (Sparse Projection for Hyperspherical Energy-Regularized Editing) operates in three stages: (1) it estimates the principal hyperspherical directions of the pretrained weight matrix by computing the top-r eigenvectors of (1/n)WᵀW, where r is chosen so that the cumulative eigenvalue ratio exceeds a threshold η (default 0.5); (2) it defines a sparse projection matrix P⊥ = I − αUUᵀ, where U is the matrix of principal directions and α (default 0.5–0.8) controls suppression strength, yielding a soft orthogonal projection that attenuates rather than fully removes principal-direction components; (3) it projects the editing perturbation onto this complementary space via Ŵ = W + ΔW·P⊥, thereby injecting new knowledge while minimizing interference with the geometric structure that encodes pretrained knowledge. The theoretical contribution proves that |ΔHE| lower-bounds the output perturbation ΔV (Theorem 1), establishing that preserving hyperspherical uniformity is necessary for knowledge retention, and further proves that the SPHERE projection monotonically reduces the ℓ₂-norm of each neuron perturbation (Theorem 4), guaranteeing bounded HE variation.

## Results

On LLaMA3-8B with 15,000 sequential edits, SPHERE achieves 90.01% Efficacy and 84.67% Generalization on ZsRE, and 52.89% Efficacy and 32.07% Generalization on CounterFact, outperforming the best baseline (AlphaEdit) by an average of 16.41% across editing metrics. On Qwen2.5-7B (5,000 edits), SPHERE reaches 70.04% Efficacy on ZsRE and 60.76% on CounterFact. As a plug-and-play enhancement, SPHERE improves existing baselines by 49.05% in Efficacy, 42.64% in Generalization, and 24.44% in Specificity on average (38.71% overall).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPHERE dominates all five baselines on both models and both datasets. Against AlphaEdit (the strongest baseline), SPHERE gains +3.37 pts Efficacy and +3.39 pts Generalization on ZsRE (LLaMA3-8B), and a massive +48.52 pts Efficacy and +30.36 pts Generalization on CounterFact, where AlphaEdit collapses to 4.37% Efficacy after 15k edits while SPHERE sustains 52.89%. On Qwen2.5-7B, SPHERE beats AlphaEdit by +28.03 pts Efficacy on ZsRE and +16.84 pts on CounterFact. PRUNE is the second-strongest baseline on Qwen2.5 (47.93% ZsRE Efficacy) but still trails SPHERE by 22.11 pts; on LLaMA3-8B, PRUNE, MEMIT, RECT, and FT all collapse to near-zero Efficacy after 15k edits, while SPHERE maintains 90.01%. As a plug-and-play add-on, SPHERE's single projection line boosts MEMIT, PRUNE, RECT, and AlphaEdit by 49.05% Efficacy, 42.64% Generalization, and 24.44% Specificity on average, confirming broad applicability beyond its default AlphaEdit base.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPHERE sits at the intersection of two research threads: sequential model editing (ROME, MEMIT, RECT, PRUNE, AlphaEdit) and hyperspherical uniformity regularization (Liu et al., 2018, 2021). While AlphaEdit already projects perturbations into the null space of prior knowledge, SPHERE reframes the problem geometrically—identifying principal hyperspherical directions via eigendecomposition and projecting into their orthogonal complement—thereby providing a principled, HE-driven justification for why such projections stabilize editing. The paper's key departure is the theoretical result linking HE fluctuations to a lower bound on pretrained-knowledge degradation, which unifies the empirical observation that editing failures coincide with weight-geometry collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of SPHERE (a single projection step improving all baselines by 38.71% on average) makes it immediately deployable alongside any existing editing pipeline, and the 16.41% gain over the strongest baseline (AlphaEdit) on 15k sequential edits addresses a critical practical bottleneck for LLM knowledge maintenance. The theoretical lower bound on knowledge degradation from HE changes provides a diagnostic tool: practitioners can monitor HE during editing to predict when collapse is imminent. Open questions include whether the eigendecomposition-based principal space estimation remains effective at 70B+ scale, whether the method extends to parameter-preserving editing (adapters, KV-cache methods), and whether the ~3–7% computational overhead becomes prohibitive in real-time deployment settings.
