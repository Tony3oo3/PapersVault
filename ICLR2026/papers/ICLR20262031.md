## Problem

Test-time scaling of diffusion models for complex reasoning is hindered by two gaps: existing scaling methods depend on external verifiers that are difficult to obtain for reasoning tasks, and the search algorithms used (e.g., Best-of-N) plateau quickly as compute budget grows. The paper asks whether the intrinsic energy function of an energy-based diffusion model can serve as a reliable verifier for number-of-sample scaling, enabling scalable intrinsic reasoning without any external feedback.

## Input / Output

The method consumes discrete grid-based reasoning instances — maze grids (walls, start, goal) and partially filled Sudoku grids — which are one-hot encoded into continuous H×W×C tensors for the diffusion process. The model is trained on smaller/easier instances (mazes up to 6×6, Sudoku with 31–42 givens) and evaluated on substantially harder out-of-distribution test sets (mazes up to 30×30, Sudoku with as few as 17 givens). The final continuous output is decoded to a discrete solution via argmax along the channel dimension, and success is measured by exact match to the ground-truth solution.

## Method

VFScale introduces two complementary innovations. On the training side, a Monotonic-Regression Negative Contrastive Learning (MRNCL) loss samples two negatives at different L2 distances from a positive and enforces a positive-slope linear relationship between energy and distance, ensuring performance-energy consistency; a KL regularization applied at every denoising step further smooths the energy landscape to improve navigability. On the inference side, a hybrid Monte Carlo Tree Search (hMCTS) denoising algorithm first runs Best-of-N over L parallel trajectories during the noisy early steps for broad exploration, then switches to MCTS for the remaining denoising steps where the learned energy function serves as both the policy (via its gradient) and value network, with DDIM used for fast rollout simulation. This hybrid strategy lets the model allocate more search depth where the energy landscape is most informative, achieving up to 88% success on 15×15 mazes (trained only up to 6×6) versus 6% for the baseline.

## Results

On the Maze task (trained on ≤6×6, tested on 15×15), VFScale with hMCTS denoising achieves 88.28% success rate at N=161, compared to only 10.94% for the original energy diffusion model with BoN under the same budget. On the harder Sudoku dataset (fewer given entries than training), VFScale hMCTS reaches 42.97% success rate at N=321, versus 28.12% for the original model. The performance-energy consistency metric improves from ~73% (original) to ~84% (VFScale), confirming that the MRNCL loss effectively aligns the energy landscape with true solution quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline (Du et al. 2024, energy diffusion with contrastive loss), VFScale achieves a dramatic improvement: on the 15×15 Maze task, the original model with BoN plateaus at 10.94% success rate at N=161, while VFScale hMCTS reaches 88.28% — an absolute gain of over 77 percentage points. On the harder Sudoku dataset, the original BoN reaches 28.12% at N=321, whereas VFScale hMCTS achieves 42.97%, a 14.85-point improvement. Against the standard DDPM baseline (Ho et al. 2020), the gap is even more pronounced: DDPM with ground-truth-guided BoN reaches only 24.22% on Maze at N=161 (Table 15), and DDPM with hMCTS and ground-truth guidance reaches 24.22% at N=161 (Table 20), both far below VFScale's 88.28%. Notably, even when the original energy diffusion model is given a perfect ground-truth verifier for BoN selection, it only reaches 17.19% on Maze at N=161 (Table 2), confirming that the training-side improvements (MRNCL + KL) are essential and cannot be compensated by a better external verifier alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the energy-based diffusion framework for iterative reasoning (Du et al., 2022, 2024) by addressing its critical limitation: the inability to scale test-time compute effectively. While prior work on test-time scaling for diffusion (Ma et al., 2025; Yoon et al., 2025) relies on external verifiers trained on labeled data, VFScale instead uses the model's own learned energy function as an intrinsic verifier, drawing on the score-energy equivalence from Du et al. (2023). The inference strategy adapts AlphaGo's MCTS with UCB (Silver et al., 2016) and DDIM fast sampling (Song et al., 2021a) into a hybrid search over the denoising trajectory, while the training side introduces a novel MRNCL loss that enforces monotonic energy-vs-distance consistency, going beyond the contrastive loss in Du et al. (2024) which only pushes positives to local minima without ordering negatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a fundamental bottleneck for diffusion-based reasoning: the energy landscape's poor alignment with actual solution quality (only ~73% performance-energy consistency in the baseline), which caps the effectiveness of any test-time scaling strategy. The finding that a properly trained intrinsic energy function can match a perfect continuous ground-truth oracle (82% vs 84% at N=81 on 15×15 Maze) is practically significant, as it eliminates the need for expensive external verifiers that are difficult to obtain for reasoning tasks. However, the approach is validated only on two grid-based tasks (Maze, Sudoku) with relatively small model architectures, and whether the energy-as-verifier paradigm generalizes to more complex, high-dimensional reasoning (e.g., mathematical proofs, code generation) remains an open question. The deliberate trade-off of sacrificing single-sample performance for scalability also raises questions about optimal energy landscape design for different deployment regimes.
