## Problem

Latent-space counterfactual explanation methods inherit the ambiguity of Wachter et al.'s objective: the distance metric determines whether perturbations are semantically meaningful counterfactuals or adversarial examples. Existing approaches adopt flat Euclidean geometry or pullback metrics from pixel space or standard classifiers, both of which are perceptually misaligned and adversarially vulnerable, causing off-manifold artifacts, semantic drift, or collapse into on-manifold adversarial regions. No prior method combines a robust, perceptually grounded Riemannian metric with global geodesic path optimization in the latent space of a high-capacity generative model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image and a target class label as input. The image is first mapped to the StyleGAN2/3 latent space via a trained image-to-latent encoder, and an arbitrary target-class sample from the dataset is similarly encoded to initialize the path endpoint. The output is a counterfactual image (the generator output at the optimized endpoint z_T) together with the full geodesic path of T+1 intermediate latent codes, each of which can be decoded into a perceptually coherent image. The path is discretized with T waypoints (T=10 in experiments) and optimized jointly, so the output is structured as a sequence rather than a single image.

## Method

PCG constructs a composite robust perceptual metric G_R(x) by summing weighted pullbacks of the Euclidean metric from K intermediate layers of an adversarially-trained robust backbone (e.g., L2-robust ResNet-50, ε=3.0), then pulls this metric back through the StyleGAN2/3 generator to define a latent-space Riemannian metric G_Z(z) that penalizes brittle, non-robust directions. Counterfactual generation proceeds in two phases: Phase 1 minimizes the discrete robust perceptual energy (a weighted sum of squared feature-space velocities across all robust layers) over intermediate path points with fixed endpoints, yielding a geodesic consistent with the robust semantic geometry; Phase 2 releases the endpoint and jointly optimizes the energy plus a classification loss with a dynamic λ schedule (starting at 1e-4, multiplied by 5 every 50 steps) and periodic re-anchoring that snaps the endpoint to the closest target-class point along the current path. The key insight is that robust models exhibit perceptually aligned gradients, making the Euclidean metric in their feature spaces a reliable proxy for perceptual similarity—unlike pixel-space L2 or standard classifier features—so geodesics under this metric naturally traverse semantically valid, on-manifold regions while avoiding adversarial shortcuts.

## Results

On three vision datasets (AFHQ, FFHQ, PlantVillage) using STYLEGAN2, PCG achieves the lowest distance under all four metrics, with the largest margins under the robust perceptual metric L_R: 0.31 (AFHQ), 0.22 (FFHQ), and 0.20 (PlantVillage), compared to 1.75–2.11 for the best baseline (RSGD-C) — a 5–10× improvement. Under the standard feature metric L_F, PCG also leads (0.34–0.53 vs. 0.45–0.65 for RSGD-C). On the explanandum-based evaluation (Table 2, AFHQ), PCG attains R-FID of 9.1 vs. 28.3 for RSGD-C, R-LPIPS of 0.17 vs. 0.53, and a mean Semantic Margin of +0.74 vs. +0.05, confirming that PCG counterfactuals land in genuinely target-class regions of robust feature space rather than adversarial neighborhoods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCG outperforms all four baselines on every metric and dataset. Against REVISE (Joshi et al., 2019), which minimizes pixel L₂ distance plus classification loss via SGD, PCG reduces L_R by 88–93% (e.g., 0.31 vs. 2.70 on AFHQ); REVISE outputs are consistently off-manifold adversarial examples that appear close under L₂ but deviate sharply in perceptual geometry. Against VSGD (Dombrowski et al., 2024), which drops the distance term and uses flat Euclidean SGD, PCG again dominates on all metrics, with VSGD producing the worst L_R scores (2.86–3.01) due to off-manifold traversal. Against RSGD and RSGD-C (Pegios et al., 2024), which use Riemannian SGD under pixel-space or standard-classifier-feature pullback metrics, PCG's advantage is most pronounced under L_R: RSGD-C achieves 1.75–2.11 while PCG reaches 0.20–0.31, a 5–10× gap. RSGD-C is the strongest baseline on L_F (0.45–0.65) but still trails PCG (0.34–0.53), and both RSGD variants produce on-manifold adversarial examples that remain smooth under L₂ and L_F yet are semantically fragile — a failure mode that L_R exposes. On the explanandum-based metrics (Table 2), PCG's R-FID of 9.1 is 3× lower than RSGD-C's 28.3, and its mean Semantic Margin of +0.74 (vs. +0.05 for RSGD-C) confirms that PCG counterfactuals reside in target-class regions of robust feature space rather than in adversarial neighborhoods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Riemannian latent-space optimization framework for counterfactual explanations (Pegios et al., 2024; Joshi et al., 2019) by replacing the pixel-space or standard-classifier pullback metric with a composite metric induced from adversarially robust vision backbones, and by replacing single-point Riemannian SGD with global two-phase geodesic path optimization. The pullback metric construction and discrete energy formulation draw directly on Shao et al. (2017), while the motivation for using robust feature spaces is grounded in the perceptually-aligned gradients (PAG) literature (Ganz et al., 2023b; Srinivas et al., 2024; Kaur et al., 2019b). The key conceptual departure from prior work is the argument that the CE/AE ambiguity identified by Wachter et al. (2017) and Browne & Swift (2020) can be resolved not by abandoning distance metrics but by endowing the latent manifold with a semantically robust geometry that penalizes adversarial directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled geometric mechanism for distinguishing meaningful counterfactuals from adversarial examples in high-dimensional vision, a problem that has resisted resolution through metric design alone. The robust perceptual metric and the associated evaluation diagnostics (L_R, R-FID, R-LPIPS, semantic margin) offer a reusable toolkit for assessing whether generated counterfactuals are genuinely on-manifold and semantically valid rather than adversarially collapsed. Limitations include the dependence on a pretrained robust backbone (adding a non-trivial model to the pipeline), the computational cost of path-based optimization relative to single-point methods, and evaluation on three relatively small datasets (AFHQ, FFHQ, PlantVillage) with binary or few-class classifiers. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large-scale multiclass settings, whether the robust geometry can be learned end-to-end rather than borrowed from a separate backbone, and whether the two-phase re-anchoring strategy generalizes to non-GAN generative models such as diffusion models.
