## Problem

Prior jailbreak research focused on additional string transformations to maximize attack success rate (ASR), but the impact of style patterns (e.g., 'create a list of') already embedded in original benchmark queries—semantically irrelevant to the malicious intent—remained unexplored. The paper asks whether these style patterns inflate ASR, how superficial style alignment during fine-tuning creates this vulnerability, and how to mitigate the risk without sacrificing style adaptation utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263537/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes instruction-tuning datasets (Alpaca-52K, Dolly-15K, or 10K synthetic style-specific pairs) where each instruction carries a style pattern (list, poem, news, legal, Shakespeare, or code) as a prefix, plus 50 safety training examples from the Anthropic Red Teaming Dataset that are GPT-4o-augmented to incorporate bigrams sampled from the fine-tuning data's style distribution. The output is a fully fine-tuned LLM (Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, or Gemma-3-12B) that adapts to the target style while preserving its original safety refusal behavior, evaluated via ΔASR on 2,134 jailbreak queries and ΔLC_WR on AlpacaEval.

## Method

SafeStyle is a minimal data-augmentation defense: during supervised fine-tuning on style-specific instructions, it injects only 50 safety examples whose prompts are rewritten by GPT-4o to embed one or more bigrams drawn from the fine-tuning data's style patterns, so the model learns to refuse malicious content even when it arrives in the same stylistic frame. The key empirical finding motivating this design is that safety examples must match the fine-tuning style to be effective—diverse or style-removed safety data reduces ASR only partially, while matched-style data fully restores the model's original safety strength. The paper further demonstrates through attention-weight analysis that ASR inflation correlates with a model's disproportionate attention to style tokens over intent tokens, and through controlled paraphrase experiments that the vulnerability is driven by semantic style compliance rather than brittle token-level backdoor triggers, confirming that the defense must operate at the semantic style level rather than blocking specific surface strings.

## Results

Across three LLMs (Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, gemma-3-12b) and six style patterns, SafeStyle is the only method that achieves negative ΔASR (i.e., ASR reduction) in every model–style cell of Table 1. The most dramatic gains appear on Shakespearean-style jailbreaks, where SafeStyle reduces ASR by 0.505 (Qwen2.5-3B) and 0.340 (Llama-3.1-8B) relative to the pre-fine-tuning baseline. On real-world instruction-tuning sets (Dolly-15K, Alpaca-52K), SafeStyle again yields the lowest ΔASR across all three models (e.g., −0.045 on Alpaca-52K for Llama-3.1-8B), while maintaining style-adaptation utility (ΔLC_WR) comparable to all baselines. In the overall safety–utility ranking (Table 9), SafeStyle achieves the best average rank of 2.438 versus 3.333 for the next-best method (Paraphrase).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SafeStyle outperforms all five baselines on the primary safety metric (ΔASR) in every model–style cell of Table 1. Compared to Vanilla (Bianchi et al., 2024), which uses diverse-style safety data, SafeStyle's style-matched augmentation is critical: Vanilla still leaves positive ΔASR on Poem (+0.166) and Legal (+0.096) for Llama-3.1-8B, whereas SafeStyle drives both negative. PTST (Lyu et al., 2024), which inserts safety system prompts only at inference time, is inconsistent—sometimes worse than No Defense (e.g., +0.310 ΔASR on Legal vs. +0.567 for No Defense) and occasionally degrades utility (−4.074 ΔLC_WR on Shakespeare). SPPFT (Li et al., 2025b), which freezes safety-related layers, provides the weakest protection among the five baselines, with ΔASR values close to No Defense in most settings. Constrained (Qi et al., 2025a) achieves near-zero ΔASR but at the cost of substantially lower style-adaptation utility (e.g., +2.939 vs. +6.978 ΔLC_WR on List for Llama). Paraphrase (Eiras et al., 2025) is the closest competitor, matching SafeStyle on some styles (List: −0.022 vs. −0.036) but failing to reduce ASR on Poem (+0.224) and News (+0.073) where SafeStyle achieves negative values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the superficial alignment hypothesis (Zhou et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024; Zhang & Wu, 2024) by identifying a concrete, measurable mechanism—style patterns in instruction-tuning data—that translates superficial alignment into a specific safety vulnerability (ASR inflation). It builds directly on Bianchi et al. (2024) for the core idea of incorporating safety training data during fine-tuning, but departs from prior safety defenses (mechanistic interventions, training objective modifications, prompt-template defenses) by targeting the style distribution mismatch between fine-tuning and safety data. Unlike prior jailbreak research that augments queries with adversarial string transformations to maximize ASR, this paper shows that the style patterns already present in unmodified benchmark queries are themselves sufficient to inflate ASR, reframing the problem as an alignment-data issue rather than an attack-craft issue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 32 of 36 models exhibit ASR inflation from semantically irrelevant style patterns has immediate implications for how the field interprets reported jailbreak benchmark scores—they may systematically overstate vulnerability to core malicious intent. Practically, any real-world fine-tuning that introduces style patterns (legal writing, code generation, poetry) can silently degrade safety, and the paper shows this can be mitigated with as few as 50 style-matched safety examples. Key limitations include reliance on GPT-4o as the sole primary judge (though cross-validated with two alternatives), inaccessibility to proprietary alignment datasets (only OLMo's data was analyzed for bigram overlap), and the use of relatively simple, template-based style patterns rather than the more nuanced stylistic variation found in natural user interactions. Open questions include whether the effect persists in multimodal or agentic settings, and whether more subtle register-level style shifts (beyond explicit format requests) produce similar inflation.
