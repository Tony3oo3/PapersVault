## Problem

Existing TTS systems struggle to synthesize long-form, multi-speaker conversational audio like podcasts, with most prior work limited to 1–2 speakers and short durations. Key challenges include maintaining vocal consistency across extended dialogues, capturing natural turn-taking and pacing, and preserving subtle non-lexical cues (breaths, lip smacks) essential for listener immersion. No publicly available system could generate coherent multi-speaker podcasts beyond ~10 minutes end-to-end.

## Input / Output

The model takes per-speaker voice prompt waveforms (encoded solely by the acoustic σ-VAE tokenizer into continuous latent features) and a text dialogue script with explicit speaker turn labels (tokenized into subword embeddings), interleaved with speaker identifier tokens and a start-of-speech token. It produces streaming multi-speaker conversational audio up to 90 minutes with up to 4 speakers, where each speech segment is generated autoregressively from historical context at an ultra-low 7.5 Hz frame rate, yielding a Real-Time Factor below 1.0 on a single GPU.

## Method

VIBEVOICE combines a Qwen2.5 LLM (1.5B or 7B) with a lightweight 4-layer diffusion head and two specialized tokenizers operating at 7.5 Hz: an Acoustic Tokenizer (σ-VAE with continuous latent space, trained with DAC-style adversarial losses) and a Semantic Tokenizer (trained via ASR proxy task). At each generation step, the LLM consumes a hybrid speech representation that linearly combines the previous step's acoustic latent with a semantic encoding of the generated waveform, stabilizing long-form generation. The diffusion head, conditioned on the LLM's hidden state, predicts the next acoustic VAE latent via classifier-free guidance (scale 1.3, 10 DPM-Solver++ steps), while the LLM simultaneously predicts a termination token to segment speech. Training uses curriculum learning (sequence length 4K→65K over 110K steps) on ~80B tokens from a pseudo-labeled podcast corpus annotated via a custom pipeline (Silero VAD + Whisper transcription + WeSpeaker/HDBSCAN diarization + quality filtering).

## Results

On the VIBEVOICE-Eval podcast benchmark (108 samples, 1–30 min, 1–4 speakers), VIBEVOICE-7B achieves the highest subjective average MOS of 3.76 (Realism 3.71, Richness 3.81, Preference 3.75), surpassing Gemini 2.5 Pro (3.66) and Elevenlabs v3 alpha (3.40). Objectively, VIBEVOICE-1.5B attains the lowest WER-W (1.11) while VIBEVOICE-7B achieves the highest speaker similarity (SIM-O 0.692). On the long-form subset (12–30 min), VIBEVOICE-7B maintains WER-W of 1.24 and SIM-O of 0.75, whereas MoonCast crashes on most long/multi-speaker samples. The 7.5 Hz acoustic tokenizer achieves leading PESQ (3.068) and UTMOS (4.181) on LibriTTS test-clean, outperforming codecs operating at 5–80× higher frame rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIBEVOICE-7B outperforms all proprietary and open-source baselines on subjective quality: it beats Gemini 2.5 Pro (3.76 vs. 3.66 avg.) and Elevenlabs v3 alpha (3.76 vs. 3.40) by a clear margin, and surpasses open-source Higgs Audio V2 (3.76 vs. 2.99) and SesameAILabs-CSM (3.76 vs. 2.89) by a wide gap. On objective WER, VIBEVOICE-1.5B (1.11) and VIBEVOICE-7B (1.29) both beat Gemini (1.73), Elevenlabs (2.39), MoonCast (2.81), and CosyVoice2 (3.45); VIBEVOICE-7B also achieves the best SIM-O (0.692) among all systems with reported values, edging out CosyVoice2 (0.68) and SesameAILabs-CSM (0.685). On the long-form (12–30 min) subset, VIBEVOICE-7B sustains WER-W 1.24 and SIM-O 0.75, while MoonCast fails to complete most samples (crashes on ≥3 speakers and long durations). On the SEED short-utterance benchmark, VIBEVOICE-1.5B (CER 1.16% zh, WER 3.04% en) is competitive with Seed-TTS (1.12% zh, 2.25% en) and CosyVoice2 (1.45% zh, 2.57% en) despite using a 7.5 Hz frame rate versus 25–50 Hz for those systems. For tokenizer reconstruction, VIBEVOICE's 7.5 Hz acoustic codec achieves PESQ 3.068 and UTMOS 4.181 on LibriTTS test-clean, surpassing Encodec (2.72/3.04 at 600 Hz), DAC (2.738/3.433 at 400 Hz), and WavTokenizer (2.373/4.049 at 75 Hz), though its STOI (0.828) trails higher-frame-rate codecs due to the lack of speech enhancement in training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIBEVOICE builds directly on the next-token diffusion framework and σ-VAE formulation from LatentLM (Sun et al., 2024), adapting them from image generation to speech synthesis, and adopts the lightweight diffusion head architecture from Li et al. (2024b) for continuous latent prediction. It departs from the dominant discrete-token paradigm (VQ-VAE, Encodec, SoundStream) by employing a continuous σ-VAE acoustic tokenizer at an ultra-low 7.5 Hz frame rate, and from prior podcast generation work (MoonCast, NotebookLM) by supporting up to 4 speakers over 90 minutes in a single end-to-end pass rather than concatenating individually synthesized utterances. The decoupled hybrid representation—separate acoustic and semantic tokenizers fused at the LLM input—represents a conceptual departure from both unified codec designs and single-stream approaches, motivated by the finding that neither acoustic nor semantic features alone suffice for multi-speaker coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that long-form, multi-speaker conversational audio generation is tractable at scale with a single autoregressive model, removing the need for pipeline-based concatenation that degrades prosodic continuity. The 7.5 Hz continuous tokenizer is a practically significant efficiency contribution: it reduces the number of LLM decoding steps per second of audio by roughly 3–10× compared to typical 25–75 Hz discrete codecs, enabling real-time streaming (RTF < 1) without sacrificing perceptual quality. Open questions remain around scalability beyond 4 speakers, multilingual generalization (training data is English-centric), and whether the pseudo-labeled annotation pipeline introduces systematic biases that limit expressiveness. The deliberate exclusion of speech enhancement, while preserving naturalness, leaves robustness to noisy real-world inputs unaddressed.
