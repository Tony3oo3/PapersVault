## Problem

When deploying an LLM on an unseen evaluation task (e.g., end-to-end encrypted user conversations), the owner has no access to the evaluation data's domain, distribution, or labels—only coarse, noisy scalar feedback such as user ratings. Existing data mixing and selection methods (DoReMi, LESS, Aioli) all require fine-grained evaluation data information (gradients, labels, or distributional assumptions) that is unavailable in this setting, leaving a gap in how to iteratively optimize training data mixtures using only black-box feedback.

## Input / Output

DUET takes n training datasets from distinct domains (e.g., Wikitext, gsm8k, TruthfulQA), pre-computed influence function scores for every data point within each domain, and a scalar noisy feedback signal (evaluation task loss or accuracy) with no closed mathematical form. It produces an optimized data mixture of exactly M data points, the corresponding optimal mixing ratio r on the probability simplex (‖r‖₁=1), and fine-tuned LLM parameters (via LoRA). The feedback is consumed as a single noisy observation per BO iteration, and the mixing ratio is the only continuous variable the optimizer directly controls—the discrete data selection is handled internally by the IF-weighted sampling step.

## Method

DUET is a global-to-local bilevel algorithm that reparameterizes the discrete data-mixture selection problem into a continuous optimization over mixing ratios (Theorem 3.1). At the outer (global) level, Bayesian Optimization with a Gaussian Process surrogate and LCB acquisition function proposes a candidate mixing ratio r_t at each iteration, treating the inner-problem solution as a noisy black-box function. At the inner (local) level, IF-weighted sampling draws data points from each domain proportionally to their pre-computed influence function scores until the proposed ratio is satisfied, yielding a high-quality data mixture sample; the best of k such samples provides the noisy observation fed back to the GP. The key theoretical contribution is characterizing the IF-driven estimator's error as a shifted truncated exponential (Theorem 3.2) and proving a sub-linear cumulative regret bound (Theorem 4.1) that guarantees convergence to the optimal mixing ratio even with noisy feedback. The algorithm is flexible: any data selection method (LESS, log-det, remove-harmful) can replace IF in the inner loop, trading off compute cost for selection quality.

## Results

DUET-IF achieves the best performance across all four evaluation task groups: 59.8% on TruthfulQA, 84.2% on gsm8k, 52.4% on PubmedQA+HeadQA, and 69.6% on CommonsenseQA+TriviaQA (all with Llama-3-8b-Instruct, 10 BO iterations, k=1). These gains are consistent across both in-domain (TruthfulQA) and out-of-domain (gsm8k, PubmedQA+HeadQA, CommonsenseQA+TriviaQA) evaluation settings. DUET-LESS, the second-best variant, achieves 58.7%, 80.5%, 50.8%, and 67.6% respectively, confirming that the IF-driven estimator is the strongest inner-loop data selection choice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUET-IF outperforms all five baselines on every evaluation task. Against Aioli (51.1/76.5/48.8/63.7), DUET-IF gains 8.7, 7.7, 3.6, and 5.9 points respectively, with the largest margin on TruthfulQA where Aioli's distributionally-robust reweighting cannot exploit task-specific feedback. Multi-Fid (52.8/73.9/47.2/65.2) trails DUET-IF by 7.0, 10.3, 5.2, and 4.4 points; its multi-fidelity BO framework still lacks the inner-loop data selection that DUET provides. ODM (46.1/77.3/45.8/60.1) is the weakest baseline, underperforming DUET-IF by 13.7, 6.9, 6.6, and 9.5 points, as its online mixing strategy cannot adapt to unseen-task feedback. DoReMi and LESS (shown as curves in Fig. 4 rather than Table 2) both fail to specifically adapt to the evaluation task because they require fine-grained data information (gradients, labels, or distribution) that is unavailable in the unseen-task setting, resulting in consistently lower performance than DUET across all four task groups.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUET sits at the intersection of Bayesian optimization (Srinivas et al., 2010; Chowdhury & Gopalan, 2017) and influence-function-based data selection (Koh & Liang, 2017), combining them into a bilevel optimization framework whose reparameterization draws directly on AutoML techniques (Chen et al., 2024b). It departs from prior data-mixing methods (DoReMi, Aioli, BiMix) and data-selection methods (LESS, TracIn) by explicitly rejecting their assumption of access to fine-grained evaluation data (gradients, labels, or distributions); instead, DUET operates under the constraint that only coarse, noisy scalar feedback from a deployed model is available. The authors position this as the first work to interleave data selection with BO for iterative data-mixture optimization under an unseen-task feedback loop, distinguishing it from both domain adaptation (which assumes labeled/unlabeled target data) and domain generalization (which assumes no feedback at all).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DUET addresses a realistic and increasingly common deployment scenario—end-to-end encrypted conversations, model marketplaces—where practitioners cannot inspect evaluation data but can collect iterative user feedback. The theoretical cumulative-regret guarantee (Theorem 4.1) provides convergence assurance that purely empirical data-mixing heuristics lack, and the modular design (swappable inner-loop data selection) makes it a practical testbed for future selection methods. Key limitations include the focus on fine-tuning rather than pre-training (where IF computation over billions of tokens becomes prohibitive) and the assumption of re-training from the same checkpoint each iteration. Open questions include whether the approach scales to pre-training with approximate IF scores, how it behaves with non-stationary feedback distributions over long deployment horizons, and whether the truncated-exponential assumption on the estimator's sampling distribution holds for more complex tasks.
