## Problem

Existing benchmarks for evaluating LLMs in single-cell biology are fragmented across narrow tasks, rely on multiple-choice or classification formats that diverge from real-world usage, and use metrics like BLEU or exact match that lack biological grounding and interpretability. No principled framework exists to determine whether an LLM genuinely understands cellular mechanisms—capturing both static identity and dynamic behavior—rather than merely memorizing superficial patterns.

## Input / Output

All five tasks standardize inputs into a unified 'cell sentence' format (natural language encoding of gene expression profiles), with the exception of Cell Generation (which takes ontology terms or cell-type names) and Scientific QA (which takes free-form mechanistic questions). Outputs are open-ended natural language responses—ontology labels, descriptive captions, synthetic cell sentences, up/down-regulated gene lists with post-perturbation profiles, and evidence-backed explanations—rather than constrained selections. The evaluation stage additionally feeds the evaluator LLM the task prompt, model response, ground-truth answer, and retrieved external knowledge (ontology paths, marker genes, gene function annotations, or PubMed excerpts), producing a 0–100 score with a structured biological rationale.

## Method

SC-ARENA introduces a Virtual Cell abstraction modeled as a Knowledge Cell class with static attributes (expression profiles, textual descriptions, ontology annotations) and dynamic methods (cell-to-environment signaling, environment-to-cell perturbation responses), unifying five tasks—cell type annotation, captioning, generation, perturbation prediction, and scientific QA—into a single evaluation paradigm. For scoring, the paper replaces brittle string-matching metrics with a knowledge-augmented LLM-as-a-judge framework inspired by Eval-RAG: an evaluator LLM (GPT-4o-mini) conditions on curated external resources (Cell Ontology, UniProt, Gene Ontology, CellMarker, and peer-reviewed literature) alongside the ground-truth answer to assign a 0–5 rating, which is rescaled to 0–100. This design enables partial credit for biologically related but imprecise predictions (e.g., scoring a generic 'NK cell' prediction against a specific 'CD16+/CD56-dim NK cell' ground truth via ontology path distance), penalizes mechanistically implausible outputs, and produces auditable rationales that explicitly cite domain knowledge. The framework is validated through Spearman correlations with ontology distance, marker gene overlap, DEG cosine similarity, and expert preference judgments across all five tasks, confirming biological fidelity and discriminative capacity over conventional NLP metrics.

## Results

Across five single-cell biology tasks, no model reaches the normalized passing threshold of 300 (5×60). The top general-purpose models are Kimi-K2 (total 277.16) and DeepSeek-R1 (276.66), with Kimi-K2 leading in Cell Generation (63.04) and Cell Captioning (67.89), while Qwen3-32B narrowly leads Perturbation Prediction (37.54) and Qwen3-235B leads Scientific QA (74.48). Cell Type Annotation remains the weakest task for general models (best: DeepSeek-R1 at 40.81), though the small domain-specialized C2S-Pythia-410m outperforms all general models on this task (47.34). Perturbation Prediction is universally poor, with all models scoring below 38.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among general-purpose models, Kimi-K2 and DeepSeek-R1 are nearly tied on total score (277.16 vs. 276.66), with Kimi-K2 winning on Cell Generation (+0.8) and Cell Captioning (+1.4), while DeepSeek-R1 leads on Cell Type Annotation (+0.8) and Scientific QA (+1.7). GPT-4o (263.81) trails both by ~13 points, and the Qwen3-235B (262.68) is within 1 point of GPT-4o. On Cell Type Annotation, the 410M-parameter C2S-Pythia-410m (47.34) outperforms every general-purpose model by 6–35 points, demonstrating that task-specific fine-tuning can surpass much larger general models on narrow ontological tasks. Conversely, domain-specialized perturbation models (ScGPT: 21.55, ScGenePT: 24.13–26.03) score 11–16 points below the best general model (Qwen3-32B: 37.54) on Perturbation Prediction, showing that specialization does not transfer uniformly. Cell-o1, a reasoning-oriented single-cell model, achieves a total of 234.20, underperforming all top general-purpose models by 28–43 points, suggesting that its constrained selection-based training limits open-ended generative capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

SC-ARENA departs from prior single-cell LLM benchmarks (C2S-Scale, Cell-o1, SOAR, CELLVERSE) that evaluate models in isolation on narrow tasks using constrained multiple-choice or prompted-completion formats with string-matching metrics. Conceptually, it builds on the Cell2Sentence representation for encoding expression profiles as text, the Eval-RAG paradigm for grounding LLM-as-a-judge in retrieved context, and the Virtual Cell challenge (Roohini et al., 2025) as an aspirational target. The key departure is the unification of five heterogeneous tasks under a single object-oriented 'Knowledge Cell' abstraction and the replacement of brittle lexical metrics with ontology- and database-grounded scoring, which no prior single-cell benchmark has attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark quantifies a 'fluent but not faithful' gap: frontier LLMs score 60–75 on captioning and QA but remain below 41 on cell type annotation and 38 on perturbation prediction, revealing that linguistic fluency does not translate to mechanistic or causal biological reasoning. The finding that a 410M-parameter specialized model (C2S-Pythia) outperforms GPT-4o on annotation while general models dominate open-ended generation highlights that no single architecture currently covers the full Virtual Cell capability space. Open questions include whether scaling alone can close the perturbation-prediction gap, how the framework extends to spatial and temporal reasoning, and whether the LLM-as-a-judge approach remains reliable as biological knowledge bases evolve.
