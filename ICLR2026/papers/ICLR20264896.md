## Problem

Existing trajectory-controlled video generation methods for robotic manipulation (e.g., Tora, DragAnything) model the robotic arm and manipulated object with separate, independent trajectories. This decentralized design causes feature entanglement in overlapping interaction regions, degrading visual fidelity and preventing the model from capturing physically plausible multi-object interactions—critical for generating high-quality demonstrations that downstream inverse dynamics models can reliably convert into executable actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264896/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 480×640 initial RGB frame, a text prompt, binary object masks for the robotic arm and manipulated object (obtainable via Grounded-SAM or a simple user brush tool), and a single collaborative trajectory decomposed into three temporal sub-phases (pre-interaction, interaction, post-interaction). It outputs a 37-frame manipulation video at 480×640. Notably, the user only annotates one unified motion path with phase boundaries rather than two full-length trajectories, and the mask-based object specification tolerates coarse brush inputs with minimal quality loss.

## Method

RoboMaster is built on the CogVideoX-5B video DiT and introduces a collaborative trajectory formulation that factorizes the joint distribution into three object-aware sub-distributions: pre-interaction (guided by the arm's trajectory and circular volumetric embedding), interaction (guided by the manipulated object's trajectory and embedding), and post-interaction (returning to arm guidance). Object identity is preserved via a coupled appearance-and-shape embedding: masked VAE latents are average-pooled into a token, then expanded into a circular volume whose radius scales with mask area, and propagated causally across frames for smooth transitions. The trajectory latent is injected into the DiT through a zero-initialized 2D spatial convolution followed by a 1D temporal convolution, added residually to hidden states. Training is supervised on a curated 21k video-trajectory dataset from Bridge, using standard diffusion noise-prediction loss with AdamW over 30k steps on 8 A800 GPUs.

## Results

On the Bridge benchmark (214 test samples), RoboMaster achieves the best FVD of 147.31, PSNR of 21.55, SSIM of 0.803, robot trajectory error of 16.47, and object trajectory error of 24.16, outperforming all baselines on every metric. In the user study, RoboMaster receives a 45.16% preference rate, more than double the next-best method (Tora at 17.74%). For embodied action planning, RoboMaster achieves the highest success rate on 7 of 9 tasks across RLBench and SIMPLER (e.g., 0.91 on 'pick coke can', 0.85 on 'put plate'), consistently surpassing Tora, TesserAct, and OpenVLA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboMaster outperforms all six baselines on every metric in the main Bridge comparison (Table 2). Against the closest competitor Tora (separate trajectories for arm and object), RoboMaster reduces FVD by 5 points (147.31 vs. 152.28), lowers object trajectory error by 2.27 (24.16 vs. 26.43), and more than doubles the user preference rate (45.16% vs. 17.74%), confirming that the collaborative trajectory design alleviates feature entanglement in interaction regions. IRASim, which only controls the robot arm trajectory, shows the largest object trajectory error (34.39) and a 10-point gap in FVD, highlighting the cost of ignoring object motion. TesserAct, a 4D-grounded method, lags substantially on all metrics (FVD 261.84, TrajError_obj 54.64), suggesting that depth/normal conditioning does not compensate for the lack of explicit interaction-phase decomposition. In the action-planning evaluation (Table 4), RoboMaster wins 7 of 9 tasks over Tora (losing only on 'put knife' 0.76 vs. 0.82 and 'open microwave' 0.54 vs. 0.61), and dominates OpenVLA by 10–30+ percentage points across all tasks, demonstrating that high-fidelity interaction videos yield more reliable inverse-dynamics action labels for downstream policy execution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CogVideoX-5B video diffusion transformer as its base architecture and extends the trajectory-controlled video generation paradigm for robotics (IRASim, Tora, DragAnything, MotionCtrl) by replacing independent per-object trajectories with a unified collaborative trajectory that decomposes the interaction into pre-interaction, interaction, and post-interaction phases. Conceptually, it departs from the object-decomposition strategy of Tora and DragAnything (which assign separate trajectories to the arm and manipulated object) in favor of a phase-decomposition strategy where each temporal segment is governed by the dominant agent. The mask-based circular volumetric object encoding draws on entity representation ideas from DragAnything but augments them with shape-aware spatial expansion, and the downstream inverse-dynamics pipeline follows the AVDC/TesserAct line of using generated video as demonstrations for action extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete bottleneck in scalable robot learning—data scarcity—by showing that interaction-aware video generation can produce demonstrations from which reliable action labels are extracted, closing the loop from generative model to executable policy. The collaborative trajectory formulation is a reusable design principle for any multi-agent interaction video generation task, and the 21k Bridge video-trajectory dataset provides a useful training resource. Open questions remain around 3D integration (the current framework is purely 2D pixel-space), generalization to diverse robot embodiments, and handling out-of-domain object categories where the model still produces distortions.
