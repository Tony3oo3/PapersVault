## Problem

Existing diffusion model watermarking methods face a fundamental trade-off: tuning-free approaches (Tree-Ring, RingID, GaussMarker) embed watermarks in the initial noise but require computationally expensive DDIM inversion for detection (>100 min per 5,000 images), while tuning-based approaches (Stable Signature) avoid inversion but demand costly fine-tuning of the diffusion decoder and generalize poorly to advanced attacks. No prior method simultaneously achieves fast injection, fast detection, high robustness to both standard perturbations and dedicated removal attacks, and low training overhead.

## Input / Output

For injection, SERUM takes the initial Gaussian diffusion noise η ∈ R^{4×64×64} (latent space of SD models) and a fixed watermark pattern A ∈ R^{4×64×64} drawn once from N(0,I), producing a watermarked image at 512×512 resolution via standard LDM sampling. For detection, the method encodes the input RGB image through the LDM's VAE encoder to obtain a latent x ∈ R^{4×W/8×H/8}, which is fed to a lightweight CNN detector (2.5M parameters) that outputs a scalar score in [0,1]. The detector operates entirely in latent space, bypassing the need for DDIM inversion or pixel-space analysis.

## Method

SERUM injects the watermark by replacing the initial diffusion noise with η' = √(1−α)·η + √α·A', where A' is the mean-normalized watermark and α (default 0.5) controls the detectability–diversity trade-off. A lightweight CNN detector is trained with a composite loss that includes terms for clean watermarked images, dynamically augmented watermarked images (via a Prioritized Experience Replay-inspired sampler that adaptively upweights hard perturbations), and precomputed augmentations, with analogous negative terms for clean images. The normalization of A provides a provably lower KL divergence from the unit Gaussian (d/2·log(1−α)) than GaussMarker's orthant-truncation approach (d·log 2), explaining SERUM's superior FID. For multi-user support, each user is assigned a unique subset of k noise patterns combined via a weighted sum, and user identification reduces to taking the argmax over per-user product scores of cached per-pattern detectors.

## Results

SERUM achieves an average TPR@1%FPR of 99.75–99.78% across SD 1.4/2.0/2.1 under eight standard image perturbations, outperforming GaussMarker (97.07–97.87%), Stable Signature (90.94–91.09%), RingID (88.48–88.77%), and TrustMark (48.22–59.43%). Against advanced generative removal attacks (VAE, Regen, Rinse, CtrlRegen, I2V), SERUM maintains 90.87% average TPR@1%FPR versus 65.40% for RingID and 58.01% for GaussMarker. Image quality impact is minimal: only a 1.06 FID increase (17.90→18.96) and a negligible CLIP Score drop (0.3143→0.3138), while injection takes just 17 ms and detection 2.5 min per 5,000 images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SERUM outperforms all four baselines on average TPR@1%FPR across every perturbation and model version. The closest competitor, GaussMarker, trails by roughly 2 percentage points on average (97.87% vs. 99.75% on SD 2.1) and shows notable weakness on Crop & Scale (88.72% vs. 99.54%). RingID collapses catastrophically on C&S (5.50% TPR) and is vulnerable at stricter 0% FPR thresholds (average drops to 85.88%), while Stable Signature degrades sharply under Gaussian noise (49.02% TPR) and TrustMark is the weakest overall (59.43% average), failing on rotation (4.30%) and random drop (0.42%). On advanced generative removal attacks, SERUM's 90.87% average TPR substantially exceeds RingID (65.40%) and GaussMarker (58.01%), with the gap widening on high-noise variants like Regen-30 (88.50% vs. 32.58% and 9.72%) and Rinse-2×25 (90.76% vs. 24.88% and 10.40%). On image quality, SERUM's FID (18.96 avg) is slightly worse than the tuning-based TrustMark (17.92) and Stable Signature (17.97) but clearly better than the other tuning-free methods RingID (19.70) and GaussMarker (20.16), confirming that its robustness advantage does not come at the cost of generation fidelity relative to comparable approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

SERUM unifies two previously separate families of diffusion-model watermarking: tuning-free methods (Tree-Ring, RingID, GaussMarker) that embed signals in the initial diffusion noise but require expensive DDIM inversion for detection, and tuning-based methods (Stable Signature) that modify the decoder but incur heavy training costs. The key departure is replacing DDIM inversion with a lightweight CNN detector trained directly on latent-space representations, while retaining the robustness advantage of noise-level injection. The augmentation sampler adapts Prioritized Experience Replay (Schaul et al., 2015) to dynamically focus training on the hardest perturbations, and the KL-divergence analysis (Appendix B) provides a theoretical justification for the weighted-sum injection being distributionally closer to the clean Gaussian than GaussMarker's sign-constrained approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 17 ms injection and 2.5 min detection for 5,000 images versus 117+ min for GaussMarker, making large-scale watermarking feasible in production pipelines. The demonstration of watermark radioactivity in diffusion models (persisting through LoRA and full fine-tuning of SD 1.4 and SANA) is, to the authors' knowledge, the first such result for DMs, opening a new avenue for provenance tracking across model lifecycles. Limitations include a notable drop to 58% TPR under the most destructive I2V attack and reduced performance on DiT-based architectures (95.24% vs. 99.75% on UNet-based SD 2.1), suggesting the method may not transfer uniformly across all generative backbones. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video, 3D, or audio generation, and whether the multi-user combinatorial scheme scales beyond ~10⁴ users without detector interference.
