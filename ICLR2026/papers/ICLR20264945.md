## Problem

Submodular maximization under cardinality, knapsack, and matroid constraints is NP-hard, and existing continuous-relaxation approaches (e.g., PGA-based methods for deep submodular functions) require O(n²/ε²) iterations, making them prohibitively slow for large-scale problems. This paper targets the specific subclass of Sums of Concave over Modular (SCM) functions and asks whether the optimization stage can be substantially accelerated while retaining strong approximation guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes an SCM objective defined by a collection of concave functions φᵢ and modular weight vectors mᵢ over n ground-set elements, together with one of three constraint types: a cardinality budget k, a knapsack budget B with per-item costs cᵢ, or a partition matroid with disjoint groups Gⱼ and rank bounds rⱼ. It outputs a binary subset of elements maximizing the submodular objective, accompanied by a provable approximation ratio of the form (1−ε−η−e^{−Ω(η²)}) for cardinality/partition matroid and half that for knapsack. The concave extension is used for the optimization phase (exploiting GPU-parallel evaluation and backpropagation for supergradients, including at non-differentiable points), while the multilinear extension is invoked only during rounding to preserve theoretical guarantees.

## Method

The framework has three stages. First, the discrete SCM is relaxed to the continuous concave extension F(x)=Σᵢ φᵢ(mᵢ(x)) over [0,1]ⁿ, whose supergradients are computed via backpropagation (handling non-differentiable activations like min{x,1} through right-derivatives). Second, the standard Projected Gradient Ascent is replaced by Accelerated Approximate Projected Gradient Ascent (AAPGA), which applies Nesterov momentum extrapolation and allows δ-approximate projections (computed in O(n log(n/δ)) via bisection for knapsack/cardinality or per-block bisection for partition matroids), yielding an O(n/T²) convergence rate and reducing the iteration count from O(n²/ε²) to O(n^{1/2}ε^{−1/2}). Third, the fractional solution is rounded: pipage rounding (O(n) time) is used for cardinality and partition matroid constraints, while a novel O(n) randomized rounding algorithm tailored to the knapsack polytope (using cost-normalized directions) achieves a constant 1/2 factor. The hybrid use of the concave extension for optimization and the multilinear extension for rounding is the key design choice that simultaneously enables GPU-parallel speedup and provable approximation bounds, giving overall query complexity O(n^{1/2}ε^{−1/2}(T₁+T₂)) for cardinality/partition matroid and O(nT₁+n^{1/2}ε^{−1/2}T₂) for knapsack.

## Results

Under the cardinality constraint (k=50), AAPGA+Rounding achieves near-optimal objective values across all four datasets (e.g., 864.60 on EU-Email, 3653.14 on Facebook-ego, 1642.38 on SBM, 3714.83 on Erdős-Rényi) while running up to 32.3× faster than Greedy (1.30 s vs. 42.02 s on Erdős-Rényi). Under the knapsack constraint, AAPGA+Rounding consistently outperforms the streaming baseline in objective value and is approximately 9.7× faster than Lazier-greedy on Facebook-ego (3.55 s vs. 34.51 s). On the large-scale Google+ network (107,614 vertices, 30.5M edges), AAPGA with pipage rounding and multi-GPU parallelization converges faster and reaches higher objective values than PGA with pipage rounding across all cardinality budgets from 10 to 5120.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Greedy (Nemhauser et al., 1978), AAPGA+Rounding matches or nearly matches the objective (within ~1.6% on Erdős-Rényi: 3714.83 vs. 3773.46) while being 32.3× faster (1.30 s vs. 42.02 s). Against Lazier-greedy (Mirzasoleiman et al., 2015), AAPGA+Rounding trades a small objective gap (e.g., 3653.14 vs. 3610.24 on Facebook-ego, actually slightly better) for a 16× speedup (1.84 s vs. 29.18 s). Against PGA+Rounding (Bai et al., 2018), AAPGA+Rounding wins decisively on both metrics: higher objective (3653.14 vs. 1661.63 on Facebook-ego) and lower runtime (1.84 s vs. 1.49 s, though the objective gap is the key differentiator). Against FAST (Breuer et al., 2020) and LS+PGB (Chen et al., 2021), AAPGA+Rounding achieves substantially higher objectives (e.g., 864.60 vs. 636.67 for FAST on EU-Email) at comparable or slightly higher wall-clock times. Against the streaming baselines (Badanidiyuru et al., 2014; Huang et al., 2020), AAPGA+Rounding dominates on both objective and runtime under both cardinality and knapsack constraints (e.g., 874.89 vs. 826.82 on EU-Email knapsack, 0.86 s vs. 2.08 s). Overall, AAPGA+Rounding is the only method that simultaneously achieves near-greedy solution quality and GPU-scale speed across all tested settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-relaxation → projected gradient ascent → pipage rounding framework of Bai et al. (2018) for deep submodular functions, specializing it to Sums of Concave over Modular (SCM) functions and replacing standard PGA with an accelerated variant (AAPGA) that borrows the Nesterov momentum/extrapolation mechanism from FISTA (Beck & Teboulle, 2009). The key conceptual departure is a hybrid strategy: the concave extension is used during optimization (enabling efficient GPU-parallel backpropagation for supergradients), while the multilinear extension is reserved for the rounding step to preserve approximation guarantees. The paper also extends supergradient computation to non-differentiable SCMs and introduces an O(n)-time randomized rounding for the knapsack constraint, trading a 1/2 factor for a dramatic reduction in rounding complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction in iteration complexity from O(n²/ε²) to O(n^1/2 ε^-1/2) is a substantial theoretical improvement that translates into a demonstrated 32.3× wall-clock speedup, making continuous-relaxation-based submodular optimization viable at scales where PGA was previously prohibitive. However, the approximation guarantee is not a clean constant (it involves a max over η with an exponential tail term), and the knapsack case incurs a 1/2 degradation, leaving open whether tighter guarantees are achievable. The method is restricted to SCMs (covering coverage, probabilistic coverage, and facility-location functions), so its applicability to general submodular objectives remains unaddressed. Open questions include whether the theoretical convergence rate is tight in practice, whether the multi-GPU parallelization advantage persists at even larger scales, and whether the hybrid concave/multilinear strategy can be extended to non-SCM submodular classes.
