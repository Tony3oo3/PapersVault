## Problem

Prior theoretical work established that well-separated clusters in the input produce well-separated clusters in t-SNE output (true positives), but no one had theoretically characterized t-SNE's failure modes: whether a clustered visualization implies a clustered input, or whether extreme outliers can be faithfully depicted. This paper proves that both cluster separation strength and outlier extremity are fundamentally unrecoverable from any stationary t-SNE embedding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260949/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is an n-point dataset X ⊂ R^D with D typically much larger than n, and the output is a d-dimensional (usually 2D) embedding Y. The paper analyzes the full set of stationary points of the KL-divergence objective (not just the local minimum found by gradient descent), parameterized by perplexity ρ ∈ [1, n−1]. The theoretical results are stated for datasets in R^{n−1} (the maximal ambient dimension for n points), and the key invariance properties hold for any perplexity value.

## Method

The core insight is that t-SNE is invariant under both multiplicative scaling and additive shifts of squared interpoint distances (Lemmas 17–18), meaning any dataset can be continuously deformed into a near-regular simplex via the map g(C) = ((1−C)·X)_{+C} without changing its t-SNE output. This implies (Theorem 3, Corollary 4) that a perfectly clustered visualization is a stationary point for inputs with arbitrarily weak cluster separation, and (Lemma 6) that an ε-neighborhood of the unit simplex generates all possible stationary t-SNE outputs. The second key property is the structural asymmetry between the input affinity P (a normalized symmetrized nearest-neighbor graph) and the output affinity Q (a radius-neighbor graph), which the paper exploits to prove (Theorem 9) that no stationary t-SNE embedding can depict an outlier with separation ratio α > 3.266 + o_n(1). These two properties together explain the paper's empirical findings: a single adversarially placed 'poison point' at the dataset mean can destroy cluster structure (Theorem 7), while far-away outliers are reliably absorbed into the bulk cluster structure.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result (Theorem 9) proves that no stationary t-SNE output can depict an outlier with separation α > 3.266 + o_n(1), regardless of input. Theorem 3 shows that any t-SNE visualization (including perfectly clustered ones with silhouette score 1) can be produced identically by inputs with arbitrarily small silhouette scores (scaled by any factor ε ∈ (0,1]). Theorem 5 establishes that pairwise distance perturbations of as little as ε = 0.01 on a unit simplex can yield entirely different t-SNE outputs. Empirically, on the BBC News dataset (n=2225, D=1024), injecting ~10% poison points halves the silhouette score of the t-SNE embedding, while injecting ~50% far outliers (α ∈ (0.8,1)) leaves cluster structure intact; on the KDD Cup 2015 fraud detection data, t-SNE mixes fraudulent users into the bulk while PCA keeps them separated.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares t-SNE against PCA and UMAP as alternative visualization methods. Against PCA, t-SNE is clearly inferior for outlier detection: PCA roughly preserves the α-outlier number in its output (fraudulent users remain separated in the KDD Cup 2015 data), while t-SNE suppresses all outliers to α < 0.2 in practice and often places them within the convex hull of the bulk. Against UMAP, t-SNE exhibits more extreme failure modes: t-SNE shows exact additive invariance (making it vulnerable to the impostor dataset construction of Theorem 3), while UMAP does not; a single poison point destroys t-SNE's cluster structure even at n=5000, whereas UMAP's response to a single poison point is less pronounced (though multiple poison points still degrade it). However, UMAP shares t-SNE's fundamental inability to depict far outliers, with the difference that UMAP tends to embed outliers deep within clusters while t-SNE places them at cluster edges. Both methods show extreme instability on near-simplex inputs (Figure 3 vs. Figure 13), confirming that the failure modes are not unique to t-SNE but are characteristic of force-based dimension reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper complements the existing line of positive theoretical analysis of t-SNE (Arora et al. 2018; Linderman & Steinerberger 2019), which proved that well-separated clustered inputs yield clustered visualizations, by establishing the first rigorous impossibility results for t-SNE's failure modes. The key technical mechanism—additive invariance of t-SNE under squared-distance shifts—was previously identified by Lee & Verleysen (2011, 2014) but not exploited to derive such broad non-identifiability theorems. The work also situates itself within the broader impossibility framework of Snoeck et al. (2026), which shows constant-dimension embeddings must misrepresent neighborhood structure, by characterizing precisely how t-SNE's specific architecture (KL-divergence between Gaussian and t-distribution affinities) produces these misrepresentations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since t-SNE is ubiquitous in single-cell genomics, NLP interpretability, and exploratory data analysis, the proof that cluster salience and outlier extremity are fundamentally unrecoverable from its output has direct consequences for scientific hypothesis generation and experimental design. The poison-point vulnerability (a single adversarial point can destroy cluster structure) and the hard outlier bound (α ≤ 3.266) provide concrete, actionable warnings for practitioners. Open questions include whether modifying the affinity construction (e.g., breaking additive invariance) can recover faithful distance representation while retaining t-SNE's practical utility, and whether the analogous failure modes for UMAP and other force-based methods can be characterized with similar precision.
