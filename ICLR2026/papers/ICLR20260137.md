## Problem

Existing 3D scene generation methods face a fundamental tradeoff: multi-view-oriented (MV-oriented) pipelines produce high-fidelity images but suffer from multi-view inconsistency that degrades reconstructed 3D scenes, while 3D-oriented pipelines guarantee geometric consistency but yield blurry, artifact-laden results. Moreover, both paradigms require minutes to hours per scene, blocking real-time applications. FlashWorld addresses both the quality-consistency gap and the efficiency bottleneck simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a single RGB image or a text prompt as content conditioning, combined with a camera trajectory specifying 24 key frames (480P resolution). Cameras are encoded as Reference-Point Plücker Coordinate raymaps. The output is a set of pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians (position, rotation quaternion, scale, opacity, and spherical harmonics coefficients) that can be rendered from arbitrary novel viewpoints. A single unified model handles both image-to-3D and text-to-3D tasks, generating a full scene in approximately 9 seconds on one H20 GPU.

## Method

FlashWorld initializes a DiT-based multi-view latent diffusion model from the Wan2.2-5B video diffusion backbone and pre-trains it in dual mode: an MV-oriented branch denoises multi-view latents directly (high visual quality), while a 3D-oriented branch decodes 3D Gaussian parameters from auxiliary features and denoises via differentiable rendering (inherent 3D consistency). Cross-mode post-training then applies DMD2 distillation where the frozen MV-oriented mode supplies real-score gradients to a few-step (4-step) 3D-oriented student that alternates rendering-based denoising with noise re-injection (LCM-style), transferring visual quality while preserving geometric consistency. A cross-mode consistency loss aligns the two modes' latent predictions to suppress floating artifacts from noisy rendering gradients. Finally, out-of-distribution co-training with single-view images, text prompts, and random camera trajectories (omitting GAN loss) broadens generalization beyond the multi-view training distribution.

## Results

On text-to-3D scene generation, FlashWorld achieves a Q-Align IQA of 4.12 on T3Bench-200, 3.96 on DL3DV-200, and 3.76 on WorldScore-200, substantially outperforming Director3D (3.24/2.51/2.55) and Prometheus (2.34/2.07/2.45). On the WorldScore benchmark, FlashWorld attains the highest average score of 68.72 (vs. WonderWorld 66.43, LucidDreamer 66.32, WonderJourney 60.30) while generating each scene in 9 seconds on a single H20 GPU—roughly 40–400× faster than baselines requiring 6–7 minutes. The method also achieves the best CLIP Score on two of three text-alignment subsets (27.63 on DL3DV, 29.13 on WorldScore), confirming strong text-to-scene alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashWorld outperforms all quantitative baselines on image quality metrics: it surpasses Director3D by +0.88 Q-Align IQA on T3Bench and +1.45 on DL3DV, and beats Prometheus by +1.78 and +1.89 respectively, while generating scenes 47× faster than Director3D (9 s vs. 7 min) and 1.7× faster than Prometheus. On the WorldScore benchmark, FlashWorld leads WonderWorld (68.72 vs. 66.43 average) by 2.3 points with comparable speed (9 s vs. 10 s), and exceeds LucidDreamer and WonderJourney by 2.4 and 8.4 points while being 40× faster. For image-to-3D generation, qualitative comparisons show CAT3D, Bolt3D, and Wonderland all suffer from multi-view inconsistency artifacts (noisy textures, repeated Gaussians, distorted geometry), whereas FlashWorld produces coherent, detailed scenes. In text-to-3D qualitative comparisons, SplatFlow and VideoRFSplat exhibit blurry artifacts and poor fine-detail reproduction, while FlashWorld generates structurally precise objects (e.g., animal fur, iron fences) with strong text alignment. The only metric where FlashWorld trails is CLIP-Aesthetic on T3Bench (4.49 vs. Prometheus 4.76), which the authors attribute to the metric favoring smooth outputs over the detailed, realistic results their method produces.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashWorld extends the dual-mode multi-view diffusion concept from Dual3D (Li et al., 2024a) by replacing image-diffusion initialization with a video diffusion model (Wan 2.2) and introducing a cross-mode distillation strategy that was absent in prior work. The 3D-oriented generation pipeline builds on 3DGS-based denoising methods (DiffusionGS, Cycle3D, Director3D), while the acceleration mechanism draws directly from DMD2 (Yin et al., 2024a) and LCM-style few-step sampling (Luo et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is treating the MV-oriented and 3D-oriented modes of a single model as teacher and student respectively, rather than treating them as separate pipelines as in prior MV-oriented (CAT3D, Bolt3D, Wonderland) or 3D-oriented (Dual3D, Director3D) approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9-second generation time for a full 24-view 480P 3D Gaussian scene represents a practical threshold that could enable interactive 3D scene creation in gaming, VR/AR, and robotics pipelines where prior methods required minutes to hours. The unified model handling both image-to-3D and text-to-3D without separate training simplifies deployment. Open questions remain around fine-grained geometry, mirror reflections, and articulated objects, which the authors acknowledge as current limitations. Future directions include autoregressive generation and extension to dynamic 4D scenes, suggesting the framework could become a foundation for real-time world generation systems.
