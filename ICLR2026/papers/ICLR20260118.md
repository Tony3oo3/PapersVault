## Problem

Generalized in-hand object rotation in the real world remains unsolved because the sim-to-real gap in contact-rich dexterous manipulation is severe, and prior methods are restricted to simple geometries, constrained wrist poses, or expensive customized hardware. A deeper bottleneck is data: learning a useful dynamics model requires massive, distributionally relevant real-world interaction data, yet catastrophic failures (dropping objects), hand-induced occlusions, and the need for human resets make such collection infeasible at scale—creating an inescapable conflict between generality and data quality.

## Input / Output

The deployed policy consumes a 10-step proprioceptive history (joint positions and previous targets), a wrist-orientation quaternion, and a 3D rotation-axis command, producing relative joint-position deltas that are integrated into absolute targets and executed through a 20 Hz PD torque controller. The joint-wise dynamics model operates on a dramatically lower-dimensional input—each joint's own 2W-dimensional state-action history (W=10 steps of position and target) rather than the full 16-joint hand state—and the residual policy outputs per-joint action corrections that are added to the base policy's output at deployment time.

## Method

The core contribution is a joint-wise neural dynamics model that factorizes the 16-joint hand-object system so each joint's next state is predicted solely from its own state-action history, acting as an information bottleneck that distills inter-joint coupling, actuation, and object-induced loads into low-dimensional effective terms (H_eff, G_eff). The paper provides a theoretical guarantee via the Data Processing Inequality for KL divergence, showing this projection strictly contracts distribution shift and tightens generalization bounds relative to a whole-hand model. To overcome the data bottleneck, they introduce a 'Chaos Box' autonomous collection strategy where the hand replays policy actions inside a container of soft balls, generating diverse stochastic contacts without catastrophic failures or human resets. A specialist-to-generalist pipeline first trains category-specific PPO experts in Isaac Gym, distills them into a single proprioception-only generalist via behavior cloning, and then trains a residual policy using the learned dynamics to compensate the base policy's actions for real-world deployment.

## Results

In simulation, DexNDM's generalist policy outperforms the AnyRotate re-implementation by 37–81% on rotation reward (RotR) across axis groups (e.g., 314.28 vs. 173.87 on general axes; 224.28 vs. 163.78 on ±y). In the real world, the joint-wise NDM (DexNDM) consistently and substantially improves over both direct transfer and whole-hand NDM across all three object sets (Regular, Small, Irregular) and four axis groups; for example, on Regular objects along ±z, DexNDM achieves 23.82 rad rotation and 37.50 s TTF versus 11.69 rad / 21.67 s (Direct Transfer) and 7.38 rad / 16.33 s (Whole-Hand NDM). On Small objects along ±z, the whole-hand NDM fails completely (0.00 rad) while DexNDM achieves 9.29 rad. The method enables unprecedented in-air rotation of objects with aspect ratios up to 5.33 and sizes as small as 2–3 cm under diverse wrist orientations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AnyRotate (Yang et al., 2024), DexNDM substantially outperforms on all four replicable real-world objects: e.g., on the Cube (palm-down), DexNDM rotates 39.10 rad vs. AnyRotate's 6.53 rad, and on the Tin Cylinder 15.68 rad vs. 2.63 rad; DexNDM also handles smaller objects (3 cm) and higher aspect ratios (up to 5.33) that AnyRotate does not test. Against Visual Dexterity (Chen et al., 2022), DexNDM achieves comparable or superior survival rotation angles on most of the 13 showcased objects (e.g., Teapot: 48 vs. 8* rad; GRAB Elephant: 7 vs. 3 rad), while uniquely handling small objects, high aspect ratios, and diverse wrist orientations that VD does not address. Against UAN (Fey et al., 2025) and ASAP (He et al., 2025), both baselines fail entirely in real-world tests (unable to rotate even a simple cylinder), attributed to out-of-distribution issues when compensators trained on free-hand data encounter object interaction dynamics; in the sim-to-sim transfer setting (Table 6), DexNDM still leads with 111.29 RotR in Genesis vs. UAN's 87.23 and ASAP's 75.72. The whole-hand NDM ablation (same data, same residual policy) consistently underperforms DexNDM, confirming that the joint-wise factorization is the critical design choice for sample efficiency and generalization under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the residual sim-to-real paradigm (ASAP, UAN) from locomotion to dexterous manipulation, but departs fundamentally from whole-system neural dynamics models (Neural Lander, MB-Max) by factorizing dynamics per joint, generalizing the RMA principle of self-history prediction to a 16-DoF hand. The specialist-to-generalist training pipeline (PPO oracles → BC distillation) follows the RotateIt/AnyRotate lineage for in-hand rotation, while the 'Chaos Box' autonomous data collection strategy is a novel departure from task-aware rollouts or wave-action exploration used in prior sim-to-real work. The theoretical contribution formalizes why this factorization aids generalization via KL-contraction under the data processing inequality, providing a principled justification absent in prior empirical sim-to-real methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper breaks what the authors call an 'inescapable conflict' in dexterous manipulation sim-to-real: the need for massive data to cover diverse objects versus the impossibility of collecting distributionally relevant data without catastrophic failures. The joint-wise model + Chaos Box combination enables a single policy to rotate objects with aspect ratios up to 5.33, complex animal shapes, and diverse wrist orientations—achievements no prior work has demonstrated simultaneously. Key limitations include reliance on proprioception-only observations (no vision or tactile), task-specific evaluation, and a single hardware platform (LEAP hand). Open questions include whether the factorization principle extends to bimanual tasks, whether richer sensory signals can raise the performance ceiling, and whether the autonomous data collection strategy generalizes to other contact-rich manipulation domains.
