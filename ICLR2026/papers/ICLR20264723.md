## Problem

Scientific LLMs face a fundamental 'tokenization dilemma' when processing raw biomolecular sequences: treating sequences as a specialized language fragments them into atomic symbols that destroy functional motifs, while treating them as a separate modality introduces semantic misalignment between bioinformatics encoders and the LLM's linguistic space. This paper asks whether bypassing direct sequence interpretation entirely—by feeding the model high-level structured context from established bioinformatics tools—can resolve this dilemma and unlock superior biological reasoning.

## Input / Output

The raw protein FASTA sequence is never fed directly to the LLM; instead it is processed through a bioinformatics toolchain (InterProScan, BLASTp, ProTrek) to produce a structured textual context containing Pfam domain descriptions, GO terms from homologs, and optional semantic fallbacks. The LLM receives only this human-readable context alongside the natural language question (e.g., 'What is the function of this protein?') and generates a free-text answer. Evaluation uses an LLM-as-judge metric (LLM-Score, 0–100) that scores factual alignment with database-extracted ground truth, avoiding the limitations of lexical-overlap metrics for open-ended biological answers.

## Method

The context-driven approach replaces sequence tokenization with a three-stage pipeline: (1) a multi-source toolchain runs InterProScan for intrinsic domain/motif detection, BLASTp against Swiss-Prot for homology-based GO annotation transfer, and ProTrek as a semantic fallback for orphan sequences; (2) an empirically-driven hierarchical strategy assembles the context, prioritizing the top BLAST homolog's GO terms, adding Pfam domain descriptions as complementary evidence, and including ProTrek output only conditionally when primary sources are absent; (3) a zero-shot general-purpose LLM (e.g., DeepSeek-V3) reasons over the structured text using a fixed prompt template. The key empirical finding is that appending the raw sequence to this context consistently degrades performance across all tested models (both specialized Sci-LLMs and general LLMs), demonstrating that raw sequences act as informational noise rather than useful signal. Representation-space analysis (t-SNE + ARI) confirms that context-derived embeddings achieve near-perfect functional separation (ARI 0.958) versus 0.49–0.69 for sequence-as-language models, and that the Q-Former alignment stage in sequence-as-modality models erases fine-grained mutation sensitivity.

## Results

On the protein QA benchmark (LLM-Score, 0–100), the context-driven approach with Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves the best overall score of 87.19, followed by Intern-S1 context-only (86.15) and DeepSeek-V3 context-only (84.99). In stark contrast, sequence-only inputs yield dramatically lower scores: NatureLM scores just 6.82, Intern-S1 43.33, and Evolla 59.93. For the specialized Sci-LLMs (Intern-S1, Evolla, NatureLM), adding raw sequence to context consistently degrades performance (e.g., Evolla drops from 74.02 to 70.53; Intern-S1 from 86.15 to 84.03), confirming that raw sequences act as informational noise. On the EC Number prediction task, the context-driven Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves an F1 of 0.406 at the 4-digit level, matching the specialized CLEAN model (0.408) while outperforming all sequence-only LLMs by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Intern-S1 (sequence-as-language), the context-driven approach with the same model achieves 86.15 (context-only) versus 43.33 (sequence-only), a 42.8-point gain, and context-only even slightly outperforms the combined input (86.15 vs. 84.03). Against Evolla (sequence-as-modality), the context-driven approach with DeepSeek-V3 (84.99) surpasses Evolla's best configuration (74.02 context-only) by 11 points, while Evolla's sequence-only score is only 59.93. Against NatureLM (sequence-as-language), the gap is most dramatic: context-driven Gemini 2.5 Pro scores 87.19 versus NatureLM's 6.82 sequence-only, a >12× improvement. Against general LLM baselines (DeepSeek-V3, Gemini 2.5 Pro, GPT-5, Qwen3), the context-driven approach demonstrates that these models already possess strong biological reasoning when given structured context, with Gemini 2.5 Pro context-only (87.19) being the single best result. On the EC Number prediction task, the context-driven approach with Gemini 2.5 Pro (F1 = 0.406 at 4-digit) matches the specialized CLEAN model (F1 = 0.408) while all sequence-only LLMs (DeepSeek-V3, Intern-S1) fall far below, confirming that high-level context enables general models to rival task-specific architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both dominant paradigms in Sci-LLM design—sequence-as-language (NatureLM, Intern-S1) and sequence-as-modality (Evolla, BioReason)—by arguing that neither resolves what the authors term the 'tokenization dilemma' (atomic tokenization destroys functional motifs; cross-modal alignment introduces semantic drift). Rather than proposing a new encoder or tokenizer, it repurposes the well-established bioinformatics toolchain (InterProScan, BLASTp, Pfam, ProTrek) as a context generator, positioning the LLM as a reasoning engine over structured textual evidence rather than a sequence interpreter. This is conceptually adjacent to retrieval-augmented generation and agent-based tool-use approaches (GeneAgent, ChemCrow), but is deliberately simpler: a fixed, deterministic pipeline rather than an adaptive agent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that raw sequences act as active informational noise (not merely redundant) when combined with context challenges the core investment thesis of the Sci-LLM field, which has focused heavily on sequence tokenization and cross-modal alignment. Practically, the context-driven pipeline is ~30× cheaper and ~150× faster in batch mode than specialized end-to-end models while achieving higher accuracy, making it immediately deployable. Key limitations include reliance on homology-based tools (performance degrades for truly novel orphan proteins), primary focus on proteins with only preliminary DNA results, and the open question of whether future architectures with better sequence representations might eventually make raw sequences useful again.
