## Problem

Conventional wisdom in MLLM training assumes that datasets from similar task categories (e.g., OCR data for chart tasks) will transfer best, but it remains unclear whether such surface-level similarity reliably predicts actual performance gains. This paper asks whether a training dataset's influence on a target benchmark can be predicted before any training takes place, and shows through controlled experiments on 14 vision-language datasets that intuitive task similarity is a poor predictor of generalizability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source dataset and a target dataset, each consisting of (image, question, answer) triples, along with the frozen encoders of a base MLLM (InternVL3-2B). It produces a scalar influence score per (source, target) pair, which is then used to rank all 14 candidate source datasets for a given target or to reweight/allocate a fixed 280K-sample training budget. For synthetic-data selection, a simplified per-datapoint variant (dropping the diversity term) scores individual QA pairs from a ~1.4M-pool generated by GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro.

## Method

DATAPROPHET is a training-free metric defined as M(s→t) = (QSim · ASim · ISim · PPL(s) · (Sil+H)) / PPL(t), combining three components: (1) multimodal perplexity, computed as the base MLLM's token-level perplexity on source vs. target answers, capturing difficulty headroom; (2) cross-dataset similarity, measured as expected cosine similarity of ℓ₂-normalized embeddings from the frozen encoders separately for questions, answers, and images; and (3) source question diversity, quantified by the silhouette coefficient plus normalized entropy of K-means clusters (K=10) over averaged field embeddings. The product form ensures the score is down-weighted when any single factor is weak, reflecting that successful transfer requires simultaneous alignment in text, vision, difficulty, and coverage. Ablations show multimodal perplexity is the dominant factor (removing it drops Kendall's τ from 0.863 to 0.491), followed by image similarity and diversity, while the full metric achieves 86.0% Kendall's τ correlation with actual fine-tuning performance rankings.

## Results

DATAPROPHET achieves a Kendall's τ of 86.0% (0.863 for target-side, 0.857 for source-side) between predicted and actual data influence rankings across 14 vision-language datasets. In real-data reweighting (280K budget), DATAPROPHET-guided selection reaches 71.0% average accuracy, improving +3.4% over uniform sampling (67.6%), +1.4% over ICONS (69.6%), and +0.2% over the oracle baseline (70.8%). In synthetic-data selection, the gains are larger: 62.0% average vs. 55.1% uniform (+6.9%) and 60.8% ICONS (+1.2%). Ablations confirm multimodal perplexity is the most critical component (removing it drops τ from 0.860 to 0.487), followed by image similarity (0.625) and diversity (0.659).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is ICONS (Wu et al., 2025), a gradient-based influence-consensus method that requires actual training to score data points. DATAPROPHET, being entirely training-free, outperforms ICONS by +1.4% average in real-data reweighting (71.0 vs. 69.6) and +1.2% in synthetic-data selection (62.0 vs. 60.8), while requiring no gradient computation or proxy training runs. Against the uniform-sampling baseline, DATAPROPHET yields +3.4% (real) and +6.9% (synthetic) average improvements. Notably, DATAPROPHET also slightly exceeds the oracle baseline (which uses ground-truth performance gains to reweight) by +0.2% in the real-data setting, suggesting the metric captures transfer dynamics that even direct performance-based reweighting misses. In the RL post-training setting (Table 4), DATAPROPHET-guided allocation improves average score from 58.3 to 59.5 (real RL) and from 56.4 to 57.7 (synthetic RL) over equal/random baselines, confirming generalizability beyond SFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data influence prediction task from the LLM literature (LESS, DSIR, perplexity-based pruning by Marion et al. and Thrush et al.) to the multimodal setting, where no prior training-free predictor existed. It repurposes the perplexity-as-quality-signal idea from LLM pretraining and augments it with MLLM-specific components (cross-modal image similarity, question/answer embedding alignment, and source diversity via silhouette + entropy). The key departure from the closest MLLM competitor, ICONS (Wu et al., 2025), is that DATAPROPHET requires no gradient computation, proxy training, or online adaptation—making it a purely forward-pass metric applicable before any fine-tuning begins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that intuitive task similarity is a poor predictor of transfer (e.g., OCR data helps spatial reasoning more than chart tasks) challenges a widely held heuristic in MLLM data curation and suggests practitioners should not rely on surface-level task matching when constructing training mixtures. The training-free nature of DATAPROPHET makes it broadly accessible, but the evaluation is limited to a single 2B model (InternVL3-2B) and 14 datasets across 7 task families, leaving open whether the metric's 86% Kendall's τ correlation and its slight edge over oracle selection generalize to larger models, more diverse task taxonomies, or pretraining-scale data. The modest 1.2–1.4% gains over the training-based ICONS baseline, while directionally positive, raise the question of whether the simplicity advantage justifies the small performance trade-off in high-stakes settings.
