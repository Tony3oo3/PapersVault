## Problem

Flow matching achieves high-fidelity density estimation but fails to expose interpretable latent structure, while VAEs learn structured representations yet underperform in generation quality. The paper asks whether one can retain the structured probabilistic encoders and decoders of VAEs while achieving the fidelity and scalability of flow matching, without the parametric likelihood constraints that limit SVAEs or the two-stage training that characterizes latent flow matching methods.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observed data x (28×28 MNIST images, 5000-dim gene expression vectors, or video frames from pendulum trajectories) along with a noise sample x_0 from a base Gaussian, and optionally a latent variable z for conditional generation. It produces generated samples via the learned conditional CNF likelihood p_1(x|z), posterior samples z|x for representation, and—under mixture or dynamical variants—class assignment probabilities or full latent trajectories. A key structural detail is that the posterior q_t(z|x_t) is indexed by the flow time t, so the model learns a time-varying posterior path rather than a single static encoder, and the marginal latent prior is derived post-hoc by integrating over the observation.

## Method

SFA augments probabilistic graphical models with conditional continuous normalizing flow (CNF) likelihoods, where the key theoretical insight (Theorem 3.1) shows that the marginal vector field v_t(x) equals the posterior expectation E_{p_t(z|x)}[v_t(x|z)], linking conditional flows to the marginal density path. Training uses the Structured Conditional Flow Matching (SCFM) objective, which regresses the posterior-averaged conditional vector field E_{q_t(z_t|x_t)}[v_t(x_t|z_t;θ)] onto a preselected reference field u_t(x_t|x_1), jointly learning both the CNF likelihood and the approximate posterior in a single objective without requiring likelihood evaluation at any training step. The posterior family Q is flexible: a time-indexed Gaussian (via reparameterization) for computational efficiency, or a full conditional CNF for maximal expressivity. The framework generalizes to continuous, finite mixture (with Gumbel-Softmax class variables), and Markov dynamical latent structures, with the SCFM objective adapting its inner expectation to integrate out the appropriate latent variables in each case.

## Results

On MNIST, SFA achieves the best balance of latent diversity (Vendi=25.6) and out-of-distribution clustering quality (NMI=0.490, ARI=0.356), outperforming VAE (NMI=0.039) and Latent FM (NMI=0.488, ARI=0.381) while maintaining competitive SSIM (0.716). On the Pinwheel density estimation task, SFA matches flow matching in marginal density quality (W₁=0.024 vs FM's 0.025) while additionally recovering interpretable latent structure. On the 5,000-dim single-cell RNA-seq dataset, SFA achieves NMI=0.633 and ARI=0.460 with a Vendi score of 737.7 (vs Latent FM's 5.8), and on the pendulum trajectory task, LDS-SFA reduces latent RMSE by over 5× compared to GLD-SVAE (1.526 vs 8.090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VAE and VampVAE, SFA dominates on all clustering metrics: NMI(OOD) of 0.490 vs 0.039 (VAE) and 0.006 (VampVAE), with ARI(OOD) of 0.356 vs 0.017 and 0.000 respectively, while also achieving far better SSIM (0.716 vs 0.419/0.866). Against Latent FM, SFA achieves comparable OOD clustering (NMI 0.490 vs 0.488) but substantially higher sample diversity (Vendi 25.6 vs 8.4), at the cost of lower SSIM (0.716 vs 0.980); Latent FM's deterministic encoder sacrifices latent diversity for pixel-level reconstruction. Against Latent FM with VAE, SFA wins on both NMI(OOD) (0.490 vs 0.309) and ARI(OOD) (0.356 vs 0.205). In the mixture setting (Table 3), Mixture-SFA outperforms Mixture-SVAE on NMI (0.489 vs 0.161), ARI (0.332 vs 0.072), and SSIM (0.779 vs 0.634). On the pendulum trajectory task, LDS-SFA reduces latent RMSE by over 5× (1.526 vs 8.090) and observed RMSE by ~30% (3.233 vs 4.574) compared to GLD-SVAE, demonstrating superior recovery of the underlying dynamical system.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022) and structured variational autoencoders (Johnson et al., 2016; Lin et al., 2018), bridging a gap that prior work has addressed only partially. Latent flow matching methods (Dao et al., 2023; Mittal et al., 2023) combine neural density estimators with encoder-decoder architectures but constrain the likelihood to simple parametric families, while SVAEs capture graphical model structure but are limited by parametric assumptions and require expensive likelihood evaluation when extended to CNFs. The key departure is the SCFM objective, which uses the identity that the marginal vector field equals the posterior expectation of conditional vector fields (Theorem 3.1) to jointly learn conditional CNF likelihoods and approximate posteriors without ever computing the log-likelihood, avoiding the numerical instability and computational cost that plague direct CNF-in-VAE extensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SFA provides a principled, unified recipe for augmenting any graphical model with flow-matching-based likelihoods, offering a path toward generative models that simultaneously achieve high-fidelity sampling and interpretable structured representations—something neither pure flow matching nor VAEs deliver alone. The framework's generality across continuous, mixture, and dynamical latents is a genuine methodological contribution. However, the authors acknowledge that scaling to complex image datasets requires solving an architectural challenge (designing decoders that meaningfully condition on a stochastic latent rather than bypassing it), and the CNF posterior variant remains computationally expensive due to ODE solves at each gradient step. Open questions include principled selection of posterior family complexity and extending SFA toward identifiable, decomposable latents for intervention and out-of-domain generation.
