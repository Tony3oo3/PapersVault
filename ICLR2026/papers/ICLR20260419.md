## Problem

Most model-free offline RL algorithms fragment offline trajectories into single-step transitions, discarding the multi-step temporal structure that is readily available in the data. Meanwhile, conservative value estimation methods like CQL suffer from over-pessimism that requires additional components (behavior policy estimation, auxiliary networks) to mitigate, and no prior work has theoretically or empirically demonstrated that multi-step operators can be effectively adapted to the offline setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes partial trajectories of fixed length n (set to 5 in experiments) sampled from the offline dataset, rather than individual (s, a, r, s') transitions. At inference, the learned policy maps a state to an action distribution. The Q-function represents the value of a mixture policy λπ_β + (1−λ)π, where λ interpolates between the empirical behavior policy and the learned target policy, rather than the value of the learned policy alone. The algorithm uses twin SAC-based critics with an entropy-regularized stochastic actor.

## Method

CPQL replaces the standard Bellman operator in CQL's conservative loss with the Peng's Q(λ) operator, which recursively blends n-step returns using a trace parameter λ over partial trajectories. The key insight is that the fixed point of the PQL operator under a fixed behavior policy converges to the Q-function of a mixture policy λπ_β + (1−λ)π, which lies closer to the behavior policy's value function and thereby induces implicit behavior regularization. This means even a small conservatism parameter α suffices to prevent OOD overestimation, avoiding the over-pessimism that plagues CQL. The algorithm uses a log-sum-exp conservative penalty (inherited from CQL) and SAC-based actor-critic updates with twin critics, setting the entropy bonus only at the final step of the trajectory to maintain numerical stability. Theoretically, CPQL guarantees a lower bound on the true state value (Theorem 1), policy improvement over the behavior policy (Theorem 2), and a reduced sub-optimality gap relative to CQL (Theorem 3) without requiring behavior policy estimation or auxiliary networks.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark (1M gradient steps, 5 seeds), CPQL achieves the highest total scores on MuJoCo (1252.1 vs. 1193.7 for EPQ and 1188.4 for MCQ) and AntMaze (397.6 vs. 378.0 for IQL), and is competitive on Adroit (166.7 vs. 168.5 for CSVE). CPQL attains the best score in 22 out of 29 individual tasks, with particularly large margins on random/medium-replay MuJoCo datasets (e.g., 38.8 on halfcheetah-random vs. 31.9 for EPQ) and AntMaze navigation (e.g., 46.6 on antmaze-large-diverse vs. 47.5 for IQL). In the offline-to-online setting, CPQL→PQL avoids the initial performance drop seen with CQL→SAC and outperforms AWAC, Cal-QL, IQL, and SPOT after only 0.25M offline + 0.3M online steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPQL outperforms all single-step baselines on MuJoCo (1252.1 total) and AntMaze (397.6 total), with the largest margins over CQL (+241.9 MuJoCo, +94.0 AntMaze) and TD3+BC (+238.9 MuJoCo, +233.8 AntMaze). On Adroit, CPQL (166.7) is narrowly behind CSVE (168.5) but ahead of MISA (162.7), MCQ (124.3), EPQ (128.7), IQL (118.1), TD3+BC (115.7), and CQL (93.6). Compared to the closest multi-step-adjacent competitor EPQ, CPQL gains +58.4 on MuJoCo and +71.4 on AntMaze. In the offline-to-online setting (Figure 3), CPQL→PQL surpasses AWAC, Cal-QL, IQL, SPOT, and CQL→SAC, avoiding the initial performance drop that CQL→SAC exhibits (especially at larger α) while improving faster than Cal-QL. CPQL also demonstrates markedly lower sensitivity to the conservatism parameter α than CQL (Figure 1), maintaining competitive scores across a wide α range where CQL's performance degrades sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPQL builds directly on CQL's conservative Q-learning framework (Kumar et al., 2020) and the Peng's Q(λ) multi-step TD operator (Peng & Williams, 1994; Kozuno et al., 2021), which had previously been studied only in online RL. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that under a fixed empirical behavior policy (the offline setting), the PQL fixed point naturally interpolates between the behavior and target policies, providing implicit behavior regularization without requiring explicit behavior policy estimation or auxiliary networks. This positions CPQL as a simpler alternative to subsequent CQL variants (MCQ, MISA, CSVE, EPQ) that address over-pessimism through additional components like quantile networks, state-value functions, or estimated behavior policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPQL is the first model-free offline RL algorithm to combine multi-step TD learning with conservative value estimation, and its theoretical guarantees (performance ≥ behavior policy, reduced sub-optimality gap) address a core limitation of prior conservative methods that could not simultaneously avoid over-pessimism and ensure policy improvement. The practical robustness to the conservatism parameter α (unlike CQL's known sensitivity) and the offline-to-online transfer benefit (pre-trained Q-function prevents performance drop during online fine-tuning) make it a potentially widely adoptable drop-in replacement for CQL-based pipelines. Limitations include additional computational overhead from multi-step backups and potential degradation on low-quality datasets where single-step updates may be preferable; open questions include whether the approach extends to model-based offline RL or to settings with non-stationary behavior policies.
