## Problem

Existing tabular data synthesis methods address fairness, differential privacy, and class balance in isolation, and most rely on GANs (prone to mode collapse) or VAEs (missing fine-grained patterns) without formal privacy guarantees. No prior framework jointly enforces statistical parity fairness for non-binary protected attributes, per-attribute differential privacy, and balanced class representation within a single generative model for tabular data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260686/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a tabular dataset with mixed continuous and categorical columns, first resampled from a Dirichlet process posterior (with adaptive truncation), then preprocessed via quantile transformation for continuous features and one-hot encoding for categorical features. The protected attribute S is never generated; instead, a randomized sensitive ratio vector S_rsr specifying desired group proportions is fed as a conditioning input to the generator. Output is synthetic tabular data in the original schema, recovered by inverse quantile transformation for continuous columns and argmax decoding for categorical columns.

## Method

The CBNP-VAECGAN architecture combines a VAE encoder-decoder, a Code-GAN (using MMD as discriminator to explore underrepresented latent regions), and a standard GAN, all conditioned on the protected attribute. Privacy is enforced at two levels: globally, the Dirichlet process resampling weights constitute an (ε,δ)-differentially private mechanism (Proposition 1), and locally, a copula-based base measure applies the Analytic Gaussian Mechanism to continuous marginals and the Randomized Response Mechanism to categorical marginals, preserving inter-feature dependencies via an estimated correlation matrix. Fairness is regularized through DirPMINE, a Dirichlet-process-weighted Donsker-Varadhan lower bound on the mutual information between generated outcomes and protected attributes, which extends naturally to non-binary attributes. Class balancing is achieved by minimizing a semi-BNP DirP-based KL divergence between generated category proportions and a uniform target. The composite loss (Eq. 9) jointly optimizes utility (MMD posterior bootstrap), fairness (λ_F · L_Fair), and balance (Σ λ_B · L_Balance) terms under adversarial training.

## Results

On the COMPAS dataset (8 ethnic groups, 3 risk levels), the proposed method reduces MI between the outcome and protected attribute from 0.0263 (no fairness) to 1e-5 (with fairness), while maintaining MMD ≤ 0.0024 and F1(DTC) of 0.903–0.917 versus a true-data F1 of 0.913. On the Adult dataset, the method outperforms FairGAN, DECAF, and TabFairGAN in both faithfulness (MMD) and fairness (SP and MI) under a (29.35, 0.01)-global and (2.5, 0.05)-local privacy budget. Class balancing on additional categorical attributes (Sex, Marital, Language) preserves utility with only a marginal MMD increase (0.0018 → 0.0024) while keeping MI at 1e-5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Adult dataset (binary sex attribute), the proposed CBNP-VAECGAN outperforms all three fairness-aware baselines—FairGAN, DECAF, and TabFairGAN—in both faithfulness (lower MMD) and fairness (lower SP and MI), with the largest gap observed against the state-of-the-art DECAF. A key structural advantage is that the method handles non-binary protected attributes natively via the DirPMINE fairness regularizer, whereas FairGAN, DECAF, and TabFairGAN are limited to binary sensitive attributes and cannot be applied to the COMPAS dataset (8 ethnic groups). Under the imposed (29.35, 0.01)-global and (2.5, 0.05)-local privacy budgets, the proposed method retains competitive utility while the baselines offer no formal privacy guarantee. The paper notes that the comparison is restricted to methods that incorporate statistical-parity fairness within VAE/GAN/hybrid architectures, positioning the contribution as a next-step in that specific line of research rather than a broad generative-model benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads—Bayesian nonparametric learning with Dirichlet Process resampling (Fong et al., 2019; Fazeli-Asl & Zhang, 2023), differential privacy mechanisms (the Dirichlet mechanism of Gohari et al., 2021; the Analytic Gaussian Mechanism of Balle & Wang, 2018; the Randomized Response Mechanism of Wang et al., 2016), and fairness-aware generative models (FairGAN, DECAF, TabFairGAN)—into a single conditional BNPL framework for tabular data. The key conceptual departure is using the DirP posterior resampling weights themselves as a global DP mechanism and constructing a copula-based base measure that applies attribute-specific local privacy (AGM for continuous, RRM for categorical) while preserving inter-feature dependencies. The fairness regularizer (DirPMINE) and class-balancing term (DirP-based KL estimator) are drawn from the authors' own prior work on BNP mutual information estimation, extending those tools from hypothesis testing to generative model training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee that a Dirichlet Process resampling scheme satisfies (ε,δ)-DP, and shows that the concentration parameter 'a' acts as a tunable global privacy knob—a clean result with practical implications for BNP-based generative modeling. Its ability to handle non-binary protected attributes (e.g., 8 ethnic groups in COMPAS) where standard fairness baselines are inapplicable addresses a genuine gap in the literature. However, the framework's complexity (copula base measure, DirPMINE, DirP-KL balancing, VAE-CGAN architecture) may limit adoption, and empirical validation is confined to two well-studied but acknowledged-problematic datasets (Adult, COMPAS) with only three fairness-aware GAN baselines. Open questions include whether the privacy-fairness tension can be resolved more elegantly at scale, whether the approach transfers to diffusion-based or LLM-based generators as the authors speculate, and whether the theoretical privacy budgets translate to meaningful protection against practical attacks like membership inference.
