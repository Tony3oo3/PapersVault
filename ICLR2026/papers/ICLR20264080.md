## Problem

Off-policy safe RL methods suffer from two coupled failures: cost-agnostic exploration drives the agent into unsafe regions with uncontrolled data-collection cost, and underestimation bias in cumulative cost value learning produces policies that violate the safety constraint at deployment. Existing off-policy approaches lack a principled mechanism to simultaneously constrain exploration within the cost budget and reliably estimate cost values, leaving a gap between the sample efficiency of off-policy methods and the constraint satisfaction guaranteed by on-policy methods.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a continuous state vector s_t (e.g., joint positions/velocities for locomotion, LiDAR point clouds for navigation, or vehicle kinematics plus surrounding traffic for driving) along with a scalar cost limit d that bounds the expected cumulative cost. It outputs a continuous control action a_t sampled from a shifted Gaussian policy N(μ_E, Σ_T), where the mean shift μ_Δ = η* Σ_T g* is computed online from the quantile critics' gradients and the cost constraint. The exploration policy's covariance is held equal to the target policy's, so only the mean is displaced, and the action is clipped to environment-specific bounds before execution.

## Method

COX-Q integrates two components on a SAC backbone with Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM) for the actor update. First, the Cost-Constrained Optimistic eXploration (COX) strategy extends single-objective OAC to the multi-objective CMDP setting: it resolves reward–cost gradient conflicts in the action space via Policy-MGDA (a projection of the Lagrangian gradient g_r − λg_c onto the cone where both reward and cost improve, measured under the policy covariance Σ_T), then bounds the exploration step length η* by solving a bi-level optimization that keeps the expected cost increment below the remaining budget (d − Q̂_c^mean), with an additional adaptive trust-region adjustment driven by the mean cost in a recent replay buffer. Second, Truncated Quantile Critics (TQC) maintain N reward and N cost critics each predicting M quantiles; mixed-and-sorted quantiles across critics are truncated (top k_r for reward, bottom k_c for cost) to flexibly control over/underestimation bias, while per-quantile ensemble disagreement is aggregated via CVaR to yield an optimistic upper bound Q̂_r^UB for exploration and a conservative lower bound Q̂_c^LB for cost-aware gradient computation.

## Results

On the SMARTS autonomous driving benchmark (512K steps, 2000 stochastic test episodes), COX-Q achieves the fewest collisions in Intersection (12) and T-junction (21) scenarios, zero rule violations across all scenarios, and near-zero timeouts (0, 0, 5), while TQC-ORAC suffers 887 timeouts in Overtaking and 86 in T-junction. During data collection, COX-Q reduces unsafe events by 48–77% compared to on-policy baselines (e.g., 1123 vs. 4969 in Intersection) and by 54–59% compared to TQC-ORAC. On Safe Velocity, COX-Q achieves near-zero test cost within fewer environment steps than all on-policy baselines while keeping training cost at or below the budget of 25. On Safe Navigation, COX-Q's cost estimation bias converges to 0, whereas all baselines remain either over-conservative or unstable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against on-policy baselines (CPPOPID, CUP, RCPO, PPOSimmerPID), COX-Q achieves substantially higher sample efficiency on Safe Velocity, reaching near-zero test cost in fewer environment steps while maintaining training cost at or below the budget. On SMARTS, COX-Q reduces collisions by 93–94% versus CPPOPID (12 vs. 183 in Intersection; 21 vs. 195 in T-junction) and eliminates all rule violations. Against the closest off-policy competitor TQC-ORAC, COX-Q reduces collisions by 33% (Intersection) and 25% (T-junction), and dramatically cuts timeouts from 887 to 0 (Overtaking) and 86 to 5 (T-junction), indicating that Policy-MGDA's gradient-conflict resolution prevents the over-conservative behaviour that plagues ORAC. Compared to CAL, COX-Q halves collisions in both Intersection (12 vs. 23) and T-junction (21 vs. 36) while matching its zero rule-violation performance. On Safe Navigation, COX-Q achieves on-par or higher returns than all baselines, with the advantage most pronounced in CarButton1, CarButton2, and PointPush1; its cost estimation bias converges to 0 while baselines remain over-conservative or unstable, demonstrating the benefit of mixed quantile critics over point-value or single-quantile approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

COX-Q extends the single-objective Optimistic Actor-Critic (OAC) exploration framework to multi-objective safe RL by introducing Policy-MGDA, which resolves gradient conflicts between reward and cost in the action space (as opposed to parameter-space gradient manipulation in prior safe RL work). The value learning component builds on Truncated Quantile Critics (TQC) for distributional estimation and CVaR-based conservative cost bounds, while the actor update retains the Augmented Lagrangian Method from CAL. The paper positions itself against the dominant on-policy safe RL paradigm (CPO, CUP, PPOSimmer) and recent off-policy attempts (CAL, ORAC, WCSAC), identifying the lack of principled cost-constrained exploration as the key unresolved gap in off-policy safe RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COX-Q addresses a practically critical need: off-policy safe RL that simultaneously achieves high sample efficiency and enforces cost constraints during both data collection and deployment, which is essential for real-world safety-critical applications like autonomous driving where data collection is expensive and risky. A notable finding from ablations is that in sparse-cost environments (Safe Navigation), the dominant bottleneck is cost-critic underestimation bias rather than the exploration mechanism itself, suggesting that for such tasks, improving cost value learning (e.g., via HER or prioritized replay) may be more impactful than refining exploration. Open questions include the reliability of epistemic uncertainty from TQC ensembles for OOD states, and how to effectively apply COX exploration when cost signals are extremely sparse.
