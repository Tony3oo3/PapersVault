## Problem

DINOv2 is a widely used self-supervised vision foundation model whose downstream capabilities (classification, segmentation, depth) are well established, yet the internal structure of its representations—what features it encodes, how they are geometrically organized, and how different tasks recruit them—remains largely unknown. The paper addresses this by operationalizing the Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH) via sparse autoencoders to extract a large concept dictionary, then systematically testing whether the observed geometry conforms to LRH's near-orthogonal, sparse-coding predictions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes DINOv2-B token activations (261 tokens × 768 dimensions) extracted from 1.4M augmented ImageNet-1K images, producing a 32,000-atom concept dictionary with k=8 active non-negative codes per token (R² > 88% reconstruction). Downstream analysis expresses linear probes for classification (ImageNet-1k), segmentation (ADE20K), and depth (NYU Depth) in the concept basis to yield per-concept importance scores, while geometric diagnostics (coherence, singular-value spectra, Hoyer scores, antipodal-pair detection) characterize the dictionary's structure. Per-image PCA maps of patch embeddings provide a model-native view of token geometry independent of the SAE factorization.

## Method

The core tool is a stable sparse autoencoder (Archetypal SAE) that factorizes activations A ≈ ZD with non-negative, k-sparse codes Z and a dictionary D = SC where S is row-stochastic and C consists of 128k k-means centroids approximating the convex hull of real activations, guaranteeing in-distribution, reproducible atoms. The paper then performs three analyses: (i) expressing task-specific linear probes in the concept basis to reveal functional specialization (Elsewhere/negation concepts for classification, border detectors for segmentation, three monocular-cue families for depth); (ii) measuring concept geometry against random and Grassmannian baselines, finding higher coherence, sharply decaying spectra, and antipodal pairs that depart from LRH's quasi-orthogonal ideal; and (iii) examining per-image token clouds via PCA, showing smooth low-dimensional structure that positional information alone cannot explain. Motivated by these departures, the paper proposes the Minkowski Representation Hypothesis (MRH): token embeddings lie in Minkowski sums of convex polytopes spanned by archetypal landmarks, and proves that multi-head attention constructively realizes this geometry (each head outputs a convex combination of its values; heads sum to a Minkowski sum), with preliminary empirical support from piecewise-linear geodesics, archetypal analysis matching SAE reconstruction, and block structure in code Gram matrices.

## Results

The paper extracts a 32,000-concept dictionary from DINOv2-B (d=768, t=261 tokens) using a stable SAE with k=8 active codes per token, achieving R² > 88% reconstruction fidelity over 1.4M ImageNet-1K images. Task analysis reveals clear functional specialization: classification recruits a broad set of concepts including 'Elsewhere' concepts implementing learned negation; segmentation concentrates on border detectors forming a tight low-dimensional subspace; depth estimation draws on three monocular cue families (projective geometry, shadow-based, frequency transitions). Geometric diagnostics show the dictionary departs from the LRH ideal: pairwise coherence is higher than both random and Grassmannian baselines (maximal Grassmannian coherence = 0.0659), the singular-value spectrum decays sharply (anisotropic capacity), and co-activation correlates only weakly with geometric affinity (r=0.28, R²=0.08). Archetypal Analysis with ~10 archetypes per image matches or exceeds SAE reconstruction, supporting the proposed Minkowski Representation Hypothesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against competing interpretability methods on a standard task; instead, it compares the learned concept dictionary against geometric and statistical baselines to test the Linear Representation Hypothesis. Relative to random vectors on the sphere and a numerically computed Grassmannian frame (solved via TAAP on an A100 in ~6 hours), the DINOv2 dictionary exhibits heavier-tailed inner products and higher coherence, indicating structured redundancy rather than near-orthogonal feature packing. Task-specific concept subsets (top-100 per task) show significantly higher intra-task cosine similarity and much faster singular-value spectral decay than random 100-concept subsets, confirming that each task activates a low-dimensional functional subspace. The co-activation Gram matrix Z^T Z has a smoothly decaying spectrum with no modular gaps, and its correlation with the geometric affinity DD^T is weak (r=0.28), showing that usage patterns only partially shape concept geometry. For the Minkowski hypothesis, piecewise-linear k-NN geodesics remain near the data support while straight-line interpolation does not, and Archetypal Analysis (|S|=1) with ~10 archetypes per image matches or exceeds full SAE reconstruction, providing preliminary evidence for convex-region-based token composition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH) from Elhage et al. (2022) in the vision domain by training a stable sparse autoencoder (building on Fel et al., ICML 2025, and the SAE framework of Cunningham et al., 2023) on DINOv2, yielding the largest concept dictionary for a vision foundation model to date. It then departs from the LRH's near-orthogonal, sparse-coding picture by documenting systematic geometric departures (higher coherence than Grassmannian baselines, anisotropic spectra, task-aligned low-dimensional subspaces) and proposes the Minkowski Representation Hypothesis (MRH) as an alternative, arguing that multi-head attention's convex-combination structure naturally realizes Minkowski sums of archetypal polytopes. The work connects to Gärdenfors' conceptual spaces and recent evidence for convex concept structure in language models (Park et al., 2025), but is the first to formalize this geometry for vision transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 32k-concept dictionary and its task-specific analysis (Elsewhere concepts for classification, border detectors for segmentation, monocular cue families for depth) provide a concrete, interactive resource for the DINOv2 community and reveal that attribution methods assuming concepts fire where their information resides can be systematically misleading. The MRH, if validated, would reframe how we design steering, probing, and decomposition tools—shifting from unbounded linear directions to bounded convex regions with saturation—though the current evidence is preliminary and confined to a single architecture (DINOv2-B). Open questions include whether MRH holds across architectures and scales, whether the non-identifiability of Minkowski decompositions can be overcome with intermediate-layer signals, and whether the observed geometric departures from LRH are artifacts of the SAE training procedure or intrinsic to the representation.
