## Problem

Evaluating whether LLM responses sound indistinguishable from native speakers across dozens of languages remains unsolved: prior benchmarks rely on translated prompts, measure only narrow axes like naturalness, or report low inter-annotator agreement. No existing framework operationalizes the full sociolinguistic notion of 'native-like' quality—encompassing fluency, tone, localized cultural alignment, and local factuality—at massively multilingual scale with high reliability, nor does it provide a path to train LLM judges that match human annotators.

## Input / Output

The judge receives a localized text prompt (with placeholders like [locale_country], [locale_holiday] instantiated by native speakers), one or two LLM-generated responses in the target language variety, and a human-written 5-point grading rubric specific to the evaluation dimension. In the pairwise setup (which the paper shows is substantially more reliable), both responses are presented simultaneously in randomized order and the model must assign a 1–5 Likert grade to each, from which a win/loss/tie preference is inferred. The same graded output doubles as a scalar reward signal when the judge is deployed as a generative reward model for policy post-training.

## Method

MENLO grounds its evaluation in Bell's (1984) audience-design theory: parametric English prompt templates with locale placeholders are translated and localized by native speakers into 47 language varieties, evoking specific addressee or reference-group contexts that elicit culturally appropriate 'native' styles. The resulting dataset (6,423 pairs, 81,014 annotations, Krippendorff's α = 0.84) is used to benchmark and train LLM judges. The key empirical finding is that zero-shot pairwise grading—scoring two responses side-by-side—outperforms pointwise and few-shot pointwise by up to +12.4% Macro-F1 and +18% Preference accuracy, and that detailed rubrics add further gains especially in pointwise mode. For fine-tuning, the authors apply GRPO reinforcement learning with a composite reward combining exact-match binary reward, ±1-grade smoothing, a preference-direction bonus, and format penalties; multi-task RL across all four dimensions with shaped rewards pushes Llama4-Scout to human-annotator-level agreement across all 47 varieties. Finally, the RL-trained pairwise judge is repurposed as a generative reward model: it scores rollout pairs from a policy model (Qwen3-4B) during GRPO post-training, yielding measurable quality gains confirmed by both LLM judges and human raters, though LLM judges systematically overestimate the magnitude of improvement by ~0.6 points relative to humans.

## Results

On the MENLO test set (1,766 pairs, 47 languages), zero-shot pairwise evaluation outperforms zero-shot pointwise by up to +12.4% Macro-F1 and +18.0% Preference accuracy across all 8 models tested. After training, RL-trained Llama4-Scout achieves the best overall performance at 45.82 Macro-F1 and 61.10 Preference accuracy (SFT+RL), surpassing the strongest frontier API model (gpt-4.1 at 38.53/59.23 zero-shot pairwise). When used as a generative reward model to post-train Qwen3-4B, human raters confirm a +10.9% average score improvement (3.31→3.67), while LLM judges report larger gains of +25–36%, revealing a systematic overestimation of ~0.5 points by automated evaluators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three evaluation paradigms (zero-shot pointwise, few-shot pointwise, zero-shot pairwise) across 8 models (Qwen3-4B/32B, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.3-70B, Llama4-Scout, o3, gpt-4o, gpt-4.1). Zero-shot pairwise consistently wins over both pointwise variants by an average of +10.4% Macro-F1 and +15.1% Preference, with gpt-4.1 achieving the highest zero-shot pairwise score (38.53 Macro-F1, 59.23 Preference). For trained judges, RL outperforms SFT for both model families: for the reasoning-oriented Qwen3-4B, SFT actually degrades performance (−2.0% Macro-F1) while RL improves it (+4.0%); for the non-reasoning Llama4-Scout, both SFT (+8.1%) and RL (+9.5%) help, with RL yielding the best Preference accuracy. The best RL-trained Llama4-Scout (45.82 Macro-F1) surpasses all zero-shot frontier models by 7+ points. In the reward-modeling experiment, LLM judges (Qwen3-32B, gpt-4.1, Llama4-Scout-RL-Judge) all confirm post-training gains of +25–36% average score, but human raters on 10 languages report a more modest +10.9%, indicating LLM judges overestimate improvements by ~0.5 points on the 1–5 scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

MENLO draws on Bell's (1984) audience-design theory from sociolinguistics to operationalize native-like quality, departing from prior multilingual benchmarks (MM-Eval, M-RewardBench, PARIKSHA, RECON) that relied on translated prompts or narrower dimensions like naturalness and hallucination. Methodologically, it builds on the RL-trained judge paradigm introduced by J1 (Whitehouse et al., 2026) but is the first to apply it in a massively multilingual setting with multi-task training and composite reward shaping. The paper also extends the LLM-as-a-judge literature by demonstrating that judges can double as generative reward models for post-training, unifying evaluation and optimization in a single pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MENLO provides the first large-scale, high-agreement (Krippendorff's α = 0.84) dataset targeting native-like conversational quality across 47 language varieties with localized, audience-design-inspired prompts, filling a gap left by prior work that used translated or non-localized content. The finding that LLM judges systematically overestimate quality improvements by ~0.6 points relative to human raters is a practically important caution for anyone using automated evaluation to drive RL post-training. Open questions include whether the overestimation gap can be closed, how to address the persistent weakness on Localized Factuality (which may require retrieval or tool use), and whether pairwise advantages generalize to listwise evaluation of more than two candidates.
