## Problem

Existing monocular geometry models (depth, point map, surface normal) are trained at low resolution (≤518×518) and degrade sharply on multi-megapixel inputs, while ViT-based high-resolution approaches incur quadratic computational cost. Compounding this, high-quality high-resolution supervision is scarce: real labels are noisy or sparse, and synthetic labels introduce domain gaps that hurt generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264544/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at arbitrary resolution (tested up to 4004×4004) and produces dense predictions at the native input resolution: relative depth maps, metric-scale 3D point maps, and surface normal maps. Internally, the ViT branch consumes a uniformly downsampled version (capped at 518×518) while the CNN branch processes the full-resolution image, so the model handles multi-megapixel inputs without quadratic scaling. The architecture is integrated as a drop-in encoder replacement into DepthAnything-v2 (depth) and MoGe2 (normals and point maps).

## Method

Hyden couples a fixed-resolution Vision Transformer (for global context) with a full-resolution ResNet-like CNN (for fine detail), fusing the two streams by upsampling ViT features to CNN resolution, concatenating, and applying a lightweight two-layer CNN. Because ViT cost is resolution-independent while CNN scales linearly, inference on multi-megapixel images remains fast. For training without high-resolution ground truth, a self-distillation pipeline uses a frozen teacher to generate pseudo-labels at both global (518×518 full image) and local (518×518 random crops) resolutions; the student is trained with a combined global loss (preserving geometric accuracy) and local crop loss (capturing sharp boundaries), optimizing only the CNN branch, fusion layer, and decoder while keeping the ViT frozen. This yields 3× lower latency at 2K and ~10× lower at 4K compared to the original ViT-only baselines while improving accuracy and boundary sharpness.

## Results

On 2K-resolution zero-shot relative depth estimation across 9 datasets, Hyden-MoGe2 achieves the best average rank (1.3) with the lowest relative errors (e.g., 3.7% on NYUv2, 3.8% on ETH3D, 2.0% on iBims-1) and highest δ₁ scores (98.5%, 98.3%, 95.8%), while running at 171.6 ms—roughly 3× faster than MoGe2 (476.8 ms) and 2× faster than DepthPro (341.3 ms). Hyden-DA2 similarly outperforms its base model DA2 on most datasets (e.g., 4.6% vs 5.4% Rel on NYUv2) at 100.7 ms vs 408.1 ms (~4× speedup). For surface normals, Hyden-MoGe2 achieves the lowest mean angular error on all four benchmarks (14.6° NYUv2, 14.8° iBims-1, 13.0° ScanNet, 27.0° vkitti) at 127.4 ms, beating MoGe2 (438.2 ms) and Metric3Dv2 (606.7 ms). At 4K resolution, Hyden models maintain accuracy while running ~10× faster than ViT-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its base model DA2, Hyden-DA2 improves relative error on 6 of 7 reported datasets (e.g., 4.6% vs 5.4% on NYUv2, 5.1% vs 5.5% on ETH3D) while reducing latency from 408.1 ms to 100.7 ms (~4×). Against MoGe2, Hyden-MoGe2 achieves lower relative error on all 7 datasets (e.g., 3.7% vs 3.9% NYUv2, 2.0% vs 2.1% iBims-1) and runs 2.8× faster (171.6 vs 476.8 ms). Against DepthPro, Hyden-MoGe2 wins on accuracy across all datasets and is ~2× faster; DepthPro is constrained to 1536×1536 input. Against FlashDepth, both Hyden variants substantially outperform it on accuracy (e.g., Hyden-MoGe2 3.7% vs FlashDepth 8.8% Rel on NYUv2) despite FlashDepth's lower latency (98.9 ms), with FlashDepth's lightweight decoder and limited supervision causing significant quality drops. Against Metric3Dv2, Hyden-MoGe2 achieves lower relative error on all datasets and runs 3.5× faster (171.6 vs 606.7 ms). For surface normals (Table 2), Hyden-MoGe2 beats all baselines including DSINE (14.6° vs 17.1° on NYUv2), Metric3Dv2 (14.8° vs 15.4° on iBims-1), and MoGe2 (14.6° vs 15.6° on NYUv2) while being the fastest at 127.4 ms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on two recent ViT-based geometry foundation models—DepthAnything-v2 and MoGe2—by replacing their monolithic ViT encoders with a hybrid dual-path design that pairs a fixed-resolution ViT (for global context) with a full-resolution ResNet-style CNN (for local detail). It departs from prior high-resolution approaches like DepthPro and FlashDepth, which either tile the image and blend ViT features or use multi-branch ViTs, both of which inherit quadratic ViT scaling. The self-distillation framework, which combines global pseudo-labels from downsampled full images with local pseudo-labels from high-resolution crops, is a novel training recipe not present in the cited baselines, addressing the well-known scarcity of clean high-resolution supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 3× speedup at 2K and ~10× at 4K while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy makes high-resolution monocular geometry feasible in latency-sensitive applications like AR/VR and autonomous driving. The self-distillation pipeline is model-agnostic and could be applied to upgrade other dense-prediction architectures to high resolution without requiring new ground-truth annotations. Open questions include whether the frozen-ViT assumption limits further gains, whether the approach extends cleanly to video or multi-view settings, and whether the 50M-image pseudo-labeling cost is prohibitive for smaller groups.
