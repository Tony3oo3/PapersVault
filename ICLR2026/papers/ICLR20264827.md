## Problem

Federated learning frameworks that protect privacy (e.g., via differential privacy) inherently obscure the sensitive demographic attributes needed to detect and correct bias, worsening performance gaps across groups. Conversely, fairness-aware re-weighting or regularization can inadvertently expose sensitive information. No existing FL method jointly optimizes privacy, group fairness, and utility in a single end-to-end pipeline while remaining robust to adversarial clients and environmental distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

Each client processes 640×640 RGB pedestrian images locally, using sensitive attribute labels (e.g., Monk Skin Tone) only for local loss computation and UFM estimation—these labels never leave the device. The modified YOLOv8 detection head outputs, per detection, a Dirichlet concentration vector (α_c = 1 + softplus(z_c)) for class uncertainty and Normal-Inverse-Gamma parameters for box-coordinate uncertainty, alongside standard bounding boxes and confidence scores. Each client transmits only its parameter update Δθ_i and a single scalar UFM_i to the server, which computes aggregation weights and updates the global model.

## Method

RESFL integrates two complementary mechanisms within a single federated pipeline. First, an adversarial privacy disentanglement module embeds a Gradient Reversal Layer between the feature extractor and an attribute classifier, training the backbone to minimize mutual information I(H; S) between representations and sensitive attributes via a minimax objective. Second, an evidential uncertainty head (Dirichlet for classification, NIG for localization) enables closed-form epistemic variance estimation without Monte Carlo sampling; from per-group mean evidence, a scale-invariant Uncertainty Fairness Metric (UFM) quantifies inter-group confidence disparity. The server aggregates client updates using temperature-scaled exponential weights ω_i ∝ exp(−β·UFM_i), up-weighting clients with lower disparity and higher confidence, while a deterministic mAP floor gates out low-confidence or potentially poisoned clients. The local composite loss L_task + λ_priv·L_adv + λ_fair·L_uncertainty is minimized per client, and the paper provides theoretical guarantees (Theorem B.1, Corollary B.5) showing that minimizing UFM tightens confidence-adjusted group generalization bounds and, via a mixture argument, bounds the global fairness gap.

## Results

On the FACET dataset (4 clients, IID), RESFL achieves 0.6654 mAP, the best equality-of-opportunity gap (ΔEOP = 0.1959), the lowest membership-inference attack success rate (MIA SR = 0.2093), the lowest attribute-inference attack success rate (AIA SR = 0.1832), and the best robustness scores (BA AD = 0.1692, DPA EODD = 0.0674) among all compared methods. Under adverse CARLA weather conditions (fog at 100% intensity), RESFL retains 0.17 mAP with fairness 0.50 and privacy 0.44, while all baselines drop below 0.10 mAP with fairness/privacy scores exceeding 0.50. The ablation confirms the optimal operating point at λ_fair = 0.1 and λ_priv = 0.01, where increasing either coefficient beyond this degrades the utility–fairness–privacy balance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESFL outperforms FedAvg on mAP (0.6654 vs. 0.6378), ΔEOP (0.1959 vs. 0.2362), MIA SR (0.2093 vs. 0.3341), AIA SR (0.1832 vs. 0.4431), BA AD (0.1692 vs. 0.3125), and DPA EODD (0.0674 vs. 0.0792), though FedAvg has a marginally lower |1−DI| (0.2159 vs. 0.2287). Against FairFed, which achieves the highest mAP (0.7013), RESFL trades ~3.6 mAP points for substantially better privacy (MIA 0.2093 vs. 0.4409; AIA 0.1832 vs. 0.5256) and robustness (BA AD 0.1692 vs. 0.4139), while also improving ΔEOP (0.1959 vs. 0.2562). Compared to the privacy-focused baselines FedAvg-DP, PUFFLE, and PFU-FL, RESFL achieves 1.2–1.7× higher mAP while simultaneously delivering the lowest MIA and AIA success rates, showing that adversarial disentanglement preserves utility far better than DP noise injection. Against PrivFairFL (both Pre and Post variants), RESFL wins on mAP (0.6654 vs. 0.6154/0.6119), ΔEOP (0.1959 vs. 0.2659/0.2505), and both privacy metrics, confirming that the uncertainty-guided aggregation and GRL-based privacy module jointly dominate pre- and post-processing fairness constraints. Overall, RESFL is the only method that simultaneously ranks in the top two on every metric column, with clear wins on all four privacy/robustness metrics and the best ΔEOP, at a modest mAP cost relative to FairFed.

## Relation to Prior Work

RESFL sits at the intersection of three well-studied FL threads: privacy preservation (DP-SGD, secure aggregation), group fairness (FairFed, PrivFairFL, PUFFLE, PFU-FL), and evidential uncertainty quantification (Sensoy et al., Amini et al.). It departs from the dominant DP-based privacy route—which the paper argues degrades both utility and fairness by obscuring minority patterns—by substituting an adversarial gradient-reversal layer for representation-level attribute suppression, and from fairness-only reweighting schemes by grounding aggregation weights in a scale-invariant evidential uncertainty metric (UFM) rather than raw performance gaps. The theoretical contribution (Theorem B.1 through Corollary B.5) formally links UFM to confidence-adjusted group generalization bounds, a connection not present in prior FL fairness or privacy analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored tension in FL: privacy mechanisms that hide sensitive attributes simultaneously remove the signal needed for bias correction, and fairness interventions that expose those attributes undermine privacy. RESFL's unified pipeline offers a practical, domain-agnostic recipe (adversarial disentanglement + evidential UFM aggregation) that practitioners can adopt without DP's utility tax. Key limitations temper the impact: no formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantee (only information-theoretic mutual-information bounds), reliance on local access to sensitive labels, evaluation on only 4 clients and a single GPU, and the UFM scalar being vulnerable to falsified reporting in untrusted settings. Open questions include scalability to hundreds of clients, integration with secure aggregation, and whether the UFM-weighted scheme remains effective under severe non-IID or adversarial UFM manipulation.
