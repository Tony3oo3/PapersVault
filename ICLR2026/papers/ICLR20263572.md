## Problem

Existing unsupervised domain adaptive hashing methods rely on fragile heuristics (e.g., softmax confidence thresholding) to filter pseudo-labels and guide domain alignment, which are unreliable under domain shift and lack theoretical guarantees. Moreover, they treat all target samples and all hash bits uniformly, conflating semantic-level judgment uncertainty with bit-level representation stability without targeted strategies, leading to noisy supervision and degraded retrieval quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263572/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled RGB images from a source domain and unlabeled RGB images from a target domain (Office-Home, Office-31, MNIST/USPS) and produces L-bit binary hash codes (16–128 bits) along with an L-dimensional bit-level confidence vector in [0,1]^L. A 20% calibration subset is carved from the source domain using a target-centroid proximity criterion, and the remaining source samples form the training set. During retrieval, the bit confidence is binarized into a mask so that the uncertainty-aware weighted Hamming distance reduces to a standard masked Hamming computation compatible with hardware-accelerated bitwise operations.

## Method

COLA introduces a two-tier conformal calibration framework. At the semantic level, a dynamic-α conformal prediction procedure (with EMA-smoothed α driven by source validation accuracy) constructs per-sample prediction sets whose cardinality yields a semantic confidence weight; this weight modulates both a soft pseudo-label cross-entropy loss and an MMD-based domain alignment loss, so that uncertain samples contribute less to both. At the representation level, a lightweight bit-confidence head is trained via a self-supervised proxy task (Gaussian-noise perturbation → sign-stability labels) to predict per-bit reliability, which then drives a stop-grad-weighted quantization loss during training and an uncertainty-aware weighted Hamming distance (UWHD) during retrieval. A self-regulating closed-loop mechanism aggregates batch-level semantic and bit confidences into adaptive loss weights (λ_target, λ_align, λ_quant), eliminating manual hyperparameter tuning. A theoretical theorem bounds the conformal coverage on the target domain by 1−α minus the total-variation distance between source and target conformity-score distributions, linking domain alignment directly to tightening the uncertainty guarantee.

## Results

On Office-Home and Office-31 (64-bit hash codes), COLA achieves an average mAP of 62.59%, outperforming the strongest baseline COUPLE (60.29%) by 2.3 percentage points and IDEA (57.03%) by 5.6 points. On the Digits benchmark (MNIST↔USPS, 16–128 bits), COLA reaches 70.40% average mAP versus 68.53% for COUPLE and 66.59% for IDEA. The method consistently leads on all 24 individual transfer tasks across both tables, with the largest gains observed on the most challenging Office-Home tasks (e.g., Re→Cl: 41.71% vs. 41.39% for COUPLE) and the Digits tasks at shorter code lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COLA achieves the highest mAP on all 12 transfer tasks in Table 1 (Office-Home + Office-31) and all 12 code-length configurations in Table 2 (MNIST↔USPS), with no losses to any baseline. Against the strongest competitor COUPLE (Luo et al., 2025), COLA gains +2.30% average mAP on Office-Home/Office-31 (62.59 vs. 60.29) and +1.87% on Digits (70.40 vs. 68.53). The margin over the second-best method IDEA (Wang et al., 2023) is substantially larger: +5.56% on Office-Home/Office-31 and +3.81% on Digits. Older domain-adaptive hashing methods such as PEACE (51.22%), DANCE (51.31%), and DAPH (31.85%) trail COLA by 11–31 percentage points, reflecting the cumulative benefit of conformal prediction-based pseudo-labeling, bit-level confidence weighting, and the self-regulating mechanism. The paper further confirms via Table 9 that even when replacing the uncertainty-aware weighted Hamming distance with standard Hamming distance, COLA still outperforms COUPLE (56.23% vs. 54.56%), indicating that the gains are not solely attributable to the retrieval metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

COLA extends the unsupervised domain adaptive hashing (UDAH) lineage (PEACE, DANCE, IDEA, COUPLE) by replacing their heuristic softmax-confidence-based pseudo-label filtering and uniform domain alignment with a principled conformal prediction framework that provides distribution-free coverage guarantees. It builds directly on the conformal prediction theory of Vovk et al. (2005) and the pseudo-labeling paradigm of Lee et al. (2013), but departs from prior uncertainty-in-retrieval work (Warburg et al., 2021; Wang & Zhou, 2023) by avoiding expensive Bayesian sampling in favor of a lightweight, distribution-free calibration procedure. The key conceptual departure is the hierarchical decomposition of uncertainty into semantic-level (prediction set size) and representation-level (bit stability) components, each with targeted loss modifications, rather than treating model uncertainty as a monolithic scalar.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) for conformal coverage under domain shift in the hashing setting, showing graceful degradation bounded by total variation distance, which gives practitioners a principled alternative to fragile threshold-based pseudo-label filtering. The bit-level calibration mechanism—modeling individual hash bit reliability and using it for both weighted quantization and a novel uncertainty-aware Hamming distance—is a practical contribution that could be adopted in other discrete representation learning tasks beyond hashing. Open questions include how the conformal calibration set strategy generalizes to multi-source or streaming domain adaptation, whether the bit-stability proxy task remains informative under severe distribution shifts, and how the method scales to very high-dimensional or multi-modal hash codes where the calibration set construction may become less effective.
