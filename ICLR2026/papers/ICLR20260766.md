## Problem

Source inference attacks (SIAs) allow an honest-but-curious federated learning server to identify which client owns a specific training data point, yet no defense against SIAs in the shuffle model had been studied. The paper demonstrates that standard shuffling at model, layer, or parameter granularity can all be defeated by reconstruction attacks that remap shuffled values back to their original owners using a small shadow dataset, leaving a critical gap in shuffle-based FL privacy guarantees.

## Input / Output

Each client's model parameters (clipped to (-1,1)) are scaled by 10^r to integers, decomposed via RNS into u residues using pairwise coprime moduli, and unary-encoded into bit vectors of length m_i (the i-th modulus). The shuffler performs bit-level permutation on concatenated vectors per parameter and per residue, and the server recovers only the sum of each parameter's residues (decoded via the Chinese Remainder Theorem and descaled by 10^r/n) to form the global model. No individual client's parameter values are ever exposed to the server.

## Method

The proposed defense (Algorithm 1) combines parameter-level shuffling with RNS encoding and unary encoding to achieve bit-level shuffling granularity. Each parameter is scaled to an integer, split into residues via RNS, and each residue is unary-encoded into a bit vector that is shuffled independently at the bit level. The key theoretical insight (Proposition A.2) is that a shuffled bit vector carries exactly the same information as its sum, so the server learns only the aggregated parameter values and no information about individual local models, reducing SIA accuracy to random guessing (Theorem 1). The paper also introduces three reconstruction attacks (Algorithms 2, 3, 5) that defeat model-level, layer-level, and parameter-level shuffling respectively by exploiting a shadow dataset to remap shuffled values to their original owners. The approach is compatible with DP-SGD, supports three trust levels on the shuffler (Fully Trusted, Semi-Honest, Partially Malicious via MixNets with onion encryption and ZKPs), and achieves a communication expansion of only 1.03×–1.81× over vanilla FL depending on precision r and trust assumptions.

## Results

The proposed Algorithm 1 (parameter-level shuffling with RNS + unary encoding) reduces SIA success rate to the random-guessing baseline (10% for 10 clients) across all evaluated datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) and heterogeneity levels, while all standard shuffling variants remain above random guessing. On CIFAR-10 (α=0.1, 10 clients), model-level shuffling still yields 45% SIA accuracy, layer-level 42%, and parameter-level 27%, versus 10% for the proposed method. Joint model accuracy is preserved with r=3–5 precision digits (e.g., ~53% on CIFAR-100/ResNet-18 at r=5 vs. ~55% vanilla), at a communication expansion of 1.81× (or 1.03× with RLE compression under a fully trusted shuffler).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against model-level shuffling (the standard shuffle-model FL approach), the proposed method wins decisively: reconstruction attacks (Alg. 2) recover SIA accuracy to ~45% on CIFAR-10 and ~78% on CIFAR-100, while Algorithm 1 holds at 10% (random guessing). Layer-level shuffling (Lebrun et al. 2022) is similarly defeated, with Alg. 3 achieving ~42% on CIFAR-10 and ~60% on CIFAR-100. Parameter-level shuffling (Alg. 5) is the strongest baseline among standard shuffling variants, reducing SIA to ~27% on CIFAR-10, but still remains well above the 10% random-guess floor. Regularization-based defenses (random cropping, dropout, weight decay) and Instahide are shown to be ineffective, with SIA accuracy remaining at or above vanilla FL levels (Tables 2–3). In terms of communication cost, the proposed method incurs a 1.81× expansion over vanilla FL (32-bit) for CIFAR-100 at r=5, comparable to Secure Aggregation's 1.73× expansion factor, but with weaker trust assumptions (only 1 of n MixNet servers needs to be trusted vs. t=n−1 for SA) and better liveness properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the shuffle model of federated learning (Bittau et al. 2017; Cheu et al. 2019) and the residue number system (Garner 1959), combining them with unary encoding to achieve bit-level shuffling granularity. It extends the authors' own preliminary CCS 2024 poster, which used unary encoding and shuffling but was impractical due to high communication cost, by introducing RNS to compress the bit vectors and reduce the expansion factor to 1.03×–1.81×. Conceptually, the paper departs from the standard assumption that shuffling provides adequate privacy in FL by demonstrating reconstruction attacks that defeat model-level, layer-level, and parameter-level shuffling, and positions its defense against alternatives (DP, regularization, DRA defenses) that it shows to be ineffective against SIAs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first robust, theoretically grounded defense against source inference attacks in the shuffle model of FL, reducing SIA accuracy to random guessing without adding noise or sacrificing model accuracy. The reconstruction attacks revealing that standard shuffling is insufficient are a significant negative result that affects the security assumptions of existing shuffle-based FL deployments. Key limitations include applicability only to sum-based aggregation (not median/clustering-based), requirement of a trusted shuffler (or MixNet with one trusted server), and the cross-silo setting focus. Open questions include extending the defense to non-sum-based frameworks, combining with disaggregation attacks, and developing defenses in settings without a shuffler.
