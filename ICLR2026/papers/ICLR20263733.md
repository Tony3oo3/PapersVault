## Problem

Existing view transformers for robotic manipulation extract visual representations in a view-specific, static manner, leading to two critical failures: inadequate 3D spatial reasoning (inability to correctly merge cross-view geometry for spatially-complex placements) and dynamic adaptation failure (inability to re-plan trajectories when target objects are displaced during approach). The paper addresses both gaps by drawing on the human cortical dual-pathway principle, where the ventral stream handles enduring scene understanding and the dorsal stream provides real-time egocentric action adjustment.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes three static virtual orthographic views (top, front, right) rendered at 128×128 as 7-channel RGB-D images, one dynamic wrist-mounted view rendered from a virtual camera using real-time extrinsics, a natural language instruction, and gripper proprioception. The 3D supervision signal is generated offline by VGGT, which predicts depth, confidence, and camera parameters to produce geometrically consistent keypoints (300 per view) stored and indexed by demonstration ID. The output is a 6-DoF gripper pose (3-DoF translation via back-projected heatmap argmax, 3-DoF rotation via global features), a binary open/close state, and a collision indicator, executed by a sampling-based motion planner over up to 25 steps.

## Method

Cortical Policy implements a dual-stream view transformer on the RVT-2 backbone. The static-view stream enforces cross-view geometric consistency by aligning features at VGGT-derived keypoints using a cyclic SmoothAP loss (L_cgc) over a closed loop of N=3 views, promoting viewpoint-invariant 3D representations. The dynamic-view stream repurposes a GLC egocentric gaze estimation model (MViT backbone, pretrained on Ego4D then position-aware fine-tuned on 3,600 rendered wrist-view videos with end-effector annotations) as a frozen feature extractor, yielding both view feature maps (concatenated Transformer Encoder and Global-Local Correlation outputs, linearly projected to RVT-2's token space) and saliency maps used as dynamic-view heatmaps. The action head fuses four viewpoints (3 static + 1 dynamic) by pooling local features at heatmap coordinates and concatenating heatmap-weighted sum/max-pooled global features for rotation, state, and collision prediction. Training is behavior cloning with 100 demos per task over 32.5K steps (LAMB optimizer, batch 512), minimizing the sum of cross-entropy action losses and the geometric consistency loss with λ=1.

## Results

On RLBench (18 tasks, 249 language variations), Cortical Policy achieves 81.0% average success rate and average rank 1.8, outperforming the strongest baseline RVT-2 (77.5%, rank 3.5) by 3.5% absolute. On the COLOSSEUM robustness benchmark (14 perturbation settings across 4 tasks), it reaches 69.9% average success, surpassing RVT-2 by 9.4%. In real-world experiments, Cortical Policy achieves 80% success under dynamic object displacement, whereas static-view-only baselines (RVT, RVT-2, 3D-MVP) fail completely (0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cortical Policy outperforms all 8 baselines on RLBench average success. The closest competitor, RVT-2 (77.5%), is surpassed by 3.5%, with particularly large gains on spatially demanding tasks like Insert Peg (+10.7%) and Stack Blocks (+1.3%). VIHE (77.0%) and SAM-E (70.6%) trail by 4.0% and 10.4% respectively. On COLOSSEUM, the gap widens: Cortical Policy (69.9%) beats RVT-2 (60.5%) by 9.4%, and the dynamic-view stream is identified as the primary driver of this robustness advantage. In real-world spatial reasoning (stacking between bottles), Cortical Policy achieves 30% higher success than RVT/RVT-2 and 10% over 3D-MVP. Under dynamic displacement, Cortical Policy reaches 80% success while all static-view baselines (RVT, RVT-2, 3D-MVP) drop to 0%, confirming that the dual-stream design is essential for handling unpredictable scene changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RVT-2 view transformer architecture by adding two orthogonal components: a cross-view geometric consistency loss (SmoothAP-based) supervised by the VGGT 3D foundation model, and a dynamic-view stream that repurposes a pretrained egocentric gaze estimation model (GLC) to extract action-oriented features from a wrist-mounted camera. Conceptually, it departs from the static-only multi-camera paradigm of prior view transformers (RVT, RVT-2, VIHE, 3D-MVP) by introducing a fourth, dynamically-moving viewpoint, motivated by the dorsal-ventral pathway distinction in visual neuroscience. The geometric consistency objective is novel in its use of VGGT-predicted keypoints as 3D supervision for feature alignment, whereas prior methods (3D-MVP, SAM-E) rely on multi-view pretraining or visual foundation models without explicit cross-view geometric anchoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete failure modes of current view transformers—imprecise 3D spatial reasoning and inability to adapt to object displacement during execution—with the dynamic-view stream proving to be the primary robustness driver under severe environmental perturbations (80% success vs. 0% for static-only methods on displacement tasks). However, zero-shot transfer to genuinely novel tasks remains weak (24% on an unseen task), and the neuroscience-inspired framing, while motivating, maps to a relatively standard dual-stream feature fusion at the implementation level. Open questions include whether the dynamic-view stream can track diverse targets beyond the end-effector, whether adaptive token-level fusion of the two streams could yield further gains, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the RLBench/COLOSSEUM task suite to open-world manipulation.
