## Problem

Existing positional encoding methods for Transformers (Sinusoidal, RoPE, ALiBi, NoPE) are largely empirically motivated with limited theoretical grounding, making their extrapolation behavior poorly understood. Additionally, the standard evaluation metric of perplexity can be misleading, as models may achieve low perplexity through localized attention patterns without genuinely attending to distant context, obscuring true long-context retrieval capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262866/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard token sequence (up to 512 tokens during training) and produces next-token predictions via self-attention, but with a critical difference: a per-head relative-position bias matrix B is added to the attention logits, where each entry b_ij = -exp(θ_α)·(|(j-i) - (e^{θ_μ} - e^{-θ_μ})| + ε)^{θ_β}. This adds only 384 learnable parameters (θ_α and θ_β per head, with θ_μ fixed at 0) to a 120M-parameter model, yet enables the attention mechanism to maintain non-vanishing probability mass over arbitrarily long distances at inference time.

## Method

BAM reframes self-attention as a Bayesian expectation over values, decomposing the attention weight p_ij into a joint probability over token content and relative position. Theorem 1 proves that with an additive scoring function, p_ij factorizes into content and position marginals coupled by a normalizing scalar Z, establishing PE as a prior distribution over positions. This unifies existing methods: NoPE corresponds to a Uniform prior, ALiBi to a Laplace prior. The proposed GGD-BAM replaces the prior with a Generalized Gaussian distribution parameterized per attention head, and critically relaxes the shape parameter to allow β < 0, which creates 'retrieval heads' that suppress local context and concentrate attention on distant tokens. Combined with Scalable Softmax to counteract attention fading, GGD-BAM achieves 500× context length extrapolation in passkey retrieval while adding negligible parameters.

## Results

BAM SSMax (120M, trained on 512 tokens) maintains 100% passkey retrieval accuracy up to 32,000 tokens (64× training length) and above 80% at 256,000 tokens (500× training length), while all other PE methods degrade to near-random beyond ~4K tokens. On the RULER NIAH Single-1 task (1B model), BAM SSMax achieves 1.00 accuracy at all context lengths up to 32,768 tokens, whereas RoPE SSMax drops to 0.00 by 8,192 tokens. On LongBenchV2 (1B), BAM SSMax scores 28.6 overall vs. 24.2 for RoPE SSMax. Short-context perplexity is comparable to baselines (BAM: 18.93 Wikipedia / 22.26 FineWeb vs. ALiBi: 18.55 / 22.18), with BAM adding only 384 trainable parameters to a 120M model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **ALiBi** (Press et al., ICLR 2022), BAM wins decisively on long-context retrieval: on RULER NIAH Single-1 (120M), BAM SSMax scores 1.00 at 6K while ALiBi SSMax drops to 0.10; on passkey retrieval, ALiBi degrades to near-random beyond ~4K tokens while BAM SSMax maintains 100% to 32K+. ALiBi has a marginal short-context perplexity advantage (18.55 vs. 18.93 on Wikipedia), which the authors attribute to ALiBi's purely local Laplace prior being well-suited to short sequences. Against **RoPE** (Su et al., 2024), BAM dominates on all long-context metrics: on 1B RULER Single-1, BAM SSMax holds 1.00 at 32K while RoPE SSMax reaches 0.00 by 8K; on LongBenchV2, BAM scores 28.6 vs. 24.2 overall. RoPE has slightly better short-context perplexity (18.36 vs. 18.93 Wikipedia). Against **Sinusoidal PE** (Vaswani, 2017) and **NoPE** (Kazemnejad et al., 2023), both collapse to near-zero retrieval accuracy beyond 1–2K tokens and show exponential perplexity growth, while BAM maintains stable performance. The paper formally shows NoPE corresponds to a Uniform prior and ALiBi to a Laplace prior within the BAM framework, making them special cases rather than competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes positional encoding as a prior distribution within a Bayesian attention mechanism, providing a unified probabilistic lens under which NoPE (uniform prior) and ALiBi (Laplace prior) emerge as special cases. The most direct methodological lineage is ALiBi's linear positional bias, which GGD-BAM generalizes by replacing the linear decay with a Generalized Gaussian whose shape parameter β controls tail heaviness. The paper also incorporates Scalable Softmax (Nakanishi, 2025) as a complementary rescaling of the softmax denominator, positioning BAM as addressing positional structure while SSMax addresses attention fading — two distinct but interacting failure modes in long-context attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification gives practitioners an interpretable, parametric knob (β) to explicitly control how much attention mass is allocated to distant versus local tokens, with the surprising finding that some heads naturally learn β<0 to act as long-range retrieval specialists. The practical method requires only ~384 additional parameters for a 120M model yet achieves 500× context extrapolation in passkey retrieval, suggesting a potentially very cheap upgrade path for existing transformer architectures. Key open questions include whether these gains persist at multi-billion-parameter scale, whether they survive instruction-tuning and RLHF stages, and whether the β<0 retrieval-head phenomenon generalizes beyond synthetic retrieval tasks to realistic long-document understanding.
