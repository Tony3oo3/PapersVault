## Problem

Discrete diffusion models require a large number of sequential sampling steps (often comparable to the data dimensionality) because the factorized reverse process cannot match the complex, non-factorized target distribution induced by a fixed forward process when the number of steps T is small. This makes inference computationally expensive and prevents few-step generation, unlike continuous diffusion where ODE-based distillation can collapse trajectories. The paper asks whether learning the forward (noising) process can reshape the target distribution to be compatible with the factorized reverse parameterization, enabling few-step sampling.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a random prior sample z_T ~ p(z_T) and iteratively applies T factorized categorical updates (as few as 10 steps) to produce a discrete output: a text sequence (ROCStories), a molecular graph (QM9, ZINC250k), or a binary image (MNIST). All D coordinates are updated in parallel at each step, so total inference cost scales with T rather than D. The forward process is used exclusively during training to define the learning target and adds no inference overhead.

## Method

FLDD replaces the fixed Markovian forward chain with a non-Markovian, learnable forward process where the marginal distributions q_φ(z_t|x) are factorized categoricals whose parameters depend on the entire data point x (not just the i-th coordinate), and the posteriors q_φ(z_s|z_t,x) are computed via a Maximum Coupling transport plan between consecutive marginals. The reverse process remains the standard factorized categorical p_θ(z_s|z_t), so the sampler is unchanged. Training minimizes the usual variational bound end-to-end: the reverse parameters θ receive standard gradients, while the forward parameters φ are optimized with REINFORCE (score-function gradients), preceded by a warm-up phase using the Concrete distribution to obtain low-variance reparameterized gradients. The key insight is that by jointly adapting the forward process to the reverse model's factorized capacity, the induced target q(z_s|z_t) becomes approximately factorized, closing the gap that forces conventional discrete diffusion to use many steps.

## Results

On molecular generation (QM9), FLDD with T=100 achieves 99.67% validity, 98.93% uniqueness, and FCD of 0.328, while at T=10 it retains 99.08% validity and FCD of 0.385—competitive with full-step baselines like CatFlow (FCD 0.441) and DirichletFM (FCD 0.888). On ZINC250k, FLDD at T=10 achieves FCD 10.414, outperforming CatFlow (13.211) and FlowMatching (18.764) at their standard step counts. On ROCStories text generation, FLDD at T=100 reaches MAUVE 0.538 and PPL 55.2, and at T=10 maintains MAUVE 0.511 with PPL 60.5, showing only a slight degradation. The key finding is that FLDD at T=10 steps produces samples of comparable quality to other diffusion models requiring T=100 or more steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On QM9, FLDD at T=100 (FCD 0.328) outperforms CatFlow (0.441), DirichletFM (0.888), FlowMatching (5.155), and MoFlow (4.467), but trails LO-ARM (0.240) and GDSS (2.900 is worse, so FLDD wins there). At T=10, FLDD's FCD of 0.385 still beats CatFlow, DirichletFM, and FlowMatching at their full step budgets. On ZINC250k, FLDD at T=100 (FCD 8.487) substantially outperforms CatFlow (13.211), DirichletFM (14.222), GDSS (14.656), and FlowMatching (18.764), though LO-ARM (3.229) remains stronger. On ROCStories, FLDD at T=100 (MAUVE 0.538, PPL 55.2) trails COSMOS (MAUVE 0.940, PPL 26.3) and SEDD (MAUVE 0.598) but beats AR-Diffusion, DiffuSeq, SeqDiffuSeq, and TESS on all three metrics; at T=10, FLDD (MAUVE 0.511) still surpasses all diffusion baselines except SEDD and COSMOS. The authors explicitly note that some baselines outperform FLDD due to suboptimal hyperparameter tuning and acknowledge that the factorized reverse parameterization is a known limitation they leave for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learnable-forward-process idea from continuous diffusion (Bartosh et al., 2024) to the discrete regime, where conventional models fix the noising dynamics (uniform noise, absorption, or masking) and rely on a factorized reverse sampler. The key departure is a non-Markovian parameterization of forward marginals and posteriors (via Maximum Coupling) that makes the induced target distribution compatible with the same factorized reverse family, rather than trying to make the reverse process more expressive. Training uses the standard variational bound from Ho et al. (2020) with a REINFORCE gradient estimator and a Concrete-distribution warm-up (Jang et al., 2016), preserving the simulation-free optimization of conventional discrete diffusion (Lou et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLDD offers a practical path to few-step discrete generation without modifying the sampler, which is valuable for domains where inference cost is a bottleneck (text, molecules, structured data). However, the current results at T=100 are below several strong baselines (e.g., COSMOS on ROCStories, LO-ARM on QM9), and the authors acknowledge suboptimal hyperparameters and parameterization choices. Open questions include whether richer forward parameterizations (partially autoregressive marginals, inter-coordinate dependencies) can close the gap, whether lower-variance gradient estimators can replace REINFORCE, and whether the learned forward process can be distilled or frozen for even faster inference.
