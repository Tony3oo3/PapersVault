## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models suffer from semantic leakage, where visually similar entities (e.g., a cow and a horse) unintentionally share features like ear shapes or mouth structures in generated images. Existing mitigations rely on costly inference-time optimization or external inputs such as bounding boxes and LLM-generated layouts, and no dedicated dataset or evaluation framework exists to systematically study this phenomenon at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262275/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DeLeaker operates entirely at inference time on a DiT-based diffusion model (FLUX.1-dev or SANA), consuming only the text prompt and the model's internal pre-softmax attention scores during the denoising process. It requires no external bounding boxes, reference images, or LLM guidance. The output is a generated image with reduced cross-entity feature transfer while preserving the original scene structure, image-text alignment (VQAScore), and perceptual similarity (LPIPS) to the unmodified generation. Evaluation uses the SLIM dataset of 1,130 human-verified (prompt, seed, image) triples spanning animal pairs, interactions, style variants, and fruit/vegetable triplets.

## Method

DeLeaker applies three synergistic interventions to the self-attention maps during diffusion: (1) it extracts entity-specific image token masks from early diffusion steps by thresholding image-to-text attention scores above a dynamic mean + β₁·σ criterion, with temporal and spatial smoothing for stability; (2) it suppresses cross-entity leakage by zeroing all image-to-text attention between different entities and zeroing image-to-image attention scores exceeding mean + β₂·σ; and (3) it strengthens self-identity by multiplying each entity's own image-to-text attention scores by a coefficient α > 1. The key insight, confirmed by ablation, is that semantic leakage in DiT models is primarily a cross-modal misalignment problem: removing the self-identity strengthening component degrades major-improvement scores by 46%, while within-modality interventions (text-text suppression) have minimal or negative effects. The method is training-free, optimization-free, and non-intrusive when no leakage is present, adding only ~44% latency over baseline generation.

## Results

On the SLIM pair subsets (840 samples), DeLeaker achieves 46.07% major mitigation and 9.76% minor mitigation (55.83% total improvement) with only 12.98% major degradation, outperforming all six baselines. It also attains the best preservation metrics: lowest LPIPS (0.22), highest VQAScore (0.68), and tied-lowest KID (0.00). Human evaluation on 60 samples confirms these findings, with annotators judging DeLeaker improved the image in 67.8% of cases (13.56% major + 54.24% minor) versus 0% major degradation. Adding entity descriptions to DeLeaker yields only marginal gains (53.57% major mitigation), confirming the method's standalone effectiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeLeaker decisively outperforms all three layout-based baselines (RAG-Diffusion, RPF, 3DIS) on both mitigation and degradation: while these methods achieve only 17–29% major mitigation, they suffer 38–65% major degradation, indicating they often worsen the image while attempting to fix leakage. Among prompt-based baselines, the Entity Description Prompt is the strongest competitor (35.60% major mitigation, 18.45% major degradation), but DeLeaker still leads by over 10 points in major mitigation and 5.5 points in major degradation. QwenFLUX (image-conditioned instruction) performs poorly, with 46.60% major degradation, suggesting that conditioning on the leaked image itself amplifies the problem. DeLeaker also achieves the best preservation scores across all three metrics (LPIPS 0.22 vs. 0.33–0.76 for baselines; VQAScore 0.68 vs. 0.42–0.64), confirming that its attention-level interventions are minimally invasive. The ablation study further reveals that the self-identity strengthening component (image-text attention boost) is the single most important intervention, contributing 90% of the major improvement when applied alone, while cross-entity image-text suppression is second most influential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the attention-control lineage in diffusion models (Prompt-to-Prompt, Make-It-Count) and the recent identification of semantic leakage in T2I outputs (Rassin et al., 2022; Dahary et al., 2025a,b), but departs sharply from prior mitigation strategies that rely on external layout guidance (bounding boxes from LLMs) or costly inference-time optimization. DeLeaker instead operates purely through dynamic reweighting of pre-softmax attention scores during the DiT diffusion process, requiring no external inputs, no training, and no optimization loop. The entity-masking step follows the early-step attention localization observed in UNet-based models, but the paper is the first to apply such interventions in the DiT setting, where image and text tokens are processed jointly in a single transformer backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of SLIM (1,130 human-verified samples) and the step-by-step VLM-based evaluation framework fills a critical gap: prior work evaluated leakage on only a few dozen samples from general-purpose benchmarks, making systematic comparison impossible. The ablation finding that self-identity strengthening (a simple α=1.2 multiplication) is the dominant intervention, while cross-entity image-image suppression contributes little, reframes the problem as one of cross-modal alignment rather than within-modality interference. Limitations include occasional loss of intended inter-entity interactions (e.g., shared actions) and inability to recover missing entities; extending the approach to 3D/video generation and using SLIM to train dedicated leakage classifiers are natural next steps.
