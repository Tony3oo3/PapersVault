## Problem

Value Iteration Networks and other deep RL planners excel in fully observable environments but degrade sharply under perceptual ambiguity. The current best POMDP solver network, QMDP-Net, relies on a policy module that assumes all uncertainty vanishes after one step, causing it to fail in environments with sustained ambiguity. This paper addresses the need for a differentiable planning module that can perform long-term information gathering before reward exploitation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264712/cropped_page9_idx9.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a 2D binary grid map (10×10 or 20×20) encoding obstacles, goal/landmark positions, and a belief distribution over the agent's state as recurrent input; after each action it receives a local observation (3×3 neighborhood or a |G|+1 vector) to update the belief. Outputs include the selected action (one of five: up, down, left, right, stay), a single-state value map V(s) highlighting rewarding regions, and a marginal pairwise value map Σ_s V(s,s′) that highlights informative regions. In the MOMDP task, the state space is factorized into a visible agent-position factor and a hidden goal-position factor, reducing pairwise module size by ~100×.

## Method

VI²N augments a standard Value Iteration module (learning f_P and f_R via convolutional kernels) with a second VI² module that performs value iteration over pairs of states, implementing the Pairwise Heuristic end-to-end. Distinguishable state pairs (where observations differ above a threshold λ) are initialized with the average MDP value, while indistinguishable pairs undergo Bellman updates in a pair-space MDP whose transition is the product of individual transitions and whose reward is the average of the two states' rewards. The 3×3 transition kernel is expanded to a (2(√S+1)+1)² kernel to encode joint pair transitions, and the pairwise belief is formed as the outer product of the belief with itself for action selection. In mixed-observable settings, factorization into visible and hidden state components reduces the pairwise computation from |S|² to |S_v|·|S_h|². The full architecture is recurrent (belief update + two planning modules) and trained by imitation of expert actions over 15,000–30,000 procedurally generated grid worlds.

## Results

On Task 2 (unknown goal, observable position), VI²N achieves 95–98% success rate under deterministic transitions and 91% under stochastic transitions, compared to QMDP-Net's 51–57% (deterministic) and 27–30% (stochastic). On Task 1 (observable goal, unknown position), VI²N outperforms QMDP-Net most dramatically in the symmetric environment (76% vs. 61% at 5% density; 65% vs. 41% at 15% density), while both methods perform comparably in simple random environments (~93%). In a scaled 20×20 environment with 4 goals, VI²N reaches 79% success versus QMDP-Net's 27%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is QMDP-Net (Karkus et al., 2017), the prior state-of-the-art differentiable POMDP solver. VI²N wins in every tested condition, with the margin scaling with environmental uncertainty. In simple random environments (Task 1, 5% density), both methods tie at ~93%, but the gap widens sharply in structured or symmetric environments (e.g., 76% vs. 61% in Symmetric-5%, 65% vs. 41% in Symmetric-15%). The most dramatic separation appears in Task 2, where VI²N's pairwise planning module enables it to visit the informative landmark 96% of the time versus QMDP-Net's 34%, yielding a 40+ point advantage under deterministic transitions and a 60+ point advantage under stochastic transitions. Notably, QMDP-Net held a computational advantage (fewer free parameters since the pairwise transition kernel was not updated), yet VI²N's higher success rate compensates for its added complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

VI²N sits at the intersection of two research threads: the Value Iteration Network (VIN) family, which embeds Bellman updates as convolutional layers for differentiable planning in fully observable MDPs (Tamar et al., 2016), and classic POMDP solvers that handle partial observability through belief-state reasoning. The paper's key architectural move is to make the Pairwise Heuristic (Khalvati & Mackworth, 2013)—a sub-problem decomposition that evaluates optimal policies over pairs of states—fully differentiable by expressing its transition, reward, and distinguishability operations as learnable convolutional kernels, then stacking it as a second planning module on top of a standard VI module. This departs from QMDP-Net (Karkus et al., 2017), the prior state-of-the-art differentiable POMDP network, which uses a one-step QMDP policy module that assumes all uncertainty vanishes after the first action; VI²N replaces this with multi-step pairwise value iteration that explicitly plans for information gathering before reward exploitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap: VINs and most deep RL planners degrade sharply under even modest perceptual ambiguity, while the existing differentiable POMDP network (QMDP-Net) relies on a policy module too simple to handle sustained uncertainty. VI²N's pairwise value iteration module provides a principled, interpretable mechanism for long-horizon information seeking, and its MOMDP factorization offers a practical route to scaling to environments where only a small subset of state dimensions is hidden. Key limitations temper the impact: all experiments are 2D binary grid-world navigation, the transition/observation kernels require spatial invariance, training is imitation-based rather than reward-based, and the sole baseline (QMDP-Net) is a known weak POMDP solver. Open questions include whether the pairwise module composes with recent VIN improvements (generalized VINs, universal VINs), whether it extends to 3D or non-grid domains, and whether it can be trained end-to-end from reward signals.
