## Problem

LLM pretraining and finetuning rely exclusively on input-space autoregressive token reconstruction, while vision research has shown that embedding-space JEPA objectives yield superior representations with fewer biases. No JEPA-style training objective had been adapted to LLMs, leaving open whether the representational benefits observed in vision could transfer to language while preserving the generative capabilities that define modern LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired views of the same underlying knowledge—e.g., a natural-language description paired with its regular expression, a database description paired with a SQL query, or a question paired with an answer span. Both views are packed into a single context window separated by a custom block-causal attention mask so neither view attends to the other, and the model simultaneously generates the target sequence (regex, SQL, label) and predicts the embedding of one view from the other. The output is standard autoregressive text generation evaluated by exact match or prefix matching, with no change to the inference-time interface.

## Method

LLM-JEPA augments the standard autoregressive cross-entropy loss with a JEPA term: cosine similarity between Pred(Enc(Text)) and Enc(Code), weighted by λ. The encoder is the LLM's last-layer hidden state of the final token; the predictor is a tied-weights module that appends k special [PRED] tokens to the Text and uses the hidden state of the last [PRED] token as the predicted embedding, reusing the LLM's own self-attention for nonlinear processing without extra parameters. A custom additive attention mask makes attention causal within each of the two blocks (Text and Code) but blocks all cross-block attention, allowing both view embeddings to be extracted in a single extra forward pass rather than two. The paper further introduces random JEPA-loss dropout at the batch level to reduce the 2× training compute overhead, showing that aggressive dropout (up to 75%) combined with proportionally scaled λ maintains or improves accuracy at lower FLOPs.

## Results

On NL-RX-SYNTH, LLM-JEPA achieves 71.46% exact-match accuracy with Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, outperforming standard next-token-prediction fine-tuning (57.29%) by 14.2 points. Across four datasets with Llama-3.2-1B, LLM-JEPA improves accuracy on NL-RX-TURK (30.94% vs 22.49%), GSM8K (36.36% vs 32.36%), and Spider (50.55% vs 47.52%). Gains are consistent across model families: +13.3 points on OpenELM-1.1B, +9.5 on Gemma-2-2B, and +0.4 on OLMo-2-1B, all with statistically significant p-values below 0.01.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline throughout the paper is standard LLM fine-tuning with next-token-prediction (NTP) cross-entropy loss (L_LLM). LLM-JEPA consistently outperforms this baseline across all four model families, all four main datasets, and all model sizes tested (1B–8B), with the largest gains observed on weaker models (OpenELM: +13.3 pts) and smaller datasets. In the ablation (Table 3), the InfoNCE contrastive loss (van den Oord et al., 2018) with τ=0.07 performs substantially worse than both the baseline (34.40% vs 57.29%) and LLM-JEPA (71.46%), suggesting that the contrastive push-apart objective is counterproductive for the alignment-based extrapolation that drives LLM-JEPA's gains. LLM-JEPA also demonstrates superior overfitting resistance: while NTP fine-tuning degrades after ~4 epochs, LLM-JEPA continues improving through 6 epochs. On pretraining (NL-RX-SYNTH from scratch), LLM-JEPA achieves 60.59% vs 54.38% for NTP, and on downstream sentiment tasks after paraphrase pretraining, it yields statistically significant improvements on both Rotten Tomatoes (+1.19 pts) and Yelp (+0.69 pts).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly adapts the JEPA framework from vision (Assran et al., 2023; I-JEPA) to LLMs, introducing an embedding-space prediction loss alongside the standard autoregressive next-token prediction objective. It departs from prior NLP embedding-space methods (SimCSE, Sentence-BERT, Data2vec) that sacrifice generative capability by treating the JEPA term as a regularizer that preserves the LLM's generative loss. The paper also distinguishes itself from text-to-code, text-to-SQL, and NL-to-regex generation work that exploits paired (text, code) views purely for generative training, by adding an explicit embedding-space alignment objective on top of generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLM-JEPA provides the first principled bridge between the JEPA paradigm that has proven superior in vision and the LLM training pipeline, with the practical implication that a single additional forward pass (mitigated by loss dropout) can yield consistent accuracy gains across model families, sizes, and tasks. Key open questions include whether the method scales to full pretraining without natural two-view structures, how to develop data-augmentation strategies that synthesize views for arbitrary datasets, and whether the 2× training overhead can be further reduced. The finding that the optimal (λ, k) configuration lacks a clear pattern remains a practical tuning burden.
