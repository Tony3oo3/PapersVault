## Problem

Existing streaming video understanding models rely on query-agnostic 'change-is-important' heuristics that conflate raw visual dynamics with semantic relevance to the user's query. This causes the model to waste computation on visually salient but query-irrelevant events (e.g., scene transitions, unrelated actors) while missing brief or visually inconspicuous events that are actually relevant, resulting in both computational redundancy and mistimed responses.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a continuous video stream at 1 FPS alongside a natural-language text query. Each incoming frame is encoded by a lightweight OpenCLIP-ViT-L/14 encoder into patch-level feature vectors, which are filtered in real time; the original unpruned tokens are held in a memory buffer and pruned just-in-time only when a response is triggered. The output is a text response generated by a backbone Video-LLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or TimeChat-Online-7B), emitted only at moments where both query relevance and information density thresholds are simultaneously met, rather than on every frame or on user prompt.

## Method

QueryStream is a training-free, plug-and-play module with two synergistic components. Query-Aware Differential Pruning (QDP) retains a visual token only if it passes both a semantic relevance filter (patch-query cosine similarity exceeds the frame-average threshold) and a temporal novelty filter (patch feature deviates from a dynamically smoothed history maintained via exponential moving average with α=0.1), yielding a logical-AND mask that preserves M-ROPE positional coherence. Relevance-Triggered Active Response (RTAR) schedules the Video-LLM invocation via a dual gate: a relevance gate checking that the mean-pooled frame feature aligns with the query embedding above τ_rel, and a density gate checking that the QDP token keep rate exceeds τ_den, so responses fire only when the scene is both topically relevant and informationally novel. The entire pipeline requires no fine-tuning and operates as a pre-processing gateway in front of an off-the-shelf Video-LLM.

## Results

On StreamingBench, QueryStream-7B achieves an overall score of 75.32 at a 57.2% token keep rate, matching the full-token TimeChat-Online baseline (75.36) while pruning ~43% of visual tokens, and outperforming the compressed TimeChat-Online (74.32 at 55.8%) by 1.0 points. On OVO-Bench, QueryStream sets a new state-of-the-art among online models at 49.4 (52.9% keep rate), surpassing full-token TimeChat-Online (46.7) by 2.7 points. On offline long-video tasks, QDP as a zero-shot plug-in on Qwen2.5-VL-7B improves Video-MME from 63.2 to 63.6 (52.4% keep rate) and LongVideoBench from 55.4 to 57.3, with aggressive pruning to 16.6% tokens further boosting LongVideoBench to 58.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QueryStream's primary comparison is with TimeChat-Online (Yao et al., 2025), the closest prior work using query-agnostic differential pruning. QueryStream outperforms TimeChat-Online at every matched keep-rate level: 75.32 vs. 74.32 on StreamingBench (57.2% vs. 55.8% keep), 74.04 vs. 72.96 (29.6% vs. 33.0%), and 49.4 vs. 46.7 on OVO-Bench (52.9% vs. 100% keep). Against the full-token Qwen2.5-VL-7B reference, QueryStream matches or exceeds its performance (75.32 vs. 73.68 on StreamingBench; 63.6 vs. 63.2 on Video-MME) while retaining only ~52% of tokens. Among other online baselines, QueryStream substantially outperforms Dispider-7B (75.32 vs. 67.63 on StreamingBench), VideoLLM-Online-8B (75.32 vs. 35.99), and Flash-VStream-7B (75.32 vs. 23.23). On OVO-Bench, QueryStream also surpasses proprietary models GPT-4o (49.4 vs. 58.6 overall, though GPT-4o uses 64 frames offline) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (49.4 vs. 65.3) in the online setting, establishing a new SOTA among all online Video-LLMs. On offline long-video benchmarks, QDP as a zero-shot module on Qwen2.5-VL outperforms the DTD variant (63.6 vs. 63.4 on Video-MME; 61.9 vs. 61.6 on LongVideoBench) and the full-token baseline, confirming its generalizability beyond streaming.

## Relation to Prior Work

QueryStream directly extends TimeChat-Online's query-agnostic differential token pruning (DTD) by replacing its 'change-is-important' heuristic with a dual criterion of semantic relevance to the user query and temporal novelty against a dynamically smoothed history. It also departs from prior proactive response models (which rely on trained EOS predictors or auxiliary classification heads) by introducing a lightweight, logic-driven dual-gated trigger (RTAR) that requires no additional training. The framework positions itself at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work—query-aware token pruning (largely studied for offline video) and streaming response scheduling—unifying them into a single, intent-driven pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that query-aware pruning can match or slightly exceed full-token performance (context denoising) suggests that the semantic redundancy in video streams relative to user intent is far greater than previously assumed, opening a direction for substantially cheaper streaming inference. However, the approach depends on a separate OpenCLIP encoder for feature extraction, adds latency, and the RTAR evaluation protocol is simulated rather than truly real-time. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to multi-turn or evolving queries, higher frame rates, and whether the fixed threshold-based gating can be replaced by learned, adaptive policies without sacrificing the training-free property.
