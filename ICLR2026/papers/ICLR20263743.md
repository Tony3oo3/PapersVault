## Problem

Existing neural branching policies for branch-and-bound (B&B) in MILP solving either ignore historical branching data entirely or model it as an unordered graph, failing to capture the inherently sequential nature of the B&B tree expansion path from root to optimal solution. This myopic perspective limits the model's ability to leverage inter-step dependencies and intra-step variable interactions over long horizons, where the sequence length grows substantially with the number of branching steps and candidate variables.

## Input / Output

At each branching step, the method consumes a parameterized B&B tree state consisting of 25-dimensional candidate variable features and 61-dimensional tree features, which are embedded via the TreeGate architecture into 8-dimensional vectors. The full input to the sequence model is the 'branching path'—a concatenation of all candidate variable embeddings plus the selected variable embedding from every preceding step, augmented with learnable positional encodings that assign identical positions to embeddings within the same step. The sequence length reaches ~30,000 tokens during training (T=99 steps) and ~4,000 tokens at inference (T=24 steps). The output is a probability distribution over candidate branching variables at the current step, obtained by extracting only the outputs at variable positions, applying average pooling, and passing through softmax.

## Method

Mamba-Branching employs the Mamba selective state-space model as the sequence backbone, exploiting its linear-time complexity (vs. Transformer's quadratic) to handle the ultra-long branching path sequences that arise from encoding every candidate variable at every step. A key architectural insight is the contrastive learning pre-training stage: before imitation learning, the TreeGate embedding layer is trained with a custom contrastive loss that pulls the expert-selected variable's embedding toward a max-pooled anchor while pushing non-selected candidates away, formalized via Proposition 1 which requires a positive similarity margin δ between the selected variable and all others. The imitation learning stage then trains the full model autoregressively under a joint cross-entropy loss, using relpscost branching as the expert policy. During inference, predictions from previous steps are fed back as inputs (autoregressive decoding), and the small 8-dimensional embedding size is deliberately chosen to keep per-step inference time under 1 ms, which is critical for the policy to actually accelerate solving rather than add overhead.

## Results

On MILP-S, Mamba-Branching achieves 2068.83 nodes and 111.78s solving time, outperforming TreeGate (2146.75 nodes, 113.85s), T-BranT (2668.62 nodes, 165.59s), and Transformer-Branching (3078.56 nodes, 3153.66s). On the larger MILP-L benchmark, Mamba-Branching achieves 1819.32 nodes on easy instances and the best primal-dual integral (12319.55) on difficult instances, surpassing even SCIP's default relpscost rule (12741.36). Transformer-Branching runs out of GPU memory entirely on MILP-L, while Mamba-Branching's linear-complexity Mamba backbone handles sequences of ~4,132 tokens with only 0.013 GB inference memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mamba-Branching outperforms all neural branching baselines (TreeGate, T-BranT, Transformer-Branching, GCNN) across every metric and dataset. Against TreeGate, it reduces nodes by ~3.6% on MILP-S and ~18% on MILP-L Easy, while cutting solving time by ~15% on MILP-L Easy. Against T-BranT, the gap is larger: ~23% fewer nodes on MILP-S and ~41% less time on MILP-L Easy, confirming that sequential modeling of history outperforms T-BranT's unordered graph approach. Against Transformer-Branching, Mamba-Branching is dramatically faster (111.78s vs. 3153.66s on MILP-S) and the only method that does not OOM on MILP-L, attributable to Mamba's linear vs. quadratic sequence complexity. Against the heuristic relpscost (SCIP's default), Mamba-Branching loses on easy instances (1819 vs. 668 nodes) but wins on difficult instances with a 3.3% lower PD integral (12319 vs. 12741), indicating faster primal-dual bound convergence under time pressure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameterized B&B tree framework initiated by TreeGate (Zarpellon et al., 2021) and T-BranT (Lin et al., 2022), which established instance-independent feature spaces for cross-instance generalization. The key departure is treating the branching path as an explicit sequence rather than an unordered graph (T-BranT) or ignoring history entirely (TreeGate), and employing Mamba's linear-complexity state-space model to handle the resulting ultra-long sequences where Transformer-based approaches become infeasible. A contrastive learning pre-training stage for the embedding layer is introduced to address the high intra-step similarity among candidate variables, a challenge absent in NLP-style embedding learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling the sequential structure of B&B tree expansion yields consistent improvements over both history-ignoring and unordered-graph approaches, and that linear-complexity sequence models are practically essential for neural branching policies to remain faster than the heuristics they replace. The outperformance of SCIP's heavily-tuned relpscost on hard instances suggests that learned sequential policies can surpass hand-engineered rules in complex regimes. A key limitation is the reliance on imitation learning with expensive expert demonstrations; the authors identify reinforcement learning as a natural next step to eliminate this dependency. Broader open questions include whether sequential modeling benefits transfer to other B&B sub-problems (node selection, cut selection) and whether the approach scales to instances with thousands of branching steps.
