## Problem

Neural routing solvers struggle with hard constraints: feasibility masking becomes NP-hard (e.g., TSPTW) or overly restrictive (e.g., multi-constraint CVRPBLTW), while implicit feasibility awareness via features or reward shaping remains limited in generality and incurs high inference overhead. No existing method is simultaneously general across constraint types, efficient, and effective at guaranteeing feasibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260407/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The construction module consumes node feature vectors (coordinates, demands, time windows, backhaul/duration flags) projected into 128-d embeddings, while the refinement module additionally encodes the current solution as a linked list with cyclic positional embeddings and variant-specific feasibility history features. Output is a feasible tour/route produced in two stages: S diverse initial solutions from the construction decoder, followed by T_R (5–20) refinement steps that apply local search operators (flexible k-opt or remove-and-reinsert), yielding a final solution with tour cost and constraint-violation status.

## Method

CaR jointly trains a construction policy and a refinement policy in a single end-to-end framework, where the construction module is guided by a tailored loss combining REINFORCE, an entropy-based diversity loss (L_DIV) to compensate for removed multi-start mechanisms, and a supervised loss (L_SL) that uses the best-refined solution as pseudo ground truth. The refinement module operates under a short-horizon CMDP (T_R=10 steps vs. 5k in prior improvement solvers), with its REINFORCE loss averaged across all refinement steps to encourage each step to contribute meaningfully. A shared 6-layer Transformer encoder with a Syn-Att mechanism fuses node-level and solution-level attention scores, enabling cross-paradigm representation learning that reduces parameters and improves constraint awareness, particularly in hard-constrained settings. The joint loss L(θ)=L(θ^C)+ω·L(θ^R) promotes co-evolution, so construction specifically generates solutions well-suited for rapid refinement rather than standalone optimality.

## Results

On TSPTW-50 (Hard), CaR-PIP (T_R=20) achieves a 0.005% optimality gap with 0.00% infeasibility in 52s, outperforming LKH-3 (0.004% gap but 11.88% infeasibility, 7m) and the best construction baseline PIP (0.152% gap, 1.87% infeasibility, 1m). On TSPTW-100, CaR-PIP reduces infeasibility from PIP's 4.47% to 0.02% and the gap from 1.026% to 0.146% in 2.4m, achieving an 8× speedup over NeuOpt-GIRE (30m). On CVRPBLTW-100, CaR(R&R) (T_R=20) achieves a −2.448% gap (beating the OR-Tools reference) with 0% infeasibility in just 19s, while NeuOpt-GIRE suffers 39.1% infeasibility even after 12.7m of search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TSPTW, CaR-PIP dominates all baselines: it beats the best construction solver PIP by 20× on infeasibility (0.00% vs. 1.87% at n=50) and 22× at n=100 (0.02% vs. 4.47%), while matching or exceeding LKH-3's optimality gap with 6–8× speedup. The hybrid solver NCS produces 100% infeasible solutions on TSPTW, and UDC+RRC also fails completely (100% infeasibility). On CVRPBLTW, CaR(R&R) achieves the best gap (−2.448% at n=100, beating the OR-Tools reference) with 0% infeasibility in 19s, while NeuOpt-GIRE suffers 27–51% infeasibility despite 5–13 minutes of search, and POMO+EAS+SGBS requires 1 hour to reach a 0.854% gap. CaR also outperforms multi-task solvers (MVMoE, ReLD-MoEL, POMO-MTL) by 5–9% in gap on CVRPBLTW-50 while maintaining 0% infeasibility. Notably, CaR finds feasible solutions on TSPTW instances where LKH-3 (even with 10,000 trials) fails, demonstrating a qualitative advantage under hard constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaR builds on the POMO/PIP construction paradigm and the NeuOpt/N2S improvement paradigm, but departs fundamentally from prior hybrid solvers (RL4CO, NCS, LCP) in three ways: it jointly trains construction and refinement end-to-end rather than coupling them only at inference or via a shared critic, it uses a shared Transformer encoder across both paradigms for cross-paradigm representation learning, and it targets feasibility refinement in ~10 steps rather than the thousands of steps used by prior improvement methods. Conceptually, it introduces explicit feasibility refinement as a third constraint-handling scheme alongside the previously dominant feasibility masking and implicit feasibility awareness, positioning itself as the first framework to combine all three (flexible masking, shared representation, and explicit refinement) for general applicability across constraint hardness levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CaR addresses a practical gap: prior neural solvers either fail on hard constraints (100% infeasibility on TSPTW) or require hours of improvement search, undermining the efficiency advantage of neural methods. The finding that joint training creates a non-trivial synergy—where construction is specifically tailored to produce solutions amenable to rapid refinement, and this cannot be replicated by simply combining pretrained modules—suggests a new design principle for hybrid neural solvers. Open questions include scalability beyond n=100, theoretical guarantees on convergence and generalization, and whether the cross-paradigm shared representation extends to broader combinatorial optimization problems like scheduling. The paper also notes that multi-task solvers are bottlenecked by their backbone, hinting that CaR could serve as a stronger foundation for future NCO foundation models.
