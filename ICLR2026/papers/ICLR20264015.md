## Problem

Fast actor-critic methods like FastTD3 achieve high wall-clock throughput via massive parallelization but remain sample-inefficient, requiring many environment interactions to reach target performance. The paper identifies that non-stationarity in bootstrapped training causes representation collapse (neuron dormancy, reduced effective rank), which destabilizes critics and degrades policy gradients. Existing remedies either add auxiliary losses that increase computational overhead or use explicit discretization with biased straight-through gradients.

## Input / Output

The method consumes either robot state vectors (e.g., 76-DoF Unitree H1 with hands on HumanoidBench, 12-DoF Booster T1) or pixel observation frames (84×84 Atari ALE screens processed through a CNN backbone) and produces continuous control actions (bounded by tanh) or discrete actions. The SEM module is inserted at the penultimate layer of the actor and/or critic MLPs: for the critic it replaces the final linear head before the C51 distributional output, and for the actor it precedes the final Linear→Tanh layer, so the policy is conditioned on bounded, sparse simplex-constrained features.

## Method

The core contribution is inserting Simplicial Embedding (SEM) modules into actor-critic networks, which partition the encoder output into L groups of V dimensions and apply a temperature-scaled softmax within each group, constraining features to a product of probability simplices Δ^{V-1}×⋯×Δ^{V-1}. This transformation is fully differentiable (avoiding the biased straight-through estimator required by Gumbel-Softmax or VQ-VAE), enforces boundedness via group-wise normalization, and induces sparsity through softmax competition at low temperature. The paper provides a formal analysis (Theorem 1) showing that non-stationary drift in bootstrapped targets increases neuron dormancy and deflates feature covariance, and that SEM's energy preservation (unit-mass blocks) and intra-block competition counteract this collapse. Empirically, applying SEM to the actor's penultimate layer yields the largest gains, as feature geometry most directly shapes policy gradients, while critic-only SEM provides more moderate improvements. The module adds negligible computational cost (a single softmax per group) and requires no auxiliary losses, ensembles, or additional networks.

## Results

The paper reports results primarily through learning curves (normalized return vs. environment steps) rather than a single summary table. Across 5 HumanoidBench tasks (6 seeds), SEM applied to the actor's penultimate layer (V=64, L=2) consistently accelerates early learning and improves asymptotic return for FastTD3, FastTD3-SimBaV2, and FastSAC. On the extended Atari-10 setup (28 ALE games, 3 seeds) and Isaac Gym (9 tasks), PPO+SEM similarly outperforms vanilla PPO in both convergence speed and final IQM return. SEM adds effectively zero wall-clock overhead (Table 2 in the paper) and outperforms Gumbel-ST, VQ-VAE, and C-RELU alternatives on the same HumanoidBench subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline FastTD3 (Seo et al., 2025), SEM-augmented variants win on both sample efficiency (faster convergence) and asymptotic performance across all 5 HumanoidBench ablation tasks and the full 28-task h1hand suite; the gains are largest when SEM is applied to the actor or both actor and critic, with V=64 being optimal. SEM also wins against FastTD3-SimBaV2 and FastSAC on HumanoidBench, confirming the benefit is algorithm-agnostic within the fast actor-critic family. On PPO, SEM improves both the pixel-based Atari ALE suite (28 games) and continuous-control Isaac Gym tasks, extending the benefit to on-policy methods. In the head-to-head comparison of structure-inducing methods (Fig. 7 left), SEM outperforms Gumbel-softmax with straight-through estimator, the Concrete distribution, Vector Quantization (VQ-VAE), and C-RELU, which the authors attribute to SEM's fully differentiable design avoiding the biased straight-through gradient. For offline-to-online RL, FQL+SEM improves sample efficiency and stability during the transition on OGBench tasks. The one notable exception is PQN (a value-based algorithm), where SEM does not yield consistent gains, suggesting the benefit is most pronounced in actor-critic settings with bootstrapped critics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Simplicial Embeddings (SEM; Lavoie et al., 2023), originally an architectural module for self-supervised representation learning, into the actor and critic networks of fast actor-critic RL algorithms (FastTD3, FastSAC, PPO). It positions itself as complementary to prior representation-learning-for-RL work (CURL, SPR, FOR SALE) that relies on auxiliary losses, and to model-based agents (TD-MPC2, Dreamer V2/V3) that use discrete latents shaped by world-model objectives. The key departure is that SEM imposes a geometric inductive bias (product-of-simplices constraint) with no auxiliary losses, no extra networks, and no biased gradient estimators like straight-through, while a formal analysis connects non-stationarity-induced representation collapse to the specific failure modes SEM addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEM offers a drop-in, zero-overhead architectural component that consistently improves sample efficiency and asymptotic performance across off-policy (FastTD3, FastSAC), on-policy (PPO), and offline-to-online (FQL) methods, making it a practical tool for practitioners already using fast actor-critic pipelines. The finding that the actor's penultimate layer benefits most from the geometric constraint is a useful design insight for where to place representation regularizers. Open questions include whether SEM generalizes to value-based methods (preliminary PQN results are inconsistent), whether adaptive schedules for (L, V, τ) can remove the need for manual tuning, and whether the approach extends to large-scale vision- or language-conditioned RL settings.
