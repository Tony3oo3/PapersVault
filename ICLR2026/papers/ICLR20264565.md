## Problem

In unsupervised domain adaptation, selecting the best source domain and pre-trained feature extractor among many candidates is critical but underexplored. Existing transferability estimation scores require target labels (inapplicable to UDA), exhaustive adaptation runs are prohibitively expensive, and symmetric distribution metrics like MMD fail to capture the asymmetric nature of domain transfer. The paper asks whether a lightweight, label-free score can predict target accuracy before any adaptation training is performed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264565/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled source dataset, an unlabeled target dataset, and a frozen pre-trained feature extractor (ResNet, DeiT, ViT, or Swin-Transformer). It produces a single scalar PAS score in [0,1] per (source, target, model) trio, computed purely from pre-trained embeddings without any adaptation training. In practice, the score is evaluated across all candidate source domains and pre-trained models, and the trio with the highest PAS is selected for subsequent domain adaptation.

## Method

PAS is an asymmetric, label-free score inspired by the Silhouette coefficient but adapted for unlabeled target samples under domain shift. It first computes per-class centroids in the pre-trained embedding space using the cosine-similarity centroid formulation of Dhillon & Modha (2001). For each target sample, it calculates cosine distances to all source class centroids, then takes the ratio (d₂ − d₁)/d₂ where d₁ and d₂ are the nearest and second-nearest centroid distances. The final score is the mean over all target samples. The key design choices—cosine distance over Euclidean, centroid distance over pairwise average, and the asymmetric (d₂−d₁)/d₂ normalization—were validated ablation-wise to maximize correlation with actual target accuracy, achieving a 0.88 Spearman correlation across four benchmarks.

## Results

PAS achieves an overall Spearman rank correlation of 0.88 and Pearson correlation of 0.83 with target accuracy across all four benchmarks (Office-Home, Office-31, ImageCLEF, DomainNet), approaching the Oracle upper bound of 0.91/0.88. Per-benchmark, PAS correlation ranges from 0.44 (ImageCLEF) to 0.76 (Office-Home) in Pearson, consistently outperforming MMD (−0.14 to 0.55) and A-distance (−0.13 to 0.32). PAS correctly identifies the best source domain in the majority of target-domain scenarios and also guides selection of the best pre-trained feature extractor (e.g., ViT-Base over ResNet-50 for A→C on Office-Home).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAS substantially outperforms both symmetric baselines on every benchmark. Against MMD (Gaussian kernel), PAS improves Pearson correlation from 0.37 to 0.83 overall; the gap is most dramatic on ImageCLEF (0.44 vs. −0.14) and DomainNet (0.53 vs. −0.09), where MMD actually shows a negative correlation with accuracy. Against A-distance (C-SVC based), PAS improves from 0.04 to 0.83 overall; A-distance is particularly unreliable, yielding near-zero or negative correlations on three of four benchmarks. PAS also approaches the Oracle baseline (which uses ground-truth target labels) with only a 0.05 Pearson gap (0.83 vs. 0.88) and 0.03 Spearman gap (0.88 vs. 0.91), validating that the cluster-distance formulation captures the right signal. The one scenario where PAS underperforms is the P (Pascal VOC) domain of ImageCLEF, where multi-object images cause high PAS scores despite low accuracy, as the nearest centroid corresponds to a co-occurring object rather than the true label.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Silhouette score (Rousseeuw, 1987) to the domain adaptation setting by replacing the supervised intra-cluster distance with the cosine distance from unlabeled target samples to source class centroids, and by using the nearest source cluster as a proxy for the true class. It departs from prior transferability estimation methods (H-score, NCE, LEEP, LogME) that require target labels and are therefore inapplicable to unsupervised domain adaptation. The paper also contrasts with symmetric distribution distance metrics (MMD, A-distance, Wasserstein) by arguing that domain adaptation is inherently asymmetric—transferring from an easy to a hard domain is harder than the reverse—and that a suitable score must reflect this asymmetry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAS offers a computationally cheap (linear in samples, no training required) way to select the best source domain and pre-trained backbone before committing to expensive domain adaptation runs, which is practically valuable when many candidates are available. However, the score's reliance on single-object class structure causes clear failures on multi-object benchmarks like ImageCLEF-P, and the method is limited to single-source, image classification tasks. Open questions include extending PAS to multi-source domain adaptation, other modalities, and scenarios where the pre-trained model's embedding space does not cleanly separate classes across domains.
