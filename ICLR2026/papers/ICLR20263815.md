## Problem

Prior automated data generation methods (X-Gen family) for robot manipulation fail to extend to multi-step bimanual mobile manipulation because a mobile base introduces a reachability problem (where to place the robot so arms can reach randomized objects) and an active camera introduces a visibility problem (ensuring task-relevant objects remain in view for visuomotor policy training). Naive replay of base trajectories from source demos renders manipulation infeasible under aggressive object randomization, and no prior method jointly addresses both constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263815/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 1–3 minute human teleoperated source demonstration (annotated with subtask boundaries, target objects, pregrasp timesteps, and retraction types) plus a randomized scene configuration (object poses, obstacles, distractors) and the robot's kinematic model. It outputs full-body trajectories spanning base pose, torso joints, both 7-DoF arm joints, and head camera pose, validated in simulation for collision-freeness and task success. The generated demonstrations are then used to train visuomotor policies (WB-VIMA with egocentric point clouds, or π₀ with RGB images from head and two wrist cameras plus proprioception) that output target joint positions.

## Method

MOMAGEN formulates demonstration generation as a constrained optimization problem with hard constraints (reachability, visibility during manipulation, kinematic feasibility, collision avoidance, task success) and soft constraints (visibility during navigation, retraction to compact configuration), unifying prior X-Gen methods as special cases with weaker constraint sets. For each subtask, it samples base and head-camera poses via inverse kinematics checks (using cuRobo) until reachability and visibility are simultaneously satisfied, then plans a base/torso trajectory with a soft visibility cost that biases the camera toward the target object during navigation. Contact-rich segments are replayed in task space by transforming end-effector poses from the source demo into the new object's frame, while free-space segments use full motion planning. The key architectural novelty is jointly optimizing over end-effector pose, head camera pose, and base pose rather than treating them independently, enabling aggressive domain randomization (D1/D2) that prior methods cannot handle.

## Results

MOMAGEN achieves a 63% average data generation success rate under D0 randomization and is the only method capable of generating data under D1 and D2 randomization (where baselines achieve 0% success due to infeasible replayed base poses). On object visibility, MOMAGEN achieves 75–100% task-relevant object visibility across all tasks and randomization levels, roughly double the baselines (e.g., 0.86 vs 0.40 on Tidy Table D0). For policy learning, MOMAGEN-generated data enables WB-VIMA to reach 0.40 success on Tidy Table (D0) where baselines overfit to replayed trajectories, and is the only method achieving non-zero success (0.25) on Pick Cup (D1). In sim-to-real transfer, π0 pretrained on 1,000 MOMAGEN demos achieves 60% real-world success vs 0% for the real-data-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOMAGEN is compared against SkillMimicGen (Garrett et al., 2024) and DexMimicGen (Jiang et al., 2025b), both extended with base trajectory replay from source demos. On D0 randomization, MOMAGEN matches or exceeds baselines on complex tasks (e.g., 0.80 vs 0.69/0.72 on Tidy Table; 0.51 vs 0.40/0.35 on Clean Frying Pan) but trails on the simplest task Pick Cup (0.86 vs 1.00), where the small randomization range makes replay sufficient. The critical advantage emerges at D1 and D2: both baselines achieve 0% generation success because replayed base poses cannot reach randomly placed objects, while MOMAGEN maintains 20–64% success by sampling novel base poses. For policy learning, MOMAGEN data yields substantially higher success on Tidy Table (D0) and is the only method achieving non-zero success on Pick Cup (D1), where baselines trained on D0 data fail completely. In sim-to-real, MOMAGEN pretraining lifts π0 from 0% to 60% real-world success and WB-VIMA from 0% to 10%, demonstrating that the diversity advantage translates to practical deployment benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOMAGEN extends the X-Gen family of automated data generation methods (MimicGen, SkillMimicGen, DexMimicGen) from static bimanual manipulation to the substantially harder setting of bimanual mobile manipulation, where a mobile base and active camera introduce reachability and visibility challenges absent in prior work. The paper's key conceptual contribution is formulating data generation as a constrained optimization problem with hard constraints (reachability, visibility during manipulation, collision-freeness) and soft constraints (visibility during navigation, retraction), providing a unified framework that subsumes all prior X-Gen methods as special cases with different constraint sets. Unlike prior approaches that replay base trajectories from source demos, MOMAGEN jointly plans base, torso, arm, and camera configurations via inverse kinematics and motion planning (cuRobo), enabling novel navigation behaviors rather than fixed replays.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to generate diverse, valid demonstrations for multi-step bimanual mobile manipulation from a single human demo, addressing a critical bottleneck in scaling teleoperation data for whole-body robot control. The visibility ablation reveals that enforcing object visibility during both navigation (soft) and manipulation (hard) is essential for downstream policy performance, with gaps of 0.35+ in success rate on Tidy Table when constraints are removed. Key limitations include the assumption of full scene knowledge (ground-truth object poses), restriction to alternating navigation-manipulation phases rather than continuous whole-body control, and modest sim-to-real success rates (10–60%) that still require 40 real demos for fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the framework extends to articulated-object manipulation and whole-body contact tasks in the real world without privileged state access.
