## Problem

Existing split conformal prediction (SCP) methods for question answering unrealistically assume that admissible answers for every instance can be obtained via finite sampling, even in open-ended QA where no fixed solution space exists. Prior work also fails to account for risk levels that are simply infeasible under a bounded sampling budget, leading to invalid prediction sets. SAFER addresses both gaps by introducing an abstention-aware sampling stage and a conformalized filtering stage that jointly control two-stage miscoverage risk.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an open-ended text question and a held-out calibration set of question-answer pairs (N samples, split 50/50 by default). At test time, it samples up to M candidate answers per question from the LLM using multinomial sampling at temperature 1.0 (generation length 36 tokens for TriviaQA/CoQA, 24 for ScienceQA), then outputs a filtered prediction set of admissible answers, a calibrated minimum sampling budget ŝ, a calibrated uncertainty threshold t̂, or an abstention decision if the target risk level α is infeasible within M samples. The final prediction set is guaranteed to cover at least one admissible answer with probability at least 1−(α+β−αβ).

## Method

SAFER is a two-stage risk control framework built on the learn-then-test (LTT) paradigm. In Stage I, it applies the Clopper-Pearson exact method on the calibration set to compute an upper confidence bound on the miscoverage rate at each candidate sampling budget s, then selects the minimum ŝ ∈ [1, M] whose bound falls below the user-specified risk level α; if no such budget exists, the method abstains. In Stage II, it restricts to calibration instances where at least one admissible answer appears within ŝ samples, computes a token-wise entropy uncertainty score U(ŷ) = Σₖ −log p(zₖ|ŷ<ₖ) for each candidate, and calibrates a threshold t̂ via the conformal risk control (CRC) framework at a second risk level β. The joint guarantee ensures the final prediction set's miscoverage rate is bounded by α + β − αβ with probability at least 1−δ over the calibration draw, while the filtering step compresses the candidate set (e.g., from ~7.9 to ~5.5 answers on TriviaQA at β=0.1) without violating the risk constraint.

## Results

SAFER achieves two-stage risk control on TriviaQA (α=0.05) and CoQA (α=0.25) across five LLMs, with test-time EER consistently below the theoretical upper bound α+β−αβ at every risk level β. On TriviaQA with β=0.05 (upper bound 0.0975), the best model (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct) achieves EER of 0.0771±0.0104, while on CoQA with β=0.05 (upper bound 0.2875), LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct achieves 0.2347±0.0122. The conformalized filtering stage compresses prediction sets substantially: on TriviaQA with LLaMA-3.1-8B at β=0.1, the average set size drops from 7.9 to 5.5 while maintaining valid risk control. SAFER also generalizes to black-box models (GPT-4o-mini) and multimodal reasoning (MMVet) with the same statistical guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is TRON (Wang et al., ICLR 2025), a prior two-stage risk control method for open-ended QA that calibrates sampling size and then applies conformal filtering. The key difference is that TRON assumes every instance can yield a correct answer via finite sampling, while SAFER introduces an abstention mechanism for cases where no admissible answer is found within the maximum sampling cap M. As shown in Figure 3, at α=0.03 on TriviaQA, TRON with OpenChat-3.5 produces a test-time EER exceeding 0.06 (well above the 0.03 target), whereas SAFER consistently keeps EER below α across all five LLMs and both datasets. This failure occurs because TRON's calibrated sampling budget does not account for calibration instances that should have been abstained from, leading to over-optimistic risk estimates at test time. SAFER resolves this by using the Clopper-Pearson exact method to derive a valid upper confidence bound on miscoverage at each sampling budget, and abstaining entirely if the bound cannot be satisfied within M samples. The paper does not report a head-to-head numerical comparison table between TRON and SAFER (the comparison is via Figure 3 plots), but the qualitative conclusion is clear: SAFER wins on risk control validity at low risk levels where TRON's assumption breaks down.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFER extends the split conformal prediction (SCP) and learn-then-test (LTT) frameworks to open-ended QA by addressing a critical assumption gap in prior methods like ConU and TRON: the unrealistic premise that every question yields an admissible answer within finite sampling. It introduces an abstention mechanism via the Clopper-Pearson exact method to calibrate a sampling budget under a user-specified risk level α, and then applies conformal risk control (CRC) with a second risk level β to filter unreliable candidates from the sampled set. The two-stage design composes the sampling-stage guarantee (α) and filtering-stage guarantee (β) into a joint miscoverage bound of α+β−αβ, building directly on the LTT paradigm and CRC framework while departing from TRON's assumption that all instances are solvable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The abstention-aware calibration makes conformal risk control practically deployable for open-ended QA, where models genuinely cannot answer every question within a bounded sampling budget—a limitation that prior methods silently ignore. The framework is model-agnostic (validated on five open-source LLMs and GPT-4o-mini) and metric-agnostic (sentence similarity, Rouge-L, NLI entailment, LLM-based judgment), broadening its applicability. However, it still relies on the data exchangeability assumption, so distribution shifts between calibration and test data remain an open challenge. Future work could explore adaptive or distribution-robust variants, and the framework's extension to multi-turn or streaming QA settings is unexplored.
