## Problem

Existing multimodal machine translation (MMT) methods rely on image-text pairs, which are scarce in multilingual settings—image-based MMT datasets cover only 11 languages, and image-free approaches using synthetic images still struggle with generalizability and multilingual coverage. This paper asks whether the speech modality, which naturally aligns with text and has abundant multilingual datasets, can serve as a scalable auxiliary modality for translation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261497/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts source text (average lengths: 59.3 chars on Multi30K, 130.4 on FLORES-200, 191.3 on WMT24++) and produces translated text in up to 28 target languages. At inference, the text is first converted to synthetic speech via CosyVoice2 TTS (cloning a reference voice), and both the original text and the generated speech waveform are jointly fed to the MLLM. The framework supports 108 translation directions from English, Japanese, Korean, and Chinese sources.

## Method

The SMT framework pairs a frozen Whisper-large-v3 speech encoder with a Q-Former + MLP adapter (~80.5M params) projecting speech features into a GemmaX2-28-9B LLM backbone (~10B total). Training follows a three-stage curriculum—ASR (speech-text alignment), S2TT (cross-lingual bridging), then SMT (joint speech-text translation)—with only LoRA adapters (r=16, ~8.9M params) trainable in the final stage. The key differentiator is a Self-Evolution Mechanism that iteratively: (1) synthesizes speech via TTS from S2TT training data, (2) scores each sample in MT-only vs. SMT mode using COMET, (3) retains only positive samples where speech improves translation (S2 > S1), and (4) fine-tunes the MLLM on those positives, repeating until evaluation COMET converges. Ablations on CoVoST-2 confirm synthetic speech performs comparably to authentic speech, validating the pipeline's reliance on TTS-generated audio.

## Results

On the Multi30K MMT benchmark, SMT-9B achieves a new SOTA average BLEU of 52.0, surpassing the best image-based model (IMAGE, 49.9) by 2.1 points. For eng→fra Test2016, SMT-9B reaches 67.0 BLEU / 90.0 COMET, and for eng→deu Test2016 it reaches 47.0 BLEU / 87.8 COMET. On FLORES-200, SMT-9B achieves SOTA spBLEU/COMET in 108 translation directions (e.g., eng→27: 40.4/89.5, jpn→27: 27.3/86.9, cmn→27: 28.3/87.4), outperforming much larger text-only LLMs such as DeepSeek-V3.1 (39.3/88.9) and Qwen3-Next-80B (34.5/86.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMT-9B (9B params) outperforms all four text-only LLM baselines on Multi30K, beating the strongest (DeepSeek-V3.1) by 2.8 BLEU on eng→deu Test2016 (47.0 vs. 44.2) and 11.7 BLEU on eng→fra Test2016 (67.0 vs. 55.3). Against the four authentic-image MMT models (Soul-Mix, ConsQA-MMT, RG-MMT-EDC, WRA-guided), SMT-9B leads by 2.2 BLEU on eng→fra Test2016 (67.0 vs. 64.8 for ConsQA-MMT) and by 2.8 BLEU on eng→deu Test2016 (47.0 vs. 44.2). Against the four synthetic-image MMT models, SMT-9B surpasses the best (IMAGE) on eng→deu Test2016 (47.0 vs. 45.3) and eng→ces Test2016 (41.4 vs. —), while IMAGE slightly edges ahead on eng→fra Test2016 (67.5 vs. 67.0). On FLORES-200, SMT-9B achieves SOTA in 108 of 108 evaluated directions, outperforming DeepSeek-V3.1 (40.4 vs. 39.3 spBLEU on eng→27) and Qwen3-Next-80B (40.4 vs. 34.5), despite being 1/67th the parameter count of DeepSeek-V3-671B. Ablation on CoVoST-2 confirms that synthetic speech performs comparably to (and sometimes slightly better than) authentic speech, with the gap being negligible (<1 BLEU).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal machine translation (MMT) paradigm, which has been dominated by image-based approaches (Multi30K, Soul-Mix, ConsQA-MMT) and image-free synthetic-image methods (VALHALLA, Bridge, IMAGE), by substituting the visual modality with speech. Architecturally, it follows the standard MLLM recipe (frozen Whisper encoder + Q-Former adapter + LLM backbone) established by BLIP-2 and SLAM-LLM, but introduces a three-stage curriculum (ASR → S2TT → SMT) and a self-evolution loop adapted from the LLM self-improvement literature (Tao et al., 2024) to iteratively refine the model using TTS-generated speech. The key conceptual departure is the 'modality-agnostic hypothesis' (Assumption 1), which argues that any semantically aligned modality can aid translation, thereby reframing MMT as a modality-selection problem rather than an image-specific one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By leveraging speech—which is naturally aligned with text and available for far more languages than paired image-text data—the framework scales MMT to 28 languages and 108 translation directions, a coverage that image-based MMT (11 languages) cannot match. The self-evolution mechanism reduces dependence on scarce human-annotated low-resource data, making the approach more practical for under-resourced settings. However, the system remains bounded by TTS language coverage, and the paper does not fully isolate whether gains come from prosodic cues specifically or from the mere addition of a redundant signal; the under-translation reduction (5.2%→3.5%) hints at a mechanistic explanation but is based on manual review of a limited sample. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-synthetic speech inputs in the wild and whether the self-evolution loop can be extended beyond COMET-scored positive/negative sampling.
