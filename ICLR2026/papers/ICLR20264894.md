## Problem

DETR-based detectors suffer from a query initialization problem: object queries start as zero embeddings without spatial priors, leading to slow convergence and unstable training. Existing denoising strategies (DN-DETR, DINO's CDN) add auxiliary training tasks but do not fundamentally reformulate how queries are generated. This paper asks whether object detection can be recast as a generative denoising diffusion process that produces query reference points directly from a Gaussian prior, eliminating the need for hand-designed initialization heuristics.

## Input / Output

The model takes standard RGB images and produces a set of 900 object predictions, each comprising a class label and a 4D bounding box (center x, center y, width, height) in normalized [0,1] coordinates. At inference, the model samples K=4 candidate reference points per query from a standard Gaussian at the final diffusion step and iteratively denoises them through the transformer decoder via a DDIM sampler, requiring only 3 decoder forward passes while the backbone and encoder are evaluated once.

## Method

DiffuDETR reformulates object detection as a conditional denoising diffusion process on the 4D reference points of object queries. During training, ground-truth reference points are corrupted with Gaussian noise at randomly sampled timesteps (T=100), and the transformer decoder—conditioned on multi-scale deformable attention-encoded image features and a sinusoidal timestep embedding—learns to predict the noise residual for denoising. At inference, a deterministic DDIM sampler with only 3 decoder evaluations generates object proposals from pure Gaussian noise, adding minimal computational overhead (~40 GFLOPs) over the baseline. Two variants are proposed: DiffuDETR built on Deformable DETR and DiffuDINO built on DINO with contrastive denoising queries (CDNs), both using a cosine noise scheduler and Gaussian noise distribution. The low dimensionality of the diffusion space (4D per query) is what enables efficient sampling with far fewer steps than image-generation diffusion models.

## Results

On COCO 2017 validation with ResNet-50, DiffuDINO achieves 51.9 mAP (50 epochs), surpassing its DINO baseline (50.9 mAP) by +1.0 and outperforming all other DETR variants including MR-DETR (51.4), Salience DETR (51.2), and Align-DETR (51.4). With ResNet-101, DiffuDINO reaches 51.2 mAP versus DINO's 50.0 mAP. On LVIS, DiffuDINO improves over DINO by +2.4 AP (28.9 vs. 26.5, R50), and on V3DET the gap widens to +8.3 AP with Swin-B (50.3 vs. 42.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffuDINO (51.9 AP) outperforms its direct baseline DINO (50.9 AP) by +1.0 on COCO-val with ResNet-50, and also beats DINO on LVIS (+2.4 AP) and V3DET (+2.2 AP R50, +8.3 AP Swin-B). Against other DETR variants, DiffuDINO surpasses MR-DETR (51.4), Salience DETR (51.2), Align-DETR (51.4), and MS-DETR (50.9) on COCO-val. DiffuDETR (50.2 AP) improves over Deformable DETR (48.2) by +2.0 and over DN-Deformable DETR (48.6) by +1.6, demonstrating that the diffusion denoising objective provides a stronger training signal than DN-DETR's denoising strategy. Both DiffuDETR and DiffuDINO substantially outperform prior diffusion-based detectors: DiffusionDet (46.8 AP) and Pix2Seq (43.2 AP), confirming the advantage of integrating diffusion into DETR-style query generation. With ResNet-101, DiffuDINO (51.2 AP) beats DINO (50.0) by +1.2, while DiffuAlignDETR (51.7) edges out Align-DETR (51.2) by +0.5 under the 1× schedule. The only comparison where the proposed method does not lead is DiffuDETR vs. DiffuAlignDETR/DiffuDINO on COCO-val, where the DINO-based variant consistently achieves higher AP due to its stronger CDN auxiliary task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies denoising diffusion (DDPM/DDIM) to the 4D reference-point space of DETR-style detectors, building directly on Deformable DETR's multi-scale deformable attention encoder and DINO's contrastive denoising queries. It departs from DN-DETR's auxiliary denoising task (which perturbs full GT boxes during training) and from DiffusionDet (which denoises proposal boxes in a Sparse R-CNN decoder) by treating the low-dimensional reference points themselves as the diffusion latent, enabling a generative query-initialization process. The DDIM deterministic sampler is repurposed for inference, requiring only 3 decoder forward passes rather than the 1000+ steps typical of image-generation diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a principled generative process over a very low-dimensional space (4D reference points) can replace ad-hoc query initialization heuristics in DETR variants, yielding consistent gains across COCO, LVIS, and V3DET with minimal inference overhead. The +8.3 AP jump on V3DET with Swin-B is the most striking result, though it may partly reflect baseline tuning rather than a fundamental advantage. Open questions include whether the diffusion formulation can be extended to higher-dimensional query representations (e.g., class embeddings or multi-scale features), whether it generalizes to open-vocabulary or video detection, and whether the gains persist under stricter compute-matched comparisons at scale.
