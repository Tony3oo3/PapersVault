## Problem

Diffusion-based large language models (dLLMs) possess architectural advantages over autoregressive models—global attention, parallel token generation, and iterative refinement—that are ideally suited for structured output generation, yet current open-source dLLMs fail to produce reliable schema-conformant outputs. Existing autoregressive approaches suffer from fundamental limitations (no lookahead, no backtracking, sequential constraint satisfaction), while semi-autoregressive dLLM implementations systematically undermine the very parallelism and global awareness that make diffusion models attractive. The paper asks whether dLLMs' innate global context awareness can be directly exploited to unlock next-level controllable structured generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user instruction/query, a structural specification (e.g., a JSON or XML schema), and a source document, and produces a schema-conformant structured output (e.g., valid JSON with correctly populated fields). The structural specification is compiled into a scaffold where invariant elements (brackets, delimiters, field names) are fixed and variable content positions are replaced with mask tokens; the scaffold is injected directly into the output context as the initial denoising state rather than being embedded in the instruction prompt. A null-token convention handles variable-length and optional fields, and a top-K remasking schedule (K = O/n) controls the number of denoising iterations, making the total computational budget tunable.

## Method

Self-adaptive Schema Scaffolding (S³) is a training-free inference-time method that initializes the dLLM's reverse denoising process from a partially structured scaffold rather than a fully masked sequence, effectively providing a warm-start that constrains the generation space while preserving semantic flexibility. The scaffold is compiled from the target schema by retaining all invariant structural tokens and replacing variable content slots with mask tokens; the self-adaptive extension introduces a null-token placeholder so the model can naturally represent absent or variable-length content without requiring fine-tuning or pre-specified field lengths. A top-K remasking strategy (remasking exactly O/n tokens per step) provides deterministic iteration control and outperforms both low-confidence and block-wise remasking. The paper proves (Theorem 4.1) that scaffold-guided denoising reduces expected reconstruction error by a factor proportional to scaffold coverage, and shows the approach reduces decoding complexity from O(L³) to O(nL²). Across WikiBio, MBPP, MultiWOZ, and FUNSD, S³ achieves near-perfect structural adherence (≈0.997 SV/FC/SC) with as few as 8 denoising steps while simultaneously lowering hallucination rates compared to both direct prompting and vanilla scaffolding.

## Results

On WikiBio (16 denoising steps), S³ achieves near-perfect structural adherence (SV/FC/SC = 0.997) compared to the LLaDA baseline (0.646/0.604/0.576) and the template-guided baseline (0.794/0.734/0.738). Content fidelity F1 reaches 0.128 for S³ versus 0.086 (baseline) and 0.110 (template), while hallucination rate drops to 0.331 from 0.403 (baseline) and 0.390 (template). Performance saturates at 16 steps, meaning S³ achieves these gains with only half the denoising budget of the 32-step setting. Cross-domain evaluation on MBPP, MultiWOZ, and FUNSD shows 15–35% absolute gains in structural metrics over the LLaDA baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LLaDA baseline (Nie et al., 2025), S³ achieves a dramatic structural adherence improvement: at 16 steps, SV jumps from 0.646 to 0.997 and SC from 0.576 to 0.997, while also improving F1 from 0.086 to 0.128 and reducing hallucination rate from 0.403 to 0.331. Against the SLOT template-guidance baseline (Wang et al., 2025), S³ wins on every metric at all step counts; at 16 steps the gap is SV 0.997 vs. 0.794, F1 0.128 vs. 0.110, and HR 0.331 vs. 0.390. The few-shot prompting baseline improves structural metrics modestly (SV 0.735 at 16 steps) but actually degrades precision and F1 relative to the raw baseline, and S³ outperforms it by a wide margin on all dimensions. Cross-architecture validation on Dream-7B (Ye et al., 2025b) confirms the gains are not model-specific: S³ lifts SV from 0.723 to 0.991 and F1 from 0.082 to 0.121 using identical hyperparameters. Notably, the vanilla (non-adaptive) schema scaffolding variant S² actually increases hallucination (HR 0.463–0.465) relative to the baseline, highlighting that the self-adaptive null-token mechanism in S³ is essential for faithfulness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaDA diffusion language model (Nie et al., 2025) and its iterative denoising mechanism, repurposing the global attention and parallel token prediction that distinguish dLLMs from autoregressive architectures for structured output generation. It departs from the dominant line of structured-generation work—grammar-constrained decoding (Koo et al., 2024), prompt-engineering heuristics, and planning-based two-stage strategies (Wang et al., 2025)—by arguing that these AR-centric approaches inherit fundamental architectural limitations (no lookahead, no backtracking, sequential commitment) that a diffusion model's multi-step reverse process can circumvent. The S3 method is a training-free, inference-time intervention: rather than modifying the model or the decoding algorithm, it pre-populates the denoising context with a structural scaffold and uses a null-token convention for variable-length fields, making it orthogonal to and composable with prior prompt-based or grammar-based techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that dLLMs, which have so far been evaluated primarily on open-ended instruction following and reasoning, can be made highly reliable for structured output with a simple scaffolding trick—near-perfect schema compliance at 8 denoising steps versus ~35% for direct prompting. However, the content fidelity scores remain low (F1 ≈ 0.13), and the vanilla scaffolding variant actually increases hallucination relative to the baseline, revealing a distributional-mismatch problem that the null-token fix only partially addresses. Open questions include whether the approach scales to deeply nested or highly variable schemas, whether the 'overthinking' degradation with more denoising steps is fundamental to diffusion LMs or an artifact of the current training objective, and whether the gains persist as dLLMs grow in scale and instruction-tuning quality. The proposed three-axis evaluation framework (structural adherence, content fidelity, faithfulness) is a useful contribution independent of the method itself.
