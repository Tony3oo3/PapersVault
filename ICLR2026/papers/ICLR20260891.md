## Problem

Extending pretrained Transformers to graph-structured data remains challenging because existing strategies either require graph-specific architectural modifications (Graph Transformers) or convert graphs into continuous embeddings that suffer from information loss. A principled, reversible, and deterministic method to tokenize arbitrary labeled graphs into discrete sequences compatible with off-the-shelf sequence models has been missing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260891/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled graph G=(V,E) with a labeling function L:V∪E→Σ mapping nodes and edges to symbols from a finite alphabet, and produces a compressed discrete token sequence S_T over a learned vocabulary V_T. The serialization emits an alternating node-edge-node sequence (e.g., C, -, C, =, O) that is then compressed by BPE to roughly 10% of its original length. The pipeline is fully reversible: applying the inverse BPE rules followed by the inverse serialization reconstructs the original graph up to isomorphism, preserving all topology and labels.

## Method

The framework composes two stages: (1) a structure-guided reversible serialization that traverses every edge via a frequency-guided Eulerian circuit (or Chinese Postman tour), where at each branching node the next edge is selected by the dataset-level frequency F(p) of the labeled edge pattern p=(L(u),L(e),L(v)), ensuring determinism and aligning common substructures into adjacent symbol pairs; (2) Byte Pair Encoding trained on the serialized corpus to iteratively merge the most frequent adjacent pairs into composite tokens, building a vocabulary of structurally meaningful graph fragments. This decouples graph representation from model architecture entirely: the resulting token sequence is fed to a standard, unmodified Transformer (BERT-Small or GTE-Base) with a [CLS] token for graph-level prediction, requiring no graph-specific attention or message-passing layers. The key insight is that the frequency-guided traversal makes BPE's greedy merging discover statistically salient substructures (e.g., functional groups in molecular graphs) rather than arbitrary symbol combinations.

## Results

GT+GTE (the proposed graph tokenizer paired with a GTE-Base Transformer) achieves state-of-the-art results on 7 of 9 main benchmarks, including 87.4 AUC on OGBG-molhiv (vs. 80.1 for GCN+), 93.6% accuracy on DBLP (vs. 89.6% for GCN+), and 0.071 MAE on QM9 (vs. 0.077 for GCN+). The smaller GT+BERT variant also outperforms most baselines, e.g., 82.6 AUC on molhiv and 93.2% on DBLP. BPE compression reduces serialized sequence lengths to ~10% of their original size, yielding a ~2.5× training speedup over GraphGPS on ZINC. The method loses to GCN+ on ZINC (0.131 vs. 0.116 MAE) and to Exphormer on COIL-DEL (89.6% vs. 91.5% accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GT+GTE decisively outperforms classic GNNs: GCN (Kipf 2016) and GIN (Chen 2019) trail by 7–17 AUC points on molhiv and 14–20 points on DBLP accuracy. Against the strongest GNN baseline GCN+ (Luo 2025), GT+GTE wins on 7 of 9 benchmarks (e.g., +7.3 AUC on molhiv, +4.0% on DBLP, −0.006 MAE on QM9) but loses on ZINC (0.131 vs. 0.116 MAE). Compared to the Graph Transformer GraphGPS (Rampašek 2022), GT+GTE leads by 8.9 AUC on molhiv and 22.0% on DBLP, while GraphGPS is competitive only on p-struct (0.251 vs. 0.242). The serialization-based GraphMamba (Wang 2024) is outperformed on all 9 benchmarks, with the largest gaps on molhiv (+6.2 AUC) and DBLP (+6.0%). In the extended appendix comparisons, GT+GTE surpasses Graph-ViT-MLPMixer (He 2023) on COIL-DEL and p-func, and substantially outperforms LLM-based Graph Foundation Models GraphGPT (Tang 2024) and LLaGA (Chen 2024), which perform near random on structure-centric tasks like COIL-DEL due to their reliance on textualized graph representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Byte Pair Encoding (originally a text compression algorithm) and classical graph traversal methods (Eulerian circuits via Hierholzer's algorithm, Chinese Postman Problem) into a unified graph tokenization pipeline. It departs from two dominant paradigms in graph learning: (1) architecture-specific approaches like Graph Transformers (GraphGPS, Graphormer) that embed attention directly into graph message passing, and (2) continuous-embedding approaches (GraphGPT, LLAGA, VQ-Graph) that project graph structure into pretrained model spaces. The key conceptual departure is treating graph serialization as a reversible, deterministic, frequency-guided process that produces discrete symbol sequences formally identical to text, thereby eliminating the need for any graph-specific architectural component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a clean discrete interface between graphs and the Transformer ecosystem, this work enables direct transfer of all sequence-modeling advances (longer context windows, FlashAttention, scaling laws, generative pre-training) to graph learning without architectural redesign. Key limitations remain: continuous-valued node/edge features require lossy quantization, BPE merging can obscure individual node/edge identities for fine-grained prediction tasks, and the CPP-based serialization is O(V³), limiting scalability. The most promising open direction is large-scale generative pre-training across domains, treating graphs from different fields as different 'languages' for a unified autoregressive model.
