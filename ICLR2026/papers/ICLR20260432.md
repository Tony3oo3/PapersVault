## Problem

Existing scientific knowledge graph construction methods fail on four interdependent challenges: they fragment long multi-word entities, collapse out-of-domain, model relations without respecting hierarchical structure, and produce logically inconsistent graphs containing cycles and shortcut edges. While general-purpose LLMs offer some adaptability, they are computationally prohibitive (10B+ parameters) and yield inconsistent accuracy on specialized hierarchical extraction tasks, leaving current KGs shallow and unreliable for downstream exploration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260432/cropped_page14_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes raw, unstructured scientific text (abstracts and paragraphs) and produces a globally consistent hierarchical knowledge graph comprising entity spans with precise boundaries and fine-grained relation triplets (head, relation, tail). Internally, HGNet operates on a latent probabilistic graph with three relation types (parent-of, peer-of, no-edge) used for structural regularization, while the final output is a standard classification over benchmark-specific relation types. The entire pipeline runs on a shared SciBERT encoder with ~300M total parameters, operating at max sequence length 512, making it orders of magnitude lighter than the LLM baselines it outperforms.

## Method

The two-stage framework first applies Z-NERD, which decomposes consecutive embedding change vectors into orthogonal sustaining and divergent components (Orthogonal Semantic Decomposition) to provide domain-invariant 'semantic turn' signals, then feeds these into a transformer with Multi-Scale TCQK attention where 1D convolutions of varying kernel sizes (1, 3, 5, 7) are applied to Q and K vectors across grouped heads to enforce n-gram-specialized attention. The second stage, HGNet, performs three-channel probabilistic message passing (parent-to-child, child-to-parent, peer-to-peer) over a latent entity graph, regularized by two structural losses: a Differentiable Hierarchy Loss that enforces acyclicity via the trace of a matrix exponential and penalizes shortcut edges skipping intermediate levels, and a Continuum Abstraction Field Loss that orients the Euclidean embedding space along a single learnable unit vector (the Abstraction Field Vector) using ranking, anchoring, and regression objectives to make abstraction level an intrinsic geometric property of each node's embedding.

## Results

On the standard scientific IE benchmarks, Z-NERD achieves 78.84% F1 on SciERC and 82.71% on SciER for NER, outperforming the previous SOTA (HGERE) by 2.92% and 1.52% respectively. For relation extraction, HGNet reaches 53.19% Rel+ F1 on SciERC and 65.38% on SciER, surpassing HGERE (43.86% / 58.47%) by 9.33% and 6.91%. In zero-shot settings on the new SPHERE benchmark, gains are substantially larger: 10.76% average NER improvement and 26.20% average RE improvement over the best supervised baseline. The full pipeline uses only ~293M parameters, matching the computational efficiency of specialized models while achieving foundation-model-level generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Z-NERD outperforms all supervised NER baselines: SciBERT (67.52%→78.84% on SciERC, +11.32%), PL-Marker (70.32%→78.84%, +8.52%), HGERE (75.92%→78.84%, +2.92%), and UniversalNER-7b (66.09%→78.84%, +12.75%). For relation extraction, HGNet dominates all supervised and GNN baselines on SciERC (53.19% vs. HGERE 43.86%, GAT 46.21%, GCN 45.62%) and SciER (65.38% vs. HGERE 58.47%, GAT 57.64%). Zero-shot LLM baselines (GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-oss-120b, LLaMA-3.3-70b, Qwen3-32b, LLaMA-3.1-8b) are dramatically outperformed, with HGNet achieving 53.19% vs. a best LLM score of 22.39% on SciERC — a 30.8-point gap. Against geometric baselines in Appendix A.8, HGNet (Euclidean+CAF) beats HGCN (hyperbolic, 45.82%) and Order-Embeddings (cone, 44.27%) on SciERC by 7.37% and 8.92% respectively. The PURE baseline (Zhong & Chen) is the weakest supervised RE method at 36.78% on SciERC, further highlighting the gap to HGNet's 53.19%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes and departs from three research threads: (1) scientific NER, where it extends transformer-based span models (PL-Marker, HGERE) with an architectural n-gram bias (Multi-Scale TCQK) and a domain-invariant feature (Orthogonal Semantic Decomposition) rather than relying on emergent contextual embeddings; (2) relation extraction, where it moves beyond sentence-level joint models and standard GNNs (GCN, GAT) by introducing a three-channel hierarchical message-passing scheme that explicitly separates parent, child, and peer information flow; and (3) geometric hierarchy representation, where it deliberately rejects hyperbolic embeddings (Poincaré, HGCN) in favor of a single learnable abstraction axis in Euclidean space enforced by the CAF loss. The framework is unified through a shared SciBERT encoder and joint end-to-end optimization, positioning itself as a lightweight (~300M parameter) alternative to billion-parameter LLMs for domain-specific KG construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of SPHERE (10K documents, 111K relations, four scientific domains with a globally consistent hierarchical scaffold) addresses a genuine data-scarcity bottleneck in scientific RE and enables meaningful zero-shot evaluation that prior benchmarks could not support. The practical impact is significant: a ~300M-parameter model matching or exceeding LLMs on specialized KG tasks while being orders of magnitude cheaper to deploy. However, key open questions remain: the SPHERE dataset is LLM-generated (even with a 96.5% entity precision audit), raising questions about whether the large zero-shot margins reflect true generalization or distributional alignment with the generator; the single-axis abstraction assumption may not hold for domains with multiple independent hierarchies; and the framework is text-only, leaving multimodal scientific content (figures, tables) unaddressed.
