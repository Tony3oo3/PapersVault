## Problem

Current referring expression comprehension methods treat the task as direct bounding box prediction, producing outputs that are neither interpretable nor trustworthy. They lack explicit reasoning traces that link decisions to specific image regions, and they cannot reliably abstain when no object in the scene matches the expression, leading to hallucinated detections. The paper asks whether reformulating referring as an explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning process over candidate objects can yield predictions that are both verifiable and resistant to hallucination.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image, a natural-language referring expression, and a set of indexed candidate bounding boxes (box hints) produced by Grounding DINO for the referred object category. Rather than regressing coordinates, the model selects from these hints, outputting a structured CoT trace enclosed in <think>... tags (planning → per-candidate action → summarization) followed by final box indices in JSON format inside <answer>...</answer> tags. The retrieval-based formulation means the model never generates free-form coordinates, which simplifies precise localization and grounds every reasoning step to a specific visual region.

## Method

Rex-Thinker is a two-stage retrieval-based MLLM built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B that decouples localization (handled by Grounding DINO) from semantic verification (handled by the MLLM). The MLLM performs structured CoT reasoning in three phases: Planning decomposes the expression into subgoals, Action evaluates each candidate box against the current subgoal using its indexed hint, and Summarization aggregates decisions into the final prediction. Training follows a DeepSeek-R1-style two-stage pipeline: first, supervised fine-tuning on the newly constructed HumanRef-CoT dataset (90,824 GPT-4o-annotated samples with planning-action-summarization traces) instills the reasoning format; second, GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a composite reward (0.9×F1 accuracy + 0.1×format compliance) refines reasoning paths and improves generalization. The F1 reward uses exact IoU=1 matching against ground-truth boxes to encourage selection directly from the provided hints, while the format reward enforces the <think>/<answer> structure.

## Results

On the in-domain HumanRef benchmark, Rex-Thinker-GRPO achieves an average DensityF1 of 83.5 (R=86.6, P=86.8), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art RexSeek-7B (DF1=82.3) and Molmo-7B-D (DF1=72.6). The model's key differentiator is its Rejection Score of 68.2, a 14.1-point improvement over RexSeek-7B (54.1) and a 13.8-point gain over its own non-CoT variant (53.5), demonstrating substantially reduced hallucination. On the out-of-domain RefCOCOg benchmark, the model trained solely on HumanRef-CoT already generalizes competitively in a zero-shot setting, with further GRPO fine-tuning on RefCOCOg yielding additional gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rex-Thinker-GRPO outperforms the previous in-domain SOTA, RexSeek-7B, by +0.7 Recall, +1.0 Precision, and +1.2 DF1, while dramatically improving the Rejection Score from 54.1 to 68.2 (+14.1), indicating far fewer hallucinated detections. Against the strongest general-purpose MLLM baseline, Molmo-7B-D, Rex-Thinker-GRPO leads by +8.9 Recall and +10.9 DF1, though Molmo slightly edges it on Rejection (68.6 vs. 68.2). The gap over direct-prediction MLLMs is substantial: Rex-Thinker-GRPO surpasses DeepSeek-VL2-small by +27.3 Recall and +24.0 DF1, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (its own base model) by +34.1 Recall and +27.3 DF1. Ablations confirm that the CoT supervision contributes +2.6 Recall and +13.8 Rejection over the non-CoT variant, while GRPO post-training adds a further +1.4 Recall and +1.2 DF1. On out-of-domain RefCOCOg, the zero-shot Rex-Thinker-CoT already matches or exceeds several supervised baselines, and GRPO fine-tuning on RefCOCOg pushes it to competitive SOTA levels without sacrificing the structured reasoning behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the retrieval-based REC paradigm (ChatRex, Groma) by inserting an explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning layer between candidate detection and final selection, drawing directly on the DeepSeek-R1 two-stage SFT-then-GRPO training recipe. It departs from prior CoT-in-MLLM work (LLaVA-CoT, LlamaV-o1) by grounding each reasoning step to a specific candidate bounding box via Set-of-Mark hints, making the trace visually verifiable rather than purely textual. The HumanRef-CoT dataset is the first large-scale CoT-annotated REC resource, generated by prompting GPT-4o with ground-truth answers as supervision and then filtering for logical consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two underexplored failure modes of REC models—lack of interpretability and inability to abstain—by showing that structured CoT supervision yields a 13.8-point gain in rejection score and that GRPO post-training further improves both accuracy and generalization. Key limitations include a ~6× inference speed penalty (6.68s vs 1.13s per image) and occasional reasoning-answer mismatches that the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include whether the planning-action-summarization scaffold generalizes beyond person-centric scenes, whether a consistency reward can close the reasoning-output gap, and whether the approach scales to denser, multi-category referring without combinatorial candidate explosion.
