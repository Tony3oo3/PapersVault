## Problem

LLM-as-a-Judge models rely on chain-of-thought reasoning to make evaluation decisions, yet prior training methods (prompting, SFT, offline DPO) do not directly optimize the quality of those reasoning traces. The paper asks how to use online reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards to teach judges to think critically before deciding, while simultaneously addressing the long-standing positional bias problem in pairwise evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260356/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In the pairwise setting, the judge receives an instruction x and two candidate responses (a, b), and outputs intermediate thought tokens t followed by a final verdict y indicating the preferred response; a score-based variant instead outputs real-valued scores s_a, s_b (0–10) whose higher value determines the verdict. In the pointwise setting, the judge receives x and a single response a and outputs t followed by a single score s. The multitask model unifies both formulations, and all variants are trained on 22K synthetic preference pairs (17K WildChat + 5K MATH) with both response orderings included in each batch.

## Method

J1 converts all judgment tasks—both verifiable (e.g., math) and non-verifiable (e.g., open-ended user prompts)—into a unified format with verifiable binary rewards by constructing synthetic preference pairs where the 'rejected' response is generated from a noisy perturbation of the original instruction. Training uses GRPO with a rule-based reward that assigns +1 for a correct verdict and an additional consistency bonus only when the model is correct under both response orderings, explicitly penalizing positional bias. A key design choice is training pointwise judges via distant supervision from pairwise labels: each preference pair is split into two independent pointwise samples, and the model is rewarded only if the relative score ordering matches the gold verdict, yielding inherently position-consistent judges without any pointwise annotations. The final multitask model jointly trains on both pairwise and pointwise data, combining the comparative context of pairwise judging with the position consistency of pointwise scoring. Experiments at 8B, 32B, and 70B scales (Llama-3.1-Instruct and Qwen3) show that the 32B multitask model achieves state-of-the-art results across five benchmarks, outperforming much larger reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1-671B and o3 on several of them while training on only 22K synthetic pairs.

## Results

J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask achieves the best overall score of 80.8 across five benchmarks (PPE, RewardBench, RM-Bench, JudgeBench, FollowBenchEval), outperforming the much larger DeepSeek-R1-671B (78.4) and OpenAI-o3 (77.4) while training only a 32B model on 22K synthetic preference pairs. On PPE Correctness, J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask reaches 76.8% accuracy, surpassing EvalPlanner-70B (70.2%) and DeepSeek-GRM-27B (59.8%) by 6.6 and 17.0 points respectively. On RewardBench, J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask scores 93.6, the highest among all generative reward models, beating the previous best (Skywork-Critic-70B at 93.3). J1-Llama-70B improves over its base Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct by 10.7 points overall (64.3→75.0), and J1-Llama-8B improves by 13.6 points (48.3→61.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask (80.8 overall) outperforms DeepSeek-R1-671B (78.4) on RewardBench (93.6 vs 90.6), RM-Bench (90.3 vs 88.6), and FollowBenchEval (77.1 vs 71.7), but trails slightly on PPE (71.8 vs 72.3) and JudgeBench (71.4 vs 68.9). Against OpenAI-o3 (77.4), J1 wins on RewardBench (93.6 vs 86.4), RM-Bench (90.3 vs 86.1), and FollowBenchEval (77.1 vs 66.8), but loses on JudgeBench (71.4 vs 75.7) and is roughly tied on PPE (71.8 vs 72.1). J1 dominates OpenAI-o1-mini (72.7) across all five benchmarks, with the largest gap on FollowBenchEval (77.1 vs 62.9, +14.2). Compared to same-data baselines, J1-Llama-70B (75.0) beats EvalPlanner-Llama-70B (73.2) trained with offline DPO on identical 22K synthetic pairs, validating the advantage of online RL over DPO. On PPE Correctness specifically, J1-Qwen-32B-MultiTask (76.8) surpasses DeepSeek-GRM-27B (59.8, trained on 237K pairs + 1270K SFT data) by 17 points and EvalPlanner-70B (70.2) by 6.6 points, while using only 22K synthetic pairs. J1-Llama-8B (59.2 on PPE Correctness) outperforms the larger Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B (54.7) and Armo-8B (61.2, trained on 1M pairs), demonstrating data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

J1 extends the LLM-as-a-Judge line of work by replacing prior prompting, SFT, and offline DPO approaches with online RL (GRPO) using verifiable rewards, directly optimizing chain-of-thought evaluation reasoning. It builds on the synthetic preference-pair generation pipeline from Wang et al. (2024a, 2024c) and the data strategy of EvalPlanner (Saha et al., 2025), but departs from these by using online RL rather than offline DPO, and introduces consistency-based rewards and pointwise judges trained solely from pairwise supervision. Concurrent RL-based judge models (DeepSeek-GRM, RM-R1, JudgeLRM, Reward Reasoning Model) share the RL-with-verifiable-rewards paradigm but rely on substantially more training data (127K–1.27M pairs) and do not address position bias or multitask unification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 32B judge trained on only 22K synthetic preference pairs can match or exceed 671B DeepSeek-R1 and o3 on several benchmarks suggests that targeted RL with verifiable rewards is far more data-efficient than scaling model size for evaluation tasks, potentially democratizing high-quality judge models. The pointwise-judge-from-pairwise-supervision trick and the multitask formulation offer practical recipes for building position-consistent, generalist judges without additional annotation. Open questions include whether the 22K synthetic data ceiling generalizes to truly open-ended or safety-critical evaluation, whether the consistency reward fully eliminates position bias at scale, and whether the approach transfers to multimodal or agentic evaluation settings.
