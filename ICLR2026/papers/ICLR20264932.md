## Problem

Modern language models rely on fixed subword tokenizers (e.g., BPE) that impose a static, non-learnable segmentation on all inputs, causing brittle behaviors in counting, arithmetic, and multilingual settings. Existing tokenizer-free alternatives either use heuristic chunking (fixed strides, word boundaries) that embeds strong inductive biases, or dynamic chunking via entropy/cosine-similarity thresholds that introduces uncertainty and lacks a principled mechanism for allocating computational resources. The paper asks whether segmentation can be framed as a learnable, information-theoretic optimization problem integrated directly into the model's forward pass.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw UTF-8 byte sequences (vocabulary of 258: 256 bytes + BOS/EOS) at a local sequence length of 8192 bytes, which is roughly 4× longer than equivalent BPE token sequences. Internally, the architecture compresses this to a global sequence of 3200 positions (Top-K selection) for deep modeling, then reconstructs back to 8192 for byte-level prediction. The output is a next-byte probability distribution over the 258-token vocabulary, evaluated via Bits-Per-Byte (BPB) normalization to enable fair comparison across tokenization schemes.

## Method

ByteFlow Net is a five-stage hierarchical architecture: (1) a local encoder of small pre-norm transformers with sliding-window attention and Canon layers (causal conv-1d, kernel 4) for efficient byte-level contextualization; (2) a downsampling module that computes the marginal lossy coding rate ΔR_t = R_ε(h_{1:t}) − R_ε(h_{1:t−1}) at each position and selects the Top-K positions with highest information gain as chunk boundaries, preserving a static computation graph; (3) a deep, wide global transformer with full causal attention operating on the compressed K-length sequence; (4) a multi-linear upsampling with B-bin position-specific projections and large residual from the local encoder; and (5) a symmetric decoder for next-byte prediction. The key insight is that the coding-rate criterion from rate-distortion theory (R_ε = ½ log det(I + d_local/ε² · h hᵀ)) naturally identifies semantically significant boundaries while preserving the geometric structure of the latent representation manifold, enabling the model to dynamically allocate its most expensive FLOPs to information-rich positions rather than redundant byte patterns.

## Results

ByteFlow Net achieves 50.89% average zero-shot accuracy at the 600M scale (50B tokens), surpassing the LLaMA BPE baseline (49.15%) and the strongest byte-level baseline AU-Net (49.38%). At 1.3B parameters (500B tokens), the gap widens to 63.19% vs. LLaMA's 60.15% and AU-Net's 60.59%. On the character-level CUTE benchmark, ByteFlow Net 1.3B scores 51.2, dramatically outperforming Llama3 (27.5, trained on 1T tokens) and Llama3.1 (20.0, trained on 16T tokens) despite using 20–32× less training data. The coding-rate chunking strategy also yields the lowest validation BPB (0.86) among all tested chunking strategies in ablation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ByteFlow Net outperforms all five baselines at both scales. Against the strongest tokenized baseline LLaMA (BPE), it gains +1.74 points at 600M (50.89 vs. 49.15) and +3.04 points at 1.3B (63.19 vs. 60.15), with the advantage growing with scale. Among byte-level models, it beats the strongest hierarchical baseline AU-Net by +1.51 points at 600M and +2.60 points at 1.3B, while pure byte-level models (LlamaByte, MambaByte, SpaceByte) trail by 2.5–3.5 points at 600M and 4.5–4.8 points at 1.3B. On the CUTE character-level benchmark, ByteFlow Net 1.3B (51.2) surpasses Llama3 trained on 1T tokens (27.5) and Llama3.1 trained on 16T tokens (20.0) by a wide margin, demonstrating that architectural design compensates for 20–32× less training data on orthographic tasks. In the chunking-strategy ablation (Table 4), the coding-rate criterion achieves the lowest BPB (0.86) and highest task accuracy (50.89%), beating word-boundary chunking (0.94 BPB, 49.38%), cosine similarity (0.92, 47.45%), entropy (0.91, 47.81%), and neural boundary (0.90, 47.13%) methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

ByteFlow Net sits at the intersection of two research threads: the lossy coding rate framework from rate-distortion theory (Ma et al., 2007; Cover, 1999), repurposed here as a dynamic chunking criterion rather than a data segmentation tool, and the Canon layer for efficient local token mixing (Allen-Zhu, 2025). It departs from prior tokenizer-free hierarchical models (MegaByte, SpaceByte, AU-Net) by replacing static heuristic chunking with an information-theoretic, input-adaptive boundary selection, and from concurrent dynamic-chunking work (Nawrot et al., BLT, H-Net) by grounding the decision in a principled compression objective rather than learned gates, entropy thresholds, or cosine similarity. The Top-K formulation preserves a static computation graph, addressing a practical limitation of threshold-based dynamic chunking that causes variable-length sequences and memory allocation issues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evidence that a principled, information-theoretic chunking criterion can consistently outperform both BPE-based transformers and other byte-level architectures at matched FLOPs, suggesting that end-to-end tokenizer-free modeling is not merely feasible but potentially superior. The t-SNE analysis linking coding-rate chunking to latent manifold preservation offers a mechanistic explanation for why this criterion works, which could guide future design. However, all experiments are at academic scale (600M–1.3B parameters, 50B–500B tokens), leaving open whether the advantage persists at frontier scale where BPE tokenizers benefit from massive pre-trained vocabularies and where the O(T·d) coding-rate computation may become a bottleneck. Generalization to multimodal inputs and the interaction with longer context windows remain unexplored.
