## Problem

Watermarking synthetic graphs generated by diffusion models remains unsolved: conventional post-editing approaches degrade graph quality and require NP-hard graph isomorphism (GI) or graph edit distance (GED) for verification, while recent sampling-time watermarking methods for images and tables fail on graphs because node indices can be arbitrarily permuted (N! representations) and the discrete adjacency matrix must be dequantized back to the continuous latent for detection. No existing framework provides polynomial-time, isomorphism-invariant watermark detection for graph diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The detection procedure takes a target binary adjacency matrix A' (which may be an isomorphic permutation of the original), a reference continuous latent G^0 (the dequantized generated graph before quantization), and the trained denoising model M. It outputs a binary SUCCESS/FAIL verdict based on whether at least N−k eigenvalues of the reconstructed noisy latent exceed the Wigner semicircle radius of 2. The method is non-blind: the reference graph G^0 is required for dequantization, and a hash-based signature of the key K = V_A^{-1} G^0 V_A enables authorship identification.

## Method

CheckWate embeds a (k,W)-checkerboard ensemble pattern into the noisy latent at sampling time, replacing k diagonal-block entries of the Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble with fixed values W_u, which pushes N−k eigenvalues into the 'blip' regime (magnitude ~√N·W_u/k) while preserving the bulk Wigner semicircle structure. Detection reverses the diffusion bridge via DBIM and performs approximate dequantization using the eigenvector permutation identity G^{0'} ≈ V_{A'} V_A^{-1} G^0 V_A V_{A'}^{-1}, which handles unknown node permutations in O(N³) without solving GI. A latent sparsification step then zeros out entries whose values are unlikely under the original GOE or Dirac-at-W distributions, preventing eigenvalue explosion and false positives under adversarial edge/node modifications. Theoretical guarantees bound the detectability gap (Theorem 3.1: proportional to W_u/k) and the dequantization reconstruction error (Theorem 3.3: governed by eigenvalue multiplicity).

## Results

CheckWate achieves state-of-the-art generative quality among watermarking methods, being best 10/20 and second-best 9/20 across four quality metrics (MMD for degree, clustering, orbits, spectrum) and V.U.N. ratio on Planar, Tree, SBM, and Proteins datasets. On Proteins, CheckWate dramatically outperforms all baselines (e.g., degree MMD 0.0473 vs. 0.4315 for None, 0.4358 for Gaussian Shading). For watermark detectability (Z-score), CheckWate achieves detectable scores (>10) on all four datasets with dequantization (67.6, 45.0, 86.8, 404.7), and remains detectable under isomorphism, edge addition/deletion, and node deletion attacks up to 20%, with a minimum Z-score of 9.7 (SBM, 20% node deletion).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CheckWate is compared against two adapted image-watermarking baselines (Gaussian Shading, TreeRing) and one graph-specific baseline (Bipartite). On generative quality, CheckWate matches or exceeds Gaussian Shading on most metrics and dramatically outperforms TreeRing (up to 10× lower MMD) and Bipartite (which yields 0% valid graphs on SBM). On detectability without attacks, CheckWate and Gaussian Shading achieve comparable Z-scores, while TreeRing is essentially undetectable (Z < 2). Under graph-specific attacks, the gap widens sharply: Gaussian Shading drops to Z = 0 under isomorphism and never exceeds 5.3 under any attack, TreeRing never reaches a positive Z-score, and Bipartite—while maintaining high Z-scores—sacrifices all generative quality. CheckWate uniquely remains detectable (Z > 10) under isomorphism and all edge/node modification attacks up to 20%, confirming its graph-invariance and robustness advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

CheckWate sits at the intersection of two research threads: sampling-time watermarking for diffusion models (Gaussian Shading, TreeRing) and random matrix theory (checkerboard ensembles, Wigner semicircle law). It departs from prior sampling-time watermarks by embedding the pattern in the eigenvalue spectrum of the noisy latent rather than in entry positions, which is what makes it isomorphism-invariant and avoids the NP-hard Graph Isomorphism problem that plagues all prior graph watermarking approaches (Zhao et al., Eppstein et al.). The dequantization mechanism (Theorem 3.2) is a novel algebraic construction that leverages eigenvector permutation properties to invert the discrete-to-continuous quantization step in O(N³), something no prior graph watermarking method achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to enable polynomial-time, isomorphism-invariant watermarking of synthetic graphs from diffusion models, filling a gap that has persisted because GI and GED are computationally intractable. Key limitations include: the watermark is non-blind (requires the original generated graph for verification), it only applies to continuous-latent diffusion models like GruM (not discrete models like DiGress), and forgery resistance relies on the hardness of dequantization rather than a cryptographic proof. Open questions include extending the checkerboard idea to discrete noisy latents (the authors sketch a community-structure approach), achieving blind verification, and providing provable forgery resistance.
