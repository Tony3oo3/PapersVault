## Problem

Test-time prompt tuning (TPT) for vision-language models improves zero-shot accuracy but produces poorly calibrated, overconfident predictions. Existing calibration approaches—Average Textual Feature Dispersion (C-TPT) and orthogonality constraints (O-TPT)—fail to guarantee uniform angular separation among class-wise text features, particularly when the number of classes exceeds the embedding dimension (N > |D|) or underutilizes the hyperspherical space when N < |D|, leaving a critical gap in angular diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262349/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single unlabeled test image and a predefined list of class names (e.g., 1000 for ImageNet, 10 for EuroSAT). Prompts are initialized from hard prompt templates (e.g., 'a photo of a [CLS]') and tuned via one gradient step at inference time. The output is a class label with a calibrated confidence score, where the softmax probabilities over all N classes reflect true accuracy rather than inflated overconfidence. No labeled training or validation data is required at any stage.

## Method

A-TPT introduces an angular diversity (AD) regularizer that maximizes the minimum pairwise angular distance between normalized textual features on the unit hypersphere, inspired by the Tammes best-packing problem. Concretely, it computes the pairwise angular distance matrix θ = arccos(ÊÊᵀ) from the L2-normalized text feature matrix, then minimizes −(1/N)Σᵢ minⱼ θᵢⱼ as an auxiliary loss added to the standard TPT entropy-minimization objective (p* = argmin L_TPT + λ·L_A-TPT, with λ=80). Unlike O-TPT's orthogonality loss whose gradient vanishes as θ→0, A-TPT's gradient norm is angle-independent (1/‖eᵢ‖), providing stable optimization even when features are near-collinear. This mechanism naturally handles both N > |D| (where full orthogonality is impossible) and N < |D| (where orthogonality underutilizes the hypersphere), achieving uniform feature distribution and substantially lower ECE across fine-grained, distribution-shift, and medical benchmarks.

## Results

On fine-grained classification (11 datasets, CLIP ViT-B/16), A-TPT achieves an average ECE of 2.61, reducing it from 11.60 (TPT), 5.13 (C-TPT), and 4.23 (O-TPT), while maintaining comparable accuracy (64.92% vs. 65.00% for TPT). On natural distribution shifts (ImageNet-V2/A/R/Sketch), A-TPT lowers average ECE to 3.92 (ViT-B/16) and 7.82 (RN50), surpassing even the zero-shot CLIP baseline (5.04 and 7.46 respectively) in calibration. On medical datasets, A-TPT reduces ECE from 0.2328 to 0.0794 on ISIC 2018 (FPT) and from 15.64 to 2.34 on Covid (BAPLe, N=10). The method incurs negligible computational overhead (1.058 s/batch vs. 1.055 for C-TPT) with the same O(N²·|D|) asymptotic complexity as O-TPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A-TPT consistently outperforms all calibration-focused TPT baselines on ECE across both N>|D| and N<|D| regimes. Against TPT (Shu et al., 2022), A-TPT reduces average ECE from 11.60 to 2.61 (ViT-B/16) and 11.70 to 2.92 (RN50) on fine-grained tasks, a ~4× improvement, while preserving TPT's accuracy gains (64.92% vs. 65.00%). Against C-TPT (Yoon et al., 2024), which uses L2 dispersion, A-TPT halves the ECE (2.61 vs. 5.13 on ViT-B/16), with the largest gains on EuroSAT (3.92 vs. 13.20) and DTD (4.76 vs. 11.90). Against O-TPT (Sharifdeen et al., 2025), which enforces orthogonality, A-TPT achieves a 1.6-point ECE reduction (2.61 vs. 4.23), particularly excelling where N>|D| (ImageNet: 1.45 vs. 1.96) and N<|D| (EuroSAT: 3.92 vs. 12.98). Notably, A-TPT surpasses the zero-shot CLIP baseline in calibration (ECE 2.61 vs. 4.43) while retaining TPT's accuracy boost, a feat no other method achieves. On medical datasets, A-TPT reduces ECE by 66% on ISIC 2018 (0.0794 vs. 0.2328 for FPT) and by 85% on Covid N=10 (2.34 vs. 15.64 for BAPLe). When combined with supervised prompt embeddings (CoOp/CoCoOp), A-TPT reduces average ECE to 3.63 and 3.22 respectively, outperforming C-TPT+CoOp (10.04) and O-TPT+CoOp (7.91).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time prompt tuning (TPT) framework of Shu et al. (2022) by introducing an angular diversity regularization term inspired by the Tammes best-packing problem and ArcFace's angular margin loss. It departs from two immediate predecessors in TPT calibration: C-TPT (Yoon et al., 2024), which disperses textual features via L2 distance from their centroid, and O-TPT (Sharifdeen et al., 2025), which enforces pairwise orthogonality. The key conceptual departure is that A-TPT directly maximizes the minimum pairwise angular distance on the unit hypersphere rather than relying on L2 dispersion or cosine-similarity-based orthogonality, drawing on Wang & Isola's (2020) observation that uniformity on the hypersphere preserves maximal information in contrastive representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Calibration of VLMs is critical for safety-critical deployments (medical diagnostics, autonomous driving) where overconfident predictions can be dangerous, and this paper demonstrates that a simple angular diversity regularizer substantially reduces ECE across fine-grained, distribution-shift, and medical benchmarks while maintaining accuracy. The gradient analysis revealing that O-TPT's gradient vanishes as pairwise angles approach zero (making optimization stall) while A-TPT's gradient remains angle-independent provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why orthogonality constraints underperform. Open questions include whether the fixed λ=80.0 hyperparameter can be adapted per-sample without labeled data, whether angular diversity generalizes to multi-modal or generative VLM settings, and whether the slight accuracy trade-off (1–2% on semantically overlapping datasets) is acceptable in all deployment contexts.
