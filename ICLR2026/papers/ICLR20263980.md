## Problem

Prior work on minimizing polarization and disagreement in the Friedkin-Johnsen opinion dynamics model assumes full knowledge of all agents' innate opinions, an assumption that is unrealistic in practice. This paper addresses the more challenging online setting where innate opinions are unknown and unqueryable, and must be learned through sequential scalar observations after each intervention on the network structure. The gap is bridged by formulating the problem as a stochastic low-rank matrix bandit, establishing a formal connection between algorithmic social media interventions and multi-armed bandit theory.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a connected undirected graph G=(V,E,w) with a finite set of admissible Laplacian interventions L={L_1,...,L_K}, each defining a forest matrix X_i=(I+L_i)^{-1}. At each round, the learner selects one intervention and receives only a single noisy scalar Y_t = <X_t, ss^T> + η_t (the equilibrium polarization plus disagreement plus Gaussian noise), with no access to individual agent opinions or vector-valued feedback. The output is a sequence of selected interventions over T rounds, evaluated by cumulative regret relative to the best fixed intervention in hindsight. The action space is discrete, symmetric, and induced by graph Laplacians (forest matrices), which do not span R^{|V|²}—a structural constraint that rules out standard continuous-sampling bandit analyses.

## Method

The proposed algorithm OPD-Min-ESTR follows an explore-subspace-then-refine paradigm tailored to the rank-one structure of the unknown parameter Θ*=ss^T. In Stage 1 (T_1 exploration rounds), arms are sampled uniformly and a nuclear-norm regularized least-squares estimator recovers Θ*; the key theoretical contribution is proving that the Restricted Strong Convexity (RSC) condition holds for the specific structured action set of forest matrices under uniform sampling, without requiring the continuous exploration distributions assumed by prior low-rank bandit work. In Stage 2, the top eigenvector of the estimator is extracted to define a rotation, each arm is bilinearly rotated into this basis, and the bottom-right block (orthogonal to the estimated subspace) is discarded, reducing the ambient dimension from |V|² to 2|V|-1. A standard linear bandit algorithm (OFUL) is then run in this compact representation for the remaining T-T_1 rounds. The resulting regret bound is Õ(max{1/κ, √|V|}·√(|V|·T)), where κ measures the diversity of the intervention set along the error cone, improving over the Õ(|V|²√T) scaling of a direct linear bandit reduction.

## Results

Across Erdős-Rényi and Stochastic Block Model graphs with |V| ∈ {8, 16}, OPD-Min-ESTR consistently achieves lower cumulative regret and faster runtime than the full-dimensional OFUL baseline over T = 10,000 rounds (100 independent runs). At |V| = 16, the advantage becomes particularly pronounced, with OFUL suffering substantially higher regret and significantly slower execution. OPD-Min closely tracks the subspace oracle (which has access to the true parameter Θ*), effectively closing the initial gap from subspace estimation. The theoretical guarantee is Õ(max{1/κ, √|V|} · √(|V|·T)), where κ measures the diversity of feasible interventions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares OPD-Min-ESTR against two baselines: (1) full-dimensional OFUL operating directly in the |V|²-dimensional vectorized space, and (2) a subspace oracle that has access to the true rank-one parameter Θ* = ss⊤. OPD-Min-ESTR wins decisively against full-dimensional OFUL on both cumulative regret and runtime across all tested configurations (|V| ∈ {8, 16}, |X| ∈ {10, 100, 1000}, σ_η ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1.0}), with the gap widening as |V| increases. Against the subspace oracle, OPD-Min-ESTR closely tracks performance, indicating that the nuclear-norm regularized subspace estimation in Stage 1 recovers the relevant direction with minimal additional regret. The key practical advantage is computational: by reducing the ambient dimension from |V|² to 2|V|−1, OPD-Min-ESTR avoids the quadratic scaling that makes full-dimensional OFUL infeasible for larger networks (the paper demonstrates scalability up to |V| = 1024 in the appendix).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: the Friedkin-Johnsen opinion dynamics literature (Musco et al., 2018; Zhu et al., 2021; Cinus et al., 2023) and low-rank matrix bandits (Lu et al., 2021; Kang et al., 2022). Prior polarization/disagreement minimization work assumes full knowledge of agents' innate opinions in an offline, one-shot setting, while existing low-rank bandit algorithms assume continuous or isotropic action spaces that admit favorable exploration distributions. The key departure is a novel Restricted Strong Convexity analysis for the highly structured, discrete action set of forest matrices (inverses of I + Laplacian), which existing bandit frameworks cannot handle directly due to their reliance on Gaussian sampling or optimal experimental design with O(|V|⁶) cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of sequential opinion-dynamics interventions under incomplete information, establishing a concrete theoretical link between social media platform interventions and multi-armed bandit regret minimization. The regret bound scaling with |V| rather than |V|² demonstrates that the rank-one structure of the innate opinion outer product is exploitable, but the global RSC curvature parameter κ_min(𝒳) may be overly pessimistic for rank-one recovery, leaving room for tighter problem-specific guarantees. Open questions include extending beyond scalar equilibrium feedback to richer signals (e.g., community-level polarization), handling non-stationary opinion dynamics, and validating the framework on real platform data where interventions are constrained by privacy and governance requirements.
