## Problem

Prevailing methods for training LLMs as text encoders rely on contrastive losses (e.g., InfoNCE) that treat the model as a black-box function, forcing inherently generative models to output static embeddings via simple pooling. This paradigm suppresses the model's capacity for structured reasoning and natural language generation, rendering the embedding process opaque—when an embedding model judges two texts similar, there is no inspectable trace of why. The paper asks whether contrastive signals can be repurposed as rewards that guide a generative policy, thereby unifying representation learning with interpretable generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and a set of documents (one positive, multiple negatives) as input, each prepended with a representation instruction via a prompting function. The LLM first generates a free-form natural language rationale (up to 2048 tokens) analyzing salient semantic features and relations for each input; the embedding is then extracted by masked mean pooling over the last-layer hidden states of the concatenated instruction+rationale sequence, explicitly excluding system-prompt tokens. The dual output is both the human-readable rationale and the resulting d-dimensional embedding vector, with a maximum prompt length of 1024 tokens and right-side truncation for over-length sequences.

## Method

GRACE reconceptualizes contrastive learning objectives as reward signals for a generative policy π_θ rather than losses to minimize via gradient descent. The policy generates K stochastic rationales for the positive document (asymmetric rollout: single sample for query and negatives), and the composite reward combines three terms: a contrastive reward (query-positive cosine similarity minus sum of query-negative similarities), a consistency reward encouraging agreement among the K positive rollouts, and a hard-negative mining reward that penalizes similarity to the most confusing distractors from other instances in the batch. Training uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), computing advantages relative to the group mean (without standard deviation normalization) and updating the policy via advantage-weighted log-likelihood on the positive-document rationales. An unsupervised variant replaces query-positive pairs with self-alignment between an anchor rollout and K stochastic rollouts of the same text, mirroring SimCSE's dropout-positive structure. The framework is agnostic to the specific policy-gradient algorithm and has been validated with GRPO, REINFORCE++, ReMax, and DAPO.

## Results

On the MTEB benchmark (56 datasets, 7 task categories), GRACE achieves an average score of 56.64 with Qwen3-4B-Instruct in the supervised setting, improving 11.52% over the untrained base model (45.49) and 2.30 points over standard contrastive learning (54.34). In the unsupervised setting, GRACE reaches 51.03 (Qwen3-4B), a 6.85% gain over base and outperforming SimCSE (50.11). Gains are broadest on retrieval (e.g., 52.11 vs. 48.66 for CL on Qwen3-4B) and pair classification (82.94 vs. 78.81), while STS and summarization also improve consistently across all four backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the supervised setting, GRACE outperforms standard InfoNCE contrastive learning (Chen et al.) on all four backbones, with the largest margin on Qwen2.5-3B (+2.64 avg) and Qwen3-4B (+2.30 avg), driven primarily by retrieval and pair classification. In the unsupervised setting, GRACE beats SimCSE (Gao et al.) by 0.9–1.8 points across backbones (e.g., 51.03 vs. 50.11 on Qwen3-4B), and surpasses both LLM2Vec (48.84) and Echo (49.02) with its best unsupervised configurations. GRACE also comfortably exceeds encoder-only baselines BERT (38.33) and RoBERTa (37.86) in the unsupervised comparison. Critically, while CL fine-tuning catastrophically destroys general capabilities (e.g., Qwen3-4B drops from 60.73 to 8.51 on GSM8K/MMLU/TriviaQA/FEVER/BBH/HumanEval average), GRACE preserves them with near-zero degradation (+0.40), confirming that the RL-based reward formulation avoids the representation collapse inherent in direct InfoNCE optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRACE repurposes the contrastive learning objective (InfoNCE, van den Oord et al. 2018) from a loss to be minimized into a reward signal that guides a generative policy via GRPO (Shao et al. 2024), drawing on the RL-for-reasoning paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1. The unsupervised variant extends SimCSE's (Gao et al. 2021) dropout-positive paradigm by replacing the static encoder with a rationale-generating policy. Conceptually, the paper departs from the LLM-as-encoder line (LLM2Vec, Echo, E5) that treats the model as a black-box function producing pooled vectors, instead making the LLM an active agent whose natural-language rationales are the intermediate representation from which embeddings are derived.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that standard InfoNCE contrastive fine-tuning catastrophically destroys general capabilities (GSM8K, MMLU, HumanEval all drop to ~0%), while GRACE's reward-based optimization preserves them nearly exactly — suggesting that the standard contrastive training paradigm is fundamentally incompatible with maintaining generative competence in decoder-only LLMs. The interpretability benefit (human-readable rationales explaining semantic judgments) addresses a long-standing opacity problem in embedding models. Open questions include the significant inference latency cost of generation (dominating end-to-end time), whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, and whether the rationale quality genuinely improves retrieval beyond what a well-tuned static encoder achieves at matched compute.
