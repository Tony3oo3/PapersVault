## Problem

Existing System 2 Thinking approaches (e.g., RL-trained reasoning models like R1) are limited to verifiable domains such as math and coding, require rule-based rewards or additional supervision, and are modality-specific. The paper asks whether deliberate, verification-based reasoning can emerge purely from unsupervised learning across arbitrary modalities and problem types, without human or reward supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264722/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For language modeling, the model takes a text context and iteratively refines a continuous-valued prediction vector (initialized from N(0,I)) toward the next token, outputting a discrete token after energy convergence. For image denoising, it takes a noisy 224×224 image encoded to 3136-dim VAE features and produces a denoised image through energy minimization. For video, it takes all previous frames and predicts the next frame in continuous VAE space. A key structural difference from standard transformers is that predictions are made in the input space (the same space as the conditioning context), enabling the model to assign a single energy scalar to each input–prediction pair that serves as an explicit verification signal.

## Method

EBTs are Transformer architectures (causal decoder-only for autoregressive tasks, bidirectional for image/video) trained as Energy-Based Models that assign a scalar energy E_θ(x, ŷ) to every input–prediction pair, where lower energy indicates higher compatibility. Training uses an optimization-based objective rather than contrastive learning: starting from a random prediction, the model performs M gradient descent steps on the energy landscape, and the final prediction is compared to ground truth via a standard loss backpropagated through the entire optimization trajectory (requiring Hessian-vector products computed in linear time). Three energy landscape regularization techniques—replay buffer, Langevin Dynamics noise injection, and randomized step size/step count—shape the landscape to have well-defined local minima around the data manifold. At inference, System 2 Thinking is realized through two mechanisms: (1) dynamic compute allocation via variable numbers of optimization steps per prediction, and (2) self-verification by generating N candidate predictions and selecting the one with minimum energy (a per-token Best-of-N that generalizes to both discrete and continuous modalities).

## Results

On language modeling (RedPajamaV2 pretraining), EBTs achieve up to 35% higher scaling rates than the Transformer++ recipe across data, batch size, depth, parameters, FLOPs, and width. At inference, EBTs improve perplexity by up to 29% through System 2 Thinking (extended optimization + self-verification), while the Transformer++ shows no improvement with additional compute. On image denoising, EBTs outperform DiTs in PSNR (27.25 vs 26.58 in-distribution; 23.29 vs 19.56 OOD) while requiring 99% fewer forward passes, and achieve ~10× higher ImageNet-1k linear probe accuracy (5.32% vs 0.31% top-1). On Sudoku OOD generalization, EBTs score 29.7% accuracy versus 17.7% for RNNs and 0.03% for feed-forward Transformers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Transformer++ recipe (Touvron et al., 2023), EBTs win on pretraining scaling rate (up to 35% faster across all six scaling axes) and on inference-time thinking (up to 29% perplexity improvement vs. zero improvement for Transformer++). On downstream generalization, EBTs beat Transformer++ on GSM8K (43.3 vs 49.6 perplexity), BigBench Math QA (72.6 vs 79.8), and BigBench Dyck (125.3 vs 131.5), but lose slightly on SQuAD (53.1 vs 52.3) and pretraining perplexity (33.43 vs 31.36). Against DiTs (Peebles & Xie, 2023), EBTs clearly win on image denoising PSNR in both in-distribution (27.25 vs 26.58) and OOD (23.29 vs 19.56) settings while using only 1% of the forward passes, and achieve roughly 10× higher ImageNet-1k linear probe accuracy (5.32% vs 0.31% top-1). Against the RNN baseline (Jolicoeur-Martineau, 2025) on Sudoku OOD generalization, EBTs achieve 29.7% accuracy versus 17.7% for the RNN and 0.03% for a feed-forward Transformer, demonstrating superior algorithmic reasoning under data constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the optimization-based EBM training framework of Du et al. (2022) and Wang et al. (2023), which reframes EBM learning as gradient descent on predictions rather than contrastive energy minimization, and on the implicit generation formulation of Du & Mordatch (2019). It departs from prior EBM work by introducing a Transformer backbone (EBT) with practical stabilization techniques (replay buffer, Langevin dynamics, randomized step sizes) to address the long-standing scalability and training instability problems of EBMs. Conceptually, it positions itself against RL-based reasoning models (O1, R1) that require verifiable rewards, arguing that a learned energy verifier can enable System 2 Thinking purely from unsupervised pretraining across modalities, and against Diffusion Transformers by showing that explicit prediction verification (not just iterative denoising) is the key architectural distinction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that EBTs out-scale the Transformer++ recipe by up to 35% across all measured axes and improve inference-time performance by up to 29%, while also generalizing better to OOD data despite slightly worse pretraining perplexity—suggesting that the verifier-centric formulation captures something qualitatively different from feed-forward generation. However, the evidence is limited to models up to 800M parameters and the authors acknowledge a significant FLOP-efficiency gap versus standard Transformers, making near-term adoption challenging. Key open questions include whether the scaling advantages persist at foundation-model scale (the authors project ~1000× more data for Llama-3-scale), whether the FLOP inefficiency can be closed, and how to handle highly multimodal distributions where the current single-minimum energy landscape produces blurry outputs.
