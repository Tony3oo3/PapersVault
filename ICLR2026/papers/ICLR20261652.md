## Problem

Long-tailed semi-supervised learning suffers from pseudo-label bias toward majority classes, yet the mechanisms by which class imbalance accumulates into systematic classifier bias during training remain poorly understood. Existing debiasing methods (logit adjustment, reweighting, resampling) lack a unified theoretical explanation for why they work, preventing principled design of new remedies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long-tailed labeled set (images with class labels), an unlabeled set (images without labels, potentially with a different or unknown imbalance ratio), and a task-irrelevant baseline image (a solid-color image, e.g., black) whose logits serve as a running bias indicator. It outputs standard class predictions. The baseline image is not a training input in the usual sense; rather, its forward-pass logits are tracked throughout training to calibrate per-class pruning ratios, making the method agnostic to the exact pixel value of the baseline.

## Method

The paper derives a per-step decomposition of logit updates in FixMatch-style SSL, showing that class imbalance dominates predictions through the eNTK kernel and gradient terms, and proves that the logits of a solid-color baseline image converge to the class prior under cross-entropy training. This yields a unified lens under which logit adjustment, reweighting, and resampling all reshape the gradient flow (modifying the gradient term, scaling the kernel, or changing the sampling measure, respectively). Building on this, DyTrim introduces a dynamic pruning framework: for labeled data, it computes class-aware pruning ratios directly from the baseline image logits (retaining a fraction r_c = π_θ(I)_c of each class's lowest-loss samples), and for unlabeled data, it applies a label-agnostic soft pruning that randomly discards low-confidence, inconsistent samples with gradient rescaling. The method is plug-and-play, requiring no architectural changes and no knowledge of the unlabeled imbalance ratio.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT with FixMatch as the base SSL algorithm, DyTrim achieves the best balanced accuracy (bACC) and geometric mean (GM) across all imbalance ratios: 88.0/87.8 at γ=50, 84.8/84.4 at γ=100, and 82.0/81.3 at γ=150, outperforming the prior SOTA CDMAD by 1.2% bACC and 1.4% GM on average. On the large-scale ImageNet-LT benchmark, DyTrim reaches 37.2% bACC versus 35.4% for CDMAD and 20.0% for vanilla FixMatch. Under the more challenging inconsistent setting (γ_l=100, γ_u unknown), DyTrim still leads with 86.4/86.0 bACC/GM at γ_u=50, surpassing CDMAD by 0.7% bACC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyTrim consistently outperforms all 13 compared baselines across CIFAR-10-LT, CIFAR-100-LT, STL-10-LT, and ImageNet-LT. Against the closest competitor CDMAD (Lee & Kim, CVPR 2024), DyTrim gains +1.2% bACC and +1.4% GM on average over γ∈{50,100,150} on CIFAR-10-LT, and +1.8% bACC on ImageNet-LT (37.2 vs 35.4). Compared to CoSSL (Fan et al., CVPR 2022), DyTrim leads by 1.6% bACC at γ=100 (84.8 vs 83.2); versus ABC (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2021), the gap is 3.7% bACC (84.8 vs 81.1). On CIFAR-100-LT (γ=100), DyTrim achieves 44.8% bACC, beating CDMAD (44.1%) and UDAL (43.7%). Under the inconsistent imbalance setting (γ_l≠γ_u, γ_u unknown), DyTrim maintains its lead over CDMAD by 0.7–2.7% bACC depending on dataset and ratio, and when plugged into FlexMatch or FreeMatch, it boosts bACC/GM by 2–3% over their vanilla versions. DyTrim also serves as a plug-in: adding it to CCL (Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2024) yields +0.5% bACC on CIFAR-10-LT (γ=100) and +0.6% on CIFAR-100-LT (γ=20), confirming its complementarity with existing debiasing strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the per-step learning dynamics framework of Ren & Sutherland (2025) from supervised to semi-supervised learning, deriving a decomposition that separates supervised and consistency contributions to logit updates. It builds on CDMAD's (Lee & Kim, 2024) empirical use of a baseline image as a bias indicator by providing a theoretical justification (invariance under affine normalization, convergence to class prior) and incorporating it into the eNTK-based influence decomposition. Unlike prior debiasing methods that modify the loss (logit adjustment, reweighting) or the sampling distribution (resampling), the paper positions its proposed DyTrim as a sample-level intervention that directly gates which examples participate in each gradient step, drawing on Infobatch's (Qin et al., 2024) randomized pruning mechanism for the unlabeled data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical characterization of how class imbalance accumulates into systematic bias through the recursive interaction of pseudo-labels and classifier updates provides a principled explanation for why naive SSL fails under long-tailed distributions, and unifies seemingly distinct debiasing strategies under a single gradient-dynamics lens. DyTrim's plug-and-play nature and consistent gains across FixMatch/FlexMatch/FreeMatch, CNN/ViT backbones, and both consistent and inconsistent imbalance settings make it practically useful. However, the eNTK-based analysis relies on first-order Taylor approximations that may degrade for deep networks or large learning rates, and the theoretical guarantees for DyTrim's bias reduction are stated at a high level without tight quantitative bounds. Open questions include whether the baseline-image indicator remains informative in non-visual modalities or under distribution shift, and whether the class-aware pruning ratio r_c = π_θ(I)_c is optimal or merely a heuristic proxy for the true class prior.
