## Problem

Existing MLLM video benchmarks focus on low- to mid-level visual perception (what happens in the video) using curated or synthetic questions, neglecting how humans actually think, feel, and linguistically respond to user-generated content on social platforms. No prior benchmark evaluates whether MLLMs can simulate user-like cognition—inferring creator intent, interpreting viewer feedback, or generating human-like comments—in authentic video contexts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260622/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video clip (durations range from 1 s to >180 s, sourced from TikTok and YouTube) plus a natural-language task instruction. The spatial branch uniformly samples 8 frames while the temporal branch processes the full frame sequence; all frames are center-cropped and resized to 448×448 before encoding. Outputs are either a four-way multiple-choice letter (for the seven structured reasoning tasks) or a free-form comment string (for the comment imitation task), both produced under a single unified decoding objective.

## Method

MIMIC-Chat pairs a dual-branch TimeSformer-based spatiotemporal encoder with InternLM2-Chat-8B as the language backbone. The spatial branch (8 sampled frames) and temporal branch (full sequence) each pass through a dedicated MLP projector, and their token streams are fused into the LM via gated integration; LoRA adapters on the attention sublayers enable parameter-efficient instruction tuning while the vision backbone stays frozen. Training uses supervised instruction tuning on 150K+ MIMIC-Data samples with a standard cross-entropy LM loss that unifies classification and generation under one objective, with multi-task batching across all seven reasoning subtasks and the comment generation task. The benchmark itself comprises 4,000 high-engagement videos with a three-axis thinking suite (Creator Intent Understanding, Content Attribute Matching, User Interaction Understanding) and a novel comment imitation task where model-generated comments are blindly judged by human annotors for origin and realism (0–5 scale).

## Results

On MIMIC-Bench's seven structured reasoning tasks, MIMIC-Chat achieves 74.1% overall accuracy, surpassing all open-source MLLMs (best: InternVL3-78B at 67.5%, Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 66.7%) and even exceeding the human baseline (73.1%), while trailing only Gemini-2.5-Pro (75.1%) and o3 (74.6%). On the comment imitation task, MIMIC-Chat achieves 64.24% 'judged as human' rate and a mean realism score of 2.88, more than doubling the next-best proprietary model (o3: 43.36%, 2.11) and substantially outperforming all open-source baselines (best: InternVL3-78B at 30.21%, 1.65). The largest remaining gap is on Comment Popularity (CoP), where MIMIC-Chat scores 43.6% versus 51.1% for humans, highlighting the difficulty of modeling collective user preferences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIMIC-Chat (74.1% overall) outperforms all 21 open-source MLLMs on the thinking tasks, with the largest margins over early models like VideoChatGPT (24.1%) and Video-LLaVA (31.6%), and a clear 6.6-point lead over the strongest open-source baselines InternVL3-78B (67.5%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (66.7%). Against proprietary models, MIMIC-Chat narrowly trails Gemini-2.5-Pro (75.1%) and o3 (74.6%) on thinking accuracy but decisively beats both on the comment imitation task (64.24% vs. 41.87% and 43.36% 'judged as human'), and also surpasses ChatGPT-4o on both axes (74.1% vs. 68.2% thinking; 64.24% vs. 40.32% mimicking). On the mimicking task specifically, MIMIC-Chat more than doubles the human-likeness rate of the best open-source model (InternVL3-78B: 30.21%) and nearly triples that of mid-tier models like Qwen2.5-VL-72B (26.93%) and InternVideo2.5 (12.67%). The only comparison where MIMIC-Chat underperforms is against the human baseline on comment imitation (64.24% vs. 87.57% judged as human), while it actually exceeds humans on overall thinking accuracy (74.1% vs. 73.1%), primarily driven by stronger performance on Title Selection (90.4% vs. 85.1%) and Tag Matching (86.7% vs. 78.7%). Fine-tuning the same open-source backbones (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL2.5-8B, InternVideo2.5-8B) on MIMIC-Data yields modest gains (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 59.9→66.4%) but none approach MIMIC-Chat's 74.1%, confirming that the architectural design (dual-branch spatiotemporal encoding + LoRA) contributes beyond data access alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of video understanding benchmarks (MVBench, Video-MME, EgoSchema) that evaluate 'what happens in the video' through perceptual and factual QA, by introducing a human-aligned evaluation axis centered on how creators frame content and how viewers cognitively and linguistically respond to it. The MIMIC-Chat model repurposes existing components—TimeSformer for spatiotemporal encoding, InternLM-8B as the language backbone, and LoRA for parameter-efficient adaptation—into a unified pipeline for both structured reasoning and open-ended comment generation, but the task formulation itself (user-like thinking across CIU/CAM/UIU axes, and comment imitation) is presented as absent from prior benchmarking efforts. The closest prior work, EmoLLM, touches on affective reasoning but does not address the full scope of social interaction simulation, comment generation, or multi-source metadata reasoning that MIMIC-Bench targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a clear and persistent gap in current MLLMs: even frontier proprietary models (Gemini 2.5 Pro, o3) score only ~43-45% on Comment Popularity versus 51% for humans, suggesting that modeling collective user preferences and sociocultural cues remains fundamentally unsolved. The comment imitation task introduces a reusable evaluation protocol (blind human judgment + realism scoring) that could generalize to other open-ended generation settings beyond video. Key open questions include whether the engagement-based video selection introduces residual bias (partially addressed via OOD evaluation), whether the human-evaluation bottleneck for comment imitation can be scaled with LLM-assisted metrics, and whether the 8B MIMIC-Chat's advantage over 72B/78B baselines reflects a genuine architectural insight or is specific to this narrow task distribution.
