## Problem

Fully modality-shared autoregressive transformers trained jointly on text and image data suffer from severe gradient conflicts, particularly in shallow and deep layers, due to fundamentally different low-level statistical properties of the two modalities. This paper is the first to formally identify and quantify this inter-modality gradient conflict in Unified Multimodal Models, showing it hinders convergence and degrades performance across both understanding and generation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264198/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Images are encoded as 512×512 pixels into a 32×32 grid of 1024 discrete VQ tokens (from an 8,192-entry codebook via VQGAN), while text uses standard BPE tokenization. Token sequences follow a fixed structure: <BOI>[Image]<EOI>[Text]<BOS> for understanding and [Text]<BOI>[Image]<EOI><BOS> for generation, with a binary mask identifying visual token positions at every layer. Outputs include 512×512 generated images (CFG=4.0), text captions, text answers, and edited images, all produced via standard next-token autoregressive decoding.

## Method

Uni-X adopts a two-end-separated, middle-shared (X-shaped) architecture: the first N layers and last M layers are duplicated into parallel text-specific and vision-specific branches that process their respective tokens in strict isolation, while the intermediate layers remain fully shared for high-level semantic fusion. The key empirical insight is that gradient conflicts are most severe at the two ends (where low-level statistics diverge—image tokens exhibit far higher conditional entropy than natural language) but diminish in middle layers where representations become abstract and semantically aligned. Forward propagation partitions hidden states via a binary vision mask; in separated layers each modality computes independently (reducing attention complexity from O(n²) to O(a²+b²)), while in shared layers all tokens pass through common parameters. The optimal configuration uses 9 shallow and 5 deep separated layers out of 28 total, trained with standard next-token cross-entropy loss over the unified vocabulary, requiring no auxiliary semantic encoders, hybrid diffusion heads, or task-specific branches.

## Results

Uni-X (3B active / 4.5B total parameters) achieves a GenEval score of 82 (with prompt rewriting) and DPG-Bench 79.8 for image generation, while scoring 67.1 average across five text benchmarks (MMLU 57.6, ARC-E 79.0, WinoGrande 68.9, BoolQ 82.2, ARC-C 47.9). On multimodal understanding, it reaches MME 1158.3, POPE 83.6, MMBench 59.3, and SEEDBench 60.2. With expanded training data (240B tokens), Uni-X improves to GenEval 83, DPG 80.3, MME 1228.2, and MMBench 62.7, matching or surpassing 7B–14B AR-based UMMs trained on up to 5.1T tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among pure AR models without a semantic encoder, Uni-X (3B/4.5B) substantially outperforms EMU3 (8B, GenEval 66) and Liquid (7B, GenEval 68) by 14–16 points on GenEval, while using fewer parameters and less training data (140B vs. ~90B tokens). It also surpasses Janus-Pro (7B, GenEval 80) by 2 points and matches BLIP3o (4B/9B, GenEval 81) with fewer active parameters. On text, Uni-X's 3B model achieves an average of 67.1 across five benchmarks, exceeding Chameleon (7B, 65.3) and Liquid (7B, 66.9). Uni-X trails Bagel (7B/14B, GenEval 88) and X-Omni (10B/20B, GenEval 83), but both rely on AR+diffusion hybrids and 1–5T tokens of training data. On image editing (ImgEdit-Bench), Uni-X scores 3.44 overall, beating Bagel (3.20) despite using fewer parameters and less data, though it remains below GPT-4o (4.20). On multimodal understanding (MMBench, SEEDBench), Uni-X is competitive among encoder-free AR models but lags behind models that incorporate a separate semantic image encoder (e.g., Janus-Pro MMB 79.2 vs. Uni-X 59.3), which the authors attribute to underutilization of the VQ codebook.

## Relation to Prior Work

Uni-X builds directly on the autoregressive unified multimodal model (AR-UMM) paradigm established by Chameleon and Liquid, which treat visual tokens as a 'foreign language' via VQGAN tokenization within a shared transformer. It departs from the dominant trend of increasing architectural complexity—Mixture-of-Transformers branches, hybrid AR-diffusion pipelines, and task-specific deep heads (UniFork, MoT)—by instead proposing a minimal structural modification: duplicating only the shallow and deep layers into modality-specific branches while keeping the middle layers shared. The conceptual motivation is borrowed from multi-task learning's gradient conflict literature (Yu et al., 2020; Shi et al., 2023), which the authors are the first to systematically transfer to the UMM setting, providing a principled, layer-wise diagnostic for where and why text-vision conflicts arise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 3B-parameter model with a simple architectural tweak can match or surpass 7B AR-based UMMs on generation and understanding benchmarks, offering a practical path to parameter-efficient unified multimodal modeling. The gradient conflict metric and its layer-wise analysis provide a reusable diagnostic tool for diagnosing modality interference in any shared-parameter multimodal system. Key limitations include weaker vision understanding compared to models with dedicated semantic encoders, underutilization of the VQ codebook (~3,127 of 8,192 tokens), and evaluation restricted to non-interleaved inputs. Open questions include whether the optimal 9:5 shallow-deep ratio generalizes across model scales and base architectures, and whether the approach extends naturally to interleaved or video modalities.
