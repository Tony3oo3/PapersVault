## Problem

Existing approaches to accelerate diffusion transformer training either introduce an external representation task (e.g., MAE, IBOT) or depend on a large-scale pre-trained representation encoder (e.g., DINOv2, CLIP) to provide guidance. This paper asks whether the diffusion transformer's own internal representations—empirically shown to progress from coarse to fine across layers and noise levels—can supply such guidance without any external component.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260645/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on noise latents extracted by the Stable Diffusion VAE (32×32×4 for 256×256 images), conditioned on class labels (ImageNet) or text prompts (COCO2014), and produces generated images at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. Unusually, the training signal is self-generated: the student model's intermediate-layer features at a given noise level are aligned against the EMA teacher's deeper-layer features at a slightly lower noise level, so no external features or labels are consumed beyond the standard generative objective.

## Method

SRA introduces a self-representation alignment loss that minimizes the patch-wise smooth-ℓ1 distance between the student's latent output at an earlier layer (e.g., layer 3) conditioned on noise level t and the EMA teacher's output at a later layer (e.g., layer 8) conditioned on noise level t−k, where k is sampled from [0, 0.2). The teacher is an exponential moving average (α=0.9999) of the student weights, updated every step, and a stop-gradient prevents backpropagation through the teacher. A lightweight two-layer MLP projection head with SiLU activations transforms the student's features before alignment, enabling a soft, nonlinear mapping that avoids disrupting the original generative field. The total loss is L_gen + λ·L_sa with λ=0.2, and the projection head can be discarded after training, leaving the diffusion transformer architecture unchanged. The key insight is that the teacher's deeper layers at lower noise already encode richer semantic representations, so aligning the student's shallower, noisier features toward them provides progressive, self-contained representation supervision throughout training.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with classifier-free guidance, SiT+SRA achieves FID 1.58 and IS 311.4 at 800 epochs, outperforming the original SiT-XL/2 (FID 2.06) with 600 fewer epochs. At just 400 epochs, SRA already reaches FID 1.85, surpassing the 1400-epoch baseline. On ImageNet 512×512, SiT+SRA at 200 epochs achieves FID 2.17 and IS 279.3, beating the 600-epoch SiT-XL/2 baseline (FID 2.62). On COCO2014 text-to-image, MMDiT+SRA improves FID from 5.86 to 4.85 and PickScore from 20.05 to 21.14.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRA substantially outperforms all baselines that rely on external representation tasks: at 400 epochs it beats MaskDiT (FID 2.28 at 1600 epochs), SD-DiT (FID 3.23 at 480 epochs), and DiT+TREAD (FID 1.69 at 740 epochs). Against the strongest external-encoder baseline SiT+REPA (FID 1.42 at 800 epochs), SRA (FID 1.58) is slightly behind on FID but wins on sFID (4.65 vs 4.70) and IS (311.4 vs 305.7), while REPA edges ahead on Recall (0.65 vs 0.63). SRA also surpasses SiT+MAETok (FID 1.67) at the same 800-epoch budget. On ImageNet 512×512, SRA at 200 epochs (FID 2.17) beats REPA at 200 epochs (FID 2.08) on sFID (4.15 vs 4.19), IS (279.3 vs 274.6), and Recall (0.59 vs 0.58), while REPA retains a small FID advantage. On COCO2014 text-to-image, MMDiT+SRA (FID 4.85, PickScore 21.14) is slightly behind MMDiT+REPA on FID (4.60) but leads on PickScore (21.14 vs 20.88), confirming SRA's transferability to text-conditioned generation without any hyperparameter tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DiT and SiT as base diffusion transformer architectures and departs from two established paradigms for representation-guided training: (1) methods that inject an external discriminative task (MaskDiT, SD-DiT, TREAD) and (2) methods that align with a large-scale pretrained encoder like DINOv2 (REPA, MAETok). SRA instead exploits the 'bad-to-good' discriminative process inherent in diffusion transformers themselves, using an EMA teacher to align earlier-layer/higher-noise features to later-layer/lower-noise features. The EMA-teacher mechanism borrows from self-supervised learning (BYOL, DINO), but the specific alignment objective—cross-layer, cross-timestep self-alignment during generative training—is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SRA removes the practical dependency on a powerful external pretrained encoder (e.g., DINOv2), which is critical for domains where no strong open-domain encoder exists (e.g., video generation). The finding that SRA provides sustained improvement throughout training while REPA saturates after ~200 epochs suggests a fundamentally different scaling behavior that could matter for long training runs. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-video pretraining (acknowledged limitation), whether the benefit persists when scaling to much larger data distributions beyond ImageNet/COCO, and whether the EMA teacher's slowly improving guidance can be further accelerated.
