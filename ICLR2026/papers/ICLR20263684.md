## Problem

Existing sequential post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for LLMs—whether layer-wise, block-wise, or multi-block-wise—treat all layers equally with the same quantization strategy. The paper empirically demonstrates that this uniform treatment is suboptimal under challenging low-bit settings (e.g., W4A4), as shallow and deep layers (especially the first and last) are significantly more sensitive to quantization error than intermediate layers, and quantization errors accumulate progressively across sequentially quantized layers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained high-precision LLM and a small calibration set (128 samples × 2048 tokens by default, drawn from WikiText2 or C4) and produces a quantized low-precision LLM. All learnable parameters introduced during quantization (channel-wise scaling vectors and rank-4 LoRA matrices) are absorbed into the original weights after quantization, introducing zero extra inference-time cost. The SliderQuant+ variant additionally applies non-absorbable Hadamard rotation transformations after Q/K projections and before O/Down projections, incurring a small fixed inference overhead.

## Method

SliderQuant replaces the uniform sequential quantization schedule with an adaptive sliding-window framework built from two coupled components. The inter-layer component assigns three distinct sliding-window designs along the depth of the model: a progressively expanded window (PESW) over the first L_s=4 shallow layers (window grows from 1 to L_s, keeping the first layer as a persistent anchor), a fixed-size window {s=2, i=1} over intermediate layers, and a progressively contracted window (PCSW) over the last L_d=4 deep layers (window shrinks to 1, keeping the last layer as anchor), with one overlapped layer at each transition to ensure smooth relay. The intra-layer component then, within each inter-layer window, jointly quantizes all s layers incrementally in N=2 stages (γ=0.5), first quantizing the first half of weight/activation dimensions then the full set, building local-to-global parameter synergy. At each layer, quantization is facilitated by learnable channel-wise scaling factors (to redistribute outlier magnitudes between activations and weights) and rank-4 LoRA matrices (to refine weights), optimized via AdamW to minimize the MSE between high-precision and quantized window outputs on calibration data.

## Results

Under the most challenging W4A4 weight-activation quantization, SliderQuant achieves 8.34/11.10 perplexity on WikiText2/C4 for Llama2-7B, substantially outperforming CBQ (12.73/14.45) and OmniQuant (14.26/18.02). On Llama3-8B under W4A4, SliderQuant reaches 15.47/21.74 while OmniQuant degrades to 150/140 and CBQ to 35.97/32.64. For zero-shot commonsense reasoning under W4A4, SliderQuant attains 56.77% average accuracy on Llama2-13B and 58.96% on Qwen2.5-14B, surpassing CBQ (54.67%/53.50%) and OmniQuant (52.71%/48.55%). With rotation transformations (SliderQuant+), the method further leads FlatQuant and SpinQuant on average across Llama2-7B/13B/70B and Llama3-8B under W4A4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SliderQuant consistently outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every setting. Among weight-only methods without extra inference cost (RTN, GPTQ, AWQ, QuIP), SliderQuant achieves the lowest perplexity at W4A16 and W2A16, e.g., 5.61 vs. GPTQ's 5.83 and AWQ's 6.15 on Llama2-7B WikiText2 at W4A16. Among weight-activation methods without extra cost (SmoothQuant, OmniQuant, AffineQuant, CBQ), the gains are most dramatic under W4A4: SliderQuant reduces Llama2-7B WikiText2 perplexity from 83.12 (SmoothQuant) and 14.26 (OmniQuant) to 8.34, and Llama3-8B from ~150 (OmniQuant) to 15.47. Against rotation-based methods with extra inference cost (QLLM, Atom, DuQuant, QuaRot, SpinQuant, FlatQuant), SliderQuant+ achieves the best average accuracy on all tested models under W4A4, e.g., 71.22% vs. FlatQuant's 70.38% on Llama2-13B and 76.32% vs. 76.02% on Llama2-70B. Against mixed-precision methods (LLM-MQ, SpQR, QUIK), SliderQuant matches or exceeds their accuracy at equal or lower effective bit-widths, e.g., achieving 76.90% average on Llama2-13B at W4A16 vs. LLM-MQ's 70.90% at W4A16 with 0.5% FP16 outliers.

## Relation to Prior Work

SliderQuant extends the sequential quantization framework that underlies layer-wise (GPTQ), block-wise (OmniQuant), and multi-block-wise (CBQ, SKVQ) PTQ methods by replacing their uniform treatment of all layers with an adaptive sliding-window schedule. The key conceptual departure is the empirical observation that shallow and deep layers—especially the first and last—are disproportionately sensitive to quantization, motivating progressively expanding/contracting windows at the boundaries and a fixed-size window in the middle. The method also incorporates channel scaling and LoRA (borrowed from OmniQuant and prior outlier-suppression work) as learnable parameters within each window, and optionally adds Hadamard rotation transformations (à la QuaRot/SpinQuant) for a higher-accuracy variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, drop-in PTQ framework that consistently outperforms strong baselines (CBQ, OmniQuant, FlatQuant, SpinQuant) across Llama/Llama2/Llama3/Qwen2.5/Qwen3 families, MoE models, and DeepSeek-R1 reasoning models under aggressive W4A4 and W2A16 settings, without requiring architectural modifications in its default mode. The empirical characterization of per-layer quantization sensitivity is a useful diagnostic that could inform future quantization-aware training or mixed-precision strategies. Open questions include whether the fixed L_s=L_d=4 default is optimal for very deep or very shallow architectures, whether the approach extends to non-Transformer backbones, and whether the quantization-time overhead (sliding windows require more optimization passes than layer-wise methods) becomes prohibitive at extreme scale.
