## Problem

Existing Explain-then-Detect systems for hateful memes (CoT prompting, LMM agents) perform worse than simple SFT baselines on binary detection, and even GRPO post-training fails to close the gap. The paper identifies two root causes: model explanations fail to identify the correct violated policy or target group, and the binary reward signal in GRPO is too weak to guide reasoning quality. This mirrors how human annotators cannot be trained on raw binary labels alone and instead require structured policy guidelines and fine-grained evaluation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a meme image with overlaid text (RGB, variable resolution) as input and produces a structured two-part output following the long CoT format: a natural-language reasoning explanation enclosed in <think> tags, followed by a label prediction in <answer> tags. The label can be binary (hateful/benign), a fine-grained attack type (e.g., dehumanizing, slurs, exclusion), a target group (e.g., religion, race, disability), or a stance label (neutral/support/oppose), depending on the task. The explanation is generated on-policy by the model itself rather than copied from gold rationales, which the authors find leads to worse performance when used in SFT warmup.

## Method

ExPO-HM is a three-stage training pipeline for LMMs (Qwen2.5-VL) that mimics human moderator training. Stage 1 (SFT-PM Warmup) fine-tunes the model with LoRA on policy-manual-augmented prompts, where each dataset's fine-grained annotation guidelines are converted into structured bullet-point policy descriptions prepended to the input, teaching the model explicit moderation policy knowledge. Stage 2 (GRPO with Curriculum Learning) applies online RL with a two-stage schedule: the first 50% of training steps use only fine-grained classification data to incentivize diverse policy-relevant reasoning, and the remaining 50% mix fine-grained and binary data 50/50. Stage 3 introduces Conditional Decision Entropy (CDE) as an additional reward term in the GRPO objective, where CDE is the entropy of the model's decision token distribution conditioned on its own generated explanation; the reward function (Eq. 11) rewards confident correct predictions, tolerates uncertainty on errors, and penalizes confident wrong predictions using tunable entropy thresholds a and b. This CDE reward is the key differentiator from standard GRPO, providing a dense, verifiable signal for reasoning quality without requiring a separate reward model or annotated rationales.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, ExPO-HM achieves 81.1 binary F1 on HatefulMemes, 82.3 on MAMI, and 78.7 on PrideMM, surpassing the best direct-detection baseline RA-HMD (80.2/81.0/77.8) on all three datasets. For fine-grained classification, ExPO-HM reaches 75.6 Attack F1 and 77.2 Target F1 on HatefulMemes, 73.0 Attack F1 on MAMI, and 68.4 Stance F1 / 65.1 Target F1 on PrideMM. On reasoning quality (LLM-as-a-judge, 0–10 scale), ExPO-HM scores 6.2 on HatefulMemes versus 5.2 for GRPO and 5.5 for RA-HMD, while reducing CDE from 0.26 (GRPO) to 0.03, indicating substantially sharper and more confident decisions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExPO-HM outperforms all five baselines across every reported metric. Against the strongest direct-detection system RA-HMD (Mei et al., 2025), ExPO-HM gains +0.9 to +1.3 binary F1 points and additionally provides fine-grained and reasoning outputs that RA-HMD does not report. Compared to the Explain-then-Detect agent LOREHM (Huang et al., 2024, 34B), ExPO-HM (7B) improves binary F1 by +15.5 on HatefulMemes and +7.0 on MAMI, while also delivering fine-grained and reasoning scores. Against U-CoT+ (Pan et al., 2025, 14B), ExPO-HM gains +8.7 binary F1 on HatefulMemes and +7.3 on PrideMM. Over the GRPO baseline (same compute budget), ExPO-HM improves binary F1 by +6.6 (HatefulMemes), +5.5 (MAMI), and +5.5 (PrideMM), and fine-grained Attack F1 by +14.4 on HatefulMemes and +9.3 on MAMI. Over DPO, the gains are even larger: +7.5 binary F1 on HatefulMemes, +10.0 on MAMI, and +9.2 on PrideMM, with the paper reporting up to 15% and 17% F1 improvement over GRPO and DPO respectively on fine-grained tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) direct hateful meme detection via CLIP/LMM classifiers (RA-HMD, MemeCLIP), which achieves strong binary F1 but provides no explanation; (2) Explain-then-Detect systems using CoT prompting or LMM agents (LOREHM, U-CoT+), which the paper shows consistently underperform even simple SFT baselines; and (3) RL post-training methods (GRPO, DPO) that, when applied naively to the Explain-then-Detect format, fail to improve reasoning quality. The key departure is a training methodology inspired by human moderator workflows: policy-manual SFT warmup, curriculum-ordered GRPO (fine-grained before binary), and a novel Conditional Decision Entropy reward that addresses the insufficient binary reward signal in standard GRPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first Explain-then-Detect system to surpass direct-detection SOTA, which matters practically because real-world moderation requires not just a binary flag but an actionable explanation of which policy is violated and why. The CDE concept—a simple entropy-based proxy for reasoning quality that correlates strongly (r=-0.78) with LLM-as-a-judge scores—could generalize to other tasks where reliable reward models are unavailable. Limitations include evaluation on only three datasets (one with gold rationales), dependence on LLM judges for reasoning quality, and the fact that policy manuals must be hand-constructed per dataset, raising questions about scalability to new moderation domains or low-resource languages.
