## Problem

Existing Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods are computationally expensive due to backpropagation-based updates, require large amounts of target data for stable adaptation, are brittle to hyperparameter choices, or impose architectural assumptions such as batch normalization layers. No prior method achieves fully TTA adaptation (no source data, no target labels) that is simultaneously optimization-free, hyperparameter-free, and effective with as few as a single sample while adding negligible latency or memory overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264373/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

NEO takes unlabeled, domain-shifted RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-based ViTs, 32×32 for CIFAR-10) as input and produces class labels with confidence scores as output. The method operates as a drop-in replacement for the standard nn.Linear classifier head in a ViT, requiring only a single line of code change. It consumes only the penultimate-layer embeddings from the frozen encoder and stores a single d-dimensional vector (the running mean of corrupted embeddings), making it batch-size independent and capable of adapting from as few as one sample or one class.

## Method

NEO's key insight is that input distribution shifts induce a largely shared global displacement in the latent embedding space, concentrated in a small number of dimensions. Under neural collapse assumptions (cross-entropy loss, weight regularization, balanced classes), the source embeddings are centered at the origin, so the global centroid shift equals the corrupted centroid itself. NEO estimates this corrupted centroid by maintaining a running average of all test-time embeddings seen so far, then subtracts it from each embedding before the linear classifier — a single vector subtraction per sample with no optimization, no backpropagation, and no hyperparameters. A continual variant (NEO-Continual) replaces the uniform running average with an exponential moving average controlled by a single interpretable hyperparameter α, enabling robust adaptation under evolving corruption sequences. The approach is architecture-agnostic in principle (also demonstrated on ResNet-50) and adds no measurable inference latency or memory overhead on edge devices.

## Results

On ImageNet-C with ViT-Base (512 adaptation samples), NEO achieves 59.2% accuracy, outperforming all 7 baseline TTA methods (best baseline: FOA at 58.4%) and improving over no adaptation (55.6%) by 3.6%. NEO also leads on ImageNet-R (60.3%) and ImageNet-Sketch (47.2%), while on CIFAR-10-C it achieves 82.4%, narrowly behind Surgeon (82.7%). Notably, NEO requires only 1 forward pass per sample (vs. up to 35 for CoTTA), is hyperparameter-free and backpropagation-free, and can improve accuracy by 1.5% with a single adaptation sample or by 3% using data from just 1 class to benefit 999 other classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEO achieves the highest average accuracy on ImageNet-C (59.2%), beating FOA (58.4%), Surgeon (56.1%), TENT (56.3%), SAR (55.9%), CoTTA (55.9%), T3A (55.2%), and LAME (54.2%). On ImageNet-R and ImageNet-Sketch, NEO also leads all baselines (60.3% and 47.2% respectively), with margins of 0.1–1.2% over the closest competitors. On CIFAR-10-C, NEO (82.4%) is narrowly outperformed by Surgeon (82.7%) but still beats all other methods. The most dramatic gap appears under contrast corruption, where NEO reaches 58.2% versus FOA's 54.5% and no-adaptation's 32.6%—nearly doubling accuracy. Critically, NEO achieves these results with only 1 forward pass per sample and no backpropagation, whereas CoTTA requires 35 forward passes and methods like TENT, SAR, and Surgeon rely on gradient-based optimization, making NEO substantially more efficient on edge devices (63% less inference time, 9% less memory on Jetson Orin Nano).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the neural collapse framework (Papyan et al., 2020) to derive a principled, optimization-free test-time adaptation method. While prior TTA work either relies on backpropagation-based updates (TENT, SAR, CoTTA), requires source data for centering (FOA, T3A), or demands multiple forward passes (Surgeon), NEO departs by showing that under neural collapse the global embedding centroid is at the origin, making a single re-centering vector sufficient without any source data or hyperparameters. It also extends the analytical TTA line (LAME, T3A) by eliminating their dependence on large batches or class-level statistics, reducing adaptation to tracking one global mean.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEO's one-line integration, zero hyperparameters, and negligible compute overhead make it immediately deployable on resource-constrained edge devices (Raspberry Pi, Jetson Orin Nano), addressing a practical gap that optimization-based TTA methods cannot fill. The theoretical connection between neural collapse and latent re-centering opens a new analytical lens for TTA that could inform future work on other architectures and layers. However, the method's effectiveness is primarily demonstrated on Vision Transformers; ResNet-50 results show NEO underperforms optimization-based baselines, suggesting the neural collapse geometry may not transfer equally to CNNs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-classification tasks, deeper layers, or architectures without the clean simplex structure assumed by neural collapse.
