## Problem

Multi-turn jailbreaks capture the real threat model for safety-aligned chatbots, but existing approaches break under two challenges: exploration complexity (the combinatorial branching of prompt-response sequences) and intent drift (the conversation gradually shifting away from the original harmful objective). Prior methods either rely on manually scripted staging paradigms that limit diversity, or template-driven pipelines that couple generation to victim responses, inherit API opacity, and amplify drift.

## Input / Output

The attacker takes a single harmful query (e.g., a text from AdvBench or HarmBench) and produces a complete, numbered multi-turn adversarial prompt sequence in one shot, with a maximum of T_max = 7 turns. Crucially, generation is open-loop and response-agnostic: all turns are emitted simultaneously without conditioning on any victim model reply, which factorizes the search from the joint prompt-response space to the prompt space alone. The output format is a simple numbered list ("1. <prompt 1>\n2. <prompt 2>\n..."), enforced by a format reward during training.

## Method

SEMA trains a multi-turn jailbreak attacker in two stages without any external jailbreak corpora or predefined strategies. Stage 1 (Prefilling Self-Tuning) fine-tunes the attacker on self-generated rollouts that are seeded with a minimal structural prefix (the list marker "1.") to bypass safety refusals and stabilize parseable multi-turn outputs, leaving the model's knowledge intact for open-ended exploration. Stage 2 (RL with Intent-Drift-Aware Reward) uses GRPO to optimize the attacker, where each rollout is executed against a training-time victim in a simulated multi-turn session and scored by an evaluation model (GPT-4.1-mini) on three dimensions: intent alignment with the original query, compliance risk of the final response, and level of detail. The composite reward R_IDA = ½ · Alignment(r_T, q) · [Risk(r_T) + Detail(r_T)] down-weights drifted or superficial responses, while a binary format reward enforces parseable numbered outputs throughout training. This design unifies single- and multi-turn attacks (T=1 vs. T>1), avoids dependence on victim feedback at generation time, and achieves SOTA ASR across multiple victims, judges, and datasets.

## Results

SEMA achieves a mean ASR@1 of 80.1% on AdvBench (LLM Classifier) and 75.0% on HarmBench (HarmBench Classifier) across three victim models (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, GPT-4.1-mini), representing a 33.9% absolute improvement over the prior SOTA (Crescendo, 39.9% on AdvBench). On the most secure open-source reasoning model GPT-oss-20B, SEMA still attains the highest ASR@1 under LLM and HarmBench Classifiers (36.0%/57.7% on AdvBench; 15.1%/39.0% on HarmBench). With only 20 attempts, SEMA reaches 99.7% ASR@20 on HarmBench against GPT-4.1-mini, exceeding Jailbreak-R1's ASR@50 of 93.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEMA outperforms all single-turn baselines by a wide margin: ADV-LLM (the strongest single-turn method) reaches only 46.2% mean on AdvBench and 55.1% on HarmBench, while SEMA achieves 80.1% and 75.0% respectively; FlipAttack and Jailbreak-R1 are far weaker (11.4%/18.1% on AdvBench). Among multi-turn baselines, Crescendo is the closest competitor at 39.9% (AdvBench) and 40.9% (HarmBench), still trailing SEMA by ~40 percentage points; Jigsaw Puzzle (39.4%/37.5%) and X-Teaming (36.0%/37.3%) are also substantially outperformed. The remaining template-driven methods (GOAT, CoA, FITD, ActorAttack) all fall below 24% mean on AdvBench. SEMA also surpasses its own offline variants SFT (31.0%/24.6%) and DPO (23.3%/26.8%), confirming that the online RL stage with intent-drift-aware reward is critical. On transferability (TASR@1), SEMA leads all baselines across every source→target pair, e.g., 92.6% on Qwen2.5-3B→GPT-4.1-mini (AdvBench) versus Crescendo's 78.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEMA extends the line of multi-turn jailbreak attacks (Crescendo, GOAT, CoA, FITD, Jigsaw Puzzle) by replacing template-driven, victim-conditioned generation with a trained, open-loop attacker that plans all turns in one shot. It builds on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) for the RL stage and repurposes prefilling—originally a jailbreak tactic (Qi et al., 2024)—as training infrastructure to stabilize rollouts. The key departure from prior training-based jailbreak work (Jailbreak-R1, ADV-LLM) is the multi-turn setting, the response-agnostic open-loop design, and the intent-drift-aware reward that anchors harmful intent across turns without relying on external corpora or predefined strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEMA provides a more realistic and scalable stress test for LLM safety by capturing the multi-turn threat model that single-turn evaluations miss, and its open-loop design reduces both exploration complexity and API cost compared to interactive baselines. The finding that response-agnostic planning outperforms response-conditioned generation challenges the assumption that conditioning on victim replies is necessary for effective multi-turn attacks. Limitations include the text-only setting, the reliance on a single 'ask the same thing differently' strategy, and the open-loop design's inability to adapt mid-conversation; future work on co-evolving defenses, turn-efficient closed-loop attackers, and non-text modalities remains open.
