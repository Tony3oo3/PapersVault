## Problem

While large language models have achieved strong text-based reasoning and visual Chain-of-Thought methods work for static images, multi-modal reasoning over long videos remains limited because existing approaches cannot explicitly localize, revisit, or verify specific temporal segments. This paper addresses the gap by asking how to build a video-language agent that provides answers directly linked to interpretable visual evidence (temporal moments), emulating how humans break down, locate, re-watch, and synthesize observations from long videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261955/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (supporting dynamic resolution and durations from seconds to over an hour) and a free-form text query. It produces a natural language answer paired with a temporal moment [t_start, t_end] as grounded evidence. During inference, the agent adaptively revisits the video at varying temporal segments and spatial resolutions (e.g., 64–256 tokens per frame, up to 150 frames for the grounder), and the planner can rephrase ambiguous queries into more precise scene descriptions before grounding.

## Method

VideoMind introduces a four-role agentic workflow (Planner → Grounder → Verifier → Answerer) built on a shared Qwen2-VL backbone, where each role is implemented as a separate LoRA adapter (rank=64, alpha=64) enabling seamless role switching at inference with only ~4.2 GB peak memory versus 16.6 GB for distributing roles across full model copies. The Grounder features a dedicated timestamp decoder that processes a <REG> token's hidden state alongside 1D-pooled visual tokens through a three-layer transformer encoder and a four-level temporal feature pyramid, predicting start/end boundaries via dual Conv1D heads (focal-loss classification + L1-regression) plus a contrastive alignment loss. The Verifier applies a 50% boundary expansion (zoom-in) with special <SEG-START>/<SEG-END> tokens to produce binary Yes/No judgments over top-5 grounder candidates, selecting the highest-confidence moment. The Planner uses a JSON-style function-call format to dynamically choose among three pre-defined reasoning plans (ground+verify+answer, ground+verify only, or answer only) based on the video and query context, achieving 93% planning accuracy.

## Results

On CG-Bench (avg. 27-min videos), VideoMind-2B achieves mIoU 5.94 and rec.@IoU 8.50, surpassing GPT-4o (5.62, 8.30) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (3.95, 5.81) on temporal grounding metrics, while VideoMind-7B sets a new SOTA with mIoU 7.10 and acc.@IoU 4.67. On Charades-STA, VideoMind-7B reaches R@0.5 59.1 and mIoU 50.2, outperforming all LLM-based temporal grounding baselines. On long-video QA benchmarks, VideoMind-7B achieves MLVU 64.4 and LVBench 40.8, exceeding GPT-4o (54.5, 30.8) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (–, 33.1) by substantial margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoMind-2B outperforms GPT-4o on CG-Bench temporal grounding (mIoU 5.94 vs. 5.62; rec.@IoU 8.50 vs. 8.30) and decisively beats Gemini-1.5-Pro (mIoU 3.95, rec.@IoU 5.81), despite being orders of magnitude smaller. On Charades-STA, VideoMind-7B (R@0.5 59.1, mIoU 50.2) surpasses all LLM-based grounding methods including LLaVA-ST (44.8, 42.4), E.T.Chat (45.9, 42.3), and VideoChat-TPO (40.2, 38.1). On long-video QA, VideoMind-7B exceeds GPT-4o by ~10 points on MLVU (64.4 vs. 54.5) and ~10 points on LVBench (40.8 vs. 30.8), and beats Gemini-1.5-Pro on LVBench (40.8 vs. 33.1). However, GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro still lead on Video-MME overall accuracy (71.9/75.0 vs. 58.2) and CG-Bench long-acc. (45.2/37.2 vs. 38.4), indicating that the agentic pipeline's gains are concentrated in temporal grounding and long-context reasoning rather than general short-video QA. On NExT-GQA, VideoMind-7B (mIoU 31.4, Acc@GQA 28.2) outperforms VideoChat-TPO (27.7, 25.5) and LLoVi (20.0, 24.3), confirming advantages over both agent-based and end-to-end baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen2-VL as its base vision-language architecture and extends the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm into a novel Chain-of-LoRA mechanism where a single model hosts multiple role-specific adapters (planner, grounder, verifier, answerer) that are switched at inference time. It departs from prior agent-based video systems (e.g., VITA with 8×7B separate models, LangRepo) that either require multiple full model copies or complex modular pipelines, and from text-based CoT reasoning approaches that cannot explicitly localize or revisit temporal segments. The timestamp decoder with a temporal feature pyramid represents a clean architectural departure from prior LLM-based grounding methods that predict timestamps via language tokens or special tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Chain-of-LoRA mechanism offers a practical, memory-efficient solution to the multi-role video reasoning problem, achieving performance on par with 4× distributed model setups while using only ~4.2 GB GPU memory. The finding that a 2B model with structured agentic reasoning can match or exceed GPT-4o on temporal grounding metrics (mIoU, rec.@IoU) for long videos suggests that structured temporal reasoning may be more important than raw model scale for grounded video tasks. Open questions remain around joint optimization of the multiple roles (currently trained separately), integration of audio modality, and whether the approach generalizes to truly open-ended multi-hop reasoning beyond the pre-defined three plans.
