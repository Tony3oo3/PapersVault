## Problem

In the linear social choice model for constitutional-style AI alignment, Ge et al. (2024) showed that an optimally trained linear reward model can violate Pareto Optimality: a response preferred by all principles can receive lower reward. This paper asks how robust such axiomatic violations are under realistic loss-based training, and whether adding expressivity to the reward class or designing the data distribution can provably restore the guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260997/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes as input a collection of pairwise strict preference comparisons, one set per voter (principle), where each voter is represented by a linear direction vector in a d-dimensional embedding space. The output is a reward function (linear or bounded-degree polynomial) whose induced ordering over the embedding space should satisfy axioms like Pareto Optimality. Unlike standard social choice, only partial pairwise comparisons are observed per voter rather than full orderings, and the reward function must generalize beyond the observed candidate set to the entire embedding space.

## Method

The paper makes three technical contributions. First, it proves that bounded-degree polynomial reward functions still fail both PO and PMC (Theorem 4.1), using a construction where candidates are positioned on parallel lines so that the unique degree-d polynomial interpolating the optimal degenerate-instance rewards is p*(x,y) = -x-y, then applying Berge's maximum theorem to show the perturbed optimum stays close to p* and thus violates PO. Second, it defines a generalized PO over the entire embedding space (directional rather than candidate-specific) and proves that under uniform data coverage—comparisons in all directions at unit length, each direction appearing uniformly—any loss-based rule with a convex, nondecreasing, lower-bounded, differentiable loss satisfies PO (Theorem 5.1), via a geometric argument showing any non-PO minimizer can be improved by rotating toward the Pareto direction. Third, it shows PMC remains unsatisfiable even in this uniform setting (Theorem 5.2), because the requirement becomes discontinuous in voter weights. The practical recipe is to enforce balanced directional coverage via dataset design to recover PO without abandoning standard loss-based training pipelines.

## Results

The paper establishes three main theoretical results. First (Theorem 4.1), it proves that even with polynomial reward functions of bounded degree, any loss-based voting rule with a strictly convex, lower-bounded, differentiable loss (including Bradley-Terry) fails both Pareto Optimality (PO) and Pairwise Majority Consistency (PMC), already in 2D with three voters in the positive quadrant. Second (Theorem 5.1), in an idealized setting where comparison data uniformly covers all directions in the embedding space, any loss-based rule with a convex, nondecreasing, lower-bounded loss satisfies PO provided there are at least two distinct voters. Third (Theorem 5.2), PMC fails even in this uniform-data setting, as the requirement becomes discontinuous in voter weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons. The primary relationship is to Ge et al. (2024), who showed that an optimal linear reward model can violate PO in the linear social-choice framework. This paper extends that negative result by showing it persists for polynomial reward functions of bounded degree (Theorem 4.1), thereby ruling out the natural hypothesis that increased expressivity resolves the issue. The paper then provides a positive result absent from prior work: under uniform directional coverage of the embedding space, PO is provably recovered in the limit (Theorem 5.1), offering a data-centric recipe for constitutional-style alignment. Unlike Ge et al.'s combinatorial social-choice fix, the proposed solution is compatible with standard loss-based training pipelines and requires only dataset design (balanced directional coverage) rather than a different aggregation algorithm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear social choice framework of Ge et al. (2024), who showed that optimally trained linear reward models can violate Pareto Optimality in a setting where principles act as linear-direction voters. While Ge et al. proposed combinatorial social-choice rules to restore axiomatic guarantees, this paper deliberately stays within the loss-based paradigm used in modern ML pipelines (Bradley-Terry, cross-entropy) and asks whether axiomatic guarantees can be recovered through model expressivity or data design. It also connects to the broader social choice literature on restricted preference domains (Elkind et al., 2022) and the Constitutional AI line of work (Bai et al., 2022b) that motivates the principles-as-voters framing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a concrete recipe: balanced directional coverage in preference data can restore Pareto Optimality without abandoning standard loss-based training, offering alignment practitioners a data-centric lever (reweighting, targeted collection) when combinatorial social-choice algorithms are infeasible. Key limitations include the reliance on a simplified linear-voter/frozen-embedding model and an idealized uniform-data assumption; the paper conjectures but does not prove that fixed-width MLPs also fail PO. Open questions include deriving explicit finite-sample bounds, formalizing reweighting strategies, and extending the analysis to other axioms (e.g., clone robustness, proportionality) in the embedding-space setting.
