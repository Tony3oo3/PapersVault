## Problem

Existing auto-regressive mesh generation methods treat meshes as flat collections of equivalent triangles, failing to preserve critical geometric properties—manifoldness, watertightness, consistent face normals, and part awareness—that are prerequisites for practical applications such as 3D printing, physics simulation, and interactive editing. This limitation stems from tokenization schemes that lack awareness of the overall topological structure during generation, making generated meshes unreliable for downstream use.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 4096-point point cloud as conditioning input, whose features are extracted by a Michelangelo encoder and injected into the autoregressive decoder via cross-attention. The output is a full 3D polygon mesh encoded as a token sequence where each vertex contributes 4 tokens (2 quantized position sub-tokens via BPT block-offset, 1 self-layer topology token, 1 between-layer topology token), with special tokens C, U, and E delimiting connected components and layer boundaries. The generated mesh is structurally guaranteed to be manifold, watertight, and part-aware by construction of the tokenization scheme.

## Method

The core contribution is a silk-weaving-inspired mesh tokenization algorithm that hierarchically layers vertices of each connected component via BFS from a canonical starting half-edge (selected by y-z-x coordinate ordering), then sorts vertices within each layer using half-edge pointer traversal in an induction-like fashion. Topology is compressed into two per-layer adjacency matrices: a Self-Layer Matrix encoded with a fixed binary window (W=8, covering 99.1% of cases in one token) and a Between-Layer Matrix encoded via a Stars-and-Bars combinatorial scheme (W'=5, yielding 26 combinations per row), achieving a 2/9 compression ratio (~26.65 bits-per-face). A decoder-only transformer (24 layers, 1024 hidden, ~500M parameters) is trained with standard cross-entropy loss on the packed token sequence. The approach is further supported by an online non-manifold edge-graph partitioning algorithm (using max-cycle-first, connected-component-first, and BFS-sequential rules) to expand trainable data, and a progressive-balanced resampling strategy that transitions from instance-balanced to class-balanced sampling over training epochs to handle the long-tail face-count distribution without manual curation.

## Results

On the gObjaverse evaluation set (500 meshes), the method achieves the best scores across all four geometric quality metrics: CD 0.025, HD 0.087, NC 0.792, and |NC| 0.924. In terms of compression efficiency, it attains a state-of-the-art Bits-per-face of 26.652 and a Compression Ratio of 0.22 (≈2 tokens per face), outperforming BPT (28.478 / 0.26), EdgeRunner (29.610 / 0.47), and TreeMeshGPT (42.000 / 0.22). The model uses a 24-layer decoder-only transformer with ~500M parameters, trained on 16×H800 GPUs for ~15 days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EdgeRunner* (trained faithfully on the same dataset), the method wins decisively on every metric: CD improves from 0.053 to 0.025, HD from 0.144 to 0.087, NC from 0.418 to 0.792, and Bits-per-face from 29.610 to 26.652. Against TreeMeshGPT, the method achieves better geometric accuracy (CD 0.025 vs 0.030, HD 0.087 vs 0.103) and dramatically better compression (Bits-per-face 26.652 vs 42.000), while tying on Compression Ratio (0.22). Against BPT, the closest competitor, the method edges out on all six metrics (e.g., CD 0.025 vs 0.027, NC 0.792 vs 0.770, Bits-per-face 26.652 vs 28.478, Comp. Ratio 0.22 vs 0.26). DeepMesh is excluded from quantitative comparison for fairness (it uses RL fine-tuning on top of BPT's tokenization) but is included qualitatively in Figure 7, where the method shows superior manifold topology and part-aware generation. In Table 1's geometric-property checklist, the method is the only one simultaneously satisfying all five properties (lossless, manifold, watertight, face-normal-consistent, part-aware), whereas each baseline fails on at least two.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive mesh generation lineage initiated by MeshGPT and scaled by MeshXL, EdgeRunner, TreeMeshGPT, and BPT, directly adopting BPT's block-offset vertex coordinate representation. The key conceptual departure is replacing the prevailing 'mesh as a flat collection of equivalent triangles' tokenization with a BFS-based vertex layering scheme (analogous to silk weaving) that imposes a canonical topological ordering, thereby structurally guaranteeing manifold topology, watertightness, face normal consistency, and part awareness during generation. Unlike MeshGPT's VQ-VAE-based tokenization or EdgeRunner/TreeMeshGPT's tree-traversal approaches, the method is fully explicit and lossless without any encoder-decoder architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous guarantee of watertightness, manifold topology, consistent face normals, and part awareness addresses a critical practical gap: previous autoregressive mesh generators produce meshes that require extensive manual repair before use in 3D printing, physics simulation, or animation rigging. The SOTA compression efficiency (0.22 ratio, 26.65 bits-per-face) makes the approach scalable, and the online non-manifold processing plus progressive-balanced resampling strategy removes the need for costly manual data curation. Open questions include whether the 7-bit quantization and 10K token cap can be lifted (the authors suggest 9-bit quantization and Hourglass Transformer architectures), whether binary matrix factorization can push compression toward 2 tokens per vertex, and whether the layering framework extends naturally to hybrid triangle-quadrilateral meshes.
