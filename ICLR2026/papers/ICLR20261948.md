## Problem

Diffusion-based LLMs (dLLMs) parallelize text generation by predicting all future suffix tokens at each denoising step, yet only a small fraction of those predictions are retained, creating a disproportionate computational bottleneck that undermines the throughput advantage of parallel decoding. The paper identifies that suffix tokens function as a redundant, low-entropy 'scratchpad' whose attention scores decay sharply with distance, but this inefficiency had not been formally characterized or exploited for acceleration.

## Input / Output

DPad operates at inference time on existing dLLM backbones (LLaDA-8B/1.5B, Dream-7B) and takes a text prompt (with optional few-shot exemplars) concatenated with L mask tokens as input, producing a fully denoised text sequence as output. Generation proceeds block-wise (block size 32) over sequence lengths ranging from 256 to 4096 tokens, and the method is compatible with parallel decoding and prefix caching, so it can be layered on top of existing dLLM inference pipelines without modifying model weights.

## Method

DPad is a training-free inference strategy that restricts suffix attention via two complementary mechanisms: a fixed-length sliding window that bounds the number of attended suffix tokens independent of total sequence length, and a distance-decay dropout that probabilistically prunes distant suffix tokens before attention is computed. The dropout uses rejection sampling from a half-Gaussian distribution whose retention probability decays exponentially with distance from the current block, controlled by a decay rate k and a magnitude scalar a. This proactive (pre-attention) pruning renders dropped tokens invisible to the computational graph, reducing suffix complexity from O(L) to O(1) and requiring only a lightweight RoPE remapping to preserve positional encoding. The paper formalizes the underlying 'Scratchpad mechanism' (an Attention Connection that stores cross-layer context in suffix tokens) and proposes the Diffusion Lottery Tickets hypothesis, arguing that a sparse, adaptively resampled subset of suffix tokens suffices for high-quality denoising, which explains why the method can be applied a priori without computing exact attention scores.

## Results

On LLaDA-Instruct across four benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, MBPP), DPad alone yields 1.18×–3.91× latency speedups over the vanilla baseline, and when combined with parallel decoding reaches 2.76×–10.32×. In the long-sequence setting (LLaDA-1.5/GSM8K, 1024 tokens, 1-shot), DPad achieves a 20.3× standalone speedup, and 61.39× when stacked with parallel decoding and prefix caching, while maintaining or improving accuracy (e.g., strict-match rises from 48.98% to 74.07%). DPad also improves strict-match accuracy on short sequences, gaining +26.46 points on GSM8K and +19.62 points on MATH for LLaDA-Instruct, by reducing interference from low-entropy suffix tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla LLaDA/Dream baselines, DPad consistently reduces latency (1.18×–3.91× standalone; up to 10.32× with parallel decoding) while matching or exceeding flexible-match accuracy and substantially improving strict-match accuracy (e.g., 63.84% vs. 37.38% on GSM8K for LLaDA-Instruct). Compared to Fast-dLLM (parallel decoding alone), DPad adds orthogonal gains: on GSM8K, +Par.+DPad cuts latency from 8.55 s to 6.64 s (4.14× vs. 3.21× over vanilla) and lifts strict-match from 38.67% to 64.97%. Against Sparse-dLLM (reactive post-attention eviction), DPad's proactive pre-attention dropout achieves a 12.87× latency speedup at 2048 tokens and 39.64× at 4096 tokens, while also improving flexible-match accuracy (84.00% vs. 79.00% at 2048) and reducing peak memory by ~50%. On the Dream-Base model, DPad yields 2.17×–4.98× speedups with broadly comparable accuracy, though the gains are smaller than on LLaDA, which the authors attribute to Dream's autoregressive initialization lacking native suffix-context sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPad extends the sliding-window attention paradigm from Longformer and StreamingLLM (which operate on prefix tokens) to the suffix region of diffusion LLMs, and contrasts sharply with Sparse-dLLM's reactive 'compute-and-evict' approach by using a proactive, distance-based pre-attention dropout that requires no attention score computation. The method also draws on the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis, reframing it as an inference-time 'Diffusion Lottery Tickets' hypothesis where a sparse subset of suffix tokens suffices for denoising. Conceptually, the paper's key departure is formalizing suffix tokens as an 'Attention Connection' (a cross-layer scratchpad), which prior dLLM acceleration work (Fast-dLLM, Sparse-dLLM) treated as an opaque computational cost rather than a structured information reservoir.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 61.4× combined speedup (and 20.3× standalone on long sequences) makes dLLM inference substantially more practical, and the training-free, few-lines-of-code implementation lowers the adoption barrier. The finding that pruning suffix tokens can *improve* strict-match accuracy (by reducing interference from low-entropy, off-format tokens) challenges the assumption that any pruning must sacrifice quality. Open questions include the observed accuracy degradation at 2048+ tokens (attributed to a train-inference distribution gap that SFT might close), whether the Gaussian decay is truly optimal versus other schedules, and whether the scratchpad mechanism generalizes to dLLMs trained with fundamentally different objectives.
