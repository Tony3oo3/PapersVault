## Problem

Existing reasoning benchmarks operate in narrow, well-defined environments with explicit instructions, testing a model's ability within a pre-specified problem space rather than its ability to discover the problem itself. Puzzlehunts — open-ended, multimodal puzzles with unstated rules and ambiguous objectives — mirror real-world discovery tasks like scientific investigation and exploratory data analysis, yet frontier models' performance in such settings remains largely untested. PUZZLEWORLD fills this gap by providing a benchmark that requires solvers to infer both what the problem is and how to solve it from nuanced, multimodal cues.

## Input / Output

Each puzzle is presented in its original image/PDF format (preserving spatial layout rather than transcribing into separate text and images) alongside a human-transcribed flavor text. The model must produce a step-by-step reasoning trace concluding with a final answer in a specified format. Evaluation yields two metrics: final answer accuracy and stepwise accuracy, where the latter is the last annotated ground-truth reasoning step the model successfully executed, scored by a GPT-4o LLM judge that evaluates each step independently as a boolean. The 667 puzzles span three input modalities (text, visual, structured) and six reasoning skills (logic, wordplay, spatial, cryptic, knowledge, commonsense), with an average of over 5 reasoning steps per puzzle.

## Method

PUZZLEWORLD is constructed from 667 Puzzled Pint puzzles (CC BY-NC-SA), each annotated with a JSON metadata schema and a sequence of reasoning steps formalized as tuples ⟨e, f⟩ (text explanation, optional figure), where each step begins with an atomic operation (pattern discovery, sketching, manipulation, combining, extraction). A two-stage verification protocol uses GPT-4o to flag ambiguous annotations (12.11% flagged) followed by human correction (10.93% modified), achieving 96.5% correctness on a 5% manual audit. For evaluation, an LLM-as-judge pipeline (GPT-4o) scores stepwise accuracy by independently checking each ground-truth step against the candidate solution, validated at r=0.829 against human raters. The paper further demonstrates the value of reasoning-trace annotations by showing that LoRA fine-tuning an 8B InternVL3 model on stepwise traces (vs. answer-only) more than doubles stepwise accuracy from 4.78% to 11.00%, with transferable gains on downstream visual reasoning tasks like Rebus puzzles and MathVista geometry.

## Results

On PUZZLEWORLD, most state-of-the-art multimodal models achieve only 1–4% final answer accuracy, with the best model (Gemini 3 Pro) solving just 18.00% of puzzles and reaching 39.99% stepwise accuracy—matching human Novice performance (13.89% acc, 23.10% step) but falling far short of human Enthusiasts (44.44% acc, 51.70% step). Open-source models perform even more poorly, with QVQ-72B-Preview reaching only 1.36% accuracy and InternVL3-78B just 0.89%. Fine-tuning an 8B InternVL3 model on PUZZLEWORLD's annotated reasoning traces more than doubles its stepwise accuracy from 4.78% to 11.00%, and transfers to downstream visual reasoning tasks (e.g., Rebus puzzles: 3.2%→5.1%, MathVista VQA: 32.40%→39.11%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, Gemini 3 Pro leads with 18.00% accuracy and 39.99% stepwise accuracy, narrowly outperforming GPT-o3 (14.22% acc, 39.81% step) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (7.65% acc, 31.61% step). All open-source models (QVQ-72B, InternVL3-78B, Kimi VL A3B) cluster at 1–1.4% accuracy, with QVQ-72B achieving the highest stepwise score among them at 30.23%. Critically, even the best model (Gemini 3 Pro) matches only human Novice performance and trails human Enthusiasts by over 26 percentage points in accuracy. Across modalities, all models perform best on text puzzles and worst on unstructured visual puzzles, where accuracy drops to less than half their text-puzzle scores. The fine-tuning experiment (Table 3) shows that training on PUZZLEWORLD reasoning traces improves stepwise accuracy from 4.78% to 11.00% for InternVL3-8B, while answer-only fine-tuning degrades performance to 2.96% stepwise and 0% accuracy, confirming the value of stepwise annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of puzzle benchmarks and open-ended reasoning evaluation. Prior puzzle benchmarks (PuzzleVQA, AlgoVQA, PUZZLES) test narrow, constrained task formats where modern models perform well, while broader multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MMMU, SciBench, OlympiadBench) present well-defined questions that resemble training distributions. The closest prior work, EnigmaEval (Wang et al., 2025), also targets puzzlehunts but is closed-source and lacks step-by-step reasoning annotations. PUZZLEWORLD departs from all of these by preserving original puzzle formats, providing human-annotated reasoning traces with intermediate states, and introducing a stepwise accuracy metric that enables fine-grained diagnostic analysis of where and how models fail in discovery-driven, instruction-free settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PUZZLEWORLD exposes a stark gap between frontier model capabilities and even novice human performance on open-ended, multimodal reasoning, with the best model (Gemini 3 Pro) solving only 18% of puzzles versus 44% for human enthusiasts. The rich stepwise annotations are not merely diagnostic: fine-tuning on reasoning traces (vs. final answers alone) meaningfully improves intermediate reasoning and transfers to downstream visual tasks, suggesting the dataset can serve as training data for more generalist reasoning. Key open questions include whether models can learn to backtrack from dead ends (the dominant failure mode), whether sketching capabilities can be integrated into inference, and whether the benchmark's difficulty will remain discriminative as models improve. Limitations include the exclusion of audio/video/interactive modalities, reliance on LLM-judge evaluation, and a relatively small puzzle count (667).
