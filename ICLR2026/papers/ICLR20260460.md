## Problem

All existing video understanding benchmarks for action recognition, captioning, and VQA are recorded under Earth's gravity, yet microgravity fundamentally removes gravity-aligned orientation, reliable support surfaces, and conventional object dynamics. This creates a critical domain gap: Earth-trained models rely on terrestrial priors (upright posture, ground contact, downward object motion) that are violated in orbit, causing significant performance degradation in safety-critical space applications where autonomous perception of astronaut actions is essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260460/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 3-second RGB video clips at 30 fps (target 480p) sourced from real ISS/Tiangong mission footage and physically plausible space-themed films. For fine-grained HAR, the output is up to five multi-label action annotations per detected individual per clip, each with a spatio-temporal bounding box (390,000+ boxes total across 50 AVA-derived action classes). For captioning, each clip yields one detailed text description encoding astronaut identity, cabin location, hand-object interactions, and equipment usage. For VQA, each clip is paired with up to six question-answer pairs spanning factual, causal, and counterfactual reasoning, with an explicit 'Not mentioned' option for unanswerable questions.

## Method

The core contribution is a dataset construction and benchmarking pipeline rather than a new architecture. Raw footage is trimmed to uniform 3-second clips, filtered via YOLOv11 person detection and PySceneDetect scene-transition analysis, and auto-annotated with bounding boxes using YOLOv11 + BoT-SORT tracking with motion-adaptive parameters. The action taxonomy is derived from AVA's 80 atomic actions by excluding physically inapplicable classes, merging near-duplicates, and re-grounding semantics in microgravity, yielding 50 actions in three macro-categories (Object Manipulation, Person Interaction, Person Movement). Captions are human-written with cross-checking against official space agency documentation, then refined by MLLMs for fluency; VQA pairs are generated via a caption-grounded two-stage MLLM pipeline with human verification. The empirical evaluation uses a cross-domain transfer protocol (Kinetics pretrain → AVA fine-tune → zero-shot on MicroG-4M vs. JHMDB) to isolate the physics-driven domain gap, revealing that even the best Earth-trained models plateau around 47% mAP on HAR and that CNNs with non-local modules outperform transformers in microgravity, inverting typical terrestrial rankings.

## Results

On the MicroG-4M test set, fine-grained HAR results plateau around 47% mAP, with I3D NLN achieving the best test mAP of 47.12% and Slow (4×16) the best F1 of 46.37%. Under the matched zero-shot transfer protocol (Kinetics pretrain → AVA fine-tune → MicroG-4M eval), the best model (SlowFast 32×2) drops to only 23.81% mAP, compared to 47.50% on JHMDB under identical settings, isolating a physics-driven domain gap. For video captioning, Gemini 1.5 Pro leads with CIDEr 3.52 and S-BERT 63.38, while for VQA, GPT-4o achieves the highest CIDEr (33.98) and S-VQA (44.56) among all evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among HAR baselines, CNN architectures with non-local modules (I3D NLN at 47.12% mAP, C2D NLN at 44.64%) outperform both plain CNNs and transformer-based models (MViTv1 at 12.86%, X3D-L at 18.70%), inverting the typical Kinetics/AVA ranking where transformers lead. Slow (4×16) achieves the best F1 (28.72%), suggesting longer temporal windows help when motion lacks gravitational consistency. Under the cross-domain transfer protocol, the same SlowFast 32×2 model scores 47.50% mAP on JHMDB but only 23.81% on MicroG-4M, a 23.7-point drop that isolates the microgravity domain gap beyond conventional terrestrial shifts. For video captioning, closed-source models (Gemini 1.5 Pro: CIDEr 3.52; GPT-4o: 1.74) substantially outperform open-source models (best: InternVideo at CIDEr 0.77), though all models show low lexical overlap due to domain-specific vocabulary. For VQA, GPT-4o leads with CIDEr 33.98 and S-VQA 44.56, while Video-LLaVA is the best open-source model (CIDEr 25.70, BLEU-4 28.47); notably, Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves the highest S-VQA (44.75) despite very low CIDEr (0.67), reflecting semantically valid but lexically divergent answers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established video understanding tasks—fine-grained multi-label action recognition, video captioning, and visual question answering—from their exclusively terrestrial formulations to the microgravity domain. It builds directly on AVA's (Gu et al., 2018) atomic action taxonomy and spatio-temporal annotation format, adapting 80 Earth-specific actions into 50 microgravity-applicable ones, and adopts Heilman & Smith's (2009) overgenerate-and-rank paradigm for VQA annotation. Conceptually, it departs from prior work by demonstrating that the gravitational priors embedded in all existing HAR and VLM datasets (Kinetics400, AVA, FineGym, ActivityNet, etc.) are not merely a distributional shift but a fundamental violation of orientation, support/contact, and object-dynamics assumptions, which no amount of naive fine-tuning resolves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MicroG-4M fills a genuine gap as the first benchmark for spatio-temporal and semantic video understanding in microgravity, providing a diagnostic testbed that surfaces gravity-dependent failure modes (e.g., floating astronauts misclassified as 'sit' or 'bend/bow') invisible in terrestrial evaluations. Key limitations include the 3-second clip length (hindering multi-step procedural reasoning), RGB-only modality, relatively small scale (~4,759 clips), and potential stylistic bias from cinematic footage. Open questions include whether microgravity-aware inductive biases (e.g., orientation-invariant features, drift-aware temporal modeling) can close the observed performance gap, and whether the benchmark generalizes to longer-horizon mission operations or additional sensor modalities (audio, IMU).
