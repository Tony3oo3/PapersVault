## Problem

Single-vector multimodal retrieval loses fine-grained cross-modal information, while existing multi-vector methods (e.g., ColPali) encode hundreds of patch-level vectors per image, making multimodal-to-multimodal retrieval computationally prohibitive in both training and inference. No prior approach offers a way to flexibly trade retrieval quality against index size and latency at test-time without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260808/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Both queries and candidates can be text, RGB images, or mixed text+image inputs, processed by a VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL, Llama-3.2-Vision, or PaliGemma). The method appends a fixed set of learnable Meta Tokens (up to 16 on the query side, 64 on the candidate side) to the input sequence; their last-layer hidden states after L2 normalization form the Meta Embeddings. At test-time, the user selects a retrieval budget (r_q, r_c) from a set of nested prefixes, so the same index can be queried at different granularities without re-encoding or re-indexing.

## Method

METAEMBED introduces a small number of learnable Meta Tokens appended to the VLM input, whose contextualized last-layer hidden states serve as compact multi-vector embeddings (16×3584 for 7B vs. hundreds of patch vectors in ColBERT-style methods). The key training innovation is Matryoshka Multi-Vector Retrieval (MMR): the Meta Embeddings are organized into G=5 prefix-nested groups (e.g., (1,1), (2,4), (4,8), (8,16), (16,64)), and an InfoNCE contrastive loss is computed in parallel across all groups, encouraging each prefix to be independently discriminative while remaining consistent with larger prefixes. At test-time, selecting a group size (r_q, r_c) determines how many vectors participate in the MaxSim late-interaction score, providing a continuous accuracy-efficiency knob. The approach is trained with LoRA (rank 32) on MMEB-train and ViDoRe-train data, and scales from 3B to 32B parameters with diminishing returns that are smaller than those of single-vector baselines.

## Results

On the Massive Multimodal Embedding Benchmark (MMEB, 36 tasks), METAEMBED-7B achieves 76.6% Precision@1 overall, outperforming the strongest single-vector baseline B3-7B (72.0%) by 4.6 points, and METAEMBED-32B pushes the state of the art to 78.7%. On the Visual Document Retrieval Benchmark (ViDoRe v2), METAEMBED-7B attains 61.3% average NDCG@5, surpassing GME (59.3%) and the multi-vector ColQwen2 (57.5%). Test-time scaling is effective: increasing the retrieval budget from (1,1) to (16,64) Meta Embeddings improves METAEMBED-7B from 71.3% to 76.6% on MMEB, with the gain growing to +6.6 points at 32B scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

METAEMBED-7B (76.6% MMEB overall) outperforms all single-vector baselines at comparable or larger scale: B3-7B (72.0%), MoCa-7B (71.5%), mmE5-11B (69.8%), and VLM2Vec-7B (62.9%), with margins of 4.6, 5.1, 6.8, and 13.7 points respectively. At the 3B scale, METAEMBED-3B (69.1%) edges out MoCa-3B (67.5%) by 1.6 points and VLM2Vec-V2-2B (64.9%) by 4.2 points. On ViDoRe v2, METAEMBED-7B (61.3% NDCG@5) beats the best single-vector method GME (59.3%) by 2.0 points and the multi-vector ColQwen2 (57.5%) by 3.8 points, while METAEMBED-3B (60.3%) surpasses ColPali (54.5%) by 5.8 points. Notably, METAEMBED-11B on the Llama-3.2-Vision backbone (65.1%) underperforms the Qwen2.5-VL-based 7B model, revealing that backbone VQA weakness propagates into retrieval quality. The gap between METAEMBED and the strongest baselines widens with model scale (from +1.6 at 3B to +6.7 at 32B vs. B3-7B), suggesting the multi-vector design scales more favorably than single-vector alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

METAEMBED sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: ColBERT-style late interaction (multi-vector retrieval with MaxSim scoring) and Matryoshka Representation Learning (prefix-nested representations enabling variable-length inference). The key architectural departure is replacing the hundreds of patch/token embeddings used by ColPali and ColQwen with a small fixed set of learnable Meta Tokens appended to the VLM input, whose contextualized hidden states serve as compact multi-vector embeddings. The MMR module then extends MRL from single-vector dimension selection to multi-vector group selection, training parallel contrastive objectives across nested prefix groups so that any prefix is independently discriminative. This combination—Meta Tokens for compactness plus MMR for granularity control—is novel; prior multi-vector multimodal methods either use all patch embeddings (infeasible for image-to-image retrieval) or lack a principled mechanism for variable-budget inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The test-time scaling property is the most practically significant contribution: a single trained model can be deployed at different quality/latency/memory operating points simply by selecting a retrieval budget, without retraining or re-indexing. This is particularly valuable for production systems where different tiers of service (e.g., mobile vs. server) require different retrieval budgets. The finding that the gap over single-vector baselines widens with model scale (3.5 pts at 3B → 6.6 pts at 32B) while single-vector methods plateau suggests multi-vector late interaction is a more favorable scaling regime for embedding models. Open questions include whether the Meta Token count and group structure can be further optimized, how the method behaves with very large candidate pools (beyond the 100K tested), and whether the approach extends to video or 3D retrieval where the token count is even larger.
