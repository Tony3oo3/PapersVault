## Problem

Integrating cellular imaging and transcriptomics data requires either cell-level pairing (impossible with destructive assays) or late-fusion strategies that fail to capture shared and modality-specific information end-to-end. Existing multimodal embedding methods either assume paired modalities or rely on contrastive objectives that require strong positive-negative structure, leaving a gap for learning unified representations from unpaired, context-matched screening data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265128/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PETRI consumes fluorescence microscopy images (80×80 px center crops for HepG2 with HOESCHT, PHALLOIDIN, BODIPY, and MITOPROBE channels; 64×64 px for Perturb-Multi) and single-cell gene expression profiles (18,082 genes for HepG2, 209 mRNAs for Perturb-Multi), where the two modalities come from different cells sharing the same perturbation context. It produces 8 latent tokens of dimension 768 per cell, extracted from the modality-specific encoder outputs before the Multimodal Set Transformer, so that at inference the image and transcriptomics encoders can be run independently on unmatched screening data. Perturbation-level embeddings are formed by averaging cell embeddings within a perturbation and then averaging across modalities.

## Method

PETRI is an early-fusion self-supervised transformer that groups cells by shared perturbation into multimodal 'documents' and performs masked joint reconstruction with cross-modal attention. Each cell is encoded by a modality-specific encoder—ViT-Base for images and a Perceiver for transcriptomics (to handle thousands of gene tokens)—with 75% of input tokens randomly masked and the remaining tokens resampled into a fixed 8-token latent via learnable latent tokens (images) or cross-attention layers (Perceiver). Latent tokens from both modalities within the same perturbation group are concatenated into a unified sequence and processed by a 4-layer Multimodal Set Transformer, enabling cross-modal and cross-cell attention. Modality-specific decoders (MAE-style ViT for images, 3-layer MLP with negative binomial NLL for gene expression) reconstruct the masked inputs, and the reconstruction loss is computed only on masked positions. The key insight is that contextual grouping by perturbation supplies mutual information that drives cross-modal attention and alignment without explicit cross-modal losses, and the jointly trained encoders remain independently usable at inference.

## Results

On the HepG2 dataset, PETRI achieves a Guide Consistency (GC) of 0.278 and StringDB edge classification AUC of 0.242, substantially outperforming all unimodal and late-fusion baselines. On Perturb-Multi, PETRI reaches GC=0.208 and StringDB AUC=0.260, again leading all competitors. Notably, PETRI trained on permuted (mismatched) data retains strong performance (HepG2: GC=0.274, StringDB=0.255), demonstrating robustness to weak cross-modal alignment. The only exception to PETRI's dominance is PCA on HepG2 GC (0.304 vs. 0.278), though PCA's StringDB score is far lower (0.150).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PETRI uniformly outperforms all four compared baselines on both datasets. Against scGPT (Cui et al., 2024), PETRI achieves 0.278 vs. 0.048 GC and 0.242 vs. 0.057 StringDB on HepG2, and 0.208 vs. 0.059 GC on Perturb-Multi. Against DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023), the gap is even wider: PETRI scores 0.278 vs. 0.008 GC on HepG2 and 0.208 vs. 0.015 GC on Perturb-Multi. The cross-modal autoencoder of Yang et al. (2021) performs near unimodal image baselines (HepG2 GC=0.032, StringDB=0.100), suggesting its latent-space alignment loss prevents effective fusion of gene expression into the frozen image embedding. CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), adapted for perturbation-level pairs on HepG2 and cell-level pairs on Perturb-Multi, underperforms even simple late-fusion concatenation (HepG2: 0.051 vs. 0.155 GC; Perturb-Multi: 0.000 vs. 0.000 GC), which the authors attribute to insufficient mutual information between the two modalities for contrastive learning to exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

PETRI departs from the dominant contrastive paradigm (CLIP, scCLIP, CellCLIP) for multimodal single-cell integration by adopting a joint-reconstruction objective inspired by VLM document processing (Flamingo, MoCa) and masked autoencoders. The architecture combines a ViT-based image encoder with a Perceiver-based transcriptomics encoder, both employing aggressive token resampling to handle the exploding sequence length when grouping multiple cells into a shared 'document.' This document-level framing—where cells are grouped by perturbation rather than paired at the cell level—repurposes the VLM insight that topical alignment (here, shared experimental context) is sufficient to enable cross-modal information sharing without explicit pairing, a setting where prior methods like cross-modal autoencoders (Yang et al.) and propensity-score alignment (Xi & Hartford) operate at the individual-cell level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PETRI addresses a fundamental practical constraint in perturbation screening: destructive assays make cell-level pairing of imaging and transcriptomics impossible, yet the two modalities capture complementary biological signals. The SAE-based analysis demonstrating 298 multimodal concepts (vs. 0 for permuted data) that are batch-robust and biologically interpretable provides a concrete pathway for linking molecular states to morphological phenotypes at single-cell resolution. Key open questions include how closely matched experimental contexts must be for cross-modal learning to engage, whether the document grouping can be re-centered on protein complexes or pathways to incorporate domain priors, and the need for task-grounded evaluation metrics beyond guide consistency and protein-protein interaction prediction that directly assess biological utility in therapeutic discovery.
