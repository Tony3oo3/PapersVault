## Problem

High-probability learning bounds for stochastic gradient descent with momentum (SGDM) in non-convex settings remain largely unexplored: existing convergence analyses are mostly in expectation, and the few high-probability results (Li & Orabona 2020; Cutkosky & Mehta 2021) yield slow rates under restrictive noise assumptions and provide no generalization guarantees. This paper fills that gap by establishing a systematic hierarchy of high-probability convergence and generalization bounds for SGDM under increasingly strong structural conditions on the objective.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes a training set S of n i.i.d. samples, an L-smooth (possibly non-convex) loss function, a step-size schedule (η_t = ct^{-1/2} in the general non-convex regime, η_t = 1/(μ(S)(t+t_0)) under PL), and a momentum parameter γ ∈ (0,1). It outputs a sequence of iterates x_t and, at the theoretical level, high-probability bounds on the average squared gradient norm (convergence), the last-iterate function-value gap (under PL), and the excess population risk (generalization). Empirically, the method is evaluated on six LIBSVM tabular classification datasets using logistic, Huber, and squared losses with sub-Weibull gradient noise injected per coordinate.

## Method

The analysis proceeds in three tiers of increasing structure. In the general non-convex case, a Lyapunov-style telescoping argument on the momentum vector m_t combined with sub-Weibull martingale concentration inequalities (a sub-Weibull Freedman-type bound) yields O(1/√T) high-probability convergence for the average squared gradient norm and O(√(d/n)) generalization via a uniform-convergence approach. Under the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition, the same machinery applied to the function-value error gives faster O(1/T) convergence and O((d+log(1/δ))/n) generalization for the last iterate. Finally, adding a Bernstein moment condition on the gradient at the optimum removes the dimension dependence and, in the low-noise regime F(x*)=O(1/n), achieves a dimension-free O(1/n²) excess-risk bound. The noise model is sub-Weibull with tail parameter θ ≥ 1/2, generalizing the sub-Gaussian assumption of prior work and explicitly quantifying how heavier tails degrade each rate.

## Results

The paper establishes high-probability convergence and generalization bounds for SGDM under sub-Weibull gradient noise. In the general non-convex case, the average squared gradient norm converges at rate Õ(1/√T), improving the log-dependence from Li & Orabona (2020). Under the PL condition, the function-value error of the last iterate achieves Õ(1/T), and the excess risk generalization bound improves to Õ((d+log(1/δ))/n). Under PL plus a Bernstein condition, the excess risk reaches Õ(1/n² + F(x*)/n), yielding Õ(1/n²) in the low-noise regime and removing the dimension dependence. Numerical experiments on six LIBSVM datasets confirm that heavier-tailed noise (larger θ) systematically degrades generalization, consistent with the theoretical predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Li & Orabona (2020), the paper tightens the sub-Gaussian convergence bound from O(log(T/δ) log T / √T) to O(log(1/δ) log T / √T), removing the log T factor from the confidence term. More importantly, the paper provides the first high-probability generalization bounds for SGDM, which are entirely absent in Li & Orabona (2020). Their delayed AdaGrad variant incurs a dimension-dependent rate O(d log^{3/2}(T/δ)/√T), whereas the present results are dimension-free in the convergence analysis. Compared to Cutkosky & Mehta (2021), the paper achieves a faster rate: their bound O(log(T/δ)/T^{(θ-1)/(3θ-2)}) for the (non-squared) gradient norm reduces to Õ(T^{-1/4}) at θ=2, while the paper's O(1/√T) for the squared gradient norm implies the same Õ(T^{-1/4}) via Jensen's inequality but with a strictly tighter logarithmic dependence. The paper also extends the noise model from θ-order moments (θ∈(1,2]) to the broader sub-Weibull family (θ≥1/2), covering sub-Gaussian, sub-exponential, and heavier-tailed distributions. Under the PL condition, the paper achieves Õ(1/T) convergence and Õ((d+log(1/δ))/n) generalization rates that have no prior counterpart for SGDM in the literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-probability analysis of SGDM initiated by Li & Orabona (2020) and Cutkosky & Mehta (2021) in two key directions: it generalizes the noise assumption from sub-Gaussian to the broader sub-Weibull family, and it introduces the first high-probability generalization bounds for SGDM, which were entirely absent from prior work. The convergence analysis builds on the Lyapunov-style telescoping argument of Li & Orabona (2020) but replaces their sub-Gaussian martingale concentration with sub-Weibull Freedman-type inequalities (Madden et al., 2024; Li, 2021). The generalization analysis departs from the algorithmic-stability approach (which is shown to fail for SGDM by Ramezani-Kebrya et al., 2024) and instead uses a uniform-convergence framework (Xu & Zeevi, 2020; Lei & Tang, 2021), augmented by a Bernstein moment condition to eliminate dimension dependence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a notable theoretical gap by providing the first high-probability generalization guarantees for SGDM and establishing a clean hierarchy of rates (non-convex → PL → PL+Bernstein) that quantifies exactly how structural assumptions on the objective and noise improve learning rates. The O(1/n²) excess-risk bound in the low-noise regime is rare in learning theory and, being dimension-free, is particularly relevant for high-dimensional neural network training. However, the empirical validation is limited to small-scale LIBSVM classification tasks with synthetic sub-Weibull noise, leaving open whether the theoretical rates manifest in practice for deep learning workloads. A key open question the authors highlight is whether momentum provides any theoretical advantage over plain SGD in the large-batch regime, where noise reduction might amplify the inertial benefit.
