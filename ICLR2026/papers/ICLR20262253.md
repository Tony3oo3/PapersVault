## Problem

Bayesian inversion of full-field physical quantities from sparse noisy observations is highly ill-posed, and standard priors are either too vague to yield accurate posteriors or require explicit knowledge of governing PDEs and boundary conditions. A critical additional challenge in engineering is that physical systems often have varying geometries, which prevents defining a single common prior over solution fields since the probability spaces are tied to each geometry. This paper addresses the gap of learning highly informative, geometry-aware priors that are entirely data-driven (no PDE knowledge required) and transferable across arbitrary in-distribution geometries.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes a dataset of (full-field solution, discretized mesh) pairs across varying geometries, with no observation process specified. At inference time, it takes sparse noisy point observations y_o (e.g., 5–50 sensor readings), the target geometry as a graph M_o, and a known observation operator H_o (a selection matrix), and produces a full posterior distribution over the solution field u_o on M_o, optionally including a posterior over the observation noise σ_o. The key design constraint is that the observation process (sensor count, placement, noise model) is entirely decoupled from training, enabling a train-once-use-everywhere foundation model.

## Method

GABI trains a geometry-conditioned graph autoencoder (encoder E^θ and decoder D^ψ, both GNNs) that maps variable-sized mesh fields to/from a fixed-dimensional latent vector z, with the latent distribution regularized toward N(0,I) via MMD divergence alongside a reconstruction loss. A central theoretical result (Lemma 2.1, Theorem 2.2) proves that the Bayesian posterior under a pushforward prior D_o#q_z equals the pushforward of the latent posterior p_{z|y_o}, so inversion can be performed entirely in the low-dimensional latent space and decoded back to the field. At inference, the latent posterior is sampled via GPU-parallelized Approximate Bayesian Computation (ABC) or NUTS MCMC, and samples are decoded through D_o^ψ to yield posterior samples of the full field. The framework is architecture-agnostic (tested with GCN + nonlocal averaging, GEN, and Transformer) and extends naturally to joint estimation of observation noise by augmenting the latent space with σ_o.

## Results

On the steady-state heat problem, GABI-ABC achieves a field MAE of 1.58×10⁻² with 80.91% of ground truth values within 1 standard deviation and 95.59% within 2 standard deviations, comparable to the supervised Direct Map (MAE 1.25×10⁻²) while providing full calibrated uncertainty. On the more challenging car resonance problem, GABI-ABC dramatically outperforms Direct Map, reducing MAE from 6.03×10⁻¹ to 2.32×10⁻¹ for the vibration field and from 2.46×10⁻¹ to 2.26×10⁻³ for source localization. GABI also uniquely estimates observational noise (σ MAE of 7.96×10⁻¹), a task infeasible for the Direct Map baseline. The method scales to 5k+ RANS terrain simulations using 4 GPUs with 52 hr training and 480 s inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the supervised Direct Map (Duthé et al., 2025), GABI-ABC achieves comparable or slightly higher MAE on the heat problem (1.58×10⁻² vs 1.25×10⁻²) and airfoil pressure (6.92×10⁻² vs 5.35×10⁻²), but uniquely provides a full calibrated posterior with 78–81% of values within 1σ. On the car resonance problem, GABI-ABC substantially outperforms Direct Map, reducing MAE by 2.6× for the vibration field (2.32×10⁻¹ vs 6.03×10⁻¹) and by 109× for source localization (2.26×10⁻³ vs 2.46×10⁻¹), likely because the learned prior captures geometry-dependent physics that the direct regression cannot generalize to. Against graph Gaussian Process baselines (Mostowsky et al., 2024), GABI-ABC achieves 5–9× lower MAE (e.g., 1.58×10⁻² vs 8.46×10⁻² for GP Matérn 1/2) and substantially better UQ calibration (80.91% vs 66.36% within 1σ), with the RBF kernel GP performing particularly poorly (only 14.88% within 1σ). A key practical advantage is that GABI's training is independent of the observation process, whereas Direct Map requires the observation distribution to be fixed at training time; GABI also supports joint noise estimation, which neither baseline can perform.

## Relation to Prior Work

GABI repurposes the statistical autoencoder (specifically the MMD variant) and conditional graph neural networks into a geometry-aware generative prior for Bayesian inversion, departing from both VAE-based approaches (which the paper argues are structurally unsuitable in Appendix C) and physics-informed methods that require PDE knowledge. The 'learn first, observe later' paradigm explicitly contrasts with direct-map supervised inversion (e.g., Duthé et al. 2025) where the observation process must be fixed at training time, and with graph Gaussian Process regression which assumes a parametric prior structure. The theoretical backbone (pushforward posterior equivalence, Lemma 2.1) is a clean application of standard measure-theoretic results to the autoencoder setting, while the ABC sampling strategy for the latent posterior is a practical repurposing of likelihood-free inference to exploit GPU parallelism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The train-once-use-everywhere property—where the learned prior is independent of the observation process—addresses a genuine practical bottleneck in engineering ML deployment, where sensor configurations, noise levels, and quantities of interest vary across applications. The multi-GPU terrain flow demo (5k+ RANS simulations, 127GB dataset) demonstrates a credible path toward foundation-model-scale geometric priors for fluid dynamics. Key open questions include: how performance degrades for truly out-of-distribution geometries (the paper shows 'mild but limited' robustness), whether the MMD-based latent regularization is optimal versus Wasserstein or sliced-Wasserstein alternatives, and whether the framework extends to time-dependent or multi-physics coupling problems where the static full-field assumption breaks down.
