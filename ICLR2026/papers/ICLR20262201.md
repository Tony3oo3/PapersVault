## Problem

Exploring the Rashomon set of deep neural networks—the collection of models matching a reference model's accuracy while differing in predictive behavior—remains computationally challenging. Existing approaches rely on costly retraining, scale poorly (Adversarial Weight Perturbation), or offer limited explicit control over diversity (dropout sampling), leaving a gap for an efficient, gradient-free method that can systematically generate functionally distinct yet accurate model variants.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single pretrained reference model (MLP, VGG16, ResNet-50, or ViT) with frozen weights and a labeled dataset, requiring no gradient access or retraining. It produces a set of diverse model variants (the empirical Rashomon set) whose membership is enforced on a validation set, with diversity metrics (discrepancy, ambiguity, Viable Prediction Range, Rashomon Capacity) reported only on held-out test data. The latent vector dimension d (ranging 2–64) controls the effective search space size, and the Rashomon tolerance ε defines the accuracy margin for set membership.

## Method

DIVERSE wraps a frozen pretrained network with Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) layers whose modulation parameters γ(z) and β(z) are derived from a shared latent vector z through frozen random projection matrices, creating a low-dimensional modulation space where z=0 recovers the reference model. CMA-ES with full covariance adaptation searches this space, guided by a fitness function that multiplies a diversity score (a λ-weighted mix of Total Variation Distance and hard label disagreement) by a Gaussian penalty centered at zero relative loss increase, softly enforcing the Rashomon ε-constraint without hard rejection. Three FiLM insertion strategies (after dense layers, after convolutional blocks, on residual skip connections) accommodate different architectures, and the shared latent vector ensures coordinated, network-wide modulation. The approach requires no retraining or gradient access, completing searches in minutes versus hours for retraining baselines while achieving comparable or superior diversity over dropout sampling.

## Results

On MNIST, DIVERSE achieves a Rashomon Ratio near 1.0 (nearly all candidates satisfy the tolerance), with discrepancy and ambiguity growing as ε increases. On the deeper PneumoniaMNIST (ResNet-50) and CIFAR-10 (VGG16) models, Rashomon Ratios are lower, but diversity metrics still increase meaningfully with ε. In terms of runtime, DIVERSE generates 640 candidate models in 3 min 16 s on MNIST, 7 min 15 s on PneumoniaMNIST, and 8 min 42 s on CIFAR-10, compared to 1 h 57 min, 8 h 16 min, and 12 h 37 min for retraining, and 1 min 57 s, 5 min 47 s, and 6 min 30 s for dropout-based sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to retraining, DIVERSE is 30–90× faster (minutes vs. hours) while achieving comparable diversity: on MNIST it exceeds retraining on discrepancy at higher ε values, and on PneumoniaMNIST it outperforms retraining on discrepancy at the highest ε level, though it falls short at stricter thresholds. Compared to dropout-based sampling (Hsu et al., 2024), DIVERSE outperforms dropout across all diversity metrics on CIFAR-10 and on most metrics on MNIST (all except VPR), while on PneumoniaMNIST it wins on discrepancy at the highest ε but trails at stricter tolerances. DIVERSE consistently reports lower VPR values than both baselines across all datasets. A key methodological advantage is that DIVERSE enforces Rashomon membership on a validation set and evaluates diversity only on held-out test data, whereas dropout defines membership directly on the test set, introducing an optimistic bias. Overall, DIVERSE offers a favorable efficiency–diversity trade-off: it is slower than dropout by a factor of ~2–3× but substantially faster than retraining, while delivering equal or superior diversity in most settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the Rashomon set exploration literature for deep neural networks, which has been approached through retraining from different seeds (Ganesh, 2024; Eerlings et al., 2025), Adversarial Weight Perturbation (Hsu & Calmon, 2022), and dropout-based stochastic sampling (Hsu et al., 2024). DIVERSE departs from all three by repurposing FiLM layers (Perez et al., 2018)—originally designed for conditional visual reasoning—as a frozen, low-dimensional modulation interface around a pretrained model, and then applying CMA-ES (Hansen, 2006) to search this latent space without gradients or retraining. The conceptual lineage is thus: FiLM provides the parameterization of a local model family, while CMA-ES provides the gradient-free search mechanism, combining two well-studied tools in a novel configuration for multiplicity exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIVERSE offers a practical, minutes-scale alternative to hours-long retraining for constructing diverse model sets, making systematic multiplicity studies more accessible. The layerwise sensitivity analysis revealing that diversity is concentrated in specific architectural positions (early layers for MLPs, mid-level convolutions for VGG16) opens a path toward targeted, more efficient modulation. However, the method's reliance on full-covariance CMA-ES limits it to low latent dimensions (d≤8 for deeper networks), and FiLM modulation does not guarantee that discovered differences are causally or interpretively meaningful. Extending to higher-dimensional search (e.g., via DD-CMA-ES), broader modalities, and Transformer architectures beyond the preliminary ViT experiment remain important open questions.
