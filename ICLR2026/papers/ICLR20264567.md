## Problem

On-policy methods like PPO achieve fast wall-clock convergence in massively parallel simulation for humanoid pretraining but are sample-inefficient and unsafe for finetuning in new environments due to stochastic exploration. Off-policy and model-based methods improve sample efficiency but have not been validated for large-scale humanoid pretraining or safe real-robot adaptation. The paper addresses how to bridge the gap between fast large-scale pretraining and safe, data-efficient finetuning for humanoid robots.

## Input / Output

The actor consumes a 47-dimensional proprioceptive state (gravity vector, gyroscope, joystick velocity command, gait phase, joint angles/velocities, previous action) while the critic receives an 110-dimensional privileged state adding raw sensor signals, actuator forces, contact booleans, and foot kinematics. The policy outputs a continuous 12D (T1 low-dim), 23D (full T1), or 29D (G1) vector of joint position targets in [-1,1], converted to motor torques via a PD controller. During finetuning, only the deterministic mean action is executed in the environment; stochastic sampling is confined to world-model rollouts.

## Method

LIFT is a three-stage pipeline using SAC as the unified backbone. Stage (i) pretrains the policy in MuJoCo Playground with 1,024–4,096 parallel environments, JAX-based large-batch updates at UTD=10, and domain randomization, achieving zero-shot sim-to-real deployment in ~30 minutes on a single RTX 4090. Stage (ii) trains a physics-informed world model offline from logged SAC transitions: a residual network predicts contact and dissipative torques, which are injected into a differentiable Lagrangian dynamics pipeline (Brax primitives M(q), C(q,q̇), G(q) integrated via semi-implicit Euler) to produce next-state predictions with learned per-feature uncertainty. Stage (iii) finetunes by executing deterministic actions in the target environment (Brax or real robot) while the stochastic SAC actor explores exclusively within the world model, generating synthetic rollouts of horizon 20 to update the actor-critic; physics-consistent rewards are computed analytically from predicted states rather than a learned reward model, and safety resets terminate rollouts when kinematic bounds are violated.

## Results

LIFT achieves zero-shot sim-to-real deployment on a physical Booster T1 humanoid after approximately 30 minutes of wall-clock pretraining on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU (reduced from ~7 hours via Optuna hyperparameter tuning). During finetuning in Brax, LIFT converges within 4×10⁴ environment steps (≈800 s of robot interaction at 50 Hz) across in-distribution, long-tail, and out-of-distribution velocity targets (0.6–1.5 m/s), while all model-free baselines fail. In real-world finetuning, only 80–590 s of collected data suffices to transform an initially unstable policy into a stable walking gait with improved velocity tracking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In pretraining (Figure 7), LIFT matches or slightly exceeds PPO and FastTD3 in peak return across six humanoid tasks, while converging faster on rough terrain. In the critical finetuning comparison (Figure 2), LIFT is the only method that stably converges to the target velocity across all three speed regimes (0.6, 1.0, 1.5 m/s). PPO initially tracks reasonably but gradually degrades and ultimately collapses under deterministic data collection; FastTD3 exhibits strong oscillations and never converges; plain SAC (without the world model) diverges rapidly due to overfitting on limited deterministic data. SSRL (physics-informed, trained from scratch) shows partial convergence only at 0.6 m/s and fails entirely at 1.0–1.5 m/s, underscoring the necessity of large-scale pretraining. In the ablation replacing the physics-informed world model with an MBPO-style ensemble (Figure 5), MBPO fails to converge (episode return remains near zero) because out-of-distribution actions produce physically implausible predictions that destabilize the critic, whereas LIFT's Lagrangian-based model maintains stable rollouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SSRL's (Levy et al., 2024) physics-informed world model—originally designed for quadruped locomotion trained from scratch—to a pretrain-then-finetune pipeline for humanoids, using SAC as the unified backbone algorithm across both stages. It departs from the PPO-dominated paradigm in massively parallel humanoid simulation by demonstrating that off-policy SAC with high UTD and large-batch updates achieves comparable pretraining performance while enabling stable, sample-efficient finetuning. The key architectural departure from prior model-based finetuning work (MBPO, Dreamer) is the separation of deterministic action execution in the real environment from stochastic exploration confined to the learned world model, motivated by the safety constraints of humanoid robots with small support polygons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, open-source pipeline that couples the wall-clock speed of large-scale GPU simulation with the sample efficiency of model-based learning, enabling humanoid finetuning with only minutes of real-world data—a significant step toward continuous on-robot adaptation. However, current limitations (Vicon-dependent base-height estimation, sequential finetuning iterations, proprioception-only inputs) constrain real-world deployment, and the sim-to-sim finetuning results, while strong, do not yet fully validate the approach across diverse real-world conditions. Open questions include scaling to vision-based tasks, object-centric dynamics, hierarchical control, and automated safety mechanisms that would reduce human supervision during real-world finetuning.
