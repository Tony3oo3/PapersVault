## Problem

Vector quantisation (VQ) for latent image representations suffers from sparse training signals and codebook collapse as embedding dimensionality grows, limiting its scalability for high-fidelity generation. Product quantisation (PQ), while a classical encoding technique, has seen limited exploration as a latent representation mechanism for generative models, and its interaction with codebook size, subspace factorisation, and embedding dimensionality remains uncharacterised. Additionally, standard continuous latent spaces (e.g., Stable Diffusion's VAE) degrade in reconstruction quality at high resolution, introducing artefacts that propagate through the generative process.

## Input / Output

The autoencoder takes RGB images (256×256 for training, up to 1536×1536 for generation) and produces a spatially downsampled latent (factor F=8, 16, or 32) where each spatial location is encoded as S independent codebook indices (one per subspace) rather than a single VQ index, yielding a fictive codebook of size K^S. For diffusion-based generation, the adapted StableDiffusion2.1 additionally accepts a text prompt and outputs a full-resolution image. The quantised latent representation uses only 128–512 codebook entries per subspace, far smaller than the 100,000+ entries required by competing VQ methods.

## Method

PQGAN replaces the single codebook lookup in VQGAN with product quantisation: each d-dimensional latent vector is split into S disjoint subspaces of dimension d/S, and each subspace is independently quantised against its own codebook of K entries, creating a combinatorial fictive codebook of size K^S without storing all combinations. The key theoretical insight is that PQ's training signal density and quantisation error remain constant in fixed low-dimensional subspaces regardless of total latent dimension d, so PQ benefits from higher d while VQ degrades — an opposite scaling behaviour the paper confirms empirically. Training uses the standard VQGAN objective (reconstruction + commitment + perceptual + adversarial loss) unchanged. For diffusion integration, only the first and last convolutional layers of StableDiffusion2.1's U-Net are widened to match the new latent channel dimensionality (4→128 or 4→256), trained in two stages (frozen backbone for 20k steps, then full fine-tuning), enabling either 4× faster generation at fixed resolution or 2× output resolution at equal compute cost.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, PQGAN with F=8 (d=128, S=64, K=512) achieves a PSNR of 37.4 dB, rFID of 0.036, CMMD of 0.011, and LPIPS of 0.0024, substantially outperforming all prior quantised and continuous baselines. The second-best PQGAN configuration at F=16 (d=128, S=64, K=128) still reaches PSNR 28.3 dB and rFID 0.41, surpassing methods that use 16× more latent pixels. When integrated into Stable Diffusion 2.1, PQSD-HR generates 1536×1536 images at the same computational cost (0.112 samples/s, 14.9 GB) as SDv2.1 generating 768×768, while PQSD-Quick achieves a 4× speed-up (0.465 samples/s) at 768×768 with only 7.7 GB memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PQGAN decisively outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric at comparable or lower spatial resolution. Against the strongest discrete baseline VQGAN-LC (Zhu et al., 2024) at F=8, PQGAN improves PSNR from 27.0 to 37.4 dB (+10.4 dB) and reduces rFID from 1.29 to 0.036 (−97%), while using a codebook 195× smaller (K=512 vs. K=100000). Compared to the continuous SDv2.1 VAE (Rombach et al., 2022) at the same F=8, PQGAN achieves 12.1 dB higher PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.0573 to 0.0024 (−96%). Against VQGAN (Esser et al., 2021) at F=16, PQGAN at the same F=16 improves PSNR by 8.6 dB and rFID by 92%. The multi-channel quantisation baseline Mo-VQGAN (Zheng et al., 2022) and residual quantisation RQ-VAE (Lee et al., 2022) are both clearly outperformed, with PQGAN at F=16 beating Mo-VQGAN by 5.9 dB PSNR and RQ-VAE at F=32 by 2.3 dB PSNR despite using 4× more spatial resolution. On the transferability evaluation (Table 2), PQGAN at F=8 also dominates SDXL VAE (Podell et al., 2024) on FFHQ (PSNR 42.1 vs. 30.0) and LSUN (PSNR 38.3 vs. 24.9), confirming that the gains are not dataset-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes product quantisation (PQ), a classical technique from approximate nearest-neighbor search (Jégou et al., 2010), into the latent autoencoder framework of VQGAN (Esser et al., 2021), replacing the single-codebook vector quantisation module with S independently learned sub-codebooks. Prior work on PQ in image compression (El-Nouby et al., 2023; Mentzer et al., 2020) was constrained by rate-distortion trade-offs, and multi-channel quantisation (Zheng et al., 2022) only partially addressed the high-dimensional codebook bottleneck. The key departure is applying PQ in the context of diffusion-based generative models, where the decoder operates directly in latent space without requiring an explicit autoregressive factorisation, thereby freeing PQ from the index-storage constraints that limited its prior exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: adapting Stable Diffusion to PQ latents enables either a 4× speedup at fixed resolution or a doubling of output resolution at equal compute cost, with no architectural changes beyond input/output projection layers. The finding that PQ and VQ exhibit opposite scaling behaviour with embedding dimension provides a principled guide for hyperparameter selection that was previously absent. Limitations include a ~50% quantisation overhead from codebook matching at the best configurations, and the approach is not directly compatible with autoregressive or index-based generation due to the exponential growth of joint index combinations. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video generation, whether the 50% overhead can be reduced via learned or approximate matching, and whether the high-dimensional latent space interacts favourably with newer transformer-based diffusion backbones (e.g., DiT, SD3).
