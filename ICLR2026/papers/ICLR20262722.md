## Problem

Existing LLM-based trajectory generation methods are developed and evaluated almost exclusively on routine (non-event) data, leaving their ability to model non-routine mobility deviations during large-scale societal events (disasters, pandemics, major events) unverified. Even when event context is provided, these methods lack a mechanism to reconcile the competing demands of a user's habitual patterns and event-imposed constraints, producing trajectories that either default to routine behavior or overfit to the event shock.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user's long-term and short-term check-in histories (POI, category, subcategory, lat/lon, timestamp) partitioned relative to the event start time, plus a structured event context derived by an LLM from raw event narratives into a four-aspect schema (event profile, intensity/scale, infrastructure impact, official directives). The output is a single-day trajectory as a sequence of (place, time) tuples at 10-minute resolution, generated iteratively with up to K=3 refinement passes. The event schema step is critical: raw free-form event text is transformed into a key-value format to prevent the LLM from overlooking mobility-relevant constraints during generation.

## Method

ELLMob is a self-aligned LLM framework grounded in Fuzzy-Trace Theory (FTT), which posits that decisions under uncertainty are driven by 'gist'—the essential meaning distilled from information. The framework extracts three structured gists: Pattern Gist (habitual tendencies from user history), Event Gist (constraint requirements from the event schema), and Action Gist (the LLM's tentative trajectory decision), each formalized along core attributes (e.g., Points of Inertia, Risk-Reward Calculus, Habit Adherence). A reflection-based alignment module then audits each candidate trajectory along two binary axes—internal alignment (coherence with habitual patterns) and external alignment (compliance with event constraints)—and triggers corrective refinement with specific failure rationales fed back to the generator. This differs from generic self-alignment methods (Reflexion, SELF-REFINE) that target hallucination correction: ELLMob's dual-axis auditing explicitly arbitrates between two competing decision bases, making the reconciliation process traceable and cognitively grounded rather than a surface-level error fix.

## Results

ELLMob achieves the lowest Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) across all four metrics (Step Interval, Step Distance, Category Distribution, Spatial Grid Distribution) on all three events. Headline improvements include a 32.3% reduction in SI for Typhoon Hagibis (0.0642 vs. 0.0949 for LLMOB) and a 16.5% reduction in SD for COVID-19 (0.0444 vs. 0.0532 for LLM-MOB). On the Tokyo 2021 Olympics, ELLMob attains the best SGD of 0.0035, nearly halving the next-best value of 0.0052. The paper reports an average 46.9% improvement over the strongest baselines across all three events.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELLMob outperforms all 13 baselines on every metric across all three events. Among deep learning baselines, TrajGAIL and DiffTraj are the strongest on spatial metrics (SGD) but fail badly on temporal (SI) and distance (SD) metrics, e.g., TrajGAIL's SD of 0.3591 on Typhoon vs. ELLMob's 0.0200. Among LLM-based baselines, LLMOB is the closest competitor on Typhoon (SI 0.0949 vs. 0.0642) and Olympics (SGD 0.0051 vs. 0.0035), while LLM-MOB is strongest on COVID-19 SD (0.0532 vs. 0.0444). LLM-ZS overcorrects toward event constraints, producing inflated SI on Typhoon (0.1574) and Olympics (0.0938). Among iterative-reflection baselines (Table 7), Air is the closest on Typhoon SI (0.0764 vs. 0.0642) and Olympics SGD (0.0035, tied), but ELLMob still leads on the remaining 11 metric-event combinations, confirming that FTT-guided dual-axis alignment outperforms generic self-correction strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM-based trajectory generation and cognitive decision theory. Prior LLM mobility methods (LLM-MOB, LLM-Move, LLMOB, LLM-ZS) treat trajectory generation as a single-pass or profile-augmented task focused on routine patterns, while iterative-refinement approaches (Reflexion, SELF-REFINE, AIR) target hallucination correction in unstructured text. ELLMob departs from both by repurposing Fuzzy-Trace Theory to frame mobility generation as a conflict-reconciliation problem between habitual gist and event gist, and by introducing structured event schemas to make exogenous shocks explicit to the model. The paper also contributes the first event-annotated mobility dataset, filling a data gap that prior methods could not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first event-annotated mobility dataset and the explicit task formulation of event-driven trajectory generation provide the field with both the empirical foundation and the problem definition needed to study non-routine mobility, which is critical for emergency planning and urban management under stress. The finding that existing LLM methods systematically fail under events (defaulting to either habit or constraint) reveals a concrete research gap that prior evaluations on stable-period data masked. Limitations include demographic bias inherent in LBSN data (skew toward younger users), primary focus on Tokyo, and reliance on proprietary LLM APIs for main results, though open-source backbone experiments partially mitigate the latter. Open questions include scalability to more diverse populations, real-time deployment, and whether the FTT-inspired alignment generalizes to multi-agent or city-scale simulations.
