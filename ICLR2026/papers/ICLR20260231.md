## Problem

Existing human motion forecasting methods handle either single-person kinematics, static scene context, or multi-person social interactions, but none simultaneously model both human-human and human-scene interactions in a unified framework for complex dynamic scenes. The closest prior work (SAST) decouples feature extraction for humans and scenes, relies on predefined semantic labels for scene objects, and fails to explicitly model interaction features, limiting its accuracy and real-world applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260231/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes three inputs: the target person's past 3D joint motion (H frames × J joints × 3D), a raw 3D scene point cloud (N=1000 points sampled within 2.5 m of the root joint, requiring no semantic segmentation), and the past motion sequences of K interactive persons. It outputs the target person's future 3D joint positions for T steps. Notably, the scene is consumed as an unlabelled point cloud rather than segmented objects, and all motion features are processed in the DCT frequency domain rather than raw time-domain coordinates.

## Method

HUMOF introduces a Hierarchical Interaction Representation that encodes human-human interactions via self-encoding (per-person motion features) plus relation-encoding (DCT-transformed interactive distances between joints), and human-scene interactions via progressive PointNet++ set abstraction layers that produce multi-scale point tokens with frequency-domain distance features. A Coarse-to-Fine Interaction Reasoning Module then processes these through 6 Transformer layers using two complementary mechanisms: (1) a coarse-to-fine injection strategy that feeds high-level (body-level, coarsest-scale) interaction tokens into early layers and low-level (joint-level, finest-scale) tokens into later layers, and (2) an adaptive DCT rescaling mechanism that element-wise suppresses high-frequency DCT coefficients in early layers and progressively restores them in deeper layers, with a sample-adaptive vector computed via MLP over pooled joint tokens. The motion encoder/decoder pair uses DCT+GCN and GCN+IDCT respectively, and the entire model is trained end-to-end with L2 path and pose losses.

## Results

On the dynamic-scene dataset HIK, HUMOF achieves a mean path error of 180.7 mm and mean pose error of 90.2 mm, outperforming the closest competitor SAST (189.0 / 93.2) by ~8 mm in path and ~3 mm in pose. On HOI-M³, HUMOF reaches 174.6 mm mean path error and 117.9 mm mean pose error, again the best among all seven baselines. On the static-scene datasets, HUMOF attains 41.7 mm mean path error (seen) and 64.1 mm mean pose error (seen) on HUMANISE, and 62.9 / 38.7 mm on GTA-IM, consistently leading all scene-aware and social-scene-aware methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HUMOF outperforms all seven baselines on both dynamic-scene datasets (HIK and HOI-M³) in both path and pose error. Against the closest social-scene-aware competitor SAST (Mueller et al., 2024), HUMOF reduces mean path error by 8.3 mm on HIK (180.7 vs. 189.0) and by 10.2 mm on HOI-M³ (174.6 vs. 184.8), while also improving pose error by 3.0 and 4.4 mm respectively. The social-aware methods IAFormer and T2P, which model only human-human interactions, trail HUMOF by 19.5–23.5 mm in mean path error on HIK and 11.7–19.5 mm on HOI-M³. Scene-aware methods (ContAware, GIMO, STAG, MutualDistance) that model only human-scene interactions show the largest gaps, with mean path errors 55–84 mm higher than HUMOF on HIK. On the static-scene datasets (HUMANISE, GTA-IM), HUMOF similarly leads all baselines, achieving 41.7 mm mean path error on HUMANISE (seen) versus 45.6 for MutualDistance and 62.1 for SAST, and 62.9 mm on GTA-IM versus 72.0 for MutualDistance and 85.2 for SAST. Notably, HUMOF does not require ground-truth instance segmentation (needed by SAST) and operates directly on raw 3D point clouds, making it more practical for real-world deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—scene-aware motion prediction (ContactAware, GIMO, STAG, MutualDistance) and social-aware multi-person prediction (T2P, IAFormer)—into a single framework that simultaneously models human-human and human-scene interactions. It builds on the DCT+GCN motion encoding/decoding paradigm from Mao et al. (2019) and repurposes PointNet++ set abstraction layers (Qi et al., 2017) to construct hierarchical scene representations without requiring semantic labels. The closest prior work, SAST (Mueller et al., 2024), also targets the combined setting but decouples feature extraction for humans and scenes and relies on ground-truth instance segmentation, which HUMOF explicitly avoids through its holistic point-cloud approach and explicit distance-based interaction encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the dependency on predefined scene semantic labels and unifying both interaction types in one architecture, HUMOF offers a more practical path toward real-world deployment where raw sensor data lacks object-level annotations. The coarse-to-fine injection strategy combined with adaptive DCT rescaling provides a generalizable recipe for integrating multi-scale environmental context into Transformer-based predictors. Key limitations include difficulty with abrupt motion changes, a monotonous distance-based interaction paradigm, and the fact that joint multi-person inference does not yet account for predicted future interactions between individuals. The preliminary validation on dynamic scene objects suggests architectural readiness but leaves open whether the approach scales to genuinely dynamic environments with sufficient training data.
