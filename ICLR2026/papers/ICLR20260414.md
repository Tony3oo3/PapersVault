## Problem

Existing utility-centric unlearning metrics (e.g., ROUGE-L, Truth Ratio, KnowMem) cannot be interpreted on their own to measure the extent of unlearning without referencing a prohibitively expensive retrained LLM, and they degrade to near-random performance when the forget and retain sets contain semantically similar data. This paper asks whether a data-centric metric exists whose values are directly interpretable, calibrated to the degree of imperfect unlearning, and robust to data similarity between forget and retain sets.

## Input / Output

The metric takes as input an LLM's text output (generated in response to a query formed from a specific data point) and a watermark key unique to the data owner, and produces a scalar verification score indicating the likelihood that the owner's watermark is present in the output. Only query access to the LLM is required—no weight or logit access is needed. The aggregate unlearning metric is the uniform average of verification scores over all data points in the forget set, yielding a continuous value where 0 corresponds to perfect unlearning and higher values indicate greater residual influence of the forget set.

## Method

WaterDrum builds on the Waterfall text watermarking framework, where each data owner watermarks their training data with a unique key by paraphrasing the text while embedding token-level perturbation signals into the LLM paraphraser's logits (using ID-dependent orthogonal perturbation functions and pseudorandom vocabulary permutations). The key insight is that watermarked data creates a clear counterfactual: a model not trained on a given owner's watermarked data will not contain that owner's watermark signal, enabling direct verification without a retrained reference model. The verification operator simply accesses the correct vocabulary permutation for the owner's key and computes a dot product in the permuted token space, yielding a signal if the watermark is present or noise otherwise. This design satisfies four desiderata: separability (AUROC ≈ 1 across all data-similarity settings), calibration (aggregate metric proportional to the fraction of forget set still influencing the model, R² > 0.96), feasibility (query-access only, no retrained LLM needed), and robustness to similar data (orthogonal watermark keys ensure identical or paraphrased content from different owners carries distinct, independently verifiable signals). The paper also introduces WaterDrum-Ax, a benchmark of 8,000 arXiv abstracts across 20 subject categories with controllable levels of data similarity (exact duplicates, semantic duplicates, no duplicates) for rigorous evaluation.

## Results

On the WaterDrum-TOFU dataset, WaterDrum achieves AUROC of 0.9926–0.9954 across all three data-similarity settings (exact duplicate, semantic duplicate, no duplicate), while ROUGE drops to 0.510 and Truth Ratio to 0.508 under exact duplicates. On the WaterDrum-Ax dataset, WaterDrum maintains AUROC ≥ 0.9957 in every setting, whereas KnowMem stays near random (0.450–0.492) and ROUGE falls to 0.334 under exact duplicates. For calibration (D2), WaterDrum achieves R² values of 0.963–0.991, while all baselines produce large negative R² (e.g., KnowMem: −498.1; MIA: −285.6) under exact duplicates, indicating they cannot measure the extent of imperfect unlearning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WaterDrum decisively outperforms all four baselines on separability (D1/AUROC) under data-similarity stress. ROUGE (Lin, 2004; Maini et al., 2024) is the strongest baseline, reaching AUROC ≈ 0.99 under 'No Duplicate' and 'Semantic Duplicate' on WaterDrum-Ax, but collapses to 0.334 under 'Exact Duplicate' (WaterDrum-Ax) and 0.510 (WaterDrum-TOFU), making it no better than random. Truth Ratio (Maini et al., 2024) performs poorly in every setting, with AUROC ranging from 0.472 to 0.747, failing D1 even without data overlap. KnowMem (Shi et al., 2025) is consistently near chance level (AUROC 0.450–0.492) across all settings on WaterDrum-Ax. MIA (Shi et al., 2024) is excluded from the D1 experiment but fails calibration (D2) with R² as low as −285.6 under exact duplicates, whereas WaterDrum maintains R² ≥ 0.963 in all settings. In summary, WaterDrum is the only metric that simultaneously satisfies separability, calibration, and robustness to similar data without requiring a retrained reference model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Waterfall text watermarking framework (Lau et al., 2024) to propose the first data-centric unlearning metric for LLMs, departing from the dominant utility-centric paradigm (ROUGE-L, Truth Ratio, KnowMem, MIA) that evaluates unlearning via model performance indicators. It extends the concept of watermark-based unlearning verification from image classification (Guo et al., 2023; Sommer et al., 2022) to the LLM setting, where longer output sequences provide richer signals. The paper also introduces WaterDrum-Ax, a new benchmark dataset that addresses a key limitation of existing unlearning benchmarks (TOFU, MUSE, WMDP): they do not account for semantically similar data across forget and retain sets, a common real-world scenario.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practical gap that undermines the current evaluation paradigm: utility-centric metrics cannot be interpreted without a retrained LLM reference, which is prohibitively expensive and contradicts the premise of unlearning. The WaterDrum-Ax dataset and the defined desiderata (D1–D4) provide the community with a more rigorous and interpretable framework for evaluating unlearning algorithms. Open questions include whether the watermarking assumption (data owners must watermark their data before submission) will be adopted in practice, and the paper leaves systematic benchmarking of unlearning algorithms to future work, providing only preliminary results. The approach is also limited to query-access verification and relies on a specific watermarking framework (Waterfall), though the authors demonstrate it can be adapted to other methods with trade-offs.
