## Problem

Multi-task learning suffers from gradient conflicts that cause some tasks to consistently dominate updates while others are compromised, yet existing fairness methods rely on pairwise metrics (cosine similarity, inner products) that cannot capture coalition-level gradient structure, or on performance-drop metrics (Δm) that require exponential retraining. No prior work provides a scalable, geometrically grounded utility function over arbitrary task subsets that is directly compatible with a principled fair-division rule for redistributing gradient updates.

## Input / Output

At each training step, the method consumes the N task gradients (each of dimension d) and a temperature parameter τ; it normalizes the gradients, constructs the N×N Gram matrix M = ĜᵀĜ in a single pass, and extracts submatrices M_S for all (or K sampled) task subsets S to compute Shapley values φ_i. The output is a set of per-task weights ω_i (softmax of φ_i/τ) and a single aggregated gradient direction d = Σ α_i g_i that is a convex combination of the task gradients, used to update θ. For large task sets (e.g., CelebA with 40 tasks), Monte Carlo permutation sampling with K ≈ 1000–5000 subsets replaces exhaustive enumeration, adding only O(K·N³) determinant evaluations on top of the dominant training cost O(C).

## Method

SVFair introduces two geometric conflict metrics—VolDet, the squared parallelotope volume det(M_S) spanned by normalized task gradients in subset S, and VolDetPro, which augments VolDet with a sign-aware penalty over negative-cosine pairs to distinguish obtuse from acute configurations sharing the same volume. These metrics serve as the set-function utility v(S) in the Shapley value formula, so that each task's φ_i quantifies its marginal contribution to overall gradient conflict (i.e., its deviation from the collective gradient direction). The Shapley values are softmax-normalized into weights ω_i and plugged into a PIVRG-style optimization problem (minimize Σ ω_i/(g_iᵀd) subject to g_iᵀd > 0), whose KKT solution yields closed-form gradient weights α_i = ω_i/(g_iᵀd)². The paper proves that under standard L-smoothness and linear-independence assumptions, the resulting updates converge to a Pareto stationary point at rate O(1/ε²), and demonstrates that the same Shapley-based weights can be dropped into existing loss-based MTL methods (RLW, DWA, UW) as a fairness-enhancing module without architectural changes.

## Results

On NYU-v2 (3-task), SVFair(VolDet) achieves the best Mean Rank of 1.44 and the largest overall performance improvement of Δm% = −8.81, substantially outperforming the next-best PIVRG (MR 3.56, Δm% −6.50). On Cityscapes (2-task), SVFair(VolDetPro) attains the best Δm% of −2.40 with MR 6.12. On the 40-task CelebA benchmark, SVFair(VolDet) achieves MR 4.15 and Δm% −0.63, the only method to improve over single-task learning. On the MT10 reinforcement learning benchmark, both SVFair variants reach a 0.97 average success rate, edging out PIVRG (0.96) and Nash-MTL (0.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVFair outperforms all 13 baselines across every benchmark. The closest competitor is PIVRG (Qin et al., 2025b), which SVFair surpasses by 2.12 MR points and 2.31 Δm% points on NYU-v2, and by 0.01 success rate on MT10 (0.97 vs. 0.96). FairGrad (Ban & Ji, 2024) is the strongest gradient-based baseline on Cityscapes (MR 3.25) but SVFair(VolDetPro) still leads with MR 6.12 and a far better Δm% (−2.40 vs. 5.12), indicating FairGrad's weakness on the harder Depth task. Nash-MTL (Navon et al., 2022) achieves the best Cityscapes MR (4.25) but suffers a positive Δm% of 6.72, whereas SVFair achieves negative Δm% on both variants. Loss-based methods (RLW, DWA, UW) are consistently the weakest, with Δm% values ranging from 0.56 to 7.78 on NYU-v2, confirming that gradient-level fairness signals provide substantially better task balancing. On the 40-task CelebA benchmark, SVFair(VolDet) is the only method achieving a negative Δm% (−0.63), while all baselines degrade relative to single-task learning (Δm% from 0.37 to 14.85). On MT10, SVFair matches or exceeds PIVRG (0.97 vs. 0.96) and Nash-MTL (0.91), while MTL SAC without any fairness mechanism collapses to 0.49 success rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVFair builds directly on the PIVRG optimization framework (Qin et al., 2025b) and the KKT-based gradient aggregation approach of FairGrad (Ban & Ji, 2024), replacing their higher-order performance-drop weights with Shapley-value-derived fairness weights. The key departure is the introduction of coalition-level geometric conflict metrics (VolDet, VolDetPro) based on Gram-determinant parallelotope volume, which supersedes the pairwise cosine-similarity and inner-product metrics used in PCGrad, CAGrad, and IMTL-G. While Shapley values have appeared in MTL-adjacent contexts (feature attribution, multi-agent RL), this paper claims to be the first to embed them as the core gradient-aggregation mechanism in MTL, and the Gram-matrix formulation makes the computation tractable in a single training pass rather than requiring per-subset retraining as Δm-based utilities would.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, game-theoretic justification for fair gradient allocation in MTL and demonstrates that the Shapley value can serve as a lightweight drop-in module that improves existing loss-based methods (RLW, DWA, UW) without architectural changes. This is practically relevant for safety-critical multi-task deployments where worst-task performance matters. Open questions include whether the VolDetPro sign-aware penalty is the optimal correction for sign insensitivity (the current formulation is heuristic), how the method behaves in highly non-convex or stochastic-gradient regimes beyond the L-smooth assumption, and whether the Monte Carlo Shapley approximation introduces bias that accumulates over long training runs. The scalability story is convincing up to 40 tasks (CelebA), but behavior at 100+ tasks remains untested.
