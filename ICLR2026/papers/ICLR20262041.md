## Problem

Existing geometry-aware federated learning methods rely on a global linearity assumption, modeling data distributions with a single linear subspace or ellipsoid. This fails catastrophically for real-world data concentrated on low-dimensional nonlinear manifolds, causing calibration to generate out-of-distribution pseudo-samples that mislead model training. No prior framework learns and leverages the local, nonlinear manifold geometry in a privacy-preserving federated setting.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely in the frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 embedding space (512-dim vectors), never touching raw images. Each client receives a Global Anonymous Basis of 512 shared reference points and a Geometry Dictionary mapping macro-prototypes to fused geometric templates (eigenvalues plus GAB-projected coefficient vectors). The output is a set of calibrated embeddings—on-manifold pseudo-samples generated via pre-image optimization—which are paired with original labels to train local prompt parameters. The GAB is constructed once offline from differentially-private prototypes, and the dictionary is updated each communication round.

## Method

FedMC replaces the global linear PCA assumption with a three-phase nonlinear manifold framework. In a preparatory phase, clients upload DP-noised K-Means prototypes that the server clusters into a Global Anonymous Basis (GAB), a shared anonymous coordinate system. In Phase I (federated aggregation), each client partitions its embeddings into small clusters, runs Kernel PCA (RBF kernel) on each to capture local curvature, then projects the resulting principal components onto the GAB to produce secure Local Geometry Descriptors; the server meta-clusters these descriptors by region and fuses them via a weighted least-squares solution into a global Geometry Dictionary. In Phase II (calibration), for each local embedding the client queries the dictionary for the nearest macro-prototype, projects the point onto the retrieved principal components, perturbs the projection coordinates with variance-scaled Gaussian noise (staying in the local tangent space), and reconstructs the pre-image in the original embedding space by minimizing a kernel-matching loss via gradient descent. This ensures all generated pseudo-samples lie on the learned manifold rather than along a global Euclidean shortcut.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (β=0.1), FedMC(FedVTP) achieves 85.08% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline FedVTP (81.01%) by 4.07% and the linear geometry baseline GGEUR (82.55%) by 2.53%. On Tiny-ImageNet (β=0.1), FedMC reaches 80.12%, a 2.54% gain over FedVTP. In the most challenging domain skew setting (DomainNet, β=0.1), FedMC achieves 85.93%, leading FedVTP by 2.11% and GGEUR by 2.08%. Across all seven general FL algorithms on Office-Home-LDS (β=0.1), FedMC consistently outperforms both the base method and GGEUR, with the largest absolute gain of 20.33% over FedDyn base (65.99% → 86.32%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMC(FedVTP) wins against all 15 baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity level. Against the primary FPL baselines, it leads FedVTP (Jia et al., ECCV 2022) by 4.07% on CIFAR-100 (β=0.1) and 2.54% on Tiny-ImageNet (β=0.1), and outperforms FedCoOp (Zhou et al., IJCV 2022) by 10.41% on CIFAR-100 (β=0.1). It also surpasses PromptFL (Guo et al., IEEE TMC 2023) by 12.89%, FedTPG (Qiu et al., ICLR 2024) by 16.45%, FedPR (Feng et al., CVPR 2023) by 6.17%, and FedCLIP (Lu et al., arXiv 2023) by 14.44% on CIFAR-100 (β=0.1). The critical comparison is against GGEUR (Ma et al., CVPR 2025), the global linear geometry baseline: FedMC leads by 2.53% on CIFAR-100 (β=0.1) and 2.10% on Tiny-ImageNet (β=0.1), directly validating the nonlinear manifold hypothesis over the linear assumption. In the general FL setting (Table 3, Office-Home-LDS β=0.1), FedMC outperforms FedAvg (McMahan et al., 2017) by 14.97%, SCAFFOLD (Karimireddy et al., 2019) by 10.43%, MOON (Li et al., CVPR 2021) by 3.90%, FedDyn (Acar et al., 2021) by 20.33%, FedOPT (Reddi et al., ICLR 2021) by 20.99%, FedNTD (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2022) by 9.45%, and FedProto (Tan et al., AAAI 2022) by 16.52%—with the FedProto comparison being particularly telling since FedProto already shares first-order prototype statistics, demonstrating that higher-order geometric information provides a fundamentally stronger calibration signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends and corrects the geometry-based calibration line initiated by Ma et al. (2025a, CVPR) and Ma et al. (2023), which model the global data distribution as a single linear subspace (hyper-ellipsoid via covariance eigendecomposition). FedMC replaces this global linear model with a localized, nonlinear approach: clients perform kernel PCA on small data patches to capture local manifold curvature, and the server aggregates these into a 'geometry dictionary' that maps regions of the manifold to their consensus tangent-space geometry. The framework is instantiated within Federated Prompt Learning (FedVTP) but is validated as a plug-and-play module across seven classic FL algorithms (FedAvg, SCAFFOLD, MOON, FedDyn, FedOPT, FedNTD, FedProto), positioning it as a general calibration layer rather than a method tied to one FL paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode—systematic generation of out-of-distribution pseudo-samples when calibrating along a global linear direction that cuts through empty space on a curved manifold—and provides a principled, privacy-preserving remedy. Its generality across seven FL algorithms and three heterogeneity types (label skew, domain skew, combined) suggests the nonlinear manifold prior is a broadly applicable calibration signal. Open questions include the sensitivity to kernel bandwidth selection, the computational cost of iterative pre-image reconstruction at scale, and whether the local-flatness assumption (small K-Means patches) holds for highly curved or multi-modal manifolds. The framework also raises the question of whether higher-order geometric invariants (beyond tangent-space PCA) could further improve calibration fidelity.
