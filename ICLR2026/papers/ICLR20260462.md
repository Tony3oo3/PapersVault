## Problem

Existing quantum federated learning (QFL) methods assume all clients run the same-depth parameterized quantum circuit, ignoring the reality that quantum hardware varies in error rates and thus constrains the maximum circuit depth each client can reliably execute. This depth heterogeneity creates parameter mismatches across clients that degrade training, and the quantum-specific challenge of measurement collapse makes it nontrivial to evaluate layerwise loss functions needed to synchronize heterogeneous-depth clients. Additionally, deeper quantum circuits suffer from barren plateaus where gradients vanish exponentially, further limiting trainability.

## Input / Output

Input images (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10) are reduced to 10 features via PCA and angle-encoded onto 10 qubits using RY gates, with data reuploaded at every layer. Each client holds 128 datapoints and runs a V-shaped ansatz of 2–6 layers (30 parameters per layer), producing a binary (or 4-class) prediction by measuring the first qubit's probability of |0⟩ vs |1⟩. The Funnel variant progressively reduces the number of active qubits per layer, while the Layerwise variant requires running the full circuit once per layer to extract each classifier's output.

## Method

Quorus trains heterogeneous-depth quantum clients using a layerwise loss that sums binary cross-entropy at every layer plus pairwise KL divergence between all layer outputs (reverse distillation), ensuring a shared optimization objective across clients of different depths. Parameters are aggregated via circular averaging (treating rotation angles as complex exponentials) only among clients that share each parameter, and three circuit designs address the measurement-collapse problem: Layerwise (re-run the circuit per layer, linear shot overhead), Ancilla/Blocking (entangle the first qubit with a fresh ancilla per layer for a single circuit run, or equivalently perform a midcircuit measurement), and Funnel (drop one qubit per layer so all measurements occur at the end with no shot overhead). The V-shaped ansatz uses nearest-neighbor CNOTs in a down-then-up pattern with RY data reuploading, and the layerwise loss is shown to maintain higher gradient norms for early-layer parameters, mitigating barren plateaus. The framework is validated on all six IBM superconducting QPUs, where Quorus-Funnel achieves accuracy within 3% of ideal simulation.

## Results

Quorus-Layerwise achieves an average 12.4% testing accuracy improvement over Q-HeteroFL across MNIST and Fashion-MNIST binary classification tasks (e.g., 98.6% vs. 88.6% on MNIST 0/1 at 6L capacity). On the hardest task (MNIST 3/4 at 2L), Quorus reaches 95.0% versus Q-HeteroFL's 58.8%, a 36.2-point gap. On CIFAR-10 binary classification, the best Quorus variant improves over Q-HeteroFL by 6.7% on average, and on 4-class MNIST/Fashion-MNIST by 24.0%. On real IBM superconducting QPUs, Quorus-Funnel achieves accuracy within 3% of ideal simulation (76% on IBM Torino vs. 79% ideal), validating practical viability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Q-HeteroFL, a quantum adaptation of the classical HeteroFL technique where each client trains its maximum-depth model and parameters are averaged only among clients sharing those parameters. Quorus-Layerwise outperforms Q-HeteroFL on nearly all capacity-class combinations, with the average gap of 12.4% and worst-case gaps exceeding 36 points (e.g., MNIST 3/4 at 2L: 95.0% vs. 58.8%). Q-HeteroFL also exhibits very high variance (std up to 33.5%), while Quorus maintains std below 8%, indicating far more stable training. Against Vanilla QFL (all clients forced to the shallowest depth), Quorus matches or exceeds accuracy at 3L+ capacities while allowing deeper clients to fully utilize their hardware. Against Standalone (no FL), Quorus provides consistent gains of 1–3% at higher capacities, confirming the value of federated aggregation. On Non-IID data, Quorus's advantage over Q-HeteroFL widens to 22.5% average with 11% lower standard deviation, highlighting robustness to data heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Quorus adapts the classical layerwise federated learning framework of DepthFL (Kim et al., 2023) — which uses per-layer cross-entropy plus KL-divergence reverse distillation to align heterogeneous-depth clients — to the quantum setting, where the central obstacle is that mid-circuit measurement collapses the state and prevents straightforward intermediate-layer evaluation. The paper departs from prior quantum FL work (Chen & Yoo, 2021; Yun et al., 2022) by addressing depth heterogeneity explicitly and by proposing three distinct circuit designs (Layerwise, Ancilla/Blocking, Funnel) that trade shot budget, qubit count, and midcircuit-measurement capability to make the layerwise loss physically realizable. Parameter aggregation uses circular averaging of rotation angles, a quantum-specific detail absent from classical HeteroFL (Diao et al., 2021), which Quorus also adapts as its Q-HeteroFL baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first QFL framework validated end-to-end on all of IBM's superconducting QPUs, demonstrating that depth-heterogeneous quantum clients can meaningfully collaborate under realistic noise (within ~3% of ideal simulation). The multiple circuit designs give practitioners a concrete menu for matching model architecture to hardware constraints (shot budget, qubit count, midcircuit measurement support), which is practically important as NISQ devices remain heterogeneous. Open questions include scaling beyond 5 clients and 6 layers, extending to multi-class problems beyond 4 classes, and whether the layerwise-loss advantage persists once quantum error correction becomes available. The evaluation is limited to relatively simple image datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10 binary/4-class) with only 128 samples per client, so generalization to harder tasks remains untested.
