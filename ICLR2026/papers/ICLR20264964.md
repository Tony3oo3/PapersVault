## Problem

Speech-adapted LLMs consistently underperform their text-based counterparts on language understanding tasks, a shortfall the authors term the text-speech understanding gap. Prior remedies either synthesize massive amounts of speech from text corpora (costly and paralinguistically impoverished) or rely on proprietary multi-million-hour speech datasets (irreproducible). The paper identifies two root causes—forgetting of pretrained text capabilities and cross-modal misalignment between speech and text—and shows that standard NLL training on narrow-domain speech data actually worsens both as scale increases.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw speech waveforms (natural or TTS-synthesized) alongside interleaved text tokens in a single multimodal sequence, with speech segments of 5–15 words and text segments of 10–30 words. The Mimi speech tokenizer (frozen, causal) converts audio into discrete multicodebook representations that are summed across codebooks, then mapped through a 122M-parameter causal transformer adapter into the LLM's embedding space. Output is next-token text probability distributions; since SALAD is a base (non-instruction-tuned) model, evaluation uses likelihood-based multiple-choice scoring on spoken versions of six broad-domain benchmarks rather than open-ended generation.

## Method

SALAD is a two-stage training procedure built on two empirical findings: (i) a cross-modal distillation objective—minimizing KL divergence between the frozen text-LLM teacher's predictions on text and the student's predictions on equivalent speech—scales far better than NLL and simultaneously reduces both misalignment and forgetting, and (ii) residual misalignment from narrow-domain speech data can be closed by targeted synthetic data. Stage I trains for 24B tokens on natural speech (LibriHeavy + Emilia, ~141K hours) with pure distillation (α=1) plus 1/3 SmolLM text to mitigate forgetting. Stage II performs active selection: the broad-domain text corpus (10B-token FineWeb-Edu subset) is partitioned into 128 k-means clusters over BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5 embeddings, per-cluster misalignment is measured on a small probe set, and clusters are sampled in proportion to their misalignment raised to a selectivity exponent γ=5 (inspired by CRISP's clustered importance sampling). Only 1% of the natural-speech budget (~1.9B tokens) is spent synthesizing speech for the selected clusters via Kokoro TTS, which is then mixed with the original speech data for continued distillation training. The architecture uses a frozen Mimi encoder, a 122M-parameter causal decoder adapter, and Qwen2.5-3B/7B as the LLM backbone, deliberately choosing low-level non-text-like speech representations as a worst-case alignment scenario.

## Results

On six broad-domain spoken language understanding benchmarks (StoryCloze, MMSU, OpenBookQA, HellaSwag, ARC-Challenge, PIQA), SALAD-7B (Stage II) achieves 75.4% average accuracy with a 6.2% text-speech gap, while SALAD-3B (Stage II) reaches 72.0% average accuracy with a 4.6% gap. These results are competitive with the strongest end-to-end baseline Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (76.7% accuracy, 5.0% gap) while training on over an order of magnitude less speech data (~144K hours vs. millions of hours). SALAD-3B outperforms all larger end-to-end baselines (Qwen2-Audio-7B, DiVA-8B, GLM-4-Voice-9B) by 8–20 percentage points in average accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SALAD-3B (72.0% avg accuracy, 4.6% gap) substantially outperforms all other open-recipe end-to-end baselines: Qwen2-Audio-7B (53.7%, 17.8% gap), DiVA-Llama3.1-8B (52.6%, 26.1% gap), and GLM-4-Voice-9B (63.4%, 20.1% gap), with gaps reduced by 13–21 percentage points. Against the strongest closed-recipe system Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (76.7%, 5.0% gap), SALAD-7B (75.4%, 6.2% gap) trails by only 1.3 points in accuracy and 1.2 points in gap, while using over an order of magnitude less training data. Relative to the cascaded topline (Whisper-large-v3 + Qwen2.5-7B: 79.4%, 2.2% gap), SALAD-7B underperforms by 4.0 points in accuracy and 4.0 points in gap, but retains end-to-end modeling advantages (preserving paralinguistic cues). On text-capability preservation (Table 5), SALAD-7B maintains 82.2% average text accuracy (−0.9% gap vs. its backbone), far exceeding Qwen2.5-Omni (78.4%, 3.3% gap) and GLM-4-Voice (69.9%, 13.6% gap), confirming that the distillation objective effectively prevents catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

SALAD builds on two distinct lines of work: cross-modal knowledge distillation for speech-text alignment (BLSP-KD, DiVA), which provides the core training objective, and clustered importance sampling (CRISP), which is repurposed here by replacing the external target-domain distribution with the model's own per-cluster misalignment signal. The paper departs from the dominant data-driven paradigm (GLM-4-Voice, Kimi-Audio) that relies on synthesizing entire text corpora or proprietary multi-million-hour speech datasets, instead showing that a small, model-guided infusion of synthetic speech (1% of natural speech budget) suffices when paired with distillation. It also formalizes the text-speech understanding gap as a sum of two measurable quantities—forgetting and cross-modal misalignment—providing a diagnostic framework absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, reproducible, and data-efficient path to closing the text-speech gap that avoids both the cost of large-scale TTS synthesis and the irreproducibility of proprietary datasets, making it directly relevant to groups building speech-adapted LLMs with limited resources. The two-factor decomposition (forgetting + misalignment) and the associated scaling laws give practitioners actionable diagnostics for choosing training objectives and data strategies. Limitations include evaluation only on English, 3B–7B models, a single TTS system (Kokoro), and a residual gap (~6%) to the strongest closed-recipe baseline; open questions include whether the active selection strategy transfers to multilingual settings, whether it composes with instruction tuning, and whether the irreducible misalignment floor can be further reduced with richer speech representations.
