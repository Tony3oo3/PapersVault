## Problem

Unified image tokenizers must simultaneously capture high-level semantic features for multimodal understanding and low-level pixel features for autoregressive generation, but these two objectives prioritize fundamentally different levels of visual information. Existing joint-training approaches (e.g., VILA-U, SDE, TokenFlow) combine semantic distillation and pixel reconstruction losses in a single optimization, which drives the tokenizer toward suboptimal trade-offs. The paper asks how to balance semantic and pixel information without token inflation, codebook overexpansion, or the degradation inherent in joint optimization.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer accepts RGB images at 256×256 or 384×384 resolution and produces a spatial grid of discrete tokens (16×16 or 27×27 patches). Each token is a hierarchical pair — a semantic code index (from a 16,384-entry codebook) and a pixel sub-code index (from an 8- or 12-entry sub-codebook) — that is flattened into a single integer via h = i×m + j, yielding a total vocabulary of ~196k entries comparable to Qwen2's text vocabulary. In the unified MLLM setting, the model additionally takes a text prompt and outputs either a text response (understanding) or a sequence of image tokens decoded back to pixels (generation), using classifier-free guidance at inference.

## Method

SemHiTok introduces a Semantic-Guided Hierarchical Codebook (SGHC) that decouples semantic and pixel representation both structurally and in training. A frozen SigLIP encoder's features are first quantized into a pretrained EMA-VQ semantic codebook (K=16,384, dim=48) following the VQKD recipe; then, for each semantic code, a dedicated pixel sub-codebook (size m=8) is trained to model the local pixel feature space conditioned on that semantic code. The key insight is that patches sharing the same semantic code exhibit intrinsic pixel-level similarity (quantified via Variance Reduction Ratio), so the pixel sub-codebook only needs to capture residual texture within a semantically homogeneous cluster. Training is strictly stage-wise: the semantic codebook is trained first, then the pixel branch (ViT encoder, sub-codebooks, ViT decoder) is trained with VQGAN-style losses while the semantic codebook remains frozen, avoiding the suboptimal solutions of joint optimization. For MLLM integration, the hierarchical codebook is flattened into a single index space, and a Dual-MLP adapter projects semantic and pixel features separately before concatenation, allowing the LLM to attend to both levels independently.

## Results

As a unified MLLM (7B, 384 resolution), SemHiTok achieves SEED 79.8, POPE 85.5, GQA 61.7, MMMU 41.0, MMB 75.2, MME-P 1512.8, and MMV 36.6, setting new state-of-the-art among discrete-token unified MLLMs on most benchmarks. For image reconstruction on ImageNet-50k, SemHiTok achieves rFID 1.16 at 256 resolution and 0.66 at 384 resolution, outperforming all unified tokenizers (VILA-U: 1.80, SDE: 2.26, TokenFlow: 1.37). For text-to-image generation, SemHiTok attains 5.40 gFID on MJHQ-30K (256 res), surpassing Liquid (5.47) and SynerGen-VL (6.10), and scores 0.83/0.64 on GenAI-Bench Basic/Advanced.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among discrete unified MLLMs, SemHiTok-384 (7B) decisively outperforms TokenFlow-L (13B) on SEED (79.8 vs 62.6), MMMU (41.0 vs 34.4), MMB (75.2 vs 60.3), and MME-P (1512.8 vs 1365.4), despite using fewer parameters. Against TokLIP (7B, 384), SemHiTok wins on SEED (79.8 vs 76.9), GQA (61.7 vs 59.5), MMB (75.2 vs 67.6), and MMV (36.6 vs 29.8), but trails slightly on MMMU (41.0 vs 43.1). Compared to the understanding-only ShareGPT4V (7B), SemHiTok-384 surpasses it on SEED (79.8 vs 69.7), MMMU (41.0 vs 37.2), MMB (75.2 vs 68.8), and MME (1993.8 vs 1943.8), though it lags on MME-P (1512.8 vs 1567.4) and GQA (61.7 vs 63.3). For reconstruction, SemHiTok achieves rFID 1.16 (256) and 0.66 (384), beating VILA-U (1.80), SDE (2.26), and TokenFlow (1.37), though UniTok (0.39) remains stronger on pure reconstruction. On generation, SemHiTok's 5.40 gFID on MJHQ-30K edges out Liquid (5.47) and SynerGen-VL (6.10), while its GenAI-Bench scores (0.83/0.64) match Liquid (0.83/0.65) and exceed VILA-U (0.76/0.64) and TokenFlow (0.65/0.65).

## Relation to Prior Work

SemHiTok builds on two well-established tokenization paradigms: VQKD-style semantic distillation from frozen text-aligned encoders (SigLIP/CLIP) for understanding, and VQGAN-style adversarial pixel reconstruction for generation. It departs from prior unified tokenizers (VILA-U, SDE/MUSE-VL, TokenFlow) that rely on joint optimization of semantic and pixel losses within a single hybrid codebook, and from dual-encoder approaches (Janus) that concatenate separate token streams. The key structural departure is the Semantic-Guided Hierarchical Codebook, which conditions pixel sub-codebooks on semantic codes and trains them in a staged (non-joint) fashion, decoupling the two objectives both architecturally and in training schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving near-continuous (SigLIP-level) multimodal understanding while simultaneously delivering SOTA autoregressive image generation (5.40 gFID on MJHQ-30K), SemHiTok demonstrates that the discrete-token bottleneck for unified MLLMs can be substantially closed without resorting to diffusion decoders or dual-encoder complexity. The hierarchical codebook design is a clean, modular solution that practitioners can adopt within existing next-token-prediction frameworks. Open questions remain around generation efficiency (256 tokens per 256-res image), scalability to higher resolutions and video, and whether the staged training strategy generalizes to more complex tasks like image editing or multi-turn visual reasoning.
