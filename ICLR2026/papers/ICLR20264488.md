## Problem

Current Omni Language Models exhibit a 'co-growth' phenomenon where increasing caption length simultaneously raises both the proportion of correct fine-grained details and the amount of hallucinated content, making it impossible to obtain richer descriptions without proportionally more factual errors. Additionally, no dedicated benchmark exists for evaluating detailed perception across audio-only, visual-only, and audio-visual modalities in a unified, stable manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264488/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The models consume short audio, video, or audio-visual clips (10–60 seconds, averaging ~34 s) and produce long-form detailed captions averaging 1,125 words per short video for Omni-Captioner. For evaluation, Omni-Cloze converts each caption into 30 cloze blanks with four plausible distractors plus a 'Not Given' option, scored via a single LLM call per caption (versus 38 calls in VDC), yielding accuracy, not-given rate, and hallucination rate as separate interpretable metrics.

## Method

The paper introduces three coupled components. First, Omni-Detective is an agentic data-generation pipeline in which an LLM 'detective' agent iteratively formulates queries, invokes specialist tools (OCR, ASR, MLLM), and cross-references observations from independent modality-specific observers over up to 10 steps, explicitly decoupling detail gain from hallucination growth. Second, Audio-Captioner and Omni-Captioner are trained on the resulting data via a two-stage curriculum over Qwen-2.5-Omni-7B: Stage 1 freezes the visual encoder and fine-tunes only the audio encoder and LLM on audio-only captions to enforce grounding in sparse audio cues; Stage 2 unfreezes all parameters for joint audio-visual fine-tuning to produce coherent cross-modal narratives. Third, Omni-Cloze is a cloze-style benchmark of 2,320 clips and 69,600 blanks spanning 9 domains and 47 sub-categories, with a modality-balanced distribution (≥8 visual, ≥8 audio, ≥4 audio-visual questions per passage) and human-verified ground truth, enabling stable single-pass evaluation that achieves r=0.91 Pearson correlation with human Elo preferences.

## Results

On the proposed Omni-Cloze benchmark, Omni-Captioner-7B achieves 56.4% overall accuracy (57.0% visual, 54.5% audio, 62.1% AV), surpassing Gemini 2.5 Pro (43.6%) by 12.8 points and the previous best open-source model video-SALMONN 2-7B (39.5%) by 16.9 points. On existing detailed captioning benchmarks, Omni-Captioner sets a new SOTA on VDC with 55.0% accuracy and 2.7 score, and achieves the best detail-hallucination trade-off on video-SALMONN 2 test (17.8% miss, 10.9% hallucination). In cascade caption-to-QA evaluation, Audio-Captioner-7B matches Gemini 2.5 Pro on MMAU (70.0%) and leads all open-source models on MMAR (59.8%), while Omni-Captioner-7B attains the highest open-source scores on Video-MME (67.1%), Video-Holmes (48.8%), WorldSense (48.2%), and Daily-Omni (67.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni-Captioner-7B decisively outperforms all open-source baselines across every benchmark: it beats video-SALMONN 2-7B by +16.9 points on Omni-Cloze total accuracy (56.4 vs 39.5), +8.9 on VDC accuracy (55.0 vs 46.1), and +1.2 on Video-MME cascade (67.1 vs 65.9). Against the strongest proprietary model Gemini 2.5 Pro, Omni-Captioner wins on VDC (55.0 vs 43.1) and Omni-Cloze (56.4 vs 43.6) but trails on cascade Video-MME (67.1 vs 75.0) and Video-Holmes (48.8 vs 59.9). Audio-Captioner-7B matches Gemini 2.5 Pro exactly on MMAU (70.0) and surpasses it on MMAR (59.8 vs 64.1 is actually a loss; it surpasses Gemini 2.5 Flash at 59.8 vs 58.2), while beating all open-source audio models by 4.8+ points on MMAU. On the Omni-Cloze audio-only split, Audio-Captioner (53.2%) exceeds Gemini 2.5 Pro (48.0%) by 5.2 points, a margin no other open-source model comes close to matching (next best is Qwen2.5-Omni-7B at 25.8%). The paper also shows that applying the Omni-Detective pipeline directly to Gemini 2.5 Pro's captions yields consistent downstream gains (+4.2 on MMAR, +1.1 on Video-MME), validating the agentic data generation approach independently of the trained models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the detailed captioning line initiated by AuroraCap (VDC) and video-SALMONN 2, which were predominantly visual-centric and relied on manually designed prompting for data collection. It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) introducing an agentic, tool-calling data generation pipeline (Omni-Detective) that replaces static prompting with iterative detective-observer interactions, (2) explicitly targeting the audio-visual omni-modal setting rather than video-only, and (3) proposing a cloze-style evaluation (Omni-Cloze) that replaces the multi-turn QA paradigm of VDC with a single-pass, more stable protocol. The backbone model (Qwen-2.5-Omni-7B) and the two-stage curriculum training strategy are the primary methodological inheritances from the Qwen omni-model lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'co-growth' phenomenon—where detail coverage and hallucination rate rise together under unconstrained generation—provides a clear diagnostic for why prior detailed captioning models struggle, and motivates the agentic pipeline as a principled solution. Omni-Cloze fills a genuine gap as the first benchmark covering audio-only, visual-only, and audio-visual detailed perception simultaneously, and its r=0.91 correlation with human Elo ratings suggests it will become a standard evaluation. Key limitations remain: the benchmark cannot detect irrelevant-generation hallucinations (content entirely unrelated to the input), and the agentic pipeline's hallucination rate plateaus around step 5–6, revealing a fundamental ceiling in current multimodal tools. Open questions include whether the decoupling of detail from hallucination generalizes to longer-form video and whether the cloze format adequately captures the open-ended nature of real-world captioning.
