## Problem

Monotonic value factorization (e.g., QMIX) restricts the expressiveness of the joint action-value function, often preventing recovery of optimal policies in non-monotonic cooperative settings. WQMIX proposed weighting optimal joint actions more heavily during training, but its practical variants (CW-QMIX, OW-QMIX) rely on heuristic approximations that misalign assigned weights with the true optimal set, leaving a persistent gap between theoretical guarantees and actual performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261625/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes local agent observation histories, the global state, and one-hot encoded joint actions; during execution, each agent acts solely on its own observation history. The Q_r recognition module uniquely takes the joint action as an explicit input alongside the global state and individual advantage values, enabling per-action discrimination. The system outputs a factorized Q_tot for decentralized execution, per-agent Q_i values, and a binary training weight (1 for actions in the recognized set A_r, 0 otherwise) that gates which joint actions contribute to Q_tot updates.

## Method

POW introduces a recognition module Q_r that explicitly conditions on the joint action to identify a set A_r of potentially optimal joint actions, defined as those whose Q_r value is within a tolerance C of the greedy IGM action's value. Q_r is parameterized as a sum of hypernetwork-scaled centered individual action-values plus a state value, guaranteeing the IGM property without monotonicity constraints. Three networks—unrestricted Q*, factorized Q_tot, and recognition Q_r—share the same Q-learning target and form a mutually reinforcing loop: Q_r proposes A_r, the resulting binary weight (α=0) shapes Q_tot updates, and Q* provides consistent bootstrapping. The paper proves that A_r converges to contain all truly optimal joint actions (Theorem 1) and that Q_tot recovers the optimal policy under this weighting (Theorem 2). The framework is architecture-agnostic and plugs into any IGM-satisfying factorization (QMIX, VDN, QPLEX) with roughly 15–20% training overhead.

## Results

On the non-monotonic Predator-Prey task, POW-QMIX achieves the optimal return of 40 under both penalty settings (p=−4, p=−5), while QMIX, VDN, and QPLEX all fail (return 0), and CW-QMIX/OW-QMIX achieve only 16/8 and 8/0 respectively. On the super-hard SMAC map MMM2, POW-QMIX reaches a 95% win rate versus 69% for QMIX, 30% for QPLEX, and 0% for ResQ. On SMACv2, POW-QMIX consistently attains the highest average test return (18.8–19.0) across protoss, terran, and zerg scenarios, outperforming all baselines by 1–3.4 points. The method is architecture-agnostic: POW-VDN and POW-QPLEX also improve over their respective baselines across all environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POW-QMIX decisively outperforms QMIX on all non-monotonic environments: Predator-Prey (40 vs. 0), Crossroads (0.92 vs. 0.28), and MMM2 (0.95 vs. 0.69), while matching QMIX on the monotonic corridor map (1.00). Against QPLEX, POW-QMIX wins on Crossroads (0.92 vs. 0.26), MMM2 (0.95 vs. 0.30), and SMACv2 zerg (18.4 vs. 0, where QPLEX collapses entirely); QPLEX marginally leads on SMACv2 terran (19.2 vs. 19.0) but this advantage is reversed by POW-QPLEX (19.4). Compared to ResQ, POW-QMIX is superior on Predator-Prey p=−5 (40 vs. 0) and MMM2 (0.95 vs. 0.00), while ResQ matches on corridor and slightly leads on Crossroads (0.88 vs. 0.92 is actually POW winning). Against the WQMIX variants, POW-QMIX dominates: CW-QMIX achieves only 16/8 on Predator-Prey and 0.43 on Crossroads, while OW-QMIX reaches 8/0 and 0.88 respectively, both substantially below POW-QMIX's 40/40 and 0.92. On SMACv2, POW-QMIX achieves the best or near-best return in all three scenarios (protoss 0.98, terran 19.0, zerg 18.4), confirming consistent gains over every baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the WQMIX line of work (Rashid et al., 2020b), which proposed weighting optimal joint actions more heavily during training but could only be realized through heuristic approximations (CW-QMIX, OW-QMIX) that introduce unavoidable error. POW replaces these heuristics with a recognition module Q_r that explicitly conditions on joint actions and satisfies IGM, enabling a principled identification of potentially optimal actions with provable convergence to the true optimal set. The design also draws on QPLEX's joint-action-conditioned advantage mixing, but repurposes it for recognition-weighting rather than expressiveness, and is architecture-agnostic across QMIX, VDN, and QPLEX backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POW closes a long-standing gap between WQMIX's theoretical optimality guarantee and its practical realizations, providing the first provable convergence result for weighted value decomposition in cooperative MARL. The architecture-agnostic design and consistent gains across five benchmark families (including non-monotonic settings where baselines fail entirely) make it a practical drop-in improvement for the value-factorization community. Key limitations include restriction to cooperative discrete-action CTDE settings, a ~15–20% training overhead from the Q_r module, and the unique-maximal-action assumption in the convergence proof. Extending POW to actor-critic or continuous-action frameworks remains an open direction.
