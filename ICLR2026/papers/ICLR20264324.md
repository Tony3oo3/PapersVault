## Problem

Standard RoPE implementations retain only the real part of the complex-valued attention score, discarding the imaginary component that encodes phase information about relative positions. This irreversible information loss limits the model's ability to capture long-context dependencies, yet the intrinsic computation of RoPE and the functional role of its imaginary part have remained largely unexplored in the position-embedding literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264324/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text token sequences identical to vanilla RoPE-based LLMs, with no change to the input format or tokenizer. The output is a dual-component attention score: the original real attention (unchanged) plus a new imaginary attention computed in parallel. The imaginary attention is obtained by rotating the query vector q_t by −π/2 before applying the standard RoPE rotation, while the key vector k_s uses the identical positional embedding as in real attention, meaning no additional KV cache entries are required. Two deployment configurations are offered: RoPE++_EH keeps the total head count fixed (halving KV cache and QKV parameters) and RoPE++_EC keeps cache size fixed (doubling the number of attention heads).

## Method

RoPE++ re-incorporates the discarded imaginary part of RoPE's complex-valued dot product as a new group of attention heads computed alongside the real heads, sharing the same W_q parameters. The key mechanism is that the imaginary attention equals (R_{−π/2} q_t)^T R_{Θ, s−t} k_s, requiring only a −π/2 rotation of q_t while leaving k_s's positional embedding unchanged, so both real and imaginary attentions execute in a single FlashAttention pass with zero extra KV cache. Theoretical analysis derives a sine-integral characteristic curve for imaginary attention that decays far more slowly than the cosine-integral curve of real attention, explaining why imaginary heads preferentially attend to distant positions and capture long-context dependencies. Additionally, the imaginary attention exposes even-index query and odd-index key dimensions to both positive and negative position-embedding values during pre-training, implicitly improving length extrapolation beyond the trained context window. Experiments at 376M, 776M, and 1.5B scales on DCLM-Baseline-1.0 confirm that RoPE++_EC outperforms vanilla RoPE on both short- and long-context benchmarks, while RoPE++_EH achieves comparable or superior results with half the KV cache and QKV parameters.

## Results

On 776M models after long-context pre-training, RoPE++EC achieves an average short-context score of 43.5, surpassing vanilla RoPE (41.3) and ALiBi (42.6 at short-context stage). On long-context benchmarks (RULER + BABILong), RoPE++EC scores 29.4 on RULER and 24.1 on BABILong at 776M, compared to RoPE's 27.4 and 22.8 respectively. The cache-efficient variant RoPE++EH matches or exceeds RoPE (42.0 vs 41.3 average) while using only half the KV cache and QKV parameters. At 376M, RoPE++EC achieves 25.0 on RULER vs RoPE's 18.8, a 6.2-point gain that widens with context length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoPE++EC outperforms vanilla RoPE on average across all model sizes and both short- and long-context evaluations: at 776M after long-context training, it gains +2.2 points on short-context average (43.5 vs 41.3) and +2.0 on RULER (29.4 vs 27.4). Against ALiBi (the strongest non-RoPE baseline at 776M short-context with 42.6 average), RoPE++EC leads by +0.2 points, while RoPE++EH (42.5) is essentially tied. RoPE++EH, which halves KV cache and QKV parameters, still beats RoPE by +0.7 on 776M short-context average (42.0 vs 41.3) and +1.2 on RULER (28.6 vs 27.4), making it a strong efficiency-oriented choice. On long-context BABILong, RoPE++EC leads RoPE by +1.3 at 776M (24.1 vs 22.8) and by +5.1 at 376M (16.1 vs 11.0), with the gap widening at longer context lengths. RoPE++EC also outperforms FoPE and Pythia on 376M short-context (41.0 vs 40.0 and 39.7 respectively), though these baselines are only evaluated at the short-context stage without long-context fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RoPE (Su et al., 2024), the dominant position embedding in modern LLMs, by identifying that the standard implementation discards the imaginary component of the complex-valued attention score. It departs from the large body of prior work that improves RoPE through interpolation (NTK, YaRN, PI), data-awareness, or dimension partitioning, instead revisiting the intrinsic complex-multiplication computation to recover the lost imaginary part as a parallel group of attention heads. The approach is conceptually distinct from prior complex-valued neural network work (Wang et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2022), which incorporated full complex arithmetic without analyzing the specific role of the imaginary component in positional encoding, and from p-RoPE (Barbero et al., 2024), which studied RoPE's properties from a different mathematical angle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since RoPE is the canonical position embedding across virtually all major LLMs (Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek), any improvement to its core computation has broad practical relevance. The RoPE++_EH configuration's ability to halve KV cache and QKV parameters while maintaining comparable performance is a meaningful efficiency gain for long-context inference, and the method plugs directly into FlashAttention and GQA without architectural changes. However, validation is limited to models up to 1.5B parameters, and the 1.5B long-context results (Table 6) show RoPE++_EH actually underperforming vanilla RoPE on RULER, raising open questions about whether the imaginary-attention advantage persists at the 7B+ scale where most production LLMs operate. The method also requires training from scratch and does not provide plug-and-play length extrapolation, limiting its immediate applicability to existing checkpoints.
