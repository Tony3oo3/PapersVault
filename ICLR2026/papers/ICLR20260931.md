## Problem

Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) suffer from prohibitive computational cost in high-dimensional settings because the d-dimensional FFT scales as O(n^d log(n^d)), making 3D+ PDE solving and high-dimensional signal restoration impractical. Existing tensor-decomposition variants like T-FNO reduce parameter count but actually increase FFT-related computation, while D-FNO reduces FFT cost at the expense of representational capacity. The paper addresses the gap of achieving both reduced FFT complexity and preserved universal approximation for tensor-decomposed neural operators.

## Input / Output

For PDE tasks, the input is a d-dimensional condition function sampled on a regular grid (e.g., 64×64 vorticity field for Navier-Stokes, 1D initial condition for Burger's), and the output is the corresponding solution field at a later time. The d-dimensional input tensor is first decomposed by a pre-lifting module that unfolds along each spatial mode and applies a linear projection, yielding d one-dimensional factor matrices of shape n_i × d_v. For signal restoration (INR mode), the input is sinusoidal positional encoding of coordinates (S=10 frequency levels), and the output is the continuous signal value; here the positional encoding naturally separates across dimensions, eliminating the need for the pre-lifting step.

## Method

Tucker-FNO decomposes a single d-dimensional FNO into d independent 1-dimensional FNOs connected through a Tucker tensor structure: a pre-lifting operator extracts 1D factor matrices from the input tensor, each factor is processed by a 1D FNO (with 1D FFT of complexity O(n log n) instead of O(n^d log n^d)), and the outputs are recombined via a core tensor contraction followed by a 2-layer MLP post-projection. This reduces the dominant FFT cost from O(d_v·d·n^d·log n) to O(d_v·d·n·log n) while keeping the weight-multiplication cost at O(d_v²·d·k). The paper proves a universal approximation theorem for Tucker-FNO by first establishing functional tensor Tucker decomposition (via Stone-Weierstrass), then extending it to operator-level decomposition in finite-dimensional Fourier spaces, and finally invoking the classical FNO UAT to approximate each 1D factor operator. For the INR/signal-restoration setting, the sinusoidal positional encoding is inherently separable across spatial dimensions, so the architecture simplifies to d 1D FNOs operating directly on per-dimension encodings without a pre-lifting step.

## Results

On PDE approximation, Tucker-FNO achieves the best NMSE and VCE across all three equations: Navier-Stokes (T=20: NMSE 0.0028, VCE 8.2e-6; T=30: NMSE 0.0061, VCE 3.0e-5), Plasticity (NMSE 0.0073, VCE 2.0e-4), and Burger's (NMSE 0.0070, VCE 2.0e-5). On multi-spectral image inpainting (SR=0.1), Tucker-FNO reaches 28.09 dB PSNR, surpassing the next-best LRTFR (26.96 dB) and OINR-FNO (24.53 dB). In terms of efficiency on 256×256 PDE data, Tucker-FNO uses 293.5K parameters and 0.0352 s/iter, reducing parameters by 50% and execution time by 48.6% compared to FNO (593.7K, 0.0685 s/iter).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tucker-FNO outperforms all PDE baselines on every metric: it beats FNO by ~18% NMSE on Navier-Stokes T=20 (0.0028 vs 0.0034), T-FNO by ~17% (0.0028 vs 0.0035), and D-FNO by ~56% (0.0028 vs 0.0063), while also achieving the lowest VCE across all tasks. Notably, D-FNO reduces FFT cost but degrades accuracy substantially (e.g., 0.0132 NMSE on NS T=30 vs Tucker-FNO's 0.0061), whereas Tucker-FNO maintains or improves accuracy while also reducing computation. For signal restoration, Tucker-FNO dominates all INR baselines: on video inpainting at SR=0.1 it achieves 28.09 dB PSNR vs LRTFR (26.96), OINR-FNO (24.53), SIREN (23.85), and MFN (22.47); on MSI denoising at noise 0.1 it reaches 35.89 dB vs OINR-FNO (33.80) and LRTFR (32.30). On the large-scale 3D Car-CFD task, GINO-Tucker-FNO achieves the lowest test error (0.1297 at k=16) compared to GINO (0.1382) and GINO-D-FNO (0.1432), while also running fastest per sample (0.0064 s vs 0.0081 s). ResNet is far behind all operator-based methods, confirming the advantage of spectral/operator architectures for PDE solution.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tucker-FNO extends the Fourier Neural Operator (Li et al., 2021) by applying Tucker tensor decomposition at the operator level rather than the weight level. This distinguishes it from T-FNO (Kossaifi et al., 2024), which decomposes FNO weights to reduce parameters but leaves FFT complexity unchanged, and from D-FNO (Li & Ye, 2025), which reduces FFT cost through decomposition but may compromise representational capacity. The pre-lifting operator design is borrowed from D-FNO, and the functional tensor decomposition framework is extended from LRTFR (Luo et al., 2024) to the operator learning setting. The signal restoration application follows the OINR framework (Pal et al., 2024), adapting it to the Tucker-decomposed operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first universal approximation theorem for a tensor-decomposed neural operator provides theoretical grounding that was previously absent for decomposed FNO variants, addressing a key concern about whether efficiency gains come at the cost of expressiveness. The reduction of FFT complexity from O(n^d log n^d) to O(dn log n) is practically significant for high-dimensional PDEs where FNO becomes intractable. However, the paper notes that the decomposition's optimization dynamics and implicit regularization remain unexplored, and the theoretical guarantees require analyticity assumptions on the function space that may not always hold in practice. The extension to signal restoration via INR suggests broader applicability, but scalability to truly high-dimensional (4D+) problems remains to be validated.
