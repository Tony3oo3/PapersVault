## Problem

Learning finite-dimensional Koopman representations for nonlinear dynamical systems is challenging because the Koopman operator is inherently infinite-dimensional, and existing data-driven approaches (VAEs, domain-specific priors) lack general principles for balancing latent simplicity with expressiveness. This imbalance causes mode collapse, spectral degeneration, and error accumulation in long-horizon predictions. The paper reframes this as an information bottleneck problem, showing that latent mutual information promotes simplicity but risks collapse, while von Neumann entropy sustains expressiveness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes time-ordered state sequences of varying dimensionality: low-dimensional vectors (Lorenz 63, n=3), high-dimensional spatial fields (64×64×2 fluid snapshots, ERA5 weather channels), RGB images of physical systems (pendulum, cartpole, 3-link manipulator), and graph-structured node features (rope with 40–56 nodes, soft robotics with 160–224 nodes). The encoder maps each state x_n to a latent code z_n, which is propagated forward by a learned linear Koopman matrix K; the decoder reconstructs x_n from z_n. Outputs include multi-step state predictions (5–100+ steps), the learned Koopman operator's eigenvalue spectrum, latent manifolds for visualization, and control actions in latent space for visual-input tasks.

## Method

The core contribution is an information-theoretic Lagrangian that explicitly balances three desiderata: temporal coherence (maximizing latent mutual information I(z_{t-n}; z_t), which has a closed-form involving the Koopman operator's n-step forward covariance), structural consistency (enforcing a linear Gaussian transition q_ψ(z_n|z_{n-1}) = N(z_n | K_ψ z_{n-1}, Σ_ψ) via KL divergence), and predictive sufficiency (maximizing the von Neumann entropy S(C/tr(C)) of the normalized latent covariance to prevent mode collapse). The key theoretical insight is that maximizing mutual information alone induces a water-filling allocation that degenerates to low-rank when the Koopman spectrum is skewed, while entropy regularization guarantees non-zero spectral weight across all modes. The resulting loss is architecture-agnostic: in a VAE setting it combines InfoNCE-based MI estimation, linear-transition KL, encoder entropy, decoder reconstruction, and a batch-computed von Neumann entropy term; in an AE setting the KL degenerates to an L² Koopman consistency loss. This formulation is validated across physical simulations, visual control, and graph dynamics, consistently outperforming VAE, KAE, KKR, PFNN, E2C, PCC, and CKO baselines.

## Results

On physical simulation tasks, the proposed method achieves the best NRMSE across all horizons: 0.003 (5-step) and 0.013 (50-step) on Lorenz 63, 0.068 (5-step) and 0.138 (50-step) on Kármán Vortex, and 0.018 (5-step) and 0.026 (50-step) on Dam Flow. On the large-scale ERA5 weather forecasting task, it reduces 5-step NRMSE to 0.028 (vs. 0.049–0.058 for baselines) and 50-step NRMSE to 0.068 (vs. 0.074–0.118), while achieving 5-step SSIM of 0.867 (vs. 0.664–0.697). Spectral distribution error (SDE) is also lowest: 0.256 on Kármán Vortex and 0.244 on Dam Flow, confirming better preservation of Koopman eigenmodes near the unit circle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VAE (Burgess et al., 2018) and KAE (Pan et al., 2023), the proposed method consistently wins on every metric across all physical simulation tasks, with particularly large gaps on long-horizon predictions (e.g., 50-step NRMSE of 0.013 vs. 0.019/0.023 on Lorenz 63) and spectral consistency (SDE 0.256 vs. 0.538/0.620 on Kármán Vortex). Against KKR (Bevanda et al., 2023), the method improves 50-step NRMSE by ~25% on Kármán Vortex (0.138 vs. 0.209) and SDE by ~34% (0.256 vs. 0.799), while matching or slightly beating KKR on short-horizon Dam Flow. Against PFNN (Cheng et al., 2025), a SOTA method specifically designed for chaotic dynamics with domain-specific priors, the proposed method achieves comparable performance on Lorenz 63 and Kármán Vortex but substantially outperforms it on ERA5 weather forecasting (5-step NRMSE 0.028 vs. 0.049; 5-step SSIM 0.867 vs. 0.697) and Dam Flow (50-step NRMSE 0.026 vs. not evaluated). For visual-input control, the method outperforms E2C (Banijamali et al., 2019) and PCC (Levine et al., 2020) in both manifold quality (Figure 3) and control success rate under noiseless and noisy settings. For graph-structured dynamics, it surpasses CKO (Li et al., 2020) on 100-step rollouts in both Rope and Soft Robotics environments (Figure 5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Koopman autoencoder paradigm (Otto & Rowley, 2019; Pan et al., 2023) by grounding representation learning in the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000), which prior Koopman methods either ignore or address only through domain-specific priors (symmetry, ergodicity, conservation laws). The key conceptual departure is the explicit decomposition of latent mutual information into temporal-coherent, fast-dissipating, and residual components, each mapped to distinct Koopman spectral regimes (|λ|≈1, |λ|<1, no counterpart), and the identification of a water-filling degeneracy that causes mode collapse when mutual information is maximized without entropy regularization. Unlike PFNN (Cheng et al., 2025) or CKO (Li et al., 2020), which encode task-specific structure, the proposed Lagrangian is architecture-agnostic and applies uniformly across physical, visual, and graph-structured dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first general information-theoretic principle for guiding Koopman representation learning, unifying three desiderata (temporal coherence, predictive sufficiency, structural consistency) into a single tractable loss. This is practically important because prior methods required hand-crafted domain priors that do not transfer across system types. However, the authors acknowledge the absence of sample complexity or nonasymptotic convergence guarantees, and the framework assumes a fixed Koopman operator structure that may not capture non-stationary or multi-scale dynamics. Open questions include whether the information-theoretic Lagrangian extends naturally to stochastic Koopman semigroups and how it interacts with kernel-based Koopman learning (Kostic et al., 2022; Bach, 2022).
