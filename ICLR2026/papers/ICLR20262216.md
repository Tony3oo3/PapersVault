## Problem

Conditional GAN discriminators must simultaneously assess sample authenticity and conditional alignment, but existing approaches struggle to balance these dual objectives. Classifier-based methods (e.g., AC-GAN, ReACGAN) require manual tuning of weighting coefficients, while projection-based methods (PD-GAN) do not fully exploit the likelihood decomposition for unconditional discrimination and lack explicit mechanisms for matching-awareness. The paper identifies three missing capabilities—robust unconditional discrimination, sensitivity to condition mismatches via negative samples, and adaptive balancing of objectives—that no single existing method satisfies.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a noise vector z and a conditioning signal (discrete class label for image generation or a text prompt for text-to-image) and produces a synthetic RGB image at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 256×256 (CUB, COCO, CC12M). The discriminator consumes the generated or real image x and the condition y, producing a scalar score decomposed into a naturalness term and an alignment term. For text-to-image, the alignment direction ω_y is instantiated by frozen CLIP text embeddings rather than learnable per-class vectors, making the method applicable to open-vocabulary conditioning without architectural changes.

## Method

SONA parameterizes the discriminator as f(x,y) = ⟨ω, h(x)⟩ + ⟨ω_y, Π_⊥ω h(x)⟩, where a shared feature extractor h projects onto a naturalness direction ω and an alignment direction ω_y that is orthogonally projected to be independent of ω. This orthogonal inductive bias ensures that optimizing the generator for alignment does not interfere with optimizing it for authenticity. Training combines three maximization objectives: a sliced-Wasserstein-based SAN loss for unconditional discrimination, a Bradley-Terry conditional loss (BT-C) comparing real vs. generated samples under the same condition, and a Bradley-Terry matching loss (BT-M) comparing condition-matched data samples against marginal data samples to enforce sensitivity to mismatches. Learnable scalar weights with a unit-norm constraint adaptively balance the three objectives during training, avoiding the unbounded coefficient instability that plagues general multi-task weighting schemes in GANs.

## Results

On ImageNet 128×128 (batch size 2048), SONA achieves FID 6.14, IS 140.14, iFID 48.45, and Top-1 accuracy 0.80, substantially outperforming ReACGAN (FID 8.44, Top-1 0.51) and PD-GAN (FID 8.85, Top-1 0.63). On CIFAR-10 with StyleGAN2 backbone, SONA reaches FID 3.38 and IS 10.45, matching or exceeding ReACGAN (FID 3.39) and PD-GAN (FID 4.06). On TinyImageNet with DiffAug, SONA achieves FID 7.76, IS 23.00, and iFID 82.23, leading all baselines by a wide margin. For text-to-image generation on COCO, GALIP+SONA improves FID from 5.30 to 4.70 and CLIP Score from 0.3639 to 0.3677 over the original GALIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SONA outperforms all three class-conditional GAN baselines (ContraGAN, ReACGAN, PD-GAN) on every metric across CIFAR-10, TinyImageNet, and ImageNet. The most dramatic gains appear on ImageNet at batch size 2048, where SONA reduces FID from 8.44 (ReACGAN) and 8.85 (PD-GAN) to 6.14, and improves Top-1 classification accuracy from 0.51/0.63 to 0.80, confirming superior conditional alignment. On CIFAR-10 with StyleGAN2, SONA (FID 3.38) essentially ties ReACGAN (3.39) while beating PD-GAN (4.06) by 0.68 FID. For text-to-image, GALIP+SONA improves FID by 0.6 on CUB (10.20 vs 11.76) and 0.6 on COCO (4.70 vs 5.30) over the original GALIP concatenation-based discriminator. In the zero-shot COCO comparison (Table 6), GALIP+SONA (zFID 12.43, 0.24B params, 12M images) is competitive with much larger models like Imagen (zFID 7.27, 7.9B params, 860M images) while being the fastest at 0.04 sec per image, though it trails Imagen and DALL-E 2 on absolute FID due to the large dataset and parameter gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

SONA builds directly on the projection-based discriminator (PD-GAN, Miyato & Koyama 2018) and the sliced-Wasserstein discriminator (SAN, Takida et al. 2024), but identifies a subtle limitation in PD-GANs: because the unconditional and conditional projections merge into a single y-dependent direction, the standard GAN loss does not fully exploit the unconditional discrimination component. The paper departs from classifier-based methods (AC-GAN, ReACGAN, ContraGAN) by achieving matching-aware discrimination through Bradley-Terry pairwise losses rather than cross-entropy classification, avoiding the need for manual tuning of weighting coefficients. The orthogonal projection inductive bias separating naturalness from alignment is the key architectural novelty, combined with bounded adaptive weighting to balance the three discrimination objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orthogonal decomposition of the discriminator into independent naturalness and alignment subspaces provides a principled way to prevent interference between authenticity and conditional alignment optimization, which is a persistent practical challenge in conditional GAN training. The bounded adaptive weighting mechanism (s²SAN + s²BT-C + s²BT-M = 1) offers a lightweight alternative to unbounded multi-task weighting schemes that the authors show can destabilize GAN training. Open questions include whether learnable ω_y (rather than frozen CLIP embeddings) would further improve text alignment, and whether the approach extends to video or other modalities. The MoG experiments revealing that PD-GANs fail to cover all modes at N≥30 while SONA achieves zero failures highlight a previously underappreciated limitation of the de facto standard projection discriminator.
