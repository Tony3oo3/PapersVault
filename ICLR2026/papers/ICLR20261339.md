## Problem

Neural Theorem Proving has achieved strong results on mathematical competition benchmarks, but its applicability to program verification—specifically the automated proving of Verification Conditions (VCs), a primary bottleneck in real-world verification—remains largely unexplored. No existing benchmark specifically targets VC proving, and prior verification-related benchmarks contain at most 17–20% actual VCs, focusing instead on auxiliary lemmas or non-industrial Lean-based tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261339/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark case is a single VC (one proof goal) embedded in a theory file that includes project-specific contextual definitions (datatypes, lemmas, axioms). The model receives the full theory context plus the target proposition via zero-shot prompting and must produce a complete formal proof in the target ITP language (Lean 4, Isabelle/HOL, or Rocq). VCs are machine-generated, often long and deeply nested formulas with dozens of quantifiers and operations, making them structurally very different from concise competition-style theorems. Proofs are verified by the corresponding ITP with a 10-minute timeout, and fake proofs (admit, sorry) are rejected.

## Method

The paper constructs the benchmark via a reliable extraction pipeline: industrial VCGs (Why3 and Frama-C) generate VCs from real-world verification projects (Linux kernel scheduler, Contiki OS, C++ STL, etc.), which are then translated into Isabelle, Lean, and Rocq using ~2400 expert-written mapping and rewriting rules that ensure semantic preservation and idiomatic target-language output. A novel complication process erases auxiliary annotations (assert, lemma, lemma-application) from the source programs, reducing Why3's strongest ATP pass rate from ~99% to ~62% while preserving provability, thereby producing genuinely challenging VCs. The final 600-case benchmark is balanced across two halves—Pearls of Programs (algorithms, data structures, competition problems) and Real C Verification (industrial C code)—with each category targeting a 20–25% ATP pass rate. Evaluation uses zero-shot prompting across seven LLMs (general-purpose and theorem-proving-specialized) and two ITP hammers (Sledgehammer, CoqHammer), reporting pass@1/4/8 metrics.

## Results

On the NTP4VC benchmark (600 VCs across Lean, Rocq, and Isabelle), all NTP models fail to achieve pass@8 above 12%, with the best NTP result being Minilang at 11.46% pass@8 on Isabelle. The strongest hammer-based baseline, Sledgehammer, achieves 18.00% pass@1 on Isabelle, outperforming every NTP model. Among Lean-specific provers, DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B leads with 1.67% pass@1, while the best general-purpose LLM (GPT-o4-mini-high) reaches only 1.19% pass@1 on Isabelle. The ATP baseline (Why3's Auto_Level_3) solves 22.00% of cases, confirming the benchmark targets genuinely hard VCs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sledgehammer (18.00% pass@1 on Isabelle) decisively outperforms all NTP models, with Minilang (11.46% pass@8) being the closest NTP competitor yet still trailing by a wide margin. CoqHammer (5.67% pass@1 on Rocq) similarly dominates all NTP models on Rocq, where the best NTP result is Qwen3-235B-A22B at 3.33% pass@8. Among Lean-specific provers, DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (1.67% pass@1, 3.00% pass@8) outperforms Goedel-Prover-V2-32B (0.50% pass@1, 2.17% pass@8), but both remain far below the ATP baseline of 22%. General-purpose LLMs (GPT-o4-mini-high, DeepSeek-V3.1, Qwen3) achieve at most 6.25% pass@8 on Isabelle, confirming that domain-specific fine-tuning provides only marginal gains. Notably, Minilang already incorporates Sledgehammer internally yet still fails to surpass Sledgehammer alone, suggesting that the LLM component adds no value on this task and that NTP4VC represents a fundamentally different reasoning domain from the mathematical problems these models were trained on.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a distinct task from the broader NTP literature by targeting Verification Condition (VC) proving specifically, rather than the mathematical competition problems (miniF2F, PutnamBench) or auxiliary lemmas from ITP projects that prior verification-related benchmarks (Lin et al. 2024, Thompson et al. 2025) predominantly contain. It departs from Lean-centric program verification benchmarks (Thakur et al. 2025, Dougherty & Mehta 2025) by grounding VCs in industrial pipelines (Why3, Frama-C) that handle real C code with mutable references, memory models, and side effects. The rule-based translation approach (~2400 expert-written rules) is positioned as a reliable alternative to LLM-based translation, and the complication process (erasing assert/lemma annotations) is novel in creating challenging VCs from already-verified projects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark and well-documented gap: NTP models achieving 55–88% on mathematical benchmarks drop to ≤2% pass@1 on VCs, while a 2010-era hammer (Sledgehammer) still outperforms all neural models at 18%. The error analysis (syntactic incoherence in 24%+ of proofs, semantic degeneration in 64% of Goedel-Prover outputs, hallucinated entities in 9%+) provides mechanistic evidence that VCs' long, deeply-nested, machine-generated structure demands fundamentally different capabilities than competition math. This establishes NTP4VC as a critical open problem for advancing automated program verification, with the benchmark's multi-language design and industrial provenance making it a likely standard reference. Open questions include whether fine-tuning on the >7.5k extracted VCs or hybrid NTP+hammer approaches can close the gap.
