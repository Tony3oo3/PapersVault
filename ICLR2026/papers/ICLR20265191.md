## Problem

Contextual multi-armed bandits with per-arm minimum aggregated revenue constraints cannot be reduced to independent per-context problems because the constraints couple all contexts, and the resulting planning problem (a linear program) lacks the closed-form optimal allocation that enables free exploration in the non-contextual setting. This paper bridges the gap between non-contextual MAB with revenue guarantees (Baudry et al., 2024) and contextual bandits with general stochastic constraints (Guo et al., 2025), showing that the free-exploration property breaks down when |C|>1 and K>2, reinstating the exploration-exploitation trade-off.

## Input / Output

At each round the learner observes a context c_t drawn from a known distribution {p_c}, and must select an arm k_t from K arms. The method produces a stationary allocation matrix w(t) ∈ π_K^{|C|} (each column a probability simplex over arms for a given context) by solving a linear program parameterized by UCB or LCB reward estimates. The LP is solved at most O(log T) times over the horizon via a lazy update scheme that triggers recomputation only when any arm-context pull count doubles.

## Method

Two algorithms are proposed: OLP solves LP(UCB(t), UCB(t)) each round, prioritizing performance (polylog regret, O(√T) constraint violation), while OPLP solves LP(UCB(t), LCB(t)) when feasible and falls back to LP(UCB(t), UCB(t)) otherwise, prioritizing constraint satisfaction (polylog constraint violation, O(√T) regret). The key analytical insight is a novel sub-optimality gap ρ(I) that decomposes the learning problem into identifying the optimal active constraint set I* (governed by ρ*) and then finding optimal weights within that structure. The analysis leverages the LP's vertex structure and a performance sensitivity coefficient S_{γ*} to derive problem-dependent bounds, and a lower bound confirms the √T dependence is optimal and refutes free exploration in the contextual setting.

## Results

The paper establishes theoretical guarantees for two proposed algorithms. OLP (Optimistic Linear Programming) achieves poly-logarithmic performance regret O(log²(T)/ρ*) with O(√T) constraint violation, while OPLP (Optimistic-Pessimistic Linear Programming) achieves O(√T) regret with poly-logarithmic constraint violation O(λ/γ*² · log²(T)). A matching lower bound of Ω(√T) for the combined regret R+V confirms the near-optimality of these guarantees, and an additional Ω(log T) lower bound on performance regret refutes the free-exploration property that holds in the non-contextual setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Guo et al. (2025), which applies a general primal-dual approach to contextual bandits with arbitrary stochastic constraints and achieves O(√T) bounds on both regret and constraint violation, the proposed OLP and OPLP exploit the specific structure of minimum aggregated revenue constraints to achieve strictly better guarantees: poly-logarithmic in one metric while retaining O(√T) in the other. Against Baudry et al. (2024), which studies the non-contextual version and achieves constant (no-regret) performance with O(√T) constraint violation thanks to a free-exploration property, the contextual setting inherently loses this advantage—the lower bound (Theorem 5.3(ii)) proves that Ω(log T) performance regret is unavoidable when |C| > 1 and K > 2, since optimal allocations can assign zero probability to certain arms, eliminating natural exploration. The paper's numerical experiments (Appendix G) confirm that OLP and OPLP outperform both the Optimistic algorithm of Guo et al. (2025) and the DOC/SPOC baselines of Baudry et al. (2024) on synthetic instances, with OPLP showing sensitivity to the feasibility margin γ* as predicted by theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the non-contextual MAB with per-arm minimum revenue guarantees of Baudry et al. (2024) to the contextual setting, where the optimal allocation is no longer available in closed form and must be recovered by solving a linear program. It deliberately departs from the primal-dual methodology of Slivkins et al. (2023) and Guo et al. (2025), which handle general stochastic constraints in contextual bandits, by exploiting the specific structure of minimum aggregated revenue constraints to achieve tighter, problem-dependent bounds. The confidence-bound + LP framework (OLP/OPLP) is a new algorithmic template that sits between the optimistic/pessimistic estimators of Baudry et al. and the Lagrangian-based approaches of the general constrained contextual bandit literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key conceptual contribution is showing that the 'free exploration' property—where the constraint structure in the non-contextual setting inherently forces linear sampling of all arms—breaks down as soon as multiple contexts and more than two arms are present, reinstating a genuine exploration-exploitation trade-off. This is confirmed by a lower bound proving that log(T) performance regret is unavoidable, ruling out the constant-regret guarantees achievable in the non-contextual case. Open questions include extending the framework to infinite context or action spaces (requiring linear or kernelized reward models), relaxing the known-context-probability assumption, and developing efficient solvers for the LP at scale. The empirical validation is limited to synthetic data, leaving practical performance on real recommendation or allocation problems untested.
