## Problem

Existing mobile device control agents conflate high-level task planning (know-what) with low-level action execution (know-how) in a single monolithic policy, leading to poor long-horizon credit assignment and weak task generalization. Training-free agents are capped by frozen foundation models, while learning-based agents require massive labeled datasets and hundreds of GPUs. The paper asks whether explicitly decoupling declarative and procedural knowledge into separate, differently-updated modules that co-evolve can yield both higher accuracy and stronger generalization with far fewer resources.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes only raw RGB screenshots at 2400×1080 resolution (no accessibility tree or view-hierarchy XML) plus a natural-language task instruction. The high-level planner (Qwen-2.5-VL-72B) outputs a sequence of single-step sub-goals in natural language, while the low-level executor (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B) outputs a structured tool-call JSON specifying one atomic action per step (click, long-press, swipe, type, system-button, terminate, or answer) with coordinate or text parameters. The executor operates without action history, relying solely on the current screenshot and the planner's sub-goal, whereas the planner receives the full episode history for multi-step reasoning and error analysis.

## Method

K²-Agent is a two-layer planner–executor hierarchy that co-evolves declarative and procedural knowledge through distinct update rules. The high-level planner runs a training-free Summarize-Reflect-Locate-Revise (SRLR) loop: it distills an initial knowledge base from a single expert demonstration, then iteratively reflects on execution failures, locates the first divergent step, and applies atomic edits (Add/Delete/Update/Highlight) to refine the knowledge base until three consecutive successes. The low-level executor is post-trained with C-GRPO, a curriculum-guided variant of Group Relative Policy Optimization that (i) decouples action errors into type and parameter dimensions to route samples into three replay pools sampled at fixed ratios, and (ii) injects variable-length expert action prefixes whose length is annealed over training steps and gated by per-sample difficulty, enabling the model to autonomously generate successful trajectories for denser reward signals. The two modules alternate in a closed loop (SRLR³ → C-GRPO), where improved planning yields more structured sub-goals for the executor and execution feedback drives further knowledge revision, achieving 76.1% on AndroidWorld with only 8×A100 GPUs and screenshot-only input.

## Results

On AndroidWorld (116 tasks, 20 apps), K²-Agent achieves a 76.1% ± 1.0% success rate using only raw screenshots and open-source backbones (Qwen-2.5-VL-72B planner + 7B executor), setting a new SOTA among screenshot-only methods. In zero-shot transfer, the low-level executor reaches 91.3% overall accuracy on ScreenSpot-v2 and 86.5% / 68.3% success rate on AitW-General / AitW-WebShopping, outperforming all prior RL- and SFT-based approaches. The full C-GRPO executor is trained on a single 8×A100 server in ~8 hours, and the planner requires only one expert demonstration per task category.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On AndroidWorld, K²-Agent (76.1%) surpasses the strongest open-source learning-based baselines—UI-TARS-2 (73.3%) and Mobile-Agent-v3 (73.3%)—by ~2.8 points, and edges out AutoGLM-Mobile (75.8%) which relies on the accessibility tree. It is within 0.6 points of FinalRun (76.7%), a closed-source GPT-5 system that also uses A11y input, while operating on screenshots alone. On ScreenSpot-v2 (zero-shot), the executor achieves 91.3% overall, outperforming general-purpose closed-source models (Claude 3.7 Sonnet: 87.6%, Operator: 70.5%) but trailing specialized grounding agents trained on massive GUI datasets (UI-Venus-Ground-72B: 95.3%, GUI-Owl-32B: 93.2%). On AitW, K²-Agent (86.5% General, 68.3% WebShopping) decisively beats the strongest prior RL-based method DigiRL (71.9%, 67.2%) by 14.6 and 1.1 points respectively, and outperforms all SFT-based and training-free baselines by wide margins (e.g., CogAgent: 25.0%, AppAgent: 17.7% on General).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024) into C-GRPO by adding two curriculum mechanisms—error-decoupled replay balancing and dynamic demonstration injection—tailored to the sparse-reward, large-action-space setting of GUI control. It departs from prior hierarchical agent designs (AgentS2, MobileUse) that either keep both layers training-free or train both with the same paradigm, by assigning fundamentally different update rules: a non-parametric SRLR self-evolution loop for the planner and a parametric RL objective for the executor. The implementation builds on the GRPOTrainer from VLM-R1 (Shen et al., 2025), and the cognitive-science motivation (Squire & Knowlton, 1995) for separating declarative and procedural knowledge provides a principled rationale for the architectural split that prior planner-executor systems treated as merely structural.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency: a 7B executor trained on only 606 curated samples on 8×A100 GPUs achieves 76.1% on AndroidWorld, rivaling systems that use 72B backbones, accessibility trees, or 10k+ training samples. The dual generalization evidence—declarative knowledge transferring across four VLM backbones and procedural skills transferring zero-shot to ScreenSpot-v2 (91.3%) and AitW (86.5%)—supports the hypothesis that decoupling know-what from know-how yields more portable capabilities. Open questions include the reflection module's dependence on visual observability (OS-level failures are undetectable), the need for one expert demonstration per task category, and whether the SRLR loop scales to tasks with massive episodic content or continuous visual dynamics that resist linguistic summarization.
