## Problem

Open-vocabulary 3D occupancy prediction faces a fundamental trade-off: sparse Gaussian representations (e.g., GaussTR) are computationally efficient but fail to capture small objects and fine-grained scene details, while dense voxel representations incur prohibitive memory and compute costs due to the high dimensionality of text-aligned features. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether a sparse-to-dense progressive strategy can retain the efficiency of Gaussian representations while recovering the fine details that fixed-query methods miss.

## Input / Output

The method consumes L surround-view RGB camera images (using the current frame plus 7 previous frames for spatio-temporal context), a pseudo depth map from Metric3D V2, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics; at inference it additionally takes arbitrary text prompts encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. Rasterization and Gaussian initialization operate at a reduced 180×320 resolution for efficiency. The output is a set of text-aligned feature Gaussian blobs that are voxelized (only at test time, at 0.4 m voxel resolution) into a dense 3D semantic occupancy field, alongside rendered depth maps and per-pixel feature renderings for open-vocabulary retrieval.

## Method

PG-Occ represents the scene as a set of feature Gaussian blobs (position, scale, rotation, opacity, 512-d text-aligned feature) and refines them through a base layer (4000 Gaussians initialized via FPS on back-projected pseudo depth) followed by B progressive layers, each adding 1000 new queries. The key mechanism is Progressive Online Densification (POD): after each layer, the model renders an expected depth map and identifies under-represented regions where the rendered depth deviates from the pseudo depth by more than half the voxel resolution, then feed-forwardly inserts new Gaussians at those locations via FPS—no gradient backpropagation is needed. Anisotropy-aware Feature Sampling (AFS) exploits each Gaussian's scale and rotation to generate 16 sample points distributed within its ellipsoid, which are projected onto multi-view feature planes for spatio-temporal aggregation, rather than treating each Gaussian as a single point. An Asymmetric Self-Attention (ASA) mask prevents newly added Gaussians from disturbing well-optimized ones while allowing them to attend to existing features. Training is fully self-supervised via 2D rasterization losses (L1 + SILog + temporal photometric for depth; MSE + cosine similarity for features), requiring no 3D labels or LiDAR.

## Results

On the Occ3D-nuScenes benchmark, PG-Occ achieves a state-of-the-art mIoU of 15.15 using only camera and text supervision (C+T), representing a 14.3% relative improvement over the previous best method GaussTR (13.25). On the nuScenes retrieval task, PG-Occ attains a visible mAP(v) of 21.2, surpassing LangOcc (18.2). For depth estimation, PG-Occ achieves an Abs Rel of 0.139, outperforming its own depth supervision source Metric3D V2 (0.170) by 18.2%. In terms of efficiency, PG-Occ runs at 2.40 FPS with 9 hours of training, compared to GaussTR's 1.04 FPS and 12 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PG-Occ (mIoU 15.15) outperforms the closest same-modality competitor GaussTR (13.25, C+T) by 1.9 mIoU, a 14.3% relative gain, and also surpasses the LiDAR-assisted VEON (13.95, C+L+T) and the camera-only GaussianFlowOcc (14.07, C). On per-class metrics, PG-Occ leads on car (26.42 vs. GaussTR's 20.43), bus (23.63 vs. 14.07), and truck (17.29 vs. 13.36), though it trails on pedestrian (2.74 vs. GaussianOcc's 7.95) and drivable surface (37.81 vs. GaussianFlowOcc's 54.40). On the nuScenes retrieval benchmark, PG-Occ achieves mAP(v) of 21.2 versus LangOcc's 18.2, the only other vision-based method reporting results. For depth estimation, PG-Occ (Abs Rel 0.139) beats all compared methods including its own depth supervision source Metric3D V2 (0.170) and GaussianOcc (0.197). In efficiency, PG-Occ is 2.3× faster at inference than GaussTR (2.40 vs. 1.04 FPS) and trains 25% faster (9 h vs. 12 h), while LangOcc requires over 48 hours of training at 1.70 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

PG-Occ extends the sparse 3D Gaussian representation for occupancy prediction introduced by GaussTR (Jiang et al., 2024) and GaussianFormer (Huang et al., 2024b), which use a fixed set of Gaussian queries. The key departure is replacing this fixed-query paradigm with progressive online densification—a feed-forward strategy that adaptively expands the Gaussian set in under-represented regions across multiple transformer layers, drawing conceptual inspiration from gradient-based densification in vanilla 3DGS but eliminating the need for backpropagation. The anisotropy-aware sampling mechanism further departs from prior work that treats Gaussians as isotropic points during feature aggregation, instead respecting each Gaussian's scale and rotation to define its effective receptive field in 2D feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14.3% relative mIoU improvement over GaussTR, combined with a 41% FPS increase, makes progressive Gaussian modeling a practical path toward real-time open-vocabulary occupancy prediction without LiDAR. The zero-shot transfer to the unseen Lyft Level-5 domain (without retraining) suggests the learned Gaussian priors generalize beyond the training distribution, which is valuable for deployment across diverse driving environments. However, the authors acknowledge that sparse viewpoints make depth-direction scale constraining difficult (causing popping artifacts), and the growing Gaussian count increases memory/compute costs that could limit real-time scalability. Open questions include whether 4D Gaussian formulations and stronger multi-view constraints can resolve these geometric instabilities while preserving the feed-forward efficiency.
