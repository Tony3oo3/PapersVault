## Problem

Existing LLM unlearning methods face a fundamental trade-off: training-based approaches (e.g., Gradient Ascent, NPO) achieve parameter-level robustness but require large paired datasets and suffer catastrophic utility degradation, while in-context unlearning is lightweight and precise but superficially vulnerable to prompt-removal or reverse-engineering attacks. No prior method combines the fine-grained supervision of contextualized refusal with the persistence of parameter optimization in a data-efficient manner.

## Input / Output

DUET consumes only text queries (no paired responses or refusal templates) from the forget set, mixed with retention queries in the same training batches. The supervision signal is the top-K raw logits (K=1000) from a frozen teacher LLM conditioned on an in-context unlearning prefix, evaluated at the first decoding position only. The output is a fine-tuned student model that naturally produces refusal responses for forget-domain queries while preserving general-knowledge generation, without requiring any explicit target response sequences during training.

## Method

DUET distills in-context refusal behavior into model parameters via Top-K logit alignment: a frozen teacher LLM is prompted with a carefully designed unlearning prefix, and the student is fine-tuned to match the teacher's top-K raw logits (selected by highest logit value) at the first decoding step using a Huber L1 loss. Crucially, the same distillation objective is applied uniformly to both forget and retain queries within mixed batches, eliminating the need for a separate retention loss or hyperparameter λ. The method requires only query-level data (no ground-truth answers or refusal responses), operates on a single decoding step rather than sequential token-by-token supervision, and embeds the refusal pattern directly into parameters, making it robust against reverse-prompt attacks that would trivially undo in-context unlearning.

## Results

On the MUSE-Books (Harry Potter) benchmark with Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, DUET achieves R-Forget of 4.27 (vs. 32.13 base) and R-Forget-500 of 5.98 (vs. 39.99 base) while preserving R-Retain at 78.33 (vs. 84.29 base) and MMLU at 61.45 (vs. 61.46 base), yielding the highest Performance Shift of 55.90. On WMDP (Zephyr-7B), DUET reduces Bio Acc-Forget from 63.70 to 29.40 and Cyber Acc-Forget from 43.68 to 26.60, while simultaneously improving MMLU to 60.63–60.65. DUET requires only 100 query-only forget samples (~1,319 tokens) plus 100 retention QA pairs, compared to the full ~1,440,000-token Harry Potter corpus used by baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUET outperforms all eight baselines on the aggregate Performance Shift metric on MUSE-Books (55.90 vs. next-best FLAT at 42.51 and SFR at 32.96). Against GA, DUET avoids catastrophic forgetting (GA drops R-Retain to 0 and MMLU to 24.87) while achieving comparable forgetting (4.27 vs. 0.00 R-Forget). Against FLAT, which achieves the lowest R-Forget (0.47), DUET sacrifices only ~4 points of forgetting but gains 20 points on R-Retain (78.33 vs. 58.33) and 2.5 points on MMLU. Against SimNPO, DUET achieves similar forgetting (4.27 vs. 17.60) but dramatically better retention (78.33 vs. 43.09 R-Retain). Against NPO and its KL-regularized variant, DUET achieves substantially lower R-Forget (4.27 vs. 24.18/28.92) with higher MMLU (61.45 vs. 54.79/59.47). Against Refusal Training, DUET achieves 7× lower R-Forget (4.27 vs. 31.02) with higher retention. On WMDP, DUET delivers the best Performance Shift on both Bio and Cyber subtasks, outperforming RMU (the closest competitor) by maintaining MMLU at 60.63–60.65 versus RMU's 25.50–57.81. Against UNDIAL and SFR (Appendix A.5), DUET achieves lower R-Forget (4.27 vs. 28.20/10.45) with higher R-Retain (78.33 vs. 73.92/71.93) and MMLU (61.45 vs. 58.08/58.28).

## Relation to Prior Work

DUET sits at the intersection of two established LLM unlearning paradigms: in-context unlearning (Pawelczyk et al., 2023), which steers behavior via prompts without parameter updates, and training-based unlearning (GA, NPO, FLAT, RMU), which modifies weights but risks catastrophic forgetting. The key departure is repurposing in-context unlearning not as the final mechanism but as a *supervision signal*: a prompted teacher's Top-K logit shifts are distilled into the student's parameters via a Huber loss, unifying forgetting and retention under a single objective rather than the conventional L_unlearn + λ·L_retain formulation. This eliminates the need for paired negative/positive responses that prior training-based methods require, and the Top-K restriction (vs. full-vocabulary KL) is a deliberate design choice to avoid noise from low-probability tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the data efficiency (100 queries ≈ 1,319 tokens vs. ~1.4M tokens in the full corpus) and the demonstrated robustness against reverse-prompt attacks, which directly addresses a critical deployment concern for unlearned models. However, open questions remain: the approach is evaluated primarily on 3B–7B models and concept-centric benchmarks, the teacher prefix quality is a sensitive hyperparameter that requires manual or LLM-assisted optimization, and the paper itself flags boundary determination (what to forget vs. retain) and comprehensive evaluation methodology as unresolved challenges. Whether the one-step Top-K distillation generalizes to multi-step reasoning tasks or larger models is left as future work.
