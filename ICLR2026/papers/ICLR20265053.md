## Problem

Multi-objective LLM alignment at inference time requires balancing diverse and often competing objectives (e.g., helpfulness, safety, truthfulness), but existing methods either demand user-specified weights, hyperparameter search over validation sets, or demographic information that is unavailable or unreliable in practice. No prior inference-time algorithm robustly guarantees that no objective drops below a critical threshold without such external information, leaving a gap for a principled worst-case alignment approach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265053/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt x, a frozen reference language model π_ref (gemma-2-2b-it), and G pre-trained value functions V_g (one per objective, each a GPT-2-sized model trained via MSE loss against reward model scores on reference-policy responses), RMOD produces a single robust text response of up to T tokens. The value functions are evaluated on B-token blocks sampled from π_ref, and the method operates entirely at inference time with no retraining of the reference model. A distilled variant (DISTILL-RMOD) further compresses the procedure into a single SFT-trained model that generates one response per prompt without blockwise search.

## Method

RMOD formulates robust decoding as a max-min two-player game between adversarial reward weights w over the (G−1)-simplex and the sampling policy π, regularized by KL divergence to π_ref. By the minimax theorem, the game reduces to a convex log-sum-exp optimization over w (dimension G), whose solution w* equalizes expected future rewards across objectives (via KKT conditions), while the optimal policy π*(·|w*) has a closed-form exponential tilt of π_ref. The practical algorithm performs blockwise best-of-K: at each decoding step, K blocks of B tokens are sampled from π_ref, scored by all G value functions, and weights are updated for I iterations via projected gradient descent on log-weights; the block with the highest worst-case weighted value is selected. Because value function evaluations are independent of w and the weight optimization is low-dimensional, the added latency is under 4.5% over standard Controlled Decoding. The trade-off parameter λ controls weight sparsity: low λ yields near-uniform weights (reducing to UNIFORM decoding), while high λ concentrates weight on the single worst objective.

## Results

On the Anthropic Helpfulness-Harmless (HH) dataset, RMOD achieves a worst-case win rate (WCWR) of 59.1% against the reference policy, outperforming CD-UNIFORM (57.6%), MO-GRPO (54.6%), and MO-DPO (52.8%) in LLM-as-Judge evaluation with GPT-4o. The distilled variant DISTILL-RMOD reaches 57.9% WCWR while maintaining a KL divergence of only 8.48 (vs. ≤27.73 for RMOD and 336.08 for MO-GRPO). On UltraFeedback (5 objectives) and ValuePrism (up to 10 objectives), RMOD consistently improves worst-case reward over the UNIFORM baseline, with the gap widening as the number of objectives increases. RMOD incurs less than 4.5% additional latency compared to standard Controlled Decoding baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RMOD outperforms all five baseline families across all three datasets. Against Controlled Decoding (Mudgal et al., 2023), RMOD achieves up to 20% higher worst-case win rates on HH, while CD-UNIFORM and single-objective CD variants (CD-HARMLESS, CD-HELPFUL) sacrifice one objective to improve another. Against Rewarded Soup (Rame et al., 2024) and Multi-Objective Decoding (Shi et al., 2024), both of which require pre-trained per-objective models and fixed weightings, RMOD achieves higher worst-case rewards without needing any user-specified weights. Against MO-GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), RMOD achieves 4.5 percentage points higher WCWR (59.1% vs. 54.6%) while MO-GRPO exhibits extreme KL divergence (336.08 vs. ≤27.73), indicating severe distribution shift. Against MO-DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), RMOD leads by 6.3 percentage points in WCWR, though MO-DPO maintains the lowest KL divergence (0.5) at the cost of much weaker alignment. The distilled variant DISTILL-RMOD matches or exceeds CD-UNIFORM in WCWR (57.9% vs. 57.6%) while generating a single response per prompt, making it substantially more compute-efficient than blockwise decoding methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

RMOD builds directly on the Controlled Decoding (CD) framework of Mudgal et al. (2023) for blockwise KL-regularized decoding and the FUDGE-style value function training of Yang & Klein (2021), but departs from prior multi-objective inference-time alignment (Shi et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024b) by eliminating the need for user-specified or validation-searched reward weights. The key conceptual departure is formulating the weight selection as a maximin two-player game whose Nash equilibrium reduces to a convex log-sum-exp optimization, whereas prior robust alignment work (Ramesh et al., 2024; Chakraborty et al., 2024b) operates at training time over user groups rather than at inference time over reward objectives. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to apply a maxmin robust objective in the inference-time alignment setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RMOD offers a practical, weight-free mechanism for balancing competing alignment objectives at deployment, with less than 4.5% latency overhead over standard Controlled Decoding, making it immediately deployable. The distillation result (DISTILL-RMOD achieving 57.9% worst-case win rate vs. RMOD's 59.1% at a fraction of the compute) suggests robust alignment behavior can be internalized into a single forward pass, which is practically significant for production systems. Open questions include scalability beyond ~10 objectives (performance degrades noticeably on ValuePrism), generalization to larger models (only 2B tested), and whether the value function approximation error compounds over long generations. The approach also assumes access to separate reward models per objective, which may not always be available.
