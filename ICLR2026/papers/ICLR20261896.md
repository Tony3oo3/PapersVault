## Problem

LoRA and its variants consistently underperform full fine-tuning on complex tasks, yet the root cause has been overlooked: the fixed low-rank subspace of LoRA is structurally misaligned with the true effective update directions of full fine-tuning gradients. The paper shows this gradient intrinsic dimensionality (GID) can exceed typical LoRA ranks by more than 100×, creating a fundamental expressiveness bottleneck that existing rank-augmentation, optimization, and initialization strategies fail to address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261896/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained models (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Base for text, T5-Base for NLU, CLIP-ViT-B/16 for vision) and requires a preliminary gradient collection pass over a small sample of task-specific data to compute per-layer full fine-tuning gradients. These gradients are used to estimate GID and loss sensitivity scores, which then configure the LoRA adapter architecture before the actual fine-tuning begins. The fine-tuned model produces standard outputs: generated text for NLG tasks, class labels for NLU and image classification, with no changes to inference-time behavior compared to vanilla LoRA.

## Method

The paper introduces an entropy-based estimator that computes the effective rank of per-layer FFT gradient matrices via the Shannon entropy of their normalized singular value distributions, yielding a robust, threshold-free measure of gradient intrinsic dimensionality. RaLoRA then uses this GID to determine the number of parallel mini-LoRA blocks (n_l) per layer via a block-diagonal decomposition, expanding the equivalent rank to n_l × r while keeping the total parameter count identical to vanilla LoRA. RaLoRA-Pro extends this with a dual-alignment strategy: it additionally computes per-layer loss sensitivity scores I(W_l) = avg(|W_l ⊙ G_l|) to proportionally reallocate rank across layers, so that more influential layers receive higher adaptation capacity. The key architectural insight is that expressive power depends not only on parameter count but on structural alignment with the gradient subspace geometry, and that GID (structural complexity) and loss sensitivity (parameter importance) are empirically decoupled properties requiring separate treatment.

## Results

On the NLG benchmarks (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Base), RaLoRA-Pro achieves 6.72 on MT-Bench (+0.57 over LoRA, surpassing FFT's 5.88), 73.01 on GSM8K (+5.23 over LoRA, closing 88.5% of the FFT gap), and 48.37 on HumanEval. RaLoRA alone reaches 48.78 on HumanEval (+5.69 over LoRA) and 72.25 on GSM8K (+4.47). On GLUE (T5-Base), both methods achieve ~87.2 average accuracy vs. 82.08 for LoRA (+5.1%), and on image classification (CLIP-ViT-B/16), RaLoRA-Pro reaches 90.66% average accuracy (+1.58% over LoRA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaLoRA and RaLoRA-Pro consistently outperform all 10 LoRA variants across all three NLG benchmarks. On GSM8K, RaLoRA (72.25) beats the strongest baseline MELoRA (71.27) by +0.98 and LoRA+ (71.29) by +0.96, while RaLoRA-Pro (73.01) further extends the lead. On HumanEval, RaLoRA (48.78) surpasses the next-best RSLoRA (45.78) by +3.0 and MoRA (29.68) by a large margin of +19.1. On MT-Bench, RaLoRA-Pro (6.72) exceeds MoRA (6.38) and LoRA+ (6.35) by +0.34 and +0.37 respectively, and notably surpasses full fine-tuning (5.88) by +0.84. On GLUE, RaLoRA (87.24 avg) outperforms OLoRA (86.32) and MoRA (86.35) and even exceeds FFT (87.91) on QNLI and MRPC individually. In the extended comparison (Appendix H), RaLoRA-Pro also beats non-LoRA methods (IA)³ (66.62 on GSM8K) and FourierFT (52.71), as well as the high-rank HiRA variant (69.93 on GSM8K, 45.73 on HumanEval).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA by identifying a specific structural mismatch that prior variants overlooked: the gap between LoRA's fixed rank and the true effective dimensionality of full fine-tuning gradients. It repurposes the entropy-based effective rank estimator from signal processing (Roy & Vetterli, 2007) to quantify per-layer gradient intrinsic dimensionality (GID), and borrows the block-diagonal decomposition from MELoRA (Ren et al., 2024) as the architectural vehicle for rank expansion. RaLoRA-Pro further integrates the loss-sensitivity-guided parameter reallocation from PLATON (Zhang et al., 2022), creating a dual-alignment framework that no prior LoRA variant has combined. Unlike rank-augmentation methods that use heuristic importance scores or fixed stacking, this work grounds rank selection in a data-driven measurement of the actual gradient subspace geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropy-based GID estimator is positioned as a plug-in tool orthogonal to most existing LoRA variants, potentially enabling adaptive rank selection across a broad range of PEFT methods. However, the estimator's effectiveness remains empirical without theoretical guarantees, and the paper only explores three specific applications (RaLoRA, RaLoRA-Pro, LoRA+GID). Open questions include how the estimator integrates with other PEFT paradigms (e.g., GaLore, FourierFT), whether it generalizes to multimodal LLMs, and whether the block-diagonal structure is truly optimal compared to other high-rank constructions. The finding that GID and Fisher Information are uncorrelated provides a clean theoretical justification for the dual-alignment design but also suggests the two signals capture fundamentally different aspects of the optimization landscape that warrant further study.
