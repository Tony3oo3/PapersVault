## Problem

Frozen pre-trained vision-language models used as zero-shot goal-conditioned value functions suffer from two coupled limitations: contrastive VLMs (e.g., CLIP) lack temporal reasoning and cannot disambiguate visually similar states at different task stages, while autoregressive VLMs (e.g., GVL) inherit a monotonic-increasing bias from chronologically ordered pre-training data. Both architectures rely on static pre-trained representations, limiting generalization across unseen tasks, environments, and robot embodiments without large-scale pre-training or task-specific demonstrations.

## Input / Output

At each timestep, the method consumes a single video frame and a natural language task description, encoded by a frozen OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 into a concatenated 2048-dim joint representation. The output is a scalar progress estimate in [0,1] per frame, which can be directly repurposed as a dense reward signal for offline RL (e.g., IQL on Meta-World MT10). Unusually, the output at timestep t is not a function of the current frame alone: the lightweight adaptation module's parameters carry accumulated history from all prior frames in the trajectory, so the value estimate is path-dependent even though no explicit memory buffer is maintained.

## Method

VITA comprises a frozen CLIP encoder, a two-layer residual MLP adaptation module (d'=64, GELU), and a two-layer MLP regression head. Training uses gradient-based meta-learning (MAML-style): the self-supervised loss is a reconstruction objective with learnable linear projections P_K and P_V, and the entire system is optimized so that a single test-time gradient step on this loss minimizes the supervised MSE prediction loss. A dissimilarity-based sampling strategy selects k=8 most visually diverse sub-trajectories (window length 8) per training trajectory to mitigate shortcut learning. At inference, the adaptation module is updated sequentially at every timestep via one gradient step (η=0.1) on the meta-learned self-supervised loss, encoding trajectory history implicitly into its parameters rather than in recurrent hidden states or cached key-value pairs. This sequential implicit memory preserves chronological order (unlike GVL's frame shuffling) while enabling zero-shot generalization without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On the primary generalization benchmark (Table 1), VITA achieves VOC scores of 0.782 (in-distribution), 0.725 (environment shift, lm_pnp), 0.820 (embodiment shift, dt_tk_pnp), and 0.490 (long-horizon ms_sweep), outperforming the state-of-the-art zero-shot method GVL-0S (0.269–0.372) and GVL-1S (0.211–0.406) across all 10 evaluation datasets. On the Meta-World MT10 offline RL benchmark, VITA's zero-shot reward shaping yields an IQM of 0.815, surpassing the simulation's own fuzzy-logic dense reward (META-WL, 0.779) and all CLIP-based baselines (0.746–0.785). VITA also achieves perfect expert-vs-non-expert discrimination (BinVOC = 1.00), matching GVL but exceeding CLIP-GRU (0.80) and CLIP-FT (0.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VITA outperforms all four external baselines across every evaluation setting. Against VLM-CL (Baumli et al., 2023) and VLM-RM (Rocamonde et al., 2024), which rely on frame-level CLIP similarity without temporal modeling, VITA achieves 0.606–0.820 VOC versus 0.017–0.108 and −0.226–0.099 respectively, a gap of over 0.5 VOC on most datasets. Against the state-of-the-art autoregressive VLM approach GVL (Ma et al., 2024), VITA wins on 8 of 10 datasets in zero-shot (GVL-0S) and 9 of 10 in one-shot (GVL-1S) settings; the two exceptions are ft_fold and rd_fold where GVL-1S edges ahead by 0.03–0.05 VOC, likely due to Gemini's bias toward folding-like manipulations. On the Meta-World MT10 offline RL benchmark, VITA's zero-shot reward shaping (IQM 0.815) exceeds the simulation's own fuzzy-logic dense reward META-WL (Yu et al., 2020, IQM 0.779) by 0.036, demonstrating that a value estimator trained solely on real-world robot data can generalize to simulated reward shaping. VITA also matches GVL's perfect BinVOC of 1.00 on expert-vs-non-expert discrimination while outperforming CLIP-GRU (0.80), suggesting that implicit memory via sequential test-time updates is more robust to temporal shortcuts than explicit recurrent hidden states.

## Relation to Prior Work

VITA builds on two main threads: (1) goal-conditioned value function estimation using VLMs, where prior work (GVL/Ma et al. 2024, Baumli et al. 2023, Rocamonde et al. 2024) relies on frozen contrastive or autoregressive representations without any test-time modification; and (2) test-time training for sequence modeling (Sun et al. 2024), where an adaptation module is updated per-timestep via a self-supervised loss. The key departure is combining these: meta-learning a self-supervised loss on a lightweight adapter over frozen CLIP representations, so that temporal history is encoded in the adapter's parameters rather than in recurrent hidden states or in-context prompts. The dissimilarity-based sampling strategy is a novel training-time contribution to counter shortcut learning in value estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a lightweight CLIP-based model with a 64-dim adaptation module outperforms Gemini 1.5 Pro (a proprietary autoregressive VLM) on zero-shot value estimation is practically significant: it suggests that expensive autoregressive VLMs may not be necessary for temporal progress reasoning if the right adaptation mechanism is in place. The finding that implicit memory via sequential parameter updates consistently outperforms explicit recurrent memory (GRU) challenges the default assumption that RNNs are the natural way to encode trajectory history. Open questions include whether the approach extends to real-time closed-loop control (the authors note per-timestep updates may be unsafe in deployment), whether it generalizes beyond the single training embodiment and pick-and-place task family, and whether a theoretical analysis of how dissimilarity-based sampling prevents shortcut learning is possible.
