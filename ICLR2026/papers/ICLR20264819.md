## Problem

Hierarchical image coding architectures theoretically offer multi-scale representations for flexible quality scalability, yet existing approaches suffer from cross-scale energy dispersion and spectral aliasing during naive training, resulting in slow convergence (e.g., ~10 days for QARV) and performance that underperforms lighter single-scale codecs. The root cause is that standard optimization fails to enforce the intended frequency-stratified information allocation across scales, causing each scale to redundantly encode overlapping frequency components.

## Input / Output

The codec takes an RGB image (trained on 256×256 crops, finetuned on 512×512) and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image. Internally, the 4-scale hierarchy generates latent variables at 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, and 1/64 spatial resolutions, each entropy-coded sequentially with the previous scale's decoded features as conditional prior. The hierarchical structure natively supports progressive decoding at any scale, and both proposed regularizers are active only during training, imposing zero additional inference cost.

## Method

The paper proposes two training-only spectral regularizers for a lightweight 4-scale hierarchical CNN codec (DHIC). First, intra-scale frequency regularization applies a DCT-based progressive radial mask that linearly expands the retained frequency radius from 0.05 to 1.0 over the first 100 epochs, forcing each scale to specialize in its assigned low-to-high frequency band before higher frequencies are introduced. Second, inter-scale latent regularization downsamples the lower-scale latent via Haar DWT (retaining LL, LH, HL sub-bands), applies a 1×1 convolution to align frequency channels with the higher-scale latent, and penalizes their L2 similarity (weight δ=0.1) to suppress spectral aliasing. Together, these regularizers accelerate convergence by 2.3× and yield an additional 9.49% BD-Rate improvement over the unregularized baseline, achieving −20.65% average BD-Rate over VTM-22.0.

## Results

DHIC-Regu achieves an average BD-Rate of −20.65% over VTM-22.0 across Kodak (−19.73%), CLIC Professional Valid (−18.13%), and Tecnick (−24.09%), setting a new state of the art in learned image compression. The proposed spectral regularization accelerates training by 2.3× (converging in ~3.8 days vs. ~10 days for QARV or TCM) while delivering an additional 9.49% bitrate reduction over the unregularized DHIC-Base. On high-resolution 4K images (LIU4K-v2), DHIC-Regu achieves −17.19% BD-Rate with a decoding time of 1297 ms, compared to −12.98% and 2754 ms for HPCM-Large, demonstrating superior scaling efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DHIC-Regu outperforms all nine compared baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest single-scale competitor HPCM-Large (ICCV'25), it achieves a 0.73% average BD-Rate improvement (−20.65% vs. −19.92%) while using fewer KMACs (977.73 vs. 1261.29) and faster decoding (68.48 ms vs. 112.27 ms). Against the representative hierarchical codec QARV (TPAMI'24), the improvement is dramatic: 13.45% average BD-Rate gain (−20.65% vs. −7.20%), with QARV also requiring ~10 days of training versus ~3.8 days for DHIC-Regu. Among other single-scale methods, DHIC-Regu surpasses MambaIC (CVPR'25) by 7.73%, MLIC++ by 6.90%, FLIC by 7.54%, TCM-Large by 9.70%, MambaVC by 12.98%, WeConvene by 11.87%, and ELIC by 16.76% in average BD-Rate. The unregularized DHIC-Base already outperforms QARV and ELIC, confirming that the hierarchical architecture itself provides a structural advantage, but the spectral regularization is responsible for the remaining ~9.5% gain that pushes the method past all single-scale SOTA codecs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical VAE (HVAE) line of work for learned image compression (QARV, NVAE, Deep Hierarchical Video Compression) by diagnosing why existing hierarchical codecs underperform their theoretical multi-scale advantages. It draws on the frequency principle / spectral bias literature (Rahaman et al., Xu & Zhou) to frame the optimization failure as spectral dispersion and inter-scale aliasing, then introduces two training-only regularizers—DCT-based progressive spectral truncation and DWT-based inter-scale L2 penalty—that are absent from prior hierarchical coding work. The base architecture (DHIC) is deliberately lightweight (single CNN block per scale) to isolate the effect of the training scheme from architectural complexity, departing from the heavy Transformer/Mamba backbones used in concurrent single-scale SOTA methods like HPCM and MambaIC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that hierarchical image coding, long considered a promising but underperforming alternative to single-scale VAEs, can actually surpass both traditional (VTM-22.0) and learned single-scale codecs when trained with explicit spectral guidance—achieving 20.65% average BD-Rate savings and 2.3× faster convergence with zero inference overhead. A key open question is whether the spectral regularization ideas can be adapted to single-scale models with complex context modeling, where the paper's own experiments show the method degrades performance (MLIC++, HPCM), suggesting the frequency-stratification benefit is intrinsic to the multi-scale latent structure. The work also raises the broader question of whether other multi-scale generative or representation-learning architectures (e.g., NVAE for generation) suffer from analogous spectral entanglement that could be addressed with similar regularizers.
