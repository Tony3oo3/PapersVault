## Problem

Current MLLM-based embodied systems suffer from two critical gaps: (1) a geometric adaptability gap, where 2D-only models lack 3D spatial reasoning while hard-coded 3D injection wastes computation and introduces noise when 3D cues are irrelevant; and (2) an embodiment constraint gap, where generated plans are semantically valid but physically infeasible on real robots due to neglected kinematic limits, workspace boundaries, and object affordances.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a natural language instruction, a sequence of RGB frames (16 during training, 32 at inference, at 448×448 resolution), and optionally per-frame depth maps with camera intrinsics K and extrinsic poses M_i for 3D world-coordinate reconstruction. Outputs span natural language responses, 2D point coordinates normalized to [0,1000], 3D bounding boxes on a 0.1 m voxel grid, and executable task plans or navigation subgoal locations. A binary depth flag per sample allows mixed mini-batches of RGB-only and RGB-D data, with the 3D component zero-masked when absent.

## Method

OmniEVA (built on InternVL3-8B) introduces two core mechanisms. First, a Task-Adaptive Gated Router (TAGR) computes a gate decision from a sentence-transformer encoding of the instruction and an average-pooled global scene descriptor, passed through a small MLP to yield two gate logits; Gumbel-Softmax hard gating then selects between pure 2D visual tokens and 2D+3D-fused tokens (a binary MoE), injecting sinusoidally encoded patch-level 3D world coordinates only when spatially necessary. Second, a Task- and Embodiment-aware GRPO (TE-GRPO) extends standard GRPO with two additional rewards—a semantic task-satisfaction score and a simulation-verified embodiment-feasibility score—combined via a progressive curriculum coefficient λ_t that shifts optimization focus from task correctness to physical executability over training. The full pipeline is a three-stage cascade: TAGR pretraining (frozen ViT, small LLM LR), supervised fine-tuning on ~5.2 M mixed 2D/3D/video samples, and TE-GRPO reinforcement with 8 candidates per group.

## Results

OmniEVA-Base (8B parameters) achieves state-of-the-art performance on 7 of 8 embodied reasoning benchmarks, with an average gain of +10.45 over the previous best (RoboBrain2.0-32B). Headline numbers include 74.95 on Where2Place, 57.17 on VSI-Bench, 91.19 on RoboRefit, and 86.96 on Where2Go. On 3D benchmarks, OmniEVA-Base leads on SQA3D (62.9), ScanQA (30.6), and Scan2Cap (94.6 CIDEr), while on object navigation it achieves 74.2 SR / 42.5 SPL on HM3D and 59.1 SR / 26.2 SPL on MP3D. After embodiment-aware RL fine-tuning (OmniEVA-ER), execution success rate jumps to 81.89% average across arm-length generalization tests, versus 43.30% for OmniEVA-Base and 18.37% for RoboBrain2.0-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniEVA-Base (8B) outperforms the strongest embodied baseline RoboBrain2.0-32B on all eight 2D/in-house benchmarks, with the largest margins on RoboRefit (+21.2), Where2Go (+45.9), and Where2Fit (+18.9). It also surpasses general-purpose MLLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, InternVL3-78B) by wide margins on every benchmark. On 3D reasoning (Table 3), OmniEVA-Base beats 3DRS on SQA3D (62.9 vs 60.6), ScanQA (30.6 vs 30.3), and Scan2Cap (94.6 vs 86.1 CIDEr), but trails 3DRS on ScanRefer without auxiliary (55.8 vs 62.9 IoU@0.25). For object navigation, OmniEVA-Base exceeds UniNavid on both HM3D (74.2 vs 73.7 SR; 42.5 vs 37.1 SPL) and MP3D (59.1 SR, 26.2 SPL, where UniNavid reports no MP3D results). After TE-GRPO reinforcement (OmniEVA-ER), embodiment execution success rate reaches 81.89% average, a +38.6-point gain over OmniEVA-Base and +63.5 points over RoboBrain2.0-7B, with robust generalization to unseen arm lengths (80.5% unseen avg vs 42.3% for OmniEVA-Base).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two parallel lines of work: (1) MLLM-based embodied reasoning (RoboPoint, RoboSpatial, RoboBrain) which operates primarily on 2D inputs, and (2) 3D LLMs (3D-LLM, LLaVA-3D, Video-3D-LLM, 3DRS) that inject 3D positional information via hard-coded, always-on mechanisms. OmniEVA departs from both by introducing a task-conditioned gated router (TAGR) that selectively activates 3D features only when spatially necessary, and by augmenting GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning with embodiment-specific rewards (kinematic feasibility, workspace constraints) that prior embodied reasoning work largely ignores. The architecture builds on InternVL3-8B as its backbone, repurposing the standard ViT-MLP-LLM pipeline with a lightweight routing module inserted between the vision encoder and the language model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical disconnect in embodied AI: models that reason well about scenes but produce plans that are physically infeasible on real robots. The embodiment-aware RL (TE-GRPO) and the four new primitive benchmarks (Where2Go, Where2Fit, Where2Approach, Where2Grasp) fill a gap in systematic evaluation of physical executability. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include scene-level (rather than patch-level) gating, which may be suboptimal in heterogeneous environments, and the reliance on a limited set of embodiment parameters (arm length) for generalization testing. Open questions include whether the gated routing scales to more complex multi-robot or dynamic-embodiment settings, and whether the curriculum-based reward scheduling generalizes beyond the specific manipulation tasks tested.
