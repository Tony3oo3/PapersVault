## Problem

Scaling visual preference optimization is hindered by two intertwined bottlenecks: existing open-source preference datasets contain substantial conflicting preference patterns (winners excel in aesthetics but underperform in alignment, etc.), causing standard Diffusion-DPO to fail at extracting meaningful signals as data scales; and these datasets suffer from low resolution (512–768px), limited prompt diversity, and imbalanced distributions from random collection. The paper asks how to make preference learning robust to noisy data while simultaneously providing a high-quality dataset that enables effective scaling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (1M unique for images, 30K for videos) and produces generated images at 1024×1024 or videos at 720p/1024p with 16:9 or 1:1 aspect ratios. Training operates on preference pairs (winner/loser) where each pair shares the same prompt; the ViPO-Image-1M dataset spans five 200K-pair categories (Aesthetics, Text-Image Alignment, Text Rendering, Portrait Quality, Composition) and ViPO-Video-300K spans three 100K-pair categories (Motion Quality, Video-Text Alignment, Visual Quality). The two-stage pipeline first performs SFT on winner images only, then applies Poly-DPO on the full preference pairs.

## Method

Poly-DPO extends the Diffusion-DPO loss with a single additive polynomial term α(1−p^{w>l}), which rescales the standard DPO gradient (1−p^{w>l}) by a confidence-dependent factor (1+αp^{w>l}). This one hyperparameter adapts the optimization to three data regimes: α>0 upweights uncertain samples to navigate conflicting multi-dimensional preferences in noisy datasets like Pick-a-Pic V2; α<0 penalizes overconfident predictions to prevent memorization on trivially distinguishable pairs; and α=0 (standard DPO) is optimal on high-quality balanced data, where the method provably converges to the original objective. The companion ViPO dataset is constructed using state-of-the-art generators (Seedream-3.0, Qwen-Image, FLUX, WanVideo, Seedance) with systematic per-category pipelines—prompt perturbation for alignment, OCR-based filtering for text rendering, multi-VLM majority voting for aesthetics and composition, and I2V pipelines for video—ensuring reliable, balanced preference signals at 1024px/720p+ resolution.

## Results

On the noisy Pick-a-Pic V2 dataset, Poly-DPO achieves 49.87 overall GenEval score for SD1.5 and 60.34 for SDXL, surpassing all off-policy and on-policy baselines. On the high-quality ViPO-Image-1M dataset, SD3.5-Medium reaches 87.71 on DPG-Bench and 0.83 on GenEval, while FLUX.1-dev reaches 87.31 on DPG-Bench, both surpassing GPT-Image 1 (85.15). Text rendering on CVTG-2K improves by 59.8% for SD3.5-Medium (0.4378→0.6995). On video generation, Dynamic Spatial Relationship on VBench-2.0 improves by 37.4% (24.64→33.82) with ViPO-Video-300K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Poly-DPO consistently outperforms all six baselines on the noisy Pick-a-Pic V2 dataset. On GenEval, it achieves 49.87 (SD1.5) and 60.34 (SDXL), beating the strongest on-policy baseline LPO (48.77/59.85) and all off-policy methods including Diffusion-DPO (43.00/58.02), Diffusion-KTO (43.65), and MAPO (52.80). The gains are most dramatic on challenging sub-tasks: counting improves from 38.75 (Diffusion-DPO) to 51.25, and attribute binding from 3.75 to 14.00 for SD1.5. On reward-based metrics (Pick-a-Pic V2 test), Poly-DPO achieves 13.1% HPSv2.1 improvement versus Diffusion-DPO's 4.4% and Diffusion-KTO's 12.2%. On the high-quality ViPO-Image-1M dataset, the trained SD3.5-Medium (87.71) and FLUX.1-dev (87.31) surpass commercial GPT-Image 1 (85.15) on DPG-Bench, while on-policy methods like DDPO and SPO are not directly compared on this dataset due to their iterative sampling cost. The key finding is that Poly-DPO's advantage is largest on noisy/conflicting data (Pick-a-Pic V2) and converges to standard DPO (α→0) on high-quality ViPO data, validating that the algorithmic contribution is most valuable when data quality is imperfect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024) by incorporating a polynomial expansion term inspired by PolyLoss (Leng et al., 2022), creating Poly-DPO which adaptively reweights samples based on prediction confidence. The DPO framework from Rafailov et al. (NeurIPS 2023) provides the foundational off-policy preference optimization objective that is adapted to the diffusion setting. The paper positions its contribution as addressing two orthogonal bottlenecks to scaling visual preference optimization: algorithmic fragility to noisy/conflicting preference signals (solved by Poly-DPO) and data quality limitations in existing open-source datasets (solved by the ViPO dataset), rather than proposing a fundamentally new optimization paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ViPO dataset (1M image pairs at 1024px across 5 categories, 300K video pairs at 720p+ across 3 categories) is a significant practical resource that addresses resolution, prompt diversity, and distributional balance limitations of prior datasets like Pick-a-Pic and HPD. The finding that Poly-DPO converges to standard DPO (α→0) on high-quality data is a useful diagnostic: it suggests that data curation may be more impactful than algorithmic sophistication, and that the polynomial term serves primarily as a robustness mechanism for imperfect data. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to on-policy methods, whether the convergence behavior holds at even larger scales, and whether the VLM-based annotation pipeline (which lags humans on video motion quality at 55% vs 67.2%) can be improved to close the remaining gap in temporal preference labeling.
