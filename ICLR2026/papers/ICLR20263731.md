## Problem

Identifiability of disentangled representations has traditionally relied on statistical independence of latent factors or additional distributional assumptions (temporal structure, auxiliary variables, multiple views), which are restrictive and may not hold in practice. This paper asks whether identifiability can be established purely from properties of the generative mechanism—how latent factors act on observations—without any assumptions on the latent density, even when factors are statistically dependent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263731/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a ground-truth generator g: S → X mapping a product manifold of latent factors to an observation manifold, and a learned decoder ĝ: Z → X, and analyzes their Jacobians (first- and higher-order derivatives) at corresponding points. The output is a guarantee that ĝ is locally (and under topological conditions, globally) disentangled with respect to g, meaning the learned factors recover the true source subspaces up to block-wise invertible transformations and permutations. Unlike prior results that assume one-dimensional factors, the framework operates at the subspace level, accommodating multi-dimensional latent factors and partial disentanglement (L < K).

## Method

The paper introduces a family of mechanistic independence criteria—Type D (Hadamard orthogonality of Jacobian columns), Type M (mutual non-inclusion of column supports), Type S (strict sparsity gap in the ℓ₀-norm of the Jacobian under basis misalignment), and Type H_n (vanishing cross-derivative blocks of order n)—each paired with a corresponding irreducibility condition that prevents spurious internal splits of a factor. For each criterion, a local identifiability theorem is proved by starting from the local reconstruction identity ĝ = g ∘ v and showing that the independence constraints force the mixing matrix B = Dv to be block-diagonal, so each source factor maps to exactly one target factor. The criteria form a hierarchy (D ⇒ H₂ ⇒ H₃ ⇒ …, D ⇒ M, D ⇒ S, S ⇒ M in the sparsest basis), and the independent irreducible factors are characterized as connected components of graphs built from the Jacobian structure. Empirically, the paper tests whether compositional contrast (a sum of pairwise products of Jacobian column norms) serves as a practical surrogate loss for enforcing Type S independence in an autoencoder trained on synthetic data with controlled support overlap.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are five identifiability theorems (Theorems 1–5) establishing that local disentanglement is guaranteed under four mechanistic independence criteria (Type D, M, S, H_n) plus a global extension theorem. The single empirical experiment (Section 5) tests whether compositional contrast C_comp serves as a surrogate loss for Type S independence: for 0% support overlap (Type D regime), C_comp reliably enforces identifiability, but as overlap increases the likelihood of convergence to bad local minima grows, with no specific numerical SIS values reported in the main text. The theoretical results hold under minimal assumptions on the latent density and extend to nonlinear, non-invertible generators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing methods on standard datasets; instead, it positions its framework as a unification and generalization of prior identifiability results. Type D identifiability (Theorem 2) extends Brady et al. (2023) to partial disentanglement and non-invertible generators. Type M identifiability (Theorem 3) generalizes Zheng & Zhang (2023) to multi-dimensional factors without requiring statistical independence of sources. Type H_n identifiability (Theorem 5) subsumes both the additive decoder setting of Lachapelle et al. (2023) (as the n=2 special case) and the interaction asymmetry principle of Brady et al. (2024), while eliminating dependence on (n+1)-th derivatives. The framework also partially subsumes sparsity-based nonlinear ICA results (Zheng et al., 2022) for the parts not requiring statistical independence. IMA (Gresele et al., 2021) appears as a special instance within the broader taxonomy of mechanistic constraints. The key advantage over all prior work is invariance to reweightings of the latent density and applicability to multi-dimensional factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and generalizes several prior identifiability results for nonlinear ICA and object-centric disentanglement under a single organizing principle: mechanistic independence, which characterizes latent factors by how they act on observations through the generator's Jacobian rather than by their latent distribution. It subsumes Type D (disjoint supports, extending Brady et al. 2023), Type M (mutual non-inclusion, generalizing Zheng & Zhang 2023), Type S (sparsity gap), and Type H_n (cross-derivative vanishing, generalizing Brady et al. 2024 and Lachapelle et al. 2023), while positioning IMA's Jacobian-orthogonality constraint (Gresele et al. 2021) as one instance within a broader taxonomy. The key conceptual departure from the classical ICA/ISA tradition is that statistical independence of the latent factors is entirely unnecessary; identifiability follows from structural properties of the mixing function alone, making results invariant to arbitrary reweightings of the latent density.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a clear hierarchy of independence criteria (D ⊃ M, D ⊃ S ⊃ M, D → H₂ → H₃ → …) that lets practitioners trade off internal factor coherence against cross-factor interaction, and a graph-theoretic characterization identifying latent subspaces as connected components. However, practical applicability is currently limited: Type D and H₂ fail for common image phenomena (shadows, reflections, occlusions), Type S is intractable to optimize directly, and the compositional-contrast surrogate loss only works reliably for small support overlaps. Open questions include finding robust surrogate losses for Type S, extending the framework to handle the infeasibility of higher-order derivatives in practice, and combining mechanistic and stochastic independence to recover multi-dimensional factors under weaker structural assumptions.
