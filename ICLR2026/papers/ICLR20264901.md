## Problem

Existing LLM-based paper quality estimation methods face a fundamental trade-off: autoregressive approaches produce interpretable reviews but incur prohibitive inference latency, while faster direct score regression (NAIPv1) is undermined by translational inconsistencies in reviewer scores across domains and years. Additionally, reviewer confidence is systematically overlooked in prior formulations, and no unified mechanism exists to aggregate multiple noisy reviewer judgments into a stable quality signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264901/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a paper's title and abstract as text input and produces a single scalar quality score via a pointwise forward pass through a fine-tuned LLaMA-3 8B backbone (LoRA, 8-bit quantized) with an MLP head. At inference, each paper is scored independently in O(1) time, enabling linear-time ranking of C candidates. The score is calibrated within domain-year subgroups and can be thresholded for binary acceptance prediction or used directly for ranking (Spearman, NDCG).

## Method

NAIPv2 introduces the Review Tendency Signal (RTS), which models each reviewer score as a Gaussian observation of latent paper quality with variance inversely controlled by reviewer confidence, yielding a confidence-adjusted precision-weighted mean truncated to [0,1]. Training uses a Bradley-Terry pairwise objective where pairs are restricted to the same hierarchical cluster (derived from Qwen3-Embedding of titles/abstracts) and same publication year, thereby eliminating cross-domain and temporal score inconsistencies. A curriculum learning schedule progressively shifts sampling from easy pairs (large RTS gaps) to hard pairs (small gaps) to stabilize optimization. Despite pairwise training, the shared backbone naturally decouples into a single-branch pointwise regressor at inference, preserving O(C) complexity while achieving 78.2% AUC and 0.432 Spearman on ICLR 2025 test data.

## Results

On the NAIDv2 test set (1,029 ICLR 2025 submissions), NAIPv2 (8B) achieves state-of-the-art performance with 78.2% AUC, 0.432 Spearman ρ, 70.6% accuracy, and 0.609 F1, substantially outperforming the random baseline (52.7% AUC, 0.002 ρ) and approaching the information-leakage upper bound (89.4% AUC, 0.984 ρ). Inference runs at linear time O(C), over 1000× faster than autoregressive methods like DeepReview (~3 min/paper on RTX 3090). On unseen NeurIPS submissions (13,223 papers, 2021–2024), predicted scores increase monotonically from Rejected → Poster → Spotlight → Oral, confirming cross-venue generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAIPv2 outperforms all API-based baselines (ChatGPT pointwise/pairwise, AgentReview, AI Scientist) by a wide margin on every reported metric, with AUC improving from 0.654–0.655 to 0.782 and Spearman from 0.097–0.315 to 0.432. Against autoregressive methods, NAIPv2 surpasses CycleReviewer (8B/70B) on accuracy (0.706 vs. 0.678), F1 (0.609 vs. 0.559/0.574), and Spearman (0.432 vs. 0.279/0.267), while matching or exceeding DeepReview (14B) on accuracy (0.706 vs. 0.689) and Spearman (0.432 vs. 0.405), though DeepReview retains a slight F1 edge (0.623 vs. 0.609). Compared to its predecessor NAIPv1, NAIPv2 improves AUC by 17.7 points (0.782 vs. 0.605) and Spearman by 0.249 (0.432 vs. 0.183), demonstrating that debiased pairwise training with RTS is far more effective than direct score regression. On the DeepReview-13K benchmark, NAIPv2 (8B) achieves higher 2024 F1 (66.13 vs. 63.07) and pairwise accuracy (63.37 vs. 62.42) than DeepReview (14B), while matching its 2025 Spearman (40.82 vs. 40.47), confirming that the gains are not dataset-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAIPv2 extends the authors' own NAIPv1 framework (Zhao et al., 2025), which used a shared LLaMA-3 backbone with an MLP head for direct score regression, by replacing absolute-value supervision with Bradley-Terry pairwise learning restricted to domain-year groups. It departs from the dominant autoregressive LLM review paradigm (DeepReview, CycleReviewer, OpenReviewer) that generates full review text, instead producing a single scalar score via pointwise inference while retaining pairwise training for debiasing. The Review Tendency Signal (RTS) introduces a confidence-weighted Gaussian aggregation of reviewer scores, building on the Bradley-Terry/Thurstone tradition of comparative judgment but applying it to noisy, confidence-modulated peer-review observations rather than clean binary preferences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that debiased pairwise training can match autoregressive review models in quality-estimation accuracy while being over a thousand times faster at inference, making scalable paper ranking feasible for literature-intelligence and recommendation systems. The cross-venue generalization from ICLR to NeurIPS (monotonic score alignment with decision categories) is encouraging but limited by NeurIPS's asymmetric review-availability policy, leaving open whether the approach transfers to venues with different review cultures or to non-AI fields. A notable limitation is that the model relies solely on titles and abstracts; the finding that adding introductions, conclusions, or figure/table content degrades performance suggests the signal is inherently limited to what the abstract conveys, capping the ceiling for quality discrimination.
