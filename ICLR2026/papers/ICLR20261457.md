## Problem

Distributed training of foundation models with adaptive optimizers faces a communication bottleneck: Local SGD-style methods that sync only parameters lack convergence guarantees for adaptive optimizers, heuristic approaches that keep or reset optimizer states locally are unstable, and Local Adam—which does converge—synchronizes all states uniformly, tripling communication costs. The paper asks whether independently assigning synchronization frequencies to parameters and momenta can reduce communication while preserving convergence and fault-tolerance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes model parameters, per-coordinate-clipped stochastic gradients (clip bound ρ), and N optimizer states (e.g., first and second momenta for Adam/ADOPT, or a single momentum buffer for Muon), along with user-specified synchronization periods K_x, K_u, K_v. It outputs updated model parameters and optimizer states after T optimization steps. The synchronization schedule is the key structural element: parameters average every K_x steps via a FedAvg (or Nesterov) outer operator, while each optimizer state j averages independently every K_j steps, with K_u and K_v typically set as constant multiples (3×, 6×) of K_x.

## Method

DES-LOC exploits the observation that optimizer states with higher decay rates β (longer half-life τ_0.5 = ln(0.5)/ln(β)) change more slowly under gradient clipping, so they require less frequent synchronization. The algorithm assigns independent communication periods: parameters sync every K_x steps, first momentum every K_u steps, and second momentum every K_v steps, with the constraint K_x ≤ K_u, K_v. Theoretical analysis proves that parameter synchronization dominates the asymptotic O(1/√T) convergence rate, while momentum sync frequency primarily affects the achievable step size (larger p_u permits larger η_0), and high-probability bounds for Adam require finite second-momentum sync when β_2 < 1. The outer optimizer defaults to FedAvg but extends to Nesterov, and the inner optimizer is agnostic—validated with Adam, ADOPT, SGDM, and Muon. Empirically, setting K_u = 3K_x and K_v = 6K_x yields a 2× communication reduction over Local Adam and 170× over DDP at billion-scale, with 1.3–2.1× wall-clock speedups on 100 Gb/s links.

## Results

On a 1.7B-parameter language model trained for 40B tokens, DES-LOC achieves an average ICL score of 51.6 (ARC Challenge 31.8, ARC Easy 59.0, PIQA 70.7, HellaSwag 44.9), matching Local Adam (51.8) while communicating 2× fewer bytes and 170× fewer than DDP. Wall-clock speedups over DDP range from 1.3× (Kx=16) to 2.1× (Kx=256) on 100Gb/s links, scaling to >93 days saved on a 13B model at low frequency. Throughput at the 13B scale reaches 23.5 batches/sec with DES-LOC versus 11.1 for DDP at Kx=256.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDP, DES-LOC trades a modest 2.2-point ICL gap (51.6 vs 53.8 avg) for a 170× reduction in communication volume and 1.3–2.1× wall-clock speedups, making it the clear choice for bandwidth-constrained or cross-datacenter settings. Against Local Adam (the prior state-of-the-art), DES-LOC matches perplexity and ICL performance (51.6 vs 51.8) while halving communication by syncing momenta less frequently (Ku=3Kx, Kv=6Kx vs uniform K). Against FAVG+OPT (persistent local optimizer states, i.e., DiLoCo-style), DES-LOC outperforms by 0.9 points on average ICL (51.6 vs 50.7) and avoids the activation-norm growth and training instabilities that degrade FAVG+OPT's downstream performance. Against FAVG-OPT (reset optimizer states), DES-LOC consistently outperforms in both perplexity and ICL, as state resets cause repeated oscillations that prevent convergence. The paper also demonstrates that DES-LOC generalizes beyond Adam to Muon (1.5× fewer bytes) and to vision models (Flux), maintaining competitive performance across all inner optimizers tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

DES-LOC extends the infrequent-synchronization paradigm of Local SGD and FedAvg to adaptive optimizers, building directly on Local Adam (Cheng & Glasgow, 2025), which proved that periodic synchronization of all optimizer states yields convergence but triples communication relative to parameter-only sync. The key departure is decoupling synchronization frequencies: rather than syncing parameters and both momenta at the same period (Local Adam) or keeping momenta purely local (DiLoCo, FAVG+OPT) or resetting them (FAVG-OPT), DES-LOC assigns each state an independent period guided by its exponential half-life. The theoretical analysis extends convergence guarantees from uniform-sync Local Adam to this decoupled setting, showing parameter sync dominates the asymptotic rate while momentum sync frequency controls achievable step size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners training foundation models across bandwidth-limited links or multiple data centers, DES-LOC offers a drop-in replacement for Local Adam with half the communication and provable convergence, plus fault tolerance (new workers can join without reinitializing optimizer states). The practical speedups (1.3–2.1× over DDP, up to 9.4× at 10 Gbit/s) and the clear heuristic (set K_u, K_v as multiples of K_x proportional to β half-lives) make this immediately actionable. Open questions include whether layer-wise or adaptive synchronization frequencies can further reduce communication, whether the approach extends to compressed or sparse updates, and how it interacts with pipeline/tensor parallelism in multi-datacenter settings. The FAVG+OPT instability at billion scale (activation and parameter-norm growth) is a useful cautionary result suggesting that 'free' communication savings from never syncing optimizer states may come at a hidden quality cost.
