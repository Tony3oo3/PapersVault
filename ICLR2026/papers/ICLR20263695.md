## Problem

While RNNs are increasingly used to reconstruct nonlinear dynamical systems, no efficient algorithm exists for detecting the stable and unstable manifolds of fixed and cyclic points in discrete-time RNNs. Existing continuation methods are designed for smooth ODE systems and suffer from the curse of dimensionality, making them inapplicable to high-dimensional ReLU-based RNNs whose Jacobians are discontinuous across linear subregion boundaries. This gap limits the ability to characterize multistability, delineate basins of attraction, and establish the existence of chaos in trained RNNs.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes the trained PLRNN weight matrices (A, W, h or their shPLRNN/ALRNN variants), a saddle or cyclic point previously located by SCYFI, and a binary flag selecting stable (+1) or unstable (−1) manifold computation. The output is a collection of manifold segments, one per visited linear subregion, each parameterized by at least d+1 support points and the corresponding eigenvectors (or kernel-PCA basis for curved segments), which together define the global manifold as a piecewise-structured object. A key practical requirement is that the RNN map be invertible, enforced during training via a determinant-sign regularization term applied to a small subset of subregions.

## Method

The algorithm exploits the piecewise-linear structure of ReLU-based RNNs to construct manifolds semi-analytically: within each linear subregion, the local manifold is an affine subspace spanned by eigenvectors of the local Jacobian A+WD(p) with eigenvalues inside (stable) or outside (unstable) the unit circle, computable in closed form. To extend the manifold across subregion boundaries, the algorithm samples N_s points on the current segment, propagates them forward (unstable) or backward (stable) through the map, and fits new segments in the newly reached subregions using PCA for linear pieces or kernel-PCA (RBF kernel) for curved pieces arising from defective or complex eigenvalues. The backward step requires solving a self-consistency problem for the ReLU activation pattern, handled by a heuristic that iteratively flips bits in the diagonal matrix D. The procedure recurses across subregions for up to N_iter iterations, assembling the global manifold following the union-of-preimages/images definition. A fallback algorithm using HDBSCAN clustering handles cases where manifolds fold discontinuously across subregions.

## Results

The manifold reconstruction quality metric Δ_σ (measuring agreement between algorithmically detected manifolds and forward/backward iteration) achieves values of 0.97 (Duffing), 0.95 (decision-making), 0.78 (Lorenz-63), and 0.76 (cortical neuron), confirming accurate manifold detection across 2D to 25D systems. For the Lorenz-63 reconstruction, the shPLRNN's detected stable and unstable manifolds closely match those computed by numerical continuation of the original ODE system, validating that the trained network faithfully captures state-space geometry beyond the attractor itself. The invertibility regularization (Eq. 5) accelerates convergence to PE(20)≤0.5 by a significant margin on a damped nonlinear oscillator (p=0.023, Mann-Whitney U-test) while having negligible impact on chaotic systems like Lorenz-63 (p=0.122).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is the first algorithm for detecting stable and unstable manifolds in discrete-time ReLU-based RNNs, so no direct method-to-method comparison exists. Validation is instead performed against ground-truth references: in 2D toy examples, the algorithm's manifolds perfectly coincide with analytically computed line segments and with the classical (non-scalable) grid-sampling approach for basin boundaries. For the Lorenz-63 reconstruction, the shPLRNN's detected manifolds are compared against numerical continuation of the original continuous-time ODE system and show close agreement, confirming faithful state-space recovery. The invertibility regularization is compared against standard training: on a damped nonlinear oscillator, adding the regularization term significantly speeds convergence (p=0.023), while on Lorenz-63 the effect is negligible (p=0.122), suggesting the regularization is most beneficial for systems with intrinsic time-reversibility. The paper also notes that existing continuation methods (Krauskopf et al., 2007; Simpson, 2023) suffer from the curse of dimensionality and effectively work only for ≤5D systems, whereas the proposed algorithm scales polynomially (often linearly) with latent space dimensionality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SCYFI (Eisenmann et al., 2023), which provides polynomial-time localization of fixed and cyclic points in PLRNNs, by extending that framework to construct the stable and unstable manifolds associated with those points. It departs from traditional continuation methods (Krauskopf et al., 2007) designed for smooth ODE systems and from recent PL-map manifold algorithms (Simpson, 2023) that suffer from the curse of dimensionality, by exploiting the piecewise-linear structure of ReLU-based RNNs to enable exact, subregion-by-subregion analytic construction. The authors explicitly note that no prior method efficiently detects invariant manifolds in high-dimensional discrete-time RNNs, positioning this work as filling that gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm that scales to the dimensions of modern PLRNNs used for dynamical systems reconstruction, enabling practical delineation of basins of attraction (multistability) and detection of homoclinic/heteroclinic intersections (chaos) in trained networks. Key limitations include breakdown of the analytic construction for fractal manifold geometries in strongly chaotic regimes, worst-case exponential scaling with the number of linear subregions, and dependence on complete detection of all saddle points. The invertibility regularization, which improves training convergence for time-reversible systems, is a useful practical byproduct. Extensions to other PL systems (SLDS, TLNs) are discussed but remain non-trivial due to discontinuities or continuous-time formulations.
