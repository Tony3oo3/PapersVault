## Problem

Existing diffusion-based data augmentation methods for offline RL (e.g., GTA, SynthER) are computationally expensive to train and require multi-step denoising for generation, while also lacking explicit guidance toward high-value regions. This limits their practicality for real-world deployment and yields only marginal improvements in downstream offline policy performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260978/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples and a pretrained any-step dynamics model (from ADMPO). The Transformer policy takes only state sequences of length L as input (no action or return-to-go tokens), and the dynamics model predicts next states and rewards autoregressively. The output is a set of synthetic trajectories (state, action, reward sequences) that are randomly shuffled with the original dataset to form the augmented training set for standard offline RL algorithms.

## Method

TGCVG replaces the diffusion module in prior augmentation pipelines with a lightweight Transformer policy trained via Conservative Q-Learning (CQL). The policy uses a state-only input representation and is optimized with an ensemble of two Q-networks employing CQL's log-sum-exp regularization to penalize OOD actions, combined with an SAC-style entropy-regularized policy objective. During data generation, the policy interacts autoregressively with a pretrained dynamics model: a state window is sampled from the dataset, the policy predicts an action, the dynamics model predicts the next state and reward, and this process repeats to construct full synthetic trajectories. The key design insight is that CQL's value penalization constrains each generated (s, a, s') tuple to remain within the dataset distribution, limiting out-of-distribution risk to a single step per model interaction and preventing error accumulation across rollouts. This yields dynamically consistent, high-quality trajectories at a fraction of the computational cost of diffusion-based alternatives.

## Results

On the MuJoCo Gym domain (D4RL), TGCVG achieves average normalized scores of 90.50 (CQL), 89.30 (TD3BC), and 87.09 (IQL), consistently outperforming the strongest augmentation baseline GTA (85.27, 84.63, 86.11 respectively) and no-augmentation baselines (78.55, 76.92, 78.52). The largest gains appear on suboptimal datasets: on hopper-medium, TGCVG+TD3BC reaches 90.47 versus GTA's 69.57 and the no-augmentation baseline's 61.04. On Maze2D and AntMaze (IQL), TGCVG achieves an average of 64.97, surpassing GTA (59.17) and the no-augmentation baseline (52.04), with a particularly strong result on maze2d-large (103.97 vs. 76.56 for GTA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGCVG outperforms all three data-augmentation baselines (S4RL, SynthER, GTA) across every MuJoCo task and algorithm combination. Against S4RL (Gaussian noise), the improvement is substantial (e.g., 89.30 vs. 77.24 average with TD3BC), confirming that simple state perturbation is insufficient. Against SynthER (diffusion-based transition synthesis), TGCVG wins on most tasks, though SynthER occasionally matches or slightly exceeds TGCVG on individual walker2d sub-tasks; notably, SynthER collapses on AntMaze (36.99 avg vs. TGCVG's 64.97). Against GTA (return-guided diffusion), the gap is most pronounced on suboptimal datasets: TGCVG+TD3BC gains ~21 points on hopper-medium (90.47 vs. 69.57) and ~10 points on halfcheetah-medium (68.14 vs. 57.84). In the model-based comparison (Appendix B.5), TGCVG+IQL substantially outperforms ADMPO+IQL on halfcheetah-medium (68.61 vs. 21.90) and antmaze-large-diverse (57.20 vs. 0.00), demonstrating that decoupling data generation from the learning loop yields more transferable synthetic trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

TGCVG sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) conservative value-based offline RL (CQL, TD3BC, IQL), (2) Transformer-based sequence modeling for decision-making (Decision Transformer, QT), and (3) generative data augmentation for offline RL (SynthER, GTA). Its core departure from prior work is replacing the diffusion model in GTA with a Transformer-CQL policy that generates actions autoregressively, coupled with a pretrained dynamics model (from ADMPO) to synthesize next states and rewards. This design inherits the conservative value penalization from CQL to constrain generated trajectories within the dataset distribution, while leveraging the SAC-style entropy-regularized policy objective for exploration. The paper also builds on the RCSL paradigm but shows that return-to-go conditioning becomes unnecessary once Q-value regularization is in place, simplifying the input to pure state sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in offering a substantially cheaper alternative to diffusion-based trajectory augmentation (GTA, SynthER) while matching or exceeding their performance, making high-quality offline data augmentation more accessible for real-world deployment where inference cost matters. The finding that Transformer-TD3BC-generated data causes CQL to collapse—due to a fundamental mismatch between policy-constraint and value-penalization paradigms—provides useful diagnostic insight for practitioners combining different offline RL components. Limitations include inheriting CQL's weaknesses on inherently difficult tasks, and the method still requires a pretrained dynamics model, which introduces its own approximation error. Open questions include whether iterative or selective augmentation (e.g., targeting only high-value trajectory segments) could further improve efficiency, and whether the approach extends to high-dimensional observation spaces like images.
