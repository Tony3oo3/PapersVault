## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) frequently miss small details, confuse similar objects, and misread spatial relationships in cluttered scenes, leading to errors in fine-grained perceptual grounding. Existing test-time interventions either crop or mask the image (destroying global context) or manipulate latent features after the vision encoder has already lost critical spatial detail. The paper asks whether a query-conditioned, information-preserving spatial transformation applied before feature extraction can make the same frozen MLLM read small objects and subtle relationships more accurately.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (resized to 512×512) and a natural-language query as input. It produces a warped image of identical resolution and regular grid structure, which is then fed to the same frozen MLLM to yield a VQA answer. The warping is entirely query-conditioned: the same image will be warped differently for different questions, and all original pixel information is retained (no cropping or masking), only redistributed non-uniformly across the grid.

## Method

AttWarp is a plug-and-play, test-time self-correction loop that requires no weight or architecture changes. It first runs the MLLM on the original image to extract cross-modal attention maps from a specific deep decoder layer (layer 20 for LLaVA, layer 16 for Qwen), aggregates them over output tokens, heads, and layers into a 2D attention score matrix, and then computes 1D marginal attention profiles (PDFs) along the horizontal and vertical axes. These marginals are converted to cumulative distribution functions (CDFs), and their inverses define rectilinear warping functions that non-uniformly resample the image grid: high-attention regions are spatially expanded while low-attention regions are compressed, with bilinear sampling producing the final warped image. The rectilinear (axis-aligned) design is critical—it preserves the regular patch grid expected by the vision encoder and keeps the warped image on the same distributional manifold as the training data (validated via FID/KID and Mahalanobis distance in CLIP feature space). Two extensions are introduced: AttWarp-Chain iteratively re-extracts attention from the warped image and re-warp until a KL-divergence stopping criterion is met, and AttWarp-Distill replaces the attention-extraction pipeline with a compact CLIP+FiLM network trained to directly predict the marginal profiles in a single forward pass, achieving ~3× speedup over ViCrop.

## Results

AttWarp-Chain (the iterative variant) improves over the base MLLM by +11.0% on TextVQA, +3.9% on GQA, +4.7% on MMMU, +2.9% on POPE, and +9.5% on DocVQA with LLaVA-1.5-7B, and by +4.9%, +2.4%, +3.7%, +1.9%, and +8.0% respectively with Qwen2.5-VL. The single-pass AttWarp variant already outperforms all four test-time baselines on every benchmark, e.g., achieving 58.1% on TextVQA (vs. 56.3% for the strongest baseline ViCrop) and 25.5% on DocVQA (vs. 22.5% for ViCrop) with LLaVA. On visual-centric benchmarks (MMVP, BLINK, RealWorldQA, MIA-Bench), AttWarp-Chain adds +2.7% to +3.8% over the base LLaVA. AttWarp-Distill, the learned single-pass predictor, retains most gains while running ~3× faster than ViCrop (8.7 vs. 24.2 TFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AttWarp outperforms all four test-time baselines on every benchmark and both MLLM backbones. The closest competitor is ViCrop (attention-guided cropping with dual-image input), which AttWarp surpasses by +1.8% on TextVQA, +2.8% on GQA, +3.2% on MMMU, +0.5% on POPE, and +3.0% on DocVQA with LLaVA; with Qwen the margins are +0.9%, +3.4%, +3.3%, +0.7%, and +1.6% respectively. FGVP (masking/blur) and SoM (set-of-mark) perform substantially worse, often degrading below the base MLLM (e.g., SoM drops TextVQA from 49.3% to 18.8% on LLaVA), because they remove or obscure global context. API Prompting (attention heatmap overlay) is the weakest of the 'gentle' baselines, matching or barely exceeding the base model on most tasks. AttWarp's consistent advantage over ViCrop is notable because ViCrop uses two image inputs (original + crop), whereas AttWarp uses a single warped image, and AttWarp-Distill further reduces compute to ~0.4× that of ViCrop while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

AttWarp builds on the classical line of saliency-aware image warping (Recasens et al., 2018) and image retargeting, but repurposes the warping mechanism for a fundamentally different goal: using an MLLM's own cross-modal attention as the saliency signal to redistribute pixel density before feature extraction. It departs from prior test-time visual interventions for MLLMs (FGVP masking, SoM segmentation markers, API attention overlays, ViCrop cropping) by preserving all original image information through a distribution-preserving rectilinear resampling rather than discarding or occluding regions. Conceptually, the key departure from standard attention usage is treating attention not as a mechanism to reweight latent features but as a spatial transformation guide applied to the input image itself, intervening before the vision encoder rather than after.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature (no fine-tuning, no architecture changes, single CDF forward pass) makes this a low-friction enhancement that practitioners can adopt immediately across diverse MLLM backbones, and the consistent gains across nine benchmarks and four architectures strengthen the case for input-level, information-preserving transformations as a complementary axis to internal attention refinement. The distribution-preservation validation (FID/KID showing AttWarp stays on the training manifold while non-rectilinear warping drifts) addresses a critical practical concern that limits other pixel-space interventions. Open questions include correcting attention misalignment (where both base and warped models fail identically), whether the approach extends to video or 3D inputs, and whether the classifier-gated variant (AttWarp†) can be made more principled than its current heuristic training set construction.
