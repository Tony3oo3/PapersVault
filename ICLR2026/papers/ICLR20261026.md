## Problem

Self-Interpretable GNNs (SI-GNNs) are trained with cross-entropy plus conciseness regularization but evaluated by faithfulness, creating a fundamental objective mismatch. The paper asks whether faithfulness can be explicitly optimized during training and whether doing so genuinely improves explanation quality, revealing that faithfulness is intrinsically tied to explanation self-consistency and that self-inconsistency concentrates on unimportant features, linking it to the redundancy problem identified in prior work.

## Input / Output

The method operates on molecular and synthetic graphs (BA-2MOTIFS, 3MR, BENZENE, MUTAGENICITY) with node features and edge structures. During SC fine-tuning, the first-pass explanation subgraph G_s^(1) (obtained via Gumbel-Sigmoid sampling from edge importance scores) is fed back into the frozen GNN encoder to produce a second-pass explanation G_s^(2); the SC loss is the L1 difference between the two. At inference, the model outputs a class label, continuous edge importance scores in [0,1], and a binary explanation subgraph, with the encoder frozen to prevent representation drift.

## Method

The proposed Self-Consistency (SC) training is a two-stage, model-agnostic fine-tuning strategy: first, the SI-GNN is pretrained with its standard objective and the GNN encoder is frozen; second, the explainer and classifier are fine-tuned with an additional SC loss L_SC = |G_s^(1) − G_s^(2)| that enforces agreement between the explanation of the full graph and the explanation of the explanation itself. Theoretically, SC drives edge scores toward near-fixed levels of the second-pass mapping T(α) = σ(g(α)), while conciseness regularization (CR) governs which levels are selected—jointly pushing unimportant edges toward low, stable scores and preserving high scores for important edges. The paper proves that SC preserves the optimal explanatory subset while reshaping the optimization landscape to discourage redundant solutions, and derives an upper bound on the expected SC loss that guides the choice of the single new hyperparameter η (set to 1 in all experiments). The approach requires no architecture modification and is compatible with attention-based, causal-based, size-constrained, and MI-constrained SI-GNNs.

## Results

On the four benchmark datasets, SC fine-tuning consistently improves all four explanation-quality metrics. For SMGNN on MUTAGENICITY, SHD drops from 12.65% to 2.51% and FID from 1.72% to 0.61%, while AUC rises from 98.12% to 98.30% and ACC from 89.53% to 91.18%. For GSAT on MUTAGENICITY, SHD drops from 10.08% to 2.38% and FID from 1.11% to 0.17%. Combining SC with Explanation Ensemble (SC+EE) yields the best results overall, e.g., SMGNN+SC+EE achieves SHD 1.52% and AUC 99.90% on BA-2MOTIFS, and GSAT+SC+EE achieves SHD 1.06% and AUC 99.41% on MUTAGENICITY.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SMGNN (Azzolin et al., 2025b), SC reduces SHD by 66–80% (e.g., 10.44→3.48 on BA-2MOTIFS, 12.65→2.51 on MUTAGENICITY) and FID by 65–100%, while improving AUC and ACC across all four datasets. Against GSAT (Miao et al., 2022), SC similarly cuts SHD by 40–76% (e.g., 10.08→2.38 on MUTAGENICITY) and FID by 85–100%. Against the post-hoc Explanation Ensemble (EE) from Tai et al. (2025), SC matches or exceeds EE on most metrics while being ~5× faster at inference (single model vs. 5-model ensemble); combining SC+EE yields further gains, confirming they are complementary. For non-CR-based models GAT (Velickovic et al., 2018) and CAL (Sui et al., 2022), SC alone degrades explanation quality (e.g., GAT+SC AUC drops from 99.31% to 99.14% on BA-2MOTIFS, and SHD worsens from 10.41% to 16.63%), but adding conciseness regularization (CR) alongside SC recovers and surpasses the baseline in 28 of 32 cases (e.g., GAT+CR+SC achieves SHD 3.48% vs. GAT's 10.41% on BA-2MOTIFS). Overall, SC is most effective when paired with a moderate conciseness regularizer, and its benefit is consistent across all four SI-GNN families tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tai et al. (2025, ICML), which identified explanation redundancy as the root cause of cross-run inconsistency in SI-GNNs and proposed Explanation Ensemble (EE) as a post-hoc remedy. Here, the authors show that the same redundancy manifests as *self*-inconsistency within a single model (first-pass vs. second-pass explanations), and propose a self-consistency (SC) alignment loss as a training-time fix that is model-agnostic and complementary to EE. The work also engages with Azzolin et al. (2025a), who questioned whether faithfulness scores reliably track explanation quality, motivating the paper's broader multi-metric evaluation (consistency, accuracy, faithfulness, informativeness) rather than faithfulness alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SC loss is a single, architecture-agnostic term that consistently improves explanation quality across four SI-GNN families and four benchmarks, offering a practical drop-in improvement for the GNN explainability community. However, the gains are moderate, the method requires adequate conciseness regularization to be effective (SC alone can degrade GAT/CAL), and evaluation is confined to small molecular and synthetic graphs. Open questions include whether SC generalizes to larger-scale or non-molecular graph tasks, whether the two-stage frozen-encoder protocol is necessary in all settings, and how the approach interacts with node-level (rather than edge-level) explanations.
