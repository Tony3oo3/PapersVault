## Problem

Data-aware dimension reduction methods like PCA/SVD are widely used for nearest neighbor search and empirically outperform random projection methods (LSH, JL), yet they lack rigorous correctness guarantees in noisy high-dimensional settings. Prior theoretical work (Abdullah et al., 2014) could only guarantee NN recovery when noise was small enough that the noisy NN equaled the true NN, and required the noise level σ to be an inverse polynomial in the ambient dimension d. This paper asks whether SVD can provably recover the true NN even when the noisy NN has changed, with noise tolerance depending only on the intrinsic subspace dimension k rather than the ambient dimension d.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a noisy matrix A ∈ R^{d×(n+1)} (first n columns are perturbed data points, last column is the perturbed query) and the intrinsic dimension k, where all uncorrupted points lie in an unknown k-dimensional subspace of R^d (k ≪ d) and noise is i.i.d. N(0, σ²) per coordinate. It outputs the index of a (1+ε)-approximate nearest neighbor for the uncorrupted data, under the assumption that the second-nearest neighbor is at least (1+ε) farther than the nearest neighbor in the latent space. The algorithm requires knowing k a priori and assumes the data matrix B has rank k with a non-vanishing k-th singular value s_k(B).

## Method

The algorithm splits the n data points into two halves and computes two SVDs: the top-k singular subspace U^(1) from the first half (plus query) and U^(2) from the second half (plus query). To estimate distances for the first half, it projects those points onto U^(2) (the subspace learned from the *other* half, ensuring stochastic independence), and vice versa; the nearest neighbor is the point with minimum projected distance to the query. The key insight is that projecting onto the SVD subspace of the independent half effectively extracts the latent k-dimensional structure, so that ‖P_{U^(2)} A^(1) x_j‖² approximates ‖B^(1) x_j‖² + 2kσ² up to a (1±ε/3) multiplicative factor. The proof combines Wedin's theorem (bounding the angle between the true subspace V and the empirical subspace U^(2) via the spectral gap s_k(B)), concentration of chi-squared distributions for the projected noise norm, and Gaussian tail bounds for cross terms. This yields a noise tolerance of σ = O(k^{-1/4}), which is shown to be information-theoretically optimal via a KL-divergence lower bound, and is strictly larger than the regime where the noisy NN equals the true NN (σ ≪ 1/√k).

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is Theorem 1.1, which proves that SVD-based denoising recovers a (1+ε)-approximate nearest neighbor with probability ≥ 1−1/n when the per-coordinate noise σ ≤ O(1/k^{1/4}), where k is the intrinsic subspace dimension. This is matched by an information-theoretic lower bound (Lemma 3.1) showing that for σ ≫ 1/k^{1/4}, the true NN is not identifiable from the noisy data. Empirically, on GloVe (d=200) and MNIST (d=784) with n=3000, k=30, ε=0.05, the SVD algorithm maintains high success probability at noise levels where the naive algorithm already fails, with the performance gap widening as the ambient dimension d increases. A second experiment on synthetic data confirms the predicted linear dependence of the noise threshold on s_k(B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its SVD-based algorithm against a naive baseline that simply selects the nearest neighbor in the noisy (unprojected) data by minimizing ‖Ax_j‖. Across both GloVe and MNIST, the SVD algorithm consistently outperforms the naive method, with a significantly higher failure threshold (the σ at which success probability drops). The gap is more pronounced for MNIST (d=784) than GloVe (d=200), consistent with the theory that the naive method's noise tolerance depends on the ambient dimension d while the SVD method depends only on the intrinsic dimension k. The authors note they could not implement the prior spectral method of Abdullah et al. (2014) as a baseline due to its infeasible time and space complexity, but theoretically their result improves the tolerable noise from σ = O(1/√k · d^{1/4}) to σ = O(1/k^{1/4}), a substantial relaxation when d ≫ k. The SVD approach also succeeds in the regime σ ≫ 1/√k where the noisy NN differs from the true NN, a setting where Abdullah et al.'s guarantees do not apply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-random NNS model of Abdullah et al. (2014), which proved that nearest neighbors are preserved under noise only when σ is an inverse polynomial in the ambient dimension d. The key departure is replacing the iterated PCA approach with a simple two-SVD algorithm that tolerates σ = O(1/k^{1/4}), depending only on the intrinsic subspace dimension k rather than d. The analysis draws on classical perturbation results (Wedin's Theorem, Davis-Kahan sin Θ) and random matrix concentration bounds, while the lower bound leverages KL divergence between Gaussian distributions. The paper also positions its contribution against the Johnson-Lindenstrauss/LSH paradigm, arguing that data-aware spectral projections can provably outperform oblivious random projections in the low-intrinsic-dimension regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretical justification for why PCA/SVD-based dimensionality reduction outperforms random projections for NNS in practice, bridging a long-standing gap between empirical success and provable guarantees. The matching information-theoretic lower bound (σ ≫ 1/k^{1/4} makes NN recovery impossible) establishes that the SVD threshold is optimal, giving practitioners a clear noise regime map. Open questions include whether the 50-50 data split is truly necessary, whether the s_k(B) dependence can be removed, and whether the results extend to non-Gaussian noise or adversarial perturbations. The requirement to know k a priori and the well-conditioning assumption limit immediate applicability to real-world data with unknown or decaying spectra.
