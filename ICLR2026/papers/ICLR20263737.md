## Problem

Existing Chinese calligraphy generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: isolated-character synthesis produces accurate glyphs but ignores column-level aesthetics such as inter-character ligatures, spatial rhythm, and layout composition, while general-purpose image generation and VLM-based systems attempt full-column synthesis but frequently render characters with incorrect forms or inconsistent styles. No prior framework unifies column-level generation and recognition in a single model with shared representations that mutually constrain each other.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three 128×640 images (a standard-font content rendering of five vertically stacked characters, a calligraphy style reference, and a rasterized bounding-box map) plus a text prompt specifying calligrapher and script type; all three images are patchified and concatenated into a single token sequence for the MMDiT. For generation, it outputs a calligraphy image and bounding-box map; for recognition, it recovers character identity via zero-shot cosine-similarity retrieval in the VAE latent space against a pre-built library of rendered reference glyphs, requiring no auxiliary OCR head.

## Method

UniCalli fine-tunes the FLUX MMDiT backbone with bidirectional attention over the full canvas, replacing autoregressive rollouts with globally coherent layout planning. It employs asymmetric flow-matching noising on two coupled latents: the content latent and the calligraphy+box-map latent, each with an independent timestep—setting the content timestep to zero yields generation, while setting the image timestep to zero yields recognition, with a shared dual-objective loss (λ=0.02) balancing the two. A Duplicate RoPE with Modulated Embedding strategy replicates the calligraphy image's rotary position encoding to the content and box-map latents, adding distinct learnable modulation vectors so all three modalities share a 2D spatial coordinate system while retaining modality identity. A conditional dropout mechanism (p_drop=0.05) stochastically replaces the content condition with pure noise to disentangle glyph structure from stylistic overfitting, and the same mechanism naturally absorbs unlabeled data as unconditional training samples. Joint training on labeled, unlabeled, and synthetic (TTF-rendered) data broadens both the character repertoire and stylistic coverage.

## Results

On reference-based calligraphy synthesis, UniCalli achieves the best scores across all four metrics: L1=0.152, SSIM=0.602, LPIPS=0.313, and FID=37.69, outperforming the closest competitor Doubao (L1=0.229, FID=47.26) by a wide margin. In the user study (20 calligraphy enthusiasts, 5-point Likert scale), UniCalli scores 4.267 on Style Fidelity, 4.827 on Glyph Accuracy, 4.520 on Naturalness, and 4.560 on Overall Preference, leading all baselines. On character-level recognition across five script styles, UniCalli attains 0.540 total accuracy, the highest among all compared systems including specialized recognizers like CalliReader (0.533) and general VLMs like Ernie-4.5 (0.534).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniCalli decisively outperforms all four generation baselines on every quantitative metric. FontDiffuser (one-shot font generation) is the weakest, with FID 80.25 and LPIPS 0.527, reflecting its inability to handle column-level composition. Among VLM-based systems, Doubao is the strongest competitor (FID 47.26, LPIPS 0.456) but still trails UniCalli by ~20% on FID and ~31% on LPIPS; ChatGPT-5 and Ernie-4.5 are further behind. In the user study, UniCalli's Style Fidelity score of 4.267 nearly doubles that of the next-best method (ChatGPT-5 at 2.987), while Glyph Accuracy is competitive with FontDiffuser (4.827 vs. 4.950) and Doubao (4.800). On recognition, UniCalli (0.540 total) edges out CalliReader (0.533) and Ernie-4.5 (0.534), and substantially surpasses GPT-4o (0.380) and GOT-OCR2.0 (0.266); however, on the out-of-distribution CalliBench benchmark, UniCalli (F1=0.498) is outperformed by GOT-OCR2.0 (F1=0.593) and Qwen-2.5-VL-7B (F1=0.534), though it still beats GPT-4o (F1=0.400) and PP-OCRv5 (F1=0.205). On Oracle Bone Script, UniCalli achieves 67% completely-correct generation but lags OracleNet on recognition (62.5% vs. 73.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniCalli builds on the MMDiT bidirectional-attention architecture (Blattmann et al., 2023) and fine-tunes the FLUX backbone (Labs et al., 2025), adapting the 'any-to-any' asymmetric noising paradigm from Unidiffuser/Uni-renderer to a calligraphy-specific setting. It departs from prior calligraphy generation work (FontDiffuser, CalliPaint, Moyun, CalliffusionV2), which either synthesizes isolated characters or rolls out columns autoregressively, by planning the entire column layout jointly in a single diffusion pass. It also departs from task-specialized recognizers (OracleNet, CalliReader) by integrating recognition into the same model via a zero-shot latent-space retrieval mechanism rather than a dedicated OCR head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real gap: no prior system produces complete, stylistically coherent calligraphic columns with correct glyph structure, and the 8,000+ work dataset (4,000+ annotated) is a valuable resource for the community. The extension to Oracle bone script and Egyptian hieroglyphs suggests a generalizable framework for digitizing ancient writing systems, though the Oracle bone recognition accuracy (62.5%) still lags the specialized OracleNet (73.9%). Open questions include whether the unified generation-recognition coupling provides genuine mutual benefit beyond standard multi-task learning, how the approach scales to longer compositions beyond five-character crops, and whether the recognition advantage on the in-domain test set (acknowledged by the authors as potentially distribution-matched) holds under stricter cross-domain evaluation.
