## Problem

Relevance-optimized recommendation models produce homogeneous top-k outputs that reinforce filter bubbles, yet existing diversification methods are either post-hoc re-ranking (which degrades accuracy), model-specific (requiring architecture changes), or lack a unified differentiable framework. The paper addresses the absence of a model-agnostic, gradient-based mechanism that can optimize top-k diversity directly within standard training pipelines without post-processing or architectural modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263955/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a partially-observed user-item relevance matrix R (ratings or interaction scores), a pre-computed m×m item affinity matrix S (Jaccard similarity over genre/category attributes), and user/item feature vectors for the underlying model (MF embeddings or NCF MLP inputs). It outputs a top-k ranked list per user where the k highest-scoring items maximize average pairwise dissimilarity under S, while preserving relevance as measured by MSE or BPR loss. The diversity objective operates on the soft top-k indicator derived from differentiable ranking, so the output is a continuous ranking vector from which hard top-k selections are extracted at inference time.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the non-differentiable top-k operator with a soft-ranking approximation: predicted scores are projected onto the permutahedron via an entropy-regularized optimization (Blondel et al., 2020), yielding a differentiable top-k indicator that enables end-to-end gradient flow through the diversity reward objective (DDRO). On top of this, the paper defines a joint loss L_JOINT = β·L_MSE + (1−β)·(−L_DDRO) and proposes two complementary strategies: (i) Direct Diversity Tuning (DDT), which optimizes the joint loss directly with an adaptive β* computed per-step via the MGDA closed-form to guarantee simultaneous descent on both objectives and convergence to Pareto-stationary points; and (ii) Meta-Diversity Reweighting (MDR), a bilevel meta-learning scheme where the joint loss serves only as a meta-objective to compute per-sample importance weights (via one-step inner update and gradient rectification), while the model itself is updated with a standard weighted relevance loss. Both strategies are model-agnostic, applicable to MF and NCF, and support either from-scratch training or efficient fine-tuning of pre-trained relevance models.

## Results

On MovieLens, DDT achieves a perfect diversity score of 1.00 and relevance of 1.00, while MDR reaches 0.97/0.99. On KuaiRec, DDT attains 0.98 diversity with 0.95 relevance, substantially outperforming all baselines on the relevance-diversity trade-off. On Yahoo-R2 (the hardest dataset with initial diversity of only 0.09), DDT lifts diversity to 0.98 while retaining 0.85 relevance, compared to DPP's 1.00 diversity but only 0.58 relevance. Diversity gains extend beyond the optimized top-k range, with positive out-of-objective gains (k+1 to 2k) observed on 4 of 5 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDT outperforms all five baselines on the joint relevance-diversity trade-off across nearly all datasets. Against the relevance-only NMF baseline, DDT improves diversity by 0.38 on MovieLens (0.62→1.00) and 0.89 on Yahoo-R2 (0.09→0.98) while maintaining or improving relevance. Compared to post-hoc methods, DDT matches or exceeds MMR and DUM in diversity while preserving far higher relevance (e.g., KuaiRec: DDT 0.95 vs. DUM 0.42 relevance). DPP achieves the highest raw diversity on Yahoo-R2 (1.00) and KuaiRec (1.00) but at a severe relevance cost (0.58 and 0.75 respectively), whereas DDT reaches 0.98/0.85 and 0.98/0.95. DGRec suffers the worst relevance degradation (KuaiRec: 0.18; MovieLens: 0.47), making it the weakest baseline overall. MDR is competitive with DDT, particularly on Netflix (0.98/0.93) and MovieLens (0.97/0.99), and is especially effective with the simpler NMF model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three existing threads—differentiable ranking via permutahedron projection (Blondel et al., 2020), multi-gradient descent for multi-objective balancing (Désidéri, 2012), and meta-learning-based sample reweighting (Ren et al., 2018)—into a single model-agnostic framework for diverse top-k recommendation. It departs from post-hoc re-ranking methods (MMR, DPP, DUM) that degrade accuracy, and from architecture-specific learning-based diversifiers (DGRec, Hurley) that require model modifications, by embedding a differentiable diversity objective directly into standard gradient-based training without altering model structure. The adaptive β derived from MGDA and the meta-reweighting strategy (MDR) are novel combinations applied specifically to the diversity-relevance trade-off, whereas prior MGDA applications targeted fairness or revenue objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, drop-in mechanism for any relevance-trained recommender to gain diversity with negligible accuracy loss, and the finding that only a few fine-tuning gradient steps suffice to encode diversity at scale has direct deployment implications. Diversity gains extending beyond the explicitly optimized top-k range (out-of-objective gains) suggest the learned updates reshape the full ranking, not just the target window. Key limitations include reliance on categorical (Jaccard) similarity rather than learned semantic embeddings, the risk that adaptive β converges to a compromised rather than globally optimal stationary point, and the absence of user studies validating perceived diversity. Future directions include more expressive diversity metrics, counterfactual data augmentation, and multi-objective formulations that avoid local optima.
