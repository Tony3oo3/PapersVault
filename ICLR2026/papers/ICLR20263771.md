## Problem

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) suffer from spectral bias, where the network preferentially learns low-frequency components and fails to capture high-frequency or multi-scale PDE solutions. Existing mitigations—weight balancing, adaptive resampling, and curriculum architectures—remain embedded in a single-level nonconvex optimization that couples feature learning with coefficient fitting, limiting both robustness and theoretical guarantees. This paper asks whether decoupling these two roles into a structured bi-level scheme can systematically recover high-frequency dynamics while preserving convergence properties.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatial coordinates x ∈ Ω, a linear (or nonlinear) PDE operator 𝔽 with source term f, and boundary/initial conditions (𝔅, g) sampled via Monte Carlo or Latin hypercube strategies. The output is an approximate solution u(x) = ψ_D(x)ᵀθ, where ψ_D is a vector of 2D random Fourier features applied to the hidden-layer activations h_ω(x) ∈ ℝ^p, and θ ∈ ℝ^{2D} are regression coefficients. A key structural detail is that the number of basis functions D is decoupled from the network width p, allowing arbitrarily many basis vectors to be generated from a fixed-width hidden layer without additional trainable parameters.

## Method

IFeF-PINN introduces a two-stage iterative training algorithm that decouples basis generation (upper-level) from coefficient regression (lower-level). In the upper level, a standard feed-forward network h_ω is trained to produce a nominal latent basis; in the lower level, a random Fourier feature mapping γ_D is applied to h_ω to form an extended basis ψ_D, and the coefficients θ are obtained by solving a closed-form convex QP (for linear PDEs) or a local minimization (for nonlinear PDEs). The iteration alternates: given ω_k, solve for θ_{k+1} = −Q(ω_k)⁻¹c(ω_k), then take one gradient-descent step on ω using the hypergradient of the upper-level loss evaluated at θ_{k+1}. The paper proves convergence to a stationary point via Lipschitz continuity of the solution map θ*(ω), and establishes a universal approximation guarantee showing the RFF-augmented hypothesis class strictly contains the standard linear-readout class as D → ∞.

## Results

On high-frequency PDEs where standard PINNs fail, IFeF-PINN achieves relative L²-errors of 0.0092 (Helmholtz, a₁=a₂=100), 0.0025 (Convection, β=200), and 0.0009 (multi-scale Convection-Diffusion), while all baselines either fail to converge or produce errors >0.05. On low-frequency benchmarks, IFeF-PD reaches 3.5×10⁻⁵ on the 2D Helmholtz equation and IFeF reaches 4.3×10⁻⁵ on the 1D convection equation, both the best among all tested methods. The method also handles the nonlinear viscous Burgers' equation with the lowest median error across all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IFeF-PINN decisively outperforms all five baselines on every high-frequency benchmark. Against Vanilla PINN (Raissi et al., 2017), IFeF-PD reduces the relative L²-error on high-frequency convection from 0.9024 to 0.0025 (~360× improvement) and on multi-scale convection-diffusion from 0.0501 to 0.0010 (~50× improvement). NTK (Wang et al., 2022) and PINNsformer (Zhao et al., 2024) both fail to converge on the high-frequency Helmholtz equation and produce errors >0.86 on high-frequency convection, whereas IFeF-PD achieves 0.0092 and 0.0025 respectively. PIG (Kang et al., 2025) is the only baseline that converges on high-frequency Helmholtz but still yields an error of 1.6884, roughly 183× worse than IFeF-PD's 0.0092. Multiple Fourier Features (Wang et al., 2021b), compared in the appendix, is also outperformed by IFeF-PINN, confirming that applying RFF to hidden-layer features (rather than raw inputs) with iterative two-stage training is the critical design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

IFeF-PINN extends the PINN framework (Raissi et al., 2017) by decoupling the traditionally monolithic training into a bilevel structure: a neural network generates a latent basis (upper level) while a linear regression fits coefficients over random Fourier feature expansions of that basis (lower level). This departs from prior Fourier-feature PINNs (Wang et al., 2021b; Tancik et al., 2020), which apply RFF to the input coordinates, by instead applying RFF to hidden-layer activations, thereby enriching the latent feature space rather than the input space. The bilevel formulation draws on classical bilevel optimization (Bard, 1991) and echoes the fixed-random-weights philosophy of extreme learning machines, but is tailored to PDE residual losses with convergence guarantees that prior single-level PINN training strategies (NTK, weight balancing, resampling) do not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first convergence guarantee (global optimality for linear PDEs) within the PINN training paradigm, which is a meaningful theoretical advance over the heuristic loss-weighting and resampling strategies that dominate the field. The ablation cleanly isolating RFF as the critical component—removing it causes complete non-convergence on high-frequency problems even with the iterative scheme—gives practitioners a clear diagnostic for spectral-bias failure. However, the nonlinear PDE case loses the closed-form lower-level solve and reverts to iterative gradient descent, and the method's memory footprint grows linearly with the number of Fourier features D, limiting scalability to very high-dimensional or very high-frequency settings. Open questions include whether the bilevel structure can be made robust for strongly nonlinear PDEs and whether the approach extends to 3D or higher-dimensional domains without prohibitive cost.
