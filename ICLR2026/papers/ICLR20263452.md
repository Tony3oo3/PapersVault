## Problem

Reasoning-based IQA models trained with visual reinforcement learning (e.g., Q-Insight) achieve markedly superior cross-domain generalization over SFT-based methods, yet the mechanism behind this generalization remains unexplained, and their multi-step autoregressive reasoning incurs inference latency and memory costs orders of magnitude higher than earlier approaches, restricting real-time and on-device deployment. This paper asks two questions: what exactly makes reasoning-based IQA models generalize, and is the reasoning process itself necessary to achieve that generalization?

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (evaluated at 512×384) and outputs a continuous no-reference quality score in [1, 5]. At inference, RALI requires only a CLIP vision encoder and a fixed set of 250 basis vectors with associated scores—no LLM and no autoregressive token generation—so scoring reduces to one forward pass followed by cosine-similarity lookups and a weighted sum, consuming less than 5% of Q-Insight's inference time and memory.

## Method

The paper first shows empirically that GRPO-based RL training shifts an MLLM's scoring dependency from >1000 visual tokens to <100 quality-reasoning text tokens, and that this text representation is both compact and domain-bridging (mitigating inter-dataset distribution gaps), which explains the observed generalization. Building on this insight, RALI replicates the image-to-text quality mapping without any reasoning process in three steps: (1) contrastive alignment of a CLIP vision encoder with quality reasoning texts generated by Q-Insight (text encoder frozen, image encoder trained), (2) feature compression via PCA (768→512) and score-bucketed k-means to distill 250 basis vectors each carrying a representative score, and (3) score prediction by computing softmax-normalized cosine similarities between the projected image embedding and the basis vectors, then taking the weighted sum of their scores. A complementary RACT framework leverages the same reasoning-text alignment to perform cross-domain SFT on the visual encoder alone (using only one dataset's numeric scores), achieving SOTA out-of-domain performance when co-training on multiple IQA datasets.

## Results

On the single-domain score regression benchmark (trained on KonIQ, tested on 7 datasets), RALI achieves an average PLCC of 0.798 and SRCC of 0.779, closely matching Q-Insight (0.806/0.783) while using only ~4% of its parameters (0.3B vs 7B). RALI surpasses the best non-MLLM method CLIP-IQA+ by 0.056 PLCC and 0.059 SRCC on average. In the cross-domain setting (trained on KonIQ+SPAQ+KADID+PIPAL), RACT achieves the best out-of-domain average PLCC of 0.858, outperforming Q-Insight (0.796) and VisualQuality-R1 (0.831). At inference, RALI consumes only 14.7% of Q-Insight's GPU memory and 3.4% of its inference time at batch size 16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RALI (0.3B params) achieves an average PLCC of 0.798, narrowly trailing Q-Insight (7B) at 0.806 while using only ~4% of its parameters and 3.4% of its inference time. Against the strongest non-MLLM baseline CLIP-IQA+, RALI wins by 0.056 PLCC and 0.059 SRCC on average, and also outperforms MUSIQ (0.726), ManIQA (0.720), and LIQE (0.761) by substantial margins. Among MLLM-based methods, RALI surpasses Q-Align (0.705), C2Score (0.706), DeQA (0.786), and VisualQuality-R1 (0.768) on average PLCC. On individual datasets, RALI leads Q-Insight on KonIQ (0.939 vs 0.933), PIPAL (0.527 vs 0.486), and LiveW (0.896 vs 0.893), but trails on KADID (0.723 vs 0.742), AGIQA (0.779 vs 0.811), and CSIQ (0.828 vs 0.870). In the cross-domain setting, RACT achieves the best out-of-domain average (0.858 PLCC), beating Q-Insight (0.796), VisualQuality-R1 (0.831), Q-Align (0.817), and DeQA (0.788) by 0.027–0.070 PLCC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two pillars: CLIP's contrastive image-text alignment (Radford et al., 2021) and Q-Insight's RL-trained reasoning-based IQA model (Li et al., 2025). It departs from prior CLIP-based IQA methods (e.g., CLIP-IQA) by aligning images specifically with quality reasoning text generated by an RL-trained MLLM rather than generic descriptive text, and by constructing a structured score space via PCA and bucketed k-means. Conceptually, it reframes the role of reasoning in RL-based IQA not as a cognitive process but as a visual-to-text compression mechanism, positioning contrastive learning as a sufficient alternative to multistep LLM reasoning for achieving the same representational mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that RL-based IQA generalization stems from compressing ~1000 visual tokens into <100 quality-relevant text tokens (with 95% of score-generation attention on reasoning text) offers a transferable lens for understanding why reasoning helps in other vision-language tasks. Practically, RALI's 95%+ reduction in inference time and memory while matching Q-Insight's accuracy makes high-quality IQA feasible in real-time, mobile, and online RLHF reward-model settings. Limitations include dependence on the CLIP encoder's representational ceiling and evaluation primarily on natural-image IQA; extending to video and AIGC quality assessment, and exploring stronger encoders like SigLIP, are natural next steps.
