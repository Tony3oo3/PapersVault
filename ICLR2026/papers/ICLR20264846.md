## Problem

Current tool-augmented MLLMs rely on single, atomic tools invoked in scripted or single-step fashion, failing to address multi-turn planning and the challenge of dynamically selecting and composing effective tool combinations for complex visual reasoning. This leaves a gap between passive recognition and the structured, iterative probing needed for fine-grained perception and multi-step spatial reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264846/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (e.g., a FrozenLake grid, a jigsaw puzzle with a black missing region, or a GUI screenshot) paired with a text task prompt. It produces a multi-turn reasoning trajectory where each turn contains textual reasoning, a tool-calling action delimited by special tokens, and the resulting observation from tool execution. The final turn yields a structured answer: a directional path sequence (VSP), a patch identifier (Jigsaw), or extracted text/coordinates (GUIQA). The tool suite spans lightweight offline utilities (CROP, DRAW2DPATH, DETECTBLACKAREA) and compute-heavy expert-model services (POINT via Molmo, OCR, A* pathfinding).

## Method

AdaReasoner introduces a two-stage training pipeline: (1) a Tool Cold Start (SFT) phase on curated multi-turn trajectories that explicitly encode reflection, backtracking, and tool-failure scenarios to bootstrap the policy, and (2) a Tool GRPO phase that extends Group Relative Policy Optimization to multi-turn tool-calling trajectories using a composite reward R_total = R_format * (lambda_tool * R_tool + lambda_acc * R_acc), where R_tool is a hierarchical 0-4 score over structure, name, parameter name, and parameter content across all tool turns. An adaptive reward mechanism asymmetrically incentivizes tool use: correct trajectories earn full credit regardless of tool usage, while incorrect trajectories receive partial credit (up to 4 points) only if tools were properly invoked, teaching the model to fall back on tools under uncertainty. The tool suite covers three reasoning functions (perception via POINT and OCR, manipulation via DRAW2DPATH and INSERTIMAGE, and calculation via A*) and the RL procedure yields emergent self-adaptive behaviors where the model autonomously adopts beneficial tools, discards irrelevant ones, and modulates invocation frequency per task.

## Results

AdaReasoner-7B (Qwen2.5-VL-7B + Tool Cold Start + Tool GRPO) achieves 97.64% on VSP Overall, 96.60% on Jigsaw, 96.00% on BLINK-J, and 88.57% on GUIChat, representing an average improvement of +38.7% over the base 7B model. On the out-of-distribution VSPO benchmark, it reaches 85.09% Overall. The 3B variant also benefits substantially, reaching 94.73% on VSP Overall (+68.00 over baseline), demonstrating that tool augmentation can overcome scale-based limitations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaReasoner-7B decisively outperforms all proprietary models on structured-reasoning benchmarks: it beats GPT-5 on VSP (97.64% vs. 55.64%), Jigsaw (96.60% vs. 80.10%), and BLINK-J (96.00% vs. 73.33%), and surpasses Claude Sonnet 4 on VSP (97.64% vs. 56.27%) and Jigsaw (96.60% vs. 58.60%). However, it trails Claude Sonnet 4 on GUIChat (88.57% vs. 93.14%) and WebMMU (68.16% vs. 71.61%), and slightly underperforms Gemini 2.5 Flash on WebMMU (68.16% vs. 69.31%). Against open-source baselines, the 7B AdaReasoner surpasses the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (70.10% Jigsaw) and InternVL3-78B (52.80% Jigsaw) by wide margins on VSP and Jigsaw, while matching or exceeding them on BLINK-J. Compared to its own ablation baselines, the full TC+TG pipeline yields +24.93 points on VSP and +19.82 on Jigsaw over Tool-GRPO alone, confirming the critical role of the supervised cold-start phase for tasks with well-defined solution spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-R1 to the multi-turn, tool-calling setting, introducing a tailored reward structure (format × tool × accuracy) that handles sequential tool invocations. It departs from prior tool-augmented MLLM work (LLaVA-Plus, CogCoM, TACO) that relied on scripted or single-step tool invocation, and from RL-based perception enhancers (DeepEyes, Pixel-Reasoner) that restricted themselves to single-tool trajectories. The key conceptual departure is treating tool selection, sequencing, and combination as an explicit planning problem solvable via RL, rather than a fixed pipeline or code-generation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a 7B model equipped with a modest tool suite can match or exceed GPT-5 and Claude Sonnet 4 on structured visual reasoning—suggests the performance bottleneck for MLLMs may shift from model scale to the quality and orchestration of external tools, opening a path for efficient, deployable reasoning agents. The emergent self-adaptive behaviors (autonomously adopting, discarding, and modulating tools) point toward more robust agentic systems, though the paper itself notes that for open-ended tasks like GUIQA, the SFT cold-start phase can actually hinder exploration, leaving the optimal training recipe for general domains unresolved. Open questions include whether these gains transfer to truly unstructured visual reasoning, how the approach scales to larger tool suites, and whether the adaptive behaviors generalize beyond the three evaluated task families.
