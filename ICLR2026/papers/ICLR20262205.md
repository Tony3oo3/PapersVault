## Problem

Wearable physiological time series are generated by multi-scale biological processes, yet most self-supervised representation learning methods—particularly transformer-based foundation models—collapse temporal structure into a single-scale embedding, obscuring which resolutions carry predictive signal for different clinical outcomes. Additionally, existing foundation models are too large (100M+ parameters) to run on smartwatch-class hardware, precluding true on-device inference at the point of signal collection.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 1D green PPG signals sampled at 100 Hz in fixed 10-second windows (1000 timesteps), preprocessed with a 0.5–8 Hz bandpass filter and normalized to [−1, 1]. Rather than producing a single summary embedding, HiMAE exposes multi-resolution feature maps at five encoder depths (temporal lengths T/2 through T/32), each corresponding to a distinct effective receptive field from ~5 to ~373 samples. Downstream, a separate linear probe is trained per resolution level, and the decoder outputs a tanh-bounded reconstruction of the masked input.

## Method

HiMAE couples a patch-masking objective (80% masking ratio, interleaved random and contiguous patterns) with a U-Net-style 1D CNN encoder-decoder: five residual convolutional blocks (kernel 5, stride-2 downsampling, channel widths 16→256) form the encoder, mirrored by transposed-convolution decoder blocks with skip connections that concatenate intermediate features. The exponential receptive-field growth (R_d = R_{d−1} + (k−1)·∏s_i) provides O(L) global context approximation without quadratic attention, while the hierarchical downsampling makes temporal resolution an explicit architectural axis rather than an emergent property. Training minimizes masked MSE restricted to occluded regions only, using AdamW (lr=1e−3) over 100k steps on ~80,000 hours of PPG from 47,644 participants. The resulting 1.2M-parameter model achieves sub-millisecond inference on smartwatch CPUs, and per-layer linear probing reveals that different clinical tasks peak at different resolutions—cardiovascular and sleep tasks at coarse layers, blood-biomarker tasks at fine layers—validating the paper's resolution hypothesis.

## Results

HiMAE (1.2M parameters) achieves the best or near-best AUROC across 12 classification tasks, winning on PVC detection (80.2%), A1C prediction (70.1%), hemoglobin (68.5%), and sodium (83.1%) with statistical significance (p<0.05 or p<0.01). On generative reconstruction, HiMAE attains the lowest MSE in all three regimes, e.g., 0.026 at 80% random imputation vs. 0.041 for MAE-1D (ViT) and 0.211 at 80% temporal extrapolation vs. 0.356 for MAE-1D (ViT), the only model achieving positive R² in extrapolation. On-device, HiMAE runs at 0.99 ms per sample with 4.8 MB memory on smartwatch-class CPUs, roughly 3–4× faster than 110M-parameter transformer baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SSL baselines (Table 2), HiMAE (1.2M) outperforms SimCLR (5M), DINO (6.5M), and MSN (2.5M) on most tasks, and decisively beats the 110.6M-parameter MAE-1D (ViT) on PVC (80.2 vs. 72.2), A1C (70.1 vs. 79.6—actually MAE ViT wins here), and sodium (63.3 vs. 48.8). Against foundation models (Table 3), HiMAE wins clearly on PVC (80.2 vs. 75.2 PaPaGei-SRA, 65.7 Chronos), A1C (70.1 vs. 61.2 PaPaGei-SRA), hemoglobin (68.5 vs. 60.5), and sodium (83.1 vs. 61.0), all with p<0.05 or p<0.01. Chronos (200M) edges out HiMAE on lab hypertension (67.3 vs. 65.1) and REM staging (75.3 vs. 58.5), while PaPaGei-SRA leads on potassium (77.5 vs. HiMAE's missing value). On generative reconstruction, HiMAE dominates all baselines by 30–40% relative MSE reduction at 80% missingness, and is the only model with positive R² in temporal extrapolation. On-device, HiMAE is 3× faster (0.99 ms vs. 2.95 ms Swin, 3.36 ms MAE-1D) and uses 1/90th the memory (4.8 MB vs. 423–441 MB) of transformer baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiMAE combines two well-established ideas—masked autoencoding (He et al., 2022) and the U-Net encoder-decoder with skip connections (Ronneberger et al., 2015)—but departs from the dominant transformer-based paradigm in wearable foundation models (LSM, PaPaGei, Chronos) by replacing global attention with a hierarchical 1D CNN whose depth explicitly indexes temporal resolution. It also connects to the multi-scale time-series forecasting literature (N-HiTS, Pyraformer, Scaleformer) but repurposes hierarchical scale decomposition for self-supervised pretraining and interpretability rather than forecasting. The key conceptual departure is treating temporal resolution not as a fixed hyperparameter but as a probeable axis of representation, enabling per-layer linear probes to reveal which scales carry task-specific signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1.2M-parameter model achieving sub-millisecond inference on smartwatch-class CPUs represents the first credible path to true on-watch SSL inference, with implications for privacy (data never leaves the device) and continuous real-time monitoring. The resolution hypothesis—demonstrating that cardiovascular/sleep tasks peak at coarse layers while lab-biomarker tasks peak at fine layers—offers a new interpretability lens that shifts the question from 'which features matter' to 'which temporal scales matter.' Key limitations include restriction to PPG at 10-second windows, reliance on proprietary Samsung datasets, and absence of clinical validation for the discovered resolution-task mappings, leaving open whether these patterns generalize across modalities, longer sequences, and independent clinical cohorts.
