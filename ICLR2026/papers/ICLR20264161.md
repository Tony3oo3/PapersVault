## Problem

Long-context LLMs can process full documents but suffer from token inefficiency, the 'lost in the middle' phenomenon, and degradation from distracting passages, while standard RAG retrieves fixed-size subsets without accounting for how co-retrieved passages interfere with the LLM's reasoning. No existing method learns to adaptively select passages that minimize distraction while balancing information coverage in a way that accounts for the specific target LLM's long-context capacity.

## Input / Output

The adaptive retriever π_θ takes only the cosine similarity vector s ∈ ℝ^N between a query and N candidate passages (no textual content) as input, where N varies per query (≈64 for 32K contexts, ≈256 for 128K). It outputs a contiguous quantile band [q_L, q_U] ⊂ [0,1] that selects a variable number of passages from the similarity-ranked list, rather than a fixed top-k or per-passage binary decisions. The selected passages are then passed to a frozen pretrained LLM for answer generation.

## Method

LDAR encodes each similarity score via periodic embeddings, processes the variable-length sequence with a 2-layer bidirectional Transformer, and aggregates via attention pooling into a global representation. Two output heads predict parameters of Beta distributions from which the lower quantile q_L and upper quantile q_U are sampled, defining a contiguous band in the similarity-ranked passage list. Training uses policy gradient (REINFORCE) with the LLM's binary prediction accuracy as reward, updating only the lightweight retriever while keeping the embedding model and LLM frozen. The key design choice is band-based retrieval over per-passage Bernoulli selection: restricting to contiguous ranges reduces the combinatorial subset space to a 2D smooth control space, enabling sample-efficient credit assignment and preventing convergence to the degenerate long-context solution. The learned strategy adapts to LLM capacity—retrieving fewer passages for open-source models and more for closed-source models—and to the similarity distribution shape, narrowing the band when high-similarity passages exist and widening it when the distribution is flat.

## Results

On the LaRA benchmark (32K context), LDAR achieves 70.00 (open-source) and 79.42 (closed-source) overall accuracy, outperforming the long-context baseline (58.62 / 74.00) by 11.4 and 5.4 points respectively, while using only 47% (open) and 63% (closed) of the tokens. At 128K context, the gap widens: LDAR scores 61.55 (open) and 76.22 (closed) versus 43.65 / 69.97 for long-context, with token usage dropping to 25% and 52%. In zero-shot transfer to HELMET (HotpotQA, NQ), LDAR trained on LaRA still outperforms both RAG and long-context across all nine LLMs tested, e.g., 60.0 vs. 44.2 (open-source avg) on HotpotQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDAR outperforms all baselines on overall accuracy across both 32K and 128K context lengths. Against the standard RAG baseline (Lewis et al., 2020 with bge-reranker-large), LDAR gains +7.9 points (open) and +8.8 points (closed) at 32K, and +6.9 / +11.8 points at 128K, while using roughly 5× more tokens than RAG but still far fewer than LC. The heuristic routing baselines Self-Route (Li et al., 2024b) and Adaptive-k (Taguchi et al., 2025) underperform LDAR by 10.6 / 18.3 points (open, 32K) and 11.9 / 20.5 points (open, 128K), confirming that simple gap-based or self-assessment routing cannot capture the distraction trade-off. The text-based bridge method BGM (Ke et al., 2024) and the listwise reranker RankZephyr (Pradeep et al., 2023) both operate only within the top-5/top-50 similarity region and yield at most +2.7 points over LC, whereas LDAR's band-based selection over the full similarity distribution achieves substantially larger gains. In the cost-efficiency analysis (Table 3), LDAR achieves the lowest inference latency among all reranker and long-context methods (3.9 s open-source, 8.0 s closed-source on Location) while simultaneously delivering the highest accuracy, and its cumulative cost advantage over LC grows with deployment scale (Figure 5). CAG (Chan et al., 2025) reduces LC latency by 2.5× but matches LC performance, whereas LDAR both improves accuracy and reduces token usage, making it strictly superior in the accuracy–cost Pareto frontier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RAG framework (Lewis et al., 2020) and the emerging long-context LLM paradigm, positioning itself against the growing body of work comparing the two (Li et al., 2024b; Li et al., 2025a). It departs from prior bridge-module approaches like BGM (Ke et al., 2024) that use textual information to select subsets from top-k candidates, and from heuristic routing methods like Self-Route and Adaptive-k, by operating solely on the cosine similarity distribution with a text-free, policy-gradient-trained Transformer. The key conceptual departure is the band-based retrieval abstraction—selecting a contiguous quantile interval rather than individual passages—which reduces the combinatorial search space and enables sample-efficient credit assignment, drawing on temporal abstraction ideas from reinforcement learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LDAR offers a practical, lightweight solution to the RAG-vs.-long-context dilemma that automatically adapts retrieval granularity to the target LLM's long-context capability, achieving near-long-context accuracy at roughly half the token cost across diverse architectures and benchmarks. The finding that even jointly retrieved gold passages can degrade performance (the 'collective distraction' effect) reframes retrieval as a set-selection problem rather than a ranking problem. Open questions include whether the approach extends to joint selection-and-ordering optimization, whether a single meta-retriever can handle heterogeneous task types without task-specific training, and whether the band-based abstraction generalizes to non-contiguous or multi-modal retrieval settings.
