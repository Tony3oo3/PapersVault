## Problem

Existing generative models for neural network weights either produce only partial weight sets for large architectures, require post-generation fine-tuning to achieve competitive accuracy, or suffer from prohibitively long generation times (hours for diffusion-based methods like RPG). A key unresolved challenge is handling the high-dimensional weight spaces (up to O(100M) parameters) and their inherent permutation symmetries without resorting to lossy nonlinear dimensionality reduction via autoencoders.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264747/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a random Gaussian noise vector x₀ ~ N(0, σ²I) in the same flattened dimension as the target network's full weight vector and produces complete, ready-to-use neural network weights for architectures ranging from small MLPs (131 params) to BERT-Base (118M params). For networks containing Batch Normalization layers, a small calibration data subset is additionally required post-generation to recompute running mean and variance statistics, since these are intrinsically tied to the training data distribution and cannot be reliably generated by the flow model alone. For large models, the noise vector lives in a PCA-reduced latent space (99 components for 100 training samples), and inverse PCA is applied after generation to recover full-dimensional weights.

## Method

DeepWeightFlow trains a time-conditioned MLP (with LayerNorm, GELU, and Dropout) to predict the velocity field of a straight-line probability path from Gaussian noise to the empirical distribution of 100 independently trained terminal network weights, using the standard Flow Matching loss. Before training, permutation symmetries are broken via canonicalization: Git Re-Basin (100 iterations of coordinate-descent linear assignment) for MLPs and ResNets, or TransFusion (10 iterations of inter-head spectral matching plus intra-head alignment) for ViTs. Sampling integrates the learned vector field with 4th-order Runge-Kutta over 100 steps. To scale beyond GPU memory limits, incremental PCA is applied for O(10M)-parameter models and Dual PCA (Gram-matrix eigendecomposition with randomized SVD) for O(100M)-parameter models, with inverse PCA applied post-generation. The approach requires no equivariant architecture, no learned autoencoder, and no conditioning on dataset or task descriptors, yet generates ensembles of hundreds of networks in minutes versus hours for diffusion baselines.

## Results

On ResNet-18 CIFAR-10, DeepWeightFlow generates complete weights achieving 93.55±0.13% accuracy (with Git Re-Basin) and 93.47±0.20% (without), matching the original training set's 94.45±0.14% without any fine-tuning. On ViT-Small-192 CIFAR-10, generated weights reach 83.07±0.42% (with TransFusion) versus 83.30±0.29% for the original. On MNIST MLPs, DeepWeightFlow achieves 96.17±0.31% accuracy, far exceeding WeightFlow (78.6%) and FLoWN (83.58%). Training takes ~10 minutes and generation takes seconds per model, compared to hours for diffusion-based methods like RPG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepWeightFlow is the only method (besides RPG) that generates complete ResNet-18 weights without fine-tuning, achieving 93.55% vs. RPG's 95.1%—a 1.5-point gap that RPG pays for with generation times of hours versus DeepWeightFlow's seconds. Against SANE, which also attempts complete generation via autoregression, DeepWeightFlow wins decisively (93.55% vs. 68.6%). The remaining baselines (D2NWG, NM, P-diff, FLoWN) only produce partial weights (limited to non-BN parameters), so their 94.36–94.57% partial scores are not directly comparable to DeepWeightFlow's complete-weight accuracy. On MNIST MLPs (Table 1), DeepWeightFlow (96.17%) substantially outperforms WeightFlow (78.6%) and FLoWN (83.58%). On ViT-Small-192 (Table 4), DeepWeightFlow (83.07%) far exceeds P-diff's 73.6% on the smaller ViT-mini, though RPG on the larger ViT-Base reaches 98.9% at the cost of multi-hour generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepWeightFlow applies Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) directly in neural network weight space, departing from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm (RPG, D2NWG, P-diff) that has characterized prior weight generation work. It repurposes canonicalization techniques—Git Re-Basin for MLPs/ResNets and TransFusion for transformers—as a preprocessing step to break permutation symmetries, rather than relying on equivariant architectures or VAE-based dimensionality reduction. Unlike SANE, which autoregressively generates weights layer-by-layer and requires fine-tuning, or FLoWN/P-diff which produce only partial weight sets, DeepWeightFlow generates complete, immediately deployable weights for diverse architectures using a simple MLP-based flow model with linear PCA for scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: generating ensembles of hundreds of high-accuracy neural networks in minutes (vs. hours for diffusion methods) opens the door to rapid model ensembling, uncertainty quantification, and privacy-preserving model distribution without training data exposure. Key limitations include the need for 100 pre-trained networks as training data, BN recalibration for architectures with batch normalization, and the current ceiling at ~100M parameters (with 1B+ left as future work). Open questions include whether multi-class and multi-architecture generation can be unified, how the method interacts with model distillation or sparsity, and whether the capacity-dependent role of canonicalization generalizes to much larger models.
