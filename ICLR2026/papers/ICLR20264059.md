## Problem

Existing neural operator methods largely overlook the prevalent setting of parameterized dynamics with fixed initial conditions (u_t = f(u;μ), u(0)=u_0), where physical parameters govern the evolution but the starting state is unchanged. Additionally, while coupled PDE systems have been addressed by specialized architectures like CMWNO, there is no systematic design-space analysis of where and how to introduce cross-variable coupling in FNOs while preserving their efficiency and compactness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264059/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The operator takes a window of T_in consecutive discretized solution snapshots (128 spatial grid points per variable) as its functional input, optionally augmented with physical parameter vectors μ (e.g., driving voltage, reaction rate, ion mass for CCP; diffusion coefficients and feed rate for Gray-Scott). It produces the next time-step solution for all coupled variables simultaneously (e.g., electron density and electric potential, or two chemical species). Multi-step forecasts are generated autoregressively by feeding predicted states back into the input window, and the spatial grid is fixed across all parameter configurations.

## Method

The paper introduces two minimal architectural extensions to FNOs. For parameterization, a compact hypernetwork maps physical parameters μ to layer-specific shift vectors s_ℓ(x,μ) that are added as biases inside each Fourier layer, conditioning the operator throughout its depth without regenerating full weights (unlike HyperFNO). For coupled systems, the authors define a systematic design space over the lift operator, point-wise linear maps, global spectral convolution, and projection operator, and identify the optimal combination (FNO_x) through ablation: shared lift, separate point-wise maps, a coupled spectral convolution that mixes Fourier-space representations of all variables via a shallow encoder-decoder before the mode-wise kernel multiplication, and separate adaptive-basis functions with separate coefficients in the projection (with layer norm). This Fourier-space coupling preserves the single-operator structure of FNOs, avoiding the repeated spatial-domain hidden-state exchanges used by CFNO/CMWNO, and keeps model size and per-epoch training time comparable to standard FNOs.

## Results

On the 1D CCP benchmark, hpFNO_x achieves nRMSE of 0.0154 (reaction rate), 0.0192 (driving voltage), and 0.0128 (ion mass), representing up to 55% improvement over the best non-parameterized baseline (CMWNO at 0.0312/0.0526/0.0241). On the Gray-Scott benchmark, hpFNO_x reaches 0.0022 nRMSE, a 72% reduction over the strongest baseline (MWT_c at 0.0092). The non-parameterized FNO_x already outperforms all baselines (e.g., 0.0193 vs. 0.0312 for CMWNO on reaction rate), and the hypernetwork-based parametric extension (hpFNO_x) further reduces errors by 20–44% over FNO_x across all three parameter scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed FNO_x and hpFNO_x consistently outperform all five baseline families across both benchmarks. Against the coupled multiwavelet operator (CMWNO, Xiao et al. 2023), FNO_x reduces nRMSE by up to 38% (0.0193 vs. 0.0312 on reaction rate), and hpFNO_x extends this to 51% (0.0154 vs. 0.0312). The multiwavelet operator (MWT_c, Gupta et al. 2021) is outperformed by all proposed variants, with hpFNO_x achieving 62% lower error on reaction rate (0.0154 vs. 0.0409). Against HyperFNO (Alesiani et al. 2022), which also uses hypernetworks for parameter conditioning, hpFNO_x achieves over 40% lower nRMSE in all three parameter scenarios (e.g., 0.0192 vs. 0.0355 on driving voltage), demonstrating that lightweight shift modulation is more effective than regenerating full FNO weights. DeepONet (Lu et al. 2021) and U-Net (Ronneberger et al. 2015) are substantially outperformed, with errors 2–10× higher than the proposed methods, confirming that spectral-domain operator architectures are better suited for these coupled, parameterized PDE tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Fourier Neural Operators (FNOs) along two axes: parameterized dynamics and coupled systems. For parameterization, it departs from HyperFNO (Alesiani et al., 2022), which regenerates full FNO weights via a hypernetwork, by instead using a lightweight hypernetwork that outputs layer-wise bias shifts, keeping core weights fixed. For coupling, it contrasts with CMWNO and CFNO, which use separate operators exchanging hidden representations in the spatial domain, by introducing a spectral-domain encoder-decoder that mixes Fourier coefficients of multiple variables within a single shared convolution. The paper also contributes a systematic design-space exploration (shared vs. separate lift, pointwise maps, and projection operators) that prior coupled-operator work has not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important but underexplored regime—parameterized PDEs with fixed initial conditions—where the T_in=1 experiment shows non-parameterized FNOs fail entirely while the proposed hpFNO_x maintains ~13% error, a meaningful step toward real-time surrogate deployment where only initial states are available. The new 1D CCP benchmark introduces rich multi-parameter plasma dynamics relevant to semiconductor manufacturing, filling a gap in the neural-operator evaluation landscape. Limitations include evaluation exclusively on 1D problems with small datasets (100 trajectories), and the spectral coupling mechanism has not been tested on higher-dimensional or strongly nonlinear coupled systems, leaving open whether the design-space conclusions generalize.
