## Problem

DP-SGD suffers severe performance degradation when private training data is limited (M < 500), and existing gradient-projection methods (PDP-SGD, GEP) that mitigate this by projecting onto a fixed public-data eigenspace have two unaddressed flaws: the fixed subspace becomes increasingly misaligned with the evolving private gradient subspace as training progresses, and the optimal point for injecting DP noise within the projection pipeline has never been systematically studied.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained generative language model (Qwen2.5-3B, Llama-3.2-3B, or GPT-2), a small private dataset of M=400 text samples, and a set of zero-shot/one-shot generation prompts, and produces a LoRA-fine-tuned model (rank 8) that adapts to the private distribution under (ε,δ)-DP. A key design choice is that the method never directly updates parameters with private gradients; instead it computes DP-protected approximate gradients in an N=200-dimensional synthetic gradient subspace, using normalization (rather than clipping) for sensitivity control and Poisson sub-sampling (β=0.2) identical to DP-SGD.

## Method

PE-SGD replaces the fixed top-k eigenspace projection of PDP-SGD/GEP with a full N-dimensional projection z = (G^T G)^{-1}(G^T H) over synthetic sample gradients G, and evolves the synthetic dataset D at each iteration by selecting seed samples proportional to |z_i| and generating L-1 variants per seed via the updated model's VARIATIONAL_API, so the projection subspace tracks the shifting private gradient geometry. For noise placement, the paper identifies three natural injection points (private gradients, gradient inner products, final projection coefficients) and empirically shows that adding Gaussian noise to the final N-dimensional coefficient vector z minimizes the ℓ2 distance and maximizes cosine similarity between the DP-protected approximate gradient and the true private gradient. The privacy guarantee is identical to DP-SGD (Theorem B.1): Poisson sub-sampling plus isotropic Gaussian noise on ℓ2-normalized columns, composed via the PRV Accountant. The method is compatible with any optimizer (e.g., AdamW) and can optionally use a stronger frozen model (e.g., GPT-4o-mini) to generate the evolving synthetic samples while fine-tuning a weaker target model.

## Results

Under the tight DP budget (ε=1, δ=1e-5) with M=400 private samples, PE-SGD achieves the lowest next-token prediction loss across all three datasets: 2.199 on PubMed, 2.753 on Congressional Speech, and 2.318 on bioRxiv, while also attaining the highest accuracy (0.5435, 0.4577, and 0.5095 respectively). At ε=∞ (no DP noise), DP-SGD slightly outperforms PE-SGD as expected, since it directly uses unnoised private gradients. PE-SGD's advantage over DP-SGD is most pronounced at ε=1, where it reduces loss by 0.17–0.35 points depending on the dataset, and also shows the largest loss reduction on the 10% hardest samples (−3.50 vs. −2.04 for DP-SGD on Congressional Speech).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PE-SGD outperforms all five baselines (DP-SGD, PDP-SGD, GEP, Aug-PE, POPri) on all three datasets under the tight DP budget ε=1, achieving the lowest loss and highest accuracy. Compared to the standard DP-SGD, PE-SGD reduces loss by 0.17 (PubMed), 0.35 (Congressional Speech), and 0.06 (bioRxiv) at ε=1, with the gap being largest on Congressional Speech where DP-SGD degrades most severely. The gradient-projection baselines PDP-SGD and GEP are the closest competitors but still trail PE-SGD by 0.05–0.15 loss points, attributed to their use of a fixed top-k eigenspace rather than the full evolving subspace. Aug-PE and POPri perform poorly under the limited-data setting (M=400), with POPri nearly matching the base model on Congressional Speech, confirming that DPO-based and synthetic-data-SFT approaches struggle without sufficient private samples. At ε=∞, DP-SGD slightly edges out PE-SGD (e.g., 2.1497 vs. 2.1839 loss on PubMed), which is expected since DP-SGD directly uses unnoised private gradients while PE-SGD still relies on synthetic gradient approximation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PE-SGD sits at the intersection of two research threads: gradient-projection-based DP training (PDP-SGD, GEP) and iterative synthetic data evolution via generative APIs (Private Evolution). The paper proves that its closed-form projection is mathematically equivalent to PDP-SGD and GEP when the full eigenspace is used, then departs from both by (1) replacing the fixed public-dataset subspace with one that evolves through the updated model's own generation, and (2) systematically identifying the optimal noise injection point (final projection coefficients) rather than the ad hoc placements used in prior work. This removes the dependency on a large public dataset that prior projection methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PE-SGD addresses a practically critical regime—limited private data (M < 500) with tight privacy budgets (ε = 1)—where standard DP-SGD degrades severely, and provides a clean, sample-efficient alternative requiring only ~200 synthetic samples per iteration. The demonstration that the private gradient subspace rotates substantially during fine-tuning (via principal-angle analysis) gives a concrete, previously unquantified reason why fixed-subspace methods fail, motivating the evolutionary design. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other modalities, full fine-tuning without LoRA, and training from scratch, as well as whether the evolutionary strategy can be made more principled to directly minimize the gradient alignment gap rather than relying on heuristic seed selection.
