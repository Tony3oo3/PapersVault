## Problem

Existing Riemannian federated learning algorithms (RFedAvg, RFedSVRG, RFedProj, ZO-RFedProj) lack convergence guarantees under the combined setting of partial participation and non-I.I.D. data heterogeneity. The curvature of manifolds makes standard parameter averaging invalid, and prior Riemannian FL methods either require full participation, I.I.D. data, or both, while also relying on expensive operations like exponential map inverses and parallel transport that lack closed forms on important manifolds such as the Stiefel manifold.

## Input / Output

Each agent holds a local dataset on a Riemannian manifold (Stiefel, Grassmann, SPD, or hyperbolic) and performs K local SGD steps using mini-batch gradients. Rather than uploading local parameters, each agent uploads a 'gradient stream'—the accumulated sum of mini-batch gradients transported back to the initial point's tangent space via vector transport. The server requires only an initial global model, the number of local iterations K, step sizes, and running frequencies to estimate participation probabilities; it then produces a new global model via retraction of the probability-weighted average of received gradient streams.

## Method

RFedAGS replaces the tangent-mean aggregation (which requires exponential map inverses and parallel transport) with an 'Averaging Gradient Streams' (AGS) scheme: each agent accumulates its K mini-batch gradients, transports each back to the tangent space at the round's starting point using a bounded vector transport, and uploads the resulting gradient stream. The server averages these streams weighted by 1/(p_i·N) where p_i is the estimated participation probability, then retracts the result to obtain the next global iterate. This design is a natural Riemannian generalization of the Euclidean observation that FedAvg's parameter increment equals the average of mini-batch gradients, and it reduces to standard FedAvg averaging when the manifold is Euclidean. The probability-weighted aggregation (AGS-AP) corrects the bias introduced by arbitrary participation, ensuring the algorithm solves the original global objective rather than a re-weighted surrogate. Theoretically, RFedAGS achieves global convergence with sublinear rates under decaying step sizes and sublinear/linear convergence to a neighborhood of a stationary point under fixed step sizes, requiring only a general C² retraction and bounded vector transport.

## Results

RFedAGS consistently outperforms all existing Riemannian FL baselines (RFedAvg, RFedSVRG, RFedProj, ZO-RFedProj) across PCA (Stiefel manifold), hyperbolic structured prediction (Lorentz model), and Fréchet mean computation (SPD manifold) in terms of both solution quality and wall-clock time under arbitrary partial participation with non-I.I.D. data. On the MovieLens 1M low-rank matrix completion task, RFedAGS with K=16 local updates achieves RMSE of 7.948×10⁻¹ (r=3), 7.654×10⁻¹ (r=5), and 7.468×10⁻¹ (r=7), approaching the centralized baselines (RSD/RCG/LRBFGS) within 0.02–0.08 RMSE. Theoretically, RFedAGS achieves O(1/ln(T)) or O(1/T^{1-p}) sublinear convergence under decaying step sizes, and linear convergence to an ε-neighborhood under fixed step sizes with RPL condition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RFedAvg and RFedSVRG (Li & Ma, 2023), RFedAGS wins on all three main tasks (PCA, HSP, FMC) and the PEC experiment with MNIST, achieving lower optimality gaps in fewer communication rounds; moreover, RFedAvg/RFedSVRG require exponential map inverses and parallel transport that lack closed forms on the Stiefel manifold, whereas RFedAGS only needs a general retraction and bounded vector transport. Against RFedProj (Zhang et al., 2024), RFedAGS wins on PCA (both synthetic and CIFAR-10) and additionally handles HSP and FMC, which RFedProj cannot address since it is restricted to compact Riemannian submanifolds with a singleton orthogonal projector. Against ZO-RFedProj (Wang et al., 2025), RFedAGS similarly outperforms on PCA and extends to manifolds where zeroth-order projection is inapplicable. On the MovieLens 1M LRMC benchmark, RFedAGS with K=16 local updates reaches RMSE within 0.02–0.08 of the best centralized methods (RSD, RCG, LRBFGS), demonstrating that the federated Riemannian approach is competitive with centralized solvers when sufficient local iterations are used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Euclidean FedAvg framework (McMahan et al., 2017) to Riemannian manifolds by replacing the tangent mean aggregation of Li & Ma (2023) with an 'averaging gradient streams' (AGS) scheme that only requires retraction and vector transport, avoiding the intractable inverse exponential map on manifolds like Stiefel. It also incorporates the arbitrary participation model with unknown probabilities from Wang & Ji (2024), adapting it to the Riemannian setting via probability-weighted aggregation (AGS-AP). The key conceptual departure is that prior Riemannian FL algorithms (RFedSVRG, RPriFed, RFedProj) either require full participation, restrict to K=1 for non-IID data, or are limited to compact embedded submanifolds, whereas RFedAGS handles general manifolds with K>1 local steps, partial participation, and non-IID data simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first Riemannian FL algorithm with convergence guarantees under both arbitrary partial participation and data heterogeneity, filling a clear gap identified in the literature. The gradient stream aggregation is more broadly applicable than tangent mean (works on Stiefel manifold where inverse exponential has no closed form) and has lower computational complexity per outer iteration. Open questions include extending the analysis to time-varying participation probabilities (the current theory assumes time-invariant statistics), and whether the probability estimation assumption (Assumption 3.8) holds robustly in highly dynamic real-world deployments. The practical impact is primarily for structured learning problems on manifolds (PCA, low-rank completion, hyperbolic embeddings) rather than general deep learning.
