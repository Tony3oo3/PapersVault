## Problem

Online reinforcement learning in non-episodic settings with nonlinear dynamics and continuous state-action spaces lacks nonasymptotic frequentist policy-regret guarantees. Prior posterior-sampling methods provide only Bayesian regret bounds, while optimistic approaches require computing confidence sets and optimistic policies that are difficult to bound beyond linear MDPs. This paper closes the gap by establishing frequentist policy-regret guarantees for a broad class of nonlinear dynamical systems, including finite model sets, bounded function classes, and parametric families (e.g., neural networks), under standard persistence-of-excitation assumptions.

## Input / Output

At each time step the algorithm receives a state observation x_k ∈ R^{d_x} and a pre-specified set of candidate models F (finite, bounded in a normed space, or parametrized by θ ∈ Ω ⊂ R^p). It outputs a control action u_k ∈ R^{d_u} constructed as a certainty-equivalent feedback policy plus Gaussian excitation noise, and maintains a posterior distribution over models updated via normalized one-step prediction errors. A distinctive design choice is the normalization of prediction errors by 1 + |x_k, u_k|²/b², which keeps accumulated losses bounded even for large states, and the periodic (every M steps) model resampling that ensures sufficient excitation richness for identifiability.

## Method

The algorithm implements a Hedge/multiplicative-weights update where the 'loss' for each candidate model is its accumulated normalized one-step prediction error, interpreted as negative log-likelihood under Gaussian process noise. Every M steps, a model is sampled from a softmax of these errors (posterior sampling), the corresponding precomputed certainty-equivalent policy is applied, and additive Gaussian excitation is injected to maintain persistence of excitation. The key architectural insight is a separation principle that decouples model identification (posterior sampling over F) from control (certainty-equivalent policy application), avoiding the need to compute confidence sets or optimistic policies. The regret analysis uses the cost-to-go function V as a Lyapunov function, decomposing the expected evolution of V into favorable (correct model selected) and unfavorable (wrong model) cases, and shows via concentration arguments that Pr(i_k ≠ i*) decays as M²/(k−M)², yielding O(d_u ln(N)/Δ + d_u ln(m)/Δ) regret for finite model sets and O(√(d_u N p)) for parametric models.

## Results

The paper establishes three main policy-regret bounds for online reinforcement learning with nonlinear dynamics over continuous state-action spaces. For a finite set of m candidate models (S1), the regret is O(d_u ln(N)/Δ + d_u ln(m)/Δ); for a bounded function class (S2), it is O(Nε² + d_u ln(N)/ε² + d_u ln(m(ε))/ε²); and for parametric models with p parameters (S3), it is O(√(d_u N p)). Numerically, on a 20-dimensional linear system, Alg.1 (finite models) reaches near-optimal steady-state in ~25 steps while Alg.3 (parametric) takes ~60 steps; on a nonlinear pendulum-on-a-cart, the learned swing-up completes in ~80 steps versus ~60 with known dynamics, with all experiments running in under two minutes on a laptop.

All bounds are frequentist (hold for any fixed environment in F), non-episodic, and require persistence of excitation. For linear dynamics (S3 with p = d_x² + d_x d_u), the bound recovers O(√(d_u N p)) matching Simchowitz & Foster (2020).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is theoretical rather than empirical. Against online switching control (Li et al., 2023; Kim & Lavaei, 2024), which achieves O(m^{1/3} N^{2/3}) + exp(O(|M|)) regret in a finite-model setting, this paper's O(ln(N) + ln(m)) bound is substantially tighter, especially as m grows. Against the linear-quadratic regulator literature (Dean et al., 2018; Mania et al., 2019; Simchowitz & Foster, 2020), the paper recovers the same O(√(d_u N p)) rate but via posterior sampling and a Hedge-type analysis rather than maximum-a-posteriori identification and Riccati perturbation bounds, extending the result to nonlinear dynamics. Compared to optimistic methods (Abeille & Lazaric, 2020; Croissant et al., 2024) that achieve O(√N) regret with eluder-dimension-dependent constants, this paper avoids computing confidence regions or optimistic policies entirely, using packing numbers as a more tractable complexity measure. Against posterior sampling RL (Osband et al., 2013; Xu et al., 2024), the key distinction is frequentist (rather than Bayesian) regret guarantees and the use of persistence-of-excitation assumptions (weaker than mixing assumptions) that apply near the stability boundary. The numerical experiments confirm that the finite-model algorithm (Alg.1) scales favorably from m=10 to m=10,000 with consistent transient behavior, and that the parametric algorithm (Alg.3) converges in roughly twice as many steps as Alg.1 due to the uncountable parameter space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three distinct lineages: posterior sampling reinforcement learning (Osband et al., 2013), Hedge/multiplicative-weights updates from online learning (Cesa-Bianchi & Lugosi, 2006), and certainty-equivalent control (Mania et al., 2019), into a unified multi-model framework for non-episodic online RL with continuous state-action spaces. It departs from prior posterior sampling RL by providing frequentist (rather than Bayesian) policy-regret guarantees through explicit excitation and persistence-of-excitation conditions borrowed from system identification, and from optimism-based methods by avoiding confidence regions and optimistic policy computation entirely. The separation principle—decoupling best-model identification from certainty-equivalent control—extends the well-known LQR separation (Dean et al., 2018; Simchowitz & Foster, 2020) to general nonlinear dynamics, while the O(ln(m)) scaling for finite model sets improves upon the O(m^{1/3}) rates from switching-control approaches (Li et al., 2023; Kim & Lavaei, 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, unified sample-complexity characterization (via packing numbers) that spans finite, infinite, and parametric model classes, recovering known O(√(d_u N p)) LQR results while extending to general nonlinear dynamics without structural assumptions like contraction or linear feature representations. The practical algorithms—integrating naturally with MPC or PPO for policy evaluation and requiring only O(p²) per-iteration computation in the linear case—offer a deployable framework for engineering applications. Open questions include developing computationally tractable algorithms for the infinite-cardinality setting (S2), designing richer exploration strategies beyond additive Gaussian noise, and extending to partial observability and non-additive noise. The reliance on persistence-of-excitation, while standard in system identification, may be restrictive for systems near the stability boundary or with highly non-ergodic trajectories.
