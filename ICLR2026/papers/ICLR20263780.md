## Problem

Unsupervised cross-modal knowledge transfer for biosignals typically relies on knowledge distillation, which requires simultaneous forward passes through both a large teacher and a student model, making it computationally prohibitive—especially as biosignal foundation models grow in size. This paper asks whether a lightweight, parameter-efficient alternative can transfer knowledge between pre-trained biosignal foundation models of different modalities without the memory and compute overhead of full distillation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263780/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a biosignal time series from the new modality (e.g., PPG at 125 Hz, ECG at 100 Hz, EEG at 200 Hz, EMG at 130 Hz, segmented into fixed-length windows) and produces a class label for the target task (stress, FOG, or sleep/wake classification). During bridge training, the method additionally requires unlabeled paired recordings from both the old and new modalities (synchronized signals from the same subject), but no labels are needed for the new modality. Both foundation models remain frozen; only the small bridge network is trained.

## Method

BioX-Bridge inserts a lightweight bridge network between the intermediate representations of two pre-trained biosignal foundation models, projecting features from the new modality model into the old modality model's representation space so that the old model's downstream layers and task head can process new-modality inputs. A two-stage position selection strategy identifies where to connect: linear probing against pseudo-labels selects the input layer m in the new modality model, and linear CKA similarity selects the output layer l in the old modality model. The bridge itself is a prototype network combining a set of learnable prototype vectors (initialized from the old model's intermediate features) with a low-rank factorization module (matrices A and B) that generates aggregation weights, reducing trainable parameters by 88–99% compared to a full-rank projection. Training minimizes an alignment loss (cosine or MAE) between the old model's final-layer representations and the bridged representations, updating only the bridge parameters while keeping both foundation models frozen.

## Results

Across three biosignal datasets (WESAD, FOG, ISRUC-Sleep) and six transfer directions, BioX-Bridge reduces trainable parameters by 88–99% (e.g., 0.4M vs. 30.4M for WESAD PPG→ECG) while matching or exceeding state-of-the-art knowledge distillation baselines. The strongest gain appears on WESAD PPG→ECG, where BioX-Bridge achieves 52.02% Balanced Accuracy with 0.4M parameters, outperforming KD (47.03%) and KD-Contrast (50.85%) by 1–5 points. On FOG EEG→EMG, BioX-Bridge reaches 72.24% BAcc with only 1.2M parameters, surpassing KD (68.64%) and matching KD-Contrast (72.21%) while using 99.1% fewer parameters. The method also demonstrates robustness to reduced paired data, with only ~2% performance decay at 20% dataset size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard knowledge distillation (KD, Hinton et al. 2015), BioX-Bridge wins on 4 of 6 transfer directions in Balanced Accuracy, with the largest margin on WESAD PPG→ECG (+5.0% BAcc: 52.02 vs. 47.03) and FOG EEG→EMG (+3.6%: 72.24 vs. 68.64), while using 88–99% fewer trainable parameters. Against KD-Contrast (Abbaspourazad et al. 2024), BioX-Bridge wins on WESAD PPG→ECG (+1.2% BAcc) and WESAD ECG→PPG (+4.3% BAcc), matches on FOG EEG→EMG (72.24 vs. 72.21), but trails on ISRUC ECG→EEG (62.55 vs. 65.92) and FOG EMG→EEG (68.04 vs. 68.51). Against CardioGAN (Sarkar & Etemad 2021), which is only applicable to the WESAD ECG→PPG direction, BioX-Bridge achieves a substantial +10.3% BAcc improvement (49.57 vs. 39.32) while requiring 99.3% fewer parameters. Overall, BioX-Bridge consistently matches or exceeds the best baseline on F1-Weighted across all six directions, and its parameter count (0.11M–1.8M) is 1–3 orders of magnitude smaller than the KD baselines (5.7M–136.1M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for unsupervised cross-modal biosignal transfer—knowledge distillation (which requires simultaneous forward passes through both teacher and student models) and data translation (which synthesizes raw signals between modalities)—by introducing a 'model bridging' framework that trains only a lightweight projection network between intermediate representations of two frozen foundation models. Conceptually, it is related to model stitching, but extends it to handle cross-modal, cross-architecture projections where token counts, embedding dimensions, and receptive fields differ substantially. The bridge architecture draws on low-rank factorization and prototype-based aggregation, while the position selection strategy repurposes linear probing (Alain & Bengio, 2016) and linear CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019) as lightweight, data-driven criteria for choosing where to connect the two models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 88–99% reduction in trainable parameters (e.g., 0.4M vs. 30.4M for KD on WESAD PPG→ECG) makes cross-modal transfer feasible on consumer hardware and in privacy-constrained local settings where large foundation models cannot be fine-tuned. The widening performance gap between BioX-Bridge and KD as the foundation model scales (10–17% with ECG-FM at 90M params) suggests the advantage will grow as biosignal foundation models increase in size. Key limitations include the requirement for paired data across modalities, the need for pre-trained models in both modalities, and potential inference-time overhead depending on bridge position. Open questions include extending to unpaired data, task-agnostic bridges, and settings with more than two modalities.
