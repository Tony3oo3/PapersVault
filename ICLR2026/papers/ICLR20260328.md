## Problem

Cooperative MARL under CTDE relies on the IGM principle to ensure decentralized greedy actions recover the team-optimal joint action, but this guarantee breaks under environmental uncertainty from sim-to-real gaps, model mismatch, and system noise. Naively applying single-agent distributionally robust Q-functions to each agent independently fails to preserve IGM because per-agent worst-case models need not coincide with the joint worst-case model. The paper asks whether robust individual action values can be constructed so that decentralized greedy execution remains provably aligned with the robust team-optimal joint action.

## Input / Output

Each agent consumes its local observation-action history (a sequence of observations and past actions) through a DRQN-style recurrent network (MLP encoder, LSTM core, MLP head), while the mixing or factorization network additionally receives the global state (for QMIX) or a joint Q-network (for QTRAN). The scalar robustness parameter rho in (0,1] controls the size of the uncertainty set around the nominal transition kernel. At execution time, each agent independently selects its action by taking argmax over its robust individual Q-values, and the resulting joint action is guaranteed to be a robust team-optimal action without any test-time communication.

## Method

The central contribution is the DrIGM principle, which defines robust individual action values by evaluating each agent's Q-function under the global worst-case model P^worst(h, a_bar) at the robust joint greedy action a_bar, rather than under each agent's own worst-case model. Theorem 1 proves this construction guarantees DrIGM, and Theorem 2 shows it is compatible with VDN (additive), QMIX (monotone mixing), and QTRAN (consistency-constrained) factorization structures. Training proceeds via robust TD losses derived from closed-form robust Bellman operators under two uncertainty sets: rho-contamination (yielding a simple (1-rho) discount on the bootstrap term) and total variation (yielding a dual-variable formulation with an additional dual loss). The overall algorithm retains the standard CTDE information structure of centralized training on global rewards with decentralized execution, requiring no bespoke per-agent reward shaping and integrating directly into existing VDN/QMIX/QTRAN codebases.

## Results

Under the most extreme distribution shift (combined climatic and seasonal shifts in SustainGym), robust value factorization methods achieve 10–40% higher normalized episodic reward than their non-robust counterparts. For example, Robust QTRAN (TV-uncertainty) reaches 0.733 ± 0.026 versus 0.654 ± 0.066 for non-robust QTRAN, and Robust QMIX (TV) reaches 0.520 ± 0.048 versus 0.478 ± 0.052 for non-robust QMIX. On SMAC (3s_vs_5z with observation noise N(0, 0.75²)), robust VDN and QMIX with small ρ significantly improve out-of-distribution win rate over their non-robust baselines, with performance peaking at moderate ρ before degrading under excessive conservatism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two categories of baselines: (1) standard non-robust value factorization (VDN, QMIX, QTRAN) and (2) the GroupDR algorithm from Liu et al. (2025), a multi-agent group distributionally robust method. Under combined climatic and seasonal shifts, all robust variants outperform their non-robust counterparts across all three factorization architectures, with the largest gain for QTRAN (0.733 vs. 0.654, ~12% improvement). Against GroupDR, the proposed methods are consistently superior: GroupDR is highly inconsistent, sometimes performing worse than the non-robust baseline (e.g., QMIX: 0.383 vs. 0.478 non-robust under combined shifts; QMIX: 0.499 vs. 0.895 under seasonal shifts), while the proposed robust methods maintain stable gains. On SMAC, the robust algorithms with small ρ clearly outperform non-robust VDN and QMIX in out-of-distribution win rate, though the advantage diminishes and reverses for large ρ due to excessive conservatism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical IGM principle (Son et al., 2019) that underpins value factorization methods (VDN, QMIX, QTRAN) to the distributionally robust setting, introducing the DrIGM principle. The key conceptual departure is from single-agent DR-RL: the paper demonstrates via counterexample that naively defining per-agent robust Q-values as inf_P Q_i^P breaks decentralized alignment, and instead anchors individual robust values to the global worst-case model evaluated at the robust joint greedy action. The robust Bellman operators for ρ-contamination and TV-uncertainty sets are derived by adapting techniques from Panaganti et al. (2022) to the multi-agent history-based setting, while the overall CTDE architecture and DRQN-style per-agent networks follow standard cooperative MARL practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides the first principled framework for distributionally robust cooperative MARL under CTDE with partial observability and a single team reward, filling a gap where prior robust MARL work either assumed full observability, individual rewards, or targeted Nash equilibria. The DrIGM principle is a clean, general condition that integrates seamlessly with existing VDN/QMIX/QTRAN codebases without bespoke per-agent reward shaping, making it practically adoptable. Open questions include extending DrIGM to agent-wise uncertainty sets, decentralized training paradigms, and scaling to larger agent populations. The observation that robustness in cooperative MARL need not sacrifice in-distribution performance (unlike single-agent robust RL) suggests a broader principle about how coupling and partial observability interact with conservatism.
