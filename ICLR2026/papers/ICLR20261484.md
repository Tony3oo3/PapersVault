## Problem

Standard k-means for text clustering updates centroids by averaging embeddings, producing opaque numeric prototypes that blur contextual nuance and offer no human-readable interpretation of cluster semantics. Recent LLM-based clustering methods improve quality but often require LLM calls that scale with dataset size and lack an explicit optimization objective, making them expensive and hard to analyze. The paper seeks a simple modification to k-means that yields interpretable, human-readable centroids while remaining scalable and LLM-optional.

## Input / Output

The method takes a corpus of text documents, a fixed embedding model (e.g., text-embedding-3-small, e5-large, S-BERT), and a summarization instruction prompt. For k-LLMmeans, only a representative sample of m documents per cluster is selected via k-means++ sampling and passed to the LLM, keeping prompts short and the per-iteration LLM budget fixed at k calls regardless of dataset size. The output consists of cluster assignments, a concise human-readable textual summary for each cluster, and the re-embedded summary vector used as the updated centroid for subsequent assignment steps.

## Method

The core mechanism, summary-as-centroid, periodically replaces the standard k-means mean update (every l iterations) with the embedding of a textual summary of each cluster, computed by the same encoder used for the documents. Two instantiations are provided: k-NLPmeans uses deterministic extractive summarizers (TextRank, LSA/SVD, or centroid-based sentence ranking) with zero LLM cost, while k-LLMmeans queries an LLM with a summarization prompt over a k-means++-sampled subset of cluster documents. Between summarization steps, the algorithm follows vanilla k-means exactly, preserving the standard within-cluster variance objective and ensuring graceful degradation to ordinary k-means if summaries are weak. A mini-batch extension inserts the same summarization steps into the incremental mini-batch k-means update rule, enabling online clustering of streaming text with low memory and bounded LLM usage. The design is modular: any textual summarization operator can be dropped in, and the number of summarization steps and their frequency are tunable hyperparameters.

## Results

On five benchmark datasets using text-embedding-3-small embeddings, k-LLMmeans (FS-multiple, GPT-4o) achieves the best overall performance: 67.9% ACC / 84.1% NMI on Bank77, 80.2% / 93.1% on CLINC, 24.2% / 22.3% on GoEmotions, 63.2% / 70.6% on MASSIVE-Domain, and 56.7% / 73.9% on MASSIVE-Intent. The LLM-free k-NLPmeans (LSA-multiple) is competitive at 67.1% / 84.0% on Bank77 and 63.3% / 70.0% on MASSIVE-Domain with zero LLM calls. When applied to BERTopic embeddings, k-NLPmeans lifts Bank77 ACC from 71.2% to 76.0% and MASSIVE-Domain from 36.1% to 65.0%, demonstrating that the summary-as-centroid step is a strong plug-in improvement over existing topic-modeling pipelines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classical baselines, both k-NLPmeans and k-LLMmeans consistently outperform k-means, k-medoids, GMM, Agglomerative, and Spectral clustering on NMI across all five datasets, with k-LLMmeans FS-multiple leading on ACC in 4 of 5 settings (e.g., CLINC 80.2% vs. k-means 77.3%, MASSIVE-Domain 63.2% vs. 59.4%). BERTopic alone is strong on Bank77 (71.2% ACC) but weak on MASSIVE-Domain (36.1%); applying k-NLPmeans to BERTopic embeddings recovers this gap, reaching 65.0% ACC on MASSIVE-Domain and 76.0% on Bank77. Against state-of-the-art LLM-based clustering (Table 3, e5-large embeddings), k-LLMmeans with GPT-4o achieves 77.2% ACC on CLINC and 62.3% on MASSIVE-Domain using only 750 and 90 LLM calls respectively, versus 83.8% / 60.9% for ClusterLLM (1,618 calls) and 86.8% / 63.1% for LLMEdgeRefine (1,350 calls); the gap in ACC is 2–6 points but the LLM budget is 1.8–15× smaller and independent of dataset size. k-NLPmeans requires zero LLM calls yet remains within 1–2 points of k-LLMmeans on most datasets, offering a strong cost-free alternative. On the streaming StackExchange benchmark (Table 4), mini-batch k-LLMmeans (FS+multiple) achieves 75.4% ACC on 2020 data, surpassing full-dataset k-means (73.4%) while using no more than 3,850 LLM calls across 205,943 posts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical k-means (MacQueen, 1967) and its mini-batch variant (Sculley, 2010) by periodically replacing numeric centroid updates with re-embedded textual summaries, a 'summary-as-centroid' mechanism the authors claim is novel within the k-means loop. It departs from recent LLM-based clustering methods (ClusterLLM, IDAS, LLMEdgeRefine) that scale LLM calls with dataset size or require fine-tuning, instead capping LLM usage at k calls per summarization step independent of corpus size. The LLM-optional design (k-NLPmeans using TextRank, LSA, or centroid-based extractive summarization) positions the method as a lightweight, deterministic alternative that gracefully degrades to vanilla k-means when summaries are weak.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The summary-as-centroid approach offers a practical middle ground: interpretable, human-readable cluster prototypes with a dataset-size-independent LLM budget (under $1 for static datasets vs. $18–25 for competing LLM-heavy methods), plus a released streaming benchmark for sequential text clustering. Open questions include whether multi-prototype or sub-summary extensions can address the minority-topic overshadowing and over-compression failure modes documented in the appendix, and whether the few-shot sampling assumption holds for highly heterogeneous clusters. The method's reliance on LLM summarization quality means biases in the underlying model can propagate to cluster semantics, a concern the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
