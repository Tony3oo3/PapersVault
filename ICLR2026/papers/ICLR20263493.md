## Problem

Open-source multimodal large language models produce significantly lower-quality visual captions than commercial models like GPT-4.1, particularly across diverse domains such as mathematics, documents, medical imaging, and UI screenshots. This gap limits large-scale data synthesis for training, and relying on commercial APIs is prohibitively expensive at scale. The paper asks whether a carefully orchestrated suite of open-source models can close this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263493/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts RGB images (short edge > 512 px, aspect ratio < 2) and videos (resolution > 480p) spanning nine visual domains and 34 subdomains, from natural scenes to code screenshots and medical radiology. CapFlow produces a single structured, domain-aware caption per input by routing through a domain classifier and then executing a domain-specific multi-agent pipeline. MetaCaptioner, the distilled model, takes the same image/video input and directly outputs a detailed caption in one forward pass, with average caption lengths ranging from ~679 words (synthetic/aesthetic) to ~1150 words (code/programming).

## Method

CapFlow is a multi-agent collaboration framework with two components: a domain router (an MLLM performing visual classification into 9 predefined domains with a 3-point confidence score) and a hierarchical captioning workflow where each domain is assigned a distinct set of functional agents (guideline, perception, reasoning, and tool agents like OCR or code parsers) operating in a task-solving layer, whose outputs are aggregated by a summary agent in an information summarization layer. The entire pipeline uses Qwen2.5-VL-72B as functional agents and Qwen2.5-72B as the summarizer, achieving GPT-4.1-comparable caption quality at 10.5% of the cost. MetaCaptioner-8B is then obtained by fine-tuning InternViT-600M + Qwen3-8B-Instruct on MetaCaption-4.1M, a dataset of 4.1 million captions generated by CapFlow and filtered through a strict reject-sampling pipeline that scores each caption on five dimensions (factual accuracy, completeness, reasoning rigor, intent capture, professionalism) using a 3-point scale and retains only captions rated at the top level across all dimensions.

## Results

MetaCaptioner-8B achieves an average score of 71.1 across 11 multimodal benchmarks (MMBench, MMMU, MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, DocVQA, ChartQA, InfoVQA, MMStar, VideoMME, MMBench-Video), outperforming InternVL3.5-8B-Instruct (69.1) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (67.1) while remaining competitive with GLM4.1V-9B (72.4). Under the visual reasoning with LLMs setting, MetaCaptioner-8B paired with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B reaches an average of 49.4, substantially exceeding OmniCaptioner-7B (42.5) and InternVL3.5-8B (38.7). CapFlow achieves a caption quality score of 2.33/3.0 (GPT-5 judged), nearly matching GPT-4.1 (2.35) at only 10.5% of the inference cost ($0.14 vs. $1.47 per 100 samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4.1, CapFlow achieves near-parity caption quality (2.33 vs. 2.35 average on 5 metrics) at 10.5% of the cost, while MetaCaptioner-8B reaches 2.04 average—still well above other open-source captioners. In the visual reasoning with LLMs setting, MetaCaptioner-8B decisively outperforms OmniCaptioner-7B (49.4 vs. 42.5 average with DS-Qwen-7B; 55.3 vs. 48.3 with DS-Qwen-32B), with the largest gaps on MathVista (+9.8) and MMMU (+7.6) at the 32B LLM scale. For direct multimodal evaluation, MetaCaptioner-8B (71.1 avg) surpasses InternVL3.5-8B-Instruct (69.1), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (67.1), InternVL3-8B (66.6), and MiniCPM-V2.6-8B (60.9), but trails GLM4.1V-9B (72.4) by 1.3 points, primarily due to GLM's reinforcement-learning-based training. On specific benchmarks, MetaCaptioner leads on MathVision (52.6 vs. 46.4 for InternVL3.5) and MMBench (80.8 vs. 79.5), while GLM4.1V retains advantages on MathVerse (68.4 vs. 56.5) and ChartQA (70.0 vs. 86.8—actually MetaCaptioner wins here). Against commercial models in Table 11, CapFlow-72B (64.0 overall) slightly trails GPT-5 (65.4) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (65.6) but at a fraction of the cost ($0.14 vs. $1.56/$2.33 per 100 samples).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the InternVL3.5 architecture (InternViT-600M + Qwen3-8B) and training recipe, but departs from prior captioning approaches in its core methodology. Where prior work like ShareGPT4V and OmniCaptioner relies on single-model prompting or multi-source expert merging (SAM, OCR parsers), CapFlow introduces a hierarchical multi-agent workflow with domain routing that decomposes captioning into specialized sub-tasks (perception, reasoning, guideline, tools) orchestrated by 30 functional agents. The reject sampling pipeline for quality filtering extends the synthetic data generation paradigm from prior captioning datasets by adding a multi-dimensional scoring gate that only retains captions achieving professional-level ratings across all criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: MetaCaption-4.1M provides a large-scale, multi-domain caption dataset that the community can use for pre-training and fine-tuning, and MetaCaptioner-8B offers a competitive open-source alternative to commercial captioning at a fraction of the cost. However, the approach's complexity (30 agents, domain routing, hierarchical summarization) raises questions about maintainability and whether the multi-agent decomposition generalizes to new domains without manual prompt engineering. The domain router's 84.8% accuracy on mixed-domain inputs and the persistent gap in factual accuracy (1.46 vs. 2.17 for GPT-4.1 in Table 4) suggest that open-source captioning, while closing the gap, has not fully eliminated it. Open questions include whether the workflow can be simplified, whether the approach transfers to other generation tasks beyond captioning, and whether the 4.1M dataset saturates or whether further scaling yields diminishing returns.
