## Problem

External Test-Time Scaling (TTS) methods select the best reasoning trajectory from multiple candidates using a separate Process Reward Model (PRM), but this introduces two major bottlenecks: the PRM adds billions of extra parameters and significant inference-time computation, and training a high-quality PRM requires expensive process-level annotations. This paper asks whether trajectory generation and scoring can be unified in a single lightweight model that learns process-level discrimination from outcome-level labels alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264050/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (e.g., a math problem or coding task) and produces a final answer. During inference, the policy model first samples k parallel reasoning trajectories (thinking processes delineated by <think>/ tokens), the SPRM head then scores each step within every trajectory using hidden representations from the second-to-last layer at step-boundary tokens (identified by existing '.\n\n' tokens in the tokenizer, requiring no special tokens), and the trajectory with the highest geometric-mean step score is selected to condition the final answer generation. The output thus includes both the selected answer and per-step process scores used for selection.

## Method

The Reflective Generative Model (RGM) unifies the policy model and process reward model in a single network by sharing the transformer backbone and attaching a lightweight SPRM head (Linear→ReLU→Dropout→Linear, ~50M parameters) to the second-to-last layer for step-level scoring. Training jointly optimizes the policy via GRPO and the SPRM head via a novel Self-supervised Process Reward (SPR) Loss, which uses the SPRM's own pseudo-labels (thresholded at 0.5) and only updates steps where the pseudo-label agrees with the final-answer correctness, thereby filtering noisy supervision without any process-level annotations. At inference, k trajectories are sampled, each step is scored by the SPRM head, scores are aggregated via geometric mean (to avoid length bias), and the highest-scoring trajectory guides the final answer. This design eliminates the need for a separate 72B reward model, reducing extra parameters from billions to ~50M while matching or exceeding the performance of large external PRMs.

## Results

On AIME24, QwQ-32B + RGM (54M SPRM params) achieves 84.2%, surpassing OpenAI o3-mini (79.6%) and DeepSeek-R1-671B (79.8%). On HMMT25, the same model reaches 53.1% vs. o3-mini's 53.0%. The SPRM head adds only 5M–54M parameters yet consistently outperforms 72B-parameter reward models (Qwen2.5-Math-RM-72B and Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B) across all five benchmarks and all tested model sizes (1.5B to 32B). R1-Distill-Qwen-7B + RGM reaches 66.3% on AIME24, exceeding OpenAI o1-mini (63.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 72B reward models (Qwen2.5-Math-RM-72B and Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B), RGM with only 5M–54M SPRM parameters wins on all five benchmarks for QwQ-32B (e.g., AIME24: 84.2 vs. 83.3 for PRM-72B; HMMT25: 53.1 vs. 51.7) and for R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (AIME24: 66.3 vs. 60.1, a +6.2 gap). For Qwen3-32B, RGM wins on AIME24, AIME25, BRUMO25, and LiveCodeBench but trails PRM-72B by 0.4 on HMMT25 (56.3 vs. 56.7). Against OpenAI o3-mini, QwQ-32B+RGM wins on AIME24 (84.2 vs. 79.6) and HMMT25 (53.1 vs. 53.0) but loses on AIME25 (73.4 vs. 74.8), BRUMO25 (78.1 vs. 80.0), and LiveCodeBench (64.0 vs. 67.4). R1-Distill-Qwen-7B+RGM (66.3 on AIME24) exceeds OpenAI o1-mini (63.6) by 2.7 points. Against DeepSeek-R1-671B, QwQ-32B+RGM wins on AIME24 (84.2 vs. 79.8), AIME25 (73.4 vs. 70.0), and HMMT25 (53.1 vs. 44.4) but trails on BRUMO25 (78.1 vs. 80.8) and LiveCodeBench (64.0 vs. 65.9). The paper also shows RGM outperforms ImplicitPRM-8B (8B extra params) on all four math benchmarks while using only 5M extra parameters, and beats majority voting by 2.7 points on AIME24.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO policy optimization framework (Shao et al., 2024) and the step-level process reward model paradigm introduced by Lightman et al. (2023), but departs from the standard external TTS pipeline in two key ways. Rather than deploying a separate 72B reward model for trajectory selection, RGM shares the policy model's backbone with a lightweight 50M-parameter SPRM head, unifying generation and evaluation in a single network. It also replaces the costly process-level annotation requirement with a self-supervised loss (SPR Loss) that derives step-level supervision from outcome correctness alone, building on the Monte Carlo estimation idea from Math-Shepherd (Wang et al., 2023) but avoiding its label-noise problem through dynamic pseudo-label filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50M-vs-72B parameter gap, validated across five base architectures (1.5B to 32B) and multiple benchmarks, suggests that process reward modeling may not require a comparably-sized external model when the backbone representations are shared, potentially reshaping how practitioners design TTS pipelines. The self-supervised training removes the expensive process-annotation bottleneck that has limited PRM adoption. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond math to open-ended reasoning, whether the 'aha moment' can be predicted or accelerated, and whether the k=8 candidate requirement introduces unacceptable latency for real-time deployment. The paper acknowledges that MCTS-based step-level search underperforms Best-of-N under limited compute, leaving the optimal search strategy for SPRM-guided TTS unresolved.
