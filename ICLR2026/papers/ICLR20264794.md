## Problem

In offline RL with small or weakly-covered datasets, the standard squared TD error objective induces a harmful cross-time covariance of gradient features that amplifies in out-of-distribution regions, biasing optimization and degrading policy learning. Existing conservative methods address distribution shift but do not target this specific covariance-driven instability mechanism, and under weak coverage they can become overly cautious exactly where improvement is needed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of transitions (state, action, reward, next state, next action) and operates on the critic network's penultimate-layer features as a tractable surrogate for feature-space gradients. It produces a learned policy and value function that are plug-and-play on top of existing backbones (CQL, TD3+BC, etc.), requiring only modifications to the minibatch sampling procedure and an additional loss term, with no architectural changes to the actor or critic networks.

## Method

C4 (Clustered Cross-Covariance Control for TD) decomposes the TD residual variance into two beneficial implicit regularizers (analogous to noisy supervised learning) and one harmful cross-time covariance term, then applies two complementary controls. First, it clusters stacked gradient pairs [g′, g] using a K-component Gaussian mixture fitted via EM, then samples each minibatch from a single cluster, which provably removes the between-cluster covariance driver (Theorem 2). Second, it adds an explicit Frobenius-norm penalty on the within-cluster cross-covariance matrix Ĉ_z(B) to cancel the remaining covariance-induced bias per update. The paper proves that single-cluster sampling preserves the lower-bound property of the CQL-style improvement objective via f-divergence convexity in the second argument, ensuring the method does not alter the core conservative behavior while reducing excessive suppression in extreme OOD regions.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo locomotion tasks restricted to 10k samples (~1% of full data), C4 achieves an average normalized score of 75.7, outperforming the best baseline TSRL (57.2) by 32% and BPPO (56.1) by 35%. C4 also leads on AntMaze (27.0 vs. 22.0 for TSRL) and Maze2D (126.9 vs. 120.6 for BPPO), though it trails BPPO on Adroit (21.6 vs. 23.1). As a plug-in module on CQL and TD3+BC backbones, C4 consistently outperforms alternative regularizers (LN, DR3) across training curves, with the largest gains on medium and medium-replay splits where OOD effects are most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C4 dominates all standard offline RL backbones by wide margins: TD3+BC (31.5), CQL (46.0), and IQL (41.4) all trail C4's locomotion average of 75.7 by 30–44 points, confirming that the cross-covariance issue is not addressed by existing conservative or implicit Q-learning formulations. Among data-efficient baselines, TSRL (57.2) and DOGE (50.7) are the strongest competitors, yet C4 still leads by 18.5 and 25 points respectively, with the gap widening on medium-replay splits (e.g., Hopper-mr: 45.9 vs. 23.5 for TSRL). BPPO (56.1) is the closest overall rival on locomotion and Maze2D (120.6 vs. 126.9), but C4 pulls ahead on AntMaze (27.0 vs. 16.5) and wins the Adroit comparison only narrowly (21.6 vs. 23.1 for BPPO). A2PR (50.6) underperforms C4 across nearly all tasks, particularly on HalfCheetah-e where C4 reaches 75.8 versus A2PR's 36.0. As plug-in regularizers, DR3 and LN provide only moderate or task-dependent gains on CQL and TD3+BC, whereas C4 yields the most consistent and substantial improvements throughout training, validating that explicitly controlling the TD cross-covariance term targets the correct failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the conservative offline RL framework (CQL, TD3+BC) and the line of work identifying implicit regularization effects in deep RL (DR3, LayerNorm), but departs by locating the harmful mechanism at the sampling level rather than the loss or architecture level. While prior clustering-based RL methods (SORL, behavior-aware deep clustering) partition trajectories or states, C4 clusters stacked gradient pairs in feature space and uses single-cluster minibatches to eliminate the between-cluster cross-covariance driver. The theoretical decomposition of TD variance into two beneficial terms (analogous to noisy supervised learning) and one harmful cross-time covariance term is derived directly from the second-moment identity, distinguishing it from Lyapunov-style or gradient-stability analyses in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a harmful implicit regularizer specific to TD learning under OOD conditions provides a mechanistic explanation for training collapse in data-scarce offline RL, complementing existing conservatism-based explanations. The plug-and-play design and the proof that clustered sampling preserves the lower bound of conservative objectives make C4 broadly applicable without altering the core behavior of existing algorithms. Open questions include how the method scales to very large datasets where the cross-covariance effect may be less dominant, whether continuous or adaptive clustering strategies could further improve performance, and how the approach interacts with model-based offline RL methods that have different TD structures.
