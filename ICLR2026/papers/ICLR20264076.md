## Problem

Prior theoretical analyses establish that full-batch Adam converges to the ℓ∞-max-margin solution on separable linear classification data, but these results do not extend to the mini-batch regime that dominates practical training. This paper asks whether Adam's characteristic ℓ∞-bias persists when using one sample per step, and finds that it does not: incremental Adam exhibits a data-dependent implicit bias that can converge to the ℓ2-max-margin, the ℓ∞-max-margin, or an entirely different direction depending on the dataset structure.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a linearly separable dataset {x_i} with all nonzero coordinates, a surrogate loss (logistic or exponential), a polynomially decaying learning rate schedule η_t = (t+2)^{-a} with a ∈ (0,1], and momentum parameters β₁ ≤ β₂. The output is the asymptotic convergence direction w_t/‖w_t‖₂, which for structured datasets (SR, shifted-diagonal) reduces to a standard max-margin solution, but for general datasets is characterized as the primal solution of a parametric Mahalanobis-norm margin problem whose covariance matrix is determined by a data-dependent dual fixed-point equation solved iteratively.

## Method

The key technical tool is an epoch-wise approximation showing that Inc-Adam's update over one full cycle of N samples depends only on the current iterate, taking the form of a weighted preconditioned gradient step where the preconditioner tracks per-sample gradient magnitudes (Proposition 2.5). On Scaled Rademacher data, this preconditioner becomes coordinate-uniform, reducing the dynamics to weighted normalized GD and yielding ℓ2-max-margin convergence (Theorem 3.3). For general datasets, the authors introduce AdamProxy—a uniform-averaging surrogate valid in the β₂→1 limit—and characterize its limit direction via a parametric optimization problem P_Adam(c) that minimizes a data-adaptive Mahalanobis-norm margin, where the covariance matrix M(c) = diag(√(Σ c_j² x_j²)) is determined by a dual fixed-point equation T(c) = c (Theorem 4.8). As a counterpoint, the paper proves that Signum with momentum β close to 1 preserves the ℓ∞-max-margin bias for any batch size, highlighting that the loss of ℓ∞-geometry is specific to Adam's per-coordinate preconditioning under mini-batching (Theorem 5.1).

## Results

The paper proves that incremental Adam (batch size 1) on Scaled Rademacher data converges to the ℓ₂-max-margin solution (Theorem 3.3), in sharp contrast to full-batch Adam's ℓ∞-max-margin bias. For general datasets, the limiting direction of AdamProxy (the β₂→1 proxy) is characterized as the solution of a data-adaptive Mahalanobis-norm max-margin problem whose covariance matrix is determined by a dual fixed-point equation (Theorem 4.8); on shifted-diagonal data this reduces to ℓ∞-max-margin, while on Gaussian data it yields a direction distinct from both ℓ₂- and ℓ∞-max-margin. Signum with momentum β close to 1 maintains ℓ∞-max-margin convergence for all batch sizes (Theorem 5.1), unlike Adam.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full-batch Adam result of Zhang et al. (2024a), which establishes ℓ∞-max-margin convergence, this paper shows mini-batch Adam (batch size 1) fundamentally departs from this behavior: on SR data it converges to ℓ₂-max-margin (cosine similarity → 1 with the ℓ₂ solution), and on Gaussian data it converges to a data-dependent fixed-point direction matching neither standard max-margin solution. Against Signum (Bernstein et al., 2018), the paper demonstrates a key asymmetry: while Adam's ℓ∞-bias is lost under mini-batching, Signum with β=0.99 consistently converges to the ℓ∞-max-margin solution across all tested batch sizes (1, 2, 5, 10), as shown in Figure 5. The fixed-point characterization (Theorem 4.8) is validated empirically on Gaussian data, where Algorithm 3's output accurately predicts the observed convergence direction of both AdamProxy and incremental Adam, confirming the theory's practical relevance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit-bias analysis of Adam from the full-batch regime (Zhang et al., 2024a, who showed full-batch Adam converges to the ℓ∞-max-margin solution) to the mini-batch setting, revealing that the bias becomes data-dependent rather than fixed to a single norm. The epoch-wise approximation technique builds on the coordinate-wise momentum bound from Zou et al. (2023), while the Signum counterpoint generalizes the full-batch ℓ∞-bias result of Bernstein et al. (2018) to arbitrary batch sizes. Conceptually, the paper departs from the SGD analogy (Nacson et al., 2019, where mini-batching preserves the ℓ2-max-margin bias) by showing that adaptive preconditioning interacts non-trivially with mini-batch sampling to produce a richer, dataset-dependent limit direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that mini-batch Adam loses its ℓ∞-max-margin bias challenges the assumption that batch size is orthogonal to an optimizer's implicit bias, and suggests a possible explanation for why Adam's advantage over SGD diminishes at small batch sizes. The data-dependent Mahalanobis-norm margin characterization (via the fixed-point formulation) is a new analytical tool, but its applicability is currently limited to the β₂→1 regime and batch size 1, with incremental Adam serving as a surrogate for more general sampling schemes. Key open questions include extending the framework to general β₂ < 1, larger batch sizes, random reshuffling, and—most importantly—beyond linear models to understand whether the loss of ℓ∞-adaptivity persists in deep networks.
