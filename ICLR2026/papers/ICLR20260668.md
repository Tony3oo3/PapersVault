## Problem

No large-scale, live benchmark exists for evaluating LLM agents on future prediction, where ground-truth answers are genuinely unknown at prediction time. Existing forecasting benchmarks are static, one-time collected, or dominated by simple prediction-market multiple-choice questions, failing to test an agent's ability to synthesize dynamic real-world information and reason under uncertainty across diverse domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260668/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each event is a future prediction question posed on a designated start date, drawn from four types: single-choice (<4 options), multi-choice, open-ended ranking (e.g., top-10 leaderboard), and open-ended numerical prediction (e.g., stock price). Agents receive web search access and produce a prediction answer; scoring occurs only after the resolution date passes, using tier-specific metrics—exact-match (Level 1), F1-score (Level 2), LLM-as-Judge with partial credit for set overlap (Level 3), and clipped MSE normalized by 7-day outcome volatility (Level 4). The one-week prediction window balances event coverage against evaluation latency.

## Method

FutureX operates a fully automated daily pipeline with four stages: (1) event database construction, curating 195 high-quality websites from a pool of 2,008 via LLM-based screening and manual review; (2) daily event curation, where templates with variable inputs (date, target, rank) are randomized to generate ~500 candidate events, then filtered to remove harmful, subjective, and binary yes/no events; (3) agent prediction, automatically running 25 models (base LLMs, SmolAgent-DR, AgentOrchestra, Think&Search LLMs, and Deep Research models) on each event's start date; and (4) answer acquisition, crawling the source website after the resolution date and using Seed1.5-Thinking to extract ground-truth outcomes (>97% success rate). Events are stratified into four difficulty tiers—Basic, Wide Search, Deep Search, and Super Agent—progressively demanding more planning, reasoning, and search capability, with overall scores weighted 10/20/30/40% toward harder tiers.

## Results

Across 25 evaluated models on 1,272 events (July 20–Aug 3), Grok-4 achieves the highest overall FutureX score, followed by Gemini-2.5-flash Deep Research, GPT-o4-mini (Think&Search), and Doubao-Seed1.6-Thinking. Performance declines sharply across the four difficulty tiers: base LLMs score well on Level 1–2 (single/multi-choice) but drop substantially on Level 3–4 (open-ended, high-volatility), where even the strongest models often score near zero. In a human annotation study with 40 industry experts (Big Four, McKinsey, top investment banks), humans significantly outperform all LLM agents on Levels 1, 3, and 4, though some models surpass humans on Level 2 multi-choice events. The answer acquisition success rate of the automated pipeline exceeds 97%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark, FutureX surpasses all six prior future-prediction benchmarks (ForecastQA, Autocast, OpenEPBench, NaviTomorrow, ForecastBench, FutureBench) on every axis: it is the only one combining daily live updates, 11 domains, 195 diverse sources, real (non-simulated) environment, and evaluation of 25 LLM agents. ForecastBench (Karger et al., 2025) is the closest prior work but relies on prediction-market multiple-choice questions, evaluates only vanilla LLMs, and updates monthly; FutureBench (Together.ai, 2025) is limited to ~42 PolyMarket events and a single open-source agent. Among the evaluated agent frameworks, SmolAgent-DR (Roucher et al., 2025) underperforms commercial Think&Search LLMs, likely due to weaker search API capabilities, while AgentOrchestra (Zhang et al., 2025) is only tested on Level 1–2 due to computational cost. Among individual models, Grok-4 outperforms Gemini Deep Research on Level 3–4 events with fewer searches and lower latency (~5 min vs. ~30 min), and Doubao-Seed1.6-Thinking (Seed et al., 2025) leads on Level 1–2 factual recall, even beating search-augmented agents. DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) and Gemini-2.5-pro (Comanici et al., 2025) serve as strong base LLMs but are outperformed by their Think&Search or Deep Research counterparts on harder tiers.

## Relation to Prior Work

FutureX extends the line of future-prediction benchmarks (ForecastQA, Autocast, OpenEPBench, ForecastBench, FutureBench) by shifting from static or limited-scope evaluation to a fully live, daily-updating, multi-domain benchmark that evaluates LLM *agents* rather than vanilla LLMs. It draws conceptually on the live-benchmark paradigm (LiveBench, LiveCodeBench) and the agent-evaluation literature (AgentBench, WebArena, BrowseComp), but departs from all of them by requiring predictions about genuinely unresolved future events with automated ground-truth collection, eliminating data contamination by design. The four-tier difficulty structure and the inclusion of open-ended ranking and numerical prediction events go beyond the multiple-choice and prediction-market formats that dominate prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FutureX provides the first scalable, contamination-free, live evaluation standard for LLM agents on real-world forecasting, filling a gap that has limited progress in this area. The finding that even the strongest models (Grok-4, Gemini Deep Research) struggle substantially on Level 3–4 events and remain well below 40 industry experts (Big Four, McKinsey, top investment banks) underscores that current agents are far from professional-analyst-level forecasting. Open questions include whether purpose-built agents (as seen in the weekly competition with MiroFlow/GPT-5) can close the human gap, how the benchmark scales as more domains and higher-volatility events are added, and whether the LLM-as-Judge and clipped-MSE metrics adequately capture nuanced forecasting quality for open-ended tasks.
