## Problem

Existing Spiking Self-Attention (SSA) mechanisms are designed for 2D visual tasks and fail on 3D point clouds because they smooth over salient edge features by prioritizing similar points, become computationally prohibitive at point-cloud scale, and cannot simultaneously capture local and global spatial structure. This paper addresses the need for an efficient, discrepancy-based spiking attention mechanism tailored to the unordered, irregular geometry of 3D point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263725/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unordered 3D point clouds (e.g., 1024 points for classification, 24000 for S3DIS segmentation) where each point has (x, y, z) coordinates. Spatial coordinates are encoded into the initial membrane potential of spiking neurons via non-learnable trigonometric functions before network processing. Outputs are class logits for classification, per-point semantic class logits for S3DIS (13 classes), and per-point part logits for ShapeNetPart (50 parts), all produced through spiking MLP heads operating on binary spike features across T time steps and C channels.

## Method

The core contribution is the Spiking Discrepancy Attention Mechanism (SDAM), which replaces dot-product similarity with spiking discrepancy (subtraction between spike sequences) to highlight discriminative edge features and satisfy translation invariance. SDAM has two variants: Spiking Element Discrepancy Attention (SEDA) computes channel-wise spiking differences between a center point and its KNN neighbors for local geometric discriminability, while Spiking Intensity Discrepancy Attention (SIDA) computes differences in cumulative spike firing rates across all points for global structural modeling. A Spatially-Aware Spiking Neuron (SASN) encodes 3D coordinates into the initial membrane potential using sinusoidal position encoding, compensating for spatial information lost in binary spike representations. The full Spiking Discrepancy Transformer (SDT) is a 4-stage hierarchical encoder where SEDA operates in early stages and SIDA in later stages, with FPS-based downsampling and KNN neighborhood aggregation at each stage, achieving SOTA SNN performance with only 2.25M parameters and ~1.33 mJ theoretical energy on ModelNet40.

## Results

On ModelNet40, SDT (T=4) achieves 92.46% OA and 89.48% mAcc with only 2.25M parameters and 1.33 mJ theoretical energy consumption. On the more challenging ScanObjectNN (PB_T50_RS), SDT reaches 86.19% OA and 84.37% mAcc at 2.11 mJ. For segmentation, SDT attains 69.6% mIoU on S3DIS and 83.7% category mIoU / 85.1% instance mIoU on ShapeNetPart, setting new SNN records on both benchmarks. The energy consumption is 1.8% of PointMLP (1.33 vs. 72.38 mJ) on ModelNet40 and 10.3% of Point-GPT (2.11 vs. 20.47 mJ) on ScanObjectNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDT outperforms all SNN baselines on both classification benchmarks: it surpasses the previous best SNN E-3DSNN by +0.76% OA on ModelNet40 (92.46% vs. 91.70%) and by +2.28% OA on ScanObjectNN (86.19% vs. 83.91%), while using fewer parameters (2.25M vs. 3.27M) and less energy (1.33 mJ vs. 1.76 mJ on ModelNet40). Against ANN methods, SDT slightly trails PointMLP (92.46% vs. 94.10%) and Point-GPT (86.19% vs. 86.90%) on accuracy, but consumes only 1.8% and 10.3% of their energy respectively. SDT actually exceeds PointNet++ (92.46% vs. 91.90%), KPConv (92.46% vs. 92.10%), and Point Transformer (92.46% vs. 93.70% on ModelNet40; 86.19% vs. 86.01% on ScanObjectNN) in OA while using far fewer parameters and dramatically less energy. On S3DIS segmentation, SDT achieves 69.6% mIoU, beating the best SNN E-3DSNN (67.4%) by 2.2% and approaching the ANN SOTA of Point Transformer V3 (73.6%) at only 1.06% of its energy. On ShapeNetPart, SDT reaches 83.7% cat. mIoU / 85.1% ins. mIoU, outperforming E-3DSNN (81.7% / 83.8%) and matching Point Transformer (83.7% / 86.6%) at 3.7% of its energy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on Spikformer (Zhou et al., ICLR 2023), which introduced Spiking Self-Attention (SSA) for 2D visual tasks, but fundamentally departs from its dot-product similarity mechanism. The authors argue that SSA's similarity-based aggregation is ill-suited for 3D point clouds because it smooths over discriminative edge features, scales poorly to the large point counts typical of 3D data, and cannot simultaneously model local and global structure. Their replacement—the Spiking Discrepancy Attention Mechanism (SDAM)—uses subtraction-based spike differences (element-wise for local SEDA, intensity-level for global SIDA) instead of dot products, and pairs this with a Spatially-Aware Spiking Neuron that injects positional encoding into the initial membrane potential, a design absent from prior spiking transformer work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first Spiking Transformer purpose-built for 3D point clouds, filling a gap where prior SNN approaches (Spiking PointNet, P2SResLNet, E-3DSNN) showed substantially weaker performance. The theoretical energy savings (1.8% of PointMLP on ModelNet40) are compelling for neuromorphic deployment, though they rest on 45nm hardware assumptions and sparsity estimates rather than measured chip-level power. Key open questions include whether the discrepancy-based attention generalizes beyond point clouds to other irregular or high-dimensional data, whether the energy advantage materializes on actual neuromorphic hardware, and whether the point-cloud-specific design (FPS/KNN sampling, SEDA/SIDA hierarchy) can be adapted to 2D or other modalities without losing its advantages.
