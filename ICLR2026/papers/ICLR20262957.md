## Problem

While SDEs have become a standard tool for analyzing non-private optimizers, they had never been applied to differentially private methods, leaving the fundamental question of how DP noise interacts with adaptivity largely unanswered. In particular, it was unclear in which privacy regimes adaptive optimizers (e.g., DP-SignSGD, DP-Adam) outperform non-adaptive ones (DP-SGD), and how the privacy budget ε shapes convergence speed and the privacy-utility trade-off for each class.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a DP optimizer (DP-SGD or DP-SignSGD) operating on a differentiable loss f with per-example clipping threshold C, batch size B, and privacy budget ε, and produces explicit convergence rate bounds, privacy-utility trade-off scalings as functions of ε, optimal learning rates, and stationary distributions. The noise model distinguishes two phases: a clipped regime where per-example gradients follow a heavy-tailed Student-t distribution, and an unclipped regime where batch-averaged noise is Gaussian. Results are derived under two protocols—fixed hyperparameters (Protocol A) and per-ε optimal tuning (Protocol B)—and empirically validated on logistic regression over IMDB and StackOverflow text datasets.

## Method

The paper derives first-order weak SDE approximations for DP-SGD and DP-SignSGD under per-example clipping, separating the dynamics into a fully-clipped phase (Student-t noise) and an unclipped phase (Gaussian noise), then unifying them via a mixed-phase bound. The key structural insight is that the sign operator in DP-SignSGD effectively caps the magnitude of the privatized gradient update, making the method inherently robust to the scale of DP noise: its convergence speed scales linearly with ε and its privacy-utility trade-off is O(1/ε), whereas DP-SGD's speed is ε-independent but its trade-off degrades as O(1/ε²). Under optimal tuning (Protocol B), the optimal learning rate of DP-SGD scales linearly with ε while that of DP-SignSGD is essentially ε-independent, meaning adaptive methods transfer across privacy levels without re-tuning. The analysis further identifies a critical threshold ε* (depending on batch noise σ_γ and batch size B) below which DP-SignSGD dominates in utility, and above which DP-SGD is preferable.

## Results

Under fixed hyperparameters (Protocol A), DP-SGD converges at a speed independent of ε with a privacy-utility trade-off of O(1/ε²), while DP-SignSGD converges at a speed linear in ε with a milder O(1/ε) trade-off, making adaptivity preferable in high-privacy regimes. Under optimal tuning (Protocol B), the optimal learning rate of DP-SGD scales as η* ∝ ε, whereas that of DP-SignSGD is ε-independent; with this tuning, both methods achieve comparable asymptotic performance of Õ(C√(dLf(X₀))/(εn)). Empirically, on logistic regression over IMDB and StackOverflow, the observed asymptotic loss of DP-SGD scales as 1/ε² while DP-SignSGD (and DP-Adam) scales as 1/ε, confirming the theoretical predictions across training and test metrics.

**Summary of key theoretical results (Protocol A: fixed hyperparameters; Protocol B: best-tuned per ε).**

| Property | DP-SGD | DP-SignSGD | DP-Adam (empirical) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Convergence speed (Prot. A) | Independent of ε | ∝ ε (slower for small ε) | ∝ ε (matches SignSGD) |
| Privacy-utility trade-off (Prot. A) | O(1/ε²) | O(1/ε) | O(1/ε) |
| Optimal η* (Prot. B) | ∝ ε | ε-independent | ε-independent |
| Asymptotic bound (Prot. B) | Õ(C√(dLf(X₀))/(εn)) | Õ(C√(dLf(X₀))/(εn)) | Comparable |
| Divergence risk (small ε, Prot. A) | Yes (sharp degradation) | No (never diverges) | No |
| Batch noise σ²_γ ≥ B | Outperformed by SignSGD | Always dominates | Always dominates |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DP-SGD (Abadi et al., 2016), DP-SignSGD achieves a strictly better privacy-utility trade-off in the high-privacy regime: O(1/ε) versus O(1/ε²), meaning that as ε → 0, the utility loss of DP-SGD degrades quadratically while DP-SignSGD degrades only linearly. Under Protocol A, DP-SignSGD always dominates when batch noise is large (σ²_γ ≥ B), and also wins for strict privacy (ε < ε*) when batch noise is small; DP-SGD only recovers the advantage for looser privacy (ε > ε*). Against DP-Adam (Zhou et al., 2020), the paper shows empirically that DP-Adam follows the same ε-scaling as DP-SignSGD (O(1/ε) trade-off, ε-independent optimal learning rate), confirming that the theoretical insights for DP-SignSGD extend to the more widely used DP-Adam. Under Protocol B with careful tuning, all three methods (DP-SGD, DP-SignSGD, DP-Adam) reach comparable asymptotic performance, but DP-SGD is fragile: if the hyperparameter grid misses the ε-dependent η*, its performance degrades sharply, whereas adaptive methods retain a portable learning rate across privacy levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDE approximation framework of Li et al. (2017) — previously applied exclusively to non-private optimizers — to the differentially private setting, providing the first continuous-time analysis of DP-SGD and DP-SignSGD. It builds on the DP-SGD formulation of Abadi et al. (2016) and the adaptive optimizer literature (Adam, SignSGD), but departs from prior DP convergence analyses (Koloskova et al. 2023, Zhou et al. 2020) by incorporating per-example clipping with a heavy-tailed Student-t noise model and by isolating the structural role of the sign operator in absorbing privacy noise. The paper positions itself as explaining the mechanism behind empirical observations in prior work (Li et al. 2022b, Zhou et al. 2020) that adaptive and non-adaptive DP optimizers sometimes match and sometimes diverge in performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of when adaptive DP optimizers are preferable to non-adaptive ones, resolving an open question that prior empirical work could only observe but not explain. The practical implication that DP-SignSGD's (and empirically DP-Adam's) optimal learning rate is ε-independent while DP-SGD's scales linearly with ε has direct consequences for hyperparameter search cost under DP, where each evaluation consumes privacy budget. Limitations include theory developed for DP-SignSGD as a proxy for Adam, experiments restricted to logistic regression on tabular data, and reliance on high-dimensionality assumptions (d = Ω(10⁴)). Open questions include extending the SDE framework to deeper architectures, non-convex losses, and more complex DP optimizers with momentum or second-moment tracking.
