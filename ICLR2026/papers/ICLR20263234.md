## Problem

Existing DRO ambiguity sets face a fundamental tension: φ-divergence-based sets (e.g., KL) require adversarial distributions to share the nominal support, degrading robustness under support shifts, while Wasserstein-based sets allow support shifts but are intractable over infinite probability spaces. This paper asks whether generative models can define ambiguity sets that are simultaneously expressive (capturing diverse worst-case distributions beyond the nominal support) and tractable (solvable in a finite parameterized space).

## Input / Output

The method takes an empirical dataset S0 drawn from a nominal distribution P0, an adversarial budget ε, and a decision variable w, and outputs a robust w optimized against worst-case distributions within the generative ambiguity set. In the time-series experiments, input is an n-step sliding window of hourly carbon intensity (gCO2eq/kWh) from Electricity Maps across four regions, and output is the predicted emission at step n+1. In image classification, input is a 28×28 MNIST or USPS digit image and output is a class label. The generative model (diffusion or VAE) is first trained on P0, then its parameters are perturbed within the reconstruction-loss budget to search for adversarial distributions.

## Method

GAS-DRO defines the ambiguity set as {P_θ : J(θ, P0) ≤ ε}, where J is the reconstruction loss of a likelihood-based generative model (diffusion or VAE) and P_θ is its sampling distribution. The key theoretical insight (Lemma 1) is that the inclusive KL divergence D_KL(P0 ‖ P_θ) is bounded by J(θ, P0) plus prior-matching and entropy terms, guaranteeing consistency with P0 while permitting broader support than P0. The inner maximization over θ is solved via Lagrangian dual learning (adaptive μ updates) combined with PPO-style policy optimization, where the diffusion backward process is treated as a policy and the probability ratio r_θ(x_{0:T}) = P_θ(x_{0:T})/P_{θ_old}(x_{0:T}) enables stable gradient-based updates. The outer minimization performs gradient descent on w using samples generated by the current worst-case generative model. Theorem 1 bounds the inner maximization error as O(1/√K) and shows the inclusive KL divergence stays within budget, while Theorem 2 establishes stationary convergence of the full algorithm via Moreau envelope analysis.

## Results

On the Electricity Maps time-series forecasting task (14 OOD test sets across 4 regions and multiple years), GAS-DRO achieves an average MSE of 0.0163 and worst-case MSE of 0.0509, outperforming all baselines. Compared to the standard ML baseline (avg. MSE 0.0450), GAS-DRO yields a 63.7% reduction in MSE. The advantage is most pronounced under large distribution shifts (e.g., QLD_21 with Wasserstein distance 0.3054: GAS-DRO 0.0186 vs. ML 0.0696), and GAS-DRO surpasses the next-best DRO method (DRAGEN, avg. MSE 0.0230) by an average margin of 25.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAS-DRO achieves the lowest MSE on all 14 OOD test sets, winning every head-to-head comparison. Against DRAGEN (Ren & Majumdar, 2022), which also uses a generative model but within a Wasserstein-DRO framework, GAS-DRO reduces average MSE from 0.0230 to 0.0163 (29% relative improvement), with the gap widening under larger distribution shifts (e.g., QLD_21: 0.0186 vs. 0.0288). Against P-DRO (Michel et al., 2021), which parameterizes adversarial distributions via a generative model but remains constrained by the KL-divergence ambiguity set (requiring shared support), GAS-DRO improves average MSE from 0.0259 to 0.0163 (37% relative improvement). Against the classic KL-DRO (Hu & Hong, 2013), GAS-DRO reduces average MSE from 0.0288 to 0.0163 (43% relative improvement), and against W-DRO/FWDRO (Staib & Jegelka, 2017), the improvement is from 0.0342 to 0.0163 (52% relative improvement). The paper attributes its advantage to defining the ambiguity set directly in the parameterized space of the generative model, which avoids both the support-restriction of KL-based sets and the intractability/over-conservativeness of Wasserstein-based relaxations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant ambiguity-set paradigms in DRO—φ-divergence balls (which enforce absolute continuity and restrict support) and Wasserstein balls (which allow support shifts but are intractable over infinite probability spaces)—by defining the ambiguity set directly in the finite parameterized space of a likelihood-based generative model (diffusion model or VAE). Prior work that incorporated generative models into DRO (DRAGEN, Michel et al.) still operated within a Wasserstein or KL-divergence outer framework, inheriting their respective limitations; here the constraint is simply a bounded reconstruction loss J(θ, P₀), and the inner maximization is solved via PPO-style policy optimization over the generative parameters. The theoretical analysis draws on dual gradient descent convergence (Lemma 2) and Moreau-envelope stationary-point arguments from nonconvex minimax optimization (Jin et al. 2020), adapted to the stochastic, finite-sample setting of generative-model sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a tractable DRO algorithm whose adversarial distributions can live outside the nominal support while remaining provably close to P₀ in inclusive KL divergence, addressing the expressiveness–tractability tension that has limited both KL-DRO and Wasserstein-DRO in deep-learning settings. Open questions include the computational cost of running K inner PPO iterations per outer step (the paper acknowledges this in Appendix D.4.6), the sensitivity of performance to the generative model's finite expressivity (quantified via the Γ-expressivity parameter but only loosely bounded), and whether the framework extends cleanly to other likelihood-based architectures beyond diffusion models and VAEs. The empirical validation, while showing a 63.7% average MSE reduction over vanilla ML on Electricity Maps time-series forecasting, is concentrated on a single primary task with modest dataset sizes, leaving open how the method scales to high-dimensional image or language domains.
