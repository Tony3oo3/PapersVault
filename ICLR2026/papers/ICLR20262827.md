## Problem

Standard decentralized GAIL for multi-agent traffic simulation is notoriously unstable, and prior remedies (parameter sharing, curriculum learning) do not address the root cause. This paper identifies 'irrelevant interaction misguidance': the discriminator penalizes an ego vehicle's realistic behavior because of unrealistic neighbor-neighbor or neighbor-map interactions that are only weakly correlated with the ego's own action, and the severity grows with neighborhood size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262827/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes HD map tokens (spatially encoded road geometry) and per-agent past motion-token sequences (categorical tokens over a shared motion vocabulary) at 10 Hz, and outputs future motion-token distributions for up to 128 agents over an 8-second horizon. The decomposed discriminator produces two separate scalar scores per agent per timestep: a scene-realism score from ego-map features and a set of pairwise interaction-realism scores from ego-neighbor features, which are combined with distance-decayed weights into a single reward signal.

## Method

DecompGAIL replaces the monolithic PS-GAIL discriminator with a decomposed architecture that computes scene realism (ego-map) via temporal and map-agent attention features and interaction realism (ego-neighbor) via pairwise temporal features with relative positional encoding, explicitly omitting neighbor-neighbor and neighbor-map terms by design. Interaction scores are aggregated with distance-decayed weights (α·exp(−d/β)) to emphasize causally relevant nearby agents. A social PPO objective then augments each agent's ego reward with distance-weighted neighborhood rewards, encouraging agents to improve their own realism without degrading nearby agents. Training proceeds in two stages: BC pretraining on expert trajectories for 32 epochs, followed by 2 epochs of adversarial fine-tuning where the discriminator is updated with a weighted BCE loss over both scene and interaction scores, and the policy is optimized with PPO using GAE-estimated advantages on the social reward, combined with a BC regularization loss.

## Results

On the WOSAC 2025 leaderboard test split (WOMD), DecompGAIL achieves the best realism meta-metric of 0.7864 and the highest interactive score of 0.8152, outperforming the closest competitor SMART-R1 (0.7858) by 0.0006. It also leads on kinematic (0.4919) and map-based (0.9176) sub-metrics, though its minADE (1.4209) is the highest among all methods, which the authors attribute to prioritizing distributional matching over pointwise distance. On the 2% validation split, the full DecompGAIL model reaches a meta-metric of 0.7889 with a collision likelihood of 0.9837, compared to 0.7836 and 0.9667 for the BC-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DecompGAIL wins on the primary meta-metric and the interactive sub-metric against all eight baselines. The closest competitor is SMART-R1 (R1-style RL fine-tuning), which trails by only 0.0006 in meta-metric but is outperformed on interactive (0.8152 vs. 0.8110) and map-based (0.9176 vs. 0.9201, where SMART-R1 actually leads slightly). Against RL fine-tuning methods (RLFTSim, LLM2AD), DecompGAIL gains 0.0007–0.0085 in meta-metric, with the largest margin over LLM2AD (0.7864 vs. 0.7779). Against BC-based methods with data augmentation (CLSFT, UniMM, TrajTok), the meta-metric advantage ranges from 0.0012 to 0.0035, and the interactive gap is consistently 0.003–0.006. Over the naive BC baseline (SMART-tiny), DecompGAIL improves the meta-metric by 0.005 and interactive by 0.006, confirming that the adversarial fine-tuning stage addresses covariate shift. The one metric where DecompGAIL underperforms is minADE (1.4209 vs. 1.28–1.32 for most baselines), which the authors explain as a consequence of optimizing for trajectory-distribution matching rather than pointwise distance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-agent GAIL line of work (Song et al., 2018; Bhattacharyya et al., 2018) that has used parameter sharing and curriculum learning to stabilize adversarial imitation in traffic simulation, but departs by diagnosing a specific root cause—irrelevant interaction misguidance—where the monolithic discriminator over-penalizes ego behavior due to unrealistic neighbor-neighbor and neighbor-map interactions. The solution is a decomposed discriminator architecture that architecturally suppresses these higher-order terms by separately scoring ego-map (scene) and ego-neighbor (interaction) realism, combined with a distance-weighted social PPO objective. The method is instantiated within the SMART tokenized Transformer backbone (Wu et al., 2024), building on the broader trend of next-token-prediction traffic models (TrajGlish, TrajTok, CAT-K) while adding an adversarial fine-tuning stage that prior tokenized models lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By naming and isolating the irrelevant interaction misguidance mechanism, the paper provides a principled explanation for why multi-agent GAIL has been notoriously unstable, offering a clean architectural fix rather than ad-hoc stabilization tricks. The SOTA result on WOSAC 2025 validates the approach, though the margin over the second-place method (0.7889 vs. 0.7858) is modest. Open questions include whether the decomposition generalizes beyond traffic to other multi-agent domains (robotics, crowd simulation), whether the social reward design is near-optimal, and whether the slight minADE degradation (prioritizing distributional realism over point accuracy) is acceptable for downstream planning tasks. The finding that gradient penalties harm DecompGAIL by smoothing away sharp collision/off-road boundaries is a useful caution for practitioners applying WGAN-style regularization to safety-critical discriminators.
