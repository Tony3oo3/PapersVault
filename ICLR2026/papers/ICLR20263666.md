## Problem

Selective prediction for vision-language foundation models has been largely unexplored for open-set tasks (captioning, image-text matching) where the label space is unbounded, unlike the closed-set classification and VQA settings dominating prior work. Even when using external CLIP-style models as plug-and-play confidence scorers (PAPSP), two fundamental issues degrade performance: high variance in image-text embeddings due to representation instability, and poor calibration of cosine similarity scores across the embedding space. The paper asks whether these can be addressed without any training of either the predictive VLM or the scoring model.

## Input / Output

Given an RGB image and a text prediction (caption, class label, or chosen sentence) from any VLM, MA-PAPSP produces a scalar confidence score in [0,1] that is thresholded to yield an accept/reject decision. The method additionally requires a retrieval dataset of image-text pairs (e.g., CC12M+CC3M+SBU-1M for out-of-domain, or task-specific training splits for in-domain), from which K nearest neighbors are retrieved at inference time. For captioning tasks, a set of hard-negative captions is also generated (via rule-based noun replacement or a small language model) to enable contrastive normalization; for classification and ITM, the existing label set serves this role.

## Method

MA-PAPSP augments a frozen SP-VLM (e.g., SigLIP) with two training-free mechanisms. First, proxy embeddings reduce representation instability by retrieving K nearest-neighbor image-text pairs from an external dataset and computing a similarity-weighted average of their embeddings as a stable proxy for the query, rather than relying on a single noisy projection. Second, contrastive scores address miscalibration by normalizing the cosine similarity between the image proxy and the predicted caption against similarities to a set of hard-negative captions (generated by replacing key nouns), yielding a softmax-like score in [0,1] with more uniform magnitudes across the embedding space. The architecture supports four retrieval variants (image-to-text, image-to-image, text-to-text, text-to-image) and is entirely plug-and-play: no parameters of either the predictive VLM or the SP-VLM are updated, and the method can be attached to any foundation model by simply swapping the retrieval dataset to match the target domain.

## Results

MA-PAPSP achieves the lowest AURC (↓) across all 12 evaluation datasets spanning captioning, classification, and image-text matching. With the large SP-VLM (SigLIP_SO-400M), MA-PAPSP-L reaches 0.109 AURC on MS-COCO (Cider-N), 0.063 on Flowers, 0.088 on UCF-101, and 0.062 on SugarCrepe. Notably, MA-PAPSP with a small 16M-parameter SP-VLM (SigLIP_B/16) outperforms PAPSP with a 1B-parameter SP-VLM on all captioning and classification tasks, with percentage gains over PAPSP ranging from 13% to 51% depending on the dataset. The method also generalizes to specialized domains, achieving 0.126 AURC on CheXpert and 0.124 on MIMIC-CXR with BioMedCLIP and domain-specific retrieval sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MA-PAPSP decisively outperforms both external ITA scoring baselines across all 12 datasets. On MS-COCO captioning (Cider-N), MA-PAPSP-L achieves 0.109 AURC versus 0.146 for VQAScore (25% lower) and 0.158 for SeeTRUE (31% lower); on UCF-101 classification, the gap widens to 0.088 vs. 0.217 (VQAScore) and 0.171 (SeeTRUE). Even the smallest MA-PAPSP variant (16M-parameter SigLIP_B/16) beats both baselines on every task, despite VQAScore and SeeTRUE relying on multiple expensive LLM inferences per sample. The paper also shows that MA-PAPSP with a small SP-VLM outperforms PAPSP with a 63× larger SP-VLM (1B vs. 16M parameters) on all captioning and classification tasks, highlighting that retrieval augmentation is more effective than simply scaling the SP-VLM. Gains are largest for fine-grained classification (up to 51% on SugarCrepe) and moderate for ITM tasks (4–6% on WinoGround, What'sUp, VL-Checklist, Foil), suggesting the proxy-embedding and contrastive-score mechanisms are most beneficial when the output vocabulary is small and discriminative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the selective prediction literature (Chow 1957; Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017; Wang & Vasconcelos 2018), which has focused almost exclusively on closed-set classification and finite-choice VQA, to the open-set regime of foundation models where output vocabularies are unbounded (e.g., captioning). The base mechanism is the CLIP-score (Hessel et al., 2021), but the key departure is the retrieval-augmented proxy embedding and contrastive normalization, drawing on the broader retrieval-augmented generation lineage (Lewis et al., 2020; Iscen et al., 2023a,b) while repurposing it for confidence scoring rather than representation learning. It explicitly positions against heavier alternatives like LLM-as-judge (Dong et al., 2024) and VQA-based alignment scorers (VQAScore, SeeTRUE), arguing for a lightweight, training-free plug-and-play module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MA-PAPSP provides a practical, zero-training confidence-scoring mechanism that can be attached to any VLM, which is important as foundation models are increasingly deployed in safety-critical settings where abstention is needed. The finding that a 16M-parameter SP-VLM with retrieval augmentation outperforms a 1B-parameter SP-VLM without it suggests that data-driven calibration can substitute for model scale, a useful insight for resource-constrained deployments. The main open question is retrieval set selection: performance depends on domain overlap, and while generic 15M-sample datasets work well for language-heavy tasks, specialized domains (e.g., medical imaging) still benefit from targeted data, requiring practitioner trial-and-error. Whether the contrastive normalization generalizes to truly novel out-of-distribution inputs beyond the retrieval set's coverage remains untested.
