## Problem

Existing organic reaction prediction methods—ranging from template-based GNNs to fine-tuned LLMs—are purely data-driven, exhibit limited interpretability, and have plateaued in performance. In particular, chemical LLMs trained with standard supervised fine-tuning fail to exploit the rich pre-trained chemical knowledge and reasoning capabilities of large models, and the field lacks both high-quality structured reasoning data and advanced learning strategies (RAG, RL) tailored to reaction prediction.

## Input / Output

The system takes reaction inputs as SMILES strings (reactants, solvents, reagents) and produces a predicted product SMILES along with a step-by-step chain-of-thought reasoning trace. Internally, a two-layer MLP classifier first maps concatenated molecular fingerprints (RDK, LAYERED, PATTERN, AVALON, MORGAN, computed via RDKit) to a reaction type label and a compact molecular embedding; this embedding then drives a Euclidean-distance similarity search (threshold M=10) that routes each sample to either the RAG or reasoning pathway. The RAG pathway additionally injects the predicted reaction type and retrieved similar-case SMILES into the prompt, while the reasoning pathway generates a multi-step mechanistic explanation before the final product.

## Method

Reaction-Thinker is a hybrid system with two specialized predictors selected by a retrieval-based router. For the RAG pathway, a similar-case retrieval database is built per reaction type using molecular-embedding distances on the ORD training set; Qwen3-32B is then SFT-trained on prompts augmented with the reaction type and retrieved analogous reactions, yielding 94.70% Exact Match on the 81.7% of test samples with retrievals. For the reasoning pathway, a 694K-sample CoT dataset is constructed in two stages—Stage 1 uses Qwen2.5-72B to generate mechanistic reasoning from USPTO-MIT reactions (119K samples), and Stage 2 distills and validates via SFT+GRPO on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B over ORD (575K samples)—then DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B is SFT-trained on this CoT data and further optimized with GRPO using a custom four-component reward (format, length, validity, accuracy). The GRPO stage with LoRA adapters provides a 13.9% relative accuracy gain over SFT alone, and the combined system achieves 89.86% Exact Match on ORD, surpassing all compared LLMs and task-specific models.

## Results

On the ORD test set (86K samples), Reaction-Thinker achieves 89.86% Exact Match and 98.92% Validity, outperforming all compared baselines. It also attains the highest fingerprint similarity scores: 95.22% (MORGAN), 96.24% (RDK), and 96.37% (AVALON). The system combines a RAG-based predictor (94.70% EM on 81.7% of samples with retrieved similar cases) and a reasoning-based predictor (68.24% EM on the remaining 18.3%). On USPTO-MIT, Reaction-Thinker reaches 92.13% Exact Match (90.90% on the filtered subset excluding training-overlapping samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Reaction-Thinker outperforms all 10 baselines on Exact Match and all fingerprint similarity metrics. Among task-specific models, it surpasses Chemformer (88.13% EM) by 1.73 points and Molecular Transformer (85.84%) by 4.02 points, while also achieving higher FTS scores across all three fingerprint types. Among chemical LLMs, the gap is substantial: ChemDFM-13B (52.41%), ChemDFM-8B (48.02%), and Text-Chem-T5 (47.88%) all trail by over 37 points in Exact Match. General-purpose LLMs perform markedly worse, with GPT-4o (28.26% on a 500-sample subset) being the strongest, followed by DeepSeek-R1 (11.68%), DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B (7.20%), and the Qwen/DeepSeek distilled variants (0.54%–6.52%). The paper also reports a Retro Validity metric (retrosynthetic validation of predicted products), where Reaction-Thinker achieves 93.89% versus 92.91% for Chemformer and 58.60% for ChemDFM-13B, further confirming its advantage in generating chemically plausible products.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of chemical LLMs (ChemDFM, ChemLLM, ChemCrow) by departing from purely data-driven fine-tuning toward a hybrid knowledge-and-data-driven architecture. It adapts GRPO (originally developed for mathematical reasoning in DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1) to organic reaction prediction, and introduces a similar-case retrieval database that feeds a RAG-based predictor (Qwen3-32B) alongside a reasoning-based predictor (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B) trained with SFT + GRPO. The system is positioned against both general-purpose LLMs and established task-specific models (Chemformer, Molecular Transformer), which it surpasses in Exact Match and fingerprint similarity on ORD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 47.8% of 'incorrect' predictions are chemically plausible (validated via Retro* retrosynthesis) exposes a fundamental limitation of single-ground-truth evaluation in reaction prediction and motivates a more realistic benchmarking paradigm. The reward-hacking failure mode—where adding a fingerprint-similarity reward to GRPO actually *decreased* accuracy (68.24% → 56.83%) by incentivizing reactant copying—provides an important caution for RL-based chemical LLM training. Open questions include closing the large gap between the RAG pathway (94.70%) and reasoning pathway (68.24%), designing reward functions that avoid structural shortcuts, and unifying RAG and reasoning into a single architecture.
