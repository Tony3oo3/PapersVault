## Problem

In Diffusion Transformers for video generation, attention dominates computation due to quadratic complexity over sequence lengths of 10K–100K. Existing sparse attention methods plateau at 40–60% sparsity for moderate sequences, while linear attention alone severely degrades video quality. The paper identifies that attention weights decompose into a small high-rank sparse component and a large very-low-rank component, but no prior method exploits both structures simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264686/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

SLA operates as a drop-in replacement for the attention layer inside a DiT (Wan2.1-1.3B for 5-second 480p video, LightningDiT-1.0B for 512×512 images). It consumes the standard Q, K, V projections (N×d, with N up to ~32K for video) plus precomputed φ(Q) and φ(K) feature maps, and produces a single attention output O = O^s + Proj(O^l) that feeds back into the transformer block. The method requires only ~2,000 fine-tuning steps (batch size 64, ~9 hours on 8×H200) to adapt a pretrained DiT to the new attention, which is less than 0.1% of original pretraining cost.

## Method

SLA predicts a compressed block-level attention matrix P_c via mean-pooled Q·K^T, then classifies each block as critical (top k_h=5%), marginal (middle), or negligible (bottom k_l=10%). Critical blocks are computed exactly with block-sparse FlashAttention using OnlineSoftmax; negligible blocks are skipped entirely; marginal blocks are processed with linear attention (φ(Q)·H / φ(Q)·Z) where H and Z are precomputed per K,V block and accumulated via simple additions. The two components are fused into a single GPU kernel for both forward and backward passes, and a learnable linear projection is applied to the linear-attention output to compensate for the distribution mismatch between softmax and linear attention. This design achieves ~95% attention sparsity (≈20× FLOPs reduction) with a 13.7× kernel speedup and 2.2× end-to-end video generation speedup on Wan2.1-1.3B, while matching full-attention quality after brief fine-tuning.

## Results

On Wan2.1-1.3B video generation, SLA achieves 95% attention sparsity (2.74T FLOPs vs. 52.75T for full attention, a ~19.3× reduction) while matching or exceeding full-attention quality across all VBench metrics (e.g., VA 76.96 vs. 76.78, VT 83.92 vs. 82.88, IQ 62.2 vs. 62.5, OC 23.6 vs. 23.3). The custom GPU kernel delivers a 13.7× speedup in attention computation and a 2.2× end-to-end video generation speedup (97s → 11s attention latency) on an RTX 5090. On ImageNet 512×512 image generation (LightningDiT), SLA achieves FID 31.49 at 87.5% sparsity, surpassing full attention's FID of 31.87.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLA dominates all four baselines on both quality and efficiency. Against the training-free SparseAttn (Sparse-F), SLA is overwhelmingly superior: Sparse-F produces near-zero quality (VA 0.002, VT 0.026) while SLA matches full attention. Against the trainable SparseAttn (Sparse-T), SLA wins on every quality metric (e.g., VA 76.96 vs. 73.83, VT 83.92 vs. 77.87, VR 0.048 vs. 0.014) while achieving 11 percentage points higher sparsity (95% vs. 84%) and 2.7× fewer FLOPs (2.74T vs. 7.38T). Against VMoBa, SLA improves VA by 44.6 points (76.96 vs. 32.33) and VT by 48.1 points (83.92 vs. 35.79) at 10 percentage points higher sparsity. Against VSA, SLA gains 21.6 points in VA (76.96 vs. 55.37) and 19.3 points in VT (83.92 vs. 64.61) while still achieving 6 percentage points higher sparsity (95% vs. 89%) and 2.2× fewer FLOPs (2.74T vs. 5.92T). In summary, SLA is the only method that simultaneously achieves >90% sparsity and full-attention-level generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLA sits at the intersection of two established lines of work on efficient attention for Diffusion Transformers: sparse attention (FlashAttention, VSA, VMoBa, SparseAttn) that computes only a subset of attention blocks, and linear attention (SANA, DiG) that reformulates the operation to O(N) complexity. The paper departs from both by identifying a structural decomposition of attention weights into a small high-rank sparse component and a large extremely-low-rank dense component, then fusing sparse FlashAttention and linear attention into a single trainable kernel that assigns each block to the appropriate computation. Unlike prior sparse methods that cap around 85–90% sparsity, or linear attention that fails on video, SLA treats the two as complementary rather than competing, with the linear component serving as a learnable residual rather than a standalone approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 95% attention sparsity (≈20× FLOPs reduction) with no measurable quality loss on Wan2.1-1.3B is a substantial practical win for video generation, where attention dominates latency at 30K+ sequence lengths. The structural insight—that the 'marginal' middle band of attention weights is extremely low-rank—provides a principled explanation for why both pure sparse and pure linear attention fail, and may generalize to other long-sequence transformer settings. Open questions include whether the required fine-tuning (2K steps) can be reduced or replaced by LoRA (current results show a quality gap), whether the approach scales to much larger models or longer sequences, and whether the learnable projection on the linear component can be made more interpretable or eliminated.
