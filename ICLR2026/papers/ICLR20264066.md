## Problem

Expressive speech-to-speech translation (S2ST) must preserve speaker identity and emotional style while accurately translating content, but progress is hindered by three gaps: scarcity of large-scale paired speech data retaining expressiveness, architectural complexity of multi-stage or multi-model pipelines, and the failure of existing LLM-based S2ST systems to explicitly leverage the pre-trained text translation capabilities embedded in large language models. No prior approach simultaneously achieves a single-stage architecture, unified speech-text modality alignment, and effective transfer of LLM text translation knowledge to the speech domain.

## Input / Output

The system takes a 16 kHz source speech waveform, a target-language control token, and a speed-ratio token (discretized in 0.1 intervals) as input. The waveform is decomposed by a triple-tokenizer into 32 fixed-length speaker tokens (BiCodec global encoder), variable-length linguistic tokens at 12.5 tok/s (GLM-4/Whisper encoder), and the model autoregressively generates semantic tokens at 50 tok/s (BiCodec), which are decoded back to a 16 kHz waveform conditioned on the source speaker tokens. Two inference modes are available: Quality Mode outputs [source transcription, target text, target semantic tokens] while Performance Mode skips transcription and outputs [target text, target semantic tokens].

## Method

UniSS builds a single-stage autoregressive language model on Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct (vocabulary expanded to 180,407) that treats speech and text uniformly as token sequences, requiring no architectural modifications beyond vocabulary expansion. The key insight is a cross-modal chain-of-thought prompting framework that decomposes S2ST into sequential listen→translate→speak steps, explicitly routing the model through its pre-trained text translation pathway before generating speech tokens, thereby transferring LLM text translation capabilities to the speech domain. A triple-tokenizer strategy separates speaker identity (32 BiCodec global tokens), linguistic content (GLM-4/Whisper tokens for understanding), and generation-oriented semantics (BiCodec semantic tokens for synthesis), with the BiCodec decoder reconstructing the target waveform conditioned on source speaker tokens for voice and emotion preservation. Training follows a three-phase progressive strategy: Phase 1 aligns speech and text via multi-task learning (ASR, TTS, S2TT, MT on 77.1k hours), Phase 2 introduces CoT-prompted S2ST on the 44.8k-hour UniST dataset, and Phase 3 fine-tunes on a 19.8k-hour high-quality subset with annealed learning rate to stabilize CoT patterns.

## Results

On the CVSS-T benchmark, UniSS (Quality mode) achieves state-of-the-art Speech-BLEU of 32.20 (EN→ZH) and 24.28 (ZH→EN), surpassing all cascaded, MLLM, and end-to-end S2ST baselines. Duration consistency is near-perfect with SLC-0.4 scores of 0.99 (EN→ZH) and 0.97 (ZH→EN), and speech quality (UTMOS) reaches 3.76/3.86, matching or exceeding cascaded systems. Subjectively, UniSS (Q) scores 4.51 on emotion similarity, 4.42 on speaker similarity, and 4.45 on naturalness (5-point MOS), outperforming all open-source baselines and approaching the closed-source Seed Live system.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against cascaded systems (3-Stage-MT, 3-Stage-LLM, 2-Stage), UniSS (Q) leads by 5.3–15.6 Speech-BLEU points and improves SLC-0.2 by 44–67% over the best cascaded baseline, while matching their UTMOS scores (3.76 vs. 3.76–3.79). Against MLLMs, UniSS (Q) edges out GPT-4o on Speech-BLEU (32.20 vs. 31.64 EN→ZH) and dramatically outperforms it on duration consistency (SLC-0.4: 0.99 vs. 0.71) and UTMOS (3.76 vs. 3.46); Qwen2.5-Omni performs poorly on EN→ZH Speech-BLEU (7.10) despite strong Text-BLEU. Among dedicated S2ST systems, UniSS (Q) surpasses Seamless-Ex by 7.75 Speech-BLEU points (EN→ZH) and 8.44 (ZH→EN), and leads on UTMOS by 1.30 (EN→ZH) and 0.96 (ZH→EN). Subjectively, UniSS (Q) achieves the highest speaker similarity (4.42) among all evaluated systems, a 27% improvement in emotion similarity over Seamless-Ex (4.51 vs. 3.56), and naturalness (4.45) that exceeds the 3-Stage-MT cascaded system (4.31) while approaching Seed Live (4.69). The only metric where UniSS trails is A.PCP (prosody), where Seamless-Ex's dedicated prosody encoder gives it a marginal 0.10–0.12 advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniSS departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM-based S2ST, where prior work (Hibiki, MSLM-S2ST, SeamlessExpressiveLM) requires architectural modifications such as multi-stream heads, nested Transformers, or additional NAR models to complete acoustic generation. Instead, UniSS retains the pre-trained Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct backbone unmodified and introduces a triple-tokenizer strategy (BiCodec speaker/semantic tokens + GLM-4 linguistic tokens) that separates speaker identity, content understanding, and generation-oriented semantics into distinct token streams. The key conceptual departure is the cross-modal chain-of-thought prompting, which explicitly routes the LLM's pre-trained text translation knowledge into the speech domain by forcing intermediate text generation (transcribe → translate → speak), rather than treating the LLM as a generic sequence-to-sequence converter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 44.8k-hour UniST dataset and the reproducible synthesis pipeline address a critical data bottleneck that has limited expressive S2ST research, and the single-stage architecture with no LLM modifications offers a simpler, more scalable recipe for the community. The ablation showing a ~15 BLEU-point collapse when CoT is removed provides strong evidence that text translation capabilities are genuinely being transferred rather than merely co-occurring with speech modeling. Key limitations include restriction to Chinese-English, vocabulary bloat from three separate tokenizers, and the reliance on synthetic data whose emotional fidelity, while rated well, may not capture the full range of natural expressiveness. Extending to multilingual settings and unifying the tokenizers are the natural next steps.
