## Problem

LoRA adapters are inherently tied to their frozen backbone and cannot be directly transferred to different base models. Existing transfer approaches either require access to original training data (knowledge distillation) or introduce additional models such as a discriminator for filtering synthetic data (TransLoRA), adding complexity and computational overhead. The paper asks whether LoRA knowledge can be transplanted across models using only the source model itself, without extra training data or auxiliary models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source expert model (base LLM + trained LoRA adapter), a target base model, and five few-shot seed prompts from the downstream task. The source expert generates a synthetic pool of 2M query-label pairs, which are then filtered down to M samples (matching the source training set size). The output is a newly initialized LoRA adapter on the target model, trained with a masked loss that only backpropagates through the top-k% most informative tokens. When source and target tokenizers differ, a dual-pointer alignment algorithm propagates binary token masks across tokenizations before training.

## Method

T1TOK introduces a token-wise contrastive excess score S(y_i) = log P_{M_s+A_s}(y_i|q, y_<i) − log P_{M_s}(y_i|q, y_<i), which measures how much the LoRA adapter shifts the predictive distribution at each token position relative to the bare backbone. This score, grounded in the log-likelihood ratio and Neyman-Pearson optimality, identifies tokens where task-specific knowledge is concentrated. A two-level filtering scheme then applies: first, samples are ranked by their mean excess score and only the top-M are retained; second, within each retained sample, only the top-k% tokens by excess score receive gradient updates during target LoRA training. The training objective is a masked negative log-likelihood over selected tokens only, requiring no discriminator, no original training data, and no additional models. For cross-tokenizer transfers, a dual-pointer alignment procedure maps source token masks to target tokens using one-to-one copy, one-to-many replication, many-to-one averaging, and many-to-many averaging rules, followed by a final top-k% selection on the target side.

## Results

Across four transfer settings (same-model, cross-family, cross-size, cross-version) and four benchmarks (BBH, MMLU, News Headline, Scholarly Title), T1TOK achieves average improvements of +9.94% over the vanilla target model, +8.5% over KD(+MinED), and +4.4% over TransLoRA. The largest gains appear in same-model transfer (Mistral 7B → Mistral 7B), where T1TOK improves BBH accuracy from 0.397 to 0.424 and Scholarly Title ROUGE-L from 0.311 to 0.413. In cross-architecture transfer (Mistral 7B → Llama3 8B), T1TOK reaches 0.484 BBH accuracy and 0.403 Scholarly Title ROUGE-L, consistently outperforming all baselines on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T1TOK outperforms all three baselines (Vanilla, KD+MinED, TransLoRA) on every metric across all four transfer settings. Against KD(+MinED), T1TOK gains an average of +8.5% across all tasks and settings; the gap is largest in same-model transfer (+21.95%) and smallest in cross-size transfer (+2.18%). Against TransLoRA, T1TOK achieves an average +4.4% improvement, with the most notable advantage on personalization tasks (e.g., Scholarly Title ROUGE-L: 0.413 vs. 0.382 in same-model transfer). KD(+MinED) provides only marginal gains over Vanilla in most cross-model settings (e.g., +0.6% on BBH for Mistral 7B→Llama3 8B), while TransLoRA shows more variance—sometimes exceeding KD on personalization tasks but underperforming on reasoning tasks (e.g., MMLU 0.534 vs. 0.560 for KD in same-model transfer). T1TOK's advantage is most pronounced on generation tasks (News Headline, Scholarly Title), where token-level selection captures stylistic knowledge that sequence-level KD and full-sequence TransLoRA miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TransLoRA (Wang et al., 2024), which established the task of transferring LoRA adapters across models via synthetic data generation, and on standard knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) as a knowledge-transfer paradigm. It departs from TransLoRA by eliminating the need for a separately trained discriminator model, replacing it with a lightweight token-wise contrastive excess score computed from the source model's own log-probabilities with and without LoRA. The token-level selectivity mechanism draws on the selective training literature (Gu et al., 2020; Lin et al., 2025), but repurposes it for cross-model knowledge transfer rather than optimization efficiency, and grounds the scoring in the Neyman-Pearson log-likelihood ratio framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As new LLMs are released rapidly, the inability to port LoRA adapters across backbones is a practical bottleneck; T1TOK offers a simple, model-agnostic mechanism that consistently improves transfer across families, sizes, and versions without additional training overhead. The finding that the method generalizes to external (non-synthetic) data and even weaker source models broadens its applicability beyond the synthetic-data-only setting of TransLoRA. Open questions include whether adaptive token-selection thresholds (beyond the fixed k%) could further improve efficiency, whether iterative self-refinement can close the gap to fully supervised training, and how the approach scales to multi-task or multi-adapter transfer scenarios.
