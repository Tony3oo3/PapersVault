## Problem

Prior work established that achieving linear mode connectivity (LMC) between independently trained networks requires both finding appropriate parameter permutations and using sufficiently wide models, with the width requirement attributed to the need for a large enough permutation search space. This paper challenges that assumption by asking whether widening alone—without any permutation—suffices for LMC, and if so, what mechanism explains this phenomenon.

## Input / Output

The method takes two independently trained weight vectors of identical architecture (MLP with 3×512 hidden layers, VGG-11, or ResNet-20) and produces a merged model via simple linear interpolation λθ_a + (1−λ)θ_b with no permutation applied. The merged model's logits are calibrated with an inverse temperature estimated on 20% of the test set to compensate for the exponential norm decay predicted by LEWC. Evaluation spans MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, reporting test accuracy, calibrated loss, and loss barrier values across width multipliers up to 32×.

## Method

The paper introduces Layerwise Exponentially Weighted Connectivity (LEWC), showing that in sufficiently wide models each layer's output of the merged model equals an exponentially weighted sum of the corresponding layer outputs of the two original models, making the merged model behave as a weighted ensemble. Two sufficient conditions for LEWC are identified: weak additivity of ReLU activations (enabled by high-dimensional concentration effects and low-rank weights reducing active-neuron overlap between models) and reciprocal orthogonality (cross-applying one model's weights to the other's activations yields near-zero). Both conditions are driven by the low-rank structure of weight matrices that emerges from widening combined with weight decay, and the paper empirically confirms that weakening weight decay increases rank, breaks both conditions, and destroys LMC. Unlike the permutation-based LLFC framework of Zhou et al. (2023), which requires commutativity (models being close), LEWC requires reciprocal orthogonality (models being approximately orthogonal), explaining why no permutation is needed. The exponential decay of activation norms inherent to LEWC is compensated by a simple softmax temperature calibration, reducing the calibrated loss barrier to near zero.

## Results

For sufficiently wide models (16× for MLP and VGG-11, 32× for ResNet-20), the calibrated loss barrier at λ=1/2 without any permutation is already very small: 0.013±0.004 (MLP/MNIST), 0.056±0.014 (MLP/FMNIST), 0.066±0.054 (VGG-11/CIFAR-10), and 0.087±0.051 (ResNet-20/CIFAR-10). Merged model test accuracy matches the original models' accuracy as width increases, even without permutations. Notably, the loss barriers without permutations are actually *smaller* than those with WM-discovered permutations in most settings (e.g., 0.056 vs. 0.155 for MLP/FMNIST; 0.066 vs. 0.177 for VGG-11/CIFAR-10), indicating that widening alone is not merely sufficient but can outperform permutation-based alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Weight Matching (WM) from Ainsworth et al. (2023), which searches for permutations minimizing the L² distance between two models' weights. The paper's central finding is that for sufficiently wide models, the permutation-free merged model achieves loss barriers that are comparable to or even *smaller* than those obtained with WM: on MLP/FMNIST the loss barrier drops from 0.155 (with WM) to 0.056 (without), on VGG-11/CIFAR-10 from 0.177 to 0.066, and on ResNet-20/CIFAR-10 from 0.173 to 0.087. On MLP/MNIST both settings yield near-zero barriers (0.013 vs. −0.003). Random permutations also maintain high accuracy at large widths, further confirming that the specific permutation found by WM is unnecessary. The paper thus demonstrates that the previously assumed critical role of permutation search for LMC is actually subsumed by sufficient model width combined with low-rank weight structure induced by weight decay.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the permutation-based LMC framework established by Entezari et al. (2022) and Ainsworth et al. (2023), which held that finding an appropriate permutation is essential for connecting independently trained models. It reuses the weak additivity condition from Zhou et al. (2023)'s LLFC framework but replaces the commutativity requirement with a new property—reciprocal orthogonality—yielding the distinct concept of Layerwise Exponentially Weighted Connectivity (LEWC). The analysis also draws on the low-rank weight structure literature (Ito et al. 2025a; Galanti et al. 2022; Timor et al. 2023) to explain why both sufficient conditions emerge in wide, weight-decayed networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that width alone (without permutation search) suffices for LMC challenges a core assumption in the mode-connectivity literature and suggests that the geometry of wide-network solutions is fundamentally different from what permutation-based analyses capture. However, the practical scope is currently narrow: the effect requires very large width multipliers (16×–32×), is demonstrated only on small CNNs and MLPs on simple image datasets, and necessitates softmax temperature calibration to eliminate the loss barrier. Key open questions include whether LEWC and its sufficient conditions extend to transformers, larger-scale models, and other modalities, and whether training procedures can be designed to promote reciprocal orthogonality and low-rank structure more directly.
