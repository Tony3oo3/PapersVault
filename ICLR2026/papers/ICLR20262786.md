## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) effectively improves LLM reasoning but depends on ground-truth labels, creating a scaling bottleneck. Recent self-rewarding alternatives (entropy minimization, self-certainty, majority voting) eliminate the label requirement but suffer from training collapse due to reward hacking: the single-view supervision signal is internally produced by the policy itself, allowing the model to exploit trivial shortcuts (repetitive tokens, consistent-but-incorrect answers) that inflate reward without genuine reasoning gains. The paper asks how to achieve stable, label-free RL training that resists this self-consistent illusion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a math question as input and, for Co-rewarding-I/III, also requires a semantically equivalent rephrased version generated by Qwen3-32B (same mathematical essence, different surface form). The model produces a step-by-step reasoning trace ending in a boxed final answer. During training, G=8 rollouts are sampled per question per step, and pseudo-labels are derived via majority voting over extracted answers; the reward is binary (1 if the rollout's answer matches the pseudo-label, 0 otherwise). Training runs on 4×H100-80GB GPUs with a global batch size of 128 and max response length of 3072 tokens.

## Method

Co-rewarding is a self-supervised RL framework built on GRPO that introduces complementary supervision from a second view to break the single-view feedback loop. Co-rewarding-I (data-side) enforces analogy-invariance: the pseudo-label from majority voting on a rephrased question supervises the original question's rollouts and vice versa, so the model must produce consistent answers across semantically equivalent inputs. Co-rewarding-II (model-side) enforces temporal invariance: the GRPO reference model is repurposed as a slowly-updated teacher via EMA (cosine-annealed from α=0.99 to 0.9999), and its rollouts provide pseudo-labels insulated from the current online policy, effectively performing self-distillation. Co-rewarding-III combines both mechanisms, using the EMA teacher to generate pseudo-labels for both original and rephrased questions in a cross-supervision scheme. The key design principle, inspired by self-supervised learning (SimCLR, BYOL, DINO), is that stable reasoning elicitation requires invariance across data views or temporal evolution rather than reliance on the policy's own single-step outputs.

## Results

Co-rewarding achieves an average improvement of +3.31% over the best self-rewarding baselines across multiple mathematical reasoning benchmarks, with the largest gain of +7.49% on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct. The standout result is Co-rewarding-II reaching 94.01% Pass@1 on GSM8K with Qwen3-8B-Base (trained on DAPO-14k), surpassing GT-Reward (87.19%) by +6.82%. On MATH training, Co-rewarding-I achieves 93.70% on GSM8K and 81.2% on MATH500 with Qwen3-8B-Base, outperforming all self-rewarding baselines while also preserving general capabilities (59.95% MMLU-Pro, 55.79% IFEval). Critically, Co-rewarding-II maintains stable training across all datasets, whereas Self-Certainty and Majority-Voting baselines exhibit training collapse (e.g., Self-Certainty drops to 44.81% on GSM8K with Qwen3-4B-Base on DAPO-14k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GT-Reward (Shao et al., 2024), Co-rewarding-II matches or exceeds ground-truth supervision on GSM8K in several settings: 94.01% vs. 87.19% (Qwen3-8B-Base, DAPO-14k) and 92.42% vs. 87.26% (Qwen3-8B-Base, MATH), while Co-rewarding-I reaches 93.70% on GSM8K (MATH). Against Self-Certainty (Zhao et al., 2025b), Co-rewarding consistently wins, with the gap most dramatic on Qwen3-4B-Base where Self-Certainty collapses to 44.81% on GSM8K (DAPO-14k) versus Co-rewarding-II's 91.89%. Against Entropy (Prabhudesai et al., 2025), Co-rewarding-II achieves +12.90% average relative gain across four math benchmarks (DAPO-14k), and Entropy's reward hacking (repetitive token generation) is clearly visible in response-length curves. Against Majority-Voting (Shafayat et al., 2025), Co-rewarding-I outperforms by +4.42% on average (MATH), and ablations confirm that the cross-supervision between original and rephrased questions is essential—simply training on the union of both yields only 93.48% vs. 93.70% for Co-rewarding-I on GSM8K. Overall, Co-rewarding-II is the strongest variant, achieving the best stability and the highest peak performance, while Co-rewarding-III (combining both data-side and model-side supervision) provides the most consistent gains across all benchmarks including general capabilities (MMLU-Pro, IFEval).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its policy optimization backbone and positions itself against the emerging family of self-rewarding RL methods (Self-Certainty, Entropy, Majority-Voting) that generate reward signals from the model's own outputs without ground-truth labels. Its key conceptual departure is importing the invariance principle from self-supervised learning—contrastive agreement across data views (à la SimCLR/InfoNCE) and temporal decoupling via a slowly-updated teacher (à la BYOL/DINO/Co-teaching)—into the RL-for-reasoning setting, reframing the collapse problem as a failure of single-view supervision rather than an inherent limitation of label-free training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that training collapse—a major bottleneck preventing scalable label-free RL—can be mitigated with relatively simple architectural changes (EMA teacher, cross-question contrastive rewards), and that the resulting stable training can match or exceed GT-labeled RL on accessible benchmarks like GSM8K. However, the GT-surpassing results are concentrated on easier tasks, Co-rewarding-I's stability is data-dependent (it still collapses on DAPO-14k), and the rephrasing quality in Co-rewarding-I introduces an external dependency (Qwen3-32B) that partially undermines the label-free claim. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to harder reasoning (AIME-level), whether the data-side component can be made dataset-agnostic, and whether the EMA schedule generalizes beyond the cosine annealing tested here.
