key_contribution: Introduces CTC-driven teacher forcing and mixed sampling for USR 2.0, which replaces slow autoregressive pseudo-label generation with a single parallel forward pass conditioned on greedily decoded CTC outputs, halving training time while improving out-of-distribution robustness and achieving state-of-the-art WERs (17.6% VSR, 0.9% ASR, 0.8% AVSR on LRS3) with a single unified model.
limitations: The approach is evaluated only on English speech recognition benchmarks; multilingual, streaming, and audio-only settings are mentioned as future work. Additionally, CTC-driven teacher forcing cannot serve as an inference-time decoding strategy (it performs worse than standard greedy or beam-search decoding), and the fixed 0.5 mixed sampling probability may require tuning for different application needs.

surprise_factor: The most interesting conceptual point is that feeding potentially incoherent CTC-derived prefixes into the decoder for pseudo-labelling works effectively, because the student only needs to learn a stable prefix-to-next-token mapping under shared conditioning rather than produce globally coherent sequences. This is moderately counterintuitive but well-supported by ablations showing that removing CTC supervision from the decoder sharply degrades OOD performance. The ~2× speedup is practical but expected given CTC's ~40× decoding advantage over AR; the OOD robustness gains across noise, length, and unseen datasets are well-evidenced but follow logically from the tighter CTC-attention coupling.

## Problem

Unified Speech Recognition (USR) trains a single model for ASR, VSR, and AVSR via semi-supervised pseudo-labelling, but its autoregressive attention-based pseudo-label generation is a major computational bottleneck (requiring one forward pass per token at every training step), and its decoupled CTC/attention supervision makes the attention decoder brittle under distribution shift, where self-reinforcing errors compound through the teacher-student EMA loop.

## Input / Output

The model consumes audio waveforms, 96×96 grayscale video frames cropped to the mouth region at 25 fps, and their concatenation as audiovisual input, with temporal zero-masking applied to student inputs (0.4 s video, 0.6 s audio) while the teacher sees unmasked signals. Output is a sequence of subword tokens from a 1,000-token SentencePiece vocabulary, decoded at inference via joint CTC-attention beam search (beam size 40, CTC weight 0.1) using ESPnet.

## Method

USR 2.0 introduces CTC-driven teacher forcing: the teacher's CTC head is greedily decoded and collapsed to token-level pseudo-labels, which are then fed as fixed input to the teacher decoder in a single parallel forward pass to produce attention-based pseudo-labels, eliminating the autoregressive bottleneck. Because both pseudo-label types share the same length U_CTC, the student decoder predicts them simultaneously under teacher forcing, coupling the two branches and transferring CTC's monotonic-alignment robustness to the attention decoder. A mixed sampling strategy alternates between CTC-driven mode (probability 0.5) and standard AR mode to mitigate exposure bias; in CTC-driven mode the decoder is supervised with both CTC and attention PLs (50/50 weight), while in AR mode the CTC branch is supervised with both PL types. The teacher is updated via exponential moving average (cosine schedule 0.998→1), and confidence-based sequence-level filtering (threshold 0.8) discards low-quality pseudo-labels.

## Results

On LRS3 with the Huge model (656h labelled + 2,649h unlabelled), USR 2.0 achieves WERs of 17.6% (VSR), 0.9% (ASR), and 0.8% (AVSR), setting new state-of-the-art with a single unified model. In the high-resource Large setting (LRS3+VoxCeleb2), USR 2.0 reaches 21.5% VSR, 1.3% ASR, and 1.0% AVSR, outperforming USR (22.3/1.2/1.1) and all self-supervised baselines. On out-of-distribution benchmarks under greedy decoding, USR 2.0 achieves 15.4% WER on LibriSpeech (vs. 25.3% for USR), 73.7% on WildVSR (vs. 80.0%), and 25.0% on AVSpeech (vs. 34.7%), demonstrating substantially improved robustness. Training time is reduced by approximately 2× compared to USR due to eliminating autoregressive pseudo-label generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USR 2.0 consistently outperforms USR across all model scales and resource settings, with the largest gains in VSR (e.g., 23.7% vs. 26.9% for Large; 21.5% vs. 22.3% for high-resource Large) and AVSR (2.2% vs. 2.4%; 1.0% vs. 1.1%), while ASR results are comparable (1.3% vs. 1.2% for high-resource Large, a marginal loss). Against self-supervised baselines, USR 2.0 dominates: in the high-resource Large setting it beats BRAVEn by 7.1% on VSR (23.7% vs. 30.8%), AV-data2vec by 2.9% (23.7% vs. 30.8%), and AV-HuBERT by 8.8% (23.7% vs. 32.5%). On out-of-distribution evaluation (greedy decoding), USR 2.0's advantage is most pronounced: it achieves 15.4% WER on LibriSpeech versus 25.3% (USR), 29.1% (AV-HuBERT), and 38.4% (BRAVEn), and 73.7% on WildVSR versus 80.0% (USR) and 82.4% (AV-HuBERT). The Huge model (17.6% VSR, 0.9% ASR, 0.8% AVSR on LRS3) surpasses heavily supervised systems like Auto-AVSR (19.1/1.0/0.9 with 3,448h labels) and approaches Whisper-Flamingo (–/0.7/0.8, initialised from 680k hours of Whisper) while using only 656h of labelled data and no external language model. Training efficiency is a key differentiator: USR 2.0 trains ~2× faster than USR at every model scale, making large-scale semi-supervised training substantially more practical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends USR (Haliassos et al., 2024a), which introduced a unified semi-supervised student-teacher framework for ASR, VSR, and AVSR with decoupled CTC and attention pseudo-labelling. The key departure is replacing slow autoregressive attention-based pseudo-label generation with CTC-driven teacher forcing—feeding greedily decoded CTC outputs into the decoder to produce attention targets in a single parallel forward pass—combined with a mixed sampling strategy inspired by scheduled sampling (Bengio et al., 2015) to mitigate exposure bias. The approach also draws on joint CTC-attention training (Kim et al., 2017) and the broader pseudo-labelling literature for speech recognition (Sohn et al., 2020; Park et al., 2020), but uniquely exploits the length alignment between CTC and attention pseudo-labels to couple the two branches during self-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~2× training speedup and improved OOD robustness make semi-supervised unified speech recognition substantially more practical at scale, enabling the Huge model trained on ~2500 hours of unlabelled data to achieve SOTA WERs across LRS3, LRS2, and WildVSR with a single model. The insight that global coherence of pseudo-labels is unnecessary in a teacher-student setting (since both share the same conditioning) is a useful conceptual contribution that could transfer to other sequence-to-sequence self-training tasks such as handwriting recognition or music transcription. Open questions include whether the mixed sampling ratio and CTC-attention loss weights generalize across languages and domains, and whether the approach extends to streaming or multilingual settings where the CTC-attention length alignment may be less clean.
