## Problem

Autoregressive VLA models face a fundamental tension in action tokenization: existing methods either produce too many tokens (hurting inference speed), sacrifice reconstruction fidelity for compression, or fail to exploit the 2D structure of action sequences (temporal × action dimension). No prior tokenizer simultaneously achieves high compression, near-lossless reconstruction, 2D structural awareness, and out-of-the-box generalization across embodiments and backbones.

## Input / Output

The policy receives an RGB image, a proprioceptive state vector, and a language instruction at each timestep, and outputs a continuous action chunk of horizon length H (e.g., 20 steps for single-arm, 32 for whole-body). Internally, the action chunk is patchified into a 2D tensor (m×n patches of size h×d) where temporal steps are uniformly grouped and action dimensions are non-uniformly grouped by physical semantics (e.g., EEF position, orientation, gripper). The VQ tokenizer maps this to a 3D discrete code tensor of shape N_c × C_h × C_a (e.g., 3×1×7 for single-arm), and the VLA generates these codes block-wise before the VQ decoder reconstructs the continuous trajectory.

## Method

FASTerVQ is a Transformer Action AutoEncoder (TAAE) that combines a hybrid transformer-convolutional encoder with Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ) over N_c=3 codebook levels (K=4096 entries each), trained with L1 reconstruction loss in both the temporal domain and the DCT (frequency) domain plus a commitment loss. The action patchifier non-uniformly partitions action dimensions by physical semantics to mitigate distributional imbalance, then flattens into patches for the encoder. FASTerVLA introduces Block-wise Autoregressive (BAR) decoding with a block-wise causal mask that predicts B tokens (e.g., 7 for single-arm) per forward pass, reducing autoregressive depth from N to N/B steps; a lightweight action expert (fewer parameters than the VLM backbone) handles action token generation to avoid interfering with pretrained weights. Decoding proceeds codebook-wise (coarse-to-fine, matching the RVQ residual hierarchy) and horizon-wise within each codebook, and RoPE with spacing augmentation prevents position overfitting on fixed-length action sequences.

## Results

FASTer achieves a 97.9% average success rate on LIBERO (Spatial 98.0, Object 99.4, Goal 98.6, Long 95.4) and 87.9% on Simpler-Bridge (Spoon 91.7, Carrot 93.3, Block 67.5, Eggplant 99.2), setting new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks. Inference latency is 112 ms on LIBERO (single-arm) and 237 ms on R1Lite-WBC (21-DoF whole-body), compared to 176 ms for π0 and 197–556 ms for π0-FAST on LIBERO. The block-wise autoregressive (BAR) decoding reduces forward passes from N to N/B, yielding up to 3× speedup over token-wise AR decoding (323 ms → 140 ms on LIBERO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FASTer outperforms all 11 baselines on both LIBERO and Simpler-Bridge. On LIBERO, it surpasses the strongest autoregressive baseline π0.5 (96.8%) by 1.1 points and the non-autoregressive π0 (94.2%) by 3.7 points; on Simpler-Bridge it leads the second-best π0 FAST-D (76.5%) by 11.4 points and π0 (66.7%) by 21.2 points. Against the closest autoregressive competitor π0 FAST-D, FASTer gains +3.7 on LIBERO and +11.4 on Simpler-Bridge, while also reducing inference time from 197–556 ms to 112 ms on LIBERO. On the high-dimensional R1Lite-WBC setting, FASTer matches π0's latency (237 ms vs 225 ms) while π0-FAST requires 1,100–3,000 ms, a 5–13× speedup. The ablation (FASTer w/o BAR) confirms that BAR contributes +2.5 points on LIBERO and +6.9 on Simpler-Bridge over vanilla AR decoding, and the action expert adds a further +2.4 points on LIBERO over the no-expert variant. Cross-backbone experiments show FASTer lifts InternVL3.5-2B from 79.35% to 96.65% (+17.3 points) on LIBERO, turning the weakest backbone into the strongest overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct successor to FAST (Pertsch et al., 2025), which introduced DCT+BPE action tokenization for autoregressive VLAs. FASTer replaces that hand-designed tokenizer with a learned neural VQ module (FASTerVQ) inspired by audio codec architectures (residual vector quantization, straight-through estimation from Van Den Oord et al., 2017), and adds block-wise autoregressive decoding to reduce sequential inference depth. It also draws on the π0/π0.5 line of work for the VLA backbone and action-expert design, and on block-wise generation strategies from video and speech synthesis. Conceptually, the paper reframes action tokenization as a 2D structured compression problem (action dimension × temporal horizon) rather than a 1D sequence problem, which prior tokenizers (binning, DCT+BPE, flat VQ) did not explicitly exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that autoregressive VLAs, long considered slower and less precise than diffusion-based counterparts, can now match or exceed them in both inference speed and task success across diverse embodiments and model scales. The finding that tokenizer quality—not VLM backbone capacity—is the primary bottleneck for VLA performance (evidenced by the 17.3% jump for InternVL3.5-2B) challenges a common assumption in the field and suggests that investment in action representation may yield larger returns than scaling the language model. Open questions include whether the fixed-length, structured tokenization generalizes to truly variable-horizon or reactive control settings, whether the block-wise decoding advantage persists as action dimensionality grows beyond 21 DoF, and whether the VQ codebook can be further compressed without fidelity loss for real-time whole-body control.
