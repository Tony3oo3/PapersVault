## Problem

Learning compact, boundary-tight convex hulls (trust regions) from labeled data in high dimensions remains unsolved: classical geometric algorithms like QuickHull scale exponentially with dimension, MIP-based formulations become intractable due to binary variables, and existing data-driven methods lack theoretical guarantees linking their outputs to the optimal separating geometry. The paper asks whether one can learn a polyhedral hull that tightly encloses all positives while excluding negatives under a fixed hyperplane budget, in a way that is both scalable and theoretically grounded.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled binary dataset D = {(x_i, y_i)} with x_i ∈ R^d and y_i ∈ {+1, −1}, plus a hyperplane budget S specifying the maximum number of facets. It outputs a polyhedral convex hull as an explicit set of linear constraints Ax ≥ b with A ∈ R^{d×S} and b ∈ R^S, where all positive samples are guaranteed to lie inside the hull. The linear-constraint form is critical for downstream embedding into optimization models and for interpretability, distinguishing PCH from neural implicit representations like DeepHull that cannot produce such constraints.

## Method

PCH starts from an exact MINLP that maximizes the separation margin between negatives and the hull boundary subject to full positive inclusion and binary complementarity constraints, then proves (Theorem 1) that each optimal hyperplane of the MINLP is a stationary point of an unconstrained soft-split surrogate objective under suitable sample weights. The algorithm operates as a divide-and-conquer loop: each hyperplane is placed at the positive support b*(a) = min_{x∈D+} a^T x to enforce boundary tightness, a local subregion is assigned via a banded neighborhood intersected with the other hyperplanes' feasible half-spaces, weights are updated by gradient ascent on the separation gap followed by successive projections onto linear, nonnegativity, and isotonic (PAVA) constraints, and one gradient step on the surrogate refines the hyperplane normal. Hyperplanes are adaptively added (via an improved PinCHD initialization targeting misclassified negatives inside the current hull) or pruned (when their subregion contains no violating samples), keeping the model compact while progressively tightening the boundary.

## Results

On polyhedral synthetic datasets (PH_15), PCH achieves 100% trust-region accuracy from 2D through 8D and 99.95% at 100D, while maintaining a fixed model complexity of 15 hyperplanes. In high dimensions (100D), PCH reaches 99.95% (PH_15_100) and 99.01% (NLC_N_100), compared to 57.20% for CGHC, 57.95% for PinCHD, and 64.39% for DeepHull; QH and DCH fail entirely. On real-world tabular data, PCH achieves 99.82% (Breast Cancer), 94.41% (Spambase), 98.61% (Bace), and 99.94% (HIV), matching or exceeding all baselines while using only 5–15 hyperplanes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCH decisively outperforms all five baselines in high-dimensional settings: at 100D on PH_15_100, PCH achieves 99.95% accuracy versus 57.20% (CGHC), 57.95% (PinCHD), and 64.39% (DeepHull), while QH and DCH fail to complete. QH (Barber et al., 1996) provides exact hulls in low dimensions (100% up to 8D) but becomes intractable beyond 8D due to exponential facet growth (23 million facets at 8D). CGHC (Barbato et al., 2024) degrades sharply with dimensionality (99.71% at 2D → 57.20% at 100D) and requires over 3.7 hours of training at 100D, whereas PCH completes in ~117 seconds. DCH (Blum et al., 2019) suffers the steepest accuracy drop (97.88% → failure) and explodes in model complexity (9 → 6.18M hyperplanes from 2D to 8D). PinCHD (Leng et al., 2019) maintains moderate accuracy in low dimensions (99.80% at 2D) but degrades to 57.95% at 100D with 1499 hyperplanes. DeepHull (Balestriero et al., 2022) is the strongest neural baseline (75.39% at 100D on NLC) but still trails PCH by 24+ points and lacks explicit linear constraint form. On real-world data, PCH wins on Spambase (94.41% vs. 91.55% DeepHull, 91.41% PinCHD) and Bace (98.61% vs. 97.37% DeepHull, 76.80% PinCHD), while matching CGHC on Breast Cancer (99.82%) and outperforming DeepHull on HIV (99.94% vs. 99.55%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MILP-based convex hull approximation framework of Barbato et al. (CGHC) by introducing margin variables to form a MINLP that explicitly optimizes boundary tightness, then derives an unconstrained surrogate objective whose stationary points recover the MINLP optimum under suitable weight assignments. For initialization, it adapts the projection-based PinCHD algorithm (Leng et al.) with a robust negative-sample selection strategy. The key departure from prior work is the divide-and-conquer decomposition: rather than solving a global combinatorial problem, PCH reduces the task to per-hyperplane gradient updates guided by strategic weight assignment with isotonic (PAVA) constraints, avoiding binary enumeration entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learned polyhedral trust regions have explicit linear form (Ax ≥ b), making them directly embeddable into optimization models for constraint learning and selective classification in safety-critical domains such as power systems. The scalability gap is substantial: PCH maintains ~99.95% accuracy at 100 dimensions with 15 hyperplanes, while CGHC degrades to 57% and QH is intractable beyond 8D. Open questions include whether the one-step surrogate approximation (rather than full fixed-point convergence) degrades on adversarially structured data, how the method handles truly nonconvex positive regions under tight facet budgets, and whether the isotonic weight constraints generalize beyond the current subregion formulation.
