## Problem

Evaluating language model reasoning is confounded by extensive parametric world knowledge, making it unclear whether benchmark performance reflects genuine reasoning or memorized factual recall. Existing strategies—temporal filtering, paraphrasing, adversarial substitution, and synthetic templates—cannot cleanly separate these two contributions while preserving realistic task complexity and interconnectedness. The paper asks: can we construct controlled parallel evaluation environments where reasoning difficulty is held constant while the utility of parametric knowledge is toggled on or off?

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes connected subgraphs of triplet facts (subject–relation–object) sampled from Wikidata, along with entity ontological types and a configurable uniformity factor for edge-type diversity. It produces two fully parallel corpora (SYNTHWORLD-RM and SYNTHWORLD-SM, each ~6,290 documents / 1.5M tokens / 161K facts) where every document, fact, and task instance has a structurally identical counterpart differing only in surface-form labels. On top of these, it emits 1,200 paired multi-hop QA questions (across six graph motifs with 2–4 hops) and 1,000 paired page-navigation pairs (bucketed by expected random-walk distance from 50 to 10M), enabling the formal knowledge advantage gap metric KA = P_R − P_S.

## Method

SYNTHWORLDS constructs parallel worlds through a three-stage pipeline: (1) universe construction, where a connected subgraph is sampled from Wikidata via iterative frontier expansion with a uniformity factor (α = 1 − uniformity) that flattens the skewed relation-type distribution, followed by k-core extraction to ensure connectivity; (2) surface-form perturbation, where named entities are renamed in breadth-first order over a directed acyclic name-dependency graph (built from substring + KG-relation constraints) so that type-consistent and name-derivation-consistent substitutions propagate correctly (e.g., Vancouver → Metrovale implies University of Vancouver → University of Metrovale), and timestamps receive a fixed offset δ; (3) parallel document generation, where documents are first written in the synthetic world (exploiting the asymmetry that synthetic names carry no parametric associations, preventing the LM from injecting auxiliary real-world facts), then converted to real-mapped counterparts via symbolic entity references that guarantee structural parallelism. Tasks are built with fine-grained difficulty control: multi-hop QA uses specific graph motifs (A–F) sampled from a validated fact graph with constraints on entity distinctness, page independence, and motif uniqueness, while page navigation uses expected random-walk distance as a difficulty proxy. The key design insight is that generating from synthetic entities first and converting to real-mapped via symbolic references ensures both corpora share identical sentence structures and world-consistent facts, differing only in surface labels, so any performance difference is attributable to parametric knowledge rather than task difficulty.

## Results

In closed-book multi-hop QA, models achieve P_R^base ≈ 20 F1 on real-mapped (RM) questions while P_S^base ≈ 0 on synthetic-mapped (SM) questions, yielding a knowledge advantage gap (KA) of approximately 20 F1 points. One-step RAG widens the gap (KA_base − KA_RAG = −4.0 for GPT-5-mini, −1.3 for Gemini-2.0-Flash), while IRCoT+RAG narrows it (KA_base − KA_IRCoT+RAG = +5.2 for GPT-5-mini, +10.3 for Gemini-2.0-Flash). For page navigation, the baseline gap is larger (KA_base ≈ 30 for GPT-5-mini and Kimi-K2; ≈ 20 for Gemini-2.0-Flash and gpt-oss), and granting access to page content narrows the gap by 9.3 (GPT-5-mini) and 7.0 (Gemini-2.0-Flash) points, though it persists on harder navigation pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper, so the 'baselines' are different knowledge-acquisition and integration settings rather than competing methods. In multi-hop QA, closed-book establishes the pure parametric-knowledge baseline (KA ≈ 20). One-step RAG improves absolute performance in both RM and SM but disproportionately benefits RM, widening the gap by 1–4 points. IRCoT+RAG (interleaved retrieval with chain-of-thought) is the most effective at closing the gap, reducing KA by 5–10 points, primarily because it improves retrieval quality in the SM setting. The Reading Comprehension condition (all gold documents provided) equalizes or even reverses the gap, confirming that the residual gap under RAG is driven by retrieval quality rather than reasoning ability. In page navigation, the Links Only condition isolates parametric knowledge (KA ≈ 20–30), while Content + Links narrows the gap by 7–9 points, showing that textual evidence partially compensates for missing world knowledge but does not eliminate the advantage of familiar entity names.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multi-hop QA construction methodology of MuSiQue (single-hop question composition over graph motifs) and the page navigation task from West & Leskovec's human wayfinding work, but departs from prior evaluation strategies (temporal filtering, paraphrasing, adversarial substitution, Fakepedia-style entity replacement) by constructing fully parallel corpora with identical graph structure but systematically perturbed surface forms. The document generation pipeline draws on knowledge-graph-to-text synthesis (Agarwal et al., Josifoski et al.) and symbolic reference insertion (Hennigen et al.), while the retrieval baselines use HippoRAG 2 and IRCoT. The key conceptual departure is the explicit parallel-worlds design that enables a formal, quantifiable 'knowledge advantage gap' metric rather than relying on ad-hoc contamination checks or single-world synthetic perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SYNTHWORLDS provides the first fully automatic, scalable framework for controlled disentanglement of reasoning from parametric knowledge, addressing a fundamental confound in LM evaluation that manual curation cannot sustain as models improve. The finding that one-step RAG *widens* the knowledge advantage gap while interleaved retrieval (IRCoT+RAG) narrows it, and that perfect-recall reading comprehension equalizes or reverses the gap, points to retrieval quality in novel environments as a critical system bottleneck. Limitations include dependence on a single Wikidata-derived corpus, two specific tasks, and six models; open questions include whether the gap persists under multi-agent workflows, long-context synthesis, or targeted retrieval-augmented planning, and how the framework generalizes to domains like code or mathematics where entity renaming has different semantics.
