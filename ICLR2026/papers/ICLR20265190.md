## Problem

Video diffusion models generate temporally consistent videos where objects persist through occlusion, yet no prior work has shown that these models can be directly prompted to perform point tracking without additional training. Existing zero-shot tracking methods (DIFT, SD-DINO) treat pretrained models as feature extractors for image matching, ignoring the temporal generation capability that is central to video diffusion. This paper asks whether tracking emerges automatically in video diffusion models and demonstrates that a simple visual prompt—a colored dot—suffices to elicit high-quality point trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input video (F frames at 480×832 resolution) and a single query point (u₀, v₀) in the first frame. A red circular dot is overlaid at the query location, and the video is encoded into a compact latent representation by a 3D causal VAE (temporal downsampling 4×, spatial 8×). The output is a per-frame trajectory of the tracked point, extracted by detecting the propagated red marker in HSV colorspace within a local search window, along with occlusion predictions when the marker is not found. Each query point requires a separate video generation (~7–30 min depending on model size).

## Method

The core mechanism is counterfactual prompting via SDEdit: after placing a red dot at the query point in the first frame, the video is regenerated from an intermediate noise level (noise strength 0.5, 50 denoising steps) conditioned on the edited first frame, which propagates the marker across all subsequent frames. To prevent the model's strong prior from erasing the unnatural marker, a negative-prompting strategy subtracts the noise estimate conditioned on the unedited first frame from that conditioned on the edited frame (Eq. 5), derived from classifier-free guidance to bias sampling toward marker-containing videos. Two refinements improve accuracy: color rebalancing suppresses natural red hues in the video to reduce false detections during occlusion, and a coarse-to-fine inpainting step (Eq. 4) restricts regeneration to a small spatiotemporal region around the initially estimated track to correct pixel-level misalignments. The approach is entirely zero-shot and architecture-agnostic, and the generated trajectories can be distilled into a lightweight CoTracker network for fast inference.

## Results

On TAP-Vid DAVIS, the zero-shot method achieves an Average Jaccard (AJ) of 42.21, positional accuracy (δ_avg) of 57.29, and occlusion accuracy (OA) of 82.90, outperforming all other zero-shot baselines by a wide margin. On TAP-Vid Kinetics, it reaches AJ 27.36, δ_avg 41.51, and OA 71.39. The method surpasses the self-supervised GMRW (AJ 36.47) and approaches the performance of the stronger self-supervised Opt-CWM (AJ 47.53), while its occlusion accuracy (82.90) exceeds both self-supervised methods and is competitive with supervised trackers like TAPIR (87.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-shot baselines, the method dominates: it outperforms SD-DINO (AJ 29.68) by 12.5 points, DIFT (AJ 21.51) by 20.7 points, and DINOv2+NN (AJ 15.19) by 27 points on TAP-Vid DAVIS, with similar margins on Kinetics. Among self-supervised methods, it beats GMRW (AJ 36.47) by 5.7 points and exceeds it on occlusion accuracy (82.90 vs. 76.36), but falls short of Opt-CWM (AJ 47.53) by 5.3 points on AJ, though it still edges out Opt-CWM on OA (82.90 vs. 80.87). Against supervised methods, the zero-shot approach surpasses RAFT (AJ 34.48) and TAP-Net (AJ 32.05) but is substantially outperformed by TAPIR (58.47), CoTracker3 (64.45), and TAPNext (66.56) on AJ. Notably, its occlusion accuracy of 82.90 is competitive with TAPIR (87.27) and far exceeds RAFT (74.90) and TAP-Net (77.35), suggesting the diffusion model's object permanence prior is particularly effective for visibility reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes SDEdit-based video regeneration and classifier-free-guidance-style negative prompting to perform point tracking, drawing a direct line from counterfactual world modeling (Bear et al., 2023; Stojanov et al., 2025) but replacing their trained masked-autoencoder predictors with off-the-shelf video diffusion models. It departs from prior zero-shot correspondence methods (DIFT, SD-DINO) that extract internal features for matching, instead prompting the generator to visually propagate a marker. The coarse-to-fine refinement step adapts RePaint's masked inpainting formulation to correct spatial drift in the regenerated video, and the negative-prompting mechanism is explicitly derived from the classifier-free guidance score decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that object permanence in video diffusion models translates directly into tracking capability, offering a training-free route to point trajectories that outperforms all prior zero-shot methods and approaches self-supervised baselines. The distillation experiment shows these emergent trajectories can supervise a lightweight CoTracker, suggesting video generators are a viable source of pseudo-labels for tracking. Key limitations include the high per-point generation cost (7–30 min), failure on synthetic/out-of-distribution videos, and symmetry confusion, leaving open whether more efficient sampling or multi-point prompting can make the approach practically competitive with feed-forward trackers.
