## Problem

Selecting the right visual representation for generalist robot policies requires expensive policy rollouts, and existing proxy metrics (segmentation, depth, shape bias) capture only narrow aspects of the visual world, failing to generalize across environments. The paper asks whether a single, unified measure of how well a backbone encodes the full environment state can serve as a reliable, cheap substitute for policy evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261213/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The state prediction head consumes a single RGB image and 2D bounding boxes of target objects (rendered from the simulator's segmentation masks). It outputs a unified state vector of dimension D = N_o(3+4+3+M) + (1+N_j+N_ee), comprising per-object position, quaternion orientation, bounding-box shape, one-hot material, plus a scene-level vector for lighting category, robot joint angles, and end-effector pose. Feature maps are interpolated to 7×7 resolution before RoI pooling, and the final proxy score per model is the mean of min-max normalized per-state scores (classification accuracy for discrete states, negative MSE for continuous ones).

## Method

The paper proposes a visual-prompted state prediction task that exploits the fact that simulators expose ground-truth world state for free. A lightweight head performs RoI average pooling within each object's bounding box on the frozen backbone's feature map (projected to the object state via a linear layer) and global average pooling over all spatial locations (projected to the environment state via another linear layer). Discrete states (material, lighting, quantized shape) are predicted with softmax + cross-entropy, while continuous states (position, orientation, joints, end-effector pose) are standardized and regressed with L2 loss. The resulting per-model proxy score is shown to correlate strongly (Pearson r up to 0.87, MMRV as low as 0.010) with diffusion-policy success rates across MetaWorld, RoboCasa, and SimplerEnv, outperforming all prior proxies including full-policy baselines. The same objective also serves as an auxiliary loss during joint fine-tuning, consistently improving downstream policy success by 3–6 points across five backbones.

## Results

The proposed state prediction proxy achieves the best average MMRV of 0.035 and Pearson correlation of 0.753 across four simulation environments (MetaWorld, RoboCasa, SimplerEnv-G, SimplerEnv-W), outperforming all existing proxy metrics. On SimplerEnv-G, the correlation is particularly strong (Pearson r = 0.871, MMRV = 0.023), while on RoboCasa it achieves the lowest MMRV of any method (0.010). The proxy is also substantially more computationally efficient, requiring only 4 minutes (ResNet) vs. 78 minutes for the Few-Shot baseline on MetaWorld, a ~20× speedup. Real-world validation on a physical Xarm6 robot confirms that simulation-based state prediction scores transfer to real-world policy success rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The state prediction proxy outperforms all six baselines on average MMRV (0.035 vs. 0.068 for the next-best Few-Shot) and Pearson correlation (0.753 vs. 0.347 for Few-Shot), and is the only method with consistently positive Pearson r across all four environments. The Segmentation proxy from Burns et al. (2024) is competitive on RoboCasa (MMRV 0.023 vs. 0.010 for Ours) but degrades sharply on MetaWorld (0.167) and SimplerEnv (0.113–0.115), confirming the paper's claim that narrow visual proxies lack generalizability. The Shape Bias metric (Geirhos et al., 2018) and Depth Estimation (Banani et al., 2024) both show negative or near-zero Pearson correlations in multiple environments, making them unreliable for representation selection. The Few-Shot and Action MSE baselines, which require actual policy training, are outperformed by the policy-free state prediction approach while being 20–150× more expensive. The evaluation protocol and MMRV metric are adopted from Li et al. (2024), ensuring comparability with the SimplerEnv benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proxy-metric paradigm introduced by Burns et al. (2024), who proposed segmentation accuracy as a lightweight indicator of representation quality for manipulation, by replacing narrow visual proxies with a comprehensive full-state regression objective (pose, shape, material, joints, end-effector pose, lighting). It also builds directly on the SimplerEnv benchmark and MMRV evaluation protocol from Li et al. (2024), leveraging the fact that simulators expose ground-truth world state for free. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of the reconstruction-based pretraining literature (MVP, RPT, R3M) and the representation-analysis line (Qi et al. 2025, VC-1), reframing the question 'what makes a visual encoder useful for control?' as a measurable state-decoding problem rather than an empirical policy-rollout comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, 20× cheaper alternative to policy rollouts for selecting visual backbones, with the notable finding that a policy-free state-regression proxy outperforms even privileged baselines (Few-Shot, Action MSE) that require actual policy training. The sim-to-real transfer of the proxy (validated on a physical Xarm6) suggests the approach could guide representation choices before committing to expensive real-world evaluation. Key limitations include reliance on simulator ground-truth state (partially mitigated by showing estimated real-world states still work), coverage of only three simulation environments, and a finite set of nine backbones. An open question is whether the state-prediction objective as an auxiliary loss during joint training (Table 3) generalizes beyond MetaWorld to more complex, long-horizon tasks.
