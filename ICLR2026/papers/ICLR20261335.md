## Problem

State-of-the-art vision-language models exhibit a critical affirmative bias in described object detection, treating contradictory phrases like 'person with a hat' and 'person without a hat' as semantically equivalent and producing identical, incorrect detections. This failure stems from two root causes: the extreme scarcity of negation in pre-training corpora (~0.04–0.08% negation words) and an architectural tendency to assign low attention to negation tokens that are fragmented from the attributes they modify during standard tokenization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261335/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a free-form text caption that may contain negation expressions (e.g., 'not', 'without', 'un-', 'n't') and produces bounding boxes with detection confidence scores, including the ability to correctly reject objects that do not satisfy a negated query. The text input is preprocessed by NEGTOME before encoding, where a parser groups sub-tokens into semantic phrases and a negation-aware boost factor (β=2.0) amplifies the embedding contribution of negation cues within merged phrases. Evaluation uses both standard AP and the stricter NMS-AP metric, which penalizes overlapping predictions on contradictory caption pairs with IoU>0.5.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary contributions. First, COVAND is a negation-focused dataset of 91,110 captions generated via a three-step chain-of-thought pipeline (present/absent attribute extraction → paired positive/negative caption generation → semantic verification) using GPT-4o, followed by a VQA-based region-level alignment step that resolves which specific bounding box each caption refers to, achieving 9.29% negation word frequency (~100× higher than Flickr30k). Second, NEGTOME is a text token merging module that uses spaCy to group fragmented sub-tokens into coherent phrases, computes a normalized weighted-average embedding per phrase, and applies a negation-aware boost (β=2.0) to the negation cue's weight within the merged representation, ensuring the negated concept is encoded as a single semantic unit distinct from its positive counterpart. This is combined with LoRA adapters (r=4, with ReLU activation) applied selectively to deep cross-attention layers in the vision-language fusion module, modifying less than 0.1% of total parameters. The ablation shows that deep LoRA placement preserves negation attention through decision-making blocks, while NEGTOME contributes gains comparable to the dataset itself (+2.0 mAP on D³ absence subset).

## Results

On the D³ described object detection benchmark, the method improves Grounding-DINO-B by +6.6 mAP overall (20.7→27.3) and +7.2 mAP on the challenging absence subset (22.5→29.7). On OVDEval-Negation, the headline gain is +10.8 NMS-AP for Grounding-DINO-B (36.8→47.6), while Qwen-2.5-VL-3B gains +7.3 AP and +3.8 NMS-AP. Zero-shot transfer to NegBench MCQ yields +10.86% overall accuracy (21.69%→32.55%), and cross-domain generalization to FG-CXR chest X-rays achieves +7.69% accuracy (54.86%→62.55%) without any medical training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Grounding-DINO-B, the method achieves the largest absolute gains: +6.6 mAP on D³ Full and a striking +10.8 NMS-AP on OVDEval-Negation, directly reducing the false positive rate by 19.1%. Compared to APE-Ti, improvements are more modest (+3.4 mAP on D³ Full, +1.2 NMS-AP on OVDEval) but still consistent across all description lengths. Against the MLLM baselines (SPHINX-7B at 10.6 mAP and Qwen-2.5-VL-3B at 18.6 mAP on D³ Full), the adapted Grounding-DINO (27.3 mAP) substantially outperforms both, and even the adapted Qwen-2.5-VL-3B (22.2 mAP) surpasses SPHINX-7B. On NegBench MCQ, the method (32.55%) nearly doubles CLIP-OpenAI (16.27%) and triples NegCLIP (10.21%), with the largest relative gain on the Positive subset (+19.49%). Notably, Qwen-2.5-VL-7B (37.8 AP) underperforms the much smaller Grounding-DINO baseline (54.0 AP) on OVDEval-Negation, underscoring that model scale alone does not resolve negation blindness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two distinct lines of work: (1) parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), which it adapts with ReLU-activated adapters placed specifically in deep cross-attention layers for vision-language fusion, and (2) token merging, originally proposed for image tokens in ViTs (ToMe, Bolya et al., 2022) and later applied to text tokens in diffusion models (Hu et al., 2024), which the authors are the first to apply to text tokens in detection models. Conceptually, it departs from prior negation-aware VLM work (NegCLIP, Park et al., 2025) that relied on template-level data augmentation, instead introducing instance-grounded negation data via CoT reasoning and VQA alignment (COVAND) combined with an architectural fix (NEGTOME) that binds negation cues to their modified attributes at the token level. The paper positions itself as addressing two root causes of affirmative bias—data scarcity and token fragmentation—that prior approaches addressed only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Negation understanding is a critical gap for VLMs in safety-critical domains (e.g., distinguishing 'tumor not malignant' from 'tumor malignant'), and this paper provides a practical, parameter-efficient recipe (<0.1% trainable parameters) that substantially closes the gap across multiple architectures. The zero-shot generalization to the biomedical FG-CXR domain suggests the learned negation mechanism is structural rather than domain-specific, though the 2.3% residual annotation error in COVAND and the limited exploration of complex logical structures (e.g., nested negations, scope ambiguity) remain open questions. A key open question is whether the fixed negation boost factor (β=2.0) and spaCy-based phrase grouping generalize to low-resource languages or highly domain-specific negation patterns without re-tuning.
