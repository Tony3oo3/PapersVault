## Problem

Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) built on LLMs struggle with effective inter-agent coordination: manual workflow design is fragile and labor-intensive, while automated topology search faces combinatorial explosion and often fails to find truly effective structures. AgentPO reframes the question from 'what is the optimal topology?' to 'given a fixed, well-designed topology, how do we train agents to cooperate more effectively?', targeting the underexplored problem of optimizing collaborative dynamics rather than architecture.

## Input / Output

The system takes a mathematical problem statement as input. In the Hint-Actor topology, the Collaborator generates a proactive hint conditioned solely on the problem, which is prepended to the query before the Actor produces a step-by-step solution with a boxed final answer. In the Critic-Actor topology, the Actor first drafts a solution, the Collaborator then generates a critique identifying errors and logical gaps, and the Actor refines its answer conditioned on the full history. The training signal is a binary indicator of whether the final answer matches ground truth, and the Collaborator's output (hint or critique) is a free-text passage of up to 2048 tokens.

## Method

AgentPO decouples collaboration from execution into two roles: a lightweight, trainable Collaborator (e.g., Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct) and a frozen, high-performance Actor (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct). Only the Collaborator's parameters are optimized via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which samples a group of G responses per prompt, computes intra-group relative advantages, and applies clipped policy gradient updates with a KL regularization term against a reference policy. The framework supports two interaction topologies—feed-forward (Hint-Actor) and feedback (Critic-Actor)—and extends to more complex multi-agent structures like debate and dynamic role assignment. A key empirical finding is that training a small Collaborator with as few as 500 samples achieves performance comparable to or exceeding direct fine-tuning of a larger Actor model, while consuming only ~7.8% of EvoAgent's inference tokens, because the Collaborator learns a meta-skill of guiding an expert rather than domain knowledge from scratch.

## Results

Using the Hint-Actor topology with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct as the Collaborator, AgentPO achieves 24.5% average Pass@1 accuracy with Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct as Actor and 31.5% with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, outperforming all eight baselines. The gains scale with Actor capability: upgrading from 3B to 8B increases AgentPO's average by 7.0 percentage points, with AIME24 accuracy more than doubling from 6.7% to 16.7%. AgentPO also demonstrates strong data efficiency, reaching 49.4% average accuracy (with Qwen2.5-Math-7B as Actor) using only 500 training samples, and operates at just 7.8% of EvoAgent's inference token cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentPO outperforms all eight baselines across both Actor model settings. The closest competitor is Role Assignment (Xu et al., 2023), which AgentPO beats by +1.8% (3B Actor) and +5.6% (8B Actor) in average accuracy; the gap widens substantially with the larger model. Against EvoAgent (Feng et al., 2025), AgentPO gains +7.2% and +11.3% respectively, while consuming only 7.8% of its inference tokens. Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2023) and Quality-Diversity (Lu et al., 2025) are the weakest baselines, trailing AgentPO by 7.7% and 6.5% (3B) and 10.3% and 11.1% (8B). Standard prompting methods—CoT (Wei et al., 2022), Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023), Step-back Abstraction (Zheng et al., 2024), and Multi-Agent Debate (Du et al., 2024)—all fall 2–12 percentage points below AgentPO, with the largest margins observed for CoT (+12.1% with 8B Actor) and Step-back Abstraction (+11.4%). Notably, AgentPO also surpasses direct Actor fine-tuning methods (SimpleRL-Zero, Prime-Zero, OpenReasoner-Zero) in a separate comparison (Table 2), achieving 49.4% average accuracy by training only the 3B Collaborator rather than the full 7B Actor.

## Relation to Prior Work

AgentPO departs from the two dominant paradigms in LLM-based multi-agent system design—manual workflow orchestration and automated topology search (e.g., ADAS, AFlow, GPTSwarm)—by reframing the problem as collaborative policy optimization within a fixed, well-chosen topology. The optimization mechanism is GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), repurposed here not to train a reasoning model end-to-end but to train a lightweight Collaborator whose sole objective is to produce effective auxiliary signals (hints or critiques) for a frozen Actor. Conceptually, the paper shifts the research question from 'what topology is optimal?' to 'given a topology, how do we train agents to cooperate?', positioning itself as complementary to rather than competitive with topology-search methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a small, RL-trained collaborator (3B) can match or exceed direct fine-tuning of a larger Actor (7B) while consuming only 500 training samples and ~8% of EvoAgent's inference tokens, offering a low-cost path to enhance even black-box API models. However, all experiments are confined to mathematical reasoning with models up to 8B parameters, and the observed performance dip at 700–1000 training samples hints at overfitting that limits generalization claims. Open questions include whether the complementary-capability effect (Qwen hints + Llama actor) generalizes beyond math, whether the approach scales to truly complex multi-agent topologies with more than two roles, and whether the gains persist on open-ended or non-verifiable tasks where binary reward signals are unavailable.
