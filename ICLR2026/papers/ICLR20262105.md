## Problem

Patch-based Transformer models for long-term time series forecasting suffer from scale bias: differing patch means cause attention to favor patches with similar absolute levels rather than similar shapes, and existing remedies like Z-score patch normalization distort intrinsic shape by dividing by standard deviation. Additionally, cross-variable attention computed over the entire historical window introduces noise from outdated, non-stationary inter-variable correlations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series of length L=720 with C variables and partitions it into N non-overlapping patches of length S (typically 24–72). It produces T-step-ahead predictions for all C variables simultaneously, evaluated at horizons T ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}. The per-patch mean is explicitly tracked as a separate trend vector throughout the network and re-added at the projection stage, so the final output is calibrated to the original scale of the input series.

## Method

PMDformer introduces three coupled modules. First, Patch-Mean Decoupling (PMD) subtracts each patch's temporal mean before embedding, preserving residual shape amplitudes while isolating the trend as a scalar per patch—unlike Z-score normalization which also rescales by standard deviation. Second, Proximal Variable Attention (PVA) restricts multi-head self-attention to only the C tokens of the most recent patch, capturing the most predictive cross-variable shape interactions while reducing complexity from O(C²N) to O(C²). Third, Trend Restoration Attention (TRA) applies a shared Transformer encoder along the patch axis where Q and K projections operate on shape embeddings to score inter-patch shape similarity, while the V pathway additively injects the decoupled per-patch means, restoring global trend information without disrupting shape-focused attention. A theoretical proposition shows that without PMD, mean-dependent terms in attention logits can uniformly dominate residual similarity terms, formally justifying the decoupling step.

## Results

PMDformer achieves the lowest average MSE and MAE on 7 out of 8 LTSF benchmarks (ECL, Traffic, Weather, Solar, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2) with input length 720 and prediction horizons of 96–720 steps. On ECL, it reaches an average MSE of 0.148 and MAE of 0.241; on Traffic, 0.378/0.234; on Weather, 0.217/0.251; and on Solar, 0.181/0.211. The only dataset where it is not the best is ETTh1, where TimeBase edges it out (MSE 0.396 vs. 0.401). Overall, PMDformer delivers an average MSE reduction of 5.68% over TimeBase, 8.62% over TQNet, and 11.44% over iTransformer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMDformer outperforms all eight baselines on 7 of 8 datasets in average MSE and MAE, with the sole exception being ETTh1 where TimeBase achieves a marginally lower MSE (0.396 vs. 0.401). Against the closest competitor TimeBase (ICML 2025), PMDformer yields a 5.68% average MSE reduction and 6.61% MAE reduction, attributed to PMD's preservation of amplitude information that TimeBase's orthogonal patch selection sacrifices. Compared to TQNet (ICML 2025), PMDformer achieves an 8.62% MSE and 9.96% MAE reduction, as TQNet's fixed periodic queries are less flexible than PMDformer's adaptive proximal variable attention. The largest gap is against iTransformer (ICLR 2024), where PMDformer delivers an 11.44% MSE and 12.38% MAE reduction, because iTransformer's full-history variable attention overfits on weakly relevant early correlations that PVA avoids by restricting cross-variable attention to the most recent patch. Against PatchTST (ICLR 2023), the patch-based SOTA, PMDformer improves average MSE by roughly 10–13% across most datasets, demonstrating that explicit trend-shape decoupling adds substantial value beyond the shared-encoder patch paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

PMDformer extends the patch-based variable-independent Transformer paradigm established by PatchTST, adding three targeted modifications: patch-mean decoupling (PMD) as a lighter alternative to the Z-score patch normalization used in SAN and SIN, proximal variable attention (PVA) that restricts cross-variable self-attention to only the most recent patch (departing from full-history variable interactions in iTransformer and Crossformer), and trend restoration attention (TRA) that re-injects decoupled patch means into the value pathway. The paper's theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) formally shows that without mean subtraction, attention logits are dominated by scale-dependent terms, providing a principled justification for PMD over prior normalization schemes. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of the VI and VD modeling literatures, attempting to recover the benefits of cross-variable shape modeling without the overfitting risks that have prevented VD methods from clearly surpassing VI baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that subtracting only the patch mean (preserving amplitude) captures shape similarity better than full Z-score normalization (which distorts shape)—is a simple but practically useful correction to a widely adopted practice in patch-based time series models. PVA's finding that restricting cross-variable attention to a single most-recent patch outperforms broader windows offers a concrete, low-cost strategy for handling non-stationary inter-variable relationships. Open questions include whether PMD generalizes to higher-dimensional multivariate settings and multimodal integration, and whether the proximal-only design remains optimal when variable relationships are genuinely long-range rather than locally evolving. The paper's efficiency advantage (lower GPU memory than PatchTST, iTransformer, ModernTCN) also makes it attractive for deployment on large-scale multivariate data.
