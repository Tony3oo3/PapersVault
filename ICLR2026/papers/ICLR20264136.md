## Problem

Inversion-free flow-based image editing methods like FlowEdit avoid the computational cost of ODE inversion but suffer from nonsmooth, unstable editing trajectories because random noise is resampled at each timestep, leading to poor source structure preservation and editing artifacts. The lack of an explicit inverted latent means there is no principled mechanism to constrain the trajectory, and existing work relies on heuristic choices (dual CFG, early-stopping timesteps) that introduce multiple hyperparameters and compromise the deterministic nature of the ODE.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image (encoded to latent space via a VAE), a source text prompt, and a target text prompt, and produces a single edited image decoded back to pixel space. It is entirely training-free, operating on a pre-trained noise-to-image flow model (Stable Diffusion 3.0 medium or FLUX.1-dev) with 33 ODE timesteps. The framework extends naturally to video editing (applied independently per frame) and 3D Gaussian splatting editing (used as a differentiable loss to optimize Gaussian parameters), though temporal consistency in video is not explicitly enforced.

## Method

FlowAlign formulates inversion-free image editing as a time-reversal optimal control problem where the running cost penalizes deviation from the original FlowEdit velocity field and a terminal L2 cost enforces similarity between the edited output and the source image. Solving this via Pontryagin's minimum principle (Lemma 1) yields a closed-form velocity field that decomposes into a 'Semantic Guidance' term (the standard source-to-target velocity difference) plus a 'Source Consistency' regularization term computed from Tweedie's denoising estimates of the clean latents at the current timestep. This terminal-point regularization implicitly smooths the entire trajectory by penalizing unnecessary deviations from the source, without requiring ODE inversion. The method adds only one extra NFE per ODE step (for the Tweedie estimates) compared to the base velocity evaluation, and applies CFG only on the target side, halving the computational cost relative to FlowEdit's dual-CFG approach. The resulting ODE is deterministic and reversible, enabling accurate backward editing that reconstructs the source image from the edited output.

## Results

On PIEBench (700 images, CFG=13.5, 33 NFEs), FlowAlign achieves the best source consistency across all metrics: Background PSNR 24.21, Background LPIPS 0.069, Background SSIM 0.864, and Structural Distance 0.031, while maintaining competitive semantic alignment (CLIP 25.65, HPS 20.85). For backward editing (reconstructing the source from the edited image), FlowAlign achieves PSNR 27.42 and LPIPS 0.085, substantially outperforming FlowEdit (PSNR 19.88, LPIPS 0.147) and all other baselines. Runtime is 7.14s per image on an RTX 4090, faster than FlowEdit (10.64s) due to applying CFG only to the target-side velocity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowAlign dominates all source-consistency metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, SSIM, MSE, Structural Distance) against every baseline, with the largest margins over DDIB (PSNR +11.0) and RF-Inv (PSNR +9.1). Against the closest inversion-free competitor FlowEdit, FlowAlign improves Background PSNR from 21.44 to 24.21 and LPIPS from 0.108 to 0.069, at a small cost in CLIP (25.65 vs 26.46) and HPS (20.85 vs 21.11). RF-Inv achieves the highest CLIP (27.67) but at severe structural degradation (PSNR 15.12, LPIPS 0.308), confirming the paper's claim that high CLIP scores from inversion-based methods come from over-expression of the target prompt. In backward editing, FlowAlign's PSNR of 27.42 nearly doubles FlowEdit's 19.88, demonstrating the deterministic, reversible nature of the regularized trajectory. On video editing (DAVIS), FlowAlign outperforms FlowEdit and FlowDirector on all source-consistency metrics while FlowDirector retains a CLIP advantage (25.41 vs 24.22). For 3D Gaussian splatting editing, FlowAlign achieves the best background LPIPS (0.06) and aesthetic score (5.52) over SDS, DDS, PDS, and IGS2GS.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowAlign extends FlowEdit's inversion-free ODE simulation between two image samples by introducing a trajectory regularization term derived from a time-reversal optimal control formulation. The optimal control machinery (Hamiltonian, Pontryagin's minimum principle) is borrowed from RF-inversion (Rout et al., 2025a), but FlowAlign departs critically by replacing the expensive ODE inversion with a simple L2 terminal-point penalty, which Proposition 1 shows yields a closed-form velocity correction via Tweedie denoising estimates. The method also simplifies classifier-free guidance by applying it only to the target-side velocity rather than both source and target as in FlowEdit, reducing NFE overhead from 4× to 1× per step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a provably stable, inversion-free editing pipeline that achieves superior source consistency (PSNR 24.21 vs. 21.44 for FlowEdit) while remaining competitive on semantic alignment, and the backward-editing experiment (PSNR 27.42) demonstrates the trajectory is genuinely deterministic rather than merely conservative. Extensions to video (frame-by-frame) and 3D Gaussian splatting editing show the regularization is broadly applicable beyond 2D images. Open questions include whether the fixed ζ=0.01 hyperparameter generalizes across editing magnitudes, whether explicit temporal constraints can be added for video, and whether the first-order velocity approximation that causes occasional out-of-domain failures (Fig. 9-d'') can be replaced with higher-order corrections without losing the closed-form solution.
