## Problem

Training-free diffusion-prior solvers for inverse problems often enforce data consistency through repeated gradient steps, inner MCMC/Langevin loops, or hand-coded adjoints and pseudo-inverses of the forward operator, incurring many denoiser evaluations and conservative step sizes. When the forward operator is nonlinear, ill-conditioned, or implemented only in autodiff frameworks without closed-form adjoints, these inner loops become a major wall-clock bottleneck. The paper asks how to impose measurement-space feasibility with a small, fixed per-level compute budget while avoiding operator-specific primitives and inner stochastic samplers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy measurement y = A(x) + n (AWGN, β = 0.05) and a forward operator A (linear or nonlinear, complex-valued measurements handled via real-imaginary stacking) implemented in an autodiff framework, and produces a reconstructed 256×256 RGB image. It operates in both pixel space (FFHQ, ImageNet diffusion priors) and latent space (LDM-VQ4), with a one-switch hybrid schedule that corrects in pixels early and in latents late. The only user-facing hyperparameters are the residual budget ε, the ADMM penalty ρ, and the number of inner iterations K and gradient steps S per level.

## Method

At each diffusion level, FAST-DIPS anchors at the denoiser prediction x_{0|t} and solves a hard-constrained proximal problem (minimize a schedule-aware trust-region term subject to ‖A(x)−y‖ ≤ ε) via a few-step ADMM-style split: the measurement variable v is updated by a closed-form radial projection onto the feasibility ball, while the image variable x is updated by one or a few steepest-descent steps whose step size α* is computed analytically from a local 1-D quadratic model using one VJP and one JVP (or a single forward-difference probe), then refined by Armijo backtracking. No hand-coded adjoints, pseudo-inverses, or inner MCMC are needed—only autodiff primitives. After correction, the state is re-annealed by injecting Gaussian noise at the next diffusion variance (mode-substitution), for which the paper derives an explicit KL bound under a local Gaussian surrogate. A latent counterpart replaces A with A∘D and a hybrid pixel→latent schedule balances early-time throughput against late-time manifold fidelity, yielding up to 19.5× speedup over DAPS with competitive PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS across eight linear and nonlinear tasks.

## Results

On FFHQ pixel-space tasks, FAST-DIPS achieves competitive or superior PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS while being 7–19× faster than DAPS and SITCOM (e.g., 2.61 s vs. 50.40 s for Gaussian deblurring, 2.62 s vs. 50.92 s for motion deblurring). On the nonlinear phase-retrieval task, FAST-DIPS is ~11.8× faster than DAPS on FFHQ (10.35 s vs. 122.10 s) while achieving higher SSIM (0.851 vs. 0.801). In latent space, the hybrid pixel→latent schedule yields ~2× speedup over LatentDAPS and 3–7× over ReSample while matching or exceeding their PSNR/SSIM across all eight tasks. The paper reports up to 19.5× end-to-end speedup over the strongest pixel-space baseline with robust default hyperparameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAPS (the primary decoupled-sampling baseline), FAST-DIPS is 7–19× faster on pixel-space tasks (e.g., 2.61 s vs. 50.40 s for Gaussian blur) while matching or exceeding SSIM on 6 of 8 tasks; PSNR is within ~0.5 dB on most tasks but lags by ~1.9 dB on nonlinear deblur. Against SITCOM, FAST-DIPS is 7–20× faster and wins on SSIM for Gaussian blur, motion blur, and phase retrieval, though SITCOM retains a small PSNR/LPIPS edge on inpainting and nonlinear deblur. Against HRDIS (the fastest pixel baseline), run-times are comparable (2–7 s), but FAST-DIPS achieves higher SSIM on 5 of 8 tasks while HRDIS wins on PSNR/LPIPS for super-resolution and inpainting. In latent space, FAST-DIPS outperforms PSLD by 4–12 dB PSNR across all tasks (e.g., 28.01 vs. 16.81 on Gaussian blur) and beats ReSample by 3–7× in run-time while matching or exceeding PSNR/SSIM; against LatentDAPS it is ~2× faster with comparable or slightly lower PSNR but higher SSIM on most tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAST-DIPS builds directly on the decoupled noise-annealing framework of DAPS (Zhang et al., CVPR 2025), adopting its per-level structure of denoiser anchor → likelihood correction → re-annealing. It departs from the broader family of training-free diffusion posterior samplers (DPS, DDNM, SITCOM, HRDIS) by replacing soft quadratic data-fidelity penalties with a hard-constrained indicator likelihood (residual ball) and by solving the resulting proximal problem via a few-step ADMM splitting with closed-form projection, rather than inner MCMC/Langevin loops or many gradient steps. The adjoint-free design—using only autodiff VJPs and a single JVP or forward-difference probe—contrasts with methods that require hand-coded adjoints, pseudo-inverses, or SVDs for linear operators, making the approach operator-agnostic. The pixel→latent hybrid schedule is a new practical contribution not present in prior latent-space solvers (PSLD, ReSample, LatentDAPS), which either backpropagate through the decoder at every step or operate purely in latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adjoint-free, fixed-budget correction removes a significant engineering barrier: practitioners can apply diffusion priors to arbitrary nonlinear forward operators (simulator-based, differentiable but lacking closed-form adjoints) without deriving operator-specific primitives, while achieving up to 19.5× speedup over DAPS with competitive quality. The hard-constrained formulation is more robust to noise miscalibration than soft penalties, and the analytic step size eliminates a key hyperparameter. Open questions include whether the local (non-global) guarantees suffice for highly nonconvex operators, how the method scales to very high-dimensional or multi-modal measurements, and whether the pixel→latent switching threshold can be made fully adaptive. The framework is explicitly composable with fast/few-step samplers, suggesting further speedups are possible.
