## Problem

Existing fairness regularizers that minimize statistical dependence between predictions and sensitive attributes (KL divergence, MMD, HSIC, gap parity) are sensitive to small model parameter perturbations and lack generalizability across different fairness notions. The paper asks whether the Cauchy-Schwarz (CS) divergence, which provably provides a tighter upper bound than KL divergence and measures cosine rather than Euclidean distance in an RKHS, can serve as a more robust and generalizable fairness regularizer.

## Input / Output

The method accepts tabular feature vectors (with the sensitive attribute excluded from the feature set) or 48×48 grayscale face images, paired with a binary sensitive attribute (gender, race, or young/non-young). It outputs a predicted probability in [0,1] and a binary class label via a threshold. The sensitive attribute is consumed only within the fairness loss to partition mini-batch predictions into two groups for the CS divergence computation; it does not enter the prediction head. For tabular data the backbone is an MLP, and for CelebA-A a ResNet is used.

## Method

The core contribution is a model-agnostic fairness regularizer that minimizes the empirical Cauchy-Schwarz divergence between the prediction distributions conditioned on S=0 and S=1, computed via a Gaussian (RBF) kernel with median-heuristic bandwidth on mini-batches. The training objective is L_BCE + α·D̃_CS(P, Q) + (β/2)||θ||², where D̃_CS is the kernel-based empirical estimator (Proposition 4.1) that reduces to the negative log of the cosine similarity between mean embeddings in an RKHS. The key theoretical insight (Proposition 4.2) is that for Gaussian distributions D_CS ≤ D_KL in both directions, yielding a tighter generalization bound; additionally, because CS measures cosine distance rather than Euclidean distance (MMD) or mean disparity (DP), it is less sensitive to scale differences between group embeddings. The regularizer is distribution-free, requires no parametric assumptions at training time, and extends naturally to multiple sensitive attributes by either treating them jointly or summing per-attribute CS divergences.

## Results

On the Adult dataset (gender), the CS regularizer achieves 83.31% accuracy (−2.71% vs. MLP) and 90.15% AUC (−0.74%), while reducing ΔDP by 85.35% (to 2.42%) and ΔEO by 73.07% (to 2.27%). On the CelebA-A image dataset (Young/Non-Young), CS achieves a 97.36% reduction in ΔDP and a 98.58% reduction in ΔEO. Across all tabular datasets, CS incurs less than 3.1% accuracy loss and less than 2.2% AUC loss relative to the unregularized MLP, while consistently achieving the best or near-best ΔEO (e.g., 95.24% reduction on Adult-race, 96.14% on COMPAS-gender, 92.39% on COMPAS-race).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CS against four fairness regularizers: DP (gap regularization, Chuang & Mroueh 2020), MMD (kernel two-sample test, Gretton et al. 2012), HSIC (kernel dependence, Li et al. 2019), and PR (prejudice remover, Kamishima et al. 2012). CS achieves the best or near-best ΔEO on nearly all dataset-attribute combinations (e.g., 0.44% on Adult-race vs. 0.86% for PR; 0.44% on COMPAS-gender vs. 2.60% for HSIC), while remaining competitive on ΔDP. DP consistently achieves the lowest ΔDP (e.g., 0.35% on ACS-I-gender) but catastrophically worsens ΔEO, ranking among the worst in 7 out of 10 dataset-attribute settings (e.g., ΔEO of 20.15% on Adult-gender vs. 8.43% for MLP). HSIC provides the best utility among baselines (e.g., +0.66% ACC on Adult-race) but sacrifices fairness, achieving only 41.35% ΔDP improvement on Adult-race. PR offers strong ΔDP reductions (95.70% on Adult-gender) but at a larger utility cost (−4.46% ACC) and moderate ΔEO improvement (−47.69%). Overall, CS uniquely balances both fairness metrics simultaneously with the smallest utility sacrifice (≤2.71% ACC on tabular data), whereas each baseline excels on one metric at the expense of the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Cauchy-Schwarz (CS) divergence—originally developed for information-theoretic learning and density estimation (Principe et al., 2000; Jenssen et al., 2006)—as an in-processing fairness regularizer, a setting where it had not previously been systematically applied. It positions itself within the 'independence minimization' family of fairness methods (alongside HSIC and mutual-information-based regularizers) and contrasts with gap-regularization approaches (DP, EO) and latent-representation methods (MMD). The key theoretical departure is Proposition 4.2, which shows D_CS ≤ D_KL for Gaussian distributions, providing a tighter generalization bound than the KL-based or MMD-based regularizers used in prior work (Kampa et al., 2011; Li et al., 2019; Gretton et al., 2012).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CS regularizer offers a principled, model-agnostic drop-in replacement for existing kernel-based fairness losses, with the practical advantage of simultaneously improving both demographic parity and equal opportunity—something DP-optimized methods fail to achieve. The tighter bound relative to KL and MMD gives a theoretical justification for the observed robustness (smaller loss-landscape sensitivity to parameter perturbations). Limitations include the restriction to binary classification with binary sensitive attributes, the Gaussian assumption in the bound being acknowledged as stylized, and evaluation on only five datasets. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-class settings, graph-structured data, and whether alternative kernel choices or bandwidth strategies could further improve the utility-fairness trade-off.
