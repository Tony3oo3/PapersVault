## Problem

Existing activation steering methods for LLMs intervene at the block level (entire attention heads, FFNs, or residual streams), treating hundreds or thousands of dimensions as a monolithic unit. The paper reveals that block activations are inherently heterogeneous, entangling beneficial, irrelevant, and harmful features, which makes block-level steering coarse, inefficient, and intrusive. The central question is whether decomposing interventions to individual dimensions (atomic units) and steering only the most discriminative ones can achieve better control with far fewer intervened activations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261346/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires contrastive sample pairs (positive/negative examples) to compute activation momentum for AU localization, plus the target text prompt at inference time. At inference, it reads scalar activation values at selected AU dimensions within MHA or FFN blocks and applies a multiplicative update (scaling the current activation by a per-AU factor), producing a steered text response. The key constraint is that at most 100 scalar activations are modified per inference, compared to thousands in block-level baselines, and the steering strength adapts per input and per AU rather than being a fixed vector.

## Method

AUSteer decomposes each linear projection y = Wx into AU-level contributions, where each AU is a single column of the weight matrix and its activation is a scalar coefficient. For AU localization, it introduces activation momentum: for each AU, it counts how consistently its activation separates positive from negative contrastive samples across N pairs, yielding a discriminative score in [0,1] that enables global cross-layer ranking without being confounded by layer-depth magnitude growth. For adaptive steering, the update is x̂_i = x_i + γ_i · x_i, which scales the current activation (preserving sign and direction) rather than adding a constant, and γ_i is set proportional to the AU's discriminative score with sign determined by whether the AU promotes or suppresses positive samples. This concentrates intervention on the most task-relevant dimensions while adapting magnitude to each input's activation pattern, achieving state-of-the-art results with ≤100 steered activations versus thousands in baselines.

## Results

AUSteer-FFN achieves the highest average accuracy across all three LLMs while steering at most 100 activations: 61.34% on LLaMA2-7B-Chat (+1.85 over SADI), 83.96% on Gemma2-9B-it (+1.91 over SADI), and 81.50% on Qwen3-8B (+0.70 over SADI). On open-ended generation, AUSteer-FFN reaches 89.24% detoxification on LLaMA2-7B-Chat and a ΔWR of 34.00 on Qwen3-8B, both substantially exceeding SADI (86.32% and 25.50) and STA (99.33% detox, 25.00 ΔWR on Gemma2). Baseline methods intervene on 3,000–4,096 activations (or k_h × 128 per head), whereas AUSteer caps intervention at 100 scalar activations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AUSteer-FFN outperforms all four baselines on average accuracy across all three LLMs: on LLaMA2-7B-Chat it beats SADI (59.49→61.34), ITI (58.97), and CAA (58.47); on Gemma2-9B-it it surpasses SADI (82.05→83.96), STA (81.86), ITI (81.17), and CAA (81.11); on Qwen3-8B it exceeds SADI (80.80→81.50), CAA (80.63), and ITI (80.16). On detoxification, AUSteer-FFN leads SADI by ~3% on LLaMA2 (89.24 vs 86.32) and ~3.6% on Qwen3 (81.65 vs 78.07), while on Gemma2 it is competitive with STA (99.25 vs 99.33). On BPO preference alignment (ΔWR), AUSteer-FFN achieves 22.00, 30.00, and 34.00 on the three models, exceeding SADI by 8.5, 4.5, and 8.5 points respectively. The critical efficiency distinction is that AUSteer intervenes on at most 100 scalar activations, whereas ITI and SADI steer k_h × 128 (or × 256) dimensions per head, CAA modifies 4096 residual-stream dimensions, and STA alters 3584 SAE-atom dimensions—making AUSteer 35–40× less intrusive while delivering superior or comparable performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant block-level activation steering paradigm (ITI, CAA, SADI, STA) by decomposing block activations into single-dimension atomic units (AUs) and showing that only a small subset of these dimensions is task-relevant. While STA also uses the term 'atom,' it refers to SAE features injected at the block level, whereas AUSteer operates on individual columns of the original weight matrices. The activation momentum metric is a novel counting-based discriminative score that replaces probing (ITI) or raw activation magnitudes (SADI), and the adaptive scaling update (γᵢ·xᵢ) replaces the constant-vector addition used by most prior methods. Conceptually, the paper reframes activation steering from a vector-level to a scalar-level intervention, grounded in the observation that different AUs govern distinct output-token distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: steering at most 100 activations outperforms methods that modify thousands, reducing both intervention footprint and computational overhead while improving accuracy across commonsense reasoning, math, detoxification, and preference alignment. The heterogeneity finding—that block activations entangle beneficial, irrelevant, and harmful features—provides an interpretability lens that could inform future steering and alignment methods. Key limitations include the continued reliance on contrastive pairs (shared with all baselines), task-specific hyperparameters (α, k) requiring a sweep, and evaluation limited to decoder-only text LLMs. Open questions include whether AU localization can be made task-agnostic, whether the approach extends to multi-modal or MoE architectures beyond the preliminary tests, and whether the contrastive-pair requirement can be eliminated.
