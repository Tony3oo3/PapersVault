## Problem

Online stochastic matching with budget constraints is NP-hard to solve even offline, making efficient online learning with sublinear regret impossible under general preferences. While single-peaked (SP) preferences are well-studied in social choice theory for their computational benefits, their application to combinatorial bandit problems with knapsack-type budget constraints remains unexplored. This paper bridges that gap by showing SP structure enables polynomial-time optimal matching and near-optimal regret guarantees, while proving the statistical hardness is unchanged from the general case.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a U×K stochastic reward matrix (each entry drawn from a distribution on [0,1]), per-arm costs c_k, and a global budget B constraining the total cost of distinct arms selected per round (not per-user, so multiple users can share an arm at no extra cost). In the known-structure regime, the SP order and user peaks are provided; in the unknown-structure regime, only the guarantee that Θ is SP is given. The output is a sequence of feasible matchings π_t: [U]→[K] over T rounds, with the SP order recovered via PQ-tree contiguity constraint solving when not given.

## Method

The paper contributes three algorithmic components. First, SP-MATCHING is a dynamic-programming algorithm that solves the offline budgeted matching optimally in O(K²(U+B)) time by exploiting the property that under SP preferences, each user is optimally assigned to the nearest selected arm to their peak. Second, MvM (Match-via-Maximal) is a UCB-based online algorithm for known SP structure that leverages a key structural insight: the confidence set of statistically plausible SP matrices admits a unique element-wise maximal matrix, reducing the per-round optimistic matching to a single SP-MATCHING call and achieving Õ(U√(TK)) regret. Third, EMC (Explore-then-Match-and-Commit) handles unknown SP structure by first exploring all arms for N rounds, then applying a novel PQ-tree-based EXTRACT-ORDER procedure that recovers an approximate SP order from empirical estimates by imposing contiguity constraints wherever preference gaps exceed 2ε, projecting onto a nearby SP matrix via a running-maximum construction, and committing to the resulting optimal matching for the remaining rounds, yielding Õ(UKT^{2/3}) regret.

## Results

The paper establishes three main regret guarantees: (1) SP-MATCHING solves the offline budgeted matching problem optimally in O(K²(U+B)) time for single-peaked instances; (2) MvM achieves Õ(U√(TK)) regret when the SP order and user peaks are known, matching the statistical lower bound up to logarithmic factors; (3) EMC achieves Õ(UKT^(2/3)) regret when the SP structure is unknown, using a PQ-tree-based order extraction procedure. The paper also proves that the general (non-SP) offline problem is NP-hard to approximate within (1−1/e), and that SP instances remain statistically as hard as general ones with a worst-case regret lower bound of Ω(U√(TK)).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The paper's contributions are measured against theoretical lower bounds rather than competing algorithms: MvM's Õ(U√(TK)) regret matches the Ω(U√(TK)) lower bound (Theorem 3) up to logarithmic factors, confirming statistical optimality for the known-structure regime. For the unknown-structure regime, EMC's Õ(UKT^(2/3)) regret exceeds the Ω(U√(TK)) lower bound by a factor of √(T/K), and the paper provides evidence (Theorem 12: NP-hardness of approximating the optimistic matching) that closing this gap may be computationally infeasible. The paper contrasts its standard regret guarantees with the weaker α-regret framework (e.g., 1/2-regret via greedy oracle) used in prior work for computationally hard combinatorial bandits, showing that the SP structure enables full regret guarantees rather than approximation-based ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of combinatorial multi-armed bandits (Chen et al., 2013; Kveton et al., 2015) and single-peaked preferences from social choice theory (Black, 1948; Faliszewski et al., 2009). Prior combinatorial bandit work either assumes polynomial-time solvable offline problems or settles for the weaker α-regret notion when the offline problem is NP-hard; this paper shows that imposing the single-peaked structure—a 75-year-old concept from voting theory—makes the budgeted matching problem polynomial-time solvable via dynamic programming (SP-MATCHING), enabling standard regret bounds. The EXTRACT-ORDER procedure is a novel application of PQ trees (Booth & Lueker, 1976) to recover approximate single-peaked orders from noisy empirical estimates, bridging the social choice recognition literature with online learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key insight is that a structural assumption borrowed from social choice theory completely circumvents the NP-hardness barrier in combinatorial bandits, offering an alternative to the α-regret compromise that has been the standard workaround. Theorem 12 (NP-hardness of even evaluating the optimistic value for a fixed matching when the SP order is unknown) provides strong evidence that the T^{2/3} regret rate of EMC may be tight for polynomial-time algorithms, though this remains open. The maximal matrix construction (Lemma 6) is a clean and potentially reusable technique for other structured bandit problems. Open questions include closing the gap between the T^{2/3} and √T rates for unknown structure, extending to richer preference structures (single-peaked on trees/circles), and whether the SP framework can simplify other computationally hard online learning problems beyond matching.
