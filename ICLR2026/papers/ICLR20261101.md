## Problem

Unified multimodal models that both understand and generate images consistently underperform their understanding-only counterparts, particularly on text-rich benchmarks, due to a fundamental conflict between the discrete tokens preferred by autoregressive generation and the continuous embeddings that benefit visual understanding. Existing dual-tokenizer or Mixture-of-Transformers approaches mitigate this conflict at the cost of parameter inefficiency or architectural incompatibility with modern MoE designs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261101/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For understanding, the model takes an RGB image (encoded at 42×42 spatial resolution) plus a text query and produces a text answer. For generation, a text prompt is fed to the LLM which autoregressively predicts a sequence of discrete image tokens (from a 64K FSQ codebook), which a DiT-Air diffusion decoder then renders into pixels at resolutions from 256 to 2048. For editing, a reference image is simultaneously provided to both the LLM (for semantic coherence) and the diffusion decoder (for pixel-level control), producing an edited image.

## Method

Manzano employs a hybrid vision tokenizer built on a shared ViT encoder with two lightweight adapters: a continuous adapter (STC spatial-to-channel compression + MLP) for understanding and a discrete adapter (STC + FSQ quantization with 64K codebook + MLP) for generation, both projecting into the LLM feature dimension. A unified autoregressive LLM (300M–30B) predicts next tokens from a joint text-and-image vocabulary using a single standard AR objective, while a separate DiT-Air flow-matching diffusion decoder (0.9B–3.52B) renders high-fidelity pixels from the predicted image tokens. Training follows a three-stage recipe—large-scale pre-training on mixed text/IT/TI data, continued pre-training on curated data, and SFT on instruction data—enabling joint learning of both capabilities with minimal cross-task interference. The key design insight is that deriving both continuous and discrete representations from the same encoder places them in a common semantic space, substantially reducing the task conflict that plagues dual-tokenizer architectures.

## Results

On understanding benchmarks, Manzano-3B achieves 74.3 on SEEDBench, 51.4 on MMMU, 69.8 on MathVista, 93.5 on DocVQA, and 85.7 on OCRBench, matching or exceeding specialist 3B models (e.g., Qwen2.5VL-3B: 93.9 DocVQA, 79.7 OCRBench) while substantially outperforming all unified models. Scaling to 30B yields 76.0 SEEDBench, 57.8 MMMU, 73.3 MathVista, 94.3 DocVQA, and 86.3 OCRBench, ranking first on ScienceQA (96.2) and MathVista among all compared models. On generation, Manzano-3B achieves 0.85 GenEval overall and 0.46 WISE overall, while Manzano-30B reaches 0.85 GenEval and 0.54 WISE, both SOTA among unified multimodal LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Manzano-3B substantially outperforms all unified models on text-rich benchmarks: DocVQA 93.5 vs. Bagel-14B (88.1), X-Omni-7B (88.6), and Janus-Pro-7B (40.8); OCRBench 85.7 vs. Bagel (73.3), X-Omni (70.4), and Janus-Pro (59.0). Against specialist 3B models, Manzano-3B matches or exceeds Qwen2.5VL-3B on OCRBench (85.7 vs. 79.7) and ChartQA (88.2 vs. 84.0), while being slightly behind on DocVQA (93.5 vs. 93.9). At 30B scale, Manzano-30B surpasses all unified models across every category and is competitive with GPT-4o on understanding (e.g., MathVista 73.3 vs. 61.3, DocVQA 94.3 vs. 92.8), though Gemini-2.5-Pro remains ahead on MMMU (81.7 vs. 57.8) and MathVista (82.7 vs. 73.3). On generation (Table 3), Manzano-3B achieves the best GenEval score (0.85) among unified models, edging out X-Omni-7B (0.83) and Bagel-14B (0.82), while Manzano-30B leads unified models on WISE (0.54 vs. Bagel 0.52, Janus-Pro 0.35), though GPT-4o remains far ahead on WISE (0.80). The paper also notes that GenEval and DPG scores saturate at larger scales, suggesting these benchmarks may not fully capture emergent generation capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

Manzano sits within the autoregressive unified multimodal model lineage (GPT-4o, Chameleon, Emu3, Janus-Pro, Bagel) but departs from the dominant dual-tokenizer strategy that pairs a semantic encoder for understanding with a separate VAE-style tokenizer for generation. Its key architectural departure is a single shared ViT backbone feeding two lightweight adapters—one producing continuous embeddings for understanding and one producing FSQ-quantized discrete tokens for generation—so both token types inhabit a common semantic space. The image decoder builds on DiT-Air with a conditional flow-matching objective, and the overall training recipe follows the three-stage (pre-train → continued pre-train → SFT) paradigm matured by specialist MLLMs like LLaVA, InternVL, and Qwen-VL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the clearest evidence to date that the understanding–generation trade-off in unified models is largely an artifact of heterogeneous tokenization rather than an inherent conflict, since a shared-encoder hybrid tokenizer yields near-zero task interference at both 300M and 3B scale. This opens a practical path for labs to build unified models without the parameter overhead of Mixture-of-Transformers or the capability ceiling of frozen-LLM-plus-diffusion designs. Open questions include whether the aesthetic-quality drop observed with larger diffusion decoders can be resolved, whether GenEval/DPG saturation masks further gains, and how the approach behaves under MoE LLM backbones that the authors note are incompatible with prior MoT mitigations.
