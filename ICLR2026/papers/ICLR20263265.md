## Problem

Learning arbitrary probability distributions over permutations is fundamental to ranking, combinatorial optimization, and structured prediction, yet existing approaches either rely on restricted parametric families (Plackett-Luce, Mallows) that cannot represent all distributions, or require expensive variational inference and complex generative procedures (diffusion, convex relaxations). A key structural limitation is that modeling permutations in inline notation with masked language modeling suffers severe capacity degradation at low forward-pass counts—reducing to only delta functions at a single pass—while the standard jigsaw puzzle benchmark only evaluates delta targets, masking this failure mode.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a conditioning signal (a scrambled jigsaw image tensor via a CNN backbone, or a user's observed sub-ranking of movie IDs) and produces a full permutation or ranking over n items. Internally, the permutation is represented as a sequence of tokens in one of three factorized encodings—Lehmer codes, Fisher-Yates draws, or Insertion-Vectors—each with position-dependent domains (e.g., the i-th Lehmer element ranges over [n−i+1]) rather than the uniform [n] domain of inline notation. The Insertion-Vector representation additionally supports conditioning on an observed sub-permutation via a reference permutation X_ref, enabling conditional ranking without retraining separate models.

## Method

The core contribution is learning distributions over permutations by operating in alternative bijective representations (Lehmer codes, Fisher-Yates draws, Insertion-Vectors) with standard transformer-based MLM or autoregressive next-token prediction trained via cross-entropy. Because these representations have position-dependent element domains, they avoid the entropy collapse that plagues inline notation at low NFEs: at a single forward pass (fully factorized), the model can still represent non-trivial distributions such as Mallows or the Repeated Insertion Model, whereas inline can only produce a delta. The number of forward passes (NFEs) provides a free trade-off between expressivity and inference speed without retraining. A new theorem (Theorem 4.3) establishes that V(X)_k = k − L(X⁻¹)_k, linking insertion vectors to the left-Lehmer code of the inverse permutation, which enables an efficient batched decoding algorithm. The approach is validated on jigsaw puzzles (SOTA), a new cyclic-permutation benchmark, and MovieLens re-ranking, where it outperforms SymDiff, Gumbel-Sinkhorn, and RIM baselines.

## Results

On the CIFAR-10 jigsaw puzzle benchmark, the proposed method (MLM with factorized representations) significantly outperforms SymDiff and Gumbel-Sinkhorn across all puzzle sizes, with baselines falling below the plotted accuracy range at larger sizes while the proposed method maintains high accuracy even at 1 NFE. On the new cyclic permutation benchmark (n=10), all factorized representations (Lehmer, Fisher-Yates, Insertion-Vector) achieve 100% valid and unique permutations at any NFE count, whereas the inline representation fails to produce valid permutations at low NFEs; Fisher-Yates perfectly models the uniform target distribution over all (n−1)! cyclic permutations at every NFE level. On MovieLens re-ranking (n=50), both AR and MLM (1 NFE) outperform the RIM and popularity-based baselines across all observed rank sizes r on NDCG@k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SymDiff (Zhang et al., 2024), the proposed method wins decisively on the jigsaw puzzle benchmark: SymDiff's accuracy degrades rapidly as puzzle size increases and falls below the plotted range at larger N, while the proposed MLM with factorized representations maintains strong accuracy and can even solve puzzles in a single forward pass (1 NFE). Against Gumbel-Sinkhorn (Mena et al., 2018), the same pattern holds—Gumbel-Sinkhorn also degrades at larger puzzle sizes and is significantly outperformed across all representations and losses. On the MovieLens re-ranking task, the proposed AR and MLM models outperform the RIM baseline (Doignon et al., 2004) with uniform insertion probabilities at every observed rank size r, as well as a simple popularity-based ranking baseline. The paper also notes that the AR baseline is particularly strong in the MovieLens setting because no semantic content is available, making the conditional structure of the insertion-vector representation especially effective. On the cyclic permutation benchmark, the inline representation (which all prior methods would use) fails to generate valid permutations at low NFEs, while all three factorized representations succeed, with Fisher-Yates achieving a perfect uniform distribution over cyclic permutations at any NFE count.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of learning over permutations via continuous relaxations (Gumbel-Sinkhorn, differentiable sorting) or discrete diffusion (SymmetricDiffusers) by instead leveraging bijective combinatorial representations—Lehmer codes, Fisher-Yates draws, and Insertion-Vectors—that allow standard masked/autoregressive language modeling to operate without invalid outputs. It subsumes classical probabilistic families (Mallows, Plackett-Luce, RIM/GRIM) as special cases of its factorized conditionals, and extends the brief use of Lehmer codes in Diallo et al. (2020) and Malagón et al. (2025) to a unified framework with three representations and a compute-expressivity trade-off via NFEs. The key conceptual departure from prior neural permutation work is that the representation's position-dependent domains are trivially learned (set to -inf logits), eliminating the need for expensive variational inference or continuous relaxation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most impactful contribution is arguably the argument that the widely-used jigsaw puzzle benchmark is fundamentally inadequate for evaluating distribution learning (it targets a delta function), coupled with two new benchmarks (cyclic permutations, MovieLens re-ranking) that require learning genuinely non-trivial distributions. The Theorem 4.3 connecting insertion vectors to inverse Lehmer codes enables efficient batched decoding, which is a practical win for inference. However, scalability remains a significant open question: the transformer-based approach scales quadratically in permutation length, and the MovieLens experiment (n=50) is still small relative to real recommender systems. A key future direction the authors identify is jointly modeling the item set alongside the ranking, which is necessary for real-world deployment.
