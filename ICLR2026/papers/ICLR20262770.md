## Problem

Sequential quadratic programming (SQP) methods frequently encounter infeasible QP subproblems due to constraint linearization, forcing solvers to either terminate or invoke specialized recovery routines that are difficult to tune and scale poorly in batched or parallel settings. Additionally, the hyperparameters of first-order QP solvers (penalty parameters, relaxation factors) have non-intuitive, problem-dependent effects that make manual tuning impractical. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing an always-feasible QP solver with provable exactness and a learned acceleration scheme that adapts parameters per-constraint.

## Input / Output

The solver consumes a standard convex QP defined by cost matrix P ∈ S⁺ⁿ, cost vector q, inequality constraints Gx ≤ h, and equality constraints Ax = b. At each ADMM iteration, the LSTM policy networks receive the current optimizer state (ADMM primal/dual variables) and QP/ADMM residuals as a 10-dimensional (inequality) or 6-dimensional (equality) per-constraint input vector, outputting positive penalty parameters (μ, σ, ρ) and a scalar relaxation α ∈ (0,2). The final output is the primal-dual solution (x*, y_I*, y_E*) when feasible, or a minimum ℓ₁-violation point with a sparsity certificate identifying which constraints are infeasible when the QP is infeasible.

## Method

FlexQP relaxes QP constraints via an ℓ₁ elastic penalty, yielding a problem that is provably equivalent to the original when feasible (Theorem 3.1) and returns a minimum-violation solution otherwise. The relaxed problem is solved with a two-block ADMM scheme inspired by OSQP, where the first block solves an equality-constrained QP (via direct LDLᵀ or indirect conjugate gradient) and the second block applies soft-thresholding and ReLU projections. Deep FlexQP unfolds this ADMM loop and replaces fixed hyperparameters with LSTM-based feedback policies that are applied per constraint coefficient, making the architecture dimension-agnostic and permutation-invariant. Training uses a supervised loss that normalizes the optimality gap by the optimal solution norm and explicitly includes the Lagrange multipliers, thereby enforcing the exactness condition μ ≥ ‖y*‖∞ during learning. A separate log-scaled residual-based loss is designed to yield informative PAC-Bayes generalization bounds even when residuals are on the order of 10⁻² or smaller.

## Results

On small-to-medium-scale QPs (50–510 variables), Deep FlexQP consistently achieves the fastest convergence to the optimal solution across all problem classes (random QPs, portfolio optimization, SVM, LASSO, Huber fitting, linear OCPs, double integrator, oscillating masses), outperforming both traditional and learned baselines. On large-scale QPs with 10k variables and 10–20k constraints, fine-tuned Deep FlexQP (5 epochs on 100 problems) outperforms all other optimizers in both iteration count (portfolio) and CG iterations (SVM). When deployed within SQP, Deep FlexQP solves nonlinear trajectory optimization problems 4–16× faster than SQP with OSQP, and on predictive safety filter problems reduces safety violations by over 70% while increasing task completion by 43%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Deep FlexQP outperforms all three learned baselines (Deep OSQP from Saravanos et al. 2025, Deep OSQP RLQP Parameterization from Ichnowski et al. 2021, and the authors' own Deep OSQP Improved) on convergence speed across every small-to-medium-scale QP problem class tested. Against the traditional OSQP solver, Deep FlexQP achieves an order-of-magnitude speedup when used as a subroutine in SQP (4–16× on quadrotor trajectory optimization). The non-learned FlexQP baseline (with hand-tuned hyperparameters) is also outperformed by Deep FlexQP, confirming the benefit of learned LSTM feedback policies. On large-scale 10k-variable problems, fine-tuning Deep FlexQP for just 5 epochs on 100 problems yields the best convergence, while the same fine-tuning strategy does not transfer as well to the Deep OSQP variants, suggesting the elastic relaxation architecture is specifically amenable to cross-scale adaptation. On the predictive safety filter task, Deep FlexQP reduces safety violations by over 70% and increases task completion by 43% compared to existing SQP-based methods, highlighting the practical benefit of always-feasible QP subproblem solving.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexQP unifies two previously separate threads: the elastic programming relaxation (Brown & Graves, 1975; SNOPT's elastic mode, Gill et al., 2005) that handles infeasible QP subproblems in SQP, and the ADMM-based operator splitting structure of OSQP (Stellato et al., 2020). The deep-unfolding acceleration follows the closed-loop feedback policy paradigm of Saravanos et al. (2025) and the dimension-agnostic vector policies of Ichnowski et al. (2021), but departs by using LSTM policies to capture long-term optimization history and by introducing a normalized loss that incorporates Lagrange multipliers to enforce the exactness conditions of the elastic relaxation. The log-scaled PAC-Bayes loss is a novel contribution that addresses a known weakness of prior generalization bounds (Sambharya & Stellato, 2025; Saravanos et al., 2025) that become uninformative at small error scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The always-feasible property eliminates the need for separate infeasibility detection and recovery routines in SQP, which is a significant practical simplification for safety-critical and real-time control applications where infeasible subproblems arise routinely from constraint linearization. The 4–16× speedup over OSQP-in-SQP and the 70% reduction in safety violations on predictive safety filter problems demonstrate concrete impact in domains where robustness matters as much as speed. Open questions include whether the fine-tuning strategy generalizes beyond the two large-scale problem classes tested, whether warm-starting can further reduce iteration counts, and whether the approach extends to distributed QP settings. The finding that fine-tuning benefits Deep FlexQP but not Deep OSQP variants suggests the elastic relaxation architecture has a specific inductive advantage that is not yet fully understood.
