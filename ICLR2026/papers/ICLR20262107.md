## Problem

Existing pre-trained time-series models entangle heterogeneous signals (time vs. frequency, local patterns vs. global semantics) into a single large embedding, limiting zero-shot transferability and making it difficult for downstream tasks to selectively access the information they need. Additionally, these models are 10–100× larger than necessary, hindering real-time, CPU-only deployment in lightweight settings. No compact pre-trained model existed that explicitly disentangled representations across both representational spaces and abstraction levels for diagnostic tasks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{S×C} (context length S=512, patch length pl=8) and produces three structurally distinct embedding segments from the decoder: N temporal patch embeddings (d=1536), N spectral patch embeddings (d=1536), and R register/semantic tokens (d=256). A key design choice is defining the mask token at the raw patch level rather than in embedding space, enabling a single token to flexibly support both full-patch and point-level masking. Pre-training operates in univariate mode (C=1) with channel-mixing deferred to fine-tuning, where identity-initialized channel mixers are inserted to capture inter-variable dependencies without disrupting pre-trained weights.

## Method

TSPulse uses a TSMixer (MLP-Mixer) backbone with only ~1M parameters, processing concatenated time-domain and frequency-domain patch embeddings plus learnable register tokens through stacked mixer blocks with gated attention. The core novelty is disentangled multi-objective masked reconstruction: the decoder's output is partitioned into three segments, each optimized by a distinct head — a Time Head (MSE on masked points), an FFT Head (MSE on frequency spectrum + inverse FFT back to time), and a Semantic Head (cross-entropy on a softmax-normalized log-magnitude frequency signature) — forcing each segment to capture complementary information. A hybrid masking strategy randomizes both mask type (block vs. point) and span length per sample to better reflect real-world missingness and reduce overfitting to fixed corruption patterns. For task specialization, lightweight post-hoc fusers are applied: Multi-Head Triangulation (MHT) fuses anomaly scores from time, FFT, and prediction heads for robust AD, while TSLens replaces standard pooling with learned cross-channel attention over all three embedding segments for classification. Pre-training on ~1B samples from Monash and LibCity takes one day on 8× A100 GPUs, with task-specific variants produced by reweighting head losses.

## Results

TSPulse (1M params) achieves state-of-the-art results across all four diagnostic tasks: +14%/+16% VUS-PR over best baselines on TSB-AD univariate/multivariate (zero-shot), +24%/+26% (fine-tuned); +5–16% mean accuracy over VQShape/UniTS/Moment on 29 UEA classification datasets; +70% lower MSE than MOMENT and +50% lower than UniTS on zero-shot imputation (LTSF benchmarks); and +25%/+40% MRR over MOMENT (family/fine-grained) and +100% over Chronos on similarity search. TSPulse outperforms all 40 models on the TSB-AD leaderboard in both uni- and multivariate settings without any target-data training. Despite 10–100× fewer parameters than baselines, TSPulse delivers 4–366× faster CPU inference and 4.5–125× faster GPU inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSPulse outperforms all 15 compared baselines across every task. On anomaly detection (TSB-AD), TSPulse-ZS beats SubPCA (+14% VUS-PR, univariate) and CNN (+16%, multivariate) without any target-data training, and surpasses all pre-trained models (MOMENT, UniTS, TimesFM, Chronos, GPT4TS/OFA, Lag-Llama) by +30% using a fraction of their size; fine-tuning further extends gains to +24%/+26%. On UEA classification, TSPulse with TSLens fine-tuning surpasses VQShape, UniTS, and MOMENT by 5–16% mean accuracy while being 10–340× smaller, and outperforms contrastive/supervised baselines (TS2Vec, TNC, TS-TCC, T-Loss, T-Rep) by 5–12%. For imputation, TSPulse-ZS achieves 70% lower MSE than MOMENT and 50% lower than prompt-tuned UniTS under hybrid masking; fine-tuned TSPulse exceeds supervised models (TimesNet, FedFormer, Non-Stationary Transformer) by over 40%. On similarity search, TSPulse's zero-shot semantic embeddings beat MOMENT by 25% (family match) and 40% (fine-grained match), and surpass Chronos by 100%, while being 2× smaller in embedding size and 10–100× faster in CPU inference. No baseline outperforms TSPulse on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSPulse extends the compact pre-trained model paradigm established by TTM (Ekambaram et al., 2024) from forecasting to diagnostic tasks, retaining the TSMixer MLP-Mixer backbone but replacing random channel-mixer initialization with identity weights for stable fine-tuning. It departs from prior masked-reconstruction models (MOMENT, UniTS) by explicitly disentangling representations across two axes—representational space (time vs. frequency) and abstraction level (fine-grained vs. semantic)—using multi-output heads on distinct embedding segments, rather than learning a single entangled embedding. The register-token mechanism is borrowed from vision transformers (Darcet et al., 2024), and the hybrid masking strategy addresses a gap identified in prior work that relied on fixed block masking, which the authors show is poorly aligned with real-world irregular missingness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 1M-parameter model can match or exceed 10–100× larger pre-trained models across four diverse diagnostic tasks while running on CPU-only hardware has direct practical implications for edge deployment, real-time monitoring, and resource-constrained settings. The disentanglement framework (temporal/spectral/semantic embeddings with validated complementary robustness properties) offers a reusable architectural principle for future TS pre-training beyond the four tasks tested. Open questions include whether the univariate pre-training + fine-tuning channel-mixing strategy scales to very high-dimensional multivariate settings, whether the disentanglement generalizes to tasks beyond diagnostics (e.g., forecasting), and whether the approach can be extended to longer context lengths without losing the efficiency advantage.
