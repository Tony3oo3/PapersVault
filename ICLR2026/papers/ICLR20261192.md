## Problem

RLAIF economizes human feedback by using proxy LLMs to generate rewards, but existing approaches have not rigorously managed the errors these proxies introduce relative to true human reward distributions. This gap is especially acute for explanation generation, where human judgments are inherently pluralistic and probabilistic, making a single scalar proxy reward insufficient. The paper asks: can a generative model learn to denoise proxy LLM rewards and provably bound its deviation from the underlying human distribution?

## Input / Output

The EXPLANATION LLM receives only the decision context (an RL trajectory or a multiple-choice question) and the candidate decision set, with the ground-truth decision deliberately excluded from the prompt to prevent superficial restatements. It outputs a multi-sentence natural language explanation. The rectified flow reward model consumes the context, the generated explanation, and the ODE time, and produces a logit vector over all candidate decisions; per-sentence rewards are defined as the change in the ground-truth decision's logit after each newly added sentence, yielding dense signal rather than a single sparse reward.

## Method

The core contribution is a rectified flow model embedded within a frozen LLM backbone that generates distributional rewards for PPO-based training of the EXPLANATION LLM. The flow's vector field is parameterized by two flow tokens (ODE time and current state, each projected via a separate MLP) that cross-attend to the LLM's last-layer hidden states of the context and explanation, allowing the flow to selectively integrate linguistic and contextual cues when assigning reward distributions. The flow is trained only on positive samples (where proxy LLMs correctly identify the decision) using the standard rectified flow loss, and under a Gaussian additive noise assumption, a theorem shows its deviation from the true human reward distribution is bounded by ε + L√|A|·|σ − σ_r|, turning an unavoidable proxy bias into a controllable term. The EXPLANATION LLM and flow model are trained alternately for two rounds, with the flow providing sentence-level dense rewards that guide PPO with LoRA. The cross-attention mechanism is critical for generalization: ablations show that without it, the flow cannot reproduce 100% accuracy on positive samples and fails to distinguish true negatives from false negatives, whereas with it the flow correctly assigns low rewards to invalid explanations and high rewards to valid ones misclassified by the proxy LLMs.

## Results

On SMAC, MMLU, and MathQA, the method achieves 76.4%, 77.2%, and 80.4% accuracy (ACC) respectively, outperforming the strongest baselines (KTO, ReFT) by 4.3%, 1.9%, and 4.1%. On AI2-THOR, it reaches 70.2% task success rate, exceeding o3-mini (67.7%) by 2.5% and ReFT (64.2%) by 6.0%. Removing the rectified flow and using proxy LLM rewards directly decreases performance by 6.9%–12.4% across datasets, confirming the value of the flow-matching reward generation. Human evaluation on MathQA shows 89.2% prediction accuracy with 60% of participants rating the explanations as most logically sound and least cognitively demanding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all eight baseline families across every dataset. Among RLHF baselines, KTO (72.1% SMAC, 75.3% MMLU, 75.8% MathQA) and DPO (71.3%, 74.6%, 75.3%) are the strongest, yet the method exceeds them by 4.3%, 1.9%, and 4.6% respectively. ReFT, the best reasoning framework, reaches 72.2%/74.3%/76.3% but still trails by 4.2%/2.9%/4.1%. Skywork (RLAIF) underperforms at 69.2%/73.7%/72.9%, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B lags further at 65.8%/72.1%/71.8%. The un-fine-tuned o3-mini is weakest on SMAC (45.5%) but competitive on MathQA (73.9%), still 6.5% below the method. On AI2-THOR, the method's 70.2% success rate surpasses o3-mini (67.7%) by 2.5%, ReFT (64.2%) by 6.0%, and KTO (62.8%) by 7.4%, with Skywork and DeepSeek far behind at 48.3% and 47.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RLAIF by replacing scalar or discretized reward models with a generative rectified flow (a continuous normalizing flow) that produces full distributions over candidate decisions, building directly on the rectified flow framework of Liu et al. (2022) and the RLAIF paradigm of Bai et al. (2022b). It departs from prior distributional reward models (QRM, DPRM, URM) by modeling continuous reward distributions without discretization and by embedding the flow into an LLM backbone with cross-attention to condition on linguistic context. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) provides a Wasserstein-distance error bound showing that the flow provably corrects proxy-LLM noise when the initial distribution matches the noise model, a guarantee absent from prior RLAIF or distributional reward work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a general-purpose, task-agnostic recipe for training explanation-generating LLMs with theoretically grounded reward signals, potentially reducing reliance on costly human annotation in RLAIF pipelines. The cross-attention architecture for embedding flow matching inside a transformer is a reusable technique for any setting where a generative model must condition on rich linguistic context. Open questions include whether the Gaussian additive noise assumption holds for real proxy-LLM errors, how the method scales to very large action spaces, and whether the two-round iterative training converges reliably in more complex multi-agent or long-horizon settings.
