## Problem

When transformers perform in-context learning, they must infer latent rules or concepts from a handful of demonstrations, but it remains unclear whether they actually decompose multi-step reasoning into intermediate latent concepts or simply map inputs to outputs via shortcuts. For numerical tasks, it is also unknown whether internal representations capture the continuous geometry of the latent parameter rather than merely encoding discrete task identity. Prior work on function vectors and task vectors analyzed single-step mappings but did not examine multi-hop latent concept composition or the geometric structure of representations for continuously parameterized tasks.

## Input / Output

For the 2-hop reasoning setting, the model receives ICL prompts of the form 'Source₁, Target₁. Source₂, Target₂ … Sourceₙ,' where the bridge entity (e.g., country) is never stated, and must predict the target entity; the analysis identifies specific attention head groups whose activations encode the bridge concept and are causally transferable across source-target type combinations. For the numerical setting, a 2-layer 1-head transformer (embedding dim 128) is trained autoregressively on sequences of pairwise examples (add-k) or point sequences on circles/rectangles, and the analysis extracts task vectors from layer-2 attention embeddings that lie on low-dimensional manifolds (1D line for add-k, 2D manifold for trajectory tasks) preserving the ordering and geometry of the latent parameter. Steering interventions along these task vectors interpolate the latent parameter value, with evaluation via top-1/top-3 accuracy (add-k) or MSE on inferred radius (trajectory tasks).

## Method

The paper employs two complementary experimental frameworks. For discrete latent concepts, it applies causal mediation analysis (activation patching) to pre-trained Gemma-2-27B and Gemma-2-2B on 2-hop factual puzzles (City→Capital, Landmark→Calling Code, etc.), using a novel 'type-corrected' evaluation where patching a bridge-resolving head's activation from an alternative prompt should shift the model toward the alternative bridge's answer while preserving the normal prompt's output type; correlational evidence comes from cosine similarity matrices showing intra-bridge clustering and inter-bridge orthogonality. For continuous latent parameters, it trains small transformers from scratch on add-k, Circular-Trajectory, and Rectangular-Trajectory tasks, then identifies task vectors via linear probing at layer-2 attention, visualizes their geometry via PCA, and provides causal evidence by steering model outputs along interpolated task vectors to verify that the representation direction encodes the latent parameter value. The key finding is that a sparse set of attention heads (e.g., group (24,30;31) in Gemma-2-27B) serves as the latent concept map C, with deeper heads and MLPs realizing the output map R, and that this mechanism strengthens with model size and number of ICL examples.

## Results

On 2-hop ICL puzzles, Gemma-2-27B achieves high accuracy at 20 shots (e.g., ~90%+ on City→Capital), while Gemma-2-2B drops substantially (23–72% depending on problem type). Causal intervention on head group (24,30;31) boosts the type-corrected alternative answer into the top-10 in ≥73% of cases and to top-1 in >40% of cases, providing strong evidence for a latent bridge-resolving mechanism. For the add-k numerical task, task vectors lie on a 1D linear manifold with >99.9% variance explained by the first PC, and steering along this manifold achieves ~100% top-3 accuracy for the target offset. For Circular-Trajectory, task vectors occupy a smooth 2D manifold with 97.05%, 96.44%, and 93.68% variance explained by the first two PCs for K=16, 32, and 64 radii respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a mechanistic interpretability study rather than a method-comparison paper, so there are no external baseline methods to benchmark against. The key internal comparison is between the 27B and 2B Gemma-2 models: the 27B model exhibits a strong, clean bridge-resolving circuit (head group (24,30;31) with high causal scores and well-disentangled concept embeddings), while the 2B model shows a much weaker and noisier version of the same circuit, with significantly lower ICL accuracy (e.g., 25.83% vs. high accuracy for University→Capital). The paper also contrasts two competing hypotheses—Shortcut (direct source-to-target mapping) versus Latent Two-hop (sequential concept composition F = R∘C)—and provides causal and correlational evidence strongly favoring the Latent Two-hop hypothesis. For the numerical tasks, the comparison is between the geometry of task vectors and the geometry of the latent parameter: the add-k task vectors align with a 1D line preserving offset ordering, and Circular-Trajectory vectors form a smooth 2D manifold preserving radius ordering, confirming that the model's internal representations faithfully capture the continuous parameterization rather than treating each task as an unrelated discrete category.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the function/task vector literature (Todd et al., 2024; Hendel et al., 2023) from single-step (1-hop) ICL mappings to two-hop reasoning with a hidden bridge entity, and from discrete task vectors to continuous-parameter tasks where the vector geometry reflects the latent variable. It also deepens the Linear Representation Hypothesis by showing not just that concept directions exist, but that they compose sequentially (bridge heads → output heads) and that their geometry is causally controllable via interpolation. Methodologically, it builds on causal mediation analysis / activation patching (Zhang & Nanda, 2024; Wang et al., 2023) but introduces a 'type-corrected' evaluation metric that isolates the bridge concept from the output type, enabling cleaner causal attribution in multi-step settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides some of the first causal evidence that LLMs perform genuine multi-step latent reasoning (resolving a bridge entity before composing it with the output type) rather than shortcutting directly from input to output, with the mechanism localized to a sparse set of attention heads. The finding that task vectors for continuous-parameter tasks lie on low-dimensional manifolds whose geometry mirrors the latent parameter (and can be steered by interpolation) offers a more nuanced picture of the Linear Representation Hypothesis than prior work. Limitations include reliance on synthetic, uniquely-answered tasks with human-interpretable concepts, a single model family (Gemma-2), and the 2-layer numerical experiments being very small-scale; open questions include whether these mechanisms persist in more complex, ambiguous, or compositional settings and whether the identified concept embeddings can be leveraged for steering or monitoring in production models.
