## Problem

Normative (top-down) theories in neuroscience lack a general, principled procedure for quantitatively arbitrating between competing theories, and it remains difficult to systematically convert theoretical knowledge into inductive biases that improve data-driven fits of real biological data. Existing approaches require laborious expert hand-design of probabilistic models and are either heuristic and system-specific or limited to idealized simple models with tractable parameterizations.

## Input / Output

The method takes temporally flattened natural images (36×32 pixels, derived by projecting 50-frame movies onto a canonical temporal filter extracted from LN model fits) as input, paired with scalar calcium fluorescence response labels for training. Beyond the standard neural response prediction with calibrated epistemic uncertainty, the framework produces a theory fidelity score β* (via exact Bayesian model comparison between the theory-informed and null kernels) and interpretable prototype images that reveal what spatial structure the task-adaptive heads have learned to exploit.

## Method

The framework meta-learns a deep kernel GP prior from synthetic data generated by a normative theory (efficient coding autoencoder), producing a Theory-Informed Kernel (TIK) with three components: a shared CNN feature extractor φ meta-learned across synthetic tasks, task-adaptive linear heads h_i that bridge the sim-to-real gap, and a task-adaptive RBF GP layer. Training uses bi-level optimization: an inner loop fits h_i and GP hyperparameters per task by maximizing marginal likelihood on support data, while an outer loop updates φ based on query-data log-likelihood. Once φ is frozen, the TIK K_TIK,i(x,x') = σ_f,i exp(−||h_i(φ(x))−h_i(φ(x'))||²/2ℓ²) defines a GP prior over functions encoding the theory's structure. For theory validation, the framework interpolates between TIK and a theory-agnostic RBF kernel via K_β = β·K_TIK + (1−β)·K_RBF, and the optimal β* = argmax_β P(Y|X,β) quantifies the degree of theory-match from data using exact marginal likelihoods.

## Results

On 86 ex vivo mouse RGCs (Qiu et al. dataset), the Theory-Informed Kernel (TIK) achieves the highest test Pearson correlation across most training set sizes (N = 16 to 1452), outperforming the Qiu et al. CNN, standard RBF GP, and LN model baselines. At the full dataset (N = 1452), TIK reaches the highest accuracy of all methods, while the LN model only outperforms TIK in the extreme low-data regime (N ≤ 8). The TIK also achieves lower NLPD scores than the RBF GP across all 86 neurons, indicating better-calibrated epistemic uncertainty. For Bayesian model comparison, the inferred theory-fidelity score β* correlates with ground-truth optimality at Pearson r = 0.88 on synthetic validation data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the Systems Identification CNN from Qiu et al. (2023b), specifically designed for RGC system identification using efficient coding constraints on first-layer filters. The TIK outperforms this CNN across the majority of training set sizes (N ≥ 16), with statistically significant improvements (p < 0.001 via paired Wilcoxon test) for N ≥ 64. The TIK also consistently beats the standard RBF GP (which operates directly on raw image pixels) and the LN model, except in the extreme low-data regime (N ≤ 8) where the LN model's simplicity gives it an edge. Against transfer learning baselines (frozen ResNet-18 features with linear/MLP heads), the TIK substantially outperforms, as the pretrained features are too abstract for single-neuron RGC responses. Ablation studies confirm that the performance gain is specifically attributable to the theory-informed meta-learned features: randomizing the feature extractor or replacing the EC-derived meta-train tasks with generic PCA tasks both significantly degrade performance (p < 0.001 at N ≥ 64), while removing the task-adaptive linear heads is most damaging (p < 10⁻⁷ at N ≥ 32).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive deep kernel GP meta-learning framework of Chen et al. (2023) and Patacchiola et al. (2020) by replacing data-driven meta-training with synthetic tasks generated from a normative theory (efficient coding), and introduces task-adaptive linear heads to bridge the sim-to-real gap. Conceptually, it departs from Mlynarski et al. (2021), who construct theory-informed priors in parameter space with intractable normalization constants, by learning the prior directly in function space via meta-learning, enabling exact marginal likelihood computation. It also contrasts with Qiu et al. (2023b), who hard-code efficient-coding constraints into CNN filters for a single system, by learning a general function-space prior that is not tied to any specific parametric form or first-layer spatial filter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides an automated, scalable path from 'black-box' normative theories to tractable probabilistic models, enabling exact Bayesian model comparison (via the β* mixture-kernel metric) to quantify the degree of theory-data match rather than a binary accept/reject decision. This addresses a long-standing methodological gap in neuroscience where rigorous theory validation has been hindered by the difficulty of hand-specifying probabilistic models and computing marginal likelihoods. Key limitations include validation on a single system (mouse RGCs) and a single theory (efficient coding), a frozen feature extractor that may underutilize biological data, and the well-known sensitivity of Bayesian model comparison to null model choice and prior adaptation. Open questions include whether the meta-learned feature extractor can be further enriched by adaptation on real data, and how to inspect what knowledge is actually stored in the shared embedding.
