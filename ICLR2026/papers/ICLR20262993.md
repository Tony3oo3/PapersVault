## Problem

Most transformer expressivity results treat transformers as language recognizers that accept or reject complete strings, but in practice transformers are used as language models that generate strings autoregressively and output probability distributions. This paper asks which established expressivity equivalences (e.g., UHATs ≡ LTL ≡ counter-free automata) survive when moving from Boolean-weighted classifiers to real-weighted autoregressors, and whether autoregression or probabilistic weighting changes the class of expressible languages.

## Input / Output

The input is a string prefix w₁…wᵢ over a finite alphabet Σ, with a BOS symbol prepended at position 0. In classifier mode, the output is a single scalar weight in a semiring K (Boolean or real) computed only at the final position. In autoregressive mode, the output at each position i is a full probability distribution over Σ ∪ {EOS}, and the model must satisfy a global normalization constraint: for every prefix u, the sum of probabilities over all possible suffixes v equals 1, ruling out dead ends and endless loops.

## Method

The paper introduces the notion of a state encoder—a function mapping strings to sequences of states where each state depends only on the prefix read so far—as a unifying abstraction under which UHATs, LTL formula tuples, and counter-free DFAs are all equivalent (Thm. 5.1). Building on this, the authors characterize expressivity by proving that LTL classifiers in the real semiring define exactly the aperiodic step functions (Prop. 5.3), while LTL autoregressors are strictly more expressive and equivalent to counter-free DFA autoregressors but less expressive than weighted counter-free NFAs. In the Boolean semiring, full LTL classifiers and autoregressors are equivalent (Thm. 5.6), with the conversion relying on Brzozowski-derivative-style next_σ and prefix operators; however, for fragments lacking Y or H (e.g., TL[H], TL[Y], TL[P]), autoregressors are strictly more expressive than classifiers (Prop. 5.8), explaining why leftmost UHATs and fixed-precision SMATs gain expressivity in the autoregressive setting.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no experimental benchmarks. The headline results are expressivity equivalences and separations: (1) UHATs, past LTL, and counter-free DFAs define equivalent state encoders (Thm. 5.1), so they express the same weighted languages as both classifiers and autoregressors. (2) In the Boolean semiring, full LTL classifiers and autoregressors are equivalent (Thm. 5.6), but for fragments lacking Y or H (e.g., TL[H], TL[Y]), autoregressors are strictly more expressive (Prop. 5.8: (ab)* is definable by a TL[∅] autoregressor but no TL[H] or TL[Y] classifier). (3) In the real semiring, LTL classifiers define exactly the aperiodic step functions (Prop. 5.3), making them incomparable with LTL autoregressors (Cor. 5.4), and both are strictly less expressive than weighted counter-free NFAs (Prop. 5.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's 'baselines' are alternative formalisms (LTL fragments, cfDFAs, cfNFAs, SMATs) compared against UHATs in classifier vs. autoregressor settings. In the Boolean semiring, full LTL/UHAT classifiers and autoregressors are equivalent (Thm. 5.6), so results transfer directly. However, for leftmost UHATs and fixed-precision SMATs (equivalent to TL[P] ≡ TL[H]), autoregressors are strictly more expressive than classifiers: (ab)* is definable as an autoregressor but not as a classifier (Prop. 5.8), while (aab)* is not definable by either (Prop. 5.9). In the real semiring, the situation is more complex: LTL classifiers (aperiodic step functions) and LTL autoregressors are incomparable (Cor. 5.4), and both are strictly less expressive than weighted counter-free NFAs, which have no equivalent DFA (Prop. 5.5). For SMATs with arbitrary precision inside attention (equivalent to TL[#]), autoregressors with depth k solve the symbol-prediction task for L_{k+2} while classifiers only reach L_k, explaining the discrepancy observed in Yang et al. (2025) experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on transformer expressivity (Yang et al. 2024; Jerad et al. 2025; Li and Cotterell 2025; Yang et al. 2025), which has characterized what languages transformers recognize as Boolean classifiers, by introducing a four-way taxonomy (Boolean/real weights × classifier/autoregressor) and asking which results transfer. It repurposes the 'state encoder' abstraction to unify UHATs, counter-free DFAs, and LTL under a single framework, enabling systematic comparison across the four settings. The paper also builds on weighted automata theory (Droste and Gastin 2008; Droste and Kuich 2009) to characterize the real-weighted case, and on Brzozowski derivatives to construct the autoregressive-to-classifier conversions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled framework for interpreting the many experimental studies that test transformer expressivity via symbol-prediction (autoregressive) tasks rather than classification, explaining discrepancies such as why SMATs with depth k solve L_{k+2} as autoregressors but only L_k as classifiers. Key open questions include: inexpressibility results for real-weighted autoregressors (the paper acknowledges it lacks good techniques here), extending results to log-precision softmax attention and positional encodings, and characterizing probability distributions computed by real-weighted autoregressors with chain-of-thought (e.g., can they match probabilistic Turing machines in BPP or PP?). The finding that Boolean LTL classifiers and autoregressors are equivalent but real-weighted ones are not suggests that the probabilistic nature of language modeling is not a mere technicality.
