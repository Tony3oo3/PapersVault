## Problem

Semi-structured 2:4 pruning offers native hardware acceleration on modern GPUs, but the rigid constraint of exactly two nonzeros per four consecutive weights severely limits which critical weights can be retained, causing large accuracy drops compared to unstructured pruning. Existing methods either sacrifice model quality for speed or achieve accuracy at the cost of impractical inference overheads, leaving an unresolved trade-off between compression efficiency and task performance.

## Input / Output

ARMOR takes a pre-trained LLM's weight matrices layer-by-layer along with a small calibration set (128 samples, ~524K–1M tokens from SlimPajama-627B) and produces, for each layer, a factorization Ŵ = A(W′⊙M)B where M is the 2:4 binary mask, W′ is a dense weight matrix, and A, B are block-diagonal matrices of size (d/d_block)×d_block×d_block. The block size d_block (128 in experiments) is a tunable hyperparameter controlling the overhead, which scales as O((d_out+d_in)·d_block) — sublinear in the original parameter count. At inference, A and B are applied via batched matrix multiplication, and the sparse core is processed by native 2:4 kernels.

## Method

ARMOR reframes 2:4 pruning as a matrix factorization problem: each weight matrix is decomposed into a 2:4 sparse core (W′⊙M) wrapped by two lightweight block-diagonal matrices A and B that act as learned, low-overhead error correctors, rotating activation and weight spaces into a basis where the 2:4 constraint is less lossy. The factorization is optimized via block coordinate descent alternating between (1) a continuous update step that applies gradient descent (or Adam in practice) to A, B, and W′ with learning rates derived from local β-smoothness of the NoWag proxy loss, and (2) a sparse core update step that greedily selects a 2:4 group per block (weighted by L1 gradient norm) and solves a closed-form least-squares over all 6 possible mask choices to find the optimal two nonzero values. The NoWag proxy loss is elementwise decomposable, enabling parallel optimization across (d_out·d_in)/d_block² independent block subproblems. A convergence theorem guarantees the proxy loss never exceeds the NoWag-P initialization, so ARMOR is provably at least as good as the SOTA baseline it builds upon.

## Results

On Llama-2-13B Wikitext2, ARMOR achieves a perplexity of 6.37, reducing the gap to the dense model (4.57) by nearly 50% compared to the next-best baseline NoWag-P (8.28). Across all five Llama models, ARMOR consistently achieves the lowest 2:4-pruned perplexity, e.g., 7.21 vs. 10.16 (SparseGPT) and 11.14 (NoWag-P) on Llama-2-7B, and 5.95 vs. 8.65 (SparseGPT) on Llama-3-70B. On Qwen 2.5-32B, ARMOR scores 39.51 on GPQA, exceeding even the dense model's 38.84, while the best baseline (Wanda) scores only 35.27. ARMOR retains ~84% of the theoretical 2× speedup of native 2:4 sparsity with only 2.4–5% additional parameter overhead from the block diagonal wrappers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARMOR outperforms all three primary 2:4 pruning baselines (SparseGPT, Wanda, NoWag-P) on every model and dataset. On Llama-2-7B Wikitext2, ARMOR (7.21) reduces perplexity by 29% over SparseGPT (10.16) and 36% over Wanda (11.35); on Llama-2-13B, the gap to NoWag-P (8.28) is 23%. Against the learnable rotation-based baselines, ARMOR consistently beats Wanda+DenoiseRotator on all four models (e.g., 7.65 vs. 9.88 on Llama-2-7B) and RotPruner on all models (7.65 vs. 9.20 on Llama-2-7B; 11.27 vs. 11.65 on Llama-3-8B). Against SparseGPT+DenoiseRotator, ARMOR wins on Llama-2-7B (7.65 vs. 8.70) and is essentially tied on Llama-2-13B (6.80 vs. 6.81), but trails on Llama-2-70B (4.80 vs. 4.64) and Llama-3-8B (11.27 vs. 10.01). The paper notes that ARMOR achieves its competitive results without the rigid orthogonal-matrix inference overhead of rotation-based methods, offering a tunable accuracy–latency trade-off via the block size hyperparameter.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARMOR builds directly on NoWag's layerwise proxy loss and initialization, using the NoWag-P solution as its starting point, but departs fundamentally from the element-wise masking paradigm (Ŵ = W′ ⊙ M) shared by SparseGPT, Wanda, and NoWag-P. Instead of selecting which weights to keep or applying fixed rotations (as in RotPruner and DenoiseRotator), ARMOR introduces learned block-diagonal matrix wrappers around the 2:4 sparse core, reframing semi-structured pruning as a constrained matrix factorization problem. This positions ARMOR between the inflexible diagonal-only scaling (which provably adds no expressivity beyond reparameterizing the mask) and full dense rotations (which impose fixed, non-tunable inference overhead), offering a hardware-compatible middle ground with a tunable block-size hyperparameter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARMOR addresses a critical practical gap: 2:4 sparsity is natively supported on NVIDIA Ampere+ GPUs with up to 2× throughput, yet existing methods degrade accuracy so severely that the speedup is rarely worth the quality loss. By reducing the perplexity gap by ~50% on Llama-2-13B while retaining most of the 2:4 inference speedup (1.14× vs 1.22× for naive 2:4), ARMOR makes hardware-accelerated sparsity practically viable for quality-sensitive deployments. Open questions include whether the 2.4–5% parameter overhead from block-diagonal wrappers becomes problematic at extreme sparsity, whether the block-size hyperparameter can be auto-tuned per layer, and whether the approach extends to pre-training or iterative fine-tuning settings beyond one-shot post-training.
