## Problem

Multimodal LLMs used as verifiers of agent behavior in open-ended domains (web navigation, computer use, robotics) exhibit a pervasive tendency to over-validate flawed trajectories—a phenomenon the authors term agreement bias. This bias is resilient to standard test-time scaling techniques (CoT, majority voting, reasoning models) and directly undermines downstream applications such as self-improvement via Reflexion, online supervision, and behavior cloning, where accurate failure detection is essential.

## Input / Output

The verifier receives a task description (text and/or reference images), the full agent trajectory as an interleaved sequence of annotated screenshots (with Set-of-Marks bounding boxes or coordinate markers) and parsed actions, and an initial environment screenshot to frame the task. It outputs a ternary Likert judgment (SUCCESS / PARTIAL SUCCESS / FAILURE) mapped to [1, 0, 0], a step-by-step reasoning trace, and natural-language feedback for the agent. In the SGV variant, the first step additionally consumes only the task description and initial screenshot (no trajectory), producing a free-form description of how such tasks are typically accomplished.

## Method

Self-Grounded Verification (SGV) is a two-step, training-free prompting method that modulates (un)conditional generation to counter agreement bias. In step 1, the MLLM is prompted to generate broad priors about how the task is typically accomplished, conditioned only on the task description and initial screenshot—deliberately excluding the trajectory under evaluation—so the model explores its probability distribution freely and extracts task-relevant knowledge independent of the data being judged. In step 2, the MLLM evaluates the candidate trajectory conditioned on its own self-generated priors, sampling from a conditional distribution that the authors hypothesize yields more balanced and accurate judgments. The approach requires no architectural changes, no additional training, and only a single-line instruction added to existing verifier prompts, making it easily integrable into pipelines that already employ MLLM verifiers. Across 13+ model families and three environments, SGV improves failure detection (TNR) by up to 25 pp and accuracy by 14 pp, and boosts downstream task completion by up to 20 pp in online supervision, setting a new state of the art on VisualWebArena.

## Results

SGV improves MLLM verification across all models and benchmarks, increasing failure detection (TNR) by up to 25 pp and overall accuracy by up to 14 pp. In downstream online supervision, SGV boosts task completion by 9 pp (20% relative) for a ReAct agent on VisualWebArena (54% SR, a new SOTA surpassing the previous best by 20 pp), 5 pp (22% relative) for UI-TARS on OSWorld, and 8 pp (33% relative) for a diffusion policy on robotimic. In self-improvement via Reflexion, SGV yields consistent gains of up to 10.4 pp over three iterations, whereas the baseline MLLM verifier plateaus quickly. The baseline verifier already achieves >90% recall and SOTA precision on AgentRewardBench, yet misclassifies ~50% of failed trajectories as successes (TNR as low as 50%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Search Agent of Koh et al. (2024b), which uses tree search with 4.9× token overhead, the SGV-based ReAct agent achieves 54% SR on VisualWebArena versus 29%, a 25 pp gain while using fewer tokens (2.2× vs. 4.9×). Against R-MCTS (Yu et al., 2024), which achieves 34% SR with 9.3× token overhead, SGV improves by 20 pp (54% vs. 34%) with substantially lower compute. Against the Pan et al. (2024a) verifier (benchmark-specific rubrics + GPT-4V), SGV improves verification accuracy from 66% to 76% and TNR from 53% to 71% on VisualWebArena, while matching TPR (84% vs. 83%). Notably, the no-SGV baseline verifier already achieves >90% recall and SOTA precision on AgentRewardBench, yet its agreement bias (TNR ≈ 50%) renders it ineffective for downstream self-improvement and online supervision, where SGV consistently outperforms it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of MLLM-as-judge work (Pan et al. 2024a, Koh et al. 2024b, Yu et al. 2024) and test-time scaling research (CoT, majority voting, reasoning models), but departs from both by identifying a systematic failure mode—agreement bias—that none of these prior approaches address. Rather than building on a specific verifier architecture, it reframes the problem through the lens of knowledge extraction limitations in transformers (Allen-Zhu & Li) and RLHF-induced sycophancy (Sharma et al.), then proposes SGV, a two-step prompting strategy that modulates unconditional vs. conditional generation to let the model ground its own evaluation in self-generated priors. The paper also extends the VisualWebArena benchmark with corrected oracles, stronger baselines, and a Lite subset, positioning itself as both a diagnostic study and a practical contribution to the agent-evaluation toolchain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that agreement bias is pervasive across 13+ model families, resilient to all standard test-time scaling techniques, and even present in the strongest reasoning models (GPT-5) fundamentally challenges the assumption that more compute or better reasoning traces will fix MLLM verification quality. SGV's simplicity (a single extra generation step) and its ability to let non-reasoning models match reasoning counterparts make it a low-cost, widely applicable intervention for any pipeline using MLLMs as verifiers. Open questions remain: whether the bias can be eliminated through training-time interventions, how to combine MLLM verification with symbolic or formal methods for open-ended tasks, and when process-based vs. outcome-based verification is most effective—particularly in environments with irreversible actions.
