## Problem

Existing Conditional Robust Optimization (CRO) methods construct prediction sets via conformal prediction with a coverage constraint P{Y ∈ U(X)} ≥ 1−α, but coverage is only a sufficient—not necessary—condition for the actual robustness requirement P{φ(Y, z(X)) ≤ r(X)} ≥ 1−α. This mismatch forces prediction sets to be larger than needed, producing overly conservative decisions with inflated risk certificates. The paper asks whether directly optimizing prediction sets under the explicit robustness constraint can yield more efficient decisions without sacrificing the robustness guarantee.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an i.i.d. labeled dataset {(X_i, Y_i)}_{i=1}^n where X ∈ R^p are context covariates and Y ∈ R^q are outcome vectors (e.g., stock returns or electricity prices). It outputs a decision policy z(X) (e.g., a portfolio weight vector in the simplex) and a risk certificate r(X) quantifying the worst-case loss over the prediction set. At test time, Cal-CRC additionally requires a held-out calibration set and a single test covariate X_{n+1}, and produces a calibrated prediction set U_Cal(X_{n+1}) from which the final decision is extracted via a minmax solve.

## Method

CRC formulates prediction set optimization as min_θ E[r_θ(X)] subject to the explicit robustness constraint P{φ(Y, z_θ(X)) ≤ r_θ(X)} ≥ 1−α, where r_θ and z_θ are induced by a parametrized prediction set (box or ellipsoidal) through a minmax CRO subproblem. The non-smooth indicator in the constraint is replaced by an erf-based smooth surrogate, and the resulting Lagrangian dual is solved via alternating gradient descent on the model parameters θ and the multiplier λ. For finite-sample guarantees at a specific test point, Cal-CRC applies a sample-splitting calibration: the trained prediction set is expanded through a nested radius parameter t, and the threshold t̂^y is selected per hypothesized label y so that the empirical robustness over the calibration set meets 1−α, yielding a distribution-free robustness guarantee via exchangeability. Non-asymptotic theorems bound both the robustness gap (Δ_n = O(√(d log n / n))) and the expected risk certificate optimality gap under Lipschitz and bounded-density conditions.

## Results

On the US stock portfolio optimization problem (α=0.1), CRC-E achieves the lowest risk certificate (1.028) and decision loss (-0.077) while maintaining robustness at 90.8%, closely matching the 90% nominal target. In contrast, CRO-E produces a risk certificate of 6.345 (6.2× higher) with 99.9% robustness, and E2E-E yields 4.995 with 98.6% robustness. On synthetic portfolio data (n=1500, α=0.1), CRC-E similarly achieves the lowest risk certificate and decision loss across all α levels while keeping robustness at the nominal level, whereas CRO and E2E consistently exceed the target by 5–10 percentage points. The battery storage problem confirms the same pattern: CRC sustains the lowest risk and loss values while matching the nominal robustness target.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRC dominates all baselines on both risk certificate and decision loss across every experimental setting. Against CRO (Sun et al., 2023), CRC-E reduces the risk certificate from 6.345 to 1.028 (84% reduction) at α=0.1 on US stocks, while CRO's 99.9% robustness reveals severe over-conservatism relative to the 90% target. Against E2E (Chenreddy & Delage, 2024; Yeh et al., 2024), CRC-E still achieves a 79% lower risk certificate (1.028 vs. 4.995) and a 9% lower decision loss (-0.077 vs. -0.071), with E2E's 98.6% robustness again indicating unnecessary conservatism. On the synthetic portfolio data, CRC-E maintains robustness at the nominal level (e.g., ~90% at α=0.1) while CRO and E2E overshoot by 5–10 percentage points, confirming that coverage-based constraints are a sufficient but overly restrictive proxy for robustness. The RAC method (Kiyani et al., 2025), applicable only to discrete decision spaces, is compared in the appendix under discretized settings and is also outperformed by CRC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Conditional Robust Optimization (CRO) framework of Chenreddy et al. (2022) by replacing the coverage constraint on prediction sets with a direct robustness constraint, arguing that coverage is sufficient but not necessary for robustness. It builds on conformal prediction (Vovk et al., 2005) for finite-sample calibration via a sample-splitting procedure, and leverages the nested prediction set framework of Gupta et al. (2022) to enable efficient radius-based calibration at test time. The paper also establishes equivalence with the RA-DPO formulation of Kiyani et al. (2025), positioning CRC as a tractable, parameterized alternative to the intractable VaR-based optimal prediction set construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for risk-sensitive applications (portfolio management, energy storage, medical diagnosis) where overly conservative decisions translate directly into lost returns or suboptimal resource allocation. The demonstrated gap—CRO achieving ~98% robustness when only 90% is required, with risk certificates 2–6× higher than CRC—quantifies the cost of the coverage-to-robustness conservatism that was previously only noted qualitatively. Open questions include scaling the Lagrangian-dual optimization to high-dimensional decision spaces, developing domain-specific prediction set parameterizations, and extending the finite-sample calibration to sequential or online decision settings.
