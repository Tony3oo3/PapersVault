## Problem

Existing video relighting methods (e.g., Light-A-Video, RelightVid) can change global illumination but offer no explicit, frame-by-frame control over where and how light moves across the video. This limits their utility for narrative-driven editing where a specific light trajectory—such as a sweeping beam or a circular highlight—must be precisely followed while maintaining temporal coherence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (typically 16 frames at 512×512), a text relighting prompt, and a user-defined light trajectory (e.g., circular, linear, or multi-source paths) that is converted into a per-frame binary light mask sequence specifying the illuminated region and its position/radius. The output is a relighted video whose illumination follows the specified trajectory with temporal consistency. Inference takes approximately 240 seconds on an A6000 GPU for a 16-frame 512×512 clip.

## Method

LightCtrl is a training-free pipeline that combines IC-Light (frame-wise image relighting) with a video diffusion model (AnimateDiff) for temporal consistency. Two key modules enable trajectory control: (1) Light Map Injection replaces the VDM's initial noise within each frame's light-mask region with a weighted blend (0.8 random Gaussian + 0.2 original noise), seeding the denoising process with a trajectory prior; (2) Geometry-Aware Relighting fuses VAE-encoded normal-map latents (from Stable Normal) with the VDM's consistent-target latents in the frequency domain via a dynamic 3D Butterworth filter whose cutoff decreases linearly over denoising steps, so early steps rely on geometry to suppress source-video lighting while later steps recover high-frequency detail. A progressive fusion weight λ_t = 1 − t/T_m blends the VDM-consistent frame with the frame-wise relighted result, and a one-time detail residual between the first-step decode and the source video compensates for VDM-induced detail loss.

## Results

LightCtrl achieves the best controllability scores among all methods: PSNR_y of 11.768 dB and PSNR_light of 18.532 dB, outperforming the next-best LAV-Traj (11.043 / 17.755) by a clear margin. It also attains the lowest FVD (993.1) for video quality, slightly ahead of IC-Light (1018.5) and LAV-Traj (1077.4), while its aesthetic quality (AQ 0.6114) is competitive with LAV-Traj (0.6157). In the user study with 40 participants, LightCtrl is preferred in 68.25% of video smoothness comparisons, 77.91% for lighting controllability, 74.86% for lighting quality, and 73.73% for lighting-text alignment, far exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightCtrl decisively outperforms all four baselines on both controllability metrics: PSNR_y (11.768 vs. 11.059 for IC-Light and 11.043 for LAV-Traj) and PSNR_light (18.532 vs. 15.850 for IC-Light and 17.755 for LAV-Traj), confirming superior adherence to the user-specified light trajectory. On video quality, LightCtrl achieves the best FVD (993.1), beating IC-Light (1018.5) and LAV-Traj (1077.4), though LAV-Traj edges it slightly on aesthetic quality (0.6157 vs. 0.6114). The SDEdit variants (noise 0.2 and 0.6) perform worst overall, with FVD degrading to 1134.8 and 1630.9 respectively, as they smooth appearance without enforcing lighting consistency. In the user study, LightCtrl dominates every dimension by a wide margin (68–78% preference) compared to LAV-Traj's best of 23.50% (video smoothness) and IC-Light's best of 15.00% (lighting controllability), indicating that the combination of Light Map Injection and Geometry-Aware Relighting provides a qualitatively distinct improvement in both controllability and perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

LightCtrl sits at the intersection of training-free video editing (SDEdit, Meng et al. 2021) and video relighting (Light-A-Video, Zhou et al. 2025), repurposing IC-Light as a per-frame relighting oracle within a VDM denoising loop. Its two novel modules draw on distinct lineages: the Light Map Injection module adapts the noise-injection-for-motion-control idea from FreeTraj (Qiu et al. 2024b) to steer illumination rather than object trajectories, while the Geometry-Aware Relighting module introduces a dynamic 3D Butterworth filter in the frequency domain to fuse normal-map latents with RGB latents—a technique not previously applied to relighting. The paper departs from prior video relighting work (RelightVid, Light-A-Video) by making the lighting trajectory an explicit, user-specified control signal rather than an implicit property of the input or a fixed prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free method to offer explicit, frame-by-frame lighting trajectory control in video relighting, opening a practical workflow for content creators who need narrative-driven illumination (e.g., a light sweeping across a scene to build tension). The frequency-domain dynamic fusion strategy for combining geometry and appearance latents is a reusable technique that could transfer to other video editing tasks requiring structural guidance. Key limitations remain: the method cannot perform 3D-aware light transport, struggles with strong pre-existing shadows, and exhibits flickering when the light path crosses foreground objects—suggesting that a dedicated 3D-aware relighting architecture is still needed for physically plausible results.
