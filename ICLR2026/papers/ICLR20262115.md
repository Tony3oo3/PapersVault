## Problem

Foundation models are widely deployed in educational settings but predominantly produce text-based explanations, leaving the critical role of structured, interpretable visualizations in supporting conceptual understanding largely unaddressed. No existing benchmark systematically evaluates whether FMs can decompose complex multi-step reasoning into pedagogically effective visual representations, and empirical evidence shows that current models frequently generate semantically misaligned, structurally inconsistent, or pedagogically vacuous visuals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262115/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a STEM multiple-choice question (text plus optional visual input) from EduVisBench—1,154 problems across Maths, Physics, and Chemistry at three difficulty levels—and produces an interactive instructional webpage (HTML/Next.js with KaTeX/MathJax rendering) or a standalone SVG diagram. For evaluation, all heterogeneous outputs are canonicalized to raster images: web pages are rendered in a headless browser with all reachable interactive states (buttons, tabs, toggles) traversed and one representative screenshot retained per state, ensuring format-agnostic scoring.

## Method

EduVisAgent is a prompt-engineered multi-agent pipeline (no parameter training) that coordinates five specialized agents in two stages: (1) instructional flow construction, where a Task Planning Agent structures the problem into subgoals with FOPS-labeled steps and anticipates student misconceptions; and (2) collaborative solution generation, where a Conceptual Mapping Agent classifies information via the Concrete-Representational-Abstract (CRA) framework, a Reasoning Decomposition Agent applies the FOPS strategy to produce step-specific scaffolding, a Metacognitive Reviewer generates reflective self-questioning prompts organized by before/during/after phases, and a Visualization Agent renders pedagogical visuals (number lines, bar charts, schematic diagrams, graphic organizers) through the v0 web-deployment system. The key architectural insight is that explicit modular separation of planning, conceptual structuring, stepwise reasoning, self-review, and visual design—each grounded in a distinct pedagogical theory—yields a 40.2% relative improvement over the best single-model baseline (v0 at 58.2%), with ablation studies confirming that removing any single agent degrades performance by 4–6 points and collapsing all prompts into a monolithic single agent drops scores by 8–15 points.

## Results

On EduVisBench (1,154 STEM questions across Maths, Physics, and Chemistry at three difficulty levels), EduVisAgent achieves an overall average score of 81.6% (out of 100), substantially outperforming the best baseline v0 (58.2%) by 23.4 percentage points (40.2% relative improvement). The best LVLM, Claude 3.7 Sonnet in Webpage mode, scores 54.6%, while the best diffusion model (SDXL) reaches only 21.8%. EduVisAgent's per-subject averages are 81.6% (Maths), 83.7% (Physics), and 73.8% (Chemistry), with the largest gap over baselines appearing on Medium and Hard difficulty levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EduVisAgent outperforms all 14 baselines across every subject and difficulty level. Among diffusion models, SDXL (21.8%) is the strongest but still trails EduVisAgent by 59.8 points, confirming that static image generation is fundamentally ill-suited for explanatory visualizations. Among LVLMs, Claude 3.7 Sonnet in Webpage mode (54.6%) is the closest competitor, yet EduVisAgent still leads by 27 points; notably, Claude's SVG output (42.0%) underperforms its Webpage output by 12.6 points, and GPT-4o shows a similar Webpage-over-SVG preference (38.1% vs. 26.3%). The specialized visualization agent v0 (58.2%) is the strongest single-agent baseline, but EduVisAgent's multi-agent coordination yields a 23.4-point absolute gain, demonstrating that modular pedagogical decomposition (Task Planning, Conceptual Mapping, Reasoning Decomposition, Metacognitive Review, Visualization) provides substantial benefits over a monolithic generation pipeline. On non-STEM MMLU subsets (Prehistory, Sociology), EduVisAgent also outperforms v0 by 17.2 points (82.1% vs. 64.9%), indicating generalization beyond STEM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM-based educational agents and scientific visualization, two threads that have developed largely in parallel. Prior educational agents (EduAgent, Teachtune, MathChat, MEDCO) focus on text-based interactions, while visualization tools (Visual Sketchpad, MatplotAgent, OmniSVG) address isolated plotting or sketching steps without pedagogical structure. EduVisAgent departs from both by embedding established pedagogical theories—CRA (Concrete-Representational-Abstract), FOPS (Find-Organize-Plan-Solve), and metacognitive prompting—directly into a multi-agent pipeline, treating the full instructional workflow (from problem decomposition to interactive visual explanation) as a single coordinated task rather than a sequence of independent generation steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a striking and underappreciated gap: models that produce correct step-by-step textual solutions consistently fail to generate pedagogically useful visualizations, with even top LVLMs scoring below 55% on a 100-point scale. This suggests that visual pedagogical reasoning is a distinct capability not captured by current text-reasoning benchmarks, and that the field needs purpose-built evaluation before models can be trusted in K-12 settings. Open questions include whether the GPT-4o-based scoring generalizes beyond the tested rubric, how error propagation in the multi-agent pipeline (demonstrated in the failure case) can be mitigated, and whether the framework scales to open-ended or non-multiple-choice problems. The 40.2% improvement, while large, is measured against relatively weak baselines, so the absolute ceiling for this task remains unclear.
