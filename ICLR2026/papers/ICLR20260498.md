## Problem

Powerful uni-modal foundation models now exist for brain imaging, genomics, and clinical text, but integrating them into a unified multi-modal Alzheimer's disease diagnosis framework remains underexplored. The central challenge is enabling meaningful cross-modal interaction among these models without disrupting the robust, modality-specific representations learned from large-scale pretraining, since naively aligning or merging their heterogeneous feature spaces compromises their integrity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260498/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes four heterogeneous inputs: 3D sMRI volumes resampled to 128×128×128, fMRI time series parcellated into 450 ROIs (Schaefer-400 cortical + Tian-Scale III subcortical atlases), clinical records serialized into natural-language prompts via a text-templating strategy, and genomic sequences constructed from up to 80 variants in APOE/TOMM40 (AD) or RIMS2/TMEM108 (PD) genes as ref/alt sequence pairs. Outputs are binary or ternary diagnosis labels (NC vs. MCI, NC vs. AD, sMCI vs. pMCI; and analogously for PD), with the final prediction aggregated across four anchor configurations. Under modality-incomplete settings, missing modalities are handled by zeroing their query tokens and applying attention masks rather than requiring all four inputs.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. Stage 1 individually fine-tunes each uni-modal foundation model (BrainMVP for sMRI, Brain-JEPA for fMRI, LLaMA-2-13B for clinical text, NT-500M for genomics) with LoRA on the AD diagnosis task. Stage 2 introduces modality-anchored interaction: each modality takes turns as the anchor while the remaining three serve as auxiliaries. A Modality-aware Q-Former projects auxiliary features into the anchor's feature space using two levels of learnable queries—uni-modal Q-formers that cross-attend to each auxiliary modality's projected features, and a cross-modal Q-former that cross-attend to all uni-modal outputs to capture inter-auxiliary correlations. The resulting aligned auxiliary tokens are concatenated with the anchor input and fed jointly into the anchor model, which is further fine-tuned with LoRA to preserve its pre-trained representation space. Final predictions aggregate outputs from all four anchor configurations, and the lightweight Q-Former (~64M parameters) scales linearly with the number of modalities.

## Results

Under the modality-incomplete setting on ADNI, the proposed modality-anchored framework achieves 97.9% accuracy (SPE 95.7%, SEN 96.3%) on NC vs. MCI, 94.5% accuracy (SPE 96.0%, SEN 93.1%) on NC vs. AD, and 84.6% accuracy (SPE 90.1%, SEN 71.1%) on sMCI vs. pMCI, outperforming all multi-modal baselines by 3–10 percentage points in accuracy. Under the modality-complete setting, the method reaches 87.1% ACC on NC vs. MCI and 84.6% ACC on NC vs. AD. Cross-domain evaluation on PPMI (Parkinson's disease) yields 96.7% ACC / 96.9% AUC on NC vs. PD, and on OASIS-3 (out-of-distribution AD) the method achieves the best AUC of 0.699.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 15 baselines across every task and dataset. Among multi-modal baselines, M4Survive (the strongest competitor) is surpassed by 5.3 pp in ACC on NC vs. MCI (0.979 vs. 0.926) and 3.4 pp on NC vs. AD (0.945 vs. 0.911) under the modality-incomplete setting; LateFusion trails by 9.8 pp on NC vs. MCI. The best bi-modal baseline, AI-diagnosis* (pretrained weights), reaches 0.950 ACC on NC vs. MCI, still 2.9 pp below the proposed method. Uni-modal foundation models (LLaMA-2 at 0.793, Brain-JEPA at 0.777, BrainMVP at 0.724, NT-Human at 0.694 ACC on NC vs. MCI under the complete setting) are all substantially outperformed, confirming the value of multi-modal integration. On PPMI, the method beats M4Survive by 3.8 pp on NC vs. MCI (0.927 vs. 0.889) and 1.3 pp on NC vs. PD (0.967 vs. 0.954). On OASIS-3, the method ties M4Survive on ACC (0.722) but leads by 5.9 pp in AUC (0.699 vs. 0.640), demonstrating superior discriminative generalization to unseen data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Q-Former mechanism from BLIP-2 (which projects image features into LLM text space) into a modality-agnostic connector that can project auxiliary features into any designated anchor modality's feature space. It departs from prior multi-modal AD diagnosis work (Ncomms, AI-diagnosis, SMART) that either trains from scratch or uses symmetric late-fusion, and from M4Survive's symmetric Mamba-based adapter, by introducing an asymmetric modality-anchored interaction where one foundation model is preserved via LoRA while auxiliary modalities are selectively injected through learned queries. The framework is the first to jointly adapt four heterogeneous foundation models (BrainMVP, Brain-JEPA, LLaMA-2, Nucleotide Transformer) spanning neuroimaging, clinical text, and genomics for a unified diagnostic task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that parameter-efficient adaptation of existing uni-modal foundation models, connected by a lightweight Q-Former (~60M params), can outperform both from-scratch multi-modal networks and symmetric fusion approaches on AD diagnosis, progression prediction, and cross-domain transfer to Parkinson's disease. The biological interpretability results (e.g., gene-anchored model attending to hippocampal regions on sMRI, consistent with APOE4 neurobiology) provide evidence that the learned cross-modal interactions capture meaningful pathology rather than spurious correlations. Open questions include whether the anchor-rotation strategy is optimal compared to learned dynamic anchoring, how the framework scales beyond four modalities, and whether the strong modality-incomplete results (0.979 ACC) hold on larger, more heterogeneous clinical cohorts where data quality varies substantially.
