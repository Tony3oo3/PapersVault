## Problem

Standard benchmarks for optical flow, scene flow, and stereo evaluate model accuracy but leave robustness to real-world image corruptions (noise, rain, blur, compression artifacts) largely unquantified. While corruption-robustness studies exist for image classification, 3D object detection, and monocular depth estimation, no systematic benchmark spans all three dense-correspondence tasks with corruptions tailored to their time-, stereo-, and depth-dependent structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261830/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RobustSpring takes 2000 Spring test frames (high-resolution rendered stereo sequences) and applies 20 corruptions in a time-, stereo-, and depth-consistent manner, producing 20,000 corrupted stereo frame pairs. Models consume these corrupted stereo image pairs or video frame sequences and produce optical flow fields, disparity maps, or scene flow predictions. The benchmark then computes a ground-truth-free corruption robustness score (distance between clean and corrupted predictions) per corruption type, with aggressive subsampling to 0.05% of pixels to keep evaluation uploads tractable (~1.2 GB per model).

## Method

The core contribution is a corruption dataset and metric designed specifically for dense matching. The 20 corruptions (spanning color, blur, noise, quality, and weather) are extended beyond standard 2D image perturbations by enforcing three consistencies: time-consistent evolution across frames, stereo-consistent application to both camera views, and depth-consistent 3D rendering for weather effects (snow, rain, fog) using multi-step particle trajectories with order-independent alpha blending. The robustness metric is derived from Lipschitz continuity, measuring the distance between clean and corrupted model predictions (e.g., EPE, 1px, Fl for flow; Abs, D1 for stereo) rather than comparing to ground truth, thereby disentangling robustness from accuracy. Three ranking strategies (Average, Median, Schulze voting) aggregate the 20 per-corruption scores into a single model ranking, and the benchmark is integrated with Spring's existing accuracy leaderboard for two-axis evaluation.

## Results

On RobustSpring, SEA-RAFT achieves the lowest average corruption robustness R_EPE^c of 2.96 (median 1.20), followed closely by GMFlow (2.98 / 1.92), while PWCNet is least robust (7.25 / 1.47). Weather corruptions cause the largest degradation (rain: R_EPE^c up to 63.71 for PWCNet; snow: up to 39.79), whereas color corruptions have minimal impact (brightness: R_EPE^c as low as 0.21 for SEA-RAFT). For scene flow, M-FUSE outperforms RAFT-3D in optical flow robustness (avg R_EPE^c 3.39 vs. 5.03), and for stereo, RAFT-Stereo shows the strongest resilience across corruption groups compared to GANet and LEA Stereo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the nine optical flow models, SEA-RAFT and GMFlow achieve the lowest average R_EPE^c (2.96 and 2.98), outperforming MS-RAFT+ (3.62), FlowFormer (3.77), GMA (4.03), SPyNet (4.29), RAFT (5.64), FlowNet2 (7.01), and PWCNet (7.25). However, rankings shift under different summarization strategies: GMA drops from 5th (Average) to 2nd (Median), while GMFlow rises from 2nd (Average) to 5th (Median/Schulze), revealing that GMFlow has consistent mid-range performance without extreme outliers. FlowNet2, despite the worst average robustness, is uniquely the most noise-resilient model (e.g., Gaussian noise R_EPE^c = 1.33 vs. 4.70 for GMFlow), likely due to its stacked progressive-refinement architecture. Transformer-based models (GMFlow, FlowFormer) excel on weather and quality corruptions but are notably more sensitive to noise, while hierarchical models (MS-RAFT+) show balanced performance. For scene flow, M-FUSE is more robust than RAFT-3D on optical flow (avg R_EPE^c 3.39 vs. 5.03) and disparity (R_Abs 0.29 vs. 0.46), though both degrade severely under rain and impulse noise. For stereo, RAFT-Stereo is the most resilient model across corruption groups, while GANet and LEA Stereo exhibit extreme sensitivity to Gaussian and impulse noise (R_1px^c exceeding 80% for LEA Stereo on Gaussian noise).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image corruption robustness paradigm from 2D classification (Hendrycks & Dietterich, 2019) to dense matching tasks (optical flow, scene flow, stereo), building on the Spring stereo video dataset as its base. It departs from prior dense-matching robustness work, which has focused almost exclusively on adversarial attacks (optimized perturbations) or isolated degradations like weather or low-light, by introducing 20 non-adversarial corruptions integrated with time-, stereo-, and depth-consistency. The robustness metric draws on Lipschitz continuity from control theory and adversarial robustness literature (Schmalfuss et al., 2022b; Hein & Andriushchenko, 2017), but adapts it to a ground-truth-free stability formulation specific to dense correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RobustSpring is the first systematic corruption robustness benchmark spanning all three dense matching tasks, and its initial results reveal that every tested model is significantly corruption-sensitive, exposing robustness deficits invisible on accuracy-only benchmarks. The finding that accuracy and robustness diverge by corruption type (accurate models are more robust to weather but less robust to noise) challenges the simple accuracy-robustness tradeoff narrative from adversarial robustness and has direct implications for model selection in deployment. Limitations include a single severity level per corruption and 20 corruptions that do not cover the full perturbation space; open questions include whether robustness can be improved without accuracy loss and whether the benchmark's findings generalize beyond the Spring rendering domain.
