## Problem

Mamba2's multi-head state-space recurrence operates as an unstructured, static filter bank: effective rank analysis shows that learned heads collapse into low-rank spectral subspaces, with most heads operating in highly overlapping low-frequency regimes rather than capturing complementary spectral characteristics. No explicit mechanism exists to differentiate heads by frequency band or to adaptively select which heads process each token, limiting the model's ability to jointly capture global and local sequence dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260101/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard text token sequence (2K training length, GPT-NeoX tokenizer) and produces next-token logits for language modeling. Internally, at each timestep it computes a spectral residual (token embedding minus running sequence mean), a vector of expert selection scores, and a scalar spectral bias that modulates the delta discretization parameter. The final output is a weighted linear combination of always-active shared filter outputs and top-Q dynamically selected expert filter outputs, rather than a uniform sum over all heads as in Mamba2.

## Method

HADES reinterprets Mamba2's multi-head SSM as a graph filter bank on a line graph and decomposes the M heads into S shared filters (always active, no per-token modulation, implicitly low-pass) and E expert filters (dynamically selected per token via top-Q routing). The router computes selection scores by concatenating the base delta with the spectral residual (x_t − running mean) and projecting through a linear layer; a separate spectral bias term (γ · f_b([Δ_base ∥ r_t])) is added to the expert filters' delta to encourage high-frequency sensitivity. Training augments the standard cross-entropy loss with a load-balance loss (squared coefficient of variation over selection scores) and a diversity loss (pairwise orthogonality penalty on ℓ₂-normalized expert outputs) to prevent filter collapse. This hierarchical design achieves comparable or better performance across language modeling, commonsense reasoning, and passkey retrieval benchmarks while using only 58.9% of Mamba2's parameters (218M vs. 370M).

## Results

HADES (218M params, 58.9% of Mamba2's 370M) achieves the best average across 8 zero-shot commonsense reasoning tasks (42.91%), outperforming Mamba2 (41.63%), DeltaNet (41.33%), and RetNet (41.54%). On language modeling, HADES attains the lowest LAMBADA perplexity (21.74) and highest LAMBADA accuracy (39.24%), while matching Mamba2 on WikiText perplexity (31.48 vs. 31.34). In long-context passkey retrieval, HADES significantly outperforms Mamba2 across all context lengths from 1K to 16K tokens. HADES also achieves 1.37× faster inference and 1.37× lower memory usage than Mamba2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mamba2 (the primary baseline), HADES wins on 8 of 11 metrics including Avg. 8 tasks (42.91 vs. 41.63), LAMBADA ppl (21.74 vs. 24.38), BoolQ (58.84 vs. 53.88), WinoGrande (52.64 vs. 50.83), and PIQA (63.93 vs. 63.44), while Mamba2 retains a slight edge on WikiText ppl (31.34 vs. 31.48) and ARC-challenge (24.15 vs. 22.01). Against DeltaNet, HADES leads on Avg. 8 tasks (42.91 vs. 41.33), LAMBADA ppl (21.74 vs. 26.82), and BoolQ (58.84 vs. 54.07), though DeltaNet edges ahead on OBQA acc_n (29.60 vs. 28.80). Against RetNet, HADES dominates on LAMBADA ppl (21.74 vs. 29.46), WikiText ppl (31.48 vs. 34.12), and Avg. 8 tasks (42.91 vs. 41.54). Against Mamba1 and Linear Transformer, HADES achieves substantially better results across nearly all metrics (e.g., LAMBADA ppl 21.74 vs. 85.53 and 73.93 respectively). Against the mixture-of-memories variant MoM, HADES achieves a higher Avg. 8 tasks score (42.91 vs. 42.17) and lower LAMBADA ppl (21.74 vs. 23.28) while using fewer parameters. Notably, HADES achieves all these gains with only 218M parameters (58.9% of the 370M baselines), and at 1.3B scale it maintains its advantage with 71.4% of parameters (Table 4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mamba2's multi-head SSM architecture, reinterpreting each recurrent head as a graph filter operating on a line graph (tokens as nodes, temporal edges). It departs from prior delta-modulation work (DeciMamba, MambaExtend, LongMamba) that adjusted Δ for long-context extrapolation by instead using Δ modulation as a spectral-bias mechanism tied to a per-token residual signal (x_t − μ_t). The routing mechanism draws on Mixture-of-Attention-Heads (MoA/MoH) and MoE load-balancing losses, but is positioned within a GSP filter-bank framework rather than a capacity-expansion one. The paper also responds to Wang et al. (2025), who identified recency bias and information bottlenecks in SSMs, by showing that Mamba2's heads collapse into low-rank spectral subspaces and proposing a structured shared/expert decomposition to restore filter diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GSP filter-bank reinterpretation provides a principled, interpretable lens for understanding and designing multi-head SSMs, moving beyond the 'black-box recurrence' view. The 58.9% parameter reduction while matching or exceeding Mamba2 on language modeling, commonsense reasoning, and passkey retrieval makes this a practically relevant efficiency gain for SSM-based language models. Open questions include whether the line-graph filter-bank structure generalizes to higher-dimensional or non-sequential graphs, whether the spectral-residual routing remains effective at much larger scales (the 1.3B experiment shows only 71.4% parameter savings), and whether the shared/expert split can be made fully differentiable or learned end-to-end without the Top-Q hard selection.
