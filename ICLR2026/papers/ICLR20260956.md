## Problem

Conformal prediction for classification relies on softmax-derived nonconformity scores, but softmax outputs are unreliable indicators of true model uncertainty—they can be overconfident on misclassified or out-of-distribution inputs. This unreliability is inherited by the prediction sets, limiting their adaptiveness: sets can be deceptively small for difficult inputs or unnecessarily large for easy ones, undermining the practical utility of the distribution-free coverage guarantee.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and the pre-softmax logit vector from a trained deep classifier (ResNet-56 or ResNet-50) as input, and produces a prediction set of class labels whose size adapts to per-sample difficulty. No additional model, ensemble, or post-hoc calibration step is required; the energy score is computed directly from the existing logits via a closed-form expression, making the approach a drop-in modification to any existing conformal classifier pipeline.

## Method

The paper reinterprets a standard classifier through an Energy-Based Model lens, defining a joint energy E(x,y) = −f_y(x) and deriving the Helmholtz free energy F(x) = −τ log Σ_k exp(f_k(x)/τ) as a principled measure of epistemic uncertainty that is linearly proportional to the negative log-likelihood of the model-implied data density. The core mechanism is a per-sample reweighting of any base adaptive nonconformity score S(x,y) by a softplus of the negative free energy: S_Energy(x,y) = S(x,y) · (1/β) log(1 + e^{−βF(x)}). For easy in-distribution inputs with large −F(x), the scaling factor magnifies the base score, pushing incorrect labels above the conformal quantile and yielding smaller, more efficient sets; for hard or OOD inputs with small or negative −F(x), the factor dampens the score, producing larger sets that signal uncertainty. The paper proves this reweighting is mathematically equivalent to applying a sample-dependent threshold θ(x) = q̂/G(x) to the original score, and validates the approach across APS, RAPS, and SAPS on CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and Places365 under balanced, long-tailed, and OOD evaluation settings.

## Results

Across CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and Places365, energy-based variants of APS, RAPS, and SAPS maintain target coverage (e.g., 0.95 at α=0.05) while consistently reducing average prediction set size. The most substantial gains appear at stricter coverage levels: on CIFAR-100 at α=0.01, RAPS set size drops from 30.88 to 23.63 (−23%), SAPS from 29.80 to 22.90 (−23%), and APS from 25.79 to 22.90 (−11%). On ImageNet at α=0.01, reductions range from 11% (APS: 39.08→32.93) to 16% (SAPS: 35.97→30.24). Under OOD evaluation (CIFAR-100-trained model tested on Places365), energy-based APS produces dramatically larger sets (86.76 vs. 6.18 at α=0.1), signaling appropriate uncertainty for out-of-distribution inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three state-of-the-art adaptive conformal scores—APS (Romano et al., 2020), RAPS (Angelopoulos et al., 2021), and SAPS (Huang et al., 2024)—each with and without the proposed Helmholtz free energy reweighting. All three baselines are consistently outperformed by their energy-based counterparts on set size across every dataset and α level, while coverage remains at the target. RAPS shows the largest relative improvement (up to 23% set size reduction on CIFAR-100 at α=0.01), likely because its rank-based score is most sensitive to the energy modulation. SAPS achieves the smallest absolute set sizes overall and also benefits substantially (e.g., 29.80→22.90 on CIFAR-100 at α=0.01). APS shows the smallest relative gains but still improves at every α. Under OOD evaluation (Table 3), the energy-based variants produce far larger prediction sets for out-of-distribution inputs (e.g., APS OOD set size jumps from 6.18 to 86.76 at α=0.1), directly addressing the paper's Desideratum 1 for adaptive uncertainty response, whereas the baselines remain deceptively small.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends adaptive conformal classification (APS, RAPS, SAPS) by introducing a per-sample reweighting factor derived from Helmholtz free energy computed in the pre-softmax logit space, drawing on the Energy-Based Model interpretation of classifiers (Grathwohl et al., 2020) and the OOD detection literature (Liu et al., 2020). Conceptually, it generalizes the Normalized Conformal Prediction principle of scaling a base score with an uncertainty estimate (Papadopoulos et al., 2002) from regression to classification, but replaces external uncertainty estimators (ensembles, MCD) with a signal intrinsic to the model's own logits. Unlike training-time conformal methods (ConfTr, uncertainty-aware conformal loss) or approaches requiring second-order predictors (BPS, EPICSCORE), the proposed modulation is a purely post-hoc, zero-cost operation applied to any existing nonconformity score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a simple, drop-in enhancement that consistently improves prediction set efficiency and adaptiveness across datasets, architectures, and distributional regimes (balanced, long-tailed, OOD) while preserving coverage guarantees, making it practically attractive for deploying conformal classifiers. A key open question is the practical utility of the very large OOD set sizes produced (e.g., ~87–93 classes for APS w/Energy on Places365), which technically signal uncertainty but may be uninformative to end users; a principled abstention mechanism or set-size cap would complement the approach. Additionally, the method's effectiveness is tied to the quality of the underlying model's energy landscape, so its behavior under severe distribution shift or with poorly trained models remains to be characterized.
