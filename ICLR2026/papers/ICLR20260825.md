## Problem

Existing RAG extraction attacks (e.g., RAG-Thief, DGEA, Pirates of RAG) rely on malicious inputs such as prompt injection or jailbreak prompts, making them easily neutralized by standard input-level defenses (intention detection, keyword filtering) and output-level defenses (content overlap checking). This paper asks whether an attacker can instead mimic a normal user and extract substantial knowledge through entirely benign, natural-looking queries that bypass all such detection mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260825/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker requires only a public topic keyword (e.g., 'healthcare'), black-box access to the RAG system's query-response interface, an LLM for query generation, and a sentence embedding model for similarity computation. The attacker has no knowledge of the target LLM, retriever, or embedding model. Output is a collection of query-response pairs whose responses collectively cover the RAG knowledge base; these pairs can be assembled into a substitute RAG system that achieves near-original performance on downstream MCQ and QA tasks. Each round issues exactly one query and receives one response, with a fixed budget (e.g., 256 rounds).

## Method

IKEA operates through an evolving anchor concepts database initialized from the topic keyword with constrained pairwise dissimilarity to ensure semantic diversity. Two complementary mechanisms drive exploration: (1) Experience Reflection Sampling maintains a query-response history and assigns penalty scores to anchor concepts associated with unrelated or refused responses, then samples the next anchor via a softmax over accumulated penalties, steering queries toward productive regions; (2) Trust Region Directed Mutation (TRDM) iteratively mutates the current anchor under a similarity constraint whose radius is proportional to the query-response semantic distance (used as a proxy for local document density), selecting the mutated word that minimizes similarity to the original query to push exploration toward unexplored areas. Mutation stops when responses indicate diminishing returns (high overlap with prior queries/responses or refusal). Queries are generated as natural, context-aware sentences centered on each anchor concept, containing no directive or suspicious language, which is what allows IKEA to evade both input-level and output-level defenses that block verbatim-extraction-style attacks.

## Results

Under the Input-Ensemble defense (intention detection + keyword filtering + defensive instructions), all three baselines (RAG-Thief, DGEA, PoR) are completely blocked with zero metrics across all five datasets, while IKEA maintains Extraction Efficiency (EE) of 0.56–0.88 and Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 0.59–0.92. Under the Output defense (Rouge-L content detection), baselines drop to near-zero (e.g., DGEA achieves EE=0 on Pokémon), whereas IKEA retains EE of 0.57–0.85 and ASR of 0.64–0.94. Under no defense, IKEA surpasses the strongest baseline (DGEA) by over 80% in EE (e.g., 0.87 vs. 0.41 on HealthCareMagic) and achieves comparable or higher Semantic Similarity (SS) despite lower Chunk Recovery Rate (CRR), confirming it extracts effective knowledge without verbatim reproduction. The substitute RAG built from IKEA's extractions achieves 0.43 MCQ accuracy under Input-Ensemble defense versus 0 for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IKEA decisively outperforms all three baselines under every defense setting. Under Input-Ensemble defense, RAG-Thief, DGEA, and PoR are entirely neutralized (all metrics = 0) because their malicious prompts (prompt injection, jailbreak) are detected and blocked, while IKEA's benign queries pass undetected, achieving EE of 0.56–0.88 and ASR of 0.59–0.92. Under Output defense (Rouge-L threshold 0.5), DGEA collapses to near-zero on most datasets (e.g., EE=0 on Pokémon, ASR=0.02 on HarryPotter) because its verbatim extraction triggers content filtering, and RAG-Thief drops to EE=0.04–0.36; IKEA is barely affected (EE drops by <3% from no-defense) since it never requests verbatim reproduction. Under no defense, DGEA achieves higher CRR (e.g., 0.96 vs. 0.28 on HealthCareMagic) due to verbatim extraction, but IKEA surpasses it in EE by 112% (0.87 vs. 0.41) and in SS (0.71 vs. 0.57), demonstrating more efficient and semantically faithful knowledge acquisition. For the substitute RAG evaluation (Table 2), IKEA achieves 0.43 MCQ accuracy and 0.33 semantic similarity under Input-Ensemble, while RAG-Thief and DGEA score 0 on accuracy and near-zero on similarity, confirming IKEA is the only method that produces usable extracted knowledge under realistic defenses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of RAG extraction attacks (RAG-Thief, DGEA, Pirates of RAG) that rely on malicious inputs such as prompt injection or jailbreaking to force verbatim document reproduction. Rather than building on any single prior method, IKEA reframes the extraction problem as a semantic-space exploration task: it repurposes the embedding-similarity structure that RAG retrievers exploit for legitimate retrieval into an iterative search strategy (Experience Reflection Sampling + Trust Region Directed Mutation) that systematically covers the knowledge base using only natural-language queries. The anchor-concept mechanism draws loosely on query-expansion ideas from information retrieval, but the iterative mutation under trust-region constraints and the history-based penalty sampling are novel contributions specific to the adversarial extraction setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that the entire mainstream defense paradigm for RAG (input-level intention detection, keyword filtering, defensive instructions, and output-level content detection) is fundamentally incompatible with benign-query attacks, as all baselines are completely blocked while IKEA retains ~90% extraction efficiency. This has immediate practical implications for any organization deploying RAG with standard safeguards, as the extracted knowledge is sufficient to build a substitute RAG system performing close to the original on downstream tasks. Open questions include whether adaptive defenses (e.g., retrieval perturbation) can be deployed without unacceptable utility loss, how the approach scales to very large or multi-tenant knowledge bases, and whether detection methods trained specifically on benign-query attack patterns can close the gap without degrading normal user experience.
