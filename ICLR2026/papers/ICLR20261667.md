## Problem

Existing cross-modal retrieval objectives (contrastive losses, IOT) collapse all embedding dimensions into a single scalar similarity, implicitly assuming every channel contributes equally to semantic alignment. Under small mini-batches, this uniform aggregation lets a few noisy or modality-specific channels dominate the scalar score, producing unstable gradients and biased metrics. No prior method explicitly models the heterogeneity across feature channels to filter out spurious alignment signals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 10-second audio clips (AudioCaps) or 15–30-second clips (Clotho) paired with human-annotated text captions, encoded by modality-specific encoders (e.g., ResNet38/Beats/CNN for audio, BERT/BPE for text) into fixed-dimensional embeddings. At inference, retrieval is bidirectional: a query audio clip is scored against all candidate captions (and vice versa) via softmax-normalized Euclidean distance, returning the top-k ranked matches. The same framework transfers to image-text retrieval on MSCOCO and zero-shot sound event detection on ESC-50 without architectural changes.

## Method

DART augments the standard instance-level IOT loss (KL divergence between the Sinkhorn-induced coupling and the ground-truth one-hot matching) with a feature-level regularizer that treats each embedding channel as an independent distribution across the mini-batch and minimizes the Unbalanced Wasserstein Distance between audio and text channel distributions. The key mechanism is Reliability-Aware Marginals (RAM): per-channel reliability scores are computed from cross-modal correlation, variance instability, and kurtosis, then normalized into probability marginals that bias the UWD transport plan toward stable semantic channels while suppressing noisy or modality-specific ones. An EMA update stabilizes these scores across training steps. Theoretically, the paper proves that the instance-level loss concentrates around a bound governed by the maximum paired distance D_max, whereas the feature-level loss is controlled by the Frobenius norm of the optimal transport plan ‖P*‖_F, yielding tighter guarantees and greater robustness under small batches and noisy labels.

## Results

On AudioCaps with ResNet38+BERT encoders (batch size 256), DART w/ RAM achieves R@1 of 41.67 (T→A) and 55.27 (A→T), outperforming the strongest baseline Luong et al. (2024) by 2.57% and 5.33% respectively. On Clotho, DART w/ RAM reaches R@1 of 17.18 (T→A) and 23.54 (A→T). Under the constrained CNN+BPE setting (batch size 6), DART w/ RAM achieves R@1 of 33.42 (T→A) and 43.30 (A→T) on AudioCaps, surpassing ONE-PEACE (Wang et al., 2023) by 4.16% on A→T. On zero-shot ESC-50 sound event detection, DART attains R@1 of 80.75% and mAP of 87.78%, and on MSCOCO image-text retrieval it reaches R@1 of 21.27 (I→T) and 23.34 (T→I).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DART w/ RAM outperforms the strongest IOT-based baseline Luong et al. (2024) by 2.57% (T→A) and 5.33% (A→T) in R@1 on AudioCaps with ResNet38+BERT, and by 1.44% and 1.44% on Clotho A→T R@1. Against the contrastive-learning baselines (Oncescu et al., Mei et al., Deshmukh et al., Wu et al.), DART leads by 13–22% in R@1 across all four methods on AudioCaps A→T. Under the constrained CNN+BPE setting (batch size 6), DART w/ RAM surpasses ONE-PEACE (Wang et al., 2023) in 5 of 8 metrics, most notably by 4.16% on AudioCaps A→T R@1 and 6.32% on Clotho A→T R@1. With Beats+BERT encoders, DART w/ RAM improves over VAST (Chen et al., 2023) by 2.7% (T→A) and 5.2% (A→T) on AudioCaps R@1. Under noisy and semi-supervised conditions (Table 2, batch size 32), DART consistently outperforms both triplet loss (Wei et al., 2021) and contrastive loss (Luong et al., 2024) by 5–15% in R@1, with the gap widening as noise increases. On MSCOCO image-text retrieval, DART improves over OT-CLIP (Shi et al., 2024) by 2.12% (I→T) and 2.44% (T→I) in R@1, confirming cross-domain generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

DART extends the inverse optimal transport (IOT) framework for cross-modal retrieval (Li et al., 2019; Shi et al., 2023) by adding a feature-level regularization layer that prior IOT-based methods lack. While Luong et al. (2024) introduced per-channel reweighting for audio-text retrieval, their approach still collapses weighted embeddings into a single pairwise scalar, preserving the uniform-aggregation vulnerability under small batches. DART departs from this by treating each embedding channel as an independent matching unit in an Unbalanced Wasserstein Distance problem, with reliability-aware marginals (variance, kurtosis, cross-modal correlation) steering transport mass toward stable semantic dimensions. The theoretical contribution—showing that instance-level IOT concentration scales with worst-case pair distance D_max while feature-level UWD scales with the Frobenius norm of the transport plan—provides a principled justification for the dual-level design that prior channel-weighting heuristics did not offer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in cross-modal retrieval: performance degradation under small batch sizes and noisy/scarce labels, which are common in real-world deployments with limited compute. The feature-level UWD loss is modular (demonstrated to plug into triplet, contrastive, and IOT losses alike) and introduces negligible GPU overhead, making it an accessible add-on for existing pipelines. The transfer to image-text retrieval (MSCOCO) and zero-shot sound event detection (ESC-50) suggests the channel-level transport view is not audio-specific. Open questions include whether the RAM statistics (correlation, variance, kurtosis) are near-optimal priors, how the method scales to very high-dimensional encoders (d > 2048) without projection, and whether the theoretical concentration bounds are tight enough to predict actual training dynamics beyond the asymptotic regime.
