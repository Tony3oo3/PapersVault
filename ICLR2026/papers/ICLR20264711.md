## Problem

Current LLM-powered feature engineering methods assign the LLM both the role of proposing and selecting feature transformation operations, relying solely on its internal heuristics that lack calibrated estimates of operation utility and uncertainty. This leads to repeated exploration of low-yield transformations and poor performance under limited iteration budgets. The paper asks whether decoupling proposal from selection via explicit Bayesian modeling, combined with selectively elicited human expert preference feedback, can yield more principled and efficient feature engineering.

## Input / Output

The method takes a tabular dataset (features + target), task metadata (objective, column names/types, missingness notes), and a performance history of previously evaluated operations. Each round, the LLM generates 15 candidate feature operations as structured JSON entries containing a snake_case name, explanation, reasoning, executable Python code, and expected benefit. The output is a sequence of selected feature transformation operations that are appended as new columns to the training and validation sets only when they yield positive utility gain. Human preference feedback, when elicited, takes the form of a binary pairwise comparison indicating which of two candidate operations is more promising.

## Method

The framework decouples LLM-based proposal from selection: the LLM samples candidate operations from its internal distribution, while a Bayesian Neural Network surrogate (trained via variational inference on accumulated performance history) estimates the expected utility μ_t(e) and uncertainty σ_t²(e) of each candidate using concatenated semantic embeddings and column-usage binary vectors. Selection follows an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) criterion with a theoretically grounded β_t schedule (Lemma 3.1) that guarantees uniform confidence bounds over all candidates. When a human expert is available, the framework selectively elicits pairwise preference feedback only when two conditions hold: (C1) the UCB of the second-best candidate exceeds the LCB of the UCB-optimal candidate (confidence interval overlap), and (C2) the maximum possible utility gain √β_t(σ_t(e_a)+σ_t(e_b)) exceeds a cost threshold γ_κ. The elicited preference is incorporated as a probit likelihood into the BNN posterior, updating the surrogate's belief before the final selection is made from the queried pair.

## Results

On 13 classification datasets with GPT-4o as the LLM backbone, the proposed method (Ours w/o human) achieves an average error reduction of 7.24% (MLP) and 9.02% (XGBoost) over the best baseline, while Ours (w/ human) improves these to 8.96% and 11.23% respectively. On the proprietary conversion dataset, Ours (w/ human) reaches 92.6% AUROC vs. 91.1% for OCTree. The method also generalizes across backbone LLMs (GPT-3.5-Turbo, GPT-4o, GPT-5, DeepSeek-V3), consistently outperforming all baselines in every setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two AutoML baselines (OpenFE, AutoGluon) and two LLM-based baselines (CAAFE, OCTree). The proposed method wins on all 13 classification datasets under both MLP and XGBoost evaluators, with the largest gains on flight (+48.1% error reduction with human feedback, MLP) and conversion (+20.2%). Among LLM-based baselines, OCTree is consistently the strongest, but the proposed method still outperforms it by 1–4 AUROC points on most datasets. The selective human preference feedback mechanism adds a further 1.72% (MLP) and 3.21% (XGBoost) average error reduction over the no-human variant, confirming that targeted expert input improves selection. On 5 regression datasets (housing, forest, bike, crab, insurance), the same pattern holds: Ours (w/ human) achieves the lowest NRMSE in 4 of 5 cases for both MLP and XGBoost. The framework is robust to backbone choice, maintaining its lead even with GPT-3.5-Turbo as the generator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two converging threads: LLM-powered feature engineering (CAAFE, OCTree), where the LLM acts as a black-box optimizer handling both proposal and selection, and human-in-the-loop Bayesian optimization (AV et al. 2022; Xu et al. 2024a), which selectively queries human experts when expected information gain justifies the cost. The key departure is decoupling these roles: the LLM is restricted to generating candidate operations, while selection is governed by an explicit Bayesian neural network surrogate that models utility and uncertainty over operation embeddings, with a principled UCB acquisition function. The selective human preference elicitation mechanism, triggered only when confidence intervals overlap and the potential gain exceeds a cost threshold, extends the on-demand advisor paradigm from prior human-AI collaboration work into the feature engineering setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, scalable recipe for improving LLM-powered feature engineering without requiring the LLM to make calibrated utility judgments—a known weakness. The user study with 31 ML practitioners provides rare empirical validation that algorithmic selective querying reduces cognitive load while improving outcomes, a finding with direct implications for designing human-AI tools in data science workflows. Limitations include the reliance on a GPT-4o proxy to simulate human expertise in the synthetic study (rather than real domain experts), the linearity assumption (A.1) underlying the theoretical guarantees, and evaluation limited to tabular settings with relatively small feature spaces. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-objective or sequential feature engineering, how it behaves when the human collaborator has genuinely noisy or biased preferences, and whether the BNN surrogate can be replaced by lighter-weight models for very large operation pools.
