## Problem

Video Large Language Models (VLLMs) process large numbers of visual tokens, causing quadratic attention cost that limits practical deployment. Existing acceleration frameworks compress spatial and temporal redundancy independently, but in dynamic video, semantically correlated elements shift in position, scale, and orientation across frames, so rigid spatial correspondence (e.g., merging tokens at fixed locations) erroneously merges less-correlated features and distorts video representations. The paper asks how to achieve joint spatiotemporal compression that respects these dynamic attributes without training.

## Input / Output

FlashVID is a training-free, plug-and-play module inserted into existing VLLMs (LLaVA-OneVision, LLaVA-Video, Qwen2.5-VL) that takes uniformly sampled video frames (e.g., 32 or 64 frames) and a text prompt, and produces a standard text response. It operates as a hybrid compression: before-LLM compression via ADTS and TSTM reduces tokens entering the LLM, followed by inner-LLM pruning at a high layer (K=20) to meet a fixed token budget. A key practical property is that under a fixed computational budget, FlashVID enables processing up to 10× more video frames (e.g., 160 vs. 16 for Qwen2.5-VL) by compressing each frame's tokens more aggressively, yielding an 8.6% relative performance gain over the vanilla model.

## Method

FlashVID combines two synergistic modules. First, Attention and Diversity-based Token Selection (ADTS) formulates per-frame token selection as a calibrated Max-Min Diversity Problem (MMDP), where the pairwise cosine distance matrix is calibrated by [CLS] attention scores and event relevance (similarity to globally-pooled frame embeddings), ensuring both informativeness and diversity of retained tokens. Second, Tree-based Spatiotemporal Token Merging (TSTM) constructs spatiotemporal redundancy trees by computing cosine similarity between all token pairs in adjacent frames and linking each token to its most similar counterpart in the previous frame if similarity exceeds a threshold T_τ; tokens within each tree are then aggregated (e.g., mean pooling) into a single compact representation. Crucially, TSTM does not enforce fixed spatial correspondence—each token can link to any spatially displaced counterpart in the neighboring frame—thereby capturing dynamic position, scale, and orientation changes that rigid temporal merging misses. The two modules are balanced by a retention ratio α (optimal at 0.7), and the overall pipeline is entirely training-free and architecture-agnostic.

## Results

On LLaVA-OneVision, FlashVID preserves 99.1% relative accuracy at 10% token retention (57.9 avg score vs. 58.4 vanilla), and notably surpasses the vanilla model (100.3–100.5% relative accuracy) at 25%, 20%, and 15% retention, revealing a 'less is more' pattern. On Qwen2.5-VL under a fixed computational budget, FlashVID enables processing 10× more video frames (160 vs. 16), achieving 108.6% relative accuracy—an 8.6% absolute gain over the vanilla 16-frame baseline. Efficiency analysis shows 6.3× prefilling speedup and 2.1× TTFT speedup at R=10% on LLaVA-OneVision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashVID consistently outperforms all four baselines (FastV, VisionZip, PruneVID, FastVID) across every retention ratio, benchmark, and VLLM tested. At the most aggressive compression (R=10%) on LLaVA-OneVision, FlashVID achieves 99.1% relative accuracy, beating the closest competitor FastVID (97.8%) by 1.3 points and VisionZip (92.5%) by 6.6 points. At moderate compression (R=25%), FlashVID is the only method to surpass the vanilla model (100.3% vs. 100.0%), while VisionZip (99.7%) and FastVID (99.0%) fall slightly short. On Qwen2.5-VL at R=10%, FlashVID (95.6%) leads FastVID (93.2%) and VisionZip (93.7%) by 2.4 and 1.9 points respectively. Under the fixed-token-budget setting on Qwen2.5-VL, FlashVID with 10× frames (160 frames) achieves 108.6% relative accuracy, outperforming FastVID (105.9%) and VisionZip (106.1%) at the same frame count, demonstrating superior ability to exploit longer temporal context.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashVID builds on the Max-Min Diversity Problem formulation from DivPrune (Alvar et al., 2025) for token selection and on the DySeg video partitioning strategy from FastVID (Shen et al., 2025), but departs fundamentally from prior VLLM acceleration methods by rejecting the decoupled treatment of spatial and temporal redundancy. Where FastV, VisionZip, PruneVID, and FastVID each compress spatial and temporal dimensions independently (e.g., frame-wise [CLS] attention pruning followed by fixed-location temporal merging), FlashVID introduces a unified tree-based structure (TSTM) that links tokens across frames by feature similarity rather than spatial correspondence, explicitly accounting for the fact that semantically correlated elements shift in position, scale, and orientation over time. The ADTS module further calibrates the MMDP selection with [CLS] attention and event relevance terms, a combination not present in prior diversity-based or attention-based selectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play module, FlashVID lowers the barrier to efficient video understanding: practitioners can drop it into LLaVA-OneVision, LLaVA-Video, or Qwen2.5-VL without retraining, gaining 6.3× prefilling speedup at 10% token retention while preserving 99.1% accuracy. The practical ability to process 10× more frames within the same computational budget (yielding an 8.6% relative improvement) addresses a core deployment bottleneck for long-video understanding. Limitations include failure cases where TSTM merges tokens from distinct entities with similar semantics, and the method's reliance on a fixed merging threshold (T_τ = 0.8) that may not adapt to all video types. Open questions include whether the tree-based approach scales to very long videos (hours) without accumulating merging errors, and whether the 'less is more' effect (surpassing vanilla at 15–25% retention) generalizes to other architectures or task types.
