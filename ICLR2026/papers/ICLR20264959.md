## Problem

Existing LLM interpretability methods (linear probes, sparse autoencoders) capture only linearly extractable or isolated features, overlooking the high-dimensional, relational, and nonlinear geometry of internal representations. Moreover, prior work examines single attack classes in isolation, leaving open whether qualitatively different adversarial mechanisms—such as inference-time prompt injection versus training-time backdoor fine-tuning—imprint a shared geometric signature on the model's latent space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes last-token activation vectors from each transformer layer (point clouds in R^D, D≈4096) and, for the local analysis, 2D embeddings of per-neuron activation pairs between layer pairs. Because full PH computation is intractable at scale, the pipeline subsamples K=64 subsets of k=4096 points per layer and condition, then vectorizes each resulting barcode into a 41-dimensional summary (statistics of 0-bar and 1-bar births, deaths, persistences, ratios, counts, total persistence, and persistent entropy). Outputs include the topological compression signature (increased mean 0-bar death, reduced 1-bar count, extended 1-bar lifetime), a clean-vs-adversarial classifier with SHAP-based feature attribution, and a local dispersion ratio linking layer-wise geometry to task-level behavior (executed, refused, ignored).

## Method

The core technique applies persistent homology via Vietoris-Rips filtrations to activation point clouds, producing persistence barcodes whose 0-bars track connected components and 1-bars track loops across a growing distance threshold. Two complementary analyses are performed: a global layer-wise pipeline that computes barcodes per layer, prunes correlated summary features via cross-correlation, and uses PCA, CCA, logistic regression, and SHAP values to identify and interpret the topological features driving clean-vs-adversarial separation; and a local neuron-level pipeline that embeds per-neuron activation pairs between consecutive layers into 2D, revealing a phase transition in topological complexity of information flow around mid-network layers. The key finding is that adversarial inputs—whether indirect prompt injection or backdoor fine-tuning (sandbagging)—induce a consistent 'topological compression': the latent space collapses from many compact, small-scale, high-entropy structures into fewer, dominant, large-scale loops, a signature that is architecture-agnostic across six models (3.8B–70B parameters) and emerges early in the network. The approach is coordinate-free and multiscale, enabling direct comparison across architectures, attack types, and fine-tuning stages in a way that linear probes and sparse autoencoders cannot.

## Results

Persistent homology (PH) barcode summaries achieve perfect 1.0000 accuracy and AUC-ROC in distinguishing clean from adversarial (poisoned) activations across all five tested layers (1, 8, 16, 24, 32) of Mistral 7B, and this extends uniformly to all six models evaluated (Phi3-mini 3.8B, Mistral 7B, LLaMA3 8B, Mixtral-8×7B, Phi3-medium 14B, LLaMA3 70B). The consistent topological compression signature—adversarial inputs increase the mean death time of 0-bars, reduce the number of 1-bars, and extend the mean lifetime of remaining 1-bars—holds across architectures and both attack modes (indirect prompt injection and backdoor fine-tuning). SHAP analysis identifies mean 0-bar deaths and 1-bar count as the dominant discriminative features, with clean inputs showing more compact, higher-entropy topological structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PH barcode summaries against three linear baselines—LDA, linear SVM, and logistic regression—each evaluated both on raw 4096-d activations and after sparse autoencoder (SAE) dimensionality reduction to 128 dimensions. Without SAE, LDA and LR are competitive in later layers (reaching 1.0000 at layers 16–32), but SVM lags in early layers (0.8875 at layer 1). With SAE, all linear methods degrade substantially: SVM drops to 0.6425 at layer 8 and LR to 0.6225, while PH maintains 1.0000 across every layer. The authors emphasize that PH's value is not merely superior classification accuracy but interpretability: SHAP values on the logistic regression over barcode summaries reveal *which* topological features (mean 0-bar deaths, 1-bar count) drive the separation, something linear probes on raw activations cannot provide. The topological compression signature is further validated as robust against adaptive attacks from the LLMail-Inject red-teaming dataset, where attacks are explicitly designed to evade activation-based defenses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the application of persistent homology from smaller architectures (CNNs, MLPs; Naitzat et al. 2020) and early transformer analyses (Gardinazzi et al. 2024, who used zigzag persistence for layer pruning) to large-scale LLM activation spaces (3.8B–70B parameters) under adversarial conditions. It builds on the barcode summary-statistic pipeline of Ali et al. (2023) and the subsampling convergence guarantees of Chazal et al. (2015) to make PH computationally tractable at LLM scale. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant linear-probe and sparse-autoencoder interpretability paradigm by capturing coordinate-free, multiscale relational geometry that those methods are structurally blind to, and it is the first to test whether qualitatively distinct attack mechanisms (inference-time prompt injection vs. training-time backdoor fine-tuning) imprint a shared topological signature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that two mechanistically unrelated attacks produce the same 'topological compression' signature across six architectures suggests a universal geometric fingerprint of adversarial influence that could inform architecture-agnostic detection and safety monitoring. The framework is complementary to linear methods: it explains *why* linear probes separate clean from adversarial activations (the underlying geometric collapse) rather than merely confirming that they do. Key limitations include the restriction to 0- and 1-dimensional homology, the absence of semantic interpretation for identified cycles, and the fact that the analysis is purely observational—no training-time intervention leverages the topological signal. Open questions include whether topological compression generalizes to all forms of model misalignment and how topological awareness might be incorporated into training objectives or architecture design.
