## Problem

Current medical VLMs operate as monolithic black boxes that map images and questions directly to answers without explicitly localizing or verifying the supporting visual findings, inviting shortcut learning and hallucination that undermine clinical accountability. Existing grounding-augmented approaches either treat segmentation as an isolated perception head whose outputs are not fed back into reasoning, or couple perception and reasoning within a single generalist model where early grounding errors amplify into confident hallucinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260363/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a medical image (spanning 10+ modalities including CT, MRI, X-ray, PET, dermoscopy) and a free-form text question as input. It produces a VQA answer wrapped in structured  and  tags, accompanied by pixel-level ROI segmentation masks and a full multi-step reasoning trace. The EG-VQA model consumes the original image plus one of three evidence views (a zoom-in crop, a binary mask overlay, or a global indicator) as a multi-image input, with mask metadata (entity name and confidence score) appended in the prompt.

## Method

CARE decomposes medical VQA into three specialized submodules that emulate the clinical diagnostic workflow: (1) a compact InternVL3-2B entity-proposal VLM fine-tuned with RLVR using a Kuhn-Munkres embedding-similarity reward to propose relevant anatomical structures; (2) a 600M referring-segmentation model built on SA-Med-2D augmented with a frozen BioClinicalBERT text encoder that produces pixel-level ROI masks with confidence scores; and (3) an InternVL3-2B/8B EG-VQA model fine-tuned via SFT+DAPO that reasons over the full image augmented by one of three evidence views. A dynamic coordinator (GPT-5 or a fine-tuned InternVL3-8B) plans tool invocations, selects the most informative evidence view, and performs iterative chain-of-thought-to-answer consistency review, while a static CARE-Flow variant executes all three evidence views and aggregates via majority vote. The key architectural insight is decoupling perception and reasoning into independently optimized specialist models with explicit pixel-level evidence fed back into the reasoning step, reducing shortcut learning and error propagation compared to single-model approaches.

## Results

On four standard medical VQA benchmarks (OmniMedVQA-3k, VQA-RAD, SLAKE, VQA-Med-2019), CARE-Flow-B (10B total parameters) achieves 74.91% overall accuracy, surpassing the heavily-trained Lingshu-32B (72.29%) by 2.6% and GPT-5 (66.97%) by 7.9%. Adding the agentic coordinator (CARE-Coord-B) further improves overall accuracy to 77.54%, outperforming Lingshu-32B by 5.2% and GPT-5 by 10.6%. Notably, even the 4B CARE-Flow-S variant (70.72%) exceeds all open-source generalist VLMs up to 38B parameters (e.g., InternVL3-38B at 67.26%), demonstrating strong parameter efficiency with only ~10k VQA training samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARE-Flow-B (10B) outperforms the heavily-trained Lingshu-32B (72.29%) by 2.6% overall and CARE-Coord-B extends this lead to 5.2%, despite using only ~10k VQA training samples versus Lingshu's 12M+. Against proprietary models, CARE-Coord-B (77.54%) surpasses GPT-5 (66.97%) by 10.6% and GPT-4o (61.44%) by 16.1%. Among open-source generalist VLMs, CARE-Flow-S (4B, 70.72%) beats InternVL3-38B (67.26%) by 3.5% and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (60.84%) by 9.9%, while CARE-Flow-B (74.91%) exceeds InternVL3-78B (70.10%) by 4.8%. Against medical-specific VLMs, CARE-Flow-B outperforms HuatuoGPT-Vision-34B (65.57%) by 9.3%, MedGemma-27B (61.47%) by 13.4%, and MedVLM-R1-2B (51.35%) by 23.6%. The one notable exception is SLAKE, where CARE-Coord-B (83.11%) slightly trails Lingshu-32B (82.25%) is actually beaten, but CARE-Coord-S (77.19%) underperforms Lingshu-7B (76.15%) only marginally; the paper attributes this to SLAKE's reliance on global context rather than local visual clues. On the segmentation sub-task (MeCo-G), CARE's referring segmentation model achieves 81.9% mean Dice, outperforming BiomedParse (30.1%), UniBiomed (24.9%), and LISA-7B (62.7%), confirming that the expert segmentation module is a key contributor to downstream VQA gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARE departs from the dominant single-shot medical VLM paradigm (Lingshu, HuatuoGPT-Vision, medgemma) and from grounded VLMs that treat segmentation as an auxiliary multi-task head (MedPLIB, UniBiomed, BiomedParse) by decomposing medical VQA into three specialist submodules—entity proposal, referring segmentation, and evidence-grounded VQA—coordinated by an agentic planner. Conceptually, it extends the agentic tool-use line (MedAgent, MedAgent-Pro) by feeding pixel-level visual evidence back into the reasoning loop rather than treating grounding as a one-shot perception step, and it builds on SA-Med-2D's SAM-style architecture augmented with a frozen BioClinicalBERT text encoder for referring segmentation. The training recipe (SFT + DAPO-based RLVR with embedding-similarity rewards) follows the broader RLVR trend (DeepEyes, MedVLM-R1) but applies it to each specialist independently rather than to a monolithic model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in clinical AI deployment: black-box medical VLMs lack the explicit, inspectable evidence trail that clinicians rely on, and CARE's staged, evidence-grounded pipeline directly targets this accountability concern, validated by a human evaluation showing higher reasoning-trace pass rates. However, the framework's reliance on a proprietary GPT-5 coordinator for its best results limits open-source reproducibility and raises cost/latency concerns (43s per query vs. 7s for the static pipeline). Open questions include whether the agentic coordination can be fully internalized in an open model, whether the approach generalizes to 3D volumetric data or tasks requiring global context (where it underperforms), and how robust the pipeline is when the entity-proposal stage fails on rare pathologies.
