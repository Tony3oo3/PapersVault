## Problem

Existing sparse attention methods for MLLMs plateau at roughly 50% token reduction because they merely exploit naturally emergent attention sparsity rather than actively enforcing it. Trainable sparse attention (MOBA, NSA) imposes rigid, input-agnostic patterns and requires training from scratch, while sharpness-inducing regularizers optimize proxy objectives (e.g., attention entropy) that do not translate into controllable end-to-end token savings. The paper asks: can token sparsity be explicitly reinforced in a well-posed MLLM via a post-training procedure that directly optimizes the efficiency–accuracy trade-off?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265276/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-resolution images or long videos (up to ~120 frames ≈ 16,384 tokens for Qwen2.5-VL, ~110 frames ≈ 20,240 tokens for LLaVA-Video) paired with a text question. During training, each query produces N rollouts (N=8 or 12) at different top-p retention thresholds p ∈ [0.94, 0.975], yielding a group of candidate answers with associated token ratios. At inference, p is fixed at 0.975 and the model outputs a text answer alongside a dynamically pruned per-layer KV cache whose size is determined by the learned attention-score distribution rather than a fixed ratio.

## Method

Sparsity Forcing is a GRPO-based post-training loop that treats an MLLM with ZipVL top-p sparse attention as the policy and the same model with frozen standard causal attention as the reference. For each query, N rollouts are sampled with independently drawn p thresholds, producing a progressive budget sweep that tests whether low-salience tokens are actually needed for correctness. The per-rollout reward is r_i = r_per,i + C · r_eff,i, where r_per is binary answer correctness, r_eff is the token reduction ratio (1−τ), and C is a group-level indicator that gates the efficiency term to prevent collapse to trivial ultra-sparse policies when no rollout is correct. Group-relative advantages (mean/std-normalized rewards) drive the clipped GRPO surrogate with a KL penalty to the reference model, so that correct-and-efficient rollouts receive positive advantage while incorrect or less-efficient ones are penalized. The top-p mechanism itself is dynamic: per-layer, accumulated column attention scores are sorted and the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative mass exceeds p·ℓ is retained, enabling input- and layer-adaptive sparsity without any architectural change.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Sparsity Forcing achieves an average score of 73.6 at only 24.7% token retention, nearly matching the full-attention baseline (73.8 at 100%) while reducing the token budget by over 75%. On Qwen2-VL-7B, it reaches 70.8 average at 23.6% tokens versus 70.9 for full attention. Across 13 image and video benchmarks, the method reduces long-context inference memory by up to 3× and speeds up decoding by up to 3.3× compared to FlashAttention-2 at 200k sequence lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ZipVL (the base sparse attention mechanism), Sparsity Forcing reduces the token retention ratio from ~80% to ~25% on Qwen2-VL/Qwen2.5-VL while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy (e.g., 73.6 vs. 72.9 avg on Qwen2.5-VL-7B). Compared to FastV and VisionZip, which both operate at ~50% token ratio, Sparsity Forcing achieves comparable or better accuracy at roughly half the token budget (e.g., 73.6 vs. 69.9 for FastV and 71.2 for VisionZip on Qwen2.5-VL-7B). On LLaVA-Video-7B, Sparsity Forcing matches Minference's accuracy (61.3 avg) while using 29.6% tokens versus Minference's 46.1%. Against the post-training baselines MOBA and L∞ sharpness loss (both at 25% tokens on Qwen2.5-VL-7B), Sparsity Forcing substantially outperforms them, achieving 72.8 avg versus 66.6 (MOBA) and 67.6 (sharpness loss), a gap of 5.2–6.2 points. The method also surpasses ZipVL† (post-trained ZipVL at 61.7% tokens, 71.5 avg) by 1.3 points while using less than half the tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work on MLLM efficiency: training-free sparse attention (ZipVL, FastV, VisionZip, Minference) that exploits inherent attention sparsity but plateaus at ~50% token reduction, and trainable sparse attention / sharpness regularizers (MOBA, NSA, L∞ losses) that enforce sparsity through rigid patterns or proxy objectives under SFT. The key departure is reframing token saving as an end-to-end RL objective via GRPO, where a joint efficiency-performance reward with multi-budget rollouts replaces both the passive exploitation of emergent sparsity and the SFT-based proxy losses. By anchoring the policy to a frozen full-attention reference model and using top-p sparse attention as the action space, the method inherits the dynamic, input-adaptive budgeting of ZipVL while actively pushing the achievable sparsity frontier further through contrastive group rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 75% token reduction (vs. ~50% for prior methods) translates to 3× lower long-context inference memory and 3.3× faster decoding, which is critical for deploying MLLMs on high-resolution images and long videos. The RL-based, inference-aligned training loop avoids the train-test mismatch of SFT-based sparsity methods and requires no architecture changes, making it a drop-in post-training step for existing MLLMs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-turn dialogue, hardware-aware objectives (latency/energy), and more aggressive budgets where spatial reasoning degrades (as shown in the failure cases). The group-level correctness gate that prevents collapse to trivial ultra-sparse policies is a useful design principle that may generalize to other efficiency-oriented RL objectives.
