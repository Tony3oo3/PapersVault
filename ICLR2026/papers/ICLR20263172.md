## Problem

Generative flow-matching policies in offline-to-online RL are overly confined to the offline dataset distribution during offline pre-training, severely limiting exploration when the agent transitions to online fine-tuning. Prior work treated online fine-tuning as a direct continuation of offline training without designing mechanisms specifically to leverage the policy's expressivity for exploration under a limited interaction budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263172/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous state vector as input and produces a continuous action vector (bounded in [-1,1]) as output. During online fine-tuning, the one-step policy generates N_sample candidate actions per state (where N_sample = 0.5 × action dimensionality), from which a single action is selected via a temperature-weighted softmax over Q-values. The offline dataset consists of (s, a, r, s') tuples, and the flow policy is conditioned on the state while the one-step policy maps (state, Gaussian noise) → action without sequential ODE integration.

## Method

FINO introduces two key mechanisms. First, during offline pre-training, time-dependent Gaussian noise ε_t ~ N(0, α_t²I) is injected into the flow matching training input, where α_t² = (η²−2η)t² + 2ηt with η=0.1. This modifies the conditional probability path variance from (1−t)²I to (1−(1−η)t)²I while preserving the mean, provably increasing the marginal distribution variance (Theorem 2) so the learned policy covers a broader action space. Second, during online fine-tuning, the agent samples multiple candidate actions from the one-step policy and selects one via a softmax distribution over their Q-values with a temperature parameter ξ. The temperature is adaptively updated using a GMM-estimated policy entropy (ξ_new = ξ − α_ξ[H − H̄]), dynamically balancing exploration (low ξ, flat distribution) and exploitation (high ξ, peaked distribution) as the policy evolves.

## Results

Across 45 tasks on OGBench and D4RL (1M offline + 500K online steps, 10 seeds), FINO achieves the strongest online fine-tuning performance in most environments. The most dramatic gains appear on OGBench humanoidmaze-medium-navigate, where FINO reaches 97±1 after online fine-tuning versus 61±2 for the FQL backbone and 70±5 for IFQL. On D4RL adroit (aggregated over 4 tasks), FINO attains 112±1, outperforming FQL (100±6) and ReBRAC (83±2). On D4RL antmaze (aggregated over 6 tasks), FINO reaches 96±1, matching FQL (95±1) while substantially outperforming ReBRAC (89±5) and Cal-QL (89±2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FINO is built on FQL (the flow matching backbone) and consistently outperforms it during online fine-tuning, with the largest gap on OGBench humanoidmaze-medium-navigate (97 vs. 61) and antmaze-giant-navigate (79 vs. 71), confirming that noise injection in the flow objective—not merely the flow architecture—drives the improvement. Against IFQL, which also samples multiple action candidates from a single state, FINO still wins on most tasks (e.g., 97 vs. 70 on humanoidmaze-medium-navigate), demonstrating that the expanded action space from noise injection provides a qualitatively different exploration advantage over candidate sampling alone. Compared to ReBRAC, a strong Gaussian-policy baseline, FINO is dramatically superior on high-dimensional humanoidmaze tasks (97 vs. 3 on medium-navigate) where the Gaussian policy cannot represent the multimodal action distribution, though ReBRAC remains competitive on simpler antmaze navigation (99 vs. 99). Cal-QL and RLPD, both designed for offline-to-online settings, frequently collapse to near-zero performance on complex OGBench environments (e.g., Cal-QL scores 0 on all humanoidmaze tasks), whereas FINO maintains strong performance throughout. On D4RL adroit, FINO achieves 112±1, surpassing FQL (100±6) and ReBRAC (83±2), while on D4RL antmaze it matches FQL (96 vs. 95) and slightly edges out RLPD (96 vs. 91).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FQL (Park et al., 2025b), which applies flow matching to offline RL policy design via a state-conditioned flow model and a one-step distilled policy. The key departure is that prior generative-policy methods (IDQL, FQL) treated online fine-tuning as a straightforward continuation of offline pre-training, inheriting the conservative, data-constrained behavior that limits exploration. FINO instead modifies the flow matching training objective itself (injecting time-scheduled noise to widen the learned action distribution) and introduces an entropy-guided sampling mechanism for the online phase, explicitly designing the offline stage to serve the subsequent online stage. The theoretical analysis connects to the conditional probability path framework of Lipman et al. (2023), showing the perturbed flow remains a valid continuous normalizing flow with provably larger marginal variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in offline-to-online RL: how to make expressive generative policies (flow matching in particular) actually explore effectively during the limited online fine-tuning budget, rather than merely reproducing offline dataset behavior. The theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1–2) that noise-injected flow matching preserves the mean while increasing variance provide a principled justification for the design, and the comprehensive evaluation across 45 tasks on OGBench and D4RL demonstrates consistent gains without degrading offline performance. Open questions include the reliance on GMM-based entropy estimation (approximate and computationally costly), the unaddressed distribution shift problem, and whether the fixed η=0.1 noise schedule generalizes to environments with non-standard action ranges. The approach could serve as a template for adapting other generative policy architectures (diffusion, score-based) to the offline-to-online setting.
