## Problem

Existing spatial intelligence benchmarks for MLLMs treat spatial ability as a monolithic block, failing to explicitly define atomic capabilities or systematically compose them into realistic tasks. They also suffer from limited scene diversity (typically <400 scenes), restricted modalities, and lack of multi-answer evaluation, making it impossible to diagnose which specific spatial sub-skills limit compositional reasoning in real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261901/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark evaluates MLLMs on 811 real indoor scenes with 5,000+ QA pairs across 8 task types. 2D MLLMs receive 8 multi-view RGB frames as visual input, while 3D MLLMs (e.g., LEO, GPT4Scene) receive point clouds sampled from the full scene. Questions are presented as multiple-choice (4 options for single-answer, 5 options with 2 correct for multi-answer), and answers are aligned via GPT-4o-2024-11-20 following the MMBench protocol. The evaluation covers both perception tasks (counting, size, spatial relations) and reasoning tasks (functional reasoning, spatial planning), with accuracy as the sole metric.

## Method

SpaCE-10 introduces a 5-stage hierarchical annotation pipeline that first generates structured scene descriptions (2D captions via GPT-4o, 3D captions from human-collected multi-direction snapshots, inspector refinement, and rule-based extraction) and then produces compositional QA pairs through a multi-stage semi-automated process. The key design is a pool of 10 explicitly defined atomic spatial capabilities (C1–C10) that are systematically combined into 8 QA types, each integrating 5+ capabilities. A cross-capability integration strategy transforms basic single-object QA into compositional questions by regrouping options to span different scene regions (forcing C7 multi-view fusion), reversing question polarity (adding C9 reverse reasoning), and embedding situated functional descriptions (adding C10 situated observation). Over 150 hours of human expert effort (38h for snapshot collection, 112h for quality filtering) ensures high-quality ground truth across 4 public 3D scan datasets (ScanNet++, ScanNet, 3RScan, ARKitScene).

## Results

On SpaCE-10, human experts achieve 91.2% overall accuracy, while the best open-source MLLM (InternVL3-241B-A28B) reaches only 55.0% and the best close-source model (GPT-5) reaches 53.4%, revealing a large gap between current MLLMs and human-level compositional spatial intelligence. 3D MLLMs perform substantially worse than 2D MLLMs: LEO-7B scores just 11.1% and GPT4Scene-7B scores 34.5%, compared to 2D MLLMs ranging from 30% to 55%. On multiple-answer questions, performance drops dramatically—small models (≤2B) fall below 5% accuracy, worse than random—while larger models (≥78B) show greater robustness across QA types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, SpaCE-10 evaluates ~50 MLLMs rather than proposing a new model, so the key comparisons are between model families. 2D MLLMs dramatically outperform 3D MLLMs: GPT-5 (53.4%) and InternVL3-241B (55.0%) exceed GPT4Scene-7B (34.5%) by ~20 points and LEO-7B (11.1%) by over 40 points, indicating that current 3D MLLMs sacrifice scene-level understanding for object-centric point-cloud processing. Among close-source models, GPT-5 leads (53.4%) but is surpassed by the open-source InternVL3-241B-A28B (55.0%), showing the open-source gap has narrowed significantly. GPT-4o excels at counting (EQ: 58.3%) but is weakest at spatial planning (SP: 23.7%), while LLaVA-OneVision-72B achieves the best FR score (67.3%) among open models. The compositional integration analysis (Table 4) reveals that adding multi-view fusion (C7) drops accuracy by 7.6–21.0%, while adding reverse reasoning (C9) and situated observation (C10) causes catastrophic drops of 41.0% (EP) and 42.9% (FR), exposing that counting (C5) and cross-view spatial reasoning remain the primary bottlenecks across all model scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of 3D spatial QA benchmarks (ScanQA, SQA3D, VSI, MSQA) by making spatial capabilities explicit and compositional rather than treating spatial intelligence as a monolithic evaluation target. Where prior benchmarks test individual relations (left/right, near/far) or simple counting in isolation, SpaCE-10 defines 10 atomic capabilities (C1–C10) and systematically composes them into 8 QA types, each requiring 5+ capabilities simultaneously. The hierarchical annotation pipeline (structured scene data → basic QA → cross-capability integration) departs from prior work's flat question generation by explicitly controlling which capabilities are activated, enabling fine-grained capability-level diagnosis. It also uniquely supports both 2D multi-view image and 3D point-cloud inputs under a unified evaluation protocol, bridging the gap between the 2D MLLM and 3D MLLM communities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even GPT-5 (53.1%) trails humans (91.2%) by a wide margin in compositional spatial intelligence, and that the bottleneck is not a single capability but the *composition* of capabilities—performance drops 40–50% when multi-view fusion and reverse reasoning are added on top of basic spatial tasks. The finding that counting (C5) remains the weakest capability across all model scales (even 241B parameters) suggests that parameter scaling alone cannot fix discrete numerical reasoning in cluttered scenes, pointing toward targeted data curation and capability-aware training as necessary next steps. The dramatic underperformance of 3D MLLMs (LEO at 11.1%) relative to 2D MLLMs challenges the assumption that native 3D input is inherently better for spatial understanding, and the observation that smaller models overfit to single-answer format while larger models generalize better to multi-answer questions has practical implications for model selection in deployment. Open questions include whether capability-decomposed training curricula can close the compositional gap, and whether the 2D-over-3D advantage persists as 3D MLLMs mature.
