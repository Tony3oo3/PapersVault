## Problem

While LLMs have been extensively evaluated on visual geometry comprehension and mathematical reasoning, their ability to interpret procedural drawing code (e.g., Asymptote) and construct internal geometric representations from it remains unexplored. Preliminary evidence shows substantial accuracy drops when problems shift from text-only to text-plus-code formats, yet no dedicated, systematic benchmark exists to isolate and measure this Program-to-Geometry capability.

## Input / Output

Each problem presents a textual geometry question alongside procedural drawing code (Asymptote or Matplotlib) that specifies the diagram's construction; the code is deliberately rescaled and parameter-masked to prevent answer leakage through direct coordinate inspection. The model must produce a single numeric answer (length, area, volume, angle, ratio, or count) in \boxed{} format. Evaluation is zero-shot with 8 samples per problem at temperature 0.6, and the benchmark spans 500 problems across six subtypes and three geometric-complexity levels, including 3D solid geometry tasks absent from prior code-based benchmarks.

## Method

GeoGramBench is constructed through a multi-stage pipeline: ~905K candidate problems are filtered for embedded Asymptote code, deduplicated via 8-gram similarity, classified as geometry problems with GPT-4o, then refined by four domain experts over two rounds (format normalization, decontamination, answer-leakage prevention, and manual verification), yielding 392 curated items augmented to 500 with AIME24, MATH-500, and hand-transcribed Mathverse solid-geometry problems. The key design choice is a three-level taxonomy (Primitive Recognition → Local Relation Composition → Global Abstract Integration) organized by geometric structural complexity rather than reasoning-step difficulty, validated empirically by showing that accuracy on code-embedded problems correlates with geometric intricacy, not reasoning complexity. The paper evaluates 19 frontier LLMs (closed-source and open-source) in zero-shot, supplements with Token Budget Forcing experiments to probe CoT's limits, and conducts data-ablation fine-tuning on s1.1-32B to disentangle data scarcity from intrinsic spatial-representation weaknesses.

## Results

On GeoGramBench (500 problems), GPT-5 achieves the highest overall accuracy at 75.01%, followed by Qwen3-235B-Thinking-2507 at 74.00% and GPT-o1 at 70.92%. A critical finding is that no model surpasses 50% accuracy on the Abstract (Global Abstract Integration) level: GPT-5 drops to 39.26%, Qwen3-235B-Thinking-2507 to 49.05%, and GPT-o1 to 44.67%, despite achieving 85–90% on the Primitive level. The most challenging subtypes shift from angle (2D) at lower levels to area and volume (3D) at the Abstract level, where GPT-5 scores only 19.29% on area and 21.76% on volume.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper evaluating 19 LLMs rather than proposing a new method, so comparisons are among the evaluated models. GPT-5 leads overall (75.01%) and on Primitive (90.44%) and Compositional (84.59%) levels, but Qwen3-235B-Thinking-2507 edges it out on the Abstract level (49.05% vs. 39.26%), making it the strongest model for the hardest geometric abstraction tasks. Among open-source models, Qwen3-235B-Thinking-2507 (74.00%) surpasses all earlier closed-source models including GPT-o1 (70.92%) and GPT-o3-mini (70.00%). A stark gap separates frontier models from older ones: GPT-4o (21.40%) and Gemini-Pro-1.5 (31.64%) trail the top models by over 40 percentage points. All 19 models fail to exceed 50% on the Abstract level, with the best (Qwen3-235B-Thinking-2507) reaching only 49.05%, confirming that program-driven spatial geometric reasoning remains an unsolved challenge across the entire model landscape.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three existing benchmark families: visual geometric perception (Euclid, GeoSense, MathVerse), mathematical reasoning (MATH-500, AIME24), and symbolic graphics parsing (SGP-Bench, SVGenius). None of these address the specific task of interpreting procedural drawing code (Asymptote/Matplotlib) and performing geometric spatial reasoning over the constructed diagram. The authors formalize this gap as the 'Program-to-Geometry' task, building on preliminary observations from the s1 paper (Muennighoff et al., 2025) that LLMs struggle with code-embedded geometry, and introduce a taxonomy based on geometric structural complexity rather than the reasoning-step difficulty used in prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoGramBench provides the first dedicated, large-scale (500-problem) diagnostic tool for a capability that underlies robotics, autonomous navigation, and automated design applications. The finding that no model exceeds 50% on the Abstract level, combined with the demonstration that Token Budget Forcing (77% more tokens) yields only 0.30% improvement, suggests that the bottleneck is architectural (weak internal spatial representation) rather than a matter of reasoning time or data volume. Key open questions include whether purpose-built spatial representation mechanisms (beyond token-level CoT) are needed, how to extend evaluation to real-world 3D scenarios, and whether the identified failure patterns (algebraic bias, inability to introduce auxiliary constructions, spatial orientation confusion) can be addressed through targeted training or architectural changes.
