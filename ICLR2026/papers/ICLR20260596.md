## Problem

Existing 3D few-shot class incremental learning methods rely heavily on iterative feature fine-tuning while retaining static decision boundaries, creating a fundamental trade-off: aggressive adaptation to scarce novel samples accelerates catastrophic forgetting of base classes, while conservative adaptation fails to discriminate new categories. The paper argues that the bottleneck is not merely feature quality but the rigidity of the decision process itself, and that uncertainty-aware dynamic decision-making can resolve the old-new class conflict without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D point clouds (1024 points per object via farthest point sampling) encoded by a frozen Uni3D backbone (EVA02-E-14+ CLIP), along with class-name text embeddings for prototype construction. It outputs a fused classification score that adaptively blends semantic classifier logits (for base classes) with cosine-similarity scores against geometric prototypes (for novel classes), where the blend weight is driven per-sample by prediction entropy. The incremental phase is entirely training-free: after base-class training, new classes are recognized solely through prototype matching and entropy-gated decision routing.

## Method

Point-UQ comprises two co-designed modules. The Attention-driven Adaptive Enhancement (AAE) module uses learnable multi-head self-attention to fuse hierarchical backbone features (shallow geometric + deep semantic) into calibrated representations, simultaneously producing prediction entropy as a per-sample epistemic uncertainty estimate. The Uncertainty-quantification Decision Decoupling (UDD) module then dynamically arbitrates between a frozen semantic classifier (base classes) and geometric prototype matching (novel classes) via a sigmoid-scaled entropy weight α: high-entropy samples are routed toward robust geometric similarity, while low-entropy samples retain semantic decisions. Prototypes are constructed by K-means centroid selection followed by semantic-weighted averaging using text-feature similarity. Training occurs only on base classes with a frozen backbone, optimizing AAE parameters and prototype construction via a combined cross-entropy and cosine-similarity loss, after which all incremental stages proceed without any parameter updates.

## Results

On the most challenging cross-dataset setting (ShapeNet→CO3D, 44 base + 50 novel classes over 11 incremental stages), Point-UQ achieves 91.4% initial accuracy dropping to 80.3% final accuracy (Δ↓ = 12.1%), outperforming the strongest baseline FoundationModel (87.3%→72.6%, Δ↓ = 16.8%) by 7.7 points in final accuracy and 4.7 points in forgetting rate. On intra-dataset ShapeNet (25 base + 30 novel), Point-UQ reaches 93.3%→86.5% (Δ↓ = 7.0%), the lowest forgetting rate among all methods. The paper reports a consistent ~4% average accuracy gain over SOTA across all benchmarks, with harmonic accuracy improvements of up to 9% on cross-dataset tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Point-UQ outperforms all 11 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the strongest dedicated 3D FSCIL method, FoundationModel (Ahmadi et al., 2024), Point-UQ gains 7.7 points in final accuracy (80.3% vs. 72.6%) and reduces forgetting by 4.7 points (Δ↓ 12.1% vs. 16.8%) on ShapeNet→CO3D. The prior SOTA C3PR (Cheraghian et al., 2025) and Microshape (Chowdhury et al., 2022) trail by 17.1 and 23.2 points respectively in final accuracy on the same task. Adapted 2D FSCIL methods (LwF, IL2M, ScalIL, EEIL, FACT, Sem-aware) suffer severe catastrophic forgetting under cross-domain shifts, with final accuracies collapsing to 1–56% versus Point-UQ's 80.3%. On the FSCIL3D-XL S2R benchmark, Point-UQ (82.0% final, Δ↓ 9.6%) dramatically outperforms BiDist (50.1%, Δ↓ 43.9%) and FILP-3D (74.6%, Δ↓ 17.1%), confirming robustness under extreme domain gaps. Point-UQ also uses only 89 cached exemplars versus FoundationModel's 445, while training 37% faster (1622s vs. 2571s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 3D FSCIL paradigm (Microshape, Cross-Domain, C3PR, FoundationModel) that focuses on iterative feature fine-tuning to improve discriminability, instead reframing the problem as one of adaptive decision-making over frozen features. It repurposes standard components—multi-head self-attention for multi-scale fusion, K-means-based prototype construction, and prediction entropy—as a unified uncertainty-driven arbitration mechanism between semantic classifiers and geometric prototypes. The frozen Uni3D backbone and CLIP-style text encoder follow the FoundationModel setup, but the key conceptual shift is that no parameters are updated during incremental stages, replacing fine-tuning with a lightweight entropy-gated fusion rule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free incremental phase offers a practical advantage for deployment scenarios where retraining is costly or infeasible, and the 4–9% accuracy gains over strong baselines across intra- and cross-dataset settings are meaningful. However, the approach inherits the limitations of its frozen backbone (Uni3D), and the single-scalar entropy-to-weight mapping (sigmoid of λ·H) is a relatively simple switching mechanism that may not capture richer uncertainty structure. Open questions include whether the decision-optimization framing generalizes beyond FSCIL to broader continual learning settings, whether more expressive uncertainty estimators (e.g., ensemble-based or evidential) could further improve the arbitration, and how the method scales when the number of incremental stages grows well beyond the 25 tested.
