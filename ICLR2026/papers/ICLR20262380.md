## Problem

LoRA adapters for edge-deployed LLMs are brittle: an adapter trained for one compression scheme (e.g., INT8) misaligns under another (e.g., FP4, pruned), and adapters trained on an older base model degrade as the model evolves. Existing methods like QLoRA follow a 'train-for-the-target' paradigm, requiring a separate retrained adapter for every hardware variant and every model release, creating an unsustainable retraining cycle.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a full-precision base LLM (Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B) and task-specific instruction data, while stochastically applying compression operators (INT8/FP4/NF4 quantization, structured row/column pruning, random layer skipping) to the base weights in the forward pass. The output is a single universal LoRA adapter (rank-8 matrices A and B, ~20M parameters) that can be merged with any compressed or temporally evolved version of the base model at inference time without retraining, producing standard text responses on reasoning benchmarks.

## Method

CAR-LoRA introduces a compression-aware bi-level optimization loop: an outer loop samples a compression operator C_j from a distribution over {quantization bit-widths, structured pruning masks, layer-skipping subsets}, and an inner loop fine-tunes the LoRA adapter on the compressed base model C_j(θ₀). A critical design choice is the 'compression-forward, full-precision-backward' scheme: the forward pass uses perturbed compressed weights to expose the adapter to diverse artifacts, while the backward pass applies a straight-through estimator (treating ∂C_j/∂θ₀ ≈ I) to maintain stable gradient flow into the adapter matrices. For structured pruning, corresponding rows in B and columns in A are masked to preserve dimensional compatibility; for layer skipping, LoRA-adapted layers are randomly deactivated so remaining adapters learn to compensate. Temporal robustness emerges as a byproduct: the compression-aware training regularizes the adapter toward a smoother, more generalizable solution that is less sensitive to the small parameter shifts (δ_t) from incremental model updates, a property formalized via a Lipschitz-based error bound decomposing the performance gap into generalization, drift, and compression-mismatch terms.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B across six reasoning benchmarks, CAR-LoRA's single universal adapter achieves performance on par with individually retrained QLoRA adapters under INT8, FP4, and NF4 quantization. For example, on MATH, CAR-LoRA [FP4] reaches 16.7%, exceeding both LoRA [BF16] (16.5%) and QLoRA [FP4] (16.1%); on SQA, CAR-LoRA [BF16] attains 68.8%, matching QLoRA [INT8]. Under layer skipping, the largest degradation occurs (GSM8K drops from 38.9% to 31.1%), while structured pruning causes only mild losses (GSM8K: 37.5% vs. 38.9% full-precision). Across Mistral-7B and Gemma-2-9B, CAR-LoRA [BF16] consistently outperforms both LoRA and all QLoRA variants (e.g., 72.1% SQA on Mistral-7B vs. 70.4% LoRA and 70.2% QLoRA [INT8]).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAR-LoRA is compared against two baselines: standard LoRA [BF16] (trained on the full-precision model) and specialized QLoRA adapters (individually retrained from scratch for each quantization scheme: INT8, FP4, NF4). Against QLoRA, CAR-LoRA achieves parity or slight wins on most benchmarks: on MATH with FP4, CAR-LoRA scores 16.7% vs. QLoRA [FP4] at 16.1% (+0.6%); on SQA, CAR-LoRA [BF16] matches QLoRA [INT8] at 68.8%; on GSM8K, CAR-LoRA [BF16] ties LoRA [BF16] at 38.9% while QLoRA variants score 38.5–38.6%. Against standard LoRA [BF16], CAR-LoRA [BF16] slightly exceeds it on ANLI (40.0% vs. 39.9%) and matches on SQA (68.8% vs. 68.7%) and GSM8K (38.9%). The critical advantage is not a large absolute gain but universality: one adapter trained once replaces five separately retrained QLoRA adapters, reducing amortized training cost from 220 GPU-hours (LoRA) or 211 GPU-hours (QLoRA) to 170 GPU-hours for CAR-LoRA. The only notable loss is under layer skipping, where CAR-LoRA degrades more than quantization variants (GSM8K: 31.1% vs. 38.9% full-precision), acknowledging an architectural perturbation that remains harder to absorb.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAR-LoRA extends the LoRA/QLoRA line of work by replacing the 'train-for-the-target' paradigm (where a separate adapter is retrained for each compression format) with a bi-level optimization that stochastically samples compression operators during a single training run. It builds directly on PortLLM's finding that temporal robustness is an emergent property of LoRA adapters, allowing the method to focus its novel contribution on inducing compression-awareness via a quantized-forward/full-precision-backward (straight-through estimator) scheme. The approach also draws on data augmentation and domain randomization ideas from robustness literature, repurposing them for the adapter-training setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge AI deployment: a single adapter eliminates the O(N×T) retraining cost (N device tiers × T model updates) that currently fragments the adapter ecosystem, with the paper showing 5× parameter reduction and ~23% GPU-hour savings for a 5-device scenario. Key limitations include pronounced degradation under layer skipping (GSM8K drops from 38.9% to 31.1%) and a 'phase transition' at INT2 where the approach breaks down, suggesting the method has a finite robustness envelope. Open questions include whether the framework extends to unstructured pruning, larger models (70B+), and whether the bi-level optimization can be made more sample-efficient than the 20-epoch training budget required.
