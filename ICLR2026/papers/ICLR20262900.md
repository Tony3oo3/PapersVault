## Problem

Spectral GNNs achieve strong node classification performance, yet their node distinguishability capacity remains poorly understood. No prior work has systematically analyzed how the graph matrix and node features jointly limit the number of distinguishable nodes, nor has a theoretical lower bound on this quantity been established. In practice, eigenvalue multiplicity in standard graph matrices and missing frequency components of node features in the eigenbasis severely constrain distinguishability, especially on heterophilic graphs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} and a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{n×h} as input, and produces node embeddings in R^{n×d} for downstream classification. Unlike standard spectral GNNs that use a fixed graph matrix (e.g., normalized adjacency or Laplacian), AdaSpec generates an adaptive graph matrix Ω(A,X) that depends on both the topology and the features. The generated matrix preserves edge connectivity (M_ij ≠ 0 ⟺ edge exists) and commutes with the graph's automorphism group, ensuring permutation equivariance of the output embeddings.

## Method

AdaSpec is a plug-in module that replaces the fixed graph matrix in any spectral GNN Ψ(M,X) with an adaptive one: Ω(A,X) = Ω_D(A) + α₁Ω_S(A) + α₂Ω_F(X). The component Ω_D(A) = (D+B)^{-1/2}(A+B)(D+B)^{-1/2} introduces a learnable diagonal bias B (initialized as 1/D_ii) to break eigenvalue degeneracy; Ω_S(A) = I shifts all eigenvalues away from zero to prevent spectral filter suppression; and Ω_F(X) = Σ_i (X_:i X_:i^T / ‖X_:i‖²_F) ∘ A uses feature outer products masked by the adjacency to inject non-zero frequency components. The paper proves a lower bound of min(d_M, ‖X̃^(M)‖₀) on distinguishable nodes (Theorem 4.3), shows Ω_D(A) strictly increases distinct eigenvalues (Theorem 5.1), proves Ω_F(X) increases non-zero frequency components (Theorem 5.2), and establishes that the full Ω(A,X) preserves permutation equivariance (Theorem 5.4, Proposition 5.5) without increasing asymptotic computational complexity.

## Results

AdaSpec, a plug-in adaptive graph matrix module, improves node classification accuracy across 18 benchmark datasets when integrated into five spectral GNNs. On small heterophilic graphs (Texas, Wisconsin, Cornell), the average accuracy improvement is 3.45%, with ChebNet on Texas jumping from 38.67% to 51.16% (+12.49%) and GPRGNN on Texas from 48.15% to 58.27% (+10.12%). On large heterophilic graphs, the average ROC AUC improvement is 1.27% (e.g., GPRGNN on Questions: 53.14→58.19, +5.05). On homophilic graphs, improvements are modest (average +0.43%), with the largest gain being JacobiConv on Cora (+6.37%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates AdaSpec as a plug-in on five spectral GNN baselines: ChebNet, ChebNetII, JacobiConv, GPRGNN, and BernNet. AdaSpec consistently improves or matches the original model across all 18 datasets, with the most dramatic gains on heterophilic graphs. ChebNet benefits the most on small heterophilic graphs (Texas +12.49%, Wisconsin +0.91%), while GPRGNN shows the largest improvement on Questions (+5.05 ROC AUC). JacobiConv achieves the most uniform gains across all heterophilic datasets (all positive, ranging from +0.37 to +3.87). On homophilic graphs, improvements are smaller and occasionally negative (e.g., ChebNet on Citeseer: −0.69%), suggesting that the additional spectral components are less necessary when low-frequency information already suffices. The only consistent losses are on Squirrel (−1.03% for ChebNet, −0.31% for ChebNetII, −0.40% for GPRGNN), indicating that AdaSpec's spectral enrichment can occasionally over-parameterize very structured heterophilic graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the expressive-power analysis of spectral GNNs from the graph-classification setting (WL-test-based analyses) to node classification, introducing 'node distinguishability' as the relevant metric. It departs from prior spectral GNN work (ChebNet, GPRGNN, BernNet, JacobiConv, ChebNetII) that treats the graph matrix as a fixed input, instead proposing an adaptive graph matrix generation module. The paper also contrasts with Lu et al. (2024), whose eigenvalue-correction method breaks permutation equivariance, and positions itself as complementary to (not competing with) spatial-domain graph rewiring methods like FoSR and GPER. The theoretical lower bound on distinguishable nodes generalizes the uniform approximation result of Wang & Zhang (2022) from linear to nonlinear spectral GNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean decomposition of node distinguishability into two factors—eigenvalue multiplicity and missing frequency components—gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic for why spectral GNNs fail on particular graphs, and the plug-in AdaSpec module offers a low-cost remedy without changing asymptotic complexity. However, the approach is limited to spectral GNNs, provides only a lower bound (not a tight characterization), and the practical gains are modest (1–3% on heterophilic graphs, ~0.4% on homophilic). Open questions include whether the adaptive matrix design generalizes to dynamic or attributed graphs, whether the lower bound can be tightened, and whether the benefit persists when combined with more advanced spectral filters or rational-type GNNs.
