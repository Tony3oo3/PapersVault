## Problem

Existing brain encoding models are limited in three critical ways: they assume linear equivalence between AI representations and brain activity, train separate models per subject, and operate on a single sensory modality. These constraints prevent capturing how the brain dynamically integrates information across modalities in high-level associative cortices, where cross-modal interactions are known to occur.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three synchronized stimulus streams: video (64 frames per 4-second bin), audio (60-second chunks), and timestamped text transcripts (1024-word context), all resampled to a common 2 Hz grid. It predicts BOLD fMRI responses at TR=1.49 s across 1,000 Schaefer atlas parcels, with targets offset by 5 seconds to account for hemodynamic lag. Windows of N=100 TRs (~149 s) are processed simultaneously, making inference compute-efficient since all timesteps in a window are predicted in one forward pass.

## Method

TRIBE extracts time-series embeddings from three frozen foundational models—Llama-3.2-3B (text, D=3072), Wav2Vec-Bert-2.0 (audio, D=1024), and Video-JEPA-2 (video, D=1408)—selecting late layers (relative depths 0.5–1.0) grouped into two bins per modality, then projecting each to a shared 1024-dim space and concatenating across modalities (3×1024). An 8-layer, 8-head Transformer encoder with learnable positional embeddings exchanges information across timesteps, followed by adaptive average pooling to compress from 2 Hz to TR resolution. A subject-conditional linear layer (following Defossez et al., 2023) maps the transformer output to the 1,000-parcel target space, enabling a single model to serve all subjects. Training uses modality dropout (p=0.2) to encourage robustness to missing modalities, and the final system ensembles 1,000 models with diverse hyperparameters, weighted per-parcel via a softmax over validation scores. The 980M-parameter encoder is trained end-to-end on 80+ hours of fMRI from four subjects watching naturalistic videos.

## Results

TRIBE achieves first place out of 267 teams in the Algonauts 2025 multimodal brain encoding competition with a mean Pearson correlation (encoding score) of 0.2146±0.0312 across four subjects, outperforming the runner-up (0.2096) by a margin larger than the gap between 2nd and 5th place. In the in-distribution setting (Friends Season 7), the model reaches 0.3195, while in extreme out-of-distribution settings (silent black-and-white films, cartoons, wildlife documentaries) it maintains scores between 0.1686 and 0.1924. The normalized Pearson correlation (relative to the noise ceiling) is 0.54±0.1 across all 1,000 parcels, with all parcels predicted significantly above chance (q(FDR)<10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against named prior encoding methods in a head-to-head table; instead, the primary external comparison is the Algonauts 2025 competition leaderboard, where TRIBE (0.2146) beats the runner-up (0.2096) by ~0.005 and the 5th-place team (0.2055) by ~0.009. Internally, ablations quantify the contribution of each design choice: removing the transformer drops the encoding score from 0.31 to 0.23, and training per-subject instead of multi-subject reduces it from 0.31 to 0.29. On modality, the best unimodal model (video, 0.25) is substantially outperformed by the best bimodal model (text+video, 0.30) and the full trimodal model (0.31), with the largest multimodal gains (up to 30% relative increase) observed in associative cortices such as prefrontal and parieto-occipito-temporal regions. Notably, the vision-only model slightly outperforms the multimodal model in primary visual cortex, consistent with the retinotopic specificity of that area.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRIBE extends the brain encoding literature, which has progressively moved from linear ridge-regression mappings of frozen AI features onto fMRI (Huth et al., 2016; Caucheteux & King, 2022) to nonlinear recurrent encoders (Güclu & Van Gerven, 2017; Chehab et al., 2021) and multimodal transformer-based encoders (Dong & Toneva, 2023; Tang et al., 2023). However, prior multimodal encoders still relied on linear readout layers and were trained per-subject, while unimodal encoders could not capture cross-modal integration. TRIBE departs from this lineage by being the first pipeline that is simultaneously nonlinear (a transformer encoder over time), multi-subject (via a subject-conditional projection layer following Defossez et al., 2023), and truly multimodal (text, audio, video processed end-to-end), learning how to integrate modalities rather than assuming a fixed linear combination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By winning the Algonauts 2025 competition with a clear margin over 267 teams and demonstrating that multimodality yields the largest gains in associative cortices (up to 30% over the best unimodal model), TRIBE provides empirical evidence that the brain's high-level integration is not reducible to any single sensory channel. The scaling-law results (no plateau with more sessions or longer context) suggest substantial headroom for future improvements with larger datasets. Key open questions include generalization to unseen subjects (only 4 were used), voxel-level spatial resolution, capturing non-stimulus-driven dynamics like the default mode network, and extending beyond perception to memory and decision-making.
