## Problem

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems are vulnerable to contextual interference: when retrieved passages are wrong, irrelevant, or self-contradictory, LLMs over-rely on the external context and suppress their own correct parametric knowledge, cascading errors. Existing remedies—prompting, decoding adjustments, and supervised fine-tuning—lack a generalizable, learnable decision rule for when to trust context versus falling back on internal knowledge, and standard RL methods like GRPO sample only under a single query+context policy, preventing the model from exploring parametric-aware answers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language query and a retrieved context passage (top-5 paragraphs from benchmark-provided retrieval, no additional retriever) as input, and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer enclosed in <answer> tags. A key structural detail is the joint sampling scheme: for each query, the model simultaneously generates a query-only (PK) trajectory and a query+context (CK) trajectory, while a third RPK branch re-evaluates the PK tokens under the query+context prompt. At inference, only the query+context input is used; the learned token distributions implicitly encode when to follow context or revert to parametric knowledge without an explicit controller switch.

## Method

Knowledgeable-R1 defines three decoding policies—PK (query-only), CK (query+context), and RPK (query+context input but PK-targeted output)—and trains them jointly via a multi-objective PPO-style policy optimization. Advantages are computed at two levels: a local term (within-policy normalization) that optimizes each knowledge type's own accuracy, and a global term (cross-policy normalization under the same query+context input) that decides whether to adopt context or fall back to PK. An asymmetric advantage transformation T(Â; β) selectively attenuates negative RPK advantages by a factor β ∈ [0.01, 1], reducing the penalty for parametric decoding that is slightly worse than context-following; β is updated adaptively per mini-batch based on the cumulative advantage gap between CK and RPK trajectories, so the model keeps PK as a viable fallback without over-correcting when context is genuinely more up-to-date. The total objective is a weighted sum of the three PPO-clipped losses (equal weights in experiments), and the framework is implemented on top of the VERL RL training infrastructure.

## Results

On the adversarial (wrong-context) scenario S2, Knowledgeable-R1 achieves 43.94% on NC-MR with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, a +30.47 pp gain over RAG prompting (13.47%) and +17.00 pp over GRPO w/ RAG (26.94%). On the parametric-knowledge-answerable subset, the method yields an average +22.89% improvement over GRPO w/ RAG across NC-MR/MC/QA. Crucially, performance on correct-context (S1) is preserved: 80.90% on PC-QA vs. 80.03% for GRPO w/ RAG, confirming no degradation when retrieved evidence is reliable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO w/ RAG (the strongest RL baseline), Knowledgeable-R1 wins decisively on adversarial context (S2): 29.40% vs. 26.01% on NC-QA and 43.94% vs. 26.94% on NC-MR for Qwen2.5-7B, and 44.59% vs. 39.26% on NC-QA for Llama3.1-8B. It also leads on irrelevant context (S4) and partly-irrelevant context (S5), e.g., 31.45% vs. 27.93% on HotPotQA. On correct context (S1), the two methods are nearly tied (80.90% vs. 80.03% on PC-QA for Qwen2.5-7B), with GRPO holding a small edge on PC-MR (77.56% vs. 75.08%). Against CK-PLUG (a decoding-time conflict-detection method), Knowledgeable-R1 outperforms by 15–30+ pp across all scenarios, most dramatically on S2 where CK-PLUG drops to 7.92% on NC-QA. Against Astute-RAG (a prompting-based consolidation method), Knowledgeable-R1 leads by 10–20 pp on S2 and S5, while Astute-RAG is competitive on S1. Overall, Knowledgeable-R1 is the only method that simultaneously maintains S1 accuracy and achieves large S2 gains, whereas all baselines sacrifice one for the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (from DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1) from a single-policy, context-only training loop to a multi-objective, three-policy scheme (PK, CK, RPK) that explicitly models the parametric-vs-contextual knowledge tradeoff during generation. It departs from prior RAG robustness methods—noise-injected training, attention masking, adversarial filtering, and decoding-time adjustments like CK-PLUG—by operating at the policy level rather than the passage or token-distribution level, learning a generative decision rule for when to trust context versus fall back on internal knowledge. The asymmetric advantage transformation and adaptive β modulation are novel components that address the known bias toward context-following in RL-trained RAG models, building on reward-shaping and focused-exploration ideas from classical RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a well-documented and practically critical failure mode of RAG systems (context dominance under adversarial or noisy retrieval) with a principled RL solution that achieves large gains in counterfactual settings (+22.89% over GRPO on PK-answerable items) without sacrificing performance on correct context. The finding that the method generalizes even when trained on 99% correct context (only 1% adversarial) suggests it learns a genuine decision boundary rather than a dataset prior, which is important for deployment where adversarial context is rare. Open questions include scalability to multi-source retrieval with varying conflict densities, the S1 performance gap relative to pure GRPO (acknowledged as a tunable Pareto trade-off), and whether the adaptive β mechanism—which converges to 0.01 within 8 training steps—provides meaningful dynamism or effectively reduces to a fixed low penalty.
