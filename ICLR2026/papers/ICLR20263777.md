## Problem

Existing parallel fusion strategies for multi-modal brain tumor segmentation (e.g., concatenation or attention-based fusion) risk losing modality-common information when one or more MRI modalities are missing, causing sharp performance degradation. This paper argues that sequential information bottleneck fusion preserves shared information more effectively and provides provable advantages: a tighter generalization upper bound and a tighter Lipschitz continuity bound compared to parallel fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263777/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes four 3D MRI volumes (T1, T1ce, T2, Flair) where missing modalities are represented as zero tensors, along with a binary availability mask M_i ∈ {0,1} per modality. A modality reordering strategy randomly selects one available modality as the sequential fusion starting point to avoid initializing the bottleneck with a zero tensor. The output is a 3D segmentation mask with three tumor subregions: Whole Tumor (WT), Tumor Core (TC), and Enhancing Tumor (ET), evaluated via Dice similarity coefficient.

## Method

SMSN employs a Two-Stage Information Bottleneck Fusion Module (IBFM) that sequentially compresses modality features: stage I fuses x₁ and x₂ into bottleneck z₁, and stage II fuses z₁ with x₃ and x₄ into z₂, with mutual information terms approximated via KL divergence against a standard normal prior. A modality-aware reconstruction loss (weighted by the availability mask) ensures the latent representations retain task-relevant information for only the present modalities. A Transformer-based specific feature extraction module then disentangles modality-specific components from the fused z₂, enforced by an orthogonality loss L_o = Σ(z₂ · x_s^i)². Theoretically, the paper proves that sequential IB fusion yields a strictly tighter mutual-information-based generalization bound (Proposition 1) and a tighter Lipschitz constant than parallel fusion (Proposition 3), explaining its superior robustness under missing-modality conditions.

## Results

On BRATS20, SMSN achieves average Dice scores of 87.14 (WT), 78.80 (TC), and 63.06 (ET) across all 14 missing-modality scenarios, outperforming the strongest parallel-fusion baseline M²FTrans (85.43/77.77/62.17) by 1.7/1.0/0.9 points respectively. On BRATS18, the margins are larger, particularly for the enhancing tumor (ET) class where SMSN reaches 62.39 versus 54.34–55.21 for all baselines. Cross-domain generalization to the Brain Metastases dataset (trained on BRATS20, no fine-tuning) yields 57.03/45.70/36.89 average Dice for WT/TC/ET, surpassing M²FTrans (55.16/38.62/31.74) by 1.9/7.1/5.2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMSN consistently outperforms all four parallel-fusion baselines (mmFormer, M²FTrans, MMMViT, IMS²Trans) on both BRATS18 and BRATS20 across all three tumor subregions. The largest advantage appears on the ET class: on BRATS18, SMSN achieves 62.39 vs. 54.34 (mmFormer), 54.95 (M²FTrans), 55.21 (MMMViT), and 54.56 (IMS²Trans) — a gap of 7–8 Dice points. On BRATS20, the ET gap narrows to ~1–3 points (63.06 vs. 60.09–62.17), while WT and TC gaps remain around 1–2 points. Against non-fusion methods (M3AE, ShaSpec), SMSN gains ~1 Dice on WT/TC and >5 Dice on ET (Table 6). In the cross-domain Brain Metastases experiment, SMSN leads M²FTrans by 7.1 Dice on TC and 5.2 on ET, with lower standard deviations indicating greater robustness. MMMViT is the weakest baseline overall, particularly on BRATS18 where it lags by 7+ Dice on WT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Information Bottleneck (IB) framework from single-modality compression (Tishby et al., 1999) and the IB-driven hierarchical perception of ITHP (Xiao et al., 2024) to the multi-modal missing-modality segmentation setting. It departs from the dominant parallel fusion paradigm (concatenation or attention-based, as in mmFormer, M²FTrans, MMMViT, IMS²Trans) by proposing sequential IB-based fusion, and provides the first formal theoretical comparison—via tighter Lipschitz and generalization bounds—between sequential and parallel fusion architectures. The modality reordering strategy and orthogonality loss for disentangling common vs. specific features build on shared-specific decomposition ideas (Wang et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2023) but integrate them within the IB objective rather than as a separate architectural branch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, information-theoretic justification for why sequential fusion should be more robust under missing modalities, moving the field beyond purely empirical comparisons of fusion strategies. The cross-domain transfer from gliomas to brain metastases without fine-tuning is practically relevant for clinical settings where labeled metastasis data is scarce. However, the theoretical guarantees rely on assumptions (1-Lipschitz components, Assumption 1 on cross-modal conditional information) that are difficult to verify in practice, and the sensitivity analysis reveals that IB hyperparameters (β, γ) require careful tuning. Open questions include whether the sequential IB advantage persists with more than four modalities, with higher-dimensional 3D volumes, or in fully unsupervised/self-supervised regimes.
