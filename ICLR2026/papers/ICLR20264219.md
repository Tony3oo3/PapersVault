## Problem

Latent diffusion models still operate on 2D spatial grids that fail to exploit the high spatial redundancies in natural images, and prior work (REPA, TiTok) either uses SSL features only for alignment or still requires multiple discrete tokens. The paper asks whether a single continuous token from a self-supervised vision transformer can serve simultaneously as a faithful image encoder and a well-structured latent space for generative modeling.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (256×256 for ImageNet, variable for COCO) and compresses it into a single continuous latent vector (typically in R^{1×768}) via the [cls] token of a frozen SSL ViT. For class-conditional generation, a learned class embedding is concatenated to the noisy token; for text-to-image, cross-attention integrates frozen language model outputs (CLIP, InternVL, or Gemma-2B). The generative decoder operates in a pre-trained 2D VAE latent space (SD-VAE) rather than pixel space, and the final output is a decoded 256×256 (or 512×512) RGB image.

## Method

RepTok fine-tunes only the [cls] token embedding of a frozen SSL encoder (DINOv2, MAE, or CLIP) while jointly training a DiT-XL/2 generative decoder with a flow matching objective, so that the single token acquires reconstruction-relevant low-level detail. A cosine-similarity loss (Eq. 3) regularizes the adapted token to stay close to its frozen SSL counterpart, preserving the smooth, semantically structured geometry needed for generation without requiring KL divergence or vector quantization. For latent space synthesis, an attention-free MLP-Mixer replaces the transformer generator, since token-to-token interactions are unnecessary when the entire image is represented by one token, reducing training FLOPs to ~1.7% of SiT. The framework extends to T2I by concatenating four learnable tokens with the noisy [cls] token and applying cross-attention to a frozen language backbone, achieving competitive MS-COCO zero-shot FID in under 20 hours on 4×A100 GPUs.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, RepTok-L (516M params, MLP-Mixer) achieves FID 1.88 with CFG=1.5 using only 42.1K total PFlops, compared to SiT-XL/2+REPA+CFG reaching FID 1.42 at 143.9K PFlops (3.4× more compute). RepTok uses just 1.7% of the per-iteration FLOPs of SiT (23.0 vs 118.6 GFLOPs/iter) due to its single-token latent and attention-free architecture. In the tokenizer comparison (Table 1), RepTok's single continuous token achieves rFID 1.85 and gFID 1.88, matching or exceeding 128-token TiTok-S (gFID 1.97) and 1–256-token FlexTok d18-d28 (gFID 1.86). For text-to-image on MS-COCO, RepTok reaches competitive zero-shot FID in under 20 hours on 4×A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest transformer-based baseline, SiT-XL/2+REPA+CFG (FID 1.42, 143.9K PFlops), RepTok-L+CFG achieves FID 1.88 at 42.1K PFlops—a 0.46 FID gap in exchange for 3.4× less total compute. Compared to SiT-XL/2+CFG without REPA (FID 2.06, 212.5K PFlops), RepTok-L+CFG actually achieves a *better* FID (1.88) while using only 20% of the compute. Against the 128-token discrete TiTok-S (gFID 1.97), RepTok's single continuous token achieves a lower gFID of 1.88, and against FlexTok d18-d28 (gFID 1.86) the gap is negligible (0.02). In reconstruction, RepTok substantially outperforms RCG (Li et al., 2024): PSNR 14.94 vs 9.31 and rFID 1.85 vs 3.20, confirming that the fine-tuned [cls] token preserves far more pixel-level information than purely semantic codes. Against the 2D-grid LDM baseline (32×32 tokens), RepTok improves gFID from 7.76 to 1.88 while reducing the latent from 1024 tokens to 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

RepTok sits at the intersection of two recent threads: (1) using SSL representations to accelerate or guide latent diffusion training (REPA aligns diffusion features with DINOv2 embeddings; RCG generates semantic codes then transports them to pixels), and (2) compressing the 2D spatial latent grid into 1D token sequences (TiTok, FlexTok). The key departure is that RepTok uses the pooled [cls] token of a pretrained SSL encoder *directly as the latent space* rather than as a guidance signal or a spatial feature map, and pushes compression to a single continuous token. A novel cosine-similarity regularization preserves the SSL space's smooth geometry while allowing the token to absorb reconstruction-relevant detail, and the resulting single-token regime enables an attention-free MLP-Mixer generator, a design choice not explored in prior latent diffusion or flow-matching work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 90%+ reduction in training FLOPs relative to transformer-based diffusion baselines (DiT, SiT) while achieving competitive FID (1.88 with CFG on ImageNet 256×256) makes high-quality image generation accessible at a fraction of the compute, which is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. The finding that only fine-tuning the [cls] token embedding—leaving the rest of the SSL encoder frozen—is sufficient to inject pixel-level detail is a clean and reusable insight. However, the single-token bottleneck limits expressiveness for complex multi-object scenes and high-resolution generation, and the cosine-similarity regularization introduces a reconstruction-vs-generation trade-off that constrains pixel-level fidelity. Whether this single-token paradigm can be extended to video, 3D, or higher resolutions without sacrificing quality remains an open question.
