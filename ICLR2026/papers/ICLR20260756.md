## Problem

Existing 3D mesh generation pipelines either produce unstructured triangle meshes via iso-surfacing or generate triangles autoregressively and then apply heuristic triangle-to-quad conversion, both of which fail to produce the clean, artist-friendly edge flow required for production-ready quadrilateral assets. No prior method generates native quadrilateral-dominant meshes in an end-to-end autoregressive manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260756/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

QuadGPT takes a point cloud with normals (40,960 points, augmented with random perturbations) as conditioning input and produces a quadrilateral-dominant mesh with a small number of triangles. The mesh is serialized as a canonical sequence of fixed 12-token blocks per face, using 10-bit quantization of vertex coordinates normalized to [-0.95, 0.95]³, with a context window of 36,864 tokens supporting high-poly meshes up to 20,000 faces.

## Method

QuadGPT introduces a unified serialization where both triangles and quads are encoded as 12-token blocks (quads flatten 4×3 coordinates; triangles prepend 3 padding tokens before 3×3 coordinates), letting the Transformer implicitly learn face type without explicit type tokens. The architecture is a 1.1B-parameter Hourglass Transformer with causality-preserving shortening layers (factors 3 and 4) that capture global context at a bottleneck and local detail at the periphery, conditioned on shape via cross-attention from a Michelangelo encoder. Training uses a curriculum strategy: the model is initialized from a converged triangle-only generator and progressively annealed from r=0 (pure triangles) to r=1 (quad-dominant) via a learnable quad-dominance embedding. A novel tDPO (truncated Direct Preference Optimization) stage then fine-tunes the model on random sequence prefixes using a topological reward that maximizes average edge-loop length and penalizes generation fractures, optimizing global quad flow without requiring full-sequence inference.

## Results

On dense AI-generated meshes, QuadGPT achieves CD=0.057, HD=0.147, Quad Ratio=80%, and a user-study score of 4.9/5, outperforming all autoregressive baselines (MeshAnythingv2, BPT, DeepMesh, FastMesh) on every metric. On artist-designed meshes, QuadGPT achieves the best geometric fidelity (CD=0.043, HD=0.095) and the highest user-study score (4.8/5), with a Quad Ratio of 78%. The field-guided baseline QuadriFlow attains 100% Quad Ratio but suffers from severe geometric degradation (CD=0.281, HD=0.531 on artist meshes) and the lowest user preference (0.3/5), indicating that perfect quad coverage without geometric accuracy is not production-ready.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuadGPT decisively outperforms all four autoregressive triangle-generation baselines (MeshAnythingv2, BPT, DeepMesh, FastMesh) on user-study scores (4.9 vs. 1.1–3.3 on dense meshes; 4.8 vs. 1.9–3.1 on artist meshes) and Quad Ratio (80%/78% vs. 3%–66%), confirming that post-hoc triangle-to-quad conversion fundamentally limits topological quality. BPT is the closest geometric competitor on artist meshes (CD=0.051 vs. 0.043) but still trails by 1.7 points in user preference and 29 points in Quad Ratio. QuadriFlow achieves 100% Quad Ratio by construction but produces severely degraded geometry (CD=0.281, HD=0.531 on artist meshes) and the worst user score (0.3), failing on complex topologies and sharp features. FastMesh has the lowest Quad Ratio (3%/17%), indicating its triangle outputs are nearly impossible to convert into coherent quads. The two commercial tools (Tripo AI and Exoside Quad Remesher) are compared only qualitatively in Figure 12, where QuadGPT produces cleaner edge flow and fewer topological artifacts on complex organic shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

QuadGPT extends the autoregressive mesh generation line (MeshGPT, MeshAnything, BPT, DeepMesh, FastMesh) — all of which are confined to triangular outputs — by introducing the first end-to-end autoregressive framework for native quadrilateral and mixed-element meshes. Architecturally, it adopts the Hourglass Transformer from Meshtron (Hao et al., 2024) for efficient long-sequence processing, and its reinforcement learning stage is a truncated-sequence adaptation of DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) with a topology-specific reward. Conceptually, it departs from both the indirect iso-surfacing pipeline (which yields unstructured triangles) and field-guided quad meshing (QuadriFlow, cross-field methods), which are multi-stage optimization procedures rather than generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a practical gap between AI-generated 3D assets and production-ready quad meshes used in game and animation pipelines, where clean edge flow is essential for subdivision, UV unwrapping, and deformation. Key open questions remain: the domain gap between clean training point clouds and noisy AI-generated inputs causes fractures on out-of-distribution shapes; the model cannot control polygon count for LOD budgets; and the off-policy DPO with a programmatic reward cannot capture full artistic preference. Future directions include end-to-end geometry-topology co-generation, part-aware generation, and online RL with artist-trained reward models.
