## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation models face a fundamental generalization bottleneck: they overfit to the limited semantic coverage of small optical MoCap datasets and fail to handle novel, long-tail, or out-of-distribution instructions. Unlike adjacent video generation models that demonstrate strong human-centric generalization from web-scale training, motion generation lacks both the data scale and a systematic mechanism to transfer video-generation priors into high-fidelity motion synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264576/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The full ViMoGen model takes a text prompt as input and, at inference, also generates a reference video offline via Wan2.1 from which visual MoCap (CameraHMR) extracts video motion tokens; the distilled ViMoGen-light variant requires only the text prompt. Output is a 100-frame (5-second) 3D human motion sequence at 20 fps in the 276-dimensional SMPL-X overparameterized representation (root translation, root rotation, joint rotations, joint locations, and joint velocities) following DARTControl. During training, video motion tokens are simulated by perturbing ground-truth motions with compound noise (random corruption, jitter, temporal dropout) and masking global translation to mimic visual MoCap artifacts.

## Method

ViMoGen is a flow-matching-based diffusion transformer initialized from the 1.3B-parameter Wan2.1 video model, employing a dual-branch architecture where a Text-to-Motion (T2M) branch conditions on T5-XXL text embeddings for high-fidelity MoCap-quality generation, and a Motion-to-Motion (M2M) branch attends to video-derived motion tokens for semantic generalization; the two branches share ~66% of DiT parameters (self-attention and FFN) and are selected adaptively via a VLM-based alignment check between the generated video and the prompt. Training uses a curriculum strategy that prioritizes T2M on manually annotated data and M2M on large-scale auto-labeled data, with synthetic data receiving double weighting. ViMoGen-light is obtained by knowledge distillation: the full model generates motions for 14,000 synthetically constructed long-tail prompts, and a T2M-only student is trained on this data, inheriting generalization priors without requiring video generation at inference. The approach is supported by ViMoGen-228K (171.5K text-motion pairs + 56.6K text-video-motion triplets from optical MoCap, in-the-wild video, and synthetic video sources) and MBench, a 9-dimension hierarchical benchmark validated against human preferences.

## Results

On MBench, ViMoGen achieves the best Motion Condition Consistency (0.53) and Motion Generalizability (0.68), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline MotionLCM (0.48 and 0.55 respectively). The distilled ViMoGen-light variant matches MotionLCM's generalization score (0.55) while eliminating the need for video generation at inference. ViMoGen also attains the lowest Jitter Degree (0.0108), though it trades off on Dynamic Degree (0.0251, lowest among all methods) and Body Penetration (1.78, worst among all methods), reflecting the stability-over-dynamics bias introduced by diverse video-sourced training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViMoGen decisively outperforms all seven baselines on the two headline semantic metrics: Motion Condition Consistency (0.53 vs. best baseline MotionLCM at 0.48) and Motion Generalizability (0.68 vs. MotionLCM at 0.55), with margins of +0.05 and +0.13 respectively. The distilled ViMoGen-light matches MotionLCM's generalization (0.55) and is within 0.01 on consistency (0.47 vs. 0.48), confirming effective knowledge transfer without video-generation overhead. On motion quality, ViMoGen achieves the best Jitter Degree (0.0108, beating MotionDiffuse's 0.0111) but the worst Dynamic Degree (0.0251 vs. MotionLCM's 0.0439) and Body Penetration (1.78 vs. MotionCraft's 1.15), a trade-off the authors attribute to the stability bias of video-sourced training data. ViMoGen-light posts the best Foot Sliding (0.0051) and Pose Quality (2.10) among all methods. On the standard HumanML3D benchmark (Appendix), ViMoGen also leads on R-Precision Top-1 and FID, confirming gains are not benchmark-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-motion generation line of work by explicitly transferring semantic priors from large-scale video generation models (Wan2.1) into a flow-matching diffusion transformer, departing from prior approaches that either rely solely on optical MoCap data (MDM, MotionLCM, T2M-GPT) or extract motion from ViGen via costly optimization-based pipelines (Millan et al., 2025; Albaba et al., 2025). The key architectural departure is a gated dual-branch design (T2M + M2M) that adaptively fuses high-fidelity MoCap dynamics with broad video-derived semantic coverage, rather than treating video generation as a one-shot initialization. The motion representation follows DARTControl's overparameterized SMPL-X formulation, and the generative backbone inherits from Wan2.1's DiT weights, positioning the work at the intersection of video generation and motion synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the core generalization bottleneck in text-to-motion generation by providing three complementary resources: a 228K multi-source dataset (the largest optical MoCap aggregation to date), a model that achieves state-of-the-art generalization, and MBench—the first hierarchical benchmark evaluating motion quality, prompt fidelity, and open-world generalization beyond the FID-centric paradigm. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to single-person motion, complex high-dynamic actions still depend heavily on ViGen initialization quality, and a trade-off exists between generalization and specific quality metrics (e.g., Dynamic Degree). Open questions include whether the gated fusion strategy scales to multi-person interaction, whether contact-aware MoCap can close the remaining quality gap, and whether this framework can evolve toward a general-purpose motion foundation model.
