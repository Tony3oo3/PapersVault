## Problem

Measuring the in-context computational effort of language models is a key challenge: next-token loss fails to capture reasoning complexity, and prior methods like Prediction of Hidden States (PHi) are invasive, require additional unstable training, and are sensitive to architectural placement. The paper asks whether a simpler, non-invasive measure of per-token computational density can be computed directly from pre-trained models without modifying their training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262498/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained autoregressive model equipped with a Multiple Token Prediction (MTP) auxiliary head (either built-in, as in MiMo-7B, or trained via teacher-student distillation on a frozen base model) and a token sequence. At each position it computes the full model's next-token distribution π and the MTP module's one-step-ahead prediction π_MTP, optionally giving the MTP module access to the current token embedding e_t to isolate non-trivial computation. The output is a per-token MTD score (KL divergence) and, for Divergence Steering, a steered sampling distribution s_α constructed by geodesic interpolation between π and π_MTP, with an optional fixed-entropy projection to decouple from temperature.

## Method

MTD is defined as the KL divergence between the full model's output distribution π and the shallow MTP module's prediction π_MTP, drawing a direct structural analogy to PHi's posterior-prior KL but operating on the discrete token distribution rather than a continuous latent space. Giving the MTP module access to the latest token embedding e_t is critical: it allows the auxiliary head to account for trivial token-level shifts, so the remaining divergence isolates information gain attributable to the model's deeper computational layers. For Divergence Steering, the paper constructs a new sampling distribution s_α by performing spherical linear interpolation (geodesic path under the Fisher-Rao metric) between the square-root representations of π and π_MTP, controlled by a single parameter α: α=0 recovers the original distribution, α=1 uses the MTP distribution, and α<0 extrapolates away from the MTP prediction to create an 'anti-speculative' distribution biased toward computationally dense tokens. An optional entropy-preserving projection (via temperature scaling of s_α) makes α orthogonal to the standard temperature parameter, providing two independent levers for shaping generation. The entire pipeline requires no additional fine-tuning of the base model when an MTP head is already present, making it a lightweight, post-hoc analysis and steering tool.

## Results

On the MATH dataset, MTD correlates positively with problem difficulty (r=0.199 [0.189, 0.208] for self-generated CoTs, controlling for NLL), while NLL correlates negatively (r=-0.205 [-0.215, -0.195]). For selecting correct reasoning chains, choosing the CoT with lower MTD yields 67.1% [65.5%, 68.5%] accuracy on MATH and 66.0% [62.4%, 69.3%] on GSM-8k; combining lower NLL and lower MTD improves this to 80.4% [78.5%, 81.9%] and 75.5% [71.7%, 79.2%] respectively. On the in-context language learning task, MTD with latest-embedding access achieves the strongest partial correlation with PFA complexity (r=0.524 [0.480, 0.565]), clearly separating the one computationally demanding task from four 'boring' ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the PHi loss (Herrmann et al., 2025), which measures in-context computation via a variational information bottleneck in latent space. MTD with latest-embedding access outperforms PHi at distinguishing the single complex task (ICLL) from four simple tasks, showing the clearest separation in normalized loss values (Figure 4). MTD with embedding access also achieves a stronger partial correlation with PFA complexity (r=0.524) than PHi, while MTD without embedding access is an outlier that is negatively correlated with complexity when controlling for NLL. Notably, the relationship between computational effort and correctness reverses: for PHi on a Llama 3B model, correct answers were associated with *higher* PHi loss, whereas for MTD on MiMo-7B, correct answers are associated with *lower* MTD (67.1% selection accuracy). MTD is complementary to NLL rather than a replacement: NLL alone gives 73.3% selection accuracy on MATH, MTD alone gives 67.1%, but their combination reaches 80.4%, indicating they capture distinct signals about reasoning quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Prediction of Hidden States (PHi) framework (Herrmann et al., 2025) by replacing its latent-space information bottleneck with a simpler, non-invasive KL divergence between the full model's output distribution and that of a shallow Multiple Token Prediction (MTP) module. The MTP architecture, developed for speculative decoding acceleration (Gloeckle et al., 2024; Xiaomi et al., 2025), is repurposed here as the 'shallow shortcut' whose divergence from the full model quantifies per-token computational effort. The Divergence Steering decoding method further builds on the Fisher-Rao geodesic interpolation framework (Miyamoto et al., 2024) to construct a principled interpolation between the two distributions, introducing a new 'computational character' control knob orthogonal to temperature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MTD provides a practical, zero-fine-tuning metric for probing when and where a language model is doing 'real' computation versus pattern matching, which is valuable for interpretability, dynamic compute allocation, and intrinsic motivation in agents. The finding that NLL *decreases* with problem difficulty while MTD *increases* is conceptually striking but the effect sizes are modest (r ≈ 0.18–0.20), and NLL still outperforms MTD for predicting answer correctness, limiting immediate practical impact. Open questions include whether the MTD–correctness relationship is model-dependent (contrasting with PHi findings), whether Divergence Steering can improve reasoning in large post-trained models, and how MTD behaves as MTP module capacity scales relative to the main model.
