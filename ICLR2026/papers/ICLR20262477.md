## Problem

Subject-to-video (S2V) generation models suffer from a 'copy-paste problem' where the generated video replicates not only the reference subject's identity but also its background and contextual details, causing poor text-prompt following. This stems from the dominant in-pair training paradigm that samples reference images from the same video as the target, entangling identity with irrelevant context. Existing cross-pair datasets that decouple reference from target are restricted to narrow domains like human faces, leaving a gap for general-purpose subject categories (animals, products, IP characters) with sufficient contextual diversity.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides ~1M identity-consistent cross-pair samples where the reference image (single or multi-subject) is captured in a visually distinct context—different background, viewpoint, or pose—from the target video clip. Training operates at 480p resolution with 5–10 second clips, and the reference-to-target pairs span over 30,000 multi-subject scenes. At inference, the model takes one or more reference images plus a free-form text prompt and produces a video that preserves subject identity while faithfully rendering the prompted scene, action, and environment.

## Method

Phantom-Data is constructed via a three-stage pipeline. First, S2V Detection uses Qwen2.5 for keyword extraction from captions, Qwen2.5-VL for open-set visual grounding, geometric bbox filtering (4%–90% area, ≥128×128 px), and InternVL2.5 for a visual-semantic recheck enforcing completeness, specificity, and text-image alignment. Second, Contextually Diverse Retrieval builds a bank of 53M video segments plus 3B LAION images, encodes subjects with category-specific identity-preserving embeddings (ArcFace for faces, a CLIP-based IR model for general objects, concatenated for humans), and retrieves candidates within upper and lower similarity bounds to ensure same-identity-but-different-context matches. Third, Prior-Based Identity Verification applies category-specific priors (brand-logo visibility for products, same-long-video constraint for living entities) followed by a VLM-based pairwise check (Doubao-1.5-Vision-Pro-32K) that confirms both identity consistency and sufficient background/scene divergence. The resulting dataset is used to train a 1.3B-parameter Phantom-wan model (Wan2.1 backbone) with Rectified Flow for 30k steps on 64 A100 GPUs, demonstrating that cross-pair training substantially improves prompt alignment and video quality while maintaining identity consistency on par with in-pair baselines.

## Results

On the 100-case test suite, Phantom-Data achieves the best prompt-following score (Reward-TA 3.827) and the highest GPT-4o subject consistency (3.041), substantially outperforming the in-pair baseline (Reward-TA 2.074, GPT-4o 2.481). It also leads on all VBench video-quality sub-metrics: IQ 0.739, BG 0.948, and Subj 0.944. Subject consistency measured by DINO (0.416) is competitive with in-pair (0.478) while being far superior to face-only cross-pair (0.354), confirming that cross-context training preserves identity without sacrificing prompt alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the in-pair baseline (Chen et al. 2025b), Phantom-Data wins decisively on prompt following (Reward-TA 3.827 vs. 2.074, a +84% improvement) and GPT-4o subject consistency (3.041 vs. 2.481), while trading a modest DINO drop (0.416 vs. 0.478) for dramatically better text alignment. The in-pair + data augmentation variant (also from Chen et al. 2025b) narrows the gap slightly (Reward-TA 2.427) but still lags Phantom-Data by 1.4 points on Reward-TA and 0.25 on GPT-4o, confirming that geometric/color augmentations cannot substitute for genuine cross-context diversity. Against the face-only cross-pair baseline (Zhong et al. 2025 / Polyak et al. 2024), Phantom-Data achieves a much higher DINO score (0.416 vs. 0.354) and GPT-4o score (3.041 vs. 2.378), showing that extending cross-pair training beyond faces to general subjects preserves identity more faithfully. The face cross-pair method retains a slight edge on Temporal (0.983) and Motion (0.989) VBench sub-metrics, but these differences are marginal (<0.01) and come at the cost of significantly weaker general-subject consistency. In the user study, Phantom-Data received 76% of preference votes versus 11% for in-pair+aug, 7% for face cross-pair, and 6% for in-pair.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-pair training paradigm introduced by Polyak et al. (MovieGen) and Zhong et al. (Concat-ID), which were restricted to face-level identity matching, to a general-purpose setting covering humans, animals, products, and multi-subject scenes. It departs from the dominant in-pair training paradigm (Chen et al., Liang et al., Huang et al., Jiang et al.) that relies on data augmentation (background removal, color jittering, geometric transforms) to mitigate the copy-paste problem, arguing that such augmentations cannot adequately decouple identity from complex contextual factors like viewpoint and motion. The three-stage pipeline repurposes existing components—Qwen2.5-VL for open-set grounding, ArcFace for facial embeddings, and CLIP-based encoders (inspired by ObjectMate) for general object identity—into a unified S2V-specific data construction workflow, but the key conceptual contribution is the systematic combination of large-scale cross-context retrieval (53M videos + 3B LAION images) with prior-guided verification to ensure both identity fidelity and contextual diversity simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Phantom-Data is the first publicly available general-purpose cross-pair dataset for subject-consistent video generation, filling a critical gap where prior cross-pair resources were limited to faces. The 76% user-study preference and substantial Reward-TA improvement (3.827 vs. 2.074 for in-pair) while maintaining competitive DINO identity scores (0.416 vs. 0.478) provide strong evidence that the copy-paste problem is fundamentally a data-construction issue rather than an architecture limitation. Open questions remain: the pipeline's reliance on VLM-based verification (Doubao-1.5-Vision-Pro-32K) introduces potential verification errors at scale, the dataset's subject categories are bounded by what the detection and retrieval stages can handle, and the authors themselves note the need to extend from specific subject references to more general, flexible image-and-text control. The dataset's practical value is high given its public availability and the growing ecosystem of open-source S2V models (Phantom-wan, VACE, SkyReels-A2) that can directly benefit from it.
