## Problem

Early-fusion open-vocabulary detectors (e.g., Grounding DINO, LLMDet) produce confident false positive predictions on images that contain none of the prompted objects, a failure mode invisible to standard benchmarks like COCO and LVIS that almost always include at least one annotated instance per image. The paper identifies vision-language fusion layers as the root cause: their cross-attention mechanism cannot select zero tokens, so when no prompted class is present, irrelevant class information is distributed across all vision features, triggering spurious detections.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images with a text prompt of one or more class names, to which one or more semantically neutral attention sink tokens are appended (e.g., 24 tokens for LLMDet). The model outputs bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores as usual, but any prediction whose assigned class is a sink token is simply discarded post-inference. No architectural modification or retraining is required; the entire intervention is a few lines of code that add tokens to the prompt and filter predictions.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free attention sink approach that appends semantically neutral tokens to the input prompt, giving the cross-attention mechanism a 'none of the above' target to absorb excess attention when no prompted class is present in the image. Sinks are initialized via one of three strategies—random Gaussian, the mean of all word embeddings in the vocabulary, or the embedding of a special-character string like '[( )]'—with the optimal choice depending on the specific model (e.g., random works best for Grounding DINO, mean for GLIP and MM-Grounding DINO, special characters for LLMDet). The mechanism works because the model has already learned to dilute irrelevant class signals below the positive-class confidence threshold during training; sinks simply provide a neutral destination for that residual information rather than letting it accumulate on negative class tokens. After inference, predictions labeled with a sink token are filtered out, reducing background false positives by up to 5× at FPR_bg = 0.01 while preserving or slightly improving AP on positive classes. The paper also shows that 64 VOC images suffice to select the best sink initialization for a given model, enabling rapid tuning without full LVIS evaluation.

## Results

On LVIS MiniVal, attention sinks dramatically improve early-fusion OVD models at low background false-positive rates: LLMDet Swin-B jumps from AP 0.047 to 0.222 at FPR_bg=0.01 (a 4.7× gain) and from 0.125 to 0.349 at FPR_bg=0.05. LLMDet Swin-T achieves a similar 5× improvement at FPR_bg=0.01 (0.045→0.223). At the standard AP setting (no FPR constraint), performance is largely preserved, with most models showing changes of less than 0.04 AP, confirming that sinks mitigate false positives without substantially hurting true-positive detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the strictest FPR_bg=0.01, late-interaction OV-DINO (AP 0.274) still outperforms all early-fusion models with attention sinks (best: LLMDet Swin-T at 0.223), and YOLO-World (0.189) is competitive with the weaker early-fusion models. However, at the more practical FPR_bg=0.05, attention-sink LLMDet Swin-B (0.349) surpasses both OV-DINO (0.316) and YOLO-World (0.238), and LLMDet Swin-T (0.326) also edges out OV-DINO. At FPR_bg=0.1 and 0.25, the gap narrows further, with LLMDet Swin-B reaching 0.403 and 0.449 respectively, well above both late-interaction baselines. Compared to their own pre-sink versions, all five early-fusion models show substantial gains: MM-GDINO Swin-T improves by +0.138 at FPR 0.01, and LLMDet Swin-B by +0.175, while standard AP changes are minimal (≤0.04 for most models). The key takeaway is that attention sinks close most of the gap between early-fusion and late-interaction models at practical operating points, making early-fusion architectures viable for real-world deployment where background images dominate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of open-vocabulary detection (GLIP, Grounding DINO, LLMDet) and the emerging literature on vision-language model hallucination (POPE, instruction-tuning remedies). Its key conceptual move is repurposing the attention-sink / register-token idea from NLP streaming (Xiao et al., 2023) and ViT registers (Darcet et al., 2023) as a semantically neutral 'none of the above' option in the cross-attention of early-fusion OVD models. It departs from the Grounding DINO 1.5 approach of scaling up negative-sample training by showing that a training-free prompt modification addresses the root cause in the vision-language fusion layers, and it explicitly contrasts early-fusion and late-interaction architectures to isolate where the failure originates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard benchmarks (COCO, LVIS) systematically mask background false positives because they rarely contain images without any target class is a significant gap for the OVD community, especially as these models move toward real-world deployment in security and medical imaging where background images dominate. The training-free, few-lines-of-code fix is immediately adoptable across all early-fusion OVD models, but the lack of a one-size-fits-all sink initialisation and the modest AP drops on positive classes for some models leave room for architectural solutions (e.g., gated attention) that the authors call for. An open question is whether the same 'attention cannot select zero tokens' pathology affects other cross-modal tasks such as referring expression comprehension or visual question answering.
