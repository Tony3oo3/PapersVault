## Problem

Mainstream Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods for vision-language models like CLIP rely on Shannon Entropy to select high-confidence augmented views, but CLIP's inherent prediction bias from imbalanced web-scale pretraining causes SE to produce systematically biased uncertainty estimates. Because SE applies a uniform formula across all classes, it cannot account for the varying degrees of bias between head and tail categories, leading to suboptimal view selection and degraded adaptation performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261657/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unlabeled test image and a set of text prompts (hand-crafted templates or GPT-generated descriptions) as input. The image is augmented N=64 times to produce diverse views, and a memory bank of size 10 per category stores pseudo-labeled samples from continuously incoming test instances for online bias estimation. The output is a single class label obtained by aggregating the probability distributions of the top N_v views (selected by lowest ADTE) and taking the argmax.

## Method

ADTE generalizes Tsallis Entropy by assigning a class-specific non-extensive parameter q^l to each category, derived via min-max normalization of estimated label biases (computed through Jacobi iteration over pseudo-labeled samples in a memory bank). The key theoretical insight is that when 0<q<1, TE naturally corrects the underestimation of entropy for tail classes that SE suffers from, and SE serves as a strict lower bound on TE's performance (Property 1: q→1 recovers SE; Property 2: smaller q selects views with higher Top-K Cumulative Reliability). The pipeline augments the test image, computes ADTE for each view using the class-specific q^l values, selects the N_v views with the smallest ADTE, and aggregates their predictions. ADTE integrates seamlessly with logit adjustment for additional debiasing, and requires no distribution-specific hyperparameter tuning beyond a fixed normalization interval [0.01, 0.9].

## Results

On the OOD benchmark (ImageNet and five variants), ADTE with ViT-B/16 achieves 67.5% average accuracy (66.5% OOD average) with template prompts and 67.5% average with CuPL prompts, outperforming the strongest baseline Zero (66.2% / 65.0%) by 1.3% / 1.5%. With the larger ViT-L/14 backbone, ADTE reaches 77.4% average accuracy (77.2% OOD average) using CuPL prompts, surpassing Frolic (76.4% / 76.2%) by 1.0%. On the 10 cross-domain benchmarks, ADTE_CuPL achieves the highest average of 71.8% (ViT-B/16) and 77.7% (ViT-L/14), beating Frolic by 0.7% and 0.6% respectively. ADTE also shows the largest gains on the most biased dataset ImageNet-A, improving over CLIP by 14.9% (ViT-B/16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADTE consistently outperforms all 8 baselines (CLIP, TPT, TDA, Zero, Dyna, BCA, CuPL, Frolic) on both ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/14 across the OOD and cross-domain benchmarks. Against the strongest SE-based TTA method Zero, ADTE_Templates gains +0.9% on ImageNet-1k and +1.5% on OOD average (ViT-B/16), while ADTE_CuPL gains +1.8% on ImageNet-1k over Frolic. The advantage is most pronounced on ImageNet-A (the most biased OOD variant), where ADTE_Templates achieves 65.5% vs. Zero's 64.0% (+1.5%) and ADTE_CuPL reaches 63.5% vs. Frolic's 60.4% (+3.1%). On the 10 cross-domain datasets, ADTE_CuPL leads Frolic by 0.7% (ViT-B/16) and 0.6% (ViT-L/14) in average accuracy, with the largest per-dataset gains on Aircraft (+2.0% over Frolic) and DTD (+1.1% over Frolic) for ViT-B/16. ADTE also integrates with DEYO, boosting worst-group accuracy by 2.26% on ColoredMNIST with negligible overhead, and generalizes to unimodal ResNext-101 and CLIP successors (SigLIP, SigLIP2, EVA-CLIP) with consistent 2–3% improvements over their zero-shot baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Zero TTA framework (Farina et al., 2024), which uses Shannon Entropy to select high-confidence augmented views, and on Frolic (Zhu et al., 2024), whose bias estimation via pseudo-labels and Jacobi iteration is adopted for computing class-specific parameters. The key conceptual departure is identifying that the entropy metric itself—not just the model's logits—is a source of bias in TTA: because CLIP's predictions are systematically skewed toward head classes, SE's uniform per-class computation produces distorted uncertainty estimates. By generalizing SE to Tsallis Entropy with a class-specific non-extensivity parameter q^l, the paper reframes view selection as a bias-aware problem rather than a pure uncertainty-minimization problem, while retaining the simplicity of the Zero pipeline (no prompt updates, no gradient steps).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that ADTE is a drop-in replacement for SE in any existing TTA pipeline (Zero, TPT, DEYO, etc.) with negligible computational overhead (~0.01s extra) and no per-dataset hyperparameter tuning, making it broadly adoptable. The paper's extensive robustness analysis (100% pseudo-label noise, continual domain mixing, unimodal and SigLIP architectures) strengthens confidence in real-world deployment. However, the authors acknowledge that ADTE's advantage diminishes when the model's predictions are already well-calibrated (low-bias scenarios), and the bias estimation relies on pseudo-labels that may be unreliable in extreme OOD settings. An open question is whether the class-specific q^l formulation can be further simplified or whether the memory-bank-based bias tracking remains stable under truly non-stationary streaming distributions.
