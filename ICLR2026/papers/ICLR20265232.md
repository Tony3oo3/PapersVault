## Problem

Standard message-passing GNNs used in Relational Deep Learning suffer from limited structural expressiveness and restricted long-range information flow, making them inadequate for capturing complex patterns in relational entity graphs. While Graph Transformers offer greater expressiveness, existing architectures fail on relational data because their positional encodings require expensive precomputation that does not scale to massive heterogeneous graphs, they lack mechanisms for temporal dynamics and schema constraints, and their tokenization schemes lose critical structural information.

## Input / Output

For each seed node, RELGT samples a fixed-size local neighborhood of K=300 nodes within 2 hops using temporal-aware sampling (only including nodes with timestamps ≤ the seed's timestamp to prevent leakage). Each neighbor is tokenized as a 5-tuple of (multi-modal features, categorical node type, relative hop distance, relative time difference, and a subgraph GNN positional encoding), which are individually encoded and concatenated into a single d-dimensional token. The model outputs a node representation embedding that is passed to a task-specific prediction head, producing either a classification score (evaluated by AUC) or a regression value (evaluated by MAE).

## Method

RELGT introduces a multi-element tokenization strategy that decomposes each node into five independently encoded components—multi-modal features, node type, hop distance, relative time, and a lightweight GNN-based positional encoding computed on the sampled subgraph with randomly re-initialized node features at each training step to break structural symmetries while approximately preserving permutation equivariance. The Transformer network combines a local module (L-layer all-pair self-attention over the K local tokens, providing broader structural coverage than GNN message passing) with a global module (attention to B=4096 learnable centroids updated via EMA K-Means on seed node features per mini-batch). The final node representation is obtained by concatenating local and global embeddings and passing them through a feed-forward network. This design avoids expensive global positional encoding precomputation while explicitly encoding heterogeneity, temporality, and local topology, and is trained end-to-end with standard task-specific losses.

## Results

Across 21 entity classification and regression tasks on RelBench, RELGT achieves relative gains of up to 18.43% (rel-trial site-success, MAE 0.3263 vs. 0.400) and 15.85% (rel-avito ad-ctr, MAE 0.0345 vs. 0.041) over the RDL HeteroGNN baseline. Using a ±1% threshold, RELGT shows clear improvements on 10 tasks, comparable results on 9, and slight degradation on 2 (rel-stack user-badge at −3.94% AUC, rel-event user-repeat at −1.04%). The largest classification gain is 10.56% on rel-f1 driver-top3 (AUC 0.8352 vs. 0.7554).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the RDL HeteroGNN baseline (Robinson et al., 2024), RELGT wins on 10 of 21 tasks with >1% relative gain, ties on 9, and loses on 2 (rel-stack user-badge −3.94%, rel-event user-repeat −1.04%), with the largest wins on rel-trial site-success (+18.43% MAE) and rel-avito ad-ctr (+15.85% MAE). The Heterogeneous Graph Transformer (HGT, Hu et al., 2020) baseline underperforms the GNN baseline on most tasks, and even HGT+PE (with Laplacian positional encodings) only improves on 8 of 21 tasks while incurring 1.8×–8.62× per-epoch runtime overhead from PE precomputation. RELGT outperforms both HGT variants on nearly all tasks without any such precomputation cost. In a separate comparison with Griffin (Wang et al., 2025), a GNN-based relational foundation model, RELGT wins on 8 of 10 entity classification tasks with gains up to 8.41% (rel-avito user-clicks: 0.6830 vs. 0.630 AUC), confirming the advantage of a Transformer backbone over GNN message passing for relational entity graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

RELGT extends the local-global Graph Transformer paradigm (GraphGPS, GOAT) to the relational entity graph setting by introducing a multi-element tokenization that decomposes node representations into features, type, hop distance, time, and a lightweight GNN-based positional encoding. It builds on the RelBench/RDL pipeline (Robinson et al., 2024) and PyTorch Frame's multi-modal encoders, while departing from prior heterogeneous GTs (HGT) and temporal graph transformers (HTGformer) that handle heterogeneity and temporality through separate iterative modules without unified structural encodings. The subgraph GNN PE with stochastic random initialization draws on Sato et al. (2021) and Kanatsoulis et al. (2025), but repurposes them as a per-token structural element rather than a global precomputed encoding, avoiding the scalability bottleneck of Laplacian or random-walk PEs on multi-million-node relational databases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first Graph Transformer architecture purpose-built for relational entity graphs, filling a clear gap between GNNs (limited long-range expressiveness) and existing GTs (incompatible with heterogeneity, temporality, and scale in relational data). The multi-element tokenization design is a clean, modular recipe that practitioners can adapt, and the empirical finding that existing GTs like HGT actually underperform GNN baselines on relational data underscores the non-triviality of the adaptation. Open questions include task-specific tuning of individual token elements (the ablations show mixed results for type, hop, and time encodings), more expressive temporal encodings, and whether the architecture scales to foundation-model regimes for relational data as the authors envision.
