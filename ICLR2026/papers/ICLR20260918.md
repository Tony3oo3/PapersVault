## Problem

Existing multivariate time series anomaly detection (MTAD) methods rely on marginal distribution shift to flag anomalies, but 'unknown unknowns' (U2s) in autonomous systems—such as unmonitored hardware failures, stealthy software parameter changes, or untested usage scenarios—alter the underlying dynamical dependency structure among sensors without shifting marginal distributions, rendering standard MTAD approaches fundamentally ill-suited. The paper further demonstrates that SOTA MTAD results are heavily inflated by point adjustment (PA) and threshold learning with test-data leakage (TL), and that without these, even untrained deterministic methods can match or surpass learned baselines, leaving the true capability of existing methods for U2 detection unclear.

## Input / Output

SPIE-AD consumes sliding windows of n-dimensional sensor time series (e.g., 3–55 variables at 20–120 Hz sampling) and produces a binary U2/anomaly label per window along with the recovered sparse nonlinear dynamical model coefficients ω. The method is zero-shot: training data contain only normal and OOD anomalous segments (no U2 examples, no labels), and the CRIE validation phase uses a subset of training data rather than a separate labeled validation set. A key operational constraint is the window size W, which must be large enough for SINDY-MPC to recover stable models but small enough to localize events; the paper shows performance is sensitive to this choice and that anomalies shorter than ~5 samples are consistently missed.

## Method

SPIE-AD detects U2s by recovering and monitoring the underlying sparse nonlinear dynamical model rather than sensor value distributions. In the training phase, SINDY-MPC (optionally refined by a Liquid Time-Constant Neural Network with automatic differentiation for noise robustness at low sampling rates) mines sparse model coefficients ω from each training window, forming a reference set Ω. The CRIE algorithm then applies conformal inference to Ω to compute a distribution-free robustness interval [σ−d, σ+d] at a chosen miscoverage level α, characterizing the most likely normal operating model without requiring any anomaly labels. During evaluation, models are continuously re-mined from test windows, their robustness ρ(ω_i, Ω) is computed, and a window is flagged as U2 if its robustness falls outside the interval. The core insight is that U2s preserve marginal sensor distributions but induce non-stationary changes in inter-variable functional relationships, so conformance to the recovered dynamical model—not statistical deviation of raw values—is the correct detection signal.

## Results

On the six synthetic U2 benchmarks (no distribution shift between normal and anomalous data), SPIE-ADL achieves F1 scores of 92.0 (F8Stuck), 70.0 (F8Slow), 94.2 (UAVSimG), 96.5 (UAVEMA), 95.4 (AIDPhantom), and 91.5 (AIDCartridge) without point adjustment (PA) or threshold learning (TL). On real-world U2 datasets, SPIE-ADL reaches F1 70.0 (Medtronic) and 69.0 (Epilepsy), substantially outperforming the best baseline (OFA at 57.9 and 45.6 respectively). On the 250-dataset UCR univariate benchmark, SPIE-ADS achieves 75.6% event-wise accuracy, exceeding the prior SOTA by 4.8%. Critically, when PA and TL are removed, most SOTA baselines collapse (e.g., AT drops from F1 88 to 80.3 on F8Stuck; GANF drops from 98.5 to 1.8 on UAVEMA), while SPIE-AD remains robust.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPIE-AD outperforms all 12 baselines on every U2 benchmark without PA, with the largest margins against GANF (e.g., F1 96.5 vs. 1.8 on UAVEMA) and OFA (F1 95.4 vs. 23.1 on AIDPhantom). Among the strongest baselines, Anomaly Transformer (AT) achieves F1 80.3 on F8Stuck and 33.5 on F8Slow without PA, while SPIE-ADL reaches 92.0 and 70.0 respectively; GANF is competitive only on UAVSimG (64.3) but collapses on all other U2 datasets. The only other zero-shot method, USAD, achieves F1 74.0 on F8Stuck but drops to 15.2 on AIDCartridge, whereas SPIE-ADL maintains 91.5+. On real-world MTAD datasets (SMD, SMAP, MSL), SPIE-ADL achieves F1 86/73/83 without PA or validation data, while AT drops to 0 and GANF to 3.4/1.1/0 under the same zero-shot constraint. On univariate UCR, SPIE-ADS (75.6%) beats TranAD (19%), OFA (50%), and FITS (47%), confirming generality beyond U2-specific scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPIE-AD synthesizes three previously separate research threads—sparse nonlinear dynamical system identification (SINDY-MPC), continuous-time neural network refinement (LTC-NN), and distribution-free conformal inference—into a unified zero-shot detection pipeline. It departs fundamentally from the dominant MTAD paradigm (autoencoders, transformers, graph networks) by shifting the detection target from marginal sensor distributions to the recovered governing model's coefficient space, arguing that U2 events preserve marginals while altering inter-variable dynamics. The only prior zero-shot MTAD method, USAD, still relies on distribution-shift cues and standard threshold learning, whereas SPIE-AD's CRIE algorithm eliminates both dependencies. The paper also positions itself against the LLM/VLM-based zero-shot anomaly detection line by explicitly targeting resource-constrained autonomous deployments where image transformation and large-scale compute are unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most consequential contribution is methodological: by demonstrating that an untrained deterministic algorithm (AnomalySimpleton) can match or exceed SOTA MTAD scores when point adjustment and threshold learning with data leakage are permitted, it exposes a systemic evaluation flaw that has likely inflated reported progress across the subfield. The eight new U2 benchmarks (spanning quadcopter, F8 aircraft, and insulin delivery systems) fill a critical gap, as existing anomaly datasets exhibit >90% marginal distribution shift and are therefore unrepresentative of true unknown-unknowns. Open questions include formal characterization of window-size sensitivity (acknowledged as a limitation), scalability to very high-dimensional systems beyond 55 sensors, and whether the conformal-inference guarantee degrades under adversarial or multi-modal U2 scenarios. The framework also opens applications beyond safety monitoring, such as detecting stealthy model manipulation or biometric liveness spoofing.
