## Problem

Self-supervised representation learning models pretrained on natural images perform poorly on medical ultrasound tasks because ultrasound exhibits fundamentally different image attributes—speckle noise, limited grayscale range, and anatomy-specific structural patterns—that are absent in natural images. Moreover, the scarcity of large-scale annotated ultrasound datasets and the substantial anatomical heterogeneity across organs (e.g., globally distributed cardiac features vs. locally confined breast lesions) make it difficult to learn generalizable, anatomy-aware representations with existing SSL frameworks.

## Input / Output

The model takes a B-mode ultrasound image (resolutions ranging from 64×64 to 1280×960, resized to 224×224 for processing) and a one-hot anatomy category label (16 classes: Breast, Cardiac, Thyroid, Lung, Gallbladder, etc.) as input. During pretraining, it produces patch-level feature representations; for downstream evaluation, these features feed into a linear probe or fine-tuned head to yield class labels (binary or multi-class) or, with a UPerNet decoder, pixel-wise segmentation masks for left ventricle delineation.

## Method

A-ViT extends ViT-B/16 with an Anatomy-Conditioned Deformable Transformer (ACDT) block: a 16-dim one-hot anatomy vector is projected to a context embedding added to 2D patch tokens, which then drive learned kernel offsets for a deformable convolution that adaptively reshapes the receptive field per anatomical region. The deformed features serve as key/value in a cross-attention where the original patches act as query, followed by a standard FFN with residual connections. Training combines three objectives—masked image modeling (MAE-style) for local structure, an adversarial GAN loss to preserve diagnostically critical high-frequency speckle patterns, and DINO-style self-distillation for global semantic alignment—with an adaptive gradient-norm ratio λ that dynamically balances the MIM and adversarial terms. The framework is pretrained on a newly curated 5.2M-image ultrasound dataset spanning 16 anatomical categories from 11 public datasets and 15 medical institutions across three countries.

## Results

Across six downstream ultrasound tasks, A-ViT achieves the best performance on every metric. On breast cancer classification (fine-tuning), it reaches 93.66% accuracy and 0.9742 AUROC, surpassing the strongest baselines SigLIP2 (89.34%) and USFM (88.73%). On the multi-task benchmark (Table 4), A-ViT attains 92.16 Dice / 85.67 mIoU for cardiac LV segmentation, 91.80% Top-1 for 15-view cardiac classification, 87.07% accuracy for thyroid cancer, 91.44% for COVID-19 lung US, and 89.89% for gallbladder tumor identification — all leading their respective baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A-ViT outperforms all eight baselines on every downstream task. The largest margins appear on COVID-19 lung US (91.44% vs. USFM's 87.67%, +3.77%) and gallbladder tumor classification (89.89% vs. USFM's 86.64%, +3.25%), where anatomy-specific deformable attention captures organ-unique spatial patterns. On breast cancer (Table 2, fine-tuning), A-ViT leads SigLIP2 by 4.32% accuracy and 0.039 AUROC, and USFM by 4.93% accuracy. Among natural-image SSL baselines, DINO v3 is the strongest on thyroid (86.24% vs. A-ViT's 87.07%) and cardiac view Top-1 (89.93% vs. 91.80%), while MAE and LVM-Med trail by 4–9% accuracy across most tasks, confirming the domain gap between natural-image and ultrasound pretraining. US-specific baselines DMAE and USFM are closer competitors but still fall 1–5% short, indicating that anatomy-conditioned deformable attention provides a consistent advantage over generic ultrasound SSL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ViT backbone with an anatomy-conditioned deformable transformer (ACDT) that conditions patch-level feature extraction on a 16-way anatomical one-hot embedding, departing from prior anatomy-aware SSL work (Jiao 2020, Fu 2022, Hu 2022) that was restricted to a single organ domain. The learning objective unifies three complementary signals—MAE-style masked image modeling, a GAN-inspired adversarial loss for speckle preservation, and DINOv3-style self-distillation—into a single framework with gradient-magnitude-based adaptive weighting. The 5.2M-image multi-institution ultrasound dataset positions this work as the first large-scale, multi-organ US pretraining corpus, analogous to how ImageNet enabled NI SSL but at a scale previously unavailable for ultrasound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5.2M-image dataset and the anatomy-conditioned A-ViT provide the ultrasound community with a practical foundation model that consistently outperforms both NI-pretrained and US-specific SSL baselines across six heterogeneous tasks (breast, thyroid, gallbladder, cardiac view, cardiac segmentation, lung), suggesting a path toward a unified US diagnostic backbone. Open questions include whether the 16-category anatomy conditioning generalizes to unseen organs, whether the approach extends to 3D or video ultrasound, and whether the modest parameter overhead (86M→95M) remains acceptable at larger ViT scales. The paper does not report statistical significance tests or multi-seed variance, leaving some uncertainty about the robustness of the reported margins.
