## Problem

Despite the central role of global context in visual perception, no rigorous mathematical definition of 'global image modeling' exists, leaving the concept to heuristic evaluation. Existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: self-attention achieves global modeling at quadratic cost, while SSMs offer linear complexity but their recursive, causal state-update mechanism causes exponential decay of long-range influence, making them structurally incompatible with the non-causal, unstructured nature of image data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (remote sensing tiles resized to 1024×1024 for segmentation; 640×960 for COCO detection/instance segmentation) and produces pixel-wise class maps, bounding boxes with class labels, or per-instance masks depending on the downstream head. GMamba is a plug-and-play block inserted via residual connections at arbitrary stages of existing CNN or Transformer backbones (UNet-ResNet34, UNet-Swin-T, UNet-ConvNeXt-S, Faster R-CNN, Mask R-CNN), requiring no architectural redesign of the host network.

## Method

The paper first formalizes global image modeling as a gradient condition: the Frobenius norm of the output's derivative with respect to every input pixel must be bounded below by a strictly positive constant, independent of spatial location. It then proves that the 2D-DFT inherently satisfies this definition because each frequency coefficient depends on all spatial positions with uniform magnitude, and establishes that DFT-based pre-modulation can endow SSMs with true global perception by injecting global semantics before the recursive state update. Concretely, the GSSM module applies a learnable 2D-DFT (Frequency Encoding Module) to extract and recalibrate high/low-frequency components, then uses adaptive modulation weights (Frequency-Guided Modulation Module) to fuse these global frequency features with the original spatial features before feeding the result into a standard selective SSM. This yields linear-logarithmic complexity O(M log M + MDN), avoids multi-directional scanning strategies, and is formally proven to satisfy both the global gradient definition and the non-sequential constraint.

## Results

On Vaihingen semantic segmentation with UNet-ResNet34, GMamba achieves 84.74% mIoU (↑3.09 over baseline), outperforming the best competing global module (Swin at 83.24%) by 1.50 points while using fewer parameters (30.96M vs. 35.81M). On the stronger ConvNeXt(S) backbone, GMamba reaches 86.00% mIoU, surpassing all existing methods including AFENet (84.55%). On MS-COCO, GMamba achieves 38.5% AP for object detection (Faster R-CNN, ResNet50) and 39.8% AP for instance segmentation (Mask R-CNN, Swin(T)), consistently outperforming all compared modules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMamba outperforms all 10 compared global modeling modules across every backbone and dataset. On Vaihingen with ResNet34, GMamba (84.74% mIoU) beats the closest competitors Swin and VMamba (both 83.24%) by 1.50 points, while using 14% fewer parameters than Swin. On the Swin(T) backbone, GMamba (84.83%) surpasses VMamba (84.07%) by 0.76 points; on ConvNeXt(S), it leads Swin (84.82%) by 1.18 points. On MS-COCO object detection with ResNet50, GMamba achieves 38.5% AP versus Swin's 38.0% and VMamba's 37.6%; for instance segmentation with Swin(T), GMamba reaches 39.8% AP, edging out FreqMamba (39.2%) and Group Mamba (39.1%). Notably, GMamba achieves these gains with linear-logarithmic complexity (O(M·D·N + M·log M)), substantially lower than the quadratic O(M²D) cost of attention-based modules like Swin and SwinV2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mamba/selective SSM line of work by introducing a 2D-DFT-based frequency-domain pre-modulation mechanism (GSSM) that injects global information into the SSM's input before the recursive state update, rather than modifying the scanning strategy itself. It departs from prior SSM-vision methods (Vim, VMamba, Spatial-Mamba, GroupMamba) that attempt to compensate for SSMs' causal, sequential nature through bidirectional or multi-directional scanning, arguing instead that the fundamental limitation is the lack of explicit global coupling at the input level. The paper also introduces a formal mathematical definition of 'global image modeling' via gradient lower bounds, positioning itself against the heuristic/empirical treatment of globality in prior Transformer and SSM literature, and distinguishes its front-end frequency modulation from post-hoc frequency fusion approaches like FreqMamba and SPANet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play GMamba module offers a practical, efficient (linear-logarithmic complexity) alternative to both quadratic-complexity attention and multi-directional SSM scanning, with consistent gains across segmentation, detection, and instance segmentation on multiple backbones. The formal definition of global image modeling, while potentially controversial, provides a useful analytical lens for evaluating whether a module truly captures global context versus merely having a large receptive field. Open questions include whether the gradient-based definition generalizes to non-differentiable or discrete architectures, whether the DFT-based global coupling remains effective at very high resolutions where frequency components become extremely fine-grained, and whether the theoretical guarantee of 'global perception' translates to meaningful gains on tasks where local context dominates (e.g., fine-grained classification).
