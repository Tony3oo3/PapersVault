## Problem

Functional data registration (removing phase variability via time warping) and classification are traditionally addressed as separate, sequential tasks, yet labels influence curve progression pace and alignment provides discriminative structure for prediction. No prior deep learning framework jointly learns diffeomorphic warping functions and a classifier in a single end-to-end architecture, and no theoretical guarantees exist for such a coupled model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260649/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes functional trajectories x_i(t_i) sampled at irregular, potentially misaligned time points (variable sequence length n per sample), optionally multi-dimensional (d≥2 channels sharing a common warping), along with class labels y_i during training. It produces a boundary-preserving diffeomorphic warping function γ_i(t) (constructed via two-step monotone cumulative sum of squared CNN features), the aligned curve, K=100 Fourier spectral coefficients, and a softmax class prediction. The warping is guaranteed C¹ with positive derivative by the cumulative-sum construction, and the spectral embedding avoids vectorizing onto a fixed grid.

## Method

DeepFRC couples three modules in a single differentiable pipeline: (1) a 4-block 1D CNN (kernel 3, channels 16→32→64) whose squared outputs are mapped through a monotone cumulative sum to yield a valid diffeomorphic time warp; (2) a Fourier basis expansion (K=100) of the linearly-interpolated aligned curve, providing a compact smooth embedding; and (3) a 3-layer MLP classifier (hidden units 8, 4) with softmax output. Training minimizes a joint objective combining a class-aware contrastive geometric alignment loss—computed in SRVF space to encourage intra-class coherence and inter-class separation—weighted by α, with a standard cross-entropy classification loss weighted by β, optimized end-to-end via AdamW. The key distinction from prior work is that class labels directly shape the warping through the contrastive term, while the classifier benefits from label-informed alignment, creating mutual reinforcement absent in sequential pipelines. The paper provides the first approximation theorem (neural nets can realize near-optimal SRVF warpings) and a data-dependent generalization bound linking registration fidelity to classification error.

## Results

DeepFRC achieves the best alignment quality (lowest ATV) across all five real-world datasets, with values ranging from 3.2 (Symbol 3-class) to 25.0 (MotionSense), substantially outperforming TTN (4.5–57.7) and all SrvfRegNet pipelines (6.5–136.0). On classification, DeepFRC reaches 96.4% ACC / 0.965 F1 on Wave, 89.8% / 0.909 on Yoga, 96.0% / 0.959 on Symbol (2-class), 96.3% / 0.963 on Symbol (3-class), and 95.0% / 0.952 on MotionSense, matching or exceeding the strongest sequential pipeline (SrvfRegNet+TSLANet) on every dataset. On the simulated two-class dataset, DeepFRC attains near-perfect test performance (ACC = 100%, F1 = 1.000, ρ = 0.976, ΔQ_reg = 0.056).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepFRC dominates on alignment quality: its ATV is 1.7–120× lower than every baseline across all datasets (e.g., 16.2 vs. 136.0 on Yoga, 25.0 vs. 37.7 on MotionSense), confirming that class-aware joint registration produces far superior warping than either unsupervised SrvfRegNet or the discrete TTN. Against TTN (the only other joint method), DeepFRC wins on both ATV and classification on every dataset, with the largest gap on MotionSense (95.0% vs. 85.0% ACC; 25.0 vs. 35.1 ATV) and Symbol 3-class (96.3% vs. 93.3% ACC). Among the five SrvfRegNet+classifier pipelines, DeepFRC matches the strongest (SrvfRegNet+TSLANet) on Wave (96.4% ACC) and MotionSense (95.0% ACC) while achieving dramatically better alignment (ATV 5.6 vs. 7.3; 25.0 vs. 37.7), and it outperforms it on Yoga (89.8% vs. 89.3% ACC) and Symbol 3-class (F1 0.963 vs. 0.960). The weaker pipelines (FCNN_raw, FuncNN, ADAFNN) trail DeepFRC by 1–16 percentage points in ACC depending on dataset, with the largest deficit on Yoga where ADAFNN drops to 73.4% ACC. Overall, DeepFRC is the only method that simultaneously achieves best-in-class alignment and top-tier classification, validating the synergy between its contrastive geometric loss and the spectral classifier.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepFRC builds directly on the SrvfRegNet architecture (Chen & Srivastava, 2021) for diffeomorphic warping via 1D CNNs and the SRVF-based EFDA geometric framework (Srivastava et al., 2011), but departs fundamentally by coupling registration and classification into a single end-to-end trainable pipeline rather than treating them as sequential stages. The closest prior joint formulations—TTN (Lohit et al., 2019) for discrete time series and Tang et al. (2022) for parametric mixed-effects models—either ignore functional smoothness or rely on alternating optimization with distributional assumptions. The key conceptual departure is the class-aware contrastive geometric loss that simultaneously enforces intra-class SRVF coherence and inter-class separation, plus the first theoretical guarantees (approximation and generalization bounds) for such a joint deep model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first theoretically grounded end-to-end deep learning framework for joint functional registration and classification, with linear-time complexity that makes it practical for long sequences where DTW-based methods become infeasible. The demonstrated mutual reinforcement between alignment and classification (removing either component significantly degrades the other, confirmed by paired t-tests) validates the joint-learning paradigm for FDA. Key limitations include reliance on smooth diffeomorphic warping (degrading on discontinuous trajectories), sensitivity to label noise, and the open question of whether the empirical basis-function invariance has a theoretical explanation. Future directions include adaptive architectures for non-smooth functions and robust loss designs for noisy label settings.
