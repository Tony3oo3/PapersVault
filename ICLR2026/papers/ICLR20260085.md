## Problem

Unsupervised RL pre-training relies on simple Gaussian or discrete skill-based policies that cannot capture the heterogeneous, nonstationary state distributions accumulated in the replay buffer, limiting both exploration coverage and downstream adaptation. Additionally, fine-tuning diffusion policies online remains unstable due to multi-step sampling and the lack of closed-form probability computation, making it difficult to leverage expressive generative models for few-shot task transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260085/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes continuous state observations (e.g., R² in Maze2d, higher-dimensional in URLB) and state-action pairs from the replay buffer to produce a score-based intrinsic reward R_score(s) that guides a lightweight Gaussian behavior policy. During fine-tuning, it takes the pre-trained diffusion policy and a small number of online interactions (100K steps, ~5% of pre-training) with extrinsic task rewards to produce adapted continuous actions. The diffusion model operates on VP-SDE schedules with 3-layer MLP ResNet blocks (hidden_dim=256), and actions are sampled via DPM-Solver in 15 steps.

## Method

ExDM trains a diffusion model on the heterogeneous replay buffer to obtain accurate state density estimates, then defines a score-based intrinsic reward R_score(s) = E_{ε,t}[||ε_θ'(s|t) − ε||²] (an ELBO-based proxy for −log p(s)) that explicitly targets under-visited regions. To avoid the computational cost of multi-step diffusion sampling during exploration, ExDM decouples modeling from acting: the diffusion model provides density estimates while a Gaussian behavior policy π_g, trained with any off-policy RL algorithm on R_score, handles efficient action selection. For downstream fine-tuning, ExDM formulates an objective J_f(π) combining cumulative return with KL regularization to the pre-trained diffusion policy, and solves it via alternating optimization: IQL updates the Q function, contrastive energy prediction (CEP) computes timestep-wise energy guidance, and score distillation produces the fine-tuned diffusion policy ε_ψ. Theorem 4.2 guarantees monotone policy improvement and convergence to the optimal policy of J_f.

## Results

On Maze2d, ExDM achieves state coverage of 0.99, 0.99, 0.98, 0.78, 0.91, 0.75, and 0.71 across the seven mazes, outperforming the best baseline (MEPOL) by up to 51% in the most challenging mazes (Square-bottleneck: 0.75 vs 0.62; Square-large: 0.71 vs 0.59). ExDM reaches comparable coverage to MEPOL on simpler mazes (Square-a: 0.99 vs 0.98) while using only 37% of the timesteps on the hardest mazes. On URLB, ExDM significantly outperforms all 12+ URL baselines in both single-embodiment and cross-embodiment fine-tuning (Fig. 3a–b), and substantially outperforms diffusion fine-tuning baselines (DQL, IDQL, QSM, DIPO) in the diffusion policy setting (Fig. 3c).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExDM wins on all seven Maze2d mazes in state coverage, with the largest margins in structurally complex environments: Square-bottleneck (0.75 vs MEPOL's 0.62, a 21% absolute gain) and Square-large (0.71 vs MEPOL's 0.59, a 20% gain), where baselines stall at wall corners and fail to cover the full maze. On simpler mazes (Square-a, Square-b), ExDM matches or slightly exceeds MEPOL (0.99 vs 0.98/0.99), while exploration baselines like ICM (0.58) and RND (0.50) and skill-discovery baselines like DIAYN (0.41) and SMM (0.47) lag by 40–60% in coverage. On URLB single-embodiment fine-tuning (Fig. 3a), ExDM outperforms all 12 URL baselines (ICM, RND, Disagreement, RE3, MEPOL, LBS, DIAYN, SMM, LSD, CIC, BeCL, CeSD) across mean, median, IQM, and optimality gap metrics. In the cross-embodiment setting (Fig. 3b), ExDM also beats PEAC and all other baselines. For diffusion policy fine-tuning (Fig. 3c), ExDM substantially outperforms DQL, IDQL, QSM, and DIPO, though its diffusion-policy performance remains below its own Gaussian-policy performance, which the authors attribute to limited fine-tuning timesteps.

## Relation to Prior Work

ExDM sits at the intersection of unsupervised RL (URL) and diffusion-based policy learning. Prior URL methods (ICM, RND, DIAYN, CIC, etc.) rely on simple Gaussian or discrete skill-based policies that cannot capture the heterogeneous, nonstationary distributions in replay buffers; ExDM is the first to use a diffusion model's score function as a density-based intrinsic reward for exploration. For fine-tuning, it builds on soft RL's energy-based policy framework (Haarnoja et al., 2017) and adapts contrastive energy prediction (CEP, Lu et al., 2023) and implicit Q-learning (IQL, Kostrikov et al., 2022) to the online diffusion-policy setting, deriving a novel alternating optimization with convergence guarantees. A key practical departure is the decoupling of diffusion-based modeling from Gaussian-based acting, avoiding the multi-step sampling bottleneck that plagues online diffusion policy training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ExDM establishes that diffusion models can serve as effective density estimators for exploration in reward-free settings, opening a path toward richer generative priors in URL beyond hand-crafted intrinsic rewards. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 4.2) providing policy-improvement and optimality guarantees for diffusion fine-tuning under limited interaction is a useful contribution to a still-maturing area. However, the paper acknowledges that diffusion policy fine-tuning still underperforms the simpler Gaussian policy fine-tuning, and experiments are confined to 2D mazes and DMC continuous control; scaling to high-dimensional visual or real-robot settings remains open. The ELBO-based approximation of the score reward and the reliance on a fixed β for regularization also leave room for more adaptive formulations.
