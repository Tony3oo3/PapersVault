## Problem

Graph neural networks have shown strong performance at identifying ineffective node-level transformations in Logic Optimization heuristics, but their high inference cost and lack of interpretability severely limit deployment in CPU-based industrial EDA tools. No prior method learns a scoring function that is simultaneously lightweight, interpretable, and high-performance for circuit graph node classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263186/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a circuit subgraph rooted at a target node, represented as a 2-layer computation tree where each node carries a 5-dimensional structural feature vector (fanin count, fanout count, etc.). It produces a hierarchical symbolic tree—a set of closed-form mathematical expressions organized in layers using operators {+,-,×,÷,log,exp}, constants {0.1, 0.2, 0.5}, and aggregation operators {min, max, mean, sum}—that evaluates to a scalar effectiveness score per node. The symbolic tree is fully deployable as a pure arithmetic expression with no learned parameters at inference time.

## Method

HIS represents the scoring function as a hierarchical symbolic tree with L layers that emulates GNN message passing: each layer applies interpretable aggregation (min/max/mean/sum) and arithmetic operations to propagate structural information from leaf nodes toward the root. To learn this tree, the framework employs L encoder-only Transformer policies (one per layer) equipped with a tree-aware embedding mechanism that explicitly encodes parent and sibling token relationships during sequence generation. Training uses Proximal Policy Optimization with a group-based advantage (normalizing rewards within a batch of m sampled expressions) and focal loss as the terminal reward, eliminating the need for a separate critic network. A Best-of-N ensemble (N=4) over the highest-reward expressions further improves prediction recall. This design achieves 296× inference speedup over the GNN-based COG baseline while matching or exceeding its prediction accuracy.

## Results

On the top-50% prediction recall metric (offline evaluation), HIS achieves 0.82–0.99 across six challenging circuits from EPFL and IWLS benchmarks, consistently outperforming all baselines. In the online evaluation, HIS-Mfs2 (k=50%) achieves an average 40.27% runtime reduction over Default Mfs2 with only 0.38% degradation in circuit size/level, while 2HIS-Mfs2 (k=30%) delivers a 27.22% average runtime improvement and 6.95% circuit size reduction. Inference efficiency is dramatically improved: HIS achieves a 296× speedup over the GNN-based COG on EPFL and 254× on IWLS under a pure CPU environment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIS outperforms the GNN-based COG (Wang et al., ICML 2024) on 4 of 6 circuits in prediction recall (e.g., 0.99 vs 0.55 on Ethernet, 0.94 vs 0.82 on Multiplier), though COG edges ahead on Hyp (0.87 vs 0.82) and ties on Conmax (0.75). Against CMO (Bai et al., ICLR 2025), the graph-enhanced symbolic method, HIS wins on 4 circuits and ties on Square (0.94), with the largest gap on Ethernet (0.99 vs 0.59). The hand-designed Effisyn (Li et al., ICCAD 2023) is dramatically outperformed by HIS on 5 of 6 circuits (e.g., 0.94 vs 0.04 on Square), though Effisyn narrowly wins on Ethernet (0.88 vs 0.99 is actually HIS winning; Effisyn's 0.88 is still far below HIS's 0.99). In online runtime, HIS-Mfs2 reduces execution time by 11.96%–22.91% over CMO-Mfs2, Effisyn-Mfs2, Random-Mfs2, and COG-Mfs2 respectively, while maintaining or improving optimization performance (And Reduction). Overall, HIS is the only method that simultaneously achieves high prediction recall, fast inference (296× faster than COG), and interpretable symbolic output.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the pruning framework for LO heuristics, where GNN-based scorers (COG) or hand-designed functions (Effisyn) identify ineffective node-level transformations, and (2) symbolic discovery from neural networks, where interpretable expressions approximate learned mappings. HIS departs from prior symbolic distillation work (Cranmer et al., Kuang et al.) by learning hierarchical symbolic trees end-to-end via RL without requiring process labels, and from GNN-based scorers by replacing the neural network with a lightweight, interpretable tree-structured function that mirrors GNN message passing. The structure-aware Transformer with parent/sibling token encoding is adapted from Kuang et al.'s bipartite-graph symbolic policy, but reorganized into a multi-layer hierarchy that directly operates on circuit computation trees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a 296× inference speedup over the SOTA GNN scorer (COG) while matching or exceeding its prediction recall makes ML-guided pruning viable in pure-CPU industrial LO flows where GPU inference is unavailable. The interpretability of the discovered symbolic trees also addresses a key adoption barrier in EDA, where engineers need to understand why a node is pruned. Open questions include whether the fixed 2-layer depth generalizes to circuits with deeper structural dependencies, whether the approach extends to other EDA tasks beyond LO, and how performance scales as circuit complexity grows beyond the ~200K-node EPFL circuits tested here.
