## Problem

Hippocampal place cells fire in theta sequences, but the mechanistic origin of these sequences remains debated: are they driven by sequential sensory input along a trajectory, or can they arise from intrinsic recurrent circuitry? This paper proposes a parsimonious account in which CA3 recurrent connectivity alone propagates transient sparse input over long horizons, and tests whether such a sequence-generating reservoir is sufficient to support egocentric visual navigation and give rise to hippocampus-like spatial representations.

## Input / Output

The agent receives first-person RGB frames from a 19×19-tile DeepMind Lab maze with uniform textures and walls on 15% of tiles. A pretrained ResNet encoder (3 conv blocks, 16–32 channels) extracts visual features that are linearly projected to F=16 dimensions and sparsified via batch normalization and a high threshold (τ=2.43) to match the ~2.5% firing rate of DG granule cells. A separate depth signal (mean depth downsampled to 10 horizontal pixels) bypasses the recurrent core and feeds directly into the decoder for collision avoidance. The network outputs a 5-way discrete navigation action (forward, strafe left/right, look-left+forward, look-right+forward) and a scalar value estimate.

## Method

The architecture pairs a fixed CA3-like shift-register reservoir with a trainable DG sparsification module and a 2-layer MLP decoder, trained end-to-end with APPO (V-trace actor-critic). Each of the 16 sparse DG features drives an independent sequence of length ℓ = L + R − 1 (L=64 theta cycles, R=8 active units per cycle, ℓ=71), implemented as a block-diagonal linear RNN X_{t+1} = AX_t + Bu_t where A = I_F ⊗ S shifts activity one slot per timestep and B = I_F ⊗ J injects input into the first R slots; the CA3 weights are frozen to isolate intrinsic sequence dynamics from recurrent plasticity. Only the DG linear projection (with batch norm and threshold) and the decoder MLP (1136+10 → 128 → 128) are learned. The key empirical finding is a regime-dependent synergy: under sparse input the CA3 shift register outperforms a parameter-matched LSTM and HiPPO SSM baselines, while under dense input (no sparsification) the LSTM wins, revealing that sparse coding and intrinsic sequence generation are complementary inductive biases for navigation.

## Results

Under sparse DG input (~2.5% activity, 16 channels), the CA3 sequence generator achieves 86.0±10.0% success rate and reaches 80% success in 173.6±77.6M frames, while all baselines (Random RNN, HiPPO-LegS/LegT/LagT, LSTM) fail to reach 80% success within 350M frames, peaking at only 51–65% success. Under dense input (no sparsification), the advantage reverses: a trainable LSTM reaches 93.0±9.0% success in 135.9±27.6M frames, outperforming CA3 (71.0±7.0%) and all SSM variants. Transfer to a new reward location requires only ~50M additional frames, and transfer to a novel map requires ~150M frames, indicating generalizable spatial representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the CA3 shift-register sequence generator against four baseline recurrent architectures: a randomly initialized fixed RNN, three HiPPO state-space model variants (LegS, LegT, LagT from Gu et al., 2020), and a trainable LSTM with matched parameter count. Under sparse input, CA3 decisively outperforms all baselines, achieving 86% success versus 51–65% for the others, and is the only architecture to reach 80% success within the training budget. Under dense input, the picture inverts: the LSTM achieves 93% success and is the only method to reach 80% success, while CA3 drops to 71% and underperforms even the random RNN (78%) and HiPPO-LegS (64%). This regime-dependent interaction is the paper's central empirical finding: sparse coding synergizes with intrinsic sequence dynamics, whereas dense input favors the mixing-oriented recurrent dynamics of LSTMs. The HiPPO variants and random RNN consistently underperform both CA3 (sparse) and LSTM (dense), confirming that the specific theta-sequence structure of CA3 provides a distinct inductive bias beyond generic recurrence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes ideas from hippocampal sequence modeling (reservoir models of Leibold 2020, successor representations of Stachenfeld et al. 2017) and structured state-space models (HiPPO of Gu et al. 2020, shift-diagonal architectures of Fu et al. 2022) into a minimal, biologically grounded architecture: a fixed shift-register CA3 proxy fed by a DG-like sparsification module. Unlike prior computational models of place cells that take allocentric input or assume sequential sensory drive, this work explicitly tests the hypothesis that intrinsic recurrent dynamics alone can generate theta sequences and support navigation from sparse egocentric visual input. It departs from the standard DeepMind Lab agent design (Espeholt et al. 2018; Hessel et al. 2019) by replacing the trainable LSTM core with a fixed, prewired sequence generator, thereby isolating the computational contribution of the sequence structure itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a parsimonious mechanistic account of hippocampal theta sequences that is both biologically plausible and computationally effective, with the key insight that sparse input and intrinsic sequence dynamics synergize in a regime-dependent way. Limitations include the fixed (non-plastic) CA3 connectivity, the use of global gradient-based RL without clear biological analogs, and results demonstrated primarily in one environment class (though validated on 5 additional maps). Open questions include whether local Hebbian plasticity can replace the fixed dynamics, how the model interacts with path integration in entorhinal cortex, and whether the sparse-input advantage generalizes to more complex or larger-scale navigation tasks. For ML practitioners, the finding that a simple shift register outperforms an LSTM under sparse input offers a concrete inductive bias for low-bandwidth sensory regimes.
