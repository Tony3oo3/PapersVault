## Problem

Existing MLLM-based Earth observation research is confined to RGB perception, single-step reasoning, and static pretrained knowledge, unable to handle raw spectral data, multi-step quantitative analysis, or domain-specific tool integration. Prior agent-based EO attempts remain in their infancy, limited to perceptual tasks like change detection, lacking a predefined tool ecosystem, and evaluated only on final answers without assessing reasoning trajectories. The paper asks how to design an EO agent with a structured tool ecosystem and systematic dual-level evaluation capable of bridging perceptual and spectral data the way Earth scientists do.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a text task goal (query) plus EO data in three modalities: RGB imagery from public datasets, raw spectral rasters (Landsat 8/9, MODIS, ASTER) with multiple bands, and processed Earth products (LST, NDVI, GPM, sea ice concentration, etc.) sourced from Google Earth Engine and NASA EarthData. Each benchmark question involves an average of 55 images and 5.4 reasoning steps, with queries ranging from ~330 characters (Auto-Planning) to ~500 characters (Instruction-Following). The output is both a final answer (classification label, detection boxes, segmentation mask, retrieved geophysical parameter, or trend analysis) and a full reproducible tool-call trajectory, which is evaluated at two levels: end-to-end accuracy/efficiency and step-by-step trajectory fidelity against expert-annotated solutions.

## Method

Earth-Agent formulates the EO analysis pipeline as a ReAct-style POMDP where an LLM controller acts as the policy π, iteratively selecting actions from a toolkit of 104 specialized tools organized into five domain-specific kits (Index, Inversion, Perception, Analysis, Statistics) built on the Model Context Protocol for interoperability. Each ReAct loop proceeds through four stages: tool calling (invoking the most suitable tool conditioned on current memory and goal), memory update (appending the observation-action pair), thinking (LLM reasoning over updated history to plan the next step), and action (executing the next tool call), continuing until a stopping condition is met. The toolkit spans EO indices (NDVI, NDWI, NBR), geophysical parameter retrieval (LST, PWV, vegetation water content, sea ice concentration), vision tasks (classification, detection, segmentation via expert models), spatiotemporal analysis (trend detection, seasonality decomposition, change point analysis), and large-scale statistical computation. Evaluation uses a dual-level protocol: end-to-end metrics (Accuracy of final answer, Efficiency as ratio of predicted to expert trajectory length) and step-by-step metrics (Tool-Any-Order, Tool-In-Order, Tool-Exact-Match, and Parameter Accuracy) that measure trajectory fidelity against expert-annotated Python solutions. The companion Earth-Bench benchmark contains 248 expert-curated questions with 13,729 images across 14 task types, supporting both Auto-Planning (step-implicit) and Instruction-Following (step-explicit) query regimes.

## Results

On Earth-Bench (248 expert-curated EO tasks), Earth-Agent with GPT-5 achieves 65.99% accuracy under Auto-Planning and 62.35% under Instruction-Following, with an efficiency ratio of 2.36 (i.e., ~2.4× more tool calls than expert solutions). On Earth-Bench-Lite (60 questions), Earth-Agent(GPT-5) reaches 55.83% average accuracy across Spectrum, Products, and RGB modalities, substantially outperforming general agents like GPT-Agent (40.42%) and Manus (26.14%). On remote sensing benchmarks, Earth-Agent achieves 93.42% on AID classification, 96.12% on WHU-RS19, 60.88% mAP on DOTA detection, 65.60% on HRSC2016, and 60.46 on DIOR-RSVG grounding, surpassing all prior MLLM-based EO methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Earth-Agent decisively outperforms all eight baselines. Against general-purpose agents on Earth-Bench-Lite, Earth-Agent(GPT-5) achieves 55.83% average accuracy versus GPT-Agent/Operator (40.42%), Manus (26.14%), MGX (18.60%), and Coze (15.30%), with the gap most pronounced on Products tasks (36.84% vs. 10.50–15.80%) where domain-specific spatiotemporal tools are critical. Against remote sensing MLLMs, Earth-Agent surpasses the strongest prior method LHRS-Bot by 2.16 points on AID classification (93.42 vs. 91.26), 2.95 points on WHU-RS19 (96.12 vs. 93.17), 43.78 points on DOTA detection (60.88 vs. 17.1), 41.2 points on HRSC2016 (65.60 vs. 24.4), and 48.63 points on DIOR-RSVG grounding (60.46 vs. 11.83). VHM achieves competitive classification (91.70 on AID) but cannot handle detection or grounding at all. GeoChat, MiniGPT-v2, LLaVA-1.5, and Sphinx all trail Earth-Agent by 20+ points on classification and 40+ points on detection/grounding, confirming that tool-augmented multi-step reasoning with 104 specialized EO tools substantially exceeds the capabilities of single-step MLLM inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

Earth-Agent extends the ReAct (Yao et al., 2023) reasoning paradigm into Earth observation by formulating the agent loop as a POMDP and coupling it with a 104-tool MCP-based toolkit spanning five domain kits (Index, Inversion, Perception, Analysis, Statistics). It departs from MLLM-based EO work (GeoChat, RS-GPT, LHRS-Bot, VHM), which is confined to RGB perception and single-step inference, and from early EO agents (Change-Agent, RS-Agent, ThinkGeo, UnivEarth), which lack a structured tool ecosystem and systematic evaluation. The key conceptual departure is the unification of perceptual (RGB) and raw observational (Spectral, Products) data within a single agentic architecture, enabling cross-modal multi-step quantitative analysis that neither MLLMs nor prior EO agents support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Earth-Bench (248 expert-curated questions, 13,729 images, 14 task types) fills a critical gap: no prior EO benchmark required multi-step quantitative reasoning with explicit tool use and dual-level (trajectory + outcome) evaluation. The finding that even GPT-5 plateaus at ~66% accuracy, with a large gap between tool identification (Tool-Any-Order ~69%) and precise execution (Parameters ~26%), reveals that the bottleneck for EO agents is not tool availability but fine-grained parameter specification and trajectory fidelity. Open questions include whether longer tool chains (>15 calls) can be handled reliably, how to reduce hallucination-driven errors in open-source models, and whether more capable expert models in the Perception kit can close the RGB accuracy gap.
