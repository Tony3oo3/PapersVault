## Problem

The k-Hyperplane Clustering problem (k-HC2) asks for k hyperplanes minimizing the sum of squared Euclidean distances from m data points to their nearest hyperplane. While a mixed-integer quadratically-constrained quadratic programming (MI-QCQP) formulation exists, solving it to global optimality via spatial branch-and-bound (SBB) is prohibitively slow: the nonconvex 2-norm constraints ‖w_j‖₂ ≥ 1 keep the trivial zero solution feasible until an exponential number (2^{k(n−1)}) of branching nodes are generated, preventing any pruning. The paper asks whether the formulation can be strengthened so that nonzero lower bounds—and hence effective pruning—occur in a polynomial number of SBB nodes.

## Input / Output

The method takes m data points in R^n (assumed nonnegative after a translation preprocessing step) and an integer k specifying the number of hyperplanes, and produces k hyperplane parameter pairs (w_j, γ_j) along with binary assignment variables x_ij indicating which point is assigned to which hyperplane. The nonconvexity is confined to the norm constraints on w_j; the objective is the sum of squared distance variables d_i. The strengthened formulations introduce additional binary variables (s_jh for 1-norm sign encoding, u_jh for ∞-norm disjunction selection) and auxiliary continuous variables (w_jh⁺, w_jh⁻) to make the polyhedral norm constraints MILP-representable.

## Method

The core idea is to intersect the classical 2-norm MI-QCQP formulation with constraints from scaled polyhedral-norm (p=1, ∞) versions of k-HC, yielding a valid but tighter relaxation. The 1-norm constraint ‖w_j‖₁ ≥ 1 is encoded via an absolute-value MILP reformulation with binary sign variables, while the ∞-norm constraint ‖w_j‖∞ ≥ 1/√n is encoded as a disjunction over n half-spaces with binary selection variables. The key theoretical result (Proposition 4) shows that branching on the ∞-norm disjunction variables first yields a nonzero global lower bound after only k(n−1) SBB nodes—linear in k and n—versus the 2^{k(n−1)} nodes required by the pure 2-norm formulation. Norm congruence inequalities (Theorem 1) guarantee that each polyhedral-norm relaxation is within a 1/n approximation factor of the 2-norm optimum, and the combined multi-norm relaxation improves this to 1/√n. Experimentally, the ∞-norm-strengthened formulation reduces median solve times by up to 41× and increases the number of instances solved to global optimality by up to 63% on Gurobi 10's SBB.

## Results

On the LowDim testbed (43 instances, m=10–30, n=2–3, k=2–3), the best strengthened formulation (k-HC(2,1),(1,1/√n)) reduces median solve time from 169.9 s to 4.15 s (40.9× speedup), while (k-HC(2,1),(∞,1)) and (k-HC(2,1),(multi,1)) achieve 27.9× and 34.0× speedups respectively. On the HighDim testbed (43 instances, m=10–17, n=2–5, k=2–5), the corresponding speedups are 11.5×, 10.1×, and 5.6×. Across both testbeds, the strengthened formulations solve up to 21 additional LowDim instances and 10 additional HighDim instances that the classical formulation fails to solve within the 46-hour time limit, increasing the total number of solved instances by up to 63%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the classical MI-QCQP formulation (k-HC(2,1)) of Amaldi & Coniglio (2013), which relies solely on 2-norm constraints ‖w_j‖₂ ≥ 1. All three strengthened formulations—adding 1-norm constraints, ∞-norm constraints, or both—dominate the baseline on every metric: median solve time, number of instances solved to global optimality, and SBB node count. The (k-HC(2,1),(1,1/√n)) formulation (which adds scaled ∞-norm constraints) is consistently the fastest, achieving 40.9× median speedup on LowDim and 11.5× on HighDim, because its disjunctive ∞-norm constraints require only k(n−1) branching nodes to produce a nonzero lower bound (vs. 2^{k(n−1)} for the baseline). The (k-HC(2,1),(∞,1)) formulation (adding 1-norm constraints) is slightly slower but still achieves 27.9× and 10.1× speedups. The multi-norm formulation, despite having the tightest theoretical approximation factor (1/√n vs. 1/n), is the slowest of the three strengthened variants because the 1-norm constraints still require exponential branching on their sign variables, partially offsetting the benefit of the ∞-norm constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical MI-QCQP formulation for k-hyperplane clustering introduced by Amaldi & Coniglio (2013) and Coniglio (2011) by intersecting it with constraints derived from polyhedral-norm (1-norm and ∞-norm) variants of the same problem. The key conceptual departure is the use of norm-congruence inequalities to show that these additional constraints are valid relaxations of the 2-norm problem, and the geometric insight that the ∞-norm constraint (with only 2n facets) dramatically outperforms the 1-norm constraint (with 2^n facets) for SBB tree structure. The paper also draws on the broader SBB literature (Belotti et al., 2009; McCormick, 1976) for the branching and convex-envelope machinery, but the multi-norm intersection strategy and the resulting node-count bounds are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that adding a single scaled ∞-norm constraint reduces the SBB node count for a nonzero lower bound from exponential (2^{k(n-1)}) to linear (k(n-1)) is a rare type of structural guarantee in nonlinear integer programming, and the 41× median speedup with 63% more solved instances makes exact k-hyperplane clustering substantially more tractable. However, the experimental evaluation is limited to small instances (m ≤ 30, n ≤ 5, k ≤ 5), leaving open whether the speedup persists at the scales where k-HC is most practically relevant (e.g., LiDAR point clouds with thousands of points). The paper itself suggests a promising direction toward hybrid coreset + exact SBB approaches for scalability, and the multi-norm strengthening technique could potentially transfer to other nonconvex MI-QCQP problems with norm-based constraints.
