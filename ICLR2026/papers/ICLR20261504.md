## Problem

Existing conformal selection methods such as cfBH are restricted to single-threshold hypotheses of the form y > c, which is insufficient for practical multi-condition scenarios like conjunctive (c₁ < y < c₂) or disjunctive (y < c₁ or y > c₂) selection targets. Naive combinations of single-condition results via intersection or union provably fail to preserve finite-sample FDR control due to error accumulation. This paper addresses the gap by extending conformal selection to arbitrary multi-condition targets with rigorous FDR guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes p-dimensional feature vectors x for test samples, a calibration set of (x_i, y_i) pairs with observed responses, a target region I_Target expressed as a union of bounded or one-sided-unbounded open intervals, and a user-specified FDR level q ∈ (0,1). The output is a selection set S of (j, k) pairs—each pair asserting that test sample j satisfies condition k—rather than a simple binary sample-level decision, enabling interval-specific selection proportions. The method is modality-agnostic: it operates on tabular regression, text toxicity scores, monocular depth estimates, and multimodal VQA confidence scores, with the only requirement being a pre-trained predictor μ̂(x) and a valid nonconformity score.

## Method

MCCS combines two key mechanisms. For conjunctive conditions, it designs a regionally monotone nonconformity score V^k(x,y) that assigns M − min(μ̂(x)−c_kL, c_kR−μ̂(x)) when y lies inside the target interval and max(c_kL−μ̂(x), μ̂(x)−c_kR) otherwise, where the large constant M (exceeding 2·sup_x max(|c_kL−μ̂(x)|, |μ̂(x)−c_kR|)) guarantees that in-interval scores always dominate out-of-interval scores, tightening the achieved FDR toward the nominal level. For disjunctive conditions, it applies a global Benjamini-Hochberg procedure over all m×K conformal p-values simultaneously, sorting them and selecting the largest l* satisfying p_(l*) ≤ q·l*/(m·K), which prevents the additive error inflation inherent in separate per-boundary BH applications. The complete algorithm (Algorithm 3) iterates over each target interval to compute interval-specific p-values via the conformal rank formula, then applies the global BH threshold to produce the final (j,k)-pair selection set. Theorem 4.1 establishes finite-sample FDR ≤ q under exchangeability and regional monotonicity, and the framework extends naturally to multivariate responses by replacing scalar thresholds with distance-to-boundary metrics over annular or multi-sphere target regions.

## Results

Under a nominal FDR of 0.3, MCCS achieves observed FDR of 0.2874 with power 0.9756 on conjunctive conditions (univariate) and 0.2848 with power 0.9515 on disjunctive conditions (univariate), staying at or just below the nominal level while maintaining high power. On real-world tasks, MCCS achieves FDR 0.291/0.289 with power 0.575/0.512 on NLP toxicity detection, FDR 0.261/0.293 with power 0.892/0.814 on depth estimation, and FDR 0.263/0.285 with power 0.589/0.726 on VQA (at q=0.3). The method scales to large K (up to 40 intervals) with FDR remaining near nominal (0.276–0.295) and power above 0.90.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCCS outperforms the naive cfBH intersection/union baselines (Int/Uni) on FDR control: while Int/Uni inflate FDR to 0.3766 (exceeding the nominal 0.3), MCCS stays at 0.2874/0.2848 while simultaneously achieving higher power (0.9756 vs. 0.9397 for conjunctive; 0.9515 vs. 0.9720 for disjunctive). Against the Bonferroni-corrected variants (Int-B/Uni-B), MCCS is substantially less conservative (FDR 0.2874 vs. 0.1081 for conjunctive) while delivering much higher power (0.9756 vs. 0.6005). The Benjamini-Yekutieli (BY) procedure, tested in Table 9, is extremely conservative with FDR around 0.02–0.04 and power as low as 0.14–0.65, whereas MCCS maintains FDR near 0.30 with power 0.95–0.99 across all six task configurations. The Prediction Interval (PI) baseline, which relies on parametric regression intervals, consistently exceeds the FDR target (0.36–0.50) while MCCS remains controlled, confirming that MCCS offers the best FDR–power trade-off among all tested methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal selection (CS) framework initiated by cfBH (Jin & Candes, 2023) and its multivariate generalization mCS (Bai et al., 2025b), both of which are restricted to single-threshold selection (y > c). The key departure is showing that naively combining single-condition cfBH results via intersection or union fails to preserve FDR control under multi-condition targets, and then providing two tailored fixes: a regionally monotone nonconformity score designed for bounded intervals (conjunctive conditions) and a global BH procedure operating on all m×K conformal p-values simultaneously (disjunctive conditions). The theoretical backbone draws on the regional monotonicity condition from mCS and the superuniformity of conformal p-values from Bates et al. (2023), but the nonconformity construction and the global aggregation strategy are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-condition selection (e.g., selecting compounds with logP in a specific range, or activating alerts when a signal exceeds or falls below thresholds) is ubiquitous in drug discovery, clinical monitoring, and LLM alignment, yet prior conformal selection methods could not handle it with finite-sample FDR guarantees. The paper's main limitation is the i.i.d. exchangeability assumption; domain shift between calibration and test data remains unaddressed, which is a realistic concern in deployment. Open questions include whether the global BH procedure's conservatism at large K can be mitigated without sacrificing validity, and how the method behaves under covariate shift (the weighted conformal p-value approach of WCS is a natural but unexplored extension).
