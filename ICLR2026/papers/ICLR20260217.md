## Problem

Evaluating and improving generalist vision-language-action (VLA) robot policies requires large numbers of costly real-world rollouts and expert-labeled corrective data. Prior action-conditioned world models are insufficient for policy-in-the-loop rollouts because they simulate only a single camera view (causing partial observability and hallucinations), lack fine-grained frame-level action control, and fail to maintain temporal consistency over long horizons. Ctrl-World addresses all three gaps to enable scalable imagination-based policy evaluation and improvement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260217/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes three simultaneous camera views (two third-person and one wrist-mounted, each 192×320), a 15-step action chunk (≈1 s) expressed as Cartesian-space end-effector poses, 7 sparse history frames sampled at 1–2 s intervals with their corresponding arm poses, and a language instruction. It jointly predicts future frames for all three cameras autoregressively, sustaining coherent rollouts beyond 20 seconds. Actions are temporally downsampled from 15 to 5 steps to reduce GPU memory, and the VAE encodes each frame into 24×40 latent tokens (8×8 spatial downsampling).

## Method

Ctrl-World is initialized from the 1.5B-parameter Stable Video Diffusion backbone, with only a 3-layer action-projection MLP (7-dim Cartesian actions → 1024-dim embedding) newly initialized; all other SVD weights are preserved. Three key adaptations are introduced: (1) multi-view joint prediction, where all N camera images are concatenated along the token dimension and predicted simultaneously, reducing hallucinations during contact-rich interactions; (2) pose-conditioned memory retrieval, which appends k=7 sparse history frames to the context and embeds their robot arm poses via frame-wise cross-attention inside the spatial transformer, allowing the model to re-anchor predictions to similar past states and stabilize long-horizon rollouts; (3) frame-level action conditioning, which converts the policy's action chunk into Cartesian-space poses and applies the same frame-wise cross-attention mechanism so each future frame's visual tokens attend to its associated pose embedding. The model is fine-tuned with standard diffusion denoising loss on the full DROID dataset (95k trajectories, 564 scenes) for 100k steps on 2×8 H100 GPUs. For policy-in-the-loop interaction, a lightweight 2-layer MLP adapter maps the VLA policy's joint-velocity outputs to future joint configurations, which are then converted to Cartesian poses via Franka Panda forward kinematics before conditioning the world model.

## Results

On the DROID validation set (256 clips, 10-second autoregressive rollouts), Ctrl-World achieves the best generation quality across most metrics: PSNR 23.56, SSIM 0.828, LPIPS 0.091, and FVD 97.4 on the third-view camera, substantially outperforming prior action-conditioned world models. The multi-view joint prediction variant further improves over the single-view version (PSNR 21.27 → 23.56, FVD 127.5 → 97.4). For policy improvement, post-training π₀.₅-DROID on synthetic trajectories generated within Ctrl-World boosts instruction-following success from 38.7% to 83.4% (+44.7%) on tasks with unseen objects and novel instructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ctrl-World outperforms both baselines across all reported metrics on the third-view camera. Against WPE (Quevedo et al., 2025), Ctrl-World improves PSNR by 3.23 dB (23.56 vs. 20.33) and reduces FVD by 37.6% (97.4 vs. 156.4); even WPE's multiview variant (FVD 147.1) trails Ctrl-World by 33.8%. Against IRASim (Zhu et al., 2024), Ctrl-World gains 2.20 dB in PSNR (23.56 vs. 21.36) and lowers FVD by 29.4% (97.4 vs. 138.1); IRASim's multiview variant actually degrades (FVD 165.4), suggesting its multi-view extension is less effective than Ctrl-World's joint prediction. The single-view Ctrl-World variant already surpasses both baselines on FVD (127.5 vs. 156.4 and 138.1), indicating that the architectural improvements (memory retrieval, frame-level conditioning) contribute independently of multi-view prediction. The FID metric is the one exception where Ctrl-World's full model (25.00) is slightly worse than its own single-view ablation (23.47), though it remains competitive with the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on pretrained video diffusion backbones (Stable Video Diffusion) and prior action-conditioned world models like IRASim and WPE, which demonstrated that video generation can simulate robot interactions. The key departure is the integration of three mechanisms—multi-view joint prediction (including wrist cameras), frame-level action conditioning via Cartesian-space pose cross-attention, and pose-conditioned memory retrieval—into a single model specifically designed for closed-loop, policy-in-the-loop rollouts with modern VLA policies. Prior action-conditioned world models were limited to single third-person views, lacked the fine-grained temporal control needed for high-frequency action chunks, and could not sustain coherent predictions over long horizons, making them incompatible with the multi-view, multi-step interaction paradigm of current generalist policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ctrl-World offers a scalable alternative to expensive real-world rollouts for both evaluating and improving generalist robot policies, with the demonstrated 44.7% success-rate boost from synthetic post-training data being practically significant for the VLA community. However, the world model underestimates low-level execution success (particularly for complex physics like collisions and rotations), is sensitive to initial observations, and currently relies on human preference judgments for trajectory scoring. Open questions include whether iterative policy-rollout-and-finetune loops can close the remaining fidelity gap, whether automated reward models can replace human evaluation at scale, and whether the approach extends to bimanual or mobile manipulation settings.
