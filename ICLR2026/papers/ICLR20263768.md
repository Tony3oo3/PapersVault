## Problem

Parameterization-based dataset distillation (DDP) has shown promise for image datasets by representing synthetic samples in compact formats to fit more diverse samples under a fixed memory budget, but its application to 3D point clouds remains unexplored due to the irregular and unordered nature of point cloud data. This paper is the first to introduce a DDP framework for 3D point clouds that leverages learnable shape morphing to generate diverse synthetic samples within the same storage constraint as conventional full-resolution distillation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263768/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large-scale 3D point cloud dataset (e.g., 1024 points per sample) and hyperparameters M (number of coarser anchors per class) and L (number of combined samples), subject to the memory constraint MN₂ ≤ N₁. It produces a synthetic dataset where each class is represented by M aligned anchor samples at coarser resolution (N₂ points each) plus L additional samples formed by convex combinations of those anchors, along with a set of learnable weight vectors (one per combined sample per class) that control the blending. The total storage—anchor coordinates plus weight vectors—fits within the same bit budget as a single full-resolution synthetic sample per class.

## Method

The framework initializes the synthetic dataset with M coarser anchor samples per class (each with N₂ points, where MN₂ ≤ N₁) rather than one full-resolution sample, then aligns anchors within each group via a linear assignment problem on pairwise Euclidean distances. L additional samples are generated by convex combination of the aligned anchors using learnable, non-negative weight vectors that sum to one, introducing diversity at negligible memory cost. For the distillation objective, the paper adapts the SADM feature-distribution-matching loss to handle the resolution mismatch by partitioning each original sample into M non-overlapping subsets via iterative farthest-point sampling, then weighting each subset's contribution by a uniformity-aware penalty coefficient η derived from the coefficient of variation of k-NN distances. The anchors and morphing weights are jointly optimized via SGD to minimize this uniformity-aware matching loss, enabling the synthetic set to capture both structural fidelity and shape diversity under a constrained memory budget.

## Results

On ModelNet10 at PPC=1, the proposed method achieves 87.7% classification accuracy with PointNet, a 51.8-point improvement over the previous state-of-the-art SADM (35.9%). At PPC=10, it reaches 92.2% on ModelNet10, 82.5% on ModelNet40, 68.9% on ShapeNet, 49.8% on ScanObjectNN, and 68.4% on OmniObject3D, consistently outperforming all baselines. The method also generalizes across architectures, achieving 69.4% on ModelNet10 with PointMamba and 55.4% with PointNet++ at PPC=1, and 56.4% average mIoU on ShapeNetPart segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and PPC setting. Against the closest competitor SADM (Yim et al., 2025), the gap is largest at PPC=1: 87.7% vs. 35.9% on ModelNet10 (+51.8 pts), 73.2% vs. 54.8% on ModelNet40 (+18.4 pts), and 32.6% vs. 17.6% on ScanObjectNN (+15.0 pts). Among image-domain distillation methods adapted to 3D (DM, DC, MTT), the proposed method leads by 10–55 points depending on dataset and PPC, with MTT suffering OOM on OmniObject3D at PPC=10. Coreset selection baselines (Random, Herding, K-Center) are consistently the weakest, confirming that learned synthetic samples substantially outperform subset selection. PCC (Zhang et al., 2024), the first 3D-specific gradient-matching distillation method, is surpassed by 8–15 points at PPC=1. PointMixup (Chen et al., 2020), a fixed-weight interpolation augmentation, achieves only 82.3% on ModelNet10 and 21.9% on ScanObjectNN at PPC=1, well below the proposed 87.7% and 32.6%, validating that learnable (rather than fixed) morphing weights are critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: dataset distillation (DD) for 3D point clouds (PCC, SADM, DD3D) and distilled dataset parameterization (DDP) for images (IDC, FreD, HaBa, DDiF). It is the first to bring the DDP paradigm—representing synthetic data in compact latent formats to fit more samples under a fixed memory budget—into the 3D point cloud domain, where the unordered, irregular structure of point clouds had made such parameterization unexplored. The method builds directly on SADM's semantically aligned distribution matching loss but adapts it to handle the resolution mismatch introduced by using coarser anchor samples, and introduces a uniformity-aware penalty to account for spatial non-uniformity in FPS-partitioned subsets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the parameterization module (demonstrated by boosting DM from 25.8% to 79.8% on ModelNet10 at PPC=1) suggests it can be layered onto any existing 3D DD method, making it broadly applicable. A key open question is whether the morphing strategy generalizes beyond classification to tasks like detection or reconstruction, and whether it extends to other 3D representations (meshes, voxels, implicit fields). The method's reliance on SADM as the underlying matching loss means it inherits SADM's weakness on certain dataset-architecture combinations (e.g., ScanObjectNN with PointNet++), and the linear assignment for point correspondence, while effective, may struggle with highly non-rigid shapes.
