## Problem

Chain-of-thought distillation from advanced LLMs has proven effective for general reasoning but struggles in scientific domains, where even the strongest models frequently produce erroneous or superficial reasoning paths due to high complexity and specialized knowledge requirements. Existing distillation methods either rely on single-teacher selection (introducing bias and failing to ensure accurate knowledge usage) or multi-teacher aggregation (increasing diversity but lacking the ability to refine internal CoT logic at a fine-grained level). The critical gap is the absence of an intra-chain aggregation mechanism that can dynamically integrate complementary reasoning steps from multiple trajectories into a single, high-quality chain.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes scientific reasoning queries paired with ground-truth answers, along with a diverse pool of CoT trajectories generated by multiple LLM thinkers (reasoning models such as DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3-235B-A22B, plus prompting-strategy variants of Llama4-Scout-17B) and externally retrieved knowledge snippets produced by GPT-5-mini. The output is an evolved CoT dataset of (query, evolved_CoT, answer) triples, where each evolved CoT is a compact, domain-accurate reasoning chain synthesized from multiple source trajectories. These evolved CoTs are then used to fine-tune compact student models (Qwen3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama3.1-8B) via supervised fine-tuning with a maximum sequence length of 16,384 tokens.

## Method

COT-EVO is an evolutionary distillation framework structured as a genetic algorithm with four core modules operating in an iterative evaluation–selection–variation–update loop. First, a diverse candidate pool is initialized by generating CoTs from multiple LLM thinkers and augmenting a subset with externally retrieved domain knowledge. Each candidate is scored by a composite fitness function combining exact-match correctness, length appropriateness (bounded by 15th–85th percentiles of reference scientific CoTs), and LLM-judged knowledge usage accuracy (1–5 scale). Rather than greedily selecting top-scoring candidates, a novelty-driven selection mechanism inspired by Novelty Search with Local Competition embeds each CoT into a behavioral space via Qwen3-Embedding-8B, computes novelty (k-NN distance) and local competition scores, and selects parents from the Pareto front to preserve both quality and reasoning diversity. Finally, reflective recombination (which identifies a binding point in a strategy-provider CoT, extracts unique correct knowledge absent from the target, and synthesizes a hybrid trajectory) and three mutation operators (additive, deletive, and innovative) generate improved offspring. Over B=5 generations with population size 6, this loop progressively refines the pool, yielding evolved CoTs that are more accurate, knowledge-rich, and logically compact than any individual teacher's raw output.

## Results

On BioProBench, CoT-EVO achieves 12.6% relative gain over Single Teacher and 8.4% over Multi Teacher distillation; on ChemCoTBench, gains reach 27.0% and 19.3% respectively. CoT-EVO also surpasses process-level baselines Retro-Search and TwT across all three student models (Llama3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-8B). Notably, on several subtasks (e.g., BioProBench ORD EM: 0.419–0.544; ChemCoTBench Edit Acc: 0.625–0.707), the distilled student models rival or exceed the raw outputs of the 235B-parameter teacher LLMs. Data utilization (pass rate) jumps to 70–73% under CoT-EVO versus 37–54% for baselines, and GPT-5-judged CoT quality scores rise from ~6.7–7.0 to 8.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoT-EVO consistently outperforms all five distillation baselines (Single Teacher, Multi Teacher, Best-of-K, Retro-Search, TwT) across both BioProBench and ChemCoTBench for all three student models. Against Single Teacher, CoT-EVO achieves 12.6% relative gain on BioProBench and 27.0% on ChemCoTBench; against Multi Teacher, gains are 8.4% and 19.3% respectively. Best-of-K (which samples K=21 trajectories per teacher, matching CoT-EVO's maximum candidate count) is substantially outperformed, confirming that gains stem from evolutionary refinement rather than brute-force sampling. Retro-Search and TwT, the strongest process-level baselines, are also surpassed on nearly all subtasks. In data utilization, CoT-EVO retains 70–73% of trajectories as correct and usable, compared to 37–54% for baselines, and GPT-5 pairwise win rates show no baseline exceeds 40% against CoT-EVO. On several tasks (e.g., Qwen3-8B on BioProBench ORD: 0.544 vs. teacher Qwen3-235B's 0.625; Llama3.1-8B on ChemCoTBench Edit: 0.707 vs. teacher DeepSeek-R1's 0.783), the distilled students approach or rival the 235B-parameter teachers, demonstrating that evolutionary CoT synthesis can extract more value per token than direct distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

COT-EVO extends the CoT distillation literature by importing the full genetic-algorithm loop (Holland, 1992) and the Novelty Search with Local Competition selection mechanism (Lehman & Stanley, 2011) into the reasoning-trajectory space. Prior distillation work either prunes/compresses a single teacher's chain (Retro-Search, LIMO) or selects the best chain from multiple teachers (TwT, multi-teacher aggregation); COT-EVO departs from both by performing *intra-chain* recombination and mutation, splicing reasoning steps from different teachers into a single evolved trajectory rather than picking one wholesale. The fitness function's knowledge-usage component and the knowledge-augmentation stage further distinguish it from generic CoT distillation, targeting the specific failure mode where scientific teachers hallucinate or misapply domain facts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that evolutionary refinement of CoTs can push a compact 7–8 B student to rival or exceed its 235 B teacher on several scientific-reasoning subtasks, and that this advantage persists under matched token budgets, suggesting a compute-efficient path to domain-specialized reasoning models. Key limitations include the substantial inference cost of running six LLM thinkers per query and the reliance on LLM-as-a-Judge reward signals that may carry their own biases. Open questions raised by the authors include generalization beyond chemistry and biology to broader scientific domains, and whether incorporating structured external knowledge (databases, knowledge graphs) in the augmentation stage can further reduce dependence on proprietary models.
