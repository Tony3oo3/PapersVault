## Problem

Conventional Chain-of-Thought reasoning in discrete token space incurs high computational and memory costs due to verbose intermediate steps, while pure latent (embedding-space) reasoning methods like Coconut compress tokens aggressively but degrade accuracy because nuanced information is lost in compressed representations. No existing approach enables a model to selectively and dynamically decide which reasoning steps to keep as explicit tokens and which to compress into latent embeddings, leaving a gap between interpretability and efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260906/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text query (e.g., a math problem) and produces a hybrid reasoning trace that interleaves explicit token steps with latent embedding segments delimited by <start-latent> and <end-latent> special tokens, followed by a final answer. Latent segments replace low-entropy CoT steps and are counted as a single [Latent] token in output length metrics. The resulting trace is approximately 60% the length of a standard CoT+RL output while preserving or improving accuracy, with an average of 3–5 mode switches per sample.

## Method

HyRea introduces a two-stage training pipeline for a standard Transformer LLM. In the cold-start SFT stage, low-entropy reasoning steps in CoT data are replaced with latent segments (the hidden state is fed back into the first layer instead of being decoded to a token), with the number of replaced steps progressively increasing from 0 to a maximum S; the loss is computed only over visible non-latent tokens, and the <start-latent>/<end-latent> token losses are scaled 4× to emphasize switching decisions. In the RL stage, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) fine-tunes the model's switching policy using a composite reward combining accuracy, format correctness, and latent-usage signals, eliminating the need for manually constructed synthetic switching data. The key design choice is entropy-based step selection: low-entropy (deterministic) steps are prioritized for latent compression because they are easier to represent faithfully in embedding space, while high-entropy steps remain explicit to preserve semantic fidelity.

## Results

On MATH-500 with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, HyRea achieves 83.6% accuracy with 387 average output tokens, nearly matching the SFT+RL baseline (84.2%) while using less than half the tokens (387 vs. 698). On Minerva Math, HyRea slightly outperforms SFT+RL (27.2% vs. 26.8%) with 425 tokens vs. 671. Scaling to Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, HyRea reaches 84.4% on MATH-500 with 369 tokens and 57.8% on AMC23 with 498 tokens, again substantially reducing output length compared to SFT+RL (588 and 905 tokens respectively). Across all four math benchmarks, HyRea averages 3.6–5.3 latent-explicit mode switches per sample, compared to zero for all token-only baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SFT+RL baseline (DeepSeek-R1-style training), HyRea matches or nearly matches accuracy on all four math benchmarks (e.g., 83.6% vs. 84.2% on MATH-500 7B; 48.2% vs. 48.2% on AMC23) while reducing token usage by 37–45% (387 vs. 698 on MATH-500; 526 vs. 892 on AMC23). On Minerva Math, HyRea actually surpasses SFT+RL in accuracy (27.2% vs. 26.8% for 7B) with 37% fewer tokens. Against Coconut (pure latent reasoning), HyRea achieves substantially higher accuracy (83.6% vs. 70.4% on MATH-500 7B; 84.4% vs. 79.8% for 32B) at the cost of more tokens (387 vs. 106), confirming that fully latent reasoning degrades performance on math. Against Soft Thinking, HyRea wins on both accuracy and efficiency (83.6% vs. 66.4% on MATH-500 7B, with 387 vs. 617 tokens), showing that the training-free soft-thinking approach is inferior to the learned hybrid switching strategy. The ablation (HyRea w/o RL) further confirms that the GRPO stage is critical: removing RL drops MATH-500 accuracy from 83.6% to 71.8% (7B), highlighting that the RL phase is what enables effective mode-switching decisions.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyRea sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: latent/continuous reasoning (Coconut, Soft Thinking) that bypasses tokenization for efficiency, and RL-based reasoning optimization (DeepSeek-R1, GRPO) that improves multi-step problem solving. The key departure is that prior latent-reasoning methods commit entirely to embedding-space computation, while HyRea introduces a *dynamic switching* mechanism where the model autonomously decides per-step whether to reason in token space or embedding space. The entropy-guided cold-start (replacing low-entropy CoT steps with latent segments) and the GRPO-based refinement of switching policy are novel combinations, but each component draws directly from established techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~50% token reduction at maintained accuracy is practically significant for deployment cost and latency, especially in RL training loops where token budgets are a major bottleneck. However, several open questions remain: latent steps are not interpretable (a concern for high-stakes applications), the method depends on well-structured CoT data for cold-start, and the entropy-based selection criterion has not been validated beyond mathematical reasoning. Whether the dynamic switching generalizes to domains with less structured reasoning (e.g., open-ended generation, multi-modal tasks) and whether latent segments can be made partially interpretable are important directions for future work.
