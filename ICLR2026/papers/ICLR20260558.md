## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for spatio-temporal point processes (STPPs) typically process spatial and temporal dimensions independently, failing to capture the intrinsic structural interdependencies between them, especially under sparse and noisy regimes. Moreover, graph abstractions have never been applied to model STPPs, and the utility of zigzag persistence for distilling time-aware topological signatures from event sequences remains entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260558/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N spatio-temporal events, each consisting of a strictly increasing timestamp and a geospatial coordinate (latitude/longitude or zipcode), and predicts the next event's spatial location and inter-arrival time interval. Spatially, events are rasterized into 2D binary images (pixels set to 1 if an event falls in that cell) within a fixed geographic bounding box, then segmented into patches of length P with stride S to form time-series image sequences. Evaluation uses Euclidean distance for spatial error and RMSE for temporal error on the next-event prediction task.

## Method

TEN-DM integrates three modules into a conditional diffusion framework: (1) a multi-relational ε-graph is built over events using cosine similarity in time, latitude, and longitude dimensions, with learned edge-type weights aggregated into a joint adjacency matrix and encoded by a GAT-based graph autoencoder; (2) a Temporal Topological Learning (TTL) module converts event patches into binary image sequences, computes cubical zigzag persistence (0- and 1-dimensional) across four time scales, vectorizes the resulting zigzag persistence diagrams into images, and extracts dynamic topological features via a 2-layer CNN; (3) a Temporal Query-guided Self-Attention (TQ-SA) module injects a learnable query matrix and sinusoidal temporal positional encodings into the attention mechanism to capture periodic patterns. These representations are fused through a topology-guided spatio-temporal multi-head attention (TST-MHA) where the topological embedding serves as the query and concatenated spatial/temporal/graph embeddings serve as keys and values. The fused conditioning vector guides a cross-attentive denoising decoder that performs joint forward/reverse Gaussian diffusion over both the spatial coordinates and the inter-arrival time of the next event.

## Results

On 5 real-world STPP datasets, TEN-DM achieves the best or near-best spatial Euclidean distance and temporal RMSE across all settings. Headline numbers include 0.391 spatial / 0.087 temporal RMSE on COVID-19, 38.543 spatial / 0.077 temporal on US Earthquake, and 0.0547 spatial / 0.792 temporal on 311 Service. The only exception is JPN Earthquake temporal RMSE, where DeepSTPP (0.341) edges out TEN-DM (0.371). Gains over the strongest STPP baseline (DSTPP) range from 3.66% to 17.08% relative improvement depending on dataset and dimension.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 3 SPP baselines (Conditional KDE, CNF, TVCNF), TEN-DM achieves 21.97%–42.97% relative spatial improvement over the best SPP method (TVCNF) on JPN Earthquake and COVID-19, with smaller but consistent gains (0.29%–0.55%) on Theft and 311 Service. Against the 10 TPP baselines, TEN-DM statistically significantly outperforms all of them, with the largest margin being 57.14% over the best TPP runner-up on US Earthquake temporal RMSE. Against the 4 STPP baselines, TEN-DM beats the strongest competitor DSTPP on 4 of 5 datasets in both dimensions: 7.16%/6.90% on COVID-19, 17.08% on Theft temporal, and 3.66% on 311 Service temporal. The sole exception is JPN Earthquake temporal RMSE, where DeepSTPP (0.341) outperforms TEN-DM (0.371), though TEN-DM still wins on spatial (6.649 vs. 9.200). On the additional Human Mobility, Wildfire, and Twitter datasets (Table 4), TEN-DM again outperforms DSTPP, with a highly significant temporal gain on Twitter (0.677 vs. 0.710, p ≈ 0.001) and a significant spatial gain on Human Mobility (5.559 vs. 5.783, p ≈ 0.09).

## Relation to Prior Work

TEN-DM sits at the intersection of three previously separate research threads: (1) zigzag persistence for time-varying topological signatures (Carlsson & De Silva, 2010; Chen et al., 2021b), (2) diffusion-based generative modeling of point processes (DSTPP, Yuan et al., 2023; Lüdke et al., 2023), and (3) graph-based representation learning for spatio-temporal data (Chen & Gel, 2025). The paper's key conceptual departure is that it is the first to use graph abstraction *to model* STPPs (rather than modeling STPPs that live on graphs), the first to apply zigzag persistence to STPP data (rather than graph diffusion), and the first to deploy a diffusion model for joint spatio-temporal event prediction. The TTL module generalizes the ZPI pipeline from Chen & Gel (2025) by introducing multi-scale temporal patching and a mixup fusion across time scales, while the TQ-SA mechanism adapts temporal-query techniques from multivariate time-series forecasting (Kulkarni et al., 2011; Lin et al., 2025) to the point-process setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded framework (zigzag stability theorem, Lipschitz bound for the fusion attention) for incorporating dynamic topological structure into STPP forecasting, addressing the 'dynamic spatio-temporal dependencies learning dilemma' where existing methods treat space and time independently. The robustness analysis—showing insensitivity to filtration resolution, patch size, zigzag directionality, and 5% coordinate noise—suggests the topological features capture genuine structural signal rather than overfitting to specific hyperparameter choices. Open questions include scalability to very large event sets (the ZPI generation, while fast per-event, must be repeated across multi-scale patches), whether the 2D rasterization of spatial coordinates loses information in non-Euclidean or high-dimensional domains, and whether the modest empirical gains over DSTPP (the closest diffusion-based competitor) would widen with more complex or higher-dimensional STPPs.
