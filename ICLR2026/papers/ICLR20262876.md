## Problem

In differentially private (DP) transfer learning, the clipping bound C and batch size B are typically fixed to a single value across all tasks, yet theory predicts smaller C under stronger privacy while empirics show the opposite. This paper identifies and explains the mismatch, showing that the optimal C and B shift systematically with learning-problem difficulty—governed by privacy budget, pretrained backbone capability, dataset complexity, and compute budget—and that fixing them across tasks systematically degrades accuracy, especially for hard classes.

## Input / Output

For image tasks, inputs are RGB images resized to 224×224 (matching ImageNet-21k pretraining resolution); for text, inputs are documents truncated to 512 tokens (DistilBERT default). The method's actual 'input' is a pretrained backbone (ViT-Base ~85M, ViT-Tiny ~5M, DistilBERT ~66M, or WRN-16-4 ~2.8M) plus a private dataset and a target (ε, δ) privacy budget; its 'output' is the jointly optimal (C, B, η) configuration for DP fine-tuning. The final model produces a single class label per input, evaluated on held-out test sets.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form optimal clipping constant C* by decomposing per-step gradient error into clipping bias and DP noise variance (Theorem 5.2), then connects this MSE to per-step loss improvement under L-smoothness (Theorem 5.4, Corollary 5.5), showing that minimizing MSE tightens the optimization bound. Empirically, a structured grid search over (C, B, η) across four image datasets and one text dataset reveals that tighter privacy and weaker backbones shift gradient norm distributions toward larger norms, making larger C optimal despite injecting more per-step noise. Clipping is reinterpreted as gradient re-weighting: small C disproportionately suppresses gradients from hard classes, while large C preserves them more uniformly. For batch size under fixed-epoch (bounded compute) settings, the paper shows that existing per-step noise heuristics fail and instead proposes selecting B by enforcing a minimum number of optimizer steps while minimizing cumulative DP noise σ√T, which recovers a plateau where moderate batches outperform full-batch under tight privacy.

## Results

The paper's central finding is that the optimal clipping bound C shifts systematically with learning-problem difficulty: tighter privacy (smaller ε) and weaker pretrained backbones both push the optimal C upward, while easier settings prefer smaller C. On SUN397 with ViT-Tiny, the optimal C ranges from ~1 (ε=8, ViT-Base) to ~26–42 (ε=0.25, ViT-Tiny). For batch size under fixed epochs, the authors show that cumulative DP noise (σ√T) combined with a minimum step count (≈20 steps) predicts the optimal batch size, whereas the per-step noise heuristic from prior work always suggests full-batch and fails. The largest accuracy gaps from suboptimal hyperparameter choices appear at the intersection of tight privacy and limited compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary quantitative comparison is against AUTO-S (Bu et al., 2023), an automatic clipping method that eliminates the need to set C explicitly. The tuned clipping bound (Ours) consistently outperforms AUTO-S across all three datasets and all five privacy levels, with the gap widening as task difficulty increases. On the hardest setting (SUN397, ε=0.5, ViT-Tiny), tuned C achieves 43.0% macro accuracy versus 40.3% for AUTO-S—a 2.7-point gap. On Cassava at ε=0.5, the gap is even larger at 3.3 points (63.7% vs. 60.4%). On the easier CIFAR-100 task, the gap shrinks to 0.1–0.5 points, confirming the paper's hypothesis that automatic clipping (equivalent to a very small C) works adequately only when the problem is easy enough that focusing on the easiest examples suffices. The authors also note that fixing a single (C, B) pair across all privacy levels and backbones systematically degrades performance, with the worst drops occurring when moving between loose and tight privacy or between plentiful and limited compute budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper challenges a core theoretical prediction in DP optimization: Koloskova et al. (2023) and related convergence analyses suggest that stronger privacy (larger noise σ) should favor smaller clipping bounds, yet the authors show empirically and theoretically (via a novel MSE-based optimal clipping theorem) that the opposite holds when gradient distributions shift under noise. For batch size, the paper departs from the fixed-steps heuristics of Räisä et al. (2024) and Ponomareva et al. (2023) by analyzing the fixed-epochs (bounded-compute) regime, where those guidelines break down. The work builds on the DP transfer learning paradigm established by De et al. (2022) and Tobaben et al. (2023), and reframes clipping as a form of gradient re-weighting that interacts with class-level difficulty—a perspective absent from prior DP-SGD analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since DP transfer learning is the current state-of-the-art for training on sensitive data, the finding that optimal C and B shift systematically with learning-problem difficulty (privacy budget, backbone capability, compute) has direct practical impact: the common practice of fixing a single (C, B) across tasks systematically suppresses accuracy, especially for hard classes. The paper's main limitation is that its 'difficulty' characterization remains informal, and the primary experiments use parameter-efficient FiLM fine-tuning; full fine-tuning of large models exhibits qualitatively different clipping behavior due to larger average gradient norms. Open questions include how to automate C and B selection from problem characteristics without exhaustive grid search, and whether the gradient-re-weighting interpretation extends to other privacy mechanisms or non-convex objectives.
