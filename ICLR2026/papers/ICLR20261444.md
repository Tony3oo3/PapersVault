## Problem

Return-conditioned supervised learning (RCSL) methods in offline RL treat policy learning as pure sequence modeling, causing the model to overfit to specific historical sub-trajectories and fail at trajectory stitching—combining the best segments from different trajectories to outperform the behavioral policy. Existing hybrid approaches that add value guidance (e.g., QT) still cannot fully counteract the adverse influence of the behavior-cloning term, and prior attempts to shorten sequence dependencies lack flexibility or require extensive per-dataset hyperparameter tuning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of return-to-go and state pairs (R̂_{t-K+1}, s_{t-K+1}, …, R̂_t, s_t) with action tokens explicitly excluded from the input, following the DD design. For pixel-based environments (Atari), raw RGB frames are first compressed by a three-layer CNN encoder into latent vectors before entering the dual-branch architecture. At inference, the policy samples multiple candidate actions conditioned on different target RTGs and selects the one maximizing the minimum of twin Q-values, producing a single action per timestep.

## Method

QDFFDT introduces a dual-feature fusion block that runs a self-attention Transformer (global, history-aware) and a lightweight MLP (local, Markovian) in parallel over the same RTG-state sequence, then adaptively combines their outputs via a state-dependent coefficient α̂(s). This coefficient is learned by an Alpha network trained with an asymmetric loss that penalizes high sequence reliance whenever an expectile-regression value function V_ψ(s) indicates the observed RTG is suboptimal relative to the empirical upper bound, effectively down-weighting sequence features on low-quality trajectories while preserving them on high-quality ones. On top of this fused representation, a Q-augmented policy loss combines the standard MSE behavior-cloning objective with a Q-value-guided improvement term using twin Q-networks and a 1-step or n-step Bellman backup (chosen per dataset), enabling explicit value-based policy improvement beyond what RTG conditioning alone provides.

## Results

On the D4RL benchmark, QDFFDT achieves the best average normalized score across all five task suites: 94.3 on Gym (vs. 90.3 for QCS, 89.8 for QT), 159.8 on Maze2D (vs. 154.4 for QT, 121.5 for DD), 88.3 on AntMaze (vs. 80.4 for QCS, 38.9 for QT), 64.9 on Kitchen (vs. 61.6 for D-QL, 60.6 for QT), and 94.5 on Adroit (vs. 90.1 for QT, 89.4 for GDT). The largest margins appear on suboptimal Gym datasets (e.g., halfcheetah-medium: 65.7 vs. 59.0 for QCS) and AntMaze navigation tasks (e.g., antmaze-medium-diverse: 97.0 vs. 75.2 for QCS). On Atari (ablation study), the hybrid fusion also outperforms both pure sequence and pure MLP variants, confirming the benefit of adaptive feature weighting in partially observable settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QDFFDT outperforms all 26 baselines on the D4RL benchmark averages across every domain. On Gym tasks, it leads QCS (90.3) and QT (89.8) by 4.0 and 4.5 points respectively, with the largest gap on suboptimal datasets (halfcheetah-medium: +6.7 over QCS; walker2d-medium-replay: +2.5 over QT). On Maze2D, QDFFDT (159.8) edges out QT (154.4) and substantially surpasses diffusion-based methods DD (121.5) and Diffuser (119.5), while pure sequence models like DT (13.8) and EDT (28.6) fail catastrophically. On AntMaze, the advantage over QT (38.9) is dramatic (+49.4 points), and QDFFDT also beats QCS (80.4) by 7.9 points, particularly on the large-play and large-diverse tasks where QT scores below 30. On Kitchen, QDFFDT (64.9) surpasses D-QL (61.6) and QT (60.6), while value-based methods like CQL (50.4) and IQL (48.7) trail by ~15 points. On Adroit, QDFFDT (94.5) leads QT (90.1) and GDT (89.4), with the clearest win on pen-cloned (+9.8 over A2PO's 85.0). The only domain where QDFFDT does not achieve the absolute best on every individual task is Maze2D-large, where QT (214.5) slightly exceeds QDFFDT (198.9), though the domain average still favors QDFFDT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Decision Transformer (DT) lineage by introducing a dual-feature fusion architecture that adaptively combines global sequential features (self-attention over RTG-state pairs) with local Markovian features (MLP on the current state), departing from prior RCSL methods that rely exclusively on sequence modeling. The Q-learning module is adapted from QT (Hu et al., 2024), while the exclusion of action tokens from the input sequence follows DD (Ajay et al., 2022). The key conceptual departure from prior work (LSDT, EDT, QCS) is the use of expectile regression to learn a state-dependent fusion weight, providing a principled mechanism for when to trust historical context versus immediate state information, rather than relying on fixed context lengths or manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically demonstrates a concrete failure mode of pure sequence modeling in offline RL—overfitting to suboptimal sub-trajectories that prevents effective trajectory stitching—which motivates the dual-feature design. The adaptive fusion mechanism offers a practical solution that avoids per-dataset tuning, a recurring pain point in the RCSL literature. However, the added architectural complexity (five networks, expectile regression, dual branches) raises questions about whether simpler alternatives could achieve similar gains, and the paper acknowledges this as a limitation. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to truly partially observable settings beyond Atari, and whether the Q-augmentation is independently necessary or merely compensates for residual sequence-modeling bias.
