## Problem

Existing MLLM-based universal multimodal embedding models treat MLLMs purely as encoders, ignoring their generative capacity. This encoding-only paradigm degrades when task instructions require compositional reasoning (e.g., visual grounding, VQA, composed retrieval), because the model must simultaneously interpret complex instructions and produce a discriminative representation in a single forward pass without an intermediate reasoning step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260100/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each query or target is a triplet ⟨visual input, textual input, task instruction⟩, where the visual input can be an image or video and the instruction is a free-form task prompt. The method produces two outputs: (1) an Embedding-Centric Reasoning (ECR) trace in the format  Final Reasoning, generated autoregressively by the reasoner, and (2) a fixed-dimensional embedding vector obtained by pooling the last-token hidden state of the embedder, which is conditioned on both the original input and the ECR trace. The ECR content is task-adaptive: for VQA it is step-by-step reasoning, for grounding it is a detailed object description with surrounding context, and for document retrieval it is a descriptive summary.

## Method

The Think-Then-Embed (TTE) framework decouples reasoning from embedding: a reasoner MLLM first generates an ECR trace via autoregressive generation, and an embedder MLLM then produces the representation conditioned on both the original multimodal input and the ECR, trained with standard InfoNCE contrastive loss. Three variants are explored: TTE_t uses a large teacher reasoner (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) with a small embedder (Qwen2VL-2B/7B); TTE_s distills the teacher's ECR traces into a same-size student reasoner via supervised fine-tuning (NLL loss), achieving the best open-source performance; and TTE_u unifies both roles in a single backbone by first SFT-ing the model as a reasoner, then freezing it and training a pluggable embedding head (self-initialized multi-head self-attention layers) on top, enabling single-pass inference with roughly half the parameters. A key design finding is that intermediate transformer layers (e.g., 8th-to-last) with a multi-layer MHSA head outperform the final layer for embedding extraction when the backbone is frozen, and that the embedder is robust to imperfect ECRs, learning to extract useful signal even from noisy reasoning traces.

## Results

On MMEB-V2 (78 tasks), TTE_t-7B achieves the highest overall score of 71.5%, surpassing the previous SOTA seed-1.6-embedding (71.3%) and VLM2Vec-V2-7B (61.2%) by +10.3% absolute. Among open-source models, TTE_s-7B achieves 68.6%, a +7.4% gain over VLM2Vec-V2-7B. On MMEB-V1 (36 tasks), TTE_t-7B reaches 78.5% overall, outperforming the next-best open-source model B3 (72.0%) by 6.5% and VLM2Vec-V1 (65.8%) by 12.7%. The 2B variants also show consistent gains: TTE_t-2B scores 68.6% on MMEB-V2 vs. 58.0% for VLM2Vec-V2-2B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct MLLM-based embedding baseline VLM2Vec-V2, TTE delivers substantial gains across all model sizes: TTE_s-7B improves from 61.2% to 68.6% (+7.4%) and TTE_t-7B from 61.2% to 71.5% (+10.3%) on MMEB-V2. On MMEB-V1, TTE_t-7B (78.5%) outperforms B3 (72.0%) by 6.5%, QQMM (72.5%) by 6.0%, and LLaVE (70.3%) by 8.2%, while TTE_s-7B (73.3%) surpasses B3 by 1.3% and VLM2Vec-V1 (65.8%) by 7.5%. The headline SOTA claim is that TTE_t-7B (71.5%) marginally beats the proprietary seed-1.6-embedding (71.3%) trained on massive in-house data, using only a 7B embedder plus a 72B reasoner at inference. Among open-source models, TTE_s-7B (68.6%) is the best, exceeding RzenEmbed-v1-7B (68.9% on MMEB-V2) by a narrow margin and Ops-MM-embedding-v1-7B (67.6%) by 1.0%. Encoder-based baselines (CLIP, SigLIP, UniIR, MagicLens) are far behind, scoring 27.8–44.7% on MMEB-V1, confirming that MLLM-based approaches with reasoning are necessary for instruction-aware multimodal retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based Universal Multimodal Embedding (UME) paradigm established by VLM2Vec and VLM2Vec-V2, which treat MLLMs purely as encoders producing task-specific representations via contrastive learning. The key departure is introducing an explicit Embedding-Centric Reasoning (ECR) step before embedding generation, drawing on the chain-of-thought literature to argue that generative reasoning traces improve representation quality. This is orthogonal to prior improvements in the UME space (hard negative mining, additional training data, architectural changes like causal masking), and while related to query rewriting in text retrieval, it generalizes the idea to multimodal settings where reasoning can be applied to both queries and targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 7B embedder augmented with a 72B reasoner outperforms proprietary models trained on massive in-house datasets suggests that reasoning quality can substitute for embedding model scale on complex multimodal tasks, offering a practical test-time scaling strategy. The unified TTE_u model (reasoner + pluggable embedding head) halves parameters while preserving performance, making the approach more deployable. Open questions include whether RL-based optimization of ECR generation (rather than SFT distillation) yields further gains, how the approach scales to even more complex compositional instructions, and whether the distribution shift between teacher and student reasoners (observed with Gemini2.5 Pro) can be mitigated.
