## Problem

Standard RLHF pipelines train a reward model from preference data and then either learn an online critic (PPO) or estimate values through trajectory sampling (DPO, ReMax, GRPO), yet no new reward signals exist once preference data is collected. This makes the two-stage reward-then-value process informationally redundant, while online critics suffer from drift and sampling-based methods discard token-level credit assignment, leading to high variance and instability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes offline preference trajectories (prompt, response, scalar reward or pairwise preference labels) in the same format used for standard reward model training, requiring no additional annotations. The GVM produces token-level return-to-go values for every position in a generated sequence, which are then aggregated into a single advantage signal per token for policy updates. The final policy outputs aligned text responses, while the frozen GVM simultaneously provides fine-grained per-token value attributions that highlight which tokens drive correctness or error.

## Method

DVPO introduces a two-stage framework: (1) a Global Value Model (GVM) is pretrained offline via temporal-difference learning to predict a policy-conditioned action-value function Q_φ(τ, s, a), where τ is a sampled trajectory that implicitly encodes the policy's behavioral characteristics, and (2) the GVM is frozen and used as a universal critic in a standard clipped PPO objective, eliminating all online critic training. The key theoretical contribution is an equivalence lemma proving that under fixed preference data (no new rewards), reward-model-then-critic training and direct value pretraining induce policy gradients that differ only by a bounded error term vanishing as approximation errors go to zero. Unlike ReMax or GRPO, which assign a single sentence-level reward to all tokens, the GVM's TD-based training assigns distinct values to each token position, enabling fine-grained credit assignment. This design reduces GPU memory by 30–40% and training time by 30–45% relative to PPO while matching or exceeding its performance on MT-Bench, Arena-Hard, and Alpaca-Eval.

## Results

In the Instruction setting (the standard RLHF scenario starting from pre-aligned models), DVPO achieves the best scores across all three benchmarks for both Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 and Llama3-8B-Instruct. On Llama3-8B-Instruct, DVPO reaches 7.72 MT-Bench, 39.2 Arena-Hard, and 42.59 AlpacaEval2, outperforming the strongest baseline (PPO: 7.55 / 36.3 / 34.98) by +0.17, +2.9, and +7.61 respectively. On Mistral-7B, DVPO scores 6.79 / 24.7 / 27.43, beating PPO (6.55 / 19.4 / 19.62) by +0.24, +5.3, and +7.81. DVPO also reduces GPU memory by 23–34% and training time by 32–44% compared to PPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVPO outperforms all four baselines (PPO, DPO, GRPO, ReMax) on every benchmark for both model families. Against PPO (the closest competitor as another actor-critic method), DVPO gains +0.24 MT-Bench and +5.3 Arena-Hard on Mistral-7B, and +0.17 MT-Bench, +2.9 Arena-Hard, and +7.61 AlpacaEval2 on Llama3-8B. Against DPO, DVPO leads by +0.49 MT-Bench and +8.4 Arena-Hard on Mistral-7B, and by +0.71 MT-Bench, +6.6 Arena-Hard, and +2.27 AlpacaEval2 on Llama3-8B. Against GRPO, DVPO wins on all six metric-model combinations, with the largest gap being +5.1 Arena-Hard on Mistral-7B. Against ReMax, DVPO leads by +0.12 MT-Bench and +2.8 Arena-Hard on Mistral-7B, and by +0.10 MT-Bench, +8.4 Arena-Hard, and +6.55 AlpacaEval2 on Llama3-8B. The paper attributes these gains to GVM's token-level credit assignment (via TD learning) versus the sentence-level scalar rewards used by ReMax and GRPO, and to the elimination of critic drift that destabilizes PPO's joint actor-critic training.

## Relation to Prior Work

DVPO departs from the standard RLHF pipeline (reward model → online critic → PPO) by arguing that the two-stage reward-then-value procedure is informationally redundant when no new rewards are available during policy training. It builds on the actor-critic framework of PPO and the TD-learning machinery of classical RL, but replaces the jointly-trained critic with a frozen Global Value Model (GVM) pretrained offline on the same preference data. This positions DVPO between the full actor-critic methods (PPO) and the reward-only or preference-only methods (ReMax, GRPO, DPO), retaining token-level credit assignment while eliminating the online critic. The theoretical equivalence lemma (Lemma 3.1) formalizes the claim that reward-derived and value-derived policy gradients converge under fixed feedback, providing a principled justification for the architectural simplification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for RLHF practitioners: DVPO reduces GPU memory by 30–40% and training time by 30–45% relative to PPO while matching or slightly exceeding its benchmark performance, making large-scale alignment more accessible. The fixed-critic design eliminates the well-known critic-drift instability of PPO, and the token-level value attributions offer interpretability that sentence-level reward methods (ReMax, GRPO) lack. Key limitations remain: the GVM cannot adapt as the policy evolves, so performance may degrade in highly non-stationary settings, and the equivalence guarantee holds only under the no-new-reward constraint. A natural open question is whether periodic GVM refreshes in a semi-online regime can recover adaptivity without reintroducing instability.
