## Problem

Current post-training recipes (instruction-tuning, RLHF, DPO) improve performance on tasks with a single correct answer but systematically degrade three properties critical for tasks with many valid outputs: in-context steerability (using novel in-context information to override priors and match a target distribution), valid output coverage (spanning the full space of acceptable generations), and distributional alignment (matching a target probability mass function). The paper identifies and empirically demonstrates that while instruction-tuning preserves general capability elicitation via ICL, it specifically impairs the model's ability to flexibly steer to novel distributions, a distinction that had not been previously characterized.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural-language task description, an optional input instance (e.g., a survey question), and a sequence of in-context input/output pairs, all formatted with description/input/output role tokens rather than standard chat roles. During training, the sequence is constructed by randomly permuting the in-context examples and applying a 20% description dropout; cross-entropy loss is computed exclusively on output tokens (including a terminal token), with all description and input tokens masked. At evaluation, the model is prompted zero-shot or k-shot and assessed via NLL, multiple-choice accuracy, Jensen-Shannon divergence from a target distribution, and diversity metrics (yield, pairwise uniqueness) over 100 sampled generations.

## Method

SPECTRUM TUNING is a one-epoch supervised fine-tuning procedure over SPECTRUM SUITE (>40 sources, >90 tasks spanning human preferences, synthetic data, random draws, and uncertainty reasoning) that frames each task as a meta-learning problem T_i: X_i, Z_i → P(Y_i), where the target is a full distribution over outputs rather than a single label. For each task, the model sees the description followed by randomly ordered in-context examples, and the cross-entropy loss on Monte Carlo samples from the target distribution encourages a well-calibrated estimate of P(Y_i) in the underfit regime. The model is initialized from pretrained (PT) weights, with only the special format-token embeddings borrowed from the instruction-tuned (IT) model to provide a usable prompt template. This differs from standard instruction SFT in four key ways: multiple i.i.d. outputs appear in-context (encouraging meta-learning), the data is inherently distributional, there is no chat-style framing, and inputs are optional. The paper shows this simple recipe matches or exceeds PT models on steerability and calibration, achieves a Pareto improvement on the diversity–validity frontier, and is the first method to improve distributional alignment over pretrained baselines on held-out tasks.

## Results

On held-out SPECTRUM SUITE test tasks, SPECTRUM TUNING (ST) achieves lower multiple-choice loss than pretrained (PT) models in 14/15 comparisons and matches or improves accuracy in 13/15 cases. On distributional alignment (JS-divergence), ST improves over PT in 10/21 model/dataset comparisons, matches in 10, and degrades in only 1—making it the first method to beat pretrained models on this metric. On the diversity–validity Pareto frontier, ST offers a Pareto or strict improvement in 8/9 model/setting comparisons, with zero-shot yield of 40.5 (Gemma), 32.2 (Qwen), and 37.4 (Llama) unique valid generations versus 6.2, 10.1, and 24.0 for PT and far fewer for IT models. ST also achieves the best expected calibration error (ECE) in 9/15 multiple-choice comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to pretrained (PT) models, SPECTRUM TUNING generally matches or slightly improves in-context steerability (14/15 MC loss comparisons better, 10/15 MC accuracy better or tied) and is the first method to improve distributional alignment over PT (10/21 JS-divergence comparisons better, 10 tied, 1 worse). On the diversity–validity trade-off, ST achieves a Pareto improvement over PT in 8/9 settings, most dramatically in the zero-shot regime where PT validity drops below 20% while ST maintains >60% validity with 50% diversity. Compared to instruction-tuned (IT) models, ST is dramatically superior on all three desiderata: IT models show 3–10× higher NLL loss on steerability tasks, 2–4× higher JS-divergence on distributional alignment, and severe mode collapse (yield of only 5–6 unique valid generations in zero-shot for Gemma/Qwen vs. 32–40 for ST). The two exceptions where IT outperforms ST are chatbot-arena individual preference accuracy (adjacent to chat optimization) and a single free-text loss comparison. Overall, ST recovers the distributional modeling capabilities that standard instruction-tuning degrades, while IT models retain an edge only on narrow chat-style tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by distinguishing 'in-context steerability' (steering to a novel data-generating distribution) from standard in-context learning for capability elicitation, and by showing empirically that instruction-tuning degrades the former while improving the latter. Methodologically, SPECTRUM TUNING is closest to supervised fine-tuning on instruction data (loss on outputs only) and to MetaICL (in-context examples during training), but differs by including multiple i.i.d. outputs per task, training on distributional rather than single-answer data, and using a description/input/output format rather than chat. The paper also builds on the diversity-collapse literature (Zhang et al. 2024a, West & Potts 2025) and distributional alignment work (Meister et al. 2024, Sorensen et al. 2024b), but is the first to propose a post-training method that improves distributional alignment over pretrained baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that instruction-tuning systematically impairs in-context steerability across three model families has direct practical implications: practitioners who need well-calibrated, steerable models for individual modeling, synthetic data generation, or pluralistic alignment may prefer pretrained or SPECTRUM-TUNED models over standard IT models. SPECTRUM SUITE (>40 sources, >90 tasks) is a substantial new evaluation resource for distributional modeling. Key limitations include testing only on ≤14B models, no safety guardrails in the ST models, and the method not being optimized for chat-style interaction. Open questions include the precise mechanism by which instruction-tuning induces strong priors that resist in-context override, whether ST can be combined with chat capabilities, and how the approach scales to larger models.
