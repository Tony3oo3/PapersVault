## Problem

Decision calibration ensures that model predictions yield loss estimates indistinguishable from true losses for downstream decision-makers, but existing guarantees are limited to linear loss functions. When nonlinear losses are handled via feature expansion φ(y) into an m-dimensional space, prior results require sample complexity polynomial in m, making them intractable for the exponentially large or infinite-dimensional feature spaces needed by practical loss classes. The open question is whether decision calibration can be achieved with complexity independent of the feature dimension.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes an initial predictor p₀: X → H constrained to the finite span of feature mappings {φ(y_i)}, i.i.d. labeled samples (x_i, y_i), an RKHS kernel K, and a tolerance ε. It outputs a decision-calibrated predictor p_T maintained implicitly as a linear combination Σ α_ti(x)φ(y_ti) (never materializing the potentially infinite-dimensional vector), along with the associated loss estimator f_{p_T}(x,a,ℓ) = Σ α_ti(x)ℓ(a,y_ti). The predictor is projected onto the Hilbert ball B(R₂) at each iteration to maintain bounded norm.

## Method

The paper first establishes an Ω(√d) sample lower bound for auditing decision calibration under deterministic best response, proving via a shattering argument that two nearly indistinguishable distributions (one calibrated, one miscalibrated) cannot be separated with fewer samples. To overcome this barrier, the authors switch to quantal (smooth) response, where the key insight is a pseudometric that projects high-dimensional loss-vector differences along the random direction given by the predictor, yielding finite covering numbers even in infinite-dimensional RKHS. This enables a dimension-free ERM-based auditing oracle with Õ(|A|³β⁴R₁⁶R₂⁶/ε²) samples. The main algorithm DimFreeDeCal then iteratively patches the predictor using a weighted-calibration template: at each round it identifies a violating loss pair (ℓ_t, ℓ'_t) via the auditor, substitutes an optimal loss ℓ_t* whose coefficient vector lies in the span of φ(y), and updates p_{t+1} = π_{B(R₂)}(p_t + Σ_a d_{ta}·k̃_{ℓ'_t}(x,a)). The algorithm terminates in O(R₁²R₂²/ε²) iterations with Õ(|A|³R₁⁸R₂⁸/ε⁴) total samples, improving the ε-dependence from 1/ε⁶ (Zhao et al.) to 1/ε⁴ while preserving the initial predictor's MSE.

## Results

The paper establishes three key theoretical guarantees. First, a lower bound showing that auditing decision calibration under deterministic best response requires Ω(√d) samples (Theorem 3.1), proving no dimension-free algorithm exists in that setting. Second, under smooth (quantal) best response, a dimension-free auditing algorithm achieves uniform convergence with Õ(|A|³β⁴R₁⁶R₂⁶/ε²) samples, independent of the feature space dimension m (Theorem 4.2). Third, the main algorithm DimFreeDeCal achieves (L_H, K̃_L_H, ε)-decision calibration in T = O(R₁²R₂²/ε²) iterations using Õ(|A|³R₁⁸R₂⁸/ε⁴) samples, while preserving the initial predictor's MSE (Theorem 5.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or datasets, so comparisons are made at the level of sample complexity guarantees. The primary comparison is with Zhao et al. (2021), whose finite-sample decision calibration algorithm under smooth best response in the finite-dimensional setting requires Õ(1/ε⁶) samples; the present paper improves this to Õ(1/ε⁴) while additionally extending to infinite-dimensional RKHS feature spaces. The lower bound (Theorem 3.1) is positioned against the full-calibration lower bound of Gopalan et al. (2024a), but applies to the weaker decision-calibration notion and uses a distinct construction leveraging the geometry of best-response half-spaces and VC shattering. The paper also contrasts its use of RKHS (to represent loss functions) with prior weighted-calibration work (Gopalan et al., 2022b; Blasiok et al., 2023) that uses RKHS for weight functions, noting the roles are fundamentally different and do not directly transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decision calibration framework of Zhao et al. (2021) from linear losses in finite-dimensional outcome spaces to nonlinear losses represented via (potentially infinite-dimensional) separable RKHS feature mappings. The patching algorithm is motivated by the weighted calibration framework of Gopalan et al. (2022b), which the authors show decision calibration is a special case of, but they must develop new techniques (implicit patching, a custom pseudometric for covering numbers) to handle the infinite-dimensional setting. A key departure from prior work is the introduction of a lower bound showing that dimension-free decision calibration is impossible under deterministic best response, necessitating the shift to smooth (quantal) response rules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first statistical lower bound for decision calibration, establishing that the natural strategy of embedding nonlinear losses into high-dimensional feature spaces and applying existing finite-dimensional algorithms is fundamentally intractable. The positive result—that quantal response enables dimension-free auditing and calibration with poly(|A|, 1/ε) samples independent of feature dimension—opens the door to decision-calibrated predictions for broad practical loss classes (piecewise linear, Cobb-Douglas, Lipschitz differentiable) without the curse of dimensionality. Open questions include whether the smooth-response requirement is truly necessary (or whether other relaxations suffice), how the algorithm performs in practice with finite samples, and whether the 1/ε⁴ sample complexity can be further improved.
