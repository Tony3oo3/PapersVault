## Problem

Static medical LLM benchmarks suffer from data contamination (models memorize test items during pretraining, inflating scores) and knowledge obsolescence (clinical guidelines evolve, rendering fixed questions outdated). Moreover, existing evaluations use single-turn, isolated snapshots that fail to capture the sequential, longitudinal reasoning required to navigate a patient's full clinical pathway from initial presentation through long-term management.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263325/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark case presents a progressive clinical scenario with 3–6 sequential 10-option MCQs, where new multimodal evidence (X-ray, MRI, CT, pathology images; lab-result and demographic tables) is introduced at each decision point. Evaluation is conversational and zero-shot: the full Q&A history is retained as context for subsequent questions, forcing the model to integrate evolving information. A case is scored correct only if every sequential question is answered correctly (Case Accuracy), and the benchmark spans 16 ICD-10 chapters with 1,407 cases and 6,605 questions total.

## Method

LiveClin is built through a three-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (Case Construction) programmatically retrieves all PMC Open Access case reports from H1 2025, parses them into structured narratives, and applies a two-stage GPT-4.1 classifier to assign each case to a three-level taxonomy (16 ICD-10 chapters → 72 disease clusters → ICD-10 codes), followed by stratified sampling of 30 cases per cluster. Stage 2 (Exam Generation) uses an o3-powered Generator-Critic architecture: the Generator reframes each report into a progressive multi-stage MCQ set, while the Critic iteratively evaluates clinical accuracy and cognitive complexity, triggering revision loops (up to 10 cycles) until 100% factual correctness and >60% high-complexity questions are achieved. Stage 3 (Quality Check) applies a conservative AI-human workflow: an o3-based Judge agent pre-screens for factual validation and logical solvability, then 239 licensed physicians perform two-phase verification (Annotation by attendings, Inspection by chief/associate-chief physicians) with a revision loop for any discrepancy. The entire set is replaced biannually to maintain contamination resistance and clinical currency.

## Results

On LiveClin (1,407 cases, 6,605 sequential MCQs), the top-performing model o3 achieves a Case Accuracy of only 35.7%, with GPT-5 close behind at 35.5%. Chief Physicians (evaluated on 100 sampled cases) achieve the highest accuracy, followed by Attending Physicians, with both groups surpassing all but the two top proprietary models. Open-source leader InternVL3.5-241B reaches 34.4%, approaching proprietary leaders, while the weakest models (MedGemma-4B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) score below 8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, o3 (35.7%) and GPT-5 (35.5%) lead but both fall short of Chief Physicians and only marginally exceed Attending Physicians. Open-source general-purpose models are closing the gap: InternVL3.5-241B (34.4%) nearly matches GPT-5, and the compact GLM-4.1V-9B (18.8%) surpasses the larger proprietary GPT-4o (14.7%). Notably, newer model versions do not guarantee improvement—Claude 3.5 Sonnet (27.3%) outperforms its successor Claude 3.7 Sonnet (25.6%), and Gemini 2.0 Flash (21.4%) beats Gemini 2.5 Flash (19.0%). Medical-specific models (MedGemma-27B at 15.6%, LingShu-32B at 20.1%, HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B at 9.2%) generally underperform comparable-scale general-purpose VLMs, suggesting that domain fine-tuning alone is insufficient for full clinical pathway reasoning. The overall spread from 35.7% (o3) to 7.5% (MedGemma-4B) underscores the extreme difficulty of sequential, multimodal clinical reasoning on authentic, contemporary cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiveClin adapts the contamination-resistant 'live benchmark' paradigm established by LiveBench and LiveCodeBench to the clinical domain, but extends it substantially by grounding evaluation in authentic, peer-reviewed case reports from PMC rather than synthetic or exam-derived questions. It departs from prior medical QA benchmarks (MedQA, MedMCQA, MedXpertQA) and agent-based clinical simulations (AgentClinic, MedAgentBench) by combining three previously separate requirements: temporal freshness (biannual updates), full sequential clinical pathway simulation (multi-stage, multi-turn), and multimodal content (imaging, tables). The Generator-Critic-Judge AI-human construction pipeline is a novel methodological contribution that the ablation study shows produces more challenging and accurate questions than physician-only authoring at a fraction of the cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest models (o3, GPT-5 at ~35.7% Case Accuracy) trail Chief Physicians, exposing a substantial gap between current AI and expert-level clinical pathway management. The finding that newer model generations do not consistently outperform older ones in clinical reasoning (Claude 3.5 > 3.7, Gemini 2.0 > 2.5) challenges the assumption that general capability scaling automatically transfers to domain-specific expertise, pointing toward the need for targeted medical optimization. Key limitations include geographic and linguistic bias from PMC's publication patterns (underrepresenting low-resource regions) and a skew toward rare conditions inherent to case reports, though the authors show the latter has limited impact on model rankings. Open questions include whether the AI-physician gap will narrow with domain-specific fine-tuning and how to expand the benchmark's global representativeness.
