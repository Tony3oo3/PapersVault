## Problem

As MoE models trend toward higher expert granularity (smaller intermediate dimensions) and higher sparsity (more total experts with constant activated experts), they become increasingly memory-bandwidth-bound with growing activation footprints and wasted compute from Grouped GEMM tile padding. Existing state-of-the-art MoE kernels (ScatterMoE, MoMoE) were not designed to handle these escalating IO costs and tile quantization effects, leading to significant training throughput degradation for fine-grained and sparse MoE configurations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes token embeddings X ∈ R^{T×d}, sparse router scores S, a binary routing mask π ∈ {0,1}^{T×E}, and per-expert weight matrices W1 ∈ R^{E×d×2n} and W2 ∈ R^{E×n×d}, producing the MoE layer output O ∈ R^{T×d} in forward and activation/weight gradients in backward. A key structural constraint is that all GEMM operations use variable-length M dimensions (varlen-M and varlen-K Grouped GEMM) since each expert receives a different number of tokens, and the token rounding routing enforces that per-expert token counts are multiples of a hardware tile size M_tile (e.g., 128) to eliminate padding waste.

## Method

SonicMoE is a hardware-architecture co-design with three components: (1) a memory-efficient algorithm that fuses token gather with HBM-to-SMEM loads and computes dS as ⟨dA′_e,t, A_e,t⟩ (reducing over n) instead of ⟨dO_t, Y_e,t⟩ (reducing over d), eliminating the need to cache Y and gathered X_e, keeping per-layer activation memory at ~2Td + 4TKn bytes independent of expert granularity; (2) IO-aware GPU kernels that overlap MMA with asynchronous IO via Ping-Pong warpgroup scheduling on Hopper and TMEM-based two-stage accumulator pipelines on Blackwell, with epilogue fusion for SwiGLU, dSwiGLU, and joint dH/dS computation; (3) a tile-aware token rounding routing that performs a two-step sort (TC then EC) and rounds each expert's token frequency to the nearest M_tile multiple by either discarding low-score TC tokens or padding EC tokens, bounding per-expert deviation to one tile while eliminating all padding waste in Grouped GEMM.

## Results

SonicMoE reduces per-layer activation memory by 45% compared to ScatterMoE for a fine-grained 7B MoE (n=256), and achieves 1.86× forward compute throughput on H100 GPUs. On B300 (Blackwell) GPUs, SonicMoE delivers a 28.7% forward and 22.1% backward speedup over a highly optimized DeepGEMM baseline on OLMoE-sized 7B MoE models. For token rounding routing, the method achieves up to 16% higher model TFLOPS in highly sparse regimes (128 experts, K=4) while maintaining validation perplexity within 0.1–0.2 of vanilla top-K routing and matching or exceeding average downstream task accuracy across 11 benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ScatterMoE, SonicMoE wins decisively on both memory (45% reduction in per-layer activation for 7B MoE with n=256) and throughput (1.86× forward TFLOPS on H100; 83% backward speedup). Against MoMoE, SonicMoE achieves a larger gap at scale: at 120B, SonicMoE saves >3 GiB per layer vs. MoMoE, and MoMoE suffers significant performance degradation at larger model sizes. Against DeepGEMM++ on B300, SonicMoE achieves 28.7% forward and 22.1% backward speedup on OLMoE-sized 7B MoE, with the gap widening to 35.2%/30.9% at higher granularity (d/n=8). For routing, TR matches or slightly beats TC top-K on validation perplexity (15.92 vs. 15.94) and average task accuracy (50.0 vs. 49.6) at 0.5B scale, while EC routing shows a large train-test gap (val PPL 18.61 vs. train 16.83) that TR avoids entirely. The EC (aux router) baseline degrades substantially (val PPL 21.80, avg accuracy 47.4), confirming that TR is a superior in-place substitute for TC during training without requiring any inference-time adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SonicMoE extends the line of MoE kernel optimization work (ScatterMoE, MoMoE, MegaBlocks, DeepGEMM) by addressing the specific hardware inefficiencies that emerge as MoEs become more fine-grained and sparse—a trend validated by scaling laws (Clark et al., 2022; Krajewski et al., 2024) and observed in frontier models like DeepSeek V3, Kimi K2, and Qwen3. It builds on the Ping-Pong warpgroup scheduling paradigm from FlashAttention 3 (Shah et al., 2024) and CUTLASS's GEMM infrastructure, but departs from prior MoE kernels in two key ways: (1) a redesigned computation graph that avoids caching activations scaling with expert granularity (eliminating Y and dY from HBM), and (2) a tile-aware token rounding routing that eliminates Grouped GEMM padding waste—a problem not addressed by prior routing methods like Rectify-Router or TMA-adaptive FP8 GEMM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As frontier MoE models trend toward higher granularity and sparsity (Table 3), the hardware inefficiency gap between FLOP-optimal and hardware-optimal MoE training will only widen, making SonicMoE's co-design approach increasingly critical. The 45% activation memory reduction and 1.86x throughput improvement are directly applicable to current training pipelines, and the open-sourced CuTe-DSL kernels lower the barrier to adoption. Open questions include extending to low-precision formats (FP8, MXFP8, MXFP4), overlapping communication with computation in expert-parallel distributed settings, and whether the 'quality per compute hour' framing the authors advocate will reshape future MoE architecture design beyond FLOP-based scaling laws.
