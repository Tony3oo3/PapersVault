## Problem

Universal photometric stereo methods (e.g., SDM UniPS, Uni MS-PS) still suffer from two unresolved issues: (1) their encoders fail to explicitly decouple illumination from surface-normal cues, forcing the decoder to perform disentanglement on top of prediction, and (2) conventional downsampling and pixel-shuffle operations discard high-frequency geometric detail. The paper identifies a direct correlation between encoder feature consistency (CSIM/SSIM) and final normal accuracy, motivating an architecture that produces a unified, illumination-invariant feature representation.

## Input / Output

The method takes F multi-light RGB images (typically 3–6 during training, up to 96 for DiLiGenT evaluation) captured from a single fixed viewpoint under arbitrary, unknown lighting, at 512×512 resolution. It outputs a full-resolution surface normal map (H×W×3); when finetuned for PBR prediction, it additionally produces albedo (3-ch), metallic (1-ch), and roughness (1-ch) maps. The decoder operates via a pixel-sampling Transformer on m=2048 randomly selected pixels, keeping memory constant regardless of image resolution and enabling 4K inference in ~85 s on an H100.

## Method

LINO UniPS is a ViT-based encoder-decoder with three encoder stages. First, a Wavelet Feature Extractor decomposes each input image into a downsampled copy and four wavelet sub-bands (LL, LH, HL, HH), converting both into patch token sequences (patch size 8, dim 384). Second, a Light Registered Attention Module prepends three learnable Light Register Tokens (point, direction, environment) to each token sequence and processes them through cascaded Interleaved Attention Blocks following a Frame → Light → Global → Light pattern; the Global attention attends to all tokens across all lighting conditions simultaneously, enabling holistic illumination aggregation. The register tokens are supervised via a Light Alignment loss (cosine similarity to encoded light-source parameters from the synthetic training data), explicitly anchoring each token to its physical illumination type. Third, a Wavelet Aggregator fuses the downsample and wavelet-branch features via a DPT-style top-down pyramid and an inverse wavelet transform, producing the unified encoder output. Training uses a Normal-gradient Perception Loss that weights reconstruction error by an exponential confidence map derived from the predicted normal gradient, plus the Light Alignment loss, with adaptive loss weighting (auxiliary terms scaled to 0.1× the primary loss). The model is trained with curriculum learning on the new PS-Verse dataset (100,000 Blender-rendered scenes graded into five geometric-complexity levels, including normal-mapped Level 5), progressing from simple to complex scenes over ~200 epochs on 2×H100 GPUs.

## Results

On DiLiGenT (96 input images), LINO UniPS achieves a new SOTA average MAE of 4.65°, outperforming Uni MS-PS (5.01°) and SDM UniPS (5.80°). On Luces (52 input images), it reaches 9.43° MAE, a substantial improvement over Uni MS-PS (11.21°) and SDM UniPS (13.50°). Feature consistency metrics (CSIM 0.90, SSIM 0.91 on PS-Mix) also surpass all baselines, confirming the encoder's superior illumination decoupling. Inference is ~1.7 s for 16 images at 512×612, roughly 20× faster than Uni MS-PS (35.3 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LINO UniPS outperforms all four baselines on both public benchmarks. Against UniPS (Ikehata 2022), the improvement is dramatic: 4.65° vs 14.70° MAE on DiLiGenT and 9.43° vs 23.77° on Luces, reflecting the shift from a single-encoder to a multi-light global-context paradigm. Against SDM UniPS (Ikehata 2023), LINO UniPS wins by 1.15° on DiLiGenT and 4.07° on Luces, while also achieving higher feature consistency (CSIM 0.90 vs 0.84; SSIM 0.91 vs 0.72) and slightly faster inference (85.1 s vs 92.7 s at 4K). Against Uni MS-PS (Hardy 2024), the gains are 0.36° on DiLiGenT and 1.78° on Luces, with a ~20× speed advantage (1.7 s vs 35.3 s at 512×612). Against IDArb (Li 2025), compared only on PBR material prediction (Table A1), LINO UniPS dominates across all outputs: Normal MAE 4.51° vs 29.51°, Albedo PSNR 30.49 dB vs 25.11 dB, Metallic PSNR 32.99 dB vs 25.62 dB, and Roughness PSNR 31.32 dB vs 24.85 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Universal Photometric Stereo line initiated by Ikehata (UniPS 2022, SDM UniPS 2023) and Hardy et al. (Uni MS-PS 2024), which replaced explicit illumination models with learnable encoder-decoder architectures. The key conceptual departure is making the encoder explicitly responsible for illumination-geometry decoupling via supervised Light Register Tokens (inspired by DINO registers and VAVAE/REPA feature alignment) and a global cross-image attention mechanism (inspired by VGGT), rather than leaving disentanglement to the decoder. For high-frequency preservation, it introduces a wavelet dual-branch architecture and a gradient-weighted perception loss, and contributes the PS-Verse dataset built from Objaverse and MatSynth with PBR materials and normal mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated correlation between encoder feature consistency (CSIM/SSIM) and reconstruction accuracy provides a useful diagnostic for evaluating Universal PS encoders, and the finding that a simple MLP decoder paired with the LINO encoder outperforms SDM UniPS's full decoder validates the encoder-centric design philosophy. PS-Verse (100K scenes, 17,805 shapes, PBR ground truth, normal-mapping level) is a substantial practical resource that could benefit the broader inverse-rendering community. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly unconstrained real-world lighting beyond the synthetic training distribution, whether the curriculum learning schedule is essential or merely helpful, and whether the Light Alignment supervision can be replaced by self-supervised alternatives for deployment without ground-truth lighting annotations.
