## Problem

In LLM reinforcement finetuning, uniform task sampling wastes computation on tasks that are already mastered or beyond reach, while existing online selection methods either incur high rollout costs for information gathering or rely on a single evidence source (explicit or implicit) without principled fusion. The paper identifies a clear complementarity: explicit evidence from direct evaluations is stable but suffers slow warm-up, while implicit evidence from inter-task correlations accelerates early selection but degrades in reliability over time, leaving information underutilized in both cases.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed pool of N tasks with binary reward functions (correct/incorrect), the current LLM parameters, and pre-computed success rates from two reference models of distinct capability (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as weak and Qwen3-30B-A3B as strong) on all tasks. At each training step it outputs a selected batch of tasks for RFT and updated Beta posterior parameters (α, β) for every task in the pool. The reference-model scores are a one-time offline cost; the online overhead of the interpolation-based estimator is negligible (≤0.2% of total training time) since all updates reduce to simple vector operations with no extra rollouts.

## Method

BOTS maintains a Beta posterior over each task's success probability and updates it via a generalized Bayesian rule that fuses two evidence sources: explicit counts from direct rollouts of selected tasks and pseudo-counts from an ultra-light interpolation-based estimator for unselected tasks. The interpolation plugin computes a relative capability coefficient μ by locating the current model between two reference models on the current batch, then linearly interpolates between the reference models' per-task scores (with momentum smoothing) to predict difficulty without any extra rollouts. A discounting factor λ interpolates the posterior back toward the prior to handle non-stationarity, while ρ balances explicit versus implicit evidence contributions. Task selection uses Thompson sampling from the Beta posteriors, choosing tasks whose sampled success probability is closest to a target p*=0.5, thereby balancing exploration and exploitation. Proposition 1 proves the update preserves the Beta family, and Proposition 2 characterizes the bounded effective sample size as a function of λ and ρ.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, BOTS (λ=0.1, ρ=0.1) achieves 10 first-place and 6 second-place finishes out of 18 reported metrics across Math, Code, and Logic domains, with a 28% training acceleration in Math (TTB(100%)=0.72) and 43% in Code (TTB(100%)=0.77). On Qwen2.5-7B, BOTS secures 6 first-place and 8 second-place finishes, including a remarkable 50% acceleration in Logic (TTB(100%)=0.50). The computational overhead of BOTS is negligible at ≤0.2% of total training time, as the interpolation-based implicit evidence requires no extra rollouts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOTS consistently outperforms all four baselines (Random, Offline, BOTS-MoPPS, BOTS-DOTS) in aggregate across both model scales. Against the purely explicit-evidence baseline BOTS-MoPPS (reducing to MoPPS/Qu et al. 2025), BOTS wins decisively: on 1.5B Math, BOTS achieves TTB(100%)=0.72 vs. 0.89 for MoPPS, and on 7B Logic, BOTS reaches TTB(100%)=0.50 vs. 0.69 for MoPPS, confirming that explicit evidence alone suffers from slow cold-start. Against the purely implicit-evidence proxy BOTS-DOTS (inspired by DOTS/Sun et al. 2025), BOTS is competitive but slightly ahead on most metrics; BOTS-DOTS is the strongest single baseline, validating the interpolation-based estimator's value. The Offline curriculum (easy-to-hard) is consistently outperformed by BOTS, particularly in Code and Logic where adaptivity matters most. The external baseline DAPO (Dynamic Sampling, Yu et al. 2025) is compared in Appendix G.5, where BOTS also demonstrates superiority, as DAPO incurs substantial extra rollout costs for oversampling-based filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOTS unifies and extends several threads of prior work on task selection for LLM RFT: offline curricula (Parashar et al., 2025; Shen et al., 2025), oversampling-based filtering (DAPO, Yu et al., 2025), non-stationary bandit formulations using only explicit evidence (MoPPS, Qu et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025b), and attention-kernel-based implicit estimation (DOTS, Sun et al., 2025). The key departure is a principled Bayesian posterior that fuses explicit and implicit evidence through a single tempered-likelihood update, combined with Thompson sampling for selection. The interpolation-based plug-in for implicit evidence is deliberately trivial (linear interpolation between two reference models with momentum), contrasting with the embedding-based attention kernels of DOTS, and requires no extra rollouts at training time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a drop-in, near-zero-overhead (<0.2%) improvement for any GRPO-based RFT pipeline, with consistent gains across math, code, and logic domains at both 1.5B and 7B scales. The empirical demonstration that implicit evidence is critical for cold-start while explicit evidence dominates long-term accuracy provides a clear design principle for future task-selection methods. Open questions include extending to non-binary rewards, developing adaptive λ/ρ schedules that shift emphasis across training stages, and replacing the simple interpolator with stronger (but still lightweight) plug-ins that better capture non-linear inter-task relationships.
