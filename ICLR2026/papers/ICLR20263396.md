## Problem

ICU multivariate time series are irregularly sampled with pervasive missingness, yet clinical deployment demands both high predictive accuracy and transparent explanations. Existing approaches either handle irregularity (e.g., GRU-D, Latent-ODEs) but rely on post-hoc explainers that add computation and yield inconsistent attributions, or are inherently interpretable (e.g., RETAIN, shapelet models) but sacrifice accuracy or assume regular sampling. No prior method simultaneously models inter-signal dependencies during missingness handling and provides built-in, observation-level explanations without degrading performance.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a K×T matrix of heterogeneous clinical signals (vital signs, lab tests, interventions) aligned to discrete time steps, paired with a binary K×T observation mask indicating which entries are truly observed versus missing. Outputs include a class prediction (binary mortality or sepsis), a K×T attribution map assigning salience to each individual observation, and reconstructed values for missing entries. Attribution is computed by backward-tracing learned attention weights and graph edges through the forward computation, requiring no external explainer.

## Method

GARLIC is a three-stage architecture: (1) a latent feature modeling stage applies per-signal exponential-decay imputation (with learnable decay rates) and signal-specific MLP encoders to produce embeddings that preserve both value and missingness information; (2) a time-lagged graph message passing stage extracts a local temporal window via signal-wise lag attention, then propagates information across signals through a learnable K×K adjacency matrix regularized by L1 sparsity; (3) a cross-dimensional sequential attention stage applies signal-level attention at each timestep, feeds the result through a GRU for local temporal continuity, and applies temporal self-attention for long-range dependencies before a classification head. Training uses an alternating decoupled optimization strategy (inspired by DeFRCN) that alternates between updating the shared backbone (imputation + graph) with a composite reconstruction/classification loss and updating the classifier alone, preventing gradient interference between the auxiliary reconstruction and primary prediction objectives.

## Results

GARLIC sets new state-of-the-art on all three ICU benchmarks: P12 (AUROC 86.40%, AUPRC 56.89%), P19 (AUROC 90.96%, AUPRC 55.29%), and MIMIC-III (AUROC 90.09%, AUPRC 64.85%), outperforming the strongest baselines by 1.5–8.7 AUROC points and 4.4–6.3 AUPRC points. The improvement is most pronounced on P12, which has the highest missingness (~95%), where GARLIC gains 1.5 AUROC and 6.2 AUPRC over the next-best model (Warpformer). On the broader Human Activity 7-class task, GARLIC achieves 91.21% accuracy and 84.70% AUPRC, surpassing WarpFormer (89.28%/80.60%) and MTSFormer (89.24%/79.14%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GARLIC outperforms all 15 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest irregularity-aware models, GARLIC beats Warpformer (the best baseline) by 1.52 AUROC / 6.27 AUPRC on P12, 1.01 AUROC / 1.19 AUPRC on P19, and 0.92 AUROC / 3.33 AUPRC on MIMIC-III. Among ODE-based methods, ODE-RNN is the closest competitor on P19 (89.97 AUROC) and MIMIC-III (88.07 AUROC), but GARLIC still leads by 0.99 and 2.02 AUROC respectively. Graph-based baselines MTGNN and RAINDROP underperform substantially (e.g., MTGNN scores only 80.95 AUROC on P12), confirming that static or weakly-learned graphs are insufficient compared to GARLIC's time-lagged dynamic graph. Among interpretable models, GARLIC dominates: RETAIN (83.08 AUROC P12), DARNN (79.84 AUROC P12), and IMV-LSTM (84.02 AUROC P12) all trail by 2.4–6.6 AUROC points while also showing weaker attribution fidelity in the ROAR perturbation tests (Table 2). On the non-clinical Human Activity 7-class task, GARLIC (91.21% accuracy) surpasses WarpFormer (89.28%) and MTSFormer (89.24%), though on PYRREGULAR's mobility and single-signal subsets where inter-signal dependencies are weak, GARLIC underperforms ROCKET, confirming its design advantage is most pronounced in domains with rich multivariate interactions.

## Relation to Prior Work

GARLIC synthesizes three established threads—exponential-decay imputation from GRU-D (Che et al., 2018), attention-based interpretability from RETAIN and IMV-LSTM, and graph-based inter-signal modeling from Raindrop and MTGNN—into a single end-to-end architecture. Its key conceptual departure is applying signal-level attention at each timestep *before* any recurrent fusion (unlike RETAIN's uniform aggregation or IMV-LSTM's post-recurrence attention), and learning the inter-signal graph with L1 sparsity rather than relying on fixed or heuristically defined edges as in Raindrop. The alternating decoupled optimization strategy is borrowed from DeFRCN (Qiao et al., 2021) in object detection and repurposed here to resolve gradient conflict between auxiliary reconstruction and primary classification objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GARLIC provides a practical, self-interpretable model for irregular ICU time series that achieves SOTA accuracy while producing observation-level attributions validated through rigorous ROAR-style perturbation tests with formal statistical equivalence and monotonicity checks—addressing a real gap where prior interpretable models (RETAIN, IMV-LSTM) sacrifice accuracy or assume regular sampling. Open questions include whether the learned time-lagged graphs can be validated by clinical experts, whether the fixed-window and discrete-lag design can be extended to continuous-time or streaming settings, and whether the framework generalizes to multi-class or forecasting tasks. The paper also acknowledges that performance degrades on domains with weak inter-signal correlations, limiting its universality.
