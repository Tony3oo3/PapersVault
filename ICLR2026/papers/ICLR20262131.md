## Problem

Tree-Sliced Wasserstein (TSW) distance incorporates both directional and positional information through its tree-based construction, unlike Sliced Wasserstein which relies solely on directional projections. However, existing TSW variants use heuristic sampling for the positional component (the tree intersection point), typically a Gaussian centered at the data mean, which fails to fully exploit the positional information that distinguishes TSW from SW. This paper identifies the Fermat-Weber problem as the principled framework for choosing the intersection point, since the non-compactness of R^d precludes a canonical uniform distribution over positions.

## Input / Output

The method takes two discrete probability measures (sets of n and m support points in R^d) and produces a scalar transport cost. The sampling distribution is data-dependent: it is constructed from the combined source and target point sets, ensuring symmetry. In downstream applications, the distance serves as a loss for gradient flow (evolving a source distribution toward a target), as a KL-replacement in VAE-based topic models (producing topic proportions), and as an AGME loss component in DDGAN training (producing generated CIFAR-10 images at 32×32).

## Method

FW-TSW replaces the heuristic positional sampling in TSW with a data-dependent distribution centered at the geometric median of the combined source and target points, computed via Weiszfeld's algorithm (a fixed number of iterations, T=100). Tree intersection points are then drawn from a Gaussian N(x*, cI_d) centered at this geometric median, while line directions are sampled uniformly from S^{d-1}. An enhanced variant, FW-TSW*, further biases directional sampling toward source-target point differences via a random-path construction θ = (ψ + ζ·s·(x_i − y_j))/‖·‖, where ψ is uniform on the sphere and s is a random sign ensuring symmetry. The splitting map uses a distance-based softmax over the k lines in each tree system. Theoretically, FW-TSW is shown to be a semi-metric (satisfying non-negativity, symmetry, identity of indiscernibles, and a quasi-triangle inequality), invariant under Euclidean transformations, and admits a tractable upper bound involving W_2(μ,ν) and the Fermat-Weber objective value. The additional computational cost over standard TSW is only O(Tnd) for the Weiszfeld iterations, which is negligible relative to the dominant O(Lkn log n) sorting cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 unconditional generation, FW-TSW*-DD achieves a FID of 2.315, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art Db-TSW-DD⊥ (2.53) by 0.215 FID and the original DDGAN (3.64) by 1.32 FID, while maintaining comparable training time (87s/epoch vs. 85s). On the 25 Gaussians gradient flow task, FW-TSW* attains the lowest Wasserstein-1 distance of 8.29e-07 at step 2500, outperforming Db-TSW (8.55e-07) and TSW-SL (8.76e-07). For topic modeling, FW*-TSW-TM achieves the highest C_V coherence on all three benchmarks: 0.511 (DBLP), 0.502 (M10), and 0.801 (BBC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FW-TSW* decisively outperforms all baselines on CIFAR-10 FID, beating the strongest prior OT-based method Db-TSW-DD⊥ (Tran et al., 2025a) by 0.215 FID (2.315 vs. 2.53) and the best SW-based method IWRPSW-DD (Nguyen et al., 2024b) by 0.385 FID (2.315 vs. 2.70). On the 25 Gaussians gradient flow task, FW-TSW* achieves the best W1 distance at step 2500 (8.29e-07), narrowly edging out Db-TSW (8.55e-07) and TSW-SL (8.76e-07), while SW (1.01e-03) and LCVSW (6.84e-04) remain orders of magnitude worse. For topic modeling, FW*-TSW-TM achieves the highest C_V coherence on all three datasets (DBLP 0.511, M10 0.502, BBC 0.801), surpassing the best SW-based baseline EBRPSW-TM (0.416/0.492/0.777) and the best TSW-based baseline TSW-SL-TM (0.453/0.456/0.796). The original DDGAN (Xiao et al., 2021) without any OT component scores 3.64 FID, confirming that the OT-based slicing strategies provide substantial gains. Training times for FW-TSW variants (85–87s/epoch) are comparable to Db-TSW variants (84–85s), indicating the geometric median computation via Weiszfeld's algorithm adds negligible overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tree-Sliced Wasserstein (TSW) framework (Tran et al., 2024e; 2025a) by replacing its heuristic positional sampling (Gaussian centered at the data mean) with a principled geometric-median-based scheme drawn from the classical Fermat-Weber problem in location theory. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that TSW's intersection point encodes positional information that prior variants underutilized, and that Weiszfeld's algorithm provides a natural, low-cost way to center the tree system near the data. The enhanced variant FW-TSW* further introduces data-dependent directional sampling analogous to random-path projecting directions in SW (Nguyen et al., 2024b), unifying positional and directional informativeness within a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric-median sampling yields a tractable upper bound (Theorem 4.5) that was unavailable for prior TSW variants due to uncontrolled positional information, providing theoretical grounding for the method's behavior. Empirically, FW-TSW* achieves the best FID (2.315) on CIFAR-10 among OT-based DDGAN variants, a ~0.2 improvement over the previous best (Db-TSW-DD⊥ at 2.53), with negligible runtime overhead. A key open limitation, shared with all TSW variants, is the absence of explicit transport maps, which the authors flag as future work. The approach also opens the door to other location-theoretic objectives (e.g., weighted or robust medians) as sampling priors for tree systems.
