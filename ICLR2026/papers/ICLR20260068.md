## Problem

Inference-time scaling for reasoning LLMs wastes substantial compute on unproductive reasoning traces, yet no training-free method reliably identifies which traces will succeed before they complete. Prior approaches rely on surface-level text heuristics, output-distribution confidence, or trained verifiers, all of which either trade accuracy for simplicity or incur additional inference cost. The paper asks whether the temporal evolution of a model's internal hidden states during reasoning contains predictive signals about final-answer correctness that can be exploited for more efficient and accurate test-time scaling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260068/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes token-level hidden states from all transformer layers at each position within the intermediate reasoning trace (delimited by special tokens), which are coarse-grained into non-overlapping segments of 500 tokens and averaged to produce segment-level representations. From these, three scalar scores per trace are produced: Net Change, Cumulative Change, and Aligned Change. These scores feed two downstream decisions: (i) an end-of-trace answer selection rule that accepts a candidate early if its score crosses a calibrated threshold (otherwise falling back to majority vote), and (ii) an early path selection rule at 2k tokens that uses a lightweight random forest over partial-trace signals to pick one of five parallel candidates for completion.

## Method

The core contribution is a family of three training-free trajectory signals computed from the temporal evolution of segment-averaged hidden states. Net Change measures the L2 norm of the drift vector (last segment minus first segment) normalized by segment count, capturing overall representational displacement. Cumulative Change sums the L2 norms of consecutive-segment update vectors, quantifying total path length through latent space. Aligned Change computes the mean cosine similarity between each update vector and the overall drift vector, measuring whether intermediate steps progress coherently toward the final state. Each signal is computed per layer and averaged across layers to yield a single score. The key empirical finding is that correct traces exhibit larger Net and Aligned Change (more directed, substantial representational shifts) and smaller Cumulative Change (less wandering), yielding ROC-AUCs of 0.71–0.74 versus 0.44–0.67 for cross-layer and output-distribution baselines. These signals are then deployed in two inference policies: a sequential threshold-based early-accept rule that reduces token usage by up to 70% while improving accuracy over majority vote, and a 2k-token early path selection using a random forest that saves 61% of tokens on average while matching or exceeding five-sample majority-vote accuracy.

## Results

LT signals achieve ROC-AUC of 0.71–0.74 (Net, Cumulative, Aligned Change) for distinguishing correct from incorrect reasoning traces, substantially outperforming cross-layer geometry (0.58–0.67) and output-distribution measures (0.44–0.59). In multi-sample inference, LT-guided answer selection reduces token usage by 48–70% relative to majority vote while improving accuracy by an average of +2.46% (range −1.4 to +14.1%). Early path selection at 2k tokens saves 61.2% of tokens on average while maintaining or improving accuracy by +2.49% over MV@5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against majority vote (Wang et al., 2023), LT-guided selection matches or exceeds MV@5 accuracy in most settings (e.g., +5.2% for R1-D on AIME2025, +14.1% for Qwen3) while reducing token usage by 48–70%, making it strictly superior on the accuracy-efficiency frontier. Against ST-BoN (Wang et al., 2025) for early path selection, LT matches or exceeds accuracy in nearly all model-dataset settings (e.g., +2.0% for Phi4R+ on GPQA vs. −4.0% for ST-BoN) while delivering comparable token savings (~65–72%), and is computationally cheaper since it operates on a single sample's latent trajectory rather than pairwise cross-layer distances. Against cross-layer signals (Wang et al., 2024), LT signals achieve consistently higher ROC-AUC (0.71–0.74 vs. 0.58–0.67) with lower variance across models and domains. Against output-distribution measures (Yona et al., 2022; Kadavath et al., 2022), LT signals are dramatically stronger: Logit Margin, Entropy, and Perplexity yield ROC-AUC of 0.44–0.59 (often at or below chance), whereas all three LT signals remain above 0.70. Against the Shortest@5 heuristic (Hassid et al., 2025), LT signals are more reliable: Shortest@5 reduces accuracy by an average of 1.4% (with drops up to −10% on AIME2025 for Phi4R+), while LT-Combined maintains or improves accuracy across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the hidden-state probing literature (which has primarily examined spatial/cross-layer structure or static snapshots) by shifting to a temporal perspective: tracking how representations evolve across tokens within a reasoning trace. It departs from Wang et al. (2024), who analyzed representational curvature across layers for answer prediction, by instead measuring the geometry of the trajectory itself (drift, cumulative path length, alignment with the final state). The approach is also distinct from output-distribution confidence measures (Kadavath et al., 2022; Yona et al., 2022) and trained verifier models, as it requires no additional training, no external model, and operates purely on internal activations already produced during decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a training-free, model-agnostic signal that reduces token usage by up to 70% while matching or improving accuracy over majority voting is directly deployable in inference pipelines for reasoning models. The finding that these signals emerge within the first 4k tokens opens the door to early compute allocation strategies that could reshape how multi-sample inference is orchestrated. Key open questions remain: the signals are predictive rather than causal, so the underlying mechanisms by which latent trajectories encode reasoning quality are not yet understood; whether LT-derived metrics can serve as training-time regularizers to shape more productive trajectories is unexplored; and whether learned classifiers or ensembles over these signals can push the moderate ROC-AUC values (~0.71–0.74) toward more reliable discrimination.
