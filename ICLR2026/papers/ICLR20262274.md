## Problem

Determining whether a suspect LLM is derived from an existing base model or genuinely trained from scratch is critical for intellectual property protection, yet no existing fingerprinting method simultaneously survives all major post-training modifications (SFT, continued pretraining up to 5.5T tokens, RL, multimodal extension, pruning, and upcycling) while maintaining a near-zero false-positive rate. Prior methods like HuRef lose discriminative power under heavy continued pretraining, and REEF produces dangerously high similarity scores for unrelated models, risking false accusations of theft.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the word embedding matrices (restricted to the shared vocabulary intersection) and the Q and K projection matrices from every Transformer layer of two LLMs, requiring no forward passes, no test inputs, and no model modification. It outputs a single scalar similarity score (the mean of absolute UCKA values across all layers and Q/K matrices) that separates related pairs (scores >96%) from independent pairs (scores <1.5%), with the full computation completing in under 30 seconds on a single NVIDIA 3090 GPU.

## Method

AWM is a training-free, two-stage fingerprinting procedure grounded in a theoretical characterization of which weight manipulations can preserve a Transformer's outputs. Stage 1 recovers the permutation matrix P and signature matrix D from word embeddings by solving a Linear Assignment Problem (Hungarian algorithm) on the absolute cosine-similarity matrix of shared-vocabulary token columns, exploiting the fact that RMSNorm commutes only with constant scaling, permutation, and sign-flip transformations (Theorem 4.3). Stage 2 computes the Unbiased Centered Kernel Alignment (UCKA) between the P,D-transformed Q and K matrices of each layer pair, leveraging CKA's invariance to orthogonal transformations and constant rescaling (Theorem 3.1) to neutralize the RoPE-compatible orthogonal rotations that can alter Q/K weights without changing attention scores (Theorem 4.4). The final score is the mean of |UCKA| values across all layers and both Q and K matrices, requiring no learned parameters and no architectural assumptions beyond the standard decoder-only Transformer with RoPE and RMSNorm.

## Results

On a testbed of 150 model pairs (60 positive base-offspring, 90 negative independent), AWM achieves perfect AUC = 1.0, pAUC (FPR < 0.05) = 1.0, and TPR@1%FPR = 1.0 across all six post-training categories (SFT, continued pretraining up to 5.5T tokens, upcycling, multi-modal extension, RL, and pruning). The average absolute Z-score separation is 302.9, roughly 10× higher than HuRef (28.1) and ~235× higher than REEF (1.3). For the 90 independent pairs, AWM yields mean similarity of only 0.49% (7B) and 0.26% (13B), compared to 42.5%/47.4% for REEF and 3.6%/2.2% for HuRef, indicating near-zero false-positive risk. The full computation completes in under 30 seconds on a single NVIDIA 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AWM decisively outperforms all three baselines on every metric and every post-training category. HuRef (Zeng et al., 2024) is the strongest baseline but degrades under heavy modifications: its AUC drops to 0.879 under continued pretraining and 0.866 under pruning, with TPR@1%FPR falling to 0.500 for CPT. REEF (Zhang et al., 2025) collapses entirely on upcycling and multi-modal extension, recording pAUC = 0.0 and TPR@1%FPR = 0.0 in both settings, and its high false-positive rates (mean similarity >42% for independent pairs) make it practically unusable. Intrinsic Fingerprint (Yoon et al., 2025) shows similar fragility to REEF, with TPR@1%FPR dropping to 0.300 under CPT and 0.400 under pruning. AWM is the only method achieving perfect 1.0 scores on AUC, pAUC, and TPR@1%FPR simultaneously across all six post-training categories, while maintaining a separation margin (|Z| ≈ 303) an order of magnitude above the next-best method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CKA similarity framework (Kornblith et al., 2019) and the unbiased HSIC estimator (Song et al., 2007), combining them with a Linear Assignment Problem solved via the Hungarian algorithm to recover permutation and sign-flip manipulations from word embeddings. The theoretical characterization of admissible weight manipulations (Theorems 4.3–4.4) extends the component-wise analysis from HuRef (Zeng et al., 2024), which established that residual connections propagate manipulations but did not fully constrain the Q/K matrices under RoPE. The key departure from both HuRef and REEF is using CKA's invariance to orthogonal transformations as a parameter-free similarity metric, avoiding the need to explicitly reconstruct rotation matrices that could have d² nuisance parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical, training-free tool for LLM IP verification that runs in 30 seconds on a single GPU and achieves perfect classification metrics across six diverse post-training regimes, including 5.5T-token continued pretraining. The near-zero false-positive risk (max similarity 1.5% for independent models vs. REEF's 95%+) is critical for preventing wrongful accusations of model theft. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to models with substantially different architectures or vocabularies, whether it can be extended to black-box settings, and whether the characterized manipulation space (cPD + orthogonal) is truly exhaustive against more sophisticated adversarial attacks. The white-box requirement also limits applicability to API-only model access.
