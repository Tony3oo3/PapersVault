## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based avatar methods from monocular videos rely on skeletal linear blend skinning of parametric naked-body templates, which fails to capture secondary motion (inertia-driven cloth dynamics) in loose garments and produces artifacts like garment splitting and needle-like distortions. Two specific gaps are identified: (1) deformation is computed as a per-frame function of the current pose with no temporal context, and (2) Gaussian point initialization from a minimally-clothed template mismatches the actual geometry of loose clothing, causing undersampling and silhouette distortion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB video (3–10 minutes, captured on a smartphone) and a sequence of SMPL joint poses as driving signals. It produces a fixed set of ~40k 3D Gaussian primitives (downsampled from a denser canonical set) whose positions, covariances, colors, and opacities are updated autoregressively per frame, rendered via a differentiable Gaussian splatting rasterizer into novel-pose or novel-view video frames. The Gaussian count N is fixed across time; only their parameters evolve, and the graph structure (k=16 nearest neighbors with Gaussian-kernel edge weights) is built once from the canonical positions.

## Method

SMAGA is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (Personalized Gaussian Initialization) trains a deformable NeRF on the input video to recover a time-averaged canonical density field, from which dense canonical Gaussians are extracted by thresholding and voxel clustering—bypassing naked-body templates entirely. Stage 2 (SMAD) structures these Gaussians into a k-NN graph with Gaussian-kernel adjacency, encodes each node with its position, a buffer of τ_v=11 past velocity vectors (finite-difference), and an MLP embedding of a window of SMPL poses, then runs a 3-layer message-passing GNN with GRU update cells that autoregressively predicts per-node acceleration as a neural surrogate for a mass-spring-damper system. The decoder outputs position offsets, velocity refinements (re-queued into the buffer), log-diagonal covariance updates, and color residuals. Training minimizes L1 photometric loss plus an isometry regularizer (preserving local geodesic distances to prevent cloth stretching) and a velocity-damping regularizer (suppressing high-frequency vibration), with λ_iso=0.1 and λ_damp=0.01.

## Results

On the 4D-Dress novel pose synthesis benchmark (the primary evaluation for loose-clothing dynamics), SMAGA achieves PSNR 24.74–29.98, SSIM 0.9548–0.9700, and LPIPS 0.0301–0.0443 across five subjects, outperforming the strongest baseline (3DGS-Avatar) by up to +2.19 PSNR and −0.0124 LPIPS. On ZJU-MoCap novel view synthesis, SMAGA reaches PSNR 28.86–33.86, surpassing 3DGS-Avatar by up to +0.53 PSNR. On the newly introduced in-the-wild LoCo-Human dataset, SMAGA leads all baselines by 1.35–3.58 PSNR, confirming robustness under uncontrolled conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMAGA outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and metric. On 4D-Dress (the most relevant benchmark for loose-clothing dynamics), it beats the closest competitor 3DGS-Avatar by +1.95 to +2.44 PSNR per subject, and GART by +1.95 to +3.95 PSNR; ExAvatar and GaussianAvatar trail by even larger margins (up to +4.5 PSNR for GaussianAvatar on subject 00190). On ZJU-MoCap novel view synthesis, SMAGA leads 3DGS-Avatar by +0.35 to +0.53 PSNR and ARAH by +0.87 to +1.36 PSNR, while also surpassing the NeRF-based baselines (HumanNeRF, NeuralBody, MonoHuman) by 0.5–2.5 PSNR. On the in-the-wild LoCo-Human dataset, SMAGA achieves 24.83–26.44 PSNR versus 22.59–25.07 for ExAvatar and 22.59–24.21 for 3DGS-Avatar, a consistent 1.35–3.58 PSNR advantage. Statistical significance testing (paired t-tests, p < 0.005) confirms all improvements over baselines are significant, and ablation p-values (p < 0.001) validate each proposed component's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for rendering, D-NeRF for canonical shape extraction, and classical mass-spring-damper physics for deformation modeling, but departs sharply from the dominant paradigm in 3DGS avatar methods (GART, GaussianAvatar, 3DGS-Avatar, ExAvatar) that rely on SMPL template initialization and linear blend skinning. The key conceptual departure is replacing template-bound, pose-conditioned deformation with a template-free, autoregressive GNN deformer on a velocity-encoded Gaussian graph that learns second-order dynamics directly from monocular video. It also positions itself against the broader cloth-dynamics literature (HOOD, SNUG, bone-driven motion networks) by operating entirely in the 3DGS primitive space without requiring 3D ground-truth meshes, cloth-body segmentation, or explicit colliders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and visually obvious failure mode of current 3DGS avatars—unrealistic cloth motion and needle artifacts in loose garments—and provides both a method (SMAD + PGI) and a new in-the-wild evaluation dataset (LoCo-Human) that the field lacks. However, the ~17-hour training cost (vs. ~4 hours for baselines) limits practical adoption, and the method explicitly does not handle multi-garment interactions or sudden large motion changes, leaving open questions about scalability to complex outfits and real-time deployment. The finding that a GNN with physics-inspired message passing generalizes to unseen motions while an MLP deformer overfits to training sequences is a useful design principle for future autoregressive deformation networks.
