## Problem

Message Passing Neural Networks are at most as expressive as the 1-WL test, limiting their ability to distinguish non-isomorphic graphs and capture fine-grained structure. While derivatives of GNN outputs with respect to node features have been widely studied for oversquashing analysis, oversmoothing, and explainability, they have never been explored as a mechanism to enhance GNN expressivity. This paper reveals that these derivatives provide a natural and computationally efficient path to higher expressivity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260866/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard graph (adjacency matrix and node feature matrix) and produces a graph-level prediction (classification score or regression value). Unusually, the forward pass internally computes high-order derivative tensors of a base MPNN with respect to each node's input features—tensors of shape n×n×d'×d×m for node-level and n×d'×d×m for output-level derivatives—which are then encoded into enriched node features. These derivative tensors remain sparse for shallow base networks or sparse graphs, enabling efficient storage and computation.

## Method

HOD-GNN consists of a base MPNN (e.g., GIN), two derivative encoder networks, and a downstream GNN. The base MPNN produces node representations and a graph-level output; high-order derivatives of both with respect to input node features are then computed via a novel message-passing-like algorithm that exploits graph sparsity and uses Faà di Bruno's formula to propagate derivatives through MLP layers. The output-level derivative tensor is encoded by a DeepSets network, while the node-level (pairwise-indexed) derivative tensor is encoded by a 2-Invariant Graph Network, and both are concatenated with the base MPNN outputs to form derivative-informed node features fed to the downstream GNN. The entire pipeline is differentiable and trained end-to-end. The k-HOD-GNN generalization extends to mixed partial derivatives across k distinct nodes, achieving expressivity matching k-OSAN subgraph GNNs and (k+2)-IGNs while offering better computational complexity for shallow base networks on sparse graphs.

## Results

On the OGB/ZINC benchmarks, HOD-GNN achieves 0.0666 MAE on ZINC-12K, 77.99 ROC-AUC on MOLTOX21, 82.10 ROC-AUC on MOLBACE, and 80.86 ROC-AUC on MOLHIV, consistently ranking within the top two tiers across all four tasks. On the Peptides LRGB benchmark, HOD-GNN attains 69.68 AP on Peptides-func and 0.2450 MAE on Peptides-struct, the best Peptides-struct score among all baselines and the only model that processes these large graphs directly without subsampling. On the BREC synthetic expressivity benchmark, 2-HOD-GNN separates 34/90 pairs that are indistinguishable by 3-WL, placing it among the strongest models tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ZINC-12K, HOD-GNN (0.0666 MAE) is competitive with the strongest baselines—MOSE (0.062) and Subgraphormer (0.063)—while clearly outperforming GIN+RWSE (0.128), GIN+Laplacian PE (0.162), and GIN (0.163). On MOLBACE, HOD-GNN (82.10) trails Subgraphormer (84.35) by ~2.3 points but surpasses HyMN (81.16) and GPSE (80.80); on MOLHIV it is within 0.15 points of HyMN (81.01) and ahead of SUN (80.03) and GSN (80.39). On the Peptides benchmark, HOD-GNN achieves the best Peptides-struct MAE (0.2450) and the best Peptides-func AP (69.68) among all Subgraph GNNs and PSE-based methods, while being the only model that processes these large graphs directly without the subsampling strategies required by Policy-Learn, Subgraphormer, and HyMN. The key practical advantage is scalability: full-bag Subgraph GNNs cannot handle Peptides graphs on standard hardware, whereas HOD-GNN's sparse derivative computation enables direct processing. On the BREC expressivity benchmark, 1-HOD-GNN matches DS-GNN performance (consistent with Theorem 4.1), while 2-HOD-GNN separates 34/90 pairs indistinguishable by 3-WL, demonstrating the expressivity gain from mixed derivatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: GNN expressivity hierarchies (where MPNNs are bounded by 1-WL and Subgraph GNNs like DS-GNN/k-OSAN provide stronger alternatives) and derivative-based GNN analysis (where node/output derivatives have been used to study oversquashing, oversmoothing, and explainability). The key conceptual move is repurposing these derivatives as architectural inputs: via Taylor expansion, high-order derivatives of a base MPNN approximate the output of a GNN with node marking, thereby inheriting the expressivity gains of Subgraph GNNs without explicitly constructing subgraph bags. The method builds on GIN as the base MPNN, uses Faà di Bruno's formula for analytic derivative propagation through message-passing layers, and employs IGN/DeepSets encoders to convert derivative tensors into node features, positioning k-HOD-GNN theoretically between MPNNs and (k+2)-IGNs in the WL hierarchy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HOD-GNN provides a principled, end-to-end trainable mechanism for expressivity enhancement that avoids the explicit subgraph enumeration of DS-GNN/k-OSAN, yielding better space and time complexity for shallow base MPNNs on sparse graphs. This makes it practical on benchmarks like Peptides (LRGB) where full-bag Subgraph GNNs cannot scale. Open questions include the deeper connection between derivative magnitudes and oversquashing/oversmoothing (noted by the authors as future work), whether the sparse-matrix derivative computation can be further optimized for very deep base MPNNs, and whether the expressivity gains from higher derivative orders (m > 4) continue to yield practical benefits or plateau.
