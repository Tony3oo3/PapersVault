## Problem

Generalization in embodied AI is hindered by the 'seeing-to-doing gap': VLMs perceive scenes well but fail to translate that understanding into reliable robotic actions, due to data scarcity in embodied settings and heterogeneity across robot morphologies. Existing pointing-based methods produce incomplete or overly simplistic spatial signals, and SFT-trained models rely on rigid Chain-of-Thought templates that limit generalization to novel tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260316/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (640×480 in real-world deployment) paired with a natural-language instruction and produces structured 2D point coordinates in the format `<point>[[x1,y1], [x2,y2], ...]</point>`, preceded by a free-form reasoning block enclosed in `<think>` tags. For Visual Trace Generation, the output is constrained to exactly 8 ordered points forming an object-centric trajectory. The 3D RGB-D variant additionally accepts depth images and outputs pixel coordinates plus a depth value in millimeters. All outputs are embodiment-agnostic pixel-space coordinates that can be mapped to any robot's action space via a motion planner or camera model.

## Method

Embodied-R1 is a 3B VLM built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, trained entirely with a two-stage Reinforced Fine-Tuning (RFT) curriculum using the GRPO algorithm. Stage 1 enhances spatial reasoning on Embodied-Spatial-84K and ViRL-subset-18K; Stage 2 trains four embodied pointing capabilities (Referring Expression Grounding, Region Referring Grounding, Object Functional Grounding, Visual Trace Generation) on the newly constructed Embodied-Points-200K dataset. The key architectural insight is using 'pointing' as a unified, embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation that bridges high-level vision-language comprehension with low-level action primitives, avoiding the mismatch between physical actions and pre-trained VLM data. A modular multi-task reward system normalizes per-task rewards to [0,1] via weighted combinations of format, accuracy, point-in-mask, distance, and trajectory-similarity components, preventing simpler tasks from dominating optimization. RFT specifically resolves the multi-solution dilemma inherent in pointing (e.g., many valid points in 'empty space') by reinforcing any correct answer rather than overfitting to a single SFT target, while also enabling free-form reasoning beyond rigid CoT templates.

## Results

On SIMPLEREnv (WidowX, 4 tasks × 24 episodes), Embodied-R1 achieves a 56.2% average zero-shot success rate, outperforming the strongest end-to-end VLA (π0-fast, 48.3%), the best modular method (Sofar, 53.8%), and the best affordance method (FSD, 40.6%). On 8 real-world xArm tasks, Embodied-R1-T reaches 87.5% success versus 25.0% (FSD), 12.5% (RoboPoint), and 9.2% (MOKA) — a >60% absolute improvement. On spatial reasoning, Embodied-R1 (3B) attains an average rank of 2.1 across CVBench, BLINK, CRPE, SAT, and EmbSpatial-Bench, surpassing larger models like RoboBrain-7B (rank 4.4) and FSD-13B (rank 4.6). On pointing benchmarks, Embodied-R1 leads on all four: RoboRefIt 85.58, Where2Place 69.50, VABench-P 66.00, Part-Afford 56.63, and achieves the lowest RMSE (77.8) and MAE (45.0) on VABench-V.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SIMPLEREnv, Embodied-R1 (56.2%) outperforms all end-to-end VLAs, with the closest competitor being π0-fast (48.3%, +7.9 pp), and also surpasses the strongest modular baseline Sofar (53.8%, +2.4 pp) and affordance baseline FSD (40.6%, +15.6 pp). On real-world xArm tasks, the gap widens dramatically: Embodied-R1-T achieves 87.5% versus FSD at 25.0% and RoboPoint at 12.5%, with the largest deficits in baselines occurring on reasoning-intensive tasks (e.g., 'move the nearest object') and rigid-object grasping. On spatial reasoning (Table 1), the 3B Embodied-R1 beats the 7B RoboBrain (rank 4.4) and 13B FSD (rank 4.6) on average rank, and also outperforms its own SFT variant (Embodied-SFT, rank 3.7) and the no-common-sense ablation (rank 3.4). On all four pointing benchmarks (Table 2), Embodied-R1 leads every baseline: RoboRefIt 85.58 vs. FSD 56.73, Where2Place 69.50 vs. RoboPoint 46.01, VABench-P 66.00 vs. FSD 61.82, and Part-Afford 56.63 vs. RoboPoint 27.60. On VABench-V (Table 3), Embodied-R1 achieves the best RMSE (77.8 vs. FSD 78.3) and MAE (45.0 vs. FSD 63.4), with a higher LLM score (7.3 vs. 6.2). In the LIBERO integration experiment, conditioning Diffusion Policy on Embodied-R1 visual traces lifts average success from 64.4% to 79.4% (+15 pp) without any additional fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pointing/affordance-based manipulation line (RoboPoint, FSD, MOKA) by unifying four distinct pointing sub-tasks (REG, RRG, OFG, VTG) under a single embodiment-agnostic representation and training them jointly. Its key methodological departure is from SFT-based embodied reasoning (e.g., FSD's rigid CoT templates) toward Reinforced Fine-Tuning with GRPO, adapting the RL-for-reasoning paradigm established in LLM math/coding (DeepSeekMath, Seg-Zero) to the embodied pointing domain. The 'question-verification' data format, rather than standard 'question-answer' pairs, is specifically designed to handle the multi-solution ambiguity inherent in spatial pointing, a problem SFT cannot resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pointing-centric intermediate representation offers a practical path to cross-embodiment generalization without the data-hungry end-to-end VLA paradigm, and the RFT training paradigm shows consistent gains over SFT across 11 benchmarks, suggesting RL is underexploited for embodied spatial tasks. However, the real-world evaluation covers only 8 tabletop tasks with 5–6 trials each, the action execution relies on a classical CuRobo planner rather than a learned policy, and long-horizon task decomposition is left to an external LLM (Gemini). The preliminary LIBERO integration with Diffusion Policy (+15%) hints at a promising but unexplored direction for closed-loop, dynamic manipulation.
