## Problem

Existing 3D vision tasks—monocular depth, SfM, MVS, SLAM—are addressed by highly specialized models, and recent unified approaches rely on complex bespoke architectures trained from scratch, preventing them from leveraging large-scale pretrained features. This paper asks whether a minimal modeling strategy—a single plain transformer with a singular depth-ray prediction target—can recover spatially consistent 3D geometry from an arbitrary number of views, with or without known camera poses.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any number of RGB images (from 1 to ~1000+ depending on model size) at variable resolutions (504×504 down to 336×504), optionally accompanied by camera intrinsics and extrinsics. For each input view it produces a pixel-aligned depth map, a 6-channel ray map (per-pixel ray origin and direction in world coordinates), and an optional camera pose (FOV, quaternion, translation). The 3D point cloud is obtained by the element-wise product of depth and ray direction plus ray origin, requiring no separate point-map head. Camera poses are injected as a single learned or MLP-encoded token per view that participates in all attention layers.

## Method

Depth Anything 3 uses a vanilla DINOv2 ViT backbone with an input-adaptive cross-view self-attention mechanism that rearranges tokens in a 2:1 ratio of within-view to cross-view attention layers, enabling arbitrary view counts without architectural changes. A Dual-DPT head processes shared backbone features through common reassembly modules before splitting into two branches with distinct fusion parameters to jointly predict depth and ray maps, promoting cross-task interaction while avoiding redundant representations. The depth-ray formulation is chosen as a minimal disentangled target set that captures both scene structure and camera motion without the entanglement inherent in point maps. Training follows a teacher-student paradigm where a synthetic-data-only teacher generates dense pseudo-depth labels aligned to sparse real-world depth via RANSAC, unifying heterogeneous data sources. For feed-forward novel view synthesis, the model is fine-tuned with an additional GS-DPT head that predicts pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian parameters (opacity, rotation, scale, color) whose positions are derived from the estimated depth.

## Results

DA3-Giant achieves state-of-the-art pose accuracy across all five benchmark datasets, with headline numbers of 81.7 Auc3 on HiRoom (vs. 49.1 for VGGT and 67.0 for Pi3) and 83.2 Auc3 on ScanNet++ (vs. 62.6 for VGGT). In geometric reconstruction (F-Score), DA3-Giant reaches 89.3 on HiRoom (pose-free) and 95.2 (pose-conditioned), outperforming VGGT by an average of 23.6% relative improvement. On monocular depth (δ₁), DA3 scores 95.3 on KITTI and 98.6 on ETH3D, surpassing Depth Anything 2 (94.6 and 86.5 respectively). The compact DA3-Large (0.30B params) surpasses the 3× larger VGGT (0.90B) in 5 out of 10 reconstruction settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA3-Giant decisively outperforms all feed-forward baselines (VGGT, Pi3, MapAnything, Fast3R, DUSt3R) on pose accuracy across every dataset, with the largest margins on HiRoom (81.7 vs. 49.1 for VGGT, +66% relative) and ScanNet++ (83.2 vs. 62.6 for VGGT, +33% relative). Classical SfM pipelines (COLMAP, GLOMAP) remain competitive only on the extremely dense DTU dataset (GLOMAP: 96.8 Auc3 vs. DA3-Giant: 85.6), but collapse on sparser or more challenging scenes (COLMAP: 13.0 on HiRoom vs. DA3-Giant: 81.7). In geometric reconstruction (F-Score), DA3-Giant leads all pose-free settings, achieving 89.3 on HiRoom vs. 75.8 for Pi3 and 56.7 for VGGT, and 76.4 on ScanNet++ vs. 66.4 for VGGT. On monocular depth, DA3 surpasses Depth Anything 2 on KITTI (95.3 vs. 94.6) and ETH3D (98.6 vs. 86.5) but trails slightly on NYU (97.4 vs. 97.9) and Sintel (75.5 vs. 77.2). For feed-forward novel view synthesis, DA3 with a GS-DPT head achieves 21.33 PSNR on DL3DV and 18.10 on Tanks&Temples, outperforming specialized 3DGS models (DepthSplat: 19.24/15.80) and alternative geometry backbones (VGGT: 20.96/17.18, MV-DUSt3R: 20.01/17.04).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in unified 3D geometry (DUSt3R, VGGT, Fast3R, Pi3) that relies on complex multi-task architectures predicting point maps, poses, and depth simultaneously. Instead, it builds on the teacher-student pseudo-labeling paradigm from Depth Anything V2 and the confidence-aware loss from DUSt3R, but replaces the point-map representation with a minimal depth-ray target and a single plain DINOv2 backbone with only an input-adaptive token-rearrangement mechanism for cross-view attention. The key conceptual departure is the argument that architectural specialization and multi-task redundancy are unnecessary: a vanilla pretrained transformer with a single disentangled prediction target suffices to unify monocular depth, SfM, and MVS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that minimal modeling (one transformer, one prediction target) can outperform purpose-built multi-task architectures by large margins (35.7% pose, 23.6% geometry over VGGT), challenging the field's assumption that unified 3D models require bespoke designs. The comprehensive visual geometry benchmark (5 datasets, 89+ scenes) and the finding that a 0.30B model rivals a 0.90B competitor make this practically important for efficient deployment. The NVS results demonstrate that strong geometry backbones can replace specialized 3DGS architectures, suggesting a broader path for foundation-model-driven 3D vision. Open questions include handling dynamic scenes, integrating language/interaction cues, and whether the depth-ray representation generalizes to metric-scale or outdoor long-baseline settings.
