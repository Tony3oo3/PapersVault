## Problem

The original Nef-Net for Electrocardio Panorama synthesis operates under idealized assumptions: it is limited to single-heartbeat reconstruction, uniformly averages features across all input views (ignoring view-specific relevance to the target), and neglects real-world deployment challenges such as inter-device signal shifts and operator-induced electrode placement deviations. Additionally, no dense-view benchmark existed to rigorously evaluate panoramic ECG generalization beyond the standard 12-lead configuration.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an arbitrary-length multi-lead ECG recording (resampled to 500 Hz) from any subset of recorded views (typically 3–7 leads, always including two limb leads as reference potentials) together with their spherical angular coordinates (θ, φ), plus the desired query view's angular coordinates. It outputs a continuous synthesized ECG signal at the queried viewpoint, enabling generation of the full 48-view Electrocardio Panorama. Unlike Nef-Net's beat-level operation, NEF-NET v2 handles arbitrary-duration signals, and the first 5 seconds of each recording are consumed for on-the-fly calibration before the remaining segment is used for synthesis.

## Method

NEF-NET v2 reformulates ECG view synthesis as a direct view-to-view transformation rather than reconstructing a shared electrocardio field. The architecture comprises three components: an Angle Embedding module that maps spherical coordinates into a higher-dimensional space; a View Encoder (1D ResNet) whose features are modulated via FiLM-style affine conditioning on the query angle to amplify relevant and suppress irrelevant signal components; and a Geometric View Transformer (GeoVT) that uses a Geometric Angular Attention map (cross-attention between query and recorded angle embeddings) to weight view contributions, followed by stacked View Transformation blocks with spatial gating and a spectral-normalized convolutional reconstruction head. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (I) Any-Pairs Pretraining on combined heterogeneous datasets with random lead pairings to learn invariant cross-view transformations; (II) Device Calibration fine-tuning on the target ECG device; and (III) On-the-fly Calibration that learns per-patient angular deviation parameters (dθ, dφ) using the first 5 seconds of each recording, freezing the View Encoder and Reconstruction Head while adapting angle embeddings to compensate for electrode misplacement and anatomical variability.

## Results

On the unseen view synthesis task (the novel capability of this work), NEF-NET V2 achieves 32.57–34.46 dB PSNR across four public datasets (ChinaDB, CPSC2018, Tianchi, PTB-XL) under the (3,8,1) lead configuration, outperforming Nef-Net by 6.8–9.3 dB. Under the (5,6,1) configuration, PSNR reaches 32.07–34.82 dB, again surpassing Nef-Net by 6.8–7.9 dB. On the standard 3-to-9 reconstruction task, NEF-NET V2 attains 35.21–37.13 dB PSNR, exceeding the best prior method (EKGAN) by 3.4–2.7 dB. On Panobench (48-view dense benchmark), NEF-NET V2 achieves 35.57 dB PSNR for synthesis with 12 supervised leads, versus 32.98 dB for Nef-Net.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEF-NET V2 decisively outperforms Nef-Net (the only prior method capable of unseen-view synthesis) by 6.8–9.3 dB PSNR across all four datasets and both synthesis configurations, with the largest gap on PTB-XL (9.31 dB under (3,8,1)). On the standard 3-to-9 reconstruction task, NEF-NET V2 surpasses the strongest baselines EKGAN (32.76–34.39 dB) and SSSD (32.53–33.59 dB) by 2.7–4.8 dB, and ECGRecover by 4.7–5.7 dB. Under the more challenging 8-to-12 reconstruction setting, NEF-NET V2 (39.54–41.52 dB) edges out EKGAN (39.43–40.37 dB) and SSSD (39.37–40.57 dB) by 0.1–1.15 dB, while maintaining a clear 6–9 dB advantage over Nef-Net. E-LSTM and KIM (a classical linear method) are substantially outperformed by 12–15 dB, confirming the superiority of learned nonlinear transformations. The consistent ~6 dB synthesis improvement over Nef-Net is attributed to the Geometric View Transformer's explicit angular cross-attention replacing Nef-Net's uniform feature averaging, which the authors show is particularly critical under sparse-lead supervision on Panobench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Nef-Net (Chen et al., 2021), which first introduced Electrocardio Panorama by reconstructing a continuous electrocardiac field via implicit neural representations. The key conceptual departure is reformulating ECG view synthesis from a shared-field reconstruction problem (where Nef-Net uniformly averages multi-view features into a latent cardiac field) to a direct view-to-view transformation using a geometry-aware cross-attention mechanism (GeoVT) that selectively weights recorded views by their angular relevance to the query. Additionally, the paper extends the task from single-heartbeat modeling to arbitrary-length continuous ECG and introduces a three-stage deployment pipeline (Any-Pairs Pretraining → Device Calibration → On-the-fly Calibration) to address practical challenges—device heterogeneity and electrode placement variability—that Nef-Net's idealized training assumptions ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~6–8 dB PSNR improvement over Nef-Net, combined with the first 48-view benchmark (Panobench) with CT-measured spherical coordinates, provides the field with both a substantially better synthesis method and a rigorous evaluation resource that prior 12-lead-only studies could not support. The three-stage deployment framework and the finding that inter-subject anatomical variability (addressed by on-the-fly calibration) has a larger impact on synthesis quality than inter-device variation offer practical guidance for clinical translation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the 48-view spherical grid to truly arbitrary body-surface positions, whether it remains effective with wearable or patch-type ECG devices with fewer and noisier channels, and whether the 5-second on-the-fly calibration window is sufficient for all patient populations (e.g., those with arrhythmias where the first 5 seconds may be unrepresentative).
