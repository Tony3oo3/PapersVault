## Problem

Prior dexterous hand manipulation methods either generate only static grasp poses without temporal structure or lack open-vocabulary language understanding, making them unable to produce smooth, physically feasible multi-step manipulation sequences from free-form instructions. No existing framework unifies dynamic hand manipulation across heterogeneous robot hand morphologies while being guided by natural language and trained without expensive teleoperation data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262636/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB-D image and a free-form text instruction (e.g., 'grasp the bottle into the box') as input. A CLIPort module infers the object target trajectory in SE(3), and Point-SAM segments the target object point cloud from the scene. The output is a temporally ordered sequence of joint-angle configurations (q-positions) for a specified robot hand, refined by physics-based optimization to ensure contact feasibility and smooth velocity/acceleration profiles, directly executable on physical hands such as Panda, XHand, or Inspire.

## Method

UniHM couples three components: (1) a Unified Hand-Dexterous Tokenizer that maps heterogeneous hand morphologies into a shared VQ-VAE codebook (K=8192) via hand-specific encoders/decoders, with new hands integrated through knowledge distillation to align encoder latent spaces with a reference encoder, bypassing the gradient discontinuity of quantization; (2) a Qwen3-0.6B VLM with a decoupled architecture where CLIPort handles scene perception and an MLP trajectory encoder serializes targets, while the VLM performs instruction-conditioned token generation under a progressive masking curriculum that gradually replaces ground-truth poses with learnable mask tokens; (3) a physics-guided dynamic refinement module that solves a frame-by-frame Gauss-Newton problem with Levenberg-Marquardt damping, combining an asymmetric smooth contact penalty, a generative prior anchoring to VLM output, and a temporal prior enforcing smooth velocity and acceleration. The entire pipeline is trained solely on human HOI datasets (DexYCB, OakInk) with GPT-4o-generated language annotations and Dex-Retargeting to robot hands, eliminating the need for teleoperation data.

## Results

On DexYCB (seen), UniHM achieves MPJPE of 61.40, FPL of 12.15, and FID of 31.24, outperforming the strongest baseline MotionGPT3 (74.80 / 19.32 / 43.35) by 13.4, 7.2, and 12.1 points respectively. On the unseen split, UniHM maintains MPJPE 63.56 and FPL 13.06 versus MotionGPT3's 77.93 and 21.48, demonstrating strong generalization. On OakInk (seen), UniHM reaches MPJPE 52.73 and FPL 19.86, again leading all baselines. In real-world cross-embodiment trials, UniHM achieves 65% grab success on seen objects and 60% on unseen objects, roughly doubling the best prior method (MotionGPT3+Dex-Retargeting at 30%/45%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniHM outperforms all four baselines (TM2T, MDM, FlowMDM, MotionGPT3) on every metric for both seen and unseen splits across DexYCB and OakInk. The largest margins are on FPL (final position error): on DexYCB-seen, UniHM's 12.15 mm beats MotionGPT3's 19.32 mm by 37%, and on DexYCB-unseen, 13.06 vs. 21.48 mm (39% improvement). On OakInk-unseen, UniHM's MPJPE of 58.62 surpasses MotionGPT3's 61.95 and FlowMDM's 65.39, while its FPL of 22.87 is 19% lower than MotionGPT3's 28.25. In real-world trials, UniHM roughly doubles the grab success rate of MotionGPT3+Dex-Retargeting (65% vs. 30% seen; 60% vs. 45% unseen) and substantially outperforms MDM+Dex-Retargeting (which achieves 0% on Pull&Push for both splits). The only metric where UniHM does not lead is Diversity: MotionGPT3 produces more diverse outputs (72.51 vs. 39.62 on DexYCB-seen), though UniHM's diversity is closer to the ground-truth value of 125.53 on the unseen split, suggesting a more balanced trade-off between fidelity and variety.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniHM sits at the intersection of three previously separate research threads: (1) language-guided dexterous grasping (SemGrasp, AffordDexGrasp, DexGYS), which produces static poses; (2) VLM-based motion/sequence generation (MotionGPT, HOIGPT), which targets digital hands or low-DoF grippers; and (3) physics-based manipulation optimization (DexGraspNet, DexMV). The key departure is the Unified Hand-Dexterous Tokenizer, which uses a shared VQ-VAE codebook with cross-morphology knowledge distillation to map heterogeneous hand kinematics into a single discrete action space—a mechanism absent from prior pose-centric or single-hand pipelines. The training paradigm also departs from teleoperation-based imitation (DexMV, AnyTeleop) by learning entirely from human HOI video with GPT-4o-generated language annotations, and the decoupled CLIPort-perception / VLM-generation architecture separates spatial grounding from sequence synthesis in a way that prior end-to-end VLM manipulation approaches do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive real-world teleoperation data and demonstrating cross-morphology token transfer (a pose encoded for one hand can be decoded for another via the shared codebook), UniHM lowers the practical barrier to deploying language-conditioned dexterous manipulation across diverse robotic hands. The physics-guided Gauss-Newton refinement with an asymmetric smooth contact kernel provides a lightweight post-processing step that substantially improves feasibility without retraining the generative model. However, the framework remains limited by its reliance on RGB-D perception without tactile or force feedback, simplified contact/friction energy terms, and the absence of bimanual or tool-use scenarios—open problems that will constrain real-world deployment in contact-rich, sequential tasks. Scaling the unified codebook to more morphologies and closing the loop with online adaptation are identified as critical next steps.
