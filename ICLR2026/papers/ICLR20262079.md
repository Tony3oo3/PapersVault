## Problem

While OCR (extracting text from images) is well-studied, the reverse capability—perceiving visual semantics embedded within consecutive text characters—remains largely unexplored for LLMs and MLLMs. Existing ASCII-related tasks rely on rule-based generation or automated image-to-text conversion, which risks models overfitting to transformation patterns rather than genuine visual understanding. This paper asks: how well can current models recognize the concept depicted in human-drawn ASCII art, and does providing the same content in both text and image modalities yield synergistic improvement?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262079/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The task is formulated as a 4-option multiple-choice question. Under Text-only, the model receives the raw ASCII art string (95 printable fixed-width characters, averaging ~300 tokens, 1–100 lines) plus candidate concept labels. Under Image-only, the ASCII art is rendered as a fixed-width-font image (DejaVu Sans Mono by default). Under Text-Image, both are provided simultaneously. The model outputs a single option letter. The test set ASCIIEVAL contains 3,526 samples across 359 concepts organized in a 3-layer tree (7 classes → 23 groups → 359 concepts), with a human upper bound of ~98.33% accuracy.

## Method

The paper introduces ASCIIEVAL (3,526 test samples) and ASCIITUNE (~11,836 training samples), constructed through a four-stage pipeline: artist-created ASCII art collection, a 3-layer classification tree, rule-based and human-annotated filtering (removing words, ambiguous art, and near-duplicates via edit distance < 0.3), and multiple-choice construction with same-group distractors. For LLMs, the authors propose rationale-assisted fine-tuning: GPT-5 is prompted with both text and image to generate detailed local-feature reasoning rationales, and the target LLM (e.g., Qwen3-8B) is fine-tuned on these rationale-annotated pairs, yielding a 26.10% relative gain. For MLLMs, two post-hoc strategies are introduced: low-resolution prompting (deliberately capping pixel count to blur character-level details and force holistic perception, improving Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 17.49%) and supervised fine-tuning with LoRA on the vision backbone (reaching 75.48%). A key empirical finding is the absence of inter-modal synergy: the Text-Image setting consistently underperforms Image-only by up to 12.23%, and a strong negative correlation (−0.72) between OCRBench and ASCIIEval scores reveals an OCR-vs-holistic-perception trade-off in open-source MLLMs.

## Results

On ASCIIEVAL (3,526 samples, 359 concepts), GPT-5 achieves the highest macro-accuracy at 87.81% (Image-only), 55.90% (Text-only), and 86.40% (Text-Image), averaging 76.70%. The best open-source LLM, DeepSeek-V3, reaches only 35.94% on text input, while the best open-source MLLM, CogVLM2-Llama3-19B, attains 67.80% on image input—yielding a 20.01% gap versus GPT-5. All models fall well short of the human upper bound of 98.33%, and the Text-Image setting consistently degrades performance relative to Image-only by up to 12.23%, revealing an absence of inter-modal synergy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 dominates all three input settings, outperforming Gemini-2.5-pro (83.07% vs 87.81% image) and GPT-4o (82.62% image, 43.40% text) by 4.79% and 14.41% respectively on image input. Against the strongest open-source LLM, DeepSeek-V3 (35.94% text), GPT-5 leads by 19.96%; against the strongest open-source MLLM, CogVLM2-Llama3-19B (67.80% image), the gap is 20.01%. A striking generational regression is observed in open-source MLLMs: Qwen-VL achieves 52.32% on image input while its successor Qwen2.5-VL-7B drops to 34.83%, whereas proprietary models improve substantially (Gemini-1.5-pro 60.69% → Gemini-2.5-pro 83.07%). The paper further shows a strong negative Pearson correlation (−0.72) between ASCIIEval and OCRBench scores, indicating that models optimized for fine-grained OCR sacrifice holistic visual perception. Correlation with TableEval (0.78) and SGP-Bench (0.85) confirms ASCIIEval measures a shared visual-perception skill, while the Text-Image setting underperforms Image-only for every model tested, with degradation reaching 12.23%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing human-drawn ASCII art as a modality-agnostic benchmark for visual perception in text strings, a direction the authors argue has received no commensurate attention relative to OCR (text-in-images). Prior ASCII art research focused on image-based CNN classification (Fujisawa et al.), automated synthesis, or narrow applications like jailbreak attacks and bot detection, while existing LLM/MLLM benchmarks evaluate textual-semantic or conventional-image understanding. The key departure is formulating ASCII art recognition as a multiple-choice task that simultaneously probes LLMs' 2D spatial reasoning from text and MLLMs' holistic visual generalization, with the same content available in both modalities to enable controlled cross-modal comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals several structural weaknesses in current models that have safety and practical implications: a fundamental OCR-versus-holistic-perception trade-off in MLLMs, a complete absence of inter-modal synergy (providing both text and image consistently degrades performance), and a concerning generational regression in open-source MLLMs. The tokenization bottleneck for 2D spatial information in LLMs points to an architectural limitation that rationale-assisted fine-tuning only partially addresses. Open questions include whether models can be trained to intrinsically balance fine-grained character recognition with collective visual perception, how to achieve true cross-modal fusion for semantically equivalent inputs, and whether the ASCII art safety vector (e.g., depicting a 'bomb' to bypass content filters) can be proactively defended.
