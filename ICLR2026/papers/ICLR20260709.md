## Problem

Efficient tactile data compression is critical for real-time robotic applications under bandwidth constraints, yet remains underexplored due to the heterogeneity of tactile signals (vision-based vs. force-based sensors, varying resolutions and sampling rates). No comprehensive benchmark exists to systematically compare compression methods across diverse tactile datasets, and no data-driven codecs have been trained specifically on tactile data to exploit its intrinsic statistical structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260709/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Inputs are either visuo-tactile RGB images (120×160 to 640×480, 30 Hz, from GelSight or DIGIT sensors) or force-based tactile signals (60 contact points × 3D force vectors at 200 Hz, mapped to RGB images of T×60 resolution by treating each 3D vector as a color channel). For TaCo-LL, inputs are tokenized into 16×16×3 patches and flattened in raster-scan order; for TaCo-L, inputs are randomly cropped or zero-padded to 256×256. Outputs are compressed bitstreams (lossless or lossy) and, for lossy codecs, reconstructed tactile images evaluated via PSNR, BD-Rate, and downstream task accuracy.

## Method

The paper introduces TaCo-LL and TaCo-L, the first purely data-driven tactile codecs, by retraining DualComp-I (an autoregressive lossless compressor) and LALIC (a VAE-based lossy compressor with hyper autoencoder) end-to-end on heterogeneous tactile datasets. TaCo-LL tokenizes tactile images into 16×16×3 patches, flattens them in raster-scan order (expanding RGB sub-pixels or 3D force components sequentially), and uses an autoregressive model to predict per-token distributions followed by arithmetic coding. TaCo-L follows LALIC's architecture with four downsampling/upsampling stages and a hyper autoencoder generating side information for density estimation, trained with a rate-distortion objective. The key insight is that tactile data exhibits statistical patterns distinct from natural images (e.g., large uniform regions in force data, specific deformation structures in visuo-tactile data), so domain-specific training yields substantially better compression than pretrained image codecs. The benchmark evaluates 30 codecs (off-the-shelf and neural) across five datasets and four task types: lossless storage, human visualization, material/object classification, and dexterous robotic grasping.

## Results

On lossless compression, TaCo-LL-96M achieves the best bits/Byte across all five datasets: 0.447 (18×) on Touch and Go, 2.709 (3×) on ObjectFolder, 1.066 (8×) on SSVTP, 0.360 (22×) on ObjTac, and 1.073 (8×) on YCB-Slide, outperforming the best off-the-shelf codec (JPEG-XL at 0.739 bits/Byte on Touch and Go) and the best pretrained neural codec (DualComp-I at 0.948). On lossy compression, TaCo-L achieves BD-Rate reductions of −61.8% (Touch and Go), −24.3% (ObjectFolder), −19.2% (SSVTP), −27.4% (YCB-Slide), and −27.0% (ObjTac) relative to the HM-Intra anchor, surpassing the best pretrained neural codec LALIC (−51.6% on Touch and Go). In downstream tasks, TaCo-L preserves near-uncompressed classification accuracy (e.g., 75.12% vs. 76.63% SVM on Touch and Go) and dexterous grasping success (62.2% lift vs. 63.8% uncompressed) at 0.0251 BPP, a ~955× reduction from raw 24 BPP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TaCo-LL-96M outperforms all 13 lossless baselines across all five datasets. Among off-the-shelf codecs, JPEG-XL is the strongest (0.739 bits/Byte on Touch and Go, 11×), but TaCo-LL-96M nearly halves that to 0.447 (18×); general-purpose compressors (gzip, zstd, bzip2) achieve only ~2.2–2.3 bits/Byte on visuo-tactile data. Among pretrained neural codecs, DualComp-I (0.948) is the best, yet TaCo-LL-96M still improves by ~53% in bits/Byte on Touch and Go, confirming the benefit of tactile-specific training. On lossy compression, TaCo-L achieves the best BD-Rate on all five datasets, beating the strongest off-the-shelf codec VTM-SCC (−23.7% on Touch and Go) by 38.1 percentage points and the best pretrained neural codec LALIC (−51.6%) by 10.2 points. On ObjTac (force-based), TaCo-L (−27.0%) substantially outperforms ELIC (+44.5%) and TCM (+97.2%), which fail to generalize to non-visual tactile data. In downstream grasping, TaCo-L at 0.0251 BPP achieves 62.2% lift success (−1.6% vs. uncompressed), outperforming JPEG-XL (55.0%, −8.8%) and LALIC (60.0%, −3.8%) while using roughly half the bitrate of VTM-Intra (0.0498 BPP, 63.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions tactile data compression from the narrow, task-specific signal-processing approaches of prior work (Shao et al. 2020, Hassen et al. 2020, Seeling et al. 2021) into the broader neural compression framework that has matured for images and video. It builds directly on DualComp-I (Zhao et al., 2025) for lossless coding and LALIC (Feng et al., 2025a) for lossy coding, retraining both on heterogeneous tactile data to produce TaCo-LL and TaCo-L. The key conceptual departure is treating tactile signals—whether visuo-tactile RGB frames or force-based 3D vectors mapped to RGB—as first-class compressible data amenable to the full spectrum of off-the-shelf and learned codecs, rather than designing ad-hoc sparse or quantization schemes per sensor type.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first systematic benchmark spanning 30 codecs, 5 heterogeneous datasets, and 4 downstream tasks (including dexterous grasping under compression), this work provides the field with a common evaluation protocol that was previously absent. The finding that tactile-specific training yields substantial gains over pretrained neural codecs—particularly on force-based data where domain mismatch is most severe—gives practitioners clear guidance on when domain adaptation is necessary. Open questions include whether a single unified codec can handle truly heterogeneous sensor modalities without per-dataset retraining, how these results scale to higher-resolution or higher-frequency sensors, and whether the RGB-mapping of force data is the optimal representation for compression.
