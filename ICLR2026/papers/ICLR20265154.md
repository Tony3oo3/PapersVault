## Problem

Existing signed graph neural networks handle negative edges through auxiliary structures (balance theory, separate positive/negative aggregation), which lead to slow convergence and inefficient memory use. A more fundamental approach—directly modeling statistical dependencies among edges via a correlation matrix—is computationally intractable because the matrix scales quadratically with the number of edges (O(|V|⁴) memory), and inference requires inverting this huge matrix. The paper asks: how can inter-edge correlations be modeled efficiently enough to be practical on real-world graphs?

## Input / Output

The method takes a signed graph with partially observed edge signs (positive/negative) and node features initialized from a standard normal distribution (non-learnable, simulating real-world attributes). The graph is preprocessed as undirected with the largest connected component extracted. Output is a predicted sign (+/−) for each unobserved edge plus a probabilistic score in [0,1] indicating the probability of a positive sign, obtained by sampling from a conditional Gaussian derived from the copula and transforming through the inverse of the relaxed Bernoulli CDF. Unlike standard classifiers, inference is a single sampling step from a low-rank multivariate Gaussian rather than a forward pass through a separate head.

## Method

CopulaLSP extends CopulaGNN from node-centric to edge-centric link sign prediction by coupling relaxed Bernoulli marginals (one per edge) with a Gaussian copula whose correlation matrix is constructed as the normalized Gramian of edge embeddings (R = D⁻¹QQᵀD⁻¹), reducing learnable parameters from O(n²) to O(nd) with d ≪ n. Edge embeddings are computed as element-wise products of node embeddings from a signed graph encoder (SNEA), so the model learns only encoder parameters plus two linear projections for marginal parameters. For inference, the Woodbury matrix identity reformulates the conditional distribution, replacing inversion of the m×m observed correlation submatrix with inversion of a d×d matrix, enabling efficient sampling without out-of-memory failures. Training uses maximum likelihood estimation with label smoothing to avoid boundary evaluation of the relaxed Bernoulli PDF. The paper further proves linear convergence of the loss under gradient descent by establishing L-smoothness and the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, with the Gramian structure and label smoothing being essential to both proofs.

## Results

On six real-world signed graph datasets, CopulaLSP achieves AUC scores of 0.864–0.899 and Macro F1 of 0.716–0.810, competitive with the state-of-the-art SLGNN (AUC up to 0.903, F1 up to 0.818). Crucially, CopulaLSP is the only method that successfully runs on all six datasets without out-of-memory failures, whereas SLGNN, SDGNN, TrustSGCN, SGAAE, and SE-SGformer all OOM on SlashDot and/or Epinions. In terms of scalability, CopulaLSP trains 44×–379× faster than its SNEA backbone (e.g., 3.0 s vs. 131.2 s on BitcoinAlpha; 12.4 s vs. 4676.5 s on SlashDot) while using only ~200 MB more GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the state-of-the-art SLGNN, CopulaLSP ties on BitcoinAlpha F1 (0.716) and SlashDot F1 (0.771), slightly trails on BitcoinOTC AUC (0.885 vs. 0.903) and WikiElec AUC (0.877 vs. 0.887), but wins on WikiRfa F1 (0.751 vs. 0.748) and is the only method that completes on Epinions (AUC 0.899, F1 0.810) where SLGNN OOMs. Compared to its SNEA backbone, CopulaLSP matches AUC within ±0.003 across all datasets while improving Macro F1 by 0.001–0.047 (e.g., 0.716 vs. 0.669 on BitcoinAlpha, 0.771 vs. 0.755 on BitcoinOTC), demonstrating that the explicit inter-edge correlation modeling adds predictive value beyond the encoder alone. CopulaLSP decisively outperforms SGCN, SDGNN, TrustSGCN, SGAAE, and SE-SGformer on every dataset where those methods can run, and additionally handles the large-scale SlashDot and Epinions datasets where all five baselines fail with OOM. Against vanilla GCN, CopulaLSP gains 0.10–0.24 AUC and 0.16–0.27 F1, confirming that signed-graph-specific modeling is essential. The scalability advantage is stark: on SlashDot, CopulaLSP trains in 12.4 s versus 4676.5 s for SNEA (379× speedup) and 5557.4 s for SGCN, while using comparable GPU memory (~5 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CopulaGNN (Ma et al., 2021), which modeled inter-node correlations via Gaussian copulas for node regression on unsigned graphs, to the edge-centric task of link sign prediction on signed graphs. It departs from the dominant SGNN paradigm (SGCN, SNEA, SDGNN, SLGNN) that handles negative edges through auxiliary structures like balance theory or separate positive/negative aggregation, instead directly modeling statistical dependencies between edges sharing a common node. The two key technical contributions—representing the correlation matrix as a Gramian of edge embeddings and applying the Woodbury matrix identity for efficient inference—are what make this edge-centric copula formulation computationally tractable, whereas the naive approach scales as O(|V|^4) in memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic convergence speedup (44–379×) backed by a proof of linear convergence suggests that explicitly modeling inter-edge correlation is a more principled and efficient alternative to the auxiliary-structure approach that dominates the SGNN literature. Practically, the method handles large-scale graphs (SlashDot, Epinions) where several state-of-the-art baselines run out of memory, making it one of the few scalable options for real-world signed networks. Limitations include the assumption of a static graph structure and dependence on an SGNN backbone (SNEA) for node embeddings, leaving open whether the copula-based edge correlation modeling can be extended to dynamic or bipartite graphs and whether it generalizes beyond link sign prediction to other edge-level tasks.
