## Problem

Existing decision-focused learning (DFL) methods are confined to batch settings with i.i.d. data and fixed objectives, leaving the non-stationary online setting entirely unexplored. This extension is technically demanding because the DFL objective is non-convex and has zero or undefined gradients (the inner linear optimization over a polytope makes the argmin mapping discontinuous), precluding standard first-order online learning methods. No provable regret guarantees existed for the online DFL problem.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the algorithm receives covariates X_t ∈ R^p and must select a decision w_t from the vertices of a bounded convex polytope W ⊂ R^d by solving a linear optimization using predicted costs g(θ_t, X_t). The true cost vector g̅_t(X_t) is only revealed after the decision is made, creating a delayed-feedback structure where the parameter update is driven by a regularized surrogate loss rather than the true (non-differentiable) decision cost. The output is a discrete decision (a vertex of W) plus updated model parameters θ_{t+1} ∈ Θ, with the polytope W and model class g fixed across all rounds.

## Method

The paper introduces two algorithms—DF-FTPL and DF-OGD—that overcome the non-differentiability and non-convexity of the DFL objective through two complementary techniques. First, the inner linear optimization is regularized with a log-barrier (general polytope) or negative entropy (simplex), yielding a differentiable surrogate decision mapping w̃_t(θ) whose Jacobian is available in closed form via the implicit function theorem. Second, to handle the resulting non-convexity, the algorithms combine an approximate offline optimization oracle (e.g., SGD) with perturbation: DF-FTPL adds exponential noise to the cumulative surrogate loss before invoking the oracle (yielding a static regret bound of Õ(T^{-1/4})), while DF-OGD evaluates the surrogate gradient at a random point between the current parameter and the oracle's output (yielding a dynamic regret bound of Õ((1+P_T)^{1/4} T^{-1/4})). The regularization parameter α_t controls a fundamental trade-off between surrogate smoothness and fidelity to the true decision, and the dynamic regret bound is independent of the parameter dimension m, depending only mildly on the decision-space dimension d through a ln ln(d) factor.

## Results

In the knapsack experiment (K=5 items, p=10 features, T=5000 rounds, 10 runs), both DF-FTPL and DF-OGD achieve lower average cumulative decision cost than PF-OGD and Online SPO, while incurring higher prediction MSE — consistent with the decision-focused objective prioritizing decision quality over statistical accuracy. Theoretically, DF-FTPL achieves a static regret rate of Õ(m^{3/4} n^{3/2} T^{-1/4} + ξ) and DF-OGD achieves a dynamic regret rate of Õ(√n (1+P_T)^{1/4} T^{-1/4} + ξ), both sublinear in T. In a higher-dimensional variant (K=80 items), the proposed algorithms continue to significantly outperform both baselines. An interpolation experiment (γ from 0 to 1) confirms that DFL's advantage over PFL grows as model misspecification increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DF-FTPL and DF-OGD against two baselines: Prediction-Focused Online Gradient Descent (PF-OGD), which minimizes MSE loss irrespective of the downstream decision, and Online SPO (Elmachtoub & Grigas, 2022), which uses a differentiable convex surrogate that upper-bounds the decision-focused loss. Both proposed algorithms outperform PF-OGD and Online SPO in average cumulative decision cost across all 10 runs, as shown in Figure 1 (left panel). However, this comes at the cost of higher prediction MSE (right panel), which is expected since decision-focused learning optimizes for decision quality rather than prediction accuracy. The advantage over PF-OGD is particularly pronounced under model misspecification (the nonlinear sin⁴ data-generating process), while in the well-specified linear case (γ=0), PFL is competitive or slightly better. Online SPO, despite being a well-established benchmark, is consistently outperformed by both DF-FTPL and DF-OGD in decision cost, suggesting that directly optimizing the regularized bi-level objective yields better decisions than the SPO⁺ surrogate approach in the online, non-stationary setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends decision-focused learning (DFL) from the batch, i.i.d. setting (Wilder et al., 2019; Mandi et al., 2024; Elmachtoub and Grigas, 2022) to the online, non-stationary setting where both the cost function and data distribution evolve over time. Methodologically, it combines the regularization-based differentiability trick from Wilder et al. (2019) (log-barrier or negative entropy on the inner problem) with the approximate-oracle-plus-perturbation framework for non-convex online learning from Suggala and Netrapalli (2020) and Agarwal et al. (2019), adapting both to the bi-level structure of DFL. The key departure from prior DFL work is the explicit handling of non-differentiability (zero or undefined gradients due to the linear inner problem) and non-convexity simultaneously, which no prior online bi-level optimization result (Shen et al., 2023; Tarzanagh et al., 2024) addresses because those rely on smoothness assumptions incompatible with DFL's structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide provable regret guarantees for online DFL, filling a clear gap between the well-studied batch DFL literature and the practical need for DFL in dynamic environments (supply chain, revenue management, healthcare scheduling). The T^{-1/4} static regret rate is slower than the T^{-1/2} achievable in single-level non-convex online learning, reflecting the inherent cost of the bi-level regularization trade-off; whether faster rates are possible (e.g., via discretization at the cost of exponential dependence on parameter dimension) remains open. The experimental validation is limited to a single knapsack problem with a linear predictor, leaving open whether the theoretical advantages persist with richer model classes (e.g., neural networks) and more complex combinatorial decision problems. The dynamic regret bound's independence from the parameter dimension m is a notable practical advantage for high-dimensional prediction models.
