## Problem

Existing vision encoders for MLLMs are trained on global image-text pairs and over-emphasize image-level feature extraction while neglecting fine-grained regional details essential for tasks like visual grounding and OCR. This limitation stems from two gaps: the scarcity of large-scale datasets with high-quality region-level annotations, and the absence of a dedicated pre-training paradigm that explicitly aligns fine-grained visual features with LLM semantic spaces.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images resized to 512×512 (padded by aspect ratio) and text prompts as input. During pretraining, prompts include normalized bounding box coordinates for Bbox2Caption tasks or local region descriptions for Caption2Bbox tasks; during downstream SFT, standard VQA prompts are used. Outputs include visual patch features projected into LLM token space, generated text captions (global or ≤10-word local), relative bounding box coordinates, and free-form VQA answers. Bbox coordinates are stored as relative values (normalized by image resolution) to eliminate absolute-coordinate dependence.

## Method

GranViT employs a two-stage pretraining-adaptation framework built on the LLaVA-Next architecture, initialized from SigLIP2. In Stage 1 (pretraining), the LLM (Qwen2.5-VL-1.5B) is frozen while the vision encoder and projector are trained via Bbox2Caption regression—given a bbox, the model generates a local caption—so that autoregressive caption loss propagates supervision directly to localized visual features; a self-distillation loss (MSE between teacher-encoded cropped regions and student-encoded ROIAlign'd features, with EMA-updated teacher) further constrains local feature quality. In Stage 2 (adaptation), the vision encoder is frozen and the projector plus LLM are tuned via Caption2Bbox regression—given a local caption, the model predicts its bbox—strengthening the LLM's ability to exploit fine-grained features for localization and enabling transfer to larger LLMs (Qwen2.5-3B/7B, LLaMA3-8B). The entire pipeline is trained on Gran-29M (29.5M images, 183.5M region annotations) with AdamW at lr 1e-5, batch size 256 on 128 Ascend 910B NPUs.

## Results

On the low-resolution evaluation setting, GranViT achieves state-of-the-art average scores of 80.78 on fine-grained recognition (surpassing SAILViT by 2.83) and 55.97 on OCR understanding (surpassing SAILViT by 2.64). On multimodal VQA, GranViT scores 53.57, comparable to SAILViT (53.85) with only a 0.3 gap. On multimodal reasoning, GranViT (50.60) trails SigLIP2 (51.09) by 0.4, as the method prioritizes fine-grained perception over reasoning. When transferred to larger LLMs (Qwen2.5-7B, LLaMA3-8B), GranViT maintains its lead on fine-grained and OCR tasks while achieving competitive or best VQA scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GranViT decisively outperforms all six baselines on fine-grained recognition, achieving 80.78 average vs. SAILViT (77.95), SigLIP2 (75.61), AIMv2 (73.50), InternViT (70.53), CLIP (66.41), and SigLip (57.67), with the largest margin over CLIP (+14.37). On OCR understanding, GranViT leads with 55.97 average, beating SAILViT (53.33) by 2.64 and SigLIP2 (51.55) by 4.42, with particularly large gains on DocVQA (+9.17 over SAILViT) and ChartQA (+4.72 over SAILViT). On multimodal VQA, GranViT (53.57) is essentially tied with SAILViT (53.85, gap of 0.3) and slightly ahead of AIMv2 (53.53), but trails SigLIP2 on HallusionBench (30.34 vs. 31.79). On multimodal reasoning, GranViT (50.60) underperforms SAILViT (52.02) and SigLIP2 (51.09), as the authors note this is expected since the method prioritizes fine-grained perception over reasoning. When transferred to larger LLMs (Qwen2.5-7B, LLaMA3-8B), GranViT extends its fine-grained lead (e.g., 92.98 on RefCOCO_testA with Qwen2.5-7B vs. SAILViT's 92.66) and achieves the best OCR scores (73.14 DocVQA, 31.69 InfoVQA with Qwen2.5-7B), confirming strong transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

GranViT extends the autoregressive vision-encoder pretraining line (AIMv2, InternViT, SAILViT) by introducing region-level fine-grained supervision (Bbox2Caption and Caption2Bbox tasks) into the pretraining loop, departing from the global image-caption alignment that dominates prior work. Architecturally it builds on the LLaVA-Next cascade (vision encoder → projector → LLM) and adapts the self-distillation mechanism from SILC, but localizes it via ROIAlign to explicitly constrain regional features. Compared to SAILViT, which injects task-specific world knowledge through SFT data during pretraining, GranViT focuses on generic fine-grained representation learning and demonstrates transferability across LLMs of varying sizes (1.5B→8B), positioning itself as a more modular vision backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Gran-29M dataset (29M images, 183M region-level annotations) is a substantial public resource that addresses the data scarcity bottleneck for fine-grained vision-language pretraining, and the two-stage pretraining-adaptation paradigm offers a practical recipe that practitioners can adopt to upgrade existing vision encoders without retraining from scratch. The strong transferability results (pretrained with a 1.5B LLM, then adapted to 3B/7B/8B LLMs) suggest the fine-grained features are LLM-agnostic, which is practically valuable for teams with diverse LLM backbones. Open questions remain: relative coordinates limit spatial precision for small or overlapping objects, the slight reasoning-task regression (−0.4 vs. SigLIP2) hints at a fine-grained-vs-reasoning trade-off, and whether the paradigm scales to video or 3D modalities is unexplored.
