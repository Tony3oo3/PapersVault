## Problem

Existing implicit degradation representations for real-world super-resolution suffer from two coupled failures: they model degradations as spatially uniform (ignoring that blur, noise, and sensor effects vary across an image), and their unconstrained latent spaces entangle degradation with image content, rendering the representation non-discriminative for the actual degradation factors. This paper addresses both gaps simultaneously by learning a per-pixel, spatially varying degradation posterior that is explicitly decoupled from content.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image y (processed as 256×256 tiles at test time) and produces a 4× super-resolved high-resolution image. In addition, it outputs a per-pixel 64-dimensional degradation representation structured as a diagonal Gaussian posterior: a 64-dim mode vector encoding degradation type and a 64-dim variance vector encoding local degradation severity/uncertainty. The representation is inferred from local Chebyshev neighborhoods via a shared amortized encoder (MANet backbone), so each pixel's posterior is computed in a single forward pass without per-pixel optimization.

## Method

SAVL (Spatially Amortized Variational Learning) models each pixel's degradation as a spatially varying Gaussian whose posterior q_ψ(r|y) is predicted by a MANet-based encoder from local image patches, with a spatially white Gaussian prior. The training objective combines two lanes sharing the same amortized networks: SAVL-LM maximizes a conditional ELBO (reconstruction + KL to the white prior), and SAVL-MIS upper-bounds the mutual information I(r;z) between the degradation field and the content code z=D(x) using a VIB-style term on I(r;y) and a Barber-Agakov lower bound on I(y;z,r). After unifying the three components (shared likelihood, shared posterior, shared prior), the composed loss reduces to a reconstruction term plus a (1+λ)-weighted KL term, making the mutual-information constraint implicit in the regularization strength. The learned posterior is injected into a HAT-based SR backbone via a DSCA module: the posterior mode drives channel-wise attention modulation (degradation type) and the posterior variance reweights spatial attention scores (degradation severity), placed before every HAB and OCAB block. Training proceeds in two stages—joint SAVL+SR optimization for 200K iterations with KL annealing over the first 50K, followed by SR fine-tuning with L1 (800K) and L1+perceptual+adversarial losses (600K)—after which the amortized estimators are frozen.

## Results

On the primary ×4 real-world SR benchmark (Table 2), SAVL achieves the best PSNR across all four datasets: 20.03 dB on DIV2K, 25.80 dB on RealSR, 28.27 dB on DRealSR, and 25.13 dB on SVSR, with 14.0M parameters and 499 GFLOPs. The gains are most pronounced on heterogeneous real-world data: +0.23 dB over KDSR on RealSR and +0.58 dB over LightBSR on DRealSR. On DRealSR, SAVL also achieves the highest SSIM (0.8139) and lowest LPIPS (0.2650), outperforming diffusion-based methods like StableSR (SSIM 0.7830) and DiffBIR (SSIM 0.7140) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAVL outperforms all 14 baselines on PSNR across every dataset. Against the strongest non-diffusion baselines, it gains +0.23 dB over KDSR on RealSR (25.80 vs 25.57), +0.51 dB over HAT-GAN on DRealSR (28.27 vs 27.76), and +0.58 dB over LightBSR on DRealSR (28.27 vs 27.69). On the synthetic DIV2K benchmark, the margin is smaller (+0.11 dB over RealESRGAN), consistent with the paper's claim that gains grow with data complexity. Against diffusion-based methods (DiffBIR, ResShift, StableSR), SAVL achieves substantially higher SSIM on DRealSR (0.8139 vs 0.7830 for StableSR, 0.7140 for DiffBIR) and lower LPIPS, indicating better structural fidelity without the hallucination artifacts typical of generative priors. On SVSR, SAVL leads KDSR by +0.04 dB and CDFormer by +0.06 dB in PSNR, while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.3181 vs 0.3436 and 0.3124 respectively). The method uses only 14.0M parameters, well below ResShift (118.6M), StableSR (919M), and DiffBIR (1670M), while matching or exceeding their fidelity metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAVL sits at the intersection of two threads in blind super-resolution: spatially variant degradation estimation (MANet, KoalaNet, Better-CMOS) and implicit degradation representation learning (KDSR, CDFormer, LightBSR, DASR). It departs from prior implicit methods in two key ways: (1) it models degradation as a per-pixel spatially varying Gaussian posterior rather than a single global code, and (2) it enforces an explicit mutual-information suppression constraint (combining a VIB-style upper bound on I(r;y) with a Barber-Agakov lower bound on I(y;z,r)) to actively filter content from the representation. The encoder backbone is MANet's local-receptive-field architecture, and the SR backbone is HAT, but the variational amortized inference framework and the DSCA dual-modulation module (mode→channel, variance→spatial) are novel contributions not present in either lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental tension in degradation-aware SR: modeling spatially variant degradations requires a per-pixel latent space, which inherently increases the risk of content leakage into the representation. The information-theoretic formulation provides a principled (rather than ad-hoc) mechanism for decoupling, and the resulting dual-component representation (mode for degradation type, variance for severity) offers interpretable, per-pixel guidance that prior global representations cannot provide. Limitations include the reliance on paired training data, the ~96-hour training cost, and the Gaussian mean-field assumption that may be insufficient for highly structured degradations. Open questions include whether the framework extends to video or multi-frame settings, whether tighter MI bounds (e.g., f-divergence variants) would further improve decoupling, and whether the approach generalizes to other restoration tasks beyond SR.
