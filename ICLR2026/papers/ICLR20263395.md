## Problem

Existing language model watermarks for autoregressive LLMs (ARLMs) rely on previously generated tokens to seed the watermark hash, an assumption that breaks down for diffusion language models (DLMs), which unmask tokens in arbitrary order. This paper is the first to address the gap by designing a watermark that operates over the distribution of context hashes rather than requiring a fixed, already-generated context, enabling reliable DLM watermarking for the first time.

## Input / Output

At each diffusion step, the method consumes the DLM's factorized probability distribution p(ω̃) over the vocabulary at each of L token positions (given a partially masked sequence) along with a secret watermark key ξ, and produces a distorted factorized distribution q(ω̃) that, when sampled, yields a watermarked sequence. The distortion is applied per-position via an exponential tilt of the logits, and the final watermarked text is detected using the standard Red-Green binomial test on the proportion of green tokens after deduplication of (context hash, token) pairs. The method supports variable context sets (e.g., C={-1}, C={-1,1}, C={-2,-1}) and works with different unmasking strategies (uniform, entropy-based, block).

## Method

The paper frames DLM watermarking as a constrained optimization problem: maximize the expected green-token ratio over generated sequences subject to a per-position KL divergence budget ε from the original model distribution. Theorem 3.1 provides a closed-form solution showing the optimal watermarked distribution is an exponential tilt q_t* ∝ p_t exp(δ_t · α_t), where α_t is the gradient of the energy function J(q) = (1/L)Σ h_t(q)ᵀ G q_t with respect to q_t. The energy function decomposes into two intuitive components: an 'expectation boost' that applies the Red-Green logit shift in expectation over the (possibly unknown) context hash distribution, and a 'predictive bias' that favors tokens whose hash makes neighboring tokens more likely to be green. Hash distributions h_t are computed efficiently via FFT-based convolution for SumHash or O(|C||Σ|) algebraic manipulation for MinHash, and the functional equation is solved with a single fixed-point iteration in practice. The detector is identical to the standard Red-Green ARLM watermark (binomial test on green token count), and the formulation recovers exactly the ARLM Red-Green watermark when restricted to left-to-right generation.

## Results

The proposed DLM watermark achieves >99% TPR@1 (true positive rate at 1% false positive rate) across all tested configurations on both LLADA-8B and DREAM-7B, while the naive ARLM-adaptation baseline reaches only 49–83% TPR@1 under the same quality budget. Quality degradation is minimal: log PPL increases by at most 0.36 over the unwatermarked model, and GPT-4o-as-a-judge scores remain within ~0.5 points. The watermark is robust to up to 30% word deletion or substitution and recovers detectability with longer sequences against paraphrasing and back-translation attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the naive ARLM-adaptation baseline (applying the Red-Green watermark only when full context is available), the proposed method wins decisively: 99% vs. 49–83% TPR@1 at comparable log PPL, with the gap largest under random remasking strategies. Against the order-agnostic Unigram watermark (Zhao et al., 2023), the proposed method achieves higher TPR@1 at the same distortion level (Fig. 5), and additionally controls Type-1 error properly (max FPR@1% of 1.7% vs. 17% for Unigram across 100 keys), while Unigram remains vulnerable to spoofing and scrubbing. Against PatternMark (Chen et al., 2025), the proposed method again outperforms in the detectability–quality trade-off, particularly in the low-distortion regime where the whole-sequence optimization (expectation boost + predictive bias) provides the largest advantage. In the appendix, the method also outperforms the AAR (Aaronson, 2023) and KTH (Kuditipudi et al., 2024) baselines in the low-distortion regime, and achieves comparable or better TPR@1 than GaussMark (Block et al., 2025) at similar quality distortion. The one area where the proposed method trades off is benchmark accuracy: on LLADA-8B with C={-2,-1}, accuracy drops from 59.4 to 54.5, whereas the baseline retains 58.5, though at a much lower TPR@1 (0.83 vs. 0.99).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Red-Green watermarking framework (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023; 2024) to diffusion language models, where the core assumption of sequential token generation with full prior context is violated. The key conceptual departure is formulating DLM watermarking as a constrained optimization problem over the joint distribution of context hashes and token probabilities, rather than relying on a fixed hash of already-generated tokens. The resulting scheme decomposes into an 'expectation boost' (applying the Red-Green shift in expectation over uncertain context) and a 'predictive bias' (favoring tokens whose hash makes neighboring tokens green), and provably recovers the standard Red-Green ARLM watermark when restricted to autoregressive generation. The paper also positions itself against order-agnostic watermarks (Unigram, PatternMark) that are directly applicable to DLMs but sacrifice security and Type-1 error control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As DLMs (LLADA, DREAM, Mercury) approach ARLM capabilities at lower cost and gain traction in multimodal and safety-critical applications, the absence of a reliable watermarking solution is a significant traceability gap that this paper closes. The >99% TPR at 1% FPR with minimal quality degradation makes the scheme practically deployable, and the robustness to context-aware substitution (a side-effect of the expectation boost) exceeds what ARLM watermarks achieve. Open questions include security against scrubbing/spoofing at low context sizes, applicability to open-source DLMs where the watermark key may be exposed, and whether the optimization framework extends to other discrete diffusion paradigms (e.g., continuous-state or latent-space DLMs). The paper also notes that for some model/hyperparameter combinations, benchmark accuracy drops can be non-negligible, suggesting a quality-strength trade-off that practitioners must navigate.
