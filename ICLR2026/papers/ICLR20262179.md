## Problem

Protein representation learning models encode proteins as flat residue sequences or single global embeddings, overlooking the modular, hierarchical substructure organization (domains, motifs, active sites) that underlies core molecular function. Although substructures are exhaustively cataloged in databases like Pfam and InterPro, they are rarely used as training signals or representation units, leaving a gap between decades of structural biology knowledge and how protein models are actually trained.

## Input / Output

The method consumes protein amino acid sequences (and optionally AlphaFold-predicted 3D structures for sequence-structure models like SaProt and ProSST) along with curated substructure annotations specifying which residue indices belong to each annotated substructure. Substructure representations are constructed by mean-pooling residue-level embeddings over the constituent residues of each annotated substructure, which may be non-contiguous in sequence. The tuned models then produce residue-level embeddings for downstream tasks (contact maps, variant effects, binding site prediction) and protein-level embeddings for function prediction (GO/EC terms, localization, thermostability), with the substructure classification head outputting type predictions across six hierarchical scales from 8-class secondary structure to homologous superfamilies.

## Method

Substructure-tuning is a model-agnostic supervised fine-tuning procedure that distills substructural knowledge into pretrained protein encoders. Given a base model's residue-level embeddings, mean pooling over each annotated substructure's constituent residues yields a substructure representation, which is fed to a type-specific single-hidden-layer MLP classifier; when tuning across multiple substructure classes (e.g., active sites, binding sites, conserved sites), each class uses its own prediction head and the total loss is the sum of cross-entropy losses across scales. Full fine-tuning of the base model is performed with Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) to prevent catastrophic forgetting of the original pretraining objective, using the Fisher information diagonal estimated from a single pass over the training set. The approach is validated across six state-of-the-art encoders (ESM2-150M/650M, ESM-C 300M/600M, SaProt-650M, ProSST-2048) and shown to improve function-related tasks while producing more consistent embeddings for substructure types never seen during tuning, including for models that already incorporate 3D structure as input.

## Results

Substructure-tuning (ST) with active, binding, and conserved site annotations consistently improves function-related protein-level tasks across all six models. For example, ESM-C 300M improves EC F_max from 0.688 to 0.761 and GO:MF F_max from 0.429 to 0.488; SaProt 650M improves EC from 0.778 to 0.839 and GO:MF from 0.538 to 0.584. Gains are smaller or neutral on localization tasks (e.g., subcellular localization drops from 0.703 to 0.681 for ESM-C 300M) and mixed on residue-level tasks (binding residue F_max improves from 0.367 to 0.411 for ESM-C 300M, but zero-shot DMS Spearman's ρ drops from 0.432 to 0.410). Silhouette scores for substructure embeddings increase substantially after ST (e.g., ESM-C 300M domain 'seen' silhouette from −0.184 to 0.486), and gains extend to substructure types never seen during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external competing methods; instead, the central comparison is between each base model and its substructure-tuned (+ST) counterpart under identical evaluation splits. Substructure-tuning produces consistent gains on function-centric tasks (EC, GO:MF, GO:BP, thermostability) across all six models, with the largest relative improvement seen in ESM-C 300M (EC +7.3 F_max points, GO:MF +5.9 points). Importantly, gains persist for sequence-structure models (SaProt, ProSST-2048) that already incorporate 3D structure as input, indicating that substructural supervision is complementary to global structural information. Effects on localization tasks (GO:CC, binary/subcellular localization) are neutral to slightly negative, and residue-level variant-effect prediction (zero-shot DMS) shows small degradations, which the authors attribute to substructure-tuning encouraging shared representations within a substructure at the expense of fine-grained per-residue distinctions. An ablation in the appendix shows that substructure-tuning outperforms existing structural distillation methods (e.g., ESM-S fold classification) on function prediction, and that aggressive task-specific finetuning largely erases the substructure-tuning benefit, suggesting the gains are most relevant in frozen-encoder or lightweight-head settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of protein representation learning and structural biology by introducing substructure-aware training as a distinct paradigm. Prior work on integrating structural information into protein models has focused on residue-level signals (contact maps, secondary structure tokens in SES-Adapter), global structural distillation (ESM-S, ISM), or intra-protein multi-view contrastive objectives (GearNet), but has largely ignored the recurrent, evolutionarily conserved substructures (domains, motifs, active sites) that are systematically cataloged in databases like InterPro and Pfam. Magneton departs from these approaches by using supervised classification on cross-protein conserved substructures as the training signal, and by providing the first large-scale curated dataset and benchmark specifically designed for this purpose. The method is model-agnostic and orthogonal to existing sequence-structure integration strategies, positioning substructure-tuning as a complementary fine-tuning step rather than a replacement for structural inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Magneton environment and its 530K-protein, 1.7M-substructure dataset fill a critical gap in protein ML resources, providing the first systematic infrastructure for studying substructure-aware representations. The finding that substructure-tuning improves function prediction even for models that already incorporate 3D structure (SaProt, ProSST) suggests that functional modularity and global geometry carry complementary information, opening a path toward models that explicitly encode the hierarchical organization of proteins. Key limitations temper the impact: gains are modest and inconsistent across task types (positive for function, negative for localization and variant effect), aggressive task-specific finetuning erases the benefits, and the current approach is a relatively straightforward supervised fine-tuning rather than an architectural innovation. Open questions include whether hierarchical or graph-based architectures can more stably integrate substructural information, how to balance the vastly different scales of substructure types (millions of secondary structures vs. tens of thousands of active sites), and whether extending beyond SwissProt to the full UniProtKB long tail would yield deeper insights.
