## Problem

Prior embodied agents either incorporate textual reasoning (via VLMs) or visual imagination (via world models) but not both in an end-to-end manner. Existing hybrid systems connect separate VLM and visual generative modules, preventing joint optimization and leaving the mutual benefits between reasoning and world models underexplored. This paper asks whether a single autoregressive Transformer can synergize reasoning and imagination to achieve superior sample efficiency and generalization in open-world embodied tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260695/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 640×360 first-person RGB frames (70° FOV), a natural-language task instruction, the previous action, and inventory state, with camera rotations quantized into 5-degree discrete text tokens. It autoregressively emits a structured sequence of textual chain-of-thought reasoning, a low-level keyboard/mouse action (14 discrete types), and a predicted next frame encoded as discrete VQ tokens (LlamaGen 16×16). A fixed '<Imagine:>' token prefixes internally simulated frames, enabling multi-step lookahead where imagined states are recursively fed back for self-review before committing to a real action.

## Method

RIG is a single 1.3B-parameter autoregressive Transformer (Janus backbone with SigLIP vision encoder and LlamaGen VQ tokenizer) trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss to jointly predict reasoning, action, and next-frame tokens in one sequence. Training follows a progressive four-stage data pipeline: (S0–S1) supervised fine-tuning on human and STEVE-1 trajectories for visual-action alignment, (S2) GPT-4o-annotated reasoning inserted before each action, (S3) Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning where GPT-4o reviews RIG-basic's failed trajectories against STEVE-1's superior ones and generates corrective reasoning (only the positive trajectory is optimized), and (S4) temporal alignment that behavior-clones autoregressive dream rollouts into the real environment for frame-by-frame grounding. The key mechanism is that at inference the model first generates a multi-step 'dream trajectory' of imagined frames, then reviews it in reasoning to detect errors and emit a revised action, providing test-time scaling where the number of lookahead steps is adjustable. This joint modeling of reasoning–action–dynamics yields 17× sample efficiency (111 h vs. ~2000 h) and state-of-the-art results on embodied, generation, and reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

On embodied tasks in Minecraft (Tool setting), RIG-lookahead achieves 94.1% accuracy and 246.6 collected samples, while RIG-basic reaches 93.4% accuracy and 101.1 samples — all using only 111 hours of training data, a 17× reduction over prior methods requiring ~2000 hours. For image generation, RIG-lookahead attains FID 77.6 and PSNR 18.4, substantially outperforming the generation-only baseline (FID 214.5). On reasoning, the full model scores 8.5/10 (blind-graded), compared to 6.1 for reasoning without generation, demonstrating the synergy between imagination and reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIG outperforms all prior embodied-agent baselines while using dramatically less data: 111 hours versus VPT's 1962h, STEVE-1's ~2000h, Jarvis-1's ~2000h, and MineDreamer's 2101h (139h + VPT). Against MineDreamer specifically, RIG is two orders of magnitude cheaper at inference (6×10¹² vs. 3×10¹⁴ FLOPs per step) while achieving higher task accuracy (94.1% vs. MineDreamer's lower reported figures) and collection efficiency. Compared to STEVE-1, RIG surpasses it in task collection efficiency (246.6 vs. STEVE-1's lower sample counts) despite using 17× less environment interaction. On general VQA benchmarks (Table A5), RIG remains on par with its Janus-1.3B backbone (VQAv2: 77.1 vs. 77.3; GQA: 59.4 vs. 59.1) and shows small consistent gains on exam-style reasoning (MMMU: 30.9 vs. 30.5; MM-Vet: 35.0 vs. 34.3), confirming no catastrophic forgetting from embodied specialization. In a direct qualitative comparison with GPT-4o under identical prompts, RIG achieves a 98% valid-action rate versus GPT-4o's 11%, with RIG correctly detecting unreachable targets via imagination while GPT-4o issues invalid attack commands.

## Relation to Prior Work

RIG builds on the Janus-1.3B unified understanding-and-generation Transformer as its backbone, extending it with a progressive data pipeline that interleaves textual reasoning, low-level actions, and next-frame prediction within a single autoregressive sequence. It departs from prior embodied-agent systems (VPT, STEVE-1, Jarvis-1) that rely on separate VLM reasoning heads or pre-trained low-level controllers, and from hybrid world-model systems (MineDreamer) that couple a visual generator with an RL policy as distinct modules. The key conceptual departure is treating reasoning, action, and visual imagination as a single joint sequence-to-sequence objective trained with plain cross-entropy, rather than as separately optimized components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 17× reduction in required interaction data (111h vs. ~2000h) and the natural test-time scaling via variable-length lookahead trajectories offer a practical path toward more data-efficient and robust embodied agents. However, evaluation is confined to Minecraft at 1.3B scale, the data pipeline depends on GPT-4o for reasoning and review annotations (limiting reproducibility and raising cost concerns), and the 17× efficiency claim compares heterogeneous data sources (video pre-training vs. agent-collected trajectories). Open questions include whether the reasoning–imagination synergy transfers to physical robotics or higher-dimensional action spaces, whether the approach scales beyond 1.3B parameters, and whether the GPT-4o annotation bottleneck can be replaced by self-generated rollouts at scale.
