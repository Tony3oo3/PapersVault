## Problem

Existing work approximates the model-specific (MS) Pareto frontier for fair classification via post-processing (random flipping or threshold adjustment), but this yields a suboptimal frontier that can be exceeded by other methods, making it an unreliable upper bound. Furthermore, the Pareto optimal trade-off between fairness notions (e.g., demographic parity vs. equalized odds) has not been properly characterized, and the dependence of this trade-off on accuracy remains unquantified.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard classification inputs (tabular features for COMPAS, Adult, Drug; 178×218 face images for CelebA) along with a binary sensitive group label, and produces both a class prediction and a Pareto frontier curve parameterized by the fairness constraint ε. The key intermediate representation is the 4-dimensional confusion vector z = [TPR₀, TNR₀, TNR₁, TNR₁], from which accuracy, DP, and EOd are computed as linear (or piecewise-linear) transformations. For multi-class tasks, the confusion vector extends to 2k dimensions (k classes × 2 groups).

## Method

The paper reformulates the MS Pareto optimal fairness-accuracy trade-off as a convex optimization problem over the confusion vector z, with the feasible region K bounded by the baseline model's overall and group-wise accuracies, eliminating dependence on any specific fairness intervention. For the practical fair retraining framework, the last linear classification head h₂ is retrained with group-dependent bias terms b₀, b₁ (i.e., the input is augmented with group indicator features), optimizing a Lagrangian combining accuracy and a fairness penalty (EOd or DP). Theoretically, the paper proves that under concave ROC curves, this retraining achieves strictly better EOd-optimum accuracy than random flipping, and derives a closed-form estimate of the accuracy drop at the EOd optimum via a Pade approximation of the error function. The formulation is further extended to characterize the Pareto frontier between DP and EOd under varying accuracy constraints, revealing that the two notions become compatible only when accuracy drops to that of a constant predictor.

## Results

On the COMPAS dataset, the proposed method achieves 63.91% accuracy with 1.04% EOd, reducing the baseline EOd of 38.89% by over 97% while incurring only a 1.67% accuracy drop. On Adult, it reaches 83.47% accuracy with 3.11% EOd (vs. 20.72% baseline), and on CelebA, 81.53% accuracy with 3.04% EOd (vs. 26.42% baseline). For the multi-class Drug dataset, the method achieves 68.76% accuracy with 5.04% EOd, outperforming DFR (67.64%, 6.17%) and SELF (68.13%, 6.34%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best EOd across all three binary datasets (COMPAS: 1.04%, Adult: 3.11%, CelebA: 3.04%) while maintaining the highest or near-highest accuracy, outperforming all post-processing baselines (FACT, Eq.Odds, G-STAR, FOC) on the joint fairness-accuracy trade-off. Against G-STAR (Jang et al., 2022), the closest post-processing competitor, the proposed method achieves strictly better EOd on COMPAS (1.04% vs. 2.18%) and CelebA (3.04% vs. 5.45%) with higher accuracy, though G-STAR edges out slightly on Adult EOd (2.95% vs. 3.11%) at the cost of 1.34% lower accuracy. Against last-layer retraining baselines DFR (Kirichenko et al., 2022) and SELF (LaBonte et al., 2023), the proposed method consistently achieves 2–4× lower EOd with comparable or better accuracy (e.g., on COMPAS: 1.04% vs. 3.34% for DFR, 4.95% for SELF). On the DP-accuracy trade-off, the method matches or beats FOC (Xian et al., 2023) in accuracy while achieving comparable DP (e.g., Adult: 83.64% acc / 3.03% DP vs. FOC's 83.25% / 3.17%), and clearly outperforms G-STAR on CelebA DP (2.93% vs. 5.16%). On the multi-class Drug dataset, the method outperforms both DFR and SELF on both accuracy and EOd, validating the extension to multi-class settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the post-processing framework of FACT (Kim et al., 2020) and the threshold-adaptation approach of G-STAR (Jang et al., 2022) for characterizing fairness-accuracy trade-offs, as well as the last-layer retraining line of work (Kirichenko et al., 2022; LaBonte et al., 2023). Its key departure is proving that the existing model-specific Pareto frontier computed via random flipping is strictly suboptimal, then reformulating the problem as a convex optimization over the confusion vector that bypasses any specific fairness intervention. The paper further extends the Pareto frontier analysis from the single fairness-accuracy axis to a two-dimensional fairness-fairness (DP vs. EOd) trade-off, a dimension prior work had not properly addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The suboptimality proof means that prior Pareto frontier evaluations of fairness interventions (e.g., FACT, G-STAR) may have been misleading, as method curves could appear to exceed a frontier that was not actually the best achievable. The nearly flat EOd-accuracy frontier challenges the common narrative that error-based fairness necessarily costs accuracy, while the DP-accuracy frontier confirms the inherent trade-off, providing a nuanced picture. Open questions include extending the analysis to model-agnostic settings, handling distribution shift, and generalizing to more than two fairness notions simultaneously. The practical last-layer retraining method with group-dependent bias offers a lightweight, provably superior alternative to post-processing for practitioners.
