## Problem

Existing token-level data selection methods for LLM supervised fine-tuning (e.g., RHO-1, TokenCleaning) require training or accessing an additional reference model to compute excess loss, incurring substantial time and resource overhead. Moreover, they rely solely on loss-based signals, which cannot preserve semantically important tokens that are not favored by loss metrics, leading to the discarding of instruction-relevant content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262680/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes prompt-response SFT pairs (standardized with <|User|>/<|Assistant|> tags, max 2048 tokens) and a pretrained base LLM, using the base model itself as the history model (or an EMA-updated version) rather than a separately trained reference. It produces a fine-tuned LLM (via LoRA rank-64 or full-parameter SFT) along with a per-token binary mask that selects the top-ρ fraction of response tokens for gradient computation; prompt tokens are never selected. The attention-based component requires only a single-layer forward pass hook for hidden-state retrieval, making it compatible with FlashAttention without materializing full attention matrices.

## Method

ssToken combines two orthogonal token-scoring signals. First, it computes Retrospective Excess Loss (REL) as the per-token loss difference between a history model (the base model before SFT, optionally EMA-updated) and the current model, replacing the external reference model with a self-modulated signal that adapts along the training trajectory. Second, it estimates semantic-aware token importance by summing, across all attention heads at a deep layer, the attention weights each response token assigns to the prompt tokens, capturing instruction-following relevance that loss alone misses. The two signals are normalized to [0,1] and combined via a balance coefficient γ (default 0.5), and the top-ρ fraction of response tokens by combined score are retained for the SFT loss. A lightweight two-stage implementation caches history-model losses in a single pre-pass, so REL is computed via a single tensor subtraction during training, adding negligible overhead.

## Results

ssToken achieves the best average score across all four base models (3B–14B) on ten general-domain benchmarks, improving over full-data SFT by 4.3% (LLaMA-3.2-3B: 52.50 vs. 50.35), 3.4% (LLaMA-3.1-8B: 59.44 vs. 57.49), 1.3% (Qwen-2.5-7B: 60.48 vs. 59.73), and 2.1% (Qwen-2.5-14B: 65.84 vs. 64.50). Gains are most pronounced on instruction-following and QA benchmarks (e.g., TyDiQA, TriviaQA, AGIEval), while knowledge-intensive tasks (MMLU, ARC) show minimal change. The method also outperforms prior token-selection baselines by up to 2.8% (on Qwen-2.5-7B) while avoiding the need to train a separate reference model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ssToken outperforms both prior token-selection baselines (RHO-1 and TokenCleaning) on the average score across all four model families. Against RHO-1, ssToken gains +0.85 (3B), +0.43 (8B), +1.99 (7B), and +1.04 (14B) in average score; against TokenCleaning, the margins are +0.63, +0.30, +1.65, and +0.97 respectively. A notable finding is that RHO-1 and TokenCleaning degrade to near or below full-data performance on the Qwen family (e.g., RHO-1 scores 58.49 vs. full-data 59.73 on Qwen-2.5-7B), whereas ssToken consistently delivers positive gains across both LLaMA and Qwen. The largest absolute advantage over prior methods appears on QA and reasoning benchmarks (TyDiQA, LogiQA, AGIEval), which the authors attribute to the attention-based semantic signal complementing loss-based selection. Additionally, ssToken avoids the reference-model training overhead required by both RHO-1 and TokenCleaning, resulting in lower total training time while achieving higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the token-level data selection paradigm introduced by RHO-1 (Lin et al., 2024) and refined by TokenCleaning (Pang et al., 2025), both of which rely on Excess Loss computed against an externally trained reference model. The key conceptual departure is replacing that fixed reference with a self-modulated history model (the base model or an EMA-updated checkpoint), making token selection reference-free and dynamically aligned with the training trajectory. Additionally, the paper introduces an attention-based semantic importance signal that is orthogonal to loss-based scoring, addressing the observation that loss alone cannot capture instruction-relevant tokens that are semantically important but not loss-favored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need to train or access a separate reference model, ssToken removes a significant practical bottleneck (extra training time, resource cost, and sensitivity to reference model quality) that limited the adoption of prior token-selection methods. The attention-based component provides a principled, FlashAttention-compatible mechanism for incorporating semantic cues into token filtering, which is particularly beneficial for instruction-following and QA tasks. Open questions remain around truly data-driven adaptive selection ratios (the current schedule is a simple decay), the behavior under multi-epoch or continual-learning settings where EMA updates may matter more, and whether the gains observed on smaller models (3B–8B) persist at much larger scales where the base model's pretraining already captures most task-relevant patterns.
