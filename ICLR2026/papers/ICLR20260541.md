## Problem

Existing alignment-stage defenses against harmful fine-tuning attacks (e.g., Vaccine, RepNoise, TAR) increase the cost of misalignment but fail to address the model's inherent trainability on harmful data, leaving them vulnerable to stronger attacks with larger learning rates or more harmful samples. The paper identifies that the root cause is that harmful-data gradients still effectively reduce the harmful fine-tuning loss, and no prior method makes the model intrinsically self-destruct under such optimization.

## Input / Output

SEAM consumes three datasets: an adversarial set of 4K harmful prompt-response pairs (BeaverTails), a benign set of 4K harmless pairs (Alpaca), and an alignment set constructed by querying GPT-4o for refusal responses to the adversarial prompts. The base LLM parameters (e.g., Llama2-7b) are updated via AdamW for 500 steps with a Hessian-free gradient estimate that requires only forward passes at perturbed parameter locations (radius ε=1e-3) rather than explicit Hessian computation. The output is a self-destructive model that retains near-identical zero-shot and fine-tuning utility on benign tasks but undergoes catastrophic performance collapse (ZS dropping below 30%) when subjected to high-intensity harmful fine-tuning.

## Method

SEAM's core mechanism is a self-destructive loss L_sd that penalizes the cosine similarity between the adversarial gradient g_a and the benign gradient g_b, engineering a 'gradient trap' where gradient descent on harmful data effectively performs gradient ascent on benign data. This is amplified by an unlearning loss L_ul (layer-wise gradient ascent on adversarial data, log-transformed to prevent catastrophic forgetting) that extends the number of optimization steps an adversary needs, and a utility preservation loss L_up (SFT on refusal responses) that stabilizes the model's current alignment. To make the self-destructive loss tractable without computing the full Hessian, the paper derives a Hessian-free gradient estimate via first-order Taylor expansion of the gradient at perturbed parameter locations, with a provable error bound of O(ε) depending on local Hessian Lipschitz constants. The overall objective L = L_ul + α·L_up + β·L_sd is optimized with AdamW, and the resulting model creates a no-win dilemma: low-intensity attacks yield minimal harmfulness, while high-intensity attacks trigger self-destruction rendering the model unusable.

## Results

SEAM preserves model utility with minimal degradation: zero-shot average drops only from 51.6% (Base) to 50.8% (SEAM), and fine-tuning scores are comparable or slightly better (e.g., AlpacaEval 43.7% vs 40.4%). Under harmful fine-tuning attacks, SEAM achieves the lowest harmfulness score (HS) among all defenses at every learning rate tested (e.g., HS=2.6% at η=2e-5 vs Base 47.3%), while uniquely exhibiting catastrophic zero-shot collapse under high-intensity attacks (ZS drops to 26.6% at η=2e-4, near random-guess level). This creates a 'no-win' dilemma: weak attacks fail to increase harmfulness, while strong attacks destroy the model's general capabilities. Training overhead is 135.7 minutes, comparable to other alignment-stage defenses (range: 63.8–161.9 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEAM is compared against five alignment-stage defenses (Vaccine, Targeted Vaccine, RMU, TAR, RepNoise) and Booster. Under strong attacks (η≥8e-5), all baselines show substantial harmfulness increases (HS rising to 70–80%+), while SEAM maintains HS below 6% and triggers self-destructive ZS collapse. Under weak attacks (η=2e-5), SEAM achieves the lowest HS (2.6%) among all methods, outperforming Vaccine, RMU, TAR, T-Vaccine, and RepNoise which all show higher residual harmfulness. The key differentiator is that baselines preserve ZS but fail on HS under strong attacks, whereas SEAM sacrifices ZS to guarantee low HS — a fundamentally different trade-off. On utility preservation (Table 1), SEAM matches or slightly exceeds the Base model on fine-tuning tasks (AlpacaEval 43.7% vs 40.4%) while maintaining comparable zero-shot scores. Training time for SEAM (135.7 min) is moderate: slower than Vaccine (63.8 min) and RMU (67.2 min) but faster than RepNoise (161.9 min), and comparable to TAR (126.2 min) and Booster (111.3 min).

## Relation to Prior Work

SEAM extends the self-destructive model concept introduced by Henderson et al. (2023), which sought parameter states amenable to benign fine-tuning but resistant to harmful fine-tuning, by engineering explicit 'gradient traps' that couple the optimization trajectories of benign and harmful data. Unlike prior alignment-stage defenses (Vaccine, Booster, TAR, RepNoise, RMU) that increase the cost of harmful fine-tuning without addressing the model's inherent trainability on harmful data, SEAM ensures that any gradient descent on harmful objectives necessarily implements gradient ascent on benign objectives. The practical Hessian-free gradient estimate with theoretical error bounds (Theorem 1) is a key implementation contribution that makes the self-destructive loss tractable for large models, building on the layer-wise gradient ascent technique from RepNoise (Rosati et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEAM creates a fundamental 'no-win dilemma' for adversaries: weak attacks leave the model safe, while strong attacks trigger catastrophic self-destruction, rendering the model unusable. This reframes LLM safety from a cat-and-mouse arms race into a structural property of the model itself. Key open questions include whether adaptive attacks can circumvent the gradient trap (e.g., optimizing for specific harmful tasks while preserving utility), whether the required benign dataset can be automatically identified or generated, and whether the approach scales to very large LLMs beyond the 7-8B models evaluated. The self-destructive modeling paradigm could influence how future foundation models are designed with intrinsic resilience against malicious manipulation.
