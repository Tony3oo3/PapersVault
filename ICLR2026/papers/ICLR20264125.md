## Problem

Graph-based deep multi-view clustering methods suffer from three unresolved issues: static graph structures fail to capture global cross-view semantic relationships, contrastive learning is corrupted by false negative samples that accumulate through backpropagation, and existing alignment strategies face a fundamental trade-off between enforcing cross-view consistency and preserving view-specific discriminative features.

## Input / Output

The method accepts m heterogeneous view matrices {X^v ∈ R^{n×d_v}} with potentially different feature dimensions per view (e.g., 3 views for Yale, 6 views for Caltech101-20). Each view is encoded by a dedicated 3-layer MLP (1024→512→256) producing 256-dimensional embeddings. The final output is cluster assignments obtained by applying spectral clustering to a symmetrically normalized, diagonally enhanced consensus affinity matrix that has been refined through multi-scale diffusion.

## Method

MANGO integrates four modules: (1) a self-expressive module that reconstructs inputs via sparse coefficient matrices with hybrid L1/Frobenius regularization; (2) a power-smoothing random walk contrastive learning module that computes a t-step transition matrix M^t, interpolates it with the identity via parameter η to form a target distribution T used as structured negative weights in InfoNCE, and applies a β-power transformation to the negative sample sum to nonlinearly suppress false-negative influence; (3) a view consistency module that learns bidirectional mapping functions between view embeddings to maximize mutual information via cosine-distance minimization; and (4) an entropy-guided multi-scale diffusion module that computes affinity matrices at T+1 diffusion steps, weights each scale by the inverse of its row-wise Shannon entropy (lower entropy indicates clearer cluster structure), and fuses them into a single refined graph before spectral clustering.

## Results

MANGO achieves state-of-the-art or near-state-of-the-art clustering performance across 12 multi-view datasets. Headline results include 92.6% ACC on ORL (vs. 88.2% for the next-best MVD), 95.9% ACC on BBC-Sport (vs. 93.6% for LSGMC), 94.9% ACC on MSRC-v1 (vs. 93.8% for LSGMC), and 88.7% ACC on ALOI-100 (a 13.4-point margin over DIVIDE's 75.3%). On the large-scale MNIST-3V dataset (60,000 samples), MANGO reaches 98.7% ACC / 96.2% NMI / 97.2% ARI, slightly trailing CANDY (99.4% ACC) but outperforming all other methods. Ablation studies confirm that the multi-scale diffusion module contributes the largest single gain (e.g., +5.9% ACC on ALOI-100, +3.4% on STL10) over a fixed-step diffusion variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MANGO outperforms all eight baselines on the majority of 12 datasets. Against the strongest shallow method LSGMC, MANGO wins on 10/12 datasets (e.g., +1.8% ACC on Yale, +4.4% on ORL, +2.3% on BBC-Sport), with LSGMC remaining competitive only on HandWritten (97.6% vs. 97.8%) and BBC-Sport (93.6% vs. 95.9%). The deep contrastive baselines DIVIDE and CANDY are the closest competitors: DIVIDE is outperformed on 11/12 datasets (notably +13.4% ACC on ALOI-100, +4.3% on STL10), while CANDY edges out MANGO only on MNIST-3V (99.4% vs. 98.7% ACC). MVD (Li et al., 2023) is surpassed on 11/12 datasets, with the largest gap on ORL (+4.4% ACC) and ALOI-100 (+18.4% ACC). The remaining baselines—MFLVC, MSESC, CVCL, and SCM—are consistently outperformed by wide margins across nearly all datasets, with MANGO achieving 5–30+ point ACC advantages in most cases. Notably, LSGMC and MVD suffer out-of-memory failures on MNIST-3V (60K samples), whereas MANGO completes training and clustering successfully.

## Relation to Prior Work

MANGO sits at the intersection of three established lines of work in multi-view clustering: graph-based deep MVC (Wen et al. 2024, Ren et al. 2024), contrastive multi-view learning (CVCL, CANDY, DIVIDE), and diffusion-based graph refinement (MVD, Li et al. 2023). It departs from prior contrastive MVC methods by replacing the standard InfoNCE negative-sample treatment with a random-walk-derived transition matrix and a β-power smoothing operation, and from prior diffusion approaches (which typically use a fixed number of steps) by introducing an entropy-weighted multi-scale fusion of diffusion matrices. The self-expressive reconstruction module follows the MSESC lineage, while the view consistency module draws on mutual-information lower-bound alignment (Hassani & Khasahmadi 2020, Trosten et al. 2023) but applies it through shared structural embeddings rather than explicit cross-view projection networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses three practical pain points in graph-based MVC—static graph topology, false-negative contamination in contrastive losses, and the consistency-vs-specificity trade-off—that are widely acknowledged but rarely tackled simultaneously. The entropy-guided multi-scale fusion is a lightweight mechanism that could be transplanted to other graph-refinement pipelines beyond MVC. However, the theoretical grounding for the β-power smoothing is limited to an intuitive 'nonlinear smoothing' argument, and the view consistency module's mutual information lower bound is standard; the paper does not analyze computational complexity of the multi-scale diffusion (which scales as O(T·n²) per step) or discuss failure modes when views are highly heterogeneous or incomplete. Open questions include whether the random-walk correction generalizes to incomplete multi-view settings and whether the entropy weighting remains stable when the number of diffusion steps T is large.
