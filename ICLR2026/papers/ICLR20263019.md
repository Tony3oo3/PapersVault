## Problem

Multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO) requires per-task surrogate fitting and acquisition optimization, making it slow, sensitive to modeling choices, and myopic in its one-step look-ahead. No existing method is simultaneously end-to-end amortized (no per-task surrogate), natively multi-objective, and capable of transferring across varying input and output dimensionalities without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263019/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

TAMO consumes the full optimization history as a set of input-output pairs, a discrete candidate query set of size N_q (default 2048), and scalar metadata (current step t, total budget T). It outputs a categorical policy over the candidate pool via softmax on per-candidate acquisition utilities, from which the next design is selected greedily at inference. The architecture is dimension-agnostic: each observation contributes O(1) tokens regardless of d_x or d_y, so the same pretrained model handles tasks with 1–4 inputs and 1–3 objectives without architectural changes. A secondary prediction head outputs a K-component Gaussian mixture for in-context regression, used only during training.

## Method

TAMO is a single transformer backbone with a novel dimension-agnostic embedder that maps each scalar input/output dimension through learnable scalar-to-vector networks, applies randomly sampled positional tokens to break permutation symmetry, and mean-pools across dimensions to yield a fixed-size representation per observation. The encoder-decoder is split into B1 layers where history tokens self-attend and query tokens cross-attend to history, followed by B2 layers where only query tokens and a small set of task-specific tokens (time-budget, aggregated input-dimension, task-type) remain, with an attention mask preventing query-query interaction. Training proceeds in two phases: a prediction warm-up minimizing NLL of a GMM head over in-context regression, then joint optimization with REINFORCE on a trajectory-level hypervolume reward normalized by the task's optimal hypervolume, encouraging long-horizon Pareto-front discovery rather than one-step gains. At inference, a single forward pass over the history and candidate set yields the next query, reducing proposal latency by 50–1000× compared to GP-based MOBO while matching or improving Pareto quality.

## Results

TAMO achieves 50×–1000× lower cumulative wall-clock proposal time than all GP-based MOBO baselines, since each proposal requires only a single transformer forward pass. On the in-distribution GP-DX2-DY2 benchmark, TAMO matches the best GP baselines in HV-based simple regret; on out-of-distribution analytic testbeds (Ackley-Rastrigin, Ackley-Rosenbrock), TAMO attains the strongest regret, while on Branin-Currin qNEHVI and qNParEGO remain slightly ahead. On the real-world Oil Sorbent problem, TAMO (pretrained solely on synthetic GPs) yields the best performance, closely followed by qNParEGO. On the OOD Laser-Plasma task (d_x=4, d_y=3, unseen at pretraining), TAMO improves over BOFormer but trails conventional MOBO baselines in regret while retaining orders-of-magnitude speed advantages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against qNParEGO (Knowles, 2006), TAMO matches or exceeds performance on GP-DX2-DY2, Ackley-Rastrigin, and Ackley-Rosenbrock, but loses on Branin-Currin where qNParEGO's scalarization-based exploration handles the long-length-scale landscape better; on Oil Sorbent, qNParEGO is the closest competitor, trailing TAMO slightly. Against qNEHVI (Daulton et al., 2020), TAMO is competitive on GP-DX2-DY2 and wins on Ackley-Rastrigin and Ackley-Rosenbrock, but qNEHVI edges ahead on Branin-Currin and on the HPO-3DGS 'Mic' scene. Against qHVKG (Daulton et al., 2023a), TAMO is broadly competitive across all synthetic benchmarks, with the speed advantage being the primary differentiator. Against BOFormer (Hung et al., 2025), which amortizes only the acquisition function while retaining a per-task GP surrogate, TAMO is consistently faster (BOFormer still incurs GP fitting overhead) and achieves lower regret on Laser-Plasma; on in-distribution synthetic tasks the two are comparable in regret, but TAMO's single-pass inference gives it a decisive wall-clock advantage (50×–1000× faster). Overall, TAMO's key differentiator is the combination of competitive Pareto quality with dramatically reduced proposal latency, making it particularly attractive for parallel or time-sensitive evaluation loops.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAMO sits at the intersection of three research threads: multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MOBO), amortized/meta-learning for black-box optimization, and dimension-agnostic neural processes. The closest prior work is BOFormer (Hung et al., 2025), which uses sequence modeling and RL to mitigate myopia in MOBO but still requires a task-specific GP surrogate and is tied to a fixed number of objectives. NAP (Maraval et al., 2023) achieves end-to-end amortized BO but only for single-objective problems, while DANP (Lee et al., 2025) introduces dimension-agnostic encoding for prediction but not for optimization. TAMO's key departure is combining all three properties—end-to-end amortization (no per-task surrogate or acquisition), native multi-objectivity, and dimension-agnostic input/output handling—into a single transformer policy trained with REINFORCE on hypervolume over full trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single pretrained model replaces the entire per-task GP-fitting-and-acquisition-optimization loop with one forward pass, yielding 50–1000× lower proposal latency while matching Pareto quality under tight budgets. This opens a path toward foundation-model-style, plug-and-play optimizers for scientific discovery workflows (drug design, materials, HPO) where evaluations are parallel or time-sensitive. Key limitations include reliance on synthetic GP pretraining (which may miss real-world structure like long length scales or anisotropic metrics), the discrete candidate-pool assumption that restricts applicability to continuous or combinatorial design spaces, and the observed performance gap on Branin-Currin. Open questions include how pretraining corpus composition (kernel families, smoothness, multi-output correlations) drives transfer, and whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional or generative action spaces.
