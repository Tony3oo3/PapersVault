## Problem

Real-world physical observations are inherently incomplete and irregularly sampled, yet existing diffusion-based imputation methods either require complete training data, assume i.i.d. pixel-level missingness, or lack convergence guarantees. This paper asks whether a diffusion model trained exclusively on incomplete samples (with no complete ground truth available) can provably recover the full data distribution, and how the structure of observation patterns should influence the training procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260372/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes partially observed spatial-temporal fields (e.g., 32×32 or 64×64 grids over 50–100 temporal frames) along with binary observation masks indicating which spatial locations are measured, and produces complete imputed fields. Critically, training data contains no complete samples—only partial observations with permanently missing regions. At inference, the model receives the full available observation and reconstructs missing regions via ensemble averaging over K randomly sampled context masks at a small noise level δ, or through multi-step diffusion sampling with per-step ensemble denoising.

## Method

The core contribution is a conditional diffusion training paradigm using data matching on incomplete data, where each observed sample is strategically partitioned into a context subset (fed to the network) and a query subset (used for loss computation). Theorem 1 proves the optimal network output equals the conditional expectation E[x₀ | M_ctx ⊙ x_obs,t, M_ctx] for all dimensions with positive query probability, and Theorem 2 shows ensemble averaging over K context masks converges to the full conditional expectation E[x₀ | x_obs,t, M]. The partitioning strategy must match the observation structure—block-wise sampling for block-structured masks, pixel-wise for i.i.d. masks—to guarantee every dimension receives gradient updates. At inference, single-step sampling at t=δ with K ensemble evaluations suffices for well-posed problems, while multi-step sampling with per-step ensemble denoising handles high-uncertainty cases.

## Results

On pixel-level masking, the proposed method achieves the lowest MSE across all tested observation rates: 0.223×10⁻³ on Navier-Stokes at 80% observed points and 1.931×10⁻³ at 20%, outperforming the closest diffusion baseline AmbientDiff (0.238 and 2.043 respectively). On ERA5 climate data, the method reaches 0.250×10⁻² at 20% observation and 1.229×10⁻² at just 1% observed points, maintaining a consistent edge over all baselines. Under block-wise masking (5/9 blocks observed), the method with correct block-wise partitioning achieves 0.759×10⁻³ MSE on Navier-Stokes, nearly halving the error of the pixel-level partitioning variant (1.493) and all other baselines (≥1.436).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the traditional inpainting methods (Temporal Consistency, Fast Marching, Navier-Stokes inpainting), the gains are substantial: at 20% observation on Navier-Stokes, the method achieves 1.931×10⁻³ MSE versus 5.709, 3.737, and 2.989 respectively. Among diffusion-based baselines, the advantage over MissDiff is most pronounced at low observation rates (1.931 vs 3.077 at 20% Navier-Stokes; 1.229 vs 1.653 at 1% ERA5), while the margin over AmbientDiff is smaller but consistent (e.g., 0.223 vs 0.238 at 80% Navier-Stokes). The block-wise experiment (Table 2) provides the clearest validation of the theoretical contribution: using the correct block-wise context-query partitioning reduces Navier-Stokes MSE from 1.493 (pixel-level, incorrect) to 0.759, a 49% improvement that no other baseline achieves, confirming that matching the partitioning strategy to the observation structure is essential for structured missing patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based imputation line initiated by AmbientDiff (Daras et al., 2023) and MissDiff (Ouyang et al., 2023), which train generative models on partially observed data but rely on heuristic mask sampling without convergence guarantees. The key departure is a strategic context-query partitioning scheme that adapts to the observation pattern (e.g., block-structured vs. pixel-level), combined with a data-matching objective and ensemble sampling at inference. Theoretically, it builds on Tweedie's formula and the data-matching formulation of diffusion training (Zheng et al., 2023) to provide the first asymptotic convergence proof for diffusion models trained exclusively on incomplete samples, distinguishing itself from prior theoretical work (Givens et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025a) that requires prohibitively expensive multi-cycle retraining or importance weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, computationally efficient recipe for learning physical dynamics from inherently sparse observations—a setting common in weather, oceanography, and fluid mechanics where complete ground truth is unavailable during training. The finding that partitioning strategy must match observation structure (block-wise vs. pixel-level) yields a near-2× improvement over baselines in block-masked Navier-Stokes, validating the theoretical analysis. Open questions include whether the single-step sampling assumption (concentrated posterior) holds for highly underdetermined inverse problems, how the method scales to 3D or multi-physics systems, and whether combining the mask-based denoising with physics-informed guided diffusion (as in DiffusionPDE) can further tighten reconstruction accuracy.
