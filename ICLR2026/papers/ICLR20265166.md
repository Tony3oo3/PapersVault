## Problem

Existing camera-controlled video generation methods fail to simultaneously maintain view, appearance, and motion consistency because they operate in 2D pixel space without explicit 3D understanding. Directly injecting camera parameters into diffusion U-Nets produces artifacts, while explicit 3D supervision (point clouds, RGB-D) limits generalization. The paper identifies that the projection of smooth 3D camera motion inherently produces three types of 2D consistency—object view, appearance, and motion—and proposes to model these as an implicit bridge to 3D structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265166/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a per-frame camera trajectory (extrinsic matrix [R|t] and intrinsic matrix K) as input, and produces a 16-frame camera-controllable video in the latent space of a VAE. At inference, camera trajectories are sampled at 49 frames from 98-frame sequences to increase motion magnitude. The model is fine-tuned from CogVideoX on RealEstate10K (65K clips with SLAM-estimated per-frame camera parameters) and evaluated on both static (RealEstate10K) and dynamic (VidGen) scenes.

## Method

MoCa is a dual-branch framework (ReferenceNet + DenoisingNet, both initialized from CogVideoX's DiT) that models three consistency types. For view consistency, a Spatial-Temporal Camera Encoder converts Plücker embeddings of camera rays into pixel-aligned latent representations, fused via cross-attention into each DiT block. For appearance consistency, a semantic guidance strategy injects frozen vision-language features from the ReferenceNet into corresponding DenoisingNet blocks, stabilizing object texture and identity under camera motion. For motion consistency, a multi-level 2D Discrete Wavelet Transform extracts high-frequency components from vision-language features to form an object-aware mask, which is then fused with camera-conditioned features via a hybrid cross-attention (spatial) and temporal-attention (inter-frame) mechanism that disentangles local object dynamics from global camera movement. Only the dual-branch fusion module is trained; both backbone networks remain frozen.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (static scenes), MoCa achieves the best FID (207.4), FVD (667.9), CLIPSIM (0.312), and Background Consistency (96.4%), while tying AC3D on Motion Smoothness (98.5%). On VidGen (dynamic scenes), MoCa leads on FVD (1643.7), CLIPSIM (0.349), Rotation Error (0.081), Object Consistency (94.7%), Background Consistency (95.1%), and Motion Smoothness (98.3%), outperforming all baselines on 7 of 8 metrics. The method's advantage is most pronounced on consistency metrics (OC, BC, MS) in dynamic scenes, where baselines degrade significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MotionCtrl and CameraCtrl, MoCa dominates on virtually every metric across both datasets, with particularly large gaps in FVD (e.g., 667.9 vs. 870.8/931.5 on RealEstate10K) and consistency scores (OC, BC, MS). The closest competitor is AC3D, which uses a similar DiT-based architecture with Plücker embedding. On RealEstate10K, MoCa beats AC3D on FID (207.4 vs. 225.2), FVD (667.9 vs. 683.4), CLIPSIM (0.312 vs. 0.309), and BC (96.4% vs. 95.3%), but AC3D retains a slight edge on TransErr (0.695 vs. 0.703), RotErr (0.196 vs. 0.208), and OC (95.1% vs. 94.9%). On VidGen (dynamic scenes), MoCa wins 7 of 8 metrics over AC3D, losing only on FID (232.2 vs. 228.4), while gaining substantial advantages in OC (+1.2%), BC (+0.4%), and MS (+0.6%), confirming that the motion disentanglement mechanism is most impactful in dynamic settings where baselines struggle to separate object motion from camera movement.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoCa builds directly on the CogVideoX DiT backbone and adopts Plücker embedding and cross-attention camera fusion from CameraCtrl and AC3D, positioning itself within the established line of camera-conditioned video diffusion work. Its key conceptual departure is reframing the 2D-to-3D gap as a three-way object consistency problem (view, appearance, motion) rather than relying on explicit 3D supervision (point clouds, RGB-D) or simple parameter injection. The dual-branch ReferenceNet/DenoisingNet architecture draws from identity-preserving video generation (EMO, AnimateAnyone), while the 2D-DWT-based high-frequency object masking is a novel application of wavelet decomposition to disentangle object dynamics from camera motion in the latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-consistency decomposition provides a principled and interpretable framework for diagnosing failures in camera-controlled generation, and the motion disentanglement mechanism addresses a real gap where prior methods produce frozen objects under strong camera motion. However, the dual-branch design adds ~75s of inference overhead (291s total), and the method cannot precisely control where moving objects appear in the frame or extend to multimodal inputs. Open questions include whether the implicit consistency modeling scales to longer videos or more complex multi-object scenes, and whether the 2D-DWT masking generalizes beyond the specific wavelet filters used here.
