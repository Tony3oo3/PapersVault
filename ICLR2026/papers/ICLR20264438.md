## Problem

Existing unlearning methods for LLMs achieve only shallow alignment: rather than erasing target knowledge from model parameters, they generate spurious unlearning neurons that amplify negative influence to merely hide the knowledge. This makes unlearned models highly vulnerable to knowledge recovery through subsequent retraining, whether adversarial (fine-tuning on private data) or benign (instruction-following data), yet prior work identified this fragility without explaining the underlying neuronal mechanism or proposing a targeted fix.

## Input / Output

The method operates on instruction-tuned LLMs (Llama-3.2 3B, Qwen-2.5 3B) and consumes a forget set and retain set of QA pairs (e.g., 5% and 10% of FaithUn's 664 celebrity-knowledge instances). At each optimization step, it computes parameter-level attribution scores (φ·∂P(y|x)/∂φ) for every scalar parameter across the forget batch to identify the set of negatively-attributed indices I⁻, which drives the regularization. The output is an unlearned model whose internal attribution profile shows decreased positive influence (faithful erasure) rather than increased negative influence (suppression), verified via per-neuron influence variation analysis and logit-lens probing.

## Method

SSIUU augments a standard unlearning loss (GD by default) with an attribution-guided regularization term that penalizes the squared L2-norm change in attribution scores between consecutive optimization steps, but only for neurons whose current attribution is negative (set I⁻). This selectively constrains the growth of negative influence while allowing positive influence to decrease freely, thereby preventing the emergence of spurious unlearning neurons that would otherwise suppress rather than erase target knowledge. For computational efficiency, attribution is computed at the parameter level (φ·g) rather than per hidden-representation neuron, and the resulting gradient involves Hessian-vector products (HVP) computed via backpropagation without constructing the full Hessian, keeping overhead comparable to a standard gradient step. The previous-step attribution A_{θ^{t-1}} is treated as a constant (detached from the computation graph), and the regularization weight λ is set to 0.001 for FaithUn and 0.0001 for TOFU.

## Results

On the FaithUn dataset with Llama-3.2, SSIUU achieves the lowest harmful attack scores (14.81% at p=0.1, 14.29% at p=0.3) and benign attack score (13.33%), substantially outperforming all baselines whose attack scores range from 18.62% to 75.53%. On Qwen-2.5, SSIUU achieves an even stronger 4.76% at p=0.1 and 13.04% benign attack, compared to baselines ranging from 23.33% to 66.67%. Retention (RS) and utility (US) scores remain competitive: SSIUU achieves 84.70/56.28 on Llama-3.2 and 75.86/60.01 on Qwen-2.5, close to or exceeding most baselines. On the TOFU dataset, SSIUU similarly leads with 31.82% (p=0.1) and 37.53% (p=0.3) harmful attack scores versus 43.71–90.97% for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSIUU (built on GD as backbone) outperforms all six baselines on the primary robustness metrics. Against GA (Jang et al., 2022), SSIUU reduces the p=0.1 harmful attack score from 68.42% to 14.81% on Llama-3.2 and from 52.63% to 4.76% on Qwen-2.5, while also improving retention (RS 84.70 vs. 58.41 on Llama). Against GD (Maini et al., 2024), SSIUU cuts the p=0.1 score from 48.13% to 14.81% (Llama) and 27.78% to 4.76% (Qwen), with a 20-point gap on benign attack for Llama (13.33 vs. 33.33). Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), SSIUU improves p=0.1 from 31.58% to 14.81% (Llama) and 47.62% to 4.76% (Qwen). Against NPO (Zhang et al., 2024), the closest competitor, SSIUU still leads on p=0.1 (14.81 vs. 18.75 on Llama; 4.76 vs. 23.33 on Qwen) and benign attack (13.33 vs. 18.62; 13.04 vs. 18.18). Against RMU (Li et al., 2024), SSIUU dramatically reduces p=0.1 from 75.53% to 14.81% (Llama) and 42.11% to 4.76% (Qwen). Against KLUE (Yang et al., 2025), SSIUU improves p=0.1 from 57.14% to 14.81% (Llama) and 33.33% to 4.76% (Qwen), while also achieving the highest retention score among all methods on both models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Gradient Difference (GD) unlearning framework from Maini et al. (2024) as its backbone optimization and on the neuron-level attribution method from Yang et al. (2023) as its diagnostic tool. Prior unlearning robustness studies (Deeb & Roger, 2024; Hu et al., 2024) demonstrated that unlearned models are vulnerable to retraining but did not explain the underlying mechanism. This paper departs from that line by introducing the concept of 'spurious unlearning neurons'—showing via attribution analysis that six widely used methods (GA, GD, DPO, NPO, RMU, KLUE) all converge to the same shallow-alignment failure mode of suppressing rather than erasing knowledge—and then proposes SSIUU, an attribution-guided regularization that directly targets this mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that diverse unlearning objectives all produce the same shallow-alignment pathology reframes how the field should evaluate unlearning: standard forget/retain scores are insufficient, and robustness to even benign fine-tuning (e.g., Alpaca) becomes a necessary criterion. Open questions include whether the attribution-based diagnostic and the Hessian-vector-product regularization scale to 70B+ models or very long contexts, whether the single fixed λ generalizes across domains, and whether alternative knowledge-quantification methods (which currently lack negative-attribution support) could replace the specific attribution formulation used here. The practical implication is that platform operators offering fine-tuning APIs must treat unlearning as a pipeline-level concern, not just a one-shot model edit.
