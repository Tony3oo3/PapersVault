## Problem

Most modern time-series forecasters are purely observational, learning correlations from past behavior without the ability to answer causal 'what if' questions about interventions or counterfactuals. While interventional forecasting on causal DAGs has seen limited exploration, no general framework existed for counterfactual time-series forecasting—i.e., re-simulating how a specific observed trajectory would have evolved under an alternative intervention while preserving patient- or system-specific unobserved factors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a K-dimensional multivariate time series with a known causal DAG (topologically sorted), a context window of observed past values, and either an intervention schedule (specifying which nodes to set to fixed values at which future time steps) or, for counterfactuals, additionally a factual trajectory over the forecasting window. Outputs include autoregressive future forecasts generated in topological order at each step, interventional forecasts where intervened nodes are pinned to specified values while downstream nodes propagate causally, a single deterministic counterfactual trajectory (not a distribution), and explicit log-densities over the generated trajectory enabling anomaly detection. The DAG is assumed known and correctly specified, and all causal influences are assumed to have at least one time-step lag.

## Method

DoFlow assigns a separate Continuous Normalizing Flow (CNF) to each node in the causal DAG, trained via Conditional Flow Matching to regress a neural velocity field onto a linear-interpolation reference velocity. An RNN (LSTM or GRU) per node summarizes its own and its parents' past histories into a hidden state that conditions the CNF at each time step, and these hidden states are updated autoregressively from predicted or intervened values, thereby propagating intervention effects forward through the system. The key mechanism for counterfactuals exploits CNF invertibility: the forward (encoding) process maps a factual observation to a latent code conditioned on factual hidden states, and the reverse (decoding) process maps that same latent code back to data but under counterfactual hidden states, implementing the standard abduction–action–prediction procedure. Under assumptions of exogenous-noise independence, monotonicity of the structural equation in the noise, and exact flow matching, the paper proves that the encoded latent is a function of the exogenous noise alone (Proposition 4.3), which yields exact pointwise counterfactual recovery (Corollary 4.5) without requiring distribution-matching assumptions.

## Results

On the Tree causal structure (additive model), DoFlow achieves RMSE 0.57 for observational forecasting, 0.54 for interventional forecasting, and 0.11 for counterfactual forecasting. The interventional advantage is dramatic: DoFlow's 0.54 RMSE compares to 0.74 (DeepVAR, the best baseline) and 1.01–1.23 (GRU, TFT, TiDE, TSMixer, MQF2). Counterfactual forecasting is uniquely supported by DoFlow (all baselines report NA), with RMSE as low as 0.11–0.19 across structures. On the FC-Layer structure, DoFlow achieves 0.39 (Obs), 0.41 (Int), and 0.16 (CF) under the additive model, again outperforming all baselines on interventional tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoFlow outperforms all six baselines on interventional forecasting by a wide margin: on the Tree (additive) structure, DoFlow achieves RMSE 0.54 versus 0.74 for DeepVAR (the closest competitor) and 0.94–1.23 for GRU, TFT, TiDE, TSMixer, and MQF2. On observational forecasting, DoFlow is competitive, matching or slightly beating the best baselines (e.g., 0.57 vs. TFT's 0.58 and MQF2's 0.58 on Tree additive; 0.39 vs. GRU's 0.38 on FC-Layer additive). The most striking distinction is that DoFlow is the only method capable of counterfactual forecasting, achieving RMSE 0.11–0.19 across structures, while all baselines report NA. On the nonlinear non-additive (NLNA) variant, DoFlow maintains its interventional advantage (e.g., 0.62 vs. 0.86–1.30 for baselines on Tree NLNA), though its observational RMSE increases slightly (0.59 vs. 0.57 additive). The gap widens further on the FC-Layer structure, where DoFlow's interventional RMSE of 0.41 compares to 0.72–1.17 for baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DoFlow sits at the intersection of two research lines: (1) continuous normalizing flows trained via conditional flow matching (Chen et al., 2018; Lipman et al., 2023), which provide the invertible generative backbone, and (2) causal generative modeling for interventional and counterfactual queries, previously studied for static data via causal normalizing flows and diffusion models (Javaloy et al., 2023; Chao et al., 2024). The key departure is extending these ideas to time-indexed causal DAGs with autoregressive RNN conditioning, enabling the model to propagate interventions and recover counterfactuals across a temporal horizon. Unlike prior causal-effect-on-time-series work (Bica et al., 2020a; Brodersen et al., 2015) that handles discrete actions and short-term expected outcomes, DoFlow supports continuous interventions on individual variables at arbitrary time steps and yields full system-wide trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a gap the authors explicitly identify: no general framework existed for counterfactual time-series forecasting on a causal DAG. The theoretical counterfactual recovery guarantee (Corollary 4.5), while relying on monotonicity and exact CNF training, provides a clean link between the encode-decode mechanism and Pearl's abduction-action-prediction procedure. Practical limitations include the requirement of a known, correctly specified DAG and causal sufficiency (no latent confounders), which restricts applicability to domains where physical structure is well understood. A natural next step the authors highlight is coupling DoFlow with time-series causal discovery or deconfounding generative models to relax the known-DAG assumption.
