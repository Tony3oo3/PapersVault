## Problem

Enforcing complex, often nonconvex operational constraints in learning and control systems remains a critical challenge: penalty methods do not guarantee feasibility, differentiable projection methods are expensive or restricted to specific constraint classes, and post-hoc repair algorithms suffer from train-test mismatch. No existing approach provides a fast, general-purpose, data-driven correction that handles arbitrary topologically difficult constraint sets (e.g., disjoint or non-ball-homeomorphic) at sub-millisecond latency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair (y, x) — an infeasible neural network prediction y and its associated problem parameter x — and produces a corrected point ŷ that lies (approximately) within the constraint set C(x). In the RL setting, the input is a state-action pair (s_k, u_k) and the output is a safe action u_k ∈ C(s_k). The autoencoder operates on the joint space I = Y × X, so the encoder receives both the prediction and the parameter simultaneously, enabling input-dependent constraint handling. The latent projection step uses a closed-form mapping onto a 0.5-radius hyperball, making the entire correction pipeline a single forward pass through encoder → projection → decoder.

## Method

FAB trains an autoencoder (encoder E_γ and decoder R_ψ) in two phases to learn a structured latent representation where feasibility corresponds to membership in a simple convex set S (a closed hyperball). Phase 1 performs standard L2 reconstruction on feasible points to capture the feasible set geometry. Phase 2 introduces an adversarial discriminator D_ξ trained to classify feasible vs. infeasible points, while the autoencoder is trained with a composite loss combining reconstruction, a hinge loss that pushes feasible latent codes inside S and infeasible codes outside, a latent loss that uses the discriminator to supervise decoded points sampled from S, and a Jacobian regularization term (variance of log-det of the decoder's Gram matrix) to encourage uniform coverage of the feasible set. At inference, the trained autoencoder is frozen and appended as a plug-and-play attachment: the base network's output is encoded, the latent code is projected onto S via a cheap closed-form operation, and the result is decoded back into the original space — a one-shot, non-iterative correction that runs in under 1 ms.

## Results

On the Two Moons constrained optimization benchmark (the most challenging nonconvex set), FAB achieves 96.8% feasibility with 0.55 ms inference time for the quadratic objective, while FAB with 2 decoder networks reaches 100% feasibility at 0.59 ms. This is 1–2 orders of magnitude faster than FSNet (4.42 ms) and Homeomorphic Projection (247 ms), which achieve 98.7% and 73.9% feasibility respectively. In Safe RL (SafetyPointGoal2-v0), PPO-FAB achieves the lowest constraint violation cost (24.32) among all methods, outperforming PPO-LAG (54.10) and TRPO-LAG (89.04), though at the cost of lower episode reward (−1.12 vs. 13.26 for PPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAB substantially outperforms Homeomorphic Projection [Liang & Chen, 2025] on both feasibility and speed: on Two Moons (quadratic), FAB achieves 96.8% feasibility at 0.55 ms versus 73.9% at 247 ms for Homeomorphic Projection, a ~450× speedup. Against FSNet [Nguyen & Donti, 2025], FAB is roughly 8× faster (0.55 ms vs. 4.42 ms) with comparable feasibility (96.8% vs. 98.7%), and FAB with 2 decoders matches or exceeds FSNet's feasibility (100% vs. 98.7%) while remaining faster. Classical solvers (Projected Gradient, Penalty, Augmented Lagrangian, Interior Point) are 1–2 orders of magnitude slower (48–86 ms) and achieve lower feasibility (20–80%) on the nonconvex Two Moons set. In Safe RL, PPO-FAB achieves the lowest constraint violation cost in both SafetyPointGoal2-v0 (24.32 vs. 54.10 for PPO-LAG and 167.46 for PPO) and SafetyPointPush2-v0 (7.70 vs. 28.22 for TRPO-LAG and 59.86 for PPO), but this safety comes at a significant reward cost (−1.12 vs. 13.26 for PPO in Goal), indicating a conservative policy. Overall, FAB wins on speed across all comparisons and is competitive or superior on feasibility, while the main trade-off is slightly lower optimality gaps compared to FSNet and a more conservative reward profile in RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAB carves new ground at the intersection of amortized optimization and adversarial representation learning. The closest prior work is homeomorphic projection (Liang et al., 2023, 2024), which learns an invertible mapping from a hyperball to a topologically-equivalent constraint set and recovers feasibility via iterative bisection; FAB departs by using a non-invertible autoencoder with a one-shot latent projection, enabling it to handle non-ball-homeomorphic (e.g., disjoint) sets and eliminating the bisection loop. The adversarial training phase draws on GAN-style discriminator training but repurposes it not for generation but for structuring a latent space so that a simple convex subset (a hyperball) decodes to feasible points. The broader framing sits within amortized optimization (Amos, 2023), where the feasibility mapping is a plug-and-play attachment to any base network rather than a standalone solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-millisecond inference time (roughly 300× faster than homeomorphic projection) with near-100% feasibility on challenging nonconvex and disjoint sets makes FAB a practical option for latency-sensitive deployment where exact solvers are prohibitive. However, the method offers no hard feasibility guarantees, relies on a representative training dataset, and the adversarial training is noted to be unstable and hyperparameter-sensitive. The experiments, while diverse, are limited to 2D constraint geometries and small Safety Gymnasium environments, leaving open whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or highly structured constraint sets. Future directions include adaptive co-training of the feasibility mapping with the base network, specialized architectures (e.g., input-convex networks), and formal verification of the learned mapping.
