## Problem

LoRA adapter placement in pretrained models remains an open question with conflicting empirical guidance: the original LoRA paper recommends attention modules, while other work suggests MLP modules, and the optimal choice depends on both the model and the downstream task. No lightweight, principled method exists to automatically identify which module types should receive LoRA adapters for a given model-task pair without incurring the full cost of gradient computation or multiple forward passes.

## Input / Output

Given a pretrained transformer model and a finetuning dataset, PLoP performs a single forward pass over a small subset (batch size ~200, sequence length ~256) to compute per-module Normalized Feature Norm (NFN) scores, then aggregates them by module type (Query, Key, Value, OutProj, GateProj, UpProj, DownProj). The output is a ranked list of module types, with LoRA adapters inserted into those with the lowest NFN scores. The entire scoring step is gradient-free and adds negligible compute cost, as it can be merged with the first forward pass of LoRA finetuning itself.

## Method

PLoP is built on the insight that feature norms ‖Wz_in‖ capture module-data alignment: under μP scaling, feature norms grow quasi-quadratically during training as weights align with their inputs, while a random baseline ‖Wz̃_in‖ (same norm, random direction) remains flat. The NFN score is defined as the ratio ‖Wz_in‖/‖Wz̃_in‖, yielding values near 1 for unaligned modules and well above 1 for aligned ones. PLoP computes NFN for every module on a subset of the finetuning data, averages by module type, and inserts LoRA adapters into the types with the lowest scores—operating on the hypothesis that less-aligned modules have greater adaptation potential. A theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) proves quasi-quadratic feature norm growth in linear networks under SignSGD/Adam with μP learning-rate scaling, and the method is validated across supervised finetuning (classification, text generation) and GRPO-based reinforcement learning for mathematical reasoning on Llama, Qwen, and Gemma models.

## Results

On GRPO for mathematical reasoning (Qwen3-1.7B, MetaMathQA→GSM8K), PLoP(V-O-D, r=16) achieves 74.52% Pass@1, outperforming Attn(Q-K-V, r=16) at 71.49% and MLP(U-G-D, r=16) at 73.61%, while using fewer trainable parameters (6.88M vs 11.01M for MLP). On SFT for MetaMathQA (Qwen3-1.7B→GSM8K), PLoP(D-O-V, r=102) reaches 75.4% accuracy versus 69.5% for Attn and 75.0% for MLP. On CommonsenseQA (Qwen2.5-32B), PLoP achieves 92.6% test accuracy compared to 91.5% (Attn) and 91.9% (MLP). PLoP consistently outperforms the Attn baseline across all tasks and model sizes, and matches or slightly exceeds the MLP baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PLoP against two primary placement baselines: Attn (LoRA in Query/Key/Value modules, following Hu et al. 2022) and MLP (LoRA in Gate/Up/Down modules, following He et al. 2021). PLoP consistently outperforms the Attn baseline across all three post-training scenarios and all model sizes tested (Llama, Qwen, Gemma), with gaps of 3–6 percentage points on GSM8K accuracy (e.g., 74.52% vs 71.49% on GRPO Qwen3-1.7B; 75.4% vs 69.5% on SFT Qwen3-1.7B). Against the MLP baseline, PLoP achieves comparable or slightly better performance while often using fewer trainable parameters (e.g., 6.88M vs 11.01M on GRPO; 43.9M vs 44.0M on SFT Qwen3-1.7B). The inverse method PLoP⁻¹ (selecting highest-NFN modules) consistently underperforms both baselines, confirming that NFN scores capture a meaningful signal rather than noise. On the AYA multilingual SFT task, PLoP(V-O-D) achieves the lowest NLL across Portuguese, Japanese, and Arabic for both Llama-3.2-1B and 3B, with the MLP baseline ranking second and Attn/Random ranking lower.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLoP builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) as the underlying PEFT method and on μP (Yang and Hu, 2022) for the theoretical analysis of feature norm growth under Adam/SignSGD updates. It departs from prior placement heuristics—attention-only (Hu et al., 2022), MLP-only (He et al., 2021), or all-modules—by introducing the Normalized Feature Norm (NFN) as a gradient-free, single-forward-pass metric for module-data alignment. Unlike gradient-based selection methods (Zhang et al., 2024; He et al., 2023) that are incompatible with LoRA's memory constraints, PLoP operates within the same computational budget as standard LoRA finetuning, making it a practical drop-in replacement for ad-hoc module selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PLoP resolves a persistent practical confusion in the LoRA community about where to place adapters, providing a principled, near-zero-cost method that consistently outperforms or matches common heuristics across classification, text generation, and RLVR settings. The counterintuitive principle that modules with the *lowest* alignment (not highest) should be adapted is well-supported empirically but its deeper mechanism remains partially unexplained, particularly the 'negative alignment' phenomenon (NFN < 1) observed in Qwen3 Value modules. Open questions include whether NFN can enable finer-grained layer-level selection (the authors report inconsistent results), whether the method generalizes to non-Transformer architectures, and whether the alignment-based selection principle extends to other PEFT methods beyond LoRA.
