## Problem

Existing long-context training data construction methods—whether simple document concatenation or heuristic-driven synthesis (e.g., Quest's topical coherence, NExtLong's discrimination interleaving)—presuppose what constitutes a useful long-range dependency without verifying its actual utility to the model. This results in training sequences that may contain spurious correlations or redundant content rather than genuine information-gain dependencies, limiting the model's ability to effectively leverage extended context windows.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 100K source documents sampled from FineWeb-Edu and Cosmopedia, a retrieval corpus of over 1B documents indexed with jina-embeddings-v3 (768-dim, Faiss), and a base Llama-3-8B model for entropy computation. It produces 4B tokens of 128K-length training sequences, each structured as [shuffled verified contexts; root document], where every included context has been empirically confirmed to reduce prediction entropy at a specific high-entropy position by at least 40% (ΔI > 0.4), yielding an average information gain of 0.68 per verified dependency.

## Method

EntropyLong replaces heuristic data synthesis with a model-in-the-loop, four-stage verification pipeline. First, it identifies high-entropy positions in each document using an adaptive threshold (τ_H = μ_H + 2.0·σ_H) over per-document Shannon entropy distributions. Second, it retrieves top-32 semantically relevant candidate contexts via dense retrieval (jina-embeddings-v3 + Faiss) using a 16-word window around each uncertain position. Third—and critically—it empirically verifies each candidate by prepending it to the document and recomputing entropy at the target position, retaining only contexts that achieve a relative entropy reduction exceeding ε=0.4. Fourth, it concatenates the verified contexts (randomly shuffled to prevent positional bias) before the root document, filling a 128K token budget sorted by entropy-reduction magnitude. The key distinction from prior work is that dependency quality is measured by the target model's own predictive uncertainty reduction rather than by topical similarity or proxy-model perplexity, ensuring each constructed dependency represents measurable information gain.

## Results

On the RULER benchmark, EntropyLong achieves an average score of 87.37 across 8K–128K context lengths, outperforming Quest (80.53) and NExtLong (85.22), with the largest gap at 128K (81.26 vs. 60.11 and 77.99 respectively). After instruction fine-tuning on UltraChat, EntropyLong reaches 27.60 overall on LongBench-v2, surpassing Quest (22.30) and NExtLong (24.10), with a particularly strong 31.50 on Long tasks. Short-text performance is preserved, with an average of 61.83 across ARC-C, ARC-E, HellaSwag, PIQA, LogiQA, and WinoGrande, essentially matching the Llama-3-8B base (61.77).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EntropyLong consistently outperforms all three baselines on RULER. Against Quest (coherence-driven retrieval), EntropyLong gains +6.84 points on average, with the advantage widening dramatically at 128K (+21.15 points), indicating Quest's topically-related concatenation fails to create effective long-range dependencies at extreme lengths. Against NExtLong (discrimination-driven interleaving), EntropyLong leads by +2.15 points on average and +3.27 at 128K, showing that entropy-verified dependencies are more effective than heuristic distractor-based training. Against UTK (data augmentation via knot-untangling, reported in Appendix G), EntropyLong achieves +6.53 points on average, with especially large margins at 16K–128K. On LongBench-v2 after instruction tuning, EntropyLong leads NExtLong by +3.50 overall and +8.40 on Long tasks, confirming that the pretraining gains transfer to downstream long-context reasoning. All comparisons use identical Llama-3-8B training configurations and 4B tokens, ensuring a controlled comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of heuristic-based long-context data synthesis (Quest's topical coherence, NExtLong's discrimination-driven interleaving, task-driven generation) by introducing a model-in-the-loop verification step grounded in predictive entropy. Conceptually, it repurposes the active learning principle that models learn most from uncertain examples, but applies it constructively to pre-training data construction rather than annotation selection. The closest prior work, RE3SYN, also uses a verification signal but operates at the document level with a proxy model's perplexity; EntropyLong operates at the token level with the target model's own entropy, ensuring alignment with the model's actual information deficits. The retrieval mechanism (dense retrieval via sentence transformers) is standard, but the entropy-reduction verification gate is the key methodological departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, information-theoretic criterion for what constitutes a 'useful' long-range dependency in training data, moving the field beyond ad-hoc heuristics. The ablation showing that removing verification costs ~1.5 RULER points (with larger gaps at 32K–128K) validates that semantic similarity alone is insufficient. However, the approach is validated only on Llama-3-8B at 128K context, and the 26.45% negative-signal rate in the LLM-based qualitative analysis suggests the verification filter is imperfect. Open questions include whether the method scales to larger models (70B+), whether the 352 GPU-hour verification cost is prohibitive at scale, and whether the entropy-reduction signal remains discriminative as models improve and baseline entropy drops.
