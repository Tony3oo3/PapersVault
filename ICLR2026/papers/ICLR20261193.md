## Problem

Existing distillation methods for causal video generation (e.g., DMD) align a few-step student with a multi-step teacher, but when the student is restricted to 1–2 denoising steps, the distributional gap becomes so large that training is unstable and quality degrades severely. Moreover, each desired step count requires a separate distillation run, and causal frame-by-frame generation suffers from error accumulation that is especially acute in the first frame, which has no prior context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261193/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and produces a video clip at 832×480 resolution using a 1.3B-parameter Wan2.1-T2V backbone operating in VAE latent space. A single distilled model flexibly supports 1-, 2-, 3-, or 4-step inference without retraining; during inference, the First-Frame Enhancement strategy allocates a minimum of 4 denoising steps to the first frame while subsequent frames use only 1–2 steps, keeping overall computational cost low.

## Method

The core contribution is Adversarial Self-Distillation (ASD), which introduces a discriminator trained with a relativistic GAN objective (R3GAN with R1/R2 regularization) to make the n-step denoising output of the student indistinguishable from its own (n+1)-step output, rather than aligning directly with the multi-step teacher. This step-wise self-alignment bridges a much smaller distributional gap, yielding smoother supervision and more stable training under extreme few-step settings. The generator is optimized with a combined objective of standard DMD loss (score-based alignment with the teacher) plus the ASD loss, while the generator, fake-score estimator, and discriminator are trained alternately. At inference, a First-Frame Enhancement (FFE) strategy assigns more denoising steps to the initial frame (where inter-step similarity is empirically lowest) and fewer steps to later frames (which exhibit higher generative redundancy), mitigating error propagation without increasing overall cost.

## Results

On VBench, the method achieves a Total Score of 83.89 under 1-step inference (with First-Frame Enhancement), surpassing the retrained 1-step Self-Forcing baseline (80.62) by 3.27 points and the 2-step Self-Forcing (83.49) by 0.40 points. Under 2-step inference, it reaches 84.32 Total Score, matching the 4-step Self-Forcing (84.31) while using half the denoising steps. At 4 steps, the method scores 84.38 Total / 85.16 Quality / 81.25 Semantic, slightly edging out Self-Forcing (84.31 / 85.07 / 81.28) and substantially outperforming CausVid (81.20 / 84.05 / 69.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Self-Forcing (the closest baseline sharing the same 1.3B Wan2.1 backbone and DMD training paradigm), the method wins at every few-step setting: +0.07 Total at 4 steps, +0.83 at 2 steps (84.32 vs 83.49), and +3.27 at 1 step (83.89 vs 80.62). Against CausVid, the gap is much larger at 4 steps (+3.18 Total, +11.45 Semantic), reflecting CausVid's weaker few-step quality. Compared to many-step models like Wan2.1 (50 steps, 84.26) and SkyReels-V2 (30 steps, 82.67), the method matches or exceeds their Total Scores using only 1–2 denoising steps (~8–13% of the compute). In user preference studies, the method is preferred over Self-Forcing 96% of the time at 1 step and 62% at 2 steps, while being on par with Self-Forcing at 4 steps (both use identical DMD supervision). The single-model design also eliminates the need for separate distillation runs per step count, a practical advantage over Self-Forcing† which requires retraining for each target step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) framework for causal video generation by replacing the standard teacher-student adversarial alignment with a self-distillation objective: a discriminator aligns the student's n-step output distribution with its own (n+1)-step output distribution. It builds directly on the Self-Forcing causal distillation paradigm and CausVid's initialization protocol, while drawing on relativistic GAN objectives (R3GAN) for the adversarial term. The key conceptual departure from prior adversarial distillation work (ADD, LADD, SDXL-Lightning) is that supervision comes from the student's own adjacent-step behavior rather than solely from a multi-step teacher, and the First-Frame Enhancement inference strategy is a novel frame-wise scheduling idea not present in prior causal video generation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The step-unified property—a single distilled model supporting 1-, 2-, 3-, and 4-step inference without retraining—addresses a practical pain point in deployment where speed-quality trade-offs must be adjusted dynamically. The empirical finding that the first frame exhibits significantly lower inter-step similarity than subsequent frames provides a principled justification for non-uniform denoising budgets in causal generation. Limitations include evaluation only on a 1.3B-parameter model at 832×480 resolution, VBench as the sole quantitative benchmark, and 20-second maximum video length; whether the ASD objective and FFE strategy scale to larger architectures or longer sequences remains open. The modest +20% training overhead and +1.3% memory cost for the discriminator are encouraging but the practical speedup at inference (especially for interactive/real-time settings) is not explicitly quantified in wall-clock terms.
