## Problem

Existing decoding-based hallucination mitigation methods (CoT, Tree-of-Thought, Guided Decoding, Predictive Decoding) lack explicit token-level hallucination checking, do not quantify hallucination risk in the model's latent space, and offer limited dynamic correction during iterative generation. These gaps allow token-level errors to propagate and compound across reasoning steps, producing factually inconsistent outputs without the resource cost of RAG or large-scale fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

Token-Guard consumes a text query, an input context passage, and a domain-specific prompt (e.g., Yes/No/Maybe constraints for PubMedQA, exact-format financial figures for FinanceBench) that together define a unified generation template. It produces a hallucination-controlled answer in a standardized 'Answer:[...]' format, accompanied by a reasoning chain composed of verified segments. Internally, the method operates on penultimate-layer hidden states from the backbone LLM, maintaining only a running average of accepted-token hidden states and compact segment vectors to keep memory bounded independent of total generation length.

## Method

Token-Guard is a three-stage self-checking decoding pipeline that requires no fine-tuning or external retrieval. Stage 1 (token-level self-checking) scores each candidate token via a hybrid hallucination score combining cosine similarity between the token's penultimate-layer hidden state and the running mean of previously accepted tokens, weighted by the model's token probability (λ=0.6); tokens below a threshold (τ_token=0.4) are pruned before propagation. Stage 2 (segment-level scoring and local refinement) groups consecutive passing tokens into segments, evaluates each segment on weighted token reliability, local consistency (smoothness of adjacent hidden-state transitions), and global alignment with the input context, then applies targeted local-window regeneration to the lowest-scoring token in sub-threshold segments. Stage 3 (global iteration) assembles valid segments into candidate reasoning chains via TF-IDF/KMeans clustering, computes a global score combining factual consistency and logical coherence via a soft-minimum, and triggers dynamic threshold adjustment and re-refinement if the score falls below τ_global=0.7, outputting 'cannot answer' if both factual and logical scores remain below 0.5 after M_max iterations.

## Results

On Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Token-Guard achieves an average F1 of 51.03 and EM of 0.29 across seven HALU benchmarks, outperforming the strongest decoding baseline (Guided Decoding, F1 34.73) by 46.9% and the base model (F1 28.29) by 80.4%. On Qwen3-8B, Token-Guard reaches an average F1 of 53.98 (EM 0.35), surpassing the best baseline (Guided Decoding, F1 45.79) by 17.9%. The largest absolute gains appear on DROP_nfl (F1 58.10 vs. 49.21 for CoT) and HaluEval (F1 78.54 vs. 57.41 for Guided Decoding) with Llama, while improvements on knowledge-intensive datasets like PubMedQA and RAGTruth are more modest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Token-Guard consistently outperforms all four primary decoding baselines (Chain-of-Thought, Tree-of-Thought, Guided Decoding, and Predictive Decoding) on average F1 across both Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B backbones, with the largest margins over Predictive Decoding (51.03 vs. 24.43 on Llama; 53.98 vs. 23.67 on Qwen3). Against Self-RAG (Table 8), Token-Guard achieves a slightly lower average F1 (51.03 vs. 51.78) but dominates on multi-step reasoning datasets DROP_history (68.52 vs. 50.13) and DROP_nfl (58.10 vs. 45.29), while Self-RAG leads on HaluEval (86.70 vs. 78.54) and PubMedQA (43.21 vs. 29.67). Token-Guard substantially outperforms Self-Refine (51.03 vs. 37.97 avg F1) and Self-Reflection (51.03 vs. 30.93), confirming that explicit token-level scoring is more effective than iterative self-feedback loops. On the open-domain StrategyQA benchmark, Token-Guard achieves 69.4% accuracy versus DoLa's 64.1%, nearly doubling the improvement rate over the base model (11.40% vs. 6.66%). In segment-level F1 comparisons (Figure 5a), Token-Guard surpasses both SAS and LSTM-based decoders across all four evaluated datasets, and in BLEU it attains the highest scores across all six benchmarks (e.g., 63.19 on DROP_history, 75.13 on HaluEval).

## Relation to Prior Work

Token-Guard extends the decoding-based hallucination mitigation line that includes CoT, Tree-of-Thought, Guided Decoding, and Predictive Decoding, and builds specifically on token-level detection ideas from DoLa (layer-contrast scoring) and KCTS (knowledge-constrained tree search) as well as self-reflective decoding (Ji et al., 2023). Its key departure is a unified three-stage pipeline that explicitly couples token-level latent-space self-checking, segment-level hallucination risk scoring with local refinement, and global iterative chain re-evaluation—whereas prior methods typically operate at only one or two of these granularities. The paper positions itself as a lightweight, training-free alternative to RAG and RLHF, drawing on multi-level control frameworks (Hiriyanna & Zhao, 2025) and adaptive pruning ideas from Phi-Decoding (Xu et al., 2025) but integrating them into a single self-checking decoding loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Token-Guard offers a modular, training-free decoding strategy that can be plugged into existing LLMs without retrieval infrastructure or fine-tuning, making it attractive for resource-constrained or proprietary settings where RAG pipelines are impractical. However, its gains are most pronounced on reasoning-heavy benchmarks (DROP, HaluEval) and diminish on knowledge-intensive tasks (PubMedQA, RAGTruth) where the model simply lacks the required domain facts—highlighting that decoding-level control cannot substitute for missing knowledge. Open questions include scalability to very long contexts (the 3B model struggles on RAGTruth's long passages), the computational overhead on challenging datasets (301 s on CovidQA), and whether the latent-space cosine-similarity scoring generalizes to multimodal or non-English settings.
