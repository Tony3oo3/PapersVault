## Problem

Existing LLM evaluation metrics—accuracy, perplexity, and confidence/calibration scores—fail to capture the local robustness of a specific next-token prediction against small perturbations in the model's internal hidden state. Softmax normalization can produce high output probabilities from fragile internal states, meaning a well-calibrated, high-confidence prediction may still be internally unstable. The paper asks: how can we quantify the maximum internal state perturbation an LLM can withstand before its dominant next-token prediction significantly changes?

## Input / Output

The method operates at a single critical prediction point (e.g., immediately before the answer letter in MMLU or before the first token of the final numerical answer in GSM8K), consuming the final-layer hidden state h ∈ ℝ^d, the output weight matrix W ∈ ℝ^{V×d}, the resulting softmax distribution o, and a user-chosen tolerance ε (set to 1.0 in all experiments). It produces a single scalar δ_TCB representing the L2-norm radius of the largest perturbation sphere around h within which the output distribution changes by at most ε, along with the intermediate Jacobian Frobenius norm and the probability-weighted mean embedding μ_w(h) that underpin the geometric interpretation.

## Method

The Token Constraint Bound is defined as δ_TCB(h) = ε / ‖J_W(h)‖_F, where J_W(h) = (diag(o) − oo^T)W is the Jacobian of the softmax output with respect to the hidden state. The key theoretical contribution is an exact closed-form derivation showing ‖J_W(h)‖_F² = Σᵢ oᵢ²‖wᵢ − μ_w(h)‖², where μ_w(h) = W^T o is the probability-weighted mean embedding, revealing that stability is inversely proportional to the oᵢ²-weighted geometric dispersion of output token embeddings from their probability-weighted centroid. Crucially, the oᵢ² weighting (squared probabilities, not linear) distinguishes this from a standard probability-weighted covariance trace, giving high-probability tokens quadratically amplified geometric influence while de-weighting low-probability competitors. This yields regime-dependent behavior: in high-confidence settings (low V_eff), δ_TCB is governed by the logit margin z_k − z_{j*} between top-1 and top-2 tokens, while in uncertain settings (high V_eff), it scales approximately as √V_eff, reflecting overall distribution flatness. Empirically, the metric is validated by synthetically clustering/dispersing competitor embeddings (confirming geometric sensitivity at 90% of prompts) and by demonstrating that δ_TCB-guided prompt optimization outperforms perplexity-guided selection in worst-case accuracy under perturbation.

## Results

The paper validates δ_TCB's regime-dependent behavior through Pearson correlations: in diverse prompts (DPD, N=309), Corr(δ_TCB, V_eff) = 0.95, confirming stability is driven by distribution flatness; in high-confidence cases (LVD, N=360), Corr(δ_TCB, z_k − z_j*) = 0.62 while Corr(δ_TCB, V_eff) drops to 0.08, confirming stability relies on top-token logit separation. A geometry sensitivity simulation confirms δ_TCB(W_cluster) > δ_TCB(W_orig) > δ_TCB(W_disperse) for 90% of prompts. In prompt optimization, δ_TCB-guided strategies achieve 0.72 average accuracy and 0.65 worst-case accuracy under perturbation, versus 0.70/0.40 for PPL-guided and 0.55/0.25 for baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark δ_TCB against competing stability metrics head-to-head; instead, it positions δ_TCB as complementary to perplexity and standard confidence indicators (V_eff, logit margin). The most direct comparison is in prompt optimization: δ_TCB-guided selection achieves 0.72 average accuracy (vs. 0.70 for PPL-guided and 0.55 for baseline) while dramatically improving worst-case accuracy under perturbation (0.65 vs. 0.40 for PPL-guided and 0.25 for baseline). The PPL-guided strategy achieves lower average PPL (1.9 vs. 2.4), confirming it optimizes sequence likelihood, but fails to capture internal state stability. On the GSM8K deep-dive (Table 5), δ_TCB reveals 'confidently and stably wrong' predictions (e.g., 0% accuracy with δ_TCB ≈ 49,450) that perplexity and V_eff alone cannot flag, demonstrating δ_TCB's unique diagnostic value over conventional metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Jacobian-based sensitivity analysis—previously applied to adversarial robustness in classification networks (Novak et al., 2018; Hoffman et al., 2019)—for the specific problem of quantifying local next-token prediction stability in LLMs. It departs from perplexity and accuracy-based evaluation by focusing on the internal hidden state's resilience to perturbation rather than aggregate output quality, and differentiates from calibration/confidence metrics (Tian et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024) by measuring representational stability independent of correctness. The key analytical contribution is an exact closed-form expression linking the softmax-linear Jacobian's Frobenius norm to the o_i²-weighted geometric dispersion of output token embeddings, a derivation not previously established in the LLM evaluation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The δ_TCB metric offers a principled, geometrically grounded diagnostic that complements perplexity and accuracy, enabling practitioners to identify 'confidently unstable' or 'confidently and stably wrong' predictions that conventional metrics miss. Its regime-dependent behavior (correlating with distribution flatness in uncertain cases but logit margins in high-confidence cases) provides mechanistic insight into what drives prediction stability. However, the evaluation is limited to a single model (LLaMA-3.1-8B) and focused scenarios; open questions include whether the geometric interpretation holds across architectures and scales, whether δ_TCB can be extended to intermediate layers, and whether it can guide training-time robustness improvements beyond prompt engineering.
