## Problem

It remains unclear how long self-supervised learning (SSL) models should be pretrained to yield transferable representations. Contrary to the prevailing heuristic that longer pretraining is always better, the paper reveals a transferability trade-off: intermediate checkpoints achieve stronger out-of-domain (OOD) generalization, while extended pretraining primarily benefits in-domain (ID) performance. This raises the question of whether SSL pretraining progresses through structured learning phases analogous to critical periods (CP) observed in supervised learning, and whether such phases can be detected and exploited during pretraining.

## Input / Output

Pretraining consumes unlabeled RGB images (224×224) from fMoW-RGB or ImageNet over 1000 epochs with checkpoints saved every 50 epochs; downstream evaluation finetunes each checkpoint on labeled ID (fMoW-val, ImageNet-val) and OOD (fMoW-WILDS, EuroSAT, EuroSAT-Spatial, SUN397, Camelyon17-WILDS) datasets. The Fisher Information trace is computed at every epoch from gradients of the pretext loss, providing a label-free scalar signal that tracks plasticity without requiring any downstream data. The CP-SD method additionally takes the CP-closure checkpoint as a frozen teacher and the final checkpoint as a student, producing a single distilled model whose early layers are regularized toward the teacher's representations while later layers are trained solely on the task loss.

## Method

The paper reformulates critical period (CP) analysis for SSL through two probes: (1) deficit injection, where inputs are replaced with Gaussian noise for a fixed window (5–50 epochs) at varying onset times, and the lasting impact on downstream accuracy is measured as a sensitivity score; and (2) tracking the trace of the Fisher Information Matrix computed on pretext objectives (e.g., contrastive alignment or masked reconstruction), which quantifies parameter sensitivity to the self-supervisory signal. Across SimCLR, VICReg, DINO, and MAE, FI rises, peaks, and stabilizes, defining a CP closure point that coincides with peak OOD transferability; beyond this point, an overspecialization phase degrades OOD performance while ID continues to improve. Two CP-guided interventions follow: CP-guided checkpoint selection (CP-CS) picks the checkpoint nearest FI stabilization for OOD transfer, and CP-guided self-distillation (CP-SD) distills early-layer features (first ResNet stage or first three ViT blocks) from the CP checkpoint into the final checkpoint during downstream finetuning via an L2 distillation loss, preserving later layers' ID-specific refinements while recovering OOD generalization.

## Results

On VICReg-RN50 pretrained on fMoW, CP-guided self-distillation (CP-SD, early layers) achieves the best OOD performance across all three OOD benchmarks: 44.5% on fMoW-WILDS, 94.4% on EuroSAT, and 92.5% on EuroSAT-Spatial, while retaining competitive ID accuracy of 61.7% on fMoW-val. The standard final checkpoint achieves the highest ID accuracy (62.1%) but suffers the worst OOD performance (34.1% on fMoW-WILDS, 91.7% on EuroSAT, 89.4% on EuroSAT-Spatial). The CP checkpoint alone improves OOD substantially (e.g., 43.0% vs. 34.1% on fMoW-WILDS) at a small ID cost (61.0% vs. 62.1%). Across all four SSL methods (SimCLR, VICReg, DINO, MAE), OOD transferability peaks at intermediate checkpoints while ID performance continues to rise, confirming a consistent transferability trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external SSL methods but instead compares three checkpoint/distillation strategies: the standard final checkpoint, CP-guided checkpoint selection (CP-CS), and CP-guided self-distillation (CP-SD). The final checkpoint wins on ID accuracy (62.1% vs. 61.0–61.7%) but loses by a wide margin on all OOD tasks (e.g., 34.1% vs. 44.5% on fMoW-WILDS, a 10.4-point gap). CP-CS recovers most of the OOD gap (43.0% on fMoW-WILDS) at only a 1.1-point ID cost, confirming that the intermediate checkpoint is a strong OOD transfer point. CP-SD (early layers) further improves OOD to 44.5% on fMoW-WILDS and 94.4% on EuroSAT while recovering ID to 61.7%, effectively dominating both the final and CP checkpoints on OOD with minimal ID sacrifice. Distilling all layers (CP-SD all) underperforms early-layer distillation on both ID and OOD, suggesting that overwriting later layers erases the ID-specific refinements gained through extended pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the critical period (CP) analysis framework of Achille et al. (2018) from supervised learning to self-supervised learning, reformulating both perturbation experiments (deficit injection on unlabeled pretraining data) and Fisher Information tracking (now computed on pretext objectives rather than classification targets). It builds on the extensive SSL transferability literature (Ericsson et al., Newell & Deng, Marks et al.) that has characterized how well SSL representations transfer, but shifts the focus from post-hoc evaluation of a fixed checkpoint to the temporal dynamics of transferability across the pretraining trajectory. The practical interventions—CP-guided checkpoint selection and CP-guided self-distillation—draw on standard knowledge distillation and the well-known principle that early layers encode general features (Yosinski et al., 2014), but apply them in a CP-informed manner that is novel to the SSL setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that OOD transferability peaks at intermediate checkpoints while ID performance continues to improve directly challenges the widespread 'pretrain as long as compute allows' heuristic, offering a label-free, low-cost criterion (FI stabilization) for checkpoint selection. The CP-SD strategy provides a lightweight post-pretraining remedy that recovers OOD performance without sacrificing ID gains, which is practically relevant for teams that have already invested in long pretraining runs. Key limitations include the restriction to unimodal vision models and a fixed 1000-epoch budget (precluding observation of epoch-wise double descent), and open questions remain about whether similar phase dynamics hold in language, audio, multimodal, or JEPA-style SSL settings, and how augmentation/masking choices interact with CP timing.
