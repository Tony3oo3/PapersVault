## Problem

While diffusion model research has extensively improved architectures and sampling schedules, the role of initial Gaussian noise in shaping outputs has remained largely unexplored. There was no systematic understanding of how noise structure affects generated samples, and no principled way to reduce the variance of Monte Carlo estimates over the noise distribution for uncertainty quantification tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260659/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is training-free and model-agnostic: it takes any pretrained diffusion model (U-Net, DiT, consistency model, or even VAE/Glow), a text prompt or class label for conditional generation, and optionally a corrupted measurement for inverse problems. It produces generated images paired as antithetic (z, -z) samples, along with confidence intervals for pixel-wise statistics (brightness, contrast, centroid, pixel mean) and reconstruction quality metrics (PSNR, L1) for inverse solvers. For QMC extensions, it additionally requires specifying a Sobol' point set size and number of randomizations.

## Method

The paper discovers that pairing each Gaussian noise z with its negation -z (antithetic sampling) universally produces strongly negatively correlated outputs across all tested architectures, datasets, and conditioning schemes. To explain this, it proposes a symmetry conjecture that the learned score network is approximately affine antisymmetric (odd up to a constant shift), supported by both empirical validation (antisymmetry scores >0.99) and theoretical results (OU process convergence, Hermite polynomial expansion, FKG inequality). Leveraging this negative correlation, the Antithetic Monte Carlo (AMC) estimator averages paired outputs to cancel opposing errors, provably reducing variance by a factor of (1+ρ) where ρ<0 is the pair correlation, yielding up to 90% narrower confidence intervals at zero additional computational cost. The approach generalizes to K-antithetic noise (K variables with pairwise correlation -1/(K-1)) and to randomized quasi-Monte Carlo (RQMC) with Sobol' sequences, which further shrinks confidence intervals by exploiting the low effective dimension of the generator.

## Results

Antithetic noise pairs (z, -z) produce strongly negative pixel-wise correlations across all tested models: centralized Pearson correlations range from -0.45 (DiT/ImageNet-256) to -0.91 (Consistency Model/LSUN-Cat), versus near-zero for independent random pairs. For uncertainty quantification, the Antithetic Monte Carlo (AMC) estimator achieves relative efficiencies of 3.1× to 136× over standard MC (e.g., 134× for Brightness on LSUN-Church, 116× for Pixel Mean on CelebA-HQ), translating to up to 90% narrower 95% confidence intervals at identical computational cost. For diffusion inverse solvers (DPS), AMC reduces evaluation cost by 34%–84% across inpainting, super-resolution, and deblurring tasks. Antithetic noise also improves generation diversity, increasing LPIPS by 3.5%–15.1% and reducing SSIM by 24%–89% over random noise pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three estimators under identical sample budgets: standard Monte Carlo (MC), the proposed Antithetic Monte Carlo (AMC), and Quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC/RQMC). AMC consistently outperforms MC across all datasets and statistics, with the largest gains on unconditional models (up to 134× efficiency on LSUN-Church Brightness) and moderate but reliable gains on conditional models (3.3×–18.5× on Stable Diffusion and DiT). QMC and AMC(k=2) produce nearly identical CI widths on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 0.35 vs. 0.35 for Brightness), confirming that the antithetic pairing captures most of the variance reduction that QMC's space-filling design provides. For diffusion inverse solvers, AMC reduces CI width by 34%–84% over MC (e.g., 1.54× efficiency for inpainting L1, 1.84× for inpainting PSNR), while reconstruction metrics (L1, PSNR) differ by less than 0.2% between estimators, confirming unbiasedness. The K-antithetic generalization (k=8) provides additional gains over k=2 on CelebA-HQ (130.7× vs. 47.4× for Brightness) but shows diminishing returns on other datasets, suggesting the benefit depends on the strength of the underlying negative correlation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical antithetic sampling technique from Owen (2013) and extends it with quasi-Monte Carlo / randomized QMC methods (Owen 1997; L'Ecuyer 2023) to the setting of diffusion model sampling, which is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application of QMC to diffusion models. It departs from prior noise-optimization work (InitNo, FIND, Golden Noise, CCS) that is task-specific by providing a systematic, model-agnostic characterization of how initial noise shapes outputs. The theoretical analysis draws on Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process theory, Hermite polynomial expansions, and the FKG inequality, connecting the empirical observation to the recently studied local linearity of score networks (Chen et al. 2024b; Li et al. 2024b; Song et al. 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that antithetic noise universally produces strongly negatively correlated outputs across all tested architectures (U-Net, DiT, VAE, Normalizing Flows) and conditioning schemes provides a new structural insight into score networks via the affine antisymmetry conjecture, which remains open in full generality. Practically, the training-free, zero-overhead estimator delivers up to 90% narrower confidence intervals and 100× cost reduction for uncertainty quantification, making large-scale benchmarking of diffusion inverse solvers far more tractable. Limitations include modest LPIPS diversity gains and small improvements on perceptual metrics (MUSIQ, CLIP Score) due to nonlinearity in transformer decoders, and the symmetry conjecture lacks a full proof. Future directions include systematically proving the conjecture, integrating antithetic noise with existing noise-optimization methods, and exploiting low-dimensional structure for more effective QMC.
