## Problem

LoRA and its many variants parameterize fine-tuning updates as a product of two low-rank matrices (AB), which introduces non-convex optimization difficulties, sensitivity to initialization, and a need for customized training strategies to achieve stable convergence. This paper asks whether a simpler structured subset of the original weight matrix—specifically its diagonal blocks—can be updated directly to match or exceed full fine-tuning performance without any matrix-product parameterization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261757/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM's frozen weight matrices (partitioned into N×N blocks of size d₁×d₂) and standard training text sequences, and produces a block-diagonal adaptation matrix D whose N diagonal blocks are the only trainable parameters. In practice, D is stored as a 3D tensor of shape N×d₁×d₂ and applied via batched matrix multiplications (torch.einsum), so no full-size dense matrix is ever materialized. The number of blocks N (commonly 32, 64, or 128) controls the parameter budget, and N must be a common factor of the layer's input and output dimensions—a condition naturally satisfied by modern LLM architectures with highly composite hidden sizes.

## Method

DiaBlo partitions each linear layer's weight matrix W ∈ R^{m₁×m₂} into an N×N grid of blocks and introduces a block-diagonal adaptation matrix D with trainable blocks D₁,…,Dₙ on the diagonal, so the forward pass becomes Y = XW₀ + XD. Unlike LoRA, which computes XAB and requires careful initialization (e.g., PiSSA, MiLoRA, LoRA-GA) to avoid vanishing or unstable gradients, DiaBlo simply initializes D to zero and applies standard AdamW optimization directly to the full-rank diagonal blocks, eliminating the non-convexity of low-rank factorization. Theoretically, the paper proves that under mild low-rank conditions on activations and output gradients (empirically verified in LLaMA3-8B), any DiaBlo stationary point is also a stationary point of full fine-tuning, and in the linear least-squares setting DiaBlo is strictly more expressive than LoRA under the same parameter budget. Computationally, DiaBlo matches LoRA's forward/backward FLOPs when parameter counts are equal (Nd² = 2mr), and the block structure maps naturally to GPU batched GEMM operations, yielding identical training speed to LoRA in practice.

## Results

On commonsense reasoning (8 benchmarks), DiaBlo with N=64 blocks achieves 87.3% average accuracy on Llama3-8B using only 1.04% trainable parameters, outperforming DoRA (85.2%), LoRA (80.8%), and SMT (86.8%). On arithmetic reasoning (GSM8K+MATH), DiaBlo with N=32 reaches 43.4% average, slightly exceeding full fine-tuning (43.2%) while using only 2.09% of parameters. For code generation on Llama3-8B, DiaBlo achieves 43.2% Pass@1 and 63.5% Pass@10 on HumanEval, surpassing LoRA (34.7%/50.8%) and LoRI (43.2%/63.2%). On safety alignment (HEx-PHI), DiaBlo reaches 97.6% refusal rate on Llama3-8B and 98.8% on Mistral-7B, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiaBlo consistently outperforms LoRA across all evaluated tasks: on Llama3-8B commonsense reasoning, DiaBlo (87.3%) exceeds LoRA (80.8%) by 6.5 points; on arithmetic reasoning, DiaBlo (43.4%) beats LoRA (38.7%) by 4.7 points; on HumanEval Pass@1, DiaBlo (43.2%) surpasses LoRA (34.7%) by 8.5 points. Against DoRA, DiaBlo wins on commonsense (87.3% vs 85.2%), code generation Pass@10 (63.5% vs 48.6%), and safety alignment (97.6% vs 93.6%), while matching on Pass@1. PiSSA and MiLoRA are both clearly outperformed on commonsense (75.4%/81.9% vs 87.3%) and arithmetic (37.0%/40.7% vs 43.4%). SMT, the closest structured-sparsity competitor, achieves 86.8% on commonsense with 0.71% params but drops to 87.2% even at 3.01% params, while DiaBlo reaches 87.3% at just 1.04%. On quantized fine-tuning, MagR-DiaBlo outperforms QLoRA, GPTQ-LoRA, LoftQ, ApiQ, and MagR-LoRA in both 4-bit (54.8% vs 53.7% best) and 2-bit (48.7% vs 47.3% best) settings on Llama2-7B. On MT-Bench, DiaBlo (6.26) surpasses LoRA-GA (5.95) at the same 0.3% parameter budget and even beats LoRA-GA at 4.7% params (6.13). LoRI is the closest competitor on code generation, matching DiaBlo on Pass@1 (43.2%) but trailing on Pass@10 (63.2% vs 63.5%) and safety (92.8% vs 97.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiaBlo positions itself as a structured-sparsity alternative to the dominant low-rank adaptation paradigm (LoRA, DoRA, PiSSA, MiLoRA, LoRA-GA), which all rely on the product of two trainable matrices and consequently require careful initialization or customized optimization. It also departs from unstructured sparsity methods (SMT, S²FT, SparseLoRA) by imposing a fixed diagonal-block structure that is hardware-friendly and requires no importance-based selection. The theoretical analysis draws on low-rank matrix completion and the Kronecker-vec identity to prove expressiveness and convergence guarantees, connecting the practical PEFT setting to classical linear algebra results.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiaBlo offers a drop-in, initialization-free replacement for LoRA that is simpler to implement, equally fast, and consistently matches or exceeds LoRA-family methods across commonsense reasoning, arithmetic, code generation, and safety alignment on 7B–13B models. The most striking practical result is in ultra-low-precision (2-bit) quantized fine-tuning, where QLoRA collapses to near-zero accuracy while DiaBlo retains 32–55% average accuracy, suggesting diagonal-block updates are more robust to quantization noise. Open questions include whether the low-rank assumption on activations and gradients (central to Theorem 2) holds reliably in very large or multimodal models, whether the block-size N can be chosen adaptively per layer, and whether the approach extends to non-transformer architectures or continuous pre-training rather than downstream fine-tuning.
