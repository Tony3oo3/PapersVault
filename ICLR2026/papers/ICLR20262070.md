## Problem

Despite LLMs being trained exclusively on text, they develop rich latent visual capabilities that can be unlocked with minimal multimodal data. However, the internal structure of these visual priors, their specific origins in pre-training data, and how to deliberately cultivate them remain unexplored. This paper asks whether visual priors form a uniform block or decompose into separable components, and which data compositions drive each.

## Input / Output

The MLLM takes an RGB image and a text question; the image is encoded by a vision encoder (MetaCLIP-B/16, DINOv2-G, or MAE-H) and projected to a fixed 576-token sequence via an MLP before being concatenated with the text prompt. The output is a free-form text answer, extracted via a multi-tiered parsing strategy (explicit letter regex → exact option-text match → longest substring match) to handle conversational responses from smaller models. The paper also introduces MLE-Bench, a 1,861-image, 4-choice existence benchmark stratified by target object size (small 0–30%, medium 30–60%, large 60–100% of image pixels) to probe scale-dependent perception.

## Method

The paper conducts 100+ controlled experiments (500,000 GPU-hours) pre-training Llama-3-style LLMs (340M–13B) on 16 distinct data sources and systematic mixtures of reasoning-centric (code, math, science) and visual-world (concept, attribute, relationship) data, then adapts each to an MLLM via a two-stage pipeline: MLP projector alignment on 1M image-caption pairs followed by supervised fine-tuning on 3.5M curated instructions. The key structural finding is that visual priors decompose into a reasoning prior—driven predominantly by reasoning-centric data and scaling progressively up to a 75% proportion—and a perception prior—emerging diffusely from broad, diverse corpora and saturating quickly. They derive a balanced data mixture (mix6: ~52% reasoning, ~15% visual, ~40% web-crawl) that maximizes VQA accuracy without sacrificing language perplexity, validated at 1T tokens with 7B models. Universality of the reasoning prior is confirmed across three different vision encoders, and MLE-Bench reveals that the perception prior is scale-dependent, being most pronounced for small-to-medium objects.

## Results

At the 1T-token, 7B-parameter scale, the Balanced model (trained with the proposed mix6 recipe) achieves 38.64% overall VQA accuracy versus 37.32% for the Language-Favorable model, while simultaneously attaining a lower perplexity (7.49 vs. 8.72) and higher language accuracy (0.655 vs. 0.647). At the 3B/50B-token scale, the optimal balanced mixture (mix6: ~52% reasoning, ~14.8% visual) achieves the top overall rank, with 33.3% VQA accuracy and 52.7% text accuracy. The perception prior is scale-dependent: web-crawl pre-training is most effective for perceiving small objects (0–30% of image pixels), while its advantage diminishes for large, dominant objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical study without external method baselines; comparisons are internal ablations across data mixtures, model scales, and vision encoders. The key comparison is between the Language-Favorable (mix0) and Balanced (mix6) 7B/1T models: the Balanced model wins on every VQA category (e.g., General 49.59 vs. 46.92, OCR & Chart 23.63 vs. 21.49) and on language perplexity, confirming that the proposed data recipe improves both modalities simultaneously. Across the 11-mixture interpolation (mix0→mix10), vision accuracy rises from 32.4% to 33.4% while text accuracy drops only from 53.0% to 52.1%, with mix6 achieving the best overall rank. On the universality test, the reasoning prior transfers consistently across three vision encoders (MetaCLIP-B/16, DINOv2-G, MAE-H), whereas the perception prior shows encoder-dependent variability. On MLE-Bench, GPT-5 leads with 82.97% overall, but all evaluated MLLMs show a pronounced drop on small objects (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Flash: 58.88% on 0–30% vs. 80.66% on 30–60%), highlighting that fine-grained perception remains a bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-mixing literature for LLM pre-training (Shukor et al., 2025a; Ge et al., 2024; Ye et al., 2024), which has focused on optimizing language performance, to the visual domain by asking how text-only pre-training choices shape emergent visual priors. It builds on the Cambrian-1 and Web-SSL adaptation pipelines for converting LLMs into MLLMs, and is motivated by the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al., 2024b) and prior observations that LLMs exhibit latent visual capabilities (Sharma et al., 2024; Pang et al., 2024). The decomposition of visual priors into separable perception and reasoning components aligns with and extends Chen et al. (2025), who identified a similar dissociation through parameter merging, but this paper traces each component back to specific pre-training data sources rather than post-hoc parameter analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a directly actionable data-mixing recipe (≈60% reasoning + 15% visual-world text) for pre-training vision-aware LLMs, validated at 1T tokens, which could reduce the multimodal data and compute required for competitive MLLMs. The MLE-Bench offers a new diagnostic tool for fine-grained perception evaluation across object scales. Key limitations include restriction to adapter-style architectures and static images, leaving open whether findings transfer to end-to-end or video settings, and the safety implications of text-derived visual biases remain unaddressed. A broader open question is whether the perception prior's diffuse, scale-dependent nature can be further decomposed or deliberately cultivated beyond what diverse web text provides.
