## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting relies on spherical harmonics for view-dependent color encoding, which inherently cannot decouple diffuse and specular components and fails to reproduce sharp specular highlights on glossy surfaces. Low-order SH captures only smooth color transitions, while higher-order SH incurs exponential memory growth and training instability. This limits 3DGS's rendering quality relative to leading NeRF variants, particularly in scenes with complex reflections.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a post-enhancement on an already-optimized 3DGS or 3DGS-MCMC scene, consuming the pre-rendered training images, their corresponding depth maps (rendered via ray-tracing at the 0.5 transmittance threshold), and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It outputs an augmented radiance field where approximately 10% of primitives are replaced by enhanced Gaussians carrying 5 additional learnable parameters (opacity lobe orientation, angular span T, sharpness β, plus base opacity and color). As a secondary output, the method supports explicit diffuse-specular decomposition by rendering the original SH₀ diffuse pass and subtracting it from the full augmented render in linear radiance space.

## Method

The core contribution is a view-dependent opacity kernel adapted from Phong shading, where each Gaussian's transparency is modulated by a half-period cosine lobe whose angular extent (T) and sharpness (β) are learnable, enabling superposition of multiple kernels to reconstruct complex specular distributions. Initialization proceeds in two stages: first, 2D Gaussians are sampled in screen space at high-error locations (using a composite L1+SSIM loss as the sampling probability) and optimized against the pre-rendered image as a fixed background; second, these 2D primitives are back-projected into world space via an inverse splatting procedure that performs foreground-background separation through single-linkage clustering, derives rotation and scale via Weighted PCA, and calibrates the uniform scaling factor by minimizing the Frobenius norm between projected and target 2D covariances. During final joint optimization, the original Gaussians' opacity is unfrozen (via SparseAdam) to resolve depth-ordering disruptions caused by the newly inserted primitives, while the enhanced Gaussians undergo full parameter optimization under multi-view photometric constraints.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, the best configuration (MCMC, sh=3) achieves 28.96 dB PSNR, 0.849 SSIM, and 0.170 LPIPS, outperforming Zip-NeRF (28.54 dB) and DBS (28.60 dB). On Tanks & Temples, it reaches 25.06 dB PSNR and 0.144 LPIPS, surpassing all baselines including DBS (24.79 dB). On Deep Blending, the method attains 30.22 dB PSNR and 0.236 LPIPS. Notably, even with second-order spherical harmonics (sh=2), the method achieves 28.89 dB on Mip-NeRF 360, already exceeding Zip-NeRF, while reducing per-primitive parameters by 21 compared to sh=3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the state-of-the-art implicit method Zip-NeRF, the proposed method (MCMC, sh=3) achieves +0.42 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF 360 (28.96 vs. 28.54) and +0.25 dB on NeRF Synthetic (34.35 vs. 33.10), while also improving LPIPS from 0.189 to 0.170. Compared to the strongest explicit baseline DBS (30k), the method wins on all three real-world datasets—Mip-NeRF 360 (+0.36 dB), Tanks & Temples (+0.27 dB), and Deep Blending (+0.12 dB PSNR)—but trails by 0.29 dB on NeRF Synthetic, which the authors attribute to the simplicity of synthetic scenes with limited material variation. Against specular-aware 3DGS variants, the method outperforms Spec-Gaussian by +0.78 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 and +1.29 dB on Tanks & Temples, and surpasses VoD-3DGS[L] by +1.17 dB and +1.15 dB respectively. In terms of efficiency, the method uses 467 MB memory (MCMC, sh=2) versus 882 MB for Spec-Gaussian on Mip-NeRF 360, while achieving 96 FPS versus 43 FPS, and the post-enhancement stage adds only ~15 minutes of training time per scene.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a post-enhancement layer on top of existing 3DGS or 3DGS-MCMC scenes, departing from the standard paradigm of jointly optimizing all primitives from scratch. Conceptually, it draws on the Phong shading model to introduce view-dependent opacity as a specular mechanism, which contrasts with Spec-Gaussian's anisotropic spherical Gaussians and VoD-3DGS's symmetric-matrix opacity, and with DBS's replacement of spherical harmonics by Spherical Beta functions for radiance modeling. The error-driven 2D-to-3D inverse splatting initialization is a novel procedural contribution not present in prior densification strategies (Scaffold-GS, Pixel-GS, 3DGS-MCMC), which all operate directly in 3D space. The plug-and-play framing distinguishes it from methods that require retraining the entire scene.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a ~10% subset of augmented Gaussians with 5 extra parameters each closes the quality gap to Zip-NeRF while preserving real-time FPS, and the result holds with only second-order SH (saving 21 parameters per primitive). This suggests that the field's push toward higher-order SH or more complex radiance models may be less necessary than assumed, and that targeted opacity modulation is a more parameter-efficient lever for specular effects. Open questions include whether the two-stage optimization can be unified with original training, whether the fixed 10% ratio can be made adaptive per scene, and whether the depth-map-dependent back-projection can be replaced by learned geometric priors to improve surface conformity.
