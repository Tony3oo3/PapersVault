## Problem

Current RLVR methods for interactive coding agents (e.g., GRPO) rely exclusively on sparse, delayed outcome-verifiable rewards, providing no guidance at intermediate reasoning steps. This causes inaccurate credit assignment: early errors may be reinforced if the final outcome succeeds, while partially correct reasoning is discarded if the trajectory ultimately fails, leading to unstable optimization and underutilization of rich environment feedback such as syntax errors and runtime exceptions.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language task instruction and, at each of up to 40 (training) or 50 (evaluation) interaction turns, generates up to 512 tokens of reasoning text followed by executable Python code. The code is executed by a code interpreter against a stateful AppWorld environment (9 simulated consumer apps, 457 APIs), and the resulting observation (API response or exception trace, truncated at 3K tokens) is appended to the trajectory. The final output is a complete multi-turn trajectory of reasoning–code–observation triples that must satisfy all associated unit tests.

## Method

GVPO extends GRPO's group-relative advantage with a shaping function f(Â, B) that fuses outcome-verifiable rewards (fraction of passed unit tests, R_i ∈ [0,1]) with process-verifiable step-level signals (execution success/failure per token). For successful tokens the advantage is unchanged; for failed tokens it is adjusted conditionally: a fixed penalty −b when the outcome advantage is zero, multiplicative amplification (1+b)·Â when Â < 0, and zeroing when Â > 0, with b = 0.2. The method also employs asymmetric clipping (ε_low = 0.2, ε_high = 0.28), sequence-mean-token-mean loss aggregation, and omits standard-deviation normalization in advantage computation. Trained on Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct via veRL with 8 rollouts per sample over 144 steps, GVPO achieves 72.6 TGC on AppWorld Test-N and 49.4 TGC on Test-C, outperforming the strongest 32B RL baseline (LOOP) by 3.7 points on Test-C.

## Results

On the AppWorld benchmark, GVPO (32B) achieves 72.6 TGC / 55.4 SGC on Test-N and 49.4 TGC / 28.8 SGC on Test-C, setting a new state-of-the-art across both splits. On the more challenging Test-C split (unseen apps, longer multi-step planning), GVPO surpasses OpenAI's o1 agent by 12.7 points in TGC (49.4 vs. 36.7) and the strongest 32B RL baseline LOOP by 3.7 TGC and 2.2 SGC. All RL fine-tuned methods consistently outperform zero-shot prompting, with GVPO showing the largest margin over prompting baselines (e.g., +38.1 TGC over GPT-4o on Test-N).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GVPO outperforms all five RL baselines on both Test-N and Test-C splits. Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), GVPO gains +11.3 TGC on Test-N (72.6 vs. 61.3) and +10.9 TGC on Test-C (49.4 vs. 38.5), demonstrating that process-verifiable shaping substantially improves credit assignment over trajectory-level group-relative advantages alone. Against DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), GVPO leads by +15.5 TGC on Test-N and +10.6 TGC on Test-C, indicating that token-mean aggregation without process signals is insufficient for long-horizon interactive tasks. Against Dr.GRPO (Liu et al., 2025), GVPO achieves +8.9 TGC on Test-N and +8.9 TGC on Test-C, showing that removing std normalization and switching to smts aggregation helps but does not match the benefit of step-level process feedback. Against RLOO (Ahmadian et al., 2024), GVPO leads by +11.9 TGC on Test-N and +9.3 TGC on Test-C. Most notably, against LOOP (Chen et al., 2025b), the strongest prior 32B RL baseline, GVPO gains +1.3 TGC on Test-N (72.6 vs. 71.3) and +3.7 TGC / +2.2 SGC on Test-C (49.4 vs. 45.7), confirming that the advantage of process-verifiable signals grows with task difficulty and generalization demands.

## Relation to Prior Work

GVPO extends the GRPO family of group-relative policy optimization (Shao et al., 2024) by introducing an advantage shaping function that integrates process-verifiable execution feedback alongside the standard outcome-verifiable reward. It inherits design choices from Dr.GRPO (removing std normalization) and DAPO (asymmetric clipping), but departs from all prior RLVR methods—including LOOP and GiGPO—by explicitly incorporating step-level execution signals (syntax errors, runtime exceptions) as corrective shaping terms rather than relying solely on trajectory-level outcome rewards. The key conceptual departure is treating intermediate environment feedback as a first-class signal in credit assignment, rather than discarding it after trajectory completion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in training interactive coding agents: sparse outcome rewards provide almost no gradient signal for the many intermediate steps in long-horizon multi-turn tasks, leading to poor credit assignment and unstable optimization. The process-verifiable shaping is rule-based and requires no additional reward model, making it directly applicable to any environment that emits deterministic execution feedback. However, the method's reliance on well-defined, deterministic process signals limits its applicability to domains with noisy or ambiguous intermediate feedback, and the generalization experiments (ALFWorld, WebShop) use a 7B model rather than the 32B setting, leaving open whether the gains scale consistently. A deeper theoretical analysis of when and why the multiplicative vs. additive shaping choice matters would strengthen the contribution.
