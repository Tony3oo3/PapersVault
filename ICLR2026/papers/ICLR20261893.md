## Problem

Diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) introduce novel jailbreak vulnerabilities fundamentally distinct from Autoregressive LLMs, stemming from their iterative parallel generation and remasking mechanisms. Prior safety analyses designed for AR LLMs do not apply, and the specific intra-step (greedy remasking bias) and inter-step (Denoising-path Dependence) dynamics that make dLLMs vulnerable had not been systematically identified or addressed.

## Input / Output

DIFFUGUARD operates as a plug-and-play inference-time module that intercepts the dLLM's iterative denoising loop. It consumes the user query (potentially a jailbreak prompt), the model's per-position logits at each generation step, and output-layer hidden representations (for computing Safety Divergence between the original malicious core and the full adversarial prompt). It produces the final generated text sequence, with the constraint that it must preserve model utility on benign queries (low false-positive rate) and introduce negligible latency overhead.

## Method

DIFFUGUARD is a training-free, dual-stage defense that activates dLLMs' inherent safety potential. Stage 1 (Stochastic Annealing Remasking) replaces the standard greedy confidence-based remasking with a noisy selection: each token's retention score becomes (1−α)·Prob + α·R where R~U(0,1), and α decays linearly from α₀ to 0 across N steps, injecting maximum randomness in critical early steps while restoring confidence-based selection later to preserve generation quality. Stage 2 (Block-level Audit and Repair) computes a Safety Divergence score (1 − cosine similarity) between the mean hidden state of the original malicious core p_origin and the full jailbreak prompt p₀ at step 1; if SD exceeds threshold λ on the first generated block, it randomly remasks a proportion γ of non-prompt tokens and regenerates them over M extra steps with the original harmful tokens' logits set to −∞, forcing exploration of alternative safe paths.

## Results

DIFFUGUARD reduces the average Attack Success Rate (ASR) across four dLLMs from 47.9% to 27.8% when used alone, and further to 14.7% when combined with the Self-reminder baseline. Against dLLM-specific jailbreak attacks (PAD and DIJA on AdvBench), the combined defense drops ASR from 96.8% to 27.9% (≈68.9% reduction). The framework preserves general capabilities (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval) and introduces negligible inference latency, confirming its plug-and-play nature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFUGUARD consistently and substantially outperforms both baselines across all four dLLMs. Against PPL-Filter, which only reduces average ASR by 4–12 percentage points (e.g., 47.14→43.13 on LLaDA-8B), DIFFUGUARD alone achieves 15–44 point reductions (47.14→31.13 on LLaDA-8B; 62.73→40.40 on MMaDA). Against Self-reminder, which is nearly ineffective on dLLM-specific attacks (DIJA ASR remains at 97.50% on LLaDA-8B), DIFFUGUARD drops DIJA ASR to 51.92% alone and 39.04% in combination. The most dramatic gap appears on PAD and DIJA (attacks exploiting dLLM's parallel/in-place generation): Self-reminder barely helps (ASR 97.50% on DIJA/LLaDA) while DIFFUGUARD cuts it to 51.92%, and the combined defense reaches 39.04%. On pre-optimized prompt attacks (WildJailbreak, JBB-Behaviors), Self-reminder is competitive or slightly better than DIFFUGUARD alone, but the combination still yields the lowest ASR in every cell of the table.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaDA diffusion LLM framework (Nie et al., 2025), specifically modifying its Low Confidence Remask decoding strategy, and extends the jailbreak defense literature from autoregressive LLMs to the dLLM paradigm. It departs from prior dLLM safety work (DIJA by Wen et al., PAD by Zhang et al.) by shifting focus from attack characterization to a structured intra-step/inter-step decomposition of the iterative denoising process, identifying the greedy remasking bias and Denoising-path Dependence as root causes. The Block-level Audit module repurposes the safety-direction representation analysis from Zhou et al. (2024b) and Arditi et al. (2024), adapting the cosine-distance-based divergence metric to the semi-autoregressive block structure unique to dLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As dLLMs approach AR LLM performance on diverse tasks, their safety is an urgent and underexplored concern; this paper provides the first systematic mechanistic analysis of why dLLMs are vulnerable and a practical training-free defense that reduces ASR by ~33% across four models and six attack types. Key open questions include whether the Denoising-path Dependence finding generalizes to continuous-diffusion or non-block dLLM architectures, how to combine inference-time defenses like DIFFUGUARD with safety alignment training for deeper robustness, and how the framework holds up against future dLLM-specific adaptive attacks that directly optimize against the Safety Divergence signal. The paper acknowledges that dLLM-specific attack methods are still nascent, limiting the breadth of threat coverage.
