## Problem

Existing open-set text classification methods train under a closed-world assumption and apply post-hoc OOD detection, producing overconfident and biased predictions on out-of-distribution inputs. The paper asks whether genuine open-set training—where OOD detection is integrated with guaranteed-correct supervision during fine-tuning—is fundamentally unattainable, and proposes that it can be achieved by explicitly modeling the unknown class within the LLM's training objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261973/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text to classify together with a sampled subset of known labels (formatted as zero-padded numbered categories in a structured prompt) and produces a single label: either one of the candidate ID labels or an explicit 'unknown' (K+1) class. At inference, the model sequentially evaluates the text against multiple label partitions, stopping at the first ID match; if all partitions return OOD, an analogy-augmented self-reflection step retrieves similar training examples and asks the LLM to confirm or reject the prediction before finalizing the OOD decision.

## Method

UnLLM reformulates text classification as subset-conditioned generation: the LLM is prompted with sampled label subsets, and any instance whose true label is excluded from the candidate set is labeled as an explicit 'unknown' class, turning OOD detection into an intrinsic K+1-class modeling task. Training jointly optimizes a generative loss over the partition-conditional label, a contrastive loss that tightens intra-class embeddings, and an orthogonality constraint that projects virtual OOD features (sampled from low-likelihood regions of class-conditional Gaussians) onto a PCA subspace and penalizes alignment with ID representations. A post-training OOD parameter calibration step shifts the K+1 token weight in the LLM's output head along a direction derived from validation-set false positives/negatives, projected orthogonally to correct predictions, aligning internal representations with output probabilities without additional training. At inference, analogy-augmented self-reflection retrieves semantically similar training examples via a PLM embedding and prompts the LLM to verify whether the text strictly aligns with the predicted label's scope, mitigating overconfidence on label-similar OOD cases.

## Results

UnLLM (LLaMA3.1-8B) achieves the best K-F1 and N-F1 across all six OSTC benchmarks at every known-class ratio (25%, 50%, 75%). On average, it improves K-F1 by 4.40%, 2.80%, and 2.55% and N-F1 by 1.63%, 1.53%, and 5.09% over the strongest SOTA baseline at the 25%, 50%, and 75% settings, respectively. For example, on CLINC at 50% known classes, UnLLM reaches 93.42 K-F1 and 93.00 N-F1, surpassing the next-best NPO (91.31 / 91.53). On THUCNews at 75%, it attains 93.55 K-F1 and 78.11 N-F1, outperforming KNNCon (90.49 / 63.10) by a wide margin on OOD detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnLLM outperforms all 13 baselines on both K-F1 and N-F1 across every dataset and ratio. Among the strongest LLM-based competitors, it beats NPO (the best LLM baseline on most datasets) by 2.64 K-F1 and 0.40 N-F1 on StackOverflow at 25%, and by 2.11 K-F1 and 1.49 N-F1 on CLINC at 50%. EnergyBased, the second-strongest LLM baseline, is surpassed by UnLLM by 3.97 K-F1 on BANKING (75.04 vs 71.07) and 1.56 N-F1 on CLINC (93.58 vs 91.46) at 25%. BERT-based methods (ADB, CLAP, KNNCon, DyEn) are consistently weaker, with KNNCon being the closest BERT competitor (e.g., 93.91 K-F1 on CLINC at 75%) yet still trailing UnLLM by 3.03 K-F1 and 9.06 N-F1. CNN/LSTM baselines (DOC, DeepUnk) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 10–30 points on K-F1. Hallucination-detection baselines (LLM-Check, CED, HaluEval-CoT) fail dramatically in the OSTC setting, achieving K-F1 scores below 20% in most configurations, confirming that generation-oriented uncertainty signals are inadequate for categorical OOD detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative fine-tuning paradigm for LLM-based OOD detection introduced by LLM-OOD (Liu et al., 2024) by reformulating closed-set classification into a subset-conditioned task where the model is explicitly trained to output an 'unknown' class when the true label is absent from the candidate set. It also adapts virtual outlier synthesis from VOS (Du et al., 2022) but departs from direct regularization on synthetic samples by instead projecting them onto a PCA subspace and enforcing orthogonality with ID representations. The key conceptual departure from all prior OSTC work (DOC, ADB, CLAP, KNNCon, DyEn, LLM-OOD) is shifting OOD detection from a post-hoc scoring procedure to an intrinsic training objective, so the model learns a K+1 classifier rather than a K-class classifier with a separate rejection mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the training-time reformulation (open-set SFT) is the dominant source of performance gain, with auxiliary losses and inference-time reflection providing incremental but meaningful improvements. This reframing has practical implications: it suggests that simply adding OOD scoring functions on top of closed-set-trained LLMs is fundamentally limited, and that explicit unknown-class supervision during fine-tuning is necessary for robust open-set behavior. Limitations include reliance on QLoRA (no full-parameter fine-tuning results), evaluation only on 8B-scale models for the main pipeline, and the label-partitioning strategy requiring multiple forward passes per sample at inference. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models or more extreme open-set settings (e.g., very few known classes), and whether the calibration step generalizes beyond the specific LLM head architecture used.
