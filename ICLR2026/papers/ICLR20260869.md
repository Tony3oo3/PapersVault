## Problem

Backpropagation's strictly sequential backward pass hinders parallelism and scalability in deep networks, while existing parallel alternatives like Direct Feedback Alignment (DFA) fail on deep convolutional networks and perform poorly on modern transformer architectures. The core gap is that fixed random feedback matrices lose positive cosine alignment with true gradients in deep and structured layers, causing learning to stall, and no prior method combines forward-gradient alignment guarantees with layer-parallel DFA-style updates at scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard supervised training data (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet, 28×28 for MNIST, and tokenized text sequences for WikiText-103) and produces trained network weights that yield class labels or language-modeling perplexity. Unusually, training requires an additional forward-mode JVP pass (via dual numbers) alongside the standard forward pass to compute rank-1 Jacobian estimates, and each layer carries a learned feedback matrix B_l in addition to the standard weight matrix W_l. The perturbation direction for JVPs is drawn in whichever space (activation or weight) has lower dimension per layer to minimize variance.

## Method

GrAPE (Gradient-Aligned Projected Error) is a hybrid feedback-alignment method with three components: (i) during the forward pass, a single Jacobian-vector product per layer via forward-mode AD yields a rank-1 Jacobian estimate Ĵ_l = (J_l p) p^⊤ with p ~ N(0, I), which provably has strictly positive expected Frobenius cosine with the true Jacobian (bounded below by √(2/πn_l) · ‖J_l‖₂/‖J_l‖_F); (ii) a local cosine-alignment loss L_align = 1 − c̄_l is minimized with one gradient step per layer to update the feedback matrix B_l toward the estimated Jacobian direction, followed by column normalization; (iii) the aligned B_l is then used in the standard DFA parallel projection δa_l = (B_l ∇L_L) ⊙ σ_l'(a_l) for layer-parallel weight updates. To counteract drift in deep networks, a single full BP step on one randomly selected mini-batch is injected every T epochs as a calibration anchor. The theoretical contribution links the estimator-level alignment bound to a standard stochastic-approximation convergence result under a positive expected-cosine condition, and a Frobenius cosine composition lemma quantifies how estimator noise and imperfect feedback learning combine.

## Results

On shallow architectures (3-layer MLP and LeNet-5 CNN), GrAPE without any BP calibration outperforms all BP-free baselines: 98.53% on MNIST MLP, 73.1% on CIFAR-10 CNN, and 38.0% on CIFAR-100 CNN, versus DFA's 98.21%, 69.34%, and 34.53% respectively. On deep ResNet-20 (CIFAR-100), a single BP calibration per epoch (T=1) lifts GrAPE to 64.82% accuracy, closing most of the gap to BP's 68.72%, while uncalibrated DFA reaches only 20.94%. On a Transformer-Base for WikiText-103 language modeling, GrAPE+calibration (T=1, macro) achieves 33.1 perplexity versus BP's 29.8, halving the gap left by uncalibrated DFA (52.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GrAPE consistently outperforms every BP-free baseline on shallow architectures: on CIFAR-10 CNN it reaches 73.1% vs. DFA (69.34%), FA (71.05%), DRTP (64.73%), and PEPITA (N/A), trailing only BP (74.66%). On deep ResNet-20 (CIFAR-100), GrAPE+calibration (T=1) achieves 64.82%, beating DFA+calibration (59.80%) by 5 points and approaching BP (68.72%); without calibration, GrAPE (24.28%) still exceeds DFA (20.94%). On ResNet-56 (CIFAR-100), the same pattern holds: GrAPE+calibration (T=1) reaches 66.92% vs. DFA+calibration (62.43%) and BP (71.42%). For Transformer-Base on WikiText-103, GrAPE+calibration (T=1, macro) achieves 33.1 perplexity, substantially better than DFA+calibration (42.7) and uncalibrated DFA (52.0), though still behind BP (29.8). The paper also notes that uncalibrated GrAPE on VGG-16 (32.40%) outperforms DFA+calibration (29.40%), suggesting the learned feedback alignment itself provides meaningful gains independent of BP injection.

## Relation to Prior Work

GrAPE sits at the intersection of two previously separate axes of BP-free learning: randomized feedback alignment (FA, DFA) and forward-mode gradient estimation (Silver et al., Baydin et al., Ren et al.). It departs from vanilla DFA by replacing fixed random feedback matrices with learned ones, trained via a local cosine-alignment loss driven by rank-1 JVP-based Jacobian estimates, and departs from pure forward-gradient methods by retaining the DFA-style parallel projection structure rather than attempting full gradient recovery. The occasional single-mini-batch BP calibration step is a novel hybrid element not present in prior FA/DFA or forward-only work, and the paper explicitly positions itself against FDFA (Bacho & Chu, 2024) and SVD-space alignment (Roy et al., 2025) as a simpler, analytically grounded alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GrAPE is the first DFA-style method demonstrated to train VGG-16, ResNet-20/56, and a Transformer-Base, closing most of the accuracy/perplexity gap to full BP with only one BP step per epoch on a single mini-batch (~0.5% of backward passes). However, a 10–15 point gap to BP persists on deep convnets, the current implementation is serialized (no realized parallel speedup), and the forward-mode estimator's variance grows with layer width, making frequent calibration essential for deep models. Open questions include adaptive/event-driven calibration schedules, variance-reduction via multiple perturbation directions or local auxiliary losses, and architecture-aware feedback structures (e.g., block-Toeplitz for convolutions) that could reduce reliance on BP resets.
