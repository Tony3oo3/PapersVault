## Problem

Pretrained discrete diffusion models must often generate samples satisfying downstream constraints (human preferences, toxicity, biological activity), but existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: fine-tuning methods risk reward over-optimisation that degrades sample quality and diversity, while training-free guidance and sampling methods suffer from reward under-optimisation that fails to enforce complex constraints. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a principled inference-time framework that scales compute at test time without either failure mode.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained discrete diffusion model (MDLM for text, MaskGit or Meissonic for images, a DNA diffusion model), a differentiable reward function, and optionally a conditioning or starting prompt, and produces N weighted particles at each denoising step that are resampled to approximate the reward-tilted target. For text, sequences of length 100 are generated over 100 denoising steps; for images, 256×256 outputs are produced; for DNA, sequences are designed to maximise predicted activity and chromatin accessibility. The number of particles N (1–16) and a temperature schedule λ_t ∈ [0,1] control the trade-off between reward alignment and sample diversity, with more particles yielding stronger alignment at the cost of reduced diversity.

## Method

The core contribution is an SMC framework for discrete diffusion that derives tractable importance weights via detailed balance, supporting both product targets (generalising classifier-free guidance) and reward-tilting targets. The key insight is that the proposal distribution governs SMC efficiency: the paper characterises the locally optimal proposal (proportional to π(x_{t-1})γ(x_t|x_{t-1})) and develops two practical approximations. The first is a first-order Taylor expansion of the reward yielding a gradient-based proposal requiring only one reward evaluation per step. The second is a learnable amortised proposal trained by minimising the log-variance of importance weights—a new objective with an auxiliary mean-estimation network that provably upper-bounds the log-variance loss and recovers the optimal proposal at its minimum. A twisted intermediate target with a temperature schedule λ_t smoothly interpolates between the base model and the fully reward-augmented target, and Gumbel-Softmax relaxation handles the non-differentiability of categorical sampling when the reward is defined only on clean data.

## Results

On toxic text generation with MDLM, SMC_amot (N=8) achieves 100% toxic rate and 99.7% holdout toxic rate, compared to 0.8% for the pretrained model and 6.3% for best-of-N (BoN), while SMC_grad reaches 95.0% toxic rate with minimal perplexity degradation (132.1 vs. 121.1 pretrained). On ImageNet256 with MaskGit (32 denoising steps, CFG=1.25), scaling particles from N=1 to N=16 reduces FID from 12.02 to 8.76 and increases Inception Score from 107.5 to 170.7. On DNA sequence design, SMC_amot with larger particle sets achieves the highest predicted activity and ATAC accuracy among all variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SMC variants (SMC_base, SMC_grad, SMC_amot) against non-SMC baselines (Prop_grad, Prop_amot) and best-of-N sampling (BoN) on toxic text generation. SMC methods dramatically outperform all non-SMC baselines: SMC_grad achieves 95.0% toxic rate versus 58.0% for Prop_grad and 63.7% for Prop_amot, while SMC_amot reaches 100% versus 6.3% for BoN. The key differentiator is proposal quality: SMC_amot (learned amortised proposal) consistently outperforms SMC_grad (first-order gradient approximation), which in turn outperforms SMC_base (pretrained diffusion as proposal). On ImageNet256, SMC with N=16 particles reduces FID by 27% (12.02→8.76) and increases IS by 59% (107.5→170.7) over the N=1 standard CFG baseline. The trade-off is that SMC_amot sacrifices diversity (Dist-1 drops from 56 to 44) due to its mode-seeking behaviour, while SMC_grad preserves diversity nearly identically to the pretrained model (57/92/96 vs. 56/92/96) at the cost of slightly lower alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) framework (Del Moral et al., 2006) to discrete diffusion models (Austin et al., 2021) for inference-time control, building on the first-order gradient approximation for discrete distributions from Grathwohl et al. (2021). It departs from prior SMC-for-discrete-diffusion work (Holderrieth et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2025) by handling intractable intermediate targets arising from pretrained models rather than assuming tractable targets, and from prior alignment methods (guidance, RL fine-tuning) by providing an asymptotically unbiased sampler that avoids both reward over-optimisation and under-optimisation. The key methodological departure is the systematic treatment of proposal design: the paper characterises the locally optimal proposal and introduces a learnable amortised proposal trained via log-variance minimisation of importance weights, a criterion not previously explored in the discrete diffusion alignment literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, training-free (for SMC_base and SMC_grad) or lightly-trained (for SMC_amot) recipe for test-time scaling of discrete diffusion models, with demonstrated gains across language modelling, DNA design, and text-to-image generation. The log-variance minimisation objective for proposal training offers a new angle on fine-tuning pretrained discrete diffusion models that is distinct from both RL-based and guidance-based approaches. Open questions include the computational cost of maintaining many particles at scale, the reliance on differentiable rewards for the first-order approximation, and whether the amortised proposal's mode-seeking behaviour (reduced diversity) can be mitigated without sacrificing alignment. The diminishing returns of additional particles as denoising steps increase also suggests a practical ceiling on the SMC scaling axis.
