## Problem

Aligning LLMs for conversational recommendation faces two fundamental challenges: pretrained LLMs lack catalog awareness, violate output formats, and produce rankings that degrade sharply toward the end of the list; and prevalent RL alignment algorithms like GRPO are structurally misaligned with rank-style outputs, applying sequence-level rewards to token-level updates, which causes non-causal credit assignment and mismatched importance weighting. No principled RL framework existed that treats individual ranks as the natural action unit for both credit assignment and policy updates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-turn user-system dialogue (x) and a predefined item catalog (C) as input, and autoregressively generates a ranked list of N=20 items, each formatted as a standardized English movie title with release year (e.g., 'The Bounty (1984)'), separated by newline delimiters. Items must belong to the target catalog, and the generation is decomposed into N sequential rank-wise decisions where each item y^(k) is conditioned on the dialogue context and all previously generated items y^(<k). The LLM operates at the token level internally, but the policy treats each multi-token item as a single action unit.

## Method

ConvRec-R1 is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 performs SFT using a Remap-Reflect-Adjust pipeline that distills catalog-grounded demonstrations from a powerful teacher LLM: raw recommendations are projected into the target catalog via semantic similarity (Remap), refined by LLM-as-a-judge contextual ratings (Reflect), and corrected for residual popularity biases via learned multiplicative/additive score adjustments (Adjust). Stage 2 introduces Rank-GRPO, a principled extension of GRPO that treats each rank k as the action unit rather than a token (too fine-grained) or the full sequence (too coarse). The key mechanisms are: (i) a rank-level return r(x, y_i^(k)) = DCG@k:N that masks out non-causal credit from earlier ranks, so each item is rewarded only for its own and downstream contributions; (ii) a rank-level importance ratio w_{i,k}(θ) defined as the geometric mean of the item's token probabilities, ensuring stable weighting across items of varying token lengths; and (iii) rank-wise advantage estimation computed over the same G sampled trajectories as GRPO, reducing complexity from exponential to linear. An exponential-decay reward variant (Rank-GRPO exp_∞) further credits each item primarily for its own relevance rather than downstream items, which the authors find provides the simplest and most effective learning signal.

## Results

On REDDIT-V2, ConvRec-R1 with Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct + Rank-GRPO(exp∞) achieves R@20 = 0.2368 and N@20 = 0.1283, surpassing GPT-4o zero-shot (R@20 = 0.2147, N@20 = 0.1197) and CRAG with GPT-4o (R@20 = 0.2212, N@20 = 0.1227). The smaller Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone with Rank-GRPO(exp∞) (R@20 = 0.2047, N@20 = 0.1079) substantially outperforms GPT-4o-mini zero-shot (R@20 = 0.1687, N@20 = 0.0973). Rank-GRPO consistently outperforms vanilla GRPO and GSPO across all backbone sizes, with the largest gains at higher ranks (e.g., N@20 improves by ~3–5% over vanilla GRPO for the 3B model).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConvRec-R1 with Llama-3.2-3B + Rank-GRPO(exp∞) outperforms GPT-4o zero-shot by +2.2% R@20 (0.2368 vs 0.2147) and +0.9% N@20 (0.1283 vs 0.1197), and surpasses CRAG (which requires 5–7 GPT-4o API calls per recommendation) by +1.6% R@20 and +0.6% N@20. The 1B model with Rank-GRPO(exp∞) performs on par with GPT-4o zero-shot (R@20: 0.2165 vs 0.2147), while the 0.5B model substantially outperforms GPT-4o-mini (R@20: 0.2047 vs 0.1687, a +21% relative gain). Against RL baselines, Rank-GRPO consistently beats vanilla GRPO by 1.4–1.8% R@20 and 1.1–1.3% N@20 across all three backbones, and also outperforms GSPO in the off-policy setting. Against DPO-based RL-free methods (DPO, S-DPO, SPRec), Rank-GRPO(exp∞) leads by 3.5–5.1% R@20 and 1.8–2.0% N@20, confirming the advantage of generative rank-aware RL over post-hoc preference optimization for list generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025) by identifying a fundamental granularity mismatch when these algorithms are applied to rank-structured outputs: GRPO uses token-level importance ratios with sequence-level rewards, while GSPO uses sequence-level for both, but neither treats individual ranks as the natural action unit. The proposed Rank-GRPO introduces rank-level advantages (via DCG@k:N or exponential-decay returns) and a rank-level importance ratio defined as the geometric mean of item-token probabilities, filling a gap left by prior RL-for-recommendation work (Rec-R1, RecLLM-R1) that still applied sequence-level rewards to token-level updates. The two-stage ConvRec-R1 framework also builds on the Remap-Reflect-Adjust distillation pipeline to address the practical challenge of constructing catalog-grounded SFT data without human annotation, complementing prior LLM-based CRS work (He et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2025) that relied on zero-shot prompting or retrieval augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that properly RL-aligned small open-source LLMs (0.5B–3B) can match or exceed GPT-4o on conversational recommendation, which has direct practical implications for latency-constrained industrial deployment. The rank-level credit assignment principle is generalizable beyond CRS to any task with ordered outputs (retrieval, listwise ranking, sequential decision-making), making Rank-GRPO a potentially reusable algorithmic component. Open questions include whether the catalog drift observed during RL can be mitigated without sacrificing ranking quality, whether the approach extends to dynamic or multi-domain catalogs, and whether the exponential-decay reward (Γ=∞) is truly optimal or merely a stable default. The reliance on a single primary dataset (REDDIT-V2) for the main claims, with REDIAL as a secondary check, leaves some uncertainty about generalization to other item domains.
