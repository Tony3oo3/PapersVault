## Problem

Current LLM development produces one model at a time from static datasets or reward functions, requiring manual reconfiguration for each new capability. No existing framework automatically discovers diverse populations of smaller LLMs with complementary expertise that collectively cover more skills than larger monolithic models, without explicitly optimizing for downstream benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261408/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

AC/DC takes at least three seed LLMs (which must be fine-tuned variants of the same base architecture, per EvoMerge constraints), a small set of manually curated seed tasks, and a large scientist LLM (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) for task generation. It produces a task force of N=3 or N=8 merged LLMs selected to maximize Coverage over an evolved synthetic task archive, where each task is a question-answer pair with a Python scoring function. Models are evaluated with 512-token max output at temperature 0, and the final task force is selected purely from synthetic task performance, independent of any downstream benchmark.

## Method

AC/DC coevolves two archives in alternating phases: an LLM archive evolved via crossover (weighted linear interpolation of task vectors, inherited from CycleQD) and mutation (perturbing the first k=256 singular values of weight matrices), and a synthetic task archive evolved by a scientist LLM that transforms parent tasks into novel variants guided by difficulty profiles. Model selection uses Dominated Novelty Search (DNS) over binary skill vectors, rewarding models that solve tasks fitter models fail, while task selection applies novelty filtering via embedding-space similarity and a reflection-validation loop where the scientist LLM must solve its own generated task. Minimal criterion filters (a gibberish filter for degenerate models and an impossible-task filter) maintain quality without constraining exploration. The key distinction from prior QD methods is that AC/DC never optimizes for any downstream benchmark; instead, open-ended coevolution of models and tasks drives emergent specialization, and the resulting task force is evaluated out-of-distribution on standard benchmarks to demonstrate broader Coverage.

## Results

AC/DC achieves an average Coverage of 60.82% (N=3) and 69.00% (N=8) across eight benchmarks (MMLU, MMLU Pro, GPQA, BBH, GSM8K, Minerva MATH, HumanEval, MBPP), outperforming prior QD methods DNS (60.18/66.48) and CQD (59.85/65.42) without any explicit benchmark optimization. Across four model families, AC/DC yields average Coverage improvements of +2.04% to +10.19% over control and big-model baselines. Notably, a collective of three Qwen2.5 7B models (29% of the 72B model's parameters) surpasses the 72B model by 3.85% Coverage at N=3, growing to 9.78% at N=8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AC/DC outperforms both prior QD baselines on average Coverage across all benchmarks: at N=3, AC/DC achieves 60.82% vs. DNS (60.18%) and CQD (59.85%); at N=8, AC/DC reaches 69.00% vs. DNS (66.48%) and CQD (65.42%). Critically, AC/DC does not optimize for any downstream benchmark, whereas DNS and CQD are explicitly trained on benchmark-specific datasets, making the margin (up to 3.58 points at N=8 over CQD) particularly notable. DNS itself improves over CQD by 0.33 points at N=3 and 1.06 points at N=8, justifying its use as the selection mechanism within AC/DC. Beyond these QD baselines, AC/DC also surpasses hand-curated expert ensembles (average +3.99% at N=3) and monolithic big models (average +10.19% at N=8) across Qwen2, Qwen2.5, Qwen3, and DeepSeek V1 families, while approaching GPT-4o-level Coverage with a fraction of the collective parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

AC/DC synthesizes evolutionary model merging (EvoMerge, CycleQD) with open-ended coevolution (Minimal Criterion Coevolution, POET) and quality-diversity selection (Dominated Novelty Search) into a unified framework for LLM population discovery. The key departure from prior QD-for-LLMs work (CycleQD, DNS) is that those methods optimize against fixed benchmark datasets, whereas AC/DC coevolves the task distribution itself via a scientist LLM, removing explicit benchmark optimization pressure. The task generation pipeline extends Lu et al. (2025) with code-extraction-based evaluation, and the mutation operator (SVD perturbation) generalizes beyond EvoMerge's crossover-only approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a collective of small merged models can match or exceed a frontier model's breadth of capabilities (Coverage) while using a fraction of the parameters, suggesting a viable alternative to monolithic scaling for diverse task coverage. However, the approach is bounded by seed model capabilities (crossover recombines but does not introduce new knowledge), requires same-base-architecture seeds, and relies on a fixed scientist LLM, limiting true open-endedness. Open questions include whether coevolutionary runs can sustain innovation indefinitely, whether mutation can inject genuinely new capabilities beyond the seed distribution, and whether recursive self-improving scientist models could break the current ceiling. The BoN results are notably weaker than Coverage gains, leaving a gap between collective breadth and practical single-answer deployment.
