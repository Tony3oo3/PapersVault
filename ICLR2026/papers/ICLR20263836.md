## Problem

One-step diffusion shortcut models (CT, SCD, IMM, sCT, MeanFlow) share the same objective of learning direct mappings along probability flow trajectories, but their theoretical derivations and practical implementations are tightly coupled, obscuring the underlying design space and making it unclear which components are independently modifiable. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a unified design framework that disentangles component-level choices and provides theoretical justification for the common two-step-target/one-step-prediction paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263836/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single sample of Gaussian noise (and optionally a class label for conditional or classifier-free-guidance generation) and produces a full image in exactly one neural function evaluation (1 NFE). For CIFAR-10, generation occurs directly in pixel space at 32×32 using a ~55M U-Net; for ImageNet-256×256, generation occurs in the latent space of a pretrained VQVAE at 256×256 using a SiT transformer (~131M or ~676M parameters). No pre-training, distillation, or curriculum learning is required.

## Method

The paper unifies all shortcut models under a flow-map framework where a one-step prediction X_{t,r}^θ(x_t) is trained to match a two-step target X_{s,r}∘X_{t,s}(x_t) via the consistency property, with the key insight that discrete-time (DTSC) and continuous-time (CTSC) variants are special cases of the same objective. Building on MeanFlow with linear paths as the baseline, the paper introduces three training improvements: (i) a plug-in velocity that computes the marginal velocity over the mini-batch (reducing supervision variance by O(1−1/B) at negligible cost) with a plug-in probability and class-consistent batching to preserve class-conditional signal under CFG; (ii) a gradual time sampler that transitions from sCT-style fixed r=0 supervision to MeanFlow-style random r sampling over the first 20k iterations; and (iii) established techniques like variational adaptive loss weighting. The resulting ESC model achieves FID50k 2.85 on ImageNet-256×256 with 1 NFE and 2.53 with 2× training, setting a new state of the art among from-scratch shortcut models.

## Results

On ImageNet-256×256 with classifier-free guidance, ESC achieves a new state-of-the-art FID50k of 2.85 under one-step generation (1 NFE) using SiT-XL/2 (~676M params), improving 16.9% over the prior best shortcut model MeanFlow (FID50k 3.43). With 2× training steps (480 epochs), ESC+ further reaches FID50k 2.53, surpassing even MeanFlow's two-step result (2.93). On unconditional CIFAR-10, ESC achieves FID 2.83 with 1 NFE, matching iCT and outperforming sCT (2.97), MeanFlow (2.92), and IMM (3.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among one-step shortcut models on ImageNet-256×256, ESC (FID50k 2.85) decisively outperforms all prior methods: MeanFlow (3.43), IMM (7.77), SCD (10.60), and iCT (34.24), with the largest gap over MeanFlow at 16.9%. ESC+ (2.53) further narrows the gap to multi-step diffusion methods, beating SimDiff (2.77, 512×2 NFE) and approaching SiT-XL/2 (2.06, 250×2 NFE) while requiring only 1 NFE. On CIFAR-10, ESC (FID 2.83) ties iCT (2.83) and beats sCT (2.97), MeanFlow (2.92), IMM (3.20), and ECT (3.60). Against GAN baselines, ESC+ (2.53) surpasses GigaGAN (3.45) and BigGAN (6.95) at 1 NFE, though StyleGAN-XL (2.30) remains slightly better. The paper also shows that ESC's gains scale with model size: the improvement over MeanFlow is larger with SiT-XL/2 (676M) than with SiT-B/2 (131M), suggesting the proposed techniques (plug-in velocity, gradual time sampler, class-consistent batching) become more impactful at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a family of one-step diffusion models trained from scratch—Consistency Training (CT), Shortcut Diffusion (SCD), Inductive Moment Matching (IMM), sCT, and MeanFlow—under a single design framework based on flow map solvers and the consistency property of probability flow ODEs. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit disentanglement of design components (flow path choice, time sampler, network parameterization, loss metric) that were previously entangled in each method's derivation, along with a theoretical justification (Theorem 2.2) that the two-step-target/one-step-prediction paradigm is valid. The practical improvements (plug-in velocity, gradual time sampler) are built directly on MeanFlow as the baseline, while the finding that linear paths dominate cosine paths for shortcut models departs from the cosine-path convention inherited from EDM and CT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework lowers the barrier to component-level innovation in shortcut models by making it clear which choices are independent and which are coupled, enabling more systematic exploration of the design space. The plug-in velocity technique—a simple O(B²) mini-batch computation that reduces supervision variance by O(1−1/B) at negligible cost—is a practical tool likely to be adopted broadly in continuous-time generative model training. Open questions include whether the design space analysis generalizes to other modalities (video, audio), whether the linear-path optimality result extends to conditional settings with complex guidance, and whether the framework can inform multi-step (but still few-step) generation beyond the one-step regime.
