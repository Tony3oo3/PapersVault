## Problem

Few-step generative models (consistency models, MeanFlow, shortcut models) suffer from training instability, model collapse, and GPU memory exhaustion when scaled to large architectures, largely due to their implicit reliance on 1st-order recursive training objectives that are fundamentally incompatible with Exponential Moving Average (EMA) stabilization. Additionally, these methods require expensive Jacobian-vector products (JVP) or complex multi-component losses, and lack a unified theoretical framework connecting them. The paper asks whether a single principled framework can subsume all existing few-step methods as special cases while enabling higher-order formulations that restore EMA compatibility and eliminate JVP.

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian noise sample z~N(0,I) and a condition (class label for ImageNet or text prompt for text-to-image) as input, and produces a synthetic image in 1–8 network function evaluations (NFEs). All ImageNet experiments operate in the latent space of pretrained autoencoders (SD-VAE, DC-AE, or VA-VAE) at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution, while text-to-image and multimodal experiments fine-tune SANA (0.6B/1.6B) or Qwen-Image-20B backbones. The network is conditioned on both the current time t and target time r via separate time embeddings, and outputs a displacement f_θ(x_t, r) = F_θ(x_t, t, r)·(t−r) that maps any point on the PF-ODE trajectory to any earlier point.

## Method

RCGM derives a unified N-th order velocity field estimator by segmenting the PF-ODE integration interval [t, t_{N+1}] into N+1 sub-intervals: the first segment is approximated by a single Euler step (the 'anchor'), while the remaining N segments are predicted by an EMA teacher model (the 'recursive tail'). The training objective matches the true instantaneous velocity dx_t/dt against the multi-step estimate (1/Δt)[f_θ(x_t, t_{N+1}) − Σ_{i=1}^{N} f_{θ⁻}(x_{t_i}, t_{i+1})], requiring only one forward pass with gradient and N without—no JVP needed. For N=0 this reduces to standard diffusion/flow-matching, and for N=1 it recovers consistency models, MeanFlow, and shortcut models; the key advance is N≥2, which breaks the 'EMA incompatibility paradox' of 1st-order self-targeted learning by making the recursive tail long enough that aggressive EMA (κ=0.999) stabilizes rather than degrades training. A variance-reduction loss reformulation decouples gradient scale from the time interval, and the method uses linear transport (α(t)=t, γ(t)=1−t) with a 675M DiT backbone, achieving FID 1.48 at 2 NFEs on ImageNet 256×256 and stable full-parameter training of a 20B unified multimodal model.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256 with VA-VAE, RCGM achieves a FID of 1.48 in just 2 NFEs, surpassing the previous best few-step result (IMM at 1.99 FID requiring 16 NFEs). At 512×512 with DC-AE, RCGM sets a new SOTA of 1.79 FID at 2 NFEs, outperforming sCD-XXL (1.88 FID, 1.5B params) with only 675M parameters. On the 20B-parameter Qwen-Image model, RCGM attains a GenEval score of 0.87 at 8 NFEs and 0.82 at 2 NFEs, while 1st-order baselines (CM, MeanFlow) suffer model collapse or OOM under the same setting. For text-to-image, RCGM-0.6B reaches GenEval 0.85 at 2 NFEs, beating SANA-Sprint-1.6B (0.77) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCGM decisively outperforms all 1st-order consistency-based methods: at 256×256, it achieves FID 1.92 (SD-VAE, 2 NFE) vs. MeanFlow's 2.93 and Shortcut's 7.80 (4 NFE), and FID 1.48 (VA-VAE, 2 NFE) vs. IMM's 1.99 (16 NFE). At 512×512, RCGM with DC-AE (FID 1.79, 2 NFE) surpasses the larger sCD-XXL (FID 1.88, 1.5B params, 2 NFE) while using only 675M parameters and 800 epochs vs. 921. Against multi-step diffusion/flow-matching baselines (DiT-XL/2, SiT-XL/2, EDM2-XXL), RCGM matches or exceeds their quality with 100–125× fewer NFEs. On the 20B Qwen-Image model, RCGM is the only method that successfully trains in full-parameter mode; CM-based approaches collapse (GenEval 0.0) and MeanFlow runs out of memory, while RCGM achieves GenEval 0.82 at 2 NFEs. In text-to-image, RCGM-0.6B (GenEval 0.85, 2 NFE) beats SANA-Sprint-1.6B (0.77) and FLUX-Schnell (0.71) by a substantial margin, and at 1 NFE it reaches 0.80 vs. SANA-Sprint-1.6B's 0.76.

## Relation to Prior Work

RCGM unifies the PF-ODE-based generative modeling landscape by showing that diffusion/flow-matching (0th-order), consistency models, shortcut models, and MeanFlow (all 1st-order) are special cases of a single N-th order recursive velocity field estimation framework derived from segmented integration along the PF-ODE trajectory. The key conceptual departure is identifying an EMA incompatibility paradox inherent in 1st-order self-targeted learning—where aggressive EMA decay rates that stabilize training simultaneously degrade generative quality—and demonstrating that higher-order formulations (N≥2) resolve this tension. Unlike prior few-step methods, RCGM requires neither Jacobian-vector products (as in sCM and MeanFlow) nor auxiliary discriminator/fake-score networks (as in DMD/LADD), relying solely on a single forward pass with gradient plus N gradient-free forward passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: RCGM enables stable full-parameter training of a 20B unified multimodal model (Qwen-Image-20B) where 1st-order consistency and MeanFlow methods suffer model collapse or GPU memory exhaustion, and achieves SOTA FID 1.48 on ImageNet 256×256 in just 2 NFEs with a 675M DiT. The EMA incompatibility insight reframes a long-standing hyperparameter sensitivity issue in consistency training as a structural limitation of 1st-order recursion rather than a tuning problem. The main open limitation is that 1-NFE generation still lags behind multi-step baselines, and the authors identify adversarial objectives as a promising but unexplored direction for closing this gap in extreme few-step regimes.
