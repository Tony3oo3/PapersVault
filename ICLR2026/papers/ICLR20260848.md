## Problem

Existing video generation models trained with pixel-space noise reconstruction objectives overemphasize appearance fidelity while producing unrealistic, physically implausible human motions—particularly for complex whole-body dynamics, long-range movement, and human-environment interactions. This structural incoherence stems from the absence of explicit human structural priors during generation, and is compounded by existing human video datasets that predominantly capture only simple facial or upper-body movements.

## Input / Output

For text-to-video generation, the method takes a free-form text prompt as input; a motion-specific subset p′ is extracted (via LLM or user specification) to drive the structure branch, while the full prompt conditions the appearance branch. For the image-to-video variant, a reference image is additionally provided. Generation operates at 720×480 resolution, and the intermediate structural output is a 2D skeleton sequence rendered from 3D keypoints, which can be projected from user-specified fixed or time-varying camera poses without retraining.

## Method

MoSA decouples human video generation into a structure branch and an appearance branch. The structure branch uses a pretrained autoregressive 3D structure transformer (trained on million-scale motion datasets) to generate 3D human keypoints from the motion-specific prompt, which are then projected into a 2D skeleton sequence serving as structural guidance. The appearance branch is a DiT-based diffusion model (CogVideoX-5B or Wan 2.1) where Human-Aware Dynamic Control (HADC) modules are inserted between DiT blocks; each HADC module uses a learnable dynamic weight predictor to propagate sparse skeleton features across the full motion region with spatially-varying control weights, supervised by a mask loss. Training incorporates a dense tracking loss (using CoTracker3 with exponential temporal weighting for long-range dependencies) and a 3D contact constraint (using VGGT to lift frames into point clouds and penalize SDF-based interpenetration with the scene mesh). Only the structure generation blocks and HADC modules are trained (base model weights frozen), on the newly curated MoVid dataset of 30K complex-motion videos.

## Results

On the primary text-to-video benchmark, MoSA achieves an FVD of 1093 and CLIP similarity of 0.3035, outperforming the strongest general video generation baseline Wan2.1 (FVD 1251, CLIP 0.2951) by 12.6% and 2.8% respectively. MoSA also leads on all VBench sub-metrics: Subject Consistency (96.83%), Background Consistency (97.43%), Motion Smoothness (99.25%), and Imaging Quality (65.43%), though it trails LaVie slightly on Dynamic Degree (52.86% vs. 53.73%). In the pose-driven human animation setting on HumanVid, MoSA achieves SSIM 0.691, PSNR 20.802, LPIPS 0.209, and FID 34.76, matching or exceeding Uni3C on all metrics except FVD (568.3 vs. 562.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against seven general video generation models (ModelScope, VideoCrafter2, LaVie, Mochi 1, CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1), MoSA achieves the best FVD (1093 vs. 1207–1959) and CLIP similarity (0.3035 vs. 0.2739–0.2951), and leads on Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, Motion Smoothness, and Imaging Quality; the sole exception is Dynamic Degree, where LaVie edges ahead (53.73% vs. 52.86%). Against four pose-driven human animation methods (Animate Anyone, HumanVid, MIMO, StableAnimator) in Table 11, MoSA wins on every metric, with the largest gaps in FVD (1108 vs. 1285–1374) and Subject Consistency (97.74% vs. 94.09–95.37%). In the pose-driven comparison on HumanVid (Table 12), MoSA matches Uni3C closely (FVD 568.3 vs. 562.7) while surpassing it on SSIM (0.691 vs. 0.687), PSNR (20.802 vs. 20.779), LPIPS (0.209 vs. 0.221), and FID (34.76 vs. 35.11), and substantially outperforms Animate Anyone, CHAMP, and HumanVid. In user studies against text-driven human video generation methods Move-in-2D and HumanDreamer, MoSA is preferred at 49.5% for Motion Quality and 51.9% for Video Quality, roughly doubling the preference rates of both baselines (24.3%/26.2% and 24.7%/23.4% respectively). Overall, MoSA wins or ties on essentially all reported metrics across all comparison settings, with the only narrow losses being Dynamic Degree vs. LaVie and FVD vs. Uni3C in the pose-driven setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: general DiT-based video generation (CogVideoX, Wan2.1) and text-driven 3D human motion generation (autoregressive transformers pretrained on motion datasets like AMASS and Motion-X). It departs from prior human video generation approaches in a key architectural way: rather than conditioning a single diffusion model on sparse skeleton inputs (as in Animate Anyone, HumanVid) or relying solely on pixel-space noise prediction (as in general T2V models), MoSA explicitly decouples structure generation (via a pretrained 3D keypoint transformer) from appearance synthesis (via a DiT backbone), with the HADC modules bridging the two. The dense tracking loss and 3D contact constraint (leveraging VGGT for point-cloud reconstruction) are novel training objectives that address known failure modes identified in prior work (Chefer et al. 2025, Jeong et al. 2024) where noise-prediction objectives overemphasize appearance at the expense of motion coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structure-appearance decoupling paradigm offers a practical, modular recipe for improving human motion coherence in any DiT-based video model, as demonstrated by consistent gains when applied to both CogVideoX-5B and Wan2.1-14B without architectural retraining of the base model. The MoVid dataset (30K clips, 17K action types, significantly higher optical-flow complexity than HumanVid) addresses a real data bottleneck that limits existing text-driven human video generators to simple or upper-body motions. Key open questions remain: fine-grained hand/finger generation still produces artifacts due to the absence of hand-joint annotations in 3D motion datasets, and the framework's scalability to long-form or multi-person scenes with complex inter-person contact is only preliminarily explored. The contact constraint's reliance on VGGT for per-frame 3D reconstruction also raises questions about computational cost at scale.
