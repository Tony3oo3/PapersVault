## Problem

Continual learning with vision-language models like CLIP suffers from two critical limitations: class prototypes constructed via passive averaging of limited exemplars progressively degrade as feature spaces evolve across sequential tasks, and existing methods exhibit asymmetric multimodal exploitation by adapting only textual prompts while treating visual encoders as static feature extractors. This combination leads to accelerating forgetting and a persistent performance bottleneck, particularly on fine-grained recognition where discriminative visual patterns lack precise linguistic descriptors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224, processed by frozen CLIP ViT-B/16) and text class templates of the form 'A photo of a [CLASS]' as input, producing class labels via nearest-prototype matching over cosine similarities. In the class-incremental setting, tasks arrive sequentially with 20 exemplars stored per class; visual prompts (K learnable tokens) are appended to image patch embeddings and textual prompts (M learnable tokens) are prepended to the text template, with task-specific linear projections mapping frozen CLIP features into a task-adapted space of dimension D'.

## Method

RLAP-CLIP integrates three complementary mechanisms on a frozen CLIP backbone. First, Reinforcement Learning-based Prototype Optimization (RLPO) replaces uniform exemplar averaging with a policy network (4-layer MLP) that learns per-sample weights to maximize a reward combining intra-class similarity, inter-class margin, and a prototype-collapse penalty, trained via REINFORCE with KL regularization against an EMA reference policy. Second, difficulty-aware mixture-of-experts routing computes a difficulty score from the average distance to visual and textual prototypes, then gates each sample through either a single linear layer (easy) or a 3-layer feed-forward network (hard) via a sigmoid threshold, followed by cross-modal attention that adaptively weights expert-processed visual features, visual prototypes, and textual prototypes. Third, dual-modal prompting with task-specific expandable projections jointly adapts both visual and textual encoders while preserving previously learned knowledge. Training optimizes a multi-objective loss combining difficulty-weighted classification, bidirectional contrastive alignment, the RLPO policy gradient, and an expert-balancing term, with 8.5M trainable parameters per task.

## Results

RLAP-CLIP achieves state-of-the-art average accuracy across all eight benchmarks, with headline results of 86.64% on CIFAR-100, 85.78% on CUB-200, 94.82% on Cars, 70.25% on Aircraft, 97.68% on UCF-101, and 53.89% on ObjectNet. The largest gains over the strongest baseline (C-CLIP) appear on fine-grained and action recognition tasks: +4.52 points average accuracy on Aircraft, +5.83 on UCF-101, and +3.64 on CUB-200. Final accuracy improvements reach up to +6.26 points (Aircraft) and +6.13 points (UCF-101) over C-CLIP, demonstrating superior long-term knowledge retention in the class-incremental setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLAP-CLIP outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and both metrics (average and final accuracy). Against the strongest vision-language baseline C-CLIP, gains range from +1.16 points (Food-101 avg) to +5.83 points (UCF-101 avg), with the most dramatic improvements on fine-grained tasks: +4.52 on Aircraft and +3.64 on CUB-200. Compared to PROOF, the prior SOTA, RLAP-CLIP leads by +3.72 on CIFAR-100, +6.66 on Aircraft, and +4.12 on UCF-101 in average accuracy. Prompt-based methods (CoOp, CODA-Prompt, DAP, PLOT) trail substantially on fine-grained recognition—CoOp achieves only 41.81% avg on Aircraft versus RLAP-CLIP's 70.25%—highlighting the benefit of dual-modal prompting and difficulty-aware routing. Classical fine-tuning baselines (Finetune, Finetune LiT) suffer catastrophic forgetting with final accuracies below 3% on most datasets, confirming that parameter-freezing strategies are essential for continual learning with CLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based continual learning line (CoOp, CODA-Prompt, PROOF, C-CLIP) by targeting two specific limitations the authors identify: prototype quality degradation from passive averaging and asymmetric text-only adaptation. The RLPO component repurposes policy-gradient optimization (with KL regularization and advantage normalization) for the previously passive task of exemplar weighting, while the difficulty-aware MoE routing borrows from the broader MoE literature but applies it to sample-level processing in a continual learning context. The dual-modal prompting with task-specific expandable projections builds on MaPLe-style multi-modal prompting but adds per-task projection layers to prevent cross-task interference, and the theoretical convergence analysis (Appendix C) provides formal guarantees absent from prior prototype-based continual learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for improving VLM continual learning by combining three complementary mechanisms, with the most actionable finding being that visual prompts contribute substantial gains (especially on fine-grained tasks like Aircraft and CUB-200) that prior text-centric methods systematically ignored. The visual prompting trade-off analysis—showing a 2.27:1 benefit-to-harm ratio concentrated in same-family numerical variants where symbolic text distinctions dominate—gives practitioners a clear picture of when dual-modal adaptation helps versus hurts. Limitations include evaluation only under class-incremental settings, task-dependent benefits of visual prompting, and the added complexity of maintaining an RL policy network alongside MoE experts and dual prompts (8.5M trainable parameters). Open questions include whether RLPO generalizes to domain- or task-incremental paradigms, and whether the RL-based weighting can be replaced by simpler learned heuristics without significant performance loss.
