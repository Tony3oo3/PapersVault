## Problem

The Learning to Explain paradigm aims to train amortized explainers that produce high-quality explanations in a single forward pass, but faces three unresolved challenges: self-supervised objectives depend on restrictive assumptions about the target model or task, prior-explanation-driven methods cannot guarantee the faithfulness of their supervisory signals, and relying on either approach alone yields poor convergence or explanations bounded by the quality of imitated labels. No existing method simultaneously addresses all three limitations.

## Input / Output

The explainer accepts sequential inputs of variable length—196 image patches (each 768-d), up to 500 text tokens (256–512-d embeddings), or 7–8 scalar tabular features—and outputs a normalized n-dimensional saliency vector in [0,1]^n via a multi-layer Transformer encoder followed by a linear projection. During training, the explainer also interacts directly with the frozen target model to compute perturbation-based faithfulness signals, meaning the target model's forward pass is part of the training loop but not the inference loop.

## Method

DeepFaith introduces a faithfulness-grounded self-supervised loss (L_LC) that maximizes the correlation between local sums of predicted saliency and the corresponding model-output changes under random feature removal, requiring no assumptions about the target model or task. In parallel, it constructs high-quality supervised signals by aggregating K prior explanation methods per sample, deduplicating via cosine-similarity thresholding, and filtering via p-quantile thresholds across ten faithfulness metrics. A dynamic weighting strategy (Algorithm 1) jointly optimizes both losses: training begins with α=1 to fit supervised signals for rapid convergence, then α is gradually annealed via a sigmoid schedule once L_PC variance drops below a threshold, shifting emphasis to L_LC; if L_PC variance subsequently exceeds C·ε, α resets to 1 to prevent drift into poor local minima. The explainer architecture is a multi-layer Transformer encoder (4–12 layers depending on task complexity) with a normalized linear head projecting to the n-dimensional saliency space.

## Results

DeepFaith achieves the best average faithfulness rank across all 12 explanation tasks, with mean ranks ranging from 1.8 (NAP+MLP, WCD+MLP) to 4.4 (ImageNet+DeiT, ImageNet+EfficientNet), consistently outperforming all 16 baseline explanation methods. Against prior Learning to Explain methods (Table 3), DeepFaith attains mean ranks of 1.3–1.6 versus 2.4–4.2 for VerT, L2X, CXPlain, and FastSHAP across four settings. For example, on NAP+MLP, DeepFaith scores 0.788 on FC and 0.952 on MC, compared to 0.772/0.560 for the strongest L2E baseline (VerT). Ablation studies confirm that the full dynamic joint optimization (L_OBJ) outperforms either loss alone, and that both deduplicating and filtering of supervised signals are necessary for peak performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepFaith outperforms all 16 post-hoc explanation baselines across every one of the 12 tasks. Among image-specific methods, Grad-CAM++ (best rank 4.9 on ImageNet+DeiT) and Score-CAM (6.0 on OCT+ResNet) are the closest competitors, yet DeepFaith still leads by 1.5–3.7 rank positions; gradient-based methods like Saliency (10.6–13.2) and Guided Backprop (6.5–12.3) are consistently far behind. General-purpose methods such as Integrated Grads (best 2.8 on NAP) and Kernel SHAP (best 3.9 on AGNews+Transformer) show moderate performance on tabular/text tasks but degrade sharply on high-dimensional image tasks (ranks 5.4–12.1). Against the four Learning to Explain baselines in Table 3, DeepFaith achieves mean ranks of 1.3–1.6 versus 2.4 (VerT, best L2E baseline) to 4.2 (CXPlain, worst), with the largest gaps on OCT+ResNet (1.4 vs. 4.2) and ImageNet+DeiT (1.6 vs. 3.9). Notably, DeepFaith surpasses even the supervised signals it was trained on, confirming that the self-supervised L_LC loss enables the explainer to exceed the faithfulness ceiling of any single prior method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two branches of the Learning to Explain (amortized explanation) paradigm: self-supervised optimization methods (VerT, L2X, CXPlain) that rely on restrictive model/task assumptions, and prior-explanation-driven methods (L2E, FastSHAP, ViT Shapley) that are bounded by the quality of their supervisory signals. Rather than extending either branch in isolation, DeepFaith repurposes the concept of faithfulness—previously used only as an evaluation criterion—as the unifying optimization principle, theoretically proving that a single saliency mapping can be simultaneously optimal across ten widely used faithfulness metrics. The dynamic joint optimization strategy and the deduplication/filtering pipeline for supervisory signals are novel engineering contributions that bridge the two branches, but the overall architecture (a Transformer encoder producing a saliency vector) is a straightforward application of existing sequence-modeling components to the explanation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical unification of ten faithfulness metrics under a single optimal mapping provides a clean, assumption-free objective for amortized explainers, addressing a long-standing gap where self-supervised methods required idealized assumptions about feature separability or causality. The consistent outperformance across 12 tasks spanning three modalities and multiple architectures strengthens the case that amortized explanation can surpass individual post-hoc methods in faithfulness. However, the approach still depends on a library of prior explanation methods to generate training signals, and the theoretical optimal mapping remains intractable in practice (only approximated via Monte Carlo), leaving open whether the gap between the approximation and the true optimum can be further closed. The explainer's reliance on a Transformer encoder also raises questions about efficiency for very high-dimensional inputs (e.g., full-resolution images without patching).
