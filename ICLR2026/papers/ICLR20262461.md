## Problem

Prefill latency dominates LLM inference (often >70% of runtime), and while chunked prefill improves scheduling, the quadratic attention cost remains. Existing sparse attention methods are either pattern-based (requiring custom CUDA kernels, limiting hardware portability) or query-dependent (designed for single-query generation, degrading significantly when extended to the multi-query prefill setting). No training-free, hardware-agnostic method existed that could efficiently subselect KVs for many queries simultaneously under chunked prefill while preserving accuracy.

## Input / Output

QUOKA operates per prefill chunk: it receives the chunk's queries Q (shape b×n_Q×B_CP×d), the accumulated KV cache from all preceding chunks (K, V of length T), and hyperparameters B_CP (chunk size), B_SA (selective attention budget), and N_Q (number of subselected queries). It outputs a reduced KV pair (K*, V*) of size B_SA that is concatenated with the current chunk's own K_i, V_i and fed into a standard dense attention kernel (e.g., FlashAttention). The method is training-free and relies only on standard linear algebra operations (normalization, matrix multiplication, topk, gather), making it deployable on any hardware without custom kernels.

## Method

QUOKA reduces the active KV cache in three stages. First, Query Subselection computes the mean query M_Q and retains only the top N_Q queries ranked by negative cosine similarity to M_Q, based on the observation (formalized in Theorem 1) that queries angularly distant from the mean attend broadly to most keys and dominate the attention distribution. Second, Cosine-Similarity Scoring normalizes both Q and K to unit length and computes bounded cosine similarity scores; for grouped-query attention, normalized queries are pre-aggregated by averaging across KV groups before the matrix product, reducing computation and memory by a factor of n_Q/n_KV. Third, Score Aggregation takes the maximum across the query axis (preserving rare but important query-key interactions) and the mean across the KV-group axis (exploiting head-level correlation), then applies topk to select B_SA keys. The resulting subselected KV set is passed to a standard dense attention kernel, reducing prefill complexity from O(T²) to sub-quadratic while remaining fully compatible with existing optimized kernels.

## Results

On RULER (B_SA=1024), QUOKA achieves 86.71/80.19/70.90/57.01 on Llama-3.2-3B at 4k/8k/16k/32k prompt lengths, outperforming the strongest baseline (SparQ: 79.36/60.80/48.59/31.14) by 7–26 points. On LongBench, QUOKA retains 94.5% of dense accuracy at B_SA=512 and 98.6% at B_SA=2048 on Llama-3.2-3B, versus 73.8% and 90.1% for SampleAttention. On Math500, QUOKA matches or exceeds dense attention (0.911 Flex Match vs. 0.893 dense on GPT-OSS-20B at budget 128). QUOKA achieves a 5× attention speedup and 3× TTFT reduction on A100 GPUs, up to 7× on Intel Xeon CPUs, while using 88% fewer KV pairs per attention evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QUOKA outperforms all seven baselines across every benchmark and model tested. On RULER (B_SA=1024), QUOKA leads the closest competitor SparQ by 7.4 points on Llama-3.2-3B at 4k (86.71 vs. 79.36) and by 25.9 points at 32k (57.01 vs. 31.14); the gap widens further on Qwen3-4B where QUOKA reaches 93.73 at 4k versus SparQ's 87.93. SampleAttention, the strongest query-dependent baseline, trails QUOKA by 8.5 points on Llama-3.2-3B (4k) and by 34 points on Qwen3-4B (32k: 74.83 vs. 40.72). On LongBench, QUOKA at B_SA=512 retains 94.5% of dense accuracy on Llama-3.2-3B, while SampleAttention drops to 73.8%, SparQ to 72.1%, and TidalDecode to 70.3%. On Math500 (generation), QUOKA at budget 128 achieves 0.911 Flex Match on GPT-OSS-20B, surpassing dense attention (0.893) and outperforming Loki (0.905), SparQ (0.744), and LessIsMore (0.782). SnapKV and KeyDiff, designed for KV eviction rather than prefill subselection, degrade most severely under chunked prefill, scoring below 30 on RULER at 4k for Llama-3.2-3B.

## Relation to Prior Work

QUOKA extends the query-dependent sparse attention line (SparQ, Loki, Quest, SampleAttention) from the single-query generation setting to the multi-query chunked prefill setting, where naively averaging query scores degrades accuracy. It departs from kernel-level sparse attention (Minference, XAttention, FlexPrefill) by relying solely on standard linear algebra operations rather than custom CUDA primitives, achieving hardware portability. The query subselection via cosine dissimilarity from the mean query is inspired by the key-geometry observations in KeyDiff (Park et al., 2025), but applied to the query side with a supporting theoretical bound (Theorem 1). The pre-aggregation trick for GQA—averaging normalized queries across KV groups before scoring—is a novel efficiency optimization that reduces both compute and memory by a factor of the number of KV groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QUOKA's training-free, hardware-agnostic design (achieving 5× attention speedup on GPUs and 7× on CPUs with 88% fewer KV pairs) makes it immediately deployable across heterogeneous inference environments without custom kernels or retraining, addressing a real gap for edge and consumer hardware. The consistent 10–20% accuracy advantage over all competing sparse methods across six model families (including MoE and NoPE variants) suggests the query-geometry insight is broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the QK^T scoring step can be further accelerated via channel sparsity or learned low-rank projections, and whether QUOKA can be combined with KV cache eviction to also reduce memory footprint rather than just compute. A limitation is that the full KV cache must still be stored; QUOKA reduces attention compute but not cache memory.
