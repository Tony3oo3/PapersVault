## Problem

Prior statistical analyses of Transformers with infinite-dimensional (measure-valued) inputs either assume a frozen, non-learnable attention kernel or restrict to linear attention, whose limited expressiveness cannot realize the sharp, near one-hot weight distributions needed for sparse associative recall. This paper asks whether a learned softmax-attention Transformer can recall a specific component from an arbitrarily long, distributional context and predict from it, with provable generalization guarantees and minimax-optimal sample complexity.

## Input / Output

The input is a mixture probability measure ν = (1/I)Σᵢ δ_{v⁽ⁱ⁾} ⊗ μ₀⁽ⁱ⁾ over a token space ℝ^{d₁}×ℝ^{d₂}, where each component is a product of a fixed document-feature point mass and a smooth content density in an RKHS ball, together with a query x_q = (v⁽ⁱ*⁾, 0) that encodes which document to retrieve. The output is a scalar F*(ν, x_q) = F̃*(μ₀⁽ⁱ*⁾, x_q) that depends on the entire mixture only through the single recalled content measure μ₀⁽ⁱ*⁾, making the task a two-stage recall-then-predict pipeline. The content densities are infinite-dimensional objects constrained to a generalized RKHS norm ball, and the number of mixture components I is bounded by the document-feature dimension d₁.

## Method

The student model is a depth-2 measure-theoretic Transformer (MLP → softmax attention → MLP → skip-connection attention) trained by empirical risk minimization over a bounded-parameter hypothesis class. The first MLP embeds each token y=(v,z) into a feature vector containing the first D Mercer eigenfunction evaluations e_j(z), compressing the infinite-dimensional content measure into D summary statistics. Softmax attention then computes scores proportional to ⟨v⁽ⁱ*⁾, v⁽ⁱ⁾⟩, producing near one-hot weights that isolate the query-relevant component and integrate its Mercer coefficients to yield a D-dimensional descriptor b = (∫e_j dμ₀⁽ⁱ*⁾)_{j=1}^D. A final Lipschitz MLP maps (b, x_q) to the scalar prediction. Under exponential eigenvalue decay λ_j ≍ exp(−cj^α), the paper proves a sub-polynomial population-risk bound exp(−Ω((log n)^{α/(α+1)})) and a matching minimax lower bound, establishing that the learned softmax Transformer is minimax-optimal in the exponent of the convergence rate.

## Results

The paper establishes a sub-polynomial population-risk upper bound for a depth-2 measure-theoretic Transformer trained by ERM: R(F*, F̂) ≲ exp{−Ω((log n)^{α/(α+1)})}, where α governs the exponential Mercer eigenvalue decay λ_j ≍ exp(−cj^α). A matching minimax lower bound (Theorem 2) shows that no estimator can achieve a rate with a larger exponent, proving the Transformer is minimax-optimal in the order of the exponent. The effective dimension driving the rate is D_eff(n) ≍ (log n)^{1/(α+1)}, meaning the infinite-dimensional measure is compressed into a finite number of Mercer coefficients whose count grows only logarithmically with sample size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The 'comparisons' are conceptual: the paper shows that (i) frozen (non-learnable) attention kernels (Liu & Zhou, 2025) cannot adaptively select the relevant measure component, and (ii) linear attention (Kim et al., 2024) is too limited in expressiveness to realize the sharp, near-one-hot softmax weight distributions needed for sparse recall when context vectors have negative correlations. The learned softmax attention is shown to be the critical architectural ingredient enabling the minimax-optimal rate, whereas prior frameworks either assume a fixed kernel or restrict to linear attention, neither of which can achieve the stated sub-polynomial convergence for the associative recall task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads: (i) the associative-memory / content-addressable retrieval view of attention (Ramsauer et al., 2021; Bietti et al., 2023) and (ii) the measure-theoretic formalization of Transformers for infinite-dimensional inputs (Vuckovic et al., 2020; Furuya et al., 2025). It departs from prior generalization analyses that either assumed a frozen (non-learnable) attention kernel (Liu & Zhou, 2025) or restricted to linear attention (Kim et al., 2024), by providing the first statistical theory for *learned* softmax attention performing measure-valued recall. The technical machinery draws on Schmidt-Hieber (2020) for covering-number-based risk bounds, E & Wang (2018) for analytic-function approximation by ReLU networks, and Lanthaler (2024) / Yang & Barron (1999) for the information-theoretic lower bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result gives a principled, minimax-optimal statistical guarantee for the core 'recall-then-predict' pipeline that underlies in-context learning in Transformers, showing that the effective dimensionality of an infinite-dimensional measure is governed by the kernel's spectral decay rate α. This explains *why* softmax attention (with its ability to produce near one-hot, query-dependent weight distributions) is the right inductive bias for associative recall, in contrast to linear attention or frozen kernels. Limitations include the restriction to exponentially decaying spectra (Gaussian-type kernels), a depth-2 architecture, and a bounded number of mixture components; extending to polynomial decay, deeper networks, and growing I are identified as natural next steps. The framework also opens a path toward rigorous analysis of long-context Transformers beyond the finite-sequence regime.
