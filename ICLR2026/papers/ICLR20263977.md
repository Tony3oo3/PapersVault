## Problem

Existing LLM-based Automated Heuristic Design (AHD) frameworks suffer from two fundamental limitations: (1) they lack global adaptive control, relying on local or reactive signals that cannot proactively intervene when the population stagnates or diversity collapses, and (2) they exhibit knowledge decay, where successful design principles remain entangled in specific code implementations and are lost when parent candidates are discarded, preventing cumulative learning across generations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263977/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a problem definition (e.g., TSP distance matrix, BPP item sequence, FSSP processing time matrix) and a set of training instances as input, and produces a complete, executable Python heuristic function as output. Crucially, the LLM is used only during the offline evolutionary design phase; at inference time, the generated function is invoked directly per decision step (e.g., selecting the next city in TSP construction or scoring bins in BPP) without any LLM calls, making the deployed heuristic fast and self-contained.

## Method

HiFo-Prompt introduces a hierarchical control architecture with two synergistic modules that dynamically construct each generation's prompt. The Foresight module (Evolutionary Navigator) monitors macroscopic indicators—performance stagnation counters and a text-based phenotypic diversity metric—and switches the search regime among Explore, Exploit, or Balance by injecting a natural-language design directive into the prompt, serving as a symbolic alternative to expensive numerical fine-tuning. The Hindsight module (Insight Pool) maintains a self-evolving repository of abstracted design principles distilled from elite heuristics via LLM prompting, with utility-based retrieval (balancing effectiveness, usage penalty, and recency bonus), credit assignment via a tiered piecewise reward and EMA updates, and adaptive pruning to maintain pool quality. The composite prompt combines foundational genetic-operator strategies (adapted from EoH), top-scoring insights, and the current regime directive, enabling the LLM to generate heuristics that are both historically informed and strategically aligned with the current evolutionary state.

## Results

On TSP step-by-step construction, HiFo-Prompt achieves 6.625% gap on TSP50, 8.582% on TSP100, and 8.877% on TSP200, outperforming the best LLM-based AHD baseline (ReEvo) by 3.6–6.0 percentage points. On TSP with Guided Local Search, it reaches 0.015% gap on TSP100 and 1.520% on TSP500, beating EoH (0.026% and 2.037%) and ReEvo (0.049% and 2.090%). On Online BPP, it achieves 0.66–0.69% gap to the lower bound across all three capacity settings, surpassing MCTS-AHD (0.95–0.99%) and EoH (1.00–1.02%). On FSSP, it consistently halves the gap of EoH (e.g., 0.13% vs. 0.23% on n=100,m=10; 0.51% vs. 0.94% on n=100,m=20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiFo-Prompt outperforms all five LLM-based AHD baselines (EoH, ReEvo, HSEvo, MCTS-AHD, FunSearch) across every task and problem scale tested. On TSP step-by-step, it reduces the gap by 3.6 pp over ReEvo (6.625% vs. 10.239% on TSP50) and by 7.7 pp over EoH (8.582% vs. 15.361% on TSP100). On TSP-GLS, it beats EoH by 0.517 pp on TSP500 (1.520% vs. 2.037%) and ReEvo by 0.570 pp. On Online BPP, it achieves the lowest gap in all nine capacity/size configurations (0.39–0.69%), edging out MCTS-AHD by 0.26–0.33 pp. On FSSP, it consistently halves EoH's gap (e.g., 0.13% vs. 0.23% at n=100,m=10). However, on TSP step-by-step it still trails the handcrafted LKH3 (0% gap) and the NCO methods POMO/LEHD, which leverage learned neural policies rather than code-based heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM+EC paradigm established by FunSearch and EoH, which cast the LLM as a semantic mutation operator over a population of heuristic code. It departs from prior work along two axes: (1) the Foresight module introduces a rule-based meta-controller (Evolutionary Navigator) that monitors population-level dynamics and switches between exploration/exploitation regimes via natural-language directives, contrasting with ReEvo's single-candidate reflection, MCTS-AHD's passive tree-structured search, and in-weight adaptation methods (EvoTune, CALM) that rely on expensive numerical gradients; and (2) the Hindsight module implements an Insight Pool that distills abstract design principles from elite code, drawing conceptually on Cultural Algorithms' belief space but operating at the prompt/semantic level rather than through numerical credit assignment. The foundational prompt strategies (I1, E1, E2, M1–M3) are directly adapted from EoH, making the contribution primarily architectural rather than a new generative mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete and well-motivated gaps in LLM-based AHD—lack of global adaptive control and knowledge decay—through a clean hierarchical design that keeps the LLM as an interpretable symbolic reasoner rather than an opaque fine-tuned policy. The consistent outperformance across TSP, Online BPP, FSSP, and BO, combined with ablations confirming both modules contribute, makes this a strong practical reference for the LLM+EC community. Open questions include whether the rule-based Navigator generalizes beyond the three discrete regimes, whether the exact-string-matching diversity metric holds under more diverse LLM outputs, and whether the Insight Pool can transfer knowledge across problem domains rather than remaining task-specific. The small population size (8) and generation count (8) also raise questions about scalability to larger search spaces.
