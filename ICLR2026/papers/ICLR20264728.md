## Problem

Existing change captioning methods treat the task as a static spatial comparison between two images, ignoring the rich temporal dynamics of how a change actually unfolds. This omission means models capture only *what* changed but not *how* it occurred, limiting their ability to disentangle genuine semantic changes from viewpoint or illumination distractors and to generalize to complex, multi-change scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264728/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a pair of 224×224 RGB images (before/after) and produces a free-form change caption. Internally, each image is encoded by a frozen CNN backbone into a 14×14 grid of patch embeddings (n_I = 196 patches, d = 768), and k=2 sets of learnable procedure queries (each of size n_I) are inserted between the two image feature sequences to form the encoder input. During training, the procedure modeling stage additionally consumes VQGAN-quantized discrete tokens (codebook size 1024, latent dim 256) as reconstruction targets, but these are not needed at inference.

## Method

ProCap is a two-stage framework. In Stage 1 (Explicit Procedure Modeling), a pre-trained optical-flow frame interpolation model (VFIformer) synthesizes l=7 intermediate frames between the image pair; a confidence-based sampling module then selects k=2 keyframes using a semantic-equidistance score (computed via DINOv2 features and a CLIP-based image-pair/text alignment model). A 12-layer Transformer encoder is trained to reconstruct masked VQGAN tokens from the sampled keyframe sequence under caption conditioning, using a multi-granularity masking strategy (entire-frame, random-patch, in-block, out-of-block) with auxiliary cross-modal alignment and temporal consistency losses. In Stage 2 (Implicit Procedure Captioning), the same encoder is reused but the explicit intermediate frames are replaced by k·n_I learnable query embeddings, prompting the encoder to infer the latent change procedure from only the two static images. A 2-layer Transformer decoder then generates the caption autoregressively, and the entire pipeline is fine-tuned end-to-end with a standard captioning loss, eliminating the need for frame synthesis at inference.

## Results

On CLEVR-Change, ProCap achieves CIDEr 135.6, BLEU-4 56.7, METEOR 41.7, and ROUGE-L 74.7, outperforming all non-LLM baselines (best: MCT-CCDiff at CIDEr 131.7). On Spot-the-Diff, ProCap reaches CIDEr 42.7, surpassing the previous non-LLM SOTA MCT-CCDiff (41.7). On Image-Editing-Request, ProCap leads all non-LLM methods across every metric (BLEU-4 11.7, METEOR 15.9, ROUGE-L 43.2, CIDEr 40.6). Against LLM-based methods, ProCap surpasses Qwen-VL and LLaVA-1.5 on CLEVR-Change but trails FINER and LLaVA-1.5+RP on the more challenging real-world datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among non-LLM methods, ProCap achieves the best CIDEr on all three datasets: 135.6 on CLEVR-Change (vs. MCT-CCDiff 131.7, +3.9), 42.7 on Spot-the-Diff (vs. MCT-CCDiff 41.7, +1.0), and 40.6 on Image-Editing-Request (vs. SMART 37.8, +2.8). On CLEVR-Change, ProCap also leads on METEOR (41.7) and BLEU-4 (56.7) among non-LLM methods, though MCT-CCDiff edges ahead on ROUGE-L (75.6 vs. 74.7). Against LLM-based baselines, ProCap surpasses Qwen-VL (CIDEr 119.8) and LLaVA-1.5 (122.4) on CLEVR-Change but falls slightly short of FINER (137.2) on that dataset; on Spot-the-Diff and Image-Editing-Request, LLM-based methods (FINER, LLaVA-1.5+RP) retain a clear advantage, with LLaVA-1.5+RP reaching CIDEr 60.9 on IER versus ProCap's 40.6. The paper notes that when training captions are augmented with LLM-generated text (matching MCT-CCDiff's protocol), ProCap's CIDEr on Spot-the-Diff rises to 44.6, surpassing MCT-CCDiff's 41.7, and inference is 22× faster (0.04 s/caption vs. 0.91 s/caption).

## Relation to Prior Work

ProCap departs from the dominant paradigm in change captioning, where all prior methods (DUDA, NCT, VARD-Trans, SCORER+CBR, SMART, MCT-CCDiff, and LLM-based approaches like FINER) treat the task as a static spatial comparison between two images. The paper repurposes frame interpolation (VFIformer) to synthesize an explicit temporal procedure between image pairs, then applies a masked-reconstruction objective inspired by video pre-training (ViT-MAE, VideoMAE) to learn procedural dynamics. The closest prior work, Change3D (Zhu et al., 2025), implicitly models temporal dynamics in remote sensing via domain-specific change maps, but ProCap explicitly generates and models intermediate transitions without requiring such supervision, enabling generalization to unconstrained natural scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of change captioning from 'what changed' to 'how it changed' offers a principled inductive bias that helps disentangle genuine semantic changes from viewpoint/illumination distractors, as evidenced by strong gains on movement changes in CLEVR-Change. The two-stage design (explicit procedure modeling → implicit captioning via learnable queries) achieves a 22× inference speedup over the prior non-LLM SOTA (MCT-CCDiff) while matching or exceeding its CIDEr, making the approach practical for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to genuinely multi-frame video inputs (rather than synthesized procedures from pairs), whether the reliance on optical-flow-based interpolation limits applicability to large-magnitude or non-rigid changes, and whether the procedure modeling paradigm transfers to other temporal reasoning tasks beyond captioning.
