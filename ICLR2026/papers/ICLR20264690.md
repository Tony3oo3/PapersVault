## Problem

Automatic camera trajectory planning for 4D scenes remains challenging: existing methods rely on specialized models trained on limited datasets, struggle to generalize to open-world scenes, and lack support for text-based preference control. The paper asks whether the cinematographic knowledge implicitly embedded in pre-trained text-to-video models—trained on vast film footage with professional camera work—can be repurposed to generate coordinate-aligned camera poses for given 4D content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264690/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a normalized 4D human motion sequence (SMPL-X 22-joint 3D positions, f×22×3, transformed to a canonical space with gravity-aligned axes) and a free-form text prompt describing scene context and desired camera style. It outputs a per-frame camera extrinsic sequence (f×9: 3D translation plus 6D rotation from the first two rows of the rotation matrix) aligned to the human motion coordinate frame, along with a 77-frame cinematic video at 384×672 that visualizes the scene from the predicted viewpoints.

## Method

AdaViewPlanner adapts a pre-trained 1B-parameter DiT-based T2V model in two stages. Stage I injects the 4D motion condition via a spatial motion attention mechanism that concatenates video and motion tokens along the spatial dimension before self-attention, with a curriculum learning scheme that provides ground-truth camera poses with probability p to prevent training collapse; only the motion encoder and attention layers are trained while the base video model is frozen. Stage II introduces a dedicated camera extrinsic diffusion branch within an MMDiT framework with three branches (video, camera, human motion), using a flow-matching objective to denoise camera parameters conditioned on both the Stage I generated video and the 4D skeleton, thereby extracting absolute camera poses directly in the human coordinate system without post-processing alignment. 3D spatial RoPE encodes motion tokens and pose-specific RoPE differentiates motion from camera tokens, enabling the model to inherit the base model's text-following and generalization capabilities while producing coordinate-consistent trajectories.

## Results

On the E.T. test set (500 samples), AdaViewPlanner achieves a Human Missing Rate of 0.044, translation jerk of 0.007, shot diversity (Dist_t) of 2.826, Text-Camera Consistency of 1.125, and Cinematographic Style Diversity of 0.686, outperforming both baselines on every metric. On the authors' curated 240-sample test set, the method further improves to HMR 0.018, Jerk_t 0.003, TCC 1.385, and CSD 0.711. In the user study, AdaViewPlanner receives 61.90% and 63.33% preference on the two test sets, compared to 23.81%/20.83% for E.T. and 14.29%/15.83% for DanceCam*. For 4D human motion control (Table 2), the method achieves WA-MPJPE of 71.65 mm on TikTok and 103.92 mm on the general domain, matching or surpassing MTVCrafter with Wan-2.1-14B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaViewPlanner outperforms both baselines on every reported metric across both test sets. Compared to E.T. (Courant et al., 2024), which models the character as a single point trajectory, the proposed method reduces HMR from 0.064 to 0.044 (E.T. test set), increases shot diversity (Dist_t) from 0.538 to 2.826, improves TCC from 0.850 to 1.125, and more than doubles user preference from 23.81% to 61.90%. Against DanceCam* (a re-implementation of DanceCamera3D with text prompts replacing audio), the method achieves lower HMR (0.044 vs. 0.053), higher Dist_t (2.826 vs. 1.236), higher TCC (1.125 vs. 0.975), and substantially higher user preference (61.90% vs. 14.29%); DanceCam* collapses to a single trajectory with limited diversity during training. For 4D human motion control (Table 2), AdaViewPlanner matches MTVCrafter with Wan-2.1-14B on the dance domain (WA-MPJPE 71.65 vs. 73.47 mm) and significantly outperforms it on the general domain (103.92 vs. 224.50 mm), benefiting from training on broader pose distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models (built on the DiT architecture) as virtual cinematographers for 4D scene viewpoint planning, departing from prior camera planning methods (E.T., DanceCamera3D, Director) that train specialized models on limited datasets. The spatial motion attention mechanism is directly inspired by 3DTrajMaster, while the Stage II camera extraction adopts the MMDiT multi-branch framework from Esser et al. The key conceptual departure is treating the T2V model's learned cinematographic prior as an extractable resource: rather than training a camera planner from scratch, the paper injects 4D scene representations into the video generation path and then regresses camera parameters from the generated video, inheriting the model's open-world generalization and text-following ability for free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a proof-of-concept that video diffusion models can serve as implicit world models for 4D interaction, opening a path toward automated cinematography that generalizes to unseen scenes and accepts natural-language style control—capabilities that specialized camera planners lack. However, the current scope is limited to human motion (not general 4D scenes), performance is bounded by the base video model's geometric fidelity, and the two-stage pipeline cannot yet be unified due to conflicting timestep requirements. Open questions include extending to non-human 4D representations (Gaussian splatting, mesh deformation), integrating 3D geometric priors to reduce video-model artifacts, and whether the paradigm scales as base video models improve.
