## Problem

Existing fine-tuning methods for diffusion models—whether RL-based (DDPO) or direct backpropagation (DRaFT, ReFL)—suffer from reward over-optimization, producing high-reward but semantically collapsed and low-diversity samples. KL-regularized alternatives mitigate this but require unstable value function training or high-variance Monte Carlo gradient estimators, making it difficult to directly exploit available reward gradients in a stable, sample-efficient manner.

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes a pretrained Stable Diffusion model (v1.5 or SDXL) via LoRA adapters, taking a text prompt and Gaussian noise as input and producing a 512×512 (or 1024×1024 for SDXL) RGB image. During training, intermediate noisy latent states x_t are drawn from a replay buffer, denoised one step by the policy, and the resulting x_{t-1} is passed through a frozen consistency model (LCM-LoRA) to estimate the clean sample x̂_0, which is then scored by a differentiable reward model (LAION aesthetic predictor or HPSv2). The reward gradient flows through this one-step estimation to update the diffusion policy, avoiding backpropagation through the full denoising chain.

## Method

SQDF formulates diffusion fine-tuning as a KL-regularized MDP where each denoising step is an action, and derives a reparameterized policy gradient that directly uses the reward gradient as the gradient of a training-free soft Q-function approximation (Q_soft ≈ γ^{t-1} r(x̂_0(x_{t-1}))). This eliminates the need for a separate Q-network while preserving the KL constraint that keeps the policy close to the pretrained model. Three stabilizing components are added: (1) a discount factor γ < 1 that exponentially down-weights early denoising steps where the posterior mean approximation is unreliable and the signal-to-noise ratio is low; (2) a consistency model replacing Tweedie's formula for uniform-accuracy one-step x_0 estimation across all timesteps; and (3) an off-policy prioritized replay buffer that reuses high-reward, diverse samples to manage the reward-diversity trade-off and improve mode coverage. The final loss combines the negative discounted reward on the consistency-model estimate with a per-step KL divergence term, optimized via the reparameterization x_{t-1} = μ_θ(x_t, t) + σ_t ε.

## Results

In online black-box optimization with a total of 15,360 oracle queries, SQDF-Bootstrap achieves the highest target aesthetic score of 7.87 (±0.11) while simultaneously attaining the best alignment (ImageReward: 1.14, HPS: 0.27) and diversity (LPIPS-Div: 0.49, DreamSim-Div: 0.53) among all methods. In text-to-image fine-tuning on Stable Diffusion 1.5, SQDF consistently occupies the Pareto frontier across aesthetic score, HPSv2, ImageReward, LPIPS, and DreamSim metrics, outperforming gradient-based baselines (DRaFT, ReFL) that suffer sharp alignment and diversity drops at equivalent reward levels. On SDXL, SQDF reaches an aesthetic score of 7.86 with ImageReward 1.21 and DreamSim-Div 0.57, compared to DRaFT's 7.18 / 0.91 / 0.48.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SQDF decisively outperforms all four baseline families. Against DDPO (Black et al., 2023), an RL-based method that does not exploit reward gradients, SQDF reaches substantially higher aesthetic scores (7.87 vs. ~6.6 for PPO+KL) while DDPO exhibits rapid diversity collapse. Against DRaFT (Clark et al., 2023) and ReFL (Xu et al., 2023), direct backpropagation methods that achieve high aesthetic scores but suffer severe semantic and diversity collapse, SQDF maintains alignment scores (ImageReward 1.14 vs. negative values for SEIKO variants) and diversity (DreamSim-Div 0.53 vs. 0.24–0.44) at equivalent or higher reward levels. Against SEIKO (Uehara et al., 2024c), the closest KL-regularized baseline, SQDF achieves a higher target reward (7.87 vs. 7.80 for SEIKO-Bootstrap) while dramatically improving alignment (ImageReward 1.14 vs. −1.69) and diversity (DreamSim-Div 0.53 vs. 0.24), demonstrating that the reparameterized soft-Q policy gradient fundamentally avoids the reward-diversity trade-off that plagues direct backpropagation even under identical KL regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SQDF sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the KL-regularized RL formulation for diffusion fine-tuning introduced by Uehara et al. (2024a), which constrains the policy to stay close to the pretrained model; (2) the training-free soft Q-function approximation via single-step posterior mean (Tweedie's formula) from Li et al. (2024), which avoids the notoriously unstable value-network training that plagues prior KL-regularized methods (Uehara et al. 2024b); and (3) the reparameterized policy gradient from SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018), which converts the reward gradient into a low-variance training signal. The paper's departure from prior work is the specific combination of these ingredients—using the reward gradient directly as the Q-function gradient within a reparameterized update—plus three practical stabilizers (discount factor, consistency model for x̂₀ estimation, and off-policy replay buffer) that prior methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a central practical pain point in diffusion alignment: reward over-optimization that destroys sample quality and diversity. By showing that a training-free, one-step Q approximation (refined by a consistency model) is sufficient for effective policy updates, SQDF removes the need for unstable value-function training or high-variance Monte Carlo estimators, making KL-regularized diffusion fine-tuning substantially more accessible. Limitations include reliance on a differentiable reward proxy, dependence on a separately trained consistency model (LCM-LoRA), and evaluation limited to image generation with LoRA fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-differentiable or black-box-only rewards without a surrogate, to video or 3D generation, and whether more advanced one-step distillation models (e.g., SDXL-Turbo) can further improve the Q-estimation quality.
