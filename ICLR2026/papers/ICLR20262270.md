## Problem

Differentiable logic gate networks (DLGNs) offer exceptional inference efficiency but suffer from vanishing gradients, large discretization errors, and high training cost when scaled in depth. Prior work addressed these symptoms with alternative initialization schemes, but the paper identifies the root cause as the redundant softmax-over-16-Boolean-functions parametrization of each neuron, which introduces sign-symmetric cancellations in gradients and suboptimal argmax rounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262270/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Images are converted to binary tensors via thermometer encoding, where each real-valued pixel x∈[0,1] becomes a k-bit vector (x>t₁, …, x>tₖ) with evenly spaced thresholds; CIFAR-100 uses 3 thresholds per channel while ImageNet32 uses 7. At inference, the trained network is discretized into a pure logic gate circuit (each neuron rounds to a single Boolean function) that can be directly embedded in FPGA or ASIC hardware, making the output both a class prediction and a deployable hardware circuit. For the NLP task, text sequences are processed by recurrent DLGNs to produce translated text.

## Method

The paper proposes an input-wise parametrization (IWP) that replaces the softmax over 2^(2^n) Boolean functions with a unique decomposition into 2^n indicator-function basis terms, reducing per-neuron parameters from 16 to 4 for binary gates. Each gate output is computed as a weighted sum of the four input-conditioned probabilities (1-p)(1-q)ω₀₀ + (1-p)q·ω₀₁ + p(1-q)ω₁₀ + pq·ω₁₁, where the weights ωᵢⱼ are learned via a sinusoidal estimator. This eliminates the sign-symmetric redundancies that cause gradient self-cancellation and guarantees minimal discretization error under any Minkowski norm, since rounding each ωᵢⱼ independently to its nearest binary value is pointwise optimal. Paired with a residual initialization (RI) that biases all gates toward the pass-through function, the IWP organizes optimization from early to late layers, enabling stable training of networks up to 20× depth with 4× fewer parameters, 1.86× faster backward passes, and 8.5× fewer training steps.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, the IWP DLGN achieves 29.5% discretized test accuracy versus 27.7% for the original parametrization (OP), while a vanilla 2-layer CNN reaches 39.2%. On CIFAR-10, IWP attains 57.47% vs. 55.33% (OP) and 64.01% (CNN). For the WMT'14 English-to-German translation task, IWP achieves 17.38 corpus BLEU compared to 15.11 for OP. In terms of training efficiency, IWP requires 4× less VRAM, converges in 8.5× fewer training steps, and processes backward passes up to 1.86× faster than OP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original DLGN parametrization (Petersen et al., 2022), IWP wins on every dataset: +1.8 pts on CIFAR-100, +2.14 pts on CIFAR-10, +0.95 pts on Fashion-MNIST, +1.59 pts on MNIST, and +2.27 BLEU on WMT'14. Against the convolutional DLGN with residual initialization (Petersen et al., 2024), IWP achieves a more than 1.3× better test accuracy at 5-fold depth on CIFAR-100, and the gap widens with depth as the OP's discretization error compounds. Against the vanilla 2-layer CNN baseline (Krizhevsky et al., 2012), IWP wins on Fashion-MNIST (82.34% vs. 77.66%) and MNIST (94.02% vs. 92.91%) but trails on CIFAR-100 (29.5% vs. 39.2%) and CIFAR-10 (57.47% vs. 64.01%), indicating that the DLGN architecture's fixed random connection topology remains a bottleneck for higher-resolution or more complex tasks. The paper also shows that IWP is far more robust to hyperparameter choices (optimizer, GroupSum temperature, learning rate, input encoding) than OP, which degrades drastically under the same perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly reparametrizes the neuron formulation of differentiable logic gate networks (DLGNs) introduced by Petersen et al. (2022), replacing the softmax-over-16-Boolean-functions parametrization with an input-wise parametrization (IWP) that assigns one parameter per input combination rather than per function. It builds on the convolutional DLGN architecture and residual initialization (RI) from Petersen et al. (2024), and on recurrent DLGNs from Bührer et al. (2025) for NLP experiments. The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis that prior initialization-based fixes (RIs, AND-OR biases) were treating symptoms of a deeper structural problem: the symmetric overparametrization of the original formulation creates sign-symmetric gradient cancellations and suboptimal argmax rounding that no initialization can fully resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IWP makes deep DLGNs practically trainable for the first time (4x fewer parameters, 8.5x faster convergence, stable accuracy with depth), and opens the door to higher-arity gates (n>2 inputs) that were previously intractable due to the 2^(2^n) parameter explosion. However, a significant generalization gap persists: even with IWP, DLGNs achieve ~29.5% on CIFAR-100 versus ~39.2% for a simple 2-layer CNN, and the random fixed connection topology is identified as a remaining expressivity bottleneck that prevents DLGNs from exploiting higher input resolution. Closing this accuracy gap while preserving the extreme inference efficiency (nanosecond-scale on FPGAs) remains the central open problem for deploying logic gate circuits in real-world hardware.
