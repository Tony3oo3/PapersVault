## Problem

Language models frequently generate flawed reasoning steps in chain-of-thought outputs, undermining both performance and interpretability. Existing approaches to stepwise error detection either require expensive per-step human annotations, depend on external evidence retrieval, or rely on fragile in-context learning prompts. This paper asks whether stepwise veracity can be inferred automatically by treating it as a latent-variable inference problem, without any step-level supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264666/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query prompt x, a chain-of-thought z parsed into N primitive statements, and (for VS only) the correct answer y* as a proxy reward signal. It produces a binary veracity vector V_z ∈ {0,1}^N assigning True/False to each step, and optionally a corrected reasoning chain z' (e.g., by negating steps flagged False). A key distinction is that the identity of the CoT is held fixed—only the veracity labels are inferred—whereas prior LVM approaches treat the entire CoT as a latent variable entangling identity and correctness. AVI operates zero-shot without y*, enabling verification before the answer is known.

## Method

The core contribution is a latent-variable model P(V_z, Y | x, z) = P_LM(v y | x z) that factors the LM's joint likelihood over a veracity vector and the final answer, making the posterior P(V_z | x, z, y) the target of inference. Veracity Search (VS) performs approximate posterior sampling via single-bit Metropolis updates with simulated annealing over the 2^N discrete veracity space, using P_LM(v_z y* | x z) as a proxy reward, initialized by a greedy depth-first tree search that sequentially fixes each bit to maximize partial likelihood. Amortized Veracity Inference (AVI) then fine-tunes an LM (LoRA rank 8, single epoch on 5,000 VS pseudo-labels) to directly predict the veracity distribution Q(V_z | x, z) without conditioning on y*, enabling zero-shot verification. The key insight is that LMs, while often unable to generate correct CoTs, can still assign higher joint probability to veracity assignments closer to ground truth, making the joint likelihood a valid search signal.

## Results

On PRONTOQA, Veracity Search (VS) achieves mean Hamming similarity of 0.91–0.96 across four LMs (Qwen-4B/8B, Llama-3B/8B), outperforming the best in-context learning baseline by 20–25 points. On COMMONSENSEQA, VS reaches 0.84–0.94 Hamming similarity, again exceeding baselines by 20–30 points. On GSM8K, VS attains 0.61–0.75, a more modest 5–10 point improvement over the strongest baseline. The amortized version (AVI) achieves comparable zero-shot accuracy (0.89–0.97 on PRONTOQA) without requiring the true answer at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares VS against four in-context learning baselines that all bypass the intractable in-filling problem by reordering the generative trajectory to X→Y→Z→V_z: Recursive (sequential per-step prediction), Many2Many (one-shot full-vector prediction), Voting (majority vote over 50 samples at T=0.5), and CoT (Many2Many with an intermediate rationale). VS wins on every benchmark and every LM configuration. The margin is largest on PRONTOQA (e.g., 0.964 vs. 0.536 for Llama-8B, a +43-point gap) and COMMONSENSEQA (e.g., 0.935 vs. 0.695 for Qwen-4B, +24 points), where reasoning steps are Boolean assertions and flagging an error is equivalent to correcting it. On GSM8K the gap narrows to roughly 5–10 points (e.g., 0.751 vs. 0.695 for Qwen-8B), which the authors attribute to the additional difficulty of redoing flawed arithmetic beyond merely flagging it. The Recursive baseline is generally the strongest competitor on PRONTOQA, while CoT-prompting is strongest on GSM8K and COMMONSENSEQA for Qwen models, but neither closes the gap to VS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent-variable model (LVM) framework for chain-of-thought reasoning introduced by Phan et al. (NeurIPS 2023), which treats the CoT as a latent variable to be inferred given the final answer. The key departure is the introduction of a per-step veracity variable that disentangles the *identity* of a reasoning chain from its *correctness*, enabling a lower-dimensional discrete search (over binary vectors) rather than over full natural-language sequences. Algorithmically, the paper borrows from MCMC/simulated annealing for the search (Veracity Search) and from variational EM for the amortized fine-tuning stage (AVI), positioning itself as complementary to process reward models (which require step-level labels or learn value rather than correctness) and to in-context-learning verifiers (which the paper shows to be fragile baselines).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, supervision-free route to stepwise error identification that does not require step-level ground-truth labels or external evidence retrieval, and the AVI component enables zero-shot veracity inference without access to the correct answer at test time. However, most quantitative results rely on synthetically corrupted CoTs (uniform negation or numeric perturbation), and the paper acknowledges that robustness to naturally occurring, correlated errors remains under-explored. A notable open question is how to best exploit accurate veracity signals for downstream self-improvement: the paper shows that verification accuracy gains (20+ points) do not translate proportionally into reasoning-accuracy gains after correction, suggesting a bottleneck in the refinement step that future work must address.
