## Problem

MLLM-based GUI agents suffer non-linear performance degradation on long-horizon tasks (15+ steps), with success rates dropping from >90% at 5 steps to ~60% beyond 15 steps due to cumulative error propagation and loss of cross-step contextual consistency. Existing benchmarks are designed for short episodes (<15 steps), leaving long-horizon reasoning capabilities largely unevaluated and unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260003/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a GUI screenshot and a dual-level task instruction (high-level macro goal plus decomposed low-level atomic operations) at each step. The perceiver assigns unique spatial indices to all detected UI elements (icons and text) via parallel detection and OCR, producing an indexed element set that serves as the state representation. Output is a structured five-field JSON action plan (historical_status, import_contents, think, Execute_goal, action) where the action field encodes the target using a priority hierarchy of index-based, relative in-box, and absolute screen coordinates, resolved to device-specific pixel positions via a scaling matrix.

## Method

LongHorizonUI is a three-component pipeline built on Gemini-2.5-Pro without fine-tuning. The Multimodal Enhanced Perceiver (MEP) fuses an icon detector and OCR module, binding each detected control to its nearest text via an IoU threshold (τ=0.8) and inserting template-matched repairs for missed elements in high-priority regions (pop-up corners, bottom bars). The Deep Reflection Decider (DRD) enforces a structured JSON schema that mandates three sequential reasoning tiers—historical state validation, screen-content extraction, and action explainability—followed by a pre-execution gate that rejects any action whose target element is absent from the current screen or whose semantics are not entailed by the task description. The Compensating Action Executor (CAE) attempts each action through a three-stage degradation policy (element centroid → uniform in-box sampling → absolute coordinates with bounded jitter), verifies success via MLLM-based post-execution state comparison, and triggers a rollback to the last committed snapshot when all candidates fail, thereby containing error propagation across the trajectory.

## Results

On the newly introduced LongGUIBench (371 long-horizon tasks, mean 22.1 steps), LongHorizonUI achieves an average step success rate (SR) of 77.3%, surpassing the strongest GUI-agent baseline UI-TARS-1.5 (65.8%) by 11.5 points and the best base model Gemini-2.5 (67.3%) by 10.0 points. On the ScreenSpot grounding benchmark, LongHorizonUI reaches 90.4% average accuracy, outperforming InfiGUI-R1 (87.5%) and UI-TARS (82.3%). On navigation benchmarks, it gains 6.4% SR over Qwen2.5-VL-7B on AndroidControl-High and 2.3% over GUI-R1-7B on average across AndroidControl and GUI-Odyssey.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongHorizonUI decisively outperforms all GUI-agent baselines on LongGUIBench: it leads UI-TARS-1.5 by 11.5% average SR (77.3 vs. 65.8), AgentCPM-GUI by 8.7%, and InfiGUI-R1 by 15.5%, with the largest margins on high-level instructions (52.3% vs. 21.8% for UI-TARS-1.5, a 30.5-point gap). Among base MLLMs, Gemini-2.5 (67.3%) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (67.4%) are the closest, yet both trail LongHorizonUI by roughly 10 points, confirming that the framework's perception-reflection-execution pipeline adds substantial value beyond raw model capability. On ScreenSpot grounding, LongHorizonUI (90.4%) surpasses InfiGUI-R1 (87.5%) by 2.9 points and UI-TARS (82.3%) by 8.1 points, though it slightly trails InfiGUI-R1 on Mobile-Text (95.6 vs. 97.1). On navigation benchmarks (AndroidControl, GUI-Odyssey), LongHorizonUI (65.5% avg) edges out GUI-R1-7B (63.2%) by 2.3% and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (58.6%) by 6.9%, demonstrating that long-horizon robustness does not come at the cost of short-horizon navigation. In zero-shot cross-domain transfer (OSWorld 50-step, AndroidWorld), LongHorizonUI leads UI-TARS-72B by 4.8% and 1.3% respectively, with the gap widening as sequence length increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a framework-level contribution on top of off-the-shelf MLLMs (Gemini-2.5-Pro) rather than training a new GUI-specific model, departing from the dominant paradigm of fine-tuned GUI agents (UI-TARS, InfiGUI-R1, AgentCPM-GUI, OS-Atlas). Its three components—Multimodal Enhanced Perceiver, Deep Reflection Decider, and Compensatory Action Executor—repurpose well-known techniques (object detection, OCR, IoU-based element fusion, structured JSON prompting, multi-level coordinate fallback) into a closed-loop pipeline specifically targeting error accumulation over sequences exceeding 15 steps. The paper also explicitly contrasts with prior benchmarks (AndroidControl, GUI-Odyssey, AITW) that it argues are biased toward short episodes (<15 steps), motivating the new LongGUIBench as a dedicated long-horizon evaluation regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of LongGUIBench (371 scenarios, mean 22.1 steps) fills a genuine evaluation gap: no prior public benchmark systematically tests GUI agents beyond 15 steps, making it difficult to measure the non-linear degradation the authors document. The practical finding that a no-fine-tuning engineering pipeline (structured prompting + multi-level fallback + rollback) can outperform trained GUI-specific models on long-horizon tasks suggests that error-recovery mechanisms may matter more than specialized training for extended sequences. However, absolute success rates remain modest (52.3% on high-level instructions), the framework inherits MLLM inference latency (~8 s/step), and the benchmark is self-constructed, leaving open whether the gains generalize beyond the authors' specific task distributions and whether the approach can be distilled into a single efficient model.
