## Problem

Path signatures and signature kernels provide universal, expressive features for time-series learning, but explicit signature computation suffers from combinatorial explosion at high truncation levels, making it impractical for high-dimensional or long sequences. While reservoir computing offers a training-efficient alternative, principled continuous-time reservoirs for path data that come with provable kernel limits and retain the algebraic structure of signatures were largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The models consume multivariate time-series sequences (piecewise-linear, resampled to length 200) or, for R-RDE, geometric p-rough paths whose increments are summarized by log-signature coordinates in a free Lie algebra. The random reservoir produces an N-dimensional feature vector per input, on top of which a linear readout (SVM/LinearSVM) is trained to yield a class label. Notably, only the readout is trained; all reservoir dynamics (random matrices, Fourier frequencies, Lie-bracket coefficients) are fixed at initialization.

## Method

The paper proposes two random-feature reservoir architectures. RF-CDE first lifts the input path pointwise via random Fourier features into R^{2F}, then evolves the lifted signal through a random CDE driven by i.i.d. Gaussian matrices; in the joint infinite-width/infinite-Fourier-features limit, the induced kernel converges to the RBF-lifted signature kernel. R-RDE operates directly on geometric rough paths using a log-ODE discretization: each time-step increment is encoded by its log-signature in a Hall/Lyndon basis of the free Lie algebra, and the state is advanced by solving an ODE with constant Lie-bracket coefficients built from random matrices; its infinite-width limit recovers the rough signature kernel. Both models require training only a linear readout, yielding linear-in-sequence-length feature extraction and avoiding kernel Gram-matrix inversion, while their Gaussian-process interpretation in the infinite-width limit provides a unified link between random-feature reservoirs, continuous-time deep architectures, and path-signature theory.

## Results

On the 16 UEA time-series classification datasets (N=250 random features), RF-CDE achieves the highest average accuracy among random-feature models at 0.741, narrowly edging out the kernel-based SigPDE baseline (0.738). RFSF-DP and RFSF-TRP follow at 0.726 and 0.725, while R-RDE and R-CDE trail at 0.708 and 0.695. In the Hurst-exponent classification task on fractional Brownian motion, R-RDE achieves the strongest performance across all settings, reaching 0.955 accuracy (V1, N=64) and maintaining a clear margin over all baselines in the harder standardised setting (V2, N=64: 0.735 vs. 0.675 for the best competitor).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RF-CDE (0.741 avg accuracy) outperforms both RFSF variants (0.726/0.725) by roughly 1.5 percentage points and the original R-CDE (0.695) by 4.6 points on the UEA benchmark, confirming that the RBF Fourier lift before the CDE dynamics is beneficial. However, the kernel-based SigPDE baseline (0.738) achieves a slightly lower average accuracy but a better average rank (2.562 vs. 3.062), indicating it wins on more individual datasets (e.g., EigenWorms 0.794 vs. 0.630, EthanolConcentration 0.460 vs. 0.358, Handwriting 0.409 vs. 0.331). R-RDE is competitive on structure-rich datasets (UWaveGestureLibrary: 0.903, the best among all methods) and dominates the Hurst-exponent classification task, achieving 0.955 (V1) and 0.735 (V2) versus 0.895–0.920 for the best neural baselines (NCDE/NRDE). The key practical advantage of RF-CDE and R-RDE over SigPDE is linear-in-samples scaling (no Gram matrix inversion), making them more scalable for large datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the random controlled differential equation (R-CDE) framework of Cirone et al. (2023), which showed that infinite-width random CDEs converge to the signature kernel, in two directions: (i) RF-CDE inserts a random Fourier feature lift before the CDE dynamics, connecting the reservoir to the RBF-lifted signature kernel and the Random Fourier Signature Features (RFSF) of Toth et al. (2025); (ii) R-RDE replaces smooth drivers with geometric rough paths and uses the log-ODE discretization of Lyons (2014) to preserve Chen's multiplicativity, converging to the rough signature kernel. The theoretical backbone draws on the Universal Limit Theorem (Lyons, 1998) for existence/uniqueness and on trace-moment identities for random matrices (Cass & Turner, 2024) to establish the infinite-width limits. Conceptually, the paper unifies reservoir computing, continuous-time sequence models, and path-signature theory under a single random-feature framework where only a linear readout is trained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in replacing explicit signature computation (which suffers from combinatorial feature explosion) with a linear-scaling random reservoir that retains the inductive bias of signature kernels, making path-signature-based learning viable at scale without kernel-matrix inversion. The Hurst-exponent experiment provides a clean validation that rough-path structure (log-signatures, higher-order interactions) genuinely helps on irregular signals, where R-RDE outperforms all other methods by a clear margin. Open questions include whether the spectral measures defining the reservoirs can be learned or sparsified, how NTK dynamics behave around the random reservoir, and whether adaptive log-structured discretizations can handle very long contexts efficiently. The modest empirical margins on UEA (0.741 vs. 0.738 for SigPDE) suggest the efficiency gains, rather than raw accuracy, are the primary practical selling point.
