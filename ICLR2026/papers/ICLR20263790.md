## Problem

Existing promptable segmentation models and discriminatively pretrained architectures fail to generalize to object types and image styles unseen during finetuning, typically requiring massive labeled datasets covering many categories. This paper asks whether a model finetuned on only a narrow slice of the visual world (indoor furnishings and cars) can still segment entirely unseen objects like people, animals, and art, and hypothesizes that generative pretraining encodes an inherent, transferable grouping mechanism that discriminative pretraining lacks.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image and forwards it through the generative backbone without adding noise (Stable Diffusion) or masking tokens (MAE), producing an output RGB image at the same resolution where each object instance is rendered as a distinct uniform color and the background is black. For point-promptable segmentation, a user clicks a point on the image; the model computes a Gaussian-weighted query from the feature neighborhood, builds a similarity map against all pixels, applies a joint bilateral filter, and thresholds to yield a binary mask. Resolutions are 480×640 (Hypersim) and 368×1024 (Virtual Kitti 2) for Stable Diffusion, and 224×224 for MAE-based models.

## Method

The core contribution is reframing instance segmentation as image-to-image translation: a pretrained generative model (Stable Diffusion 2 U-Net or MAE encoder+decoder) is finetuned end-to-end to output a color-coded segmentation image, with no task-specific mask decoder or feature pyramid. The training signal is a novel instance coloring loss with three terms—(1) intra-instance variance loss (smooth ℓ₁) enforcing uniform color within each mask, (2) inter-instance separation loss pushing pixels outside a mask away from that mask's mean color, and (3) mean-level separation loss ensuring distinct instances receive distinct colors—so the model learns grouping without being told which color to assign. Finetuning uses only 86K synthetic images (Hypersim + Virtual Kitti 2) containing masks for indoor furnishings and cars, yet the model generalizes zero-shot to people, animals, art, x-rays, and fine structures. For Stable Diffusion, the timestep is fixed at t=999 with no noise injection and a frozen VAE, yielding a one-step deterministic segmenter; for MAE, no tokens are masked and both encoder and decoder are updated jointly.

## Results

On zero-shot point-promptable instance segmentation (single prompt at object center, mIoU), gen2seg(SD) achieves 57.6 on COCO_exc^L, 51.4 on iShape, and 48.2 on DRAM, matching or exceeding SAM (57.0, 16.8, 50.2) on large objects and fine structures. gen2seg(MAE-H), pretrained only on ImageNet-1K, reaches 50.0 on COCO_exc^L and 34.9 on iShape, still surpassing SAM on iShape. Both gen2seg models dramatically outperform SimpleClick (1.4 on COCO_exc^L) and DINO-B (35.0 on COCO_exc^L), confirming that generative pretraining is essential for zero-shot generalization. The main limitation is small-object segmentation, where SAM retains a large advantage (59.5 vs 38.8 on COCO_exc^M; 56.9 vs 8.5 on COCO_exc^S).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAM (the heavily supervised high-water mark trained on 1B+ masks), gen2seg(SD) matches SAM on COCO_exc^L (57.6 vs 57.0) and dramatically outperforms it on iShape (51.4 vs 16.8), a dataset of fine structures like wires and fences, while losing on small objects (COCO_exc^M: 38.8 vs 59.5; COCO_exc^S: 8.5 vs 56.9), EgoHOS (40.0 vs 56.4), and PIDRay (30.9 vs 44.2). Even gen2seg(MAE-H), which uses only ImageNet-1K pretraining without internet-scale data, beats SAM on iShape (34.9 vs 16.8). Against SimpleClick (a SOTA promptable segmenter with a mask decoder trained from scratch), gen2seg models win by 50+ mIoU points on every dataset (e.g., 57.6 vs 1.4 on COCO_exc^L), demonstrating that existing segmentation architectures fail to generalize beyond their training categories. Against DINO-B (discriminative self-supervised features + VAE decoder), gen2seg(SD) leads by 22.6 points on COCO_exc^L (57.6 vs 35.0) and by 24 points on iShape (51.4 vs 27.4), supporting the paper's hypothesis that generative pretraining learns equivariant representations necessary for instance grouping, whereas discriminative pretraining over-emphasizes semantic invariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes generative models (Stable Diffusion 2 and MAE encoder+decoder) for category-agnostic instance segmentation by framing it as image-to-image translation, building on the one-step deterministic diffusion inference of Garcia et al. (2024) and the MAE architecture of He et al. (2022). It departs from SAM's paradigm of finetuning a discriminative MAE backbone with a mask decoder on 1.1B masks, instead relying on the generative prior itself to encode object boundaries and groupings. The novel instance coloring loss (intra-instance variance + inter-instance separation + mean-level separation) avoids the need for fixed color assignments, drawing on clustering-based instance segmentation ideas from De Brabandere et al. (2017) and Kong & Fowlkes (2018), while the point-prompting mechanism is a lightweight alternative to learned mask decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that generative pretraining—especially even ImageNet-1K-only MAE—encodes a transferable object-grouping mechanism that discriminative pretraining (DINO) lacks, offers a new paradigm for data-efficient segmentation in domains where labeled masks are scarce (medical imaging, robotics, autonomous systems). Key limitations include weaker small-object performance (likely due to lower finetuning resolution and pretraining biases toward prominent objects) and the absence of a learned high-resolution mask decoder. Open questions include whether scaling to stronger generative models (e.g., FLUX.1) closes the small-object gap, whether the approach extends to video or 3D, and how to combine this generative-prior finetuning with pseudo-label-based methods that use diverse unlabeled data.
