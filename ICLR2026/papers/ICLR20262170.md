## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) such as DeepSeek-R1 produce long, structured chains of thought but frequently ignore user-specified output formats, constraints, and operational requirements in their final responses. While merging an instruction-tuned model (ITM) into an LRM via task vectors is a natural remedy, naive merging disrupts the LRM's thinking format (e.g., <think>... delimiters) because the two model families have fundamentally different output structures, and existing data-free or activation-guided merging methods lack an explicit mechanism to protect this format during parameter fusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three model weight sets (LRM, ITM, and their shared base) plus two small calibration sets: 150 reasoning examples (sampled uniformly from Mixture-of-Thoughts across math, code, and science) used to extract forward features at thinking special tokens, and 365 instruction examples (distilled from IFEval with LLM-as-Judge filtering) used to compute per-head attention statistics. Only the Q, K, V, O, and FFN parameters are merged, computed in FP64 for numerical stability. The output is a single merged model whose weights equal the LRM plus a scaled, null-space-projected ITM task vector, along with per-layer, per-head merging coefficients that encode instruction-attention alignment and leakage scores.

## Method

RAIN-Merging is a two-stage, fully gradient-free procedure. In Stage 1, the ITM task vector is projected onto the null space of a forward-feature operator constructed from hidden states at thinking special tokens (e.g., ) on the reasoning calibration set, guaranteeing via a second-order softmax-KL bound that the merged model's logits at thinking positions remain nearly unchanged. In Stage 2, per-head merging coefficients are derived by maximizing a quadratic surrogate of an instruction-attention score (alignment minus ρ·leakage) computed from attention outputs over instruction-relevant and instruction-irrelevant spans on the instruction calibration set; the Hessian is approximated diagonally to yield a closed-form clipped solution. The final merged model is θ_R + λ ⊕_k α_k^* Δ_I^{⊥,k}, where α_k^* are the instruction-attention-guided coefficients and Δ_I^{⊥,k} are the null-space-projected task vectors. The key theoretical insight is that although LRM and ITM task vectors are nearly orthogonal in parameter principal subspaces (cosine similarity < 0.1), this orthogonality does not imply invariance of output distributions at thinking tokens, motivating the explicit null-space constraint.

## Results

On the primary 7B setup (merging Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct into DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B), RAIN-Merging achieves an instruction-following average of 48.11 (IFEval 63.22, CELLO 19.03, InfoBench 74.53, ComplexBench 35.66) and a reasoning & general average of 55.59 (Math 68.75, GPQA 54.55, Aider 33.33, Arena-Hard-v2 65.73), outperforming all merging baselines and SFT on both axes. The method completes in 20.96 minutes versus 120.32 minutes for SFT, a ~6× speedup. Across model scales (1.5B–32B) and architectures (Qwen, Llama), RAIN-Merging yields consistent relative gains of 1.57%–8.20% on instruction following and 3.83%–8.20% on reasoning over the LRM baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAIN-Merging outperforms all nine baselines on both instruction-following and reasoning & general averages. Against the strongest data-free method (Karcher, IF 46.83 / R&G 52.58), it gains +1.28 on IF and +3.01 on R&G; against the strongest data-dependent method (AIM-TIES, IF 47.02 / R&G 53.10), it gains +1.09 on IF and +2.49 on R&G. Compared to SFT (IF 45.08 / R&G 49.51), RAIN-Merging improves IF by +3.03 and R&G by +6.08 while running ~6× faster (20.96 vs. 120.32 min). The merged model still trails the standalone ITM on instruction following (48.11 vs. 52.92), indicating residual room for improvement, but it substantially surpasses the LRM on IF (48.11 vs. 44.12) while simultaneously improving reasoning (55.59 vs. 51.03), a combination no baseline achieves. On the joint MathIF benchmark, RAIN-Merging lifts the 'Both Acc.' metric from 12.62% (LRM) to 20.48% (+62.26% relative), outperforming both the LRM and the ITM (19.76%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task-vector merging paradigm (Ilharco et al., 2023) by introducing a two-stage, gradient-free pipeline that explicitly accounts for the structural mismatch between LRMs (with  thinking segments) and ITMs (answer-only outputs). Stage 1 adapts the null-space projection idea from continual learning (Wang et al., 2021) to constrain parameter perturbations at thinking-token positions, while Stage 2 introduces instruction-attention-guided per-head scaling coefficients derived from a quadratic approximation of an alignment-minus-leakage objective. The paper departs from prior activation-based merging methods (ACM, LEWIS, AIM) by grounding the merging constraint in the output-distribution invariance of the thinking format rather than generic forward-activation statistics, and provides a theoretical argument (Prop. 1) showing that parameter-space orthogonality between LRM and ITM task vectors is insufficient to guarantee output invariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAIN-Merging offers a practical, training-free alternative to SFT for closing the instruction-following gap in LRMs, requiring only ~500 calibration samples and ~21 minutes of compute versus ~120 minutes for SFT, while simultaneously preserving (and in some cases improving) reasoning quality. The finding that enhanced instruction adherence tightens reasoning-answer alignment (RAA +13% on AIME25/GPQA) suggests a previously underappreciated coupling between format compliance and internal reasoning coherence. Open questions include whether the gains plateau or reverse at larger scales (32B shows the smallest relative improvement), whether the instruction-attention proxy generalizes to non-English or multimodal settings, and whether the method extends to other structured-output paradigms beyond the  format.
