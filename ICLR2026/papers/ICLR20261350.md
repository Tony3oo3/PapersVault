## Problem

Frontier video diffusion transformers (DiTs) require massive compute and memory, confining high-quality text-to-video generation to cloud infrastructure with associated latency, privacy, and cost barriers. While UNet-based mobile video models exist, DiT-based VDMs for on-device deployment remain largely unexplored due to the enormous memory and compute cost of the MMDiT denoiser, the oversized T5-XXL text encoder, and the 480-step iterative denoising process. This paper asks whether a full DiT-based video generation pipeline can be made to run on a low-power mobile NPU without sacrificing generation quality.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 49-frame video at 640×1024 resolution (internally generated at 320×512 then 2× super-resolved by QuickSRNet) in approximately 6.7 seconds on a Qualcomm Hexagon NPU with ~3.5 GB peak memory. The first frame is generated by a separate SSD-1B text-to-image model in 4 steps, which is then VAE-encoded and fed as the initial condition for the autoregressive 1-1-1 step-distilled MMDiT pipeline that generates the remaining 48 frames. The entire pipeline operates in fixed-point on the NPU, requiring careful compilation of multi-resolution MMDiT graphs with static input paddings for the causal autoregressive history.

## Method

Neodragon applies four systematic optimizations to the Pyramidal-Flow MMDiT pipeline. (1) Text-Encoder Distillation compresses T5-XXL (4.76B) to DT5 (0.13B) via a 4-layer ContextAdapter MLP trained with MSE + Cosine Distance loss on ~1.4M text prompts alone (no image/video data), achieving a 35× parameter reduction with only 0.67% VBench drop. (2) Asymmetric Decoder Distillation freezes the original encoder while replacing the 226M-param native decoder with a 10M-param TinyAEHV decoder, fine-tuned end-to-end with MSE + LPIPS reconstruction loss on ~350K videos to preserve the generative latent space. (3) MMDiT Block Pruning removes 6 of 24 blocks (25% reduction) selected via cosine-distance importance scores on visual and textual tokens, followed by a two-stage curriculum: brief data-based flow-matching fine-tuning (300 iterations) then full-teacher feature-matching distillation (60K iterations). (4) Pyramidal DMD extends Distribution Matching Distillation to the multi-resolution flow-matching objective, reducing NFEs from 480 to 21 (1-1-1 schedule) by training a student to predict clean latents in one step per stage while a fake-score-model is trained on student-predicted distributions. The E2E system chains SSD-1B first-frame generation, the 1-1-1 step-distilled block-pruned MMDiT, the TinyAEHV decoder, and QuickSRNet upsampling.

## Results

Neodragon E2E achieves a VBench total score of 81.61, setting a new state-of-the-art among on-device video generation systems, with a semantic score of 73.36 and object consistency of 56.56. The full pipeline generates 49 frames at 640×1024 resolution in approximately 6.7 seconds on the Qualcomm Hexagon NPU with only ~3.5 GB peak memory. The system reduces NFEs from 480 to 21 (>95% reduction) via Pyramidal DMD step distillation while maintaining or exceeding the base model's VBench score. The block-pruned 18-block MMDiT (1.518B params) recovers to 80.21 VBench after two-stage distillation, only 0.1 below the full 24-block model (80.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neodragon E2E (81.61) outperforms all on-device baselines in VBench total: Pyramidal-Flow‡ (80.31, +1.30), SnapGenV (81.14, +0.47), Hummingbird 16frame (81.35, +0.26), and Snap Mobile Video DiT (81.45, +0.16). Its semantic score of 73.36 is the highest among on-device methods, exceeding Snap Mobile Video DiT (74.76 is actually higher here—correction: Snap Mobile Video DiT leads on Sem at 74.76 vs. Neodragon's 73.36, but Neodragon wins on total). Neodragon also achieves the best object consistency (92.37) and ties Hummingbird 16frame on scene consistency (28.09). Against server-side models, Neodragon's 81.61 total is competitive with CogVideoX 2B (81.55) and Pyramidal-Flow server (81.72), though it trails Wan2.1 1.3B (83.31) and CogVideoX1.5 5B (82.01) by 1.7 and 0.4 points respectively. The key differentiator is that Neodragon achieves these scores on a mobile NPU in 6.7 seconds with 3.5 GB peak memory, whereas server baselines require GPU clusters and minutes of inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper takes Pyramidal-Flow (Jin et al., 2025) as its architectural foundation and systematically applies four compression/distillation techniques to make it deployable on a Qualcomm Hexagon NPU: text-encoder distillation building on DistilT5 (Wang et al., 2025), an asymmetric decoder distillation that borrows decoders from other video models (TinyAEHV, Wan, Cosmos, LTX-Video), MMDiT block pruning inspired by SANA-1.5 (Xie et al., 2025), and a pyramidal adaptation of DMD (Yin et al., 2024). It positions itself as orthogonal to concurrent mobile video generation efforts (SnapGen-V, Hummingbird, MoViE, On-device Sora, Wu et al. 2025a), which predominantly target UNet-based architectures, whereas Neodragon is the first to demonstrate a full DiT-based video pipeline on mobile hardware. The work is fundamentally a systems integration paper: each individual technique (distillation, pruning, step reduction) has prior art, but their specific adaptations to the pyramidal MMDiT architecture and their stacking into a coherent 6.7-second on-device pipeline are the contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Neodragon demonstrates that DiT-based video generation—previously considered infeasible on mobile due to memory and compute constraints—can run at interactive speeds (~6.7s for 49 frames at 640×1024) with competitive quality (VBench 81.61, exceeding the unmodified server baseline of 80.31). The finding that video latent spaces across different models are sufficiently transferable to enable asymmetric decoder distillation with minimal fine-tuning is a useful empirical result that could accelerate future mobile VAE design. Key limitations include the reliance on a separate text-to-image model (SSD-1B) for the first frame to mitigate semantic artifacts, the 2× super-resolution step (native generation is 320×512), and the ~3.5 GB peak memory requirement. Open questions the authors identify include scaling to higher resolutions and longer durations on-device, and extending the framework to video-to-video editing applications.
