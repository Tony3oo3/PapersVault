## Problem

Existing human animation methods assume a single identity and inject all conditions (text, image, audio) globally across the entire video, making them unable to handle multi-person scenes where each individual requires distinct appearance and voice. Multi-concept video customization methods support multiple reference images but still apply audio and other local conditions in a video-level, identity-agnostic manner, causing audio-visual misalignment in human-centric generation. The paper addresses the need for explicit, region-specific binding of local modalities (especially audio) to each identity's spatiotemporal footprint.

## Input / Output

The method takes N reference images (head, full-body, objects, or scenes), a text prompt, and per-identity audio tracks as input, and produces a 720p, 109-frame (28 VAE latent frames) multi-concept human animation video with per-speaker lip synchronization. Notably, it operates without requiring a start frame, supports 2–5 simultaneous speakers, and uses Qwen2.5-VL to automatically rephrase and enrich the text prompt from each reference image. Audio features are extracted via wav2vec 2.0 and injected only within predicted mask regions, with muted audio applied to non-speaking identities.

## Method

InterActHuman builds on a pretrained MMDiT-based DiT backbone (Seaweed-7B) with a 3D VAE and flow-matching objective, adding a lightweight mask-predictor head (~56M params) to each DiT block that performs cross-attention between video and reference features to predict per-identity spatiotemporal masks. The key insight is an iterative mask-caching strategy that resolves the chicken-and-egg problem: masks predicted at denoising step k are cached and used to gate local audio cross-attention at step k+1, progressively sharpening spatial alignment without ground-truth video. Reference images are injected via self-attention (reusing DiT parameters, no extra networks), while audio is injected via a separate cross-attention layer that blends meaningful and muted wav2vec features per token using mask confidence with soft boundary weighting. Training combines flow-matching diffusion loss with focal loss for mask classification, and the authors curate 2.6M video-entity triplets with per-frame Grounding-SAM2 masks to supervise the mask predictor.

## Results

On the multi-person audio-driven test set, InterActHuman achieves the best lip-sync accuracy (Sync-D 6.670) and the lowest FVD (22.881), outperforming OmniHuman without masks (Sync-D 9.482, FVD 33.895) and Kling 1.6 + lip-sync (Sync-D 8.401, FVD 33.555). On the multi-concept customization benchmark, the method attains the highest CLIP-I (0.744), DINO-I (0.533), and all three face-fidelity metrics, while also leading in aesthetic score (AES 3.565) and image quality (IQA 4.903). In user studies, InterActHuman achieves 59.9% top-1 preference for audio-driven generation and 49.4% for multi-concept customization, substantially ahead of all commercial and research baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OmniHuman (the closest single-identity audio-driven baseline), InterActHuman reduces Sync-D from 9.482 to 6.670 and FVD from 33.895 to 22.881 in the multi-person setting, while OmniHuman with oracle fixed masks still degrades FVD to 40.239, showing that static spatial priors are insufficient. Compared to Kling 1.6 with post-hoc lip-sync, the method improves Sync-D by 20% (8.401→6.670) and FVD by 32% (33.555→22.881), and in user studies achieves 59.9% top-1 preference versus Kling's 14.5%. MultiTalk, a concurrent multi-person talking system, is outperformed on Sync-D (7.671→6.670) and FVD (35.472→22.881). On the multi-concept customization track (Table 3), InterActHuman leads all baselines (Vidu 2.0, Pika 2.1, Kling 1.6, Phantom) on every identity-fidelity metric (CLIP-I 0.744 vs. 0.659–0.703; DINO-I 0.533 vs. 0.420–0.476) and on visual quality (AES 3.565, IQA 4.903), though it trails Pika 2.1 slightly on prompt following (ViCLIP-T 18.87 vs. 19.39). The method also achieves the best hand-keypoint variance (HKV 59.635 vs. 47.561 for OmniHuman), indicating richer motion diversity, while remaining competitive on single-person lip-sync (Sync-C 7.272 vs. 7.443 for OmniHuman).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: audio-driven single-identity human animation (OmniHuman, CyberHost) and multi-concept video customization (Video-Alchemist, ConceptMaster, Phantom). It builds directly on OmniHuman's audio-conditioned DiT backbone and the MMDiT/flow-matching architecture, but departs from both families by introducing an explicit mask-prediction head that spatially binds local audio conditions to per-identity regions during iterative denoising. The key conceptual departure is rejecting the single-entity assumption that pervades human animation and the video-level (global) condition injection that characterizes multi-concept customization, replacing both with region-specific, layout-aligned conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly support multi-reference-image customization, per-identity audio-driven lip sync, and explicit spatial layout control in a single end-to-end model, filling a gap that neither human animation nor multi-concept customization methods address alone. The iterative mask-prediction strategy (using step k's mask to guide step k+1's audio injection) is a clean, generalizable solution to the chicken-and-egg problem of spatial conditioning during diffusion inference. Limitations include a narrower training domain (predominantly 2–3 people, talking/singing) that constrains prompt diversity, and the reliance on a 7B DiT backbone that limits deployment flexibility. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully to more than 5 entities, whether the mask predictor can be distilled for real-time use, and how well it generalizes to non-human multi-concept interactions (e.g., multiple animals, complex object scenes).
