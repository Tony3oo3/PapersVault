## Problem

Existing persona-following strategies for role-playing language agents—static prompt engineering or resource-intensive fine-tuning—treat all persona attributes with fixed importance regardless of the scenario, contradicting psychological evidence (e.g., CAPS) that persona influence is context-dependent. This static treatment prevents agents from dynamically adapting which character traits to emphasize in different conversational contexts, limiting realism in social simulations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full prompt T = {C, P, x} comprising scenario context (multi-turn dialogue history), a structured character persona profile decomposed into n discrete attributes (e.g., personality traits, skills, life views), and a user query. It produces a persona-following text response y generated token-by-token via reward-modulated decoding, alongside per-attribute importance scores I_i computed from inference-time log-probability ratios. In practice, PIA aligns only the top-2 highest-importance attributes per step to balance fidelity and computational cost, and the entire pipeline is training-free, requiring only forward passes on a single L40S GPU.

## Method

PDD comprises two inference-time modules. The Persona Importance Estimation (PIE) module quantifies each attribute's contextual relevance via Conditional Mutual Information, approximated as I_i = log(Pr(G|T)/Pr(G|T_i)) where G is the model's own generated output used as a self-supervised proxy for ground-truth (Proposition 3.2 provides a probabilistic ranking-consistency guarantee under a positive-correlation assumption). The Persona-Guided Inference-Time Alignment (PIA) module then constructs a step-wise KL-based reward r_i for each attribute, weights them by I_i into a multi-objective reward, and normalizes via R_norm = ΣI_i·r_i / ||r||₂ to preserve the importance hierarchy (enforced by Cauchy-Schwarz). The optimal aligned policy p_r(y_t|T,y_<t) = (1/Z)·π_θ(y_t|T,y_<t)·exp(R_norm/β) is derived in closed form from a KL-constrained RL objective, enabling token-level probability modulation at decoding time without any parameter updates.

## Results

On CharacterEval, PDD achieves the highest average CharacterRM score of 2.85 (Qwen2.5-7B) and 2.81 (LLaMA-3-8B), outperforming all prompting and decoding baselines and approaching GPT-4o's 2.87. On PERSONALITYBENCH, PDD attains the best average Big-Five trait score of 4.57±0.22 (Qwen) and 4.57±0.29 (LLaMA) with the lowest variance among all methods. In pairwise LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations, PDD wins 63–65% of comparisons against ICL and 51–64% against Simple Prompting across both CharacterEval and BeyondDialogue.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OPAD (Zhu et al., ICLR 2025), PDD wins 52.8%/48.2% (Qwen/LLaMA) on CharacterEval and 49.0%/47.6% on BeyondDialogue, with a clear advantage in Persona-Utterance alignment (3.01 vs 2.31 on Qwen). Against PAS (Zhu et al., ICLR 2025) and NPTI (Deng et al., ICLR 2025) on PERSONALITYBENCH, PDD achieves the highest average trait score (4.57) with substantially lower variance (±0.22/±0.29) compared to PAS (4.49±0.39) and NPTI (4.48±0.35) on Qwen. Against GPT-4o (Achiam et al., 2023), PDD on Qwen2.5-7B is nearly competitive (2.85 vs 2.87 average CharacterRM), while clearly surpassing DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) at 2.85 vs 2.43. PDD also outperforms standard prompting baselines (SP, PP, ICL) by 10–32 percentage points in win rate, with the largest margin against ICL (63–65% win rate), confirming that dynamic importance estimation provides a meaningful advantage over static prompt-based persona injection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoding-time alignment paradigm (OPAD, Zhu et al. 2025a; multi-objective alignment, Shi et al. 2024) from single-objective preference following to multi-attribute persona alignment, introducing a dynamic importance weighting mechanism grounded in conditional mutual information. It departs from prior role-playing approaches (prompting, SFT/LoRA fine-tuning, neuron activation manipulation) by operating entirely at inference time without parameter updates, and from prior decoding-time methods by treating multiple persona attributes as a weighted multi-objective problem rather than a single scalar reward. The theoretical link to the Cognitive-Affective Personality Systems (CAPS) framework from psychology provides a principled motivation for why persona importance must be context-dependent, a distinction absent from prior static-weight approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PDD offers a practical, training-free recipe for context-adaptive persona following that could be plugged into existing RPLA pipelines without retraining, which is valuable for social simulation scenarios where diverse personas make dataset curation infeasible. However, the theoretical guarantee (Proposition 3.2) relies on a positive-correlation assumption between model and ground-truth probabilities that yields only moderate confidence bounds (~0.43–0.83), and the method requires n+1 forward passes per token (one per persona attribute), raising scalability concerns for profiles with many attributes. Open questions include whether the CMI-based importance estimation remains reliable in long multi-turn dialogues, whether the approach transfers to multimodal role-playing, and whether the moderate empirical gains over strong prompting baselines (e.g., ICL) justify the additional computational overhead in production settings.
