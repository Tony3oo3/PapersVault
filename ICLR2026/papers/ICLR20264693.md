## Problem

Non-autoregressive (NAR) language models generate all tokens simultaneously, avoiding the sequential bottleneck of autoregressive decoding, but accurately modeling inter-token dependencies in discrete sequences remains difficult. Prior discrete flow matching methods (DFM, Dirichlet FM, Fisher-Flow) interpolate token distributions along different simplex geodesics, yet naive linear interpolation in probability space collapses the KL divergence to near zero too early in the transport, depriving the model of informative gradients. Additionally, standard deterministic ODE-based inference under these frameworks yields suboptimal generation quality, leaving a gap between theoretical guarantees and practical performance.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model starts from a uniform Dirichlet(1) sample on the (V−1)-simplex and produces a full text sequence (up to 512 or 1024 tokens). For conditional tasks, a text prompt (instruction or source-language sentence) is concatenated with the target and truncated to 512 tokens; the model generates the response. For code infilling, the input is a Python file with a random 10–90% of lines masked plus a natural-language task description, and the model reconstructs each masked span up to 40 tokens. All tokens are represented as β-smoothed one-hot vectors (β=0.01) on the simplex, and the architecture uses bidirectional (non-causal) attention with DiT-style continuous time embeddings.

## Method

The core contribution is using KL-divergence geodesics—equivalent to linear interpolation in logit space, l_t = (1−t)·log x_0 + t·log x_1—as the transport path for conditional flow matching over discrete sequences. The denoiser is a bidirectional Transformer trained to maximize the sequence-level conditional likelihood p_θ(x_1|x_t); the paper proves that the likelihood maximizer exactly recovers the flow matching velocity field in logit space, extending prior single-token guarantees to full sequences via token-wise factorization. For inference, three schemes are proposed: (1) basic deterministic ODE integration of the learned vector field, (2) a stochastic iterative sampler that at each step samples x_1 from the factorized conditional p_θ(x_1|x_t) and re-noises it to x_{t+h} along the KL-geodesic, and (3) a hybrid that runs the ODE from t=0 to a threshold t*=0.28 (where the KL-geodesic term dominates and the token-wise factorization becomes accurate) before switching to the stochastic sampler. The hybrid scheme achieves the best perplexity–diversity trade-off across unconditional, conditional, and code-infilling tasks.

## Results

On FineFineWeb (10B tokens, 1.5B params, 1024 NFE), KL-Flow achieves 32.7 perplexity under Llama-2 scoring, outperforming SEDD (47.6), DFM (75.0), and GPT-2 (97.2). On WMT14 De-En translation, KL-Flow (sampling) reaches 27.0 BLEU (top-5) and 18.1 BLEU (avg), surpassing GPT-2 (19.7/9.8), DFM (21.3/11.2), and SEDD (14.6/6.5). For code infilling at 10% masking on MBPP, KL-Flow achieves 17.4 Pass@1 and 29.2 Pass@10, compared to GPT-2 (8.8/20.1), DFM (11.1/25.5), and SEDD (9.2/22.1). On the simpler TinyStories benchmark, autoregressive GPT-2 still leads on grammar (5.3 vs. 4.4) and perplexity (15.4 vs. 19.0), but KL-Flow outperforms both NAR baselines (DFM, SEDD) on all quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three baselines (GPT-2, DFM, SEDD) are trained from scratch on identical data subsets to ensure fair comparison. KL-Flow decisively outperforms DFM across every task: on FineFineWeb at 1024 NFE, KL-Flow (1.5B) achieves 32.7 vs. 75.0 Llama-2 perplexity (a 56% reduction); on WMT14 De-En, KL-Flow (sampling) reaches 27.0 vs. 21.3 BLEU; and on code infilling at 10% masking, 17.4 vs. 11.1 Pass@1. Against SEDD, KL-Flow also wins consistently: 32.7 vs. 47.6 perplexity on FineFineWeb, 27.0 vs. 14.6 BLEU on WMT14, and 17.4 vs. 9.2 Pass@1 on code. The autoregressive GPT-2 baseline is the strongest competitor on the simpler TinyStories dataset (15.4 vs. 19.0 perplexity), but on the more challenging FineFineWeb corpus KL-Flow (1.5B) surpasses it by a wide margin (32.7 vs. 97.2 Llama-2 perplexity at 1024 NFE). Notably, the hybrid inference variant excels on high-entropy tasks (Lamini Instruction) while the pure sampling variant dominates on lower-entropy deterministic tasks (WMT14 translation), and both KL-Flow variants beat all baselines on code infilling across all masking ratios (10%, 50%, 90%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the discrete flow matching lineage initiated by DFM (Gat et al., 2024) and Dirichlet Flow Matching (Stärk et al., 2024), which represent tokens as points on a probability simplex and learn a transport from a uniform prior to the data distribution. The key departure is the choice of interpolation geometry: rather than linear interpolation in probability space (DFM) or Fisher-Rao geodesics (Fisher-Flow, Davis et al., 2024), the authors use KL-divergence geodesics, which are equivalent to linear interpolation in logit space. They further extend the theoretical analysis beyond the single-token setting previously established, proving that conditional likelihood maximization exactly recovers the flow-matching velocity field for sequences, and introduce a sampling-hybrid inference scheme that has no direct analogue in prior discrete flow matching work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic perplexity gap between KL-geodesic and Linear/Fisher-Rao interpolations (e.g., 41 vs. 1344 on Llama-2 scoring for 150M models) suggests that the geometric choice of path is far more consequential than previously appreciated in discrete flow matching, and that logit-space linear paths are the natural geometry for token-level transport. The sampling-hybrid inference, despite lacking a full theoretical guarantee, consistently outperforms the deterministic ODE integrator, pointing to an open question about the role of stochasticity in discrete generative transport. Limitations include the relatively small model scale (≤1.5B parameters), the approximate token-wise factorization in the sampling kernel, and the fact that absolute quality still lags substantially behind autoregressive baselines, leaving open whether the approach scales favorably to frontier model sizes.
