## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for optimal transport either rely on adversarial min-max training with multiple networks (dual formulations) or require iterative ODE/SDE integration during both training and inference (dynamical models), making them computationally expensive and unstable. The Hamilton-Jacobi equation provides a principled characterization of the OT map, but its viscosity solution is non-smooth and ill-posed, and prior PINN-based approaches lack guarantees of recovering the correct solution. This paper asks whether the HJ formulation can be exploited to yield a single-network, integration-free, pure-minimization OT solver with provable optimality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes finite samples from a source distribution μ and a target distribution ν in R^d (d ranging from 2 to 64 in experiments), optionally with class labels for conditional transport. It outputs both the forward map T_μ^ν and the backward map T_ν^μ in closed form as x + t_f ∇h(∇u_θ(x,0)) and y − t_f ∇h(∇u_θ(y,t_f)) respectively, requiring only a single gradient evaluation at inference rather than iterative ODE stepping. For image tasks (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CycleGAN color transfer), transport is performed in β-VAE latent space, and the VAE decoder reconstructs pixel-space outputs.

## Method

NCF represents the HJ viscosity solution u(x,t) with a single implicit neural network u_θ(x,t) and exploits the method of characteristics to obtain closed-form, bidirectional transport maps as straight-line trajectories, eliminating all ODE integration. Training is a pure minimization of two losses: (1) an implicit HJ loss that enforces the implicit solution formula u(x,t) = −th(∇u) + t∇u^⊤∇h(∇u) + g(x − t∇h(∇u)) via Monte Carlo sampling over the spatial domain, and (2) an MMD loss using the negative-distance kernel (equivalent to squared energy distance) that aligns the pushforward of μ under the learned map with ν. The forward map is evaluated at t=0 and the backward map at t=t_f from the same network, so a single architecture yields both directions without separate training. Theoretical analysis proves that the loss minimizer exactly recovers the OT map (consistency) and that small loss implies small transport-map error in the Gaussian setting (stability), with the HJ loss uniquely selecting the OT map among multiple MMD-minimizing candidates.

## Results

On the Gaussian OT benchmark (Table 2), NCF achieves UVP of 0.010 at d=2 and 0.858 at d=64, outperforming HJ-PINN (1.683 at d=64), MM-v1 (0.415 at d=64), and MM:R (0.604 at d=64) in most dimensions, with NCF-Adaptive (enlarged network) reaching 0.407 at d=64. On class-conditional Fashion MNIST→MNIST transport (Table 4), NCF achieves 83.42% class accuracy, the highest among all methods, though its FID of 18.27 is higher than GNOT's 5.26 due to VAE decoder effects (latent-space FID is 2.73). On color transfer (Table 3), NCF achieves the best EMD (0.0003–0.0005) and HI (0.89–0.92) across all three CycleGAN domains, outperforming MM-v1, NOT, and classical methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCF outperforms all baselines at low dimensions (d≤16) on the Gaussian benchmark, achieving the lowest UVP at d=2 (0.010 vs. MM:R's 0.012) and d=4 (0.021 vs. MM:R's 0.048). At higher dimensions (d=32, 64), MM-v1 and MM:R remain competitive (0.415 and 0.604 at d=64), but NCF-Adaptive with a slightly enlarged network recovers to 0.407, matching or beating both while using fewer parameters. NCF decisively outperforms HJ-PINN (the ablation replacing the implicit loss with a PINN residual), confirming the importance of the implicit solution formula: at d=64, NCF achieves 0.858 vs. HJ-PINN's 1.683. On color transfer, NCF dominates all baselines in both EMD and HI across all three domains (e.g., EMD 0.0003 vs. MM-v1's 0.0007 on Gogh-Photograph). On Fashion MNIST→MNIST class-conditional transport, NCF achieves the highest accuracy (83.42%) but a higher FID (18.27) than GNOT (5.26), which the authors attribute to VAE decoder artifacts; the latent-space FID of 2.73 confirms faithful transport. NOT and WGAN-QC are consistently the weakest baselines across all tasks, with UVP exceeding 100 at d=64.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the implicit solution formula for Hamilton-Jacobi equations developed by Park & Osher (2025), repurposing it as a training loss for optimal transport. It departs from the dominant neural OT paradigms in two key ways: it eliminates adversarial min-max optimization (as in WGAN-QC, MM-v1, MM:R) by using a pure minimization framework, and it avoids ODE/SDE integration (as in OT-Flow, TrajectoryNet) by exploiting the method of characteristics to obtain closed-form bidirectional transport maps. The HJ-PINN ablation further distinguishes the implicit solution formula loss from standard physics-informed residual penalties, showing the former reliably recovers the viscosity solution while the latter does not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistency theorem (zero loss implies exact OT map) and the Gaussian stability bound provide theoretical guarantees that most neural OT methods lack, making NCF a principled alternative to heuristic adversarial or ODE-based solvers. The single-network, integration-free design offers practical efficiency gains in training stability and inference speed. However, the stability analysis is restricted to the quadratic-cost Gaussian setting, high-dimensional performance degrades without network enlargement, and the MNIST FID is inflated by the VAE bottleneck—open questions include extending the theory to general neural architectures and non-quadratic costs, and whether the framework scales to high-dimensional image spaces without latent-space compression.
