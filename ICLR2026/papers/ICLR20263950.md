## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods for LLM factuality guarantees (e.g., SCP, CondCP) rely on exchangeability between calibration and test data, an assumption that is routinely violated in real-world settings where user prompts exhibit continuous covariate shifts (topic drift, changing user composition). The paper asks: given a stream of prompts from an unknown, dynamically evolving distribution, how can we provide factuality guarantees for LLM outputs without access to ground-truth labels on test samples?

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the method receives a single test sample consisting of a user prompt P_{n+t}, an LLM-generated response R_{n+t} parsed into atomic claims C_{n+t}, and a factuality score p(C_{n+t,j}, P_{n+t}) for each claim. It outputs a filtered claim set F̂_t(C_{n+t}) that retains only claims whose factuality score meets an adaptively computed threshold τ̂_t. Crucially, unlike standard conformal methods, the threshold is recomputed at every step using online density-ratio reweighing of a fixed calibration set, and no ground-truth factuality labels for test claims are required during the online phase.

## Method

CoFact integrates online density ratio estimation (DRE) into conformal factuality control to bypass the exchangeability assumption. It maintains an ensemble of experts with geometrically increasing lifetimes (2⁰, 2¹, 2², …), each modeling the density ratio r_t(Z) = D_t(Z)/D_0(Z) as a logistic-regression function exp(−φ(z)ᵀθ) trained via Bregman-divergence minimization. At each step, active experts are updated with an online Newton step (ONS) using the newly arrived test sample, and their parameters are aggregated into a global model weighted by historical performance (potential × step-size). The resulting estimated density ratio r̂_t is normalized into importance weights ŵ_t that reweigh the fixed calibration conformity scores when computing the (1−α)-quantile threshold τ̂_t, which then filters hallucinated claims. Theoretically, the gap between the average hallucination rate and target α is bounded by Õ(max{T^{−2/3}V_T^{2/3}, T^{−1/2}} + n^{−1/2}), where V_T measures total variation of input densities, and the bound vanishes as T and n grow.

## Results

On MedLFQA under four simulated covariate shifts (T=2000), CoFact achieves factuality of 0.894–0.900 with 0.714–0.718 claims retained, closely matching the target 0.9 factuality level. On WikiData, CoFact achieves 0.895–0.897 factuality with 0.748–0.749 claims retained. In contrast, SCP drops to 0.808–0.828 factuality on MedLFQA and 0.875–0.884 on WikiData, failing to meet the 0.9 target, while CondCP achieves high factuality on MedLFQA (0.937–0.949) but retains only 36–40% of claims, and collapses to 0.716–0.726 factuality on WikiData.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoFact consistently outperforms SCP (Mohri & Hashimoto, 2024) on factuality across all shift types and both datasets: on MedLFQA, CoFact reaches ~0.895–0.900 vs. SCP's 0.808–0.828, and on WikiData, 0.895–0.897 vs. 0.875–0.884, while retaining more claims than SCP on MedLFQA (0.71 vs. 0.91 is actually SCP retaining more, but SCP's factuality is well below target). Against CondCP (Cherian et al., 2024), CoFact trades a small factuality gap on MedLFQA (0.895 vs. 0.940) for a dramatically higher claims retention rate (0.715 vs. 0.389), and decisively wins on WikiData where CondCP's factuality collapses to 0.72 while CoFact holds at 0.895. Against the Tibshirani et al. (2019) oracle baselines (Total and Partial), CoFact achieves superior or comparable factuality across nearly all shift types, including the fixed-shift setting, despite not requiring access to future test samples. Against ACI (Gibbs & Candes, 2021), CoFact achieves comparable factuality control without requiring ground-truth labels after each prediction; ACI's performance is highly sensitive to the step-size γ, with γ=0.005 (the paper's recommended value) producing overly conservative filtering (only 20% claims retained on MedLFQA). Against rACI (Xi et al., 2025) under noisy labels, CoFact maintains substantially higher claims retention (0.71 vs. 0.25–0.36 on MedLFQA) while achieving comparable factuality, and does not require assumptions about noise structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoFact sits at the intersection of two research threads: conformal factuality control for LLMs (Mohri & Hashimoto, 2024; Cherian et al., 2024) and online density ratio estimation under continuous covariate shift (Zhang et al., 2023). The key departure from prior conformal factuality methods is the removal of the exchangeability assumption: rather than computing a single static threshold from calibration data, CoFact dynamically reweighs calibration samples using an online ensemble of density ratio estimators that tracks the evolving test distribution. This is distinct from both the static covariate-shift conformal prediction of Tibshirani et al. (2019), which requires the full test set in advance, and online conformal inference (ACI, Gibbs & Candès 2021), which assumes immediate access to ground-truth labels after each prediction—neither of which is feasible in the LLM hallucination-mitigation setting where correctness feedback is absent or delayed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: LLMs deployed in production face continuously shifting user queries (topic drift, changing user populations), and existing conformal factuality guarantees silently break under this shift. The theoretical bound (Theorem 1) showing the hallucination-rate gap converges to zero as T and n grow, with a rate depending on the total variation of input densities, provides a principled foundation. Open questions include: (1) the method still depends on a factuality score function p(C, P) whose quality bounds the achievable guarantee; (2) Assumption 1 (invariant conditional W|Z) may be violated if the LLM's hallucination behavior itself changes over time (concept drift); (3) the ~150-step burn-in observed on WildChat+ limits applicability in very short deployment windows. The WildChat+ dataset is a useful contribution for evaluating real-world shift scenarios.
