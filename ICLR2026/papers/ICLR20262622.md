## Problem

Causal discovery methods rely on strong, often unverifiable assumptions (e.g., no hidden confounders, stationarity, independent noise), yet empirical evaluations predominantly use idealized synthetic data that overestimates performance. Prior robustness studies either test only binary violation/no-violation scenarios or focus on i.i.d. data, leaving a gap in understanding how time series CD methods degrade under stepwise increasing, real-world-inspired assumption violations.

## Input / Output

TCD-Arena takes a D-variate time series generated from a linear SCM (Eq. 2) with a specific assumption violation applied at one of five calibrated severity levels, and produces a normalized SHD robustness score (minimized over decision thresholds) for each of three graph structures: the lagged window causal graph (G^LWCG), the instantaneous graph (G^INST), and the lagged summary graph (G^LSG). The 33 violation types span observational noise structures, hidden confounding, unfaithfulness, nonlinear mechanisms, non-independent innovation noise, non-stationarity, reduced sample size, missing data, and data scaling, each individually calibrated so the baseline Cross-Correlation method degrades from near-perfect to chance performance across the five levels.

## Method

TCD-Arena is a modularized, open-source testing kit that systematically generates 8,000 unique time series per violation type across 16 data regimes (varying D∈{5,7}, L∈{3,4}, T∈{250,1000}, graph density, and presence of instantaneous effects), yielding ~30 million CD attempts. Ten CD algorithms spanning all four common paradigms (constraint-based, continuous-optimization, information-theoretic, Granger-causality) are evaluated, with robustness quantified as the average normalized SHD over all data regimes and violation levels for a single hyperparameter configuration chosen to maximize general robustness. Beyond individual method evaluation, the paper investigates ensembling by training meta-learners (linear, MLP, Transformer) on predicted graphs from multiple base CD methods, finding that simple linear ensembling outperforms all individual methods and even an oracle Pareto selection strategy for G^LWCG and G^LSG. The toolkit also supports free combination of violations, model misspecification analysis (over/under-specified maximum lag), and hyperparameter sensitivity profiling.

## Results

Across ~30 million CD attempts over 33 stepwise-intensified assumption violations, VarLiNGAM achieves the best individual-method robustness for G^LWCG (normalized SHD 0.408±0.29), while GVAR leads for G^LSG (0.330±0.23) and Dynotears for G^INST (0.515±0.28). Simple ensembling further improves robustness: Ensemble_Linear reduces G^LWCG SHD to 0.362±0.20 and G^LSG to 0.281±0.16, outperforming even the oracle Ensemble_Pareto on both graph types. Constraint-based methods (PCMCI, PCMCI+) show the weakest robustness, with PCMCI scoring 0.601±0.25 on G^LWCG and PCMCI+ stuck near 0.998 on G^INST due to its undirected output.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VarLiNGAM (Hyvärinen et al., 2010) is the most robust individual method for lagged window graphs (SHD 0.408) but underperforms on instantaneous graphs (0.692), consistent with its non-Gaussian identifiability requirement. GVAR (Granger, 1969) is the strongest individual method for lagged summary graphs (0.330) and remains competitive on G^LWCG (0.424), though it cannot recover instantaneous links. Dynotears (Pamfil et al., 2020) and NTS-NOTEARS (Sun et al., 2023) lead on G^INST (0.515 and 0.674 respectively) but show the steepest hyperparameter sensitivity, with NTS-NOTEARS averaging 0.634±0.18 across all hyperparameters versus 0.420±0.02 for VarLiNGAM. Constraint-based PCMCI (Runge et al., 2019) is the least robust overall (0.601 on G^LWCG), while its variant PCMCI+ and SVAR-RFCI (Gerhardus & Runge, 2021) are effectively unable to score below 1.0 on G^INST due to their undirected output. F-PCMCI (Castri et al., 2023) and CausalPretraining (Stein et al., 2024b) occupy the mid-range; CausalPretraining's advantage is that it does not require specifying the maximum lag, making it uniquely robust under the ↓L misspecification regime (SHD 0.530 unchanged). Across all comparisons, simple linear ensembling outperforms every individual method on G^LWCG and G^LSG, and even surpasses the oracle Pareto selection, suggesting genuine recombination of complementary method strengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of assumption-violation robustness studies (Yi et al. 2025, Montagna et al. 2023a, Ferdous et al. 2025) from i.i.d. data and binary violation settings to time series with stepwise, continuously scaled violation intensities. It unifies and builds upon the growing CD benchmarking literature (CausalRivers, CausalDynamics, OCDB) by providing a modular toolkit (TCD-Arena) rather than a fixed dataset, and incorporates the hyperparameter sensitivity analysis advocated by Machlanski et al. (2024) as a first-class evaluation dimension. The ensembling investigation repurposes well-established ensemble learning principles from general ML (Arpit et al. 2022) to the CD setting, where they have received almost no prior attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TCD-Arena provides the first comprehensive, open-source framework for systematically quantifying how CD methods degrade under realistic, graded assumption violations in time series, filling a gap between idealized synthetic benchmarks and the scarcity of real-world causal ground truth. The finding that simple linear ensembling can outperform an oracle Pareto-front selection strategy suggests a practical, low-cost path to more robust CD pipelines, though the authors acknowledge this is a proof-of-concept requiring domain adaptation for real-world use. Open questions include whether the robustness rankings transfer to genuinely non-synthetic data, how multi-violation interactions compound, and whether the ensembling gains persist under distributional shift.
