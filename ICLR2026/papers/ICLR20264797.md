## Problem

Reasoning models (o1/R1-type) are known to overthink, producing redundant reflection steps that waste computation, yet the underlying cause of this behavior remained unexplored. This paper identifies internal bias—a preliminary, intuitive guess the model forms upon encountering a question before engaging in formal reasoning—as a key trigger: when this implicit guess conflicts with the model's reasoned answer, it drives excessive reflection and can even lead to parroting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264797/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a text question and a reasoning LLM (DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B, or R1-Distill-Qwen-14B) as input. For each question, the model generates 64 'direct answers' (forced immediate outputs via a no-reasoning prompt with a  token) to approximate the internal bias distribution, alongside one full reasoning trajectory. Outputs include the deviation degree (MAE for numerical tasks, inconsistency rate for categorical tasks), reasoning length split by deviation group, reflection keyword counts, and layer-wise attention scores categorized by token type (question, intermediate results, others).

## Method

The paper establishes causality through two counterfactual interventions: (1) removing the input question from the prompt immediately after the model's first reasoned answer, forcing continuation based solely on prior reasoning, which reduces redundant length by 31–53% while maintaining or improving accuracy; and (2) bias injection via LoRA fine-tuning on 50 rephrased declarative statements per sample, demonstrating that injecting wrong biases increases reasoning length while correct biases decrease it. Interpretability analysis reveals the mechanism: at reflection decision points, the model's attention to the question segment spikes to over 4× its baseline level in middle-to-later layers, reactivating the internal bias and triggering unnecessary further reflection. The paper further tests existing overthinking mitigations (FCS, SEAL, PROBE) and shows they reduce average length but fail to eliminate the bias-driven reflection pattern.

## Results

Across three model families (DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B, R1-Distill-Qwen-14B) and five datasets, the relative reasoning length increase (R_Δ) between high- and low-bias-deviation groups ranges from 21.0% to 43.1%, confirming that greater internal bias deviation triggers more overthinking. The question-removal counterfactual intervention reduces post-answer reasoning length by 31.1%–53.5% while maintaining or improving accuracy (e.g., AIME 2024 accuracy rises from 63.3% to 66.7%). Bias injection experiments show that injecting wrong biases increases reasoning length from 355.5 to 454.9 tokens, while injecting correct biases decreases it from 600.8 to 412.1 tokens, establishing a causal link.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's question-removal intervention achieves the lowest R_Δ (15.2% on CharCount, 12.5% on AIME 2024), indicating it most effectively eliminates the internal-bias-driven length gap. FCS_SFT (Chen et al., 2025b) produces the shortest absolute reasoning length (451.3 tokens) and highest accuracy (78.9%) on CharCount, but its R_Δ of 26.7% is still nearly double that of Remove, and on AIME 2024 it drops accuracy from 63.3% to 50.0% while Remove improves it to 66.7%. SEAL (Chen et al., 2025a) actually worsens the bias effect, increasing R_Δ from 31.5% to 38.5%, suggesting its hidden-state steering does not address the root cause. PROBE (Zhang et al., 2025a) yields a modest R_Δ reduction (31.5% → 29.9%) but remains far from the 15.2% achieved by Remove. Overall, all three existing methods reduce average reasoning length yet fail to eliminate the internal-bias-driven reflection pattern, whereas the question-removal intervention directly targets and suppresses this mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a causal-explanation study within the overthinking literature, departing from the dominant line of work that proposes mitigation techniques (training-time length penalties, inference-time early exit, prompting) without investigating root causes. It draws conceptual connections to the unfaithfulness and bias literature (Arcuschin et al. 2025, Turpin et al. 2023, Ameisen et al. 2025), but distinguishes its contribution by showing that internal bias triggers overthinking even when the model follows correct reasoning procedures — a regime prior unfaithfulness work does not cover. Methodologically, the counterfactual interventions (question removal, bias injection via fine-tuning) and the attention-based interpretability analysis are novel tools applied to this specific question.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying internal bias as a root cause and demonstrating that existing overthinking mitigations (FCS, SEAL, PROBE) reduce token count without eliminating the underlying bias-driven reflection loop, the paper reframes the efficiency problem: shorter chains are not the same as well-calibrated reasoning. The attention mechanism finding — a ~4× spike in attention to the question at reflection decision points — offers a concrete, potentially exploitable signal for future early-exit or attention-gating strategies. Key limitations include restriction to tasks with explicit numerical or categorical outputs (open-ended bias remains undefined), and the practical solutions proposed (attention-based early exit) are preliminary. Open questions include the origins of internal bias in training dynamics, the transition dynamics by which reasoning eventually overrides the initial guess, and whether decoupling bias at inference time can be made robust across task types.
