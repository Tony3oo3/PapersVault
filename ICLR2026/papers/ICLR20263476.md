## Problem

Existing two-person motion generation systems treat interactive (bidirectional coordination) and reactive (asymmetric actor-to-reactor) settings as separate tasks with incompatible architectures, training objectives, and conditioning signals, and support only single-modality inputs (text-only or music-only). No unified model performs both tasks under one architecture while jointly leveraging multi-modal cues, forcing practitioners to maintain and retrain separate pipelines for each scenario.

## Input / Output

The model consumes text prompts (768-d CLIP ViT-L/14 embeddings), music audio (4800-d Jukebox features), an optional actor motion sequence (for reactive mode), and top-k retrieved motion exemplars from four databases (spatial, body, rhythm, music). Motion is represented in SMPL format with 22 joints at 262 dimensions per frame (global joint positions, velocities, 6D local rotations, and binary foot contacts) in a coordinate system anchored at person A's root. Output is a synchronized two-person 3D motion sequence (interactive) or a single reactor motion sequence (reactive), generated in 20 rectified-flow integration steps.

## Method

DualFlow uses 20 cascaded DualFlow Blocks, each combining multi-scale temporal convolutions (strides 7/11/21 with learnable gating), self-attention, and a structured cross-attention sequence (Music → Motion → Retrieval) with text injected via LayerNorm conditioning. Task switching is achieved through masking: both person branches operate symmetrically for interactive generation, while the actor branch is masked and Motion Cross-Attention is replaced by Causal Cross-Attention with a 10-frame Look-Ahead for reactive generation. A novel RAG module uses GPT-4o to decompose text into spatial-relationship, body-movement, and rhythm descriptors, retrieves semantically matched motion exemplars via CLIP/Jukebox similarity scoring, and injects them through Retrieval Cross-Attention. Training employs Contrastive Rectified Flow (deterministic straight-line ODE transport with a triplet contrastive loss on velocity embeddings) combined with geometric losses (foot contact, velocity, bone length) and an anatomically weighted synchronization loss that enforces inter-person joint-distance coherence, achieving 2.5× faster inference than 50-step DDIM baselines.

## Results

On the MDD dataset (text + music conditioning), DualFlow(Both) achieves the best R-Precision@3 (0.513) and lowest MMDist (0.513) for the interactive duet task, with FID of 0.415 (nearly matching InterGen's 0.405). For the reactive task, DualFlow(Both) leads in FID (0.686), MMDist (1.056), and R-Precision@3 (0.471), while maintaining competitive BED (0.215) and the best BAS (0.226). On InterHuman-AS, DualFlow outperforms InterGen on R-Precision (0.681 vs 0.624 Top-3) and MMDist (4.394 vs 5.108). On DD100, DualFlow achieves the best FID_k (19.22), FID_g (28.85), and FID_cd (5.57) for reactive generation. Inference requires only 20 rectified flow steps (1.24s AITS) versus 50 DDIM steps (1.92s) for InterGen, a 2.5× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MDM (Tevet et al., 2022), a single-person diffusion model adapted to two-person generation, DualFlow(Both) dramatically outperforms on all metrics: FID drops from 1.739 to 0.415 and MMDist from 2.244 to 0.513 in the interactive task, confirming that purpose-built two-person architectures are essential. Against InterGen (Liang et al., 2024), the strongest two-person diffusion baseline, DualFlow(Both) achieves a 3× lower MMDist (0.513 vs 1.532) and 70% higher R-Precision@3 (0.513 vs 0.302), though InterGen(Text) retains a marginal FID advantage (0.405 vs 0.415) and higher Diversity (1.405 vs 1.392). In the reactive task, DualFlow(Both) beats DuoLando (Siyao et al., 2024) on FID (0.686 vs 0.698), MMDist (1.056 vs 2.113, a 2× improvement), and R-Precision@3 (0.471 vs 0.219, more than 2×), though DuoLando achieves a higher BED (0.395 vs 0.215) indicating stronger beat synchronization. On InterHuman-AS, DualFlow surpasses ReGenNet (Xu et al., 2024) in the unconstrained reactive setting on R-Precision@3 (0.572 vs 0.407), MMDist (6.314 vs 6.860), and Multimodality (2.502 vs 2.391). Overall, DualFlow establishes state-of-the-art on the MDD and DD100 benchmarks while requiring only 20 inference steps versus 50 for diffusion-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualFlow sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) two-person motion generation, where prior work (InterGen, Duolando, ReGenNet) treats interactive and reactive settings as architecturally separate tasks with single-modality conditioning; (2) retrieval-augmented generation for motion, which has been applied only to single-person text-to-motion (ReMoDiffuse, TMR) and never to multi-person interaction; and (3) rectified flow / flow matching for generative modeling, which offers deterministic straight-line sampling paths versus stochastic diffusion. The paper's core departure is unifying interactive and reactive generation under one transformer architecture with masking, and extending RAG from single-person to two-person motion via LLM-decomposed text cues (spatial, body, rhythm) and music features, combined with a contrastive rectified flow objective borrowed from recent contrastive flow matching work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying interactive and reactive two-person motion under a single multi-modal rectified flow architecture, DualFlow removes the need for separate models and retraining when switching tasks, which is practically important for embodied AI, VR/AR companions, and social robotics that must flexibly coordinate with or react to human partners. The 2.5× inference speedup (20 vs. 50 steps) makes near-real-time generation more feasible. Key open questions remain: RAG quality is sensitive to retrieval accuracy and degrades on ambiguous or uncommon prompts; close-contact physical penetration persists due to the absence of explicit contact constraints; and long-sequence temporal drift limits structural coherence. Future directions include learned re-ranking for retrieval, contact-based physical losses, hierarchical temporal modeling, and few-shot adaptation to novel styles.
