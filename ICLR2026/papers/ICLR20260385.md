## Problem

Despite conflicting reports on whether additional modalities improve or degrade multimodal reasoning, no prior work provides a controlled framework that isolates how factual information is distributed across modalities and logically combined. Moreover, MLLMs are typically evaluated as black boxes, leaving their internal mechanisms for modality integration, evidence selection, and cross-modal fusion poorly understood.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260385/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance consists of synthetic facts (subject-is-attribute triples) rendered in three controlled modalities—text sentences, audio synthesized via CosyVoice 2 TTS, and schematic graph diagrams generated with Graphviz—alongside a text-based rule set and a four-way multiple-choice question. The six interaction types (Equivalence, Alternative, Entailment, Independence, Contradictory, Complementary) systematically control which modality carries which fact and how facts must be logically combined (redundantly, disjunctively, chained, independently, conflictively, or conjunctively). In the two-step prompting variant, the model first outputs a recognized fact list per modality before producing the final answer.

## Method

The paper introduces a propositional-logic-based evaluation framework with six canonical modality interaction types, instantiated through controlled synthetic multiple-choice reasoning tasks, to systematically assess when and why additional modalities help or hurt reasoning across four open-source MLLMs (Baichuan-Omni, Qwen2.5-Omni, MiniCPM-o, Phi-4 Multimodal). To diagnose the underlying causes of identified failures, the authors train linear probes on decoder attention distributions to test whether models internally encode fact usefulness and modality identity, finding that attention patterns fail to distinguish relevant from distractor facts while modality identity remains perfectly recoverable in early layers. Two targeted interventions validate the identified bottlenecks: a two-step prompting strategy (recognize facts, then reason) that alleviates the task-composition bottleneck, and softmax temperature manipulation in the first four decoder layers that reduces early-fusion bias and improves cross-modal reasoning accuracy.

## Results

Across four MLLMs (Baichuan-Omni-1.5, Qwen2.5-Omni, MiniCPM-o-2.6, Phi-4 Multimodal), text-only baselines consistently approach ceiling accuracy (94.5–97.0% average across interaction types). Multimodal input helps only in the Alternative setting (+12.7% vision, +14.8% audio, +1.7% text over unimodal baselines), while Entailment (multi-hop chains split across modalities) causes consistent drops of 7.1–12.8% and the Complementary interaction degrades accuracy to 52.0% (average), below even the weakest unimodal condition (73.2% vision-only). Two-step prompting (recognize then reason) substantially recovers performance, and increasing attention temperature in the first four decoder layers (0.4→1.8) significantly improves cross-modal reasoning accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a diagnostic evaluation paper comparing four open-source MLLMs rather than proposing a new model, so the 'baselines' are the four evaluated systems. Qwen2.5-Omni is the strongest performer overall, reaching 98.9% on Equivalence and 100.0% on Alternative, with the smallest Entailment drops (−8.2% to −15.7%). Phi-4 Multimodal is the weakest, scoring only 84.1% on Equivalence and 69.3–79.7% on Entailment, though it still benefits most from Alternative (+20.3% V, +26.3% A). MiniCPM-o-2.6 and Baichuan-Omni-1.5 fall in between, with MiniCPM showing the most balanced cross-modality performance (80.0–88.4% on Entailment) and Baichuan exhibiting the largest text-to-multimodal gap (−27.2% on Independence). All four models share the same qualitative pattern: text-only is strongest, multimodal helps only with independent reasoning paths, and cross-modal chaining or complementary fusion consistently degrades accuracy. On the real-world IsoBench validation, the same pattern holds: multimodal (T+V) inputs provide little or no gain over text-only baselines (63.6% vs. 66.2% average), confirming the synthetic findings generalize.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the growing body of multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MMMU, MMBench, HallusionBench, EMMA) and modality-dominance analyses (Sinha et al., Wu et al., Zhang et al.), but departs by introducing a controlled, logic-based evaluation framework with six canonical interaction types grounded in propositional logic that explicitly vary where decision-relevant facts are placed and how they must be combined. It also extends the single-step logical reasoning setup of Clark et al. (2020) into a multimodal context, and goes beyond prior black-box evaluations by adding interpretability probes (attention-based linear probes) and causal interventions (two-step prompting, attention temperature manipulation) to isolate specific failure mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic taxonomy for multimodal reasoning failures—distinguishing task-composition from fusion bottlenecks—and shows that simple, low-cost interventions (two-step prompting, early-layer attention temperature adjustment) can substantially recover performance, offering actionable guidance for practitioners building MLLMs. Key open questions include whether composition-aware training objectives can internalize the two-step decomposition, whether architectural changes to early fusion (e.g., modality-agnostic gating) generalize beyond the four tested models, and whether the synthetic single-step setting captures the complexity of real-world multimodal reasoning. The synthetic data and limited model diversity (four open-source models, 5–8B parameters) are notable limitations for generalizability.
