## Problem

Existing learning-based Koopman approaches for nonlinear system identification focus on trajectory reconstruction but neither guarantee controllability of the learned model nor exploit it as a structural prior during training, producing models that predict well yet are unsuitable for downstream control. The paper addresses this by enforcing controllability by construction during learning, which simultaneously reduces the effective parameter search space and improves data efficiency under limited samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264763/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes continuous-time time-series data of state trajectories x(t) ∈ R^n and control inputs u(t) ∈ R^m, sampled at potentially irregular or multi-rate intervals. It produces a linear Koopman surrogate (A_θ, B_θ, C) in a lifted space of dimension N ≫ n, where the encoder φ_θ(x) = [x; ψ_θ(x)] preserves the nominal state via identity lifting and the output C = [I_n 0] recovers x(t) directly. Because the dynamics are learned in continuous time, the same model supports long-horizon prediction and convex QP-based MPC at arbitrary control frequencies without retraining.

## Method

The core contribution is a canonical parameterization of the Koopman matrices that guarantees output-to-output controllability (OOC) by construction: the first n−1 rows of A_θ contain only superdiagonal 1s, the first n−1 rows of B_θ are zero with the nth row equal to 1, and all remaining entries are free learnable parameters, with a learnable block-diagonal similarity transform P_θ = diag(P_1, P_2) providing additional expressiveness while preserving the guarantee. The authors prove that OOC of the Koopman linear representation is equivalent to controllability of the original nonlinear system and that OOC coincides with state-to-output controllability under the identity-lift setup, reducing verification to a Kalman rank condition. A Gramian-based regularizer further shapes the degree of controllability by penalizing small minimum eigenvalues and large condition numbers of the finite-horizon output Gramian. The entire pipeline is implemented as an end-to-end Neural ODE with a differentiable ODE solver, trained with a weighted trajectory prediction loss plus the Gramian regularizer, and extended to multi-input systems via a Brunovsky block decomposition.

## Results

On prediction (MSE), the proposed method consistently outperforms both DKO and MLP when less than 30% of data is available: e.g., on Pendulum at 1% data, Ours achieves 0.3747 vs. DKO's 1.5347 and MLP's 1.1778; on Cartpole at 1%, Ours achieves 0.0095 vs. DKO's 0.1306. For MPC control, the method reduces input cost by 44.5% on Mountain Car (5% data) and 81.8% on Pendulum (10% data) compared to DKO, while DKO outright fails on several tasks (Mountain Car 1%, Pendulum 5%, Cartpole 30%). On multi-input systems, the advantage is even more pronounced: on Reacher at 1% data, DKO's MSE exceeds 5 while Ours achieves 0.0199.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Deep Koopman Operator (DKO) and a multilayer perceptron (MLP) trained directly on nonlinear dynamics. Against DKO, the proposed method wins decisively under data-limited settings (≤30% data) across all single-input benchmarks, with MSE improvements of 4–14× at 1% data (e.g., Pendulum: 0.3747 vs. 1.5347; Cartpole: 0.0095 vs. 0.1306). In MPC control, DKO fails entirely on Mountain Car (1%), Pendulum (5%), and Cartpole (30%), while the proposed method completes all tasks with substantially lower input energy (up to 81.8% reduction). Against MLP, the proposed method is superior at every data fraction on all three single-input tasks, with the gap most dramatic at 1% data (e.g., Pendulum: 0.3747 vs. 1.1778). With abundant data (≥50%), the proposed method and DKO converge to comparable prediction accuracy, but the proposed method retains its controllability guarantee by construction, which DKO can only verify a posteriori. On multi-input systems (Reacher, Franka, GRN), the advantage over DKO is even larger, with Reacher at 1% data showing DKO MSE >5 versus Ours at 0.0199.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) and Koopman operator-based system identification (Koopman, 1931; Lusch et al., 2018; Han et al., 2020), extending the standard pipeline of learning a linear surrogate in a lifted observable space. The key departure from prior Koopman learning methods (DKO, Deep Koopman with control) is the shift from treating controllability as a soft loss term or a post-hoc verification to enforcing it by construction via a canonical parameterization of the Koopman matrices, drawing on the Brunovsky canonical form (Luenberger, 2003) and extending it to the state-to-output controllability setting. The Gramian-based regularizer for shaping the degree of controllability is a new addition not present in prior Koopman identification work, and the equivalence between OOC and SOC under identity lifting (Lemma 1) provides the theoretical bridge that makes the canonical form applicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical disconnect in data-driven control: models that predict trajectories accurately but fail under MPC because they are uncontrollable or poorly conditioned. By encoding controllability as an architectural constraint rather than an optimization objective, the method simultaneously provides a hard guarantee and acts as an inductive bias that improves data efficiency (4–6× lower MSE at 1% data on standard benchmarks). Open questions remain around multi-input settings where the assignment of control channels to state variables is nontrivial, the block-diagonal structure of P introduces conservativeness, and the approach has not been tested on high-dimensional or safety-critical systems. The framework could become a useful template for embedding other structural priors (stability, dissipativity) into Koopman learning by construction.
