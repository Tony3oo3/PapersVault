## Problem

Existing proxy-based fine-tuning methods (e.g., Proxy-tuning, CombLM) require access to the full output probability distributions of frozen LLMs, which are unavailable for black-box API models and empirically unstable even when accessible. This limits their applicability to open-source models with shared vocabularies and introduces reliability issues that can degrade downstream performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query x and a set of LLM-elicited textual knowledge pieces k (each paired with a confidence score c ∈ [0,1]), filtered by a threshold α, as input to the proxy model. The proxy produces a task-specific prediction y, which can be a multiple-choice label, a binary true/false answer, or open-ended text depending on the benchmark. At inference, the system additionally consumes aggregated confidence scores (C_final = C_knowledge × C_prediction) to decide whether to route the query to the LLM directly or to the proxy.

## Method

KNOWPROXY replaces distributional alignment with knowledge-guided adaptation: it elicits multiple textual knowledge-confidence pairs from a frozen LLM via task-specific prompting, filters them by a confidence threshold α, and trains a small proxy model (via LoRA) on the knowledge-augmented dataset D_K = {(x, k, y)} with a standard supervised objective. At inference, a dynamic routing mechanism computes C_final as the product of the prediction confidence and the geometric mean of all knowledge confidence scores; if C_final ≥ τ, the LLM's prediction is returned directly, otherwise the proxy is invoked with the generated knowledge to refine the answer. This design makes the framework applicable to black-box LLMs (only text outputs needed), avoids the instability of LLM probability distributions, and decouples the proxy from the backbone LLM so that a proxy trained on one LLM can be transferred to another without retraining.

## Results

On nine reasoning benchmarks (using Llama-3.2-3B as frozen LLM and Llama-3.2-1B as proxy), KNOWPROXY achieves 78.2% average test accuracy, outperforming all proxy-based baselines (CombLM 74.3%, Proxy-tuning 71.9%, BBox-Adapter 70.2%) and all zero-shot reasoning methods (CoT 73.1%, Plan-and-Solve 71.7%, Self-Talk 70.6%). It approaches direct fine-tuning of the LLM (81.6%) while requiring no access to LLM parameters or probability distributions. On individual benchmarks, KNOWPROXY matches or exceeds direct fine-tuning on OpenBookQA (80.2 vs. 82.2), ARC-Challenge (75.2 vs. 76.2), StrategyQA (72.9 vs. 71.5), and BoolQ (85.1 vs. 86.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KNOWPROXY (78.2% avg.) decisively outperforms all three proxy-based baselines: CombLM (74.3%, +3.9%), Proxy-tuning (71.9%, +6.3%), and BBox-Adapter (70.2%, +8.0%). It also surpasses all zero-shot reasoning baselines—Chain-of-Thought (73.1%, +5.1%), Plan-and-Solve (71.7%, +6.5%), Self-Talk (70.6%, +7.6%), and plain Zero-shot (68.7%, +9.5%). Notably, existing proxy-based methods sometimes underperform even the frozen LLM's zero-shot performance on QASC (Proxy-tuning 69.9% vs. Zero-shot 75.9%) and BoolQ (BBox-Adapter 70.5% vs. Zero-shot 78.3%), whereas KNOWPROXY improves on both. The gap to direct fine-tuning of the LLM (81.6%) is only 3.4% on average, and KNOWPROXY actually matches or exceeds fine-tuning on StrategyQA (72.9 vs. 71.5) and is within 2 points on four of nine benchmarks, all without accessing LLM parameters or probability distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

KNOWPROXY extends the proxy-based fine-tuning paradigm established by Proxy-tuning (Liu et al., 2024) and CombLM (Ormazabal et al., 2023), which adapt frozen LLMs by reweighting or combining their output probability distributions with a smaller model. The key conceptual departure is replacing distributional alignment with textual knowledge: rather than requiring access to LLM logits, the proxy is trained on knowledge and reasoning elicited via prompting, making the framework applicable to black-box API models. The dynamic routing mechanism draws on prompting-based confidence elicitation (Tian et al., 2023) but extends it by aggregating uncertainty across multiple generated knowledge pieces rather than relying on a single prediction-level score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KNOWPROXY addresses a practical gap: adapting proprietary or API-based LLMs without access to internal parameters or probability distributions, a setting where prior proxy-based methods simply cannot operate. The transferability result—that a proxy trained on one LLM's knowledge generalizes to a different backbone LLM—suggests a reusable adaptation layer that could reduce retraining costs as models are upgraded. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-ended generation tasks beyond multiple-choice and binary reasoning, how sensitive performance is to the quality of elicited knowledge from weaker LLMs, and whether the routing threshold can be learned rather than hand-tuned per benchmark.
