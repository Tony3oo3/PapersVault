## Problem

Existing 4D human-scene reconstruction methods rely on multi-stage pipelines with heavy off-the-shelf dependencies (human detection, tracking, SLAM, metric depth, segmentation) and iterative contact-aware refinement between humans and scenes, making them slow (hours), non-end-to-end, and impractical for real-time online use. No prior work jointly recovers global multi-person SMPL-X bodies, dense 3D scene geometry, and camera trajectories from monocular video in a single forward pass at real-time speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261450/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous stream of RGB images (casually captured monocular video) in an online, frame-by-frame fashion, maintaining a fixed-size recurrent state that encodes all prior observations. At each timestep it outputs: (1) N SMPL-X body meshes (10,475 vertices, 54 joints) parameterized by pose θ, shape β, expression α, and global root transform P in the world coordinate system; (2) camera extrinsic pose T_t ∈ SE(3) and intrinsic C_t ∈ R^{3×3}; and (3) a dense pixel-aligned metric-scale 3D point cloud X_t ∈ R^{W×H×3}. Despite being trained on 4-frame sequences, it generalizes to thousands of frames via state rolling with periodic resets, operating at 15 FPS with 8 GB GPU memory.

## Method

Human3R fine-tunes the CUT3R recurrent 4D reconstruction foundation model via parameter-efficient visual prompt tuning, keeping the entire CUT3R backbone frozen and training only lightweight human-related MLP layers. Instead of standard VPT's randomly initialized tokens, it detects human head tokens from CUT3R's ViT image features via a patch-level confidence MLP, concatenates each detected head token with the corresponding Multi-HMR ViT-DINO human-prior token (frozen, fine-tuned on human-specific data), and projects the concatenation through a learnable MLP to produce discriminative human prompts. These prompts are inserted into the decoder input space where they self-attend with image tokens to aggregate spatial whole-body information and cross-attend with the persistent scene state to retrieve temporally consistent 3D human parameters in scene context. For test-time sequence length adaptation beyond the 4-frame training context, the method adopts TTT3R's closed-form state update rule with a state reset every 100 frames aligned by global camera pose. The entire model is trained on the small-scale synthetic BEDLAM dataset (6k sequences) for one day on a single 48 GB GPU, using confidence-aware pointmap loss, camera pose loss, detection BCE, SMPL parameter L1, mesh L1, and reprojection losses.

## Results

On global human motion estimation (EMDB-2), Human3R achieves WA-MJPE 112.2 mm, W-MJPE 267.9 mm, and RTE 2.2%, outperforming the best online baseline WHAM by 20% in W-MJPE (267.9 vs. 354.8) and 60% in RTE (2.2 vs. 6.0). On RICH, it reaches WA-MJPE 110.0 mm and RTE 3.3%, competitive with offline methods like GVHMR (78.8 WA-MJPE) while requiring no pre-cached conditions. For local human mesh recovery (EMDB-1), Human3R achieves PA-MPJPE 48.5 mm and PVE 86.0 mm, surpassing the one-stage Multi-HMR (50.1 / 95.7) and BEV (70.9 / 133.4) while remaining crop-free, detection-free, and intrinsic-free. The lightweight ViT-S/672 variant runs at 15 FPS with 8 GB GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among online methods, Human3R substantially outperforms WHAM [82] on EMDB-2 (W-MJPE 267.9 vs. 354.8, RTE 2.2 vs. 6.0) and JOSH3R [53] (WA-MJPE 112.2 vs. 220.0, W-MJPE 267.9 vs. 661.7), while being the only method that requires zero preprocessed inputs (detection, tracking, local HMR, camera, mask, depth, contact all ✓). Against offline methods, Human3R is competitive with GVHMR [80] on EMDB-2 (WA-MJPE 112.2 vs. 111.0, W-MJPE 267.9 vs. 276.5) but trails TRAM [99] (76.4) and JOSH [53] (68.9) in WA-MJPE, which benefit from iterative optimization and pre-cached conditions. On RICH, Human3R (WA-MJPE 110.0) is competitive with WHAM (108.4) and GVHMR (78.8) while remaining fully online and dependency-free. For local HMR (Table 1), Human3R beats one-stage baselines Multi-HMR [3] and BEV [88] on EMDB-1 (PA-MPJPE 48.5 vs. 50.1 and 70.9) but trails multi-stage methods like NLF [76] (41.2) and PromptHMR [100] (41.0) that use detection/cropping. The key differentiator is that Human3R simultaneously outputs global human meshes, dense scene geometry, and camera poses in a single 15 FPS forward pass, whereas all baselines either require multiple off-the-shelf modules or operate offline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 4D reconstruction foundation model CUT3R (which maintains a persistent state for online metric-scale scene and camera reconstruction) by introducing parameter-efficient visual prompt tuning to enable direct readout of multi-person SMPL-X bodies in the world frame. It departs from prior global human motion estimators (WHAM, TRAM, GVHMR, JOSH) that rely on multi-stage pipelines combining SLAM, human detection, tracking, segmentation, and iterative human-scene contact refinement, and from prior human-scene reconstruction methods (JOSH, HAMSt3R) that require multi-view inputs or heavy preprocessing. The key conceptual move is treating detected head tokens (augmented with Multi-HMR human-prior features) as discriminative queries that self-attend to image tokens and cross-attend to the scene state, unifying human, scene, and camera reasoning in a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Human3R provides the first unified, real-time (15 FPS, 8 GB) feed-forward model that jointly reconstructs multi-person global human meshes, dense 3D scene geometry, and camera trajectories from monocular video without any off-the-shelf dependencies, trained on a small synthetic dataset for one day on one GPU. This opens practical pathways for humanoid policy learning, autonomous navigation, and human-robot interaction where latency and deployment simplicity are critical. Key limitations remain: reliance on the head as the sole discriminative keypoint causes failures under severe occlusion, the deterministic pipeline regresses to average poses for unobserved body parts, and proxy SMPL meshes lack clothing/appearance modeling. Extending the framework to other dynamic entities (animals, vehicles) and incorporating generative modeling for multimodal pose distributions are natural next steps.
