## Problem

Machine learning methods for seismic wavefield modeling are proliferating rapidly, but the field lacks a standardized evaluation protocol, leading to weak baselines, reporting bias, and inconsistent comparisons that obscure true progress. This paper introduces the Seismic Wavefield Common Task Framework (CTF), a structured benchmark with curated datasets at multiple scales and a 12-metric scoring system that enables fair, head-to-head algorithm evaluation on withheld test sets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265265/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Input consists of seismic wavefield data organized as matrices of shape [time_steps, sensors] (e.g., [2000, 2048] for global wavefields on a Fibonacci sphere, [2000, 3000] for DAS fiber-optic channels, [500, 62451] for 3D crustal grids), normalized to zero mean and unit variance per event. The 12 tasks require producing forecasted states (2m×n matrices), denoised reconstructions (4m×n), and parametric generalization predictions from limited snapshots (M×n) or burn-in segments, with m=500 for global/DAS and m=250 for crustal data. Scoring combines short-time RMSE (E_ST) and long-time spectral error via power spectral density matching over the first 100 wavenumbers (E_LT), each scaled to a 0–100 range.

## Method

The CTF defines four task categories—forecasting, noisy data handling, limited-data forecasting, and parametric generalization—each with specific train/test matrix splits and a composite score computed as the mean of all 12 task scores. Eighteen models spanning RNNs (LSTM, ODE-LSTM), neural operators (FNO, DeepONet), state-space models (SpaceTime), dynamical systems methods (DMD, Opt DMD, SINDy, PyKoopman), reservoir computing, NeuralODE, KAN, and time-series foundation models (Chronos, Moirai, Panda, TabPFN, LLMTime, Sundial) are evaluated under identical hyperparameter optimization via Ray Tune with ASHA scheduling on A100 GPUs. The key empirical finding is that most complex architectures fail to exceed the trivial zero-prediction baseline, while RNN-based models (LSTM, ODE-LSTM) achieve the highest composite scores, excelling particularly at denoising but performing poorly on limited-data forecasting—demonstrating that no single model dominates across all task regimes.

## Results

On the Global Wavefields Dataset, LSTM achieves the highest average score (13.18) across 12 task-specific metrics, followed by ODE-LSTM (5.71), while 10 of 18 evaluated models score below the zero-prediction baseline (0.0). On the DAS Dataset, PyKoopman leads with an average score of 12.7, with ODE-LSTM a close second at 11.58. No model exceeds a score of 50 on any forecasting metric (E1, E2, E7–E10), and the best denoising scores (E3, E5) reach only ~70–90, indicating substantial room for improvement. The multi-metric design reveals that no single model dominates: LSTM excels at denoising but fails at limited-data forecasting (E7 = −40.07), while Sundial achieves the best short-term DAS forecast (E1 = 49.57) yet scores −0.57 overall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSTM (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997) achieves the best overall score on the Global Wavefields Dataset (13.18) and is second on DAS (−8.0), excelling at denoising (E3 = 69.7, E5 = 48.83) but failing at limited-data forecasting (E7 = −40.07). ODE-LSTM (Coelho et al., 2024) is the most consistently strong model, ranking second on both datasets (5.71 Global, 11.58 DAS) with the best high-noise denoising on DAS (E5 = 82.88). PyKoopman (Pan et al., 2024) wins the DAS overall (12.7) but is weak on Global (−34.48), showing dataset-dependent performance. Sundial (Liu et al., 2025) achieves the best short-term DAS forecasts (E1 = 49.57, E7 = 33.24, E9 = 13.83) yet scores −100 on Global, indicating it cannot generalize across scales. Neural operators (FNO: −30.92 Global, −26.6 DAS; DeepONet: −50.1, −45.11) and state-space models (SpaceTime: −45.19, −56.04) all fall below the zero baseline on both datasets. DMD variants (Higher Order DMD: 0.0, −3.59; Opt DMD: −25.76, −44.08) overfit noise, while SINDy (−26.44, −18.55) and Reservoir Computing (−14.76, not listed on DAS) struggle with the high-dimensional spatiotemporal structure. Foundation models (Moirai, Chronos, Panda, LLMTime, TabPFN) all score −100 on Global, indicating complete failure on this task, with only TabPFN and Sundial producing non-trivial scores on individual DAS sub-tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Common Task Framework (CTF) paradigm established by Wyder et al. (ICML Workshop 2025) for canonical nonlinear dynamical systems, transplanting its 12-metric scoring protocol to seismology. The CTF concept itself draws from landmark benchmarks in computer vision (ImageNet), NLP (MATH, HumanEval), and reinforcement learning (Atari, MuJoCo), all of which catalyzed major methodological advances through standardized, hidden-test-set evaluation. The paper departs from prior seismological ML work—which has relied on self-reported results on author-chosen datasets—by introducing three curated datasets (global IASP91 wavefields, offshore DAS recordings, and 3D crustal simulations) with explicitly withheld test sets and an independent scoring referee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that 18 diverse ML methods, including neural operators, foundation models, and state-space models, largely fail to exceed the zero-prediction baseline on the global wavefields dataset—challenges the prevailing narrative that current AI is ready for seismological forecasting and reconstruction. The multi-metric design reveals that no single architecture dominates: simple RNNs (LSTM, ODE-LSTM) lead overall but collapse on limited-data tasks, while the Sundial foundation model excels at short-term DAS forecasting but fails elsewhere. Key open questions include whether the planned Kaggle competition with 100 training simulations will unlock better performance, whether heterogeneous (non-axisymmetric) Earth models fundamentally change the difficulty landscape, and whether the intentionally limited data volumes are the primary bottleneck or whether the task structure itself is too hard for current methods.
