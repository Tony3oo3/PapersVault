## Problem

Existing exploratory bonus methods for RLHF (e.g., SELM, XPO, VPO) are designed to operationalize 'optimism in the face of uncertainty,' but the paper proves they fundamentally fail: under KL or α-divergence regularization, the bonus formulations inadvertently bias exploration toward high-probability regions of the reference model, reinforcing conservative behavior rather than driving discovery of underexplored regions. No prior theoretical framework provably satisfies the optimism principle for exploratory bonuses across the divergence family.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts, a reference policy π_ref, preference pairs (winning/losing responses), and a divergence parameter α ∈ [0,1], and produces an aligned policy model plus a differentiable exploratory bonus term added to the reward-modeling loss. The bonus is computed as an expectation over π_ref(·|x), so it requires no additional sampling beyond what the iterative RLHF loop already performs. The bonus is parameterized by a flexible function u(x,y) of π(y|x) and π_ref(y|x) that must satisfy u > α, and in practice is restricted to rejected responses to avoid decreasing the likelihood of preferred outputs.

## Method

The paper first proves (Lemma 3.1, Lemma 3.2, Theorem 3.3) that the standard bonus L_bonus = max_π J_{β,f}(π,r) is ineffective or actively harmful: after reward reparameterization, the resulting gradient is positively correlated with π_ref, driving the policy to collapse toward the reference. To fix this, GEB introduces a reference-dependent reward R(x,y) that depends on both r(x,y) and π_ref(y|x), yielding L_bonus = max_π J_{β,f}(π,R). After substituting the optimal policy and applying reward reparameterization, the bonus simplifies to β E_{x~ρ,y~π_ref}[u(x,y)f'(u(x,y)) − f(u(x,y))], where u is a freely chosen function of π and π_ref satisfying u > α. Theorem 4.2 then proves this form satisfies the optimism condition (∂²L_bonus/∂π∂π_ref ≤ 0) for any u meeting a mild derivative inequality. Three concrete instantiations—u = 1+α−π, u = 1/π, and u = arctanh(1−π)+α—are provided, and prior heuristic bonuses (SELM, XPO, VPO) are shown to be special cases of this unified framework.

## Results

On in-domain UltraFeedback evaluation, GEB variants consistently outperform f-DPO across all three divergence settings (KL, Hellinger, forward KL) and both LLM backbones. Under KL divergence, GEB-π achieves 81.00% win-rate on Mistral-Instruct-v0.3 (+2.58% over f-DPO's 78.42%) and 74.34% on LLaMA-3-8B-SFT (+1.23% over f-DPO's 73.11%). Under Hellinger distance, GEB-arctanh(π−1) reaches 75.69% on Mistral (+3.0% over f-DPO) and 73.26% on LLaMA (+2.15%). On math reasoning (Pass@16), GEB-1/π improves over DPO by +3.2 on MATH-500 (93.00 vs 89.80) and +8.0 on OlympiadBench (65.78 vs 57.78).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEB outperforms f-DPO (Wang et al., 2024) across all divergence settings and both backbones: under KL, GEB-π gains +2.58% WR on Mistral and +1.23% on LLaMA; under Hellinger, GEB-arctanh gains +3.0% and +2.15% respectively. Against the three prior exploratory-bonus methods evaluated only under KL, GEB-π (81.00% Mistral, 74.34% LLaMA) beats SELM (77.56%, 74.19%), XPO (79.71%, 72.40%), and VPO (78.57%, 71.61%). The Failed Exploratory Bonus (FEB) baseline, which removes the practical approximations from prior methods, confirms the theoretical failure: FEB matches f-DPO under KL but degrades under Hellinger (71.54% vs 72.69%) and forward KL (47.53% vs 51.11%) on Mistral, while GEB consistently improves over both. On math reasoning (Table 6), all three GEB variants surpass DPO by 3–8 points on OlympiadBench and AIME 2025, with GEB-1/π achieving the best average (62.75 vs DPO's 56.94). Out-of-domain AlpacaEval2 scores (Table 4) show GEB-π improving from 25.72 to 28.27 under KL on LLaMA, while MATH-500 scores remain stable or improve, indicating no significant alignment tax.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DPO reward-reparameterization framework (Rafailov et al., 2024) and its f-divergence generalization f-DPO (Wang et al., 2024) within the iterative online RLHF loop (Dong et al., 2024). It departs sharply from prior exploratory-bonus methods (SELM, XPO, VPO) by proving that their theoretical formulation—maximizing the KL- or α-divergence-regularized objective as a bonus—actually biases exploration toward high-π_ref regions, contradicting the optimism principle they were designed to implement. GEB repositions the bonus as a reference-dependent reward regulation that provably counteracts divergence-induced conservatism, while subsuming prior heuristic bonuses (e.g., log π, log(π/π_ref)) as special cases within a unified α-divergence framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative theoretical result—that widely adopted exploratory bonuses are provably non-optimistic under standard divergence regularization—challenges a working assumption in the RLHF exploration literature and explains why prior methods may underperform in practice. GEB's unified formulation gives practitioners a principled menu of bonus designs (parameterized by α and the function u) that are guaranteed to encourage sampling in underexplored regions, without additional compute cost. Open questions include whether the optimism guarantee holds under multi-step (non-iterative) RLHF, whether the framework extends to token-level or segment-level rewards, and whether the modest empirical gains (1–2% win-rate) scale to larger models or longer training horizons where exploration pressure is more acute.
