## Problem

Existing denoising schedules for Diffusion Language Models suffer from a fundamental misalignment with semantic structure: Standard Diffusion finalizes tokens based solely on local confidence without sufficient context, causing premature end-of-sequence predictions and cascading errors, while BlockDiffusion imposes rigid fixed-size block boundaries that artificially fragment coherent reasoning steps and code segments. No prior schedule dynamically adapts its update region to the evolving generation context while preserving the computational efficiency of block-based methods.

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditioning prompt and a fully masked token sequence of fixed length N (up to 1024 tokens), and iteratively denoises it over T=1024 steps using a pre-trained discrete diffusion model (LLaDA-8B-Instruct, LLaDA-1.5, or Dream-7B). At each step, only a bounded subset of masked positions within the dynamic wavefront is updated, with the per-step finalize quota k_t = ⌊N/T⌋ distributed evenly. The output is a fully denoised text sequence evaluated via exact match (reasoning) or pass@1 (code), with all experiments run at temperature 0 for determinism.

## Method

WavefrontDiffusion is a training-free dynamic scheduling strategy that replaces fixed block partitions with an expanding wavefront of candidate positions. At each iteration, it (1) scores all masked positions via a single forward pass, (2) finalizes the top-k_t highest-confidence tokens from the current wavefront, (3) expands the wavefront to include all masked neighbors within radius R of newly finalized positions, and (4) prunes to a maximum size F to enforce compute parity with BlockDiffusion. The theoretical grounding is the Information Gradient Hypothesis, which posits that token conditional entropy increases monotonically with distance from finalized context, making the wavefront an optimal restricted search space over entropy isosurfaces. This guarantees that the dynamic boundary contains a higher density of low-entropy candidates than any static block partition, while the total number of token updates (F×T) remains identical to block-based methods, ensuring all quality gains arise purely from scheduling rather than additional computation.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, WavefrontDiffusion achieves 82.03% on GSM8K, 41.04% on MATH, 47.56% on HumanEval, 42.40% on MBPP, and 44.30% on BBH, consistently outperforming BlockDiffusion by +0.4 to +1.8 points across all five benchmarks. The same pattern holds for LLaDA-1.5 (e.g., 82.94% GSM8K, 48.17% HumanEval) and Dream-7B (e.g., 80.66% GSM8K, 54.27% HumanEval). All gains are achieved at identical computational cost (T=1024 steps, 2.517×10¹⁵ FLOPs), with wall-clock time differences within ±2%. On BERTScore (WikiText), WavefrontDiffusion reaches F1=0.8094, P=0.7749, R=0.8236, surpassing BlockDiffusion (F1=0.7946) and Standard Diffusion (F1=0.7885).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Standard Diffusion (MaskGIT-style global denoising), WavefrontDiffusion wins by large margins on every benchmark and model (e.g., +58.9 on GSM8K, +30.0 on HumanEval for LLaDA-8B), confirming that structured scheduling is essential. Against BlockDiffusion, the primary competitive baseline, WavefrontDiffusion achieves consistent but modest gains of +0.4 to +2.2 points (e.g., +1.27 on GSM8K, +1.83 on HumanEval for LLaDA-8B; +2.16 on MBPP for LLaDA-1.5), with identical FLOPs. In the extended comparison (Table 9, LLaDA-8B), WavefrontDiffusion also outperforms three advanced dynamic baselines: Truncated-BlockDiffusion (TBD) by +1.13 on GSM8K and +0.20 on MATH, Running Confidence Remasking (RCR) by +1.43 on GSM8K and +1.02 on MATH, and Self-Speculative Decoding (SSD) by +2.88 on GSM8K and +2.16 on MATH. The method's advantage is most pronounced on code generation (HumanEval: +1.83 over Block, +1.83 over TBD) and least on MATH (+0.42 over Block), suggesting the dynamic frontier helps most when semantic units have clear local structure. On BBH, WavefrontDiffusion shows mixed per-subset results (gains up to +0.112 on geometric shapes, losses up to −0.056 on snarks), but the aggregate still favors it over all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the restricted-search framework introduced by BlockDiffusion (Arriola et al., 2025), which showed that limiting the denoising search space to a constrained subset improves stability over global Standard Diffusion (MaskGIT-style, Chang et al., 2022). The key departure is replacing BlockDiffusion's static, pre-defined block boundaries with a dynamic wavefront that expands outward from finalized tokens, guided by an Information Gradient Hypothesis linking token entropy to distance from known context. The confidence-based top-k selection mechanism is inherited from MaskGIT, but the scheduling of *which* positions are eligible for selection is made adaptive. The paper also positions itself against other dynamic baselines (Truncated-BlockDiffusion, Running Confidence Remasking, Self-Speculative Decoding), arguing that aligning the update frontier with semantic dependencies is more effective than adaptive block sizing or post-hoc remasking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WavefrontDiffusion offers a training-free, compute-matched drop-in replacement for block-based decoding in diffusion language models, making it immediately deployable without retraining or additional hardware. The consistent gains across three model families and five benchmarks, combined with the calibration analysis showing ~40% ECE reduction, suggest that scheduling quality is an underexploited lever for DLM performance. However, the method's reliance on well-calibrated confidence scores remains a vulnerability, especially out-of-domain, and the zero-shot-only evaluation leaves open whether the approach transfers to few-shot or chain-of-thought settings. Extending the wavefront mechanism to multi-modal or structured domains (code ASTs, graphs) is a natural next step that the authors identify but do not pursue.
