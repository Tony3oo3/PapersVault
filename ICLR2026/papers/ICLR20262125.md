## Problem

Transportation-based similarity between GNN node embeddings is the state-of-the-art relevance measure for graph retrieval, but exact computation scales as O(n³) and no principled locality-sensitive hashing scheme exists for the asymmetric transportation distances (e.g., subgraph matching, GED with non-uniform costs) that arise in practice. The paper identifies a previously uncharacterized probabilistic symmetry—exchangeability of embedding dimensions under random initialization—that bridges the gap between costly high-dimensional transport and indexable Euclidean similarity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query graph G_q and a corpus of n-node graphs C (padded to equal size), along with a trained GNN that produces per-node embedding matrices X ∈ R^{n×D}. Rather than hashing raw embeddings, GRAPHASH operates on the order statistics (sorted columns) of each embedding dimension, constructing 4nM-dimensional Fourier-feature vectors T̂ from M frequency samples drawn from p(ω) ∝ |S(iω)|, where S is the Fourier transform of the scoring function. The output is a dim_h-bit binary hash code per graph per dimension (via random hyperplane sign projections), and at query time the top-b corpus graphs are returned from matching hash buckets across all D dimensions.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution is proving that under i.i.d. parameter initialization, permutation-invariant loss, and standard optimizers, the embedding dimensions of a trained GNN are exchangeable random variables (Theorem 5), meaning each dimension yields an identically distributed estimate of the full transportation similarity. This allows approximating the D-dimensional transportation distance by aggregating D independent 1-D Euclidean similarities between sorted embedding vectors (Proposition 7 provides concentration bounds). For LSH, the paper extends the Fourier-feature framework of Roy et al. (2023) to arbitrary (potentially asymmetric) scoring functions: sim_d is expressed as an integral of dot products of 4-dimensional Fourier vectors F(ιω_u), frequencies are sampled from the spectral density of the score, and the resulting finite-dimensional vectors are hashed via random hyperplane projections (Charikar-style), yielding a valid asymmetric LSH for the original transportation-based graph similarity.

## Results

GRAPHASH is evaluated on four TUDatasets (ptc-fr, ptc-fm, cox2, ptc-mr) under two asymmetric graph similarity tasks: Subgraph Matching (SM) and Graph Edit Distance (GED), with 500 query graphs and a corpus of 100,000 graphs each. Results are reported as MAP vs. number of retrieved graphs trade-off curves (Figure 4), where a horizontal red line marks 50% of exhaustive MAP. GRAPHASH consistently achieves the highest MAP at every retrieval budget across all eight dataset-task combinations, with the optimal hashcode size (dim_h) found to be approximately 10 bits (Figure 5). The exchangeability property is empirically validated via MMD² estimates of −3.89×10⁻⁵ (GED) and −1.18×10⁻⁶ (SM) on cox2, and the mean embedding matrix converges to rank one (σ₁²/Σσᵢ² → 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAPHASH outperforms all four baselines across all datasets and both tasks. FourierHashNet (Roy et al., 2023) is the strongest competitor but fails to span the full selectivity spectrum on SM tasks—most notably on ptc-fr and ptc-mr, where its MAP plateaus below 50% of exhaustive MAP. Random Hyperplanes (Charikar, 2002) performs reasonably on GED, occasionally matching GRAPHASH in MAP, but exhibits high variance at fixed selectivity levels; on SM tasks it performs worse than random, as cosine similarity over pooled vectors is ill-suited to asymmetric containment queries. IVF (FAISS-based ColBERT-style multi-vector indexing) and DiskANN (HNSW over node embeddings) both perform poorly because they rely on symmetric similarity metrics (L₂, cosine) that are incompatible with the asymmetric transport-based supervision used in graph retrieval. Random sampling yields substantially lower MAP than both GRAPHASH and FourierHashNet, confirming that the learned transport-based structure is non-trivially captured by the LSH framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the long line of work on algebraic weight-space symmetries in neural networks (Hecht-Nielsen 1990; Neyshabur et al. 2015b; Brea et al. 2019) by identifying a fundamentally different, *probabilistic* symmetry: exchangeability of embedding dimensions induced by random initialization and preserved through training. It directly extends Roy et al. (2023), who designed Fourier-domain LSH for hinge-based set containment, by generalizing the Fourier-feature construction to arbitrary (potentially asymmetric) graph similarity functions. The transportation-based relevance measures it builds upon (Roy et al. 2022; Jain et al. 2024; Zhuo et al. 2022) and the LSH framework (Charikar 2002; Indyk et al. 1998) are well-established, but the connection between exchangeability and per-dimension 1D reduction of optimal transport is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exchangeability theorem provides a principled justification for reducing high-dimensional transportation-based graph similarity to per-dimension sorted-vector Euclidean similarity, with a concentration bound (Proposition 7) guaranteeing the approximation quality. This opens the door to LSH for asymmetric transportation distances—a gap the authors explicitly note has remained unexplored. Limitations include the first-order (node-level, not edge-level) approximation, the restriction to permutation symmetry in weight space, and the fact that exchangeability does not imply i.i.d. as D→∞ in this setting. Open questions include whether the rank-1 collapse of expected embeddings has consequences for training dynamics, and whether the framework extends to richer similarity structures over 3D molecular or object representations.
