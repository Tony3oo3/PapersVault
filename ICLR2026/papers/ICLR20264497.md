## Problem

Full attention's quadratic complexity bottlenecks Video Diffusion Models (VDMs) from generating long-duration, high-resolution videos (e.g., 720p exceeding 76K tokens). Existing sparse attention methods are either training-free inference accelerators or, like MoBA, designed for 1D text locality and fail to capture the 3D spatio-temporal structure of video data, resulting in significant quality degradation when applied to VDM training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264497/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

VMoBA operates within a DiT-based video diffusion model (Wan 2.1 1.3B), taking a text prompt as input and producing a generated video. The attention mechanism processes video latents with spatio-temporal dimensions (T×H×W), where token sequences range from 13K (320×512) to 76K (720×1280) tokens. The method is applied natively during training (not just inference), with the first 25% of denoising steps retaining full attention for stability.

## Method

VMoBA adapts MoBA's block attention for video with three key modifications: (1) a layer-wise recurrent 1D-2D-3D key block partition that cycles through temporal, spatial, and spatio-temporal grouping across layers to match the diverse locality patterns observed in video DiTs; (2) global block selection that picks top-scoring key blocks from a pool aggregated across all query-key block products per attention head, rather than per-query top-k, to allocate sparsity toward the most salient interactions; and (3) threshold-based block selection that dynamically determines the number of attended blocks by accumulating normalized similarity scores until a threshold τ is exceeded, accommodating the varying concentration levels across heads. The implementation leverages FlashAttention for efficient training, and the method is trained natively on the Koala-36M dataset, achieving 2.92× FLOPs and 1.48× latency speedup while matching or exceeding full attention quality.

## Results

On spatially extended video (93×576×1024, 55K tokens), VMoBA achieves a VBench mean score of 68.34 vs. 68.25 for Full Attention while reducing training time from 276 to 187 GPU hours (1.48× speedup) and FLOPs by 2.49×. On temporally extended video (141×480×832, 56K tokens), VMoBA delivers a 1.44× training speedup and outperforms Full Attention by 3.3% in Image Quality (67.66% vs. 63.49%). In training-free inference at 76K tokens, VMoBA achieves 2.40× FLOPs reduction and 1.35× latency speedup over Full Attention while maintaining competitive quality scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Full Attention (Wan 2.1), VMoBA matches or exceeds overall quality (VBench mean 68.34 vs. 68.25 on spatially extended video) while cutting training time by 1.48×; on temporally extended video, VMoBA surpasses Full Attention by 3.3% in Image Quality. Against MoBA, VMoBA is dramatically superior on Dynamic Degree (56.91% vs. 5.80% for spatially extended; 31.36% vs. 11.97% for temporally extended), revealing that MoBA's 1D block partitioning fundamentally fails to capture spatio-temporal motion. Against the training-free baselines DiTFastAttn and SVG, VMoBA's trained model achieves higher quality scores than these methods applied to the same Full Attention checkpoint (e.g., 96.76% BGConsis vs. 88.59% for DiTFastAttn on spatially extended), and unlike those methods, VMoBA provides actual training acceleration rather than only inference-time savings. In the training-free inference setting (Table 1), VMoBA trails DiTFastAttn and SVG on PSNR similarity to Full Attention but leads on BGConsis and is competitive on TextConsis, while achieving 2.40× FLOPs speedup at 76K tokens—substantially better than MoBA's 2.73× FLOPs but 1.13× latency (MoBA is actually slower in wall-clock time due to its excessive block count from 1D partitioning).

## Relation to Prior Work

VMoBA is a direct adaptation of MoBA (Lu et al., 2025), a mixture-of-block sparse attention mechanism originally designed for long-context LLM training where locality is inherently 1D. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that video data exhibits 3D spatio-temporal locality, and MoBA's uniform 1D block partitioning fundamentally disrupts this structure, leading to catastrophic quality degradation (Dynamic Degree dropping to 5.80%). The paper also positions itself against the broader family of training-free sparse attention methods for video DiTs (DiTFastAttn, SVG, SpargeAttn), which accelerate inference but offer no benefit for training, and against linear attention alternatives (Mamba, RWKV) that cannot be seamlessly swapped with full attention layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VMoBA is the first sparse attention mechanism specifically designed for *training* video diffusion models on long sequences, filling a gap where prior sparse attention work for VDMs was exclusively inference-oriented. The practical impact is significant: 1.48× training speedup at 55K tokens enables fine-tuning on longer sequences that would be prohibitively expensive with full attention, while maintaining or slightly exceeding full-attention quality. Open questions include whether the FlashAttention-based implementation can be made more hardware-friendly for short sequences (where VMoBA is currently slower than full attention), whether the 1-2-3D recurrent partition generalizes to other video architectures beyond Wan 2.1, and whether the threshold-based selection strategy transfers to other modalities with different locality structures.
