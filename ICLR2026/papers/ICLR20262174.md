## Problem

Offline RL methods struggle with complex, long-horizon sequential decision making, and existing horizon-reduction strategies (n-step TD, hierarchical policies) either introduce bias without explicit goal conditioning or require expert-provided goals unavailable in practice. While action sequences offer a natural way to reduce the effective planning horizon, directly incorporating them into actor-critic frameworks causes severe value overestimation due to the expanded action space, a problem exacerbated by distribution shift in offline settings. The paper addresses how to leverage action sequences for principled horizon reduction in offline RL while avoiding value overestimation and maintaining compatibility with expressive policy architectures such as large-scale Vision-Language-Action models.

## Input / Output

The critic Q(s, a_t) takes a state s (proprioceptive state, or encoded camera views + language instruction for VLA experiments) and an H-step action sequence a_t = (a_t, ..., a_{t+H-1}) as input, producing a distributional Q-value over m=101 bins on a fixed support [v_min, v_max]. The value network V(s) conditions only on the state. H is set to 4 (cube tasks), 8 (scene/puzzle tasks), 16 (GR00T N1.5), or 50 (π₀) to match the native action chunk size of each policy. The final policy outputs H-step action sequences and is extracted via best-of-N sampling, where N candidates are scored by the trained critic and the highest-Q sequence is selected (or sampled via temperature-controlled softmax over Q-values).

## Method

DEAS treats each fixed-length H-step action sequence as a single decision unit, performing TD learning directly over the expanded action space A^H with a multi-step return R̃(s_t, a_t, γ₁) accumulated within the sequence and a bootstrap term γ₂^H V(s_{t+H}) across sequence-level decision points. To prevent the actor from exploiting critic approximation errors in the high-dimensional sequence space, the method employs detached value learning (IQL-style): the value network V is trained via expectile regression on the target critic Q̄ over in-distribution action sequences, and the critic Q is trained via MSE against R̃ + γ₂^H V(s_{t+H}), with no dependence on the actor's output. Both Q and V are modeled as categorical distributions over a fixed support using classification-based losses (HLG-style truncated normal targets) rather than scalar regression, reducing variance from accumulated multi-step returns. Two separate discount factors (γ₁ for within-sequence rewards, γ₂ for across-sequence bootstrapping) provide additional stability, and the framework is agnostic to the policy extraction method, enabling plug-and-play integration with any expressive policy including billion-parameter VLAs. The paper provides a theoretical proof (Appendix E) that the IQL convergence guarantees extend without modification when single actions are replaced by fixed-length action sequences.

## Results

On OGBench (30 tasks across 6 categories), DEAS achieves the highest success rate in every category, reaching 91±3% on puzzle-3x3, 82±6% on puzzle-4x4, and 64±8% on the most challenging cube-quadruple task, compared to 44±3%, 32±4%, and 17±8% for FQL respectively. On RoboCasa Kitchen, DEAS fine-tuned on GR00T N1.5 achieves 25.2% average success rate across 4 hard manipulation tasks, outperforming the base model (12.0%), Filtered BC (18.5%), IQL (20.2%), and QC (17.5%). In real-world pick-and-place experiments with a Franka Emika arm, DEAS reaches 78.4% average partial success rate versus 64.0% for the base model and 66.3% for IQL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEAS consistently outperforms FQL (Park et al., 2025c) across all 6 OGBench categories, with the largest gap on puzzle-3x3 (91% vs. 44%) and cube-quadruple (64% vs. 17%). N-step FQL (Park et al., 2025b) degrades relative to single-step FQL on most tasks due to increased bootstrap bias, while DEAS's action-sequence formulation achieves horizon reduction without this penalty. QC-FQL (Li et al., 2025b) is the closest competitor, matching or slightly exceeding DEAS on cube-triple (83% vs. 82%), but DEAS wins on all other categories, particularly on puzzle tasks where the gap reaches 29 points (91% vs. 62% on puzzle-3x3). CQN-AS (Seo & Abbeel, 2025) fails almost entirely on OGBench (0–2% success), attributed to cumulative discretization errors and strong BC regularization on suboptimal data. On RoboCasa Kitchen, DEAS with GR00T N1.5 (25.2% avg) surpasses Filtered BC (18.5%), IQL (20.2%), and QC (17.5%), and also improves over the π₀ backbone (21.8% vs. 12.3% base). In real-world experiments, DEAS (78.4%) substantially outperforms QC (39.6%), which actually degrades below the base model (64.0%), highlighting QC's instability with small datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEAS synthesizes three existing threads: IQL's detached value learning (Kostrikov et al., 2022), which avoids actor-critic coupling to prevent value overestimation; Q-Chunking's (Li et al., 2025b) formulation of TD learning over H-step action sequences for horizon reduction; and HLG's (Farebrother et al., 2024) classification-based distributional value learning for stability. The key departure from Q-Chunking is removing the actor from the critic update loop, and the key departure from CQN-AS (Seo & Abbeel, 2025) is retaining a flexible, expressive policy class rather than relying on iterative value discretization. The dual discount factor (γ₁ within sequences, γ₂ across sequence boundaries) is a practical addition not present in either parent method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEAS provides a simple, policy-agnostic recipe for applying offline RL to large-scale Vision-Language-Action models, demonstrated with GR00T N1.5 and π₀ on both simulation and real-robot tasks using as few as 5 expert demonstrations plus 25 rollouts. This is practically significant because it reduces reliance on expensive expert data collection for robot deployment. Open questions remain: the fixed action sequence length H is a hyperparameter that must be tuned per task, the method's sensitivity to distributional RL support ranges (v_min, v_max) limits cross-domain robustness, and scaling to unified multi-task value functions (hundreds of tasks) is unaddressed. The theoretical guarantee (Appendix E) that action-sequence IQL recovers the dataset-constrained optimal value function is reassuring but does not address generalization beyond the dataset support.
