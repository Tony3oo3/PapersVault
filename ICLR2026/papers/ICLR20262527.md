## Problem

Current satellite imagery segmentation models reliably extract entities defined by physical attributes (buildings, water bodies) but fail on socially defined categories (schools, parks, residential districts) whose boundaries are determined by social semantics rather than distinct visual cues. Existing multi-modal approaches that incorporate auxiliary geospatial data (e.g., POIs) suffer from data accessibility constraints, heterogeneous format alignment, and inability to generalize beyond a fixed set of predefined categories, leaving no versatile framework for open-ended socio-semantic segmentation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of co-registered images—a high-resolution satellite image and a rendered digital map (sourced from Amap's public API, showing roads and POI labels in Chinese or English)—along with a free-form textual instruction specifying the target socio-semantic entity (e.g., a specific university name, a class like 'college', or a function like 'educational'). The VLM emits structured JSON containing 2D bounding boxes and point coordinates, which are parsed and fed as prompts to a frozen SAM 2 model to produce the final pixel-level segmentation mask. The digital map representation is a key design choice: it replaces raw multi-modal geospatial data with a single visual modality that is inherently spatially aligned with the satellite imagery, eliminating complex preprocessing and data access issues.

## Method

SocioReasoner employs a two-stage render-and-refine reasoning strategy that mimics the human annotation workflow. In Stage 1 (localization), a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3b) generates bounding boxes from the dual satellite+map input, which are fed to SAM to produce a coarse mask; in Stage 2 (refinement), the boxes and coarse mask are rendered back onto both images as visual feedback, and the VLM then emits refined bounding boxes plus point prompts to correct boundary imprecision before a final SAM pass. Because this entire pipeline is non-differentiable, the authors optimize the VLM policy end-to-end using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), with a composite reward combining format validity, localization accuracy (Hungarian-matched box IoU in Stage 1, pixel IoU in Stage 2), and a length penalty that encourages exactly two refinement points. A single shared VLM policy handles both stages, and the two-stage RL updates are executed sequentially within each training step, allowing the model to learn iterative self-correction that single-stage VLM+SAM baselines cannot achieve.

## Results

On the SocioSeg test set, SocioReasoner achieves cIoU 47.9, gIoU 52.8, and F1 59.7 across all hierarchical tasks combined, outperforming the strongest baseline VisionReasoner (cIoU 44.0, gIoU 48.5, F1 54.3) by 3.9 cIoU points. On the most granular Socio-name task, the gap widens to 8.6 cIoU (52.6 vs. 44.0). On the out-of-domain OOD (New Region) dataset spanning five global cities, SocioReasoner (RL) achieves cIoU 40.2 and F1 42.9, substantially exceeding VisionReasoner (RL) at cIoU 32.8 and F1 35.0, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization to unseen map styles and geographic regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SocioReasoner outperforms all nine baselines across every hierarchical task and metric. Against the closest competitors—VisionReasoner (cIoU 44.0) and RemoteReasoner (cIoU 43.2)—the gains are 3.9 and 4.7 cIoU points respectively on the full dataset, with the advantage growing to 6.0 and 6.4 cIoU on the Socio-name task. Standard segmentation models (UNet, SegFormer) and open-vocabulary methods (SegEarth-OV) perform poorly because they cannot process the dual satellite+map input or handle socially defined categories lacking distinct visual cues. Among satellite-specific methods, SegEarth-R1 (cIoU 38.3) and RSRefSeg (cIoU 29.0) are limited by single-image input and fully supervised training on physical attributes. SAM-R1 (cIoU 22.5) underperforms due to unconstrained point prompt generation that degrades segmentation quality. On the OOD (New Region) benchmark, SocioReasoner (RL) leads all methods by a wide margin (cIoU 40.2 vs. 32.8 for VisionReasoner-RL), confirming that the RL-optimized two-stage reasoning generalizes effectively across map styles and continents.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM+SAM reasoning segmentation paradigm (VisionReasoner, Seg-R1, SAM-R1) from natural images to satellite imagery, but makes two key departures. First, it replaces the single-stage prompting pipeline with a two-stage render-and-refine mechanism that mimics human annotation: the VLM first localizes with bounding boxes, observes the coarse SAM mask rendered back onto the inputs, then refines with additional point prompts. Second, it sidesteps the multi-modal encoder architecture used in prior urban land-use work (Xiong et al., Zhang et al.) by rendering heterogeneous geospatial data (POIs, roads) into a unified digital map image, converting a cross-modal alignment problem into a visual reasoning task. The GRPO reinforcement learning objective from DeepSeek-R1/DeepSeekMath is repurposed to optimize this non-differentiable two-stage prompting policy end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SocioSeg benchmark (13,000+ samples, 5,000+ entity names, 90 classes, 10 functions) fills a genuine gap: no prior dataset targets entities defined by social rather than physical attributes, which are critical for urban planning, 15-minute-city analysis, and industrial AOI inference. The digital-map-as-visual-input paradigm is an elegant simplification that eliminates data access, alignment, and heterogeneity bottlenecks of prior multi-modal urban methods. Key open questions include the single-instance bias (the model converges to one dominant instance when multiple exist), the inference-time cost of the two-stage pipeline (~2.7s vs ~1.1s for single-stage baselines), and whether the approach scales to larger VLMs or denser urban environments where POI clutter degrades localization.
