## Problem

The Muon optimizer achieves superior data efficiency over AdamW for LLM pretraining, but its gradient orthogonalization step is not coordinate-wise and requires gathering full gradient matrices across devices under model parallelism (TP, FSDP), incurring a 5–10% per-iteration throughput penalty. The paper asks how to recover Muon's convergence quality while eliminating this per-step communication overhead so that the optimizer's wall-clock cost matches that of coordinate-wise methods like AdamW.

## Input / Output

On each device the method consumes a local gradient shard G_t^(m) (sized m×(n/c) under column-parallel TP or (m/r)×n under row-parallel TP), a local momentum buffer M_t^(m), and the local parameter shard X_t^(m). It produces an updated parameter shard X_{t+1}^(m). The block boundaries are fixed by the model-parallelism layout (TP degree, FSDP dim-0 slice), so no tensor redistribution is needed; the only cross-device traffic is a periodic all-gather/all-scatter of momentum shards every P steps to form the full matrix for global orthogonalization.

## Method

MuonBP alternates between (P−1) block-orthogonalization steps, where each device independently orthogonalizes its local momentum shard via Newton–Schulz iterations with no cross-device communication, and 1 full-orthogonalization step, where shards are gathered, the complete momentum matrix is orthogonalized, and the result is scattered back. A key theoretical contribution (Theorem 2) shows that two distinct stepsizes—η_full for the global step and η_block for the local steps—are essential: the optimal ratio η_block/η_full lies between 1 and 1/√(rc), and forcing a single stepsize degrades the convergence constant from a weighted harmonic mean to a weighted arithmetic mean of the smoothness constants. The convergence rate √(L̄_BP) smoothly interpolates between Muon (P=1, L̄_BP = L_op) and BlockMuon (P→∞, L̄_BP = L_B), with the period P acting as a tunable knob. Empirically, P=5 recovers Muon's validation perplexity while cutting optimizer-step communication volume by 5×, yielding an ~8% throughput gain at 8B scale with 8-way TP and ZeRO layer-wise sharding.

## Results

On the 8B-parameter Llama-style model with 8-way tensor parallelism and ZeRO layerwise sharding, MuonBP achieves 12.77 validation perplexity (11.59 training), outperforming baseline Muon (12.90/11.74), BlockMuon (13.68/12.62), and Adam (14.47/13.48). MuonBP also delivers ~8% higher throughput (113.37 vs. 105.09 TFLOP/s/GPU) compared to Muon at 8B scale, while matching BlockMuon's communication-free per-step cost. At the 160M scale with TP=2 and FSDP=4, MuonBP achieves the best validation loss (3.34) among all methods tested, with throughput (51.40 TFLOP/s/GPU) comparable to coordinate-wise AdamW (52.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuonBP consistently outperforms baseline Muon in both perplexity and throughput: at 8B scale it achieves 12.77 vs. 12.90 validation perplexity while running ~8% faster (113.37 vs. 105.09 TFLOP/s/GPU), and at 160M scale it reaches 3.34 vs. 3.36 validation loss. Against BlockMuon (P=∞), MuonBP is dramatically better, especially at larger learning rates where BlockMuon becomes unstable (24.68 vs. 12.97 validation perplexity at 8B with large lr), and even at smaller learning rates MuonBP maintains a clear advantage (12.77 vs. 13.68 at 8B). Compared to Adam/AdamW, MuonBP achieves substantially lower perplexity at all scales (e.g., 12.77 vs. 14.47 at 8B, 3.34 vs. 3.62 at 160M), confirming Muon's data-efficiency advantage is preserved. Against Dion, MuonBP achieves slightly better validation loss (3.34 vs. 3.37) and notably higher throughput (51.40 vs. 45.64 TFLOP/s/GPU) at the 160M scale, though the authors note this comparison is limited to small-scale experiments. The key practical takeaway is that MuonBP recovers Muon's convergence quality while eliminating the per-step all-gather communication that caused 5–10% throughput loss under model parallelism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Muon (Jordan et al., 2024b) by replacing its per-step full matrix orthogonalization with a block-periodic schedule: local shard orthogonalization on most steps, interleaved with occasional full orthogonalization. It sits between Muon (full orthogonalization every step) and BlockMuon (Boreiko et al., 2025, block-only), and is concurrent with Dion (Ahn et al., 2025), which distributes orthogonalization via low-rank momentum approximation. The theoretical analysis is grounded in the Non-Euclidean Trust Region framework of Kovalev (2025) and Bernstein & Newhouse (2024b), and the communication-efficiency motivation draws on the broader distributed optimization literature on intermittent communication and gradient compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8% throughput gain at 8B scale with no accuracy degradation translates directly into substantial cost savings for large-scale pretraining, and the method requires minimal hyperparameter changes (period P=5, two learning rates scaled by block dimensions). The convergence theorem cleanly shows that P interpolates between Muon and BlockMuon rates and that two distinct stepsizes are provably necessary, giving practitioners a principled tuning knob. Open questions include adaptive period scheduling during training, extension to expert parallelism, and understanding why MuonBP empirically outperforms Muon despite performing fewer full orthogonalizations (hypothesized regularization effect). Experiments are limited to 8B parameters, and the comparison with Dion was only conducted at 160M scale.
