## Problem

Fine-tuning vision-language models (VLMs) on robot teleoperation data to create vision-language-action (VLA) models causes catastrophic forgetting, where the VLM's foundational reasoning and multimodal understanding are overwritten by overfitting to the narrow robotics dataset. The paper identifies the root cause as a distribution mismatch between the VLM's internet-scale pretraining corpus and the low-level action space of robotic control, which forces practitioners to use full-parameter fine-tuning or expensive co-training schemes to retain general capabilities.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image observation and a natural language task instruction as input, and produces a three-stage autoregressive text output: a high-level subtask description, a mid-level directional motion plan (e.g., 'move left and slightly forward'), and a variable-length action chunk expressed as a Python list of [dx, dy, dz, gripper] tuples in the VLM's native text vocabulary. Actions are constrained to 4 translational degrees of freedom (no rotation), and generation uses nucleus sampling with stage-specific temperatures (0.1 for motion planning, 0.5 for action prediction). A separate external verifier (Gemini 2.5 Pro) determines whether to retry or advance to the next subtask after each action chunk is executed.

## Method

VLM2VLA resolves the distribution mismatch at the data level by re-representing robot actions as natural language text strings within the VLM's existing vocabulary, rather than mapping them to least-likely tokens or a separate action head. A Gemini 2.5-based pipeline automatically decomposes Bridgev2 teleoperation trajectories into hierarchical sub-trajectories, each annotated with a subtask, a coarse directional motion plan, and the corresponding action chunk, producing a language-annotated dataset D_lan. The Gemma-3-12B-IT backbone is then fine-tuned exclusively with LoRA (rank 16, alpha 32) on all linear modules using cross-entropy loss for one epoch (~300 GPU-hours on 4×A100), with no architectural modifications, no co-training on web-scale data, and no randomly initialized action decoder. At test time, the model generates all N subtasks from the initial observation, then iteratively produces motion plans and action chunks in a closed loop, with an external verifier gating subtask transitions. This data-centric alignment allows LoRA to adapt the VLM for control while preserving over 85% of the base model's VQA performance and enabling zero-shot generalization to multilingual instructions and open-world semantic reasoning tasks.

## Results

On VQA benchmarks, VLM2VLA (Gemma-3-12B-IT backbone) retains over 85% of the base model's performance, e.g., 42.7 vs. 46.0 on MMMU, 1391.7 vs. 1182.3 on MME, and 78.4 vs. 80.6 on DocVQA, while OpenVLA and ECoT (Prismatic-7B backbone) collapse to near-zero on most benchmarks (e.g., OpenVLA scores 0 on MMStar, MME, OCRBench, TextVQA, DocVQA, InfoVQA, AI2D, ChartQA, RealWorldQA). On real-world robotic manipulation (800+ trials on a WidowX 250S arm), VLM2VLA achieves a 60% success rate on the OOD 'Pick Up the Item Above Ash Ketchum' task (vs. 30% for the token-based ablation and near-zero for OpenVLA/ECoT), and significantly outperforms baselines on multilingual instruction following. The action-token ablation (VLM2VLA-AT) matches VLM2VLA on VQA but degrades substantially on OOD manipulation, confirming that the language-based action representation—not just LoRA—is critical for generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenVLA and ECoT (both Prismatic-7B backbone), VLM2VLA is competitive on in-distribution pick-and-place tasks but decisively outperforms both on out-of-distribution tasks: on the multilingual 'Pick Up the Carrot' task, VLM2VLA translates non-English instructions and identifies the correct object, while OpenVLA and ECoT rely on heuristics; on 'Pick Up the Item Above Ash Ketchum,' VLM2VLA achieves 60% success versus near-zero for both baselines. Relative to the Gemma-3-12B-IT base model, VLM2VLA retains over 85% of VQA performance (e.g., 42.7 vs. 46.0 MMMU, 78.4 vs. 80.6 DocVQA), whereas OpenVLA and ECoT suffer catastrophic forgetting (scoring 0 on most benchmarks relative to Prismatic VLM's 35.0 MMMU). Against the co-trained MolmoAct (7b), VLM2VLA outperforms on all reported VQA benchmarks (e.g., 42.7 vs. 28.4 MMMU, 48.0 vs. 1.2 MMStar, 58.3 vs. 55.9 ChartQA), despite MolmoAct's larger co-training data. Against π₀.₅ (3b, co-trained), VLM2VLA substantially outperforms across nearly all VQA benchmarks (e.g., 42.7 vs. 24.0 MMMU, 63.9 vs. 6.8 OCRBench, 78.4 vs. 4.6 DocVQA), demonstrating that co-training alone does not guarantee knowledge retention. The action-token ablation (VLM2VLA-AT) matches VLM2VLA on VQA scores but drops to 30% on the Ash Ketchum task (vs. 60%) and struggles with multilingual commands, isolating the language-based action representation as the key differentiator for downstream generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VLA training paradigms—discretizing continuous actions into least-likely tokens (RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA) or attaching separate action heads with diffusion/flow-matching (π₀, π₀.₅, MolmoAct)—by instead representing low-level robot actions as natural language text within the VLM's existing vocabulary. It builds on the parameter-efficient fine-tuning literature (LoRA) and the observation that catastrophic forgetting in VLAs stems from distribution mismatch between pretraining corpora and robot data, but resolves this mismatch at the data level rather than through architectural modifications or expensive co-training. The hierarchical subtask→motion-plan→action decomposition draws on embodied chain-of-thought (ECoT) and action-hierarchy work (RT-H), but the key distinction is that all three levels, including the lowest-level end-effector commands, are expressed as text rather than discrete tokens or continuous vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a simple data re-labeling step (actions as language) plus LoRA suffices to train a VLA that retains >85% of its VLM backbone's VQA performance and generalizes zero-shot to multilingual and open-world semantic tasks, this work offers a practical, low-cost alternative to the expensive co-training regimens currently required to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Key limitations temper the impact: inference latency (~6 s median per action cycle), restriction to translational DoFs on a single WidowX embodiment, reliance on an external Gemini verifier for subtask transitions, and evaluation limited to relatively simple pick-and-place tasks in a toy kitchen. Open questions include whether the approach scales to dexterous manipulation, cross-embodiment settings, and larger robotics datasets, and whether the verifier can be internalized into the policy itself.
