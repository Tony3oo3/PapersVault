## Problem

The standard Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron model assumes linear summation of synaptic inputs, yet neurophysiological evidence shows that dendrites integrate inputs nonlinearly, enabling computations such as XOR that a single LIF neuron cannot perform. No prior spiking neuron model has incorporated the bilinear dendritic integration rule observed in experiments, leaving a gap between biological plausibility and computational expressiveness in SNNs.

## Input / Output

The DLIF model accepts the same inputs as standard SNNs: static images (replicated T times to form temporal spike sequences), event-based DVS streams, Atari game frames, and spike-encoded audio. The key implementation detail is that the bilinear term s_i(t)s_j(t) is computed via a binary AND operation on spike trains, introducing no additional multiplications and preserving SNNs' event-driven efficiency. The bilinear coefficient matrix K is constrained to 90% sparsity (matching biological observations), with only ~10% of entries trainable, and is applied only to the first two blocks of ResNet/VGG or the first two encoder blocks of Transformer architectures.

## Method

The DLIF model augments the standard LIF input current w^T s(t) with a bilinear dendritic term s^T(t)Ks(t), where K is a symmetric matrix with zero diagonal representing pairwise dendritic interactions. Theoretically, the paper proves two results: (1) a single DLIF neuron can always find a K that separates two input classes with identical mean firing rates but different pairwise correlations, with the optimal K proportional to the correlation difference (C1−C2)/‖C1−C2‖_F; and (2) in a two-layer network, DLIF neurons strictly preserve and amplify input correlation structure more than LIF neurons, as measured by the Frobenius norm of the output correlation matrix difference. Numerically, a single DLIF neuron solves XOR while LIF fails, and the learned K matches the theoretical optimum. Across ResNet, VGG, and Transformer architectures trained with diverse paradigms (SLTT, STBP-tdBN, TET, ESL, TSER, FSTA, SSSA, Spike-driven Transformer, Meta-SpikeFormer), DLIF achieves 0.33–3.66% accuracy gains over LIF with only ~0.17 mJ (3.05%) energy overhead, and outperforms PLIF, GLIF, QIF, EIF, and DH-LIF on all benchmarks.

## Results

Across static benchmarks, DLIF-based SNNs achieve absolute accuracy gains of 0.33%–1.19% on CIFAR-10, 0.48%–3.66% on CIFAR-100, and 0.52%–3.07% on ImageNet over LIF baselines, with an average energy overhead of only 0.23 mJ (2.61% relative increase). On neuromorphic datasets, improvements reach 0.13%–1.36% on DVS-Gesture and 0.28%–3.08% on DVS-CIFAR10. In direct comparison with other advanced spiking neuron models (PLIF, GLIF, QIF, EIF), DLIF achieves the best accuracy on every dataset tested, e.g., 95.78% on CIFAR-10, 78.27% on CIFAR-100, 71.27% on ImageNet, 80.46% on DVS-CIFAR10, and 98.61% on DVS-Gesture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard LIF baseline, DLIF consistently wins across all 24 architecture–training-method configurations on CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet (Table 1), with the largest gain being +3.66% on CIFAR-100 (ESG/VGG-16: 73.52% vs 70.18%) and +3.07% on ImageNet (TET/ResNet-34: 67.32% vs 64.79%). Compared to PLIF (Fang et al., 2021), DLIF outperforms by 2.28% on CIFAR-10, 2.01% on ImageNet, 5.66% on DVS-CIFAR10, and 0.69% on DVS-Gesture. Against GLIF (Yao et al., 2022), DLIF leads by 0.75% on CIFAR-10, 0.92% on CIFAR-100, 2.18% on ImageNet, and 2.36% on DVS-CIFAR10. DLIF also surpasses QIF and EIF by 2.80–7.19% across all five datasets. Finally, against the multi-compartment DH-LIF model (Zheng et al., 2024), DLIF achieves higher accuracy on both spiking speech datasets: 92.71% vs 92.10% on SHD and 83.13% vs 82.46% on SSC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard LIF spiking neuron model by incorporating a bilinear dendritic integration rule first identified in neurophysiological experiments (Li et al., 2019, PNAS), which used a conductance-based formulation too complex for large-scale SNNs. The DLIF model recasts this rule as a current-based abstraction with a sparse symmetric matrix K, making it scalable while preserving the biological mechanism. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate literatures: bilinear pooling networks in ANNs (Lin et al., 2015; Gao et al., 2016) and dendritic computation in SNNs (Guerguiev et al., 2017; Zheng et al., 2024), while providing the first theoretical analysis showing that bilinear integration specifically preserves and propagates input correlation structures across layers—a property absent in prior bilinear ANN work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a biologically grounded, theoretically justified neuron model that consistently improves SNN accuracy across ResNet, VGG, and Transformer architectures on static, neuromorphic, and RL benchmarks with only ~3% energy overhead, making it a practical drop-in replacement for LIF in existing SNN pipelines. The AND-gate implementability of the bilinear term (s_i·s_j) is particularly attractive for neuromorphic hardware where multiplications are costly. Open questions include whether the 90% random-sparse parameterization of K can be replaced by richer biologically motivated structures, how DLIF performs in NLP/LLM settings, and whether the model can be efficiently mapped onto existing neuromorphic chips (Loihi, Tianjic) to realize its energy advantages in practice.
