## Problem

Fixed-budget pure exploration in MDPs—where the learner receives a fixed number of episodes and must return a near-optimal policy without specifying target accuracy ε or failure rate δ—remained largely unexplored despite being well-studied in multi-armed bandits. The core difficulty is that standard PAC/fixed-confidence techniques rely on prespecified ε and δ for concentration bounds, and converting them to fixed-budget settings requires knowledge of instance-dependent quantities that are unavailable in advance. This paper provides the first instance-dependent ε-uniform guarantee for fixed-budget policy identification in episodic MDPs, meaning the failure probability is bounded simultaneously for all ε above a budget-dependent threshold.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes only the episode budget B as input (no ε, no δ, no knowledge of gaps or reachability) and operates on a finite-horizon episodic MDP with S states, A actions, and horizon H. After exactly B episodes, it outputs a single deterministic policy π̂ = (π̂₁, …, π̂_H). The guarantee is ε-uniform: the probability that V₀* − V₀^{π̂} > ε is bounded by an exponentially decaying function of B and instance-dependent hardness quantities, simultaneously for all ε ≥ 2SH²ε_B where ε_B is a budget-dependent threshold. A variant that accepts ε as an additional input provides a refined guarantee with an extra term depending on the number of near-optimal actions.

## Method

BREA (Backward Reachability Estimation and Action elimination) proceeds backward from step H to 1, combining two components at each step. First, FB-L2E performs reward-free exploration by resetting rewards to indicator functions of target state-action pairs and running STRONGEULER, estimating the reachability W_h(s) for each state via a multi-scale elimination procedure that partitions state-action pairs into sets of geometrically decreasing reachability. Second, the SAR (Successive Accepts and Rejects) multiple-bandit algorithm is applied to eliminate suboptimal actions within each reachability group, adaptively allocating the remaining budget across bandit instances based on empirical gap rankings. The key analytical insight is that Proposition 1 (Wagenmaker et al., 2022) permits using approximate future policies for trajectory generation, enabling backward induction without knowing the optimal policy. The paper also establishes, for the first time, an ε-uniform correctness guarantee for SAR in the multiple-bandit setting, which reduces the state-count dependence compared to applying a single-arm bandit algorithm independently at each state.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 3.3) establishes an ε-uniform failure probability bound for the BREA algorithm: for any ε ≥ 2SH²ε_B, the probability that the returned policy is not ε-optimal decays as exp(−Θ̃(εB/C_L2E(B/2SH))) + exp(−Θ̃(B/(H⁵ max_h C_h² Σ_s W_h(s)⁻¹ Σ_a (Δ̄_h(s,a) ∨ ε/W_h(s))⁻²))). Under the additional assumption of unique optimal actions (Corollary 3), this yields a best-policy identification guarantee with failure probability exp(−Θ̃(ε*B/C_L2E)) + exp(−Θ̃(B/(H³ max_h Σ_s W_h(s)⁻¹ Σ_a Δ̄_h(s,a)⁻²))). The derived sample complexity (Remark 4) is τ_{ε,δ} = Θ̃(poly(S,A,H)/ε + H⁵ max_h C_h² Σ_s W_h(s)⁻¹ Σ_a (Δ̄_h(s,a) ∨ ε/W_h(s))⁻²) · log(1/δ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. However, the paper positions its sample complexity relative to existing theoretical results. For the multi-armed bandit special case (S=H=1), the bound reduces to Σ_a 1/(Δ(a)∨ε)², matching the known instance-dependent rates from Even-Dar et al. (2006), Audibert et al. (2010), and Karnin et al. (2013). Compared to PAC RL algorithms (Wagenmaker et al. 2022; Wagenmaker and Jamieson 2022; Tirinzoni et al. 2023), BREA's sample complexity has an H⁵ max_h dependence rather than the H⁴ Σ_h dependence, which the authors attribute to the inherent difficulty of budget allocation in the fixed-budget setting. The modified algorithm with target accuracy (Theorem 3.4, Remark 6) achieves a sample complexity closely aligned with MOCA (Wagenmaker et al. 2022), differing mainly in the H⁵ max_h vs. H⁴ Σ_h terms and the |OPT_h(3ε)|/ε² refinement term. The key distinction is that BREA's guarantee is ε-uniform (simultaneous for all ε above a threshold) rather than for a single prespecified ε, and it requires only the budget B as input rather than both ε and δ.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges the fixed-budget pure exploration setting, well-studied for multi-armed bandits (Even-Dar et al., 2006; Bubeck et al., 2009), to episodic MDPs, where prior work has focused almost exclusively on the fixed-confidence (PAC) formulation. The algorithmic core adapts three existing components: the Learn2Explore reward-free exploration procedure (Wagenmaker et al., 2022) is reworked into a fixed-budget variant (FB-L2E), the SAR multiple-bandit elimination algorithm (Bubeck et al., 2013) is given its first ε-uniform correctness guarantee, and STRONGEULER (Simchowitz and Jamieson, 2019) provides the underlying trajectory generation. The key conceptual departure from PAC RL is the introduction of an ε-uniform guarantee—bounding the failure probability simultaneously for all ε above a budget-dependent threshold—rather than requiring ε and δ as algorithm inputs, which eliminates the verification step inherent in fixed-confidence algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first instance-dependent fixed-budget pure exploration result for MDPs, opening a setting that is arguably more practical than PAC RL since the user controls the budget explicitly and no verification hyperparameters are needed. The ε-uniform guarantee framework, which characterizes the tail distribution of suboptimality, may prove useful beyond this specific algorithm. Open questions highlighted by the authors include whether instance-dependent acceleration (analogous to bandit results by Katz-Samuels and Jamieson, 2020; Zhao et al., 2023) and data-poor regime guarantees can be established for MDPs, whether the H⁵ max_h dependence (compared to H⁴ sum_h in PAC RL) can be tightened via variance-dependent concentration, and how the approach extends to function approximation. The deterministic sample complexity (as opposed to the probabilistic guarantees typical of PAC RL) is a notable structural difference that may simplify practical deployment.
