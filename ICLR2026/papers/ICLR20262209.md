## Problem

Existing equivariant neural networks are rigidly tied to a single symmetry group chosen a priori, requiring bespoke architectural designs for each new symmetry and preventing knowledge transfer across domains with different symmetries. This inflexibility blocks the development of unified, multi-modal equivariant foundation models that can process diverse structured data (graphs, sequences, images) under varying group actions within a single architecture.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard data input x (graph node features in R^{n×d}, sequence tokens, or image pixel grids) together with a fixed, task-specific symmetry-breaking auxiliary input v consisting of positional features A^(1) ∈ R^n and/or edge features A^(2) ∈ R^{n×n} whose automorphism group equals the target subgroup G. The output is a G-equivariant node representation or an invariant scalar prediction (e.g., 3D joint positions, traffic flow, classification labels). The symmetry-breaking input v is precomputed via Algorithm 1 (scaling as O(rn²) in nodes and generators) and is not learned from data; instead, a learnable EdgeEmbedder module assigns trainable embeddings to the discrete edge-orbit labels encoded in v.

## Method

ASEN starts with a fully permutation-equivariant base model h_θ (a GATv2-based GNN equivariant to S_n) and augments its input with a symmetry-breaking feature v whose automorphism group is the desired subgroup G, yielding f_θ(x) = h_θ(x, v) that is provably G-equivariant but not equivariant to S_n \ G. Computing v with exact automorphism group G may require hypergraph tensors of order up to n, so the paper relaxes to the 2-closure G^(2) and constructs edge features A^(2) encoding the G-orbit partition on node pairs via a SymPy-based orbit computation (Algorithm 1), giving Aut(A^(2)) = G^(2) ≈ G. Theoretically, the paper proves exact subgroup equivariance under injectivity conditions on MPNN layers (Prop. 1, Lemma 1), shows ASEN can simulate order-1 equivariant MLPs to arbitrary accuracy (Thm. 1), and inherits universality from the base model (Thm. 2). Empirically, a single ASEN backbone with task-specific EdgeEmbedder and TokenEmbedder modules is applied to symmetry selection (human pose, traffic, Pathfinder), multitask learning, and transfer learning on synthetic sequence tasks, demonstrating that one architecture can flexibly model multiple permutation subgroups.

## Results

On traffic flow prediction (METR-LA), ASEN with sparse graph and subgroup S_{n1}·S_{n2} achieves MAE 2.69, outperforming DCRNN with full S_n symmetry (MAE 2.77) by 0.08. On human pose estimation (Human3.6M), the best ASEN configuration (sparse edges, trivial group I) achieves P-MPJPE 33.39, while the weakly sparse variant with S_2 reflection symmetry reaches 38.80. On Pathfinder-64, 2D positional encoding with local permutation symmetry (S_9)^{455} achieves 82.7% test accuracy versus 65.6% for standard 1D positional encoding, with fewer parameters (111.2k vs. 370.5k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is DCRNN (Li et al., 2018), a diffusion convolutional recurrent network with full permutation symmetry S_n, which achieves MAE 2.77 on METR-LA traffic prediction. ASEN with the smaller subgroup S_{n1}·S_{n2} (two highway branches) and sparse edges achieves MAE 2.69, a 0.08 improvement over DCRNN, demonstrating that exploiting the correct subgroup symmetry outperforms full permutation equivariance. ASEN with the finer 9-cluster grouping S_{n1}·…·S_{n9} matches DCRNN (2.77 sparse) but does not surpass it, suggesting the two-branch structure is the more informative symmetry for this dataset. In the synthetic sequence experiments, ASEN with the correct equivariant group consistently outperforms its non-equivariant (trivial symmetry) counterpart across all tasks, converging faster and to lower loss (Fig. 4). The paper also shows that ASEN's single unified architecture reproduces results previously requiring multiple distinct equivariant MLPs (as in Huang et al., 2023) for human pose estimation, matching their reported P-MPJPE values across all tested symmetry groups.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASEN extends the recent line of subgroup-equivariant network design (Blum-Smith et al. 2025, Ashman et al. 2024, Lim et al. 2024) that augments a base equivariant model with an auxiliary symmetry-breaking input, but generalizes from single-task to multitask and transfer learning settings where one architecture must handle multiple distinct subgroups. The practical computation of symmetry-breaking objects via the 2-closure group (Ponomarenko and Vasil'ev 2020) is a key technical contribution that makes the framework tractable for permutation subgroups, connecting group-theoretic orbit structure to learnable edge features in GNNs. The paper also positions itself against approximate equivariance methods (Wang et al. 2022, Huang et al. 2023) by showing that the 2-closure approximation provides a principled, group-theoretic relaxation rather than a learned soft constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ASEN provides a concrete recipe toward equivariant foundation models—a single architecture that can be reconfigured for different symmetry groups by swapping a lightweight symmetry-breaking input—addressing a key limitation of current equivariant architectures that must be redesigned per domain. The negative result showing complete failure when the 2-closure strictly contains the target group (alternating group example) is an important practical caveat: the approximation is not always benign, and practitioners must verify G = G^(2) for their target symmetry. Open questions include extending to local (non-global) symmetries for molecular graphs, input-dependent symmetry breaking, and whether the framework scales to the multi-physics pretraining setting where many distinct symmetry groups must coexist.
