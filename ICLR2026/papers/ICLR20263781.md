## Problem

Modern visual generative models excel at realistic appearance but struggle with complex text instructions and real-world physical constraints, while integrating knowledge from heterogeneous sources (VLMs, physics simulators, multiple generative models) into a single model is prohibitively expensive. Existing compositional generative methods typically combine only homogeneous generator experts or require application-specific designs, leaving a gap for a general, training-free framework that can jointly compose arbitrary mixtures of generative and discriminative models at inference time.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts task-dependent inputs at varying granularities: high-level text prompts, low-level 3D asset poses from graphics engines, physics simulator renderings (depth maps, trajectory maps, RGB overlays), and object bounding boxes with paired regional text descriptions. Outputs are photorealistic images (960×1664 or 1024×1024) or 81-frame videos (480×832 or 512×768) that simultaneously satisfy all specified constraints. A key design choice is that each generative expert can be restricted to a spatial or spatio-temporal region via a Gaussian-blurred foreground mask, allowing different experts to govern different parts of the scene.

## Method

The paper formulates controllable visual synthesis as sampling from a Product-of-Experts (PoE) distribution over the output, where generative experts (flow/diffusion or autoregressive models) contribute probability factors and discriminative experts (VLMs like VQAScore) contribute Boltzmann-weighted reward factors. To sample from this intractable product, the method constructs an annealing schedule of T intermediate distributions interpolating from a Gaussian prior to the target product, and at each step runs MCMC (Langevin dynamics for flow models, Gibbs sampling for autoregressive models) under the composed score of all generative experts. Parallel Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) maintains L particles across the annealing schedule, resampling at selected timesteps based on discriminative expert scores to improve coverage of the product distribution's modes. A conditional sampling mechanism (Eq. 6) makes each generative expert's velocity prediction depend on parent experts' predictions, reducing multi-modality and improving visual harmonization. The entire framework is training-free, requiring only black-box access to pre-trained models and their score/velocity predictions.

## Results

On the graphics-engine-instructed image editing task, the method achieves 0.827 GPT-4o controllability and 0.065 LPIPS(fg) fidelity, substantially outperforming RF-Solver (0.518 and 0.178 respectively). On the character insertion benchmark (Magic Insert dataset), the method attains 0.818 GPT-4o controllability, 0.763 GPT-4o text alignment, and 1.711 ImageReward, surpassing all four baselines (Magic Insert, Add-it, FLUX-Fill, SDEdit) on most metrics. For physics-simulator-instructed video generation, the method achieves 0.842 GPT-4o video quality and 0.817 GPT-4o text alignment, outperforming monolithic depth-to-video and trajectory-to-video baselines. On layout-controlled text-to-image generation (MIG-Bench), the method with L=32 particles reaches 0.728 mIoU and 0.904 VQAScore, exceeding 3DIS-FLUX (0.673 and 0.800).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RF-Solver on graphics-engine-instructed editing, the method wins on every metric, most dramatically on GPT-4o text alignment (0.817 vs. 0.438) and LPIPS foreground fidelity (0.065 vs. 0.178), confirming that text-prompt-only conditioning cannot match explicit pose specifications. On the Magic Insert character insertion benchmark, the method outperforms Magic Insert, Add-it, and FLUX-Fill on GPT-4o controllability (0.818 vs. 0.743/0.763/0.655) and ImageReward (1.711 vs. 1.169/1.078/0.828), though FLUX-Fill preserves background better (MSE 0.058 vs. 0.365) and SDEdit achieves lower LPIPS(fg) (0.026 vs. 0.064) and higher GPT-4o quality (0.871 vs. 0.769). For physics-simulator-instructed video generation, the method surpasses Depth2V on GPT-4o quality (0.842 vs. 0.750) and text alignment (0.817 vs. 0.775) but trails it on foreground IoU (0.739 vs. 0.787), while decisively beating Traj2V and Image2V on most quality metrics. On full-scene PhysGen3D simulation, the method improves GPT-4o quality (0.763 vs. 0.513 for PhysGen3D) and text alignment (0.825 vs. 0.588). On MIG-Bench layout-controlled generation, the method with L=32 particles exceeds 3DIS-FLUX on all three metrics (mIoU 0.728 vs. 0.673, VQAScore 0.904 vs. 0.800, VQAScore-R 0.881 vs. 0.808), demonstrating that the general PoE framework matches or surpasses a purpose-built application-specific method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the Product-of-Experts (PoE) formulation (Hinton, 1999) with Annealed Importance Sampling (Neal, 2001) and Sequential Monte Carlo (Doucet et al., 2001) to create a general sampler for heterogeneous expert compositions. It extends the compositional generative modeling line (Du et al., 2020, 2023; Li et al., 2022), which typically combined homogeneous diffusion experts or a single generator with discriminative rewards, by supporting autoregressive, flow-based, and discriminative experts simultaneously. The key methodological departure from concurrent SMC-based reward steering work (Wu et al., 2023; Skreta et al., 2025; He et al., 2025) is the use of per-timestep MCMC kernels that leave each annealed distribution invariant, avoiding the weight degeneracy that plagues path-wise importance weighting as sequence length grows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of the framework is practically significant: users can compose any combination of pre-trained generative models, VLMs, and physics simulators at inference time without retraining, enabling flexible control interfaces (text, pose, trajectory, layout) that adapt to application needs. However, the method incurs substantial extra compute (4–30 min per sample vs. seconds for feedforward inference), relies on expert predictions being reasonably compatible, and has only been validated with 2–3 experts per task. Open questions include scaling to many experts, handling strongly contradictory constraints, and whether the MCMC mixing assumptions hold for very high-dimensional video generation.
