## Problem

Existing generalist graph anomaly detection models (e.g., ARC, UNPrompt) fail to properly align heterogeneous feature spaces across domains, lack mechanisms for continual knowledge accumulation, and often assume access to labeled nodes in the target graph. The core gap is that standard preprocessing (PCA/SVD + normalization) either separates graphs in embedding space or distorts critical structural patterns (e.g., reversing Normal-Normal vs. Normal-Anomaly distance relationships), making zero-shot cross-domain detection unreliable.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unseen graph with node features of arbitrary dimensionality and an adjacency matrix, plus a pre-built pattern dictionary (n_sup=2000 patterns per training graph) stored from prior training. Features are first projected to a common dimension via PCA, then normalized using pairwise distance statistics computed over training graphs. The output is a per-node anomaly score computed as the squared L2 reconstruction error between the original and dictionary-reconstructed attribute-level and structure-level embeddings, with no labeled data required from the test graph.

## Method

OWLEYE comprises three components: (1) a cross-domain feature alignment module that normalizes node features using a scaling factor derived from the median pairwise distance ratio across training graphs, preserving domain-specific structural patterns while unifying distributions; (2) a multi-domain pattern dictionary that stores attribute-level representations (from a 3-layer GCN on real features) and structure-level representations (from a parallel GCN initialized with all-ones) extracted from each training graph, with cross-graph similarity computed solely on structure-level embeddings to avoid camouflaged anomalies; (3) a truncated attention-based reconstruction module that computes attention between query nodes and dictionary patterns, masks the top-k least representative patterns to −∞ before softmax, and reconstructs node embeddings as a similarity-weighted combination of surviving patterns. Training minimizes a combined cosine-similarity reconstruction loss and triplet loss on labeled training graphs; inference is fully zero-shot, requiring only pattern extraction and reconstruction on the unseen graph.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting (Table 1), OWLEYE achieves an average AUPRC of 36.17% across 8 test datasets, outperforming the best generalist baseline ARC (30.74%) by 5.4 percentage points. OWLEYE also surpasses the best supervised method GHRN (12.80% AUPRC with 10-shot labels) by over 23 points, and the best unsupervised single-graph method CARE (28.72%) by 7.5 points. In the 10-shot setting (Table 2), OWLEYE further improves to 36.73% average AUPRC, maintaining its lead over ARC (31.68%) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OWLEYE outperforms the strongest generalist baseline ARC on 6 out of 8 test datasets in zero-shot AUPRC, with the largest margin on Amazon (62.20 vs. 20.48, a 41.7-point gap) and consistent gains on Flickr (+2.56), BlogCatalog (+1.56), Facebook (+1.37), and Reddit (+0.05); ARC retains the edge on Cora (45.20 vs. 43.94) and Weibo (64.18 vs. 60.90). Against the best unsupervised single-graph method CARE, OWLEYE leads on 7 of 8 datasets, most dramatically on Amazon (+5.44) and Weibo (+20.20). The supervised baselines BWGNN and GHRN, despite receiving 10 labeled nodes per test graph, are outperformed by OWLEYE (which uses zero labels) on 6 of 8 datasets in AUPRC. UNPrompt, the other generalist baseline, is consistently the weakest one-for-all method (12.82% avg AUPRC), trailing OWLEYE by over 23 points. In the 10-shot setting (Table 2), OWLEYE's advantage over ARC narrows slightly (36.73 vs. 31.68) but remains the best overall, and it surpasses both supervised methods on 6/8 datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

OWLEYE extends the 'one-for-all' generalist graph anomaly detection line initiated by ARC (Liu et al., 2024) and UNPrompt (Niu et al., 2024), which introduced in-context learning and neighborhood prompts for zero-shot GAD. It departs from these methods in three key ways: (1) replacing their PCA/SVD-based feature preprocessing with a pairwise-distance-preserving cross-domain normalization that maintains structural patterns (e.g., Normal-Normal vs. Normal-Anomaly density relationships), (2) introducing a multi-domain pattern dictionary that enables plug-and-play continual learning without retraining, and (3) designing a truncated attention mechanism that filters out potentially anomalous pseudo-support nodes during reconstruction, removing the need for any labeled data in the test graph. The dictionary-learning and truncated-attention components draw on ideas from sparse coding and one-class classification (TAM, Qiao & Pang 2023) but repurpose them for cross-domain transfer rather than single-graph detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot, label-free cross-domain GAD setting addresses a practical bottleneck: in real deployments (fraud detection, intrusion detection), obtaining labeled anomalies in a new domain is expensive and requires domain expertise. OWLEYE's dictionary-based design offers a concrete path toward continuously evolving anomaly detectors that absorb knowledge from new graphs without retraining, which is a meaningful step toward graph foundation models. However, performance on certain domains (Facebook, Reddit) remains very low (AUPRC ~4–6%) across all methods, suggesting that some graph structures or anomaly types are fundamentally harder to detect cross-domain. Open questions include scalability to very large graphs (the dictionary grows linearly), handling of truly novel anomaly patterns absent from all training graphs, and whether the approach extends to edge-level or subgraph-level anomaly detection.
