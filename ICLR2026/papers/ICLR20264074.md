## Problem

Model-level explanations for GNNs are typically evaluated solely by the target class score, but high-scoring motifs can be pathological, off-distribution, or fail to capture the full diversity of patterns the classifier relies on. No principled, theoretically grounded framework exists to assess whether an explanation set genuinely reflects the classifier's reasoning, leaving comparisons vulnerable to cherry-picking and qualitative inspection.

## Input / Output

The framework operates entirely in the GNN's embedding space: it takes the classifier's embedding function φ, the embeddings of K explanation motifs, the embeddings of all target-class graphs, and the Lipschitz constant L of the classifier head (computed via spectral norm for linear heads or upper-bounded by product of spectral norms for MLPs). It outputs three scores—Coverage, GGA, and Overlap—each with Hoeffding confidence intervals at a chosen significance level, plus a certified distribution-free upper bound on sufficiency risk at the optimal radius r* = 1/(2L). Because all metrics are defined through neighborhood relations in embedding space, they are invariant to uniform rescaling and affine gain transformations of the embedding.

## Method

The paper introduces sufficiency risk SR_c(M, E_c) as the mean squared error between the classifier score and its conditional expectation given a membership code, then proves (Theorem 1) that any proxy membership's risk upper-bounds the true (unobservable) membership's risk. Building on this, three metrics are defined: Coverage measures the fraction of class-c graphs within radius r* of the nearest motif; GGA is the normalized area under the greedy set-cover curve, capturing how efficiently motifs accumulate coverage; and Overlap quantifies redundant coverage across motifs. Theorem 2 provides a distribution-free certificate bounding sufficiency risk in terms of Coverage and the Lipschitz constant, and Theorem 3 shows the bound is tightest at the universal radius r* = 1/(2L), independent of the distance distribution. Finite-sample Hoeffding concentration bounds yield confidence intervals for Coverage and GGA, enabling statistically reliable comparisons between explainers. A scale-invariant angular-distance formulation (Theorem 5) extends the certificate to normalized embeddings, with optimal angular radius r*_θ = 2 arcsin(min{1, 1/(4L)}).

## Results

On the synthetic 4Shapes dataset, good explanation sets achieve Coverage of 0.85–0.95 while bad (random BA) sets drop to 0.10–0.30, despite both receiving similar or even higher class scores for Class 2 (where bad sets score 0.97 vs. good sets at 0.93). On real datasets, XGNN and PAGE achieve Coverage of 0.77–0.89 on MUTAG versus GNNInterpreter's 0.41–0.60, while GNNInterpreter leads on Reddit-Binary (0.864 vs. PAGE's 0.799 for Class 0). On OGB-MOLHIV, no explainer exceeds 0.5 Coverage on the majority non-HIV class, and GGA values remain close to Coverage, indicating stagnation. The meta-measure analysis over 1,000 random explanation sets confirms the three metrics (Coverage, GGA, Overlap) explore a broad feasible region without collapsing to a single deterministic relationship.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three state-of-the-art model-level explainers—XGNN (RL-based generation), GNNInterpreter (probabilistic generative model), and PAGE (prototype-based discovery)—using the proposed Coverage, GGA, and Overlap metrics alongside class score. On MUTAG, XGNN and PAGE achieve comparable Coverage (0.77–0.89) while GNNInterpreter lags significantly (0.41–0.60) despite achieving the highest class scores (0.987–1.000), revealing that GNNInterpreter's disconnected, chemically invalid motifs are off-manifold. On Reddit-Binary, GNNInterpreter outperforms PAGE in Coverage (0.864 vs. 0.799 for Class 0) and class score, likely because PAGE's subgraph search fails on large graphs (~430 nodes). XGNN completely fails on both Reddit-Binary and IMDB-Multi, producing trivial single-node or line structures with zero Coverage and GGA, and cannot run on OGB-MOLHIV due to its discrete-feature requirement. On IMDB-Multi, all three methods achieve high Coverage (0.91–1.00) and GGA, with PAGE attaining the highest class scores (0.855–0.955), indicating that few motifs suffice for this dataset. On OGB-MOLHIV, no method exceeds 0.5 Coverage on the majority non-HIV class, and the paper attributes this to classifier memorization rather than learned discriminative patterns (67% of minority-class graphs flip labels after removing a single random node).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the evaluation of model-level GNN explanations by introducing a formal sufficiency-risk criterion and three certified metrics (Coverage, GGA, Overlap), where prior work relied almost exclusively on target class score and qualitative inspection. Conceptually, it repurposes tools from statistical sufficiency theory, Lipschitz analysis, and greedy set cover to the GNN explanation setting, while drawing on the observation (noted by Wang & Shen 2023, Chen et al. 2023, and Saha & Bandyopadhyay) that high-scoring motifs can be pathological or suffer from mode collapse. Unlike instance-level explanation evaluation, which has a rich literature on fidelity and faithfulness metrics, no principled quantitative framework existed for model-level explanations; this paper fills that gap by showing that standard instance-level metrics (fidelity, accuracy) are inapplicable when explanations are generated rather than extracted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretically grounded evaluation protocol for model-level GNN explanations, providing distribution-free certificates and finite-sample confidence intervals that enable statistically reliable comparison between explainers—something the field has lacked. The practical impact is significant: practitioners can now diagnose off-manifold motifs, mode collapse, and redundancy that class score alone conceals, and the stopping criterion from GGA stagnation offers a principled way to decide when to terminate explanation generation. Limitations include the dependence on the Lipschitz constant of the classifier head (tractable for linear heads but requiring upper-bound estimation for deeper MLPs), the assumption that the proxy nearest-motif membership approximates the true (unobservable) membership, and the fact that all metrics operate in the embedding space rather than directly on graph structure. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-class joint explanations, how it behaves under distribution shift, and whether the angular formulation fully resolves scale-sensitivity in practice for very deep encoders.
