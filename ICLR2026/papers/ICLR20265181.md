## Problem

Existing multimodal anomaly detection methods degrade severely in few-shot settings where only a handful of normal samples per class are available. Cross-modal alignment approaches learn only narrow correspondences from scarce data, while memory-bank methods misclassify unseen yet normal variations as anomalies. PIRN addresses this by replacing dense alignment or large memory banks with a compact, adaptive prototype codebook that enforces an information bottleneck on normal patterns.

## Input / Output

PIRN takes an RGB image and a corresponding surface-normal map (generated from 3D point clouds via local plane fitting with k=30 KD-tree neighbors and directional coherence enforcement, following FIND's procedure) as input at 224×224 resolution. A frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 encoder extracts multi-scale patch tokens from layers 2–10, aggregated by element-wise averaging into a single N×C feature map per modality. The output is a fused anomaly heatmap (patch-level cosine-distance scores upsampled to input resolution and summed across the RGB and surface-normal branches) and an image-level anomaly score taken as the maximum value in the fused map.

## Method

PIRN is the first MAD framework to integrate a vector-quantized prototype codebook (K=10 per modality) into a cascaded ViT decoder (L=2 layers). Each decoder layer performs three sequential operations: (1) Adaptive Prototype Refinement (APR) computes an OT-weighted context vector per prototype from the previous layer's patch tokens and updates prototypes via a GRU whose gating mechanism suppresses anomalous context; (2) Balanced Prototype Assignment (BPA) solves a balanced optimal transport problem (Sinkhorn with entropic regularization) to assign each patch token to prototypes under equal-mass constraints, preventing codebook collapse and reconstructing tokens as OT-weighted prototype combinations; (3) Multimodal Normality Communication (MNC) first aligns the two modalities' prototypes via a multi-head GAT on a 2K-node KNN graph, then injects cross-modal normality by using purified patch tokens (reweighted by intra-modal BPA output through sigmoid) as queries attending to the other modality's refined prototypes as keys/values, modulated by a learnable tanh gate. Training minimizes cosine distance between frozen encoder features and the final reconstructed features (BPA + MNC outputs summed) using a soft mining loss; inference computes per-patch anomaly scores as 1 − cos(encoder, reconstructed) and fuses across modalities.

## Results

On MVTec-3D-AD (10-shot), PIRN achieves AUROC_I of 0.922, AUROC_P of 0.991, and AUPRO of 0.966, outperforming the strongest baseline INP-Former (0.885/0.989/0.960) by +3.7 points in image-level AUROC. On Eyecandies (10-shot), PIRN reaches AUROC_I 0.912, AUROC_P 0.969, and AUPRO 0.896, again leading all baselines. In the full-data (All-shot) setting on MVTec-3D-AD, PIRN attains AUROC_I 0.963 and AUPRO 0.973. PIRN also achieves the best AUROC_P (0.961) on Real-IAD D3 across 13 of 20 categories while using only two modalities (RGB + surface normals) versus D³M's three.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIRN consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every shot setting and both primary benchmarks. Against the strongest few-shot baseline INP-Former (Luo et al., 2025), PIRN gains +3.7 AUROC_I on MVTec-3D-AD (0.922 vs. 0.885) and +4.0 on Eyecandies (0.912 vs. 0.872) at 10-shot, with the gap widening to +3.9/+3.6 at 5-shot. Cross-modal alignment methods CFM (Costanzino et al., 2024) and memory-based M3DM (Wang et al., 2023) trail PIRN by 7.7 and 7.7 AUROC_I points respectively at 10-shot on MVTec-3D-AD, confirming their degradation under data scarcity. Architecture-search method 3D-ADNAS (Long et al., 2025) achieves 0.848 AUROC_I at 10-shot, 7.4 points below PIRN. On the Real-IAD D3 benchmark, PIRN (AUROC_P 0.961) surpasses the tri-modal D³M (AUROC_P 0.937) in localization while using fewer input modalities. In terms of efficiency, PIRN requires only 103.36G FLOPs and 17.49 ms latency versus FIND's 728.46G and 76.09 ms, making it 4.35× faster while matching or exceeding its AUROC_I (0.922 vs. 0.921). BTF (Horwitz & Hoshen, 2023) and AST (Rudolph et al., 2023) are the weakest baselines, with PIRN exceeding them by 22+ AUROC_I points in the 10-shot MVTec-3D-AD setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIRN extends the prototype-based reconstruction paradigm from 2D anomaly detection (INP-Former, HVQ-Trans) to the multimodal setting, but departs fundamentally from both cross-modal alignment methods (CFM, LSFA) and memory-bank approaches (M3DM, SG-DM) that dominate prior MAD work. Rather than learning dense patch-to-patch correspondences or storing exemplar features, PIRN constrains reconstruction through a compact vector-quantized codebook of normality-aware prototypes, drawing on the VQ-VAE lineage (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) and the information bottleneck principle. The key methodological departure is the use of balanced optimal transport (Sinkhorn) for prototype assignment to prevent codebook collapse, combined with a GRU-based adaptive refinement mechanism and a two-stage cross-modal prototype communication module (graph attention + cross-attention) that operates at the prototype level rather than the patch level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PIRN addresses a practically critical gap: existing MAD methods degrade sharply when only a handful of normal samples are available per class, which is the norm in industrial deployment. The 85% FLOPs reduction and 4.35× speedup over the prior SOTA (FIND) while matching or exceeding accuracy makes the approach attractive for real-time inspection pipelines. Open questions include whether the frozen-encoder assumption (DINOv2) limits ceiling performance on out-of-domain categories, whether the fixed prototype count (K=10) generalizes across vastly different object geometries, and whether the prototype-level communication strategy scales to more than two modalities (e.g., adding thermal or acoustic signals).
