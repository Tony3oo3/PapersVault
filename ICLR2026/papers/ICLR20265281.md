## Problem

Prior identifiability analyses for multimodal contrastive learning (MMCL) assume the underlying latent causal variables follow a single Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG). However, large-scale multimodal datasets (e.g., those training CLIP) are generated from heterogeneous, sometimes opposing, causal processes—text-to-image pipelines and image-to-text annotation—making a single DAG an inadequate generative model. This paper asks whether a more flexible, non-DAG causal structure can still yield identifiability guarantees for MMCL and whether such guarantees translate into practical benefits for pre-trained models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired image-text data through a pre-trained CLIP encoder (RN50, RN101, ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16), which produces L2-normalized embeddings on the unit hypersphere. These embeddings are then post-processed with FastICA (hypersphere setting) or PCA followed by FastICA (convex-body setting) to yield component-wise disentangled representations. Downstream, these representations feed a linear probe for few-shot classification (2-shot on ImageNet) or domain-generalization evaluation (ImageNet-V2, Sketch, R, A), and a decoder for reconstruction-based visualization on CelebA.

## Method

The paper proposes a latent partial causal model in which two latent coupled variables z_x and z_t are linked by an undirected edge (capturing transferable cross-modal knowledge), each paired with a modality-specific variable m_x or m_t, and observations are generated via invertible, differentiable maps g_x and g_t. Under a uniform prior on z_x and a von Mises-Fisher (hypersphere) or exponential (convex body) conditional p(z_t|z_x), the authors prove that the asymptotic MMCL loss reduces to a symmetric cross-entropy between the true conditional and a model parameterized by the learned representation h = f_x ∘ g_x = f_t ∘ g_t. Minimizing this cross-entropy identifies z_x up to an orthogonal linear transformation (hypersphere, Corollary 1) or a permutation-with-scaling transformation (convex body, Corollary 2), establishing the first component-wise disentanglement guarantee for MMCL. Practically, this means applying FastICA (or PCA+FastICA) to CLIP's L2-normalized embeddings recovers the latent coupled variables, enabling plug-and-play improvements in few-shot learning and domain generalization without retraining the backbone.

## Results

On 2-shot ImageNet classification, the best configuration (ViT16 + PCA + FastICA) achieves 46.57% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the standard Linear Probe baseline (44.97%) by 1.6 points. For domain generalization, the same configuration yields the strongest average target accuracy of 37.13% across ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-Sketch, ImageNet-R, and ImageNet-A, compared to 32.51% for the baseline. The largest gains appear on ImageNet-R (49.69% vs. 43.86%, +5.83) and ImageNet-Sketch (26.67% vs. 22.06%, +4.61), indicating that disentangled representations are particularly beneficial under distribution shift. Synthetic experiments (Table 1) confirm identifiability with R² values of 99.5% (hypersphere) and MCC of 99.1% (convex body) under ideal assumptions, with robustness to assumption violations (R² ≥ 88.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the standard Linear Probe, which trains a linear classifier directly on raw CLIP representations. Both proposed post-processing methods (FastICA alone, and PCA + FastICA) consistently outperform this baseline across all four CLIP encoders and all five evaluation datasets. The PCA + FastICA pipeline (aligned with the convex-body identifiability result) delivers the largest improvements on domain generalization targets: +5.83 points on ImageNet-R and +4.61 on ImageNet-Sketch for ViT16, while FastICA alone (aligned with the hypersphere result) provides more modest gains on those targets but stronger gains on ImageNet-A (+4.48 for ViT16). In the few-shot adaptation comparison (Figure 4), incorporating FastICA into Tip-Adapter and Tip-Adapter-F yields consistent accuracy improvements over the original methods across 11 cross-domain datasets, confirming that the disentanglement benefit is plug-and-play and encoder-agnostic. Overall, the paper's method wins on every reported metric against the Linear Probe baseline, with the magnitude of improvement growing under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability analysis line for multimodal contrastive learning (Daunhawer et al., 2023; Yao et al., 2024; Gresele et al., 2020) by replacing the standard DAG generative assumption with a latent partial causal model featuring undirected edges between latent coupled variables. The motivation is that large-scale multimodal data (e.g., CLIP's training corpus) arises from heterogeneous and sometimes opposing causal mechanisms (text-to-image vs. image-to-text), making a single DAG structurally inadequate. The theoretical bridge to single-modal contrastive learning identifiability (Zimmermann et al., 2021; Wang & Isola, 2020) is established via a symmetric cross-entropy reduction, and the disentanglement angle draws on nonlinear ICA (Hyvärinen et al., 2001) to show that CLIP representations can be unmixed via FastICA or PCA+FastICA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee for component-wise disentanglement in MMCL, opening a practical route to extracting disentangled features from pre-trained CLIP models with a simple plug-and-play FastICA step. This has immediate utility for few-shot learning and domain generalization, where disentangled representations are known to help. However, the parametric assumptions (uniform priors, vMF/exponential conditionals, invertible generative maps) are strong and unverifiable in practice, and the empirical gains over raw CLIP linear probes are modest (1–4 points). Open questions include whether the disentanglement potential transfers to other pre-trained architectures (e.g., diffusion models, LLM-based multimodal models) and whether the undirected-edge formulation can be relaxed further to handle truly arbitrary mixture-of-DAGs.
