## Problem

Existing multimodal story visualization systems produce visually high-quality individual images but generate disjointed, causally incoherent sequences that fail to convey a readable narrative. The paper identifies this failure as a lack of attention to 'visual logic'—the perceptual and causal coherence among characters, actions, and object states across time—which remains an underexplored dimension of visual sequence generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263462/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a paragraph-length text-form story as input and produces a sequence of T rendered images (generated via Flux) that visually depict the narrative in a coherent, logically consistent manner. Intermediate structured outputs include entity definitions with visual attributes, causal event tuples (actor, action, target, result), panel-level shot specifications with camera parameters, and a directed causal graph. The pipeline also produces refinement instructions and causal plausibility scores at each generation step.

## Method

LogiStory combines two components: a Logic-Aware Multi-agent System (SceneCrafter for entity/visual-attribute extraction, LogicMiner for causal event 4-tuple extraction, and ShotPlanner for panel-level composition with camera parameters) that transforms the narrative into structured visual representations, and a Visual Logic Enhancement Module that enforces coherence during generation. The enhancement module operates at two levels: a Local Causal Monitor that maintains a text-form memory buffer and assigns a causal plausibility score ψ_t to each generated panel by simulating a reader's linear comprehension, and a Global Causal Verifier that constructs a directed causal graph of states and actions, tracking pre/post-condition transitions to flag inconsistencies. Panels scoring below τ₁=0.4 are regenerated, those in [τ₁, τ₂) are sent to the Global Causal Verifier for targeted editing via inpainting models (GPT-image-1, Gemini 2.0 Flash), and those above τ₂=0.7 are accepted. The key insight is reframing story visualization as an explicit logic-aware reasoning problem rather than isolated image synthesis, making narrative coherence a first-class modeling objective.

## Results

On the LogicTale benchmark, LogiStory achieves the highest scores in Narrative Causality (4.45/5), Story Readability (0.8267), Aesthetic Quality (0.3088), and Character Expressiveness (4.32/5) among all compared methods. In the human evaluation, LogiStory leads on Narrative Causality (4.26) and Story Readability (3.83), while GPT-4o+GPT-image-1 edges ahead on Instance Consistency (4.38 vs. 4.25) and Aesthetic Appeal (4.6 vs. 4.2). Pearson correlations between automatic and human scores are strong for logic metrics (0.909–0.978), validating the evaluation protocol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LogiStory achieves the best Narrative Causality (4.45) and Story Readability (0.8267) among all eight baselines, outperforming the strongest competitor GPT-4o+GPT-image-1 by 0.49 and 0.0645 respectively on these logic-centric metrics. However, GPT-4o+GPT-image-1 surpasses LogiStory on Instance Consistency (4.67 vs. 4.23) and Style Consistency (0.8984 vs. 0.8572), attributed to its strong reference-image editing capability. Nano Banana is the closest overall competitor, trailing LogiStory on NCausal. (4.08 vs. 4.45) and SRead. (0.7440 vs. 0.8267) while slightly leading on AesthQ. (0.3102 vs. 0.3088) and SCons. (0.9034 vs. 0.8572). Open-source baselines (SDXL, Flux, StoryDiffusion, ConsiStory, MM-StoryAgent) are substantially weaker on narrative logic, with NCausal. scores ranging from 1.98 to 3.43, confirming that explicit causal modeling provides a significant advantage over purely appearance-consistent generation. In the human study, LogiStory leads on both NCausal. (4.26) and SRead. (3.83), while GPT-4o+GPT-image-1 retains a narrow edge on ICons. (4.38 vs. 4.25) and Aesthetic Appeal (4.6 vs. 4.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the story visualization line (StoryDiffusion, ConsiStory, MM-StoryAgent) and the causal-reasoning-in-text-generation literature (event graphs, structured planning), but departs from both by reframing story visualization as an explicit logic-modeling problem rather than an image-consistency problem. Prior methods treat narrative coherence as an implicit byproduct of good generation or identity conditioning; LogiStory instead introduces 'visual logic' as a first-class objective and inserts a two-level causal verification loop (Local Causal Monitor + Global Causal Verifier) between planning and rendering. The multi-agent planning stage (SceneCrafter, LogicMiner, ShotPlanner) extends agent-based story planners like MM-StoryAgent by adding structured causal event extraction with pre/post-condition tracking, while the verification modules have no direct analogue in prior story visualization pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of 'visual logic' as a formal, measurable dimension and the LogicTale benchmark (60 stories with annotated causal chains) fill a genuine gap: no prior benchmark explicitly evaluates story-level causal coherence in multi-image sequences. The framework demonstrates that explicit causal modeling can outperform the strongest end-to-end multimodal systems (GPT-4o + GPT-image-1) on narrative logic metrics, suggesting that structured reasoning layers remain valuable even as foundation models improve. Key open questions include scalability to high-entity stories, whether the multi-agent overhead is justified versus simpler verification heuristics, and whether the visual-logic formulation transfers to video generation where temporal continuity adds another constraint.
