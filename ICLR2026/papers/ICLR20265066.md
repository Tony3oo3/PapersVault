## Problem

No single acquisition function is universally optimal in Bayesian Optimization, and the best choice shifts dynamically throughout a run. Existing adaptive portfolio methods (GP-Hedge, No-PASt-BO, SETUP-BO, ESP) base their selection decisions almost exclusively on past function-value rewards, ignoring richer state information such as remaining budget, surrogate model hyperparameters (e.g., GP lengthscales), and exploration metrics. This narrow view makes it difficult to design a principled algorithm that reasons over the full strategic and tactical context of the optimization process.

## Input / Output

At each iteration, the method consumes a structured textual serialization of the optimization state comprising three blocks: process status (evaluations performed, remaining budget, dimensionality), performance history (incumbent value, observed function range, shortest distance from last point), and GP model characteristics (lengthscale min/max/mean/std, outputscale). The LLM returns a single acquisition function abbreviation followed by a brief justification string (e.g., 'LogEI: ...'), parsed with a strict format constraint; invalid responses fall back to UCB (observed failure rate ~0.11%). The initial prompt P0 is sent once to establish context, and subsequent prompts append only the new state summary, relying on the LLM's conversational memory for prior history.

## Method

LMABO recasts acquisition function selection as a zero-shot, in-context decision-making task solved by a pretrained LLM (Gemini-2.5 Flash by default) acting as a closed-loop 'semantic controller' over the BO process. The key architectural contribution is a structured state representation S_t that serializes process status, performance history, and GP hyperparameters into a compact textual prompt, designed so that no component is redundant (confirmed by ablations showing significant degradation when any block is removed). A static initial prompt P0 establishes the LLM's role as a BO expert, enumerates the 12-ACF portfolio without descriptive bias, defines the state schema, and enforces a strict output format; the LLM's strategic behavior is guided entirely through in-context learning with no fine-tuning. At each iteration, the fitted GP's hyperparameters and optimization history are serialized into S_t, appended to the running context, and the LLM selects the most appropriate AF from the portfolio. The approach preserves the rigorous GP surrogate and standard AF optimization (multi-start L-BFGS-B) as the mathematical backbone, using the LLM solely for the meta-decision of which AF to deploy, thereby combining the LLM's ability to synthesize heterogeneous state signals with the reliability of classical BO machinery.

## Results

Across 50 benchmark problems (30 synthetic from COCO/BoTorch + 20 real-world HPO from Bayesmark), LMABO achieves a mean Relative Performance (RP) of 1.21 and mean rank of 5.62 out of 38 methods, with a coefficient of variation of 0.37 indicating high consistency. Its total AUC is 9.7% lower than the best static AF (EI, RP 1.34), 14.8% lower than the best simple meta-strategy (Random over EI/LogEI/TS, RP 1.42), 54.7% lower than the best LLM-based method (LLAMBO, RP 2.67), and 16.6% lower than the best adaptive portfolio method (GP-Hedge, RP 1.45). All pairwise differences are statistically significant (Friedman test p = 1.38×10⁻¹⁰⁶; post-hoc Wilcoxon with Holm-Bonferroni correction confirms significance for every baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMABO outperforms all 25 baselines across every category with statistically significant margins. Against static AFs, it beats the best (EI, RP 1.34, rank 13.08) by 9.7% in AUC, while weaker static choices like TS (RP 2.07) and PosSTD (RP 7.02) are far behind. Among adaptive portfolio methods, GP-Hedge (Hoffman et al., 2011; RP 1.45) is the strongest baseline, yet LMABO still leads by 16.6% in AUC; No-PASt-BO (Vasconcelos et al., 2019; RP 1.53), SETUP-BO (Vasconcelos et al., 2022; RP 1.56), and ESP (Shahriari et al., 2014; RP 1.62) all trail further. The LLM-based baselines LLAMBO (Liu et al., 2024; RP 2.67) and LLMP (Requeima et al., 2024; RP 2.78) are substantially outperformed by 54.7% and 56.6% respectively, confirming that using an LLM as a surrogate or point-proposer is far less effective than using it as a strategic AF selector. Simple meta-strategies that mimic LMABO's most common AF choices (e.g., random selection among EI/LogEI/TS, RP 1.42) still underperform LMABO by 14.8%, demonstrating that the LLM's state-aware switching policy—not merely the AF portfolio—drives the gains. The BoTorch library (Balandat et al., 2020) serves as the implementation framework for all GP-based methods rather than a competing baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the portfolio-based adaptive BO line (GP-Hedge, No-PASt-BO, SETUP-BO, ESP), which selects acquisition functions based on narrow reward signals from past function values, by instead feeding a rich, multi-faceted state representation (budget, GP hyperparameters, exploration metrics) to a pre-trained LLM as a zero-shot in-context decision-maker. It also distinguishes itself from prior LLM+BO work (FunBO, LLAMBO, LLMP) that uses LLMs as offline AF generators or surrogate replacements; LMABO keeps the GP backbone intact and repurposes the LLM purely as a semantic controller for AF selection. The approach is conceptually closest to learning-based meta-policies (MetaBO, FSAF) but replaces learned policies with in-context reasoning, eliminating the need for task-specific training or transfer learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a zero-shot LLM, prompted with a structured state summary, can outperform both hand-crafted adaptive portfolio methods and other LLM-based BO approaches across 50 diverse benchmarks, with the practical overhead (~1s/iteration, ~6000 tokens) being negligible relative to expensive function evaluations. The ablation revealing that a simple instruction to avoid previously ineffective AFs causes the largest performance drop (RP 1.92 vs. 1.21) highlights how even minimal guidance shapes LLM decision quality. Open questions include whether the approach extends to constrained BO, non-GP surrogates, and higher-dimensional settings, and whether the structured state representation can be further optimized or whether the LLM's sensitivity to specific state elements (as shown in the perturbation analysis) can be exploited for more targeted prompting.
