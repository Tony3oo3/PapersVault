## Problem

Existing domain-generalized semantic segmentation (DGSS) methods achieve strong cross-domain robustness but run at only 3–11 FPS, far below the real-time requirement for safety-critical driving and robotics, while real-time segmentation methods sacrifice domain adaptability due to fixed class embeddings. The paper identifies that the speed bottleneck in DGSS lies in the heavy query-based segmentation head rather than the VFM backbone, and formalizes Real-Time Domain-Generalized Semantic Segmentation (RT-DGSS) as a distinct research setting that must jointly optimize inference speed and cross-domain accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB driving-scene images at 512×1024 resolution (inference) and produces a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask over 19 Cityscapes classes. During training, images are cropped into 512×512 patches via a sliding window. Inference runs at 54 FPS on a single RTX 4090 with batch size 1, using a DINOv2-L ViT backbone with 315M inference-time parameters. The model is trained on GTAV (synthetic) or Cityscapes (real) and evaluated on BDD100K, Mapillary Vistas, ACDC adverse-condition splits, and Cityscapes-C corruptions.

## Method

QPrompt replaces the multi-layer query-based decoder (pixel encoder + transformer decoder) with a single-layer interaction: K learnable queries are concatenated to the (L−1)-th block output of a DINOv2 ViT and processed by only the final transformer block, reducing complexity from O(M(N+K)²d) to O((N+K)²d) while preserving adaptive query-image fusion. To compensate for the limited single-layer interaction under domain shifts, GRQA introduces a training-only objective inspired by GRPO: a momentum-updated prototype bank provides class anchors, query-prototype cosine similarity serves as a per-query reward, queries are partitioned into groups by their most similar prototype, and group-relative advantages (reward minus group mean, normalized by group std) drive a PPO-style clipped objective with KL regularization to an EMA reference model. Unlike Hungarian matching, which supervises only one query per class, GRQA jointly optimizes all queries within each group, enabling multiple queries to acquire segmentation competence and mitigate domain-shift failures. GRQA is fully disabled at test time, adding zero inference overhead, and functions as a plug-and-play module that improves existing DGSS methods (REIN +1.2, SoMA +0.6 mIoU) without architectural changes.

## Results

QPrompt-R1 achieves 64.1 mIoU on GTAV→Real, 67.8 mIoU on Real→Real, and 69.4 mIoU on Real→ACDC, all at a sustained 54 FPS on a single RTX 4090. On Cityscapes-C (level-5 corruptions), it reaches 69.8 mIoU, substantially outperforming the next-best method REIN (60.0 mIoU) while running 5.4× faster. The model uses 315M inference parameters, comparable to Mask2Former (325M) and REIN (328M), yet achieves 5–18× higher throughput than DGSS baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QPrompt-R1 decisively outperforms all real-time segmentation baselines: it surpasses the strongest RTSS method EoMT by +3.1 mIoU on GTAV→Real (64.1 vs 61.0), +1.7 on Real→Real (67.8 vs 66.1), and +3.0 on Real→ACDC (69.4 vs 66.4), while maintaining comparable speed (54 vs 52 FPS). Against DGSS methods, QPrompt-R1 nearly matches REIN on GTAV→Real (64.1 vs 64.3) and Real→ACDC (69.4 vs 69.6) but runs 5× faster (54 vs 10 FPS); it trails FADA by 2.0 mIoU and SoMA by 4.1 mIoU on GTAV→Real, though at 5–7× higher throughput. On Cityscapes-C corruptions, QPrompt-R1 (69.8 mIoU) leads all baselines by a wide margin over REIN (60.0), TAPADL (54.1), and FAN (53.0), with particularly large gains under Noise (60.4–66.7 vs 6.2–32.4). As a plug-and-play module, GRQA improves REIN by +1.2 mIoU and SoMA by +0.6 mIoU on GTAV→Real without any inference-time overhead, confirming its generality across DGSS architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

QPrompt-R1 sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: query-based segmentation (Mask2Former) and RL-inspired post-training (GRPO from DeepSeekMath). Architecturally, it collapses Mask2Former's multi-layer pixel-encoder-plus-decoder stack into a single Transformer block where learnable queries are injected at the final VFM layer, preserving adaptive query-image interaction at a fraction of the cost. The GRQA training objective transplants GRPO's group-relative advantage, PPO-style clipping, and KL-to-reference regularization into the query-prototype alignment problem, replacing Hungarian matching's one-query-per-class supervision with cooperative multi-query optimization. The paper explicitly positions itself against both DGSS methods (REIN, SoMA, FADA, MFuser) that sacrifice speed and RTSS methods (PIDNet, EoMT, Next-ViT) that sacrifice domain robustness, framing their joint optimization as a new RT-DGSS setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear for safety-critical deployment: 54 FPS with competitive cross-domain mIoU narrows the gap between research-grade DGSS and deployable systems. The diagnostic finding that the segmentation head—not the VFM backbone—is the primary speed bottleneck in DGSS is a useful reframing that could guide future architecture design. GRQA's plug-and-play nature (improving REIN by +1.2 and SoMA by +0.6 mIoU with zero inference overhead) makes it a broadly applicable training trick for query-based models. Open questions include whether the single-layer query interaction remains sufficient as domain shifts grow more extreme, whether GRQA's benefits persist at larger scales or with more classes, and whether the approach extends beyond driving scenes to other dense-prediction domains.
