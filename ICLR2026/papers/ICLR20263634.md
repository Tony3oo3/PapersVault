## Problem

Large-scale optimal transport problems with squared Euclidean cost face a fundamental memory bottleneck: the LP formulation has mn variables (n² for n×n images, reaching ~10¹² at 1024² resolution), making classical solvers intractable. Existing approaches either sacrifice accuracy via regularization or low-rank approximation, lack worst-case iteration guarantees (multiscale methods like ShortCut), or remain CPU-bound and memory-hungry (LP-based solvers). No prior method simultaneously achieves O(n) memory, GPU parallelism, and near-exact accuracy.

## Input / Output

HALO takes two discrete marginal distributions (u ∈ ℝ^m, v ∈ ℝ^n), a squared Euclidean ground cost matrix, and a number of hierarchy levels L as input, and returns the optimal coupling x^(0) ∈ ℝ_+^{m×n}. It operates on grayscale images up to 1024×1024 pixels and 3D point clouds up to 2^19 points, with the hierarchy constructed via recursive 2×2 pixel merging (images) or 2^d-tree spatial partitioning (point clouds). The key practical constraint is that the method requires only O(n) GPU memory rather than the O(n²) needed to store the full cost matrix, enabling problems that would otherwise OOM on a 24 GB GPU.

## Method

HALO exploits two structural properties of OT: (i) a coarse-to-fine multiscale hierarchy where solutions at level ℓ+1 are prolongated to warm-start level ℓ, and (ii) the sparsity of the optimal transport plan (at most m+n nonzeros by LP theory). At each level, the algorithm alternates between an active-support update (combining a shielding-based geometric component with a dual-violation correction via a Top_K pricing step) and solving the restricted LP on the active support using cuPDLPx, a factorization-free PDHG-based GPU solver. A Pock-Chambolle diagonal rescaling of the constraint matrix guarantees ‖B̃‖₂=1, justifying a constant stepsize that eliminates per-iteration norm estimation. Theoretically, under directional coverage, bounded neighborhood radius, bounded target density, uniform Lipschitz regularity, and coupling stability assumptions, the refinement phase enjoys a scale-independent O(1) iteration bound per level, with the active support size bounded by C|S| independent of problem scale.

## Results

On the DOTmark image benchmark at r=1024 (n≈10⁶ pixels), HALO solves the OT problem in 27.73 s using 6.25 GB GPU memory, achieving an 8.9× speedup and 70.5% memory reduction over M3S while attaining 1–2 orders of magnitude tighter objective gaps. On the 3D ModelNet10 point cloud benchmark at n=2¹⁸, HALO runs in 229.7 s with 1.83 GB memory, delivering a 1.84× speedup, 83.2% memory reduction, and 24.9% lower transport cost compared to HiRef. The scale-independent iteration bound (Theorem 1) is empirically validated: average inner iterations per level never exceed 2 across all resolutions from 16 to 1024.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HOT (Zhang et al., 2025), HALO dominates on all three metrics: at r=512 it is 7.02× faster (11.17 s vs 78.43 s) with 89.2% less GPU memory (2.07 vs 19.25 GB), and achieves 2–3 orders of magnitude tighter objective gaps; HOT simply OOMs at r=1024. Against ShortCut (Schmitzer, 2016), HALO is dramatically faster at comparable accuracy—37.36× speedup at r=512 (11.17 s vs 438.14 s)—while ShortCut's CPU memory footprint is lower (0.56 GB vs 2.07 GB), reflecting its CPU-based design. Against the entropy-regularized M3S (Chen et al., 2022), HALO is 8.92× faster at r=1024 (27.73 s vs 247.22 s), uses 70.5% less memory (6.25 vs 21.21 GB), and achieves 1–2 orders tighter gaps, confirming that exact LP-based solutions can outperform regularized approximations in both speed and accuracy. On 3D point clouds (ModelNet10), HALO outperforms HiRef (Halmos et al., 2025) by 1.84× in runtime and 83.2% in memory at n=2¹⁸, while also achieving a 24.9% lower transport cost, indicating superior solution quality. Sinkhorn (Cuturi, 2013) OOMs at n=2¹⁶ on the 3D benchmark, making it infeasible beyond that scale, whereas HALO scales to n=2¹⁹ using only 2.99 GB.

## Relation to Prior Work

HALO extends the sparse multiscale OT lineage initiated by ShortCut (Schmitzer 2016) and the broader multiscale framework of Mérigot (2011) and Gerber & Maggioni (2017), but departs in two critical ways. First, it replaces the CPU-based LP solvers used by ShortCut and MSSN with factorization-free first-order GPU solvers (cuPDLPx), whose dominant matrix-vector operations are naturally parallelizable. Second, it modifies the active-support update from ShortCut's aggressive pruning (retaining only the current coupling's support) to a conservative monotone expansion augmented with a dual-violation correction, which is shown to be necessary for stability with the lower-precision solutions produced by GPU first-order methods. The Pock-Chambolle rescaling result (Proposition 1) providing an exact spectral-norm bound of 1 is a clean theoretical addition that justifies constant stepsizes without per-iteration norm estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(n) memory footprint and scale-independent per-level iteration bound (Theorem 1, empirically validated at ≤2 iterations) address two long-standing gaps in multiscale OT: prior methods lacked worst-case complexity guarantees and were bottlenecked by CPU solvers. The practical impact is substantial — 8.9× speedup and 70.5% memory reduction at 1024×1024, and 83.2% memory reduction at n=2^18 point clouds — making previously intractable instances feasible on a single 24GB GPU. Key limitations restrict broader applicability: the method is designed for squared Euclidean cost (general costs reduce solution sparsity and degrade the refinement phase), the shielding-based update faces scaling challenges in high dimensions, and the theoretical assumptions (uniform Lipschitz regularity, coupling stability) are only intuitively justified under multiscale warm-starts rather than rigorously verified for all practical instances.
