## Problem

Existing mobile agent security benchmarks evaluate agents against static, predefined threats (harmful prompts, fixed UI states) but overlook dynamic environmental injection, where adversarial UI elements such as deceptive overlays and spoofed notifications are injected into the GUI at runtime during task execution. This attack vector exploits the agent's primary reliance on visual perception, bypassing textual safeguards entirely, yet its severity remains unquantified due to the absence of a systematic evaluation framework in fully operational on-device environments.

## Input / Output

The benchmark runs on Android emulators (1080×2400px, 420dpi, API 36) with 14 apps across 7 domains. The agent consumes sequential UI screenshots and natural-language task instructions, while adversarial elements are injected at precisely timed moments via a hook-based adb trigger mechanism. The LLM judge receives the full execution trace (per-step screenshots plus timestamped actions), the task's ground-truth result conditions, and outputs four binary labels (Task Completion, Full/Partial Attack Success, Benign Failure) per case, aggregated into a Vulnerability Rate that normalizes attack success by excluding capability-only failures.

## Method

GhostEI-Bench comprises 110 test cases spanning three attack vectors (Deceptive Instruction, Static Environmental Injection, Dynamic Environmental Injection) and seven risk fields, instantiated within realistic multi-step workflows on Android emulators. Dynamic attacks are realized through a hook-based trigger: a predefined agent action broadcasts an adb command intercepted by a custom on-device helper app that renders the adversarial overlay or popup SMS in real-time, enabling precise temporal control over when the injection appears relative to the agent's task progress. Evaluation employs an LLM-as-judge protocol that jointly reviews the agent's action trajectory and corresponding screenshot sequence to assign fine-grained security labels and identify the precise failure stage (perception, recognition, or reasoning). The Vulnerability Rate metric (VR = (FAS+PAS)/(Total−BF)) isolates security susceptibility from functional capability, and the benchmark is validated with a 93.6% human-LLM agreement rate. The paper evaluates 8 VLM agents across two agentic frameworks (Mobile-Agent-v2, AppAgent) and specialized GUI models (UI-TARS), further probing the effects of self-reflection and explicit reasoning modules on robustness.

## Results

On GhostEI-Bench (110 test cases across 7 domains and 3 attack vectors), the best-performing agent, GPT-5 under Mobile-Agent-v2, achieves a Task Completion rate of 56.4% and the lowest Vulnerability Rate of 16.43%. All other evaluated VLM agents exhibit substantially higher vulnerability, with VR ranging from 40.00% (Gemini-2.5 Pro) to 56.25% (UI-TARS-7B-SFT), indicating that even functionally capable agents are compromised in 40–55% of solvable scenarios. The benchmark reveals a clear capability–security trade-off: Gemini-2.5 Pro has the lowest Benign Failure rate (18.2%) yet a 40% VR, while GPT-4o combines modest TC (34.6%) with a 54.87% VR, making it highly susceptible to manipulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 dominates on both capability and security, achieving the highest TC (56.4%) and lowest VR (16.43%), outperforming GPT-4o (VR 54.87%) by over 38 percentage points in vulnerability and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (VR 55.12%) by nearly 39 points. Gemini-2.5 Pro achieves the lowest Benign Failure rate (18.2%) among all models, indicating superior functional competence, but its VR of 40.00% places it in the same vulnerability tier as GPT-5-chat-latest (41.67%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (41.42%). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct shows a lower FAS (10.9%) than most proprietary models but still succumbs to partial attacks in 15.5% of cases. The specialized GUI model UI-TARS-1.5-7B achieves a competitive TC of 40.9% with a VR of 46.99%, while its predecessor UI-TARS-7B-SFT is the most vulnerable model overall (VR 56.25%). Framework choice has model-dependent effects: Mobile-Agent-v2 reduces Qwen2.5-VL's VR from 52.56% (AppAgent) to 41.42%, but increases GPT-4o's VR from 50.00% (AppAgent) to 54.87%, showing that planning architectures do not uniformly improve security.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on GUI agent security evaluation (MobileSafetyBench, MLA-Trust, MMBench-GUI) and environmental injection attack demonstrations (Zhang et al. 2024, RiOSWorld, AgentHazard, Chen et al. 2025) by unifying three distinct attack vectors—deceptive instruction, static injection, and dynamic injection—into a single executable benchmark within Android emulators. Unlike prior work that either assesses static UI states or demonstrates individual attacks in isolation, GhostEI-Bench injects adversarial events into multi-step task workflows at runtime, leveraging a hook-based trigger mechanism for precise timing. The LLM-as-judge evaluation protocol builds on the paradigm established by DigiRL and DistRL but extends it to fine-grained failure attribution across perception, recognition, and reasoning stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GhostEI-Bench fills a critical gap by providing the first systematic, reproducible framework for quantifying mobile agent vulnerability to dynamic environmental injection, a threat vector that prior benchmarks largely overlooked. The finding that most evaluated models exhibit 40–55% vulnerability rates underscores that current safety alignment is insufficient for real-world deployment where notifications and pop-ups are ubiquitous. Open questions include whether the observed vulnerability generalizes beyond Android, whether the 110-case scale is sufficient for reliable model ranking, and how to design agents that are simultaneously highly capable and resistant to visual deception without sacrificing utility. The mixed results from reflection and reasoning modules suggest that auxiliary mechanisms require careful tuning rather than blanket application.
