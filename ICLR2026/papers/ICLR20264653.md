## Problem

Video generation and editing remain fragmented across task-specific architectures (cross-attention, MMDiT) that require bespoke input configurations and masks, unlike the image domain where unified frameworks have emerged. Compounding this, high-quality instruction-based video editing data is orders of magnitude scarcer than its image counterpart, making it difficult to train capable video editors without architectural mechanisms to transfer knowledge from the data-rich image domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264653/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EditVerse accepts an arbitrary interleaved sequence of text instructions, source images, source videos, and reference images, all tokenized into a single 1D sequence with no fixed positional or modal constraints. Images and videos are resized to areas between 256×256 and 512×512 pixels, and videos can have arbitrary temporal duration. The model outputs a denoised visual token sequence (image or video) at the designated generation position within the sequence, guided by a Flow Matching ODE solver over 50 timesteps with classifier-free guidance (scale 5.0) applied only to text conditions.

## Method

EditVerse represents all modalities as a unified interleaved token sequence processed by a 2B dense transformer with full self-attention, enabling in-context learning and cross-modal knowledge transfer between images and videos. A four-dimensional Rotary Positional Embedding (sequential, temporal, height, width; dimensions 12/4/56/56) disambiguates modal and positional information, with NTK-aware interpolation for variable-length inputs. Vision inputs are compressed by a convolutional VAE (8× spatial downsampling) and patchified with a 1×2×2 kernel, while text is encoded by Flan-T5-XXL; both are projected into a shared embedding space via linear layers and concatenated with learnable start/end-of-vision tokens. Training uses a Flow Matching velocity-prediction objective where one randomly selected visual segment is denoised while all other segments remain clean, and the model is trained on a mixture of 1.9M image generation, 6M image editing, 3.9M video generation, and 288K video editing samples (the latter curated via a pipeline using Grounded-SAM-2, DiffuEraser, VACE, and ReCamMaster with VLM-based quality filtering).

## Results

On EditVerseBench, EditVerse achieves a VLM editing quality score of 7.65, surpassing the best open-source baseline Señorita-2M (6.97) by 0.68 points and Lucy Edit (5.89) by 1.76 points. It also leads all open-source methods on video quality (Pick Score 20.07), text alignment (Frame CLIP 26.73, Video CLIP 23.93), and temporal consistency (CLIP 98.56, DINO 98.42). On TGVE+, EditVerse matches Movie Gen Edit on ViCLIP_dir (0.225) and slightly exceeds it on ViCLIP_out (0.252 vs. 0.248), despite being trained without any square video editing samples. Against the commercial model Runway Aleph, EditVerse wins on VLM editing quality (7.65 vs. 7.44) but trails on raw video quality metrics (e.g., Pick Score 20.07 vs. 20.42), which the authors attribute to base model differences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EditVerse outperforms all open-source baselines on every EditVerseBench metric. Against training-free methods TokenFlow (VLM 5.26) and STDF (VLM 4.41), the gains are substantial (7.65 vs. 5.26/4.41), confirming that learned editing substantially surpasses attention-manipulation approaches. Among instruction-guided methods, EditVerse beats InsV2V (VLM 5.21) and Lucy Edit (VLM 5.89) by 2.44 and 1.76 points respectively, and also leads the first-frame propagation method Señorita-2M (VLM 6.97) by 0.68 points. Against the commercial Runway Aleph, EditVerse wins on VLM editing faithfulness (7.65 vs. 7.44) but loses on video quality metrics (Pick Score 20.07 vs. 20.42; Frame CLIP 26.73 vs. 27.70), which the authors attribute to a weaker base model rather than the editing framework itself. On TGVE+, EditVerse ties Movie Gen Edit on ViCLIP_dir (0.225) and edges it out on ViCLIP_out (0.252 vs. 0.248), while clearly surpassing EVE (0.198/0.251) and InsV2V (0.174/0.236). The user study (3,000 pairwise ratings) confirms the VLM evaluation ranking, showing a Pearson correlation of 0.84 between human preference and automated VLM scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

EditVerse extends the full self-attention, interleaved token sequence paradigm established for image editing (FullDiT, UniReal) to the video domain, addressing the architectural fragmentation that has kept video editing in task-specific pipelines (VACE's inpainting branch, InsV2V's channel concatenation, UNIC's task-aware positional embeddings). The training objective follows Flow Matching, and the 4D RoPE (sequential, temporal, height, width) is a novel positional design specifically to disambiguate modalities within a single sequence. On the data side, the paper departs from prior video editing datasets (Señorita-2M, InsV2V) by building a multi-stage generation-and-filtering pipeline that achieves a 6× higher retention rate, and by deliberately mixing image editing data to compensate for video editing data scarcity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that cross-modal knowledge transfer from the data-rich image domain can substantially mitigate video editing data scarcity, with ablations showing image data is more critical than video generation data for editing quality. EditVerseBench fills a genuine gap as the first instruction-based video editing benchmark covering diverse tasks and mixed aspect ratios. However, the full self-attention design incurs significant computational overhead (44.8 GB GPU memory at 110K tokens, 873s inference at 50 steps), and the model does not reach SOTA on image editing or match commercial models on raw video quality, leaving open whether efficiency improvements (higher-compression VAEs, dynamic token pruning, distillation) can close these gaps without sacrificing the cross-modal transfer that drives the emergent editing abilities.
