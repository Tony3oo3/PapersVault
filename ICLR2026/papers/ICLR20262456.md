## Problem

Current state-of-the-art video generation models (Sora, Veo3, Wan2.1) are trained predominantly on macroscopic, human-scale videos and consistently fail at microscale biological simulation, producing outputs that look visually plausible but violate fundamental physical and biological laws. No dedicated benchmark, training dataset, or model has been developed to evaluate or address this gap in microscale simulation (organ-level, cellular-level, and subcellular-level processes).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262456/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt describing a specific microscale biological mechanism (e.g., cell mitosis, cardiac contraction, molecular signaling) and generates an 81-frame video at 480×832 resolution. Evaluation is performed via a rubric-based system where GPT-5 scores each generated video against task-specific, expert-weighted criteria spanning scientific fidelity, visual quality, and instruction following, with a single-shot inference policy (fixed seed, one sample per prompt) to prevent cherry-picking. The benchmark comprises 459 tasks with 459 unique expert-annotated rubric criteria, and the training dataset (MicroSim-10K) contains 9,601 clips of 5–60 seconds each at ≥720p resolution.

## Method

MicroVerse fine-tunes the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B diffusion transformer (DiT) with full parameter updates on MicroSim-10K, a purpose-built dataset of 9,601 expert-verified microscale simulation clips collected from YouTube and filtered through a multi-stage pipeline (OpenCLIP-based segmentation, VideoMAE classifier at 92% accuracy, OpenCV/EasyOCR cleanup, and human expert review). Training uses 8 H200 GPUs with a learning rate of 1e-5, batch size 8, 5,000 steps, bf16 mixed precision, and a 10% classifier-free guidance dropout rate. The key finding is that domain-specific data drives scientific fidelity gains more effectively than model scale: fine-tuning the 1.3B model on MicroSim-10K improves scientific fidelity by +2.7 over the base model, while scaling to 14B with mixed-domain training (MicroSim-10K + equivalent OpenVid data) further lifts fidelity to 48.3. The evaluation framework (MicroWorldBench) uses LLM-as-a-Judge with GPT-5 scoring against weighted, polarity-tagged rubric criteria that were LLM-drafted and then revised by three domain experts, achieving Fleiss' Kappa of 0.771 agreement with human-only panels.

## Results

On MicroWorldBench (459 expert-annotated tasks), MicroVerse-1.3B achieves an average score of 50.2, with 47.6 on organ-level, 51.7 on cellular-level, and 53.3 on subcellular-level tasks. In the dimension-wise evaluation, MicroVerse attains the highest Scientific Fidelity score (43.0) among all open-source models, surpassing Wan2.1-T2V-14B (42.7) and Wan2.2-T2V-A14B (37.8), while Veo3 leads overall with 77.2 average and 65.7 Scientific Fidelity. The paper also reports that scaling to 14B with mixed-domain training (MicroSim-10K + OpenVid) further lifts Scientific Fidelity to 48.3 and Instruction Following to 56.9, achieving state-of-the-art among open-source models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MicroVerse-1.3B outperforms all open-source baselines on Scientific Fidelity (43.0 vs. 42.7 for Wan2.1-14B, 37.8 for Wan2.2-A14B, 35.3 for Sora) and on subcellular-level tasks (53.3, tied with Wan2.2-A14B and ahead of Wan2.1-14B's 52.8). However, it trails the larger Wan2.1-T2V-14B (54.8) and Wan2.2-T2V-A14B (53.8) on overall average score, and falls well short of Veo3 (77.2), which dominates across all dimensions. Against Sora (50.7 average), MicroVerse is slightly behind on overall score but leads by 7.7 points on Scientific Fidelity (43.0 vs. 35.3), indicating Sora's strength is visual quality (96.4) rather than biological accuracy. The paper notes that scaling MicroVerse to 14B with mixed-domain training closes the gap, reaching 48.3 Scientific Fidelity and 56.9 Instruction Following, surpassing all open-source baselines on every dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video-generation-as-world-simulator paradigm (Sora, Veo3, Wan2.1) from macroscopic natural scenes to microscale biological simulation, a domain the authors argue has been entirely unexplored. Methodologically, it is a straightforward fine-tuning of the Wan2.1 DiT architecture on a purpose-built dataset (MicroSim-10K), with no architectural modifications. The evaluation framework draws on the rubric-based LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm popularized by HealthBench and V-Bench, but adapts it with expert-weighted scientific fidelity criteria specific to organ, cellular, and subcellular processes. The data pipeline borrows standard tools (OpenCLIP for segmentation, VideoMAE for classification, GPT-4o for captioning) but applies them in a novel microscale-specific filtering context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most useful contribution is MicroWorldBench, the first rubric-based benchmark that quantitatively separates visual quality from scientific fidelity in video generation, revealing a systematic and large gap (e.g., Veo3: 97.0 visual vs. 65.7 scientific; most open-source models: 80+ visual vs. 15–43 scientific). This finding challenges the implicit assumption that high visual quality implies physical/biological correctness. However, the model contribution (MicroVerse) is preliminary: the 1.3B model improves scientific fidelity by only ~2.7 points over its base, and the authors acknowledge that no explicit physical laws (fluid dynamics, diffusion-reaction equations) are incorporated, limiting applicability to high-precision scientific prediction. Open questions include whether scaling to 14B+ with mixed-domain training closes the gap to commercial models, and whether physics-informed losses or constraints could fundamentally improve scientific fidelity beyond what data-driven fine-tuning achieves.
