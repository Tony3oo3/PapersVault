## Problem

Cross-embodiment pre-training for robot foundation models has relied almost exclusively on imitation learning with high-quality expert demonstrations, leaving abundant suboptimal interaction data unused. While offline RL can exploit such data and cross-embodiment learning can pool heterogeneous robot trajectories, their combination remains largely unexplored with no systematic analysis of how suboptimal data and embodiment diversity interact. This paper identifies that, under high suboptimal-data ratios and many robot morphologies, inter-robot gradient conflicts cause severe negative transfer that standard offline RL cannot resolve.

## Input / Output

The policy receives a shared proprioceptive observation vector (joint angles, velocities, base state) plus a continuous morphology descriptor (link lengths, masses, actuation parameters) rather than a one-hot embodiment index, enabling generalization to unseen robots. Observations are factorized into an embodiment-agnostic general stream and variable-length joint/foot streams that are aggregated via descriptor-conditioned attention into a fixed-size latent. Actions are continuous joint-level torques, zero-padded to the maximum action dimension across all 16 robots to feed a shared Q-network.

## Method

The paper builds on the URMA multi-embodiment architecture extended with a state-action value function for offline RL (IQL or TD3+BC). The core contribution, Embodiment Grouping (EG), represents each robot as a morphology graph (nodes = torso/joints/feet, edges = kinematic adjacency) and computes pairwise Fused Gromov-Wasserstein distances to capture both structural and actuation similarity. Hierarchical clustering on this distance matrix yields M fixed robot groups; training then alternates one global critic update with sequential group-wise actor updates, so that gradients from morphologically similar robots are applied together rather than mixed with conflicting gradients from dissimilar robots. The paper further provides a systematic gradient-conflict analysis showing that the fraction of negative pairwise cosine similarities grows with both suboptimal-data proportion and embodiment diversity, and that embodiment similarity (FGW distance) correlates strongly (r=0.63 for IQL, r=0.71 for TD3+BC) with gradient alignment, validating the grouping principle.

## Results

On the 16-robot cross-embodiment locomotion benchmark, IQL+EG (the proposed method) achieves the best mean return of 57.29 across six datasets, outperforming IQL+SEL (55.07), IQL+PCGrad (53.48), and the IQL baseline (52.05). The gains are most pronounced on the 70% Suboptimal datasets, where IQL+EG reaches 51.19 (Forward) and 49.60 (Backward) versus 36.62 and 38.69 for vanilla IQL — a 33.99% average improvement over the IQL baseline. On Expert datasets with uniform data quality, all methods converge to similar performance (~63–67), confirming that the advantage of embodiment grouping is specifically activated by suboptimal data and gradient conflicts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IQL+EG outperforms all four baselines on the overall mean, with the largest margins on suboptimal-data settings. Against IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021), the proposed method gains +5.24 mean return overall and +12.74 on the 70% Suboptimal Forward dataset (51.19 vs. 36.62), confirming that embodiment grouping specifically addresses the gradient-conflict failure mode that vanilla IQL suffers under heterogeneous suboptimal data. Against IQL+SEL (Jeong & Yoon, 2025), which uses dynamic task-affinity-based grouping, IQL+EG leads by +2.22 mean and +6.60 on 70% Suboptimal Forward (51.19 vs. 44.59), showing that static morphology-based grouping via FGW distance is more effective than learned task affinity in this cross-embodiment setting. Against IQL+PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020), which projects conflicting gradients, IQL+EG achieves +3.81 mean and +11.56 on 70% Suboptimal Forward (51.19 vs. 39.63), indicating that preventing conflicts through sequential group updates is superior to post-hoc gradient surgery. Against TD3+BC (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021), IQL+EG leads by +10.86 mean, though TD3+BC+EG also benefits from grouping (+4.96 over plain TD3+BC), demonstrating that the grouping strategy transfers across offline RL backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established threads: offline RL (IQL, TD3+BC) for learning from static datasets containing suboptimal trajectories, and cross-embodiment pre-training (Open X-Embodiment, Octo, URMA) for pooling heterogeneous robot data. While each thread is mature, their combination—systematically pre-training a single policy across many robot morphologies using offline RL—had not been studied; prior attempts (Nakamoto et al., 2024; Springenberg et al., 2024) involved only two platforms and did not analyze cross-embodiment interactions. The proposed Embodiment Grouping mechanism borrows the group-update idea from selective task grouping (Jeong & Yoon, 2025) and the gradient-conflict framing from PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020), but replaces dynamic per-step conflict resolution with a static, morphology-driven clustering via Fused Gromov-Wasserstein distance on robot kinematic graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic benchmark (16 robots, 6 dataset variants) and mechanistic analysis for cross-embodiment offline RL, identifying inter-robot gradient conflicts as the root cause of negative transfer and showing that a simple static grouping outperforms both gradient-projection and dynamic-affinity baselines by up to ~34%. This gives practitioners a concrete, low-overhead recipe for scaling offline RL pre-training to heterogeneous robot fleets. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to MuJoCo locomotion in simulation, a static grouping that cannot adapt to changing data quality or learning dynamics, and reliance on the URMA architecture; open questions include sim-to-real transfer, applicability to manipulation, and whether dynamic or learned grouping could further close the gap.
