## Problem

Dexterous robot manipulation is jointly limited by hardware morphology and control policy, yet traditional development decouples these two aspects, and prior co-design work has remained confined to simulation with simplified hardware assumptions. No existing framework simultaneously optimizes hand morphology and dexterous control policies while supporting scalable evaluation across a wide design space and zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with physically manufacturable components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260506/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At design time, the system consumes grammar parameters (finger count 3–5, per-finger joint count 2–3, segment-scale codes 1–10, fingertip type, palm layout mode) and produces an attributed star-graph morphology (one palm node + up to five finger-slot nodes) along with 3D-printable modular specifications. At deployment, the blind policy receives only joint encoder positions and a one-hot morphology vector (dimension F_max × (1+2L_max) = 35) as input—no vision, no object state, no tactile—and outputs joint position commands masked by an action mask M(G) to handle variable degrees of freedom across embodiments. The design value network outputs a scalar performance score per candidate morphology to guide search.

## Method

The framework formulates co-design as a bi-level optimization: an inner PPO loop optimizes a morphology-conditioned policy for a fixed hand, while an outer Graph Heuristic Search explores the morphology space using a message-passing GNN as a design value network. The key efficiency insight is cross-embodiment pre-training: rather than training a separate 26-hour PPO policy per design, a single policy conditioned on a one-hot morphology vector and equipped with an action mask for variable actuator counts is trained across 2,000–8,000 hand variants grouped into kinematic families, yielding a 400× speedup in design evaluation. Candidate morphologies are generated via a modular grammar that enforces physical constraints (pre-computed CoACD collision meshes, CAD-derived joint limits, Dynamixel XL330 actuator specs), and the search uses Gumbel-perturbed value scores with an annealing exploration schedule (0.4→0.05) and tabu tracking to balance exploitation and diversity. The best-ranked design is then fine-tuned with adaptive domain randomization and deployed zero-shot as a blind proprioceptive policy on 3D-printed hardware.

## Results

On the continuous in-hand rotation task, the best co-designed hand achieves 3.3 rad/s continuous angular velocity with fine-tuning (6.48 h total runtime) and 1.85 rad/s without fine-tuning (5.18 h). The cross-embodied evaluation framework evaluates 2,000 designs in 5.18 hours, a 400× speedup over training individual PPO policies per morphology. In sim-to-real deployment, the optimal 3-finger hand successfully rotates 15 out of 17 unseen objects within one minute, substantially outperforming the 5-finger (4/17), 4-finger (3/17), and anthropomorphic (3/17) baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all baselines on continuous in-hand rotation. Against the LEAP Hand with vision (Shaw et al., 2023), the co-designed hand achieves 3.3 rad/s versus 0.47 rad/s — a 7× improvement — while also operating blind (no object state), whereas the LEAP baseline requires vision and is trained on a single object. Against RoboGrammar (Zhao et al., 2020a), the improvement is 12.7× (3.3 vs. 0.26 rad/s), and the runtime is 3.5× shorter (6.48 h vs. 23.0 h). Monte Carlo Tree Search performs worst among search-based methods at 0.20 rad/s with a time-to-fall of only 2.71 s, confirming that graph heuristic search with a learned value network is more effective for this design space. The MPPI variant of the proposed method (0.62 rad/s) demonstrates that model-predictive control is insufficient for long-horizon dexterous manipulation, validating the use of PPO-based reinforcement learning for the control component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph-grammar procedural generation and Graph Heuristic Search framework from RoboGrammar (Zhao et al., 2020a) from locomotion/terrain design to dexterous manipulation, and combines it with cross-embodiment reinforcement learning (building on Huang et al., 2020; Doshi et al., 2024) to avoid training separate policies per morphology. The key departure from prior co-design work (Ha et al., 2016; Schaff et al., 2019; Yuan et al., 2021) is the explicit sim-to-real pipeline: prior manipulation co-design has remained in simulation due to manufacturing and contact-modeling gaps, while this work grounds the grammar in physically realizable modular components (Dynamixel actuators, 3D-printed links) and deploys zero-shot. The morphology-conditioned PPO with action masking for variable degrees of freedom is a practical adaptation of shared-policy architectures to the discrete, combinatorial design space of hand morphology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, open-source pipeline (under 24 hours, under $1,500 hardware) that lowers the barrier to dexterous hand co-design research, and the 400× evaluation speedup from cross-embodiment policies makes large-scale morphology search tractable. The finding that morphology parameters (finger length, palm width) dominate over material and contact properties for manipulation performance provides a clear design prioritization for future work. Key limitations include task-specific optimization (no multi-task generalization), restricted exploration of palm geometry, and neglect of compliance and actuation scheme variation. Open questions include whether the cross-embodiment evaluation approach generalizes to contact-rich tasks with larger morphological gaps, and whether non-anthropomorphic designs can be made to generalize across diverse manipulation skills rather than single tasks.
