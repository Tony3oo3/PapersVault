## Problem

Existing fMRI generative models either synthesize static functional connectivity matrices that discard transient network states, or operate directly in the raw time domain where disentangling physiologically driven fluctuations (cardiac, respiratory, motion artifacts) from neural signals is difficult. Prior time-frequency image approaches like T2I-Diff rely on fixed-resolution STFT, which neglects fine-grained transients and attenuates frequency amplitude modulations, leading to reconstruction artifacts. No prior framework jointly captures both global multi-scale temporal dynamics and localized spectral energy compaction of BOLD signals.

## Input / Output

The model takes Gaussian noise in the DCT domain and an optional class label (e.g., HC vs. MDD) as input, and produces synthetic BOLD time series of shape D×T (116×232 for MDD with AAL atlas, 100×200 for ABIDE with Schaefer atlas) after inverse DCT and inverse DWT reconstruction. Generation occurs in a dual-spectral image space: the DWT scalogram is further decomposed into non-overlapping B×B blocks (B=4) that are individually DCT-encoded, so the U-ViT operates on a grid of small spectral patches rather than a single large image. Downstream, the reconstructed signals yield Ledoit-Wolf regularized sparse FC matrices (top 40% edges) for classification.

## Method

DSFM cascades two spectral transforms: a 5-level Haar DWT converts each ROI's BOLD signal into a multiresolution time-scale scalogram, and a blockwise 2D type-II DCT (B=4) exploits low-frequency energy compaction within each subband. The key insight is that the Laplacian operator in the forward heat-dissipation SPDE is diagonalized by the DCT eigenvectors, yielding a mode-wise ODE where each DCT coefficient evolves independently with its own Laplacian eigenvalue λ_k. This enables a spectral flow matching framework with per-mode conditional perturbation kernels p(z_t[k]|z_0[k]) = N(μ(t,k)·z_0[k], σ(t,k)²), where the mean schedule ω(t,k)=exp(−λ_k·τ(t)) encodes frequency-ordered heat dissipation. A U-ViT backbone parameterizes the velocity field and is trained with a conditional spectral flow matching (CSFM) loss; classifier-free guidance enables class-conditioned generation, and samples are produced by adaptive ODE integration (20–100 NFE). The dual-transform design imposes structured frequency priors that preserve both global slow drifts and local high-frequency transients, which ablations confirm are both necessary for downstream brain disorder classification.

## Results

On the MDD dataset (1× augmentation), DSFM achieves 70.84% accuracy and 71.49% ROC, outperforming the strongest baseline T2I-Diff (66.87% accuracy, 67.26% ROC) by ~4 points, while also attaining the best Context-FID (1.51) and Correlational score (57.30) among time-series generators. On ABIDE (1× augmentation), DSFM reaches 71.54% accuracy and 73.78% ROC versus T2I-Diff's 69.69% and 71.88%, with a Context-FID of 0.07 (vs. 0.82 for T2I-Diff). On the unconditional NetSim benchmark, DSFM achieves a cFID of 0.193, competitive with Diffusion-TS (0.105) and TimeGAN (0.126) but trailing them slightly, while substantially outperforming T2I-Diff (1.384) and all GAN-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSFM outperforms all 11 baselines on downstream brain disorder classification across both MDD and ABIDE. On MDD, DSFM (70.84% accuracy) beats the closest time-series generator Diffusion-TS (67.29%) by 3.55 points and T2I-Diff (66.87%) by 3.97 points, while FC-based GANs (Vanilla-GAN 58.86%, 2D-DCGAN 62.88%) lag by 8–12 points. On ABIDE, the margin over T2I-Diff narrows to 1.85 points (71.54% vs. 69.69%), but DSFM still leads Diffusion-TS (66.60%) and TimeGAN (66.59%) by ~5 points. On generation quality, DSFM achieves the best Context-FID on both MDD (1.51 vs. 2.06 for Diffusion-TS) and ABIDE (0.07 vs. 0.51 for Diffusion-TS), and the best Correlational score on ABIDE (13.42 vs. 25.75 for T2I-Diff). However, on the unconditional NetSim benchmark, DSFM (cFID 0.193) trails Diffusion-TS (0.105) and TimeGAN (0.126), indicating that the dual-spectral transform's advantage is most pronounced in the conditional, class-specific setting. For FC topology preservation (Table 6), DSFM achieves near-perfect Pearson correlation (0.99) on FC edges and node strength, far exceeding the best GAN baseline (WGAN-GP at 0.52 and 0.62 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

DSFM extends the time-frequency image generation paradigm introduced by T2I-Diff and ImagenTime, which recast fMRI BOLD signals as 2D images for generative modeling, but replaces the fixed-resolution STFT with a cascaded DWT + blockwise DCT dual-spectral transform to capture multi-scale transients and localized energy compaction. The generative backbone departs from the diffusion (SDE) framework used in T2I-Diff and Diffusion-TS, instead adopting flow matching (ODE-based) in the DCT domain, where the authors derive a mode-wise probability flow ODE from the inverse heat dissipation SPDE of Rissanen et al. (2023). The velocity field is parameterized with U-ViT, and classifier-free guidance enables class-conditional synthesis, positioning DSFM as a spectral-domain generalization of standard flow matching to a frequency-ordered, heat-dissipation perturbation process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-spectral representation addresses a genuine gap: FC-based generation loses transient network states, and STFT-based approaches (T2I-Diff) suffer from fixed time-frequency resolution that attenuates amplitude modulations. The derived mode-wise ODE in DCT space provides a principled, frequency-aware noise schedule that is more sample-efficient than isotropic diffusion, and the ablations confirm that mid-frequency BOLD oscillations (0.01–0.06 Hz) carry the strongest disease-discriminative signal. However, the method is currently limited to resting-state fMRI with two real datasets, classification gains over T2I-Diff are modest (~3–4% accuracy), and the 130M-parameter U-ViT backbone with 300k training iterations limits accessibility. Open questions include extension to task-based fMRI, other neuroimaging modalities (EEG, MEG, fNIRS), and whether the spectral flow matching framework generalizes to 3D volumetric BOLD data.
