## Problem

Optimization-based jailbreak attacks like GCG and its variants restrict adversarial tokens to a fixed suffix position, assuming the prompt end is the most vulnerable insertion point. This paper shows that the optimal slot varies substantially across prompts and is never the suffix, leaving a large positional vulnerability dimension entirely unexplored and making attacks easier to detect since only the prompt tail needs scanning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262370/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires a harmful prompt (text) and white-box access to the target LLM's attention weights. A single forward pass with probing tokens inserted at every slot (L+1 positions) yields the VSS scores, adding only ~200 ms of preprocessing. The output is an optimized adversarial prompt where tokens are distributed across multiple high-VSS slots (not just the suffix), specified as token sequences paired with their slot indices, ready for standard GCG-based optimization.

## Method

SlotGCG introduces the Vulnerable Slot Score (VSS), which quantifies positional vulnerability by summing attention weights from after-chat template tokens to adversarial tokens at each slot, restricted to upper-half transformer layers where jailbreak-relevant attention concentrates. The four-step pipeline is: (1) insert probing tokens into all L+1 slots and compute VSS per slot; (2) convert VSS to an insertion probability distribution via temperature-scaled softmax; (3) allocate the adversarial token budget proportionally across slots using largest-remainder rounding; (4) run standard GCG-based optimization on the multi-slot adversarial sequences. The key empirical finding is that high-VSS slots are inherent to the prompt (VSS^init correlates 0.4–0.9 with VSS^final across optimization), so the slot selection is a one-shot, attack-agnostic preprocessing step that can be plugged into any GCG variant. This positional targeting yields 14% higher ASR, up to 10× fewer optimization steps, and 42% higher ASR under input-filtering defenses compared to suffix-only baselines.

## Results

On the AdvBench dataset (50 harmful prompts), SlotGCG achieves an average ASR of 80.0% when combined with GCG (vs. 66.7% baseline, +13.3%), 86.3% with AttnGCG (+24.6%), and 85.7% with I-GCG (+12.7%) across six LLMs. Under defense methods, SlotGCG+GCG maintains 46.2% average ASR versus 18.9% for baseline GCG (+27.3%). In terms of efficiency, SlotGCG reduces the average number of GCG iterations from 72.59 to 28.59 (a ~2.5× speedup), with up to 10× speedup on Llama-2-7B (138.11 → 40.50 iterations).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SlotGCG consistently outperforms all five baselines on average, with the largest gains on Llama models where suffix-based attacks are weakest: +28.0% over GCG, +48.0% over AttnGCG, and +38.0% over I-GCG on Llama-2-7B. The most dramatic single improvement is +62.0% ASR for AttnGCG on Llama-2-13B (20.0% → 82.0%), revealing vulnerabilities invisible to suffix-only optimization. GBDA, the weakest baseline (20.3% average ASR), benefits most in absolute terms (+19.7%), reaching 40.0% average ASR. However, on already-vulnerable models like Mistral-7B and Vicuna-7B, SlotGCG sometimes slightly underperforms the baseline (e.g., −6.0% for AttnGCG on Vicuna-7B), suggesting diminishing returns when the suffix is already near-optimal. Under defenses (Table 3), SlotGCG maintains a 27.3–32.9% ASR advantage over baselines across GCG, AttnGCG, I-GCG, and GCG-Hij, with the largest gap under Erase-and-Check (suffix) where baselines drop to 0% but SlotGCG retains 52–66% ASR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GCG family of optimization-based jailbreak attacks (GCG, AttnGCG, I-GCG, GCG-Hij, GBDA) by introducing a positional dimension that all prior methods ignore: they uniformly restrict adversarial tokens to the prompt suffix. The slot/insertion formalism is borrowed from Stern et al. (2019), and the attention-based vulnerability signal builds on recent observations that adversarial suffixes hijack attention in upper layers (Ben-Tov et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is treating token *position* as a first-class optimization variable rather than a fixed design choice, yielding an attack-agnostic position-search module (VSS) that can be prepended to any GCG variant with ~200 ms overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the suffix is never the optimal insertion slot across 50 diverse prompts challenges a foundational assumption in the jailbreak literature and suggests that current defenses (e.g., Erase-and-Check suffix removal) are structurally mismatched to the actual attack surface. The VSS metric offers an interpretable, attention-grounded diagnostic that could inform defense design (e.g., monitoring attention dispersion across positions rather than just suffix tokens). Limitations include the white-box requirement for attention access, evaluation only on 7B–13B open models, and the fact that Perplexity Filter still blocks all variants completely. Open questions include whether positional vulnerability persists in larger or closed-source models, and whether adaptive defenses can learn to detect multi-position insertions.
