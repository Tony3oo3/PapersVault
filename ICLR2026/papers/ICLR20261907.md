## Problem

Open-vocabulary detectors struggle to generalize to out-of-distribution classes and require heavy text encoders for fine-tuning, while specialist real-time detectors like D-FINE and RT-DETR are implicitly over-optimized for COCO through bespoke architectures, learning-rate schedulers, and augmentation pipelines that do not transfer to diverse real-world datasets. No prior work applies end-to-end weight-sharing neural architecture search to object detection, leaving a gap in discovering accuracy-latency Pareto curves for arbitrary target domains without re-training.

## Input / Output

RF-DETR takes a single RGB image as input, with the input resolution itself being a tunable NAS parameter (ranging across multiple resolutions during training via interpolated positional embeddings). At inference, a specific operating point on the Pareto curve is selected by fixing patch size, image resolution, number of decoder layers, number of query tokens, and window count. The model outputs end-to-end bounding boxes with class labels (no NMS required), and the RF-DETR-Seg variant additionally produces per-object instance segmentation masks via a lightweight pixel-embedding head that computes dot products between projected query embeddings and a bilinearly interpolated feature map.

## Method

RF-DETR builds on LW-DETR by replacing its CAEv2 backbone with a DINOv2 ViT and introducing an end-to-end weight-sharing NAS that jointly trains thousands of sub-network configurations in a single run. At each training iteration, a random architecture configuration is sampled from a search space spanning patch size (FlexiViT-style interpolation), number of decoder layers, number of query tokens, image resolution, and number of windows per windowed-attention block; a gradient update is then applied, effectively performing 'architecture augmentation' as a regularizer. After full training on a target dataset, any sub-net within the search space achieves strong performance without further fine-tuning, and the optimal configuration for a given latency budget is selected via grid search on a validation set. The approach is deliberately scheduler-free—using layer normalization instead of batch normalization, a lower learning rate to preserve DINOv2's pre-trained knowledge, and minimal augmentations (horizontal flip and random crop only)—to avoid the COCO-specific biases introduced by aggressive augmentation and fixed-horizon cosine schedules. For segmentation, a lightweight head is added that upsamples the same low-resolution feature map used for detection and learns pixel embeddings interpretable as segmentation prototypes, pre-trained on Objects-365 pseudo-labeled with SAM2.

## Results

On COCO detection, RF-DETR (nano) achieves 48.0 AP at 2.3 ms latency, outperforming D-FINE (nano) (42.7 AP) and LW-DETR (tiny) (42.9 AP) by over 5 AP at comparable speed. RF-DETR (2x-large) reaches 60.1 AP at 17.2 ms, the first real-time detector to surpass 60 AP on COCO. On Roboflow100-VL, RF-DETR (2x-large) achieves 63.3 AP, beating GroundingDINO (tiny) (62.3 AP) by 1.0 AP while running 20× faster (15.6 ms vs 309.9 ms). For instance segmentation, RF-DETR-Seg (nano) achieves 40.3 AP at 3.4 ms, outperforming all YOLOv8/YOLOv11 sizes and beating FastInst (34.9 AP) by 5.4 AP at nearly 10× lower latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RF-DETR (nano) decisively outperforms D-FINE (nano) by 5.3 AP (48.0 vs 42.7) and LW-DETR (tiny) by 5.1 AP (48.0 vs 42.9) on COCO at nearly identical latency (~2.1–2.3 ms). At the medium size, RF-DETR (M) matches D-FINE (M) within 0.3 AP (54.7 vs 55.0) while being 1 ms faster, and beats RT-DETR (R18) by 5.7 AP (54.7 vs 49.0). On Roboflow100-VL, RF-DETR (2x-large) surpasses GroundingDINO (tiny) and LLMDet (tiny) by ~1 AP (63.3 vs 62.3) at 20× lower latency (15.6 ms vs ~310 ms), and outperforms D-FINE (M) by 1.1 AP (61.7 vs 60.6). For segmentation, RF-DETR-Seg (nano) beats FastInst (R50) by 5.4 AP (40.3 vs 34.9) at ~10× lower latency, and approaches MaskDINO (R50) (45.3 vs 46.3 AP) at 40× lower latency. Notably, RF-DETR (nano) matches the accuracy of YOLOv8/YOLOv11 (medium) while using fewer parameters and running faster, and the paper shows that D-FINE's COCO-tuned hyperparameters actually hurt its RF100-VL generalization relative to the simpler RT-DETR baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

RF-DETR modernizes LW-DETR by replacing its CAEv2 backbone with DINOv2 and simplifying the training procedure, while adopting the weight-sharing NAS paradigm from OFA (Cai et al., 2019) and extending it from image classification to end-to-end object detection and segmentation. Unlike prior NAS work that searched only backbones or FPNs, RF-DETR treats the full detection pipeline—patch size, decoder depth, query count, image resolution, and window attention—as a joint search space, with FlexiViT-style interpolation enabling continuous patch-size variation. Conceptually, the paper departs from the specialist-detector tradition of bespoke learning-rate and augmentation schedulers tuned to COCO, arguing instead that internet-scale pretraining plus scheduler-free training yields better cross-domain transferability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RF-DETR is the first real-time detector to exceed 60 AP on COCO and demonstrates that a single training run can produce a continuous accuracy-latency Pareto curve deployable across hardware targets without retraining, which has direct practical value for production systems. The finding that D-FINE (built on RT-DETR) underperforms its own base on RF100-VL provides concrete evidence that COCO-tuned hyperparameters harm generalization, motivating broader evaluation protocols. The standardized 200 ms-buffering latency benchmarking protocol addresses a real reproducibility gap in the field. Open questions include whether the NAS-mined configurations generalize to truly unseen architectures at scale, and whether the 'architecture augmentation' regularization effect holds for even smaller or more diverse target datasets.
