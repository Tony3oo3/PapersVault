## Problem

Online RL algorithms like GRPO cannot learn from problems where the base policy produces zero correct rollouts, since no reward signal generates any gradient. This means the model's upper limit (pass@k) remains unchanged after RL training, as hard, unsolvable samples contribute nothing to learning. The paper asks whether self-generated hints can transform previously unlearnable problems into productive training signals, effectively expanding the model's reasoning boundary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261037/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes text questions paired with gold answers (for offline hint generation) and produces G rollouts per question, each a chain-of-thought reasoning trace ending in a boxed final answer. When all rollouts fail, the offline-generated abstract hint is concatenated to the question and G-1 new rollouts are regenerated. At test time, only the question is provided (no hints), and the model outputs a reasoning chain with a final answer, evaluated by rule-based outcome correctness.

## Method

NuRL operates in two stages. Offline, the model generates a Chain-of-Thought explanation conditioned on the gold answer, then abstracts it into a high-level cue that identifies the core knowledge needed without revealing the answer or solution steps. Online, standard GRPO training runs first until convergence; then, for any problem where all G rollouts are incorrect (0% pass rate), the pre-generated hint is appended to the question and G-1 new rollouts are regenerated (one rollout remains hint-free to avoid uniform correctness). The key design choices are that hints must be abstract and high-level (revealing the answer causes reward hacking and hurts generalization), and hints should be applied only after GRPO has stabilized and only when triggered by complete rollout failure. This two-stage, difficulty-triggered injection strategy consistently outperforms applying hints from the start or uniformly across all problems.

## Results

Across six benchmarks (MATH 500, MATH Hard, AIME 2024, GPQA Diamond, MMLU-Pro, Date Understanding) and three models, NuRL with self-generated abstract cues improves average pass@1 over GRPO by +1.62 points on Llama3.2-3B (37.49 vs. 35.87), +1.75 points on OctoThinker-3B (44.38 vs. 42.63), and +0.79 points on Qwen3-4B (80.10 vs. 79.31). When hints are generated by GPT-o4-mini, the average gain over GRPO widens to +3.44 points on Llama (39.31 vs. 35.87) and +3.04 points on OctoThinker (45.67 vs. 42.63). Notably, NuRL uses only 8 rollouts per question (vs. 16 for GRPO) yet still outperforms, and it raises pass@1024 on tasks where the base model's ceiling is not saturated (e.g., 86.4%→94.0% on Date Understanding, 63.6%→69.7% on GPQA), whereas GRPO leaves pass@1024 unchanged.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NuRL consistently outperforms all four baselines across all three models. Compared to Zero-shot (Kojima et al., 2022), NuRL gains +23.9 to +9.8 average points depending on model, confirming that RL-based training is essential. Against RFT (Yuan et al., 2023), which performs rejection-sampling SFT, NuRL improves by +20.4 to +9.4 points, showing that RL with verifiable rewards substantially surpasses supervised fine-tuning on correct trajectories. Versus ReFT (Trung et al., 2024), which chains SFT followed by GRPO, NuRL still leads by +3.7 to +1.0 points, indicating that the hint-injection mechanism adds value beyond standard SFT+RL pipelines. The most critical comparison is against GRPO (Guo et al., 2025): NuRL wins on average by +1.62 (Llama), +1.75 (OctoThinker), and +0.79 (Qwen) points while using half the rollouts (8 vs. 16), and it uniquely raises pass@1024 on tasks where the base model's ceiling is not yet saturated (e.g., +7.6 points on Date Understanding, +6.1 on GPQA), whereas GRPO leaves pass@1024 unchanged from the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

NuRL builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its policy-optimization backbone, extending it with a targeted hint-injection mechanism for samples where all rollouts fail. It positions itself against the 'distribution sharpening' view of RL (Yue et al., 2025; He et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025) by demonstrating that the pass@k ceiling can be raised, not merely pass@1 sharpened. The approach draws on a growing line of hint-based training methods (Zhang et al., 2025b; Huang et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025; Qu et al., 2025) and the STaR paradigm (Zelikman et al., 2022) of conditioning on answers, but departs from them by (a) operating purely within the RL stage without SFT, (b) using self-generated abstract cues rather than full explanations or partial solutions, and (c) applying hints only under a difficulty trigger after GRPO convergence, framing the intervention through Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and widely acknowledged limitation of online RLVR: hard samples that yield zero reward across all rollouts produce no gradient and are effectively discarded. By showing that lightweight, self-generated abstract hints can convert these dead samples into productive training signals—and that this translates into measurable pass@k gains on tasks where the base model's ceiling is not yet saturated—NuRL offers a practical recipe for expanding a model's reasoning boundary without external teacher models. The systematic ablation revealing that more answer-disclosure in hints monotonically degrades performance (with direct answer injection causing reward hacking) is a useful design principle for future hint-based or curriculum-based RL. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models where the 'unsolvable' fraction shrinks, whether hints can be generated without gold answers (removing the need for verified training data), and whether the two-stage protocol (GRPO convergence first) is strictly necessary or an artifact of the small model sizes tested.
