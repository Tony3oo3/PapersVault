## Problem

Existing LLM safety alignment pipelines suffer from two key limitations: (1) training datasets are imbalanced, overrepresenting common hazards while neglecting long-tail threats, and (2) reward models are static, applying uniform reward signals regardless of task difficulty, which limits optimization efficiency and leaves rare but high-impact threats under-trained. The paper asks whether combining balanced demonstration-based reward learning with difficulty-aware dynamic scaling can simultaneously improve safety and preserve utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262017/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes harmful instruction prompts (text) spanning seven predefined categories (Insult, Unfairness, Crimes, Physical Harm, Mental Health, Privacy, Ethics) and produces an aligned LLM policy that generates structured safe refusals. Training data uses Chain-of-Draft (CoD) demonstrations—concise 5-word-per-step reasoning chains followed by a final refusal—rather than the more verbose Chain-of-Thought format or pairwise preference labels. The per-category shadow reward models score (prompt, response) pairs, and the final output is a fine-tuned policy optimized via GRPO with dynamically scaled advantages.

## Method

DR-IRL first trains seven category-specific shadow reward models via maximum-likelihood IRL on a balanced CoD demonstration dataset, where the bilevel optimization jointly learns reward parameters and an induced policy (following Li et al., 2024). During GRPO-based policy optimization, each sample's advantage is multiplied by a combined hardness coefficient α = α^D · α^M: the data-level term α^D measures semantic dissimilarity between the demonstration and the model's generated response via text-encoder cosine similarity over sub-sentences, while the model-level term α^M captures the reward gap between demonstration and generated response (with outlier filtering). This multiplicative AND-gate concentrates optimization on samples that are simultaneously content-hard and still uncertain for the model, preventing over-optimization on trivial cases. The per-category reward design avoids reward interference across heterogeneous safety intents, and the hardness mechanism is shown to generalize to PPO and DPO as well.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B, DR-IRL achieves the highest StrongReject score (0.9361), WildChat refusal rate (74.21%), and Stereotype refusal rate (98.87%), while also leading on helpfulness benchmarks (AdvGLUE 70.71%, GSM8k 88.10%, HHH 86.16%). On Qwen-2-7B, DR-IRL similarly dominates with StrongReject 0.8798, WildChat 81.53%, Stereotype 99.03%, and AdvGLUE 75.15%. Across both models, DR-IRL improves StrongReject by 4.4–36.4 points over GRPO and by 5.6–3.1 points over STAIR, while maintaining or improving general capability metrics, effectively reducing the alignment tax.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DR-IRL outperforms all eight baselines on the primary safety metric (StrongReject) for both Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen-2-7B. Against the closest competitor STAIR (Zhang et al., 2025), DR-IRL gains +5.63 points on Llama (0.9361 vs. 0.8798) and +3.12 points on Qwen (0.8798 vs. 0.8486), while also improving WildChat refusal by +4.35 and +0.97 points respectively. Compared to GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), the improvement is more dramatic: +12.56 points on Llama and +36.43 points on Qwen for StrongReject, confirming that dynamic reward scaling is the key differentiator over static group-relative optimization. Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and SACPO (Wachi et al., 2024), DR-IRL leads by 43+ and 21+ points on StrongReject (Llama), showing that reward-based IRL with hardness-aware scaling substantially surpasses preference-optimization approaches. On helpfulness, DR-IRL matches or exceeds STAIR on AdvGLUE and GSM8k while improving HHH, indicating that the alignment tax is reduced rather than merely shifted. PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) is the weakest RL baseline (0.6902 on Llama StrongReject), and even the difficulty-weighted PPO-S variant (0.7724) trails DR-IRL by 16.4 points, underscoring the advantage of GRPO's group-relative normalization combined with dynamic scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Li et al. (2024), who demonstrated that reward learning from single-response demonstrations via IRL can outperform preference-pair-based reward models for LLM alignment. It departs from that work by (1) training category-specific shadow reward models rather than a single unified one, and (2) decoupling reward learning from policy optimization into separate stages. The policy optimization stage extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) with a dynamic reward-scaling mechanism inspired by the data-and-model-aware alignment of multimodal LLMs (Lu et al., 2025), but applied here to text-only safety alignment with a multiplicative hardness gate combining text-encoder cosine similarity and reward-gap signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical pain points in safety alignment—imbalanced training data that neglects long-tail threats and static reward models that treat all samples equally—offering a modular solution (per-category IRL + difficulty-aware GRPO) that practitioners can adopt incrementally. The finding that the hardness coefficient generalizes across PPO, DPO, and GRPO suggests the difficulty-weighting idea is a broadly applicable training trick rather than a GRPO-specific hack. Key open questions include whether per-category reward models scale to finer or evolving threat taxonomies without prohibitive compute, and whether the synthetic CoD demonstrations capture subtle cultural and adversarial patterns that real-world deployment demands.
