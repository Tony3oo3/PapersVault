## Problem

The best fully first-order method for stochastic bilevel optimization, F²SA, achieves only Õ(ε⁻⁶) stochastic first-order oracle complexity, leaving a significant gap from the Ω(ε⁻⁴) lower bound known for single-level SGD. This paper asks whether faster rates are achievable when the lower-level function enjoys higher-order smoothness in y, and whether the gap can be closed in the highly-smooth regime.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires only stochastic gradient estimators of the upper-level function f and lower-level function g (standard SGD assumptions with bounded variance σ², no Hessian or HVP access), plus an initial point (x₀, y₀). At each outer iteration it solves p (even p) or p+1 (odd p) perturbed lower-level subproblems in parallel via K-step SGD, then combines their x-gradients with finite-difference coefficients to produce a single hypergradient estimate. The output is a point x satisfying E‖∇φ(x)‖ ≤ ε, with total SFO complexity Õ(p·κ^{9+2/p}·ε^{-4-2/p}).

## Method

The key insight is reinterpreting F²SA's penalty-based hypergradient estimator as a first-order forward difference approximation of ∂²ℓ_ν(x)/∂ν∂x at ν=0. By replacing the forward difference with a pth-order finite difference (central difference for even p, asymmetric for odd p), the approximation error improves from O(ν) to O(ν^p), allowing the perturbation parameter ν to be set as small as O(ε^{1/p}) while maintaining O(ε) hypergradient accuracy. Concretely, F²SA-p runs K-step SGD on p (or p+1) perturbed lower-level objectives g_{jν}(x,·) = jν·f(x,·) + g(x,·) in parallel, then forms a weighted linear combination of the resulting x-gradients using the finite-difference coefficients from Lemma 3.1. The outer loop performs normalized gradient descent on this composite estimate. Combined with a high-dimensional Faà di Bruno analysis showing the (p+1)th mixed derivative is O(κ^{2p+1}L̄)-Lipschitz in ν, this yields the Õ(p·κ^{9+2/p}·ε^{-4-2/p}) SFO bound, which matches the Ω(ε⁻⁴) lower bound up to logarithmic factors when p = Ω(log(κ/ε)/log log(κ/ε)).

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is an improved SFO complexity bound for finding an ε-stationary point in stochastic bilevel optimization: F²SA-p achieves $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(p\kappa^{9+2/p}\epsilon^{-4-2/p})$, improving upon the prior best $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{12}\epsilon^{-6})$ for first-order smooth problems (p=1) and matching the $\Omega(\epsilon^{-4})$ lower bound up to logarithmic factors when $p=\Omega(\log\epsilon^{-1}/\log\log\epsilon^{-1})$. In the highly-smooth regime, the complexity simplifies to $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{9}\epsilon^{-4})$, matching the best-known HVP-based methods under stronger stochastic Hessian assumptions. Empirically, on the 20 Newsgroups learn-to-regularize problem (18,000 samples, 130,107 features), F²SA-p with p∈{2,3,5,8,10} is compared against F²SA, stocBiO, MRBO, VRBO, and an unregularized SGD baseline over 1000 outer-loop iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's F²SA-p strictly improves upon the prior best fully first-order method F²SA: for p=1, the condition number dependency tightens from $\kappa^{12}$ to $\kappa^{11}$, and for p≥2 the ε-dependency improves from $\epsilon^{-6}$ to $\epsilon^{-4-2/p}$. In the highly-smooth regime ($p=\Omega(\log\epsilon^{-1}/\log\log\epsilon^{-1})$), F²SA-p achieves $\tilde{\mathcal{O}}(\kappa^{9}\epsilon^{-4})$, matching the complexity of HVP-based methods like stocBiO (Ji et al., 2021) that require the stronger stochastic Hessian assumption (5). Against MRBO and VRBO (Yang et al., 2021), which rely on both stochastic Hessian and mean-squared smoothness assumptions, F²SA-p operates under only standard SGD assumptions. In the empirical comparison on 20 Newsgroups (Figure 1), F²SA-p with p∈{2,3,5,8,10} is evaluated alongside F²SA, stocBiO, MRBO, VRBO, and an unregularized SGD baseline; the paper reports that higher-p variants of F²SA-p achieve competitive or superior test loss/accuracy trajectories, with F²SA-2 being particularly attractive since it requires the same number of lower-level solves as F²SA per iteration while benefiting from second-order smoothness when available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the F²SA method (Kwon et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025b) as a forward-difference approximation of the hypergradient, a connection previously established only in the meta-learning context by Chayti & Jaggi (2024). Building on this insight, the authors generalize from first-order to pth-order finite differences, yielding the F²SA-p family that exploits high-order smoothness in the lower-level variable. The two-time-scale stepsize framework from Chen et al. (2025b) is retained, and the Ω(ε⁻⁴) lower bound is extended from single-level SGD (Arjevani et al., 2023) to bilevel optimization via a fully separable construction that avoids the smoothness violations present in prior bilevel lower-bound attempts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The F²SA-p method closes the gap between the O(ε⁻⁶) upper bound and Ω(ε⁻⁴) lower bound in the highly-smooth regime (p = Ω(log ε⁻¹ / log log ε⁻¹)), matching the best-known HVP-based complexity with purely first-order oracles. This is practically significant because F²SA is currently the only bilevel method scalable to 32B-parameter LLM training. However, a gap persists for small p (especially p=1, where the bound is O(κ¹¹ε⁻⁶) vs. Ω(ε⁻⁴)), and the condition-number dependency (κ⁹) remains far from tight, with concurrent works only partially closing this gap. The experiments are limited to a single logistic-regression hyperparameter-tuning task on 20 Newsgroups, leaving open whether the theoretical gains translate to more complex nonconvex-nonconvex or deep-learning settings.
