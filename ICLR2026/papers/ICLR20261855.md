## Problem

Existing reasoning benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, GPQA, SuperGPQA) are saturated on simpler tasks, while agentic benchmarks (GAIA, BrowseComp, PaperBench) lack either multi-domain breadth or the depth of professional, cutting-edge reasoning required for graduate-level research. No benchmark simultaneously covers diverse academic fields (CS, economics, law, math, philosophy) with questions demanding full multi-step derivations from recent top-tier publications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261855/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task presents a self-contained research question (with minimal necessary background) extracted from a single 2023–2025 top-tier paper; the model must produce a long-form research report without access to the source text. Optionally, three types of hints (background, definition, methodology) can be appended to the prompt. Evaluation yields two scores: a binary Pass Rate (exact match to a golden answer) and a Checklist Score (fraction of 5 per-question, 250 total verifiable checkpoints satisfied), both on a 0–100 scale.

## Method

The benchmark is built via a three-stage expert annotation pipeline: (1) 430 papers from leading journals/conferences are filtered to 50 purely theoretical works across five domains; (2) one comprehensive research question and a full-scope golden answer are extracted per paper; (3) dynamic checklists (5 verifiable items per question, length adapted to complexity) and three-category hints are derived. Evaluation uses GPT-5-mini as an LLM judge, validated against three human experts per domain (Cohen's κ = 0.861; 89.55% checklist accuracy vs. human consensus). A URL-masking ablation confirms agents are not simply retrieving source papers, and a hint ablation isolates the contribution of background, definition, and methodology knowledge, revealing methodology hints as the most impactful.

## Results

On the ACADREASON benchmark (50 expert-annotated academic research problems across CS, Economics, Law, Math, and Philosophy), the best single model GPT-5 achieves only a 16% pass rate and 40.5 checklist score, while the top agent framework OAgents reaches 34% pass rate and 65.1 checklist score. Most general models (GPT-4.1, Claude-Sonnet-4, DeepSeek-V3) score below 10 pass rate, with GPT-4.1 and Claude-Sonnet-4 scoring 0. Reasoning models consistently outperform their general counterparts (e.g., DeepSeek-R1: 23.8 vs. DeepSeek-V3: 15.9 checklist score), and agents substantially outperform all standalone LLMs, yet no system exceeds 40 pass rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among general models, GPT-5 (16/40.5) dominates, outperforming GPT-OSS (4/32.2), DeepSeek-V3.1 (2/24.8), DeepSeek-V3 (2/15.9), Claude-Sonnet-4 (0/24.7), and GPT-4.1 (0/21.0) on both pass rate and checklist score. Within reasoning models, Qwen3 and Kimi-K2 tie at 6/20.3 pass rate, while o3 leads on checklist score (33.4) and DeepSeek-R1 (23.8) outperforms its general counterpart DeepSeek-V3 (15.9), confirming the reasoning advantage. Among agents, OAgents (34/65.1) is the clear leader, beating Gemini-2.5-Pro-DeepResearch (28/53.4), Tongyi DeepResearch (20/30.9), o3-DeepResearch (14/47.1), AFM (14/40.5), WebThinker (8/36.4), MiroThinker (0/26.5), and WebDancer (0/16.4). Notably, even the best agent OAgents achieves only 34% pass rate, and URL-masking experiments confirm its performance is not inflated by direct source-paper retrieval (32 vs. 34 pass rate with/without masking). The ablation with hints shows GPT-5 jumps from 16/40.5 to 40/67.8 with all hints, surpassing OAgents, with methodology hints yielding the largest single-type gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining two dimensions that prior benchmarks address separately: domain breadth (spanning CS, Economics, Law, Math, and Philosophy) and reasoning depth (full multi-step solution trajectories from top-tier 2023–2025 publications). It departs from short-answer QA benchmarks (GPQA, MMLU-Pro, HLE) and code-reproduction benchmarks (PaperBench) by requiring models to produce complete research-level reports, and from search-centric benchmarks (BrowseComp, GAIA, XBench-DeepSearch) by emphasizing reasoning over retrieval. The evaluation methodology builds on the LLM-as-Judge paradigm (Yue et al., Rein et al.) but introduces dynamic, per-question checklists and a three-tier hint ablation (background/definition/methodology) not present in prior evaluation frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a substantial capability gap: even GPT-5 achieves only 16/100 pass rate and the best agent (OAgents) only 34/100, while the same models score 84%+ on GPQA Diamond—suggesting current systems lack the deep, multi-step methodological reasoning required for genuine academic research. The finding that methodology hints yield the largest performance gains (vs. background hints) points to a specific bottleneck in current models' knowledge. Key limitations include the small size (50 questions), reliance on a single LLM judge (GPT-5-mini), and the fact that all questions are drawn from a single annotation cycle, raising questions about generalizability. Open questions include whether semi-automatic scaling can maintain annotation quality and whether the observed LLM-vs-agent gap will narrow as models improve.
