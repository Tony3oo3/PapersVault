## Problem

Generating jointly correlated samples from multiple pre-trained diffusion models while simultaneously enforcing task-specific hard constraints (e.g., velocity limits, collision avoidance, attribute constraints) without costly retraining has remained challenging. Existing test-time techniques address either constrained single-variable generation via projection or joint generation via coupling, but not both simultaneously, leaving a gap for tasks requiring correlated outputs that must also satisfy strict feasibility requirements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264418/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two or more independently trained diffusion score models, a differentiable coupling cost function (analytic or neural), and test-time constraint sets defined as feasible regions (e.g., velocity-bounded trajectory spaces, convex hulls of exemplar latents). It produces joint sample tuples—multi-robot trajectory sets, distinct trajectory pairs, or paired images—that are mutually correlated through the cost function and guaranteed to lie within the constraint sets. For the image-pair task, inputs include exemplar images whose VAE-encoded latents form convex hulls; for robotics, inputs are start/goal positions or initial observations, and outputs are full trajectory sequences of shape H×2.

## Method

PCD extends standard Langevin Monte Carlo dynamics by adding a coupling term −γδ∇c(X_t, Y_t) to each variable's update, where c(x,y) is a differentiable cost function that encourages desired correlations (e.g., collision avoidance, age-group contrast, trajectory diversity), and then applies a projection operator Π_K onto the constraint set at every diffusion step. This two-part update—coupled dynamics followed by projection—guarantees hard constraint satisfaction while promoting inter-variable coordination, and it generalizes classifier guidance (one variable fixed, no projection) and projected diffusion (zero coupling) as special cases. The framework is inference-only: no retraining of the base diffusion models is required, and the coupling cost can be an analytic function, a trained classifier, or a regression model. A posterior-sampling variant uses Tweedie's formula to compute the cost on denoised estimates rather than noisy latents, improving gradient quality. Three inference algorithms are provided (LMC, DDPM, and DPS-based), with the projection solved via ADMM for velocity constraints or mirror descent for convex-hull constraints.

## Results

On the 4-robot multi-robot navigation benchmark (MMD), PCD-SHD achieves 100% constraint satisfaction (CS) and 100% inter-robot safety (RS) on both the Empty and Highways tasks, while also attaining 100% success rate (SU) and 0.96–0.99 data adherence (DA). On the PushT manipulation task, PCD-DPP-PS achieves 100% velocity constraint satisfaction with the highest DTW (4.4) and DFD (0.62) among projection-based methods, indicating strong trajectory separation. On paired face generation, PCD attains 96% age-group XOR contrast and 100% gender/attribute constraint satisfaction, outperforming all baselines on both coupling and projection metrics simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFUSER (Janner et al., 2022) serves as the unconstrained baseline: it achieves only 65% SU and 62.3% CS on the Empty task, and 53% SU with 66.7% CS on Highways, confirming that vanilla diffusion cannot satisfy hard velocity constraints or reliably avoid collisions. MMD-CBS (Shaoul et al., 2025) achieves perfect SU by selecting a single optimal trajectory but fails to enforce velocity constraints (11% CS on Empty, 63% on Highways), since it has no projection mechanism. On PushT, vanilla DP (Chi et al., 2023) achieves only 65% CS and the lowest DTW (3.2) and DFD (0.47), meaning trajectories are neither constraint-compliant nor spatially separated; PCD-DPP-PS improves DTW to 4.4 and DFD to 0.62 while reaching 100% CS. For paired face generation, vanilla SD (Rombach et al., 2022) achieves only 20% XOR contrast and 71/14% M/F satisfaction; SyncDiffusion (Lee et al., 2023) matches SD+C on XOR (48%) but shows attribute drift (95/17% M/F); ControlNet-XS (Zavadski et al., 2024) enforces gender (100% M) but only achieves 8% XOR contrast. PCD dominates all baselines by simultaneously achieving 100% constraint satisfaction and the highest coupling metric (96% XOR) across all three experimental domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCD unifies two previously separate lines of diffusion-based inference: (i) coupled/synchronized generation from multiple pre-trained score models (as in SyncDiffusion, MultiDiffusion, MMDisco) and (ii) per-step projection for hard constraint enforcement (as in Projected Diffusion Models, CGD). The paper's key conceptual contribution is showing that classifier guidance, projected diffusion, compositional diffusion, and discriminator-based joint diffusion are all special cases of a single coupled-plus-projected Langevin update, providing a clean algebraic lineage. The coupling mechanism draws on the cost-function guidance paradigm (DPS, Tweedie's formula) while the projection step inherits the ADMM-based solvers from constrained optimization, making the framework modular in both the cost and the constraint set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For robotics and safety-critical applications, PCD offers a practical test-time recipe: take any number of independently trained diffusion policies, add a differentiable coupling cost for inter-agent coordination, and wrap each update in a projection onto the feasible set — all without retraining. The main open questions are (i) scaling the coupling cost to many variables (the pairwise sum grows quadratically), (ii) handling non-differentiable or non-convex constraints where ADMM-based projection no longer applies cleanly, and (iii) the coupling-strength hyperparameter, which trades data fidelity against correlation strength and currently requires manual tuning. The assumption that the feasible region must overlap the learned distribution's support is a practical limitation that becomes critical under aggressive constraints.
