## Problem

Existing 3D thermal reconstruction methods are limited to static scenes, while dynamic approaches either rely on data-driven temporal embeddings without physical consistency (4DGS, ThermalGS) or use implicit neural networks with numerical integration that are computationally expensive (NTR-Gaussian). No prior method combines explicit thermodynamic physics with efficient Gaussian splatting to reconstruct rapidly evolving thermal scenes at near-static rendering speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263995/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pixel-aligned RGB-IR image pairs at 512×410 resolution with millisecond-accurate timestamps; RGB images provide the initial point cloud and camera poses, while raw grayscale thermal images serve as differentiable training targets. The output is a 3D field of thermal Gaussians, each carrying physically interpretable parameters (heat capacity, heat transfer coefficient, harmonic heat-source amplitudes), from which novel thermal views can be rendered at any arbitrary timestamp via a closed-form temperature evaluation and linear temperature-to-intensity mapping.

## Method

Each Gaussian in the scene is augmented with equivalent heat capacity C_i, heat transfer coefficient h_i, and a heat-source excitation Q_i(t) expanded over K=24 log-uniformly spaced harmonic frequencies. The temperature state T_i(t) is governed by a first-order linear ODE (Newtonian cooling plus heat-source forcing), whose closed-form solution is derived analytically via the integrating-factor method, yielding an explicit expression (Eq. 8) evaluable at any timestamp without numerical integration. This closed-form formulation naturally handles irregular and out-of-order sampling, and its differentiability allows standard 3DGS-style gradient-based optimization (L1 + D-SSIM loss plus L2 regularization on harmonic coefficients) to learn all thermal parameters end-to-end. Rendering replaces the spherical-harmonic color term with a linearly normalized grayscale intensity, preserving the alpha-compositing pipeline and achieving 458 FPS—comparable to static 3DGS—while outperforming all dynamic baselines by ~5 dB PSNR over the closest competitor.

## Results

On the RHD dataset (10 dynamic thermal scenes, 2363 views), ETGS achieves an average PSNR of 40.68 dB, SSIM of 0.989, and LPIPS of 0.050, outperforming all five baselines across every metric. The closest competitor, NTR-Gaussian, scores 34.96 dB PSNR and 0.089 LPIPS, yielding a ~5.7 dB PSNR gap and a 0.039 LPIPS improvement for ETGS. In terms of efficiency, ETGS trains in 197 s and renders at 458 FPS—close to static 3DGS (166 s, 557 FPS) and roughly 7× faster in training than NTR-Gaussian (1469 s, 68 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETGS outperforms all five baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all 10 RHD scenes. Against the strongest dynamic thermal baseline NTR-Gaussian, ETGS gains ~5.7 dB in PSNR (40.68 vs. 34.96) and reduces LPIPS from 0.089 to 0.050, while training ~7.5× faster (197 s vs. 1469 s) and rendering ~6.7× faster (458 vs. 68 FPS). Compared to the best static thermal method Thermal3D-GS, ETGS improves PSNR by 6.0 dB and LPIPS by 0.022, demonstrating that explicit thermodynamic modeling captures rapid temperature changes that static representations cannot. Against the general-purpose dynamic method 4DGS, ETGS leads by 6.7 dB PSNR and 0.017 SSIM, confirming that data-driven temporal embeddings are insufficient for physically consistent thermal evolution. The static baselines 3DGS and Mip-Splatting trail by 8.5 and 9.2 dB PSNR respectively, as they can only learn a single average temperature per Gaussian and cannot represent any temporal variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ETGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting by replacing the spherical-harmonic color term with explicit thermodynamic parameters (equivalent heat capacity, heat transfer coefficient, harmonic heat-source excitation), then derives a closed-form solution to the first-order heat-transfer ODE at arbitrary timestamps. This departs from prior dynamic thermal methods in two key ways: unlike 4DGS and ThermalGS, which model temporal evolution through data-driven deformation fields or temporal embeddings without physical consistency, and unlike NTR-Gaussian, which embeds thermodynamic equations but relies on implicit neural networks and numerical integration, ETGS keeps the representation fully explicit and avoids integration entirely. The RHD dataset also carves new ground by providing millisecond-aligned, pixel-registered RGB-IR pairs specifically designed for rapid thermal transients, filling a gap left by existing thermal benchmarks that target static or slowly varying scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ETGS demonstrates that physically grounded, closed-form temporal modeling can match or exceed data-driven dynamic reconstruction in quality while retaining near-static training and rendering speed, which is practically important for real-time thermal monitoring in industrial inspection, energy auditing, and medical diagnostics. The RHD dataset (10 scenes, 2363 views, −1 °C to 101 °C, ms-level timestamps) provides a much-needed benchmark for rapid thermal dynamics that prior datasets do not cover. Key limitations remain: the first-order linear model cannot capture nonlinear effects (temperature-dependent conductivity, phase transitions, radiative coupling), inter-Gaussian conduction is only implicitly enforced through dense supervision rather than explicit coupling, and geometry is assumed static. Open directions include extending to nonlinear/higher-order thermal PDEs, moving heat sources, joint geometry-temperature evolution, and RGB-thermal cross-modal fusion.
