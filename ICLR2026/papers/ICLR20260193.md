## Problem

Existing LLM benchmarks are constrained to tasks with easily verifiable answers (math, code, short-form QA) or are limited to single professional domains (health, ML research). There is no robust, publicly available benchmark with expert-written rubrics spanning multiple professional domains that captures the kind of multi-page, report-style work real PhD and MBA professionals perform, making it impossible to systematically evaluate LLMs on open-ended, real-world professional tasks.

## Input / Output

For the LLM-Judge task, the input is a model-generated response paired with a single rubric criterion, and the output is a binary Yes/No judgment (1 token for non-reasoning models, up to 32K tokens for reasoning models). For the Report-Generator task, the input is a multi-part professional prompt (averaging ~2052 characters) plus grounding documents with publicly accessible URLs, and the output is a comprehensive multi-page report (up to 32K tokens for non-reasoning, 64K for reasoning models). Final scores are computed as importance-weighted criterion-fulfillment rates (weights 1–4 for additional/minor/major/critical criteria), and the judge's overall quality is measured by Macro-F1 minus a Bias-Index that penalizes self-enhancement toward any single model family.

## Method

ProfBench comprises 7,347 human-written response-criterion pairs across 80 tasks in four domains (Physics PhD, Chemistry PhD, Finance MBA, Consulting MBA), each annotated by domain experts spending 10–20 hours per task with multi-round reviewer approval and no LLM assistance. The key methodological contribution is a cost-efficient, low-bias LLM-Judge pipeline: GPT-OSS-120B is used as the judge with adaptive reasoning effort (high for Physics/Chemistry/Style criteria, low for others), matching the best proprietary judge (Gemini-2.5-Pro) at 78.2% overall while costing only 1.68% as much. A bias-index metric (max-minus-min per-model bias relative to human ground truth) is subtracted from Macro-F1 to penalize self-enhancement, and an integer-programming-based optimal sample allocation (solved via dynamic programming) distributes a fixed generation budget across tasks to minimize overall variance, reducing inference cost to 25% of uniform sampling without sacrificing estimation robustness.

## Results

On ProfBench, the top-performing model GPT-5 (high reasoning) achieves only 65.9% overall criteria-fulfillment score, confirming the benchmark's challenging nature (compared to GPT-5's 94.6% on AIME 25 and 87.0% on GPQA-Diamond). Among domains, Physics is hardest (49.3%), followed by Finance (63.7%), Chemistry (70.6%), and Consulting (80.0%). For LLM-Judge evaluation, Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the best overall score of 78.2% (Macro-F1 minus Bias-Index), while the selected practical judge GPT-OSS-120B matches this at 78.2% while costing only 1.68% of Gemini-2.5-Pro's cost ($0.70 vs $41.46). The full benchmark can be run for as little as $12 using the o3 model, 2–3 orders of magnitude cheaper than existing rubric-based evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to PaperBench (Starace et al., 2025), ProfBench covers 4 diverse professional domains versus PaperBench's single ML domain, uses human-written rubrics (both do), and reduces judge evaluation cost from $1,320 to $0.70 (a ~1,886× reduction). Against HealthBench (Arora et al., 2025), ProfBench is approximately equally challenging (GPT-5 scores 65.9% vs 67.2%) but covers far more diverse domains and costs $48 per round versus up to $300. Versus DeepResearch-Bench RACE (Du et al., 2025), ProfBench provides human-verified rubrics rather than synthetically generated criteria, requires genuine professional (PhD/MBA) knowledge rather than generalist internet-searchable answers, and eliminates the pervasive self-enhancement bias (DeepResearch-Bench's Gemini-2.5-Pro-generated criteria inflated that model's score to >97%). The key practical advantage is accessibility: ProfBench's GPT-OSS-120B judge achieves the same 78.2% overall score as the best proprietary judge (Gemini-2.5-Pro) at only 1.68% of the cost, making rubric-based professional evaluation feasible for the broader research community.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rubric-based LLM evaluation paradigm established by PaperBench (single-domain: ML research reproduction) and HealthBench (single-domain: patient-physician conversations) to a multi-domain professional setting spanning Physics PhD, Chemistry PhD, Finance MBA, and Consulting MBA. It departs from DeepResearch-Bench RACE by replacing synthetically generated criteria with expert-annotated rubrics, addressing the bias and reliability issues of self-generated scoring anchors. The LLM-as-a-judge formulation follows the NLI-style binary criterion-fulfillment task from prior rubric benchmarks, but introduces a novel bias-index metric to quantify and mitigate self-enhancement bias across model providers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProfBench fills a clear gap: no prior publicly available benchmark combines expert-written rubrics across multiple professional domains with fair, low-cost LLM-judge evaluation. The 2-3 order-of-magnitude cost reduction (from ~$1320 for PaperBench JudgeEval to ~$0.70) makes rubric-based evaluation accessible to the broader research community, potentially enabling RLVR training on professional-domain tasks that were previously infeasible to verify. Open questions include whether rubric-guided RL can close the 15% Finance gap between open-weight and proprietary models, whether the bias-mitigation strategies generalize beyond the three reference models used, and whether the benchmark's 80-task scale is sufficient for stable cross-model comparisons given the high per-task variance observed.
