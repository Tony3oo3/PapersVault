## Problem

Attention sinks (heads collapsing onto semantically uninformative tokens) and compression valleys (mid-layer entropy drops) have been studied in isolation with separate, incomplete explanations, and no formal causal link between them has been established. The precise mechanism driving both phenomena simultaneously in middle layers remained unknown, leaving the depth-wise organization of transformer computation unexplained.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes frozen pretrained decoder-only LLMs (Pythia 410M/6.9B, LLaMA3 8B/70B, Qwen2 7B/72B, Gemma 7B, Bloom 1.7B, GPT OSS 120B) and text prompts (truncated to 1024–4096 tokens) as input, running forward passes to extract per-layer representation matrices, attention maps, and token norms. Outputs include tight theoretical bounds on singular-value dominance and matrix entropy as functions of the norm ratio c and alignment α, layer-wise entropy/sink-rate/norm trajectories, a three-phase depth characterization, and task-dependent optimal-depth profiles from LogitLens, TunedLens, and linear-probing evaluations.

## Method

The paper proves (Theorem 1) that when a single token's squared norm M dominates the rest (ratio c = M/R ≫ 1), the largest singular value σ₁² ≥ M + αR necessarily grows, yielding tight upper bounds on matrix entropy and lower bounds on anisotropy (Corollary 2). Empirically, across six model families, the BOS token's norm spikes by 10³–10⁴× in middle layers exactly when entropy collapses and sink rates surge (Pearson r = −0.9 between ΔBOS-norm and Δentropy). Causality is confirmed by ablating the MLP's contribution to the BOS token, which eliminates both compression and sinks simultaneously. Building on this unified mechanism, the authors propose the Mix-Compress-Refine theory: early layers (0–20%) perform broad diffuse mixing, middle layers (20–85%) compress representations and halt mixing via attention sinks triggered by massive activations, and late layers (85–100%) re-equalize norms and switch to sharp positional/identity heads for selective refinement. This phase structure explains why embedding tasks peak during Phase 2 compression while generation tasks require full-depth Phase 3 refinement.

## Results

Across six model families (410M–120B parameters), the Pearson correlation between layer-wise BOS norm change and entropy change is r = −0.9 ± 0.18, while BOS norm and sink rate correlate at r = 0.58 ± 0.25, confirming synchronized emergence. When massive activations appear (BOS norm spiking by 10³–10⁴×), matrix entropy drops below 0.5 bits (to ~0.02 bits in LLaMA3 8B) and sink rates surge to 0.85–1.0 simultaneously. Targeted MLP ablation at the massive-activation layer eliminates both effects: entropy remains at ~0.5 bits, sink rate drops to 0.0, and BOS norm stays within 2× of other tokens (vs. 10²× normally). For downstream tasks, embedding/linear-probing accuracy peaks at 25–75% depth (outperforming early/late layers by 10–20% on 32 MTEB tasks), while generation perplexity declines monotonically from >10⁴ to 10–25 and multiple-choice QA accuracy remains flat until ~50% depth before rising sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against baselines; instead, it provides a unified causal mechanism that subsumes and extends four prior lines of work. Sun et al. (2024) identified massive activations and linked them to attention sinks but did not connect them to representational compression; this paper proves (Theorem 1) that massive activations mathematically necessitate a dominant singular value, closing that gap. Skean et al. (2025) observed compression valleys and hypothesized an information bottleneck but lacked causal evidence; here, targeted MLP ablations demonstrate that removing the massive activation eliminates compression, providing the missing causal link. Xiao et al. (2024) and Gu et al. (2025) characterized attention sinks empirically and traced their emergence to pretraining, respectively, but treated them as a separate phenomenon from compression; this paper shows both are two sides of the same coin, emerging at the same layer index and vanishing together under ablation. The Mix-Compress-Refine framework further reconciles apparently contradictory findings in the literature: why embedding tasks peak in compressed mid-layers (Skean et al., 2025; Razzhigaev et al., 2024) while generation benefits from full depth (Csordás et al., 2025; Lad et al., 2024), by attributing each to a distinct phase demarcated by massive-activation dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously studied-in-isolation phenomena—attention sinks (Xiao et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2025) and compression valleys (Skean et al., 2025)—under a single causal mechanism: massive activations in the residual stream (Sun et al., 2024). While Sun et al. linked massive activations to attention sinks and Skean et al. proposed an information-bottleneck explanation for compression, no prior work established a formal mathematical connection between the two. The paper extends this lineage by proving (Theorem 1) that a single high-norm token necessarily induces spectral dominance, and by proposing the Mix-Compress-Refine framework as a unified account of depth-wise computation that subsumes observations from Barbero et al. (2025a) on over-mixing prevention and Csordas et al. (2025) on depth efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical proof that massive activations mathematically necessitate compression (with tight, empirically validated bounds) provides the first causal mechanism for compression valleys, replacing prior correlation-based or information-bottleneck hypotheses. The Mix-Compress-Refine framework offers a practical lens for understanding why embedding tasks peak at intermediate layers while generation requires full depth, potentially guiding phase-aware early exiting and layer selection. Limitations include the single-massive-activation assumption (real models may have multiple), focus on decoder-only architectures with explicit BOS tokens, and model-dependent exceptions (e.g., Gemma 7B achieves compression by suppressing other norms rather than boosting BOS). Open questions include whether the three-phase structure holds for encoder-decoder or multi-modal architectures, and whether the framework extends to models without explicit BOS tokens or with ALiBi positional encodings.
