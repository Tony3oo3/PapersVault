## Problem

Conventional raster-order autoregressive image generation is inherently sequential, making it inefficient for high-resolution synthesis and preventing zero-shot generalization to tasks requiring non-causal dependencies like inpainting and outpainting. Existing alternatives—bidirectional masked models (MaskGIT), fixed-order block-wise AR (VAR, PAR), and position-token-augmented random-order models (RandAR)—each sacrifice either KV-cache efficiency, scheduling flexibility, or sequence-length economy. The paper tackles the open question of how to achieve fully random-order, parallel generation within a causal attention paradigm without inflating sequence length or memory.

## Input / Output

The model consumes discrete image tokens (16,384-vocabulary VQGAN-style tokenizer) at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution, conditioned on class labels, FLAN-T5-XL text embeddings, or ViT-adapter-processed conditional maps (Canny edges, depth). For zero-shot tasks, known image regions are pre-filled as content tokens in Pass-1 while target regions are represented by positional [MASK] queries in Pass-2, enabling inpainting, outpainting, and resolution expansion without task-specific fine-tuning. Output is a full 2D image decoded from the generated token sequence, with parallel decoding producing multiple tokens per step (32–64 total steps).

## Method

ARPG employs a two-pass decoder that decouples content representation from positional prediction. Pass-1 is a standard causal self-attention decoder that processes a randomly shuffled sequence of known content tokens to produce context-aware key-value pairs, without directly predicting any token. Pass-2 is a causal cross-attention decoder where data-independent [MASK] tokens, infused with RoPE positional encodings for target locations, serve as queries attending to the Pass-1 KV cache to predict the corresponding tokens. This design eliminates bidirectional attention, enables KV-cache reuse across parallel queries (multiple [MASK] tokens processed simultaneously in one step), and avoids the sequence-length doubling of RandAR. Training uses teacher forcing under a causal mask over the shuffled sequence, while inference generalizes from causal to block-wise attention for locally bidirectional context, improving fidelity at fewer steps. The shared global KV projection across all Pass-2 layers further reduces memory by 75% compared to per-layer projections.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256, ARPG-XXL (1.3B) achieves FID 1.83 and IS 336.1 with only 32 parallel decoding steps, while ARPG-XL (719M) reaches FID 1.93 and ARPG-L (320M) reaches FID 2.30. ARPG-XXL delivers 55.28 img/s throughput with 7.22 GB memory, representing a ~30× speedup over raster-order LlamaGen-XXL (1.58 img/s) and a ~3× speedup over the best parallel AR baselines (NAR-XXL at 15.23 img/s), while reducing peak memory by ~75% compared to VAR-d24 (22.29 GB). For text-to-image at 512×512, ARPG-XL trained on only 4M samples matches LlamaGen-XL (60M samples) on GenEval overall score (0.31 vs 0.32) at 36× higher throughput (30.11 vs 0.83 img/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARPG-XXL (FID 1.83, 32 steps) outperforms raster-order LlamaGen-XXL (FID 2.62, 576 steps) by 0.79 FID while being ~35× faster (55.28 vs 1.58 img/s) and using 72% less memory (7.22 vs 26.22 GB). Against block-wise VAR-d24 (FID 2.09, 10 steps), ARPG-XXL achieves better FID at comparable throughput (55.28 vs 49.94 img/s) but with 68% lower memory (7.22 vs 22.29 GB). Among parallel AR methods, ARPG-XXL beats RandAR-XXL (FID 2.15) by 0.32 FID at 5.3× higher throughput, and outperforms NAR-XXL (FID 2.58) and PAR-XXL (FID 2.35) by 0.75 and 0.52 FID respectively, at 3.6× and 10.6× higher throughput. The only baselines with lower FID are RAR-XL (1.50) and MAR-L (1.78), but both require 256 and 100 sequential steps respectively, making them 8× and 102× slower than ARPG-XXL. For text-to-image, ARPG-XL matches LlamaGen-XL on GenEval overall (0.31 vs 0.32) using only 7% of the training data (4M vs 60M) at 36× higher throughput, though it trails SD-v1.5 (0.43) which uses 2B samples. In controllable generation, ControlARPG (ARPG-L) achieves the best Canny FID (7.39) and Depth FID (4.06), beating ControlAR-LlamaGen-L (7.69 / 4.19) and ControlVAR-d30 (7.85 / 6.50) with fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARPG builds directly on the LlamaGen architecture and tokenizer (Sun et al., 2024a) and adopts the shared KV projection design from the decoder-decoder framework (Sun et al., 2024b), but fundamentally restructures the decoding process into a two-pass scheme that decouples content representation (causal self-attention) from position-guided prediction (causal cross-attention with [MASK] queries). This departs from raster-order AR (VQGAN, LlamaGen) by enabling random-order generation, from masked models (MaskGIT, MAR) by retaining causal attention and KV caching, from block-wise AR (VAR, PAR, NAR) by removing fixed orderings, and from RandAR by avoiding the coupling of position and content tokens that doubles sequence length. The key conceptual contribution is treating positional guidance as a query in a cross-attention mechanism rather than as an in-sequence token, which the authors argue resolves the training inefficiency and attention redundancy inherent in masked modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30× speedup over raster-order AR and 3× over recent parallel AR methods, combined with a 75% memory reduction and competitive FID (1.83 at 32 steps), makes high-quality autoregressive image generation substantially more practical for deployment. The zero-shot generalization to inpainting, outpainting, and resolution expansion without task-specific fine-tuning is a notable advantage over fixed-order methods. Open questions include whether the approach scales effectively to much larger models and higher resolutions, how it performs on large-scale text-to-image (the current T2I model uses only 4M samples), and whether the two-pass architecture can be further streamlined or combined with diffusion-based refinement to close remaining quality gaps with top diffusion models.
