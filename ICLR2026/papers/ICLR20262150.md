## Problem

Humans acquire rich semantic object representations from egocentric visual streams with minimal supervision, yet current self-supervised learning models trained on human-like visual experience underperform at capturing human semantic object similarity. Two biologically grounded aspects of visual learning—preferential high-resolution processing of the central visual field and the principle that close-in-time inputs receive similar representations—have been largely neglected in prior egocentric SSL work, leaving it unclear how their interplay shapes the semantic structure of learned representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 540×540-pixel egocentric video frames from Ego4D sampled at ~5 fps, along with gaze coordinates (ground-truth from eye-tracker for 45 hours, or predicted by the GLC model for the remaining 3,600+ hours). Frames are cropped to N×N squares (N ∈ {112, 224, 336, 540}) centered on the gaze location, yielding 64.4 million training images organized into 5-second clips of 25 frames. The model outputs 256-dimensional visual embeddings that are evaluated via frozen linear probing on object/scene recognition benchmarks and via CKA alignment against GloVe co-occurrence embeddings derived from COCO, ADE20K, and Visual Genome.

## Method

The core approach adapts MoCoV3 (with ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16 backbones) by replacing standard spatial/color augmentations with a temporal slowness objective: for each query frame x_t, a positive key frame x_t' is sampled from the same video within a temporal window ΔT (3 s for ResNet, 1 s for ViT), and the InfoNCE loss with cosine similarity pulls their embeddings together while pushing apart all other batch keys. To simulate central vision, each frame is cropped to a gaze-centered square, and the gaze trajectory is constrained so that positive pairs fall within the same fixation (identified via a velocity threshold), effectively suppressing the learning signal during saccades. The momentum encoder is updated via exponential moving average, and training runs for a single epoch over the full Ego4D dataset. The key finding is that the combination of gaze-centered cropping (which biases representations toward foreground objects over background) and temporal slowness over human eye-movement patterns (which distills contextual and viewpoint-invariant semantics) jointly produces representations that better encode basic category, fine-grained category, instance identity, and co-occurrence context than full-frame training without a slowness objective.

## Results

Bio-inspired learning (gaze-centered crops + temporal slowness) outperforms the Frames Learning baseline on all object-centric tasks: fine-grained recognition improves from 33.84% to 38.42% (ResNet-50) and 50.56% to 51.58% (ViT-B/16), while instance recognition jumps from 59.03% to 67.00% (ResNet-50) and 65.40% to 68.58% (ViT-B/16). Category recognition shows modest gains (45.65%→46.94% ResNet, 47.68%→48.17% ViT). Scene recognition (Places365) is the one area where the baseline wins, dropping from 44.49% to 39.84% for ViT-B/16, indicating a trade-off between object and scene encoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is 'Frames Learning' from Orhan & Lake (2024), which trains on the full egocentric field of view without a temporal slowness objective. Bio-inspired learning wins on all three object-centric semantic groups: fine-grained recognition (+4.58 pts ResNet, +1.02 pts ViT), instance recognition (+7.97 pts ResNet, +3.18 pts ViT), and category recognition (+1.29 pts ResNet, +0.49 pts ViT). The largest single-dataset gain is COIL100 instance recognition, where ResNet-50 improves from 64.53% to 80.12% (+15.59 pts). The one consistent loss is scene recognition on Places365, where the full-frame baseline retains an advantage (44.49% vs 39.84% for ViT), suggesting that restricting to central vision sacrifices background/scene information. Additional ablations (Table 2) show that gaze-based cropping outperforms both center-crop and saliency-based cropping baselines, with the gaze advantage being largest for instance recognition (+4.17 pts over center-crop for ResNet-50).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoCo-v3 contrastive SSL framework (Chen et al., 2021) by incorporating a temporal slowness objective following Aubret et al. (2022a), and applies it to large-scale egocentric video (Ego4D) with gaze-based central vision cropping. Prior egocentric SSL work (Orhan & Lake, 2024) trains on full frames without temporal structure, while prior time-based SSL studies (Aubret et al., 2022a; Schneider et al., 2021) rely on synthetic or curated sequences rather than in-the-wild human video. The paper's key departure is the joint use of biologically motivated constraints—central vision cropping, temporal slowness, and fixation/saccade segmentation—at the scale of 5 months of egocentric experience, a combination not previously explored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides mechanistic evidence that three biological principles (central vision emphasis, temporal slowness, and fixation-based learning) each contribute distinct facets to semantic object representation, offering a more grounded account of how humans might build object semantics from natural experience. The trade-off between improved object recognition and degraded scene recognition highlights a fundamental tension in foveated learning that is relevant to embodied AI design. Key limitations include reliance on a gaze prediction model (sidestepping the learning of eye movement control), the use of hard cropping rather than gradual foveation, and the gap between adult and infant visual experience. Open questions include whether these findings generalize to developmental stages and whether more realistic retinal processing would further improve or alter the results.
