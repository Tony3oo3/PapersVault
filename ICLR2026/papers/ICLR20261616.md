## Problem

Physical simulation requires spatially-varying mechanical properties (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, density) throughout the volume of every object, yet 3D capture methods and repositories rarely provide such annotations, forcing artists to hand-craft or guess material presets. Prior learning-based approaches either require per-object optimization, produce simulator-specific parameters that don't transfer across frameworks, only predict surface-level properties, or rely on slow run-time aggregation of vision-language model outputs. VoMP addresses this as the first fully feed-forward method that predicts physically valid, simulation-ready volumetric mechanical properties across arbitrary 3D representations in seconds.

## Input / Output

The method accepts any 3D representation that can be voxelized and rendered from turnaround views (meshes, 3D Gaussian Splats, NeRFs, SDFs), with no per-object optimization. Internally, the geometry is voxelized onto a fixed grid (up to 32,768 active voxels per object) and multi-view DINOv2 image features are projected onto each voxel center and averaged. The output is a per-voxel triplet of (E, ν, ρ) in real-world SI units throughout the object's interior volume, which can be transferred back to the original representation (splat means, FEM tets, quadrature points) via nearest-neighbor interpolation and is directly compatible with any accurate simulator.

## Method

VoMP decouples material validity from spatial assignment via two components. First, MatVAE is a VAE trained on 100K real-world material triplets from databases, mapping (E, ν, ρ) to a 2D latent space using a Normalizing Flow posterior, decomposed KL-divergence with a total-correlation penalty, and per-dimension capacity constraints to prevent collapse onto a single property. Second, a Geometry Transformer (initialized from TRELLIS weights, using 3D shifted-window attention) takes the aggregated multi-view DINOv2 voxel features and predicts per-voxel latents in MatVAE's space, which are decoded by the frozen MatVAE decoder into physically valid (E, ν, ρ) values. Training data is generated by an annotation pipeline that feeds part-segmented 3D assets, PBR texture renderings, material names, and nearest real-world material ranges from the MTD dataset into a VLM (Qwen 2.5 VL-72B), producing 37M annotated voxels across 1,624 objects. The key design insight is that the MatVAE latent space guarantees all decoded outputs fall within the range of real materials, even under interpolation, while the Geometry Transformer learns only the spatial assignment problem.

## Results

On the new GVM benchmark (166 objects, 4.9M voxel annotations), VoMP achieves ALDE of 0.38 and ALRE of 0.041 for Young's modulus, ADE of 0.024 and ARE of 0.082 for Poisson's ratio, and ADE of 142.7 kg/m³ and ARE of 0.092 for density — dramatically outperforming all baselines. On the ABO-500 mass estimation benchmark, VoMP achieves ALDE 0.631, ADE 8.433, and ARE 0.887, matching or exceeding prior methods. Inference takes 3.59 s end-to-end (vs. 51–1455 s for baselines), a 14×–405× speedup, with the Geometry Transformer itself running in under 1 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoMP outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against NeRF2Physics (Zhai et al., 2024) and PUGS (Shuai et al., 2025), which only predict E and ρ (no Poisson's ratio), VoMP reduces ALDE for E from 2.80/3.39 to 0.38 and ARE for ρ from 1.04/3.24 to 0.09, while also providing accurate ν estimates. Against Phys4DGen* (Lin et al., 2025a), the only baseline predicting all three properties, VoMP cuts ALDE for E from 4.90 to 0.38 (13×) and ARE for ρ from 1.44 to 0.09 (16×). Against the concurrent feed-forward method Pixie (Le et al., 2025), VoMP is 56× faster (3.59 s vs. 201.63 s) and produces far more physically valid materials: mean relative error to the nearest real-world material range is 0.29% for log(E) and 11.75% for ρ, versus 11.90% and 46.58% for Pixie. On the ABO-500 mass estimation benchmark, VoMP achieves the best ALDE (0.631) and ADE (8.433), while PUGS has a slightly lower ARE (0.767 vs. 0.887), indicating VoMP's volumetric density is marginally less accurate in aggregate but more spatially consistent.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoMP builds on the TRELLIS 3D transformer backbone (initialized with its pretrained weights) and the VAE framework, but departs sharply from prior material-prediction methods in three key ways. Where NeRF2Physics and PUGS require per-object optimization of language-embedded feature fields, and Phys4DGen aggregates VLM outputs at inference time, VoMP is a fully feed-forward model that uses a VLM (Qwen 2.5 VL) only during a one-time training-data annotation pipeline. It also introduces a novel MatVAE latent space over real-world (E, ν, ρ) triplets—something no prior work attempts—ensuring that decoded outputs are physically valid even under interpolation, in contrast to simulator-specific parameters produced by methods like PhysSplat or DreamPhysics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VoMP lowers the barrier to realistic physics simulation by providing simulation-ready, physically valid volumetric material fields in ~3.6 seconds across arbitrary 3D representations, a 5–100× speedup over optimization-based baselines. The released GVM benchmark (4.9M voxel annotations across 166 objects) and MTD dataset (100K real material triplets) address a critical data scarcity that has hampered this area. Key limitations include fixed-grid voxelization capping resolution (oversmoothing heterogeneous regions), an isotropic-material assumption that fails for anisotropic materials like wood, and the inability to predict additional properties (yield strength, thermal expansion). Open questions include whether the feed-forward paradigm can be extended to anisotropic or viscoelastic materials, and whether the MatVAE latent space can be adapted to simulator-specific parameterizations for faster solvers.
