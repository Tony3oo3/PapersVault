## Problem

Most heterogeneous graph research assumes a fixed, manually specified schema, yet multiple valid schemas can be derived from the same data and the choice substantially influences downstream performance. The design space of HIN schemas remains open-ended and largely heuristic, with no principled framework for making schema decisions explicit or optimizing them for a given task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265033/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a heterogeneous information network with typed nodes, typed edges, and arbitrary node attributes (categorical, numerical, or pretrained embeddings) and first converts every attribute into a star-shaped subgraph of attribute-induced nodes connected by weighted edges, producing the atomic HIN. It then outputs a refined schema as a pair of binary vectors (node-type selectors β and edge-type selectors α) that determine which node types receive unique identity embeddings and which relations participate in message passing, alongside standard node classification labels or link prediction scores from the downstream HGNN.

## Method

The core mechanism is a two-stage pipeline: (1) attribute atomization, which uniformly converts all feature matrices into explicit edges to newly induced attribute nodes, yielding a canonical atomic HIN with maximal expressiveness; and (2) task-specific schema refinement, a binary selection over node types and edge types searched via a genetic algorithm (1024 candidates) that jointly optimizes schema parameters with model depth L. A key technical ingredient is pre-propagation feature initialization: before refinement, each selected node type propagates its identity once along shortest paths to all other node types, guaranteeing that node-type selection is independent of edge-type selection (Lemma 4.1) while preserving the full expressive power of the SHGC filter (Lemma 4.2). The downstream model is sRGCN, a simplified RGCN where relation-specific weight matrices collapse to scalars (W_r^(ℓ) = θ_r^(ℓ) I), which is naturally aligned with atomic HINs where all inputs reduce to identity embeddings and heavy feature transformations become redundant.

## Results

On node classification, sRGCN on refined atomic HINs achieves Macro-F1 of 68.97 (IMDb), 55.40 (Freebase), 95.55 (DBLP), 94.36 (ACM), and 57.35 (OGBN-MAG validation accuracy), outperforming the strongest baselines by up to 3.2% Macro-F1 (Freebase) and 1.2% accuracy (OGBN-MAG). On link prediction, sRGCNAtomic achieves ROC-AUC of 97.85 (Amazon), 77.10 (LastFM), and 90.11 (PubMed), improving over the best baselines by up to 6.8% ROC-AUC (LastFM) and 3.0% (PubMed). The paper reports an average 4.9% ROC-AUC gain for link prediction and up to 6.2% Macro-F1 gain for node classification over recent advanced HGNNs. Notably, several advanced HGNNs (GTN, SimpleHGN, HINormer, REGCN, SlotGAT) run out of memory on OGBN-MAG, while sRGCNAtomic completes training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

sRGCNAtomic consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across the 8 datasets. On node classification, it beats the strongest baseline PSHGCN on IMDb (+1.9% Macro-F1), DBLP (+0.3%), and OGBN-MAG (+1.2% Acc), and beats HINormer on Freebase (+3.2% Macro-F1); on ACM it essentially ties PSHGCN (94.36 vs 94.35). On link prediction, the gains are larger: sRGCNAtomic surpasses SlotGAT (the best link-prediction baseline) by +2.7% ROC-AUC on Amazon, +6.8% on LastFM, and +3.0% on PubMed over PSHGCN. Five advanced HGNNs (GTN, SimpleHGN, HINormer, REGCN, SlotGAT) fail to run on OGBN-MAG due to out-of-memory, while sRGCNAtomic completes training thanks to its minimal parameterization (relation-specific scalars instead of full matrices). The paper further shows (Table 3) that schemas refined with sRGCN transfer effectively to SimpleHGN and PSHGCN, with subsequent re-optimization yielding only modest additional gains, confirming that the schema design—not the model architecture—drives most of the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the spectral heterogeneous graph convolution (SHGC) framework of Butler et al. (2023) as a unifying theoretical lens, showing that widely used HGNNs (RGCN, GTN, SimpleHGN, SeHGNN, PSHGCN) are all instances or first-order approximations of SHGC with different parameter-sharing schemes. It generalizes the long-standing but ad-hoc practice of converting categorical/binary attributes into entities (seen in HGB, RelBench, and OGB) into a systematic principle of entity-attribute duality, producing the atomic HIN as a canonical, maximally expressive representation. The key departure from prior work is elevating schema design from an implicit preprocessing choice to an explicit, optimizable structural learning problem, using genetic-algorithm-based search over node-type and edge-type selections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a simplified RGCN on a refined atomic schema outperforms advanced HGNNs on their original benchmark schemas, this paper reframes schema design as a first-class modeling dimension comparable to architecture choice, with direct implications for how HIN benchmarks are constructed and evaluated. The released atomic HINs, searched schemas, and refinement framework enable principled benchmarking and open avenues for schema-aware learning and automated structure discovery. Limitations include the reliance on heuristic GA search (rather than differentiable or end-to-end schema optimization), the assumption of undirected graphs, and the fact that pre-propagation, while theoretically neutral, adds implementation complexity. A key open question is whether the schema-refinement paradigm extends to dynamic or streaming HINs where the schema itself evolves over time.
