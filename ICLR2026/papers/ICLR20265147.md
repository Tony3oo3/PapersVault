## Problem

Standard scaled dot-product attention in Transformers suffers from training instabilities caused by arbitrarily growing query and key norms, leading to attention logit explosion and entropy collapse. The paper shows this can occur even in small models when spurious correlations in the data cause certain tokens to 'steal attention' globally. Existing stabilizing solutions like QKNorm normalize both queries and keys, which limits expressivity by forcing all tokens to share the same attention sharpness.

## Input / Output

QUEST attention is a drop-in replacement that consumes the same query, key, and value tensors (Q, K, V ∈ R^{N×D_H}) as standard attention and produces the same output format, requiring no architectural changes. The only modification is an l2-normalization of the key vectors before the dot product, and the constant scaling factor C=1/√D_H is removed entirely. The paper validates this across diverse input formats: 224×224 RGB images (ImageNet, ADE20K), text sequences (WikiText-103), multivariate time series (UEA archive), graph structures (GNN benchmarks), and 3D point clouds (nuScenes).

## Method

QUEST (QUEry-modulated Spherical aTtention) projects all key vectors onto a unit hypersphere via l2-normalization while leaving query vectors unnormalized, computing attention as A = softmax(Q K̄^T) with no additional scaling. This design separates two roles: the cosine similarity between query and key directions determines the rank order of attention (which tokens to attend to), while the query norm independently controls the sharpness of each token's softmax distribution. By constraining keys to the sphere, no individual token can 'steal attention' globally through a large key norm, breaking the cross-play between query and key gradient updates that drives norm growth and logit explosion. The paper provides a theoretical analysis showing that normalizing at least one of queries or keys mitigates the feedback loop, and demonstrates that QUEST (normalizing keys only) outperforms QNorm (normalizing queries only) because it preserves the useful norm information in keys for distinguishing relevant tokens. QUEST can be orthogonally combined with other attention variants such as Elliptical attention to further improve adversarial robustness.

## Results

On ImageNet classification, QUEST attention consistently outperforms standard attention across all model sizes: +0.8% Top-1 on ViT-S/16 (80.2% vs 79.6%), +2.4% on ViT-L/16 (74.9% vs 72.5% with QKNorm-DS baseline), and +0.5% on ViT-B/16 with DeiT-3 (83.2% vs 82.7%). QUEST also shows stronger robustness gains, e.g. +1.6% Top-1 under FGSM and +3.5% under SPSA on ViT-Tiny, and lower corruption MCE (43.2 vs 44.8 on ViT-S/16). Across other domains, QUEST achieves 73.53% average accuracy on UEA time series (vs 72.90%), 80.83% mIoU on nuScenes point cloud segmentation (vs 80.40%), and marginally lower perplexity on WikiText-103 (26.98 vs 27.44 for Transformer-Medium).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QUEST outperforms standard attention (Vaswani et al., 2017) by 0.2–2.4% Top-1 across all ViT sizes, with the largest gap on ViT-L/16 under DeiT training (74.9% vs 72.5% QKNorm-DS baseline, since standard attention crashes). Against QKNorm-HS (Liu et al., 2022), QUEST gains +0.9% Top-1 on ViT-Tiny (73.4% vs 72.5%) and substantially more on robustness metrics (IN-A Top-1: 8.5% vs 7.9%). Against QKNorm-DS (Dehghani et al., 2023), QUEST improves Top-1 by 0.7% (ViT-B) to 2.4% (ViT-L) and reduces corruption MCE by 1.5–4.1 points. Compared to Elliptical attention (Nielsen et al., 2024), QUEST achieves higher clean accuracy (73.33% vs 71.53%) while also being more robust under FGSM (56.90% vs 55.96%) and SPSA (50.70% vs 57.96% — here Elliptical wins on SPSA), and the combined Elliptical-QUEST variant further improves SPSA robustness to 59.15% Top-1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the scaled dot-product attention of Vaswani et al. (2017) and departs from the QKNorm family (Dehghani et al. 2023; Liu et al. 2022), which l2-normalizes both queries and keys to stabilize large ViT training. The key conceptual departure is that normalizing only the keys (while leaving queries unnormalized) breaks the cross-play between query and key norm growth identified in the gradient analysis, while preserving per-token control over attention sharpness. The formulation is also shown to be orthogonal to Elliptical attention (Nielsen et al. 2024), which replaces cosine similarity with a Mahalanobis metric, and the two can be combined as 'Elliptical-QUEST.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QUEST provides a single-line, parameter-free modification (l2-normalize keys, drop the 1/√D scaling) that simultaneously improves training stability, clean accuracy, and robustness to corruptions and adversarial attacks across vision, language, time-series, graph, and point-cloud domains. The finding that QKNorm actually degrades performance on smaller models (ViT-Tiny: 71.6% vs 72.6% for standard) challenges the implicit assumption that more aggressive normalization is always beneficial, and the toy example demonstrates that spurious-correlation lock-in is not solely a large-scale phenomenon. Open questions include extending QUEST to linear-attention variants, exploring Riemannian optimization on the hyperspherical key space, and deeper evaluation in non-vision SOTA architectures.
