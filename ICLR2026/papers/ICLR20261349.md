## Problem

Fine-tuning vision-language models like CLIP inevitably causes catastrophic forgetting of pretrained knowledge, and existing PEFT and robust fine-tuning methods only mitigate rather than eliminate this loss. The paper identifies a gap: no prior approach recovers forgotten knowledge *after* adaptation is already complete, treating post-hoc knowledge restoration as an unexplored direction distinct from prevention during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261349/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MERGETUNE takes two existing checkpoints as input: a zero-shot CLIP model (ŵ₁) and an already fine-tuned downstream model (ŵ₂, e.g., CoOp prompts, MMA adapters, or a linear head), and produces a single continued model w that integrates both. At inference time, the continued model accepts a standard RGB image and outputs a class label, requiring no architectural changes or additional parameters compared to the original fine-tuned model. The method is applied post hoc under the same training configuration as the base method, so no re-architecting or re-initialisation of the downstream pipeline is needed.

## Method

MERGETUNE formulates continued fine-tuning as searching for a model w that is linearly mode-connected to both the zero-shot and fine-tuned endpoints, i.e., the loss along the interpolation paths ŵ₁→w and ŵ₂→w remains low for all α∈[0,1]. The vanilla LMC objective requires replaying the pretraining data to compute the Task-1 interpolation loss, which is infeasible for CLIP's web-scale corpus; the paper replaces this with a second-order Taylor surrogate that, under the assumptions of near-zero gradient and isotropic Hessian at ŵ₁, reduces to a simple L2 regulariser λ‖w−ŵ₁‖². The final replay-free training loss combines the downstream task loss L₂(w), the surrogate proximity term, and a Task-2 LMC regulariser β·E_α[L₂(ŵ₂+α(w−ŵ₂))] approximated by evaluating a small set of evenly spaced α values. The continued model is initialised as a blend w=(1−τ)ŵ₁+τŵ₂ and trained with the same optimiser and schedule as the base method, making the approach model-agnostic and applicable to prompt-based, adapter-based, and full fine-tuning regimes alike.

## Results

On base-to-novel generalisation across 11 datasets, CoOp+MERGETUNE improves the harmonic mean (HM) by +5.58% (71.66→77.24) without adding parameters, while KgCoOp+MERGETUNE gains +0.97% and MMA+MERGETUNE gains +0.57%. On cross-dataset generalisation (trained on ImageNet, evaluated on 10 targets), CoOp+MERGETUNE improves Avg-C by +1.92 points (63.88→65.80). In the many-shot robust fine-tuning setting, E2E-FT+MERGETUNE achieves 62.29% Avg-D OOD accuracy (+8.59 over vanilla E2E-FT), and when combined with weight-space ensembling reaches 62.90%, surpassing VRF (61.72) and all training-free merging baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERGETUNE consistently outperforms all training-free merging baselines: TIES degrades HM by 4.21–10.46% and DARE by 1.07–8.06% across CoOp, KgCoOp, MMA, and PromptKD, whereas MERGETUNE yields positive gains of +0.36% to +5.58%. Against the zero-shot CLIP baseline, MERGETUNE-augmented methods surpass CLIP's 71.70% HM on all four base methods (e.g., CoOp+MERGETUNE at 77.24%), confirming recovery of forgotten pretrained knowledge. In the robust fine-tuning (many-shot) setting, MERGETUNE outperforms VRF (Zhu et al., 2024) by +0.79% Avg-D on Linear Probing (59.66 vs. 58.87) and +0.57% on E2E-FT (62.29 vs. 61.72), while using a single model at inference (no per-sample distance computation). Weight-space ensembling with the MERGETUNE checkpoint further boosts E2E-FT to 62.90% Avg-D, exceeding VRF+Weight ens. (61.72) and setting a new state-of-the-art. The improvement magnitude inversely correlates with baseline quality: CoOp (most forgetting, +5.58%) benefits far more than PromptKD (least forgetting, +0.36%), validating the LMC-based knowledge recovery mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the linear mode connectivity (LMC) framework from Garipov et al. (2018) and its multi-task extension by Mirzadeh et al. (2021), repurposing LMC not as a diagnostic tool but as an explicit training objective for a new 'continued fine-tuning' paradigm. It departs from prior PEFT methods (CoOp, KgCoOp, MMA, PromptKD) that attempt to mitigate forgetting *during* adaptation, and from training-free model merging (TIES, DARE) that directly interpolate weights, by instead searching for a new point in weight space through gradient-based optimization guided by low-loss connectivity to both the zero-shot and fine-tuned endpoints. The second-order Taylor surrogate for the pretraining loss term is a practical departure from the vanilla LMC formulation, which would require replaying the infeasible web-scale CLIP pretraining corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MERGETUNE offers a model-agnostic, post-hoc enhancement that can be applied to any existing fine-tuned VLM without architectural changes, making it broadly applicable across the PEFT and robust fine-tuning literature. The consistent failure of training-free merging (TIES, DARE) across all baselines and evaluation settings, contrasted with the reliable gains from LMC-guided continued fine-tuning, provides a clear practical guideline: when zero-shot and fine-tuned models are far apart in weight space, learning-based merging is necessary. Open questions include whether the second-order surrogate remains accurate for larger weight-space gaps (e.g., full end-to-end fine-tuning vs. prompt tuning), whether the approach extends to generative VLMs or multi-modal tasks beyond classification, and whether the additional training cost (roughly 3× the base method at N_α=5) is acceptable in compute-constrained settings.
