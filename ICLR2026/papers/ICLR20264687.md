## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for mathematical reasoning is constrained by training data that is either insufficiently difficult, contaminated with standard evaluation benchmarks, or lacks verifiable answers suitable for rule-based reward functions. Existing open datasets also predominantly recombine well-formatted problems from common sources like AIME, leading to significant overlap and limited novelty. DeepMath-103K addresses all four gaps simultaneously at scale by sourcing from less-structured mathematical forums and applying a rigorous multi-stage curation pipeline.

## Input / Output

Each dataset sample is a structured record containing a mathematical problem statement, a single verifiable final answer (numerical or symbolic, validated for rule-based extraction), a numerical difficulty score (levels 3–9), a hierarchical topic label, and three distinct reasoning solutions generated by DeepSeek-R1. The verifiable answer is the critical output for RLVR: a rule-based verifier extracts the final answer from a model's generated solution and assigns a binary reward (+1 correct, −1 incorrect). The three R1 solutions additionally support supervised fine-tuning and answer-consistency verification during curation.

## Method

The dataset is constructed through a four-stage pipeline starting from 2,869K raw questions sourced primarily from Math StackExchange discussions (via MMIQC and WebInstructSub) plus NuminaMath-CoT for topical diversity. Stage 2 performs semantic decontamination against 14 benchmarks using embedding similarity search (paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2, top-5 retrieval) followed by LLM-Judge (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) paraphrase detection, removing problems that are duplicates or near-paraphrases of benchmark items. Stage 3 assigns difficulty via six averaged GPT-4o queries following OmniMATH annotation guidelines, retaining only level ≥5 problems. Stage 4 enforces answer verifiability by generating three DeepSeek-R1 solutions per problem and requiring all extracted final answers (plus the original source answer) to be identical. The resulting 95K problems are augmented with 8K from SimpleRL to form the final 103K dataset. Models are trained using GRPO with a rule-based binary reward for zero-RL from base models, or RLVR on instruction-tuned models, with the dataset's difficulty distribution (heavily skewed toward levels 5–9) and decontamination ensuring both challenge and evaluation integrity.

## Results

Under zero RL training from Qwen-2.5-Math-7B, DeepMath-Zero-Math-7B achieves pass@1 of 34.2 on AIME24 (+23.0 over base) and 23.5 on AIME25 (+19.1 over base), surpassing all baselines including Oat-Zero-7B (32.7/11.7) and Eurus-2-7B-PRIME (19.0/12.7). Under RL from instruction-tuned models, DeepMath-Omn-1.5B reaches 64.0 on AIME24 and 57.3 on AIME25, exceeding o1-mini (63.6 on AIME24) and low-effort o3-mini (60.0 on AIME24). The strongest zero-RL model, DeepMath-Zero-Qwen3-8B, attains 93.2 on MATH500, 86.2 on AMC23, and 44.6 on AIME24. On generalization benchmarks, DeepMath-Zero-7B achieves 41.7 on GPQA-Diamond and 72.7 on MMLU-STEM, both best among zero-RL baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the zero-RL setting, DeepMath-Zero-Math-7B outperforms Oat-Zero-7B by +1.5 on AIME24 (34.2 vs 32.7) and +11.8 on AIME25 (23.5 vs 11.7), and beats Eurus-2-7B-PRIME by +15.2 on AIME24 (34.2 vs 19.0). DeepMath-Zero-7B surpasses Open-Reasoner-Zero-7B on MATH500 (85.5 vs 81.8), AIME24 (20.4 vs 15.6), and AIME25 (17.5 vs 14.4). In the RL-from-instruct setting, DeepMath-Omn-1.5B improves over its base OpenMath-Nemotron-1.5B on every benchmark, most notably AIME24 (64.0 vs 61.3) and AIME25 (57.3 vs 50.6), while DeepMath-1.5B edges out DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview on AMC23 (82.3 vs 80.3) and OlympiadBench (61.8 vs 60.9) but trails on AIME24 (37.3 vs 42.3). On generalization, DeepMath-Zero-7B leads all zero-RL baselines on GPQA-Diamond (41.7 vs 38.1 for Open-Reasoner-Zero-7B) and MMLU-STEM (72.7 vs 61.1), confirming that math-focused RLVR training transfers to broader STEM reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR dataset construction line initiated by Open-R1, ORZ-129K, DAPO-17K, and DSR-Preview, but departs from their reliance on recombining well-formatted sources like AIME and GSM8K. It adopts the GRPO training algorithm from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) with DAPO fixes, the difficulty assessment and topic taxonomy from OmniMath (Gao et al., 2024), and the semantic decontamination procedure from OpenMathInstruct-2 (Toshiwal et al., 2024a). The key conceptual departure is sourcing from informal Math StackExchange discussions rather than structured competition problems, transforming unstructured discourse into verifiable QA pairs through a multi-stage curation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that raw training data sources contain up to 90% contamination with AIME24/AMC23 test problems is a significant finding that undermines the reliability of prior RLVR evaluations and motivates the rigorous decontamination protocol adopted here. The demonstrated generalization from math-only RLVR training to GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-STEM, and BBH suggests that data diversity (sourcing from forums rather than homogeneous competition sets) may be a key driver of transferable reasoning, an open question worth further investigation. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4o for difficulty scoring and DeepSeek-R1 for solution generation, both of which introduce potential biases; the long-term sustainability of forum-based sourcing as these communities evolve also remains an open practical question.
