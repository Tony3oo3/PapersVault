## Problem

Existing distributionally robust Markov game (DRMG) algorithms require either a generative model (simulator) or a large pre-collected offline dataset, assumptions that are untenable in high-stakes domains like autonomous driving or healthcare where neither is available. This paper pioneers the study of online learning in DRMGs, where agents must learn robust equilibrium policies purely from sequential interaction with an unknown environment, and provides the first provable sample-efficiency guarantees for this setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates on finite-horizon, tabular DRMGs with discrete state and joint action spaces. At each episode it receives an initial state and then consumes sequential (state, joint action, next state) tuples from the nominal environment, using them to maintain empirical transition kernel estimates for every (h, s, a, s') tuple. The output is a sequence of per-step equilibrium policies (Nash, CCE, or CE) computed from optimistic robust Q-matrices, along with upper and lower robust value/Q-function estimates that form high-probability confidence intervals around the true robust values.

## Method

f-MORNAVI is a three-stage model-based meta-algorithm that synergizes pessimism (for robustness) with optimism (for exploration). Stage 1 estimates the nominal transition kernel from all historical interaction data. Stage 2 performs optimistic robust planning: it computes upper and lower robust Q-estimates by applying the support function σ_P[V] = inf_{P∈P} E_P[V] (via the f-divergence dual representation) to the empirical uncertainty set, then adds a data-driven bonus β tailored to the geometry of the chosen divergence (TV or KL) to ensure the estimates form valid confidence intervals. The EQUILIBRIUM subroutine then computes a Nash/CCE/CE from the optimistic Q-matrices. Stage 3 executes the resulting policy and appends new transitions to the dataset. The key theoretical contribution is proving that this dual pessimism-optimism structure yields near-optimal regret bounds of Õ(√(min{ρ⁻¹,H}·H²·S·K·∏A_i)) for TV and Õ(√(H⁴·exp(2H²)·K·S·∏A_i·(ρ²P_min*)⁻¹)) for KL, matching or improving upon generative-model and offline baselines in all parameters except the joint action product.

## Results

The paper establishes the first provable regret bounds for online learning in general-sum DRMGs. For TV-divergence uncertainty (under a failure-state assumption), f-MORNAVI achieves regret Õ(√(min{ρ_min⁻¹, H} · H²SK(∏ᵢAᵢ))), and for KL-divergence uncertainty, Õ(√(H⁴ exp(2H²)KS(∏ᵢAᵢ)(ρ_min²P_min*)⁻¹)). The corresponding sample complexity to find an ε-approximate equilibrium is Õ(ε⁻² min{ρ_min⁻¹, H} H³S(∏ᵢAᵢ)) for TV and Õ(ε⁻² H⁵ exp(2H²)S(∏ᵢAᵢ)(ρ_min²P_min*)⁻¹) for KL. The paper also proves matching lower bounds: Ω(ρK·min{H, ∏ᵢAᵢ}) regret with support shift, and Ω(√(K∏ᵢAᵢ)) without, establishing the inherent hardness of the online setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the generative-model baselines (Shi et al. 2024, Jiao & Li 2024, Shi et al. 2025), f-MORNAVI's online TV result matches the best generative TV bound (Shi et al. 2024) exactly in all parameters, despite operating in the strictly harder online setting without a simulator. The KL online result is comparable to the offline KL bound of Blanchet et al. 2023, differing only in the exp(2H²) vs. exp(H) factor (a standard artifact of KL duality) and the absence of a coverage coefficient. Against the only prior online method (Ma et al. 2023), f-MORNAVI removes the need for an impractical oracle that provides ε-accurate worst-case performance estimates without exploration, and does not require the restrictive assumption ρ ≤ P_min*/H. In the empirical experiments (2-agent, 2-step DRMGs under both KL and TV uncertainty), f-MORNAVI converges to the robust equilibrium while the non-robust Nash value iteration baseline (Liu et al. 2021) degrades significantly as the uncertainty radius ρ increases, confirming the robustness advantage. The paper argues that the ∏ᵢAᵢ dependence (curse of multi-agency) present in its bounds is likely unavoidable in general online DRMGs, as agents must estimate the full transition kernel to solve the robust optimization, unlike in non-robust settings where direct performance estimation suffices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distributionally robust Markov game (DRMG) framework from the generative-model and offline settings (Shi et al. 2024/2025; Jiao & Li 2024; Blanchet et al. 2023; Li et al. 2025) to the online, interactive setting, which had no prior provable guarantees. Methodologically, it combines the f-divergence dual representation of the robust Bellman operator (Yang et al. 2022) and the robust Bellman recursion (Blanchet et al. 2023) with UCB-style optimistic exploration from online RL, and uses the Nash Value Iteration equilibrium subroutine (Liu et al. 2021) for policy computation. The only prior online robust MARL work (Ma et al. 2023) relies on an oracle that bypasses the core estimation challenge, making MORNAVI the first algorithm to handle the full online estimation problem end-to-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that online DRMG learning is fundamentally harder than its generative or offline counterparts: a support-shift phenomenon yields linear regret, and even without support shift, a curse of multi-agency (Ω(√(K∏A_i))) is unavoidable because robustness requires estimating the full transition kernel to identify worst-case perturbations. This hardness result reframes the open question of whether the joint-action-space dependence can be eliminated in general DRMGs. Practical impact is currently limited by small-scale experiments (2-agent, 2-step games) and the exp(2H²) factor in the KL bound, but the framework provides a template for robust MARL in domains where simulators and offline data are unavailable (autonomous systems, healthcare).
