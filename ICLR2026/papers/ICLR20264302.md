## Problem

Unsupervised cross-modal hashing methods rely on flat, sparse pairwise image-text connections as supervision, which fails to capture the multi-level hierarchical semantic structure (local communities of varying granularity) naturally present in visual and textual data. Additionally, directly aligning heterogeneous modalities in a single optimization step poses high learning difficulty due to structural and semantic gaps between images and text, leading to suboptimal hash codes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264302/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted visual feature vectors (from standard pre-trained visual encoders) and text feature vectors (Bag-of-Words embeddings of 1000–2026 dimensions depending on dataset) for N paired image-text samples without any label information. It outputs L-length binary hash codes in {−1,+1}^L for each sample in both modalities, enabling O(1) Hamming-distance-based approximate nearest neighbor retrieval. The hierarchical encoding tree and proxy samples are used exclusively during training; at inference, only the two MLP hash encoders (512-dim hidden layer) are deployed, ensuring minimal computational overhead.

## Method

HINT first constructs a cross-modal encoding tree by enhancing sparse inter-modal pairs with intra-modal KNN edges (k=3, cosine similarity), then condensing the resulting graph via greedy Merge and Compress operations guided by hierarchical structural entropy minimization, yielding a tree with dense intra-community and sparse inter-community connections. For each sample, the tree defines same-modal and cross-modal neighbor sets from which proxy samples are computed as mean hash-model outputs. A curriculum-based modality mixup then interpolates between these proxies with a data-driven ratio λ computed via MMD (Gaussian kernel) between their cosine-distance distributions, progressively shifting from intra-modal to cross-modal alignment as training proceeds. A global-view consistency loss (KL divergence between batch-level semantic distributions of original samples and their cross-modal proxies) further enforces modality alignment. A theoretical lemma bounds the variance of the mixed proxy as O(α²/n⁺ + (1−α)²/n⁻), showing that when inter-community separation exceeds this variance, the tree-guided proxy yields a positive retrieval margin over cross-community samples.

## Results

HINT achieves state-of-the-art MAP across all three benchmark datasets (MIRFlickr-25K, NUS-WIDE, MS-COCO) for both Image→Text and Text→Image retrieval at all code lengths (16–128 bits). At 128 bits, HINT reaches 75.5% MAP (I→T) and 74.6% (T→I) on MIRFlickr-25K, 67.3% and 67.8% on NUS-WIDE, and 61.1% and 60.8% on MS-COCO, consistently outperforming the strongest baselines (VTM-UCH, DEMO, HuggingHash+) by 0.5–1.5 percentage points. The method also demonstrates 26–31× retrieval speedup over dense vector search and maintains <1% standard deviation across five independent runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HINT outperforms all 15 baselines across every dataset and code length for both retrieval directions. Among the strongest recent unsupervised methods, HINT leads VTM-UCH by 1.0% (I→T) and 1.6% (T→I) on MIRFlickr-25K at 128 bits, and DEMO by 1.2% and 1.8% respectively; on NUS-WIDE the margins are 0.7% (I→T) and 0.5% (T→I) over VTM-UCH. On MS-COCO, HINT surpasses DEMO and HuggingHash+ (both at 60.5% I→T) by 0.6% and leads HuggingHash+ (60.1% T→I) by 0.7%. Traditional hashing methods (CVH, LSSH, CMFH, FSH, MTFH, FOMH, DCH) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 10–20 percentage points, confirming the advantage of deep unsupervised approaches. The performance gap is notably larger on Text→Image tasks, which the authors attribute to the sparsity and lower initial quality of text features benefiting more from HINT's hierarchical neighborhood aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HINT builds on the structural entropy framework (Li et al., 2018; Zou et al., 2023) originally developed for graph community detection, repurposing it to guide the construction of a hierarchical encoding tree over cross-modal relationship graphs. It departs from the dominant paradigm in unsupervised cross-modal hashing (UCCH, DEMO, UDDH, VTM-UCH), which relies on flat pairwise image-text supervision and direct modality alignment, by introducing tree-structured neighborhood aggregation and a curriculum-based modality mixup that progressively transitions from intra-modal to cross-modal alignment. The proxy-sample mechanism and MMD-driven adaptive weighting draw on manifold mixup (Verma et al., 2019) and domain adaptation (Long et al., 2015), but their integration with a structurally-optimized encoding tree is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HINT offers a principled, label-free mechanism for recovering hierarchical semantic communities in cross-modal data, which could benefit any unsupervised retrieval setting where pairwise supervision is sparse. The theoretical variance bound (Lemma 4.1) provides a clean justification for why community-level proxy alignment outperforms instance-level alignment under inter-community separation. However, the method is limited to static tree construction (no iterative refinement), relies on pre-extracted features from fixed encoders, and is validated only on three mid-scale datasets; open questions include scalability to very large corpora, extension to additional modalities (audio, video), and whether the structural entropy objective generalizes beyond the binary Merge/Compress operations used here.
