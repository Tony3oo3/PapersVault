## Problem

Existing fair GNN methods assume sensitive attributes are fully observed or missing completely at random, but adversarial missingness processes can strategically withhold sensitive values to make imputed data appear fair while the complete data remains biased. This causes fairness-aware models trained on imputed data to overestimate their own fairness and produce misleading guarantees. The paper formalizes this threat (proving it NP-hard) and shows that standard imputation under degree-based adversarial missingness can underestimate true bias by up to 433%.

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph with node attributes, a subset of nodes with observed binary sensitive attributes (default 30%), and labeled training nodes, and produces both node class predictions and imputed sensitive values for all nodes. Unusually, the imputation is deliberately adversarial—f_imp is trained to generate worst-case sensitive values that maximize the demographic parity gap rather than the most accurate values. When no sensitive information is available at all, f_imp falls back to imputing from ground-truth class labels using the LDAM loss, exploiting the worst-case assumption that the minority class maps to the sensitive group.

## Method

BFtS (Better Fair than Sorry) is a 3-player adversarial training framework where a GNN classifier f_class, a DNN sensitive-attribute predictor f_bias, and a GNN imputation model f_imp are jointly trained via a min-max objective. f_class minimizes classification cross-entropy plus a weighted bias loss; f_bias maximizes the bias loss by predicting sensitive attributes from f_class's final-layer representations; and f_imp minimizes an LDAM imputation loss while simultaneously maximizing the bias loss, thereby generating worst-case imputations that stress-test the classifier's fairness. The key theoretical result is that with optimal f_bias, the min-max game reduces to minimizing the Jensen-Shannon divergence between sensitive-group representations, so f_imp provably produces imputations that maximize the demographic parity gap and f_class achieves ΔDP=0 in the worst case. The LDAM loss handles the inherent imbalance in sensitive attributes by assigning larger margins to the smaller class, and the 3-player structure avoids the convergence failures (vanishing JS divergence, mode collapse) that plague 2-player schemes with independent imputation.

## Results

On the six real-world graph datasets (BAIL, CREDIT, GERMAN, NBA, POKEC-Z, POKEC-N) with 30% observed sensitive attributes and GCN as the backbone, BFtS achieves the best fairness across all datasets: e.g., on BAIL it reaches 6.70% ΔDP and 2.70% ΔEQOP (vs. 7.90% and 4.10% for FairGNN), and on GERMAN it reaches 2.74% ΔDP and 1.70% ΔEQOP (vs. 5.70% and 5.70% for FairGNN). BFtS also matches or exceeds the best baseline in accuracy on BAIL (0.85 F1), GERMAN (0.74 F1), and NBA (0.71 F1), while sacrificing at most ~1% AVPR on CREDIT. In the no-sensitive-information setting (Table 1), BFtS outperforms RNF on all six datasets in both fairness and accuracy, e.g., reducing ΔDP from 11.65% to 8.01% on BAIL while improving F1 from 85.3% to 86.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BFtS consistently outperforms all five baselines on fairness metrics across all six datasets. Against Debias (Zhang et al., 2018), BFtS reduces ΔDP by 1–5 percentage points on every dataset while matching or exceeding its F1 score (e.g., 6.70% vs. 8.78% ΔDP on BAIL). Against FairGNN (Dai & Wang, 2021), which uses independent imputation, BFtS achieves substantially lower bias—e.g., 2.74% vs. 5.70% ΔDP on GERMAN and 1.70% vs. 5.70% ΔEQOP—while also improving F1 by 1–2 points. Against RNF (Du et al., 2021), the only baseline that operates without any sensitive information, BFtS wins on both accuracy and fairness on BAIL, GERMAN, and CREDIT, and on fairness alone on POKEC-Z, POKEC-N, and NBA. Against FairVGNN (Wang et al., 2022) and FairSIN (Yang et al., 2024), which require complete sensitive attributes (supplied via independent imputation), BFtS achieves lower ΔDP and ΔEQOP on all datasets with comparable or slightly lower AVPR (≤1% loss on CREDIT). The advantage is most pronounced under the adversarial degree-based missingness process, where independent imputation underestimates true bias by up to 433% (NBA) while BFtS rarely underestimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

BFtS extends the line of fair GNN work (Debias, FairGNN, RNF, FairVGNN, FairSIN) that assumes sensitive attributes are fully observed or missing completely at random, by introducing an adversarial missingness threat model where the pattern of missing sensitive values is strategically chosen to disguise true disparities. The 3-player adversarial framework draws on dual-discriminator GANs (Nguyen et al., 2017; Vandenhende et al., 2019) and distributionally robust optimization, repurposing the imputation model as a second adversary that generates worst-case sensitive values. The adversarial missingness angle connects to Koyuncu et al.'s work on adversarial missingness in causal structure learning, but is the first to apply it to fair graph classification, where prior adversarial fairness attacks focus on edge perturbation, node injection, or data poisoning rather than selective attribute withholding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically relevant and previously unexplored vulnerability: a simple degree-based heuristic for selecting which sensitive attributes to withhold can cause standard imputation to underestimate true bias by up to 433%, making a biased model appear fair. The worst-case imputation guarantee (Theorem 3) provides a minimax fairness bound that independent imputation methods cannot offer, and the framework handles the extreme case of zero observed sensitive attributes. Limitations include the restriction to binary sensitive attributes and demographic parity, the reliance on the degree bias assumption for the adversary, and evaluation primarily on small-to-medium graphs; extending to multi-class/continuous sensitive attributes, other fairness metrics (e.g., ranking-based), and large-scale real-world graphs remains open.
