## Problem

In cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning, product policies scale well but are provably suboptimal, while fully correlated joint policies are expressive but intractable. This paper asks whether structured subteam-based correlations—where agents within a group act fully correlated and across groups act independently—can yield near-optimal policies with finite-time convergence guarantees, and how to construct such subteams in practice without oracle knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a global state, the actions of DAG parent agents (zero-padded to a fixed-length vector of size N·Σ|A^i| for batch processing), pairwise dependency scores, and an edge budget B. It outputs a correlated joint action distribution factorized over subteams (π(a|s) = ∏_k π^{C_k}(a^{C_k}|s)) and, in the dynamic variant, a context-aware DAG partition that is re-constructed as dependency scores change. In the deep MARL setting, parent actions are detached from the computation graph to prevent backpropagation through the correlation structure.

## Method

The paper represents correlated joint policies via Bayesian networks (DAGs) where agents are partitioned into subteams that are fully connected internally and have no edges between them. Theoretically, under a decomposability condition on the reward and transition functions (each decomposable into per-subteam components plus bounded errors), log-barrier-regularized policy gradient ascent converges to a policy whose suboptimality is bounded by a term scaling with g({C_k}) = Σ_k 2^{|C_k|} − K (favored by finer partitions) plus a term scaling with the decomposition errors (favored by coarser partitions), revealing a fundamental trade-off. Practically, a greedy heuristic iteratively merges the pair of subteams with the highest average pairwise dependency score until the edge budget B is exhausted, producing a context-aware DAG that can be re-constructed dynamically. This heuristic is integrated as the actor into MAPPO/MADDPG for policy-based methods and repurposed to determine subgroups in VAST for value-factorization methods, outperforming both the original VAST meta-learning approach and ungrouped QTRAN across multiple benchmarks.

## Results

In the tabular Coordination Game (N=5), coarser subteam partitions consistently achieve lower suboptimality: full DAG and 1+4 partition perform best, followed by 2+3, then product policy. The fitted decomposition errors (Table 1) confirm this ordering, with the full DAG achieving |ε_P|=7.13e-08 and |ε_r|=3.73e-08 versus |ε_P|=6.03e-01 and |ε_r|=1.56 for the product policy. In the deep MARL experiments (MAPPO/MADDPG), the dependency-based heuristic achieves the highest final performance across all three environments (Coordination Game, Aloha, Predator-Prey), with the ordering heuristic ≈ full > random ≈ product in Coordination Game, and heuristic clearly best while full becomes worst in Predator-Prey. In the VAST value-factorization setting, the heuristic outperforms both the original VAST meta-learning approach and ungrouped QTRAN on Warehouse (N=16) and Battle (N=40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VAST (Phan et al., 2021), the paper's dependency-based heuristic for determining subgroups outperforms VAST's meta-learning approach on both Warehouse and Battle, which is the best variant reported in the original VAST paper. Both VAST variants (with and without the heuristic) surpass ungrouped QTRAN (Son et al., 2019), confirming that subgroup-level value factorization is beneficial. In the policy-gradient setting (MAPPO/MADDPG), the heuristic DAG consistently outperforms the product policy (no edges) and random DAG under the same edge budget across all three deep MARL environments. The full DAG (all agents in one subteam) performs well in Coordination Game and Aloha but degrades to the worst performer in Predator-Prey (N=15, continuous actions), where the heuristic's context-aware edge allocation provides a clear advantage. The line DAG (from prior work) does not satisfy Assumption 3 and is only included as a reference in the tabular experiments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Chen & Zhang (2023), which introduced Bayesian network (BN) correlated policies for cooperative MARL and proved asymptotic convergence, by establishing finite-time convergence rates via log barrier regularization (analogous to Agarwal et al., 2021, in the single-agent setting). It then goes further by proving near-optimality guarantees for a subclass of BNs where agents are partitioned into fully-correlated subteams with no inter-subteam edges, under a decomposability condition on the environment's reward and transition functions. This positions the work as the first to establish optimality guarantees for BN policies without requiring full independence among agents, bridging the gap between scalable product policies (MAPPO, MADDPG) and intractable fully-correlated joint policies. The practical heuristic for dynamic DAG construction also draws on the coordination graph literature (Guestrin et al., 2002; Wang et al., 2022) but applies it to the policy side rather than the value side.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled theoretical framework explaining when and why subteam-based coordination in MARL achieves near-optimality, with the suboptimality bound explicitly quantified by decomposition errors and subteam sizes. The identified trade-off—finer partitions speed up convergence but increase decomposition errors—gives practitioners a concrete design criterion for choosing subteam granularity. Limitations include the reliance on a decomposability assumption that may not hold in highly entangled environments, the tabular nature of the theoretical analysis, and the greedy heuristic's lack of guarantees in the dynamic setting. Open questions include whether the DAG structure can be learned end-to-end, whether the decomposability condition can be relaxed to handle partially overlapping subteams, and how these ideas scale to hundreds of agents in continuous-action domains.
