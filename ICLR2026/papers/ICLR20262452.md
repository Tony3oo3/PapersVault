## Problem

Self-Explainable GNNs (SE-GNNs) are designed to provide ante-hoc explanations of their predictions, yet no prior work has characterized the catastrophic failure where explanations are unambiguously unrelated to the model's actual decision process. This paper identifies that SE-GNNs can achieve optimal true risk while outputting degenerate explanations (e.g., highlighting background nodes with no class-discriminative power), and that most existing faithfulness metrics fail to detect such explanations.

## Input / Output

The SE-GNN takes a graph G=(V,E) with node features as input and jointly produces a class label and a per-node relevance score vector p_u ∈ [0,1], which is thresholded or top-k selected to form the explanation subgraph R ⊆ G. The proposed EST metric takes the explanation R and the full graph G, then computes a faithfulness score by evaluating the classifier's prediction on all supergraphs G' satisfying R ⊆ G' ⊆ G, reporting the maximum prediction distance. The attack setup additionally takes a designated malicious explanation (a set of node indices per class) and injects it via a binary cross-entropy regularizer during training.

## Method

The paper's core theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that for SE-GNNs like GSAT, LRI, CAL, GMT-lin, and SMGNN, a hard explanation extractor can select a single node from an anchor set (nodes present in every graph) to encode the predicted label, while a trivial classifier maps that node back to the label, achieving optimal true risk with completely unfaithful explanations. To demonstrate exploitability, the authors introduce an attack that adds a BCE loss pushing relevance scores toward attacker-designated nodes, successfully producing degenerate explanations without degrading (and sometimes improving) accuracy. To address undetectability, they propose the Extension Sufficiency Test (EST), which evaluates sufficiency by maximizing prediction distance over all supergraphs of the explanation within the input graph, avoiding the blind spots of metrics restricted to edge removals or complement removals. EST is shown to consistently reject ≥50% of known-unfaithful explanations where other metrics score near zero, and it also detects naturally occurring degenerate explanations in standard training runs.

## Results

The paper's central quantitative result is the RejRatio (fraction of known-unfaithful explanations correctly rejected) across four datasets and three SE-GNN architectures. EST achieves 100% RejRatio on MNISTsp for all three models (SMGNN, GSAT, DIR), 94–97% on MUTAG, and 48–49% on RBGV, where competing metrics (Suf, RFid-, CF) score 0–12%. On SST2P, EST reaches 49–62% while most baselines remain below 14%. The attack itself succeeds with F1 ≥ 92% alignment to designated degenerate explanations in 11 of 12 model-dataset configurations, while preserving or even improving task accuracy (e.g., DIR on MNISTsp jumps from 41.8% to 94.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EST dominates or ties all four baseline metric families in the majority of configurations. Against Yuan et al.'s Fid-/Fid+ (complement/explanation removal), EST is substantially better on RBGV (48–49% vs. 12–32% / 15–20%) and SST2P (49–62% vs. 8–51% / 9–44%), though Fid- occasionally matches EST on SST2P-DIR (50% vs. 49%). Against Azzolin et al.'s Suf (complement swap), EST is dramatically superior on RBGV (48–49% vs. 0–3%) and SST2P (49–62% vs. 7–14%), while Suf matches EST on MNISTsp and MUTAG (both ~99–100%). Against Chen et al.'s CF (Counterfactual Fidelity), EST wins on RBGV (48–49% vs. 5–50%) and SST2P (49–62% vs. 23–42%), but CF is competitive on MNISTsp (91–95% vs. 100%). Against Zheng et al.'s RFid-/RFid+ (edge removal), EST is far superior on RBGV (48–49% vs. 0–11% / –) and SST2P (49–62% vs. 4–14% / –), while RFid- matches EST on MNISTsp (99% vs. 100%). The key finding is that EST is the only metric that never drops below ~48% RejRatio across all 12 model-dataset pairs, whereas every baseline has at least one configuration where it scores near zero.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SE-GNN literature (GSAT, LRI, CAL, GMT-lin, SMGNN, DIR) and prior identifications of explanation weaknesses such as redundancy (Tai et al., 2025), ambiguity (Azzolin et al., 2025b), and spurious correlations (Wu et al., 2022), but departs by identifying a qualitatively distinct failure: explanations that are provably unrelated to the model's decision process while the model achieves optimal risk. The degeneracy concept echoes rationalization work in NLP (Yu et al., 2019; Jethani et al., 2021), but Theorem 1 provides the first formal characterization of which SE-GNN loss functions admit such solutions. For evaluation, the paper extends the faithfulness-metric literature (Fid±, RFid±, Suf, Nec, CF) by introducing a controlled benchmark where metrics are tested against known-unfaithful explanations rather than ground-truth correlation, and proposes EST as a more robust alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SE-GNNs are explicitly positioned for high-stakes domains (health forecasting, power grid analysis, drug discovery), so the demonstration that their explanations can be completely unfaithful while maintaining optimal accuracy undermines the core interpretability guarantee that motivates their use. The finding that standard faithfulness metrics score near-zero rejection on these known-unfaithful cases means current auditing tooling is inadequate, and the EST metric plus benchmark provide a concrete path forward. Key open questions include: whether architectural changes can prevent degenerate explanations from being optimal (the paper notes DIR with small K still exhibits the issue), whether EST's OOD sensitivity can be reduced without losing its detection power, and how to extend detection to the redundancy failure mode (explanations containing both relevant and irrelevant elements) that EST explicitly cannot catch.
