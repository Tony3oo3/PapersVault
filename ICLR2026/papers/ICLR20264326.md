## Problem

Existing embodied navigation methods are confined to narrow task domains and embodiment-specific architectures: cross-task approaches assume a fixed camera configuration, while cross-embodiment methods are restricted to specific navigation tasks. No unified foundation model exists that can handle diverse navigation tasks (VLN, object searching, tracking, autonomous driving) across heterogeneous embodiments (quadrupeds, drones, wheeled robots, vehicles) without task-specific fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264326/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes egocentric RGB video streams from N cameras (1–8 views, randomized FoV 75°–120° and height 0.6–1.5 m) at time steps 1:T, plus a natural-language instruction. Visual features are extracted via DINOv2 and SigLIP (576 patches per frame) and compressed via grid pooling to 64 fine-grained tokens (latest frame) or 4 coarse tokens (history), all constrained within a 1024–2048 token budget. For navigation, the output is a sequence of 8 waypoints (x, y, z, θ) normalized to [-1,1] and rescaled by a task-specific factor (e.g., 1.0 m for indoor, 50.8 m for cars); for QA tasks, the output is autoregressive text tokens.

## Method

NavFoM extends a video-based VLM (Qwen2-7B backbone) into a dual-branch architecture that co-tunes navigation and QA in an end-to-end manner. Two key mechanisms enable cross-embodiment and cross-task unification: (1) Temporal-Viewpoint Indicator (TVI) tokens, which compose sinusoidal angle embeddings (preserving circular continuity of azimuth), sinusoidal time embeddings, and a learnable base embedding via task-specific combinations to let the LLM distinguish camera viewpoints and temporal order without architecture changes; and (2) Budget-Aware Temporal Sampling (BATS), which applies an exponential forgetting-curve probability P(t) = (1−ε)e^{k(t−T)/T} + ε to dynamically select history frames under a fixed token budget, favoring recent observations while retaining long-range context. The model is trained on 8.02 M navigation samples (VLN, object nav, tracking, driving, web-video) plus 4.76 M image/video QA samples across 56 H100 GPUs for ~72 hours, using MSE loss for waypoints and cross-entropy for QA with a β=10 loss-balancing factor.

## Results

On VLN-CE RxR (the most challenging single-view setting), NavFoM achieves 57.4% SR, improving over the previous best StreamVLN (51.8%) by 5.6 points, and in the four-camera setting reaches 64.4% SR, surpassing HNR (56.3%) which relies on RGB-D and odometry. On HM3D-OVON object goal navigation, NavFoM achieves 45.2% SR zero-shot (four views), outperforming the fine-tuned SOTA MTU3D (40.8%). On EVT-Bench tracking, NavFoM matches or exceeds TrackVLA (88.4% vs. 85.1% SR in four views), and on NAVSIM autonomous driving achieves 84.3 PDMS, competitive with specialized driving models like LAW (84.6) and PARA-Drive (84.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In single-view VLN-CE, NavFoM outperforms all prior vision-only baselines: it beats StreamVLN (51.8%→57.4% SR on RxR), NaVILA (49.3%→57.4%), and Uni-NaVid (48.7%→57.4%), with the largest margin over NaVid (45.7%→57.4%). In multi-view VLN-CE, NavFoM (64.4% SR on RxR) surpasses HNR (56.3%) and ETPNav (54.7%) by 8.1 and 9.7 points respectively, notably without using depth or odometry inputs that those methods rely on. On HM3D-OVON object goal navigation, NavFoM achieves 45.2% SR zero-shot (four views), exceeding MTU3D (40.8%) and Uni-NaVid (39.5%), though MTU3D leads on VAL SEEN (55.0% vs. 40.1%). On EVT-Bench tracking, NavFoM (four views) achieves 88.4% SR, slightly exceeding TrackVLA (85.1%), while in single view they are essentially tied (85.0% vs. 85.1%). On NAVSIM autonomous driving, NavFoM (84.3 PDMS) is competitive with specialized driving models—slightly below LAW (84.6) and PARA-Drive (84.0) but above UniAD (83.4) and DrivingGPT (82.4)—demonstrating that a unified navigation model can match task-specific driving architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video-based VLM navigation line (NaVid, Uni-NaVid, NaVILA) by unifying cross-task and cross-embodiment navigation within a single VLA architecture, whereas prior cross-task methods (Uni-NaVid, NaVILA) assume a fixed camera configuration and prior cross-embodiment methods (GnM, ViNT, ExAuG) are restricted to specific task types. The architectural foundation follows the Visual Instruction Tuning / Llama-vid recipe (dual-branch VLM with cross-modality projector), but introduces two key departures: Temporal-Viewpoint Indicator (TVI) tokens that encode camera azimuth and temporal position via sinusoidal embeddings rather than learned positional encodings, and Budget-Aware Temporal Sampling (BATS) that replaces uniform sampling or token merging with a forgetting-curve-based probabilistic frame selection under a fixed token budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NavFoM provides the first practical, deployable navigation foundation model that handles quadrupeds, drones, wheeled robots, and vehicles across VLN, object search, tracking, and driving without task-specific fine-tuning, validated on seven benchmarks and real-world hardware. Key limitations include poor performance on long-horizon unseen-map UAV navigation (suggesting data quality/scale bottlenecks), task-specific scaling factors for trajectory normalization that limit true embodiment-agnosticism, and reliance on a 7B LLM backbone that constrains on-device deployment. Open questions include whether TVI tokens generalize to truly novel camera geometries (e.g., fisheye, event cameras), whether the BATS forgetting curve is optimal versus learned attention-based history compression, and whether the 8M-sample scale is sufficient for robust open-world generalization beyond the tested benchmarks.
