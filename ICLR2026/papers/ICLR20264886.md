## Problem

While LLMs achieve near-human performance on mathematical benchmarks, their robustness to minor input perturbations in mathematical reasoning remains largely unexplored. Existing adversarial attack methods either target classification models, focus on general jailbreak tasks, or require manual construction of perturbation rules, leaving no automated, scalable approach specifically designed to probe the vulnerability of generative LLMs in math reasoning with minimal semantic disruption.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264886/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a mathematical word problem (from GSM8K or MATH500) and leverages three external resources: the target LLM's own embedding matrix, the WordNet dictionary, and BERT-large-uncased as a masked language model. It produces a single-word perturbed question where exactly one word is replaced. Attack success is determined by a two-step mechanism: first, the target model's answer to the perturbed question is compared against the ground-truth answer; second, a commercial LLM (qwen3-max-preview) performs secondary verification to confirm the model genuinely produced an incorrect answer, filtering out cases where the perturbation merely changed the phrasing of a correct solution.

## Method

MSCR generates candidate replacement words for each token in the input question by unioning three complementary sources: (1) the top-10 most cosine-similar vectors from the target LLM's normalized embedding space, further filtered by character-level constraints and morphological stem matching; (2) the top-5 WordNet synonyms ranked by cosine similarity to the original word; and (3) MLM predictions from BERT-large-uncased at the masked position, retaining only candidates with predicted probability above 0.1. Candidates are tried sequentially per word, and the first one that causes the target model to produce an incorrect answer (confirmed by secondary LLM evaluation) terminates the attack. A key design choice is the differential replacement strategy: cosine-similarity candidates replace only the local occurrence of the target word, while WordNet and MLM candidates perform global replacement of all matching words in the sentence to preserve grammatical coherence. This multi-source integration combines the model's internal distributed representations with external structured lexical knowledge and context-sensitive generative predictions, enabling effective single-word attacks without human intervention.

## Results

On GSM8K, the MSCR single-word perturbation causes accuracy drops of 18.80%–49.89% across 12 open-source LLMs, with Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct suffering the largest degradation (85.90% → 36.01%). On the harder MATH500 benchmark, all models drop by over 10%, with gemma-2-9b-it showing the steepest decline (47.00% → 11.60%, a 35.40% absolute drop). Transferability experiments on commercial models confirm the effect: OpenAI-o3 drops 15.88% on GSM8K and 6.80% on MATH500, while GPT-4o drops 17.21% and 7.80% respectively. Additionally, attacked responses are on average 1.04×–2.14× longer than original responses, with some reasoning models exceeding 10× in individual cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a head-to-head comparison against other adversarial attack methods (e.g., TextFooler, BERT-ATTACK, or jailbreak-based attacks); instead, the primary comparison is each model's own accuracy before versus after the MSCR single-word perturbation. The key finding is that even the strongest open-source models (gemma-3-27b-it at 98.10% on GSM8K, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B at 94.39%) still lose 18–20% accuracy from a single-word change, while smaller models (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, gemma-2-9b-it) lose 35–50%. The ablation in Appendix D shows that combining all three candidate sources (cosine similarity + WordNet + MLM) consistently outperforms any single source alone—for example, on gemma-3-4b-it the full MSCR drops accuracy to 63.84% versus 72.86% (cosine only), 66.87% (WordNet only), and 70.43% (MLM only). Transferability to commercial models (OpenAI-o3, GPT-4o) further demonstrates that adversarial samples generated targeting one open-source model generalize across model families, with 15–17% drops on GSM8K.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSCR repurposes the word-substitution adversarial attack paradigm (originally developed for classification models like TextFooler and BERT-ATTACK) for generative LLMs, while drawing on the mathematical-reasoning vulnerability literature (Shi et al. 2023, Li et al. 2024, Mirzadeh et al. 2024) that relied on manually designed perturbation rules. Its key departure is full automation via a multi-source candidate generation pipeline—combining embedding-space cosine similarity, WordNet synsets, and MLM contextual predictions—eliminating the manual rule construction that limited prior math-perturbation benchmarks. Unlike jailbreak attacks that target safety alignment, MSCR targets the correctness of mathematical reasoning specifically, and unlike prior perturbation studies that modified numbers or added sentences, it restricts itself to a single-word substitution to isolate the effect of minimal semantic drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single trivially similar word swap (e.g., 'Josh'→'he', 'Below'→'under') can degrade accuracy by up to ~50% and simultaneously inflate response length by 2×+ raises urgent questions about whether current LLMs perform genuine mathematical reasoning or fragile pattern matching, and whether deployment in safety-critical numerical contexts is advisable without robustness guarantees. The cross-model transferability to GPT-4o and o3 suggests this is not an artifact of any single architecture. Open questions include: what defenses (if any) can mitigate single-word sensitivity without degrading normal performance, whether the response-length inflation indicates a specific failure mode in CoT reasoning, and whether the vulnerability persists under stronger alignment or fine-tuning. The paper does not propose defenses, leaving the practical mitigation question entirely open.
