## Problem

Adapting large-scale flow and diffusion models to downstream rewards via fine-tuning (RL or stochastic optimal control) requires full-model backpropagation, making memory scale linearly with model size and become prohibitive at billions of parameters. Classifier Guidance avoids this cost but is inherently offline—training only on the fixed pre-trained distribution—which limits exploration beyond the data distribution and causes numerical instability at moderate reward scalings. The paper asks how to retain the online, control-theoretic advantages of SOC-based adaptation while decoupling resource costs from base model complexity and supporting non-differentiable (black-box) rewards.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained flow or diffusion model (DiT on 256×256 ImageNet, 32×32 CIFAR-10, Stable Diffusion 2 with text prompts, or FlowMol for 3D molecules) and a scalar reward function accessible only through function evaluations (e.g., JPEG compression ratio, GFN2-xTB dipole moment, rdkit QED, LAION aesthetics score). It produces reward-adapted samples from the same modality as the base model. The only trainable component is a small value network (0.5M–92M parameters for images, 2.5M for molecules), which is orders of magnitude smaller than the base model it guides, and the base model weights are never updated.

## Method

Value Matching (VM) frames reward adaptation as a quadratic-cost, control-affine stochastic optimal control problem and learns the value function V(x,t) online via iterative regression. At each iteration, trajectories are sampled under the current policy u(x,t) = −σᵀ(t)∇ₓV_θ(x,t); single-sample Monte Carlo estimates of the cost functional Ĵ_t are computed along these trajectories; and V_θ is updated by minimizing an L2 loss between V_θ(x_t,t) and Ĵ_t with a timestep-dependent weighting w(t) that normalizes by λ² and down-weights high-variance early timesteps. A key theoretical result (Proposition 1) shows that stochastic noise acts as a smoothing kernel, guaranteeing V is differentiable in x for t<1 even when the reward is non-differentiable, enabling black-box reward optimization. Proposition 2 proves V is the unique critical point of the expected VM loss, providing convergence guarantees. Unlike CG (offline, trains on p_pre), VM is on-policy and aligns its training distribution with the evolving policy; unlike Adjoint Matching (which regresses ∇V onto ∇J estimates and requires reward gradients), VM is a zeroth-order analogue that regresses V onto J estimates and obtains gradients via backpropagation through the value network alone.

## Results

On FlowMol GEOM-Drugs with QED reward, VM(λ=500) improves molecular stability from 49.5% to 67.6%, validity from 48.3% to 51.7%, and QED from 0.42 to 0.49 over the base model. For dipole moment optimization, VM increases the mean from 6.4 to 7.5 Debye while reducing fragmentation from 31% to 28%. In terms of resource efficiency, VM requires less than 12 GB of memory across all models (vs. up to 250 GB for fine-tuning SD2, a >95% reduction) and completes training in under 35 hours (vs. up to 800 GPU-hours). Inference overhead is minimal at only 1–30% additional sampling time compared to the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Classifier Guidance (CG), VM wins decisively on both stability and sample efficiency: CG becomes numerically unstable at moderate reward scales (λ) due to exponential overflow in its loss, while VM maintains stable training and achieves substantially higher rewards; in a 2D KL-convergence test, VM reaches the optimal tilted distribution significantly faster than CG. Against fine-tuning methods (DDPO, DPOK, CT-PPO), VM avoids the mode collapse that afflicts all three at higher λ values, achieves comparable or superior mean reward, and requires only a single hyperparameter (λ) versus CT-PPO's extensive grid search over clipping, exploration, and scaling parameters. Against the inference-time scheme SVDD, VM is 40× faster for compression and 600× faster for aesthetics rewards while simultaneously achieving higher aesthetic scores and better diversity (Vendi score). Overall, VM matches or exceeds fine-tuning performance in reward while using less than 5% of their memory, and outperforms CG in both expressivity and convergence speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

Value Matching (VM) is positioned as an online, gradient-free generalization of Classifier Guidance (CG), reframed through the stochastic optimal control (SOC) lens established by Domingo-Enrich et al. (SOCM, Adjoint Matching) and Tang. Conceptually, VM is the zeroth-order analogue of Adjoint Matching (regressing V onto J estimates rather than matching ∇V to ∇J estimates), and it emerges as a simplification of CT-PPO where the actor optimization step becomes redundant once the actor is parameterized as s_pre − ∇V. The key departure from fine-tuning methods (DDPO, DPOK, AM, CT-PPO) is that the base model remains frozen, shifting all learning to a small value network, while the key departure from CG is the on-policy training that resolves the offline distribution mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 95% memory reduction (from up to 250 GB to under 12 GB) while matching fine-tuning performance makes reward-guided adaptation of billion-parameter models (SD2, DiT) practically accessible on a single GPU, which was previously infeasible. The ability to handle non-differentiable black-box rewards (JPEG compression, GFN2-xTB dipole moments, QED) opens the door to domains like drug discovery where reward functions are simulators or experimental measurements. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-objective or risk-sensitive settings, whether value network capacity bottlenecks emerge at even larger scales, and how VM interacts with classifier-free guidance at inference time beyond the simple additive drift modification demonstrated here.
