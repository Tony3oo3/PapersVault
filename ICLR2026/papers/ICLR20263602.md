## Problem

Prevailing world model pre-training on action-free video optimizes a deterministic single-step prediction objective, which is fundamentally ill-posed for stochastic environments where multiple plausible futures exist from the same state. This deterministic bias suppresses environmental stochasticity, causing predictive uncertainty to remain artificially low and flat across temporal horizons rather than growing naturally with prediction distance. No existing method explicitly structures or calibrates temporal uncertainty during pre-training.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the model consumes 64×64×3 RGB video frames from Something-Something-v2 in variable-horizon pairs (o_t, o_{t+k}) where k is randomly sampled from {1,…,5}, with a sinusoidal relative temporal embedding Δt_k^e conditioning each prediction. During fine-tuning, it additionally receives agent actions a_{t-1} alongside the frozen pre-trained latent state. Outputs include per-head Gaussian parameters (mean and variance) over future latent states, ensemble-mean reconstructed frames, a scalar predictive uncertainty estimate from model disagreement, and an actor-critic control policy operating on the composite latent state [ŝ_t; s̃_t].

## Method

HAUWM pre-trains an ensemble of M=7 RSSM dynamics heads (deterministic state 200, stochastic state 30) to predict future latent states at randomly sampled horizons, conditioned on sinusoidal relative temporal embeddings that encode the physical time gap k/T. The core novelty is the Horizon-Calibrated Uncertainty (HCU) loss, which maximizes the ensemble's mean-squared disagreement scaled by horizon length k, explicitly enforcing that predictive variance grows monotonically with prediction distance. A dual optimization with adaptive weight λ balances this uncertainty-diversity objective against the standard ELBO reconstruction loss, preventing degenerate solutions of either extreme uncertainty or artificial determinism. For fine-tuning, the pre-trained ensemble is frozen and a lightweight action-conditioned RSSM stream is trained from scratch in parallel, producing a dual-stream composite world model that preserves calibrated uncertainty while learning task-specific action effects for actor-critic policy optimization.

## Results

On the imitation learning benchmark (VMAIL), HAUWM achieves the highest normalized returns on 4 of 5 tasks, reaching 0.94±0.03 on Cheetah Run, 0.89±0.05 on Hopper Hop, 0.77±0.06 on Dial Turn, and 0.97±0.02 on Drawer Close, while trailing ContextWM (0.69) on Push Green (0.63±0.06). In offline RL (LOMPO), HAUWM leads on 4 of 5 tasks with 0.81±0.13 (Cheetah Run), 0.71±0.08 (Hopper Hop), 0.74±0.07 (Drawer Close), and 0.70±0.04 (Push Green), though APV edges ahead on Dial Turn (0.74 vs. 0.67). The ablation confirms the HCU loss is critical: removing it drops DMC performance from 0.74 to 0.64 and RoboDesk from 0.71 to 0.55.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAUWM outperforms all three tabulated baselines (APV, ContextWM, PreLAR) on the majority of tasks in both imitation learning and offline RL. In imitation learning, HAUWM leads ContextWM by 3 points on Cheetah Run (0.94 vs. 0.91) and by 8 points on Dial Turn (0.77 vs. 0.69), but loses to ContextWM on Push Green (0.63 vs. 0.69), which the authors attribute to the task's more deterministic dynamics reducing the benefit of uncertainty modeling. In offline RL, HAUWM's advantage is most pronounced on Cheetah Run (0.81 vs. 0.71 for PreLAR, the next best) and Drawer Close (0.74 vs. 0.71 for APV), while APV narrowly wins on Dial Turn (0.74 vs. 0.67). The learning-curve results in Figure 3 (not tabulated) show HAUWM achieving the fastest convergence and highest asymptotic return on locomotion tasks like Walker Run and Hopper Hop across DMC, MetaWorld, and RoboDesk, with the gap over APV and ContextWM widening over training. iVideoGPT is compared only in Figure 3 learning curves and is not included in the tabulated results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the action-free video pre-training paradigm established by APV (Seo et al., 2022) and refined by ContextWM (Wu et al., 2023) and iVideoGPT (Wu et al., 2024), all of which build on the RSSM/Dreamer latent dynamics architecture. The key departure is a reframe of the pre-training objective: rather than the standard deterministic single-step prediction (frame t+1 from frame t), HAUWM introduces variable-horizon prediction with an ensemble of dynamics heads and a novel Horizon-Calibrated Uncertainty (HCU) loss that explicitly forces predictive variance to grow monotonically with the temporal gap. This positions the work as a targeted fix to what the authors argue is a fundamental misalignment in all prior video-based pre-training methods—namely, that deterministic objectives suppress the very stochasticity that action-free video inherently contains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard RSSM-based baselines exhibit 'uncertainty collapse'—near-flat, artificially low ensemble disagreement even when equipped with multiple heads—challenges the implicit assumption that ensemble methods naturally capture temporal uncertainty, and provides a concrete mechanism (the ELBO objective) for why this happens. Practically, the HCU loss is a simple, low-cost addition that could be adopted by existing pre-training pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to 64×64 pixel observations, a single pre-training dataset (Something-Something-v2), and relatively small-scale benchmarks; whether the approach scales to high-resolution, large-scale video pre-training (e.g., millions of trajectories) remains an open question the authors acknowledge. The ensemble-based architecture also introduces computational overhead that may limit deployment in resource-constrained settings.
