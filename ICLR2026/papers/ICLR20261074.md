## Problem

Automated sewing pattern generation is hindered by two fundamental limitations: diffusion-based methods act as data replicators without intrinsic understanding of garment construction principles, and VLM-based methods are forced to regress thousands of raw floating-point coordinates, a low-level task where errors accumulate catastrophically. Additionally, no large-scale dataset pairs real-world human photographs with their corresponding precise sewing patterns, preventing training of practical image-conditioned generation models.

## Input / Output

The method accepts real human photos, rendered garment images, text descriptions, or existing garment token sequences (for editing) as multimodal input. Output is a structured GarmentCode sewing pattern encoded as a hierarchical discrete token sequence: each garment contains dimension info, multiple panels (each with an identifier, A-pose translation/rotation parameters, and a closed contour of edges classified as straight lines, quadratic/cubic Bezier curves, or circular arcs), and inter-panel stitching relationships. Edges are uniformly sampled into 32 points and quantized with 5 residual indices (codebook size 1024), while RT parameters use 3 residual indices, yielding sequences of length up to 4096–16384 tokens.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline that reframes pattern generation as discrete token prediction rather than continuous coordinate regression. First, an RVQ-VAE with separate ResNet-based encoder-decoder pairs for edges and RT parameters maps continuous curve geometry into discrete codebook indices via residual vector quantization (5 levels for edges, 3 for RT, codebook size 1024), trained with classification, vertex, control-point, and commitment losses. Second, a fine-tuned VLM (LLaVA-1.5 7B or Qwen-2.5-VL) with extended vocabulary (special hierarchical tokens like ⟨SoG⟩, ⟨SoP⟩, ⟨SoE⟩ plus codebook indices 0–1023) autoregressively predicts the full garment token sequence from multimodal inputs using cross-entropy loss. A dedicated data curation pipeline synthesizes over 1M photorealistic image-pattern pairs by extracting textures via Grounded-SAM and FabricDiffusion, simulating diverse poses from AMASS through GarmentCode and Contourcraft, translating renderings to photorealism via Qwen-image-edit, and applying multi-stage quality filtering (improving alignment acceptability from 64.3% to 99.6%).

## Results

On the structured GarmentCode dataset, GarmentGPT (LLaVA-1.5, Image+Text) achieves 95.62% Panel Accuracy, 90.48% Edge Accuracy, and 81.84% Stitch Accuracy, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline AIpparel (78.92%, 74.31%, 56.57% respectively). On the more challenging Real-Garments Benchmark with photorealistic inputs and diverse poses, GarmentGPT retains 90.84% Panel Accuracy and 77.56% Stitch Accuracy, a 2.3× improvement over the best baseline (AIpparel at 38.76% Panel Accuracy). The editing setting further demonstrates the robustness of the discrete representation, reaching 92.95% Stitch Accuracy with only 11.07 vertex L2 error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GarmentGPT decisively outperforms both baselines across all structural and geometric metrics. Against AIpparel (the strongest regression-based baseline), GarmentGPT gains +16.7 percentage points in Panel Accuracy (95.62% vs. 78.92%) and +25.3 points in Stitch Accuracy (81.84% vs. 56.57%) on the structured GarmentCode dataset, while also reducing vertex L2 error from 25.55 to 18.43. Against ChatGarment, the margins are even larger: +35.4 points in Panel Accuracy and +32.6 in Stitch Accuracy. On the Real-Garments Benchmark, where all methods are retrained on the curated real-photo dataset, baselines degrade severely (ChatGarment drops to 25.34% and AIpparel to 38.76% Panel Accuracy), whereas GarmentGPT retains ~95% of its structured-dataset performance at 90.84%, a 2.3× improvement over the best baseline. SewFormer is included only in qualitative comparisons (Fig. 6), where it produces incomplete or distorted patterns on real photos, while GarmentGPT generates well-structured outputs closely matching ground truth. The paper attributes these gains to the discrete compositional paradigm: by reframing generation as token selection over a learned codebook rather than continuous coordinate regression, GarmentGPT avoids the catastrophic error accumulation inherent in regressing thousands of floating-point values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in garment pattern generation: diffusion-based feed-forward models (StableGarment, SewingLDM) that learn distributions over raw coordinates without structural understanding, and VLM-based autoregressive models (AIpparel, ChatGarment) that regress thousands of continuous floating-point values. By borrowing the residual vector quantization mechanism from audio codecs (SoundStream) and applying it to sewing pattern geometry, the authors reframe pattern generation as discrete token prediction, aligning it with the symbolic reasoning strengths of VLMs like LLaVA and Qwen-VL. The GarmentCode representation and its physics-based simulation (Contourcraft) serve as both the target output format and the engine for the data curation pipeline, making the work tightly coupled to that ecosystem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1M+ photorealistic image-pattern pairs and the RealGarment-Bench address a genuine data bottleneck that has limited prior methods to clean A-pose renderings, making this the first practical pathway from casual photos to manufacturing-ready patterns. The discrete tokenization paradigm, if it generalizes, could be a template for other structured geometric generation tasks (CAD, vector graphics, 3D meshes) where continuous regression causes error accumulation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-garment outfits with complex layering, whether the RVQ codebook size (1024) and residual depth (8) are optimal for all garment categories, and whether the simulated-to-real translation via Qwen-image-edit introduces subtle structural biases that affect downstream manufacturing fidelity.
