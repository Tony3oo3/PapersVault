## Problem

LLM-based speech emotion recognition either relies on opaque acoustic embeddings that require an audio encoder at inference, or augments transcripts with coarse sentence-level prosodic descriptions that blur fine-grained cues. There is no interpretable, segment-level mechanism for injecting the localized pitch, intensity, and duration patterns that phonetic evidence identifies as primary carriers of affective prosody into a text-only LLM pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260700/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw speech audio only offline to extract time-aligned vowel segments via forced alignment; at inference the LLM receives purely textual input consisting of the conversational transcript, the target utterance, and per-vowel natural-language prosodic descriptors (e.g., 'high F0, rising, loud, lengthened'). The model outputs a structured response with an explicit reasoning trace inside  tags followed by a single emotion label inside <answer></answer> tags, enabling rule-based verification of both the rationale and the prediction.

## Method

VowelPrompt extracts six low-level descriptors (pitch level, pitch slope, pitch variation, intensity level, intensity variation, and duration) from IPA-identified vowel segments obtained via Montreal Forced Aligner, applies speaker-level z-normalization followed by vowel-type normalization, and discretizes each feature into K=5 ordinal bins mapped to natural-language terms. These descriptors are appended to the transcript so a text-only LLM jointly reasons over lexical semantics and localized prosody without any audio encoder at inference. Model adaptation uses a two-stage regimen: supervised fine-tuning with LoRA on GPT-4o-generated reasoning traces for cold-start alignment, followed by Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that optimizes a composite reward combining exact-match accuracy and format compliance, with a KL penalty anchoring the policy to the SFT reference to preserve in-domain performance while improving cross-domain robustness.

## Results

Under SFT & GRPO with LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, VowelPrompt achieves 73.02% Weighted F1 on IEMOCAP and 68.98% on MELD, outperforming the strongest baseline SpeechCueLLM (71.55% and 67.10%) by 1.47 and 1.88 points respectively. In the zero-shot setting with GPT-4o and Transcript & Context input, VowelPrompt reaches 62.26% UACC on IEMOCAP and 64.34% UACC on MELD, surpassing the transcript-only baseline by up to 6.75 points. Cross-domain transfer (IEMOCAP→MELD) under SFT & GRPO yields 60.28% WF1, a 5.12-point gain over SpeechCueLLM. On the multilingual ASVP-ESD corpus, VowelPrompt achieves 71.36% WF1 with Qwen2-7B-Instruct, outperforming all baselines by over 3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VowelPrompt consistently outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting. Against SpeechCueLLM (sentence-level prosodic prompting), VowelPrompt gains 1.47–1.88% WF1 under SFT & GRPO on IEMOCAP/MELD, 5.12–6.96% in cross-domain transfer, and 2.26–2.53% in zero-shot multilingual evaluation (CaFE, EmoDB), confirming that vowel-level granularity surpasses utterance-level summaries. Against InstructERC (retrieval-augmented multi-task LLM), VowelPrompt leads by 1.70% WF1 on IEMOCAP and 2.02% on MELD under SFT & GRPO with LLaMA-3. Against SALMONN (multimodal speech-language alignment), VowelPrompt achieves 2.00% higher WF1 on IEMOCAP and 2.13% on MELD under the same setting, despite not using raw audio at inference. Against the non-LLM HuBERT-large + MLP baseline, VowelPrompt's advantage is most pronounced: 5.8% WF1 on IEMOCAP in-domain and 15.3% in cross-domain (IEMOCAP→MELD), demonstrating that LLM-based reasoning over interpretable prosodic text substantially exceeds what a classifier can extract from the same acoustic signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

VowelPrompt sits at the intersection of three research threads: classical phonetic analysis of emotional prosody (Ringeval & Chetouani, 2008; Schuller et al., 2009), LLM-based emotion recognition via textual prompting (SpeechCueLLM, Wu et al. 2025), and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards for LLM adaptation (GRPO from DeepSeek-R1). It departs from prior prompting-based approaches by replacing coarse, sentence-level prosodic summaries with fine-grained, vowel-segment-level descriptors derived from forced alignment, and from audio-LLM approaches (SALMONN, Emotion-LLaMA) by operating entirely in text space at inference. The two-stage SFT + GRPO training pipeline is borrowed from the DeepSeek-R1 recipe but applied here with a composite accuracy-and-format reward tailored to structured emotion reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to interpretable, text-only emotion recognition that does not require an audio encoder at deployment, which is valuable for privacy-sensitive or bandwidth-constrained settings. The cross-domain results (e.g., 60.3% vs. 45.0% on IEMOCAP→MELD compared to HuBERT-large) suggest that well-chosen phonetic features may generalize more robustly than learned acoustic embeddings, though this claim would benefit from more controlled comparison. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dimensional (valence-arousal) emotion spaces, whether forced alignment quality is a practical bottleneck for low-resource languages, and whether the gains persist on truly naturalistic (non-acted) speech beyond the TV-derived MELD corpus.
