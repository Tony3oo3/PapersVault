## Problem

Existing localization-based LLM unlearning methods identify a single entangled set of 'important' nodes from joint optimization over forget and retain sets, but cannot disentangle which nodes are exclusively responsible for forgetting, exclusively for retaining, or for both (conflict nodes). This forces uniform interventions across all identified nodes, risking catastrophic over-forgetting of unrelated capabilities or incomplete erasure of target knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261764/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (Zephyr-7B-beta or LLaMA2-7B), a forget set containing harmful/sensitive prompts, and a retain set of specific task datasets (e.g., Winogrande, SST-2, RTE, Bool). It produces an unlearned LLM with selectively modified parameter matrices, along with a per-node classification into three categories: forget nodes (state=0), retain nodes (state=1), and conflict nodes (no consistent assignment). The node granularity is at the level of individual parameter matrices (q, k, v, o, MLP_up, MLP_down, MLP_gate) across 32 layers, and the classification is operationalized as binary masks (forget mask and conflict mask) that gate which parameters receive gradient updates during fine-tuning.

## Method

CLUE is a three-step pipeline that leverages mechanistic interpretability to achieve fine-grained node localization. First, it extracts logical circuits for the forget and retain sets separately using Edge-Pruning with both noising-based and denoising-based interventions, classifying each edge as an AND, OR, or ADDER gate based on set-theoretic relationships between the two intervention circuits. Second, it converts both circuits into Conjunctive Normal Form via Tseitin's transformation and combines them into a single CNF with the constraint that the forget circuit output must be False (0) and the retain circuit output must be True (1). Third, it solves this CNF as a Boolean satisfiability problem using a conflict-driven clause learning SAT solver, assigning each node a state: 1 (retain), 0 (forget), or unassigned (conflict). The unlearning then proceeds in two stages: forget nodes are fine-tuned for 1 epoch using only the forget loss (PO), while conflict nodes are fine-tuned for 5 additional epochs using both forget and retain losses, with all other parameters frozen via the masks.

## Results

On WMDP Cyber, CLUE achieves an average forget efficacy (1-accuracy) of 0.725, retain utility of 0.798, and general utility of 0.429, while modifying only 58.16% of parameters—compared to 74–100% for all baselines. On PKU-SafeRLHF, CLUE reaches 0.680 FE and 0.764 RU with just 54.88% of parameters modified, substantially outperforming the best localization baseline WAGLE (0.649 FE, 0.608 RU at 90.01%). The retain utility advantage is the most pronounced: CLUE's RU exceeds WAGLE by 0.157 on WMDP Cyber and by 0.156 on PKU-SafeRLHF, while full fine-tuning methods (GA, NPO) collapse to RU values below 0.20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLUE outperforms all seven baselines on retain utility across every dataset, with the largest margins against full fine-tuning methods: GA drops to 0.149 RU on WMDP Cyber versus CLUE's 0.798, and NPO reaches only 0.273. Among localization-based methods, CLUE beats WAGLE (the strongest baseline) by +0.157 RU on WMDP Cyber and +0.156 on PKU-SafeRLHF, while also using roughly 32% fewer modified parameters (58% vs. 90%). On forget efficacy, CLUE leads WAGLE by +0.021 on WMDP Cyber and +0.031 on PKU-SafeRLHF, and surpasses DEPN by +0.019 and +0.031 respectively; the gap is smallest on WMDP Bio where CLUE (0.632) edges out WAGLE (0.612) by +0.020. General utility follows the same pattern: CLUE achieves 0.429 on WMDP Cyber versus 0.407 for WAGLE and 0.406 for DEPN. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that removing the forget mask drops FE by 0.045 while replacing the conflict mask drops RU by 0.264, validating that both node categories contribute distinctively to the final performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two threads: (1) circuit discovery for mechanistic interpretability, specifically Edge-Pruning (Bhaskar et al., 2024) for identifying task-relevant subgraphs and the logical circuit framework (Chen et al., 2025) that classifies edges as AND, OR, or ADDER gates; and (2) localization-based LLM unlearning methods (WAGLE, MEMIT, DEPN, PCGU) that identify important neurons but treat them as a single entangled set. CLUE departs from prior localization methods by converting discovered circuits into Conjunctive Normal Form and solving a SAT problem to disentangle nodes into three functionally distinct categories—forget, retain, and conflict—enabling category-specific fine-tuning objectives rather than uniform intervention on all 'important' nodes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conflict-node concept addresses a practical gap in LLM unlearning: nodes that simultaneously encode target knowledge to be removed and non-target capabilities to be preserved. By identifying and separately supervising these nodes with both forget and retain losses, CLUE achieves better forget efficacy and retain utility with roughly 30% fewer modified parameters than competing localization methods. Key limitations include the static nature of circuits (they don't update during fine-tuning), scalability constraints of circuit discovery to very large models, and the inability to provide targeted solutions for every possible conflict combination when multiple retain sets are used. Open questions include whether dynamic circuit tracking during optimization could further improve results, and whether non-fine-tuning editing methods (e.g., direct weight surgery) could handle conflict nodes more efficiently.
