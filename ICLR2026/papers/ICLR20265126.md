## Problem

Protein sequence design must simultaneously satisfy designability (recovering the target backbone) and multiple often-competing developability properties such as solubility and thermostability. Existing strategies—post-hoc mutation, inference-time biasing, and retraining on property-specific subsets—are target-dependent, require careful hyperparameter tuning, and struggle to generalize across diverse objectives without degrading structural fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265126/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein backbone structure (Cα coordinates, e.g., from CATH crystal structures or RFDiffusion-generated de novo backbones) as input and produces an amino acid sequence via an order-agnostic autoregressive decoder (ProteinMPNN). During the semi-online training loop, the current policy generates n rollouts per backbone at an elevated temperature (τ=1.0) to encourage diversity, which are then scored by in silico property predictors (Protein-Sol, TemBERTure, ESM-2 pseudo-likelihood) to construct pairwise preference data. At evaluation, sequences are scored at a low temperature (0.1) and their 3D structures are predicted by ESMFold or AlphaFold2 to compute designability metrics.

## Method

ProtAlign fine-tunes a pretrained inverse folding model (ProteinMPNN) using a semi-online Direct Preference Optimization strategy with a flexible preference margin. The key loss (Eq. 4) extends standard DPO by introducing an adaptive margin m_k(y_w, y_l) that sums weighted reward differences on all *other* properties, so that pairs where the preferred sequence is worse on an auxiliary objective receive a reduced margin, preventing one property from dominating optimization. Training proceeds iteratively: at each round, the current policy generates rollouts at high temperature, property predictors score them, and preference pairs are constructed (top-half vs. bottom-half with a score-gap threshold δ_k); the model is then updated offline on the new pairs before the next rollout round. For the order-agnostic ProteinMPNN architecture, sequence probabilities are estimated by averaging over K shared random residue permutations for both the policy and reference model, reducing variance in the log-ratio term. Applied to ProteinMPNN on CATH 4.3, the resulting MoMPNN outperforms subset-trained baselines (SolubleMPNN, HyperMPNN) on both solubility and thermostability while preserving designability across crystal, de novo, and binder design benchmarks.

## Results

On the CATH 4.3 test set, MoMPNN[Sol+TM] achieves the best solubility score (0.884) while maintaining a TM-score of 0.738 (vs. ProteinMPNN's 0.740), and MoMPNN[Thermo+IG] achieves the best thermostability (0.963) with a TM-score of 0.734. On the de novo RFDiffusion benchmark, MoMPNN[Sol+IG+EP] surpasses even ProteinMPNN in structural consistency (TM 0.751 vs. 0.718) while also improving solubility (0.843 vs. 0.731). In the binder design task, MoMPNN[Sol+IG+EP] achieves slightly higher sequence and backbone success rates than ProteinMPNN with substantially better evolutionary perplexity and solubility, while matching SolubleMPNN on designability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoMPNN decisively outperforms the property-specific subset-trained baselines: it beats SolubleMPNN on solubility (0.884 vs. 0.794) while matching its designability (TM 0.738 vs. 0.733), and beats HyperMPNN on thermostability (0.963 vs. 0.929) while recovering the designability loss HyperMPNN suffers (TM 0.734 vs. 0.706). Against the guidance-based methods, MoMPNN[Sol+TM] improves solubility by 0.122 over Guidance[Sol] (0.884 vs. 0.762) with comparable TM-score, and MoMPNN[Thermo+IG] improves thermostability by 0.062 over Guidance[Thermo] (0.963 vs. 0.901). ProteinDPO, which optimizes only a single stability objective, degrades severely on all metrics (TM drops to 0.667, EP worsens to 10.50), highlighting the instability of single-objective DPO for protein design. On the de novo benchmark, MoMPNN variants surpass ProteinMPNN on both designability (TM 0.751 vs. 0.718) and developability, whereas ESM-IF and InstructPLM collapse (TM 0.461 and 0.134 respectively). In the binder design task, MoMPNN[Sol+IG+EP] achieves slightly higher success rates than ProteinMPNN and matches SolubleMPNN on designability while delivering better evolutionary perplexity and solubility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) framework from LLM alignment to protein inverse folding, extending the single-objective DPO variants already applied to proteins (ProteinDPO for stability, ResiDPO for designability, InstructPLM-DPO for structure matching) to a multi-objective setting with a flexible adaptive preference margin derived from MODPO (Zhou et al., 2023a). The semi-online training paradigm, which decouples rollout generation from parameter updates, is borrowed from recent LLM alignment work (Lanchantin et al., 2025) and repurposed here to avoid running property predictors during training. The key departure from prior protein DPO work is the explicit handling of competing objectives (e.g., solubility vs. thermostability vs. designability) through the margin mechanism, rather than optimizing a single scalarized reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProtAlign provides a general, plug-and-play framework for aligning any pretrained inverse folding model with arbitrary developability properties, removing the need for task-specific retraining on curated subsets or careful inference-time hyperparameter tuning. The introduction of de novo backbone and binder design benchmarks, combined with developability metrics in inverse folding evaluation, offers a more realistic assessment pipeline than the standard CATH recovery benchmark. Key limitations include the absence of wet-lab validation and the focus on monomer properties; extending the framework to complex-specific objectives (e.g., binding affinity) and validating predictions experimentally are natural next steps that would strengthen the practical impact.
