## Problem

Existing Concept Bottleneck Generative Models (CBGMs) rely on deterministic mappings to soft concept probabilities, making them vulnerable to concept leakage and restricting steerability to interventions on existing outputs. Because they do not model a generative process over the concept space, direct generation from a specified set of concepts is not supported, and soft representations allow the model to exploit information shortcuts rather than genuinely encoding concept semantics.

## Input / Output

The VHCB layer is inserted post-hoc between the mapping network and synthesis network of a pretrained StyleGAN2 (or at the U-Net bottleneck of a DDPM), consuming the pre-bottleneck latent embedding w and producing a post-concept embedding ŵ that feeds the frozen generator to yield 256×256 RGB images. For steerable generation, the user specifies a binary concept configuration c ∈ {0,1}^K (e.g., 40 CelebA-HQ attributes or 10 CUB bird features) which is sampled alongside a 5-bit unsupervised side channel to produce the output. For interventions, a generated image x and a target concept bit are provided, and the model returns a modified image with that concept toggled while preserving all others. Concept supervision during training comes from ResNet18 classifiers or CLIP zero-shot classifiers applied to synthetic images from the pretrained generator.

## Method

The VHCB extends the Coded Discrete Variational Autoencoder (Coded DVAE) with two binary latent vectors: a K-bit concept vector c aligned to predefined hard concepts and a compact 5-bit unsupervised side channel s. Each binary latent is protected by a uniform repetition Error-Correcting Code that increases Hamming distance, enabling robust inference via soft majority voting over codeword groups. A smoothing transformation using overlapping exponential distributions converts the discrete codewords into differentiable continuous variables z ∈ [0,1]^{K'+L'} that feed a 4-layer MLP decoder reconstructing w. Training optimizes a reconstruction loss on w, a symmetric KL (Jeffreys) divergence aligning the concept posterior q(c|w) with classifier-derived p(y|x), a KL regularizer on the side channel, and an image-level MSE term; no explicit intervention losses are needed. The probabilistic formulation allows direct sampling of (c, s) from the latent space to generate images conditioned on arbitrary concept configurations, while interventions reduce to toggling individual bits in c, making manipulation transparent and leakage-free compared to soft-concept baselines.

## Results

On StyleGAN2 pretrained on CelebA-HQ with ResNet18 pseudo-labels, VHCB achieves 0.855 concept inference accuracy and 0.927 disentanglement accuracy (vs. 0.857 and 0.901 for CB-AE). For single-concept interventions on the low-correlation set, VHCB reaches 0.769 target accuracy (activation) and 0.765 (deactivation), compared to 0.420 and 0.554 for CB-AE — an average ~46% improvement in target accuracy with only a ~7% drop in non-target accuracy. Direct generation from specified concept configurations yields 0.814 target accuracy (low-correlation set, pattern-sampled) with VHCB, a capability entirely absent in the deterministic CB-AE baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is CB-AE (Kulkarni et al., CVPR 2025), a deterministic post-hoc concept bottleneck autoencoder. VHCB consistently outperforms CB-AE on target intervention accuracy across all settings: on the low-correlation set with ResNet18, target accuracy rises from 0.420 to 0.769 (activation) and from 0.554 to 0.765 (deactivation); with CLIP, from 0.317 to 0.567 and 0.404 to 0.532. On the full 40-concept set, gains are smaller (e.g., 0.105→0.170 for activation with ResNet18) due to dataset imbalance and out-of-distribution configurations. CB-AE shows slightly higher non-target accuracy (e.g., 0.924 vs. 0.903), but the authors argue this reflects CB-AE's failure to actually modify the target concept rather than superior preservation. VHCB also uniquely supports direct generation from specified concept configurations (Table 2), achieving 0.873 target accuracy with pattern-sampled concepts on the full set — a capability CB-AE does not possess. On concept inference, both methods are nearly tied on hard accuracy (0.855 vs. 0.857) but VHCB leads on soft metrics (cosine similarity 0.804 vs. 0.763; TV 0.148 vs. 0.161) and disentanglement accuracy (0.927 vs. 0.901).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Coded DVAE (Martínez-García et al., 2025), a binary VAE that uses error-correcting codes for stable inference over discrete latent spaces, into the concept bottleneck generative model (CBGM) setting. It departs from the two existing CBGM approaches—CEM-based (Ismail et al., 2024) and CB-AE (Kulkarni et al., 2025)—which both rely on soft concept representations and deterministic mappings, by introducing the first probabilistic CBGM with hard binary concepts. The hard concept formulation draws on the broader CBM literature showing that binary representations mitigate concept leakage (Margeloiu et al., 2021; Havasi et al., 2022), while the probabilistic VAE structure enables a capability absent in prior CBGMs: direct sampling from specified concept configurations rather than only interventions on existing outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VHCB layer provides a practical post-hoc module that can be inserted into pretrained StyleGAN2 or DDPM models to enable interpretable, steerable generation without retraining the base generator, and the systematic evaluation framework it introduces quantifies how dataset biases and inter-concept correlations fundamentally limit steerability—insights that apply broadly to any CBGM. A key open question is how to model statistical dependencies between concepts in the latent space, since the current independence assumption causes out-of-distribution failures for correlated or mutually exclusive concept pairs. The paper also leaves open whether the benefits of hard concepts and compact binary side channels generalize to higher-dimensional or non-binary concept spaces, and whether fine-tuning the base generator to correct spurious biases can further improve steerability.
