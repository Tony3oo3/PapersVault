## Problem

Standard predictive coding networks require dense, floating-point message passing during iterative settling to converge, which negates the communication efficiency gains that spiking neural networks offer on event-driven neuromorphic hardware. While PC provides biologically plausible local learning rules, its continuous-valued communication is incompatible with the sparse, discrete spike-based operation of platforms like Intel Loihi 2. This paper addresses the gap by reformulating PC into a fully spike-native algorithm.

## Input / Output

The method consumes grayscale 28×28 images (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST) or color 32×32 images (CIFAR-10) and outputs a 10-class label. During training, the input layer is clamped to the image and the output layer is clamped to the ground-truth label, after which the network iteratively relaxes via bidirectional spike exchange. All inter-layer communication is restricted to ternary spikes (−1, 0, +1), with a spike emitted only when a neuron's state deviation exceeds an adaptive threshold, yielding extreme communication sparsity (0.08–1.9 bits per neuron vs. 32 for backpropagation).

## Method

DiffPC replaces the dense floating-point messages of standard PC with sparse ternary spikes by maintaining, for each neuron, a target state x_T (the continuous PC target) and an actual state x_A (the discrete spiking state). Spikes are generated only when |x_T − x_A| exceeds an adaptive threshold T_θ(t), and a 'spiking ReLU' mask prevents x_A from going negative. The key scheduling mechanism is a cyclic threshold T_θ(t) = 2^m / 2^(t mod n) that halves the step size each timestep within a cycle, provably driving the spiking state exponentially close to the continuous target within one cycle (Theorem 4.1). A cyclic decay variant further scales the threshold over the full iteration to reduce timesteps as the network converges. Weight updates follow the standard local Hebbian rule ΔW ∝ e_T · φ(x_T), computed from the accumulated spike-encoded error, making the entire training loop deployable on Loihi 2's instruction set.

## Results

On MNIST, DiffPC-L (784-1024-512-10 FC) achieves 99.3% test accuracy, matching the backpropagation baseline and exceeding all other spiking and predictive-coding baselines. On Fashion-MNIST, DiffPC-M reaches 89.6%, and on CIFAR-10, DiffPC-Long (CNN) achieves 65.6%, surpassing the backpropagation baseline (63.5%) and standard PC (65.3%). The headline efficiency gain is a >100× reduction in transmitted bits per neuron during error propagation: DiffPC-L uses 0.18 bits/neuron versus 32 bits (backprop) and 960 bits (standard PC) on MNIST, and 1.9 bits/neuron versus 960 bits on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MNIST, DiffPC-L (99.3%) ties the backpropagation baseline and the CNN variant of FastSNN, while outperforming STiDi-BP (99.2%), SRC-RNN (98.4%), PC-SE (98.3%), SSTDP (98.1%), PC-SNN (98.1%), and the FC FastSNN (97.9%). On Fashion-MNIST, DiffPC-M (89.6%) beats SRC-RNN (88.5%) and the FC FastSNN (89.1%) but falls short of the CNN FastSNN (90.6%). On CIFAR-10, DiffPC-Long (65.6%) surpasses both the backpropagation baseline (63.5%) and PC-SE (65.3%), while DiffPC-Efficient (63.3%) is slightly below backprop. The most striking comparison is communication cost: DiffPC transmits 0.18 bits/neuron (MNIST) and 1.9 bits/neuron (CIFAR-10) during error propagation, versus 32 bits for backpropagation and 960 bits for standard PC—a reduction of over two orders of magnitude. All spiking baselines (PC-SNN, SSTDP, STiDi-BP) still rely on dense floating-point messages during training, whereas DiffPC is fully spike-native.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffPC sits at the intersection of predictive coding (PC) and spiking neural networks, building directly on the PC-SNN line of work (Lan et al. 2022; Ororbia 2023; Lee et al. 2024) that sought to make PC training compatible with SNNs. However, prior PC-SNN methods either retained dense floating-point communication during training (Lan et al.) or relied on a hybrid pipeline with a separate non-spiking linear classifier for final readout (Ororbia; Lee et al.), meaning the reported accuracies did not reflect an end-to-end spiking system. DiffPC departs from both by making the entire training loop—state updates, error propagation, and weight updates—operate exclusively through sparse ternary spikes with adaptive threshold scheduling, drawing conceptually on event-driven gradient-by-spikes approaches (Bohte et al. 2000; Cai et al. 2024) but applying them specifically to the bidirectional error flow of predictive coding rather than to backpropagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-orders-of-magnitude reduction in transmitted bits (0.08–1.9 bits/neuron vs. 32 for backprop or 960 for standard PC) directly addresses the data-movement bottleneck that limits on-chip training on neuromorphic platforms like Intel Loihi 2, making DiffPC a practically relevant step toward fully local, event-driven learning hardware. The fact that this efficiency comes without sacrificing accuracy—and even slightly exceeds backpropagation on CIFAR-10—is encouraging, though the evidence is confined to small-scale benchmarks (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10) and relatively shallow networks. Key open questions include whether the spike-communication window scales gracefully to deep architectures (e.g., 100+ layer ResNets as explored by μ-PC), whether temporal-correlation warm-starting can further reduce inference iterations, and whether the algorithm performs as predicted under real Loihi 2 hardware constraints rather than simulation.
