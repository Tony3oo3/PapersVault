## Problem

Current VLM-based autonomous driving systems suffer from two critical gaps: they either bypass chain-of-thought reasoning entirely (outputting trajectories directly) or treat perception, prediction, and planning as independent modules without synergistic interaction. Moreover, existing GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning optimizes only planning metrics, leaving perception and prediction without direct reward supervision, which undermines the reliability and interpretability of final driving decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261063/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-camera video clips (6 front-view frames at 448×252 for nuScenes; 4 frames from front, front-left, and front-right cameras at 672×168 for NAVSIM) along with ego-vehicle state (speed only on nuScenes; full vx, vy, ax, ay on NAVSIM), a driving command, and a text prompt. It produces a structured P³-CoT output where each of the three modules (perception, prediction, planning) emits a thinking trace followed by a concrete answer: key-object bounding boxes, per-object future action labels (stop/straight/left/right), and 6 ego-vehicle trajectory waypoints over 3 seconds. Two inference modes are supported—detailed thinking (full reasoning per module) and fast thinking (answers only)—to trade off latency against performance.

## Method

AutoDrive-P³ introduces a unified P³-CoT dataset (25K nuScenes + 115K NAVSIM frames) where each sample contains a single coherent reasoning chain that explicitly links perception→prediction→planning, with key-object annotations and Qwen2.5-VL-72B-synthesized CoT text verified by human annotators. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning on P³-CoT to cold-start the Qwen2.5-VL-3B base model into the driving domain and the structured output format, and (2) P³-GRPO, a hierarchical reinforcement fine-tuning algorithm that extends standard GRPO with a four-component reward (format compliance, perception IoU·F1, prediction IoU-weighted behavior accuracy, and planning L2 distance) weighted 1:2:2:5. The key design insight is that the prediction reward is multiplicatively coupled to the perception reward, and the planning reward sits at the apex, so the model cannot earn high planning scores without first producing accurate detections and correct behavior forecasts—encoding the causal dependency perception→prediction→planning directly into the optimization signal. Eight P³-CoT samples are generated per scenario during RL, and a KL penalty (β=0.01) prevents policy collapse.

## Results

On NAVSIMv1 (closed-loop), AutoDrive-P³ achieves a state-of-the-art PDMS of 90.6 (detailed mode) and 90.2 (fast mode), surpassing WoTE (88.3) and DiffusionDrive (88.1) by over 2 points. On NAVSIMv2, it reaches 89.9 EPDMS (human_penalty_filter=True), outperforming DiffusionDrive (88.2) and WoTE (87.7). On nuScenes (open-loop), it matches the best L2 displacement (0.33 m avg, tied with OmniDrive and OpenDriveVLA) while reducing average collision rate to 0.06%, roughly 40% lower than the next best methods (0.10–0.11%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NAVSIMv1, AutoDrive-P³ (90.6 PDMS) outperforms all prior vision-only and LiDAR-fusion baselines, including WoTE (88.3), DiffusionDrive (88.1), and Hydra-MDP (86.5), with the largest gap (+2.3) over WoTE driven by superior Ego Progress (84.8 vs 81.9) and No Collision (99.1 vs 98.5). On NAVSIMv2, it leads DiffusionDrive (89.9 vs 88.2 EPDMS-True) and WoTE (89.9 vs 87.7), again with the best EP (88.0) and TTC (98.7). On nuScenes, it ties OmniDrive and OpenDriveVLA on average L2 (0.33 m) but achieves a substantially lower collision rate (0.06% vs 0.10–0.11%), and notably uses a smaller backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-3B vs LLaVA-7B) and far less training data (~20k vs ~1M samples for OpenDriveVLA). Against non-autoregressive baselines (UniAD, VAD, ST-P3), the collision advantage is even more pronounced (0.06% vs 0.12–0.71%). The fast-thinking mode retains 90.2 PDMS on NAVSIMv1 at near-real-time 1 Hz inference, showing minimal performance loss for a significant speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO algorithm (Shao et al., 2024) from LLM/VLM reasoning to autonomous driving by decomposing the reward into perception, prediction, and planning components, departing from prior driving-specific GRPO applications (AutoVLA, AutoDrive-R², AlphaDrive) that optimize only planning metrics. It also builds on the VLM-based driving paradigm (DriveVLM, OmniDrive, OpenDriveVLA) but fundamentally restructures their fragmented QA-style outputs into a unified Perception-Prediction-Planning Chain-of-Thought (P³-CoT) format, where each stage's output explicitly conditions the next. The P³-CoT dataset and the hierarchical multi-module reward design are the core departures from prior work, which either bypasses CoT reasoning entirely or treats the three driving subtasks as independent queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that joint reinforcement supervision across perception, prediction, and planning (rather than planning-only) yields a 40% collision rate reduction on nuScenes and SOTA PDMS/EPDMS on NAVSIMv1/v2, validating that intermediate-stage accuracy is a genuine prerequisite for reliable planning rather than a byproduct. The dual thinking modes (detailed vs. fast) and near-real-time 1 Hz inference on a 3B model make the approach practically deployable, addressing a key gap in VLM-based driving systems. However, the authors acknowledge hallucination during reasoning and the limitation of offline RL without real-world interaction, leaving open whether the multi-module GRPO framework transfers to online, reactive driving settings and whether it scales to larger VLMs.
