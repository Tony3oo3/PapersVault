## Problem

Existing cascade serving systems for LLMs fail to jointly address three coupled challenges: heterogeneous resource demands across model sizes, workload heterogeneity in input/output lengths and arrival rates, and the mutual dependency between system deployment (resource allocation, parallelism) and request routing. No prior framework co-optimizes these dimensions, leading to unbalanced loads, suboptimal parallelism choices, and degraded end-to-end latency and throughput.

## Input / Output

The system consumes incoming LLM text requests along with their workload characteristics (input/output token length distributions and arrival rates per model stage), a total GPU budget N, and a user-specified minimum quality score q_min. It produces a cascade routing strategy parameterized by thresholds H = {h_1, ..., h_{C-1}} that determine when a GPT-4o judge (scoring 0–100) escalates a request to the next model, plus a deployment plan specifying per-model GPU counts f_i and per-replica parallelism degrees (DP, TP, PP). The routing and deployment are solved iteratively and can be re-scheduled online when workload characteristics shift.

## Method

CASCADIA employs a bi-level optimization that alternates between a deployment solver and a routing solver until convergence. The deployment solver formulates resource allocation as a mixed-integer linear program with binary variables x_{i,f} assigning GPU counts to each model type, minimizing the maximum p95 latency across all stages; latency values are precomputed via a simulator that enumerates all feasible (DP, TP, PP) combinations for each allocation. The routing solver applies a Chebyshev-guided penalty objective J(θ) = L(θ) + μ·max{0, (q_min − Q(θ))/(z_1* − z_2*)}, where z_1* and z_2* are utopia/nadir quality anchors, to minimize latency while enforcing the quality constraint. The two levels are coupled because routing determines the workload distribution seen by each model (driving the MILP), while the resulting deployment determines the latency landscape that the routing objective minimizes over. A re-scheduling mechanism periodically re-runs the bi-level loop when online workload statistics shift, using rolling updates to avoid service interruption.

## Results

On MT-Bench-derived workload traces with DeepSeek and Llama model cascades, CASCADIA achieves up to 4× (2.3× on average) tighter latency SLOs and up to 5× (2.4× on average) higher throughput compared to stand-alone LLM serving, while maintaining target answer quality. Against the CascadeServe baseline, CASCADIA achieves up to 2.5× (1.7× on average) lower latency deadlines and up to 3.3× (1.7× on average) higher throughput. Scheduling completes within 20 s on a 32-GPU cluster and under 1 minute on 80 GPUs, with near-optimal performance (2–6% gap vs. exhaustive search).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASCADIA is compared against three baselines. Against stand-alone LLMs served by SGLang (DeepSeek-671B or DeepSeek-dist-70B depending on quality target), CASCADIA achieves up to 4× tighter SLO scales and up to 5× higher throughput (e.g., on Trace 3 at quality 85, standalone DeepSeek-671B requires 11.88 SLO scales vs. CASCADIA's 3.75). Against CascadeServe, the state-of-the-art cascade serving system, CASCADIA achieves up to 2.5× lower latency deadlines and up to 3.3× higher throughput (e.g., on Trace 1 at quality 90, CascadeServe needs 17.3 SLO scales vs. CASCADIA's 11.73), with the gap attributed to CascadeServe's failure to account for LLM-specific workload characteristics in parallelism and routing. Against Sarathi-Serve (a chunked-prefill serving system), CASCADIA achieves 1.95× higher throughput (1.64× average) across three traces. CASCADIA also outperforms RouteLLM by 21.3% lower SLO scale and 18.8% higher throughput, with the advantage attributed to system-algorithm co-design rather than routing alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cascade model serving paradigm established by FrugalGPT, AutoMix, and CascadeServe by introducing a bi-level co-optimization of system deployment (GPU allocation and parallelism strategy via MILP) and request routing (via Chebyshev-guided penalty optimization). Prior cascade systems either fix the deployment and adapt routing to real-time load (CascadeServe) or focus solely on routing heuristics (FrugalGPT, AutoMix), neglecting LLM-specific workload heterogeneity (compute-bound prefill vs. memory-bound decoding) and the feedback loop between routing decisions and optimal resource allocation. The Chebyshev-guided multi-objective optimization framework from Steuer & Choo (1983) is repurposed here to handle the latency-quality trade-off under a user-specified quality constraint, while the MILP formulation for parallelism-aware resource allocation draws on AlpaServe-style statistical multiplexing adapted to cascade settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 4× tighter SLOs and 5× higher throughput over single-model baselines, along with 20–61% cost reductions, make cascade serving a practically viable strategy for production LLM deployments where both latency and quality SLAs matter. The core insight—that deployment and routing must be co-optimized rather than treated independently—generalizes beyond cascades to any multi-model serving architecture. Open questions include scalability to very large clusters (beyond 80 GPUs), handling multi-tenant or multi-SLA scenarios, robustness to model updates mid-cascade, and whether the simulator-based latency estimation (2–7% error) remains sufficient as hardware heterogeneity increases. The reliance on an LLM-as-a-Judge (GPT-4o) for routing decisions, while shown to be low-overhead, introduces a dependency on a proprietary service that may not be available in all deployment settings.
