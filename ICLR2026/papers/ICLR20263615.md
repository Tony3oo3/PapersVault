## Problem

Low-precision quantization of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLMs is severely degraded by activation outliers that expand the dynamic range and cause large quantization errors, particularly at 4-bit. Existing rotation-based smoothing methods (QuaRot, SpinQuant) reduce but do not eliminate these errors, and uniform weight quantization mismatches the non-uniform weight distributions. Moreover, prior quantization approaches do not account for the MoE-specific routing mechanism, where quantization-induced changes in router logits can alter token-expert assignments and compound accuracy loss.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained MoE LLM (e.g., Phi-mini-MoE-Instruct, Qwen3-30B-A3B, DeepSeek-V2-Lite, Mixtral 8x7B) and a small calibration set (1,024 WikiText2 samples for AOS, 512 for ACCF) and produces a fully quantized model with 4-bit linearly quantized activations and 16-centroid clustered weights, deployable via a LUT-based GEMM kernel. All transformations (rotation matrices, permutation matrices) are folded into the weight parameters offline, so inference proceeds identically to a standard MoE with no additional runtime computation. The framework supports both Embedding-wise (full-vector) and Block-wise (g=1024) quantization granularities.

## Method

CodeQuant is a three-stage post-training pipeline. Stage 1 (AOS) parameterizes a rotation matrix R via the Cayley transform of a learnable skew-symmetric matrix and fine-tunes it to minimize the L2 quantization error of rotated activations, explicitly pushing outlier magnitude into the weight space. Stage 2 (ACCF) performs row-wise K-means clustering on the rotated weights with two key innovations: centroids are refined by gradient descent on the output-difference objective (rather than fixed after K-means), and the assignment matrix is updated analytically via a diagonal-approximation of the gradient, yielding a closed-form nearest-centroid rule that aligns with the output-level loss. For MoE FFN layers, the objective additionally includes a KL divergence term on router logits to preserve the original token-expert routing pattern. Stage 3 (POG, optional for Block-wise) reorders weight columns by pairing high-variance and low-variance sub-groups to reduce within-cluster variance before ACCF. A dedicated LUT-based GEMM kernel precomputes 16×16 lookup tables in shared memory, replacing multiply-accumulate operations with table lookups for up to 4.15× speedup on CPU.

## Results

Under A4W4 Embedding-wise quantization, CodeQuant achieves the best perplexity and average accuracy across all four MoE models. On Mixtral 8x7B, CodeQuant reduces WikiText2 perplexity to 4.65 (vs. 16.79 for QuaRot and 77.32 for SmoothQuant) and reaches 0.725 average accuracy (vs. 0.497 for QuaRot). On Qwen3-30B-A3B, CodeQuant cuts C4 perplexity from 24.27 (QuaRot) to 15.75 and lifts average accuracy from 0.581 to 0.694. On the math-reasoning benchmark GSM8K (8-shot), CodeQuant scores 0.867 on Qwen3-30B-A3B versus 0.508 for QuaRot, a 35.9% relative improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CodeQuant consistently outperforms all six baselines across the four MoE models. Against QuaRot (the strongest rotation-based baseline), CodeQuant reduces WikiText2 perplexity by 12.14 on Mixtral 8x7B (4.65 vs. 16.79) and by 5.73 on Qwen3-30B-A3B (10.31 vs. 16.04), while improving average accuracy by 22.8% and 11.3% respectively. Against SmoothQuant and SqueezeLLM, CodeQuant's advantage is even more dramatic, as both methods suffer catastrophic perplexity degradation (often exceeding 1000) under A4W4 Embedding-wise settings. Against the stronger rotation-based baselines DuQuant and SpinQuant (Table 2, with online Hadamard transforms), CodeQuant_had still wins on both DeepSeek-V2-Lite (0.629 vs. 0.624 and 0.610 avg accuracy) and Qwen3-30B-A3B (0.653 vs. 0.637 and 0.590). On CPU latency (Table 9), CodeQuant's T-MAC LUT kernel achieves up to 4.15× speedup over the BF16 Llama.cpp baseline and consistently beats the A8W4 Llama.cpp quantized baseline (e.g., 14.2 s vs. 17.1 s on DeepSeek-V2-Lite).

## Relation to Prior Work

CodeQuant unifies three previously separate lines of work: rotation-based activation smoothing (QuaRot, SpinQuant, DuQuant), K-means weight clustering (SqueezeLLM), and LUT-based hardware kernels (T-MAC, LUT-GEMM). Its key departure is treating these as a single pipeline rather than independent choices—learnable Cayley-transform rotations handle activations while adaptive row-wise clustering with gradient-based centroid fine-tuning handles weights, and a MoE-specific KL-divergence objective preserves router behavior. The Permutation-Invariant Outlier Grouping (POG) step is a novel preprocessing that reorders weight columns to improve clusterability, distinct from prior permutation methods designed for uniform quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoE architectures are becoming the dominant paradigm for scaling LLMs, yet low-precision deployment remains underexplored; CodeQuant provides a practical, end-to-end recipe that consistently outperforms rotation-only and clustering-only baselines across four MoE models. However, the GPU speedup is simulated via Accel-Sim rather than measured on real hardware, and the headline 4.15× speedup is on CPU, limiting the practical impact claim. Preprocessing costs (up to 240 minutes for Mixtral 8×7B) and the reliance on a fixed calibration set (WikiText2) are open concerns for production adoption. Whether the LUT kernel design translates to next-generation hardware with native LUT support (e.g., Cerebras CS-3, Apple Neural Engine) remains to be validated.
