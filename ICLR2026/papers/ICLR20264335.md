## Problem

Despite Lean's growing adoption for formal mathematics, no end-to-end hammer (automated proof tool) existed for it, and existing Lean premise selectors were either non-neural, not directly callable in Lean, or unable to incorporate user-defined local lemmas. This paper addresses the gap by building the first domain-general hammer for Lean, with a premise selector specifically designed for hammer integration in dependent type theory rather than for next-tactic generation.

## Input / Output

The system takes a Lean proof state (goal) and the set of accessible premises in the current environment—comprising imported library lemmas, definitions from earlier in the file, and user-defined local facts—as input, and produces a ranked list of k relevant premises (k=16–32) that feed into the hammer pipeline to yield a formal Lean proof. Premises are represented via a normalized serialization (fully qualified names, no notation pretty-printing, docstring + kind + name + arguments + type) to ensure representation is independent of open namespaces and surface syntax. The system dynamically embeds new user-defined premises at runtime via a server-side FAISS index, so it adapts to arbitrary project contexts without retraining.

## Method

LEANPREMISE uses an encoder-only transformer (MiniLM-L6/L12 or DistilRoBERTa-base) trained with a masked contrastive InfoNCE loss that handles shared in-batch positives by masking them from the negative set, and samples explicit negative premises to address the long-tail of unused library facts. The key differentiator is a hammer-aware data extraction pipeline that collects (state, premise) pairs from both term-style and tactic-style proofs, including implicit premises invoked by simp/rw automation and definitional equalities not visible in final proof terms—yielding 5.8M training pairs from Mathlib. The full LEANHAMMER pipeline orchestrates Aesop (best-first proof search with premise-application rules), Lean-auto (DTT-to-HOL translation), Zipperposition (external ATP), and Duper (proof reconstruction), with premises fed to both Aesop and the external prover path. Retrieval uses cosine similarity over FAISS-indexed embeddings with a 479-theorem blacklist of trivially relevant but unhelpful logic lemmas, and the system supports multiple pipeline variants (aesop, auto, aesop+auto, full, cumul) at different computational costs.

## Results

On Mathlib-test, LEANPREMISE (large, 82M params) achieves 72.7% recall@32 and 30.1% proof rate in the full pipeline setting, with 33.3% in the cumulative (cumul) setting. Combining LEANPREMISE with the symbolic MePo selector (cumulative ∪ MePo) reaches 39.6% cumul proof rate, approaching the 43.0% ground-truth upper bound. On out-of-Mathlib miniCTX-v2-test, LEANPREMISE (large) achieves 20.7% proof rate with the full pipeline, reaching 79.4% of the ground-truth ceiling (26.1%), demonstrating strong domain generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEANPREMISE (large) substantially outperforms all three baselines on both recall and proof rate. Against MePo (symbolic), LEANPREMISE achieves 72.7% recall@32 versus 42.1% (73% relative improvement) and 33.3% cumul proof rate versus 27.5% (21% relative improvement). Against ReProver (218M params, LM-based), LEANPREMISE (82M params) proves 150% more theorems in the full setting (30.1% vs. 12.0%) and 50% more in cumul (33.3% vs. 22.3%), despite being smaller. Against the random forest baseline, LEANPREMISE more than triples recall@32 (72.7% vs. 22.3%) and nearly doubles cumul proof rate (33.3% vs. 19.1%). The authors attribute the gap over ReProver to hammer-aware data extraction (term-style proofs, implicit premises, normalized signatures) and the use of a masked contrastive loss rather than ReProver's ℓ² loss. Notably, the union of LEANPREMISE and MePo (39.6% cumul) exceeds either method alone, indicating complementary strengths between neural and symbolic premise selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hammer paradigm established by Isabelle's Sledgehammer and CoqHammer to the Lean proof assistant, which had previously lacked any hammer despite its growing adoption by mathematicians. For premise selection specifically, it departs from prior neural approaches (ReProver, Lean Copilot, Magnushammer) that were designed for tactic generation or direct proof reconstruction, instead formulating a hammer-aware contrastive learning objective with data extraction that captures implicit premises from automation and term-style proofs. The retrieval architecture builds on standard encoder-based dense retrieval (MiniLM/DistilRoBERTa with InfoNCE-style loss) and FAISS indexing, while the symbolic pipeline composes existing components—Aesop for proof search, Lean-auto for DTT-to-HOL translation, Zipperposition as the external prover, and Duper for proof reconstruction—into a unified end-to-end system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Lean and Mathlib become central infrastructure for formal mathematics (Liquid Tensor Experiment, Sphere Eversion, PFR), the absence of a practical hammer was a significant gap; this work provides the first domain-general solution that is directly callable as a Lean tactic with sub-10-second latency, making automated reasoning accessible to working mathematicians without complex setup. The out-of-Mathlib generalization (79.4% of ground-truth performance on miniCTX-v2) and dynamic incorporation of user-defined premises suggest the approach scales to new libraries and local contexts. Key limitations remain: the pipeline cannot handle induction, arithmetic, or DTT features that resist HOL translation, and the gap to ground-truth premises (33.3% vs. 43.0% proof rate) indicates substantial room for improvement in both retrieval recall and external prover capability.
