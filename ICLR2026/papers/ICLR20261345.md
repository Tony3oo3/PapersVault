## Problem

LLM-based planners for embodied agents generate fluent but semantically ungrounded plans that can be physically unsafe, yet existing safety paradigms either rely on the same stochastic LLM for self-verification (a self-referential loop with no deterministic guarantee) or act as passive gatekeepers that reject unsafe plans without explaining why, causing agents to abandon solvable tasks. The paper identifies a further evaluation blind spot: current safety benchmarks disproportionately cover simple physical hazards while neglecting complex semantic risks like chemical cross-contamination and food safety.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a natural-language user command and one or more RGB scene images from the AI2-THOR simulator. The scene images are processed through a three-stage VLM-Cascade pipeline (global VLM detection, per-object attribute refinement, spatial relation refinement) to produce a Rich Semantic Scene Graph, which is then deterministically translated into OWL ABox assertions. The output is either a verified safe action plan (a sequence of typed actions) or an explicit TASK_REJECTED signal; when the verifier encounters an UNKNOWN state (e.g., an object's material is undetermined), the system emits a clarification question to the user before resuming the loop.

## Method

VIRF is a neuro-symbolic architecture built on three pillars. First, a Traceable Axiom Synthesis (TAS) workflow uses RAG retrieval over safety documents plus an LLM synthesizer and human arbiter to author a comprehensive OWL 2 DL safety ontology (TBox) grounded in BFO, with every axiom traceable to its source text. Second, a VLM-Cascade perception pipeline produces a high-fidelity ABox with fine-grained safety attributes (isHot, isConductive, isFlammable) that standard detectors miss. Third, the core Tutor-Apprentice loop: an LLM planner proposes a plan, the Pellet reasoner checks it against the full TBox+ABox under the Open-World Assumption, and if the plan is UNSAFE, the reasoner's proof trace is converted into a structured causal diagnostic report (Action → Axiom → Violation chain) that is fed back to the LLM as pedagogical feedback, enabling iterative plan repair rather than simple rejection. The compositional ontology design (safety rules defined over abstract material/state classes rather than specific objects) provides zero-shot generalization to unseen objects, and parallel multi-step verification keeps the loop practical for pre-execution use.

## Results

On SafeAgentBench, VIRF achieves a perfect 0% Hazardous Action Rate (HAR) while attaining the highest Goal-Condition Rate (GCR) of 77.3% among all baselines, with only 1.1 average refinement iterations and 25.5 s verification latency. The closest competitor, Thinker+Diagnostic, also reaches 0% HAR but with a slightly lower GCR of 76.8%. The VIRF-Reject ablation (simple rejection without causal explanation) matches the 0% HAR but drops GCR to 63.4% and inflates FNR to 33.0%, confirming that pedagogical feedback is essential for task completion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIRF dominates all baselines on the safety metric (HAR), achieving 0% hazardous actions versus 11.9% for Impulsive, 9.8% for Thinker (CoT), and 7.6% for the SAFER-like Committee baseline (Khan et al., 2025). On task efficacy (GCR), VIRF's 77.3% surpasses the SAFER-like Committee (57.3%) by 20 points, Impulsive+Rules (70.5%) by 6.8 points, and Thinker+Rules (67.0%) by 10.3 points. The nearest competitor, Thinker+Diagnostic (which receives the same causal diagnostic reports but without the full iterative verification loop), reaches near-parity at 76.8% GCR and 0% HAR, validating the precision of VIRF's Logic Tutor feedback. The VIRF-Reject ablation demonstrates that replacing pedagogical feedback with simple rejection causes a 13.9-point GCR drop (63.4% vs. 77.3%) and a 33.0% FNR, confirming that causal explanations are critical for the planner to recover from safety violations rather than abandoning solvable tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIRF positions itself at the intersection of neuro-symbolic AI and embodied safety, departing from two dominant paradigms: introspective LLM self-refinement (Chain-of-Thought, multi-agent debate) which it argues is self-referential and cannot provide verifiable guarantees, and tool-augmented corrective verifiers (VeriPlan, SAFER) which it recharacterizes as insufficiently pedagogical. Its knowledge core is grounded in BFO (Arp et al., 2015) and its design-time knowledge acquisition pipeline repurposes the RAG paradigm (Lewis et al., 2020) into a human-in-the-loop axiom synthesis workflow. The architecture explicitly rejects PDDL's Closed-World Assumption in favor of OWL 2 DL's Open-World Assumption, and frames the LLM-verifier interaction through Kahneman's Dual Process Theory, distinguishing itself from end-to-end VLA models by insisting on a separable, formally verifiable symbolic layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of systematic evaluation blind spots in SafeAgentBench (food safety and chemical hazards entirely absent) is a useful empirical contribution that should influence how the community designs safety benchmarks. The 'Paradox of Knowledge' finding—that an impulsive model with explicit rules outperforms a Chain-of-Thought model with the same rules—challenges the prevailing assumption that deliberative reasoning plus constraints monotonically improves safety compliance. Key limitations remain: the knowledge core is static (no learn-verify-write cycle), the evaluation relies on a manually-verified golden ABox that decouples reasoning from perception, and the entire framework is validated only in AI2-THOR kitchen scenarios, leaving the sim-to-real gap and continuous physical dynamics as open questions for physical deployment.
