## Problem

Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) is non-differentiable due to its hard minimum operator, preventing end-to-end gradient-based optimization. While soft-DTW provides a differentiable relaxation via a smooth soft-min, it lacks a principled probabilistic interpretation and offers no mechanism to control the skewness or correlation structure of the induced alignment distribution. This paper asks whether a perturbation-based framework can both explain soft-DTW probabilistically and generalize it to richer alignment models.

## Input / Output

The method takes two p-dimensional time series of lengths m and n, from which a local cost matrix C ∈ R^{m×n} is computed (typically via squared Euclidean distance). Beyond the scalar ns-DTW distance, it produces an expected alignment matrix E ∈ (0,1]^{m×n} and a transition probability tensor G ∈ (0,1]^{m×n×3} that assigns stochastic weights to the three admissible moves (→, ↓, ↘) at each grid cell. A key structural choice is the grouping scheme (g₁, g₂, or g₃) that partitions the three transition directions into a correlated pair and a singleton, combined with the GEV similarity parameter τ ∈ (0,1] that governs the degree of within-group correlation and alignment skewness.

## Method

Perturbed-DTW replaces the hard minimum over all valid alignment paths with the expected minimum of alignment costs minus a random perturbation γε, where ε is drawn from a distribution P. Under i.i.d. Gumbel(−c,1) noise, this expectation exactly recovers soft-DTW, yielding a probabilistic interpretation as the expected DTW under Gumbel-perturbed local costs. Extending the perturbation to the generalized extreme value (GEV) family introduces groupwise correlation among alignment costs, producing nested-soft-DTW (ns-DTW) whose DP recursion replaces the standard log-sum-exp with a nested operator: −γ log[(exp(−V_{i,j−1}/γτ) + exp(−V_{i−1,j}/γτ))^τ + exp(−V_{i−1,j−1}/γ)] + C_{i,j}. The three grouping schemes assign the correlated pair to different subsets of {→, ↓, ↘}, enabling tunable skewness toward vertical, horizontal, or diagonal transitions. The variational form reveals ns-DTW as an optimization regularized by nested Shannon entropy, which reduces to standard Shannon entropy when τ = 1 (recovering soft-DTW) and becomes increasingly selective as τ → 0.

## Results

On 47 UCR datasets, ns-DTW achieves lower barycenter DTW loss than the subgradient method on 100% of datasets, DBA on 97.87%, and soft-DTW on 74.47% (at optimal γ). For nearest-centroid classification, ns-DTW matches or exceeds subgradient on 93.02%, DBA on 88.37%, and soft-DTW on 86.05% of datasets. In k-means clustering, ns-DTW outperforms DBA on 100% and soft-DTW on 76.60% of datasets. The gains over soft-DTW are most pronounced at smaller γ values (e.g., 91.49% at γ=0.0001 vs. 36.17% at γ=0.1), indicating that the GEV perturbation's skewness control is most beneficial when the alignment is near-deterministic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the subgradient method (Schultz & Jain, 2018), ns-DTW dominates across all three tasks: 100% of datasets for barycenter averaging, 93.02% for NCC classification, and 100% for clustering. Against DBA (Petitjean et al., 2011), ns-DTW wins on 97.87% of averaging datasets, 88.37% of NCC classification datasets, and 100% of clustering datasets. Against soft-DTW (Cuturi & Blondel, 2017), the closest competitor, ns-DTW achieves superior results on 74.47% of averaging datasets, 86.05% of NCC classification datasets, and 76.60% of clustering datasets. The advantage over soft-DTW grows as γ decreases (from 36.17% at γ=0.1 to 91.49% at γ=0.0001 for averaging), suggesting that the tunable skewness introduced by the GEV perturbation parameter τ is most valuable when alignments are near-deterministic. Notably, ns-DTW's gains are dataset-dependent: it excels on datasets with asymmetric warping structures (e.g., Trace, Worms, SyntheticControl) but can underperform soft-DTW on datasets where symmetric alignment is optimal (e.g., CBF, ArrowHead for averaging).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions soft-DTW (Cuturi & Blondel, 2017) as a special case of a broader perturbed-optimizer framework (Berthet et al., 2020), showing that the soft-min operator in soft-DTW is exactly the expectation of DTW under i.i.d. Gumbel perturbations. It then extends this framework by replacing Gumbel noise with the generalized extreme value (GEV) distribution, which introduces groupwise correlation among alignment directions and yields nested-soft-DTW (ns-DTW). The dynamic programming recursion builds directly on the entropy-regularized DP formulation of Mensch & Blondel (2018), with the nested Shannon entropy replacing standard Shannon entropy as the regularization term. The connection to random utility theory and the nest logit model (Train, 2009) provides an econometric interpretation that was absent from prior soft-DTW work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic interpretation unifies soft-DTW within a general perturbation framework, offering a principled path to new differentiable DTW variants beyond the single temperature parameter γ. The tunable skewness via τ and grouping schemes gives practitioners a new degree of freedom for modeling asymmetric alignment structures, which is practically useful for barycenter computation and clustering. However, the paper acknowledges that the O(mn) DP recursion is a tractable approximation rather than an exact evaluation of the global GEV definition, and the empirical gains over soft-DTW are modest (74–86% of datasets). Open questions include extending the framework to divergence-based formulations, handling the approximation gap between local and global GEV perturbations, and whether the added hyperparameters (grouping scheme, τ) can be learned end-to-end rather than grid-searched.
