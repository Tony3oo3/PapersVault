## Problem

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for diffusion models is critically vulnerable to noisy preference data (mislabeled pairs and subjective 'also OK' annotations), yet the underlying reason for this fragility has not been formally identified. The paper proves that Diffusion-DPO's objective is equivalent to minimizing Forward KL divergence, whose mass-covering property amplifies the influence of noisy samples, and that existing noise-robust DPO variants were designed for LLMs rather than diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263427/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes pairwise preference data (winner image, loser image, text prompt) at square resolutions of 512×512 (SD1.5) or 1024×1024 (SDXL), with the preference signal encoded through per-timestep noise-prediction differences across the full diffusion trajectory. At inference, a single text prompt produces one generated image via 50 denoising steps with classifier-free guidance (scale 7.5 for SD1.5, 5 for SDXL). The training set comprises ~851K preference pairs from Pick-a-Pic V2 with 58,960 unique prompts.

## Method

The paper reformulates Diffusion-DPO by replacing the implicit Forward KL divergence with a general α-divergence, yielding a loss (Eq. 14) where the per-timestep preference signal u_t(θ) is raised to a power controlled by α, structurally reshaping how deviations from the target preference distribution are penalized. A key insight is that α ∈ (0,1) interpolates between mass-covering (α→1, equivalent to FKL) and mode-seeking (α→0, equivalent to reverse KL) behavior, with smaller α bounding the influence of outlier/noisy samples. To avoid a fixed α, the method introduces a dynamic scheduling mechanism: the stop-gradient of u_t(θ) serves as an implicit confidence score (validated to correlate monotonically with ΔHPSv2), and α is set per-sample as α = μ·f(x^w, x^l, c), so high-confidence pairs receive larger α (closer to standard DPO) while uncertain pairs receive smaller α (more mode-seeking and noise-tolerant). The entire procedure requires no additional reward model, no extra forward passes, and incurs the same computational cost as vanilla Diffusion-DPO.

## Results

On the real-world Pick-a-Pic V2 dataset (SDXL backbone), α-DPO achieves 30.86 HPSv2, 1.054 ImageReward, 0.3326 CLIP, 22.51 PickScore, and 5.972 Aesthetic Score on the Pick-a-Pic Test set, outperforming all baselines across every metric. Under synthetic 20% label-flipping noise (SDXL), α-DPO reaches 30.38 HPSv2 and 1.001 ImageReward, surpassing the strongest noise-robust baseline rDPO (28.77 HPSv2, 0.8519 IR) by a substantial margin. Even at 40% flip rate, α-DPO maintains a 70.6% winning rate on HPSv2 against the pretrained SDXL, while most baselines drop below 50%. The method incurs no additional computational cost over standard DPO training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

α-DPO consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluation metric and dataset. Against standard Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024), α-DPO gains +1.09 HPSv2 and +0.082 ImageReward on the real-world Pick-a-Pic V2 test (SDXL), with the gap widening to +1.61 HPSv2 and +0.150 IR under 20% synthetic label-flipping. Among noise-robust DPO variants, α-DPO surpasses cDPO (Mitchell, 2023) by +0.74 HPSv2, rDPO (Chowdhury et al., 2024) by +0.48 HPSv2, and H-DPO (Fujisawa et al., 2025) by +0.89 HPSv2 on the real-world set; under 20% flip noise, the advantage over rDPO grows to +1.61 HPSv2. Against other alignment methods, α-DPO beats KTO (Li et al., 2024) by +0.50 HPSv2 and SPIN (Yuan et al., 2024) by +0.54 HPSv2 on SD1.5 (Table 8), and outperforms MaPO (Hong et al., 2024) by +1.68 HPSv2 and +0.194 IR on SDXL (Table 9). Notably, α-DPO also integrates as a plug-and-play module with SPO (Liang et al., 2025), boosting its HPSv2 from 0.3208 to 0.3231 and ImageReward from 1.116 to 1.134, confirming its generality beyond standalone use.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024), which adapted the DPO framework from LLMs to diffusion models, and on the broader divergence-based preference optimization literature (f-DPO, f-PO). The key conceptual departure is showing that Diffusion-DPO's objective is provably equivalent to Forward KL divergence minimization, whose mass-covering property makes it inherently vulnerable to label-flipping noise. Unlike prior robust-DPO variants (rDPO, H-DPO, cDPO) that were designed for autoregressive LLMs under simplified I.I.D. flip assumptions, this work is the first to address noise-robust preference alignment specifically for diffusion models, replacing FKL with α-divergence to induce mode-seeking behavior and adding a dynamic α scheduling mechanism driven by the model's own per-sample confidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is strong: real-world preference datasets like Pick-a-Pic V2 inevitably contain mislabeled and subjective pairs, and the paper demonstrates that standard DPO degrades substantially under even moderate noise (20–40% flip rates), while α-DPO maintains consistent performance at no additional computational cost. The plug-and-play compatibility with other DPO variants (e.g., SPO) suggests broad applicability. Open questions include the need for an automated mechanism to tune the hyperparameter μ, the sensitivity of dynamic α scheduling to its activation timing, and whether the approach generalizes beyond pairwise preference data to more complex feedback structures.
