## Problem

Inference-time control of diffusion models via Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) suffers from three key limitations: it requires maintaining a large particle batch throughout denoising (memory-intensive), it produces poor sample diversity especially at small batch sizes due to resampling-induced mode collapse, and it cannot refine or extend samples after the sampling trajectory completes. This paper asks whether replica exchange (parallel tempering), a computationally dual MCMC framework, can be adapted to the diffusion setting where only a pretrained model is available rather than an explicit target density.

## Input / Output

CREPE takes a pretrained diffusion model (Gaussian SDE or discrete CTMC/mask diffusion) plus a target specification — an inverse-temperature β for tempering, a reward function r for reward-tilting, a guidance weight w for CFG debiasing, or J pretrained models for composition — and produces samples from the corresponding target distribution π₀. The method operates entirely at inference time without retraining, and the output is a Markov chain of samples collected after a burn-in period; unlike SMC, samples can be generated indefinitely and new constraints can be injected mid-run. The method requires choosing an annealing path, a discretisation schedule over diffusion times, and a truncation point t₀ below which standard denoising resumes.

## Method

CREPE reformulates inference-time control as accelerated parallel tempering (APT) over the diffusion time axis: instead of propagating N particles in parallel from t=1 to t=0 (SMC), it runs M chains at different diffusion times in parallel and generates new particles sequentially via MCMC iterations. The communication step between adjacent diffusion times t and t′ simulates forward and backward proposal paths and accepts swaps with a Metropolis-Hastings probability computed from Radon-Nikodym Estimators (RNE) of the pretrained diffusion model, avoiding the need for an explicit target density. The annealing path interpolates between the target π₀ (e.g., p₀^β, p₀·exp(r₀), or ∏p₀ʲ) and a tractable reference (Gaussian or fully masked), with the marginal density ratio expressed entirely in terms of RNEs. Local exploration uses Unadjusted Langevin Algorithm (Gaussian) or Metropolis-Hastings with the concrete score (CTMC) targeting the annealed distribution. PT is truncated at a small t₀>0 to avoid numerical instability in high dimensions, after which standard denoising with the proposal drift completes the sample.

## Results

On inference-time tempering for molecular Boltzmann sampling, CREPE achieves the best TICA MMD across all three molecules (0.096, 0.035, 0.009 for dipeptide, tetrapeptide, and hexapeptide respectively), substantially outperforming SMC-based baselines (e.g., 0.035 vs. 0.155 for tetrapeptide). CREPE also achieves the lowest Sample W2 distance (0.264, 0.856, 1.299) and Distance TVD across all molecules. For CFG debiasing on ImageNet-64, CREPE with 512 samples achieves FID 1.79 and ImageReward +0.09, outperforming FKC (FID 1.96, IR −0.08). For CTMC text generation, CREPE reduces perplexity by up to a factor of 5 compared to SMC at target strength 1.8 while maintaining comparable sentiment accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FKC (Skreta et al., 2025), CREPE wins on TICA MMD for all three molecules (e.g., 0.035 vs. 0.138 for tetrapeptide) and on Sample W2 and Distance TVD, but loses on hexapeptide Energy TVD (0.398 vs. 0.091), which the authors attribute to pretrained model error amplified by PT iterations. Against RNE-based SMC (He et al., 2025c), CREPE achieves substantially lower TICA MMD (0.096 vs. 0.168 for dipeptide; 0.009 vs. 0.042 for hexapeptide), indicating better preservation of mode structure and diversity. For CTMC CFG debiasing on text, CREPE reduces perplexity by up to 5× compared to SMC (Lee et al., 2025) at target strength 1.8, while both methods achieve similar sentiment accuracy. For maze trajectory stitching, CREPE with an unconditional diffusion model achieves comparable or better success rates than the conditional-model approach of Luo et al. (2025), at the cost of more compute. Overall, CREPE's key advantage is maintaining sample diversity (lower TICA MMD, visually more varied outputs) while matching or exceeding SMC baselines on distributional fidelity metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

CREPE repurposes Accelerated Parallel Tempering (APT), a classical MCMC technique for sampling from known unnormalised densities, into the inference-time control setting where only a pretrained diffusion model is available and no explicit target density exists. The key conceptual move is the computational duality with SMC: where SMC propagates a batch of particles sequentially along the denoising path, CREPE runs chains at different diffusion times in parallel and generates new samples sequentially via MCMC. The Radon-Nikodym Estimator (RNE) framework from He et al. (2025c) and Vargas et al. (2024) provides the bridge, allowing acceptance probabilities to be expressed purely in terms of the pretrained model's forward and backward transition kernels. This positions CREPE as a direct alternative to the SMC-based debiasing methods of Skreta et al. (2025), Lee et al. (2025), and Wu et al. (2023), while inheriting the flexibility of the APT proposal-process design from Zhang et al. (2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CREPE offers practitioners a complementary tool to SMC for inference-time control with three practical advantages: maintained sample diversity (avoiding the mode-collapse of small-batch SMC), online refinement (new constraints can be injected mid-run), and anytime termination. However, the method carries a burn-in cost and relies on the assumption of a discretisation-error-free diffusion model, both of which limit its applicability to expensive or large-scale settings. The paper also reveals that CREPE can be less stable than SMC for discrete diffusion on text, where iterative refinement of a poor sample can destabilise the chain. Open questions include adapting classical PT schedule-tuning and path-selection techniques to the diffusion-control setting, and understanding when the approximation errors in the acceptance rate accumulate to problematic levels.
