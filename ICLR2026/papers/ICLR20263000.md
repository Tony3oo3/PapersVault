## Problem

Existing video subject swapping methods are either confined to narrow domains (human-body animation, hand-object interaction) or rely on indirect editing paradigms such as text prompts or per-subject LoRA tuning that compromise appearance fidelity and interaction realism. No dedicated, subject-agnostic, end-to-end framework exists that can swap any subject in any video while preserving fine-grained details and natural subject-context interaction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (up to 720p, 65–69 frames), a user-specified binary mask sequence localizing the source subject across all frames, a single RGB reference image of the target subject, and auxiliary pose/3D-hand sequences as conditioning. It produces a swapped video where the target subject replaces the source subject while following the original motion trajectory and interacting naturally with the preserved background. The swapping is recast as a video inpainting problem: the model recovers the target subject's appearance within the masked region as if it inherently belonged there, with all unmasked pixels strictly preserved.

## Method

DreamSwapV builds on the Wan2.1-I2V-14B DiT backbone and introduces a dedicated condition fusion module that encodes all inputs (masked video, agnostic video, pose, 3D hand, reference image) into a shared 3D-VAE latent space with strict spatial-temporal alignment. The reference image is injected via temporal concatenation rather than channel concatenation, cross-attention, or a separate ReferenceNet: the reference latent is appended along the frame axis, and an asymmetric attention mask lets the video attend to the reference while the reference only attends to itself, enabling high-fidelity appearance transfer without parameter redundancy. An adaptive mask strategy scales the grid-based augmentation inversely with subject size (finer grids for large subjects, coarser for small ones) and adds random geometric shapes to mask edges to decouple the subject from the mask boundary, reducing shape leakage while improving subject-context interaction. Training follows a two-phase scheme: 15,000 iterations of self-attention-only pre-training on a HumanVID-derived dataset (8,160 videos, 16,219 subject instances) with same-domain references, followed by 10,000 iterations of full fine-tuning on cross-domain reference-video pairs from AnyInsertion, Subject200K, and AnchorCrafter-400. Additional techniques include tunnel inpainting for small subjects (mask area < 5%) and a subject-region reweighting loss that amplifies the learning signal inside the mask to preserve fine details.

## Results

On DreamSwapV-Benchmark (100 videos, 167 subject instances), DreamSwapV achieves the best VBench average score of 80.44% and the best overall average of 71.49% across all eight automatic metrics. It leads on all three task-specific metrics—Reference Appearance (45.22%), Background Preservation (52.49%), and Semantic Consistency (72.01%)—and also tops the user study on Reference Detail (3.35/5), Subject Interaction (3.39/5), and Visual Fidelity (3.32/5). The closest competitor, Kling 1.6 Multimodal, trails by 0.65 points on VBench average and by 2.69 points on the total average, with a notable weakness in Background Preservation (39.17% vs. 52.49%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamSwapV outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest competitor Kling 1.6 Multimodal, DreamSwapV gains +0.65 on VBench average, +2.95 on Reference Appearance, +13.32 on Background Preservation, and +2.06 on Semantic Consistency; Kling's regeneration-like paradigm alters large background regions, which is its primary weakness. Among the open-source baselines, HunyuanCustom (78.17% VBench avg) and VACE (74.99%) both underperform DreamSwapV by 2.27 and 5.45 points respectively on VBench average, and both struggle with reference appearance fidelity (41.33% and 39.66% vs. 45.22%) due to their unified multi-task design where subject swapping is only one objective. AnyV2V is the weakest baseline overall (63.51% total average), with its tuning-free attention manipulation frequently causing complete video collapse, reflected in the lowest user-study scores (0.87/0.65/0.42). In the user study, DreamSwapV leads Kling 1.6 by +0.31 on Reference Detail, +0.50 on Subject Interaction, and +0.18 on Visual Fidelity, confirming that the quantitative advantages translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamSwapV builds on the video inpainting paradigm (VideoPainter, VACE, HunyuanCustom) but recasts subject swapping as a dedicated, mask-guided inpainting task rather than a side capability of unified editing frameworks. It inherits the grid-based mask augmentation from Animate Anyone 2 and the reweighting loss / tunnel inpainting from AnchorCrafter, but generalizes both to be subject-agnostic and scale-adaptive. The condition fusion module departs from the ReferenceNet approach common in animation methods (Animate Anyone 2, UniAnimate) by showing that simple temporal token concatenation with KV-cache attention masking achieves equivalent or better reference injection without parameter redundancy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end framework and the first dedicated benchmark (DreamSwapV-Benchmark) for generic, subject-agnostic video subject swapping, filling a gap where prior methods were either domain-specific (humans only, handheld objects only) or treated swapping as an afterthought in unified editing pipelines. Key open questions include handling cross-domain swaps (animal→human, object→character) where pose conditioning breaks down, the limitation of a single reference image for multi-view subjects, and the high compute cost (~13 days on 32 H100s) that limits accessibility. The framework's model-agnostic design (plug-and-play on any DiT-based I2V model) positions it as a reusable component for the broader video customization ecosystem.
