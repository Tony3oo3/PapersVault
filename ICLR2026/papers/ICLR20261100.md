## Problem

Existing noisy-label learning methods such as AUM and HMW treat all label errors equally, ignoring the semantic relations that inherently exist between classes (e.g., 'anger' is closer to 'fear' than to 'joy' in emotion detection). Furthermore, approaches that rely on hard thresholds to remove low-margin samples can discard valuable but difficult clean examples. LANE addresses both gaps by incorporating inter-class semantic similarity into the margin metric and by dynamically re-weighting rather than removing samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes text sentences or documents (ranging from short Reddit comments to scientific paper abstracts) as input and produces a single class label from 5 to 105 classes depending on the dataset. Both the main classifier θ and the auxiliary network Π are BERT base uncased models, so the input is tokenized and processed through standard transformer encoding. During training, the method additionally produces per-sample weights (λ_CE) that modulate the cross-entropy loss, and the auxiliary network outputs soft class-assignment probabilities used to compute semantic similarity weights for the label-aware margin.

## Method

LANE jointly trains two BERT networks: a main classifier θ and an auxiliary network Π. The core mechanism is the Label-Aware Margin (LM), which rescales the standard margin M by dividing by the auxiliary network's semantic similarity weight w_{x,j} whenever M is negative (where j is the model's predicted class), so that mislabels semantically close to the true class are penalized less than distant ones. The Average Label-Aware Margin (ALM) is accumulated across training iterations, and a weighted cross-entropy loss assigns reduced weights (via a dynamically fitted truncated Gaussian) to samples whose ALM falls below the mean of the negative-ALM distribution, preserving hard-but-clean samples. A label-aware supervised contrastive loss (extending Gunel et al.'s SCL with soft class-assignment weights from Π) is jointly optimized to learn inter-class semantic relations. Data augmentation via synonym replacement, SwitchOut, and backtranslation expands the positive set for the contrastive objective.

## Results

On the original (manually annotated) datasets, LANE achieves the best performance across all 10 benchmarks, with headline results including 66.5% weighted F1 on GoEmotions, 78.2% weighted F1 on CancerEMO, 59.3% weighted F1 on RCV1, and 78.4% accuracy on Yahoo Answer. LANE improves the average F1 by 2.88% over AUM and 2.32% over HMW on original datasets, and by 3.37% and 3.4% respectively under 20% injected label noise. Under extreme 40% noise, the gap widens to 4.75% F1 over AUM and 4.01% over HMW, while many baselines (e.g., BASE, E2L, H2L, AMG, PLF, SCL, LCL) fail to converge entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LANE outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset in both the original and 20%-noise settings. Against the closest margin-based competitor HMW (Zhang et al., 2024), LANE gains an average of 2.32% F1 on original data and 3.4% under 20% noise; against AUM (Pleiss et al., 2020), the gains are 2.88% and 3.37% respectively, widening to 4.75% at 40% noise where AUM degrades severely. LCL (Suresh & Ong, 2021), the strongest single-network baseline (best on 5/10 datasets), is surpassed by LANE on all 10 datasets, with the largest gap on GoEmotions (66.5 vs. 64.8 wF1) and RCV1 (59.3 vs. 57.9 wF1). In the dual-network comparison (Table 5), LANE beats DivideMix by >9 points on Amazon Review (69.7 vs. 60.66), >4.5 points on Yelp (69.2 vs. 64.66), and >9 points on Yahoo (78.4 vs. 69.17), and outperforms Co-teaching by similar margins. Against LLMs under 20% noise, LANE exceeds ChatGPT on 9/10 datasets (e.g., +3.1% on ISEAR, +6.5% MF1 on RCV1) and Llama-2-13B on 9/10 (e.g., +15.4% MF1 on SciHTC), losing only on SST-5 to Llama-2 (53.1 vs. 54.2). Finally, LANE edges out the recent interpolation-based correction of Xu et al. (2025) on 4/5 datasets (e.g., 59.3 vs. 58.7 on RCV1, 69.2 vs. 68.5 on Yelp).

## Relation to Prior Work

LANE extends the margin-based sample identification framework of AUM (Pleiss et al., 2020) and its weighting extension HMW (Zhang et al., 2024) by introducing a label-aware margin that rescales negative margins using inter-class semantic similarity weights from an auxiliary network. It also builds on supervised contrastive learning (Gunel et al., 2020) and label-aware contrastive loss (Suresh & Ong, 2021), extending them into a joint training objective that simultaneously learns class relations and re-weights the cross-entropy loss. The key conceptual departure from prior margin-based methods is the replacement of hard thresholding (AUM) or fixed weighting (HMW) with a dynamically fitted truncated Gaussian that preserves hard-but-clean samples while down-weighting genuinely noisy ones, and the incorporation of semantic distance between the assigned and predicted labels into the margin computation itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LANE provides a practical, model-agnostic mechanism for mitigating label noise in fine-grained text classification with only ~1.8× additional compute, making it a viable alternative to expensive LLM-based label correction. The consistent gains across 10 diverse datasets (emotion, topic, sentiment) and noise levels (original, 20%, 40%) suggest the label-aware margin is a robust signal rather than a dataset-specific hack. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to domains without clear inter-class semantic structure (e.g., arbitrary topic taxonomies), whether the auxiliary network's semantic weights remain reliable under extreme noise (40%+), and whether the method extends naturally to multi-label or hierarchical classification settings. The authors note future work on image classification and the legal domain.
