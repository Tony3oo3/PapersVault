## Problem

Autoregressive video generation models produce ultra-long videos by sequentially denoising fixed-frame chunks, but inference remains prohibitively slow (e.g., ~50 minutes for a 10-second 720×720 video on MAGI-1). Existing caching strategies for diffusion transformers assume uniform denoising across all frames at a given timestep, an assumption that breaks down in autoregressive settings where different chunks are at heterogeneous denoising stages simultaneously, making uniform reuse either wasteful or quality-degrading.

## Input / Output

FlowCache is a training-free, plug-and-play acceleration layer applied on top of existing autoregressive video diffusion models (MAGI-1-4.5B-distill, SkyReels-V2-1.3B-540P). It takes a text prompt as conditioning input and produces a generated video, operating entirely in the latent space of the base model's video chunks (e.g., 24-frame chunks for MAGI-1, 97-frame chunks with 17-frame overlap for SkyReels-V2). No retraining or architectural modification of the base model is required; the method only modifies the inference-time caching and KV cache management logic.

## Method

FlowCache introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, a chunkwise adaptive caching strategy assigns each video chunk an independent reuse policy: a per-chunk accumulator f(X,t,i) tracks the cumulative relative L1 distance between consecutive timesteps, and the chunk is recomputed only when this accumulator exceeds a threshold ε (or during the first m timesteps). This is grounded in Theorem 1, which proves that relative L1 distance monotonically increases as denoising progresses, and Corollary 1, which shows that heterogeneous chunk content yields divergent L1 distances at the same timestep—justifying per-chunk decisions rather than a global uniform policy. Second, a KV cache compression mechanism jointly scores historical key-value entries by attention-based importance (averaged softmax attention from the last 50 query tokens) and cosine-similarity-based redundancy, combined via a mixing coefficient λ (set to 0.07), selecting top-B entries per attention head to maintain a fixed memory budget while preserving long-range temporal consistency. Together these yield 2.38× speedup on MAGI-1 and 6.7× on SkyReels-V2 with negligible VBench degradation.

## Results

FlowCache achieves 2.38× speedup on MAGI-1 (140 PFLOPs, 1209 s latency) with a VBench score of 77.93%, slightly exceeding the vanilla baseline (77.06%), and 6.7× speedup on SkyReels-V2 (28 PFLOPs, 230 s latency) with VBench 83.05% versus 83.84% for vanilla. In the slower configuration, FlowCache reaches 1.86× on MAGI-1 (VBench 78.96%) and 5.88× on SkyReels-V2 (VBench 83.12%). Perceptual quality metrics (LPIPS, SSIM, PSNR) confirm that FlowCache-fast preserves visual fidelity far better than TeaCache-fast at comparable or higher acceleration ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline compared is TeaCache (Liu et al., 2025a), a uniform caching strategy designed for synchronous video diffusion models. On MAGI-1, FlowCache-fast delivers a 2.38× speedup with VBench 77.93%, whereas TeaCache-fast achieves only 1.44× while suffering a severe quality drop (VBench 70.11%, a 6.9-point decline from vanilla). On SkyReels-V2, the gap widens dramatically: FlowCache-fast reaches 6.7× speedup with VBench 83.05% (only 0.79 points below vanilla), while TeaCache-fast manages just 2.2× with VBench 80.06% (a 3.78-point drop). In the slower configuration, FlowCache-slow on SkyReels-V2 achieves 5.88× speedup with VBench 83.12%, outperforming TeaCache-slow (1.89×, VBench 82.67%) by over 3× in acceleration while maintaining comparable quality. Across all settings, FlowCache consistently wins on both speedup and quality, with the advantage being most pronounced on SkyReels-V2 where the hierarchical block-based denoising creates greater inter-chunk heterogeneity that uniform caching cannot exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free feature caching paradigm (TeaCache, DiCache, ToCa) from synchronous video diffusion models to the autoregressive setting, where the core assumption of uniform denoising across all frames at a given timestep breaks down. The chunkwise adaptive caching strategy is motivated by a theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) showing that relative L1 distance between adjacent timesteps monotonically increases during denoising, combined with the empirical observation that different chunks occupy heterogeneous denoising stages simultaneously. For KV cache compression, the paper adapts the importance-redundancy balance from R-KV (Cai et al., 2025) to the video generation domain, addressing the high spatial-temporal redundancy that makes importance-only selection (H2O, SnapKV) ineffective for video tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Autoregressive video generation is the leading paradigm for ultra-long video synthesis (linear vs. quadratic complexity), but current inference times (50 minutes for 10 seconds at 720×720 on MAGI-1) remain far from practical. FlowCache's 6.7× speedup on SkyReels-V2 with negligible quality loss is a concrete step toward real-time long video generation, and the method is training-free and plug-and-play. Open questions include whether the chunkwise caching principle generalizes to other autoregressive architectures beyond MAGI-1 and SkyReels-V2, whether the KV cache compression granularity findings (token-level keys, token-level queries) hold at substantially larger scales, and whether the approach composes well with other acceleration techniques like distillation or quantization at production scale.
