## Problem

Existing materials discovery methods either require task-specific retraining (limiting generalizability), rely on unguided LLM generation that produces theoretically plausible but thermodynamically unstable or impractical candidates, or formulate discovery as a single-objective task. Real-world materials design is inherently multi-objective and must enforce synthesizability, yet no prior framework simultaneously integrates LLM scientific priors, chemistry-informed evolutionary operators, and constrained multi-objective scoring to generate physically realizable candidates.

## Input / Output

The user supplies a CSV specifying the task name and property constraints; the framework then iteratively constructs prompts combining the task description, quantitative constraints (e.g., band gap ≥ 2.5 eV, formation energy ≤ −1.0 eV/atom), a randomly sampled subset of 6 chemistry-informed evolution rules, and in-context demonstrations drawn from success and failure memory pools. The LLM outputs candidates as structured JSON specifying reduced chemical formula, lattice parameters, atomic species, and fractional coordinates, which are converted to CIF files for downstream evaluation. A hierarchical oracle first queries the Materials Project API and falls back to pretrained CGCNN/ALIGNN surrogates for out-of-distribution candidates, producing a property vector scored against all constraints.

## Method

LLEMA is an agentic evolutionary framework where an LLM (GPT-4o-mini or Mistral-Small-3.2-24B) generates crystallographic candidates under explicit property constraints, guided by 14 chemistry-informed design rules (same-group substitution, stoichiometry preservation, oxidation-state matching, prototype substitution, etc.) that act as mutation/crossover operators. Candidates are evaluated by a multi-objective scoring function with normalized, clipped feasibility margins per constraint, then partitioned into success and failure memory pools. A multi-island model (m=5 islands) with Boltzmann sampling selects which island's top-k success and failure exemplars populate the next prompt, balancing exploitation of high-scoring regions against exploration of underexplored chemical space. The LLM is never fine-tuned; all learning occurs through in-context feedback across N iterations, with the surrogate oracle providing the fitness signal that prevents collapse when candidates fall outside database coverage.

## Results

On the 14-task LLEMABench, LLEMA (GPT-4o-mini) achieves the highest hit-rate and stability across nearly all domains. For example, on Wide-Bandgap Semiconductors, LLEMA (GPT) reaches 33.62% hit-rate and 22.42% stability, versus 4.19%/1.13% for LLMatDesign and 6.56%/4.15% for MatterGen. On Solid-State Electrolytes, LLEMA (GPT) attains 46.17%/25.37%, an order of magnitude above LLMatDesign (2.51%/2.44%). The Mistral-Small-3.2-24B backbone also consistently surpasses all non-LLM generative baselines, confirming that the framework's gains are not backbone-specific.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLEMA decisively outperforms all five baselines on both hit-rate and stability across the 14-task benchmark. CDVAE, G-SchNet, and DiffCSP (traditional generative models) produce near-zero stability on most tasks and hit-rates typically below 15%, indicating they generate chemically valid but thermodynamically unstable structures. MatterGen achieves moderate hit-rates on a few tasks (e.g., 26.27% on SAW/BAW, 21.64% on Piezo Energy Harvesters) but consistently yields 0% stability, confirming it cannot enforce thermodynamic feasibility. LLMatDesign, the strongest LLM-based baseline, reaches 47.59% hit-rate on SAW/BAW and 32.16% on Piezo Energy Harvesters but still trails LLEMA (GPT) by 12–14 points on hit-rate and by 10+ points on stability; its single-objective, single-step modification strategy limits multi-constraint satisfaction. The End2end direct-prompting baseline is highly inconsistent, excelling on Photovoltaic Absorbers (24.59% H.R) but failing entirely on most other tasks, underscoring the necessity of LLEMA's iterative memory-based refinement and chemistry-informed rules.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLEMA synthesizes three previously separate research threads: (1) LLM-based materials hypothesis generation (LLMatDesign, MatAgent, MatLLMSearch), (2) evolutionary search with LLMs as generators (Romera-Paredes et al. 2024, Shojaee et al. 2025a, Abhyankar et al. 2025), and (3) surrogate-assisted property prediction (CGCNN, ALIGNN). Unlike prior LLM-based methods that perform single-step, single-objective modifications, LLEMA introduces chemistry-informed evolutionary operators (14 domain rules), a multi-island memory architecture with Boltzmann sampling, and a constrained multi-objective scoring function. It also departs from generative crystal models (CDVAE, G-SchNet, DiffCSP, MatterGen) that require task-specific retraining by leveraging the broad scientific priors already embedded in pretrained LLMs, requiring only a CSV of constraints to configure a new task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of LLEMABench—a 14-task, multi-objective, industrially motivated benchmark with explicit synthesizability constraints—fills a gap left by prior single-property benchmarks and provides a rigorous testbed for future methods. The ablation showing near-total performance collapse (<5% hit-rate) when surrogate models are removed underscores that LLMs alone cannot serve as reliable property oracles, a finding that tempers enthusiasm for purely language-driven discovery. Key open questions include: whether the framework scales to tasks requiring experimental validation beyond DFT-level surrogates, how performance changes with larger or domain-specific LLMs, and whether the iterative LLM query cost remains practical for high-throughput screening. The modest absolute hit-rates (often 10–30%) and stability scores (often <25%) suggest significant room for improvement before the approach can replace expert-driven discovery pipelines.
