## Problem

Fine-tuning has been consistently dismissed as a weak baseline for model editing, attributed to overfitting and catastrophic forgetting. This paper argues the reported failure stems not from fine-tuning itself but from a mis-specified depth-first pipeline (single-pass, sample-wise optimization to convergence) that was adopted to mimic sequential edit arrival, combined with suboptimal tuning locations inherited from locate-then-edit methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262433/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a stream of edit requests as fact triples (subject, relation, new object) and applies localized fine-tuning to a single module (typically the MLP down-projection matrix) in a specific layer of the LLM. For lifelong editing, each new edit is incrementally appended to the cumulative dataset and the model is re-trained over all accumulated edits using standard cross-entropy loss with prompt masking. The output is a set of updated parameters at the chosen layer/module, producing an edited model that encodes all target facts while preserving general capabilities measured on MMLU, GSM8K, SST2, WMT16, and Natural Questions.

## Method

LocFT-BF restores fine-tuning to its standard breadth-first (epoch-based) pipeline with mini-batch gradient aggregation, replacing the depth-first single-pass procedure that caused catastrophic overwriting and high-variance gradient updates. A systematic study across layers and modules (entire layer, full attention, full MLP, MLP_up, MLP_down) in three LLMs identifies the optimal tuning location, revealing that the MLP down-projection in later layers consistently achieves near-perfect editing reliability while minimizing capability degradation. This localized, single-module update avoids the matrix precomputation of locate-then-edit methods, the hypernetwork training of meta-learning methods, and the architectural modifications of parameter-extension methods. For scaling to larger models, a proportional layer-selection heuristic (scaling the optimal layer index by depth ratio) eliminates the need for exhaustive position search, enabling seamless extension from 7B to 72B parameters and up to 100K sequential edits.

## Results

On the lifelong editing task, LocFT-BF achieves near-perfect reliability (98.9–99.7%) across all nine dataset–LLM combinations (ZsRE, COUNTERFACT, WikiBigEdit × LLaMA3-8B, Mistral-7B, Qwen2.5-7B), exceeding the best baseline by an average of 33.72% and up to 58.50%. Capability is preserved at 42–60% (vs. 45–59% pre-edited), and each edit completes in 0.24–0.98 s, roughly 50× faster than locate-then-edit baselines. At scale, LocFT-BF sustains 100K sequential edits with 99.80% reliability on Qwen2.5-72B while retaining 62.54% capability (vs. 64.84% pre-edited).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LocFT-BF decisively outperforms all six baselines on reliability across every dataset–LLM combination, with the largest gaps against locate-then-edit methods: MEMIT (26–72% vs. 99%), RECT (0–10% vs. 99%), and WISE (4–38% vs. 99%). Among the more competitive baselines, RLEdit reaches 66.67% reliability on ZsRE/LLaMA and 71.10% on WikiBigEdit/LLaMA, still trailing LocFT-BF by 32–28 points; UltraEdit peaks at 74.20% on WikiBigEdit/Qwen, a 25-point gap. AlphaEdit is the only method that beats LocFT-BF on generalization for COUNTERFACT/LLaMA (39.90 vs. 33.23), owing to its prefix-augmentation strategy, but loses on reliability (94.27 vs. 99.73) and collapses on Mistral and Qwen (6.67% and 32.17% reliability). On capability, LocFT-BF is most stable across LLMs (42–60%), whereas RLEdit and UltraEdit degrade sharply on Mistral-7B (21–45%), and AlphaEdit fails to generalize beyond LLaMA (15–17% on Mistral/Qwen). In efficiency, LocFT-BF (0.24–0.98 s/edit) is comparable to RLEdit and UltraEdit but roughly 50× faster than MEMIT, RECT, WISE, and AlphaEdit (9–34 s/edit).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-examines fine-tuning-based model editing (FT-M, AdaLoRA, RoseLoRA) that has been consistently dismissed as a weak baseline in the knowledge editing literature. It departs from the locate-then-edit paradigm (ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit) and meta-learning approaches (MEND, RLEdit, UltraEdit) by arguing that fine-tuning's reported failure stems not from inherent limitations but from a mis-specified depth-first, single-pass, batch-size-1 training pipeline adopted to mimic sequential edit arrival. By restoring the standard breadth-first, mini-batch fine-tuning paradigm and systematically studying tuning locations (building on ROME's causal-tracing identification of MLP down-projection), the paper repositions fine-tuning as a leading editing method without requiring architectural modifications, hypernetworks, or matrix precomputation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work overturns a long-standing misconception in the model editing community and provides a simple, plug-and-play method (LocFT-BF) that outperforms state-of-the-art by large margins while being trivially implementable in standard fine-tuning frameworks like LlamaFactory. It is the first method to sustain 100K sequential edits and scale to 72B-parameter models without degrading general capabilities, pushing the field toward practical deployment. Open questions include whether the breadth-first pipeline is optimal for truly real-time streaming edits, how to automate tuning-location selection for unseen architectures without exhaustive search, and whether the generalization gap on COUNTERFACT (mitigated by augmentation) reveals a deeper limitation of single-QA-pair editing data.
