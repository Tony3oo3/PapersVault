## Problem

Existing vision-language-action (VLA) models face a trilemma: they sacrifice multimodal reasoning for action generation, narrow their abilities to task-specific manipulation data, and suffer catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained VLM capabilities. The paper tackles the open question of how to acquire manipulation skills without eroding the VLM's multimodal reasoning, and how that reasoning can in turn enhance manipulation performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265188/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at 448×448 (VLM) and 224×224 (action expert), diverse text instructions ranging from atomic commands to multi-step contextual goals, and optional proprioceptive robot state. It produces two types of output: autoregressive text (reasoning chains, scene captions, QA responses, instruction rewrites) and continuous robot action sequences. Text is generated greedily until the first action query token appears, after which N=64 latent action queries are decoded in a single parallel forward pass; a separate flow-matching expert then decodes these latent actions into continuous actions, supporting dual-frequency inference at a 1:2 VLM-to-expert ratio.

## Method

InstructVLA builds on an Eagle2-2B VLM backbone augmented with a mixture-of-experts (MoE) adaptation layer—LoRA modules serving as experts with a scalar gating head that adaptively blends language and action processing based on input context. A compact 12-layer transformer action expert, conditioned on DINOv2 image features modulated by FiLM and the VLM's latent actions, generates continuous actions via flow matching. Training follows two stages: (1) Action Pretraining tunes only the action LoRA and latent action embeddings (650M params) on manipulation data with language-motion supervision, decoupling low-level control from the VLM; (2) VLA-IT adds a language LoRA and scalar head to form the full MoE (220M params), jointly training on multimodal corpora, manipulation data, and a curated 650K-sample VLA-IT dataset with four annotation types (scenario captioning, QA, command rewriting, context creation). The key insight is treating language-conditioned action generation as an integral component of instruction following, so that the VLM's preserved reasoning capabilities directly guide manipulation through test-time textual thinking.

## Results

On SimplerEnv (closed-loop manipulation), InstructVLA-Expert(S.) achieves 61.2% average success rate, a 33% relative improvement over SpatialVLA (45.9%). On the new SimplerEnv-Instruct benchmark (80 zero-shot instruction-following tasks), InstructVLA-Generalist scores 46.2% overall (43.6% Task Aggregation, 48.8% Situated Reasoning), outperforming fine-tuned OpenVLA by ~96% (46.2 vs. 23.9) and an OpenVLA+GPT-4o system by ~29% (46.2 vs. 35.6). On multimodal understanding benchmarks, InstructVLA-Generalist (1.5B) matches or exceeds its Eagle2-2B base model (e.g., MMMU 44.2 vs. 43.1, MMBench 76.1 vs. 74.9) while substantially outperforming co-trained baselines like Magma (MMMU 38.8) and fine-tuned OpenVLA (MMMU 26.0). On LIBERO, InstructVLA-1.5B achieves 95.8% average success, competitive with π0 (94.2%) and π0.5+KI (94.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructVLA-Generalist (1.5B) decisively outperforms all generalist VLA baselines on SimplerEnv-Instruct: it beats Magma-8B (46.2 vs. 12.7) and Magma-β with sampling (46.2 vs. 23.8) by large margins, and surpasses the OpenVLA+GPT-4o system (46.2 vs. 35.6) by ~29%, showing that internal reasoning outperforms external LLM assistance. On closed-loop SimplerEnv manipulation, InstructVLA-Expert(S.) (61.2%) exceeds SpatialVLA-3B (45.9%) by 33% and π0-3B (41.7%) by 47%, while the Generalist variant (54.9% with state) remains competitive. On multimodal understanding, InstructVLA-Generalist matches its Eagle2-2B backbone (MMMU 44.2 vs. 43.1; MMBench 76.1 vs. 74.9) and outperforms Magma-8B (MMMU 44.2 vs. 38.8) and fine-tuned OpenVLA-7B (MMMU 44.2 vs. 26.0), confirming that the two-stage MoE training avoids the catastrophic forgetting seen in ECoT (MMMU 16.2, MM-Vet 0.0) and standard fine-tuning. On LIBERO, InstructVLA-1.5B (95.8% avg) slightly trails OpenVLA-OFT-7B (97.1%) but exceeds π0-2B (94.2%) and π0.5+KI (94.3%) with a much smaller action head (134M vs. 300M parameters). In real-world experiments, InstructVLA achieves a 41.7% improvement over OpenVLA on few-shot reasoning tasks and a 2.5× improvement over π0 on math-centric manipulation, where π0 behaves near random.

## Relation to Prior Work

InstructVLA builds on the Eagle2-2B VLM backbone, the flow-matching action generation paradigm from π0, and the X-LoRA mixture-of-experts framework, unifying them under a two-stage training recipe (action pretraining followed by VLA instruction tuning). It departs from the autoregressive-token VLA line (RT-2, OpenVLA, SpatialVLA) by treating actions as continuous flow-matching outputs conditioned on VLM-derived latent action queries, and from the structured-CoT line (ECoT, Emma-X) by using diverse, free-form instruction annotations rather than rigid subtask/grounding templates. The key conceptual departure is framing language-conditioned manipulation as an integral component of instruction following—extending visual instruction tuning (Liu et al., 2023) into the embodied domain—rather than as a separate policy-learning objective bolted onto a VLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and widely felt pain point: VLA models that fine-tune on manipulation data lose their VLM reasoning capabilities (catastrophic forgetting), while models that preserve VLM capabilities (Magma, co-training) underperform on manipulation. The SimplerEnv-Instruct benchmark (80 zero-shot tasks with situated reasoning) fills a real evaluation gap, and the 650K-sample VLA-IT dataset provides a practical resource for the community. Open questions include whether the MoE + two-stage paradigm scales to larger VLM backbones and more embodiments, whether the 1:7 multimodal-to-action training ratio generalizes, and how well the approach transfers to dexterous or multi-robot settings beyond the tested WidowX/Franka configurations.
