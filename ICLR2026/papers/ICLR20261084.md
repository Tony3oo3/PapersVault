## Problem

Reasoning LLMs generate lengthy chain-of-thought traces for every problem regardless of difficulty, wasting compute on easy questions while not necessarily helping on hard ones. The paper identifies a counterintuitive U-shaped entropy pattern—high uncertainty on easy problems despite high accuracy, low entropy at medium difficulty, and high entropy on hard problems—revealing systematic overthinking that existing uniform-budget or confidence-based early-exit methods do not address.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text question as input and produces a difficulty label (Easy/Normal/Hard) from a lightweight probe, which then triggers a corresponding inference strategy (distinct prompt template, sampling temperature, and max-token cap) to generate a reasoning trace with a final answer. The probe consumes only the last-layer hidden state after the prefill pass over the question, so it adds no decoding latency and remains compatible with KV-cache, prefix-cache, and batching optimizations. Each strategy uses a fixed configuration: Easy (T=0.5, 0.4×Max tokens, direct-solving prompt), Normal (T=0.8, 1.0×Max tokens, step-by-step prompt), Hard (T=0.4, 0.5×Max tokens, fail-fast prompt).

## Method

DiffAdapt is a three-stage framework that adapts inference strategy per question without retraining the base LLM. In Stage 1, a proxy model (the same LLM) generates 10 sampled responses per problem; correctness rate and average generation entropy are computed, and a heuristic rule (thresholds α, β, γ) assigns each problem an Easy/Normal/Hard label. In Stage 2, a 2-layer MLP probe is trained via cross-entropy loss on the frozen LLM's last-layer hidden state (extracted after prefill) to predict the three-way difficulty label, using only ~300 problems per difficulty level from DeepMath-103K. In Stage 3, at inference time the probe classifies the incoming question and the corresponding strategy's prompt, temperature, and token budget are applied to generate the answer. The key empirical insight driving the design is the U-shaped entropy curve: easy problems show high entropy despite high accuracy (overthinking), motivating a low-budget Easy strategy, while hard problems show high entropy with low accuracy (capability limit), motivating a 'fail-fast' Hard strategy that cuts losses rather than extending unproductive reasoning.

## Results

DiffAdapt achieves up to 22.4% token savings on Qwen3-4B and ~10% on DeepSeek-R1-Qwen-7B and ThinkPrune-7B relative to a fixed Normal strategy, while maintaining or improving accuracy across eight benchmarks. End-to-end inference latency drops from 64 min (vLLM baseline) to 10 min with DiffAdapt (6× speedup). In oracle experiments, per-question strategy selection yields a 7.2% average accuracy gain over the best fixed strategy with 50% token reduction. On out-of-domain MMLU-Pro, DiffAdapt improves over the fixed baseline by 3–7% across token budgets using a probe trained solely on math data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DEER (Yang et al., 2025b), the training-free dynamic early-exit baseline, DiffAdapt wins decisively on both efficiency and accuracy: DiffAdapt saves 22.4% tokens on Qwen3-4B while DEER *increases* token usage by 27.5%, and saves 9.7% on DS-R1-Qwen-7B where DEER increases usage by 53.3%. The mechanistic reason is that DEER's confidence-based continuation drives generations to the maximum token cap rather than allocating tokens by difficulty. On latency, DiffAdapt is 5× faster than DEER (10 min vs. 57 min) and 6× faster than the vLLM baseline. On out-of-domain benchmarks (Minerva, MMLU-Pro, GPQA), DEER exhibits limited generalization and larger performance degradation, whereas DiffAdapt maintains consistent gains. Against ThinkLess (Fang et al., 2025), which requires expensive two-stage SFT+RL training, DiffAdapt applied to the untrained TL-Warmup model outperforms the fully RL-trained TL-1.5B-RL on GSM8K at low budgets (67.0% vs. 66.1% at 33.3% budget with 336 vs. 409 tokens) and on MATH500 at 33.3% budget (64.4% vs. 55.6%), though the RL model peaks higher at full budget (73.5% vs. 68.3% on MATH500). DiffAdapt thus matches or exceeds RL-trained methods in low-resource regimes using ~35–50% fewer tokens, without any RL training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three lines of work on efficient LLM reasoning: (1) training-based budget control via RL (ThinkPrune, LC-R1, AdaCoT, ThinkLess), (2) inference-time early-exit methods (DEER, AlphaOne), and (3) auxiliary-model switchers (BERT predictors, R1-7B switchers). It departs from all three by training only a tiny MLP probe on the LLM's own prefill hidden states—no base-model fine-tuning, no separate inference-time model—while grounding the three-tier strategy design in a newly characterized U-shaped entropy pattern across difficulty levels. The empirical overthinking analysis (Section 3) extends prior qualitative observations (Ma et al., Sui et al.) with a quantitative entropy-based formulation that directly motivates the Easy/Normal/Hard partition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: up to 22.4% token reduction and a 6× end-to-end latency speedup (64→10 min) with no LLM retraining, making it a drop-in efficiency layer compatible with vLLM, KV caching, and existing RL-trained models. Key limitations include the prefill-only design (ignoring generation-time dynamics), the need for per-model threshold calibration (though shown to be robust across families), and the coarse three-level granularity. Open questions include whether incorporating early generation signals (e.g., step-wise entropy) could improve classification, whether continuous difficulty scoring would outperform discrete tiers, and whether the approach transfers to non-mathematical reasoning domains without re-labeling.
