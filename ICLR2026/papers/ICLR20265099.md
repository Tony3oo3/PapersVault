## Problem

Deploying vision-and-language navigation (VLN) agents across diverse scenes and illumination conditions (low-light, overexposure, scattering) causes catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuning on one scenario degrades performance on others. Existing parameter-efficient adapters like LoRA and its MoE variants represent knowledge in two-dimensional matrices, which cannot capture the multi-hierarchical structure of navigation knowledge spanning both scene-specific and environment-specific factors simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265099/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes an RGB video stream observation (encoded by a CLIP vision encoder) and a natural language navigation instruction at each step, producing one of four discrete low-level actions (FORWARD 0.25 m, TURN LEFT 15°, TURN RIGHT 15°, STOP) in a continuous Habitat environment. Critically, during inference the task identity is agnostic: the agent must autonomously identify the correct scene and environment experts by matching CLIP features of the current observation against stored retrieval features via cosine similarity, then reconstruct the appropriate adapter weight on the fly.

## Method

TuKA lifts LoRA-style adaptation into a fourth-order tensor X ∈ R^(a_l × b_l × M × N) and applies Tucker decomposition to decouple multi-hierarchical knowledge: a shared core tensor G encodes common navigation skills, factor matrices U^1 and U^2 serve as shared decoder and encoder, while U^3 (M rows) and U^4 (N rows) store scene-specific and environment-specific experts respectively. For a given scenario (scene s, environment e), the 2D adaptation weight ΔW is reconstructed by contracting the core tensor with the selected expert rows, aligning the high-order representation back to the LLM's matrix backbone. The Decoupled Knowledge Incremental Learning (DKIL) strategy applies EWC regularization on shared subspaces (G, U^1, U^2) to consolidate cross-task knowledge, imposes consistency constraints on previously learned expert rows, enforces orthogonality on newly trained experts to prevent interference, and freezes all non-current experts to preserve prior knowledge. At inference, CLIP-based feature matching selects the appropriate scene and environment expert rows, enabling a single compact adapter (~0.3 M parameters) to serve all 24+ scenarios without storing per-task weights.

## Results

On the Allday-Habitat benchmark (24 sequential navigation tasks spanning 5 simulation scenes × 4 environments + 2 real-world scenes × 2 environments), AlldayWalker achieves an average success rate (SR) of 65%, outperforming the strongest baseline SD-LoRA (56%) by 9 points. In terms of forgetting resistance, AlldayWalker attains an average SR forgetting rate (F-SR) of 11%, compared to 18% for SD-LoRA and 23% for O-LoRA. The advantage is most pronounced over MoE-LoRA variants: HydraLoRA (38% SR, 46% F-SR) and BranchLoRA (44% SR, 36% F-SR) trail by 27 and 21 SR points respectively. On generalization to 6 completely unseen scenarios, AlldayWalker reaches 55% average SR versus 39% for SD-LoRA and 40% for BranchLoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlldayWalker outperforms all 11 baselines on both SR and F-SR. The closest competitor is SD-LoRA (56% SR, 18% F-SR), which adaptively composes previously learned LoRA modules; AlldayWalker leads by 9 SR points and 7 F-SR points. O-LoRA (52% SR, 23% F-SR) uses orthogonal loss for disentangled task representations but still lags by 13 SR points. Among MoE-LoRA variants, BranchLoRA (44% SR) and HydraLoRA (38% SR) share a global A matrix for common knowledge, yet their two-dimensional matrix form limits multi-hierarchical knowledge capture. Regularization-based methods (EWC-LoRA at 15% SR, LwF-LoRA at 12% SR) and sequential fine-tuning (Seq-FT at 11% SR) suffer severe catastrophic forgetting (F-SR > 79%). Test-time adaptation methods (FeedTTA 50% SR, FSTTA 44% SR) do not accumulate knowledge across tasks, explaining their inferior lifelong performance despite competitive single-scene adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning lineage from LoRA and its MoE variants (HydraLoRA, BranchLoRA, Dense/Sparse MoLE) by replacing the two-dimensional matrix representation of task knowledge with a fourth-order tensor decomposed via Tucker factorization. The base agent is StreamVLN built on Qwen2-7B with CLIP vision encoding, and the continual learning strategy draws on EWC-style Fisher information regularization and orthogonal subspace constraints. The key conceptual departure is that existing LoRA-based methods can only represent a two-hierarchical structure (one shared matrix plus task-specific matrices), whereas TuKA explicitly decouples scene-specific, environment-specific, and shared core knowledge into distinct tensor modes, enabling a principled multi-hierarchical representation that aligns with the natural factorization of navigation scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AML-VLN formulation and Allday-Habitat benchmark address a practical gap: real-world VLN agents must operate across diverse scenes and illumination conditions simultaneously, yet prior work treated these as separate single-task adaptation problems. The tensor-based decoupling offers a reusable architectural pattern for any multi-hierarchical continual learning problem where knowledge naturally factors along multiple axes. Limitations include reliance on CLIP-based expert retrieval at inference (which may fail for truly novel scenes), evaluation primarily in simulation with only two real-world scenes, and the assumption that the number of scenes and environments is bounded and known a priori. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-ended scene discovery and whether the tensor rank selection generalizes across backbone architectures.
