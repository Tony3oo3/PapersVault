## Problem

Existing DPO-based hallucination mitigation in LVLMs relies on preference pairs that are vision-agnostic (constructed from textual edits alone) and off-policy (sourced from external human or AI feedback), making them poorly targeted at the actual visual processing failures. The paper identifies two underexplored Perceptual Processing deficits—a Perceptual Bottleneck where the model correctly localizes attention yet still hallucinates, and a Lack of Robustness where minor image degradation triggers hallucination—and shows that standard preference data provides insufficient gradient signal to address either.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text question as input and produces a text answer. During data generation, the reference model's attention maps are extracted to derive three visual variants of the input image (an enhanced version with a salient crop appended, a degraded version with the salient region erased, and a Gaussian-noised version), from which two orthogonal preference pairs are constructed entirely from the model's own outputs. Training uses LoRA fine-tuning on LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL backbones, with prompts sourced from RLHF-V but no human preference labels.

## Method

P²-DPO is a self-correcting DPO framework that generates on-policy, vision-aware preference pairs without external annotation. For the Perceptual Bottleneck, it uses attention-guided adaptive cropping to build Focus-and-Enhance pairs contrasting outputs from an enhanced input (original + salient crop) versus a degraded input (salient region erased); for Visual Robustness, it contrasts outputs from a clean image decoded via Contrastive Amplification against outputs from a Gaussian-noised image. A novel Calibration Loss is derived from a Bradley-Terry preference model over Perceptual Confidence Gain (the log-probability difference between enhanced and degraded contexts), which provably maximizes Visual Information Dependency of the winning response. A Dynamic Deficit-Weighting mechanism uses CLIP scores between the prompt and the cropped region versus the full image to adaptively rebalance the two loss components per sample, and the entire objective is grounded in an information-theoretic analysis showing that visually-grounded contrastive pairs produce larger Fisher information on vision-dominant parameters than post-hoc textual corrections.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, P²-DPO achieves a POPE average F1 of 87.44 (+2.34 over base), HallusionBench qAcc of 26.37 (+12.47), fAcc of 30.05 (+9.82), and aAcc of 55.62 (+7.46), while boosting AMBER relational reasoning F1_R by +8.5 points to 70.9. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, it reaches POPE Avg 88.21 (+1.31) and HallusionBench qAcc 36.70 (+4.83). The method also demonstrates superior robustness, outperforming LLaVA-1.5-7B by over 4 F1 points on POPE under Gaussian noise at σ=0.20, and achieves an Attention Focus Ratio (AFR) of 18.71 with 70.10% Processing Accuracy on TextVQA, confirming targeted mitigation of the perceptual bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P²-DPO outperforms all four baselines on LLaVA-1.5-7B across every reported metric. Against the strongest human-feedback baseline V-DPO_RLHF-V, it gains +0.16 on POPE Avg, +9.01 on HallusionBench qAcc, +10.11 on fAcc, and +6.8 on AMBER F1_R, while using no human annotations. Compared to the AI-feedback method HA-DPO, P²-DPO leads by +0.58 on POPE Avg and +2.1 on AMBER F1_R. Against the training-free VCD baseline, P²-DPO improves POPE Avg by +2.93 and also reports HallusionBench scores where VCD provides none. The most dramatic gap is on HallusionBench, where P²-DPO's qAcc of 26.37 exceeds the next-best V-DPO_SAD (22.20) by 4.17 points, confirming that vision-grounded, on-policy preference pairs yield substantially stronger fine-grained discrimination than text-only post-hoc corrections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) to LVLM hallucination mitigation by reframing the problem around 'Perceptual Processing' failures—specifically a bottleneck in attended regions and insufficient robustness to image degradation—rather than the more commonly studied perception-level failures. It departs from prior DPO-based alignment methods (V-DPO, HA-DPO, RLHF-V) by arguing that their preference pairs are vision-agnostic and off-policy, and instead generates on-policy, vision-aware pairs through attention-guided cropping (inspired by Zhang et al., 2025) and contrastive amplification decoding (building on Leng et al., 2024). The Calibration Loss and Dynamic Deficit-Weighting mechanism are novel additions that go beyond standard DPO's correlational signal to enforce causal visual grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: eliminating the need for costly human or AI-annotated preference labels while matching or exceeding their performance on hallucination benchmarks makes DPO-based alignment significantly more scalable for LVLMs. The 'perceptual processing' framing—where the model correctly localizes attention but still hallucinates—offers a useful diagnostic lens that could guide future targeted interventions. However, the approach is tightly coupled to attention-map extraction and CLIP-based scoring, raising questions about portability to architectures without interpretable attention (e.g., state-space models) or to video. The theoretical grounding via VID and Fisher Information, while suggestive, remains largely heuristic and would benefit from tighter formal guarantees.
