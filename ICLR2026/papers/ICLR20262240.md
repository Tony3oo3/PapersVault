## Problem

Preference-based reward learning relies on binary comparisons that convey at most one bit of information, leaving the reward model free to latch onto spurious, non-causal features that merely co-occur with preferred trajectories. When these correlations shift or reverse at test time, the learned reward misidentifies the true causal feature and the downstream policy fails. Existing remedies either require structured, task-specific feature definitions or treat language as a mere conditioning input, failing to explicitly enforce causal grounding of the preference signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of trajectory segments (each a sequence of state-action pairs of fixed horizon H), a binary preference label, a free-form natural language rationale, and a task description string. Trajectories are encoded via a modality-appropriate encoder (convolutional for 128×128 RGB frames, fully connected for proprioceptive states) into per-step embeddings that are summed over time; the rationale and task description are mapped to fixed-dimensional vectors by a frozen T5 language model. The output is a decomposed reward function r(τ, ℓ_task) = r_∥ + r_⊥ that scores trajectories, which is then used to label offline play data for training downstream RL policies.

## Method

ReCouPLe treats the frozen rationale embedding ψ as a projection axis in a shared trajectory-language space, orthogonally decomposing the trainable trajectory embedding φ(τ) into a reason-aligned component φ_∥ (parallel to ψ) and a reason-orthogonal component φ_⊥. The reward r(τ, ℓ_task) = φ(τ)ᵀθ is correspondingly split into r_∥ (causal, explained by the rationale) and r_⊥ (residual task signal). Training minimizes three terms: a Bradley-Terry BCE loss applied to r_∥ alone (forcing the preference to be explained through the stated causal feature), an orthogonal consistency loss that either enforces r_⊥(τ_A) ≈ r_⊥(τ_B) (ReCouPLe-EC) or incentivizes r_∥ differences to dominate r_⊥ differences (ReCouPLe-IC), and a reward-ratio regularizer that caps |r_∥|/|r_∥+r_⊥| below a threshold α to prevent trivial collapse into the causal subspace. Because the same T5 encoder is shared across all tasks, semantically identical rationales (e.g., 'avoids collisions') project onto the same causal direction, enabling zero-shot reward transfer to novel tasks without additional preference queries or language-model fine-tuning.

## Results

On the ManiSkill causal-confusion benchmark (2-task, color-swapped OOD), ReCouPLe-EC achieves 0.820 reward accuracy on Pick and 0.940 on Place, compared to 0.540/0.830 for single-task BT and 0.600/0.820 for BT-Multi — a roughly 1.5× improvement over the strongest baseline on the hardest task. On the 4-task OOD setting, ReCouPLe-EC reaches 0.773–1.000 across all four tasks versus 0.707–1.000 for BT-Multi. For cross-task transfer on Meta-World, ReCouPLe-EC attains 0.663 reward accuracy on the held-out Pick-Place task, outperforming BT-Multi (0.547) and RFP (0.553) by 0.11–0.12 absolute. Downstream offline RL policy success on the OOD ManiSkill environments and the novel Meta-World task follows the same ordering, confirming that reward accuracy gains translate to policy performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines that share the same multi-task reward formulation (trajectory encoder × frozen T5 task embedding) but differ in how they handle rationales. **BT-Multi** (standard multi-task Bradley-Terry, no rationale input) is the weakest baseline: on the ManiSkill 2-task OOD Pick task it scores 0.600, while ReCouPLe-EC reaches 0.820 (+0.22) and ReCouPLe-IC reaches 0.633 (+0.03). On the Meta-World novel Pick-Place task, BT-Multi achieves 0.547 versus ReCouPLe-EC's 0.663 (+0.12). **RFP** (Reason-Feature Preference, an adaptation of Peng et al. 2024 that adds an auxiliary BCE loss on the rationale score) provides only modest gains over BT-Multi: 0.620 on ManiSkill Pick OOD and 0.553 on Meta-World novel task, both still well below ReCouPLe-EC (0.820 and 0.663 respectively). ReCouPLe-EC consistently outperforms both baselines on OOD/distribution-shift settings, while all methods saturate near 1.0 on in-distribution splits. ReCouPLe-IC is less effective than ReCouPLe-EC on ManiSkill (where a single causal feature dominates) but performs comparably or slightly better on Meta-World (where multiple diverse reasons co-occur), confirming the paper's hypothesis about when each orthogonal-consistency variant is preferable.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReCouPLe extends the standard Bradley-Terry preference learning framework (Bradley & Terry, 1952; Christiano et al., 2017) by introducing a new data modality—free-form natural language rationales—and a corresponding orthogonal-projection loss that decomposes trajectory representations into reason-aligned and reason-orthogonal subspaces. It departs from prior language-in-robotics work (Yang et al., 2024; Holk et al., 2024) that either treats language as a conditioning input or relies on predefined feature sets, and from Peng et al. (2024) which requires structured, task-specific features. The key conceptual departure is using the frozen language embedding of a rationale directly as a geometric projection axis in a shared trajectory-language space, enabling causal disentanglement without hand-designed features or LLM-based feature extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Causal confusion in preference-based reward learning is a well-documented failure mode (Tien et al., 2023) that undermines real-world deployment, and ReCouPLe offers a lightweight, training-time-only fix that requires no architecture changes beyond a shared encoder. The zero-shot task transfer via shared rationales is practically attractive for multi-task robot learning where preference annotation is expensive. However, the method assumes rationales are readily available and reliable, evaluation is restricted to simulation, and the two-stage pipeline (reward learning + offline RL) adds latency. Open questions include active rationale querying, robustness to noisy or adversarial rationales, and whether the projection-based decomposition generalizes to continuous or multi-objective reward settings.
