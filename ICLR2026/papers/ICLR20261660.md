## Problem

Standard Riemannian gradient descent requires global L-smoothness and prior knowledge of the smoothness constant L, assumptions that are restrictive on curved manifolds and exclude important applications like Gaussian variational inference with non-L-smooth potentials. While adaptive, line-search-free methods have emerged in Euclidean space, their extension to Riemannian optimization remains largely unexplored, and no prior method provides non-ergodic O(1/k) convergence guarantees for adaptive Riemannian algorithms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261660/cropped_page9_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a continuously differentiable function on a geodesically convex subset of a Riemannian manifold, an initial point x₀, an initial step size s₀, and three design sequences {A_k}, {B_k}, {B̃_k} that encode curvature information. For the GVI application, the input is a convex potential V satisfying a polynomial-exponential growth condition, and the output is a sequence of Gaussian parameters (mean m_k, covariance Σ_k) on the Bures-Wasserstein manifold. The step size is fully self-adaptive—no line search or user-specified L is needed—and the method requires only that exponential maps, Riemannian gradients, and parallel transports be computable.

## Method

RAdaGD approximates the local smoothness parameter at each iteration using consecutive iterates via a Riemannian analogue of the Euclidean smoothness estimator (Eq. 5), then selects the next step size as a minimum of two candidate rates governed by sequences A_k, B_k, and B̃_k. The key theoretical contribution is a Lyapunov analysis where B̃_k is introduced specifically to absorb the curvature-dependent parameter ζ_k = ζ(d(x_k, x_★)), enabling convergence on possibly negatively curved manifolds. The analysis requires only local geodesic smoothness (satisfied by all C² functions on complete manifolds) and generalized geodesic convexity, yielding a non-ergodic O(1/k) function-value rate and O(1/k²) gradient-norm rate. For GVI, BWAdaGVI adds a step-size upper bound involving the largest eigenvalue of E[∇²V] − Σ⁻¹ to handle the geodesic incompleteness of BW(ℝⁿ), providing the first convergence guarantee for GVI without assuming global L-smoothness of the target log-density.

## Results

The primary contribution is theoretical: RAdaGD achieves a non-ergodic convergence rate of f(x_k) − f_* ≤ O(L/k) and a gradient norm rate of min‖Grad f(x_i)‖² ≤ O(L²/k²), which is the first such guarantee for a Riemannian adaptive method. In the GVI application (BWAdaGVI), the method provides the first convergence guarantee for Gaussian variational inference without requiring global L-smoothness of the target log-density, under only a mild growth condition (16). Empirically, on Poisson regression GVI (ℓ=50, n=25), RAdaGD outperforms FBGVI (Diao et al., 2023) in objective value F(μ_k) − F_min across all tested step sizes s ∈ {1, 1/2, 1/4}, while maintaining minimum eigenvalues λ_min(Σ_k) bounded away from zero throughout iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RAdaGD (BWAdaGVI) against FBGVI (Diao et al., 2023), the state-of-the-art forward-backward Gaussian variational inference method, on a Poisson regression target with ℓ=50 observations and n=25 features. RAdaGD outperforms FBGVI in the objective gap F(μ_k) − F_min across all three step sizes tested (s=1, 1/2, 1/4), as shown in Figure 1 (left panel). Importantly, FBGVI requires global L-smoothness of the potential V for its theoretical guarantees, which the Poisson regression potential V(θ) = Σ(exp(X_i^T θ) − Y_i X_i^T θ) does not satisfy due to its exponential growth; RAdaGD, by contrast, only requires the mild growth condition (16) and convexity, making it the first method with provable convergence for this problem. The right panel of Figure 1 confirms that the minimum eigenvalues of the covariance iterates remain bounded away from zero, validating the technical assumption in Corollary 5.4. No other baselines (e.g., Lambert et al. 2022, Kim et al. 2023) are included in the direct numerical comparison, though the paper notes that all prior GVI algorithms require L-smoothness and thus lack guarantees for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAdaGD extends the Euclidean adaptive gradient descent framework of Suh & Ma (2025) to general Riemannian manifolds, requiring nontrivial modifications to handle the curvature-dependent parameter ζ and the geometry of parallel transport. It departs from standard Riemannian gradient descent (Zhang & Sra, 2016) by replacing the global L-smoothness assumption with local geodesic smoothness (satisfied by all C² functions) and by eliminating the need for prior knowledge of L through an adaptive step-size rule. For the GVI application, it builds on the Bures-Wasserstein geometric framework of Lambert et al. (2022) and Diao et al. (2023), but is the first to provide convergence guarantees when the target potential V lacks global L-smoothness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first adaptive Riemannian method with a non-ergodic O(1/k) convergence rate and the first GVI algorithm with provable guarantees under only local smoothness, broadening the class of solvable Bayesian inference problems (e.g., Poisson regression with exponential potentials). A key open question is whether the additional O(ζ̄) factor in the convergence rate relative to non-adaptive RGD is an inherent cost of adaptivity or an artifact of the analysis. Future directions include stochastic variants, Nesterov-type acceleration on manifolds, and proximal extensions, all of which the authors identify as natural next steps.
