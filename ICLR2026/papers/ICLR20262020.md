## Problem

Existing Mamba-based 3D detectors encode all non-empty voxels, yet ~80% of the scene is uninformative background. Directly restricting encoding to foreground-only voxels degrades performance because sparse, spatially scattered foreground sequences suffer from response attenuation and restricted context representation in linear autoregressive models. The paper asks how to achieve efficient foreground-focused encoding while preserving long-range inter-instance dependencies and rich semantic-geometric context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262020/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes LiDAR point clouds voxelized into 3D grids (L×H×W×D) and produces 3D bounding boxes with class labels. Internally, it predicts per-voxel foreground scores (supervised with focal loss) and semantic category labels, then selects the top-k voxels (default α=0.2) and flattens them into 1D sequences via Hilbert curves under multiple Z-axis rotations (θ=0, π/2). The encoded foreground features are concatenated with the retained background features to form the final backbone output fed to the detection head.

## Method

Fore-Mamba3D replaces the full-voxel Mamba encoder with a three-stage foreground-enhanced pipeline. First, a submanifold convolution predicts foreground scores; the top-k voxels are flattened via Hilbert curves with two Z-axis rotations to mitigate regional truncation. Second, a Regional-to-Global Sliding Window (RGSW) divides the sequence into patches, inserts local tokens to aggregate regional context, then iteratively slides overlapping windows across patches to propagate information globally without bidirectional encoding. Third, SASF-Mamba enriches the state variables h through Semantic-Assisted Fusion (reordering h by predicted semantic categories, applying a 1D convolution with kernel K=7, then reversing) and State Spatial Fusion (mapping h into a sparse 3D tensor, applying large-kernel dimension-wise convolution along each axis, then flattening back), enabling non-causal, semantically and geometrically aware encoding. The backbone is trained end-to-end with focal losses on foreground scores and semantic categories (weight w=2) alongside standard cross-entropy and smooth-L1 detection losses.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, Fore-Mamba3D achieves 68.4 mAP and 72.3 NDS, setting a new state-of-the-art among LiDAR-only methods and surpassing LION (68.0 mAP, 72.1 NDS) and Voxel-Mamba (67.5 mAP, 71.9 NDS). On the nuScenes test set, it reaches 70.1 mAP and 74.0 NDS. On KITTI validation, Fore-Mamba3D yields 90.3/82.2/79.5 AP (Car Hard/Mod/Easy), outperforming VoxelMamba by an average of 1.7%. On Waymo (20% training split), it achieves 71.9% mAP at L2, exceeding CenterPoint by 7.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fore-Mamba3D outperforms all 23 compared baselines across the three benchmarks. On nuScenes validation, it leads LION by 0.4 mAP / 0.2 NDS and Voxel-Mamba by 0.9 mAP / 0.4 NDS, while maintaining competitive per-category scores (e.g., 88.7 Car vs. 88.7 for FSHNet, 89.3 Ped. tied with Voxel-Mamba and FSHNet). On KITTI validation, it surpasses VoxelMamba (the strongest Mamba baseline) by 1.2 AP on Car Hard (90.3 vs. 89.1) and 2.5 AP on Ped. Mod (62.2 vs. 59.7), and beats Transformer-based DSVT by 2.5 AP on Car Hard. On Waymo, Fore-Mamba3D achieves 71.9% mAP L2, exceeding CenterPoint (64.5%) by 7.4% and Voxel-RCNN (68.6%) by 3.3%, while also leading all L1 metrics (e.g., 82.1 Ped. AP L1 vs. 78.2 for Voxel-RCNN). The method also demonstrates efficiency gains: at α=0.2 sampling ratio it reduces FLOPs by 43.7% and increases FPS by 23.9% compared to LION, while simultaneously improving detection accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mamba-based 3D detection line initiated by LION (group-based) and Voxel-Mamba (group-free), both of which encode all non-empty voxels via bidirectional SSM scanning. The key conceptual departure is restricting linear encoding to a foreground-only subset (~20% of voxels), which prior work found degrades performance due to response attenuation across sparse, multi-instance sequences. To recover the lost global interaction, the authors introduce a regional-to-global sliding window (RGSW) that replaces bidirectional encoding, and a SASFMamba module that explicitly injects semantic and geometric structure into the SSM state variables—departing from the treatment of state variables as opaque in prior Mamba-3D methods. The detection frameworks (TransFusion, SECOND, CenterPoint) serve as plug-in hosts rather than architectural foundations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that encoding only 20% of voxels can outperform full-sequence encoding while cutting FLOPs by ~44% offers a practical efficiency-accuracy tradeoff for real-time LiDAR perception, and the RGSW strategy provides a general recipe for overcoming causal limitations in autoregressive encoders without resorting to bidirectional passes. The theoretical analysis showing how semantic rearrangement of state variables enables non-causal long-range interaction is a useful formal tool that could transfer to other SSM-based architectures. Open questions include whether the foreground prediction head's accuracy is sufficient in extremely cluttered or novel scenes, whether the approach generalizes to other SSM variants (RetNet, RWKV), and whether the Hilbert-curve-plus-rotation serialization remains optimal as scene complexity grows.
