## Problem

Unified semantic-controlled video generation—where the condition shares semantics with the target but lacks pixel-wise alignment (e.g., style, motion, camera, concept)—remains fragmented. Existing approaches either enforce inappropriate pixel-wise mapping priors from structure-based controls, require costly per-condition fine-tuning, or rely on task-specific architectures, none of which yield a single generalizable model with zero-shot transfer to unseen semantics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263894/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VAP takes a reference video (480×720, 49 frames at 16 fps) carrying the desired semantics, a reference image providing the initial subject appearance, and paired text captions for both reference and target, and produces a semantically controlled video at the same resolution. The reference video and its caption form an in-context prompt token sequence [Ref_text, Ref_video, Tar_text, Tar_video] processed jointly with the noisy target latents, with first-frame tokens concatenated to video tokens to anchor identity.

## Method

VAP reframes semantic control as in-context generation by treating the reference video as a direct prompt to a frozen Video Diffusion Transformer. A plug-and-play Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) expert—initialized from the backbone and trained on VAP-Data (100K+ paired videos across 100 conditions)—processes the reference tokens while the frozen DiT handles target tokens, with bidirectional full-attention fusion at every layer to enable synchronous reference guidance without catastrophic forgetting. A temporally biased Rotary Position Embedding shifts the reference's temporal indices by a fixed offset Δ (placing it before the target along time while keeping spatial indices fixed), eliminating the spurious pixel-mapping prior that a shared RoPE would impose. Training uses a standard flow-matching velocity prediction loss, and the resulting single model handles concept, style, motion, and camera conditions uniformly while generalizing zero-shot to unseen semantics.

## Results

VAP achieves a 38.7% user preference rate, rivaling the closed-source commercial models Kling/Vidu (38.2%) while serving as a single unified model. On the VAP-Data benchmark, VAP attains the highest CLIP score (24.13), motion smoothness (98.59), and dynamic degree (77.08) among all open-source methods, and outperforms the condition-specific CogVideoX-I2V(LoRA) baseline by 25.6 percentage points in preference rate (38.7% vs. 13.1%). VAP trails Kling/Vidu slightly on aesthetic quality (57.71 vs. 59.16) and semantic alignment score (70.44 vs. 74.02), but leads on text alignment and motion smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA structure-controlled method VACE, VAP dominates on every metric: VACE(Original) scores only 5.88 CLIP and 0.6% preference due to its pixel-aligned prior breaking under semantic conditions, while even VACE(Optical Flow) reaches just 46.71 alignment and 1.8% preference. Versus the condition-specific CogVideoX-I2V(LoRA) baseline (a separate LoRA per semantic condition), VAP wins on CLIP score (24.13 vs. 23.59), dynamic degree (77.08 vs. 70.83), aesthetic quality (57.71 vs. 54.23), and preference rate (38.7% vs. 13.1%), while also achieving higher semantic alignment (70.44 vs. 68.60) with a single unified model. Compared to the closed-source Kling/Vidu commercial models, VAP achieves a marginally higher preference rate (38.7% vs. 38.2%) and better CLIP score (24.13 vs. 24.05) and motion smoothness (98.59 vs. 98.12), but trails on dynamic degree (77.08 vs. 79.17), aesthetic quality (57.71 vs. 59.16), and semantic alignment (70.44 vs. 74.02). The base CogVideoX-I2V without any semantic control achieves only 26.04 alignment and 6.9% preference, confirming that semantic cues in captions alone are insufficient. Overall, VAP is the first unified open-source model to match or exceed condition-specific commercial systems across diverse semantic categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant structure-controlled video generation paradigm (VACE, ControlNet-style adapters) that assumes pixel-aligned conditions, and from condition-specific approaches (per-semantic LoRA fine-tuning, task-specific modules for style/camera/motion) that lack unification. It draws on two key threads: (1) in-context control in diffusion transformers demonstrated for image generation (OmniControl, Tan et al. 2025) and structure-controlled video (FullDiT, Ju et al. 2025), and (2) the Mixture-of-Transformers architecture (Liang et al., 2024) for plug-and-play expert modules. The core conceptual move is reframing semantic-controlled generation—where reference and target share semantics but lack pixel correspondence—as an in-context prompting problem, using a temporally biased RoPE to avoid the spurious pixel-mapping prior that plagues shared-position-embedding designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VAP provides the first single unified model that handles concept, style, motion, and camera semantic control simultaneously, with zero-shot transfer to unseen semantic conditions—a capability no prior condition-specific or task-specific method offers. The released VAP-Data (100K+ paired videos, 100 conditions) is a significant resource that was previously absent from the literature. Key open questions remain: the dataset is entirely synthetic (derived from commercial VFX templates and community LoRAs), so VAP may inherit source-model biases; caption quality and subject structural similarity critically affect performance; multi-reference prompting leaks unwanted appearance cues; and inference cost roughly doubles due to the parallel expert. Whether the in-context video-prompt paradigm scales to real-world, diverse semantic data without task-specific modifications is the central open question.
