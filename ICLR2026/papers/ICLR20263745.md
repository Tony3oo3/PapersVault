## Problem

Classical time-frequency front-ends for speech emotion recognition (STFT, fixed wavelets) impose non-adaptable resolution trade-offs, and prior superlet variants rely on integer orders with hand-tuned hyperparameters that cannot be optimized end-to-end. No existing front-end provides a fully differentiable, learnable continuum of fractional-order superlet transforms with jointly optimized frequency grids, cycle schedules, and order mixtures tailored to the emotion classification objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263745/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes raw, peak-normalized audio waveforms at 16 kHz (IEMOCAP, EMO-DB) or 8 kHz (NSPL-CRISE) with variable utterance durations handled via batch-wise padding and explicit masks. It produces a final emotion class label (4–7 classes depending on corpus) along with intermediate 96-band time-frequency representations: a magnitude map S and a phase-congruency map κ, both of shape [B, 96, T], plus per-band effective fractional orders o_eff that condition downstream processing. The STEE encoder is deliberately compact (128 channels, depthwise/1×1 convolutions, local 1D attention), trading parameter efficiency for the richer front-end computation of multi-order complex convolutions.

## Method

LFST formulates a learnable continuum of multiplicative wavelet ensembles: at each of 96 log-spaced frequency bands, DC-corrected analytic Morlet filters at O=8 discrete orders are convolved with the input, and their magnitudes are aggregated via a softmax-weighted log-domain geometric mean, yielding a smooth effective order o_eff ∈ [1,8] per band. The frequency grid (monotone log-spaced via softplus-normalized increments), per-band base cycles (≥1 via softplus), and order logits are all learned end-to-end. A weighted phase-congruency channel κ is computed from order-weighted unit phasors, and a Learnable Asymmetric Hard Thresholding (LAHT) module sparsifies the magnitude map. The compact STEE encoder processes the two-channel [S, κ] input through depthwise temporal/spectral residual blocks, TF-hybrid blocks with squeeze-excitation, an Adaptive FiLM gate conditioned on per-frequency statistics of S, κ, and o_eff, local axial time-attention, and attentive statistics pooling, all trained jointly with focal loss under AdamW with mixed precision.

## Results

LFST+STEE achieves 87.5% accuracy and 86.8% macro-F1 on IEMOCAP (4-class), 91.4% accuracy and 90.4% macro-F1 on EMO-DB (7-class), and 76.9% accuracy and 76.6% macro-F1 on NSPL-CRISE (5-class, 8 kHz telephony). All EMO-DB classes exceed 0.83 F1, and Cohen's κ reaches 0.898 on EMO-DB and 0.833 on IEMOCAP. Under capacity-matched ablations (same STEE backbone), LFST outperforms STFT, CWT, fixed superlet, and LEAF front-ends by 2–4 percentage points in accuracy across all three datasets, with the largest gap on noisy NSPL-CRISE (+3.8 pp over the best baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFST+STEE outperforms all nine compared baselines on every dataset where they overlap. On IEMOCAP, it beats the strongest prior method (Li et al., 81.6% Acc) by 5.9 pp and the weakest (Zhao et al., 52.1%) by 35.4 pp; on NSPL-CRISE it surpasses Li et al. (68.7%) by 8.2 pp and Mirsamadi et al. (51.3%) by 25.6 pp. On EMO-DB, it exceeds Liu et al. (89.13%) by 2.3 pp in accuracy and Tuncer et al. (88.35%) by 3.1 pp. Against the learnable-filterbank baseline LEAF (Zeghidour & Grangier, 2021) under the identical STEE encoder, LFST gains +4.4 pp accuracy on NSPL-CRISE (76.9 vs. 72.5), +2.6 pp on IEMOCAP (87.5 vs. 84.9), and +2.4 pp on EMO-DB (91.4 vs. 89.0), indicating that the fractional superlet mechanism provides a consistent advantage over generic learnable filterbanks. The capacity-matched ablations further show that the front-end choice (not the encoder) drives the gap: STFT, CWT, fixed superlet, and LEAF all fall within a 2.4 pp band, while LFST extends beyond them by 2–4 pp.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the superlet framework (Moca et al., 2021) and its fractional-order generalization (Bârzan et al., 2021) by making the entire transform differentiable and learnable end-to-end. Where prior superlets used fixed, hand-tuned cycle schedules and fractional superlets only mixed adjacent integer orders (piecewise), LFST learns a full softmax simplex over all orders at each frequency, a monotone log-frequency grid, and per-band base cycles simultaneously. It also draws on the learnable front-end lineage (LEAF, SincNet) and wavelet-based SER work (SigWavNet, multi-level wavelet packet networks), but departs by using multiplicative log-domain geometric aggregation rather than additive filterbank mixing, and by introducing a phase-congruency channel and asymmetric hard-thresholding module not present in prior superlet formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that learning the TF analysis front-end itself—rather than treating it as a fixed preprocessing step—yields consistent gains over STFT, CWT, fixed superlets, and LEAF under a controlled encoder, with the phase-congruency channel (κ) contributing the largest single-component improvement (+9.7 pp on noisy telephony data). However, the computational cost is substantial (201.5 GFLOPs, 74.9 ms latency, 4.5 GB peak memory vs. 0.36 GFLOPs for STFT), which limits practical deployment and raises the open question of whether the learned TF structure can be distilled or approximated more cheaply. The authors explicitly do not compare against fully fine-tuned self-supervised models (wav2vec 2.0, HuBERT), leaving open whether LFST's structured, interpretable front-end can complement or replace SSL pretraining in a unified pipeline. Cross-lingual transfer and in-the-wild generalization remain untested.
