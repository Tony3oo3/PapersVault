## Problem

Existing editing-based concept erasure methods for diffusion models rely on weighted least-squares optimization that provably admits a non-zero lower bound on preservation error, which accumulates as the number of target concepts grows, causing progressive degradation of non-target semantics. Training-based alternatives avoid this but are prohibitively slow for large-scale multi-concept erasure (e.g., 100+ concepts). The paper addresses how to achieve scalable, precise erasure with zero preservation error while remaining computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the value projection matrices in cross-attention layers of a pretrained T2I diffusion model (e.g., SDv1.4, SDXL, SDv3). It consumes CLIP-encoded concept embeddings for target concepts (to be erased), anchor concepts (shared semantics they map to), and a retain set of non-target concepts, plus invariant embeddings ([SOT] token, null-text). The output is a closed-form parameter update ΔP applied to the value matrices, requiring no iterative training; the entire pipeline (including null-space computation and refinement) completes in ~5 seconds for 100 concepts.

## Method

SPEED formulates concept erasure as a null-space constrained optimization: parameter updates are projected onto the null space of the retain set's correlation matrix C₀C₀ᵀ (via SVD), guaranteeing zero preservation error (e₀=0) by construction. The key challenge is that a large retain set drives C₀C₀ᵀ toward full rank, shrinking the null space and forcing approximate projections that degrade semantics. To resolve this, SPEED introduces Prior Knowledge Refinement with three complementary techniques: (1) Influence-Based Prior Filtering (IPF) computes a closed-form erasure-only update Δ_erase and prunes the retain set to only highly-affected concepts, keeping the correlation matrix rank-deficient; (2) Directed Prior Augmentation (DPA) expands the filtered set with perturbations projected onto the smallest-singular-value directions of W, adding semantically consistent coverage without inflating rank; (3) Invariant Equality Constraints (IEC) impose hard equality constraints on invariant embeddings ([SOT], null-text) via Lagrange multipliers. The final update is a closed-form expression (Eq. 11) combining the null-space projection P, an erasure term, and a constraint-enforcing projection Q, all computed in a single matrix solve.

## Results

On multi-concept erasure of 100 celebrities, SPEED achieves the best overall erasure performance H_o of 89.63% and the highest non-target preservation Acc_r of 85.54%, while erasing all 100 concepts in just 5 seconds—a 350× speedup over MACE (~30 min). On MS-COCO general prior preservation, SPEED attains the lowest FID (44.97) and highest CLIP Score (26.22) among all methods. For few-concept erasure (e.g., erasing Snoopy), SPEED achieves the best non-target FID of 19.95 on MS-COCO, outperforming UCE (22.18), RECE (25.61), and MACE (42.62).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPEED outperforms all four baselines on prior preservation (Acc_r and MS-COCO FID) across all multi-concept settings. Against MACE (Lu et al., 2024a), the closest competitor, SPEED achieves higher Acc_r (85.54% vs. 80.20%) and H_o (89.63 vs. 87.06) when erasing 100 celebrities, while running 350× faster (5 s vs. 1736 s); MACE has a marginally lower Acc_e (4.80% vs. 5.87%), indicating slightly more aggressive erasure. Against UCE (Gandikota et al., 2024) and RECE (Gong et al., 2025), SPEED's advantage is dramatic: both methods suffer catastrophic prior degradation at scale (Acc_r drops to 20.92% and 23.71% respectively for 100 celebrities, with MS-COCO FIDs of 185.46 and 177.57), whereas SPEED maintains Acc_r of 85.54% and FID of 44.97. Against ConAbl (Kumari et al., 2023), SPEED dominates on every metric for 100 celebrities (H_o 89.63 vs. 57.97) and is 1800× faster (5 s vs. 9000 s). On few-concept erasure (Table 1), SPEED consistently achieves the lowest non-target FID (e.g., 19.95 vs. UCE's 22.18 for erasing Snoopy), though its target CS is not always the lowest—RECE achieves lower CS (18.38 vs. 23.50) but at the cost of over-erasure that degrades non-target quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPEED extends the null-space projection paradigm from AlphaEdit (Fang et al., 2024), which was originally designed for language model knowledge editing, to diffusion model concept erasure. It departs from the standard weighted least-squares formulation of UCE (Gandikota et al., 2024) by replacing the soft preservation penalty with a hard null-space constraint that provably forces preservation error to zero. The key conceptual departure is identifying a fundamental dilemma in the existing null-space approach—larger retain sets drive the feature matrix toward full rank, making null-space estimation inaccurate—and addressing it through the Prior Knowledge Refinement suite (IPF, DPA, IEC), which strategically refines the retain set before null-space construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 350× speedup (5 seconds for 100 concepts vs. ~30 minutes for MACE) makes large-scale, real-time concept erasure practically viable for deployment scenarios requiring rapid response to new safety concerns. The theoretical proof that UCE's preservation error admits a non-zero lower bound provides a principled justification for null-space constraints over weighted optimization, clarifying a trade-off that was previously only observed empirically. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond cross-attention value matrix editing, how it behaves when the retain set is extremely large (approaching full rank even after IPF), and whether the linear parameter-update assumption captures all relevant interactions in deeper or transformer-based architectures.
