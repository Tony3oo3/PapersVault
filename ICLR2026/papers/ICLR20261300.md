## Problem

Existing protein structure tokenizers (PSTs) rely on fixed-size vector-quantized codebooks that lack interpretability, multi-scale resolution control, and hierarchical structure. No geometric analog of byte-pair encoding exists that can discretize continuous, noisy backbone conformations into symbolic representations while preserving global fold consistency, limiting their utility for multimodal protein models that require joint reasoning over sequence, structure, and function.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261300/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes protein backbone Cartesian coordinates (N, CA, C per residue) and optionally pretrained ESM3 residue-level embeddings for the transfer-learning variant. It produces a discrete token sequence of structural motifs (variable-length, unlike fixed-size VQ-VAE codes), a hierarchical merge tree encoding multi-resolution decompositions, per-boundary glue angle parameters (θ^CNCA, φ, ω), and a reconstructed backbone. Resolution is elastically controllable: increasing the number of BPE merge steps or vocabulary size trades bits-per-residue for lower distortion along a smooth Pareto front, rather than being locked to a fixed codebook dimension.

## Method

GEOBPE iteratively merges the most frequent Geo-Pair (adjacent motif pair plus boundary glue) by clustering its occurrences with k-medoids to produce K representative prototypes, then hard-quantizes all occurrences to their nearest medoid. Because quantization introduces geometric drift that accumulates across the chain, GEOBPE corrects it via differentiable forward kinematics: it optimizes the three boundary glue angles per link under an SE(3) end-frame loss (weighted rotation + translation), treating all glue DOFs globally in a batch least-squares solve. The algorithm alternates between this local quantization and global rigid-body refinement, with the Geo-Pair dictionary re-synchronized after each step to maintain consistency between discrete keys and continuous backbone states. For downstream transfer (GEOBPE-TRANSFER), the merge tree is used as a recursive computation graph: pretrained ESM3 features at leaf nodes propagate up through parent-child motif aggregations to protein-level roots and back down, yielding residue-level embeddings enriched with multi-scale structural context.

## Results

GEOBPE-TRANSFER achieves the best average AUROC (80.20%) across 12 downstream tasks (24 test splits), outperforming the best continuous baseline MIF (79.82%) and the best discrete baseline AminoASeed (72.43%), with relative gains of +18.13% over ESM3. On structure classification (Macro F1), GEOBPE-TRANSFER reaches 52.21%, a +43.28% improvement over ESM3 (36.44%). For compression, GEOBPE achieves 0.27–0.36× the bits-per-residue of ProToken at comparable distortion, and its OOD generalization (test/train RMSD ratio 1.16–1.28) is dramatically better than VQ-VAE baselines (up to 6.4× degradation). GEOBPE also matches full-data downstream accuracy when trained on only 1% of pretraining structures (~300 PDBs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEOBPE-TRANSFER outperforms all discrete PST baselines (FoldSeek, ProTokens, ESM3, VanillaVQ, AminoASeed) on both AUROC and Macro F1, with the largest gaps over FoldSeek (+28.3% AUROC, +41.7% F1) and ProTokens (+14.8% AUROC, +42.1% F1). Against the strongest continuous baselines, GEOBPE-TRANSFER wins on AUROC (80.20 vs. MIF 79.82, ProteinMPNN 75.92) and Macro F1 (52.21 vs. MIF 43.55, ProteinMPNN 39.94), but trails on Spearman's ρ for flexibility prediction (45.26 vs. ProteinMPNN 54.41, MIF 52.73), suggesting continuous inverse-folding models retain an edge on fine-grained residue-level regression. On the compression-distortion Pareto front (Fig. 4), GEOBPE and ProToken jointly form the frontier, with GEOBPE achieving 0.27–0.36× ProToken's BPR at only 18–22% LDDT loss, while ESM3 requires 48–63× more BPR for comparable distortion. For OOD generalization, GEOBPE's test/train RMSD ratio stays at 1.16–1.28 on CAMEO/CASP14, whereas VQ-VAE-based AminoASeed degrades by up to 6.4×. In the SSLM-Eval generative comparison, GEOBPE tokens yield 99% unique designable backbones with up to 49% higher scTM than VQ-VAE tokens under identical model and data budgets, though VQ-VAE produces 58% more diverse (but less designable) structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEOBPE draws on two distinct lineages: (1) structural alphabets (de Brevern et al., 2000; Camproux et al., 1999) that approximate folds as sequences of fixed-size geometric motifs, and (2) the BPE compression algorithm (Larsson & Moffat, 2002) that iteratively merges frequent symbol pairs into a hierarchical vocabulary. The paper departs from the dominant VQ-VAE-based PST paradigm (FoldSeek, ESM3, ProToken) by replacing fixed-size continuous vector codebooks with variable-length geometric primitives clustered via k-medoids, and by enforcing global SE(3) consistency through differentiable inverse kinematics on boundary glue angles rather than relying on an end-to-end decoder. This makes GEOBPE the first geometry-grounded BPE analog for protein backbones, combining the interpretability of structural alphabets with the hierarchical compression of BPE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GEOBPE offers a principled, interpretable alternative to VQ-VAE tokenizers that simultaneously improves compression (0.27–0.36× BPR of ProToken), OOD generalization (1.16–1.28× test/train RMSD ratio vs. 6.4× for VQ-VAE), and downstream transfer across 12 tasks, while its hierarchical merge trees align with CATH functional families at 99.97% domain recall. The finding that 1% of pretraining data suffices for competitive downstream performance suggests the geometric 'language' of protein folds is low-complexity and learnable from very few examples. Key limitations include the absence of side chains and sequence information, the small-scale (~7.3M) language model used for generative evaluation, and the open question of whether the hierarchical inductive bias provides genuine advantages when integrated into billion-parameter PLMs at ESM3 scale.
