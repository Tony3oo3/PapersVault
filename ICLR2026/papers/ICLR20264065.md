## Problem

Reliable uncertainty estimation on battery-powered microcontrollers (TinyML) remains unsolved: common approaches like deep ensembles, MC dropout, and early-exit ensembles require multiple forward passes, extra branches, or temporal state that exceed the strict flash, RAM, and latency budgets of milliwatt MCUs. This paper asks whether a single-pass, label-free uncertainty signal can be extracted from a network's own internal dynamics without any architectural changes to the backbone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard classification inputs—28×28 grayscale (MNIST), 32×32 RGB (CIFAR-10), 64×64 RGB (TinyImageNet), or 1-second mono 16 kHz audio clips converted to 40×98 log-Mel features (SpeechCommands)—and produces, in a single forward pass, a class label, a calibrated scalar uncertainty score U(x) ∈ [0,1], and an optional binary abstain decision via thresholding. No temporal buffers, repeated evaluations, or online labels are required; the uncertainty is computed entirely from intermediate activations already available during the standard forward pass, keeping inference state-free and constant-memory.

## Method

SNAP-UQ attaches two or three tiny int8 predictor heads at selected backbone depths (typically a mid block and the penultimate block). Each head takes a low-rank projection (1×1 pointwise convolution + global average pooling) of the previous layer's activation and outputs per-channel mean and log-variance, defining a diagonal-Gaussian conditional density p(a_ℓ | a_{ℓ−1}). The standardized squared residual between the realized and predicted activation yields a per-layer surprisal (negative log-likelihood), which is width-normalized and aggregated across taps into a scalar depth-wise energy S(x). A lightweight monotone calibrator (3-parameter logistic or isotonic regression, fit offline on a small dev split) maps S(x)—optionally blended with an instantaneous confidence proxy—into the final uncertainty score. Training is self-supervised: the heads are fitted with a lightweight auxiliary NLL loss on in-distribution data while the backbone is trained normally, requiring no OOD exposure or architectural modification. The key insight is that inter-layer feature transitions degrade earlier and more reliably than softmax confidence under corruption or domain shift, so scoring how surprising each layer-to-layer mapping is provides an early, label-free failure signal that is invariant to per-channel rescaling and adds only a few tens of KB of flash and <2% extra MACs.

## Results

On failure detection (Table 3), SNAP-UQ achieves AUROC 0.90 (MNIST), 0.94 (SpeechCommands), and 0.87 (CIFAR-10) for ID√–ID×, and 0.86, 0.92, 0.94 for ID√–OOD, leading or tying the best baseline on every setting. On corrupted-stream monitoring (Table 2), it attains AUPRC 0.66 (MNIST-C) and 0.65 (SpeechCmd-C) with median detection delays of 24 and 41 frames respectively, outperforming all baselines. On MCU deployability (Table 1), SNAP-UQ reduces latency by 24–35% and flash by 37–57% relative to early-exit ensembles and deep ensembles, and is the only method that fits on the Small-MCU for CIFAR-10 where both ensemble baselines are out-of-memory. On ID calibration (Table 4), it lowers NLL to 0.202 (MNIST) and 0.197 (SpeechCommands) with ECE 0.016, improving over both the base model and temperature scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNAP-UQ outperforms all five baselines on the primary failure-detection task (Table 3): it beats Deep Ensembles (DEEP) by 5 AUROC points on MNIST ID√–ID× (0.90 vs. 0.85) and by 3 points on SpeechCommands (0.94 vs. 0.91), while matching or exceeding DEEP on CIFAR-10 (0.87 vs. 0.86). Against Early-Exit Ensembles (EE-ens), SNAP-UQ gains 5 points on MNIST (0.90 vs. 0.85) and 4 points on SpeechCommands (0.94 vs. 0.90), and also reduces latency by 24–35% and flash by 37–57% (Table 1). MC Dropout (MCD) is the weakest baseline, trailing SNAP-UQ by 16 points on MNIST ID√–ID× (0.74 vs. 0.90) and 5 points on SpeechCommands (0.89 vs. 0.94), and is restricted to Big-MCU due to multi-pass cost. G-ODIN underperforms substantially on ID√–ID× (0.72–0.83) and ID√–OOD on MNIST/SpCmd (0.40–0.74), though it edges out SNAP-UQ on CIFAR-10 OOD (0.95 vs. 0.94). QUTE, the closest competitor, is beaten by SNAP-UQ on every ID√–ID× setting (0.90 vs. 0.87, 0.94 vs. 0.91, 0.87 vs. 0.86) and on SpeechCommands OOD (0.92 vs. 0.91), while tying on CIFAR-10 OOD (0.94 vs. 0.91).

## Relation to Prior Work

SNAP-UQ carves out a distinct niche by repurposing conditional density modeling (predicting the next layer's activation from the previous one) as a single-pass uncertainty signal, rather than relying on the dominant paradigms of multi-pass sampling (MC Dropout, Deep Ensembles), auxiliary early-exit branches (QUTE, BranchNet), or unconditional feature statistics (classwise Mahalanobis, energy-based OOD). Its closest conceptual neighbor is the Tiny Deep Ensemble (Ahmed et al., 2024), which also targets MCU-friendly uncertainty but via duplicated normalization layers; SNAP-UQ instead derives its score from the network's own depth-wise dynamics, requiring no ensemble members and no state. The theoretical grounding connects the surprisal score to a conditional Mahalanobis energy (Prop. 2.2), distinguishing it from prior OOD detectors that compare against class-averaged, depth-agnostic feature distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For TinyML practitioners, the practical impact is significant: a few tens of KB of int8 heads and a single scalar surprisal score can rival multi-model ensembles on failure detection while fitting on sub-MB MCUs where those ensembles are simply out-of-memory. The finding that inter-layer feature dynamics degrade earlier than softmax confidence under corruption offers a useful diagnostic principle for on-device monitoring. Open questions include whether the diagonal/low-rank covariance assumption holds for deeper or more entangled architectures, how tap placement and projector rank should be selected in a hardware-aware manner, and whether the approach extends to transformer-based TinyML backbones where the notion of 'next activation' is less spatially structured.
