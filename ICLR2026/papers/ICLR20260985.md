## Problem

Practical solvers for homotopy-based problems—robust optimization, global optimization, polynomial root-finding, and sampling—universally follow a predictor-corrector structure, yet rely on hand-crafted, task-specific heuristics for step sizes and termination rules that are suboptimal and require per-instance tuning. No prior work has unified these independently developed solvers under a single framework or replaced their heuristic control with learned, adaptive policies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260985/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a homotopy problem instance (e.g., a point cloud pair with 95% outliers, a non-convex objective, a polynomial system, or a target distribution) and at each homotopy level observes a 4-dimensional state: current homotopy level t, previous corrector tolerance ε, previous corrector iteration count i, and convergence velocity τ (relative change in objective or KSD). It outputs two actions per step—a predictor step size Δt and a corrector termination criterion (tolerance ε or max iterations i_max)—and ultimately produces the solution at t=1 (rotation/translation, 3D points, function minimum, polynomial roots, or samples). The neural policy is trained once on a distribution of instances and then deployed training-free on new instances from the same problem class.

## Method

NPC formulates the predictor-corrector loop as a Markov Decision Process: a small MLP (two hidden layers, 16 units, ReLU) maps the state to actions (Δt, ε or i_max) that control how far the predictor advances and when the corrector stops. The policy is trained with PPO using a composite reward combining step-wise accuracy (convergence velocity or relative error change) and a terminal efficiency bonus (T_max − T, where T is total corrector iterations), with task-specific scaling coefficients λ₁, λ₂. The key design choice is amortized training: the agent is trained over a distribution of problem instances (e.g., randomized Ackley functions, randomized polynomial coefficients, randomized GMM modes) so that a single learned policy generalizes to unseen instances without per-instance fine-tuning. RL is specifically chosen over supervised or self-supervised alternatives because early step-size decisions have long-horizon effects on the entire trajectory, making local geometric assumptions infeasible; RL's cumulative reward evaluation naturally captures this sequential dependency.

## Results

Across four homotopy problems, NPC consistently reduces corrector iterations by 60–80% while matching or improving solution quality. On GNC point cloud registration, NPC cuts iterations from 783 to 169 (bunny) and runtime from 161 ms to 19.15 ms with comparable rotation/translation errors. On Gaussian homotopy (Himmelblau), NPC achieves f(x*)=0.00 in 345 iterations and 8.91 ms, versus 501 iterations and 11.39 ms for Classic GH, while SLGH_d fails entirely (f=2.57). On polynomial root-finding (UPnP), NPC tracks all roots in 29 iterations (3.86 ms) versus 53 iterations (8.25 ms) for Classic HC, and on ALD sampling (40-mode GMM), NPC requires only 110 iterations (772 ms) versus 410 (1353 ms) for Classic ALD with comparable W₂ and KSD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Classic GNC, NPC matches accuracy (log E_R within 0.01) while reducing iterations by 70–80% and runtime by 80–90% across all six point cloud sequences. IRLS GNC is faster than Classic GNC on registration but catastrophically fails on multi-view triangulation (log E_p = 1.74 vs. NPC's −4.72), confirming its lack of generalization. In Gaussian homotopy, NPC outperforms SLGH_d and PGS on Himmelblau (f=0.00 vs. 2.57 and 1.18 respectively) and beats CPL by two orders of magnitude in runtime (8.91 ms vs. 2160 ms) while avoiding per-instance training. For polynomial root-finding, NPC achieves 100% success with 29 iterations on UPnP versus 53 for Classic HC and 100 for Simulator HC, and is the only method applicable to all benchmark systems without task-specific pre-training. In ALD sampling, NPC reduces iterations from 410 to 105–110 with comparable W₂ and KSD; iDEM achieves slightly lower W₂ on GMM (7.42 vs. 11.91) but requires 1000 iterations and runs on a more powerful GPU, making direct runtime comparison infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical predictor-corrector (PC) framework for numerical continuation (Allgower & Georg, 2012) and unifies four previously independent homotopy-based solvers—Graduated Non-Convexity, Gaussian homotopy, homotopy continuation, and annealed Langevin dynamics—under a single MDP formulation. It departs from prior learning-based homotopy improvements (Lin et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2025, Richter & Berner 2024), which each target a single domain and require per-instance or per-problem training, by introducing an amortized RL policy (PPO) that learns adaptive step-size and termination strategies transferable across unseen instances within a problem class. The RL-for-optimization line (Li 2019, Belder et al. 2023) informs the sequential decision framing but does not address the full PC control loop across diverse homotopy families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification demonstrates that a single learned policy can replace hand-tuned schedules across fundamentally different problem domains, offering a practical path toward general-purpose homotopy solvers with 70–90% iteration reductions. The amortized training regime (train once on a distribution, deploy training-free on new instances) is particularly valuable for real-time applications like robotics and computer vision. Key limitations include the need for manual reward scaling per problem (acknowledged in Appendix D) and the restriction to problems expressible in homotopy form; open questions include whether the approach extends to higher-dimensional or combinatorial homotopy problems and whether reward normalization can be made fully automatic.
