## Problem

Topological neural networks (TNNs) require a graph lifting step to transform input graphs into higher-order structures (hypergraphs, cell complexes, simplicial complexes), yet existing lifting methods are static and task-agnostic, leading to suboptimal topological representations whose impact on downstream performance is highly data-dependent. Differentiable, task-informed lifting had only been explored for cell complexes (DCM), leaving hypergraphs and other topological domains without a learnable lifting mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph (nodes, edges, node feature vectors) and produces a stochastically sampled lifted topological structure—hypergraph, cell complex, simplicial complex, or combinatorial complex—whose cells have adaptively learned cardinalities. The lifted structure is then consumed by an off-the-shelf TNN (CWN, CXN, CIN, UniGCNII, UniGIN, or TopoTune) to yield graph-level or node-level predictions. During training, cell inclusion is stochastic via Bernoulli sampling with straight-through gradients; a deterministic variant is available by thresholding acceptance probabilities at a fixed value (e.g., 0.5).

## Method

∂lift (DiffLift) is a general, end-to-end differentiable lifting framework that parameterizes distributions over candidate higher-order cells using node embeddings from an arbitrary GNN backbone (e.g., GIN or GPS). For each candidate cell, a permutation-invariant function (MLP or DeepSet) computes an acceptance probability, and cells are sampled via Bernoulli draws with the straight-through estimator to propagate gradients. Cell sizes are adaptive: a learned categorical distribution over k_v controls the number of nearest neighbors in embedding space used to form each candidate. For hierarchical domains (cell/simplicial complexes), the procedure iterates in increasing dimensionality—1-cells are learned from k-NN in embedding space, while D≥2 cells are elicited from a cycle basis of the partially constructed complex and accepted/rejected by the same probabilistic mechanism. The framework is domain-agnostic (only Steps 2–3 of the recipe change per target domain) and integrates seamlessly into standard TNN pipelines without requiring auxiliary losses.

## Results

On graph classification, ∂lift is the best-performing lifting method in over 90% of TNN/dataset combinations (22 out of 24 experiments). With CWN on cell complexes, ∂lift achieves 81.06% on NCI109, 88.72% on MUTAG, and 0.20 MAE on ZINC, representing gains of up to ~45% over cycle lifting (76.71%, 66.67%, 0.46 respectively). For hypergraphs with UniGCNII, ∂lift reaches 77.45% on NCI1 and 89.47% on MUTAG, substantially outperforming k-hop (72.70%, 61.40%) and kernel lifting (73.80%, 63.16%). On node classification, CWN+∂lift achieves 80.18% on Texas and 80.17% on Cora, outperforming DCM (56.76% and 80.73%) and cycle lifting (63.06% and 74.80%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against cycle lifting (Bodnar et al., 2021a), ∂lift wins on nearly all datasets: CWN+∂lift improves MUTAG from 66.67% to 88.72% (+22 pts) and ZINC MAE from 0.46 to 0.20 (−57%), while CXN+∂lift lifts NCI1 from 72.02% to 79.59%. Against kernel lifting (Bernárdez et al., 2024), the strongest static hypergraph baseline, UniGCNII+∂lift gains +3.65 pts on NCI1 (77.45% vs 73.80%) and +26.31 pts on MUTAG (89.47% vs 63.16%). Against DCM (Battiloro et al., 2024), the only prior differentiable lifting method, CWN+∂lift outperforms on Texas (80.18% vs 56.76%) and Cora (80.17% vs 80.73%, near-tie), while TopoTune+∂lift leads on Cora (86.82% vs 80.73%). Against IPR-MPNN (Qian et al., 2024), CXN+∂lift achieves 82.57% on NCI109 vs 77.08% and 0.17 MAE on ZINC vs 0.39 (56% error reduction). Against plain GNNs (GCN, GIN), ∂lift-equipped TNNs consistently surpass them, e.g., CWN+∂lift reaches 81.06% on NCI109 vs GIN's 76.90%, confirming that the learned topological structure adds value beyond pairwise message passing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the differentiable lifting concept introduced by Battiloro et al. (DCM, ICLR 2024), which was restricted to cell complexes, into a unified framework covering hypergraphs, simplicial complexes, and combinatorial complexes. It departs from the dominant paradigm of static, task-agnostic liftings (cycle, k-hop, k-NN, kernel) by parameterizing distributions over candidate higher-order cells using GNN-derived node embeddings, with Bernoulli sampling and straight-through estimators for end-to-end training. The iterative, dimension-increasing sampling procedure for hierarchical domains (cell/simplicial complexes) builds on the cycle-basis machinery from Bodnar et al. (CW Networks, NeurIPS 2021) but makes cell inclusion a learned, probabilistic decision rather than a deterministic structural rule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The lifting step is a critical yet largely overlooked design choice in TNN pipelines, and this work demonstrates that making it task-informed and differentiable yields substantial, consistent gains (up to 45% over static liftings) across multiple TNN architectures and topological domains. Key limitations include the cubic cost of cycle-basis computation for cell complexes (restricting experiments to 2D) and the dependence on the quality of the backbone GNN's embeddings. Open questions include scaling to higher-dimensional complexes and larger graphs, extending to dynamic/temporal topologies, and providing theoretical guarantees on how learnable liftings mitigate oversmoothing and oversquashing in TNNs.
