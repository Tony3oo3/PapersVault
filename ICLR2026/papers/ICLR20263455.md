## Problem

No open-source system could reliably generate full-length songs (minutes-long) with coherent vocals and accompaniment from lyrics, despite proprietary systems like Suno and Udio demonstrating feasibility. The core technical barriers are: standard next-token prediction fails when a single token must simultaneously encode both vocal and accompaniment signals (especially in acoustically dense genres like metal), and prefix text conditioning degrades beyond ~3K audio tokens, making long-form lyrical alignment intractable with conventional approaches.

## Input / Output

The model consumes structured lyrics (with section tags like [verse], [chorus], [bridge]), a genre/style instruction, and optionally a 30-second reference audio segment for in-context learning. It produces a full song of up to five minutes at 16 kHz (upsampled to 44.1 kHz via a lightweight Vocos-based vocoder), with the audio represented as 50 Hz frames from X-Codec's 8-layer RVQ (codebook size 1024). The two-stage pipeline first generates semantic codebook-0 tokens (Stage-1, 7B LM) then refines with residual codebooks 1–7 (Stage-2, 2B LM), with codebook-0 clamped at inference to preserve alignment.

## Method

YuE is a LLaMA2-based autoregressive LM trained on trillions of tokens (70k hours speech + 650k hours music) with three key architectural innovations. First, Track-Decoupled Next-Token Prediction (Dual-NTP) splits each time step into a paired vocal token and accompaniment token, leveraging a source-separation prior so that neither signal overwhelms the other; this reduces training loss by ~0.4 over standard NTP and preserves lyric intelligibility even at low vocal-to-accompaniment ratios. Second, Structural Progressive Conditioning (SPC) interleaves text conditions (lyrics + structure tags) with audio tokens at musical section boundaries (segmented via all-in-one), avoiding the degradation of a single long prefix that fails beyond 6K tokens. Third, a redesigned music ICL framework prepends a 30s reference audio to the SPC sequence, introduced only during the annealing phase (~10B tokens) to prevent shortcut copying and enable disentangled control over style, voice, and content. The system is trained with multitask learning (TTS, lyrics-to-song, unconditional music) and uses classifier-free guidance plus ICL-based test-time conditioning to improve musicality.

## Results

YuE achieves the best KL divergence (0.372) among all compared systems, indicating superior distributional matching of generated audio, and the highest CLaMP 3 alignment score (0.240), reflecting strong text-to-music semantic alignment. In human A/B evaluation, YuE surpasses Hailuo by a clear margin, performs on par with Tiangong and Udio, but trails Suno V4 (the strongest proprietary system). YuE also demonstrates one of the widest song-level vocal ranges (~27 semitones, matching Suno V4) and generates the longest audio durations among all compared systems, confirming its capability for full-song (up to 5-minute) generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

YuE is compared against four proprietary systems: Suno V4, Udio, Hailuo, and Tiangong. On distributional matching (KL), YuE (0.372) substantially outperforms all four baselines (range 0.503–0.756), while on FAD it is competitive (1.624) but trails Udio (1.222) and Suno V4 (1.544). On the CLaMP 3 alignment metric, YuE (0.240) leads all baselines by a wide margin (next best: Suno V4 at 0.160), though it scores lowest on CLAP (0.118 vs. 0.244–0.310), which the authors attribute to CLAP's limited sensitivity to singing-specific content. In human A/B evaluation, YuE decisively outperforms Hailuo, ties with Tiangong and Udio on musicality, but remains behind Suno V4, which leads across all subjective dimensions. On vocal agility, YuE's ~27-semitone range matches Suno V4 and exceeds Hailuo and Tiangong (~20 semitones), while on generation duration YuE produces the longest and most variable-length outputs, confirming its advantage in long-form song modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

YuE extends the multi-stage codec language model paradigm established by AudioLM and VALL-E (predict codebook-0 in Stage-1, residual codebooks in Stage-2) to the full-song lyrics-to-song setting, using LLaMA2 as the base architecture and X-Codec as the semantic-acoustic fused tokenizer. It departs from prior music generation systems (Jukebox, MusicLM, SongCreator, MelodyLM) in three key ways: (1) Dual-NTP explicitly models vocal and accompaniment as separate tokens per frame rather than a single mixed token, leveraging a source separation prior; (2) Structural Progressive Conditioning interleaves text and audio at musical section boundaries instead of using prefix text conditioning that degrades beyond ~3K tokens; and (3) a redesigned in-context learning framework that prepends a 30s reference audio to SPC data with delayed activation, replacing the continuation-based ICL inherited from TTS. The paper positions itself as the first open-source system to reliably generate coherent five-minute songs with lyrics, filling a gap left by proprietary systems like Suno and Udio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

YuE provides the first open-source foundation model family for long-form lyrics-to-song generation, democratizing access to a capability previously locked behind proprietary APIs and enabling downstream research in music generation, voice cloning, and style transfer. The SPC finding—that standard long-context techniques (RoPE scaling, curriculum learning) fail completely for music beyond 6K tokens, while exploiting inherent musical structure (section-level interleaving) resolves the issue—offers a generalizable insight for any autoregressive model handling structured long-form content. Key limitations remain: acoustic quality (vocal and accompaniment fidelity) still trails Suno V4, the CLAP alignment score is notably low, and the semantic-fused tokenizer sacrifices acoustic detail for LM convergence. Open questions include whether Dual-NTP generalizes to more than two tracks, whether SPC can be automated without all-in-one segmentation, and whether the delayed-activation ICL strategy transfers to other generative domains.
