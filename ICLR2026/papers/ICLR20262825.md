## Problem

Discrete graph generation is challenging for traditional generative models due to the unordered, variable-size, and categorical nature of graph structures. While Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs) offer a parameter-space approach for discrete data, their information-theoretic motivation adds complexity and they fix the prior deterministically. Bayesian Sample Inference (BSI) provides a simpler Bayesian-update interpretation but was previously limited to continuous data, leaving a gap for discrete structures like graphs and sequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a Gaussian random vector z_0 (logits of a categorical belief) as input and iteratively refines it over k timesteps to produce a discrete graph. The latent variable z_t is not a graph itself but a distribution over graphs (logits on the probability simplex for each node and edge category). Variable-sized graphs are handled by first sampling the node count from a marginal distribution, then masking inactive nodes and edges. The final output is obtained by quantizing the reconstructor's prediction at t=1 rather than sampling from the belief, which is more efficient when the belief is sufficiently sharp.

## Method

GraphBSI extends BSI to categorical data by representing each node and edge as an independent categorical variable with logits as the belief parameters. The Bayesian update is a simple logit accumulation (z_post = z + αy), and training minimizes an ELBO that reduces to a weighted L2 reconstruction loss. The key theoretical contribution is formulating the discrete update as an SDE in the infinite-step limit, then using the Fokker-Planck equation to derive a noise-controlled family of SDEs parameterized by γ that preserves marginal distributions while interpolating between a deterministic probability-flow ODE (γ=0) and a highly stochastic sampler (γ→∞). Two discretization schemes are provided: Euler-Maruyama and a custom Ornstein-Uhlenbeck scheme that remains stable at high noise levels. A graph transformer serves as the reconstructor f_θ, and the noise level γ is identified as a critical hyperparameter for performance, with the OU scheme and moderate-to-high γ values yielding state-of-the-art results on molecular and synthetic graph benchmarks.

## Results

On the GuacaMol benchmark with 500 sampling steps, GraphBSI(OU) achieves 99.6% validity and 80.3 FCD, outperforming all baselines including DeFoG (99.0% Val., 73.8 FCD) and COMETH (98.9% Val., 72.7 FCD). On Moses with 500 steps, GraphBSI(EM) achieves a FCD of 0.72, substantially beating the previous best of 1.07 (GraphBFN), while GraphBSI(OU) reaches 99.9% validity and 0.90 FCD. Even at only 50 steps, GraphBSI(OU) achieves 99.2% validity on GuacaMol and 99.7% on Moses, already surpassing DeFoG (91.7% and 83.9% respectively). On synthetic graph benchmarks at 1000 steps, GraphBSI saturates validity (100%) on both planar and tree graph generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphBSI outperforms DeFoG (Qin et al., 2025) on nearly all metrics at both 50 and 500 steps, with the largest gap in validity (99.7% vs. 83.9% on Moses at 50 steps) and FCD (0.72 vs. 1.95 on Moses at 500 steps). Against DiGress (Vignac et al., 2023) and DisCo, GraphBSI achieves substantially higher validity (99.6–99.9% vs. 85–88%) and lower FCD on both benchmarks. Compared to COMETH (Siraudin et al., 2024), GraphBSI wins on validity, FCD, and SNN at 500 steps. Against GraphBFN (Song et al., 2025), GraphBSI(EM) achieves a markedly lower Moses FCD (0.72 vs. 1.07) while GraphBFN retains a slight edge on SNN (0.59 vs. 0.54–0.55). On synthetic graph benchmarks, GraphBSI(OU) at 1000 steps saturates validity on planar (100%) and tree (100%) graphs, outperforming HSpectre (Bergmeister et al., 2024) which achieves 95% and 100% respectively, though the ratio metric remains competitive rather than dominant. For sequence generation, SeqBSI outperforms Dirichlet Flow Matching (Stark et al., 2024) on the FlyBrain DNA dataset (FBD 12.3 vs. 15.2 at 100 steps).

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphBSI extends Bayesian Sample Inference (BSI) from continuous to categorical data, providing a simplified interpretation of Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs) where BFNs emerge as a special case with a fixed prior. The key theoretical departure is formulating categorical BSI as an SDE and, via the Fokker-Planck equation, deriving a noise-controlled family of SDEs analogous to Karras et al.'s guidance for diffusion models. Unlike BFNs, which operate in probability space with a fixed uniform prior, BSI works in logit space with a learnable normal prior, yielding a simpler Bayesian update (additive accumulation of logits) and avoiding limit approximations. The graph generation application leverages a permutation-invariant graph transformer as the reconstructor, following the architecture of DeFoG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical unification of BSI, BFNs, and score-based diffusion models for discrete data, clarifying their relationships and offering a practical noise-control knob (γ) that interpolates between deterministic ODE sampling and highly stochastic overwriting. The finding that moderate-to-high noise levels substantially outperform the deterministic probability-flow ODE (γ=0) is a practical insight for discrete generative modeling that may transfer to other BFN/BSI-based systems. Limitations include quadratic scaling from the graph transformer (restricting graph size), the node count being sampled a priori rather than jointly generated, and the score function being approximated rather than computed exactly, which makes the noise level a critical hyperparameter to tune per dataset.
