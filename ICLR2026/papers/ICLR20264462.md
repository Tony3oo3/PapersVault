## Problem

Standard MSE training for multi-step time-series forecasting is a biased estimator of the conditional negative log-likelihood because it ignores autocorrelation among future time steps. Existing remedies (Fourier or PCA label transformations) only guarantee marginal, not conditional, decorrelation, so the bias persists and hampers model training. The paper asks whether a distributional alignment objective can bypass this likelihood-estimation bottleneck entirely.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of univariate history sequences X ∈ R^{B×H} (H=96 fixed) and produces forecast sequences Ŷ ∈ R^{B×T} for T ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}. During training, X is concatenated with both Y and Ŷ along the time axis to form joint sequences Z=[X,Y] and Ẑ=[X,Ŷ], from which first- and second-order statistics are computed for the Wasserstein discrepancy. The formulation is univariate but generalizes to multivariate settings, and the extra computation is confined to training with no inference overhead.

## Method

DistDF replaces the biased point-wise MSE objective with a joint-distribution Wasserstein discrepancy W_p(P_{x,y}, P_{x,ŷ}) that provably upper-bounds the expected conditional Wasserstein W_p(P_{y|x}, P_{ŷ|x}) (Lemma 3.3, Kim et al. 2022). Under a Gaussian assumption, this reduces to the closed-form Bures-Wasserstein metric combining a mean-alignment term ‖μ_Z − μ_Ẑ‖² and a covariance-alignment term B(Σ_Z, Σ_Ẑ), both differentiable and estimable from finite batches. The final loss is a weighted combination L_DistDF = γ·L_Dist + (1−γ)·L_MSE, where MSE preserves element-wise correspondences that the moment-based discrepancy discards. The framework is model-agnostic and plug-and-play: it can be attached to any direct-forecast architecture (TimeBridge, Fredformer, iTransformer, etc.) without modifying the network, and Theorem 3.4 guarantees that driving the joint Wasserstein to zero exactly aligns the conditional distributions.

## Results

DistDF achieves the best MSE across all 8 model–dataset combinations (TimeBridge and Fredformer on ETTm1, ETTh1, ECL, Weather), with headline values of 0.383 (ETTm1), 0.434 (ETTh1), 0.172 (ECL), and 0.248 (Weather) for TimeBridge, and 0.378, 0.430, 0.173, and 0.255 for Fredformer. Compared to the standard MSE (DF) baseline, DistDF reduces MSE by up to 9.4% (Fredformer on ECL: 0.191→0.173) and by 1.6–3.2% on other datasets. The improvement is consistent across all four forecast lengths (T=96, 192, 336, 720) and both MAE and MSE metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DistDF outperforms the standard MSE (DF) baseline on all 8 model–dataset pairs, with the largest gap on Fredformer+ECL (0.173 vs. 0.191, a 9.4% MSE reduction). Against the likelihood-based objectives Time-o1 and FreDF, DistDF wins on 7 of 8 cases (tying Time-o1 on TimeBridge+ETTm1 at 0.383), confirming that their partial decorrelation strategies leave residual autocorrelation bias. The shape-alignment baselines (Dilate, Soft-DTW) are consistently outperformed, with Soft-DTW losing by up to 16.5% MSE on Fredformer+ECL (0.206 vs. 0.173). Koopman is the weakest baseline, trailing DistDF by 10–20% MSE across all settings. The ablation (Table 2) further shows that both the mean-alignment and covariance-alignment components of the Bures-Wasserstein term contribute independently, with their combination yielding the best results (e.g., 0.379 avg MSE on ETTm1 vs. 0.387 for DF alone).

## Relation to Prior Work

DistDF repurposes the joint-distribution Wasserstein discrepancy from domain adaptation (Courty et al., 2017a) to the problem of aligning conditional distributions in time-series forecasting, leveraging the upper-bound guarantee from Kim et al. (2022) to justify the joint proxy for the intractable conditional Wasserstein. It departs from the dominant likelihood-based training paradigm (MSE, FreDF, Time-o1, Koopman) by arguing that these methods suffer from an irreducible autocorrelation bias that label transformations (Fourier, PCA) cannot fully eliminate because they guarantee only marginal, not conditional, decorrelation. The Bures-Wasserstein closed-form (Peyré and Cuturi, 2019) makes the discrepancy tractable and differentiable, positioning DistDF as a model-agnostic regularization term rather than an architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical diagnosis (Theorem 3.1) of why MSE is a biased surrogate for the true conditional negative log-likelihood under autocorrelation, and a principled alternative that is plug-and-play across architectures. However, the Bures-Wasserstein metric captures only first- and second-order moments under a Gaussian assumption, so non-Gaussian higher-order structure in real time series remains unaddressed. Empirical gains are consistent but modest (typically 0.5–2% MSE reduction), and the method's practical impact depends on whether the distributional alignment signal is strong enough to matter beyond the MSE term it regularizes. Extending to higher-order or non-Gaussian discrepancies while preserving tractability is the natural open direction.
