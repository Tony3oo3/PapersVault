## Problem

Process Reward Models (PRMs) have proven effective for step-level reasoning supervision in mathematics and programming, but no PRM has been developed for Operations Research (OR). The authors discover that directly training a PRM on existing OR datasets yields surprisingly weak performance, tracing the root cause to pervasive data quality issues: over 30% of samples in mainstream OR benchmarks contain serious errors in final answers, modeling, or code, making faithful step-level learning nearly impossible.

## Input / Output

The model takes an optimization problem described in natural language together with a candidate step-by-step solution that may include a five-element tuple formulation (sets, parameters, variables, objective, constraints), Pyomo solver code, and execution output. For each of four sequential components—variable definitions, objective and constraints, code implementation, and final answer—it produces a structured critique containing an intent statement, a focused analysis of critical issues, a binary correct/incorrect judgment, and (only if the first flawed section is identified) a corrected version. In Best-of-N mode, the model scores N generated CoT paths and selects the one with the most correct steps; in the Modeling-Critique-Code pipeline, critiques are concatenated back into the prompt to guide iterative code generation.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, a high-quality seed dataset (~8,656 instances) is constructed by collecting all existing synthetic OR data and applying a three-stage filter: code execution verification, constraint satisfaction checking via Qwen3-8B numeric substitution, and modeling-accuracy validation via GPT-4o. Second, OR-ProcessQA is built by running MCTS over the seed problems to generate 550K+ candidate reasoning steps with preliminary binary labels, then re-evaluating every step with GPT-4o in a structured four-field format (Issue, Judgement, Corrected Version, Corrected Step), and retaining only samples where MCTS and GPT-4o labels agree (~20K final samples). Third, OR-PRM is trained in two phases: SFT on the annotated critiques to learn the generative format, followed by DPO using preference pairs constructed from SFT-model failure cases to sharpen logical judgment. Unlike scalar-score PRMs, OR-PRM is a generative model (inspired by GM-PRM) that outputs natural-language critiques and targeted corrections, enabling interpretable, step-wise error diagnosis specific to OR structure.

## Results

Under Best-of-8 sampling, OR-PRM delivers consistent absolute gains across all model scales: +8.0% overall for Qwen2.5-7B, +12.5% for Qwen2.5-14B (the largest average improvement), +10.7% for Qwen2.5-32B, and +6.9% for the specialized LLM-OPT model. The most striking single-benchmark gain is +24.2% on Complex-LP for Qwen2.5-32B. In the Modeling-Critique-Code pipeline, OR-PRM boosts pass@1 accuracy by 23.4% for Qwen2.5-14B and 8.1% for GPT-4o on Complex-LP, with pass@8 gains of 36.1% and 6.3% respectively. LLM-OPT+PRM reaches 80.5% overall accuracy, surpassing the proprietary DeepSeek-v3 (73.6%) and GPT-4o (50.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the proprietary DeepSeek-v3 (73.6% overall), LLM-OPT+OR-PRM achieves 80.5% overall, a 6.9-point absolute gain, and also surpasses it on every individual benchmark (e.g., 67.6% vs. 39.6% on Complex-LP, 97.8% vs. 91.9% on Easy-LP). GPT-4o (50.4% overall) is outperformed by all OR-PRM-enhanced open-source models, with Qwen2.5-32B+OR-PRM reaching 70.3% overall. Within the Qwen2.5 family, OR-PRM yields the largest relative improvement at the 14B scale (+12.5% overall, +23.2% on Easy-LP), while the 32B model shows the largest single-benchmark jump (+24.2% on Complex-LP). The specialized LLM-OPT model, already strong at 73.6% without OR-PRM, still gains +6.9% overall and +19.0% on Complex-LP, confirming that process-level supervision complements rather than replaces domain-specific fine-tuning. In the Modeling-Critique-Code pipeline, OR-PRM also improves GPT-4o's pass@1 by 8.1% on Complex-LP, demonstrating that the critic generalizes beyond the models it was trained with.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative process reward model paradigm from GM-PRM (Zhang et al., 2025a) to the Operations Research domain, replacing scalar step scores with structured natural-language critiques and corrections. The MCTS-based trajectory generation pipeline is adapted from OmegaPRM (Luo et al., 2024), but is combined with a novel dual-validation consensus mechanism (MCTS labels cross-checked against GPT-4o structured error analysis) to address the severe data quality problems unique to OR. Unlike prior PRMs in math, programming, or finance (Skywork-PRM, Qwen2.5-Math-PRM, Fin-PRM), OR-PRM must reason over a five-element optimization tuple, solver code, and constraint satisfaction simultaneously, which motivates its multi-component evaluation protocol. The training recipe (SFT followed by DPO) is standard, but the paper's key methodological departure is identifying and resolving the data bottleneck—over 30% error rates in existing OR datasets—as the primary obstacle rather than model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OR-PRM and OR-ProcessQA establish the first process-level supervision infrastructure for optimization modeling, a domain where step-by-step logical consistency (constraint satisfaction, correct variable definitions, valid solver code) is critical for trustworthy decision-making in logistics, finance, and resource allocation. The finding that mainstream OR benchmarks contain 30–50% serious errors has broader implications for the LLM-for-OR community, as it undermines prior evaluations and motivates re-auditing of existing datasets. Key limitations include the relatively small final dataset (~20K samples after consensus filtering), the absence of comparable PRM training datasets for direct ablation, and Best-of-N performance still falling short of the theoretical upper bound. Open questions include whether the generative critique format generalizes to nonlinear or stochastic optimization, and whether scaling the dataset and model closes the remaining gap to oracle-level step discrimination.
