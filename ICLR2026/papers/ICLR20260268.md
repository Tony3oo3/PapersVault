## Problem

Continuous-time Consistency Models (CMs) suffer from severe training instability when learning the one-step shortcut objective in isolation. The paper identifies the root cause as catastrophic forgetting of the instantaneous velocity field: the self-referential CM target dilutes the supervisory signal of the underlying flow, causing gradient explosion and training collapse. Prior stabilization methods (sCM, MeanFlow, Flow Mapping) either require architectural modifications or over-couple the flow and shortcut tasks, failing to address this fundamental decoupling issue.

## Input / Output

The model takes random Gaussian noise (and optionally a text prompt or class label) and produces a synthesized image in 1–8 function evaluations, compared to 250+ steps for the teacher. For ImageNet 256×256, generation occurs in latent space via a VAE; for CIFAR-10, it operates in pixel space. A distinctive aspect is the expanded time conditioning: the network receives time values in [0,2] rather than [0,1], with the CM task using t∈[0,1] and the FM anchor task using the mirrored interval [1,2] via c_FM = 2−t, requiring no architectural changes beyond the existing time embedder.

## Method

FACM trains a single network with two complementary objectives: a Flow Matching (FM) loss that anchors the model to the instantaneous velocity field (the 'anchor'), and a Consistency Model (CM) loss that learns the one-step shortcut mapping (the 'accelerator'). The key mechanism is the Expanded Time Interval strategy, which assigns the CM task to t∈[0,1] and the FM task to the mirrored interval [1,2] (conditioned as 2−t), functionally decoupling the two tasks while maintaining a continuous, unified target at the boundary t=1. The CM loss uses a stop-gradient model to compute a consistency residual, clamps it to [−1,1], and forms a relaxed target via interpolation with schedule α(t)=1−t^0.5, providing a stable fixed-point iteration. For scalability, the paper introduces Chain-JVP, which computes the Jacobian-vector product sequentially per module within FSDP's all-gather logic, reducing peak memory from OOM to ~72 GB for a 14B model and enabling distillation of Wan 2.2 from 2×40 steps to 2–8 steps.

## Results

FACM achieves state-of-the-art few-step generation on both benchmarks: FID 1.70 (NFE=1) and 1.32 (NFE=2) on ImageNet 256×256, and FID 2.69 (NFE=1) and 1.87 (NFE=2) on CIFAR-10. Notably, the 2-step ImageNet result (1.32) surpasses the multi-step LightningDiT baseline (1.35 at 250×2 NFE) and the REPA baseline (1.42 at 250×2 NFE). On the 14B-parameter Wan 2.2 text-to-image model, FACM accelerates inference from 2×40 steps to just 2–8 steps using the proposed Chain-JVP for FSDP-compatible training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet 256×256 at NFE=1, FACM (FID 1.70) dramatically outperforms MeanFlow (3.43), Shortcut (10.60), and iCT (34.24), and even surpasses the 250-step LightningDiT baseline (1.35) at NFE=2 (FID 1.32). On CIFAR-10, FACM leads all few-step methods at both NFE=1 (2.69 vs. best prior 2.83 by iCT) and NFE=2 (1.87 vs. best prior 1.98 by IMM). Across four different backbone architectures (SiT-XL/2, REPA, DiT-XL/2, LightningDiT), FACM consistently achieves the lowest FID compared to sCM and MeanFlow, confirming architectural agnosticism. Against sCM specifically, FACM avoids the need for architectural modifications (pixel normalization) and is not sensitive to hyperparameter tuning, whereas sCM's stability degrades with more training epochs. The 14B Wan 2.2 distillation demonstrates that FACM's Chain-JVP enables scalable training where prior DDE-based methods exhibit significant derivative deviation on models above 1B parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

FACM sits at the intersection of Consistency Models (Song et al., 2023) and Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022), reframing the long-standing instability of continuous-time CM training as a 'missing anchor' problem: the shortcut objective alone causes catastrophic forgetting of the instantaneous velocity field. This departs from sCM (Lu & Song, 2024), which stabilizes training via architectural modifications (pixel normalization) and regularization, and from MeanFlow/Flow Mapping methods (Geng et al., 2025; Sabour et al., 2025), which reformulate the objective to embed velocity as a special case of an average-velocity target. FACM's key conceptual departure is explicit task decoupling via an expanded time interval, treating FM and CM as functionally separate objectives on distinct conditioning domains rather than a single over-coupled loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FACM establishes the first continuous-time consistency model scaled to 14B parameters (Wan 2.2), with the Chain-JVP/FSDP solution removing a previously hard memory bottleneck that made such scaling infeasible. The Flow-Anchoring principle is architecturally agnostic and sets new SOTA on ImageNet 256×256 (FID 1.32 at NFE=2), making it a practical default for few-step distillation pipelines. Open questions include closing the quality gap between 1–2 step and 8-step generation on large models, reducing Chain-JVP's computational overhead, and whether the T2I-finetuned model's ability to accelerate T2V low-noise steps generalizes to full video synthesis.
