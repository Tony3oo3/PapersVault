## Problem

Open-source text-to-image models lag behind closed-source systems because the community lacks (1) large-scale, reasoning-focused training data that teaches compositional logic beyond flat image-caption pairs, and (2) comprehensive evaluation benchmarks that align with human judgment across diverse dimensions like imagination, emotion, and text rendering. Existing reasoning datasets (e.g., GoT) are narrow in scope, and existing benchmarks rely on saturated metrics like CLIP scores or object detectors that fail to differentiate model performance.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 20 million bilingual (English/Chinese) text prompts paired with 6 million FLUX.1-dev-generated images, where each image carries at least three category-specific annotations plus a Generation Chain-of-Thought (GCoT) narrative. PRISM-Bench takes 700 curated prompts (100 per track across 7 tracks) as input to T2I models and produces per-image alignment and aesthetic scores (1–10 scale, mapped to 0–100) via VLM judges. The Long Text track specifically uses dense multi-sentence GCoT prompts to stress-test complex instruction following.

## Method

FLUX-Reason-6M is constructed through a multi-stage pipeline: captions from LAION-Aesthetics are rewritten by a VLM, augmented with targeted imagination (progressive Gemini-2.5-Pro → Qwen3-32B expansion) and text-rendering (mine-generate-synthesize with Qwen2.5-VL-32B) strategies, then 8M FLUX.1-dev images are quality-filtered and scored on six characteristics (Imagination, Entity, Text rendering, Style, Affection, Composition) by Qwen-VL. The key innovation is Generation Chain-of-Thought (GCoT): a VLM receives the image plus all category-specific captions and produces a step-by-step compositional plan covering spatial relations, typography, emotional tone, and style decisions, providing structured reasoning supervision rather than mere layout boxes. PRISM-Bench evaluates 19 models using GPT-4.1 and Qwen2.5-VL-72B as judges with track-specific alignment rubrics and a unified aesthetic rubric, validated against human ratings (Spearman ρ > 0.55 across tracks, substantially outperforming CLIPScore). Fine-tuning Bagel on FLUX-Reason-6M with GCoT supervision yields consistent gains on both PRISM-Bench and the external GenEval benchmark, confirming generalizability of the learned reasoning.

## Results

On PRISM-Bench (evaluated by GPT-4.1), GPT-Image-1 achieves the highest overall score of 86.3, followed by Gemini2.5-Flash-Image at 85.3. Among open-source models, Qwen-Image leads at 79.9, with SEEDream 3.0 close behind at 79.6. Text rendering (best: 74.5 for GPT-Image-1) and Long text (best: 81.1 for Qwen-Image) remain the most challenging tracks, while Style (best: 93.1) and Composition (best: 92.8) are relatively mature. Fine-tuning Bagel on FLUX-Reason-6M with GCoT supervision improves its overall PRISM-Bench score from 65.1 to 73.3 and GenEval from 0.82 to 0.86.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 19 T2I models on PRISM-Bench, revealing a clear hierarchy. GPT-Image-1 (86.3) and Gemini2.5-Flash-Image (85.3) dominate all tracks, with GPT-Image-1 leading in Entity (88.2), Text rendering (74.5), Style (93.1), and Composition (92.8), while Gemini2.5-Flash-Image edges ahead in Imagination (88.6 vs. 86.4) and Long text (76.2 vs. 72.8). Among open-source models, Qwen-Image (79.9) and SEEDream 3.0 (79.6) form a competitive tier, with Qwen-Image winning Long text (65.1 vs. 66.7 for SEEDream) and SEEDream leading in Entity (77.0 vs. 76.3). HiDream-I1-Full (75.9) and FLUX.1-Krea-dev (74.7) occupy the next tier, with FLUX.1-Krea-dev notably strong in Composition (88.4) and Long text (64.1). Older models show steep degradation: SDXL (60.4) and JanusPro-7B (60.7) trail the leaders by ~25 points, while SD1.5 (44.2) and SD2.1 (48.0) are particularly weak on Text rendering (20.6 and 20.9 respectively). The gap between closed-source and open-source leaders (~6 points) is narrowing but remains significant, with Text rendering and Long text being the widest gaps across all models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of web-crawled image-text pairs (LAION, CC3M, CC12M) that provide flat descriptions without compositional rationale, and from the GoT dataset which introduces reasoning only through bounding-box layout planning. It extends the VLM-as-judge evaluation direction (Hu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024a) by organizing assessment into six characteristic-specific tracks plus a Long Text track, and replaces CLIP-based metrics with GPT-4.1 and Qwen2.5-VL-72B scoring. The core conceptual departure is the Generation Chain-of-Thought (GCoT) annotation, which provides multi-dimensional, natural-language reasoning supervision across Imagination, Entity, Text rendering, Style, Affection, and Composition simultaneously, rather than the layout-centric CoT in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6M-image, 20M-bilingual-caption dataset with GCoT annotations is the first million-scale resource explicitly designed to teach reasoning to T2I models, and its public release lowers the barrier for the open-source community to close the gap with closed-source systems. The PRISM-Bench evaluation reveals that Text rendering and Long Text instruction following remain the most challenging dimensions even for GPT-Image-1 and Gemini2.5-Flash-Image, highlighting a concrete research frontier. A key limitation is the circular dependency on FLUX.1-dev as the synthesis engine, which may constrain the diversity of visual concepts the dataset can capture, and the 15,000 A100 GPU-day cost limits reproducibility. Open questions include whether GCoT supervision generalizes beyond the FLUX architecture family and whether the VLM-judge protocol remains reliable as new models push past the judges' own capabilities.
