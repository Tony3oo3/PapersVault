## Problem

LDM-based All-in-One image Restoration (AiOR) methods suffer from slow iterative denoising inference and loss of fine structural details due to VAEs trained for generative diversity rather than pixel-level reconstruction, while non-generative methods lack generalization to real-world degradations. Visual autoregressive modeling (VAR) offers 10×+ faster inference and a scale-space decomposition that naturally separates degradations (coarse scales) from scene details (fine scales), but its application to AiOR remained largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264814/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (haze, rain, snow, blur, or low-light) at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. The degraded image is encoded by a frozen VQVAE encoder to produce a continuous latent that serves as cross-attention conditioning throughout the autoregressive transformer. The output is a restored clean RGB image, produced by autoregressively predicting 10 scales of discrete index maps (from 1×1 to 32×32 in latent space), refining them into continuous latents via a lightweight transformer, and decoding with a fine-tuned VAE decoder.

## Method

RestoreVAR adapts the VAR scale-space autoregressive transformer for AiOR by injecting degraded-image conditioning through cross-attention at every transformer block, where keys and values come from the continuous VQVAE latent of the degraded input (initialized with zero-gated projections to preserve pretrained behavior). The architecture replaces absolute positional embeddings with 2D RoPE for resolution scaling and removes AdaLN layers to reduce ~100M parameters. To counteract detail loss from vector quantization and VAE decoding, a lightweight non-generative Latent Refiner Transformer (LRT, ~3% overhead) predicts a residual added to the discrete latent to produce a continuous latent, guided by cross-attention with the final transformer block output. The VAE decoder is separately fine-tuned on continuous latents using L1, SSIM, perceptual, and PatchGAN adversarial losses. The key insight is that VAR's residual scale-space decomposition places degradations predominantly in coarse scales (few tokens) and scene details in fine scales, making restoration tractable by correctly predicting only early scales while reconstructing details in later ones.

## Results

On the five standard AiOR benchmarks (RESIDE, Snow100k, Rain13K, LOLv1, GoPro), RestoreVAR achieves the best generative performance across all PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics, e.g., 24.67 dB / 0.821 / 0.074 on RESIDE and 24.05 dB / 0.713 / 0.156 on Snow100k, outperforming the strongest LDM-based baseline AutoDIR by up to 5.05 dB (Snow100k) and 0.198 SSIM. Inference takes only 0.201 s per image versus 2.04 s (Diff-Plugin), 8.48 s (AutoDIR), and 8.25 s (PixWizard), a >10× speedup. On real-world and mixed-degradation generalization (Table 2), RestoreVAR attains the highest average MUSIQ (56.22) and CLIPIQA (0.383) among all methods, surpassing the best non-generative baseline DCPT (51.68 / 0.366) by ~4.5 MUSIQ points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the three LDM-based generative baselines, RestoreVAR wins on every metric across all five datasets: it beats AutoDIR (the strongest LDM baseline) by 0.19 dB PSNR and 0.041 SSIM on RESIDE, by 5.05 dB and 0.198 SSIM on Snow100k, and by 0.41 dB on GoPro, while also achieving lower LPIPS in all cases. Diff-Plugin and PixWizard trail RestoreVAR by a wider margin (e.g., PixWizard scores only 15.84 dB on LOLv1 vs. RestoreVAR's 21.72 dB). Against the five non-generative baselines (PromptIR, InstructIR, AWRaCLe, DCPT, DFPIR), RestoreVAR scores lower on pixel-level metrics (PSNR/SSIM) by 1–8 dB depending on the dataset, which the authors attribute to the inherent VAE decoder ceiling shared by all latent generative methods. However, on the real-world and mixed-degradation generalization benchmarks (Table 2), RestoreVAR surpasses all non-generative methods in average MUSIQ (56.22 vs. DCPT's 51.68) and CLIPIQA (0.383 vs. DCPT's 0.366), and is competitive with the best LDM-based generative methods (PixWizard: 56.05 / 0.394). In a user study over 50 real-world scenes, RestoreVAR received the highest mean rating (4.36/5) versus AutoDIR (3.68) and all non-generative methods (2.11–2.93), with statistically significant differences (p < 10⁻⁴). Inference speed is 0.201 s per image, roughly 10× faster than Diff-Plugin (2.04 s) and over 40× faster than AutoDIR (8.48 s) and PixWizard (8.25 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

RestoreVAR adapts the Visual Autoregressive (VAR) model (Tian et al., NeurIPS 2024) — originally designed for class-conditional image generation — to the All-in-One image Restoration (AiOR) task, replacing the iterative denoising of latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion-based methods like Diff-Plugin, AutoDIR, PixWizard) with scale-space next-scale prediction. It also draws on HART (Tang et al., 2024) for the idea of refining discrete VQVAE latents into continuous ones, but replaces HART's 37M-parameter diffusion-based MLP refiner with a lightweight non-generative Latent Refiner Transformer (LRT) and fine-tunes the VAE decoder on continuous latents only. Unlike prior VAR-for-restoration efforts (VarSR for super-resolution, Varformer which uses VAR features to guide a separate non-generative network), RestoreVAR is fully generative and directly fine-tunes the VAR transformer for AiOR with cross-attention conditioning on the degraded image's continuous latent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference speedup over LDM-based AiOR methods (0.201s vs. 2–8.5s per image) while simultaneously improving PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS over all generative baselines makes VAR a practical alternative for time-sensitive restoration applications (video surveillance, autonomous navigation). The scale-space analysis — showing that degradations concentrate in coarse VAR scales while scene details reside in fine scales — provides a conceptual simplification that could inform future restoration architectures. However, performance remains bounded by the VQVAE decoder quality (a limitation shared by all latent generative methods), the VAR backbone is pretrained on only ~1M ImageNet images versus hundreds of millions for Stable Diffusion, and the method struggles with extremely severe degradations. Open questions include whether scaling to larger VAR backbones or improved VQVAE/refiner architectures can close the remaining gap to non-generative methods on pixel-level metrics.
