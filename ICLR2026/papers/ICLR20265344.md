## Problem

Target networks in deep RL preserve training stability but slow learning by relying on stale, periodically-updated estimates, while bootstrapping directly from the online network causes overestimation bias and instability. The paper asks whether both networks can be jointly leveraged to achieve fast and stable learning, and what selection criterion between them is appropriate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265344/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard state-action pairs (Atari frame sequences via CNNs, or continuous state vectors for MuJoCo/DMC/HBench tasks) and produces Q-value estimates for policy evaluation. The only computational difference from vanilla DQN is one additional forward pass of the online network on the next state during target computation, with a stop-gradient operator preventing backpropagation through both networks in the target. No new hyperparameters are introduced; the target update frequency T and all other settings remain unchanged from the base algorithm.

## Method

MINTO modifies the Bellman target to y = r + γ max_{a'} min(Q_target(s',a'), Q_online(s',a')), applying the minimum operator between the target and online network estimates before the max. The minimum acts as a principled selection criterion: when the online estimate is lower (less overestimated), it is used to incorporate fresher information and accelerate learning; when the online estimate is higher, the target network is preferred to maintain stability and suppress overestimation bias. This single-line modification integrates into DQN, IQN, CQL, and SAC with negligible overhead and no additional hyperparameters. The paper proves convergence in the tabular setting by casting MINTO as a special case of the Generalized Q-learning framework (Lan et al., 2020), and empirically validates the minimum operator against alternatives (max, mean, random, online-only) on 15 Atari games, showing it dominates all other selection criteria.

## Results

On 15 Atari games, MINTO improves DQN by ~17% in AUC (CNN) and ~22% (IMPALA+LN), and outperforms Double DQN, FR-DQN, ScDQN, and Maxmin DQN (N=2) on the same benchmark. In the distributional RL setting, MINTO+IQN gains ~7% AUC over IQN (CNN) and ~10% (IMPALA+LN). In offline RL, CQL+MINTO achieves a dramatic ~125% AUC improvement over CQL with CNN and ~20% with IMPALA+LN on 14 Atari games. In continuous control (26 tasks across MuJoCo, HBench, DMC-Hard), MINTO consistently improves sample efficiency for both SimbaV1 and SimbaV2, with the clearest gains on MuJoCo and HBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MINTO consistently outperforms DQN by 17–22% in AUC across both CNN and IMPALA+LN architectures on 15 Atari games, demonstrating both faster sample efficiency and higher asymptotic performance. Against the three single-estimator baselines designed to mitigate overestimation—Double DQN, FR-DQN, and ScDQN—MINTO wins on all 15 games, indicating that the minimum operator between online and target networks is a more effective overestimation control than decoupled action selection or functional regularization. MINTO also beats Maxmin DQN (N=2 ensemble) in the online setting, showing that leveraging up-to-date online estimates with the min operator is superior to training multiple target networks, while also avoiding the extra memory and compute cost. In the offline RL setting, CQL+MINTO dramatically outperforms CQL (+125% AUC with CNN), but Maxmin CQL (N=2) still edges out CQL+MINTO, likely due to the conservative nature of the ensemble being less detrimental without exploration; however, MINTO further enhances Maxmin CQL when combined. In continuous control, SimbaV1+MINTO and SimbaV2+MINTO show clear sample-efficiency gains on MuJoCo and HBench, while DMC-Hard results are comparable to or slightly below the base algorithm, suggesting the benefit is most pronounced in tasks where overestimation bias is more impactful. CrossQ+WN+MINTO also shows consistent improvements over CrossQ+WN alone, confirming MINTO's compatibility with modern actor-critic architectures that already use normalization-based stabilization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MINTO sits at the intersection of two well-studied threads in off-policy deep RL: the target network (introduced by DQN to stabilize bootstrapping) and the overestimation-bias literature (Double DQN, Clipped Double Q-Learning, Maxmin Q-learning). Rather than replacing the target network with the online network (as in MellowMax, CrossQ, or FR-DQN) or using two independent estimators (as in Double DQN or Maxmin DQN), MINTO applies the minimum operator directly between the existing online and target networks, borrowing the non-expansion property of min from the Maxmin Q-learning framework while avoiding the ensemble overhead. The convergence analysis is cast as a special case of the Generalized Q-learning framework of Lan et al. (2020), and the method is positioned as orthogonal to architectural innovations like LayerNorm or batch normalization that have been shown to complement (not replace) target networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MINTO's appeal is its extreme simplicity: one extra forward pass, zero new hyperparameters, and a few lines of code change, yet it yields consistent gains across online/offline RL, discrete/continuous control, and both value-based and actor-critic algorithms. The ~125% AUC improvement for CQL+MINTO (CNN) in offline RL is particularly striking, suggesting that the original CQL formulation under-leverages recent online estimates. Open questions remain: the minimum operator may be overly conservative in low-noise environments (mild underestimation), its interaction with exploration strategies is not fully characterized, and the authors note that adaptive operator selection (e.g., switching between min and other criteria based on uncertainty) is a natural next step. Testing in multi-task and multi-agent settings is also left for future work.
