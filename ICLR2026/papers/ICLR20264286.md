## Problem

Unmeasured confounding is a fundamental obstacle to causal inference from observational data, and existing latent-variable methods (e.g., CEVAE, Wang & Blei's multiple-cause model) lack explicit model-based identification guarantees, rely on restrictive assumptions such as no single-cause confounders or linear structure, or fail consistency when the latent variable is misspecified. This paper addresses the gap by providing a latent-variable imputation framework with provable model-based identifiability and consistent estimation of causal effects within a capacity-controlled sparse deep network class.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular observational data consisting of a treatment variable A (binary, continuous, or multi-dimensional), an outcome variable Y, and optionally high-dimensional proxy variables X that are noisy indicators of the unobserved confounder. It produces imputed latent confounders Z drawn from the posterior conditional π(Z|A) or π(Z|X), from which the mean potential outcome E[Y(a)] under a fixed intervention is computed as a Monte Carlo average over M imputed Z samples per observation, yielding both ATE and unit-level CATE estimates with bootstrap-based confidence intervals.

## Method

CI-StoNet encodes the causal DAG's Markov factorization as a two-module stochastic neural network: a first module μ₁(A,θ₁) imputes the latent confounder Z (or μ₁(X,θ₁) in the proxy setting), and a second module μ₂(Z,A,θ₂) predicts the outcome Y, with Gaussian noise on both layers. Parameters are regularized with a mixture Gaussian prior that induces sparsity, and the model is trained via an adaptive stochastic gradient Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (SGHMC) algorithm that alternates between Hamiltonian updates for Z and gradient steps for θ, solving a Bayesian Fisher identity. The key theoretical contribution is a model-based identifiability result: although Z is identifiable only up to loss-invariant reparameterizations, the causal functional E[Y(a)] is invariant across the observationally equivalent class, and the estimator is consistent as n→∞ and M→∞. The total error decomposes into a statistical estimation error (vanishing under sparse DNN consistency) and a misspecification error bounded by the L² approximation rate ωₙ of the true structural functions within the sparse DNN class.

## Results

On the simulated proxy-variable benchmark (10 datasets, 2000 train / 500 test), CI-StoNet achieves an out-of-sample PEHE of 0.3731 (±0.0350), outperforming the next-best method DragonNet (0.4305) by ~13% and the closest latent-variable competitor CEVAE (0.6246) by ~40%. On the 10 ACIC 2019 datasets, CI-StoNet attains the lowest out-of-sample ATE MAE of 0.0709 (±0.0133), ahead of X-Learner-BART (0.0817) and CEVAE (0.0875). On the Twins dataset with a key confounder (gestat10) removed, CI-StoNet achieves an absolute ATE error of 0.0135 (RMSE 0.0153), roughly 3–9× lower than all baselines including TMLE (0.0932–0.1238) and DONUT (0.0490).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CI-StoNet outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric. On the PEHE benchmark, the closest competitor is DragonNet (0.4305 vs. 0.3731 out-of-sample), followed by CEVAE (0.6246) and X-Learner-BART (0.6570); the gap widens substantially against GANITE (1.1797), CMGP (2.2116), and CFRNet-MMD (2.0250). On ACIC 2019 ATE estimation, CI-StoNet (0.0709 MAE) beats X-Learner-BART (0.0817), CEVAE (0.0875), and CMDE (0.0877), with the largest margins over GANITE (0.1747) and X-Learner-RF (0.1903). On the Twins dataset with the confounder gestat10 removed, CI-StoNet (absolute error 0.0135, RMSE 0.0153) dominates all baselines: DSE (0.0211), ARBE (0.0168), DONUT (0.0490), CEVAE (0.0327), TMLE-ensemble (0.1238), and GANITE (0.4198). Notably, X-Learner-RF is competitive when the confounder is observed (0.0056 absolute error) but degrades to 0.0157 when it is missing, whereas CI-StoNet's degradation is minimal (0.0099 → 0.0135), highlighting the robustness of its latent-confounder imputation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CI-StoNet builds directly on the stochastic neural network (StoNet) framework of Sun & Liang (2022) and the adaptive SGHMC algorithm of Liang et al. (2022), repurposing them to encode the Markov factorization of a causal DAG and impute latent confounders via the resulting conditional distributions. It departs from prior deep latent-variable causal inference methods (CEVAE, Wang & Blei's latent factor model, Kallus et al.'s matrix factorization) by grounding its guarantees in sparse deep learning theory (Sun et al., 2022) rather than variational approximations or low-rank assumptions, thereby addressing the consistency failures identified by Rissanen & Marttinen (2021) for CEVAE under misspecification. The key conceptual shift is from nonparametric identifiability to model-based identifiability: the causal estimand is shown to be invariant across the observationally equivalent class of sparse DNN parameterizations, even though the latent confounder itself is not uniquely recoverable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first model-based consistency and misspecification-error guarantees for a deep latent-variable causal inference method, filling a theoretical gap that has limited the adoption of approaches like CEVAE in settings where the true mechanism may not match the assumed model class. The misspecification bound (Theorem 2) explicitly quantifies how approximation error in the sparse-DNN class propagates to the causal estimand, giving practitioners a principled way to assess model adequacy. Key limitations include reliance on correct DAG specification (an unrecognized mediator can be absorbed into the latent confounder), the absence of a fully Bayesian posterior for the causal functional (only bootstrap CIs are provided), and the fact that the latent confounder is identifiable only up to loss-invariant reparameterizations. Open questions include extending the framework to time-varying treatments, formal posterior concentration for the causal estimand, and scaling the SGHMC imputation to very high-dimensional latent spaces.
