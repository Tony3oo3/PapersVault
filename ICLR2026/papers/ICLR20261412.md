## Problem

LLM-based search agents (RAG 2.0) interleave multi-step reasoning with dynamic retrieval, but this paradigm introduces severe efficiency bottlenecks that are poorly understood. The paper identifies two root causes: a non-monotonic relationship between retrieval accuracy and end-to-end efficiency (both exact search and low-recall ANN degrade throughput), and extreme sensitivity to retrieval latency caused by FCFS scheduling that evicts KV-cache for longer requests and retrieval-induced stalls that force unproductive waiting.

## Input / Output

The system accepts text queries and a pre-built ANN vector index over ~21M Wikipedia chunks (384-dim embeddings from all-MiniLM-L6-v2, indexed via HNSWlib with 32 neighbors and efConstruction=500). During generation, the LLM emits special tags (<search>, <answer>) that trigger asynchronous ANN queries (efSearch=10,000, 4 threads); retrieved documents are concatenated into the existing sequence and re-injected for continued reasoning. The final output is a free-text answer, with the system operating at token-generation granularity under iteration-level scheduling on a single L20 GPU (7B) or dual A100 GPUs (14B).

## Method

SearchAgent-X is an inference framework built on vLLM that coordinates interleaved reasoning and retrieval through two mechanisms. First, priority-aware scheduling discretizes three per-request metrics—completed retrieval count, current context length, and total waiting time—into G=6 priority levels (via min-max normalization), processing higher-priority sequences first to maximize prefix KV-cache reuse (raising hit rate from 0.07 to 0.65). Second, non-stall retrieval adaptively terminates ANN search when an EMA-smoothed relative-quality signal (RQ_t, threshold τ=0.9) indicates diminishing returns AND the LLM engine is ready for its next token step, preventing retrieval-induced stalls without sacrificing recall (avg. 0.963). The system deliberately uses high-recall ANN rather than exact search, exploiting the finding that once retrieval quality sufficiently supports reasoning, additional search effort only adds overhead.

## Results

On MuSiQue with Search-R1 7B, SearchAgent-X achieves 1.3–3.4× higher throughput (up to 1.59 req/s at top-1 vs. 0.44 for vLLM_ENN) and 0.2–0.6× lower end-to-end latency (238 ms vs. 1301 ms at top-1) compared to all baselines. The prefix KV-cache hit rate improves from 0.07 (vanilla vLLM) to 0.65 (SearchAgent-X). Generation quality is preserved: SearchAgent-X matches exact retrieval at 0.430 average Exact Match across six datasets (MuSiQue, NQ, 2Wiki, HotpotQA, Bamboogle, StrategyQA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SearchAgent-X is compared against three vLLM-based baselines: vLLM_ENN (exact nearest-neighbor retrieval), vLLM_ANN (HNSW approximate retrieval), and CachevLLM_ANN (ANN + prefix caching). Against the strongest baseline CachevLLM_ANN, SearchAgent-X achieves 1.3–1.7× higher throughput and 0.46–0.60× lower latency across top-k values on MuSiQue (7B), with the gap widening on harder tasks (1.84× on MuSiQue vs. 1.52× on HotpotQA at top-k=3). Against vLLM_ENN, the throughput advantage reaches 3.4× (top-1) because exact search incurs prohibitive retrieval overhead. The prefix cache hit rate jumps from 0.07 (vLLM_ANN) or ~0.39 (CachevLLM_ANN) to 0.65, driven by priority scheduling (0.07→0.51) and non-stall retrieval (0.51→0.65). In online inference at 5 req/s, SearchAgent-X completes up to 3.5× more requests and maintains a stable pending-sequence ratio of ~0.2, while baselines exceed 0.6. Generation accuracy is statistically indistinguishable from exact retrieval (0.430 avg), confirming no quality trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on vLLM's PagedAttention and prefix caching infrastructure, extending it with two search-agent-specific mechanisms: priority-aware scheduling and non-stall adaptive retrieval termination. It departs from prior RAG serving systems (Telerag, RAGO, RAGServe, PipeRAG, etc.) that treat retrieval and generation as separable pipeline stages, instead targeting the fundamentally different efficiency challenges introduced by the tightly interleaved reasoning-retrieval loop of search agents (Search-R1, ReCall). The paper also positions itself against standard ANN search practice by showing that the optimal retrieval accuracy for search agents is neither exact nor maximally approximate, but a specific high-recall operating point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As search agents (Deep Research, Grok agents, etc.) become the dominant paradigm for complex multi-hop QA, the efficiency gap identified here (up to 83× latency magnification from minor retrieval delays) is a critical deployment bottleneck. The non-monotonic retrieval-accuracy finding provides actionable guidance for practitioners tuning ANN parameters, and the 3.4× throughput / 5× latency gains are directly relevant to production systems. Limitations include evaluation on 7B/14B models with local HNSW retrieval on a single or dual GPU; open questions include scaling to multi-agent coordination, non-graph ANN indices (IVF, PQ), and integration with external search APIs where retrieval latency is less controllable.
