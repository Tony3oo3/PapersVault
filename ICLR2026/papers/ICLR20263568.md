## Problem

Active reconstruction of radiance fields with 3D Gaussian Splatting lacks practical guidance for image capture: existing methods rely on rendering and scoring candidate views (missing occluded regions), ignore semantic quality of object parts, and assume perfect camera poses. In real-world handheld capture, pose noise from user operation further degrades reconstruction, yet no prior 3DGS-based method directly generates informative poses or handles pose uncertainty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263568/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates iteratively: at each step it consumes the current partially-trained 3DGS model (a set of Gaussian ellipsoids with position, color, opacity, and covariance attributes) and the history of previously acquired poses, then outputs a set of l next-best camera poses in SE(3) along with an adaptively determined count l proportional to the size of the target region. After the user captures images at those poses, the system refines the actual poses via a relative SE(3) optimization (Lie algebra parameterization) before resuming 3DGS training. The Point-MAE encoder (12 Transformer blocks, 512-dim features) is applied to Alpha Shape surface vertices, and the visibility matrix is computed from N=100 uniformly sampled hemisphere cameras using PyTorch3D rasterization depth buffers.

## Method

The core contribution is a bottom-up divide-and-conquer pipeline that directly generates informative poses from the 3DGS structure without rendering candidate views. First, Gaussians are clustered into local consistent regions via K-Means on a concatenated feature vector of normalized position, color, quaternion rotation, and a visibility matrix (binary per-Gaussian-per-camera occlusion indicators from Alpha Shape mesh rasterization). Second, the most under-reconstructed region is selected by a composite score combining per-Gaussian semantic feature variance (computed over T=10 stochastic Point-MAE forward passes with 0.8 dropout, following MC-dropout epistemic uncertainty) and a distance penalty from previously covered regions. Third, camera positions are sampled from a von Mises-Fisher distribution on a bounding sphere, with concentration and count scaled by the region's point proportion, oriented via look-at toward the region center. Finally, a robust pose refinement step freezes all Gaussian attributes and optimizes only the relative SE(3) transform between the estimated best pose and the actual captured pose using a photometric reprojection loss, preventing pose noise from corrupting the Gaussian model.

## Results

On the NeRF-Synthetic dataset with 10 total views, the proposed method achieves 25.54 PSNR, 0.896 SSIM, and 0.063 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline (3DGS+FisherRF) by 1.9 dB in PSNR and 0.039 in LPIPS. With 20 views, the method reaches 30.19 PSNR and 0.033 LPIPS, maintaining a 0.6 dB PSNR advantage over FisherRF. On the Objaverse dataset, the method achieves 38.32 PSNR and 0.970 SSIM, surpassing FisherRF by 5.7 dB in PSNR, demonstrating generalization to real-world scanned objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five active view-selection baselines (NeurAR, ActiveNeRF, FisherRF, BayesRays, NVF) across NeRF, Plenoxel, and 3DGS backbones. The proposed method consistently outperforms all baselines on both 10-view and 20-view settings. Against the strongest baseline FisherRF (3DGS backbone), the method gains 1.9 dB PSNR at 10 views and 0.6 dB at 20 views, with the gap narrowing as more views reduce information redundancy. Against ActiveNeRF (3DGS), the improvement is 2.6 dB PSNR at 10 views and 3.6 dB at 20 views. Against NeurAR and BayesRays (NeRF-based), the gains are larger (6.6 and 10.6 dB PSNR respectively at 10 views), partly reflecting the 3DGS backbone advantage. On the Objaverse dataset, the method beats FisherRF by 5.7 dB PSNR on average, confirming generalization beyond synthetic Blender scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the active radiance field reconstruction line (NeurAR, ActiveNeRF, FisherRF, BayesRays) by replacing the top-down paradigm of rendering candidate views and scoring them with uncertainty/information-gain metrics with a bottom-up strategy that directly generates informative poses from the 3DGS model's geometric and visibility structure. It repurposes Monte Carlo dropout uncertainty estimation (Kendall & Gal, 2017) from image classification to 3D Gaussian quality assessment via Point-MAE semantic feature variance, and introduces visibility-consistent region partitioning (alpha-shape + visibility matrix + K-Means) as a mechanism to avoid the occlusion blind spots inherent in rendered-image-based selection. The robust pose optimization step, which freezes Gaussian attributes and optimizes only the relative SE(3) transform via photometric loss, addresses a practical gap that prior active reconstruction works (which assume perfect poses from SfM or robot arms) leave unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a ~4 dB PSNR improvement is achievable on real-world handheld captures simply by adding a lightweight pose refinement step, making active 3DGS reconstruction viable in AR-guided mobile settings where perfect pose alignment is unrealistic. The visibility-based partitioning (the largest ablation drop at ~4.3 dB) suggests that occlusion-aware region decomposition is a critical but underexploited component in active view selection. Open questions include whether the fixed Point-MAE semantic encoder generalizes to unseen object categories, whether the method extends to multi-object or scene-level reconstruction, and whether the bottom-up region strategy scales to the path-planning constraints of large-scale exploration.
