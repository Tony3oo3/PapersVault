## Problem

CTR/CVR estimation models with large-scale sparse categorical features suffer severe performance degradation after the first training epoch, yet the root cause remained unexplained and prior fixes (e.g., MEDA's epoch-boundary reinitialization, uniform weight decay) were heuristic and suboptimal. This paper provides a Rademacher-complexity-based theoretical explanation for the one-epoch overfitting phenomenon and derives a principled, frequency-aware regularization strategy from first principles.

## Input / Output

The model consumes high-dimensional sparse categorical inputs (item IDs, brand IDs, seller IDs, IP addresses, etc.) where the number of distinct values per feature ranges from tens to hundreds of millions and most values appear only a handful of times across the entire training set. Each one-hot feature vector is looked up through a dedicated embedding matrix to produce a dense vector of fixed dimension (e.g., 32), and the concatenated embeddings are fed into an MLP backbone (DNN, WDL, xDeepFM, or WuKong) that outputs a single logit for CTR or CVR. The embedding layers dominate the parameter count, and the extreme sparsity means individual embedding rows are updated only every hundreds of steps on average.

## Method

The paper formulates the trade-off between training loss and generalization as a constrained optimization over per-embedding norm budgets τ_ij, subject to a global norm cap C that controls the Rademacher complexity bound. Solving the KKT conditions (Proposition 1) yields that the optimal regularization multiplier for each embedding row is inversely proportional to its sample frequency m_ij. In practice, frequency is estimated online via a 'last valid update step' counter: at each step k, the update interval I^k_ij = k − s^k_ij − 1 measures how many steps have elapsed since embedding e_ij last received a nonzero gradient, and the adaptive coefficient is set to λ^k_ij = min(1, α·I^k_ij). This coefficient is injected as a decoupled weight-decay term in the Adam or Adagrad update rule, so that stale sparse embeddings are exponentially attenuated toward the current gradient while frequently-updated MLP parameters (I=0) remain essentially unregularized. Proposition 2 formalizes this attenuation, and Proposition 3 proves the method preserves Adam's minimum convergence rate in the non-convex stochastic setting.

## Results

On the DNN backbone with Adam optimizer, AdamAR achieves the highest test AUC across all four datasets in multi-epoch training. For example, on iPinYou (DNN), AdamAR reaches 0.7724 AUC at epoch 4 versus 0.7014 for vanilla Adam (a 7.1-point drop due to one-epoch overfitting) and 0.7717 for MEDA. On the industrial LZD dataset, AdamAR attains 0.7234 AUC at epoch 4 compared to 0.6065 for Adam and 0.7176 for MEDA. The method also improves single-epoch performance, e.g., 0.7617 vs. 0.7461 (Adam) on Avazu and 0.7229 vs. 0.7118 (Adam) on LZD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MEDA (Liu et al., 2023), AdamAR wins on every dataset and backbone in multi-epoch training (E2–E4), e.g., 0.7724 vs. 0.7717 on iPinYou DNN at E4 and 0.7234 vs. 0.7176 on LZD DNN at E4; at E1 the two are tied since MEDA does not alter the first epoch. Against SAM (Foret et al., 2020), AdamAR dominates in multi-epoch settings across all datasets (e.g., 0.7629 vs. 0.6899 on Avazu DNN at E4), while SAM edges out AdamAR only on the smaller Amazon dataset in single-epoch training (0.8588 vs. 0.8582 on WuKong E1). Against AdamW/AdagradW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017), AdamAR consistently outperforms in both single- and multi-epoch settings, with the gap widening in later epochs (e.g., 0.7629 vs. 0.7583 on Avazu DNN at E4, and 0.8708 vs. 0.8426 on Amazon DNN at E3). The paper notes that AdamAR is also less sensitive to the weight-decay hyperparameter α than AdamW, reducing tuning complexity in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical explanation for the one-epoch overfitting phenomenon in CTR/CVR models, grounding it in Rademacher complexity bounds (building on Golowich et al., 2018) rather than the purely empirical observations of Zhang et al. (2022b). It departs from the heuristic MEDA approach (Liu et al., 2023), which reinitializes embeddings at epoch boundaries, by showing MEDA is a special case of a more general per-parameter adaptive regularization scheme derived from a constrained optimization over embedding norm budgets. The method also contrasts with uniform weight decay (AdamW) and sharpness-aware minimization (SAM), which the authors argue are suboptimal because they apply the same regularization strength to both dense and sparse parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the method is already deployed in Alibaba's sponsored search production system and consistently outperforms all baselines across four datasets and four backbone architectures, while also improving single-epoch performance rather than merely preventing multi-epoch degradation. The theoretical framing (norm budget allocation proportional to inverse feature frequency) gives practitioners a principled way to set regularization strength without per-feature tuning. Open questions include whether the i.i.d. sampling assumption for frequency estimation holds under non-stationary production data, and the authors explicitly note that the analogous one-epoch phenomenon in LLM supervised fine-tuning (Ouyang et al., 2022) is left for future work.
