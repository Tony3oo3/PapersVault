## Problem

Standard RLHF trains a single Bradley-Terry-Luce reward model over the entire user population, assuming all users share identical preferences. When users have diverse or conflicting preferences, this single model either ignores minority preferences or becomes inaccurate for all groups. The paper addresses how to learn personalized, interpretable reward models that capture individual user variability without requiring explicit user identifiers, while generalizing to new users and conversation topics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264168/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The summarizer takes a user's past conversation context (chosen/rejected response pairs, or unstructured conversation data without explicit preference labels) and produces a concise natural-language summary. This summary conditions the reward model, which takes a new prompt with two candidate responses and outputs a scalar reward score for each, enabling preference prediction. The same summary can also be injected into a proprietary LLM's prompt to generate personalized responses without any fine-tuning of that LLM.

## Method

PLUS (Preference Learning Using Summarization) jointly trains two components in an online co-adaptation loop: a summarizer π_θ (a 3B LLM fine-tuned with PPO) that generates text-based user summaries from conversation context, and a summary-conditioned reward model r_φ (a 0.5B–3B LLM) that predicts preference likelihoods given the summary. Training alternates between fixing the summarizer and minimizing the reward model's negative log-likelihood loss, then fixing the reward model and training the summarizer via PPO where the reward signal is the reward model's prediction log-likelihood. PPO with GAE is used because the continuously updating reward model creates non-stationary rewards analogous to a multi-agent RL setting. The key design choice is representing users as natural-language summaries rather than fixed-dimension embedding vectors (as in VPL) or full conversation histories (as in ICL), which preserves LLM reasoning capability, yields interpretable user representations, and enables transfer to unseen users and topics.

## Results

PLUS achieves 11–77% improvement in reward model accuracy over the standard BTL baseline across all evaluated domains. On UltraFeedback-P2 with Qwen-3B, PLUS reaches 69.85% accuracy versus 58.75% for BTL and 59.40% for VPL; on UltraFeedback-P4 with Qwen-3B, PLUS scores 62.45% versus 56.25% for BTL. On the real-world PRISM dataset (1,000 users, 75 countries), PLUS achieves 62.9% reward prediction accuracy, outperforming BTL (59.8%), VPL (60.4%), and ICL (60.1%). PLUS-generated summaries also enable personalization of GPT-4o without fine-tuning, achieving a 72% win rate over default GPT-4o responses (28%) on held-out PRISM users.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLUS outperforms all three main baselines across every domain. Against BTL (Bradley & Terry, 1952), PLUS improves accuracy by 11–77% (e.g., 69.85% vs. 58.75% on UF-P2/Qwen-3B; 62.9% vs. 59.8% on PRISM). Against DPL (Siththaranjan et al., 2024), PLUS leads by 1.7–11.3% on UltraFeedback and by 1.1% on PRISM (62.9% vs. 61.8%). Against VPL (Poddar et al., 2024), PLUS shows the largest gap on UltraFeedback (10.7–11.3% on P2/P4 with Qwen-3B), where VPL's fixed-length 512-dim user embedding struggles to capture nuanced preferences; on PRISM, PLUS leads VPL by 2.5% (62.9% vs. 60.4%). The ICL baseline (full conversation history as conditioning) is the closest competitor on UltraFeedback but still trails PLUS by 10.4–17.7%, and collapses on the Pets OOD test (72.5% vs. PLUS's 93.67%), confirming that summarization is critical for generalization to new topics and users.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard RLHF pipeline (Ouyang et al., 2022) and its Bradley-Terry-Luce reward model formulation by introducing a user-conditioned reward model where the conditioning variable z is a text-based summary learned via PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) rather than a fixed embedding vector. It departs from prior personalized RLHF methods—VPL (Poddar et al., 2024) which uses VAE-learned 512-dim user embeddings, DPL (Siththaranjan et al., 2024) which models reward distributions, and ICL which conditions on raw conversation history—by arguing that natural-language summaries better leverage LLM reasoning and offer interpretability. The key architectural departure is the online co-adaptation loop: the summarizer is trained with PPO using the reward model's prediction loss as the RL signal, while the reward model is trained on the summarizer's outputs, creating a mutual dependency absent in prior work where the user encoder and reward model are trained with separate objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLMs serve increasingly diverse user populations, the inability of standard BTL reward models to capture conflicting preferences is a practical limitation that this paper addresses with a method that is both interpretable (human-readable summaries) and portable (summaries can condition proprietary models like GPT-4 without fine-tuning). However, the PRISM results reveal a significant open question: on truly out-of-distribution users, unpersonalized BTL/DPL models outperform all personalized methods including the oracle, suggesting that current personalized RLHF techniques may not generalize beyond the training population without substantially larger, more heterogeneous datasets. The paper also highlights that PPO-based co-adaptation is sensitive to hyperparameters, and the small-scale evaluation (0.5B–3B models) leaves open whether the approach scales to production-sized reward models.
