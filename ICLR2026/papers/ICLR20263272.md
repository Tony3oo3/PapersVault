## Problem

Existing deepfake detectors are typically trained on a single dataset, limiting the diversity of forgery artifacts they can learn and causing poor generalization to unseen domains. When researchers attempt to scale training across multiple heterogeneous datasets, state-of-the-art CLIP-based methods actually degrade in performance rather than improving, revealing a fundamental inability to consolidate diverse forensic knowledge at scale. No prior work had systematically unified the growing body of deepfake benchmarks into a single large-scale training resource or built a pre-trained model specifically for deepfake detection.

## Input / Output

At training time, the model consumes face-cropped RGB images (from 11 source datasets, uniformly preprocessed via MTCNN detection and square cropping) paired with three text modalities: a randomly sampled type-specific label from a curated set of ~30 augmented labels per category, a VLM-generated facial attribute description, and a VLM-generated environmental description. At inference, only the image is required; it is encoded and matched via cosine similarity against the simplest predefined text label for each of the five types (REAL, FS, FR, EFS, FE), with the highest-scoring label determining the prediction. The model also supports a linear-probing mode where only the vision encoder is used for binary real/fake classification on non-facial AIGC images.

## Method

SD² builds on CLIP-ViT-L/14 with LoRA fine-tuning on both image and text encoders, augmented by a Cross-Layer Attention Module (CLAM) that extracts [CLS] tokens from all L transformer layers, applies multi-head self-attention across them to capture inter-layer dependencies, and concatenates the pooled result with the final-layer representation through a two-layer GELU adapter to fuse low-level forensic artifacts with high-level semantics. Training is driven by three complementary objectives: (1) a fine-grained image-text classification loss that computes cosine-similarity logits between image embeddings and type-specific text label embeddings, (2) a text label separation loss that minimizes the Frobenius distance between the inter-label similarity matrix and the identity matrix to enforce orthogonality among the five type embeddings, and (3) a dual SigLIP contrastive loss that aligns each image with both its facial and environmental VLM-generated descriptions using sigmoid-based binary cross-entropy with cross-GPU negative gathering. The overall loss is α·L_C + L_S + L_D (α=2.0), and the entire pipeline is trained on the newly constructed MMI-DD dataset of 3.6 million images spanning 11 sources, each annotated with one of five manipulation types and two VLM-generated text descriptions.

## Results

On cross-domain facial deepfake detection, SD² achieves a mean AUC of 87.79% across UADFV, WildDeepFake, DFDC, and DF40-Test, outperforming the strongest baseline CLIP-SVD (Effort) by 5.81 points and conventional methods (SPSL, Xception) by 12+ points. On intra-domain evaluation across 11 subsets, SD² reaches 95.76% mAUC, surpassing SPSL (94.58%) and CLIP-SVD (87.48%). On the GenImage AIGC benchmark, SD² attains 88.50% mACC, exceeding the prior SOTA AIDE by 1.62% and CLIP-SVD by 8.56%. Crucially, SD² is the only method that consistently improves with training data scale (82.90% → 84.85% → 87.79% mAUC from 100K to 3M images), while all baselines plateau or degrade.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD² outperforms all 17 baselines across every evaluation setting. Among conventional detectors, Xception (72.67% cross-domain mAUC), RECCE (70.84%), and SPSL (75.68%) are all surpassed by 12–17 points, with the largest gaps on WildDeepFake and DFDC where domain shift is most severe. Among CLIP-based methods, fully fine-tuned CLIP (69.83%) and CLIP-LoRA (72.12%) perform poorly on cross-domain data, while CLIP-SVD/Effort (81.98%) is the strongest baseline but still trails SD² by 5.81 points, particularly on DF40-Test EFS (74.06 vs. 60.60) and WildDeepFake (82.80 vs. 75.89). On the GenImage AIGC benchmark, SD² (88.50% mACC) beats the prior SOTA AIDE (86.88%) by 1.62%, PatchCraft (82.30%) by 6.20%, and CLIP-SVD (79.94%) by 8.56%, with the largest individual gains on VQDM (91.15 vs. 80.26 for AIDE) and BigGAN (79.34 vs. 66.89). The scalability experiment (Table 6) is the most striking differentiator: while CLIP-LoRA degrades from 78.84% to 72.12% and CLIP-SVD plateaus at ~82% as data grows from 100K to 3M, SD² monotonically improves from 82.90% to 87.79%, confirming that the multi-modal supervision strategy is essential for leveraging large-scale heterogeneous data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) as its visual-language backbone and SigLIP (Zhai et al., 2023) for efficient contrastive learning, but departs substantially from prior CLIP-based deepfake detectors that rely on frozen encoders or single-dataset training. Where previous methods (Ojha et al., 2023; Baraldi et al., 2024; Yan et al., 2025a) extract forgery cues from a fixed representation, SD2 fine-tunes both image and text encoders via LoRA and introduces a Cross-Layer Attention Module to fuse intermediate and final-layer features, capturing low-level artifacts that the final layer alone misses. The multi-modal supervision strategy—combining type-specific text labels with VLM-generated facial and environmental descriptions—extends the line of work on language-guided deepfake detection (Sun et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024b) by using rich, per-image descriptions rather than coarse class prompts, and by explicitly separating text label embeddings to stabilize the classification objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of MMI-DD (3.6M images from 11 sources with fine-grained type annotations and VLM descriptions) addresses a critical gap: no prior effort had consolidated the growing body of deepfake benchmarks into a unified, preprocessed training resource, and the dataset is likely to become a standard training corpus for the community. The finding that prior CLIP-based methods degrade with scale while SD2 improves consistently challenges the implicit assumption that more data monotonically helps, suggesting that multi-modal supervision is a prerequisite for exploiting large heterogeneous datasets. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video-level detection, whether the VLM-generated descriptions (which add significant annotation cost) can be replaced by cheaper alternatives, and whether the fine-grained four-type classification provides practical benefit over binary real/fake detection in deployment settings.
