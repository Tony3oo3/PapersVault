## Problem

Existing world models for end-to-end autonomous driving redundantly model static scene elements (ground, buildings) that remain unchanged across timestamps, while under-modeling the dynamic objects that actually require precise future prediction. Additionally, predicted future scene representations are not deeply interacted with the planned trajectory, leaving the world model's output underutilized for planning and vulnerable to collapse without auxiliary supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260229/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view camera images at k+1 timestamps (current plus k past frames, with k=2 on nuScenes), which are converted to BEV features via GeoBEV and aligned into the current frame's coordinate system. On NAVSIM, LiDAR BEV maps are additionally fused via a TransFuser-style architecture. The key input to the world model is not the full scene but the temporal residuals—differences between sparse scene queries extracted at adjacent timestamps—which isolate dynamic object information. The output is a refined ego-vehicle trajectory (N_t waypoints in 2D) plus future BEV features expressed in the current coordinate system, where static objects are inherited directly from the current frame.

## Method

ResWorld introduces two core components. First, the Temporal Residual World Model (TR-World) extracts dynamic object information by subtracting sparse scene queries (obtained via TokenLearner with a shared spatial attention mask) from adjacent timestamps, then processes these residuals through self-attention and accumulates them across time; the result is expanded back onto the current BEV features via TokenFuser to produce future BEV features, with static objects implicitly preserved by the current-frame coordinate system. Second, the Future-Guided Trajectory Refinement (FGTR) module applies deformable attention between waypoint queries and the predicted future BEV features, using the prior trajectory as spatial reference points, which simultaneously refines the trajectory by checking for collisions or drivable-area violations and provides sparse spatial-temporal supervision that prevents world model collapse. Notably, no explicit supervision is applied to the future BEV features themselves—only L1 loss on prior and final trajectories—allowing the model to retain multi-timestamp dynamic information rather than collapsing to a single future frame.

## Results

On nuScenes (VAD evaluation protocol), ResWorld without auxiliary tasks achieves 0.35 m average L2 error and 0.07% average collision rate, outperforming all perception-free baselines. With ego status in the planner, ResWorld further improves to 0.30 m L2 and 0.06% CR. On NAVSIM (closed-loop), ResWorld achieves 88.3% PDMS with detection and map auxiliary tasks, and 89.0% PDMS when leveraging historical frames for temporal residual extraction, setting a new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among perception-free methods on nuScenes, ResWorld (0.35 m L2, 0.07% CR) substantially outperforms SSR (0.39 m, 0.15%) and LAW (0.61 m, 0.30%), reducing average L2 by 10% and collision rate by 53% over SSR. With ego status, ResWorld (0.30 m, 0.06%) surpasses BEV-Planner++ (0.35 m, 0.34%), cutting collision rate by over 80%. On NAVSIM, ResWorld with auxiliary tasks (88.3% PDMS) edges out DiffusionDrive (88.1%) and Hydra-MDP-W-EP (86.5%), while the full model with historical frames reaches 89.0% PDMS. In the perception-free NAVSIM setting, ResWorld (87.3% PDMS) leads LAW (84.6%) and World4Drive (85.1%) by 2.7 and 2.2 points respectively, confirming that temporal residual modeling provides stronger scene understanding than full-scene world models without any auxiliary supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResWorld builds directly on the perception-free planning paradigm established by SSR (Li & Cui, 2025), which introduced sparse scene queries and a world model as a proxy task for scene understanding, and on GeoBEV (Zhang et al., 2025) for generating geometrically aligned multi-timestamp BEV features. The key departure from prior world-model-based planners (OccWorld, LAW, World4Drive, Drive-WM) is the use of temporal residuals of scene queries to isolate dynamic-object information without any detection or tracking module, thereby avoiding the redundant modeling of static objects that plagues existing approaches. The Future-Guided Trajectory Refinement (FGTR) module further distinguishes ResWorld by introducing explicit deformable-attention interaction between prior trajectories and predicted future BEV features, a form of trajectory–scene coupling that prior world-model frameworks lacked entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating auxiliary perception tasks while achieving state-of-the-art planning on both nuScenes and NAVSIM, ResWorld demonstrates that a lightweight, residual-based world model can match or exceed systems relying on full detection, tracking, and map segmentation pipelines. The finding that withholding explicit supervision on future BEV features (relying instead on FGTR's sparse spatial-temporal signal) yields better planning than direct ground-truth supervision is a useful counterintuitive result for world-model design. A notable limitation is that temporal residuals cannot capture potential dynamic objects (e.g., parked cars, stationary pedestrians) that exhibit no motion between frames, leaving a safety gap that the authors acknowledge and plan to address with coarse perception in future work.
