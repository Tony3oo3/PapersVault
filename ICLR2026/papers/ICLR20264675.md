## Problem

IDP-SGD, which enforces per-owner privacy budgets in differentially private training, induces a utility imbalance: data from owners with stronger privacy (smaller ε) become underrepresented in the trained model, causing significantly worse performance on their data at deployment. Existing data-imbalance correction techniques either violate IDP constraints, waste privacy budgets, or are neutralized by gradient clipping, leaving no prior method that addresses this IDP-specific imbalance while preserving each owner's privacy guarantee.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264675/cropped_page16_idx15.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled image dataset (MNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10/100 32×32) partitioned among N data owners, each specifying an individualized privacy budget ε_n that determines their sampling rate q_n and clipping threshold C_n. A tail importance function (TIF) parameterized by a Beta(α,β) distribution and tail length γ controls how aggressively low-loss gradients are down-weighted. The output is a trained CNN (Papernot et al.'s tempered-sigmoid architecture) whose parameters satisfy (α, ε̄)-IRDP per owner, producing class labels at inference.

## Method

INO-SGD sorts per-sample gradients within each batch by descending loss and applies a 'continuized' importance weighting: each datum's clipped gradient is conceptually decomposed into infinitesimal pieces, and a batch importance function (BIF) assigns scores in [0,1] to each piece based on its position relative to a tail of length γ. The average importance score ρ_k for the k-th ranked gradient is computed by integrating the BIF over that gradient's interval, and the gradient is scaled by ρ_k before summation. This strategically discards less important (low-loss) gradient information while keeping the modular sensitivity bounded by each datum's clipping threshold C_{o(d)}, yielding the same IRDP guarantee as IDP-SGD (ε̄_n = 2Tα_n q_n² C_n²/σ²). Theoretically, INO-SGD optimizes a non-increasingly weighted loss (equivalent to a mixed CVaR / ordered weighted average) that assigns larger weights to harder-to-learn data, provably mitigating both the minority initial drop and biased optimization objective that cause the utility imbalance.

## Results

INO-SGD improves per-group model utility by approximately 10% accuracy for the more private data owners on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100-FV without degrading the utility of less private owners (Fig. 5). On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, the per-group recall gains of INO-SGD over IDP-SGD show statistically significant Pearson correlations with privacy budgets (p-value < 0.001), confirming that more private owners benefit most. Overall (aggregated) model utility is preserved or slightly improved: INO-SGD achieves smaller validation loss / higher accuracy than IDP-SGD throughout training on all tested datasets (Fig. 7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is IDP-SGD (Boenisch et al., NeurIPS 2024), the state-of-the-art individualized DP training algorithm. INO-SGD wins on the primary metric of per-group utility for more private owners, delivering ~10% higher accuracy/recall for those groups across MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100-FV while maintaining or slightly improving overall model accuracy. On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (with many owners at varying privacy budgets), INO-SGD's recall gains are positively correlated with privacy stringency (p < 0.001), meaning the most privacy-constrained owners see the largest improvements. INO-SGD also matches or exceeds IDP-SGD on overall (aggregated) validation accuracy and loss, indicating no utility trade-off for the balance correction. The paper does not compare against non-IDP baselines (e.g., standard DP-SGD or non-private SGD) in the main experiments, as the focus is specifically on correcting IDP-induced imbalance while preserving IDP guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on IDP-SGD (Boenisch et al., 2024), which extends DP-SGD to accommodate per-owner privacy budgets via individualized sampling rates (SAMPLE) or clipping thresholds (SCALE). It departs from the standard data-imbalance literature (oversampling, loss rescaling, gradient reweighting) by showing that those techniques either violate IDP, waste privacy budgets, or are neutralized by gradient clipping. It also distinguishes itself from DP disparate-impact work (Bagdasaryan et al., 2019), which attributes accuracy disparity to uniform DP noise and clipping on already-imbalanced data, whereas here the imbalance is *induced* by the individualized privacy mechanism itself on a balanced dataset. The INO-SGD algorithm introduces a tail importance function (TIF) that strategically down-weights low-loss gradients within each batch while provably preserving each owner's modular sensitivity bound, a mechanism not present in prior IDP or DP-SGD variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a previously unrecognized failure mode: even on a perfectly balanced training set, individualized privacy budgets create the same minority-initial-drop and biased-optimization-objective dynamics as class imbalance, degrading model utility for more-private groups during deployment. This has direct practical implications for medical AI, sensitive-attribute classification, and any setting where data owners with different sensitivity levels co-train a single model. The main open question is the IDP-balance-utility tradeoff: improving the worst-off group necessarily costs some utility for less-private owners, and characterizing the Pareto frontier of this tradeoff remains future work. The INO-SGM generalization also opens the door to applying the importance-weighted mechanism beyond SGD to other IDP-compliant algorithms.
