## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) perform near-randomly on Abstract Visual Reasoning (AVR) tasks despite strong general reasoning, and the paper identifies the primary bottleneck as a lack of fine-grained perception for abstract graphics (position, style, attribute, spatial structure) rather than insufficient reasoning. Existing AVR benchmarks lack intermediate perceptual annotations, preventing explicit modeling of the perception-to-reasoning pipeline, and common remedies—model scaling, CoT prompting, and inference-time scaling—fail to close the gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264621/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark presents 1,000 questions across eight categories (Numerical, Stylistic, Attribute, Positional, Spatial, Sudoku, RAVEN, Other), each pairing an abstract visual riddle image with a short question prompt (~32 tokens average). Outputs are task-dependent: seven categories require a single-letter multiple-choice answer (A–D), while Sudoku demands a full 9×9 grid solution and RAVEN requires selecting one of eight pattern options. The synthesizer additionally produces structured JSON perceptual descriptions (layout metadata, element-level shape/position/state properties) alongside each visual instance, which serve as training supervision rather than being part of the evaluation input.

## Method

PAVR is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B through a two-stage paradigm. Stage 1 (SFT) trains for 20 epochs on 20K synthesized AVR instances enriched with structured perceptual descriptions generated by the VisuRiddles Synthesizer, which constructs abstract graphics via rule selection, element configuration, and rendering, then uses Gemini 2.5-Flash-Think to produce validated chain-of-thought reasoning traces from the perceptual descriptions (discarding any where the generated answer mismatches the ground truth). Stage 2 applies GRPO reinforcement learning for 40 epochs on 4K instances with a composite reward (answer correctness + format compliance for  tags), stabilizing perceptual strategy selection (e.g., choosing between symmetry-axis vs. right-angle cues) and strengthening multi-step reasoning. The key insight is that perception must be explicitly trained first—SFT alone lifts average accuracy from 24.6% to 39.5%, and adding GRPO further raises it to 46.8%, outperforming all open-source and commercial MLLMs including Gemini-2.5-Pro (33.9%) and GPT-5 (28.7%).

## Results

On the VisuRiddles benchmark, PAVR (7B) achieves 46.8% average accuracy, substantially outperforming the strongest commercial model Gemini-2.5-Pro (33.9%) and the strongest open-source model Qwen3-VL-235B-Thinking (34.9%) by 12.9 and 11.9 points respectively. The two-stage training paradigm yields consistent gains: SFT alone lifts the 7B baseline from 24.6% to 39.5%, and adding GRPO further improves to 46.8%. On the VisuLogic benchmark, PAVR achieves 30.6% overall accuracy, competitive with the best RL-trained baseline (InternVL2.5-38B-RL at 31.1%) while using only 7B parameters versus 38B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAVR (7B) decisively outperforms all 17 evaluated baselines on VisuRiddles, achieving 46.8% average accuracy versus 33.9% for Gemini-2.5-Pro (the strongest commercial model) and 34.9% for Qwen3-VL-235B-Thinking (the strongest open-source model at 235B parameters). The gap is particularly pronounced on high-level reasoning tasks: PAVR scores 46.0% on Sudoku and 65.0% on RAVEN, compared to 39.0%/30.0% for Gemini-2.5-Pro and 33.0%/49.0% for Qwen3-VL-235B-Thinking. Against GPT-5 (28.7%) and o3 (27.0%), PAVR leads by 18.1 and 19.8 points respectively, despite being an order of magnitude smaller. On the VisuLogic benchmark, PAVR (30.6%) is competitive with the best RL-trained baseline InternVL2.5-38B-RL (31.1%) while using 5.4× fewer parameters, and outperforms all non-RL baselines including Qwen2.5-VL-72B (26.2%) and GPT-4o (26.3%). The ablation confirms that the perceptual SFT stage contributes +14.9 points over the raw baseline, while GRPO adds a further +7.3 points, demonstrating that perception and reasoning are complementary but perception is the dominant bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen2.5-VL as its base architecture and adopts GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) as its RL algorithm, but its conceptual departure is significant: whereas prior AVR work (RAVEN, PuzzleVQA, VisuLogic) and recent MLLM reasoning research (CoT prompting, inference-time scaling, RL-based reasoning) have treated abstract visual reasoning as primarily a reasoning challenge, this paper reframes the bottleneck as fine-grained perception. The VisuRiddles Synthesizer introduces a programmatic approach to generating perceptual descriptions that sidesteps the hallucination problems of using commercial MLLMs to annotate abstract graphics, and the two-stage SFT-then-GRPO paradigm explicitly separates perceptual grounding from reasoning optimization rather than conflating them as prior CoT or RL-only approaches do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, dual-purpose resource (benchmark + synthesizer) that the AVR community can immediately use for both evaluation and training, addressing a gap where existing datasets lack intermediate perceptual annotations. The finding that replacing raw images with structured text descriptions lifts Qwen2.5-VL-72B from 30.9% to 73.6% average accuracy is a strong diagnostic result that redirects attention toward perception as the primary failure mode. However, the synthetic data's diversity is limited by the rule library and icon set, and it remains open whether the perception-first training strategy generalizes beyond the specific AVR subtasks tested or to other domains requiring fine-grained visual understanding (e.g., scientific diagrams, medical imaging).
