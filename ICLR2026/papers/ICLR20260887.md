## Problem

Existing text-to-audio (TTA) models can generate plausible single audio events but fail dramatically when prompts require multiple events with correct spatial, temporal, perceptual, or compositional relations. Prior investigations (e.g., RiTTA) confirmed this limitation but relied on small-scale relation and event corpora, preventing systematic study at scale. No large-scale, high-quality benchmark existed to diagnose and drive progress in relation-aware TTA generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260887/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a natural-language text prompt describing one or more audio events and their relations, written in a 'Head-Modifier Structure with Progressive Verb Form' style (e.g., 'food frying audio followed by water dripping audio'). The output is a 10-second audio waveform at 16 kHz sampling rate. Text prompts are generated by instantiating relation-specific templates (5 per relation) with event names drawn from a synonym list, and the relation's arity (1–5 events) determines how many events must appear in the generated audio.

## Method

Aurelius introduces two disentangled, tree-structured corpora: AudioEventSet (110 fine-grained events across 7 categories, depth-3 hierarchy, ~75 realistic snippets per event) and AudioRelSet (100 relations across 6 categories—Temporality, Spatiality, Count, Perceptuality, Compositionality, and Nested Combination—depth-2 hierarchy, each with an explicit arity). A pair-generation strategy combines events and relations via curated text templates to produce nearly unlimited <text, audio> pairs (36,000 training / 10,000 test, 100 h / 28 h). The paper benchmarks 9 state-of-the-art TTA models (diffusion- and flow-matching-based) plus two agentic LLM-decomposition workflows under both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings, evaluating with general metrics (FAD, FD, KL) and a multi-stage relation-aware (MSR) protocol (mAPre, mARel, mAPar, mAMSR). Key empirical findings include: all zero-shot models achieve <10% relation-aware accuracy; fine-tuning TangoFlux on the curated data yields the largest gains (mAMSR 1.77→5.58); and training from scratch continues to improve with data scale (100→300 h) while fine-tuning saturates, indicating that scaling relation-aware TTA ultimately requires massive dedicated datasets.

## Results

Across 9 zero-shot TTA models benchmarked on the Aurelius relation-aware evaluation, all methods achieve less than 10% on relation-aware metrics, confirming the task's difficulty. TangoFlux leads in event presence (mAPre=12.38) and relation correctness (mARel=3.34), while AudioGen leads in parsimony (mAPar=9.13) and overall MSR score (mAMSR=2.22). Fine-tuning TangoFlux on the curated 100-hour dataset nearly doubles relation-aware performance (mAPre=28.57, mARel=8.02, mAPar=20.84, mAMSR=5.58), and training from scratch at 300 hours continues to improve, whereas fine-tuning saturates by 200 hours. Agentic workflow baselines (Qwen + TangoFlux) perform substantially worse than direct TTA inference, with mAMSR dropping to 0.04–0.60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 9 zero-shot TTA baselines, TangoFlux (ICLR 2026) achieves the highest event presence (mAPre=12.38) and relation correctness (mARel=3.34), while AudioGen (ICLR 2023) leads in parsimony (mAPar=9.13) and overall MSR (mAMSR=2.22); both remain below 13% on any relation-aware metric. Tango 2 (ACM MM 2024) is the second-best overall zero-shot method (mAMSR=1.29), outperforming Tango (0.79), AudioLDM variants (0.63–0.73), MakeAnAudio (0.73), Auffusion (0.79), and LAFMA (0.07). AudioLDM2 (TASLP 2024) and LAFMA (Interspeech 2024) are the weakest, with mAMSR of only 0.03 and 0.07 respectively, suggesting their architectures or training objectives are particularly ill-suited for multi-event relational generation. The two agentic workflow baselines (Qwen 27B/32B + TangoFlux) perform far worse than direct TangoFlux inference (mAMSR 0.04–0.60 vs. 1.77), indicating that decomposing relation-aware prompts into single-event sub-prompts via an LLM planner does not help and actually degrades generation quality. After fine-tuning on the 100-hour Aurelius dataset, TangoFlux improves most dramatically (mAMSR 1.77→5.58), while Tango shows minimal gain from fine-tuning versus training from scratch (2.73 vs. 2.64), suggesting architecture-specific limits on cross-domain knowledge transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends RiTTA (He et al., 2025), which first demonstrated that existing TTA models fail at relation-aware generation but was limited to 11 relations and ~27 audio events. By decoupling audio events (AudioEventSet, 110 classes) from relations (AudioRelSet, 100 relations) into independently scalable, tree-structured corpora, the authors create a combinatorial pair-generation strategy that yields orders of magnitude more training and test data than prior relation-aware TTA work. The benchmarking scope also extends beyond RiTTA by covering both diffusion-based and flow-matching-based TTA models, and by introducing an agentic LLM-decomposition workflow as a novel experimental setup for multi-event generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that no existing TTA model achieves meaningful relation-aware generation (all below 10% on relation metrics), and the dramatic 75%→12%→3% collapse from single-event to multi-event to relation fidelity quantifies a fundamental blind spot in current architectures. The finding that fine-tuning saturates around 200–300 hours while training from scratch continues to improve suggests that relation-aware TTA will require substantially larger datasets than general TTA, raising open questions about data efficiency and whether architectural changes (explicit event-relation decomposition) are necessary. The agentic workflow's poor performance—worse than direct generation—challenges the intuitive assumption that LLM-based task decomposition can bridge the relation-modeling gap, pointing toward the need for native multi-event generation capabilities.
