## Problem

Vision-language models used for OCR frequently hallucinate on degraded document images, producing fluent but incorrect text without any signal of uncertainty. Current post-training objectives emphasize transcription accuracy, which incentivizes the model to guess even when visual evidence is insufficient, and errors appear plausible enough to propagate silently into downstream analytics and digitization pipelines. No existing method trains VLMs to explicitly mark which spans of their own transcription they consider unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262160/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded document image (subject to blur, low resolution, compression artifacts, occlusion, stains, scratches, and other synthetic corruptions) and produces a full-page transcription in which spans the model judges unreliable are enclosed in paired <C>...</C> uncertainty tags. Tags can appear multiple times per page, must be well-formed (no nesting or overlap), and are evaluated at both character and word granularities. The output preserves natural reading order, line breaks, and the original language/punctuation/casing of the source document.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-phase training pipeline: (1) a pseudo-labeled cold start where a frozen base VLM transcribes degraded images, each prediction is aligned to ground truth to localize substitutions/insertions/deletions, and UNC tags are inserted into the model's own transcript (not the GT) around detected errors, followed by SFT; and (2) Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a multi-objective sequence-level reward R = max{0, (1−e_L) + λ·η·e_L·F_β,L} that jointly optimizes transcription accuracy and uncertainty-tag F_β while a length-mismatch damping factor η prevents reward hacking via truncation or over-generation. A key empirical finding is that a character-level cold start paired with a word-level reward yields the best calibration, because character-level tags teach tight boundaries while the word-level reward provides denser credit for stable improvement. The reward is designed so that 0 < λ < 1 guarantees the upper bound R_max(e) = 1 − (1−λ)e strictly decreases with error, eliminating any incentive to manufacture and tag errors. The approach is instantiated on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and InternVL2.5, and evaluated on the newly introduced Blur-OCR benchmark (107,520 training / 2,048 evaluation images with diverse synthetic degradations from Project Gutenberg sources).

## Results

On the Blur-OCR benchmark, the proposed uncertainty-aware Qwen-2.5-VL-7B model achieves a word-level uncertainty-tag F1 of 0.685 (precision 0.839, recall 0.620) while maintaining 0.839 word-level transcription accuracy. The error-rate gap between inside and outside UNC spans is 0.739, indicating strong calibration of uncertainty localization. These results substantially outperform all general VLM baselines (best: Claude-opus-4 at word F1 0.205) and conventional uncertainty-quantification methods (best: multi-model Ensembles at word F1 0.491).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins on word-level uncertainty-tag F1 against every baseline. Against general VLMs prompted to emit UNC tags, the gap is dramatic: GPT-4o achieves only 0.039 word F1 and Qwen-2.5-VL-72B 0.013, while the best general VLM (Claude-opus-4) reaches 0.205—still less than one-third of the proposed 0.685. Against the two conventional UQ baselines, the method also wins clearly: multi-model Esembles achieves 0.491 word F1 (high recall 0.742 but low precision 0.487), and the token-entropy baseline on Qwen-7B reaches 0.410 word F1. On transcription accuracy, the proposed model (0.839) is essentially tied with the entropy baseline (0.840) and GPT-4o (0.839), while OCR-specialized systems (MinerU2.5 at 0.732, PaddleOCR at 0.308, GOT at 0.219) cannot emit uncertainty tags and thus have no F1/Gap scores. The error-rate gap metric (0.739) further confirms that the method localizes errors far more effectively than any baseline, with the next-best (Esembles) at only 0.418.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-OCR line (GOT, Dolphin, MinerU2.5, Nougat) by adding an explicit uncertainty-tagging objective, and adapts GRPO from DeepSeekMath to optimize a composite reward that jointly balances transcription accuracy and span-level uncertainty coverage. It departs from prior uncertainty-estimation approaches for VLMs (token-level entropy, multi-model ensembles, post-hoc disagreement) by training the model to emit structured <C>…</C> delimiters during generation, making uncertainty a first-class output rather than a post-hoc signal. The pseudo-labeled cold start—tagging the model's own errors rather than ground-truth positions—is a novel initialization strategy that addresses the train/inference mismatch inherent in GT-based tagging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VLM OCR hallucinations on degraded documents are a practical reliability bottleneck for cultural-heritage digitization, RAG pipelines, and downstream analytics, yet current systems produce fluent but unsupported text without any uncertainty signal. The Blur-OCR benchmark (107k training / 2k eval images with diverse synthetic degradations) is the first dedicated resource for evaluating uncertainty-aware OCR, and the GRPO-based training recipe with anti-reward-hacking damping is a reusable recipe for any VLM task requiring calibrated self-reporting. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond document OCR (e.g., ASR, compressed visual tokens), how to handle genuinely unreadable regions where no transcription is possible, and whether the synthetic-degradation distribution adequately captures real-world document wear.
