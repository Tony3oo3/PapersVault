## Problem

Linear attention models (GLA, Mamba, DeltaNet) manage their fixed-size hidden state through decay gates that only modulate key-value norms, lacking the complementary rotation mechanism that softmax attention performs implicitly. The paper shows via Random Fourier Features that softmax attention encodes relative position through input-dependent selective rotations, and that purely rotational (complex-only) linear recurrences suffer from spectral leakage without a decaying component. The missing ingredient in linear models is precisely this rotation, and the paper formalizes why both rotation and decay are jointly necessary for strong recall.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text sequence and, at each position, computes rotation angles from the current query (or ℓ²-normalized input) via a learned weight-normalized projection, a 1D convolution, a SiLU activation, and a cumulative sum, yielding per-channel angles that are then passed through a sigmoid phase gate. These angles parameterize 2×2 rotation matrices applied to interleaved real/imaginary pairs of the query and key vectors, producing rotated q/k pairs that feed into the existing linear-attention recurrence. The final output is the standard language-model logits; the recurrent state is complex-valued in principle but computed entirely in the real domain via the RoPE trick, so no additional memory or arithmetic overhead is incurred beyond a small prelude to the existing RoPE kernel.

## Method

Selective RoPE replaces the fixed, index-dependent rotation angles of standard RoPE with input-dependent, learnable angles computed from the query at each timestep, making the state transition matrix R_t = diag(exp(i·Ω·(q_t − q_{t−1}))) both input-dependent and norm-preserving. The theoretical contribution has two parts: (i) an RFF-based derivation showing that softmax attention's exponential kernel decomposes into a recurrence with a diagonal input-dependent rotation matrix, and (ii) a spectral-leakage analysis of diagonal SSMs showing that the imaginary (rotation) part extracts frequency content while the real (decay) part acts as a window to suppress leakage. In practice, Selective RoPE is composed with the existing forget gate of GLA (or FoX, Gated DeltaNet) by factorizing the transition as A_t = Λ_t · R_t, so the model retains both selective forgetting and relative-position encoding. The implementation reuses the RoPE trick to express the complex recurrence as real-valued rotations on interleaved query/key pairs, enabling drop-in integration with flash-linear-attention kernels and standard RoPE code paths.

## Results

On 370M-parameter GLA trained on 10B FineWeb tokens, Selective RoPE achieves 47.1% macro-average downstream accuracy (vs. 46.8% NoPE and 46.5% RoPE) and 16.33 LAMBADA perplexity. At 1.3B scale (26B tokens), Selective RoPE reaches 54.6% average accuracy and 17.87 WikiText perplexity, the best perplexity among all variants. On synthetic recall tasks, Selective RoPE + phase gate & bias achieves 73.2 average on the MAD benchmark (vs. 65.9 NoPE, 72.5 RoPE), and GLA with Selective RoPE is the only single-layer variant to solve the S₂ parity state-tracking task and length-extrapolate on A₃.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RoPE, Selective RoPE consistently improves macro-average downstream accuracy: +0.6 on GLA 370M (47.1 vs 46.5), +1.0 on FoX 370M (46.1 vs 45.1), +0.8 on Gated DeltaNet 400M (47.5 vs 46.7), and +0.2 on GLA 1.3B (54.6 vs 54.4). Against NoPE, the gains are larger at 370M scale (+0.3 GLA, +0.8 FoX, +0.9 DeltaNet) but Selective RoPE trails NoPE by 0.6 points on GLA 1.3B (54.6 vs 55.2), though it still achieves the best WikiText perplexity (17.87 vs 18.18). On synthetic recall tasks the advantage is more pronounced: Selective RoPE + phase gate & bias scores 73.2 on MAD (vs 72.5 RoPE, 65.9 NoPE), achieves the best MQAR recall, and is the only single-layer GLA variant to solve S₂ parity and A₃ state tracking with length extrapolation, where both NoPE and RoPE fail to learn even the training context length. The paper notes that Selective RoPE shifts the perplexity–accuracy trade-off in a model-dependent way, sometimes improving one at the expense of the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: (1) the RFF-based approximation of softmax attention (Performer, RFA), (2) RoPE's fixed-angle rotations in softmax Transformers, and (3) selective gating in linear attention (GLA, Mamba, DeltaNet). The key conceptual departure is showing that softmax attention's exponential kernel, when decomposed via RFF, implicitly performs input-dependent rotations of query-key pairs — a capability entirely absent in standard linear attention, which only modulates norms through decay gates. The proposed Selective RoPE generalizes RoPE from fixed, index-dependent angles to learned, input-dependent angles and composes with existing forget gates, bridging the complex-valued SSM literature (S4D, Orvieto et al.) with practical linear attention architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rotation-plus-decay design principle provides a clear theoretical recipe for closing the recall gap between linear and softmax attention, and the method is trivially implementable via the RoPE trick with minimal overhead. However, the language-modeling gains are modest and architecture-dependent (Selective RoPE does not consistently outperform RoPE on perplexity or macro-average accuracy at 370M/1.3B scale), and the paper explicitly defers the important question of length extrapolation, where RoPE is known to be detrimental. Open questions include whether the diagonal-vs-scalar gate choice interacts with the rotation component, how Selective RoPE composes with other RoPE variants (YaRN, NTK-aware), and whether the spectral-leakage framing generalizes beyond diagonal state transitions.
