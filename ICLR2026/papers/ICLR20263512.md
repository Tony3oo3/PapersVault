## Problem

VLN agents trained on static datasets suffer catastrophic forgetting when adapting to new environments, and existing continual learning approaches rely on rehearsal buffers that replay past trajectories, introducing storage overhead and privacy concerns. The paper argues that effective continual VLN requires explicitly decoupling global scene-level reasoning (e.g., office vs. residential layout patterns) from local token-level perceptual alignment, rather than conflating both in a monolithic policy.

## Input / Output

At each navigation step, the agent consumes a natural-language instruction, a set of 36 discretized panoramic views (encoded by a pretrained ViT into viewpoint-level scene embeddings), and an online-constructed cognitive map of visited and frontier nodes. The output is a single-step action selecting the next reachable viewpoint from the candidate set, repeated autoregressively until a STOP token is emitted or the step budget is exhausted. The cognitive map is built incrementally from the agent's own exploration history rather than from any pre-defined global map.

## Method

M³E replaces the standard FFN layers in a NaviLLM-based LLM backbone with MoE-LoRA layers (6 experts, Top-K=2, LoRA rank 16) governed by a dual-router architecture. The Macro Router performs topology-aware, task-focused (TATF) routing: it feeds the cognitive map's node features and sparse adjacency into a GNN for spatial message passing, then applies instruction-embedding-guided attention to produce a scene-level expert weight vector. The Micro Router computes token-wise expert weights directly from LLM hidden states, capturing fine-grained linguistic semantics (e.g., action verbs vs. object nouns). The two signals are fused via convex interpolation (β=0.3) to yield final routing weights. For continual learning without replay, a Dynamic MoE Momentum Update accumulates per-expert routing weights across all tokens in the current task, selects the top-K most active experts, and consolidates parameters via element-wise interpolation with differentiated momentum (γ=0.4 for task-critical experts, 1−γ for the rest), enabling rapid adaptation while preserving generalizable knowledge in less-relevant experts.

## Results

On R2R under domain-incremental continual learning, M³E achieves 71.92% AvgSR, 66.96% AvgSPL, and 2.95m AvgNE, with near-zero backward transfer (BWT = 0.04) and the highest forward transfer (FWT = 2.15) among all methods. On the more challenging REVERIE benchmark, M³E reaches 48.30% SPL versus 39.86% for naive finetuning, while reducing catastrophic forgetting from BWT −16.91 to −5.91. The method is replay-free, requiring no storage of past trajectories, yet outperforms all rehearsal-based and regularization-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M³E outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. Against regularization methods, it surpasses EWC (Kirkpatrick et al.) by +7.77% AvgSR and +6.75% AvgSPL while achieving BWT 0.04 vs. −3.50, and dominates L2 by +13.14% AvgSR. Against rehearsal-based methods, M³E beats the strongest (Dual-SR, Li et al.) by +1.67% AvgSR and +1.56% AvgSPL, and outperforms ESR (Jeong et al.) by +3.80% AvgSR and +3.08% AvgSPL, all without storing any past trajectories. On the full val-unseen post-training protocol (Table 3), M³E shows positive gains on R2R val-seen (+2.15% SR) while NaviLLM (Zheng et al.) degrades by −11.18% SR on REVERIE val-seen and HAMT (Chen et al.) drops −12.54% SR on R2R val-seen. In the continual TTA comparison (Table 9), M³E achieves 66.96% AvgSPL with BWT 0.04, whereas FSTTA (Gao et al.) and FeedTTA (Kim et al.) reach only 63.45% and 63.84% AvgSPL respectively with BWT −6.04 and −5.78, confirming that architectural expert isolation provides superior long-term retention over gradient-based test-time adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NaviLLM (Zheng et al., CVPR 2024) as its end-to-end trainable LLM navigation backbone, replacing standard FFN layers with MoE-LoRA modules. Conceptually, it extends the MoE-based continual learning idea from CL-MoE (Huai et al., 2025) for visual question answering into the sequential, partially-observable VLN domain, where the hierarchical structure of navigation (global scene layout vs. local instruction grounding) motivates a dual-router decomposition absent in prior work. It departs from the dominant rehearsal-based continual VLN approaches (PerR/ESR, Dual-SR) by being entirely replay-free, and from regularization methods (EWC, L2) by using architectural parameter isolation via expert specialization rather than loss-based constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first replay-free MoE framework for continual VLN, addressing practical scalability and privacy concerns inherent in trajectory-replay methods. The macro-micro decomposition offers a principled inductive bias aligned with the hierarchical nature of navigation, and the near-zero backward transfer (BWT ≈ 0) without any stored past data is a strong practical signal for deploying agents in privacy-sensitive or storage-constrained settings. Key limitations include evaluation only in discrete simulators (Matterport3D), a fixed expert count, and a single LLM backbone; open questions include whether the approach transfers to continuous control, whether dynamic expert allocation is needed for truly open-ended environments, and how it behaves under Sim-to-Real domain gaps.
