## Problem

Existing graph-based category-agnostic pose estimation (CAPE) methods rely on user-provided unweighted pose-graphs, which are suboptimal because edge strengths should vary per instance and per category. No prior work has predicted optimal weighted pose-graphs in a category-agnostic setting, as self-attention-based edge prediction fails to model structural relations, GCN variants assume fixed topologies, and generalizing to unseen categories demands implicit 3D anatomical understanding that is impractical to specify manually.

## Input / Output

The method consumes one or few support images with annotated keypoints, a query image, a keypoint definition, and a user-provided unweighted binary adjacency matrix A_prior (the skeletal skeleton). It outputs refined keypoint coordinates on the query image via a DETR-style iterative decoder, along with a predicted right-stochastic weighted adjacency matrix that encodes instance-specific edge strengths. The predicted graph is not a standalone output but is consumed internally by both the GCN feed-forward layers and the Markov attention bias in the decoder, making the graph a learned intermediate representation rather than a user-facing artifact.

## Method

EdgeCape refines the user-provided unweighted graph by predicting a residual adjacency matrix ΔA via pairwise cosine similarities of structure-aware keypoint features, which are produced by a dual-attention graph decoder that cross-attends between support image features and keypoint features to capture global structural context. The residual is combined with A_prior through a learnable scalar (initialized to zero for training stability), passed through ReLU, symmetrized, and row-normalized into a right-stochastic matrix. Training uses a self-supervised masking strategy: support keypoint features are randomly masked and replaced with learnable tokens, and the adjacency predictor is optimized (with the decoder frozen) to minimize the L1 localization error on the resulting predictions, ensuring the graph encodes information useful for overcoming occlusions. A Markov Attention Bias is added to the decoder's self-attention by concatenating powers of the stochastic adjacency matrix (A^0 through A^{K-1}) into a per-pair vector, passing it through an MLP, and adding the result to the attention logits, thereby encoding multi-hop structural distances as a continuous, learnable bias. The full system is trained in three sequential phases—base model, skeleton predictor, then Markov bias—to ensure stable convergence of each component.

## Results

On the MP-100 benchmark (PCK@0.2), EdgeCape achieves 89.42% average in the 1-shot setting and 91.87% in the 5-shot setting, setting new state-of-the-art results. On the mPCK metric (mean PCK over thresholds 0.05–0.2), it reaches 77.72% (1-shot) and 81.07% (5-shot), improving over the previous graph-based SOTA GraphCape by +1.99% and +0.9% respectively. On the AP-10K single-category animal pose benchmark, EdgeCape achieves 72.48% PCK@0.1, 87.29% AUC, and 0.085 NME, outperforming both CapeFormer and GraphCape.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EdgeCape outperforms all nine compared baselines on MP-100 PCK@0.2 in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. Against the closest graph-based competitor GraphCape (Hirschorn & Avidan, 2024), it gains +0.92% (1-shot) and +0.37% (5-shot), with larger margins at stricter thresholds (+4.0% and +2.23% at PCK@0.05). Over the previous non-graph SOTA SCAPE (Liang et al., 2024), improvements are +1.62% (1-shot) and +0.5% (5-shot). Against CapeFormer (Shi et al., 2023), the gains are +2.15% (1-shot) and +0.67% (5-shot). The remaining point-based baselines—POMNet, ESCAPE, MetaPoint+, SDPNet, X-Pose, and FMMP—are all surpassed by 2–11 percentage points, with the largest gaps over POMNet (+9.72% 1-shot) and ESCAPE (+6.68% 1-shot). Notably, EdgeCape also wins in the challenging super cross-category setting (e.g., 91.38% on Human Body vs. 88.38% for GraphCape) and remains robust when the input pose-graph is noisy or fully connected, where GraphCape degrades to CapeFormer-level performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GraphCape (Hirschorn & Avidan, 2024), which first introduced user-provided unweighted pose-graphs into category-agnostic pose estimation, by learning to predict instance-specific weighted edge adjustments rather than relying on a fixed binary adjacency matrix. The two-stage DETR-like architecture and similarity-aware proposal generator are inherited from CapeFormer (Shi et al., 2023), while the Markov Attention Bias draws on graph-transformer literature—specifically the shortest-path attention bias of Ying et al. (2021) and the stochastic-process encodings of Ma et al. (2023)—to replace discrete hop-count biases with a continuous, learnable function over multi-hop transition probabilities. The key conceptual departure from prior graph-based CAPE methods is treating the pose-graph as a learnable, real-valued object refined from a user prior, rather than a fixed structural constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that even modest improvements in structural priors (weighted edges + Markov bias) yield consistent gains across all MP-100 splits, and that the predicted graphs are robust to noisy or even fully-connected input topologies—suggesting the model can recover useful structure without reliable human annotation. A notable limitation is that gains shrink in higher-shot settings, and the method adds ~2 ms latency and 2.5M parameters, which may matter for deployment. Open questions include combining EdgeCape with point-based CAPE methods (which the authors note are orthogonal), extending to real-time inference, and whether the Markov bias generalizes to other graph-structured vision tasks beyond pose estimation.
