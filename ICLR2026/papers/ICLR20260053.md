## Problem

Training dense retrievers typically requires annotated query-document pairs, which are costly to create and scarce in specialized domains (e.g., code, law) or complex reasoning-intensive settings. This paper asks how the self-supervised next-token prediction objective used to train language models can be adapted to learn inter-sequence dependencies, thereby training retrievers directly from raw text without any query-document supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260053/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, Revela consumes only raw text chunks (≤120 words for code, Wikipedia passages for general retrieval) grouped into batches of 16, with no separate query or document inputs. At inference, the retriever encodes queries and documents (up to 2048 tokens) into dense embeddings via the EOS token representation, with 'Query:' and 'Passage:' prefixes prepended. The output is a pairwise cosine similarity score between query and document embeddings, used for ranking.

## Method

Revela augments a standard decoder-only transformer with an in-batch attention mechanism that allows each sequence to attend to all other sequences in the batch during next-token prediction. The retriever (a separate encoder sharing the same backbone) computes temperature-scaled softmax cosine similarities between all chunk pairs, and these similarity scores weight the cross-document attention outputs, so the retriever is jointly optimized with the LM via the NTP loss. Concretely, each transformer block produces two streams: a self-attention output e (standard causal attention within the sequence) and an in-batch attention output h, where h combines self-attention over the sequence's own prefix with a weighted sum of cross-document attentions b_ij computed using cached keys and values from other sequences' self-attention outputs. The final in-batch attention output is h = s + b, where s is the self-attention contribution and b is the similarity-weighted cross-document contribution. Training uses LoRA (rank 256) on both the retriever and LM, with a temperature of 1e-4, on 4 A100 GPUs for one epoch (~44–48 hours), requiring no query-document pairs or synthetic data.

## Results

On CoIR (code retrieval, nDCG@10), Revela_3B achieves 60.1%, surpassing the 7B supervised E5-Mistral-7B-Instruct (57.3%) by 2.8 points and the weakly-supervised E5-PT (46.4%) by 9.7 points at comparable 0.5B scale (56.1%). On BRIGHT (reasoning-intensive retrieval), Revela_3B reaches 20.1% nDCG@10, outperforming E5-Mistral (17.9%) and proprietary APIs (Cohere 17.9%, OpenAI 17.9%, Voyage 17.9%). On BEIR (general-domain), Revela_3B achieves 45.2% nDCG@10, matching E5-PT (45.6%) while using approximately 1000× less training data and 10× less compute. Performance scales consistently with model size from 0.1B to 3B parameters across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Revela_3B (60.1% nDCG@10) outperforms the much larger supervised E5-Mistral-7B-Instruct (57.3%) by 2.8 points on CoIR, despite requiring no query-document pairs. At comparable 0.5B scale, Revela (56.1%) surpasses E5-PT (46.4%) by 9.7 points and BGE-M3 (49.1%) by 7.0 points, both of which were pre-trained on massive curated text pairs. Against the self-supervised REPLUG baseline, Revela wins at every scale: 48.4% vs 48.2% (1B) and 60.1% vs 53.9% (3B) on CoIR, and 42.7% vs 36.3% (1B) and 45.2% vs 36.3% (3B) on BEIR. On BRIGHT, Revela_3B (20.1%) beats E5-Mistral (17.9%) and all proprietary APIs (Cohere 17.9%, OpenAI 17.9%, Voyage 17.9%). In controlled experiments with identical LLaMA-3.2-1B backbones, Revela outperforms Contriever on both in-domain (CoIR: 58.6% vs 52.1%) and out-of-domain (BEIR: 39.6% vs 32.3%) settings, demonstrating stronger cross-domain generalization than contrastive learning. On BEIR, Revela_3B (45.2%) matches E5-PT (45.6%) while using ~1000× less training data and 10× less compute, and outperforms LaPraDoR by over 3 points at the 0.1B scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

REVELA departs from the dominant self-supervised retriever learning paradigms—contrastive learning with pseudo query-document pairs (Contriever, E5-PT) and LM-perplexity distillation (REPLUG, Atlas)—by reframing retrieval as an integral part of the next-token prediction objective itself. The key conceptual move is to treat inter-document dependencies as analogous to intra-sequence token dependencies in language modeling, implemented through an in-batch attention mechanism whose weights are computed by the retriever's similarity scores. This positions the retriever not as a separate module trained with a proxy signal, but as a co-optimized component of the LM's attention, following the decoder-only architecture trend established by RepLLaMA and REPLUG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for annotated or synthetic query-document pairs entirely, REVELA removes a major practical bottleneck for training retrievers in specialized or rapidly evolving domains (code, law, scientific literature). The finding that a 3B self-supervised model can surpass a 7B supervised model on code retrieval while matching E5-PT (trained on 270M pairs) with ~1000× less data suggests that the annotation bottleneck may be less fundamental than previously assumed. Open questions include whether the in-batch attention's fixed batch size (16) limits the effective context of inter-document modeling, how the approach scales to iterative indexing over large corpora, and whether the paradigm generalizes to multimodal retrieval as the authors speculate.
