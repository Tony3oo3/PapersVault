## Problem

Neural network-based actor-critic RL methods deliver strong performance but remain opaque black boxes, limiting their use in safety-critical domains requiring transparent decision-making. Existing interpretable alternatives either depend on predefined domain knowledge, act as post-hoc approximations of neural teachers, or produce complex mathematical expressions that are not truly simulatable by humans. The paper asks whether a policy can be learned directly from environmental interactions that is both competitive with deep RL baselines and intrinsically interpretable without external rule-generation tools.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard environment state vectors (continuous or discrete, ranging from 2-D MountainCar to high-dimensional multi-zone HVAC states) and scalar reward signals, with no requirement for predefined rules or domain priors. It produces a discrete action selected via softmax over per-action rule ensemble scores, alongside the full set of learned IF-THEN rules (Boolean conjunctions of threshold comparisons on state features, each with a real-valued weight) that constitute the policy. The rule budget is fixed (e.g., 12 rules per action), keeping the output compact and auditable, and the critic's value estimates are internal to the training loop rather than part of the final policy artifact.

## Method

NSAC replaces the neural actor in A2C with per-action additive rule ensembles while retaining a standard neural network critic for value estimation. Each action's ensemble is a weighted sum of Boolean query functions (conjunctions of threshold comparisons), and the policy is a softmax over these ensemble scores. New rules are discovered via Orthogonal Gradient Boosting, which selects the candidate query maximizing alignment with the orthogonalized policy-gradient residual, ensuring each new rule captures information not already explained by existing rules. A rule-replacement step (removing the smallest-weight rule before adding a new one) maintains a fixed rule budget, and fully-corrective weight re-optimization after each addition guarantees the ensemble stays near the empirical risk minimizer. Convergence to a local optimum is established via two-timescale stochastic approximation, with the critic tracking the true value function on a fast timescale and the rule-based actor following the true policy gradient on a slow timescale, supported by L2 regularization that ensures local strong convexity of the actor loss.

## Results

NSAC achieves the best mean episodic return on MountainCar-v0 (−132.25 ± 16.08), HVAC-1Zone (−1,251,321 ± 85,241), and HVAC-5Zone (−1,463,601 ± 121,678), and near-best on CartPole-v1 (499.14 ± 1.91, vs. SYMPOL's 500). On the challenging multi-zone HVAC benchmark, NSAC outperforms the strongest deep RL baseline (SDSAC) by approximately 120,000 reward units on 1-zone and 119,000 on 5-zone. Across all seven environments, NSAC uses an average of 61.23 symbolic units (parameters) per action ensemble, compared to 95.6 for SYMPOL, while matching or exceeding the performance of all neural and symbolic baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against deep RL baselines, NSAC wins on MountainCar (−132.25 vs. DQN's −135.07 and PPO's −150.40) and both HVAC zones (beating the best deep RL method SDSAC by ~120K on 1-zone and ~119K on 5-zone), ties PPO on CartPole (499.14 vs. 498.73) and Blackjack (−0.06 vs. −0.06), and trails PPO slightly on Acrobot (−87.71 vs. −82.63) and Q-table on Postman (27.14 vs. 35.24). Against symbolic baselines, NSAC dominates: SYMPOL achieves perfect 500 on CartPole but collapses to −200 on MountainCar and Acrobot, while π_affine-D and D-SDT are far behind on most tasks (e.g., π_affine-D scores −425 on Acrobot vs. NSAC's −88). The HVAC results are the most striking: NSAC's rule-based actor outperforms all seven deep RL methods by a wide margin, suggesting that the interpretable rule structure provides a useful inductive bias in high-dimensional, coupled control settings. Overall, NSAC matches or exceeds the best neural baseline on 5 of 7 environments and clearly outperforms all three symbolic baselines on 6 of 7, while using fewer parameters (61.23 vs. 95.6 symbolic units for SYMPOL).

## Relation to Prior Work

NSAC sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: the A2C actor-critic framework (Mnih et al., 2016) and additive rule ensembles learned via Orthogonal Gradient Boosting (Friedman & Popescu, 2008; Yang et al., 2024). It departs from prior interpretable RL in a specific way: rather than using hierarchical decision trees (which face a scalability-interpretability trade-off), post-hoc distillation from neural teachers (which introduces approximation error), or symbolic policies requiring predefined domain knowledge, NSAC trains a flat rule-ensemble actor directly via policy gradients on environmental rewards, with a neural critic providing advantage estimates. The two-timescale convergence analysis (building on Borkar & Meyn, 2000; Wu et al., 2020) is tailored to this specific neural-critic/symbolic-actor coupling, which had not been formally analyzed before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: safety-critical RL deployments (HVAC biocontainment, healthcare, finance) require policies whose decisions can be audited, and NSAC provides this without sacrificing competitive performance. The HVAC benchmark with Sinergym is a meaningful step toward real-world applicability, and the finding that 12 rules per action suffice to match deep RL on these tasks is encouraging for auditability. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to discrete action spaces and low-to-medium dimensional feature spaces, the rule budget caps expressiveness, and scaling to continuous actions or high-dimensional inputs (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving) is left as future work. Whether the convergence guarantees hold under the practical approximations of finite-sample OGB and rule replacement in non-stationary RL settings is an open question.
