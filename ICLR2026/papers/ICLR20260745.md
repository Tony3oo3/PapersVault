## Problem

Training-free model merging methods rely on hand-tuned coefficients and operate purely in parameter space without explicit task-level alignment, while existing training-based methods either minimize prediction entropy without anchoring to expert behavior or only control local linear interference on selected layers. Additionally, all trainable approaches assign a single coefficient per layer, ignoring the substantial inter-layer heterogeneity in parameter counts, learned coefficient magnitudes, and task sensitivity that the paper's analysis reveals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen base model, K frozen SFT expert models, and a tiny unlabeled calibration set (5–10 samples per task; text-only for LLMs, image+text pairs for MLLMs) and produces a single merged model whose parameters equal the base model plus a weighted sum of expert task vectors. The only trainable parameters are K×L layer-wise scalars (or a slightly larger set of chunk-wise scalars in Expert Merging++); the base and expert weights remain frozen throughout. Calibration inputs are used solely to compute forward-pass hidden states and logits for alignment—no labels are required at any stage.

## Method

Expert Merging parameterizes the merged model as θ_merged = θ_base + Σ_k α_k^ℓ τ_k^ℓ with one learnable scalar per expert–layer pair, and optimizes these coefficients by jointly minimizing a hidden-state L2 loss (matching intermediate representations at selected layers) and a temperature-scaled KL divergence on pre-softmax logits (matching predictive distributions) against the corresponding expert on task-partitioned unlabeled inputs. A coefficient L1 regularizer anchored to Task Arithmetic–validated initial values prevents overfitting on the tiny calibration set, while explicit nonnegative task weights β_k provide interpretable control over cross-domain trade-offs. Expert Merging++ extends this with importance-guided chunking: after the first stage, a normalized layer-importance score (combining learned coefficient magnitude, task-vector weight, and parameter count) determines how many contiguous parameter chunks each layer receives, allocating more trainable coefficients to high-importance layers (typically late-stage MLP blocks) while keeping low-importance layers at a single fixed scalar. The second stage then optimizes these chunk-wise coefficients with the same alignment objectives, yielding finer-grained control where it matters most while preserving a near-identical total parameter count to the layer-wise variant.

## Results

Expert Merging++ achieves the best average score across all three backbones: 58.45 on InternVL2.5 (vs. 56.96 for WUDI v2, +1.49), 63.63 on Qwen2-VL (vs. 62.63 for WUDI v2, +1.00), and 48.71 on Mistral-7B (vs. 45.48 for WUDI v2, +3.23). On InternVL2.5, it surpasses supervised Mixture Training (57.66) by +0.79, and on Qwen2-VL by +1.40. Gains are most pronounced on grounding benchmarks (e.g., 80.53 on RefCOCO vs. 76.30 for WUDI v2 on InternVL) and code generation (53.66 on HumanEval for Mistral, +4.64 over WUDI v2). Only 5–10 unlabeled calibration samples per task are required for optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Expert Merging/++ outperforms all nine baselines on macro-average across every backbone. Among training-free methods, TIES w/ DARE (56.76) and TIES Merging (56.59) are the strongest on InternVL2.5, yet both trail Expert Merging++ by 1.69 and 1.86 points respectively; TSV Merging and Iso-C underperform notably on grounding (59.17 and 68.50 on RefCOCOg vs. 79.31). Among training-based methods, WUDI v2 (56.96) is the closest prior baseline on InternVL2.5, but Expert Merging++ leads by +1.49, with the gap driven primarily by grounding (+4.23 on RefCOCO) and MATH-Vision. On Mistral-7B, the advantage over WUDI v2 widens to +3.23, with HumanEval jumping from 39.02 to 53.66. Notably, Expert Merging++ surpasses supervised Mixture Training on both MLLM backbones (+0.79 on InternVL, +1.40 on Qwen2-VL), demonstrating that unlabeled calibration with explicit alignment can rival or exceed multi-task fine-tuning. The only domain where Mixture Training retains an edge is OCRVQA (60.25 vs. 46.00 on InternVL), suggesting OCR-specific supervised signal remains valuable for that narrow task.

## Relation to Prior Work

Expert Merging extends the Task Arithmetic parameterization (θ_merged = θ_base + Σ α_k τ_k) by replacing fixed coefficients with learnable layer-wise ones, and departs from AdaMerging's entropy-minimization objective by introducing explicit hidden-state and logit alignment losses on unlabeled task-partitioned data. It also generalizes WUDI's local linear interference minimization to full representation-trajectory and predictive-distribution matching across all layers. The Expert Merging++ extension adds a novel importance-guided chunking mechanism that allocates finer-grained coefficients to high-importance layers, addressing inter-layer heterogeneity that prior trainable merging methods (AdaMerging, WUDI) treat uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, label-free path to consolidate multiple domain experts into a single deployable model with only 5–10 unlabeled samples per task and K×L trainable scalars, making it attractive for production settings where joint training or multi-model serving is infeasible. The finding that this training-light approach can match or exceed supervised Mixture Training on MLLM backbones suggests that careful behavior-space alignment may be a viable substitute for expensive multi-task fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to heterogeneous architectures (different base models), very large expert counts, or settings where experts were trained on substantially different data distributions; the authors explicitly flag heterogeneous model merging as future work.
