## Problem

Structured column pruning of LLMs inevitably introduces output discrepancies, and existing compensation strategies are inadequate: least-squares fitting overfits to small calibration sets and distorts the geometry of pretrained representations, while bias-based methods (e.g., FLAP) only correct mean errors and cannot address input-dependent directional mismatches. The paper asks how to re-align the pruned subspace with the original outputs while preserving the norm and inner-product structure that encodes pretrained semantic knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM (Llama-7B, Llama-2-13B, or Vicuna-7B), a small calibration set of 128–512 token positions from WikiText-2, and a target pruning ratio (10–30%). It operates layer-by-layer on the o_proj and down_proj sub-layers, recording input activations X and original outputs Y for each. The output is a physically pruned model where dropped columns are removed and the retained weight matrix is left-multiplied by a per-layer orthogonal rotation (optionally scaled by a single isotropic factor), yielding a compact weight of shape d_out × k with no architectural changes.

## Method

RCPU formulates post-pruning error compensation as an Orthogonal Procrustes problem: given the kept output Z = W_K X_K and the original output Y, it finds the optimal rotation Q* = argmin_{Q^TQ=I} ||Y − QZ||_F², solved in closed form via the SVD of the cross-covariance matrix M = YZ^T (giving Q* = UV^T). The kept weights are then updated as W̃_K = Q* W_K, which rotates the retained subspace to match the original output orientation while preserving all angles and relative norms. To make the rotation effective, the paper introduces a variance-aware column importance score γ_j = ||W_{:,j}|| · ||X_{[j,:]}|| · Var(X_{[j,:]}), which extends the WANDA-sp magnitude-and-activation heuristic by an input-variance factor so that columns contributing to principal output directions are preferentially retained. An optional scaled variant adds a single isotropic factor s* = tr(Σ)/||Z||_F² to also match overall magnitude. The procedure is applied greedily and layer-wise, requires no hyper-parameters, and has per-layer cost dominated by an O(d_out³) SVD.

## Results

On WikiText-2 perplexity, RCPU(Rot.+Scale) achieves 16.91 PPL at 30% pruning on Llama-7B (N_calib=512) versus 18.31 for FLAP and 26.20 for WANDA-sp; on Llama-2-13B the gap widens dramatically to 15.96 vs. 16.71 (FLAP) and 63.35 (WANDA-sp). On zero-shot benchmark accuracy (7-task mean), RCPU(Rot.+Scale) reaches 56.34% at 30% pruning on Llama-7B (N=128) and 59.79% on Llama-2-13B (N=512), outperforming FLAP (56.20% and 58.37%) and WANDA-sp (54.00% and 43.97%). At 10% pruning, RCPU(Rot.+Scale) attains 68.05% mean accuracy on Llama-2-13B, exceeding the original unpruned model's 68.97% by only 0.92 points while removing 10% of parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCPU consistently outperforms WANDA-sp on PPL across all pruning ratios and both models; the advantage is most dramatic at 30% pruning on Llama-2-13B, where WANDA-sp's PPL collapses to 63.35 while RCPU(Rot.+Scale) maintains 15.96. Against FLAP, RCPU wins on PPL in every setting (e.g., 16.91 vs. 18.31 at 30%/Llama-7B/512; 15.96 vs. 16.71 at 30%/Llama-2-13B/512), and on benchmark mean accuracy RCPU leads at 20% and 30% pruning (61.57 vs. 61.32 and 56.34 vs. 56.20 on Llama-7B; 64.55 vs. 61.66 and 59.79 vs. 58.37 on Llama-2-13B), though the gap narrows at 10% pruning. Against SliceGPT (evaluated only on Llama-2-13B), RCPU achieves substantially higher accuracy at high pruning ratios: 59.79% vs. 50.77% at 30% and 64.55% vs. 58.64% at 20%, while at 10% the two are closer (68.05% vs. 64.90%). The paper also notes that a Ridge-regularized least-squares baseline (Prop.Score+LS) underperforms RCPU on downstream benchmarks despite sometimes matching PPL at larger calibration sizes, attributed to RCPU's lower effective degrees of freedom (5.36×10⁸ vs. ~1.4–1.6×10⁹) providing better preservation of pretrained geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

RCPU extends the WANDA-sp column-pruning framework by augmenting its magnitude-and-activation score with an input-variance factor, and replaces the post-pruning error compensation step with an Orthogonal Procrustes (rotation-constrained) update rather than the bias-only correction used by FLAP or the unconstrained least-squares fit that risks overfitting under small calibration sets. It is conceptually distinct from SliceGPT, RotPruner, and DenoiseRotator, which apply or learn rotations *before* or *during* pruning to create a favorable parameterization; RCPU instead applies an analytically derived rotation *after* column removal to re-align the retained subspace with the original output geometry. The method is layer-wise and greedy, in contrast to SliceGPT's single global orthogonal reparameterization followed by global deletion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rotation-constrained formulation eliminates the need for a regularization hyperparameter (unlike Ridge) while provably preserving output norms and inner products, making it a practical, drop-in compensation step that adds only ~10 s per layer and no architectural changes. A notable open question is whether the layer-wise greedy strategy can be replaced by a joint or iterative scheme that accounts for cross-layer error propagation, and whether the variance-aware score generalizes to row pruning or block-level structured removal. The paper also leaves open how the method behaves at pruning ratios above 30% or on models with substantially different attention/MLP geometries.
