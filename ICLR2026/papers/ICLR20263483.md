## Problem

Existing Text-to-TikZ datasets are too small and noisy, with raw captions that omit critical structural details needed to reconstruct scientific figures. Prior approaches rely solely on supervised fine-tuning, which never exposes the model to the rendered semantics of the figure, producing errors such as infinite loops, hallucinated content, and incorrect spatial relations. No domain-specific reward model existed to provide semantically faithful feedback for reinforcement learning in this setting.

## Input / Output

The model takes a VLM-generated prose description of a scientific figure (typically 120–170 words specifying shapes, labels, colors, spatial relations, and coordinates) and produces a complete, compilable LaTeX document wrapped in \documentclass[tikz]{standalone} with \begin{document}…\end{document}. The output is constrained to valid TikZ code with no extraneous text, and a format reward enforces the correct document envelope. Descriptions are generated by Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct from rendered TikZ figures, replacing the sparse and often unhelpful raw captions found in arXiv papers.

## Method

TikZilla is trained in two stages on small Qwen-based models (3B and 8B). Stage 1 is standard autoregressive SFT on DaTikZ-V4 (2M+ samples sourced from arXiv, GitHub, TeX SE, and synthetic data, with LLM-based debugging via Qwen3-32B to repair ~600K uncompilable snippets). Stage 2 applies GRPO reinforcement learning where the reward is computed by a domain-specific image encoder: DeTikZify-V2's SigLIP encoder (from PaliGemma-3b) is retrained end-to-end on DaTikZ-V4 for inverse graphics (image→TikZ), and its frozen patch-level embeddings are compared via Earth Mover's Distance between ground-truth and rendered predicted images to yield a scalar similarity reward in [0,1]. The RL implementation uses the Dr.GRPO token-level normalization variant and the DAPO Clip-Higher strategy (ε_low=0.2, ε_high=0.28) to remove length bias and encourage exploration, with KL penalty disabled (β=0) and temperature 1.0 sampling.

## Results

On the DaTikZ-V4 test set (1,047 samples), TikZilla-3B-RL achieves the best aggregate score (AVG) of 0.385, with CLIP 0.189, DSim 0.731, TED 0.766, and a 98% compilation rate using only 481 average tokens. TikZilla-8B-RL follows closely at 0.384 AVG with 95% compilation. In human evaluation (1–5 Likert scale, 9 expert annotators), TikZilla-8B-RL ties GPT-5 on image alignment (3.46 vs. 3.48) and GPT-5 leads on textual alignment (4.18 vs. 3.40 for TikZilla-3B-RL). On the out-of-distribution SPIQA benchmark, TikZilla-3B-RL outperforms GPT-5 on all metrics (CLIP 0.193 vs. 0.115, DSim 0.637 vs. 0.432, CR 97% vs. 60%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TikZilla-3B-RL (0.385 AVG) surpasses GPT-5 (0.365) by 0.020 on the aggregate metric while achieving a 10-point higher compilation rate (98% vs. 88%) and fewer tokens (481 vs. 480). Against GPT-4o (0.320), TikZilla-3B-RL gains +0.042 on CLIP and +0.151 on DSim, with a 20-point compilation advantage (98% vs. 78%). Compared to the prior state-of-the-art TikZero-Plus-10B (0.231), TikZilla-3B-RL improves by +0.085 on CLIP, +0.334 on DSim, and achieves a 37% higher compilation rate (98% vs. 61%) while using 261 fewer tokens on average. Against the largest open-source baseline Qwen3-32B (0.322), TikZilla-3B-RL leads by 0.063 AVG despite being over 10× smaller. On the OOD SPIQA benchmark, TikZilla-3B-RL outperforms GPT-5 on every metric (CLIP 0.193 vs. 0.115, DSim 0.637 vs. 0.432, CR 97% vs. 60%), while Llama3.1-8B (0.214 AVG) and Qwen2.5-3B (0.202 AVG) remain far behind all RL-tuned variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Text-to-TikZ line initiated by AutomaTikZ (Belouadi et al., 2024a) and TikZero (Belouadi et al., 2025), which relied on noisy raw captions and supervised fine-tuning alone. The key departures are: (1) a substantially larger and cleaner dataset (DaTikZ-V4, 2M+ samples) with VLM-generated descriptions replacing captions and an LLM debugging pipeline to repair uncompilable code; (2) a two-stage SFT + GRPO training pipeline where the RL reward is computed by a domain-specific image encoder (retrained DeTikZify-V2 via inverse graphics) using Earth Mover's Distance over patch embeddings, rather than general-purpose metrics like CLIPScore or DreamSIM. The RL formulation draws on Dr.GRPO and DAPO's Clip-Higher strategy, adapted to remove length bias in TikZ sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: small open-source models (3B/8B) with a well-designed data pipeline and domain-specific reward can match or surpass GPT-4o and GPT-5 on scientific figure generation, reducing reliance on proprietary APIs. The finding that the inverse-graphics encoder reward correlates with human judgment at ρ=0.714 (vs. 0.260 for CLIPScore) validates a principled approach to reward design for structured code generation. Key limitations include potential hallucinations in VLM-generated descriptions that can bias both training and reward optimization, and the observation that models score higher on textual than image alignment, suggesting a gap between description fidelity and true figure reproduction. Future work should address more reliable annotation, fine-grained reward functions, and extension to other structured generation tasks (LaTeX tables, CAD, flowcharts).
