## Problem

In multi-follower Bayesian Stackelberg games, the leader must commit to a mixed strategy without knowing the followers' type distribution, yet prior online learning results only covered the single-follower case. With n followers, the joint type space is exponentially large (K^n), the leader's utility is discontinuous and non-convex due to best-response structure, and even the offline single-follower problem is NP-hard in the number of leader actions. This paper is the first to study online learning in multi-follower BSGs, asking how the feedback model (types vs. actions) affects the achievable regret and whether the exponential type space necessarily forces exponential regret.

## Input / Output

The leader receives a game instance specifying n followers, L leader actions, A follower actions, K types per follower, and known utility functions u(ℓ,a) and v_i(ℓ,a_i,θ_i), but not the type distribution D. At each round t, the leader outputs a mixed strategy x^t ∈ Δ(L); after followers best-respond, the leader observes either the type profile θ^t (type feedback) or only the joint action a^t (action feedback). The objective is to minimize cumulative regret over T rounds relative to the optimal Stackelberg equilibrium strategy x*, with the constraint that the algorithm must be computationally tractable (polynomial in input size for constant L).

## Method

The core technical tool is a geometric partition of the leader's strategy space Δ(L) into O(n^L K^L A^{2L}) best-response regions, where within each region all followers' best responses are fixed and the leader's expected utility is linear in x. This partition is key because it decouples the exponential type space from the strategy space: the number of regions is polynomial in n, K, A for fixed L, and the pseudo-dimension of linear functions in L dimensions is only L, enabling uniform concentration over all strategies simultaneously. For type feedback, the algorithm performs empirical risk minimization over regions (solving a linear program per region), achieving Õ(√(min{L log(nKAT), nK}·T)) regret for independent types. For action feedback, a UCB algorithm treats each best-response region as an arm, estimating the region's utility from observed joint actions, yielding Õ(√(n^L K^L A^{2L} L T log T)) regret; a complementary linear-bandit approach (via LP reformulation and OFUL) gives Õ(K^n √T log T) regret, which is better when n is small. A matching lower bound of Ω(√(min{L, nK}·T)) is established via a reduction from distribution learning.

## Results

The paper establishes regret bounds for online learning in multi-follower Bayesian Stackelberg games. Under type feedback with independent types, the algorithm achieves Õ(√(min{L, nK}·T)) regret, matching the lower bound Ω(√(min{L, nK}·T)) up to log factors. Under type feedback with general (correlated) types, the bound is Õ(√(min{L, K^n}·T)). Under action feedback, the best algorithm achieves Õ(min{√(n^L K^L A^{2L} L), K^n}·√T) regret. Notably, the type-feedback bounds do not grow polynomially in the number of followers n, which is a key improvement over prior single-follower results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; comparisons are made against prior theoretical regret bounds. For the action-feedback setting, the paper's UCB-over-best-response-regions algorithm (Algorithm 3) achieves Õ(√(n^L K^L A^{2L} L·T)) regret, which is strictly better than the Bernasconi et al. (2023) linear-bandit reduction that yields Õ(K^n·√T) when L is small relative to n. For type feedback, the paper's Õ(√(min{L, nK}·T)) bound for independent types improves upon the naive distribution-estimation bound of Õ(√(K^n·T)) by exploiting the geometric structure of best-response regions and the pseudo-dimension of linear functions. The lower bound Ω(√(min{L, nK}·T)) is the first for the multi-follower setting and nearly matches the upper bounds, confirming that the Õ(√L) factor (rather than Õ(√(K^n))) is the correct dependence when n is large. The exponential dependence on L in the action-feedback bound is shown to be computationally unavoidable (NP-hardness of BSGs in L by Conitzer & Sandholm, 2006).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online learning literature on Bayesian Stackelberg games from the single-follower setting (Balcan et al., 2015; Bollini et al., 2026) to the multi-follower case, which the authors identify as the first such treatment. The key technical departure is a geometric characterization showing that the leader's strategy space partitions into only O(n^L K^L A^{2L}) best-response regions—polynomial in the number of followers n—enabling a UCB-over-regions algorithm (Algorithm 3) that avoids the exponential K^n blowup one would naively expect. For the action-feedback setting, the paper also leverages the LP-to-linear-bandit reduction of Bernasconi et al. (2023) combined with OFUL (Abbasi-yadkori et al., 2011), adapting an adversarial technique to the stochastic setting for a tighter bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that regret scales only logarithmically (or polynomially in L) in the number of followers n, rather than exponentially in K^n—resolves a natural concern about multi-agent learning and provides near-tight characterizations of the exploration-exploitation tradeoff in this setting. Open questions include closing the gap between upper and lower bounds for action feedback, extending to adversarial type distributions (conjectured feasible via the same geometric decomposition), and handling inter-follower externalities which break the independence structure underpinning the best-response region analysis. The exponential computational dependence on L is shown to be unavoidable (NP-hardness of Conitzer & Sandholm, 2006), framing a fundamental computation-vs-regret tradeoff.
