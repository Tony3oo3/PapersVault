## Problem

Current Vision-Language-Action models assume instructions are explicitly issued as text or ASR-transcribed speech, discarding paralinguistic cues (prosody, emotion, speaker identity) and ignoring environmental sounds entirely. In real-world settings, humans rarely give direct commands; instead, intent emerges implicitly from dialogue, tone, and ambient audio. No prior work jointly reasons over speech, environmental sounds, and visual observations to proactively infer and verify user intent for robotic manipulation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB frames at 224×224 resolution, raw audio at 16 kHz (containing multi-speaker speech with overlapping voices, non-verbal sound events, and environmental backgrounds), and dialogue history text, all fused into a single unified token sequence. It produces 7-DoF delta end-effector control vectors in chunks of 6 timesteps, speech waveforms for proactive clarification (e.g., 'Should I move the pan?'), and text responses, all generated autoregressively from a shared vocabulary space that interleaves text tokens, speech representations, and 2048 discrete FAST+ action tokens.

## Method

RoboOmni adopts a Perceiver-Thinker-Talker-Executor architecture built on Qwen2.5-Omni: the Perceiver encodes vision, audio, and text into a unified embedding space via modality-specific encoders; the Thinker (LLM backbone) autoregressively generates from a joint vocabulary of text and action tokens; the Talker converts generated text into speech waveforms; and the Executor decodes FAST+ discrete action tokens back into continuous 7-DoF control commands. Training uses a single autoregressive maximum-likelihood objective that unifies conversational supervision (text generation) and manipulation supervision (action token generation) through batch interleaving, eliminating the need for separate heads or cascaded pipelines. The key architectural insight is that end-to-end audio processing within the LLM preserves paralinguistic cues—prosody, speaker identity, non-verbal events—that ASR-based cascades inevitably discard, enabling robust intent recognition under ambiguity. The model is pretrained on the newly constructed OmniAction dataset (141k episodes, 5k+ speakers, 2.4k sound events, 640 backgrounds, six contextual instruction types) using 64 A100 GPUs for 10 days, then fine-tuned for downstream tasks.

## Results

On the OmniAction-LIBERO-TTS benchmark (240 tasks across 4 suites × 6 contextual instruction types), RoboOmni achieves an overall 85.6% success rate, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline NORA (25.9% under ASR→Text) and all other cascaded methods (≤10%). On the real-speech OmniAction-LIBERO-Real benchmark, RoboOmni reaches 76.6% average, edging out π₀ (73.8%) and far surpassing OpenVLA (40.1%) and NORA (17.4%). In real-world WidowX 250S experiments, RoboOmni achieves 73.9% task success versus 52.2% for the best ASR+VLA baseline, while also reducing per-inference latency to 0.49× that of the ASR+OpenVLA pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboOmni decisively outperforms all four VLA baselines (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, NORA, π₀) on both the TTS and Real benchmarks, with the largest gaps on the Goal and Long-Horizon suites where baselines collapse to 0–16% while RoboOmni sustains 79–86%. NORA is the strongest baseline (25.9% overall on TTS, 17.4% on Real), benefiting from its Qwen2.5-VL backbone and FAST+ action decoding, yet still trails RoboOmni by ~60 points on TTS. π₀ shows the most robustness among text-based VLAs on the Real benchmark (73.8%), likely due to large-scale co-training on diverse web data, but RoboOmni still leads by 2.8 points and by a wider margin on the TTS benchmark (85.6% vs. 4.4%). For intent recognition specifically, RoboOmni (88.9%) surpasses ASR+GPT-4o (55.6%), Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (50.0%), and Qwen2.5-Omni-3B (27.8%), confirming that end-to-end audio-action modeling preserves paralinguistic cues that ASR pipelines discard. On inference latency, RoboOmni runs at 0.49× the cost of ASR+OpenVLA, whereas other cascaded pipelines (ASR+NORA at 1.02×, ASR+π₀ at 0.96×) incur similar overhead because the ASR stage dominates computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboOmni builds directly on the Qwen2.5-Omni omni-modal LLM architecture (Perceiver-Thinker-Talker) and the FAST+ discrete action tokenizer, extending them from linguistic outputs to embodied robotic control. It departs from the dominant VLA paradigm (OpenVLA, π0, NORA) that assumes explicit textual or ASR-transcribed instructions, instead introducing cross-modal contextual instructions where intent must be inferred from raw speech, environmental sounds, and visual observations. The OmniAction dataset is constructed by transforming Open-X-Embodiment trajectories into multi-turn dialogues with diverse acoustic conditions, bridging the gap between existing manipulation datasets (which lack audio) and the new proactive-intent setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental mismatch between how VLA models are trained (explicit commands) and how humans actually communicate with robots (implicit, context-dependent cues). The OmniAction dataset (140k episodes, 5k+ speakers) and OmniAction-LIBERO benchmark fill a critical gap in evaluation infrastructure for proactive robotic interaction. However, the 42.6% intention-related failure rate in real-world settings and the reliance on a single robot platform (WidowX 250S) suggest significant headroom for improvement in audio grounding and low-level control. Open questions include whether the end-to-end approach generalizes to more complex multi-robot or long-horizon scenarios, and whether the paralinguistic cues that drive gains are robust across languages and cultural contexts.
