## Problem

Generating preference-aligned LLM annotations for categorical NLU tasks in semi-supervised settings faces two unresolved gaps: there is no reference-free metric to assess whether LLM-generated labels actually reflect user-specific preferences (existing metrics require ground truth, and LLM self-evaluation is overconfident), and there is no efficient, training-free mechanism to correct misaligned annotations using only a small set of user-preference exemplars. Existing approaches—SFT, RLHF, and prompt-based methods—either demand costly large-scale annotations or remain locked to population-level pretraining distributions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled text corpus D_u (N instances) and a small user-preference seed set H (5% of D_u, with at least one labeled instance per class), processed jointly by two heterogeneous annotators: a general LLM (GPT-3.5, GPT-4o Mini, or Llama-3-8B) operating at the token level and a task-specialized sentence encoder (MiniLM or BERT) operating in embedding space. It produces a fully corrected annotation dataset D^(final) with preference-aligned class labels for every unlabeled instance, a CAI ratio serving as a reference-free reliability score, and an explicit partition of the corpus into consistent (C) and inconsistent (I) subsets that tracks pre- and post-alignment quality.

## Method

HCC is a training-free, two-stage framework built on heterogeneous consistency between a token-likelihood LLM and an embedding-similarity specialized model. For verification, it introduces the CAI Ratio (N_C / (N_IC + ε)), which quantifies inter-model agreement as a reference-free uncertainty signal: samples where both models assign the same label form the consistent set C, while disagreements form the inconsistent set I. For co-alignment, it applies a two-round Divide-and-Conquer Co-Alignment (DCCA) strategy using MV-VTES—a non-parametric majority-vote over the top-k nearest neighbors in embedding space within clusters seeded by user-preference exemplars. Round 1 reassigns all inconsistent samples using the reference set C ∪ H; Round 2 reassigns the remaining stubborn inconsistencies using the expanded reference C ∪ CI ∪ H. The key design choice is treating embedding similarity as a structural prior for label propagation rather than a correctness proxy, and using inter-model disagreement (rather than ad-hoc confidence thresholds) to trigger correction, thereby avoiding the overconfidence inherent in LLM-only annotation.

## Results

Across eight NLU benchmarks, HCC with correction (HCC w/ Corr.) achieves the best accuracy on 6 of 8 datasets with GPT-4o Mini (e.g., 87.93% on CLINC, 80.18% on Massive Scenario, 82.45% on Banking77) and on 7 of 8 with Meta-Llama-3-8B Instruct (e.g., 82.43% on CLINC, 77.71% on Banking77). The CAI Ratio increases substantially after co-alignment (e.g., 1.36→4.03 on Banking77, 0.56→4.43 on CLINC for Llama-3-8B), and Pearson correlation between CAI and accuracy is strong (ρ=0.93 for GPT-3.5, ρ=0.86 for GPT-4o Mini, ρ=0.81 for Llama-3-8B). Notably, HCC enables Llama-3-8B to surpass GPT-3.5 Turbo on 6 datasets and GPT-4o Mini on 5, despite the open-source model's much weaker zero-shot performance (e.g., 32.49% vs. 81.44% on CLINC before correction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HCC w/ Corr. outperforms all five prompting/clustering baselines on the majority of datasets. Against Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2022), HCC gains +3.71% on CLINC (87.93 vs. 84.22) and +15.63% on Banking77 (82.45 vs. 66.82) with GPT-4o Mini. Self-Refine (Madaan et al., 2024) is consistently the weakest prompting baseline, with HCC exceeding it by +19.87% on CLINC and +42.22% on Banking77. Chain-of-Thought (Wei et al., 2022) and Few-shot prompting (Brown et al., 2020) also trail HCC substantially on most datasets (e.g., CoT 74.93% vs. HCC 87.93% on CLINC; FoT 77.17% vs. 87.93%). ClusterLLM (Zhang et al., 2023) is the closest non-HCC competitor on CLINC (78.58%) and FewRel-Nat (51.22%), but HCC still leads by +9.35% and −4.69% respectively; on FewRel-Nat, HCC w/o Corr. (49.53%) is the best HCC variant, slightly below ClusterLLM. The one clear loss for HCC w/ Corr. is MTOP Intent, where HCC w/o Corr. (80.16%) beats the LLM zero-shot (75.03%) but HCC w/ Corr. drops to 67.10%, suggesting the correction step can over-rotate on already-strong LLM predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

HCC carves out a distinct niche by combining two ideas that have been explored separately: inter-model consistency checking (analogous to self-consistency in prompting, but across heterogeneous model families rather than repeated sampling of one model) and non-parametric embedding-based label propagation (similar to k-NN or ClusterLLM, but explicitly framed as a structural prior for correction rather than a correctness proxy). It departs from RLHF and SFT by requiring no parameter updates or large preference corpora, and from IRR metrics (Cohen's kappa, Krippendorff's alpha) by being label-set agnostic and reference-free. The CAI Ratio and DCCA mechanism together form a training-free verification-and-correction loop that is conceptually closer to active learning's uncertainty-based selection than to any single prior prompting or clustering method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that a weak open-source LLM (Llama-3-8B) paired with a small sentence encoder can match or exceed GPT-3.5/4o-mini on categorical NLU annotation, reducing dependence on proprietary APIs for preference-aligned labeling. The CAI Ratio offers a genuinely useful reference-free signal for monitoring annotation quality at scale without ground truth, which is a gap the paper identifies clearly in existing evaluation practice. However, the relationship between CAI and accuracy is not strictly monotonic (e.g., MTOP Intent under GPT-4o Mini shows CAI rising while accuracy drops), and the method requires at least one labeled exemplar per class, limiting applicability to fully open-set or zero-shot preference scenarios. Open questions include whether the heterogeneous-consistency principle extends beyond categorical NLU to generative or multi-label settings, and whether the divide-and-conquer correction converges under more adversarial preference distributions.
