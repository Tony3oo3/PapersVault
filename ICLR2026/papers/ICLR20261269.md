## Problem

Existing time series data quality rating methods (influence functions, Shapley values) are limited to single domains and suffer from prohibitive computational costs (Hessian computations or exponential Shapley evaluations). Real-world time series span vastly different domains with distinct properties, yet no unified, domain-adaptive framework exists for efficiently rating quality across diverse sources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261269/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes fixed-length time series blocks (128 points for long-term forecasting, 36 for short-term, 100 for classification) extracted via sliding windows. For LLM judgment, two blocks are presented as numerical sequences in a pairwise prompt with position swapping to mitigate positional bias; for multivariate series, judgment is performed channel-by-channel and averaged. The TSRater model outputs a scalar quality score per block, which is distributed back to individual time points and aggregated to a sample-level score, ultimately producing a ranked selection of top-k% data for downstream training.

## Method

TSRating uses LLMs (GPT-4o-mini) to make pairwise quality comparisons on time series blocks along four criteria (trend, frequency, amplitude, pattern), converting preferences to scalar scores via the Bradley-Terry model. A lightweight TSRater model—comprising a frozen MOMENT encoder (~109M params) followed by a 3-layer MLP (hidden dim 256, LayerNorm, ReLU, residual connections)—is trained to replicate these LLM judgments efficiently. To achieve cross-domain generalization, TSRater is trained via MAML meta-learning over 22 datasets spanning 9 domains from the Time-300B corpus, with SignSGD used for inner-loop updates to avoid expensive hypergradient (second-order) computations. The four criterion-specific scores are normalized and fused into a single quality rating, and the meta-trained model requires only ~200 seconds of few-shot fine-tuning and inference per new dataset.

## Results

Across 11 benchmark datasets and three time series tasks (long-term forecasting, short-term forecasting, classification) with three model architectures (Linear, CNN, PatchTST), TSRating achieves the best metric in 16 out of 36 total evaluations and is second-best in most remaining cases. On classification, TSRating attains the highest accuracy in 10 of 12 cases (e.g., 0.679 on CBF with CNN vs. 0.593 for DataShapley and 0.563 for TimeInf). On short-term forecasting (M4-Yearly with PatchTST), TSRating achieves the lowest MAPE of 3.901, outperforming DataOob (3.982) and DataShapley (4.273). In total runtime on the Time-300B benchmark, TSRater completes evaluation in 4,687 s, comparable to DataOob (4,785 s) and TimeInf (4,938 s) but ~45× faster than DataShapley (210,000 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSRating is compared against four data-selection baselines: DataShapley (Shapley-value based), KNNShapley (KNN-approximated Shapley), DataOob (out-of-bag estimation), and TimeInf (time-aware influence functions). On classification, TSRating dominates all four baselines in 10 of 12 cases; for example, on CBF with CNN it reaches 0.679 accuracy versus 0.593 (DataShapley), 0.563 (TimeInf), 0.621 (KNNShapley), and 0.663 (DataOob). On long-term forecasting, TSRating is competitive but not uniformly best: it ties DataShapley on Electricity with PatchTST (0.397 vs. 0.396 RMSE) and trails TimeInf on Traffic (0.683 vs. 0.609 RMSE with Linear), yet it wins on Weather and Exchange Rate across all three architectures. On short-term forecasting (M4), TSRating achieves the lowest MAPE in 3 of 9 cases, with its clearest margin on M4-Yearly/PatchTST (3.901 vs. 3.982 for DataOob and 4.273 for DataShapley). In terms of runtime, TSRater (4,687 s total) is comparable to DataOob and TimeInf but ~45× faster than DataShapley (210,000 s); KNNShapley is fastest (152 s) but sacrifices accuracy substantially, particularly on short-term forecasting where its MAPE degrades to 3.624 on M4Y/Linear.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) time-series-specific data attribution methods (TimeInf, TimeShap) that extend influence functions and Shapley values to capture temporal structure but are limited to single domains; (2) LLM-as-judge frameworks for text data quality (Qurating, Ask-LLM) that use pairwise prompting and Bradley-Terry scoring but have not been applied to time series; and (3) model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML) with SignSGD inner-loop updates (Fan et al., 2021) that avoids expensive hypergradient computation. The key conceptual departure is repurposing LLM pairwise judgment—previously confined to text—as the supervision signal for a meta-learned scalar quality rater over time series blocks, thereby inheriting cross-domain knowledge from LLM pretraining while amortizing inference cost through a lightweight MLP head on frozen MOMENT embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in the amortized evaluation cost: once meta-trained across nine domains, scoring a new dataset requires only ~200 seconds of few-shot tuning and inference, versus retraining influence-function or Shapley-based methods from scratch. The meta-rater matching single-dataset rater performance (Table 3) is a meaningful validation that cross-domain meta-learning preserves task-specific signal. Open questions include whether the fixed four-criteria set (trend, frequency, amplitude, pattern) captures all quality dimensions relevant to downstream tasks, how the method handles very long or highly irregular multivariate series, and whether the LLM judgment bottleneck (4,167 s of API calls) can be reduced via distillation or smaller local models. The reliance on GPT-4o-mini for all judgments also raises reproducibility and cost concerns for groups without API access.
