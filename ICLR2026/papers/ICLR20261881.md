## Problem

Existing geometry distribution models represent a single human shape as a flow from a Gaussian to surface points, but extending this to a dataset is non-trivial: geometry is stored in per-shape network parameters (prohibitive memory for generative tasks), and learning flow velocity fields from a single Gaussian to many diverse shapes is computationally inefficient. The paper addresses how to build a scalable, dataset-level generative model over human geometry distributions that preserves fine clothing details and pose-dependent wrinkles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261881/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an SMPL template mesh (rendered into UV position maps) as the primary conditioning signal, and optionally a frontal normal image (encoded by a frozen DINO-ViT) for avatar identity in novel pose generation. The intermediate representation is a compact 2D feature map of shape 8×24×24, which is decompressed by a UNet-style decoder using SMPL vertex UV coordinates to yield per-point latents. The final output is a 3D point cloud (up to 1 million points) with pose-dependent clothing geometry, rendered via Gaussian Splatting for visualization.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage flow-matching framework that models a distribution over geometry distributions. In Stage 1, an auto-decoder learns to compress each human geometry into a 2D feature map by training a denoising flow from a perturbed SMPL template point N(x₀′, σ) to a normalized displacement field Δx = x₁ − x₀′, where x₀′ is the nearest SMPL point to target point x₁; this normalization eliminates spatial imbalance from uneven surface sampling. In Stage 2, a U-Net (EDM2 architecture) generates the feature maps in latent space, conditioned on SMPL UV maps (pose) and optionally DINO-ViT features (identity). The key insight is replacing the Gaussian source with the SMPL template distribution and constructing short training pairs via nearest-neighbor matching with Gaussian perturbation, which dramatically reduces flow path complexity and improves convergence over prior formulations.

## Results

On the THuman2 dataset for pose-conditioned random generation, the method achieves a FID of 16.16 on raw geometry, a 57% improvement over the previous state-of-the-art gDNA (42.90). Even when compared to gDNA's enhanced rendering result (17.43), the method's raw geometry still leads by 7%. In the novel pose generation user study, the method scores 4.04/5 in quality and 4.36/5 in physical plausibility, substantially outperforming gDNA (2.54/2.66), GetAvatar (2.16/2.20), and E3Gen (2.12/2.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all six baselines on raw geometry FID. Against the strongest prior method gDNA (implicit function), it reduces FID from 42.90 to 16.16 (57% improvement), and even beats gDNA's enhanced rendering (17.43) using only raw geometry. GetAvatar (generative textured mesh) and E3Gen (Gaussian splatting) show larger gaps at 56.07 and 65.32 respectively, with their enhanced rendering closing the gap somewhat (22.77 and 28.12) but still trailing the proposed method. The NeRF-based baselines (ENARF at 223.72, GNARF at 166.62, EVA3D at 60.37) are far behind, reflecting the inherent resolution and rendering-speed limitations of radiance field representations. In the novel pose generation user study, the method's advantage is even more pronounced: it scores roughly 1.5–2 points higher than all baselines on both quality and physical plausibility, because prior methods produce static canonical-space geometry that cannot adapt clothing wrinkles to new poses, whereas this method synthesizes pose-dependent details directly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Geometry Distributions (Zhang et al., ICCV 2025), which models a single 3D shape as a flow-matched point distribution, into a dataset-scale generative framework by two key departures: replacing the Gaussian source with the SMPL template distribution and encoding each geometry distribution as a compact 2D UV feature map rather than network weights. The two-stage architecture (auto-decoder compressing geometry into latents, then a latent-space flow model generating those latents) mirrors the VAE-plus-diffusion paradigm from image and 3D generative models, but applied to the novel 'distribution-over-distributions' setting. It departs from prior 3D human generation methods (gDNA, E3Gen, GetAvatar, NeRF-based approaches) that rely on implicit functions, point clouds, or rendering supervision, instead directly synthesizing high-fidelity surface geometry through flow matching in a regularized displacement space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce fine-grained, pose-dependent clothing geometry (wrinkles, folds, loose garments) in a fully generative setting, achieving a 57% FID improvement over the prior SOTA (gDNA) on raw geometry. The SMPL-as-source-distribution insight and the feature-map encoding of geometry distributions offer a reusable recipe for extending single-shape flow models to dataset-scale generation. Open questions remain around non-uniform sampling on the target surface, UV seam artifacts, and generalization to clothing styles absent from training data; the authors note that garment-aligned UV segmentation and more sophisticated training-pair construction are natural next steps.
