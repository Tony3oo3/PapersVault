## Problem

Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) have revealed interpretable features in LLM activations but have been almost exclusively applied to text, leaving their generality to other domains untested. Moreover, existing SAE work is largely descriptive—cataloging what features exist—rather than explanatory, offering no account of why or how the model chose to organize its latent space in a particular way. This paper addresses both gaps by applying SAEs to 3D point-cloud representations and proposing a gradient-based learning-dynamics framework that explains the resulting feature structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 53k 3D objects from Objaverse-XL, each encoded by Dora-VAE into M=4096 latent vectors of dimension 128 (pre-KL) or 64 (post-KL), where each latent is spatially anchored to a specific point in the original point cloud via furthest-point sampling. The BatchTopK SAE (codebook n=512, top-k=8) is trained on 217 million freshly KL-sampled latents per epoch, producing sparse presence vectors α ∈ ℝ⁵¹² and a dictionary of feature identity vectors W^Dec. Downstream, 848k targeted ablation/addition interventions on individual features yield ablation-response curves (ARCs) that quantify each feature's causal effect on reconstruction MSE.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part analysis: (1) an empirical demonstration that Dora-VAE's latent features are discrete and state-like rather than continuous, verified through 848k feature ablations showing sigmoidal ARCs with sharp phase transitions, and (2) a theoretical framework derived by deconstructing the gradient step (Eq. 6) into a presence term ∇_{θ_f}α_j and an identity term ∇_{θ_f}e_j, where α_j acts as a multiplicative learning-rate gate on the identity signal. This dichotomy explains three otherwise unintuitive behaviors: positional features adopt binary state-like encodings because high-presence features receive stronger identity gradients; high-impact features exhibit unimodal transition-point distributions centered at t≈0.5 because the presence term pushes both on/off states away from the high-gradient transition region; and low-impact features exhibit bimodal transition-point distributions because the model redistributes superposition interference onto them, shifting their effective presence by a variable offset. The SAE itself is a standard BatchTopK architecture (n=512, k=8, β=0.125, Adam lr=1e-3, 10 epochs on a single A100) trained on Dora-VAE's pre-KL embeddings, with feature ablation performed by directly modifying latents along SAE decoder weight directions rather than relying on SAE reconstruction.

## Results

The paper applies a BatchTopK SAE (codebook size 512, threshold k=8) to Dora-VAE latents from 53k Objaverse-XL objects, performing 848k independent feature ablations. Key quantitative findings include: (1) ablation-response curves (ARCs) exhibit sigmoidal behavior with a well-defined transition point, and higher-impact features show more discrete (binary-like) activation patterns; (2) transition points of high-impact ablations form a unimodal distribution centered at t≈0.5, while low-impact ablations form a symmetric bimodal distribution; (3) SAE reconstruction loss (relative ℓ₂) ranges from 0.182–0.518 depending on codebook size and threshold, with Dora-VAE decoder loss ranging from 0.216–0.538. The SAE trains in 2 hours on a single A100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an interpretability and feature-learning-dynamics study rather than a method comparison paper, so there are no direct baseline methods being benchmarked against. The closest comparative analysis is the SAE hyperparameter ablation (Table 1), which shows that increasing codebook size from 256 to 1024 improves reconstruction fidelity (relative ℓ₂ drops from 0.518 to 0.501 at k=4) but decreases universality (0.420 → 0.206), suggesting larger dictionaries learn more idiosyncratic positional tilings. The paper's primary contribution is qualitative: demonstrating that Dora-VAE's latent features behave as discrete states rather than continuous encodings, validated through 848k targeted interventions showing sigmoidal loss curves and bimodal transition-point distributions. The authors position their work as complementary to prior SAE applications on LLMs (Bricken et al. 2023, Templeton et al. 2024) by extending the analysis to an unstructured, continuous 3D domain where feature semantics are visually verifiable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAE-based feature decomposition lineage established for LLMs (Bricken et al. 2023; Templeton et al. 2024) to the 3D domain, applying a BatchTopK SAE (Bussmann et al. 2024) to latents from Dora-VAE (Chen et al. 2024). While prior SAE work has been text-focused and primarily descriptive—cataloging what features exist—this paper deliberately shifts toward explanation, proposing a gradient-decomposition framework (Eq. 6) that accounts for why features are learned with discrete, state-like presences and why superposition interference is redistributed toward low-impact features. The superposition theory of Elhage et al. (2022, 2023) provides the conceptual backbone, but the claim that models dynamically redistribute interference at inference time (rather than only during training) is a novel extension of that framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a model trained on continuous 3D geometry learns a discrete, phase-transition-based feature space challenges the intuitive expectation that continuous domains yield continuous representations, and the 848k ablation experiments provide substantial empirical grounding for this claim. However, the explanatory framework—particularly the superposition redistribution hypothesis—is speculative, resting on a single model (Dora-VAE) and a single dataset (Objaverse-XL), with no validation on other architectures or modalities. Open questions include whether the discrete-state behavior generalizes to text and image models, whether the bimodal transition-point pattern holds under different training regimes, and whether the proposed gradient-decomposition framework can be formalized into a predictive theory of feature learning dynamics.
