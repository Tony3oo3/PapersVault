## Problem

Deep learning optimization is governed by non-convex loss landscapes, yet empirical dynamics closely resemble convex optimization. It remains unclear when and how convexity-based convergence guarantees (e.g., O(1/√T) loss and O(1/√T) optimal learning rate) actually hold in practice, and whether they can be leveraged to build a unified scaling law that simultaneously predicts loss and optimal learning rate across model sizes and training horizons.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a model size N, training horizon T, a learning rate schedule function, an optimizer choice, and a reference peak learning rate η_ref obtained from a few small-scale runs. It outputs a closed-form loss prediction E[L(N,T)] ≈ L̃_∞(N) + Q̃(η_ref*; N)/√T and the corresponding optimal peak learning rate η_peak*(N,T) = η_peak*(N_small, T_small)·√(T_small/T). The fitting is performed via simple linear regression of observed loss against 1/√T, and the schedule qualification test (Condition 2.5) is a training-free symbolic computation over the schedule function.

## Method

The paper builds a four-step bridge from convex SGD theory to deep learning. Starting from the non-asymptotic last-iterate bound of Defazio et al. (2023, Corollary 12) under convex loss and bounded gradient, it derives asymptotic upper bounds for five schedules, showing that 'qualified' schedules (linear, cosine, WSD) achieve O(1/√T) loss convergence while constant and square-root-inverse schedules only reach O(√(ln T)/T). It then generalizes to deep learning by replacing the unknown constants D, G, L* with data-driven estimates D̃, G̃, L̃_∞ and extending the bound to arbitrary optimizers (AdamW, Muon-NSGD, LoRA), validating the fit with R² ≥ 0.95 across ResNet, ViT, GPT-2, and VLM architectures. The key scaling law (Generalization 4) states that under any 1/√T-scaled peak learning rate, loss converges as L̃_∞ + Q̃(η_ref)/√T, enabling a two-dimensional extrapolation across model sizes (70×) and training horizons (80×) from a handful of small-scale runs. A training-free 'qualifying exam' (Condition 2.5) uses symbolic integration to certify whether a given schedule achieves the optimal rate without running any optimization.

## Results

The paper's scaling law achieves R² ≥ 0.978 when fitting loss against 1/√T across 15 Chinchilla model sizes (0.074B–12.56B) and up to FLOPs = 1e22 (over 300B tokens), with all loss values within 1% relative error of prediction. For sequence-to-sequence loss prediction (Generalization 1), R² ≥ 0.95 is consistently achieved across ResNet18, GPT2, AdamW, Muon-NSGD, and multiple learning rate schedules. The scaling law extrapolates approximately 80× across training horizons (from 100 to 500k iterations) and 70× across model sizes (from 0.1B to 7B GPT2), with O(1/√T) loss convergence under 1/√T-scaled peak learning rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing methods in the traditional sense; instead, it validates its theoretical scaling law against empirical training data. The key comparison is with existing scaling laws: prior loss-scaling laws (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) predict loss as a function of model size and tokens but do not explicitly predict the optimal learning rate, while prior learning-rate scaling laws (Bi et al., 2024 with α=0.125; Bjorck et al., 2024 with α∈{0.32–0.70}) predict learning rate scaling but do not predict loss. The proposed unified law (Equation 5.1) simultaneously predicts both loss and optimal peak learning rate with a fixed exponent α=0.5, matching the convex-theory prediction. Empirically, the law's predictions match actual Chinchilla training losses within 1% relative error across 15 model sizes, and the 1/√T-scaled learning rate transferred from a 0.1B model to 1B and 7B models (70× size extrapolation) yields precise loss predictions without re-tuning, outperforming the horizon-unaware transfer (α=0) proposed by Porian et al. (2024) and Wang et al. (2024).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the convex optimization convergence analysis of Defazio et al. (2023), specifically their Corollary 12 last-iterate bound, by showing it generalizes to non-convex deep learning with arbitrary optimizers (AdamW, Muon, LoRA) and by introducing a training-free 'qualifying exam' (Condition 2.5) that certifies which learning rate schedules achieve the optimal O(1/√T) rate. It is most closely related to Schaipp et al. (2025), which also bridges convex theory and LR scheduling for large models, but this paper broadens the scope to multiple architectures, optimizers, and scales, and adds the 2D scaling law that simultaneously predicts loss and optimal learning rate. In contrast to existing scaling laws (Kaplan et al. 2020, Hoffmann et al. 2022 for loss; Bi et al. 2024, Bjorck et al. 2024 for learning rate), this work unifies both predictions under a single O(1/√T) framework with a fixed exponent α=0.5.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners can determine the optimal peak learning rate for a large-scale training run (e.g., 7B model, 500k steps) from a handful of small-scale runs (0.1B model, 100–500k steps), with the scaling law extrapolating 80× across horizons and 70× across model sizes at R² ≥ 0.95. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include the failure to predict test loss under severe overfitting, the lack of a mechanistic explanation for why convex-like behavior emerges in non-convex landscapes, and the unknown number of initial iterations before the bound becomes tight. Open questions include whether the 1/√T scaling holds universally across all architectures and whether the qualifying exam can be extended to non-qualified schedules in a predictive (rather than merely diagnostic) way.
