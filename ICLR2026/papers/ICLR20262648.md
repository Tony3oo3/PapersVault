## Problem

Standard Mamba applied to learned image compression suffers from two fundamental limitations: its content-agnostic raster-scan order fails to group content-correlated but spatially distant tokens, weakening redundancy elimination, and its strict causality misaligns with the non-causal nature of 2D images. Multi-directional scanning, the common workaround, quadruples computational cost while still relying on fixed Euclidean paths.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (evaluated at 768×512, 1200×1200, and 2K resolutions) and produces a compressed bitstream via a standard VAE-style pipeline with entropy coding, alongside a reconstructed image. Training is performed on the Flickr2W dataset with MSE and MS-SSIM distortion metrics at multiple Lagrangian multipliers. The architecture uses a six-stage non-linear transform with channel dimensions {128, 192, 256, 320} and a fixed cluster count of 64 per CAM block.

## Method

Content-Aware Mamba (CAM) introduces two mechanisms to make Mamba content-adaptive for compression. First, Content-Adaptive Token Permutation (CTP) employs a shared, learnable codebook of 64 centroids (updated via EMA-smoothed cosine K-Means during training) to cluster latent tokens by feature similarity, then permutes the 1-D sequence so that content-correlated tokens become contiguous before the selective scan. Second, Global-Prior Prompting (GPP) projects the cluster centroids through a differentiable linear map into a redundancy-aware prompt dictionary, generates a sample-specific prompt matrix via one-hot cluster assignment, and injects it into the SSM output equation as O_i = (C + P)h_i + Dx_i, thereby relaxing strict causality without multi-directional scanning. The full CMIC model combines window attention for local dependencies with CAM blocks for long-range modeling in a VAE layout, paired with an enhanced SCCTX entropy model using depthwise convolution and gated MLPs.

## Results

CMIC achieves BD-rate savings of 15.91%, 21.34%, and 17.58% over VTM-21.0 on Kodak, Tecnick, and CLIC respectively, with 69.11M parameters, 2.39 TFLOPs, 0.405s decoding latency, and 4.44 GB peak memory on 2K images. On the high-resolution Tecnick dataset, CMIC sets a new best BD-rate of −21.34%, surpassing the previous best (MLICv2, −20.13%) by 1.21%. On CLIC, CMIC leads with −17.58%, outperforming DCAE (−16.46%) and MLICv2 (−15.79%). In terms of BD-PSNR, CMIC gains 0.36 dB over FTIC on Tecnick and 0.53 dB over TCM-L on the same dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the traditional codec VTM-21.0, CMIC achieves substantial BD-rate reductions of 15.91–21.34% across all three datasets. Among learned methods, CMIC outperforms the strongest Transformer-based baseline FTIC by 0.15 dB (Kodak), 0.36 dB (Tecnick), and 0.32 dB (CLIC) in BD-PSNR, and beats the hybrid CNN-Transformer TCM-L by up to 0.53 dB. On Kodak, MLICv2 (−16.16%) marginally edges out CMIC (−15.91%), but CMIC clearly leads on Tecnick (−21.34% vs. −20.13%) and CLIC (−17.58% vs. −15.79%), and uses fewer parameters (69.11M vs. 84.30M) and lower FLOPs (2.39T vs. 2.78T). Compared to the two Mamba-based baselines, CMIC surpasses MambaVC by 7.51–10.09% BD-rate and MambaIC by 2.17–6.48%, while reducing peak GPU memory by 78% relative to MambaIC (4.44 GB vs. 20.32 GB) thanks to its single-scan design. CMIC also outperforms DCAE on Tecnick and CLIC despite using roughly half the parameters (69.11M vs. 119.22M), and beats HPCM, S2CFormer, LALIC, CCA, WeConvene, and ELIC across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Mamba's selective state-space model for learned image compression by addressing two specific limitations of prior Mamba-based LIC models (MambaVC, MambaIC): the content-agnostic raster-scan order and strict one-directional causality. It draws on VQ-VAE-style codebook clustering for token grouping, the SCCTX entropy model from ELIC for the coding stage, and the attentive state-space prompting equation from MambaIRv2 for global-prior injection. The key conceptual departure is making the scan order and state modulation content-adaptive rather than fixed, so that the SSM processes tokens in feature-space proximity rather than Euclidean spatial order.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CMIC demonstrates that a single-scan, content-adaptive Mamba can outperform multi-directional-scan Mamba-based LIC models (MambaIC) by 6+ BD-rate points while using 78% less GPU memory, suggesting that smarter token ordering is more effective than simply scanning in multiple directions. The content-adaptive permutation idea is potentially transferable to other SSM-based vision tasks beyond compression. Open questions include whether the non-differentiable codebook clustering (updated via EMA K-Means) limits scalability to very large or diverse datasets, whether the approach extends to video compression, and why the CAM blocks provide negligible gains when applied to the entropy model despite strong gains in the transform network.
