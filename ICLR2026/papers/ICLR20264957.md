## Problem

Existing dual-system robot architectures separate high-level reasoning (System Two, e.g., a VLM) from low-level action generation (System One, e.g., a VLA), causing the two systems to lack mutual awareness of each other's capabilities and introducing significant inference latency. This paper asks whether a single unified vision-language-action model can perform both reasoning and acting, and crucially, adaptively decide *when* to engage each mode rather than reasoning at every step or never.

## Input / Output

The model consumes current multi-camera RGB images, reference images from the most recent reasoning timestep (to anchor potentially stale textual scene descriptions), a language instruction (which accumulates human interaction text over time), the latest reasoning content R, and the robot's proprioceptive state (including states from 0.05 s and 0.25 s earlier for temporal context). At each timestep it first emits a binary decision token—[BOR] or [BOA]—then either auto-regressively generates a four-field textual reasoning block (scene description, high-level plan, historical summary, next-step instruction) or produces a continuous action chunk via a flow-matching action expert. In deployment, temporal ensemble averaging over overlapping 0.2 s action windows smooths execution.

## Method

OneTwoVLA builds on π₀'s transformer backbone and flow-matching action expert, adding two special decision tokens that let the model autonomously switch between a reasoning mode (cross-entropy-supervised text generation) and an acting mode (flow-matching action prediction). Robot demonstrations are segmented into reasoning intervals (at subtask completions, error detections, or interaction points) and acting intervals; a two-stage Gemini 2.5 Pro pipeline auto-annotates interval boundaries and generates the four-field reasoning content, achieving 81.5% interval accuracy and 83.3% scene-description quality per human evaluation. To scale beyond costly robot data, the authors design a fully automated synthesis pipeline that uses Gemini 2.5 Pro to generate tabletop layout descriptions, FLUX.1-dev to render images (augmented with fisheye distortion and gripper compositing), and Gemini again to produce task instructions with embodied reasoning, yielding 16,000 vision-language samples co-trained with robot data. This co-training activates web-scale commonsense knowledge already in the pretrained VLM, enabling generalization to unseen objects, novel task instructions, and open-world visual grounding without additional robot demonstrations.

## Results

On three challenging long-horizon manipulation tasks (Tomato-Egg, Hotpot, Cocktail), OneTwoVLA achieves an 87% average success rate, outperforming the flat VLA baseline π₀ by 30 percentage points and the dual-system (Hi Robot-inspired) baseline by 24 percentage points. On error detection and recovery, OneTwoVLA recovers from 80% of induced failures versus 57.1% for π₀ and 58.3% for the dual-system approach. In human-robot interaction, OneTwoVLA achieves 100% subtask success (20/20) compared to 65% for the dual-system baseline. On visual grounding, OneTwoVLA-VL (with synthetic VL co-training) reaches 88% in the Single-Env setting and 73% in the Open-World setting, versus 5% and 3% for π₀ respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: (1) π₀, a state-of-the-art flat VLA fine-tuned on the same robot data, and (2) a dual-system approach inspired by Hi Robot where Gemini 2.5 Pro serves as System Two (high-level planner) and a fine-tuned π₀ serves as System One (low-level executor). OneTwoVLA decisively outperforms both on long-horizon task planning (87% vs. ~57% for π₀ and ~63% for dual-system), error recovery (80% vs. 57.1% and 58.3%), and human-robot interaction (100% vs. 65% for dual-system; π₀ is omitted as it cannot generate language). The dual-system baseline suffers from two specific failure modes: System Two occasionally emits infeasible atomic commands that System One cannot execute, and Gemini's inference latency causes outdated reasoning that pushes System One into out-of-distribution states. On visual grounding, the gap is most dramatic: π₀ achieves only 5% in Single-Env (failing even when the target object is explicitly named), while OneTwoVLA reaches 78%, and OneTwoVLA-VL further improves to 88% and generalizes to 73% in the Open-World setting with unseen objects and environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on π₀ (Black et al., 2024) as its base VLA, inheriting its flow-matching action expert and overall architecture, while departing from the dominant dual-system paradigm (Hi Robot, ViLa, ECoT) that separates a VLM-based System Two from a VLA-based System One. The key conceptual departure is unifying both reasoning and acting within a single model that adaptively switches between modes via learned decision tokens, rather than invoking an external VLM at fixed intervals. The co-training strategy with synthetic embodied-reasoning vision-language data extends prior co-training work (RT-2, EmbodiedGPT) by generating task-specific reasoning content at scale via a Gemini + FLUX pipeline, addressing the domain gap that limits existing VL datasets for robot learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified adaptive-reasoning architecture addresses a practical bottleneck in dual-system robot control: the lack of mutual capability awareness and inference latency between separate reasoning and acting modules. The 30% improvement over π₀ on long-horizon tasks and the dramatic visual grounding gap (5% vs. 78%) suggest that explicit, in-model reasoning is not merely auxiliary but essential for VLAs to truly ground language in the visual world. Key open questions include whether the hand-selected heuristics for triggering reasoning can be replaced by RL-based policies, whether asynchronous reasoning-action architectures can eliminate the 2–3 second pauses, and whether the approach scales to larger model sizes without action-inference becoming a bottleneck. The scalable synthetic data pipeline is a practically important contribution that could benefit the broader VLA community.
