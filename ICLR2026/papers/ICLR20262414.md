## Problem

Existing evolution-based alpha mining approaches—whether Genetic Programming or LLM-driven multi-agent frameworks—lack mechanisms for systematic, self-directed evolution. GP relies on heuristic mutation without learning from failures, while LLM-based agents tend to fall into repetitive local modifications without long-horizon planning or reflective reasoning, reducing alpha mining to a brittle 'search-backtest-restart' cycle rather than a continuous evolutionary trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262414/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an expert-designed seed alpha expression and a stock universe's feature matrix (price, chip-distribution, order-flow, and benchmark data over a time horizon) as input. Over up to 3 evolution turns, the agent proposes multiple offspring alphas per turn (4 by default) that are constrained to remain structurally similar to the seed (AST-based similarity ≤ δ) to preserve interpretability. The output is a set of evolved alpha expressions evaluated by Information Ratio (IR) and Annualized Excess Return (AER) on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution market regimes.

## Method

AlphaAgentEvo extends GRPO into an Agentic Reinforcement Learning (ARL) formulation for multi-turn, tool-in-the-loop alpha evolution, where the policy LLM generates reasoning and tool-call tokens across turns while conditioning on the full past trajectory for reflective reasoning. A hierarchical reward function with five capped components—tool-call validity, seed consistency, exploration diversity, performance improvement, and consecutive-improvement streak—progressively guides the agent from basic compliance (valid tool calls) to higher-level objectives (sustained performance gains), with the tool-use term acting as a cost to prevent brute-force search. Trajectories originating from the same seed are grouped for advantage estimation, and only policy-generated tokens (not tool-emitted tokens) contribute to gradient updates via a masking mechanism. Trained on Qwen3 (1.7B/4B) for 150 steps on 10× RTX4090 GPUs, the agent acquires long-horizon planning and reflective reasoning behaviors that enable it to adapt to market regime shifts and avoid reward hacking through over-concentration on narrow patterns.

## Results

On AlphaEvo500 (HS300, 2024–2025), AlphaAgentEvo-4B achieves a pass@3 of 0.97 and pass@5 of 0.97 with a valid ratio of 0.979, outperforming the strongest baseline GEPA (GPT-5-mini) which scores 0.87/0.90 on the same metrics. On the external Alpha158 library, AlphaAgentEvo-4B reaches pass@5 of 0.994 in the bullish period (2024–2025) and 0.725 in the bearish period (2023–2024), while GP with 4 offspring scores near zero (0.003 pass@5). In the multi-factor portfolio comparison (Table 3), AlphaAgentEvo-4B attains an AER of 0.129 and IR of 2.442, surpassing AlphaAgent (IR 2.046) and AlphaQCM (IR 1.815).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlphaAgentEvo-4B decisively outperforms all baselines on AlphaEvo500: it beats GEPA (GPT-5-mini) by +0.10 on HS300 pass@3 (0.97 vs. 0.87) and +0.06 on CSI500 pass@5 (0.95 vs. 0.91), and surpasses GPT-5-mini by +0.22 on HS300 pass@3. Even the 1.7B variant exceeds GPT-5-mini on HS300 pass@5 (0.90 vs. 0.88) and matches it on pass@3 (0.77 vs. 0.75). On Alpha158, AlphaAgentEvo-4B dominates GP (4 offspring) by a wide margin (pass@5: 0.994 vs. 0.003 in the bullish period) and outperforms AlphaAgent (DeepSeek-R1) by +0.144 on pass@5 (0.994 vs. 0.848). ToolRL-4B, the closest agentic-RL baseline, trails AlphaAgentEvo-4B by +0.22 on HS300 pass@3 (0.75 vs. 0.97), confirming that multi-turn planning and reflective reasoning—rather than single-turn tool-use RL—are critical for sustained alpha evolution. In the multi-factor portfolio setting, AlphaAgentEvo-4B achieves the highest AER (0.129) and IR (2.442) while also recording the lowest maximum drawdown (−0.176), outperforming AlphaAgent (IR 2.046) and AlphaQCM (IR 1.815).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024; DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025) from single-turn text optimization to a multi-turn, tool-in-loop Agentic Reinforcement Learning (ARL) formulation, where the policy LLM plans, proposes, and reflects across an entire evolution trajectory. It departs from traditional Genetic Programming (Lin et al., 2019; Schmidt & Lipson, 2010) by replacing heuristic mutation with LLM-driven semantic reasoning, and from existing LLM/multi-agent alpha mining frameworks (AlphaAgent, GEPA) by introducing a self-evolving agent that acquires long-horizon planning and reflective reasoning through RL rather than relying on prompt engineering. The hierarchical reward design (tool-call validity, AST-based consistency, exploration diversity, performance, and streak) is novel to the alpha mining domain, drawing on ideas from reward shaping in RL but tailored to the noisy, high-variance feedback of financial backtesting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 4B-parameter model trained with ARL consistently outperforms GPT-5-mini and DeepSeek-R1 on alpha evolution is practically significant for quant finance, suggesting that domain-specific RL fine-tuning can substitute for much larger general-purpose models in structured search tasks. The ARL formulation (extending GRPO to multi-turn tool-in-loop trajectories with a mask over tool-emitted tokens) is a reusable technique that could transfer to other iterative, tool-augmented reasoning domains. Open questions include whether the self-evolution behaviors (long-horizon planning, reflective reasoning) generalize beyond alpha mining, how the approach scales to larger stock universes or multi-factor portfolio construction, and whether the gains persist under live trading conditions with transaction costs and market impact. The evaluation is limited to Chinese markets (HS300, CSI500) and a single-factor protocol, leaving cross-market and cross-asset generalization untested.
