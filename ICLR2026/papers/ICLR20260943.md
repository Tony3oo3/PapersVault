## Problem

Existing deep multi-view clustering methods typically prioritize either semantic (intrinsic sample) or structural (inter-sample relational) information while treating the other as auxiliary, and they enforce alignment at only one or two granularities. This leads to imbalanced integration, suboptimal generalization across datasets where the relative reliability of semantic versus structural cues varies, and insufficient preservation of semantic-structural coherence in the learned embeddings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260943/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes V view-specific feature matrices X^(v) ∈ R^{N×d^(v)} (with heterogeneous dimensions across views) and a user-specified number of clusters K. It produces a single fused latent embedding l_i per sample (combining semantic and structural information across all views) and a hard cluster assignment y_pred^(i) derived from a sharpened Student's t-distribution soft assignment. Graph structures are initialized per view using k-nearest neighbors (Euclidean distance for image datasets, cosine similarity for text datasets), and pseudo-labels from the clustering head are refreshed every 3 epochs to drive the inter-cluster alignment loss.

## Method

DREAM disentangles semantic and structural information through two parallel encoders: a VAE branch that learns sample-level semantic content via reconstruction and KL regularization, and a GCN branch that captures inter-sample relations by propagating features over a k-NN graph with a graph-reconstruction loss. A gated fusion module then integrates these branches in two stages: an intra-view sigmoid gate adaptively balances semantic and structural embeddings within each view, and a cross-view softmax weighting—guided by structural embeddings as reliability cues—aggregates the gated representations across views into a single fused embedding. A triple-granularity alignment objective enforces consistency at three levels: cross-view distillation (pulling per-view semantic and structural embeddings toward consensus targets), intra-sample InfoNCE (keeping the fused embedding close to its own semantic and structural counterparts while repelling other samples'), and inter-cluster triplet loss (using pseudo-labels to enforce intra-cluster compactness and inter-cluster separation). The overall loss jointly optimizes encoding, alignment, and clustering (Student's t-distribution with entropy and KL terms) in an end-to-end unsupervised manner.

## Results

DREAM achieves the best ACC, NMI, and Purity across all six benchmark datasets (Yale, NGS, BBC, UCI, HW, ALOI100). Headline numbers include 97.80% ACC / 95.05% NMI on HW, 95.90% ACC / 92.01% NMI on UCI, and 87.00% ACC / 90.88% NMI on ALOI100. On the most challenging dataset ALOI100 (10,800 samples, 100 clusters), DREAM surpasses the second-best method GDMVC by 5.19% in ACC, 4.22% in NMI, and 5.93% in Purity. The method also shows the largest margin on BBC (text-based, 4 views), where it reaches 90.07% ACC versus 86.57% for the next-best SCMVC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DREAM outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and every metric. The closest competitor is GDMVC (Bai et al., 2024), which is second-best on Yale (76.97 vs. 78.18 ACC), NGS (40.20 vs. 97.80 ACC — a massive gap), UCI (85.50 vs. 95.90 ACC), HW (88.25 vs. 97.80 ACC), and ALOI100 (81.81 vs. 87.00 ACC). On BBC, SCMVC (Wu et al., 2024) is the strongest baseline at 86.57% ACC, still trailing DREAM by 3.50 points. On NGS, SCM (Luo et al., 2024) reaches 97.20% ACC, only 0.60 points below DREAM's 97.80%. Notably, several baselines (MFLVC, SEM, GCFAggMVC, MVCAN, SCM) collapse to near-random or very low scores on ALOI100 (e.g., MVCAN at 67.98% ACC, GCFAggMVC at 4.85% ACC), while DREAM maintains 87.00% ACC, highlighting its robustness on large-scale, fine-grained multi-view data.

## Relation to Prior Work

DREAM carves new ground at the intersection of autoencoder-based MVC (Xu et al., 2022a; Du et al., 2021), graph-based MVC (Fan et al., 2020; Ling et al., 2023), and contrastive MVC (Lin et al., 2022b; Xu et al., 2022b), but departs from all three by explicitly disentangling semantic (VAE) and structural (GCN) information into parallel branches rather than treating one as primary and the other as auxiliary. The gated fusion module repurposes the gating mechanism from sequence modeling to adaptively balance heterogeneous feature distributions, and the triple-granularity alignment (cross-view distillation, InfoNCE intra-sample, triplet inter-cluster) extends prior single- or dual-level contrastive objectives into a unified multi-scale consistency framework. The clustering head follows the Student's t-distribution soft-assignment paradigm popularized by deep embedded clustering (DEC/IDEC), making the overall architecture a recombination of established components into a new pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in MVC: the implicit assumption that semantic and structural information have fixed relative importance across datasets. The ablation finding that removing the semantic branch *improves* performance on the text-based BBC dataset (91.53 vs. 90.07 ACC) while removing the structural branch causes catastrophic failure (29.20 ACC) provides concrete evidence that dataset-dependent information reliability is real and that adaptive fusion is necessary rather than merely convenient. Open questions include scalability to incomplete multi-view settings (where some views are missing for some samples), extension to more than two information types, and whether the pseudo-label refresh schedule (every 3 epochs) is optimal or dataset-sensitive. The method's reliance on pre-constructed k-NN graphs also limits its applicability to very large-scale or streaming scenarios.
