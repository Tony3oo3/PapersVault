## Problem

Bold language in ML papers can stem from either strong empirical results or mere rhetorical style, yet no systematic framework exists to disentangle the two. Prior approaches (promotional lexicons, certainty classifiers) score only the observed text, conflating stylistic framing with the strength of underlying evidence. This paper formalizes rhetorical style as a latent variable conditional on fixed substantive content, enabling measurement of 'how' a contribution is framed independent of 'what' it contributes.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the substantive content (methods, experiments, and results sections) extracted from a paper via GPT-4o-mini, along with the paper's original abstract. The substantive content is used to generate 30 counterfactual abstracts under different rhetorical personas, each length-constrained to within ±15 words of the original. The output is a single continuous rhetorical strength score (a real-valued scalar on a calibrated Bradley-Terry scale, empirically ranging from −4.74 to 4.53 across the ICLR corpus), estimated via MAP with a Gaussian prior to avoid degenerate solutions when a query abstract wins or loses all its pairwise comparisons.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. First, a panel of 30 hand-designed LLM personas (ranging from 'Sleep-Deprived PhD Student' to 'Geoffrey Hinton') generates counterfactual abstracts from identical substantive content; GPT-4o then performs pairwise judgments on which abstract makes stronger claims, and the 8,700 resulting comparisons are aggregated via a Bradley-Terry model to assign each persona a calibrated rhetorical strength parameter. Second, for any query abstract, the LLM judge compares it against all 30 persona abstracts derived from the same paper's content, and the query's score is inferred by MAP estimation under the Bradley-Terry likelihood with a Gaussian prior. The key design principle is the counterfactual structure: by holding X (substantive content) fixed and varying only Y (descriptive language), the latent variable Z (rhetorical strength) is isolated, paralleling a 'what-if' intervention in causal inference. The approach is validated by robustness to persona subset selection (mean Spearman 0.89 across 1,000 random splits) and strong agreement with human annotations (88.4% pairwise agreement, Spearman ρ = 0.92 at the aggregate level).

## Results

Applying the counterfactual framework to 8,485 ICLR submissions (2017–2025), the proposed rhetorical score significantly predicts all six downstream attention outcomes after controlling for peer-review rating, subfield, and year. A one-unit increase in rhetorical score is associated with +24.53 citations (p<0.001), +3.19 media posts (p<0.001), +2.51 tweets (p<0.001), +0.03 RSS feeds (p<0.001), +0.04 patents (p<0.01), and +2.71 unique accounts (p<0.001). For context, a one-unit increase in average reviewer score corresponds to ~89 additional citations, indicating the rhetorical effect is non-trivial. The rhetorical score shows no significant correlation with reviewer ratings (Spearman ρ = −0.015, p = 0.225), confirming it captures style rather than quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two published baselines and one LLM-based direct-rating baseline. The Promotion Score (Peng et al., 2024), which computes the proportion of promotional-lexicon words in the abstract, reaches only marginal significance on citations (20.01†) and feeds (0.02*) and is non-significant on all other outcomes. The Certainty Score (Pei & Jurgens, 2021), a pretrained sentence-level certainty classifier, produces a large but statistically non-significant citation coefficient (59.56, SE 76.96) and, notably, significant *negative* coefficients for media posts (−12.74*), tweets (−9.74*), and accounts (−9.97*), suggesting higher certainty is associated with *less* public attention—an inconsistent and counterintuitive pattern. The Direct Rating baseline (LLM 1–10 overclaiming score) yields a significant negative citation coefficient (−26.11*) and is non-significant everywhere else. In contrast, the proposed counterfactual rhetorical score is the only measure that achieves statistical significance (p<0.01 or better) on all six outcomes simultaneously, demonstrating that disentangling style from content via counterfactual generation yields a far more reliable and consistent predictor of downstream attention than surface-level lexical or classifier-based proxies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior approaches to measuring rhetorical style in scientific writing—promotional lexicon scoring (Peng et al., 2024; Gentzkow & Shapiro, 2010), certainty classifiers (Pei & Jurgens, 2021), and sensationalism labels (Prabhakaran et al., 2016)—which all infer style directly from observed text and thus conflate framing with underlying evidence. It repurposes the Bradley-Terry pairwise-comparison framework (originally from psychometrics) and the preference-based learning paradigm (RLHF/DPO) for a distinct purpose: constructing a calibrated measurement scale rather than training a reward model. The counterfactual design draws on causal inference, and the use of multiple texts describing the same event to infer latent attributes parallels political-science methods (Carlson & Montgomery, 2017; Licht et al., 2025), but the specific application to disentangling rhetorical style from substantive content in ML papers is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale quantitative evidence that LLM-based writing tools are systematically reshaping rhetorical norms in scientific research, with the post-2023 rise in rhetorical strength strongly correlated with estimated LLM usage (r=0.904). The finding that visionary framing predicts citations and media attention independently of peer-review quality raises normative questions about how presentation shapes scientific visibility. Key limitations include the single-dimension 'strength' formulation, restriction to abstracts, reliance on LLM judges that may inherit model biases, and the correlational (not causal) nature of the LLM-adoption link. Open questions include extending to multi-dimensional rhetorical style, researcher-level outcomes, and tracking how generative AI continues to alter communication norms over time.
