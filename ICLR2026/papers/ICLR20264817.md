## Problem

Existing trajectory planners for autonomous driving either use rasterized occupancy representations that lose fine individual semantics or vectorized representations that oversimplify evolving multi-agent interactions. Most methods generate trajectories without explicitly exploiting predicted future scene evolution, and no prior work effectively combines the probabilistic joint-state modeling of occupancy grids with the individual fidelity of vectorized trajectory decoding in a unified planning pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264817/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 2 seconds of ego and agent kinematic states at 10 Hz, static object positions, HD map polylines, plus a 128×128 rasterized BEV grid (covering [-20m, 60m] × [-40m, 40m] at 0.625 m/pixel) encoding current ego/agent/map occupancy and a backward flow field. It outputs 6 multimodal ego trajectories over an 8-second horizon at 0.1 s intervals, separated ego and agent future occupancy grids at 1 s intervals, corresponding flow fields, per-modality confidence scores, and short-term (2 s) marginal occupancy for pruned interactive agents used only during training.

## Method

OccDriver employs a dual-branch transformer with a three-stage iterative decoding structure: a coarse trajectory decoder produces multimodal ego and agent trajectories, a future scene decoder (acting as a 2D occupancy world model) predicts scene evolution conditioned on those coarse trajectories, and a fine trajectory decoder refines trajectories using the predicted future scene as cross-attention context. The rasterized branch uses Swin-Transformers to encode occupancy and flow, fused with vectorized features via a two-layer cross-attention joint scene encoder. Three specialized losses bridge the branches: occupancy interference loss enforces spatial exclusivity between ego and agent occupancy, occupancy guidance loss (alignment + collision) steers trajectories within ego's high-occupancy region and away from agents' high-occupancy region, and a contingency planning objective takes the element-wise maximum of joint and marginal occupancy over a short horizon to account for individual behavioral uncertainty. Agent pruning via rule-based role classification limits marginal occupancy computation to directly interactive agents, reducing memory from O(n) to a manageable constant.

## Results

On the nuPlan Val14 closed-loop benchmark, OccDriver achieves state-of-the-art planning scores of 0.896 NR-Score and 0.838 R-Score, while also attaining the best safety metrics among all learning-based methods (0.971 Collisions, 0.938 TTC). On the more challenging Test14-Hard split, OccDriver reaches 0.794 NR-S and 0.759 R-S, outperforming vectorized-only (PLUTO), topology-guided (BeTopNet), and diffusion-based (DiffusionPlanner) methods. On the NavSim end-to-end benchmark, OccDriver achieves a PDM Score of 89.2, surpassing the TransFuser-based baseline (87.7) and prior end-to-end methods (UniAD 83.4, TransFuser 84.0, PARA-Drive 84.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OccDriver outperforms the strongest vectorized-only baseline PLUTO on all safety and driving-score metrics (Collisions 0.971 vs. 0.962, TTC 0.938 vs. 0.933, NR-S 0.896 vs. 0.890, R-S 0.838 vs. 0.800), though PLUTO retains a slight edge on Progress (0.896 vs. 0.885). Against the topology-guided BeTopNet, OccDriver wins on NR-S (+1.3%), R-S (+0.1%), Collisions, TTC, and Comfort, while BeTopNet shows a higher Collision score on Test14-Hard (0.968 vs. 0.941) but suffers a large Progress drop (0.747 vs. 0.829), indicating over-conservative planning. DiffusionPlanner achieves a marginally higher NR-S on Val14 (0.899 vs. 0.896) but trails OccDriver on R-S (0.828 vs. 0.838) and all safety metrics; on Test14-Hard the gap widens (NR-S 0.760 vs. 0.794, R-S 0.692 vs. 0.759). On the NavSim end-to-end benchmark, OccDriver surpasses the TransFuser-based baseline by +1.5 PDM-S (89.2 vs. 87.7) and improves DAC from 95.7 to 97.5, while also outperforming UniAD, TransFuser, and PARA-Drive by 4–6 PDM-S points. Overall, OccDriver consistently wins or ties on the primary driving-score metrics across all three benchmarks, with the occupancy-guided dual-branch design providing the most notable gains in safety (Collisions, TTC) and reactive closed-loop performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

OccDriver builds on the occupancy flow field prediction framework of Liu et al. (2023a) for its rasterized branch, but departs from prior occupancy-assisted planners (e.g., Liu et al. 2023b, Yang et al. 2025) by integrating occupancy prediction as an internal world model within the planner rather than as a separate evaluation or perception module. Conceptually, it bridges the rasterized (scene-level probabilistic) and vectorized (individual-level precise) planning paradigms that have been treated as competing approaches, and reformulates contingency planning from tree-structured trajectory optimization into a dense probabilistic occupancy space using marginal and joint distributions. The coarse-to-fine iterative decoding structure, where a future scene decoder conditions trajectory refinement, is a novel architectural departure from both single-pass vectorized planners and occupancy-as-cost-function approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that treating future occupancy as an in-planner world model—rather than a post-hoc scoring signal—yields consistent safety gains (0.971 collision, 0.938 TTC) without the over-conservatism that plagues topology-based or diffusion-based planners, as evidenced by the +10.3% R-S over BeTopNet while maintaining progress. The occupancy-space contingency formulation (element-wise max of marginal and joint distributions over a short horizon) offers a scalable, differentiable alternative to combinatorial scenario-tree planning. Open questions include whether the discretized temporal occupancy can be unified into a continuous spatiotemporal distribution, whether the two branches can be made fully end-to-end, and whether safe RL with CVaR objectives can further exploit the occupancy world model for risk-aware planning.
