## Problem

Multi-modal 3D object detectors are trained on synchronized sensor data, but real-world deployment inevitably introduces time offsets between sensors due to differing sampling frequencies, network latency, and processing bottlenecks. Ego Motion Compensation (EMC) aligns static objects but fails on dynamic objects, while existing scene flow methods require two point clouds and assume fixed time offsets, making them unsuitable for asynchronous multi-modal settings with arbitrary, varying delays.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260873/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view camera images, a LiDAR point cloud, the known time offset Δt between the two sensor timestamps, and ego-motion information as input. It produces a dense 2D velocity/flow field in BEV space (H_BEV × W_BEV × 2) that is used to warp the asynchronous sensor's BEV features to the reference timestamp, ultimately yielding 3D bounding boxes. Notably, the flow is 2D in BEV space rather than 3D scene flow, and the time offset is explicitly conditioned on the prediction, allowing the same trained module to handle any Δt at inference without retraining.

## Method

AsyncBEV introduces a new task, Δ-BEVFlow, which predicts a 2D flow field in BEV space from concatenated multi-modal BEV features (camera and LiDAR) conditioned on the known time offset Δt. The preferred velocity-based formulation (BEV-VE) first predicts per-cell velocity independent of Δt via a U-Net decoder, then multiplies by Δt to obtain motion, which regularizes the task by forcing zero displacement at Δt=0. For token-based detectors, the predicted flow corrects the 3D positional encodings of object queries; for grid-based detectors, it builds a lookup table to warp BEV feature maps via grid sampling. A lightweight SimpleBEV encoder generates image BEV features when the detector does not already produce them. Training uses a weighted combination of flow supervision (L2 distance to dense GT flow from bounding boxes) and standard detection losses, with the option to freeze the entire detector and train only the AsyncBEV module.

## Results

On nuScenes val with LiDAR as the asynchronous sensor, CMT+AsyncBEV achieves 70.0% NDS and 64.4% mAP at a 0.5 s time offset, surpassing the CMT+EMC baseline (63.3% NDS, 54.1% mAP) by 6.7% NDS and 12.3% mAP. For dynamic objects specifically, CMT+AsyncBEV reaches 43.4% NDS versus 26.8% for EMC, a 16.6% gain. Similarly, UniBEV+AsyncBEV achieves 63.3% NDS at 0.5 s (vs. 58.9% for UniBEV+EMC), with dynamic-object NDS of 37.8% vs. 25.9% (11.9% improvement). In the synchronized (0 s) case, AsyncBEV incurs only a 0.4% NDS drop for CMT and 1.0% for UniBEV, while adding just 0.1–0.4 FPS of latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla CMT detector (Yan et al., 2023), AsyncBEV recovers most of the performance lost to asynchrony: at 0.5 s offset, CMT+AsyncBEV achieves 70.0% NDS versus 43.2% for vanilla CMT (a 26.8% absolute gain), and 43.4% NDS on dynamic objects versus 26.1%. Similarly, UniBEV (Wang et al., 2024) drops from 66.7% to 39.7% NDS at 0.5 s, while UniBEV+AsyncBEV reaches 63.3% NDS (23.6% gain) and 37.8% on dynamic objects. Compared to Fan et al. (2025), which propagates temporal features into point-cloud inputs, AsyncBEV outperforms at every time offset, with the gap widening from 1.9% NDS at 0.25 s to 3.8% at 0.5 s for All Objects; Fan et al. also degrades the synchronous case by 2.3% NDS. Against StreamingFlow (Shi et al., 2024), which uses a GRU-ODE over a sequence of past frames, AsyncBEV achieves higher NDS at all offsets (e.g., 63.3% vs. 54.1% at 0.5 s for UniBEV) while running 2.7× faster (2.7 FPS vs. 1.0 FPS), since AsyncBEV only processes the single latest asynchronous frame rather than a full history.

## Relation to Prior Work

AsyncBEV generalizes the scene flow estimation paradigm (DeFlow, HIMO, SEFlow) from raw point-cloud pairs with fixed time offsets to a new Δ-BEVFlow task operating on multi-modal BEV features with arbitrary, continuously varying time offsets. It extends ego-motion compensation, which aligns only static objects, by predicting the residual dynamic motion in BEV space, and departs from prior asynchronous fusion work (CoBEVFlow, UniV2X) that relies on object-proposal quality and homogeneous sensor features. The velocity-based formulation (BEV-VE) borrows the motion = velocity × Δt decomposition to regularize the learning task, a design choice absent from prior scene flow or temporal alignment methods like StreamingFlow and TimeAlign that instead use heavy RNN/GRU-ODE modules over long feature sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sensor asynchrony is an unavoidable practical issue in deployed autonomous driving systems, yet most multi-modal 3D detectors are trained and evaluated only on perfectly synchronized data. AsyncBEV's 304K-parameter module recovers most of the performance lost to asynchrony (e.g., 16.6% NDS on dynamic objects at 0.5 s) with negligible latency, and its architecture-agnostic design makes it a drop-in robustness layer for both token-based and grid-based detectors. Key limitations include the assumption that one sensor is always the synchronous reference, evaluation on a single dataset (nuScenes), and the open question of scaling to fully streaming multi-sensor asynchrony where multiple sensors are simultaneously stale. The finding that a lightweight flow-prediction module substantially outperforms both data augmentation and sensor-fallback strategies suggests that explicitly modeling dynamic motion is the right inductive bias for this problem.
