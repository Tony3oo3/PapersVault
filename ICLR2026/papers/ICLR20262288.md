## Problem

Previous work established that a QAT phase following full-precision training improves quantized model accuracy, and suggested a fixed ~10% QAT fraction is optimal. However, how the optimal QAT-to-FP compute allocation varies with total compute budget, model size, and quantization bit width remained unexplored, leaving practitioners without guidance for scaling QAT planning as training budgets grow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262288/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full-precision model checkpoint, a total token budget, a QAT bit width B, and a model parameter count N, and produces a predicted final loss, the optimal QAT fraction f*, and (via the scaling law) the optimal QAT bit width under a given memory constraint. A key design choice is the tokens-per-parameter-byte statistic S = D/(N·B/8), which collapses model size, bit width, and token count into a single predictive variable, enabling one unified formula to span 1-bit through 6-bit QAT across 86M–2.2B parameter models without per-bit-width refitting.

## Method

The paper derives a unified loss scaling law L(N, D_qat, D_fp, B) = Chinchilla-like term + δ(N, D_qat, D_fp, B), where the QAT penalty δ decomposes into three sub-terms: an irreducible QAT error (θ·2^(−κB)), a pure QAT penalty decaying with S_qat, and an FP/QAT interaction term decaying with both S_fp and S_qat. Fitted across 757 QAT experiments (R² = 0.98–0.99 per bit width), this single formula captures the empirically observed phenomenon that the optimal QAT fraction grows with S_total, and enables inference of optimal fractions, wasted-token costs of sub-optimal allocation, and the minimum bit width matching FP accuracy. Additionally, the paper proposes QAT & Learning Rate Cooldown Fusion, where learning rate decay is performed jointly with QAT (starting QAT from the constant-LR stage rather than after a separate FP cooldown), eliminating redundant high-precision updates that would be discarded by quantization initialization and yielding 2–39% wasted-token savings across model sizes.

## Results

The proposed loss scaling law achieves R²=0.982 (1-bit) to R²=0.991 (6-bit) fit quality across 757 QAT experiments, with optimal QAT fraction prediction MAE of 0.074–0.102. The optimal QAT fraction prediction (Section 3.1) achieves 0.091 MAE across all model sizes (86M–759M) and bit widths, and generalizes to a 2.2B model. For the QAT & Cooldown Fusion method, perplexity improvements of 0.06%–1.72% translate to 2.2%–38.8% wasted-token savings across model sizes (74M–816M) and token counts (10.5B–1.4T), with the largest gains at 6-bit QAT (up to 38.8% at 425M/289.7B tokens).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the classic QAT pipeline (full FP training with cooldown, then QAT with learning-rate re-warmup) and the proposed QAT & Cooldown Fusion (QAT starts directly from the constant-LR stage, with cooldown performed jointly with QAT). The fused scheme consistently outperforms the classic scheme for 4-bit and 6-bit QAT across all model sizes and token counts, saving 2.2%–38.8% of total tokens (measured as 'wasted tokens' in the scaling law). For 1-bit and 2-bit QAT, improvements are mixed and less prominent, which the authors attribute to the large optimal QAT fraction at low bit widths leaving little FP cooldown to eliminate. A second key comparison is between a fixed 10% QAT fraction (the previously assumed optimal) and the computed optimal fraction: for 1-bit QAT at high token counts, using the optimal fraction achieves the same loss with roughly 50% of the compute, while for 6-bit the savings are much smaller. The paper also contrasts its unified scaling law (handling FP+QAT jointly across bit widths) with Chen et al. (2025b), whose QAT-only scaling law cannot model the FP→QAT transition and whose QAT penalty term diverges as token count grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the QAT scaling law of Chen et al. (2025b), which modeled loss for QAT-from-scratch, by introducing explicit dependence on both FP and QAT token counts and unifying across bit widths. It departs from Liu et al. (2025), who demonstrated that a fixed ~10% QAT fraction works well in limited setups, by showing this fraction is not stationary but grows with the tokens-per-parameter-byte statistic. The QAT & Cooldown Fusion technique builds on the WSD learning-rate analysis of Wen et al. (2024) and Hägele et al. (2024), repurposing the observation that cooldown performs low-magnitude weight adjustments to argue those adjustments are wasted when QAT initialization discards high-precision information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that optimal QAT fraction is compute-dependent directly changes how practitioners plan training pipelines for on-device LLMs: a fixed 10% rule can waste up to ~50% of compute at low bit widths, and the derived scaling law enables prediction of optimal bit-width under memory constraints. The QAT & Cooldown Fusion technique offers an immediate, low-cost improvement to the standard QAT pipeline. Key limitations include reliance on a single Llama-2-style architecture and the DCLM dataset (though SlimPajama verification supports generality), and open questions remain about interaction with FP8/FP4 pretraining precision and with downstream SFT/RL stages.
