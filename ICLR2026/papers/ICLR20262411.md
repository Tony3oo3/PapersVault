## Problem

Existing unified multimodal generative models face a fundamental trade-off: autoregressive architectures enable strong language modeling but require slow sequential decoding of thousands of image tokens, while non-autoregressive discrete diffusion models (e.g., UniDisc) support parallel generation but, when trained from scratch, lack the rich visual priors needed for high-fidelity image synthesis. Moreover, most 'unified' models rely on hybrid designs that couple autoregressive text with continuous or discrete diffusion for images, introducing architectural complexity and preventing a truly symmetric generative paradigm across modalities.

## Input / Output

The model consumes text prompts (truncated to 77 CLIP tokens), RGB images (resized to 512×512 or 1024×1024 and quantized to VQ-VAE codebook indices), or an image-plus-question pair for VQA. Outputs are pixel-space images decoded from VQ-VAE latents, or text sequences of up to 77 tokens decoded through a lightweight linear head. All modalities are represented in a shared discrete token space, so the same generator and sampler handle every task—only the conditioning signal (text embedding, image embedding, or both) changes between text-to-image, image-to-text, and VQA.

## Method

Muddit is a MaskGIT-style unified discrete diffusion transformer whose MM-DiT backbone (following FLUX's dual/single-stream design) is initialized from the pretrained Meissonic text-to-image model, injecting a strong visual prior that prior from-scratch discrete diffusion models lack. Both image and text tokens are corrupted via an absorbing-state continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) with a cosine masking schedule, and a single masked-token predictor is trained with a re-weighted cross-entropy (negative ELBO) loss that is identical for text-to-image and image-to-text directions—switching tasks only changes the cross-modal conditioning input. At inference, the model starts from an all-mask sequence and iteratively un-masks a fraction of tokens per step (64 steps by default) using the analytic reverse posterior, with classifier-free guidance applied uniformly regardless of modality. A lightweight linear text decoder converts predicted text token IDs back to vocabulary tokens, while the frozen VQ-VAE decoder reconstructs images from predicted codebook indices. This visual-first, fully discrete diffusion design eliminates the need for separate AR and diffusion branches, enabling parallel sampling and 4–11× speedup over autoregressive baselines.

## Results

On GenEval (text-to-image), Muddit (512×512, 1B params) achieves an overall score of 0.61, surpassing prior discrete diffusion models Monetico (0.44) and Meissonic (0.54) while closely matching Stable Diffusion 3 (0.62). On image-to-text benchmarks, Muddit attains CIDEr 59.9 on MS-COCO, 68.2% on VQAv2, 1107.4 on MME, 57.5 on GQA, and 27.6 on MMMU. The scaled 1024×1024 variant further improves to CIDEr 60.1, VQAv2 70.2%, MME 1139.2, and MMMU 28.7, all with only 1B parameters and 10M training pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest unified discrete diffusion baseline UniDisc, Muddit substantially outperforms on MS-COCO CIDEr (59.9 vs. 46.8), confirming that the pretrained visual prior from Meissonic is critical for generation quality. Compared to the hybrid D-DiT (discrete diffusion for text + continuous diffusion for image), Muddit wins on CIDEr (59.9 vs. 56.2) and VQAv2 (68.2% vs. 60.1%) but trails slightly on MME (1107.4 vs. 1124.7) and GQA (57.5 vs. 59.2). Against Show-O (AR text + discrete diffusion image), Muddit achieves comparable VQAv2 (68.2% vs. 69.4%) and MME (1107.4 vs. 1097.2) while using fewer parameters (1B vs. 1.3B) and a fully unified discrete diffusion architecture. On GenEval for text-to-image, Muddit (0.61) beats all prior pure discrete diffusion models (Monetico 0.44, Meissonic 0.54, UniDisc 0.42) but remains below hybrid models Show-O (0.68) and D-DiT (0.65), as well as SD3 (0.62). Against much larger autoregressive VLMs (LLaVA-Next 13B, InternVL-2.0 8B), Muddit is competitive on VQAv2 and MME but lags on GQA and MMMU, reflecting the trade-off of a visual-prior backbone versus a language-prior backbone. The 1024×1024 scaled Muddit narrows these gaps, reaching VQAv2 70.2% and MME 1139.2 while still using only 1B parameters and 16M training pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

Muddit sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) discrete diffusion over token spaces (MaskGIT, absorbing-state CTMC formulations), (2) unified multimodal generation (Show-O, D-DiT, Transfusion, UniDisc), and (3) pretrained text-to-image backbones (Meissonic, FLUX's MM-DiT). Its key departure is a 'visual-first' initialization: rather than building a unified model from a language-model prior (as MMaDA and Lumina-DiMOO do with LLaDA) or training from scratch (as UniDisc does), Muddit starts from a high-resolution T2I backbone and attaches a lightweight text decoder, using purely discrete diffusion for both modalities. This contrasts with D-DiT, which mixes continuous diffusion for images with discrete diffusion for text, and with AR-based unified models that suffer from sequential decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 1B-parameter model trained on ~10M pairs can match or outperform 8B-parameter unified models trained on 80M pairs, suggesting that strong visual priors are a more data-efficient foundation for unified generation than language priors. The 4×–11× inference speedup over AR baselines (via parallel discrete diffusion) makes interactive multimodal generation more practical. However, limitations remain: token-level discrete representation may cap photorealistic image quality relative to continuous diffusion, and the model's text understanding lags behind modern LLMs on long-form reasoning. Open questions include whether the visual-first strategy generalizes to additional modalities (video, audio) and whether scaling the discrete diffusion backbone further closes the gap with continuous-diffusion image quality.
