## Problem

Existing mobile agent benchmarks suffer from two fundamental limitations: online evaluations produce unstable step rewards due to dynamic environment changes (OS/app updates, user preferences), while offline evaluations rely on single-path golden trajectories that ignore the inherently multi-solution nature of GUI tasks. Additionally, prior benchmarks evaluate agents in artificially clean environments (e.g., Google apps without ads or pop-ups) and assume users provide complete, unambiguous instructions, failing to capture the noisy and interactive conditions of real-world smartphone use.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides each agent with a mobile GUI screenshot (RGB image), a natural-language task instruction, the action history so far, and optionally an XML/accessibility tree describing the current page. The agent must output a single next action from a three-type space: click (bounding-box coordinates), scroll (direction), or text input. Evaluation is conducted in two modes—single-path (comparing each predicted action against a fixed golden trajectory) and multi-path (allowing the agent to navigate a pre-collected graph of UI states and accumulating step rewards at annotated key nodes)—yielding four metrics: Success Rate, Step Efficiency, Step Accuracy, and TYPE Accuracy. The full benchmark spans 12,856 instructions across 49 apps and 15 categories, with ~48k screenshots, and is further split into a noisy subset (100 instructions from ad-heavy apps plus AITZ-Noise with injected advertisements) and an ambiguous subset (100 instructions with 5+ preset Q&A pairs per trajectory).

## Method

SMAN-Bench is constructed on Mobile3M's graph-structured corpus (3.1M UI pages, 20M+ actions across 49 apps), where nodes are UI pages and edges are transition actions. The core annotation method, GIAS (Generating Instructions From Action Sequences), automatically pairs instructions with multiple trajectories via a six-step pipeline: multi-path sampling between fixed start/end nodes, VLM-based GUI captioning, action-intent inference between consecutive pages, slot extraction from GUI state changes, template filling with extracted slots, and similarity-based deduplication—requiring no human intervention except a final verification pass. For multi-path evaluation, discrete trajectories are merged into a unified graph using BM25 action-space/pixel-difference thresholds and button-value consistency, and agents accumulate step rewards when they reach annotated key nodes rather than following a single golden path. The noisy split is built by (1) manually annotating trajectories in ad-heavy third-party apps (static pop-ups, video ads, redirecting links) and (2) contaminating AITZ trajectories with randomly inserted advertisements. The ambiguous split is created by removing slot information from full instructions to produce underspecified queries, then assigning slot-specific Q&A pairs to corresponding GUI steps so agents must proactively ask clarification questions to recover the missing details.

## Results

On SMAN-Bench Common (simple/complex), the top-performing agents—Claude 4.5 Sonnet, Doubao-1.5-Thinking-pro, OpenCUA-32B, and UI-TARS-1.5-7B—achieve Success Rates of 39.0%/39.0% (simple/complex) and Step Accuracy of 76–78%. Noisy tasks are dramatically harder: the best agents (Claude 4.5 Sonnet, UI-TARS-1.5-7B) drop to only 15.0–15.5% SR, a ~60% relative decline. On the Ambiguous split, the best agents reach 41.5–43.0% SR, and the RL-based GUI-G1-3B (25.5% simple SR) matches the pretrained OS-Atlas-7B-pro (28.0%), highlighting the potential of online RL for mobile agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Specialized mobile agents substantially outperform general VLMs paired with agent frameworks: OpenCUA-32B (39.0% simple SR) and UI-TARS-1.5-7B (39.0%) surpass the best framework-based result (Qwen-VL-Max + MobileAgent-E at 32.5% simple SR, 25.5% complex SR) by 6–13 points. Among continuous pre-training agents, UI-TARS-1.5-7B and OpenCUA-32B lead, while smaller models like CogAgent-18B (13.0% SR) and Kimi-VL-A3B (12.0%) lag by over 25 points. The RL-based GUI-G1-3B (25.5% simple SR) nearly matches the pretrained OS-Atlas-7B-pro (28.0%), and UI-S1-7B (33.0%) approaches the 7B RL agents' ceiling. On noisy tasks, all agents suffer severe degradation—Claude 4.5 Sonnet drops from 39.0% to 15.5% SR—while general VLMs in Table 2 achieve near-zero SR (0–7%), confirming that noise robustness remains an open challenge. Reasoning models (Doubao-1.5-Thinking-pro, Claude 4.5 Sonnet) match or slightly exceed the best specialized agents on ambiguous tasks (41.5–43.0% SR), but integrating them into multi-agent frameworks yields no additional gains, suggesting inherent thinking patterns are equivalent to framework-level planning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mobile3M's graph-structured UI corpus (Wu et al., 2024a) as its data foundation and on AITZ (Zhang et al., 2024b) for the noisy contamination split, but departs from prior mobile agent benchmarks in three key ways: it introduces an offline multi-path evaluation that allows agents to explore within the graph rather than following a single golden trajectory, it adds a realistic noisy environment split (ads, pop-ups, redirects), and it constructs an ambiguous instruction split with preset Q&A interactions. The slot-based instruction generation method (GIAS) is positioned as a solution to the fundamental mismatch between single-path offline annotations and the inherently multi-solution nature of GUI tasks, drawing on the action-intent and slot-matching ideas from Netscape (Murty et al., 2024) for web agents. Compared to other graph-based benchmarks (ColorBench, WebGraphEval, CRAB, OmniBench), SMAN-Bench targets everyday mobile scenarios with a broader app coverage and uses action-space validity checking combined with page-content clustering for node merging, rather than VLM-based embedding similarity or reverse DAG decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SMAN-Bench addresses a practical gap: existing offline benchmarks reward agents for matching a single annotated path, which can overstate real-world capability, while online benchmarks suffer from environmental instability. The multi-path evaluation with slot-based key-node rewards offers a more stable and fair signal, and the noisy and ambiguous splits probe robustness and proactive interaction in ways prior benchmarks largely ignore. A notable open question raised by the results is whether the equivalence between agent frameworks and inherent thinking patterns (thinking models show no additional gain when wrapped in frameworks) reflects a fundamental ceiling or simply a limitation of current framework designs. The dramatic generalization failure of open-source agents on out-of-domain noisy data (Step.Acc dropping from ~45% to ~17–22%) highlights that current training pipelines do not adequately prepare agents for the ad-laden reality of consumer mobile apps.
