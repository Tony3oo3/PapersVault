## Problem

MeanFlow enables efficient 1-NFE generative modeling, but collapsing the transport to a single neural network evaluation causes significant quality degradation—producing fragmented, invalid molecular structures and poor image fidelity compared to multi-NFE variants. The paper asks whether 1-NFE MeanFlow can be improved for multimodal generation without increasing the number of function evaluations.

## Input / Output

For text-to-image, a text prompt is encoded via a frozen e5-base model plus a trainable MLP into a 4×32×32 latent prior, and the output is a 256×256 RGB image decoded by a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion VAE. For molecule generation, seven scalar properties (atom count, HOMO, LUMO, polarizability, gap, dipole moment, heat capacity) form the context vector mapped through an EGNN encoder, producing 3D atomic coordinates for up to 29 atoms. For time-series, an event-guidance vector concatenated with the first three trajectory states is embedded via an MLP. In all cases, the neural network is evaluated exactly once (1-NFE) and a Gaussian noise term is added in parallel, making the noise injection step computationally negligible.

## Method

RMFlow decomposes generation into a 1-NFE MeanFlow transport to an intermediate noisy state x₁ = x_data + σ·ε₁ (with σ < σ_min) followed by a single noise-injection step adding √(σ_min² − σ²)·ε₂, yielding a final sample in one forward pass. The key training innovation is a joint loss L_RMFlow = L_CMFM + λ₁·L_NLL + λ₂·E[‖φ_ω(c)‖²], where the NLL term exploits the Gaussian conditional distribution induced by the noise injection to maximize sample likelihood, providing a provable lower bound on expected log-likelihood and hence KL-divergence control (Theorem 4.1). For large-scale tasks (COCO, QM9), the model is first pretrained with L_CMFM and then fine-tuned with L_RMFlow using LoRA-style parameter-efficient fine-tuning, optionally augmented with a policy-gradient objective that uses molecule stability as a physical reward. The approach requires no distillation, curriculum learning, or auxiliary teacher models, and the entire refinement adds only a single noise addition to the baseline MeanFlow pipeline.

## Results

On QM9 molecule generation, 1-NFE RMFlow with contexts achieves 98.9% atomic stability and 93.2% molecular stability, approaching the 99.0%/93.9% of GeoBFN which requires >>1 NFE. On COCO text-to-image, RMFlow attains FID-30K of 18.91 with 1-NFE, substantially improving over MeanFlow's 27.31 and approaching distilled single-step methods like InstaFlow (13.10) and UFOGen (12.78). On dynamical system trajectory forecasting (Lorenz and FitzHugh-Nagumo), 1-NFE RMFlow reduces TV distance from 0.0469 (MeanFlow 1-NFE) to 0.0332, approaching 32-NFE MeanFlow (0.0351) and 128-NFE diffusion baselines (0.0306–0.0314).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct baseline MeanFlow (1-NFE), RMFlow wins decisively on every task: QM9 molecular stability improves from 84.3% to 93.2% (with contexts), COCO FID-30K drops from 27.31 to 18.91, and Lorenz TV distance falls from 0.0469 to 0.0332. On QM9, RMFlow with contexts (93.2% mol. stability) nearly matches GeoBFN (93.9%) and E-DM+RLPF (93.4%), both of which require >>1 NFE, while RMFlow achieves this in a single network evaluation. On COCO, RMFlow (18.91 FID) still trails multi-step methods like PixArt (7.32) and Show-o (9.24) but outperforms other 1-NFE teacher-free or distilled methods such as Stable Diffusion+Distill (34.6) and Rectified Flow+Distill (20.0), and is competitive with StyleGAN-T (13.90) and UFOGen (12.78) which rely on GAN discriminators or pretrained teachers. On time-series forecasting, 1-NFE RMFlow (TV 0.0332 on Lorenz) surpasses 1-NFE MeanFlow (0.0469) and approaches 32-NFE MeanFlow (0.0351) and 128-NFE diffusion/FM baselines (0.0306–0.0348), demonstrating that the noise-injection refinement recovers most of the quality lost by collapsing to a single step.

## Relation to Prior Work

RMFlow is a direct refinement of MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2025), which itself extends the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023) by learning an averaged velocity field for 1-NFE generation. The key departure is the addition of a tailored noise-injection step after the single transport step, drawing on VAE-style likelihood maximization (Kingma & Welling, 2013) to complement the Wasserstein-distance control already provided by the MeanFlow loss (Boffi et al., 2025). The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to improve MeanFlows via noise-injection refinement, distinguishing from Diff2Flow (which distills from pretrained diffusion models) and generator matching (which couples diffusion and flow under Markov processes). The theoretical grounding combines the Wasserstein bound from Boffi et al. with a KL-divergance bound derived from the ELBO, unifying two previously separate objectives into a single training loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: 1-NFE generation eliminates the dominant computational bottleneck of flow/diffusion-based models, and RMFlow shows that a single noise-injection step closes most of the quality gap between 1-NFE and 32-NFE MeanFlow across diverse tasks (molecules, images, time series) while remaining teacher-free and discriminator-free. Key limitations include the fixed noise parameter √(σ²_min − σ²), the restriction to a single transport step, and training on modest hardware (RTX 3090/4090), leaving open whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or more complex modalities. The authors identify extending noise injection to multi-step transport and making the noise schedule adaptive as natural next steps, and the policy-gradient fine-tuning with physical feedback (RLPF) suggests a path toward domain-specific reward shaping.
