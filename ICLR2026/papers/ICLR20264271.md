## Problem

Despite extensive empirical work on activation functions for implicit neural representations, the theoretical reason why certain activations outperform others remains poorly understood. The paper identifies a structural limitation: odd and even symmetric activation functions (e.g., SIREN, ReLU, Gaussian) provably lose spectral information at post-activation due to alternating zero Chebyshev coefficients, causing attenuated frequency support and contributing to spectral bias.

## Input / Output

The INR MLP receives 2D spatial coordinates (x, y) for image tasks or 3D coordinates (x, y, z) for occupancy/NeRF tasks, and outputs the corresponding signal value (RGB pixel, occupancy label, or density+color). Intermediate layers produce complex-valued activations that are normalized to the unit circle to preserve phase while stabilizing training; only the real part is extracted at the final layer. A separate prior embedder (ResNet-34 for 2D images, ResNet3D-18 for 3D voxels) consumes the full input signal to predict per-layer activation parameters (bandwidth T and frequency shift ζ), which are bounded via sigmoid projection into pre-specified ranges (T ∈ [0,10], ζ ∈ [0,3]).

## Method

The paper proves via Chebyshev polynomial approximation that even-symmetric activations have zero odd-order coefficients and odd-symmetric activations have zero even-order coefficients, meaning half the harmonic content is structurally absent from the post-activation spectrum. To eliminate this attenuation, the authors multiply the activation by a complex exponential e^(jζx), which shifts the spectrum so that both odd and even Chebyshev coefficients become non-zero simultaneously. The resulting COSMO-RC activation is a Raised Cosine (chosen for its slowest Chebyshev coefficient decay among tested activations) modulated by this complex sinusoid, with learnable bandwidth T and frequency shift ζ per layer. A prior knowledge embedder extracts a compact latent from the input signal and maps it to (T, ζ) pairs via a small MLP with sigmoid-bounded outputs, removing the need for manual hyperparameter search. The full network uses 5 layers of 256 neurons (8 layers for NeRF) with complex-valued intermediate activations normalized to the unit circle, trained end-to-end with Adam under a standard MSE loss.

## Results

On the Kodak image reconstruction task, COSMO-RC achieves an average PSNR of 41.24 dB, surpassing the nearest competitor INCODE (35.57 dB) by +5.67 dB. For 6× super-resolution, COSMO-RC reaches 27.66 dB PSNR and 0.93 SSIM, outperforming the best baseline FINER (27.02 dB / 0.80 SSIM) by +0.64 dB. On the NeRF Lego benchmark, COSMO-RC attains 29.50 dB PSNR, exceeding INCODE (26.05 dB) by +3.45 dB. In image denoising, COSMO-RC gains +0.46 dB PSNR over INCODE while better preserving color and structural integrity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSMO-RC consistently outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated task. Against INCODE (the nearest competitor in most settings), COSMO-RC gains +5.67 dB on Kodak reconstruction, +0.46 dB on denoising, +1.03 dB on 6× super-resolution, and +3.45 dB on NeRF view synthesis. FINER is the strongest baseline on super-resolution (32.94 dB at 2×, 27.02 dB at 6×) but is still surpassed by COSMO-RC by 1.09 dB and 0.64 dB respectively. SIREN and WIRE trail further behind, with WIRE showing the weakest overall performance (e.g., 25.35 dB at 6× SR, 25.26 dB on NeRF). ReLU+PE and Gauss (Gaussian activation) serve as lower-bound references, confirming that periodic and wavelet-based activations are substantially superior to piecewise-linear or non-periodic alternatives. The only notable trade-off is computational: COSMO-RC's complex-sinusoidal activation and prior embedder increase per-iteration training time to 3.5 s versus 0.43 s for INCODE, reducing throughput from 267 to 33 GFLOPs/s, though the accuracy gains (up to +8.9 dB) justify the cost for quality-critical applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the harmonic distortion analysis framework of Mehmeti-Göpel et al. (2021) and Yüce et al. (2022) by introducing Chebyshev polynomial approximation to rigorously characterize how activation symmetry causes spectral attenuation in INRs. It departs from the ad-hoc activation design paradigm (SIREN, WIRE, FINER) by identifying a principled structural limitation—zero Chebyshev coefficients at alternating orders for odd/even symmetric functions—and proposing complex sinusoidal modulation as a general fix. The Raised Cosine base activation is drawn from the MIRE dictionary (Jayasundara et al., 2025), and the prior-knowledge embedder for parameter regularization is adapted from INCODE (Kazerouni et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proof that symmetric activations inherently lose spectral information at alternating frequency orders provides a unifying explanation for why many popular INR activations underperform, and the complex modulation remedy is applicable beyond the specific COSMO-RC instantiation. However, the practical cost is significant: the complex-valued computation and prior embedder reduce throughput from 267 to 33 GFLOPs/s, raising open questions about whether the accuracy gains justify the overhead at scale. Experimental scope is also somewhat narrow (24 Kodak images, a single DIV2K image for super-resolution, one Celtic image for inpainting, and NeRF evaluated only on LEGO), leaving open whether the spectral-completeness advantage persists on larger, more diverse benchmarks or in other modalities such as audio and video.
