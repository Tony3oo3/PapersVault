## Problem

Prior approaches to simulating human online shopping behavior rely on supervised fine-tuning with LLM-generated rationales, which inherently caps performance at the reasoning ability of the rationale-generating model. No prior work has applied reinforcement learning to this simulation-oriented task, where ground-truth rationales are unavailable and actions vary widely in difficulty, making reward design non-trivial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260299/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a simplified HTML rendering of the current web page (preserving layout and interactable element names while stripping scripts and styles) concatenated with the full session history of past observations and actions. It outputs a single JSON object containing a first-person rationale string and a structured action object whose type is one of 'type_and_submit' (with name and text fields), 'click' (with a name field), or 'terminate' (no sub-fields). The task is sequential: at each step the model must jointly produce the rationale and the next action conditioned on the entire preceding context.

## Method

Shop-R1 decomposes each prediction step into rationale generation and action prediction, each with a distinct reward signal, and trains via GRPO after an SFT cold-start on Claude 3.5 Sonnet-generated rationales. For rationale generation, a self-certainty reward computed as the average KL divergence between the model's token-level output distribution and a uniform vocabulary distribution provides supervision-free confidence feedback. For action prediction, a hierarchical reward scheme grants partial credit at three levels—action type (0.3), sub-action attribute presence, and ROUGE-L text similarity—while a difficulty-aware reward scaling (DARS) factor (default 1000) amplifies rewards for the harder long-text sub-actions to make trivial 'terminate' spamming uneconomical. A binary format reward (0.5) gates all other rewards on valid JSON output, and a KL penalty to a reference policy regularizes the optimization. The full objective maximizes the combined action reward plus α-weighted self-certainty minus β-weighted KL divergence, with α=0.005 and β=0.001.

## Results

On the SHOP-CART dataset with Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct, Shop-R1 achieves 27.72% exact action accuracy, 36.40% action type accuracy, and 31.28% action type F1, representing a 65% relative improvement over the SFT baseline (16.76% exact match). The method also generalizes across model scales: Qwen-2.5-1.5B-Instruct reaches 24.11% exact accuracy and Qwen-2.5-0.5B-Instruct reaches 27.72% (though the latter relies heavily on the 'terminate' action at 97.07% exact). On the OPeRA dataset, SFT+RL achieves 38.44% exact action accuracy versus 20.23% for SFT alone, confirming the RL pipeline's effectiveness on a second benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Shop-R1 decisively outperforms all four baselines on the primary SHOP-CART benchmark. Against zero-shot prompting (0.32% exact), the improvement is over 85×, confirming that generic instruction tuning is insufficient for long-horizon web behavior simulation. Sparse binary RL from scratch (1.01%) also fails to provide meaningful guidance, as the reward signal is too sparse to shape long-text action arguments. The SFT baseline (16.76%) is the strongest competitor, and Shop-R1 improves upon it by 10.96 absolute points (65% relative), with the gap driven primarily by better reproduction of fine-grained sub-action details (button names, search queries). Notably, SFT + RL with binary reward (16.55%) actually slightly underperforms SFT alone on exact match, indicating that the binary objective cannot push the model beyond what teacher forcing already achieves and even pulls it backwards on long-text fidelity. On the OPeRA dataset, the SFT+RL variant reaches 38.44% exact accuracy versus 20.23% for SFT, further validating the RL training pipeline's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-based human behavior simulation in web environments (Lu et al. 2025a; Wang et al. 2025c), where prior work relied on SFT with LLM-generated rationales whose quality is bounded by the generating model. It departs from that paradigm by introducing RL as the training signal, adopting the SFT-cold-start-then-RL pipeline from DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al. 2025) and the GRPO optimizer (Shao et al. 2024), while replacing the sparse binary reward with a hierarchical, difficulty-scaled reward structure. The self-certainty rationale reward draws on KL-divergence-based confidence scoring (Kang et al. 2025; Zhao et al. 2025), repurposing a best-of-N selection metric as a training signal. The key conceptual departure is framing behavior simulation (distributional, non-deterministic) rather than task completion as the RL objective, which necessitates the partial-credit and anti-hacking reward design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that structured RL rewards can substantially outperform SFT for simulation-oriented behavior modeling (65% relative gain in exact-match accuracy), and that naive binary RL can actually degrade fine-grained performance below SFT. The finding that the 0.5B model matches the 3B model's headline accuracy purely by over-predicting 'terminate' (97% exact) is a cautionary illustration of how difficulty-aware scaling interacts with model capacity. Open questions include whether the reward design generalizes beyond shopping to other web-GUI tasks, whether it scales to larger backbones (≥7B), and whether the self-certainty signal remains informative as model confidence calibrates with scale. The framework is limited to Qwen-2.5 models up to 3B and a single e-commerce domain, so broader validation is needed before the reward architecture can be considered a general recipe.
