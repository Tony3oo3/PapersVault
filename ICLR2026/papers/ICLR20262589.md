## Problem

Diffusion language models (DLMs) require a pre-specified, fixed-length masked sequence for generation, which severely degrades code infilling performance when the mask size mismatches the ideal completion length (an average 38% drop on HumanEval-Infilling). This fixed-canvas constraint blocks practical deployment of DLMs for tasks where output length varies significantly across examples, such as code infilling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262589/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a code prefix, a span of [mask] tokens (initial length configurable, tested from 4 to 64), and a code suffix as context. During denoising, the masked span dynamically changes length: [expand] predictions split one position into two [mask] tokens, while [delete] predictions remove a position entirely. Generation terminates when all [mask] positions are resolved, with a hard cap of L_max=128 tokens to prevent unbounded growth. Inference uses temperature 0.2 and top_p 0.9, with a confidence-based unmasking budget n per diffusion step.

## Method

DREAMON introduces two sentinel tokens, [expand] and [delete], into the diffusion vocabulary to enable native length control without architectural changes. Training augments the original sequence x_0 into a variable-length z_0 by merging random consecutive mask-index spans into [expand] tokens (gated by a merge-rate scheduler) and inserting [delete] tokens, then applies standard masked diffusion over z_0 with a per-token loss weight (Eq. 3) that down-scales [delete] contributions to prevent overfitting to deletion signals. At inference, the model predicts all masked positions each step, immediately expands [expand] into two [mask] tokens, removes [delete] positions, and uses a 'deletion broadcasting' heuristic that eliminates all trailing [mask] tokens after a predicted [delete], reducing average inference steps from 122.8 to 52.4 at mask length 64. The approach is model-agnostic, requiring only fine-tuning on ~110K instruction-solution pairs (≈0.15% of base-model pretraining compute) and no modifications to the underlying transformer architecture.

## Results

On HumanEval-Infilling (Pass@1), DREAMON applied to DiffuCoder-7B achieves 92.2% on single-line and 63.1% on multi-line infilling, while DreamCoder-7B + DREAMON reaches 92.1% and 63.8% respectively. On SantaCoder-FIM (exact match), the best DREAMON variant (DreamCoder-7B) scores 79.0%. Across all three diffusion base models, DREAMON delivers an average absolute improvement of 26.4% over the fixed-length baselines, and approaches oracle-length performance (e.g., 91.6% vs. 93.3% oracle on single-line for DreamCoder-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest autoregressive baseline Qwen2.5-Coder-7B (92.6 / 58.7 / 79.8), DREAMON-equipped diffusion models match or exceed it: DiffuCoder-7B + DREAMON scores 92.2 on single-line (−0.4) but surpasses on multi-line (63.1 vs. 58.7), and DreamCoder-7B + DREAMON scores 92.1 on single-line (−0.5) while clearly winning multi-line (63.8 vs. 58.7) and SantaCoder-FIM (79.0 vs. 79.8 is a near-tie). Against the other AR baselines, DREAMON variants dominate: Seed-Coder-8B (89.7 / 59.3 / 77.2) is outperformed on all three metrics by both DiffuCoder and DreamCoder DREAMON variants, and DeepSeek-Coder-6.7B (73.0 / 45.7 / 76.3) is surpassed by a wide margin. Among diffusion baselines, DREAMON yields the largest gains over the weakest models: LLaDA-8B (48.3 / 21.1 / 35.1) and Dream-7B (48.2 / 21.9 / 60.3) see absolute improvements of +40.4 and +31.4 on single-line and multi-line respectively, confirming the method's model-agnostic nature. The paper also shows that without DREAMON, diffusion models suffer a 38% average performance drop when the predefined mask length mismatches the ground-truth length, whereas DREAMON maintains near-oracle performance across all initial mask lengths (4–64).

## Relation to Prior Work

DREAMON extends the masked diffusion language model lineage (Dream-7B, DiffuCoder-7B, Dream-Coder-7B) by introducing two sentinel tokens—[expand] and [delete]—that enable variable-length generation without architectural changes. It departs from concurrent variable-length diffusion approaches (Edit Flows, DDOT, FlexMDAL, DAEDAL) by avoiding edit-operation spaces, joint position-token denoising, or separate length predictors, instead relying on a simple data-augmentation strategy (span merging) that augments the standard masked diffusion training objective. The inference procedure builds on the entropy-based selective unmasking of Ye et al. (2025) but replaces fixed masking schedulers with a direct unmasking budget to accommodate dynamic sequence lengths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the fixed-length mask constraint with only 0.15% of pretraining compute and no architectural modifications, DREAMON makes diffusion language models practically deployable for variable-length tasks like code infilling, where solution lengths vary widely. The paper's evaluation is limited to code infilling and ROCStories, leaving open whether the mechanism generalizes to open-ended generation, long-form writing, or multimodal settings. The reliance on heuristic schedulers (static/dynamic inverse merge rates) and a single binary expansion state (one [expand] → two [mask]) suggests a more principled, multi-factor length-prediction formulation remains an important direction.
