## Problem

Existing Salient Object Ranking (SOR) methods rely exclusively on bottom-up visual cues (shape, texture, pose, spatial relations) to predict human attention shifts, ignoring the top-down cognitive process whereby scene-level understanding (i.e., forming a mental 'story') actively drives where and in what order humans look. This paper identifies and models the cyclical perception–viewing interaction: attention shift shapes scene comprehension, which in turn redirects attention, a feedback loop absent from prior SOR approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 1024×1024 and produces three outputs: (1) per-instance saliency masks via a Mask2Former-style segmentation head, (2) a saliency ranking (viewing order) over detected salient objects (those with confidence > 0.7), and (3) a natural-language image caption. The caption is generated autoregressively by a BLIP text decoder initialized from MS-COCO captions (one of five per image selected at random during training). The cyclic interaction runs for K=5 iterations at inference, adding only ~14 ms over a single-pass forward.

## Method

The architecture is a dual-branch framework built on a Swin-L backbone and Mask2Former query decoder, augmented with two novel modules that interact cyclically for K=5 steps. The Story Prediction (SP) module uses a Saliency-Infused Textual Augmentation (SITA) mechanism: it computes a saliency-weighted visual context vector from object queries and their scores, projects it into the text feature space, and applies a learned gating network (GELU + sigmoid) to adaptively modulate the text decoder's hidden states, mimicking neural gain modulation. The Guided Ranking (GR) module uses Cross-Modal Query Contextualization (CMQC): it projects text features into the query dimension and applies multi-head cross-attention with residual updates to iteratively refine object queries, analogous to predictive coding error minimization. Each iteration, SP injects the latest saliency scores into text features while GR incorporates the latest text features into object queries, creating a closed perception–viewing loop. Training is end-to-end and multi-task, combining Mask2Former segmentation/classification losses, a Spearman-based ranking loss, and a cross-entropy captioning loss.

## Results

On the ASSR benchmark with a Swin-L backbone, the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art SA-SOR of 0.787, SOR of 0.869, and MAE of 5.28, outperforming the previous best (QAGNet) by 1.95% on SA-SOR while reducing MAE by 8.65%. On the IRSR benchmark, it achieves SA-SOR 0.624, SOR 0.822, and MAE 6.89, again leading on SA-SOR and SOR. The method also demonstrates strong efficiency: the cyclical interaction (K=5) adds only ~14 ms over K=1 at 1024×1024 resolution, and with a Swin-B backbone reaches 20.1 FPS at 512×512.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best SA-SOR on both benchmarks: 0.787 on ASSR (beating QAGNet's 0.771 by 1.95% and DSGNN's 0.761 by 2.6%) and 0.624 on IRSR (beating QAGNet's 0.616 by 0.8% and DSGNN's 0.602 by 2.2%). It also attains the best SOR scores (0.869 on ASSR, 0.822 on IRSR) and the lowest MAE on ASSR (5.28 vs. QAGNet's 5.78 and DSGNN's 5.41). On IRSR MAE, QAGNet (6.71) slightly edges out the proposed method (6.89), but the gap is small and the SA-SOR/SOR advantages are consistent. Compared to earlier SOR methods relying on bottom-up cues (SeqRank, PoseSOR, OCOR, PSR), the cyclical perception-viewing framework yields substantially larger gains, particularly on SA-SOR where detection and ranking accuracy are jointly penalized. The paper also shows that the advantage grows with semantic density (Pearson r = 0.714 between caption word count per object and SA-SOR), confirming that the top-down caption-guided mechanism is most beneficial in semantically rich scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Mask2Former query-decoder architecture for visual feature extraction and segmentation, but departs fundamentally from all prior SOR methods (ASRNet, PPA, IRSR, OCOR, SeqRank, QAGNet, DSGNN, PoseSOR) that rely exclusively on bottom-up visual cues such as object attributes, spatial relations, scene graphs, or human poses. The key conceptual departure is introducing an explicit top-down cognitive pathway by formulating scene understanding as image caption generation and establishing a cyclical interaction between perception (caption refinement via SITA) and viewing (ranking refinement via CMQC), grounded in predictive coding and neural gain modulation theories from cognitive science. The use of a pre-trained BLIP text decoder as the initial caption generator and the iterative cross-modal refinement loop represent a novel integration of vision-language modeling into the SOR pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that incorporating top-down scene understanding (via captioning) into SOR yields consistent improvements over purely bottom-up methods, with the strongest gains on semantically rich scenes (Pearson r=0.714 between semantic density and SA-SOR). This suggests a broader principle: perception tasks may benefit from explicit language-grounded reasoning rather than relying solely on low-level visual features. However, the acknowledged limitation—failure when saliency is driven by low-level cues like color or shape—highlights an open question about how to balance top-down semantic guidance with bottom-up visual salience. The bidirectional utility shown in the SOR-guided captioning experiment (Appendix D.3) also opens the possibility that ranking and captioning can mutually enhance each other in multimodal systems.
