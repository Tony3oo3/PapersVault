## Problem

Current multimodal dialogue generation methods produce realistic outputs in isolated modalities (speech, vision) but neglect the systematic modeling of cross-modal interactional style, resulting in limited expressiveness and controllability. This gap is compounded by the absence of large-scale native multimodal dialogue data with interaction-level annotations, scalable annotation methods for continuous (rather than categorical) interaction semantics, and benchmarks that evaluate dialogue-level cross-modal style consistency rather than modality-isolated fidelity.

## Input / Output

The framework defines three conditional generation tasks: (1) a text transcript plus an explicit style condition (either a structured Affective Triplet of Relationship × Interaction Mode × Emotional Tone, or a free-form per-speaker description) produces a continuous multi-speaker dialogue audio stream modeled end-to-end rather than as turn-by-turn concatenation; (2) a temporally ordered keyframe sequence plus aligned transcript produces dialogue audio where the style is implicitly inferred from visual cues via a latent representation ψ(V_key); (3) dialogue audio plus transcript produces a temporally synchronized, affectively consistent dialogue video. The MM-DIA dataset provides 360+ hours of movie/TV-sourced clips with hierarchical annotations at both sentence-level (non-verbal sounds, speaker identity, visibility) and dialogue-level (affective triplet, freestyle description, emotional intensity/volatility scores).

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a multimodal data curation pipeline that extracts synchronized dialogues from in-the-wild movies and TV series, combining multi-sourced subtitle calibration with ASR, a VLM-based buffer mechanism with a dynamic keyframe pool for robust dialogue boundary detection across complex cinematic transitions, and Gemini-2.5-pro for speaker attribution and fine-grained expressiveness annotation. Two complementary annotation paradigms are introduced: a structured Affective Triplet (Relationship, Interaction Mode, Emotional Tone) for scenario-level control, and per-speaker freestyle descriptions capturing turn-level style trajectories, supplemented by quantitative emotional intensity and volatility scores. For explicit style-controllable speech synthesis (Task 1), the method performs supervised fine-tuning of Higgs-Audio-V2 and Dia-1.6B backbones on MM-DIA, with a lightweight adapter projecting style embeddings into the decoder for Dia-1.6B. For implicit cross-modal control (Tasks 2 and 3), the paper evaluates cascaded VLM+speech-synthesis pipelines and existing SI2V/T2V video generators on MM-DIA-BENCH, revealing that current systems produce fluent but stylistically incoherent cross-modal outputs, with significant drops in timbre similarity and instruction-following scores when style must be inferred rather than explicitly prompted.

## Results

On the primary Task 1 (Style-controllable Dialogue Speech Synthesis, Test set), fine-tuning Higgs-Audio-V2 on MM-DIA reduces WER from 31.25 to 4.45 and cp-WER from 104.87 to 33.77, while improving Human-MOS Quality from 3.58 to 4.44 and Instruction Following from 3.11 to 4.13. On Task 2 (Vision-conditioned Dialogue Speech), the cascaded GPT+Higgs pipeline achieves WER 5.79 and cp-WER 14.58, substantially outperforming the end-to-end HarmoniVox (WER 21.22, cp-WER 30.98). On Task 3 (Speech-driven Dialogue Video), no system approaches ground-truth quality: the best SI2V model (Sonic) achieves FVD 117.10 and LSE-C 4.99 vs. GT LSE-C 6.28, while T2V models (Wan-2.2 T2V, HunyuanVideo) score only 53.7% and 48.0% on Relationship label accuracy respectively, far below the 100% GT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For Task 1, Higgs-Audio-V2-SFT (fine-tuned on MM-DIA) decisively outperforms both Dia-Base (WER 19.99, cp-WER 51.71) and its own base model Higgs-Audio-V2-Base (WER 31.25, cp-WER 104.87), confirming that MM-DIA training is the key driver of controllability gains. Dia-SFT actually degrades relative to Dia-Base on WER (29.07 vs. 19.99) and cp-WER (57.81 vs. 51.71), suggesting the adapter-based conditioning is less effective for that architecture. For Task 2, the cascaded GPT+Higgs (WER 5.79, cp-WER 14.58, Mean Label Acc. 52.17%) and cascaded Gemini+Higgs (WER 5.78, cp-WER 16.27, Mean Acc. 42.33%) both substantially outperform the end-to-end HarmoniVox (WER 21.22, cp-WER 30.98, Mean Acc. 40.47%), though all methods show degraded Gemini-as-Judge Timbre Similarity and Instruction Following scores compared to the explicit-prompt setting, revealing a cross-modal style consistency gap. For Task 3, among SI2V baselines, Sonic achieves the best FVD (117.10) and competitive LSE-C (4.99), while MultiTalk (FVD 124.54, LSE-C 5.31) and FLOAT (FVD 572.19) trail; among T2V baselines, Wan-2.2 T2V (FVD 300.09, Rela. Acc. 53.66%) marginally beats HunyuanVideo (FVD 335.59, Rela. Acc. 47.97%), but both fall far short of ground-truth label accuracy (100%) and Gemini-as-Judge scores (e.g., GT Spontaneity 4.89 vs. best T2V 3.11), confirming that no current system adequately solves dialogue video generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of spoken dialogue generation (ZSDG, CosyVoice, Higgs-Audio, Dia) and cinematic video generation (talking-head systems like Sonic/MultiTalk, T2V models like Wan/HunyuanVideo), arguing that both lines of work treat modalities in isolation and neglect cross-modal interactional style. It departs from prior multimodal dialogue datasets (MELD, MC-EIU) that rely on human-annotated categorical emotion tags by introducing an automated VLM-based pipeline (Gemini-2.5-pro) that produces continuous, fine-grained style annotations via an Affective Triplet schema and free-form descriptions. The generation experiments build directly on Higgs-Audio-V2 and Dia-1.6B as pretrained backbones, adding only a lightweight adapter for style conditioning, so the contribution is primarily in data curation, task formulation, and benchmarking rather than in a new generative architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MM-DIA (360+ hours, 54.7K dialogues) is the first large-scale dataset explicitly centered on cross-modal dialogue expressiveness, and MM-DIA-BENCH provides the first standardized testbed for evaluating audio-visual style consistency in dialogue generation—addressing a gap the authors identify in existing benchmarks that only measure local semantic coherence or modality-isolated fidelity. The key open question the paper surfaces is that no current system (SI2V or T2V) can adequately generate dialogue video with faithful cross-modal alignment: T2V models achieve only ~14–19% accuracy on Interaction Mode, and even the best SI2V pipelines struggle with long-range staging and lip-sync under natural shot complexity. Future work the authors outline—end-to-end dialogue-to-video modeling, multi-granularity alignment learning, and cross-modal semantic discriminators—remains entirely unsolved, making this a foundational resource for the next generation of controllable multimodal dialogue systems.
