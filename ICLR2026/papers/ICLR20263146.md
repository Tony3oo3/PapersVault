## Problem

Existing time series anomaly detection methods rely on unimodal numerical data, overlooking complementary textual context. When text is incorporated, prior approaches either generate limited endogenous descriptions from the series itself or naively fuse scattered exogenous text without ensuring semantic alignment, and they treat all textual content as equally useful, allowing redundancy to dilute anomaly signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263146/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×D} (instance-normalized, channel-independent, then split into N patches of size p with stride l), a shared exogenous text sequence C of length S (e.g., news or reports), and per-patch endogenous text O generated by an LLM using unified prompt templates (mean, extrema, trend). It outputs a per-timestamp anomaly score computed as the mean squared error between the original series and its cross-modal reconstruction, along with the reconstructed series X̂ ∈ R^{T×D}.

## Method

MindTS introduces two core mechanisms. First, fine-grained time-text semantic alignment: endogenous text (time-specific, per-patch) serves as the query in a cross-attention layer with exogenous text (shared background) as key and value, producing fused text representations Z_text that are then aligned with time patch embeddings H_time via a symmetric InfoNCE-style contrastive loss. Second, content condenser reconstruction: an Information Bottleneck-inspired module computes per-patch Bernoulli retention probabilities via an MLP, samples a binary mask F (with straight-through estimator for gradients), and produces condensed text Z_con = Z_text ⊙ F; a KL-divergence regularizer against a target Bernoulli(μ) controls compression level, and a smoothness term penalizes abrupt mask changes between adjacent patches. The condensed text is then used in a cross-attention decoder to reconstruct a randomly masked version of the time series, forcing the text to carry genuine temporal information and strengthening cross-modal dependency. The total loss jointly optimizes the contrastive alignment, condenser (KL + smoothness), and reconstruction (Frobenius-norm MSE) objectives.

## Results

MindTS achieves state-of-the-art performance on all six real-world multimodal datasets (Weather, Energy, Environment, KR, EWJ, MDT) across all three primary metrics: Affiliated-F1 (Aff-F), Volume Under the Precision-Recall Surface (V-PR), and Volume Under the ROC Surface (V-ROC). Headline numbers include Aff-F of 90.28 on KR, 89.19 on MDT, and 85.29 on Environment; V-PR of 65.44 on MDT and 57.48 on Weather; and V-ROC of 93.78 on Environment and 89.86 on KR. The largest margins over the strongest unimodal baselines appear on MDT (Aff-F +8.4 over G4TS/Modern) and Energy (V-ROC +9.4 over Modern).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MindTS outperforms all 19 baselines on every dataset–metric combination (18/18). The closest competitors are ModernTCN and TimesNet among deep-learning methods, and Timer/UniTS among pre-trained models: on Weather, MindTS leads Modern by 1.6 Aff-F and TsNet by 0.7 V-ROC; on Environment, the gap over Timer is 1.1 Aff-F and 1.7 V-ROC; on KR, MindTS edges Timer by 0.7 Aff-F and Modern by 1.0 V-ROC. LLM-based baselines (LMixer, UTime, G4TS) show particularly large V-PR gaps (e.g., LMixer scores only 15.13 V-PR on KR vs. MindTS's 53.15), indicating that simply feeding text prompts into a frozen LLM fails to capture cross-modal anomaly signals. Non-learning methods (PCA, IForest, LODA, HBOS) trail by 20–40 points on Aff-F across all datasets. When baselines are extended to multimodal form via the MM-TSFLib framework (Table 2), MindTS still wins on all 18 comparisons, with the largest multimodal gap on MDT V-PR (65.44 vs. 52.65 for G4TS*). Ablation results confirm that both the semantic alignment module and the content condenser contribute meaningfully: removing either causes 1–4 point drops in Aff-F.

## Relation to Prior Work

MindTS synthesizes ideas from three distinct research threads: (1) the patching-and-transformer paradigm for time series encoding (PatchTST, TimesNet), (2) LLM-based endogenous text generation for time series (Time-LLM, LLM-Mixer, GPT4TS), and (3) exogenous text incorporation via frameworks like Time-MMD/MM-TSFLib. It departs from prior multimodal time series work by replacing simple linear interpolation or hard temporal alignment with a contrastive alignment loss and a cross-view fusion mechanism that jointly exploits endogenous and exogenous text. The content condenser is a novel application of Information Bottleneck theory and Bernoulli masking (borrowed from VAE-style discrete latent spaces) to the specific problem of filtering redundant text before cross-modal reconstruction, rather than the random masking or paraphrasing strategies used in NLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MindTS provides the first systematic treatment of both semantic alignment and redundancy filtering in multimodal time series anomaly detection, addressing two failure modes that prior methods (e.g., MM-TSFLib's linear interpolation) leave unaddressed. The content condenser's IB-based approach offers a principled, tunable mechanism for controlling how much text information flows into reconstruction, which could generalize to other multimodal time series tasks. Limitations include reliance on LLM-generated endogenous text (incurring inference cost), restriction to time+text modalities, and dependence on the quality and availability of exogenous text. Open questions include whether the alignment-and-condensation pipeline extends to image/video modalities, whether the approach scales to streaming or real-time settings, and whether the contrastive alignment loss remains effective when text and time series are only loosely temporally aligned.
