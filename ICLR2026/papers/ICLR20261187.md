## Problem

Existing video token compression methods for MLLMs rely on similarity or importance to prune redundant tokens but share two blind spots: they model spatial and temporal redundancy in isolation rather than jointly, and they entirely neglect temporal *difference* as a signal for preserving key events and turning points that drive video narrative. This leads to over-smoothing of dynamic content and suboptimal compression under aggressive token budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261187/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the full sequence of visual tokens produced by a pre-trained vision encoder (e.g., 64 frames × hundreds of tokens per frame) together with a text query, and outputs a compressed token subset at a user-specified retention ratio r (30% or 50% in experiments) that is fed to the LLM for generation. The compression is entirely training-free and plug-and-play, requiring only two hyperparameters (similarity threshold τ_sim and difference threshold τ_diff) and incurring O(Nd) overhead relative to the LLM's O(N²d) self-attention cost.

## Method

ST-SimDiff constructs a sparse spatio-temporal graph over all visual tokens, where edges connect spatially adjacent tokens within a frame and temporally adjacent tokens at the same spatial position across consecutive frames, weighted by cosine similarity. A parallel dual-selection strategy then operates on this graph: (1) Similarity-based Representative Token Selection thresholds the graph and applies connected-component community detection to group semantically redundant tokens, retaining only the highest-centrality tokens from each cluster; (2) Difference-based Event Token Selection scans temporal edges for sharp similarity drops (below a dynamic percentile threshold) and retains the subsequent token as a critical event marker. The two token sets are unioned, and a final attention-score-based pruning step trims the candidate set to exactly meet the target token count, ensuring the method adapts to any computational budget while preserving both stable background content and key dynamic transitions.

## Results

On LLaVA-Video-7B with 64 input frames, ST-SimDiff at a 30% token retention ratio achieves 63.2% on VideoMME (Overall), 57.5% on LongVideoBench (Overall), and 56.0% on EgoSchema, outperforming all baselines. At 50% retention, it reaches 63.8% VideoMME, 57.9% LongVideoBench, and 57.3% EgoSchema—matching or exceeding the full-token upper bound (63.3%, 58.2%, 57.3%) on VideoMME and EgoSchema. The method also reduces peak GPU memory by 31.7% and inference time by up to 30.2% compared to the uncompressed baseline at 128 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-SimDiff consistently outperforms all five baselines across both token retention ratios and both base models (LLaVA-Video-7B and NVILA-8B). The strongest competitor is FrameFusion, which combines similarity-based merging with importance-based pruning; ST-SimDiff surpasses it by 1.9 points on VideoMME Overall and 1.5 points on EgoSchema at r=30%, and by 1.2 and 1.5 points respectively at r=50%. Importance-only methods (FastV, FasterVLM) trail by 3–5 points on VideoMME, reflecting their inability to exploit spatio-temporal redundancy. VisionZip, which operates at the vision encoder level, shows the largest gap (up to 4.9 points on VideoMME at r=30%), suggesting that encoder-level selection misses cross-frame temporal structure. Notably, at r=50% ST-SimDiff matches or exceeds the full-token upper bound on VideoMME (63.8 vs. 63.3) and EgoSchema (57.3 vs. 57.3), indicating that the dual similarity-plus-difference strategy can actually improve understanding by removing noise while preserving key events.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual token compression literature for LVLMs (FastV, FasterVLM, FrameFusion, VisionZip, PruMerge) by introducing a unified spatio-temporal graph that jointly models spatial and temporal token relationships, rather than treating them in isolation as prior methods do. Its key conceptual departure is the dual-selection principle: similarity-based community detection for redundancy compression paired with temporal-difference-based detection for preserving key event tokens. This 'similarity for redundancy, difference for events' framing is presented as a new perspective that prior importance- or similarity-only methods lack, and the graph-based formulation (connected components + centrality ranking) is a repurposing of standard network science tools applied to the token compression setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ST-SimDiff offers a training-free, plug-and-play token compression strategy that consistently outperforms strong baselines across three benchmarks and three base models, with the notable finding that at 50% retention it can match or exceed full-token performance. The practical impact is significant for long-video and real-time applications where quadratic attention cost is prohibitive, and the O(Nd) overhead of the graph construction is negligible relative to LLM inference. Open questions include how the method scales to very long videos (hundreds or thousands of frames) where graph construction and community detection may become bottlenecks, whether the fixed percentile-based difference threshold generalizes across diverse video domains, and how the approach interacts with other efficiency techniques like quantization or speculative decoding.
