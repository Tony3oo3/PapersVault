## Problem

Existing sparse attention methods for reducing decoding latency—whether approximate top-k, top-p, or LSH-based sampling—lack any guarantees on approximation quality and offer no user control over the quality-efficiency tradeoff. They also fail to provide consistent approximations across heads and query vectors, since deterministic sparsity (e.g., top-k) assumes attention is dominated by a few tokens, an assumption that breaks down when attention scores are relatively uniform.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261774/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

vAttention takes the KV cache (K, V ∈ R^{n×d}), a single query vector q ∈ R^{1×d}, and user-specified tolerance parameters (ε, δ) as input, and produces a sparse attention output vector approximating full SDPA. The token budget is computed adaptively per head, per layer, and per query using a small base sample (fraction f_b of residual tokens) to estimate population statistics, so the number of attended tokens varies dynamically rather than being fixed. The final index set is a union of deterministic indices (sinks, sliding window, approximate top-k) and uniformly sampled residual indices, with the stochastic portion's size governed by the (ε, δ) guarantee.

## Method

vAttention's core insight is that top-k selection and uniform random sampling are complementary: top-k excels when attention scores are sharply dominated by a few tokens, while sampling provides better estimates when scores are relatively uniform. The method combines both by allocating a fixed fraction of the budget to deterministic indices (attention sinks, local sliding window, and a pluggable approximate top-k method such as HashAttention) and the remainder to uniform random sampling from the residual tokens. The sample size is chosen via a Central Limit Theorem-based bound that guarantees the numerator and denominator of SDPA are each (ε, δ)-approximated, and a combination theorem then ensures the overall attention output has at most ε relative error with probability 1−δ. In practice, population statistics (variance for the denominator, trace of covariance for the numerator) are estimated from a small base sample, making the budget fully adaptive and the guarantees hold per head, per layer, per query without any retraining.

## Results

On RULER32K-HARD at 10% sparsity, vAttention(oracle-top-k) achieves 88.61 on Llama-3.1-8B-Inst (vs. 88.74 full attention), 65.15 on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B (vs. 65.41 full), and 64.12 on Mistral-7B-Inst-v0.3 (vs. 64.05 full), nearly matching full-attention quality. vAttention(HashAttention) improves over standalone HashAttention by 4.6 percentage points on Llama-3.1-8B (86.56 vs. 81.94) and 4.4 points on DeepSeek-R1 (65.06 vs. 60.70). On AIME2024 with 32K-token generation, vAttention(oracle-top-k) and vAttention(HashAttention) both match full-model average accuracy (36.67 and 35.00 vs. 36.66 dense) at ~10–15% average density, demonstrating reliable long-form reasoning under sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

vAttention consistently outperforms all four compared baselines. Against HashAttention (Desai et al., 2025), vAttention(HAT) recovers 4.4–4.6 percentage points on RULER32K-HARD at 10% sparsity (e.g., 86.56 vs. 81.94 on Llama-3.1-8B), and in the wider multi-model evaluation (Appendix J) the gap widens at lower densities (e.g., at 2% density on Llama-3.1-8B, vAttention(OracleTopK) scores 87.18 vs. HashAttention's implicit ~74). Against MagicPig (Chen et al., 2024), vAttention is dramatically superior: at 10% density on Llama-3.1-8B, vAttention(OracleTopK) achieves 87.50 while MagicPig scores only 30.20, and the paper shows MagicPig's apparent strength under a full-prompt-preprocessing setup collapses under the more realistic split-prompt setup. Against Double Sparsity (Yang et al., 2024), vAttention(OracleTopK) leads by 12.75 points at 10% density on Llama-3.1-8B (87.50 vs. 74.75) and by 24.6 points at 2% density (87.18 vs. 34.90). Against PQCache (Zhang et al., 2025), vAttention(OracleTopK) edges ahead at 10% density (87.50 vs. 86.29 on Llama-3.1-8B) and pulls further ahead at lower densities (87.18 vs. 68.95 at 2%), indicating PQCache degrades more sharply as sparsity increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

vAttention sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: approximate top-k sparse attention (HashAttention, Quest, InfLLM, Double Sparsity) and LSH-based sampling (MagicPig). It departs from the dominant top-k paradigm by demonstrating that even oracle top-k and top-p are fundamentally insufficient when attention scores are relatively uniform, and from MagicPig by replacing LSH-based importance sampling with uniform random sampling whose budget is governed by CLT-derived (ε,δ) guarantees. The method is composable with any off-the-shelf approximate top-k backend, inheriting the attention-sink and sliding-window conventions from StreamingLLM while adding a statistically principled stochastic component that prior methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first practical (ε,δ) verified guarantees for sparse attention, vAttention addresses a critical gap for production deployment: users can now explicitly control the quality-efficiency trade-off rather than relying on heuristic sparsity levels with no error bounds. The near-perfect empirical correlation between the user-specified ε and observed layer error validates the theoretical framework, and the ability to match full-attention quality at up to 20× sparsity (and sustain 32K-token reasoning on AIME2024) makes sparse attention a viable default for long-context inference. Open questions include whether the CLT-based budget (which is tighter but less conservative than Hoeffding's bound) holds under adversarial or out-of-distribution inputs, whether optimized CUDA kernels can close the remaining efficiency gap, and whether the guarantees extend to multi-head/multi-layer error propagation in a fully end-to-end sense.
