## Problem

De novo RNA sequence-structure co-design remains difficult due to RNA's conformational flexibility, non-canonical base pairing, and severe scarcity of experimentally resolved 3D structures. Existing diffusion and flow-matching approaches for RNA design train bespoke models from scratch on this limited data, capping their fidelity. No prior work has integrated a comprehensively pre-trained biomolecular predictor (covering proteins, RNA, DNA, and complexes) into a joint generative diffusion framework for RNA.

## Input / Output

In unconditional mode, the model starts from pure noise over both the discrete nucleotide alphabet {A,C,G,U,N} and continuous 3D coordinates, producing a full RNA sequence and all-atom structure (up to 27 heavy atoms per nucleotide, including backbone phosphate, ribose, and base atoms). In conditional mode, a fixed protein structure and sequence are provided as context while only the RNA components are diffused; the protein is preserved throughout the forward process. Training sequences are capped at 256 nucleotides, with longer sequences handled via random windowing as data augmentation.

## Method

RiboDiff embeds the pre-trained RoseTTAFold2NA three-track architecture (sequence, pair, and SE(3)-equivariant structure tracks) as the shared denoiser within a dual diffusion process: a discrete absorbing-state diffusion over categorical nucleotides with a cosine schedule, coupled with an SE(3)-equivariant continuous diffusion over per-nucleotide rigid frames (Gaussian noise on translations, isotropic Gaussian on SO(3) rotations via axis-angle sampling, and Gaussian noise on local atomic positions). New diffusion heads (categorical logits, translational noise, and SO(3) tangent velocities) are added to RF2NA and fine-tuned jointly with the trunk, injecting timestep embeddings into all tracks. Training uses a combined loss of sequence cross-entropy, frame-aligned point error (FAPE), global RMSD, bond/angle geometry penalties, and a Lennard-Jones steric clash term, with stochastic masking to support sequence-only, structure-only, and joint generation modes. At inference, a soft value-based decoding (SVDD) procedure generates M candidate next states per denoising step and selects the one maximizing a task-aligned reward (self-consistency, pLDDT/pTM, or ipTM) plus a KL regularization term, improving quality without any parameter updates.

## Results

On the single-RNA co-design task (RNASolo), RiboDiff achieves a 97.38% success rate (scRMSD < 5 Å), reducing average scRMSD from 35.77 Å (MMDiff) to 3.43 Å — a >10× improvement — while raising scTM-score from 0.12 to 0.71 and LDDT from 0.33 to 0.74. On protein-conditioned RNA binder design (PDBBind), RiboDiff doubles sequence recovery over RNAFlow (56.26% vs. 27.98% GT-SeqRec) and improves LDDT from 0.40 to 0.73. On RNA-protein complex co-design, scRMSD drops from 30.84 Å (MMDiff) to 7.43 Å, and scSeqRec rises from 17.46% to 52.91%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RiboDiff substantially outperforms MMDiff (Morehead et al., 2023) across all self-consistency metrics on single RNA: scRMSD drops from 35.77 Å to 3.43 Å, scTM-score rises 6× (0.12→0.71), and success rate jumps from 8.86% to 97.38%. Against the two-stage RNA-FrameFlow + gRNAde pipeline (Anand et al., 2025; Joshi et al., 2025), RiboDiff achieves a 5.4× lower scRMSD (3.43 vs. 18.65 Å) and a 6.3× higher success rate (97.38% vs. 15.52%), while matching or exceeding scSeqRec (48.57% vs. 45.65%) and maintaining full structural diversity (qTMclust 1.00 vs. 0.76). On protein-conditioned RNA binder design, RiboDiff roughly doubles GT-SeqRec over RNAFlow (Nori & Jin, 2024) (56.26% vs. 27.98%) and conditional MMDiff (24%), while improving LDDT from 0.40/0.35 to 0.73. On RNA-protein complex co-design, RiboDiff reduces scRMSD by 4.1× over MMDiff (7.43 vs. 30.84 Å) and raises scSeqRec from 17.46% to 52.91%. RiboGen (Rubin et al., 2025) is compared only visually (no public code); RiboDiff shows consistently lower top-10 scRMSD and higher scTM-score across sequence lengths 40–150 nt.

## Relation to Prior Work

RiboDiff extends the RFdiffusion paradigm—embedding a pre-trained structure predictor as the denoiser inside a diffusion model—from proteins (RoseTTAFold) to RNA (RoseTTAFold2NA), while adding a discrete diffusion channel for nucleotide sequences alongside the SE(3)-equivariant continuous diffusion for all-atom frames. Prior joint RNA generation methods (MMDiff, RiboGen, RNA-FrameFlow) train bespoke diffusion or flow-matching models from scratch on the scarce ~15k-structure RNA corpus, whereas RiboDiff repurposes RF2NA's cross-molecular pre-training (proteins, RNA, DNA, complexes) as an inductive bias. The RL-enhanced inference step (SVDD) draws from recent value-based decoding work for diffusion models, adapted here with RNA-specific rewards (scRMSD, ipTM, clash penalties).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RNA design is bottlenecked by extreme data scarcity (~15k resolved structures vs. millions for proteins), and this work demonstrates that pre-training-guided diffusion can overcome that bottleneck, yielding a >10× reduction in self-consistency RMSD over from-scratch baselines. The dual discrete-continuous formulation and conditional protein-conditioned generation open a path toward designing RNA therapeutics (aptamers, riboswitches) that bind specific protein targets. Key open questions include handling very long RNAs (>256 nt), explicit modeling of ions/cofactors/solvent, experimental validation of generated binders, and whether the pre-trained-predictor-as-denoiser paradigm generalizes to other data-scarce biomolecular domains (e.g., DNA, multi-component ribonucleoprotein machines).
