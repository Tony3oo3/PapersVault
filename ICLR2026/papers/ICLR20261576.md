## Problem

Generating realistic human-object interaction (HOI) animations requires jointly modeling dynamic human actions and diverse object geometries, but plain diffusion models produce contact artifacts such as missed grasps, object penetration, and unstable contact over time. Prior methods address this with external classifiers, hand-crafted kinematic constraints, or physics simulation, all of which sacrifice generality or overfit to specific priors. The paper asks whether guidance for contact quality can emerge purely from the data and the denoising process itself, without any auxiliary classifier or hand-designed constraint.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt, a canonical object geometry represented as a 1024-point cloud (encoded via a Basis Point Set descriptor with normalized scale), and an SMPL-H body shape parameter β. It outputs a T-frame sequence where each frame contains 52 human joint positions (30 for hands), 30 hand scalar rotation angles, and a 6D object pose (3D translation + 6D rotation). The representation is explicitly decomposed into three modality-specific token groups—body (22 joints), hands (30 joints), and object—each receiving its own noise level during both training and inference, which is central to the guidance mechanism.

## Method

LIGHT (Learning Implicit Guidance for Human-object in Teraction) extends diffusion forcing by assigning independent noise schedules to modality-specific token groups (body, hand, object) and exploiting the resulting asynchronous denoising to produce guidance without external classifiers. At inference, two coupled passes share the same 8-layer transformer denoiser: a uniform schedule denoises all modalities synchronously with standard text CFG, while a staged schedule keeps one modality (e.g., body+hand) δ steps ahead of the other (e.g., object), so the cleaner component guides the noisier one through cross-attention. The final prediction combines the staged denoiser output with an incremental guidance term weighted by ω₂ that contrasts the staged prediction against the uniform prediction, yielding a soft, contact-aware correction analogous to but distinct from classifier-free guidance. Training uses independently sampled per-modality noise levels and a composite loss (reconstruction + foot-skating + velocity + contact regularization), and generalization is further improved by contact-aware shape-spectrum augmentation that retargets original HOI sequences onto geometrically diverse objects from ShapeNet and Objaverse while preserving contact semantics.

## Results

On the InterAct dataset, LIGHT with pace-induced guidance achieves the best FID (0.148), Contact ratio (0.132), and frame-wise contact F1 (0.627) among all compared methods. It also attains the lowest MM Dist (2.785) and competitive R-Precision Top-1 (0.421). The guidance mechanism improves over the no-guidance variant by +0.026 in R-Precision Top-1, −0.048 in FID, and +0.028 in C_F1, confirming that asynchronous denoising schedules add meaningful contact-aware correction beyond plain diffusion forcing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIGHT with guidance outperforms all four baselines on FID (0.148 vs. 0.215–0.689), Contact ratio (0.132 vs. 0.084–0.124), and frame-wise C_F1 (0.627 vs. 0.501–0.584), demonstrating superior contact fidelity and distributional quality. However, InterDiff retains the best R-Precision Top-1 (0.501 vs. 0.421) and MM Dist (2.461 vs. 2.785), indicating stronger text–motion alignment, while CHOIS leads on R-Precision Top-2/Top-3 (0.660/0.766 vs. 0.637/0.754). On penetration (Pene↓), HOI-Diff (0.103) and Text2HOI (0.105) are slightly better than LIGHT (0.132), though the gap is small. The ablation (LIGHT w/o guidance vs. w/ guidance) shows the pace-induced guidance contributes +0.028 C_F1 and −0.048 FID, confirming that the asynchronous schedule—not merely the architecture—drives the contact-quality gains. Overall, LIGHT trades a modest amount of text-alignment precision for substantially better physical contact realism, which the authors argue is the more critical failure mode in HOI animation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIGHT extends diffusion forcing (Chen et al., 2024) from a sequential generation mechanism into a guidance framework by exploiting the contrast between modality-specific denoising paces. It departs from prior HOI guidance methods (HOI-Diff's affordance classifier, InterDiff's kinematics-informed predictor, CG-HOI's contact regression) that rely on external networks or hand-crafted constraints, instead deriving guidance purely from the relative noise levels assigned to body, hand, and object tokens. The token-separation strategy follows Cha et al. (2024), while the contact-aware shape-spectrum augmentation introduces a new optimization-based data augmentation that preserves contact semantics across geometrically diverse objects from ShapeNet and Objaverse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that asynchronous denoising schedules produce inherently contact-aware guidance—without any explicit contact supervision in the guidance signal—offers a principled, data-driven alternative to the hand-crafted priors that have dominated HOI generation. The gradient analysis showing LIGHT's direction correlates more strongly with penetration-reducing gradients than text CFG provides mechanistic evidence for why this works. However, the 5× runtime cost (72s vs. 15s per batch) limits practical deployment, and the hyperparameter sensitivity (ω₂, δ) suggests the method may not transfer trivially to other multi-modal generation tasks. Open questions include whether the pace-induced guidance principle generalizes beyond HOI to other multi-component generation problems and whether the augmentation strategy scales to open-vocabulary object categories.
