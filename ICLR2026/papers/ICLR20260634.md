## Problem

Existing block sparse attention methods estimate block importance by compressing along the sequence dimension, which is inherently coarse-grained and causes high-scoring tokens to be overlooked at high sparsity rates. Achieving token-level importance estimation would solve this, but naively computing full dot-products incurs the same quadratic cost as full attention, defeating the purpose of sparsification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260634/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the Q, K, V tensors of a transformer attention layer during the pre-filling stage of long-context inference (sequences up to 256K tokens). It produces per-head binary block sparse masks at a fixed block granularity (size b), where each head receives a different number of selected blocks determined by its dynamically estimated budget. A stride parameter (default 4) further subsamples the sequence dimension during estimation, and a minimum budget (e.g., 2048 tokens) ensures a floor on retained blocks.

## Method

ProxyAttn exploits two empirical observations: (1) attention heads in deeper layers focus on a large intersection of tokens, and (2) the primary difference between heads is their sparsity rather than their focus. For importance estimation, it compresses along the head dimension rather than the sequence dimension: heads are partitioned into groups, and a single proxy head per group (formed by average-pooling Q and K within the group) computes full token-level attention scores, which are then max-pooled to block level. This yields fine-grained block importance at a cost of only g/(n·stride²) of full attention. For budget allocation, it uses the last block's query to estimate each head's sparsity via a cumulative probability threshold γ, producing a per-head budget b_i that determines how many top-scoring blocks each head retains. The combination of shared proxy scores and per-head dynamic budgets generates diverse sparse masks across heads while maintaining a unified importance ranking.

## Results

On RULER, ProxyAttn (γ=0.95) achieves a weighted average of 87.43 on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, surpassing full attention (86.49) while maintaining 69% sparsity, and 84.53 on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M (vs. 85.53 full attention at 61% sparsity). On real-world benchmarks (InfiniteBench + LongBench-v2), ProxyAttn attains the best overall scores of 36.06 (Llama) and 38.33 (Qwen), both exceeding full attention. In terms of efficiency, ProxyAttn achieves up to 10.3× attention kernel speedup at 256K tokens and 2.4× end-to-end prefilling acceleration on 128K sequences, with a 2.08× speedup on the 70B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProxyAttn outperforms all four baselines on both models across RULER and real-world benchmarks. On Llama3.1-8B, ProxyAttn (γ=0.95) achieves 90.18 average vs. FlexPrefill (89.47), SeerAttention (88.96), XAttention (88.88), and MInference (88.09), even surpassing full attention (89.46). The advantage is most pronounced on Qwen2.5-7B, where XAttention degrades severely (83.90 vs. ProxyAttn's 87.30) due to its reliance on offline head budgets, while ProxyAttn's online budget estimation transfers without re-tuning. On real-world tasks (InfiniteBench + LongBench-v2), ProxyAttn leads with 36.06 overall on Llama (vs. XAttention 34.89, MInference 34.78, FlexPrefill 33.96) and 38.33 on Qwen (vs. MInference 37.91, FlexPrefill 37.39, XAttention 37.26). In efficiency, ProxyAttn achieves 10.3× attention speedup at 256K tokens, substantially exceeding the speedups of MInference and FlexPrefill at comparable sparsity levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProxyAttn departs from the dominant paradigm in block sparse attention (MInference, FlexPrefill, XAttention, SeerAttention), which all compress along the sequence dimension to estimate block importance. Instead, it compresses along the head dimension, using a small number of pooled 'proxy heads' to produce token-level scores that guide all heads in a group. The dynamic budget allocation is conceptually inspired by MInference's per-head pattern assignment but made fully online and block-aware, while the observation of cross-head consistency in token focus extends the head-diversity analyses of MoA and DuoAttention by exploiting their shared structure rather than their differences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free method, ProxyAttn can be immediately applied to any existing LLM without retraining, and its 10.3× attention and 2.4× prefilling speedups at high sparsity make it practically attractive for long-context deployment. The empirical finding that a single proxy head can represent all 32 heads in Llama3.1-8B (validated by sink-token ablation) suggests substantial redundancy in multi-head attention that could inform future architecture design. Open questions include whether the proxy-head approach transfers to the decoding phase, how it interacts with KV-cache compression or speculative decoding, and whether the head-consistency assumption holds for models with very different GQA ratios or for non-English/multilingual settings.
