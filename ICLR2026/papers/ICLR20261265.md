## Problem

Existing 3D-LLM training data provide only question-answer pairs without explicit reasoning traces, reducing models to opaque black boxes that cannot explain their spatial decisions. Prior attempts to add Chain-of-Thought annotations suffer from two critical flaws: they apply CoT indiscriminately across all task types (causing hallucinations and accuracy drops on simple perception) and remain limited in scale, preventing robust reasoning pattern learning. The paper asks whether a principled, scene-grounded CoT supervision strategy—applied selectively by task complexity—can teach 3D-LLMs to reason transparently without degrading basic perception.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text instruction, a 3D point cloud segmented into object proposals (bounding boxes + semantic labels via Mask3D), and multi-view video frames. Internally, the scene is decomposed into a scene graph encoding spatial relations (adjacency, support, relative direction) and projected into a 2D BEV map, forming a multi-modal scene context. Outputs are task-dependent: perception tasks yield direct answers only, while analysis and planning tasks produce a structured four-step CoT (identify goal → analyze constraints → leverage scene evidence → answer) where every scene-derived claim is explicitly tagged with an <SI> token, followed by the final answer or action plan.

## Method

SCoT introduces a three-tier task taxonomy (Perception → Analysis → Planning) that dictates when CoT supervision is applied: answer-only for perception, grounded CoT for analysis and planning. The 1.1M-sample dataset is generated by constructing rich scene contexts (scene graph + BEV images + object crops) and prompting a VLM to produce Query-CoT-Answer triples, with a dedicated <SI> tagging mechanism that forces every scene-derived inference to cite specific geometric or relational evidence, enabling verifiability and filtering of ungrounded rationales. The SCoT-Reasoner architecture builds on Vicuna-7B with an Object-Relationship-Scene (ORS) refinement module: Uni3D encodes 3D object features, DINOv2 encodes 2D video features, and a GraphTransformer with learnable spatial bias from pairwise offset embeddings refines object-centric representations before projection into the LLM space. Training follows a two-stage protocol (perception first, then analysis/planning) using LoRA (rank 64) on a single A100 GPU, with quality control via multi-LLM cross-checking and manual verification achieving ~90% acceptance.

## Results

On SCoT-Analysis tasks, SCoT-Reasoner(CoT) achieves the best comprehensive scores across all three sub-tasks: Object Analysis (Explainability 7.04, Faithfulness 6.15, Trustworthiness 6.41), Relationship Analysis (5.77, 5.34, 5.41), and Scene Analysis (7.95, 7.55, 7.68). CoT supervision yields average gains of 6.21% in explainability, 11.74% in faithfulness, and 10.02% in trustworthiness over the answer-only setting. On the implicit detection sub-task, CoT is indispensable: SCoT-Reasoner jumps from 8.6% to 32.2% Acc@0.25 (+23.6%). Conversely, applying CoT to simple perception tasks causes a 4.9% accuracy drop (e.g., ScanRefer Acc@0.25 drops from 58.4 to 56.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCoT-Reasoner(CoT) outperforms all three primary 3D-LLM baselines (Chat3D V2, Video 3D LLM, Chat Scene) on every comprehensive metric (Explainability, Faithfulness, Trustworthiness) across all analysis and planning sub-tasks, with the largest gaps over Chat3D V2 (e.g., Faithfulness 6.15 vs. 5.50 on Object Analysis). On the implicit detection task, SCoT-Reasoner(CoT) achieves 32.2% Acc@0.25, dramatically surpassing 3D VG Transformer (12.5%), Chat Scene (26.9%), and Video 3D LLM (24.1%). On the MSQA-ScanNet generalization benchmark, SCoT-Reasoner (54.4 overall) matches in-domain supervised models LEO (54.8) and MSR3D (54.2) while surpassing zero-shot GPT-4o (52.3) by 2.1 points. On MSQA-ARKitScenes (cross-domain), SCoT-Reasoner (41.2) outperforms both GPT-4o (41.0) and Qwen-VL (39.7), with a +3.3 advantage in reasoning-intensive categories. In a controlled comparison using the same Chat Scene architecture, training on SCoT yields 47.6 overall on MSQA-ScanNet versus 43.1 for 3D-R1, with a +14.6 gap on Navigation. On perception tasks, SCoT-Reasoner (without CoT) achieves the best grounding accuracy (ScanRefer Acc@0.50 = 53.4, Multi3DRefer F1@0.50 = 55.7), slightly trailing Video 3D LLM on captioning (CIDEr 79.6 vs. 83.8) but leading on all other perception metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D-LLM training paradigm from answer-only supervision to structured, scene-grounded Chain-of-Thought reasoning, building on architectures like Chat Scene, Video 3D LLM, and Chat 3D V2. It departs from prior CoT-for-3D efforts (3D-CoT, 3D-R1, SpaceR, Spatial-MLLM) in two key ways: (1) it introduces a three-tier taxonomy (perception/analysis/planning) that explicitly dictates *when* CoT should be applied rather than applying it indiscriminately, and (2) it enforces scene grounding via a dedicated <SI> tagging mechanism that ties every reasoning step to verifiable scene evidence. The SCoT-Reasoner architecture itself is a modest combination of existing components (Mask3D, Uni3D, DINOv2, GraphTransformer, Vicuna-7B with LoRA), positioned as a unified multimodal testbed rather than a novel model contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.1M-sample SCoT dataset with its three-tier taxonomy and <SI>-grounded CoT annotations provides a practical resource for training 3D-LLMs with transparent, verifiable reasoning, addressing a gap where prior datasets (3D-R1: 30k, Spatial-MLLM: 120k) were limited in scale and task diversity. The empirical finding that CoT supervision *degrades* simple perception accuracy by ~4.9% while substantially improving analysis and planning (6–12% in faithfulness/trustworthiness) offers a useful design principle for practitioners: reasoning supervision should be task-conditional, not universal. Key limitations include validation only on indoor ScanNet scenes, a 2–3× inference latency increase from CoT generation, and reliance on VLM-generated annotations that may inherit systematic biases. Open questions include whether the taxonomy and grounding mechanism transfer to outdoor, dynamic, or city-scale environments, and whether the <SI> mechanism can be replaced by learned grounding without explicit prompting.
