## Problem

Existing cache-then-forecast acceleration methods for diffusion transformers, such as TaylorSeer, rely on standard monomial Taylor bases to extrapolate features across skipped timesteps. This monomial basis is fundamentally mismatched to the non-monotonic, turning-point-rich trajectories of diffusion features, causing prediction error to grow rapidly with extrapolation horizon and polynomial order, which caps achievable speedup before visible quality degradation. The paper asks whether a basis better aligned with the actual statistical structure of feature evolution can close this gap without retraining.

## Input / Output

HiCache is a training-free, model-agnostic plug-in that operates entirely at the intermediate feature level inside a diffusion transformer: at every Nth timestep it performs a full forward pass to cache finite-difference derivative approximations, and at intervening timesteps it predicts features from the cache using scaled Hermite polynomials, skipping the expensive network evaluation. It accepts the same inputs as the underlying diffusion model (text prompts at 1024×1024 or 1328×1328 for FLUX/Qwen-Image, class labels for ImageNet DiT-XL/2, low-resolution images for Inf-DiT super-resolution) and produces the same outputs (generated images, 4-second video clips, or 4× upscaled images) with no change to the model interface or sampling schedule.

## Method

HiCache replaces the monomial Taylor basis in the cache-then-forecast predictor with scaled Hermite polynomials, motivated by the empirical finding (validated via energy-distance tests across 75 configurations) that finite-difference feature derivatives in DiTs follow approximate multivariate Gaussian distributions, for which Hermite polynomials are the Karhunen-Loève-optimal orthogonal basis under Gaussian-weighted L². A single contraction factor σ ∈ (0,1) provides dual stabilization: it shrinks the polynomial argument (σx) to keep evaluations within the stable oscillatory regime and multiplies each coefficient by σⁿ to suppress the exponential growth of high-order Hermite terms. The algorithm preserves the exact finite-difference caching structure of TaylorSeer—only the basis function evaluations change—so it drops into any existing cache-then-forecast pipeline (TaylorSeer, ClusCa, Chipmunk) with negligible overhead (a few per-step scalar polynomial evaluations via the three-term recurrence, no extra matrix multiplications). The dual-scaling mechanism also works standalone on TaylorSeer's monomial basis, providing measurable quality gains without changing the predictor form.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev (text-to-image, 1024×1024, 200 DrawBench prompts), HiCache (N=7, O=2, σ=0.5) achieves a 5.55× FLOPs speedup with ImageReward 0.9979, exceeding the unaccelerated 50-step baseline (0.9872) by 4.25%. As a plug-in upgrade, replacing ClusCa's Taylor predictor with HiCache's Hermite basis lifts ImageReward from 0.9480 to 0.9840 at the same N=7 setting. On text-to-video (HunyuanVideo, VBench), HiCache reaches 79.65% at N=7, O=2 versus TaylorSeer's 79.28%, and on ImageNet class-conditional generation (DiT-XL/2), HiCache achieves FID 4.31 vs. TaylorSeer's 4.58 at ~7.1× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiCache outperforms all cache-then-reuse baselines (FORA, ToCa, TeaCache, DBCache) by a wide margin on ImageReward at 5.55× speedup (0.9979 vs. 0.74–0.84), confirming that the cache-then-forecast paradigm is essential at high acceleration. Against the closest competitor TaylorSeer (same N=7, O=2), HiCache improves ImageReward by 4.07% (0.9979 vs. 0.9572), PSNR by 0.30 dB, and reduces LPIPS from 0.4520 to 0.3982; the gap widens at N=9 (0.9113 vs. 0.8562, +6.4%). As a plug-in, HiCache upgrades ClusCa from 0.9480 to 0.9840 ImageReward (Hi-ClusCa) at zero extra FLOPs, and on Chipmunk-Flux it recovers ImageReward from 0.845 (Taylor) to 0.938 (HiCache) at comparable latency. On text-to-video (HunyuanVideo), HiCache consistently edges out TaylorSeer on VBench (79.89 vs. 79.78 at N=6; 79.65 vs. 79.28 at N=7, O=2). On ImageNet DiT-XL/2, HiCache achieves lower FID than TaylorSeer at every speedup level tested (e.g., 4.31 vs. 4.58 at ~7.1×), and on super-resolution (Inf-DiT, DIV8K) it maintains PSNR 30.18 vs. TaylorSeer's 29.71 at interval=8 (5.93× FLOPs speedup).

## Relation to Prior Work

HiCache extends the cache-then-forecast paradigm introduced by TaylorSeer (Liu et al., 2025a), which itself advanced beyond earlier cache-then-reuse methods (DeepCache, FORA, ToCa, ClusCa) by using Taylor series to extrapolate features rather than simply reusing them. The key departure is replacing the monomial Taylor basis with scaled Hermite polynomials, motivated by the empirical observation that finite-difference feature statistics in DiTs are approximately Gaussian, making Hermite polynomials the Karhunen-Loève-optimal orthogonal basis under Gaussian correlation. A dual-scaling mechanism (input contraction + coefficient suppression via a single hyperparameter σ) addresses the numerical instability of Hermite polynomials at large arguments, and this scaling also provides standalone gains when applied to TaylorSeer's monomial basis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of HiCache—requiring only a polynomial basis swap with negligible compute overhead—makes it immediately applicable to upgrade any existing cache-then-forecast pipeline (TaylorSeer, ClusCa, Chipmunk) without retraining or architectural changes, which is practically significant for deployed diffusion systems. The consistent quality improvements across text-to-image, text-to-video, class-conditional generation, and super-resolution tasks suggest the Gaussian structure in feature dynamics is a broadly exploitable property of transformer-based diffusion models. Open questions include whether the Gaussian finite-difference assumption holds for non-DiT architectures (e.g., U-Net-based models), whether the adaptive per-layer σ can be further refined for extreme acceleration ratios, and whether the theoretical conditional advantage (σ√2|Δs| < 1) can be made unconditional.
