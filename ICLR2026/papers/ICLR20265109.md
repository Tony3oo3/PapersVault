## Problem

Existing open-source joint audio-video generation (JAVG) methods lag significantly behind proprietary systems like Veo3 in generation quality, temporal synchrony, and alignment with human preferences. Prior approaches rely on complex dual-stream architectures with ad-hoc implicit synchrony mechanisms, and none have incorporated preference-based alignment into the JAVG pipeline. The paper addresses these gaps with a unified, efficient framework that simultaneously improves single-modal quality, explicit temporal alignment, and human-preference alignment.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a temporally synchronized audio-video pair. It supports arbitrary durations from 2 to 5 seconds and resolutions from 240p to 480p across multiple aspect ratios. Audio is internally represented as 64-bin mel-spectrograms at 16 kHz (compressed via AudioLDM2's VAE into 8-channel embeddings), while video uses Wan2.1's VAE (16-channel embeddings with 4× temporal and 8×8 spatial compression). Both modalities are further patchified (2×2) before entering the DiT backbone.

## Method

JavisDiT++ builds on Wan2.1-1.3B-T2V with three key innovations. First, a Modality-Specific Mixture-of-Experts (MS-MoE) design uses shared multi-head self-attention for dense cross-modal interaction, then routes audio and video tokens deterministically to separate FFN layers, expanding total parameters from 1.3B to 2.1B while keeping 1.3B active per token. Second, Temporal-Aligned RoPE (TA-RoPE) assigns audio tokens the same temporal position ID as their corresponding video frame along dimension 0, with offsets of H and W in the remaining two dimensions to prevent position-ID overlap between modalities, achieving frame-level synchrony at zero additional inference cost. Third, AV-DPO introduces the first preference alignment for JAVG: multiple reward models (AudioBox, VideoAlign, ImageBind, SyncFormer) score generated samples across audio quality, video quality, and AV alignment dimensions, and modality-aware ranking selects winning-losing pairs that are superior across all dimensions simultaneously; the DPO loss is applied separately per modality with modality-specific β values (β=1000 for audio, β=3000 for video) and regularized with flow-matching loss. Training proceeds in three stages: audio pre-training on 780K pairs, audio-video SFT with LoRA on 330K triplets, and AV-DPO on 25K curated preference pairs.

## Results

On JavisBench (240p, 4s sounding videos), JavisDiT++ (2.1B) achieves the best score on all 11 evaluation metrics: FVD 141.5, FAD 5.5, CLIP 0.316, CLAP 0.424, AV-IB 0.198, JavisScore 0.159, and DeSync 0.832. It outperforms the larger JavisDiT (3.1B) by 31% on FVD (141.5 vs. 204.1) and UniVerse-1 (6.4B) by 27% on FVD (141.5 vs. 194.2), while running in 10 s—faster than both (30 s and 13 s). The model also leads on synchrony (DeSync 0.832 vs. 1.039 for JavisDiT) and text-audio alignment (CLAP 0.424 vs. 0.391 for JavisDiT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JavisDiT++ wins on every metric against all nine baselines. Against the strongest joint-generation competitor JavisDiT (3.1B), it reduces FVD by 31% (141.5 vs. 204.1), FAD by 24% (5.5 vs. 7.2), and DeSync by 20% (0.832 vs. 1.039), while being 3× faster at inference (10 s vs. 30 s). Against UniVerse-1 (6.4B), the gains are even larger on synchrony: JavisScore more than doubles (0.159 vs. 0.077) and FAD drops 37% (5.5 vs. 8.7), despite using roughly one-third the parameters. Among the T2V+V2A cascaded baselines, MMAudio (0.1B) is the closest competitor on audio quality (FAD 6.1 vs. 5.5) and synchrony (DeSync 0.849 vs. 0.832), but JavisDiT++ still leads on every reported metric. The T2A+A2V pipelines (TempoTkn, TPoS) are far behind, with FVD values 4–6× higher, confirming that cascaded generation cannot match native joint modeling. In human evaluation, JavisDiT++ is preferred over JavisDiT and UniVerse-1 by more than 70% of annotators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Wan2.1-1.3B-T2V video generation backbone and AudioLDM2's audio VAE, extending them into a unified joint audio-video generation (JAVG) framework. It departs from prior JAVG architectures (JavisDiT's dual-stream DiT with ST-Prior, UniVerse-1's stitched two-DiT design) by proposing a single-stream DiT with modality-specific MoE (MS-MoE) for cross-modal interaction and intra-modal specialization, and replaces implicit synchrony mechanisms with an explicit temporal-aligned RoPE (TA-RoPE) that enforces frame-level alignment at zero inference cost. The AV-DPO algorithm extends Direct Preference Optimization from single-modality settings (text-to-image, video) to the cross-modal JAVG domain for the first time, introducing modality-aware reward ranking to ensure consistent chosen-rejected pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JavisDiT++ establishes a practical, open-source SOTA baseline for JAVG using only ~1M public data entries and 2.1B parameters, significantly outperforming larger 3.1B and 6.4B competitors, which is important for democratizing the field. The zero-cost TA-RoPE synchronization and the first application of preference alignment to JAVG offer reusable techniques for future multimodal generative systems. Key limitations include the modest 1M training scale, reliance on parameter-efficient tuning (LoRA) rather than full fine-tuning, lack of fine-grained controllability (rhythm, pitch, speech content), and no support for unified cross-modal tasks (A2V, V2A, AI2V), all of which remain open directions for scaling and generalization.
