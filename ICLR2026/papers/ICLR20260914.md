## Problem

Pre-trained text-to-image models are trained predominantly on short, label-like captions and fail to capture key details when given descriptive paragraphs, often missing more than half of specified objects. Existing remedies either fine-tune on long prompts (poor generalization beyond training lengths, risk of catastrophic forgetting) or project long inputs into a compact context window (information bottleneck that sacrifices the very details that make long prompts compelling). The open question is how to leverage a model's existing short-prompt expertise to render long, intricate paragraphs without fine-tuning or information loss.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long descriptive paragraph (tested from ~200 to 500+ tokens; DetailMaster averages 284.9 tokens) as input, which is first encoded by the T2I model's text encoder (T5-XL for SD-1.5/SD-3.5/FLUX, or Qwen2.5-VL for Qwen-Image). The output is a single RGB image generated by a standard diffusion denoising loop. A key operational detail is that at each denoising step the noisy latent is cloned into N copies (default N=4), each conditioned on a different constituent representation, and the N independent noise predictions are summed before the next step, roughly doubling inference latency compared to a single-prompt generation.

## Method

PRISM reframes long-text-to-image generation as a compositional task: the complex conditional distribution p(x|C) is factorized into a product of simpler distributions p(x|c_i), each conditioned on a decomposed component. A lightweight decomposition module (a Querying Transformer with N learnable query vectors for bidirectional encoders, or LoRA-adapted causal LLM for Qwen-Image) extracts N constituent representations from the long-prompt encoding. The frozen T2I model makes independent noise predictions for each component, and these are merged via energy-based conjunction—summing the noise predictions, which corresponds to summing the implicit score functions of the constituent EBMs. The decomposition module is trained end-to-end in an unsupervised manner using only the standard diffusion loss computed on the composite score, with the T2I model held frozen, so the learned components are explicitly optimized for compositional generation rather than linguistic coherence. This avoids fine-tuning the backbone, preserves pre-trained knowledge, and distributes semantic load across multiple dedicated components to maintain fidelity.

## Results

On the DetailMaster benchmark (avg. 284.9-token prompts), PRISM-SD1.5 achieves 28.21% Character Presence and 20.88% Spatial Relation, outperforming the strongest SD-1.5-based baseline LongAlign (25.88% and 21.24% respectively). When applied to the modern Qwen-Image architecture, PRISM-Qwen reaches 46.84% Character Presence, 41.49% Character Location, and 49.23% Spatial Relation, surpassing FLUX-Dev. (42.02/38.18/44.94) and Qwen-Image (40.46/40.14/47.02). On image quality metrics, PRISM-Qwen attains the best DenScore (22.93), PickScore (22.04), and VQAScore (86.21) among all SOTA baselines. Critically, PRISM maintains robust performance as prompt length increases, achieving an average 7.4% improvement over baselines on prompts exceeding 500 tokens where fine-tuning methods degrade by up to 30%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SD-1.5-based long-text methods, PRISM-SD1.5 wins on Character Presence (28.21 vs. 25.88 for LongAlign, 25.57 for ELLA, 19.43 for LLM4GEN) and Character Location (16.57 vs. 14.12 for LongAlign, 15.04 for ELLA), though LongAlign edges out on Spatial Relation (21.24 vs. 20.88) and Style (70.49 vs. 64.10); with reward tuning, PRISM w/tuning surpasses LongAlign across all metrics by 4.65% on average. On the SOTA model group, PRISM-Qwen outperforms Qwen-Image by 6.38% on Character Presence (46.84 vs. 40.46) and 1.35% on Spatial Relation (49.23 vs. 47.02), and beats FLUX-Dev. by 4.82% on Character Presence (46.84 vs. 42.02) and 4.29% on Spatial Relation (49.23 vs. 44.94). PRISM-Qwen also surpasses StableDiffusion-3.5 by 7.83% on Character Presence (46.84 vs. 39.01) and 9.87% on Spatial Relation (49.23 vs. 39.36). On image quality (Table 2), PRISM-Qwen leads on DenScore (22.93), PickScore (22.04), and VQAScore (86.21) over all SOTA baselines, while SD-3.5 retains a slight edge on CLIPScore (34.97 vs. 34.12) and HPSv3 (13.39 vs. 12.05). The generalization gap widens with prompt length: on prompts >500 tokens, PRISM achieves an average 7.4% improvement over baselines, whereas LongAlign degrades by up to 30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM reframes long-text-to-image generation as a compositional problem, building directly on the concept conjunction principle from composable diffusion models (Liu et al., 2022) and the energy-based compositionality framework (Du et al., 2020), where summing noise predictions from multiple conditioned models yields a product distribution. Rather than following the two dominant strategies in the long-prompt T2I literature—fine-tuning on long captions (LongAlign) or projecting long inputs into a fixed context window (ELLA, LLM4Gen)—PRISM decomposes the long prompt into constituent representations that each fall within the pre-trained model's in-distribution input space. The decomposition module borrows its architectural design from ELLA's Querying Transformer, and the broader compositional generative modeling philosophy follows Du & Kaelbling (2024), but the key departure is learning the decomposition end-to-end via the frozen T2I model's diffusion loss rather than relying on linguistic splitting or explicit supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the primary bottleneck for long-prompt generation is the scarcity of long-captioned training data rather than text encoder capacity, since even SOTA models with powerful LLM encoders (FLUX, Qwen-Image) omit over half of specified objects. PRISM's compositional strategy offers a practical, fine-tuning-free path to improve any pre-trained T2I model on descriptive paragraphs, with the added benefit of superior generalization to unseen prompt lengths (7.4% average gain on >500 tokens). Key limitations include the lack of explicit spatial control in the composition operation, which remains data-driven, and inconsistent results across architectures (notably degradation on FLUX in supplementary experiments). Open questions include whether adaptive decomposition granularity based on prompt complexity and more structured composition operators could further close the gap with dedicated long-prompt models.
