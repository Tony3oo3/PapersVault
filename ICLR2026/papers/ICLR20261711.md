## Problem

Standalone LLMs produce highly unreliable citations (hallucination rates of 86–91.4%), and current systems compensate by querying external retrievers at inference time, introducing latency, infrastructure dependence, and vulnerability to retrieval noise. This paper asks whether LLMs can be trained to reliably attribute their answers to documents seen during continual pretraining—without any test-time retrieval—by revising the training process itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261711/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a text question and produces a sequence of (factual statement, set of supporting document titles) pairs in a single closed-book pass. Citation decoding is constrained to the known corpus title set for verifiability. The training corpus spans Wikipedia, Common Crawl, arXiv, and novel unseen documents (≈392M tokens total), each assigned a unique human-readable title as its identifier; for noisy sources like Common Crawl, titles are LLM-generated and deduplicated to ensure stability.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage framework: (1) continual pretraining with Active Indexing, and (2) instruction tuning to elicit citation behavior. Active Indexing comprises two complementary augmentation strategies: Forward Augmentation generates entity-based QA pairs from individual documents to strengthen source→fact recall (conditioning on a document ID to retrieve its facts), and Backward Augmentation clusters related chunks across distinct documents to synthesize multi-source QA pairs with citations, teaching fact→source attribution. This contrasts with the Passive Indexing baseline, which merely appends a document title to each text chunk. The key insight is that real-world facts are rarely verbatim quotes—requiring synthesis and paraphrase—so the model must learn to recognize and cite the correct document even when the underlying fact is variously expressed. Ablations show that combining both directions, scaling augmented data (up to 16× the original corpus), and explicit QA-style supervision (rather than mere rephrasing) are all necessary for robust citation, yielding up to 30.2% precision gains over passive indexing on Qwen-2.5-7B/3B.

## Results

On Qwen-2.5-7B, Active Indexing (combining forward and backward augmentation) achieves citation precision of 30.9 (ASQA), 29.3 (ELI5), 32.6 (SciQAG), and 24.4 (RepliQA), outperforming Passive Indexing by up to 30.2 points (e.g., 2.4→32.6 on SciQAG). Answer correctness also improves, reaching 27.6 (ASQA) and 17.6 (ELI5) versus 21.5 and 14.5 for Passive Indexing. On Qwen-2.5-3B, the same trends hold with lower absolute values (e.g., 20.0 C-Pr on SciQAG vs. 1.1 for Passive Indexing). GPT-4.1 with 3-shot prompting achieves substantially higher answer correctness (52.7 on ASQA, 93.0 on SciQAG) but near-zero citation precision on ELI5 and SciQAG (0.0), confirming that proprietary models lack reliable internal citation without targeted training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Passive Indexing (the direct extension of Khalifa et al. 2024's source-aware training), Active Indexing wins decisively on citation precision across all four datasets and both model sizes, with the largest gap on SciQAG (32.6 vs. 2.4 for Q-7B) and ELI5 (29.3 vs. 8.9). Against PI-SCP (Yang et al. 2025, synthetic continued pretraining that paraphrases facts without explicit ID training), Active Indexing outperforms because it explicitly teaches the model to use document identifiers in QA-style contexts, not merely to rephrase facts. Against the external RAG citation baseline (Gao et al. 2023b), the comparison is conditional on retrieval quality: under poor retrieval (BM25), internal-only and hybrid methods significantly outperform external-only, while under strong dense retrieval, external-only surpasses internal-only; the hybrid approach combining both sources achieves the best overall performance across the full retrieval-quality spectrum. Notably, GPT-4.1 with 3-shot prompting achieves far higher answer correctness (e.g., 93.0 vs. 66.6 on SciQAG) but produces near-zero citation precision on non-Wikipedia sources (0.0 on ELI5 and SciQAG), demonstrating that even frontier proprietary models cannot reliably generate internal citations without the targeted Active Indexing training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Khalifa et al. (2024), who showed that source-aware continual pretraining can let models associate facts with document identifiers—but only on a synthetic biography corpus with uniform, single-fact citations. CitePretrain departs by confronting the complexity of real-world documents (Wikipedia, Common Crawl, arXiv) where facts are paraphrased, compositional, and distributed across multiple sources, and by replacing the passive ID-appending baseline with Active Indexing: bidirectional synthetic augmentation that explicitly trains source→fact and fact→source mappings. It also positions internal citation as a complement to RAG-based external citation (Gao et al. 2023b), arguing that a closed-book model can unify retrieval and attribution in a single pass, unlike generative-retrieval pipelines where the retriever and QA model remain separate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical stakes are transparency and regulatory compliance: if models can cite their own training data, outputs become auditable without external infrastructure. The finding that GPT-4.1 answers correctly 93% of the time on SciQAG yet achieves 0% citation precision is a sharp reminder that answer correctness and attribution are decoupled capabilities, and that frontier models do not inherit citation ability for free. Limitations include still-modest absolute precision (30–40% on the best trained models), dependence on compute-heavy synthetic augmentation, and privacy risks from surfacing verbatim training snippets. Open questions include whether Active Indexing scales to truly novel knowledge beyond the pretraining cutoff, whether it survives to 70B+ parameter models, and how to reconcile internal citations with copyright-sensitive content.
