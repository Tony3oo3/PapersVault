## Problem

Current Quality-Diversity (QD) methods partition the behavior space into discrete cells, which makes the objective non-differentiable and prevents direct use of modern gradient-based optimizers. Additionally, discretization suffers from the curse of dimensionality: grid archives require exponentially many cells, and CVT archives produce cells whose volume grows exponentially, both making high-dimensional behavior spaces intractable. This paper asks whether QD can be reformulated as a fully differentiable, discretization-free optimization problem.

## Input / Output

The method requires a differentiable quality function f: Θ→ℝ and a differentiable behavior descriptor desc: Θ→ℝ^d, plus an initial population of N solution parameters. Bounded behavior descriptors (e.g., in [0,1]^d) are mapped to an unbounded space via a logit transform before optimization. The output is a population of N solution parameters (not a single solution) that jointly maximize quality and behavioral spread, evaluated on metrics like QD Score, Vendi Score, and Quality-weighted Vendi Score against a fixed CVT archive for comparison.

## Method

The paper introduces the Soft QD Score, which treats each solution as a Gaussian light source of brightness proportional to its quality and integrates the pointwise maximum of all sources over the continuous behavior space, eliminating any discretization. To make this objective tractable, SQUAD derives a closed-form lower bound by truncating the max-min inclusion-exclusion expansion after pairwise terms and replacing pairwise minima with geometric means, yielding an objective that decomposes into a quality sum (Σf_n) and a pairwise repulsion term (−Σ√(f_i·f_j)·exp(−‖b_i−b_j‖²/γ²)). This objective is fully differentiable with respect to solution parameters, enabling end-to-end optimization with Adam. For efficiency, repulsion is computed only against k-nearest neighbors in behavior space (reducing cost from O(N²) to O(Nk)), updates are applied in minibatches, and the kernel bandwidth γ² serves as a single hyperparameter controlling the quality-diversity trade-off.

## Results

On the Latent Space Illumination (LSI) benchmark, SQUAD achieves a QD Score of 13.41×10³ and QVS of 177.0, nearly doubling the best baseline (CMA-MAEGA: 6.82×10³, QVS 121.6). On the harder 7-d LSI(Hard) variant, SQUAD's QD Score of 2.55×10³ is 6.5× higher than CMA-MAEGA (0.39×10³), while four of six baselines fail entirely (negative QD Score, zero QVS). On the Image Composition (IC) domain, SQUAD leads on Mean Objective (83.37 vs. 74.83 for CMA-MAEGA) and Vendi Score (5.49 vs. 3.93), with a single hyperparameter γ² controlling the quality-diversity trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SQUAD outperforms all five baselines across every benchmark and metric. Against CMA-MAEGA (the strongest baseline), SQUAD achieves roughly 2× higher QD Score on LSI and 6.5× on LSI(Hard), while also leading on Vendi Score in the IC domain (5.49 vs. 3.93); CMA-MAEGA's advantage in low-dimensional (4-d) LP tasks disappears as dimensionality increases to 8 and 16, where SQUAD's discretization-free approach scales better. CMA-MEGA is consistently second-best but trails SQUAD by a wide margin on all LSI metrics. DNS and its gradient variant DNS-G fail catastrophically on LSI, producing negative mean objectives and zero QVS, indicating complete inability to find valid solutions in the StyleGAN2 latent space. Sep-CMA-MAE and GA-ME also fail on LSI (negative or near-zero QD Scores), with GA-ME's gradient ascent updates proving insufficient to escape local optima in the high-dimensional non-linear landscape. The key differentiator is that SQUAD's continuous, differentiable objective avoids the curse of dimensionality inherent in CVT-based archives used by all baselines, giving it a structural advantage that grows with behavior-space dimensionality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Differentiable Quality-Diversity (DQD) framework of Fontaine & Nikolaidis (2021), which made QD amenable to gradient-based optimization but still relied on discrete archives (CVT tessellations). The key conceptual departure is replacing the discrete archive with a continuous 'illumination' formulation (building on the metaphor from Cully et al., 2015), where each solution contributes a Gaussian-weighted quality field over the behavior space. The resulting Soft QD Score is related to the continuous QD Score of Kent et al. (2022), but uses a smooth Gaussian kernel that admits a tractable closed-form lower bound, enabling end-to-end differentiable optimization. The pairwise repulsion mechanism is analogous to kernel-based repulsion in Stein Variational Gradient Descent, though applied to quality-diversity rather than distribution matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating discretization, SQUAD sidesteps the curse of dimensionality that plagues grid and CVT archives in high-dimensional behavior spaces, and the single bandwidth hyperparameter γ² provides an intuitive quality-diversity tradeoff knob. The theoretical grounding (monotonicity, submodularity, limiting equivalence to QD Score) gives the formulation strong mathematical properties. Key limitations include the requirement for differentiable objectives and descriptors, the second-order approximation that discards higher-order interactions, and the ad hoc logit transformation for bounded spaces. Open questions include adaptive or per-solution γ schedules, extension to non-differentiable or reinforcement learning settings, and whether richer interaction models (message passing, sparsification) can improve the approximation.
