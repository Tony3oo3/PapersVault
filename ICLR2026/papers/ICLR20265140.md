## Problem

Existing utilization-based hallucination detection methods for RAG systems (e.g., ReDeEP) rely on selecting specific attention heads and transformer layers, requiring extensive model- and dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning that limits generalizability. Moreover, prior work demonstrated correlation between proposed scores and hallucination but did not statistically validate whether those scores genuinely reflect external context or internal knowledge utilization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265140/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LUMINA requires two forward passes per token: one with the actual retrieved documents and one with random (irrelevant) documents, plus access to all intermediate layer hidden states for logit lens projection. The external context score is computed by approximating MMD over the top-100 vocabulary tokens' embeddings, while the internal knowledge score uses the logit lens projection at every layer. The final output is a scalar hallucination score per token (aggregated to response level by averaging), decomposable into the two component scores for interpretability.

## Method

LUMINA quantifies external context utilization as the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (with a cosine kernel) between the model's next-token probability distributions over token embeddings when conditioned on real vs. random documents, making it layer-agnostic and non-parametric. For internal knowledge utilization, it introduces an information processing rate that measures how far each layer's logit-lens-projected prediction for the most probable token is from the final output (weighted by layer depth in the numerator) and normalizes by each layer's prediction entropy (denominator), capturing how actively the model refines its prediction across layers. The hallucination score combines both signals as H = λ·I − (1−λ)·E with λ=0.5. A statistical validation framework derives four verifiable implications (e.g., summarization should show higher external context utilization than QA) and confirms them via one-tailed t-tests across four LLMs, establishing a stronger link between the scores and actual utilization than prior correlation-only analyses.

## Results

LUMINA achieves >0.9 AUROC on HalluRAG across all tested LLMs (0.9153 for Llama2-7B, 0.9166 for Llama2-13B, 0.9899 for Mistral-7B), outperforming the prior utilization-based method ReDeEP by up to +13% AUROC (e.g., 0.9899 vs. 0.7870 for Mistral-7B). On RAGTruth, LUMINA reaches 0.8569 AUROC / 0.8436 AUPRC with Llama2-13B and 0.7685 AUROC / 0.7942 AUPRC with Mistral-7B. LUMINA is also competitive with the supervised SAPLMA baseline, matching or exceeding it on RAGTruth for Llama2-7B (0.7646 vs. 0.6508 AUROC) and Llama2-13B (0.8569 vs. 0.8337 AUROC), all without any training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LUMINA outperforms all eight unsupervised baselines on both RAGTruth and HalluRAG across nearly all LLMs. The most relevant comparison is ReDeEP (Sun et al., ICLR 2025), the prior utilization-based method: LUMINA surpasses it by +3.7% AUROC on RAGTruth (Llama2-13B: 0.8569 vs. 0.8055) and by up to +20.3% AUROC on HalluRAG (Mistral-7B: 0.9899 vs. 0.7870). Among uncertainty-based methods, LN-Entropy is the strongest single baseline, reaching 0.9188 AUROC on HalluRAG with Mistral-7B, but LUMINA still edges ahead (0.9899) and dominates on RAGTruth. Cross-sample consistency methods (SelfCKGPT, EigenScore) and verbalization methods (P(True), RefChecker) are consistently weaker, often falling below 0.6 AUROC. Against the supervised SAPLMA baseline (Azaria & Mitchell, EMNLP 2023), LUMINA matches or exceeds it on RAGTruth for Llama2-7B (0.7646 vs. 0.6508 AUROC) and Llama2-13B (0.8569 vs. 0.8337 AUROC) without any training data, though SAPLMA slightly leads on HalluRAG for Mistral-7B (0.9667 vs. 0.9899 AUROC is actually LUMINA winning; SAPLMA leads on AUPRC: 0.9088 vs. 0.9431 is also LUMINA). The one exception is Llama3-8B on RAGTruth, where ReDeEP (0.7495) marginally beats LUMINA (0.7446) on AUROC, though LUMINA wins on AUPRC (0.7874 vs. 0.7817).

## Relation to Prior Work

LUMINA builds directly on the context-knowledge signal framework introduced by ReDeEP (Sun et al., 2025b) and SeReDeEP (Wang, 2025), which first proposed decomposing hallucination into external context utilization and internal knowledge utilization. The key departure is methodological: prior approaches require selecting specific attention heads and transformer layers (a non-trivial, model- and dataset-specific hyperparameter search), whereas LUMINA replaces these with a layer-agnostic MMD over token embeddings for context sensitivity and an information-processing-rate metric derived from the logit lens (nostalgebraist, 2020) that aggregates across all layers. Additionally, LUMINA introduces a statistical hypothesis-testing framework to validate that the proposed scores actually measure the intended utilization signals, addressing a gap where prior works demonstrated correlation with hallucination but not causal alignment with the underlying mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LUMINA offers a practical, training-free hallucination detector for RAG systems that requires only two forward passes and no hyperparameter tuning beyond a single balancing weight, making it deployable across diverse LLMs and retrieval settings. The statistical validation framework (hypothesis testing on verifiable implications) provides a template for rigorously grounding mechanistic-interpretability-based scores, which prior work lacked. Open questions include whether the fixed λ=0.5 balance generalizes to reasoning-intensive tasks where internal knowledge is legitimately needed, and whether the method extends to multi-turn or agentic RAG settings where context utilization dynamics differ. The paper's error analysis also reveals that dataset label noise and low-quality retrieval remain significant confounders, suggesting the true performance ceiling may be higher.
