## Problem

Virtual screening in low-label regimes—where only a handful of known actives exist for a target—remains difficult because existing network propagation methods rely on general-purpose fingerprints (e.g., ECFP) that blur fine-grained substructural distinctions critical to bioactivity, and because propagation over dense similarity graphs introduces topological bias that inflates false positives when the seed set is small. Foundation-model approaches screen compounds independently and fail to capture higher-order molecular dependencies that could generalize from sparse labels.

## Input / Output

SubDyve takes a small set of known active compounds (as few as 50 seeds) for a target protein, a large unlabeled candidate library (up to 10 million ZINC compounds), and optionally a target protein sequence used to retrieve homologous PubChem proteins via MMseqs2 for zero-shot seed curation. The candidate library is first filtered to retain only compounds matching at least one mined discriminative subgraph, reducing the graph to a tractable subset (~30K from 10M). The output is a ranked list of all candidate compounds with a propagation-derived relevance score, evaluated via early-recognition metrics (BEDROC, EF at 0.5%–10% thresholds).

## Method

SubDyve constructs a subgraph fingerprint network by mining class-discriminative subgraph patterns via Supervised Subgraph Mining (SSM), encoding each molecule as a Discriminative Subgraph Combination (DiSC) fingerprint, and building a weighted similarity graph via pairwise cosine similarity. On this graph, it performs iterative dynamic seed refinement: a two-layer GCN is trained with a composite loss (class-balanced BCE + pairwise RankNet + InfoNCE contrastive) to produce logits, from which local false discovery rate (LFDR) estimates determine which compounds are promoted to or removed from the seed set, with seed weights updated via a sigmoid-based rule that provably bounds the FDR. The refinement is repeated across N stratified splits of the initial seeds, and the best-performing seed weight vectors are aggregated via element-wise max pooling before a final network propagation (random walk with restart) produces the compound ranking. This combination of substructure-aware graph construction and uncertainty-calibrated propagation addresses both the representational gap of generic fingerprints and the topological-bias problem of standard propagation.

## Results

On the 10-target DUD-E zero-shot benchmark, SubDyve achieves the best average rank (1.6) on both BEDROC and EF₁%, with headline scores of BEDROC 86 and EF₁% 60.0 on EGFR, and EF₁% 57.0 on the structurally challenging ACES target. On the 10M-compound CDK7 PU dataset, SubDyve reaches BEDROC 83.44, EF₀.₅% 155.31, and EF₁% 97.59, outperforming the best fingerprint+NP baseline (RDKit+NP: BEDROC 79.04, EF₁% 89.24) and the PU-learning method GRAB (BEDROC 40.68, EF₁% 45.21) by over 2× in early enrichment. The method also runs ~12.3× faster than AutoDock Vina and requires 15× less memory than DrugCLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SubDyve achieves the best average rank (1.6) on both BEDROC and EF₁% across all 10 DUD-E targets, outperforming DrugCLIP (rank 2.4/2.0), MoLFormer (3.0/3.3), PharmacoMatch (4.5/4.4), and AutoDock Vina (4.0/4.0). On the CDK7 PU dataset, SubDyve (BEDROC 83.44, EF₁% 97.59) surpasses the best fingerprint+NP baseline RDKit+NP (79.04, 89.24) and the PU-learning method GRAB (40.68, 45.21) by >2× in EF₁% and >80% in BEDROC. Against foundation-model baselines, SubDyve improves EF₀.₅% by 38× over PSICHIC (155.31 vs 4.07) and achieves a BEDROC nearly 9× higher (83.44 vs 9.37); BIND performs poorly (EF₁₀% = 0.04) likely due to distribution mismatch. SubDyve also outperforms the 3D pharmacophore method PharmacoMatch by large margins on most targets (e.g., EGFR: BEDROC 86 vs 11; ACES: EF₁% 57.0 vs 8.4), though DrugCLIP retains the edge on FA10 (BEDROC 86 vs 58) and THRB (BEDROC 83 vs 61). The ablation confirms both subgraph network construction and LFDR refinement are necessary: removing either component drops BEDROC from 83.44 to ≤79.04.

## Relation to Prior Work

SubDyve extends the network propagation (NP) framework for label-efficient virtual screening (Yi et al., 2023) by replacing generic molecular fingerprints (ECFP, MACCS) with a subgraph-aware similarity graph built from class-discriminative substructures mined via Supervised Subgraph Mining (Lim et al., 2023). It further departs from prior NP methods by introducing an iterative seed refinement loop calibrated by local false discovery rate (LFDR, Efron 2005), which provides a provable FDR bound on the expanded seed set and directly addresses the topological bias that inflates false positives in dense graph regions. The GNN-based refinement step integrates a composite loss (weighted BCE + RankNet + InfoNCE), borrowing from recommendation and contrastive learning literature, to jointly optimize classification, ranking, and embedding objectives during seed expansion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a practically critical gap in early-stage drug discovery: retrieving actives from billion-scale libraries when only a handful of known actives exist, while controlling false positives that waste experimental resources. The LFDR-based seed refinement provides a statistical guarantee (FDR ≤ α) absent from prior NP methods, and the subgraph-aware network construction captures activity-relevant structural motifs that generic fingerprints blur. Key limitations include the target-specific formulation (multi-target transfer is only briefly explored), the ligand-only setting without protein structural information, and the dependence on at least some homologous protein annotations for zero-shot seed generation. Open questions include whether the framework extends to truly novel targets with no close homologs, integration with protein-ligand interaction features, and whether the subgraph mining step can be made more scalable for very large compound libraries.
