## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit limited visual-spatial reasoning because visual tokens are overshadowed by language tokens in the attention mechanism, preventing the model from consistently recognizing the same visual cues across frames. This leads to hallucinated or visually inconsistent predictions when reasoning about relative positions, shared structures, or spatial references across multiple visual inputs. The paper identifies the absence of a mechanism to reliably preserve and amplify shared visual evidence within attention as the core bottleneck.

## Input / Output

VideoAnchor operates at test time on a sequence of video frames (8–64 frames) or multi-view images paired with a text question, producing a text response (numerical or multiple-choice). The module is plug-and-play: it requires no retraining and is inserted into every Transformer block of the backbone MLLM (InternVL2, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-Video, LLaVA-OneVision). The only additional computation is an ADMM-solved SSC step over the flattened visual tokens (O(N²) complexity), which adds roughly 0.8 s per sample on an NVIDIA V100.

## Method

VideoAnchor consists of two units that connect the self-expressiveness property of Sparse Subspace Clustering (SSC) to Transformer attention. The Subspace-to-Scaler Unit solves an ℓ₁-regularized self-expression problem via ADMM to obtain a coefficient matrix W, applies spectral clustering on W+Wᵀ to assign subspace labels, and computes a per-token 'sharing expression score' that multiplies subspace cardinality by self-expression row-sum, then normalizes it to [0,1] and converts it into multiplicative scalers γ_Q, γ_K, γ_V (text tokens receive zero boost). The Attention Regularization Unit injects these scalers into every attention block by (i) applying a pairwise gate γ_Q·γ_Kᵀ to the post-exponential QK similarities (equivalently adding log(γ_Q·γ_Kᵀ) to the pre-softmax logits) and (ii) per-token amplifying the value vectors by γ_V, thereby concentrating probability mass and representational capacity on structurally representative, widely shared visual anchor tokens while leaving textual tokens unmodified.

## Results

On VSI-Bench (the primary visual-spatial reasoning benchmark), VideoAnchor achieves consistent gains across all tested MLLMs: +3.2% for InternVL2-8B (34.6→37.8), +2.8% for InternVL2-4B (31.7→34.5), and +1.5% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B (31.8→33.3). On All-Angles-Bench (multi-view spatial reasoning), the largest gain is +3.1% for LLaVA-Video-72B (49.9→53.0). On Video-MME spatial tasks, Qwen2.5VL-72B improves by +4.6% (75.4→80.0) and InternVL2.5-8B by +4.5% (69.9→74.4), confirming that the plug-and-play module generalizes across model scales and input modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the unmodified MLLM backbones (InternVL2, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-Video, LLaVA-OneVision), where VideoAnchor consistently improves average scores by +0.9 to +3.2 on VSI-Bench and +1.2 to +3.1 on All-Angles-Bench, with gains scaling with model size. The only external method directly compared is Video-R1 (Feng et al., 2025), a GRPO-fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B model. At 16 frames (Video-R1's training setting), Video-R1 scores 34.6 vs. VideoAnchor's 33.3, a 1.3-point loss. However, at 32 frames VideoAnchor clearly surpasses Video-R1 (37.4 vs. 35.8), and at 64 frames it edges ahead (37.4 vs. 37.1), demonstrating superior generalization across frame sampling strategies. The paper also shows that a simple text prompt ('Please anchor common objects across frames') yields only +0.4 to +0.5, far below VideoAnchor's +2.8 to +3.2, confirming that the subspace-based attention mechanism provides substantially more benefit than linguistic guidance alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoAnchor draws a novel conceptual bridge between the self-expressiveness property in Sparse Subspace Clustering (Ji et al., 2017; Zhang et al., 2019) and the self-attention mechanism in Transformers, using SSC to identify semantically coherent visual anchors across frames and then injecting them as multiplicative scalers into Q/K/V. It positions itself within the training-free MLLM enhancement literature (DC², ControlMLLM, VisionFuse) but departs from those methods by operating at the level of subspace-structured token groups rather than individual patches or manually specified regions, and by requiring no additional encoders or recursive partitioning. The approach is applied to existing MLLM backbones (InternVL2, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-Video, LLaVA-OneVision) without any retraining, distinguishing it from video-centric fine-tuning methods like Video-R1 that incur heavy training costs and risk overfitting to specific frame configurations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-identified failure mode in MLLMs—visual tokens being overshadowed by textual priors during attention—and offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic fix that practitioners can adopt without retraining. The SSC-to-attention connection provides a principled, interpretable mechanism for identifying which visual tokens carry cross-frame structural evidence, which could inform future attention design beyond this specific plug-in. Limitations remain: SSC's tendency to merge near-identical instances hampers counting, the method cannot recover information absent from sampled frames, and the O(N²) ADMM solver (mitigated by GPU acceleration and relaxed convergence) may become a bottleneck for very long videos. Open questions include whether the subspace partitioning generalizes to 3D spatial reasoning or embodied navigation settings, and whether the fixed 24-subspace choice transfers across domains.
