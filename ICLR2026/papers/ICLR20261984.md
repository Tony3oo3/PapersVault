## Problem

Monocular Gaussian Splatting SLAM is bottlenecked by the Train-from-Scratch paradigm, which requires dozens to hundreds of optimization iterations per keyframe, capping performance at ~1 FPS. Additionally, single-frame geometric priors lack inter-frame scale consistency, and vanilla 3DGS produces noisy geometry with floater artifacts. Feed-forward reconstruction models offer consistent multi-frame geometry but require offline batch processing, making them incompatible with the streaming, low-latency requirements of real-time SLAM.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a monocular RGB video stream in a streaming fashion, processing one frame at a time with a persistent hidden state that aggregates multi-frame context. For each frame it outputs an SE(3) camera pose relative to the submap origin, a dense per-pixel 2DGS attribute map (position, color, opacity, rotation, 2D scale) in the local camera frame, and an updated hidden state. The backend fuses these into a globally consistent 2DGS map that supports real-time rendering of novel RGB and depth views. The stream is partitioned into submaps of ~8 frames to manage recurrent drift, with one-frame overlaps providing inter-submap alignment constraints.

## Method

Flash-Mono replaces per-frame Gaussian optimization with a recurrent feed-forward transformer (initialized from CUT3R) that jointly predicts camera pose and per-pixel 2DGS surfel attributes in a single forward pass conditioned on a hidden state, using a ViT encoder, two cross-attention decoders, and DPT heads. The hidden state doubles as a compact submap descriptor: upon loop detection, a single conditional forward pass on the current frame with a cached past hidden state yields a Sim(3) relocalization constraint, which feeds into GTSAM-based pose graph optimization for global drift correction. The backend adopts a Predict-and-Refine strategy, applying only 20 lightweight refinement iterations (vs. 250+ in prior methods) after adaptive voxelization and map fusion. 2D Gaussian surfels replace 3D ellipsoids to enforce a stronger surface prior, and a rigid-transform correction strategy efficiently warps the map after loop closure without costly re-rendering.

## Results

Flash-Mono achieves state-of-the-art tracking accuracy with ATE RMSE of 8.89–13.00 cm on ScanNetV1 and 7.34–12.36 cm on BundleFusion, outperforming the closest feed-forward baseline MASt3R-SLAM (9.28–15.83 cm) and all optimization-based GS-SLAM methods by a wide margin. On rendering quality, Flash-Mono achieves the lowest Depth L1 error (0.34 m on ScanNet, 0.21 m on BundleFusion) while using only 20 refinement iterations per keyframe—a 10× reduction over MonoGS and S3PO-GS (250 iterations). On the outdoor KITTI benchmark, Flash-Mono achieves ATE RMSE of 9.93–45.25 m, substantially outperforming S3PO-GS (16.43–64.74 m, with one complete failure), and delivers higher PSNR (15.13–17.89 dB vs. 13.55–17.25 dB) and lower LPIPS across all sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flash-Mono outperforms all seven baselines on tracking (ATE RMSE) across both ScanNetV1 and BundleFusion. Against the closest competitor MASt3R-SLAM (a feed-forward SLAM system), Flash-Mono wins on 9 of 11 scenes, with the largest margin on ScanNet 0106 (10.83 vs. 15.83 cm) and BundleFusion copyroom (7.34 vs. 9.28 cm); it loses narrowly on ScanNet 0233 (12.13 vs. 10.99 cm) and BundleFusion apt0 (11.44 vs. 9.65 cm). Against optimization-based GS-SLAM methods (MonoGS, DepthGS, S3PO-GS), Flash-Mono achieves 5–20× lower ATE on most scenes—for example, 10.83 cm vs. 150.89 cm (MonoGS) and 26.15 cm (S3PO-GS) on ScanNet 0106. On rendering, Flash-Mono achieves the best Depth L1 (0.34 m ScanNet, 0.21 m BundleFusion) while using only 20 refinement iterations versus 250 for MonoGS and S3PO-GS. On KITTI outdoor sequences, Flash-Mono beats S3PO-GS on all six sequences (e.g., 9.93 m vs. 16.43 m on seq 06) and avoids the complete failure S3PO-GS suffers on seq 07. Photo-SLAM and other dense SLAM baselines (SplaTAM, Gaussian-SLAM, GS-ICP-SLAM) are compared only on map compactness (Gaussian count on TUM), where Flash-Mono (0.61–0.98M) is competitive with MonoGS (26–44k) but uses more primitives than the most compact methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Train-from-Scratch paradigm in monocular GS-SLAM (MonoGS, DepthGS, S3PO-GS), where Gaussians are randomly initialized and optimized per keyframe, and instead adopts a Predict-and-Refine approach inspired by the recurrent feed-forward reconstruction line (CUT3R, VGGT). The recurrent transformer architecture with a persistent hidden state is directly inherited from CUT3R, but the paper repurposes this hidden state as a compact submap descriptor for loop closure relocalization—a use not explored in prior feed-forward reconstruction work. The choice of 2D Gaussian surfels over 3DGS ellipsoids follows the geometric-accuracy motivation established by 2DGS (Huang et al., 2024), and the Sim(3) pose graph optimization framework is standard in monocular SLAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× speedup (from ~1 FPS to 10+ FPS) makes monocular GS-SLAM viable for real-time embodied perception, a regime previously inaccessible to optimization-based methods. The hidden-state-based loop closure is a conceptually elegant mechanism that avoids expensive feature matching or PnP, though its robustness under large appearance changes remains demonstrated only in a single case study. Key open questions include the practicality of a 795M-parameter model requiring ~3 GB VRAM for edge deployment, the long-term stability of the recurrent hidden state over very long sequences, and whether the life-long mapping extension (discussed in Appendix G) can be made systematic rather than ad hoc.
