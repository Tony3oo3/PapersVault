## Problem

Implicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods offer token-efficient reasoning in LLMs but suffer from a persistent performance gap compared to explicit CoT. The paper identifies a core latent instability issue: when scaling the number of implicit reasoning tokens beyond a small default (e.g., from 3 to 5), training becomes unstable and collapses because latent representations become homogeneous and lose semantic diversity, caused by insufficient step-level supervision in existing methods.

## Input / Output

The method takes a math word problem as text input and produces a final numeric answer as output, with no explicit reasoning chain generated at inference. Internally, the model autoregressively generates K continuous d-dimensional latent vectors (one per reasoning step) that are appended to the context before switching to explicit vocabulary decoding for the answer. Each latent corresponds to two implicit tokens in the Coconut convention, and the total decoding length is T + K + L_a (input length + implicit steps + answer length), far shorter than explicit CoT trajectories.

## Method

SIM-CoT introduces step-level supervision via an auxiliary decoder (architecturally identical to the LLM) that, during training only, takes each implicit latent z_k as a conditioning prefix and autoregressively generates the corresponding k-th explicit reasoning step s_k. The total training objective is a weighted sum of step-level cross-entropy (through the decoder, grounding each latent to specific reasoning content) and answer-level cross-entropy (through the base LLM). At inference, the decoder is discarded entirely, so runtime equals standard implicit CoT with no added overhead. The method is plug-and-play: it can be layered on top of Coconut (answer-level supervision), CODI (trajectory-level distillation), or training-free approaches like soft thinking, and boundary conditions handle cases where the number of reasoning steps N differs from the fixed latent budget K (many-to-one mapping when N > K, answer-alignment for surplus latents when N < K).

## Results

On GPT-2, SIM-CoT (Coconut backbone) achieves 44.8% accuracy on GSM8k-Aug, surpassing the explicit CoT baseline SFT-CoT (42.7%) by 2.1 points while using only 12.2 tokens vs. 27.6 (2.3× fewer). On LLaMA-3.2 1B, SIM-CoT (CODI backbone) reaches 56.1% on GSM8k-Aug, a +3.4 point gain over CODI alone (52.7%), and 64.1% on LLaMA-3.1 8B (+3.0 over CODI). Out-of-domain, SIM-CoT consistently improves average accuracy: +4.3 points over Coconut and +0.3 over CODI on GPT-2, and +9.0 and +1.0 respectively on LLaMA-1B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against iCoT (Deng et al., 2024), SIM-CoT with Coconut backbone achieves 44.8% vs. 30.1% on GSM8k-Aug (+14.7 points) and 46.9% vs. 30.2% average OOD accuracy (+16.7 points), a substantial margin reflecting iCoT's reliance on curriculum-based internalization without explicit latent supervision. Against Coconut (Hao et al., 2025), SIM-CoT provides a consistent +8.2 point gain on GSM8k-Aug (44.8 vs. 36.6) and +4.3 on average OOD (46.9 vs. 42.6) on GPT-2, and +9.0 on both in-domain and OOD on LLaMA-1B (42.2 vs. 33.2), confirming that step-level supervision directly addresses Coconut's latent instability. Against CODI (Shen et al., 2025), the gains are smaller but positive: +0.6 on GSM8k-Aug and +0.3 on average OOD for GPT-2, growing to +3.4 and +1.0 on LLaMA-1B, and +3.0 on LLaMA-3.1 8B, indicating that step-level supervision complements CODI's trajectory-level distillation most effectively at larger model scales. Notably, SIM-CoT is the first implicit CoT method to surpass the explicit SFT-CoT baseline on GPT-2 (44.8% vs. 42.7%) while using 2.3× fewer tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIM-CoT sits at the intersection of two lines of implicit CoT work: Coconut's answer-level supervision (Hao et al., 2025) and CODI's trajectory-level distillation (Shen et al., 2025). Both prior methods leave intermediate latent tokens weakly constrained, and SIM-CoT departs by introducing step-level supervision through an auxiliary decoder that aligns each latent with its corresponding explicit reasoning step during training. The paper also positions itself relative to training-free latent construction (Soft Thinking) and knowledge-internalization approaches (iCoT), but its core contribution is a new loss formulation that distributes supervision across individual latent positions rather than treating the latent chain as a monolithic unit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The latent instability diagnosis—sharp collapse when scaling implicit tokens from 3 to 5—is a practical barrier that has likely hindered broader adoption of implicit CoT, and the geometric analysis (distance shrinkage, vocabulary drift, semantic homogenization) gives a clear mechanistic account of why it happens. The plug-and-play nature of the fix (auxiliary decoder removed at inference) makes it immediately applicable to existing pipelines. However, evaluation is confined to arithmetic word problems (GSM8K family) and models up to 8B, leaving open whether step-level supervision generalizes to longer-horizon reasoning, non-math domains, or RL-based training regimes. The modest gains over CODI on larger models (+1.5 to +3.0%) also suggest diminishing returns as the backbone itself becomes stronger.
