## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards for LLM reasoning provides only binary correctness feedback, which fails to distinguish concise correct answers from verbose ones or partially correct reasoning from completely irrelevant output. This coarse signal induces 'overthinking'—unnecessary elaboration that increases latency and cost—while existing length-penalty remedies sacrifice accuracy by uniformly suppressing tokens regardless of their informational content. The paper asks whether an internally computed, content-aware reward can simultaneously improve reasoning accuracy and efficiency without external reward models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text math or logic question as input and produces a free-form reasoning chain ending in a final numerical answer (integer or fraction), parsed from the last number in the output. The self-aligned reward is computed entirely from the model's own token-level log-probabilities: it requires the answer's perplexity both conditioned on the query (already available from the RL pipeline's KL penalty computation) and standalone (one additional forward pass with the query removed). No external reward model, human annotation, or answer-format constraint is needed at inference time.

## Method

Self-Aligned Reward (SAR) is defined as the relative perplexity drop of an answer when conditioned on the query: R_SA = clip((ppl(a) − ppl(a|q)) / ppl(a), −1, 1), where ppl(a) is the standalone perplexity and ppl(a|q) is the conditioned perplexity. This is combined additively with the binary verifiable reward (R = R_VR + α·R_SA) inside PPO or Dr.GRPO, so the advantage signal now reflects both correctness and query-answer alignment. At the token level, SAR decomposes into per-token importance scores v(a_j) = log P(a_j|q, a_{<j}) / P(a_j|a_{<j}), which are high for tokens that extract novel information from the query and low or negative for redundant repetitions—naturally favoring concise, query-specific reasoning while penalizing memorized or irrelevant text. The computation adds only one forward pass per rollout (for ppl(a)), making it negligible in training cost, and the α hyperparameter provides a tunable accuracy–efficiency trade-off that achieves a Pareto-optimal frontier over length-based baselines.

## Results

Across 4 base models (Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, Phi-3.5-mini, Gemma3-1B) and 5 math benchmarks, SA-GRPO achieves the highest average accuracy while reducing average response length by ~30% compared to vanilla GRPO. For example, on Qwen3-4B, SA-GRPO reaches 71.41% average accuracy with 894 tokens vs. GRPO's 69.07% with 1031 tokens. The Accuracy-Efficiency Score (AES) is consistently highest for SA-GRPO across all models (e.g., 1.564 for Qwen3-4B vs. 1.165 for GRPO and 1.596 for GRPO-ER). On out-of-domain logical reasoning (LogicBench, ProntoQA), SA-GRPO also outperforms all baselines on accuracy in most settings while maintaining shorter responses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA-GRPO outperforms vanilla GRPO on both accuracy and efficiency across all 4 models: e.g., on Qwen3-4B it gains +2.34% accuracy (71.41% vs. 69.07%) while reducing length by 13% (894 vs. 1031 tokens), and on Gemma3-1B it gains +0.46% accuracy while cutting length by 46% (1017 vs. 1866 tokens). Against the length-based baselines GRPO-O1 and GRPO-ER, SA-GRPO achieves strictly higher accuracy (e.g., 71.41% vs. 67.75% for O1 and 67.38% for ER on Qwen3-4B) while producing responses of comparable or shorter length, establishing a Pareto-optimal frontier. SA-PPO similarly dominates PPO on both axes (e.g., 52.49% acc / 624 tokens vs. 48.95% / 930 tokens on Qwen3-1.7B). In the ablation (Table 5), SA-GRPO outperforms the entropy-minimization reward (R_EM) by +15.95% accuracy and −27% length on Qwen3-4B, confirming that conditioned perplexity drop is a more effective internal signal than raw entropy. On out-of-domain logical reasoning (LogicBench, ProntoQA), SA-GRPO wins on accuracy in 5/8 and 4/8 columns respectively while maintaining shorter responses than GRPO-O1 and GRPO-ER in all cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-with-verifiable-rewards paradigm (PPO, GRPO/Dr.GRPO) by introducing a perplexity-based internal signal that measures the relative drop in answer perplexity when conditioned on the query versus standalone. It departs from length-penalty approaches (O1-pruner, Efficient Reasoner) that are content-blind and sacrifice accuracy, and from entropy-minimization methods (Agarwal et al., 2025) that measure self-confidence but not query-answer alignment. The key conceptual move is reframing efficiency as a query-conditioning property: tokens that are highly predictable given the query but unpredictable without it are 'valuable,' while repeated or query-independent tokens are penalized. This positions SAR as the first fine-grained, content-aware, internally-computed reward that simultaneously targets correctness and conciseness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Pareto-optimal accuracy-efficiency frontier demonstrated across four model families and seven benchmarks is practically significant: it shows that efficient reasoning need not come at the cost of accuracy, a trade-off that has constrained prior length-penalty methods. The signal requires only one additional forward pass, making it nearly free to integrate into existing RL pipelines (VERL, etc.). However, the paper reveals a clear limitation: SAR's advantages diminish substantially for vision-language models, suggesting the text-centric perplexity signal cannot capture errors in visual grounding. Open questions include whether hybrid multimodal reward components can extend SAR to VLMs, and whether the signal generalizes beyond math/logic to open-ended generation tasks where 'concise and query-aligned' is harder to define.
