## Problem

Existing video generation models for robotics face a fundamental trade-off: pixel-based diffusion models produce temporally coherent but geometrically inconsistent videos (flickering, object deformation), while 3D-aware approaches enforce spatial structure but are limited to simple static scenes. For robot manipulation, both temporal coherence and cross-view 3D consistency are essential to extract accurate end-effector trajectories, yet no prior method unifies these objectives for multi-object, dynamic manipulation scenarios.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB-D image (not a video sequence) from each of two camera views as conditioning, with the latest observation repeated 10 times to match the prediction horizon. It outputs future 4D pointmaps (W×H×3 per-pixel 3D coordinates) in the reference view v_n's coordinate frame for both views, plus separate RGB video sequences per view. Notably, camera poses are required only during training to define the cross-view projection; at inference the model predicts pointmaps in the reference frame purely from RGB-D observations, bypassing extrinsics calibration for novel viewpoints.

## Method

The architecture extends Stable Video Diffusion's U-Net with two key additions: (1) a Pointmap VAE (initialized from the RGB VAE and fine-tuned on pointmap data) that encodes/decodes 3D pointmaps into the same latent space as RGB, and (2) two separate U-Net decoders—one per view—connected by 12 cross-attention layers inserted after each decoder block, where view v_m's decoder attends to v_n's features to learn geometric correspondences. Training uses a joint diffusion loss combining standard RGB denoising with a DUSt3R-inspired cross-view pointmap alignment loss: view v_m's pointmaps are expressed in v_n's coordinate frame, and the model is supervised to minimize the difference between the two views' 3D predictions. A gripper-region re-weighting mechanism (doubling the loss in gripper mask areas) improves prediction accuracy where pose extraction is most sensitive. The full U-Net is fine-tuned from SVD pretrained weights at 1×10⁻⁶ learning rate over ~60 epochs, with the VAE encoders frozen.

## Results

On three simulation manipulation tasks and a real-world multi-task dataset, the proposed method achieves the best cross-view 3D consistency (mIoU 0.64–0.70 in simulation, 0.56 real-world), lowest FVD (377–561 in simulation, 331–384 real-world), and best depth accuracy (AbsRel as low as 0.03, δ₁ up to 0.98). For robot policy, the method achieves 64% average task success rate across three simulation tasks with novel camera views, substantially outperforming Dreamitate (12%), Diffusion Policy (12%), and DP3 (25%). The multi-view cross-attention ablation drops mIoU by 30–60% and degrades depth metrics significantly, confirming its critical role in cross-view geometric alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all baselines on cross-view 3D consistency (mIoU) and depth accuracy across all four evaluation settings. Against 4D Gaussian (Wang et al., 2024a), the gap is largest: FVD is roughly 2–3× lower (e.g., 411 vs. 1208 on Task 1) and mIoU is nearly double (0.70 vs. 0.39), because 4D Gaussian reconstructs a 4D scene from a single generated video and cannot synthesize novel views well. Against SVD and SVD w/MV attn, the proposed method achieves lower FVD in most settings (e.g., 411 vs. 977/942 on Task 1) and additionally provides depth and cross-view metrics that those RGB-only baselines cannot report. The ablation without multi-view cross-attention (OURS w/o MV attn) drops mIoU by 30–60% (e.g., 0.70→0.41 on Task 1, 0.64→0.26 on Task 3) and degrades AbsRel-m substantially, confirming that cross-attention is essential for learning the coordinate-frame transformation between views. For robot policy (Table 2), the method achieves 64% average success rate, more than 2× the best baseline DP3 (25%) and over 5× Dreamitate (12%) and Diffusion Policy (12%), demonstrating that geometry-consistent 4D video generation translates directly into more reliable manipulation under novel camera viewpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: latent video diffusion (building on Stable Video Diffusion) and cross-view geometric supervision (adapted from DUSt3R's pointmap alignment). Prior 4D generation works (Xie et al. 2024, Li et al. 2024a, Zhang et al. 2024) enforce 3D consistency but are restricted to single-object, static-background scenes, while pixel-based video models capture temporal dynamics but produce geometrically inconsistent outputs. The key architectural departure is the introduction of multi-view cross-attention between separate U-Net decoders, allowing the second view's pointmap prediction to attend to the reference view's geometric features, combined with a joint RGB + pointmap diffusion loss that simultaneously optimizes temporal coherence and cross-view 3D alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical pipeline from geometry-aware video generation to robot manipulation via off-the-shelf 6DoF pose tracking, achieving 64% average success rate versus 12–25% for behavior-cloning baselines on novel camera views. However, the 30-second inference time per 10-frame prediction severely limits closed-loop deployment, and the method requires multi-view RGB-D training data that is expensive to collect in real-world settings. Open questions include whether the geometric supervision can be transferred to faster generative backbones (flow matching, autoregressive transformers), whether depth can be replaced by monocular estimation, and whether the approach scales beyond two views without significant quality degradation.
