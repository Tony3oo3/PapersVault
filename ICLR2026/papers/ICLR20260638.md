## Problem

Conditional Semantic Textual Similarity (C-STS) requires fine-grained, condition-dependent reasoning that existing discriminative models (SFT, contrastive learning) handle only indirectly, and no prior work has applied an end-to-end LLM-based cross-encoder or reinforcement learning to this task. Naively applying listwise RL to C-STS fails because the coarse, batch-level ranking reward is too complex for a model that has not yet learned basic scoring rules, and a single reward across all completions provides no precise credit assignment for individual predictions.

## Input / Output

The model receives a unified prompt containing an instruction, K few-shot demonstrations, and a query consisting of two text segments plus a natural language condition. It generates a free-form completion from which a binary judgment ('yes' or 'no') and an integer score on a 1–5 Likert scale are parsed, with the required output format being the judgment followed by the score in parentheses wrapped in answer tags. The C-STS dataset has a distinctive paired structure where adjacent samples share the same text segments but differ in condition and maintain a strict ordinal relationship between their labels, which the method exploits for pairwise reward computation.

## Method

PoLi-RL is a two-stage RL curriculum built on DAPO (an extension of GRPO) applied to Qwen3 cross-encoders. Stage I grounds the model in basic scoring using a pointwise accuracy reward (normalized distance to ground truth), a binary judgment reward (similar vs. dissimilar), and a format reward. Stage II refines fine-grained distinctions via a hybrid reward combining the pointwise anchor with pairwise and listwise ranking rewards, both computed through the Parallel Slice Ranking Reward (PSRR) mechanism. PSRR restructures the N×G generated completions into G parallel slices (each slice containing the j-th completion from all N samples), then assigns each completion a differentiated reward based on its rank deviation from the ideal rank within its slice, enabling granular credit assignment that a flat batch-level ranking cannot provide. The pairwise reward additionally leverages the dataset's paired ordinal structure, giving a base reward for correct directionality plus a penalty proportional to prediction error magnitude.

## Results

On the official C-STS benchmark, PoLi-RL (Qwen3-8B) achieves a Spearman correlation of 48.18 (Pearson 48.27), establishing a new SOTA for the cross-encoder architecture and surpassing the previous best SEAVER (43.83) by 4.35 points. The 0.6B variant (44.34) already outperforms GPT-4 (43.6) and SEAVER, while the 4B variant reaches 46.23. On the re-annotated C-STS dataset, PoLi-RL (8B) achieves 76.08 Spearman, and on WMT-QE 2020 (en-zh), the PSRR mechanism alone yields 54.33 Spearman versus 50.90 for SFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoLi-RL (8B) outperforms the previous cross-encoder SOTA SEAVER (43.83) by 4.35 Spearman points and the strongest proprietary few-shot model GPT-4o (44.23) by 3.95 points. Against DeepSeek-R1 (42.85), the 8B model gains 5.33 points, and even the 0.6B variant (44.34) surpasses GPT-4 (43.6) and SEAVER (43.83). Compared to its own SFT baseline on Qwen3-8B (40.42), PoLi-RL yields a 7.76-point improvement, and over few-shot inference (37.9) a 10.28-point gain. On the re-annotated C-STS dataset, PoLi-RL (76.08) beats QA-SimCSE (72.9) by 3.18 points and its own SFT (72.09) by 3.99 points. On WMT-QE 2020, the PSRR mechanism alone (54.33) outperforms SFT (50.90) by 3.43 points, confirming generalizability beyond C-STS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DAPO reinforcement learning framework (Yu et al., 2025), which extends GRPO's group-relative advantage estimation, and applies it to the Conditional Semantic Textual Similarity (C-STS) task introduced by Deshpande et al. (2023). Prior C-STS work is confined to discriminative architectures (bi-encoder, tri-encoder, cross-encoder) trained with SFT or contrastive learning, and LLMs have only been used via few-shot prompting or as feature extractors. PoLi-RL departs from this lineage by being the first end-to-end LLM-based cross-encoder for C-STS and the first to employ RL, introducing the Parallel Slice Ranking Reward (PSRR) mechanism to solve the coarse credit-assignment problem that causes naive listwise RL to fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PSRR mechanism provides a generalizable recipe for computing fine-grained ranking rewards in multi-candidate generation settings, validated beyond C-STS on WMT-QE 2020. The finding that a 0.6B model trained with PoLi-RL (44.34) outperforms GPT-4 few-shot (43.6) suggests that explicit alignment with ranking objectives can substitute for raw parameter scale on conditional judgment tasks. Open questions include whether the two-stage curriculum is strictly necessary or whether alternative reward schedules achieve similar results, and whether PSRR extends to tasks with continuous label spaces or non-paired data structures beyond the simplified WMT-QE validation.
