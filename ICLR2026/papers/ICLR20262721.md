## Problem

Existing data filtering methods for vision-language models evaluate each sample independently against a quality threshold, overlooking higher-order interactions such as redundancy among high-quality samples and the value of low-scoring samples that capture rare concepts. This per-sample strategy produces imbalanced datasets that limit model generalization, because it cannot reason about the distribution of underlying concepts at the set level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262721/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large pool of image-caption pairs (128M from DataComp-medium), a target dataset (e.g., ImageNet-1K training set) whose concept distribution the selected subset should match, precomputed CLIP ViT-L/14 image embeddings (768-dim, frozen encoder), and per-sample NegCLIPLoss quality scores. It outputs a filtered subset (e.g., 18M pairs) selected via CPU-based stochastic greedy optimization; the quality score is not used directly but quantized into three bins with learned weights, and the final subset is the intersection of 5 independent stochastic greedy runs to reduce variance.

## Method

SDM (Submodular Distribution Matching) is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a k-SAE (k=128, d_sparse=98,304) is trained on frozen CLIP embeddings with a novel monotonicity loss that contrasts heterogeneous vs. homogeneous image sets to encourage features whose activations scale monotonically with concept presence; (2) an empirical distribution over sparse features is computed from the target dataset; (3) a subset is selected by maximizing a feature-based submodular function that minimizes KL divergence between the selected set's concept distribution and the target, combined with a binned quality term. The paper proves the distribution-matching objective is a Difference-of-Submodular (DS) maximization problem, then derives a tractable submodular lower bound exploiting the k-SAE's sparsity (each sample activates at most k features with bounded magnitude), enabling efficient stochastic greedy optimization with (1−1/e−ε) approximation guarantees.

## Results

On the DataComp-medium benchmark (128M image-caption pairs), SDM selects 18M samples and achieves 35.2% zero-shot accuracy on ImageNet-1K and 36.4% average across 38 downstream tasks, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art NCL∩NormSim (22M samples) by 2.5% on ImageNet-1K and 0.7% on average while using 33% fewer samples. When combined with external models (SDM+), the method reaches 39.2% on ImageNet-1K and 39.2% average, ranking second on the full DataComp-medium leaderboard. The full pipeline requires approximately 120 A100 hours total (60 for features, 15 for SAE training, 5 for quality scores, 40 for CLIP training), which is over 5× fewer GPU hours than the Metagradient Descent approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDM outperforms all CLIP-only baselines in Table 1, most notably beating the previous state-of-the-art NCL∩NormSim(Target) by 2.5% on ImageNet-1K (35.2% vs. 32.7%) and 0.7% on average (36.4% vs. 35.7%) while selecting 33% fewer samples (18M vs. 22M). Against the simpler DataComp baselines (CLIP-Score, Image-Based, Text-Based, Basic Filtering), SDM gains 3.4–10.6% on ImageNet-1K and 3.6–7.9% on average. D2 Pruning, which optimizes for diversity and difficulty via graph message passing, is outperformed by 11.1% on ImageNet-1K and 6.6% on average. When external models are available (Table 4), SDM+ (combined with NCL+DFN+HYPE ensemble) achieves 39.2% on ImageNet-1K and 39.2% average, surpassing NCL+DFN+HYPE alone (38.2%/38.8%), DFN (37.1%/37.3%), HYPE (34.6%/37.3%), and TMARS+SSFT (33.8%/36.2%). The only method exceeding SDM+ on average is Metagradient Descent (40.2%), but it achieves only 27.0% on ImageNet-1K and requires over 5× more GPU hours. M-FLYT+SCS achieves a higher ImageNet-1K score (40.1%) but a lower average (37.7%), indicating SDM+ offers a more balanced profile across tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—sparse autoencoders for feature disentanglement (Makhzani & Frey, 2014; Gao et al., 2025) and submodular feature-based functions for set selection (Stobbe & Krause, 2010; Bilmes & Bai, 2017)—into a single data-filtering pipeline. It departs from the dominant paradigm in DataComp filtering, where each sample is scored independently (CLIP-Score, NegCLIPLoss, NormSim) and retained above a threshold, by reframing selection as a set-level distribution-matching problem over disentangled concept features. The monotonicity loss is a direct adaptation of the peripteral loss from Bhatt et al. (2024), repurposed here to make SAE activations behave like concept counts rather than arbitrary activations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDM provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to ad-hoc quality-threshold filtering that achieves state-of-the-art DataComp-medium results using only a single CLIP model—no external curated data or dedicated filtering networks—while requiring over 5× fewer GPU hours than the leading Metagradient Descent approach. The ablation showing a 12% ImageNet drop when replacing SAE features with dense CLIP embeddings underscores that disentangled, monotone representations are not merely interpretable but functionally critical for set-level selection. Open questions include how to choose the target distribution in a principled way (the paper shows performance degrades when using a 24-dataset target), whether the framework transfers to non-visual modalities, and whether the stochastic-greedy approximation remains tight at even larger scales (beyond 128M samples).
