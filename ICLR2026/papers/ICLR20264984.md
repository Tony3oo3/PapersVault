## Problem

Reliably attributing a RAG model's generated response to specific context sentences remains challenging: existing methods rely on human annotation, gradient-based feature attribution, reward-driven fine-tuning, or hundreds of forward passes to train a linear surrogate model. No training-free, gradient-free method exists that can efficiently and accurately identify which context sentences ground a response while also revealing the internal model components responsible for this attribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264984/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full context (a set of sentences), a query, and an already-generated response as input, along with an instruction-tuned LLM operating in inference mode. It produces a JSD score per context sentence (summed over all response tokens), from which the top-relevant sentence is selected by argmax. For mechanistic analysis, it additionally outputs per-attention-head and per-MLP-layer JSD scores computed via Logit Lens projections of intermediate representations into vocabulary space. The method requires exactly |C| forward passes (one per sentence ablation) plus one full-context pass, with no backpropagation or iterative masking.

## Method

ARC-JSD computes the Jensen-Shannon Divergence between the response token probability distribution under the full context and under each single-sentence-ablated context, aggregated over all generated tokens; the sentence with the highest JSD is identified as the most relevant. JSD is chosen over KL, TV, Wasserstein, and MMD because it is symmetric, finite, scale-free, and bounded in [0, log 2], enabling direct cross-layer comparison without sensitivity to zero-probability bins or arbitrary logit shifts. For mechanistic attribution, the same JSD metric is applied at the level of individual attention heads and MLP layers by projecting their outputs through the Logit Lens (unembedding + softmax) and computing JSD between full-context and top-sentence-ablated distributions. The located components are independently verified via a semantic-gain metric (cosine similarity change in the residual stream before/after each component), and the two rankings show strong Spearman correlation across all datasets and model scales. The approach requires no fine-tuning, no surrogate model, and no gradient computation, making it directly integrable into any existing RAG pipeline.

## Results

ARC-JSD achieves an average top-1 context attribution accuracy improvement of approximately 10.7% over all baselines across TyDi QA, Hotpot QA, and MuSiQue, using four instruction-tuned LLMs (Qwen2-1.5B/7B-IT, Gemma2-2B/9B-IT). It also delivers up to a 3-fold computational speedup compared to ContextCite and gradient-based baselines, while remaining Pareto-optimal across model scales. When used to gate top-5 identified attention heads and MLPs, ARC-JSD reduces the hallucination rate from 13.4% to 8.2% (~39% reduction) on Hotpot QA without degrading factual F1 (76.1 → 75.9). GPT-4.1-as-judge semantic equivalence between correctly attributed responses and gold answers reaches up to 99.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARC-JSD is compared against three training-free baselines: ALTI-Logit (Ferrando et al., 2023), a layerwise logit-difference method; MIRAGE (Qi et al., 2024), a gradient-based contrastive feature attribution method; and ContextCite (Cohen-Wang et al., 2024), which trains a linear surrogate model from 32 or 256 context-ablation forward passes. ARC-JSD consistently outperforms all three in top-1 context attribution accuracy by ~10.7% on average across MuSiQue, Hotpot QA, and TyDi QA, while requiring only |C| forward passes (one per sentence ablation) versus 32 or 256 for ContextCite and back-propagation for MIRAGE. ContextCite-32 becomes computationally cheaper than ARC-JSD only when the context exceeds 32 sentences, yet its attribution accuracy still lags behind ARC-JSD. ALTI-Logit and MIRAGE, being token-level methods, require accumulated operations to produce sentence-level predictions and are both slower and less accurate. ARC-JSD is Pareto-optimal (best accuracy at every compute level) across all four LLM backbones tested, and the advantage persists when generalising to LLaMA-3.1-8B-IT and Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-IT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the inference-time attribution assumption from Cohen-Wang et al. (2024, ContextCite) that removing grounded context segments reduces response probability, but departs by replacing the expensive linear surrogate model (trained on hundreds of ablation forward passes) with a single-pass Jensen-Shannon Divergence computation between full-context and sentence-ablated response distributions. For mechanistic analysis, it integrates the Logit Lens technique (nostalgebraist, 2020) with JSD scoring to identify specific attention heads and MLP layers, positioning itself as complementary to Sun et al. (2025) which locates hallucination sources rather than correct-response attribution drivers. The method is contrasted against gradient-based (MIRAGE), logit-difference (ALTI-Logit), and surrogate-model (ContextCite) baselines, and extends the NIAH-focused retrieval-head analysis of Wu et al. (2025a) to the more realistic paraphrasing-and-integration setting of real RAG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARC-JSD offers a drop-in, training-free attribution module that any RAG pipeline can adopt immediately, with up to 3× speedup over prior methods while improving accuracy by ~10%, making context attribution practical for long-document settings where gradient-based or surrogate approaches become prohibitive. The mechanistic identification of specific attention heads and MLP layers, verified via an independent semantic-gain metric, opens a path toward targeted hallucination mitigation (≈39% reduction demonstrated) and interpretable RAG auditing. However, the analysis remains at the layer/head level without neuron-level resolution within MLPs, and generalisation beyond QA to tasks like membership inference or summarisation is untested, leaving open whether JSD-based attribution transfers to settings where the response is not a short factual answer.
