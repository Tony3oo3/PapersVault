## Problem

Existing GraphRAG methods depend on ad-hoc graph designs, heuristic search (e.g., PageRank), or costly multi-step agent pipelines, limiting scalability and cross-domain generalization. Prior GNN-based approaches like GFM-RAG are restricted to knowledge graphs and cannot handle the diverse node types and rich text semantics found in document graphs or hierarchical graphs. The paper asks whether a single unified framework can reason over arbitrary graph-structured knowledge without task-specific graph engineering.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language query and a graph constructed from a document corpus, standardized into the QuadGraph format with four typed layers (attribute, entity, document, community) and typed edges. The GFM outputs a relevance score for every node across all four layers in a single forward pass; top-k nodes per type (k=5 for multi-hop QA, k=10 for G-bench) are extracted and formatted into a prompt for an LLM (GPT-4o-mini) that produces the final answer and a reasoning explanation. Inference latency is 0.2 s/sample on G-bench (CS), roughly 10× faster than the next-best GNN-based method.

## Method

G-reasoner introduces QuadGraph, a four-layer typed graph schema that maps heterogeneous graph constructors (HippoRAG, LightRAG, Youtu-GraphRAG) into a single representation, enabling one model to handle knowledge graphs, document graphs, and hierarchical graphs interchangeably. On top of QuadGraph, a 34M-parameter 6-layer query-dependent GNN with DistMult message passing and sum aggregation jointly encodes graph topology and text semantics: node, relation, and query embeddings are initialized from a frozen Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B encoder, and a type-specific binary predictor scores each node's relevance to the query. Training combines a supervised likelihood loss on labeled relevant nodes with a KL-divergence distillation loss against the frozen text encoder's pseudo-labels over all nodes, providing weak supervision at scale. For scalability, BFloat16 mixed-precision training (with custom CUDA backward kernels for relational message passing) yields 2.1× throughput and 17.5% memory reduction, while a METIS-based distributed message-passing scheme partitions the graph across GPUs to enable full-graph training with O(|V|/N × d) per-device memory.

## Results

G-reasoner achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six benchmarks: 61.4 EM / 76.0 F1 on HotpotQA, 38.5 EM / 52.5 F1 on MuSiQue, 74.9 EM / 82.1 F1 on 2Wiki, and 58.9 / 73.3 / 73.9 ACC on G-bench (Novel/Medical/CS). It outperforms the closest GNN-based competitor GFM-RAG by 5.2 EM on HotpotQA (61.4 vs. 56.2), 8.3 EM on MuSiQue (38.5 vs. 30.2), and 5.1 EM on 2Wiki (74.9 vs. 69.8). On retrieval, G-reasoner achieves 85.9 R@2 on HotpotQA and 93.8 evidence recall on G-bench (Medical), surpassing the next-best method by 10.3 and 11.6 points respectively. Inference latency is 0.2 s/sample on G-bench (CS), roughly 12× faster than the fastest graph-search baseline (HippoRAG at 2.4 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-reasoner outperforms all 19 baselines across every dataset where they overlap. Against the strongest non-structure retriever Qwen3-Emb-8B, it gains +8.0 EM on HotpotQA (61.4 vs. 53.4), +6.6 EM on MuSiQue (38.5 vs. 31.9), and +17.7 EM on 2Wiki (74.9 vs. 57.2), confirming that graph topology provides substantial gains over pure semantic similarity. Among graph-enhanced methods, G-reasoner beats the closest GNN-based competitor GFM-RAG by 5.2 EM on HotpotQA, 8.3 EM on MuSiQue, and 5.1 EM on 2Wiki, while also surpassing it on all three G-bench domains (e.g., 73.9 vs. 72.1 ACC on G-bench CS). It also outperforms the agent-based KAG by +1.9 EM on HotpotQA and +7.6 EM on 2Wiki, and the graph-search HippoRAG2 by +5.1 EM on HotpotQA and +14.4 EM on 2Wiki. Against multi-step RAG systems (IRCoT, R1-Searcher, Search-R1), G-reasoner achieves higher EM on all three multi-hop QA datasets while being 4–12× faster per sample (0.058–0.125 s vs. 0.5–2.1 s). On G-bench (CS), it surpasses the fastest graph-search method HippoRAG (73.9 vs. 72.6 ACC) with 12× lower latency (0.2 s vs. 2.4 s), and outperforms agent-based methods KGP, ToG, and DALK by 2.0–4.6 ACC points.

## Relation to Prior Work

G-reasoner directly extends GFM-RAG (Luo et al., 2025), which introduced an 8M-parameter GNN-powered graph foundation model for knowledge-graph-only retrieval, by generalizing it to arbitrary graph structures via the QuadGraph four-layer abstraction (attribute, entity, document, community). Architecturally, it adopts the query-dependent GNN backbone from Galkin et al. (2024) but augments it with node-level text semantics (via Qwen3-Embedding) and a knowledge-distillation objective from a frozen text encoder to handle weak supervision. Conceptually, it positions itself against the broader GraphRAG literature (HippoRAG, LightRAG, GraphRAG-MS, Youtu-GraphRAG) by replacing ad-hoc graph-specific retrievers (PageRank, agent-based tool calling, dual-level search) with a single trained GFM that generalizes across graph constructors without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified QuadGraph interface and a single 34M-parameter GFM that generalizes across knowledge graphs, document graphs, and hierarchical graphs without fine-tuning is a practical step toward a 'one model for all graph structures' paradigm in GraphRAG, reducing the engineering burden of maintaining separate pipelines per graph type. The 10× inference speedup over GFM-RAG (0.2s vs 2.0s per sample) while improving accuracy makes graph-enhanced RAG viable for latency-sensitive applications. Key limitations remain: the framework is text-only (no multi-modal graphs), the GFM and LLM are loosely coupled separate modules rather than co-trained end-to-end, and evaluation is confined to QA tasks, leaving open whether the approach transfers to agent planning or other reasoning settings.
