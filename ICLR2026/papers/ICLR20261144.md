## Problem

General-utility MDPs (GUMDPs) allow non-linear objectives over state-action occupancies, unifying tasks like exploration, imitation learning, and adversarial MDPs. However, all prior algorithms assume policy performance is evaluated over infinitely many trajectories, which is violated in practical settings where an agent is assessed on a single trajectory. This paper is the first to solve infinite-horizon discounted GUMDPs in the single-trial regime, where the objective depends on the empirical occupancy of one trajectory rather than the expected occupancy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a GUMDP specification (discrete state space S, action space A, transition matrices, initial distribution, and a general utility function f over the occupancy simplex) and, at each timestep, receives the current state paired with a running occupancy vector o ∈ R^{|S||A|} that tracks discounted empirical visitation frequencies. It outputs a single action per timestep, inducing a non-Markovian deterministic policy. The running occupancy is updated deterministically (adding γ^t to the visited state-action entry), and the sparse terminal cost f((1-γ)/(1-γ^H) · o) is only evaluated at the final timestep H, making the occupancy MDP's state space grow combinatorially with H since each state encodes a unique history.

## Method

The core contribution is an 'occupancy MDP' that augments each original state with a running occupancy vector, creating a one-to-one mapping between histories in the GUMDP and states in the occupancy MDP. This equivalence means non-Markovian policies for the GUMDP correspond exactly to stationary policies for the occupancy MDP, reducing the single-trial GUMDP to a standard finite-horizon MDP. The paper then applies Monte-Carlo Tree Search as an online planner: at each timestep, a search tree is built rooted at the current occupancy MDP state, and after a fixed number of iterations the best action is selected and executed. Theoretically, the paper proves that non-Markovianity is necessary (stationary and Markovian policies are strictly dominated), that the truncated objective F_{1,H} approximates the full single-trial objective within 8Lγ^H, that the problem is NP-hard (via a subset-sum reduction), and that MCTS provably converges to the optimal action with O(1/√n) regret. Unlike Mutti et al.'s extended MDP which tracks full history, the occupancy MDP compresses history into an incrementally-updated vector, making it more amenable to practical implementation.

## Results

The paper evaluates the single-trial truncated objective F_{1,H}(π) (lower is better) across three tasks (maximum state entropy exploration, imitation learning, adversarial MDP) on five environments. The proposed MCTS-based policy (π_MCTS) achieves the best F_{1,H} in nearly all settings, with the most dramatic gains on the illustrative GUMDPs: 0.01 vs. 0.05 for the infinite-trials solver on the entropy task (M_f,1), and 0.002 vs. 0.02 on the imitation task (M_f,2). On OpenAI Gym environments, π_MCTS improves over the infinite-trials solver by 13% on MountainCar (entropy: 0.61 vs. 0.70) and 43% on MountainCar (imitation: 0.04 vs. 0.07). The only near-tie is Taxi under imitation learning, where both π_MCTS and π*_Solver achieve 0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: a random policy (π_Random) and the optimal policy for the infinite-trials formulation (π*_Solver), computed via a constrained optimization solver. π_MCTS dominates both baselines in 8 out of 9 experimental settings. The most striking advantage is over π*_Solver on the illustrative GUMDPs, where the single-trial MCTS policy achieves 0.01 vs. 0.05 (entropy, M_f,1) and 0.002 vs. 0.02 (imitation, M_f,2), demonstrating that the infinite-trials optimal policy can be substantially suboptimal when evaluated on a single trajectory. On OpenAI Gym environments, π_MCTS consistently outperforms π*_Solver by 5–43% depending on the task and environment, with the largest gap on MountainCar for imitation learning (0.04 vs. 0.07). The sole exception is Taxi under imitation learning, where π_MCTS (0.05) matches π*_Solver (0.05). Against the random policy, π_MCTS achieves improvements of 17–96% across all settings, confirming that the online planning approach effectively leverages the environment dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-trial GUMDP formulation introduced by Mutti et al. (2023) and Santos et al. (2024) from the finite-horizon undiscounted setting to the infinite-horizon discounted setting, which is the standard framework in much of the RL literature. It refines Mutti et al.'s 'extended MDP' (which tracks full history) into an 'occupancy MDP' that compresses history into a running occupancy vector, preserving optimality while being more amenable to practical implementation. Algorithmically, it leverages MCTS (Kocsis & Szepesvári, 2006) as an online planner over the occupancy MDP, building on the GUMDP framework with convex objectives established by Zahavy et al. (2021) and Zhang et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first approach to compute (approximately) optimal policies for GUMDPs when performance is evaluated on a single trajectory in the infinite-horizon discounted regime, a setting that better matches practical applications where an agent's lifetime is uncertain. The NP-hardness result (even for smooth convex objectives via a one-step subset-sum reduction) and the strict policy-class hierarchy (stationary < Markovian < non-Markovian) clarify fundamental limits. Key limitations include the requirement of a simulator and the combinatorial growth of the occupancy vector with |S|×|A|, leaving open questions about compression, continuous-state generalization, and scalability to larger environments.
