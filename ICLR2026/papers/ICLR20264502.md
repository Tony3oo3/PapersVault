## Problem

State-of-the-art neural LiDAR compression methods rely on computationally expensive 3D context structures (octrees, voxel grids) that achieve strong rate-distortion performance but incur coding latencies of hundreds of milliseconds, making them impractical for real-time applications such as autonomous driving where LiDAR generates 10 FPS. Additionally, existing approaches require separate networks for geometry and intensity compression, introducing redundant computation. The paper tackles how to achieve high compression efficiency at low latency while unifying both modalities in a single framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 2D range image (H×W pixels, each storing a range value and reflectance intensity), the decoded previous frame as a temporal reference, and RGB camera images (5 views on Waymo, 2 on KITTI) that must be decoded before LiDAR compression in a serial pipeline. The range image is quantized in two stages: a coarse sketch map (step b₁=2) and a fine detail map (step b₂ ∈ {1/5,…,1/100}), with intensity quantized at step 2×b₂. The output is a compressed bitstream containing the quantized range/intensity symbols, VAE latent variables, hyperpriors, and compressed optical flow; the 3D point cloud is recovered losslessly from range values via the known sensor emission angles.

## Method

RangeCM replaces all 3D context operators with a 2D CNN-based pipeline operating directly on range-view features, achieving ~100× lower inference latency. The core architecture is a conditional VAE whose analysis/synthesis transforms are conditioned on three complementary contexts: (1) a multi-scale spatial context that decomposes the range image into sketch and detail maps predicted in a coarse-to-fine checkerboard order, (2) a flow-based temporal context that warps reference-frame features via a compressed range-view optical flow, and (3) a camera context generated by deformable attention that adaptively samples RGB features using LiDAR features as queries with geometrically informed reference points. A context refinement step re-computes the camera context after the sketch map is decoded to improve LiDAR-camera alignment under causality. Crucially, a single hybrid context (spatial + temporal + refined camera) is shared to predict both the detail range map and the intensity map via lightweight prediction heads, eliminating the need for a separate intensity-specific network.

## Results

On geometry compression, RangeCM-G achieves a BD-Rate of −61.96% on Waymo Open Dataset (WOD) and −56.07% on SemanticKITTI, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art RIDDLE (−54.21% on WOD) by 17.14% while running 3.5× faster (0.20 s vs. 0.97 s total decoding on WOD). For intensity compression, RangeCM-GI achieves −20.93% BD-Rate on WOD and −6.96% on KITTI, with inference latency of only ~10 ms—over 100× faster than Unicorn (14.84 s encoding on KITTI). The overall coding latency is approximately 0.1 s per frame on KITTI, meeting real-time requirements for 10 FPS LiDAR sensors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RangeCM dominates all geometry compression baselines: it outperforms RIDDLE (the prior SOTA range-image method) by 17.14% BD-Rate on WOD and 15.41% on KITTI, while being 3.5× faster in decoding. Against voxel-based Unicorn, RangeCM-G achieves 28.73% better BD-Rate on KITTI with 66× lower encoding latency (0.04 s vs. 2.65 s). Compared to octree-based G-PCC and EHEM, RangeCM yields 56% and 36% BD-Rate improvements respectively on KITTI. For intensity compression, RangeCM-GI is over 100× faster than Unicorn (0.05 s vs. 14.84 s encoding on KITTI) while achieving comparable BD-Rate (−6.96% vs. −12.16%); on WOD, RangeCM-GI achieves −20.93% BD-Rate with no Unicorn comparison available. Against the lossless reflectance compressor SerLiC, RangeCM achieves a comparable BPP (3.66 vs. 3.64 on KITTI) with 40% faster encoding. The only comparison where RangeCM does not clearly win is intensity compression on KITTI, where Unicorn achieves a slightly better BD-Rate (−12.16% vs. −6.96%), though at 300× the encoding cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 3D context paradigm (octree-based G-PCC/EHEM, voxel-based Unicorn/RENO) by performing all compression computations in the 2D range-image domain, building on the range-image compression line (RICNet, RIDDLE) but replacing their 3D feature extractors with a purely 2D CNN backbone. It integrates three context sources—spatial (via a VAE with checkerboard multi-scale prediction, building on Ballé et al. 2018 and He et al. 2021), temporal (via optical-flow warping, building on Li et al. 2021's contextual video compression), and cross-modal camera (via deformable attention from Zhu et al. 2021)—into a single hybrid context that jointly predicts geometry and intensity, avoiding the redundant recomputation required by prior methods that use separate networks for each modality. The camera context extends the single prior attempt by Lin et al. 2023 (which used depth estimation + octree fusion with only ~2% gain) by operating directly in the range-view with learned deformable sampling, achieving a substantially larger improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving and robotics: RangeCM achieves ~0.1s coding latency (satisfying 10 FPS LiDAR requirements) while outperforming the best 3D-context method (RIDDLE) by 17% BD-Rate on WOD, and is 100× faster than the state-of-the-art intensity compressor. The finding that a comprehensive 2D context can surpass 3D counterparts challenges the field's assumption that explicit 3D local geometry is necessary for accurate density estimation. Key limitations include dependence on camera image quality (24.4% bitrate increase in dark scenes), the need for LiDAR-camera calibration (unavailable in KITTI's official test split), and the marginal geometry degradation when jointly compressing intensity. Open questions include robustness to extreme weather, generalization to novel LiDAR hardware without fine-tuning, and whether the 2D paradigm extends to other point-cloud attributes (e.g., normals, semantics).
