## Problem

Under sequential adaptation, continual vision-language models preserve first-order primitive recognition (attributes, objects) while degrading in higher-order compositional binding, especially under tight rehearsal budgets and without task IDs at test time. Prior continual VL work has focused on global geometry preservation, distillation, or replay to curb average forgetting, but has not addressed whether the internal structure that enables attribute-object binding remains recoverable and stable across tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (224×224), a composition text (e.g., 'a red cube'), and its decomposed primitive set (e.g., 'red', 'cube'), all encoded by a frozen CLIP backbone. It produces a composed embedding built from the primitives via an orthogonal map, which is jointly aligned with the textual composition embedding and the image embedding through a symmetric multi-positive InfoNCE. Downstream, the aligned embeddings yield retrieval rankings (R@K, MRR), image-text matching scores, and VQA answer selections; an optional tiny text-centric buffer (M=64 snippets) supplies paraphrastic positives without storing images.

## Method

COMPO-REALIGN is a minimal parameter-efficient head (frozen encoders, <1% extra parameters) built on three coupled components. First, a reversible composer averages adapted primitive embeddings and applies an orthogonal core parameterized by the Cayley transform, making the primitive-to-composition map invertible by construction (R^T = R^{-1}). Second, a single symmetric multi-positive InfoNCE treats the textual composition embedding and the composed-from-primitives embedding as two joint positives for the image, implicitly co-locating the two composition views without explicit cycle-consistency or set losses. Third, a spectral trust region estimates the Jacobian spectral radius of the similarity with respect to primitive embeddings via one or two power iterations and rescales head gradients by α = min(1, γ/σ̂_max), capping harmful alignment sensitivity without adding a separate loss. The entire pipeline updates only the head parameters (orthogonal core Θ, adapter A, MLP φ) per task with no task IDs, and a theoretical analysis provides an exact identifiability condition (coherence μ < 1/(2m−1)) and an exponential CRR lower bound under sub-Gaussian perturbations.

## Results

On compositional DIL and multi-domain MTIL retrieval (Table 1), COMPO-REALIGN achieves 58.8 Avg R@1 (Image→Text) and 45.1 (Text→Image), improving +2.4 over the strongest prior (C-CLIP at 56.4) and reducing average forgetting from 5.0–5.1 to 3.2 (≈37% relative reduction). Compositional Retention Ratio (CRR) rises to 0.91 (vs. 0.88–0.89 for the best baselines), and zero-shot transfer degradation (ZSTD) is the smallest in magnitude at −1.3. On continual VQA (Table 2), COMPO-REALIGN reaches 65.1 / 60.4 / 56.8 Avg accuracy on CLOVE-scene / CLOVE-function / VQACL with AF 3.6, outperforming the strongest prior (CL-MoE: 63.5 / 59.2 / 55.4, AF 4.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMPO-REALIGN outperforms all 13 compared baselines on every reported metric across both retrieval and VQA tracks. On retrieval (Table 1), the closest competitors are C-CLIP (56.4 R@1 I→T, AF 5.1) and DIKI (56.0, AF 5.0); COMPO-REALIGN leads by +2.4 and +2.8 R@1 respectively, while cutting AF by roughly 37% (3.2 vs. 5.0–5.1) and halving ZSTD magnitude (−1.3 vs. −1.9 to −2.1). Replay-based methods (IncCLIP, ConStruct-VL, GIFT) and regularization/distillation methods (Mod-X, ZSCL, DKR, ZAF) trail by 2.8–7.9 R@1 points and show 1.5–2.5× higher forgetting. On continual VQA (Table 2), the strongest prior CL-MoE (CVPR 2025) achieves 63.5/59.2/55.4 with AF 4.7; COMPO-REALIGN gains +1.6/+1.2/+1.4 accuracy and reduces AF to 3.6. Earlier prompt/replay baselines (SGP, TRIPLET, QUAD) are 3–5 points behind on VQA accuracy with AF 5.2–6.3. No baseline matches COMPO-REALIGN on CRR (all ≤ 0.89 vs. 0.91) or ZSTD (all ≥ −1.9 vs. −1.3), confirming that the gains are not merely accuracy-driven but reflect genuinely better compositional structure retention and zero-shot stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a 'structure-first' niche within continual vision-language learning, departing from the dominant replay/distillation/topology-preservation paradigm (Mod-X, ZSCL, CTP, DKR, TiC-CLIP) that targets global geometry or task retention. Rather than adding regularization losses or large rehearsal buffers, it repurposes two existing ideas—orthogonal parameterization via the Cayley transform and multi-positive contrastive alignment—into a minimal reversible composer head that makes primitive-to-composition binding invertible by construction. The spectral trust region (Jacobian clipping) borrows the conceptual spirit of EWC-style regularization but operates on alignment sensitivity rather than parameter importance, and the text-centric micro-buffer reframes replay as symbolic anchoring rather than raw memory. The theoretical identifiability analysis (coherence-based CRR lower bounds) is novel in this context, connecting the architectural design to a formal guarantee that prior continual-VL work has not provided.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a concrete failure mode—compositional binding degrades while primitives remain stable—that prior continual-VL benchmarks (focused on average accuracy or zero-shot scores) mask, and provides lightweight diagnostics (CRR, CCE, Jacobian spectral radius) to monitor it. The 'structure-before-memory' principle and the finding that 64 text snippets outperform image buffers of equal size for compositional retention offer a practical, low-cost recipe for deployment under tight rehearsal and no task IDs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to streaming video, multilingual settings, and lightly unfrozen encoders, and whether the coherence-based identifiability bounds hold under the non-ideal conditions of real CLIP embeddings (which are far from equiangular tight frames). The +2.4 R@1 gain over the strongest prior is meaningful but incremental, so the broader impact hinges on the compositional-retention framing becoming a standard evaluation axis.
