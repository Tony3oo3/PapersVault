## Problem

Existing fingerspelling datasets overwhelmingly focus on single-handed systems (ASL, GSL), leaving the two-handed fingerspelling system shared by BANZSL (British, Australian, and New Zealand Sign Language) comparatively underexplored. Moreover, prior datasets lack the scale and linguistic realism needed for practical research, rarely capturing naturally occurring phenomena such as spelling errors, lexical abbreviations, acronyms, and inline corrections that are common in real-world signing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261247/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 35,028 video segments (1280×720@25 for News, 1920×1080@30 or 1280×720@60 for Lab, various for Web) with multi-level temporal annotations: video↔subtitle alignment, video↔fingerspelled letter boundaries, and video↔target lexicon mapping. For benchmarking, IFSR takes a pre-segmented FS clip and outputs a letter sequence; FSD takes an untrimmed 10-second sliding-window video and outputs temporal (start, end) segments; FSD-R chains detection with recognition; and FSR-Context evaluates character-level accuracy of fingerspelled spans within full sentence-level sign language translation output.

## Method

BANZ-FS is curated from three complementary sources—live Auslan news interpretation (ABC News), controlled multi-camera RGB-D lab recordings (Kinect-V2 + RealSense), and user-generated BSL/NZSL vlogs—yielding 116 signers and 207,292 total FS characters. Annotation employs AlphaPose for signer tracking, followed by a structured three-step expert protocol (temporal identification, character-level transcription, English alignment) with a recognition-based cross-check verification (5% random sampling, ~95% first-round pass rate) completed by five Auslan experts over ~500 work hours. For benchmarking, video features are extracted via DWPose key points and I3D (fine-tuned on MM-WAuslan), and five SOTA models per task are evaluated under a cross-domain protocol (train on one of News/Lab/Web/Full, test on all four), revealing that HandReader achieves the best IFSR accuracy (75.4% on Full) while SL-Seg leads FSD (66.9% AP@IoU₀.₅), with Web-domain generalization remaining the primary bottleneck across all tasks.

## Results

On the IFSR task, HandReader (Korotaev et al., 2025) achieves the best overall Letter Accuracy of 75.4% on the Full set, with 71.8% on Web, 86.7% on Lab, and 64.4% on News. For Fingerspelling Detection, SL-Seg (Moryossef et al., 2023) leads with 66.9% AP@IoU₀.₅ on the Full set and 47.3% on the challenging Web domain. In FSR-Context, ByT5 (character-level tokenization) achieves 25.8% Letter Accuracy, substantially outperforming T5 (10.2%) and the gloss-free SLT model GFSLT-VLP (16.4%). Cross-domain generalization remains a major challenge: models trained on Lab data drop 30–50 points when tested on Web, and even the best model (HandReader) scores only 40.2% on Web when trained on that domain alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among IFSR baselines, HandReader (Korotaev et al., 2025) consistently outperforms all others on the Full training set (75.4% vs. 74.7% for FS-PoseNet and 69.7% for TS-FS-Reg), with its advantage most pronounced on the Web domain (71.8% vs. 70.1% and 62.4%). FS-PoseNet (Fayyazsanavi et al., 2024) is the strongest pose-based method and shows the best cross-domain robustness when trained on a single domain (e.g., 66.2% News→News, 92.8% Lab→Lab). Iterative-Att (Shi et al., 2019) and MiCT-RANet (Mahoudeau, 2020) show the weakest generalization, with Iterative-Att dropping to 21.0% on News when trained only on Web. For FSD, SL-Seg (Moryossef et al., 2023) dominates all detection baselines (66.9% AP@IoU Full vs. 62.7% for MT-FS-Det and 54.1% for TS-FS-Det), particularly on Web (47.3% vs. 41.6% and 37.4%). In FSR-Context, ByT5 (25.8% Letter Accuracy) nearly triples T5's score (10.2%), confirming that character-level tokenization is critical for preserving fingerspelled content in translation output, while the best gloss-free SLT model GFSLT-VLP (Zhou et al., 2023) achieves only 16.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first large-scale dataset dedicated to two-handed BANZSL fingerspelling, a system fundamentally different from the single-handed ASL/GSL systems that dominate existing fingerspelling literature (ChicagoFSWild+, FSBoard, Fleurs-ASL-FS). It extends the authors' own Auslan-Daily News benchmark (Shen et al., 2023) by tripling its scale and adding fine-grained fingerspelling annotations, while also drawing on the BOBSL-FS (Prajwal et al., 2022) lineage for BSL coverage. The benchmarking framework repurposes established methods (FS-PoseNet, SL-Seg, HandReader, I3D-based features) but applies them to a domain where two-handed coarticulation, self-occlusion, and rapid inter-letter transitions create qualitatively different visual challenges than single-handed systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a critical gap for the BANZSL community and provides the first systematic evidence that two-handed fingerspelling is substantially harder than its single-handed counterparts: the best IFSR model (HandReader) reaches only 75.4% letter accuracy, and FSR-Context performance is strikingly low (16.4% for the best SLT model, 25.8% for ByT5), revealing fingerspelling as a major unsolved bottleneck in sign language translation. Cross-domain generalization remains a severe challenge (Lab-trained models drop from ~93% to ~30% on Web data), and the FSD-R gap (66.9% AP@IoU vs. 53.5% AP@Acc) shows that correct localization does not guarantee correct recognition. Open questions include recognition-aware detection, multi-view/depth modeling from the lab recordings, dialect balance (Auslan-heavy), and whether synthetic augmentation can address the long-tail letter distribution.
