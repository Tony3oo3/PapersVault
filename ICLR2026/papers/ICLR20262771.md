## Problem

Assistant LMs post-trained to be helpful, structured, and cooperative make poor user simulators when prompted to role-play users, with the surprising finding that better assistants yield worse simulators. This limits the realism of multi-turn evaluation environments, since overly cooperative simulated users overestimate assistant performance. The paper asks how to train purpose-built models that faithfully replicate the indirect, effortful, and inconsistent behavior of real human users in multi-turn conversations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a high-level user intent (a short abstract goal like 'write a Python function to sort and reverse an array') and the conversation history so far, producing the next user utterance or a special <|endconversation|> token. The intent is deliberately under-specified—capturing the overall objective without explicit details—so the model controls language choices and gradually reveals information across turns. Each K-turn conversation is converted into K+1 training samples, with the first turn conditioned only on the intent and subsequent turns conditioned on both intent and prior dialogue state.

## Method

The core technique is 'flipping the dialogue': real human-assistant conversations from WildChat (384K after deduplication) are reformatted so the model learns to predict user utterances rather than assistant responses. Full-parameter fine-tuning is applied to Llama3-8b-Base and Llama3.2-1b-Base (base checkpoints, not instruction-tuned), with a special <|endconversation|> token appended after the last assistant turn to teach dialogue termination. High-level intents are generated per-conversation via few-shot GPT-4o prompting and used as conditioning signals during both training and inference. A key design finding is that starting from base pre-trained checkpoints substantially outperforms instruction-tuned initialization, because assistant-style post-training pushes the model away from the natural, varied distribution of human user language.

## Results

UserLM-8b achieves the lowest perplexity on both in-domain (4.33 PPL on WildChat) and out-of-domain (7.42 PPL on PRISM) user utterances, often 60–70% lower than all baselines. On intrinsic evaluations, UserLM-8b matches or exceeds real human behavior on first-turn diversity (94.55% vs. 94.01% human), intent decomposition (2.69% overlap vs. 1.68% human), and naturalness (80.21% vs. 90.15% human), while dramatically outperforming prompted GPT-4o on dialogue termination (63.54% F1 vs. 1.38%) and role adherence (93.95% vs. 38.85%). In the extrinsic simulation experiment, deploying UserLM-8b as the user simulator causes GPT-4o assistant performance to drop from 74.6% to 57.4% on coding and math tasks, confirming that more realistic user simulation exposes assistant weaknesses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prompted GPT-4o (the most common user-simulation approach in prior work), UserLM-8b wins decisively on all six intrinsic metrics: diversity (94.55 vs. 74.42), intent decomposition (2.69 vs. 7.68), dialogue termination (63.54 vs. 1.38 F1), naturalness (80.21 vs. 3.31), role adherence (93.95 vs. 38.85), and intent adherence (94.65 vs. 70.95). Against USP-8b (Wang et al., 2025), the closest fine-tuned baseline, UserLM-8b wins on diversity (94.55 vs. 94.37), intent decomposition (2.69 vs. 6.33), dialogue termination (63.54 vs. 21.31), and naturalness (80.21 vs. 77.73), but loses slightly on role adherence (93.95 vs. 98.05) and intent adherence (94.65 vs. 97.55). Notably, scaling assistant LMs does not improve simulation quality: GPT-4o underperforms GPT-4o-mini on role and intent adherence, and Llama3-8b-Instruct is worse than Llama3.2-1b-Instruct on most metrics, reinforcing the paper's central claim that better assistants make worse user simulators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the predominant approach of prompting assistant LMs to role-play users (Li et al., 2024; Chang et al., 2025; Ivey et al., 2024) by instead training purpose-built User LMs through a 'dialogue-flipping' procedure on real human-assistant conversations from WildChat. It extends earlier fine-tuning work on task-oriented dialogue datasets (MultiWOZ-based simulators by Liu et al., 2022; Sekulić et al., 2024) and the closely related USP-8b (Wang et al., 2025) by introducing intent-conditioning for steerable simulation and by training from base (rather than instruction-tuned) checkpoints. The conceptual lineage traces back to rule-based and neural user simulators in task-oriented dialogue (Schatzmann et al., 2007; Asri et al., 2016), but the paper's key departure is treating user simulation as a distinct post-training objective rather than a prompting trick applied to assistant models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that assistant LMs overestimate their own multi-turn performance when evaluated against assistant-based simulators (GPT-4o drops from 74.6% to 57.4% with UserLM-8b) has immediate practical implications for how the field benchmarks conversational AI. Open questions include whether User LMs can be personalized to specific demographics or domains, whether the need for generation guardrails disappears at larger scales, and how well these simulators transfer to expert or non-English user populations. The released UserLM-8b provides a concrete, reusable tool that lowers the barrier to realistic multi-turn evaluation, but the 8b parameter scale and English-only training data limit current applicability.
