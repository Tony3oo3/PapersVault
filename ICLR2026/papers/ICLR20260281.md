## Problem

Existing battery datasets are limited in scale, provide only coarse-grained labels (binary normal/abnormal or SOH-based), and lack coverage of real-world operating conditions such as discharging and multi-chemistry scenarios. No unified benchmarking framework exists for evaluating fault diagnosis algorithms under standardized protocols, making cross-study comparison difficult and hindering engineering deployment.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes multi-dimensional time-series telemetry (cell voltage, current, temperature, SOC) sampled at 10 s over 30–60 min segments from 1000 EVs across LFP and NCM chemistries. For the CNN model, each voltage curve is rendered as a 512×512 single-channel grayscale image with the time axis normalized to [0,1], supplemented by 12 handcrafted statistical descriptors (mean, std, extrema, range, consistency metrics). Outputs are a 4-class fault label (normal, self-discharge, high resistance, low capacity) for classification or a 3-class severity label (mild, moderate, severe) for grading, evaluated under both segment-level and strict vehicle-level splits.

## Method

The dataset is generated via a physics-informed pipeline: Diffusion-TS produces realistic charging/discharging current sequences, which are then mapped to cell voltages through a first-order RC equivalent circuit model for LFP or a discrete convolution wavelet transform (DCWT) mapping model for NCM, with fault signatures injected by tuning equivalent-circuit parameters. The proposed BatteryMultiModalCNN benchmark model combines a pre-trained ResNet50 (modified for single-channel input) with a CBAM attention module for image features and a two-layer fully connected network for the 12-dimensional statistical features, concatenating both streams before a multi-layer FC classifier. The benchmark systematically evaluates RF, SVM, LSTM, and CNN under two partition strategies (segment-level 8:2 stratified split and strict vehicle-level split to prevent leakage), reporting precision, recall, and F1-score across all chemistry × operating-mode combinations.

## Results

On the primary fault classification task (single-vehicle split), CNN achieves the highest F1-score of 0.9280 on LFP discharging and 0.8897 on NCM charging, outperforming LSTM (0.8664 / 0.8313), RF (0.7051 / 0.7782), and SVM (0.6391 / 0.8129). On the fault grading task (single-vehicle split), CNN reaches an F1-score of 0.8813 on LFP charging and 0.7823 on LFP discharging, while traditional methods (RF, SVM) remain below 0.69. Under discharging conditions, traditional methods suffer F1-score drops exceeding 20% relative to charging, whereas CNN degrades by only 3–4 percentage points, demonstrating markedly stronger robustness to operating-condition shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four model families—Random Forest, SVM, LSTM, and CNN—on the same CH-BatteryGen dataset under both segment-level and vehicle-level splits. CNN dominates every scenario: on single-vehicle fault classification it leads LSTM by 6–14 F1 points (e.g., 0.9280 vs. 0.8664 on LFP discharge) and RF/SVM by 12–33 points. On fault grading, CNN's advantage over LSTM narrows to roughly 8–16 F1 points (0.8813 vs. 0.7273 on LFP charge), while RF and SVM trail by 17–34 points. A key finding is the cross-scenario sensitivity: RF and SVM lose more than 20% F1 when moving from charging to discharging, whereas CNN's degradation is limited to 3–4 points, and LSTM shows intermediate robustness. The paper does not compare against external published baselines (e.g., the GDN model from Deng & Hooi 2021 or the GRU+attention from Ma et al. 2022), so all comparisons are internal to the proposed benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of generative modeling and physics-informed simulation for battery data synthesis. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes Diffusion-TS (a general time-series diffusion model) as a current-sequence generator and couples it with chemistry-specific electrochemical models (first-order RC equivalent circuits for LFP, DCWT-based mapping for NCM) to produce fault-labeled voltage data at fleet scale. Conceptually, it departs from prior battery datasets (EVBattery, BatteryML, BatteryLife) that relied on raw lab or on-board recordings with coarse labels, by introducing a mechanism-constrained generative pipeline that enables fine-grained multi-class and multi-severity annotation while preserving statistical fidelity to real fleet telemetry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CH-BatteryGen fills a concrete gap: no prior public dataset combined real-world operating conditions (both charging and discharging), two mainstream chemistries, four fault types, and three severity levels in a single standardized benchmark. The finding that traditional methods (RF, SVM) degrade by over 20% F1 under discharging while CNN/LSTM degrade far less provides actionable guidance for BMS developers choosing diagnostic algorithms. Key limitations remain: severity grading is defined only for LFP, the data is generated rather than raw (raising questions about unmodeled fault interactions), and only four fault types are covered. Open questions include whether the generative pipeline faithfully captures rare multi-fault coupling scenarios and whether the benchmark conclusions transfer to truly heterogeneous fleet data beyond the two chemistries studied.
