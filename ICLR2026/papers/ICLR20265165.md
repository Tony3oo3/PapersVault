## Problem

Existing data science benchmarks for LLM agents suffer from two critical gaps: they lack standardized, process-aware evaluation that captures instruction adherence and process fidelity (relying instead on human or model-based judges), and they provide no large-scale, accurately labeled training data. This leaves the field unable to objectively measure whether an agent follows a prescribed workflow or merely produces a plausible final answer, and prevents systematic fine-tuning of models on domain-specific data science skills.

## Input / Output

Each task provides a natural-language question, a metadata file describing the dataset and columns, and structured train/test data files (parquet, CSV, or SQLite) with controlled noise injected into ~20% of training rows for IF tasks. The agent interacts with a stateless python_executor sandbox tool (200 s wall-clock limit, 5 turns) and must produce a prediction.csv aligned by row_id. Scoring is fully automatic: binary exact-match against sandbox-generated reference outputs for IF tasks, macro-F1 for classification-MM, and clipped R² for regression and time-series tasks, with no human or LLM judge involved.

## Method

DARE-bench is constructed via a four-stage automated pipeline (dataset sourcing from Kaggle, LLM-assisted task design and feasibility analysis, post-processing with noise injection or chronological splitting, and sandbox-verified finalization) that yields 6,300 tasks across three families—classification, regression, and time-series—each with an instruction-following and an ML-modeling variant. The key design insight is that by fixing random seeds and providing explicit procedural instructions, a procedurally faithful execution produces a deterministic outcome, so process fidelity can be verified by comparing the agent's prediction against the reference solution's output rather than requiring human judgment. For training, the paper applies rejection sampling with four strategies (FV, AV, BV, DV) to curate SFT data from multi-run trajectories, and performs GRPO-based reinforcement learning with verifiable outcome rewards (exact match for IF, macro-F1/clipped R² for predictive tasks) using the verl framework with SGLang rollouts. This dual evaluation-and-training design enables both reproducible benchmarking and substantial performance gains: SFT boosts Qwen3-32B's total score by ~1.83× and RL boosts Qwen3-4B's by over 8×.

## Results

Under the standard (5 turns, 200 s) configuration, Claude-Sonnet-3.7 achieves the highest scores on four of six DARE-bench metrics (classification-MM: 61.03, regression-MM: 63.20, time-series-XF: 49.88, time-series-CF: 13.70), while gpt-5 leads on both instruction-following columns (classification-IF: 69.81, regression-IF: 57.24). Open-source models lag substantially: Qwen3-32B scores a total of 23.25 and Qwen3-4B only 4.39. Fine-tuning on DARE-bench yields dramatic gains—supervised fine-tuning (SFT-DV) boosts Qwen3-32B's total score from 23.25 to 42.83 (+19.58), and reinforcement learning (GRPO) lifts Qwen3-4B from 4.39 to 37.40 (+33.01, an 8.5× relative improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Claude-Sonnet-3.7 is the strongest overall model, leading on four of six metrics (classification-MM 61.03, regression-MM 63.20, time-series-XF 49.88, time-series-CF 13.70), while gpt-5 narrowly wins both IF columns (classification-IF 69.81 vs. 61.48; regression-IF 57.24 vs. 46.37). gpt-o4-mini is competitive with gpt-5 on MM tasks (classification-MM 57.89 vs. 43.40) but trails on IF tasks. Claude-Sonnet-4 underperforms its predecessor Claude-Sonnet-3.7 by a wide margin across all metrics (e.g., classification-IF drops from 61.48 to 16.21), attributed to its tendency to decompose tasks into overly fine-grained substeps that exceed the turn limit. Among open-source models, Qwen3-32B (total 23.25) and Qwen3-4B (total 4.39) trail all proprietary models by 20–60 points and score near zero on time-series-CF. In the external validation on DSBench, the SFT-DV fine-tuned Qwen3-32B reaches 42.41, outperforming the DataWiseAgent framework (29.17) by 13.24 points, and the RL-trained Qwen3-4B jumps from 18.23 to 40.00 (+21.77), surpassing the DataWiseAgent score as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data science benchmarking line (DS-1000, DSBench, MLE-bench, DSEval, DA-Code, DABstep, DSBC) by introducing two key departures: (1) a dual task family that separates process-aware instruction following (verified against reference-code outputs) from open-ended ML modeling (verified against dataset ground truth), and (2) a large-scale training set (6,300 Kaggle-derived tasks) that enables both SFT and RLVR, which prior DS benchmarks entirely lacked. The verifiable-evaluation paradigm is borrowed from coding benchmarks (SWE-bench, HumanEval) and math benchmarks, while the RL training methodology builds on GRPO and the verl/SGLang infrastructure. The automated LLM-assisted curation pipeline departs from the manual, competition-centric curation of prior Kaggle-based benchmarks, pivoting to the broader Kaggle dataset pool to gain diversity (e.g., time-series forecasting) and scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DARE-bench fills a critical gap by providing a training-focused, verifiable benchmark for DS agents, enabling RLVR without human-in-the-loop reward modeling. The finding that even frontier models (gpt-o4-mini, Claude-Sonnet-3.7) score only in the 50s on a 0–100 scale, and that all models fail near-zero on canonical time-series forecasting, highlights a substantial capability gap in current LLMs for autonomous data science. The 8× RL improvement on Qwen3-4B and the complementary IF/MM ablation demonstrate that targeted training data can close much of this gap, making the benchmark practically useful for model developers. Limitations include the tabular-only scope (no multimodal or unstructured data), potential bias in rejection sampling toward shorter trajectories, and the fact that the automated curation pipeline's quality relative to expert curation remains unvalidated at scale.
