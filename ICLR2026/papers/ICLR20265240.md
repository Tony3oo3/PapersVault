## Problem

Deep learning-based image watermarking systems are vulnerable to a previously unexplored attack called self-re-watermarking, where an adversary with white-box access reuses the same encoder to embed a conflicting message into an already watermarked image, irretrievably overwriting the original watermark. The paper demonstrates that all state-of-the-art watermarking methods (HiDDeN, MBRS, WFormer, VINE, etc.) fail under this attack, exposing a systemic design gap: existing architectures implicitly assume single-use embedding and provide no mechanism to resist malicious encoder reuse.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a 128×128 RGB image (normalized to [-1,1]) concatenated with a 30-bit bipolar message (spatially expanded via spectrally normalized linear layers into a 4-channel tensor) and produces a watermarked image of the same dimensions. The decoder takes the watermarked image and outputs 30-dimensional logits that are thresholded to recover the binary message. A post-processing module (Gaussian blur plus low-magnitude suppression) is optionally applied at inference to improve visual fidelity, trading a small amount of robustness for higher PSNR.

## Method

The proposed self-aware watermarking framework uses a U-Net encoder (with a ResNet-50 auxiliary backbone) and a CNN decoder, both with spectral normalization on every convolutional and linear layer to enforce Lipschitz constraints that bound the decoder's sensitivity to input perturbations. Training jointly optimizes three losses: a fidelity loss (MSE + LPIPS), a nominal recovery loss (BCE on clean watermarked images), and a robustness loss (BCE on images that have been re-watermarked by feeding them back through the encoder with a different message, or perturbed via PGD). An adaptive loss-weighting schedule (Algorithm 2) dynamically adjusts the relative emphasis on fidelity, recovery, and robustness across training epochs based on observed bit-error rates. The paper further derives a theoretical BER upper bound showing that if the product of the decoder's Lipschitz constant and the overwrite distortion is below the minimum clean logit margin, no bits can flip, and empirically validates this bound across 3,000 test images.

## Results

The proposed self-aware watermarking framework achieves 100% ACC_orig under self-re-watermarking (same encoder reuse), compared to ~50% (random-guessing level) for all seven state-of-the-art baselines. Under PGD adversarial attacks, it maintains 99.95% (moderate, ε=0.03) and 99.37% (strong, ε=0.04) original-watermark recovery, while baselines drop to 49–89%. On standard distortions, the method achieves 95.06% under JPEG(50), 99.66% under Gaussian blur, and 99.85% under cropping, with visual quality of 34.03 dB PSNR and 0.97 SSIM. Across four datasets (COCO, MIRFLICKR-1M, CelebA, ImageNet), ACC_orig under self-re-embed remains at or near 100%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The decisive result is under self-re-watermarking (same encoder reused to embed a new message): all seven baselines collapse to ~50% ACC_orig (random-guessing), while the proposed method recovers the original watermark at 100%. Under PGD-based adversarial overwriting, the gap is equally stark — WFormer (88.64% moderate, 80.15% strong) and VINE (82.00%, 79.41%) are the strongest baselines but still fall 11–20 points below the proposed method (99.95%, 99.37%). On standard image-processing distortions, the proposed method is competitive but not uniformly best: MBRS and WFormer achieve 100% on Gaussian blur and dropout, and VINE leads on PSNR (37.07 vs. 34.03 dB) and JPEG robustness (99.97% vs. 95.06%). The proposed method's JPEG(50) accuracy (95.06%) is notably lower than dwtDctSvd (99.97%) and VINE (99.97%), reflecting a fidelity-robustness trade-off imposed by the Lipschitz constraints. Overall, the paper's method trades a modest amount of standard-distortion robustness and visual quality for an order-of-magnitude improvement in adversarial overwrite resilience, which is the paper's central contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep learning-based image watermarking lineage (HiDDeN, MBRS, ARWGAN, WFormer, VINE) by introducing a new threat model—self-re-watermarking, where the same encoder is maliciously reused to overwrite an existing watermark—and a corresponding defense. The defense repurposes Lipschitz-constrained architectures (originally developed for adversarial robustness in classification, e.g., Parseval networks) into the watermarking setting, combining spectral normalization with re-watermarking adversarial training and adaptive loss weighting. It departs from prior re-watermarking defenses (cross-model overwriting, dual-watermarking, high-frequency embedding) by targeting the more severe same-encoder scenario and providing a formal BER bound under this specific attack class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a systemic, previously unaddressed vulnerability: every evaluated SOTA watermarking method drops to random-guessing accuracy under self-re-watermarking, undermining the core ownership guarantee of these systems. The proposed Lipschitz-constrained framework offers both a theoretical guarantee (BER upper bound) and strong empirical protection (100% recovery under self-overwrite), establishing a new design principle—bounding encoder-decoder sensitivity—for watermarking robustness. Key limitations include the white-box assumption, the narrow focus on single-stage self-overwriting (multi-stage removal-then-re-embedding is only preliminarily explored), and the trade-off where spectral normalization constrains encoder capacity. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to concurrent multi-attack defenses and to higher-fidelity or video watermarking settings.
