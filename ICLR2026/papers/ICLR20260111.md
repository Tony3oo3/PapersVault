## Problem

Existing EC number prediction methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: they represent enzymes in Euclidean space, which cannot faithfully embed the tree-like EC hierarchy without high-dimensional overhead or significant distortion, and they predominantly rely on sequence information while ignoring structural and active site features that directly determine catalytic specificity. Additionally, active site annotations are available for only a small subset of enzymes, creating a severe modality imbalance that hinders multi-modal learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260111/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modalities per enzyme: a protein sequence (encoded by a frozen PLM such as ESM-2 into a 512-d vector), a 3D structure (from PDB or AlphaFold2/ESMFold predictions), and active site residue annotations from UniProt (which are missing for over half the dataset). The output is a multi-class, multi-label prediction of the full 4-digit EC number, evaluated hierarchically at each of the four digit levels. Because individual enzymes can catalyze multiple reactions, a single input may map to up to 9 distinct EC numbers (as in the Promiscuous test set).

## Method

PoinnCARE constructs two pairwise similarity graphs over the enzyme set—one from structural similarity via Foldseek and one from active-site motif similarity via Folddisco—then applies Personalized PageRank graph diffusion to each to propagate information through multi-hop neighbors and alleviate annotation sparsity. The diffused adjacency matrices are fed into two independent hyperbolic GNNs operating in the Poincaré ball, where node updates are performed via tangent-space linear transformations and neighbor aggregation followed by exponential mapping back to hyperbolic space. A decorrelation-based alignment loss enforces invariance between the structural and active-site embeddings of the same enzyme while preventing degenerate representations. Final EC predictions are a weighted fusion of the two modality-specific classifiers, and the whole system is trained end-to-end with a joint alignment-plus-cross-entropy objective. The key geometric insight is that the EC taxonomy's tree structure (verified by near-zero Gromov δ-hyperbolicity) embeds into 2D hyperbolic space with 1+ε distortion, whereas Euclidean embedding requires O(log n) dimensions for O(log n) distortion, explaining PoinnCARE's superior performance even at 32 dimensions.

## Results

On the CARE benchmark, PoinnCARE achieves Level-4 EC number accuracy of 0.648 on the <30% Identity test set and 0.822 on the 30-50% Identity test set, outperforming the strongest baseline CLEAN (0.535 and 0.798) by 10.4% and 2.4% respectively. On the challenging Promiscuous test set (multi-EC enzymes), PoinnCARE reaches 0.785 Level-4 accuracy, surpassing CLEAN by 9.4%. On the Price test set, PoinnCARE leads at Levels 1–3 (0.955, 0.909, 0.827) but trails ProtT5 (0.380) and ESM-2 (0.362) at Level 4 (0.349). PoinnCARE also demonstrates superior dimensional efficiency: at 32 dimensions it retains 0.597 Level-4 accuracy on <30% Identity, whereas CLEAN drops to 0.354.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoinnCARE achieves the best average rank (1.00) across all four CARE test sets, dominating at every EC level on the <30% Identity, 30-50% Identity, and Promiscuous sets. Against the strongest contrastive baseline CLEAN, PoinnCARE gains 10.4% at Level 4 on <30% Identity (0.648 vs 0.535) and 9.4% on Promiscuous (0.785 vs 0.691), confirming that hyperbolic multi-modal encoding substantially outperforms Euclidean contrastive learning. Structure-based methods (Foldseek, S-PLM) and sequence-only PLMs (ESM-2, ProtT5) are all consistently outperformed at every level on the low-homology test sets. The one notable exception is the Price test set at Level 4, where ProtT5 (0.380) and ESM-2 (0.362) slightly edge out PoinnCARE (0.349), though PoinnCARE still leads at Levels 1–3 by 1.7–3.1%. The ablation further shows that the hyperbolic space transformation alone contributes +9.3% Level-4 accuracy over a naive MLP, while graph diffusion and modality alignment add further gains on top.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoinnCARE sits at the intersection of three research threads: hyperbolic graph neural networks (Chami et al., 2019; Ganea et al., 2018), graph diffusion via Personalized PageRank (Gasteiger et al., 2019), and CCA-inspired multi-modal alignment (Zhang et al., 2021a). Its key departure from prior enzyme function prediction work (CLEAN, CLEAN-Concat, Top-EC, ProteinF3S) is twofold: it is the first to embed EC number prediction in hyperbolic space, motivated by the tree-like hierarchy of the EC taxonomy, and it is the first to incorporate active site annotations as a distinct modality alongside sequence and structure. The theoretical grounding via Gromov δ-hyperbolicity (δ ≈ 0.00–0.01 for EC vs. 0.73–0.92 for random graphs) and Theorem 1 (arbitrarily low-distortion tree embedding in 2D hyperbolic space vs. O(log n) distortion in Euclidean) provides a principled justification for the geometric choice that prior Euclidean-based methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dimensional efficiency result is the most practically impactful finding: PoinnCARE at 32 dimensions (0.597 level-4 accuracy) outperforms CLEAN at 512 dimensions (0.535), a 16× compression with a 10% accuracy gain, validated across two test sets and five dimension settings. This suggests hyperbolic representations could substantially reduce storage and inference costs for large-scale enzyme annotation pipelines. However, the method's reliance on sparse active site annotations (over half the dataset lacks them) and on external tools (Foldseek, Folddisco) limits immediate reproducibility and scalability. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic advantage persists at much larger dataset scales, whether the graph diffusion step is truly necessary beyond simple k-hop aggregation, and whether the framework generalizes to other hierarchical biological ontologies (e.g., Gene Ontology) beyond the EC system.
