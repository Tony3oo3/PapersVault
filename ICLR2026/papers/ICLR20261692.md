## Problem

It was unclear whether graph and hypergraph spectral sparsification in insertion-only data streams necessarily incurs additional space overhead compared to the best offline sample complexity. Prior streaming algorithms lost extra log n factors for graphs and polylog(m) factors for hypergraphs, which are prohibitively large when m = Ω(2^n). This paper resolves the question by achieving streaming sparsifiers whose space matches the offline sample complexity up to poly-iterated logarithmic factors.

## Input / Output

The input is an insertion-only stream of weighted edges (for graphs) or hyperedges (for hypergraphs with rank r), where weights are positive integers bounded by poly(n). The algorithm processes each item sequentially under strict memory constraints and outputs a re-weighted subgraph or sub-hypergraph that preserves the energy Q_H(x) = Σ_e w(e)·max_{(u,v)∈e}(x_u − x_v)² for all x ∈ ℝ^n within a (1+ε) multiplicative factor. For the sliding window variant, the algorithm must maintain an accurate approximation over only the most recent W updates, and for the adversarially robust variant, future inputs may adaptively depend on the algorithm's prior outputs.

## Method

The core technique is a novel local weight assignment scheme that, upon arrival of a new hyperedge e, assigns weights z_uv to each edge in e's clique so that the effective-resistance ratios τ_uv/z_uv are γ-balanced, using only the prefix weighted incidence matrix Z_t^{1/2}A_t and without modifying previously assigned weights. This is combined with online row sampling (Cohen et al., 2020) to maintain a compact sketch matrix M that is a 2-spectral approximation of Z_t^{1/2}A_t, reducing working memory to n log n poly(log log m) bits. A decoupling argument ensures the hyperedge sampling probabilities are independent of which hyperedges were previously sampled, satisfying the conditions of the Jambulapati et al. (2023) chaining argument. For the streaming setting, the algorithm feeds the merge-and-reduce output back as the sketch defining the next online sampling probability, avoiding storage of the full online coreset and converting polylog(n) factors into polylog log(n) factors. Adversarial robustness is achieved by bounding the ε-flip number via Laplacian eigenvalue changes and applying the Ben-Eliezer et al. (2020) computational path framework.

## Results

For graph spectral sparsification in insertion-only streams, the paper achieves O(n/ε² log n · poly(log log n)) bits of space (Theorem 1.1), removing the multiplicative log n factor over the previous best streaming bound of O(n/ε² log² n) bits. For hypergraph spectral sparsification, the streaming algorithm uses O(rn/ε² log² n · poly(log r, log log m)) bits (Theorem 1.3), matching the offline sample complexity of Jambulapati et al. (2023) and Lee (2023) up to poly(log log m) factors. The online hypergraph sparsifier (Theorem 1.4) requires only n log n poly(log log m) bits of working memory while sampling O(n/ε² log n log m log r) hyperedges, resolving an open question from Soma et al. (2024). For min-cut approximation, the streaming algorithm uses O(n/ε · log^c(n/ε) · log(1/ε)) bits, improving the log dependence in n over Ding et al. (2024).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For graph spectral sparsification, the paper's streaming algorithm (Theorem 1.1) improves on Kapralov et al. (2020) by removing one log n factor, going from O(n/ε² log² n) to O(n/ε² log n · poly(log log n)) bits, while matching the offline bound of Batson et al. (2014) up to poly(log log n) factors. For min-cut approximation, the paper improves on Ding et al. (2024) by reducing the log dependence from log^(c+1)(n/ε) to log^c(n/ε) · log(1/ε), a strict improvement in the n-dependence. For hypergraph sparsification, the streaming result (Theorem 1.3) is tight in both r and polylog(m) factors compared to the offline bounds of Jambulapati et al. (2023) and Lee (2023), whereas Khanna et al. (2025a) lose polylog(m) factors and Soma et al. (2024) lose polynomial factors in r. The online algorithm (Theorem 1.4) significantly reduces working memory from O(n²) bits (Soma et al., 2024) to n log n poly(log log m) bits, while Khanna et al. (2025a) achieve comparable sample complexity but with less efficient working memory. Overall, the paper wins on space complexity in every comparison, with the trade-off being poly(n) update time rather than near-linear time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of several well-developed threads: leverage-score-based sampling (Spielman & Srivastava, 2008), online row sampling (Cohen et al., 2020), the chaining/group-leverage-score-overestimate framework for hypergraph sparsification (Jambulapati et al., 2023; Lee, 2023), and the merge-and-reduce streaming coreset framework (Cohen-Addad et al., 2023). The key departure is a novel local weight assignment scheme that extends the offline balancing technique of Kapralov et al. (2021) to the online setting, where only prefix information is available, combined with a decoupling argument that ensures the sketch-based sampling probabilities remain valid. The adversarial robustness extension builds on the computational-path framework of Ben-Eliezer et al. (2020), applied here via an ε-flip number argument on Laplacian eigenvalues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves the open question of whether streaming graph/hypergraph sparsification inherently requires more space than the offline setting, showing that only poly-iterated logarithmic factors are lost. This is practically significant for massive hypergraphs where m can be Ω(2^n), as prior streaming algorithms incurred prohibitive poly(n) factors in that regime. Limitations include poly(n) update time (bottlenecked by matrix multiplication) and a worse space bound in the sliding window model. Open questions include whether the poly(log log m) factors can be eliminated, whether update time can be improved without sacrificing space, and whether the local weight assignment technique generalizes to other streaming linear-algebra problems.
