## Problem

Despite the empirical success of Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs), a rigorous theoretical understanding of their training dynamics remains largely absent. In particular, no prior work established convergence guarantees for KANs under a simplified first-layer-only training protocol, nor derived how the structure of data labels influences optimization speed. This paper fills that gap by proving global convergence and a fine-grained, label-dependent convergence rate for two-layer KANs in the overparameterized regime.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training set of n pairs (x_i, y_i) where x_i ∈ R^d and y_i ∈ R, along with a choice of g RBF basis functions φ_j(x) = exp(−(x−μ_j)²/2σ²). The second-layer coefficients β_pl are drawn once from {−1,+1} and held fixed; only the first-layer coefficients α_pjk (initialized from N(0,σ²)) are updated. The output is the trained first-layer parameter vector, the resulting network predictions, and a theoretical convergence bound whose rate is governed by the projection of the label vector y onto the eigenvectors of the infinite-width KAN Tangent Kernel.

## Method

The paper analyzes a two-layer KAN in which learnable univariate RBF basis functions sit on the edges (replacing fixed node activations in MLPs) and only the first-layer coefficients are trained via full-batch gradient descent. The key technical tool is a closed-form expression for the KAN Tangent Kernel (KAN-TK) in the infinite-width limit, derived by decomposing the kernel into contributions from the β and α parameters and evaluating Gaussian quadratic-form expectations. Using this kernel, the authors prove that gradient descent achieves linear convergence to zero training error provided the hidden width satisfies m = O(d²g⁶n²/λ₀²), a polynomial improvement over the O(n⁶) width required for two-layer ReLU networks under the same NTK framework. A second result refines the rate to be label-dependent: the error decays as √(Σᵢ(1−ηλᵢ)²ᵗ(vᵢᵀy)²), so labels aligned with large-eigenvalue eigenvectors of the KAN-TK converge faster. The analysis relies on three stability lemmas (coefficient boundedness, kernel stability over time, and initial kernel concentration) that together ensure the network remains in the lazy-training regime throughout optimization.

## Results

The paper's headline theoretical result is that a two-layer KAN trained on first-layer coefficients only requires hidden width m = O(d²g⁶n²/λ₀²) for guaranteed global convergence, a polynomial improvement over the O(n⁶/λ₀⁴δ³) width needed for two-layer ReLU networks under the same NTK framework. The dependence on the minimum kernel eigenvalue λ₀ improves from λ₀⁻⁴ (both baselines) to λ₀⁻², making the guarantee more robust when λ₀ is small. Empirically, on MNIST and CIFAR-10, wider KANs converge faster and exhibit smaller weight deviations from initialization, confirming the lazy-training regime; on a synthetic d=100 dataset, FastKAN with m=1000 converges substantially faster than a ReLU network of the same width across sample sizes of 500–1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical two-layer ReLU network of Du et al. (2019), the proposed first-layer-only KAN achieves a dramatic width reduction from O(n⁶) to O(n²) (ignoring d, g, λ₀ factors) and a stability improvement from λ₀⁻⁴ to λ₀⁻², meaning that if λ₀ shrinks by a factor k, the KAN needs only k² more width versus k⁴ for the ReLU network. The trade-off is a smaller allowable learning rate: O(λ₀/(n³d²g⁶)) versus O(λ₀/n²) for the ReLU network, which slows the per-step convergence factor. Against the full-parameter KAN of Gao & Tan (2025), the first-layer-only variant again improves the λ₀ dependence from λ₀⁻⁴ to λ₀⁻², but introduces stronger polynomial dependence on the input dimension d and the number of basis functions g (d²g⁶ vs. g⁹ in the width). Empirically, at a fixed width of m=1000, the FastKAN model converges substantially faster than a ReLU network of the same width on a 100-dimensional synthetic task, and on MNIST/CIFAR-10 the same width-scaling and lazy-training trends hold, confirming the theoretical predictions in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) convergence framework of Du et al. (2019) and Arora et al. (2019)—originally developed for two-layer ReLU networks with first-layer-only training—to the Kolmogorov-Arnold Network architecture introduced by Liu et al. (2025). It also builds on Gao & Tan (2025), who proved convergence for two-layer KANs with all parameters jointly trained, by restricting to first-layer training to obtain a cleaner analysis and a closed-form KAN-TK expression for RBF basis functions. The key conceptual departure is showing that the smooth, learnable edge functions inherent to KANs eliminate the combinatorial activation-pattern instability that drives the O(n⁶) width requirement in ReLU networks, yielding an O(n²) width bound under the same tangent kernel methodology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(n²) vs. O(n⁶) width improvement provides a concrete theoretical explanation for why KANs are more parameter-efficient than MLPs, strengthening the case for KANs in resource-constrained or interpretability-critical settings. The label-dependent convergence rate (Theorem 4.6) offers a fine-grained diagnostic: convergence speed is governed by the projection of labels onto the KAN-TK eigenspectrum, which could guide data curation or curriculum design. However, the analysis is limited to two-layer networks, first-layer-only training, and RBF basis functions in closed form; extending to deep KANs, stochastic optimizers like Adam, and multi-dimensional inputs with other basis families remains open. The required learning rate scales as O(λ₀/(n³d²g⁶)), which is quite restrictive in practice and may limit immediate applicability.
