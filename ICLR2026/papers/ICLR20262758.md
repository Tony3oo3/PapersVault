## Problem

Autoregressive LLMs generate chain-of-thought reasoning sequentially, preventing them from revising earlier tokens or exploring diverse solution paths in a holistic manner. While diffusion models have been applied to text generation, prior latent diffusion approaches focus on fluency rather than reasoning, and discrete masked-diffusion LMs cannot perform semantic-level self-correction. The paper asks how to leverage the iterative refinement and parallel exploration capabilities of latent diffusion to enhance the reasoning ability of an existing LLM at the semantic level.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text question or problem statement as input. It first generates a variable-length sequence of latent reasoning blocks, where each block is a fixed-size vector (4–6 continuous tokens) representing one sentence of reasoning, delimited by <BOT>/<EOT> special tokens. Once the model predicts a <SOA> (start-of-answer) token, it switches to autoregressive text generation to produce the final answer conditioned on all denoised latent blocks. The latent blocks are individually decodable to human-readable text via the VAE decoder, providing interpretability.

## Method

LaDiR constructs a structured latent reasoning space using a β-VAE (encoder initialized from a pretrained LLM, decoder frozen) that compresses each reasoning sentence into a block of continuous thought tokens, with robustness augmentations (Gaussian noise injection and random token substitution) to smooth the latent space. A latent diffusion model, trained with a flow-matching objective, then denoises these blocks using a hybrid attention mask: bidirectional within each block and strictly causal across blocks, enabling local refinement while preserving sequential dependencies. Training proceeds in two stages—teacher-forcing with oracle VAE latents, followed by rollout training where the model generates its own latents from noise and backpropagates answer supervision through the denoising trajectory to mitigate error accumulation. At inference, a diversity-guidance mechanism applies time-decaying repulsive forces between batch members in latent space, pushing trajectories apart to explore multiple distinct reasoning paths rather than collapsing to repetitive solutions.

## Results

On the primary math reasoning evaluation across 7 benchmarks (2 in-domain, 5 out-of-domain), LaDiR achieves an average Pass@1 of 43.5% and Pass@100 of 52.0%, surpassing the best prior autoregressive latent reasoning method TaH+ (42.0/45.5) by 1.5% on Pass@1 and 6.5% on Pass@100. On code generation, LaDiR (Qwen3-8B backbone) reaches 74.5% average Pass@1, outperforming the same-backbone AR SFT baseline (69.3%) by 5.2% and the strongest latent reasoning baseline TaH+ (71.8%) by 2.7%. On the Countdown planning task, LaDiR improves Pass@1 by over 25% (CD-4) and nearly 30% (CD-5) over the LLaMA 8B SFT autoregressive baseline, while also achieving the highest diversity score (7.3 and 8.9 unique solutions among 100 samples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest autoregressive latent reasoning baselines, LaDiR wins on average: it surpasses TaH+ (42.0/45.5) by 1.5% Pass@1 and 6.5% Pass@100, and outperforms Discrete Latent (40.8/46.4) and Soft Think (41.0/43.5) on both metrics, with the largest gaps on out-of-domain benchmarks like DM-Math (+4.1% over TaH+) and College (+1.9% over TaH+). LaDiR decisively outperforms all masked diffusion baselines (LLaDA 8B: 35.8/44.3) and prior latent diffusion methods (LD4LG: 15.7/21.7, PLANNER: 13.6/20.3), indicating that blockwise latent diffusion with flow matching is far more effective for reasoning than token-level masked diffusion or earlier latent diffusion approaches designed for text generation. On code generation, LaDiR (74.5% avg) beats same-backbone AR SFT (69.3%) and TaH+ (71.8%) but trails the specialized Ouro 2.6B looped model (74.0%) on MBPP (66.8 vs 80.4) while surpassing it on HumanEval+ (84.2 vs 70.7). On Countdown planning, LaDiR (76.6% CD-4 Pass@1) loses to the task-specific MGDM model (91.5%) but exceeds it on diversity (7.3 vs 3.2 unique solutions) and on CD-5 Pass@100 (75.2 vs 70.4), while beating the general-purpose LLaMA 8B SFT baseline by 30+ points on CD-5. The ablation removing Stage 2 rollout training drops average Pass@1 from 43.5% to 27.9%, confirming that the rollout stage is critical for closing the train-inference gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaDiR sits at the intersection of three research threads: latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022; Lipman et al., 2022), block-level diffusion for variable-length generation (Arriola et al., 2025), and latent reasoning for LLMs (Coconut, CODI, Soft Thinking, TaH+). It departs from prior latent diffusion for text generation (LD4LG, PLANNER), which targeted fluency and diversity of generated text, by explicitly structuring the latent space around reasoning steps via a β-VAE and training with answer-supervised objectives. It also diverges from prior latent reasoning methods that decode continuous tokens autoregressively, instead applying iterative denoising within each block with bidirectional attention, enabling semantic-level self-correction that discrete or AR latent approaches cannot achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that reasoning at the semantic (latent) level via diffusion can outperform both token-level autoregressive CoT and prior continuous-latent AR methods, with the iterative denoising process providing a natural mechanism for self-correction and adaptive test-time compute. The diversity-guidance mechanism addresses a known failure mode of AR sampling (trajectory collapse) and shows that parallel diverse exploration in latent space yields substantially more unique valid solutions, particularly on planning tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models (only 8B evaluated), whether the VAE bottleneck limits expressiveness for very long reasoning chains, and how the method interacts with reinforcement learning post-training, which the authors note as a promising direction given the high pass@k curves.
