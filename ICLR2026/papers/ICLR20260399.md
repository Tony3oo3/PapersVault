## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) apply a fixed patch size across all denoising timesteps, enforcing a constant token budget even at high-noise steps where fine spatial detail is unnecessary. Existing multi-scale approaches (e.g., Pyramidal Flow, cascaded generation) reduce early-stage tokens by changing the latent representation resolution, which introduces trajectory jump points, breaks the continuity equation, and requires ad-hoc re-noising tricks at inference. PPFlow asks whether token reduction can be achieved by varying only the patchification granularity while keeping the latent spatial size fixed.

## Input / Output

The model consumes Gaussian noise in the Stable Diffusion VAE latent space (H/8 × W/8 × 4) conditioned on either a class label (ImageNet, 256×256 or 512×512) or a text prompt (FLUX.1, 512×512 up to 2048×2048). The spatial dimensions of the latent tensor remain unchanged at every timestep; only the number of tokens fed to the transformer blocks varies. The output is a standard RGB image decoded from the denoised latent, with no auxiliary outputs or multi-scale representations exposed to the user.

## Method

PPFlow partitions the flow-matching timestep interval into 2–3 stages and assigns a larger patch size (e.g., 4×4) to high-noise stages and a smaller one (e.g., 2×2) to low-noise stages. Each stage owns its own linear projection matrices in Patchify and Unpatchify, but all stages share the identical DiT transformer blocks, so the only added parameters are the per-stage projection layers. Because the latent spatial resolution never changes, the trajectory satisfies the continuity equation and inference proceeds exactly as in a standard DiT—no re-noising or resolution-switching logic is needed. For transfer learning, DiT block weights are copied directly from a pretrained model, while the new Patchify projection is initialized by averaging the original 2×2-patch weights across the larger patch area and the Unpatchify projection by duplicating them, requiring only ~7–9% additional training FLOPs to converge. Each patch-size model is trained exclusively on the timesteps assigned to that stage, avoiding the train/test inconsistency present in concurrent methods like FlexiDiT and Lumina-Video that train every patch size over all noise levels.

## Results

Training from pretrained SiT-XL/2 with only 8.9% additional training FLOPs, PPF-XL-2 achieves FID-50K of 1.99 (vs. 2.15 for SiT-XL/2) at 256×256 with 62.6% testing FLOPs, while PPF-XL-3 reaches FID 2.23 at 49.4% testing FLOPs. At 512×512, PPF-XL-2 matches DiT-XL/2's FID of 3.04 (achieving 3.01) with 58.7% testing FLOPs. For text-to-image, PPF-FLUX.1 matches FLUX.1-ft on GenEval (0.68), DPG Bench (84.00 at 1024), and T2I-CompBench while reducing testing FLOPs from 100% to 53.9–62.2% across 512–2048 resolutions, yielding 1.61–1.86× inference speedups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SiT-XL/2 (256×256), PPF-XL-2 improves FID from 2.15 to 1.99 and IS from 258.09 to 271.62 while using only 62.6% of the testing FLOPs; PPF-XL-3 pushes IS to 286.67 at 49.4% testing FLOPs with a slight FID increase to 2.23. Against DiT-XL/2 (512×512), PPF-XL-2 matches FID (3.01 vs. 3.04) at 58.7% testing FLOPs, and PPF-XL-3 is within 0.02 FID at 45.4% testing FLOPs. Against FlexiDiT (Table 4), PPFlow achieves FID 2.15 at ~63% FLOPs versus FlexiDiT's 2.25, and FID 2.31 at ~50% FLOPs versus FlexiDiT's 2.64, showing a clear advantage from stage-specific training. Against Pyramidal Flow (Jin et al., 2024) and Lumina-Video (Table 5, 1M steps from scratch), PPFlow substantially outperforms: FID 15.68 vs. 27.69 (Pyramid Rep.+Renoising) and 18.77 (Lumina-Video), with better sFID, IS, and Precision/Recall across the board. Against FLUX.1-ft (text-to-image, Table 3), PPF-FLUX.1 matches or marginally exceeds GenEval, DPG Bench, and T2I-CompBench scores at all three resolutions (512, 1024, 2048) while reducing testing FLOPs by 37.8–46.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPFlow builds directly on the DiT/SiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023; Ma et al., 2024) and the flow matching training objective (Lipman et al., 2022), modifying only the Patchify/Unpatchify linear projections to use stage-dependent patch sizes while sharing all DiT block parameters. It is conceptually distinct from Pyramidal Flow (Jin et al., 2024), which operates on pyramid representations and requires a re-noising trick to handle resolution jumps, and from concurrent work FlexiDiT (Anagnostidis et al., 2025) and Lumina-Video (Liu et al., 2025), which train each patch-size model across all timesteps, creating a train-test mismatch. PPFlow's key departure is operating on full-resolution latents throughout the trajectory, preserving the continuity equation and eliminating the need for any re-noising or resolution-change handling at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1.6–2.0× denoising speedup with comparable FID requires only ~9% additional training FLOPs when adapting from a pretrained DiT, making it a low-cost, high-return modification that could be widely adopted across DiT-based systems. The approach scales cleanly from 256 to 2048 resolution (demonstrated on FLUX.1) and is trivially implementable (just separate linear projections per stage). Open questions include generalization to video generation, jointly learning optimal stage boundaries and patch sizes end-to-end, and combining PPFlow with distillation or step-reduction techniques for further efficiency gains. The slightly worse sFID scores, while explained, suggest subtle spatial distribution shifts that may matter for downstream applications sensitive to object placement.
