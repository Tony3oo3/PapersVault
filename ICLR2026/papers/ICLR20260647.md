## Problem

Single-cell dynamics are inherently unbalanced—cells proliferate and undergo apoptosis—yet existing WFR solvers are unstable, computationally expensive, and rely on costly ODE simulation during training. Prior unbalanced flow-matching methods either regress only velocity fields while neglecting explicit growth dynamics, or still require ODE integration for post-training refinement, leaving no fully simulation-free framework that jointly recovers transport and birth-death under the WFR geometry.

## Input / Output

The method consumes empirical scRNA-seq distributions at K+1 discrete time points, where total cell counts (masses) may differ across snapshots. It outputs two neural-network-parameterized functions—a vector field v_θ(x,t) governing displacement and a scalar growth rate g_φ(x,t) governing local mass creation/annihilation—which together define a continuous WFR geodesic path. The coupling between source and target is pre-computed via mini-batch WFR-OET (entropy-regularized Sinkhorn), and the learned fields can be integrated at inference time to interpolate unobserved intermediate distributions or propagate mass forward in time.

## Method

WFR-FM extends conditional flow matching to the unbalanced setting by jointly regressing a transport velocity field and a growth rate function against targets derived from a 'traveling Gaussian' conditional path, which is the Gaussian-smoothed analogue of the closed-form WFR geodesic (traveling Dirac) between two point masses. The key architectural difference from balanced CFM is a time-dependent mass weight m_t(z) in the conditional loss (CUFM), which accounts for the fact that particle mass evolves along each trajectory; this mass weighting is what makes the regression target consistent with the WFR action. The coupling is obtained by solving the equivalent optimal entropy-transport (OET) problem, with a mini-batch strategy for scalability. A central theoretical result (Theorem 4.2) shows that the CUFM gradient equals the gradient of the intractable unbalanced flow-matching objective, so minimizing CUFM exactly recovers WFR geodesics without any ODE simulation. For multi-time-point data, the global WFR problem decomposes into a sequence of pairwise WFR steps with shared network parameters across all intervals.

## Results

On the synthetic Gene (2D) and Dyngen (5D) datasets, WFR-FM achieves the lowest 1-Wasserstein distance (W₁) of 0.019 and 0.135 respectively, substantially outperforming the best baselines TIGON (0.045, 0.512) and DeepRUOT (0.043, 0.623). Relative Mass Error (RME) is near-zero across all datasets (0.001–0.044), confirming accurate mass tracking. On the hold-one-out real-data interpolation task (Table 3), WFR-FM achieves the best W₁ on EMT (0.298), CITE-seq (37.221), and Mouse (6.586), while remaining competitive on EB (10.157 vs. DeepRUOT's 10.075). For growth-rate recovery, WFR-FM attains a Pearson correlation of 0.9913 with ground truth, exceeding TIGON (0.9705) and DeepRUOT (0.9688).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WFR-FM dominates on the low- and mid-dimensional synthetic benchmarks: on Gene (2D) it reduces W₁ by ~57% over TIGON (0.019 vs. 0.045) and ~56% over DeepRUOT (0.019 vs. 0.043), while on Dyngen (5D) the improvement is more dramatic (0.135 vs. 0.512 for TIGON, 0.623 for DeepRUOT). On the high-dimensional Gaussian (1000D) dataset, WFR-FM (2.233) is competitive with TIGON (2.263) and clearly beats DeepRUOT (3.785) and VGFM (3.010), though Var-RUOT (2.813) and UOT-FM (2.771) remain in between. Among unbalanced methods, WFR-FM achieves the lowest RME on Gene (0.001) and Dyngen (0.005), confirming superior mass conservation. On real scRNA-seq hold-one-out interpolation, WFR-FM wins on EMT (0.298 vs. VGFM's 0.301), CITE-seq (37.221 vs. 37.386), and Mouse (6.586 vs. 6.847 for DeepRUOT), while DeepRUOT edges it slightly on EB (10.075 vs. 10.157). For growth-rate estimation, WFR-FM achieves the highest Pearson correlation (0.9913) versus TIGON (0.9705) and DeepRUOT (0.9688), and far exceeds Action Matching (0.5851). Balanced FM baselines (MMFM, Metric FM, SF2M, MIOFlow) cannot report RME and consistently trail all unbalanced methods on W₁, underscoring the importance of explicit mass modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

WFR-FM sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines: (1) flow matching as a simulation-free training paradigm for learning velocity fields (Lipman et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2024a), and (2) the Wasserstein-Fisher-Rao metric for unbalanced optimal transport that couples displacement with mass creation/annihilation (Chizat et al., 2018a; Liero et al., 2016). The key departure from prior unbalanced FM work (Eyring et al., 2024; Corso et al., 2025; Cao et al., 2025) is that those methods regress only velocity fields and neglect explicit growth dynamics, while VGFM (Wang et al., 2025) jointly models both but still requires ODE simulation for post-training refinement and uses a coupling mechanism inconsistent with WFR geometry. WFR-FM is the first to provide a fully simulation-free, OT-principled formulation where the conditional path (traveling Gaussian) is derived directly from the closed-form WFR Dirac-to-Dirac geodesic, yielding a theoretical guarantee that minimizing the loss exactly recovers WFR geodesics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For single-cell biology, where destructive sequencing means only sparse snapshots are available and cell populations are inherently unbalanced due to proliferation and apoptosis, WFR-FM offers a scalable and stable alternative to ODE-based solvers (TIGON, DeepRUOT, Var-RUOT) that suffer from training instabilities and high computational cost. The practical limitation is that WFR-FM still requires solving a static WFR-OET coupling (via Sinkhorn iterations or mini-batch approximation), which can be expensive for very large datasets; the paper acknowledges this and uses mini-batch strategies as a workaround. Open questions include incorporating uncertainty quantification for noisy scRNA-seq data, extending to other unbalanced transport functionals beyond WFR (e.g., linear growth penalties), and whether the mini-batch coupling approximation degrades solution quality at scale.
