## Problem

Diffusion LLM decoders recompute QKV for all tokens at every denoising step and layer, despite KV states changing only marginally across most steps, especially in shallow layers. Prior acceleration methods (Fast-dLLM, dKV-Cache, DeepCache) rely on fixed-period or block-wise schedules that recompute when nothing has changed and miss updates when rapid semantic revisions occur, leaving substantial redundant computation on the table. No existing method adaptively decides both when and where to refresh the KV cache based on the model's own attention dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained diffusion LLMs (LLaDA-Instruct, LLaDA-1.5, LLaDA-V, Dream-7B) with no retraining, taking a text prompt (or image+text for LLaDA-V) and producing a generated text sequence of configurable length (256–1024 tokens). Decoding uses a sliding window of size β (typically 16–32) over the leftmost masked positions, with confidence-aware parallel unmasking (threshold ε) to predict multiple tokens per step. The method is architecture-agnostic and requires only two inference-time hyperparameters: the attention similarity threshold γ and the window size β.

## Method

Elastic-Cache couples two adaptive mechanisms: (1) an attention-aware drift test that identifies the most-attended decoded token per layer and computes the cosine similarity of its attention weights between consecutive denoising steps; if similarity drops below threshold γ at layer l*, a refresh is triggered; and (2) a layer-aware update that recomputes KV only for layers l*+1 through L while reusing shallow-layer caches and block-caching distant MASK tokens outside the sliding window. The design rests on three empirical observations: distant MASK tokens act primarily as a length-bias prior, KV drift increases monotonically with layer depth, and the most-attended token exhibits the smallest KV drift, providing a conservative lower bound on cache staleness for all other tokens. A theoretical analysis (Theorem A.9) proves the most-attended token's drift is bounded by the average drift plus an O(√d_k/(R_ℓ√N)) term, validating the attention-based proxy. This yields up to 45.1× speedup on GSM8K (512 tokens) with LLaDA-1.5 while maintaining or exceeding baseline accuracy.

## Results

On LLaDA-1.5 (GSM8K, 512 tokens), Elastic-Cache achieves 81.35% accuracy at 117.2 tokens/sec, a 45.1× speedup over the 2.6 t/s baseline, while matching the baseline's accuracy exactly. On LLaDA-Instruct (GSM8K, 512 tokens), it reaches 77.71% at 90.1 t/s (25.2×), surpassing Fast-dLLM's 74.83% at 44.0 t/s (12.3×) in both accuracy and throughput. On Dream-7B (GSM8K, 512), Elastic-Cache delivers a 21.4× speedup (169.4 t/s) at 75.6% accuracy. In the multimodal setting (LLaDA-V, MathVerse-256), it achieves 29.2% at 32.3 t/s versus Fast-dLLM's 26.8% at 30.3 t/s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Elastic-Cache consistently outperforms Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025) on both accuracy and throughput across all tested models and benchmarks; for example, on LLaDA-1.5 GSM8K-512 it achieves 81.35% at 117.2 t/s versus Fast-dLLM's 80.82% at 36.8 t/s, and on MBPP-512 it reaches 39.00% at 32.8 t/s versus 36.20% at 17.8 t/s. Against the unmodified LLaDA and LLaDA-1.5 baselines, Elastic-Cache delivers 8.7×–45.1× speedups while matching or exceeding accuracy (e.g., 81.35% vs. 81.35% on GSM8K-512; 41.20% vs. 38.00% on MBPP-256). Compared to dKV-Cache (Ma et al., 2025), Elastic-Cache achieves substantially higher accuracy (83.7% vs. 67.02%) and throughput (139.4 vs. 14.82 t/s) on GSM8K-512 with LLaDA-1.5. Against dLLM-Cache (Liu et al., 2025), Elastic-Cache wins on both accuracy (83.7% vs. 80.97%) and throughput (139.4 vs. 16.84 t/s). Versus DeepCache (Ma et al., 2024) with fixed intervals N=10/20, Elastic-Cache achieves 2.3× higher throughput (139.4 vs. 58.4–60.9 t/s) at comparable or slightly better accuracy (83.7% vs. 81.4–83.1%). On Dream-7B, Elastic-Cache achieves 21.4× and 5.5× speedups on GSM8K and HumanEval respectively while improving accuracy over both the Dream baseline and Fast-dLLM. In the multimodal LLaDA-V setting, Elastic-Cache matches or exceeds Fast-dLLM on MathVista (55.9% vs. 55.9% at 256) and MathVerse (29.2% vs. 26.8% at 256) while delivering higher throughput (32.3 vs. 30.3 t/s on MathVerse-256).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KV caching paradigm from autoregressive transformers (where cached keys remain invariant) to diffusion LLMs, where bidirectional attention causes all token representations to evolve across denoising steps. It builds directly on Fast-dLLM's block-wise KV caching and confidence-aware decoding framework, but departs from all prior diffusion-specific caching methods (dKV-Cache, dLLM-Cache, DeepCache) by replacing fixed-interval or fixed-period refresh schedules with an adaptive, attention-aware drift test that jointly decides *when* and *where* (which layers) to recompute. The sliding-window decoding mechanism is a refinement of Fast-dLLM's block-wise approach, motivated by the observation that nearby MASK tokens attend strongly to each other while distant ones act as a length prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Elastic-Cache addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for diffusion LLMs: the redundant all-tokens, all-layers QKV recomputation at every denoising step. The 45× speedup on longer sequences while maintaining or exceeding baseline accuracy makes diffusion LLM inference practically viable, and the training-free, architecture-agnostic design means it can be applied to any existing diffusion LLM checkpoint without retraining. Open questions include whether the fixed cosine-similarity threshold γ can be replaced by a learned predictor, how the method interacts with speculative decoding or hardware-aware scheduling, and whether the same principles transfer to autoregressive LLMs where KV invariance already holds. The method's effectiveness being tightly coupled to model accuracy (better models → fewer cache updates needed) suggests it will scale favorably as diffusion LLMs improve.
