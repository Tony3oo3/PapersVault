## Problem

Despite growing evidence of connections between transformers and energy-based models (e.g., in-context learning as gradient descent, Energy Transformer for masked tokens), it remained unknown how to cast the standard autoregressive GPT setting into a well-defined energy-based framework. The Energy Transformer's bidirectional, masked-token design is fundamentally incompatible with causal next-token generation, where every token must evolve into the following token rather than filling in missing parts of a fixed pattern.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of N tokens embedded in D dimensions and produces the next token via a dynamical process: each token starts from the previous token's embedding and evolves through T recurrent steps of energy minimization on a per-token energy landscape. Unlike a standard transformer's single forward pass through distinct layers, NRGPT applies a single weight-shared block recurrently, so the 'depth' of the model is the number of gradient steps rather than the number of distinct layer parameters. The causal attention mask ensures token A's energy depends only on tokens B < A, preserving autoregressive information flow.

## Method

NRGPT defines a per-token energy E_A = E_A^AT + E_A^FF whose gradient with respect to normalized tokens g_A yields a transformer-like update: the attention energy is a log-sum-exp over previous tokens (producing a softmax-weighted attention update), and the feed-forward energy is a Dense Associative Memory-style scalar function (producing an MLP update). The inference rule is x^(t+1) = x^(t) − η ∂E/∂g^(t), where η is a learnable inference rate matrix that replaces the standard attention projection and FF output weights. The paper proves that under conditions on η (e.g., η = c·diag(γ) for LayerNorm/RMSNorm), the energy provably decreases after a transient convergence period of preceding tokens — a phenomenon termed asymptotic stability. The architecture targets the parallel (GPT-J-style) transformer design, and the single NRGPT block is applied recurrently with weight sharing to replace multiple distinct transformer layers.

## Results

On OpenWebText (OWT) at n_embed=768, NRGPT_H_FF2W (90M params) achieves a perplexity of 104 and grammar quality score of 0.966, compared to GPT (124M params) at perplexity 75 and GPT_Rec_Parallel (85M params) at perplexity 99. At a more comparable ~125M parameter scale, NRGPT achieves 29.3% average MMLU accuracy (five-shot), outperforming both GPT (28.0%) and RecGPT (26.0%). On ListOps, NRGPT variants reach the learning transition at 2.4×10⁴ (FF1) and 2.98×10⁴ (FF2W) parameters, closely matching the recurrent GPT baseline at 2.3×10⁴. On Shakespeare, NRGPT achieves validation loss parity with recurrent GPT across parameter sizes while exhibiting notably less overfitting at large model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the deep GPT baseline (124M params), NRGPT (90M params) uses ~34M fewer parameters but achieves a higher perplexity (104 vs. 75) and slightly lower grammar quality (0.966 vs. 0.978), though it produces more lexically diverse text (Distinct-1: 0.674 vs. 0.619; Distinct-2: 0.984 vs. 0.965). Against the recurrent GPT baseline (GPT_Rec_Parallel, 85M params), NRGPT is very close in perplexity (104 vs. 99) and grammar quality (0.966 vs. 0.976), with NRGPT showing a small disadvantage on fluency but an advantage on diversity. At equal parameter count (~125M), NRGPT clearly outperforms both baselines on MMLU five-shot accuracy (29.3% vs. 28.0% for GPT and 26.0% for RecGPT), suggesting the energy-based formulation may scale favorably. On ListOps, all three model variants reach comparable accuracy with similar parameter-transition points, indicating the energy-based update rule does not sacrifice task performance. On Shakespeare, NRGPT matches recurrent GPT in validation loss but does not overfit at large parameter sizes, a behavior the authors attribute to the energy landscape structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

NRGPT adapts the Energy Transformer (Hoover et al., 2023) from its original masked-token, bidirectional setting to causal autoregressive language modeling, replacing the global sequence energy with per-token energies E_A that respect the causal mask. The architecture inherits the parallel transformer design from GPT-J and the Dense Associative Memory formulation for the feed-forward energy, while the inference rate matrix η generalizes the fixed scalar step of ET to a learnable preconditioner. Conceptually, the paper complements the line of work showing transformers implicitly perform gradient descent during in-context learning (Von Oswald et al., 2023; Ahn et al., 2024) by making inference explicitly a gradient-based energy minimization, rather than proving it is one in hindsight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymptotic stability proof—that causal structure guarantees each token's energy monotonically decreases once all preceding tokens have converged—provides a principled dynamical-systems foundation for transformer inference that was previously only observed empirically. However, the practical impact is tempered by NRGPT's 1–2× FLOP overhead relative to equivalent recurrent GPTs, greater hyperparameter sensitivity, and a tendency to resist overfitting that is beneficial at small scale but undesirable for the massive datasets used in modern LLMs. Open questions include whether the energy perspective enables the alignment regularizers and variable-computation early-stopping the authors envision, and whether the FLOP penalty can be eliminated by adapting the multi-head attention convention (narrower J_h per head) that standard GPTs already exploit.
