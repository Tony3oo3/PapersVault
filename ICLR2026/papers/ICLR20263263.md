## Problem

In noisy low-tubal-rank tensor recovery via factorized gradient descent (FGD), over-parameterization (using an estimated rank R > true rank r) causes the recovery error to grow linearly with R under standard spectral initialization. The paper asks whether an error bound depending only on the true tubal-rank r can be achieved even when R is significantly overestimated, and whether such a bound is attainable in practice without prior knowledge of the target tensor.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263263/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes m noisy linear measurements y = M(X*) + s of a symmetric tensor X* ∈ R^{n×n×k} with unknown tubal-rank r, along with an overestimated rank R ≥ r and a measurement operator M satisfying the (2r+1, δ) t-RIP. It outputs a recovered tensor U_t * U_t^T (a t-product factorization) whose Frobenius-norm error is bounded by O~(nkrσ²/m), independent of R. For real-data experiments, the input is a partially observed color image tensor (481×321×3) under Bernoulli sampling with Gaussian noise, and the output is the completed tensor evaluated via PSNR and relative error.

## Method

The core contribution is showing that FGD with a near-zero Gaussian initialization (α ≈ 10^{-10}) achieves nearly minimax optimal recovery error O~(nkrσ²/m) that is independent of the overestimated tubal-rank R, in contrast to spectral initialization whose error scales as O~(nkRσ²/m). The analysis decomposes the FGD trajectory into four phases—alignment, signal amplification, local refinement, and overfitting—where small initialization ensures the over-parameterization term ‖U_t * W_{t,⊥}‖ remains negligible during the critical local refinement phase, while the signal term grows exponentially to recover the true tensor. A validation-based early stopping criterion (selecting t̃ = argmin_t e_t over a held-out set) provably halts the algorithm before the overfitting phase, requiring only m_val ≳ r²κ⁸ log T validation samples. The theoretical framework relies on the (2r+1, δ) t-RIP condition (rather than the (4R, δ) condition in prior work), yielding both a tighter sample complexity and the first rank-independent error bound for noisy tubal-rank tensor recovery.

## Results

Theoretically, FGD with small initialization achieves a recovery error of $\tilde{O}(nkr\sigma^2/m)$ that depends only on the true tubal-rank $r$, improving upon the prior bound $\tilde{O}(nkR\sigma^2/m)$ from Liu et al. (2024b) which degrades with the overestimated rank $R$. On the Berkeley Segmentation Dataset (50 color images, 481×321×3), FGD-best achieves the best PSNR of 23.84 dB and lowest RE of 0.1395 at p=0.3, σ=0.07, outperforming the strongest baseline GTNN (23.15 dB, RE 0.1481) by ~0.7 dB. FGD-ES (with validation-based early stopping) achieves 23.66 dB / RE 0.1426 under the same setting, requiring no prior knowledge of the true rank. In synthetic experiments, small initialization matches the exact-rank baseline error even when R=3r, while spectral and large random initializations show error growing linearly with R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGD with small initialization (both FGD-ES and FGD-best) outperforms all five baselines—TCTF, TNN, TC-RE, UTF, and GTNN—on both PSNR and RE across all four sampling/noise configurations. The closest competitor is GTNN (non-convex generalized tensor nuclear norm), which trails FGD-best by 0.63–0.79 dB in PSNR and by 0.007–0.028 in RE; the gap widens under higher noise (σ=0.1) where GTNN drops to 21.11 dB while FGD-best maintains 22.86 dB at p=0.3. Convex TNN and rank-estimation methods TC-RE are consistently 1.5–2.5 dB below FGD-best, while TCTF and UTF are substantially worse (6–8 dB lower PSNR), particularly at the lower sampling rate p=0.2. Theoretically, the key advantage over Liu et al. (2024b) is that the error bound is independent of the overestimated rank R (depending only on true rank r), whereas their bound scales linearly with R; compared to Karnik et al. (2025), this paper extends the noiseless small-initialization analysis to the noisy setting with a four-phase decomposition yielding a near-minimax optimal guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Burer-Monteiro factorization framework to the t-SVD/tubal-rank tensor setting, building directly on Liu et al. (2024b) who first applied factorized gradient descent (FGD) to noisy low-tubal-rank tensor recovery with spectral initialization, and on Karnik et al. (2025) who proved global convergence of FGD with small initialization in the noiseless tubal tensor case. The key departure is a four-phase trajectory analysis (alignment, signal amplification, local refinement, overfitting) that replaces the two-phase decomposition in Karnik et al., enabling precise tracking of noise propagation and yielding the first recovery error bound in the noisy over-parameterized tensor setting that is independent of the overestimated tubal-rank R. The paper also introduces a minimax lower bound for noisy tubal-rank tensor recovery, a result that had no prior analogue in the tensor literature (unlike the matrix case covered by Candes & Plan, 2011).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rank-independent error bound resolves a practical pain point: practitioners who must overestimate tubal-rank (since the true rank is rarely known) no longer face linearly growing recovery error, and the validation-based early stopping procedure makes the optimal stopping time achievable without any prior knowledge of the target tensor. The nearly minimax-optimal guarantee (gap only in condition number κ) establishes a clear statistical limit for the problem. Open questions include extending the analysis to the asymmetric factorization (discussed briefly in Remark 6), to higher-order tensors beyond third-order, and to non-Gaussian or structured noise models; the four-phase structure's robustness to these generalizations remains unverified.
