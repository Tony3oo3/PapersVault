## Problem

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) suffer from two root-cause bottlenecks: the high-dimensional, multi-objective, non-convex optimization landscape that makes gradient-descent training slow and inaccurate, and the non-causal treatment of time as an extra spatial dimension that prevents accurate long-time and high-frequency simulations. Prior work addresses symptoms (loss balancing, regularization, architectural tweaks) rather than these structural issues, and no existing method simultaneously achieves fast training, high precision, and inherent temporal causality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a PDE specification (linear spatial operator L, optional nonlinear operator N, forcing f, boundary condition g, initial condition u0), a spatial domain Ω ⊂ ℝ^d, and a set of collocation points X ∈ ℝ^{Nc×d}; no solution data is required (self-supervised). It outputs the PDE solution û(x,t) evaluated at arbitrary test grid points, with the time dependence fully encoded in a low-dimensional vector of output-layer parameters C(t) ∈ ℝ^{1×(r+1)} that is evolved by an ODE solver. The SVD layer compresses the basis to r ≪ M dimensions, and the entire pipeline runs on CPU without GPUs.

## Method

Frozen-PINN enforces space-time separation by sampling and freezing spatial hidden-layer parameters (via data-agnostic ELM or data-dependent SWIM sampling of tanh basis functions), so that all time dependence resides in the output-layer coefficients C(t). The PDE is reformulated as a low-dimensional ODE for C(t) using a pseudo-inverse projection onto the frozen spatial basis, and this ODE is integrated with adaptive solvers (RK45, LSODA) instead of gradient descent. The PINN loss is fully decoupled: the initial condition is fit by a single least-squares solve, boundary conditions are enforced either by a boundary-compliant linear layer (satisfying them by construction) or by an augmented ODE correction term, and the PDE residual drives the ODE. An SVD layer orthogonalizes and truncates the basis, reducing the ODE system dimension by up to 20× and accelerating training by up to 75×. Temporal causality is enforced by construction because the ODE evolves C(t) forward in time in a Markovian fashion, eliminating the need for curriculum learning or causal loss weighting.

## Results

Across nine PDE benchmarks, Frozen-PINNs achieve 45–84,000× faster training than state-of-the-art PINNs at comparable or superior accuracy, all on CPU without GPUs. In the high-precision regime, Frozen-PINN-swim reaches relative L² errors of 8.42e-9 (advection, β=40), 1.42e-7 (Euler-Bernoulli Winkler), and 2.27e-7 (Burgers), matching or approaching IGA-FEM fidelity (1.17e-10, 2.70e-8, 1.12e-7 respectively). For high-dimensional problems, Frozen-PINN-elm solves the 100-d heat equation in 0.13 s with 4.12e-4 error, outperforming GPU-trained PINNs by over 1000× in speed and an order of magnitude in accuracy. The SVD layer further reduces ODE system dimension by up to 20× and accelerates training by up to 75×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Frozen-PINNs decisively outperform all gradient-descent-based PINN variants (Raissi et al., Wang et al. Causal PINN, Hao et al. Pinnacle, Kiyani et al., Chen et al., McClenny & Braga-Neto, Urbán et al.) by 45–84,000× in training time while achieving equal or superior accuracy; for example, on the Burgers equation, Frozen-PINN-swim (5.25 s, 2.27e-7) beats PINN(RAdm+BFGS) (1070 s, 6e-6) by 203× in speed and 26× in accuracy. Against vanilla random-feature baselines (ELM, SWIM from Bolager et al. and Huang et al.), Frozen-PINNs succeed where those methods completely fail at high advection speeds (β=40), thanks to space-time separation and ODE-based time evolution. Against mesh-based IGA-FEM, Frozen-PINNs are slightly slower (0.1–14.5×) but match accuracy within 1–2 orders of magnitude, while retaining mesh-free flexibility and high-dimensional scalability where IGA-FEM suffers from the curse of dimensionality. Against FEM on the nonlinear diffusion equation, Frozen-PINN-elm is 4.83× faster at comparable accuracy (2.60e-3 vs. 2.68e-3). For high-dimensional benchmarks, Frozen-PINN-elm outperforms He et al.'s 100-d heat PINN by 1085× in speed and 15× in accuracy, and outperforms Wang & Dong's 10-d heat PINN by 574× in speed with comparable error. Against Kapoor et al.'s beam-simulation PINNs (Adaptive, Self-adaptive, Wavelet, Causal), Frozen-PINN-swim is 37,000–95,000× faster on the Euler-Bernoulli Winkler equation while achieving 1.42e-7 error versus their 3e-2 to 5.33e+0 errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

Frozen-PINN departs fundamentally from the PINN paradigm (Raissi et al., 2019) by eliminating gradient-descent-based optimization entirely, replacing it with a space-time separation principle: spatial hidden-layer parameters are sampled once (via ELM or the data-dependent SWIM strategy from Bolager et al., 2023) and frozen, while time-dependent output-layer parameters are evolved via adaptive ODE solvers initialized by a least-squares fit to the initial condition. This contrasts with prior PINN variants (causal PINNs, adaptive PINNs, wavelet PINNs) that still rely on iterative back-propagation over a coupled multi-objective loss, and with randomized-network PDE solvers (PIELM, ELM-based methods) that treat time as an additional spatial dimension rather than enforcing temporal causality by construction. The SVD layer for basis orthogonalization and the boundary-compliant layer are novel architectural additions that further decouple the optimization problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing gradient descent, Frozen-PINN addresses what the authors identify as the root cause of PINN training difficulty (high-dimensional, non-convex, multi-objective optimization) rather than its symptoms, yielding 4–5 orders of magnitude faster training on CPU without GPUs while matching or exceeding IGA-FEM accuracy in low dimensions and scaling to 100-dimensional PDEs where mesh-based methods are infeasible. Key limitations include the assumption that the PDE is known (limiting direct use for inverse problems), the Kolmogorov n-width barrier for the vanilla (non-resampling) variant, and the open challenge of spatial complexity in problems like Navier-Stokes. Open theoretical questions include universal approximation guarantees for the frozen-basis ansatz and a rigorous understanding of how periodic resampling of SWIM bases can break the Kolmogorov barrier.
