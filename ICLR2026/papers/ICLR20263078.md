## Problem

Rendering photorealistic, view-consistent images directly from sparse point clouds without per-scene optimization remains difficult: traditional rasterization produces holes and aliasing, while naively applying image diffusion models introduces per-view stochasticity that breaks multi-view coherence. The paper asks whether a purely point-based pipeline—without auxiliary 3D representations like Gaussians or NeRFs—can achieve both high fidelity and cross-view consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263078/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a colored point cloud (3D positions + RGB) and calibrated camera parameters (3×3 intrinsics, 4×4 extrinsics) as input, and produces photorealistic rendered images at arbitrary viewpoints. During training, rendered images are randomly cropped to 256×256; at inference, the original resolution is preserved (up to ~1K on a single RTX 3090). Two variants are provided: DiffPBR-Q (5-step sampling, ~2 FPS) for maximum quality and DiffPBR-E (single-step, ~10 FPS) for efficiency. The point cloud is internally augmented with per-point 3D Gaussian noise vectors and adaptive isotropic scales before splatting.

## Method

DiffPBR is a two-stage pipeline. First, Adaptive CoNo-Splatting assigns each point a KNN-derived scale (globally clamped by a learnable β optimized via complementary coverage and compactness losses) and a fixed 3D Gaussian noise vector, then differentially splats the combined 6-channel features to produce an RGB image I_c, a structured noise map I_ε, and a soft mask I_m. Second, a Spatial-Aware Residual Diffusion Module (RDDM) with a U-Net backbone refines the splatted image: instead of denoising from pure 2D Gaussian noise, it predicts a residual-noise target that combines the rendered-to-GT image difference with the 3D-consistent splatted noise, starting the reverse process from a truncated timestep T′ where the GT coefficient vanishes. Because I_ε is rendered from the same 3D point cloud across all views, it inherently encodes depth/occlusion geometry and preserves cross-view correlation, eliminating the need for explicit temporal or multi-view consistency losses. The multi-step variant (DiffPBR-Q) uses ε-prediction with 5 sampling steps, while the one-step variant (DiffPBR-E) uses x₀-prediction supervised by L2 + LPIPS + Gram losses, achieving ~3× faster inference with only marginal quality loss.

## Results

On the three primary benchmarks, DiffPBR-Q achieves PSNR of 23.28 (ScanNet), 28.45 (DTU), and 41.27 (THuman2.0), outperforming the strongest generalizable baseline PFGS by 3.4–6.5 dB across datasets. The one-step variant DiffPBR-E retains most of the quality (22.92/28.15/40.89 PSNR) while running at 10 FPS versus PFGS's 3.6 FPS. Training cost drops from ~41 GPU hours (PFGS) to ~8 GPU hours, and on the unbounded Tanks and Temples dataset DiffPBR reaches 25.25 PSNR, surpassing even per-scene-optimized 3DGS (24.69).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest generalizable baseline PFGS, DiffPBR-Q improves PSNR by 3.4 dB on ScanNet (23.28 vs 19.86), 3.0 dB on DTU (28.45 vs 25.44), and 6.5 dB on THuman2.0 (41.27 vs 34.74), while also reducing training time from ~41 to ~8 GPU hours and increasing inference speed from 3.6 to 10 FPS (one-step variant). Against earlier point-based methods (NPBG, NPBG++, TriVol), the gains are even larger (5–11 dB on ScanNet), confirming that the residual diffusion refinement substantially closes the gap left by rasterization artifacts. In the per-scene comparison (Table 19), DiffPBR outperforms 3DGS on ScanNet (31.62 vs 31.29 PSNR) and DTU (28.33 vs 24.92 PSNR) without any scene-specific optimization, and is comparable on THuman2.0 (41.21 vs 41.57). On the unbounded Tanks and Temples benchmark, DiffPBR (25.25 PSNR) surpasses both per-scene 3DGS (24.69) and generalizable PFGS (24.31), and also leads under sparse COLMAP point clouds (22.97 vs 22.30 for RPBG). The one-step DiffPBR-E variant sacrifices only ~0.4 dB over DiffPBR-Q while tripling inference speed, making it competitive with real-time renderers on quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of neural point-based graphics (NPBG, NPBG++, PFGS) and diffusion-based image restoration. It departs from the dominant paradigm of learning per-point descriptors (neural textures in NPBG, Gaussian attributes in PFGS) by working purely on original point colors and delegating detail recovery to a residual diffusion module. The 3D-consistent noise idea draws from recent 3D-consistent generation work (ConsistDreamer, SyncNoise, GSD), but here the noise is obtained for free by splatting per-point Gaussian vectors rather than by learned noise prediction or cross-view feature exchange. The adaptive KNN-based scale with a single global β regularizer replaces the heavier CNN/MLP-based scale predictors used in PFGS, trading per-point expressiveness for simplicity and speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffPBR demonstrates that a purely point-based pipeline—without auxiliary 3D representations like Gaussians or NeRFs—can match or exceed methods that rely on them, while being substantially faster to train (8 vs. 41 GPU hours) and to render (10 vs. 3.6 FPS for the one-step variant). The residual diffusion formulation and 3D-consistent noise guidance offer a general recipe that could transfer to other sparse-primitive rendering settings. Key limitations remain: the model cannot hallucinate large missing regions due to the absence of strong image priors, and high-resolution inference is still below real-time. Whether incorporating pretrained image diffusion backbones (e.g., SDXL) can close the gap on large occlusions while preserving the 3D-consistency benefit is an open direction.
