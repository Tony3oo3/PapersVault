## Problem

Existing benchmarks for evaluating physical commonsense in video generation suffer from limited scale (160–688 prompts), lack of fine-grained physical rule annotations, sim-to-real gaps, or rigid one-to-one prompt-to-law mappings that ignore imperfect semantic adherence. No prior benchmark combines a large, action-centric prompt set with human-rated physical rule violations and an open automatic evaluator, leaving the physical fidelity of modern video models largely unmeasured.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264021/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes detailed text prompts (average 138 tokens after upsampling from ~16-token originals) describing multi-event real-world actions, and short generated videos (<6 s) from nine text-to-video models. Evaluation outputs are three-way: a 5-point Likert semantic adherence score, a 5-point Likert physical commonsense score (assessed independently of the prompt), and per-rule binary/CBD classifications for candidate physical laws. The joint performance metric is the fraction of videos scoring ≥4 on both SA and PC simultaneously, and the auto-evaluator (VIDEOPHY-2-AUTOEVAL, 7B params) predicts all three outputs from a single video–prompt pair.

## Method

The dataset is built in four stages: (1) 197 seed actions curated from Kinetics/UCF-101/SSv2 via dual-annotator filtering; (2) 3,940 multi-event prompts generated by Gemini-2.0-Flash-Exp and upsampled with Mistral-NeMo-12B; (3) candidate physical rules extracted by captioning generated videos with a VLM and prompting for three rules per video, ensuring rules are grounded in what the model actually produced rather than the prompt; (4) a 1,200-prompt hard subset selected where CogVideoX-5B achieves zero joint performance. For scalable evaluation, VIDEOPHY-2-AUTOEVAL fine-tunes a VideoCon-Physics 7B video-language model with multi-task supervised learning on ~50K human annotations (SA score, PC score, rule classification) using a shared backbone for inter-task transfer, achieving 42% average Pearson correlation on unseen prompts—outperforming Gemini-2.0-Flash-Exp by 81% on SA and 236% on PC.

## Results

On the full VIDEOPHY-2 dataset (590 test prompts), the best-performing video model Wan2.2-27B-A14B achieves only 55.4% joint performance (SA≥4 and PC≥4), dropping to 47.7% on the hard subset of 60 physics-rich actions. The second-best model, Wan2.1-14B, scores 32.6% (full) and 21.9% (hard), while closed models Sora and Ray2 achieve 23.3% and 20.3% respectively. For the automatic evaluator, VIDEOPHY-2-AUTOEVAL achieves 42.0% average Pearson correlation on unseen prompts, outperforming the best baseline (VideoCon-Physics at 28.5%) by 47.4% and Gemini-2.0-Flash-Exp by 127%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among video generative models, Wan2.2-27B-A14B dominates all others, achieving 55.4% joint performance on the full set and 47.7% on the hard subset—nearly double the next-best open model Wan2.1-14B (32.6%/21.9%). Notably, closed models underperform open ones: Sora (23.3%) and Ray2 (20.3%) trail CogVideoX-5B (25.0%) and Cosmos-Diff-7B (24.1%), suggesting proprietary models are not superior in physical commonsense. CogVideoX-5B, used as the reference for constructing the hard subset, scores 0.0% on it by design. For the auto-evaluator, VIDEOPHY-2-AUTOEVAL outperforms all baselines: it beats VideoCon-Physics (28.5%) by 47.4% in average Pearson correlation, Gemini-2.0-Flash-Exp (18.5%) by 127%, and achieves 78.7% physical rule classification accuracy versus Gemini-2.0-Flash-Exp's 59.2% (+32.9% relative). On joint score F1, VIDEOPHY-2-AUTOEVAL reaches 51.1% versus VideoCon-Physics's 2.6%, a dramatic improvement in discriminative power.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends VIDEOPHY (Bansal et al., ICLR 2025), which first evaluated physical commonsense in generated videos using 688 prompts focused on material interactions. VIDEOPHY-2 shifts the evaluation paradigm from single-material scenarios to action-centric, multi-event real-world activities (sports, object interactions), scales the dataset to 3940 prompts, and introduces fine-grained physical rule annotations that prior benchmarks (PhyGenBench's 160 prompts, Physics-IQ's frame-conditioned prediction) lacked. The auto-evaluator builds on VideoCon-Physics from VIDEOPHY but extends it to a multi-task setup covering semantic adherence, physical commonsense scoring, and rule classification simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even the strongest model (Wan2.2-27B-A14B) achieves only 47.7% joint performance on the hard subset, and that conservation laws (mass, momentum) are the most frequently violated physical principles (~40% violation rate), provides a concrete, actionable diagnostic for where video generation models fail. Critically, the near-zero correlation between physical commonsense and standard video quality metrics (aesthetics: r=0.09, motion: r=0.002) challenges the assumption that optimizing visual fidelity will automatically yield physically plausible videos. Open questions include whether physics-aware training objectives or larger-scale physics-grounded data can close this gap, and whether the action-centric framing generalizes to longer, multi-scene videos beyond the <6s clips evaluated here.
