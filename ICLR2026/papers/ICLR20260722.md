## Problem

Discrete diffusion models for image generation (e.g., MaskGIT) consistently underperform their continuous counterparts, which the authors attribute to two design flaws: the single [mask] absorbing state provides far less variability than Gaussian noise, limiting the model's expressive capacity, and the confidence-based Gumbel-max re-mask sampler introduces handcrafted randomness that compromises probabilistic fidelity and requires extensive hyperparameter tuning, especially at large vocabulary sizes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260722/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the model takes a single class label and generates a 256×256 RGB image from scratch; for inpainting, it takes a partially masked image (with a region replaced by noise tokens) plus a class label and reconstructs the missing content. Images are tokenized into 256 discrete tokens (IBQ-f16, 16×16 spatial) or 1024 tokens (LlamaGen-f8, 32×32 spatial) from a 16384-entry codebook, and generation proceeds over 20–64 sampling steps with classifier-free guidance.

## Method

ReDDiT replaces the single absorbing [mask] token with a set of m absorbing states (noise capacity, e.g., 1024 for IBQ, 128 for LlamaGen-f16), so the forward process transitions from valid tokens E_d to the absorbing subspace M_m with uniform random assignment. At each reverse sampling step, a rehash operation shuffles all currently-absorbed tokens to new random absorbing states before the network predicts, preventing overly deterministic decoding and encouraging path diversity. The rehash sampler computes transition probabilities via softmax over network logits combined with the analytical DDM reverse kernel, then draws tokens using torch.multinomial (low-discrepancy categorical sampling) instead of Gumbel-max argmax. Training uses a time-weighted cross-entropy objective derived from continuous-time DDM theory (Eq. 4) plus a DINOv2-based representation alignment (RepA) auxiliary loss for faster convergence, within a DiT architecture augmented with 2D-RoPE and min-SNR weighting.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256, ReDDiT-XL_f8 achieves a gFID of 1.61 and IS of 318.5 with 64 sampling steps, while the smaller ReDDiT-L reaches gFID 2.13 and IS 294.7 in just 20 steps. This represents a dramatic improvement over the MaskGIT baseline (gFID 6.18) and the prior DDM method ITM (gFID 5.30). ReDDiT-XL (gFID 1.74) matches or exceeds continuous diffusion models such as DiT-XL/2 (2.27) and SiT-XL (2.42) while using far fewer inference steps (32 vs. 250).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReDDiT decisively outperforms all MVTM baselines: MaskGIT (gFID 6.18) is beaten by 4.05 points by ReDDiT-L (2.13), and even the fine-tuned TiTok-S-128ft (1.97) is surpassed by ReDDiT-XL (1.74). Against the prior DDM method ITM (gFID 5.30), ReDDiT-L improves by 3.17 points. Among continuous diffusion models, ReDDiT-XL (1.74) beats DiT-XL/2 (2.27) and SiT-XL (2.42) with 32 steps versus 250, though MDTv2 (1.58) retains a slight edge. In the autoregressive category, ReDDiT-XL_f8 (1.61) outperforms VAR-d30 (1.97), LlamaGen-3B (2.18), and RandAR-XXL (2.15) while requiring fewer steps (64 vs. 576/88/10). In Table 2, using the same LlamaGen-f16 tokenizer, ReDDiT-L (gFID 2.41) beats both LlamaGen-LAR (3.80) and RandAR-LAR (2.55), and with the IBQ tokenizer, ReDDiT-L (2.13) outperforms IBQ-BAR (2.88).

## Relation to Prior Work

ReDDiT reformulates the discrete diffusion framework inherited from MaskGIT (MVTM) by replacing the single absorbing [mask] state with a set of m randomized absorbing indices, and by swapping the Gumbel-max predict-remask sampler for a principled rehash sampler derived from the DDM reverse process (Eq. 9). Theoretically, it aligns with MDLM's time-weighted cross-entropy objective and discrete flow matching (DFM), enabling shared model weights across samplers and optional DFM refinement steps. It also adapts the RepA representation-alignment loss from continuous DiT training to the discrete setting, validating its transferability. The key conceptual departure from MVTM is the argument that the discrete–continuous performance gap stems from noise design and sampling heuristics rather than from quantization alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the gFID gap to within ~0.03 of the best continuous diffusion model (1.61 vs 1.58) while using only 20–64 sampling steps, ReDDiT provides strong evidence that discrete diffusion is a viable, efficient alternative to continuous latent diffusion for image generation. The rehashing mechanism is a clean, generalizable design principle that could transfer to other discrete generative tasks (text, molecules, multimodal). Open questions remain around optimal noise capacity m (which varies by tokenizer), the role of rehash frequency/intensity, and how to best integrate DFM-style token refreshment for complex multimodal settings. The paper also notes that RepA accelerates convergence but does not yield a relative quality gain under sufficient training for discrete latents, suggesting the auxiliary loss may be less critical than in the continuous case.
