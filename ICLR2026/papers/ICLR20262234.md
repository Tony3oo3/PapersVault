## Problem

Domain Generalization (DG) methods ignore label noise, while Learning with Noisy Labels (LNL) methods ignore domain shifts, and naive combinations of both fail because distribution shifts caused by noise and by domain look similar in feature space, making them hard to separate. Prior work on this intersection (which the authors term Noise-Aware Generalization, NAG) only benchmarked existing methods without proposing a solution to the core challenge of distinguishing noise-induced shifts from domain-induced shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262234/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-domain image datasets (e.g., VLCS, CHAMMI-CP, OfficeHome) where each sample has a potentially noisy class label and a known domain label. A ResNet-50 (or ConvNeXt for multi-channel CHAMMI-CP) pretrained on ImageNet serves as the backbone, and the model outputs a single class label per image. The domain labels are used exclusively during training to construct (class, domain) proxy representations for cross-domain noise detection; at inference time, only the image is needed.

## Method

DL4ND (Domain Labels for Noise Detection) exploits the observation that noisy samples, which may appear indistinguishable from clean ones within a single domain due to spurious features (e.g., shared background colors), show greater variation when compared across domains. After a brief ERM warmup, a Gaussian Mixture Model splits the loss distribution into low-loss and high-loss clusters; low-loss samples are assumed clean and their features are averaged per (class, domain) group to create frozen proxy representations. High-loss samples are then relabeled by finding the nearest proxy from a *different* domain (cross-domain comparison), which forces reliance on intrinsic, domain-agnostic features rather than domain-specific shortcuts. This relabeling is performed once during training and requires no additional data or learning overhead. DL4ND can be applied standalone or layered on top of any DG method (SAGM, SWAD, ERM++, etc.), and ablations confirm that each component—cross-domain comparison, low-loss proxy construction, and relabeling (vs. removal)—contributes 2–4% gains.

## Results

On RotatedMNIST with 30% asymmetric noise, DL4ND improves label accuracy from 75.7% to 98.1%, boosting both ID (87.7%→98.1%) and OOD (87.9%→97.8%) accuracy. On real-world noise datasets, DL4ND achieves the best LNL performance on both VLCS (88.6% AVG) and CHAMMI-CP (60.3% AVG), outperforming ELR, UNICON, DISC, and PLM. When combined with DG methods, DL4ND yields up to a 12.5% OOD gain on OfficeHome with 60% symmetric noise (ERM++ w/DL4ND: 59.4% OOD vs. 48.7% for ERM++ alone) and a 2.3% AVG gain on PACS (SAGM w/DL4ND: 93.1% vs. 90.8% for SAGM alone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DL4ND outperforms all four LNL baselines on both real-world noise datasets: on VLCS it leads ELR (88.6% vs. 86.3% AVG), UNICON (88.6% vs. 86.9%), DISC (88.6% vs. 85.6%), and PLM (88.6% vs. 85.2%); on CHAMMI-CP it leads ELR (60.3% vs. 60.2%), UNICON (60.3% vs. 59.4%), DISC (60.3% vs. 42.3%), and PLM (60.3% vs. 57.5%). Among DG methods, SAGM achieves the best standalone VLCS AVG (89.1%), but DL4ND combined with SAGM+SWAD reaches 90.3% AVG, surpassing SAGM+SWAD alone (89.5%) and SAGM+SWAD+UNICON (85.3%) by 0.8% and 5.0% respectively. On OfficeHome with 60% symmetric noise, ERM++ w/DL4ND achieves 59.8% AVG versus 52.7% for ERM++ alone (a 7.1% gain), and SAGM w/DL4ND reaches 52.2% versus 44.4% for SAGM alone (7.8% gain). The paper also shows that naive LNL+DG combinations (e.g., SAGM+SWAD+UNICON) underperform DG alone on VLCS (85.3% vs. 89.5%), whereas DL4ND consistently improves over the DG baseline on both datasets. On controlled synthetic noise (OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet with 20–40% asymmetric noise), DL4ND boosts performance in 8 of 10 settings, with a 9% OOD gain on DomainNet (35.6% vs. 26.9% for SAGM alone at 40% noise).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of Domain Generalization (DG) and Learning with Noisy Labels (LNL), two well-established but largely independent research threads. DG methods (SAGM, SWAD, ERM++, VREx, Fishr) learn domain-invariant features but ignore label noise, while LNL methods (ELR, UNICON, DISC, PLM) detect and correct noise within a single domain but do not account for domain shifts. The paper's key conceptual departure is the observation that these two types of distribution shift are confounded in multi-domain noisy settings, making naive LNL+DG combinations ineffective. DL4ND repurposes the early-learning clean-sample observation (Liu et al., 2020) and the relabeling paradigm (UNICON, DISC) but introduces cross-domain comparison as the noise-detection signal, a mechanism motivated by the multi-domain structure that neither LNL nor DG literature alone would suggest.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that real-world deployment scenarios—where data is both noisy and multi-domain—cannot be adequately addressed by existing DG or LNL methods in isolation or in naive combination, and provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why (domain distribution skewing from LNL selection, DG methods being compromised by noise). The cross-domain comparison principle is a simple, interpretable insight that could inform future noise-robust learning in any multi-source setting. Limitations include the requirement for domain labels (though per-domain sampling experiments show the benefit persists), evaluation primarily on image classification with ResNet-50/ConvNeXt backbones, and a warmup hyperparameter that requires a validation set. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other modalities, very large numbers of domains, or settings where domain labels are unavailable.
