## Problem

Current end-to-end speech dialogue systems still rely on intermediate text representations during generation, creating a fundamental bottleneck that introduces latency, restricts expressivity (non-verbal vocalizations like laughter or hesitation have no natural text equivalents), and degrades the backbone LLM's text capabilities. Closing the gap between text-guided and direct speech generation remains an open challenge for building truly expressive speech-to-speech models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260662/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts both speech audio and text as input, and produces both speech audio and text as output, supporting four modality pairings (speech→speech, speech→text, text→speech, text→text) controlled by system prompts. Speech is tokenized by a fully causal, single-codebook encoder at 12.5 Hz and 175 bps, enabling true streaming operation without block-causal constraints. The speech decoder uses a flow-matching architecture with compressed chunk size to reduce latency while maintaining reconstruction fidelity, and the maximum context length is 14,336 tokens during pre-training and 10,240 during fine-tuning.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is a modality-based layer split at the 32nd block of a 36-layer Transformer (initialized from Qwen3-8B), where the shared hidden state routes into parallel four-layer stacks for text and speech token prediction respectively. This split point is empirically motivated by layer-wise cosine similarity analysis showing that speech-text representations fuse in lower-to-middle layers but diverge in the top layers. Training follows a two-stage frozen pre-training strategy: Stage 1 freezes the entire text backbone and trains only speech embeddings, speech-specific layers, and the speech LM head on ~4M hours of interleaved and unsupervised speech data; Stage 2 unfreezes parameters for joint cross-modal adaptation while mixing in text-only data (FineWeb-Edu) to preserve linguistic competence. The speech tokenizer pairs an ASR-trained single-codebook encoder (modified from GLM-4-Voice to be fully causal) with a CosyVoice 2-based flow-matching decoder, and supervised fine-tuning uses ~1.5M synthetically constructed QA pairs across all four modality configurations.

## Results

On spoken question answering (SFT model), the proposed method achieves 77.33% on LLaMA-QA (S→T), 45.90% on WebQA (S→T), and 36.71% on WebQA (S→S) without text guidance, with a UTMOS speech quality score of 4.37. On pre-training evaluation, the model attains 84.87% on StoryCloze (text input) and 63.17% (speech input), while preserving strong text capabilities with 67.19% MMLU and 69.53% CMMLU. The speech encoder achieves 10.80% overall WER on Librispeech in a fully streaming setting, and the decoder reaches 4.14% WER / 3.10 DNSMOS on Seed-TTS-Eval-EN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GLM-4-Voice (the closest competitor), the proposed method wins on S→T accuracy for LLaMA-QA (77.33 vs. 74.33) and WebQA (45.90 vs. 39.22), and on UTMOS speech quality (4.37 vs. 4.25), but trails on S→S TriviaQA (28.80 vs. 43.20) and slightly on S→S LLaMA-QA (63.67 vs. 65.67); critically, GLM-4-Voice's S→S numbers are obtained with text guidance, whereas Ours generates speech directly. On text preservation, Ours substantially outperforms GLM-4-Voice (MMLU 67.19 vs. 57.49; CMMLU 69.53 vs. 54.39) and SpiritLM (MMLU 67.19 vs. 36.90), confirming that the frozen pre-training strategy avoids the knowledge degradation seen in prior speech-augmented LLMs. Against Moshi, Ours achieves markedly higher S→S scores across all three QA benchmarks (e.g., 63.67 vs. 21.00 on LLaMA-QA) and far better UTMOS (4.37 vs. 2.81). SpeechGPT and TWIST are far behind on all S→S QA metrics (e.g., 6.50 and 1.50 on WebQA S→S respectively vs. 36.71 for Ours). For the speech codec, Ours's encoder achieves 10.80% overall WER in full streaming, outperforming Mimi-8 (14.45%), XCodec2.0 (20.07%), and CosyVoice 2 (13.78%), while the decoder improves WER and DNSMOS over CosyVoice 2 at a lower frame rate (12.5 Hz vs. 25 Hz).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of text-guided speech-to-speech models (Moshi, GLM-4-Voice, SpeechGPT, PSLM) by removing the text intermediate entirely during generation. Architecturally, it builds on the GLM-4-Voice tokenizer (modified to fully causal streaming) and the CosyVoice 2 flow-matching decoder, but introduces a modality-based layer-splitting scheme motivated by empirical observation that speech-text hidden-state alignment peaks in mid-depth layers and diverges in the final layers. The frozen pre-training strategy directly addresses the known text-capability degradation observed in prior multimodal extensions (e.g., SpiritLM's MMLU drop from 45.3 to 36.9), positioning the work as a principled alternative to the common practice of jointly training all parameters when adding a new modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a true speech-to-speech model can approach text-guided performance while preserving most text capabilities (MMLU 67.19 vs. Qwen3-8B's 76.60), this work narrows a gap that prior systems could not close, and the streaming tokenizer design makes the approach practical for real-time interaction. However, the S→S TriviaQA score (28.80) remains substantially below the text-guided GLM-4-Voice* (43.20), indicating the text bottleneck still confers a meaningful advantage for knowledge-intensive tasks. Open questions include whether the ~9-point MMLU degradation can be further reduced, whether the layer-splitting principle generalizes to other backbone architectures and modalities, and whether the approach scales to longer-form or multi-speaker dialogue without additional architectural changes.
