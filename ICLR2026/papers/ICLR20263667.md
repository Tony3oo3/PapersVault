## Problem

Existing subject-to-video (S2V) generation methods rely on a separate-then-fuse paradigm where image and text features are extracted independently and combined via simple concatenation or cross-attention. This shallow fusion fails to parse complex prompts involving spatial relationships, temporal logic, and interactions among multiple subjects, leading to identity confusion, action misplacement, and attribute blending in generated videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263667/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 1–4 reference images (each depicting a distinct subject) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired scene, behaviors, and inter-subject interactions. Unlike image-to-video, the reference images are not treated as the first frame but as identity anchors; during inference a prompt rephraser rewrites the text to explicitly reference the provided images. The output is a temporally coherent video in which each subject maintains high-fidelity identity and appearance throughout the sequence while executing the prompted actions and spatial arrangements.

## Method

BindWeave replaces shallow post-hoc fusion with a Multimodal Large Language Model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) that performs deep cross-modal reasoning over a unified interleaved sequence of the text prompt and image placeholders, producing hidden states that bind textual commands to specific visual entities and disentangle roles, attributes, and interactions. These MLLM hidden states are projected through a two-layer MLP connector and concatenated with T5 text embeddings to form a joint relational conditioning signal injected via cross-attention into the DiT. A second cross-attention stream uses CLIP features from the reference images for semantic identity anchoring, while a third stream injects VAE-encoded reference features at K padded temporal slots (with binary subject masks) for fine-grained appearance preservation. The DiT backbone (initialized from Wan) is trained with rectified flow matching under a two-stage curriculum on ~1M curated OpenS2V-5M samples, first stabilizing on a high-quality core subset then scaling to the full dataset.

## Results

On the OpenS2V-Eval benchmark (180 prompts, 7 categories), BindWeave achieves a Total Score of 57.61%, the highest among all compared methods, with a particularly strong NexusScore of 46.84% (subject consistency) and FaceSim of 53.71% (identity preservation). The method also scores 67.79% on GmeScore (text-video relevance), 95.90% on MotionSmoothness, and 66.85% on NaturalScore. In the user study (20 participants, 1–5 MOS scale), BindWeave attains the highest Subject Consistency score (3.94) and the highest overall average (3.76).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BindWeave achieves the highest Total Score (57.61%) and NexusScore (46.84%) among all 11 compared methods, edging out VACE-14B (57.55% / 44.08%) and Kling 1.6 (56.23% / 45.89%) on the two headline metrics. On FaceSim, VACE-14B (55.09%) slightly outperforms BindWeave (53.71%), while BindWeave substantially surpasses commercial models like Kling (40.10%), Vidu (35.11%), and Pika (30.38%). On MotionAmplitude and NaturalScore, Kling (41.60% / 74.59%) and Phantom-14B (33.42% / 69.35%) lead, indicating BindWeave trades some motion magnitude and perceived naturalness for stronger subject fidelity. On Aesthetics, VACE-1.3B (48.24%) and Pika 2.1 (46.88%) score higher than BindWeave (45.55%). In the user study, BindWeave ranks first in Subject Consistency (3.94 vs. VACE's 3.88 and Kling's 3.62) and overall average (3.76 vs. VACE's 3.73 and Kling's 3.71), confirming the automated-metric advantage in perceptual terms.

## Relation to Prior Work

BindWeave builds on the Wan DiT-based video generation backbone and rectified-flow training formulation, but its core departure is replacing the 'separate-then-fuse' paradigm used by prior S2V methods (Phantom, VACE, SkyReels-A2, MAGREF) with an MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) that performs deep cross-modal reasoning over interleaved text and reference images before generation. Where prior work injects independently encoded text and image features via post-hoc cross-attention or concatenation, BindWeave first lets the MLLM ground entities, disentangle roles, and resolve spatio-temporal relations, then feeds the resulting hidden states alongside T5 embeddings, CLIP features, and VAE latents into the DiT as a multi-level conditioning stack. This positions the paper at the intersection of multimodal LLMs and diffusion-based video synthesis, treating the MLLM as an instruction planner rather than a feature extractor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Subject-consistent video generation is a practical bottleneck for personalized content creation, brand marketing, and virtual try-on, and BindWeave's MLLM-as-parser approach offers a generalizable recipe for injecting compositional reasoning into diffusion-based generators. However, the approach carries a significant inference cost (a 7B MLLM must run at every generation step), and the finding that the MLLM-only variant fails to converge without T5 suggests the method is not yet self-contained. Open questions include whether the MLLM component can be distilled into a lighter module, how the framework scales beyond 4 reference subjects or to longer video durations, and whether the cross-modal reasoning benefit persists as base DiT models improve in native instruction-following.
