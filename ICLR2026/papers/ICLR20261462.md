## Problem

Long chain-of-thought LLMs trained with RLVR frequently exhibit suboptimal reasoning behaviors such as overthinking, redundant backtracking, and oscillation around local optima, inflating token costs without improving accuracy. Existing RLVR pipelines rely solely on terminal outcome rewards, providing no signal about the quality of intermediate reasoning steps. This paper asks whether the reasoning process itself can be supervised by treating it as an optimization trajectory and rewarding steps that make effective progress toward the answer.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a text question (math, science, or code) and generates a chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory delimited by think tags followed by a final answer. During training, the method additionally requires the ground-truth answer to compute a surrogate objective (average log-probability of answer tokens conditioned on the partial reasoning prefix) at selected decision points. Rather than scoring every token, an entropy-based selection strategy partitions the thinking segment into sub-segments and retains only the top-k segments by first-token entropy, keeping computational overhead manageable while targeting the most uncertain decision points.

## Method

REPRO frames each reasoning step as a gradient-descent update on the LLM's internal state and defines a proxy objective J̃ as the mean log-probability of the ground-truth answer tokens given the partial reasoning context. Two scores are computed along the trajectory: a Magnitude Score (tanh-normalized relative increase of J̃ over a no-reasoning baseline) capturing optimization intensity, and a Stability Score (Kendall's Tau between J̃ values and their positions) capturing smoothness of progress. These are combined into a composite score S, and the rectifying process-level reward for each selected segment is the difference between consecutive S values, so that steps causing oscillation or backtracking receive negative reward while steps making steady progress receive positive reward. The resulting process-level advantages are group-normalized separately from the outcome advantage and added to the standard clipped surrogate objective, making REPRO a plug-and-play addition to PPO, GRPO, REINFORCE++, and related critic-free RL algorithms.

## Results

On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, GRPO+REPRO achieves 36.0% on AIME24, 26.5% on AIME25, 87.1% on MATH500, 37.0% on GPQA-Diamond, and 65.4% on MBPP, consistently outperforming all RL baselines (PPO, REINFORCE++, GRPO) by 1–3.5 points. On Qwen3-1.7B, GRPO+REPRO reaches 49.8% on AIME24 and 37.9% on AIME25, surpassing the original model by 3.0 and 1.8 points respectively. In the efficiency comparison (Table 3), REPRO achieves 36.0% on AIME24 with only 6,558 average tokens—roughly 50% fewer than vanilla GRPO (12,367 tokens)—while outperforming all efficiency-focused baselines (ThinkPrune, L1, Laser, AdaThink) by 4.3–9.3 points on AIME24.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REPRO consistently improves over all four RL algorithm baselines (PPO, REINFORCE++, REINFORCE++ Baseline, GRPO) across every benchmark, with gains of +1.0 to +3.5 points on AIME24/25 and +0.7 to +2.9 points on GPQA-Diamond, confirming its plug-and-play compatibility. Against efficiency-focused methods in Table 3, REPRO dominates: it outperforms ThinkPrune-4k by +4.3 points on AIME24 (36.0 vs. 31.7) while using fewer tokens (6,558 vs. 8,376); it surpasses L1-Max by +9.3 points on AIME24 (36.0 vs. 26.7) at comparable token cost (6,558 vs. 6,249); it beats Laser-D-L4096 by +8.9 points on AIME24 (36.0 vs. 27.1) with only 14% more tokens; and it exceeds AdaThink δ0.05 by +4.3 points on AIME24 (36.0 vs. 31.7) while requiring 28% fewer tokens (6,558 vs. 8,549). The key distinction is that REPRO achieves these gains without sacrificing accuracy for brevity—unlike L1 and Laser, which trade substantial reasoning performance for shorter outputs. REPRO also reduces inference token cost by ~50% relative to vanilla GRPO (12,367 → 6,558 tokens), demonstrating that the optimization-based process reward simultaneously improves both accuracy and efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

REPRO builds directly on the optimization-theoretic framing of LLM reasoning trajectories as implicit gradient descent (Liu et al., 2025a), extending it from analysis to a concrete training signal. Structurally, it adapts the process-level reward paradigm introduced by Lightman et al. (2024), but replaces learned process reward models with a computed proxy objective (perplexity over ground-truth tokens) and a dual scoring mechanism (magnitude via tanh-normalized relative improvement, stability via Kendall's Tau). It departs from efficiency-focused methods (ThinkPrune, L1, Laser, AdaThink) that impose coarse length penalties, instead providing fine-grained, content-aware process supervision that penalizes oscillatory reasoning without uniformly penalizing length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REPRO offers a principled, plug-and-play mechanism to address overthinking in long-CoT LLMs, decoupling reasoning efficiency from accuracy in a way that length-penalty methods cannot. Its broad applicability across RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO, REINFORCE++) and model families (DeepSeek, Qwen, Hunyuan, MobileLLM) suggests it could become a standard component in RLVR pipelines. However, the method requires ground-truth answers during training (limiting applicability to open-ended or non-verifiable tasks), incurs additional forward-pass cost for score computation, and the empirical gains, while consistent, are modest (+1–3 points). Open questions include whether the optimization framing generalizes to multi-turn or agentic settings, and whether the perplexity-based proxy remains informative as models scale beyond 8B parameters.
