## Problem

Existing visual manipulation localization methods face two unresolved challenges: (1) resolution diversity, where uniform resizing destroys subtle forensic artifacts and padding wastes computation on non-standard aspect ratios, and (2) a modeling gap between image and video, forcing practitioners to maintain separate architectures for spatial and spatio-temporal inputs. No prior framework handles both variable-resolution inputs and unified image-video processing within a single, efficient architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263024/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images at arbitrary resolutions (from 256×256 up to 4K, e.g., 2958×4437) and video clips (4-frame segments), partitioning them into fixed-size overlapping sub-images (512×512 for images, 256×256 for videos) without any interpolation or resizing. All sub-images from mixed image and video batches are flattened into a unified tensor and processed in parallel. The output is a pixel-level binary manipulation mask per input, produced by a lightweight query-based decoder with 8 learnable queries and 3 decoder layers.

## Method

RelayFormer introduces Global-Local Relay Attention (GLRA), where each sub-image is processed by local ViT self-attention augmented with a small set (n=2) of learnable GLR tokens that absorb local details; these tokens are then concatenated across all sub-images and passed through a global self-attention layer with 4D Rotary Positional Embeddings (encoding temporal, token-index, height, and width axes) to exchange scene-level context before being reinjected into their originating sub-images. A parameter-efficient strategy shares a single Transformer backbone between local and global attention, using two LoRA adapters (rank 8) to specialize the shared weights for fine-grained vs. long-range reasoning without doubling parameters. The framework is trained end-to-end with ViT-Base or SegFormer backbones using BCE plus edge loss, and a Mask2Former-inspired query decoder produces the final manipulation mask. This design scales computation linearly with the number of sub-images rather than quadratically with input resolution, enabling native extrapolation to resolutions far exceeding the training scale.

## Results

Under the MVSS protocol, Relay-ViT achieves the highest average F1 of 0.554 across five image benchmarks (Coverage, Columbia, NIST16, CASIA v1.0, IMD2020), surpassing TruFor (0.527) and IML-ViT (0.500). Under the more challenging CAT protocol, Relay-Seg reaches 0.705 average F1, outperforming APSC-Net (0.691) and Mesorch (0.677). For video manipulation localization on MOSE100, Relay-ViT attains 0.689 F1 on E2FGVI and 0.684 on STTN, while Relay-Seg leads on E2FGVI with 0.698 F1, both exceeding TruVIL and ViLocal on most inpainting methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the MVSS protocol, Relay-ViT (0.554 avg F1) outperforms the strongest prior methods TruFor (0.527) by +2.7 points and IML-ViT (0.500) by +5.4 points, with particularly large margins over Mesorch (+11.8) and SparseViT (+11.6). Under the CAT protocol, Relay-Seg (0.705) edges out APSC-Net (0.691) by 1.4 points and surpasses Mesorch (0.677) and SparseViT (0.671) by 2.8 and 3.4 points respectively. For video localization, Relay-ViT beats TruVIL on E2FGVI (0.689 vs. 0.674 F1) and STTN (0.684 vs. 0.612), while ViLocal retains a narrow lead on FuseFormer (0.721 vs. 0.695). Relay-Seg achieves the best E2FGVI score (0.698) among all methods. On the additional AI-generated forgery datasets (AutoSplice, CocoGlide, Defacto-12k), Relay-Seg leads with 0.372 average F1, outperforming IML-ViT (0.266) and SparseViT (0.257), confirming robustness beyond traditional splicing and copy-move forgeries.

## Relation to Prior Work

RelayFormer extends the vision-transformer line of work on manipulation localization (IML-ViT, SparseViT, Mesorch) by replacing uniform-resolution processing or sparse attention with a partition-and-relay scheme: fixed-size sub-images exchange global context through a small set of learnable GLR tokens rather than through dense pairwise attention. The parameter-efficient local/global specialization borrows the LoRA low-rank adaptation mechanism (Hu et al., 2022) to share a single Transformer backbone between two functionally distinct attention stages, while the mask decoder directly adapts Mask2Former's query-based architecture (Cheng et al., 2022). The 4D RoPE formulation follows Qwen2-VL (Wang et al., 2024) to encode temporal, token-identity, and spatial positions simultaneously, enabling resolution extrapolation. Conceptually, the relay mechanism occupies a middle ground between FOCAL-style local-global attention (Yang et al., 2021) and the sparse-attention strategies of SparseViT, but differs in that global information is compressed into a fixed number of bottleneck tokens per sub-image rather than routed through a learned sparse pattern.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses two practical pain points in forensic deployment: the inability to handle in-the-wild resolution diversity without destructive interpolation, and the need to maintain separate image and video models. The relay-based design decouples global reasoning cost from input resolution (GFLOPs grow linearly with sub-image count rather than quadratically with pixel count), making high-resolution and long-sequence processing tractable. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that relay tokens, while efficient, cannot capture cross-partition dependencies as precisely as full global attention when manipulations span multiple sub-images. Open questions include how the approach scales to very long video sequences beyond the 4-frame clips tested, whether the relay bottleneck becomes a bottleneck for fine-grained cross-region forgeries, and whether the unified image-video training benefit (image data aiding video detection but not vice versa) persists with larger, more diverse video datasets.
