## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks against LLMs evaluate success using a single greedy generation, treating sampling as an afterthought rather than an integral attack component. This point-estimate protocol overestimates model robustness because it ignores the stochastic output distribution, where even small per-sample probabilities of harmful responses compound under repeated querying at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262532/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a harmful text prompt (e.g., from HarmBench) and a base optimization algorithm (GCG, AutoDAN, BEAST, PAIR, REINFORCE-GCG) as input, and produces an optimized adversarial prompt along with a configurable number of sampled completions scored by the StrongREJECT judge. The key structural difference from prior work is that the number of samples per step (vector n) and the total number of optimization steps (T) are explicit attack parameters chosen under a fixed FLOP budget, rather than fixed hyperparameters with a single greedy evaluation at the end.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified sampling-aware attack framework (Algorithm 1) that parameterizes any attack by a horizon T and a sample-count vector n, casting the attack as a cost-constrained resource allocation problem between prompt optimization and model sampling under a fixed FLOP budget. Three practical sampling schedules (optimize-then-sample, uniform, block) are evaluated, all yielding up to two orders of magnitude FLOP reduction and up to 37 percentage point ASR gains over greedy baselines. A key empirical finding is that most optimization strategies primarily suppress refusal rates without increasing the harmfulness of already-compliant responses, explaining why sampling is so efficient: the harmful tail is already present early in optimization. The paper further proposes a label-free entropy-maximization loss that optimizes the spread of the first-token prediction distribution rather than targeting a specific affirmative prefix, enabling model-agnostic attacks that produce more natural harmful completions and remain effective even on deeply aligned models.

## Results

Across five attacks (GCG, AutoDAN, BEAST, PAIR, REINFORCE-GCG) and four models, sampling-aware evaluation at n=50 consistently yields substantially higher ASR than the standard greedy (n=1) protocol. For example, GCG on Gemma 3 1B jumps from ASR_q@1 = 0.25 to ASR_q@50 = 0.73, and AutoDAN on Llama 3.1 8B goes from 0.71 to 0.96. The compute-optimal trade-off consistently lies at or near the maximum sampling budget explored (500 samples), with the 100–200 sample range being FLOP-optimal for matching baseline harm. The proposed label-free entropy objective achieves 64% ASR_q@50 on Llama 3.1 8B after only 5 GCG steps (vs. 46% for the affirmative objective), and remains competitive through 250 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All five base attacks (GCG, AutoDAN, BEAST, PAIR, REINFORCE-GCG) are evaluated both with their standard greedy protocol (n=1) and with sampling-aware evaluation (n=50). GCG is the strongest optimization-based attack, achieving the highest ASR_q@50 on most models (e.g., 0.73 on Gemma 3 1B, 0.65 on Llama 3.1 8B), while AutoDAN achieves the highest ASR_q@50 (0.96) on both Gemma 3 1B and Llama 3.1 8B due to its effective seed prompts. The key finding is that every attack benefits substantially from sampling: the gap between ASR_q@1 and ASR_q@50 ranges from 7 p.p. (AutoDAN on Llama 3.1 8B) to 48 p.p. (GCG on Gemma 3 1B). Model rankings also shift: Gemma 3 1B appears more robust than Llama 3.1 8B under GCG at n=1 (0.25 vs. 0.32) but less robust at n=50 (0.73 vs. 0.65), revealing that greedy evaluation mischaracterizes tail-risk behavior. The new entropy objective matches or exceeds the affirmative objective at ASR_q@50 on three of four models (e.g., 0.64 vs. 0.46 on Llama 3.1 8B at T=5) while underperforming only on Llama 3 8B with Circuit Breakers (0.03 vs. 0.07).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing LLM adversarial attacks as a resource allocation problem between prompt optimization and output sampling, rather than treating sampling as a fixed post-hoc evaluation step. It subsumes existing optimization-based attacks (GCG, AutoDAN, BEAST, REINFORCE-GCG) and sampling-based attacks (Best-of-N, PAIR) under a unified framework (Algorithm 1) parameterized by optimization steps T and a sampling vector n, drawing conceptual inspiration from worst-case robustness analysis in computer vision. The paper departs from prior work by introducing a label-free entropy-maximization objective that targets the spread of the output distribution rather than its mean or mode, and by explicitly accounting for FLOP costs of both optimization and sampling to enable fair cross-method comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that model robustness rankings can reverse under multi-sample evaluation (e.g., Gemma 31B appears more robust than Llama 3.1 8B at ASR@1 but less robust at ASR@50) has direct implications for how the field conducts safety assessments at scale, suggesting that greedy single-sample protocols systematically overestimate robustness. The discovery that most optimization strategies primarily suppress refusals without increasing the harmfulness of compliant responses challenges a core assumption about what adversarial optimization is actually achieving. Open questions include whether adaptive sampling schedules that interleave optimization and sampling can further improve trade-offs, whether findings transfer to larger and architecturally diverse models, and whether the entropy objective's effectiveness against 'deeply aligned' models reflects a fundamental gap in current alignment training.
