## Problem

Despite the empirical success of Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) for visual generation, the internal mechanisms by which they organize spatial content remain underexplored. Existing single-image novel view synthesis methods either rely on video generation (producing unnecessary intermediate frames), warping-and-inpainting pipelines prone to reprojection errors, or 2D coordinate representations that are inherently ambiguous for 3D view transformation. This paper reveals that DiT patch tokens are surprisingly independent and that spatial coherence is governed primarily by positional encodings rather than token-to-token interactions, opening a new avenue for geometry-aware generation through PE manipulation alone.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a target camera pose as input. The source image is processed by VGGT to obtain per-pixel depth maps and camera parameters, which are used to project source-view patch tokens into the target view's 3D coordinate frame. The output is a single novel-view image at the same resolution as the input; for spatial editing applications, the output is a locally or globally reorganized version of the input. The model operates in the latent space of Flux.1 Kontext's VAE, with source image tokens and noise tokens jointly processed by the transformer.

## Method

The core contribution is the Positional Encoding Field (PE-Field), which extends standard 2D RoPE into a structured 3D field with two key components. First, a depth-aware 3D RoPE adds a z-axis encoding (distance along the optical axis) to the existing (x, y) encodings, allowing the transformer to disambiguate overlapping 2D projections and model volumetric correspondences. Second, a hierarchical multi-level scheme assigns different attention heads to different spatial resolution grids (e.g., 16×16 px, 64×64 px, 16×16 px sub-patch), so that some heads preserve pretrained patch-level priors while others capture fine-grained intra-patch detail. The number of levels and head-to-level assignment follows a geometric 1:4:16:… quota derived from the total head count. Built on Flux.1 Kontext (with text conditioning removed), the model is trained with a rectified flow-matching objective on multi-view data (DL3DV, MannequinChallenge), where source-view tokens are repositioned via monocular reconstruction and view transformation, and empty target-grid locations are filled with noise for generative completion.

## Results

On single-image novel view synthesis, PE-Field achieves PSNR of 22.12 / 21.65 / 22.23 and LPIPS of 0.174 / 0.162 / 0.154 on Tanks-and-Temples, RE10K, and DL3DV respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline GEN3C (PSNR 19.18 / 20.64 / 19.14) by 2.9–3.1 dB in PSNR. The method also leads on SSIM (0.732 / 0.816 / 0.742) and LPIPS across all three benchmarks. Ablations confirm both the depth-aware encoding and the multi-level hierarchical RoPE contribute meaningfully, with removing either component dropping PSNR by 1.5–1.8 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PE-Field outperforms all 12 quantitative baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all three datasets. The closest competitor, GEN3C (CVPR 2025), trails by 2.9–3.1 dB in PSNR and 0.05–0.08 in SSIM, with the gap most pronounced on DL3DV (+3.09 dB). Video-diffusion-based methods (DimensionX, Voyager, FlexWorld, See3D) cluster in the 17.6–18.9 PSNR range, while reconstruction-and-inpainting pipelines (GenWarp, NVS-Solver, ViewCrafter) score lower still (15.3–17.2 PSNR), with the paper attributing their weaknesses to depth-warping errors and reprojection artifacts. In qualitative prompt-based editing comparisons (Figure 6), Flux.1 Kontext produces only minor viewpoint shifts with artifacts, and Qwen-Image-Edit achieves larger spatial changes but alters identity and over-smooths content, whereas PE-Field maintains precise angular control while preserving source-image fidelity. The paper also notes that, unlike video-based baselines requiring intermediate frame generation, PE-Field synthesizes the target view directly, making it over an order of magnitude faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Flux.1 Kontext as its base architecture and extends the DiT paradigm (Peebles & Xie, 2023) by rethinking the role of positional encodings. It departs from the dominant NVS pipeline of monocular reconstruction → warping → inpainting (GenWarp, ViewCrafter, GEN3C) and from video-diffusion-based approaches that generate intermediate frames (Voyager, DimensionX, FlexWorld) by instead manipulating only the positional encodings of image tokens to reorganize spatial content within the DiT's generative process. The two technical contributions—depth-aware 3D RoPE and hierarchical sub-patch RoPE—extend the standard 2D RoPE (Su et al., 2024) into a structured volumetric field, a design not previously explored in diffusion-transformer architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight that DiT spatial coherence is governed primarily by positional encodings rather than explicit token-to-token attention offers a new lens for understanding and controlling transformer-based visual generation, potentially applicable beyond NVS to any spatially structured task. The PE-Field approach avoids the reprojection-error and semantic-disruption problems inherent in warp-and-inpaint pipelines, and naturally generalizes to spatial editing (object rotation, removal) without task-specific training. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video generation, whether it can operate without explicit monocular depth (VGGT), and whether the patch-independence property holds across other DiT architectures beyond Flux. The multi-step decomposition needed for large viewpoint changes also suggests a fundamental limitation in how much unseen content a single forward pass can hallucinate consistently.
