## Problem

Video-text retrieval (VTR) models suffer severe performance degradation under real-world query shifts, yet existing robustness benchmarks and test-time adaptation (TTA) methods are designed for static images and ignore the spatio-temporal dynamics unique to video. This paper identifies that video perturbations dramatically amplify the hubness phenomenon—where a few gallery items disproportionately dominate nearest-neighbor rankings—and introduces both a comprehensive video-specific perturbation benchmark (MLVP) and a TTA framework (HAT-VTR) that directly counteracts this failure mode.

## Input / Output

The method operates online in batches of 16 queries against pre-computed gallery embeddings from a dual-encoder VTR model (CLIP4Clip or X-Pool). For v2t, inputs are video clips (10–32 s for MSRVTT, longer for ActivityNet) encoded by the query encoder; for t2v, inputs are text queries. The HSM module maintains a FIFO queue of K recent similarity matrices (S ∈ R^{B×N_G}) to compute bilateral re-weighting, and the adaptation step updates only the Layer Normalization parameters of the query encoder. Outputs are hub-suppressed similarity scores for final ranking and updated encoder parameters for subsequent batches.

## Method

HAT-VTR combines two complementary mechanisms to counteract amplified hubness in test-time VTR. First, the Hubness Suppression Memory (HSM) maintains a FIFO memory of K−1 recent query-gallery similarity matrices and computes bilateral normalization weights—a gallery-centric weight (softmax over columns) capturing item popularity and a query-centric weight (softmax over rows) capturing query concentration—then re-weights the aggregated similarity matrix to demote over-popular hubs. HSM is applied at two stages: selecting reliable pseudo-positives for adaptation and reranking final retrieval scores. Second, multi-granular TCR learning extends TCR's uniformity and cross-modal alignment objectives to the video domain by adding intra-video (frame-level) scattering and frame-level cross-covariance alignment terms alongside the standard inter-video (global) terms, all stabilized by a noise-robust entropy loss with a Reliable Memory of hub-suppressed pseudo-positive pairs. The key insight is that directly suppressing hubness at the similarity-score level, rather than relying solely on representation-level uniformity, is necessary to handle the heavy-tailed k-occurrence distributions that video perturbations induce.

## Results

On MSRVTT-1kA (severity 5), HAT-VTR achieves 26.2% average Recall@1 with CLIP4Clip and 30.3% with X-Pool, outperforming the strongest baseline TCR (21.4% and 24.0%) by 4.8 and 6.3 points respectively. On ActivityNet, gains are even more pronounced: 22.28% vs. TCR's 12.86% (CLIP4Clip) and 20.35% vs. 14.50% (X-Pool). Under zero-shot adaptation, HAT-VTR reaches 35.40% R@1 on MSRVTT v2t, surpassing TCR (27.90%) by 7.5 points, and 28.92% on ActivityNet v2t vs. TCR's 18.65%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAT-VTR consistently outperforms all five TTA baselines (TENT, READ, SAR, EATA, TCR) across both base models and all 12 perturbation types. The most relevant prior method, TCR (Li et al., 2025b), is surpassed by 4.8 points (CLIP4Clip) and 6.3 points (X-Pool) on MSRVTT-1kA average R@1, with the gap widening to ~9.4 and 5.9 points on ActivityNet. Among the older TTA methods, EATA is the strongest non-TCR baseline (19.1% / 22.4% avg), yet HAT-VTR still leads by 7.1 and 7.9 points respectively. On the more challenging Query-Gallery-Shift settings, HAT-VTR achieves 36.10% R@1 (MSRVTT→ActivityNet v2t, CLIP4Clip) and 40.30% (ActivityNet→MSRVTT v2t, X-Pool), outperforming TCR by 16.8 and 2.7 points. Under zero-shot adaptation, HAT-VTR gains 7.5 points over TCR on MSRVTT v2t (35.40% vs. 27.90%) and 10.3 points on ActivityNet v2t (28.92% vs. 18.65%), demonstrating that the hubness-mitigation strategy generalizes to severe domain gaps where most TTA methods fail or even degrade performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TCR (Li et al., 2025), which pioneered test-time adaptation for image-text retrieval under query shift by enforcing representation uniformity, into the video domain by introducing multi-granular losses that exploit temporal hierarchy. It also repurposes the bilateral normalization idea from DSL (Cheng et al., 2021)—originally a training-time hubness mitigation for VTR—into an online FIFO memory (HSM) that operates at test time. The paper departs from prior VTR robustness benchmarks (e.g., VTR-C by Schiappa et al., 2022) by introducing perturbations that explicitly target inter-frame dynamics (motion blur, temporal scrambling, event insertion) rather than applying frame-wise image corruptions, and from standard TTA methods (TENT, EATA, SAR) by identifying hubness amplification as the specific failure mode to target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MLVP benchmark fills a genuine gap as the first video-specific perturbation suite designed for comparing TTA methods in VTR, and the diagnosis that test-time corruptions specifically amplify hubness (shifting k-occurrence distributions from balanced to heavy-tailed) reframes how practitioners should think about VTR robustness. Limitations include adaptation restricted to LayerNorm parameters, evaluation on only two base architectures (CLIP4Clip, X-Pool), and residual weakness on semantically disruptive perturbations like temporal scrambling. Open questions include whether hubness-aware TTA generalizes to larger foundation models (e.g., VideoCLIP, LanguageBind) and whether the FIFO memory approach scales to very large galleries in production settings.
