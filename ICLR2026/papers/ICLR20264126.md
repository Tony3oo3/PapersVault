## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation methods rely on text prompts for fine-grained control, but face two fundamental bottlenecks: training data conflates acoustically distinct sounds under coarse labels (e.g., all dog barks), and text cannot encode microacoustic attributes like impact transients versus resonance decay. This makes it impossible to synthesize creator-specified acoustic variations (e.g., footsteps on wood vs. marble) or perform timbre transfer using text-controlled modes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a silent 8-second video clip, a 2-second reference audio clip (which can be from a completely different video and does not need to match the video duration), and a text prompt as input, producing an 8-second synchronized Foley audio waveform at 44.1 kHz. Audio is generated in a 40-dimensional latent space at 43.07 fps, then decoded through a VAE decoder to mel-spectrogram and a BigVGAN vocoder to waveform. The reference audio is processed through a pretrained VAE encoder (preserving full spectral/timbral signature) rather than a semantic encoder like CLAP, enabling precise acoustic control.

## Method

AC-Foley extends conditional flow matching to jointly model video, audio, and text modalities within a multimodal transformer (7 multimodal + 14 single-modal blocks, hidden dim 896). A multimodal conditioning vector—combining VAE-encoded reference audio features (average-pooled), CLIP text/video embeddings, Synchformer sync features, and Fourier-encoded flow timestep—is injected via adaptive layer normalization (adaLN) across all transformer blocks. The key training innovation is a two-stage scheme: Stage I uses a random 2-second overlapping segment from the 8-second target audio as condition to teach acoustic feature extraction, while Stage II switches to the non-overlapping final 2 seconds of the 10-second clip to force temporal adaptation and prevent trivial copy-paste behavior. This is followed by fine-tuning on a high-quality VGGSound subset (ImageBind score > 0.3) for 40k iterations to sharpen audio-visual alignment.

## Results

With audio conditioning, AC-Foley achieves the best scores across nearly all metrics on the VGGSound test set: FD_PaSST of 56.00, FD_PANNs of 4.93, KL_PANNs of 0.95, ImageBind score of 37.1, Onset AP of 0.5317, and MCD of 11.37, outperforming the MMAudio+CLAP baseline by 21% on FD_PaSST and 22% on MCD. Without audio conditioning (null embedding), AC-Foley remains competitive with the strongest video-to-audio baseline MMAudio-L-V2, achieving the lowest FD_PaSST (64.90 vs. 69.25) and FD_PANNs (8.59 vs. 8.81). On the timbre transfer task (Greatest Hits), AC-Foley surpasses CondFoley on all three metrics (Onset AP 0.6629 vs. 0.6611, MCD 3.39 vs. 4.18) despite not being trained on that dataset. In human evaluation, AC-Foley achieves an 83.5% win rate on acoustic fidelity over MMAudio-L-V2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among audio-conditioned methods, AC-Foley dominates the MMAudio+CLAP baseline on every metric except DeSync (0.465 vs. 0.431), with particularly large gains in spectral fidelity (MCD 11.37 vs. 14.63, a 22% reduction) and distribution matching (FD_PaSST 56.00 vs. 70.80). The prior audio-conditioned method Video-Foley is far behind on all metrics (e.g., FD_PaSST 613.05 vs. 56.00). In the unconditional setting, AC-Foley (w/o audio) trades a small DeSync penalty (0.410 vs. 0.392) and IB score (36.6 vs. 37.8) against MMAudio-L-V2 for better distribution matching (FD_PaSST 64.90 vs. 69.25), while clearly outperforming Frieren, MultiFoley, FoleyCrafter, SSV2A, V-AURA, ThinkSound, and HunyuanVideo-Foley on most metrics. On timbre transfer, AC-Foley beats CondFoley on all three metrics (Onset Acc. 0.3948 vs. 0.3906, Onset AP 0.6629 vs. 0.6611, MCD 3.39 vs. 4.18) despite CondFoley being trained on the Greatest Hits dataset while AC-Foley is not. In the human study, AC-Foley achieves an 83.5% win rate on acoustic fidelity and a 61.1% win rate on temporal alignment over MMAudio-L-V2, with 21.8% of participants finding temporal sync equally good for both.

## Relation to Prior Work

AC-Foley builds directly on the multimodal joint training framework of MMAudio (Cheng et al., 2024), extending it by adding a reference-audio conditioning pathway through a VAE encoder rather than relying solely on text and video. It departs from prior audio-conditioned approaches—CondFoley (Du et al., 2023) requires duration-matched audio-video pairs, and MultiFoley (Chen et al., 2024) performs audio extension anchored to pre-existing clips—by enabling variable-length, non-overlapping reference audio that need not temporally coincide with the target. The key architectural departure from semantic audio encoders like CLAP is the use of VAE latents to preserve the full acoustic signature (timbre, spectral envelope, transients) rather than compressing it into a semantic embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap for sound designers: the inability of text prompts to specify microacoustic details (e.g., distinguishing a Chihuahua bark from a German Shepherd growl, or a hammer-on-anvil clang from a chain-drop). The two-stage training strategy (overlap → non-overlap) is a clean solution to the copy-paste failure mode, and the demonstrated zero-shot generalization (reference audio from a completely different video) opens the door to creative workflows like timbre transfer and novel sound effect synthesis without dataset-specific training. Limitations remain for multi-source scenes and extreme temporal mismatches, and the approach's scalability to longer sequences or more complex soundscapes is untested.
