## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) degrades generation diversity in Large Reasoning Models, causing them to collapse toward single-answer reasoning that fails on subjective questions where valid answers vary by stakeholder perspective. Existing diversity-enhanced training methods rely on a single ground truth for optimization, making them fundamentally incompatible with subjective tasks that have multiple equally valid role-dependent answers, and no training framework had been designed specifically for this setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes subjective multiple-choice questions (from BBQ, GlobalOpinionQA, and ETHICS) as input and produces a structured multi-role reasoning chain that sequentially reasons from three contrastive stakeholder perspectives before yielding a final answer. The ground truth is role-dependent: for datasets like GLOQA and CALI, each role (e.g., a country) has its own correct answer, while for others like BBQ and ETHICS, all roles should converge on the same answer. Evaluation uses divergent merging (per-role accuracy) or convergent merging (majority-vote consensus) accordingly.

## Method

MultiRole-R1 is a two-stage diversity-enhanced training framework. Stage 1 (perspective diversity via SFT) synthesizes multi-role reasoning paths by generating contrastive roles through few-shot prompting with a relevance-plus-contrast selection score, sampling k reasoning paths per role at temperature 1, applying self-consistency filtering via majority voting, and concatenating filtered paths in random role orderings to form 2,700 training examples for supervised fine-tuning. Stage 2 (token-level diversity via RL) applies GRPO with a shaped reward R = δ·R_acc + (1−δ)·R_div, where R_div is an equally-weighted composite of eight diversity metrics (lexical TTR, token entropy, sentence length, sentence pattern, adjacent-sentence similarity, Yule's K, distinct N-gram, and function-word diversity). The diversity term ensures intra-group reward variance in GRPO advantages, preventing the zero-gradient stall that occurs when all sampled responses receive identical verifiable rewards. The key insight is that role perspectives provide semantically coherent scaffolding (anchored to real-world stakeholder groups) rather than random variation, and that diversity is a stronger predictor of accuracy (r=0.74) than reasoning length (r=0.55).

## Results

MultiRole-R1 achieves an average 14.1% pass@1 accuracy gain on three in-domain subjective tasks (BBQ, GLOQA, ETHICS) and 7.64% on four out-of-domain tasks (CALI, CSQA, GSM8K, AIME 2024) over zero-shot CoT, while also improving diversity by 18.3%. On the challenging AIME 2024 math benchmark, the method yields a 5.78% average accuracy gain across four models (e.g., 63.2% vs. 55.5% zero-shot for R1-Distill-Qwen-7B). The overall average accuracy gain is 10.6%, with 7.5% attributed to multi-role SFT (perspective diversity) and 3.1% to GRPO with diversity reward shaping (token-level diversity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiRole-R1 consistently outperforms all four external baselines across all six evaluation datasets and all four tested models. Compared to Zero-shot CoT, the gains are largest on subjective tasks (e.g., BBQ: 94.50% vs. 62.45%; GLOQA: 49.10% vs. 32.62% for R1-Distill-Qwen-7B). Against Self-Refine and Role-Play prompting, MultiRole-R1 leads by 10–20 points on in-domain subjective benchmarks, confirming that training-based multi-role reasoning substantially surpasses inference-time prompting strategies. The More Think baseline (budget-forcing with 3× reasoning length) is the strongest single-prompting competitor, reaching 80.76% on BBQ, but MultiRole-R1 still exceeds it by 13.7 points on BBQ and 12.7 points on CALI. Notably, MultiRole-R1 also surpasses its own ablation (SelfConsis SFT + vanilla GRPO without diversity reward) by a small but consistent margin (e.g., 94.50% vs. 94.30% on BBQ; 49.10% vs. 47.22% on GLOQA), validating the contribution of diversity reward shaping. On the OOD AIME 2024 benchmark, MultiRole-R1 achieves 63.2% (R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) and 80.1% (Qwen3-8B), outperforming all baselines including More Think (58.6% and 78.8% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR training paradigm (DeepSeek-R1, GRPO from DeepSeekMath) by introducing a two-level diversity objective—perspective diversity via multi-role SFT and token-level diversity via reward shaping—specifically designed for subjective reasoning where multiple answers are valid. It builds on self-consistency filtering (Chen et al., 2023) and budget-forcing (Muennighoff et al., 2025) for data construction, and on the potential-based reward shaping theory (Ng et al., 1999) to justify that the diversity reward preserves optimal policies. The key departure from prior diversity-enhanced training work (Song et al., 2025a; Yan et al., 2025) is that those methods target objective reasoning with a single ground truth, whereas MultiRole-R1 handles role-dependent ground truths and explicitly optimizes for semantically meaningful perspective variation rather than random token-level noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that diversity (r=0.74) is a stronger predictor of accuracy than reasoning length (r=0.55) challenges the prevailing 'longer thinking = better' narrative in test-time scaling and suggests a more efficient path to improved reasoning. The cross-domain transfer from subjective-only training to a 5.78% gain on AIME 2024 is notable, though the absolute improvement is modest. Key limitations include restriction to multiple-choice format (precluding free-form creative tasks), a fixed 3-role design, and reliance on role-dependent ground truths that are unavailable for many real-world subjective questions. Open questions include whether the framework extends to open-ended generation and whether the diversity reward can be made task-adaptive without the instability observed with entropy regularization.
