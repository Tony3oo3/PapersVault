## Problem

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has driven progress in math, code, and theorem proving, but the conditions under which it yields robust generalization beyond training data remain poorly understood. This paper uses probabilistic inference over causal graphical models as a controlled testbed, exploiting the causal ladder (association, intervention, counterfactual) and query structural complexity as two natural axes along which to probe within- and across-level generalization of RLVR versus supervised fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262015/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a fully specified structural causal model over 10 binary variables, provided as text: a DAG in DOT-like syntax, conditional probability tables for each node, and a natural-language query at one of three levels (association, intervention, or counterfactual). For RLVR, the model outputs a free-form reasoning chain followed by a probability distribution (e.g., [0.52, 0.48]) rounded to two decimal places; for SFT, it outputs only the distribution. Correctness is judged by total variation distance ≤ 0.01 between the extracted answer and a reference computed via variable elimination, with a separate format reward component.

## Method

The authors construct RLCausal, a synthetic dataset of 8,000 training / 2,000 dev / 8,000 test instances per query level, generated by sampling random 10-node DAGs (Lampinen et al. workflow), uniform CPTs from the simplex, and queries stratified by level and relevant-subgraph size. They fine-tune Qwen2.5-Instruct models (3B, 7B, 32B) using GRPO/DAPO with a verifiable reward (0.8 × answer accuracy via TV distance + 0.2 × format compliance) or standard SFT maximizing likelihood of the reference answer, varying which query level appears in training to measure across-level transfer. The key empirical finding is that RLVR outperforms SFT on association and intervention queries only for models ≥ 7B, and fails on counterfactual queries at all sizes; analysis via LLM-judged reasoning traces shows RLVR shifts the marginalization strategy from brute-force to incremental summation and reduces abstract probability-derivation errors, but only when the base model already has sufficient reasoning competence to attempt correct marginalization before fine-tuning.

## Results

On the filtered within-level test set, RLVR (GRPO) substantially outperforms SFT on intervention queries for models ≥7B: 97.4% vs 69.8% (32B) and 90.4% vs 58.2% (7B). On association queries, the gap is even wider: 78.0% vs 48.0% (32B) and 57.4% vs 40.4% (7B). However, RLVR underperforms SFT on counterfactual queries at all model sizes (e.g., 16.4% vs 18.1% for 32B) and on 3B models across all levels (e.g., 50.0% vs 56.4% on intervention). The advantage of RLVR over SFT grows with query complexity, with the largest gaps appearing on medium and hard (large relevant subgraph) instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between RLVR (GRPO/DAPO) and SFT as post-training paradigms on the same RLCausal dataset. RLVR decisively wins on intervention queries for 7B and 32B models (e.g., 97.4% vs 69.8% at 32B, a 27.6 pp margin) and on association queries (78.0% vs 48.0% at 32B, a 30.0 pp margin). RLVR loses to SFT on counterfactual queries at every model size (16.4% vs 18.1% at 32B; 13.6% vs 15.3% at 7B) and on 3B models across all three levels, where SFT's direct-prediction approach outperforms RLVR's reasoning-chain approach. The DAPO variant (with curriculum filtering) produces results statistically indistinguishable from GRPO. Across-level generalization (training on one query level, evaluating on another) shows the same pattern: RLVR ≥7B outperforms SFT, while 3B models show no consistent advantage for either method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the growing line of work comparing RLVR and SFT generalization (Chu et al., 2025; Swamy et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025) by moving from text/visual reasoning tasks to formal causal inference, where the causal ladder (association → intervention → counterfactual) provides a natural hierarchy for probing within- and across-level transfer. It builds on the RLVR training infrastructure of GRPO and DAPO (Shao et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025b) and the structural causal model framework of Pearl et al. (2016), but departs from prior causal reasoning benchmarks like CLadder (Jin et al., 2023) by providing fully specified 10-node graphs with random topologies rather than natural-language scenarios, isolating derivation and calculation from language understanding. The RLCausal dataset and the use of variable elimination as a ground-truth solver are new contributions, but the underlying task of probabilistic inference over SCMs is well-established in the causal inference literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, mechanistic account of when RLVR helps and when it fails in a structured reasoning domain: it works only when the base model already has sufficient reasoning competence (≥7B here), and it specifically improves marginalization strategy and reduces derivation errors rather than teaching new algorithms from scratch. The persistent failure on counterfactual queries—even with explicit twin-network hints—highlights a sharp boundary between what RLVR can fix (execution-level errors) and what it cannot (strategic-level gaps like constructing twin networks), contrasting with more positive results in commonsense causal settings. Open questions include whether these findings generalize beyond Qwen-2.5-Instruct, whether the counterfactual gap can be bridged with different training signals or larger models, and how execution quality versus strategy quality decompose in RLVR's generalization mechanism.
