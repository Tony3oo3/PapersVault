## Problem

Existing diffusion-based data augmentation methods inflate training datasets by 10–30× to achieve meaningful gains, incurring substantial computational cost while struggling to ensure generation diversity. The paper asks whether augmenting the full dataset is necessary, or whether a targeted subset—specifically examples not learned early in training—can be synthetically augmented to outperform full-data augmentation at a fraction of the cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263211/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes real training images identified as slow-learnable (via early-training clustering of model outputs) together with a text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a dog'). It produces synthetic images by adding Gaussian noise to the real image up to a chosen timestep t* and then denoising with GLIDE (guidance scale 3, 100 steps, saving every 10 steps), yielding up to 5× synthetic copies per slow-learnable example. The final augmented dataset contains only 30–40% additional examples overall, rather than doubling or tripling the full set.

## Method

TADA (TArgeted Diffusion Augmentation) selectively augments only the slow-learnable subset of training data using faithful diffusion-generated images. It identifies this subset by clustering model outputs after a few epochs and selecting the higher-loss cluster, then generates synthetic images by noising the real example to timestep t* and denoising with a text-to-image model conditioned on both the class prompt and the original image, preserving semantic features while introducing independent noise. The theoretical analysis on a two-layer CNN proves that (i) SAM suppresses noise learning while accelerating slow-learnable features, (ii) synthetic generation with independent noise amplifies features without magnifying noise—unlike upsampling, which replicates the same noise k times and inflates gradient variance—and (iii) mini-batch SGD converges faster on the generated dataset than on the upsampled one. Empirically, augmenting just 30–40% of data with k=4–5 synthetic copies improves generalization by up to 2.8% across ResNet, ViT, ConvNeXt, and Swin on CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet, and ImageNet, and enables SGD to surpass SAM on CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet.

## Results

TADA improves test classification error by up to 2.8% on TinyImageNet (with SAM) while augmenting only 30–40% of the training data (k=4–5). On CIFAR-10, TADA reduces ConvNeXt-T test error from 37.33% (Original) to 27.40% and Swin-T from 16.10% to 14.57%, substantially outperforming the upsampling baseline USEFUL (34.16% and 14.93% respectively). Notably, TADA combined with plain SGD outperforms the SAM optimizer on CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet, and on ImageNet TADA achieves the highest Top-1 and Top-5 accuracies for both ResNet-18 and ResNet-50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TADA consistently outperforms USEFUL (upsampling of slow-learnable examples, Nguyen et al. 2024) across all datasets and architectures; the gap widens with larger augmentation factors (k>2 hurts USEFUL but helps TADA). TADA+SGD surpasses the SAM optimizer on CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet, demonstrating that targeted synthetic augmentation can replicate SAM's benefit in feature-learning homogeneity without doubling training time. On ImageNet, TADA outperforms Boomerang (Luzi et al. 2022) while augmenting only 65% of the dataset versus Boomerang's 100% synthetic augmentation. In transfer learning on fine-grained datasets (Flowers-102, Aircraft, Stanford Cars), TADA+DiffuseMix beats both no-augmentation and full-dataset DiffuseMix (Islam et al. 2024) by 0.47–0.90% test error. Stacked with TrivialAugment (Müller & Hutter 2021), TADA achieves state-of-the-art test classification error on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, confirming compatibility with existing strong augmentation pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

TADA directly extends the USEFUL framework (Nguyen et al., 2024), which identified that upsampling slow-learnable examples improves generalization by making feature learning more homogeneous. The key departure is replacing upsampling with faithful synthetic generation via diffusion models, motivated by theoretical analysis showing that upsampling amplifies noise while generation introduces independent noise that behaves more like SAM's noise suppression. The generation technique (adding noise to real images and denoising from a partial timestep) builds on He et al. (2023) for few-shot learning, and the broader positioning contrasts with the 10–30× full-dataset augmentation paradigm of Azizi et al. (2023), Fu et al. (2024), and methods like DiffuseMix and Boomerang.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: augmenting only 30–40% of training data with synthetic images outperforms augmenting 100% while reducing generation cost to 0.3–0.4×, making diffusion-based augmentation far more accessible. The theoretical analysis (two-layer CNN) provides a principled explanation for why targeted generation beats upsampling, but the gap between this simplified model and deep architectures remains an open question. The finding that TADA+SGD surpasses SAM on CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet suggests that data-level interventions can substitute for optimizer-level ones, potentially reducing training compute. Limitations include the need for an initial training phase to identify slow-learnable examples and reliance on a pre-trained diffusion model (GLIDE) for generation.
