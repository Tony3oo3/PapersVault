## Problem

Existing model-free RL algorithms for episodic tabular MDPs achieve minimax worst-case regret, but their gap-dependent bounds rely on the coarse global factor SA/Δ_min rather than individual suboptimality gaps Δ_h(s,a), failing to capture the structure of the MDP. The only prior fine-grained model-free algorithm, AMB, contains an improper truncation in its Q-updates and a violation of the martingale difference condition in its concentration analysis, casting doubt on its theoretical guarantees. This paper asks whether rigorous fine-grained gap-dependent regret upper bounds can be established for both UCB-based and non-UCB-based model-free algorithms.

## Input / Output

The algorithms operate on episodic tabular MDPs with S states, A actions, and H steps per episode, where rewards are deterministic in [0,1] and transition kernels are inhomogeneous across steps. At each step the agent observes a state, selects an action via a greedy or ULCB rule, and updates Q-estimates using a step-size η_t = (H+1)/(H+t) with a Hoeffding bonus b_t = 2√(H³ι/t). The theoretical output is a regret upper bound of the form Õ(Σ_{Δ_h(s,a)>0} H⁵/Δ_h(s,a) + H³(Σ√|Z_opt(t)|)²/Δ_min,h + SAH³), which decomposes the regret into per-gap contributions and a term controlled by the number of optimal triples, rather than a single SA/Δ_min factor.

## Method

The core contribution is a novel fine-grained analytical framework that explicitly separates the analysis of optimal and suboptimal state-action pairs at every step. Instead of applying Cauchy-Schwarz uniformly over all SA pairs (as in prior work), the framework bounds the cumulative weighted estimation error for each (s,a) pair individually (Lemmas 3.2–3.3), then applies Cauchy-Schwarz separately to optimal pairs and collectively to suboptimal pairs, exploiting the fact that suboptimal pairs are visited only Õ(log T) times. A recursive relationship for cumulative weighted visitation counts across steps (Lemma 3.4) enables an inductive argument from step H down to 1, yielding the first fine-grained regret bound for UCB-Hoeffding. For the non-UCB setting, the paper identifies and fixes two issues in AMB: it removes improper truncations from Q-updates (applying them to V-estimates instead) and proves via a state-specific decomposition of conditional expectations that the two multi-step bootstrapping estimators jointly form an unbiased estimate of Q*, thereby restoring the martingale difference condition needed for valid Azuma-Hoeffding concentration.

## Results

The paper establishes the first fine-grained gap-dependent regret upper bound for UCB-Hoeffding: Õ(H⁵/Δ_min) in the ideal single-suboptimal-triple case, improving over the prior Õ(H⁶SA/Δ_min) bound of Yang et al. (2021). ULCB-Hoeffding achieves the same fine-grained bound, while Refined AMB yields Õ(Σ H⁵/Δ_h(s,a) + H⁵|Z_mul|/Δ_min). Empirically, across four MDP scales (H,S,A) ranging from (2,3,3) to (10,15,10), all algorithms except AMB exhibit flattening regret curves (logarithmic growth in K), with UCB-Hoeffding performing best overall and ULCB-Hoeffding and Refined AMB achieving comparable performance that both outperform the original AMB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AMB (Xu et al., 2021), the paper identifies two critical flaws: improper truncation in Q-updates that breaks the recursive link to V-estimates, and a violation of the martingale difference condition in the concentration analysis. Both proposed refinements (ULCB-Hoeffding and Refined AMB) outperform AMB empirically across all four MDP scales, with Refined AMB additionally halving the bonus constant by jointly analyzing the two multi-step bootstrapping estimators. Against the prior gap-dependent analysis of UCB-Hoeffding by Yang et al. (2021), the new framework reduces the dependence from Õ(H⁶SA/Δ_min) to Õ(H⁵/Δ_min) in the best case and matches the Simchowitz & Jamieson (2019) lower bound up to polynomial factors in H. UCB-Hoeffding itself remains the strongest performer empirically, while ULCB-Hoeffding and Refined AMB are comparable to each other and both clearly beat AMB, confirming that the ULCB mechanism alone (without multi-step bootstrapping) suffices for fine-grained guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gap-dependent regret analysis for model-free RL from coarse-grained bounds (using a global SA/Δ_min term, as in Yang et al. 2021 and Zheng et al. 2025b) to fine-grained bounds that track individual suboptimality gaps Δ_h(s,a) per state-action-step triple. It builds directly on UCB-Hoeffding (Jin et al., 2018) as the base algorithm and revisits AMB (Xu et al., 2021), the only prior non-UCB-based algorithm claiming fine-grained guarantees, by identifying two fundamental flaws: improper truncation breaking the Q-to-V recursive link and a violation of the martingale difference condition in the concentration argument. The analytical framework departs from model-based fine-grained analyses (Simchowitz & Jamieson, 2019; Dann et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2025) by handling the temporal mismatch between historical and current value estimates inherent in model-free Q-updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of invalidity in AMB's fine-grained guarantee is significant: it means the only prior rigorous fine-grained model-free bound was actually unsound, and this paper provides the first correct one. The novel analytical framework (separating optimal from suboptimal pairs, recursive weighted visitation bounds) is general and the authors note it can potentially extend to UCB-Bernstein, UCB-Advantage, and Q-EarlySettled-Advantage to further improve H-dependence. An open question is whether the H^5 dependence in the gap terms can be tightened to match the H^2 achieved by model-based methods (Chen et al., 2025) while retaining linear memory, and whether the framework applies to federated or linear-function-approximation settings.
