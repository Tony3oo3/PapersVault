## Problem

Existing algorithms for quantile-fair policy aggregation in multi-objective MDPs require explicit access to the full transition probability table and solve linear programs whose size scales with the number of states and actions, making them intractable for practical deep RL settings. The paper asks whether provably oracle-efficient algorithms exist for fair reinforcement learning that only require a black-box policy optimization oracle, with no direct dependence on |S| or |A|.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes as input a MOMDP with n agents (each having a distinct reward function), a policy optimization oracle (any standard single-reward RL solver), and a target accuracy ε. It outputs a mixed policy (uniform distribution over T = O(log n / ε²) individual policies) that is (q*−ε)-max-quantile fair with respect to the optimal occupancy distribution, along with the maximum achievable quantile q*. The total oracle complexity is O(n) calls to the policy optimization oracle and O(log n) calls to a policy evaluation oracle, with no explicit dependence on state or action space sizes.

## Method

The method has two key technical components. First, it replaces the uniform distribution over the full occupancy polytope (which provably requires exponential samples to estimate quantiles) with a distribution induced by the convex hull of individually optimal policies for each agent — the 'optimal occupancy distribution' — which guarantees that 1/e-quantile fair policies exist and that each agent receives at least a 1/n fraction of their maximum return. Sampling from this distribution is efficient: when the n reward-embedding vectors are linearly independent (which holds with probability 1 under noisy reward estimates), it reduces to drawing from a simplex via normalized exponential random variables; otherwise, a hit-and-run Markov chain in Õ(n³) iterations suffices. Second, it replaces the linear-program-based fair policy computation with a multiplicative weights update (MWU) scheme from online convex optimization, where at each of T = O(log n / ε²) steps the algorithm solves a standard single-reward RL problem with a weighted combination of the n reward functions, updating weights multiplicatively based on the gap between achieved returns and the estimated quantile thresholds. This yields a (q−ε)-quantile fair mixed policy with total welfare at least U−ε, and a binary search over q and U recovers the max-quantile fair solution.

## Results

On the factory monitoring MDP (5 warehouses, 10 agents), the oracle-efficient max-quantile algorithm achieves a Nash welfare of 0.701±0.015 and a Gini coefficient of 0.087±0.007, with a maximum quantile q* = 0.883±0.055. The original max-quantile method (Alamdari et al.) achieves Nash welfare 0.654±0.010, Gini 0.066±0.003, and q* = 0.999±0.001. Computationally, the oracle-efficient method runs in approximately 10 minutes on a laptop, compared to roughly 2 hours on a 32-core AMD EPYC server with 258 GB RAM for the original method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original max-quantile algorithm of Alamdari et al. (2024), the oracle-efficient method wins on Nash welfare (0.701 vs. 0.654) but loses slightly on the Gini coefficient (0.087 vs. 0.066, where lower is more equal). The reported q* of 0.999 for the original method is misleading: the paper shows (Theorem 5.1) that uniform sampling from the full occupancy polytope requires exponentially many samples to estimate meaningful quantiles, and even 5×10⁵ random policies only yield q* = 1 − 0.001, indicating nearly all sampled policies achieve trivially low returns. In contrast, the oracle-efficient method's q* = 0.883 reflects a well-estimated quantile over the optimal reward embedding polytope. Both max-quantile variants substantially outperform the egalitarian baseline (Nash welfare 0.4655) and the utilitarian baseline (Nash welfare 0.5736) on all fairness metrics. The most striking practical advantage is computational: the oracle-efficient method requires only O(n) policy optimization oracle calls and runs in ~10 minutes on a laptop, versus ~2 hours on a 32-core server for the original method that requires explicit access to the full transition table.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fair policy aggregation framework of Alamdari et al. (2024), which computed q-quantile fair policies in MOMDPs via linear programs over the full state-action space, to the oracle-efficient setting where only a policy optimization subroutine is available. It also departs from the prior work's choice of uniform distribution over the entire occupancy polytope, which the paper proves leads to exponential sample complexity, by instead using the uniform distribution over the convex hull of individually optimal policies (the optimal reward embedding polytope). The algorithmic core replaces the prior linear-programming approach with multiplicative weights update from online convex optimization, requiring only O(n) oracle calls and O(log n) policy evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first provably oracle-efficient algorithm for max-quantile fair reinforcement learning, opening a path to fairness guarantees in any setting where standard policy optimization is possible, including deep RL. The key open question the paper identifies is how to obtain individual reward functions in practical RLHF pipelines, where current practice treats multiple raters as noisy estimates of a single ground-truth reward. The empirical validation on a factory monitoring MDP shows orders-of-magnitude speedup (10 minutes vs. ~2 hours) and competitive fairness metrics, but the scope remains limited to tabular or oracle-accessible MDPs with explicitly available per-agent reward functions.
