## Problem

A gap exists between the best theoretical and practical algorithms for ℓp regression: the state-of-the-art theoretical method (Adil et al., SODA 2019) achieves O(p² n^{(p-2)/(3p-2)} log(n/ε)) iterations but relies on complex subroutines with tuned hyperparameters, while the practical p-IRLS algorithm (Adil et al., NeurIPS 2019) is efficient in practice but only guarantees O(p³ n^{(p-2)/(2p-2)} log(n/ε)) iterations. This paper asks whether one can retain the empirical efficiency of an IRLS approach while matching the state-of-the-art theoretical runtime.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a matrix A ∈ R^{d×n}, a vector b ∈ R^d in the column span of A, and an accuracy parameter ε, and produces a vector x satisfying Ax = b with ‖x‖_p ≤ (1+ε)‖x*‖_p. Each iteration requires solving a linear system of the form ADA^T φ = b where D is a non-negative diagonal matrix, which standard numerical solvers handle efficiently. The main analysis covers p ≥ 2, with a duality-based reduction extending the guarantees to 1 < p < 2.

## Method

The algorithm uses a primal-dual framework in which the update rule is derived from maintaining an invariant on the dual objective: the increase in electrical energy E(r) must be at least M² per unit increase in ‖r‖_q. Rather than relying on Taylor expansion of ‖x‖_p^p as in prior IRLS methods, the approach multiplicatively updates dual coordinates by large polynomial factors (up to n^{2/(2q+1)}), enabling much longer steps than standard multiplicative weights or mirror descent schemes. For the high-precision regime, this IRLS solver is embedded inside the iterative refinement template of Adil et al. (2019a), where the residual solver handles a mixed ℓp + ℓ2 objective to constant approximation. The resulting algorithm achieves O(p² n^{(p-2)/(3p-2)} log(n/ε)) linear system solves, matching the best theoretical bound via a simple, lightweight iterative scheme that empirically outperforms both p-IRLS and CVX solvers.

## Results

On six real-world UCI regression datasets (p=8, ε=10⁻¹⁰), the proposed IRLS algorithm uses 36–42 linear system solves versus 44–50 for p-IRLS, and runs in 1.1–18.1 s versus 1.6–32 s. On synthetic random matrices and random graphs, the method is 1–2.6× faster than p-IRLS on average, with the gap widening as problem size and p increase. Theoretically, the algorithm achieves O(p² n^{(p−2)/(3p−2)} log(n/ε)) linear system solves in the high-precision regime, matching the state-of-the-art bound of Adil et al. (2019a) while using a simpler IRLS-based scheme rather than complex subroutines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: p-IRLS (Adil et al., NeurIPS 2019) and CVX (using SDPT3 and SeDuMi solvers). Against p-IRLS, the proposed method wins on both iteration count and wall-clock time across all six real-world datasets and all synthetic instances, with a 1–2.6× average speedup that grows with problem size and p. Against CVX, the proposed method is significantly faster on every instance tested; CVX requires fewer iterations but each iteration is far more expensive, and on large instances (e.g., 1844352×11 Power Consumption) CVX cannot complete within a reasonable time while the proposed method finishes in ~16 s. The paper also notes that the theoretical algorithm of Adil et al. (SODA 2019 / J. ACM 2024) achieves the same iteration bound but relies on complex subroutines and tuned hyperparameters, making it impractical—whereas the proposed IRLS scheme is lightweight and directly implementable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the iterative refinement framework of Adil et al. (SODA 2019) for high-precision ℓ_p regression and the practical IRLS solver of Adil et al. (NeurIPS 2019), but departs from both by deriving the IRLS update rule from a primal-dual invariant on the dual energy function rather than from Taylor expansion or multiplicative weights. The approach is conceptually related to Ene and Vladu's (2019) primal-dual method for ℓ_1/ℓ_∞ regression, but requires new techniques because general ℓ_p regression lacks the coordinate-decomposable structure of those special cases. The key departure is that the update allows dual coordinates to change by large polynomial factors per step, avoiding the small-step constraint of standard mirror descent or MWU frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a notable gap in ℓ_p regression algorithms: the state-of-the-art theoretical runtime (O(p² n^((p-2)/(3p-2)) log(n/ε)) linear system solves) was previously achievable only through complex subroutines with tuned hyperparameters that did not yield practical implementations, while the practical p-IRLS had a worse theoretical bound. The mixed ℓ_p + ℓ_2 residual solver is of independent interest for regularized regression problems common in machine learning. Open questions include whether the primal-dual energy invariant technique generalizes to other structured convex objectives, and whether the approach can be extended to the overconstrained regime (where Jambulapati et al. 2022 use Lewis weights) to further reduce dependence on n.
