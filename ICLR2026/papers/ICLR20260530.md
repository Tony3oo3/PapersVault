## Problem

Existing Latent Video Diffusion Model (LVDM) based VFI methods suffer from a pixel-fidelity bias: their reconstruction objectives prioritize local texture over global motion coherence, producing ghosting and deformation artifacts under large, complex motions. Additionally, the iterative multi-step diffusion sampling required to synthesize full high-frequency frames imposes prohibitive computational cost, preventing real-time deployment. The paper asks whether a diffusion model can be re-tasked to predict only motion (rather than full frames), enabling one-step sampling at low resolution while preserving interpolation quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of endpoint RGB frames I_0 and I_1 and produces L intermediate frames at arbitrary timestamps τ_j ∈ (0,1). The LVDM operates at a fixed low resolution (448×256) with 7 sparse latent keyframes, while the final output is rendered at the input resolution (up to 1280×720) by upscaling optical flows and warping full-resolution inputs. This decoupling means the expensive diffusion step is resolution-independent, so runtime remains nearly constant (~55–63 ms) across 576×320 to 1280×720 outputs.

## Method

RDVFI formulates VFI as a two-phase pipeline: (1) a one-step LVDM (built on SVD or Wan2.1) generates N sparse latent keyframes at low resolution, which serve as temporal anchors to determine coefficients of high-order continuous pixel trajectories via cubic convolution; (2) a lightweight synthesis network samples these trajectories for dense optical flows, warps the input frames, and fuses them with warped latent keyframes to handle appearance changes (occlusions, dynamic textures) that optical flow alone cannot model. The key insight is that motion estimation decomposes large displacements into small inter-keyframe components estimated iteratively and fused recursively, making the problem tractable for one-step diffusion. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the trajectory estimator is trained unsupervised via motion-guided decoding (LPIPS + L2 on reconstructed frames); second, the LVDM is trained with a velocity-prediction loss plus a pixel-space reconstruction loss that acts as an implicit motion regularization term, forcing the diffusion model to produce latents that accurately restore inter-frame motion rather than merely matching pixel statistics.

## Results

RDVFI-D achieves the best LPIPS, FID, and FVD across all three benchmark datasets: on DAVIS-7 (LPIPS 0.251, FID 21.17, FVD 189.37), FCVG (LPIPS 0.201, FID 19.98, FVD 197.86), and the self-collected Pixels set (LPIPS 0.253, FID 19.48, FVD 119.21). In terms of efficiency, RDVFI-D runs at 0.057 s per frame (≈17 FPS) at 1024×576 with 13.1 GB GPU memory, achieving a 44× speedup over the strongest video-diffusion baseline Wan (2.579 s) and a 252× speedup over FCVG (14.381 s). In human evaluation, 75–77% of 25 participants selected RDVFI-D as the best method for motion, details, and overall quality, roughly 5× the second-place method (Wan at ~14–16%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDVFI-D outperforms all ten baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest video-diffusion baseline Wan (Wang et al., 2025), RDVFI-D reduces FID from 28.52→19.98 and FVD from 214.45→197.86 on FCVG, while also improving LPIPS from 0.223→0.201; on DAVIS-7 the gap is even larger (FID 26.97→21.17, FVD 248.14→189.37). Compared to FCVG (Zhu et al., 2024), which uses linear motion control, RDVFI-D cuts FID by ~36% (31.24→19.98) and FVD by ~12% on FCVG, and is 252× faster (14.381 s→0.057 s). Against GI (Wang et al., 2024), RDVFI-D improves FID by 53% (43.08→19.98) and FVD by 30% (282.22→197.86) on FCVG. Among non-generative methods, RIFE (Huang et al., 2022) is the only baseline faster than RDVFI-D (0.025 s vs 0.057 s) but trails significantly on quality (FID 39.01 vs 19.98, FVD 366.14 vs 197.86 on FCVG). Image-diffusion methods LDMVFI and MoMo produce severe ghosting on large motions and are outperformed on all metrics by RDVFI-D.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDVFI departs from the dominant LVDM-based VFI paradigm (GI, FCVG, Wan) where the diffusion model directly synthesizes intermediate frames via multi-step sampling, by repurposing the LVDM to generate only sparse latent keyframes that parameterize high-order continuous pixel trajectories. The actual frame synthesis is then handled by optical flow warping of the input frames plus a lightweight fusion network, drawing on the task-oriented flow tradition from Xue et al. (2019) and the iterative flow-fusion strategies of RIFE/FILM. Conceptually, this inverts the role of the diffusion model: rather than a frame generator, it becomes a motion-coefficient estimator, which is what makes one-step sampling sufficient. The architecture builds on pretrained SVD (U-Net) and Wan2.1 (DiT) backbones, demonstrating backbone-agnostic applicability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RDVFI is the first LVDM-based VFI method to achieve real-time performance (17 FPS at 1024×576), a 44× speedup over the prior SOTA (Wan), while simultaneously improving motion coherence and reducing artifacts. The core insight—that decoupling motion estimation from high-resolution rendering eliminates the need for multi-step diffusion sampling—suggests a broader principle for applying diffusion models to tasks where the generative component is structural rather than textural. A clear limitation is the dependence on sufficient overlap between input frames; the method degrades severely at 7-frame skips, leaving large temporal gaps as an open challenge. Future work could explore whether the trajectory-based formulation extends to super-slow-motion or video compression settings the authors mention.
