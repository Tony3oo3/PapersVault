## Problem

Standard PAC-Bayes bounds can be vacuous (infinite) for deterministic learning algorithms regardless of prior choice, and existing MAC-Bayes bounds use the full training set in the KL divergence term, limiting their tightness. This paper asks whether partitioning the training data into blocks and using block-level divergences can yield meaningfully tighter expected generalization bounds, and whether high-probability (PAC) versions of such block-based bounds are possible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264234/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a training set S of n i.i.d. samples partitioned into J = n/m contiguous blocks, a (possibly stochastic) learning algorithm P_{W|S}, a prior Q_W independent of S, and a loss function ℓ. The output is an upper bound on the expected generalization error where the divergence terms involve P_{W|S_j} — the distribution of W conditional only on block S_j, obtained by marginalizing the full algorithm over all other blocks — rather than the full D(P_{W|S}||Q_W). The bound also contains a moment-generating function term that depends on the block size m and number of blocks J.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 1) applies the Donsker-Varadhan variational representation of KL divergence block-by-block, changing measure from the marginalized algorithm P_{W|S_j} to the prior Q_W for each block independently. The proof chains Jensen's inequality (for convexity of the comparator d), Fubini-Tonelli (to separate block expectations), and Donsker-Varadhan (for the per-block change of measure), yielding a bound whose right-hand side is a sum of J block-level divergences plus a moment-generating term scaled by J. Specializations with the Catoni, binary KL, and difference comparators yield explicit bounds on the generalization gap (Corollaries 1–2). The paper further proves an impossibility result (Theorem 2) constructing a learning scenario where the MAC-Bayes bound vanishes at O(n^{-1/2}) but no PAC-Bayes bound of the same form can hold with both a slowly growing error-probability dependence and a fast-converging coefficient.

## Results

In the Gaussian mean estimation example with truncated loss, the original PAC-Bayes bound (corresponding to m=n) is vacuous (right-hand side is infinite) for every possible choice of prior Q_W, while the proposed block-sample MAC-Bayes bound with any m<n yields the finite bound gen ≤ (1/2)√(1/(2(n−m))) = O(n^{-1/2}), with m=1 being optimal. Table 1 characterizes the convergence rate as O(n^{(min(γ−1,0)−1)/2}) under the general assumption that the per-block KL divergence grows as O(m^γ/n), showing a dichotomy: for γ<1, constant block size is optimal, while for γ>1, linear block size (m=Θ(n)) is optimal. Theorem 2 further establishes that no high-probability (PAC-Bayes) version of the block-sample bound can simultaneously achieve O(n^{-1/2}) convergence and logarithmic dependence on the error probability δ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the original PAC-Bayes bound (m=n) and its MAC-Bayes expectation version (Eq. 2). In the Gaussian mean estimation example, both of these baselines are vacuous (infinite) for any prior Q_W because the algorithm is deterministic (Dirac posterior) while the marginal P_W is continuous, making D(P_{W|S}‖Q_W) = ∞ almost surely. The proposed block-sample bound is strictly tighter, yielding a finite O(n^{-1/2}) bound for any m<n. The paper also compares against two suboptimal specializations of its own framework: the binary-KL comparator (Remark 3, Eq. 11) which introduces an extra log(2√m)/m term, and the subgaussian-loss corollary (Corollary 2, Eq. 12) which gives a looser constant. Numerical plots (Figure 1) confirm that the Catoni-based bound (Eq. 14) is the tightest and is not overly sensitive to suboptimal choices of m as long as m≠n. The impossibility result (Theorem 2) shows that the block-sample MAC-Bayes bound cannot be upgraded to a high-probability PAC-Bayes statement with both O(n^{-1/2}) rate and log(1/δ) dependence, a limitation that does not apply to the original PAC-Bayes bound.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the MAC-Bayes (mean approximately correct) framework of Grunwald et al. (2021) by replacing the full-dataset KL divergence term D(P_{W|S}||Q_W) with a sum over block-wise divergences D(P_{W|S_j}||Q_W), where the training set is partitioned into J blocks of size m. The proof technique relies on the Donsker-Varadhan variational representation applied to the marginalized conditional distribution P_{W|S_j}, and the specialization to specific comparator functions (Catoni, binary KL, difference) follows the framework of Germain et al. (2009). The block structure is conceptually inspired by the individual-sample information-theoretic bounds of Bu et al. (2020) and Harutyunyan et al. (2021), but operates on blocks rather than single samples, and the impossibility result in Theorem 2 is distinct from the related negative result in Harutyunyan et al. (2022), which only applies in the m=1 special case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that for deterministic learning algorithms (e.g., computing the sample mean), the standard PAC-Bayes bound is vacuous for every choice of prior, while the block-sample bound yields finite, decaying guarantees—demonstrated concretely in the Gaussian mean estimation example. The impossibility result (Theorem 2) is a meaningful negative contribution: it proves that high-probability (PAC) versions of these block-sample bounds with logarithmic dependence on the error probability cannot exist in general, closing off a natural research direction. A significant limitation is the dependence on the data distribution P_Z through the divergence terms, which restricts immediate applicability when distributional knowledge is unavailable; the authors note that partial knowledge (e.g., an upper bound of the form O(m^γ)/Θ(n)) suffices, but full practical deployment to complex algorithms like deep networks remains an open challenge.
