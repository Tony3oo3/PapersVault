## Problem

Diffusion-based image compositing methods perform well on single-object insertion but degrade in pairwise or sequential settings, where the second insertion overwrites or distorts the first object and spatial relations such as occlusion, support, and containment are not explicitly modeled. The paper identifies this instability as stemming from the absence of object-object interaction modeling: existing pipelines treat each compositing step as an independent foreground-background problem, ignoring cross-object contacts and visibility ordering.

## Input / Output

The model receives a masked background image (object regions zeroed out via a union mask), two cropped object images, and their corresponding binary segmentation masks. Objects are encoded into latent codes by a frozen DINOv2 shape encoder, while the background is encoded by the VAE encoder; the masks are bilinearly downsampled to feature-map resolution to define background, per-object exclusive, and overlap routing regions. The output is a single full-resolution composited image decoded from the VAE after 50 DDIM denoising steps with classifier-free guidance at scale 5.0.

## Method

PICS replaces all 25 U-Net residual blocks (and 13 ControlNet blocks) of a Stable Diffusion v2.1 + ControlNet backbone with Interaction Transformer Blocks that apply self-attention followed by a mask-guided Mixture-of-Experts routing features to three dedicated experts: an identity background expert, per-object exclusive-region experts that inject appearance via cross-attention from scene queries to object codes, and an overlap expert that computes a context-conditioned softmax mixing weight α from background-derived gating queries and adaptively blends the two aggregated object codes before a final cross-attention injection. Training is self-supervised via a denoising diffusion objective that reconstructs the original image from its decomposed background and object pair, requiring no paired compositing data. Two geometry-aware augmentations—multi-view shape priors rendered by Zero123++ and fused through an MLP, and random in-plane rotations—improve robustness to viewpoint and alignment variations. The design is order-agnostic: the α-blending mechanism learns visibility semantics from the background context rather than relying on input ordering, enabling parallel compositing of both objects in a single forward pass.

## Results

On the DreamBooth pairwise compositing benchmark, PICS achieves the best FID (255.5) and CLIP-score (54.02) among all seven compared methods, outperforming the next-best baselines (ObjectStitch/OmniPaint at FID 260.4; ControlCom at CLIP-score 52.38). On the LVIS recompositing validation set, PICS leads in mPSNR (13.88), mSSIM (0.5823), PSNR (18.27), FID (24.99), LPIPS (0.2530), and DreamSim (0.2659), while AnyDoor edges ahead on DINOv2-score (0.6089 vs. 0.5713) and ControlCom marginally leads on CLIP-score (85.39 vs. 85.25). In the user study, PICS scores highest on Quality (17.7%) and Consistency (22.5%), and is competitive on Fidelity (17.7%) with AnyDoor (18.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PICS achieves the best FID (255.5) and CLIP-score (54.02) on the DreamBooth compositing benchmark, beating the closest competitors ObjectStitch and OmniPaint (FID 260.4) by ~5 FID points and ControlCom (CLIP 52.38) by 1.64 points. On DINOv2-score, OmniPaint (0.3741) and InsertAnything (0.3612) slightly outperform PICS (0.3631), with the paper attributing OmniPaint's edge to its stronger FLUX flow-matching backbone. On DreamSim, OmniPaint (0.2632) and AnyDoor (0.2733) both beat PICS (0.3054); the authors note AnyDoor leverages high-frequency edge maps that preserve object structure at the cost of background consistency. On the LVIS recompositing set, PICS dominates in reconstruction metrics (mPSNR 13.88, PSNR 18.27, FID 24.99, LPIPS 0.2530) over all five baselines, while AnyDoor's DINOv2-score advantage (0.6089 vs. 0.5713) is attributed to its additional edge-map input aiding semantic preservation. In the user study, PICS leads on Quality (17.7%) and Consistency (22.5%) but trails AnyDoor and OmniPaint slightly on Fidelity (17.7% vs. 18.4%/19.0%), which the authors link to both methods using DINOv2 for identity encoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

PICS builds on the Stable Diffusion v2.1 + ControlNet architecture as its generative backbone, replacing all U-Net residual blocks with proposed Interaction Transformer blocks that employ mask-guided Mixture-of-Experts to route background, exclusive, and overlap regions to dedicated experts. It departs from the dominant single-object or sequential (Painter's Algorithm) compositing paradigm used by ObjectStitch, AnyDoor, Paint-by-Example, and ControlCom by formulating pairwise compositing as a parallel, single-pass problem that explicitly models object-object spatial interactions. The multi-view shape prior leverages Zero123++ for out-of-plane augmentation, while the adaptive α-blending mechanism in the overlap expert is a novel contribution not present in prior diffusion-based compositing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and recurring failure mode in diffusion-based image editing: sequential compositing degrades previously inserted content and produces contact/occlusion artifacts, limiting the usability of these tools in iterative creative workflows. By demonstrating that parallel pairwise compositing with explicit interaction modeling yields consistent gains across virtual try-on, indoor, and street-scene settings, PICS provides a concrete architectural recipe (region-routed MoE + attention-gated blending) that other editing pipelines could adopt. Open questions remain around scalability beyond four objects, robustness under diverse lighting, and the limited capacity of the frozen shape encoder in extremely cluttered scenes, which the authors acknowledge as sources of geometry and texture degradation.
