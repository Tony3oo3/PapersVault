## Problem

Transformer-based models excel at capturing temporal dependencies in medical time series (MedTS) but struggle with channel dependencies due to a structural mismatch: MedTS signals originate from centralized biological sources (the brain for EEG, the sinoatrial node for ECG), while self-attention treats all token pairs symmetrically in a decentralized manner. This uniform pairwise interaction dilutes the principal centrally-driven synchronization patterns essential for accurate channel-dependency modeling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×C} (e.g., 256 timestamps × 16–33 channels for EEG, 250–300 timestamps × 12–15 channels for ECG) and produces a class label Ŷ ∈ R^K (disease type or activity class). A key design choice is Adaptive Dual Tokenization: the input is simultaneously embedded as temporal tokens (flattened patches of L timestamps across all channels) and channel tokens (the full T-length series of each channel), and the framework can ablate either branch (M=0 or N=0) to match datasets where only one dependency pattern dominates.

## Method

The core contribution is CoTAR (Core Token Aggregation-Redistribution), an MLP-based module that replaces self-attention with a centralized star-shaped communication pattern. Given S tokens of dimension D, CoTAR first projects each token through a two-layer MLP to a lower-dimensional core space (D_c = D/4), applies softmax across the token axis to obtain aggregation weights, computes a weighted sum to produce a single data-conditional core token, then concatenates this core token back onto every original token and fuses them through a second MLP. This reduces inter-token interaction complexity from O(S²) to O(S) while enforcing a centralized aggregation-redistribution structure that mirrors the brain/heart as a physiological coordinator. The full TeCh framework stacks M temporal and N channel Transformer encoders (each with CoTAR in place of attention) over the dual tokenization, averages the learned representations across tokens, and projects to classification logits. Unlike parameter-initialized global tokens in TimeXer or CATS, the CoTAR core token is generated adaptively from each input, making it subject-conditional and better suited to inter-patient heterogeneity in clinical settings.

## Results

On five medical time series benchmarks (3 EEG, 2 ECG) under the Subject-Independent protocol, TeCh achieves a 4.11% relative average-metric gain over the previous state-of-the-art Medformer, with the largest improvement of 11.6% on APAVA (88.99 vs. 79.74 average across six metrics). TeCh also uses only 33% of the memory and 20% of the inference time of Medformer while delivering 10.3% better accuracy on APAVA. On two human activity recognition datasets (FLAAP, UCI-HAR), TeCh further outperforms Medformer by an average of 4.28%, and exhibits lower variance across seeds (mean std 0.84 vs. 0.96, a 12.4% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TeCh outperforms all ten Transformer-based baselines (Autoformer, FEDformer, Informer, iTransformer, MTST, Nonformer, PatchTST, Reformer, vanilla Transformer, and Medformer) on the average of six metrics across four of five MedTS datasets. Against the previous SOTA Medformer, TeCh achieves the largest margin on APAVA (+9.25 avg, 11.6% relative) and TDBrain (+3.16 avg), while on ADFTD it is marginally behind in average (54.16 vs. 54.63) but leads in Accuracy (+1.27) and Precision (+2.00). On the two HAR generalization datasets, TeCh beats Medformer by +3.80 avg on FLAAP and +3.57 avg on UCI-HAR. In the supplementary comparison with dual-dependency and global-token methods (Leddam, TimeXer), TeCh leads on all five MedTS datasets by 2–15 points in accuracy, confirming that the centralized CoTAR mechanism provides a consistent advantage over decentralized attention-based alternatives. Notably, TeCh also achieves lower standard deviations than Medformer across all datasets (mean 0.84 vs. 0.96), indicating more stable and robust generalization to unseen subjects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) and the Channel embedding tokenization strategy from iTransformer (Liu et al., 2024), but departs fundamentally from the standard self-attention mechanism. The authors argue that attention's decentralized all-to-all token interaction is structurally mismatched with medical time series signals (EEG, ECG) that originate from centralized biological sources (thalamo-cortical circuits, sinoatrial node). Their CoTAR module replaces attention with a centralized MLP-based aggregation-redistribution via a single core token, drawing an analogy to star-topology networks in distributed systems, and positions itself against Medformer (Wang et al., 2024) as the prior SOTA for MedTS classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, domain-motivated argument for why a simple centralized bottleneck can outperform the more expressive but decentralized attention mechanism on biologically centralized signals, supported by quantitative centralization metrics (SCI, DIC) that formally distinguish MedTS from decentralized domains like energy and climate. The quadratic-to-linear complexity reduction is practically significant for long, high-dimensional medical sequences. However, the ADFTD results are mixed (TeCh trails Medformer slightly on average), and the PTB-XL gain is marginal, suggesting the centralized inductive bias helps most when channel dependencies are the dominant signal. Open questions include whether this generalizes to other centralized biological signals (fMRI is mentioned but not tested) and whether the core token's interpretability as a 'latent global physiological state' holds under adversarial or out-of-distribution conditions.
