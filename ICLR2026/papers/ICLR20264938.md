## Problem

Existing equivariant metanetworks enforce strict equivariance (F(gθ)=gF(θ)), which is sufficient but not necessary for preserving functional identity and often yields sparse, less expressive models. The paper identifies that the true requirement is only that functionally equivalent parameterizations map to functionally equivalent outputs, motivating a relaxed notion—quasi-equivariance—that allows the output group element to depend on both the input transformation and the specific parameter configuration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264938/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The metanetwork consumes structured weight matrices organized by layer (W_i, b_i for MLPs/CNNs; W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O per head for Transformers) rather than flattened vectors. The quasi-equivariant layer first extracts compact statistical features (mean, variance, five quantiles) from each layer's weights and biases, then uses these to learn a group action applied to the output of the underlying equivariant backbone. Outputs are a scalar generalization prediction (via Kendall's τ ranking) or a class label for INR source identification.

## Method

The core contribution is a quasi-equivariant layer defined as F(θ) = α(θ)β(θ), where β is a standard equivariant map and α: Θ→G is a learned group-valued map that relaxes strict equivariance. For MLPs/CNNs, α outputs positive scale vectors per layer via a Gated-MLP with a sine-based structured noise mechanism (sin(W·x+b)·ε + 1_n), ensuring the learned action stays near identity while adding expressivity. For Transformers, α outputs invertible matrices per attention head using an analogous sine-plus-identity construction (sin(γ(θ))·ε + I_n). The small learnable parameter ε controls the degree of relaxation, and the discrete component of the symmetry group (permutations) is handled by a constant map due to continuity constraints. This adds only ~3–5% parameters over the base equivariant architecture while achieving consistent performance gains across CNN generalization prediction, INR classification, and Transformer generalization benchmarks.

## Results

On the Small CNN Zoo (ReLU subset), Monomial-NFN Quasi achieves 0.926 Kendall's τ with only 3.89% more parameters, matching HNP (0.926) and outperforming the 68.65%-larger Monomial-NFN variant (0.923). On INR classification, Monomial-NFN Quasi reaches 70.21% on MNIST, 35.32% on CIFAR-10, and 62.11% on FashionMNIST, setting new state-of-the-art across all three datasets. On Transformer generalization prediction, Transformer-NFN Quasi achieves 0.911 Kendall's τ on MNIST-Transformers and 0.914 on AGNews-Transformers (no threshold), surpassing the 57–59%-larger Transformer-NFN variants with only 4.5–5.3% additional parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Monomial-NFN Quasi outperforms all metanetwork baselines on CNN generalization: it beats STATNN (0.926 vs 0.915), NP (0.926 vs 0.920), Graph-NN (0.926 vs 0.897), and its own base Monomial-NFN (0.926 vs 0.922), while tying HNP (0.926 vs 0.926) with far fewer parameters (0.26M vs 2.81M). On INR classification, Monomial-NFN Quasi surpasses NP by 0.39% on MNIST (70.21% vs 69.82%) and by 3.90% on FashionMNIST (62.11% vs 58.21%), and outperforms HNP by 4.19% on MNIST (70.21% vs 66.02%). On Transformer generalization, Transformer-NFN Quasi beats Transformer-NFN by 0.006 Kendall's τ on MNIST-Transformers (0.911 vs 0.905) and by 0.004 on AGNews-Transformers (0.914 vs 0.910), while also exceeding the much larger Transformer-NFN variants (57–59% more parameters) that only reach 0.907 and 0.913 respectively. Classical ML baselines (XGBoost, LightGBM, Random Forest) are consistently outperformed by 0.03–0.14 Kendall's τ on both Transformer datasets. The key finding is that the quasi-equivariant layer delivers gains comparable to or exceeding 57–69% parameter expansions while adding only 3.9–5.3% parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own line of work on equivariant metanetworks—Monomial-NFN (Tran et al., NeurIPS 2024) for MLP/CNNs and Transformer-NFN (Tran et al., ICLR 2025) for Transformers—by introducing quasi-equivariance as a principled relaxation of strict equivariance. Conceptually, it departs from the broader equivariant metanetwork literature (Navon et al. 2023; Zhou et al. 2024a; Kofinas et al. 2024) that enforces exact group-action symmetry, and from the relaxed-equivariance thread in general deep learning (Kaba & Ravanbakhsh 2023; Wang et al. 2022), by formalizing a necessary-and-sufficient condition (Definition 3.1) tied directly to functional equivalence classes rather than approximate symmetry. The maximal symmetry group framework (Definition 2.2) unifies prior identifiability results for ReLU networks, CNNs, and multihead attention under a single algebraic lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical justification for why strict equivariance, while sufficient, is overly rigid for metanetwork design, and shows that a minimal relaxation (a learned group-valued map α) yields consistent gains across three benchmarks with only 3–5% additional parameters—far more efficient than the 57–68% parameter increase from simply scaling up equivariant layers. A key limitation is that the quasi-equivariant layer has been applied only to linear-architecture metanetworks (Monomial-NFN, Transformer-NFN); extension to graph-based or other nonlinear metanetwork structures remains open. The framework also opens the door to applications in computational chemistry, physics, and materials science where symmetries hold only approximately, though no such experiments are presented here.
