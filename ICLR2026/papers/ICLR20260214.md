## Problem

Spiking Neural Networks suffer from two fundamental limitations: the binary spike firing mechanism constrains the representational space, limiting the network's ability to encode complex spatiotemporal patterns, and existing surrogate gradient functions for integer-valued spikes introduce gradient mismatch and oscillation that hinder convergence. Prior approaches—learnable membrane parameters, normalization, and multi-bit spikes—reduce information loss locally but do not fundamentally expand the output space or capacity of SNNs, nor do they provide stable training for deep networks with integer spike forms.

## Input / Output

The method accepts static RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) or DVS event streams (128×128 for CIFAR10-DVS, variable for DVS-Gesture) and outputs a class label. Inputs are temporally encoded via direct encoding, where pixel values are fed at each timestep. During training the network emits real-valued (integer) spikes, but at inference the PIT matrix is folded into the next layer's weights via reparameterization, restoring pure binary spike-driven (AC-only) computation with no additional MAC operations.

## Method

The core contribution is a Parametric Invertible Transformation (PIT): a learnable diagonal matrix A_t^l applied in conjugate manner around the firing operation, yielding s_t = A_t · clip(⌊A_t⁻¹ · u_t⌋, 0, D) within an Integer LIF neuron. The diagonal structure keeps computation to element-wise multiply/divide, and the matrix is initialized channel-wise using a 3-sigma rule on the membrane potential distribution rather than min-max, avoiding outlier sensitivity and wasted quantization levels. A rectified surrogate gradient is designed based on the distance between the input and the rounding decision boundary, adding an ℓ₂ penalty that pushes inputs away from boundaries to suppress oscillation. A theoretical framework formalizes the spatiotemporal representation capacity as log(T·(D+1)^N), showing that PIT expands the representation space by introducing time-varying, channel-specific degrees of freedom into the combination coefficients while preserving the logarithmic scaling with quantization level and time steps.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, PIT with ResNet19 (T×D=1×4) achieves 96.72% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best prior SNN training method (Trainable Ternary Spike, 95.80%) by 0.92%. On CIFAR-100, PIT with ResNet19 reaches 81.59%, outperforming Trainable Ternary Spike (80.20%) by 1.39%. On ImageNet-1k, PIT with E-SpikeFormer-M (T×D=1×4) achieves 79.41%, exceeding the E-SpikeFormer-M baseline (78.50%) by 0.91%, and PIT with SEW ResNet34 (72.66%) surpasses the SEW ResNet34 baseline (67.04%) by 5.62%. On neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS, PIT with VGG11 (T×D=10×1) reaches 80.50%, beating Trainable Ternary Spike (79.80%) by 0.70%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIT consistently outperforms all compared baselines across all five datasets. On CIFAR-10, PIT (ResNet19, 1×4) beats Trainable Ternary Spike (95.80%) by 0.92% and IM-Loss/DSR (95.40%) by 1.32%, while using fewer equivalent timesteps (1×4 vs. 2×2 or 4×1). On CIFAR-100, PIT (ResNet19) surpasses Trainable Ternary Spike by 1.39% and TET by 7.12%. On ImageNet-1k, the gains are most dramatic: PIT with SEW ResNet34 improves over the SEW ResNet34 baseline by 5.62% (72.66% vs. 67.04%), and PIT with E-SpikeFormer-M exceeds the E-SpikeFormer-M baseline by 0.91% (79.41% vs. 78.50%). On CIFAR10-DVS, PIT (VGG11, 10×1) outperforms Trainable Ternary Spike (79.80%) by 0.70% and Real Spike (72.85%) by 7.65%. On DVS-Gesture, PIT (VGG11, 20×1) surpasses Real Spike by 2.02% and PIT (20×2) beats Trainable Ternary Spike by 1.84%. Notably, PIT achieves these gains with fewer or equal equivalent timesteps (T×D) compared to most baselines, indicating improved per-timestep representational efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the I-LIF neuron (Luo et al., 2024) for integer-valued training with spike-driven inference, and on the reparameterization/weight-folding technique from Real Spike (Guo et al., 2022c) to preserve event-driven computation. However, it departs from prior spike-enhancement approaches (ternary spikes, real-valued spikes, membrane potential normalization) by introducing a conjugate parametric invertible transformation that simultaneously reorganizes the potential distribution before firing and expands the output space after firing, rather than modifying only one side of the firing operation. The rectified surrogate gradient for integer spikes also extends beyond the rectangular STE used in prior I-LIF work, addressing a known oscillation problem in quantized training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical framework (logarithmic capacity scaling with D and T) that gives practitioners a principled way to reason about the representational trade-offs in SNN design, and the conjugate PIT offers a drop-in enhancement that preserves the energy-efficient spike-driven inference paradigm while substantially closing the gap to ANN counterparts. The finding that fewer timesteps with more firing levels (1×4) outperforms more timesteps with fewer levels (4×1) for static images is a useful practical guideline. Open questions include whether the diagonal PIT form is truly optimal versus full-rank transformations, how the method scales to video or language tasks with genuine temporal structure, and whether the additional memory overhead during training limits deployment in the most resource-constrained neuromorphic hardware.
