## Problem

While replicable RL algorithms exist for tabular settings, extending provable replicability guarantees to function approximation has remained an open problem. RL algorithms are known to be unstable in practice, and the formal notion of replicability—requiring two runs on independently sampled data to produce identical outputs with high probability—had not been established for the linear MDP setting where rewards and transitions are linear functions of a shared feature embedding.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume state-action feature vectors φ(s,a) (bounded in ℓ₂ norm), scalar rewards, next-state transitions, and a shared random string r that must be identical across two independent runs. In the generative model setting, a core set of state-action pairs covering the feature space is required; in the episodic setting, the algorithm collects batches of full trajectories under the current policy. The output is a list of per-step policies {π̂_h} that must be exactly identical (not merely similar) across two runs, along with intermediate Q-function and value-function estimates and rounded weight/covariance matrices.

## Method

The paper introduces two replicable estimation primitives—R-Ridge-Regression and R-UC-Cov-Estimation—that apply randomized hypergrid rounding (a randomly shifted grid of intervals) to the outputs of standard ridge regression and uncentered covariance estimation, respectively. The key insight for regression is that ridge regression's strong convexity yields a direct parameter-distance bound (O(ε′/λ) rather than O(√ε′/λ)), ensuring two independent runs produce weight vectors close enough that the shared rounding grid maps them to the same point. For covariance estimation, the algorithm rounds only the upper-triangular entries, explicitly symmetrizes, and projects onto the PSD cone to preserve matrix structure. These primitives are then composed into R-LSVI (generative model) and R-LSVI-UCB (episodic exploration), where the latter uses a batched variant of LSVI-UCB—holding the policy fixed within each round and collecting a fresh batch of trajectories—because the replicable primitives require i.i.d. samples from a fixed distribution. A novel perturbation analysis bounds Mahalanobis norms under PSD perturbations of the Gram matrix to show the rounded-covariance UCB bonus still drives sufficient exploration.

## Results

On the offline CartPole dataset (d3rlpy), replicable fitted Q-iteration with rounding (α=0.2) achieves high cumulative return while producing identical weight vectors across runs, with replicability attainable using only a fraction of the available data. On Atari MsPacman and Breakout, quantizing PQN Q-values onto a fixed grid increases pairwise action agreement across seeds compared to standard PQN, while combining quantization with regularization maintains returns within the variance of the unmodified baseline. The paper reports results as mean ± 1.96× standard error over 15 runs, with action agreement measured on holdout expert datasets from Minari.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard PQN (Gallici et al., 2025), a recent simplified deep temporal-difference algorithm used to train Q-functions on MsPacman and Atari Breakout. On both tasks, regularization alone is insufficient to increase cross-seed action agreement, while quantization of Q-values onto a fixed grid consistently improves agreement. However, quantization without regularization can cause the policy to agree on suboptimal actions, as evidenced by lower return on Breakout. The combined approach (regularization + quantization) preserves the agreement gains from quantization while keeping final return within the variance of the unmodified PQN baseline, indicating no meaningful performance loss. Overall, the paper demonstrates that the replicability-inspired techniques (rounding/quantization and regularization) improve policy consistency across random seeds without sacrificing reward, though the improvements are measured qualitatively via figures rather than a single headline metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the replicability framework of Impagliazzo et al. (2022) from tabular RL (Eaton et al., 2023; Karbasi et al., 2023) to linear function approximation, and extends replicable regression from fixed-design bandits (Esfandiari et al., 2023a) to random-design settings. The core technical move is adapting the hypergrid rounding technique to matrix spaces and leveraging the strong convexity of ridge regression to convert gradient uniform convergence (via Foster et al., 2018 and Bartlett et al., 2005) into parameter-space closeness sufficient for replicability. The RL algorithms (R-LSVI and R-LSVI-UCB) are direct replicable analogues of the standard LSVI/LSVI-UCB procedures from Jin et al. (2020), with the key departure being batched data collection to satisfy the i.i.d. assumptions of the replicable primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably replicable RL result beyond the tabular setting, establishing that function approximation need not sacrifice the strong guarantee of exact output identity across runs. However, the sample complexity is extremely high (polynomial in d with exponents up to 56 in the episodic case), and the authors acknowledge that linear MDPs alone are insufficient in practice without a replicable feature learning procedure. The empirical validation on CartPole and two Atari games suggests practical sample requirements are far below worst-case bounds, and the quantization experiment hints that rounding Q-values can improve policy consistency in deep RL, but the evidence is limited to small-scale settings. Key open questions include extending replicability to learned feature representations, continuous action spaces, and whether the near-zero-overhead tabular results of Hopkins et al. (2025) can be adapted to the linear setting.
