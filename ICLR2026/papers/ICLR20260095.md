## Problem

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has proven effective for LLM reasoning but is fundamentally limited by its dependence on domain-specific verifiers and gold-standard solutions, which are costly to construct and restrict applicability to carefully curated tasks. This paper asks whether LLMs can improve their reasoning abilities using only intrinsic, self-generated signals—without any external rewards, labeled data, or verifiable ground truth—thereby enabling scalable self-improvement in open-ended domains where such supervision is unavailable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text prompts (mathematical problems from MATH, competitive programming problems from Codeforces) in a chat-template format and produces free-form reasoning chains followed by a final answer enclosed in a \boxed{} delimiter or Python code within delimiters. Unusually, the reward signal is computed entirely from the model's own next-token probability distributions over the generated sequence—no external evaluator, test suite, or answer key is consulted. During training, the model generates G candidate completions per prompt (typically 7–14), and the relative self-certainty scores across the group determine which trajectories are reinforced.

## Method

INTUITOR introduces Reinforcement Learning from Internal Feedback (RLIF) by replacing the verifiable reward in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with self-certainty, defined as the average KL divergence between a uniform distribution over the vocabulary and the model's next-token distribution at each position. For each query, G candidate outputs are sampled, their self-certainty scores are computed, and group-normalized advantages (mean-centered, std-scaled) drive the policy gradient update—favoring outputs the model itself finds more confident. Critically, the self-certainty is computed online from the evolving policy rather than a fixed base model, which prevents reward exploitation: the paper shows that an offline (frozen) annotator is quickly gamed by the policy appending irrelevant content to inflate its score, while the co-evolving online signal maintains stable training. Self-certainty is preferred over entropy or log-probability rewards because it is less susceptible to length bias and, as demonstrated by Kang et al. (2025), its discriminative power improves with more candidates rather than degrading.

## Results

On in-domain mathematical benchmarks, INTUITOR (Qwen2.5-3B) achieves 79.2% on GSM8K and 61.2% on MATH500, closely matching GRPO's 82.6% and 63.6% without any gold answers. On out-of-domain code generation, INTUITOR substantially outperforms GRPO: 15.3% on LiveCodeBench (vs. 8.5%, a 65% relative gain) and 41.6% on CRUXEval-O (vs. 34.1%, a 76% relative gain). On instruction following, INTUITOR achieves 7.10 length-controlled win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0, surpassing GRPO's 6.91. INTUITOR also learns faster in early training, reaching 81.1% GSM8K and 61.8% MATH after only 10 steps versus GRPO's 75.8% and 59.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), the primary supervised RLVR baseline using golden answers, INTUITOR trades a small in-domain cost for a large out-of-domain gain: on Qwen2.5-3B, INTUITOR scores 79.2% vs. 82.6% on GSM8K and 61.2% vs. 63.6% on MATH500, but achieves 15.3% vs. 8.5% on LiveCodeBench and 41.6% vs. 34.1% on CRUXEval-O, while also edging out GRPO on AlpacaEval (7.10 vs. 6.91). GRPO-PV, which replaces golden answers with plurality voting, performs nearly identically to GRPO on in-domain tasks but still lags INTUITOR on code generation. Against entropy minimization (Agarwal et al., 2025), INTUITOR is markedly more stable: over two epochs on Qwen2.5-3B, entropy minimization initially reaches similar peak accuracy but then exhibits steady decline with a growing length bias, whereas INTUITOR stabilizes around its peak. On Qwen2.5-1.5B, entropy minimization causes immediate collapse into repetitive single-character outputs, while INTUITOR improves all benchmarks from near-zero baselines. The paper also shows that a random-reward baseline (Shao et al., 2025) severely degrades performance, confirming that INTUITOR's gains are non-trivial and attributable to the self-certainty signal specifically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces RLIF (Reinforcement Learning from Internal Feedback) as a new training paradigm, positioning it as a successor to both RLHF (which requires costly human annotation) and RLVR (which demands domain-specific verifiers and gold solutions). The concrete method, INTUITOR, is a minimal modification of GRPO: the externally verifiable reward is replaced by the model's own self-certainty score (the average KL divergence between a uniform distribution and the model's next-token distribution, from Kang et al., 2025). Conceptually, it sits alongside concurrent intrinsic-signal approaches (EMPO, SEED-GRPO, EM-RL, TTRL, Absolute Zero) but distinguishes itself by using a single, continuous, process-aware confidence signal rather than binary outcome checks or entropy minimization, and by demonstrating that the online (co-evolving) version of this signal is critical to prevent reward exploitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that INTUITOR provides a scalable, domain-agnostic training recipe for settings where verifiable rewards are unavailable or prohibitively expensive to construct, and it shows that intrinsic confidence signals can induce structured, R1-like reasoning even in 1.5B-parameter models. Key limitations include the need for careful KL-penalty tuning (out-of-domain performance is highly sensitive to this hyperparameter), the requirement that the base model already possess sufficient domain knowledge to avoid collapse, and the fact that in-domain accuracy is slightly below GRPO. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds-of-billions-parameter models, whether a theoretical account of why self-certainty is a better intrinsic signal than entropy or log-probability exists, and how to principledly combine intrinsic and external reward signals (the paper's two-stage INTUITOR-then-GRPO schedule outperforms two epochs of GRPO alone, but a principled combination rule remains elusive).
