## Problem

Temporal Graph Neural Networks (TGNNs) are widely used for dynamic graph analysis but remain opaque black boxes, and existing explainers either focus on static node features, sample motifs that may include non-counterfactual structures, or lack support for temporal querying. No prior method unifies instance-level counterfactual explanations as temporally cohesive subgraphs, global statistical summaries over time, and queryable access to evolving dependencies in a single training-free framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261099/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a pre-trained TGNN model, a temporal graph represented as a sequence of snapshots, a target node v, a sliding-window duration δ, and a budget k, TemGX produces a set of temporal explanatory subgraphs η where each subgraph contains an explanatory node set V_s (counterfactual nodes whose removal flips the TGNN prediction), a connecting node set V_c ensuring δ-reachability to v, and the induced temporal edges. The method is training-free and model-agnostic, operating directly on the frozen TGNN without retraining. Additionally, TemGX constructs a Dynamic Bayesian Network summary over the discovered subgraphs to support probabilistic 'what-if' queries and temporal pattern matching.

## Method

TemGX formulates explanation discovery as a constrained submodular maximization problem over a sliding window, where the objective Φ combines three components: (1) an Independent Cascade Model–based temporal influence score that recursively aggregates edge-level counterfactual probabilities along δ-reachable paths, (2) a temporal resistance distance (TRD) computed via the Laplacian pseudo-inverse of each snapshot to capture embedding-space structural proximity, and (3) an exponential time-decay factor e^{−λ(t−t′)} that discounts older interactions. The algorithm uses a greedy selection-and-verify loop: at each step it picks the candidate node maximizing Φ, invokes a PTIME counterfactual verification (removing the node's edges and checking if the TGNN output changes), and applies a replacement policy once the budget k is reached, yielding a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee. The generation problem is shown NP-hard via reduction from maximum coverage, while verification is in PTIME. The entire pipeline is one-pass over the sliding window, making it efficient (6–13 s per explanation) compared to training-based or Monte Carlo search baselines.

## Results

TemGX achieves the best AUFSC and Fidelity across all six datasets and four TGNN backbones. On UCIM+TGN (link prediction), TemGX reaches AUFSC 0.475 and Fidelity 0.468, surpassing the strongest baseline CoDy (0.361/0.394) by 31%/19%, and outperforming TempME (0.218/0.219) by over 113%. On spatio-temporal regression (PEMS-BAY+STGCN), TemGX attains AUFSC 0.480 and Fidelity 0.473, exceeding TGVex (0.277/0.277) by ~73%. TemGX is also the fastest training-free method, completing a single explanation in 6–13 s versus 65–125 s for baselines that require additional training or Monte Carlo search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TemGX dominates all four baselines on every dataset and backbone. Against CoDy (the strongest counterfactual baseline using Monte Carlo Tree Search), TemGX gains 19–33% in AUFSC and 9–20% in Fidelity on link prediction, while running 4–10× faster (e.g., 8.2 s vs. 35.0 s on UCIM+TGN; 12.5 s vs. 125.0 s on Wikipedia+TGN). Against TempME (motif-based, learning-based), TemGX improves AUFSC by 113–176% and Fidelity by 113–166%, with a 10× runtime advantage. Against TGNNExplainer (explorer-navigator, learning-based), TemGX achieves 2.2–6.5× higher AUFSC and Fidelity while being 5–8× faster. On spatio-temporal regression, TemGX outperforms TGVex (snapshot-based view mining) by 65–73% in AUFSC and Fidelity with comparable runtime (~6 s), confirming that δ-reachability and temporal decay modeling capture dynamic dependencies that static-snapshot approaches miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TGNN explanation line initiated by TGNNExplainer (explorer-navigator with Monte Carlo search), TempME (motif sampling via information bottleneck), and CoDy (MCTS-based counterfactual edge removal) by unifying three previously separate ingredients: counterfactual verification (borrowed from CF-GNNExplainer for static GNNs), temporal influence scoring (combining Independent Cascade propagation with effective resistance distance and exponential decay), and queryable temporally-cohesive subgraphs. It departs from prior work by enforcing δ-reachability to guarantee temporal cohesiveness, distinguishing explanatory nodes from connecting nodes, and adding a DBN-based summarization layer for cross-time probabilistic queries—none of which appear in the existing TGNN explainer literature. The submodular optimization framing with a (1−1/e) guarantee is standard, but its application to temporal influence scores under a sliding-window constraint is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, model-agnostic design makes TemGX immediately applicable to any pre-trained TGNN without retraining an explainer, which is practically important for high-stakes domains (financial forensics, cybersecurity) where model retraining is costly or infeasible. The queryable temporal subgraph output and DBN summarization open a path toward interactive, hypothesis-driven analysis of dynamic networks that static explainers cannot support. Key limitations include the NP-hardness of exact explanation discovery (mitigated by the greedy approximation), the assumption that pairwise influence scores are fixed during selection (which trivializes the submodularity proof and ignores interaction effects), and untested scalability to very large or streaming temporal graphs. The domain-dependent ablation—where ICM influence is critical for social networks (34% drop) but nearly irrelevant for traffic regression (6% drop)—suggests the framework's components may need task-specific tuning, an open question for broader adoption.
