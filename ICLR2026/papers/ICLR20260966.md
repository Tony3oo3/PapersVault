## Problem

Pretrained LLMs used as text encoders in text-to-motion (T2M) generation lack explicit alignment between motion-related words (e.g., 'clockwise', 'quickly') and their corresponding human skeletal movements, because they are trained on generic text corpora. This word-level cross-modal misalignment, rooted in the word embedding layers, limits T2M models' ability to understand and generalize fine-grained motion semantics, as demonstrated by failures on simple prompts like 'jogs in a clockwise motion.'

## Input / Output

MATE takes a textual description of human motion and, through a MATE-enhanced text encoder (CLIP or DistilBERT with updated word embeddings), produces a 3D skeletal motion sequence via a downstream T2M model (e.g., MoMask, MDM, MMM). The method operates on paired text-motion data from HumanML3D (14,616 sequences / 44,970 descriptions) and KIT (3,911 sequences / 6,278 descriptions), where each motion is a sequence of 3D joint positions. The enhanced encoder is a drop-in replacement: only the word embedding layer (3.2M parameters) is modified, and the rest of the T2M pipeline is retrained from scratch without architectural changes.

## Method

MATE introduces two components to align word embeddings with motion semantics while freezing all higher LLM layers. First, a text-guided motion localization strategy uses ChatGPT to decompose sentences into sub-texts, then solves an optimal partitioning problem to segment motion sequences into aligned clips; a Gaussian-shaped attention prior derived from these segments guides a multi-head attention mechanism that extracts word-specific motion features. Second, a word-guided motion disentanglement module isolates word-specific semantics via two contrastive mechanisms: self-disentanglement pulls disentangled features toward learnable kinematic prototypes (one per word) while pushing away from others, and cross-disentanglement enforces orthogonality when a word's semantics is absent from a motion. The final alignment uses a symmetric InfoNCE loss between projected word embeddings and disentangled motion features, combined with sentence-level InfoNCE and motion reconstruction losses, optimizing only 3.2M word-embedding parameters.

## Results

On HumanML3D, MoMask+MATE achieves the best R-Precision Top-1 of 0.550 and lowest MM-Dist of 2.811 among all compared methods, with FID of 0.040. The +MATE variants consistently improve their base models: MDM jumps from 0.320 to 0.509 Top-1 R-Precision, MotionDiffuse from 0.491 to 0.536, and MMM from 0.515 to 0.541. On KIT, MoMask+MATE reaches 0.443 Top-1 R-Precision and 0.197 FID, outperforming all baselines, though gains are more modest due to the smaller dataset (3,911 sequences vs. 14,616).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoMask+MATE (the default configuration) outperforms all nine baselines on R-Precision Top-1 (0.550 vs. best baseline BAMM at 0.522) and MM-Dist (2.811 vs. best baseline BAMM at 2.936) on HumanML3D. Against the strongest standalone baselines, MoMask+MATE leads BAMM by 2.8 points in Top-1 R-Precision and 0.125 in MM-Dist, and leads ParCo by 3.5 points in Top-1 R-Precision. The +MATE plug-in yields the largest relative gains on weaker base models: MDM improves from 0.320 to 0.509 Top-1 R-Precision (+59% relative) and MotionDiffuse from 0.491 to 0.536 (+9.2%), while the already-strong MoMask sees a more modest but consistent lift (0.521→0.550, +5.6%). On KIT, MoMask+MATE similarly leads all baselines in Top-1 R-Precision (0.443 vs. BAMM 0.436) and FID (0.197 vs. BAMM 0.200), though margins narrow due to the smaller dataset. Notably, MDM+MATE on KIT (0.407 Top-1) still trails several baselines, indicating that the word-embedding fine-tuning benefits are most pronounced when the base generator is already competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

MATE departs from the dominant paradigm in text-to-motion generation where pretrained LLMs (CLIP, DistilBERT) are used as frozen text encoders, arguing that the word embedding layer is the root cause of text-motion misalignment. It contrasts with motion-aware language models (LaMP, MotionGPT, MotionGPT-2) that train new encoders from scratch on small text-motion corpora, and with full LLM fine-tuning approaches (LMM, AvatarGPT, Motion-Agent) that are computationally expensive. The method also extends contrastive learning for text-motion alignment (MotionCLIP, TMR, HumanTOMATO) from the sentence level to the word level, introducing prototype-based contrastive disentanglement and a Gaussian attention prior derived from automatic text-motion joint segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MATE offers a practically attractive plug-and-play solution: by updating only 3.2M word-embedding parameters, it improves multiple existing T2M architectures (MoMask, MDM, MotionDiffuse, MMM) without any architectural changes, making it easy to adopt. The ablation showing that fine-tuning deeper layers causes severe overfitting (Top-1 collapsing to 0.022) while word-embedding-only tuning succeeds is a useful cautionary finding for the broader LLM-adaptation community. Open questions remain around handling words with ambiguous or context-dependent kinematic semantics, addressing word-frequency imbalance in motion descriptions, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two relatively small benchmarks (HumanML3D, KIT) to larger or more diverse motion corpora.
