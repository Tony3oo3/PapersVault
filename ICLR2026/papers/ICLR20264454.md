## Problem

Virtually all existing sequence models—from Transformers to modern linear RNNs like Mamba2, DeltaNet, and Titans—rely exclusively on L2 loss or dot-product similarity for both their learning objective and forgetting mechanism, and prior unification frameworks (Liu et al. 2024a; Wang et al. 2025) remain confined to this Euclidean paradigm. This narrow design space leaves the sensitivity of L2-based optimization to outliers and noise unaddressed, and provides no principled pathway to derive novel architectures with improved robustness and stability.

## Input / Output

The models consume text token sequences and produce next-token predictions in a standard autoregressive fashion. Unusually, the memory is updated at test time via online optimization: as each token arrives, the 2-layer MLP memory parameters are adjusted using a non-standard loss (Huber, Lp norm, or KL-based), meaning the model literally memorizes context into its weights during inference. The fixed-size MLP memory (expansion factor 4, GELU activation) replaces the quadratic KV cache, yielding O(1) per-token inference cost, and the models are evaluated on perplexity, commonsense reasoning accuracy, and needle-in-a-haystack retrieval at up to 8K context length.

## Method

MIRAS reconceptualizes sequence models as associative-memory modules governed by online optimization, decomposing each memory update into an attentional bias (the learning objective, e.g., Lp norm, Huber loss) and a retention gate (the memory regularizer, e.g., Lq norm, KL divergence, f-divergence). Theorem 2.2 formally proves equivalence between the local Learning-Retaining Viewpoint and the global FTRL Viewpoint via Bregman divergence, establishing that retention gates are principled regularizers rather than heuristic forget mechanisms. From this expanded design space, three attention-free, parallelizable architectures are derived: MONETA (Lp attentional bias with Lq retention, p=3, q=4), YAAD (Huber loss providing context-dependent switching between L2 and L1 updates for outlier robustness), and MEMORA (KL-divergence retention constraining memory to a scaled probability simplex via a Softmax update). All three use 2-layer MLP memories with residual connections and LayerNorm, are trained with chunked hybrid linear/non-linear parallelization, and follow a Llama-style backbone with SwiGLU, RoPE, RMSNorm, and 1D depthwise-separable convolutions.

## Results

At 1.3B parameters trained on 100B tokens, all three MIRAS variants (MONETA, YAAD, MEMORA) outperform every baseline on average commonsense reasoning accuracy (56.52, 56.39, 55.87 vs. 56.82 for Titans-LMM, the closest competitor). On language modeling, YAAD achieves the best WikiText perplexity (15.18) and MONETA the best LAMBADA perplexity (11.47), both beating Titans-LMM (15.60 / 11.41). On the RULER Needle-in-a-Haystack benchmark, MONETA achieves 93.5% average accuracy across S-NIAH-PK/N/W at 2K–8K lengths, substantially outperforming Gated DeltaNet (75.8%), TTT (66.1%), DeltaNet (57.9%), and Mamba2 (52.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three MIRAS variants (MONETA, YAAD, MEMORA) are pure recurrent (attention-free) models that outperform every linear RNN baseline. Against RetNet (Sun et al., 2023), Mamba2 (Dao & Gu, 2024), and DeltaNet (Schlag et al., 2021), the MIRAS models win decisively on both perplexity and reasoning accuracy (e.g., MONETA 56.52 avg vs. RetNet 52.02, Mamba2 54.89, DeltaNet 52.14). Against Gated DeltaNet (Yang et al., 2024), the gap narrows (56.52 vs. 55.32 avg) but MONETA and YAAD still lead on WikiText perplexity (15.52/15.18 vs. 16.42) and most reasoning tasks. The closest competitor is Titans-LMM (Behrouz et al., 2025), which edges out MONETA on average reasoning accuracy (56.82 vs. 56.52) and LAMBADA perplexity (11.41 vs. 11.47), but MONETA achieves lower WikiText perplexity (15.52 vs. 15.60) and higher PIQA/BoolQ scores. On the RULER NIAH benchmark, the advantage is overwhelming: MONETA (93.5%) and YAAD (92.9%) surpass Gated DeltaNet (75.8%) by ~18 points, TTT (Sun et al., 2024) by ~27 points, and Mamba2 (52.0%) by ~41 points, with MONETA particularly strong on the synthetic-noise variant (S-NIAH-PK: 99.4% at 2K) thanks to its ℓ₃ attentional bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIRAS builds directly on two concurrent unification efforts: Liu et al. (2024a)'s online-learner viewpoint and Wang et al. (2025)'s online L2-regression viewpoint, formally connecting them via Theorem 2.2 (Bregman divergence equivalence). It also extends the test-time memorization line (Titans, TTT) by replacing their L2-only attentional bias and retention with non-Euclidean alternatives (ℓp norms, Huber loss, KL/f-divergence). The key departure from all prior unification frameworks is that they are implicitly restricted to dot-product similarity and L2 regularization, whereas MIRAS treats these as one point in a broader design space informed by robust optimization and information geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled design tool for the next generation of efficient sequence models, reframing 'forget gates' as regularization and showing that the L2 paradigm is not a necessity but a historical accident. The strong NIAH results (93+ average vs. 75.8 for Gated DeltaNet) demonstrate that non-Euclidean retention and bias genuinely improve long-context recall, not just perplexity. However, the paper acknowledges that all variants still lag behind quadratic Transformers on complex in-context retrieval, and theoretical guarantees for non-convex (MLP) memory optimization remain open. Future work could combine MIRAS's retention mechanisms with growing-memory techniques to close the gap with full attention.
