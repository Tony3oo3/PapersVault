## Problem

Most internal cluster validation indices (CVIs) either assume compact, ball-shaped clusters or ignore noise labels entirely, making them unsuitable for evaluating density-based clusterings like DBSCAN or HDBSCAN. The only existing CVI that handles noise, DBCV, merely penalizes the fraction of noise points without assessing whether those labels are correct, and is further shown to be non-deterministic due to non-unique minimum spanning trees. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a CVI that explicitly evaluates the quality of noise assignments while remaining deterministic and well-defined for all edge cases.

## Input / Output

DISCO takes a dataset X ∈ R^{n×m} and a clustering C consisting of k disjoint clusters plus an optional set of noise points N = X \ ∪C_i. It produces a pointwise score ρ(x) ∈ [-1, 1] for every point (cluster or noise), enabling per-point explainability, and an overall score ρ(X) = avg_{x∈X} ρ(x) bounded in [-1, 1]. Unlike most competitors, DISCO is defined for all edge cases that clustering algorithms may produce, including singleton clusters, a single cluster with noise, and all-noise clusterings, where it assigns scores of 0 or -1 respectively.

## Method

DISCO redefines the Silhouette Coefficient using the density-connectivity distance (dc-dist), which is the minimax path length in the MST over pairwise mutual reachability distances, thereby capturing arbitrary cluster shapes and density structure. For cluster points, ρ_cluster(x) compares the average dc-dist from x to its own cluster against the average dc-dist to the closest other cluster, following the compactness-vs-separation principle. For noise points, DISCO introduces an explicit two-part evaluation: ρ_sparse checks that the noise point's core-distance exceeds the maximum core-distance of every cluster (i.e., it lies in a genuinely sparse region), and ρ_far checks that the dc-dist from the noise point to each cluster exceeds that cluster's maximum core-distance (i.e., it is not density-connected to any cluster); the noise score is the minimum of the two. This pointwise, density-connectivity-based design makes DISCO deterministic, bounded, and the first CVI to reward correctly assigned noise while penalizing noise labels where a cluster assignment would be more appropriate.

## Results

DISCO achieves the highest Pearson Correlation Coefficient (PCC) with ARI on the majority of 19 evaluated datasets, reaching 98.99 on diamond9, 96.83 on dartboard1, 95.79 on COIL20, and 95.71 on complex8. DISCO is the only CVI that is well-defined for all edge cases (singleton clusters, single-cluster-plus-noise, all-noise) and the only one that explicitly evaluates noise label quality. In the density-based vs. centroid-based comparison (Table 2), DISCO scores the correct DBSCAN clustering at 0.59 vs. 0.00 for k-Means on 3-spiral, while Silhouette Coefficient incorrectly prefers k-Means (0.36 vs. 0.0). DISCO is also the only CVI to correctly prefer both optimal clusterings over their suboptimal counterparts in the noise-handling experiments (Table 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISCO outperforms Silhouette Coefficient and S_Dbw on all arbitrarily-shaped clusterings: on 3-spiral, DISCO scores the correct DBSCAN clustering at 0.59 while Silhouette gives it 0.0 (preferring the wrong k-Means at 0.36). Against DBCV, the current state-of-the-art density-based CVI, DISCO is deterministic (DBCV's scores vary by ±0.1–0.3 across 1000 runs on the same clustering due to non-unique MSTs) and explicitly evaluates noise quality rather than merely penalizing the noise fraction; DBCV incorrectly rates a suboptimal clustering (0.63) above the ground truth (0.46) in Figure 1d vs. 1c. Compared to DCSI, MMJ-SC, and LCCV, which handle arbitrary shapes but ignore or implicitly mishandle noise, DISCO is the only method that rewards correctly assigned noise and penalizes mislabeled noise points. Against unbounded methods (CVDD, CDbw, CVNN), DISCO provides scores in [−1, 1] and is defined for all edge cases (singleton clusters, one-cluster clusterings, all-noise), whereas these methods are undefined or produce scores spanning orders of magnitude. On the PCC-with-ARI benchmark (Table 4), DISCO ties or leads on most datasets, with MMJ-SC and LCCV being the closest competitors; however, DISCO is the only CVI that returns a valid score for every dataset-clustering combination without failures.

## Relation to Prior Work

DISCO redefines the Silhouette Coefficient's compactness-vs-separation principle using the density-connectivity distance (dc-dist) from Beer et al. (2023), which itself is built on the mutual reachability distance from OPTICS (Ankerst et al., 1999). It departs from the entire line of density-based CVIs (DBCV, DCSI, LCCV, VIASCKDE, etc.) by being the first to explicitly score the quality of noise labels rather than ignoring them or applying a flat penalty. The paper also reveals that DBCV—the long-standing state-of-the-art density-based CVI—is non-deterministic due to non-unique MSTs, a property never discussed in the original 2014 paper or subsequent literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DBCV non-determinism finding is practically significant: it means that a widely-used evaluation tool can lead users to select suboptimal DBSCAN parameters (70% of the time in one experiment), undermining reproducible scientific assessment of clustering quality. DISCO addresses a genuine gap—noise quality evaluation—while also being the only CVI defined for all edge cases (singleton clusters, one-cluster-plus-noise, duplicate points) that density-based methods routinely produce. Open questions include whether the O(n²) complexity limits applicability to very large datasets, and whether the single hyperparameter μ=5 remains robust across all data distributions and noise levels in practice.
