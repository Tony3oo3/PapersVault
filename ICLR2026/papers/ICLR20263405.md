## Problem

Existing principal-agent MDP literature assumes the agent is perfectly rational, always selecting the utility-maximizing policy. In practice, agents exhibit bounded rationality due to cognitive limits, incomplete information, or finite computation, making the principal's subsidy design a robust worst-case problem. This paper asks: how should the principal design subsidies to guarantee the best possible expected return when the agent may deviate from optimality by up to ε?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263405/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite-horizon MDP with dual reward functions (r_P, r_A) over state-action-timestep triples, a non-negative subsidy scheme Δr, and an agent rationality model parameterized by ε. The output is a subsidy scheme Δr* that is structurally sparse—positive transfers are assigned exclusively to social-welfare-maximizing actions—and the corresponding optimal principal value. A key unusual aspect is that the agent's feasible policy set Π(Δr) depends on the subsidy itself, creating a bi-level coupling that prevents standard convex optimization; the agent's worst-case response may be stochastic (a mixture of the social-welfare-optimal policy and a suboptimal one) rather than deterministic.

## Method

The core technique is a duality-based reformulation of the minimax subsidy design problem. For the globally ε-IC agent, the inner LP over occupancy measures is dualized with variables (α, V), and the order of optimization is swapped so that the outer maximization over Δr becomes tractable: the optimal subsidy is the upper bound imposed by the dual constraints, yielding Δr*(s,a,h) = V*_{x*}(s,h) − Q*_{x*}(s,a,h). This collapses the infinite-dimensional bi-level problem to maximizing a one-dimensional concave function F(x) = x·V*_sw − V*_x − xε/(1−x) over x ∈ [0,1), solvable by standard first-order methods. The structural insight is that optimal subsidies concentrate on social-welfare-maximizing actions, and the agent's worst-case response assigns probability at least x* to the welfare-optimal policy. For state-wise ε-IC agents, the paper proves intractability: one formalization forces non-Markovian agent policies, while the other renders the principal's problem NP-hard.

## Results

The paper's main quantitative result is Proposition 4.3: under the globally ε-IC agent model, the social welfare gap δ_sw = ε/(1−x*) is upper bounded by O(√ε), where x* is the optimal solution to a one-dimensional concave program (Theorem 4.1). For the perfectly rational baseline (Theorem 3.1), the principal's optimal value equals V_sw*(ŝ,0) − V̄_A^{Δr=0}(ŝ,0), i.e., maximum social welfare minus the agent's stand-alone value. Theorem 5.1 establishes NP-hardness for the greedy state-wise ε-IC formulation, while the value-consistent state-wise variant yields non-Markovian agent policies that break the MDP framework entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or competing methods to benchmark against. The paper instead compares three agent rationality models as a progression: (1) the perfectly rational agent serves as a structural baseline, where the optimal subsidy equalizes the agent's Q-values across all actions and the principal achieves the full social-welfare gap; (2) the globally ε-IC agent generalizes this baseline, and the paper shows the optimal subsidy still concentrates on social-welfare-maximizing actions (Proposition 4.2) with the agent assigning probability at least x* to the welfare-optimal policy; (3) the state-wise ε-IC agent, under either formalization, renders the problem intractable—either non-Markovian (value-consistent) or NP-hard (greedy). The key structural insight across all tractable cases is that optimal subsidies exclusively reward social-welfare-maximizing actions, a property that degrades to intractability when the ε-tolerance is enforced per-state rather than globally.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the principal-agent MDP literature (contract-based models, reward shaping, policy teaching) by introducing a robust subsidy design framework for bounded-rationality agents. While prior work on reward shaping (Gan et al., 2022b; Ben-Porat et al., 2024) and steering by payments (Monderer & Tennenholtz, 2003; Zhang et al., 2023) assumes perfectly rational agents or treats budget as an external constraint, this paper incorporates subsidy costs directly into the principal's objective and formulates a minimax problem over ε-incentive-compatible agent responses. The globally ε-IC model is a novel intermediate between perfect rationality and full irrationality, and the reduction to a one-dimensional concave optimization via LP duality is a new structural result not present in prior MDP principal-agent work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical characterization of how much social welfare is lost when agents are only approximately rational, with the O(√ε) gap bound offering a quantitative guarantee for mechanism designers. The finding that optimal subsidies still concentrate on social-welfare-maximizing actions even under bounded rationality gives practitioners a simple structural principle for subsidy design. However, the state-wise ε-IC results (NP-hardness, non-Markovian policies) suggest that finer-grained rationality constraints are computationally intractable, limiting practical applicability. Open questions include learning-based settings where the principal lacks prior knowledge of the agent's reward function or ε value, and whether the 1D reduction extends to stochastic or partially observable environments.
