## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves photorealistic novel view synthesis but is impractical on mobile devices due to two bottlenecks: the depth-sorting step required for alpha blending (the primary computational cost) and the large storage footprint (hundreds of MB). No prior method simultaneously eliminates sorting, compresses the Gaussian representation to mobile-scale storage, and preserves rendering quality comparable to the original 3DGS.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260828/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes a compact 4.6 MB compressed model (quantized Gaussian attributes via K-means codebooks + Huffman coding, first-order SH coefficients decoded by 16-bit MLPs) and a camera pose, producing a novel-view RGB image at up to 1600×1063 resolution. Rendering is implemented in Vulkan 2.0 on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 GPU, achieving 127 FPS cold-start and 74 FPS steady-state, with total power draw of 0.83 W versus 5.89 W for quantized 3DGS.

## Method

Mobile-GS replaces the sorting-dependent alpha blending with a depth-aware order-independent rendering formula C = (1−T)·Σ(c_i α_i w_i)/Σ(α_i w_i) + T·c_bg, where the per-Gaussian weight w_i = φ_i² + φ_i/d_i² + exp(s_max/d_i) uses inverse depth and maximum scale to modulate contribution without any ordering. A lightweight 3-layer MLP (256→128→64) conditioned on the camera-to-Gaussian direction, scale, rotation, and SH coefficients predicts a view-dependent opacity and weight φ, compensating for transparency artifacts introduced by the unordered blending. For compression, third-order SH (3×16) is distilled to first-order (3×4) via a Mini-Splatting teacher, and all Gaussian attributes are quantized through K-means clustering into 5 sub-vector groups with per-group codebooks followed by Huffman entropy coding; SH features are further decomposed into diffuse and view-dependent components decoded by compact MLPs. A contribution-based pruning strategy jointly removes Gaussians with both low opacity and low scale using an accumulated voting mechanism over training iterations, reducing the primitive count from 0.56M to 0.47M with minimal quality loss.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, Mobile-GS achieves PSNR 27.12, SSIM 0.807, and LPIPS 0.235 with only 4.6 MB storage, matching or exceeding 3DGS (PSNR 27.21, 839.9 MB) while reducing storage by ~180×. On Deep Blending, Mobile-GS surpasses 3DGS in PSNR (29.93 vs. 29.41) and SSIM (0.906 vs. 0.903) at 4.6 MB vs. 697.3 MB. On the Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 mobile GPU, Mobile-GS reaches 127 FPS (cold-start) and 74 FPS (steady-state) with 4.6 MB storage, compared to 8 FPS for quantized 3DGS and 24 FPS for SortFreeGS*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mobile-GS outperforms all lightweight baselines on storage (4.6 MB vs. 8.5 MB for LocoGS-S, 30.6 MB for C3DGS, 79.4 MB for Speedy-Splat) while matching or exceeding their rendering quality. On Mip-NeRF 360, Mobile-GS achieves the highest PSNR (27.12) among all lightweight methods, beating LocoGS-S (27.02) and C3DGS (27.03), and is within 0.09 dB of full 3DGS (27.21). On Deep Blending, Mobile-GS surpasses even 3DGS in PSNR (29.93 vs. 29.41) and SSIM (0.906 vs. 0.903). On Tanks & Temples, Mobile-GS is slightly behind LocoGS-S in PSNR (23.09 vs. 23.23) and C3DGS (23.32), but achieves the lowest storage (2.5 MB). On the mobile GPU, Mobile-GS achieves 127 FPS, outperforming SortFreeGS* (24 FPS), Speedy-Splat (19 FPS), and 3DGS* (8 FPS) by 5–16×, while also consuming the least power (0.83 W total vs. 5.89 W for 3DGS*).

## Relation to Prior Work

Mobile-GS builds directly on the 3DGS rendering pipeline (Kerbl et al., 2023), replacing the sorting-dependent alpha blending with a depth-aware order-independent formulation inspired by SortFreeGS (Hou et al., 2025) but enriched with a neural view-dependent opacity correction. The first-order spherical harmonics distillation extends the concept from LightGaussian (Fan et al., 2024), which projected 3rd-order SH to 2nd-order, down to 1st-order using Mini-Splatting (Fang & Wang, 2024) as the teacher. The neural vector quantization with K-means sub-vector decomposition and Huffman coding, combined with contribution-based pruning, positions the work at the intersection of the Gaussian compression literature (C3DGS, LocoGS) and the sorting-free rendering line (SortFreeGS, GES), but uniquely integrates all components into a single mobile-optimized pipeline deployed via Vulkan.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that full 3DGS-quality novel view synthesis can run at 127 FPS on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 mobile GPU with only 4.6 MB storage (~180× compression over vanilla 3DGS), opening the door to 3DGS on smartphones and AR headsets. Key limitations remain: the method is per-scene (no generalization to unseen environments), training still requires a desktop GPU for 1.5 hours, and aggressive quantization can introduce minor color shifts in highly textured regions. Open questions include whether the order-independent + neural-correction paradigm can extend to dynamic or multi-scene settings, whether on-device retraining is feasible, and whether the training cost can be reduced to enable real-time capture-and-render workflows.
