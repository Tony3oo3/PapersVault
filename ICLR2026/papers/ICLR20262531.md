## Problem

Compute for language model pre-training grows ~4× per year while web text grows only ~1.03× per year, making data the binding constraint. Existing data-constrained recipes that increase epoch count or parameter count overfit, bounding achievable performance. The paper asks how to design pre-training recipes that monotonically improve under fixed data with unlimited compute, and proposes evaluating recipes by their loss asymptotes rather than at a fixed compute budget.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262531/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed seed corpus of DCLM web text (default 200M tokens, scaled up to 1.6B) and Llama-style autoregressive transformer configurations (150M–1.4B parameters, 4096 context length). It produces pre-trained models evaluated on a held-out i.i.d. validation set and downstream benchmarks (PIQA, SciQ, ARC Easy). Unusually, the output is characterized by the asymptotic loss as parameter count N→∞ or ensemble size K→∞ rather than a single model at a fixed compute budget, and the best recipe outputs either a logit-averaged ensemble or a distilled single student model of 300M parameters.

## Method

The paper identifies three composable interventions for data-constrained pre-training. First, regularized parameter scaling: jointly tuning weight decay (up to 30× the standard 0.1), learning rate, and epoch count at each parameter count via coordinate descent yields monotone power-law loss scaling in N (exponent ≈1.02) with asymptote 3.43, versus overfitting in the unregularized recipe. Second, ensemble scaling: independently training K models (varying only data order and initialization) and averaging their logits achieves a lower asymptote (3.34) than parameter scaling alone, with the optimal per-member hyperparameters being 2× epochs and 0.5× weight decay relative to the single-model optimum. Third, joint scaling composing both limits (N,K→∞) reaches an asymptote of 3.17, equivalent to 5.17× more data efficiency than the standard recipe. Distillation compresses these gains: an 8-ensemble of 300M models distilled into a single 300M student retains 83% of the ensemble loss improvement, and self-distillation (300M teacher → 300M student) outperforms the teacher without ever training a larger model.

## Results

At 200M tokens, the regularized parameter scaling recipe achieves a loss asymptote of 3.43 (vs. 3.75 for the unregularized standard recipe), and the joint scaling recipe (scaling both parameter count N and ensemble size K to infinity) achieves a lower asymptote of 3.17. The joint recipe is 5.17× more data-efficient than the standard recipe at 200M tokens, and data scaling laws predict this advantage persists at higher token counts. Distilling an 8-ensemble of 300M models into a single 300M student retains 83% of the ensembling loss improvement (loss 3.36 vs. 3.32 for the ensemble), and the best ensemble outperforms the best unregularized model by 9% on average across PIQA, SciQ, and ARC Easy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the standard data-constrained recipe of increasing epoch count and parameter count with default weight decay (0.1), following Muennighoff et al. (2023) and Kaplan et al. (2020). The standard recipe overfits: loss increases with both epoch count and parameter count, with the 1.4B model performing worse than the 600M model. The regularized recipe (tuning weight decay up to 3.2, 30× the standard 0.1) fixes this and achieves monotone power-law scaling in N, reaching an asymptote of 3.43 versus the standard recipe's ~3.75. The ensembling recipe further improves the asymptote to 3.34, and the joint recipe to 3.17, representing a 5.17× data efficiency gain over the standard recipe at 200M tokens. In the continued pre-training setting (Appendix A), the paper's best 8-ensemble using only 4B tokens of MegaMath-Web-Pro data (average accuracy 40.58%) outperforms Wang et al.'s (2025) default CPT on the full 73B tokens (39.23%), a 17.5× data efficiency improvement on GSM8K, MATH, and MathQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-constrained pre-training line of work (Muennighoff et al., 2023; Gadre et al., 2024) by lifting compute constraints entirely and proposing asymptote-based evaluation as a new metric for comparing scaling recipes. It departs from Chinchilla-style compute-optimal scaling (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022) by inverting the problem: fixed data, unbounded compute. The regularization finding is grounded in double-descent theory (Nakkiran et al., 2021; Advani and Ganguli, 2016), while the ensembling and distillation components repurpose classical techniques (Dietterich, 2000; Hinton et al., 2015; Kim and Rush, 2016) in a pre-training scaling context where they had not been systematically studied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With compute growing ~4×/year versus web text at ~1.03×/year, the data-constrained regime is imminent, and this paper provides a concrete, actionable recipe (30× weight decay, ensembling, distillation) achieving 5.17× data efficiency. The asymptote-based evaluation framework offers a principled way to compare recipes when compute is not the binding constraint, which is a useful methodological contribution beyond the specific interventions. Open questions include whether findings generalize to much larger token budgets (extrapolation is based on 4-point power-law fits), whether the non-standard 1.4B architecture (trading depth for width) affects conclusions, and whether alternatives like MoE or dropout could match or exceed ensembling at scale.
