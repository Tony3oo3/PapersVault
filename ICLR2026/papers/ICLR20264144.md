## Problem

Deploying large neural networks in resource-constrained or dynamic environments requires trading off performance against inference cost on-the-fly, but existing approaches force a choice between static compression (which must be repeated for each new budget) and dynamic architectures like slimmable networks (which require specialized from-scratch training and are difficult to retrofit onto pre-trained foundation models). No prior method provides a single, post-hoc-applicable mechanism to smoothly adjust a pre-trained model across a continuous spectrum of compute budgets at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method accepts the same inputs as the underlying model (e.g., premise-hypothesis text pairs for NLI, or 512-dim ResNet-18 features for CIFAR-10) plus a scalar rank parameter r ∈ {1,…,R} that selects the operating point. At inference, the model produces standard outputs (class logits, predictions) computed using only the first r rank-1 components of each factorized layer, yielding a FLOP reduction proportional to r without changing input/output dimensions or normalization layers. The rank can be chosen per-request, enabling per-sample compute allocation.

## Method

NSNs re-parameterize every linear layer W ∈ R^{d_out × d_in} as a shared pair of factor matrices A ∈ R^{R×d_in} and B ∈ R^{d_out×R}, where the effective weight at rank r is W_r = B_r A_r (first r rows of A, first r columns of B), guaranteeing the nested subspace property Im(W_r) ⊆ Im(W_{r+1}). Training uses a multi-rank uncertainty-weighted objective: at each step, the model is evaluated at a fixed anchor rank R̃ and a randomly sampled variant rank r, and their cross-entropies are combined with learnable log-variance weights (exp(−s_k)·L_CE(k) + s_k) that automatically balance the intrinsic difficulty of each rank. For post-hoc application to pre-trained LLMs, the factor matrices are initialized via SVD of the original weights, preserving learned information before a short fine-tuning phase. Under a mild energy-decay assumption on the learned basis vectors, the paper proves a bound on performance change between any two ranks, ensuring smooth interpolation even at untrained intermediate ranks.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (MLP classifier on frozen ResNet-18 features), the proposed Two CEs uncertainty-weighted objective achieves 88% highest-rank test accuracy, 79% average in-distribution sub-rank accuracy, and 81% average out-of-distribution interpolated-rank accuracy, compared to 48% and 57% respectively for the CE-only anchor baseline. On pre-trained LLMs (Pythia-2.8B, GPT-Neo 2.7B, Gemma-2B, Qwen2-0.5B), NSNs unlock a smooth compute-performance frontier: a 50% reduction in inference FLOPs costs only ~5 percentage points of accuracy on the NLI task, with monotonic degradation as rank decreases. This behavior is consistent across all four tested language models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against external dynamic-inference methods on a shared quantitative protocol; instead, it compares against internal baselines and positions itself qualitatively. Against the CE-only anchor baseline, the Two CEs joint objective improves average in-distribution sub-rank accuracy from 48% to 79% and out-of-distribution interpolated accuracy from 57% to 81% (Table 1), while maintaining the same 88% highest-rank accuracy. Against naive rank truncation (train at rank 64, truncate at test time), the joint uncertainty-weighted training closes a large performance gap at lower ranks (Figure 2). Qualitatively, Table 3 shows NSNs are the first method to simultaneously satisfy all five desiderata criteria (trade-off parametrization, test-time adaptability, post-training re-parameterization, architectural agnosticism, and smooth trade-off frontier), whereas LoRA, DyLoRA, Once-for-All, and slimmable networks each fail on at least two. The LLM experiments (Figure 7) demonstrate that SVD-initialized NSN fine-tuning achieves a smooth, monotonic accuracy-rank curve across Pythia-2.8B, GPT-Neo 2.7B, Gemma-2B, and Qwen2-0.5B, with the 50%-FLOPs-reduction / 5pp-accuracy-drop operating point as the headline result.

## Relation to Prior Work

NSNs extend the low-rank factorization of LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) from a static, fixed-rank adapter into a continuous, nested hierarchy where the rank-r weight is a strict subspace of rank-(r+1), enabling dynamic inference-time adjustment. The training objective adapts Kendall et al.'s (2018) uncertainty-weighted multi-task loss to balance ranks of differing intrinsic difficulty, replacing the specialized from-scratch training schemes required by slimmable networks (Yu et al., 2018) and dynamic architectures like Once-for-All or MatFormer. The key conceptual departure from prior dynamic-inference work is that NSNs vary only the rank of a shared factorization (keeping all tensor shapes and normalization layers fixed), making them surgically insertable into pre-trained transformers via SVD initialization rather than requiring architectural redesign or supernet training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NSNs offer the first practical recipe for converting any pre-trained foundation model into a compute-adjustable one with minimal fine-tuning, which is directly relevant to deploying LLMs in resource-constrained or latency-sensitive settings. The theoretical interpolation bound (Proposition 1) and the empirical 50% FLOPs reduction with only 5pp accuracy loss on Pythia-2.8B are encouraging, but the LLM experiments are limited to models ≤2.8B and a single NLI task, leaving open whether the smooth frontier persists at 7B+ scale or on more complex generation tasks. A significant open direction is layer-specific rank allocation (currently all layers share the same rank), which would require correlating problem difficulty with per-layer representational capacity—a problem the authors acknowledge has attracted little prior attention.
