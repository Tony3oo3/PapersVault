## Problem

Test-time entropy minimization is prone to collapse: the model can trivially minimize entropy by inflating logit norms and driving all predictions to a dominant class, producing constant one-hot outputs that degrade performance. Existing stabilization methods rely on heuristic gradient-filtering thresholds that are hard to generalize and still leave collapsed solutions as valid optima. The paper asks how to design an efficient, architecturally grounded entropy optimization mechanism that inherently rules out these trivial minima without augmentations, extra backbone passes, or teacher models.

## Input / Output

For vision adaptation, the method consumes corrupted RGB images from ImageNet-C (15 corruption types × 5 severity levels) in online streams—potentially non-i.i.d., imbalanced, or batch-size-1—and outputs class labels, updating only normalization-layer affine parameters per a single forward pass. For LLM reasoning, it takes mathematical problem statements (Math-500, CollegeMath, AIME24, Minerva) and produces reasoning answers, updating LoRA parameters (rank 8) on the first 8 output tokens. In both settings, no ground-truth labels are used; the model's own predicted entropy serves as the self-supervised signal.

## Method

ZeroSiam embeds asymmetry within a single forward pass by inserting a lightweight linear predictor h before the shared classifier g, decoupling one encoder feature z into two asymmetric branches: an online branch (z → h(z) → g → p^o) where entropy is minimized, and a target branch (z → g → p^r) with stop-gradient. The combined objective L = H(p^o) + α·D(p^o ‖ sg[p^r]) uses a divergence (symmetric KL by default, α=1) to align the online output toward the target, converting any collapsed constant solution into a non-zero alignment penalty that the predictor must absorb. The predictor is initialized as the identity (warm start) and quickly diverges during online learning, creating the asymmetry needed to break trivial minima. Theoretically, the predictor acts as a filter that suppresses gradient directions corresponding to over-amplified logits, guaranteeing convergence to a stable equilibrium where H(p^o) > h_min > 0. Empirically, the predictor absorbs biased shortcut signals (e.g., logit-norm inflation, center dominance) that would otherwise cause collapse, improving generalization even in scenarios where no full collapse occurs.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (level 5) under online label shifts (imbalance ratio = ∞), ZeroSiam achieves 52.9% average accuracy across five architectures (ResNet50-GN, ViT-Base, ViT-Small, ConvNeXt-Tiny, Swin-Tiny), outperforming the best prior method DeYO (43.1%) by +9.8% and Tent (30.9%) by +22.0%. Under the blind-spot stress test (adapting only on initially misclassified samples), ZeroSiam reaches 52.0% average accuracy versus 29.0% for NoAdapt and ~33.6% for the best prior method, drastically breaking the prior performance ceiling. For LLM reasoning (Llama3.1-8B-Instruct), ZeroSiam achieves 28.56% average accuracy across Math-500, CollegeMath, AIME24, and Minerva, a +3.94% gain over the base model and +2.40% over the best prior method (COME, 26.16%). ZeroSiam matches Tent's inference speed (193s for 50K images on ViT-Base) while prior methods like SAR (382s) and DeYO (280s) are significantly slower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroSiam consistently outperforms all six baselines across every evaluation scenario. Against Tent (Wang et al., 2021), the gap is most dramatic: ZeroSiam achieves 52.9% vs. 30.9% average accuracy under label shifts (Table 3), and 52.0% vs. 21.7% under the blind-spot stress test (Table 6), where Tent collapses to near-zero accuracy on several models. Against SAR (Niu et al., 2023), ZeroSiam gains +14.1% on label shifts and +18.2% on blind-spot adaptation; SAR also fails to improve over NoAdapt on Swin-Tiny under mixed corruption (Table 2). Against EATA (Niu et al., 2022), ZeroSiam leads by +12.2% on label shifts and +18.5% on blind-spot; EATA's performance degrades sharply under batch size = 1 (32.9%→25.3% on Swin-Tiny blur). Against COME (Zhang et al., 2025a), ZeroSiam gains +10.4% on label shifts and +18.4% on blind-spot; COME is particularly unstable on ViT-Small (0.2% on noise under label shifts). Against DeYO (Lee et al., 2024), ZeroSiam leads by +9.8% on label shifts and +22.2% on blind-spot; DeYO deteriorates below NoAdapt on 12 of 20 blind-spot scenarios. Against TLM (Hu et al., 2025a) on LLM reasoning, ZeroSiam achieves 28.56% vs. 25.44% average, with the largest gain on AIME24 (+10.00% vs. +3.34% for TLM). ZeroSiam also integrates additively with prior methods (Table 9), improving EATA by +8.4% and DeYO by +7.4% average accuracy, confirming its role as a plug-and-play stabilizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroSiam repurposes the asymmetric predictor–stop-gradient mechanism from SimSiam (Chen & He, 2021), which was originally designed to prevent representation collapse in negative-free self-supervised learning with augmentations and dual backbone passes. The key departure is embedding this asymmetry within a single forward pass for test-time entropy minimization (Tent, Wang et al., 2021), using only a lightweight linear predictor inserted before the classifier—no augmentations, no extra encoder passes, no teacher model. This contrasts with prior TTA stability methods (SAR, EATA, DeYO, COME) that rely on heuristic entropy thresholds or sample filtering, which the paper argues cannot fundamentally eliminate collapsed constant solutions as valid optima of the entropy objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZeroSiam provides a principled, architecture-level guarantee against collapse in test-time entropy minimization rather than a heuristic patch, and does so at zero additional latency (193s vs. 193s for Tent on 50K images). The broader finding that the predictor absorbs biased shortcut signals even when no collapse occurs suggests the asymmetric alignment acts as a general regularizer for unsupervised self-training, potentially applicable beyond entropy minimization. Open questions include how to best combine ZeroSiam with sample-selection strategies (the paper notes integration with EATA/DeYO does not always help), whether the approach extends to other unsupervised objectives, and how to set the predictor learning rate in a fully data-free manner for deployment.
