## Problem

Current MLLMs generate outputs in a single direct pass, lacking intrinsic mechanisms for self-correction and iterative refinement, making their capacity for long-chain reflective reasoning in multimodal contexts largely unexplored and underevaluated. Existing benchmarks (Enigmata, VGRP-Bench, Code2Logic) focus on text-only problems or constrained multiple-choice formats, failing to assess end-to-end multimodal reflective reasoning. This paper addresses both the evaluation gap and the training gap: how to measure this capability and how to effectively teach it to MLLMs.

## Input / Output

Each task instance pairs a textual prompt (task description, game rules, coordinate system, and output format specification) with a rendered visual problem state (e.g., a game board, graph, or grid image). The model must produce an extended chain-of-thought trace that includes explicit reflection and backtracking steps, followed by a structured final answer whose format varies by task type: a sequence of directional moves (Snake, Sokoban), coordinate-value pairs (Kakuro, Minesweeper), a completed grid (Sudoku, Numbrix), or a path/cycle as a vertex list (Eulerian, Hamiltonian). Text-only variants replace the image with symbolic representations (adjacency lists for graphs, 2D arrays for grids, list serialization for algorithms) to isolate the reasoning component from perception.

## Method

The paper introduces three components. First, MM-HELIX is a benchmark of 1,260 samples across 42 tasks in four categories (Algorithm, Graph, Puzzle, Game), generated by a procedural pipeline with a rule-based Instance Generator, a deterministic Solver, and an automated Verifier that also serves as the RL reward oracle; difficulty is controlled by scaling task-specific parameters across five levels. Second, the Step-Elicited Response Generation (SERG) pipeline constructs MM-HELIX-100K (100k reflective CoT traces) by first building a rule-based skeletal reasoning path with embedded anchor states, then prompting Qwen3-235B to refine it into natural, reflective prose, with final answers validated by the automated Verifier. Third, Adaptive Hybrid Policy Optimization (AHPO) unifies off-policy and on-policy learning in a single stage: the total loss combines a negative log-likelihood term on expert CoT data (weighted by an adaptive coefficient ξ) with a GRPO-style clipped policy gradient on on-policy rollouts. The coefficient ξ is a binary gate that activates off-policy supervision only when the fraction of successful on-policy trajectories in a group falls below a predefined threshold R̂, providing dense expert guidance during early training when rewards are sparse and fading out once the model achieves consistent success, thereby preventing distributional collapse onto the expert data while still enabling effective learning from sparse reward signals.

## Results

On the MM-HELIX benchmark, AHPO applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves 24.9% accuracy, a +18.6-point improvement over the 6.3% baseline. This 7B model outperforms significantly larger models including Qwen2.5-VL-72B (13.9%) and GLM-4.5V-106B (19.5%). On general reasoning tasks (MathVision, MathVerse-V, LogicVista, WeMath), AHPO yields a +5.7-point average gain (42.2% vs. 36.5% baseline), demonstrating transfer of reflective reasoning skills to out-of-domain mathematics and logic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AHPO decisively outperforms all four training baselines on both in-domain and general reasoning metrics. Against pure on-policy RL (GRPO), AHPO gains +15.9 points on MM-HELIX (24.9% vs. 9.0%) and +5.5 points on average general reasoning (42.2% vs. 36.7%), confirming that sparse rewards alone are insufficient for complex reflective tasks. Against SFT, AHPO matches in-domain performance (24.9% vs. 23.8%) while avoiding catastrophic forgetting: SFT drops general reasoning by 6.6 points (29.9% avg.), whereas AHPO improves it by 5.7 points. The sequential SFT+GRPO pipeline recovers some general performance (36.7%) but still trails AHPO by 5.5 points on average and 1.6 points on MM-HELIX. LUFFY, which substitutes policy rollouts with expert data in a preference-based framework, achieves only 9.1% on MM-HELIX and 35.4% average general reasoning, far below AHPO's 24.9% and 42.2%. Notably, the 7B AHPO model (24.9%) surpasses open-source models 10–30× its size, including Qwen2.5-VL-72B (13.9%), InternVL3-78B (9.9%), and GLM-4.5V-106B (19.5%), though it remains well below proprietary GPT-5 (58.1%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO/DAPO line of on-policy RL for LLMs by introducing an adaptive off-policy supervision mechanism (AHPO) that dynamically gates expert-data loss based on real-time reward density, addressing the sparse-reward failure mode that renders pure on-policy methods ineffective on complex tasks. It departs from prior multimodal reasoning benchmarks (Enigmata, VGRP-Bench, Code2Logic) which are constrained to text-only inputs or multiple-choice/fill-in-the-blank formats, by constructing an end-to-end, procedurally generated benchmark requiring long-chain visual reasoning with iterative backtracking. The SERG data pipeline builds on the rule-based CoT construction idea but adds an LLM refinement stage, positioning itself between rigid programmatic traces and unconstrained model rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that long-chain reflective reasoning is a distinct, measurable capability in MLLMs that current models (even GPT-5 at 58.1%) handle poorly, and that this capability is learnable and transferable to general math/logic tasks via the proposed AHPO training. A key practical finding is that SFT on reflective traces achieves comparable in-domain accuracy to AHPO but causes catastrophic forgetting on out-of-domain tasks, whereas AHPO's adaptive gating avoids this trade-off. Limitations include evaluation on a single 7B base model, a fully synthetic benchmark whose ecological validity for real-world reflective reasoning is unclear, and a modest +5.7% generalization gain that may not scale to larger models or more diverse task distributions.
