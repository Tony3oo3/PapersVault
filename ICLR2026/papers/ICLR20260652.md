## Problem

Existing MD Emulators learn a fixed-lag transition density, causing the training signal to be dominated by frequent but uninformative intra-state transitions while rare, high-barrier inter-state transitions (e.g., folding/unfolding) remain underrepresented. This data imbalance limits their ability to capture large conformational changes and generalize across proteins with significant structural diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260652/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, MARS-FM takes a protein sequence and a single input 3D conformation from an unseen protein (no MSM information is provided) and generates a set of protein conformations in SE(3) roto-translational space (unit quaternions, translations, and 7 torsion angles per residue). Conformations can be produced in parallel via hierarchical tree sampling or sequentially via a hybrid scheme with an MD-Emu, yielding 100–10,000 structures per protein domain (up to 500 residues) at a fraction of MD cost (~600× speedup over implicit-solvent MD).

## Method

MARS-FM introduces a new class of generative models (MSM-Emus) that learn to sample from the Markov-chain transition distribution induced by a per-domain Markov State Model, rather than from a fixed-lag frame-to-frame transition density. During training, the model first selects a source state uniformly, then draws a destination state from the MSM transition matrix T, and finally samples a target frame from that state's empirical ensemble; a Flow Matching objective then trains a DiT-style velocity network (with Invariant Point Attention layers) to transport Gaussian noise to this state-conditioned mixture. This state-based sampling ensures rare metastable states are encountered with equal frequency, decoupling the learning objective from temporal ordering and enabling parallel generation. At inference, two sampling strategies are offered: hierarchical tree sampling (MARS-FM alone) for efficient exploration, and a hybrid MARS-FM ⊕ MDGen scheme that first jumps across states then refines local dynamics, combining long-timescale thermodynamics with short-timescale kinetic fidelity.

## Results

On the MD-Cath dataset (495 test domains, ≤500 residues, 450 K with unfolding), MARS-FM achieves a Pairwise RMSD Pearson r of 0.65 and Global RMSF r of 0.71, closely matching the MD oracle (0.82/0.82) and substantially outperforming MDGen-100 (0.42/0.49) and BioEmu (0.25/0.41). MARS-FM matches the oracle on MSM JSD (0.19 vs 0.19) and achieves a folding free energy MAE of 1.05 kcal/mol versus 1.21 for MDGen-100 and 4.67 for BioEmu. In the low-sample regime (100 conformations), MARS-FM even surpasses the MD oracle on several metrics, and the method provides a 600× wall-clock speedup over implicit-solvent MD on an NVIDIA H100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARS-FM outperforms all MDGen variants (K=20, K=100, and their parallel counterparts) across every metric on MD-Cath. For example, on Pairwise RMSD correlation, MARS-FM reaches 0.65 versus 0.42 for MDGen-100 and 0.40 for MDGen-20; on MSM JSD, MARS-FM achieves 0.19 (matching the MD oracle) while MDGen-100 only reaches 0.29. Against BioEmu, MARS-FM wins decisively: 0.65 vs 0.25 on Pairwise RMSD r, 0.10 vs 0.40 on Gyration Radius JSD, and 1.05 vs 4.67 kcal/mol on folding free energy MAE. The hybrid MARS-FM ⊕ MDGen-20 variant is slightly behind pure MARS-FM on most metrics but recovers near-MD torsion relaxation times (Appendix C.2), making it useful when temporal fidelity matters. Notably, MARS-FM's advantage over MDGen is not attributable to reduced autoregressive calls, as the parallel MDGen baselines (which eliminate autoregression entirely) still underperform MARS-FM by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARS-FM builds directly on the MDGen architecture (Jing et al., 2024b) and its Flow Matching training objective (Lipman et al., 2023), but departs conceptually from the entire MD-Emu class by replacing fixed-lag frame-to-frame transition learning with state-to-state transitions defined by a Markov State Model. This coarse-graining via MSMs (Noé et al., 2009; Prinz et al., 2011) decouples the generative objective from temporal dynamics, so the model learns to interpolate across metastable states rather than replicate specific trajectory segments. The paper positions itself as complementary to MD-Emus (which capture fine-grained intra-state kinetics) and distinct from Boltzmann Generators and sequence-to-structure flows like BioEmu, which still suffer from the same data-imbalance issues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 600× speedup over implicit-solvent MD and the ability to match oracle-level MSM reconstruction on proteins with <20% sequence similarity to training data make this a practically significant advance for drug discovery and protein dynamics studies. Key open questions include extending MSM-Emus to protein-ligand complexes and inorganic systems, removing the requirement for an input 3D structure (e.g., via sequence-to-structure models), and combining MARS-FM with short MD simulations for hybrid workflows. A fundamental limitation is the scarcity of long, unbiased MD data for large proteins, which currently forces evaluation on high-temperature replicas to observe unfolding.
