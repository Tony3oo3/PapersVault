## Problem

Standard image description metrics (CIDEr, SPICE) were designed for short captions and fail to capture attribute/relation attachment errors or localize mistakes to specific text spans in long, detailed descriptions. Additionally, existing benchmarks lack granular human judgments of model generations on visually complex imagery, making it impossible to validate fine-grained evaluation metrics. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a structured, interpretable metric and introducing a benchmark with expert-annotated artwork descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264280/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

POSH takes two text descriptions — a model-generated description and its expert-written reference — and requires no image input for scoring. It produces granular scores (1–5) for every scene graph component (object, attribute, relation) localized to specific text spans, identifying whether each element is present or absent in the counterpart text. These granular scores are then averaged into three coarse scores: mistakes (precision), omissions (recall), and overall quality. The metric is reference-based and text-only, distinguishing it from image-conditioned baselines like CLIPScore or LLaVA-Critic.

## Method

POSH operates in three steps. First, it extracts sentence-level scene graphs from both the generation and reference using spaCy dependency parsing and Maverick coreference resolution, preserving object-attribute and object-relation attachments that prior metrics like SPICE and CAPTURE discard. Second, it uses each scene graph as a structured rubric: for every component, it generates templated QA questions (e.g., 'Is the tall man described as wearing a hat?') and prompts an open-weight LLM (Qwen3-14B) to score presence on a 1–5 scale, using unique identifiers (e.g., 'woman in white') tested in three passes to disambiguate repeated entity classes. Third, it aggregates granular scores by averaging to produce coarse mistake and omission scores, ensuring interpretability since every coarse score is grounded in localized text-span errors. The QA-based verification avoids forcing a rigid alignment between the two scene graphs, so paraphrases and collectives (e.g., 'trio' vs. three individually named figures) are handled naturally.

## Results

On DOCENT, PoSH (14B) achieves the best Spearman ρ among all replicable metrics across all three dimensions: 0.519 for mistakes, 0.581 for omissions, and 0.599 for overall quality, outperforming the next best replicable metric (LLaVA-Critic, 72B) by +0.11, +0.07, and +0.05 respectively. PoSH also surpasses GPT4o (reference-based with image) on all three DOCENT dimensions (ρ = 0.519 vs. 0.484 for mistakes, 0.581 vs. 0.380 for omissions, 0.599 vs. 0.510 for overall). On CapArena, PoSH achieves 59.2% caption-level accuracy and model-ranking ρ = 0.931, second only to LLaVA-Critic (64.0%, ρ = 0.987), but PoSH outperforms LLaVA-Critic on the visually complex subset with 3+ people (ρ = 0.727 vs. 0.686). For granular evaluation, PoSH achieves the highest F1 for both mistake localization (0.580) and omission localization (0.680) on DOCENT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoSH outperforms all n-gram and embedding-based baselines (BLEU-4, CIDEr, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CLIPScore) by large margins on DOCENT, where these metrics show near-zero or negative correlations with human judgments. Among scene-graph-based metrics, PoSH surpasses SPICE (ρ = 0.519 vs. 0.308 for mistakes) and CAPTURE (ρ = 0.581 vs. 0.447 for omissions) by preserving object attachments that those methods discard. Against LLM/VLM-as-a-Judge baselines, PoSH (14B) beats Qwen3-32B on all three DOCENT dimensions and outperforms GPT4o (reference-based with image) on mistakes, omissions, and overall quality. The sole exception is LLaVA-Critic (72B), which edges out PoSH on CapArena overall (ρ = 0.987 vs. 0.931), but PoSH reverses this on the visually complex 3+ people subset (ρ = 0.727 vs. 0.686). DCScore achieves the best mistake prediction (ρ = 0.541) but underperforms PoSH on omissions (0.395 vs. 0.581) and overall quality (0.471 vs. 0.599) due to incomplete reference coverage. DSG (Davidsonian Scene Graph) performs near chance on DOCENT (ρ ≈ 0.09 for mistakes), confirming its inapplicability to reference-based detailed description evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoSH synthesizes two previously separate evaluation paradigms: scene-graph-based metrics (SPICE, CAPTURE) that reduce descriptions to structured visual components, and LLMs-as-a-Judge (Zheng et al., 2023; LLaVA-Critic) that flexibly compare surface realizations. The key departure is using scene graphs not as the final scoring mechanism (as in SPICE) or as atomic propositions to verify against images (as in Davidsonian Scene Graph), but as *structured rubrics* that guide an open-weight LLM to perform localized, span-level QA verification between two texts. This QA-based verification draws on QuestEval's summarization evaluation paradigm, repurposed for text-to-text detailed description comparison with unique-identifier disambiguation for entity collisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: detailed image description lacks a metric that is simultaneously interpretable (granular, span-localized errors), replicable (fully open-weight), and calibrated to human judgments. DOCENT is the first benchmark combining granular and coarse human judgments of long descriptions of visually complex artwork, making it a valuable resource for the accessibility community. The finding that even GPT4o covers only ~50% of reference detail in DOCENT underscores that full, error-free description of rich scenes remains unsolved. Open questions include whether PoSH's text-only scene graph approach transfers to structurally-guided generation models, and whether the QA verification step can be further improved to close the remaining gap with human raters.
