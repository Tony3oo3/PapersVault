## Problem

Thinking-capable LLMs allocate a fixed or uniform reasoning budget to all queries, wasting tokens on simple problems while potentially under-allocating on hard ones. Existing adaptive approaches rely on superficial proxies like token-level entropy that fail to capture intrinsic reasoning difficulty, or require costly online sampling. The paper asks whether a model's internal representations can predict, before any reasoning tokens are generated, how much extended thinking a given query actually requires.

## Input / Output

At inference, Sonata consumes the last token's hidden vector from the final transformer layer, already computed during the standard prefilling pass, so no extra LLM forward pass is needed. The 2-layer MLP (64 hidden units, ~262K parameters for Qwen3-8B) outputs a scalar self-consistency prediction in [0,1], which is compared against a fixed threshold τ₀=0.3 to produce a binary decision: skip thinking entirely or engage the model's default chain-of-thought process. The final output is the model's answer, either direct or preceded by intermediate reasoning tokens.

## Method

Sonata's key insight is that self-consistency—the fraction of N sampled non-thinking answers that match the ground truth—serves as a principled proxy for thinking necessity, and that queries with different self-consistency levels form well-separated clusters in the final-layer latent space (validated via PCA). A lightweight 2-layer MLP adapter is trained offline with MSE loss on a calibration set (1000 problems from OpenMathReasoning, N=32 samples per query) to map last-layer hidden states to self-consistency scores. At test time, the adapter performs a single forward pass on the prefilling hidden state, introducing <1% computational overhead, and its prediction gates whether the model enters thinking mode. The adapter is model-specific (retrained per architecture) but task-agnostic, generalizing from math calibration to GPQA and LiveCodeBench without retraining, and composes orthogonally with existing CoT compression methods like REFRAIN for additional token savings.

## Results

Across four thinking-capable models (Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-32B, GPT-OSS-120B, Qwen3-235B-A22B) and five benchmarks (AIME25, MATH-500, GSM8K, LiveCodeBench, GPQA), Sonata achieves 20–60% reduction in average thinking tokens while maintaining or improving accuracy. On Qwen3-8B, Sonata reaches 75.3% average accuracy with 7,535 tokens (82% of vanilla's 9,154), a 1.4% accuracy gain over the vanilla model. On Qwen3-235B-A22B, Sonata achieves 84.0% average accuracy with 5,698 tokens (83% of vanilla), a 1.2% accuracy improvement. The largest token savings appear on simpler tasks: GSM8K sees 55–56% token reduction for smaller models while preserving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sonata consistently outperforms the Constant Budget baseline (Yang et al., 2025a) across all models and benchmarks: on Qwen3-8B AIME25, Sonata achieves 63.3% accuracy vs. 30.0% for constant budget, while using 97% of vanilla tokens rather than a fixed 4,096. Against the Self-Judge baseline, Sonata wins on average accuracy for Qwen3-8B (75.3% vs. 72.7%) and Qwen3-235B-A22B (84.0% vs. 83.2%) while using fewer tokens (82% vs. 91% and 83% vs. 93% of vanilla, respectively); Self-Judge occasionally matches Sonata on individual benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K for Qwen3-32B) but is less consistent overall. In the proxy-metric ablation (Table 3), Sonata's self-consistency signal dramatically outperforms both LM Logits Entropy (Fu et al., 2025) and Attention Entropy (Li et al., 2025b): on AIME25 with Qwen3-8B, Sonata reaches 63.3% accuracy while LM Logits Entropy drops to 23.3% and Attention Entropy to 30.0%, revealing that single-token-level uncertainty metrics fail to capture true reasoning difficulty. Sonata also generalizes beyond its math-only calibration set, improving GPQA accuracy by 1.9% (Qwen3-8B) while cutting tokens by 52%, a gain neither baseline achieves on out-of-domain tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023) from a majority-vote decoding strategy into a principled proxy for thinking necessity, then leverages the emerging literature on latent reasoning in LLMs (Yang et al., 2025b; Shalev et al., 2024b) to show that self-consistency patterns are linearly separable in last-layer hidden states. It departs from prior adaptive-thinking approaches that rely on surface-level entropy proxies (Xia et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025a) or costly online sampling (Han et al., 2025) by training a 2-layer MLP adapter offline to predict self-consistency from prefilling-stage hidden representations, introducing <1% inference overhead. The method is positioned as complementary to existing CoT compression techniques (REFRAIN, ThinkPrune, RetroSearch), serving as an outer 'when to think' controller that composes with inner 'how long to think' methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20–60% thinking-token reduction while maintaining or improving accuracy is a practically significant efficiency gain for deploying thinking LLMs at scale, and the task-agnostic generalization (trained on math, transferred to GPQA and code) makes it broadly deployable. Key limitations include the binary think/don't-think decision (fine-grained 4-level control underperformed), model-specific adapter training requiring ~1000 calibration queries with N=32 samples each, and the absence of dynamic mid-reasoning budget adjustment. Open questions include whether the latent-space separability finding holds for non-thinking LLMs, whether the adapter can be distilled into the base model, and how to extend to continuous budget allocation without the brittleness observed in the 4-level ablation.
