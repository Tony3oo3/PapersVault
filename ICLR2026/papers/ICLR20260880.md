## Problem

LLM pre-training data faces a growing legal crisis: major datasets (Books3, LAION, MATH) have been taken down via DMCA, 45% of C4 is now restricted by Terms of Service, and web scraping for AI training is increasingly blocked. No existing corpus simultaneously provides multilingual coverage, multi-domain diversity beyond web crawls, and fully open licensing at the scale needed for pre-training. This paper addresses that gap by assembling and releasing the largest fully open pre-training dataset (~2 trillion tokens) with verified permissive licensing across 50+ languages.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests highly heterogeneous raw sources: OCR'd PDFs from national libraries (Gallica, Library of Congress, Delpher), government financial and legal documents (SEC filings, EUR-lex, USPTO), scientific papers retrieved via OpenAlex API, permissively licensed code from Stack v1/v2 (600+ languages), rendered HTML from Wikimedia Enterprise dumps, 2M YouTube CC-BY transcripts, and Wikidata RDF triples converted into natural-language sequences across 300 languages. Each output document carries structured metadata (license, language, collection, creation date, source URL, word/token counts) enabling fine-grained filtering by domain, time period, or rights status. Beyond the dataset itself, the authors release two Llama-architecture models (350M and 1.2B parameters) with a custom 65,536-vocabulary BPE tokenizer trained on a representative corpus subsample.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a multilingual data curation pipeline with purpose-built tools: Segmentext (DeBERTa-v2 token classifier fine-tuned on 3,500 annotated documents) for structural segmentation of broken OCR text, OCRoscope (CLD2-based 7-gram detector) and OCRerrcr (400M DeBERTa-v2) for OCR quality estimation at different scales, OCRonos (Llama 3 8B) for OCR error correction and text restoration, Celadon (140M DeBERTa-v3-small trained on 2M synthetic samples) for multilingual toxicity filtering, and Microsoft Presidio with custom regex patterns for PII replacement with fictitious but realistic values. Data is organized into six thematic collections (Open Government, Open Culture, Open Science, Open Code, Open Web, Open Semantic), with rights verification following Berne Convention (author+70) and US (publication+95) public domain rules, plus a universal 1884 cutoff for undatable works. Two Llama-based models are trained on the corpus (350M on ~1T tokens for 2,944 H100 hours; 1.2B on the full corpus plus three epochs for 23,040 H100 hours) and evaluated on MultiBLiMP, XStoryCloze, and XCOPA, where they match or exceed comparably sized models trained on non-permissively licensed data.

## Results

PleIAs 350M, trained on ~1T tokens from Common Corpus, achieves 0.774 on MultiBLiMP, outperforming larger models (XGLM 564M: 0.711, BLOOM 560M: 0.683) and the smaller Gemma3 270M (0.762). PleIAs 1.2B reaches 0.797 on MultiBLiMP, nearly matching Gemma3 1B (0.799) while substantially outperforming OLMo 1B (0.699). On XStoryCloze and XCOPA, both models trail Gemma3 by 2–7 points but remain competitive with or above XGLM and BLOOM of similar or larger size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gemma3, PleIAs 350M wins on MultiBLiMP (0.774 vs. 0.762) but trails on XStoryCloze (0.509 vs. 0.533) and XCOPA (0.533 vs. 0.544); PleIAs 1.2B nearly ties Gemma3 1B on MultiBLiMP (0.797 vs. 0.799) but falls 6–7 points behind on XStoryCloze (0.526 vs. 0.594) and XCOPA (0.541 vs. 0.593). Against XGLM, both PleIAs models dominate on MultiBLiMP (350M: 0.774 vs. 0.711; 1.2B: 0.797 vs. 0.710) but lose on the other two benchmarks. Against BLOOM 560M, PleIAs 350M achieves a large MultiBLiMP margin (0.774 vs. 0.683) but is slightly behind on XStoryCloze and XCOPA. Against OLMo 1B (also trained on a publicly released dataset), PleIAs 1.2B wins on all three benchmarks (MultiBLiMP 0.797 vs. 0.699, XStoryCloze 0.526 vs. 0.517, XCOPA 0.541 vs. 0.518), confirming that fully open-licensed data can match or exceed models trained on less transparent corpora.

## Relation to Prior Work

Common Corpus carves new ground by being the first pre-training dataset to simultaneously satisfy four criteria: multilingual coverage, multi-domain diversity, sources beyond web crawls, and fully open licensing (public domain or permissive licenses). Prior efforts each satisfy a subset: C4C and Common Pile are open but English-only or web-crawl-centric; KL3M is open and beyond web crawl but English-only; Dolma and FineWeb 2 are multilingual and multi-domain but rely on non-permissively licensed web data. The paper also repurposes existing infrastructure (OpenAlex, The Stack v2, Wikidata, Wikimedia Enterprise dumps) through a novel aggregation and curation pipeline with custom tools (Segmentext, OCRonos, Celadon) designed for the specific challenges of historical, multilingual, and OCRed content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This dataset provides a legally unambiguous path for open LLM research, directly addressing the growing legal risks (NYT v. OpenAI, Books3 DMCA takedown, GEITje removal) that have made previously reproducible research artifacts disappear. The 'open data paradox'—that major sources of open content are paradoxically underrepresented in leading pre-training corpora—highlights a structural gap that this work begins to fill but does not fully resolve, as 2T tokens suffice only for small models. The released curation tools (OCR correction, toxicity filtering, PII removal, text segmentation) are reusable infrastructure for future open-data initiatives. Open questions include whether the dataset's quality holds at the 10T+ token scale needed for frontier models, and whether the open-data paradox can be systematically overcome through the proposed combination of domain curation and LLM-assisted license annotation.
