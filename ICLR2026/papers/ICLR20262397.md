## Problem

Training transformer models with flash attention in BF16 low-precision settings can cause catastrophic loss explosion after several thousand steps, a failure reported for over two years in the community (nanoGPT, flash-attention issues) without a mechanistic explanation. While empirical patches like QK normalization and Gated Attention exist, the underlying causal chain from numerical error to training divergence remained unknown, leaving the community reliant on ad-hoc fixes rather than principled solutions.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard attention inputs (Q, K, V ∈ ℝ^{N×d}) and produces the attention output O and log-sum-exp statistics L, identical in form to standard flash attention 2. The modification is confined to the forward pass: it conditionally adjusts the row-max normalization factor m in the online softmax when a row of the score matrix S contains multiple identical maxima (detected via rowsum(r_m − S ≤ ε) > 1), ensuring all elements of P̃ = exp(S − m) are strictly less than 1. The backward pass, including the δ = rowsum(dO ∘ O) computation, remains entirely unchanged.

## Method

The paper identifies two intertwined root causes of the failure: (1) the emergence of structurally similar low-rank representations (PK)[T]ᵀX[T] across tokens and training steps, and (2) biased BF16 rounding errors in the P̃V accumulation. When multiple identical maxima exist in a row of pre-softmax scores, the corresponding P̃ elements equal exactly 1; adding two such unit-scaled negative V values in BF16 can overflow the significand, and the FP32 sticky bit forces a round-up that systematically biases O negative. This produces a positive (δ_lp − δ_hp)[T] that acts as a coefficient for the shared low-rank direction R, causing error to accumulate rather than cancel across steps, inflating spectral norms and triggering loss explosion. The proposed fix is a minimal conditional adjustment to the online softmax normalization: when repeated maxima are detected, m is set to β·r_m (β > 1) for positive maxima or 0 for negative maxima, guaranteeing max(P̃) < 1 and eliminating the biased rounding condition while preserving mathematical equivalence to standard attention via softmax shift-invariance.

## Results

The paper's primary result is qualitative: the proposed stabilized flash attention (with β=2) completely prevents the catastrophic loss explosion that occurs with classical Flash Attention 2 in BF16, across all tested configurations. Specifically, GPT-2S trained for 600K steps with AdamW, GPT-2S with Muon (lr=5×10⁻⁴, weight decay=0.01), and GPT-2M trained for 100K steps with AdamW all converge stably under the modified attention, while the unmodified FA2 exhibits sudden loss divergence after several thousand steps. The modification is mathematically equivalent to standard attention in exact arithmetic (leveraging softmax shift-invariance), so it introduces no approximation error in the ideal case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is classical Flash Attention 2 (Dao, 2024) run in BF16 mixed precision. The comparison is binary and decisive: classical FA2 suffers catastrophic loss explosion (loss diverges to infinity after ~3,000–7,000 steps depending on configuration), while the paper's stabilized FA2 maintains stable, monotonically decreasing validation loss throughout the full training run. This holds across three model/optimizer combinations (GPT-2S+AdamW, GPT-2S+Muon, GPT-2M+AdamW) and has been verified on multiple hardware platforms (NVIDIA A100, RTX 4090, Huawei Ascend 910B). The paper also notes that the same structural phenomena (similar low-rank (PK)[T]ᵀX[T] patterns and multiple maxima in attention scores) are observed in Llama-3.1-8B, suggesting the failure mode is not unique to GPT-2, though no full training comparison is provided for that model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Flash Attention 2 algorithm (Dao, 2024; Dao et al., 2022) and the mixed-precision BF16 training paradigm (Micikevicius et al., 2017; Kalamkar et al., 2019), but departs from prior stabilization work (QK normalization, QK-clip, Gated Attention) by providing the first mechanistic causal chain for a specific, long-standing failure case rather than empirical patches. It connects the attention-sink phenomenon (Xiao et al., 2023) to a concrete arithmetic failure mode in BF16 addition, and leverages the spectral-norm monitoring framework (Yang et al., 2023; Rybakov et al., 2024) as a diagnostic tool. The analytical approach—locating the error source, identifying structurally similar low-rank update directions, and tracing back to biased rounding—repurposes ideas from numerical linear algebra and floating-point arithmetic in a novel diagnostic pipeline for deep learning training instability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper resolves a two-year-old community mystery (nanoGPT Issue 303) with a concrete, minimal fix that any practitioner using BF16 flash attention can adopt, and provides a reusable analytical framework for diagnosing similar numerical instabilities. The connection between attention sinks and the specific rounding-error trigger offers a principled explanation for why existing stabilization techniques (QK normalization, Gated Attention) work—they disrupt the structural similarity in low-rank error matrices. Key limitations include the focus on a single GPT-2 failure case and BF16 precision; generalizability to FP8 training, other architectures, and larger scales remains open, as does whether other independent sources of numerical instability exist in the same pipeline.
