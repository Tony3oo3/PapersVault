## Problem

CLIP models are vulnerable to backdoor attacks that cause poisoned images to be misclassified as attacker-chosen target labels, yet existing test-time detection methods (STRIP, SCALE-UP) are designed for unimodal models and perform poorly on multi-modal CLIP. This paper identifies that backdoored CLIP exhibits 'shallow malicious matching'—poisoned images align only with the specific target text but not its semantically equivalent variants—whereas benign images show 'deep benign matching' that generalizes across the local text manifold, providing a geometric signal for detection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB test image and queries it against a backdoored CLIP model to obtain its predicted label and image feature. It additionally requires a small benign reference set for threshold calibration and access to text transformation functions (font styling, descriptive rephrasing via GPT-4, and Arabic language translation). The output is a binary decision (poisoned or benign) determined by whether the image feature's average Euclidean distance to text features sampled from a pre-computed Gaussian mixture exceeds a calibrated threshold.

## Method

The core mechanism exploits the geometric divergence between benign and poisoned images in CLIP's joint feature space. For each predicted label, the method generates text variants via three transformations, fits a PCA subspace to approximate the local text manifold, then samples along positive directions (away from the predicted text toward variants) to construct a discriminative region-of-interest that amplifies the separation. This region is modeled as a uniform mixture of L Gaussians, and detection is performed by measuring the average L2 distance from the image feature to features sampled from this distribution. Crucially, the Gaussian mixture is label-specific rather than instance-specific, enabling offline pre-computation per class and reducing online detection to a single CLIP forward pass plus distance computation (~0.009 s per image).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50, Subspace Detection achieves an average AUROC of 0.922 and F1-score of 0.913 across seven backdoor attacks, substantially outperforming the best unimodal baseline SCALE-UP (AUROC 0.577) and the best text-transformation baseline Descriptive Rephrasing (AUROC 0.686). On the more challenging ImageNet-R and ImageNet-Sketch, it maintains average AUROC of 0.858 and 0.873 respectively, while all baselines drop below 0.65. The method also generalizes to a ViT-B/32 visual encoder, achieving average AUROC of 0.934 on ImageNet-1K and 0.928 on ImageNet-Sketch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Subspace Detection dominates all baselines across every dataset and attack. Against the unimodal baselines, it outperforms SCALE-UP by 0.345 AUROC on ImageNet-1K (0.922 vs. 0.577) and by 0.446 on ImageNet-Sketch (0.873 vs. 0.427); STRIP is even weaker, with average AUROC below 0.50 on all three datasets. Among the multi-modal text-transformation baselines, Descriptive Rephrasing is the strongest single method (AUROC 0.686 on ImageNet-1K), yet Subspace Detection still exceeds it by 0.236 AUROC. Against the dedicated multi-modal detector BDetCLIP (Appendix F.5), Subspace Detection achieves higher average AUROC on all three datasets (e.g., 0.946 vs. 0.938 on ImageNet-1K; 0.858 vs. 0.783 on ImageNet-R; 0.878 vs. 0.739 on ImageNet-Sketch). The gap is most pronounced on the harder ImageNet-Sketch, where BDetCLIP's AUROC drops to 0.739 while Subspace Detection retains 0.878.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the well-known geometric property that CLIP text features reside on a low-dimensional manifold (Patashnik et al., 2021; Jeon et al., 2023) for a new purpose: test-time backdoor detection. While prior multimodal detection baselines (Descriptive Rephrasing, Font Styling, Language Translation) already exploit text transformations to measure similarity shifts, this paper elevates the idea from a single-variant comparison to a principled subspace construction via PCA and Gaussian mixture modeling, explicitly framing the detection signal as a geometric deviation from a local text manifold. The detection task itself (test-time poisoned sample identification) is well-established in the unimodal setting (STRIP, SCALE-UP, TeCo), and the paper positions its contribution as extending this to the multimodal CLIP context with a more structured geometric approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Given CLIP's widespread deployment in real-world systems, an efficient (0.009s/image) and architecture-agnostic detection method that outperforms all baselines by a substantial margin (e.g., 0.922 vs. 0.686 average AUROC on ImageNet-1K) has clear practical value. However, the adaptive attack experiments reveal a meaningful vulnerability: when the attacker has full knowledge of all three text transformations and their parameters, AUROC drops to 0.6559, suggesting the defense's security margin depends on the secrecy of the transformation set. Open questions include whether the manifold-based approach generalizes to more complex multimodal tasks (VQA, segmentation) beyond classification, and whether the reliance on a small reference set for threshold calibration introduces a practical deployment bottleneck.
