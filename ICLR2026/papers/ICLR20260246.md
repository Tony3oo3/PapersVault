## Problem

Existing lightweight training approaches for few-step generation (≤10 NFEs) are restricted to learning discrete ODE timesteps, limiting the search space to path discretizations rather than the sampling trajectory geometry itself. The only prior method that learns trajectories (Bespoke Solver) uses a discrete per-step parameterization that breaks the intrinsic link between values and their derivatives, causing unstable optimization. The paper asks whether one can learn the continuous sampling path directly, and how to parameterize it so that boundary conditions, SNR monotonicity, and differentiability are all satisfied simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained diffusion or flow model (EDM, ReFlow, FlowDCN, or Stable Diffusion v3.5) and learns only 32 Bezier control points per scheduler coefficient (α and σ), requiring roughly 15 minutes of training on a single GPU. At inference, random noise (or a text prompt for SD) is integrated along the learned Bezier-parameterized path using a standard ODE solver (UniPC, iPNDM, RK1, or RK2) with as few as 1–10 NFEs, producing images at 32×32 to 512×512, 3D point clouds, or layout arrangements. Because the scheduler is a continuous function, a single training run at NFE=10 generalizes directly to unseen NFEs (e.g., 6 or 8) without retraining.

## Method

BezierFlow reframes few-step acceleration as learning a Stochastic Interpolant scheduler (α(s), σ(s)) that reparameterizes the sampling path while preserving endpoint marginals, rather than learning discrete ODE timesteps. The scheduler is parameterized as a pair of 1D Bézier curves whose control points are learned via a cumulative-softmax mapping, which simultaneously enforces the boundary conditions (endpoints fixed at 0 and 1), strict SNR monotonicity (nondecreasing control-point order), and C∞ differentiability needed to compute the transformed velocity field in closed form. Training uses a teacher-forcing objective: a high-NFE adaptive solver (RK45) generates teacher samples, and the low-NFE student integrates along the learned Bezier path, minimizing LPIPS distance between the two outputs. Theoretically, the paper proves that the Bezier trajectory space is a strict superset of the LD3 timestep space (Theorem B.1), so the optimal Bezier scheduler can match or exceed any timestep-only solution. Empirically, this yields 2–3× FID improvements over prior timestep-learning methods at ≤10 NFEs across diffusion and flow models, with training time of only ~15 minutes versus 8 days for distillation-based approaches.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with EDM (diffusion), BézierFlow achieves FID 9.55 at NFE=4 with UniPC (vs. 12.04 for LD3, 24.83 for GITS) and FID 6.93 with iPNDM (vs. 9.97 for LD3). On CIFAR-10 with ReFlow (flow), the gains are even larger: FID 20.64 at NFE=4 with RK1 (vs. 38.95 for LD3) and 13.18 with RK2 (vs. 29.45 for LD3). On ImageNet 256×256 with FlowDCN, BézierFlow reaches FID 5.94 at NFE=6 with RK2, outperforming the base solver (10.54) and LD3 (10.17). Training requires only ~15 minutes on a single A6000 GPU, compared to 8 days for distillation-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BézierFlow consistently outperforms all timestep-learning baselines (DMN, GITS, LD3) across every dataset, solver, and NFE setting, with the largest margins at low NFEs (e.g., 18.31 FID improvement over LD3 at NFE=4/RK1 on CIFAR-10 flow). Against Bespoke Solver, which also learns sampling trajectories but uses discrete per-step parameterization, BézierFlow wins by 17.7 FID at NFE=4/RK1 on CIFAR-10; even when Bespoke is retrained with BézierFlow's global loss (Bespoke*), the Bezier parameterization still yields a 17.7 FID gap, isolating the benefit of continuous parameterization. In the training-efficiency comparison, BézierFlow (15 min) matches or beats Consistency Distillation (8 days) and 2-Rectified Flow (8 days) on FID while using ~0.13% of the training time. Against alternative scheduler parameterizations, BézierFlow outperforms both VDM (neural-network SNR, >1024 params) and Multi-Marginal SI (trigonometric basis, K=32) on CIFAR-10/ReFlow at all NFEs, e.g., FID 5.51 vs. 5.86 (MMSI) and 19.00 (VDM) at NFE=10/RK1. The only setting where BézierFlow does not clearly dominate is NFE=4 on ImageNet/FlowDCN with RK1 (FID 15.60 vs. 11.62 for LD3), though it recovers at NFE≥6.

## Relation to Prior Work

BezierFlow extends the lightweight training paradigm for few-step generation (DMN, GITS, LD3) by shifting the optimization target from discrete ODE timesteps to continuous sampling trajectories within the Stochastic Interpolant framework (Albergo et al., 2023). The training objective and teacher-forcing setup are inherited from LD3 (Tong et al., 2025), while the path reparameterization trick follows Karras et al. (2022). The key conceptual departure is the Bezier-based parameterization of SI schedulers, which replaces the discrete per-step variables of Bespoke Solver (Shaul et al., 2024) with a smooth, differentiable, and monotonic continuous function — a design choice that the paper proves yields a strictly larger trajectory space than LD3's discrete timesteps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15-minute single-GPU training cost (vs. days for distillation) makes few-step acceleration practically accessible for any pretrained diffusion or flow model, and the continuous Bezier parameterization enables generalization to unseen NFEs without retraining. A notable limitation is that BezierFlow can underperform the base solver at very low NFEs on harder datasets (e.g., ImageNet 256×256 with RK1 at NFE=4), and the combination with LD3 provides no additional gain, suggesting the scheduler learning already subsumes timestep optimization. Open questions include whether alternative basis functions could improve expressiveness with fewer control points, and whether the approach extends naturally to SDE-based or conditional generation settings at scale.
