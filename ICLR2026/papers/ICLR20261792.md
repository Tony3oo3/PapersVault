## Problem

In offline goal-conditioned RL with sparse rewards, the temporal lag between actions and their long-term consequences causes severe credit assignment failures, manifesting as high non-monotonicity in learned value functions that trap policies in sub-optimal regions. Existing reward-shaping approaches rely on local temporal distance estimators composed into semi-parametric graphs, which scale poorly with task complexity and fail on long-horizon tasks. The paper asks how the temporal information already encoded in generative world models (occupancy measures) can be explicitly extracted to produce reward functions that directly capture global, long-horizon goal-reaching structure.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of trajectories (state-action-next_state tuples) and a set of goal states. Internally, the flow-matching occupancy model takes a (state, action) pair and produces a velocity field parameterizing the conditional distribution over all future states under the dataset policy; the learned reward function then takes (state, action, goal) and outputs a scalar shaped reward equal to the negative squared Wasserstein-2 distance between the goal Dirac and the occupancy distribution. The final output is a goal-conditioned policy (state, goal → action) trained via GCIQL with the shaped reward, alongside the reward network itself which can be reused across goals.

## Method

ORS learns a generative model of the state-action-conditioned occupancy measure d^π_D(s⁺|s,a) using flow matching with a bootstrapped loss that combines a next-state prediction term and a future-state regression term (analogous to temporal-difference learning for generative models). The key theoretical insight (Proposition 1) is that the squared Wasserstein-2 distance between the goal Dirac δ_g and the occupancy distribution is monotonically increasing in shortest-path layer index, so defining r^W(s,a,g) = −W₂²(δ_g, d^π_D(s⁺|s,a)) yields a reward that directly encodes global goal-reaching geometry. Proposition 2 shows this W₂² distance is upper-bounded by the mean-squared error of the flow-matching velocity field against the target velocity toward g, enabling efficient reward computation without multi-step ODE integration. A neural network is trained to predict this velocity-field MSE as the shaped reward, and Theorem 1 proves that the greedy policy under r^W coincides with the optimal shortest-path policy, preserving policy invariance. The shaped reward is then plugged into GCIQL (expectile-regression Q-learning with DDPG+BC policy extraction) for the final offline policy training.

## Results

ORS achieves a mean success rate of 44.2% across 13 OGBench tasks, a 2.2× improvement over its sparse-reward base algorithm GCIQL (20.0%) and a 24% improvement over the next-best method Go-Fresh (35.8%). On the most challenging long-horizon task antmaze-giant-navigate, ORS reaches 56% success rate versus 30% for Go-Fresh and 39% for CRL. On real-world Tokamak plasma control, ORS achieves the best cumulative return across all 3 tasks (e.g., −22.4 for rotation control vs. −24.4 for HIQL and −28.9 for Go-Fresh), demonstrating effectiveness in complex stochastic systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORS wins on 11 of 13 OGBench tasks against Go-Fresh (Mezghani et al., 2023), the closest graph-based reward-shaping competitor, with the largest gaps on antmaze-giant-navigate (56% vs. 30%) and cube-triple-play (37% vs. 18%), though Go-Fresh edges out ORS on antmaze-large-explore (38% vs. 22%) and puzzle-4x4-play (74% vs. 70%). Against the base algorithm GCIQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021), ORS achieves a 2.2× mean improvement (44.2% vs. 20.0%), with particularly dramatic gains on manipulation tasks like cube-triple-play (37% vs. 7%). ORS substantially outperforms hierarchical HIQL (Park et al., 2023; 20.0% mean) and policy-bootstrapping SAW (Zhou & Kao, 2025; 25.8% mean), which struggle on manipulation tasks despite strong locomotion performance. SMORE (Sikchi et al., 2023) is nearly ineffective (2.8% mean), while n-step GCIQL and GCIQL-OTA (Ahn et al., 2025) perform poorly on noisy datasets. On the 3 real-world Tokamak tasks, ORS achieves the best cumulative return on all three, outperforming HIQL, SAW, SMORE, n-step GCIQL, GCIQL-OTA, and Go-Fresh, with the largest margin on rotation control (−22.4 vs. −24.4 for HIQL). CRL (Eysenbach et al., 2022) and QRL (Wang et al., 2023) are competitive on individual tasks (CRL reaches 90% on antmaze-large-navigate) but fail on most manipulation and long-horizon tasks, yielding much lower mean scores (13.7% and 6.9% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of generative world models (flow matching, temporal difference flows) and reward shaping for offline goal-conditioned RL. It departs from prior reward-shaping approaches that rely on local temporal distance classifiers (Hartikainen et al., 2019) or graph-based shortest-path search (Go-Fresh, Mezghani et al., 2023) by instead learning a full generative model of the occupancy measure and extracting global goal-reaching information via the squared Wasserstein-2 distance to the goal. The key technical departure is using the velocity field of a flow matching model to estimate this Wasserstein distance (Proposition 2), avoiding intractable integrals while preserving the optimal policy (Theorem 1). The base algorithm is GCIQL, and the method is explicitly complementary to hierarchical RL and n-step return approaches rather than a replacement for them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Credit assignment under sparse rewards remains a central bottleneck in offline GCRL, and ORS provides a principled, policy-invariant way to inject dense temporal signal without manual reward design. The 2.2× improvement over GCIQL and consistent gains over hierarchical and n-step baselines across 13 diverse tasks, plus real-world Tokamak plasma control results, demonstrate practical utility beyond simulation. However, the authors acknowledge that in highly combinatorial state spaces with very few success paths (e.g., large combination-lock puzzles), the occupancy measure's Wasserstein distance from goal is large for nearly all states, limiting the shaped reward's informativeness. Open questions include whether filtering the occupancy measure to 'useful' future states or augmenting with local rewards can address this limitation, and whether the theoretical guarantees (which assume deterministic dynamics and layer-monotone dataset policies) extend to the stochastic, sub-optimal settings where ORS is empirically evaluated.
