## Problem

Estimating the L1 calibration error of a binary classifier from finite data is impossible without structural assumptions on the calibration function, yet existing approaches either rely on unreliable bucketing heuristics, asymptotic hypothesis tests that only detect deviation from perfect calibration, or unjustified smoothness assumptions. This paper provides the first non-asymptotic, distribution-free upper bounds on calibration error under two principled structural conditions: bounded variation and bounded derivatives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261943/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of (score, label) pairs where scores lie in [0,1] and labels are binary, split into independent training and validation sets. It outputs a certified probabilistic upper bound on the L1 calibration error at a user-specified confidence level δ, along with a surrogate calibration function. In the perturbation variant, the classifier's raw score s_orig is replaced at inference time by a perturbed score s drawn from a normalized hyperbolic secant distribution centered at s_orig with bandwidth h, which must be small enough (e.g., h = 2^{-6}) to preserve AUROC.

## Method

The paper offers two complementary estimation strategies, both built on constructing a surrogate for the unknown calibration function η and bounding the calibration error as the sum of the empirical error against the surrogate plus a concentration term (via Bernstein's inequality) and a surrogate-approximation error. Under bounded variation, the surrogate is obtained by solving a total-variation denoising optimization (ℓ2 data fidelity + ℓ1 total-variation penalty) on the training set, yielding a piecewise-constant estimate whose reconstruction error is controlled by the known variation bound V. Under bounded derivatives, the key insight is a score perturbation: replacing each classifier output s_orig with a sample from a normalized sech((s − s_orig)/h) kernel guarantees that the resulting calibration function is twice differentiable with first derivative bounded by 1/(2h) and second derivative by 3/(2h²), regardless of the original classifier's properties. The surrogate is then a Nadaraya-Watson kernel smoother (Epanechnikov weights with boundary renormalization), and the smoothing error is bounded via a Taylor-expansion bias-variance decomposition. In practice, K-fold cross-fitting is used to preserve the train/validation independence required by the theory while maximizing data utilization.

## Results

The paper's kernel smoothing estimator (NW) achieves the tightest certified upper bounds on L1 calibration error across both synthetic and real datasets, with an empirical convergence rate of approximately -1/3 (matching theory) and significantly better constants than Lipschitz bucketing. Perturbing classifier outputs by h=2⁻⁶ preserves AUROC with negligible loss on IMDb, Spam Detection, and CIFAR-10. Approximately 10⁷ samples are needed to certify a calibration error bound of ~0.02, and all experiments are repeated 64 times for statistical significance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four methods: the proposed NW kernel smoother, the proposed TV denoising estimator, Lipschitz bucketing, and the standard ECE heuristic. On synthetic data (Figure 3), NW produces the tightest bounds across all four calibration function shapes, while ECE is competitive on three of four shapes but fails completely on the fourth (error stays large and does not decrease with sample size). On real data (Figure 4: Amazon Polarity, Civil Comments, Phishing, Yelp Polarity), NW again yields the tightest upper bounds. NW matches the theoretical rate of Lipschitz bucketing (-1/3) but with substantially better constants, producing significantly tighter bounds in practice. TV denoising is less sample-efficient (rate -1/4 vs. -1/3) but remains useful when only a bounded variation assumption is acceptable. The ECE heuristic, while sometimes competitive, lacks any certified guarantee and can be arbitrarily wrong, underscoring the paper's central argument for principled bounds over heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the total variation denoising literature (Mammen & Van De Geer, 1997; Hutter & Rigollet, 2016) to obtain finite-sample reconstruction guarantees, and on Bernstein-type concentration inequalities for empirical means. It departs from the dominant bucketing-based ECE paradigm (Zadrozny & Elkan; Naeini et al.; Kumar et al.) and from KS/Kuiper-style calibration tests (Arrieta-Ibarra et al.; Tygert) by providing distribution-free, non-asymptotic *upper bounds* on L1 calibration error rather than point estimates or hypothesis tests. The key conceptual departure is the perturbation trick: rather than assuming smoothness (as Lipschitz-based approaches by Vaicenavicius et al. or Hölder-based approaches by Zhang et al. do without justification), the paper *enforces* bounded derivatives by adding a small sech-kernel noise to classifier scores, making the smoothness assumption a design choice rather than an unverifiable premise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide certified, distribution-free upper bounds on L1 calibration error from finite data, filling a gap identified by impossibility results (Lee et al., 2023; Gupta et al., 2020) that showed no estimator can work without structural assumptions. The perturbation approach is broadly applicable to any binary classifier and the practical advice (choose the largest h that doesn't degrade AUROC) gives a concrete recipe. The main limitation is sample complexity: roughly 10^7 samples are needed for a 10^-2 certified bound, which restricts immediate deployment to large-scale settings. Open questions include extension to multiclass calibration, whether the sample rate can be improved, and whether the sech kernel's derivative advantage over Gaussian translates to tighter constants in practice.
