## Problem

Existing medical VQA benchmarks test single-image or short-context reasoning with at most 1.43 images per case, while real clinical diagnosis requires integrating distributed evidence across multiple views, modalities, and time points. Prior work also lacks expert-annotated intermediate reasoning steps and beyond-accuracy evaluation, making it impossible to localize whether failures stem from image reading, cross-view fusion, or higher-level reasoning. This paper asks whether current VLMs can reliably ground, align, and compose evidence across multiple images before performing differential diagnosis.

## Input / Output

Each case provides a brief clinical history and an average of 6.62 images spanning up to 9 distinct modalities (X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound, endoscopy, histology, etc.), with ~30% of cases involving longitudinal follow-up at multiple time points. The model must produce a structured three-step output: per-image radiological findings, a case-level integrated imaging summary, and differential-diagnosis reasoning that selects one answer from five expert-curated options. A separate Medical Education Case Discussion task requires generating structured teaching content across five fixed sections (Background, Clinical Perspective, Imaging Perspective, Outcome, Take-Home Messages), evaluated with both automatic sentence-level factuality checks and human/LLM rubric scoring.

## Method

MedThinkVQA is built from 8,067 peer-reviewed Eurorad teaching cases, with MCQ options anchored in expert differential diagnoses and pruned to five choices via confusion-aware ranking (o4-mini identifies frequently confused distractors). The 720-case test set is constructed through multi-stage filtering: leakage detection, two-stage text-solvability removal (four large + four SFT text-only models must all fail), and surface-bias mitigation to eliminate option-length shortcuts. Evaluation follows a three-step Think-with-Images (TwI) protocol: Steps 1–2 are scored with ROUGE and RadCliQ against expert references, while Step 3 and case discussions are assessed via structured LLM-judge rubrics covering factual correctness, four-category error-type tagging, and education-value scoring, validated against two human experts (Cohen's κ = 0.82). The central empirical finding is that providing expert per-image findings and integrated summaries boosts accuracy by 41–50 points, whereas replacing them with model self-generated intermediates consistently reduces performance, identifying grounded visual evidence extraction—not reasoning length—as the primary bottleneck for current medical VLMs.

## Results

On the MedThinkVQA test set (N=720, full visual evidence), the best closed-source models achieve only 54.9–57.2% accuracy: Claude-4.6-Opus (57.2%), Gemini-3-Pro (55.7%), and GPT-5.2-xhigh (54.9%). Top open-source models trail behind, led by Qwen3.5-397B-A17B in thinking mode (52.2%) and Qwen3.5-27B thinking (50.6%), while smaller models drop sharply (MedGemma-27B: 31.8%, Phi-4: 22.2%). Human experts achieve 77.1% on a 96-case subset, leaving a 20-point gap over the best model. Step-level error analysis shows 77.3% of error-bearing steps involve image-understanding failures rather than reasoning or knowledge errors, identifying grounded multi-image evidence extraction as the primary bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, MedThinkVQA evaluates 30+ vision-language models rather than proposing a new method. Among closed-source systems, Claude-4.6-Opus leads at 57.2%, narrowly ahead of Gemini-3-Pro (55.7%) and GPT-5.2-xhigh (54.9%); enabling thinking mode on GPT-5.2 adds ~5 points over its non-thinking baseline (49.6%→54.9%). Open-source models lag by 3–25 points: Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (52.2% thinking) is the strongest open model, followed by Qwen3.5-27B (50.6%), Lingshu-32B (43.2%), and InternVL3.5-38B (40.7%), while MedGemma-27B (31.8%) and Phi-4 (22.2%) fall well below. Inference-time thinking consistently helps by 5–7 points for GPT-5 variants and larger Qwen3.5 models, but yields flat or negative gains for Qwen3-VL and small Qwen3.5 models (e.g., Qwen3.5-0.8B drops from 31.4% to 21.0% in thinking mode), indicating that extra reasoning amplifies early visual grounding errors when the model's image reading is unreliable. All models remain 20+ points below the 77.1% human-expert accuracy, and the dominant error type (image understanding, 77.3% of error steps) confirms that the bottleneck is cross-view evidence extraction rather than final-answer reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the medical VQA benchmark lineage (VQA-Rad, OmniMedVQA, GMAI-MMBench, MedXpertQA-MM) by deliberately shifting from single-image or image-pair tasks to a genuinely multi-image diagnostic setting (6.62 images/case vs. prior max 1.43), drawing on the Eurorad educational corpus that prior work (Kim et al. 2025; Fallahpour et al. 2025) used only for text-only prompting or agent-style tool orchestration. The step-level supervision (per-image findings → integrated summary → differential reasoning) generalizes the visual-grounded chain-of-thought ideas from GEMeX-ThinkVG and S-Chain, which were limited to single-image cases without clinical scenarios. The beyond-accuracy evaluation suite (stepwise error tagging, RadCliQ/ROUGE intermediate metrics, education-value rubrics) builds on recent stepwise medical evaluation work (Wang et al. 2025a; Yun et al. 2025) but applies it to a multi-view, multi-modality setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding that the dominant bottleneck is reliable cross-image visual grounding (77% of errors are image-understanding failures) rather than reasoning depth challenges the field's current emphasis on longer chain-of-thought and inference-time scaling. The demonstration that self-generated intermediates consistently degrade performance while expert-provided ones substantially improve it provides a concrete, actionable diagnostic for model developers: the fix is better visual evidence extraction, not more tokens. Open questions include whether view-aware architectures (temporal indexing, spatial grounding modules) can close the 20-point gap to human experts, how to handle the realistic minority of redundant images, and whether the conditional scaling law (reasoning helps only after grounding is reliable) generalizes beyond radiology to other multi-view clinical tasks.
