## Problem

Most spectral clustering approaches rely on single-modal visual representations, leaving the semantic structure encoded in vision-language models largely untapped. While prior work (e.g., TAC) incorporates text at the feature level, simply applying standard kernels to image-text-concentrated features does not adequately guide affinity construction, as evidenced by TAC(SC) performing comparably to TAC(KMeans). The core question is how to principledly integrate textual semantics with visual similarities when building the affinity matrix for spectral clustering.

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled RGB images (encoded via CLIP ViT-B/32 or ViT-L/14 into unit-norm hyperspherical features), a set of positive nouns filtered from WordNet using TAC's train-split-based strategy, and 7 prompt templates (e.g., 'a photo of the large { }'). The positive nouns are encoded through CLIP's text encoder to form a matrix W ∈ R^{d×N} that anchors the NTK. The output is a K-way cluster assignment obtained by spectral clustering on a final ensembled affinity matrix, which itself is produced by iteratively diffusing and weighting B prompt-specific NTK affinity matrices.

## Method

The paper proposes Neural Tangent Kernel Spectral Clustering, which anchors the empirical NTK with CLIP text features of positive nouns (θ₀ = vec(W)) and designs the proxy network in a log-sum-exp form g_θ₀(f_X(x_i)) = log Σ_k exp(w_k^T f_X(x_i)/τ). The resulting NTK decomposes into a multiplicative coupling of visual proximity U_ij (inner product of CLIP image embeddings) and semantic overlap V_ij (dot product of per-image softmax distributions over positive nouns), which theoretically amplifies within-cluster affinities while suppressing cross-cluster links, sharpening the block-diagonal structure. To handle multiple prompt templates, the paper introduces Regularized Affinity Diffusion (RAD), a joint optimization over ensemble weights β and the ensembled affinity matrix Â that alternates between a closed-form diffusion update (solved via an efficient iterative solver avoiding M²×M² inversion) and a Lasso-style weight update solved in one shot via KKT conditions. The final affinity matrix is fed into standard normalized-cut spectral clustering to obtain cluster assignments.

## Results

On the five classical datasets, the proposed NTK Spectral Clustering achieves 98.3% ACC and 96.3% ARI on STL-10, 92.0% ACC on CIFAR-10, and 84.9% ACC on ImageNet-Dogs, outperforming the strongest prior method TAC by 3.8% and 9.8% ACC respectively. On three challenging datasets (DTD, UCF-101, ImageNet-1K), the method surpasses TAC by an average of 7.7%, 2.5%, and 6.3% in ACC, NMI, and ARI. In fine-grained and domain-shifted settings, it outperforms TAC by 5.1% ACC on Pets and 5.3% ACC on ImageNet-Sketch, while maintaining consistent gains over TAC on ImageNet-C, ImageNet-V2, and ImageNet-Sketch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms TAC (Li et al., 2024) in both its KMeans and SC variants across all five classical datasets, with the largest gap on ImageNet-Dogs where it achieves 84.9% ACC vs. TAC(SC)'s 75.8% (+9.1%) and TAC(KMeans)'s 75.1% (+9.8%). Against the strongest gradient-based competitor GradNorm, the method wins on CIFAR-10 (92.0% vs. 91.1% ACC), CIFAR-20 (59.6% vs. 60.6% ACC — a narrow loss), ImageNet-Dogs (84.9% vs. 81.2% ACC), and ImageNet-10 (99.2% vs. 99.4% ACC — a near-tie), while matching on STL-10 ACC (98.3%). On the three challenging datasets, the method dominates TAC(SC) by 8.0% ACC on DTD (52.0% vs. 44.0%), 7.9% ACC on UCF-101 (67.9% vs. 60.0%), and 7.2% ACC on ImageNet-1K (56.3% vs. 49.1%). The only notable loss is on CIFAR-10 NMI where SIC (Cai et al., 2023) achieves 84.7% vs. the proposed method's 83.3%, attributed to SIC's additional trainable parameters and more sophisticated training strategy. All traditional deep clustering baselines (DAC, DCCM, IIC, PICA, CC, IDFD, SCAN, MiCE, GCC, NNM, TCC, SPICE, SeCu, DivClust, RPSC, TCL, ProPos) are outperformed by large margins (typically 10–50+ points in ACC), confirming the advantage of CLIP-based multi-modal representations over purely visual deep clustering approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends spectral clustering from the single-modal visual regime to a multi-modal setting by anchoring the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) with CLIP text features of positive nouns, yielding an affinity that multiplicatively couples visual proximity with semantic overlap. It builds directly on CLIP's cross-modal alignment (Radford et al., 2021), TAC's positive-noun selection and prompt templates (Li et al., 2024), and the NTK framework (Jacot et al., 2018), while departing from prior multi-modal clustering methods (SIC, TAC) that incorporate text only at the feature level rather than in the affinity construction itself. The Regularized Affinity Diffusion (RAD) mechanism for adaptively ensembling prompt-specific affinity matrices draws on the regularized diffusion literature (Bai et al., 2016–2018) but applies it to the novel setting of NTK-induced, multi-prompt spectral clustering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, training-free recipe for injecting textual semantics into spectral clustering affinity matrices, and the key negative result—that TAC features combined with standard RBF spectral clustering perform no better than TAC + K-Means—sharply motivates why affinity construction, not just feature quality, is the bottleneck. Open questions include scalability to very large datasets (the M×M affinity matrix and M²×M² diffusion operator), sensitivity to the quality and coverage of the positive-noun set, and whether the NTK-based coupling generalizes beyond CLIP to other vision-language models. The method's dependence on a pre-trained CLIP backbone and WordNet-derived nouns limits its applicability in domains where such resources are unavailable or misaligned.
