## Problem

Existing retrieval-augmented generation methods struggle to integrate reasoning effectively: complex queries require multi-step decomposition, yet LLMs frequently generate imprecise subqueries and cannot reliably distinguish when external retrieval is needed versus when parametric knowledge suffices. This leads to either redundant retrieval that introduces noise or missed retrieval that causes hallucinations, and iterative retrieval methods often fall into continuous retrieval loops without converging.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language question and a Wikipedia knowledge base accessed via BM25 retrieval. It produces a structured reasoning trace consisting of a sequence of 'Follow up:' subqueries, each annotated with a binary atomic decision (direct parametric answer vs. 'Let's search the question in Wikipedia' followed by retrieved context), intermediate answers, and a final answer. The retrieval budget is capped at T=5 steps, and the model is trained to minimize retrieval count while maintaining answer correctness.

## Method

DeepRAG frames step-by-step RAG reasoning as a Markov Decision Process where each action comprises a termination decision (continue vs. finalize) and an atomic decision (retrieve vs. parametric) for the next subquery. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) Binary Tree Search explores both parametric and retrieval paths for each subquery, using a priority queue to find the correct-answer path with minimal retrieval cost; (2) Imitation Learning fine-tunes the model on these optimal trajectories with a masked loss over retrieved tokens; (3) Chain of Calibration further refines knowledge-boundary awareness via either offline DPO (constructing preference pairs from optimal-path nodes) or online GRPO with an over-retrieve reward that penalizes retrieval count beyond a threshold while rewarding correctness. The key insight is that explicitly calibrating the model's recognition of its own knowledge limits—rather than relying on confidence scores or external classifiers—enables end-to-end adaptive retrieval without additional parameters.

## Results

On Llama-3-8B, DeepRAG-RLon achieves the best average EM of 42.36 across five QA benchmarks (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, PopQA, WebQuestions, MuSiQue), outperforming the strongest baseline R1-Searcher++ (40.85) and Search-R1 (36.54). On Qwen-2.5-32B, DeepRAG-RLon reaches 41.05 avg EM versus R1-Searcher++'s 37.94. The paper reports a 25.41% overall accuracy improvement over prior methods, with DeepRAG-RLon also achieving the highest retrieval-efficiency trade-off (0.14 avg retrievals on WebQuestions vs. 4.52 for Auto-RAG) and the best knowledge-boundary calibration (MCC 0.543 vs. 0.458 for R1-Searcher++).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepRAG-RLon (Llama-3-8B) outperforms the strongest RL-based baseline R1-Searcher++ by 1.51 avg EM (42.36 vs. 40.85) and Search-R1 by 5.82 avg EM, with the largest gaps on out-of-distribution datasets: PopQA (+1.10 EM) and MuSiQue (+2.90 EM). Against adaptive retrieval baselines, DeepRAG-RLon surpasses TAARE (33.84) and UAR (33.58) by roughly 8–9 avg EM, and confidence-based methods FLARE (30.13) and DRAGIN (27.33) by over 12 EM. On retrieval efficiency (WebQuestions), DeepRAG-RLon uses only 0.14 average retrievals versus 4.52 for Auto-RAG, 2.25 for IterDRAG, and 0.51 for Search-R1, while simultaneously achieving higher EM (30.00 vs. 17.40, 15.90, 27.70 respectively). On knowledge-boundary calibration (2WikiMultihopQA), DeepRAG-RLon achieves the best MCC (0.543) and balanced accuracy (0.801), exceeding R1-Searcher++ (MCC 0.458, BalAcc 0.752) and Search-R1 (MCC 0.366, BalAcc 0.701), indicating more precise retrieval/parametric decisions. On Qwen-2.5-32B, DeepRAG-RLon extends its lead to 41.05 avg EM over R1-Searcher++ (37.94) and Search-R1 (27.85), confirming cross-backbone generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepRAG sits at the intersection of adaptive RAG (FLARE, DRAGIN, UAR, TAARE), iterative retrieval (IterDRAG, Auto-RAG), and RL-based retrieval optimization (Search-R1, R1-Searcher++). It departs from prior adaptive RAG by replacing classifier heads or confidence thresholds with an explicit MDP formulation where both termination and per-subquery retrieval decisions are learned end-to-end. The training pipeline builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) for offline preference calibration and GRPO from DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025) for online RL, but wraps them in a novel binary-tree-search data synthesis step that is specific to the retrieval-augmented reasoning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MDP framing provides a clean, model-agnostic recipe for any LLM to learn when and what to retrieve, which is practically valuable as RAG systems move toward agentic, multi-step reasoning. The knowledge-boundary calibration results (Table 3) showing that confidence-based methods have near-zero MCC while DeepRAG-RLon achieves 0.543 suggest that explicit preference/RL training is a more reliable path to calibrated retrieval than heuristic uncertainty metrics. Limitations include reliance on BM25 + Wikipedia as the retrieval backend, evaluation only on 8B/32B models, and the computational cost of binary tree search during data synthesis; open questions include whether the MDP formulation scales to dense retrieval, multi-source knowledge bases, or substantially longer reasoning chains.
