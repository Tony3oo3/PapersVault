## Problem

No coreset construction method with theoretical guarantees existed for standard LASSO regression. Directly applying existing sensitivity sampling frameworks yields coreset sizes scaling with Õ(Gd/ε²) where G is the total sensitivity, which is prohibitively large in high-dimensional settings. The core difficulty is that the ℓ1 penalty induces a sparse, non-smooth geometry in the parameter space that interacts complexly with the residual term, making standard empirical process and ε-net arguments yield loose bounds.

## Input / Output

The method takes an n×d design matrix A, an n-dimensional response vector b, and a regularization parameter λ>0, and produces a weighted subset of m rows (indices Q and weight vector w) such that solving LASSO on the coreset gives a (1±ε)-approximation to the full-data solution with probability at least 1−δ. The coreset size is Õ(ε⁻²d·(log³d·min{1, log d/λ²} + log(1/δ))), which depends on the dimension d, approximation parameter ε, confidence δ, and the regularization strength λ. Sensitivity scores are computed via approximate ℓ2 leverage scores of the augmented matrix [A|b] plus a 1/n term, computable in O(nnz(A)log n + d³ log(n/d) log d) time.

## Method

The key technical contribution is a localized empirical process framework that decomposes the LASSO function space Ω = {x : ‖Ax−b‖₂² + λ‖x‖₁ ≤ 1} into two separable convex components: the residual ball B₂(A′) and the ℓ1-penalty ball B₁(1/λ). After symmetrizing the sampling error to a Gaussian process via Rademacher comparison, the authors bound the Gaussian diameter (O(τ√log d/λ)) and metric entropy separately over each localized component using weighted chaining with multi-scale ε-nets. The metric entropy integral is split at a critical radius t₀ = τ√(log m/d), yielding different covering-number regimes: a quadratic-in-1/t bound for large radii and a logarithmic bound for small radii, the latter exploiting the Sudakov minoration inequality for the ℓ1 ball's volume concentration. This decomposition avoids the Õ(Gd) dependence of standard union-bound analyses and achieves a coreset size of Õ(ε⁻²d·(log³d·min{1, log d/λ²} + log(1/δ))), which nearly matches the information-theoretic lower bound Ω(d log d/ε²) in the regime λ = O(1/√d).

## Results

On the CTs dataset (n=53,500, d=386, λ=0.5), LASSO-Sens at a coreset size of 5d achieves a loss of 25.32±0.02, closely matching the full-data LASSO solver (29.27±7.05) while being at least 4× faster and up to 18× faster across datasets. On the large-scale mnist8m dataset (n=8,100,000, d=784), LASSO-Sens produces a feasible solution in 15 minutes, whereas the standard FISTA-based LASSO solver fails to converge even after 48 hours. At a coreset size of 10d, the sparsity of LASSO-Sens solutions closely matches that of the exact LASSO solver across all four datasets, confirming that sensitivity sampling preserves the sparsity-inducing property of the ℓ₁ penalty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LASSO-Sens against two baselines: (1) the full-data LASSO solver (FISTA) and (2) LASSO-Uniform, which uniformly samples rows before applying FISTA. Against the full-data LASSO solver, LASSO-Sens achieves comparable or better loss (e.g., 25.32 vs. 29.27 on CTs at λ=0.5) while being at least 4× faster on medium-scale datasets and dramatically faster on mnist8m (15 min vs. >48 hours). Against LASSO-Uniform, LASSO-Sens consistently achieves lower loss at the same coreset size — for example, at 1d on CTs, LASSO-Sens reaches 25.43±0.17 while LASSO-Uniform only achieves 61.58±22.86 — and also produces sparser solutions (322 vs. 337 nonzero coefficients at 10d). The advantage of sensitivity sampling over uniform sampling is most pronounced at small coreset sizes, where uniform sampling fails to capture high-leverage rows, and diminishes as the coreset size grows toward 20d where both methods converge to similar loss values.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sensitivity-sampling coreset framework from unregularized and ridge regression to the standard LASSO objective, which had no prior theoretical treatment. It builds directly on the chaining-based empirical process machinery of Woodruff & Yasuda (2023) and the covering-number bounds of Munteanu & Omlor (2024), while adapting the well-conditioned basis technique of Cohen et al. (2015) to bound LASSO sensitivity scores by leverage scores. The key departure from Chhaya et al. (2020), who handled a modified LASSO with an ℓ₁² penalty, is the introduction of a localized decomposition of the function space into residual and ℓ₁-penalty components, enabling tighter Gaussian-process bounds that avoid the general Õ(𝒢d) scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provable coreset construction for the standard LASSO objective, closing a gap that left practitioners without theoretical guarantees for row subsampling in sparse regression. The near-matching lower bound (Ω(d log d / ε²) when λ = O(d^{-1/2})) confirms the upper bound is essentially tight up to polylog factors, giving the field a clear picture of the fundamental limits. The practical speedups (4–9×, with an 8M-sample dataset solved in 15 minutes versus 48+ hours for full LASSO) make the result immediately useful for large-scale sparse regression. Open questions include extending the localized empirical process technique to elastic net and other non-smooth regularizers, and whether the min{1, log d/λ²} dependence can be further tightened.
