## Problem

Zero-shot time series forecasting is hindered by limited, biased, and leakage-prone real data corpora, and prior synthetic-data approaches (KernelSynth, ForecastPFN) have failed to fully substitute for real data when used in isolation. This paper asks whether a fast, stable, and sufficiently expressive simulator can enable strong zero-shot forecasting from purely synthetic training data at scale, without any exposure to target datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263729/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a univariate time series history of up to 4096 points (left-padded with a zero-padding mask to handle variable-length inputs) and produces 512-step-ahead multi-horizon quantile forecasts across a fixed set of quantile levels. For target datasets requiring longer horizons, the input is downsampled by a factor of 2 and the forecast is upsampled accordingly. Training windows are extracted from 6000-point simulator sequences by randomly selecting start and end points, with the left portion of the 4096-length context zeroed out at a random position to simulate variable-length padding.

## Method

SarSim0 is a three-stage synthetic time series simulator grounded in SARIMA dynamics. First, it samples AR and MA poles directly within the unit circle (rather than drawing coefficients, which destabilizes the process), using a 50/50 mixture of non-seasonal-AR-only and seasonal-AR-only subcases to sidestep the non-convex joint stability region; fractional differencing (order sampled in [0,1]) enriches trend diversity. Second, a SARIMA-2 superposition block combines two independently generated seasonal processes (e.g., s=24 and s=7) via additive mixing or multiplicative modulation with random depth, producing bi-seasonal envelope-on-carrier structure. Third, rate-based heavy-tailed noisers (Poisson, Generalized Gamma, Lognormal) condition their variance on the local series level to inject burstiness and intermittency. The entire pipeline is vectorized across 256 parallel trajectories per parameter draw, enabling on-the-fly generation of ~1B unique series during training of NBEATS, PatchTST, MLP, and Chronos-Small backbones—orders of magnitude faster than kernel-based generators like KernelSynth.

## Results

On GiftEval, PatchTST-SarSim0 achieves the best sCRPS of 0.573 and MASE of 0.837, outperforming all real-data-pretrained foundation models (Chronos-Base: 0.647/0.870, MOIRAI-Large: 0.599/0.874, TimesFM: 0.680/1.077) and the statistical AutoARIMA baseline (0.912/1.074). On M-Series, NBEATS-SarSim0 achieves sCRPS 0.096 and MASE 0.869, matching AutoARIMA's sCRPS (0.096) while remaining competitive on MASE (0.869 vs. 0.843). Inference times for SarSim0-trained models are 45–52 minutes, substantially faster than Chronos-Base (2103 min) or MOIRAI-Large (3976 min). Ablations confirm SARIMA-2 (multi-seasonality superposition) is the largest single contributor to zero-shot generalization, with removing it causing the biggest accuracy drop across all backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GiftEval, all SarSim0-trained models (NBEATS, PatchTST, Chronos) decisively outperform AutoARIMA (sCRPS 0.573–0.608 vs. 0.912), demonstrating a 'student-beats-teacher' effect where neural models trained on SARIMA-derived simulations exceed the statistical model that generated their training data. SarSim0-trained models also surpass all real-data-pretrained foundation models: PatchTST-SarSim0 (0.573) beats MOIRAI-Large (0.599), Chronos-Base (0.647), and TimesFM (0.680) on GiftEval sCRPS. On M-Series, the picture is more nuanced: AutoARIMA remains very strong (sCRPS 0.096, MASE 0.843), and NBEATS-SarSim0 matches its sCRPS but trails slightly on MASE (0.869 vs. 0.843), while PatchTST-SarSim0 is slightly worse on sCRPS (0.097) but far better on MASE (0.877 vs. 1.613 for PatchTST from scratch). Against prior synthetic-data pipelines, SarSim0 uniformly dominates: NBEATS-SarSim0 (0.602) beats NBEATS-KernelSynth (0.686) and NBEATS-ForecastPFN (1.070) on GiftEval sCRPS, and PatchTST-SarSim0 (0.573) beats PatchTST-KernelSynth (0.702) and PatchTST-ForecastPFN (1.060). Notably, the small NBEATS architecture trained on SarSim0 (46 min inference) closes the gap to and slightly exceeds the much larger Chronos-Base (2103 min inference), suggesting data diversity and scale can partially substitute for architectural complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot time series forecasting paradigm (Oreshkin et al., 2021; Garza & Mergenthaler-Canseco, 2023) by addressing its core bottleneck—data—rather than architecture. It departs from prior synthetic-data generators (ForecastPFN's ETS decomposition, KernelSynth's GP kernels) by grounding simulation in stable SARIMA pole-space sampling, adding a superposition layer for multi-seasonality, and overlaying rate-based heavy-tailed noise, yielding a pipeline orders of magnitude faster than kernel-based alternatives. The forecasting backbones (NBEATS, PatchTST, Chronos) are standard; the contribution is entirely in the data generation pipeline and the empirical demonstration that synthetic-only pretraining at ~1B series scale suffices for competitive zero-shot generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a fast, privacy-safe, license-free simulator that enables on-the-fly generation of billions of training series removes the data bottleneck for zero-shot forecasting in industrial settings. The 'student-beats-teacher' observation on GiftEval (neural models trained on SARIMA-derived simulations outperform AutoARIMA) hints at emergent generalization beyond the generative model, though the effect is mixed on M-Series and the paper itself frames it as early, benchmark-specific evidence. Key open questions include whether hybrid synthetic-real training further improves results, how to handle exogenous covariates and regime-switching/structural breaks (identified as the main fidelity gap via LOF novelty analysis), and a deeper understanding of when and why learners can surpass their generative teachers.
