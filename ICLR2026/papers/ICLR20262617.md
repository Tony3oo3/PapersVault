## Problem

Scientific answer verification is hindered by two gaps: existing benchmarks lack cross-disciplinary coverage and discipline-specific equivalence forms, and current verifiers rely on brittle rule-based templates or unstable prompt engineering that fails on complex multi-step equivalence judgments. The paper further identifies that chain-of-thought reasoning, which is essential for assessing answer equivalence from multiple perspectives, has been systematically overlooked by prior verifier designs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262617/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The verifier receives a scientific question, a gold reference answer, and a candidate LLM response (averaging ~2980 tokens), and produces a structured judgment (A=correct, B=incorrect, C=incomplete/repetitive/refusal) preceded by a concise reasoning chain. Outputs are deliberately kept short (~490 tokens for the 8B model) via an overlong penalty during RL, in contrast to general reasoning models that emit 1000–5000 tokens. The benchmark test set mixes 350 real LLM responses and 150 synthetic equivalence-transformed answers per domain, with the latter generated via domain-specific transformations (e.g., Taylor expansion, unit conversion, SMILES rewriting, synonymous protein substitutions).

## Method

SCI-Verifier is built on Qwen3-4B/8B-Base through a two-stage post-training pipeline. In the SFT stage, large models generate diverse reasoning paths via rejection sampling, which are then strictly filtered to retain only concise, structured traces (for reasoning models, only the conclusive summary is kept), effectively distilling domain-specific verification knowledge into a small model. In the RL stage, DAPO (a GRPO variant that filters overly easy/hard samples) is used with a binary alignment reward plus a piecewise overlong penalty to encourage short, stable outputs while maximizing correctness. The key design insight is that verification requires short, perspective-fixed reasoning (algebraic equivalence, unit conversion, semantic matching) rather than long open-ended chains, and that rebalancing positive/negative examples during RL prevents the model from exploiting label priors. The 8B model achieves 92.72% accuracy on SCI-VerifyBench, matching GPT-5 (88.92%) while using far fewer output tokens.

## Results

On SCI-VerifyBench (2,500 samples across 5 scientific domains), SCI-Verifier-8B achieves 92.72% total accuracy, surpassing GPT-5 (88.92%) by 3.8 points and the best specialized verifier CompassVerifier-32B (86.24%) by 6.5 points. The 4B variant also outperforms GPT-5, reaching 90.20% accuracy. On VerifierBench, SCI-Verifier-8B attains 93.01% accuracy and 93.06% F1, exceeding GPT-5 (91.80% Acc, 90.48% F1); on VerifyBench-Hard it reaches 90.30% accuracy and 87.45% F1, slightly trailing GPT-5's 90.40% accuracy but leading on F1 (85.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCI-Verifier-8B (92.72%) outperforms GPT-5 (88.92%) by 3.8 points on SCI-VerifyBench, and also beats Gemini-2.5-Flash (87.56%) and o4-mini (86.96%). Against specialized verifiers, SCI-Verifier-8B surpasses CompassVerifier-32B (86.24%) by 6.5 points and xVerify-8B (80.16%) by 12.6 points, while using only ~490 output tokens versus 1–212 tokens for the specialized verifiers. On VerifierBench, SCI-Verifier-8B leads GPT-5 by 1.2 points in accuracy (93.01% vs. 91.80%) and 2.6 points in F1 (93.06% vs. 90.48%). On VerifyBench-Hard, GPT-5 holds a marginal 0.1-point accuracy edge (90.40% vs. 90.30%), but SCI-Verifier-8B leads by 2.1 points in F1 (87.45% vs. 85.34%). Notably, the 4B model (90.20%) already exceeds GPT-5, and both SCI-Verifier sizes outperform all open-source instruct and reasoning models (Qwen3-235B-A22B at 81.64%, LLaMa-3.3-70B at 78.64%) by 8–14 points, demonstrating that targeted post-training for verification is more effective than raw model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on LLM answer verification (xVerify, CompassVerifier, VerifyBench, VerifierBench) by addressing two gaps the authors identify: narrow disciplinary coverage and the absence of reasoning in specialized verifiers. Conceptually, it departs from the efficiency-first design of prior verifiers (which output a single token) by arguing that chain-of-thought reasoning is essential for complex equivalence judgment, and it borrows the DAPO/GRPO reinforcement learning framework from the reasoning-model training literature to inject this capability into a small model. The benchmark construction draws on domain-specific equivalence transformations (formula rewrites, unit conversions, SMILES variants, RNA structure notations) that go beyond the simple paraphrasing or multiple-choice formats in prior verification benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-disciplinary benchmark (5 domains, 2500 samples with equivalence transformations) fills a real gap in systematic evaluation of scientific verification, and the 8B model matching GPT-5 offers a practical, deployable verifier for scientific LLM pipelines. However, the work is limited to text-only verification and binary correctness judgment; it does not address partial credit, multi-step reasoning verification, or multimodal scientific outputs (e.g., diagrams, spectra). The authors note that future work should leverage explicit reasoning to enhance implicit reasoning for efficiency, suggesting the current CoT-based approach, while effective, is not yet optimal for latency-sensitive deployment.
