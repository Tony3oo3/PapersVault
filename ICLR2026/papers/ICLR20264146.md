## Problem

While GRPO has become the standard for RLVR training of LLMs on reasoning tasks, there is no principled, fine-grained mechanism to explicitly steer training toward exploration or exploitation. Existing approaches either reweight at the question level (Pass@K training) or model only a token's self-influence (entropy regularization), overlooking the cross-token and cross-response interactions that actually govern training dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264146/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a group of G responses generated for a single question, binary correctness rewards, and the model's token hidden states. For each token, THR is computed as a weighted sum of inner products between that token's hidden state and hidden states of tokens in all other responses, scaled by prediction-error similarity coefficients (α). The output is a per-token multiplicative reweighting factor applied to the standard GRPO advantage, where the sign of THR and the hyperparameter p jointly determine whether a token's learning signal is amplified or suppressed.

## Method

The paper introduces Token Hidden Reward (THR), a token-level metric derived from GRPO's likelihood-change dynamics (Theorem 3.1) that quantifies each token's contribution to the probability of correct outputs. The key finding is that a small subset of tokens carries disproportionately high absolute THR values, and the sign of THR encodes the exploration-exploitation trade-off: positive THR tokens strengthen confidence in correct outputs (exploitation), while negative THR tokens preserve probability mass for alternatives (exploration). The proposed algorithm applies sign-dependent reweighting to token advantages via A_i,k^THR(p) = 1[|THR_i,k| > τ] · (1 + sign(THR_i,k) · p) · A_i,k, where p > 0 biases toward exploitation (improving greedy accuracy) and p < 0 biases toward exploration (improving Pass@K). Unlike entropy-based methods that capture only a token's self-influence, THR explicitly models cross-token and cross-response interactions through hidden-state inner products, making it a more efficient and targeted proxy for controlling exploration. The approach integrates seamlessly with other RL objectives such as GSPO-token and generalizes across model families including Qwen and Llama.

## Results

On exploitation (greedy decoding), THR(p=0.1) improves total average accuracy over GRPO by 1.1% on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (36.3% vs. 34.8%) and 4.0% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (46.8% vs. 42.7%), with the largest single-dataset gain being +12.5% on AMC23 for the 7B model. On exploration (Pass@K), THR(p<0) achieves a 4.2% average improvement over GRPO on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B (76.7% vs. 72.5% across K=1–256) and ~1% on Qwen2.5-Math-7B. THR-dominant-token-only training (p=0) matches full GRPO performance, confirming that a small subset of high-THR tokens drives training dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), THR(p=0.1) wins on exploitation with +1.1% (1.5B) and +4.0% (7B) total average greedy accuracy, while THR(p<0) wins on exploration with +4.2% average Pass@K (1.5B) and ~+1% (7B). Against Pass@K-mixed (Chen et al., 2025), THR(p<0) consistently outperforms across all K values on both Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and 7B, with average improvements exceeding 1.1%; directly combining the two methods is suboptimal because Pass@K-mixed over-suppresses easy questions. Against Cov-KL (Cui et al., 2025), THR(p<0) wins in every Pass@K setting (e.g., 76.7% vs. 69.2% average on 1.5B), attributed to THR's modeling of cross-token interactions versus Cov-KL's self-influence-only analysis. Against GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025), THR(p<0) integrated into GSPO-token yields +0.9% over vanilla THR and +1.4% over GSPO alone in average Pass@K, confirming seamless integration with alternative RL objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-dynamics analysis of GRPO from Deng et al. (2025), which characterized how negative gradients affect confidence in correct responses, by defining a symmetric token-level metric (THR) that captures both positive and negative contributions across all tokens in a group. It departs from question-level reweighting approaches (Pass@K training by Chen et al., Mahdavi et al.) by operating at the token level, and from entropy-based exploration methods (Cov-KL by Cui et al.) by explicitly modeling cross-token interactions rather than self-influence. The method is demonstrated to integrate with both GRPO and GSPO, positioning it as a plug-in advantage-shaping layer for the broader family of group-relative policy optimization algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

THR provides a simple, interpretable, and computationally cheap mechanism (≈37 s overhead per step) for practitioners to explicitly bias RLVR training toward exploitation (greedy accuracy) or exploration (Pass@K) via a single hyperparameter p, without modifying the base algorithm. The finding that a small subset of high-THR tokens dominates training dynamics and overlaps ~90% with high-entropy tokens suggests that existing entropy-based heuristics are already partially capturing this signal, yet THR's cross-token formulation still yields consistent gains over Cov-KL. Open questions include whether adaptive scheduling of p based on training progress or question difficulty can further improve results, whether the approach generalizes beyond math reasoning to code or scientific domains, and how it behaves at larger model scales where the dominant-token fraction may shift.
