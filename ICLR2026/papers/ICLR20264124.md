## Problem

Black-box prompt optimization for generative AI has been studied almost exclusively in offline settings with pre-established datasets, yet real-world deployments (real-time customer support, e-commerce personalization, adaptive tutoring) require continuous prompt adaptation to streaming data. Moreover, existing methods overlook the compound uncertainty arising from both the inherent stochasticity of generative model outputs and the high variance of zeroth-order gradient estimates, leaving the online black-box prompt tuning problem largely unexplored without theoretical guarantees.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the method receives a streaming sample ξ^t = (x^t, y^t) consisting of an input sentence and its ground-truth label, and queries a black-box generative model (text-to-text LLM or text-to-image diffusion model) via API only. The method maintains a low-dimensional continuous soft prompt vector z^t ∈ R^d (d=10 in experiments) that is projected through a random matrix A ∈ R^{D×d} into the embedding space of a frozen open-source LLM (e.g., Vicuna-7B/13B) to produce a discrete hard prompt φ^t, which is then fed to the target generative model. The output is the updated soft prompt z^{t+1} after each round, with no access to model weights or intermediate representations.

## Method

AOZPT integrates zeroth-order optimization with online learning in a non-convex setting. It uses a central two-point random gradient estimator to approximate the gradient of the loss with respect to the soft prompt, requiring only two function evaluations per round. The key innovation is an adaptive uncertainty scale adjustment mechanism that replaces standard Adam's infinite-history moment estimates with exponentially weighted moving averages over a finite sliding window of size w, using separate decay parameters α (for the first moment m_t) and β (for the second moment v_t). This windowed adaptive scaling simultaneously mitigates the noise from generative AI's stochastic outputs and the high variance of zeroth-order gradient estimates, while the finite window enables adaptation to non-stationary data streams. The paper provides a formal local regret analysis under Lipschitz smoothness, bounded loss, and bounded noise assumptions, proving sublinear regret convergence (O(T/W + T√M/W)) when α, β → 1⁻ and w = T^{1/2}.

## Results

On text-to-text tasks, AOZPT achieves the best cumulative F1 score on CNN/DailyMail across all three LLMs (24.71 with Llama-3.1-8B, 35.40 with GPT-3.5-turbo, 24.77 with Qwen2.5-14B) and the best cumulative accuracy on GSM8K (69.73, 78.13, and 92.93 respectively). On text-to-image tasks, AOZPT attains the highest aesthetic scores on both Anime (6.282 with Dreamlike-2.0, 5.930 with Stable Diffusion v1.5) and Painting (6.656, 6.313) datasets. The method consistently outperforms the online ZO-OGD baseline, with the largest gains on GSM8K (up to +8.6 points with GPT-3.5-turbo) and Qwen2.5-14B on CNN/DailyMail (+2.73 F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AOZPT outperforms all five baselines (MP, ICL, BDPL, RLPROMPT, ZO-OGD) on the majority of model–dataset combinations. Against the closest online competitor ZO-OGD, AOZPT gains +4.67 accuracy points on GSM8K with GPT-3.5-turbo (78.13 vs. 69.53), +2.73 F1 on CNN/DailyMail with Qwen2.5-14B (24.77 vs. 22.03), and +0.67 on GSM8K with Llama-3.1-8B (69.73 vs. 65.07). The offline baselines BDPL and RLPROMPT show particularly large gaps on GSM8K (e.g., BDPL drops to 36.4–37.7 accuracy vs. AOZPT's 69.7–92.9), confirming that offline methods struggle in the streaming setting. On text-to-image tasks (Table 2), AOZPT edges out ZO-OGD by 0.02–0.05 aesthetic score and surpasses SFT/Promptist by 0.1–0.3, with the advantage most pronounced on the Painting dataset (6.656 vs. 6.645 for SFT with Dreamlike-2.0). The only near-tie is GSM8K with Qwen2.5-14B, where AOZPT (92.93) marginally beats ZO-OGD (92.53) by 0.4 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: black-box prompt tuning (BBT, RLPROMPT, BDPL, ZOPO) and online non-convex optimization (local regret frameworks of Hazan et al., Aydore et al.). It builds directly on INSTRUCTZERO's soft-prompt-to-hard-prompt mechanism via random projection and a frozen LLM, and adapts Adam's first/second moment estimates into a sliding-window form to handle noisy zeroth-order gradients. The key departure is that all prior black-box prompt tuning methods operate offline on fixed datasets, while this paper is the first to formulate and analyze the problem in an online streaming setting with explicit noise from generative model outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal regret guarantee (sublinear) for online black-box prompt tuning, giving theoretical grounding to a practically important scenario—real-time prompt adaptation in customer support, tutoring, and e-commerce where retraining is infeasible. However, the experimental gains over the ZO-OGD baseline are modest and model-dependent (e.g., ~8 points on GSM8K/GPT-3.5-turbo but <1 point on GSM8K/Qwen2.5-14B), and the online setting is simulated rather than truly non-stationary. Open questions include whether the bounded-noise and Lipschitz-gradient assumptions hold for frontier models, how the method behaves under severe concept drift, and whether the frozen-LLM prompt generator adds sufficient value over direct soft-prompt projection (Table 10 shows a large gap, but only on two models).
