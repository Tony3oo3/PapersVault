## Problem

Existing unified multimodal models rely on autoregressive architectures that suffer inherent conflicts between understanding and generation tasks, or adopt hybrid/decoupling designs that add parameterized modules, increase structural complexity, and fail to support tasks requiring deep multimodal feature fusion such as cross-modal retrieval. Discrete flow matching (DFM) offers a parallel denoising paradigm with bidirectional information integration, but its potential for unified omnimodal understanding, generation, and retrieval remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved multimodal sequences where images are encoded at 256×256 (pre-training) or 384×384 (continued pre-training) with 16× downsampling, video is decomposed into 8 uniformly sampled frames, and audio is chunked to 15-second segments, all within a 16K context window. Outputs include text responses, discrete token sequences decoded into images/videos/audio via modality-specific heads, and <EOS>-token features for retrieval ranking. A key design choice is extracting continuous representative vectors from VQVAE codebooks (rather than using raw discrete tokens) and projecting them to align dimensionally with text embeddings before feeding into the backbone.

## Method

NExT-OMNI replaces the autoregressive backbone with discrete flow matching using metric-induced probability paths (cosine-distance-based softmax interpolation) and kinetic optimal velocities, enabling parallel denoising of entire sequences rather than sequential token prediction. The architecture employs a single unified encoder per modality (CLIP-ViT-Large for vision, Whisper-Turbo for audio) trained with combined VQVAE reconstruction and semantic alignment losses, eliminating the need for separate understanding/generation encoders or additional diffusion heads. Training uses a three-stage progressive curriculum (pre-training → continued pre-training → SFT) with a composite objective balancing DFM cross-entropy loss against modality reconstruction losses via GradNorm, preserving fine-grained information that pure semantic alignment would discard. Two inference accelerations are introduced: a dynamic generation strategy that pads responses to block-size multiples during training and adjusts generation length at inference based on <EOS> confidence, and a vanilla adaptive cache that selectively updates instruction and response features based on cosine similarity, yielding 1.2× speedup over AR baselines.

## Results

On omnimodal understanding benchmarks (OmniBench, WorldSense, AV-Odyssey), NExT-OMNI achieves an average score of 39.7, outperforming the strongest prior open-source model OpenOmni (36.5) by 3.2 points and VITA-1.5 (33.9) by 5.8 points. On multi-turn speech interaction (Spoken QA), it reaches 62.0 (S→T) and 47.4 (S→S), surpassing Stream-Omni (60.3/46.3). On the OpenING interleaved image-text generation benchmark, NExT-OMNI scores 55.0 average, exceeding SEED-X (50.2) and Anole (50.4). On multimodal retrieval (InfoSeek, OVEN, FashionIQ, CIRR), it achieves 32.9 average Top-5 accuracy, the best among all compared unified models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NExT-OMNI outperforms all 28 compared baselines on the omnimodal understanding average (39.7 vs. OpenOmni's 36.5, a +3.2 gap), with the largest margins over AR-based models like NExT-GPT (+5.8) and OneLLM (+12.1). On multi-turn speech interaction, it leads Stream-Omni by +1.7 on S→T and +1.1 on S→S, though it trails OpenOmni slightly on S→S (47.4 vs. 48.1). On the OpenING interleaved generation benchmark, NExT-OMNI (55.0) surpasses the best AR-based unified model SEED-X (50.2) by 4.8 points and the discrete-diffusion model MMaDA (47.7) by 7.3 points. On multimodal retrieval, NExT-OMNI (32.9 avg) beats the closest DFM-based competitor FUDOKI (30.5) by 2.4 points and the unified-representation AR model Show-o (30.6) by 2.3 points, confirming that the combination of DFM paradigm and unified representations yields the strongest retrieval features. The paper also demonstrates a 1.2× inference speedup over AR architectures via adaptive caching and parallel decoding, while maintaining or exceeding AR-level quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

NExT-OMNI builds directly on the discrete flow matching (DFM) framework of Shaul et al. (2024), which generalizes continuous flow matching to discrete token spaces via metric-induced probability paths and kinetic optimal velocities, and on VQVAE-based discrete tokenization for multimodal representation. It departs from the dominant autoregressive paradigm used in prior unified multimodal models (SEED, Emu, Janus, VILA-U) and from hybrid/decoupled architectures (Show-o, Bagel, BLIP3-o) that separate understanding and generation into distinct modules. The key conceptual departure is combining DFM with a single unified encoder (rather than decoupled or MoE-routed encoders) to produce representations that simultaneously support generation, understanding, and—uniquely—cross-modal retrieval, extending the scope of unified multimodal modeling beyond the binary understanding/generation split.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that DFM-based unified architectures can match or exceed AR-based models on understanding and generation while unlocking cross-modal retrieval as a first-class capability, suggesting a broader applicability for unified multimodal models than previously assumed. The ablation finding that unified representations initially degrade understanding but recover and surpass AR baselines when combined with dynamic generation strategy and reconstruction losses provides actionable guidance for future unified model design. However, the 7B parameter scale and 2T token training budget limit confidence in whether these architectural advantages persist at frontier scale, and the paper explicitly acknowledges that full potential requires stronger LLM foundation support. Open questions include whether DFM's parallel denoising advantage translates to video generation quality at longer durations, and whether the retrieval capability generalizes to more complex information-seeking tasks beyond the M-BEIR subset.
