## Problem

Directly trained Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) inherit the robustness vulnerabilities of ANNs through their reliance on Backpropagation Through Time, yet no unified theoretical analysis has identified the specific architectural factor limiting their adversarial robustness. Existing defenses (adversarial training, certified training, gradient sparsity regularization) are either computationally expensive or only partially effective, leaving a gap in understanding why SNNs fail under adversarial perturbations and how to systematically address the root cause.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100) or event-based data (DVS-CRIAF10) encoded into spike trains over T=4 time steps, and outputs a class label via the final layer's spike pattern. The TGO method is architecture-agnostic (tested on WRN-16 and VGG-11) and training-strategy-agnostic (compatible with vanilla BPTT, adversarial training, and regularized adversarial training), with no additional computational overhead at inference time since the noisy-LIF and membrane potential constraints only affect training.

## Method

The paper theoretically identifies threshold-neighboring spiking neurons as the key robustness bottleneck: they inflate the Jacobian norm (raising the theoretical upper bound of adversarial attack strength) and exhibit high state-flipping probability under minor perturbations. To address this, the proposed Threshold Guarding Optimization (TGO) method combines two mechanisms: (1) a membrane potential constraint added to the loss function that penalizes neurons whose potentials fall within a margin δ of the firing threshold, with a dynamically scheduled Lagrange multiplier λ following a cosine annealing curve to balance exploration and constraint enforcement; and (2) replacement of deterministic LIF neurons with Noisy-LIF neurons that inject Gaussian white noise into the membrane potential, transitioning spike generation from a binary threshold crossing to a probabilistic firing mechanism that monotonically reduces state-flipping probability as noise variance σ increases. The two components work synergistically—constraints push most neurons away from the threshold while noisy-LIF buffers the residual threshold-neighboring neurons that remain critical for classification.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with WRN-16 under BPTT training, TGO improves FGSM accuracy from 14.05% (Vanilla) to 51.40% and PGD-7 from 0.00% to 11.67%, at a clean-accuracy cost of ~4.5%. Under RAT training, TGO+RAT achieves 69.16% FGSM and 54.59% PGD-7 accuracy, surpassing the RAT baseline (42.02% and 23.90%) by 27 and 31 percentage points respectively. On CIFAR-100 with WRN-16 under AT, TGO reaches 41.99% FGSM and 27.78% PGD-7, outperforming both SR (28.27%/21.93%) and DLIF (25.90%/15.20%). The method also generalizes to neuromorphic data, improving PGD-7 accuracy by 4.1% on DVS-CRIAF10 over standard AT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Vanilla BPTT baseline (Deng et al., 2022), TGO yields the largest absolute gains: on CIFAR-10 WRN-16, FGSM accuracy jumps from 14.05% to 51.40% (+37.3 pp) and PGD-7 from 0.00% to 11.67%, confirming that standard directly-trained SNNs are nearly completely defeated by iterative attacks. Compared to AT (Kundu et al., 2021), TGO+AT consistently wins across all attack strengths on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100; for example, on CIFAR-100 WRN-16 under AT, TGO achieves 41.99% FGSM vs. 21.18% for AT alone (+20.8 pp). Against RAT (Ding et al., 2022), TGO+RAT similarly dominates: on CIFAR-10 WRN-16, PGD-7 accuracy rises from 23.90% to 54.59% (+30.7 pp). On CIFAR-100, TGO outperforms both DLIF (Ding et al., 2024a) and SR/StoG (Ding et al., 2024b) under all three training strategies; under WRN-16 AT, TGO reaches 41.99% FGSM and 27.78% PGD-7 versus 25.90%/15.20% (DLIF) and 28.27%/21.93% (SR). The only consistent trade-off is a 3–5% drop in clean accuracy relative to the corresponding baseline, which the authors attribute to the membrane-potential constraint pushing neurons away from the threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on adversarial robustness of directly trained SNNs, which has previously relied on adapting ANN strategies (adversarial training, regularized adversarial training) or developing spike-specific regularizers (gradient sparsity, stochastic gating, DLIF). Its key departure is providing a unified theoretical analysis that identifies threshold-neighboring neurons as the structural bottleneck for SNN vulnerability, then proposing a two-component TGO method (membrane potential constraints plus noisy-LIF neurons) that addresses both the gradient-norm upper bound and the state-flipping probability simultaneously. Unlike prior spike-specific defenses that require synergistic integration with AT or RAT, TGO is designed to be composable with any training strategy while incurring no additional inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical framing—linking adversarial upper bounds directly to the count of threshold-neighboring neurons—gives SNN practitioners a concrete, interpretable target for robustness optimization beyond black-box adversarial training. The practical finding that TGO is compatible with vanilla BPTT (no adversarial training overhead) and adds no inference cost makes it attractive for edge neuromorphic deployment. However, evaluation is limited to CIFAR-10/100 and DVS-CRIAF10 with only WRN-16 and VGG-11, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger architectures, ImageNet-scale data, or real hardware where stochastic noise injection may be costly. The interplay between the noise level σ in Noisy-LIF and the constraint margin δ also warrants further study for principled hyperparameter selection.
