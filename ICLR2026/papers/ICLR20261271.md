## Problem

Existing SE(p)-invariant distribution comparison methods face a fundamental trade-off: extrinsic approaches (Wasserstein Procrustes, SPM) incur high computational costs from iterative OT-orthogonal alignment, intrinsic approaches (GW, GH) are NP-hard or O(n³)–O(n⁴), and representation-based methods (SHR, RIT) may only yield pseudometrics. No prior method simultaneously achieves rigorous metric guarantees, SE(p)-invariance, cross-space comparability, and near-linear computational complexity.

## Input / Output

The method takes two point clouds as sample matrices X ∈ R^{n×p} and Y ∈ R^{m×q} (potentially in different ambient dimensions) along with probability weight vectors in the (n−1)- and (m−1)-simplex. It produces a scalar SEINT distance via a min-max formulation over couplings and random reference measures, with intermediate 1D DcPTD feature vectors of length n and m. The reference measures are discrete uniform distributions on k i.i.d. samples from Unif(0, M), where M is set to mean(‖x‖) + std(‖x‖) for robustness to outliers.

## Method

SEINT constructs SE(p)-invariant 1D features in two stages: (1) Polar Transport Discrepancy (PTD) solves a 1D OT problem between the norm distribution ‖x‖_X and a random reference measure μ_Z on R, yielding per-point values ζ(x) = E[|‖x‖ − z | Z=x] under the optimal coupling; (2) Distance-convoluted PTD (DcPTD) convolves ζ with the pairwise distance matrix C^X to inject intrinsic geometric structure, producing φ(x) = ∫ d_X(x,x') ζ(x') dμ_X(x'). The SEINT distance is then defined as inf_π sup_{μ_Z} (E_π[|φ_X(x) − φ_Y(y)|^p])^{1/p}, where the sup over reference measures enforces a worst-case (Max-SW-like) criterion. The paper proves this defines a true metric on isometry classes of normed spaces (not merely a pseudometric), with O(n log n) complexity when the distance matrix is decomposable (e.g., squared Euclidean) and O(n²) in general. An integrated variant (ISEINT) replaces the sup with an expectation over reference measures, analogous to standard Sliced Wasserstein.

## Results

On the ModelNet40-SE(3) benchmark, SEINT achieves 100% classification accuracy at all k-NN settings (k=1, 5, 10) while running in 9.01 hours, making it the fastest method with perfect SE(p)-invariance. In contrast, the only other SE(p)-invariant baselines (RISGW) drop to 50.0% at k=10 and require 400.6 hours, while non-invariant methods (W2, SW, SGW) score below 60% and are slower. As a regularizer for molecular generation, SEINT at weight 0.3 boosts UniGEM's molecular stability from 89.4% to 93.5% and atomic stability to 99.3% on QM9, achieving SOTA on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEINT decisively outperforms all baselines on the SE(p)-invariance task: it achieves 100% accuracy at every k-NN setting, while the only other SE(p)-invariant method (RISGW) degrades to 50.0% at k=10 and is 44x slower (400.6h vs 9.01h). GW and EGW, though theoretically SE(p)-invariant, are computationally infeasible (2655.9h and 2644.3h respectively) and their results are not reported. Non-invariant baselines (W2, SW, SGW) all fall below 60% accuracy at k=1 and are slower than SEINT. In the molecular generation setting, SEINT as a regularizer consistently improves over the L2-Norm baseline: on UniGEM pre-training, SEINT_0.3 raises molecular stability from 89.4% (baseline) to 93.5% and validity from 94.6% to 96.9%, while on GEOM-Drugs fine-tuning it boosts molecular stability from 1.2% to 13.0%. The only trade-off is a slight drop in uniqueness (V*U) at higher SEINT weights, which the authors justify as acceptable given the substantial validity gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEINT belongs to the representation-based family of SE(p)-invariant OT methods but departs from prior work (SHR, RIT, learned descriptors) by constructing training-free 1D scalar features via Polar Transport Discrepancy and its distance-convoluted variant, then comparing them with 1D Wasserstein distance. Conceptually, the sup-over-reference-measures structure mirrors Max-Sliced Wasserstein, while the DcPTD convolution with the intrinsic distance matrix borrows the geometric-fidelity principle of Gromov-Wasserstein. The key departure from all three prior families (extrinsic, intrinsic, representation) is achieving a provable metric on isometry classes at O(n log n)–O(n²) cost, a combination no existing method provides simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEINT fills a concrete gap in the SE(p)-invariant distance landscape: it is the first method to jointly guarantee metric properties, cross-space comparability, and near-linear scalability, making it practical for large-scale point cloud and molecular workloads where GW is intractable. Its dual role as both a classification metric and a generative-model regularizer (with SOTA molecular stability on QM9/GEOM-Drugs) broadens its applicability beyond pure distance computation. Open questions include whether the continuous formulation retains convexity, how the random reference-measure sampling affects stability at very small n, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-Euclidean or unbalanced settings.
