## Problem

Fourier-transform-based adapters offer greater representational capacity than low-rank adapters (LoRA) for PEFT, but their direct application to quantized models yields worse performance than LoRA-based QA-PEFT methods because they lack quantization-aware initialization. No prior work has explored how to effectively integrate FT-based adapters into the QA-PEFT setting, where reducing quantization error prior to fine-tuning is critical for accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a quantized LLM weight matrix (2–4 bit, group size 64), calibration activations from WikiText-2 (128 sequences of length 2048) used to compute the Hessian-weighted reduction, and a precomputed WHT matrix H of dimension d_in × d_in. It outputs a fine-tuned quantized model whose only trainable parameters are sparse coefficient values c ∈ ℝ^p and their index locations E ∈ ℕ^{p×2} within the adapter matrix F, with the WHT kernel H held constant throughout training and inference. The parameter budget is fixed at P(r=64) across all linear layers.

## Method

QWHA introduces a WHT-based adapter (WHA) that formulates the weight update as ΔW = FH⁻¹ using a single transform (rather than the conventional double transform), where F is a sparse matrix of trainable coefficients. The WHT kernel, consisting solely of ±1 entries, enables computation via additions and subtractions only, and its square-wave basis functions are structurally better aligned with the heavy-tailed outlier structure of quantization errors than the sinusoidal bases of DCT or DHT. For quantization-aware initialization, AdaAlloc adaptively allocates the parameter budget across output channels in proportion to their activation error magnitudes (with a temperature hyperparameter), guaranteeing at least two parameters per channel to maintain full rank while concentrating more parameters on high-error channels; within each channel, the highest-magnitude WHT coefficients are selected. A value refinement step then re-projects the target error vector onto the selected basis rows to account for inter-basis interactions, yielding substantially lower layer output error than naively reusing dense-solution values.

## Results

QWHA consistently outperforms all baselines across three model families (LLaMA-3.1-8B, LLaMA-3.2-3B, Mistral-7B-v0.3) at 4-bit, 3-bit, and 2-bit quantization on both CSQA and GSM8k. The advantage is most pronounced at 2-bit, where QWHA achieves 60.98% CSQA and 37.83% GSM8k on LLaMA-3.1-8B, exceeding the best baseline (LoCA at 56.71%/33.97%) by over 4 points. Training time for QWHA (3.9 h on Alpaca, batch size 16) is comparable to LoRA-based CLoQ (3.6 h) and sparse SHiRA (3.7 h), while being roughly 2× faster than FT-based baselines SSH (8.3 h) and LoCA (9.8 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QWHA outperforms all five baselines across every model and bit-width setting. Against the strongest LoRA-based QA-PEFT method CLoQ, QWHA gains 4.49 points on CSQA and 3.94 points on GSM8k at 2-bit for LLaMA-3.1-8B (60.98 vs. 56.49; 37.83 vs. 33.89), with similar margins on the other models. Among FT-based adapters, QWHA beats LoCA (DCT) by 4.27 CSQA points and 3.86 GSM8k points at 2-bit on LLaMA-3.1-8B, and SSH (DHT) by 4.92 and 7.28 points respectively, confirming that the WHT kernel and AdaAlloc initialization are critical for quantization error reduction. The non-FT sparse adapter SHiRA (random selection) trails QWHA by 9.14 CSQA points at 2-bit, highlighting the importance of both the transform structure and quantization-aware initialization. Notably, without QA initialization, FT-based adapters (LoCA, SSH) perform comparably to or worse than LoRA-based CLoQ, underscoring that the initialization scheme—not just the adapter architecture—drives the gains. On training efficiency, QWHA matches CLoQ and SHiRA in wall-clock time (3.9 h vs. 3.6 h and 3.7 h at batch size 16) while being ~2× faster than SSH and LoCA, thanks to the single-transform WHT design and its addition/subtraction-only kernel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: quantization-aware PEFT (QA-PEFT), which has been exclusively explored with low-rank adapters (LoRA, CLoQ, RA-LoRA), and Fourier-transform-based sparse adapters (FourierFT/DFT, LoCA/DCT, SSH/DHT), which have only been applied to full-precision fine-tuning. QWHA is the first work to bring FT-based adapters into the QA-PEFT setting, identifying that their direct application to quantized models fails without a quantization-aware initialization. The adapter design departs from prior FT-based adapters by adopting WHT (whose ±1 kernel enables addition-only computation) and reducing from a double transform to a single transform, while the AdaAlloc initialization draws on the Hessian-weighted calibration framework from GPTQ and CLoQ but solves a channel-wise sparse approximation problem rather than a low-rank SVD-based one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QWHA provides a practical path to combining the superior representational capacity of FT-based adapters (near-full-rank vs. rank-r for LoRA) with the quantization error compensation needed for low-bit inference, at a training cost comparable to LoRA. The finding that FT-based adapters without quantization-aware initialization underperform LoRA-based QA-PEFT methods is a useful cautionary result for the community. Limitations include evaluation only on 3B–8B models, two task families (commonsense QA and arithmetic), and a single quantization scheme (GPTQ+MagR); whether the single-transform design and WHT kernel generalize to larger models, other quantization methods (e.g., SpinQuant, AWQ), or non-Transformer architectures remains open.
