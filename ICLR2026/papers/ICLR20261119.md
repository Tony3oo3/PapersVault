## Problem

Existing 3D-QA benchmarks like SQA3D cannot distinguish genuine 3D spatial reasoning from linguistic shortcut learning: a language model fine-tuned on text-only QA pairs (a 'blind' model) matches or surpasses state-of-the-art 3D-LLMs on these benchmarks. This exposes that reported progress in 3D-LLMs may be illusory, driven by dataset biases and guessable answers rather than true spatial understanding, and no prior benchmark or training strategy addresses this gap.

## Input / Output

The evaluated 3D-LLMs take a 3D point cloud of a ScanNet scene plus a text prompt containing an egocentric situation description (e.g., 'I am facing the trash can, with a table behind me') and a spatial question, and produce a short text answer. Evaluation uses standard Exact Match (EM) and Refined EM (EM_R), plus the novel Viewpoint Rotation Score (VRS), which groups each question with three rotated variants (90°, 180°, 270°) and scores the fraction of batches where the model answers at least k of 4 variants correctly, averaged over k=1..4. The Real-3DQA test set contains only questions that survive both model-based filtering (correct for both the 3D-LLM and its blind counterpart) and GPT-based filtering, ensuring 3D dependency.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary contributions. First, Real-3DQA is constructed by (a) filtering out questions answered correctly by both a 3D-LLM and its text-only blind fine-tuned variant across three models (LEO, Chat-Scene, 3D-LLM), taking the union of filtered sets, then (b) removing any remaining questions that GPT-4o-mini can answer from text alone; the surviving questions are augmented via GPT-generated viewpoint rotations (90°/180°/270°) with expert-verified quality control. Second, 3D-aware Reweighted Fine-tuning (3DR-FT) defines a per-token reweighting function w_j as the ratio of the blind model's surprise to the current model's surprise on the ground-truth token, upweighting tokens that are harder to predict from text alone. The resulting loss multiplies the standard cross-entropy by w_j, pushing the model to increase its conditional-independence gap δ_j (the degree to which the 3D input shifts the answer distribution), thereby forcing reliance on 3D context rather than linguistic priors.

## Results

On the proposed Real-3DQA benchmark, all five evaluated 3D-LLMs suffer dramatic performance drops compared to SQA3D: GPT4Scene falls from 60.6% to 33.1% EM, Chat-Scene from 54.4% to 17.0%, LEO from 49.4% to 14.3%, 3D-LLM from 47.8% to 7.5%, and Chat-3D v2 from 45.0% to 3.4%. Viewpoint Rotation Scores (VRS) are uniformly low, ranging from 6.6% (Chat-3D v2) to 18.2% (GPT4Scene), with all models dropping to near-zero (≤0.5%) when required to answer all four rotated variants correctly. The proposed 3DR-FT training strategy recovers substantial performance on Real-3DQA, improving LEO from 19.1% to 29.3% EM_R and Chat-Scene from 22.1% to 33.9% EM_R over standard supervised fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates five 3D-LLM baselines (3D-LLM, Chat-3D v2, LEO, Chat-Scene, GPT4Scene) on both the original SQA3D and the proposed Real-3DQA benchmark. On Real-3DQA, GPT4Scene is the strongest baseline at 33.1% EM, but still drops 27.5 points from its SQA3D score, while the remaining four models collapse to below 20% EM. On the Viewpoint Rotation Score, GPT4Scene leads at 18.2% VRS, followed by LEO (14.3%), Chat-Scene (12.9%), 3D-LLM (9.6%), and Chat-3D v2 (6.6%); all models fail to maintain consistency across even two rotated viewpoints (≤14.3% correct on 2+ rotations). The proposed 3DR-FT strategy outperforms both standard SFT and Blind FT on Real-3DQA for both LEO (29.3 vs. 19.1 vs. 13.6 EM_R) and Chat-Scene (33.9 vs. 22.1 vs. 14.4 EM_R), confirming that reweighting toward 3D-dependent questions yields consistent gains. Notably, 3DR-FT slightly reduces scores on the original SQA3D (e.g., Chat-Scene 57.2→48.9), which the authors attribute to the model abandoning linguistic shortcuts that inflated prior benchmark scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SQA3D (Ma et al., ICLR 2023) as its foundational benchmark, repurposing its scene graphs and question structure while introducing a model-based filtering pipeline that contrasts full 3D-LLMs against their text-only (blind) fine-tuned counterparts to remove linguistically shortcut-solvable questions. It departs from prior debiasing approaches in 2D VQA (Agrawal et al., Niu et al.) that rely on rule-based or distributional balancing, instead using a consistency-based model comparison that is architecture-agnostic. On the training side, it extends the standard supervised fine-tuning paradigm used by LEO, Chat-Scene, and other 3D-LLMs with a 3D-aware reweighted loss (3DR-FT) that upweights tokens where the blind model is most surprised, drawing on information-theoretic surprise functions to explicitly penalize textual shortcut learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that text-only models can match or surpass 3D-LLMs on SQA3D, and that performance drops by over 60% once 3D-independent questions are filtered, fundamentally challenges the community's interpretation of progress in 3D spatial reasoning. The Real-3DQA benchmark and Viewpoint Rotation Score provide a more rigorous evaluation that is likely to become a standard diagnostic tool, while 3DR-FT offers a practical, architecture-agnostic training strategy. Key limitations include the benchmark's derivation from ScanNet scenes, the use of only four rotation angles, and the model-dependence of the filtering step. Open questions include whether the shortcut problem extends to 3D grounding and captioning tasks (which currently lack egocentric formulations), whether more rotation angles or continuous viewpoint changes would reveal further fragility, and whether architectural changes (e.g., rotation-invariant encoders) can address the systemic failure in viewpoint consistency.
