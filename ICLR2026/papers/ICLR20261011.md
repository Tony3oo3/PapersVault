## Problem

High-quality numeric labels for regression are scarce and expensive, while pairwise rankings from domain experts or LLMs are cheaper to collect. Prior post-training enhancement methods (RankRefine, Projection) fuse regressor and ranker outputs via heuristic rules (inverse-variance weighting or interval projection) without a principled probabilistic framework, and the underlying Bradley-Terry model suffers from a fundamental scale mismatch that causes performance degradation when the reference set is large.

## Input / Output

Given a pretrained regressor's point prediction and pointwise uncertainty (σ²_re), a query item, and binary pairwise rankings from an external ranker comparing the query to k reference items with known scalar labels, the method produces a refined scalar prediction and an approximate posterior variance. It operates as a plug-and-play post-training step requiring no regressor retraining, and remains effective with as few as k=3 references. The ranker can be an oracle (for controlled experiments) or an off-the-shelf LLM (ChatGPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4) prompted to compare SMILES strings.

## Method

RANKREFINE++ frames regression enhancement as Bayesian inference: the posterior over the unknown scalar is proportional to a Gaussian regressor likelihood times a Bradley-Terry ranker likelihood times a prior, yielding a strictly log-concave objective solvable by fast Newton iterations. The paper identifies a fundamental failure mode where the BT sigmoid's soft-count equation targets a pseudo ground truth outside the feasible interval (Lemma 3.4), and the rank Fisher information grows linearly with k (Lemma 3.5), causing the rank term to dominate the Newton step and bias the estimate. To fix this, a temperature τ is estimated via a one-parameter logistic fit on the labeled reference set to align the sigmoid slope with label units, and an accuracy-aware soft gate τ(a) = 1 + (τ_cal − 1)(max(0, 2a−1))^γ smoothly interpolates between no calibration (a≈0.5) and full calibration (a→1) based on the estimated ranker accuracy. The paper further proves that the prior state-of-the-art RankRefine is a special case under a Gaussian approximation of the BT likelihood (Proposition 3.3), unifying the two approaches within a single Bayesian framework.

## Results

Across 12 cross-domain datasets, RANKREFINE++ (MLE-GatedTemp) achieves a 19.33% median MAE reduction using 30 reference samples and a ranker with 65% accuracy, translating to a 97.65% relative improvement over RankRefine's 9.78% median MAE reduction. With LLM rankers (ChatGPT5, Claude4) at N=50, k=20, RANKREFINE++ achieves β values of 0.853–0.997 across five ADMET datasets, consistently outperforming RankRefine (β = 0.951–1.070) and Projection (β = 0.949–1.153). The method runs in under 1 ms per query on a consumer-grade CPU (Intel i7-13700) for reference set sizes up to k=1000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RANKREFINE++ consistently outperforms RankRefine (Wijaya et al., 2025), the prior state-of-the-art post-training enhancement method, across all nine TDC ADMET datasets with oracle rankers (Figure 3) and on 9 out of 10 LLM-ranker dataset configurations in Table 1. The single exception is Claude4 on Solubility, where RankRefine (β=0.988) marginally beats RANKREFINE++ (β=0.990). Against the Projection baseline (Yan et al., 2024), RANKREFINE++ wins decisively: Projection frequently yields β > 1 (i.e., worse than the unrefined regressor), particularly with LLM rankers (e.g., β=1.153 for Claude4 on FreeSolv), while RANKREFINE++ maintains β < 1 in nearly all settings. The paper further proves that RankRefine is a strict special case of RANKREFINE++ under a Gaussian approximation (Proposition 3.3), verified empirically with mean absolute prediction differences at machine-epsilon level (Table 2). Gains over both baselines are largest in the mid-accuracy regime (65%–90% ranker accuracy), which corresponds to realistic LLM performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes post-training regression enhancement via pairwise rankings as a Bayesian inference problem, building directly on RankRefine (Wijaya et al., 2025) and the Projection method (Yan et al., 2024). It proves that RankRefine's inverse-variance weighting is a special case of the proposed Gaussian-Bradley-Terry posterior under a local quadratic approximation, thereby unifying the prior heuristic fusion into a principled likelihood framework. The key conceptual departure is identifying a fundamental failure mode in the uncalibrated Bradley-Terry model—scale mismatch between sigmoid soft-counts and hard rank counts that causes curvature dominance at large reference set sizes—and addressing it with temperature calibration and accuracy-aware soft gating. The method also connects to the LLM-as-a-judge literature by demonstrating that off-the-shelf LLMs serve as practical, if imperfect, pairwise rankers for molecular property prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is highly practical: it requires only 3 reference samples, runs in under 1 ms on a consumer CPU, and is plug-and-play (no regressor retraining), making it immediately deployable in data-scarce scientific domains like drug discovery and materials science. The identification of the Bradley-Terry scale-mismatch failure mode provides a general diagnostic for any application of pairwise comparison models to continuous-scale regression, with implications beyond this specific setting. Open questions include handling systematic (non-uniform) biases in real rankers, extending to correlated multi-target regression, and developing adaptive reference-set selection strategies. The dependence on reliable regressor uncertainty estimates remains a practical hurdle for off-the-shelf models that lack calibrated confidence.
