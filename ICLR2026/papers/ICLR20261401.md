## Problem

Modern probabilistic models exhibit a stark and unexplained divergence in robustness to low-quality training data: autoregressive language models and large-scale classifiers remain resilient even at 50% corruption, while class-conditional diffusion models degrade catastrophically under identical conditions. Prior work documented these observations in isolation (discriminative robustness, generative fragility) but offered no unified account of what fundamental properties drive this spectrum.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261401/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The study spans four model families with distinct I/O structures: GPT-2 (124M) maps a text token prefix to a next-token distribution; EDM diffusion (U-Net, 15.7M) maps a single class label to a 32×32 RGB image; ResNet-18 and ViT-Base map an RGB image to a class label; and encoder-decoder Transformers map a source text sequence to a target text sequence. Noise is injected at the target side (tokens, labels, or target sequences) at ratios r ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0} yielding effective error rates up to 50%, with total training compute scaled by (1+r) to hold clean-data exposure constant.

## Method

The paper combines controlled empirical experiments with a three-pronged theoretical analysis. Empirically, it trains GPT-2 on OpenWebText, EDM diffusion models on CIFAR-10/100, ResNet-18/ViT-Base on CIFAR and ImageNet subsets, and seq2seq Transformers on WMT 2014 and CNN/DailyMail, systematically injecting unstructured random noise (and structured 'noisy-teacher' noise for seq2seq) while scaling compute to isolate intrinsic robustness. The analytical framework integrates (1) information theory—quantifying residual signal via relative information loss and identifying absolute information content and conditioning richness as the two governing principles; (2) PAC learning—showing that richer conditioning lowers effective VC dimension d while absolute information content must exceed the sample complexity bound m; and (3) gradient dynamics—decomposing batch gradients into a coherent signal from correct samples and divergent, near-orthogonal noise from corrupted ones, with per-example cosine-similarity measurements confirming that larger batches systematically improve the signal-to-noise ratio, explaining why batch-size increases were necessary for convergence under high corruption.

## Results

The paper reveals a stark robustness spectrum across model families under controlled label/token corruption. GPT-2 (124M) on OpenWebText shows remarkable resilience: test NLL increases only from 2.87 to 3.59 at a 50% effective error rate (100:100 ratio). In sharp contrast, class-conditional EDM diffusion models on CIFAR-10 suffer catastrophic degradation, with image-label consistency plummeting from 94.082% to 40.630% under the same 50% corruption. Large-scale classification (ViT-Base on ImageNet-1000) is nearly impervious, with accuracy actually improving slightly from 73.784% to 74.778% at 50% noise, while smaller CIFAR-10 classification degrades moderately from 95.30% to 85.35%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against baselines; rather, it conducts a controlled comparative study across three model families (autoregressive LMs, class-conditional diffusion, and image classifiers) under identical noise protocols. The central comparison is cross-architectural: at a 50% effective error rate, GPT-2's test NLL rises only ~25% (2.87→3.59), while EDM diffusion on CIFAR-10 loses 56.8% of its label consistency (94.1%→40.6%), and ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 loses ~10.5% accuracy (95.3%→85.4%). Scaling the classifier to ImageNet-1000 (1.28M samples) eliminates degradation entirely, with accuracy even improving slightly, demonstrating that absolute information content can overcome noise. The sequence-to-sequence comparison further validates the conditioning-richness hypothesis: CNN/DailyMail (2343-token context) degrades only 17.9% in NLL versus 31.5% for WMT 2014 (153-token context) at the same 50% target corruption rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing model-specific engineering approach to noise robustness (noise-robust loss functions, label correction, architectural patches for diffusion models) by conducting a systematic comparative investigation across autoregressive, diffusion, and discriminative model families. It synthesizes three established theoretical lenses—information theory (Shannon; Tishby & Zaslavsky), PAC learning (Valiant; Kearns & Vazirani), and SGD gradient dynamics (Keskar et al.; Zhu et al.)—into a unified analytical framework, which the authors claim is the first such cross-perspective synthesis applied to explain the robustness divergence. Empirically, it builds on the documented resilience of classifiers to label noise (Rolnick et al., 2018) and the fragility of diffusion models (Na et al., 2023), but reframes these as two ends of a spectrum governed by two identifiable principles rather than as isolated phenomena requiring separate fixes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-principle framework (richness of conditioning information and absolute information content) offers practitioners a principled way to estimate data quality requirements for a given model: if the conditioning signal is information-rich relative to the target, data quality constraints can be relaxed; if sparse, a larger volume of high-quality data is necessary. Key limitations include the reliance on unstructured random noise (though structured noise is partially validated), training from scratch rather than the dominant pretrain-then-finetune paradigm, and computational constraints that limited diffusion experiments to CIFAR-scale data. Open questions include how these principles interact with correlated or input-dependent noise, whether the findings transfer to the fine-tuning regime where pre-trained representations act as a confound, and whether the gradient-averaging mechanism holds at the scale of modern trillion-parameter training runs.
