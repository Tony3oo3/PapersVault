## Problem

Current Spoken Language Models (SLMs) generate spoken responses without any internal reasoning process, unlike humans who silently reason before speaking. While generating a full chain-of-thought (CoT) before speaking improves answer quality, it introduces uncontrollable latency since the CoT length is unpredictable. The paper asks: can an SLM perform unspoken reasoning simultaneously with speaking, without adding any first-packet latency?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261480/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a speech query (audio waveform) as input and produces two streams: (1) a spoken response as an audio waveform synthesized from speech tokens via a speech decoder, and (2) unspoken reasoning as text tokens that are never converted to audio. The output is structured in fixed-length chunks: N_reason=100 reasoning tokens, N_text=13 text tokens, and N_speech=26 speech tokens, where each speech chunk corresponds to roughly 2 seconds of audio. The text tokens serve as a transcription aligned with the speech tokens, while the reasoning tokens form a complete CoT when concatenated across chunks.

## Method

STITCH exploits the temporal gap between audio playback duration (~2 s per chunk) and token generation time (~0.5 s per chunk) to generate unspoken reasoning tokens 'for free' while the user listens to the current audio chunk. Two variants are proposed: STITCH-R generates a reasoning chunk before each text-speech chunk (adding N_reason tokens of first-packet latency), while STITCH-S generates the first text-speech chunk immediately and inserts reasoning chunks afterward (zero additional latency). Training data is constructed by splitting full CoT reasoning into fixed-length 100-token chunks enclosed by [SOPR]/[EOPR] tokens and interleaving them with the standard text-speech token sequence; samples where reasoning outlasts the spoken response are discarded. The model is fully fine-tuned from GLM-4-Voice-9B (LoRA was found insufficient for teaching mathematical reasoning), using cross-entropy loss over the interleaved reasoning-text-speech token sequence. At inference, the model can also accept externally generated reasoning from a stronger model, and the reasoning chunk length can be adjusted at test time by inserting [EOPR] early without retraining.

## Results

On five math reasoning QA datasets (AddSub, MultiArith, SinglEq, SVAMP, GSM8K), STITCH-S achieves 78.04% average accuracy, outperforming the GLM-4-Voice baseline (53.08%) by 24.96% and the no-reasoning fine-tuned baseline (62.98%) by 15.06%, while maintaining the same first-packet latency (N_text + N_speech tokens) as models that cannot generate reasoning. STITCH-R achieves 78.70% average accuracy with a slightly higher latency (N_reason + N_text + N_speech). On non-reasoning tasks (Llama Questions, TriviaQA, WebQuestions, AlpacaEval), STITCH-S scores 57.03% average, comparable to or slightly above the GLM-4-Voice baseline (55.22%), confirming that unspoken reasoning does not harm general performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STITCH-S (78.04% avg) outperforms GLM-4-Voice (53.08%) by 24.96% and the no-reasoning fine-tuned baseline (62.98%) by 15.06% on math QA, while matching GLM-4-Voice's first-packet latency exactly. STITCH-R (78.70%) is marginally better than STITCH-S on average but adds N_reason=100 tokens of initial latency. Both STITCH variants are within 1.1% of TBS (79.12%), which generates the full chain-of-thought before any speech and thus has uncontrollable latency. On the hardest dataset GSM8K, STITCH-S (56.72%) still outperforms GLM-4-Voice (29.00%) by 27.72% and the no-reasoning baseline (35.73%) by 20.99%. On non-reasoning tasks, STITCH-S (57.03%) slightly exceeds GLM-4-Voice (55.22%) and the no-reasoning baseline (55.19%), while STITCH-R (55.97%) is roughly on par, indicating no degradation from the reasoning-augmented training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interleaved text-speech generation paradigm of GLM-4-Voice (Zeng et al., 2024), which alternates fixed-length text and speech token chunks, by inserting unspoken chain-of-thought reasoning chunks between them. It draws on the chain-of-thought reasoning literature for text LLMs (Wei et al., 2022; OpenAI o1; DeepSeek-R1) but is the first to introduce an unspoken thinking process into spoken language models, which previously only generated text tokens that transcribe the upcoming speech. The approach is positioned against both the interleaved SLM family (GLM-4-Voice, Baichuan-Audio) and the Thinker-Talker architecture (Qwen2.5-Omni, LLaMA-Omni), with the authors choosing the interleaved design for its single-backbone simplicity and ease of fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STITCH-S's ability to match the first-packet latency of non-reasoning baselines while improving math reasoning accuracy by ~15% is practically significant for real-time speech interaction, where even small latency penalties are perceptible to users. However, the validation is limited to a single 9B base model (GLM-4-Voice) and relatively simple arithmetic word problems; whether the chunked-reasoning approach transfers to more complex reasoning, other SLM architectures, or longer-form dialogue remains open. The hardware-dependent constraint (reasoning chunk length must fit within the audio chunk duration) also limits portability across deployment environments, though the paper shows some flexibility in adjusting chunk size at inference time.
