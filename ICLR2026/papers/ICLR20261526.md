## Problem

Fine-grained image tasks, which require distinguishing objects at the subordinate category level (e.g., species, car models), are fundamental to computer vision yet remain largely unexplored in LVLM evaluation. Existing benchmarks target holistic perception or specialized tasks like OCR and visual reasoning, leaving the capability boundaries of LVLMs in fine-grained domains poorly understood.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261526/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes RGB images paired with text queries in three formats (true/false, multiple-choice, short-answer) for human-oriented evaluation, and raw images for machine-oriented retrieval and classification. The 1.01M questions are generated from rule-based templates applied to fine-grained annotations across 12 datasets, with negative samples constructed by pairing images with incorrect labels at the same hierarchical level. Outputs include text answers for conversational tasks, pairwise similarity scores for retrieval (scored by mAP), and class predictions for classification (scored by Top-1 accuracy).

## Method

FG-BMK introduces two complementary evaluation paradigms: a human-oriented track using dialogue-like interactions to test semantic recognition (attribute recognition, knowledge bias estimation, hierarchical granularity recognition), and a machine-oriented track that directly measures visual feature discriminability via image retrieval and classification following the DINOv2 protocol. The paper evaluates 12 models spanning contrastive, generative, reconstruction, and self-supervised training paradigms, then conducts controlled experiments isolating the effects of training paradigm, vision-language alignment granularity, perturbation robustness (via PGD), and vision encoder scale. Key findings show that contrastive training enhances fine-grained discriminability, aligning visual features with granularity-mismatched text degrades them, and LVLMs are more vulnerable to feature perturbations on fine-grained than generic tasks, while still lagging behind specialized fine-grained models.

## Results

On the FG-BMK benchmark, LVLMs consistently underperform fine-grained tailored models across all five classification datasets: the best LVLM linear-probe (LC) scores range from 78.88% (FGVC Aircraft) to 95.67% (Food-101), while fine-grained tailored models achieve 95.40%–98.60%. In the machine-oriented evaluation, contrastive-trained encoders (EVA-CLIP, DINOv2, InternVL) significantly outperform generative (Qwen) and reconstruction (BEiT3) paradigms on both retrieval (mAP) and classification (Top-1 accuracy) across 12 fine-grained datasets, as confirmed by Nemenyi statistical tests. Under PGD perturbations, LVLM visual features suffer severe accuracy drops on fine-grained CUB-200-2011 (e.g., EVA-CLIP: 88.95%→24.94%) compared to the more modest decline on generic CIFAR-100 (93.05%→50.76%), revealing heightened vulnerability in fine-grained settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 12 LVLMs/VLMs (InternVL3, InternVL, LLaVA-1.5, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen-VL, BLIP-2, EVA-CLIP, BEiT3, CoCa, DINOv2, GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-flash) against each other and against five fine-grained tailored models. All LVLMs lose to fine-grained tailored models on every dataset in Table 3, with the largest gap on FGVC Aircraft (78.88% LC vs. 95.40% FG-Tailored, a 16.52% deficit) and the smallest on Food-101 (95.67% vs. 98.60%, a 2.93% gap). Among LVLMs, contrastive-trained encoders dominate: EVA-CLIP and DINOv2 achieve the highest retrieval mAP and classification Top-1 accuracy, while BEiT3 (reconstruction) and Qwen (generative) rank lowest per Nemenyi tests. In multi-meta-category classification, EVA-CLIP drops only 1.96% from single to multiple categories, versus 4.16% for Qwen and 7.41% for BEiT3. DINOv2-B (smaller encoder) outperforms BEiT3-L (larger encoder) by 8.08% on CUB-200-2011 and 9.49% on Stanford Dogs, showing that training paradigm matters more than model scale. For perturbation robustness, a standard ViT trained with cross-entropy on ImageNet retains 73.85% on CUB after PGD, far exceeding EVA-CLIP (24.94%), CoCa (23.40%), and DINOv2 (25.94%), highlighting that LVLM visual features are significantly more fragile in fine-grained settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: fine-grained visual categorization (building on datasets like CUB-200-2011, Stanford Dogs, and models like CAP, MetaFormer, SR-GNN) and LVLM evaluation benchmarks (MMBench, LVLM-eHub, MathVista). It adopts the DINOv2 evaluation protocol (mAP for retrieval, Top-1 for classification) as its machine-oriented backbone, while introducing a novel dual-paradigm structure—human-oriented (conversational VQA) plus machine-oriented (retrieval/classification)—that no prior LVLM benchmark has applied specifically to fine-grained tasks. Conceptually, it repurposes existing fine-grained datasets and existing LVLMs but reframes the evaluation question: rather than asking 'can the model answer a question about an image?', it asks 'does the model's visual feature space actually encode fine-grained discriminative structure?'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FG-BMK fills a clear and previously unaddressed gap: no systematic benchmark existed for evaluating LVLMs on fine-grained tasks, despite these being fundamental to computer vision applications like biodiversity monitoring and product retrieval. The actionable findings—contrastive training outperforms generative/reconstruction for fine-grained discriminability, granularity-mismatched alignment degrades visual features, and LVLMs are more perturbation-vulnerable on fine-grained than generic tasks—provide concrete guidance for model designers. Open questions include whether incorporating contrastive objectives during the alignment stage can fully recover discriminability, how to enhance fine-grained performance without sacrificing general-task capabilities, and whether the knowledge-bias findings generalize beyond the tested model families.
