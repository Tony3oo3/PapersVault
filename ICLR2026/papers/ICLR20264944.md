## Problem

Gradient-based optimization methods are inherently local, providing no information about the global structure of the objective function landscape, which causes sensitivity to initialization and convergence to suboptimal solutions. Existing adaptive optimizers (Adam, AdamW, Muon) compensate through heuristic momentum and learning-rate schedules that lack theoretical grounding in the geometry of the optimization problem. The paper asks whether the objective function values at all critical points—global landscape information—can be extracted from purely local perturbative computations.

## Input / Output

The method takes initial model parameters, the objective function, a base learning rate, and a resurgence weight λ. During a one-time analysis phase it computes the partition function Z(g) = ∫ exp(−L(θ)/g) dθ at multiple small coupling values via a variational lower bound (training a separate neural network to maximize a concave objective), fits an asymptotic power series, and locates Borel-plane singularities on the positive real axis as target critical objective values. The output is the optimized parameter set, with the Borel singularity targets acting as a dynamic learning-rate scaling factor applied as a wrapper around any base optimizer.

## Method

The central theoretical result (Theorem 3) proves via the co-area formula that the Borel transform of the partition function Z(g) has singularities precisely at the objective function values of critical points, establishing a one-to-one correspondence between Borel-plane poles and landscape critical values. The algorithm runs in two phases: an analysis phase that computes Z(g) at several small couplings using a variational lower bound, extracts asymptotic coefficients by weighted least-squares, and detects Borel singularities via ratio test and direct evaluation on the interval (0, L₀); and an optimization phase where at each step the nearest target below the current loss determines a learning-rate multiplier α = 1 + λ·min(|(L − ζ_t)/L|, 1). This scaling amplifies the step when the optimizer is far from a known critical value (e.g., trapped in a local minimum) and gracefully degrades to the unmodified base optimizer when approaching a target. The method is optimizer-agnostic, requiring only a multiplicative factor on the learning rate, and the Borel singularity search operates in O(1) space on the positive real axis.

## Results

On MNIST (MLP, 200 epochs), SURGE-wrapped Adam achieves a best validation loss of 0.9913 at LR=3e-3, outperforming the best standard scheduler (StepLR at 1.1810) by approximately 16%. At LR=1e-2, SURGE reaches 1.1127 versus 1.2215 for ReduceOnPlateau (~9% improvement). On the Shakespeare small-transformer task (~10k parameters), SURGE-wrapped optimizers (Adam, AdamW, SGD, Muon) consistently accelerate initial convergence and enable faster escape from local minima, though the paper notes this can introduce training instability and accelerate overfitting when the base optimizer already generalizes poorly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against bare Adam (Kingma & Ba, 2017), SURGE-wrapped Adam shows faster initial convergence and quicker escape from local minima on both MNIST and Shakespeare, but the paper acknowledges it introduces training instability from aggressive learning-rate scaling and can worsen overfitting when the base optimizer already generalizes poorly. Against SGD (Robbins & Monro, 1951) and Muon (Jordan et al., 2024), the same qualitative pattern holds: SURGE acts as a drop-in wrapper that accelerates convergence in the early-to-mid training phase across all three optimizers. In the head-to-head comparison with five standard LR schedulers (Constant, StepLR, ExponentialLR, CosineAnnealing, ReduceOnPlateau) on MNIST, SURGE outperforms the best scheduler at LR=3e-3 (0.9913 vs. 1.1810, ~16% lower loss) and LR=1e-2 (1.1127 vs. 1.2215, ~9% lower), but underperforms at LR=1e-3 (1.2617 vs. 1.1417, ~10.5% higher) and LR=1e-4 (1.4284 vs. 1.4079, ~1.5% higher), indicating the benefit is most pronounced at intermediate-to-high learning rates where local minima trapping is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants resurgence theory from Écalle's complex analysis framework (1981) and the specific saddle-point/Borel-singularity correspondence established by Bhattacharya et al. (2024) into the domain of neural network optimization. It departs from the standard adaptive-optimizer lineage (Adam, AdamW, Muon) that relies on local curvature and momentum heuristics, and from Polyak-style step-size methods that require knowledge of the optimal objective value, by instead extracting global critical-point values from the divergent asymptotic structure of a statistical-mechanics partition function. The partition-function formulation itself draws on the long history of simulated annealing and Langevin dynamics in optimization, but the Borel-resurgence extraction of singularity locations as optimization targets is a new application of these tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical correspondence between Borel-plane singularities and objective-function critical values is elegant and, if it scales, would provide a principled global signal that current local optimizers fundamentally lack. However, the practical validation is confined to very small networks (a ~10k-parameter transformer on Shakespeare, a small MLP on MNIST), and the partition-function computation that underpins the method is itself an intractable high-dimensional integral that the paper addresses only with a variational lower-bound trick of unclear scalability. The acknowledged instabilities and accelerated overfitting, combined with the absence of experiments on modern-scale architectures or datasets, leave open the central question of whether the one-time analysis cost and the quality of singularity detection remain viable as parameter count grows. The paper is best read as a proof-of-concept invitation rather than a deployable method.
