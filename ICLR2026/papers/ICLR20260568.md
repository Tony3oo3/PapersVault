## Problem

Existing LLM distillation objectives operate on softmax probabilities, which blurs the teacher's rich logit-level knowledge—especially critical for large vocabularies where most tokens receive near-zero probabilities. Direct logit distillation (DLD) avoids this smoothing but fails to respect logit shift invariance (softmax is invariant to additive constants), thereby restricting the optimal solution set and hindering distillation when teacher and student have large capacity gaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260568/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw logit vectors from both teacher and student over the full vocabulary at each autoregressive step, along with two separable weighting functions w₁(·) and w₂(·) that control pairwise token relationships. The output is a trained student model whose per-token logit differences across all vocabulary pairs match those of the teacher. A key practical detail is that although the loss involves a double sum over the vocabulary (O(|V|²)), the gradient is computed in O(|V|) via analytic factorization, making it feasible for standard LLM vocabulary sizes.

## Method

CSD formulates distillation as matching log-transformed concrete scores (log probability ratios) between student and teacher, which algebraically reduces to an MSE on relative logit differences: (f_θ[x] − f_θ[y_t]) vs. (f_T[x] − f_T[y_t]) for all vocabulary pairs. The log transform resolves the training instability of raw likelihood-ratio matching and naturally yields a logit-level objective. By factorizing the pairwise weight as w(y_t,x) = w₁(y_t)·w₂(x), the gradient decomposes into a linear-time computation involving weighted-mean-centered logits, with w₁ controlling gradient update weighting and w₂ governing coefficient normalization (and vice versa in a second term). Theorem 2 proves the optimal solution set strictly contains that of DLD because CSD is invariant to additive logit shifts, and the two weighting functions provide a design space spanning mode-seeking (S,S) and mode-covering (U,S) or (T,S) behaviors along the fidelity–diversity trade-off.

## Results

On the primary task-agnostic instruction-following distillation (GPT-2-1.5B → GPT-2-0.1B), CSD achieves the highest average ROUGE-L of 20.65 across five benchmarks, outperforming the next-best method SRKL (20.00) and TV (19.97). CSD ranks first on three of five benchmarks (Dolly Eval: 24.94, Super-NI: 24.60, UnNI: 25.88) and second on Self-Instruct (12.06). In task-specific distillation (Gemma-7B-IT → Gemma-2B-IT), CSD(T,S) achieves the best scores on all three tasks: 35.67 ROUGE-L (summarization), 74.14 COMET (translation), and 25.78% accuracy (GSM8K), where several baselines collapse to 0.00.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSD outperforms all 11 probability-matching and logit-matching baselines in the primary loss-level comparison (Table 1), with the largest margins over KL (+3.97 avg ROUGE-L) and RKL (+1.77). Among the strongest f-divergence baselines, CSD beats SRKL by 0.65 and TV by 0.68 in average ROUGE-L, while also achieving higher diversity (Self-BLEU) than the mode-seeking variants. In the on-policy setting (Table 2), CSD integrated with ImitKD, GKD, and DistiLLM consistently surpasses both the original methods and their DLD-augmented counterparts across GPT-2-0.1B, GPT-2-0.3B, and OpenLLaMA-3B students (e.g., 23.12 vs. 22.32 for ImitKD+DLD on GPT-2-0.3B). In task-specific distillation (Table 3), CSD(T,S) is the only method that avoids complete collapse on GSM8K (25.78% vs. 0.00 for RKL, TV, SKL, SRKL, and DLD(S)), and it also leads on summarization (35.67 vs. 35.60 for JS) and translation (74.14 vs. 74.05 for JS). For general chat capability (Table 4), CSD(S,S) achieves the best MT-Bench score (7.69 GPT-4) for Qwen2.5-7B→1.5B, while CSD(T,S) leads for Gemma2-9B→2B (7.85 GPT-4), both surpassing DistiLLM-2 and DLD variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant f-divergence family (KL, RKL, TV, GJS, skew-KL, α-β) that has governed LLM distillation losses, instead grounding the objective in concrete score matching (Meng et al., 2022) adapted from the energy-based model literature (Hyvärinen & Dayan, 2005; Song & Kingma, 2021). It also extends direct logit distillation (Ba & Caruana, 2014) by identifying and resolving its previously unacknowledged restriction on the optimal solution set (lack of logit shift invariance), using a logarithmic transformation inspired by Higuchi & Suzuki (2025) to ensure training stability. The resulting CSD objective is positioned as a superset of DLD's solution space, with two independent weighting functions providing a flexible design space that envelopes the fidelity-diversity trade-off of prior probability-level losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSD provides a theoretically grounded, linear-time-computable loss that simultaneously avoids softmax-induced information loss and the solution-set restriction of DLD, offering practitioners a principled alternative to the f-divergence zoo. The two-weighting-function design space (w1, w2) gives a practical lever for controlling the fidelity-diversity trade-off without retraining, which is valuable for deployment. Open questions include whether joint (non-factorized) weighting functions or data-adaptive weighting (fixed vs. on-policy) can yield further gains, and whether the approach extends to settings where teacher and student do not share a vocabulary. The moderate but consistent improvements over strong baselines (e.g., ~0.65 ROUGE-L over SRKL on average) suggest the design space may still contain better instances.
