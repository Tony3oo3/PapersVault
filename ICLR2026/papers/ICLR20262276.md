## Problem

Existing image-caption error detection methods rely on a single similarity score per pair, which fails on subtle errors (e.g., a single negation word or wrong color) that still yield high alignment scores, while penalizing correct but imprecisely worded captions. No prior approach captures how a caption *evolves* under iterative refinement, a signal that distinguishes easily-fixable correct captions from fundamentally erroneous ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262276/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and its associated text caption, then produces a binary error label (correct vs. erroneous) via a lightweight classifier (XGBoost or CART) trained on trajectory feature vectors. It additionally outputs token-level error localization—identifying which specific words in the caption are most likely incorrect—and, when coupled with a VLM (InternVL3-1B or 14B), a corrected caption. The trajectory feature vector has dimension 2T+1, concatenating T+1 alignment scores and T semantic-similarity scores, where T is the number of exploration steps (typically equal to caption length for the Elimination algorithm).

## Method

TRACED generates a caption trajectory by iteratively editing the caption to maximize an image-text relevance score s (BLIP, CLIP, or LEMoN). At each of T steps, candidate edits are produced via one of three exploration strategies—Elimination (remove one token), Greedy Coordinate Descent (gradient-guided token replacement), or Fast GCD (hybrid that first identifies the most impactful token via Elimination, then explores top-K replacements for that token)—and the best candidate is selected. The trajectory is encoded as a feature vector combining alignment scores s(x_t, y) and caption-to-original semantic similarity c(x_t, x_0) at every step, which is fed to a classifier trained with 3-fold cross-validation. The core insight is that correct captions stabilize after minor edits (small alignment gains, low semantic drift), while erroneous captions undergo large alignment jumps accompanied by substantial semantic changes. The Elimination algorithm proves most effective because it reflects both positive and negative contributions of each word and operates in a constrained search space that regularizes against spurious token substitutions.

## Results

TRACED consistently improves error detection accuracy across all three datasets and all three baselines (BLIP, LEMoN, CLIP), with the Elimination algorithm yielding the best results. On Flickr30k, TRACED-CLIP achieves 85.7% accuracy (+2.8% over CLIP's 83.8%), and TRACED-BLIP(ITM) reaches 89.5% (+1.3% over BLIP's 88.5%). On MS-COCO, TRACED-BLIP(ITM) achieves 90.5% (+1.7%) and TRACED-CLIP reaches 84.5% (+2.5%). On MM-IMDb, TRACED-LEMONFIX achieves 78.3% (+2.4% over LEMoN's 76.5%). Under the most challenging fine-grained noise setting, improvements are largest, reaching up to 7.5% over baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACED is evaluated as a plug-in on top of three state-of-the-art error detection baselines: BLIP (ITM and ITC blocks), LEMoN (FIX and OPT variants), and CLIP (cosine similarity). Across all three datasets (Flickr30k, MS-COCO, MM-IMDb) and all three noise types (random, noun, fine-grained), TRACED consistently outperforms every baseline, with no instance of degradation. The largest absolute gains appear when TRACED is applied to CLIP (+2.8% on Flickr30k, +2.5% on MS-COCO) and LEMoN-FIX (+2.4% on MM-IMDb, +2.3% on MS-COCO), suggesting that simpler scoring functions benefit most from trajectory-based signals. Under the hardest fine-grained noise setting (subtle word-level errors generated by GPT-4o-mini), improvements are most pronounced, reaching up to 7.5% over baselines, while under random noise (where baselines already exceed 97% accuracy) gains are modest (0.1–0.5%). Among the three exploration algorithms, Elimination (iterative token removal) generally produces the most informative trajectories, outperforming GCD and Fast GCD in most settings, likely because it captures both positive and negative contributions of individual words in a constrained search space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on image-caption error detection (CLIP, BLIP, LEMoN) by replacing the single-similarity-score paradigm with a trajectory of iteratively refined captions. The trajectory generation adapts the Greedy Coordinate Descent (GCD) token-replacement procedure from Zou et al. (2023), originally designed for adversarial attacks on language models, to the task of improving image-caption alignment. Conceptually, the key departure is that error detection becomes a question of *how much* and *how semantically* a caption can be improved, rather than *how well* it already aligns, making the framework model-agnostic and composable with any existing scoring baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trajectory-based signal addresses a real practical gap: single-score methods fail on subtle errors (negations, wrong colors) while penalizing correct but imprecise captions, and the introduced fine-grained noise benchmark better reflects real-world annotation errors than full caption swaps. The token-level interpretability enabling guided VLM correction (up to 14.5% BLIP alignment gain) opens a path toward automated dataset cleaning pipelines. However, the detection improvements remain modest (1–3% on standard noise, up to 7.5% on fine-grained), evaluation is limited to three datasets with synthetic noise, and it is unclear whether the trajectory approach transfers to video, audio, or other multimodal settings where iterative refinement is more expensive.
