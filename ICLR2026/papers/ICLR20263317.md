## Problem

Posterior sampling (Thompson Sampling) is empirically superior to UCB-based exploration in bandit and deep RL settings, but provable regret guarantees for a natural Q-learning-based posterior sampling algorithm were missing. The closest prior result (Staged-RandQL) required a more complex design with Dirichlet posteriors on transition probabilities and randomized learning rates, rather than the straightforward approach of maintaining Gaussian posteriors directly on Q-values. This paper closes that gap by providing the first near-optimal regret bound for a simple Q-learning algorithm with Gaussian posterior sampling on Q-values.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in a tabular episodic MDP with S states, A actions, and horizon H over K episodes. At each step it receives the current state s_h, selects an action via argmax over posterior samples, and observes a scalar reward r_h and next state s_{h+1}. It maintains a Gaussian posterior N(Q̂_h(s,a), σ²/n) for every state-action pair, where the variance σ(n) = 64H³/(n+1)·log(KH/δ) shrinks with visit count n. The target for the Q-learning update is constructed optimistically by taking the maximum of J = log(SAT/δ)/log(4/(4-p₁)) posterior samples at a designated action â (argmax of posterior mean + std), rather than a single sample at the played action.

## Method

PSQL derives the Q-learning TD update rule as the posterior mean update of a Bayesian inference problem with a regularized ELBO objective that includes an entropy term λ_n·H(q), which naturally yields the learning rate α_n = (H+1)/(H+n) needed to handle bootstrapping bias. For action selection, the algorithm draws a single Gaussian sample from each action's posterior and plays the argmax (vanilla Thompson Sampling). The key design choice is in target computation: rather than using the single-sample max (which causes exponential error accumulation under bootstrapping), PSQL computes an optimistic value V̄_{h+1}(s') as the maximum of J posterior samples drawn at the action â = argmax_a Q̂_{h+1}(s',a) + σ(N(s',a)), which is shown to be close in variance to the actually played action with constant probability. This separation—single-sample for decisions, multi-sample for targets—breaks the recursive multiplicative decay of optimism probability that plagues naive posterior sampling in TD-learning, yielding a regret bound of Õ(H²√(SAT)) that closely matches the Ω(H√(SAT)) lower bound.

## Results

PSQL achieves a cumulative regret bound of Õ(H²√(SAT)) in the tabular episodic MDP setting, closely matching the known lower bound of Ω(H√(SAT)) and matching the bound of Staged-RandQL (Tiapkin et al., 2023). Empirically, the vanilla variant PSQL* (single-sample target) shows significantly lower cumulative regret than UCBQL, RLSVI, and Staged-RandQL on both chain and grid-world MDPs, with the lowest variance across 10 random instances. The theoretically analyzed PSQL (with optimistic multi-sample target) still outperforms UCBQL substantially, though slightly worse than PSQL*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSQL matches the regret bound of Staged-RandQL (Tiapkin et al., 2023) at Õ(H²√(SAT)) while being substantially simpler: PSQL uses direct Gaussian posteriors on Q-values with a single posterior sample for action selection, whereas Staged-RandQL relies on Dirichlet posteriors on transition models and randomized learning rates. PSQL improves over RLSVI (Russo, 2019) which has a suboptimal H³S^1.5 dependence, and over C-RLSVI (Agrawal et al., 2021) which has an H²S dependence. Compared to UCBQL (Jin et al., 2018), PSQL achieves a tighter H² vs. H^1.5 dependence in the theoretical bound, and empirically shows significantly lower cumulative regret with lower variance across runs. The vanilla PSQL* variant (single-sample target) empirically outperforms both Staged-RandQL and RLSVI on chain and grid-world MDPs, though its theoretical analysis remains open. PSQL is also simpler than the computationally intractable Conditional Posterior Sampling (Dann et al., 2021) and avoids the heavy re-fitting costs of RLSVI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Q-learning (Watkins & Dayan, 1992), Thompson/posterior sampling (Thompson, 1933), and the Bayesian interpretation of TD-learning. It departs from prior posterior sampling RL work (PSRL, RLSVI, Staged-RandQL) by placing Gaussian posteriors directly on Q-values rather than on transition models or via learning-rate randomization, making it the first model-free Q-learning algorithm with natural posterior sampling. The learning rate α_n = (H+1)/(H+n) from Jin et al. (2018) is re-derived here as the solution to a regularized ELBO objective with an entropy term, providing a principled Bayesian justification that was previously motivated only by analysis mechanics. The key technical departure from prior posterior sampling analyses is the recognition that the standard multiple-sampling trick for high-probability optimism causes exponential error accumulation under bootstrapping, necessitating a hybrid design where optimism is injected only in target computation rather than in action selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a notable gap by providing the first near-optimal regret guarantee (Õ(H²√(SAT))) for a simple, natural posterior sampling Q-learning algorithm, matching the lower bound up to a factor of H and matching the bound of the more complex Staged-RandQL. The conceptual insight that naive multiple posterior sampling leads to exponential-in-H error under bootstrapping is a fundamental obstacle that any future analysis of posterior sampling in TD-learning must address. Open questions include analyzing the empirically superior vanilla version (PSQL*) that uses a single sample in the target, tightening the H-dependence (a sketch is provided in Appendix F), and extending the framework to function approximation and non-tabular settings where posterior sampling is most practically relevant.
