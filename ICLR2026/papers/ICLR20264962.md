## Problem

Modern LLMs use GLU-based FFNs with SiLU/GELU activations that lack exact zero activations, making ReLU-era sparsity methods inapplicable. The literature on LLM activation sparsity is fragmented across model-specific strategies (input-based, gate-based, predictor-based) with no unified framework for comparing sparsity robustness across architectures, scales, and training recipes, and no systematic study of sparsity in diffusion-based or MoE LLMs.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes FFN activation vectors (input, up-projection, gate, intermediate) from any pretrained LLM and a scalar threshold p, producing a binary sparsity mask via the top-p rule without requiring auxiliary training, calibration data, or architectural modifications. The key output is the critical sparsity value—the maximum fraction of zeroed activations that retains at least 99% of dense-model accuracy—alongside full sparsity-performance tradeoff curves evaluated across a 9-task zero-shot benchmark suite. For diffusion LLMs, masks are computed independently at each denoising step and averaged, and temporal stability is quantified via Jaccard similarity between consecutive-step masks.

## Method

The paper introduces a training-free top-p sparsification rule that retains the minimum number of entries whose absolute values sum to at least fraction p of the vector's L1 norm, enabling a unified, model-agnostic comparison of sparsity tolerance across FFN components. It defines critical sparsity as the highest sparsity level causing at most 1% performance drop, providing a practical, performance-anchored metric. The systematic evaluation spans Gemma3 (1B–27B), LLaMA3.1/3.2 (1B–8B), Qwen2.5 (0.5B–32B), Qwen3-30B-A3B MoE, and the diffusion-based LLaDA-8B, revealing that critical sparsity increases with model size, input-based methods match or exceed gate-based approaches, instruction-tuned and reasoning models show comparable or greater sparsity tolerance, MoE experts reach sparsity levels comparable to dense models, and diffusion LLMs exhibit substantially higher critical sparsity than autoregressive counterparts of the same size.

## Results

Across Gemma3 models (1B–27B), critical sparsity on intermediate activations increases from 50.2% (1B) to 74.1% (27B) for pretrained models, and from 50.0% to 84.1% for instruction-tuned variants. For the diffusion-based LLaDA-8B, average critical sparsity reaches 68.1% (intermediate) and 56.8% (all-inputs), substantially exceeding the 51.9% intermediate sparsity of the same-size autoregressive LLaMA3.1-8B. Input-based sparsification matches or exceeds gate-based sparsity across all tested scales, with all-inputs critical sparsity reaching 66.3% (Gemma3-27B pretrained) and 74.3% (instruction-tuned). MoE experts in Qwen3-30B-A3B exhibit critical sparsity levels comparable to dense LLMs, confirming the universality of the phenomenon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the key contrasts are across activation types, model scales, and architectures. Among FFN components, intermediate activations consistently show the highest critical sparsity (e.g., 74.1% for Gemma3-27B pretrained), followed by all-inputs (66.3%), while gate, input, and up-projection sparsification cluster together at 42–53%. The paper challenges the common assumption in prior work (Song et al. 2024a; Lee et al. 2024) that gate-based sparsification is superior, showing that input-based methods match or exceed gate sparsity at all tested scales up to ~30B parameters. Comparing training recipes, instruction-tuned models show notably higher sparsity tolerance at larger scales (e.g., Gemma3-27B: 84.1% vs. 74.1% intermediate critical sparsity), while at 1B the difference is negligible. The autoregressive-to-diffusion comparison (LLaMA3.1-8B vs. LLaDA-8B) reveals that diffusion models tolerate substantially more sparsity (68.1% vs. 51.9% average intermediate), likely due to the denoising process's noise tolerance. For MoE models, Qwen3-30B-A3B experts reach sparsity levels comparable to dense LLMs of similar active parameter count, with individual experts sometimes exceeding the dense-model baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented landscape of LLM activation sparsity research, which has been studied in isolation across specific FFN components (input, gate, intermediate) using different methods (thresholding, predictors, ReLU retrofitting). It departs from prior work by introducing a single, training-free top-p sparsification rule and a unified 'critical sparsity' metric applicable to any GLU-based FFN, enabling fair cross-model and cross-component comparisons. The study also extends the analysis to two underexplored settings: diffusion-based LLMs (first such study) and MoE expert-level sparsity, building on prior diffusion-model caching work (Ma et al., 2024) and MoE routing literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that critical sparsity grows consistently with model size across Gemma, LLaMA, and Qwen families suggests activation sparsity will become an increasingly important efficiency lever as frontier models scale, with practical implications already visible in releases like Gemma3n. The result that input-based sparsification matches or exceeds gate-based methods challenges a common design assumption in prior acceleration work and points toward simpler, training-free approaches. Open questions include whether sparsity patterns can be exploited in attention layers, whether diffusion LLMs admit step-reuse strategies given their low temporal mask stability, and how to design sparsification methods that are truly data-free given the observed task-dependence.
