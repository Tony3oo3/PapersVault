## Problem

Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for image super-resolution merely tune quantizer parameters on fixed weights, leaving the model unable to actively compensate for the severe information loss under extreme low-bit compression (2-bit, 1-bit). Moreover, Mamba-specific quantization methods (PTQ4VM, Quamba, MambaQuant) were validated on classification or language tasks and fail to meet the pixel-level fidelity demands of super-resolution, producing blurred textures and lost fine details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263527/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained super-resolution backbone (MambaIRv2-light or SwinIR-light) and compresses its weights to 4-bit, 2-bit, or 1-bit precision. At inference, a low-resolution RGB image (×2 or ×4 scale factor) is fed into the quantized model to produce a high-resolution reconstruction. All rectifier parameters are fused offline into the backbone weights, so the inference-time model structure and computational graph remain identical to the original architecture with zero additional overhead.

## Method

SPR²Q introduces two synergistic components. First, Pre-Quantization Fine-tuning with Fused Rectifier (PQFR) augments frozen backbone weights W with a low-rank increment ΔW = BA, jointly optimizing the rectifier matrices and trainable clipping bounds (a, b) under a hybrid loss combining pixel-level L1 reconstruction and block-wise L2 feature alignment, with gradients propagated via the Straight-Through Estimator. Second, Static Priority-Based Rectifier Routing extends the single rectifier into a group of N=4 rectifiers trained with dynamic input-dependent gating, then performs offline gradient-based calibration to assign a fixed optimal weighting vector per layer, producing a static routing table. At inference, each layer retrieves its precomputed optimal increment from this table and fuses it into the weights before quantization, expanding representational capacity without any runtime routing cost.

## Results

On MambaIRv2-light at 4-bit ×2, SPR²Q achieves 37.72 dB PSNR on Set5, outperforming the best baseline (PTQ4VM, 37.17 dB) by 0.55 dB. At the more challenging 2-bit ×2 setting, SPR²Q reaches 35.97 dB on Set5, a 1.31 dB gain over Quamba (34.66 dB) and 1.59 dB over PTQ4VM (34.38 dB). On the texture-rich Urban100 dataset at 4-bit ×2, SPR²Q leads by approximately 1 dB over all baselines (31.53 vs. 30.47–30.54 dB), and the gap widens to 3.45 dB over MambaQuant (28.08 dB). Cross-architecture validation on SwinIR-light at 2-bit ×2 shows SPR²Q improving PSNR by 1.14–1.34 dB over Transformer-specific quantization methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPR²Q consistently outperforms all three Mamba-specific baselines (PTQ4VM, Quamba, MambaQuant) across every dataset and bit-width tested. At 4-bit ×2, the margins are 0.55 dB over PTQ4VM and 1.05 dB over MambaQuant on Set5, while on Urban100 the gap over MambaQuant reaches 3.45 dB, indicating MambaQuant's variance-aligned rotation is particularly ill-suited for texture-heavy SR. At 2-bit ×2, SPR²Q leads Quamba by 1.31 dB on Set5 and by 0.75–0.94 dB on Urban100, confirming the advantage persists under extreme compression. For cross-architecture validation on SwinIR-light at 2-bit ×2, SPR²Q surpasses 2DQuant by 1.28 dB, FIMA-Q by 1.22 dB, and APHQ-ViT by 1.14 dB on Set5, with even larger gains (1.34–1.58 dB) on Urban100, demonstrating that the rectifier-routing strategy generalizes beyond Mamba to Transformer-based SR models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA low-rank factorization paradigm from LLM fine-tuning into the post-training quantization (PTQ) domain for super-resolution, repurposing low-rank rectifiers not as adaptation modules but as pre-quantization compensation injectors. It departs from existing Mamba quantization methods (PTQ4VM, Quamba, MambaQuant) that focus exclusively on optimizing quantizer parameters or applying architecture-specific rotations, by introducing learnable weight increments fused into the backbone before quantization. The static priority-based routing mechanism further distinguishes it from dynamic expert-routing approaches by pre-computing a fixed routing table offline, eliminating inference-time overhead while preserving the diversity benefits of multiple rectifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: Mamba-based SR models (MambaIRv2) had no dedicated low-bit quantization method, and existing Mamba quantization techniques designed for classification or language tasks degrade severely on pixel-fidelity-sensitive SR. The cross-architecture validation on SwinIR-light suggests the rectifier-fusion + static-routing recipe is not Mamba-specific, which broadens its practical relevance. However, the evaluation is limited to two 'light' model variants at two scale factors, and the 1-bit results, while encouraging, still show a ~1 dB gap to 2-bit, leaving open whether the approach scales to full-size models or more aggressive compression regimes. A key open question is whether the static routing table remains optimal under distribution shift (e.g., different image domains or degradation types).
