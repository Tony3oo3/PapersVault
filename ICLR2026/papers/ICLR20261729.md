## Problem

LLMs still lag behind human experts in university-level, discipline-specific reasoning, largely because existing reasoning datasets are skewed toward math and coding, lack disciplinary breadth and reasoning depth, and offer no principled mechanism for controlling question difficulty and diversity during synthesis. Document-centric generation methods, while grounded in authentic knowledge, tend to degenerate into factual recall and cannot produce the structured multi-step reasoning patterns that characterize expert-designed exam questions.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw text segments from a proprietary book corpus (chapter-level, filtered by readability and educational helpfulness) and the FineFineWeb web corpus (scored on a five-level reasoning rubric), each aligned to a 75-discipline taxonomy. For each segment, a Design Logic—a reusable, Mermaid-formatted flowchart abstracting the reasoning structure of an expert-designed question—is retrieved via cosine similarity over Qwen3-Embedding-4B vectors (top-5 candidates). The output is a graduate-level multidisciplinary reasoning question with a concise reference answer and a long chain-of-thought response generated by Qwen3-235B, yielding DLR-Book (3.04 M questions) and DLR-Web (1.66 M questions) spanning 75 disciplines.

## Method

The central contribution is the notion of Design Logic: a reusable meta-knowledge unit that encodes the structured design process (identify objectives → construct context → design reasoning path → formulate answer → add distractors → validate) human educators follow when turning knowledge points into complex exam questions. The pipeline first reverse-engineers over 125 K Design Logics from a curated 132 K-question subset of a 150 M-item question bank using DeepSeek-R1-0528, then deduplicates them via a graph-based centroid-selection algorithm (similarity threshold τ = 0.85). At synthesis time, a two-stage retrieve-and-generate mechanism avoids combinatorial explosion: vector similarity provides coarse recall of the top-5 candidate logics for each text segment, and an LLM performs fine-grained selection and strictly follows the chosen logic's steps to produce a new question from entirely different source material. This decouples the reasoning structure (Design Logic) from the surface content (raw corpus), enabling explicit control over difficulty, diversity, and question type. Supervised fine-tuning on the resulting question–response pairs substantially improves multidisciplinary reasoning on Qwen3 and Llama3 base models, with SFT-only models surpassing their officially post-trained counterparts on GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro, and SuperGPQA.

## Results

On the Qwen3-8B-Base model, DLR-Book achieves the best performance across MMLU (86.43), MMLU-Pro (74.98), and GPQA-Diamond (60.35±1.93, 66.67 CoT-SC), while DLR-Web attains the highest SuperGPQA score (47.23). More strikingly, SFT on the base Qwen3-8B model using only DLR-Web+Book data surpasses the official Qwen3-8B (Thinking Mode) that underwent full post-training: 87.60 vs. 85.85 on MMLU, 76.72 vs. 73.62 on MMLU-Pro, and 71.01 vs. 59.44 on GPQA-Diamond. The same pattern holds for Llama-3.1-8B, where SFT(DLR-Web+Book) reaches 84.13 MMLU and 65.45 GPQA-Diamond versus 70.86 and 23.18 for the official Instruct model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenThoughts3, DLR-Book leads by 13.94 points on MMLU, 17.22 on MMLU-Pro, and 14.49 on GPQA-Diamond, indicating a substantially larger gap than any other comparison. Nemotron-Post-Training-v1 is the weakest baseline, trailing DLR-Book by 9.26 on MMLU and 21.76 on GPQA-Diamond, consistent with its lower question difficulty (only 3.04% Very Hard vs. 54.66% for DLR-Book). WebInstruct (Full) is the closest competitor on MMLU (86.34 vs. 86.43) but DLR-Book still wins on MMLU-Pro (+2.15), GPQA-Diamond (+4.74), and CoT-SC (+4.04), while DLR-Web edges out WebInstruct on SuperGPQA (47.23 vs. 45.37). NaturalReasoning, despite having the highest Very Hard proportion among baselines (31.11%), is outperformed by both DLR datasets on every benchmark, with DLR-Book gaining 4.68 on GPQA-Diamond. Finally, the most notable comparison is against the official post-trained models: SFT on DLR data alone surpasses Qwen3-8B Thinking Mode by 11.57 points on GPQA-Diamond and 3.38 on SuperGPQA, and Llama-3.1-8B Instruct by 42.27 points on GPQA-Diamond, demonstrating that the synthesized data's quality compensates for the absence of full post-training pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both query-centric synthesis (Self-Instruct, WizardLM, Evol-Instruct) and document-centric synthesis (MAmmoTH2, NaturalReasoning, Humpback) by introducing a third axis: reverse-engineered 'Design Logics' extracted from human-authored exam questions and used as reusable, structured guidance when generating new questions from raw documents. The two-stage retrieve-and-generate mechanism borrows from retrieval-augmented generation, but the key conceptual departure is treating the question-design process itself as extractable meta-knowledge (expressed in Mermaid flowcharts) rather than relying on exemplar imitation or single-prompt templates. The approach is explicitly positioned as discipline-agnostic, in contrast to prior structurally-guided methods (KPDDS, Xu et al.) that target math or code.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.7M-question, 75-discipline datasets (DLR-Book, DLR-Web) represent one of the largest multidisciplinary reasoning corpora available, and the Design Logic library (125K entries) is released for the community to apply to their own raw documents. The finding that SFT on base models using only this data surpasses official full post-training models on GPQA-Diamond and SuperGPQA challenges the assumption that multi-stage post-training pipelines are necessary for strong reasoning, though the gap narrows on pure-math benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500) where the data is less specialized. Open questions include whether Design Logics can be extracted without a proprietary question bank, whether the 71.48% answer-consistency rate in open-ended disciplines limits downstream SFT quality, and whether the method's advantage persists as base models themselves improve.
