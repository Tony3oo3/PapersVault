## Problem

Continual learning in spiking neural networks suffers from catastrophic forgetting because deterministic K-WTA mechanisms select neurons based solely on instantaneous firing rates, ignoring the temporal richness of spiking signals. Even trace-based K-WTA variants that incorporate temporal dynamics remain deterministic, making them vulnerable to input perturbations and task interference. The paper asks how to make SNN selective activation robust to temporal perturbations by combining trace-dependent neuronal dynamics with controlled stochasticity in the winner-take-all competition.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (28×28 MNIST, 32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 Tiny ImageNet) and produces class labels. For CIFAR and Tiny ImageNet, a frozen ResNet-18 or ViT backbone first extracts fixed-dimensional embeddings that are then fed into a two-layer fully-connected SNN; for MNIST, raw pixel values are used directly. The SNN processes each input over a fixed temporal window (e.g., 16 time steps), and the RTK-WTA mask is applied independently at every time step, making the output a temporally integrated spike-based classification rather than a single-shot ANN prediction.

## Method

RTK-WTA replaces deterministic spatial K-WTA with a two-stage mechanism operating at each time step: (1) a neuronal trace tr_i[t] accumulates spike history with exponential decay (tr_i[t+1] = tr_i[t] − tr_i[t]/τ + S_i[t+1]), providing a temporally integrated activity signal; (2) a probabilistic top-k selector assigns selection probability (1−α)/K to neurons in the top-K trace and α/(d−K) to the rest, where α ∈ [0,1] controls the degree of randomness. The resulting binary mask gates the spike output (S*[t] = S[t]·Mask[t]), so that across the temporal window the network explores a combinatorially larger set of neuron configurations than deterministic K-WTA. Theoretically, this expands the effective spatiotemporal feature space volume by a factor proportional to {C(d,K)·[1+αK/((1−α)(d−K))]^K}^T and increases the minimal inter-class margin, reducing generalization error. In practice, RTK-WTA is combined with EWC regularization for continual learning, and the optimal α is empirically found to be 0.1, balancing feature diversity against temporal coherence.

## Results

On splitCIFAR-100, RTK-WTA achieves 32.91% standalone accuracy, surpassing the best deterministic baseline (Trace-based SA-SNN at 22.86%) by 10.05 percentage points. With EWC regularization, RTK-WTA+EWC reaches 85.25% on splitMNIST (+3.07% over SA-SNN+EWC) and 41.46% on splitCIFAR-100 (+5.0% over SA-SNN+EWC). On splitCIFAR-10, RTK-WTA attains 78.37% standalone and 80.56% with EWC, maintaining a consistent lead over all baselines. The method also demonstrates superior noise robustness, retaining 68.85% accuracy on CIFAR-10 at 0.8 noise level with EWC versus 59.69% for the deterministic trace-based baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTK-WTA outperforms all four baseline families across every dataset. Against the closest competitor, Trace-based SA-SNN (Shen et al., AAAI 2024), RTK-WTA gains +0.31% on splitMNIST, +0.64% on splitCIFAR-10, and a substantial +10.05% on splitCIFAR-100 (32.91% vs. 22.86%), with the gap widening as task complexity increases. With EWC, RTK-WTA+EWC leads SA-SNN+EWC by +3.07% on splitMNIST (85.25% vs. 82.18%) and +5.0% on splitCIFAR-100 (41.46% vs. 36.47%). The ANN-based SDMLP baseline (Bricken et al., 2023) achieves 73.27% on splitCIFAR-10 but is not evaluated on splitCIFAR-100; RTK-WTA surpasses it by 5.1% on splitCIFAR-10. FlyModel (Shen et al., 2021) and the original EWC-SNN (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) are substantially outperformed, with EWC-SNN alone achieving only 3.93% on splitCIFAR-100, confirming that the trace-based temporal dynamics and stochastic selection are the primary drivers of the performance gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the trace-based K-WTA mechanism introduced in SA-SNN (Shen et al., AAAI 2024), which replaced rate-based deterministic top-k selection with temporally accumulated neuronal traces for SNN continual learning. The key departure is injecting controlled stochasticity (parameterized by α) into the top-k selection, so that non-top-K neurons can occasionally be activated, expanding the effective spatiotemporal feature space beyond what deterministic trace-based selection allows. The approach also draws on the broader WTA literature in SNNs (Lynch et al. 2019, Su et al. 2019) and the biological observation that cortical WTA circuits combine deterministic competition with controlled randomness, while complementing EWC (Kirkpatrick et al. 2017) as a weight-consolidation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a lightweight, biologically grounded mechanism for reducing representational overlap in SNN continual learning without requiring additional network modules or task-specific architectures, which is practically relevant for neuromorphic deployment where energy efficiency is paramount. However, the current formulation is restricted to shallow (two-layer fully connected) classifiers, and for CIFAR experiments it relies on frozen pretrained ResNet-18 embeddings, limiting the generality of the claims. A key open question is whether RTK-WTA's benefits persist in deeper end-to-end SNNs without external feature extractors, and whether the modest optimal randomness level (α=0.1) generalizes across architectures and task sequences of varying difficulty.
