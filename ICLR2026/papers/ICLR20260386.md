## Problem

Frame selection for VideoQA faces a critical trade-off: lightweight similarity models like CLIP treat complex queries as bags of keywords and produce inaccurate relevance scores, while VLM-based deep analysis achieves higher accuracy but incurs prohibitive computational cost (e.g., ~162 s to analyze 128 frames with InternVL-3-8B). No existing method simultaneously handles temporally-reasoning queries and keeps VLM inference tractable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260386/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (N total frames at 24 fps) and a multiple-choice text query as input. It first samples frames at 1 FPS and computes a sparse CLIP similarity vector S ∈ ℝ^N. The output is an adaptive set of B selected frame indices (B scales linearly with video duration, clamped between V_min=8 and V_max=32) plus a single answer letter (A–E) produced by the Answering VLM in one final inference pass. The same VLM backbone serves as both the Analysis VLM (scoring relevance 1–5 per frame) and the Answering VLM, making the framework plug-and-play across VILA, QwenVL, InternVL-3, LLaVA-OneVision, and GPT-4o.

## Method

A.I.R. is a training-free, two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (Adaptive Initial Sampling) fits a two-component GMM to the CLIP similarity scores to derive a per-video adaptive threshold, segments the video into contiguous 'events' above that threshold, merges/prunes noisy segments, and allocates a proportional sampling budget to each event to yield K initial candidate frames. Stage 2 (Iterative Frame Selection) runs a four-step loop: (i) Interval Potential Ranking scores each temporal interval between sampled frames by a product of mean relevance, total-variation complexity, and log-length, selecting only C=12 high-potential candidates; (ii) a Reasoning-based VLM Analysis prompt instructs the VLM to assign a 1–5 relevance score with justification, retaining only frames scoring above θ=3; (iii) an Early Stop mechanism halts the loop once the cumulative selected set meets the adaptive budget B; (iv) Localized Density Sampling generates new fine-grained frames from the original N-frame video using an exponentially growing stride (β=1.5) around each validated frame, feeding them back into the next iteration. This design bounds the total VLM workload to C·I_max = 72 frames in the worst case (vs. 128 for conventional fixed-budget methods) while the Early Stop typically triggers after only ~36 frames on average.

## Results

On long-video benchmarks, A.I.R. with InternVL-3-8B achieves 68.2% on Video-MME (w/o sub), 74.5% on MLVU, and 62.8% on LongVideoBench, outperforming the uniform-sampling baseline by +2.6%, +6.1%, and +4.5% respectively while using ≤32 frames. On short-video NextQA, the method delivers its largest gain: +7.0% with QwenVL-2.5 (74.3% → 81.3%) and +0.3% with InternVL-3 (82.3% → 82.6%). In terms of efficiency, A.I.R. analyzes only 36.5 frames on average (vs. 128 for conventional VLM analysis), reducing VLM inference time from 162.03s to 42.31s on Video-MME while maintaining or exceeding accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A.I.R. consistently outperforms all CLIP-based frame selection baselines (MDP3, BOLT, Q-Frame, AKS) across every benchmark and VLM backbone tested; for example, with InternVL-3 on Video-MME it reaches 68.2% vs. MDP3's 66.8% and with LLaVA-OneVision it scores 69.3% on MLVU vs. BOLT's 66.8%. Against VLM-analysis-based methods, A.I.R. achieves higher accuracy while analyzing far fewer frames: on NextQA it scores 82.6% with only 32.2 analyzed frames versus Hu et al.'s 78.4% with 128 frames, VideoTree's 75.6% with 128 frames, and T*'s 80.4% with 8 frames. On the temporal grounding benchmark Charades-STA, A.I.R. (training-free) achieves 59.5% R1@0.3 and 39.5% R1@0.5, surpassing GPT-4o (55.0% / 32.0%) and the trained GenS method (62.9% / 38.7%) on R1@0.5 while remaining competitive on R1@0.3. The method also generalizes across VLM scales, providing +4.2% gain on QwenVL-2.5-7B, +2.5% on 32B, +1.2% on 72B, and +3.3% on GPT-4o, confirming that the benefit is largest for smaller models with weaker intrinsic relevance discrimination. No baseline comparison shows A.I.R. losing on any reported metric; the closest margin is on NextQA with InternVL-3 where it ties MDP3 at 82.3% before pulling ahead to 82.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

A.I.R. sits at the intersection of two established lines of frame selection research: lightweight CLIP-based methods (BOLT, MDP3, Q-Frame, AKS) that are fast but fail on complex queries requiring temporal reasoning, and VLM-based analysis methods (VideoTree, MVU, Frame-Voyager, SeViLA) that achieve higher accuracy but incur prohibitive single-pass computational costs over large fixed frame sets. Rather than building on a single prior method, A.I.R. repurposes CLIP (EVA-CLIP-L) for a new role—coarse event detection via GMM-based adaptive thresholding—while introducing an iterative, reasoning-based VLM analysis loop that processes only small high-potential batches per iteration. The Interval Potential Ranking draws on signal processing concepts (DC component, total variation, logarithmic length) to prioritize temporal regions, and the Localized Density Sampling with exponential stride growth is a novel exploration mechanism not present in prior frame selection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: A.I.R. reduces VLM analysis time from ~162s to ~42s (4× speedup) while simultaneously improving accuracy, making powerful VLM-based video understanding feasible for real-world deployment. Its training-free, plug-and-play design means it can be dropped onto any foundation VLM without retraining, and the generalization to temporal grounding (Charades-STA) without task-specific supervision suggests broader applicability. Open questions include whether the GMM bimodality assumption on similarity scores holds for all video types, how the method scales to very long videos (>1 hour) where the adaptive budget may still be insufficient, and whether the iterative loop's gains diminish as base VLMs become more powerful (the paper notes the accuracy gap narrows from +4.2% at 7B to +1.2% at 72B).
