## Problem

Existing vision-language models for taxonomic open-set classification extract hierarchical features from text but represent each image with a single feature, creating an asymmetric alignment that fails to capture complete textual information. Moreover, textual and visual features encode fundamentally different geometric structures that reside on heterogeneous manifolds, and alignment across such manifolds with distinct curvatures remains underexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260394/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of hierarchical text labels (e.g., taxonomic levels from kingdom to species) as input, and produces class predictions at every level of the taxonomic tree. It operates on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone, extracting class tokens from the 4th, 7th, and final Transformer layers to build the visual feature tree, while textual features at each hierarchy level are derived from prompt-tuned language tokens. Evaluation uses three metrics—Leaf Accuracy, Hierarchical Consistent Accuracy, and Mean Treecut Accuracy—under few-shot (1/16-shot) and cross-domain (base-to-novel) settings.

## Method

The method has two core components. First, a semantic-aware visual feature extraction framework projects intermediate-layer class tokens (4th, 7th) into the final layer's representation space by disabling cross-token self-attention, then applies a cross-attention module where textual features at each hierarchy serve as queries and class tokens from different layers act as keys/values, producing hierarchy-specific visual features that form a symmetric tree with the textual tree. Second, a heterogeneous manifold alignment algorithm embeds the two feature trees into separate Lorentz-model hyperbolic manifolds with independently learnable curvatures c₁ (text) and c₂ (image), then constructs an intermediate manifold L^{c₃} by minimizing a KL-divergence-based manifold distance, for which the paper proves existence and uniqueness of the optimal c₃. Cross-modal alignment is enforced via hyperbolic entailment cones on the intermediate manifold (forcing visual features inside textual cones), while in-modal hierarchical constraints push finer-grained features into coarser-grained cones on each modality's own manifold. Curvature gradients are computed through the implicit function theorem to handle the non-differentiable golden-section search over c₃.

## Results

On the taxonomic open-set (TOS) classification task, the method achieves up to 7.72% HCA improvement over ProTeCt in the 1-shot setting (e.g., 20.94% vs. 13.22% on Rare Species) and up to 28.83% HCA improvement in the 16-shot setting (e.g., 53.65% vs. 24.82% on Rare Species). In the 16-shot setting, the method reaches 90.89% MTA and 69.38% HCA on Cifar100 (MaPLe backbone), and 91.12% MTA on Cifar100 (PromptSRC backbone). Across all four datasets (Cifar100, SUN, ImageNet, Rare Species) and both few-shot and cross-domain generalization settings, the method consistently outperforms all baselines on LA, HCA, and MTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against five baselines: MaPLe (Khattak et al., CVPR 2023) and PromptSRC (Khattak et al., ICCV 2023) as prompt-learning backbones, ProTeCt (Wu et al., CVPR 2024) which adds hierarchical textual constraints, and two hyperbolic alignment methods—MERU (Desai et al., ICML 2023) and HyCoCLIP (Pal et al., ICLR 2025). Against ProTeCt, the method wins on all datasets and settings, with the largest HCA gains on Rare Species (7.72% in 1-shot, 28.83% in 16-shot) and ImageNet (5.12% in 1-shot, 12.55% in 16-shot). Against the hyperbolic baselines, the method achieves up to 25.89% LA, 41.17% HCA, and 50.79% MTA improvements over HyCoCLIP, demonstrating that existing hyperbolic methods are limited in aligning modalities with hierarchical semantic structures. The method also outperforms the vanilla MaPLe and PromptSRC baselines by large margins (e.g., 53.19% vs. 4.65% HCA on Cifar100 in 1-shot), confirming that both the semantic-aware visual feature extraction and the heterogeneous manifold alignment contribute substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning paradigm for vision-language models (MaPLe, PromptSRC) and the taxonomic open-set classification framework of ProTeCt (Wu et al., 2024) by replacing the standard single-visual-feature alignment with a symmetric tree-of-features alignment. It draws on hyperbolic geometry for hierarchical data (Nickel & Kiela, 2017; Desai et al., 2023; Pal et al., 2025) but departs from prior hyperbolic VLM work by assigning distinct learnable curvatures to each modality and introducing an intermediate manifold optimized via a KL-divergence-based manifold distance. The semantic-aware visual feature extraction leverages the observation that intermediate ViT layers encode coarse semantics (Chen et al., 2024), combined with a text-guided cross-attention mechanism to produce hierarchy-specific visual features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asymmetric alignment problem—matching a single visual vector to a hierarchy of textual features—is a structural limitation in current VLMs for taxonomic and hierarchical tasks, and this paper provides a principled geometric solution with theoretical guarantees (existence and uniqueness of the optimal intermediate manifold). The finding that existing hyperbolic alignment methods (MERU, HyCoCLIP) underperform dramatically on hierarchical consistency metrics despite operating in hyperbolic space suggests that the specific alignment mechanism, not merely the choice of geometry, is the critical factor. Limitations include reliance on single-curvature manifolds (which may not capture all geometric structure), the approximate KL divergence, and no explicit handling of domain shift; the authors note that mixed-curvature spaces and cross-domain robustness are natural next steps.
