## Problem

Interactive segmentation models can leverage user clicks to correct predictions, but they lack mechanisms to adapt their parameters from those corrections, leaving them vulnerable to distribution shifts common in medical imaging (different scanners, modalities, pathologies). Prior online adaptation methods for interactive segmentation (IA+SA, TSCA) optimize only on the few clicked pixels with sparse losses and require additional regularizers, ignoring the surrounding clinically relevant regions. The paper asks how to design a practical online adaptation framework that effectively uses the full user-refined prediction as a learning signal to handle distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

The model receives a 2D medical image (RGB fundus photograph or single MRI slice) concatenated with two Gaussian-smoothed guidance maps encoding foreground and background click positions, all fed into a U-Net. User interaction is iterative: a single localization click initiates prediction, followed by up to T correction clicks (default 10) placed in erroneous regions, each producing a refined mask. The output is a pixel-wise segmentation mask (binary for brain lesions; 3-class for fundus: background, outer-ring, cup). Images arrive sequentially, and the model parameters are updated after each click and after each image is fully corrected.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-level online adaptation framework (OAIMS) built on a U-Net with ICNN-style click encoding. A novel Click-Centered Gaussian (CCG) loss penalizes prediction errors in a Gaussian-weighted neighborhood around each click, restricted to pixels sharing the click's class label, and is applied in all three learning stages (pretraining, mid-interaction, post-interaction). Post-Interaction adaptation treats the user-corrected final mask P_final as pseudo-ground-truth and performs two fine-tuning steps: one with the localization click using Dice-Focal loss, and one with artificially generated correction clicks (placed in disagreement regions between Stage-1 output and P_final) using Dice-Focal plus CCG loss. Mid-Interaction adaptation updates parameters after every user click by comparing the pre-correction prediction P_{t-1} against the post-click prediction P_t^initial, optimizing CCG and Dice-Focal loss with P_{t-1} as pseudo-label, so the model benefits both the current and subsequent images. This design avoids the need for complex regularizers and leverages the full corrected mask rather than only clicked pixels.

## Results

On brain MRI (the most challenging distribution-shift setting), OAIMS (PI+MI) achieves 71.2/83.4/88.0% Dice at 1/5/10 clicks on BRATS T1, compared to 34.4/60.9/74.0% for the strongest baseline TSCA — a gain of over 36 points at 1 click and 14 points at 10 clicks. On fundus imaging (G1020, Cup), OAIMS reaches 82.2/90.0/92.7% Dice at 1/5/10 clicks vs. 77.6/85.0/90.7% for TSCA. The method requires only 2 back-propagations per image (Post-Interaction) plus 1 per click (Mid-Interaction), with negligible latency (0.05 s MI, 0.09 s PI on GPU). On the extreme domain-shift scenario (BRATS Flair → ATLAS T1), OAIMS recovers 80.0% Dice at 10 clicks where the non-adaptive model achieves only 24.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OAIMS (PI+MI) outperforms all four baselines across every tested dataset and modality. Against TSCA (the strongest prior online-adaptation method), OAIMS gains +36.8 Dice points at 1 click and +14.0 at 10 clicks on BRATS T1, and +20.3 / +12.7 / +4.0 on BRATS T1c/T2 respectively. Against IA+SA, the margins are similar or larger (e.g., +42.6 at 1 click on BRATS T1). The non-adaptive ICNN* baseline is surpassed by 50+ points on BRATS T1 at 1 click, confirming that online adaptation is essential under large distribution shift. Med-SA (frozen SAM-based model) performs worst among all methods on brain MRI (e.g., 35.7% at 10 clicks on BRATS T1 vs. 88.0% for OAIMS), and is dropped from further comparisons due to its poor cross-distribution performance and high computational cost. On fundus imaging where distribution shift is smaller, OAIMS still leads by 2–5 Dice points over TSCA, with the gap widening on the Cup class (e.g., 92.7 vs. 90.7 on G1020 at 10 clicks).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interactive segmentation paradigm (ICNN, DeepIGeoS, SAM/MedSAM) by adding online parameter adaptation to handle distribution shifts, building directly on prior online-adaptation methods IA+SA (Kontogianni et al., 2020) and TSCA (Atanyan et al., 2024). The key departure is twofold: (i) it uses the full user-refined segmentation mask as pseudo-ground-truth rather than restricting optimization to the few clicked pixels, eliminating the need for complex regularizers; and (ii) it introduces a Click-Centered Gaussian (CCG) loss that spatially weights the penalty around each click with a class-restricted Gaussian kernel, applied consistently across pretraining, mid-interaction, and post-interaction stages. The method is model-agnostic (demonstrated on U-Net) and complements rather than replaces the Transformer-based interactive models (Med-SA, MedSAM) it benchmarks against.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Distribution shift across scanners, modalities, and pathologies is a persistent barrier to deploying medical image segmentation in clinical practice, and this work shows that lightweight online adaptation (0.05–0.41 s per update) can close much of the gap with minimal user effort. The robustness to 40% erroneous clicks and to 'worst-case-first' image ordering addresses a practical concern that prior adaptation methods do not explicitly handle. Open questions include extension to 3D volumes (the paper tests 2D slices only), compatibility with non-click prompts (scribbles, bounding boxes), and whether the pseudo-ground-truth assumption holds when users provide only partial corrections. The method's simplicity (two back-propagations per image for Post-Interaction) makes it attractive for real-time clinical deployment.
