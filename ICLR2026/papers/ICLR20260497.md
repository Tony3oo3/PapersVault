## Problem

Imitation-learning-based VLA models for autonomous driving cannot inherently encode inviolable physical rules such as collision avoidance and drivable-area compliance, producing trajectories that are probable under the learned distribution yet still unsafe. Existing remedies—rule-based post-refinement, simulation-only reinforcement learning, or gradient-based diffusion guidance—each introduce significant engineering overhead, sim-to-real gaps, or prohibitive computational cost at inference time.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three synchronized camera views (front, front-left, front-right), a natural-language navigation instruction (e.g., 'turn left'), and ego-vehicle state, and outputs a sequence of N future waypoints. Each 2D waypoint is quantized into a token pair from a uniform 1D codebook of ~667 tokens per axis (0.3 m resolution over a ±100 m range), so the full trajectory is a flattened sequence of 2N discrete tokens. Generation uses classifier-free guidance with 5 parallel decoding steps, and the reflection loop can perform up to 10 inpainting iterations to repair unsafe segments.

## Method

ReflectDrive discretizes the 2D driving plane into a token codebook and fine-tunes a pre-trained Diffusion Language Model (LLaDA-V) with a standard masked-diffusion objective, enabling bidirectional inpainting and parallel decoding of trajectory tokens. At inference, a two-stage reflection mechanism operates entirely in the discrete token space without gradient computation: (1) Goal-Conditioned Generation samples K spatially diverse terminal waypoints via NMS, inpaints a full trajectory for each, and selects the best via a global scorer; (2) Safety-Guided Regeneration iteratively identifies violating waypoints with a safety oracle, performs a local Manhattan-neighborhood search for corrective token pairs, fixes them as safety anchors, and re-inpaints the surrounding trajectory to restore global coherence. The key insight is that the discrete token representation makes hard safety constraints directly enforceable through search, masking, and inpainting—operations that are intractable or expensive in continuous diffusion—while preserving the model's learned behavioral distribution.

## Results

On the NAVSIM closed-loop benchmark, ReflectDrive achieves a PDMS score of 91.1, outperforming all compared baselines including GoalFlow (90.3), AutoVLA (89.1), and DiffusionDrive (88.1). The method particularly excels on Drivable Area Compliance (DAC: 99.3, approaching the human level of 100.0) and Ego Progress (EP: 86.9, near human 87.5). With ground-truth agent states (ReflectDrive†), the system reaches PDMS 94.7, matching human driving performance (94.8) across NC, DAC, and TTC metrics. The base model without reflective inference (PDMS 84.8) is competitive with standard E2E planners, confirming that the safety gains come primarily from the reflection mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReflectDrive (PDMS 91.1) outperforms all seven baselines on the NAVSIM closed-loop benchmark. Against the strongest augmented E2E planner GoalFlow (90.3), it gains +0.8 PDMS, driven primarily by a +1.0 DAC improvement (99.3 vs. 98.3) and a +1.9 EP gain (86.9 vs. 85.0). Compared to the VLA baseline AutoVLA (89.1), ReflectDrive leads by +2.0 PDMS, with a substantial +3.7 DAC advantage (99.3 vs. 95.6), though AutoVLA achieves a higher TTC (98.0 vs. 93.5) due to its reinforcement fine-tuning. Against diffusion-based planners DiffusionDrive (88.1) and GoalFlow (90.3), ReflectDrive's discrete token representation enables the safety-guided regeneration to push DAC to near-perfect levels. The gap to human performance (94.8) narrows to just 0.1 PDMS when ground-truth agent states are used (ReflectDrive†: 94.7), with EP (88.9) actually exceeding the human value (87.5). The paper attributes the remaining TTC shortfall (93.5 vs. human 100.0) to the use of constant-velocity agent assumptions in the safety oracle rather than a fundamental limitation of the architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReflectDrive sits at the intersection of three research threads: discrete diffusion models (Austin et al., 2021; Lou et al., 2023), VLA-based driving planners (AutoVLA, EMMa, DriveVLM), and safety-aware planning beyond imitation learning (Hydra-MDP anchors, DiffusionDrive, RL-based approaches). It departs from prior diffusion-based driving planners (DiffusionDrive, GoalFlow, Diffusion Planner) by operating in a discrete token space rather than continuous coordinates, which enables gradient-free safety enforcement through local search, masking, and inpainting. The reflection mechanism specifically contrasts with diffusion guidance methods (Jiang et al., 2023; Zheng et al., 2025) that require expensive gradient computations during inference, and with RL-based approaches that are confined to simulation. The use of a pre-trained Diffusion Language Model (LLaDA-V) as the backbone for trajectory generation is a novel instantiation, repurposing a text-generation architecture for spatial planning via trajectory discretization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental gap in E2E driving: imitation learning cannot inherently encode hard safety constraints like collision avoidance or drivable-area compliance. The discrete token representation provides a natural substrate for constraint integration (search, masking, inpainting) that continuous spaces lack, and the gradient-free reflection loop offers a practical alternative to both RL and diffusion guidance. However, the near-human results (PDMS 94.7) rely on a ground-truth oracle for surrounding agents, and the constant-velocity assumption in the deployable version limits TTC performance. Open questions include whether the reflection mechanism generalizes to multi-agent interactive scenarios, whether the oscillation failure mode in tight spaces can be resolved, and whether the approach scales to longer horizons or more complex navigation tasks without the current single-frame input limitation.
