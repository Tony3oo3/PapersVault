## Problem

Existing benchmarks for zero-shot text classification either evaluate only a single model family, rely on supervised linear probes (as in MTEB) that mask genuine zero-shot capability, or lack a unified protocol for comparing generative and non-generative architectures. There is no systematic, controlled evaluation that places NLI cross-encoders, embedding models, rerankers, and instruction-tuned LLMs side-by-side under a strict zero-shot protocol across diverse task types and domains.

## Input / Output

Each input is a single English text document (ranging from 8 to 293 tokens) paired with a set of verbalized label descriptions generated from fixed per-dataset templates (e.g., 'The overall sentiment within the Amazon product review is {label}'). The same verbalizers are shared across all model families to ensure comparability. NLI cross-encoders consume text-label pairs as premise-hypothesis inputs, embedding models encode both sides independently and compare via cosine similarity, rerankers treat the text as a query and labels as candidate documents, and LLMs receive a multiple-choice prompt with letter-prefixed options. The output is a single predicted class label per document, scored with macro F1 as the primary metric.

## Method

BTZSC comprises 22 English single-label datasets spanning sentiment, topic, intent, and emotion classification, selected for diversity in domain (news, social media, finance, politics, e-commerce), class cardinality (2–77 labels), and document length. The benchmark evaluates 38 public and custom checkpoints across four model families under a strict zero-shot protocol: no fine-tuning, no labeled examples from BTZSC tasks, and no per-model verbalizer tuning. Custom NLI cross-encoders are trained on a mixture of MNLI, ANLI, WANLI, FEVERNLI, and LINGNLI with both binary and three-way loss variants. The key empirical finding is that modern rerankers (Qwen3-Reranker-8B, macro F1 = 0.72) set a new state-of-the-art, strong embedding models (GTE-large-en-v1.5, F1 = 0.62) offer the best accuracy-latency trade-off, instruction-tuned LLMs at 4–12B parameters reach competitive F1 (up to 0.67) while excelling on topic classification, and NLI cross-encoders plateau even as backbone size increases.

## Results

Across 22 datasets and 38 models, Qwen3-Reranker-8B sets the new state-of-the-art with a macro F1 of 0.72 and micro accuracy of 0.76, outperforming all other model families by 10+ F1 points. Among embedding models, GTE-large-en-v1.5 achieves the best accuracy-latency trade-off at 0.62 F1, while the strongest instruction-tuned LLM (Mistral-Nemo-Instruct-2407, 12B) reaches 0.67 F1. NLI cross-encoders plateau at 0.60 F1 (DeBERTa-v3-large-nli-triplet) regardless of backbone scale, and base encoders without task-specific training perform poorly (0.27–0.30 F1). Task difficulty varies substantially: sentiment is near-saturated (median F1 ≈ 0.88–0.92 for top models), while emotion classification remains the hardest (F1 ≈ 0.32–0.49).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen3-Reranker-8B (0.72 F1) dominates all other model families, beating the best NLI cross-encoder (DeBERTa-v3-large-nli-triplet, 0.60 F1) by 12 points and the best embedding model (GTE-large-en-v1.5, 0.62 F1) by 10 points. Among NLI cross-encoders, DeBERTa-v3 backbones outperform BERT and ModernBERT variants by 3–5 F1 points, and scaling from base to large adds ~3.5 F1; however, the original bart-large-mnli (0.51 F1) and nli-roberta-base (0.49 F1) trail the custom multi-dataset-trained models by 6+ points. In the embedding family, GTE-large-en-v1.5 (0.62 F1) and E5-large-v2 (0.60 F1) substantially surpass the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 baseline (0.37 F1) by 23–25 points, while Qwen3-Embedding-8B (0.59 F1) shows minimal gain over its 0.6B variant (0.58 F1), indicating embedding models saturate around 0.60 F1. For instruction-tuned LLMs, Qwen3-4B (0.65 F1) and Qwen3-8B (0.66 F1) far outperform Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (0.43 F1) and Phi-4-mini-instruct (0.43 F1) at similar parameter counts, while Mistral-Nemo-Instruct-2407 (0.67 F1) edges ahead of Qwen3-8B but still trails Qwen3-Reranker-8B by 5 F1 points. Base encoders (BERT, DeBERTa-v3, ModernBERT without NLI fine-tuning) all cluster at 0.27–0.30 F1, confirming that explicit semantic-matching training is essential for zero-shot classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

BTZSC builds directly on the dataset pool, label verbalizers, and NLI cross-encoder training recipe from Laurer et al. (2023), repurposing them into a standardized zero-shot evaluation suite. It departs from MTEB, which assesses classification via supervised linear probes on frozen embeddings, by evaluating all models in a strictly zero-shot setting with no labeled examples. Prior ZSC benchmarks (Yin et al. 2019, Chalkidis et al. 2020, Gretz et al. 2023, Lepagnol et al. 2024) each evaluated only a single model family or a narrow task slice; BTZSC is the first to jointly compare NLI cross-encoders, embedding models, rerankers, and instruction-tuned LLMs under one consistent protocol across 22 datasets and four task categories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides practitioners with a clear, reproducible decision framework: rerankers deliver the highest accuracy, embedding models offer the best accuracy-latency trade-off for deployment, and NLI cross-encoders—long the default for zero-shot classification—show diminishing returns with scale. Key open questions include whether the reranker advantage persists in multilingual settings, whether improved label verbalizations or prompt engineering can close the remaining gap for LLMs, and whether the data-contamination caveat (public datasets potentially in pretraining corpora) undermines the zero-shot claim at scale. The finding that NLI AUROC predicts ZSC quality for cross-encoders and LLMs but not for embedding models suggests the field needs task-specific evaluation beyond generic NLI benchmarks.
