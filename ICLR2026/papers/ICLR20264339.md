## Problem

Large-scale video generation models achieve high fidelity but are computationally expensive and lack explicit object structure, while existing object-centric world models have been confined to simple simulated or single-object settings and cannot handle stochastic multi-object real-world dynamics. No prior self-supervised object-centric model could be trained end-to-end on complex real-world multi-object video while supporting stochastic dynamics and diverse conditioning modalities (actions, language, goal images).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264339/cropped_page0_idx14.webp)

## Input / Output

LPWM takes a sequence of T RGB frames (typically 128×128) and optionally conditioning signals—discrete control actions, natural-language instructions, or goal images—and autoregressively generates a rollout of τ future frames. Internally, each frame is decomposed into M foreground latent particles (each carrying a 2D keypoint, bounding-box scale, depth, transparency, and appearance features) plus one background particle, all discovered self-supervisedly. For the imitation-learning application, the model additionally outputs a sequence of global actions mapped from per-particle latent actions via a lightweight attention-pooling transformer.

## Method

LPWM is a four-module VAE (Encoder, Decoder, Context, Dynamics) trained end-to-end via a temporal ELBO. The key architectural novelty is the Context module, a causal spatio-temporal transformer that learns a *per-particle* latent action distribution (dimension 7) with two heads: an inverse-dynamics head that infers the latent action from consecutive particle states during training, and a latent-policy head that serves as a learned prior for stochastic sampling at inference. The Dynamics module, also a causal spatio-temporal transformer, predicts next-step particle states conditioned on current particles and their latent actions via AdaLN modulation. Unlike the predecessor DDLP, LPWM retains all M particles with fixed patch-origin identities across timesteps, eliminating explicit tracking and enabling fully parallel frame encoding. The per-particle (rather than global) latent action formulation allows the model to represent multiple simultaneous stochastic interactions—e.g., independent object movements or gripper contacts—and supports multimodal rollouts from identical initial conditions.

## Results

On stochastic video prediction, LPWM achieves state-of-the-art LPIPS and FVD across all evaluated datasets. On Sketchy-U, LPWM reaches FVD 85.45 and LPIPS 0.070, substantially outperforming DVAE (FVD 140.06, LPIPS 0.113) and PlaySlot (LPIPS 0.275). On the language-conditioned Bridge-L task, LPWM reduces FVD from 146.85 (DVAE) to 47.78, a 3× improvement. For imitation learning, LPWM achieves 100% success on OGBench task1 and 89% on task3, outperforming all baselines on those tasks, while remaining competitive on PandaPush (92.7% on 1-cube, 74% on 2-cubes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the patch-based DVAE baseline (same architecture and parameter count, no object-centric structure), LPWM wins on LPIPS and FVD across all stochastic datasets: FVD drops from 140.06→85.45 on Sketchy-U, 277.41→195.95 on Mario-U, and 146.85→47.78 on Bridge-L (language-conditioned). On BAIR-U the two methods are nearly tied (FVD 163.91 vs 164.41), suggesting DVAE can match LPWM when object interactions are simpler. Against the slot-based PlaySlot, LPWM dominates on every metric—LPIPS improves from 0.275→0.070 on Sketchy-U and 0.314→0.035 on Mario-U—attributed to per-particle latent actions vs. PlaySlot's global latent action and limited slot count. For imitation learning on PandaPush, LPWM trails EC Diffuser (94.8/91.7/89.4 vs. 92.7/74/62.1 on 1/2/3-cube) but beats EC Diffusion Policy on 2-cubes (74 vs. 38.8). On OGBench-Scene, LPWM achieves 100% on task1 and 89% on task3, surpassing both GCIVL (84/16) and HIQL (80/61), though it underperforms on task2 (6% vs. HIQL's 81%), likely due to the highly suboptimal 'play' data in that task.

## Relation to Prior Work

LPWM extends the Deep Latent Particles (DLP) lineage—moving from static image decomposition (DLP, 2022) to video prediction with explicit particle tracking (DDLP, 2024)—by eliminating the tracking requirement and introducing a per-particle latent action module. This departs from both the slot-based object-centric video prediction literature (SlotFormer, OCVP, PlaySlot), which typically uses two-stage training and global latent actions, and the general-purpose latent action video generation line (CADDY, Genie, AdaWorld), which operates on patch-based representations with a single global action vector. The key architectural departure is modeling continuous, per-particle latent actions end-to-end within a causal spatio-temporal transformer, enabling stochastic multi-object dynamics without sequential encoding or explicit tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LPWM is the first self-supervised object-centric world model trained end-to-end on real-world multi-object video, bridging a gap between interpretable particle-based representations and scalable stochastic world modeling. Its demonstrated applicability to goal-conditioned imitation learning (PandaPush, OGBench) with a single model across all tasks suggests a practical path toward efficient, interpretable robot policies. However, the model currently requires small camera motion and recurring scenarios, limiting applicability to general-purpose large-scale video; open questions include scaling to diverse domains, unified multi-modal conditioning, and integration with explicit reward modeling for reinforcement learning.
