## Problem

Existing automated jailbreaking methods suffer from 'strategy collapse': they generate semantically concentrated prompts that converge on a few well-known patterns (e.g., role-playing, negative consequence awareness), failing to probe the broader spectrum of latent vulnerabilities in aligned LLMs. This diversity gap limits comprehensive security evaluation and leaves defense systems vulnerable to undiscovered attack strategies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a harmful instruction (from the DAN or StrongREJECT benchmark) as input and produces two types of output: (1) novel abstract jailbreaking strategies, each structured as a name, underlying principle, and 80–120-word description of the attack methodology; and (2) concrete single-turn jailbreak prompts that faithfully translate a selected strategy into a deceptive prompt preserving the original harmful intent. The entire pipeline operates under a black-box threat model where the adversary only queries the target model via API and observes text responses, while maintaining white-box access to local models for strategy extraction and prompt optimization.

## Method

STAR decouples jailbreaking into two modules. The strategy generation module represents known jailbreaking strategies as steering vectors in the model's latent activation space (trained via gradient descent on paraphrase log-probability rather than contrastive pairs), applies PCA to extract orthogonal 'strategy primitives,' and synthesizes novel strategies by sampling random linear combinations of these primitives with weights drawn from N(0, λ_i). The prompt generation module trains Qwen3-4B (via LoRA) with GRPO to act as a high-fidelity 'compiler' that translates an abstract strategy plus harmful query into a concrete jailbreak prompt, using a 0–3 LLM-as-a-judge reward that penalizes intent drift (score 0), refusal (score 1), partial compliance (score 2), and rewards full harmful compliance (score 3). The key insight is that exploring the continuous activation space via PCA reveals orthogonal strategic axes invisible to text-space mutation or summarization, enabling systematic generation of semantically distinct strategies beyond the seed set.

## Results

On the DAN dataset (ASR), STAR achieves 0.77 on Llama-2-7B (its training target), 0.84 on Llama-3.1-8B, 0.86 on GPT-3.5-Turbo, 0.83 on GPT-4-Turbo, and 0.89 on Gemini-2.5-Pro, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline AutoDAN-Turbo (0.45–0.76) by 13–32 percentage points. On the StrongREJECT benchmark, STAR scores 0.93 on Llama-2-7B, 0.97 on Llama-3.1-8B, 0.97 on GPT-3.5-Turbo, 0.96 on GPT-4-Turbo, and 0.94 on Gemini-2.5-Pro, versus 0.46–0.74 for AutoDAN-Turbo. In terms of strategy diversity, STAR achieves a pairwise distance of 0.5126 and ANC of 0.3960, roughly 1.6× and 2.4× higher than AutoDAN-Turbo (0.3151 and 0.1680 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR outperforms all four baselines on the majority of target models for both ASR and StrongREJECT. The largest margin is against AutoDAN-Turbo on the training target Llama-2-7B, where STAR achieves 0.77 ASR versus 0.45 (a 32 pp gap), and 0.93 versus 0.46 StrongREJECT. Against GPTFuzz, the closest competitor on closed-source models, STAR leads by 4 pp on GPT-3.5-Turbo (0.86 vs 0.82) and 3 pp on GPT-4-Turbo (0.83 vs 0.82), while GPTFuzz narrowly edges STAR on Llama-3.1-8B (0.80 vs 0.84) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (0.86 vs 0.89). PAIR is consistently the weakest baseline, trailing STAR by 37–58 pp across targets. RLbreaker sits in the middle, with STAR exceeding it by 20–53 pp depending on the target. On the StrongREJECT benchmark the gaps widen further, with STAR reaching 0.93–0.97 versus 0.46–0.74 for AutoDAN-Turbo and 0.15–0.55 for GPTFuzz.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR builds on the representation engineering / activation engineering literature (Turner et al., 2023; Panickssery et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2023a), repurposing steering vectors not for content control but for encoding abstract jailbreak strategies in a model's latent space. It departs from prior automated jailbreaking methods (PAIR, AutoDAN-Turbo, GPTFuzz, RLbreaker) that operate entirely in text space by applying PCA to strategy-aligned steering vectors to extract orthogonal 'strategy primitives,' enabling systematic generation of novel strategies beyond the seed set. The prompt generation module distinguishes itself from RL-based jailbreak methods (RLbreaker, LLMStinger) that use RL for direct strategy discovery or text mutation; here, GRPO is used solely to train a 'compiler' that translates a given strategy into an effective prompt, decoupling exploration from exploitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the 'strategy collapse' problem that limits the coverage of existing automated red-teaming, showing that activation-space exploration can surface genuinely new attack patterns (e.g., syntactic decomposition, paradoxical choice) absent from text-based search. The decoupled architecture—where the prompt generator works effectively even with strategies from external LLMs—suggests a modular red-teaming pipeline that can be updated as new strategies are discovered without retraining. Key limitations include the white-box requirement for a local model (Qwen3-4B) during strategy generation, the significant RL training cost, and the single-turn, black-box-only evaluation setting; open questions include whether strategy primitives transfer stably across model families and whether the approach extends to multi-turn or multi-modal attacks.
