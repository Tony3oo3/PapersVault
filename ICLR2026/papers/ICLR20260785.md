## Problem

Training CLIP models requires accurate estimation of the per-sample normalization term (partition function) in the global contrastive loss, but existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: large-batch methods demand prohibitive GPU resources, while per-sample moving-average estimators (FastCLIP/SogCLR) incur optimization error that scales with the ratio of dataset size to batch size. AmorLIP attempts to use a lightweight network to predict normalizers but suffers a chicken-and-egg problem because its training objective still depends on the very quantity it tries to estimate. The paper addresses the gap of providing a principled, unified framework for jointly learning CLIP encoders and a normalizer predictor without the n/B scaling limitation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes image-text pairs, where images are processed by a ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, or ResNet-50 encoder and texts by a Transformer encoder, both producing d-dimensional cosine-normalized embeddings (d=512 or 1024). On top of these embeddings, a compact Normalizer-Prediction Network (NPN) with m=4096 learnable prototype vectors of dimension d predicts the log-normalizer for each sample via a log-sum-exp pooling operation. The trained model outputs image-text embeddings used for zero-shot classification (top-1 accuracy across 35 Datacomp tasks) and cross-modal retrieval (recall@1 on Flickr30K and MSCOCO).

## Method

NeuCLIP reformulates each sample's contrastive loss log(ε+g) via Fenchel conjugate into min_α {exp(−α)(ε+g) + α − 1}, whose optimal solution α* equals the log-normalizer. A variational analysis theorem then converts the minimization over n individual α variables into minimization over a single function α(·), which is approximated by a compact NPN: a single feedforward layer (W ∈ R^{d×m}) whose columns serve as prototypical embeddings summarizing all text/image embeddings, followed by a log-sum-exp pooling layer. The encoders and NPNs are trained under a unified objective (Eq. 12) using an alternating optimization scheme: the NPNs receive T_u=10 stochastic gradient updates per iteration (using the same batch to avoid extra forward passes), followed by one update of the CLIP model, with periodic re-initialization of NPN parameters every T_r=500 iterations using freshly sampled embeddings. This design avoids the chicken-and-egg problem of AmorLIP because the gradient of the unified objective with respect to the NPN parameters does not involve a nonlinear function of the true normalizer, enabling standard stochastic gradient methods without estimation bias.

## Results

NeuCLIP achieves the best Datacomp Average performance across all five datasets, ranging from 2.7M to 1B image-text pairs. On DFN-14M (ViT-B/32, batch 4096), NeuCLIP reaches 39.16 Datacomp Average, 33.79 ImageNet & Variants, and 26.60 Retrieval, outperforming the strongest baseline FastCLIP (38.45 / 33.03 / 25.15). On the largest dataset DFN-1B (ViT-B/16, batch 5120), NeuCLIP attains 57.34 Datacomp Average and 58.69 ImageNet & Variants, surpassing FastCLIP by 0.66 and 0.82 points respectively. The improvement is most pronounced on smaller datasets (CC3M: +3.24 over OpenCLIP) and in the later stages of training, where the NPNs better track the evolving encoders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuCLIP consistently outperforms all four baselines on every dataset and every evaluation metric (Datacomp Average, ImageNet & Variants, Retrieval). Against OpenCLIP (large-batch mini-batch contrastive loss), NeuCLIP gains +3.24 on CC3M, +3.98 on CC12M, +1.38 on DFN-14M, +0.32 on DFN-192M, and +1.09 on DFN-1B in Datacomp Average. Against FastCLIP (moving-average normalizer estimation), the margin is smaller but still positive: +0.34 to +0.71 on Datacomp Average, with the largest gap on DFN-14M (+0.71) and DFN-1B (+0.66), confirming that the O(n/B) error scaling of FastCLIP is mitigated by the neural normalizer. Against SigLIP (sigmoid loss avoiding the partition function), NeuCLIP leads by +2.89 to +3.29 on smaller datasets and +0.64 to +1.02 on larger ones. Against AmorLIP (amortized normalizer prediction with separate objectives), NeuCLIP wins by +1.07 to +2.19 on Datacomp Average, with the advantage attributed to the unified training objective and the inductive-biased single-layer NPN architecture (validated in ablation studies where replacing either component degrades performance by 0.2–0.9 points).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the global contrastive loss framework of SogCLR (Yuan et al., 2022) and its CLIP-specific instantiation FastCLIP (Wei et al., 2024), which maintain per-sample moving-average estimators for the partition function. The key departure is replacing these n per-sample estimators with a compact neural normalizer-prediction network (NPN), motivated by a convex-conjugate reformulation of the contrastive loss and a variational-analysis theorem that justifies replacing pointwise minimization over n variables with a single function approximation. Unlike AmorLIP (Sun et al., 2025), which trains its normalizer network with a separate divergence-minimization objective (creating a chicken-and-egg problem), NeuCLIP uses a unified objective where the NPN and CLIP encoders are jointly optimized, and its NPN architecture exploits the log-sum-exp structure of the optimal solution as inductive bias rather than using a generic MLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuCLIP addresses a fundamental scaling limitation of FastCLIP: the O(n/B) error factor that degrades performance on large datasets or small batches. The estimation-error experiments confirm that NeuCLIP's normalizer accuracy is largely insensitive to both batch size and dataset size, a practical advantage for resource-constrained training. The minimal overhead (~6–9% wall-clock time, <2.25% peak memory) makes the approach drop-in compatible with existing CLIP training pipelines. Open questions include whether the NPN framework extends to other contrastive or energy-based losses, whether the log-sum-exp inductive bias generalizes beyond the specific structure of the CLIP normalizer, and whether the convergence guarantee (O(ε⁻⁴) iterations) can be tightened for the practical multi-sample, multi-update algorithm.
