## Problem

RL training of LLM agents on long-horizon, sparsely-rewarded tasks faces a fundamental exploration-exploitation trade-off: entropy-based exploration is fragile under multi-turn distribution shifting, causing either irreversible entropy collapse (overfitting to few successful trajectories) or runaway divergence (excessive tool-calling that prevents answer submission). Existing remedies like SFT cold-starts compromise the policy's ability to discover novel strategies beyond the training corpus.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262213/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a text task prompt concatenated with sequential environment observations (textual descriptions for ALFWorld/WebShop, RGB grid arrays plus text for Sokoban, or code interpreter stdout/stderr for AIME) and produces structured actions (tool calls such as code execution, web search queries, or embodied commands) followed by a final answer. Context lengths range from 8K to 38K tokens depending on the task, and the tool-call reward is capped at 0.1× the outcome reward to prevent reward hacking via meaningless repeated interactions.

## Method

SPEAR extends vanilla Self-Imitation Learning with three mechanisms for progressive exploration: (1) a curriculum schedule that simultaneously warms up the SIL loss weight γ from 0→1 (cosine) and decays the tool-call intrinsic reward μ from 1→0, shifting the agent from broad skill-level exploration (frequent tool use) to focused action-level exploitation; (2) advantage recalibration for off-policy replay using a FIFO buffer of intra-group baselines and the 50th percentile as a robust policy baseline, filtering stale experiences without extra sampling; and (3) covariance-based clipping that masks tokens whose log-probability changes are highly correlated with advantage gains, preventing over-confident updates that collapse entropy. The method is plug-and-play atop GRPO, GiGPO, or the authors' Dr.BoT baseline (which combines DAPO clip-higher, Dr.GRPO normalization removal, and void-turn filtering), adding only 10–25% theoretical complexity with negligible runtime overhead.

## Results

On ALFWorld (Qwen2.5-1.5B), SPEAR boosts GRPO's success rate from 72.8% to 88.9% (+16.1%) and WebShop SR from 56.8% to 77.5% (+20.7%). On AIME25 (Qwen2.5-32B, 16K context), Dr.BoT + SPEAR reaches 60.1% mean@30, surpassing the best prior RL baseline (AFM at 59.8%) and improving over Dr.BoT alone by +6.1%. On Sokoban (Qwen2.5-VL-3B), SPEAR lifts GRPO from 67.1% to 86.7% success rate (+19.6%). Gains are consistent across 1.5B, 7B, and 32B model scales with only 10–25% extra theoretical complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPEAR consistently outperforms all RL baselines. Against GRPO (the primary baseline), SPEAR adds +16.1% ALFWorld All and +20.7% WebShop SR on 1.5B, and +7.6%/+18.5% on 7B. Against GiGPO (w/o std), gains are +5.1%/+11.8% (1.5B) and +3.9%/+8.6% (7B), confirming plug-and-play compatibility. Against PPO and RLOO, SPEAR-equipped GRPO surpasses them by 10–34% on ALFWorld All. On AIME25, Dr.BoT + SPEAR (60.1%) beats ReTool (49.3%), SimpleTIR (49.2%), ZeroTIR (33.3%), and AFM (59.8%) with a 16K context, and at 32K context reaches 61.0% vs. AFM's 59.8%. On search-augmented QA, SPEAR (GRPO) improves over Search-R1 by +2.2% average (7B) and +0.2% (14B), with the largest gains on multi-hop benchmarks (HotpotQA +9.9%, MuSiQue +2.6%, Bamboogle +8.0% for 7B). Framework baselines (ReAct, Reflexion) are far below all RL methods, confirming that RL training is essential for agentic competence.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPEAR extends vanilla Self-Imitation Learning (Oh et al., 2018) to the LLM-agent setting by introducing advantage recalibration for off-policy updates, a curriculum schedule that progressively shifts from skill-level to action-level exploration, and intrinsic reward shaping for tool-call behavior. It departs from the dominant entropy-maximization paradigm (SAC, maximum-entropy RL) which the authors show is fragile under multi-turn distribution shifting, and from SFT cold-start approaches that constrain the policy to patterns present in the training corpus. The Dr.BoT baseline is a 'bag-of-tricks' synthesis of DAPO's clip-higher and dynamic sampling, Dr.GRPO's removal of length and difficulty biases, and other industrial practices, positioned as a strong reference point rather than a novel algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play compatibility of SPEAR with GRPO, GiGPO, and Dr.BoT across embodied, web, mathematical, visual, and search-augmented tasks makes it a practical recipe for agentic RL practitioners, with only 10–25% theoretical overhead and negligible runtime cost. Two limitations remain open: (1) in highly stochastic environments with unreliable tools, sparse outcome rewards cannot reliably distinguish good from bad experiences, motivating step-wise process rewards; (2) the curriculum schedule and covariance clipping are prior-based and may not be optimal for all task types, pointing toward adaptive, confidence-driven entropy control. The finding that naive SIL causes irreversible entropy collapse in LLM agents—requiring the warm-up schedule to avoid overfitting to a handful of early trajectories—is a useful empirical warning for anyone extending experience replay to language-model policies.
