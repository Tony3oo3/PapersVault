## Problem

State-of-the-art ColPali-based multimodal retrievers achieve strong visual document retrieval performance but at the cost of massively scaled multi-vector representations (e.g., 10 MB per page for Llama-NemoRetriever-ColEmbed-3B), creating severe latency and storage bottlenecks for deployment. Additionally, purely vision-centric matching may be constrained by the modality gap in modern VLMs. The paper asks whether a lightweight text retriever can complement a vision-centric model, and argues that standard hybrid fusion (rank or score aggregation) is too coarse-grained to exploit the rich query-document interactions within each model's representation space.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form textual query and a corpus of visual document pages (PDFs containing charts, tables, figures). The primary ColPali-based retriever encodes pages as multi-vector image embeddings (e.g., 767 vectors of dimension 128 for Colnomic-7B), while the complementary text retriever encodes OCR-extracted page text as a single dense vector. At test time, GQR operates on the union of both retrievers' top-K candidate lists (K=10 by default) and outputs a re-ranked top-K document list scored by the refined primary query embedding. The method is architecture-agnostic, supporting both single-vector and multi-vector (MaxSim) similarity functions.

## Method

Guided Query Refinement (GQR) is a test-time optimization procedure that iteratively refines the primary retriever's query embedding using gradient descent guided by the complementary retriever's scores. At each of T steps, a consensus distribution p_avg is formed by averaging the primary retriever's softmax over the candidate pool (parameterized by the current query embedding z^(t)) with the complementary retriever's fixed softmax. The loss KL(p_avg ‖ p_1) is minimized via a single gradient step on z, so the update is constrained by the primary model's embedding geometry rather than directly overriding scores. After T iterations, the refined embedding z^(T) is used to re-score the candidate pool with the primary retriever's native similarity (MaxSim or cosine), producing the final ranking. The approach requires no retraining, no cross-encoder, and only two hyperparameters (learning rate α and step count T), with typical configurations around α=10⁻⁴ and T=50.

## Results

On ViDoRe 2, GQR achieves an average +3.9% NDCG@5 gain over the primary retriever across 9 model pairs, outperforming all other hybrid fusion methods (best baseline: Score Aggregation Min-Max Tuned at +3.4%). The strongest configuration, Colnomic-7B with Jina (text) as complementary model, reaches 63.1 NDCG@5 (vs. 60.3 baseline), while Llama-Nemo with Linq-Embed reaches 65.2 (vs. 63.0). Critically, the Colnomic+GQR hybrid matches or exceeds Llama-Nemo (62.9 NDCG@5) while being ~14× faster (181 ms vs. 2,591 ms per query) and requiring ~54× less index memory (0.20 MB vs. 10.6 MB per document). On ViDoRe 3 (zero-shot), GQR consistently improves all model pairs, with gains up to +4.4 NDCG@5 on individual subsets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GQR outperforms all standard hybrid fusion baselines on ViDoRe 2: ranking-level methods (RRF, Average Ranking) actually degrade performance by ~3% on average, while score-level methods (Min-Max, Softmax) yield only +0.4% and +1.5% respectively; even the best tuned score aggregation (Min-Max Tuned, +3.4%) falls short of GQR's +3.9%. Against the strongest standalone model Llama-Nemo (62.9 NDCG@5, 2,591 ms, 10.6 MB/doc), the Colnomic+GQR hybrid with Linq-Embed matches it at 62.7 NDCG@5 in 181 ms (14× faster, 54× less memory), and with Jina (text) exceeds it at 63.0 NDCG@5 in 350 ms (7× faster, 28× less memory). Compared to the MonoQwen2-VL cross-encoder reranker, GQR achieves comparable NDCG@5 while being 21× faster for Colnomic, 16× faster for Jina (vision), and 2× faster for Llama-Nemo. On ViDoRe 3 (zero-shot, no per-subset tuning), GQR maintains a +2.89% average gain with the lowest standard deviation (0.67) among all positive-gain methods, confirming robustness without hyperparameter tuning. The text-only retrievers (Linq-Embed, Qwen3, Jina text) individually underperform the ColPali-based vision models by 5–16 NDCG@5 points, yet their complementary signal through GQR consistently improves the primary retriever, demonstrating that even weaker models provide useful guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

GQR sits at the intersection of two established threads: test-time query optimization (Sung et al. 2023; Yu et al. 2021; Gangi Reddy et al. 2025), which iteratively refines query embeddings via gradient descent using pseudo-relevance feedback, and hybrid retrieval (Bruch et al. 2023; Cormack et al. 2009), which aggregates outputs from multiple retrievers. The key departure is replacing the expensive cross-encoder feedback signal with a lightweight bi-encoder of a different modality, and operating at a granularity between score-level fusion and full representation-level alignment. This allows the complementary model's signal to be filtered through the primary model's embedding geometry rather than overriding scores directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: GQR enables ColPali-based retrievers to match or exceed the strongest available model (Llama-Nemo) at 14× lower latency and 54× less index storage, pushing the latency-memory Pareto frontier for visual document retrieval. The finding that even substantially weaker text retrievers (16+ NDCG points below the primary model) provide consistent, non-trivial guidance challenges the assumption that useful test-time feedback requires a stronger model. Open questions include whether the learning-rate/steps tradeoff can be resolved via scheduling, whether the approach generalizes beyond visual document retrieval to other multimodal tasks, and whether the modest per-query gains compound meaningfully in downstream RAG pipelines.
