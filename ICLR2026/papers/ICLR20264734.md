## Problem

Existing jailbreak attack techniques suffer from two fundamental limitations: they rely on fixed auxiliary models that do not adapt as the attack progresses, and they depend on a single attack strategy that can be neutralized by targeted defenses (e.g., perplexity-based filters). No prior work has jointly optimized attacker and defender within a game-theoretic framework to produce diverse, defense-robust jailbreak strategies with theoretical convergence guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264734/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In the white-box variant (W-ADJ), the method takes full LLM parameters and a set of harmful prompts (50 from AdvBench or HarmBench) and produces N diverse jailbreak strategies, each decomposed into K-step attack prompts, along with corresponding defense strategies and harmful scores. In the black-box variant (B-ADJ), parameter optimization is replaced by in-context learning: the attacker and defender LLMs consume historical evaluation records (strategy descriptions, prompts, and harmful scores) as context to generate the next round of strategies without gradient access. Evaluation uses GPT-4 as an impartial judge to assign harmful scores (HS) and a reject-list check for attack success rate (ASR).

## Method

ADJ models the jailbreak process as a Stackelberg multi-objective game (SMOG) where an attacker LLM (leader) and defender LLM (follower) engage in iterative Hegelian dialectic cycles—thesis (attack strategy), antithesis (defense counter-strategy), synthesis (refined attack)—jointly optimizing three objectives: attack effectiveness, defense robustness, and language capability. To handle the non-smooth, high-dimensional multi-objective gradient landscape, the method maps the parameter space into a Hilbert space via orthogonal Haar wavelet decomposition, then solves a convex minimum-norm problem in the resulting wavelet subspace to construct a common descent direction that simultaneously improves all three objectives. Armijo backtracking line search regulates step sizes to guarantee convergence to a Pareto-Nash equilibrium, whose existence is formally proven under compactness and continuity assumptions. The framework is instantiated in both white-box (gradient-based parameter updates) and black-box (in-context learning with accumulated evaluation history) settings, with the same model serving as attacker, defender, and target.

## Results

On the AdvBench dataset, W-ADJ (white-box) achieves an average ASR of 88% and HS of 93.33% across LLaMA-2-7B, Mistral-7B, and Vicuna-7B, outperforming the best baseline (PAP) by 31.71% in HS and 13.9% in ASR. B-ADJ (black-box) achieves an average ASR of 79.43% and HS of 89.71% across all seven evaluated models (including GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro, DeepSeek R1, and DeepSeek V3), outperforming the best baseline by 23.14% in HS and 10.29% in ASR. Under RAIN defense, W-ADJ exhibits only a 0.66% drop in ASR and 2% drop in HS, compared to an average baseline drop of 18.22% (ASR) and 18.73% (HS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADJ outperforms all seven baselines across every model and metric. Against the strongest baseline PAP (prompt-based, 40 human-crafted strategies), W-ADJ leads by 34–45 percentage points in HS on open-source models (e.g., 84% vs. 50% on LLaMA-2-7B) and B-ADJ leads by 18–33 points (e.g., 78% vs. 52% on GPT-4o). Among LLM-based baselines, PAIR and TAP are the weakest in HS (6–44%), while AutoDAN-turbo is the strongest LLM-based competitor (up to 64% HS on Vicuna-7B), yet still trails B-ADJ by 12–48 points. Model-based baselines GCG and I-GCG are limited to white-box open-source models and achieve substantially lower HS (29–56%) than W-ADJ (84–92%). Bijection, an encoding-based prompt method, is the closest ASR competitor on some models (e.g., 81% vs. B-ADJ's 88% on Vicuna-7B) but lags significantly in HS (31% vs. 76%), indicating its prompts are more easily rejected by safety filters. Under RAIN defense, W-ADJ's ASR drops only 0.66% versus an 18.22% average drop for baselines, confirming superior robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant single-objective, single-strategy paradigm in jailbreak research (GCG, PAIR, TAP, AutoDAN-turbo) by reformulating the attack as a Stackelberg multi-objective game structured around iterative Hegelian dialectic cycles (thesis-antithesis-synthesis) between an attacker and defender LLM. The optimization machinery draws on the nonsmooth multi-objective descent framework in Hilbert spaces (Sonntag et al., 2024), adapting Haar wavelet projection to construct common descent directions across the three objectives (effectiveness, robustness, language capability). Unlike prior LLM-based jailbreak methods that rely on a fixed auxiliary model as optimizer, ADJ jointly optimizes both attacker and defender parameters, eliminating the adaptability bottleneck identified in PAIR, TAP, and AutoDAN-turbo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-objective game formulation addresses a recognized gap: single-strategy jailbreaks are brittle against targeted defenses (e.g., perplexity-based filters nullify GCG-style suffixes), and the dialectic structure naturally produces diverse strategies that resist such defenses, as evidenced by the 0.66% ASR drop under RAIN versus 18.22% for baselines. The theoretical guarantees (Pareto-Nash equilibrium existence and convergence via Armijo rules) are rare in the jailbreak literature and provide a principled stopping criterion. Open questions include whether the white-box parameter-optimization variant transfers to truly closed-source models, whether the framework scales to multi-turn or multi-agent settings, and whether the same game-theoretic dialectic structure can be repurposed for defense-side strategy generation.
