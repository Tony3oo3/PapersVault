## Problem

Existing planning benchmarks for LLMs (ACPBench, PlanBench, TRAC) rely on boolean or multiple-choice questions that do not capture the generative nature of real planning decisions, where a planner must produce actions from a large action space rather than select from a shortlist. This paper asks whether current LLMs can actually generate the specific outputs that symbolic planners consume—such as all applicable actions in a state or the next action that reduces goal distance—and finds that even frontier models fail on most such tasks.

## Input / Output

Each question presents a natural-language description of a PDDL planning domain and problem instance (objects, current state, available actions, goal) followed by an open-ended prompt for one of 8 reasoning tasks. The expected output is a free-form generative answer: a complete list of applicable actions, two lists of positive/negative state effects, a single unreachable proposition or 'None', a single unreachable action or 'None', an integer index of the first inapplicable plan step, a simplified action sequence, a landmark proposition, or a single next action. Answers are parsed with a lenient grammar-based parser and validated symbolically (not via string matching or LLM-as-judge), with some validators requiring PSPACE-complete planning queries.

## Method

The paper converts the 7 boolean/MCQ tasks from ACPBench into open-ended generative versions and introduces a new 'next action' task, yielding 8 tasks across 13 PDDL domains (1,040 questions total). For each task, a dedicated symbolic validator is designed: simple set-comparison for applicability and progression, off-the-shelf planner invocations for reachability, action reachability, landmarks, and next action (all PSPACE-complete), and subsequence-plus-plan-checking for justification. Questions are generated by sampling intermediate states from top-quality plans (K* search) or diverse plans, then translating PDDL to natural language via templates. Fifteen models (small 8B through 405B LLMs and reasoning models o1-preview, o1-mini, DeepSeek R1, GPT OSS) are evaluated with 2-shot prompting at temperature 0, revealing that no single model dominates all tasks and that action reachability and applicability remain below 12% and 44% accuracy respectively even for the best reasoning models.

## Results

Across 1,040 open-ended questions (10 per domain × 13 PDDL domains × 8 tasks), no single model dominates all tasks. Among large language models, GPT-4o achieves the best overall performance with 78% on progression, 62% on validation, and 55% on next action, but only 25% on applicability and 1% on action reachability. Among reasoning models, o1-preview leads with 89% on progression and 80% on next action, yet still scores only 12% on action reachability and 26% on validation. With the exception of progression (where most large models exceed 65%), all tested models score below 65% on the majority of tasks, and small/medium models (8B–34B) peak at just 43% on the easiest task (progression).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o is the top-performing language model, winning 5 of 8 tasks, but is outperformed by Mixtral 8x22B on reachability (37% vs. 32%) and by DeepSeek V3 on action reachability (5% vs. 1%) and justification (65% vs. 54%). Among reasoning models, o1-preview leads on 6 of 8 tasks (e.g., 89% progression vs. 79% for GPT OSS 120B, 80% next action vs. 78%), while o1-mini surprisingly outperforms it on validation (78% vs. 26%) and reachability (60% vs. 66% is close). DeepSeek V3 remains the best model overall on justification (65%), beating even o1-preview (46%) and o1-mini (44%). Small and medium models (Granite 3.1 8B, Llama 3.1 8B, DeepSeek Coder 33B, Granite 34B) are uniformly weak, with progression as their best task (36–43%) and applicability/action reachability near zero (0–2%). The generative format is substantially harder than the boolean/MCQ formats from ACPBench: GPT-4o's error rate roughly doubles or triples on generative questions compared to the original ACPBench formats, except for validation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ACPBench (Kokel et al., 2025), which introduced seven reasoning tasks about action, change, and planning in boolean and multiple-choice formats, by converting all tasks to open-ended generative questions and adding an eighth task (next-action selection) that corresponds to optimal planning. It positions itself against other PDDL-based planning benchmarks—PlanBench, TRAC, AutoPlanBench, and ActionReasoningBench—distinguishing itself through the use of dedicated symbolic validators (rather than LLM-as-judge or string matching) and by covering all eight atomic reasoning tasks that symbolic planners deploy. The generative formulation is motivated by the observation that real planners must produce actions from a large action space rather than select from shortlisted options, making the closed-form ACPBench questions insufficient for evaluating planner-integrable LLM capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides a concrete, reproducible, and symbolically validated way to measure whether LLMs can serve as components of automated planners, a question that prior end-to-end planning benchmarks could not answer at the level of individual reasoning steps. The consistent finding that no tested model—including frontier reasoning models like o1-preview—exceeds 65% on most tasks, and that even basic applicability identification peaks at 44%, suggests a substantial gap between current LLM capabilities and the reliability required for planner integration. Key limitations include coverage of only classical PDDL domains (closed-world, deterministic, fully observable), evaluation of final answers only (not reasoning traces), and a lenient parser that may misattribute multi-answer responses. Open questions include whether chain-of-thought training data specific to planning could close the gap, and whether the benchmark extends to richer planning formalisms (hierarchical, probabilistic, or partially observable).
