## Problem

Existing 3D mesh compression methods face a fundamental tension: explicit vertex-and-connectivity encoders (Draco, V-DMC) suffer severe geometric distortion and topological breakage at low bitrates, while dense implicit field representations (TSDF/SDF volumes) incur cubic memory growth that caps practical resolution at 128³, limiting geometric fidelity. No prior approach combines the continuous surface precision of implicit distance fields with the data efficiency of sparse representations to achieve high-resolution mesh compression.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh (watertight or non-watertight) as input and first converts it into a sparse SDF tensor at resolutions ranging from 192³ to 512³, retaining only grid points within one voxel diagonal of the surface. The output is a compact bitstream comprising entropy-coded quantized latent features and G-PCC-encoded coordinates, which decodes back to a reconstructed mesh via Sparse Marching Cubes. Variable-rate operation is achieved by applying the same model at different input resolutions, and for dynamic sequences the overfitted decoder parameters are additionally entropy-coded into the bitstream.

## Method

The framework has two components. SIR (Sparse Implicit Representation) stores SDF values only on a sparse set of grid points near the surface, reducing data volume by an order of magnitude versus dense SDF while preserving the same geometric precision at a given resolution; a coarse-to-fine sampling strategy accelerates SDF computation. SNC (Sparse Neural Compression) is a lightweight sparse convolutional autoencoder (16 channels, 5 ResNet blocks, 8-bit quantized, ~0.42 MB total) that downscales the sparse SDF tensor into compact latent features and reconstructs it with occupancy-based voxel pruning after each upscaling step. Training uses end-to-end rate-distortion optimization combining MAE on SDF values, BCE on occupancy predictions, and a factorized entropy model for bitrate estimation. Surface recovery employs an adapted Sparse Marching Cubes that extracts triangles only from cubes whose eight vertices are present in the sparse tensor, enabling efficient reconstruction and natural handling of non-watertight surfaces.

## Results

The proposed SIR-SNC method achieves BD-BR gains (bitrate savings) of 50–61% over G-PCC, 32–91% over SparsePCGC, 57–94% over Draco, 30.5% over V-DMC, and 39–51% over NeCGS, measured by Chamfer Distance across the Mixed, ShapeNet, and MPEG test sets. The method uses a compact 0.42 MB model with core encoding/decoding times of approximately 0.1 s each, and total decoding plus surface reconstruction completes in 0.12 s. On the ShapeNet dataset (82k/162k verts/faces), the largest gains are observed: 92% BD-BR reduction vs. Draco and 91% vs. SparsePCGC, reflecting the advantage of implicit representation on high-complexity meshes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines spanning mesh coding, point cloud compression, and SDF-based compression. Against Draco (mesh coding), SIR-SNC achieves the largest absolute gains (57–94% BD-BR), with the most dramatic improvement on ShapeNet (−92%) and MPEG (−94%), where complex geometries expose Draco's vertex-quantization and topological-simplification limitations. Against V-DMC (the latest MPEG mesh standard), the method saves 30.5% bitrate on the MPEG test set, where V-DMC's subdivision-based approach still suffers from low-bitrate geometric deformation. Against G-PCC (standardized point cloud compression), gains are consistent at 50–61% across all datasets, as PCC's discrete point representation inherently cannot capture continuous surface details. Against SparsePCGC (learning-based PCC), the advantage ranges from 32% (MPEG) to 91% (ShapeNet), with the smaller MPEG gain attributed to the human-body geometry being more amenable to point-based encoding. Against NeCGS (the closest SDF-based competitor), SIR-SNC saves 39–51% bitrate, primarily because NeCGS is capped at 128³ resolution due to its dense volumetric representation, whereas SIR-SNC operates at 192–512 resolution with sparse tensors, enabling finer geometric detail capture at equivalent or lower bitrates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three established threads—implicit SDF-based geometry representation, sparse convolutional networks for 3D data, and learned autoencoder compression—into a unified sparse mesh compression pipeline. It departs from prior SDF compression work (Tang et al. 2020, NeCGS 2025) by replacing dense volumetric grids with a sparse SDF tensor that stores values only near the surface, and from explicit mesh/point-cloud coders (Draco, V-DMC, G-PCC) by avoiding vertex/topology or point-to-surface reconstruction entirely. The dynamic-sequence extension repurposes the implicit neural representation idea (overfitting decoder parameters to a sequence) to exploit inter-frame correlation without explicit motion estimation, a conceptual borrowing from video neural rendering rather than from mesh coding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.42 MB model achieving ~0.1 s encode/decode with 30–90% BD-BR savings over diverse SOTA baselines makes neural mesh compression practically deployable for bandwidth-constrained applications (VR, streaming), a regime where prior neural methods were too slow or too large. Key open questions remain: the mesh-to-SDF pre-processing still runs on CPU (~0.4 s), dynamic encoding requires per-sequence training (offline only), and texture compression is not addressed. The paper also flags UDFs as a path to further improve open-surface boundary artifacts, and the resolution-agnostic variable-rate scheme hints at scalable coding as a future direction.
