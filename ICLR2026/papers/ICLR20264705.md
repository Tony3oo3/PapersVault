## Problem

Existing bilevel optimization algorithms require lower-level strong convexity or PL conditions to guarantee convergence to small hypergradients, yet Chen et al. (2024) showed that general convex lower-level problems are intractable for this goal. This paper identifies a tractable intermediate class—lower-level uniform convexity (LLUC) with exponent p ≥ 2—that interpolates between strong convexity (p=2) and general convexity, and develops the first non-asymptotic convergence theory and algorithm for this setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes stochastic oracle access to the upper-level function f and lower-level function g, specifically stochastic gradients and Hessian-vector products, along with the uniform convexity exponent p (which must be known a priori). It produces an ε-stationary point x for the hyperobjective Φ(x) = f(x, y*(x)) with Õ(ε^(-5p+6)) oracle complexity. A key structural requirement is that the lower-level function g must be (μ,p)-uniformly convex in y and satisfy a relaxed smoothness condition ‖∇²_yy g‖ ≤ L₀ + L₁‖∇_y g‖, which is incompatible with standard L-smoothness on unbounded domains.

## Method

The paper's core theoretical contribution is a novel implicit differentiation theorem under LLUC that replaces the standard Hessian-based formula with a generalized derivative with respect to [y]^(p-1), yielding a hypergradient formula and establishing that the hyperobjective is Hölder-smooth (exponent 1/(p-1)) rather than Lipschitz-smooth. The UniBiO algorithm combines three mechanisms: (1) a warm-start phase using Epoch-SGD to initialize the lower-level variable, (2) periodic lower-level updates every I iterations via a variant of Epoch-SGD with a shrinking ball strategy (projecting iterates onto a ball of radius D_k/2^(k/p)), and (3) upper-level updates via normalized stochastic momentum (x_{t+1} = x_t − η·m_t/‖m_t‖) at every iteration. The hypergradient is approximated through a Neumann series expansion of the inverse generalized Hessian. When p=2, the complexity reduces to Õ(ε^(-4)), matching the known optimal rate for strongly convex lower-level problems.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is an oracle complexity of $\widetilde{O}(\epsilon^{-5p+6})$ for finding an $\epsilon$-stationary point of the hyperobjective under lower-level uniform convexity with exponent $p$. When $p=2$ (recovering strong convexity), this reduces to $\widetilde{O}(\epsilon^{-4})$, matching the known optimal rate for stochastic bilevel optimization under LLSC. Empirically, on the synthetic bilevel task, UniBiO converges for all tested values $p \in \{2,4,6,8\}$ in both deterministic and stochastic (Gaussian noise) settings, with convergence speed degrading as $p$ increases, consistent with theory. On the data hypercleaning task (SNLI, label-flip probability $\tilde{p}=0.1$, $\ell_3$ regularizer), UniBiO achieves higher training and test accuracy than all baselines while also demonstrating superior computational efficiency (lower running time for comparable accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares UniBiO against three bilevel optimization baselines: StocBiO (Ji et al., 2021), TTSA (Hong et al., 2023), and MA-SOBA (Chen et al., 2023). On the synthetic bilevel optimization task, all three baselines are designed for strongly convex lower-level problems ($p=2$) and are not directly applicable when $p>2$; UniBiO is the first algorithm to handle the general $p \geq 2$ case, and its convergence curves confirm the predicted degradation in complexity as $p$ increases. On the data hypercleaning experiment (SNLI with $\tilde{p}=0.1$, $p=3$), UniBiO achieves higher training and test accuracy than StocBiO, TTSA, and MA-SOBA, as shown in Figure 2. Additionally, UniBiO demonstrates stronger computational efficiency, reaching comparable or better accuracy in less wall-clock time than the baselines. The detailed numerical comparisons and additional experiments (including $p=4$) are deferred to Appendices E.1 and E.2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bilevel optimization literature that has been dominated by lower-level strong convexity (p=2) or PL-condition assumptions since Ghadimi & Wang (2018). It departs from that lineage by identifying lower-level uniform convexity (p≥2) as a tractable intermediate class between strong convexity and general convexity, where the latter was shown intractable by Chen et al. (2024). Algorithmically, UniBiO borrows the periodic-update structure from BO-REP (Hao et al., 2024) and the Epoch-SGD subroutine from Hazan & Kale (2014), but replaces the standard implicit differentiation with a novel theorem that handles the singular Hessian arising when p>2, and uses normalized momentum (Cutkosky & Mehta, 2020) adapted to Hölder-smooth objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first non-asymptotic convergence guarantee for bilevel optimization under lower-level uniform convexity, filling a gap between the well-understood strong-convexity regime and the intractable general-convexity regime. The O(ε^(-5p+6)) complexity matches optimal rates at p=2 up to logarithmic factors, but it remains open whether this bound is tight for p>2. A key practical limitation is that the algorithm requires prior knowledge of the uniform convexity exponent p, and designing an adaptive or universal algorithm that works without this knowledge (in the spirit of Nesterov's universal methods) is identified as an important open problem. The data hypercleaning application with p-norm regularization provides a concrete setting where LLUC arises naturally, but the empirical validation is limited to a single NLP dataset and small-scale synthetic tasks.
