## Problem

Existing hallucination detection methods for LLMs are designed around single-step autoregressive generation signals (token logits, hidden states from one forward pass), making them poorly suited for diffusion LLMs (D-LLMs) where hallucination signals emerge and evolve across a multi-step denoising trajectory. The hallucination problem in D-LLMs remains largely unexplored, with no method that explicitly exploits the intermediate denoising dynamics that distinguish hallucinated from factual outputs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the token-level entropy trace A ∈ ℝ^{T×B×D} extracted from the D-LLM's denoising process (where T is the number of denoising steps, B the batch size, and D the vocabulary dimension), concatenated with sinusoidal time embeddings. It produces two outputs: a binary temporal mask M ∈ {0,1}^{T×B} identifying which denoising steps are most informative for hallucination, and a scalar hallucination probability in [0,1] per sample. Unlike multi-sampling baselines that require 128+ forward passes for stable estimates, TraceDet operates on signals naturally exposed during a single denoising run, requiring no additional sampling.

## Method

TraceDet formulates the D-LLM denoising process as a Markov Decision Process, where each action is the model's full-sequence prediction at one denoising step, and the collection of actions forms an 'action trace.' Guided by the information bottleneck principle, it jointly optimizes a sub-instance extractor g_θ (a lightweight Transformer encoder with cross-attention that produces a probabilistic mask via Gumbel-Softmax sampling) and a sub-instance predictor f_φ (temporal aggregation followed by a two-layer MLP) under the objective L = L_cls + β·L_ext. The classification loss L_cls minimizes cross-entropy between predicted and ground-truth hallucination labels, while the extraction regularizer L_ext is a sum of per-step Bernoulli KL divergences that penalizes the mask from retaining too much of the full trace, enforcing a minimally sufficient sub-trace. This avoids the need for explicit step-level supervision: the mask is learned end-to-end to select the steps most predictive of hallucination, with the hyperparameter τ controlling the expected fraction of retained steps and β balancing information preservation against compression.

## Results

TraceDet achieves an average AUROC of 72.0% on LLaDA-8B-Instruct and 80.8% on Dream-7B-Instruct across TriviaQA, HotpotQA, and CommonsenseQA, outperforming the strongest baseline (EigenScore) by 8.8% and 11.0% AUROC respectively. The method also attains the highest F1 scores across all dataset-model combinations (e.g., 89.7% on CommonsenseQA-128 with Dream-7B-Instruct). Inference time is 147.52s for 100 samples, comparable to the fastest baselines (CCS at 140.73s, TSV at 160.31s) while being substantially faster than multi-sampling methods like Semantic Entropy (801.35s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TraceDet outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset-model combination. Among output-based methods, Semantic Entropy (Kuhn et al., 2023) is the strongest on LLaDA-8B (55.9% avg AUROC) but collapses to 44.1% on CommonsenseQA, while Lexical Similarity (Lin et al., 2023) reaches 66.5% on Dream-7B yet remains 14.3% below TraceDet. Among latent-based methods, EigenScore (Chen et al., 2024a) is the closest competitor at 63.2% (LLaDA-8B) and 69.8% (Dream-7B) average AUROC, still trailing TraceDet by 8.8% and 11.0% respectively. TSV (Park et al., 2025) achieves 75.6% on TriviaQA-128 with Dream-7B but drops to 56.8% on CommonsenseQA-64, showing high variance across tasks. CCS (Burns et al., 2022) consistently underperforms, scoring below 60% average AUROC on both models. Perplexity and LN-Entropy (Ren et al., 2022; Malinin & Gales, 2020) are the weakest baselines, with Perplexity unable to produce stable results on Dream-7B due to numerical instabilities in estimating token-level log-likelihoods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends hallucination detection from the autoregressive LLM setting to diffusion LLMs, where the multi-step denoising process invalidates single-step signals (token logits, hidden states) that underpin existing output-based and latent-based methods. Conceptually, it repurposes the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000) from its typical role in feature selection to temporal sub-trace extraction over denoising steps, and adopts the MDP formulation of diffusion decoding (Black et al., 2024) to define the action trace. The method departs from prior IB applications (e.g., Bai et al., 2025 for VLMs) by operating on sequential, stepwise generation signals rather than spatial or token-level features, and from AR-LLM detection by exploiting the full denoising trajectory rather than a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As D-LLMs (LLaDA, Dream) scale to 8B parameters and approach AR-LLM performance, the absence of reliable hallucination detection is a critical barrier to deployment in safety-sensitive domains. The paper's identification of three distinctive D-LLM hallucination patterns (interleaving, inconsistent guesses, persistent errors) provides a diagnostic vocabulary absent from AR-LLM literature. However, the evaluation is limited to two open-source D-LLMs and three QA datasets with only 400 samples each, and several baselines (Perplexity, LN-Entropy) are inapplicable to one of the two models, weakening the comparative evidence. Open questions include whether the IB-based sub-trace selection generalizes to other D-LLM architectures with different noise schedules, and how to move from detection to active mitigation of the identified hallucination patterns.
