## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks are constructed in a task-oriented manner where different task samples are drawn from different data distributions, making it impossible to measure how lower-level perceptual capabilities synergistically support higher-order reasoning. They also lack contemporary, real-world imagery and a hierarchical multi-level evaluation structure that mirrors the progression from perception to understanding to reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260098/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the 3.4K high-resolution real-world photographs (53% published after January 2025, manually collected from social media) is annotated with questions for all eight tasks simultaneously, enabling image-invariable prompt evaluation from basic perception to compositional reasoning within a unified distribution. The Scene Knowledge Inference task uniquely separates 'thought paths' from 'final answers' via a think token, providing finer-grained supervision for test-time scaling and R1-style reinforcement learning. Evaluation uses AP@50 for object detection, ACC@0.5 for visual grounding, and LLM-as-judge accuracy for remaining tasks, with majority voting over multiple candidate responses for reasoning tasks.

## Method

LENS organizes 60K+ human-authored questions across eight tasks into three progressive tiers—perception (Object Counting, Detection, Existence), understanding (Relation Extraction, Visual Grounding, Region-wise OCR), and reasoning (Spatial Relationship Comprehension, Scene Knowledge Inference)—all grounded in the same image set to enable controlled cross-task synergy analysis via Pearson correlations and regression. The paper further proposes SMEC, a self-driven multi-expert collaborative framework in which a single MLLM iteratively generates role-specific expert descriptions (e.g., spatial analyst, text interpreter) through a meta-generation prompt, dynamically updates the prompt when semantically redundant experts emerge, discards low-information descriptions, and synthesizes a consensus answer via a collaboration prompt. Unlike tool-calling or fixed multi-agent systems, SMEC requires no external modules or hand-crafted personas; all expert instantiation, screening, and coordination occur through the model's own generative loop, yielding consistent ~3% accuracy gains on reasoning tasks across model scales.

## Results

On the Lens benchmark, no evaluated model exceeds 60% accuracy on the reasoning tier (SRC, SKI), with Gemini 2.5 Pro achieving the highest SKI score of 59.31% and SRC of 56.20%. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads with 54.79% on SKI and 53.65% on SRC, while InternVL3-78B reaches 47.44% AP₅₀ on object detection. The proposed SMEC framework improves SKI accuracy by +3.44% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 39.80%→43.24%) and +3.12% on the full test set (Qwen2.5-VL-32B: 51.54%→54.66%), consistently outperforming Self-Refine (+0.71%) and Majority Voting (+0.86%) under the same setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, Gemini 2.5 Pro dominates on 6 of 8 tasks (OC, OD, OE, RE, OCR, SRC, SKI), outperforming GPT-4o by up to 11.1 points on OCR (61.95 vs. 42.86) and 5.1 points on SKI (59.31 vs. 55.20); GPT-4o does not support OD or VG. Among open-source generalist MLLMs, Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads on VG (44.98%), OCR (68.51%), and SRC (53.65%), while InternVL3-78B edges it out on OD (47.44 vs. 43.48) and OE (84.87 vs. 85.67 is actually Qwen2.5-VL-72B). The reasoning-specialized QVQ-Max approaches closed-source SKI performance (58.86%) despite lacking OD and VG, suggesting test-time scaling can partially compensate for perceptual gaps. For the SMEC framework, it consistently outperforms both Self-Refine (40.51%) and Majority Voting (40.66%) on the SKI task with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, achieving 43.24% with 3 iterations (+3.44 over direct inference), and the gain scales to +3.12% on the full test set with Qwen2.5-VL-32B. The benchmark itself differentiates from prior multimodal benchmarks (MMBench, V*, SPEC, etc.) by being the only one covering all five capability dimensions (attribute, counting, localization, relation, reasoning) with interleaved image-text and 53% of images published after January 2025.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant task-oriented benchmark paradigm (MMBench, V*, SPEC, MMVP, etc.) where different task samples come from different data distributions, making it impossible to isolate how lower-level perceptual skills causally support higher-order reasoning. By annotating the same 3.4K real-world images across all eight tasks, Lens enables controlled cross-task synergy analysis that prior benchmarks structurally cannot support. The proposed SMEC framework also carves new ground relative to both tool-calling approaches (which require external modules) and fixed multi-agent systems (which rely on hand-crafted personas), instead using self-generated role-specific prompts within a single MLLM to instantiate a dynamic expert panel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a persistent and quantified gap: no evaluated model (including GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and reasoning-specialized QVQ-Max) exceeds 60% on reasoning tasks, and visual grounding remains a severe bottleneck even at 72B scale (~45%). The well-supported synergistic effects analysis (Pearson correlations, regression, and controlled context-injection experiments) provides the first systematic evidence that perception and understanding are not merely prerequisites but active enablers of reasoning, with OCR playing a disproportionately central role. Open questions include whether the hierarchical structure generalizes beyond the 12 Chinese-centric scenarios, whether SMEC's ~3% gains scale to more complex reasoning chains, and whether the benchmark's 3.4K-image scale is sufficient for robust statistical conclusions across 8 tasks.
