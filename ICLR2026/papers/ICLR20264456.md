## Problem

Standard MSE-based learning objectives for multistep time-series forecasting treat each future step as an independent, equally weighted task, ignoring two structural properties of the label sequence: (1) residual autocorrelation among future steps even after conditioning on the history, which makes MSE a biased objective, and (2) heterogeneous uncertainty across forecast horizons that calls for non-uniform task weights. Existing corrections (FreDF, Time-o1) only achieve marginal rather than conditional decorrelation and still assign uniform weights, leaving both challenges unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a history window X ∈ R^{H×D} (H=96 for ETT/Weather/ECL, variable for PEMS) and outputs a full forecast sequence Ŷ ∈ R^{T×D} for horizons T ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720} (or {12, 24, 36, 48} for PEMS). The distinguishing element is a learned T×T weighting matrix Σ, parameterized via its Cholesky factor to enforce positive semi-definiteness, which is estimated once before model training and then held fixed during the standard gradient-descent training loop. No additional input is required at inference time; the weighting matrix affects only the training objective.

## Method

QDF replaces the standard MSE with a quadratic-form negative log-likelihood L_Σ = (Y − g_θ(X))ᵀ Σ̄ (Y − g_θ(X)), where Σ̄ is the inverse of the conditional covariance of the label sequence given X; off-diagonal entries of Σ̄ capture label autocorrelation and non-uniform diagonals encode heterogeneous task weights. Because the true Σ is intractable to estimate from a single label sequence, QDF learns a proxy Σ via a bilevel (meta-learning-style) optimization: an inner loop trains the forecast model on a data split D_in with Σ held fixed, and an outer loop updates Σ to minimize the resulting model's loss on a disjoint holdout split D_out, with gradients backpropagated through the updated model parameters. The training set is further partitioned into K=3 chronological subsets to reduce overfitting of Σ to any single distributional regime, and the iteration stops when the Frobenius-norm change in Σ falls below 1e-4. After convergence, the learned Σ is frozen and the forecast model is trained end-to-end on the full training set using the quadratic objective, making QDF a model-agnostic drop-in replacement for MSE in any direct-forecasting architecture.

## Results

QDF (using TQNet as the underlying forecast model) achieves the best MSE and MAE across all 8 long-term forecasting datasets, with results averaged over forecast horizons T=96, 192, 336, and 720. The most dramatic gains appear on traffic data: PEMS03 MSE drops from 0.119 (TQNet) to 0.089 (QDF), a 25% reduction, and PEMS08 MSE drops from 0.139 to 0.120 (14% reduction). On the ECL dataset, QDF achieves MSE 0.165 / MAE 0.257, outperforming the next-best TQNet (0.175 / 0.265) and the strongest non-Transformer baseline DLinear (0.212 / 0.301). On the M4 short-term forecasting benchmark, QDF achieves the best average OWA of 0.851 (TQNet) and 0.866 (PDF), outperforming all learning-objective alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QDF outperforms all 10 forecast-model baselines (TQNet, PDF, Fredformer, iTransformer, FreTS, TimesNet, MICN, TiDE, PatchTST, DLinear) on every dataset in Table 1, with the largest margins on PEMS03 (MSE 0.089 vs. 0.119 for TQNet, 0.181 for PDF) and PEMS08 (MSE 0.120 vs. 0.139 for TQNet, 0.210 for PDF). Against learning-objective baselines (Table 2), QDF consistently beats Time-o1, FreDF, Koopman, and Soft-DTW on both TQNet and PDF; for example, on ECL with TQNet, QDF achieves MSE 0.165 vs. 0.167 (Time-o1), 0.168 (FreDF), 0.166 (Koopman), and 0.623 (Soft-DTW). Among meta-learning methods used to optimize the weighting matrix (Table 4, ECL dataset), QDF's bilevel optimization outperforms iMAML, MAML, MAML++, and Reptile across all forecast horizons, e.g., at T=720 QDF achieves MSE 0.202 vs. 0.205 (iMAML/MAML/MAML++) and 0.206 (Reptile). The canonical DF baseline (identity weighting matrix) is the weakest across all comparisons, confirming that both the autocorrelation and heterogeneous-weight components contribute to QDF's gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

QDF positions itself at the intersection of likelihood-based loss design and meta-learning for time-series forecasting. It departs from the same group's prior work (FreDF, Time-o1), which transforms labels into decorrelated components but achieves only marginal rather than conditional decorrelation, and from shape-alignment objectives (Soft-DTW, Koopman) that lack theoretical guarantees for bias elimination. The bilevel optimization for learning the weighting matrix borrows the structural template from MAML/Reptile-style meta-learning, but repurposes it for a fundamentally different goal: constructing a static, generalization-targeted training objective rather than enabling rapid adaptation to new tasks. Conceptually, the quadratic-form NLL with a learned conditional covariance connects to Gaussian process regression, but its application as a plug-in training objective for arbitrary direct-forecast architectures is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling case that learning-objective design is an underexplored axis of improvement in time-series forecasting, orthogonal to the heavily studied architecture design. Its model-agnostic, training-only overhead (<2 ms) makes it a practical drop-in enhancement for any direct-forecast model. The key limitation is the assumption of a static weighting matrix; the authors themselves suggest a hyper-network generating input-dependent Σ as a natural next step, which could substantially increase expressiveness. An open question is whether the gains (typically 1–3% MSE reduction) compound when combined with future architectural advances, or whether the objective-level headroom is already near its ceiling for the current model class.
