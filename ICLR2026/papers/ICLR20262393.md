## Problem

Existing gradient-based data attribution methods either treat all network parameters uniformly or rely on implicit Hessian-based reweighting that only partially captures the functional heterogeneity of parameters. The paper shows empirically that attribution quality varies dramatically across parameter groups (by depth, by functional component such as self-attention vs. cross-attention), yet no prior method explicitly learns parameter importance weights directly from data to account for this heterogeneity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262393/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query sample (image or text sequence), a training dataset, and a trained model whose parameters are partitioned into M disjoint groups (e.g., individual tensors or layers within a UNet or Transformer). It produces (i) weighted attribution scores for every training example relative to the query, (ii) a non-negative weight vector of length M (one scalar per parameter group, enforced via softmax), and (iii) optionally separate specialized weight sets for fine-grained semantic attribution (subject, style, background). The weights are applied only to the query-side gradient features, while training-side kernel terms are precomputed once, keeping attribution scalable.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified parameter-weighted attribution framework where the score between a query and a training example is g(query)ᵀ · Diag(w) · K · g(training), with K being the identity (TracIn) or a kernel matrix (TRAK, D-TRAK, DAS, etc.), subsuming all major gradient-based methods. A self-supervised loss maximizes the average top-k attribution score normalized by the ℓ₂ norm of the full score vector, which the paper derives (Appendix A) as a principled proxy for maximizing the signal-to-noise ratio under a per-group signal-plus-noise model, with optimal weights proportional to αⱼ/σⱼ². The top-k index set is re-evaluated at every optimization step, allowing the method to bootstrap progressively better pseudo-positives without any ground-truth attribution labels. For fine-grained attribution, the same procedure is repeated with query sets curated to emphasize a single semantic element (e.g., varying only the background in prompts), yielding specialized weight vectors that up-weight the parameter groups most responsible for that element. Weight learning is extremely efficient—typically under one minute—because only M scalar weights are optimized and group-wise attribution contributions are precomputed.

## Results

On ImageNet classification, learned parameter weights more than double TracIn's LDS on ResNet-18 (11.39→23.92) and improve TRAK from 16.86→23.30. On diffusion models (Table 5), improvements are consistent across all five attribution methods and four datasets, with the largest gains on CIFAR-2 (e.g., TracIn 1.39→8.48) and JourneyTRAK on ArtBench-2 (11.56→16.00). On WikiText-103 language modeling, EKFAC improves from 15.14→18.33 LDS and TracIn from 6.31→9.23. For fine-grained attribution on SB-Pokemon, specialized weights boost subject Recall@10 from 22.1→51.6 (train) and style from 64.9→82.1, while the unweighted baseline scores only 96.0% on the combined 'All' metric versus 98.0–99.8% with specialized weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's learned parameter weights consistently improve every baseline attribution method across all evaluated domains. TracIn (Pruthi et al., 2020) and TRAK (Park et al., 2023) show the largest relative gains: on ResNet-18/ImageNet, TracIn's LDS more than doubles (11.39→23.92) and TRAK improves by 38% (16.86→23.30). On diffusion models, JourneyTRAK (Georgiev et al., 2023) benefits most on ArtBench-2 (11.56→16.00, +38%), while D-TRAK (Zheng et al., 2023) and DAS (Lin et al., 2024) show more modest but consistent gains (e.g., DAS on SB-Pokemon: 33.55→36.12). For language modeling, LoGRA (Choe et al., 2024) improves from 11.42→12.86 LDS and EKFAC (Grosse et al., 2023) from 15.14→18.33 on WikiText-103. On the fine-grained attribution task, the method substantially outperforms the Rep. Dist. baseline (Singla et al., 2023): subject Recall@10 jumps from 22.0 to 51.6 (train) and style from 10.5 to 82.1, while the combined 'All' metric goes from 38.2 to 98.0–99.8. No baseline is ever degraded by the learned weights; the smallest improvement is DAS on ArtBench-2 (+1.11 LDS), while the largest is TracIn on CIFAR-2 (+7.09 LDS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-based data attribution lineage (TracIn, TRAK, Influence Functions) by introducing explicitly learned, non-negative parameter-group weights that reweight the contribution of each layer or tensor to the attribution score. Prior methods either treat all parameters uniformly (TracIn) or derive implicit reweighting from Hessian approximations (TRAK's kernel, EKFAC), which the authors argue are noisy and indirect proxies for true parameter importance. The work also draws conceptual parallels to parameter importance in pruning and knowledge localization, but positions itself as the first to explicitly model parameter heterogeneity in the data attribution setting. The self-supervised loss bootstraps from rankings of an existing base method, making it a plug-in wrapper rather than a replacement for the underlying attribution mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learned weights provide a simple, efficient (converges in under a minute) drop-in improvement that consistently boosts attribution accuracy across classification, language modeling, and diffusion, and additionally enables fine-grained semantic attribution (subject vs. style vs. background) that was not previously accessible through gradient-based methods. Key limitations include the coarse layer-wise granularity of the weights and the inherited inductive bias from the base attribution method used for bootstrapping. Open questions include whether parameter-wise (rather than layer-wise) weights are feasible without overfitting, and whether the approach transfers to architectures and training regimes far from those tested (e.g., very large LLMs or video diffusion).
