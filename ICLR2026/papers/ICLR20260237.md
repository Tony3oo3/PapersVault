## Problem

Attribute correlations in training data cause deep generative models to fail at synthesizing under-represented (minority) attribute combinations, since the independent effects of each attribute on the image can only be observed in the small number of samples where the correlation does not hold. Existing mitigations—re-sampling, pseudo-supervision from classifiers trained under the same correlations, or inductive-bias decomposition—each suffer from limited sample diversity, unreliable supervisory signals, or strong prior-knowledge requirements, leaving the core challenge of learning correct minority generations under strong correlations largely unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method conditions on two correlated attribute variables x1 and x2 (e.g., digit and color, or object class and corruption type) plus a latent code z, and outputs a full-resolution RGB image. Crucially, x2 is not used directly: it is decomposed via a learned predictor m(x1) into a predictable component and a residual γ = x2 − m(x1), so the generator is actually conditioned on the orthogonal pair (x1, γ). The residual γ is broadcast to match the spatial dimensions of intermediate feature maps and concatenated as extra channels; for high-dimensional x2 (e.g., zigzag endpoint coordinates in MNIST-Correlation), only the unpredictable dimensions form γ and may be used to mask predictable ones.

## Method

ProReGen applies Robinson's partialling-out transformation to recast the generation task from learning g(x1, x2) under correlated inputs to learning g̃(x1, γ) under orthogonal inputs, where γ captures the part of x2's effect on the image that cannot be explained by x1. It then trains progressively in two stages: Stage I learns a backbone generator g_mjr(x1) ≈ g̃(x1, γ=0) from abundant majority samples, and Stage II expands it with lightweight residual layers g_res(h_mjr(x1), x1, γ) trained on limited minority samples to resolve the difference between majority and minority generations. The residual sub-networks are deliberately small (0.5–5% of Stage-I parameters for VAE/GAN), maintaining spatial dimensions via 3×3 convolutions with stride 1. The approach is instantiated in conditional VAEs (expanding decoder/encoder with mirrored residual blocks), conditional GANs (expanding generator/discriminator analogously), and conditional diffusion models (injecting frozen Stage-I U-Net features into a trainable Stage-II denoiser in a ControlNet-style fashion). An ablation confirms that progressive two-stage training is essential: simultaneous training of both stages collapses minority correctness from ~93% to ~36% on Colored-MNIST.

## Results

On Colored-MNIST at 95% correlation, ProReGen-GAN achieves 0.9256 minority correctness and 17.26 FID with two-stage progressive training, versus 0.3557 correctness and 65.02 FID with simultaneous (single-stage) training of the same architecture. The method's performance is highly sensitive to the assumed causal direction: with digit→color, minority correctness reaches 0.9396±0.0016, but inverting to color→digit drops it to 0.0811±0.0127. ProReGen remains robust to errors in the attribute-prediction function m(x₁), retaining 0.8589 overall correctness even at 80% perturbation, still outperforming the naive model. Stage-II training adds only ~2% computational overhead over Stage-I across all model families (VAE, GAN, DM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProReGen consistently outperforms naively-trained c-VAE, c-GAN, and c-DM baselines in minority generation correctness across all correlation strengths, with the largest gains observed for c-GAN and c-DM. Against Causal-cHVAE (Ribeiro et al., 2023), which uses a classifier for pseudo-supervision on counterfactual generations, ProReGen achieves comparable or better minority correctness while maintaining stable majority correctness; Causal-cHVAE's improvement in minority correctness comes at the cost of degraded majority correctness at higher correlation strengths. Causal-GAN (Kocaoglu et al., 2017) fails to improve minority correctness over the naive baseline on MNIST-Correlation, with FID metrics similar to the unmodified model. Re-weighting (upsampling minority samples) achieves comparable correctness to ProReGen but exhibits clear overfitting in minority generations, reflected in rapidly degrading coverage metrics and visually limited diversity as correlation strength increases. On CelebA with natural gender–hair-color correlations, ProReGen-DM produces consistently realistic minority images where the naive c-DM fails in both attribute correctness and generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Robinson's partialling-out transformation (1988) from econometrics as the conceptual foundation for designing conditional deep generative models under attribute correlations, a departure from existing strategies that rely on re-sampling, pseudo-supervision via classifiers, or inductive bias for mechanism decomposition. Rather than attempting to recover independent causal mechanisms g1 and g2 from correlated inputs x1 and x2, ProReGen orthogonalizes the inputs by decomposing x2 into its predictable component m(x1) and residual γ, then progressively learns the generator in two stages: a majority-sample backbone g_mjr(x1) followed by a lightweight residual expansion g_res conditioned on γ. This positions the work as a fundamentally different perspective from prior DGM-under-correlation literature, which has focused on data-level interventions or architectural constraints rather than input-space reformulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProReGen addresses a practical and underexplored gap: how to reliably synthesize under-represented attribute combinations when training data exhibits strong correlations, a problem that affects counterfactual explanation, data augmentation, and fairness in generative AI. The progressive residual design is computationally efficient (stage-II adds only ~2% overhead) and architecture-agnostic across VAEs, GANs, and diffusion models, making it broadly applicable. Key limitations include the assumption of discrete attribute subgroups, operation at the pixel/image level rather than latent space, and sensitivity to the assumed causal direction between attributes (inverting digit→color to color→digit drops minority correctness from ~0.94 to ~0.08). Open questions include extending to continuous attributes, latent-space residual generation, and evaluating under the sparsity and heavy-tailed distributions characteristic of text-to-image models.
