## Problem

Generating street-level 3D scenes from a single satellite image faces a stark trade-off: geometry-colorization methods produce clean building geometry but miss non-building semantics, while proxy-based feed-forward image-to-3D methods capture holistic content but yield coarse, unstable geometry with floating artifacts, warped boundaries, and unrealistic rooftops. The paper attributes these geometric failures to the extreme aerial-to-ground viewpoint gap and the extremely sparse, inconsistent supervision available from one satellite patch paired with only a few ground-level panoramas.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 satellite image (Google Maps zoom level 20) and an optional global illumination feature extracted from a real street-view image to control rendering lighting. It produces a tri-plane NeRF radiance field (320×320×32×3 feature planes) that can be rendered as satellite views, 512×128 panoramic street views, 256×256 perspective street-view images at arbitrary camera poses, and exported as a watertight mesh via Marching Cubes. The model can also render satellite-view metric depth (DSM) without any metric-depth supervision during training.

## Method

Sat3DGen uses a frozen DINOv3 ViT encoder to map the satellite image to a 16×16×1024 token grid, which is padded with zero-valued spatial tokens (expanding to 20×20) to regularize scene boundaries, then decoded by a VAE-style upsampler into a 320×320 tri-plane feature field. A lightweight MLP predicts density and illumination-conditioned color for volumetric ray-marching rendering, with a spherical sky feature map ensuring consistent sky appearance across panoramic and perspective views. Three novel geometry-oriented components address the core failures: a Gravity-based Density Variation Loss that penalizes density increasing with altitude to suppress floating artifacts, a satellite-view depth regularization using Depth Anything v2 pseudo-labels with a scale-and-shift invariant MiDaS loss to resolve rooftop ambiguity, and a perspective-view training strategy that jointly supervises on panoramas and randomly projected perspective crops to densify effective viewpoint coverage. Training combines photometric (L2 + LPIPS + StyleGAN2 hinge GAN) losses across satellite, panorama, and perspective views with the geometric regularizers, all under a weighted sum objective.

## Results

On the VIGOR-OOD test set (Seattle, out-of-domain), Sat3DGen achieves a FID of 19.2 and KID of 0.014 for street-view rendering, substantially outperforming the leading proxy-based method Sat2Density++ (FID 40.8, KID 0.035) and diffusion-based baselines ControlNet (FID 23.6) and ControlS2S (FID 28.0). For geometric accuracy, measured against 1 m-resolution LiDAR-derived DSM ground truth, Sat3DGen reduces RMSE from 6.76 m (Sat2Density++) to 5.20 m and MAE from 4.72 m to 3.47 m, with 62.69% of surface pixels within 2.5 m error (vs. 49.69% for Sat2Density++). The method also leads on semantic alignment (DINO 0.525 vs. 0.465) and structural similarity (SSIM 0.37 vs. 0.34).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary state-of-the-art proxy-based method Sat2Density++ (Qian et al., 2026), Sat3DGen wins decisively on every reported metric: FID drops from 40.8 to 19.2 (a 53% reduction), KID from 0.035 to 0.014, DINO semantic similarity rises from 0.465 to 0.525, and geometric RMSE improves from 6.76 m to 5.20 m. The older Sat2Density (Qian et al., 2023) is far behind on all metrics (FID 85.6), confirming the gains from the geometry-first methodology. Against diffusion-based image generation baselines ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023) and ControlS2S (Ze et al., 2025), Sat3DGen achieves lower FID (19.2 vs. 23.6 and 28.0) and lower KID, demonstrating that a view-consistent 3D representation handles the extreme aerial-to-ground viewpoint gap more effectively than 2D diffusion. The geometry-colorization methods Sat2Scene (Li et al., 2024b) and Sat2City (Hua et al., 2025) are compared only qualitatively; both produce building-only shells that miss non-building semantics (roads, trees, crosswalks), whereas Sat3DGen generates holistic, semantically faithful scenes. Notably, the internal ablation 'Canonical Image-to-3D' baseline (same backbone without the proposed geometry modules) already improves RMSE to 6.21 m over Sat2Density++, but the full model's additional losses and spatial tokens push it to 5.20 m while simultaneously halving FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proxy-based feed-forward image-to-3D paradigm established by Sat2Density and Sat2Density++ (Qian et al., 2023; 2026), which learn a differentiable 3D radiance field from satellite-ground image pairs under 2D photometric supervision. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it elevates the existing tri-plane NeRF backbone by introducing three geometry-specific regularizers (gravity-based density variation loss, spatial tokens for boundary stabilization, and a monocular relative-depth prior from Depth Anything v2) plus a perspective-view training strategy, drawing conceptual inspiration from object-level feed-forward reconstruction models (LRM, InstantMesh) that demonstrated high-quality 3D from 2D supervision alone. It explicitly departs from the geometry-colorization line (Sat2Scene, Sat2City) that produces building-centric outputs by committing to the proxy paradigm while addressing its geometric weaknesses through targeted constraints rather than architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that geometry-first regularization in a feed-forward 3D scene generator directly improves photorealism (FID 40→19) without dedicated image-quality modules, suggesting that geometric and perceptual quality are more coupled than previously assumed in this setting. The new VIGOR-OOD + King County LiDAR DSM benchmark enables the first quantitative geometric evaluation (RMSE, MAE, accuracy thresholds) for satellite-to-3D methods, filling a gap where prior work relied solely on qualitative comparison. Limitations include the flat-ground assumption, reliance on GPS-only poses without precise intrinsics/extrinsics, and evaluation on a single OOD city (Seattle), leaving open whether the geometric priors generalize to hilly terrain, atypical architectures, or non-urban environments. The downstream applications (semantic-map-to-3D, multi-camera surround-view video, large-scale meshing, unsupervised DSM estimation) broaden the practical utility beyond street-view rendering.
