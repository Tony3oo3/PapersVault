## Problem

Differentially private domain discovery algorithms (set union, top-k selection, k-hitting set) are widely deployed in industrial DP frameworks, yet almost no provable utility guarantees exist for them, making it impossible to quantify how well they recover the underlying item domain. This paper is the first to prove absolute utility guarantees for DP set union by reframing the objective from cardinality (number of unique items recovered) to missing mass (fraction of total frequency mass recovered).

## Input / Output

The input is a dataset of n users each holding a finite subset of items from an a priori unknown countable domain, along with privacy parameters (ε, δ), a user contribution bound Δ₀, and an output size k. All mechanisms enforce a soundness constraint: the output must be a subset of the union of all user item sets, which precludes pure DP and necessitates approximate (ε, δ)-DP. Quality is measured by missing mass MM(W,S) = Σ_{x∉S} N(x)/N (the ℓ₁ norm of missing frequencies), with generalizations to ℓ∞ (maximum single-item missing mass) and top-k missing mass that account for the k most frequent items.

## Method

The core mechanism is the Weighted Gaussian Mechanism (WGM): each user's item set is subsampled to at most Δ₀ items, a weighted histogram is computed with each user's contribution scaled by 1/√|W̃ᵢ|, independent Gaussian noise is added to each count, and items exceeding a threshold T are retained. The key theoretical insight is that this simple mechanism achieves near-optimal ℓ₁ missing mass on (C,s)-Zipfian data (Theorem 3.3) and a distribution-free ℓ∞ missing mass bound (Theorem 3.6) that requires no structural assumptions on the dataset. For top-k and k-hitting set, the paper composes WGM (spending half the privacy budget) with a known-domain algorithm—the peeling exponential mechanism for top-k and a user peeling mechanism for k-hitting set (spending the other half)—where the ℓ∞ missing mass bound on the discovered domain is the critical ingredient that eliminates the need for Zipfian assumptions in these downstream guarantees.

## Results

For set union, the WGM achieves missing mass (MM) within 5% of the Policy Gaussian and Policy Greedy mechanisms across all six datasets, while being significantly more computationally efficient. For top-k selection on the three small datasets (Steam Games, Amazon Magazine, Amazon Pantry), the WGM-then-top-k method consistently achieves smaller top-k MM than all limited-domain baselines, with the advantage growing as k increases from 5 to 200. For k-hitting set, the WGM-then-peeling method performs comparably to the non-private greedy algorithm and outperforms the known-domain private greedy algorithm (which assumes public knowledge of the full domain) on Steam Games and Amazon Magazine. All experiments use (1, 10⁻⁵)-DP with Δ₀ = 100 for top-k and k-hitting set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Policy Gaussian mechanism (Gopi et al., 2020) and Policy Greedy mechanism (Carvalho et al., 2022) for set union, the WGM achieves MM within 5% of both baselines across all six datasets, while being far more scalable since it avoids sequential per-user computation. Against the limited-domain top-k mechanism (Durfee & Rogers, 2019), the WGM-then-top-k method wins on all three small datasets across all values of k ∈ {5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200}, and the gap widens with larger k; this holds for both the top-k MM metric and the more stringent top-k ℓ₁ loss. Against the private non-domain algorithm from Mitrovic et al. (2017) (run with ∪ᵢ Wᵢ treated as a public known-domain, which is not a valid private algorithm in the unknown-domain setting), the WGM-then-peeling method performs comparably overall but actually outperforms it on Steam Games and Amazon Magazine, because the WGM domain discovery step produces a smaller domain that still contains high-quality items, making the subsequent peeling step easier. The paper also compares against the non-private greedy algorithm for k-hitting set, which serves as an upper bound; the WGM method approaches this bound while maintaining full differential privacy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Weighted Gaussian Mechanism (WGM) from Gopi et al. (2020) for DP set union, but is the first to prove absolute utility guarantees for this problem, reframing the objective from cardinality to missing mass. It extends the WGM as a domain-discovery precursor to known-domain algorithms: the peeling exponential mechanism for top-k (building on Durfee & Rogers, 2019) and the privatized greedy submodular maximization for k-hitting set (building on Mitrovic et al., 2017). A key conceptual departure is the distribution-free ℓ∞ missing mass guarantee (Theorem 3.6), which avoids the Zipfian assumption required for the ℓ1 bound and enables the downstream top-k and k-hitting set results without distributional restrictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first provable absolute utility guarantees for DP set union and the first private algorithms with guarantees for unknown-domain top-k and k-hitting set, filling a significant gap where prior work offered only relative comparisons or no guarantees at all. The practical finding that the simple, scalable WGM matches or outperforms more complex sequential/policy mechanisms within 5% missing mass suggests that practitioners can use a much simpler pipeline without sacrificing utility. Open questions include closing the gap between upper and lower bounds for top-k and k-hitting set, and whether data-dependent subsampling (as in Chen et al., 2025) can improve the missing mass guarantees beyond uniform subsampling.
