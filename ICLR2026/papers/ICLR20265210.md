## Problem

Existing prompt-aware generative model selection methods (e.g., PAK-UCB) optimize only fidelity scores such as CLIP-Score, ignoring the diversity of generated outputs. Because diversity is a group-level property determined by the relative positioning of multiple samples, it cannot be expressed as a simple average of per-sample rewards, making it fundamentally incompatible with standard contextual bandit reward formulations. This paper addresses how to incorporate diversity-aware objectives into online model selection while retaining the theoretical guarantees of kernelized UCB.

## Input / Output

At each round, the algorithm receives a text prompt (embedded via CLIP) as context and selects either a single generative model or a mixture weight vector over G candidate models. The selected model produces one output (image or text), which is embedded (DINOv2 for images) to form an unbiased diversity label using a stage-frozen archive of past (prompt, output) pairs. The method requires only one generated sample per prompt per round, and the product kernel k_text(t,t')·k_data(x,x') operates on the joint prompt-output space to capture prompt-conditional diversity structure.

## Method

DAK-UCB extends the kernelized UCB framework by defining two prompt-aware diversity scores—Joint Kernel Distance (JKD) and Joint RKE (JRKE)—as expectations over the joint (prompt, data) variable using product kernels. The key structural insight (Proposition 1) is that both scores decompose into expectations of a prompt-level function of a single generated sample, enabling online estimation via kernel ridge regression with unbiased stochastic labels. The per-arm UCB score combines an upper confidence bound on fidelity (e.g., CLIP-Score) with a lower confidence bound on the signed diversity penalty, and the algorithm selects the arm maximizing this combined optimistic estimate. Mixture-DAK-UCB further generalizes to prompt-dependent mixture selection by solving a concave quadratic program over the simplex at each round, with a kernel-Lipschitz constraint ensuring nearby prompts yield similar mixtures. A phased variant (Sup-DAK-UCB) is analyzed to establish a regret bound of Õ(√(GTΓ_T^(s)) + λ√(GTΓ_T^(D))), extending standard kernelized UCB guarantees to the diversity-aware objective.

## Results

On the MS-COCO text-to-image experiment (2000 iterations, 10 prompt clusters, Kandinsky/SDXL/GigaGAN), Mixture-DAK-UCB achieves the highest Joint-RKE diversity score and the best Joint Kernel Distance (JKD) score against MS-COCO reference images, outperforming PAK-UCB, Random Selection, and One Arm Oracle. In the simulated animal-generation experiment (500 rounds, 3 arms), DAK-UCB preferentially selects the more diverse unconditioned arm (Arm 3) over the cat- and dog-conditioned arms, whereas PAK-UCB (CLIP-Score only) selects the less-diverse dog-conditioned arm more frequently. On the LLM diversity experiment (Llama3.2, Qwen2, Gemma3), the Mixture-DAK-UCB-selected mixture attains substantially higher Cond-Vendi diversity than any single model, exploiting complementary collapse modes across the three LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is PAK-UCB (Hu et al., 2025a), a diversity-unaware contextual bandit that selects models solely on CLIP-Score fidelity. DAK-UCB consistently outperforms PAK-UCB on diversity metrics (Joint-RKE, Cond-Vendi) while maintaining comparable fidelity (CLIP-Score, JKD). Specifically, in the MS-COCO experiment, Mixture-DAK-UCB achieves the best Joint-RKE and JKD scores, while PAK-UCB lags on diversity. In the simulated animal experiment, PAK-UCB over-selects the less-diverse dog-conditioned arm, whereas DAK-UCB correctly favors the unconditioned (more diverse) arm. Against Random Selection and One Arm Oracle, DAK-UCB and Mixture-DAK-UCB also achieve superior diversity scores, confirming that the online adaptive selection with diversity awareness provides meaningful gains over both uniform random assignment and static best-model selection. The paper also notes that in the expert-selection experiment (Figure 4), DAK-UCB avoids the failure mode of generating prompt-irrelevant content to artificially inflate diversity, a concern that does not arise with the fidelity-only PAK-UCB baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the kernelized contextual bandit framework for prompt-aware generative model selection (PAK-UCB, Hu et al. 2025a; Valko et al. 2013) by incorporating diversity-oriented objectives into the UCB reward. The key technical bridge is showing that Joint-RKE and Joint Kernel Distance scores—conditional extensions of the RKE (Jalali et al., 2023) and MMD/KD (Binkowski et al., 2018) metrics—admit a two-sample expectation decomposition that is compatible with kernel ridge regression and standard UCB confidence bounds. The Mixture-DAK-UCB variant further generalizes the prompt-free Mixture-UCB algorithm (Rezaei et al., 2025) to the conditional, prompt-aware setting, enabling prompt-dependent mixtures of generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in model routing: fidelity-only selection can systematically underutilize more diverse models, leading to collectively homogeneous outputs even when individual samples are prompt-aligned. The two-sample decomposition insight is the main technical contribution, as it identifies which diversity metrics are structurally compatible with online bandit estimation—a constraint that rules out many natural diversity scores. Limitations include the theoretical analysis being restricted to a phased (Sup-DAK-UCB) variant, experiments at relatively small scale (500–2000 rounds), and the framework being limited to kernel scores with the specific two-sample structure. Open questions include extending to other modalities (protein, molecular, graph generation), identifying additional diversity metrics amenable to the same decomposition, and studying the framework in non-stationary or adversarial prompt distributions.
