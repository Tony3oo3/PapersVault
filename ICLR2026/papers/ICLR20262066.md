## Problem

Despite rapid progress in 3D molecular representation models, no existing benchmark evaluates whether representations can discriminate between conformers of the *same* molecule. Prior benchmarks (MoleculeNet, Molecule3D, MARCEL) treat molecules as static entities or focus on inter-molecular tasks like property regression, leaving open whether representations preserve geometric variation, capture chirality, and align with the conformational energy landscape.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 3D conformers (atomic coordinates + atom types) from three curated datasets: ~10M relaxed torsional-scan conformers (2.5° increments, xTB-optimized), ~52K chiral drug-candidate conformers with validated R/S labels, and 100K AIMD conformers per molecule with DFT energies and forces. For each conformer set, the framework computes pairwise representation distances (cosine distance for learned embeddings, Tanimoto for binary fingerprints), then scores them against physical references (RMSD, chirality-signature Hamming distance, |ΔE|) using rank correlations, CKA, isotonic R², and manifold-level metrics such as local isometry error, enantiomer-separation AUC, and energy-jump sensitivity.

## Method

The core contribution is the Geometry-Chirality-Energy (GCE) evaluation framework, organized in two layers. Layer 1 (Reference Alignment) quantifies how well pairwise representation distances track physical references via Spearman/Kendall rank correlations, RBF-kernel CKA, and isotonic-regression R². Layer 2 (Manifold Consistency) probes structural coherence beyond pairwise scores: Local Isometry Error tests neighborhood preservation, angular smoothness measures representation change per degree of dihedral rotation, enantiomer separation is scored by ESA-AUC, NN1-accuracy, silhouette coefficients, and Hopkins statistics, and energy-jump sensitivity (EJS) measures the conditional probability that large energy gaps imply large representation separations. The framework is model-agnostic and applies uniformly to handcrafted descriptors (E3FP) and learned encoders (UniMol, MolAE, MolSpectra, GemNet, MACE, FMG). Datasets are constructed via programmatic scaffold assembly with xTB relaxed scans and DBSCAN deduplication (geometry), ChEMBL-filtered stereoisomer enumeration with torsional perturbation and R/S re-validation (chirality), and Revised MD17 AIMD trajectories (energy). Empirical results show learned models excel at geometry but fail at chirality (coordinate-denoising objectives can invert stereocenters) and energy alignment, with only energy/force-supervised models (GemNet, MACE) and the field-based FMG encoder showing strong chirality and energy sensitivity.

## Results

On the geometry benchmark, UniMol achieves the highest Spearman correlation (0.697) with RMSD, while MolSpectra attains the best CKA (0.904) and lowest Local Isometry Error (0.238), outperforming the E3FP fingerprint baseline (Spearman 0.406, CKA 0.757). On chirality, MolAE leads with ES-AUC of 0.782 and NN@1-Acc of 0.497, while the field-based FMG encoder achieves the best unsupervised silhouette (0.509) and Hopkins statistic (0.752). On energy, MACE dominates all metrics—Spearman 0.236, EJS 0.578, EJS-ROCAUC 0.764, isoR² 0.080—far exceeding GemNet (Spearman 0.078) and all other models (Spearman ≤ 0.043), confirming that explicit energy/force supervision is critical for energy-sensitive representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MACE, trained with explicit energy/force supervision, overwhelmingly outperforms all other baselines on energy metrics (Spearman 0.236 vs. next-best GemNet at 0.078; EJS-ROCAUC 0.764 vs. 0.592), confirming that force-field training objectives are essential for energy-sensitive representations. GemNet, also trained on energy/forces but with spherical-harmonic inputs, is the second-best energy model and outperforms MACE on the thresholded smoothness metric (TS 0.444 vs. 0.424), though MACE still leads on all correlation and jump-detection metrics. For chirality, MolAE (3D Cloze Test) achieves the best supervised ES-AUC (0.782) and NN@1-Acc (0.497), substantially beating UniMol (0.622) despite both using SE(3)-equivariant Transformers—the difference attributed to MolAE's atom-dropping objective avoiding chirality-flipping perturbations. FMG, the field-based voxel encoder, achieves the best unsupervised chirality separation (SCI_unsup 0.509, Hopkins 0.752) and near-perfect ES-AUC (0.999) after contrastive fine-tuning, validating that sacrificing strict E(3) equivariance for reflection sensitivity aids enantiomer discrimination. On geometry, UniMol leads in Spearman (0.697) and Kendall (0.563) correlations with RMSD, while MolSpectra achieves the best CKA (0.904) and lowest LIE (0.238), indicating superior local neighborhood preservation; E3FP trails all learned models across every geometry metric. E3FP, the handcrafted 3D fingerprint, is consistently the weakest baseline: near-random chirality separation (ES-AUC 0.486) and near-zero energy correlation (Spearman 0.026), underscoring the limitations of binary substructure encoding for conformational sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by shifting molecular representation evaluation from inter-molecular tasks (property regression, cross-molecule classification as in MoleculeNet, Molecule3D, MARCEL) to intra-molecular conformational sensitivity — whether a representation can discriminate conformers of the *same* molecule. It repurposes existing 3D encoders (UniMol, MolAE, GemNet, MACE, MolSpectra, FMG) and the E3FP fingerprint as evaluation subjects rather than as methods to improve, and introduces a unified Geometry-Chirality-Energy (GCE) framework with two layers (reference alignment and manifold consistency) that has no direct precedent in the molecular benchmarking literature. The datasets themselves — relaxed torsional scans at ~10M conformers, stereochemically validated enantiomer pairs from ChEMBL, and DFT-annotated AIMD trajectories — are new contributions that complement but do not overlap with GEOM or Molecule3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3DCS is the first systematic benchmark to quantify how well molecular representations handle the three axes that govern real-world molecular behavior (geometry, chirality, energy), and its findings — that modern learned encoders are geometry-strong but chirality-inconsistent and energy-blind — provide concrete, actionable design guidance (e.g., chirality-preserving augmentations, energy-aware objectives, spherical-harmonic bases over raw coordinates). The benchmark is model-agnostic and zero-shot, making it immediately usable for any new 3D encoder. Open questions include extending to conditional settings (protein-ligand docking, pose prediction), scaling the chirality dataset beyond ~4K molecules, and whether the energy-alignment gap can be closed without explicit force-field supervision. The PCA visualization revealing energy barriers in representation space hints at broader utility for transition-state analysis and reaction-path studies.
