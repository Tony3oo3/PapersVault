## Problem

Selecting the correct prior for Bayesian inverse imaging problems requires computing the model evidence p(y|M) under candidate diffusion priors, but this integral is intractable with existing estimators. Methods like SMC, AIS, and thermodynamic integration rely on the clean prior score or unnormalized density, which diffusion models provide poorly (inaccurate at low noise, ill-conditioned at high noise), and density-based approaches require thousands of posterior samples. This gap prevents principled model selection and validation when using state-of-the-art diffusion priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a measurement y from a forward model y = A(x) + ε (e.g., visibilities for black hole imaging, magnitude measurements for phase retrieval) and a pre-trained score-based diffusion model s_θ as the prior. It produces a scalar log-evidence estimate log p(y) alongside posterior sample reconstructions, requiring only ~20 sample paths of N annealing steps (e.g., 50 steps × 20 paths). The evidence is normalized (unlike the PnP-DM variant), enabling direct comparison across candidate priors for model selection.

## Method

DiME decomposes the model evidence into a log-likelihood term (estimated from posterior samples) minus the KL divergence between posterior and prior, then computes the KL by integrating the squared likelihood score norm along the posterior time-marginals p(x_t|y) that are naturally generated during diffusion posterior sampling. Two key technical contributions enable this: (1) an improved Gaussian covariance approximation Σ_{x_0|x_t} = [Σ_0^{-1} + (a_t²/σ_t²)I]^{-1} that incorporates the prior covariance, fixing the overestimation at high noise levels in the original DAPS heuristic; and (2) two unbiased estimators Θ_high and Θ_low for the intractable likelihood score ∇_{x_t} log p(y|x_t), with a per-timestep variance-minimizing selection (Θ_high at high noise, Θ_low at low noise) and a two-sample product trick to obtain an unbiased estimate of the squared norm. The full estimator is implemented alongside DAPS, requiring no clean prior score or density evaluations, and achieves near-unbiased evidence estimates with only 20 sample paths.

## Results

On the 1000-D Gaussian mixture benchmark with analytic ground truth, DiME achieves relative errors of 1.5% (in-distribution), 0.3% (out-of-distribution), and 0.8% (saddle point) using only 20 sample paths and 100 annealing steps, matching or exceeding the best baseline (SMC) without ever using the clean prior score. On MNIST model selection with 10 diffusion priors, DiME correctly identifies the true prior for all 100 (digit, measurement) pairs in both Gaussian and Fourier phase retrieval, while SMC frequently selects the wrong model. On real M87* EHT data, DiME ranks GRMHD as the most likely prior and assigns a z-score of −0.81 (p-value 0.209), confirming the observation is statistically in-distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiME outperforms all three score-based baselines (TI, AIS, SMC) on the Gaussian mixture benchmark while never requiring the clean prior score ∇ₓ log p(x). Specifically, DiME achieves 1.5% relative error in-distribution vs. 3.2% (TI), 5.6% (AIS), and 2.6% (SMC); 0.3% out-of-distribution vs. 5.6%, 4.4%, and 1.2% respectively; and 0.8% at the saddle point vs. 1.2%, 3.1%, and 0.7%. The advantage is most pronounced in the out-of-distribution regime, where DiME's 0.3% error is roughly 5–19× lower than the baselines. On the MNIST model selection task (10 diffusion priors, non-convex phase retrieval), DiME selects the correct prior in all 100 cases for both Gaussian and Fourier phase retrieval, whereas SMC (using the learned score as a surrogate) frequently fails, demonstrating that methods relying on a clean prior score are fundamentally unsuitable for diffusion-based model selection. The PnP-DM variant (DiME-PnPDM) performs well in-distribution (2.4%) but degrades to 13.8% out-of-distribution, making it less suitable for model selection where OOD measurements are common.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiME builds directly on two recent diffusion posterior sampling methods—DAPS (Zhang et al., CVPR 2025) and PnP-DM (Wu et al., NeurIPS 2024)—by repurposing their intermediate time-marginal samples to estimate the KL divergence between posterior and prior, rather than requiring the clean prior score or density that traditional estimators (SMC, AIS, TI, nested sampling, harmonic mean) depend on. Conceptually, it departs from the power-posterior path framework used by TI/AIS/SMC and instead integrates along the diffusion annealing path, leveraging the fact that at the terminal noise level the posterior and prior marginals coincide. The improved Gaussian covariance approximation for p(x₀|xₜ) (Lemma 1) extends the heuristic in DAPS by incorporating the prior covariance, and the two unbiased score estimators (Θ_high, Θ_low) are novel constructions that avoid direct evaluation of the intractable marginal likelihood score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiME makes principled Bayesian model selection and validation feasible with diffusion priors using only ~20 posterior samples, a regime where all prior estimators either fail or require thousands of samples. The M87* black hole application demonstrates real scientific impact: it both selects the correct physical prior (GRMHD over RIAF, SpaceNet, Faces, MNIST) and validates the prior's adequacy via prior predictive checking (z-score −0.81, p=0.209). Limitations include the reliance on posterior sampling methods that anneal to the true posterior (PnP-DM variant shows 13.8% error for out-of-distribution data), the Gaussian approximation of p(x₀|xₜ) which may be poor for highly multimodal priors, and the fact that the method inherits the computational cost of the underlying sampler. Open questions include scaling to very high-dimensional problems (e.g., full-resolution medical imaging), handling non-Gaussian noise models, and whether the estimator's accuracy degrades with fewer annealing steps in practice.
