## Problem

Persistent homology pipelines for graph learning suffer from two practical bottlenecks: expensive global boundary-matrix reductions for persistence diagram computation, and ad hoc vectorization steps that destroy the sequential structure of topological features. Additionally, standard sublevel filtrations on graphs saturate early—high-valued vertices activate first, quickly filling the complex and suppressing late-emerging topological signals—making PH-based representations less informative than their theoretical promise suggests.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262354/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unattributed or attributed graph and applies one or more scalar filtration functions (degree centrality, Ollivier-Ricci curvature, Heat Kernel Signature) to produce a fixed-length sequence of 4-dimensional topological tokens per filtration (Betti-0, Betti-1, node count, edge count per sliding-window slice), yielding 4N features where N is the number of slices. For molecular property prediction, ECFP fingerprints are additionally processed by a parallel MLP branch. The output is a graph-level embedding mapped to a class label or continuous property score, with the key constraint that the token sequence length is fixed by the filtration resolution (20 thresholds, width 2 → 19 slices → 76 features per filtration), independent of graph size.

## Method

Topo-Scan replaces the standard PH pipeline (cumulative sublevel filtration → persistence diagram → vectorization) with a sliding-window interlevel slicing scheme: rather than including all nodes up to a threshold, it extracts overlapping subgraphs over windows of width m along the filtration axis, computing Betti numbers on each slice's clique complex. This avoids early saturation and preserves late-emerging topological features, producing ordered sequences that are natively compatible with transformer positional encodings and self-attention. Multiple filtration functions are processed by independent transformer encoders whose outputs are fused via a learnable weighted sum before a classification head. The paper proves a discrete ℓ1 stability theorem showing the Topo-Scan sequences are Lipschitz-continuous in the filtration function (bounded by C·‖f−g‖∞), and demonstrates that per-slice Betti computation via union-find and sparse edge-triangle operators is fully parallelizable, yielding multi-fold runtime savings over PH on dense graphs.

## Results

On graph classification (10-fold CV), TOPOFORMER achieves the best or second-best accuracy on 7 of 8 benchmarks, with an average deviation from the best model (AvD) of 0.5 and average rank (AvR) of 1.5. It sets new state-of-the-art on BZR (92.36%), MUTAG (94.68%), PROTEINS (77.64%), IMDB-M (55.40%), REDDIT-B (91.50%), and REDDIT-5K (57.99%). On molecular property prediction (scaffold-split AUC), TOPOFORMER* achieves the best ROC AUC on ToxCast (75.3), ClinTox (96.5), and BACE (95.9), with an AvD of 2.5 and AvR of 2.8 across seven MoleculeNet datasets. On the large-scale OGBG-MOLHIV benchmark, TOPOFORMER* reaches 78.19 AUC, within ~2 points of the strongest published baseline (Graphormer, 80.51).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOPOFORMER outperforms all 15 graph classification baselines in aggregate (AvD = 0.5 vs. next-best DASP at 1.6; AvR = 1.5 vs. DASP at 2.8). Against the strongest topological baseline TopoGCL (AvD 3.5), TOPOFORMER gains 4.5–5.2 points on BZR, MUTAG, and PROTEINS, and 2.2–3.5 points on IMDB-B and REDDIT-B. Versus the most recent kernel method DASP, TOPOFORMER wins on BZR (+2.96), MUTAG (+2.78), PROTEINS (+0.44), IMDB-M (+4.20), and REDDIT-5K (+0.39), while DASP retains a narrow edge on COX2 (+0.87) and IMDB-B (+2.50). Among GNN baselines (Errica et al.), TOPOFORMER improves by 5–14 points across datasets, and among contrastive methods (RGCL, AutoGCL) by 3–8 points. On MPP, TOPOFORMER* beats KANO (the prior SOTA) on ToxCast (+2.1), ClinTox (+2.1), and BACE (+2.8), but trails it on BBBP (−6.5) and HIV (−3.9). On OGBG-MOLHIV, TOPOFORMER* (78.19) is 2.3 points below Graphormer (80.51) and 2.0 below TopER (80.21), indicating room for improvement on very large graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOPOFORMER repurposes interlevel (level-set) persistence concepts—previously used mainly in theoretical stability results (Botnan & Lesnick, 2018)—into a practical, parallelizable feature-extraction pipeline (Topo-Scan) that replaces the standard PH workflow of global persistence diagram computation followed by ad hoc vectorization. It departs from GNN-based graph learning (which learns node embeddings then pools) and from prior topology-based methods (PersLay, DMP, FC-V, TopoGCL) by producing fixed-length ordered sequences of local Betti numbers on sliding windows, which are natively consumable by Transformer encoders without graph-specific positional encodings or attention biases. The theoretical stability guarantee is a discrete ℓ₁ analogue of classical bottleneck-distance stability, adapted to the windowed interlevel setting. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a bridge between TDA and attention-based sequence models, arguing that the sequential structure lost in standard PH vectorization is precisely what Transformers are designed to exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: (1) a multi-fold computational speedup over PH pipelines (13–14× on dense graphs) with fully parallelizable slice extraction, making topological features viable in large-scale graph learning; and (2) a fixed-size, domain-agnostic sequential representation that is well-suited as an inductive bias for future graph foundation models. Key limitations include restriction to H₀/H₁ on a fixed clique complex, a small set of hand-chosen filtrations (degree, Ollivier-Ricci, HKS), and graph-level tasks only—node/edge-level prediction, learnable filtrations, and large-scale self-supervised pretraining are left to future work. An open question is whether the minimal 4-value-per-slice token set is truly sufficient or whether richer per-slice invariants (e.g., higher Betti numbers, persistent entropy) would yield further gains without sacrificing the parallelism advantage.
