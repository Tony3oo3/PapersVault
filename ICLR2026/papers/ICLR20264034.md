## Problem

State-of-the-art generative policies for robotic manipulation face a fundamental trade-off: diffusion policies require multiple denoising steps per action (slow inference), while autoregressive policies sample one token per forward pass (latency scales with sequence length) and lack memory for non-Markovian tasks. No existing method simultaneously achieves rapid closed-loop inference, high task success, and robust globally-coherent planning over long horizons with dynamic adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264034/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a conditioning vector c_t comprising T_p past observations (RGB images, depth maps, and/or 1024-point colorized point clouds), robot proprioceptive state (joint positions, base position/rotation, EE position and orientation, gripper state), and T_p past actions. It outputs a sequence of T_f future continuous actions in R^(T_f × j) covering end-effector absolute position, rotation, and gripper state. Actions are discretized via a VQ-VAE tokenizer (4 consecutive primitive actions per token, codebook of 1024–8192 entries), so the transformer operates over a compressed discrete latent space; at inference, only the VQ decoder reconstructs continuous actions from sampled tokens.

## Method

MGP is a two-stage system: (1) a VQ-VAE Action Tokenizer learns a discrete codebook mapping 4-action windows to single tokens, and (2) a Masked Generative Transformer (MGT) — an encoder-only architecture with 2 cross-attention layers (observing observation embeddings) and 2 self-attention layers over token embeddings — is trained via masked token prediction (random masking + perturbation, cross-entropy loss) to reconstruct full action-token sequences from partial masks. Two sampling paradigms are proposed: MGP-Short generates tokens in parallel with Gumbel-Max sampling and performs 2 score-based refinement iterations (re-masking the lowest-confidence tokens) for Markovian tasks, achieving 3 ms/step inference; MGP-Long predicts a full-episode trajectory (50 tokens) in one pass, then applies Adaptive Token Refinement (ATR) — a Posterior Confidence Estimation step that recomputes confidence scores for unexecuted tokens given new observations, selectively re-masks the bottom 70% by confidence, and refines only those tokens while retaining executed and high-confidence tokens as anchors. This yields globally-coherent long-horizon planning with targeted, efficient edits rather than full regeneration, enabling robustness to missing observations, dynamic environments, and non-Markovian tasks where prior methods fail entirely.

## Results

On 50 Meta-World tasks (single-task), MGP-Short achieves 63.7% average success rate at 3 ms/step (135 ms/sequence), outperforming DP3 (59.9%, 145 ms/step) and Consistency Policy (61.2%, 5 ms/step) while being 49× faster than DP3 per step. On LIBERO-90 (multi-task), MGP-Short reaches 88.9% success, matching QueST (88.6%) but at 5 ms/step vs. 17 ms/step. MGP-Long further improves to 82.0% on LIBERO-Long and achieves 100% success on two non-Markovian button-press tasks where all short-horizon baselines (DP3, QueST, MGP-Short) score 0%. In dynamic and observation-missing environments, MGP-Long improves average success by ~60% over short-horizon methods (e.g., 52.5% vs. 20.5% on observation-missing Hard/Very Hard tasks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGP-Short outperforms all diffusion-based baselines on Meta-World: it beats DP3 by 3.8% in average success (63.7% vs. 59.9%) while being 49× faster per step (3 ms vs. 145 ms), and surpasses Consistency Policy (61.2%) and FlowPolicy (57.1%) in both accuracy and speed without requiring distillation. On LIBERO-90, MGP-Short (88.9%) marginally edges out QueST (88.6%) but is 3.4× faster per step (5 ms vs. 17 ms), and substantially outperforms VQ-BeT (81.3%, +7.6%), DP (75.4%, +13.5%), PRISE (54.4%, +34.5%), ACT (50.8%, +38.1%), and ResNet-T (84.4%, +4.5%). On LIBERO-Long, MGP-Short (77.0%) beats QueST (68.0%) by 9% and DP (50.1%) by 27%, while MGP-Long further reaches 82.0%. In dynamic environments, MGP-Long (43.6%) surpasses DP3 (36.0%) by 7.6% and MGP-Short (43.0%) by 0.6%, while one-shot full-sequence methods (DP3-Full Seq. at 4.0%, MGP-Full Seq. at 10.0%) fail catastrophically. On the two non-Markovian button-press tasks, MGP-Long achieves 100% success while DP3, QueST, and MGP-Short all score 0%, and in the real-world towel-sorting task MGP-Long reaches 96% vs. DP3-Full Seq.'s 84%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the MaskGIT masked-generative paradigm (Chang et al., 2022) from image synthesis into visuomotor imitation learning, positioning it as a third sampling family alongside diffusion policies (Chi et al., 2023; Ze et al., 2024) and autoregressive token policies (Mete et al., 2024; Lee et al., 2024). The action tokenizer follows the VQ-VAE discretization approach shared with QueST and VQ-BeT, but the conditional masked transformer reconstructs full token sequences in parallel rather than sequentially, eliminating the per-token latency of autoregressive decoding and the multi-step denoising of diffusion. The key architectural departure is the Adaptive Token Refinement (ATR) mechanism with Posterior Confidence Estimation, which enables selective, confidence-driven re-masking of only uncertain future tokens during long-horizon execution—a capability absent from both diffusion and autoregressive baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MGP offers a practically significant efficiency win (35× faster per-sequence inference than DP3, 7M vs. 262M parameters) while matching or exceeding state-of-the-art success rates across 150 standard tasks, making it attractive for real-time closed-loop deployment. The demonstration that a globally-coherent, confidence-guided replanning strategy can solve non-Markovian tasks where all short-horizon and open-loop methods score zero is conceptually important for long-horizon and memory-dependent control. However, the two-stage training (VQ-VAE then MGT) introduces a potential representation mismatch, the non-Markovian and dynamic evaluations rely on custom-designed tasks with limited baselines and small rollout counts (20), and the real-world validation covers only a single task—leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more diverse, unstructured real-world scenarios.
