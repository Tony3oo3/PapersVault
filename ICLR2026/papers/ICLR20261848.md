## Problem

Standard metrics for statistical text analysis with language models—perplexity, log-likelihood, log-rank—fail to account for the shape of the conditional distribution at each position, making it impossible to interpret a given token probability without knowing how many reasonable alternatives exist. This 'contextualization problem' limits both the interpretability of text statistics and the reliability of machine-generated text detectors built on probability curvature, which the paper shows are critically vulnerable when base models use pure sampling rather than the top-k=50 default that silently affected much of the prior literature.

## Input / Output

The method takes a token sequence w_1…w_T and an evaluator language model p (as small as OPT-125m, runnable on consumer hardware in minutes), and produces T points x_1…x_T in [0,1] that jointly encode rank and probability information. For visualization, the unit interval is split into k bins (typically 40, chosen via the Terrell-Scott rule k=(2T)^(1/3)) and a histogram is plotted; for quantitative analysis, a chi-squared uniformity statistic is computed with p-values from the χ²_{k-1} distribution. The entropy-weighted variant replaces the random samples with a deterministic step function D̂(w):[0,1]→R of integral one, weighted by next-token entropy clipped at λ=2.

## Method

At each position i, DMAP orders all vocabulary tokens by decreasing probability under p, defines the interval I_i=[a_i, b_i] where a_i is the cumulative mass of tokens ranked above w_i and b_i=a_i+p(w_i|context), then samples x_i uniformly from I_i. This is analogous to the probability integral transform but with a crucial dynamic re-ordering step that encodes rank information, and Proposition 3.1 proves that when text is generated by pure sampling from p and evaluated by p, the resulting samples are i.i.d. uniform on [0,1], enabling off-the-shelf chi-squared tests for generation-parameter validation. The entropy-weighted variant removes sampling randomness by replacing each point with the characteristic function χ_{I_i}/|I_i| and weights each position by the entropy of the next-token distribution (clipped at 2), amplifying signal from high-uncertainty positions where the text carries the most information about its source. The approach is model-agnostic, requires only forward passes, and reveals distinct signatures—head bias, tail bias, tail collapse—that differentiate decoding strategies, base versus instruction-tuned models, and the statistical fingerprints of synthetic fine-tuning data.

## Results

The central quantitative finding (Table 1) shows that all three probability-curvature-based detectors (Fast-DetectGPT, DetectGPT, Binoculars) achieve AUROC of 0.61–0.95 under top-k=50 sampling but collapse to AUROC of 0.20–0.49 (worse than random) under pure sampling across Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, and Qwen3-8B on XSum, SQuAD, and WritingPrompts. DMAP's chi-squared uniformity test provides a quantitative validation tool: evaluating 10,000 tokens of Llama-generated text with Mistral-7B as evaluator yields p-values below 10⁻¹⁰, conclusively ruling out pure sampling from that model. In the SFT fingerprinting experiment, the only head-biased fine-tuned Pythia model was the one trained on temperature-1.0 Llama-generated responses, matching the most head-biased fine-tuning data in DMAP plots.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMAP is not itself a detector but a diagnostic tool that explains why existing detectors succeed or fail. Against Fast-DetectGPT (Bao et al., 2023), DetectGPT (Mitchell et al., 2023), and Binoculars (Hans et al., 2024), the paper demonstrates that all three methods are effective under top-k=50 sampling (AUROC 0.61–0.95) but systematically fail under pure sampling (AUROC 0.20–0.49, i.e., worse than a coin flip) because the probability curvature they exploit is inverted for base models generating with pure sampling. DMAP's histograms directly visualize this inversion: pure-sampled base-model text evaluated by a different base model shows tail-bias rather than the head-bias these detectors assume. The paper also identifies a critical data-integrity issue: HuggingFace's default top-k=50 setting caused many published detection papers to inadvertently use top-k-sampled text while reporting pure sampling, meaning their positive results do not generalize. In the SFT fingerprinting comparison, DMAP shows that fine-tuning on temperature-1.0 Llama responses produces the strongest head-bias in downstream Pythia models, while fine-tuning on human OASST2 data produces a dramatic tail-collapse, revealing that the statistical signature of the fine-tuning data persists in generated text.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMAP extends the classical Probability Integral Transform (PIT) to the setting of language model next-token distributions, adding a crucial step of dynamic re-ordering tokens by model probability to encode both rank and probability information in a single unit-interval sample. It is conceptually inspired by the DetectGPT line of work (DetectGPT, DetectLLM, Fast-DetectGPT, Binoculars) that exploits probability curvature for machine-generated text detection, but departs from these by providing a unified, model-agnostic statistical representation rather than a detection score. The method also generalizes GLTR's rank-based visualization into a continuous, mathematically grounded framework that addresses the 'contextualization problem'—the difficulty of interpreting raw per-token statistics without accounting for the shape of the conditional distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's discovery that HuggingFace's default top-k=50 silently corrupted data in most top zero-shot detection papers has immediate practical impact, and the finding that probability-curvature detectors perform worse than chance (AUROC < 0.5) for pure-sampled base-model text challenges a foundational assumption in the field. DMAP's practical accessibility (runs on consumer hardware with OPT-125m, open-sourced) makes it a broadly adoptable diagnostic tool. Open questions include whether DMAP signatures can formalize domain-specific model underperformance, serve as a basis for language model identification, or inform calibration strategies that go beyond perplexity matching. A limitation is that the white-box setting requires access to the evaluator model's full probability distributions, and the entropy-weighted variant sacrifices the clean i.i.d. guarantee of the base DMAP.
