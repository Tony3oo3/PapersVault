## Problem

While μP (Maximal Update Parameterization) empirically enables fast hyperparameter transfer—allowing optimal HPs tuned on small proxy models to generalize to much larger models—the underlying mechanisms remain unexplained. It is unclear when transfer is computationally advantageous over direct tuning, and whether the phenomenon is guaranteed or depends on structural properties of the optimization dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262211/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes EMA-smoothed optimization trajectories from networks of varying widths trained under μP, along with per-layer weight matrices, gradients, and parameter updates. It produces a per-layer, per-timestep spectral decomposition of the linearized loss change into a top-k component (capturing dominant descent directions) and a residual component, yielding width-invariant and width-sensitive loss curves that can be compared across scales to diagnose transfer quality. The theoretical framework additionally outputs convergence-rate quantities (a_n, b_n, c_n) that quantify whether transfer is fast and computationally useful.

## Method

The paper introduces a trajectory-level loss decomposition: after EMA-smoothing the optimization path to remove oscillatory behavior, the one-step loss change is linearized as ⟨G, δW⟩, and for each matrix layer the symmetrized alignment matrix S(G, δW) = ½(GᵀδW + δWᵀG) is eigendecomposed. The sum of the top-k eigenvalues defines the top-k loss, while the remainder defines the residual loss. The central hypothesis is that the top-k loss converges rapidly with width (making optimal HPs width-stable) while the residual loss continues to improve with width but is too flat to shift the HP optimum. The paper formalizes this via three conditions (top-k strong convexity, top-k invariance, residual flatness), proves that fast transfer (c_n = o(a_n)) is equivalent to the HP gap converging faster than the square root of the loss gap (β > α/2), and shows this condition is necessary and sufficient for transfer to beat direct grid search under a fixed compute budget. Synthetic examples (random features ridge regression for fast transfer; two-layer ReLU for slow transfer) and experiments on Llama/GPT-2 transformers and CIFAR-10 MLPs validate the decomposition, with a sample-wise extension (Mean Component Index) linking easy vs. hard examples to transfer quality.

## Results

On Llama-style transformers trained on WikiText-103 with Adam, the optimal learning rate converges rapidly with width, and transferring the width-128 optimal LR to larger widths (up to 2048) yields essentially zero suboptimality, while the reducible loss converges at rate n^{-0.52}. Under Muon, top-k invariance holds only up to k≈10 (vs. k≈60 for Adam), leading to visible transfer suboptimality at large widths. In the provable random features regression setting, the transfer suboptimality gap scales as ψ₂^{-2} while the loss gap scales as ψ₂^{-1}, confirming fast transfer. On CIFAR-10 with SGD, transfer is reliable but imperfect, with loss converging at the faster rate n^{-0.77}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Adam, Muon, and Dion optimizers for hyperparameter transfer on Llama transformers (WikiText-103). Adam exhibits the strongest top-k invariance (up to k≈60), enabling near-perfect learning rate transfer from width 128 to 2048 with overlapping scaling curves. Muon's matrix sign (whitening) step spreads loss decrease over more directions, reducing top-k invariance to k≈10 and causing visible LR drift and transfer suboptimality at large widths. Dion with bounded rank min(n/2, 128) recovers much of Adam's transfer stability compared to full-rank Muon, suggesting the whitening dimension is the key factor. In the two-layer ReLU synthetic setting, even under μP, the optimal learning rate converges at the slow 'agnostic' rate b_n ~ √a_n, showing that fast transfer is not guaranteed by μP alone. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 2) establishes that transfer is computationally useful if and only if the HP gap converges faster than the square root of the loss gap (β > α/2), a condition met by Adam but not by Muon in the tested settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Tensor Programs line of work, particularly Yang & Hu (2021) which established μP as the unique optimal parameterization for feature learning, and Yang et al. (2022) which introduced the empirical phenomenon of zero-shot hyperparameter transfer under μP. The key departure is providing the missing mechanistic explanation: prior work established that transfer works but not why, while this paper introduces a trajectory-level top-k loss decomposition to identify the low-dimensional structure responsible. It also connects to the low-dimensional optimization trajectory literature (Gur-Ari et al. 2018, Song et al. 2024) and edge-of-stability dynamics, using EMA smoothing to isolate the non-oscillatory component where genuine loss decrease occurs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first paper to provide a formal framework distinguishing weak transfer (asymptotic convergence of optimal HPs) from fast and useful transfer (computational advantage over direct tuning), and to show that the former does not imply the latter. The top-k decomposition offers a practical diagnostic for practitioners to assess whether transfer should work in a given setting, and the finding that Muon exhibits weaker top-k invariance than Adam explains its less stable transfer behavior. Open questions include quantitative characterization of the decomposition's scaling exponents, understanding the correct invariance notion for full-matrix preconditioned optimizers like Muon/Dion, and extending the sample-wise MCI analysis to language model settings where evaluation metrics may concentrate on 'hard' examples.
