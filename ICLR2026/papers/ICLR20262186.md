## Problem

Existing RoPE extension methods (PI, NTK, YaRN) are highly diverse and lack a unified theoretical foundation, making it difficult to systematically compare or improve them. The paper asks whether YaRN's linear interpolation for middle-frequency dimensions is truly optimal, and whether a principled framework can guide the design of better training-free extension strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262186/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at inference time on a pre-trained RoPE-based LLM (e.g., LLaMA3-8B trained at 8K, Qwen2.5-3B trained at 32K), requiring no fine-tuning or additional training data. It modifies the rotation angles of query and key vectors by applying dimension-wise scaling factors derived from the mixed-radix formulation, producing an extended context window (up to 16× the original training length) with no architectural changes to the model.

## Method

The key insight is that RoPE's rotation angle computation is mathematically equivalent to a biased radix (base) conversion, where each dimension corresponds to a digit position; this reframing unifies PI, NTK, and YaRN as specific radix conversion strategies parameterized by a per-dimension expansion vector λ. MrRoPE-Pro applies a progressive (monotonically increasing) radix conversion to middle dimensions, where the scaling exponent follows an arithmetic progression ε_j = 2(1+j−d_l)/((1+d_h−d_l)(d_h−d_l)), so lower high-frequency dimensions receive smaller expansions while higher low-frequency dimensions receive larger ones. This preserves fine-grained high-frequency positional information while extending the representable positional range, in contrast to YaRN's regressive (monotonically decreasing) strategy that the paper proves via algebraic manipulation. The method reuses YaRN's segmentation boundaries (d_l, d_h) and attention rescaling factor t, with the sole difference being the middle-dimension conversion policy.

## Results

On the RULER benchmark with LLaMA3-8B (8K→128K), MrRoPE-Pro achieves 86.6% retrieval accuracy at 128K context, compared to YaRN's 79.9%, maintaining stable performance where YaRN degrades sharply beyond 64K. On Infinite-Bench (100K–128K samples), MrRoPE-Pro reaches 49.8% average accuracy versus YaRN's 44%, with particularly large gains on KV Retrieval (27% vs. 9%) and QA Dialogue (22% vs. 10%). In the Needle-in-a-Haystack test, MrRoPE-Pro sustains over 85% recall at 120K tokens (15× pre-training length), extending the effective context window to ~96K. On Proofpile perplexity, MrRoPE-Pro achieves 2.34 at 128K context on LLaMA3-8B, the lowest among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MrRoPE-Pro consistently outperforms YaRN across all evaluated benchmarks: on RULER at 128K it scores 86.6% vs. YaRN's 79.9% (LLaMA3-8B) and 53.2% vs. 50.1% (Qwen2.5-3B); on Infinite-Bench it achieves 49.8% average vs. YaRN's 44%, with the largest gaps on KV Retrieval (27% vs. 9%) and QA Dialogue (22% vs. 10%). Against NTK, MrRoPE-Pro achieves lower perplexity at all extended lengths (e.g., 2.34 vs. >10 at 128K on LLaMA3-8B), while NTK fails to maintain coherence beyond 32K. On Infinite-Bench, MrRoPE-Pro surpasses the specialized fine-tuned long-context model Yi-34B-200K on KV Retrieval (27% vs. 2%) and QA Dialogue (22% vs. 2%), and approaches or exceeds Kimi-Chat on MathFind (58% vs. 13%) and QADialogue (22% vs. 12%), all without any additional training. On LongBench-V2, MrRoPE-Pro outperforms YaRN on most subtasks (e.g., LD: 15.4 vs. 12.8 for LLaMA2-7B; LICL: 27.5 vs. 25 for Qwen2.5-3B), though the margins are smaller than on retrieval-focused benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RoPE positional encoding (Su et al., 2024) and the line of training-free context extension methods including Position Interpolation, NTK-aware Interpolation (bloc97, 2023), and YaRN (Peng et al., 2023). Its central conceptual contribution is reframing all these methods through a mixed-radix conversion lens, showing that each corresponds to a specific policy for redistributing positional information across frequency dimensions. The practical departure from prior work is the identification of YaRN's middle-dimension strategy as a 'regressive' radix conversion that unnecessarily disrupts high-frequency positional information, motivating the progressive (monotonically increasing) scaling in MrRoPE-Pro.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The radix conversion framework provides a principled design space for future RoPE extensions beyond the ad-hoc strategies currently in use, and the theoretical bound analysis (extending the RoPE Bound from ~1K to ~28K tokens) gives a concrete metric for comparing methods. Practically, MrRoPE-Pro is a drop-in, training-free modification that nearly doubles the effective context window of off-the-shelf models, which is immediately useful for practitioners deploying long-context inference. Open questions include whether the progressive strategy is truly optimal among all monotonic scalings, whether the framework generalizes to non-RoPE positional encodings (e.g., ALiBi, Fourier features), and whether the theoretical bound improvements translate to gains on tasks beyond retrieval and perplexity.
