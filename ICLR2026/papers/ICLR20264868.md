## Problem

Dynamic data pruning methods suffer from two fundamental consistency violations: score context drift, where importance scores computed at different model states are compared without statistical validity, and temporal gradient bias, where non-uniform sample selection across epochs skews the expected cumulative gradient away from full-dataset training. These issues undermine the reliability of existing dynamic pruning approaches like InfoBatch, which perform global score comparisons and only correct instantaneous (not cumulative) gradient bias.

## Input / Output

RePB takes a standard training dataset (images, text, audio, video, or depth maps) and any differentiable model architecture as input, operating entirely within the existing training loop without modifying the model or loss function. The output is a trained model whose effective training data is dynamically pruned each epoch; the pruning rate (percentage of samples skipped) serves as the efficiency metric, with the method achieving 23–50% data reduction while matching or exceeding full-dataset accuracy. The method is metric-agnostic, supporting both sample loss and gradient norm as importance scores.

## Method

RePB resolves both consistency biases through three synergistic mechanisms. First, Local Window Pruning (LWP) restricts importance score comparisons to short windows of 1–few batches, where parameter drift is bounded by WηG, ensuring scores are computed under a near-constant model state for valid relative ranking. Second, uniform probability resampling reintroduces samples excluded from the current epoch's training set with probability (1−ρ) at each epoch boundary, preventing sample pool collapse. Third, Cumulative Temporal Rescaling (CTR) reweights each sample's gradient during training by w_i = E/N_i(E), the ratio of elapsed epochs to the sample's historical inclusion count, effectively applying an empirical inverse-frequency correction that aligns the long-term expected gradient direction with full-dataset training without requiring explicit knowledge of selection probability distributions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet18), RePB achieves 95.6% / 95.4% / 94.9% top-1 accuracy at 30% / 50% / 70% pruning, matching or exceeding full-dataset training (95.6%). On CIFAR-100, RePB reaches 78.4% / 78.1% / 77.2% at the same pruning rates versus 78.2% full-dataset. Across 16 datasets, 17 model architectures, and 13 tasks, RePB consistently prunes 23–50% of data while maintaining near-full-dataset accuracy, with the highest pruning rate reaching 49.5% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet50) at 80.8% accuracy (vs. 80.6% full).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RePB outperforms the closest dynamic competitor, InfoBatch, at most pruning rates: on CIFAR-100 at 50% pruning, RePB achieves 78.1% vs. InfoBatch‡ (same epochs) at 77.7%, and at 70% pruning RePB reaches 77.2% vs. InfoBatch† at 76.5%. Against static methods, the gap widens substantially at higher pruning rates—on CIFAR-100 at 70%, RePB (77.2%) exceeds GraNd-4 (68.8%) by 8.4 points and EL2N-20 (not reported) by a similar margin. Among dynamic baselines, RePB surpasses UCB (75.3%) and Random* (75.3%) by 2.8 points on CIFAR-100 at 50% pruning. On large-scale vision-language tasks, RePB prunes more data than InfoBatch while matching or exceeding full-dataset performance (e.g., 44.4% pruned on MJ+ST with 96.1% IIIT5k accuracy vs. InfoBatch's 38.1% pruned at 95.9%). The ablation confirms both LWP and CTR are necessary: removing resampling causes collapse to 19.0%, and the full framework (78.4%) exceeds either component alone (78.1–78.2%). RePB also integrates with external importance sampling (Salaun et al.) to yield further gains (+0.5% at 30% pruning on CIFAR-100), demonstrating metric-agnostic compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends dynamic data pruning (InfoBatch, Raju et al., Zhou et al.) by identifying and formally addressing two consistency violations that prior methods overlook: score context drift (importance scores computed at different model states are not comparable) and temporal gradient bias (non-uniform sample inclusion across epochs skews the cumulative gradient). Its Local Window Pruning departs from InfoBatch's epoch-wide global score comparison by restricting comparisons to short windows where parameter drift is bounded, while its Cumulative Temporal Rescaling replaces InfoBatch's instantaneous inverse-probability rescaling with a long-term empirical frequency correction. The approach also draws on classical importance sampling (Katharopoulos & Fleuret, Needell et al.) and score-staleness mitigation (Jiang et al., Borsos et al.), but reframes these ideas around the specific structural guarantees needed for dynamic pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RePB offers a practically useful recipe—30–50% data reduction with near-lossless accuracy—validated across an unusually broad sweep (16 datasets, 17 architectures including CNNs, Transformers, Mamba, and generative models, 13 task types), making it a strong default for efficient training pipelines. The theoretical grounding (Lipschitz-bounded intra-window drift, LLN-based CTR convergence) gives practitioners confidence that the method's guarantees are not purely empirical. Open questions include adaptive window sizing, CTR behavior under highly non-stationary training (e.g., curriculum or multi-stage schedules), and whether the framework extends to multi-metric or influence-based importance scores beyond sample loss.
