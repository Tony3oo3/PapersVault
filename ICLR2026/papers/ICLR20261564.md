## Problem

Causal attention masks in vision-language models are inherited from text-only LLMs, where they enforce strict left-to-right decoding. However, visual information is inherently non-sequential and holistic, so rigidly masking future positions for visual queries unnecessarily restricts access to semantic cues that are critical for accurate inference. The paper asks whether causal attention truly fits visual tokens in VLMs and how it should be revised for the multi-modal setting.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard LLaVA-style VLMs where images are encoded into m visual tokens by a pretrained vision encoder and concatenated with n text tokens into a unified sequence of length L=m+n. The output is a generated text response produced via autoregressive decoding. Crucially, the method is entirely inference-time: it modifies only the attention mask during the prefill stage without any retraining, and the final decoding phase uses the standard causal mask, so no architectural changes or additional parameters are introduced.

## Method

The paper proposes three future-aware causal masking strategies that selectively relax the upper-triangular mask for visual queries only: M^f (full future access), M^{v2v} (future visual tokens only), and M^{v2t} (future text tokens only), while text queries retain strict causality. To avoid the computational overhead of full future visibility during decoding, a lightweight 1D kernel pooling mechanism compresses the future attention scores into a small prefix of past positions (as few as one token, exploiting the attention sink phenomenon). The pooled summary is added back into the past region of the attention matrix, so the final attention pattern remains strictly lower-triangular and standard KV-cache-based decoding applies unchanged. This yields a 2–3× decoding speedup over unmerged future-aware masks while retaining most of the performance gains, and the approach is training-free, requiring only a mask modification at inference time.

## Results

Across 13 vision-language tasks on LLaVA-7b, the full future-aware mask M^f improves the baseline causal mask M^c on most temporal and relational tasks (e.g., TQA: 0.320→0.400, SpotDiff: 0.162→0.171, Nav: 0.310→0.320, Moving: 0.490→0.505), while the visual-to-visual mask M^v2v excels on visual-relation tasks (Object: 0.485→0.500, Moving: 0.490→0.515) and M^v2t on text-rich tasks (OCRVQA: 0.225→0.230, TQA: 0.320→0.385). The lightweight merge variants (e.g., M^f+merge) retain most of these gains (TQA: 0.375, SpotDiff: 0.173) while reducing decoding latency from 83.18 ms/token to 26.54 ms/token (≈3.1× speedup). On LLaVA-13b, M^f+merge achieves the best overall profile (TQA: 0.525, ActionL: 0.255, CLEVR: 0.158) with comparable latency savings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard causal mask M^c (LLaVA's default autoregressive attention). All three future-aware variants (M^f, M^v2v, M^v2t) outperform M^c on at least 8 of 13 tasks for LLaVA-7b, with the most consistent gains on temporal multi-image tasks (Nav, Moving, State, TQA) and visual-relation tasks (SpotDiff, Object). Specifically, M^f achieves the largest single-task improvement on TQA (0.320→0.400, +25% relative) and CLEVR (0.166→0.187, +13%), while M^v2v leads on Moving (0.490→0.515) and Object (0.485→0.500). The merge variants (M^f+merge, M^v2v+merge) match or nearly match their unmerged counterparts on most tasks (e.g., M^f+merge: TQA 0.375 vs. M^f 0.400; M^v2v+merge: Object 0.510 vs. M^v2v 0.500) while cutting decoding latency by 2–3× (from 83.18 to 26.54 ms/token for M^f). On LLaVA-13b, M^f+merge achieves the best TQA score (0.525 vs. 0.495 baseline) and ActionL (0.255 vs. 0.230), confirming that the gains transfer to larger models. The paper notes that no single mask dominates all tasks: M^v2t is best for text-rich QA, M^v2v for visual relations, and M^f for temporal reasoning, suggesting task-adaptive mask selection is the natural next step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LLaVA's decoder-only VLM architecture and the standard causal attention mechanism inherited from LLMs, but departs from the assumption that uniform left-to-right masking is optimal for all token modalities. It connects to the attention sink phenomenon (Gu et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2024) by leveraging early prefix positions as semantic reservoirs, and to StableMask (Yin et al., 2024) which first showed that future semantic attention scores in LLMs can be exploited without violating causal logic. Unlike prior architectural modifications (e.g., D-Attn, BLIP-2) that alter model structure, this work keeps the backbone unchanged and introduces training-free, modality-aware mask variants, positioning itself as an empirical and theoretical study of attention design rather than a new model proposal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational design choice in VLMs—uniform causal masking—and provides a practical, training-free recipe (kernel pooling into attention sinks) that yields modest but consistent gains across 13+ multimodal benchmarks with a 2–3× decoding speedup. However, the improvements are often small (1–5 points), inconsistent across task categories, and validated primarily on LLaVA-7B/13B with inference-only modifications, leaving open whether the benefits persist under training, generalize to other VLM families (Qwen-VL, InternVL), or scale to longer contexts. A key open question is whether task-adaptive mask selection (beyond the manual choice of M^f, M^v2v, or M^v2t) can be learned end-to-end without the overhead the authors note for their MLP adapter.
