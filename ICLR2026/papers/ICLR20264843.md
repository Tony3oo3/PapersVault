## Problem

Self-supervised learning on neural recordings is destabilized by intrinsic population heterogeneity: statistically regular neurons (inhibitory interneurons, modulatory cells) are mixed with highly stochastic, stimulus-contingent excitatory neurons, causing the reconstruction loss to be dominated by unpredictable signals and preventing reliable scaling. No prior SSL framework for neuroscience uses the intrinsic statistical properties of neurons to guide data selection, treating all cells uniformly regardless of their dynamical regime.

## Input / Output

The method consumes calcium imaging traces (ΔF/F) from the Allen Brain Observatory across 13 Cre driver lines, tokenized into sequences and compressed via cross-attention into latent tokens. Pretraining uses 134 sessions (80,146 samples) from four 'predictable' Cre lines; fine-tuning uses 299 sessions (1,170,931 samples) from the remaining 'unpredictable' lines. Outputs are high-resolution movie frames (304×608 pixels, 900-frame sequences) generated from a single neural embedding via a Skip-Connection U-Net, and 8-class drifting grating orientation predictions.

## Method

POYO-CAP introduces a cell-pattern-aware curriculum that first pretrains on statistically regular neurons identified a priori via skewness (≤3.51) and kurtosis (≤22.62) thresholds determined by knee-detection across Cre lines, then fine-tunes on more stochastic populations. Pretraining combines 50% temporal masked reconstruction (causal masking of the second half of the context window) with a lightweight auxiliary cross-entropy loss on drifting grating orientations (λ=0.01) to prevent representational collapse, using a POYO+ Perceiver-based encoder. For movie reconstruction, a dedicated Skip-Connection U-Net decoder replaces standard encoder skip connections with direct latent-vector projections at each upsampling stage, injecting the neural embedding repeatedly to preserve semantic information across scales. The key insight is that predictable neurons induce a 138× smoother loss landscape and 1.93× higher Fisher information, making them an information-theoretically superior pretraining substrate that unlocks monotonic scaling with model capacity where mixed-population baselines plateau.

## Results

On the Allen Brain Observatory calcium imaging dataset, POYO-CAP achieves a movie reconstruction SSIM of 0.593±0.013 and drifting-gratings classification accuracy of 55.5%±2.2%, outperforming the from-scratch baseline (SSIM 0.528±0.023; accuracy 49.2%±4.1%) by 12.3% and 12.8% respectively. The method also improves effective data efficiency by 1.98× over unpredictable neurons, as measured by Fisher Information-weighted dataset size. Critically, POYO-CAP enables smooth, monotonic scaling with model capacity (slope=0.018, p<0.01), whereas from-scratch and mixed-pretraining baselines plateau (slopes ≈0.005–0.013).

## Baselines & Comparisons

POYO-CAP outperforms the from-scratch baseline (identical architecture, no pretraining) by 6.5 SSIM points on movie reconstruction (0.593 vs. 0.528) and by 6.3 percentage points on drifting-gratings accuracy (55.5% vs. 49.2%), confirming that the cell-pattern-aware pretraining stage provides a meaningful representational advantage. Against the CEBRA contrastive-learning baseline (adapted to feed its encoder into the same U-Net decoder), POYO-CAP achieves a 11.2 SSIM point margin (0.593 vs. 0.481), demonstrating that contrastive latent spaces optimized for behavioral alignment do not transfer effectively to high-fidelity pixel-level generation. The paper also notes that reversing the curriculum (pretraining on unpredictable neurons first) degrades performance below the from-scratch baseline (SSIM 0.489; DG accuracy 21.3%), underscoring that the predictable-first ordering is essential rather than merely beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the POYO+ architecture (Azabou et al., 2024) for multi-session neural decoding, repurposing its Perceiver-based encoder for a self-supervised pretraining stage. It departs from prior neural SSL methods (CEBRA, Neuro-BERT) that treat all neurons uniformly by introducing a data-selection criterion grounded in higher-order statistics (skewness and kurtosis), drawing an analogy to the 'data diet' literature in ML (Paul et al., 2021) but applied at the neuron level rather than the sample level. The curriculum-learning framing (Bengio et al., 2009) is applied at the population level: statistically regular inhibitory/modulatory neurons serve as 'easy' examples before the model encounters sparse, stimulus-encoding excitatory neurons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that pretraining on a smaller, statistically regular neural subset enables smooth model scaling while mixed-population training plateaus or destabilizes challenges the common assumption that more neural data is always better for SSL. This provides a principled, biologically interpretable recipe for scaling neural decoders beyond the current small-data regime. Key limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (Allen Brain Observatory, mouse V1, controlled stimuli), a coarse Cre-line-level partition (4 vs. 9 lines), and the knee-detection threshold being applied a priori without sensitivity analysis. Open questions include whether the skewness/kurtosis criterion generalizes across brain regions, species, and recording modalities (e.g., electrophysiology), and whether neuron-level (rather than line-level) selection would yield further gains.
