## Problem

Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models generate images via next-scale prediction, but at inference time limited model capacity and accumulated error cause each step to redundantly re-predict lower-frequency information already established in earlier scales, rather than introducing the novel high-frequency details that the coarse-to-fine hierarchy demands. This train-inference discrepancy leads to structural degradation and spatial disorientation, and existing remedies (refinement modules, architectural changes, retraining) are costly and still susceptible to the same error accumulation.

## Input / Output

SSG is applied to pretrained VAR-structured models that accept either an ImageNet class label or a free-form text prompt and produce a synthesized RGB image at 256×256, 512×512, or up to 1024×1024 resolution. The method operates entirely in the pre-quantization logit space at each autoregressive step, modifying residual logits before categorical sampling without altering model weights, tokenization, or conditioning. It is agnostic to the specific token design (standard VQVAE, hybrid continuous-discrete, or bit-wise) and adds negligible wall-clock latency (e.g., 0.273 s → 0.279 s for VAR-d16 at 256×256).

## Method

SSG derives a closed-form logit-space guidance from an Information Bottleneck reinterpretation of VAR sampling: at step k, the ideal residual should maximize mutual information with new high-frequency content while minimizing redundancy with the established coarse state, yielding the update ℓ_k^SSG = ℓ_k + β_k(ℓ_k − ℓ_prior). The prior ℓ_prior is constructed via Discrete Spatial Enhancement (DSE), a frequency-domain procedure that applies DCT to both the previous step's logits and a simple upscaled version, then fuses low-frequency coefficients from the former with high-frequency coefficients from the latter to produce a prior that rigorously preserves coarse structure while providing a plausible high-frequency extrapolation. A linear decay schedule β_k = β(1 − (k−1)/K) prevents over-guidance at later steps. The entire mechanism requires no retraining, no architectural modification, and only a few lines of code operating on cached logits, making it broadly applicable across VAR models regardless of tokenization or conditioning modality.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, VAR-d30+SSG achieves FID 1.68, IS 313.2, Precision 0.81, and Recall 0.62 in just 10 inference steps (2.0B params), surpassing DiffiT (FID 1.73 at 250 steps) and MAR-H (FID 1.78 at 64 steps, 13.4× slower). On ImageNet 512×512, VAR-d36+SSG reaches FID 2.39 and IS 320.6, outperforming DiffiT (FID 2.67) and HMAR-d24 (FID 2.99) at the same 10-step latency. For text-to-image on MJHQ-30K, SSG improves HART-0.7B FID by 13.9% (8.46→7.28) and Infinity-2B FID by 3.3% (10.01→9.68) with negligible latency overhead (~0.01–0.03 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSG-enhanced VAR-d30 (FID 1.68) outperforms the strongest diffusion baseline DiffiT (FID 1.73) by 0.05 FID while using 25× fewer inference steps (10 vs. 250), and beats MAR-H (FID 1.78) by 0.10 FID with 6.4× fewer steps. Among VAR-family methods, SSG surpasses VAR-CoDe (FID 1.94) and HMAR-d30 (FID 1.95) by 0.26–0.27 FID without any retraining or architectural modification, whereas those methods require collaborative refiners or multi-step masked prediction. On 512×512, SSG (FID 2.39) beats DiffiT (2.67) and HMAR-d24 (2.99) at the same 10-step latency, though MAR-L achieves a lower FID (1.73) at an estimated 214× inference cost. For text-to-image, SSG improves HART-0.7B FID from 8.46 to 7.28 (−13.9%) and Infinity-2B from 10.01 to 9.68 (−3.3%) on MJHQ-30K, with CLIPScore remaining stable or slightly improved. SSG does not surpass L-DiT-7B on IS (313.2 vs. 316.2) or D_ISSU SSM-XL-G on Precision (0.81 vs. 0.86), but it matches or exceeds all baselines on FID while maintaining the lowest inference latency among competitive methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VAR next-scale prediction paradigm (Tian et al., 2024) and reinterprets its sequential generation through the Information Bottleneck lens (Tishby et al., 2000), deriving a closed-form logit-space guidance update. It departs from prior VAR improvements (CoDe, HMAR, Infinity) that require architectural modifications and retraining, and from diffusion guidance methods (CFG, SAG, PAG) that are not tailored to the coarse-to-fine hierarchical structure. The key conceptual departure is framing the train-inference discrepancy as an information-redundancy problem solvable by amplifying the per-step semantic residual, rather than adding auxiliary refinement modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSG provides a drop-in, training-free enhancement for any pretrained VAR-structured model with discrete tokens, achieving FID 1.68 on ImageNet 256×256 (surpassing DiffiT and MAR-H) at ~45× lower inference cost, which is practically significant for the VAR community. The information-theoretic derivation gives principled justification for the guidance rather than ad-hoc heuristics, and the method's agnosticism to tokenization design (standard, hybrid, bit-wise) broadens its applicability. Limitations include restriction to models exposing discrete logits (excluding latent diffusion and flow matching), bounded corrective power from poor initial states, and the preliminary (marginal) generalization to VQ-Diffusion. Open questions include whether the IB framing extends to continuous-space hierarchical generators and whether the DCT-based prior construction is optimal versus learned frequency-domain operations.
