## Problem

Existing weak-to-strong (W2S) generalization theory assumes clean, group-balanced downstream data, yet in practice both the weak teacher's labeled set and the unlabeled set for pseudolabeling often carry spurious correlations from group imbalance. This paper asks: does W2S still succeed when the minority fractions in labeled (η_ℓ) and unlabeled (η_u) data differ, and how can W2S be improved when it fails?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261122/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a frozen pre-trained weak teacher backbone and a frozen pre-trained strong student backbone (e.g., ResNet-18, CLIP ViT-B/32, DINOv2 ViT-L/14), a small labeled set with minority fraction η_ℓ, and a large unlabeled set with minority fraction η_u. Only the linear classification head is trained in each stage (kernel regime), so the effective input to the learner is the high-dimensional pre-trained feature vector φ(x) ∈ ℝ^D. The output is a fine-tuned student head producing class predictions, evaluated on average and worst-group accuracy under a test distribution with minority fraction η_t.

## Method

The theoretical contribution models fine-tuning as ridgeless regression over low-intrinsic-dimension kernel representations in the proportional asymptotic limit, showing that W2S gain is governed by the teacher-student similarity matrix Ξ = TᵀS and the gap (η_u − η_ℓ): W2S always succeeds when η_u = η_ℓ but its gain diminishes quadratically as (η_u − η_ℓ)² grows, and can vanish entirely for well-separated groups. The algorithmic contribution (Enhanced-W2S) addresses the failure regime by, after standard W2S fine-tuning, retraining the student on a high-confidence subset (lowest-entropy predictions) of the pseudolabeled data using the generalized cross-entropy loss with parameter q ∈ (0,1]. This two-component remedy—confidence-based selection plus GCE—requires no group annotations, filters out samples where the student over-relies on spurious features, and mitigates the penalty on high-confidence but incorrect pseudolabels, yielding consistent 4–12% average-accuracy gains over vanilla W2S across four spurious-correlation benchmarks and ten teacher-student pairs.

## Results

The Enhanced-W2S method achieves consistent positive gains over vanilla W2S across 67 out of 70 teacher-student pairs (measured by average accuracy) on four spurious correlation benchmarks. Mean relative improvements over vanilla W2S reach 7.02% (Waterbirds), 4.32% (BFFHQ), 7.50% (BG-COCO), and 11.73% (ImageNet-9) in average accuracy. For worst-group accuracy, the gains are even more pronounced: 21.14% on Waterbirds, 3.81% on BFFHQ, and 7.73% on BG-COCO. The method requires no group annotations and uses only confidence-based subset selection combined with the generalized cross-entropy (GCE) loss for retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is vanilla W2S (the standard two-stage pipeline from Burns et al., 2024: SFT the weak teacher on labeled data, then fine-tune the strong student on teacher pseudolabels). Enhanced-W2S outperforms vanilla W2S in 67 out of 70 model-pair evaluations on average accuracy, with only 3 negative cases (e.g., DINOv2→MAE on BG-COCO at −3.52%, ConvNeXt→MAE on BG-COCO at −3.07%, and Clipb32→MAE on BFFHQ at −1.37%). The gains are most substantial when the gap between η_ℓ and η_u is large: for instance, on Waterbirds with η_ℓ=η_o, η_u=0.5, the DINOv2→Clipb32 pair improves by 13.86%, and on ImageNet-9 with η_ℓ=0.5, η_u=η_o, the Clipb32→MAE pair improves by 21.73%. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that both the GCE loss and confidence-based selection contribute positively, with confidence-based selection playing a more critical role when η_ℓ=η_o and η_u=0.5 (where it effectively reduces the minority proportion during retraining).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the W2S generalization framework of Burns et al. (2024) and the theoretical analysis of Dong et al. (2025) (ridgeless regression in the kernel regime at the proportional asymptotic limit) to the setting of spurious correlations induced by group imbalance. While prior W2S theory assumed clean downstream data, this work introduces two sources of bias—imbalanced labeled data for the teacher (η_ℓ) and imbalanced unlabeled data for pseudolabeling (η_u)—and precisely characterizes how their mismatch affects W2S gain. The algorithmic remedy (Enhanced-W2S) repurposes confidence-based selection and generalized cross-entropy loss, techniques previously applied independently to spurious correlation mitigation and self-training, into a principled post-hoc retraining step motivated by the theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

W2S generalization is central to the superalignment question, yet in practice both teacher training data and unlabeled data carry systematic biases (medical imaging, legal NLP, autonomous driving). This paper provides the first systematic theoretical characterization showing W2S can fail even with infinite unlabeled data when η_u ≠ η_ℓ, with gain diminishing quadratically in the gap—a non-obvious failure mode that practitioners would not anticipate. The Enhanced-W2S method is group-label-free and achieves consistent improvements across 4 benchmarks and 10 model pairs, making it immediately deployable. Open questions include extending the analysis beyond the kernel regime to capture training dynamics, and whether the (η_u − η_ℓ)² characterization holds for non-Gaussian or multi-group settings.
