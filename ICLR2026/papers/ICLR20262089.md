## Problem

While MLLM-based embedding models have shown strong results for static images, audio and synchronized audio-visual streams remain largely unexplored in the unified embedding paradigm. No existing model produces a single shared representation space spanning text, audio, silent video, and synchronized audio-visual inputs, and conventional embedding models generate task-agnostic representations that cannot be conditioned on user instructions for downstream tasks like multimodal QA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262089/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

WAVE accepts four input configurations—text-only, visual-only (video frames sampled at 2 fps, max 128 frames, 176400 pixels/frame), audio-only (waveform resampled to 16 kHz), and synchronized audio-visual—each accompanied by a text prompt that instructs the LLM. The output is a single unified embedding vector in a shared semantic space, produced by concatenating last-token hidden states from all 28 LLM layers and compressing them through a two-layer MLP fusion module. For text-only inputs, standard last-token pooling is used instead of the multi-layer fusion.

## Method

WAVE is built on Qwen2.5-Omni 7B with LoRA (rank 128, scale 2.0) and introduces two key architectural components: a dual audio encoder combining a speech encoder (from Qwen2.5-Omni) with a BEATs audio encoder (with a trainable MLP aligner) to capture complementary speech and environmental-sound cues, and a hierarchical all-layer feature fusion that concatenates last-token states from every LLM layer and compresses them via a 2-layer GELU MLP, preserving both low-level perceptual and high-level semantic information. Temporal alignment across modalities is enforced through TMRoPE, which assigns identical rotary positions to tokens from the same frame. Training uses a joint multi-modal, multi-task contrastive objective: a symmetric InfoNCE loss (τ=0.01) over in-batch negatives for any-to-any retrieval, plus a cross-entropy QA loss with n distractor answers to learn prompt-aware embeddings. A task-aware data sampler ensures each mini-batch contains samples from a single task, and a dedicated pre-training phase aligns the BEATs encoder to the LLM before the main 4.9M-sample contrastive training on 192 H20 GPUs.

## Results

On the MMEB-v2 video track, WAVE achieves an overall score of 59.9, surpassing the industrial-grade Seed-1.6-Embedding (55.3) and the best open-source model CAFe 7B (42.4) by 4.6 and 17.5 points respectively. WAVE also leads on LoVR with 62.9 R@1 for text-to-clip and 66.0 R@25 for theme-to-clip retrieval. In the audio domain, WAVE attains 44.2 R@1 on AudioCaps and 25.6 R@1 on Clotho for audio-text retrieval, and 25.0 R@1 on VGGSound and 20.4 R@1 on MusicCaps for video-to-audio retrieval, substantially outperforming the encoder-only baseline (10.3 and 8.6). On audio QA, WAVE reaches 76.6% on MMAU and 68.1% on MMAR, exceeding its Qwen2.5-Omni base model (71.5% and 56.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WAVE outperforms all open-source embedding MLLMs (LamRA 7B, GME 7B, CAFe 7B) across every MMEB-v2-Video sub-task, with the largest margin on QA (72.5 vs. 58.7 for CAFe) and RET (54.7 vs. 34.4 for CAFe). Against the industrial Seed-1.6-Embedding, WAVE leads on Overall (+4.6), CLS (+2.8), QA (+11.6), and RET (+3.4), though it trails slightly on MRET (50.8 vs. 53.5). For audio-text retrieval, WAVE surpasses the WavCaps-based reference model (Mei et al., 2024) by 2.0 R@1 on AudioCaps (44.2 vs. 42.2) and 4.1 R@1 on Clotho (25.6 vs. 21.5). On video-to-audio retrieval, WAVE dramatically outperforms the encoder-only retrieval model (25.0 vs. 10.3 on VGGSound; 20.4 vs. 8.6 on MusicCaps), demonstrating the advantage of LLM-based unified embeddings over separate-encoder pipelines. On audio QA, WAVE exceeds its Qwen2.5-Omni base by 5.1 points on MMAU (76.6 vs. 71.5) and 11.4 points on MMAR (68.1 vs. 56.7), confirming that the embedding training enhances rather than degrades the base model's reasoning capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

WAVE extends the MLLM-based embedding line (VLM2Vec, GME, E5-V, LamRA, CAFe) by being the first to unify text, audio, silent video, and synchronised audio-visual inputs in a single LLM-derived semantic space, whereas prior efforts concentrated on vision-centric modalities. Architecturally, it builds on Qwen2.5-Omni's TMRoPE for temporal alignment and augments the Whisper-derived speech encoder with a BEATs audio encoder to capture non-speech events, departing from single-encoder audio pipelines like CLAP or AudioCLIP. The hierarchical all-layer last-token MLP fusion departs from the standard last-token pooling used in most LLM embedding models, motivated by the observation that different LLM layers encode complementary levels of abstraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WAVE provides a practical, open-source baseline for any-to-any audio-visual-text retrieval and demonstrates that prompt-aware embeddings from an MLLM can substantially outperform task-agnostic representations on QA, opening a path toward instruction-conditioned retrieval systems. The positive cross-modal transfer observed in the joint-training ablation (video QA training improving audio QA) suggests that unified spaces may reduce the need for modality-specific fine-tuning. Limitations include reliance on a 7B backbone, 2 fps video sampling, a single training epoch on ~4.9M samples, and evaluation limited to relatively short clips; scaling to longer videos, larger models, and real-time streaming remains open.
