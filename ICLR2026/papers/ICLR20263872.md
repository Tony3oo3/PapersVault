## Problem

Existing in-generation watermarking for diffusion models either crafts the latent seed (Tree-Rings, Gaussian Shading) with uncontrolled false alarm rates and limited geometric robustness, or requires fine-tuning the VAE (Stable Signature) which concentrates watermark energy in high frequencies. Post-hoc watermarking, while robust, is not specific to generative AI and operates as an external add-on. The paper asks whether any off-the-shelf differentiable watermark decoder can be used to guide the diffusion process itself, converting post-hoc schemes into in-generation ones without retraining either the generator or the detector.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a random latent seed z_T, a pre-trained differentiable watermark decoder (Stable Signature with 48-bit or VideoSeal with 256-bit watermarks), and a secret reference vector u_m, and produces a watermarked RGB image (512×512 for SD2/Sana, 256×256 for Flux). Unlike seed-based in-generation methods that require a reverse diffusion process at detection time, the output is detected by a single forward pass through the same pre-trained decoder, making detection up to 50× faster. The method supports both multi-bit message decoding (via sign of logits) and zero-bit detection (via cosine similarity with certified p-values).

## Method

At each diffusion denoising step, the estimated noise ε_θ(z_t, t) is modified by subtracting a scaled gradient of a loss measuring the cosine similarity between the watermark decoder's output φ(x_0(z_t)) and the target secret vector u_m. To compute this gradient, the diffusion is completed from step t to 0, the VAE decodes the latent to an image, and the watermark decoder extracts features — all differentiable end-to-end. For robustness, the loss is evaluated under a set of image augmentations (JPEG compression, brightness, contrast, central crop) and the resulting gradients are aggregated using PCGrad to resolve conflicting directions. Two simplifications reduce cost: guidance is activated only in the last T_w steps (e.g., 15 of 50), and gradient propagation uses an identity transform (∇_{z_0} replaces ∇_{z_t}) rather than backpropagating through the full backward diffusion. The method requires no retraining of either the diffusion model or the watermark decoder, and can improve robustness against previously unseen attacks simply by adding them to the augmentation set during gradient computation.

## Results

On Sana with G-VS, the method achieves a multi-bit capacity of 207.5 bits (vs. 178.7 for post-hoc VideoSeal) and a zero-bit detectability of -log10(PFA)@PD=0.9 of 96.4 (vs. 47.2 for post-hoc VS), while preserving image quality (FID 4.1 vs. 4.3 baseline, CLIP 0.346 vs. 0.346). On SD2, G-VS reaches 212.2 bits capacity and -log10(PFA) of 105.6, and G-SSig doubles the zero-bit detection performance of the original Stable Signature (PD@10⁻¹⁰ PFA: 0.99 vs. ~0.49). The guidance introduces negligible quality degradation: PSNR between watermarked and non-watermarked images ranges from 18.5–28.6 dB, while FID and CLIP scores remain essentially unchanged across all three diffusion models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Stable Signature (SSig), G-SSig doubles the zero-bit detection performance at PFA=10⁻¹⁰ (PD 0.99 vs. ~0.49 on SD2) and improves -log10(PFA)@PD=0.9 by +12.2 to +12.8 across models, all without fine-tuning the VAE. Against post-hoc VideoSeal (VS), G-VS achieves substantially higher capacity (212.2 vs. 174.5 on SD2; 207.5 vs. 178.7 on Sana) and dramatically higher zero-bit detectability (-log10(PFA) 105.6 vs. 43.8 on SD2; 96.4 vs. 47.2 on Sana). Against Tree-Rings (in-generation, zero-bit), G-VS is overwhelmingly superior: 154.6 vs. 11.7 on -log10(PFA)@PD=0.9 for Identity, and critically, G-VS remains robust to 50% crop (101.9) while Tree-Rings collapses to 0.4. Against Gaussian Shading (in-generation, multi-bit), G-VS matches capacity on Identity (222 vs. 221 bits) but is robust to rotation (194 vs. 0) and crop (206 vs. 0), where Gaussian Shading completely fails. Under adversarial attacks (VAE purification, regeneration, average attack), G-VS is robust to all three, while Gaussian Shading is vulnerable to the average attack and Tree-Rings to all three.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the gradient-based conditional sampling mechanism introduced by Dhariwal & Nichol (2021) and extended to arbitrary solvers by Lu et al. (2022), originally designed for classifier guidance and counterfactual explanations, to embed watermarks during the diffusion denoising process. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: post-hoc watermarking (Stable Signature, VideoSeal, TrustMark) which provides robust, provably low false-alarm decoders but operates as an external add-on, and in-generation seed-crafting schemes (Tree-Rings, Gaussian Shading, RoBIN) which embed watermarks intrinsically but suffer from uncontrolled false alarm rates and content distortion. The key departure is that no retraining of either the diffusion model or the watermark decoder is required; the pre-trained decoder's gradient simply steers the sampling trajectory, with PCGrad (Yu et al., 2020) aggregating gradients across multiple augmentations to enhance robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, drop-in upgrade path for any deployment already using a post-hoc watermark decoder: simply add guidance steps during generation to obtain an in-generation watermark with substantially improved robustness (e.g., -log10(PFA) of 96–154 vs. 0.4–11.7 for Tree-Rings under geometric attacks). A notable open question is the content-dependency of the embedded signal, which makes averaging attacks less effective but also means robustness varies with image content (flat, textureless images are harder to watermark). The ability to 'patch' a decoder's weaknesses against previously unseen attacks (90° rotation, median filtering, VAE purification) purely by adding those transforms to the gradient computation—without any retraining—is a clean property that could accelerate the watermarking arms race. Limitations include the additional 2–13 diffusion steps at generation time and the fact that the approach inherits the decoder's fundamental capacity constraints.
