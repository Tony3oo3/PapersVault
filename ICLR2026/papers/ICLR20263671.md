## Problem

State-of-the-art reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1 and o3 rely on proprietary training data with minimal public documentation, leaving the community without a clear understanding of which data curation strategies actually drive reasoning performance. This paper addresses that gap by conducting over 1,000 controlled ablation experiments across math, code, and science domains to systematically identify which pipeline design choices—question sourcing, filtering, deduplication, sampling, and teacher selection—matter most for building effective SFT reasoning datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263671/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes text questions from curated sources (e.g., OpenMath-2-Math for math, CodeGolf and OpenCodeReasoning for code, StackExchange-Physics and OrganicChemistry-PDFs for science) and produces question–reasoning-trace–answer triplets where the reasoning trace is enclosed in <think>/ tokens. The final OpenThoughts3-1.2M dataset contains 1.2M examples (850K math, 250K code, 100K science), with each question annotated 16 times by the teacher model to generate diverse reasoning traces. The trained model (OpenThinker3-7B) outputs a long chain-of-thought followed by a final answer, with code answers in fenced code blocks.

## Method

The core contribution is a six-stage data curation pipeline validated through 1,000+ ablation experiments at a fixed 31.6K-example scale, where each stage's winning strategy is selected by average benchmark performance before proceeding to the next. Key findings include: (1) selecting only the top 1–2 question sources outperforms mixing 8–16 sources, prioritizing quality over diversity; (2) LLM-based filtering (difficulty scoring or response-length selection) substantially outperforms classical fastText or embedding-based filters; (3) sampling 16 answers per question with exact deduplication provides a 16× data-scale axis that drives significant gains; (4) no answer-filtering strategy (verification, majority consensus, length-based) improved over using all samples; and (5) QwQ-32B is a stronger teacher than DeepSeek-R1 despite scoring lower on target benchmarks. The winning pipeline is scaled to 1.2M examples and used to fine-tune Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct via SFT (LlamaFactory, DeepSpeed v3, cosine LR schedule), yielding OpenThinker3-7B, which achieves 53% on AIME 2025, 51% on LiveCodeBench 06/24–01/25, and 54% on GPQA Diamond—surpassing DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B by 12.4 points on average across 12 tasks.

## Results

OpenThinker3-7B, fine-tuned from Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct on the 1.2M OpenThoughts3 dataset, achieves an average score of 55.3 across 12 reasoning benchmarks, outperforming DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (42.9) by 12.4 points and the next-best open-data model Nemotron-Nano-8B (53.2) by 2.1 points. On held-out benchmarks not used during pipeline design, it scores 53.3% on AIME 2025, 51.7% on LiveCodeBench 06/24–01/25, and 42.7% on HMMT 02/25. The model also leads on GPQA Diamond (53.7%) and JEEBench (72.4%) among open-data 7B/8B models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenThinker3-7B (55.3 avg) decisively outperforms DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (42.9 avg) by 12.4 points across all 12 benchmarks, with the largest gaps on GPQA Diamond (+20.5), AIME25 (+15.3), and LCB 06/24–01/25 (+17.2). Against the strongest open-data competitor Nemotron-Nano-8B (53.2 avg, trained with 3.9M SFT/RL examples), OpenThinker3-7B wins by 2.1 points on average, leading on AIME24 (+7.0), AIME25 (+5.3), HMMT (+16.0), and GPQA Diamond (+0.8), while losing on CodeElo (−1.9) and LCB 05/23–05/24 (−3.5). AceReason-7B (52.9 avg, RL-only) is beaten on average but edges out OpenThinker3-7B on AIME24 (71.0 vs 69.0) and CodeElo (32.9 vs 31.0). Skywork-7B (51.6 avg, RL-only) is outperformed on every benchmark except CodeElo (37.0 vs 31.0). The base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model (24.0 avg) shows a 31.3-point improvement after SFT on OpenThoughts3, confirming the data recipe's contribution rather than base-model strength.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepSeek-R1 distillation paradigm—SFT on teacher-generated question-thinking-answer triplets—by systematically ablating every stage of the data curation pipeline (sourcing, mixing, filtering, deduplication, sampling, answer filtering, teacher selection) through 1,000+ controlled experiments. Prior open-data efforts (S1, LIMO, OpenR1, OpenMathReasoning, SkyT1) each explored a limited subset of design choices, often relying on heuristics or a single teacher model. The key departure is the empirical, step-by-step isolation of each pipeline decision rather than adopting a monolithic recipe, and the finding that the optimal choices differ from common intuitions (e.g., fewer sources beat more, weaker teachers beat stronger ones).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive, fully open recipe for building a competitive reasoning SFT dataset, yielding OpenThoughts3-1.2M and OpenThinker3-7B as the SOTA open-data 7B reasoning model. The counterintuitive findings—particularly that QwQ-32B outperforms DeepSeek-R1 as a teacher despite lower benchmark scores, and that answer verification/filtering provides no benefit—should reshape how practitioners allocate compute in distillation pipelines. Limitations include no exploration of RL training, staged SFT, or curriculum learning, and the recipe is validated primarily at 7B scale on Qwen and Llama base models. Open questions include whether the recipe scales beyond 1.2M examples, whether it transfers to stronger base models (e.g., Qwen-3), and how it interacts with RL fine-tuning.
