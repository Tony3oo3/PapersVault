## Problem

Existing dynamic Gaussian splatting methods each rely on a single deformation prior (HexPlane, per-Gaussian volumetric, polynomial, or interpolation-based), and empirical analysis shows no single method consistently dominates across scenes, spatial regions, or temporal frames. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether a unified framework can adaptively combine heterogeneous deformation experts to handle the full spectrum of real-world motion patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262110/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences with known camera poses, initializing each expert's Gaussians from COLMAP SfM point clouds (dense or downsampled depending on the expert). It outputs per-pixel novel view renderings of the dynamic scene at arbitrary timestamps and viewpoints, and additionally produces a post-hoc fused 3D Gaussian model by lifting pixel-level routing responsibilities back to per-Gaussian opacities, enabling standard volumetric rendering without 2D compositing.

## Method

MoE-GS integrates N independently trained dynamic Gaussian experts (e.g., 4DGaussians, E-D3DGS, STG, Ex4DGS) through a novel Volume-aware Pixel Router that projects learnable per-Gaussian weights—encoding temporal and view-dependent variation—into pixel space via differentiable weight splatting, then refines them with a lightweight convolutional MLP to produce spatially and temporally coherent gating weights. Training follows a two-stage strategy: experts are first optimized independently to full convergence, then the router (per-Gaussian weights and MLP) is trained with all expert parameters frozen to avoid dominance by faster-converging experts. For efficiency, the paper introduces single-pass multi-expert rendering (merging all Gaussians into one batched rasterization pass with one-hot expert identity) and gate-aware Gaussian pruning (removing Gaussians whose gradient contribution to routing weights falls below a threshold). A complementary knowledge distillation strategy re-trains individual experts using routing-weighted pseudo-labels from the MoE model, enabling lightweight single-expert deployment that approximates full MoE performance without architectural changes.

## Results

On the N3V dataset, MoE-GS (N=4) achieves a mean PSNR of 33.27 dB, outperforming the best single-expert baseline E-D3DGS (32.33 dB) by 0.94 dB and the next-best GS method SaRO-GS (32.15 dB) by 1.12 dB. On Technicolor, MoE-GS (N=3) reaches 34.55 dB mean PSNR, surpassing STG (33.69 dB) and Ex4DGS (33.45 dB) by 0.86 and 1.10 dB respectively. On the monocular HyperNeRF dataset, MoE-GS (N=2) attains 24.81 dB, exceeding 4DGaussians (24.02 dB) and E-D3DGS (23.74 dB). MoE-GS also improves efficiency: with 55% gate-aware pruning, N3V FPS rises from 44 to 83 while retaining 32.80 dB PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoE-GS consistently outperforms all 12 baselines on both N3V and Technicolor. Among the four Gaussian-based experts used internally (4DGaussians, E-D3DGS, STG, Ex4DGS), MoE-GS (N=4) exceeds the strongest individual expert E-D3DGS by 0.94 dB on N3V and by 1.49 dB on Technicolor (34.55 vs. 33.06), confirming that the mixture provides genuine complementary gains rather than merely selecting the best expert. Against NeRF-based baselines (HyperReel, K-Planes, MixVoxels-L, DyNeRF), MoE-GS leads by 1.2–2.5 dB on N3V and by 1.8–2.8 dB on Technicolor. Among other GS-based streaming methods (3DGStream, DASS, SaRO-GS, SwinGS), MoE-GS achieves the highest average PSNR on N3V (33.27 vs. 32.15 for SaRO-GS, the closest competitor). On the monocular HyperNeRF dataset, MoE-GS (N=2) beats both 4DGaussians and E-D3DGS by 0.79 and 1.07 dB respectively, demonstrating that the framework generalizes beyond multi-view settings. Notably, even with only two experts (STG + Ex4DGS), MoE-GS already surpasses all single-expert baselines, and the marginal gain from adding a third or fourth expert is modest (32.54 → 33.23 → 33.27 dB), suggesting diminishing returns beyond N=3.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoE-GS builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (Kerbl et al., 2023) and extends the feature-splatting idea from STG (Li et al., 2024) by replacing color refinement with differentiable weight splatting for adaptive expert gating. Conceptually, it transplants the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm from large language models (Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022) into the dynamic 4D reconstruction setting, but with a key departure: rather than using sparsity to reduce FLOPs, the MoE here combines heterogeneous deformation priors (HexPlane, per-Gaussian embedding, polynomial, interpolation) to increase representational capacity. The four expert models (4DGaussians, E-D3DGS, STG, Ex4DGS) are used as-is, making MoE-GS a meta-framework that orchestrates existing methods rather than proposing a new deformation model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that no single deformation prior dominates across scenes, spatial regions, or timesteps, and then showing that adaptive blending recovers 1–1.5 dB over the best individual expert, MoE-GS provides a practical recipe for combining complementary 4D reconstruction methods without retraining them jointly. The distillation pipeline is particularly useful for practitioners who need single-model deployment: it transfers MoE-level quality into any individual expert with no architectural change. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond four experts without prohibitive cost, whether the router generalizes to unseen motion types, and whether the post-hoc Gaussian fusion (Appendix D) can be made differentiable for end-to-end geometric refinement. The inherent FPS reduction from multi-expert inference remains a practical barrier for real-time applications despite the single-pass rendering and pruning mitigations.
