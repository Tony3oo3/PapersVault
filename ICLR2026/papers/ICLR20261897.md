## Problem

Ternary quantization of LLMs constrains weights to {-1, 0, +1}, replacing multiplications with hardware-efficient additions, but a large fraction of weights fall into the deadzone (|w| < Δ) and are quantized to zero. These dead weights receive only noisy, uninformative gradients via the Straight-Through Estimator, trapping them in ineffective oscillation at the deadzone boundary and severely degrading model accuracy. Existing methods require 100B+ tokens of QAT to partially recover performance, and no prior work has identified or resolved this deadzone trapping mechanism.

## Input / Output

Tequila takes a pre-trained LLM (LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B, Qwen3-4B) and standard text prompts as input, producing generated text as output. During inference, the model operates with ternary weights {-1, 0, +1} packed into 4-bit indices and 1-bit signs, plus a precomputed per-channel bias term that is fused into the lookup-table computation kernel. The bias is input-agnostic and computed offline, so the forward pass remains multiplication-free with less than 0.1% additional computational cost compared to pure ternary inference.

## Method

Tequila identifies deadzone trapping as the root cause of ternary quantization failure and resolves it by repurposing dead weights as dynamic biases through three key designs. First, it replaces the non-differentiable sign-based reactivation with a smooth scaling λw_i, bypassing the STE and providing direct, informative gradients to previously trapped weights. Second, it converts the input-dependent bias-like term into an input-agnostic bias C(W) = Σ_{i∈D} λw_i that can be precomputed offline, eliminating inference overhead. Third, it assigns dead weights a hybrid role: they simultaneously contribute to the ternary matrix multiplication and the bias term, yielding mixed gradients ∂L/∂w_i = x_i·∂L/∂Y + λ·∂L/∂Y that preserve input information while delivering a stable optimization signal. The resulting forward pass Y = X·Ŵ·α + C(W) is plugged into standard AbsMean ternary quantization with QAT, requiring only 10B tokens to achieve near-full-precision accuracy with a 3× inference speedup on CPU.

## Results

On the 1B model (LLaMA-3.2-1B), TequilaLLM trained on only 10B tokens achieves 0.519 average accuracy across five benchmarks, outperforming BitNet-1.3B (0.483, 100B tokens) and Spectra-1.1B (0.489, 100B tokens) while using 10× fewer training tokens. On the 3B model, TequilaLLM reaches 0.576 average accuracy, surpassing Spectra-3.9B (0.567) and BitNet-3B (0.527). On ARC-Easy specifically, Tequila achieves >4% accuracy gain over the SOTA ternary baseline and nearly matches full-precision BF16 performance (0.645 vs. 0.654 for 1B; 0.702 vs. 0.745 for 3B). TequilaLLM also delivers a 3.0× inference speedup over BF16 LLaMA on an Intel 8263C CPU, matching the practical speed of BitNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TequilaLLM decisively outperforms all ternary LLM baselines. Against BitNet (Ma et al., 2025), TequilaLLM-1B achieves 0.519 average accuracy versus BitNet-1.3B's 0.483, a 3.6% gain while using 10× fewer training tokens (10B vs. 100B); at 3B scale the gap widens to 4.9% (0.576 vs. 0.527). Against Spectra (Kaushal et al., 2025), TequilaLLM-3B (0.576) edges out Spectra-3.9B (0.567) by 0.9% despite being smaller and trained on 10% of the tokens. Against the learnable-quantization baselines TernaryLLM/DLT (Chen et al., 2024) and ParetoQ/SEQ (Liu et al., 2025), TequilaLLM leads by 13–21% in average accuracy at both 1B and 3B scales, confirming that static AbsMean quantization combined with Tequila's reactivation is far more effective than learned step-size approaches. On Qwen3-4B, Tequila (0.5985 average) substantially outperforms the PTQ baseline AWQ (Lin et al., 2023) at 0.3592, highlighting the necessity of QAT for ternary quantization. In the quantization-method ablation (Table 1), Tequila+AbsMean achieves 0.471 average on 1B versus 0.354 for LSQ (Esser et al., 2019) and 0.370 for SEQ, a >10% improvement over learnable methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tequila builds on the foundational ternary quantization framework (TWN, TTQ) and the AbsMean quantization scheme popularized by BitNet, but departs from prior work that focused on optimizing threshold and scaling parameters to reduce quantization error. Instead, it reframes the core problem as an optimization pathology—deadzone trapping—where weights receive only noisy STE gradients and cannot stably escape the zero region. The proposed solution of repurposing dead weights as input-agnostic dynamic biases is conceptually distinct from prior capacity-enhancing approaches (DLT's learnable bias, SEQ's non-zero zero-point) that broke the multiplication-free hardware advantage, since Tequila's bias is precomputed offline with nearly zero inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of deadzone trapping as the fundamental bottleneck in ternary QAT provides a clear diagnostic that explains why prior methods required 100B+ tokens to approach full-precision performance, and Tequila's fix (a simple differentiable bias repurposing) reduces this to 10B tokens while preserving the 3× CPU speedup that makes ternary quantization attractive for edge deployment. The plug-and-play design means it can be integrated into existing ternary LLM pipelines without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the mechanism generalizes to even more extreme regimes (binary quantization), whether the bias term can be further compressed or learned more efficiently, and whether the approach transfers to non-transformer architectures or multimodal models.
