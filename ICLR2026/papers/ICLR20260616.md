## Problem

Despite the widespread adoption of diffusion transformers for class-conditional and multimodal generation, the internal structure of their learned conditional embeddings—compact vectors injected globally via AdaLN—remains poorly understood. This paper asks whether the full embedding dimensionality is actually necessary for encoding semantic conditioning, and what the geometric and statistical properties of these vectors reveal about how diffusion transformers represent class or condition information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260616/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis targets the conditional vector c ∈ R^d (d = 1152 for ImageNet XL models, 1024 for X-MDPT, 768 for MDSGen), formed by summing a learned class or condition embedding with a timestep embedding. This single vector modulates every Transformer layer through AdaLN's learned scale γ(c) and shift β(c) parameters, unlike U-Net architectures that distribute conditioning across spatial scales. During pruning experiments, only this conditional vector is modified (zeroing low-magnitude dimensions) at selected inference steps while all other model weights remain frozen, and the output is the standard generated image, person image, or audio mel-spectrogram.

## Method

The paper conducts a systematic empirical analysis across eight pretrained diffusion transformers, computing full 1000×1000 pairwise cosine similarity matrices (revealing >99% alignment for discrete tasks and >99.9% for continuous tasks), magnitude histograms, and participation ratios (nPR ≈ 1–2% for ImageNet models) to quantify effective dimensionality. Variance decomposition and t-SNE projections confirm that semantic class structure is concentrated in roughly 15–20 high-magnitude 'head' dimensions while the remaining ~98% 'tail' dimensions carry negligible signal. The key experimental intervention is progressive pruning: zeroing tail dimensions at thresholds τ ∈ {0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 5.0} applied at the initial step, every step, or only the final denoising steps, showing that removing up to 66% of dimensions preserves or slightly improves FID and CLIP scores. The paper further provides mechanistic hypotheses attributing the sparsity to AdaLN's linear amplification of dominant coordinates, the need for a stable conditioning signal across all diffusion timesteps, and an implicit information-bottleneck effect during training.

## Results

Across six ImageNet-1K diffusion transformers (DiT, SiT, MDT, LightningDiT, MG, REPA), class-conditioned embeddings exhibit pairwise cosine similarity ranging from 90.0% (DiT) to 99.5% (REPA), with normalized participation ratios (nPR) as low as 1.43–2.28%, meaning only ~10–20 of 1,152 dimensions carry meaningful signal. Continuous-condition tasks (X-MDPT on DeepFashion, MDSGen on VGGSound) show even higher similarity (99.98–99.99%) but higher nPR (13.6–48.4%). Pruning up to 66% of low-magnitude tail dimensions (τ=0.02) on REPA increases FID from 7.1694 to 9.2202, while pruning 39% (τ=0.01) with late-step application actually improves FID to 7.1598 and CLIP to 29.805, confirming substantial redundancy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an analysis paper rather than a method paper, so the 'comparison' is across eight existing diffusion transformer models to demonstrate that the semantic bottleneck is a universal property. Among the six ImageNet-1K models, REPA and MG show the highest cosine similarity (0.9946 and 0.9934) and lowest nPR (1.53% and 1.73%), while DiT is the outlier with the lowest similarity (0.9001) and highest nPR (10.47%), consistent with its weaker FID. The two continuous-condition models (X-MDPT, MDSGen) exhibit near-perfect alignment (≥99.98%) but engage more dimensions (nPR 13.6–48.4%), suggesting continuous conditioning distributes signal more broadly. In pruning experiments (Table 2), REPA's baseline FID of 7.1694 is matched or improved by tail pruning at τ=0.01 (FID 7.1598 with late-step pruning), while head pruning of just 2 dimensions (τ=5.0) degrades FID to 7.8478, confirming that the few high-magnitude dimensions are irreplaceable. Additional baselines (LightningDiT, MG) in Table 3 show the same pattern: tail pruning at τ=0.01 preserves or slightly improves FID and CLIP across all three pruning schedules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by presenting the first systematic analysis of learned conditional embeddings in diffusion transformers, a gap the authors explicitly identify relative to prior work on U-Net conditioning (Rombach et al., 2022; Saharia et al., 2022) and activation sparsity in classification Transformers (Li et al., 2023). Conceptually, it draws on representation collapse literature from contrastive learning (BYOL, Barlow Twins, SimCLR) and information bottleneck theory to frame the observed near-uniform cosine similarity, while distinguishing the diffusion transformer case by showing that extreme alignment does not degrade generation quality. The AdaLN conditioning mechanism is positioned as the architectural driver differentiating transformers from U-Net-based diffusion models, which use distributed concatenation or cross-attention and do not exhibit the same degree of embedding compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that up to 66% of conditional embedding dimensions can be pruned without quality loss (and sometimes with improvement) reveals a fundamental over-parameterization in how diffusion transformers encode semantics, suggesting that future architectures could adopt compressed or hybrid conditioning strategies for efficiency. However, the mechanistic explanations remain largely hypothesis-driven rather than rigorously proven, and the analysis is limited to AdaLN-based transformer backbones; whether the semantic bottleneck generalizes to cross-attention conditioning, text-to-image models with token-level conditioning, or non-diffusion generative frameworks (GANs, autoregressive) remains open. A deeper theoretical account of why iterative denoising amplifies subtle directional differences in near-parallel embeddings is explicitly flagged as an open problem.
