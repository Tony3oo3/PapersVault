## Problem

GRPO-based reinforcement learning for multimodal LLMs suffers from reward sparsity and advantage vanishing: when a problem is too easy or too hard for the current model, all sampled responses receive identical rewards, yielding zero relative advantage and stalling optimization. Existing remedies—blind sample expansion, selective sample filtering, and indirect reward shaping—fail to guarantee that the within-group reward distribution retains sufficient variance to produce meaningful gradient signals as training progresses and problem difficulty shifts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multimodal problem consisting of an RGB image and a textual question, and during training it dynamically generates semantically consistent variants (rephrased text, perturbed images, or partial reasoning hints) that preserve the original ground-truth answer. At inference, the trained model produces a step-by-step reasoning chain terminated by a boxed final answer. Variant generation uses GPT-o3 offline for textual rephrasings and reasoning steps, while image perturbations (Gaussian/salt noise, rotation, blur) are applied online at negligible cost.

## Method

DIVA-GRPO dynamically tracks each problem's difficulty via a running accuracy estimate updated every epoch, then adaptively samples variants matched to that difficulty: easy problems receive text-and-image perturbations, moderate problems receive textual paraphrases, and hard problems receive partial reasoning-step hints from a closed-source model. Advantages are computed at two granularities—local (within a single problem's k rollouts) and global (across all m×k rollouts in the variant set)—then brought to comparable scale via separate batch-level z-score normalization. A difficulty-weighted exponential rescaling amplifies correct answers on harder-than-average variants and penalizes incorrect ones on easier-than-average variants, using relative difficulty within each problem group. Finally, a reward-range-based rescaling (RRB-Rescaling) multiplies normalized advantages by the actual reward spread divided by the maximum possible range, preventing trivial reward differences from being exaggerated into misleading optimization signals. Theoretical analysis (Appendices B–C) shows this pipeline reduces gradient variance while preserving unbiasedness, and that the optimal update magnitude under binary rewards is maximized at a 50/50 correct-incorrect ratio, justifying the adaptive difficulty targeting.

## Results

DIVA-GRPO-7B achieves an average score of 54.58 across six multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, OlympiadBench, WeMath, MMK12-test), setting a new state-of-the-art at the 7B scale. It surpasses its backbone Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 8.23 points on average and matches or exceeds the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (59.0) on MathVista (74.2 vs 74.8), MathVerse (57.6 vs 57.6), and WeMath (69.3 vs 72.4). In terms of training efficiency, DIVA-GRPO reaches optimal performance with a 2.55× reduction in required steps (N=3) and delivers a 1.76× end-to-end wall-clock speedup (N=1) compared to standard GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIVA-GRPO-7B (54.58 avg) outperforms all 7B-scale fine-tuned baselines: R1-ShareVL-7B (52.30, +2.28), MM-Eureka-7B (49.93, +4.65), Adora-7B (48.17, +6.41), and OpenVLThinker-7B (48.07, +6.51). It also surpasses the proprietary GPT-4o (49.68, +4.90) and the open-source Qwen-2.5-VL-32B (54.6, roughly tied) and InternVL2.5-VL-78B (52.53, +2.05), while remaining competitive with the larger Qwen-2.5-VL-72B (59.0, −4.42) on most benchmarks except OlympiadBench and MathVision. Against the backbone Qwen-2.5-VL-7B (46.23), the method yields a consistent +8.23 average gain across all six benchmarks. In training-curve comparisons (Figure 3a), DIVA-GRPO outperforms standard GRPO, GSPO, and DAPO in both convergence speed and final validation accuracy under matched compute budgets. The method still trails frontier proprietary systems (o1 at 72.30, Gemini2-flash at 59.77, Claude3.7-Sonnet at 56.15) on the most challenging competition-level tasks, particularly OlympiadBench (23.1 vs 68.0 for o1), reflecting inherent capacity limits at the 7B scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIVA-GRPO extends the GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024) and the variant-based global advantage computation introduced by R1-ShareVL (Yao et al., 2025) by adding a difficulty-adaptive mechanism that dynamically adjusts which variants are sampled per problem. It departs from three existing solution families—sample expansion (Noisy Rollout, Hint-GRPO, DeepVideo-R1), selective utilization (VL-Rethinker, MM-Eureka), and indirect reward design (StepGRPO, Reg-GRPO, GRPO-LEAD)—by targeting the root cause directly: maintaining stable within-group reward variance through difficulty-calibrated variant sampling rather than enlarging data, filtering samples, or redesigning reward signals. The reward-range-based rescaling (RRB) component is positioned as a drop-in improvement compatible with any GRPO variant, building on the variance-reduction motivation shared with Dr.GRPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.55× reduction in training steps and 1.76× wall-clock speedup make DIVA-GRPO practically significant for teams running GRPO-based RL on MLLMs, where compute cost is a primary bottleneck. The RRB-Rescaling component, which generalizes to standard GRPO with a ~2-point accuracy gain, offers a low-cost improvement that any practitioner can adopt. However, the method still relies on a strong external model (GPT-o3) for offline variant and think-step generation, and the difficulty assessment remains a heuristic that may not transfer cleanly to non-binary reward settings or video modalities. Open questions include whether the 50/50 correct/incorrect balance assumption (formally proven for binary rewards) holds under the multi-level reward schemes used in practice, and whether the approach scales to longer-horizon or multi-turn reasoning tasks.
