## Problem

In Generalized Schrödinger Bridge Matching (GSBM), the task-specific stage cost V_t(x)—encoding obstacles, congestion penalties, or latent reconstruction errors—is treated as a deterministic, noise-free quantity in the CondSOC optimization. In practice, this cost is often noisy or uncertain (e.g., imperfect LiDAR projections, approximate VAE reconstructions), yet existing methods produce only a single point-estimate path that fully trusts the objective. The paper asks whether a Bayesian treatment of the pinned marginal path can yield more robust bridge solutions under such uncertainty.

## Input / Output

The method takes samples from two endpoint distributions π₀ and π₁ (64×64 RGB images for AFHQ cat/dog translation; 3D LiDAR point clouds or 2D positions for crowd navigation) along with a task-specific stage cost function V_t(x), which may be corrupted by noise. It outputs a learned SDE drift v_θ(t, x_t) for sampling bridged paths, together with a full posterior GP over the pinned marginal mean μ_t and stdev γ_t, providing per-time-step uncertainty quantification (visualized as shaded regions) that is absent in deterministic baselines like GSBM.

## Method

GP-GSBM replaces GSBM's deterministic spline parametrization of the pinned marginal P_t(x|x₀,x₁) = N(x; μ_t, γ_t²I) with a sparse variational GP posterior. A GP prior (squared-exponential kernel, linear-interpolation mean) is placed on μ_t and on a log-transformed γ_t to enforce positivity, with boundary conditions μ₀=x₀, μ₁=x₁, γ₀=γ₁=0 handled via GP conditioning. The CondSOC objective J(P_•; V_•) is reinterpreted as a noisy likelihood, and the posterior is approximated via Titsias-style sparse variational free-energy inference with n fixed inducing points, yielding closed-form GP mean and covariance for the variational posterior. The ELBO is optimized jointly over variational parameters Λ, kernel hyperparameters η, and a likelihood temperature τ (empirical Bayes), after which the neural-network SDE is trained by conditional flow matching on samples drawn from the GP posterior. This adds O(n²d) overhead over GSBM's spline optimization but yields lower CondSOC losses and better FID scores under noisy stage-cost scenarios.

## Results

On the AFHQ dog→cat image translation task, GP-GSBM achieves a FID of 10.21, outperforming GSBM (12.39), DSBM (14.16), and Stream-level GP (18.77). On the Stunnel crowd navigation problem under uncertain (randomly toggled) obstacles, GP-GSBM achieves a CondSOC loss of 452.30 versus GSBM's 502.20, demonstrating its robustness advantage. On the GMM obstacle problem under uncertainty, GP-GSBM reaches 89.2 CondSOC loss compared to GSBM's 101.6. Under deterministic (noise-free) settings, GP-GSBM matches or slightly beats GSBM (e.g., 488.78 vs 492.94 on Stunnel), confirming no performance degradation when noise is absent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GP-GSBM consistently outperforms all three baselines across every evaluated setting. Against DSBM (Shi et al., 2023), which ignores the stage cost entirely, GP-GSBM achieves dramatically lower CondSOC losses (e.g., 452.30 vs 9549.2 on uncertain Stunnel) and lower FID (10.21 vs 14.16 on AFHQ), as expected since DSBM produces straight-line paths that ignore obstacles. Against GSBM (Liu et al., 2024), the primary deterministic baseline, GP-GSBM wins on all metrics: it achieves lower CondSOC loss under both deterministic and uncertain settings (e.g., 452.30 vs 502.20 on uncertain Stunnel; 89.2 vs 101.6 on uncertain GMM) and a lower FID of 10.21 vs 12.39 on AFHQ. The gap widens under noisy/uncertain conditions, confirming the robustness benefit of the GP posterior. Against Stream-level GP (Wei & Ma, 2025), which conditions a GP on fixed stream points and cannot incorporate task-specific stage costs, GP-GSBM achieves substantially lower CondSOC losses (e.g., 8300.0 vs 12679.1 on noisy LiDAR) and lower FID (10.21 vs 18.77), as the stream-level GP has no mechanism to penalize paths through obstacles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Generalized Schrödinger Bridge Matching (GSBM) framework of Liu et al. (2024) by replacing its deterministic spline-based CondSOC optimization with a sparse variational Gaussian process posterior over the pinned marginal path. The key conceptual departure is treating the CondSOC objective (which includes a task-specific stage cost) as a noisy likelihood function rather than a deterministic quantity to minimize, drawing on the sparse variational GP inference machinery of Titsias (2009). The paper also positions itself against stream-level GP flow matching (Wei & Ma, 2025), which conditions a GP on fixed stream points and cannot systematically incorporate stage costs, and against GP-prior flow matching for time-series (Kollovieh et al., 2025), which uses GP as a prior on the source distribution rather than on the path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: stage costs in GSB applications (LiDAR projections, VAE reconstruction errors, obstacle presence) are inherently noisy, yet GSBM treats them as deterministic, leading to fragile solutions under uncertainty. The GP posterior provides principled uncertainty quantification and the empirical Bayes model selection for the temperature τ automatically balances prior smoothness against likelihood fidelity, which is a useful adaptive mechanism. However, the computational overhead from kernel inversion (O(n²d) plus O(n³) for model selection) remains a concern for high-dimensional states, and the paper acknowledges this as an open problem. The improvements over GSBM, while consistent across noisy LiDAR, Stunnel, GMM, and AFHQ experiments, are modest in magnitude, suggesting the benefit is primarily robustness rather than raw performance.
