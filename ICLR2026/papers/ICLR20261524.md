## Problem

Standard preference learning methods (RLHF and NLHF) produce policies that concentrate on a single alternative even when evaluator preferences are nearly tied, failing to reflect the proportional distribution of evaluator groups in the population. This leads to biased policies and high sensitivity to small perturbations, making them vulnerable to strategic manipulation by any single group. The paper identifies this gap through a motivating binary-alternative example and formalizes it via two new axioms—population-proportional alignment (PPA) and population-bounded manipulability (PBM)—that neither RLHF nor NLHF satisfies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only pairwise comparison data (probabilities P(y ≻ y′)) without requiring full rankings or explicit group labels, and outputs a probability distribution over alternatives whose entries are proportional to inferred upper bounds on each group's population share. In the LLM setting, the input is a context prompt paired with sampled pairwise comparisons, and the output is a fine-tuned policy (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct) whose response distribution approximates the population-proportional policy. A key constraint is that the true population distribution w^σ is fundamentally non-identifiable from pairwise data alone, so the method operates on a polyhedral outer approximation of the feasible set.

## Method

The core insight is that the set of feasible population distributions W(P) consistent with a given preference function can be characterized as all w satisfying w_i ≤ u_i where u_i = min_{y≠y_i} P(y_i ≻ y), yielding a tractable polyhedral outer approximation with only M linear constraints. The proposed algorithm F* assigns policy mass π(y_i) = u_i / Σ_j u_j, a conservative choice that minimizes worst-case misalignment over the feasible set, and provably satisfies monotonicity, Pareto efficiency, α-PPA (with α depending on the number of non-dominated alternatives), and γ-PBM with (γ_1, γ_2) = (1/2, 1/2). A softmax relaxation F^β with parameter β ≥ 0 smoothly interpolates between perfect proportional alignment (β=0) and the minimax Condorcet method (β→∞), enabling a tunable trade-off between PPA and Condorcet consistency. For high-dimensional LLM alignment, a two-phase function approximation approach first learns a selector model to estimate u(x) per context, then optimizes the softmax policy, validated on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct with the Alpaca-GPT4 dataset.

## Results

On the MovieLens tabular task, the proposed F^β achieves a PPA level of 0.4869 at β=0 (vs. 0 for both RLHF and NLHF), while matching their win rate of 0.7784 at β=100. The PBM (manipulation gain) drops from 0.0611 (RLHF) and 0.0124 (NLHF) to 8.9×10⁻⁴ for F^β at β=1. On the Alpaca-GPT4 LLM task, F^β at β=0 achieves a PPA of 0.5012 (Style) and 0.1428 (Expertise) with competitive win rates (0.8398 and 0.7613), while DPO achieves PPA of 0.3786 and 0.1321 respectively. The softmax parameter β provides a smooth trade-off: increasing β from 0 to 100 raises synthetic win rate from 0.6157 to 0.8429 while PPA drops from 0.0883 to 0.0003.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RLHF (Maximal Borda), the proposed F^β at β=0 sacrifices win rate (0.5987 vs. 0.7784 on MovieLens) but achieves a PPA of 0.4869 versus RLHF's 0, and reduces manipulation gain (PBM) from 0.0611 to 8.9×10⁻⁴. Against NLHF (Maximal Lotteries), the same pattern holds: F^β at β=0 has lower win rate (0.5987 vs. 0.7712) but nonzero PPA and a 14× lower PBM (8.9×10⁻⁴ vs. 0.0124). Against DPO on the Alpaca-GPT4 LLM task, F^β at β=0 achieves higher PPA (0.5012 vs. 0.3786 on Style; 0.1428 vs. 0.1321 on Expertise) with comparable win rates (0.8398 vs. 0.8478; 0.7613 vs. 0.7697). On the synthetic color task, DPO achieves the highest win rate (0.8566) but zero PPA, while F^β at β=0 achieves the highest PPA (0.0883) at a lower win rate (0.6157). Overall, the proposed method trades a modest win-rate reduction for substantially better population-proportional alignment and robustness to strategic manipulation, with β providing a tunable knob to recover baseline-level win rates at the cost of PPA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the axiomatic social-choice-theory approach to AI alignment (Ge et al., 2024; Mishra, 2023; Siththaranjan et al., 2024) by introducing two new axioms—population-proportional alignment (PPA) and population-bounded manipulability (PBM)—that neither RLHF (maximal Borda) nor NLHF (maximal lotteries) satisfy. It builds on Tatli et al. (2024) for recovering population distributions from pairwise comparison data, but departs from pluralistic alignment work (Sorensen et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2025) by requiring no explicit group labels, instead inferring feasible population distributions directly from the preference function. The softmax relaxation connecting PPA to Condorcet consistency (minimax Condorcet method) provides a principled bridge between the proportional and majority-based paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that proportional representation of evaluator populations is fundamentally incompatible with Condorcet consistency (Proposition 4.13), a clean impossibility result that clarifies the design space for alignment algorithms. The PBM guarantee (γ₁=1/2) provides a concrete robustness property—non-majority groups cannot manipulate their preferred alternative into majority status—that is absent from both RLHF and NLHF. However, the empirical validation is preliminary: the LLM experiments use a 3B model with small-scale data, the two-phase function approximation is computationally heavier than DPO, and accurately measuring PPA in high-dimensional settings remains an open challenge. Future work on direct policy optimization and incorporating lower-ranked preferences will be needed to assess practical impact.
