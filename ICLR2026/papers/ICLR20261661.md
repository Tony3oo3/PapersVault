## Problem

Full fine-tuning of LLMs is computationally prohibitive due to full gradient computation, while PEFT methods (LoRA, prompt tuning) are efficient but fail to learn new knowledge and exhibit suboptimal performance. No existing approach combines the adaptation power of full fine-tuning with the efficiency of PEFT, and no theoretical framework accounts for the heterogeneous smoothness landscape that arises when jointly optimizing a large pre-trained model and a small adapter module.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen-then-unfrozen pre-trained LLM (OPT-1.3b, Vicuna-7b, or LLaMA-2-7b), a lightweight PEFT module (prompt tokens, prefix vectors, or LoRA matrices), and a supervised training set (1,000 examples per task). The LLM parameters are updated via a two-point zeroth-order gradient estimator (requiring only forward passes with Gaussian perturbations, no backpropagation through the full model), while the PEFT module receives standard first-order gradients. The output is a jointly fine-tuned model whose peak GPU memory matches that of FO-PEFT alone (e.g., 46 GB vs. 54 GB for FO full fine-tuning on LLaMA-2-7B), since the ZO update avoids storing per-layer gradient states for the base model.

## Method

Hybrid fine-tuning jointly optimizes LLM parameters x (via zeroth-order SGD with a two-point Gaussian perturbation estimator) and PEFT module parameters y (via standard first-order SGD) under a single SGD-with-Random-Reshuffling schedule, using separate learning rates η_x ≪ η_y. The key theoretical contribution is the hybrid smoothness condition (Definition 1), which bounds the Hessian by a block-diagonal matrix with distinct non-decreasing sub-quadratic functions ℓ_x and ℓ_y for each parameter block, capturing both the dynamic (gradient-norm-dependent) and heterogeneous (module-dependent) smoothness observed empirically. Under this condition, Theorem 1 establishes that SGD with Random Reshuffling achieves O(ε^{-2}/δ + ε^{-4}/n) epoch complexity to reach an ε-stationary point, matching known lower bounds for non-convex SGD. The analysis reveals that the learning rate for x must scale as O(1/(L_x n d_x)) while y scales as O(1/(L_y n)), formally justifying the empirically necessary use of distinct learning rates. Empirically, the method outperforms both FO-PEFT and ZO full fine-tuning in 17/18 model-task combinations and converges ~5× faster in training steps.

## Results

Across 18 model-task combinations (3 architectures × 6 tasks), hybrid fine-tuning achieves the highest accuracy in 17 out of 18 cases (94.5%) compared to its FO-PEFT counterpart. In pairwise comparisons (each FO method vs. its hybrid variant), the hybrid approach wins in 41 out of 54 combinations (~76%). On convergence, hybrid fine-tuning reaches 90% accuracy on SST-2 in ~2,500 steps versus 12,500+ steps for FO-PEFT baselines, while matching the memory footprint of FO prompt tuning (46 GB on Llama-2-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FO-Prompt (Lester et al., 2021), Hybrid-Prompt wins on 11 of 18 model-task pairs, with the largest gains on WSC (e.g., 61.5 vs. 36.5 on Llama-2-7b) and WiC (64.4 vs. 58.5 on Llama-2-7b), though it loses on RTE and WSC for Vicuna-7b. Against FO-LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), Hybrid-LoRA achieves the most consistent advantage, winning on 10 of 18 pairs with notable gains on WSC (72.1 vs. 53.8 on Vicuna-7b) and RTE (61.0 vs. 54.8 on OPT-1.3b), while losing only on WinoGrande for OPT-1.3b (58.3 vs. 59.0). Against FO-Prefix (Li & Liang, 2021), results are mixed: Hybrid-Prefix wins on COPA and WinoGrande for Vicuna-7b (83.0 vs. 80.0, 74.0 vs. 64.1) but underperforms on WSC for Llama-2-7b (42.3 vs. 51.9). Against ZO full fine-tuning (Zhang et al., 2024), hybrid fine-tuning outperforms both ZO-FT and FO-FT in 13 of 18 combinations (~72.2%), confirming that the hybrid approach captures the knowledge-learning benefit of full fine-tuning while retaining the convergence speed of first-order PEFT updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of zeroth-order LLM fine-tuning (Malladi et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2024 ZO-Bench) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, Prompt/Prefix Tuning), proposing to jointly update both the base LLM (via ZO gradient estimation) and a PEFT module (via standard FO gradients). The theoretical contribution extends the generalized smoothness framework of Zhang et al. (2019) and Li et al. (2024) to a heterogeneous, multi-block setting, introducing a 'hybrid smoothness condition' that assigns distinct smoothness functions to the LLM and PEFT parameter subspaces. The convergence analysis builds on the SGD-with-Random-Reshuffling literature (Mishchenko et al. 2020; Safran & Shamir 2020) but is the first to handle the combined ZO/FO optimization under non-uniform, gradient-dependent smoothness bounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid smoothness condition provides a principled theoretical justification for the empirically observed need for different learning rates in joint LLM+PEFT training, filling a gap where standard L-smoothness analysis is inapplicable. Practically, the method achieves consistent accuracy gains (94.5% in aggregate comparisons) and a notable convergence speedup (~5× fewer steps to 90% accuracy on OPT-1.3B/SST-2) without additional memory overhead over FO prompt tuning. However, the convergence speedup evidence is primarily from a single model-task pair, the pairwise win rate (76%) is more modest than the aggregate figure, and the theoretical guarantees are specific to SGD with Random Reshuffling—extending to adaptive optimizers like Adam (acknowledged as future work) or to larger-scale settings remains open. The ZO gradient estimation also introduces an approximation error term that may limit gains in high-dimensional regimes.
