## Problem

Existing generative models for protein dynamics are confined to nanosecond-scale trajectories and small proteins due to quadratic/cubic memory growth from pairwise representations, error accumulation during autoregressive rollouts, and factorized spatial-then-temporal attention that cannot capture coupled spatio-temporal dependencies. This prevents modeling of biologically relevant microsecond-to-millisecond conformational transitions that underlie catalysis, binding, and allostery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265073/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a protein amino-acid sequence and a single starting conformation (SE(3) backbone rigids: per-residue translations in R^3 and rotations in SO(3), plus torsional angles for side-chain atoms) and autoregressively generates a full conformation trajectory frame-by-frame. A key design feature is continuous-time conditioning: the physical stride Δt between frames is sampled from LogUniform[10⁻², 10¹] ns during training, enabling generation at arbitrary temporal resolutions (e.g., 1.2 ns or 2.5 ns per frame) without retraining, and decoupling the number of context frames from the physical timescale covered.

## Method

STAR-MD is a causal SE(3) diffusion transformer that generates each frame via denoising score matching on translations and rotations, conditioned on all prior clean frames through a joint spatio-temporal (S×T) attention mechanism operating on single-residue tokens indexed by (residue, frame) pairs with 2D-RoPE embeddings. Critically, it removes the Pairformer module (eliminating O(N³) spatial cost) and instead relies on the S×T attention over singles to implicitly learn pairwise relationships, justified theoretically via the Mori-Zwanzig formalism: projecting out pair features inflates the memory kernel with non-separable spatio-temporal coupling that factorized attention cannot capture. For training, a block-causal attention pattern concatenates clean history and noisy target frames in a single sequence, enabling parallel teacher-forcing that matches sequential autoregressive inference. A contextual noise perturbation (inspired by Diffusion Forcing) adds small forward-diffusion noise to historical frames during both training and inference, ensuring training-inference consistency and preventing compounding drift over microsecond-scale rollouts. The architecture achieves O(N²L²) compute and O(NL) KV-cache memory per layer, versus O(N³L) compute and O(N²L) cache for Pairformer-based baselines.

## Results

On the standard 100 ns ATLAS benchmark, STAR-MD achieves state-of-the-art performance across all metrics: 0.43 JSD, 0.54 conformational recall, 0.17 tICA correlation, 0.07 RMSD deviation, and 85.29% combined structural validity (CA+AA), approaching the MD oracle (0.31 JSD, 0.67 recall, 96.43% validity). Critically, STAR-MD extrapolates to 1 μs trajectories (400 frames at 2.5 ns stride) while maintaining 79.93% structural validity and 0.61 recall, whereas all baselines degrade catastrophically—MDGen drops to 24.81% validity, AlphaFolding to 0.06%, and ConfRover-W to 36.91%. The model also remains stable at a finer 1.2 ns stride (~833 frames for 1 μs) without retraining, thanks to continuous-time conditioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR-MD outperforms all three baselines on every metric at the standard 100 ns benchmark. Against MDGen, STAR-MD improves conformational recall from 0.28 to 0.54, reduces RMSD deviation from 0.38 to 0.07, and raises combined validity from 68.31% to 85.29%. Against ConfRover, the gains are even larger: recall improves from 0.36 to 0.54, VAMP-2 score drops from 0.47 to 0.10, and CA+AA validity jumps from 52.06% to 85.29%. AlphaFolding is effectively non-functional for this task, producing only 0.47% structurally valid frames and a 3.31 Å RMSD deviation (vs. 0.07 for STAR-MD). At the 1 μs extrapolation setting, the gap widens dramatically: STAR-MD maintains 79.93% validity and 0.61 recall, while MDGen collapses to 24.81% validity, ConfRover-W to 36.91%, and AlphaFolding to 0.06%. Notably, ConfRover (full-attention) could not even be evaluated at 240 ns or 1 μs due to prohibitive KV-cache memory requirements (pair features scale as O(N²L)), necessitating a windowed variant (ConfRover-W) that further degrades its already inferior performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR-MD extends the autoregressive protein dynamics framework of ConfRover (Shen et al., 2025) by replacing its Pairformer-based spatial modeling with a joint spatio-temporal attention mechanism over single-residue features, eliminating the O(N³) spatial bottleneck. The per-frame generation mechanism builds on SE(3) diffusion (Yim et al., 2023), while the contextual noise perturbation for long-horizon stability is adapted from Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024), and the block-causal training strategy follows recent video diffusion work (Arriola et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure is the theoretical argument via Mori-Zwanzig formalism that removing explicit pairwise features 'inflates' the memory kernel in a non-separable spatio-temporal way, thereby justifying joint (rather than factorized) attention as a necessity rather than a design preference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAR-MD is the first generative model to stably produce microsecond-scale protein trajectories with high structural validity (~80% combined validity at 1 μs), a regime where all prior methods degrade catastrophically. The elimination of cubic spatial scaling and the 196× KV-cache reduction make long-horizon generation of large proteins practically feasible on standard GPU hardware. Key limitations include the gap between generated and oracle temporal consistency, evaluation on only 8 proteins at the 1 μs scale, and restriction to single-chain proteins without solvent or small-molecule interactions. Open questions include whether the joint S×T attention + contextual noise recipe transfers to protein complexes, ligand binding, or other biomolecular systems, and whether the Mori-Zwanzig-inspired architecture can be further refined to close the remaining fidelity gap to MD.
