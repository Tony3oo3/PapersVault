## Problem

Testing whether a Boolean function is s-Fourier sparse or ε-far from every such function under Hamming distance has remained open with a large gap: the best known tester required Õ(s^14) queries while the best lower bound was only Ω(√s). The Hamming-distance setting is intrinsically harder than the Euclidean one because the tester must certify not only the presence of s large Fourier coefficients but also the absence of a Fourier tail, and the class of Fourier-sparse functions is strictly more general than juntas (e.g., a linear function is 1-sparse yet depends on O(n) variables), so existing junta-testing frameworks do not directly apply.

## Input / Output

The tester receives black-box membership query access to an unknown Boolean function f: F_2^n → {−1,+1} along with a sparsity parameter s and a distance threshold ε, and outputs an accept/reject decision distinguishing s-Fourier-sparse functions from those ε-far (in Hamming distance) from every s-sparse function. Crucially, the query complexity Õ(max{s², 1/ε}·s²) is independent of the ambient dimension n, and the function is consumed solely through point evaluations f(x) chosen adaptively or non-adaptively by the tester—no structural access to Fourier coefficients or circuit description is assumed.

## Method

The upper bound combines three ingredients: (i) a refined implicit coset sampler (Algorithm 3) that, given query access to f, discovers the Fourier span S_{f∘A}(θ) of f composed with an unknown invertible linear map A and generates uniform samples from the leaves of the associated parity decision tree, using the Economical Sieve (a local list-correction procedure for the Hadamard code) to identify heavy Fourier coefficients implicitly; (ii) Sanyal's structural theorem bounding the dimension of the Fourier span by O(√s), which reduces the problem to reconstructing a low-dimensional function f*: F_2^r → {−1,+1} with r = O(√s); and (iii) ℓ₁-minimization-based compressed sensing with the RIP guarantee of Haviv & Regev to exactly recover the Fourier spectrum of f* from Õ(s²) random coset samples. The lower bound of Ω(s) is obtained by reducing Fourier sparsity testing to the approximate matrix rank problem in randomized communication complexity, exploiting the fact that Maiorana-McFarland functions composed with rank-r linear maps have Fourier sparsity exactly r², and invoking the Ω(r²) communication lower bound of Sherstov & Storozhenko.

## Results

The paper establishes two main theoretical results for testing Fourier sparsity of Boolean functions under Hamming distance. The upper bound (Theorem 1.1) gives a non-adaptive tester with query complexity Õ(max{s², 1/ε} · s²), which simplifies to Õ(s⁴) when ε is a constant. The lower bound (Theorem 1.2) proves that any (possibly adaptive) tester distinguishing s-Fourier-sparse functions from (1/4)-far functions requires Ω(s) queries. Both results are independent of the ambient dimension n.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is Gopalan et al. (SICOMP 2011), who previously established the best-known bounds for Fourier sparsity testing under Hamming distance. This paper improves the upper bound from Õ(s¹⁴) to Õ(s⁴), a polynomial reduction of s¹⁰ in the sparsity parameter, by replacing the hashing-based coefficient identification approach with a compressed-sensing-based reconstruction pipeline combined with a novel coset sampler. The lower bound is improved quadratically from Ω(√s) to Ω(s), achieved via a reduction from the approximate matrix rank problem in communication complexity (Sherstov & Storozhenko, FOCS 2024) applied to the Maiorana-McFarland family of Boolean functions. The gap between upper and lower bounds is thus narrowed from s¹⁴ vs. √s (a gap of s^13.5) to s⁴ vs. s (a gap of s³), representing a substantial tightening of the complexity landscape for this problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the hashing-based approach of Gopalan et al. (2011), which exploited the granularity of nonzero Fourier coefficients, and instead adopts the testing-via-implicit-learning paradigm (Diakonikolas et al. 2007; Chakraborty et al. 2011) combined with ℓ₁-minimization-based compressed sensing (Haviv & Regev 2017). A key conceptual departure is recognizing that Fourier-sparse functions are strictly more general than juntas (e.g., a linear function is 1-sparse but depends on O(n) variables), necessitating a new coset-sampling mechanism for parity decision trees rather than the existing junta sample extractors. The lower bound introduces a novel reduction from the approximate matrix rank problem in communication complexity (Sherstov & Storozhenko 2024), leveraging structural properties of Maiorana-McFarland functions to achieve a quadratically stronger Ω(s) bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper narrows the gap between upper and lower bounds for Fourier sparsity testing from a factor of s^(13.5) to s^3, providing a much tighter characterization of the problem's complexity. The coset-sampling technique for parity decision trees and the communication-complexity reduction are tools of independent interest that may apply to other affine-invariant property testing problems. Open questions include extending the framework to tolerant testing (sketched in the conclusion), understanding whether the s⁴ upper bound is tight, and exploring connections to practical compressed-sensing-based learning pipelines where sparsity certification is a prerequisite for exact recovery.
