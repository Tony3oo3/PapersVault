## Problem

Consistency-based uncertainty quantification methods for LLMs rely on multinomial sampling to generate candidate outputs, but in short-form QA this produces 30–50% duplicates for short answers and introduces high run-to-run variance in uncertainty estimates. The stochastic nature of sampling means each run yields a different, noisy set of candidates, undermining the robustness of the resulting confidence scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264740/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a question x and a greedy (or top-1 beam) answer y*, the method runs beam search with width M=10 to obtain distinct candidate sequences and their autoregressive probabilities, then computes a scalar uncertainty score for y* using an NLI entailment probability (DeBERTa-large fine-tuned on MNLI) as the pairwise similarity function. Evaluation is performed via the Prediction-Rejection Ratio (PRR) with AlignScore as the quality metric, measuring how well the uncertainty score ranks correct vs. incorrect answers across rejection thresholds.

## Method

The paper replaces multinomial sampling with beam search to generate M distinct candidate outputs, then applies importance weighting using the normalized beam probabilities w_i = p(b^(i)|x) / Σ_j p(b^(j)|x) to form a probability-weighted estimator of dissimilarity, U_D^b(y*|x) = Σ_i w_i(1 − s(b^(i), y*)). A distribution-free sufficient condition (Theorem 1) guarantees that this beam-weighted estimator achieves lower mean-squared error than the Monte Carlo estimator whenever the beam set captures probability mass exceeding 1 − 1/(2√M) (≈ 0.842 for M=10). The same beam-weighted formulation is adapted to Eccentricity (weighted centroid distance in a Graph Laplacian embedding), CoCoA (multiplying the weighted dissimilarity by a model-based sequence uncertainty), and a new EigVecDissimilarity estimator (weighted average distance to individual sample embeddings). Across six QA datasets and six LLMs, the beam-guided variants consistently outperform their multinomial-sampling counterparts, achieving state-of-the-art PRR.

## Results

The primary metric is Prediction-Rejection Ratio (PRR), averaged over six QA datasets. Beam-search variants of Dissimilarity achieve the best PRR for all three base models (0.543 for Llama 3.1 8B, 0.650 for Gemma 3 4B, 0.478 for Qwen 3 8B), while CocoaMSP+beamsearch leads for Llama 3.1 8B instruct (0.426) and Gemma 3 4B instruct (0.345). Every beam-guided variant improves over its multinomial-sampling counterpart across all six models, with gains of 0.02–0.05 PRR. The best beam-search methods also outperform all information-based and reflexive baselines (e.g., Perplexity, CCP, P(True)) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All beam-search variants dominate every baseline across all six models. Dissimilarity+beamsearch achieves the highest PRR for all three base models (0.543, 0.650, 0.478), surpassing the strongest information-based baseline (Perplexity: 0.452, 0.525, 0.372) by 0.09–0.13 PRR. For instruct models, CocoaMSP+beamsearch leads on Llama 3.1 8B (0.426) and Gemma 3 4B (0.345), while EigVecDissimilarity+beamsearch is best on Qwen 3 8B (0.376). The reflexive P(True) baseline is consistently the weakest (often near or below zero), confirming that verbalized confidence is unreliable. Among the original consistency-based methods (Dissimilarity, Eccentricity, EigVecDissimilarity, CoCoA), replacing multinomial sampling with beam search yields a uniform 0.02–0.05 PRR gain, and the beam variants also outperform the non-consistency baselines (Semantic Entropy, Lexical Similarity, EigValLaplacian, NumSemSets) by 0.05–0.20 PRR depending on the model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the consistency-based UQ framework introduced by Lin et al. (2024) for Dissimilarity and Eccentricity, and by Vashurin et al. (2025b) for CoCoA, by replacing their multinomial sampling component with beam search candidates weighted by autoregressive sequence probabilities. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that beam search—already a standard decoding strategy—naturally produces distinct, high-probability candidates that better approximate the model's output distribution for short-form QA, and that a simple importance-weighted estimator over these candidates (with a theoretical guarantee from Theorem 1) dominates the Monte Carlo estimator. The paper also introduces EigVecDissimilarity as a new estimator that bridges Dissimilarity and Eccentricity in the Graph Laplacian embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that beam search is often already run for decoding, so the UQ signal comes at essentially no additional computational cost, and the method consistently improves PRR across six models and six QA datasets. The distribution-free sufficient condition (beam mass > 1 − 1/(2√M)) gives practitioners a clear, checkable criterion for when beam search is guaranteed to outperform multinomial sampling. Key limitations include restriction to short-form QA (generalizability to longer generations is untested), white-box requirement (access to token probabilities), and dependence on an external NLI model for the similarity function. Open questions include whether the approach extends to black-box settings via empirical probability estimates and whether the gains persist for open-ended, multi-sentence generation where duplication is less of an issue.
