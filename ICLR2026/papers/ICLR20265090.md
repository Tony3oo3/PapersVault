## Problem

Prior Wasserstein-based adversarial imitation learning methods using the Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual are forced to use Euclidean distance as the ground metric due to the 1-Lipschitz constraint, which fails to capture environment dynamics and can severely mislead learning. Additionally, most IL methods require large amounts of expert data including actions, both of which are often unavailable in practice. The paper asks whether a dynamics-aware distance metric can be learned from minimal, low-quality state-only data to enable expert-level imitation from just a single state-only trajectory.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three data sources: a single state-only expert trajectory (1000 steps, no actions), a small set of 10K random state transitions (only ~1% of the 1M online samples, and of very low quality with normalized rewards near zero), and online environment interactions. It produces a deterministic policy π: S → A for continuous control. Notably, the expert data contains no action labels, and the auxiliary random data requires no reward or action annotations—only state pairs (s, s′) are needed for ICVF pre-training.

## Method

LWAIL is a two-stage adversarial imitation learning framework. In the pre-training stage, an Intention-Conditioned Value Function (ICVF) is trained via IQL on random state-only transitions to learn a state embedding φ(s) whose Euclidean distance encodes dynamics-aware reachability; the discriminator f is then pre-trained in this frozen latent space. In the imitation stage, φ remains frozen and the Euclidean distance ‖φ(s)−φ(s′)‖₂ serves as the Wasserstein ground metric within a KR-dual adversarial objective, where f acts simultaneously as discriminator, pseudo-reward (via sigmoid), and KR dual function, driving a TD3 policy via online RL. A key theoretical result (Thm. 3.1) shows that in near-deterministic MDPs, the state-pair occupancy is approximately a linear combination of φ, justifying the use of the ICVF embedding as the metric space for Wasserstein optimization.

## Results

On four MuJoCo locomotion tasks (Hopper, HalfCheetah, Walker2D, Ant) using only a single state-only expert trajectory, LWAIL achieves an average normalized reward of 99.07, the highest among all 15 baselines. It approaches or exceeds expert-level performance on Hopper (108.84 vs. expert 113.23) and HalfCheetah (90.40 vs. expert 88.42), and matches expert on Walker2D (106.51 vs. 106.84). On navigation tasks with perturbed initial states, LWAIL with ICVF embedding maintains scores above 135 while the no-embedding variant collapses to negative returns at noise level 0.2, demonstrating the critical role of the dynamics-aware metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LWAIL (avg 99.07) outperforms the strongest state-only baseline DIFO (92.51) by 6.6 points on average, and also surpasses WDAIL (89.92) and LS-IQ (88.20), both of which have access to expert actions. Against classic adversarial methods (GAIL 1.90, AIRL 2.83, BC 4.56), LWAIL achieves orders-of-magnitude improvement, confirming the benefit of Wasserstein-based state-pair matching over f-divergence approaches. The closest per-environment competitors are DiffAIL on Walker2D (133.90 vs. 106.51) and OPOLO/LS-IQ on Ant (129.46/132.28 vs. 90.53), but LWAIL is the only method that remains consistently strong across all four tasks without large variance. On navigation with perturbed initial states, LWAIL with ICVF embedding (143–157) dramatically outperforms the no-embedding variant (−11 to −17 at noise ≥ 0.2), and on antmaze-umaze-v2 it scores 34.76 versus 0.03 without embedding, 31.03 for WDAIL, and 0.00 for IQ-Learn. When ICVF embedding is applied to prior methods (WDAIL+ICVF 53.04, IQlearn+ICVF 12.27, GAIL+ICVF 2.49), LWAIL still substantially outperforms them, indicating the synergy between the ICVF metric and the adversarial Wasserstein framework with TD3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Wasserstein-based adversarial imitation learning (WDAIL, IQlearn) and state embedding methods (ICVF, CURL, PW-DICE). It identifies a specific and previously under-addressed limitation of the Kantorovich-Rubinstein dual: the 1-Lipschitz constraint effectively forces the ground metric to be Euclidean in the raw state space, which fails to capture environment dynamics. The key departure is repurposing ICVF (Ghosh et al., 2023), originally designed for offline RL from passive data, as a pre-trained dynamics-aware embedding that makes Euclidean distance in latent space a meaningful ground metric for Wasserstein occupancy matching. The overall objective is a special case of IQ-learn with Wasserstein distance, but the ICVF embedding and TD3-based downstream RL distinguish it from prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving expert-level performance (99.07 avg normalized reward) from a single state-only expert trajectory and 10K random transitions (1% of online data) substantially lowers the data barrier for imitation learning in robotics and control. The navigation experiments reveal a striking robustness property—LWAIL with ICVF maintains performance under initial-state perturbations where the no-embedding variant catastrophically fails—suggesting the learned metric provides genuine generalization beyond memorized expert paths. Key limitations include the near-deterministic MDP assumption in the theoretical guarantee, evaluation restricted to low-dimensional MuJoCo state-vector environments (no visual or high-dimensional inputs), and the iterative actor-discriminator update schedule that requires careful tuning. Open questions include whether the ICVF-embedding approach transfers to image-based or partially observable settings and whether the two-stage pre-training can be made fully end-to-end.
