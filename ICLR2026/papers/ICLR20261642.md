## Problem

Reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) with verifiable rewards has proven effective at eliciting slow-thinking reasoning in vision-language models, but training large models (>7B) with RFT is prohibitively expensive due to multi-copy memory requirements and alternating rollout-optimization phases. Recent evidence suggests RFT does not impart new knowledge but rather amplifies reasoning behaviors already latent in base model distributions, yet no practical method existed to transfer these behaviors to larger untuned models at inference time.

## Input / Output

PROXYTHINKER takes a set of input images and a text prompt, and autoregressively generates a reasoning trace that exhibits self-verification, backtracking, and sub-goal branching before producing a final answer. Unconventionally, each decoding step requires simultaneous forward passes through three models (a large base VLM, a small RFT-trained expert, and a small amateur model) conditioned on the same image-text prefix, with the sampled token fed back to all three for the next step. The method operates on standard VLM benchmarks with multiple-choice and open-ended formats, using greedy decoding by default.

## Method

PROXYTHINKER is a training-free, contrastive-decoding-style inference technique that steers a large base VLM toward slow-thinking reasoning by adding a scaled logit difference to its output distribution at every decoding step. Specifically, at step t the adjusted logits are z_Ψ + α·(z_ψ₁ − z_ψ₀), where z_Ψ are the large base model's logits, z_ψ₁ are the small RFT expert's logits, and z_ψ₀ are the small amateur model's logits, all conditioned on the same images and text prefix; the amateur model acts as a calibration baseline so that only the reasoning-specific signal (e.g., tokens like 'Wait', 'However', 'Verify') is amplified. The hyperparameter α (default 0.5) controls the strength of the steering signal, and the method is robust across α ∈ [0.1, 1.0]. For efficient multi-model inference, the authors implement collaborative decoding on vLLM with tensor parallelism applied to the large model and asynchronous separate process groups for the two small models, synchronizing logits via collective communication before sampling, yielding a 38× speedup over prior decoding-time steering implementations and runtime comparable to running a single 32B RFT model.

## Results

PROXYTHINKER achieves consistent improvements across all five main benchmarks without any training. At the 32B scale with VL-Rethinker-7B as expert, it improves Qwen2.5-VL-32B by +3.4% on MathVista (74.7→78.1), +1.5% on MathVerse (53.8→55.3), +0.8% on MathVision (38.4→39.2), +3.3% on MMMU-Pro (49.5→52.8), and +3.1% on R1-Bench (49.4→52.5), yielding an average relative gain of +2.4%. At the 72B scale, the same expert yields +2.7% average improvement, with the largest single gain of +4.0% on R1-Bench (50.4→54.4). Notably, with OpenVLThinker-7B as expert, PROXYTHINKER-32B reaches 40.8% on MathVision, surpassing the full-scale RFT model VL-Rethinker-32B (40.5%) despite the expert itself scoring only 25.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROXYTHINKER is compared against (1) the untuned base models (Qwen2.5-VL-32B/72B), (2) three 7B RFT experts used standalone (OpenVLThinker-7B, ThinkLite-VL-7B, VL-Rethinker-7B), and (3) full-scale RFT models (VL-Rethinker-32B/72B) as performance ceilings. Against the base models, PROXYTHINKER wins on every benchmark–expert combination except MathVerse with OpenVLThinker-7B (tie at 53.8%) and MathVision with OpenVLThinker-7B at 72B (−1.9%), confirming consistent positive transfer. Against the full-scale RFT ceiling VL-Rethinker-32B, PROXYTHINKER-32B with VL-Rethinker-7B is competitive: it trails on MathVerse (55.3 vs 56.9) and MathVision (39.2 vs 40.5) but surpasses it on MMMU-Pro (52.8 vs 50.6) and R1-Bench (52.5 vs 50.8); with OpenVLThinker-7B it even beats the ceiling on MathVision (40.8 vs 40.5). At the 72B scale, PROXYTHINKER-72B with VL-Rethinker-7B narrows the gap to VL-Rethinker-72B to within 2–4 points on most benchmarks, though the full RFT model retains a clear lead on MathVision (43.9 vs 39.5) and MathVerse (61.7 vs 58.6). Among the three 7B experts, VL-Rethinker-7B consistently provides the strongest guidance, while the weaker OpenVLThinker-7B yields smaller or occasionally negative gains, and the 3B VLAA-Thinker-3B actually degrades performance (MathVista drops from 74.7 to 51.5), demonstrating that expert quality is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROXYTHINKER sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) decoding-time steering methods (DExperts, Contrastive Decoding, DoLa, VCD) that modify LM output distributions at inference without retraining, and (2) RFT/RLVR-based visual reasoners (OpenVLThinker, VL-Rethinker, ThinkLite-VL) that elicit slow-thinking behaviors through reinforcement learning. The key conceptual departure from contrastive decoding is that PROXYTHINKER does not suppress undesirable tokens but rather transfers structured reasoning patterns (self-verification, backtracking) captured in the logit difference between an RFT expert and its base counterpart. The method is further motivated by recent evidence (Shah et al., Yue et al., Gandhi et al.) that RFT amplifies pre-existing reasoning capabilities rather than injecting new knowledge, making logit-space transfer a natural mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PROXYTHINKER offers a practical, training-free path to endow large VLMs (32B–72B) with slow-thinking reasoning at a fraction of the cost of full-scale RFT, and the 38× inference speedup over prior decoding-time methods makes it deployable. However, the method inherits limitations of the small expert: it struggles on knowledge-intensive benchmarks (MMMU) where the expert cannot verify factual content, and undersized experts (3B) can cause negative transfer, degrading the base model below its standalone performance. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to embodied or language-only reasoning at scale, whether the logit-delta mechanism can be distilled into a single-model approximation, and how it interacts with emerging test-time compute strategies like best-of-n sampling.
