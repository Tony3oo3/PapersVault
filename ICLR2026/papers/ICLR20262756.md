## Problem

Existing Topological Deep Learning (TDL) models operate on undirected combinatorial spaces (simplicial or cell complexes) and cannot capture the higher-order directed motifs that are abundant and functionally critical in systems like brain networks. While directed GNNs handle pairwise asymmetry and undirected TDL handles multi-way interactions, no principled framework jointly encodes directionality and higher-order structure in a learnable architecture, leaving a gap in both expressivity and the ability to recover neurotopologically meaningful invariants.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262756/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a directed graph (connectome) with binary time-evolving neuronal firing patterns, which are jointly lifted into an attributed semi-simplicial set called a Dynamical Activity Complex (DAC): a simplex is active at time t iff all its constituent vertices fire simultaneously at t (a min-based lifting over T discrete time bins). The primary output is an 8-class stimulus label from a biologically realistic neocortical microcircuit (31,346 neurons, 7.8M synapses, 4,495 dynamics), with secondary outputs including node/edge/simplice feature vectors and recoverable topological invariants (size, directionality, higher-order directionality, reciprocity, transitive degree, Euler characteristic).

## Method

SSNs propagate information across simplices using face-map-induced relations drawn from a relational algebra closed under union, intersection, composition, and converse, which generalizes standard graph and topological adjacencies to directed higher-order structures (e.g., relating two 2-simplices that share a directed edge). Each layer applies relation-dependent learnable message functions ω_R aggregated via a tensor product operator, subsuming MPNNs, Dir-GNNs, MPSNNs, and transformer-like variants as special cases. To address scalability, Routing-SSNs (R-SSNs) partition relations into semantic classes and apply a learnable top-k gating mechanism to dynamically select the most informative relations per class, reducing active parameters to ~18% of the full SSN. Theoretically, SSNs are proven strictly more expressive than Dir-GNNs and MPSNNs in the WL hierarchy (Theorems 1–2) and uniquely capable of recovering the full set of neurotopological invariants validated for brain activity characterization (Theorem 4), thanks to their joint encoding of directionality and higher-order structure.

## Results

On the 8-class brain stimulus classification task using the NMC neocortical microcircuit, SSNs achieve 87.16% accuracy on the (4, 325μm) fixed volumetric sample and 64.72% on the most challenging varying-topology setting (M=5 neuron neighborhoods). SSNs outperform the second-best model (R-SSN or MPSNN-64, depending on setting) by 14–27 percentage points across all six experimental configurations, and exceed message-passing GNNs by up to ~50% in relative accuracy. The routing variant R-SSN consistently ranks second or third while using only 18% of SSN's active parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSNs decisively outperform all six baselines across every experimental setting. Against message-passing GNNs (Gilmer et al., 2017; Hamilton et al., 2017), SSNs gain over 50 percentage points in the best case (87.16% vs. 23.02% on (4,325μm)), confirming that pairwise undirected aggregation is fundamentally insufficient for directed higher-order brain dynamics. Directed GNNs (Rossi et al., 2024) capture edge directionality but lack higher-order structure, peaking at 60.02% on (4,325μm) versus SSN's 87.16%. Message-passing Simplicial Neural Networks (Bodnar et al., 2021b) model hierarchy but ignore directionality, reaching 54.24% on (4,325μm). The handcrafted TopoFeat+SVM pipeline (Conceição et al., 2022) achieves 35.91% on (4,325μm), limited by its fixed invariants and sampling heuristics. Deep Sets (Zaheer et al., 2017), which ignore structure entirely, perform worst at ~19–25%. Notably, in the extreme data-scarcity regime (M=1, one neighborhood per dynamic), SSNs still lead by 17+ percentage points, demonstrating strong sample efficiency from their topological inductive bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Topological Deep Learning (TDL) line of work—message-passing simplicial networks (MPSNNs), CW networks, and combinatorial complex models—by introducing the first TDL architectures explicitly designed for semi-simplicial sets, which encode directed higher-order motifs via face maps. It builds directly on Riihimäki's (2023) algebraic foundations for directed simplicial complexes and the preliminary Dir-SNNs of Lecha et al. (2025), but generalizes from a narrow class of directed cliques to the full semi-simplicial setting with a relational algebra of face-map-induced relations. The brain dynamics framework departs from the handcrafted neurotopology pipeline of Conceição et al. (2022) and Reimann et al. (2022), replacing predefined invariants and sampling heuristics with end-to-end learned representations over Dynamical Activity Complexes. The routing mechanism (R-SSNs) borrows the top-k gating idea from mixture-of-experts literature (Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022) to address scalability of the relation set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSNs establish a principled, provably more expressive framework for learning on directed higher-order structure, filling a gap identified in the TDL position paper (Papamarkou et al., 2024) where all existing models were restricted to undirected settings. The brain dynamics classification task provides a unique, biologically grounded real-world benchmark for TDL that is unlikely to be addressed by graph-only methods, and the open-source data and code make it a competitive public benchmark. Key limitations include dependence on detailed connectomic data (currently unavailable in vivo), evaluation on a single simulated brain model (NMC) with only 8 stimulus classes and T=2 time bins, and the fact that the routing mechanism, while reducing parameters, still requires the full relation set to be predefined. Open directions include unsupervised motif discovery, extension to tournaplexes and other directed structures, incorporation of signed (excitatory/inhibitory) interactions, and scaling to in vivo or larger-scale connectomes.
