## Problem

While ReLU networks are known to partition input space into polyhedral regions, and bounds on the total number of regions have been studied for over a decade, almost nothing was known about how these regions connect to one another. The paper addresses this gap by establishing the first architecture-independent bounds on the average degree and diameter of the connectivity graph (where nodes are regions and edges link regions sharing a face), properties that govern how nonlinearities propagate across the complex.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263012/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained fully-connected ReLU network (all layer weights and biases) and a single valid sign sequence (obtained by passing any input point through the network). It produces the complete polyhedral complex as a set of sign sequences, the full connectivity graph with all edges, and derived statistics including per-region neighbor counts, average degree, estimated graph diameter, and distributions of bounded vs. unbounded regions. The sign sequence representation means each region is encoded as a binary vector of length n (total hidden neurons), and the polyhedron boundaries are recovered layer-by-layer via closed-form matrix products (Eqs. 2–3) rather than explicit geometric intersection.

## Method

The theoretical core exploits the 1-1 mapping between polyhedral cells and sign sequences (from Masden, 2025) to generalize a classical hyperplane-arrangement face-counting result (Fukuda et al., 1991) to deep networks with bent hyperplanes. By categorizing cells into three types relative to a removed neuron's bent hyperplane (Lemma 3.2) and deriving a recursive cell-counting identity (Lemma 3.3), the authors prove via induction that the average degree is at most 2d and the diameter is O(m^ℓ), independent of input dimension. For enumeration, Algorithm 1 performs a BFS over sign sequences, using a linear program at each step to test whether relaxing one inequality produces a feasible point outside the current polyhedron (i.e., whether that inequality is non-redundant and defines a real face). This LP-based redundancy check, combined with a small relaxation of the constraint to handle numerical precision, allows exact traversal of the entire complex without explicitly constructing polyhedron vertices.

## Results

The paper proves that the average degree of the connectivity graph of any fully-connected ReLU network in d dimensions is upper bounded by 2d, regardless of network width or depth. Empirically, for d=4 with width 16 and depth 4, the average degree reaches 7.85 (bound: 8), and for d=5 with the same architecture it reaches 9.80 (bound: 10), confirming the bound is tight. The connectivity graph diameter is bounded above by (m+1)^ℓ and is empirically nearly independent of input dimension: for width 16, depth 4, the estimated diameter is 76.35 (d=4) vs. 70.88 (d=5). On real datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, California Housing), polyhedra containing training data consistently exhibit higher neighbor counts than the overall average, with the effect more pronounced for classification than regression tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not compare against competing methods in the standard ML sense; instead, it validates its theoretical bounds against empirical measurements. The key validation is that the proven upper bound of 2d on average connectivity graph degree is approached but never exceeded across all 12 architecture configurations tested (e.g., 7.85 vs. bound 8 for d=4, 9.80 vs. bound 10 for d=5). The diameter upper bound of (m+1)^ℓ is rarely reached in practice, but the paper's central claim—that diameter is independent of input dimension—is confirmed: for fixed width and depth, diameter estimates differ by less than 10% between d=4 and d=5. The paper also contrasts its results with prior work by Fan et al. (2024), which bounds average faces only under restrictive assumptions (no bias terms, low-rank first layer) and provides only asymptotic bounds, whereas this work gives architecture-independent, non-asymptotic bounds. On real datasets, the paper observes that data-containing polyhedra have neighbor counts exceeding the overall average, a phenomenon not previously characterized in the literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the topological framework of Masden (2025) and the bent hyperplane/genericity assumptions of Grigsby & Lindsey (2022) to study a previously unexamined aspect of ReLU network geometry: the connectivity graph whose nodes are polyhedral regions and whose edges encode shared faces. The key theoretical result (average degree ≤ 2d) generalizes a classical bound from Fukuda et al. (1991) on hyperplane arrangements to deep ReLU networks via the sign-sequence-to-cell bijection, a step the authors explicitly note does not follow from the original proof. The BFS-based enumeration algorithm builds on the traversal methods of Xu et al. (2022) and Liu et al. (2023b) but adds the step of recording the full connectivity graph during the search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These are the first architecture-independent bounds on the average connectivity and graph-theoretic diameter of ReLU network complexes, providing a new lens for problems in verification, robustness, and error prediction that previously relied on intractable exact computation or loose region-count bounds. The diameter bound being independent of input dimension d (despite exponential growth in region count) has direct implications for bounding empirical error via shortest-path metrics, as discussed in the paper's connection to Ji et al. (2022). Open questions include why training concentrates data in high-connectivity regions, how results extend to convolutional or skip-connected architectures, and whether analogous bounds hold for non-ReLU piecewise-linear activations.
