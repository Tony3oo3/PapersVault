## Problem

Parallel test-time compute (generating multiple reasoning paths and aggregating) requires the model to produce diverse yet correct solutions, but the forking tokens that trigger distinct correct reasoning modes are typically deep in the sampling tree, making temperature scaling ineffective due to a worsening diversity-accuracy trade-off. Moreover, standard SFT on multiple reasoning traces per question collapses these distinct modes into a single one, destroying the diversity needed for effective parallel scaling.

## Input / Output

At training, the method takes a text question paired with a set of M diverse reasoning traces (distilled from multiple teacher models) and N reserved global forking tokens (special tokens like <think1>…<think6> added to the vocabulary). At inference, each parallel generation is prompted with the question plus a distinct forking token, producing M parallel reasoning traces from which a final answer is extracted via majority voting (Cons@k) or a single-path heuristic/GFPO-sampled token (Pass@1). The forking tokens always appear at fixed positions immediately after the question, making them trivially sliceable for policy-gradient optimization.

## Method

SSFT (Set Supervised Fine-Tuning) frames parallel reasoning as a set-of-next-token-prediction problem by incorporating a set-based global loss into SFT via bipartite matching. At each training step, an N×M cost matrix is computed where entry (i,j) is the length-normalized NTP loss of trace j conditioned on forking token i (with stop-gradient to save VRAM); the Hungarian algorithm then finds the optimal matching σ that uniquely pairs each trace with a distinct forking token, and gradients are backpropagated only through the M matched sequences. This prevents the mode collapse observed in standard SFT, where all traces are treated as independent data points and the forking tokens become indistinguishable. A lightweight RL step, GFPO (Global Forking Policy Optimization), then applies GRPO for ~10 steps using only the policy gradients of the forking-token tokens (not full sequences) to learn which forking token to select per question, enabling Pass@1 inference without manual heuristics. The matching cost uses only the first L≈1000 tokens for efficiency, reducing matching time to O(NM/k) while backpropagation still uses full trace length.

## Results

SSFT-32B achieves the best Pass@1 accuracy of 64.06 on AIME24 and 58.13 on AIME25, outperforming the multi-target SFT baseline (SFT-mixed-distill-32B-tags) by 8.33% and 6.57% respectively. Under parallel test-time compute, SSFT-32B reaches Cons@6 = 73.94% and Cons@32 = 86.67% on AIME25, and Cons@6 = 75.45% on AIME24. Adding GFPO (10 RL steps on forking tokens only) further improves Pass@1 to 64.22/58.80 on AIME24/25 and boosts out-of-distribution LiveCodeBench Pass@1 to 42.10, the highest among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSFT-32B consistently outperforms SFT-mixed-distill-32B-tags (the strongest multi-target SFT baseline trained on the same 4 traces per question) across all Pass@1 metrics, with the largest gaps on AIME24 (+5.83) and AIME25 (+6.17), and on Cons@6 for AIME25 (+3.94). Against the single-target baselines s1.1-32B and SFT-OSS-distill-32B, SSFT-32B leads on AIME24/25 Pass@1 by 9.27/13.86 and 6.24/9.38 points respectively, while SFT-OSS-distill-32B retains a slight edge on MATH-500 Pass@1 (89.54 vs 90.02 is actually SSFT winning by 0.48). The critical ablation is SSFT-32B (random σ), which uses the same set loss but with random bipartite matching instead of the Hungarian algorithm; SSFT with optimal matching beats it by 2.29/3.03 on AIME24/25 Pass@1 and by 5.86 on AIME25 Cons@6, confirming that the optimal matching is essential to prevent reasoning-mode collapse. On the out-of-distribution LiveCodeBench, SSFT-32B-GFPO achieves 42.10 Pass@1, surpassing SFT-OSS-distill-32B (34.13) by 7.97 points. Multiverse-32B (Yang et al., 2025b), which trains on parallel CoTs decomposed from sequential ones, is outperformed by SSFT-32B on AIME24 Pass@1 (64.06 vs 53.80) and AIME25 (58.13 vs 45.80).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the set-based global loss paradigm from DETR (object detection) to autoregressive language modeling, framing parallel reasoning as a set-of-next-token-prediction problem solved via bipartite matching (Hungarian algorithm) between reserved 'global forking tokens' and diverse reasoning traces. It departs from standard SFT on multiple reasoning traces—which the paper shows collapses distinct reasoning modes into one—by enforcing permutation-invariance and unique token-to-trace assignment through the matching objective. The lightweight RL step (GFPO) builds on GRPO/RLVR but restricts policy gradients to only the forking-token positions, making it a minimal, targeted refinement rather than full-sequence optimization. The work is positioned against temperature-based diversity (which suffers a diversity-accuracy trade-off) and sequential test-time scaling (which suffers overthinking), and is concurrent with Parathinker (Wen et al., 2025) which trains on multiple distilled traces but without the set-based matching loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, low-overhead training recipe (SSFT) that preserves distinct reasoning modes during SFT and produces emergent global forking tokens that can steer diverse yet accurate reasoning at inference time, offering an alternative to temperature scaling for parallel test-time compute. The mode-collapse finding under standard multi-trace SFT is a useful diagnostic for practitioners fine-tuning on diverse distillation targets. Limitations include reliance on multiple diverse traces per question (typically 4), evaluation primarily at 32B scale with small training sets (1k questions), and the bipartite graph size (N=6, M=4) remaining modest; the authors note plans to scale up the graph and explore settings beyond multi-teacher distillation. Whether the learned forking-token associations generalize to substantially larger N, M, or to open-ended domains without verifiable rewards remains an open question.
