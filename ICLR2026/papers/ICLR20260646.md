## Problem

Existing non-stationary RL methods model task evolution via Markov chains, Gaussian Processes, or reward-based detection, all of which assume regular or smoothly varying periods. This limits adaptability in realistic settings where task changes occur at stochastic, irregular intervals. The paper asks whether multi-scale frequency decomposition (wavelet analysis) can capture the heterogeneous temporal structure of non-stationary task sequences more faithfully than these single-scale or parametric approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sliding window of trajectory transitions (s, a, s', r) as context, which the MLP context encoder maps to a 5-dimensional task representation sequence z. The wavelet representation network then applies M levels of discrete wavelet decomposition (M=2–5 depending on task) via dilated causal convolutions, producing a reconstructed time-domain wavelet task representation ẑ that feeds into a SAC policy. The final output is a continuous or discrete action conditioned on (s, ẑ), with the wavelet coefficients (approximation u_M and selected detail g̃_m) serving as intermediate structural features.

## Method

WISDOM decomposes the problem into three modules: (A) a PEARL-style context encoder trained with KL-divergence to produce task representations z; (B) a trainable wavelet representation network Y_φ that performs DWT using dilated causal convolutions with learnable low-pass/high-pass filters (initialized as Haar wavelets), iteratively separating z into low-frequency approximation coefficients (global trends) and high-frequency detail coefficients (local changes), then reconstructing a denoised time-domain representation ẑ; and (C) a SAC policy conditioned on ẑ. The key training innovation is a wavelet TD update operator F W(z_t) = z_t + Γ E_π[W(z_{t+1})] applied to the low-pass (Conv2) branch, proven to be a contraction mapping (Theorem 1), which explicitly updates wavelet features without relying on reward signals. This is complemented by an auto-regressive loss that enforces temporal consistency and long-range dependencies. Theorems 2 and 3 further establish that wavelet-domain features bound policy performance differences and that the restored ẑ yields a policy that improves over intermediate iterates.

## Results

On Meta-World (8 robotic manipulation tasks), WISDOM achieves an average success rate of ~97.2%, with near-perfect scores on Faucet-Close (99.92%), Button-Press (99.42%), Door-Lock (99.67%), Door-Close (100%), and Handle-Press (100%). The best baseline, COREP, reaches only ~83.5% average, with particularly weak performance on Plate-Slide-Back (43.0%) and Door-Unlock (67.5%). On MuJoCo and Type-1 Diabetes benchmarks, WISDOM similarly demonstrates the fastest convergence and highest asymptotic return across all non-stationary degrees tested (0.7–0.99).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WISDOM decisively outperforms all seven baselines on Meta-World, with the largest gaps over CEMRL (~90% improvement in average success rate) and TRIO (~85% improvement), both of which struggle with complex non-stationary task distributions. Against the strongest NSRL baseline COREP (causal-graph based), WISDOM gains ~14 percentage points on average, with the most dramatic improvement on Plate-Slide-Back (90.57% vs. 43.00%) and Door-Unlock (91.58% vs. 67.50%). SeCBAD (reward-function based) and PEARL (classical meta-RL) achieve moderate performance (~66–69% average) but exhibit high variance and poor adaptability on tasks with 3D target position changes. The standard SAC baseline shows that without explicit task evolution modeling, the policy cannot reliably adapt to non-stationary dynamics, while RL² (RNN-based meta-RL) performs comparably to PEARL, confirming the importance of explicit wavelet-domain task tracking. On MuJoCo and Type-1 Diabetes, WISDOM maintains its lead with faster convergence and higher final returns, and ablations confirm that the wavelet TD loss stabilizes training while the AR loss accelerates convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

WISDOM extends the non-stationary RL (NSRL) line of work that models task evolution through history-dependent stochastic processes (Xie et al. 2021, Poiani et al. 2021, Chen et al. 2022), but departs from prior methods that rely on Markov chains, Gaussian Processes, reward-based detection, or causal graphs by instead transforming task representation sequences into the wavelet domain via a learnable discrete wavelet transform network. The wavelet TD update operator draws on the Bellman-equation structure of successor features (Barreto et al. 2017) but applies it explicitly to wavelet-domain features rather than implicitly through value functions, and the auto-regressive loss complements it in the spirit of time-series forecasting architectures (Oord 2016, Shi et al. 2023). The method is built on top of SAC (Haarnoja et al. 2018) and a standard context encoder (Rakelly et al. 2019), positioning the wavelet representation network as a plug-in module between task inference and policy learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WISDOM addresses a practical gap in NSRL: existing methods assume regular or fixed-period task evolution, while real-world non-stationary tasks (weather, traffic, physiological signals) exhibit stochastic and time-varying periods. The wavelet-domain formulation naturally captures multi-scale temporal patterns without requiring prior knowledge of evolution periods, and the theoretical guarantees (contraction mapping for the TD operator, policy improvement bound) add rigor beyond purely empirical NSRL methods. Key limitations include the need to manually tune the decomposition level M per environment and the relatively narrow set of non-stationary degrees tested; open questions include adaptive decomposition, principled detail-coefficient selection, and whether the approach extends to high-dimensional or partially observable settings.
