## Problem

Diffusion models for time series anomaly detection suffer from inherent stochasticity: starting the reverse process from pure Gaussian noise yields multiple plausible reconstructions, contradicting the input-consistent reconstruction that reconstruction-based TSAD requires and inflating false positive rates. Existing conditional diffusion approaches (e.g., DiffAD, ImDiffusion) mitigate this by masking segments of the input, but risk conditioning on anomalous points, propagating errors through the reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series window X ∈ R^{L×K} (sliding window of 1024 timesteps) and reformulates it into a 2D matrix Z ∈ R^{L×K̂} via channel-wise concatenation to enable cross-variate learning through convolutional filters. At inference, the test instance is decomposed via a zero-phase Gaussian low-pass filter into a low-frequency signal component and a high-frequency residual; the anomaly score is the L2 norm between the original window and its implicitly-guided reconstruction, yielding a per-timestamp score vector y ∈ R^L.

## Method

ICDiffAD introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, an SNR Scheduler reparameterizes the DDPM noise curriculum by a single target signal-to-noise ratio (TSNR_dB), deriving per-step corruption factors α_t via a temporal schedule g(t) so that cumulative signal energy decay exactly matches the target, eliminating heuristic tuning of β_min, β_max, and T. Second, an SNR Implicit Conditioning (SIC) module operates at inference: it computes an instance-specific ISNR from the Gaussian-filtered decomposition of the test input, derives an optimal corruption factor α* (Eq. 13) from ISNR and the input's mean/variance, and projects α* onto the nearest pretrained diffusion step T̂. The reverse process then starts from the partially corrupted input Z_{T̂} = √(α_{T̂})·Z_test + √(1−α_{T̂})·ε rather than pure noise, producing an input-consistent reconstruction that suppresses anomalous high-frequency components while preserving normative temporal trends.

## Results

ICDiffAD achieves an average F1 score of 43.81% across five multivariate time series benchmarks (MSL, SMAP, SMD, PSM, SWaT), outperforming the best prior diffusion-based method DiffAD (24.24%) by +19.57% and the best reconstruction-based method SARAD (38.71%) by +5.1%. On the PSM dataset, ICDiffAD reaches 56.87% F1, an 8.27-point absolute gain over SARAD. The method also reduces false positives by 60.23% compared to DiffAD while maintaining competitive recall, and achieves 75.78% AUC-ROC on MSL (vs. 52.37% for DiffAD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICDiffAD decisively outperforms both diffusion-based baselines: it improves F1 by +19.57% over DiffAD (43.81 vs. 24.24) and by +34.06% over ImDiffusion (43.81 vs. 9.75), with the largest per-dataset gains on MSL (+14.11 pts) and PSM (+13.39 pts). Against the strongest reconstruction-based method SARAD (38.71% avg F1), ICDiffAD leads by +5.1% on average, winning on MSL (+13.93), SMAP (+5.33), and PSM (+8.27), but trailing slightly on SMD (23.68 vs. 24.10) and SWaT (77.34 vs. 78.92). Among classical methods, CBLOF (38.98%) is the closest competitor, yet ICDiffAD still leads by +4.83% on average. Notably, ICDiffAD reduces false positives by 60.23% relative to DiffAD (confirmed via confusion matrices in Appendix 8.4.1), indicating that the SIC mechanism's input-consistent generation substantially suppresses spurious anomaly scores. On the AUC-ROC metric, ICDiffAD achieves 71.93% average (vs. 48.48% for DiffAD and 65.16% for SARAD), further validating its ranking quality across thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICDiffAD extends the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) to time series anomaly detection by reparameterizing the noise schedule with a physically interpretable SNR metric and introducing an implicit conditioning mechanism that initializes the reverse diffusion from a partially corrupted input rather than pure Gaussian noise. It builds on DiffAD (Xiao et al., 2023) for the dimensional transformation and the general diffusion-based TSAD paradigm, but departs from both DiffAD and ImDiffusion by rejecting the mask-then-impute strategy in favor of SNR-guided spectral decomposition (Gaussian low-pass filtering) to determine how much corruption to apply per input. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the 'generate from noise' assumption of vanilla diffusion, arguing that reconstruction-based TSAD requires input-consistent generation, which is achieved by conditioning on the input's own signal-to-noise ratio.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SNR reparameterization replaces three independently tuned hyperparameters (β_min, β_max, T) with a single TSNR_dB, simplifying the diffusion-based TSAD pipeline and making noise levels physically interpretable for time series practitioners. The 60% reduction in false positives over DiffAD is practically significant for operational monitoring where alert fatigue is a major concern. However, the method still underperforms classical methods (IF, PCA) on SWaT and shows modest gains on SMD, indicating that rapid distributional shifts and regime changes remain unresolved challenges for diffusion-based TSAD. Open questions include whether the SIC mechanism generalizes to streaming/online settings and whether the Gaussian low-pass filter assumption holds for highly non-stationary or multi-scale anomaly patterns.
