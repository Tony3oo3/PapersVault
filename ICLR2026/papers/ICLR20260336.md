## Problem

Current iSTFT-based vocoders predict complex-valued spectrograms but process them with real-valued networks that treat the real and imaginary parts as independent channels, preventing the model from capturing the inherent coupling between magnitude and phase. This paper investigates whether native complex-valued neural networks (CVNNs) can better exploit the algebraic structure of complex spectrograms to improve waveform synthesis quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260336/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 100-bin log-Mel-spectrogram (24 kHz, FFT 1024, hop 256) as input, which is cast into the complex domain by initializing the imaginary part to zero. It outputs a 24 kHz audio waveform via iSTFT (FFT 1024, hop 256, Hann window) on the predicted complex STFT coefficients, avoiding any learned upsampling or autoregressive generation. The generator is a feed-forward ConvNeXt-style network operating entirely in the complex domain, so each spectrogram coefficient is processed as a unified complex entity rather than two separate real channels.

## Method

ComVo builds a GAN vocoder where both the generator (adapted from Vocos with complex ConvNeXt blocks and split GELU) and a novel complex multi-resolution discriminator (cMRD) operate natively in the complex domain, establishing a complex-domain adversarial training loop. A phase quantization layer, inserted after the first complex convolution, discretizes phase angles into 128 levels using a straight-through estimator, acting as a structured inductive bias that regularizes phase learning. A real-valued multi-period discriminator (MPD) is retained for waveform-level periodicity supervision. For efficiency, all complex linear and convolutional operations are reformulated as 2×2 block-matrix multiplications over real tensors, replacing four independent real-valued multiplies with a single fused operation and reducing training time by 25% while preserving numerical equivalence to standard complex arithmetic.

## Results

On LibriTTS, ComVo achieves the best objective scores across all metrics: UTMOS 3.69, MR-STFT 0.844, PESQ 3.82, periodicity RMSE 0.090, and V/UV F1 0.961, outperforming the strongest baseline Vocos (UTMOS 3.60, MR-STFT 0.886, PESQ 3.63). Subjectively, ComVo attains a MOS of 4.07 (nearly matching ground-truth 4.08) and a CMOS of 0, while the closest competitor Vocos scores −0.02. On the out-of-distribution MUSDB18-HQ dataset, ComVo again leads all objective metrics (MR-STFT 0.878, PESQ 3.52, periodicity 0.130, V/UV F1 0.938). In a TTS pipeline with Matcha-TTS, ComVo achieves the highest UTMOS (3.44) and a CMOS of 0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ComVo outperforms all four main baselines on every objective metric in Table 2. Against Vocos (the closest iSTFT-based competitor), ComVo improves UTMOS by 0.09 (3.69 vs. 3.60), reduces MR-STFT error by 4.7% (0.844 vs. 0.886), and raises PESQ by 0.20 (3.82 vs. 3.63). Against BigVGAN, the gains are larger: UTMOS +0.17, MR-STFT −6.1%, PESQ +0.21. Against HiFi-GAN and iSTFTNet (upsampling-based and earlier iSTFT-based models respectively), ComVo leads by substantial margins (e.g., PESQ 3.82 vs. 2.94 and 2.81). Subjectively, ComVo's MOS of 4.07 is statistically indistinguishable from BigVGAN and Vocos (both 4.05) but its CMOS of 0 indicates listeners do not prefer any baseline over it, whereas all baselines receive negative CMOS scores. On the out-of-distribution MUSDB18-HQ dataset, ComVo extends its lead across all objective metrics, and in the TTS pipeline it matches BigVGAN's MOS (3.92) while achieving the highest UTMOS (3.44 vs. 3.30). Against amplitude-phase prediction vocoders (APNet, APNet2, FreeV), ComVo dominates by wide margins (e.g., UTMOS 3.69 vs. 2.40–2.74), confirming that joint complex-domain modeling surpasses separate magnitude/phase regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iSTFT-based vocoder lineage (iSTFTNet, APNet, Vocos) by replacing the standard real-valued network that processes real and imaginary spectrogram channels independently with native complex-valued neural networks in both the generator and discriminator. The generator architecture is adapted from Vocos (ConvNeXt-style blocks with iSTFT synthesis), while the multi-period discriminator follows HiFi-GAN's design. The key departure is the introduction of a complex multi-resolution discriminator (cMRD) that operates directly on complex spectrograms, establishing the first fully complex-domain adversarial training framework for waveform generation. The phase quantization layer draws on complex activation ideas from MRI reconstruction, and the block-matrix computation scheme addresses a practical efficiency gap in existing CVNN implementations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that complex-domain modeling yields quality gains that exceed those obtainable by simply doubling real-valued parameters at matched memory cost, suggesting that the structural advantage of jointly modeling real-imaginary coupling is not merely a capacity effect. The block-matrix formulation offers a practical 25% training-time reduction that could lower the barrier to adopting CVNNs in audio. However, limitations remain: the system is restricted to single-GPU training due to under-optimized multi-GPU support for complex parameters, uses split-style losses and activations that partially undermine the complex-domain philosophy, and the memory footprint is roughly doubled. Open questions include whether richer complex-domain losses (beyond component-wise hinge) and non-split activations could further close the gap, and whether the approach extends to diffusion or flow-matching vocoder paradigms.
