## Problem

Existing hallucination detection methods for LLMs rely on handcrafted heuristics or shallow classifiers applied to isolated computational traces—either activations or attention maps—without a unified framework that jointly exploits these complementary signals. The field lacks a principled, learnable architecture operating on structured representations of the full computational trace, leaving detection to single-signal, heuristic approaches that cannot flexibly integrate heterogeneous information or adapt across hallucination types.

## Input / Output

CHARM consumes attention matrices (across all layers and heads) and residual stream activations from a decoder-only LLM's forward pass on a prompt-response pair, converting them into a directed attributed graph where tokens are nodes, edges follow causal attention flows (i > j), node features encode self-attention scores and optional activation vectors, and edge features encode cross-token attention scores. A threshold τ sparsifies the graph by zeroing attention values below τ and dropping unsupported edges. The output is either a per-token hallucination score (node classification, f_pool = identity) or a single response-level score (graph classification, f_pool = mean/sum), supporting both granularities within the same architecture.

## Method

CHARM parameterises hallucination detection as a message-passing GNN over computational trace graphs, structured as f = f_pred ∘ f_pool ∘ f_mp. Each message-passing layer updates token embeddings by aggregating MLP-computed messages over the attention-defined neighbourhood, where messages condition on both endpoint node features, edge attention features, and a one-hot prompt/response indicator that lets the network route prompt- and response-originated signals into separate subspaces. The framework provably subsumes prior heuristics: a single-layer CHARM can approximate Lookback Lens token scores (by summing attention to prompt vs. response tokens and computing their ratio) and LLM-Check global scores (by log-transforming self-attention node features and pooling), establishing expressiveness guarantees. Graph sparsification via thresholding τ trades a small accuracy drop for dramatic reductions in edge count, memory, and inference time (≈10⁻³ s), while ablations confirm that the attention-induced graph structure itself—rather than just the node features—contributes meaningfully to detection performance.

## Results

On token-level contextual hallucination detection (NQ, CNN), CHARM(att) achieves 74.8% AUROC / 40.3% AUPR on NQ and 75.4% AUROC / 22.7% AUPR on CNN, outperforming the best attention-based heuristic (Lookback Lens†) by ~3 AUROC and ~6 AUPR points. On response-level detection across diverse hallucination types, CHARM(att+act-24) reaches 79.7% AUROC / 91.8% AUPR on Movies, 77.8% AUROC / 39.8% AUPR on WinoBias, and 80.8% AUROC / 83.1% AUPR on Math, consistently surpassing all activation-probe and attention-heuristic baselines. The method also demonstrates robust zero-shot cross-dataset transfer (e.g., 65.5% AUROC / 31.6% AUPR when trained on CNN and tested on NQ) and maintains performance under aggressive graph sparsification (τ=0.5 retains 38.4% AUPR vs. 40.3% at τ=0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHARM consistently outperforms all six baseline families across the five evaluation datasets. Against probability-based detectors (Probas / P(True) [Kadavath et al.]), CHARM gains 15–26 AUROC points, confirming that internal computational traces are far more informative than next-token confidence. Against activation probes (Act-24/28/32 [Orgad et al.]), CHARM(att) wins on NQ and CNN (74.8 vs. 73.0 AUROC on NQ), while CHARM(att+act-24) dominates on WinoBias (77.8 vs. 76.6 AUROC) and Math (80.8 vs. 78.1 AUROC), showing that combining attention and activation signals in a graph framework yields complementary gains. Against the attention-based Lookback Lens heuristic [Chuang et al.], CHARM(att) improves by 3–4 AUROC and 5–6 AUPR points on both NQ and CNN, validating the expressiveness advantage of learnable message-passing over fixed handcrafted features. Against LLM-Check [Sriramanan et al.] and its enhanced variant, CHARM gains 8–39 AUROC points (e.g., 77.8 vs. 67.2 on WinoBias), and against the spectral LapEig method [Binkowski et al.], it leads by 3–14 AUROC points. Finally, against Semantic Entropy [Kuhn et al.] (which requires 10 additional generations and ~5.9 s per prediction), CHARM achieves 80.3% vs. 70.1% AUC on Movies while running in ~10⁻⁴ seconds, demonstrating both superior accuracy and orders-of-magnitude lower latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHARM unifies two previously separate lines of hallucination detection—attention-based heuristics (Lookback Lens, LLM-Check, spectral methods) and activation probing—by representing both as features on an attributed graph whose topology is induced by attention scores. Architecturally, it instantiates a standard message-passing GNN (Gilmer et al., 2017; Kipf & Welling, 2017) over these graphs, extending the descriptive attention-graph analyses of Barbero et al. (2024) from structural characterization to a learnable detection framework. The paper proves that Lookback Lens and LLM-Check emerge as special cases of CHARM's single-layer message-passing, positioning the method as a strict generalization rather than an unrelated alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified graph formulation offers a principled path to integrating additional computational traces (logits, multi-layer activations) and to handling both token-level and response-level detection within one architecture, something prior heuristic methods could not do simultaneously. The practical efficiency gains from graph sparsification (~1 ms inference, orders of magnitude faster than multi-prompting baselines) make the approach viable for real-time deployment. Open questions remain: zero-shot cross-dataset transfer is still unreliable, the interaction between attention and activation features is dataset-dependent (helpful for non-contextual, harmful for contextual hallucinations), and whether the framework extends to other LLM safety tasks (data contamination, jailbreak detection) is untested.
