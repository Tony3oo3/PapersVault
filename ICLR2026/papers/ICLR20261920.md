## Problem

Transformers' key-value memory lacks inherent spatial priors, constraining their capacity for spatial reasoning and long-horizon generalization. Existing hippocampus-inspired models like the Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine (TEM) and its transformer variant (TEM-t) suffer from computationally expensive outer-product operations or context-length bottlenecks, limiting their scalability and practical integration into modern deep learning frameworks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261920/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

In 2D grid tasks, the model consumes interleaved one-hot action vectors and discrete sensory observations (sampled from 64 objects) over sequences of 128–256 steps in 9×9 to 11×11 environments; in 3D tasks, it takes egocentric view images (encoded via a 4-layer CNN) concatenated with discrete actions over 64-step sequences in a 9×9 environment with 12 orientation bins. The model outputs the next sensory observation (one-step prediction) and multi-step future observations (imagination up to 512 steps). In 2D, predictions are only scored at previously visited locations since observations are random; in 3D, performance is measured by pixel-wise MSE split into visible and non-visible frames.

## Method

mm-TEM replaces TEM's tensor-product Hebbian weights with a meta-MLP relational memory using hierarchical fast weights (inspired by Titans), where projection matrices are metatrained in an outer loop while MLP connection weights are updated online via prediction-error-driven gradient steps with data-dependent forgetting gates. A two-layer MLP path integration network maps actions to transformation matrices producing structural codes, and three auxiliary relational losses (structure-from-content, structure-from-structure, content-from-structure) enforce bidirectional binding between structural and sensory domains. A feedback loop from the relational memory corrects path integration drift, and the memory update frequency hyperparameter mb implicitly sets the prediction horizon, which emergently controls grid-cell scale. Hippoformer integrates mm-TEM with a one-layer Transformer (window size 32) in parallel: the Transformer provides precise short-term key-value memory while mm-TEM supplies structured long-horizon spatial memory, with outputs concatenated and fused by an MLP.

## Results

On the 3D environment prediction task (Table 1), Hippoformer achieves the lowest multi-step imagination error of 9.71×10⁻³ (full sequence), 2.72×10⁻³ (visible frames), and 10.27×10⁻³ (non-visible frames), substantially outperforming Transformer (36.13/11.49/38.07) and Titans (33.42/10.60/35.21). For 1-step prediction, Hippoformer matches or slightly edges out Transformer and Titans (1.27 vs 1.29/1.32 full error). In 2D grid prediction, mm-TEM reaches ~90% accuracy within 5,000 gradient steps versus TEM's ~60% at 20,000 steps, and retains 40% accuracy at 4096-step context where Transformer and Titans collapse to near-zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Transformer (3-layer, 128-step window), Hippoformer wins decisively on multi-step imagination in 3D (9.71 vs 36.13 full error, a ~3.7× reduction) and on long-context 2D prediction (maintaining 40% accuracy at 4096 steps vs near-zero for Transformer). Against Titans (3-layer MAC), Hippoformer similarly dominates multi-step 3D prediction (9.71 vs 33.42) and long-horizon 2D generalization. Against the original TEM, mm-TEM converges ~4× faster (90% at 5K steps vs 60% at 20K steps) and generalizes far better on multi-step imagination. Against TEM-t, mm-TEM achieves higher multi-step accuracy with substantially lower wall-clock training time (Fig. A.6). Notably, standalone mm-TEM with mb=8 sacrifices 1-step accuracy (5.10 vs 1.27 for Hippoformer), but the Transformer component in Hippoformer compensates for this, yielding the best combined performance across both short- and long-horizon settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine (TEM) lineage by replacing its computationally expensive tensor-product Hebbian relational memory with a meta-MLP fast-weight system inspired by Titans, yielding mm-TEM. It then departs from both TEM-t (which inherits transformer window-size constraints) and standard Transformers (which lack spatial structure) by proposing Hippoformer, a hybrid that runs a one-layer Transformer in parallel with mm-TEM to combine precise short-term key-value memory with structured long-horizon spatial memory. The path-integration module follows Gao et al. (2021), and the 3D evaluation builds on the MemoryMaze3D benchmark (Pasukonis et al., 2022), but the core contribution is the architectural integration and the emergent grid-scale mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete, scalable recipe for embedding hippocampal-inspired structural spatial memory into transformer-based architectures, addressing a gap the authors identify as a key open challenge for spatial reasoning in deep learning. The emergent link between memory-update frequency (prediction horizon) and grid-cell scale provides a testable mechanistic account of grid-scale diversity in MEC that prior theoretical work (Waniek, Stachenfeld) only posited analytically. However, the current Hippoformer is limited to a single-layer Transformer with a simple parallel concatenation, the 3D environment is a simplified empty plane, and no multi-layer scaling or real-world navigation tasks are explored—so the practical path to foundation-model integration remains open.
