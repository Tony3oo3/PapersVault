## Problem

Existing two-stage LoRA-based motion transfer methods applied to video Diffusion Transformers suffer from motion inconsistency (spatial and temporal attention heads are jointly updated, coupling appearance with dynamics) and tuning inefficiency (all heads are tuned indiscriminately and all frames are processed, ignoring the latent interpolation capacity of 3D VAEs). Additionally, no dedicated benchmark exists for systematically evaluating video motion transfer across diverse motion types.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference video (~5 s, 150 frames) providing the motion to transfer and a text prompt describing the desired target appearance, producing a generated video (evaluated at 32 frames, 512×512) that replicates the reference motion while following the prompt. During temporal tuning, the reference video is sparsely sampled to 17 frames (stride 5) to reduce sequence length from ~24,276 tokens to a manageable size, while inference still uses the full 81-frame sequence. The base model is the 14B-parameter WAN-2.1 video diffusion transformer with flow matching.

## Method

EffiVMT is a three-stage framework that decouples spatial and temporal adaptation in 3D attention. Stage 1 classifies each attention head as spatial or temporal by computing cosine similarity between the head's actual attention map and pseudo ground-truth maps (diagonal for spatial, parallel-diagonal for temporal), using a threshold α=1.25; the q/k/v/o channels are then rearranged into two parallel branches. Stage 2 tunes spatial-branch LoRAs (rank 128) on a single randomly sampled frame as a text-to-image task for ~3,000 steps. Stage 3 freezes spatial LoRAs and tunes temporal-branch LoRAs on a sparsely sampled 17-frame video for 2,000 steps, using an adaptive RoPE that rescales frame indices to the original [0, F] range so the model can infer correct positional relationships from fewer frames, plus a motion loss (negative cosine similarity on frame-difference latents) to further decouple motion from appearance. This yields a 3.89× speedup over full-frame tuning while matching or exceeding motion fidelity.

## Results

On MotionBench (200 videos, 32 frames, 512×512), EffiVMT achieves the best scores across all metrics: Motion Fidelity 0.971, Temporal Consistency 0.976, Text Similarity 0.380, and tuning time of 727 s. In the user study (20 raters, 7 methods ranked 1–7), EffiVMT obtains the best overall rank of 1.132, compared to 2.216 for the next-best tuning-based method (MotionDirector) and 3.284 for the best training-free method (DiTFlow). On the DAVIS dataset (50 videos), EffiVMT similarly leads with Motion FID 0.883 and Temporal Consistency 0.936.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EffiVMT outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest tuning-based competitor MotionDirector, EffiVMT improves Motion Fidelity by 0.075 (0.971 vs 0.896), Temporal Consistency by 0.037 (0.976 vs 0.939), and reduces tuning time from 3008 s to 727 s (≈4.1× faster). Against the best training-free method DiTFlow, EffiVMT gains 0.164 in Motion Fidelity (0.971 vs 0.807) and 0.035 in Temporal Consistency (0.976 vs 0.941) while matching its inference time (727 s vs 712 s). SMM leads among training-free methods on Motion Fidelity (0.932) but still trails EffiVMT by 0.039. In the user study, EffiVMT achieves rank 1.132 overall versus 2.216 for MotionDirector and 3.284 for DiTFlow, confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality. On the DAVIS dataset, EffiVMT extends its lead with Motion FID 0.883 vs MotionDirector's 0.727 and DiTFlow's 0.680, demonstrating generalization beyond MotionBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage spatial-temporal decoupled LoRA tuning paradigm from Abdal et al. (2025) and MotionDirector (Zhao et al., 2023b) from UNet-based video diffusion models to the DiT architecture, where 3D full self-attention entangles spatial and temporal processing. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a pseudo-label-based attention head classification (Stage 1) that identifies which heads in the unified 3D attention are spatial vs. temporal, enabling truly decoupled tuning rather than the naive joint tuning of all heads. The efficiency mechanisms—sparse motion sampling and adaptive RoPE—are novel contributions that address the computational bottleneck of fine-tuning on full-length video sequences in DiTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As video DiTs (WAN, HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX) become the dominant architecture for video generation, this work provides the first practical recipe for motion transfer on these models, filling a gap where prior tuning-based methods were limited to UNet backbones. The introduction of MotionBench (200 videos across four motion categories) addresses a clear lack of standardized evaluation in the field. Open questions include whether the head classification generalizes across DiT architectures with different attention designs (e.g., CogVideoX's expert attention), whether the approach scales to longer videos or higher resolutions, and whether the sparse sampling strategy remains effective as base models improve their temporal interpolation.
