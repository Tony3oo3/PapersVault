## Problem

In linear off-policy evaluation under the minimal realizability assumption (Q^π is linear in features), the appropriate coverage parameter remains unclear and fragmented. The natural candidate 1/σ_min(A) lacks scale invariance, has no meaningful off-policy characterization, and cannot recover known coverage notions from related settings such as state abstraction or Bellman-complete linear OPE, leaving a gap between the minimal setting and the rest of the RL theory literature.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes an offline dataset of n i.i.d. tuples (s, a, r, s', a') sampled from a behavior policy, a fixed feature map φ: S×A → R^d with bounded norm, a target policy π, and a known initial state distribution μ₀. It produces a return estimate J(π) via the LSTDQ plug-in estimator θ̂ = Â⁻¹b̂, accompanied by a finite-sample error bound whose leading term is governed by the feature-dynamics coverage C_φ^π = (1−γ)²φ₀ᵀA⁻¹ΣA⁻ᵀφ₀, where φ₀ = E_{μ₀,π}[φ(s,a)] is the expected initial feature. The bound is 'directional'—it depends on the specific φ₀ rather than holding uniformly over all directions—yielding dimension-free rates in the main 1/√n term.

## Method

The paper re-analyzes LSTDQ through an instrumental-variable lens: when γ>0, the LSTDQ normal equation E[ZXᵀ]θ* = E[ZY] becomes an IV problem because the observed regressor X = φ(s,a) − γφ(s',a') contains zero-mean noise relative to the expected TD feature, and Z = φ(s,a) serves as a valid instrument. This IV perspective yields a new coverage parameter C_φ^π that is invariant to feature rescaling and admits a natural interpretation as linear coverage in a deterministic feature-dynamics system with transition matrix B^π = (Σ⁻¹Σ_cr)ᵀ, where the feature occupancy μ_φ^π = (1−γ)Σ_t γ^t (B^π)^t φ₀ replaces the true MDP occupancy. Under Bellman completeness, B^π becomes an exact next-feature predictor and C_φ^π recovers the standard linear coverage (φ^π)ᵀΣ⁻¹φ^π; under state-abstraction features it recovers the χ² version of aggregated concentrability; and in the tabular case it reduces to the χ²-divergence between target and data occupancies, thereby unifying previously disconnected coverage notions across the literature.

## Results

The paper establishes that LSTDQ's return estimation error scales as O(V_max/(1-γ) · √(C_φ^π · log(1/δ)/n)) (Theorem 1), where C_φ^π is the proposed feature-dynamics coverage parameter. This bound is dimension-free in the leading 1/√n term (no dependence on d), matching the tight rate for linear regression in the γ=0 special case. Under Bellman completeness (Proposition 5), C_φ^π exactly recovers the standard linear coverage C_lin^π = (φ^π)ᵀΣ⁻¹φ^π, and under state abstraction features (Proposition 4), it recovers the χ² version of aggregated concentrability. Proposition 3 further shows that √C_φ^π ≤ (1-γ)·‖φ₀‖_{Σ⁻¹}/σ_min(I-γΣ⁻¹/²Σ_crΣ⁻¹/²), with the gap between the two sides being arbitrarily large, demonstrating instance-dependent sharpness over prior bounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's main comparison is against Perdomo et al. (2023), whose return-estimation guarantee carries a multiplicative factor 1/σ_min(I-γΣ⁻¹/²Σ_crΣ⁻¹/²) in front of the statistical error. Proposition 3 shows that √C_φ^π is always ≤ this factor (times ‖φ₀‖_{Σ⁻¹}), and constructs an explicit instance where the gap is 1/ε → ∞, meaning the paper's bound can be arbitrarily tighter. Against the naive coverage candidate 1/σ_min(A), the paper demonstrates three fundamental deficiencies: lack of scale invariance, lack of off-policy characterization, and inability to recover known coverage parameters in special cases. Compared to BRM (Antos et al., 2008; Xie et al., 2021), the paper's Theorem 1 is dimension-free (no log|F| ≈ d factor) while BRM's bound (Eq. 17) retains this dependence, making the paper's guarantee strictly sharper in the linear setting. The paper also shows that MWL (Uehara et al., 2020) with Bernstein's inequality yields the same C_φ^π as its coverage parameter (E_{μ^D}[w*(s,a)²] = C_φ^π), confirming the unification across algorithmic families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-analyzes LSTDQ (Boyan, 1999) through an instrumental-variable lens inherited from Bradtke & Barto (1996) and Chen et al. (2022), extending the clean linear-regression analysis of the γ=0 (contextual bandit) case to the general discounted setting. It departs from prior LSTDQ analyses (Perdomo et al., 2023; Mou et al., 2022) that either required Bellman completeness or produced obscure, hard-to-interpret coverage conditions, by introducing a single interpretable parameter—feature-dynamics coverage—that works under mere realizability. The paper positions itself as a unifying bridge: it shows that the χ² density-ratio coverage in tabular MDPs, aggregated concentrability for state abstractions (Jia et al., 2024), and standard linear coverage under Bellman completeness (Zanette et al., 2021) are all special cases of this one quantity, resolving a fragmentation that the authors identify as a gap in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification is the main practical contribution for RL theory: it gives practitioners a single, interpretable diagnostic (coverage in a feature-induced linear dynamical system) that simultaneously explains when LSTDQ is tractable and how it relates to model-based, abstraction-based, and importance-sampling approaches. The paper also identifies concrete limitations: the population bound (Theorem 1) still carries a lower-order dependence on 1/σ_min(A), and the empirical bound (Theorem 2) pays a √d factor for uniformity over initial distributions. Open questions include whether the directional (dimension-free) bound can be extended to non-linear function classes, whether the IV-based analysis sharpens further via leverage-score-type conditions, and whether the feature-dynamics coverage admits efficient empirical estimation in high-dimensional settings.
