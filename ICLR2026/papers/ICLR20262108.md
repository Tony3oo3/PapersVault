## Problem

Existing federated optimization metrics treat all communication rounds as equally costly, yet different client-selection strategies (random sampling vs. targeted full synchronization) incur very different practical costs due to client reliability, network conditions, and device heterogeneity. This makes it unclear whether SARAH-based methods (which exploit dissimilarity δ but require periodic full synchronization) are truly more efficient than SAGA-based methods (which avoid full sync but depend on the larger individual smoothness constant L_max). The paper addresses this by introducing a cost-aware model and designing an algorithm that achieves the best-known communication and local complexities for non-convex federated optimization.

## Input / Output

The server coordinates n clients, each holding a local differentiable function f_i accessible only through a first-order oracle, and can communicate with at most m clients per round. Three client-selection strategies are distinguished with non-uniform costs: arbitrary selection (cost C_A), random selection (cost C_R), and delegated selection to a fixed reliable client (cost 1), with 1 ≤ C_R ≤ C_A. The algorithm outputs an ε-stationary point x̂ (uniformly sampled from the iterate sequence) after a number of communication rounds and local oracle queries whose costs are weighted by the strategy used, enabling fair comparison across methods with fundamentally different synchronization patterns.

## Method

The core algorithm is I-CGM-RG-SAGA, built on three key components. First, the Inexact Composite Gradient Method (I-CGM) decomposes f = f_1 + h_1 and solves each subproblem inexactly by running local CGM for a random number of steps drawn from a geometric distribution, achieving O(L_1/λ) expected local work per iteration instead of O(L_1 T/λ). Second, a new variance bound for the SAGA estimator is derived that depends only on the average dissimilarity constant δ rather than individual smoothness L_max, showing SAGA can exploit functional similarity. Third, the Recursive Gradient (RG) technique is introduced as a general wrapper that improves the error bound of any conditionally unbiased estimator (including SAGA and SVRG) by a factor of n_m or 1/p_B respectively, yielding RG-SAGA and RG-SVRG with tighter concentration. The resulting I-CGM-RG-SAGA achieves communication complexity O(C_A n_m + C_R(Δ_1 + √(n_m)δ_m)F^0/ε²) and local complexity O(n_m + (L_1 + Δ_1 + √(n_m)δ_m)F^0/ε²), matching or improving upon all prior methods in Table 1.

## Results

I-CGM-RG-SAGA achieves the best-known communication complexity of $C_A n_m + C_R \frac{(\Delta_1 + \sqrt{n_m}\,\delta_m)F^0}{\varepsilon^2}$ and local complexity of $n_m + \frac{(\Delta_1 + \sqrt{n_m}\,\delta_m + L_1)F^0}{\varepsilon^2}$ for finding an $\varepsilon$-stationary point in non-convex federated optimization. This improves upon SABER-full by replacing the expensive $C_A$ cost with the cheaper $C_R$ in the main $\varepsilon^{-2}$ term, and upon Scaffold by depending on the dissimilarity constant $\delta$ rather than the individual smoothness $L_{\max}$. In experiments on quadratic minimization with log-sum penalty and logistic regression on LIBSVM datasets (mushrooms, duke), I-CGM-RG-SAGA is the most efficient method in both communication and local computation across all tested settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I-CGM-RG-SAGA outperforms all eight baselines in communication complexity. Compared to Scaffold, it replaces the dependence on $L_{\max}$ with the much smaller dissimilarity constant $\delta$ and improves the $n$-dependence from $n^{2/3}$ to $\sqrt{n_m}$ in the $\varepsilon^{-2}$ term. Against SABER-full (which uses PAGE), I-CGM-RG-SAGA replaces the expensive $C_A$ coefficient with the cheaper $C_R$ in the main term and uses $\Delta_1$ instead of $\Delta_{\max}$. Versus FedAvg, MimeMVR, and CE-LGD, I-CGM-RG-SAGA avoids the restrictive BGD assumption and the $\varepsilon^{-3}$ or $\varepsilon^{-4}$ terms, achieving a clean $\varepsilon^{-2}$ rate. Against FedRed, I-CGM-RG-SAGA improves the $n$-dependence from $n_m$ to $\sqrt{n_m}$ in the $\varepsilon^{-2}$ term. In the empirical experiments (quadratic with log-sum penalty, logistic regression on mushrooms and duke), I-CGM-RG-SAGA is consistently the most efficient in both communication rounds and local oracle queries, while Scaffold's local complexity remains comparable to plain GD because it cannot exploit $\delta$-similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the composite gradient method (CGM) framework and variance-reduced estimators (SAGA, SVRG, SARAH, STORM) to develop a new federated optimization algorithm. Its key departure is a cost-aware model that assigns distinct costs to three client-selection strategies (arbitrary, random, delegated), enabling fair comparison across methods that previously appeared incomparable because they rely on fundamentally different participation patterns. The Recursive-Gradient (RG) technique unifies and generalizes SARAH, STORM, and ZEROSARAH into a single framework, while the new SAGA variance bound depending on the dissimilarity constant δ (rather than individual smoothness L_max) represents a genuine theoretical advance over prior SAGA analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cost-aware model addresses a real practical gap: in cross-device federated learning, full synchronization (required by SARAH-based methods) is often infeasible, yet prior complexity comparisons treated all communication rounds as equally expensive. The result that I-CGM-RG-SAGA achieves the best-known communication complexity by exploiting δ-similarity through SAGA (rather than SARAH) reframes which estimator families are competitive under realistic cost structures. Limitations include the requirement of a reliable delegate client (client 1) and the assumption that the cost hierarchy 1 ≤ C_R ≤ C_A holds; extending the framework to fully symmetric client settings or to lower-bound results remains open.
