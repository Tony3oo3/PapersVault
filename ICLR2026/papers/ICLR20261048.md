## Problem

Behavior-regularized actor-critic (BRAC) methods for offline RL constrain policies to stay close to the dataset distribution, but their behavior-cloning regularization treats all state-action pairs indiscriminately, failing to distinguish high-value from low-value actions. This limitation is particularly harmful on suboptimal datasets, where regularizing toward poor transitions actively degrades performance. Additionally, flow- and diffusion-based policies that avoid backpropagation through time (BPTT) still train their BC component without any reward signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors (or RGB images for the 5 pixel-based OGBench tasks) and outputs continuous actions in the environment's action space. The one-step actor π_θ maps a state and a single noise sample z~N(0,I_d) directly to an action, while the VaBC flow policy π_ω requires integrating a learned velocity field over 10 Euler steps (30 for humanoidmaze-large) from the same noise to produce its action. The critic Q_φ evaluates state-action pairs, and all three networks are 4-layer MLPs with 512 units and GeLU activations.

## Method

GFP introduces a bidirectional guidance mechanism between a multi-step flow-matching policy (VaBC) and a distilled one-step actor. VaBC is trained via weighted flow-matching BC where the weight g_η(s,a) is a temperature-controlled softmax comparing the critic's Q-value of the dataset action a against the actor's proposed action μ_θ(s,z), so that high-value dataset transitions receive greater cloning emphasis while low-value ones are downweighted. The actor, in turn, maximizes the critic while being regularized toward VaBC's output rather than raw dataset actions, avoiding BPTT and iterative sampling at inference. This makes the regularization component value-aware: unlike standard BRAC methods that penalize deviation from all dataset actions equally, GFP's regularizer selectively focuses on the most promising transitions. A modified Bellman target y^VaBC that averages Q-estimates from both the actor and VaBC further stabilizes critic training on challenging tasks.

## Results

GFP achieves state-of-the-art performance across 144 offline RL tasks. On the 105-task OGBench benchmark, GFP's actor π_θ scores 53.2 average, outperforming FQL (46.7), ReBRAC (43.9), and IQL (20.4). Gains are most pronounced on suboptimal/noisy datasets: on cube-double-noisy, GFP reaches 63.1 vs. 38.2 for FQL and 19.6 for ReBRAC; on cube-triple-noisy, GFP scores 24.5 vs. 3.5 for FQL. On Minari (21 tasks), GFP achieves 74.1 average vs. 65.9 for FQL, and on D4RL (18 tasks) it scores 63.0, competitive with ReBRAC (64.8) and FQL (62.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFP is compared against four main baselines: IQL, ReBRAC, FQL, and IDQL. Against FQL (Park et al., 2025), the closest competitor, GFP achieves a 6.5-point average gain on OGBench (53.2 vs. 46.7) and an 8.2-point gain on Minari (74.1 vs. 65.9), with the largest margins on noisy and suboptimal datasets (e.g., cube-double-noisy: 63.1 vs. 38.2; cube-triple-noisy: 24.5 vs. 3.5). Against ReBRAC (Tarasov et al., 2023), GFP leads on OGBench (53.2 vs. 43.9) but trails slightly on D4RL (63.0 vs. 64.8), where ReBRAC's value-agnostic BC regularization is less penalized by the higher-quality datasets. Against IQL (Kostrikov et al., 2021), GFP dominates across all benchmarks (53.2 vs. 20.4 on OGBench; 63.0 vs. 54.0 on D4RL), confirming that in-sampling approaches struggle on challenging tasks. Against IDQL (Hansen-Estruch et al., 2023), evaluated on 50 OGBench tasks in Table 10, GFP consistently outperforms, e.g., scoring 95.4 vs. 0 on antmaze-large-navigate-task1 and 83.5 vs. 1 on humanoidmaze-medium-navigate-task1.

## Relation to Prior Work

GFP extends the Behavior-Regularized Actor-Critic (BRAC) family initiated by TD3+BC (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021) and the flow-matching distillation architecture of FQL (Park et al., 2025), which avoids backpropagation through time by distilling a multi-step flow policy into a one-step actor. The key departure is making the BC regularizer value-aware: rather than indiscriminately cloning all dataset state-action pairs (as in TD3+BC, ReBRAC, and FQL), GFP's VaBC component uses a temperature-controlled softmax guidance function to selectively clone high-value actions, drawing conceptually from weighted-BC methods like AWR and AWAC but applying them to the regularizer rather than the main actor. This creates a bidirectional guidance loop absent in prior BRAC methods: the actor shapes which dataset actions VaBC prioritizes, while VaBC constrains the actor to remain within the dataset's high-value support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantial performance gaps on suboptimal and noisy datasets (e.g., cube-triple-noisy: 24.5 vs. 3.5 for FQL) demonstrate that value-aware regularization is critical when the dataset contains many low-quality transitions, a common practical scenario. The re-evaluation of prior methods revealing that task-specific hyperparameters (discount factor, batch size, critic aggregation) significantly underreported ReBRAC and FQL scores is an important methodological finding for the community. However, GFP's reliance on an accurate critic limits its applicability when the dataset lacks high-value actions or rewards are sparse, and the temperature parameter, while less sensitive than α, still requires per-task tuning. Future work could explore reducing critic dependence or extending the framework to sparse-reward and multi-task settings.
