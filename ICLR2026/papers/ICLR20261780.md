## Problem

Existing KV cache quantization methods apply uniform bit-width across all channels, causing severe accuracy degradation in long-context retrieval tasks at low-bit settings (e.g., 2-bit). The paper identifies that quantization sensitivity is highly asymmetric across individual KV channels—retrieval and outlier channels are disproportionately vulnerable to precision loss, while subnormal channels remain stable—yet no prior method exploits this channel-level heterogeneity for bit allocation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261780/cropped_page5_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes full-precision KV cache tensors produced during the prefill stage, along with pre-computed channel assignments (outlier, subnormal, and retrieval channels identified offline in ~10 minutes via K-means clustering on channel dynamic ranges and a one-shot retrieval-mask scoring pass). The output is a mixed-precision quantized KV cache stored as integer tensors with per-group scale and zero-point parameters in float8_e4m3fnuz format, packed into 8-bit-aligned memory via channel reordering. During decoding, a fused Triton kernel reads and dequantizes the cache back to full precision for FlashAttention-compatible computation.

## Method

ChanMix introduces a channel-aware mixed-precision quantization framework that allocates bits according to a three-way channel sensitivity asymmetry: 4 bits for retrieval channels (identified via a novel A-shaped attention mask that isolates copy-paste dependencies), 3 bits for outlier key channels (detected by K-means clustering on per-channel dynamic ranges), 2 bits for normal channels, and 1 bit for subnormal channels. To maintain 8-bit-aligned storage, only the same number of subnormal channels as outlier channels are downgraded, with channel reordering ensuring efficient packing. The framework follows KIVI's asymmetric design—channel-wise quantization for keys and token-wise for values—with group size 32 and residual length 128. Custom Triton kernels fuse quantization, reordering, and storage into a single pass, and a separate kernel fuses reading and dequantization, minimizing memory copy overhead while remaining fully compatible with FlashAttention and existing token-pruning or error-compensation methods.

## Results

On the RULER benchmark, ChanMix achieves near-vanilla performance across all models: 67.85 (vs. 67.87 vanilla) on Llama-2-32K, 86.35 (vs. 86.99) on Mistral-32K, 78.97 (vs. 79.52) on Llama-3-128K, and 82.16 (vs. 84.47) on Llama-3.1-128K, while reducing KV cache size to ~19%. On InfiniteBench, ChanMix matches or exceeds baselines (e.g., 35.72 vs. 32.57 for DuoAttn on Llama-3.1). Efficiency experiments show ChanMix enables a 2.3× larger batch size and 1.5× longer context length compared to the vanilla model under the same 80 GB GPU memory budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChanMix outperforms all five baselines on RULER by at least 5 absolute percentage points in average accuracy. Against the closest quantization baseline KIVI, ChanMix gains +10.85 on Llama-2 (67.85 vs. 57.00), +7.15 on Mistral (86.35 vs. 79.20), and +5.65 on Llama-3.1 (82.16 vs. 76.51). Token-eviction methods (ZipCache, DuoAttn) and outlier-token tracing (OTT) suffer severe degradation at longer contexts—OTT collapses to 46.03 average on Llama-3.1 (vs. ChanMix's 82.16), and DuoAttn drops to 59.03. On InfiniteBench, ChanMix matches or slightly exceeds KIVI and KVQuant (e.g., 34.81 vs. 34.77 on Llama-3) while clearly beating DuoAttn (35.72 vs. 32.57 on Llama-3.1). On short-context tasks (MMLU, MBPP, GSM8K), ChanMix consistently beats KIVI (e.g., MBPP: 33.4 vs. 29.4 on Mistral; 47.2 vs. 44.2 on Llama-3.1), confirming that the channel-aware bit allocation does not sacrifice general capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChanMix builds directly on KIVI's asymmetric quantization framework (channel-wise for keys, token-wise for values with group scaling and residual parameters) but departs from its uniform bit allocation by introducing channel-level mixed precision. It draws on two distinct research threads: the outlier-channel analysis from KVQuant and KIVI, and the retrieval-head mechanistic findings from Wu et al. and Xiao et al., which prior quantization work had not connected to bit allocation. In the broader mixed-precision quantization literature (LLM-MQ, SliM-LLM, CMPQ), ChanMix is the first to apply sensitivity-aware bit allocation at the individual KV channel granularity rather than at the layer, group, or token level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: enabling 2.3× larger batch sizes and 1.5× longer contexts on a single GPU addresses the most acute memory bottleneck in long-context LLM serving. The finding that retrieval-sensitive channels are disproportionately vulnerable to quantization provides a mechanistic explanation for why uniform low-bit quantization degrades long-context performance, opening a path for targeted compression. Limitations include the need for offline channel profiling (though only ~10 minutes), evaluation primarily on 7B–14B models (with 32B/70B in appendix), and the assumption that K-means clustering on channel ranges generalizes across architectures. An open question is whether the 1-bit subnormal allocation remains safe for tasks beyond retrieval, such as multi-turn dialogue or code generation at extreme compression ratios.
