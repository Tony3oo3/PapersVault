## Problem

Training urban embodied AI agents (delivery robots, quadrupeds) requires diverse, high-fidelity interactive environments, but existing simulators either rely on hand-crafted scenes that cannot scale (e.g., CARLA's 15 scenes) or procedurally generate rigid, template-driven layouts that deviate from real-world distributions (e.g., randomly parked scooters). The central question is whether realistic, interactive urban scenes can be automatically constructed from casual real-world city-tour videos to enable scalable, generalizable robot navigation training.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes uncalibrated RGB city-tour videos (walking or driving, ~3 min clips from YouTube under Creative Commons) and produces metric-scale, physics-aware simulation scenes in IsaacSim, each containing a 3D urban scene graph with object nodes (category, centroid, oriented 3D box, yaw, 2D crops), ground nodes (road/sidewalk point clouds with PBR textures), and a sky node (HDRI illumination). For downstream navigation, the agent receives only RGB observation plus its relative position to a GPS-projected goal and waypoints sampled every 5 m, with no access to a global map. Each input video yields 5 'digital cousin' variants sharing the same layout but differing in object appearance, ground material, and lighting, producing 160 training scenes from 32 videos across 24 countries.

## Method

UrbanVerse combines two pillars: (1) UrbanVerse-100K, a curated database of 102,444 metric-scale GLB objects (659 categories), 288 PBR ground materials, and 306 HDRI sky maps, built by filtering Objaverse through human quality inspection, CLIP-based classification into a three-level urban ontology, and GPT-4.1 annotation of 33 semantic/affordance/physical attributes; and (2) UrbanVerse-Gen, a three-stage real-to-sim pipeline. The distillation stage lifts uncalibrated video to metric 3D via MASt3R SfM, parses objects with YOLO-World + SAM2 open-vocabulary detection, segments ground with Mask2Former, and fuses multi-view evidence into a persistent scene graph. The materialization stage retrieves k_cousin assets per object through CLIP semantic matching, minimal Bounding Box Distortion geometry filtering, and DINOv2 appearance re-ranking. The generation stage assembles scenes in IsaacSim with ground plane fitting, HDRI dome lighting, rigid-body dynamics, and ORCA-based dynamic agent population. For policy learning, a vision-only PPO agent is trained on the 160-scene library with scene sets rotated every 100 episodes, exploiting the real-world distributional fidelity to achieve power-law scaling in generalization.

## Results

On the CraftBench simulation benchmark, PPO-UrbanVerse achieves 41.9% success rate (SR), 62.4% route completion (RC), and 35.5 collision times (CT), outperforming the second-best baseline MBRA (35.6% SR) by +6.3%. In zero-shot sim-to-real transfer across 16 real-world streets, PPO-UrbanVerse reaches 77.1% SR on the Coco wheeled robot and 89.7% SR on the Unitree Go2 quadruped, with only 22.9 and 10.4 collisions respectively. The policy also completes a 337 m long-horizon public-space mission with only two human interventions and zero collisions. Scene reconstruction fidelity on KITTI-360 achieves 93.1% category recovery with 1.4 m positional error over 198.7 m horizons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPO-UrbanVerse decisively outperforms all four baselines on both simulation and real-world metrics. On CraftBench (simulation), it achieves 41.9% SR versus MBRA (35.6%), S2E (33.1%), CityWalker (29.2%), and PPO-UrbanSim on procedurally generated scenes (9.1%). In zero-shot real-world transfer, the gap widens dramatically: on the wheeled robot, PPO-UrbanVerse (77.1% SR) surpasses S2E (47.9%) by +29.2%, NoMad (33.3%) by +43.8%, and CityWalker (25.0%) by +52.1%; on the quadruped, it reaches 89.7% SR versus S2E's 58.6% (+31.1%). The PPO-UrbanSim baseline (trained on procedurally generated UrbanSim scenes) performs worst across all settings, confirming that layout realism from real-world video grounding is critical. Foundation models like CityWalker and NoMad, trained on large-scale but non-interactive passive data, fail particularly on obstacle-avoidance scenarios, while PPO-UrbanVerse's interactive RL training in realistic scenes transfers robustly to real-world collision avoidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

UrbanVerse builds directly on the UrbanSim simulation platform (Wu et al., 2025b) for its IsaacSim backend and ORCA-based dynamic agent population, but replaces UrbanSim's procedurally generated layouts with a video-driven real-to-sim pipeline. Conceptually, it extends the 'digital cousin' paradigm from Dai et al. (2024)—which generates multiple virtual indoor scenes from a single calibrated image—to large-scale, street-level outdoor scenes from uncalibrated RGB videos, and departs from 3DGS-based digital twins (OmniRe, Vid2Sim) by producing editable, object-level, physics-annotated scenes rather than fused radiance fields. The asset retrieval strategy (CLIP semantic matching → BBD geometry filtering → DINOv2 appearance selection) repurposes standard vision-language models for a novel scene-construction role, while the policy learning side follows the RL-based navigation line established by S2E (He et al., 2025) and UrbanSim.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 102K-asset physics-annotated database and 160-scene library address a critical scalability bottleneck in urban embodied AI: prior simulators offer at most 15 hand-crafted or 7–6 procedurally generated scenes, while UrbanVerse provides effectively unlimited scene generation grounded in real-world distributions. The demonstrated scaling power laws and the finding that a simple PPO policy on video-derived scenes outperforms large navigation foundation models (MBRA, CityWalker) by 30+ points in zero-shot sim-to-real transfer challenge the assumption that web-scale passive data and complex architectures are prerequisites for robust urban navigation. Key limitations include restriction to street-level environments (no parks, campuses, or indoor-outdoor transitions), sensitivity to challenging video conditions (low light, fast motion, heavy occlusion), and the 3.58/5 human-rated realism still trailing artist-designed scenes at 4.08/5, leaving open whether further fidelity gains are needed for safety-critical deployment.
