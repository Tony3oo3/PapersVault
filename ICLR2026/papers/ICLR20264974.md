## Problem

Prior work on active sequential prediction-powered mean estimation (Zrnic & Candes, 2024) provides only asymptotic normality guarantees and a query policy that mixes model uncertainty with a uniform sampling rate, but offers no non-asymptotic confidence bounds valid at every time step. Empirically, the uncertainty-based component of the query policy appears to contribute little: setting the mixing weight to the uniform policy (λ=1) yields confidence intervals as narrow as or narrower than the recommended λ=0.5 mixture. This paper seeks to explain this phenomenon theoretically and to provide finite-sample guarantees for the sequential estimator.

## Input / Output

At each round t, the method observes a covariate x_t, receives a prediction f_t(x_t) from a black-box ML model that is updated in batches, and must decide a query probability p_t ∈ [β, τ] for collecting the ground-truth label y_t under a total labeling budget T_b ≪ T. The output at every time t is a running mean estimate w_{t+1} together with a (1−α)-confidence interval, and the final output is the interval at t = T. A key structural constraint is that p_t must be F_{t−1}-measurable (oblivious to the current x_t), distinguishing it from the prior work's policy that conditions on x_t.

## Method

The paper first reformulates the sequential active mean estimator as an online update w_{t+1} = w_t + (1/T)·g_t and applies Freedman's martingale concentration inequality to obtain a data-dependent non-asymptotic bound of Õ(1/√t) that holds simultaneously for all t ∈ [T]. It then decomposes the conditional variance of g_t to isolate the term (1/p_t)·E[(y_t − f_t(x_t))² | F_{t−1}], showing that the query probability is the only tunable lever for controlling variance. The core algorithmic contribution formulates the choice of p_t as a convex online optimization problem solved by Follow-the-Regularized-Leader (FTRL) with loss ℓ̃_t(p) = Φ_t(x_t)/p, where Φ_t is an oracle approximating the conditional squared error. Because the oracle output is non-negative, the best fixed benchmark in hindsight is always the upper bound τ = T_b/T, so FTRL's no-regret guarantee forces p_t to converge to τ, effectively reducing the policy to uniform sampling. Theorem 2 then combines the FTRL regret bound with the variance decomposition to yield a data-dependent confidence interval whose width depends on the best-fixed-probability variance plus a sublinear regret term.

## Results

Across four datasets (post-election survey, politeness score, wine review, and a synthetic dataset) evaluated over 50 trials, the proposed FTRL query policy achieves average confidence interval widths comparable to the mixture policy of Zrnic & Candes (2024) with λ=0.5, while both outperform the fixed-model uniform sampling baseline. In two of the four datasets, the FTRL policy attains marginally narrower confidence intervals than the mixture policy. All three methods maintain high coverage of the true mean (the right panels of Figures 2–4 show the percentage of trials whose confidence intervals include the true value). The paper also demonstrates that setting the mixing parameter to λ=1 (pure uniform query probability) yields intervals that are slightly narrower than λ=0.5, motivating the FTRL approach that converges to the budget-constrained maximum query probability T_b/T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed FTRL policy against two baselines from Zrnic & Candes (2024): (1) a uniform sampling baseline with a fixed ML predictor (Eq. 6), and (2) the mixture query policy with λ=0.5 that combines an uncertainty-based component with a uniform component. Against the mixture policy, the FTRL policy performs comparably in terms of average confidence width across all four datasets, with marginally narrower intervals in two of them (politeness score and wine review, per Figures 2–3). Against the uniform sampling baseline, both the FTRL policy and the mixture policy clearly outperform it, confirming the benefit of sequential data collection and model updating. Notably, the FTRL policy—which quickly converges to the constant query probability T_b/T and then maintains it—matches the performance of the more sophisticated mixture policy that explicitly conditions on current features x_t, suggesting that the uncertainty-based component provides no clear advantage for this type of oblivious-to-covariates policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Active Statistical Inference framework of Zrnic & Candes (2024), which proposed a sequential active mean estimator with a query policy mixing model-uncertainty-based sampling and uniform sampling. The key departure is twofold: (1) the paper provides the first non-asymptotic confidence interval guarantee for the sequential estimator via Freedman's inequality, where prior work only established asymptotic normality; and (2) it reframes the query probability determination as a no-regret online learning problem using FTRL (Abernethy et al., 2012), rather than relying on a hand-crafted uncertainty-weighted mixture. The broader PPI literature (Angelopoulos et al., 2023a,b) provides the foundational prediction-powered inference setting, but this work specifically targets the sequential, budget-constrained variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that the sophisticated uncertainty-prediction machinery at the heart of active statistical inference may be unnecessary for mean estimation: the FTRL policy provably converges to the uniform budget rate T_b/T, and experiments confirm this matches or slightly beats the uncertainty-based mixture policy. This simplifies the active inference pipeline considerably. Open questions include whether the result extends beyond mean estimation to other functionals (medians, quantiles, treatment effects), whether the oracle approximation assumption (Φ_t bounding the conditional squared error) is satisfiable in practice without the very labels one is trying to avoid querying, and whether conditioning on current covariates x_t (as Zrnic & Candes do) could in principle help in settings where the FTRL obliviousness constraint is relaxed.
