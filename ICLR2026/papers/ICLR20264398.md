## Problem

Existing pruning methods for LLM-based embedding models treat all parameters uniformly, failing to distinguish between general semantic representations and domain-specific patterns. This uniform treatment causes two failure modes: parameters encoding crucial domain knowledge may be incorrectly removed, or domain-only importance scoring may strip essential general linguistic capabilities. Domain-aware pruning strategies specifically designed for instruction-tuned embedding models remain largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LLM-based embedding model (Qwen3-Embedding-4B or E5-Mistral-7B-Instruct) along with two sets of contrastive triplets (query, positive, negative) — one general (from MSMARCO/SQuAD) and one domain-specific (finance or chemistry) — each reduced to 5,000 representative samples via k-means clustering in embedding space. Gradients are computed via InfoNCE loss over these triplets. The output is a one-shot pruned sparse model at a target sparsity (30% or 50% on MLP layers), optionally followed by 100 steps of InfoNCE retraining on domain data.

## Method

GAPrune characterizes each parameter along two dimensions: domain-specific importance via diagonal Fisher Information (computed separately on general and domain data) and cross-domain alignment via cosine similarity between averaged general and domain gradients. These signals are combined into a Domain Alignment Importance (DAI) score guided by Information Bottleneck principles: (F_dom − β·F_gen)·|θ| + γ·√|θ|, modulated by (1 + α·s_g), which rewards parameters serving both domains and penalizes those creating conflicts. A one-shot pruning mask retains the top-k parameters by DAI score, with hyperparameters β=1.0, α=0.2, γ=0.5. The key insight is that gradient alignment provides complementary information beyond Fisher Information alone — Domain Fisher and General Fisher show 0.978 correlation (domain-agnostic), while GAPrune shows negative correlation (−0.406/−0.459) with both, identifying a distinct set of critical parameters.

## Results

In one-shot pruning at 50% sparsity on Qwen3-Embedding-4B, GAPrune maintains 0.5224 average on FinMTEB (−2.41% vs. dense) and 0.7462 on ChemTEB (−2.31% vs. dense), while General Fisher collapses to 0.3623 (−32.32%) and Random pruning drops to 0.2165 (−59.55%). With 100-step retraining, GAPrune surpasses the dense model by +4.51% on FinMTEB (0.5594 vs. 0.5353) and +1.73% on ChemTEB (0.7770 vs. 0.7639). At 30% sparsity, GAPrune already exceeds the dense baseline by +1.35% on FinMTEB, indicating the DAI score identifies parameters whose removal actively benefits domain performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Magnitude-based Pruning (Han et al., 2015), GAPrune wins at 50% sparsity on both benchmarks: 0.5224 vs. 0.5171 on FinMTEB and 0.7462 vs. 0.7299 on ChemTEB, with the gap widening at 65% sparsity where Magnitude collapses to 0.2414 while GAPrune retains 0.4929. Against Fisher Pruning (Theis et al., 2018), GAPrune shows a decisive advantage: General Fisher degrades by −32.32% on FinMTEB at 50% sparsity while GAPrune stays within −2.41%, and Domain Fisher still lags GAPrune by 3.4 points on FinMTEB (0.4887 vs. 0.5224). The correlation analysis reveals why: Domain Fisher and General Fisher are nearly identical (Pearson ρ = 0.978), confirming Fisher Information alone is domain-agnostic, whereas GAPrune's DAI score is negatively correlated with both (−0.406 and −0.459), capturing complementary parameter importance signals. Against L3Prune (Thenal et al., 2025) in the prune-and-retrain setting, GAPrune achieves 0.5594 vs. 0.5422 on FinMTEB and 0.7770 vs. 0.7642 on ChemTEB, with the advantage driven by better preservation of retrieval performance (0.6401 vs. 0.6361) and STS (0.3980 vs. 0.3577). Overall, GAPrune outperforms all baselines across every sparsity level and both domains tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAPrune extends Fisher Information-based pruning (Theis et al., 2018) by introducing a cross-domain gradient alignment signal, positioning itself against both magnitude-based methods (Han et al., 2015) and single-objective Fisher variants that treat all parameters uniformly. It builds on the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al., 2000) to formalize the trade-off between domain specialization and general capability preservation, and draws on recent domain-aware compression work (Zhang et al., 2024; Williams et al., 2025) while specifically targeting LLM-based embedding models evaluated via contrastive metrics rather than perplexity. The method departs from generative LLM pruning (SparseGPT, Wanda) by recognizing that embedding models exhibit distinct parameter sensitivity patterns, particularly in attention heads encoding semantic relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete deployment bottleneck: the 9-fold download gap between compact and large embedding models shows that computational constraints, not raw performance, drive real-world adoption of domain-specific models. The finding that principled pruning followed by brief domain retraining can exceed dense model performance (+4.51% on FinMTEB) suggests pruning acts as a form of domain-specific regularization rather than mere compression. Limitations include evaluation on only two domains and two architectures, pruning restricted to MLP layers, and reliance on a small calibration set whose representativeness may not hold for highly heterogeneous domains. Open questions include whether the DAI framework extends to structured pruning, interacts productively with quantization, or generalizes to domains with weaker signal separation between general and specialized knowledge.
