## Problem

Existing representation-editing methods for stylized LLM generation naively inject style steering vectors, which contaminates the model's truthfulness representations and causes a phenomenon the authors term stylization-induced truthfulness collapse. The root cause is latent entanglement between style- and truth-relevant activation directions in a subset of attention heads (Relevant Heads), where editing one attribute inadvertently perturbs the other. No prior method explicitly disentangles these coupled subspaces to enable simultaneous, non-interfering control of both style and truth.

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method takes a text question as input and intervenes on the multi-head attention activations of a frozen LLM (Qwen 1.5-14B-Chat, Qwen2-7B, Qwen2.5-7B, or Llama3-8B) at a small set of pre-selected attention heads. The pre-computed steering subspaces (top-K right singular vectors from SVD on contrastive activation differences) are injected per-token with adaptive strength coefficients that scale with each token's deviation from the target style or truth direction. The output is a single generated response that simultaneously satisfies a target style (Shakespeare or Dream of the Red Chamber) and preserves factual correctness on TruthfulQA.

## Method

StyliTruth is a training-free, four-stage inference-time intervention. First, linear probes are trained on final-token activations to identify the top-H heads most discriminative for style and for truth, respectively. Second, for heads belonging to only one set, SVD on the corresponding activation-difference matrix yields an orthogonal subspace basis directly. For the critical case of heads in the intersection (style-truth-coupled heads), an orthogonal deflation step projects the truth activation differences onto the orthogonal complement of the style subspace (P_s^⊥ = I − V_{s,K}V_{s,K}^⊤) before re-applying SVD, guaranteeing that the resulting truth basis is exactly orthogonal to the style basis. Third, steering vectors are constructed as linear combinations of the respective subspace basis vectors. Fourth, an adaptive token-level mechanism modulates each basis vector's contribution per token via a scaling factor κ computed as the projection of (mean positive activation − current activation) onto that basis vector, multiplied by a global strength g (proportional to the singular value) and a cap γ, enabling precise, token-specific control without uniform over-perturbation.

## Results

On the DRC→TruthfulQA(ZH) setting, StyliTruth achieves an S-TI (harmonic mean of style OA and truth TI) of 0.2366, a 30.65% improvement over the strongest baseline DRESS (0.1560), while maintaining a high truthfulness score (TI=0.5000) alongside strong style control (OA=0.1550). On Shakespeare→TruthfulQA, StyliTruth reaches S-TI=0.2803, a 10.83% improvement over DRESS (0.2529), with TI=0.3889 and OA=0.2191. Ablations confirm both subspace disentanglement and adaptive token-level editing are necessary: removing either component drops S-TI from 0.2366 to 0.1575–0.1632.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StyliTruth outperforms all five baselines on the combined S-TI metric in both language settings. DRESS (Ma et al., 2025), the strongest baseline, achieves the best style scores among competitors (OA=0.2037 on Shakespeare) but still trails StyliTruth by 10.83% in S-TI, primarily because its TI (0.3333) is substantially lower than StyliTruth's (0.3889). Vector Prompt and ITI (Li et al., 2023) achieve strong style intensity (SI≈0.97–0.99) but suffer severe stylization-induced truthfulness collapse, with TI dropping to 0.08–0.22. CAA (Panickssery et al., 2023) shows moderate style but similarly degraded truthfulness (TI=0.14–0.29). LLM Steer (Han et al., 2023) is the only method that avoids truthfulness collapse (TI=0.5972 on DRC) but fails at style transfer entirely (OA=0.0142), indicating its steering is too weak to induce any meaningful stylistic change.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representation-editing / activation-steering lineage (CAA, ITI, LLM-Steer, Vector Prompt) and builds most directly on DRESS (Ma et al., 2025), which already performs style-subspace editing via SVD-based decomposition. The key departure is that prior methods treat style injection as a single-directional intervention and ignore its collateral damage on truthfulness; StyliTruth explicitly models the entanglement between style and truth in shared attention heads and introduces an orthogonal deflation step to construct mutually orthogonal subspaces before steering. The adaptive token-level editing mechanism is also inherited from DRESS but is applied independently within each disentangled subspace rather than in a single style subspace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By naming and mechanistically explaining 'stylization-induced truthfulness collapse,' the paper gives the community a concrete diagnostic for a failure mode that was previously observed anecdotally but not attributed to a specific representational cause. The orthogonal deflation solution is lightweight, training-free, and conceptually generalizable to other multi-attribute steering scenarios (e.g., tone + safety, formality + factual grounding). Limitations include evaluation on only two styles and models up to 14B parameters, and the information-loss argument for deflation relies on an incoherence assumption that is not empirically stress-tested at scale. Open questions include whether the approach extends to three or more simultaneously controlled attributes and whether the entanglement structure persists in much larger or differently-architected models.
