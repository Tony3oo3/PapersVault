## Problem

Most existing ML-based MILP model reduction methods focus exclusively on variable reduction (predicting and fixing a subset of variables), while constraint reduction—transforming a subset of inequality constraints into equalities—has been largely overlooked despite its potential to shrink the search space. The paper tackles two intertwined challenges: identifying which inequality constraints are critical (i.e., fixing them accelerates solving without sacrificing feasibility) and learning to predict those critical constraints efficiently from the MILP structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes an MILP instance (objective vector c, constraint matrix A, RHS vector b) encoded as a weighted bipartite graph, together with a JSON-formatted textual description of the problem's abstract model (variable types, constraint types, and their structural relationships), which is embedded via T5-base to initialize the abstract-level graph. The output is a reduced MILP in which a top-k subset of predicted critical tight constraints is converted to equalities (with a Big-M trust region parameter Δc to tolerate prediction errors), alongside predicted binary variable assignments; this reduced model is then handed to a standard solver (Gurobi or SCIP) for final optimization.

## Method

The core architecture is a two-level GNN with intra-layer message passing (a high-level GNN on the abstract category graph and a low-level GNN on the instance bipartite graph, following Gasse et al.) and inter-layer message passing that fuses the two via Cross-Attention (sending), MLP concatenation (receiving), and a learned gating mechanism with Hadamard product (updating). Critical tight constraints are identified through an information-theory-inspired heuristic: each prototypical constraint type is assigned a fixed constraint strength ρ (the ratio of feasible-space size after fixing to before), and constraints from categories with the lowest ρ (highest entropy reduction ΔH = −log ρ) are labeled as critical. Training uses Focal Loss to counteract selective learning bias where networks over-confidently predict simple, non-critical constraints, and the final loss balances variable-prediction and constraint-prediction objectives. The reduced model introduces auxiliary binary variables z_i and a trust-region budget Δc to allow a small number of constraint violations, preserving feasibility even under prediction noise.

## Results

Across four benchmark datasets (CA, MIS, MVC, WA) with both Gurobi and SCIP solvers under an 800s time limit, the proposed constraint-based model reduction achieves an average improvement of 51.06% in absolute primal gap (gap_abs) and 17.47% in primal-dual integral (PDI) over the best-performing baseline. On the most challenging CA dataset with SCIP, the method reduces gap_abs from 3545.92 (ConPaS) to 3401.63 and PDI from 146.88 to 109.41. On MIS and MVC, the method achieves near-zero gap_abs (0.00–0.34) with Gurobi, outperforming all baselines by 70–100%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: vanilla Gurobi, vanilla SCIP, Predict-and-Search (PS, Han et al. 2023), and Contrastive Predict-and-Search (ConPaS, Huang et al. 2024). The proposed method outperforms all baselines on every dataset and solver combination. Against ConPaS (the strongest ML baseline), the method improves gap_abs by 47% on CA with Gurobi (104.72 vs. 197.36) and by 4.1% on CA with SCIP (3401.63 vs. 3545.92), while achieving substantially larger gains on MIS (100% gap_abs improvement with Gurobi) and MVC (100% with Gurobi, 42% with SCIP). Against PS, the improvements are even more pronounced, with up to 100% gap_abs reduction on MIS and MVC. The PDI improvements are more modest on CA (4–42%) but dramatic on MIS and MVC (90–99%), indicating the constraint reduction is most effective on graph-structured problems with many tight constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GNN-based MILP solving lineage (Gasse et al. 2019 for bipartite graph representation; Han et al. 2023 for Predict-and-Search) by introducing constraint reduction as a complement to the dominant variable-reduction paradigm. While prior work (Bertsimas & Stellato 2022; Li et al. 2024) attempted to predict all tight constraints, this paper departs by selecting only a critical subset via an entropy-based heuristic, and introduces a multi-modal representation that fuses instance-level bipartite graphs with abstract-model text embeddings (via T5) through cross-attention and gating mechanisms. The method also builds on the trust-region neighborhood search framework from Han et al. (2023) for variable fixing, extending it with a parallel constraint-fixing mechanism using Big-M indicator constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Constraint reduction is a largely unexplored axis of MILP model reduction, and this paper provides the first systematic framework for identifying and learning which inequality constraints to fix, grounded in an information-theoretic (entropy reduction) criterion. The multi-modal representation—combining structural instance features with semantic category information from text—offers a generalizable encoding that could transfer across MILP problem classes. Key limitations include the reliance on explicit mathematical model descriptions (preventing direct use on MIPLIB), the heuristic nature of the constraint selection (validated empirically but not guaranteed), and the modest gain from the multi-modal component over a plain GNN (~3% in constraint prediction accuracy). Open questions include developing instance-level (rather than category-level) critical constraint identification and reducing dependence on hand-crafted problem descriptions.
