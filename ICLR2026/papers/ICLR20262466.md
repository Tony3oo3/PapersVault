## Problem

Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) use a shared encoder to amortize inference across all samples, but this parameter-sharing paradigm inherently conflicts with the instance-level optimality required for monosemantic features. The paper demonstrates that pursuing the global reconstruction-sparsity Pareto frontier does not improve monosemanticity; instead, increasing sparsity exacerbates pathological phenomena (dead latents, feature splitting, feature absorption, dense latents) because the shared encoder must compromise per-sample fidelity to satisfy a global budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes d-dimensional residual stream activations (resid_post) from a specific transformer layer — 2304-dim for Gemma-2-2B layer 12 or 768-dim for Pythia-160M layer 8 — and produces an m=16,384-dimensional sparse latent code via an overcomplete dictionary. In LocA-SAE, the single global encoder is replaced by 8 group-specific encoders, each applying a group-specific Top-k_g threshold (budgets 6,5,4,3,3,2,2,1) before ReLU, so the output code has heterogeneous sparsity across latent groups while sharing one decoder for reconstruction.

## Method

LocA-SAE groups latent units by angular variance (AVar_j = 1 − ‖μ_j‖₂, where μ_j is the mean direction of normalized inputs activating latent j) into G=8 contiguous groups, then assigns each group an independent encoder with a group-specific Top-k_g sparsity budget, while retaining a single shared decoder. Training follows a four-stage procedure: pretrain a global SAE, partition latents by angular variance, initialize group encoders via weight copying from the global encoder, and fine-tune all parameters under group-specific k_g constraints. The paper further validates its amortization-gap hypothesis through semi-amortized inference (encoder initialization refined by ~30 ISTA steps) and non-amortized inference (200 ISTA steps from zero), showing that reducing parameter sharing consistently alleviates dead latents, feature absorption, and feature splitting across six SAE architectures, with LocA-SAE eliminating dead latents entirely at only a marginal NMSE cost.

## Results

On Pythia-160M (layer 8), LocA-SAE completely eliminates dead latents (Dead Rate = 0.000) while achieving the lowest feature-splitting score (ΔF1 = 0.013) and a low absorption rate (0.055) among all full-amortized baselines, at a moderate NMSE of 0.427. On Gemma-2-2B (layer 12), LocA-SAE again achieves zero dead latents with ΔF1 = 0.024 and Absorption Rate = 0.023, with NMSE = 0.211. In downstream intervention tasks, semi-amortized TopK SAE achieves a target accuracy drop of 0.552 (vs. 0.303 for full-amortized) and a GIS intervention score of 1.975 at κ=1.00 (vs. 1.415 for full-amortized), confirming that reducing amortization improves feature controllability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LocA-SAE outperforms all six full-amortized baselines on dead latents (0.000 vs. 0.219–0.371), feature splitting (ΔF1 = 0.013 vs. 0.033–0.070), and feature absorption (0.055 vs. 0.144–0.240) on Pythia-160M. Against Standard SAE (Cunningham et al.), LocA-SAE trades a higher NMSE (0.427 vs. 0.109) for dramatically better monosemanticity metrics. Against Gated SAE, LocA-SAE achieves substantially lower NMSE (0.427 vs. 1.947) while matching its near-zero dead rate and halving the absorption rate. Against TopK SAE (Gao et al.), LocA-SAE reduces NMSE from 1.499 to 0.427 and eliminates dead latents (0.307 → 0.000). Against BatchTopK and Matryoshka (Bussmann et al.), LocA-SAE achieves comparable or better ΔF1 and absorption while matching their zero-dead-latent performance. The key trade-off is that LocA-SAE's NMSE is higher than the semi-amortized or non-amortized versions of most baselines (e.g., non-amortized BatchTopK achieves NMSE 0.017), reflecting the paper's central argument that full per-sample optimization is most effective but computationally prohibitive, and LocA-SAE occupies a favorable point on the fidelity-compute trade-off curve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SAE framework for monosemantic feature extraction (Bricken et al., 2023) and the Top-k sparsification mechanism (Gao et al., 2024), but departs fundamentally from the fully-amortized encoding paradigm that underlies all prior SAE variants. It reframes the amortization gap concept from variational inference (O'Neill et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2018) as the root cause of SAE pathologies—dead latents, dense latents, feature splitting, and absorption—rather than treating them as independent architectural failures. The proposed LocA-SAE introduces local amortization via angular-variance-based latent grouping, drawing on the local amortization idea from meta-learned VAEs (Wu et al., 2020; Liu & Liu, 2020) but adapting it to the discrete, sparse, instance-level setting of interpretability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a core working assumption in the SAE community: that optimizing the global reconstruction-sparsity Pareto frontier (the objective of nearly all SAE variants) improves monosemanticity. The finding that increased sparsity simultaneously worsens dead latents, feature splitting, and absorption—while the amortization gap shrinks—suggests the field has been optimizing the wrong metric. However, the practical impact is currently limited: per-sample optimization is computationally prohibitive at scale, LocA-SAE is explicitly positioned as 'the first step,' and experiments are confined to small models (Pythia-160M, Gemma-2-2B). Open questions include whether angular-variance grouping is the right partitioning criterion, how local amortization scales to 7B+ models, and whether meta-learning or online dictionary learning can close the remaining gap without per-sample iteration.
