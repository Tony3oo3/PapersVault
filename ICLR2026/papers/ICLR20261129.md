## Problem

Vanilla next-scale prediction methods (e.g., VAR) condition each scale on all preceding scales and require every token to attend to all preceding tokens, incurring quadratic attention complexity and heavy KV cache memory. Attention weight analysis reveals that each scale predominantly relies on its immediately adjacent predecessor and each token primarily attends to local spatial neighbors, exposing substantial scale and spatial redundancy that inflates GPU memory and computational cost without contributing meaningfully to generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261129/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single class label from ImageNet-1K (or a class-conditional start token [s]) as input and generates a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image through a coarse-to-fine sequence of 10 multi-scale residual token maps with spatial sizes {1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,13,16} for 256×256. Unlike standard VAR, MVAR eliminates the KV cache entirely during inference since each scale only depends on its immediate predecessor, and the final image is decoded from the last-scale token map via the VQ-VAE decoder.

## Method

MVAR introduces two Markovian constraints into the next-scale autoregressive framework. First, a scale-Markov trajectory restricts the conditioning prefix for scale r_l to only its adjacent predecessor r_{l-1}, discarding all earlier scales; this enables parallel training of scales r_1 through r_8 using a diagonal-pattern causal mask and removes the need for KV cache at inference. Second, spatial-Markov attention confines each query token to attend exclusively to a k×k neighborhood (k=7×7) at the corresponding position on the adjacent scale, reducing attention complexity from O(N²) to O(Nk). Scales r_9 and r_10, which account for 60% of all tokens, are handled separately via custom CUDA kernels implementing the localized attention. The model is trained from scratch on eight RTX 4090 GPUs (310M params, 300 epochs) or fine-tuned from VAR checkpoints, achieving comparable or better FID/IS while reducing average GPU memory by 3.0×.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, MVAR-d16 (310M params, 10 steps) achieves FID 3.09, IS 285.5, Precision 0.85, and Recall 0.51, outperforming the same-size VAR-d16 baseline (FID 3.55, IS 280.4) by 0.46 FID and 5.1 IS. The fine-tuned MVAR-d24† (1B params) reaches FID 2.23 and IS 300.1 while reducing inference GPU memory by 3.2× (7,216 MB vs. 23,056 MB) and eliminating KV cache entirely. Across all model sizes, MVAR reduces average GPU memory footprint by 3.0× and attention GFLOPs by ~18% compared to VAR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVAR-d16 directly outperforms its closest baseline VAR-d16 (same 310M architecture) by 0.46 FID (3.09 vs. 3.55) and 5.1 IS (285.5 vs. 280.4), while also reducing inference memory from 10,882 MB to 3,846 MB (2.8×) and eliminating the 5,704 MB KV cache. Among GAN baselines, MVAR-d16's FID of 3.09 is competitive with GigaGAN (3.45) and BigGAN (6.95) but trails StyleGAN-XL (2.30); however, MVAR requires only 10 steps versus 1 for GANs. Against diffusion models, MVAR-d16 beats ADM (10.94), CDM (4.88), LDM-4-G (3.60), and DiT-L/2 (5.02) on FID, though DiT-XL/2 (2.27) and Simple-Diffusion (2.44) remain stronger at the cost of 250+ sampling steps. Among token-wise autoregressive methods, MVAR-d16 achieves the best FID (3.09) compared to ViTVQ-re (3.04 at 1.7B params and 1024 steps), RCG (3.49), and DART-AR (3.98), while using only 310M parameters and 10 steps. The fine-tuned MVAR-d24† (1B) reaches FID 2.23, surpassing VAR-d24 (2.33) and matching DiT-XL/2 (2.27) with 3.2× less memory and no KV cache.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVAR extends the next-scale prediction paradigm introduced by VAR (Tian et al., 2024), which redefined autoregressive image generation from next-token to next-scale prediction. The key departure is the introduction of two Markovian assumptions—scale-Markov (conditioning only on the immediately preceding scale rather than all prior scales) and spatial-Markov (restricting attention to a local neighborhood of size k)—motivated by empirical attention-weight analyses showing that vanilla next-scale models exhibit strong inter-scale and spatial redundancy. The spatial-Markov attention mechanism directly adopts the neighborhood attention design from Hassani & Shi (2022) and Hassani et al. (2023), repurposing it within the multi-scale autoregressive setting to replace dense cross-scale attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating KV cache and reducing GPU memory by ~3× makes next-scale autoregressive image generation feasible on consumer hardware (8× RTX 4090), lowering the barrier to entry for this model class. The finding that removing redundant cross-scale and global spatial dependencies not only saves compute but also *improves* generation quality (lower FID, higher IS) suggests that the vanilla next-scale formulation was over-parameterized in its conditioning. Open questions include whether the Markovian assumptions hold at higher resolutions or for video generation, whether more advanced tokenizers (beyond the inherited VQ-VAE) further benefit from this framework, and whether the spatial-Markov constraint can be made adaptive rather than fixed at k=7×7.
