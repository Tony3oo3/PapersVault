## Problem

In RLVR methods like GRPO, zero-variance prompts—where all G sampled responses to a question receive identical rewards—produce zero advantage values, yielding no gradient signal. These prompts account for 30–99% of training batches, yet existing approaches simply discard or filter them, wasting the substantial rollout-generation cost (≈50% of training time). No prior work has investigated whether zero-variance prompts themselves can serve as a source of useful learning signal for policy optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a question-answer pair (q, a) from a math dataset, samples G responses (8 for 1.7B, 16 for 8B) from the current policy, and verifies each against the ground truth to obtain binary rewards. The output is an updated policy model trained via the RL-ZVP objective, which branches: standard GRPO for non-zero-variance prompts and an entropy-shaped advantage for zero-variance ones. Training uses the verl framework with clip ratios (0.20, 0.28), token-level loss, and no KL term.

## Method

RL-ZVP generalizes GRPO by replacing the zero advantage assigned to zero-variance prompts with an entropy-guided, token-level advantage. For positive prompts (all responses correct), each token's advantage is α·H_{i,t}, rewarding high-entropy tokens (reasoning-critical branching points) more than trivial tokens. For negative prompts (all responses incorrect), the advantage is −α·(max_k H_{i,k} − H_{i,t}), which penalizes low-entropy tokens more severely while sparing high-entropy tokens to preserve exploratory flexibility. The scaling factor α (set to 0.10) prevents exploding updates from large entropy values. This asymmetric design—rewarding proportionally to entropy but penalizing inversely—encourages exploitation of correct reasoning paths while maintaining exploration capacity on incorrect ones, and the formulation reduces exactly to GRPO when prompt variance is non-zero.

## Results

On six math reasoning benchmarks, RL-ZVP achieves average improvements of +2.84 Acc@8 and +4.62 Pass@8 over GRPO on Qwen3-1.7B-Base (small scale), and +5.15 Acc@8 and +3.95 Pass@8 on Qwen3-8B-Base (large scale). The largest single-benchmark gains are +8.61 Acc@8 on OlympiadBench and +7.77 Pass@8 on AIME25 (large scale). Even when prompt-filtering baselines are granted 3×–5× more rollouts, RL-ZVP still outperforms them by +0.99 Acc@8 / +1.19 Pass@8 (small) and +5.51 Acc@8 / +5.32 Pass@8 (large).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), RL-ZVP wins on all six benchmarks at both model scales, with average gains of +2.84 Acc@8 / +4.62 Pass@8 (1.7B) and +5.15 Acc@8 / +3.95 Pass@8 (8B); the largest single-benchmark improvement is +8.61 Acc@8 on OlympiadBench (8B). Against GRPO-DS (Yu et al., 2025), which discards zero-variance prompts via post-rollout oversampling, RL-ZVP achieves +3.28 Acc@8 / +4.03 Pass@8 under equal rollout budgets (small scale) and a striking +19.20 Acc@8 / +13.56 Pass@8 (large scale), where GRPO-DS-r collapses to near-random performance because the model initially solves only ~2% of problems per batch. Even when GRPO-DS-g and GRESO-g are granted 3×–5× more rollouts to match gradient-step counts, RL-ZVP still outperforms them by +0.99 Acc@8 / +1.19 Pass@8 (small) and +5.51 Acc@8 / +5.32 Pass@8 (large). Against GRESO (Zheng et al., 2025), which uses a pre-rollout probabilistic filter to skip zero-variance prompts, RL-ZVP similarly dominates across all benchmarks and both scales. The paper also validates generalization: on a different model family (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct) and an alternative training set (NuminaMath-CoT), RL-ZVP continues to outperform GRPO, and out-of-domain evaluation on LiveCodeBench, MMLU-Pro, GPQA-Diamond, and BIG-Bench Hard confirms cross-domain transfer of the learned reasoning improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), itself a PPO variant for LLM post-training, by reformulating the advantage computation specifically for the zero-variance prompt case where all rollouts receive identical rewards. It departs fundamentally from the prevailing practice in DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) and GRESO (Zheng et al., 2025), which treat zero-variance prompts as wasted computation to be filtered out pre- or post-rollout. The entropy-guided magnitude design draws on the token-level reward shaping literature (Cui et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025; Agarwal et al., 2025), but applies it in a new context: providing non-trivial learning signal where GRPO's normalized advantage collapses to zero. The result is a strict generalization of GRPO that introduces no new loss terms, only a different advantage formula for a previously discarded sub-case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since zero-variance prompts account for 30–99% of training batches depending on model scale and dataset difficulty, extracting signal from them directly addresses a major throughput bottleneck in RLVR training. The technique is a minimal, drop-in modification to GRPO (one hyperparameter α, one entropy computation per token) that could be readily integrated into existing RLVR pipelines. Key limitations include evaluation only up to 8B parameters, restriction to binary verifiable rewards on math tasks, and the open question of whether the asymmetric entropy formulation generalizes to graded or ambiguous feedback. Future work on scaling to 14B+ models and combining with other token-level shaping methods would strengthen the practical impact.
