## Problem

Open-ended deep research (OEDR) requires agents to synthesize 100+ web pages into comprehensive, citation-grounded reports, but existing approaches suffer from two coupled failures: static pipelines that decouple planning from evidence acquisition (either fixing an outline before searching or searching before outlining), and monolithic generation that feeds all gathered evidence into a single context window, causing 'lost in the middle' attention failures, hallucinations, and poor citation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260150/cropped_page2_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single open-ended research question (no ground-truth answer) and produces a structured, multi-level outline where each subsection carries explicit citation IDs linking to a curated memory bank of extracted evidence, followed by a 20k+ token long-form report with inline citations. During planning, only short query-relevant summaries (not raw pages) enter the LLM context; during writing, only the specific evidence chunks referenced by a section's citation IDs are retrieved from the memory bank, keeping each writing step's context focused and small.

## Method

WebWeaver is a dual-agent ReAct framework with a planner and a writer. The planner runs a dynamic agentic loop that iteratively interleaves two actions—web search (with two-stage URL filtering, page parsing, summary extraction, and evidence extraction into a structured memory bank) and outline optimization—so that the outline and search strategy co-evolve over multiple rounds (averaging ~2.2 optimization cycles) until a comprehensive, citation-grounded outline is produced. The writer then executes a hierarchical, section-by-section synthesis: for each outline section it performs a targeted retrieve action pulling only the evidence chunks matching that section's citation IDs, reasons over the retrieved content, writes the section, and then prunes the used evidence from context before moving to the next section. This retrieve-and-prune mechanism is the key architectural insight that decouples total evidence volume (67k+ tokens) from per-step context size, mitigating long-context degradation. The authors further construct WebWeaver-3k, a 3.3k-trajectory SFT dataset generated by the full pipeline, and fine-tune Qwen3-30b to achieve expert-level performance with a smaller, self-hosted model.

## Results

On DeepResearch Bench, WebWeaver (Claude-sonnet-4) achieves a state-of-the-art overall RACE score of 50.48, surpassing the previous best Gemini-2.5-pro-deepresearch (49.71) and openai-deepresearch (46.45). It also attains the highest citation accuracy of 92.13% and 216.99 effective citations per task. On DeepConsult, WebWeaver (Qwen3-235b) achieves the highest win rate of 67.69% and average score of 6.90, while on DeepResearchGym, WebWeaver (Claude-sonnet-4) reaches the top average score of 96.74 with near-perfect depth (99.96) and breadth (99.90).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WebWeaver (Claude-sonnet-4) outperforms all nine baselines on DeepResearch Bench overall RACE score (50.48), with the closest competitor being Gemini-2.5-pro-deepresearch (49.71) and openai-deepresearch (46.45). The gap is most dramatic against open-source systems: OpenScholar (25.56) and WebShaper-32B (34.93) trail by 25 and 16 points respectively, while even the strong ReAct baseline with the same Qwen3-235b backbone (46.16) is surpassed by 4.3 points. On citation accuracy, WebWeaver achieves 92.13%, far exceeding openai-deepresearch (75.01%), Gemini-2.5-pro-deepresearch (78.30%), and doubao-research (52.86%). On DeepConsult, WebWeaver (Qwen3-235b) wins 67.69% of pairwise comparisons versus openai-deepresearch, compared to 61.27% for Gemini and 0% for openai-deepresearch itself (tie-only). On DeepResearchGym, WebWeaver (Claude-sonnet-4) achieves the highest average score of 96.74, edging out Gemini-2.5-pro-deepresearch (96.02) and WebWeaver (Qwen3-235b) (95.57), with near-perfect depth (99.96) and breadth (99.90) scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

WebWeaver builds on the ReAct agent framework (Yao et al., 2023) but departs fundamentally from both dominant deep-research paradigms: the 'search-then-generate' pipeline (Tao et al., 2025; Roucher et al., 2025) and the decoupled 'outline-guided search' or 'search-then-outlining' approaches (Han et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2024; Xiong et al., 2025). Its core architectural departure is a dynamic research cycle where outline optimization and evidence acquisition co-evolve iteratively, rather than following a fixed one-way sequence. On the writing side, it extends the plan-then-write lineage of LongWriter (Bai et al., 2025) and CogWriter (Wan et al., 2025) by replacing brute-force full-context generation with a memory-bank-based, citation-driven hierarchical retrieval that prunes context per section.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-context bottleneck in deep research can be addressed at the system level (memory bank + targeted retrieval) rather than requiring ever-larger model contexts, and that the resulting agentic skills are transferable via SFT to a 30B model (citation accuracy jumping from 25% to 86%). A key open question is whether this capability can be cascaded to substantially smaller models, since even 30B remains a significant compute barrier. The framework's reliance on a powerful proprietary model (Claude-sonnet-4) for data generation and its higher wall-clock cost (~29 min, ~$2.82/task) relative to Gemini's ~16 min also limit immediate practical adoption despite the quality gains.
