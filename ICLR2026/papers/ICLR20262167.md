## Problem

Deep generative models like VAEs and IWAEs typically require 32–128 latent dimensions for competitive reconstruction quality, forcing practitioners to apply post-hoc dimensionality reduction (UMAP, t-SNE) and making interpretability analyses like clustering and density estimation nontrivial. This paper asks whether the variational encoder and its associated lower bound are actually necessary in the extreme low-dimensional regime (1–3D), or whether direct numerical integration of the marginal likelihood—long dismissed as impractical—can serve as a viable training objective.

## Input / Output

The model consumes high-dimensional data points (28×28 MNIST digits, 80×80 grayscale Celeb-A faces, spectrogram representations of bird/gerbil vocalizations, or 10-frame stacks of 44-joint motion-capture poses) and produces a d-dimensional embedding (d ∈ {1,2,3}) in a periodic unit hypercube [0,1)^d. Because there is no encoder, the embedding of a held-out point is obtained by evaluating the decoder at all m lattice points and normalizing the resulting likelihoods via Bayes' rule under the uniform prior, yielding a discrete posterior over the lattice. The model also exposes the full aggregated posterior density and the decoder Jacobian over the latent grid, enabling downstream analyses (mean-shift clustering, geodesic computation, smoothness profiling) that are intractable in higher dimensions.

## Method

QLVMs replace the VAE encoder and ELBO with a randomized quasi-Monte Carlo (RQMC) estimate of the marginal log-likelihood: the training objective is the log-sum-exp of log p_θ(x_i | z̃_j) over m jointly sampled lattice points z̃_1,…,z̃_m, where the lattice is a randomly shifted Fibonacci grid (2D) or Korobov grid (3D) on [0,1)^d. The decoder's first layer is fixed to z ↦ (sin z, cos z) to enforce periodic boundary conditions that match the lattice's optimality properties, and the prior is uniform. Because the same randomly shifted lattice can be reused across all elements of a minibatch, QLVMs achieve m samples per data point at the cost of only one lattice evaluation per batch—unlike IWAEs, where each data point requires its own proposal-specific samples. At evaluation, the posterior p(z|x) is approximated as a normalized mixture of m Dirac masses weighted by p_θ(x|z̃_j), and the decoder Jacobian can be queried on a dense grid to reveal cluster boundaries and embedding smoothness.

## Results

Across MNIST, Celeb-A, and zebra finch vocalizations, 2D QLVMs achieve higher marginal log likelihood estimates than 2D VAEs and 2D IWAEs (Fig. 2A), with the gap between VAE/IWAE ELBOs and the true QMC estimate being statistically significant (all p < 0.05, Bonferroni-corrected one-sided binomial tests). On the performance-vs-computational-cost Pareto front (Fig. 4), 2D QLVMs dominate: all tested 2D VAE and IWAE configurations fall above or to the right of the QLVM curve, indicating inferior tradeoffs. On the 3dShapes dataset, the 2D QLVM embedding smoothly encodes three of six continuous latent factors (wall hue, floor hue, object hue) as continua, whereas 2D UMAP artificially fragments the data into spurious clusters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 2D VAEs, QLVMs win on marginal log likelihood, reconstruction quality, and sample diversity across all three primary datasets (MNIST, Celeb-A, zebra finch); the paper attributes the VAE gap to the encoder's inability to approximate the true posterior in 2D, producing loose ELBOs (Fig. 3A–B). Against 2D IWAEs (m=10 importance samples), QLVMs similarly outperform on the same metrics and additionally enjoy a computational advantage: the randomly shifted lattice can be reused across all batch elements, whereas IWAEs must draw separate samples per datapoint, constraining them to m = s/b samples per point. Against UMAP on 3dShapes, QLVMs preserve the continuous structure of latent factors (hue, scale, orientation) while UMAP hallucinates discrete clusters, a known failure mode of manifold-learning methods. Against iterative amortized inference (Marino et al., 2018), QLVMs train considerably faster and achieve comparable or better 2D MNIST performance (Appendix F). Higher-dimensional VAEs (8D+) do outperform 2D QLVMs quantitatively on complex datasets like Celeb-A, but the qualitative gap narrows substantially on simpler datasets like MNIST and zebra finch vocalizations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard variational inference paradigm in deep latent variable models by eliminating the encoder network entirely and instead using randomized quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) lattice integration to directly approximate the marginal likelihood. Conceptually, QLVMs are positioned as a special case of importance weighted autoencoders (IWAEs) with a fixed proposal distribution (the prior), but the practical difference is significant: no encoder to train, tune, or debug. The numerical integration machinery is drawn from the QMC literature (Korobov and Fibonacci lattice rules), which had previously been applied only marginally to deep generative models (e.g., Buchholz et al. 2018 for variance reduction in ELBO estimates, Andral 2024 for normalizing flows). The paper's key conceptual move is to show that the 'simple-minded' Monte Carlo estimator dismissed as impractical by Doersch (2016) becomes viable precisely in the very low-dimensional regime (1–3D) where interpretability is most desired.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QLVMs offer a principled alternative to heuristic dimensionality reduction methods (UMAP, t-SNE) that do not learn an explicit generative process, enabling transparent post-hoc analyses—nonparametric clustering, geodesic path computation, Jacobian-based smoothness characterization—that become nontrivial or impossible in higher-dimensional latent spaces. The finding that VAE and IWAE variational posteriors are systematically poorly matched to true posteriors in 2D spaces explains a practical failure mode that practitioners encounter but rarely diagnose. Key limitations include the curse of dimensionality (the method does not scale beyond ~3D latent spaces), reduced sample quality on complex high-resolution datasets, and the unresolved identifiability question of how non-unique embeddings affect downstream analyses. Future work on adaptive importance sampling initialized from lattices and conditional QLVMs with domain-specific covariates could address some of these gaps.
