## Problem

MeanFlow achieves strong few-step generation but its success is not well understood: it empirically requires 75% of training compute on a border-case flow matching supervision (r=t) that seems unrelated to its primary goal of learning average velocities over [r,t] intervals. This paper reveals that the MeanFlow objective decomposes into trajectory flow matching and trajectory consistency terms whose gradients are strongly negatively correlated, causing optimization conflict and slow convergence, and asks whether this conflict can be resolved more efficiently without the heavy border-case overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264893/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a random Gaussian noise vector z_1 ~ N(0,I) in the SD-VAE latent space and an optional class label (for classifier-free guidance) as input, and produces a generated image at 256×256 or a video at 17×256×256 frames. Generation is performed in 1 or 2 function evaluations (NFE), with 2-NFE using either ODE sampling or consistency sampling at a chosen intermediate timestep. The DiT backbone operates entirely in the VAE latent space, so the actual pixel-space output is decoded by the frozen SD-VAE decoder.

## Method

The paper introduces the α-Flow loss, a single formulation parameterized by α ∈ (0,1] that unifies trajectory flow matching (α=1), Shortcut Models (α=1/2), and MeanFlow (α→0) by controlling the position of an intermediate timestep s = α·r + (1−α)·t within the [r,t] interval. The key training strategy is a three-phase curriculum: (1) pretrain with α=1 (pure trajectory flow matching) to quickly converge to the narrow TFM manifold, (2) smoothly anneal α from 1 to 0 via a sigmoid schedule to transition toward the MeanFlow objective, and (3) fine-tune at α→0 (full MeanFlow with JVP). This curriculum disentangles the conflicting gradients between trajectory flow matching and trajectory consistency, reducing the need for the 75% border-case flow matching supervision that MeanFlow relies on. The model uses a vanilla DiT backbone with an adaptive loss weight ω = α/(‖Δ‖²+c), classifier-free guidance integrated into the shift velocity, and consistency sampling for 2-NFE generation on larger models.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet-1K 256×256, α-Flow-XL/2+ (240+60 epochs) achieves a new state-of-the-art FID of 2.58 (1-NFE) and 2.15 (2-NFE) with vanilla DiT backbones, outperforming MeanFlow-XL/2+ (1000 epochs, FID 2.20 at 2-NFE) with only 30% of the training budget. At the same 240-epoch budget, α-Flow-XL/2 reaches FID 2.95 (1-NFE) and 2.34 (2-NFE), a 15% relative improvement over MeanFlow-XL/2 (FID 3.47 / 2.46). On Kinetics-700 17×256² video generation, α-Flow-B/2-cfg improves over MeanFlow-B/2-cfg by 2.7 FID (1-NFE) and 1.7 FID (2-NFE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline MeanFlow-XL/2 at the same 240-epoch budget, α-Flow-XL/2 improves 1-NFE FID from 3.47 to 2.95 (15% relative) and 2-NFE FID from 2.46 to 2.34. The fine-tuned α-Flow-XL/2+ (300 epochs total) surpasses MeanFlow-XL/2+ (1000 epochs) at 2-NFE (FID 2.15 vs. 2.20) using only 30% of the training compute. Against FACM-XL/2, α-Flow-XL/2+ achieves FID† 1.95 at 2-NFE versus FACM's 2.07, while requiring only 23% of FACM's training epochs (300 vs. ~1300 effective). At the smaller DiT-B/2 scale, α-Flow-B/2 consistently beats MeanFlow-B/2 (FID 5.40 vs. 6.04 at 1-NFE; 5.01 vs. 5.17 at 2-NFE). The gap over earlier few-step methods is substantial: Shortcut-XL/2 (FID 10.60), IMM-XL/2 (FID 8.05), and CT-XL/2 (FID 7.44) all trail α-Flow-XL/2 (FID 2.95) by more than 5 FID points at 1-NFE. On Kinetics-700 video, α-Flow-B/2-cfg outperforms MeanFlow-B/2-cfg across all metrics (FID 29.6 vs. 32.3 at 1-NFE; FVD 217.3 vs. 249.6 at 2-NFE), confirming generalization beyond images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2025a), decomposing its training objective into trajectory flow matching and trajectory consistency components, and revealing a strong negative gradient correlation between them that explains MeanFlow's optimization difficulties. The proposed α-Flow loss provides a unified formulation that subsumes trajectory flow matching (α=1), Shortcut Models (α=1/2), and MeanFlow (α→0) as special cases, while also recovering discrete and continuous consistency training under a z₀-reparameterization. The curriculum strategy of annealing α from 1 to 0 is inspired by discrete CT scheduling but is motivated here by the gradient-conflict analysis rather than discretization error reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-conflict analysis provides the first clear theoretical explanation for why MeanFlow works and why its 75% border-case flow matching supervision is necessary, resolving a key open question in few-step generative modeling. The α-Flow framework offers a practical path to reduce reliance on this expensive surrogate loss while maintaining or improving quality, and the SOTA results (FID 2.58 at 1-NFE) with vanilla DiT backbones establish a new reference point. Open questions remain: the gradient analysis is empirical rather than fully theoretical, the α→0 limit still requires JVP computation, and the paper acknowledges occasional training instability with classifier-free guidance at scale. Whether the curriculum strategy generalizes to other consistency-based frameworks beyond the Euler-method family is unexplored.
