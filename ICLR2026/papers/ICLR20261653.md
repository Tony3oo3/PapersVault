## Problem

Gradient projection methods for continual learning partition the gradient space of previous tasks into major and minor subspaces, but existing approaches (e.g., InfLoRA) determine the minor subspace size using a single fixed threshold applied uniformly across all model modules. This ignores the heterogeneous contribution of different layers to knowledge retention, failing to achieve an optimal stability-plasticity trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261653/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For vision tasks, the method takes RGB images (ImageNet-R 200 classes, CIFAR-100, DomainNet, etc.) and outputs class labels, operating on a frozen ViT-Base backbone pre-trained on ImageNet-21K with LoRA modules (rank 10, embedding dim 768) inserted into key and value projections of multi-head attention. For NLP tasks, it processes text prompts through T5-Large or LLaMA2-7B and produces generated text or classification scores. The method requires one extra forward pass per task to compute the average gradient of old tasks for subspace decomposition.

## Method

SplitLoRA derives a theoretical upper bound on total loss growth that decomposes into a stability term (proportional to ε(k), the fraction of singular mass in the minor subspace) and a plasticity term (proportional to k/d, the fraction of directions available for learning). It then solves an optimization problem per LoRA module to find the optimal minor subspace dimension k* = argmin_k ((t−1)ε_t(k) − α·k/d), where α is a fixed hyperparameter controlling the stability-plasticity trade-off. To enforce the constraint, it constructs the LoRA matrix A_t = U_t^k · R (where U_t^k is the orthonormal basis of the minor subspace from SVD of the old-task gradient and R is a random Gaussian matrix), fixes A_t during training, and optimizes only B_t. This confines all parameter updates to the minor subspace, ensuring new-task learning minimally interferes with previously acquired knowledge while still providing sufficient capacity for adaptation.

## Results

On ImageNet-R (200 classes), SplitLoRA achieves 81.92% FAA and 85.83% CAA in the 5-task setting, 81.00% FAA and 85.84% CAA in the 10-task setting, and 78.82% FAA and 84.57% CAA in the 20-task setting, consistently outperforming the strongest baselines (VPT-NSP², InfLoRA, VQ-Prompt) by 2–5% in FAA. On CIFAR-100 (10 tasks), SplitLoRA reaches 90.33% FAA and 93.70% CAA, and on DomainNet (5 tasks) it achieves 84.31% FAA and 88.99% CAA. Across seven diverse benchmarks (CIFAR-100, CUB200, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A, ObjectNet, Omnibenchmark, VTAB), SplitLoRA attains the best average score of 79.95%, surpassing the next-best method RanPAC (79.17%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplitLoRA outperforms all 24 baselines across every dataset and task setting evaluated. On ImageNet-R, the closest competitors are VPT-NSP² (79.71/79.35/76.72 FAA for 5/10/20 tasks) and InfLoRA (79.82/78.10/73.81 FAA); SplitLoRA leads by 2.2–5.0% FAA, with the largest gap (5.0%) appearing in the 20-task setting where forgetting is most severe. On CIFAR-100, SplitLoRA (90.33% FAA) surpasses CoSO (88.76%) and VPT-NSP² (88.04%) by 1.6–2.3%; on DomainNet it leads VPT-NSP² (83.83%) by 0.5% FAA but by a much larger 8.9% in CAA over CoSO (80.05%). In the multi-benchmark evaluation (Table 10), SplitLoRA achieves the best average (79.95%) over RanPAC (79.17%) and EASE (78.39%), with particularly strong gains on ImageNet-R (+6.0% over RanPAC) and VTAB (+1.83% over EASE). For language models on SuperNI (T5-Large), SplitLoRA reaches 51.64/52.89 AP with only 1.14/0.73 forgetting, beating Gain-OLoRA (47.84/46.84 AP) and InfLoRA (39.78/39.57 AP) by 4–13% in AP while reducing forgetting by 50–85%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplitLoRA sits at the intersection of two active research threads: gradient projection for continual learning (GPM, Saha et al. 2021) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA (Hu et al. 2022). Its most direct predecessor is InfLoRA (Liang & Li, CVPR 2024), which fixes the LoRA projection matrix A to a subspace basis determined by a uniform singular-value threshold τ across all modules. SplitLoRA departs from this by replacing the heuristic threshold with a per-module optimization derived from a theoretical stability-plasticity loss bound (Theorem 4.2), allowing each LoRA module to select its own minor subspace dimension. The orthogonal decomposition machinery and the fixed-A / trainable-B design are inherited from InfLoRA and GPM, while the optimization objective and the random-projection construction of A_t are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The per-module subspace allocation addresses a concrete weakness of prior gradient-projection CL methods: a single global threshold cannot account for heterogeneous knowledge contributions across layers, as the strategy-transplantation experiment (Table 7) demonstrates by showing that fixing only layer 0's subspace nearly matches full SplitLoRA and halves forgetting. Open questions remain: the uniform-distribution assumption on ΔW_t is a simplification that may not hold in practice, the minor subspace shrinks toward zero as the number of tasks grows (raising scalability concerns for very long sequences), and the method is validated primarily on ViT-Base with LoRA rank 10, leaving open whether the optimization framework transfers cleanly to other architectures or higher-rank settings. The fixed α hyperparameter, while shown to be robust, still requires a practitioner to choose a value that balances stability and plasticity.
