## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting and its variants approximate 3D Gaussians as 2D conics via local affine (EWA) projection, introducing substantial errors under wide-FoV and fisheye cameras where nonlinear distortions make the true projection far from a symmetric 2D Gaussian. Ray-based methods eliminate this projective approximation but rely on costly BVH traversal for ray-particle association, preventing real-time performance. No prior method simultaneously achieves geometric exactness in projective geometry and splatting-level rendering speed for arbitrary camera models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes camera intrinsics (pinhole or Kannala-Brandt fisheye distortion parameters) and extrinsics, along with a trained 3D Gaussian scene parameterized by per-Gaussian mean, scaling vector, rotation quaternion, spherical-harmonic color coefficients, and opacity. Rays are sampled uniformly in spherical angular coordinates (θ, φ) under the BEAP representation and mapped to a discrete w×h image via a linear scaling-and-shift; for fisheye datasets, rendered outputs are reprojected to the native KB-Fisheye pixel grid before evaluation. The output is a rendered novel-view RGB image at the target camera's FoV and resolution.

## Method

3DGEER derives a closed-form, differentiable transmittance by mapping each anisotropic 3D Gaussian into a shared canonical isotropic space via the RS transformation, reducing the ray integral to σ·exp(−D²/2) where D is the perpendicular Mahalanobis distance from the ray to the Gaussian center—revealing the rigorous basis for the previously heuristic 'maximum response' assumption. For efficient ray-particle association without BVH, the method introduces the Particle Bounding Frustum (PBF): for each Gaussian, it solves a pair of quadratic equations (enforcing tangency of angular planes to the λ-sigma ellipsoid in canonical space) to obtain tight angular bounds [θ₁,θ₂]×[φ₁,φ₂], then intersects these frustums with Camera Sub-Frustums (CSFs) in a manner analogous to tile-AABB mapping in 3DGS, yielding ~3–5× fewer Gaussian-tile associations than EWA or UT. A mirror-angle transformation handles cases where PBF bounds fall outside the monotonic domain of tan under extreme FoV. Finally, the Bipolar Equiangular Projection (BEAP) samples rays uniformly in (θ,φ) and maps them linearly to pixel space, providing more balanced 3D spatial coverage than pinhole or equidistant sampling and improving reconstruction quality, particularly at image peripheries.

## Results

On ScanNet++ (180° fisheye), 3DGEER achieves 31.50 PSNR / 0.953 SSIM / 0.126 LPIPS, outperforming 3DGUT (30.64), EVER (29.47), and FisheyeGS (27.81). On MipNeRF360 (pinhole), it reaches 27.76 PSNR / 0.821 SSIM / 0.210 LPIPS at 327 FPS—5× faster than EVER (36 FPS) and 3DGRT (52–68 FPS) while matching 3DGS speed (343 FPS). On ZipNeRF cross-camera generalization, 3DGEER leads in all four train/test configurations, with the largest margin in the pinhole-to-fisheye setting (23.39 vs. 18.61 PSNR for 3DGUT). The paper reports 0.9–4.8 PSNR gains under wide-FoV conditions and 0.3–2.0 on pinhole views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023), 3DGEER wins on MipNeRF360 by 0.55 PSNR (27.76 vs. 27.21) and 0.004 SSIM while running at 327 vs. 343 FPS—essentially matching speed. Against FisheyeGS (Liao et al., 2024), 3DGEER achieves a 3.69 PSNR gap on ScanNet++ (31.50 vs. 27.81) and 3.06 on ZipNeRF fisheye-to-fisheye (26.24 vs. 23.18), with the advantage widening at image peripheries. Against EVER (Mai et al., 2025), 3DGEER gains 2.03 PSNR on ScanNet++ and 0.25 on MipNeRF360, while being 5× faster (327 vs. 36 FPS) due to PBF-based association replacing BVH traversal. Against 3DGUT (Wu et al., 2025), 3DGEER leads by 0.86 PSNR on ScanNet++ and 0.50 on MipNeRF360, with the gap driven by elimination of the Unscented Transform approximation in association. Against 3DGRT (Moenne-Loccoz et al., 2024), 3DGEER improves PSNR by 0.56 on MipNeRF360 (27.76 vs. 27.20) and runs 5–6× faster (327 vs. 52/68 FPS). In the cross-camera ZipNeRF setting, 3DGEER is the only method that maintains strong performance when trained on pinhole and tested on fisheye (23.39 PSNR), where 3DGUT drops to 18.61 and FisheyeGS to 19.43.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of 3D Gaussian rendering: splatting-based methods (3DGS, FisheyeGS, GS++) that are fast but rely on local affine (EWA) approximations of the projection, and ray-based methods (3DGRT, EVER, 3DGUT) that are geometrically exact but suffer from BVH traversal overhead. The key theoretical contribution is showing that the 'maximum response' heuristic from Keselman & Hebert (2022), used as an assumption in 3DGRT, is in fact the exact closed-form integral of Gaussian density along a ray—derived here from first principles via a canonical isotropic transformation. The practical contribution (PBF association + BEAP projection) then closes the efficiency gap, making the exact formulation competitive with splatting in speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously achieve projective exactness and real-time rendering speed for arbitrary camera models (pinhole, fisheye, omnidirectional), which is critical for robotics and autonomous driving applications where wide-FoV cameras are standard. The demonstration that brute-force scaling of Gaussian counts (up to 5M) cannot close the projective approximation gap challenges the recent argument by Celarek et al. (2025) and clarifies that the error is structural, not a matter of representation capacity. Remaining limitations include depth-based ordering (no analytic self-occlusion resolution) and the absence of self-attenuation modeling, which the authors identify as orthogonal future work. The BEAP representation and PBF solver are likely to be adopted by subsequent work on generic-camera Gaussian rendering.
