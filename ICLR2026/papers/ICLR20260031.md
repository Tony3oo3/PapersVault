## Problem

Classical Safe Policy Improvement (SPI) guarantees are restricted to offline, tabular MDPs and do not extend to online, high-dimensional settings with learned representations and world models. Two critical failure modes remain unaddressed in practice: out-of-trajectory (OOT) errors where the world model hallucinates in underexplored regions, and confounding policy updates where simultaneous changes to the policy and its encoder lock the agent into suboptimal behavior. The paper asks whether principled, provably safe policy improvement can be achieved in general state spaces when both a world model and a representation are learned jointly.

## Input / Output

The agent receives 4 stacked RGB frames from ALE environments (standard Atari resolution) as raw pixel observations, which are encoded by a CNN into a latent representation. The latent policy outputs a distribution over discrete actions, while the world model predicts both a reward scalar and a transition distribution (mixture of 5 multivariate normals) over the latent space. All three components—encoder, world model, and policy—are updated simultaneously on-policy from fresh interaction data collected via 128 vectorized environments over 8-step horizons, with no replay buffer.

## Method

DeepSPI constrains policy updates to a trust region defined by bounding the importance ratio between the new and current policy to [2−C, C] (a neighborhood operator N^C), which the paper proves is a valid mirror learning neighborhood yielding monotonic improvement and convergence. The key mechanism replaces PPO's advantage A with a utility U = A − α_R·ℓ_R − α_P·ℓ_P, where ℓ_R is the per-step reward prediction error and ℓ_P is the per-step transition error (Wasserstein distance between the pushforward of true transitions and the model's predicted transition in latent space). This single clipped objective simultaneously improves the policy, refines the world model, and enforces representation quality (states with distinct values remain separated in latent space) while keeping updates within the safe neighborhood. The world model's reward and transition networks use norm-constrained GroupSort architectures to enforce 1-Lipschitzness, and the transition function is a 5-component mixture of multivariate normals. The theoretical contribution links local transition/reward losses to a bound ζ on the return gap between the world model and the true environment, guaranteeing that minimizing these losses under the importance-ratio constraint yields safe policy improvement (Theorem 3) and almost-Lipschitz value functions in the latent space (Theorem 4).

## Results

On the stochastic ALE-57 benchmark (sticky actions p_a=0.3, n_NOOP=60), DeepSPI achieves higher aggregate mean, median, and interquartile mean (IQM) scores than both PPO and DeepMDP+PPO, while also reducing the optimality gap (Fig. 5). DeepSPI consistently achieves lower median transition losses L_P during training compared to baselines (Fig. 7), indicating more accurate world-model predictions. In the illustrative toy maze, PPO collapses the two critical states into one latent representation and plateaus at a return of ~4.8, whereas DeepSPI separates them and reaches ~8. Sample-efficiency curves (Fig. 6) show DeepSPI reaching higher IQM-normalized scores faster than both baselines across training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DeepSPI against two baselines on stochastic ALE-57: (1) PPO (vectorized cleanRL) and (2) DeepMDP losses plugged into PPO (Gelada et al., 2019). DeepSPI outperforms both on aggregate mean, median, IQM, and optimality gap metrics (Fig. 5), and reaches higher IQM-normalized scores at every point in training (Fig. 6). Against PPO, DeepSPI's advantage comes from the auxiliary transition/reward losses that improve the learned representation while the PPO-style clipped objective keeps updates within the safe neighborhood. Against DeepMDP+PPO, DeepSPI wins because DeepMDP's unconstrained representation updates can push the policy outside the trust region, violating the SPI guarantees; DeepSPI instead incorporates the losses directly into the clipped utility (Eq. 6), preserving the neighborhood constraint. Notably, DeepSPI also achieves lower transition losses than DeepMDP+PPO (Fig. 7), suggesting the on-policy, neighborhood-constrained training yields a more accurate world model. A naive model-based variant (DreamSPI) that plans in the learned world model underperforms the baselines in aggregate, confirming that on-policy execution remains necessary for competitive scores in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classical Safe Policy Improvement (SPI) from the offline, tabular RL literature (Thomas et al. 2015; Laroche et al. 2019) to online, high-dimensional settings with learned representations, replacing global state-action coverage requirements with local, on-policy transition and reward losses. The neighborhood operator constraining policy updates via importance ratios is shown to be an instance of mirror learning (Kuba et al. 2022), and its practical implementation connects directly to PPO's clipped objective (Schulman et al. 2017). The auxiliary losses generalize those from DeepMDP (Gelada et al. 2019), but the key departure is that they are incorporated into the policy gradient utility rather than added as separate objectives, preserving the neighborhood constraint during joint representation and policy updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical framework that simultaneously controls world-model accuracy, representation quality, and policy improvement in the online deep RL setting, directly addressing out-of-trajectory hallucinations and confounding policy updates that plague model-based RL. The DreamSPI variant (planning in the learned world model) remains below baseline performance, highlighting that on-policy model-based planning is still sample-inefficient and a significant open problem. The theoretical guarantees also open a path toward safety-critical applications via formal methods (shielding, synthesis), though the current empirical gains over PPO are modest and the on-policy requirement limits practical applicability compared to off-policy alternatives.
