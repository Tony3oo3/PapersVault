## Problem

Object-level manipulation in images and videos faces a fundamental trade-off: geometry-based methods (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting, explicit mesh pipelines) provide precise viewpoint control but require per-scene 3D reconstruction and generalize poorly to in-the-wild content, while diffusion-based editors generalize well but lack fine-grained geometric control over object pose. No existing framework achieves both precise, user-controllable geometric transformations and strong real-world generalization without explicit 3D modeling at inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes source video frames (T×3×640×960), a reference object image cropped from the first source frame, binary source and estimated target masks, and an 8D relative camera-pose descriptor (axis-angle rotation, translation, and two NDC shifts). At inference the target mask is not available as ground truth; it is estimated by bounding-box tightening, distance-ratio scaling, NDC shifting, and frame truncation. The model outputs target frames at the same resolution with the object relocated and reoriented, or clean background frames in the object-removal sub-task. Masks are encoded via pixel-unshuffle (space-to-depth) rather than the VAE to preserve their binary edit-region semantics and avoid confusion with RGB appearance.

## Method

Ctrl&Shift decomposes object manipulation into two sub-tasks—object removal and reference-guided inpainting under explicit camera pose—and unifies them in a single ControlNet-style DiT (Wan-1.3B backbone, hidden size 1536, 8 zero-initialized control blocks) trained with flow-matching. Five conditioning signals are disentangled: source frames and reference image enter via VAE-encoded latents in the control branch, masks enter via stride-aligned pixel-unshuffle, and the 8D relative pose descriptor is mapped through Fourier positional encoding and a 3-layer MLP into 8 cross-attention tokens (d=4096) that guide viewpoint without corrupting identity. A multi-task training scheme (main manipulation : removal : reference inpainting = 8:1:1) forces each signal to play a distinct, interpretable role. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage I pretrains on ~2M synthetic pairs (Hunyuan3D-2 meshes rendered under randomized poses on white backgrounds) to instill object priors and camera representations, then Stage II fine-tunes on ~100K curated real-world pairs with the main branch frozen and only the control branch updated to preserve geometric understanding while learning photorealistic background harmonization. A scalable data pipeline reconstructs object meshes, estimates source camera poses via differentiable rasterization (IoU ≥ 0.90 threshold), samples target poses, and composites rendered objects onto backgrounds using a learned object-pasting model for seamless lighting and boundary blending.

## Results

On GeoEditBench, Ctrl&Shift achieves PSNR 28.71, DINO 85.23, CLIP 90.44, DreamSim 0.112, Pose MAPE 17.70%, and Obj IoU 0.83, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On the ObjectMover-A benchmark (zero-shot), it reaches PSNR 28.69, DINO 88.07, CLIP 93.58, and DreamSim 0.075, surpassing the previous best (ObjectMover) by 3.42 dB in PSNR and 0.067 in DreamSim. The method is the only one supporting both translation and rotation with the lowest pose error (17.70% MAPE vs. 24.36% for the next-best Nano-Banana).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ctrl&Shift outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric in both benchmarks. The closest competitor, Nano-Banana (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image), trails by 2.33 dB in PSNR, 7.18 in DINO, 4.81 in CLIP, 0.033 in DreamSim, 6.66% in Pose MAPE, and 0.05 in Obj IoU on GeoEditBench. VACE, which uses bounding-box trajectories, achieves reasonable background preservation (PSNR 24.32) but cannot perform rotation and has 30.56% pose error. Methods relying solely on text/coordinate prompts (3DiT, Flux-Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit) show the weakest spatial accuracy, with Pose MAPE ranging from 39.56% to 51.62%. DragAnything, despite supporting translation, produces the worst background fidelity (PSNR 17.65) and cannot handle rotation. On ObjectMover-A, Ctrl&Shift leads ObjectMover by 3.42 dB PSNR and halves the DreamSim distance (0.075 vs. 0.142), confirming superior identity preservation under translation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ctrl&Shift sits at the intersection of two lines of work: geometry-based 3D-aware editing (3DiT, Diffusion Handles, BlenderFusion) that achieves multi-view consistency through explicit reconstruction, and diffusion-based image/video editing (DragAnything, VACE, Flux-Kontext) that generalizes well but lacks precise geometric control. The paper departs from both by injecting an 8D relative camera-pose descriptor directly into a ControlNet-style DiT (built on Wan-1.3B) via cross-attention, eliminating the need for 3D representations at inference while retaining fine-grained viewpoint control. The multi-task decomposition into object removal, reference-guided inpainting, and full manipulation mirrors the semantic structure of the editing task, and the training objective follows the flow-matching formulation of Lipman et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to achieve precise camera-pose-controlled object manipulation on real-world content without any explicit 3D modeling at inference, bridging a long-standing trade-off between geometric rigor and generative flexibility. The introduction of GeoEditBench (346 curated pairs with verified camera poses) provides the community a standardized evaluation for geometry-aware editing that prior benchmarks lacked. Key open questions remain: the pipeline is restricted to rigid objects (no cloth, hair, or transparent materials), physical realism (dynamic shadows, specular reflections) is learned implicitly rather than modeled, and video manipulation lags behind image editing due to non-rigid deformation. The 8D pose descriptor, while precise, is not yet user-friendly, and mapping intuitive 2D/3D interactions to it is identified as a critical next step.
