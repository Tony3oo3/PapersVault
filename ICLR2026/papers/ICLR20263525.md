## Problem

Existing subcellular localization benchmarks like DeepLoc provide only amino acid sequences with coarse 10-class compartment labels, making it impossible to evaluate structure-based protein models or discover fine-grained biological localization patterns. This gap severely limits the application of promising 3D-structure-aware methods to subcellular localization prediction and hinders interpretable, organelle-specific biological discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263525/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each protein in CAPSUL is represented by three structural modalities: the full amino acid sequence, Cα Cartesian coordinates extracted from AlphaFold2-predicted structures (with fragmented sliding-window predictions excluded), and 3Di structural tokens generated by FoldSeek for compact local-structure encoding. The output is a 20-dimensional multi-label vector indicating presence/absence in each fine-grained compartment (e.g., distinguishing nuclear membrane, nucleoli, and nucleoplasm separately), with each annotation carrying an evidence-level flag (experimental, non-experimental, or unverified) sourced from UniProt and HPA. The dataset contains 20,181 human proteins split 70:15:15, with an average of 2.51 localization annotations per protein and up to 14.

## Method

CAPSUL is constructed by cross-referencing AlphaFold2 structures with UniProt and HPA localization annotations, then unifying terminology into 20 fine-grained compartments validated by domain experts. For evaluation, the paper extends GCN-based structure encoders (CDConv, GearNet-Edge) with Transformer layers to enable attention-based interpretability, and additionally adapts Graph Transformer, Graph Mamba, and Graph Diffusion architectures to the task. Key training strategies include focal loss with log-inverse frequency reweighting to mitigate severe class imbalance (minority classes as low as 0.5% positive rate), single-label binary classifiers for underrepresented compartments, contrastive loss to pull same-compartment embeddings together, and early/late fusion of ESM-C sequence representations with CDConv structural features. Ablation studies replacing true Cα coordinates with random samples within each protein's spatial bounding box confirm that 3D geometry is decisive for prediction, and attention visualization on Golgi apparatus targets reveals a consistent α-helix transmembrane domain pattern concordant with prior experimental evidence.

## Results

On CAPSUL (20,181 human proteins, 20 subcellular compartments), ESM-C 600M achieves the best overall Micro Avg F1-score of 0.495 and Macro Avg F1-score of 0.263 among all evaluated methods. Among pure structure-based models, CDConv with Transformer layers achieves the highest Micro Avg F1-score of 0.452, while the ESM-C+CDConv Late Fusion model reaches 0.476. An ablation study confirms the critical role of 3D structure: randomly sampling Cα coordinates drops CDConv's Micro F1 from 0.452 to 0.329 and GearNet-Edge's from 0.417 to 0.348, and both structure-based models substantially outperform the randomly-initialized ESM-C 600M (Micro F1 0.338).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESM-C 600M (Micro F1 0.495) is the strongest single model, outperforming the best structure-based method CDConv† (0.452) by ~4.3 points, largely due to its extensive pre-training on 250M+ sequences. However, CDConv† and GearNet-Edge† (0.417) both substantially exceed the randomly-initialized ESM-C 600M (0.338), demonstrating that 3D structural information provides predictive signal beyond what a non-pretrained sequence model captures. Among structure-based baselines, CDConv† leads GearNet-Edge† by 3.5 Micro F1 points, while Graph Diffusion (0.424) and Graph Mamba (0.411) trail CDConv† by 2.8 and 4.1 points respectively. FoldSeek, which uses only 3Di structural tokens without sequence information, performs worst (0.248), suggesting that coarse structural tokenization alone is insufficient. The ESM-C+CDConv Late Fusion model (0.476) narrows the gap to ESM-C 600M to just 1.9 points and achieves the best Micro Recall (0.381) among all methods, indicating that integrating structural features into a pretrained sequence model is a promising direction. On minority classes (e.g., Golgi Apparatus, Endosome, Primary Cilium), structure-based models with reweighting or single-label training show notable improvements over the multi-label baselines, with GearNet-Edge achieving F1 0.210 on Golgi Apparatus in single-label mode versus 0.026 in multi-label.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: sequence-based protein language models (ESM-2, ESM-C, DeepLoc) and structure-based geometric deep learning (CDConv, GearNet-Edge, Graph Transformer, Graph Mamba, Graph Diffusion). The key gap it addresses is that the only widely adopted subcellular localization benchmark (DeepLoc) provides only amino acid sequences with coarse 10-class labels, making it impossible to evaluate the promising structure-based models that have succeeded in other protein tasks. CAPSUL bridges this by integrating AlphaFold2 Cα coordinates and FoldSeek 3Di tokens alongside fine-grained 20-class annotations cross-referenced from UniProt and HPA, and extends existing GCN-based encoders with Transformer layers for interpretability while introducing contrastive learning and sequence-structure fusion strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAPSUL is the first benchmark to make structure-based subcellular localization prediction feasible at scale, unblocking an entire class of methods that were previously unevaluable on this task. The fine-grained 20-class scheme with evidence-level annotations enables both high-throughput prediction and rigorous hypothesis-driven studies (e.g., the Golgi α-helix case study). Open questions remain: whether causal links between specific 3D structural motifs and localization can be established, how to best align the three structural representations (sequence, Cα, 3Di), and whether the modest performance gap between structure-based and pre-trained sequence models can be closed with better architectural integration. The class imbalance problem for rare compartments (lipid droplet, peroxisome) remains largely unresolved despite reweighting and single-label strategies.
