## Problem

LLMs exhibit strong multi-document reasoning but their evidence identification is highly sensitive to input order, with attention heads overemphasizing sequence boundaries and neglecting central content. It remains unclear which specific attention heads are responsible for robust, position-invariant evidence integration, and whether such heads can be reliably identified and leveraged to improve document ranking and reduce hallucination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260945/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query, two gold documents, and eight distractor documents (10 candidates total), constructing m+1 input permutations per query by varying gold document insertion positions among distractors. Attention maps are extracted from all heads across all layers under two sources: Query-as-Source (query tokens attending to documents) and Response-as-Source (generated response tokens attending to documents). The output is a set of Expert Head indices (layer, head) identified via activation frequency and attention-score thresholds, which are then used to produce a ranked document list through a voting scheme across five selected heads.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-stage identification framework for Expert Heads. First, a head is marked as 'Activated' for a given permutation if it allocates more attention to every gold document than to every distractor simultaneously—a stricter criterion than naive top-attention selection that avoids positional bias. Second, activation frequency (proportion of samples where the head is activated) and average attention score (mean attention to gold documents across activated samples) are computed per permutation; a head qualifies as a Sensitive Head if it exceeds both thresholds (frequency > 0.6, top-10% attention percentile) for at least one permutation, and is promoted to an Expert Head only if it satisfies both thresholds across all permutations. For document ranking, each of the five selected Expert Heads independently ranks candidates by its query-to-document attention scores, and final rankings are aggregated via a top-K voting scheme. The analysis reveals architecture-specific layer distributions: mid-layer heads dominate in LLaMA and Mistral, while deeper-layer heads specialize in Qwen, and Expert Heads show concentrated focus during understanding but more distributed engagement during generation.

## Results

Expert Heads (Response-as-Source) from LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct achieve the strongest HotpotQA results: 90.72 P@2, 91.98 NDCG@2, and 95.08 MAP, surpassing the best dense retriever BGE (75.23 P@2) by 15.5 points. On 2WikiMultiHopQA, Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct Expert Heads (R) lead with 77.94 P@2 and 88.29 MAP, and on MuSiQue the same model reaches 82.70 P@2 and 90.11 MAP. Across all three datasets and all three LLMs, Expert Heads consistently outperform every baseline retriever and the direct LLM Rank baseline by 10–30+ points on P@2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Expert Heads decisively outperform all eight retrieval baselines across every dataset and metric. The strongest baseline, BGE, achieves 75.23 P@2 on HotpotQA, while the best Expert Heads configuration (LLaMA, Response) reaches 90.72—a 15.5-point gap. On 2WikiMultiHopQA, BGE scores 77.12 P@2 versus 77.94 for Qwen Expert Heads (R), a narrower but still consistent margin; on MuSiQue the gap widens to 12.5 points (82.70 vs. 70.25). Sparse BM25 and early dense retrievers (DPR, Contriever, ColBERTv2) trail Expert Heads by 25–40 points on P@2. Notably, direct LLM Rank (having the model generate a ranking) is substantially worse than Expert Heads for Mistral (36.45 vs. 88.89 P@2 on HotpotQA) and Qwen (77.81 vs. 88.45), indicating that extracting attention from a small head subset is far more reliable than free-form generation for document ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-head interpretability literature (Jain & Wallace, 2019; Voita et al., 2019; Li et al., 2023b) by introducing a position-robustness criterion: rather than identifying heads with a general functional role (induction, name-mover), it isolates heads that consistently attend to gold documents across all document permutations. It also builds on the 'lost in the middle' line of work (Liu et al., 2023; Press et al., 2021) that documents positional sensitivity in LLMs, but reframes the problem from a failure mode to a diagnostic signal. The practical application—using a handful of identified heads as a voting mechanism for document ranking—repurposes interpretability findings as a lightweight retrieval component, departing from both dense-retrieval pipelines and full-model LLM reranking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that five attention heads out of over a thousand can outperform purpose-built dense retrievers by 10–15 points on multi-hop document identification offers a cheap, interpretable alternative to black-box rerankers and a concrete diagnostic for hallucination (weak/diffuse Expert Head activation correlates with errors). Architecture-specific layer patterns (mid-layer dominance in LLaMA/Mistral vs. deep-layer specialization in Qwen) suggest that evidence-integration circuits are not universal and may need to be re-identified per model family. Open questions include whether Expert Heads generalize beyond the 2-gold/8-distractor setup to longer contexts, whether they transfer across tasks (e.g., summarization, fact verification), and whether head-guided training or pruning can internalize this robustness without explicit permutation-based selection.
