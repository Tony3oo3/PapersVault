## Problem

Pattern matching is widely invoked to explain LLM failures on compositional tasks, yet it lacks a precise, testable definition: behavioral studies conflate it with other generalization sources (algebraic invariances, structural repetition) and diagnose it post hoc rather than characterizing it predictively. As a result, it remains unclear which behaviors constitute pattern matching, how much data suffices for it to succeed, and where its boundary lies.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on fixed-length discrete token sequences (e.g., 3 tokens for 2-HOP, 4 for PARALLEL 2-HOP) drawn from a finite vocabulary of size |X|, with ground-truth mappings randomly constructed to eliminate algebraic shortcuts. Beyond the predicted output token, the method produces a task-agnostic k-coverage set (the boundary of inputs reachable via functionally equivalent substitutions), a power-law scaling exponent relating required training data size to |X|, and Intra-Inter Cosine Gap (IICG) scores quantifying whether intermediate-state representations are unified or context-dependent. The coverage determination is computed algorithmically (Alg. 1) from the training set alone, independent of model architecture.

## Method

The paper formalizes pattern matching as functional k-equivalence: two input subsequences are equivalent if they produce identical outputs in at least k shared contexts, inducing a substitution graph whose connected components define the k-coverage boundary. Within this framework, the authors (1) show that instance-wise generalization success is tightly predicted by the k-cutoff (evidence strength), with Transformers forming latent clusters at specific layers/positions whose coherence scales with k; (2) prove a tight sample-complexity bound N ≳ n^(2.5 − 0.5/k) for perfect in-domain generalization on 2-HOP tasks, verified empirically across 20× parameter scaling (68M–1.5B GPT-2) and Mamba; (3) identify path ambiguity—where a variable influences the output through multiple computational paths—as a structural barrier that prevents unified intermediate representations and causes generalization failure even near-exhaustive data; and (4) demonstrate that Chain-of-Thought supervision flattens multi-hop structures (reducing the 3-HOP exponent from 2.58 to 1.76) but does not resolve path ambiguity. All experiments use four synthetic tasks with randomly constructed primitive functions to isolate functional-equivalence-based generalization from other mechanisms.

## Results

The paper proves a tight sample complexity bound (Theorem 6.1) showing that perfect in-domain generalization on a 2-HOP task via pattern matching requires N ≳ n^(2.5−0.5/k) training examples, where n is the token set size and k is the evidence threshold. Empirically, the measured power-law exponents are c=2.26 (2-HOP), c=2.43 (PARALLEL 2-HOP), and c=2.58 (3-HOP), all stable across a ~20× parameter range (68M to 1.5B GPT-2) and also holding for Mamba. Out-of-coverage accuracy remains at chance level (~1/50) regardless of training duration, while a sharp phase transition from ID generalization failure to complete success occurs near N=20k for the 2-HOP task. CoT supervision reduces the 3-HOP exponent from 2.58 to 1.76 but does not resolve the path ambiguity failure on NON-TREE structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a characterization study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so no external baselines are benchmarked. The key internal comparisons are: (1) GPT-2 vs. Mamba — both architectures exhibit the same power-law scaling exponents and the same path-ambiguity failure, confirming the results are architecture-invariant; (2) Standard training vs. CoT supervision — CoT reduces the 3-HOP exponent from 2.58 to 1.76 (a ~32% reduction in data requirement) and makes 2-HOP, 3-HOP, and 5-HOP exponents nearly identical, but fails to resolve NON-TREE path ambiguity; (3) 2-HOP vs. NON-TREE — 2-HOP achieves a sharp transition to perfect ID accuracy near N=20k, while NON-TREE fails to generalize even with near-exhaustive training data and 36k epochs, with IICG analysis confirming the model forms context-dependent (x₂-conditioned) representations rather than unified intermediate state representations. Scaling to 1.5B parameters does not significantly improve NON-TREE performance, indicating the failure is structural rather than capacity-limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior compositional generalization studies (Lake & Baroni, 2018; Hupkes et al., 2020; Csordas et al., 2022) that discuss pattern matching behaviorally and post-hoc, by introducing a formal, data-centric definition of pattern matching as functional equivalence with a precisely defined coverage boundary. It builds on mechanistic interpretability work on grokking and intermediate state representations (Wang et al., 2024a; Nanda et al., 2023) but provides a more precise, predictive account of what training data properties drive representation clustering. The sample complexity analysis connects to data scaling law literature but applies it specifically to the data requirements of pattern-matching-based generalization, and the CoT analysis extends prior findings on CoT sample efficiency (Srivastava et al., 2023; Kim & Suzuki, 2025) by showing where CoT's benefits break down.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a falsifiable, quantitative boundary for what pattern matching alone can achieve, with a tight sample complexity bound (Theorem 6.1) confirmed across 20× parameter scaling and two architectures. The identification of path ambiguity as a structural barrier that no amount of data or model scaling can overcome within the pattern matching paradigm offers a concrete explanation for LLM failures on planning and multi-path reasoning tasks. Key limitations include restriction to synthetic fixed-length discrete-sequence tasks and autoregressive architectures, leaving open whether the framework extends to variable-length natural language and broader model families. Future work on the proposed taxonomy of generalization mechanisms (property-based, shared-operator) and targeted data augmentation to maximize k-coverage are natural next steps.
