## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is the standard technique for conditional generation in diffusion and flow-matching models, yet its theoretical properties remain incompletely understood: it is unknown whether CFG can ever reproduce the exact target distribution, and the well-documented artifacts of mean overshoot and variance shrinkage lack a principled characterization of how they depend on dimensionality. The paper asks whether these artifacts are inherent to CFG or can be provably mitigated while preserving the practical quality gains that make CFG indispensable.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes a class label or text prompt along with the model's conditional and unconditional score estimates at each denoising step, and produces a generated image (256×256 or 512×512 for class-conditional models; standard resolutions for text-to-image). The key modification is applied to the score difference vector S_t(x,c) − S_t(x): its Euclidean norm is raised to a power α > 0 and multiplied back onto the difference, so the guidance strength adapts locally to the magnitude of the conditional signal. The method operates identically in latent space (where α = 0.9 was consistently optimal) and pixel space, and is compatible with both diffusion (DDPM/EDM) and flow-matching objectives.

## Method

The paper connects CFG to the high-dimensional dynamical-regime framework of Biroli et al. (2024), identifying the speciation time t_s = ½ log d as the critical transition. It proves a 'blessing-of-dimensionality' result: in infinite and sufficiently high dimensions, CFG-guided trajectories generate the correct conditional distribution because (i) before t_s, CFG accelerates convergence toward the target class, (ii) just before t_s, guided paths realign with unguided conditional paths, and (iii) after t_s, the CFG term vanishes identically. In finite dimensions, the failure of path realignment produces mean overshoot of relative amplitude O(1/√d) and variance shrinkage via increased potential curvature. To mitigate both effects, the paper proposes Power-Law CFG, which replaces the linear score difference ΔS with ΔS · |ΔS|^α, naturally dampening guidance when the conditional signal is weak (and potentially unreliable) while amplifying it when strong. This nonlinear modification is proven to reduce the overshoot-driving term B(q) and the curvature increment, and is validated on Gaussian mixture simulations and four state-of-the-art models (DiT/XL-2, EDM2-S, and two MMDiT text-to-image models), where it consistently improves FID, precision, and recall over standard CFG and composes additively with existing variants like CADS and limited-interval guidance.

## Results

Power-law CFG (α=0.9) consistently improves FID over standard CFG across all four evaluated models: EDM2-S on ImageNet-1K 512×512 (FID 1.93 vs. 2.29), DiT/XL-2 on ImageNet-1K 256×256 (FID 2.05 vs. 2.27), a diffusion MMDiT on CC12M (FID 8.11 vs. 8.58), and a flow-matching MMDiT on COCO (FID 4.81 vs. 5.20). When combined with CADS, the method achieves the best FID on all four models (1.52, 1.63, 7.98, and 4.71 respectively), while also improving recall over standard CFG in most settings. The method is robust to the guidance scale ω, with larger α values yielding more stable FID across a wider range of ω.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Power-law CFG + CADS achieves the best FID on all four models, outperforming standalone CADS by 0.08 (EDM2-S: 1.52 vs. 1.60), 0.07 (DiT/XL-2: 1.63 vs. 1.70), 0.34 (Diff. MMDiT: 7.98 vs. 8.32), and 0.20 (FM MMDiT: 4.71 vs. 4.91). It also beats CFG++ on both text-to-image models (7.98 vs. 8.35 on CC12M; 4.71 vs. 4.85 on COCO) and REG on all four models. Power-law CFG + Limited improves over standalone Limited guidance on FID for EDM2-S (1.73 vs. 1.87), DiT/XL-2 (1.87 vs. 1.97), and FM MMDiT (4.84 vs. 5.00). Standalone power-law CFG (without CADS or Limited) improves FID over standard CFG on all four models but does not always beat the strongest individual baselines (e.g., CADS on EDM2-S: 1.93 vs. 1.60), confirming that the nonlinear extension is most effective when combined with existing guidance strategies. On recall, power-law CFG + CADS achieves the best or near-best values on EDM2-S (0.622) and FM MMDiT (0.624), while on DiT/XL-2 the Limited variant achieves the highest recall (0.642).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper connects Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) to the high-dimensional dynamical-regimes framework of Biroli et al. (2024) and Biroli & Mezard (2023), which had previously been applied to analyze unguided diffusion but not to guidance. Prior theoretical work (Chidambaram et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024) characterized CFG's mean overshoot and variance shrinkage in low-dimensional Gaussian mixtures, treating these as inherent distortions. This paper departs by showing these artifacts are finite-dimensional corrections that vanish as d→∞, establishing a 'blessing-of-dimensionality' result, and then proposes a nonlinear power-law extension of CFG (Eq. 7) that provably mitigates both effects while unifying many existing CFG variants (limited-interval, CADS, weight schedulers) under a general nonlinear framework (Eq. 8).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The blessing-of-dimensionality result provides a principled explanation for why CFG works well in practice on high-dimensional data (images) despite distorting the target in low dimensions, resolving a long-standing theoretical gap. The power-law CFG is a simple, drop-in modification that consistently improves FID, precision, and recall across four architectures (DiT, EDM2, MMDiT) and both diffusion and flow-matching objectives, with the added benefit of increased robustness to the guidance scale ω. A key limitation is that the theory assumes exact scores; the paper acknowledges it cannot explain why CFG-modified distributions outperform the true conditional in practice with imperfect score estimators, leaving the interplay between score approximation error and guidance effectiveness as an important open question.
