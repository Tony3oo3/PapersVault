## Problem

Existing GraphRAG systems rely on explicit relation extraction to build knowledge graphs, but this process is both computationally expensive and error-prone, introducing local inaccuracies (incorrect triples) and global inconsistencies (contradictory or redundant relations) that degrade retrieval quality. As a result, GraphRAG methods frequently underperform even naive RAG on real-world tasks, as the noise from imperfect graph construction outweighs the structural benefits. The paper asks whether graph-based multi-hop retrieval can be achieved without unstable relation modeling.

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language query and a pre-indexed text corpus of passages as input. Offline, each passage is segmented into sentences and processed with lightweight NER (spaCy) to extract entities—no LLM calls are made during indexing. At query time, the system outputs a ranked list of top-k passages (k=5 by default) scored by Personalized PageRank importance, which are then fed to an LLM (GPT-4o-mini) to generate the final answer. The entire retrieval pipeline operates in approximately 0.1 seconds even on corpora of over 1 million nodes.

## Method

LinearRAG constructs a relation-free hierarchical graph called Tri-Graph with three node types (entities, sentences, passages) connected by two sparse binary adjacency matrices: a contain matrix C (passage→entity) and a mention matrix M (sentence→entity), built solely via sentence segmentation and NER with zero LLM token cost. Retrieval proceeds in two stages: first, local semantic bridging iteratively activates relevant entities on the entity-sentence bipartite graph by propagating query-sentence similarity scores through M with a fixed-threshold dynamic pruning mechanism (n iterations for n-hop activation); second, global importance aggregation applies Personalized PageRank on the entity-passage bipartite graph, initializing entity nodes with their activation scores and passage nodes with a hybrid score combining direct query-passage similarity and entity-level evidence. This design achieves linear scalability in both indexing and retrieval while preserving all original passage context, eliminating the need for explicit relation triples.

## Results

LinearRAG achieves the best generation accuracy across all four benchmark datasets: 64.30% Contain-Acc. and 66.50% GPT-Acc. on HotpotQA, 70.20% Contain-Acc. and 63.70% GPT-Acc. on 2WikiMultiHopQA, 33.90% Contain-Acc. and 37.00% GPT-Acc. on MuSiQue, and 63.72% GPT-Acc. on the Medical dataset. In terms of efficiency, LinearRAG requires zero LLM token consumption during both indexing and retrieval, with an indexing time of 249.78s and average retrieval latency of 0.093s on 2Wiki, compared to 4,933s indexing and 10.96s retrieval for LightRAG. On the large-scale ATLAS-Wiki corpus (10M tokens), LinearRAG indexes in 3,084s with zero tokens versus 46,431s and 16.62M prompt tokens for RAPTOR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LinearRAG outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest GraphRAG competitor HippoRAG2 (Gutiérrez et al., 2025), LinearRAG gains +1.4% Contain-Acc. and +2.2% GPT-Acc. on HotpotQA, +7.5% Contain-Acc. and +8.7% GPT-Acc. on 2Wiki, +2.9% Contain-Acc. and +2.0% GPT-Acc. on MuSiQue, and +2.95% GPT-Acc. on Medical. GFM-RAG (Luo et al., 2025) is the second-strongest baseline on HotpotQA (65.60% GPT-Acc.) but trails LinearRAG by 0.9% there and by a wider margin on 2Wiki (59.50% vs. 63.70% GPT-Acc.). Among relation-extraction-based methods, LightRAG (Guo et al., 2024) and G-Retriever (He et al., 2024) underperform significantly, with G-Retriever scoring only 40.60% GPT-Acc. on HotpotQA versus LinearRAG's 66.50%. Clustering-based methods RAPTOR (Sarthi et al., 2024) and E²GraphRAG (Zhao et al., 2025) achieve moderate results but fall 8–12 points behind LinearRAG on 2Wiki. Vanilla RAG with Top-5 retrieval (58.60% GPT-Acc. on HotpotQA) and IRCOT (Trivedi et al., 2023) (60.60%) are both clearly surpassed, confirming that structural graph reasoning provides substantial gains over flat retrieval. Zero-shot LLM baselines (Llama-3 8B/13B, GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4o-mini) score 15–43% GPT-Acc., far below any retrieval-augmented method.

## Relation to Prior Work

LinearRAG departs fundamentally from the dominant GraphRAG paradigm (HippoRAG, LightRAG, GFM-RAG, G-Retriever) that relies on LLM-based relation extraction to build entity-relation triple graphs. Instead, it repurposes lightweight NER (spaCy) and semantic similarity to construct a relation-free Tri-Graph of entities, sentences, and passages, drawing conceptually on RAPTOR's hierarchical organization and HippoRAG's Personalized PageRank for retrieval, but applying PPR over a much simpler bipartite entity-passage subgraph. The two-stage retrieval (local semantic bridging followed by global importance aggregation) is a novel combination that performs implicit relation matching without ever materializing explicit relational edges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a core assumption in GraphRAG research—that explicit relation extraction is necessary for multi-hop retrieval—and provides well-evidenced evidence (four datasets, multiple LLM evaluators, large-scale efficiency tests) that it is not only unnecessary but actively harmful due to local inaccuracies and global inconsistencies. The zero-token, linear-scalability indexing (12.8×–15.1× faster than RAPTOR on 5M–10M token corpora) makes graph-based RAG practical for large-scale enterprise deployments without API costs. Open questions include how the method performs in low-resource languages where NER quality degrades, on corpora with dense but non-entity-based cross-references, and whether the binary edge assumption limits expressiveness for very long or highly structured documents.
