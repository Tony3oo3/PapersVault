## Problem

Fine-tuning-as-a-Service (FTaaS) exposes safety-aligned LLMs to harmful fine-tuning attacks where users inject malicious samples into their training data, subverting the model's safety alignment. Existing defenses either provide insufficient protection against varying attack configurations (epochs, learning rates, harmful ratios) or come at the cost of degraded task performance, leaving no method that simultaneously preserves safety and maintains competitive fine-tuning accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264132/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in two stages: first, a one-time alignment stage consumes a safety alignment dataset (harmful prompt–refusal pairs) and a harmful dataset (harmful prompt–compliant answer pairs) to produce a robustly aligned model; second, a per-user fine-tuning stage consumes the user-submitted dataset (a mixture of benign task samples and harmful samples at ratio p) and produces a fine-tuned LLM. The weighting mechanism at fine-tuning time computes, for each sample in a mini-batch, the log-likelihood ratio of the target completion versus a generic refusal string, then normalizes via softmax to yield per-sample gradient weights—no external classifier or pre-trained selector is required.

## Method

Antibody is a two-stage defense that attenuates harmful gradient influence. In the alignment stage, it applies sharpness-aware minimization on the harmful loss to drive the model into a flat loss region with respect to harmful samples, using a step-adaptive regularizer λ_t derived from KKT conditions (reducing to Booster's constant-intensity form as a special case), plus a refusal loss computed on a harmful-perturbed model to ensure the weighting scheme remains effective after drift. In the fine-tuning stage, it applies a dynamic per-sample weighting: each sample's weight is proportional to exp(r_θ/τ) where r_θ = log(π(y_i|x_i)/π(y_r|x_i)), so a safety-aligned model naturally assigns low weights to harmful samples (high refusal likelihood) and high weights to benign ones. The theoretical analysis (Propositions 4.2–4.3) decomposes the test-sample loss change via the empirical neural tangent kernel, showing that the weighted update preserves safety on harmful-dissimilar test samples while amplifying learning on benign-dissimilar ones.

## Results

On Llama-2-7B across four fine-tuning datasets (SST2, AGNEWS, GSM8K, AlpacaEval) with a 20% harmful ratio, Antibody achieves the lowest average Harmful Score (HS) of 7.04%, compared to 15.29% for Lisa, 19.04% for Booster, 33.57% for SFT, and 40.86% for Vaccine. It also attains the best Fine-tuning Accuracy (FA) on SST2 (93.55%) and AGNEWS (87.30%), with a competitive 15.07% on GSM8K and 58.10% on AlpacaEval, yielding an average FA of 63.51%. Across model architectures (Llama-2-7B, Qwen-2-7B, Gemma-2-9B) on GSM8K, Antibody maintains an average HS below 1.24% while achieving 46.60% average FA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Antibody decisively outperforms SFT and Vaccine on both safety and utility: it reduces average HS from 33.57% (SFT) and 40.86% (Vaccine) to 7.04%, while also improving average FA (63.51% vs. 62.61% and 60.04%). Against Lisa, the strongest fine-tuning-stage baseline, Antibody halves the average HS (7.04% vs. 15.29%) and gains 2.54 percentage points in average FA (63.51% vs. 60.97%). The closest competitor is Booster, which achieves a slightly higher average FA (65.20% vs. 63.51%) driven by better GSM8K (16.27% vs. 15.07%) and AlpacaEval (65.24% vs. 58.10%) scores, but at the cost of a substantially higher average HS (19.04% vs. 7.04%); Antibody's HS advantage is especially pronounced on SST2 (1.48% vs. 14.31%) and AGNEWS (1.24% vs. 15.88%). Across all three model architectures on GSM8K, Antibody maintains an average HS below 1.24%, well under Lisa (7.60%) and Booster (11.13%), confirming consistent safety preservation. Ablation results confirm that each component contributes: flatness alignment alone reduces HS from 23.94% to 13.02%, adding the weighted fine-tuning drops it to 4.44%, and the refusal loss completes the reduction to 1.24%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) framework to the LLM safety-alignment setting, drawing on the well-established SAM literature (Foret et al., 2021) and its recent extensions to quantization and Bayesian networks. It builds directly on Ren & Sutherland's (2025) eNTK-based analysis of LLM fine-tuning dynamics, extending their single-sample decomposition to a weighted mini-batch setting (Propositions 4.2–4.3). The alignment-stage update rule reduces to Booster (Huang et al., 2025b) when the step-adaptive regularizer λ_t is held constant, but Antibody departs from Booster by explicitly targeting a flat harmful-loss region rather than minimizing harmful loss reduction rate, and by coupling this with a dynamic, model-dependent weighting scheme during fine-tuning that requires no additional alignment data mixing (unlike Lisa).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For FTaaS providers, Antibody offers a practical two-stage defense that maintains safety (≈1% harmful score) across diverse models, datasets, and attack ratios while preserving competitive task accuracy, and notably remains robust to aggressive fine-tuning hyperparameters (up to 100 epochs, 1e-4 learning rate) where single-stage methods like Booster degrade sharply. Limitations include the extra forward passes needed to compute refusal-likelihood weights at every fine-tuning step, the added computational cost of the sharpness computation during alignment, and the fact that GSM8K accuracy still lags Booster by ~1.2 points. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to full-parameter fine-tuning (all experiments use LoRA), to more adaptive or multi-turn attacks, and to much larger models where the flatness regularization cost may become prohibitive.
