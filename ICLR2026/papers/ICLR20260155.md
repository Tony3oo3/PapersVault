## Problem

Existing computational models capture only fragments of human dual learning: RL agents require millions of trial-and-error steps, theory-based RL achieves sample efficiency but cannot incorporate social knowledge, and language-conditioned RL demands massive paired (state, language) supervision. The paper asks how humans integrate linguistic guidance from others with direct experience to learn complex tasks efficiently, and whether a unified computational model can replicate this capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260155/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes symbolic VGDL game states (object IDs, colors, positions, rewards, win/loss events) and a single natural language advice message per game from a previous player (human or model). It maintains a posterior over 20 VGDL program particles representing candidate world models, outputs 10-step action sequences refined by a genetic algorithm, and generates new natural language advice by sampling from the LM speaker model. The language likelihood is computed by prompting LLaMA-3.1-70B with a theory description and measuring the probability of the exact received message tokens, while language-guided proposals use the LM to answer multiple-choice questions about specific VGDL rules.

## Method

The core contribution is a Bayesian framework that jointly infers executable VGDL world models from both experiential and linguistic evidence, using LLaMA-3.1-70B in three distinct roles: (1) as a probabilistic speaker model approximating P(L|T) via Bayesian Theory of Mind, (2) as a likelihood evaluator scoring how well each candidate theory explains received advice, and (3) as a proposal generator biasing Metropolis-Hastings moves toward rules compatible with linguistic input. Inference uses a particle filter with 20 VGDL program particles and Metropolis rejuvenations, where experience likelihood is estimated by replaying actions under each theory across 20 simulations. Planning alternates between exploration (subgoals scored by disagreement across particles) and exploitation (subgoals contributing to win conditions), optimizing 10-step action sequences via genetic algorithm under the MAP theory. The agent generates advice for future players by sampling from the same speaker model, enabling bidirectional human-model knowledge transfer and iterated generational learning.

## Results

Across 10 VGDL games, the proposed model solves all 10 games within a 10-life budget from experience alone (median 2.5 lives to first level), while human participants solve 9/10 (median 4 lives). Adding human-written advice reduces median lives to first level to 2.25 for humans (ΔnAUC = 0.12, p = 2.2×10⁻⁴) and 1.25 for models (ΔnAUC = 0.04, p = 3.3×10⁻²). Model-generated advice improves human learning by ΔnAUC = 0.15 (p < 10⁻⁵) and model learning by ΔnAUC = 0.088 (p < 10⁻⁸). In iterated learning (10 generations, 2 lives each), performance increases significantly across generations with ΔnAUC ∈ [0.44, 0.57] (all p < 10⁻¹⁰).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two main baselines: Deep RL (Double DQN) and a pure LM agent (LLaMA-3.1-70B with ReAct, scratchpad, and chain-of-thought). Deep RL fails catastrophically, solving zero levels within 10 lives across all 10 games, while the proposed model solves all 10 games with a median of 2.5 lives to the first level. The pure LM baseline never solves more than one level per game and fails entirely on 7/10 games, whereas the proposed model achieves full mastery. The gap is attributed to the lack of structured, executable world models: Deep RL relies on pure trial-and-error without causal representations, and the LM agent cannot perform reliable multi-step planning or maintain consistent beliefs about game dynamics. The proposed model also outperforms both baselines when given linguistic advice, showing that structured Bayesian inference over VGDL programs is essential for integrating language with experience. An ablation removing language-guided proposals (but retaining language likelihood) still outperforms experience-only learning (ΔnAUC = −0.030, p = 5.7×10⁻³), confirming that both components contribute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: theory-based reinforcement learning (Tsividis et al., 2021), which performs Bayesian inference over executable world models for planning but cannot incorporate social input, and Bayesian Theory of Mind (Baker et al., 2011; Frank and Goodman, 2012), which models how language reveals beliefs but operates in simple, non-interactive settings with predefined hypothesis spaces. The key architectural contribution is using a pretrained LLM (LLaMA-3.1-70B) as a probabilistic speaker model within a particle-filter inference loop over VGDL programs, so that linguistic advice enters as a likelihood term P(L|T) alongside experiential evidence P(E|T). This departs from language-conditioned RL (Luketina et al., 2019; Zhong et al., 2020), which requires thousands of paired (state, language) episodes, by treating each message as a one-shot Bayesian update over a structured hypothesis space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates bidirectional human–model knowledge transfer in a controlled setting: model-generated advice significantly improves human learning (ΔnAUC=0.15, p<10⁻⁵), and iterated learning across 10 generations shows reliable cumulative knowledge accumulation. A notable open question is whether the framework scales beyond VGDL's discrete, symbolic state space to continuous, high-dimensional environments, which would require advances in program synthesis and scalable probabilistic inference over richer languages. The finding that models learn better from model-generated than human-generated advice (while humans show no such preference) highlights a communication-style gap that limits current human–AI collaborative learning systems and points toward the need for meta-cognitive trust mechanisms and richer linguistic abstractions (strategies, planning heuristics) in future work.
