## Problem

Existing automated presentation generation methods (e.g., PPTAgent, PresentAgent, Paper2Poster) produce outputs with limited narrative coherence, rigid template-based designs, and no mechanism for iterative self-correction. A fundamental bottleneck is the absence of a reliable aesthetic evaluation model: current VLM-as-judge approaches lack the consistency and robustness needed to provide actionable feedback, making it impossible for agents to improve their own visual design iteratively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264535/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single academic paper PDF as input, extracting text, figures, and formulas via Marker into a unified library. It produces three synchronized outputs: HTML-rendered slides (chosen over PPTX for precise positional control and CSS-based styling), a narration script, and a presentation video where TTS audio segments are temporally aligned to their corresponding slides with optional transitions. The HTML format allows the Design Agent to apply dynamic effects, adjust font hierarchies, and select styles from a predefined CSS library while preserving layout flexibility.

## Method

EvoPresent is a four-agent pipeline (Storyline → Scholar → Design → Checker) operating in a draft-feedback-refinement loop. The Checker Agent integrates PresAesth, a multi-task aesthetic model built on Qwen-2.5-VL-7B and trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to jointly perform scoring (1–10 scale), defect adjustment (categorized into Composition & Layout, Typography, Imagery & Visualizations), and pairwise comparison. The reward function combines a format reward (structured think/answer tags) with a task-specific accuracy reward (exact match for comparison, F1 threshold for adjustment, absolute-error tolerance for scoring). The Checker iteratively scores slides against a threshold of 8.0, reverts to the best-scoring version if quality degrades, and feeds targeted feedback to the Layout Planner until convergence. Multi-task GRPO training outperforms both single-task RL and multi-task SFT, as the comparative reward structure mirrors how humans judge aesthetics and produces actionable reasoning rather than static labels.

## Results

On the EvoPresent Benchmark (650 papers, 13,000 slides), EvoPresent-claude-4 achieves the best overall fine-grained score of 3.90/5, with the lowest perplexity (18.57) and highest aesthetic score (8.05/10) among all generated methods, approaching the oracle upper bound (PPL 16.64, Aesth. 8.50, Overall 4.01). For aesthetic awareness, PresAesth (trained via multi-task GRPO on Qwen-2.5-VL-7B) achieves a scoring MAE of 1.33, an average defect-adjustment F1 of 0.389, and a pairwise comparison accuracy of 87.8%, outperforming all closed-source VLMs. The self-improvement loop with PresAesth as checker improves aesthetic scores from 3.2 to over 8.0 within three iterations, versus five+ iterations for other checkers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoPresent outperforms all end-to-end baselines (GPT-4o, GPT-5, DeepSeek-R1, Claude-4-Sonnet, GPT-4o-Image) on overall quality, with EvoPresent-claude-4 scoring 3.90 vs. the best end-to-end Claude-4-Sonnet at 3.78; the gap is driven primarily by aesthetic score (8.05 vs. 7.70) and narrative/engagement dimensions. Against multi-agent baselines (PPTAgent, PresentAgent, Paper2Poster), EvoPresent achieves higher overall scores (3.82–3.90 vs. 3.63–3.76), with the largest gains in narrative coherence (+0.12 over Paper2Poster) and aesthetic quality (+0.17–0.77). For aesthetic awareness, PresAesth surpasses GPT-4o (MAE 1.33 vs. 1.64; comparison accuracy 87.8% vs. 77.1%), GPT-5 (MAE 1.33 vs. 1.39; accuracy 87.8% vs. 59.7%), and Claude-4-Sonnet (MAE 1.33 vs. 1.61; accuracy 87.8% vs. 69.5%), with the comparison task showing the largest margin (~40% higher accuracy). In the ablation on self-improvement, PresAesth as checker converges in 3 iterations to score >8.0, while GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 checkers require 5+ iterations and plateau at lower scores, confirming that high initial model capability does not guarantee effective self-correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the automated presentation generation line (PPTAgent, PresentAgent, Paper2Poster) by introducing a self-improvement loop driven by a dedicated aesthetic model, rather than relying on fixed templates or VLM-as-judge heuristics. The aesthetic evaluation component (PresAesth) is trained with GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) in a multi-task setup (scoring, defect adjustment, pairwise comparison), departing from prior aesthetic assessment work that targets natural images or uses supervised fine-tuning on simplistic labels. The EvoPresent Benchmark also extends prior generation benchmarks (which cover 30–500 samples, single format) to 650 papers with multimodal resources (slides, videos, scripts) and a separate 2,000-pair aesthetic awareness suite.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic benchmark for academic presentation generation with both content and design dimensions, which will likely become a standard evaluation resource. A key practical finding is that an agent's initial generation quality does not predict its self-correction ability, suggesting that feedback quality (from the aesthetic model) matters more than raw model capability for iterative refinement — a counterintuitive result for practitioners choosing backbone models. Open questions include whether the aesthetic model generalizes beyond academic slides to other visual design domains, whether the self-improvement loop scales to longer presentations without quality degradation, and whether the HTML rendering choice limits deployment to non-web contexts.
