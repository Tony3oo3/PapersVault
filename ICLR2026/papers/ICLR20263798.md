## Problem

Existing automated circuit discovery methods in mechanistic interpretability rely on heuristics or sampling-based evaluations over discrete input sets, offering no provable guarantees that a discovered circuit remains faithful under continuous perturbations. Even infinitesimal input or patching perturbations can break circuit faithfulness, a critical concern given the safety implications of interpretability. This paper bridges circuit discovery and neural network verification to produce circuits with formal guarantees over entire continuous domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a neural network computation graph G (at a user-chosen granularity: neurons for MNIST, convolutional filters for CIFAR-10/GTSRB/TaxiNet), a continuous input domain Z defined as a union of ℓ_p-balls around reference points, and a faithfulness predicate Φ (e.g., logit-difference bounded by δ). It outputs a circuit subgraph C ⊆ G accompanied by a robustness certificate proving that the circuit-model logit difference stays below δ for all inputs in Z (input-robustness) and/or for all feasible patching activations over a continuous patching domain Z′ (patching-robustness), rather than merely at sampled points. The certification is performed via a siamese encoding that duplicates the circuit and stacks it with the full model, converting the circuit-faithfulness check into a standard neural network verification query solvable by α-β-CROWN.

## Method

The core mechanism is a siamese encoding that duplicates circuit C as C′ and stacks it with the full graph G (sharing or disjointing the input layer depending on whether input- or patching-robustness is certified), reducing the circuit-faithfulness predicate to a verification query over the combined model. For minimality, the paper introduces a circuit monotonicity property: when Φ combines input-robustness over Z with patching-robustness over a superset Z′ whose activation space is closed under concatenation, Φ is monotonic, causing the standard greedy removal algorithm to converge to a subset-minimal circuit rather than merely a quasi- or locally-minimal one. To reach cardinal-minimality, the paper proves a duality between faithful circuits and 'blocking-sets' (subgraphs whose removal breaks all faithful circuits), reducing the search to a minimum-hitting-set (MHS) problem solvable by the RC2 MaxSAT solver. The full pipeline iterates: enumerate small blocking-sets in parallel, compute their MHS to obtain a candidate circuit, verify its faithfulness via the siamese encoding, and refine until convergence, yielding circuits that are simultaneously input-robust, patching-robust, and (under monotonicity) subset- or cardinally-minimal.

## Results

The provable circuit discovery method achieves 100% certified robustness across all four datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, GTSRB, TaxiNet), while sampling-based baselines achieve only 9.5%–46.5% input-robustness and zero/mean-patching baselines achieve 33.3%–63.3% patching-robustness. Circuit sizes remain comparable: the provable method produces circuits within ~3 components of the sampling-based baseline (e.g., 19.18 vs. 16.47 on CIFAR-10; 6.82 vs. 5.77 on TaxiNet). The computational cost is substantially higher due to verification queries (e.g., ~2971s vs. 0.23s on CIFAR-10), but the robustness gap is decisive: even infinitesimal perturbations break sampling-based circuits, while the provable circuits are certified over the full continuous domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two families of standard circuit discovery baselines. Against the sampling-based approach of Conmy et al. (2023), the provable method wins decisively on robustness: 100% certified input-robustness versus 9.5% (TaxiNet), 19.2% (MNIST), 27.6% (GTSRB), and 46.5% (CIFAR-10), while maintaining comparable circuit sizes (within ~3 components). Against the mean-patching baseline from Wang et al. (2023), the provable patching-robust method again achieves 100% robustness versus 33.3%–63.3% for mean patching and 38.0%–58.0% for zero patching, again at similar circuit sizes. The trade-off is computational: verification-based certification is 100–1000× slower than sampling-based evaluation, but the robustness guarantee is qualitatively different—certified over the entire continuous domain rather than at finitely many sampled points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: mechanistic interpretability's circuit discovery (Conmy et al. 2023; Adolfi et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2023) and neural network verification (α-β-CROWN, Brix et al. 2024). Prior circuit discovery methods rely on sampling-based faithfulness checks over discrete inputs, while verification provides continuous-domain guarantees but has not been applied to circuit discovery. The paper introduces a siamese encoding that duplicates the circuit alongside the full model to make circuit faithfulness amenable to standard verification queries, and adapts the minimum-hitting-set duality from formal XAI (Bassan & Katz 2023; Ignatiev et al. 2019c) to the circuit setting, yielding a novel circuit-blocking-set duality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide provable, continuous-domain guarantees for circuit discovery, addressing a fundamental gap where even infinitesimal perturbations can break the faithfulness of sampling-based circuits. The circuit monotonicity property revealing deep connections between input robustness, patching robustness, and minimality is a clean theoretical contribution that clarifies when greedy algorithms converge to stronger minimality notions. However, the practical scope is currently limited to small-to-medium CNNs (MNIST, CIFAR-10, GTSRB, TaxiNet) where α-β-CROWN is tractable; scaling to transformers or large language models remains an open challenge that will determine the framework's real-world impact.
