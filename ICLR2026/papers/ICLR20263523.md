## Problem

Next-token-prediction behavior generation models rely on trajectory tokenizers to discretize continuous trajectory space, yet the community lacks a systematic understanding of what properties make a good tokenizer. Data-driven tokenizers (K-disks, K-means, VQ-VAE) achieve high utilization but suffer from insufficient coverage, noise sensitivity, and asymmetry, while rule-based methods (gridding) offer broad coverage and symmetry but include many tokens that never appear in real data, resulting in poor performance at the same vocabulary size.

## Input / Output

The model consumes the past 1 second of states (position and heading) for up to 128 agents plus a high-definition map, and autoregressively generates 0.5-second trajectory tokens at 2 Hz re-planning frequency over an 8-second horizon. Each trajectory token is a sequence of L=5 points with (x, y, yaw) in agent-centric coordinates, drawn from type-specific vocabularies (8040 for vehicles, 2798 for bicycles, 3001 for pedestrians). A coordinate transform then maps the selected tokens back to continuous agent states for the next interval.

## Method

TrajTok generates its vocabulary in four steps: (1) flip all logged trajectories along the x-axis to enforce symmetry, (2) set up a grid-based candidate set and mark cells valid if they contain at least s_p trajectories, (3) apply data-driven filtering and expanding by adding unselected cells with ≥s_a selected neighbors and removing selected cells with ≤s_r neighbors, balancing coverage against utilization, and (4) produce final tokens by averaging logged trajectories in valid cells or interpolating curves for expanded cells with no logged data. The paper also introduces spatial-aware label smoothing, which replaces the uniform non-ground-truth probability in standard label smoothing with weights inversely proportional to the squared spatial distance between each candidate token and the ground-truth token, making the loss more tolerant of nearby tokens while penalizing distant ones. The approach is plug-and-play and is evaluated on the SMART-tiny NTP architecture, winning first place in the 2025 Waymo Open Sim Agents Challenge.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Motion Dataset validation split, TrajTok achieves a Realism Meta of 0.7702 and minADE of 1.3428 m with the SMART-tiny model, outperforming all other tokenizers tested (VQ-VAE: 0.7596, K-means: 0.7476, K-disks: 0.7584, Grid: 0.7527). On the full WOSAC 2025 leaderboard, TrajTok attains a Realism Meta of 0.7852 and a state-of-the-art Map-based score of 0.9207, placing it second overall behind SMART-R1 (0.7855) and ahead of unimotion (0.7851). The method also demonstrates superior generalization, with only a 0.0061 Realism Meta drop when the vocabulary is built from nuScenes data instead of Waymo, compared to a 0.0234 drop for K-disks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrajTok outperforms all four tokenizer baselines on both Realism Meta and minADE under the identical SMART-tiny model. Against the strongest data-driven baseline K-disks (Philion et al., 2023), TrajTok gains +0.0118 Realism Meta (0.7702 vs 0.7584) and reduces minADE by 0.0104 m (1.3428 vs 1.3532). Against VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., 2017), the improvement is +0.0106 Realism Meta and −0.0554 m minADE. Against K-means (Arthur & Vassilvitskii, 2006) and the rule-based Grid tokenizer from MotionLM (Seff et al., 2023), TrajTok leads by +0.0226 and +0.0175 Realism Meta respectively. On the full WOSAC 2025 leaderboard, TrajTok (0.7852) edges out SMART-tiny-CLSFT (Zhang et al., 2025, 0.7846) and UniMM (Lin et al., 2025, 0.7829) without any fine-tuning, while trailing only SMART-R1 (0.7855) by 0.0003.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the NTP-based behavior generation paradigm established by Trajeglish (K-disks tokenizer), SMART (scalable multi-agent NTP), and MotionLM (grid-based tokenization), all of which discretize continuous trajectory space into a vocabulary for autoregressive prediction. Its key departure is a systematic, property-based analysis of tokenizers along four axes (coverage, utilization, symmetry, robustness) that prior work had not formalized, and a hybrid design that uses rule-based grid candidates as a stable scaffold and data-driven filtering/expansion to adapt to the logged distribution. The spatial-aware label smoothing extends standard label smoothing (Szegedy et al., 2016) by weighting non-ground-truth tokens by their spatial distance to the true token, a modification not previously explored in trajectory NTP models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a principled, plug-and-play tokenizer that balances the coverage-utilization trade-off and is notably robust to reduced training data (degrading only 0.0027 in Realism Meta at 10^5 samples vs. 0.0142 for K-disks), which is practically important as datasets vary in size and domain. The finding that average discretization error—a metric used by Trajeglish to evaluate tokenizers—does not predict downstream generation quality (K-disks has lower error but worse performance than TrajTok) challenges a working assumption in the field and suggests the community should evaluate tokenizers by their effect on the full generation pipeline rather than in isolation. Open questions include whether the hybrid approach generalizes to other agent types or driving domains beyond Waymo, and whether the grid-based candidate setup can be replaced by learned priors as data scales.
