## Problem

Navigation foundation models pretrained on large-scale offline videos capture diverse visual observations but lack grounded causality and interactive reasoning, leading to poor obstacle avoidance and pedestrian interaction in real-world urban settings. Simply scaling offline data yields diminishing returns, and naive RL fine-tuning of the full model causes catastrophic forgetting of pretrained capabilities and severe sim-to-real domain shift in the visual encoder. The paper asks how to scale navigation foundation models with reinforcement learning while preserving the generalizable representations learned from offline pretraining.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10 past RGB frames at 5 Hz (256×256) encoded by a frozen DINOv3 backbone into 74 tokens (10 frame-level + 64 path-level via grid pooling), plus a goal signal (target coordinates via a linear MLP or a goal image via DINOv3, 16 tokens after grid pooling) and M spatial anchor points obtained by K-Means clustering over the unified training set. During training, goal signals are stochastically masked (35% neither, 20% image-only, 40% point-only, 5% both) to prevent shortcut learning. The output is a Gaussian Mixture Model over a 10-step relative waypoint trajectory (10×2), where each of the M modes predicts a 2D mean, per-axis standard deviations, a correlation coefficient, and a positive velocity scale, along with a categorical score selecting the active mode.

## Method

S2E combines two technical innovations. First, Anchor-Guided Distribution Matching (AGDM) represents the action distribution as a structured GMM in which K-Means anchors serve as interpretable high-level intentions (e.g., go straight, turn, yield), each corresponding to one Gaussian mode; the model is pretrained end-to-end with a negative log-likelihood loss (using an assignment strategy that selects the mode whose predicted direction best matches ground truth) plus an L2 regression loss on the velocity scale. Second, the Residual-Attention Module (RAM) addresses RL post-training by freezing the pretrained cross-attention parameters and adding a parallel trainable copy gated by zero-initialized linear projections, so that only a lightweight residual branch is optimized with PPO in simulation—preventing both forgetting of pretrained capabilities and the observation-level domain shift that full-parameter fine-tuning would introduce in the visual encoder. The full pipeline pretrains on 100 hours of multi-platform navigation video, then fine-tunes the RAM branches for 8 hours on a single GPU using 64 parallel URBAN-SIM environments with a composite reward (dense/sparse goal-reaching, collision penalties, walkable-area enforcement, trajectory similarity, and heading smoothness). The paper also introduces NavBench-GS, a closed-loop evaluation benchmark built on photorealistic 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions with physical interactions, spanning 26 scenes × 4 task variants (empty, obstacle, pedestrian, obstacle+pedestrian).

## Results

On the NavBench-GS benchmark, S2E (trained on 100h of video data) achieves success rates of 0.82 (Empty), 0.57 (Obstacle), 0.74 (Pedestrian), and 0.51 (Obstacle+Pedestrian), outperforming all baselines across all four task categories. Notably, S2E achieves zero collisions (CT=0.00) in the Empty scenario and reduces collision times to 1.58 in the most challenging Obstacle+Pedestrian setting, compared to 2.25 for CityWalker (2000h data). In real-world deployment on wheeled and quadruped robots, S2E-Full achieves 51% and 55% success rates respectively, roughly doubling the performance of the best baseline (CityWalker at 28% and 31%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2E decisively outperforms all five baselines across every metric in every scenario on NavBench-GS. The most striking comparison is against CityWalker (2000h data): S2E with only 100h of pretraining data plus RL achieves 0.57 SR in the Obstacle scenario versus CityWalker's 0.43, and 0.51 vs 0.37 in Obstacle+Pedestrian, while also reducing collisions from 2.25 to 1.58. Against the same-data control CityWalker* (100h, no RL), S2E gains +10% SR in Empty, +5% in Obstacle, +11% in Pedestrian, and +4% in Obstacle+Pedestrian, confirming that the gains come from RL rather than data volume. Among image-based baselines, GNM (70h) and ViNT (80h) are the weakest, with SR below 0.30 in all interactive scenarios; NoMaD (100h, diffusion-based) is slightly stronger but still trails S2E by 15–20+ points in SR. MBRA (700h) is the strongest point-based baseline, reaching 0.51 SR in Obstacle, but S2E still leads by 6 points in SR and reduces CT from 0.53 to 0.69 (though MBRA has a slight CT edge in that specific setting). In real-world tests, S2E-Full doubles the success rate of both NoMaD and CityWalker on both wheeled and quadruped platforms, with collision times reduced by 20–30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two previously separate lines of work: navigation foundation models trained on large-scale offline video (GNM, ViNT, NoMaD, CityWalker, MBRA) and RL-based navigation in simulation (Mirowski et al., Shen et al., Putta et al.). Prior foundation models rely on passive visual imitation and lack interactive/reactive capabilities, while prior RL navigation methods suffer from poor generalization due to narrow synthetic training environments. The S2E framework bridges this gap by using offline pretraining for generalizable visual representations and RL in simulation for interactive behavior, with two technical contributions: an Anchor-Guided Gaussian Mixture Model for multimodal action distributions (building on GMM-based trajectory prediction from Gu et al. and Shi et al.) and a Residual-Attention Module inspired by LoRA-style adapters and Flamingo's cross-attention to avoid catastrophic forgetting and sim-to-real domain shift during RL fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong evidence that RL post-training is the correct paradigm for scaling navigation foundation models, analogous to RLHF for LLMs, and that it overcomes the diminishing returns of offline data scaling. The NavBench-GS benchmark, built on 3D Gaussian Splatting with physical interactions, addresses a longstanding gap in reproducible end-to-end evaluation of navigation policies in photorealistic 3D scenes. Key limitations include the absence of 3D perception (depth/occupancy), which still causes occasional collision failures, and the fact that the humanoid embodiment shows notably degraded performance. Open questions include whether this pretrain-then-RL paradigm scales to more complex multi-task settings and how to integrate 3D structural cues without sacrificing the vision-only generality.
