## Problem

Fine-tuning large language models requires prohibitive GPU memory for weights, gradients, and optimizer states simultaneously. While quantization compresses weights and zeroth-order optimization eliminates gradients and optimizer states, directly combining them is infeasible: discrete quantized weights cannot be perturbed in continuous space, and continuous gradient estimates cannot update discrete weights without costly de-quantization and re-quantization at every step.

## Input / Output

The method takes a post-training-quantized model (e.g., int4 via GPTQ with group size 128, or int2 via AQLM with 16-bit codebooks) and a small training set (1,000 examples for NLP tasks; 8,000 image-caption pairs at 512×512 for diffusion). It outputs a fine-tuned model where only the continuous quantization scales (and optionally unquantized components in codebook-based schemes) are updated, while all discrete integer weights remain frozen. The entire pipeline runs on a single 24GB consumer GPU (RTX 4090), reducing peak memory by over 18× compared to 16-bit full-parameter fine-tuning.

## Method

QZO decomposes each weight as the product of a continuous quantization scale Δ and a fixed discrete integer weight, then applies SPSA-style perturbation only to Δ (Q-SPSA), estimating gradients via two forward passes with Δ±εz while keeping integer weights frozen. To stabilize training against the high variance inherent in zeroth-order gradient estimation, QZO introduces Directional Derivative Clipping (DDC), which clips the scalar directional derivative to [−C, C] before the ZO-SGD update; a theoretical proof (Theorem 1) shows the clipped estimate remains unbiased while strictly reducing element-wise gradient variance. The approach is orthogonal to both scalar-based (GPTQ) and codebook-based (AQLM) quantization, and can be combined with PEFT methods like QLoRA. For 4-bit models, QZO uses only ~1% of the trainable parameters and FLOPs of MeZO, since it updates only the small set of continuous scales rather than all model weights.

## Results

QZO fine-tunes 4-bit LLMs using only 4.8–6.3 GB of GPU memory (vs. 14.8–20.5 GB for MeZO and 26–32 GB for full fine-tuning), achieving a >18× memory reduction over 16-bit fine-tuning. On Llama-2-7B, QZO reaches 90.0% on SST-2, 69.6% on CB, and 85.5 F1 on SQuAD, matching or exceeding MeZO (83.5, 67.9, 80.7) while using 3× less memory. On Llama-3.1-8B, QZO achieves 93.0% SST-2 and 88.3 F1 SQuAD, slightly outperforming MeZO (92.5, 86.9). Under extreme 2-bit quantization (AQLM on Llama-2-13B), QZO still improves over the zero-shot quantized baseline by 22.9 points on SST-2 (80.5 vs. 57.6) using only 5.78 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MeZO (16-bit zeroth-order fine-tuning), QZO (4-bit) uses roughly 3× less GPU memory (e.g., 5.0 GB vs. 14.8 GB on Llama-2-7B) while matching or exceeding MeZO on most tasks: QZO wins on SST-2 (90.0 vs. 83.5), CB (69.6 vs. 67.9), and SQuAD (85.5 vs. 80.7) for Llama-2-7B, and on SST-2 (93.0 vs. 92.5) and SQuAD (88.3 vs. 86.9) for Llama-3.1-8B, though it trails on RTE and BoolQ for Llama-3.1-8B. Against the 16-bit full fine-tuning upper bound, QZO closes much of the gap (e.g., 90.0 vs. 92.8 on SST-2 for Llama-2-7B) but still lags by 5–8 points on some tasks, reflecting the inherent inaccuracy of zeroth-order gradient estimation. Against the zero-shot quantized lower bound, QZO delivers large gains (e.g., +32.1 points on SST-2 for OPT-6.7B, +22.9 points for 2-bit Llama-2-13B), confirming that the quantization scales are effectively learned. In the PEFT comparison (Appendix D), QLoRA (first-order, 4-bit) achieves higher accuracy (96.1 SST-2) but requires gradient checkpointing and a paged optimizer; the QZO+QLoRA hybrid reaches 93.3 SST-2 at 4.9 GB with only 8,000 steps, suggesting complementary strengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

QZO extends the zeroth-order optimization lineage from SPSA (Spall, 1992) and MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023) by addressing the previously infeasible combination of ZO with model quantization. Prior attempts at quantized ZO fine-tuning (ZOsignSGD, ZOQO, QUZO) required quantizing perturbation noises and re-quantizing discrete weights at every step; QZO sidesteps both by perturbing only the continuous quantization scale while keeping integer weights fixed. This makes QZO orthogonal to and directly compatible with existing PTQ methods (GPTQ, AQLM) in a plug-and-play manner, rather than being tied to a specific quantization scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QZO enables fine-tuning a 13B LLM on a single 24GB consumer GPU and reduces total memory by over 18× compared to 16-bit full fine-tuning, making LLM adaptation accessible to resource-constrained practitioners. The key open question is how much performance is lost relative to first-order methods (QLoRA still leads by a noticeable margin), and whether more effective combinations of QZO with PEFT adapters can close that gap. The weaker results on diffusion models highlight an unresolved tension between ZO's injected noise and diffusion's pre-configured noise schedule, suggesting that domain-specific noise scheduling redesigns may be needed for broader applicability beyond LLMs.
