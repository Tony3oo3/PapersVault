## Problem

Prior work has documented that in-context learning (ICL) and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) differ in generalization performance, inductive biases, and out-of-distribution behavior, but the mechanistic origins of these differences remain poorly understood. The paper asks: if we treat ICL and SFT as two distinct learning algorithms applied to the same task, what specific differences in inductive biases and internal representations emerge across a learning trajectory?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265340/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The task takes ordered integer pairs (n1, n2) ∈ {0,…,99}² as input and requires the model to output one of two semantically unrelated single-token labels ("_Baz"/"_Rud"), with the ground-truth decision boundary parameterized by a single angle θ ∈ [0°, 180°]. For ICL, K example-label pairs per class are prepended to the query in the prompt; for SFT, the same cumulative set of examples is used for gradient-based training from scratch at each shot count. Beyond scalar accuracy, the paper extracts per-input decision probabilities over the full 100×100 grid, layer-wise last-token activations for RSA, and inferred linear boundary angles to characterize generalization geometry.

## Method

The paper conducts a controlled head-to-head comparison of ICL and SFT on medium-sized pretrained transformers (Llama3-8B as primary, plus Qwen3-8B, Gemma3-12B/27B, and smaller Qwen3 variants) using a 2-D linear classification task with semantically unrelated labels to minimize verbalizer and linguistic priors. Both procedures are matched on the exact same training items, ordering, and shot counts, forming parallel learning trajectories where shots per class are progressively increased. The authors track accuracy, smoothness, confidence, and inferred decision-boundary angle across trajectories, and perform layer-wise representational similarity analysis (RSA) on last-token activations to compare internal geometry. Key findings include: ICL exhibits stronger pretraining-inherited priors (a diagonal "comparison bias" favoring θ≈45° and a "previously-seen feature value bias" along rows/columns), while SFT shows representational collapse along label-separating directions; ICL also displays short-horizon sensitivity to label ordering patterns (e.g., strict alternation triggers pattern-matching over feature-based classification). LoRA mitigates but does not eliminate SFT's representational collapse, and the qualitative biases are conserved across model families and task variants (semantic adjectives, non-linear XOR).

## Results

Both ICL and SFT achieve similar held-out accuracy and near-perfect training accuracy on the 2-D linear classification task across all shot counts (0–200 shots/class) with Llama3-8B. However, SFT consistently yields higher confidence (maximum softmax probability) than ICL at comparable shots, while ICL exhibits stronger pretraining-inherited inductive biases: a 'comparison bias' favoring θ≈45° (overestimating the inferred angle for θ=30° and underestimating for θ=60°) and a 'previously-seen feature value bias' favoring θ=0° and θ=90°. RSA analysis reveals that SFT induces representational collapse in middle layers (activations cluster along two label-separating directions), whereas ICL preserves input-specific representational structure across all 32 layers. These qualitative differences are conserved across Qwen3-8B and Gemma3-12B/27B, and LoRA mitigates but does not eliminate the SFT collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper performs a head-to-head comparison of ICL and SFT as two distinct learning algorithms on the same task instance, data, and example ordering. On held-out and training accuracy, the two methods are essentially tied, both reaching near-perfect performance by 200 shots/class. On confidence, SFT wins, producing consistently higher maximum softmax probabilities, reflecting stronger logit alignment with task labels. On inductive bias strength, ICL shows markedly stronger pretraining-inherited priors (diagonal pull toward θ=45°, row/column extrapolation), while SFT exhibits weaker but still present angle-dependent biases. On internal representations, ICL preserves rich, input-specific structure across layers, whereas SFT rapidly collapses representations along label-separating directions, a difference that persists even when the unembedding matrix is frozen and is only partially mitigated by LoRA. The paper frames these not as a simple win/lose but as a mechanistic distinction: ICL performs task-conditioned inference using pretraining priors (consistent with Bayesian/implicit-optimizer views), while SFT acts as a weight-driven specialization that suppresses task-irrelevant features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the 2-D linear classification task from Agarwal et al. (2024, Many-shot ICL) and positions itself at the intersection of two active threads: (1) the ICL-vs-SFT generalization comparison (Lampinen et al. 2025, Chan et al. 2022b, Akyurek et al. 2022) and (2) the theoretical account of ICL as implicit optimization or Bayesian inference (Von Oswald et al. 2023, Garg et al. 2022, Dai et al. 2022). It departs from prior comparisons by tracking full learning trajectories (not just endpoint accuracy) on a minimally confounded task with semantically unrelated labels, and by coupling behavioral metrics (inferred boundary angle, smoothness, confidence) with layer-wise representational similarity analysis. The representational analysis builds on Doimo et al. (2024) and Kumar et al. (2022), but extends them by showing how representations evolve across shots rather than comparing a single snapshot.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic account for why ICL and SFT generalize differently: ICL preserves rich input-specific structure while SFT collapses representations along label axes, which may explain SFT's weaker few-shot generalization and potential hindrance of transfer learning. The finding that ICL in medium-sized models does not implement literal gradient descent (contradicting simplified theoretical accounts) but instead leverages pretraining-inherited priors (comparison heuristics, pattern matching) has implications for how we think about the ICL mechanism at practical scales. Key open questions include whether these representational differences persist at larger model scales, whether causal interventions on the collapsed representations can restore ICL-like generalization, and whether the findings extend beyond the controlled 2-D geometry to hierarchical or multi-stage reasoning tasks.
