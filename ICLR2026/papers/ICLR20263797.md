## Problem

Autoregressive temporal point process (TPP) models suffer from sequential sampling that scales linearly with sequence length, error compounding in multi-step forecasting, and restriction of conditional generation to forecasting only. Existing non-autoregressive alternatives (ADDTHIN, PSDIFF) interpolate between noise and data using only insert and delete operations in a discrete-time Markov chain, missing the substitution operation that Edit Flows introduce for discrete token sequences. No prior work has unified the full edit-based flow-matching framework (insert, delete, substitute) with continuous-time TPPs.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes a noise event sequence t_0 drawn from a Poisson process on [0,T] and produces a full event sequence t_1 by simulating the CTMC forward from s=0 to s=1 via Euler steps of size h=1/n_steps. For conditional generation (forecasting or inpainting), a binary time mask c: T→{0,1} partitions the sequence into a conditioned part C(t) and a free part C'(t); the conditioned subsequence is reconditioned at each Euler step to follow a noisy interpolation between C(t_0) and C(t_1), while the complement evolves freely. Event times are continuous scalars in [0,T], sequences have variable length, and the model is trained unconditionally but can be conditioned at inference without retraining.

## Method

EDITPP defines a continuous-time Markov chain over padded TPP sequences using three atomic edit operations: insertion (quantized into b_ins=64 bins between adjacent events with uniform dequantization), substitution (quantized into b_sub=64 bins within a maximum distance δ=T/100 of an existing event), and deletion. Training proceeds in an auxiliary alignment space constructed via Needleman-Wunsch alignment with cost functions that enforce valid edit operations (substitution only within δ, joint sorting of aligned pairs), and the rate model u_s^θ is trained by minimizing a Bregman divergence that matches per-element transition rates to the ground-truth conditional path. The rate model is a 2-layer Llama transformer with FlexAttention for native variable-length handling, sinusoidal embeddings for event times, and MLP heads that output per-event total rates (via exp(λ_M·tanh)) and categorical bin distributions (via softmax). The substitution operation acts as a shortcut for local delete-insert pairs, reducing the average total edit count from 234.5 (PSDIFF) to 199.7, and the continuous-time formulation allows trading off inference compute against sample quality by adjusting the number of Euler steps without retraining.

## Results

On unconditional generation across 13 datasets, EDITPP achieves the best rank on all three metrics (MMD, W₁-d_l, W₁-d_IET), e.g., MMD of 1.1×10⁻² on Hawkes-1 (vs. 1.6 for IFTPP, 2.4 for AddThin, 3.3 for PSDIFF) and W₁-d_l of 7.6×10⁻³ on Hawkes-1 (vs. 20.5, 33.3, 26.9). On forecasting over 7 real-world datasets, EDITPP closely matches PSDIFF (e.g., d_Xiao 2.5 vs. 2.4 on PUBG; MRE 3.5 vs. 3.4 on PUBG) while substantially outperforming the autoregressive IFTPP baseline (d_Xiao 6.0, MRE 38.9 on PUBG). EDITPP also uses ~15% fewer edit operations (199.65 vs. 234.52 for PSDIFF) and runs 1.9× faster than PSDIFF (4,120 ms vs. 7,776 ms on R/P) and 4.4× faster than AddThin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the autoregressive IFTPP baseline, EDITPP wins decisively on unconditional generation: MMD is lower on 11/13 datasets (e.g., 1.1 vs. 1.6 on H1, 1.4 vs. 16.2 on PG) and W₁-d_l is lower on 12/13 datasets (e.g., 7.6 vs. 20.5 on H1, 6.2 vs. 294.6 on PG). On forecasting, IFTPP suffers from error accumulation, yielding d_Xiao of 6.0 and MRE of 38.9 on PUBG versus 2.5 and 3.5 for EDITPP. Against AddThin, EDITPP dominates on MMD (best on 10/13 datasets) and W₁-d_l (best on 11/13), with AddThin particularly struggling on NSR (MMD 15.7 vs. 3.5) and R/C (MMD 63.0×10⁻³ vs. 8.2×10⁻³). Against PSDIFF, the closest competitor, EDITPP wins on MMD for 10/13 datasets and W₁-d_l for 11/13, but the gap narrows on forecasting where PSDIFF edges ahead on d_Xiao (2.4 vs. 2.5 on PG) and MRE (3.4 vs. 3.5 on PG, 3.3 vs. 3.6 on R/C). EDITPP's substitution operation reduces total edit operations by ~15% (199.65 vs. 234.52) and yields a 1.9× speedup over PSDIFF and 4.4× over AddThin at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDITPP unifies two previously separate research threads: (1) non-autoregressive TPP generation via stochastic set interpolation (ADDTHIN, PSDIFF), which uses only insert and delete operations in a discrete-time Markov chain, and (2) Edit Flows for discrete token sequences (Havasi et al., 2025), which formalize insert/delete/substitute as a CTMC trained via Bregman divergence in an auxiliary alignment space. The key conceptual departure is adapting the Edit Flow framework from discrete vocabularies to continuous event times, which requires a carefully designed Needleman-Wunsch alignment (rather than simple positional shifting) to respect the ordering constraints of TPPs and the bounded substitution distance δ. The move from discrete-time to continuous-time dynamics also inherits the κ-schedule and Euler-sampling machinery from discrete flow matching (Gat et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EDITPP provides the first continuous-time generative model for TPPs, enabling a compute-vs-quality trade-off at inference time that discrete-time baselines (ADDTHIN, PSDIFF) cannot offer without retraining. The substitution operation reduces total edit operations by ~15% and yields a ~2× speedup over PSDIFF, making joint-sequence TPP generation more practical for long sequences. Open questions include whether the Euler approximation error becomes problematic for highly bursty processes, whether the O(n·m) Needleman-Wunsch alignment scales to very long sequences (e.g., R/P with ~1100 events), and whether the unconditionally-trained model can be extended to multi-type or marked TPPs without architectural changes.
