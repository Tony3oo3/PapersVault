## Problem

Conventional image tokenizers implicitly encourage global collaborative encoding, making each token's representation depend on its non-causal (future) context in raster-scan order. This bidirectional dependency fundamentally conflicts with the strictly unidirectional prediction paradigm of decoder-only autoregressive models, rendering the next-token conditional distribution high-entropy and severely limiting generation quality. The paper asks whether the token sequence itself can be reshaped to possess forward-dependency, aligning the data with the model rather than modifying the model to fit the data.

## Input / Output

For tokenization, an RGB image (256×256 or 512×512) is split into 16×16 patches and encoded into a sequence of 272 discrete tokens (16 prefix tokens + 256 patch tokens) over a 4096-entry codebook. For class-conditional generation, the AR model takes a class label as conditioning and sequentially predicts the 272-token sequence, which is then decoded back into an image. The tokenizer also supports reconstruction: given the 272-token sequence, the (bidirectional) decoder reconstructs the original image, achieving rFID of 0.86 at 256×256.

## Method

AliTok's core mechanism is a bidirectional ViT encoder coupled with a causal ViT decoder during Stage-1 training: the decoder can only attend to preceding tokens in raster-scan order when reconstructing each patch, acting as an implicit regularizer that forces the encoder to pack all reconstruction-relevant context into each token's causal history. To handle the first-row context deficit caused by the causal constraint, 16 prefix tokens are introduced with a dedicated auxiliary loss (MSE + perceptual) that provides spatial priors for the topmost row. Stage-2 freezes the encoder and codebook, then retrains a separate bidirectional decoder (with 64 buffer tokens) to recover fine-grained reconstruction fidelity without disturbing the generation-friendly token structure. The generative model is a standard LlamaGen-style decoder-only transformer (177M–662M params) using 1D RoPE for prefix tokens, 2D RoPE for patch tokens, and QK-Norm, trained with classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, AliTok-XL (662M) achieves a gFID of 1.28 (w/ cfg) and 1.88 (w/o cfg), surpassing the SOTA diffusion model LightningDiT (1.35 w/ cfg, 2.17 w/o cfg) while sampling 10× faster. Even the smallest model, AliTok-B (177M), reaches gFID 1.44 (w/ cfg), outperforming LlamaGen-3B (2.18) with less than 6% of its parameters. On ImageNet-512, AliTok-L (318M) achieves a SOTA gFID of 1.39, beating MAR-L (1.73) and REPA (2.08). The tokenizer also achieves competitive reconstruction quality (rFID 0.86) with a 4096 codebook, matching GigaTok-B-L (rFID 0.81) which uses a 16384 codebook and 60% more parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AliTok-XL (662M) outperforms the SOTA diffusion model LightningDiT (675M) on both gFID w/o cfg (1.88 vs 2.17) and w/ cfg (1.28 vs 1.35), while achieving 10× faster sampling (6.3 vs 0.6 images/sec). Against the strongest autoregressive baseline RAR-XXL (1.5B), AliTok-L (318M) wins on gFID w/ cfg (1.38 vs 1.48) and IS (326.2 vs 326.0) with less than a quarter of the parameters. Compared to LlamaGen-3B (3B), AliTok-B (177M) achieves a substantially better gFID w/ cfg (1.44 vs 2.18) using only 6% of the parameters. On the 512×512 benchmark, AliTok-L (318M) achieves gFID 1.39, beating MAR-L (481M, gFID 1.73) and REPA (675M, gFID 2.08). In sampling throughput, AliTok-L (10.1 img/s) is 33.7× faster than MAR-H (0.3 img/s) and 2× faster than RAR-XXL (5.0 img/s) at comparable gFID. The only area where AliTok does not lead is reconstruction rFID, where GigaTok-B-L (0.81) slightly edges out AliTok Stage 2 (0.86) at 4096 codebook size, though AliTok matches it (0.81) when scaled to 16384.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant strategy in autoregressive image generation—modifying the model to handle bidirectional visual dependencies (via masked attention in MAR/MaskGIT, next-scale prediction in VAR, or random orders in RAR)—by instead reshaping the data: it redesigns the tokenizer so that encoded tokens inherently carry a causal, forward-only dependency structure. The tokenizer architecture builds on TA-TiTok (Kim et al., 2025) and the autoregressive backbone follows LlamaGen (Sun et al., 2024), but the core contribution is the causal-decoder-constrained bidirectional encoder, prefix tokens, and two-stage training that decouple semantic richness from causal predictability. This positions the work as a data-side complement to the model-side adaptations that have dominated the field since LlamaGen revealed the fundamental misalignment between raster-scan visual sequences and unidirectional transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a standard decoder-only AR model (662M) surpasses SOTA diffusion (LightningDiT) on ImageNet-256 gFID while sampling 10× faster is practically significant, as it validates the simplest possible AR paradigm for image generation and removes a key argument for more complex generation schedules. The conceptual insight—that the tokenizer's implicit bidirectional encoding, not the model architecture, is the primary bottleneck for AR image generation—is broadly applicable to any sequential generation task and could reshape how tokenizers are designed for multimodal unification. Open questions include whether the reconstruction FID ceiling (~1.15) can be broken with larger codebooks or continuous tokens, whether the approach scales to video or higher resolutions without the prefix-token overhead becoming non-negligible, and whether the 16384-codebook variant can close its generation gap with extended training.
