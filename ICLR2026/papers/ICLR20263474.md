## Problem

Existing benchmarks and RL training paradigms for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) are confined to isolated, single-horizon problems where questions and answers are independent. This leaves a critical gap: no systematic evaluation or training signal exists for multi-horizon scenarios where a model must solve a sequence of interdependent problems, each requiring the previous answer to proceed. The paper asks how far LRMs can truly go in reasoning breadth and depth when forced to maintain correctness across long, dependent reasoning chains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263474/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes single-horizon seed problems (math, code, or agentic) that contain integer answers and identifiable key variables, then composes them into sequential dependency chains of length n (up to 20 for math, 5 for code/agentic). The composed input is a short prompt containing all sub-problems with explicit dependency constraints, but the expected output is a long CoT response (up to 64k tokens) containing answers to every sub-problem. Evaluation uses all-or-nothing scoring via GPT-4.1-based answer extraction rather than rule-based pattern matching, since models increasingly fail to follow output format as problem count grows.

## Method

R-HORIZON constructs multi-horizon reasoning tasks through a two-stage pipeline: (1) seed problem filtering that retains only problems with integer answers and verified key variables (integers whose removal makes the problem unsolvable), and (2) dependency chain construction where each subsequent problem's key variable is replaced by a placeholder resolved via a linear dependency function f_i(x) = x + (m_{i+1} − a_i) on the previous problem's answer. For evaluation, this yields a 6-dataset benchmark spanning math, code, and agentic tasks with all-or-nothing accuracy and an expected-accuracy baseline computed from atomic pass rates. For training, R-HORIZON data is used in RLVR with GRPO, employing two reward schemes (last-answer-only vs. all-correct) to study how reward structure shapes long-horizon reasoning. The key finding is that training on even 2-query composed data substantially improves both multi-horizon performance (+17.4 on AIME24 n=2) and single-horizon performance (+7.5 on AIME24), while also improving thinking budget allocation and reducing overthinking.

## Results

On the R-HORIZON benchmark, even the strongest models suffer severe degradation: DeepSeek-R1 drops from 87.3% (n=1) to 24.6% (n=5) on AIME25, and R1-Qwen-7B falls from 93.6% (n=1) to 0% (n=16) on MATH500. Training R1-Qwen-7B with 2-query composed data (R_last reward) yields +7.5 on AIME24 original tasks and +17.4 on AIME24 (n=2) composed tasks over the base model, while the n=4 configuration achieves the highest multi-horizon average (43.2 vs. 20.1 baseline). Using R_all reward with n=2 further improves multi-horizon average to 40.2 and AIME24 (n=2) to 38.8, outperforming R_last on composed scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between standard single-problem RLVR training (n=1) and R-HORIZON composed-data training (n=2, n=4, mixed) on R1-Qwen-7B. Composed data with n=2 achieves 65.4 on AIME24 Origin (vs. 57.9 for n=1) and 34.1 on AIME24 n=2 (vs. 16.7), demonstrating that multi-horizon training transfers to both single and multi-problem settings. The n=4 configuration further extends this, reaching 50.6 on MATH500 n=8 (vs. 8.4 for n=1) and 43.2 multi-horizon average (vs. 21.3). For reward design, R_all with n=2 outperforms R_last on composed tasks (38.8 vs. 34.1 on AIME24 n=2; 83.4 vs. 80.6 on AMC23 n=2) but slightly underperforms on MATH500 n=8 (26.8 vs. 21.4 is actually R_all winning here: 26.8 > 21.4). The mixed configuration (n=1,2,3,4) achieves the best MATH500 Origin score (96.8) and competitive multi-horizon average (43.1), suggesting curriculum-style mixing is effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

R-HORIZON extends the test-time scaling and RLVR training paradigm established by OpenAI o1 and DeepSeek-R1 by addressing a gap in how long-horizon reasoning is evaluated and trained. It departs from NEST/REST, which concatenate independent problems without logical dependencies, and from GSM-Infinite, which builds dependency chains but focuses on long-context input rather than long CoT output. The method builds directly on Skywork-OR1's multi-stage RLVR pipeline and GRPO, repurposing them with composed multi-horizon data and novel reward schemes (last-only vs. all-correct) to study how dependency structure shapes reasoning behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even frontier models like DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3-235B-Thinking suffer severe, systematic degradation on multi-horizon dependent tasks, exposing concrete failure modes (limited effective reasoning length, localized reflection, poor thinking-budget allocation) that single-problem benchmarks mask. The finding that RLVR with composed data transfers to single-problem gains (+7.5 AIME24) suggests a low-cost, scalable path to improve general reasoning efficiency. Open questions include whether the dependency construction generalizes beyond arithmetic substitution, whether the effective-reasoning-length boundary shifts with model scale or training, and whether the observed data-contamination anomalies (models solving 'unsolvable' dependent problems) indicate deeper memorization issues in current LRMs.
