## Problem

Multi-task Bayesian optimization methods that share a surrogate across tasks (multi-output GPs, deep kernel transfer) saturate in performance after only tens of training tasks and fail to extract additional gains from hundreds or thousands of related tasks. The paper asks whether an LLM-based initialization strategy can break this saturation by distilling prior optimization experience into a model that scales with the number of solved tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263423/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a task-specific textual context (a full SQL query string for database plan optimization, or a seed amino acid sequence for antimicrobial peptide design) and feeds it to a fine-tuned LLM (BOLT-T) that generates a fixed-size batch of candidate solutions (50 query plans or 1,000 peptide sequences). These candidates are decoded from the LLM's token output, filtered for syntactic validity, and mapped into a VAE latent space where a standard single-task BO loop (LOL-BO with a PPGPR surrogate) refines them over 4,000 (DB) or 20,000 (peptide) oracle calls. The LLM is never used as a surrogate or acquisition function at test time; it contributes only the initialization set.

## Method

BOLT decouples cross-task transfer from the test-time surrogate by using an LLM strictly as an initialization policy. The outer loop periodically fine-tunes GPT-4O-MINI on (task context, top-K BO solutions) pairs collected from completed optimization runs, minimizing the negative log-likelihood of solution tokens given the context. The inner loop then runs a standard single-task BO (constrained LOL-BO in a VAE latent space) starting from the LLM's sampled candidates, with the GP surrogate remaining entirely single-task. A self-augmentation step further refines the LLM by prompting it to generate additional solutions, scoring them with the problem oracle, and fine-tuning on its own best outputs without additional BO runs. This closed loop—BO discovers solutions, LLM is fine-tuned on them, LLM provides stronger starts for the next BO round—scales to roughly 1,500 tasks without the plateau observed in shared-surrogate MTBO, and after sufficient fine-tuning the LLM's few-shot samples alone often match or surpass full from-scratch BO runs.

## Results

On the database query optimization task (2,933 queries from the Cardinality Estimation Benchmark), BOLT-1426 achieves a Best@50 summed runtime of 61.54 s across 99 validation queries, surpassing PostgreSQL in a single sample and outperforming BAO's best-of-50 (106.72 s) by ~42%. On the antimicrobial peptide design task (1,000 extinct seed peptides), BOLT-600 reaches a summed MIC of 92.10 at 1,000 oracle calls versus 625.95 for STBO/MTBO baselines. BOLT scales to ~1,426 tasks without the saturation observed in shared-surrogate MTBO (which plateaus after ~20 tasks), and adds only ~1% compute overhead relative to single-task BO at full workload scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOLT decisively outperforms all shared-surrogate MTBO baselines: DKT/FSBO (Patacchiola 2020; Wistuba 2021) plateaus after ~20 training tasks while BOLT continues improving through 1,426 tasks; POGPE/SGPE (Schilling 2016) are consistently worse than BOLT and even fall behind plain STBO in several configurations, with quadratic scaling making them infeasible at scale. Among LLM-based MTBO methods, Optformer (Chen 2022) performs slightly worse than STBO on the DB task and significantly worse on peptides, while LLAMBO (Liu 2024) shows minimal optimization progress (fewer than 100 steps within its 10M-token budget) and is substantially outperformed by BOLT on both domains. Against the domain-specific BAO optimizer (Marcus 2021), BOLT-1426 surpasses BAO's best-of-50 in a single sample, and all BOLT variants (even BOLT-50) outperform BAO within 5 samples. At the initialization stage, BOLT-1426 starts at 7.43 s summed runtime versus 8.23 s for STBO and 9.18 s for reusing prior best solutions, confirming that task-conditioned LLM generation provides benefits beyond simple solution reuse or local perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOLT departs from the dominant multi-task BO paradigm of embedding cross-task information into a shared surrogate (multi-output GPs, deep kernel transfer via DKT/FSBO, GP expert ensembles like POGPE/SGPE), which the paper shows saturates after tens of tasks. It also contrasts with recent LLM-based MTBO systems (Optformer, LLAMBO) that use the LLM as a surrogate or trajectory predictor within the BO loop. Instead, BOLT decouples transfer entirely from the test-time surrogate: the LLM is fine-tuned on BO-discovered solutions and used strictly to propose initialization candidates, after which a standard single-task BO run proceeds unchanged. The self-augmentation step draws on the self-instruction literature for LLMs, but applies it in the context of optimization trajectories rather than instruction-following.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that BOLT scales to ~1,400+ tasks without the performance saturation observed in shared-surrogate MTBO, while adding only ~1% amortized compute overhead—making it viable for high-throughput domains like database query optimization and peptide design where thousands of related tasks arise naturally. The finding that a few LLM samples can match or surpass full BO runs after sufficient fine-tuning opens the door to extremely low-latency deployment (single-shot query planning competitive with PostgreSQL). Limitations include the requirement for a shared input domain and concise textual task descriptions, which excludes data-differentiated MTBO settings (e.g., hyperparameter optimization across datasets). Open questions include whether the closed-loop scaling advantage persists with larger or more diverse LLMs, and whether the approach extends to tasks where the 'context' is high-dimensional or non-textual.
