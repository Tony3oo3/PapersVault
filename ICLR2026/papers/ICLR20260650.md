## Problem

Current LLM alignment methods (DPO, RLHF) optimize for surface-level, outcome-based signals, creating a dual gap: models fail to infer users' deep implicit preferences (unstated goals, risk tolerances, semantic context) and lack the defensive reasoning to proactively identify and mitigate latent risks in ambiguous queries. This leads to responses that are technically compliant with explicit constraints but violate the user's deeper principles of autonomy, privacy, or narrative control.

## Input / Output

The method takes a detailed user preference profile P (containing nuanced, often conflicting values and unstated goals, e.g., 'I value convenience but am extremely privacy-conscious') paired with a deliberately open-ended, ambiguous query q. It produces a personalized text response y that addresses the user's latent intent, along with a step-wise reasoning chain where each step is annotated with a textual critique and a scalar quality score. The preference profile is consumed as structured context throughout both the reward modeling and policy generation stages, rather than being reduced to a single prompt instruction.

## Method

CDRA comprises three components. First, the DeepPref dataset (3000 preference-query pairs across 20 domains) is constructed by simulating a 5-persona cognitive council (Sociologist, Psychologist, Pragmatist, Pedagogue, Contrarian) within a Tree-of-Thoughts framework to generate diverse reasoning paths, which are then annotated step-by-step with textual critiques and scalar scores by an LLM evaluator. Second, Pers-GenPRM is trained via SFT to operate as a step-wise generative critic: given the preference, query, and partial reasoning chain, it first produces an explicit textual critique of the current step, then derives a scalar reward grounded in that critique, making the reward signal interpretable and auditable. Third, CDPA (Critique-Driven Policy Alignment) is an online RL algorithm built on GRPO that assigns a token-level advantage to each position based on the step-wise critique-grounded rewards, normalized across a group of G=5 sampled responses, and updates the policy via a PPO-style clipped objective with KL regularization. The policy is first initialized via Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning on the highest-scoring DeepPref paths before entering the RL loop, and the dense process-level reward resolves the 'zero-advantage' problem where multiple responses achieve the same final outcome but differ in reasoning quality.

## Results

On the DeepPref benchmark, CDRA achieves 84.7% Preference Following Accuracy (AccPF) and 76.3% Deep Alignment Accuracy (AccDA), with a Misleading Risk (AccMis) of 32.3%. On PrefEval, CDRA reaches 68.8% AccPF and 62.5% AccDA. In human evaluation on the multi-turn ALOE dataset, CDRA attains the highest average alignment score of 3.92/5, peaking at 4.6 in later conversational turns, while baselines plateau or degrade.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDRA outperforms the strongest RL baseline GRPO on Deep Alignment Accuracy by 6.0 points on DeepPref (76.3% vs. 70.3%) and 10.7 points on PrefEval (62.5% vs. 51.8%), and leads in Innovative Expansion by 8.7 points on DeepPref (42.7% vs. 34.0%). Against SFT, CDRA gains 1.3 points on AccDA and 2.4 points on mie on DeepPref, while matching SFT's AccPF (84.7% vs. 83.3%). CDRA trades a slightly higher Misleading Risk than GRPO (32.3% vs. 30.7% on DeepPref) for substantially better deep preference discovery. On PrefEval, CDRA wins on AccPF and AccDA but trails GRPO and CoT on Thoughtfulness (27.7% vs. 53.6%/54.5%), reflecting a design trade-off favoring novel preference expansion over structured option presentation. In the ALOE human evaluation, CDRA achieves the highest average alignment (3.92/5) over TPO (3.86), CoT (3.72), SFT (3.68), and GRPO (3.18), with its advantage growing in later turns (4.6 at turn 7 vs. 4.2 for TPO). All in-context baselines (Zero-shot, Few-shot, CoT) and TPO are outperformed by wide margins across every metric on both benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (DeepSeek-R1) by replacing its scalar outcome or step-wise rewards with a generative, critique-grounded process reward signal. It draws on the step-wise process supervision paradigm from Lightman et al. (2023) and the Tree-of-Thoughts data construction methodology from Yao et al. (2023), but departs from both by framing reward modeling itself as a reasoning task: the Pers-GenPRM must first produce an explicit textual critique before emitting a score, making the reward interpretable and causally anchored to a rationale. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the DPO/RLHF lineage that optimizes for surface-level preference matching, arguing that outcome-based supervision creates an irreducible 'preference gap' and 'process gap' that only process-level critique supervision can bridge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DeepPref benchmark (3000 critique-annotated preference-query pairs across 20 domains) is a practically useful resource for evaluating whether models reason about latent user intent rather than merely following explicit instructions. The generative reward model design—where a critique precedes and grounds the numerical score—offers a template for making RL reward signals auditable, which is increasingly important as alignment systems are deployed in high-stakes personalization settings. However, the empirical gains over GRPO are moderate (e.g., ~6% on DeepPref AccDA), the evaluation relies on a single LLM-as-judge (DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp) with acknowledged homology risk, and all experiments use a single 7B base model, leaving open whether the critique-driven advantage signal scales to larger architectures or truly divergent preference distributions. The trade-off observed on PrefEval (gains in deep mining but losses in thoughtfulness) suggests the approach may over-index on novelty at the expense of collaborative tone, a tension that future work should address.
