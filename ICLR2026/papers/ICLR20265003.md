## Problem

Most deep neural network-based sound source localization methods are tightly coupled to specific microphone array geometries and fixed DOA grids, requiring retraining whenever either changes. Existing geometry-invariant and grid-flexible approaches alleviate some constraints but still fail to achieve robust, universal SSL across arbitrary geometries and grids without significant performance degradation under unseen conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265003/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes C-channel time-domain audio (16 kHz, 4-second utterances), the 3D Cartesian coordinates of each microphone, and a user-specified set of D candidate DOA points sampled on a Fibonacci sphere. It produces a probabilistic spatial spectrum of shape D×L (one sigmoid-activated likelihood per grid point per frame), from which up to T source DOAs are extracted via iterative max-peak selection with a 10° suppression margin. Critically, D and the array geometry can be changed at inference time without retraining, and the number of channels C is variable (4–16 in experiments).

## Method

AGG-RL jointly learns two representations in a shared latent space: audio-geometry representations (AGRs) from AuGeonet and grid representations (GRs) from Gridnet, whose scaled inner product (followed by sigmoid) yields the spatial spectrum. AuGeonet integrates two physics-informed components: a learnable non-uniform DFT (LNuDFT) that treats inter-bin frequency spacing as learnable parameters to densely allocate bins in acoustically informative mid-frequency regions, and a relative microphone positional encoding (rMPE) that encodes each microphone's position relative to a reference channel in spherical coordinates, consistent with the physics of TDOA. The AGR extraction pipeline applies LNuDFT-based GCC-PHAT (reference-channel scheme, O(C) complexity), concatenates relative Cartesian and sinusoidal spherical coordinates, then processes through residual convolutions, channel-wise multi-head self-attention with rMPE, and frame-wise GRUs. Gridnet is a small 3-block DNN that maps sinusoidally-encoded candidate DOAs into G-dimensional GRs, trained independently of audio and geometry. Training uses soft-labeled oracle spatial spectra with varying beamwidths under a weighted BCE loss, combined with multi-stage geometry learning and deeply supervised curriculum learning for stable convergence.

## Results

The proposed AGG-RL framework achieves the best MAE and ACC₁₀ across all four evaluation datasets: 8.25°/90.78% on NAO robot (12-ch, seen), 11.24°/72.17% on Eigenmike (32-ch, unseen), 10.32°/77.34% on Dynamic-S (4–12 ch, seen), and 14.12°/63.17% on Dynamic-U (13–16 ch, unseen). The largest gains appear under unseen conditions, where the method reduces MAE by over 40° compared to GI-DOAEnet on Eigenmike (11.24° vs. 93.61°) and by ~41° on Dynamic-U (14.12° vs. 54.81°). On the STARSS23 real-world benchmark, the proposed method also leads with 27.32° MAE and 36.09% ACC₁₀, outperforming Unet (43.91°/23.55%) and Neural-SRP (55.54°/5.74%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest DNN baseline, GI-DOAEnet, the gap is most dramatic under unseen conditions: on Eigenmike (32-ch), GI-DOAEnet-FM collapses to 93.61° MAE / 0% ACC₁₀ while the proposed method achieves 11.24° / 72.17%; on Dynamic-U (13–16 ch), GI-DOAEnet-FM scores 54.81° / 6.10% versus 14.12° / 63.17% for the proposed method. Neural-SRP shows a similar failure mode on Eigenmike (52.75° MAE) but remains competitive on seen conditions, where the proposed method still leads by ~1.5° MAE on NAO robot (8.25° vs. 9.72°) and ~9° on Dynamic-S (10.32° vs. 19.60°). Unet is the most consistent DNN baseline but is outperformed by 2.6–9.6° MAE across all four datasets. Classical methods (MUSIC, SRP-PHAT) are uniformly inferior, with MAE values 2–4× higher than the proposed method, confirming that the learned representation approach provides substantial gains over handcrafted spatial filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GI-DOAEnet (Baek et al., 2025), which introduced absolute microphone positional encoding (aMPE) and channel-wise multi-head self-attention for geometry-invariant SSL, by replacing aMPE with a physics-grounded relative microphone positional encoding (rMPE) and the standard DFT with a learnable non-uniform DFT (LNuDFT). The AGG-RL framework departs from prior fixed-grid classification and template-matching approaches by jointly learning audio-geometry and grid representations in a shared latent space, enabling flexible-grid SSL without retraining. The LNuDFT component generalizes the classical NuDFT (Bagchi & Mitra, 1996) by making frequency bin locations learnable rather than fixed, while the rMPE design is directly motivated by the physics of TDOA depending on relative rather than absolute microphone positions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in SSL: most DNN-based methods require retraining when the microphone array geometry or DOA grid changes, limiting deployment across diverse hardware. The physics-informed components (LNuDFT, rMPE) provide interpretability by concentrating frequency resolution in acoustically informative bands and encoding geometry in a physically consistent relative form. Open questions include the optimal initialization strategy for LNuDFT (the paper acknowledges this was chosen empirically), theoretical analysis of why relative representations mitigate MHSA length-extrapolation failure, and extension to multimodal or moving-source scenarios. The persistent performance gap between seen and unseen conditions, while much smaller than for baselines, suggests further gains are possible.
