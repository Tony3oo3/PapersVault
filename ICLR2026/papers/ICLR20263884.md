## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods face a fundamental tension: LoRA's matrix factorization imposes a hard lower bound on trainable parameters tied to rank r, while tensor-based variants like LoRETTA and QuanTA treat each layer's decomposition independently, causing parameter counts to scale linearly with model depth. No prior approach combines tensor decomposition with systematic cross-layer freezing and sharing to reach extremely low-parameter regimes (e.g., <0.1M trainable parameters for 13B models) without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

TRAC takes a frozen pretrained model (text encoders/decoders or vision transformers) and standard inputs (text sequences or RGB images) and produces a compact set of trainable parameters: per-layer G1 tensor cores, one shared G3 core per matrix type, and per-layer 1-D controller vectors b and d. At inference, the adaptation is merged into the pretrained weights without architectural change, yielding standard outputs (class logits, generated tokens). The unusual aspect is that the dominant cores (G2) are randomly initialized and permanently frozen, so the actual gradient-updated footprint is orders of magnitude smaller than the full TT representation would suggest.

## Method

TRAC replaces each LoRA matrix A and B with a 3rd-order Tensor-Train decomposition whose three cores play distinct roles: G1 (trainable, layer-specific), G2 (frozen, randomly initialized, shared across all layers of the same matrix type), and G3 (shared across layers but multiplicatively gated by two lightweight per-layer vector controllers b ∈ R^{r2} and d ∈ R^{n3} with task-dependent activations). This across-layer compression reduces the total trainable parameter order from O(Lnr) (LoRA) or O(Ln^{1/3}r²) (LoRETTA) to O(n^{1/3}r²), independent of depth L. Asymmetric TT-ranks can be assigned to A and B (e.g., 3× larger ranks for B) to exploit the empirically observed greater importance of the B matrix. A specialized TT-normal initialization accounts for tensor ranks and shape factors to ensure variance stability, and the theoretical analysis shows that shared-core approximation error is bounded by a constant times the error of approximating the shared component, justifying the design.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa-Base, TRAC achieves an average score of 85.0 using only 0.026M trainable parameters, matching LoRA (85.6) while using 19× fewer parameters (0.295M) and outperforming VeRA (83.9) and LoRETTA (83.2). On SuperGLUE with LLaMA2-13B, TRAC achieves 88.6 average accuracy with 0.166M parameters, surpassing LoRA (88.3) by 0.3 points while using 20× fewer parameters (3.28M). On image classification with ViT-Large, TRAC reaches 92.60 average top-1 accuracy with 0.056M parameters, outperforming LoRA (91.74) by 0.86 points with 14× fewer parameters (0.786M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRAC consistently outperforms VeRA and LoRETTA across all evaluated benchmarks: on GLUE (RoBERTa-Base), TRAC scores 85.0 vs. VeRA's 83.9 and LoRETTA's 83.2; on SuperGLUE (LLaMA2-13B), TRAC achieves 88.6 vs. VeRA's 87.3 and LoRETTA's 87.6. Against LoRA, TRAC trades a small accuracy gap (e.g., 85.0 vs. 85.6 on GLUE-Base) for a 19× parameter reduction, but actually surpasses LoRA on SuperGLUE-13B (88.6 vs. 88.3), E2E text generation (BLEU 70.3 vs. 68.9 on GPT-2 Medium), and image classification (92.60 vs. 91.74 on ViT-Large). On the E2E benchmark, TRAC also outperforms AdaLoRA (BLEU 70.3 vs. 68.2) and VB-LoRA (70.3 vs. 70.0) while using fewer trainable parameters. In the commonsense reasoning and math/code benchmarks (LLaMA3-8B, Qwen3-8B), TRAC matches or slightly exceeds LoRA (e.g., 81.33 vs. 80.44 avg on commonsense; 64.90 vs. 64.67 on GSM8K) with only 35–59% of LoRA's parameters. Overall, TRAC's key advantage is achieving near-LoRA or better accuracy at 10–20× lower parameter budgets, making it particularly suited for scenarios requiring storage of many user-specific adapters.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRAC sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: (1) matrix-factorization-based PEFT (LoRA, VeRA, AdaLoRA, VB-LoRA) that exploits cross-layer redundancy but is bounded below by the discrete rank r, and (2) tensor-decomposition-based PEFT (LoRETTA, TT-LoRA, QuanTA, LoTR) that offers finer parameter granularity but treats each layer's tensor cores independently, so parameters still scale with depth L. The paper's core departure is to apply Tensor-Train decomposition to LoRA's A and B matrices while strategically freezing one core and sharing another across all layers of the same type, augmented by per-layer vector controllers. This unifies the two directions: the TT structure provides the expressiveness and rank flexibility that matrix factorization lacks, while the across-layer freeze/share mechanism removes the linear-in-L parameter growth that independent tensor cores incur.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRAC's parameter order drops from O(Lnr) (LoRA) or O(Ln^{1/3}r^2) (LoRETTA) to O(n^{1/3}r^2), eliminating the dependence on model depth L — a meaningful asymptotic gain for very deep transformers. The practical implication is significant for service providers storing millions of user-specific adapters, where even LoRA's reduced footprint becomes non-trivial. Open questions remain: the theoretical analysis of shared-structure approximation (Appendix B.2) relies on the somewhat strong assumption that layer matrices factor as Q_0 R^l with a common Q_0, and the paper acknowledges that rigorous analysis of how freezing/sharing affects TT representational capacity is still needed. Additionally, automated search over tensor shapes, ranks, and which cores to freeze/share remains an open practical challenge.
