## Problem

LLMs are highly sensitive to small prompt perturbations (spelling errors, word substitutions, irrelevant insertions), causing significant performance degradation in real-world settings with strict output formats. Existing mitigations rely on external preprocessing pipelines (grammar checkers, LLM-based rewriting) that add inference latency, incur cascading errors, and ignore the model's own capacity to handle noisy inputs. The paper asks whether intrinsic prompt robustness can be built into the model itself through post-training, eliminating the need for auxiliary components.

## Input / Output

During training, the model receives triplets: a noisy prompt P′, its corresponding clean prompt P̂₁, and an unrelated clean prompt P̂₂, each concatenated with the ground-truth label y. A masking operator retains only the logits at label-token positions (discarding prompt positions), yielding per-token conditional distributions over the vocabulary. At inference, the model takes a single (potentially noisy) text prompt and produces a standard text response or classification label with no additional preprocessing overhead. Perturbations span character-level (DeepWordBug), word-level (TextFooler), and sentence-level (CheckList, StressTest) granularities.

## Method

CoIPO inverts the standard DPO comparison direction: rather than comparing different outputs under the same input, it fixes the ground-truth label and compares the conditional logit distributions of different prompts (noisy vs. correct clean vs. unrelated clean) at label positions. The loss minimizes the sequence-level KL divergence between the noisy prompt's label logits and the correct clean prompt's label logits, while simultaneously maximizing the KL divergence to an unrelated clean prompt's label logits, effectively a contrastive preference signal over inputs. The theoretical justification derives from mutual information: minimizing the CoIPO loss is shown to be equivalent to maximizing the relative mutual information gain ΔÎ_q = KL(q‖p_θ(·|P̂₂)) − KL(q‖p_θ(·|P̂₁)), where q is the model's output distribution under the noisy prompt, providing an information-theoretic guarantee that the correct prompt becomes more discriminative relative to incorrect prompts in the noisy context. Training is performed via LoRA fine-tuning (r=16, α=16) on a Paired FLAN dataset where each clean prompt is augmented with a randomly selected perturbation type, using a contrastive loss ratio of 1000 and temperature 0.05.

## Results

On NoisyPromptBench (5 GLUE datasets × 4 perturbation types), CoIPO achieves 83.45% average accuracy on Qwen2.5-7B, surpassing COIN (81.48%), SFT (76.85%), and Base (72.24%). Under perturbed prompts, CoIPO exhibits only a 0.54% average accuracy drop on Qwen (vs. 1.81% for COIN and 3.76% for Base), and 3.88% on Llama-7B (vs. 4.08% for COIN and 10.58% for Base). On broader capability benchmarks (GSM8K, TruthfulQA, MBPP), CoIPO does not degrade performance and in several cases slightly improves over the base model (e.g., GSM8K exact match: 86.28% vs. 80.97% on Qwen2.5-72B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against COIN (Contrastive Instruction Tuning, the closest intrinsic-robustness baseline), CoIPO wins on average accuracy by 1.97% on Qwen2.5-7B (83.45% vs. 81.48%) and 5.30% on Llama-7B (63.90% vs. 58.60%), while also reducing the perturbation-induced accuracy drop from 1.81% to 0.54% on Qwen. Against the pre-processing baseline PromptAgent, CoIPO is dramatically superior on MRPC: 83.45% average accuracy versus PromptAgent's 39.61%, a gap of over 43 points, with PromptAgent failing even on the clean setting. Against BATprompt (adversarial prompt rewriting), CoIPO again leads by a wide margin (83.45% vs. 73.42% average on MRPC), and additionally incurs zero extra inference latency whereas BATprompt adds ~16 seconds per sample. The ablation confirms that both the Inverse DPO and Contrastive Learning components contribute: removing either degrades performance (InvDPO alone: 82.86%; CL alone: 81.48% on Qwen), but their combination in CoIPO yields the best result (83.45%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the standard DPO formulation (Rafailov et al., 2023), which compares different outputs under a fixed input, into an 'Inverse DPO' that compares different inputs under a fixed ground-truth label, and combines this with contrastive learning at the logit level (as opposed to COIN's hidden-state contrastive learning, Yan et al., 2024). It departs from the dominant line of prompt-robustness work (PromptAgent, BAT, RoP) that relies on external preprocessing pipelines by instead enhancing intrinsic model robustness through a single LoRA post-training step, eliminating inference-time overhead. The mutual information analysis in Section 3.3 provides a principled information-theoretic derivation showing the CoIPO loss is equivalent to maximizing relative mutual information gain, grounding the heuristic contrastive design in a formal objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that CoIPO achieves near-zero performance degradation on Qwen (0.54% average drop vs. 3.76% for base) with no additional inference cost, making it directly deployable in noisy real-world settings like customer service or office assistants. The NoisyPromptBench, with its multi-repetition perturbation protocol, addresses a gap in existing benchmarks (PromptBench only supports single-step perturbations) and could become a standard evaluation tool. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more semantically complex noise (e.g., adversarial paraphrasing, multi-turn dialogue drift), whether it helps with open-ended generation quality under noise (the GSM8K/TruthfulQA/MBPP results are mixed), and whether the gains persist as models scale beyond 72B or as perturbation types become more adversarial.
