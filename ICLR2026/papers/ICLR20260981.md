## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods for LLMs rely heavily on supervised fine-tuning, forcing student models to mimic fixed teacher outputs and encouraging superficial pattern memorization that breaks down under even mild distribution shifts. The paper identifies that this generalization limitation is not merely inherited but actively amplified in distilled models, as exemplified by the reversal curse where a model solves a forward problem but fails its inverse. Prior attempts like RevThink address specific failure modes through A-to-Q data augmentation but still encourage directional mapping rather than genuine conceptual understanding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text reasoning question (e.g., math word problems, commonsense QA, NLI) as input and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer. During training, the input is expanded into a multi-turn dialogue where k randomly sampled explanatory probes (out of 10 EI rule categories) are presented sequentially before the original question, and the student must respond to each probe and then answer the original question. At inference, the distilled model processes the input in a single pass without any probes, producing reasoning and answer directly.

## Method

The framework has three stages: (1) Explanatory Inversion (EI) generates up to 10 categories of cognitively-inspired 'explanatory probes' from teacher CoT traces (e.g., counterfactual scenarios, why-based transformations, decomposition), filtered for consistency and difficulty; (2) SFT warm-up on the curated EI-augmented dataset aligns the student with teacher reasoning; (3) ExGRPO, a novel RL algorithm adapting GRPO, refines the student via multi-turn probe dialogues where a Dialogue Structure Utility Bonus (r_dsu) is awarded when the full k-turn dialogue yields better final-answer accuracy than a partial k'-turn dialogue, explicitly rewarding coherent processing of the entire explanatory sequence. An auxiliary SFT loss (L_SFT-aux) provides imitation-based regularization on intermediate probe responses, and a KL penalty anchors the policy to the SFT-warmed reference. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) shows that the clipped policy update with r_dsu ensures monotonic improvement in expected outcome reward when the full dialogue is more informative than the partial one.

## Results

On eight held-in reasoning datasets, ExGRPO achieves an average accuracy of 82.54% with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (+4.55% over its zero-shot baseline of 77.99%) and 67.19% with Gemma-7B-it (+20.39% over its zero-shot baseline of 46.80%). The method also improves the Gemini-1.5-Pro teacher's own zero-shot performance by +2.22% (85.70% vs. 83.48%) when using EI-augmented prompts. On OOD datasets (BoolQ, OpenBookQA, e-SNLI, GSM8K-Reversal), ExGRPO gains +8.47% (Qwen) and +13.00% (Gemma) over zero-shot, outperforming the strongest baseline RevThink by +3.28% and +4.89% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExGRPO outperforms all 8 baselines on average for both student models. Against the strongest KD baseline (On-Policy Distillation), ExGRPO gains +0.94% (Qwen: 82.54 vs. 81.60) and +1.79% (Gemma: 67.19 vs. 65.40). Against the strongest data-augmentation baseline (RevThink), ExGRPO gains +1.65% (Qwen: 82.54 vs. 80.89) and +6.02% (Gemma: 67.19 vs. 61.17). The gap is most pronounced for the weaker Gemma student, where ExGRPO's RL-based refinement yields substantially larger gains than SFT-only methods like SKD (+12.54%) or Distill Step-by-Step (+12.46%). On GSM8K specifically, ExGRPO is the only method that improves upon the already-strong Qwen zero-shot score (91.46% vs. 90.90%), while all other baselines degrade. On OOD evaluation, ExGRPO leads RevThink by +3.28% (Qwen) and +4.89% (Gemma) on average, with the largest single-dataset margin on GSM8K-Reversal (+3.82% Qwen, +4.76% Gemma over RevThink).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation pipeline for LLMs by replacing the standard SFT-only training with a two-stage SFT + RL procedure, and by introducing Explanatory Inversion (EI) as a cognitively-motivated data augmentation strategy that goes beyond the A-to-Q reversal used by RevThink. The RL component adapts GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) to the distillation setting via a novel Dialogue Structure Utility Bonus, which is necessary because standard GRPO's outcome-only reward cannot supervise intermediate reasoning steps present in teacher traces. The EI probe design draws on cognitive science literature on explanation-seeking and counterfactual reasoning, positioning the work at the intersection of KD, rule-based RL, and data-centric augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete recipe for making distilled LLMs generalize better, with the practical finding that RL during distillation (not just post-training) combined with structured probing yields 6%+ gains over the strongest KD baselines and 20%+ gains for weaker student models. Key limitations include the manual design of 10 EI probe templates (raising scalability questions), dependence on a strong proprietary teacher for probe quality, and OOD evaluation limited to structural perturbations rather than broad domain shifts. Open questions include whether automated or learned probe generation can replace the hand-crafted templates, and whether the DSU reward generalizes to multi-teacher or multi-modal distillation settings.
