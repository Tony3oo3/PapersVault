## Problem

Federated LoRA fine-tuning of LLMs is vulnerable to gradient inversion attacks that reconstruct private training data, yet existing privacy defenses (DP, MPC, full HE) either degrade model accuracy, impose prohibitive costs, or fail under heterogeneous device capabilities and Non-IID data. Existing selective HE methods further suffer from two structural issues: LoRA matrix multiplication expands the set of parameters requiring encryption, and heterogeneous clients selecting different encryption positions causes ciphertext inflation during aggregation.

## Input / Output

Each client provides local private training data, a frozen pre-trained model (BERT-Large or OpenLLaMA-3B), and device-specific constraints including compute capability (GFlops), LoRA rank (8–32), and an encryption budget γ_i (0.125%–1.6% of parameters). The server outputs per-client LoRA adapter pairs (B̂ ∈ R^{m×r_i}, Â ∈ R^{r_i×n}) reparameterized via SVD to match each client's local rank, where sensitive columns are protected by CKKS ciphertexts and the remaining columns are transmitted in plaintext. The encryption budget directly determines the number of columns k_i = ⌊n·γ_i⌋ that each client encrypts, making ciphertext size proportional to device capability.

## Method

SHE-LoRA integrates selective homomorphic encryption into federated LoRA through four coordinated steps. First, an HE subset negotiation mechanism uses Wanda-based column-wise sensitivity scores (|W_ij|·‖x_j‖₂), order-preserving encryption to hide client preferences, and Bayesian-optimized coefficients to select a global encrypted column set that jointly maximizes worst-case coverage and minimizes worst-case privacy risk. Second, column-swapping obfuscation clusters encrypted columns to the right and unencrypted columns to the left, enabling batch CKKS encryption, efficient plaintext matrix operations, and positional obfuscation that increases adversarial uncertainty. Third, a column-aware adaptive aggregation performs separate column-wise weighted averaging on plaintext and ciphertext blocks, correctly handling heterogeneous shapes (different k_i per client) without the mathematical inconsistency of naive separate A/B aggregation. Fourth, each client reparameterizes the merged plaintext and decrypted ciphertext SVD results back into LoRA matrices at its own rank, with a formal proof that no meaningful model update is lost in the process.

## Results

SHE-LoRA reduces mean encryption time by 99.87%–98.10% (vs. full-HE Baseline) and 99.81%–99.31% (vs. MaskCrypt) on OpenLLaMA-3B, and reduces communication overhead by 99.71%–98.18% on OpenLLaMA-3B and 98.78%–98.18% on Bert-Large. Against the DAGER gradient-inversion attack on SST-2, SHE-LoRA achieves near-zero reconstruction scores (R-1 ≤ 0.98, R-2 ≤ 0.14) at γ=0.3%, while Flex-LoRA scores 95.18 R-1 and MaskCrypt scores 89.16 R-1 at batch size 4. On Rotten Tomatoes, SHE-LoRA scores 0.0 across all batch sizes, completely defeating DAGER. Model tuning performance is reported as comparable to the SOTA heterogeneous method Flex-LoRA on GLUE and MMLU benchmarks (detailed in Appendix F.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA heterogeneous LoRA method Flex-LoRA (Bai et al., 2024), SHE-LoRA matches model tuning performance on GLUE and MMLU while providing orders-of-magnitude stronger privacy: on SST-2 at batch size 4, Flex-LoRA leaks 95.18 R-1 to DAGER whereas SHE-LoRA leaks only 0.72 R-1. Compared to MaskCrypt (Hu & Li, 2024), the SOTA selective-HE method, SHE-LoRA reduces encryption time by 99.81%–99.31% and communication overhead by 98.78%–98.18% on Bert-Large, and achieves near-zero DAGER scores while MaskCrypt still scores 89.16 R-1 on SST-2. Against the homogeneous baselines FedIT and FedSA, SHE-LoRA outperforms them on model tuning quality (per Appendix F.1) while additionally providing HE-based privacy guarantees they lack. Versus HeterLoRA (Cho et al., 2024), SHE-LoRA avoids the parameter dilution caused by zero-padding aggregation and instead uses column-aware SVD-based reparameterization. The DP-protected variant Flex-LoRA-DP (σ=10⁻³) remains far less secure than SHE-LoRA (86.25 vs. 0.72 R-1 on SST-2, B=4) and degrades model accuracy, confirming that selective HE is a more effective privacy mechanism for LoRA-based federated tuning than differential privacy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: federated LoRA fine-tuning (FedIT, HeterLoRA, Flex-LoRA) and selective homomorphic encryption for privacy-preserving FL (FedML-HE, MaskCrypt). It departs from prior SHE methods by addressing the specific challenges that LoRA's matrix multiplication introduces—namely, expansion of encryption positions and ciphertext inflation under heterogeneous clients—and from prior heterogeneous LoRA methods by adding a cryptographic privacy layer. The parameter sensitivity estimation borrows directly from Wanda's pruning criterion, and the aggregation paradigm extends FedAvg's weighted averaging to handle variable-size encrypted and plaintext column blocks across clients with different LoRA ranks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SHE-LoRA provides the first practical framework for privacy-preserving federated LoRA in genuinely heterogeneous cross-device settings, where prior methods either assume homogeneous ranks or suffer from ciphertext explosion. The demonstrated 99%+ reductions in encryption time and communication overhead make HE-based protection feasible on resource-constrained edge devices, which was previously a major barrier to adoption. Open questions include: whether the semi-honest threat model extends to active adversaries (poisoning, backdoor), how the method scales to 70B+ parameter LLMs, and whether the negotiated HE subset remains stable under dynamic data drift in long-running deployments. The reliance on a trusted third party for key distribution also limits deployment in fully decentralized settings.
