## Problem

Predicting variable-sized sets of objects—molecules, detected instances, astrophysical components—requires either pre-allocating fixed capacity (padding, query slots) or explicitly inferring the set size, both of which create support gaps and architectural bottlenecks. Prior continuous-representation methods (neural fields, voxel grids) relax the fixed-size constraint but recover cardinality and object attributes only indirectly through thresholding or auxiliary classifiers, leaving no unified, invertible treatment of counts and features together.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a finite set of sampled tuples {r_i, ρ(r_i), h(r_i)}—coordinates paired with density and feature-field values—rather than raw graphs or images directly. For molecules, M locations are drawn by importance sampling proportional to the density over R³; for images and light curves, points lie on uniform 2D or 1D grids. Outputs are recovered as discrete sets only at evaluation time: molecular graphs (3D positions + atom types/charges), bounding boxes with class labels, or burst-component parameter vectors, all decoded from the learned fields via kernel-center fitting and Gram-matrix projection.

## Method

CORDS establishes a bijective mapping between a finite set S = {(r_i, x_i)} and a pair of continuous fields: a density ρ(r) = (1/α)Σ K(r; r_i) whose total mass equals |S|, and a feature field h(r) = (1/α)Σ x_i K(r; r_i) built from the same kernels. Decoding inverts this in three steps—cardinality from ∫ρ, positions by minimizing the L² gap between ρ and a sum of N kernel translates, and features by solving a small linear system with Gram matrix G—guaranteeing exact recovery under mild identifiability of equal-weight kernel mixtures. For learning, fields are discretized (importance sampling for 3D molecules, uniform grids for images/time series) and processed by domain-appropriate backbones (Erwin hierarchical transformer for unordered point sets, CNNs for regular grids), trained with diffusion (EDM) for molecular generation, pixel-wise MSE plus a differentiable count penalty for detection, or flow matching for simulation-based inference. The key design choice is that cardinality is a continuous, differentiable quantity (total density mass), enabling direct regularization and seamless generalization to out-of-distribution object counts without architectural changes.

## Results

On QM9 unconditional generation (standard RDKit evaluation), CORDS achieves 97.9% atom stability, 82.3% molecule stability, 91.0% validity, and 97.1% uniqueness, placing it in the competitive range of E(3)-equivariant GNN baselines (EDM: 82.0% Mol; GeoLDM: 89.4% Mol; Rapidash: 92.9% Mol) despite using a non-equivariant backbone. Under the OpenBabel postprocessing protocol, CORDS outperforms prior continuous/voxel methods (VoxMol: 89.3% Mol, 92.1% Unique; FuncMol: 89.2% Mol, 92.8% Unique) with 93.8% Mol and 97.1% Unique. On MultiMNIST object detection, CORDS shows the smallest out-of-distribution performance drop (16.4% AP decrease vs. 19.5% for DETR and 24.5% for YOLO), indicating more robust handling of unseen object counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On QM9 standard metrics, CORDS (82.3% Mol stability) trails the strongest equivariant baselines—Rapidash (92.9%), GeoLDM (89.4%), and EDM-Bridge (84.6%)—but matches EDM (82.0%) and substantially outperforms G-SchNet (68.1%) and ENF (4.9%). On GeomDrugs, CORDS (78.4% Atom, 94.6% Valid) is behind GeoLDM (84.4%, 99.3%) and EDM-Bridge (82.4%, 92.8%) but ahead of GDM-AUG (77.7%, 91.8%). Under the OpenBabel postprocessing protocol, CORDS clearly wins over all continuous/voxel baselines: it achieves 93.8% Mol stability vs. VoxMol (89.3%), FuncMol (89.2%), and FuncMoldec (88.6%), and 97.1% uniqueness vs. 92.1% (VoxMol) and 92.8% (FuncMol). On MultiMNIST detection, CORDS is middle-of-the-pack in-distribution (76.8 AP vs. DETR 81.2, YOLO 71.9) but exhibits the smallest OOD drop (16.4% vs. 19.5% DETR, 24.5% YOLO), suggesting its density-mass-based counting generalizes better to unseen object counts than fixed-query or grid-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

CORDS extends the line of continuous-representation methods for discrete structures (VoxMol, FuncMol, ProxelGen) by making the encoding-decoding mapping explicitly bijective: density mass gives cardinality, kernel-center fitting recovers positions, and a Gram-matrix projection recovers features, all with formal identifiability guarantees. Conceptually, the encoding mirrors kernel mean embeddings, but CORDS repurposes the kernel-superposition principle for finite-set representation with constructive inversion rather than distributional embedding. For the generative backbone, the paper adapts EDM's preconditioning to jointly denoise coordinates, density, and feature channels, and uses the Erwin hierarchical transformer for unordered point sets, while for detection and SBI it leverages standard CNNs and flow-matching posterior estimation respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a single, domain-agnostic representation that sidesteps the cardinality problem across molecules, images, and time series, with the practical advantage that OOD object counts are handled naturally through density mass rather than fixed slot budgets. Key limitations include the computational cost of dense importance sampling for large molecules, the speed-accuracy trade-off in kernel-center refinement, and kernel overlap hindering separation of nearby objects. Open questions include whether learned spatially adaptive kernels can resolve crowding, whether the approach scales to large benchmarks like COCO, and how it integrates with hybrid discrete-continuous modalities such as DFT electron densities alongside atomic graphs.
