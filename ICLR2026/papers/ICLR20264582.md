## Problem

Existing Generative Process Reward Models (GenPRMs) for RL training of Large Reasoning Models suffer from three critical pitfalls: they implicitly require the evaluator's reasoning ability to exceed the actor's, dense step-level rewards suppress exploration and dominate advantage estimation, and static reward assignments trigger reward hacking where the model optimizes for process profits rather than final correctness. The paper asks whether a GenPRM can be redesigned to improve training efficiency without these failure modes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264582/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a mathematical problem as input and the LRM produces a long-form chain-of-thought output comprising a 'think' (multi-round reasoning trajectory) and a concise 'answer.' The GenPRM segments the think into atomic logical steps, merges consecutive same-correctness steps into 'thoughts,' and assigns thought-level process rewards. These rewards are integrated into GRPO's per-token advantage computation, where the key design constraint is that tokens in correct thoughts must retain the same advantage as under outcome-only rewards (formally proven in Propositions 1–2) to prevent optimization drift.

## Method

TP-GRPO introduces two core mechanisms. First, an Intrinsic-Signal-Driven Evaluation that decouples correctness judgment from reasoning: for correct solutions, it localizes self-reflection statements, traces back to the error source, and verifies the error interval using heuristic rules; for incorrect solutions, it performs coarse semantic matching between think steps and the erroneous answer. Second, a Thought-Level Difficulty-Aware Adaptive Reward that merges consecutive same-correctness steps into 'thoughts' to reduce reward density, then scales the reward magnitude by group accuracy (r^c = α·acc_G) so that hard problems receive weaker process signals (preserving exploration) while easy problems receive stronger reinforcement. For incorrect solutions, only thoughts semantically matched to the wrong answer are penalized, while unmatched exploratory thoughts receive non-negative rewards to avoid over-penalization. The entire pipeline is integrated into process-supervised GRPO with an off-policy training loop, and theoretical propositions guarantee that the optimization target of key tokens is preserved.

## Results

On the 1.5B model, TP-GRPO achieves an average improvement of +2.04 over the base model (50.10 vs. 48.06), with the largest gains on AIME 24 (+4.32) and AIME 25 (+3.13), while using only 5.6K training solutions compared to 34K for the on-policy GRPO baseline. On the 7B model, the average improvement rises to +3.43 (67.23 vs. 63.80), including +6.67 on AIME 24 and +6.66 on AIME 25, with 8.56K solutions versus 16K for GRPO. The proposed efficiency metric (Effic.) reaches 36.43 (1.5B) and 40.07 (7B), roughly 8× and 4× higher than the outcome-only GRPO baseline (4.65 and 9.6 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TP-GRPO outperforms the on-policy outcome-only GRPO baseline on all five benchmarks for both model sizes, achieving +0.46 avg (1.5B) and +1.89 avg (7B) with roughly 6× and 2× fewer training solutions respectively. Against the two GenPRM-based baselines from Zhao et al. (2025) — LLM-as-a-judge with Qwen3-32B and the pretrained GenPRM-32B — TP-GRPO achieves substantially higher average scores (50.10 vs. 48.85 and 48.86 on 1.5B), indicating that naive GenPRM reward assignment fails to effectively leverage process signals. Compared to Open-RS1 (Hugging Face, 57.6K solutions, Avg 48.39), TP-GRPO surpasses it by +1.71 avg while using 10× fewer solutions. Against DeepScaler-1.5B-Preview (Luo et al., >3584K solutions, Avg 56.78) and Skywork-OR1-7B (>5406.7K solutions, Avg 72.62), TP-GRPO achieves lower absolute accuracy but does so with 600×+ fewer training samples, yielding an Effic. score of 36.43–40.07 versus <0.49 and <0.26. On the 7B model, TP-GRPO also outperforms ReasonFlux-PRM (Zou et al., Avg 63.11, 60K solutions) by +4.12 avg with 7× fewer solutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on process-supervision GRPO from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) and the generative PRM paradigm from GenPRM (Zhao et al., 2025), but departs from both in a targeted way. Rather than treating GenPRM as a black-box step evaluator that re-derives reasoning, the authors decompose evaluation into semantic-matching sub-tasks (reflection localization, error-source tracking, interval verification) that rely on fundamental LLM capabilities rather than strong reasoning. The reward design further departs from the static +1/−1 step rewards common in prior PRM work by introducing thought-level granularity and difficulty-adaptive scaling, with formal propositions showing that correct thoughts retain exactly the same advantage as outcome-only rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that well-designed process rewards can cut training sample requirements by roughly 5× (1.5B) to 2× (7B) while improving accuracy, which is meaningful for teams with limited compute. The robustness finding—that even a 4B evaluator with 55.9% GPQA accuracy works nearly as well as a 32B one under TP-GRPO, while LLM-as-a-Judge degrades sharply—challenges the assumption that GenPRM quality is bottlenecked by the evaluator's reasoning strength. Key limitations include evaluation only on 1.5B/7B models, an off-policy training pipeline (acknowledged as inferior to on-policy), and a single training dataset (DeepScaler-40K); whether the efficiency gains and reward-hacking mitigation hold at larger scales or in on-policy settings remains open.
