## Problem

Current multimodal LLMs reason exclusively in text space, treating visual inputs as static preconditions even when visual and textual tokens already share a joint semantic embedding space. Existing remedies—text-space chain-of-thought ('Thinking about Images') and external image-editing tools ('Thinking with Images')—still leave a fundamental gap between visual perception and text generation, as the LLM never directly manipulates visual representations during reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265024/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (encoded by a frozen ViT into a grid of visual patches, with resolution between 128×28×28 and 5120×28×28 pixels) and a text question. During inference, upon generating a special <|lvr_start|> token, the model enters latent reasoning mode where it autoregressively produces a fixed number of hidden-state vectors (4, 8, or 16 steps) that reconstruct question-relevant visual tokens, then emits <|lvr_end|> and resumes standard text generation to produce the final answer. The visual encoder and projector remain frozen throughout; only LLM parameters are updated.

## Method

LVR modifies the standard Vision-Projector-LLM pipeline so the LLM can interleave latent visual reasoning with text generation: in LVR mode, the model's last hidden states are propagated directly as input embeddings (bypassing the language modeling head) to approximate ground-truth ROI visual tokens in the shared semantic space. Training is two-stage: (1) SFT jointly optimizes an MSE reconstruction loss (hidden states → annotated ROI visual embeddings from Visual CoT bounding boxes) and a cross-entropy next-token prediction loss; (2) GRPO_latent, an adapted GRPO algorithm that replays recorded latent hidden states during the teacher-forcing log-probability pass to compute token-wise importance ratios, with rewards for format compliance (presence of LVR trigger tokens) and answer accuracy. The key architectural insight is that no extra projection head is needed—the LLM's native hidden states already align with the visual embedding space, as confirmed by ablations showing MLP and GLU heads degrade performance.

## Results

On the 7B model, LVR (8 steps) achieves 81.7% on V* Bench, 84.4% on V*_D.A. (detail search), 77.6% on V*_R.P. (relative position), and 71.7% on MMVP, outperforming the Qwen2.5-VL base model by 3.2%, 2.7%, 3.9%, and 5.0% respectively. The largest relative gains appear on MMVP (72.0% at 4 steps vs. 66.7% base) and V*_R.P. (79.0% at 16 steps vs. 73.7% base), confirming that latent visual reconstruction is most beneficial for perception-intensive and spatial-reasoning tasks. LVR also leads on Counting (70.0%), IQ-Test (29.3%), and JigSaw (52.0%), while trailing slightly on Spatial Relation (86.0% vs. 87.4% base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen2.5-VL base model, LVR (8 steps) improves V* by 3.2 points, MMVP by 5.0 points, and V*_R.P. by 3.9 points, while matching JigSaw and trailing by 1.4 points on Spatial Relation. Compared to PixelReasoner (the strongest 'Think with Images' baseline that uses external cropping tools), LVR wins on V* (+1.6), V*_D.A. (+2.7), MMVP (+4.7), Counting (+3.3), and IQ-Test (+4.0), ties on V*_R.P. and Relative Reflect, but loses narrowly on JigSaw (−0.7) and Spatial Relation (−2.1). Against the 'Think about Images' baselines, LVR dominates: it outperforms Vision-R1 by 11.5 points on V* and 25.0 points on MMVP, and surpasses PAPO by 45.6 points on V* and 17.4 points on MMVP, indicating that text-space CoT reasoning actively degrades fine-grained perception. The SFT baseline (trained on the same Visual CoT data without latent reasoning) confirms that the gains come from the LVR mechanism itself rather than data, with LVR leading by 6.0 points on MMVP and 8.9 points on Relative Reflect. The sole consistent weakness is Spatial Relation, where LVR trails all baselines by 1–3 points, likely due to the single-image training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established multimodal reasoning paradigms—'Thinking about Images' (text-space CoT, e.g., Vision-R1, PAPO) and 'Thinking with Images' (external visual tools like cropping or OCR, e.g., PixelReasoner, Argus-X3)—by proposing a third mode: autoregressive reasoning directly in the visual embedding space. Conceptually, it extends Hao et al.'s (2024) latent reasoning idea from NLP (passing last hidden states instead of discrete tokens) to the visual domain, where the latent states are supervised to reconstruct question-relevant visual tokens from a ViT encoder. The training pipeline combines standard SFT with an MSE reconstruction loss and adapts GRPO to handle the absence of a token distribution during latent reasoning steps, a non-trivial extension of the RL framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LVR addresses a recognized limitation in MLLMs where visual details are underutilized during text-centric generation, and it does so without external tools or auxiliary images, making it architecturally simpler and more scalable than 'Thinking with Images' approaches. However, the current implementation is constrained to a fixed number of latent reasoning steps (variable-length decoding via Latent End Token or Mode Switching Loss proved unstable), and RL was only applied to the 3B model due to compute limits. Open questions include whether truly free-form latent visual reasoning can be stabilized, whether the approach extends to multi-image or video settings, and whether the gains persist at larger scales or on tasks requiring compositional visual reasoning beyond single-ROI reconstruction.
