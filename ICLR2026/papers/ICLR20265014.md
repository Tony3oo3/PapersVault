## Problem

Existing weight-space model merging methods assume architecturally homogeneous models (matching widths, depths, and layer shapes), making heterogeneous merges between models of different sizes or families brittle or infeasible. Additionally, most approaches require at least two distinct pretrained models, precluding single-model augmentation. This paper asks whether encoding weights into a learned latent space can decouple merging from architectural constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pretrained LLM weight tensors, which are flattened per-layer, zero-padded to a fixed length, and partitioned into non-overlapping chunks of size 640 to form a sequence input for the encoder. For heterogeneous merging, the output is always decoded through the target architecture's decoder, so the merged weights conform to the target's shape regardless of the source's dimensions. The latent code is a fixed-dimension vector (640-d per chunk) that supports arbitrary convex combinations before decoding.

## Method

LS-Merge encodes LLM weights with a 6-layer transformer-based β-VAE trained via a two-stage curriculum (deterministic autoencoder first, then KL regularization enabled) to handle the heavy-tailed, low-variance weight distributions that cause early training collapse. For heterogeneous pairs, a proportional dimensionality-matching projection rescales per-layer latents to a common size, and an Optimal Transport map (Monge solution under 2-Wasserstein distance, computed via the closed-form affine transformation between Gaussian approximations) aligns the source latent distribution to the target's, ensuring interpolated codes remain within the target decoder's valid manifold. Merging is then a simple linear interpolation (or barycentric combination for N experts) in the aligned latent space, followed by decoding. The paper demonstrates that this non-linear latent geometry is essential: a linear PCA baseline collapses to near-random accuracy at all compression ratios, while the VAE preserves functional performance even at 4× compression.

## Results

On the primary expert-merging benchmark (10 LoRA experts on Gemma-7B-it), LS-Merge(soup) achieves the best overall scores: 56.0 on MMLU, 60.1 on HellaSwag, 56.1 on NLGraph, 35.2 on K-Crossword, and 4.0 on AbstainQA, outperforming the best single expert (45.7 MMLU) and the base model (48.8 MMLU) by 10.3 and 7.2 points respectively. LS-Merge(lerp) leads on MMLU-Pro (21.6), GSM8K (28.1), and TruthfulQA (33.0). In the cross-architecture comparison on Llama-2-13B, LS-Merge reaches 55.07 MMLU and 36.41 IFEval, surpassing Task Arithmetic (52.18 / 25.10) and matching or exceeding AIM (54.18 / 32.00) on most tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LS-Merge consistently outperforms all weight-space baselines across the 8-benchmark expert-merging suite. Against Uniform Soup, LS-Merge(soup) gains +6.3 on MMLU (56.0 vs 49.7), +6.1 on HellaSwag (60.1 vs 54.0), and +16.3 on GSM8K (24.2 vs 7.9); against Greedy Soup it leads by +5.2 on MMLU and +5.5 on HellaSwag. SLERP is the closest weight-space competitor on MMLU (52.5) but trails LS-Merge by 3.5 points and by 9.7 on HellaSwag. DARE-Ties, which combines sparsification with sign alignment, is outperformed on every benchmark, with the largest gap on GSM8K (24.2 vs 7.3). In the Llama-2-13B comparison (Table 4), LS-Merge substantially outperforms Task Arithmetic (55.07 vs 52.18 MMLU; 36.41 vs 25.10 IFEval) and edges out AIM on MMLU (+0.89) and IFEval (+4.41), while AIM retains a small advantage on HumanEval (29.27 vs 28.14) and GSM8K (46.20 vs 44.12).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weight-space learning literature (Schürholt et al., Peebles et al.) by applying a transformer-based VAE to encode LLM weights into a compact latent space, then performing model merging (Model Soups, Task Arithmetic) in that space rather than in raw parameter space. It departs from all prior merging methods—Model Soup, SLERP, DARE-TIES, Task Arithmetic, AIM—which operate directly on weights and require architectural homogeneity—by introducing a dimensionality-matching projection and Optimal Transport (Monge map) alignment that register heterogeneous latent distributions before interpolation. The two-stage compression curriculum (deterministic AE → VAE) is motivated by the paper's empirical finding that LLM weights exhibit heavy-tailed, high-kurtosis distributions that destabilize standard VAE training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LS-Merge is the first method to enable stable merging across architecturally heterogeneous LLMs (different depths, widths, or even model families like Gemma ↔ LLaMA), a capability that all prior weight-space methods fundamentally lack. The ablation showing that linear PCA reconstruction collapses to near-random accuracy even at mild compression (r=1.6) while the non-linear VAE preserves functional performance is a clear demonstration that pretrained weights inhabit a non-linear manifold, with implications for any weight-space compression or interpolation task. Key limitations include the need to train a VAE per architecture (or model family), the degradation at higher compression ratios due to posterior collapse on heavy-tailed weights, and moderate (rather than transformative) downstream gains. Open questions include scaling to 70B+ models, more efficient property-aware encoders, and iterative latent barycentric interpolation for complex multi-model compositions.
