## Problem

Distributed reinforcement learning with policy gradient methods under asynchronous parallel computation remains poorly understood theoretically, especially when agents have heterogeneous computation speeds and communication is a bottleneck. The prior state-of-the-art asynchronous method (AFedPG) does not support heterogeneous environments, has suboptimal communication complexity (O(κ ε^{-3}) vs. the achievable O(κ ε^{-2})), does not support AllReduce, and its computational complexity grows with the number of agents n. This paper closes these gaps with new algorithms and matching lower bounds.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a discrete-time discounted MDP with continuous state-action spaces, where n agents asynchronously sample finite trajectories of length H from (potentially different) environment copies and compute stochastic policy gradients g_H. The key operational parameters are per-agent computation times h_i = ḣ_i × H and a single communication round time κ; the algorithm requires only that M total gradients be collected (not that all n agents finish), making it robust to stragglers. The output is an ε-stationary policy parameter θ with E‖∇J(θ)‖ ≤ ε, accompanied by closed-form time complexity bounds in both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings.

## Method

Rennala NIGT and Malenia NIGT combine the NIGT normalized-gradient-with-momentum update (extrapolation step θ̃_t = θ_t + (1−η)/η(θ_t − θ_{t−1}), then θ_{t+1} = θ_t + α d_t/‖d_t‖) with a new asynchronous aggregation procedure. In each iteration, agents compute stochastic gradients in parallel; the server waits until M gradients have arrived from any subset of agents (not all n), locally accumulates them into a single vector, and performs exactly one AllReduce communication before the normalized momentum update. The homogeneous version (Rennala) uses a simple count-based stopping rule, while the heterogeneous version (Malenia) uses a harmonic-mean criterion (1/n Σ 1/M_i)^{-1} ≥ M/n to ensure the aggregated gradient remains unbiased when agents sample from different distributions. This design yields a harmonic-mean-like computational complexity min_m[(1/m Σ 1/h_i)^{-1}(1/ε² + 1/m ε^{7/2})] in the homogeneous case and a mean-like complexity in the heterogeneous case, both strictly improving on prior bounds.

## Results

In the homogeneous setup, Rennala NIGT achieves a total time complexity of Õ(κ(L_gΔ/ε² + √(L_h)Δ/ε^(3/2)) + (1/(1−γ)) min_m[(1/m Σ 1/h_i)^(−1)(L_gΔ/ε² + √(L_h)Δ/ε^(3/2) + σ²/(mε²) + σ²√(L_h)Δ/(mε^(7/2)))], improving AFedPG's communication complexity from O(κ ε^(−3)) to O(κ ε^(−2)) and eliminating the n^(4/3) growth in computational cost. In the heterogeneous setup, Malenia NIGT achieves Õ(κ(L_gΔ/ε² + √(L_h)Δ/ε^(3/2)) + (1/(1−γ))[h̃_n(L_gΔ/ε² + √(L_h)Δ/ε^(3/2)) + (1/n Σ h̃_i)(σ²/(nε²) + σ²√(L_h)Δ/(nε^(7/2)))], the first state-of-the-art result for asynchronous heterogeneous RL. On Humanoid-v4 (MuJoCo), Rennala NIGT matches other methods under equal computation times, converges faster under heterogeneous times, and is the only method that remains robust when communication times are large.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rennala NIGT strictly dominates AFedPG (Lan et al., 2025) on all three axes: communication complexity improves from O(κ ε^(−3)) to O(κ ε^(−2)) (and from O(κ ε^(−7/2)) in the worst single-agent case to O(κ ε^(−2))), computational complexity eliminates the n^(4/3) factor that causes AFedPG's cost to grow with the number of agents, and Rennala NIGT supports AllReduce while AFedPG does not. Compared to the synchronous NIGT (Fatkhullin et al., 2023), Rennala NIGT replaces the max_i h̃_i bottleneck with a harmonic-mean-like min_m expression, making it robust to stragglers (if h_n → ∞, the slowest agent is simply ignored). Against Rennala PG/SGD (Tyurin & Richtárik, 2023), the improvement comes from the NIGT normalization and momentum, reducing the ε-dependence in the variance term from 1/(mε⁴) to 1/(mε^(7/2)). In the heterogeneous setup, Malenia NIGT is the first method to handle asynchronous heterogeneous RL with a provable guarantee, whereas AFedPG does not support this setting at all. Empirically on Humanoid-v4, all methods perform similarly under equal computation times, but Rennala NIGT converges faster under heterogeneous times and is the only method that remains stable when communication delays are large.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate research threads: the NIGT (normalized gradient with momentum) algorithm from Cutkosky & Mehta (2020) and Fatkhullin et al. (2023), and the Rennala/Malenia asynchronous aggregation framework from Tyurin & Richtárik (2023). The most direct prior work is AFedPG (Lan et al., 2025), which applies NIGT to asynchronous federated RL but only in the homogeneous setting and with a greedy per-gradient communication strategy. The key departures are: (1) extending to heterogeneous environments via the Malenia aggregation scheme, (2) replacing per-gradient communication with a single AllReduce per iteration, and (3) using the min-over-m harmonic-mean formulation that makes complexity robust to stragglers and scale-invariant in n.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first state-of-the-art time complexities for asynchronous distributed RL in both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings, with provable improvements over AFedPG in all dimensions (computational, communication, and AllReduce support). A notable open question is the gap between the upper bound O(κ ε^{-2}) and the lower bound O(κ ε^{-12/7}) for communication complexity, which the authors note remains unresolved even in the non-distributed deterministic case. Empirical validation is limited to a single MuJoCo task (Humanoid-v4), so broader empirical confirmation across diverse environments and scales would strengthen the practical claims. The results are most relevant to practitioners training large-scale RL systems where communication bottlenecks and heterogeneous hardware are common.
