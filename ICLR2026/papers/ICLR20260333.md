## Problem

Model-based value expansion in offline RL faces a fundamental trade-off: longer model rollouts reduce bias in value bootstrapping but amplify compounding errors from autoregressive dynamics model queries. This trade-off has prevented model-based RL from scaling to complex, long-horizon tasks, where prior approaches (single-step models, planning-based methods) fail to achieve non-trivial performance on 700–3000 step robotic manipulation tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors from the environment and produces action chunks—sequences of n=10 actions generated simultaneously by a flow-based policy. The dynamics model takes a state and an action chunk as joint input and outputs a single predicted state n steps ahead, collapsing n recursive single-step calls into one. During training, M imaginary rollouts of H=10 chunks (100 environment steps total) are synthesized from dataset states to train the value function, while at test time N=32 action chunks are sampled from the flow policy and the argmax under the learned Q-function is selected.

## Method

MAC resolves the rollout-length dilemma by training a deterministic action-chunk dynamics model p_ψ(s_{t+n} | s_t, a_{t:t+n-1}) that predicts a future state from an entire action sequence in a single MLP call, reducing recursive model queries by a factor of n and thereby mitigating compounding errors. For the policy, MAC trains a state-conditioned flow-matching velocity field to model the multi-modal behavioral action-chunk distribution, then extracts actions via rejection sampling: N chunks are generated from the flow and the one maximizing a learned Q-function is selected, which inherently prevents out-of-distribution model exploitation without explicit uncertainty penalties. A one-step distilled MLP policy π_ω is trained to directly predict the flow's ODE output, reducing rejection sampling cost from NF to N network queries per action chunk. Value learning proceeds on-policy: imaginary rollouts of length H chunks are generated by alternating the rejection-sampling policy with the dynamics and reward models, and the value function is updated with nH-step bootstrapped targets using exponentially averaged target networks.

## Results

On six large-scale, long-horizon OGBench tasks (up to 100M transitions), MAC achieves the best performance among all model-based RL algorithms, reaching 100±1 on cube-double, 30±6 on cube-octuple, 100±0 on puzzle-3x3, and 99±3 on puzzle-4x5, where all prior model-based methods score 0. MAC also outperforms the strongest model-free baseline (SHARSA) on 4 of 6 tasks, including the hardest manipulation tasks (cube-octuple: 30 vs. 19; puzzle-4x5: 99 vs. 91). On 25 standard reward-based OGBench tasks, MAC achieves the best average success rate on 4 of 5 environments (e.g., 99±2 on cube-single vs. 96±3 for FQL; 97±4 on scene vs. 56±45 for FQL). The one notable failure is on contact-rich locomotion (humanoidmaze-giant), where all model-based methods including MAC score 0, while model-free n-SAC+BC reaches 82±5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAC decisively outperforms all four model-based baselines (MOPO, MOBILE, LEQ, F-MPC) on every long-horizon task: on cube-octuple and puzzle-4x5, all prior model-based methods score 0 while MAC reaches 30±6 and 99±3 respectively. Against sequence-modeling baselines (Diffuser, HD-DA), MAC is dramatically superior on all long-horizon tasks where those methods also score 0. Among model-free baselines, MAC beats SHARSA (the SOTA long-horizon model-free method) on cube-double (100 vs. 95), cube-octuple (30 vs. 19), and puzzle-4x5 (99 vs. 91), but loses on humanoidmaze-medium (36 vs. 95) and humanoidmaze-giant (0 vs. 43), confirming that model-based RL still struggles with contact-rich locomotion. On standard reward-based benchmarks, MAC outperforms FQL (SOTA model-free) on cube-single (99 vs. 96), cube-double (53 vs. 29), scene (97 vs. 56), and puzzle-4x4 (78 vs. 29), but underperforms FQL on puzzle-3x3 (20 vs. 30). On D4RL MuJoCo, MAC is competitive but not dominant: MOBILE (960 total) and LEQ (923) outperform MAC (771), with MAC trailing most notably on halfcheetah tasks (e.g., 68 vs. 105 on halfcheetah-me for MOBILE), suggesting the method's strengths are in manipulation rather than continuous locomotion.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAC extends model-based value expansion (Feinberg et al., 2018) by replacing the standard single-step dynamics model with an action-chunk model that predicts n-step-ahead states from a sequence of actions, directly addressing the compounding-error problem that limits prior model-based offline RL methods (MOPO, MOBILE, LEQ). For the policy, it departs from the Gaussian policies and uncertainty-penalization mechanisms used in prior work by employing a flow-matching behavioral policy with rejection sampling, drawing on the flow distillation technique from FQL (Park et al., 2025c) to make rejection sampling tractable during training. Unlike prior action-chunk dynamics works (Asadi et al., 2019; Lambert et al., 2021; Zhou et al., 2025) that use multi-step models only for planning or model full state-action trajectories, MAC integrates the action-chunk model into a full on-policy actor-critic loop without additional planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAC demonstrates that offline model-based RL can scale to 100M-transition datasets and 700–3000-step manipulation tasks where all prior model-based methods fail completely, achieving 99% on puzzle-4x5 and 30% on cube-octuple. The practical finding that a single set of hyperparameters (n=10, H=10) works across all tasks—while baselines require task-specific rollout horizons and uncertainty penalties—makes the method significantly easier to deploy. However, the complete failure on contact-rich locomotion tasks (humanoidmaze-giant) highlights that sequential dynamics modeling remains insufficient for discontinuous physics, and the authors note that very large chunk sizes or low-quality datasets may limit performance, pointing to future work on more expressive generative models and latent dynamics.
