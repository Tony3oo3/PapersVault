## Problem

Existing theoretical analyses of regularization-based continual learning study specific regularizers with fixed memory costs, leaving the fundamental question of how memory allocation in the regularization matrix governs statistical performance unaddressed. This paper asks: what is the provable trade-off between the number of vectors needed to define a structural regularizer and the resulting excess risk, and how much memory is required to fully eliminate catastrophic forgetting?

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes two sequential batches of n linear regression samples each (drawn from distributions D^(1) and D^(2) under covariate shift) plus a user-specified PSD regularization matrix Σ = W^T W with W ∈ R^{k×d}, where k is the memory budget. The output is a single model parameter w^(2) evaluated by the joint excess risk Δ(w^(2)) = R_1(w^(2)) + R_2(w^(2)) − min R, which the paper decomposes into explicit bias and variance terms that are functions of the covariance spectra (μ_i, λ_i), noise level σ², sample size n, and the regularization matrix Σ.

## Method

The paper analyzes the Generalized ℓ2-Regularized Continual Learning (GRCL) algorithm, which in its second phase solves a ridge regression with a quadratic penalty ‖w − w^(1)‖²_Σ rather than an isotropic penalty. The key technical contribution is Theorem 2, which gives matching upper and lower bounds on the joint excess risk of GRCL in the one-hot random design as an explicit function of Σ, the data covariance matrices G and H, and the sample size n. By specializing Σ to the top-k eigenvectors of G with eigenvalues γ_i = μ_i, the paper shows GRCL achieves joint-learning performance when k captures all eigenvalues above 1/n, while with only k < k+1 vectors the excess risk remains Ω(1) regardless of the regularization choice. For power-law spectra μ_i = i^{−α}, the excess risk scales as joint risk × (1 + n/k^α), yielding a clean memory-statistics trade-off where k = n^{1/α} suffices to match joint learning. The results extend to Gaussian design (where tail-feature differences also cause forgetting) and to the NTK regime of neural networks via a reduction to the linear model.

## Results

The paper establishes sharp upper and lower bounds on the joint excess risk of GRCL in the one-hot random design setting (Theorem 2), decomposing it into bias and variance terms that are explicit functions of the regularization matrix Σ. The key quantitative finding is a provable memory-statistics trade-off: with top-k regularization (γ_i = μ_i for i ≤ k), the GRCL excess risk scales as EΔ(w_joint)·(1 + n/k^α) for power-law eigenvalue spectra (Example 8), approaching joint learning performance at k = n^{1/α}. Numerical experiments (d=200, n=5000) confirm that OCL suffers a constant excess risk due to catastrophic forgetting, while GRCL matches joint learning convergence at k=5 (Fig. 1a) and reaches joint learning performance by k≤15 (Fig. 1b).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares GRCL against three baselines: Ordinary Continual Learning (OCL, Evron et al. 2022), ℓ₂-regularized CL (ℓ₂-RCL), and Joint Learning (the oracle upper bound). OCL provably suffers catastrophic forgetting (constant excess risk Ω(1)) whenever there is an eigenvalue mismatch between the two task covariances on dominant features (e.g., μ₁=1, λ₁=1/n), and no amount of sample size can fix this. ℓ₂-RCL, which adds only a scalar γ of memory, also fails for certain task configurations (Example 5: alternating dominant/intermediate eigenvalues), confirming that isotropic regularization is insufficient. GRCL with a full-rank diagonal Σ (memory size |J|) provably matches joint learning performance (Corollary 6: EΔ(w^(2)) ≲ EΔ(w_joint)), eliminating catastrophic forgetting entirely. With constrained memory k < |J|, GRCL exhibits a smooth degradation: the excess risk ratio to joint learning grows as n/k^α, providing a quantitative characterization of how much forgetting is incurred per unit of memory saved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of theoretical CL analysis by Evron et al. (2022), Li et al. (2023), and Zhao et al. (2024), all of which studied specific regularizers with fixed memory costs in fixed-design settings. The key departure is moving to random design (one-hot and Gaussian) and, crucially, treating the regularization matrix Σ as a tunable parameter whose rank directly controls memory usage, thereby establishing the first explicit theoretical link between memory cost and statistical performance in structural-regularization-based CL. The GRCL framework subsumes EWC (diagonal Σ), ℓ₂-RCL (isotropic Σ), and OCL (zero Σ) as special cases, unifying them under a single analytical lens. The NTK extension leverages the linearization result of Lee et al. (2019) to transfer the linear-regression bounds to wide neural networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first provable characterization of the memory-statistics trade-off in CL, giving practitioners a theoretical basis for how much regularization memory (rank of Σ) is needed to avoid catastrophic forgetting on a given task pair. The finding that isotropic ℓ₂-regularization (ℓ₂-RCL) fundamentally cannot prevent forgetting for certain covariate-shift configurations, while a rank-|J| diagonal regularizer can match joint learning, sharpens the practical guidance for choosing between EWC-style diagonal approximations and full Hessian methods. Limitations include the two-task focus, the commutable-covariance assumption, and the gap between the one-hot main results and the Gaussian setting (where only OCL bounds are fully derived). Open questions include extending the GRCL bounds to Gaussian design, handling non-commutable covariances, and quantifying the trade-off in the multi-task regime beyond the sketch in Corollary 11.
