## Problem

Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) guides models toward flat minima to improve generalization, but it only enforces flatness on a single training subset without explicitly ensuring that the model remains stable under different resamplings of the same data distribution. The paper identifies that generalization also requires gradient alignment across independently drawn subsets, a property that SAM does not directly optimize.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images at various resolutions (224×224 for ImageNet/Food101, 32×32 for CIFAR) and outputs class labels. The key operational detail is that each training iteration consumes two independently sampled mini-batches from the training set: a primary batch B^t (e.g., 2048 samples) and a smaller auxiliary batch B^a (e.g., 512 samples), whose gradients jointly shape the parameter update. The auxiliary batch size is deliberately kept small to limit computational overhead relative to standard SAM.

## Method

Align-SAM extends SAM's perturbation mechanism by incorporating a second mini-batch's gradient into the perturbation direction. At each iteration, it computes a perturbed parameter θ̃^t = θ + ρ(λ·g_t/‖g_t‖ − g_a/‖g_a‖), where g_t and g_a are normalized gradients from the primary and auxiliary batches respectively, and λ > 1 (typically 2) prioritizes the training gradient. A first-order Taylor expansion reveals that the subsequent SGD step simultaneously minimizes the training loss, minimizes the gradient norm (flatness, as in SAM), and maximizes the dot product between the two subsets' gradients (cross-subset alignment). Theoretically, a PAC-Bayes bound (Theorem 1) shows that sharpness on an auxiliary subset upper-bounds the true generalization loss, and Theorem 2 proves that the update rule provably increases gradient congruence over training. For efficiency, the gradient at the perturbed auxiliary point is approximated by the gradient at the current model, and the auxiliary batch is kept much smaller than the primary batch, making Align-SAM only marginally slower than standard SAM.

## Results

On ImageNet (trained from scratch, 200 epochs), Align-SAM improves Top-1 accuracy by +1.18% over SAM with ResNet18 (63.64% vs. 62.46%) and by +2.16% with ResNet34 (65.89% vs. 63.73%). On CIFAR-100 with WideResNet28x10, Align-SAM reaches 83.72% Top-1, outperforming SAM (83.00%), VASSO (83.11%), GSAM (83.13%), and ASAM (83.16%). Under 80% label noise on CIFAR-100, Align-SAM achieves 27.66% vs. SAM's 24.26%, and Align-ASAM reaches 32.77% vs. ASAM's 30.33%. In meta-learning on Mini-ImageNet, Align-SAM_low attains 50.08% 5-way 1-shot accuracy, surpassing both MAML (47.13%) and Sharp-MAML_low (49.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Align-SAM consistently outperforms SAM across all evaluated settings: +1.18–2.16% Top-1 on ImageNet, +0.30–0.60% on Food101, and +0.38–0.72% on CIFAR-100. Against other SAM variants on CIFAR-100 (WideResNet28x10), Align-SAM (83.72%) beats VASSO (83.11%), GSAM (83.13%), LookSAM (82.89%), and ASAM (83.16%), with the largest margin over LookSAM (+0.83%). Under noisy labels (80% corruption, CIFAR-100), Align-SAM gains +3.40% over SAM and +1.75% over VASSO(θ=0.9), while Align-ASAM leads ASAM by +2.44%. In transfer learning on ImageNet (ResNet50 fine-tuning), Align-SAM improves Top-1 by +0.74% over SAM (75.91% vs. 75.17%). In meta-learning on Mini-ImageNet, Align-SAM_low surpasses Sharp-MAML_low by +0.36% (5-way 1-shot) and +1.11% (5-way 5-shot), and outperforms vanilla MAML by +2.95% (1-shot). The method does not show a clear advantage on CIFAR-10 where performance is near saturation (96.91% vs. SAM's 96.87%), though it still edges out most baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) by Foret et al. (2021) through a cross-subset alignment lens: rather than minimizing worst-case loss on a single mini-batch, Align-SAM simultaneously optimizes sharpness on a primary batch and loss on an independently drawn auxiliary batch, enforcing gradient congruence between the two. Theoretically, it generalizes the PAC-Bayes bound underlying SAM from 0-1 loss to arbitrary bounded loss (Theorem 1) and proves that the update rule provably increases gradient alignment (Theorem 2). The method is conceptually inspired by MAML's bi-level structure but explicitly departs from it by using two subsets from the same distribution rather than distinct training/validation sets, and by operating within a single optimization step rather than a meta-update/fine-tune loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Align-SAM offers a principled way to improve generalization beyond flatness alone by explicitly enforcing stability across resamplings of the data distribution, with consistent (if modest) gains across from-scratch training, transfer learning, noisy labels, meta-learning, and domain generalization. The main practical limitation is the additional forward/backward pass for the auxiliary batch, which the authors mitigate by using a smaller auxiliary batch size and gradient reuse, but the overhead remains non-trivial for large-scale training. An open question is whether the gradient-alignment objective can be approximated more cheaply (e.g., via gradient caching or variance-reduction techniques) without sacrificing the generalization benefit, and whether the theoretical PAC-Bayes extension tightens meaningfully in high-dimensional regimes typical of modern networks.
