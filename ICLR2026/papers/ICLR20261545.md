## Problem

Existing 3D avatar reconstruction methods face a trilemma: optimization-based approaches (GaussianAvatars, MonoGaussianAvatar) require minutes of per-scene training and fail on sparse inputs, while feedforward methods (LAM, Avat3r) are locked to fixed input lengths (1 or 4 frames), wasting available observations. No prior system can accept an arbitrary number of unordered frames in a single unified model and progressively improve quality as more data arrives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261545/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an unordered set of 1–16 RGB frames at 504×504, each accompanied by its camera pose, FLAME expression coefficients, and head pose parameters obtained from a coarse multi-view FLAME tracker. It outputs a single animatable 3DGS avatar whose per-Gaussian attributes (color, opacity, per-axis scale, quaternion rotation, importance score, and 3D offset) are anchored to FLAME mesh vertices and driven at render time by arbitrary expression/pose via Linear Blend Skinning. For long sequences, a FramePack-style strided-convolution compression folds all frames beyond the 16 base frames into a single aggregated token, enabling hundreds of frames in one forward pass.

## Method

FastAvatar's core is a Large Gaussian Reconstruction Transformer (LGRT), a VGGT variant that replaces the DPT head with a two-layer MLP (GS Head) directly predicting canonical 3DGS attributes, using FLAME mesh vertices as 3D positional prompts. Each frame is encoded by DINOv2 into patch tokens, then augmented with multi-granular positional encodings (camera pose, expression, head pose) through lightweight MLPs before entering an alternating stack of 10 Global Attention layers (cross-frame 3D registration) and 10 Frame Attention layers (intra-frame aggregation with 3D prompt fusion, initialized from LAM weights). Two novel losses enable variable-length and incremental operation: a Sliced Fusion Loss that randomly fuses a subset of per-frame Gaussians during training so the model generalizes to any frame count, and a Landmark Tracking Loss that supervises 68 facial landmark projections to enforce precise cross-frame Gaussian alignment. A Gumbel-Softmax mask with L1 sparsity regularization prunes over 50% of Gaussian primitives without quality loss, and a sliding-window streaming variant (16-frame windows with 4-frame overlap) extends the framework to continuous video input.

## Results

On NeRSemble, FastAvatar achieves PSNR 20.08 / LPIPS 0.143 / Identity 0.116 with a single input view, improving to PSNR 22.29 / LPIPS 0.092 / Identity 0.095 at 16 views, while completing reconstruction in 1.33 s (1 view) to 26.06 s (16 views) on an RTX 4090. Rendering speed ranges from 240 FPS (1 view) to 17.65 FPS (16 views). In contrast, optimization-based baselines (MonoGaussianAvatar, GaussianAvatars) require >100 s of per-scene training and degrade sharply at 1 view (PSNR 11.83 and 16.35 respectively), while the feedforward LAM baseline actually degrades as more views are added (PSNR drops from 17.30 to 16.49).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastAvatar outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against LAM (SIGGRAPH 2025), FastAvatar gains +2.78 dB PSNR at 1 view and the gap widens to +5.80 dB at 16 views, because LAM's generative bias causes its quality to *degrade* with additional inputs (PSNR 17.30→16.49) while FastAvatar monotonically improves (20.08→22.29). Against the optimization-based MonoGaussianAvatar and GaussianAvatars, FastAvatar achieves substantially higher PSNR (e.g., 22.29 vs. 15.81 and 21.48 at 16 views) and lower LPIPS (0.092 vs. 0.406 and 0.281), while completing reconstruction in 26 s versus >100 s of per-scene training. On the Ava256 multi-view benchmark, FastAvatar produces more identity-faithful reconstructions than Avat3r, which consistently reconstructs wider facial structures than observed in the source. On the INSTA single-image benchmark, FastAvatar achieves LPIPS 0.1332 and Identity 0.076, beating LAM (0.1479 / 0.124), Arc2Avatar (0.4585 / 0.411), and SynShot (0.1523 / 0.115). On NeRSemble single-image, FastAvatar (LPIPS 0.1267, Identity 0.070) also surpasses HeadGap (0.1592 / 0.101) and Arc2Avatar (0.4665 / 0.308). The unique incremental-reconstruction capability—progressively fusing new frames without retraining—has no direct counterpart in any baseline, which are locked to fixed input lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

FastAvatar is architecturally derived from VGGT, replacing its Dense Prediction Transformer head with an MLP that directly predicts canonical 3DGS attributes, and anchoring outputs to FLAME mesh vertices as positional prompts. It departs from prior feed-forward avatar methods (LAM, which handles only single frames; Avat3r, which is fixed to 4 frames) by introducing variable-length input processing via interleaved global and frame attention with multi-granular positional encodings (camera pose, expression, head pose). The method also diverges from optimization-based 3DGS avatar pipelines (GaussianAvatars, MonoGaussianAvatar) by eliminating per-scene optimization entirely, instead using a Sliced Fusion Loss and Landmark Tracking Loss to supervise cross-frame Gaussian registration in a single forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FastAvatar establishes incremental 3D avatar reconstruction as a viable paradigm: a single unified model can progressively refine quality as more observations arrive, addressing the rigid input-length constraints that have limited both optimization-based and feed-forward methods. The practical impact is significant—seconds-level reconstruction from arbitrary-length daily recordings (selfies, vlogs, multi-view captures) opens the door to low-cost avatar creation without studio setups. Key limitations remain: reliance on FLAME/LBS restricts expression fidelity (wrinkles, eye-gaze, tongue), and the 16-frame attention window (mitigated by FramePack compression) still bounds per-pass capacity. Open questions include whether the FLAME dependency can be replaced with learned deformations and whether incremental fusion scales beyond head avatars to full-body reconstruction.
