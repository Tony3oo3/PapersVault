## Problem

Self-evolving LLMs that generate, refine, and learn from their own experiences offer a scalable path toward superintelligence, but existing training methods still depend on human-curated tasks and labels or on external verification oracles (e.g., code executors). This creates a fundamental bottleneck: truly autonomous self-improvement requires eliminating both the need for pre-existing task corpora and the need for domain-specific verifiers, which prior label-free RL and self-challenging approaches have not achieved simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a single base LLM as input—no external tasks, labels, seed questions, or verification environments. The Challenger outputs math problems in a strict format enclosed in <question> tags with a \boxed{} answer, while the Solver outputs step-by-step reasoning ending in a \boxed{} final answer. The final output is an improved reasoning LLM whose capabilities generalize beyond math to general-domain benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA, BBEH).

## Method

R-Zero initializes two independent models from the same base LLM—a Challenger and a Solver—that co-evolve through an iterative GRPO-based loop. In the Challenger phase, the model is rewarded via an uncertainty score r = 1 − 2|p̂ − 0.5| (maximized when the frozen Solver's majority-vote accuracy is 50%) minus a BLEU-clustering repetition penalty, pushing it to generate questions at the edge of the Solver's capability. In the Solver phase, the model is fine-tuned via GRPO on a filtered subset of Challenger-generated questions (retained only if |p̂ − 0.5| ≤ δ, discarding too-easy or too-hard items) using pseudo-labels from its own majority vote as verifiable binary rewards. The theoretical motivation for the 50% target comes from a KL-divergence lower bound showing that learning potential is maximized at maximum reward variance. This self-supervised cycle repeats without any human intervention, progressively raising question difficulty while the Solver's pseudo-label accuracy systematically declines—a trade-off the paper identifies as a key limitation.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning benchmarks (Table 1), R-Zero improves Qwen3-4B-Base from 42.57 to 49.93 average (+7.36) and Qwen3-8B-Base from 48.64 to 53.72 (+5.08) after three co-evolution iterations. On general-domain reasoning (Table 2), Qwen3-4B-Base gains +4.81 (26.34→31.15) and Qwen3-8B-Base gains +2.52 (31.98→34.50) on the overall average across SuperGPQA, MMLU-Pro, and BBEH. The framework is model-agnostic, also improving OctoThinker-3B (+2.09) and OctoThinker-8B (+2.11) on math, though gains on the smaller OctoThinker-3B are more modest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is Absolute Zero (Zhao et al., 2025a), a reinforced self-play method that also operates without external data. R-Zero outperforms Absolute Zero on math benchmarks across all four model families: by +3.51 on Qwen3-4B-Base (49.93 vs. 46.42), +1.04 on Qwen3-8B-Base (53.72 vs. 52.68), +1.81 on OctoThinker-3B (29.32 vs. 27.51), and +1.92 on OctoThinker-8B (38.52 vs. 36.60). On general-domain reasoning, R-Zero also beats Absolute Zero on both Qwen models (+1.82 on 4B, +0.10 on 8B), though the margin narrows at 8B scale. An internal ablation baseline, R-Zero(challenger), trains the Solver on questions from an untrained (base) Challenger; the full R-Zero with RL-trained Challenger consistently surpasses it, confirming that the uncertainty-reward-driven curriculum is essential (e.g., +4.92 on Qwen3-4B-Base math: 49.93 vs. 45.01). Notably, on OctoThinker-3B general reasoning, R-Zero actually degrades performance (11.12 vs. 16.03 base), suggesting the self-generated curriculum can be harmful for weaker models on out-of-domain tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

R-Zero extends the self-play paradigm for LLMs (previously applied mainly to verifiable domains like code generation with external executors) to general mathematical reasoning, where no such oracle exists. It departs from label-free RL methods (which still require a pre-existing task corpus) and from self-challenging approaches like Absolute Zero (which use a single model for both generation and solving) by introducing a two-model Challenger-Solver architecture with an uncertainty-based reward that targets the Solver's capability frontier. The training mechanism builds directly on GRPO and RLVR, but the key conceptual contribution is the co-evolutionary curriculum that eliminates all external data dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

R-Zero demonstrates that a fully autonomous, zero-data self-evolution loop can meaningfully improve reasoning across multiple architectures and scales, and that math-focused self-generated training generalizes to broader domains. However, the paper's most important finding is the inevitable performance collapse after a small number of iterations, driven by degrading pseudo-label accuracy (79%→63%) and potential model collapse from self-synthesized data — a critical open problem for the self-evolving LLM agenda. The framework is also limited to domains with objective correctness criteria, leaving open-ended or subjective tasks unaddressed. The finding that R-Zero acts as an effective mid-training step before SFT (+2.35 points over SFT alone) suggests a practical near-term role even if full autonomy remains elusive.
