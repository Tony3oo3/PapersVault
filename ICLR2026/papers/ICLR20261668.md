## Problem

Diffusion model sampling suffers from two coupled limitations: the large number of transition steps required for generation (slow inference) and the accumulation of approximation errors in the learned transition kernels (reduced fidelity). Prior work treats these as separate problems—acceleration methods reduce NFE without theoretical convergence guarantees, while correction methods improve distributional accuracy at the cost of additional computation—making it difficult to achieve both speed and accuracy simultaneously in a training-free setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261668/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained score network and a lightweight time-dependent discriminator (trained separately with weighted BCE loss), plus optional conditioning signals (class labels for ImageNet, text prompts for Stable Diffusion v1.5). It produces generated images at standard resolutions (32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 and 256×256 ImageNet, 256×256 CelebA-HQ, 512×512 text-to-image). A key operational detail is that the discriminator is invoked only during the MALA chain construction at the target timestep τ, not throughout the full denoising trajectory, yielding significantly lower discriminator NFE than prior correction methods like DG or DiffRS.

## Method

AC-Sampler accelerates sampling by denoising only from the prior to an intermediate timestep τ (skipping T−τ steps), then runs a Metropolis-Adjusted Langevin Algorithm (MALA) chain at τ to correct the sample toward the true marginal q_τ before final denoising to t=0. The acceptance probability is made tractable via a density-ratio decomposition (Theorem 4.1) that factors into a forward Gaussian term, a likelihood ratio q_t/p_t^θ estimated by the discriminator, and a proposal Gaussian term; a clever choice of x̂_{t-1} as the midpoint of the two transition-kernel means causes the transition-kernel terms to cancel exactly. The Langevin proposal reuses the same score evaluation as the denoising step, so a single network call serves both roles. Theoretically, each MALA step monotonically decreases the KL divergence from the true data distribution (Theorem 4.4), and the expected NFE reduction is strictly positive when the acceptance probability exceeds 1/(T−τ+1) (Proposition 4.2). In practice, a propose-until-accept (Jump Markov chain) design replaces the standard MH reject-and-stay rule to avoid empirical distribution distortion under finite sample budgets.

## Results

On unconditional CIFAR-10, AC-Sampler achieves FID 2.38 with only 15.8 NFEs, improving over the base EDM(Heun) sampler's FID 3.23 at 17 NFEs. On CelebA-HQ 256×256, it attains FID 6.60 with 98.27 NFEs (vs. 8.14 for DLG at 107.21 NFEs). On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, AC-Sampler reduces NFE from 511 to 483.86 while slightly improving FID from 1.43 to 1.42. For text-to-image with Stable Diffusion v1.5, it improves FID from 24.34 to 23.16 and ImageReward from 0.2097 to 0.4531 while reducing NFE from 50 to 45.24.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against correction methods on CIFAR-10, AC-Sampler consistently outperforms: DG achieves FID 1.93 at 27 NFEs while DG+AC reaches 1.84 at 26.19 NFEs; DiffRS yields FID 2.02 at 30.73 NFEs, whereas AC-Sampler alone achieves 2.38 at 15.81 NFEs (a 48% NFE reduction with better FID than DiffRS at the same budget). Against acceleration methods, DPM-v3 produces FID 12.41 at 5 NFEs, but DPM-v3+AC improves this to 9.88 at 4.78 NFEs, demonstrating that the MH correction recovers quality lost by aggressive step skipping. On CelebA-HQ 256×256, AC-Sampler (joint) achieves FID 6.60 at 98.27 NFEs, substantially outperforming DLG (FID 8.14 at 107.21 NFEs) and the base ScoreSDE KAR2 (FID 29.74 at 198 NFEs). On ImageNet 256×256, AC-Sampler matches or slightly improves FID (1.42 vs. 1.43) while reducing NFE by ~5%, and on DiT(DDPM) it improves FID from 2.35 to 2.31 with 234.38 NFEs vs. 250. For text-to-image (SD v1.5), AC-Sampler improves FID (23.16 vs. 24.34), ImageReward (0.4531 vs. 0.2097), and GenEval overall score (0.4453 vs. 0.4219) while using fewer NFEs (45.24 vs. 50.00).

## Relation to Prior Work

AC-Sampler sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads in diffusion sampling: acceleration (DDIM, DPM-Solver, UniPC) and correction (DG, DiffRS, Restart). It builds directly on the Metropolis-Hastings framework with Langevin (MALA) proposals for the correction mechanism, and on the time-dependent discriminator from DG (Kim et al., 2023) for tractable density-ratio estimation. A key departure is the formal demonstration (Proposition A.1) that DLG's joint-space Gibbs sampling cannot converge to the true distribution even with an optimal time classifier, motivating the MH accept-reject step as a necessary correction. The method is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to and composable with existing acceleration solvers and correction schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical tension in deployment: real-world text-to-image services need many samples quickly and accurately, yet prior work treated speed and fidelity as competing objectives. AC-Sampler's training-free design (only a lightweight discriminator is trained) and composability with existing solvers make it a drop-in improvement for practitioners. Limitations include sensitivity to the target timestep τ and Langevin SNR, the propose-until-accept design deviating from strict MH theory (mitigating but not eliminating finite-sample distortion), and the fact that class-conditional settings inherently constrain MCMC chain length. An open question is whether the discriminator quality bottleneck becomes the limiting factor at very high resolutions or with very strong base models where the model-data gap is already small.
