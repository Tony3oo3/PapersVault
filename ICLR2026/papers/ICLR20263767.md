## Problem

GRPO's clipped surrogate objective lacked a first-principles derivation guaranteeing reward improvement, and its iterative form with sample reuse implicitly operates off-policy without formal analysis. Additionally, the zero-variance masking heuristic proposed in DAPO had no theoretical justification, and no off-policy GRPO formulation existed that could reduce the communication overhead of serving large models for inference at every training iteration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (e.g., math word problems from GSM8K or DeepScaleR) and a verifiable binary reward signal indicating answer correctness. For each prompt, a group of G responses is sampled from a policy (current or lagged), and the advantage is computed as the reward standardized by the group's empirical mean and standard deviation. The output is an updated LLM policy; in the off-policy variant, the vLLM inference server is refreshed only every v iterations rather than every iteration, reducing inter-GPU weight-transfer costs during tensor-parallel serving.

## Method

The paper derives policy-improvement lower bounds (Theorem 1, Corollary 1) showing that maximizing the expected GRPO advantage—whether computed on-policy or off-policy—guarantees reward improvement, with the gap controlled by total variation distances weighted by policy-dependent constants (1−σ_{α,r,ε})/σ_{α,r,ε}. From these bounds, a KL-constrained optimization problem is formulated and relaxed into a clipped surrogate objective f_ε(π/α, π_k/α, A_α) that generalizes the standard on-policy GRPO clip. The off-policy variant sets α = π_{k−v} and draws fresh samples each iteration, approximating π_k/α ≈ 1 for small v, which stabilizes training and enables serving the model on the vLLM server only every v steps. The lower bound also provides theoretical grounding for masking zero-variance groups, since the constant (1−σ)/σ diverges when all samples in a group share the same reward. Experiments on GSM8K (0.5B), DeepScaleR (1.5B), and orz-math-57k (7B) confirm that off-policy GRPO matches or exceeds on-policy performance while yielding a 1.35× wall-clock speedup under tensor-parallel colocated serving.

## Results

On GSM8K with Qwen2.5-0.5B, off-policy GRPO (v=10, i=1) achieves a Pass@1 of ~50%, matching the best on-policy variant with zero-variance masking and outperforming standard on-policy GRPO (~45%). On DeepScaleR with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, off-policy GRPO (v10-i1) achieves a mean AIME24 Pass@1 of 30.49% (max 32.50%) and mean Math500 of 84.74% (max 87.2%), performing on par with on-policy GRPO (v1-i1: 30.22% AIME24, 85.19% Math500). On Qwen2.5-7B with tensor parallelism (TP=4), off-policy GRPO delivers a 1.35× wall-clock speedup (0.038 vs 0.028 iterations/sec) while reaching the same 50% reward as on-policy GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is on-policy GRPO (v=1, i=1) as originally proposed in Shao et al. (2024). On GSM8K, off-policy GRPO (v=10, i=1) matches the best on-policy variant with zero-variance masking at ~50% Pass@1, while standard on-policy GRPO without masking plateaus at ~45%. On AIME24, off-policy GRPO achieves a slightly higher mean (30.49% vs 30.22%) and max (32.50% vs 32.29%) than on-policy GRPO. On Math500, the two are essentially tied (84.74% vs 85.19% mean), with off-policy reaching a marginally higher max (87.2% vs 87.0%). The paper also compares against Shao et al.'s sample-reuse off-policy variant (v=1, i=10), which converges more slowly over an epoch due to 10× computational cost per iteration. Overall, off-policy GRPO never underperforms on-policy GRPO and provides a 1.35× speedup in the 7B tensor-parallelism setting, confirming the theoretical guarantee that off-policy training does not sacrifice reward improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the off-policy PPO framework (G-PPO, ToPPO) to the GRPO setting, where the advantage is a whitened reward computed from group statistics rather than a learned critic. It builds directly on Shao et al.'s GRPO algorithm and Mroueh's analysis showing that iterative GRPO with sample reuse implicitly performs off-policy advantage estimation, but formalizes this into a principled off-policy objective with a clipped surrogate derived from constrained optimization. The key departure from prior off-policy PPO work is that the proof technique exploits the analytical form of the GRPO advantage (whitened reward) rather than relying on state-visitation distributions, making the analysis applicable beyond MDP settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee that off-policy GRPO (with a lagged policy for advantage estimation) preserves reward improvement, and gives a principled justification for zero-variance masking previously proposed heuristically in DAPO. Practically, the 1.35× wall-clock speedup from reducing vLLM model-update frequency is directly relevant to large-scale LLM post-training where tensor-parallel serving dominates communication costs. Limitations include experiments on relatively small models (0.5B–7B) on a single node, and the theory requires the off-policy to remain close to the current policy (small v), leaving open how much staleness is tolerable at scale. A natural open question is whether the off-policy advantage estimation interacts beneficially with the success-rate amplification effect in multi-stage training at larger model scales.
