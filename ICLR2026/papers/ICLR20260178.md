## Problem

Existing multivariate time series forecasting methods primarily capture coarse-grained, static correlations between variables, overlooking the fact that variable interactions are highly dynamic and manifest differently at each time step and frequency band. While attention-based and graph-based approaches can model fine-grained dependencies, they incur quadratic computational and memory costs that scale poorly with the number of variables. The paper asks whether a convolutional architecture can explicitly model these fine-grained, temporally evolving cross-variable interactions at both time and frequency granularities while remaining substantially more efficient than attention.

## Input / Output

The model takes a C×L multivariate time series (C variables, fixed lookback L=96) and projects each variable into a D-dimensional embedding (C×D) before processing. Variables are reshaped into a 2D grid of size ⌈√C⌉×⌈√C⌉ (row-major, with former-padding if C is not a perfect square) to reduce effective inter-variable distance. The output is a C×H predicted sequence obtained by a linear projection along the hidden dimension after fusing time-domain and frequency-domain representations with a learned weight α.

## Method

FACT's core component is the DConvBlock, which replaces attention with multi-dilated depth-wise Inception convolutions that assign each channel (granularity) its own kernel to capture variable-specific interactions. The architecture operates in a dual-domain strategy: in the time domain, stacked DConvBlocks process embeddings directly; in the frequency domain, embeddings are transformed via DFT, decomposed into amplitude and phase, each modeled by separate DConvBlocks, then recombined via inverse DFT. To expand the receptive field without stacking many layers, the 1D variable dimension is reshaped into a 2D grid and convolved with progressively increasing dilation rates (default [1,2,3,2,1] across 5 layers), reducing the required layer count by roughly √(S/s) compared to 1D. A feed-forward network with batch normalization and residual connections integrates cross-granularity information, and the final time- and frequency-domain outputs are fused with a dataset-specific weight α before a linear head produces the forecast.

## Results

FACT achieves state-of-the-art performance on 21 out of 24 MSE/MAE metrics across 12 benchmark datasets, winning all 12 metrics on the PEMS suite (Table 1) and 9 out of 12 on the remaining datasets (Table 2). On PEMS03, FACT reduces MSE by 23.9% over iTransformer (0.086 vs. 0.113), 22.5% over ModernTCN (0.111), and 27.1% over CycleNet (0.118). On METR-LA, FACT achieves MSE 0.704, a 15.8% improvement over iTransformer (0.837) and 14.0% over TimeFilter (0.819). FACT also delivers up to 50% reduction in training time and memory cost compared to attention-based models on large-scale datasets (Traffic, PEMS07, Traffic2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FACT outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric in Table 1 (PEMS suite), achieving 1st place on all 12 MSE/MAE scores. Against the strongest attention-based baselines, FACT reduces MSE by 23.9% over iTransformer and 15.8% over TimeXer on PEMS03, and by 15.8% over iTransformer on METR-LA. Against the strongest CNN baseline ModernTCN, FACT improves MSE by 22.5% on PEMS03 and 12.8% on METR-LA. Against the graph-based TimeFilter, FACT achieves 20.4% MSE reduction on PEMS03 and 14.0% on METR-LA. Against the variable-independent CycleNet, FACT reduces MSE by 27.1% on PEMS03 and 5.3% on METR-LA. On Table 2 datasets, FACT wins 9 of 12 metrics; the three losses are on ECL (MSE 0.157 vs. TimeFilter 0.158 — a near-tie) and Solar-Energy (MSE 0.196 vs. TQNet 0.198 — also a near-tie), indicating FACT is competitive but not dominant on smaller or less variable-interaction-heavy datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

FACT positions itself as a convolutional alternative to the dominant attention-based paradigm for multivariate time series forecasting (iTransformer, TimeXer, Crossformer), drawing its core building block from the Inception architecture (Szegedy et al., 2015) and adapting it into a depth-wise, multi-dilated variant. It also departs from graph-based approaches (TimeFilter, MTGNN) that model variable interactions via adjacency matrices, arguing that depth-wise convolutions can capture equivalent fine-grained interactions at lower cost. In the frequency domain, FACT builds on the amplitude-phase representation used by FREDformer and FilterNet but applies it to a separate DConvBlock rather than a shared attention or MLP module, and it explicitly combines time- and frequency-domain modeling with a learned weighting factor—a dual-domain strategy less common in prior CNN-based time series work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the demonstrated 50% reduction in training time and memory over attention-based models on high-dimensional datasets (800+ variables), which is meaningful for deployment in traffic, energy, and weather systems where variable counts are large. The 2D reshaping of variables into a grid to reduce required network depth is a simple but effective trick that could be adopted by other convolutional time series architectures. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully to datasets with thousands of variables, whether the fixed row-major reshaping (shown to be order-agnostic) is truly sufficient or whether learned spatial arrangements could further improve performance, and whether the dual time-frequency modeling generalizes to non-stationary or regime-switching series where the weighting factor α may need to be time-varying.
