## Problem

Existing dense 3D reconstruction methods (DUSt3R, MASt3R, MoGe) predict geometric quantities in isolation, typically focusing on pointmaps or depth while neglecting surface normals, leading to inconsistent and less accurate predictions. The paper asks whether a single unified model can jointly regress multiple geometric quantities—pointmaps, depth, normals, and matching features—while explicitly modeling their structural coupling to improve overall accuracy and consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262724/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Dens3R takes unposed RGB image pairs (no camera intrinsics or extrinsics required) as input and supports multi-view and multi-resolution inputs ranging from 512 to 2K+ pixels. The shared encoder-decoder processes the pair in a single forward pass, eliminating the main/reference view distinction used in DUSt3R/MASt3R. Outputs include a 3D pointmap (the core representation), depth map, surface normal map, C-dimensional matching features, and camera parameters (9-dim intrinsics + extrinsics), all produced simultaneously from the same backbone.

## Method

Dens3R uses a dense visual transformer with a weight-shared encoder-decoder and multiple DPT prediction heads, trained in two stages. Stage 1 learns a scale-invariant pointmap via cross-view consistency using local/global 3D regression losses and an infoNCE-based pixel matching loss. Stage 2 introduces an intrinsic-invariant pointmap by concatenating normal features to the pointmap representation (P_i^n = P_i ⊕ n), switching from one-to-many to one-to-one mapping, and adding a dedicated normal head with L1 supervision—exploiting the deterministic one-to-one nature of normals to resolve monocular ambiguity and regularize the pointmap. Position-interpolated RoPE (adapted from LLM context-window extension) enables stable high-resolution inference, and a coarse-to-fine schedule (512→1024) further improves fidelity. A post-processing pipeline using one-versus-all matching and triangulation extends inference to multi-view inputs for geometrically consistent reconstruction.

## Results

On normal prediction, Dens3R achieves the best mean angular error across all five benchmarks: 16.1° on NYUv2, 16.9° on ScanNet, 16.0° on IBims-1, 30.7° on Sintel, and 20.8° on DIODE-outdoor, with δ₁₁.₂₅° reaching 62.5%, 64.0%, 72.2%, 28.9%, and 43.0% respectively. On image matching (ZEB benchmark), Dens3R achieves a mean AUC@5° of 64.5, surpassing MASt3R (59.9) and ROMA (53.2). For monocular depth on DIODE-outdoor, Dens3R attains the lowest REL of 0.387 (vs. VGGT 0.400, MoGe 0.578), and for camera pose estimation on Map-free, it reduces reprojection error to 30.4 px (vs. MASt3R 57.2 px, VGGT 48.8 px).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dens3R outperforms all normal-prediction baselines (DSINE, Lotus, GeoWizard, StableNormal) by 1.4–4.7° in mean angular error across every dataset, with the largest gains on Sintel (30.7° vs. 34.9° for DSINE) and DIODE-outdoor (20.8° vs. 22.0°). For image matching, Dens3R surpasses MASt3R by 4.6 AUC@5° points on ZEB (64.5 vs. 59.9) and by 3.2 points on ScanNet-1500 (65.6 vs. 62.4), while also beating 2D matchers (SuperGlue, LoFTR, DKM, ROMA) by 11–33 points. On monocular depth, Dens3R wins on DIODE-outdoor (REL 0.387) but trails MoGe (0.035) and VGGT (0.038) on NYUv2, and VGGT (0.064) on DIODE-indoor, indicating its depth advantage is most pronounced in challenging outdoor/reflective scenes. For camera pose estimation on Map-free, Dens3R achieves the lowest reprojection error (30.4 px) and highest pose AUC (0.852), outperforming MASt3R (57.2 px, 0.746) and VGGT (48.8 px, 0.577). Overall, Dens3R wins or ties on 12 of 14 baseline comparisons, with the two losses being narrow depth-estimation gaps on indoor benchmarks where MoGe and VGGT have a slight edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dens3R is directly built on the DUSt3R/MASt3R lineage of feed-forward pointmap regression from unposed image pairs, adopting their shared-weight encoder architecture and loss formulations (local/global 3D regression, infoNCE matching loss). The key architectural departure is a fully shared encoder-decoder (rather than separate decoders for main and reference views) and the introduction of position-interpolated RoPE borrowed from LLM context-window extension to handle multi-resolution inputs. Conceptually, the paper extends MoGe's affine-invariant pointmap into an 'intrinsic-invariant' formulation by concatenating surface normals to the pointmap representation, motivated by the observation that normals are locally deterministic and thus provide a more stable geometric anchor than depth alone. The two-stage training (scale-invariant → intrinsic-invariant) is a novel training strategy not present in any of the prior DUSt3R-family methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying depth, normals, pointmaps, and image matching in a single feed-forward model, Dens3R offers a practical backbone that can be fine-tuned with lightweight DPT heads for diverse downstream tasks (segmentation, surface reconstruction, pose estimation) without retraining the backbone. The bidirectional pointmap–normal interaction—where multi-view geometric context resolves monocular normal ambiguity while normals regularize the pointmap—provides a principled mechanism for improving all predicted quantities simultaneously. Limitations remain for thin structures and very low-texture regions, and the two-stage training with 32 H20 GPUs for ~two weeks per stage is a non-trivial compute barrier. Open questions include whether the intrinsic-invariant formulation generalizes to video or 4D settings, and whether the coarse-to-fine resolution schedule can be replaced by a single high-resolution training pass with the interpolated RoPE.
