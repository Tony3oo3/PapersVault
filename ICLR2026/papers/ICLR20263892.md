## Problem

Existing membership inference attacks on text-to-image latent diffusion models assume access to ground-truth training captions, an assumption that fails in realistic auditing scenarios where only images are available. Replacing ground-truth captions with VLM-generated approximations causes substantial performance degradation in state-of-the-art methods such as CLiD, rendering prior approaches ineffective in the caption-free setting.

## Input / Output

The method requires a single query image and white-box access to the target LDM (to evaluate conditional and unconditional denoising losses at a fixed timestep t=140 within a T=1000 schedule). It outputs a binary membership decision computed from a weighted combination of the MOFIT score (L_cond under the model-fitted embedding minus L_uncond) and an auxiliary loss, both robustly scaled and thresholded. The two-stage optimization (surrogate perturbation via gradient-sign updates, then embedding extraction via Adam) takes approximately 7–9 minutes per image on a single RTX 4090, though early stopping can reduce this to under 30 seconds at a small performance cost.

## Method

MOFIT exploits the empirical observation that member samples exhibit significantly larger increases in conditional denoising loss under mismatched conditioning than hold-out samples, while unconditional loss remains stable for both groups. In the first stage (Model-Fitted Surrogate Optimization), a perturbation δ is iteratively applied to the query image via gradient-sign updates to minimize the unconditional loss at a fixed noise vector and timestep, producing a surrogate x₀* that is overfitted to the model's learned distribution. In the second stage (Surrogate-Driven Embedding Extraction), a text embedding φ* is optimized (initialized from a VLM caption embedding) to minimize the conditional loss on the surrogate, forming a tightly coupled (x₀*, φ*) pair. At inference, the original query x₀ is conditioned on φ*—a deliberate image-condition mismatch—causing member samples to show pronounced L_cond increases while hold-outs remain relatively unaffected. The final membership score is a weighted, robustly-scaled combination of this amplified L_cond − L_uncond discrepancy and an auxiliary loss (either L_uncond or a VLM-based CLiD score), thresholded to produce the binary decision.

## Results

Under the caption-free setting, MOFIT achieves 94.48% ASR / 97.30 AUC on Pokemon, 88.00% ASR / 94.17 AUC on MS-COCO, and 86.00% ASR / 91.32 AUC on Flickr, consistently outperforming all VLM-conditioned baselines by up to +25% ASR and +30–47% TPR@1%FPR. Notably, on MS-COCO MOFIT surpasses even CLiD with ground-truth captions in ASR (88.00 vs. 86.50) and AUC (94.17 vs. 90.27), though it trails on TPR@1%FPR (47.00 vs. 68.80). On the pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.5 (Webster memorized samples), MOFIT reaches 77.61% ASR, exceeding GT-captioned CLiD (77.38%) and all VLM-conditioned baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOFIT decisively outperforms all five VLM-conditioned baselines (Loss, SecMI, PIA, PFAMI, CLiD) across every dataset and metric. The largest gaps are against CLiD-VLM: +16.93 ASR on Pokemon (94.48 vs. 77.55), +7.10 on MS-COCO (88.00 vs. 80.90), and +7.00 on Flickr (86.00 vs. 79.00). Against the strongest VLM baseline PFAMI, MOFIT gains +20.05 ASR on Pokemon and +7.10 on MS-COCO. Compared to the GT-captioned CLiD upper bound, MOFIT is competitive on Pokemon (94.48 vs. 96.52 ASR) and actually surpasses it on MS-COCO in both ASR (88.00 vs. 86.50) and AUC (94.17 vs. 90.27), though it lags on TPR@1%FPR (47.00 vs. 68.80) and on Flickr (86.00 vs. 91.10 ASR). The black-box method ReDiffuse (Appendix A.15) also degrades substantially under the distributionally-aligned evaluation protocol, further confirming MOFIT's advantage in realistic caption-free scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the membership inference attack (MIA) literature for text-to-image latent diffusion models, building most directly on CLiD (Zhai et al., 2024), which scores membership via the discrepancy between conditional and unconditional denoising losses. All prior MIA methods for text-conditioned LDMs (CLiD, SecMI, PIA, PFAMI, Loss-based) assume access to ground-truth image captions, an assumption the authors argue is impractical for real-world auditors. MOFIT departs from this lineage by replacing the ground-truth caption with a two-stage optimization that first constructs a model-fitted surrogate image (overfitting the query to the model's unconditional prior) and then extracts a tightly coupled embedding from that surrogate, deliberately creating an image-condition mismatch at inference time to exploit members' heightened sensitivity to misaligned conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: auditors and artists checking for training-data memorization typically lack access to the exact captions used during training, rendering all prior MIA methods ineffective when VLM-generated captions are substituted. The empirical finding that member samples exhibit systematically larger conditional-loss increases under misaligned conditioning than hold-out samples provides a reusable signal for future caption-free auditing work. Key limitations include the 7–9 minute per-image runtime (mitigated partially by early stopping) and the observation that LoRA fine-tuning largely neutralizes the attack, suggesting parameter-efficient adaptation may be a viable defense. Open questions include whether the sensitivity asymmetry persists for models with fundamentally different architectures (e.g., DiT-based SD v3 shows weaker absolute gains) and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D diffusion models.
