## Problem

Attention-based multiple instance learning (MIL) for whole slide image (WSI) diagnosis suffers from three interrelated failure modes: unstable attention dynamics (attention distributions oscillate across epochs without converging, a previously unreported issue), over-concentrated attention (softmax assigns excessive mass to a few tiles), and overfitting (limited WSI training samples). No prior method simultaneously addresses all three, and the instability problem in particular undermines both predictive performance and clinical interpretability.

## Input / Output

Each WSI is segmented into non-overlapping 256×256 tiles at 20× magnification and embedded by a frozen pretrained encoder (ResNet-18 ImageNet or ViT-SSL). These tile tokens are concatenated with a small set of N trainable FEAT tokens (N ≪ M tiles) that serve as an information-aggregation bottleneck. The model outputs a slide-level class label (binary for CAMELYON, three-class for BRACS) and a per-tile attention map used for tumor localization; at inference the anchor branch is discarded, so no extra FLOPs are incurred.

## Method

ASMIL introduces a dual-branch architecture where an anchor model (identical attention module to the online model) is updated via exponential moving average rather than backpropagation, providing a temporally smoothed reference attention distribution. The online model is regularized by minimizing KL divergence between its softmax attention and the anchor's attention, which is computed using a normalized sigmoid function (NSF) instead of softmax; Theorem 1 proves NSF achieves selective flattening—equalizing high-score tokens while suppressing low-score ones—that no single softmax temperature can replicate. During training, a Bernoulli mask randomly drops a fraction B of FEAT tokens to prevent co-adaptation and reduce overfitting, while all tokens are retained at inference. The joint training objective is L = L_CE + β·KL(α^nsf ‖ α), where the anchor is treated as stop-gradient and updated by EMA after each online gradient step.

## Results

With a ViT-SSL backbone, ASMIL achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three WSI subtyping benchmarks: F1 0.965 / AUC 0.985 on CAMELYON-16, F1 0.689 / AUC 0.898 on CAMELYON-17, and F1 0.781 / AUC 0.914 on BRACS. The largest gain is on CAMELYON-17, where ASMIL improves F1 by 6.49 percentage points over the strongest baseline (AEM, 0.647). On BRACS, ASMIL exceeds the previous best (AEM, 0.742 F1) by 3.9 points and AUC by 0.9 points. With an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 backbone, ASMIL remains competitive, achieving 0.814 F1 on CAMELYON-16 and 0.601 F1 on BRACS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASMIL outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset with the ViT-SSL backbone. The closest competitors are HDMIL (CVPR 2025) on CAMELYON-16 (0.958 vs. 0.965 F1, a 0.7-point gap) and AEM (MICCAI 2025) on CAMELYON-17 (0.647 vs. 0.689 F1, a 4.2-point gap) and BRACS (0.742 vs. 0.781 F1, a 3.9-point gap). On CAMELYON-17, ASMIL's advantage is most pronounced: it surpasses the second-best method (AEM) by 6.49 F1 points, highlighting its strength under sparse-tumor, weakly supervised conditions. On BRACS, ASMIL also leads in AUC (0.914 vs. AEM's 0.905). Earlier baselines such as TransMIL, DSMIL, and ABMIL trail by 4–10 F1 points on CAMELYON-17, indicating that the attention-stabilization and NSF components yield substantial gains over standard attention-based MIL. The paper further shows that plugging the anchor model and NSF into existing methods (e.g., ABMIL, CLAM-SB, TransMIL, DSMIL) consistently improves their F1 by up to 10.73 points (ABMIL on BRACS), confirming the components' generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

ASMIL extends the attention-based MIL paradigm established by ABMIL (Ilse et al., 2018) and TransMIL (Shao et al., 2021) by introducing an EMA-updated anchor model that stabilizes attention distributions, a normalized sigmoid function (NSF) in the anchor to prevent over-concentration, and token random dropping to combat overfitting. The anchor model conceptually draws from EMA-based self-supervised methods (Mean Teacher, BYOL, DINO) but repurposes the mechanism: rather than anchoring global representations or patch-patch similarity structure, it anchors the attention distribution itself via a KL-divergence loss. The NSF is positioned as a theoretically grounded alternative to softmax (with a proof that no single softmax temperature achieves selective flattening), and the token dropout is specialized to the FEAT-token aggregation architecture, distinguishing it from prior instance-level dropout strategies in MIL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of unstable attention dynamics as a distinct, previously unaddressed failure mode in attention-based MIL is the paper's most significant conceptual contribution, as it reframes how the community thinks about training convergence in weakly supervised WSI analysis. The anchor model and NSF are presented as a general plug-in module that consistently improves existing MIL methods (up to 10.73% F1 gain), suggesting broad practical utility beyond ASMIL itself. Open questions include whether the instability phenomenon and its remedy generalize to other weakly supervised aggregation settings beyond WSI, whether the specific NSF formulation is optimal compared to other non-softmax normalizations, and how the approach scales to even larger slide collections or multi-task clinical pipelines. The method adds training cost (one extra forward pass for the anchor) but no inference overhead, making it deployable in clinical settings.
