## Problem

KV cache eviction in LLMs requires estimating which prompt tokens are important for future response generation, but the true response is unknown during prefill. Recent 'glimpsing into the future' methods (e.g., LAQ, SpecKV) improve eviction accuracy by generating a surrogate draft response, but this draft generation step introduces substantial prefill latency (up to 14.5× overhead), making them impractical for latency-sensitive deployment. The paper asks whether one can capture the accuracy benefits of future-response-based importance estimation without paying the cost of explicit draft generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard prompt token sequence and appends 32 learnable soft lookahead tokens during the prefill phase only; these tokens are discarded before autoregressive decoding begins, so they introduce zero overhead to the decoding stage. The output is a compressed KV cache retaining the Top-K most important key-value pairs per layer and head, selected via importance scores computed as the mean cross-attention from the lookahead tokens' queries to the prompt keys. The lookahead LoRA modules are selectively activated exclusively for the lookahead tokens, leaving the forward pass of all normal input tokens bit-identical to the original model.

## Method

LOOKAHEADKV augments a frozen LLM with two lightweight components: (1) a set of 32 learnable lookahead tokens whose queries serve as an implicit 'observation window' replacing the need for a generated draft response, and (2) lookahead LoRA adapters (rank 8, applied to all projection and FFN layers) that activate only on these tokens to enable richer importance-predictive representations. Training uses a KL-divergence loss (equivalent to a ListNet ranking loss) between L1-normalized ground-truth importance scores—computed from the model's own generated responses—and the scores predicted by the lookahead tokens, across all layers and heads. The key insight is that the attention pattern of the true future response can be distilled into a fixed set of learned queries, eliminating the autoregressive draft generation step entirely. At inference, a single prefill forward pass with the appended tokens yields importance scores for Top-K eviction, with the additional compute being negligible (<2.16% TTFT overhead at 32K context) compared to the multi-pass draft generation required by LAQ or SpecKV.

## Results

On MT-Bench, LOOKAHEADKV achieves the best or near-best scores across all six models (LLaMA3.2-1B/3B, LLaMA3.1-8B, Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B) and all cache budgets (64–256), e.g., 7.26 vs. 7.10 (LAQ) and 6.80 (SnapKV) for LLaMA3.1-8B at budget 64, and 8.04 vs. 7.69 (SpecKV) and 7.26 (SnapKV) for Qwen3-8B at budget 64. On LongBench, LOOKAHEADKV leads all baselines at every budget from 64 to 2048 across all models (e.g., 27.93 vs. 26.36 LAQ and 24.22 SnapKV for LLaMA3.2-1B at budget 64). The method introduces less than 0.5% additional trainable parameters and incurs a TTFT overhead of only 1.74 ms at 32K context length, which is up to 14.5× lower than the 239 ms overhead of LAQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOOKAHEADKV consistently outperforms all five baselines on MT-Bench at low-to-mid budgets (64–128) across every model, with the largest margins over StreamingLLM (e.g., +1.09 over StreamingLLM for LLaMA3.1-8B at budget 64) and SnapKV (e.g., +0.46 over SnapKV for Qwen3-8B at budget 64). Against the draft-based methods, LOOKAHEADKV beats LAQ by 0.16–0.46 points and SpecKV by 0.35–0.78 points at budget 64, while incurring 14.5× less TTFT overhead (1.74 ms vs. 239 ms for LAQ at 32K). At the highest budget (256), the gap narrows and LAQ or SpecKV occasionally edges out LOOKAHEADKV by 0.01–0.10 points (e.g., LAQ 8.56 vs. LOOKAHEADKV 8.51 for Qwen3-8B), indicating that the advantage of learned importance prediction is most pronounced under tight cache constraints. On LongBench, LOOKAHEADKV leads all baselines at every budget from 64 to 2048 for all six models, with the advantage over LAQ ranging from +0.5 to +1.6 points at budget 64. On RULER at 32K context with budget 128, LOOKAHEADKV also outperforms all baselines, and it generalizes to 64K and 128K contexts (69.45 and 54.83 vs. 64.10 and 50.67 for LAQ, respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

LOOKAHEADKV extends the observation-window paradigm introduced by SnapKV, which uses the input suffix to approximate future attention importance, by replacing the fixed suffix with a small set of learnable soft tokens trained to compress the attention pattern of the true model response. It departs from the recent draft-based line of work (SpecKV, LAQ) that generates a surrogate response to improve eviction quality, eliminating that costly generation step entirely. The method repurposes prompt-tuning-style learnable embeddings and LoRA adapters—previously used for task adaptation—toward predicting internal model statistics (cross-attention distributions), with a KL-divergence training objective drawn from attention distillation (MiniLM) and listwise ranking losses (ListNet).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving draft-level eviction accuracy at a fraction of the latency (up to 14.5× lower TTFT overhead than LAQ), LOOKAHEADKV makes aggressive KV cache compression practical for latency-sensitive and resource-constrained deployments (mobile, edge, high-throughput serving). The method adds less than 0.5% trainable parameters and can be toggled on/off without altering base model weights, lowering adoption friction. Key limitations include evaluation only up to 8B-scale models and restriction to prefill-stage eviction; extending to decoding-stage eviction and validating on 70B+ models remain open. The robustness to training context length (trained at 16K, evaluated at 128K) is encouraging but the generalization ceiling is not yet characterized.
