## Problem

Existing guardrails for LLM agents mostly operate post-execution, leaving little room for controllable supervision at the plan level where intervention is safest. The paper identifies three critical gaps: a data gap (scarcity of diverse, high-quality harmful trajectory data), a model gap (lack of a compact, generalizable guardian that handles heterogeneous agent outputs), and an evaluation gap (benchmarks emphasizing execution-time risks rather than planning-stage plan quality).

## Input / Output

The guardrail consumes a planned action trajectory (a sequence of tool invocations) produced by an agent during its planning phase, which may arrive in any of nine heterogeneous formats (XML, JSON compact/pretty, Markdown, semicolon-delimited, tab-separated, timestamped, numbered steps, key-value, bullets). A cross-planner adapter first normalizes the input into a canonical schema before the guardian model processes it. The output is a structured triple: a binary harmless/risky decision, a fine-grained risk category drawn from an 8-category taxonomy (e.g., sensitive data privacy violations, compromised availability, external adversarial attack), and a concise human-interpretable rationale.

## Method

The system comprises three components. AuraGen is a synthetic data engine that (i) generates benign trajectories from structured metadata profiles, (ii) injects category-labeled risks via four strategies—single-step perturbation, multi-step corruption, new-branch diversion, and bridged-branch diversion—and (iii) filters outputs through a reward model scoring five quality dimensions (causal consistency, postcondition continuity, rationality, justification sufficiency, risk matching), with a lightweight SVM classifier replacing naive threshold filtering. Safiron, built on Ministral-8B, is trained in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on the AuraGen corpus followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning, where the reward function grants full credit for correct detection plus correct risk category, partial credit (0.5) for correct detection with wrong category, and zero otherwise; the RL dataset deliberately mixes easy and hard samples to prevent collapse. A cross-planner adapter, trained on programmatically and LLM-synthesized format variations, normalizes heterogeneous agent outputs into a unified representation, enabling the compact guardian to generalize across planner architectures without retraining.

## Results

On Pre-Exec Bench, Safiron (SFT+GRPO) achieves 94.9% classification accuracy, 97.3% harmful detection precision, 64.6% risk category accuracy, and 57.0% explanation correctness, substantially outperforming all proprietary and open-weight baselines. The best proprietary model (Claude-3.7-Sonnet) reaches only 62.3% classification accuracy and 31.8% risk category accuracy, while the best open-weight model (DeepSeek-V3) achieves 65.2% classification accuracy and 24.7% risk category accuracy. In the case study on real multi-agent systems (MetaGPT and AutoGen), the full guardrail with adapter exceeds 90% classification accuracy, confirming robustness under distributional shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Safiron (SFT+GRPO) decisively outperforms all general-purpose LLM baselines on Pre-Exec Bench: it achieves 94.9% classification accuracy versus 65.2% for the best open-weight model (DeepSeek-V3) and 62.3% for the best proprietary model (Claude-3.7-Sonnet), a gap of roughly 30 percentage points. On risk category accuracy, Safiron reaches 64.6% compared to 41.6% for Gemini-2.5-Pro (the strongest proprietary baseline on this metric) and 24.7% for DeepSeek-V3. Notably, GPT-5 achieves near-perfect harmful detection (99.0%) but only 42.5% overall classification accuracy, indicating severe over-flagging of benign trajectories. Against dedicated guardrail baselines, Llama-Guard-3-8B and LlamaFireWall (both basic scanning and alignment checker modules) all score below 60% classification accuracy on Pre-Exec Bench, confirming that existing content-moderation guardrails are not transferable to plan-level pre-execution safety. The paper attributes this gap to the fact that prior guardrails target toxicity and prompt injection rather than multi-step trajectory risk, underscoring the need for a purpose-built pre-execution guardian.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of post-execution content-moderation guardrails (Llama Guard, LlamaFireWall, Granite Guardian, ShieldGemma) by shifting the intervention point to the planning stage, where full action trajectories are analyzed before any tool is invoked. It builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for the RL training stage and draws on the agent-safety benchmarking literature (Agent-SafetyBench, R-Judge, ASB) as a starting point for scenario and tool coverage, but explicitly argues that those benchmarks emphasize execution-time or adversarial risks rather than plan-level, non-adversarial failures. The cross-planner adapter and the four-strategy risk injection mechanism (single-step, multi-step, new-branch, bridged-branch) are novel architectural choices not present in prior guardrail work, which typically assumes a fixed input format and a single risk taxonomy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM agents are deployed in high-stakes domains (healthcare, finance), the ability to intercept harmful plans before execution is a practical prerequisite for safe deployment, and this paper provides the first end-to-end pipeline (synthetic data engine + trained guardian + dedicated benchmark) targeting exactly that gap. Key limitations include reliance on LLM-synthesized trajectories that may not fully capture the long tail of real-world failure modes, a relatively small human-verified benchmark (~1,700 samples), and a case study limited to 100 trajectories per agentic system. Open questions include whether the 8-category risk taxonomy generalizes to truly novel or compound risks, how the guardrail performs under multi-agent cascading failures at production scale, and whether the data-ratio findings (harmful:harmless ≈ 1:4 optimal) transfer to other base models or task formulations.
