## Problem

ML-driven EDA for early-stage timing and power prediction is hindered by the absence of large-scale, open datasets that span the full RTL-to-layout design flow. Existing datasets either lack sufficient RTL entries (CircuitNet 2.0) or omit netlist and layout representations entirely (RTLCoder, VerilogEval), preventing models from learning cross-stage relationships between behavioral descriptions and physical outcomes. Additionally, prior datasets do not employ task-oriented augmentation to concentrate on industrially critical, performance-limiting designs.

## Input / Output

For timing prediction, the model consumes only RTL Verilog code as input and predicts post-layout metrics (arrival time, WNS, TNS), effectively performing cross-stage prediction from the earliest design abstraction to final physical implementation. For power prediction, both RTL text and netlist graph representations are jointly processed to estimate toggle rate and total power. The dataset comprises 15,863 designs (8,659 original + augmented variants) on a 45nm GSCLIB PDK at 1 GHz, with the test set restricted to un-augmented original designs to rigorously evaluate generalization.

## Method

The dataset is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) collection and validation of 8,659 RTL designs from open-source repositories, verified via synthesis and simulation; (2) AST-based mutation that applies context-aware operators (arithmetic substitution, logical cross-replacement, edge polarity toggling, constant ±1 modification) to Verilog syntax trees, preserving type safety and synthesis validity while generating structural diversity; (3) task-oriented filtering at the netlist/layout stage, where timing augmentation prioritizes designs with long critical paths and power augmentation uses Cadence Voltus vectorless analysis to exclude circuits with excessive inactive logic. All designs undergo iterative physical implementation (10–50 iterations) with Synopsys Design Compiler and Cadence Innovus, converging on placement density, timing stability, and power within tight tolerances. Evaluation employs multi-modal models—RTLDistil (cross-stage knowledge distillation from layout to RTL for timing) and MOSS (joint RTL+netlist graph learning for power)—demonstrating ~36% and ~13% error reductions over single-modal baselines on the augmented dataset.

## Results

On the timing prediction task (primary task), RTLDistil achieves PCC of 0.887 (AT), 0.871 (WNS), and 0.918 (TNS) with MAPE of 19.72%, 35.28%, and 40.15% respectively, representing a 70.4% PCC improvement over MasterRTL and 6.0% over RTL-Timer on arrival time. On the power prediction task, MOSS (Full) achieves PCC of 0.948 and MAPE of 7.4% for total power, surpassing DeepSeq2 by 19.0% in PCC. Across all models, training on the augmented CircuitNet 3.0 dataset yields approximately 36.0% and 12.9% error reductions for timing and power tasks respectively compared to the existing Resyn-27k dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RTLDistil (multi-stage knowledge distillation) dominates all single-modal timing baselines: it improves AT PCC by 70.4% over MasterRTL (0.887 vs 0.520) and by 6.0% over RTL-Timer (0.887 vs 0.835), while reducing WNS MAPE by 45.8% compared to MasterRTL (35.28% vs 65.12%). For power prediction, MOSS (Full) with multi-modal learning outperforms the netlist-only DeepSeq2 by 21.4% in toggle rate PCC (0.871 vs 0.759) and 19.0% in total power PCC (0.948 vs 0.872), and beats its own single-modal ablation (MOSS w/o Multi-Modal) by 31.1% in toggle rate PCC. GRASPE and VIRTUAL, evaluated on the power task across datasets, show that training on augmented CircuitNet 3.0 data yields 11.6% PCC gain and 12.9% MAPE reduction for VIRTUAL over Resyn-27k. All models consistently improve when trained on the augmented dataset versus original or Resyn-27k data, confirming that the task-oriented augmentation strategy benefits every baseline rather than only the proposed multi-modal architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

CircuitNet 3.0 extends the CircuitNet 1.0/2.0 lineage (Chai et al., 2023; Jiang et al., 2024), which provided layout-stage data for routability and IR-drop prediction but lacked sufficient RTL designs for early-stage modeling. It also departs from RTL-focused datasets (VerilogEval, RTLLM, RTLCoder) that offer synthesizable Verilog but omit netlist, layout, and performance metrics entirely. The key conceptual advance is the task-oriented augmentation framework: rather than simply collecting more designs, the paper uses AST-based Verilog mutation at the RTL stage followed by fine-grained, task-specific filtering at the netlist/layout stages (prioritizing timing-critical paths for timing tasks, and high-switching-activity circuits for power tasks), creating a dataset explicitly shaped around the hardest industrial cases rather than average-case designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale, fully open-source dataset spanning the complete RTL-to-layout workflow with ground-truth performance metrics, filling a critical gap that has limited ML-driven EDA research due to IP restrictions on commercial designs. The task-oriented augmentation strategy—focusing on designs that push timing and power budgets—directly addresses the industrial observation that model value is determined by worst-case accuracy, not average performance. Key limitations include the single 45nm GSCLIB technology node, single TT PVT corner, and reliance on specific commercial EDA tools (Synopsys DC, Cadence Innovus), raising open questions about generalizability to advanced nodes (7nm/5nm) and multi-corner scenarios. Whether the AST mutation strategy scales to SoC-level designs with millions of gates, and whether the 45nm-trained models transfer to other process technologies, remain important open questions for the community.
