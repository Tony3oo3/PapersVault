## Problem

Traditional cartoon production requires separate inbetweening and colorization stages, each demanding dense per-frame sketches and suffering from cross-stage error accumulation. Existing AI-assisted methods either struggle with large motions from sparse sketch inputs or require detailed per-frame line art, and their sequential processing compounds inaccuracies. The paper asks whether these two stages can be unified into a single generative process that works from as few as one sketch and one colored reference frame.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a variable number of sparse keyframe sketches (as few as one) at arbitrary temporal positions, one or more colored reference frames, an optional text prompt, and an optional binary region-wise mask indicating which spatial areas of each sketch are intentionally blank. It outputs a complete K-frame cartoon video (33 or 69 frames in demonstrations). Sketches are encoded through the same Video VAE as the target video (4 pixel frames compressed to 1 latent frame), with a binary temporal mask preserving exact keyframe location within each 4-frame block, and the model supports flexible inference with varying numbers of input sketches and output lengths.

## Method

ToonComposer builds on the Wan 2.1 DiT-based image-to-video model and introduces three key mechanisms. First, a sparse sketch injection mechanism embeds sketch latents as additional tokens appended to the video token sequence, with RoPE positional encodings borrowed from the corresponding video tokens to provide precise temporal alignment, avoiding the structural disruption of channel-wise concatenation. Second, a Spatial Low-Rank Adapter (SLRA) projects hidden states into a low-rank space, reshapes them to separate spatial and temporal dimensions, performs self-attention only along the spatial dimension of each frame, then projects back and adds as a residual to the main attention output—this adapts the DiT's appearance to the cartoon domain while leaving its temporal motion prior untouched, unlike standard LoRA which perturbs both. Third, a region-wise control mechanism concatenates a binary mask channel to sketch tokens during training, enabling artists to leave spatial regions blank for context-driven generation. The model is trained with a Rectified Flow velocity-prediction objective, and a position-aware residual with adjustable weight α provides tunable sketch adherence at inference time.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark, ToonComposer (14B) achieves a DISTS of 0.0926, roughly 5× lower than the best baseline (AniDoc at 0.5461), and a CLIP similarity of 0.9449 vs. 0.8665 for AniDoc. On the human-drawn PKBench, ToonComposer (14B) scores 0.9509 subject consistency, 0.9910 motion smoothness, and 0.7345 aesthetic quality, leading all baselines on every metric. In a 47-participant user study, ToonComposer wins 70.99% of aesthetic-quality votes and 68.58% of motion-quality votes, compared to 17.02% and 18.19% for the next-best method (ToonCrafter).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ToonComposer is compared against three prior AI-assisted cartoon production methods: ToonCrafter (inbetweening), AniDoc (colorization), and LVCD (colorization). All three baselines require a two-stage pipeline (interpolate sketches → colorize), whereas ToonComposer performs both in a single generative pass. ToonComposer (14B) outperforms every baseline on all seven metrics in the synthetic evaluation, with the most striking gap in DISTS (0.0926 vs. 0.5461–0.5571, a ~5× improvement in perceptual quality) and CLIP similarity (0.9449 vs. 0.8428–0.8665). On the out-of-distribution PKBench with human-drawn sketches, ToonComposer again leads on all four reference-free metrics, with aesthetic quality at 0.7345 versus 0.6479–0.6822 for baselines. The human evaluation confirms this dominance: ToonComposer receives ~71% of aesthetic-quality votes and ~69% of motion-quality votes, while the strongest baseline (ToonCrafter) receives only ~17–18%, indicating a clear and consistent win across both automated and perceptual measures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Wan 2.1 DiT-based video generation model and departs from the established two-stage cartoon production pipeline (inbetweening via ToonCrafter, then colorization via AniDoc/LVCD) by proposing a unified 'post-keyframing' formulation that handles both in a single generative pass. The key architectural departure is the Spatial Low-Rank Adapter (SLRA), which addresses a gap left by prior cartoon adaptation work (e.g., ToonCrafter's spatial-layer freezing in UNet-based models) that is inapplicable to DiT's fully coupled spatio-temporal attention. The sparse sketch injection mechanism, which appends sketch tokens to the sequence with RoPE positional mapping, extends the I2V conditioning paradigm of the base model to support arbitrary-temporal-position keyframe control without altering the latent channel structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By collapsing two error-prone sequential stages into one, ToonComposer eliminates cross-stage error accumulation and reduces artist workload to a single colored reference frame plus sparse sketches, making AI-assisted cartoon production more practical. The SLRA technique is potentially transferable to other domain-adaptation problems on DiT-based video models where preserving temporal priors is critical. Open questions include the substantial computational cost of the underlying diffusion model (limiting real-time or resource-constrained use), performance degradation on highly unconventional sketch styles (e.g., thick ink strokes), and whether the spatial-only adaptation principle generalizes beyond the cartoon domain to other stylized video generation tasks.
