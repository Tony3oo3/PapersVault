## Problem

The Muon optimizer, which uses Newton-Schulz orthogonalization for matrix updates, offers ~50% memory savings over AdamW by storing only the first moment. However, directly applying existing low-bit quantization techniques (designed for element-wise optimizers like AdamW) to Muon fails because the orthogonalization step dramatically amplifies quantization errors. This paper is the first to systematically analyze and solve the problem of compressing Muon's optimizer states to 4 bits.

## Input / Output

At each step, the method consumes the current weight matrix W, its gradient ∇L(W), and scalar hyperparameters (learning rate, momentum, weight decay) to produce an updated weight matrix. The optimizer state is stored as two components: a 4-bit quantized residual matrix (M_res) of the same shape as the original moment matrix, and 8-bit quantized factor matrices P_t ∈ R^{m×k} and R_t ∈ R^{n×k} capturing the top-k singular subspace, where k is typically 1/16 of the matrix rank. The dequantized moment is reconstructed as M_res + P_t R_t^T before the Newton-Schulz orthogonalization step.

## Method

4-bit-Muon-GRASP decomposes the moment matrix into a top singular subspace and a residual, exploiting the finding that Newton-Schulz iteration amplifies quantization error ~40× in the top subspace versus only ~5× in the residual. The top subspace is extracted via a single warm-started power iteration (reusing the previous step's right singular vectors) and stored at 8-bit, while the residual is compressed to 4-bit using grid quantization. Grid quantization computes element-wise normalization scales as min(row_max, col_max) within each block, providing tighter bounds than per-channel or per-token schemes and handling the outlier patterns that appear across both matrix dimensions. This asymmetric two-part compression reduces the relative error after orthogonalization from 1.78 to 0.14 while keeping the memory overhead of the subspace factors negligible (k·n + k·m ≪ m·n).

## Results

On pre-trained LLaMA models (130M, 350M, 1.1B) evaluated across 7 zero-shot benchmarks, 4-bit-Muon-GRASP achieves average accuracies of 41.9, 44.5, and 48.2, matching or exceeding fp32-Muon (41.8, 44.6, 48.0) while reducing total training memory by up to 28% (e.g., 10.14 GB vs. 13.22 GB for the 1.1B model). Validation perplexity is identical to fp32-Muon on the 1.1B model (12.48) and within 0.1 on the 350M model (15.67 vs. 15.57). On 7B fine-tuning (Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen2.5-7B-Math), 4-bit-Muon-GRASP achieves an average score of 62.8, slightly exceeding fp32-Muon's 62.6 across MMLU, HumanEval, MBPP, GSM8K, MATH, Minerva Math, and Olympiad Bench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fp32-Muon, 4-bit-Muon-GRASP matches or slightly exceeds full-precision accuracy on all three model sizes (e.g., 48.2 vs. 48.0 avg on 1.1B) while cutting total training memory by 23–28% (10.14 GB vs. 13.22 GB at 1.1B scale). Compared to 8-bit-Muon (group quantization), 4-bit-Muon-GRASP achieves equivalent or better accuracy (48.2 vs. 48.2 on 1.1B) at half the bit-width, yielding an additional ~1.1 GB memory saving. Against the naive 4-bit-Muon-base (direct group quantization without subspace preservation), GRASP closes the accuracy gap from 0.4–0.9 points to essentially zero (48.2 vs. 47.6 on 1.1B; 44.5 vs. 43.7 on 350M), confirming that the top-singular-subspace preservation and grid quantization are the critical components. In the fine-tuning setting on 7B models, 4-bit-Muon-GRASP (62.8 avg) marginally outperforms both fp32-Muon (62.6) and 4-bit-Muon-base (62.5), with the largest gains on Minerva Math (29.0 vs. 26.8/27.9). The paper also reports that 4-bit-Muon-GRASP is the most memory-efficient optimizer overall, achieving up to 48% memory reduction compared to fp32-AdamW at 5B scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of low-bit optimizer compression (8-bit AdamW by Dettmers et al., 4-bit AdamW by Li et al., 4-bit Shampoo by Wang et al.) to the Muon optimizer, whose Newton-Schulz orthogonalization step creates a fundamentally different error landscape than element-wise optimizers. The method borrows the warm-started power iteration from PowerSGD (Vogels et al., 2019) to extract the top singular subspace, and builds on the LLM-specific Muon adaptations from Liu et al. (2025). The key departure from prior quantization work is the recognition that orthogonalization amplifies quantization error anisotropically across singular subspaces, necessitating a split-compression strategy rather than uniform group quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With Muon gaining production adoption (Kimi-K2) and reported to nearly double AdamW's training efficiency, reducing its already-halved optimizer memory by another ~50% (to 4-bit) is practically significant for scaling LLM training on constrained hardware. The paper's systematic diagnosis of *where* and *why* NS iteration amplifies quantization error (top singular subspace, 40× vs 5×) provides a reusable analytical framework for future optimizer compression. Open questions include automatic rank selection, validation at larger pretraining scales (>1.1B), and efficient distributed communication of the mixed-precision optimizer state.
