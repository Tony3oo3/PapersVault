## Problem

While prior work established that fixed-precision transformers recognize exactly the star-free languages (the same class as LTL and counter-free automata), it remained unknown how much more compactly transformers can describe those languages relative to other formalisms. This paper fills that gap by proving that transformers are exponentially more succinct than LTL and RNNs, and doubly exponentially more succinct than finite automata, and that this succinctness makes basic verification problems (emptiness, equivalence) provably intractable.

## Input / Output

The primary object of study is a unique-hard attention transformer (UHAT) that takes a word over a finite alphabet Σ and produces an accept/reject decision based on whether the dot product of an acceptance vector with the final position's output vector is positive. The paper also constructs translations: a UHAT of size n yields an equivalent LTL formula of size at most exponential in n, and a B-RASP program (an intermediate formalism expressively equivalent to UHATs) of size polynomial in N encodes a 2^N-tiling instance whose shortest accepted word has length at least 2^(2^N). The emptiness and equivalence problems take one or two UHATs as input and produce a yes/no decision.

## Method

The key technical ingredient is showing that a polynomial-size UHAT can implement a doubly-exponentially large binary counter via attention: it recognizes encodings of correct tilings of a 2^N-tiling instance (constructed from a Turing machine with 2^n-bit tape), where the shortest accepted word has length at least 2^(2^N). This is achieved by reducing the EXPSPACE-complete 2^N-tiling problem to B-RASP non-emptiness (Lemma 8), then translating the resulting B-RASP program to a UHAT in polynomial time (Lemma 9), exploiting the fact that strict future masking with rightmost tie-breaking lets attention check both horizontal and vertical tile adjacency constraints. For the matching upper bound, the paper proves that all rational values arising in a UHAT computation have polynomially bounded bit-length (Proposition 12), making the set of possible layer outputs finite and enumerable in exponential time, which enables a layer-by-layer construction of an equivalent LTL formula of exponential size (Proposition 13). This improves the prior doubly-exponential UHAT-to-LTL translation and pins the succinctness gap: at most exponential universally, at least exponential on the witness family.

## Results

The paper proves that fixed-precision UHATs are exponentially more succinct than LTL and RNNs (Theorem 15, Corollary 18), and doubly exponentially more succinct than finite automata (Theorem 17): there exist polynomial-size UHATs whose smallest equivalent LTL formula or RNN is exponentially large, and whose smallest equivalent automaton is doubly exponentially large. Matching upper bounds are established: every UHAT translates to an LTL formula with at most exponential blow-up (Proposition 13), improving the prior doubly exponential translation of Yang et al. (2024). As a consequence, the non-emptiness and equivalence problems for UHATs are both EXPSPACE-complete (Theorems 4 and 19), meaning no algorithm can solve them in less than double-exponential time under standard complexity assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares UHATs against four baseline formalisms: LTL, finite automata, RNNs, and state-space models (SSMs). Against LTL, UHATs achieve an exponential succinctness advantage (polynomial-size UHATs vs. exponentially large LTL formulas), while the reverse direction (LTL to UHAT) incurs only polynomial blow-up, pinning the gap precisely. Against finite automata, the gap widens to doubly exponential: the witness languages require automata of size 2^{2^{Ω(n)}} for polynomial-size UHATs. Against RNNs (and by extension SSMs), the gap is exponential, since fixed-precision RNNs are equivalent to finite automata with 2^{kD} states (Proposition 1). These results substantially improve on Sälzer et al. (2025), who showed only NEXP-hardness and a singly exponential gap over automata, by establishing the full EXPSPACE-completeness and the doubly exponential gap over automata.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the UHAT/B-RASP formalism of Yang et al. (2024), which established that masked hard-attention transformers recognize exactly the star-free languages, and on Jerad et al. (2025), who connected UHAT expressivity bounds to fixed-precision softmax transformers. It departs from the prior expressivity-focused line of work by introducing succinctness—the size of the smallest recognizer—as the analytical lens, a classical notion from logic (Stockmeyer, 1974; Grohe and Schweikardt, 2004) previously applied to LTL vs. automata but not to neural architectures. The technical core leverages van Emde Boas's (1997) Turing-machine-to-tiling reduction, and the EXPSPACE-hardness result substantially improves on Sälzer et al. (2025), who established only NEXP-hardness for a more complex attention model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The doubly exponential succinctness gap between polynomial-size transformers and finite automata, together with the EXPSPACE-completeness of emptiness and equivalence, establishes that formal verification of transformers is fundamentally intractable under standard complexity assumptions—no algorithm can solve these problems in less than double-exponential time. This has direct implications for the growing field of transformer verification and explainable AI, where practitioners may have assumed that verification difficulty was merely a practical obstacle rather than a theoretical barrier. Open questions include extending the succinctness analysis to fixed-precision softmax and average-hard attention transformers, identifying transformer subclasses that cannot encode large counters and thus admit lower-complexity verification, and understanding whether succinct transformers are learnable in practice.
