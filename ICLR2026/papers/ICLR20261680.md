## Problem

Smooth RM+ variants like SPRM+ achieve an O(1/T) theoretical convergence rate for learning Nash equilibria, but they lose the parameter-free property by requiring careful tuning of the step size η, with performance varying up to tenfold across different η values. Existing parameter-free RM+ variants (RM+, PRM+) avoid this tuning burden but only guarantee O(1/√T) convergence. The paper addresses the open question of whether an RM+ variant can simultaneously be parameter-free and achieve O(1/T) convergence.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes a two-player zero-sum normal-form game (defined by its payoff matrix) and, at each iteration, evaluates the loss gradient once (single-call). The output is a *weighted* average strategy profile x̄^T = (Σ R^t x^t)/(Σ R^t), where the weights R^t are the adaptively increasing lower bounds on the 1-norm of accumulated regrets, rather than the uniform weights of a standard time average. Convergence is measured by the duality gap, which quantifies the distance to the Nash equilibrium set.

## Method

MI-SPRM+ introduces the Adaptive Regret Domain (ARD) mechanism: instead of fixing the decision space at R_{≥R}^{|A_i|} with a constant R (as in SPRM+), it uses a time-varying lower bound R^t that starts at 1 and increases by 1 at each iteration where the instantaneous regret fluctuation exceeds a Bregman-divergence threshold. This ensures R^t monotonically grows to a constant ≥ 1/C₀ (where C₀ is a game-dependent constant), which is precisely the condition needed for O(1/T) convergence in SPRM+. Because the 1/η factor in the Bregman divergence is absorbed by the quadratic regularizer's symmetry, the generated sequence of strategy profiles is invariant to the choice of η, restoring the parameter-free property. The algorithm retains the two-step predictive update structure of PRM+ (computing θ^t from the previous prediction and updating the prediction θ̂^{t+1} from the current feedback), but operates in the dynamically expanding non-negative orthant R_{≥R^t}^{|A_i|} rather than the fixed R_{≥R}^{|A_i|}.

## Results

On the 3×3 two-player zero-sum NFG from the SPRM+ paper, MI-SPRM+ achieves a duality gap of 2.6e-5 at 10⁵ iterations, outperforming SPRM+ (η=0.01) at 3.9e-4 (~15×), SPRM+ (η=1) at 2.1e-4 (~8×), and SPRM+ (η=0.1) at 4.0e-5 (~1.5×). On randomly generated two-player zero-sum NFGs (dimensions 10–100), MI-SPRM+ achieves an O(1/T) empirical convergence rate and reduces the duality gap by 92% (std=100), 74% (std=10), and ~37% (std=1) relative to the best baseline SPRM+. On eight EFG benchmarks (Kuhn Poker, Leduc Poker, Liar's Dice, Goofspiel), MI-SPCFR+ significantly outperforms SPCFR+ in all games and PCFR+ in 7 of 8 games. Runtime overhead is modest: MI-SPRM+ is ~1.5× slower than SPRM+ per iteration (e.g., 0.24 min vs 0.16 min at dim=100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MI-SPRM+ outperforms all six baselines (RM⁺, PRM⁺, SPRM⁺, OGDA, OMWU, DS-OptMD) on two-player zero-sum NFGs in terms of duality gap, achieving O(1/T) convergence while RM⁺ and PRM⁺ only achieve O(1/√T). Against SPRM⁺ (the closest competitor with the same O(1/T) theoretical rate), MI-SPRM+ eliminates the need for step-size tuning entirely—SPRM⁺'s performance varies by up to 10× depending on η—while also converging faster. DS-OptMD, despite its O(1/T) theoretical guarantee, empirically converges significantly slower than O(1/T) due to its conservative adaptive step-size reduction, and is also ~2.5× slower in runtime. OGDA and OMWU are highly sensitive to step size, converging only with very small η (0.001) in high-variance games (std=100) and only for dimensions <10. On EFGs, MI-SPCFR+ beats SPCFR+ in all 8 games and PCFR+ in 7 of 8 (losing only on Leduc Poker), confirming the advantage transfers to the CFR framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends SPRM+ (Farina et al., 2023), which achieves an O(1/T) convergence rate for Nash equilibrium learning but sacrifices the parameter-free property by requiring a fixed step size η and a fixed lower bound R on the 1-norm of accumulated regrets. The key conceptual departure is the Adaptive Regret Domain (ARD) mechanism, which adaptively increases R at each iteration based on observed regret fluctuations, rather than the alternative approach of adaptively reducing η as in DS-OptMD (Hsieh et al., 2021). The predictive two-step update structure is inherited from PRM+ (Farina et al., 2021), and the overall framework sits within the RM+ lineage (Tammelin, 2014) that updates in the non-negative orthant rather than the original strategy simplex.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first parameter-free RM variant with an O(1/T) theoretical convergence rate, eliminating the need for hyperparameter tuning that is a major practical obstacle in game-solving (the paper demonstrates up to 10× performance variation in SPRM+ across step sizes). Open questions include: (i) the O(1/T) rate does not extend to multi-player general-sum NFGs (only an O(1) social regret bound is established), (ii) the O(1/T) guarantee does not formally hold for EFGs despite strong empirical performance, and (iii) the ARD mechanism adds ~50% runtime overhead over SPRM+. The finding that DS-OptMD's theoretical O(1/T) guarantee is not empirically realized, while MI-SPRM+ achieves it in practice, highlights a gap between theory and practice in adaptive optimization.
