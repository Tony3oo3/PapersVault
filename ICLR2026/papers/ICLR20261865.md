## Problem

Existing CZSL methods represent each primitive (state or object) with a single shared prototype, ignoring the fact that the same primitive carries substantially different semantics in different compositional contexts (e.g., 'young' in 'young dog' vs. 'young city'). This one-to-all cross-modal matching disrupts the topological structure of the multimodal space and limits fine-grained image-composition alignment, particularly in open-world settings where primitives combine in unseen ways.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and outputs a state-object composition label (e.g., 'eroded clay'). It operates on frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoders, with learnable soft prompts for compositions ('a photo of [state] [object]') and primitives ('a photo of [state]', 'a photo of [object]'). At inference, the final prediction combines composition scores with state and object probabilities weighted by a fixed coefficient β=0.5, and in open-world evaluation, post-training calibration filters infeasible compositions from the full Cartesian product space.

## Method

EVA introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, domain-expert adaption inserts LoRA-based Mixture-of-Experts adapters in parallel to the FFN of every layer in both the image and text encoders; a router dynamically assigns each token to a shared expert (capturing common knowledge) plus K=2 routed experts (capturing domain-specific knowledge), enabling token-aware primitive representation learning without modifying the frozen backbone. Second, semantic variant alignment performs global-to-local cross-modal matching from both views: in the text domain, it selects the highest-scoring composition within each state/object set as the primitive matching score; in the image domain, it treats CLS tokens from all experts as feature variants and selects the most relevant one via a combined inter-model affinity (similarity to primitive text features) and intra-model affinity (similarity to the global image feature), thereby decoupling primitive semantics from compositional context.

## Results

EVA achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three CZSL benchmarks in both closed- and open-world settings. In closed-world evaluation, EVA attains AUC of 24.0, 50.2, and 18.8 on MIT-States, UT-Zappos, and C-GQA respectively, with corresponding HM scores of 41.0, 60.2, and 36.9. In the more challenging open-world setting, EVA achieves AUC gains of +0.1%, +0.7%, and +2.6% over the previous best (CLUSPRO) on MIT-States, UT-Zappos, and C-GQA, with a notable Unseen accuracy of 13.3% on C-GQA (vs. 8.3% for CLUSPRO). The method also demonstrates superior efficiency, with 12.0 ms inference time vs. 28.4 ms for RAPR while using fewer trainable parameters (36.7M vs. 37.9M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVA outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every dataset and both evaluation settings. In closed-world, the largest margins are on UT-Zappos where EVA's AUC of 50.2 surpasses CLUSPRO (46.6) by +3.6 and LOGICZSL (45.8) by +4.4, and on C-GQA where AUC of 18.8 exceeds LOGICZSL (15.3) by +3.5 and RAPR (14.4) by +4.4. In open-world, EVA leads CLUSPRO by +0.7 AUC on UT-Zappos (40.2 vs. 39.5) and by a substantial +2.6 AUC on C-GQA (5.6 vs. 3.0), while also achieving the highest Unseen accuracy on C-GQA (13.3% vs. 8.3% for CLUSPRO). Earlier prompt-based methods (CSP, DFSP, GIPCOL) trail significantly, with AUC gaps exceeding 10 points on UT-Zappos in closed-world. The efficiency comparison (Table 8) shows EVA matches RAPR's training time (3 min 30 s vs. 3 min 32 s) while reducing inference time from 28.4 ms to 12.0 ms and using fewer parameters (36.7M vs. 37.9M), all while improving AUC from 44.5 to 50.2 on UT-Zappos.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVA builds on the CLIP-based CZSL lineage (CSP, Troika, GIPCOL, RAPR, CLUSPRO, LOGICZSL) that learns composition-level alignment via contrastive or classification objectives, but departs fundamentally from the single-prototype primitive representation that all prior methods share. The key conceptual departure is importing the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm from NLP (Mixtral, DeepSeek-V3) into CZSL primitive learning, using LoRA-based MoE adapters at every encoder layer to produce context-dependent semantic variants per primitive rather than one fixed embedding. This contrasts with prior suffix-module approaches (PLID, Troika) that append a single expert to the encoder output, and with graph-based or prompt-based methods (GIPCOL, CSP) that model inter-primitive relations without addressing intra-primitive polysemy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that primitive polysemy—where the same state or object carries different visual semantics across compositions—is a first-order bottleneck in CZSL, and that dynamic expert routing can address it more effectively than uniform capacity increases or single-prototype alignment. The finding that semantic isolation (separate MoE stacks for states vs. objects) and load-balancing losses both degrade performance is a useful negative result: it suggests that cross-domain token mixing and natural routing imbalance are beneficial for compositional generalization. Open questions remain around abstract or highly ambiguous primitives, the scalability of expert count to very large composition spaces, and whether the MoE-based primitive decomposition transfers to other zero-shot recognition settings beyond CZSL.
