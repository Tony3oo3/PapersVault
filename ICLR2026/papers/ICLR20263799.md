## Problem

Existing AIGC detection methods rely on low-level forensic cues (spectral artifacts, texture statistics, up-convolution patterns) that are imperceptible to humans and fail to capture content-level semantic anomalies such as physical law violations, anatomical implausibilities, and commonsense inconsistencies. No standardized benchmark or structured annotation protocol exists for detecting and explaining these semantic-level anomalies in AI-generated images, leaving a gap between what humans perceive as 'wrong' in a synthetic scene and what current VLM-based detectors can identify and justify.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single photorealistic AI-generated RGB image as input and produces a set of structured anomaly quadruples, each containing a concise Name, a detailed Phenomenon description, a commonsense-grounded Reasoning chain, and a continuous Severity Score in [0, 100] (0 = fully implausible, 100 = fully realistic). For the explainable deepfake detection variant, the model first outputs a binary real/AI classification, then conditionally produces the structured anomaly list only when the image is predicted as AI-generated; for real images it outputs a neutral scene description instead. Evaluation uses BERTScore-based one-to-one semantic matching at thresholds τ ∈ {0.7, 0.8, 0.9} to compute SemAP and SemF1 across Phenomenon, Reasoning, and combined views.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part system: AnomAgent, a modular multi-agent annotation pipeline, and AnomReason, the resulting 21,539-image benchmark with ~127K human-verified structured anomalies. AnomAgent decomposes anomaly detection into three stages—(1) ObjectPerceiver extracts entities via T repeated prompt-varied detections, (2) AttributeAnalyzer and RelationReasoner perform intra-object and inter-object consistency checks in a two-step identify-then-verify protocol, and (3) AnomalyIntegrator and AnomalyFormatter consolidate and structure candidates into the final quadruple format. A lightweight human-in-the-loop stage (single ACCEPT/REJECT/UNSURE question per candidate) filters hallucinations, reducing average annotations from 8 to 5.9 per image. For detection, Qwen2.5-VL-7B is fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 8, α=16, dropout 0.5, every 4th layer, frozen ViT) on the AnomReason train split to produce AnomReasonor-7B, which achieves SemAP_Full = 0.5162, surpassing all open-source baselines and GPT-4o on detection while approaching it on alignment quality. Novel classification-aware metrics (CSemAP, CSemF1) gate explanation scoring on correct binary classification to prevent post-hoc rationalization.

## Results

On the AnomReason-Test benchmark, the proposed AnomReasonor-7B (Qwen2.5-VL-7B fine-tuned via LoRA) achieves a new state-of-the-art SemAP_Full of 0.5162 and SemAP_Rea of 0.5130, surpassing GPT-4o (SemAP_Full = 0.4727) on all SemAP metrics. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B is the strongest baseline (SemAP_Full = 0.4568), but AnomReasonor-7B outperforms it by over 6 points. On alignment quality (SemF1), GPT-4o retains a narrow edge (SemF1_Full = 0.5109 vs. 0.5009), while AnomReasonor-7B leads on reasoning-specific F1 (SemF1_Rea = 0.4977 vs. 0.4930). Most off-the-shelf VLMs score below 0.42 on SemAP_Full, indicating limited semantic anomaly competence without targeted supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnomReasonor-7B outperforms GPT-4o on all three SemAP metrics (0.5221/0.5130/0.5162 vs. 0.4908/0.4562/0.4727 for Phe/Rea/Full), with the largest gap in reasoning (SemAP_Rea: +5.7 points), while GPT-4o retains a marginal SemF1_Full advantage (0.5109 vs. 0.5009). Against the strongest open-source baseline Qwen2.5-VL-72B, AnomReasonor-7B leads by 5.9 points on SemAP_Full (0.5162 vs. 0.4568) and 9.1 points on SemF1_Full (0.5009 vs. 0.4104). The model also decisively surpasses GPT-5 (SemAP_Full: 0.5162 vs. 0.3469) and GPT-o3 (0.5162 vs. 0.4058), suggesting that task-specific structured supervision on AnomReason is more effective than general-purpose reasoning for semantic anomaly detection. Among open-source 7–9B models, InternVL3-8B shows the strongest observation capability (SemAP_Phe = 0.4552) but a large observation-reasoning gap (0.4552 vs. 0.3676), whereas AnomReasonor-7B exhibits the most balanced profile with reasoning nearly matching observation. Gemini-2.5-pro underperforms expectations (SemAP_Full = 0.3384), trailing even several 7B open-source models, highlighting that proprietary scale alone does not guarantee semantic anomaly competence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of AIGC forensics that relies on low-level pixel/spectral artifacts (up-convolution fingerprints, frequency-domain signatures) and from recent VLM-based forgery detectors that explain predictions via surface-level cues like lighting inconsistencies or texture statistics. It repositions the problem at the content-semantic level—commonsense, physics, and anatomical violations—and formalizes a structured quadruple output (Name, Phenomenon, Reasoning, Severity) that prior benchmarks (FakeClue, Ivy-Fake) do not provide. Methodologically, it builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B as a fine-tunable backbone and introduces a multi-agent annotation pipeline (AnomAgent) that decomposes anomaly reasoning into object perception, attribute analysis, relational reasoning, and consolidation, drawing on the broader multi-agent LLM paradigm but applying it specifically to structured visual anomaly annotation at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnomReason is the first large-scale benchmark targeting content-level semantic anomalies in AIGC images with interpretable, severity-scored annotations, filling a gap between pixel-level forensics and high-level authenticity classification. The structured output format and SemAP/SemF1 metrics provide a reusable evaluation protocol for any model claiming to reason about visual plausibility, and the generator-auditing application (Table 3) offers a practical diagnostic for text-to-image model developers. Key limitations include the moderate dataset size (~21K images), reliance on GPT-4o for annotation (introducing potential model-specific biases), and restriction to still images; extending to video, reducing annotation cost, and validating whether semantic signals complement or subsume low-level forensic cues remain open.
