## Problem

Flow-based generative models (diffusion, flow matching) require hundreds to thousands of neural function evaluations to compute a single log-likelihood, making likelihood-based applications like model comparison, RL fine-tuning, and preference optimization prohibitively expensive. While recent few-step distillation methods (shortcut models, MeanFlow) have solved the sampling speed bottleneck, they either abandon likelihood computation entirely or still require full-trajectory integration, leaving fast likelihood evaluation as an unsolved problem.

## Input / Output

For sampling, the model takes Gaussian noise x_0 ~ N(0,I) and produces a generated image in 1–8 Euler steps; for likelihood evaluation, it takes a data or generated image x_1 and produces a calibrated log-likelihood (in BPD) by integrating backward in 1–8 steps. Experiments use 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10) and 64×64 RGB images (ImageNet-64, CelebA-64). The architecture is a U-Net backbone with two output heads: a standard velocity head for sampling and a small scalar MLP head (2 FC layers with SiLU) for divergence prediction, sharing all backbone parameters.

## Method

F2D2's key insight is that in continuous normalizing flows, the sampling ODE and the log-density evolution ODE are coupled through the same learned velocity field, so both can be jointly distilled into a single flow map Φ_Y that predicts the average velocity u_θ(x,t,s) and average divergence D_θ(x,t,s) over any interval [t,s]. The joint flow map is trained via self-distillation objectives that enforce flow-map validity conditions (semigroup property for Shortcut-F2D2, Eulerian equation for MeanFlow-F2D2) on both the sampling and divergence components simultaneously, using a shared U-Net backbone with separate prediction heads. Divergence supervision is obtained via the Hutchinson trace estimator at O(1) cost per step, and training follows a staged protocol: pre-train the velocity component with existing flow-map distillation, then add the divergence head and jointly fine-tune both components. As an application, the paper introduces maximum likelihood self-guidance, which uses the one-step divergence prediction to optimize the initial noise via a single Adam step before sampling, enabling a 2-step MeanFlow-F2D2 model to outperform a 1024-step flow matching model on CIFAR-10.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, MeanFlow-F2D2 achieves the best combined performance: FID 3.78 with calibrated NLL of 2.38 BPD at 8 steps, and FID 2.59 with NLL 1.63 BPD at 2 steps, compared to the 1024-step flow matching teacher (3.12 BPD, FID 2.60 at 200 steps). Without F2D2, all few-step baselines (Flow Matching, Shortcut, MeanFlow) produce invalid NLL estimates (e.g., −9.93 to −124 BPD at 8 steps) that are orders of magnitude off from the teacher. On ImageNet 64×64, Shortcut-Distill-F2D2 achieves NLL 3.51 BPD (vs. teacher 3.34) with FID 21.91 at 8 steps, while the non-F2D2 Shortcut-Distill baseline yields invalid NLL (−9.03). As a downstream application, 2-step MeanFlow-F2D2 with maximum likelihood self-guidance achieves lower FID than the 1024-step flow matching model of the same size on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Flow Matching, all F2D2 variants produce calibrated NLL (within ~1 BPD of the 3.12 teacher) while Flow Matching yields wildly invalid values (−9.93 to −111 BPD) at 8–1 steps; FID also improves dramatically (e.g., MeanFlow-F2D2: 3.78 vs. 20.63 at 8 steps). Against the Shortcut Model, Shortcut-F2D2 and Shortcut-Distill-F2D2 fix the invalid NLL (from −12.07/−11.42 to 3.07/3.12 BPD) at a modest FID cost (8.78/5.68 vs. 7.10/5.01), while Shortcut-Distill-F2D2 at 8 steps matches the teacher's NLL exactly (3.12 BPD). Against MeanFlow, MeanFlow-F2D2 simultaneously improves FID (3.78 vs. 4.34 at 8 steps; 2.59 vs. 2.84 at 2 steps) and adds calibrated likelihood (2.38 BPD vs. invalid −9.00), demonstrating that the divergence loss acts as a complementary training signal. On CelebA-64, LSD-F2D2 outperforms LSD in both FID (2.41 vs. 3.33 at 8 steps) and NLL calibration (1.64 vs. −6.78 BPD, with the teacher at 1.75 BPD). Per-sample NLL error (Table 8) confirms F2D2 reduces mean absolute error by 7–171× over baselines, e.g., Shortcut-Distill-F2D2 achieves 0.41 BPD error at 8 steps vs. 14.47 for Shortcut-Distill.

## Relation to Prior Work

F2D2 extends the flow map distillation framework (Boffi et al., 2025a) and its instantiations—shortcut models (Frans et al., 2024) via the semigroup property and MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2025) via the Eulerian equation—to the joint ODE system that couples sampling trajectories with log-density evolution. While prior few-step methods distill only the sampling flow and thereby lose likelihood tractability, and the few that preserve likelihood (e.g., consistency trajectory models) still require full-trajectory integration, F2D2 introduces a shared-backbone architecture with an additional scalar divergence head that jointly distills both components. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that the divergence term is a derived output of the same velocity field, making it amenable to the same flow map parametrization and self-distillation objectives already used for sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

F2D2 solves a long-standing computational bottleneck by reducing likelihood evaluation from 100–1000+ NFEs to fewer than 10, unlocking likelihood-based fine-tuning objectives (PPO, DPO, GRPO), model comparison, and inference-time optimization for flow-based generative models. The maximum likelihood self-guidance application—where a 2-step MeanFlow outperforms a 1024-step flow matching model on CIFAR-10—demonstrates the expanded algorithmic space enabled by cheap likelihood access. However, current results are limited to unconditional generation on small-to-medium datasets (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-64, CelebA-64), the ImageNet experiments use a reduced model size, and the method is sensitive to divergence target scaling and requires careful early stopping. Open questions include whether the approach scales to conditional generation, larger architectures, and other modalities (video, audio), and whether the first-order backward-integration approximation for forward-only models remains adequate at larger step sizes.
