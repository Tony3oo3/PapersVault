## Problem

Competitive debate requires LLMs to make strategic choices under strict time limits and evaluate persuasiveness through dynamic back-and-forth interaction rather than a single final state. Existing multi-agent debate systems like Agent4Debate lack mechanisms for anticipating opponent responses, tracking which arguments remain standing, and allocating limited speaking time to the most impactful actions, resulting in less persuasive and less strategically diverse statements compared to human experts.

## Input / Output

The system receives a debate motion, an assigned Pro/Con stance, the opponent's prior statements, and hard stage time limits (4 min opening, 4 min rebuttal, 2 min closing). It produces a coherent debate statement as text and audio (via TTS) that must fit within the precise time window, along with a strategic action plan specifying which claims to propose, reinforce, attack, or rebut. A key constraint is that word count alone cannot guarantee speech duration, so the system iteratively adjusts via a FastSpeech-based speech time controller using binary search over word budgets until the audio falls within [t_l, t_r].

## Method

TreeDebater introduces two complementary tree structures: a Rehearsal Tree built top-down before the debate to anticipate opponent attacks and defenses (scoring each node with a minimax-like k-step strength score that assumes the opponent always plays the counterargument minimizing our utility), and a Debate Flow Tree maintained during the debate to track claim states (proposed/attacked) and extract candidate actions. Two LLaMA-3.2-3B reward models trained on the Kialo dataset provide support and attack impact scores for the Rehearsal Tree. During each stage, the system retrieves prepared arguments from the Rehearsal Tree via embedding similarity (Gemini-text-embedding-4, threshold 0.8), drafts a statement informed by the Debate Flow Tree's action candidates and strength scores, then revises using simulated audience feedback grounded in retrieved human debate flow trees from PanelBench. A binary-search speech time controller with FastSpeech iteratively adjusts the word budget to meet the precise time constraint.

## Results

In stage-level head-to-head human evaluation, TreeDebater achieves an average persuasiveness score of 3.69 (Gemini) and 4.01 (DeepSeek) versus 3.54 and 3.47 for Agent4Debate, winning 11 out of 12 stage-level comparisons. In end-to-end Oxford-style debate, TreeDebater achieves an opinion shift win rate of 46% with Gemini (vs. 13% for Agent4Debate, a 3.5× improvement) and 40% with DeepSeek (vs. 30%, a 1.3× improvement). TreeDebater also achieves 100% format and time validity across all stages, while Agent4Debate with Gemini drops to 77% format validity and as low as 13.5% time validity in the closing stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Agent4Debate (Zhang et al., 2024), a multi-agent debate framework with searcher, analyzer, writer, and reviewer agents. TreeDebater consistently outperforms Agent4Debate on both evaluation axes: in stage-level head-to-head comparison, TreeDebater is preferred 1.5× (Gemini) and 2.5× (DeepSeek) over the baseline, with statistically significant wins in Opening Pro (p=0.002), Rebuttal Pro (p=0.019), and Closing Con (p=0.006). In end-to-end debate, TreeDebater achieves a 3.5× higher opinion shift win rate with Gemini (46% vs. 13%) and a 1.3× higher rate with DeepSeek (40% vs. 30%). The advantage is more pronounced with the DeepSeek backbone, where TreeDebater's average persuasiveness jumps from 3.47 to 4.01. Agent4Debate also suffers from practical issues: it frequently exceeds time limits (especially in the 2-minute closing stage, where only 5.8–13.5% of statements are time-valid) and occasionally produces format-invalid outputs (77% format validity with Gemini), whereas TreeDebater achieves 100% validity in both dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-agent debate framework of Agent4Debate (Zhang et al., 2024) by introducing explicit strategic planning via two tree structures: a Rehearsal Tree (top-down argument anticipation with minimax-like strength scoring) and a Debate Flow Tree (bottom-up tracking of debate state). It departs from Project Debater's template-based debate construction and from LLM debate-for-reasoning work (which assumes an optimal solution) by treating competitive debate as a zero-sum, time-constrained planning problem with no rule-based winning condition. The minimax-inspired strength scoring borrows conceptually from game-tree search (AlphaGo, Werewolf agents), but adapts it to the soft, audience-judged evaluation of debate rather than a hard win/lose signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that strategic action selection (which points to attack, defend, or reinforce under time pressure) is a distinct and critical bottleneck for LLM debaters, separate from argument generation quality. The speech time controller using binary search over TTS-estimated duration is a practical technique likely transferable to other time-constrained spoken generation tasks. Limitations include the narrow Oxford-style format, modest reward model accuracy (0.67–0.72), moderate annotator agreement (60.7%), and a small motion set (13), leaving open whether tree-based planning generalizes to longer, multi-round, or non-Oxford debate formats and whether the minimax zero-sum assumption holds when audience persuasion is inherently non-zero-sum.
