## Problem

Universal Test-Time Adaptation (UTTA) must handle simultaneous domain and class shifts in unsupervised streaming settings, but existing methods rely on pseudo-labels that become unreliable under dual shifts, and traditional active learning strategies (entropy-based) select samples biased toward class-shift regions rather than the critical mixed regions where confusion is highest. This paper introduces Active UTTA (AUTTA), which incorporates a small budget of human annotations during testing to resolve the dual-shift confusion that purely unsupervised methods cannot address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262595/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled RGB target-domain images in mini-batches, with the model initialized from a source-domain pretrained backbone (no source data at test time). During the streaming process, a limited annotation budget (e.g., 800–1500 samples) is spent by querying an oracle for ground-truth labels on selected samples, which are accumulated into a growing labeled set. For each test image, the model outputs either a source-class label (old classes) or a rejection decision (new/target-private classes), evaluated via A_O, A_N, and their harmonic mean A_H.

## Method

EMAC operates in three stages. First, it decomposes the classifier weight matrix via SVD into source-known and source-unknown subspaces, projects target features onto these subspaces, and fits a bimodal GMM on the unknown-feature norm to expose a mixed region (between the two component means) where dual-shift confusion is highest. Second, a reward-guided selection strategy computes a Max-Min Entropy objective that quantifies marginal information gain—entropy reduction for old classes and entropy increase for new classes—with EMA smoothing, then selects the highest-entropy old-class or lowest-entropy new-class samples from the mixed candidates depending on which reward is larger. Third, a clustering-based contrastive loss computes class prototypes from both annotated and high-confidence pseudo-labeled features, then applies a contrastive objective over augmented feature triplets (original, augmented, prototype) to enforce intra-class cohesion and inter-class separation, preventing decision-boundary ambiguity under label scarcity.

## Results

On DomainNet (6 domain shifts, 345 classes), EMAC achieves an average harmonic mean accuracy (A_H) of 52.2%, outperforming the best UTTA baseline OPTTT (49.8%) by 2.4 points and the best ATTA baseline EATTA (47.7%) by 4.5 points. On VisDA-C (12 classes, style transfer), EMAC reaches 76.5% A_H, surpassing OPTTT (74.1%) and SimATTA (67.9%). The reinforced variant EMAC* further improves to 53.1% on DomainNet and 77.3% on VisDA-C. Runtime is 735s on DomainNet, only ~5% slower than the best-performing UTTA baseline OPTTT (617s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMAC outperforms all 12 baselines across both DomainNet and VisDA-C. Against the strongest UTTA method OPTTT, EMAC gains +2.4 A_H on DomainNet (52.2 vs. 49.8) and +2.4 on VisDA-C (76.5 vs. 74.1), with particularly large A_O improvements (41.7 vs. 38.9 on DomainNet). Among ATTA methods, EMAC surpasses EATTA by +4.5 A_H on DomainNet and +9.0 on VisDA-C, and beats SimATTA by +5.1 and +8.6 respectively, demonstrating that reward-guided selection from GMM-exposed mixed regions is substantially more effective than confidence-driven or entropy-based querying. Against traditional TTA baselines (TENT, SHOT, BN), EMAC leads by 6–10 A_H points, confirming that active annotation is critical under dual shifts. In the active-learning comparison (Table 3), EMAC with B=1000 achieves 52.2 A_H on DomainNet, exceeding the best AL baseline BADGE (49.5) by 2.7 points and Coreset (49.4) by 2.8, while using fewer annotations than most baselines (B=800 already yields 50.8). On VisDA-C, EMAC (76.5) leads BADGE (68.2) by 8.3 points, highlighting the advantage of dual-shift-aware selection over generic diversity/uncertainty criteria.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two parallel lines of work: Active Test-Time Adaptation (ATTA, Gui et al. 2024), which uses active learning to handle domain shifts but ignores class shifts, and Universal Test-Time Adaptation (UTTA/OPTTT, Li et al. 2023), which handles dual shifts but is entirely unsupervised. By combining these, the authors introduce the AUTTA protocol and instantiate it with EMAC, which uses SVD-based orthogonal decoupling of the classifier followed by GMM fitting to expose 'mixed regions' where dual shifts co-occur, then applies a reward-guided (Max-Min Entropy) selection strategy rather than standard entropy-based active learning. The clustering-contrastive loss for balancing scarce annotations with pseudo-labels draws on contrastive learning but is specifically designed for the open-set, label-scarce test-time setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-shift scenario (simultaneous domain and class shift) is a realistic deployment challenge where pseudo-labeling degrades, and the paper's core insight—that standard entropy-based active learning selects the wrong samples under dual shifts, wasting annotation budget on high-class-shift regions rather than the truly ambiguous mixed regions—is practically useful. However, the method still depends on human annotation, which the authors acknowledge becomes a bottleneck in high-stakes or fine-grained settings. Open questions include whether the GMM bimodality assumption holds reliably in streaming, non-stationary environments with very small batches, and whether the annotation oracle can be replaced by self-supervised or model-based signals to remove the human-in-the-loop dependency entirely.
