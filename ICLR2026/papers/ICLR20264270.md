## Problem

Existing LLM safety benchmarks are limited in three critical ways: they predominantly evaluate single-turn dialogues rather than realistic multi-turn interactions, they rely on a single jailbreak attack strategy while ignoring the diversity of real-world adversarial tactics, and they assess only surface-level content censorship (e.g., aggressive language) without examining whether models can identify, handle, and remain consistent under sustained multi-turn pressure across dimensions like legality, ethics, fairness, and privacy.

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds 4,053 bilingual (Chinese/English) multi-turn dialogues (3–10 turns each) spanning 22 scenarios and 7 jailbreak attack types into 19 evaluated LLMs, which generate responses turn-by-turn using golden context as dialogue history. Evaluation produces per-turn scores on a 1–10 scale across three abilities (identification, handling, consistency) for each of six safety dimensions, aggregated via a minimum-score-taking metric where the lowest turn score determines the final dialogue score. A score below 7 on any turn marks the dialogue as successfully attacked, yielding the Attack Success Rate (ASR).

## Method

SafeDialBench introduces a two-tier hierarchical safety taxonomy decomposing six dimensions (Fairness, Legality, Morality, Aggression, Ethics, Privacy) into 20+ specific safety points, and deploys seven distinct jailbreak strategies (Scene Construct, Purpose Reverse, Role Play, Topic Change, Reference Attack, Fallacy Attack, Probing Question) to construct adversarial dialogues. Data generation is human-in-the-loop: annotators craft the initial query and iteratively steer multi-turn conversations with three different assistant LLMs (GPT-4, Doubao, ChatGLM) to mitigate single-model bias, followed by two rounds of expert review. The evaluation framework uses LLM-as-judge (GPT-3.5-turbo and Qwen-72B) with dimension-specific prompts scoring each turn on identification, handling, and consistency, validated against 5 human experts who achieve >80% agreement with the automated evaluator. The minimum-score aggregation reflects the practical principle that a single compromised response in an interconnected dialogue undermines overall safety.

## Results

Across 19 LLMs evaluated on SafeDialBench (4,053 multi-turn dialogues, 6 safety dimensions, 3 safety abilities), Yi-34B-Chat achieves the highest open-source scores (e.g., 8.33 in Morality-Identify, 8.23 in Privacy-Identify), while ChatGPT-4o attains the lowest Attack Success Rate (ASR). Baichuan2-7B-Chat exhibits the highest ASR at 69.60%, and o3-mini shows the weakest safety among reasoning models (e.g., 6.83 in Privacy-Identify). GPT-3.5-turbo as an automated evaluator achieves >80% agreement with human experts across all three safety abilities (Identify: 82%, Handle: 81%, Consistency: 81%). Among 7 jailbreak attack methods, fallacy attack and purpose reverse are the most effective at compromising model safety.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This benchmark paper evaluates 19 LLMs (4 closed-source, 15 open-source, including 3 reasoning models) rather than comparing a single method against baselines. Among open-source models, Yi-34B-Chat leads across most dimensions (e.g., 8.33 Morality-Identify, 7.65 Privacy-Identify), outperforming GLM4-9B-Chat (strongest in Ethics at 7.50 Identify and Legality at 8.29 Identify) by 0.1–0.3 points on average. Among closed-source models, ChatGPT-4o achieves the lowest ASR, while MoonShot-v1 excels in Fairness (7.38 Identify, 8.12 Handle) but lags in Ethics (7.07 Identify). The reasoning model o3-mini underperforms both DeepSeek-R1 and ChatGPT-4o across nearly all dimensions (e.g., 6.83 vs. 6.95 Privacy-Identify), and Baichuan2-7B-Chat has the highest ASR (69.60%) among all evaluated models. The Qwen2.5 series (7B/14B-Instruct) shows notable weaknesses in identifying Aggression (6.64/6.75) and Legality (6.95/6.89), trailing the original Qwen-Chat series by ~0.2–0.3 points in these dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by synthesizing ideas from multiple prior lines of work: jailbreak attack taxonomies from red-teaming literature (reference attacks, purpose reversal, fallacy-based attacks), safety dimension frameworks from single-turn benchmarks (SafetyBench, SALAD-Bench), and LLM-as-judge evaluation protocols from MT-Bench/Chatbot Arena. It departs from prior multi-turn safety benchmarks (CoSafe, RED QUEEN, SC-Safety) in three key ways: extending dialogue length to 3–10 turns, deploying 7 distinct attack strategies simultaneously rather than 1–2, and introducing a fine-grained three-ability evaluation (identification, handling, consistency) rather than a binary safe/unsafe judgment. The human-in-the-loop, multi-LLM data generation approach also distinguishes it from template-based or single-model generation used in prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SafeDialBench provides the first bilingual, multi-attack, multi-dimension benchmark for multi-turn LLM safety, filling a gap where prior work either focused on single-turn settings, covered only aggression, or used a single attack method. The finding that reasoning models like o3-mini exhibit safety vulnerabilities comparable to or worse than smaller non-reasoning models challenges the assumption that chain-of-thought reasoning inherently improves safety alignment. Open questions include whether the 7 attack methods capture the full adversarial landscape, how to design stateful defenses that track cumulative risk across turns, and whether the minimum-score aggregation metric adequately reflects real-world harm from a single compromised turn in an otherwise safe dialogue.
