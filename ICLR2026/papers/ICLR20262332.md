## Problem

In RL for LLMs, the actor (inference) and policy (training) distributions frequently diverge due to large-batch rollouts, asynchronous updates, or using a different model for generation, causing training instability and collapse. Existing truncated importance sampling (TIS) corrections face an inherent trade-off: conservative thresholds underperform, while aggressive thresholds crash before the policy plateaus. The paper asks whether one can directly modify the actor's sampling distribution to provably reduce its gap to the policy, rather than merely reweighting samples after the fact.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard LLM rollout trajectories along with top-K log-probabilities (K=20) from both the inference model and the current training model, plus reward and advantage estimates from the standard PPO/GRPO pipeline. It produces per-token acceptance/rejection masks (rejected tokens are zeroed out of the loss), a reweighted PPO objective, and updated policy parameters. Notably, no additional trajectories, forward passes, or vLLM modifications are required; the only extra data is the top-K logprobs returned during the standard rollout, adding less than 3% overhead.

## Method

JACKPOT applies Optimal Budgeted Rejection Sampling (OBRS) to directly reshape the actor distribution toward the policy: each token sampled from p_inf is accepted with probability min(1, p_target/(λ·p_inf)), and rejected tokens are masked from the loss. To handle the intractable full-vocabulary normalization constant Z, the method approximates it via the union of top-K tokens under both distributions and corrects the resulting underestimation bias using a batch-level calibration factor κ derived from the empirical acceptance rate. The final JACKPOT-PPO loss multiplies the standard PPO clipped objective by a composite weight: min(Z·max(λ, p_new/p_inf), C₁) · min(p_ref/p_new, C₂), which jointly accounts for the OBRS reweighting, the importance sampling correction, and the PPO trust-region constraint. The paper proves that for any λ>0, the post-rejection distribution is strictly closer to the target in KL divergence than the original proposal, and that the OBRS rule is the unique optimal acceptance mechanism for a fixed acceptance budget.

## Results

Under the extreme 128× actor-policy update ratio (rollout batch 4096, train batch 32) for Qwen3-4B-Base, Jackpot achieves 92.00 GSM8K, 80.00 MATH-500, and 51.20 AMC22&23 (Mean@4), while TIS+Adjustment collapses to 20.70/19.10/7.80 respectively. At the 64× ratio for Qwen3-8B-Base, Jackpot reaches 63.55 AMC22&23 and 54.44 AMC12 2024 (Mean@4), matching the on-policy baseline on AMC12 2024 and exceeding it on AMC22&23 (63.55 vs. 28.95 on-policy, though on-policy was evaluated at a different checkpoint). In the extreme off-policy setting (Qwen2.5-1.5B-IT rollout → Qwen2.5-3B-Base training), Jackpot sustains 13.4/31.3 Mean@k/Pass@k on AMC22&23 at step 70, whereas Vanilla GRPO collapses to 5.7/16.9 and TIS to 0/0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TIS (truncated importance sampling from IMPALA, Espeholt et al. 2018), Jackpot consistently outperforms across all settings. At the 64× ratio for Qwen3-4B-Base, Jackpot scores 92.24 vs. 89.67 on GSM8K and 53.92 vs. 42.77 on AMC22&23; at the 128× ratio, TIS+Adjustment catastrophically fails (20.70 GSM8K, 7.80 AMC22&23) while Jackpot maintains near-on-policy performance (92.00, 51.20). In the extreme off-policy setting (Table 2, Qwen2.5-1.5B-IT rollout → Qwen2.5-3B-Base training), TIS collapses to 0/0 on AMC22&23 by step 60, while Jackpot sustains 13.4/31.3 at step 70. Against Vanilla GRPO (Shao et al. 2024), the gap is even starker in the extreme off-policy regime: GRPO degrades from 33.3/59.0 at step 40 to 5.7/16.9 at step 70, whereas Jackpot holds 31.9/62.7 at step 50 and 13.4/31.3 at step 70, representing a 12% improvement on MATH-500 over the GRPO baseline under the same misaligned conditions. Jackpot also matches or exceeds the on-policy baseline on several benchmarks (e.g., 54.44 AMC12 2024 for Qwen3-8B-Base, equal to on-policy) while operating at 64× the staleness ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

JACKPOT departs from the dominant line of work on actor-policy distribution mismatch in LLM RL (IMPALA's truncated importance sampling, FlashRL, AReal, LlamaRL), which corrects the mismatch post-hoc via importance weight ratios that suffer an inherent stability-performance trade-off. Instead, it repurposes Optimal Budgeted Rejection Sampling (OBRS) from the statistical literature (Verine et al., 2024) to directly reshape the actor's sampling distribution before training, provably reducing KL divergence to the target policy. The acceptance probability formula echoes speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023), but the application to RL loss correction and the top-k bias-corrected normalization are new. The paper positions JACKPOT as orthogonal to and complementary with TIS: OBRS narrows the gap, and residual mismatch can still be handled by standard importance sampling on top.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since rollouts consume over 70% of RL training cost for LLMs, enabling 64–128× actor-policy update ratios (or even a smaller/different rollout model) could reduce end-to-end training cost by several-fold, making large-scale RL post-training far more accessible. The method is lightweight (<3% overhead, no vLLM modifications) and plugs into existing PPO/GRPO pipelines, lowering the adoption barrier. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond mathematical reasoning to coding or agentic tasks, whether it works with fundamentally different architectures for rollout vs. training (not just different checkpoints of the same family), and how it interacts with other efficiency techniques like quantized or sparse rollout models. The extreme off-policy setting still shows meaningful degradation versus on-policy, suggesting the method mitigates but does not eliminate the cost of distribution mismatch.
