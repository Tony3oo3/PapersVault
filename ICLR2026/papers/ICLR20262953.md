## Problem

State-of-the-art NL2SQL systems rely on proprietary LLMs (e.g., GPT-4) with expensive test-time scaling, raising concerns about openness, reproducibility, and computational cost. While task decomposition can substantially improve small LLM performance on complex queries (30.4%↑ with manual subtasks), LLMs fail to decompose queries effectively on their own (only 3.4%↑), and generated subtasks are often unreliable due to hallucination. The paper addresses how to enable small-scale public LLMs to perform verifiable, self-directed task decomposition for NL2SQL under resource-constrained settings.

## Input / Output

The model receives a natural language query Q, database schema DB, and optional external knowledge K as a single prompt, and outputs a sequence of (subtask, sub-SQL) pairs whose SQL statements collectively reconstruct the target query. At inference time, the final SQL is produced in a single forward pass (model-level), requiring only ~1.8K tokens per query versus 160K+ for system-level baselines. Training data is synthesized from BIRD-train (9,003 valid samples) using GLM-4-Plus, yielding only 7.2k training queries with verifiable intermediate subtasks rather than large-scale synthetic data.

## Method

LearNAT comprises two components. First, a Decomposition Synthesis Procedure formulates subtask generation as MCTS where the LLM predicts the next subtask and sub-SQL at each node; ASTs of generated sub-SQLs are compared against the gold SQL's AST to classify nodes as progressive, redundant, or invalid, enabling aggressive pruning of the infinite text action space and rule-based reward estimation via a weighted combination of node-level overlap and tree-edit-distance structural similarity. Adaptive few-shot demonstrations from prior successful decompositions further improve search success rate to 80%. Second, Margin-Aware Reinforcement Learning fine-tunes the LLM in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on successful decomposition trajectories, followed by DPO with an AST-computed margin offset (Δr = R(s_w) − R(s_l)) that encodes how much more correct the preferred step is relative to the dispreferred one, enabling finer-grained preference alignment than standard DPO which treats all positive/negative pairs equally.

## Results

On Spider-dev (out-of-domain), LearNAT with Qwen2.5-Coder-7B achieves 86.4% Execution Accuracy, outperforming the strongest model-level baseline CodeS-7B (85.4%) by 1.0% and surpassing most GPT-4-based system-level methods. The 32B variant reaches 88.4%, exceeding SuperSQL (GPT-4) by 1.4%. On BIRD-dev (in-domain), LearNAT-7B scores 58.1% and LearNAT-32B scores 65.0%, with the 32B model outperforming all system-level baselines by at least 5.6%. Under identical evaluation protocols (Table 3), LearNAT-7B consistently beats SynCoT, SQL-o1, and Alpha-SQL on both benchmarks, achieving 77.4% total (BIRD+Spider average) vs. 73.7% for Alpha-SQL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against model-level baselines, LearNAT-7B outperforms CodeS-7B by +1.0% on Spider-dev and +1.1% on BIRD-dev, while LearNAT-14B beats CodeS-15B by +2.0% and +2.7% respectively, demonstrating consistent gains across model scales. Against system-level GPT-4 methods, LearNAT-7B is slightly behind MAC-SQL (58.1 vs. 59.4 on BIRD-dev) and SuperSQL (86.4 vs. 87.0 on Spider-dev), but LearNAT-32B surpasses all system-level baselines on both benchmarks (65.0 vs. 59.4 on BIRD-dev; 88.4 vs. 87.0 on Spider-dev). Under identical evaluation protocols (Table 3), LearNAT-7B wins against SynCoT (+0.4 BIRD, +4.7 Spider), SQL-o1 (+1.4 BIRD, +1.7 Spider), and Alpha-SQL (+1.6 BIRD, +6.6 Spider), though it trails OmniSQL on BIRD-dev (61.1 vs. 63.9) while leading on Spider-dev (86.0 vs. 81.2). Notably, LearNAT achieves these results with only 1.8K tokens per query at inference, compared to 160K+ for system-level methods like CHASE-SQL, highlighting a substantial efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

LearNAT extends the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023) by introducing an AST-based margin offset into the preference loss, enabling fine-grained step-level supervision for multi-step reasoning. It also builds on MCTS-based NL2SQL approaches (Alpha-SQL, SQL-o1) but departs from their reliance on LLM self-evaluation for node rewards, instead using rule-based AST similarity for verifiable and computationally cheaper reward estimation. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the dominant system-level paradigm (CHASE-SQL, MAC-SQL, SuperSQL) that achieves SOTA through proprietary LLMs and test-time scaling, arguing instead that model-level improvements via verifiable task decomposition can close much of the gap with far fewer parameters and tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical pathway for deploying competitive NL2SQL systems with open-source 7B models at 1.8K tokens per query, versus 160K+ for system-level GPT-4/Gemini pipelines, which is significant for resource-constrained and privacy-sensitive deployments. However, it remains 9.9% behind the best system-level method on BIRD-dev, and the strict AST-based verification limits SQL expression diversity (addressed only partially by the LearNAT+ variant). Open questions include whether system-level integration of LearNAT (e.g., with inference-time search) can close the remaining gap without incurring prohibitive token costs, and whether the decomposition framework generalizes beyond NL2SQL to other structured-output tasks.
