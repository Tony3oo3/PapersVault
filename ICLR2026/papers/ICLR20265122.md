## Problem

Generating high-resolution 3D CT volumes at voxel-level remains intractable with existing generative models: U-Net-based diffusion methods cannot capture long-range anatomical dependencies, autoencoder bottlenecks (VAE/VQ-VAE) lose critical fine-grained details due to limited 3D training data, and naively scaling Transformers to higher resolutions increases token count by 8× and attention cost by ~64×. No prior method simultaneously preserves local detail, global structural coherence, and computational feasibility for dense 3D medical volume synthesis.

## Input / Output

The model takes a random Gaussian noise 3D volume as input and produces an unconditional 3D CT volume directly at voxel-level (no latent space), evaluated at 128³ and 256³ resolutions. During diffusion, the model jointly predicts both the clean signal and the noise at each timestep using a cosine-sine schedule over 1000 steps. The architecture processes the volume as overlapping 12×12×12 patches with stride 8, and the final output is reassembled from patch-level predictions into a full isotropic 1 mm-spaced volume.

## Method

PRDiT decomposes 3D diffusion into a two-stage residual pipeline: (1) a lightweight MLP-based Local Denoiser with two SwiGLU layers and AdaLN time conditioning operates independently on overlapping 3D patches to estimate coarse signal and noise, and (2) a Global Residual Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with memory-efficient multi-head self-attention attends across all patch embeddings to refine the local predictions via global residuals, trained with the local denoiser frozen. For sampling, the paper introduces a predictor-corrector scheme that takes a k-step deterministic (cold) gradient jump followed by a (k−1)-step stochastic (hot) correction with fresh noise injection, which empirically outperforms both pure cold (k=1) and conventional hot diffusion. For high-resolution scaling (e.g., 128³→256³), the pretrained low-resolution backbone is frozen and only a lightweight high-resolution residual refinement module is trained, using nearest downsampling to preserve noise energy and trilinear upsampling for structural continuity, reducing training cost by over 6× compared to from-scratch high-resolution training.

## Results

On LIDC-IDRI (128³), PRDiT-12L achieves a 3D FID of 1.41±0.17 and MMD of 0.1501±0.010, outperforming all baselines; even the smallest PRDiT-4L variant (FID 2.04±0.18) beats every competitor. On Rad-ChestCT (128³), PRDiT-12L reaches FID 1.45±0.21 and MMD 0.159±0.007, again leading all methods. At 256³ resolution on LIDC-IDRI, PRDiT-4L↑256 achieves FID 2.28 and MMD 0.1370 in only 36 GPU-hours, while 3D-LDM fails entirely (OOM) and WDM-3D scores FID 5.60 at 120 GPU-hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRDiT outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against HA-GAN, PRDiT-12L reduces FID from 3.26 to 1.41 on LIDC-IDRI (57% lower) and from 3.92 to 1.45 on Rad-ChestCT (63% lower), while also achieving substantially lower W-Scores (1.00 vs. 5.42 and 2.48). Against 3D-LDM, the gap is even wider: 3D-LDM records the worst FID of all methods (7.62 on LIDC-IDRI, 4.14 on Rad-ChestCT) and is entirely infeasible at 256³ resolution due to out-of-memory failures. Against WDM-3D, PRDiT-12L improves FID from 3.67 to 1.41 on LIDC-IDRI and from 4.11 to 1.45 on Rad-ChestCT, with MMD improvements of roughly 20–25%. Notably, even the shallowest PRDiT-4L variant already surpasses all baselines on FID and MMD, and at 256³ resolution PRDiT-4L↑256 achieves the best FID (2.28) and MMD (0.1370) while requiring only 36 GPU-hours versus 120–140 GPU-hours for the competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRDiT builds directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and the cosine-sine diffusion schedule with joint signal-and-noise estimation from Zhang et al. (2023), but departs from both by decomposing the 3D denoising task into a lightweight MLP-based local patch denoiser and a global residual Transformer, operating entirely at voxel level without any autoencoder or VQ-VAE bottleneck. This contrasts with prior 3D medical diffusion models (3D-LDM, WDM-3D) that rely on U-Net backbones in latent or wavelet spaces, and with GAN-based methods (HA-GAN, 3D-StyleGAN) that suffer from mode collapse. The predictor-corrector sampling scheme is a novel reformulation of the cold gradient-update path from Zhang et al. (2023) into a k-step predictor plus (k−1)-step stochastic corrector, and the high-resolution scaling strategy of reusing a frozen low-resolution backbone with a lightweight refinement module is a practical departure from training from scratch at target resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the autoencoder bottleneck and showing that a two-stage local-global residual decomposition can match or exceed latent-space and wavelet-based diffusion models at substantially lower training cost, PRDiT offers a practical path to high-resolution 3D medical volume synthesis that preserves fine anatomical detail. The 6–7× training-cost reduction for high-resolution generation via backbone reuse is particularly relevant given the prohibitive memory demands of 3D attention. However, the paper is limited to unconditional generation on two chest-CT datasets, provides no downstream clinical validation (segmentation, detection), and acknowledges that the MLP-based local denoiser may underperform a CNN-based alternative; extending to conditional generation, 3D-specific attention variants, and broader anatomical regions remain open.
