## Problem

In long-tailed classification (e.g., plant or species identification with thousands of classes), existing conformal prediction methods force a binary choice: STANDARD CP produces small prediction sets but systematically omits rare classes, while CLASSWISE CP covers all classes but yields impractically large sets (hundreds to thousands of labels). The paper addresses the lack of methods that smoothly trade off set size against class-conditional coverage while maintaining marginal coverage guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262783/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (plant or animal species) and a calibration set of labeled examples exchangeable with the test point. It outputs a prediction set of class labels (a subset of the full label space) rather than a single point prediction, with set size varying from ~2 to ~780 labels depending on the method and parameters. The base classifier is a ResNet-50 trained on the training split, and the calibration set (70% of a validation split) is used solely to compute conformal score thresholds.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary approaches. First, a new conformal score function called Prevalence-Adjusted Softmax (PAS), defined as s_PAS(x,y) = −p̂(y|x)/p̂(y), which is derived from oracle-optimal sets that threshold on the likelihood ratio p(y|x)/p(y) to optimally balance expected set size and macro-coverage (the unweighted average of class-conditional coverages). Running STANDARD CP with this score inherits marginal coverage guarantees while approximately achieving the oracle-optimal size/coverage trade-off; a weighted variant (WPAS) allows upweighting user-specified classes (e.g., endangered species). Second, INTERP-Q linearly interpolates between the CLASSWISE and STANDARD conformal quantile thresholds via q̂_y^IQ = τ·q̂_y^CW + (1−τ)·q̂, providing a single tunable parameter τ that traces out the full trade-off curve between set size and class-conditional coverage, with a guaranteed marginal coverage of at least 1−2α.

## Results

On Pl@ntNet-300K (α=0.1), STANDARD with PAS achieves an average set size of 2.57 with FracBelow50% of 0.167 and MacroCov of 0.755, compared to STANDARD's 1.57 set size but 0.389 FracBelow50% and 0.525 MacroCov. INTERP-Q at τ=0.99 further improves to FracBelow50% of 0.151 and MacroCov of 0.785 while maintaining marginal coverage of 0.905. On iNaturalist-2018, CLASSWISE produces an average set size of 7430, whereas INTERP-Q at τ=0.99 reduces this to 55.8 while retaining strong class-conditional coverage. All proposed methods maintain marginal coverage at or above the 1−α target.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STANDARD CP (Vovk et al., 2005), both PAS and INTERP-Q dramatically improve class-conditional coverage: PAS reduces FracBelow50% from 0.389 to 0.167 and raises MacroCov from 0.525 to 0.755, at the cost of only a 64% increase in average set size (1.57→2.57). Against CLASSWISE CP (Vovk et al., 2005), the proposed methods achieve comparable class-conditional coverage (FracBelow50% of 0.151–0.167 vs. 0.000) while reducing average set size by over 99% (from 780 to 2.57–7.6 on Pl@ntNet-300K, and from 7430 to 55.8 on iNaturalist-2018). Against CLUSTERED CP (Ding et al., 2023), the proposed methods strictly dominate: CLUSTERED performs nearly identically to STANDARD (FracBelow50% 0.398, MacroCov 0.513) because it defaults to STANDARD thresholds for rare classes it cannot cluster. Against RC3P (Shi et al., 2024), the paper reports that the proposed methods 'strictly dominate' RC3P, achieving much better class-conditional coverage at any given average set size, while RC3P also violates its marginal coverage guarantee due to exchangeability violations in its estimation procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformal prediction framework (Vovk et al., 2005) to the long-tailed classification setting, where existing methods like CLASSWISE CP and CLUSTERED CP (Ding et al., 2023) produce impractically large sets or default to marginal-only guarantees for rare classes. The PAS score function is derived from a Neyman-Pearson-style optimality argument for a new coverage target (macro-coverage), while INTERP-Q and the FUZZY method in the appendix build on weighted conformal prediction (Tibshirani et al., 2019; Podkopaev & Ramdas, 2021) by interpolating or kernel-weighting class-specific quantile thresholds. The paper departs from prior class-conditional conformal work, which has focused on few-class or mildly imbalanced settings, by explicitly targeting the many-class long-tail regime where per-class calibration data is scarce.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-tailed classification is central to biodiversity monitoring, disease diagnosis, and citizen-science platforms like Pl@ntNet, where missing rare classes has outsized consequences (e.g., endangered species detection, model collapse in iterative human-AI loops). The paper provides two simple, tunable methods (PAS and INTERP-Q) that let practitioners navigate the set-size vs. class-conditional-coverage trade-off without the binary choice forced by STANDARD or CLASSWISE CP. Key limitations include the weaker 1−2α marginal guarantee for INTERP-Q (though empirically close to 1−α), the need to choose τ, and the fact that PAS targets macro-coverage rather than per-class guarantees. Open questions include incorporating 'effort' (search cost) into the utility of prediction sets and whether the methods extend to open-world or multi-label settings.
