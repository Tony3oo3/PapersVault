## Problem

Existing 3DGS compression methods focus on storage (file-size) compression but fail to reduce the rendering VRAM that actually determines whether real-time rendering is feasible on edge devices. Methods based on neural decoding, vector quantization, or hash grids must fully decompress parameters before rendering, often resulting in rendering memory equal to or exceeding vanilla 3DGS. The open question is how to simultaneously reduce both the number of Gaussian primitives and the per-primitive parameter count to achieve genuine rendering-memory compression.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully trained 3DGS scene (standard 3D Gaussian primitives with 3rd-order Spherical Harmonics color) and produces a compressed, directly renderable 3DGS scene where SH is replaced by a variable number of Spherical Gaussian lobes (0–3 per primitive) and both primitives and lobes are pruned. No decoding step is required at render time: the output is a standard set of 3D Gaussians with a lightweight SG color model, so it can be loaded into any 3DGS-compatible renderer (including WebGL) without additional memory overhead. The compression is controlled by a single total parameter budget κ, yielding two operating points (HQ and LM) that trade off quality against VRAM.

## Method

MEGS² replaces Spherical Harmonics entirely with arbitrarily-oriented Spherical Gaussian lobes (G(v;μ,s,a) = a·e^{s(μ·v−1)}), where each lobe's axis is unconstrained (unlike the fixed orthogonal axes in SG-Splatting), enabling both higher expressiveness and per-lobe pruning. The key innovation is a unified soft-pruning framework that formulates both primitive-count reduction (via L₀ norm of opacity) and lobe-count reduction (via L₀ norm of sharpness) as a single memory-constrained optimization: min L(o,s,Θ) s.t. ρₒ‖o‖₀ + ρₛ‖s‖₀ ≤ κ. Because the L₀ constraint is non-differentiable, the problem is solved with an ADMM-inspired algorithm that alternates gradient steps on the primal variables with a proximal projection onto the sparsity constraint (using importance-based selection for primitives and a dynamic-range metric Dᵢ = |aᵢ|(1−e^{−2sᵢ}) for lobes) and dual-variable updates. A post-processing step removes near-zero primitives and lobes, applying an analytically derived color compensation term Δc₀ = aᵢ·(1−e^{−2sᵢ})/(2sᵢ) to the diffuse channel to recover the average energy of pruned lobes, followed by brief fine-tuning.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, MEGS² (HQ) achieves PSNR 27.54, SSIM 0.824, and LPIPS 0.209 with only 55 MB static VRAM and 265 MB rendering VRAM, compared to vanilla 3DGS's 648/1717 MB — an 8× static and ~6× rendering VRAM reduction. Against the SOTA lightweight method GaussianSpa (115/448 MB), MEGS² halves static VRAM and cuts rendering VRAM by ~40% while matching or slightly exceeding quality (LPIPS 0.209 vs 0.215). On DeepBlending, MEGS² (HQ) achieves the best PSNR (30.17) and LPIPS (0.233) among all methods with 54/243 MB VRAM. The LM variant pushes compression further to 40/224 MB on Mip-NeRF360 with only a 0.33 dB PSNR drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla 3DGS, MEGS² (HQ) achieves an 8× static VRAM reduction (55 vs 648 MB on Mip-NeRF360) and ~6× rendering VRAM reduction (265 vs 1717 MB) while matching or slightly improving PSNR (27.54 vs 27.48) and LPIPS (0.209 vs 0.217). Versus the SOTA lightweight method GaussianSpa, MEGS² halves static VRAM (55 vs 115 MB) and reduces rendering VRAM by ~40% (265 vs 448 MB) with comparable quality — LPIPS is actually better (0.209 vs 0.215) though PSNR is marginally lower (27.54 vs 27.56). Against SG-Splatting, MEGS² improves PSNR by 0.27 dB and cuts static VRAM by 7.6× (55 vs 416 MB), demonstrating the advantage of fully standalone SG over the hybrid SG+SH model. Compared to neural/quantization-based methods (EAGLES, CompactGaussian, MesonGS, LocoGS), MEGS² achieves substantially lower VRAM (e.g., 55 vs 609 MB for LocoGS) because it renders directly from the compressed state without a decode step. Among pruning-only baselines (Mini-Splatting, MaskGaussian, LP-3DGS), MEGS² matches or exceeds quality while using 2–5× less VRAM, confirming that the unified lobe+primitive pruning outperforms primitive-only pruning. On the Shiny Blender Real dataset (Table 11), MEGS² achieves the best mean SSIM (0.695) and LPIPS (0.230), outperforming Ref-Gaussian and 3DGS-DR on perceptual metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three lines of work: 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) as the base representation, SG-Splatting (Wang et al., 2024a) which first introduced Spherical Gaussians as a hybrid complement to Spherical Harmonics, and GaussianSpa (Zhang et al., 2025b) which formulated primitive pruning as a constrained optimization problem. The key departures are: (1) replacing the hybrid SG+SH model with fully standalone, arbitrarily-oriented SG lobes, and (2) extending GaussianSpa's primitive-count-only pruning to a unified framework that jointly optimizes both primitive count and per-primitive lobe count under a single memory budget. Unlike storage-focused compression methods (EAGLES, CompactGaussian, HAC++), the paper explicitly targets rendering VRAM as the optimization objective, arguing that decode overhead in neural/quantization-based methods negates their storage savings at render time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper shifts the 3DGS compression community's focus from file-size reduction to the rendering VRAM bottleneck that actually determines whether a scene can run on edge devices, and demonstrates this shift is practically consequential: their WebGL viewer achieves 60–120 FPS on mid-range phones where vanilla 3DGS cannot render at all. The unified soft-pruning formulation (ADMM with a joint L0 constraint on opacity and sharpness) is a clean, generalizable template that could extend to other per-primitive parameter groups. Limitations noted by the authors include leaving dynamic (runtime) VRAM optimization for future work and potential quality degradation on highly complex specular highlights, suggesting the approach may need further refinement for materials with dense, multi-lobed view-dependent signals.
