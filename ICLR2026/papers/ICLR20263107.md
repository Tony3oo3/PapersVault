## Problem

Neural Combinatorial Optimization (NCO) solvers for Vehicle Routing Problems face a critical sim-to-real gap: they are trained on symmetric Euclidean distances and use node-centric architectures that cannot natively encode the asymmetric distance and duration matrices inherent in real road networks (one-way streets, traffic patterns, turn restrictions). This paper addresses both the data gap and the architectural gap simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263107/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes node coordinates, a full asymmetric distance matrix D ∈ R^{N×N}, an asymmetric duration matrix T ∈ R^{N×N}, and optional node features (demand, time windows), all derived from real-world road topologies via OSRM/OpenStreetMap. It autoregressively outputs a complete vehicle route as a sequence of visited nodes, maintaining feasibility constraints (capacity, time windows) throughout generation. Instances are generated on-the-fly by subsampling from precomputed 1000×1000 city base maps, enabling virtually unlimited training data from ~1.5 GB of topology files.

## Method

RRNCO introduces two architectural innovations within an encoder-decoder framework trained via REINFORCE with POMO baseline. The Adaptive Node Embedding (ANE) module efficiently fuses coordinates with distance information by sampling k nearest nodes per node using inverse-distance probability weighting, then combining the resulting distance and coordinate embeddings via a learned contextual gating MLP, producing dual row/column embeddings for asymmetry. The Neural Adaptive Bias (NAB) replaces the hand-crafted heuristic bias in the Adaptation Attention-Free Module (AAFM) with a learned mechanism that jointly embeds the distance matrix, directional angle matrix, and duration matrix through separate linear projections, fuses them via softmax-normalized multi-channel gating, and projects to a scalar bias matrix that modulates AAFM's attention-free computation. The decoder integrates ReLD's residual identity mapping and MatNet's compatibility layer with a negative-log-distance heuristic to autoregressively select the next node at each step.

## Results

On the real-world VRP benchmark, RRNCO achieves the lowest gap to best-known solutions across all three tasks (ATSP, ACVRP, ACVRPTW) and all three evaluation distributions. In-distribution, RRNCO attains gaps of 1.80% (ATSP), 3.45% (ACVRP), and 3.74% (ACVRPTW), compared to the next-best neural method MatNet at 3.98%, 7.26%, and AAFM at 9.93% respectively. Out-of-distribution (cluster), the advantage widens: RRNCO reaches 2.25% (ATSP), 3.23% (ACVRP), and 4.29% (ACVRPTW) gaps, while MatNet degrades to 5.88%, 10.16%, and GOAL to 22.20%. On the stochastic SMTVRP benchmark, RRNCO achieves the lowest cost (601.03) with 100% feasibility and zero time-window violations, outperforming OR-Tools (610.08, 24.2% feasibility) and RF (602.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RRNCO outperforms all 17 baselines across every task and distribution. Among traditional solvers, LKH3 and PyVRP serve as the best-known reference (gap = 0); OR-Tools trails by 1.4–4.5% gap. Among node-only neural methods, POMO (34.2% gap on ATSP in-dist), ELG (33.0%), BQ-NCO (45.7%), LEHD (46.1%), MTPOMO/MVMoE/RF (~23–24% on ACVRP) all fall far behind RRNCO. Among node-and-edge methods, MatNet is the closest competitor (3.98% vs. 1.80% on ATSP in-dist; 7.26% vs. 3.45% on ACVRP), but RRNCO nearly halves its gap. AAFM (19.8% ATSP, 9.9% ACVRP) and GOAL (9.4% ATSP, 20.9% ACVRP) are substantially worse, while GCN (29.8% ACVRP) and ReLD variants (26.4% ACVRP, 12.3% ACVRPTW) lag further. The gap widens dramatically under OOD-cluster: RRNCO maintains 2.25–4.29% gaps while MatNet degrades to 5.88–10.16% and GOAL to 12.19–52.17%, confirming that the learned NAB mechanism provides robust generalization to unseen spatial distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

RRNCO extends the encoder-decoder autoregressive framework of Kool et al. (2019) and the dual row/column matrix encoding of MatNet (Kwon et al., 2021) by replacing the heuristic adaptation bias in AAFM (Zhou et al., 2024a) with a learned Neural Adaptive Bias that jointly models asymmetric distance, duration, and directional angle matrices. The decoder borrows residual and identity-mapping refinements from ReLD (Huang et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure from prior NCO work is the shift from node-centric, symmetric-Euclidean formulations to a node-and-edge architecture that natively ingests correlated asymmetric cost matrices, addressing what the authors identify as the primary topological barrier to real-world deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100-city real-world dataset with asymmetric distance and duration matrices from OSRM fills a critical gap: prior NCO benchmarks (TSPLIB, CVRPLIB) assume symmetric Euclidean costs, making it impossible to evaluate whether learned solvers generalize to actual road networks. The dramatic performance collapse of MatNet when trained on synthetic data versus real data (110% vs 4% gap in Table 3) quantifies the sim-to-real gap and motivates the dataset's utility. Open questions include scalability to very large instances (beyond ~100 nodes), whether the multi-snapshot NAB approach extends to truly dynamic online traffic, and whether the architecture generalizes to other combinatorial problems with asymmetric edge features beyond routing.
