## Problem

Standard Error Feedback (EF) methods, widely used for communication-efficient distributed optimization, fail in the composite setting where the objective includes a non-smooth regularizer or constraints. The classical virtual-iterate analysis framework breaks down because the proximal update destroys the additive structure of iterates that the analysis relies on, and no prior work achieved convergence rates for EF in the convex composite setting matching the best-known uncomposite results.

## Input / Output

The algorithm operates in a distributed setting with n clients, each holding a local loss f_i accessible only via stochastic gradient oracles, while the server maintains the composite regularizer ψ and performs proximal updates. Communication uses two channels: a compressed channel (cost 1 per vector, governed by a contractive compression operator with quality parameter δ) and an uncompressed channel (cost m per vector, typically m ≈ 1/δ). The output is a random sample of virtual iterates x̄_T, obtained via a reservoir-sampling procedure over accumulated true gradients, rather than the last real iterate, ensuring the suboptimality guarantee E[F(x̄_T) − F*] ≤ ε holds.

## Method

The core contribution is a new algorithm, EControl with Dual Averaging, that replaces the standard proximal-gradient update with a dual averaging update, restoring the property that iterates are defined by (weighted) sums of all past gradient estimates. This summation structure allows the accumulated compression error e_t to be subtracted inside the proximal operator to define a virtual iterate that follows the true-gradient trajectory, fixing the breakdown of the classical virtual-iterate framework in the composite case. The compressed gradient estimates are generated by the EControl mechanism, which blends gradient difference compression with a scaled error feedback term (scaling factor η chosen to optimize δ-dependence). A novel analysis template for inexact dual averaging (Theorem 3.3) bounds the suboptimality of virtual iterates in terms of accumulated errors and gradient noise, and a reservoir-sampling procedure (Algorithm 3) enables outputting a valid random sample of virtual iterates without explicitly storing them, at the cost of one extra bit per round plus one final full-communication round.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is Theorem 4.4, which establishes that EControl with Dual Averaging achieves a convergence rate of O(R₀²σ²/(nε²) + R₀²√ℓσ/(δ²ε^{3/2}) + ℓR₀²/(δε)) iterations for convex composite optimization with contractive compression of quality δ, matching the best-known rates in the uncomposite (ψ≡0) setting for the first time. Experimentally, on a synthetic regularized softmax objective, the method exhibits linear speedup in the number of clients n, and the virtual iterates and real iterates perform nearly identically in suboptimality. On FashionMNIST with ℓ₁ regularization and Top-K compression (K/d=0.1, δ=0.1), EControl with Dual Averaging significantly outperforms both proximal EF and proximal EF21 in convergence speed, and admits a much larger stepsize than the competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares EControl with Dual Averaging against two baselines on FashionMNIST logistic regression with ℓ₁ regularization: proximal EF (EC-ProxSGD, Qian et al. 2020) and proximal EF21 (Fatkhullin et al. 2025). EControl with Dual Averaging significantly outperforms both methods in convergence speed (Figure 2), reaching lower suboptimality faster under the same Top-K compression (δ=0.1) and distributed setup (n=10 clients, heterogeneous data). The paper attributes this advantage partly to the fact that EControl with Dual Averaging admits a much larger stepsize than the other methods. Theoretically, the proposed method is the first to match the state-of-the-art uncomposite EF rates (e.g., EControl, Gao et al. 2024) in the composite setting, whereas prior composite EF methods (Qian et al. 2020) had suboptimal O(1/δ²) dependence on compression quality, and Safe-EF (Islamov et al. 2025) required additional structural assumptions on the constraint set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Error Feedback (EF) mechanism from the unconstrained setting to the general convex composite setting by identifying a fundamental structural incompatibility: the proximal update destroys the additive gradient-sum structure that the virtual-iterate analysis (Mania et al., 2017) relies on. The fix is to replace the proximal gradient step with a Dual Averaging update, which restores the summation structure and allows the accumulated compression error to be corrected inside the proximal operator. The method builds directly on EControl (Gao et al., 2024a), a state-of-the-art EF variant that blends error feedback with gradient difference compression, and is the first to achieve convergence rates in the composite setting that match the best-known uncomposite rates. Prior composite-EF works (Qian et al., 2020; Condat et al., 2022; Islamov et al., 2025) either imposed restrictive assumptions, could not handle stochastic gradients, or yielded suboptimal dependence on the compression quality δ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first convergence analysis for error feedback in the general convex composite setting that recovers the optimal O(1/δ) dependence on compression quality, closing a gap that has persisted since EF was introduced. The inexact dual averaging analysis template (Theorem 3.3) is of independent interest and could apply to other settings where iterative updates are distorted by approximation or constraints, such as safe reinforcement learning or constrained distributed optimization. A key open question is whether the real iterates (which are what practitioners actually use) can be analyzed as strongly as the virtual iterates; the paper's experiments suggest they perform nearly identically, but a matching theory remains elusive. The analysis is restricted to the convex setting and requires an initial exact gradient step in the composite case, limiting immediate applicability to non-convex problems.
