## Problem

Prior work on in-context learning (ICL) in Transformers has identified the statistical conditions under which ICL emerges and documented its transience, but existing rational-adaptation frameworks focus only on the asymptotically optimal strategy and cannot explain the learning dynamics—specifically, what drives transitions between ICL and in-weights learning (IWL) during training. This paper asks whether the same principles of environmental predictability that govern the evolution of plasticity versus genetic assimilation in biology can account for both the asymptotic strategy selection and the transient learning trajectories in Transformers.

## Input / Output

For sinusoid regression, the model receives N=10 (x, y) prompt pairs with x ∈ [−π, π] plus a scalar query x_q, and outputs a predicted scalar y_q. For Omniglot classification, the model receives N=2 image-label prompt pairs (with one example guaranteed to share the query's character class) plus a query image, and outputs a binary label. Critically, evaluation prompts are constructed from a task independent of the current training task, so the prompt-implied target (ICL) and the weight-encoded target (IWL) explicitly conflict, allowing the model's strategy preference to be quantified via a ratio of prediction errors to each target.

## Method

The paper trains a 4-layer decoder-only Transformer (4 heads, 128-dim embeddings, learned positional encodings) on two controlled meta-learning tasks—sinusoid regression and few-shot Omniglot classification—systematically manipulating environmental stability (via an AR(1) coefficient for sinusoid parameters or a Bernoulli persistence probability for character labels) and cue reliability (via observation noise variance or label-flip probability). A shallow ResNet (3 stages, 16/32/64 channels) is jointly trained with the Transformer for image processing. The core analytical contribution is a cost-based arbitration framework: the asymptotic strategy is determined by environmental predictability (stability favors IWL, reliable cues favor ICL), while the transient learning trajectory is governed by the relative acquisition cost of each strategy, quantified via prequential codelength. This is causally validated by reducing the Omniglot vocabulary size (lowering IWL's sample complexity via the coupon-collector bound), which reverses the observed ICL→IWL transience into IWL→ICL transience, matching the sinusoid task's dynamics.

## Results

The paper quantifies strategy preference via the ICL preference score S_ICL (ranging from 0 = pure IWL to 1 = pure ICL). In sinusoid regression, increasing environmental stability (α→1) causes a precipitous decline in S_ICL toward 0, confirming that stable environments favor IWL. In Omniglot classification, S_ICL remains high even at α→1 when cues are perfectly reliable (ρ=1), deviating from the biological prediction and suggesting negligible maintenance cost for ICL. Prequential codelength analysis confirms that IWL is cheaper to acquire for sinusoid regression while ICL is cheaper for Omniglot, predicting the observed transience direction (IWL→ICL for sinusoid, ICL→IWL for Omniglot). Reducing the Omniglot vocabulary from 1623 to 100 characters reverses the transience pattern, with the model initially favoring IWL (S_ICL < 0.2) before switching to ICL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare a proposed method against external baselines; instead, it contrasts two internal learning strategies (ICL vs. IWL) within the same Transformer architecture across controlled environments. The key 'comparison' is between the asymptotic optimality of each strategy (determined by environmental stability and cue reliability) and the acquisition cost of each strategy (measured via prequential codelength). The results show that the cheaper strategy always emerges first in training regardless of long-term optimality: ICL emerges first in Omniglot (where memorizing 1623 labels is expensive) while IWL emerges first in sinusoid regression (where approximating a sine wave is cheap). The causal manipulation of vocabulary size (1623→100) confirms that acquisition cost, not asymptotic optimality, governs the initial strategy preference, effectively reversing the transience direction and aligning Omniglot's dynamics with those of sinusoid regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ICL evaluation protocol of Chan et al. (2022) and the prequential codelength metric of Blier & Ollivier (2018), but its primary contribution is a conceptual reframing: it imports the evolutionary biology distinction between phenotypic plasticity and genetic evolution (Stearns, 1989; Waddington, 1953) as a formal lens for understanding ICL vs. IWL competition in Transformers. While prior work (Wurgaft et al., 2025) has argued ICL emerges rationally from training data statistics, and Singh et al. (2023, 2025) have documented the transience of ICL, this paper unifies both the asymptotic strategy selection and the learning dynamics under a single cost-based arbitration principle, drawing on resource-rational analysis (Lieder & Griffiths, 2020) to formalize the tradeoff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, testable account of why different tasks exhibit opposite transience patterns (ICL→IWL in memorization-heavy tasks vs. IWL→ICL in function-fitting tasks), with a clean causal validation via vocabulary-size manipulation. However, the analysis is restricted to two simplified meta-learning benchmarks and operates at Marr's computational level, leaving open whether the evolutionary principles generalize to the complex, multi-task environments of real LLMs. The paper also raises the intriguing but unexplored question of whether early ICL serves a Baldwin-effect-like scaffolding role that accelerates IWL acquisition on harder tasks.
