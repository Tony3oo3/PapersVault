## Problem

Naively applying standard masked diffusion models (MDMs) to molecular graph generation causes severe performance degradation because element-agnostic noise schedules lead to a state-clashing problem: semantically distinct molecules collapse into identical intermediate masked states, creating multimodal reconstruction targets that a unimodal denoiser cannot resolve. This issue is particularly acute for molecules with symmetric motifs and small atom/bond vocabularies, where many distinct graphs map to the same corrupted state, forcing the model to average over incompatible targets and produce distributionally misaligned or chemically invalid outputs.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model starts from a fully masked graph (all nodes and edges set to [mask]) and iteratively unmasks elements over T diffusion steps; for conditional generation, property targets (e.g., O₂/N₂/CO₂ permeability, synthesizability) are injected via classifier-free guidance with adaptive layer normalization. The molecular graph is represented as one-hot encoded node features (atom types) and edge features (bond types), each augmented with an absorbing [mask] token, with graph sizes ranging from ≤9 atoms (QM9) to ≤50 atoms (Polymers). The output is a complete 2D molecular graph where all nodes and edges are predicted in parallel (non-autoregressive) at each denoising step.

## Method

MELD introduces a parameterized noise scheduling network that learns distinct corruption rates for each individual graph element (node and edge), replacing the fixed element-agnostic schedule. Each element is assigned a learnable embedding h^i (with edge embeddings h^{ij} = h^i + h^j), which is fed through a 2-layer MLP to produce an element-specific exponent w_φ^i in a power-law schedule γ_{t,φ}^i = 1 − (1−ε)·t^{w_φ^i}, ensuring that different molecules follow distinguishable forward trajectories and minimizing state collisions. To enable end-to-end gradient flow through the discrete masking operation, MELD employs a Straight-Through Gumbel-Softmax estimator, and random column permutation of the embedding matrix H during training enforces permutation invariance without architectural constraints. The denoiser is a 6-layer Diffusion Transformer (hidden dim 1152, 16 heads) trained jointly with the noise schedule via a weighted cross-entropy objective, with classifier-free guidance applied for property-conditioned generation.

## Results

On QM9, MELD achieves 100% validity with FCD of 0.09 and NSPDK of 0.0002, reducing FCD by 91% and NSPDK by 98% over the autoregressive masked diffusion baseline GraphARM. On ZINC250K, MELD attains state-of-the-art on 5 of 6 metrics, achieving FCD 1.51 and scaffold similarity 0.559, surpassing the strongest continuous diffusion baseline GruM (FCD 2.26, Scaf. 0.530). In property-conditioned generation on Polymers, MELD achieves a 13.4% reduction in average MAE (0.798 vs. 0.921 for GraphDiT) while maintaining 99.10% validity and the best FCD (5.93) and fragment similarity (0.974).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MELD dramatically outperforms all standard MDM variants (cosine, polynomial, power-law schedules) on both QM9 and ZINC250K: on ZINC250K, FCD drops from 25.41–26.43 to 1.51 (a ~94% reduction) and scaffold similarity jumps from 0.001 to 0.559, confirming that element-agnostic scheduling causes severe distributional misalignment. Against the strongest continuous diffusion baseline GruM, MELD wins on FCD (0.09 vs. 0.11 on QM9; 1.51 vs. 2.26 on ZINC250K), NSPDK, and scaffold similarity, while matching validity at 100%. Versus the discrete diffusion baseline DiGress, MELD achieves lower FCD (0.09 vs. 0.10 on QM9; 1.51 vs. 3.48 on ZINC250K) and higher scaffold similarity (0.947 vs. 0.936; 0.559 vs. 0.416). In property-conditioned generation on Polymers, MELD surpasses GraphDiT (the strongest discrete diffusion baseline) by 13.4% in average MAE (0.798 vs. 0.921) and by 10.8% in FCD (5.93 vs. 6.64), while also achieving substantially higher validity (99.10% vs. 82.45%). On the large-scale Guacamol dataset, MELD achieves 100% validity and the best FCD (68.8) and KL divergence (93.4) among all diffusion-based methods, using 70% fewer training epochs than DiGress. On the synthetic SBM graph benchmark, MELD outperforms both DiGress and GruM on degree, orbit, and spectral MMD metrics, with a V.U.N. score of 97.50 vs. 85.00 (GruM) and 74.00 (DiGress).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked diffusion model (MDM) framework from D3PM (Austin et al., 2021) and the simplified weighted cross-entropy objective of MD4 (Shi et al., 2024) to molecular graph generation. Its key conceptual departure is diagnosing a 'state-clashing' problem—where element-agnostic masking causes semantically distinct molecules to collapse into identical intermediate states—and addressing it with per-element learnable noise schedules, a finer-grained parameterization than the class-wise (GenMD4) or column-wise (TabDiff) schedules from prior learnable-schedule work. The denoising backbone uses a Diffusion Transformer (Peebles & Xie, 2023), and the Straight-Through Gumbel-Softmax estimator (Jang et al., 2017) enables end-to-end gradient flow through the discrete forward process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete, previously unaddressed failure mode (state-clashing) that explains why MDMs—despite their success in text and images—degrade severely on molecular graphs, and provides a lightweight fix (per-element learnable schedules adding ~0.01M parameters) that restores 100% validity with SOTA distributional metrics. An open limitation is that state-clashing cannot be fully eliminated at very late timesteps when most elements are masked, though the authors argue this affects only a small fraction of the reverse trajectory. Broader open questions include whether the element-wise scheduling principle transfers to other high-symmetry discrete domains (e.g., protein structures, crystal lattices) and whether the approach interacts favorably with 3D-aware molecular generation.
