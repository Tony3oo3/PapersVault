## Problem

Existing image generation models—whether autoregressive, diffusion, or visual autoregressive (VAR)—treat images as flat token sequences, spatial pyramids, or holistic distributions, thereby ignoring the rich hierarchical spatial structure inherent in natural images. This leads to representation ambiguity in early generation stages, error accumulation, and a lack of explicit, interpretable control over how structure emerges during generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263094/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (text condition) and an optional user-provided structure map as input, and produces a 256×256 RGB image along with a full visual granularity sequence (9 stages of structure maps and content tokens in a 16×16 latent space). Generation proceeds iteratively: at each of the 9 stages, a structure map (binary partition of the latent space into 2^i clusters) is generated first, followed by the corresponding content tokens drawn from a shared 4096×32 codebook. The structure map can be supplied externally (e.g., geometric shapes or segmentation layouts) to guide generation without additional training.

## Method

NVG decomposes an image into a structured visual granularity sequence via a multi-granularity quantized autoencoder that iteratively clusters the 16×16 latent tokens bottom-up (merging k=2 most similar tokens per stage), yielding 9 stages with 1, 2, 4, …, 256 unique tokens and corresponding hierarchical structure maps. Generation uses two separate models: a lightweight rectified-flow transformer (1/4 the size of the content model) performs structure 'inpainting'—denoising a K-dimensional bit-style embedding where known stages are fixed and unknown stages are generated—followed by Gumbel-top-k sampling to produce balanced binary splits; and a FLUX-style transformer content generator predicts the final canvas from the current canvas (residual formulation), using structure-aware RoPE to encode hierarchical cluster relationships and a combined MSE + cross-entropy loss. This residual, coarse-to-fine design reduces exposure bias relative to standard autoregressive models while making structure control an integral part of the generation pipeline rather than a post-hoc conditioning module.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, NVG-d24 (1.1B params) achieves FID 2.06, IS 317.0, precision 0.79, and recall 0.61 in 9 generation steps. NVG consistently outperforms VAR at every model size: FID improves from 3.30→3.03 (d16), 2.57→2.44 (d20), and 2.09→2.06 (d24), with corresponding gains in IS and recall. The tokenizer also achieves rFID 0.74 with only 511 unique tokens, outperforming VAR's 1.06 rFID with 680 tokens. Clear scaling behavior is observed: FID drops monotonically as model depth increases from d16 to d24.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NVG's most direct comparison is against VAR, which it outperforms at all three model sizes: FID improves by 0.27 (d16), 0.13 (d20), and 0.03 (d24), while IS and recall also improve consistently. Against diffusion models, NVG-d24 (FID 2.06) matches SiT-X (FID 2.06) with fewer generation steps (9 vs. 250) and beats DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27) by 0.21. Against autoregressive baselines, NVG-d24 beats LlamaGen-XXL (FID 2.34) and IBQ-XL (FID 2.14), and is within 0.01 of IBQ-XXL (FID 2.05) while using roughly half the parameters (1.1B vs. 2.1B). NVG-d24 trails the top masked/X-AR methods (MAR-H FID 1.55, CausalFusion-XL FID 1.77), but those require 800 training epochs versus NVG's 350, and use 250–256 generation steps versus NVG's 9. Overall, NVG achieves the best FID among models trained for ≤350 epochs and uses the fewest generation steps among all competitive methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VAR's residual visual pyramid, replacing its fixed spatial-resolution compression with a data-driven clustering process that produces structure maps encoding how tokens are spatially arranged at each granularity level. The architecture borrows FLUX's parallel linear-layer blocks and extends RoPE with a structure-aware encoding that treats tokens within the same cluster as co-located. Structure generation is handled by a lightweight rectified flow model (Lipman et al., 2023) with Gumbel-top-k sampling, while content generation follows an autoregressive residual formulation analogous to VAR. The key conceptual departure from VAR is that granularity is defined by the number of unique tokens (data-driven) rather than by spatial scale, which the authors argue alleviates representation ambiguity in early stages where a single token must cover a large, semantically diverse region.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NVG introduces structure maps as first-class, user-controllable intermediates in image generation, enabling structure-guided synthesis (e.g., from segmentation masks or geometric layouts) without post-hoc conditioning modules. The consistent FID improvements over VAR across all model sizes (3.30→3.03, 2.57→2.44, 2.09→2.06) and the clear scaling trend validate the granularity-based decomposition. However, the structure generator's 25-step flow model adds meaningful inference cost, and the evaluation is limited to ImageNet class-conditional generation at 256×256. Open directions include learned or domain-specific structure designs, region-aware generation, and extending the framework to video with physically consistent structure tracking over time.
