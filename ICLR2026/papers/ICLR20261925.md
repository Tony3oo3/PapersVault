## Problem

Safe exploration in RL requires agents to collect informative experience while avoiding catastrophic failures, yet prior uncertainty-aware methods focus almost exclusively on the critic side (ensembles, dropout, distributional critics) and leave the actor's epistemic uncertainty unaddressed in the policy update rule. This paper identifies a gap: policy gradient steps can be overconfident in data-scarce regions of the state-action space, and no practical, optimization-ready mechanism existed to make actor updates pessimistic with respect to parameter uncertainty in deep on-policy RL.

## Input / Output

The method operates within a Constrained MDP framework, consuming state observations, reward signals, and nonnegative cost signals from continuous-control environments (Safety Gym PointGoal1/PointButton1, MuJoCo Ant-v4/Walker2d-v4). It produces continuous actions and updated policy parameters under a Lagrangian objective J_r − λ(J_c − β), where the cost constraint threshold β is environment-specific (β=10 in Safety Gym, β=0 in MuJoCo). SHAPO is a drop-in modification to any on-policy algorithm (TRPO, CPO, PPO, CRPO, PIDLag, SauteRL), requiring only two additional hyperparameters: the Fisher-metric perturbation radius δ_Down and an optional critic SAM coefficient ρ_critic, while all baseline hyperparameters remain frozen.

## Method

SHAPO replaces the standard policy gradient g = ∇L|_{θ₀} with a perturbed gradient g̃ = ∇L|_{θ₀+ε_Down}, where ε_Down is computed by solving a Fisher-metric trust-region minimization of the policy surrogate L_{π₀}^λ within radius δ_Down (i.e., ε_Down = U_F(−g, δ_Down)). The perturbed gradient is then used for the actual update within the standard trust region δ_Up, making the update pessimistic about the actor's epistemic uncertainty. Proposition 2 proves this perturbation is equivalent to optimizing at the α-quantile of the expected return under a Bernstein-von Mises posterior N(θ₀, (1/n)F⁻¹), with δ_Down = z_α²/(2n) linking the trust constraint to a confidence level. A 1D Gaussian analysis (Proposition 3) shows the mechanism amplifies gradients for rare unsafe actions (negative advantage) while suppressing them for rare safe actions, biasing learning toward conservative behavior in under-explored regions. Fisher-metric perturbations are shown empirically superior to Euclidean ones, and applying SHAPO to the actor (rather than the critic) is the dominant factor for safe exploration gains.

## Results

SHAPO consistently expands the safety-efficiency Pareto frontier across all four on-policy baselines (CPO, PIDLag, CRPO, SauteRL) on Safety Gym (PointGoal1-v0, PointButton1-v0) and MuJoCo (Ant-v4, Walker2d-v4) environments. On Safety Gym tasks, SHAPO reduces cost-rate (total cost / total env steps) while maintaining or improving episodic return; on MuJoCo tasks, it significantly reduces cumulative failures (total cost). Beyond mean statistics, SHAPO suppresses heavy tails in episodic cost distributions (95th percentile on Ant-v4, 80th percentile on Walker2d-v4), reducing rare catastrophic failures. The computational overhead is modest: per-update time increases from 4s to 5.5s (~37%), and total 10M-step training on PointGoal1 goes from 85 min to 100 min, since most runtime is dominated by environment rollouts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHAPO is designed as an orthogonal plug-in that improves any on-policy safe RL baseline without altering its hyperparameters. Against PIDLag (Stooke et al., 2020), SHAPO expands the return-vs-cost Pareto frontier for every tested initial multiplier λ_init (Figure 4), and is the primary baseline used for ablation studies. Against SauteRL (Sootla et al., 2022), which already achieves the lowest cost-rate on PointGoal1 via state augmentation, SHAPO further reduces cost without degrading return. Against CPO (Achiam et al., 2017) and CRPO (Xu et al., 2021), SHAPO similarly improves both safety and efficiency, with pseudo-code provided for both (Algorithms 5–6). Ablations confirm that the Fisher-metric perturbation on the actor is the critical component: applying SAM only to the critic (Euclidean) does not promote safe exploration and can even increase cost, while SHAPO on the actor alone yields the largest safety gains. In unconstrained MuJoCo (Appendix F), SHAPO-PPO reduces premature terminations on Ant-v4 and Walker2d-v4 but offers limited benefit on HalfCheetah-v4, which lacks a failure mode, confirming that the method's gains are tied to the presence of tail risk in the return distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHAPO sits at the intersection of sharpness-aware optimization (Foret et al., 2020; Kim et al., 2022) and trust-region policy optimization (Schulman et al., 2015), reinterpreting the Fisher-metric perturbation of Fisher-SAM as a pessimistic update in face of the actor's epistemic uncertainty. The key conceptual departure from prior safe RL work is shifting the uncertainty treatment from the critic side (ensembles, distributional methods, risk measures) to the actor's parameter update itself, using the Bernstein-von Mises asymptotic normality to formalize when and how the sharpness-aware gradient becomes a quantile-based pessimistic estimate. The method is deliberately modular: it wraps around existing on-policy algorithms (TRPO, CPO, CRPO, PPO, SauteRL) as a drop-in gradient replacement rather than proposing a new algorithmic framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, optimization-ready proxy for actor-side epistemic uncertainty that avoids the infeasibility of maintaining explicit posteriors over deep policy parameters, filling a gap the authors identify in the safe RL literature. The consistent Pareto frontier expansion across four baselines and four environments, combined with the tail-suppression evidence (reduced 80th/95th percentile episodic costs), suggests the mechanism is robust rather than hyperparameter-sensitive. Open questions include whether the fixed-δ_Down strategy generalizes to high-dimensional or partially observable settings where the effective sample size is harder to reason about, whether the approach extends to discrete action spaces, and whether the ~30% per-update compute overhead remains acceptable at scale. The HalfCheetah negative result (no benefit without tail events) validates the mechanism but also delineates its scope.
