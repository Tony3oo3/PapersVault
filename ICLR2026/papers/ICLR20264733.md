## Problem

Masked diffusion models (MDMs) offer parallel generation as an alternative to autoregressive language models, but their fundamental reasoning capabilities remain poorly understood. Prior theoretical work on MDMs focused on convergence properties of the factorized backward process, yielding only asymptotic bounds that are difficult to translate into concrete conclusions about practical reasoning. This paper provides the first formal characterization of MDM expressivity, clarifying exactly what types of reasoning problems MDMs can solve and how efficiently.

## Input / Output

For language recognition, the MDM receives an input string w of length N and produces a binary accept/reject decision (0 or 1) at the final position of a generated string of length P. The generation process starts from a fully masked string of P positions and iteratively unmasks positions over T denoising steps, with a planner deciding which positions to unmask at each step and a predictor sampling the corresponding symbols. Both the planner and predictor are implemented as finite-precision transformers with logarithmically growing width (D = O(log N)), and the model family is L-uniform, meaning a log-space Turing machine constructs the transformer for each input length.

## Method

The paper introduces an idealized MDM with two components—a planner that selects which positions to unmask (allowing resampling of already-unmasked positions) and a predictor that samples symbols at those positions—both implemented as unmasked finite-precision transformers. The core technical contribution is establishing equivalences between MDMs and two well-studied frameworks: (1) MDMs are equivalent to padded looped transformers (PLTs), where denoising steps map to loop iterations and generated tokens map to padding positions, and (2) MDMs can simulate parallel chain-of-thought (pCoT) transformers and vice versa, with polynomial overheads. These equivalences yield precise complexity-class characterizations: MDMs with O(1) denoising steps equal AC^0, MDMs with O(log^d N) steps equal AC^d (converging to NC as d→∞), and MDMs with poly(N) steps remain in P. A key separation result shows CoT[log N] ⊊ MDM[log N, N], formalizing the 'sequentiality bottleneck' of CoT: MDMs are provably more efficient at parallelizable problems (e.g., NC^1-complete regular languages) because their parallel generation avoids the step-by-step constraint of autoregressive decoding.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical benchmarks. The headline results are complexity-class characterizations: (1) MDMs are exactly equivalent to padded looped transformers (PLTs) up to a logarithmic factor in padding (Cor. 3.1); (2) MDMs with O(log^d N) denoising steps and poly(N) output space are equivalent to L-uniform AC^d, converging to NC as d→∞ (Cor. 3.4); (3) MDMs with constantly many steps are limited to AC^0 (Cor. 3.3); and (4) a strict separation CoT[log N] ⊊ MDM[log N, N] holds, meaning MDMs can solve NC^1-complete regular languages that CoT transformers with the same number of steps cannot (Cor. 3.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between MDMs and CoT transformers on parallelizable reasoning tasks. With logarithmically many model evaluations (log N steps), CoT transformers remain within TC^0, while MDMs can solve all NC^1-complete problems (including specific regular languages), establishing a strict expressivity gap (Cor. 3.7). The paper also shows MDMs and PLTs are essentially equivalent (Cor. 3.1), with the only overhead being a factor-D increase in padding where D = O(log N) is the model width. In the other direction, MDMs can simulate CoT with a quadratic padding blow-up (Thm. 3.3), and CoT can simulate MDMs with a linear-in-L (number of layers) overhead (Thm. 3.4). The practical implication is that MDMs are provably more efficient for highly-parallelizable problems (e.g., state-tracking, parallel arithmetic), while CoT transformers remain preferable for inherently sequential P-complete problems (circuit value, CFG membership, linear programming) where parallelism offers no asymptotic gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-developed expressivity analysis of autoregressive transformers (Li et al., 2024; Merrill & Sabharwal, 2024; London & Kanade, 2025) to masked diffusion language models, a paradigm that prior theoretical work had largely ignored in favor of convergence analysis of the factorized backward process (Feng et al., 2025; Li & Cai, 2025). The key methodological move is establishing a formal equivalence between MDMs and padded looped transformers (building on Dehghani et al., 2019; Saunshi et al., 2025; Svete et al., 2025), which allows the authors to import the entire circuit-complexity characterization of PLTs directly. The CoT connection (Wei et al., 2022) is used to make the results more intuitive and to identify a strict expressivity gap at logarithmic depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous characterization of what MDMs can and cannot compute, providing a principled framework for deciding when parallel generation offers genuine advantages over sequential CoT. The strict separation (CoT[log N] ⊊ MDM[log N, N]) formalizes the 'sequentiality bottleneck' and suggests MDMs are inherently better suited for parallelizable reasoning (e.g., state-tracking, arithmetic sub-expression evaluation), while P-complete problems remain sequential regardless of architecture. Key open questions include whether the idealized planner/predictor assumptions are learnable in practice, whether the separation persists at polylogarithmic depth, and how these theoretical bounds translate to empirical performance on real benchmarks.
