## Problem

Prior training-free fast sampling methods for diffusion and flow-matching models fail to simultaneously address two challenges: the stiffness of the sampling ODE (non-straightness of the velocity field) and dependence on the semi-linear structure of noise-based diffusion ODEs. Methods like DPM-Solver and DEIS handle stiffness via exponential integrators but require the semi-linear form, forcing additional error-introducing approximations (e.g., data prediction) when applied to flow-matching models that lack this structure.

## Input / Output

STORK is a drop-in training-free sampler that replaces the ODE solver in any pre-trained diffusion or flow-matching pipeline. It takes a text prompt (for conditional generation) and Gaussian noise as the initial condition, and produces images at resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) up to 1024×1024 (SANA-1.6B) or videos at 512×320 with 129 frames at 15 fps (Hunyuan). The method operates on both pixel-space and latent-space models, and the only tunable hyperparameter beyond NFE count is the number of internal sub-steps s (typically 9–24), which does not increase the actual NFE budget.

## Method

STORK builds on Stabilized Runge-Kutta (SRK) methods—specifically the second-order Runge-Kutta-Gegenbauer (RKG2) and fourth-order orthogonal Runge-Kutta-Chebyshev (ROCK4) schemes—which use orthogonal polynomials to achieve a region of absolute stability growing as O(s²), directly addressing ODE stiffness without requiring any semi-linear structure. The key practical innovation is replacing the s actual network evaluations per super-step with a single evaluation plus Taylor-expanded 'virtual NFEs' at intermediate sub-steps, where velocity derivatives are approximated via finite differences from previously stored evaluations. This reduces the NFE cost from O(s) per step to O(1) while preserving the stiff-solver stability properties. The resulting STORK-4 method is both a stiff solver and a structure-independent solver, making it directly applicable to both noise-prediction diffusion ODEs and velocity-prediction flow-matching ODEs without modification.

## Results

On conditional flow-matching image generation, STORK-4 achieves FID 6.526 at 9 NFEs with SANA-0.6B (512px) and FID 6.901 at 9 NFEs with SANA-1.6B (1024px), outperforming Flow-DPM-Solver++ (7.628 / 8.288) and Flow-UniPC (7.801 / 7.981) by a clear margin. On FLUX.1-dev (512px), STORK-4 reaches FID 10.952 at 9 NFEs versus 11.925 (Flow-DPM-Solver++) and 11.810 (Flow-UniPC). For video generation on Hunyuan (EvalCrafter), STORK achieves a final score of 219 at 7 NFEs, surpassing Flow-DPM-Solver++ (215) and Flow-UniPC (216). Inference wall-clock time and GPU memory are comparable to baselines (1.224s avg, 32.071 GB max allocated at 10 NFEs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STORK-4 consistently outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated task and NFE range. Against DPM-Solver++ (Lu et al., 2025), STORK-4 achieves a 1.1 FID improvement at 9 NFEs on SANA-0.6B (6.526 vs 7.628) and a 1.4 FID gap on SANA-1.6B at 1024px (6.901 vs 8.288); the advantage is most pronounced at low NFEs (7–10) and narrows as NFEs increase toward 50. Against UniPC (Zhao et al., 2023), STORK-4 leads by 1.3 FID at 9 NFEs on SANA-0.6B (6.526 vs 7.801) and by 2.7 FID on SD-3.5-Large/MS-COCO (15.017 vs 17.759). Against DEIS (Zhang & Chen, 2023), STORK-4 wins by 1.5 FID at 9 NFEs on PixArt-α/MJHQ-30K (6.712 vs 8.162). Against DDIM (Song et al., 2022), STORK-4 achieves 6.753 FID at 8 NFEs on CIFAR-10 versus DDIM's 23.260, a 3× improvement. On video generation (EvalCrafter), STORK wins the final score at 4–7 NFEs (e.g., 219 vs 215/216 at 7 NFEs) and leads in visual quality across all NFEs, though it trails slightly in temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

STORK is built directly on stabilized Runge-Kutta (SRK) methods from classical numerical analysis—specifically the fourth-order ROCK4 method (Abdulle, 2002) and the second-order RKG2 method (Skaras et al., 2021)—which use orthogonal polynomials (Chebyshev, Gegenbauer) to enlarge the region of absolute stability for stiff ODEs. The key conceptual departure from prior fast samplers like DPM-Solver and DEIS is that those methods exploit the semi-linear structure of noise-based diffusion ODEs via exponential integrators, making them structure-dependent and inapplicable to flow-matching models without ad hoc data-prediction workarounds. STORK instead uses SRK methods, which are inherently structure-independent, combined with a Taylor-expansion-based 'virtual NFE' trick to reduce the prohibitively high function-evaluation count of vanilla SRK. This positions STORK as the first training-free sampler that simultaneously addresses both stiffness and structure-dependence without requiring any special ODE form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being both a stiff solver and structure-independent, STORK offers a unified, drop-in replacement for DPM-Solver++ and UniPC across both noise-prediction and flow-matching architectures without any model-specific modifications, which is practically significant given the rapid shift toward flow-matching models (FLUX, SD-3.5, SANA). The consistent FID improvements across six model/dataset combinations and the first training-free video-generation benchmark (Hunyuan) broaden the method's applicability. However, the Taylor-expansion approximation introduces a convergence-order reduction (from O(h^k) to O(h^2) relative to the base SRK), and the temporal-consistency metric for video generation is slightly worse than baselines, suggesting the method may not fully capture long-range temporal dynamics. Open questions include whether even fewer NFEs (below 7) are achievable, whether the sub-step count s can be adapted per-timestep, and whether the approach extends to other generative paradigms beyond diffusion and flow matching.
