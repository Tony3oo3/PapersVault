## Problem

Identifying inputs that trigger specific latent features or problematic behaviours in language models is critical for AI safety, but existing methods fail to balance elicitation strength with linguistic fluency: black-box prompting produces natural text yet weak activations, while white-box gradient methods achieve strong activations at the cost of incoherent output. No standardised benchmark exists to systematically evaluate this tradeoff across diverse target features and safety-relevant tasks.

## Input / Output

Each task provides a text prompt containing a modifiable context section (10–100 tokens) and a target specification: either a specific SAE latent to maximally activate or a target token whose logit should be shifted relative to a source token. The method outputs a rewritten context section, scored on two axes: elicitation strength (normalised SAE activation or token logit difference) and fluency (cross-entropy constrained to the 3–9 range validated against human ratings at ρ=0.94). The benchmark comprises 715 tasks across SAE activation (205 latents from Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3.1-8B), story inpainting (500 stories), and backdoor trigger recovery (10 finetuned models).

## Method

The paper builds on Evolutionary Prompt Optimisation (EPO), which iteratively replaces tokens by backpropagating gradients to score candidate substitutions against a combined objective of target activation and cross-entropy fluency, tracing a Pareto frontier. Two novel variants address EPO's local-optima limitation: EPO-Assist periodically (every 50 iterations) feeds the current EPO population to GPT-4o, which proposes naturalised multi-token candidates that are then refined by further gradient steps, creating an LLM-to-gradient feedback loop. EPO-Inpainting periodically (every 15 iterations) freezes the top 25% max-activating tokens plus random anchor tokens and uses the bidirectional LLaDA-8B-Instruct diffusion model to inpaint remaining positions, acting as a fluency projection that preserves high-value tokens while restoring coherence. Both variants add negligible computational overhead since the helper models are invoked sparsely, and EPO-Inpainting achieves the best Pareto coverage across all three benchmark task categories.

## Results

On the SAE Activation task (205 latents), EPO-Inpainting achieves the best Pareto coverage, beating standard EPO on 60% of features and EPO-Assist on 56%, while all EPO variants outperform GPT-4o on ~97–99% of features and max-activating training examples on ~95–97% within the 3–9 cross-entropy fluency range. On Story Inpainting (500 stories), GPT-4o (given the target word) tops all methods at 87.5% win rate over EPO, while EPO-Assist (without being told the target) beats EPO 68.8% of the time. On the Backdoor task, EPO recovers single-token password triggers in 5.1% of cases (vs. 2.5% for GCG) but fails entirely on multi-token triggers (0%), and scores 1.1/10 on temporal trigger identification versus 0.0 for GCG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard EPO (Thompson et al., 2024), both proposed variants improve the fluency–elicitation tradeoff: EPO-Inpainting wins on 60% of SAE features and EPO-Assist on 57%, while on Story Inpainting EPO-Assist beats EPO 68.8% of the time (without being told the target word, unlike GPT-4o). Against GCG (Zou et al., 2023), all EPO variants dominate within the acceptable fluency range (3–9 cross-entropy): GCG achieves higher raw activations but the vast majority of its outputs fall outside the fluency threshold, yielding only 5–8% win rates over EPO variants. On the Backdoor task, EPO outperforms GCG on single-token password recovery (5.1% vs. 2.5%) and temporal trigger identification (1.1 vs. 0.0 out of 10), though both methods fail on multi-token triggers. The black-box GPT-4o baseline is decisively outperformed on SAE Activation (~97–99% win rate for EPO variants) but wins on Story Inpainting (87.5% over EPO) because it is given the full story and target word, a privilege the white-box methods do not have.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Evolutionary Prompt Optimisation (EPO, Thompson et al. 2024), which itself augments Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG, Zou et al. 2023) with a cross-entropy fluency penalty, by introducing two variants that periodically inject external model proposals to escape local optima: EPO-Assist uses an LLM (GPT-4o) as a mutation operator, and EPO-Inpainting uses the bidirectional diffusion model LLaDA (Nie et al. 2025) to replace low-activation tokens while freezing high-activation ones. Conceptually, the work bridges feature visualisation (originally developed for vision), discrete prompt optimisation, and sparse autoencoder interpretability, formalising the combined objective as 'context modification'—rewriting existing text to maximise a target latent activation while preserving linguistic fluency. The paper positions itself against both white-box gradient methods (GCG, EPO) and black-box prompting (GPT-4o), showing that neither alone achieves the fluency-elicitation balance needed for practical safety applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ContextBench is the first multi-task benchmark (715 tasks across SAE activation, story inpainting, and backdoor recovery) that jointly measures elicitation strength and linguistic fluency, providing a standardised testbed for the interpretability and AI safety communities. The practical stakes are significant: fluent, targeted context modifications could reveal backdoors, expose refusal-mediating features, and enable 'honey-potting' to detect audit-aware behaviour in deployed models. However, the paper's own results expose substantial limitations—EPO variants fail entirely on multi-token backdoor triggers, cross-entropy as a fluency proxy promotes generic or repetitive text, and the methods remain trapped in local minima. Open questions include whether richer latent targets (e.g., linear probes, as the paper demonstrates) or multi-token coordinated search strategies can close the gap to practical trigger recovery, and whether the benchmark can be extended to deceptive alignment and other safety-critical scenarios.
