## Problem

Reasoning capabilities in LLMs exhibit emergent behavior, making small proxy models (≤1B) unreliable for predicting large-model reasoning performance: they produce noisy signals and can even slope in the wrong direction on benchmarks like MATH500. Existing evaluation metrics (Acc., Pass@K) are misaligned with the pre-training objective (next-token prediction), and standard NLL fails to distinguish task-critical tokens from formatting artifacts, further degrading proxy signal quality at small scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a benchmark question, a frontier model's (GPT-4o) reasoning trace R^φ extracted via a structured JSON prompt, and the frontier model's per-token probabilities. It produces a single scalar RBRIDGE score (mean of MinMax-normalized, confidence-weighted NLL across tokens) that serves as a continuous proxy metric for dataset ranking and target-scale Acc./p@k prediction. The letter-level averaging of frontier model probabilities within each proxy-model token is a key design choice to handle tokenizer mismatches between the proxy and frontier models.

## Method

RBRIDGE improves proxy-to-target evaluation along two axes: (1) pre-training objective alignment by using only the frontier model's reasoning trace R^φ (excluding formatting artifacts like 'Final Answer:' and newlines) as the gold label for NLL computation, which is empirically 74.7% more in-distribution than the full ScalingBench format; and (2) task alignment by weighting each token's NLL by the frontier model's confidence in that token, computed as the mean letter-level probability within the token and then MinMax-normalized across the sequence. The final RBRIDGE score is the mean of these weighted NLL values. This dual alignment mechanism allows models as small as 3.7M parameters to achieve 80.8% decision accuracy in dataset ranking (100×+ compute savings) and the strongest proxy-target correlation (R² 0.82–0.97) across six reasoning benchmarks at 1B→32B scale, outperforming even 7–13× larger proxy models using the target metric directly.

## Results

On the primary 1B→13B proxy-target relationship study across six reasoning benchmarks, RBRIDGE achieves an average Train R² of 0.874 and Test MAE of 1.384, outperforming all six baselines. At 1B→32B scale, RBRIDGE maintains the best average Train R² (0.826) and Test MAE (1.481). In the dataset ranking experiment (<100M→1.2B), RBRIDGE achieves 80.8% decision accuracy across 25 pre-training datasets, reducing compute cost by 100.2×–733.4× relative to the best baseline. In zero-shot functional transfer (1B→7B), RBRIDGE achieves 5/5 perfect dataset ranking with average MAE of 2.490 (excluding one outlier at 9.716).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RBRIDGE decisively outperforms all six baselines across all three experimental settings. Against the target metric Acc./p@1 (Kulal et al., 2019), RBRIDGE achieves roughly 2.8× higher Train R² (0.874 vs. 0.304) and 2.7× lower Test MAE (1.384 vs. 3.709) at 1B→13B; the gap widens dramatically at 1B→32B where Acc./p@1's MAE explodes to 19.785. The iSFT baseline (Snell et al., 2024), which adds a supervised fine-tuning stage to intermediate checkpoints, also underperforms RBRIDGE by a wide margin (R² 0.302 vs. 0.846 at 1B→13B+SFT). Schaeffer et al.'s (2023) continuous metrics TED and MPCA show moderate improvement over Acc./p@1 but remain far below RBRIDGE; MPCA in particular is catastrophically unreliable with Test MAE values exceeding 200 on some benchmarks. In the dataset ranking experiment, RBRIDGE surpasses the Doremi-style metrics (Xie et al., 2023), RegMix metrics (Liu et al., 2025), and DataDecide's CF Accuracy (Magnusson et al., 2025) by 100×–733× in FLOPs efficiency to reach equivalent decision accuracy. Notably, even scaling the proxy model by 7×–13× and using the target metric directly fails to match RBRIDGE's 1B proxy performance, confirming that the evaluation scheme matters more than proxy model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

RBRIDGE extends the ScalingBench (ScB) framework, which proposed using frontier-model outputs as gold labels for NLL-based proxy evaluation, by (a) restricting the gold label to the reasoning trace R^φ (removing formatting artifacts that are out-of-distribution) and (b) introducing token-level weighting by frontier-model confidence to achieve task alignment. It departs from prior proxy-model work (DoReMi, RegMix, DataDecide) that relied on standard NLL or perplexity on non-emergent benchmarks, and from emergence-focused metrics (TED, MPCA, iSFT) that attempted to make discontinuous target metrics continuous. The paper positions itself as addressing a specific gap: existing methods fail for reasoning benchmarks where small models exhibit noisy, directionally-wrong signals due to emergent behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100×+ compute savings for dataset ranking and the ability to proxy 32B-scale reasoning performance from 1B models have direct practical impact on pre-training pipeline design, reducing both cost and environmental footprint. The zero-shot transfer of proxy-target functions across datasets is a novel capability that could enable rapid evaluation of new data sources without re-fitting. Key limitations include dependence on frontier-model quality for R^φ generation, validation only on OLMo architecture, and a single alternative dataset for the transfer experiment; whether the approach generalizes to long-CoT models or more complex multi-stage training pipelines remains open.
