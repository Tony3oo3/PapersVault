## Problem

Online 3D reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: offline approaches like VGGT achieve high-quality geometry through full cross-frame attention but cannot process streams in real time, while online methods like CUT3R achieve streaming inference but produce suboptimal reconstructions because adjacent frames' image tokens can only interact indirectly through shared state tokens, and camera pose estimation lacks access to global scene context. This paper tackles both limitations simultaneously to close the quality gap between online and offline reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a continuous RGB image stream, processing frames in sliding windows of size 4 with a stride of 2 (50% overlap); during training, images have variable aspect ratios with the longest edge fixed at 512 pixels. For each frame it outputs a local point map (3×H×W) defined in the frame's own camera coordinate system, a per-pixel confidence map, and a 7-dimensional camera pose (4D rotation quaternion + 3D translation). The system achieves 17.2 FPS on a single A800 GPU, and for overlapping window regions it selects the camera pose from the updated prediction and the point map with higher confidence as the final output.

## Method

WinT3R introduces two key mechanisms on top of a CUT3R-style state-token architecture. First, a sliding-window decoder (window size 4, stride 2) feeds all image tokens within the current window simultaneously into an alternating-attention decoder, enabling direct cross-frame token interaction and 50% overlap between consecutive windows for continuity. Second, a compact camera token pool stores a single 1536-dimensional token per frame (channel-concatenation of local and global camera tokens); a dedicated camera head with sliding-window masked attention conditions on all historical camera tokens to predict poses, providing global context at negligible storage cost compared to caching full attention keys/values. The point map is predicted by a lightweight convolutional head (adapted from MoGe) operating on local image tokens, while training uses a confidence-aware L2 point map loss plus a relative-pose L1 camera loss (following π³), with the model initialized from DUSt3R and trained in two stages (12-frame then 60-frame sequences).

## Results

WinT3R achieves state-of-the-art performance among online methods across all reconstruction benchmarks. On DTU, it obtains an Overall Chamfer distance of 2.738 (vs. CUT3R 3.199, StreamVGGT 2.823), and on ETH3D, 0.341 (vs. CUT3R 0.518, StreamVGGT 0.470). On scene-level 7-Scenes and NRGBD, WinT3R achieves Overall scores of 0.022 and 0.026 respectively, substantially outperforming CUT3R (0.025, 0.067) and StreamVGGT (0.038, 0.074). For camera pose estimation, WinT3R reaches AUC@30 of 81.34 on Tanks and Temples (vs. CUT3R 76.22, StreamVGGT 74.98), and runs at 17.2 FPS on KITTI, the fastest among all online methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WinT3R outperforms all online baselines on every reconstruction and camera pose benchmark. Against CUT3R (the closest online competitor), WinT3R reduces DTU Overall from 3.199 to 2.738 and ETH3D Overall from 0.518 to 0.341, while also improving NRGBD Overall from 0.067 to 0.026 and Tanks-and-Temples AUC@30 from 76.22 to 81.34. StreamVGGT, the second-strongest online method, is beaten on DTU Overall (2.823→2.738), ETH3D Overall (0.470→0.341), 7-Scenes Overall (0.038→0.022), and NRGBD Overall (0.074→0.026), though StreamVGGT slightly edges WinT3R on CO3Dv2 AUC@30 (84.68 vs. 84.61). Spann3R, SLAM3R, and Point3R are all substantially outperformed, with Spann3R showing the largest gap (DTU Overall 4.788 vs. 2.738). Among offline methods, VGGT still leads on DTU (1.289 vs. 2.738) and ETH3D (0.165 vs. 0.341), but WinT3R narrows the gap considerably while operating in real time at 17.2 FPS versus VGGT's 46.6 FPS on KITTI depth estimation. On video depth estimation (KITTI), WinT3R achieves the best δ<1.25 score (0.949) among online methods and the highest FPS (17.2), surpassing StreamVGGT (0.700, 13.7 FPS) and CUT3R (0.876, 12.9 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

WinT3R extends the CUT3R lineage of state-token-based streaming reconstruction by introducing two key architectural departures: a sliding-window mechanism that enables direct image-token interaction within a local neighborhood (addressing CUT3R's reliance on indirect communication through state tokens), and a compact camera token pool that replaces expensive full-attention over all historical frames with a lightweight 1536-dim per-frame representation for global pose estimation. The alternating-attention decoder borrows from VGGT, the convolutional point-map head from MoGe, and the confidence-aware loss from MASt3R, but the combination of local window attention with a global camera token pool is the paper's core architectural novelty. Compared to Spann3R and CUT3R, which suffer from geometric distortion due to over-reliance on memory/state tokens, WinT3R's design explicitly separates local geometric prediction (point maps via windowed image tokens) from global pose estimation (via the camera token pool).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WinT3R demonstrates that real-time (17 FPS) streaming 3D reconstruction can approach offline-quality results, narrowing the long-standing quality-speed gap that has constrained practical deployment in robotics and AR. The camera token pool is a particularly elegant contribution: a single 1536-dim vector per frame serves as a 'global memory' for pose estimation, drastically reducing storage and compute compared to caching full attention keys/values. However, the paper acknowledges that accumulated errors persist in very long sequences, and the sequential training requirement (frames must pass through the model in order) limits training efficiency compared to offline models. Open questions include whether the camera token pool can be made drift-resistant over thousands of frames and whether the sequential training bottleneck can be addressed through parallelization strategies.
