## Problem

Existing off-dynamics offline RL methods either penalize source rewards or filter out high-shift transitions, restricting policy optimization to low-dynamics-shift regions and preventing exploration of high-reward target states that lie outside those regions. No model-based approach exists for this setting that can learn accurate target dynamics from limited target data (5K transitions) augmented by abundant source data (1M transitions) with fundamentally different transition functions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two offline datasets with mismatched dynamics: a large source dataset (~1M transitions) and a small target dataset (~5K transitions, a 200:1 ratio), both collected by an unknown behavior policy. It produces a target-domain policy (state-to-action mapping) and a learned target dynamics model parameterized by a shared state encoder, a shared transition function, and domain-specific action encoders. The policy is trained on an enhanced dataset combining DARA-regularized source transitions, raw target transitions, and model-based rollouts generated from the learned target dynamics.

## Method

MOBODY learns target dynamics by decomposing the transition into a shared state encoder φ_E, a shared transition function φ_T, and separate action encoders ψ_src and ψ_trg that map domain-specific actions into a unified latent space, motivated by the observation that reaching the same next state requires different actions in each domain. Three losses jointly train the model: a transition loss (MSE on predicted next state), an encoder loss (matching the state-action latent representation to the next-state representation via stop-gradient), and a VAE-style cycle transition loss that regularizes the state representation and prevents mode collapse. For policy optimization, MOBODY performs model-based rollouts from the learned target dynamics to generate exploratory transitions, then trains the policy with a target Q-weighted behavior cloning loss that up-weights actions with high target-domain Q-values rather than uniformly imitating all offline actions or using advantage-weighted regression. Source data is incorporated via DARA reward regularization to penalize high-dynamics-shift transitions before being included in the enhanced training set.

## Results

On the ODRL benchmark with gravity and friction shifts (levels 0.1–5.0) across HalfCheetah, Ant, Walker2d, and Hopper, MOBODY achieves a total normalized score of 1427.26, a 44% improvement over the best baseline REAG (986.13). On kinematics and morphology shifts, MOBODY scores 1515.10, a 25% improvement over the best baseline MOPO (1205.70). MOBODY is best or second-best in 28 of 32 gravity/friction tasks and 29 of 40 kinematics/morphology tasks, with the largest gains appearing at extreme shift levels (0.1× and 5.0×) where baselines collapse to near-random performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOBODY outperforms all seven baselines on the gravity/friction shift benchmark, achieving a 44% total-score improvement over the strongest baseline REAG (1427.26 vs. 986.13) and 59% over SRPO (647.91). The gap widens at extreme shift levels: at 5.0× gravity, MOBODY scores 83.05 on HalfCheetah while SRPO collapses to −2.33, and at 0.1× friction, MOBODY reaches 57.53 versus REAG's 9.74. On kinematics/morphology shifts, MOBODY leads with 1515.10 total score (25% above MOPO's 1205.70), winning 29 of 40 individual tasks. On Adroit manipulation (Pen, Door), REAG narrowly edges MOBODY on the aggregate (348.28 vs. 333.10), but MOBODY is best or second-best in 6 of 8 sub-tasks and substantially outperforms DARA (251.84), IQL (300.82), and BOSA (184.93). Model-free baselines (IQL, TD3-BC) and reward-regularization methods (DARA, BOSA) are consistently outperformed, confirming that the model-based exploration via learned target dynamics is the primary source of MOBODY's advantage, especially under large dynamics shifts where conservative regularization fails to reach high-reward target states.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOBODY departs from the dominant paradigm in off-dynamics offline RL—reward regularization (DARA, REAG) and data filtering (BOSA, SRPO)—which restricts policy optimization to low-dynamics-shift regions and cannot explore high-reward target states outside those regions. It introduces the first model-based approach for this setting, building on MOPO's ensemble uncertainty framework for conservative rollouts and TD3-BC's behavior cloning regularization, but replaces the uniform BC with a target-Q-weighted variant inspired by AWR/IQL. The dynamics learning architecture, which uses separate action encoders per domain while sharing a state encoder and transition function in latent space, draws on representation learning ideas from TD-MPC2 and contrastive dynamics models but is specifically designed to handle the conditional next-state mismatch between source and target domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly learning target dynamics and using them for exploratory rollouts can substantially outperform conservative reward-regularization or filtering approaches, particularly when dynamics shifts are large (44% improvement over the best baseline on gravity/friction settings). This suggests a broader principle: in domain-adaptation RL, the bottleneck is often not regularization of source data but the inability to generate and evaluate target-domain transitions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to sparse-reward or goal-conditional settings (acknowledged by the authors), whether the DARA-based source regularization can be replaced by a more principled mechanism, and how the method scales to higher-dimensional or continuous-deployment scenarios where even 5,000 target transitions may be insufficient.
