## Problem

Existing expert-level compression methods for MoE LLMs rely on empirical heuristics (activation frequency, router scores, reconstruction loss) to identify critical experts, without a mechanistic understanding of why certain experts matter. The paper asks whether a small, distinct subset of experts plays an exceptionally critical role in the underlying forward-inference mechanisms of MoE LLMs, and if so, what that role is.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264018/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The profiling tool takes a pre-trained MoE LLM and any calibration dataset (no specific dataset is required, as SE distribution is data-agnostic) and produces a set of (layer, expert) index pairs identifying Super Experts. The identification uses a two-stage procedure: first detecting which layers are responsible for massive activation (MA) formation, then applying a dual threshold (P99.5 percentile and 0.1×a_max of down_proj output magnitudes) within those layers. The tool also outputs quantitative impact metrics including per-task accuracy drops, perplexity changes, and the Attention Sinks Decay Rate (D_sink) across all attention heads.

## Method

The paper discovers that a tiny subset of experts (<0.5% of all experts) consistently produces extreme activation outliers in their down_proj outputs, which propagate through residual connections to generate massive activations (MAs) in inter-layer hidden states. These Super Experts (SEs) are identified via a threshold criterion on down_proj output magnitudes within MA-formation layers, and their distribution is model-specific, data-agnostic, and invariant to post-training. The key mechanistic insight is that SEs are the primary source of the systematic outlier chain in MoE LLMs: the router assigns disproportionately high scores to SEs for the attention sink token (typically the first token), causing SEs to produce extreme activations through weight outliers in down_proj; these propagate as MAs via residual connections and ultimately manifest as attention sinks in the attention mechanism. Pruning SEs collapses attention sinks (D_sink > 90%), which destroys the model's ability to regulate attention score distributions and sentence termination, leading to complete performance collapse (Pass@1 → 0 on reasoning tasks) and repetitive degenerate outputs.

## Results

Pruning just 3 Super Experts (out of 6,144 total) from Qwen3-30B-A3B causes a 21.68% average accuracy drop on non-reasoning benchmarks and a 93.62% drop on reasoning tasks (average Pass@1 falls from 69.37 to 4.02), while randomly pruning the same number of experts yields less than 0.5% degradation. For DeepSeek-R1, pruning 10 SEs (out of 15,677) collapses the average score from 75.64 to 1.81 (97.61% drop), with AIME and Math-500 Pass@1 dropping to 0. The attention sink decay rate (D_sink) exceeds 90% across nearly all layers after SE pruning, confirming that SEs are the primary source of systematic outliers and attention sinks in MoE LLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is a discovery and characterization study rather than a method comparison, so the 'baselines' are control conditions: random pruning of an equivalent number of experts (globally) and layer-controlled (LC) random pruning (within the same layers as SEs). Across all three non-reasoning models, SE pruning causes 21–27% average accuracy drops, while both random baselines show changes within ±0.5%, confirming that the damage is specific to SEs and not a general consequence of removing any experts. On reasoning benchmarks (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-30B-A3B thinking mode), the contrast is even more extreme: SE pruning drives Pass@1 to 0–5% on AIME and Math-500, whereas random pruning maintains scores within 1–2% of the original. The paper does not directly benchmark against prior expert compression methods (e.g., M-SMoE, NAEE, MC), but positions SEs as a previously unidentified subset that existing importance metrics (activation frequency, router scores, reconstruction loss) would not flag, since SEs are defined by extreme activation outliers in down_proj rather than by routing frequency or output similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the systematic outliers framework established for dense Transformers (weight outliers, activation outliers, attention sinks) to the MoE setting, identifying a previously unknown mechanistic source: a tiny subset of experts whose extreme down_proj outputs generate massive activations and, through residual connections, drive attention sink formation. It departs from prior expert-level compression work (M-SMoE, NAEE, MC, MC-Suite), which relied on empirical heuristics like activation frequency or router scores to rank expert importance, by providing a causal, mechanistic explanation for why specific experts are irreplaceable. The SE profiling methodology builds directly on the massive activations literature (Sun et al., 2024) and the attention sinks framework (Xiao et al., 2023b), but the key conceptual advance is showing that in MoE LLMs, SEs are the *origin* of the entire systematic outlier chain rather than a downstream consequence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that fewer than 0.1% of experts are mechanistically load-bearing for MoE LLMs has immediate practical consequences: any expert compression strategy that does not explicitly preserve SEs will cause catastrophic failure, and the provided profiling tool makes this check tractable. More broadly, the finding that SEs are data-agnostic, stable across training stages, and tied to end-of-text token recognition (via SAE analysis) suggests they encode a fundamental architectural property rather than a training artifact, raising open questions about how they emerge during pre-training and whether balanced training regimes can eliminate the extreme imbalance. The paper does not address how to *replace* or *redistribute* SE functionality, leaving a gap for future compression work that must either protect SEs or find equivalent substitutes.
