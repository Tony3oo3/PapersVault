## Problem

Existing multimodal embedding models, whether CLIP-based or VLM-based, support only text as the human-machine interaction modality and rely on holistic image representations. They cannot accept visual prompts (bounding boxes, points, masks) to specify regions of interest, leaving them unable to perform entity-level retrieval while preserving global scene context — a capability that generative models have already explored but remains unexplored in representation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263896/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

VIRTUE takes an RGB image at 1344×1344 resolution, an optional visual prompt (bounding box, point, or mask), and a text query or task instruction. When no explicit prompt is provided, N=9 uniformly sampled points serve as a surrogate to extract entity-level cues. The output is a single unified embedding vector derived from the final hidden state of the last token in the LLM, which concatenates 256 segmentation embeddings (entity-level), variable-length vision embeddings (global context), and text embeddings in that order.

## Method

VIRTUE integrates a frozen SAM-2 segmentation model with a pretrained VLM (Qwen2-VL-2B/7B) via a novel segmentation-language connector. The segmentation model's prompt encoder processes visual prompts to produce a 64×64 feature map, which a Conv2D layer compresses from 4096 tokens to 256, followed by two MLP layers projecting to the LLM's hidden dimension. The LLM then ingests the concatenated segmentation-vision-text sequence and produces the final embedding for contrastive learning with InfoNCE loss and GradCache (batch size 1024). LoRA (rank 8) is applied to the LLM while the segmentation model, vision encoder, and vision-language connector remain frozen. The key architectural insight is that treating segmentation as structured entity-level features — rather than naively cropping bounding boxes — preserves inter-entity relations and global scene context while enabling precise region-specific grounding.

## Results

On the 36-task MMEB benchmark, VIRTUE-2B achieves 64.8 overall precision@1 (a +5.1-point gain over the best 2B baseline VLM2Vec-2B at 59.7), while VIRTUE-7B reaches 68.6 (a +2.0-point gain over UniME-7B at 66.6). On the newly introduced 5-dataset SCaR visual-interactive retrieval benchmark, VIRTUE-2B scores 30.4 overall (vs. 24.1 for VLM2Vec-2B, +6.3 points) and VIRTUE-7B scores 27.8 (vs. 26.3 for UniME-7B, +1.5 points). After 1k steps of SCaR-specific finetuning, VIRTUE-2B jumps to 56.2 and VIRTUE-7B to 56.9, outperforming the best finetuned baselines by +9.5 and +7.5 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIRTUE outperforms all 14 baselines on both MMEB and SCaR. On MMEB, VIRTUE-2B surpasses the strongest 2B competitor VLM2Vec-2B by 5.1 points overall (64.8 vs. 59.7), with the largest gains in VQA (+6.4) and Classification (+5.4); VIRTUE-7B beats UniME-7B (the best 7B baseline) by 2.0 points (68.6 vs. 66.6) and VLM2Vec-7B by 3.1 points. On SCaR, the visual-interactive advantage is more pronounced: VIRTUE-2B leads VLM2Vec-2B by 6.3 points (30.4 vs. 24.1), and after SCaR-specific finetuning the gap widens to 9.5 points (56.2 vs. 46.7). CLIP-based methods (CLIP, BLIP2, SigLIP, OpenCLIP, UniIR, Magiclens) are consistently outperformed by 10–40+ points on MMEB and 10–25 points on SCaR, confirming that VLM-based architectures with visual prompts are substantially stronger for compositional retrieval. Notably, VIRTUE also outperforms GME and LamRA (which share the same Qwen2-VL backbone) by 12.7 and 14.5 points on MMEB-7B, and by 20+ points on SCaR, demonstrating that the segmentation streamline—not just the VLM backbone—drives the gains. Cropping-based baselines (ReCLIP, +Cropping variants) consistently underperform VIRTUE on SCaR, validating that preserving global scene context alongside entity-level segmentation is critical for compositional retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIRTUE extends the VLM-based embedding paradigm (VLM2Vec, GME, LamRA) by integrating a pretrained segmentation model (SAM-2) as a parallel streamline that converts visual prompts—bounding boxes, points, or masks—into entity-level embeddings, which are then fused with global vision and text embeddings in the LLM. Prior embedding models, whether CLIP-based or VLM-based, accept only text instructions and holistic images; none natively process spatial visual prompts as inputs to the embedder. The training pipeline (InfoNCE with GradCache, LoRA on the LLM) follows VLM2Vec closely, so the architectural novelty is specifically the segmentation-language connector and the prompt-conditioned feature map that supplements the VLM's global vision encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIRTUE introduces a genuinely new interaction channel (visual prompts) for embedding models and provides SCaR, a 1M-sample benchmark that is the first to systematically evaluate visual-interactive retrieval, filling a gap the authors identify as entirely unexplored. The finding that VIRTUE's segmentation streamline makes it remarkably robust to catastrophic forgetting during domain-specific fine-tuning (64.8→64.9 on MMEB vs. VLM2Vec's 59.7→19.8) suggests the architecture is a more stable foundation for multi-task embedding models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video, whether larger segmentation models or alternative prompt types (e.g., scribbles) yield further gains, and whether the benchmark's GPT-4V-generated negatives fully capture the difficulty of real-world compositional retrieval.
