## Problem

Existing unlearnable example (UE) methods rely on empirical heuristics without a principled explanation for why they work. The prevailing linear-separability interpretation is incomplete: linear classifiers trained on UEs still achieve over 30% test accuracy on CIFAR-10, and some UEs (e.g., autoregressive poisons) are not linearly separable. The paper asks what underlying mechanism actually drives the generalization failure of models trained on UEs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a clean training dataset of RGB images with labels (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, ImageNet-subset at 224×224) and a shadow model (e.g., ResNet-18) whose feature extractor g is used to compute intra- and inter-class feature similarities during poison generation. It outputs per-sample perturbation vectors δ_i constrained to an l∞ ball of radius 8/255, which when added to the original images produce unlearnable examples. The shadow model is updated via cross-entropy in the inner loop, while the poisons are optimized via PGD in the outer loop.

## Method

The paper establishes that effective UEs consistently reduce mutual information (MI) between clean features g(X) and poisoned features g(X′), and proves (Theorem 5.1) that under a Gaussian-mixture assumption on per-class poisoned features, minimizing the conditional covariance Σ_Y of intra-class features upper-bounds and thus reduces I(g(X), g(X′)). Based on this, MI-UE introduces a novel loss L_mi that maximizes cosine similarity among intra-class poisoned features (reducing covariance) while minimizing inter-class cosine similarity (preventing class collapse), with an auxiliary Euclidean distance term weighted by ζ=0.1. The optimization follows a bi-level min-min scheme: the shadow model is trained with cross-entropy on poisoned data, and the poisons are updated via 10-step PGD attacks on L_mi per epoch. Empirically, MI-UE achieves the lowest test accuracy (9.95% on CIFAR-10, 1.17% on CIFAR-100, 1.03% on ImageNet-subset) across all victim architectures and remains effective under adversarial training with budgets up to 8/255.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, MI-UE achieves 9.95% test accuracy (84.50% accuracy drop from clean 94.45%), outperforming all baselines including TUE (11.25%), AP (11.21%), and EM (24.17%). On CIFAR-100, MI-UE reaches 1.17% test accuracy, below TUE (1.34%) and EM (2.09%). On ImageNet-subset, MI-UE achieves 1.03% test accuracy, the lowest among all methods, compared to EM (1.26%), SEM (4.10%), and REM (13.74%). Across all three datasets, MI-UE consistently produces the lowest test accuracy, indicating the strongest poisoning effectiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MI-UE outperforms all nine baselines on all three datasets. Against the original error-minimizing method EM (Huang et al., 2020), MI-UE reduces test accuracy from 24.17% to 9.95% on CIFAR-10 and from 1.26% to 1.03% on ImageNet-subset. Compared to the strong adversarial poison AP (Fowl et al., 2021), MI-UE achieves 9.95% vs. 11.21% on CIFAR-10 and 1.03% vs. 9.10% on ImageNet-subset. Against the most competitive baseline TUE (Ren et al., 2022), MI-UE edges ahead with 9.95% vs. 11.25% on CIFAR-10 and 1.17% vs. 1.34% on CIFAR-100. Under adversarial training defense (Table 4), MI-UE achieves 45.55% at AT-6/255, substantially outperforming SEM (86.82%), REM (81.91%), and ENTF (76.84%), while matching SEM at AT-4 (31.79% vs. 29.77%). On transferability across architectures (Table 3), MI-UE consistently yields the lowest accuracy on deep networks (ResNet-50: 9.98%, DenseNet-121: 9.93%) and shallow networks (LeNet-5: 10.80%, 2-NN: 17.82%), where methods like AP and AR degrade significantly (e.g., AP reaches 55.78% on 2-NN vs. MI-UE's 17.82%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the bi-level min-min optimization framework of error-minimization poisoning (Huang et al., 2020), replacing the cross-entropy poison loss with a mutual information reduction loss based on intra-class cosine similarity. It departs from the prevailing 'linear shortcut' explanation of UE effectiveness (Yu et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2024b) by demonstrating that linear classifiers still achieve over 30% accuracy on UEs, and instead proposes MI reduction between clean and poisoned features as the unifying mechanism. The theoretical analysis draws on domain generalization bounds involving MI (Zhao et al., 2022) and connects to the broader MI estimation literature (sliced MI, MINE, histogram-based estimators) to make the optimization tractable via a covariance-reduction surrogate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MI reduction perspective provides the first principled theoretical explanation for why UEs degrade generalization, moving the field beyond empirical heuristics and the incomplete linear-separability narrative. Practically, MI-UE achieves the lowest test accuracy across three datasets and nine architectures, and notably maintains strong poisoning under adversarial training budgets where prior robust UEs (REM, SEM) fail. However, the paper acknowledges that state-of-the-art tailored defenses (ISS, AVA) still recover accuracy above 80% for all UEs including MI-UE, leaving open whether truly unlearnable data can be constructed against all purification strategies. The moderate computational overhead (~1.5× EM) and sensitivity to the distance-term hyperparameter are practical considerations for adoption.
