## Problem

While VLMs have been enhanced for embodied reasoning and separately extended into VLA models for robot control, the critical gap between upstream VLM-based reasoning and downstream VLA policy learning remains poorly understood. Specifically, it is unclear which multi-modal data streams and reasoning abilities from VLM pretraining most effectively transfer to closed-loop robot manipulation, and how the domain shift between internet-scale data and robot-embodied observations affects this transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262236/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single-frame RGB observation (448×448, dynamic resolution up to 12 patches), a natural-language task instruction, and the current robot proprioceptive state. For VLA inference, it outputs an action chunk of length 4 (with 2 actions executed per step) generated via 10-step Euler integration of a learned flow-matching vector field starting from Gaussian noise. For embodied reasoning tasks, it additionally produces grounding coordinates normalized to [0,1000] (as bounding boxes or center points), spatial relationship descriptions, and multi-step planning text.

## Method

Vlaser builds on InternVL3 (InternViT + Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B) and introduces a flow-matching-based action expert that shares self-attention with the LLM while using non-causal attention for the VLA stream; robot state is encoded as a state token and noised actions as action tokens. Training is two-stage: first, supervised fine-tuning on the Vlaser-6M dataset (1.5M grounding, 1.2M RoboVQA, 500k spatial, 400k planning, and 2M in-domain simulation QA pairs) with standard autoregressive loss; second, VLA finetuning where the action expert is trained with a flow-matching denoising objective on robot episodes. The key empirical finding is that in-domain data annotated directly on robot simulation (SimplerEnv, RoboTwin) substantially accelerates VLA convergence and improves success rates, whereas out-of-domain embodied reasoning data—despite boosting benchmark scores—yields no clear VLA gain, revealing a significant domain gap between internet-scale embodied reasoning and robot-embodied control.

## Results

On the 12-benchmark embodied reasoning suite (Table 1), Vlaser-8B achieves an average score of 51.3, surpassing the best open-source baseline RoboBrain2.0-7B (37.0) by +14.3 points and the base InternVL3-8B (22.3) by +29.0 points. Vlaser-2B reaches 45.3 average, outperforming all small-scale MLLMs (best: RoboBrain2.0-3B at 35.3) and even exceeding Gemini-2.5-Pro (44.4) in the small-size category. On closed-loop WidowX manipulation (Table 2), Vlaser-All(2B) achieves 65.1% average success rate, setting a new state of the art over π0 (54.9%) and SpatialVLA (42.7%). On Google Robot (Table 3), Vlaser-All(2B) reaches 76.2% on Visual Matching and 59.0% on Variant Aggregation, competitive with Magma-8B (68.4%/62.6%) and π0 (58.3%/54.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vlaser-8B decisively outperforms all open-source embodied VLMs of comparable scale: it leads RoboBrain2.0-7B (37.0) by +14.3 and Embodied-R1-7B (38.9) by +12.4 in average score, with particularly large margins on spatial intelligence (VSI-Bench: 60.3 vs. 36.1/38.6) and planning (Ego-Plan2: 53.4 vs. 33.2/37.1). Against closed-source models, Vlaser-8B (51.3) surpasses GPT-4o (34.2) by +17.1 and approaches Gemini-2.5-Pro (44.4), exceeding it by +6.9. In the small-size category, Vlaser-2B (45.3) dominates all 2B–3B baselines, beating RoboBrain2.0-3B (35.3) by +10.0 and Qwen2.5VL-3B (31.6) by +13.7, and even edges out Gemini-2.5-Pro. On closed-loop WidowX manipulation, Vlaser-All(2B) achieves 65.1% average success, outperforming π0 (54.9%) by +10.2 and SpatialVLA (42.7%) by +22.4. On Google Robot, Vlaser-All(2B) is competitive with Magma-8B (68.4% vs. 76.2% Visual Matching; 62.6% vs. 59.0% Variant Aggregation) and π0 (58.3%/54.8%), while on RoboTwin bimanual tasks it achieves 67.5% average, surpassing InternVL3-2B (55.8%) and RDT-1B (36.8%) by substantial margins. A key finding is that out-of-domain embodied reasoning data (Vlaser-OOD) provides minimal VLA gains over the base model, whereas in-domain simulation-annotated data (QA, grounding, spatial) yields the largest improvements, highlighting a domain gap between internet-scale benchmarks and robot-specific observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vlaser builds directly on InternVL3 as its VLM backbone and adopts the flow-matching action expert design from π₀/π₀.5 (shared self-attention with non-causal attention for the VLA stream), positioning itself at the intersection of embodied VLMs (RoboBrain, Embodied-R1, Cosmos-Reason1) and VLA policy models (OpenVLA, RT-2, π₀). Its conceptual departure is the systematic empirical study of which VLM pretraining data streams actually transfer to downstream VLA fine-tuning—a question the authors identify as under-explored in the literature. The Vlaser-6M dataset curates and reorganizes existing public embodied datasets (grounding, spatial, planning, QA) plus 2M in-domain simulation-generated pairs, distinguishing it from prior embodied VLM datasets that focus primarily on out-of-domain internet-scale data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical finding—that out-of-domain embodied reasoning data improves benchmark scores but does not meaningfully accelerate VLA convergence or improve closed-loop success rates, while in-domain simulation data does—challenges the implicit assumption in the field that stronger embodied reasoning automatically yields better robot control. This has direct implications for how future embodied VLMs should be constructed: the domain gap between internet-scale observations and robot-embodied viewpoints must be explicitly addressed. Key limitations include the absence of real-robot experiments (all VLA evaluation is simulation-based in SimplerEnv and RoboTwin), and the finding is demonstrated primarily at 2B scale, leaving open whether the domain-shift conclusion holds at larger model sizes or with different action representations.
