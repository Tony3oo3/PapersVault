## Problem

Training neural eigenvalue methods requires large labeled datasets of operator eigenpairs, but generating these datasets with traditional solvers (Krylov-Schur, LOBPCG, etc.) is prohibitively expensive—often taking days for 10⁴–10⁵ samples. Existing data generation methods solve each matrix eigenvalue problem independently, ignoring the strong spectral similarities between operators derived from related physical phenomena, leading to massive computational redundancy. No prior method has exploited this inter-problem similarity to accelerate eigenvalue dataset generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N discretized Hermitian operator matrices A⁽ⁱ⁾ ∈ ℂⁿˣⁿ (from PDEs discretized via FDM or FEM) along with their corresponding parameter matrices P⁽ⁱ⁾ ∈ ℂᵖˣᵖ (e.g., diffusion coefficients, wavenumbers). The parameter matrices are consumed only for the sorting step via truncated FFT, while the operator matrices are the actual eigenvalue problems being solved. The output is a complete eigenvalue dataset: for each operator, the L smallest eigenvalues in absolute value and their corresponding eigenvectors, achieving relative residual precision of at least 1e-8 (effectively ground truth for neural network training). The method requires the parameter-to-operator mapping to be continuous for the sorting to be effective.

## Method

SCSF comprises two synergistic components: (1) a truncated FFT sorting algorithm that extracts low-frequency components (p₀=20) of parameter matrices and performs greedy nearest-neighbor sorting by Frobenius distance, reducing sorting complexity from O(N²p²) to O(N²p₀² + Np²log p) while achieving >98% sequence agreement with full greedy sorting; and (2) a Chebyshev Filtered Subspace Iteration (ChFSI) that uses the eigenpairs (Λ⁽ⁱ⁻¹⁾, V⁽ⁱ⁻¹⁾) from the previously solved problem as a warm-start invariant subspace. The Chebyshev filter of degree m=20 amplifies the target spectral interval via three-term recurrence, followed by QR orthonormalization, Rayleigh-Ritz projection onto the reduced subspace, and locking of converged eigenpairs. The key insight is that sorting ensures adjacent problems share close spectral distributions and invariant subspaces, so the inherited subspace from problem i-1 provides a high-quality initialization for problem i, dramatically reducing the number of Chebyshev filtering iterations needed. This transforms N independent eigenvalue solves into a sequential pipeline where each solution accelerates the next, achieving up to 3.5× speedup over the best standalone solver (ChFSI with random init) and up to 95× over Jacobi-Davidson.

## Results

SCSF achieves the lowest average computation time across all four operator datasets (Poisson, Ellipse, Helmholtz, Vibration) and all tested eigenvalue counts (L = 200–600). The headline speedup is up to 3.5× over ChFSI (the closest baseline) and up to 95× over Jacobi-Davidson on the Helmholtz dataset (L=600, dim=6400, tol=1e-8). On the Vibration dataset (dim=10000), SCSF reduces solve time from 1037 s (Eigsh) to 131.4 s at L=600. The advantage grows with matrix dimension: at dim=10000 on Poisson, SCSF is 20× faster than Eigsh and 3.7× faster than ChFSI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCSF outperforms all five numerical solver baselines (Eigsh, LOBPCG, KS, JD, ChFSI) on every dataset and eigenvalue count tested. The largest margins appear on the Helmholtz dataset, where SCSF is 8× faster than Eigsh, 20× faster than LOBPCG, 6× faster than KS, and 95× faster than JD (which fails to converge at L≥500); the smallest margin is on the Poisson dataset, where SCSF is only ~10% faster than Eigsh at L=200. Against ChFSI (the closest competitor, using the same Chebyshev filter but with random initialization), SCSF achieves a consistent 1.2–3.5× speedup, with the gap widening as L increases. In the paradigm comparison (Appendix E.6), SCSF's 1-minute solve time per operator matches Eigsh but eliminates the 20-hour dataset generation bottleneck required by the supervised NeurKItt model, while unsupervised methods (NeuralEF: 2 h, NeuralSVD: 3 h per operator) are orders of magnitude slower per-instance and achieve only 1e-2 relative residual versus SCSF's 1e-8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chebyshev Filtered Subspace Iteration (ChFSI) of Berljafa et al. (2015) from a single-problem solver to a sequential dataset-generation pipeline, adding a novel truncated-FFT sorting step that orders operators by low-frequency spectral similarity before solving. It departs from prior data-generation acceleration work (Wang et al. 2024, Dong et al. 2024), which targeted linear-system PDE datasets via Krylov subspace recycling, by addressing the eigenvalue setting where the relevant reusable information is invariant subspaces rather than Krylov bases. The sorting algorithm itself is new: by exploiting Parseval's identity to replace full-matrix Frobenius distances with truncated-FFT comparisons, it reduces greedy-sort cost from O(N²p²) to O(N²p₀² + Np² log p) while producing sequences >98% identical to the expensive full-metric sort.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eigenvalue dataset generation is a recognized bottleneck for training neural eigenvalue solvers and neural operators, often requiring days of compute; a 3.5× reduction (up to 95× vs. Jacobi-Davidson) directly lowers this barrier. The paper is transparent about limitations: SCSF requires Hermitian operators, a continuous parameter-to-operator mapping, and a fixed similarity metric (Frobenius norm of parameter matrices), all of which restrict applicability to non-Hermitian, nonlinear, or discontinuously parameterized problems. Open questions include whether operator-level (rather than parameter-level) similarity metrics would improve robustness, and whether the framework extends to non-Hermitian or nonlinear eigenvalue problems where the Chebyshev filter's spectral amplification guarantees no longer hold.
