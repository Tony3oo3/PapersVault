## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors degrade sharply on high-resolution images because standard practice resizes or center-crops inputs to fit fixed-size backbones, irreversibly discarding the high-frequency artifacts most indicative of synthesis. Additionally, detectors suffer from shortcut learning (compression-history biases, source-model cues) and lack fine-grained spatial awareness needed to detect localized forgeries such as AI inpainting within otherwise authentic images.

## Input / Output

The method accepts arbitrary-resolution RGB images (up to 64 MP) and internally splits them into a resized 224×224 global view plus K full-resolution 224×224 tiles that deterministically cover every pixel during inference (randomly sampled 1–16 tiles during training). It produces three outputs: a binary real/fake probability from an MLP head, a per-patch-token forgery probability map (TFL) for spatial localization of manipulated regions, and a scalar JPEG quality factor estimate (QFE) regressed from the aggregated local feature.

## Method

HiDA-Net is a dual-path detector built on a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone with a lightweight trainable refinement Transformer. The local path crops K native-resolution 224×224 tiles whose union covers the entire image, preserving high-frequency cues via spectral leakage (Dirichlet-kernel convolution) that resizing would irreversibly truncate; the global path provides semantic context from a single resized view. The Feature Aggregation Module (FAM) concatenates the global [CLS] token with an aggregated local feature (obtained by a single-layer Transformer encoder over all tile [CLS] tokens plus a learnable output token) and feeds the result to a classification MLP. Two auxiliary losses are trained jointly: Token-wise Forgery Localization (TFL) uses Random Patch Swap augmentation to create composite images with soft per-token labels, supervising a shared linear+Sigmoid head on every patch token to build spatial sensitivity to localized edits; and JPEG Quality Factor Estimation (QFE) regresses the true JPEG quality (from a pre-trained FBCNN estimator) via a 4-layer MLP on the local detail feature, explicitly disentangling compression noise from generative artifacts. The total loss is L_cls + α·L_tfl + β·L_qfe with α=β=1.

## Results

On the challenging Chameleon dataset (high-resolution, visually indistinguishable synthetic images), HiDA-Net achieves 79.10% accuracy when trained on the full GenImage dataset, surpassing the previous SOTA (AIDE at 65.77%) by 13.33%. On the newly introduced HiRes-50K benchmark (50,568 images up to 64 MP), HiDA-Net reaches 80.33% average accuracy, outperforming the next-best method SPAI (71.87%) by over 8%. On the standard GenImage cross-model benchmark, HiDA-Net with SDv1.4 VAE augmentation achieves 97.1% average accuracy, setting a new SOTA over C2P-CLIP (95.8%). On the DRCT benchmark, the method reaches 98.4% average accuracy, exceeding DRCT/SDv2 (96.6%) by 1.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiDA-Net decisively outperforms all 25 compared baselines on the Chameleon dataset, achieving 79.10% vs. AIDE's 65.77% (+13.33%) and UnivFD's 60.42% (+18.68%), with notably balanced fake/real accuracy (76.77%/82.20%) unlike methods that heavily favor one class. On HiRes-50K, HiDA-Net leads SPAI (the strongest competitor) by 8.46% on average and by up to 16.46% in the 2000–2500 px resolution bin (88.26% vs. 71.80%), while SPAI's accuracy degrades with increasing resolution but HiDA-Net remains stable. On GenImage, HiDA-Net with VAE augmentation (97.1%) surpasses C2P-CLIP (95.8%) and SAFE (95.6%), with the largest gap on ADM (92.7% vs. 96.4% for C2P-CLIP, but 86.2% without VAE). On DRCT, HiDA-Net (98.4%) exceeds DRCT/SDv2 (96.6%) by 1.8%, particularly on SDXL-DR (94.91% vs. 90.39%). The one notable loss is on the Mix Set AUC evaluation, where HiDA-Net (89.8%) trails SPAI (91.0%) by 0.2%, though the authors note this benchmark's maximum resolution (~1 MP) is far below HiRes-50K's range where HiDA-Net's advantage is most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant resizing paradigm (UnivFD, C2P-CLIP, FreqNet) that compresses high-resolution inputs to 224×224, and from partial-cropping approaches (TextureCrop, SAFE, PatchCraft, AIDE) that analyze only selected regions. It draws on the multi-crop idea from B-Free but replaces simple averaging with a learned Feature Aggregation Module that fuses full-coverage native-resolution tiles with a global semantic view, grounded in a Fourier-domain argument that cropping preserves high-frequency content via spectral leakage while resizing irreversibly truncates it. The auxiliary tasks (Token-wise Forgery Localization, JPEG Quality Factor Estimation) extend the reconstruction-based detection line (DIRE, DRCT, Aeroblade) by using VAE-reconstructed pairs for token-level supervision and by explicitly regressing compression quality to disentangle it from generative artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HiRes-50K benchmark (50,568 images up to 64 MP with aligned compression distributions) fills a genuine gap: prior high-resolution benchmarks like Chameleon top out around 1,500 px on the long edge, while real-world AI images on social platforms routinely exceed 4K. The 13% accuracy gain on Chameleon and 8% on HiRes-50K, together with the notably balanced fake/real accuracy (65/79 vs. the 1–2%/98–100% lopsidedness of most baselines), suggest that prior detectors were largely learning compression or dataset shortcuts rather than synthesis artifacts. The main open limitation is inference cost: full-coverage tiling scales linearly with image size, and the authors acknowledge that tile-fusion efficiency remains a barrier to real-time deployment. Whether the spectral-leakage argument translates to even more extreme resolutions (beyond 64 MP) or to video remains untested.
