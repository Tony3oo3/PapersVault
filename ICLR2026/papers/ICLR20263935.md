## Problem

Test-time scaling for deep search agents has not been systematically studied, and existing sequential scaling methods (e.g., budget forcing) degrade performance when over-applied in agentic search. While parallel scaling broadens exploration, it fails to improve exploitation—the agent cannot reliably identify the correct answer among many candidates. The paper asks how to optimally allocate test-time compute between search and verification, motivated by the observation that verifying a candidate answer in deep search requires far fewer tool calls than generating one (asymmetric verification).

## Input / Output

The system takes a complex natural-language search query (e.g., multi-constraint questions from BrowseComp requiring navigation across hundreds of web pages) and produces a final answer text selected from K independently sampled trajectories. Compute is measured in actual tool calls (web search + browsing invocations) rather than tokens, reflecting the agentic nature of the task. The verifier outputs a confidence score per candidate answer, which drives Best-of-K or Weighted Voting aggregation; the final output is the single highest-scoring answer string.

## Method

The paper introduces a unified framework for test-time scaling of deep search agents along three orthogonal dimensions: scaling target (search agent vs. verifier agent), scaling strategy (Max # Tool Call, Budget Forcing, Parallel Scaling), and aggregation metric (Maj@K, Best-of-K, Weighted Voting). The search agent operates under a ReAct scaffold with WebThinker's combined search-and-browse tool, while the verifier is the same model with a modified system prompt that instructs it to check a candidate answer against the question's constraints and assign a confidence score. The key mechanism is exploiting asymmetric verification: on BrowseComp, GLM-4.5 needs ~75 tool calls to search but only ~18 to verify, so shifting compute from additional search trajectories to verifier evaluation yields a 5× better accuracy-per-tool-call trade-off (e.g., +10 points with ~100 extra calls vs. ~500). By combining sequential scaling (budget forcing to reach Pass@1 peaks) with parallel scaling (32 trajectories for GLM-4.5) and verifier-based selection (Best-of-K or Weighted Voting with parallel verifier sampling), the authors produce 'Heavy' variants that push open-source models to competitive levels with proprietary systems.

## Results

GLM-4.5 Heavy achieves 54.0% on BrowseComp, 49.0% on BrowseComp-zh, 66.0% on GAIA, and 68.0% on xbench-DeepSearch, placing it on par with OpenAI o3 and OpenAI Deep Research. Tongyi-DeepResearch Heavy pushes further, reaching 69.0% on BrowseComp, 55.0% on BrowseComp-zh, 72.8% on GAIA, and 80.0% on xbench-DeepSearch. Qwen3-2507 Heavy and K2 Heavy gain approximately 20 and 13 absolute points respectively on BrowseComp over their base models. The key efficiency finding is that adding a verifier requires only ~100 extra tool calls to gain 10 accuracy points, versus ~500 extra calls for the same gain from search-agent scaling alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLM-4.5 Heavy (54.0% BrowseComp) essentially matches OpenAI o3 (55.0%) and surpasses OpenAI Deep Research (51.5%) on BrowseComp, while also edging out OpenAI Deep Research on xbench-DeepSearch (68.0% vs. 66.7%) and tying o3 (68.0% vs. 68.0%). On GAIA, GLM-4.5 Heavy (66.0%) trails OpenAI Deep Research (70.5%) and o3 (68.0%) by 2–4.5 points. Tongyi-DeepResearch Heavy (69.0% BrowseComp, 72.8% GAIA, 80.0% xbench-DeepSearch) surpasses all proprietary systems across every reported benchmark, including o3 and OpenAI Deep Research. Against the weaker proprietary baselines (Gemini 2.5 Pro at 7.6%, OpenAI o1 at 9.9%, GPT-5 at 19.8% on BrowseComp), the Heavy variants lead by 30–60+ points. Among open-source base models, GLM-4.5 Heavy gains +35 points over GLM-4.5 (19.0→54.0) on BrowseComp, Qwen3-2507 Heavy gains +21 points (8.0→29.0), and K2 Heavy gains +13 points (11.0→24.0), confirming that the asymmetric-verification scaling strategy transfers across model families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time scaling literature (Snell et al., 2024; Muennighoff et al., 2025) from narrow domains like math and coding—where verification is automated via unit tests or proof checkers—to open-ended agentic web search, where no such automated verifier exists. It operationalizes the asymmetric verification principle (Wei, 2025) by repurposing the search agent itself as an agentic verifier that performs targeted web searches to confirm candidate answers, rather than relying on a static LLM-as-judge score. The framework builds on ReAct-style scaffolding and the WebThinker search tool, and the aggregation strategies (Best-of-K, Weighted Voting, Majority Voting) follow standard inference-time scaling practice, but the key departure is the first quantitative analysis of compute allocation between an agentic searcher and an agentic verifier in a deep research setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-evidenced recipe for transforming open-source deep research models into 'Heavy' variants that match or exceed proprietary systems (e.g., GLM-4.5 Heavy at 54% on BrowseComp vs. o3 at 55%), which is significant for the open-source ecosystem. The finding that verification requires roughly 4× fewer tool calls than search on BrowseComp, and that ~100 verifier tool calls achieve what ~500 additional search tool calls accomplish, offers a concrete compute-optimal allocation principle. However, the current verifier is applied only at the end of trajectories; the authors note that step-level verification, mid-trajectory guidance, and integrating asymmetric verification into training remain open directions. The xbench-DeepSearch negative result—where solving and verifying are equally hard—importantly bounds the generality of the asymmetry assumption.
