## Problem

Existing circuit benchmarks test only shallow recognition tasks (component identification, multiple-choice Q&A) at a single abstraction level, leaving the fundamental engineering capability of deriving symbolic equations from visual circuit topology completely untested. Moreover, no benchmark evaluates the hierarchical nature of real engineering design, where engineers must seamlessly navigate between system-level block diagrams and component-level schematics, translating visual representations into mathematical models at each level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264131/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes circuit schematic images, block diagram images, and text questions, producing task-specific outputs: component/topology labels for Perception, symbolic transfer functions or nodal equations for Analysis, and transistor sizing/biasing specifications for Design. Symbolic equation evaluation uses a SymPy-based pipeline that parses both predicted and ground-truth expressions into symbolic trees, performs algebraic simplification and symbolic subtraction, and falls back to numerical validation at 100 random complex frequency points when symbolic comparison is intractable. Design tasks are validated by Ngspice simulation with the SkyWater 130nm PDK, and open-ended answers are judged by Gemini-2.5-Flash for mathematical equivalence rather than string matching.

## Method

CircuitSense introduces a hierarchical synthetic generation pipeline with two complementary generators. The circuit schematic generator extends the MAPS framework to an m×n grid supporting 18 component types (passive, sources, controlled sources, ideal op-amps as template subcircuits), enforcing electrical validity constraints (no floating nodes, degree-2 connectivity, single voltage source), and producing ground-truth transfer functions via Lcapy's Modified Nodal Analysis after SPICE simulation validation. The block diagram generator constructs control systems with configurable feedback/feedforward loops and computes overall transfer functions symbolically using Mason's gain formula, handling nested and non-touching loop combinations. The benchmark combines 2,986 curated textbook problems with 5,020 synthetic circuits across six hierarchy levels (resistor networks through system diagrams) and three task categories (Perception, Analysis, Design), enabling isolated evaluation of visual comprehension versus mathematical reasoning at each abstraction level.

## Results

On the CircuitSense benchmark (8,006 problems), closed-source MLLMs achieve over 85% accuracy on perception tasks (component detection, connection identification, function classification), with GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro reaching 94–100%. However, on the critical synthetic equation-derivation tasks (5,020 novel circuits with guaranteed symbolic ground truth), even the best model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, achieves only 19.06% overall accuracy, while all other models fall below 7%. On curated multiple-choice analysis problems, Gemini-2.5-Pro reaches 80.71% and Claude-Sonnet-4 reaches 69.67%, but these collapse to 70.32% and 34.76% respectively in open-ended format, and further to 19.06% and 6.29% on synthetic derivation—exposing a 61-point gap between pattern-matching and genuine symbolic reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.5-Pro dominates all three task categories: perception (98.3% avg), analysis (13–90% across subcategories), and design (36.38% schematic, 67.27% block, 51.35% hierarchical), outperforming GPT-4o (94–100% perception, 6–52% analysis, 10.52–36.36% design) and Claude-Sonnet-4 (91.3% perception, 9–73% analysis, 17.54–51.83% design). The most striking comparison is on synthetic equation derivation: Gemini-2.5-Pro's 19.06% overall is 3× higher than Claude-Sonnet-4 (6.29%) and ~4× higher than GPT-4o (4.98%), yet still represents catastrophic failure relative to its 80.71% on curated multiple-choice problems. Open-source models (InternVL3-78B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, GLM-4.5V, Gemma-3-27B) cluster between 3.5–5.0% on synthetic derivation and below 40% on curated analysis, with InternVL3-78B and GLM-4.5V also showing severe perception deficits (12–26% on function classification vs. 86–100% for closed-source). The paper confirms that symbolic reasoning capability is the strongest predictor of design task performance: Gemini-2.5-Pro's superior equation derivation directly correlates with its leading design scores, while models with weaker symbolic skills (e.g., GPT-4o at 16% transfer function analysis) show correspondingly poor schematic-level design (10.52%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of visual circuit understanding benchmarks (CIRCUIT, EEE-Bench, MMCircuitEval, AMSbench) that focus on single-level recognition tasks like component identification and multiple-choice Q&A. It departs from these by introducing hierarchical multi-level evaluation (six abstraction levels from resistor networks to system block diagrams) and, critically, by requiring open-ended symbolic equation derivation rather than answer selection. The synthetic generation pipeline builds on the MAPS framework for linear resistive circuits, extending it to full analog components and adding a novel block diagram generator using Mason's gain formula for ground-truth transfer functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a fundamental and quantified gap: MLLMs achieve 85%+ on visual perception but collapse below 19% on symbolic derivation, revealing that current models recognize circuit topology without understanding its mathematical structure. This has direct implications for AI-assisted EDA, where early-stage mathematical analysis (stability, noise, frequency response) is the primary value proposition. Open questions include whether the gap narrows with larger circuits (limited here to 12–15 components), whether dedicated symbolic reasoning modules can bridge the perception-to-derivation gap, and whether the hierarchical structure (block diagrams vs. transistor schematics) can be exploited for compositional reasoning in future models.
