## Problem

Inferring the intrinsic dynamics (constitutive laws and material parameters) of deformable objects from visual observations remains challenging: existing methods either depend on manually defined constitutive priors that fail to capture complex real-world behaviors, or learn black-box neural constitutive laws that lack interpretability and generalize poorly to novel scenarios. No prior approach can automatically discover symbolic, physically meaningful constitutive expressions directly from video while maintaining both interpretability and strong generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263260/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view video (400 frames for synthetic, 19 frames for real-world) along with camera extrinsic and intrinsic parameters as input; a static 3DGS representation is reconstructed from the first frame and bound to MPM particles. The output is a pair of symbolic constitutive laws (elastic and plastic) expressed as interpretable PyTorch code snippets, together with optimized continuous material parameters (e.g., Young's modulus, yield stress), which together drive a differentiable MPM+3DGS pipeline to produce 4D interactive dynamics renderable from arbitrary viewpoints.

## Method

VisionLaw formulates intrinsic dynamics inference as a bilevel optimization: at the upper level, GPT-4.1-mini is prompted as a physics expert to iteratively generate and refine symbolic constitutive law expressions via an evolutionary search (initialize → evaluate → select → correct → iterate), while a decoupled evolution strategy alternates optimization of the elastic and plastic components before a joint refinement phase to reduce search-space explosion. At the lower level, each candidate constitutive law is embedded into a differentiable MPM simulator coupled with 3DGS rendering; continuous material parameters are optimized via backpropagation against the observed video using a combined L2 and D-SSIM loss, and the resulting minimum loss and parameter trajectory serve as fitness and feedback for the upper-level LLM. This closed-loop design introduces physical inductive biases through LLM reasoning while grounding the search in visual evidence, yielding constitutive laws that are both interpretable (readable Python code) and generalizable to unseen views, temporal regimes, and novel 4D interaction scenarios.

## Results

On the NeuMA synthetic dataset (6 scenes), VisionLaw achieves an average Chamfer distance of 1.65 between simulated and ground-truth particle trajectories, outperforming NeuMA (2.86), NCLaw (12.86), and PAC-NeRF (114.80). VisionLaw wins on 5 of 6 scenes, with the largest margins on BouncyBall (1.08 vs. NeuMA's 1.78) and JellyDuck (5.19 vs. 10.96). On the Spring-Gaus real-world dataset (Bun, Burger), VisionLaw also achieves higher PSNR than both NeuMA and Spring-Gaus, confirming superior visual fidelity in real-world settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisionLaw outperforms all four baselines on the primary Chamfer distance metric. Against PAC-NeRF (avg. 114.80) and NCLaw (avg. 12.86), which rely on manually defined or predefined constitutive laws, VisionLaw achieves a 69× and 8× improvement respectively, demonstrating that fixed constitutive priors fail to capture complex material behaviors. The closest competitor is NeuMA (avg. 2.86), which learns neural constitutive laws from visual observations; VisionLaw improves by 42% on average and wins on 5 of 6 scenes, with the sole exception being SandFish (1.10 vs. 1.07, a negligible 0.03 gap). On the real-world Spring-Gaus dataset, VisionLaw surpasses both NeuMA and Spring-Gaus in PSNR, with Spring-Gaus's spring-mass model failing to capture nonlinear elasticity. Additionally, VisionLaw exhibits substantially lower PSNR variance across views compared to NeuMA, indicating that its LLM-driven physical inductive bias mitigates the overfitting that plagues purely neural approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PhysGaussian paradigm of integrating a differentiable MPM simulator with 3D Gaussian Splatting for physics-based rendering, and on NeuMA's formulation of learning constitutive laws from visual observations. It departs from prior work in two key ways: rather than estimating parameters within a fixed constitutive law (PAC-NeRF, GIC) or learning a black-box neural constitutive model (NeuMA, NCLaw), it uses LLMs as an evolutionary operator to discover symbolic constitutive law expressions, and it introduces a decoupled evolution strategy that alternates optimization of elastic and plastic components to reduce search complexity. The bilevel structure—LLM-driven symbolic evolution at the upper level and differentiable simulation-based parameter fitting at the lower level—unifies two previously separate concerns (law discovery and parameter estimation) into a single closed-loop framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that interpretable symbolic constitutive laws inferred from visual observations can outperform neural black-box approaches on both accuracy and generalization, suggesting that physical inductive biases from LLMs are a viable alternative to purely data-driven learning for dynamics inference. This opens a path toward physics-driven 4D interaction where the inferred dynamics are human-readable and transferable to novel scenarios. Key limitations include the computational cost of the evolutionary search (requiring many forward simulations and backward passes), reliance on a differentiable MPM simulator (though gradient-free alternatives are shown to work), and evaluation limited to single-object scenes with relatively simple geometries. Open questions include scalability to multi-material objects, more complex contact scenarios, and whether the LLM-driven approach can discover genuinely new constitutive laws beyond recombining known ones.
