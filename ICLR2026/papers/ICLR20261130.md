## Problem

Existing ensemble-based Thompson Sampling methods select ensemble members via a fixed index distribution (e.g., discrete uniform or standard Gaussian) that never adapts during the decision-making process. This ignores the fact that individual ensemble members vary in quality due to random initialization and prior functions, so a static sampling strategy cannot fully capture the posterior uncertainty. The paper asks whether the index distribution itself can be learned online from reward feedback to yield a more faithful posterior approximation.

## Input / Output

In the bandit setting, the agent receives a context vector x in R^d for each candidate action in a set of K arms and a binary reward r in {0,1} after selection; in the RL setting (MiniGrid), the observation is a 7x7x3 tile-based image encoding object ID, color, and agent orientation. The method outputs a selected action, per-ensemble-member Q-value estimates, and updated index-distribution parameters (Beta alpha,beta for discrete members or Gaussian mu,sigma^2 for continuous indices) that are consumed at the next iteration to resample which ensemble member acts.

## Method

Bayesian Ensemble (BE) reframes ensemble-member selection as an inner bandit problem: at each step the agent samples an index z from a learnable distribution p, uses the corresponding base model to pick an action, observes the reward, and then updates p via Bayesian inference (exact for Beta-Bernoulli conjugacy in ensemble+, variational for Gaussian indices in hypermodel). For reinforcement learning, BE-DQN maintains K Q-networks with Beta-distributed weights; a single Q-network (the argmax-weighted one) drives action selection to encourage exploration, while the target Q-value is computed as a weighted average across all K networks to suppress overestimation bias. Two theorems bound the variance of BE-DQN's Q-estimator between that of standard DQN (upper) and Ensemble DQN (lower), guaranteeing no worse stability than DQN while retaining ensemble-level variance reduction. The framework is modular: it can be bolted onto any existing ensemble-based Thompson Sampling or ensemble DQN method by simply replacing its fixed index distribution with the dynamically updated Bayesian one.

## Results

On the Neural Testbed (d=2,10,50), ensemble+(BEB) reduces cumulative regret by 37.0%, 12.8%, and 42.2% over ensemble+, while hypermodel(BEB) reduces regret by 69.8%, 22.8%, and 30.3% over hypermodel. On the real-world Yahoo!R6B dataset, hypermodel(BEB) achieves 50,322.1 cumulative clicks, outperforming the best non-BEB baseline (hypermodel at 49,676.8) by 1.28% and the strongest non-ensemble method (Gradient TS at 47,033.7) by 6.99%. On MiniGrid RL tasks, BE-DQN achieves the highest average reward across all five environments (e.g., 0.35±0.066 on LavaGapS5, 0.118±0.226 on MultiRoom-N2-S4), surpassing the SOTA UAAC method in every setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEB consistently outperforms its corresponding base methods: ensemble+(BEB) beats ensemble+ by 37.0–42.2% regret reduction on Neural Testbed and 8.7% on Mushroom, while hypermodel(BEB) beats hypermodel by 22.8–69.8% on Neural Testbed and 4.8% on Mushroom. On Yahoo!R6B, hypermodel(BEB) (50,322 clicks) surpasses hypermodel (49,677), Gradient TS (47,034), MC Dropout (45,907), ε-Greedy (43,645), Greedy (39,423), and Random (25,434). In MiniGrid RL, BE-DQN wins against all four baselines (DQN, E-DQN, RE-DQN, UAAC) across all five environments, with the largest margin on LavaGapS5 (0.35 vs. 0.178 for E-DQN, a 97% improvement) and MultiRoom-N2-S4 (0.118 vs. 0.042 for E-DQN, a 181% improvement). BE-DQN also significantly outperforms the SOTA POMDP method UAAC in every MiniGrid environment, confirming that dynamic Bayesian index updating provides a clear advantage over static weighting schemes. The only notable trade-off is computational: hypermodel(BEB) incurs ~20% extra wall time due to variational inference for the continuous index, whereas ensemble+(BEB) adds negligible overhead thanks to exact Beta-Bernoulli conjugate updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ensemble-based Thompson Sampling (ensemble+, hypermodel) and Ensemble DQN by treating the selection of an ensemble member as an inner bandit problem, dynamically updating the index distribution via Bayesian inference on observed rewards. Prior methods (Osband et al., 2018; Dwaracherla et al., 2020; Anschel et al., 2017) maintain a fixed uniform or standard Gaussian index distribution throughout training, implicitly assuming all ensemble members are equally informative. BE departs from this by coupling the index distribution to reward feedback, using conjugate Beta updates for discrete ensembles and variational inference for continuous (hypermodel) indices, thereby forging a direct link between member selection and the posterior over reward parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a lightweight, drop-in enhancement to existing ensemble methods that consistently reduces regret across synthetic and real-world bandit settings with minimal overhead (near-zero for conjugate Beta updates, ~20% for variational inference). However, the real-world gains on Yahoo!R6B are modest (~1.3% over hypermodel), and the theoretical variance analysis is restricted to zero-reward MDPs with stationary TAE variance, limiting its generality. Open questions include whether the Beta-distribution assumption on binary rewards generalizes cleanly to continuous or multi-class reward spaces, and whether the adaptive index distribution can be extended to non-stationary environments where member quality shifts over time.
