## Problem

Sequential thinking-aware image generation, where a reasoning trace is produced autoregressively before the image, can paradoxically degrade output quality because vague or incorrect reasoning propagates errors into the visual synthesis. Existing benchmarks evaluate only the final image against the prompt, making it impossible to diagnose whether the intermediate reasoning is the source of failure. This paper identifies the root cause as poor cross-modal alignment between generated text and image, and proposes both a diagnostic benchmark and a parallel generation paradigm to resolve it.

## Input / Output

For editing tasks the model receives an input image (encoded to 1024 discrete tokens via MAGVIT-v2 at 512×512) plus a text instruction; for generation tasks it receives only a text prompt. The output is a jointly generated pair: a reasoning trace (text, up to 256 tokens via the LLaDA tokenizer) and a 512×512 output image, both decoded simultaneously through a shared denoising trajectory rather than one after the other. The interleaved token sequence uses explicit sentinels and task tags (<|thinkgen|> or <|thinkedit|>) to enable full bidirectional cross-modal attention between the two output modalities.

## Method

MMaDA-Parallel represents both text and image as discrete tokens in a single interleaved sequence and applies a unified masked-token diffusion objective with modality-specific timestep weighting (w_text(t)=1/t, w_img(t)=1) so that both modalities are denoised in parallel with bidirectional attention at every step. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on 150K quadruplets (input image, instruction, reasoning trace, output image) where reasoning traces are synthesized by Qwen-2.5-VL, followed by ParaRL—a trajectory-level reinforcement learning strategy that computes CLIP-based semantic alignment rewards at sparsely sampled intermediate denoising steps (s=3) rather than only on the final output, using a diffusion-adapted GRPO objective. The key insight is that cross-modal alignment emerges progressively along the denoising trajectory, so stepwise reward signals provide a denser and more effective optimization signal than output-level supervision alone. Decoding uses dual modality-specific schedulers: a linear reveal with semi-autoregressive confidence-based sampling for text (blocks of 64 tokens, 128 steps) and a cosine reveal with global confidence-based sampling for image (30 steps).

## Results

On ParaBench, MMaDA-Parallel with ParaRL achieves an Output Alignment score of 59.8, a 6.9-point improvement over the strongest open-source thinking-aware baseline Bagel (52.9), while maintaining comparable text and image quality (Overall 71.5 vs. 71.2). The model narrows the gap to closed-source systems: GPT-4o scores 69.5 on Output Alignment and Gemini-2.5 scores 63.4, while MMaDA-Parallel reaches 59.8. Ablations confirm that parallel decoding (vs. sequential) improves Output Alignment from 48.9 to 51.5, and trajectory-level ParaRL further lifts it to 59.8 over output-level RL (53.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary open-source thinking-aware baseline Bagel (w/ think), MMaDA-Parallel wins decisively on Output Alignment (59.8 vs. 52.9, +6.9 pts) and Overall (71.5 vs. 71.2), while Bagel retains a slight edge on Text Quality (82.0 vs. 80.4), Image Consistency (76.7 vs. 73.4), and Image Quality (81.5 vs. 81.2); the authors note Bagel was trained on a dataset roughly three orders of magnitude larger. Compared to Show-o* (tuned), MMaDA-Parallel outperforms on every reported metric, with the largest gap on Output Alignment (59.8 vs. 48.9). Against closed-source GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5, MMaDA-Parallel trails on all metrics—GPT-4o leads by 9.7 pts on Output Alignment (69.5 vs. 59.8) and 14.4 pts on Overall (85.9 vs. 71.5)—but the gap is substantially smaller than that of other open-source models. On image-only benchmarks (GenEval, WISE, RISEBench, GEdit-Bench), MMaDA-Parallel consistently outperforms its sequential counterpart MMaDA-Sequential (e.g., GenEval Overall 0.71 vs. 0.68; WISE Overall 0.47 vs. 0.44) but remains well behind Bagel (GenEval 0.88; WISE 0.52), confirming that the parallel framework's primary advantage is cross-modal alignment rather than raw image fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MMaDA (Yang et al., 2025a), a unified discrete-diffusion framework for multimodal generation, and LLaDA (Nie et al., 2025), which introduced confidence-based parallel decoding for text. It departs from the dominant sequential thinking-aware paradigm (Bagel, GoT, Image-CoT) where chain-of-thought reasoning is generated autoregressively before image synthesis, instead proposing a purely parallel diffusion architecture where text and image tokens are denoised simultaneously with bidirectional cross-modal attention at every step. The training pipeline combines supervised fine-tuning on VLM-generated reasoning traces with a novel trajectory-level reinforcement learning strategy (ParaRL) that adapts GRPO to discrete diffusion by assigning CLIP-based semantic alignment rewards at intermediate denoising steps rather than only at the final output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and counterintuitive failure mode—sequential reasoning can actively degrade image generation in ~23% of cases—supported by the new ParaBench benchmark that evaluates cross-modal alignment, a dimension absent from prior benchmarks. The parallel diffusion paradigm offers a principled architectural alternative to autoregressive pipelines, and ParaRL demonstrates that trajectory-level supervision provides a denser, more effective training signal than output-level RL. However, the practical impact is currently limited by the MAGVIT-v2 tokenizer bottleneck (acknowledged in Appendix A, where swapping to amused-VQ via Lumina-DiMOO substantially closes the gap), and the model remains well behind closed-source systems like GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5 on absolute quality metrics. Open questions include whether the parallel paradigm scales to higher-resolution generation, whether the CLIP-based trajectory reward generalizes beyond the 150K-sample training set, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation.
