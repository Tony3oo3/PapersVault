## Problem

Reasoning LLMs generate tens of thousands of chain-of-thought tokens, causing quantization errors to accumulate and degrade accuracy far more than in short-generation tasks. Existing PTQ methods face a trade-off: channel-wise scaling (AWQ) is fast but insufficient at suppressing outliers, while rotation-based methods (QTIP, SpinQuant) achieve higher accuracy but introduce 25–30% inference overhead from expensive orthogonal transforms. No prior method simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art quantization fidelity and near-zero runtime overhead for long-generation reasoning workloads.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (LLaMA-2/3, Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1-distilled) and a small calibration set of 2048 sequences (2048 tokens each, drawn from WikiText2, C4, and RedPajama) to optimize per-layer transforms. It outputs INT4 block-wise quantized weights (group size 128) accompanied by 8 sets of independent Givens rotation angles (up to 64 non-overlapping pairs per 128-channel group) and per-channel scaling factors. At inference, the inverse rotation-scaling transform is applied to FP16 activations via a fused CUDA kernel before standard INT4 GEMM, adding less than 10% latency over AWQ.

## Method

ParoQuant introduces a scaled pairwise rotation transform T(W) = (∏_{t=1}^{K} R(P_t, Θ_t)) · diag(α) · W that combines K=8 independent Givens rotations with channel-wise scaling per 128-channel group. The key insight is that only ~10% of channel pairs (those with large magnitude differences) are needed to match a full orthogonal rotation's outlier-suppression capability, and constraining pairs to be mutually non-overlapping makes all rotations fully parallelizable on GPU. Optimization proceeds layer-wise in two stages: first, rotation angles and scaling factors are optimized to minimize layer output error; second, weights and quantization parameters (s, z) are fine-tuned in a QAT-like manner to absorb residual outliers. A single fused CUDA kernel exploits three levels of parallelism—across tokens, channel groups (assigned to CUDA blocks), and rotation pairs (assigned to threads)—keeping the activation tensor in shared memory and rotation parameters in registers, enabling multiple sequential rotations to be applied with a single memory load.

## Results

On reasoning tasks (MMLU-Pro, GPQA Diamond, AIME-24/25), ParoQuant achieves an average accuracy of 61.9%, improving by 2.4% over AWQ (59.5%), 6.3% over EfficientQAT (55.6%), and 0.9% over QTIP (61.0%). On perplexity, ParoQuant achieves SOTA among linear quantization methods across all model sizes (e.g., 5.73 WikiText2 / 7.27 C4 on LLaMA-3-8B vs. AWQ's 5.92 / 7.42). In terms of efficiency, ParoQuant is only ~10% slower than AWQ in decoding throughput while matching QTIP's accuracy at 15–30% higher throughput (e.g., 112 vs. 95 tokens/s on LLaMA-3-8B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParoQuant consistently outperforms AWQ across all evaluation axes: +2.4% average on reasoning tasks, +0.9% on non-reasoning tasks, and lower perplexity (e.g., 5.73 vs. 5.92 WikiText2 on LLaMA-3-8B), at only ~10% additional decoding overhead. Against QTIP (the SOTA vector quantization method), ParoQuant matches or slightly exceeds accuracy (+0.9% avg on reasoning, +0.2% on non-reasoning) while running 15–30% faster in decoding throughput (e.g., 112 vs. 95 tokens/s on LLaMA-3-8B). ParoQuant substantially outperforms EfficientQAT by 6.3% on reasoning tasks and 0.7% on non-reasoning tasks, with better perplexity across all model sizes. On perplexity, ParoQuant also beats QuIP# (5.73 vs. 5.81 on L3-8 WikiText2) and SpinQuant (5.73 vs. 5.83), and is comparable to OmniQuant on the limited LLaMA-2-7B results. In the W4A4 extension (Appendix A.3), ParoQuant outperforms SpinQuant by a large margin (6.62 vs. 7.13 WikiText2) and is comparable to FlatQuant (6.62 vs. 6.51), while also beating MR-GPTQ on MXFP4 (6.75 vs. 6.99 WikiText2).

## Relation to Prior Work

ParoQuant sits at the intersection of two established lines of LLM quantization research: channel-wise scaling (AWQ, SmoothQuant) and rotation-based outlier suppression (QuIP, QuIP#, QTIP, SpinQuant, QuaRot). It departs from prior rotation-based methods by replacing full orthogonal matrices or fixed Hadamard transforms with a series of sparse, optimizable, and mutually independent Givens rotations, motivated by the observation that only a small fraction of channel pairs in a full rotation contribute meaningfully to outlier suppression. The algorithm-system co-design—constraining rotations to be independent so they parallelize across GPU threads—distinguishes ParoQuant from SpinQuant (which merges rotations into preceding layers) and Hadamard-based methods (which have inherent cross-channel dependencies), positioning it as a middle ground between AWQ's speed and QTIP's accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As reasoning LLMs generate tens of thousands of chain-of-thought tokens, quantization error accumulation becomes a first-order concern that prior PTQ methods (designed for short-generation tasks) do not adequately address. ParoQuant's practical impact is that it offers a near-drop-in upgrade to AWQ pipelines with <10% overhead while closing most of the accuracy gap to QTIP, making it immediately deployable in existing inference frameworks. Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to 2-bit or sub-4-bit regimes, whether the preliminary W4A4 results can be further improved with dedicated tuning, and whether the independent-rotation constraint remains optimal as hardware parallelism characteristics evolve.
