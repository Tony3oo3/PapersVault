## Problem

Long-context LLMs exhibit sharp retrieval failures as context length grows, yet the dominant inference-time scaling strategy—generating additional 'thinking' tokens—shows rapid diminishing returns and provably cannot recover buried signals. The paper formalizes this as 'score dilution' in static self-attention, proving that avoiding vanishing target probability requires a target-distractor logit margin scaling as Ω(log T), a requirement that autoregressive decoding with fixed weights cannot reliably satisfy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long context (up to ~10^5 tokens) paired with a task question, performing a single O(T²) prefill to cache all key and value tensors before any adaptation begins. During the adaptation phase, only short random spans (k=128 tokens) of the context are used per gradient step, keeping the KV cache length fixed at T rather than growing it. The output is a standard final answer generated after N_TTT query-only updates, using the same prompt templates and decoding protocol as the in-context and thinking baselines.

## Method

Query-only TTT (qTTT) performs one full forward pass to cache K/V, then applies N_TTT lightweight gradient steps that update exclusively the query projection matrices W_Q while keeping all other parameters and the KV cache frozen. Each step computes next-token prediction loss on a randomly sampled span of length k ≪ T and backpropagates only into W_Q, which provably moves q_i toward the needle key k_{j*} and away from the attention-weighted mean, directly increasing the target-distractor logit margin. Under FLOP-matched budgets (T_think ≈ 2·N_TTT·k), this reallocates compute from growing the KV cache with thinking tokens to reshaping attention access against existing keys, yielding 12–14 point average improvements over thinking tokens on LongBench-v2 and ZeroScrolls across Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B models.

## Results

Under FLOP-matched inference-time compute budgets, qTTT delivers consistent and substantial gains across all model sizes (Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B) and both benchmarks. On LongBench-v2, qTTT improves average accuracy from 27.0% (in-context) to 39.6% for Qwen3-4B (+12.6 pts), and from 32.3% to 48.8% for Qwen3-8B (+16.5 pts). On ZeroScrolls, the corresponding gains are +14.1 pts for Qwen3-4B (18.4% → 32.5%) and +10.5 pts for Qwen3-8B (24.1% → 34.6%). The largest improvements appear on retrieval-heavy tasks such as Multi-Document QA (30.0% → 46.0% for Qwen3-4B) and Long Dialogue History (20.5% → 43.6%), while summarization tasks show smaller, more modest gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the chain-of-thought 'Thinking' baseline (Wei et al., 2022), qTTT consistently wins on average across both benchmarks and all model sizes: on LongBench-v2, qTTT achieves 39.6% vs. 33.5% for Thinking (Qwen3-4B) and 48.8% vs. 42.3% (Qwen3-8B); on ZeroScrolls, 32.5% vs. 23.9% (Qwen3-4B) and 34.6% vs. 30.0% (Qwen3-8B). The gap widens with context length, as Thinking shows rapid diminishing returns while qTTT maintains gains. Against self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023) under strict FLOP-matching (Table 8/9, Qwen3-4B), qTTT outperforms SC-8 (39.7% vs. 36.6% on LongBench-v2; 32.5% vs. 28.2% on ZeroScrolls) and decisively beats SC-16 and SC-32, which degrade sharply as per-sample token budgets shrink. Beam search also trails qTTT under equal budgets due to correlated beams and imperfect ranking. The one exception is Single-Document QA, where SC-32 (51.8%) narrowly edges qTTT (48.0%), suggesting that when single-run accuracy is already high, majority voting over many samples can be competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the test-time training (TTT) paradigm (Sun et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021; Hardt & Sun, 2024) for long-context retrieval by restricting gradient updates to query projections only, while keeping the KV cache frozen from a single prefill. It departs from both standard TTT (which updates all parameters and invalidates the cache) and from the dominant inference-time scaling strategies (chain-of-thought thinking tokens, self-consistency, best-of-n) by arguing that decoding more tokens with unchanged static attention is provably insufficient to overcome score dilution. The theoretical contribution—formalizing score dilution and proving an Ω(log T) target-distractor margin requirement—provides a new lens on why long-context LLMs fail, complementing prior empirical observations of the 'lost in the middle' effect and needle-in-a-haystack degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that for long-context retrieval and multi-hop reasoning, a small number of query-only gradient updates (e.g., 32 steps on 128-token spans) is a more effective use of inference-time FLOPs than generating thousands of thinking tokens, without altering pre-training, architecture, or data. Open questions include: (i) the paper evaluates a single (k, N_TTT) operating point, and budget schedules across span size and step count remain unexplored; (ii) gains are task-dependent, with smaller improvements on summarization where generation quality rather than retrieval is the bottleneck; (iii) developing lightweight predictors for when qTTT should be preferred over decoding-based scaling would make the approach deployable in production. The theoretical result that no amount of autoregressive decoding with fixed weights can recover a buried target beyond an ε-fraction of signal is a strong negative result that should reshape how the community thinks about inference-time compute allocation for long contexts.
