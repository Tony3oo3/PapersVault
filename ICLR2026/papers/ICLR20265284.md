## Problem

Prior 4D human-scene reconstruction methods either rely on constrained multi-sensor environments or perform sequential, separate optimization of camera poses, human motion, and scene geometry, ignoring the mutual refinement possible between these components. This separation leads to physically implausible reconstructions with inconsistent human-scene contacts, foot penetration, and sliding. No prior method jointly optimizes all three in a single stage from casual monocular web videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265284/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video (sequence of RGB frames) as input, with no requirement for known camera intrinsics or ground-truth annotations. It outputs 4D global human motion as per-frame SMPL parameters (pose, shape, and world-coordinate transformation) for all detected humans simultaneously, a dense 3D scene point cloud on metric scale, and full camera poses (intrinsics and extrinsics). The metric scale is achieved through the human prior loss, eliminating the need for global scale alignment.

## Method

JOSH is a general single-stage optimization framework that jointly refines camera poses, global SMPL parameters for all humans, and dense scene depth maps using a unified loss combining scene reconstruction (3D/2D correspondence), human prior (temporal smoothness, SMPL regularization, 2D keypoint reprojection), and two key human-scene contact losses. The contact scene loss (L_c1) constrains predicted human contact vertices to align with nearby scene points, while the contact static loss (L_c2) penalizes sliding by enforcing that persistent contact points remain fixed in world space across frames. Initialization leverages off-the-shelf models (HMR2.0/WHAM/VIMO for local human meshes, DROID-SLAM/MonST3R/MASt3R for scene geometry, BSTRO for per-vertex contact labels, DEVA for human segmentation), and the focal length is jointly optimized alongside the local root depth to handle unknown intrinsics. JOSH3R, an end-to-end variant built on MASt3R's siamese architecture with an added human trajectory head, is trained on ~460K pseudo-labeled frames from 20 hours of web videos annotated by JOSH, achieving 15.4 FPS real-time inference as a speed-accuracy trade-off.

## Results

On SLOPER4D, JOSH₃ (VIMO + MASt3R initialization) achieves 120.0 WA-MPJPE, 438.3 W-MPJPE, and 1.7% RTE for global human motion, while reducing chamfer distance to 5.31 m and ATE to 3.21 m for scene reconstruction. On EMDB, JOSH₃ sets a new state-of-the-art with 68.9 WA-MPJPE and 174.7 W-MPJPE, outperforming the previous best (TRAM) by 21.6% in W-MPJPE. Physics plausibility metrics also improve dramatically: foot floating rate drops from 9.0% (SynCHMR*) to 2.9%, and jittering drops from 123.9 to 7.1 (×10⁻³ m/s³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JOSH outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluated metric and dataset. Against the primary 4D reconstruction baseline SynCHMR* (Zhao et al., 2024), JOSH₃ reduces W-MPJPE by 61% (438.3 vs. 1125.4) and chamfer distance by 70% (5.31 vs. 17.76 m) on SLOPER4D, while cutting foot floating rate from 9.0% to 2.9%. For global human motion estimation on EMDB, JOSH₃ (174.7 W-MPJPE) beats TRAM (222.4), WHAM (335.3), SLAHMR (776.1), and all other baselines including PromptHMR-Vid (216.5) and GVHMR (274.9). For dense scene reconstruction, JOSH₃ achieves 5.31 m chamfer distance on SLOPER4D versus 12.35 m for MASt3R, 13.28 m for MonST3R, and 21.33 m for DROID-SLAM, with ATE of 3.21 m vs. 22.47 m (MASt3R) and 30.00 m (DROID-SLAM). The consistent wins across all three datasets (SLOPER4D, EMDB, RICH) and both human and scene metrics confirm that joint optimization of camera, human, and scene yields substantial gains over any sequential or single-task approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

JOSH departs from prior 4D human-scene reconstruction methods (SynCHMR, HSR, Luvizon et al.) that perform sequential optimization of camera poses, scene geometry, and human motion, instead unifying all three into a single joint optimization stage bridged by human-scene contact constraints. For the end-to-end JOSH3R model, it builds directly on MASt3R's siamese architecture, adding a lightweight human trajectory head in parallel with the scene reconstruction heads. The framework is deliberately agnostic to initialization, plugging in state-of-the-art local human mesh recovery (HMR2.0, WHAM, VIMO) and dense scene reconstruction (DROID-SLAM, MonST3R, MASt3R) as interchangeable front-ends, positioning JOSH as a general optimization layer rather than a monolithic pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JOSH demonstrates that joint optimization with contact constraints can substantially improve both human motion and scene reconstruction simultaneously, and that pseudo-labels generated from ~20 hours of web videos can outperform ground-truth annotations from curated datasets (EMDB) for training end-to-end models—a finding with broad implications for scalable data generation in 4D reconstruction. Key limitations include dependence on off-the-shelf initializations (poor initializations can degrade results even after optimization), the requirement that contact points be visible in 2D, and failure modes when no human-scene contact exists. Open questions include whether scaling web-video pseudo-labels further can close the remaining gap between JOSH3R and the optimization-based JOSH, and whether the framework extends to more complex interactions (e.g., dynamic objects, non-rigid scene elements).
