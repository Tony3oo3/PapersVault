## Problem

Training LLMs for operations research faces two critical gaps: outcome-based rewards suffer from the credit assignment problem (a correct final answer can reinforce flawed intermediate reasoning), and conventional discriminative process reward models are myopic, scoring steps in isolation and missing the long-range interdependencies inherent in OR modeling (e.g., a variable definition's validity may only become apparent after subsequent constraints are written). No existing method provides holistic, trajectory-level process supervision grounded in solver feasibility for the OR domain.

## Input / Output

The policy model takes a natural-language OR problem description and produces a structured 9-step trajectory (problem restatement, sets/parameters, decision variables, objective, constraints, mathematical model summary, nonlinear handling, final model, and executable Python code using the COPT solver), with each step enclosed in <step>...</step> tags. The co-evolved GenPRM takes the full problem plus the complete student trajectory as a single long-context input and outputs a structured verdict for each of the 9 steps (CORRECT/INCORRECT) accompanied by a 1–3 sentence explanation, enabling it to retroactively flag early steps whose errors only manifest in later code execution.

## Method

StepORLM introduces a self-evolving co-evolutionary loop between a policy model (Qwen3-8B) and a generative process reward model (GenPRM). At each iteration, the policy generates k candidate trajectories on a 13K hard-problem subset; each trajectory is evaluated by a dual-feedback signal: a definitive solver verdict (outcome) and a holistic GenPRM critique (process). The GenPRM is generative rather than discriminative—it first performs chain-of-thought reasoning over the entire trajectory before assigning step-level verdicts, allowing it to detect cascading errors (e.g., flagging a locally valid but globally unimplementable DFJ subtour formulation as incorrect because the code step fails). Preference pairs are constructed by prioritizing solver-verified solutions and, when outcomes tie, by process-score difference; these feed a Weighted DPO (W-DPO) objective where solver-distinguished pairs receive weight 1.0 and process-only pairs are weighted by the absolute difference in GenPRM scores, stabilizing training relative to step-wise DPO. The GenPRM is simultaneously refined via SFT on solver-consistent samples, creating a positive feedback loop. Training proceeds in two stages: a warm-up SFT on 50K solver-verified synthetic instances, followed by three self-evolving iterations, with the full pipeline requiring approximately 10² GPU-hours on 8×H100s.

## Results

StepORLM (8B parameters) achieves a macro-average Pass@1 of 81.4% across six OR benchmarks, outperforming all zero-shot generalist LLMs (best: OpenAI o3 at 80.3%, DeepSeek-V3 671B at 75.4%) and all agentic methods (best: OptiMUS-v0.3 at 67.1%). With the co-evolved GenPRM as an inference-time verifier (Best-of-4), StepORLM+GenPRM pushes the average to 85.6%, with particularly large gains on ComplexOR (+9.9%) and IndustryOR (+9.5%). The GenPRM also generalizes to other models, boosting ORLM (8B) from 65.0% to 75.0% average Pass@1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StepORLM (8B) decisively outperforms all zero-shot generalist LLMs: it beats OpenAI o3 (80.3%) by 1.1 points, Gemini-2.5-Pro (78.9%) by 2.5 points, and DeepSeek-V3 671B (75.4%) by 6.0 points on average Pass@1, while being 84× smaller than the 671B models. Against specialized fine-tuned OR models, StepORLM surpasses ORLM (8B, 65.0%) by 16.4 points and LLMOPT (14B, 65.7%) by 15.7 points. All agentic baselines built on GPT-4o are substantially outperformed, with the best (OptiMUS-v0.3 at 67.1%) trailing StepORLM by 14.3 points. The co-evolved GenPRM further extends the lead: StepORLM+GenPRM reaches 85.6% average, and when applied to ORLM it lifts performance from 65.0% to 75.0% (+10 points), demonstrating the verifier's model-agnostic generalization. On the hardest benchmarks (ComplexOR, IndustryOR), StepORLM+GenPRM achieves 61.1% and 61.9% respectively, exceeding the best zero-shot model (o3: 72.2% and 76.2%) only on ComplexOR but leading all other methods by 5+ points on both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the generative process reward model (GenPRM) paradigm from Zhao et al. (2025) to the solver-based operations research domain, where supervision must be grounded in numerical feasibility rather than answer-string matching. It departs from standard discriminative step-wise PRMs (which suffer from myopia in interdependent OR modeling) and from outcome-only RL (which suffers from credit assignment) by introducing a co-evolutionary loop between a policy model and a GenPRM, coupled with a Weighted DPO objective that aggregates trajectory-level process quality into a scalar weight. The self-evolving training structure draws on rStar-Math-style iterative refinement, while the data synthesis pipeline and solver-verified filtering extend the ORLM and SIRL lines of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The co-evolved GenPRM's ability to serve as a universal inference-time verifier—boosting an unrelated model (ORLM) by 10% average Pass@1—suggests that holistic process reasoning about OR formulations is a transferable skill, not merely a co-adapted artifact. Open questions include whether the co-evolution loop scales beyond three iterations or to non-linear/MILP-heavy problem classes, whether the 13K hard-subset selection generalizes to open-ended OR queries, and whether the W-DPO weighting mechanism is necessary or whether simpler trajectory-level DPO suffices at larger scale. The 8B-vs-671B performance gap also raises the broader question of how much domain-specific process supervision can substitute for raw model scale in structured reasoning tasks.
