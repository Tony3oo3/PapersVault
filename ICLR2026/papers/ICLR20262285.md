## Problem

In modern retrieval scenarios such as multi-hop QA, table QA, and text-to-SQL, no single corpus item is self-complete: a small subset of items must collectively cover the query. Standard top-K dense retrieval (e.g., ColBERT/PLAID with MaxSim) ranks items independently, so redundant passages fill the K budget while critical query aspects go uncovered. The open challenge is to retrieve a near-optimal covering subset in sublinear time over the corpus, rather than exhaustively scoring all items at each greedy selection round.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query encoded into a set of unit-norm contextual atom embeddings (via BERT) and a pre-built multi-replica IVF index, where each corpus item is stored as a set of augmented token vectors [x; -1] projected through R random hyperplanes and clustered into B centroids with quantized residuals. The output is an ordered subset S of K corpus items, where the selection order reflects the greedy marginal-gain sequence. Unusually, the query representation is state-dependent: at each of the K rounds, the query atom vectors are augmented with the current coverage value F(S, q), so the effective query changes as items are selected, while all corpus-side index structures remain static and query-agnostic.

## Method

DISCO formulates collective retrieval as maximizing a monotone submodular facility-location coverage objective F(S, Q) = Σ_q max_{x ∈ ∪_{c∈S} X_c} q^T x, then makes the per-round marginal gain amenable to ANN retrieval via two key transformations. First, a lifted (d+1)-dimensional vector space encodes the evolving coverage into the query side (q̂_S = [q; F(S,q)]) while keeping corpus vectors static (x̂ = [x; -1]), expressing the marginal gain as a sum of hinge functions [q̂_S^T x̂]+. Second, R random hyperplane projections with a sign-based feature map Φ_w approximate each hinge as a plain dot product with probability ≥ 1/2 per hyperplane, and taking the max over R ≥ log(|Q|/δ) replicas recovers the exact hinge with probability ≥ 1−δ, yielding a (1−1/e−δ) approximation guarantee. At query time, a six-stage pruning pipeline (coarse IVF probe → centroid scoring → replica pooling → fine-grained cross-replica scoring → residual reconstruction → full-precision marginal gain) progressively narrows candidates from the full corpus to a small set C_3, from which the item with the true maximum marginal gain is selected and the query state is updated for the next round.

## Results

DISCO achieves the best coverage-vs-efficiency trade-off across all four main datasets (MS-MARCO, HotpotQA, Fever, Pooled), matching Exact Greedy and Lazy Greedy in average coverage F_K while being at least 100× faster on MS-MARCO. On HotpotQA with gold labels (|S_gold|=2, K=2), DISCO attains the lowest Error(F) and highest MAP among all seven baselines, closely matching Exact Greedy and Lazy Greedy in retrieval quality. DISCO consistently outperforms all IR baselines (PLAID, MUVERA, WARP) in coverage, with PLAID the strongest IR competitor and MUVERA (single-vector) the weakest. The approximation quality of the random-projection marginal gain (G_{w_{1:R}}) matches Exact Greedy coverage even with as few as R=5 hyperplanes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four submodular greedy baselines, DISCO matches Exact Greedy and Lazy Greedy in coverage quality (Error(F) and MAP on HotpotQA) while being at least 100× faster on MS-MARCO, since it replaces the O(|C|) per-round marginal gain scan with a sublinear ANN search. Stochastic Greedy and Lazier-than-lazy Greedy perform worst among all methods due to their query-agnostic uniform random pruning, which degrades coverage significantly. Against the three IR baselines, DISCO consistently outperforms in average coverage F_K: PLAID (the strongest IR method, using multi-vector IVF with centroid-first pruning) is the closest competitor but still lags DISCO in coverage because it optimizes independent MaxSim rather than collective coverage; MUVERA (single-vector encoding) performs worst among IR methods; WARP falls in between. The key distinction is that IR baselines retrieve top-K items that each independently cover the query, whereas DISCO selects a subset that collectively covers the query, avoiding redundancy and capturing multi-hop evidence that no single passage contains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-vector dense retrieval lineage (ColBERT, PLAID, MUVERA, WARP) by replacing the independent top-K MaxSim objective with a collective coverage objective drawn from facility-location submodular maximization. The key technical departure is a two-step approximation of the marginal gain: first, a 'lifted' vector augmentation that absorbs the state-dependent coverage into the query representation, and second, a random hyperplane projection (building on Charikar's LSH) that converts the resulting hinge-of-dot-product into an indexable dot-product form. The indexing architecture adapts PLAID's centroid-residual IVF but adds multiple projection replicas and a six-stage progressive pruning pipeline tailored to the coverage objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and growing gap in RAG pipelines: multi-hop QA, table QA, and text-to-SQL all require evidence scattered across multiple items, yet standard retrievers score items independently and waste budget on redundant top-K hits. The demonstrated >100× speedup over exact greedy while matching coverage makes submodular coverage maximization practical for first-stage retrieval at corpus scale. Open questions include incorporating fairness and diversity into the coverage objective, handling evolving corpora, and whether the loose per-hyperplane approximation guarantee (p ≥ 0.5) degrades in adversarial or high-dimensional regimes beyond the tested settings.
