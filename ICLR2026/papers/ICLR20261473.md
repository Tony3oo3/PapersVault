## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods—both deformable and explicit parameterizations—fail under fast motion with large inter-frame displacements, where Gaussian attributes (color, opacity, scale, rotation) collapse during training and fast-moving objects blur or vanish entirely. The root cause is that reconstruction loss is dominated by static background Gaussians, and frame-by-frame rasterization creates cross-frame interference that prevents a single shared Gaussian from being optimal at multiple timestamps simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261473/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video (31 cameras at 640×360, 30 FPS, 150 frames per scene) and produces real-time novel-view renderings of dynamic scenes at 104 FPS. Rather than storing per-Gaussian attributes for every frame, the 4D representation stores only explicit spatiotemporal positions plus a lightweight feed-forward network (1261 MB total), enabling reconstruction of arbitrary time intervals without materializing the full sequence. Positions are initially gathered from a Realtime-4DGS early-training run and then refined frame-by-frame before being fed to the attribute-prediction network.

## Method

SPIN-4DGS decouples position estimation from attribute learning in two stages. First, spatiotemporal Gaussian positions (x,y,z,t) are collected via Realtime-4DGS time-slicing and refined frame-by-frame with rasterization loss (densifying salient points, pruning redundant ones), explicitly separating Gaussians by timestamp to eliminate cross-frame interference. Second, a lightweight feed-forward network—comprising a 4D multi-resolution hash encoder (Instant-NGP extended along the temporal axis, L=16 levels, F=4 channels, hash map 2^21) and four separate three-layer MLP decoder heads—predicts all Gaussian attributes (scale via exp, rotation via normalized quaternion, 48-dim spherical harmonics, opacity via sigmoid) from Mip-NeRF-contracted and time-normalized positions. Attributes are stored implicitly in network weights rather than per-Gaussian, yielding a shared representation that enforces spatiotemporal consistency while avoiding the memory cost of explicit per-position optimization. Training uses the standard 3DGS L1+D-SSIM loss with Adam, separate learning rates per parameter group, and 40K iterations.

## Results

On the CMU Panoptic Sports dataset (six fast-motion sports scenes), SPIN-4DGS achieves an average PSNR of 30.11 dB and SSIM of 0.93, outperforming the strongest baseline D3DGS (28.70 dB) by +1.41 dB and the best explicit method Realtime-4DGS (28.38 dB) by +1.73 dB. The largest per-scene gain is on Basketball, where SPIN-4DGS reaches 30.05 dB versus D3DGS's 28.22 dB (+1.83 dB). SPIN-4DGS also renders at 104 FPS with 1261 MB storage, compared to D3DGS's 760 FPS but 1994 MB storage, and requires no external supervision (e.g., segmentation masks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPIN-4DGS wins on all six CMU Panoptic Sports scenes against every baseline. Against the strongest external-supervision method D3DGS (28.70 avg PSNR), SPIN-4DGS gains +1.41 dB on average and +1.83 dB on Basketball, while also using less storage (1261 vs. 1994 MB) and requiring no segmentation masks. Against the best explicit method Realtime-4DGS (28.38 avg PSNR), the margin is +1.73 dB, with the largest per-scene gap on Tennis (+1.64 dB). Deformable baselines (4DGaussian 27.53, MoDec-GS 27.23, Grid4D 27.06) trail by 2.6–3.1 dB on average and visibly blur or lose fast-moving objects. The explicit 4D-Rotor-Gaussians (27.16) and TC3DGS (27.88) also fall well behind. On the Neu3DV benchmark (smaller motions), SPIN-4DGS still leads with 32.19 avg PSNR vs. Realtime-4DGS's 32.01 and D3DGS's 31.04, and on MeetRoom it reaches 32.04 avg PSNR vs. Realtime-4DGS's 30.47, confirming generality beyond extreme fast-motion settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPIN-4DGS builds on the 3DGS rendering pipeline (Kerbl et al., 2023), the Instant-NGP multi-resolution hash encoding extended to 4D (Müller et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2025), and Mip-NeRF coordinate contraction (Barron et al., 2021), while using Realtime-4DGS (Yang et al., 2024a) solely for initial spatiotemporal position estimation. Its key conceptual departure from both deformable 4DGS (4DGaussian, Grid4D, MoDec-GS) and explicit 4DGS (Realtime-4DGS, 4D-Rotor-Gaussians) is decoupling Gaussian attribute learning from position: rather than sharing a single attribute vector across timesteps (which causes cross-frame interference during frame-by-frame rasterization), SPIN-4DGS predicts all attributes as a feed-forward function of explicit (x,y,z,t) coordinates, breaking temporal coupling at the attribute level. This positions the work as a reparameterization of 4DGS that retains explicit positions but moves temporal appearance modeling into a shared implicit network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete, well-documented failure mode in 4DGS—attribute collapse under large inter-frame displacements—that has limited the method's applicability to fast-motion scenarios. The plug-and-play compatibility (reusing positions from D3DGS or Realtime-4DGS and retraining only attributes yields +2.49 dB gains) suggests the attribute-learning bottleneck is broader than any single method, making the implicit decoder a potentially reusable component. Limitations include dependence on sufficiently dense initial positions (sparse outdoor scenes or very small objects remain challenging) and the need for a refinement stage that adds training time. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences or larger scenes without proportional increases in hash map size, and whether the 4D hash encoder can be replaced by lighter alternatives without sacrificing the observed fidelity.
