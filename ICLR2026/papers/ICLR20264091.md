## Problem

LLMs store vast knowledge in their parameters but recall it unreliably, particularly for rare or long-tail facts that appear sparsely in training corpora. Existing approaches to teaching models new knowledge—simple repetition, paraphrasing, or fixed-template synthetic QA—exhibit limited effectiveness and plateau in downstream performance as synthetic data scale increases. The paper asks: given a closed body of knowledge, how can we systematically and scalably train models to internalize facts with robust generalization rather than rote memorization?

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a source document (e.g., a Wikipedia article or financial disclosure) and a strategy prompt as input, and produces a set of diverse synthetic training documents as output. The two-stage design means the model first generates document-specific learning strategies (e.g., 'create a timeline of events,' 'explain the concept via analogy'), then applies each strategy independently to produce separate synthetic documents. At pre-training scale, the synthetic data is mixed with generic pre-training data at a 1:1 ratio, and training uses continued pre-training hyperparameters (lr=3e-4, batch size 4M tokens) rather than standard fine-tuning settings.

## Method

Active Reading is a two-stage synthetic data generation framework where the model itself proposes diverse learning strategies for a given document, then instantiates each strategy to produce a separate synthetic training document. Two prompt variants are used: task-agnostic (generate general study strategies for the material) and task-specific (first imagine downstream evaluation questions, then generate strategies targeting those question types, yielding both more relevant and more diverse data). The key insight is that self-generated strategies produce substantially more diverse data (measured by lower Self-BLEU) than fixed strategies like paraphrasing or synthetic QA, which explains the continued performance gains as data scales to 4B words while baselines plateau. At pre-training scale (1T tokens over 6M Wikipedia articles), the approach requires higher learning rates and mixing in pre-training data to prevent catastrophic forgetting, and the resulting WikiExpert-8B model outperforms models with hundreds of billions of parameters on factual QA benchmarks.

## Results

On SimpleWikiQA (a Wikipedia-grounded subset of SimpleQA), Active Reading (task-specific) achieves 66.25% factual recall, a 312% relative improvement over vanilla finetuning (15.92%) and matching the gold-context upper bound (65.85%). On FinanceBench (information extraction subset), Active Reading (task-agnostic) reaches 66.18%, a 160% relative gain over vanilla finetuning (18.43%). At pre-training scale, WikiExpert-8B (trained on 1T tokens of Active Reading-augmented Wikipedia) scores 23.5% on SimpleQA, outperforming the 236B DeepSeekV2 (10.2%) and 405B Llama 3.1 (17.1%), and approaching the 671B DeepSeekV3 (24.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the paraphrasing baseline from Allen-Zhu & Li (2024), Active Reading (task-specific) achieves 66.25% vs. 25.74% on SimpleWikiQA (+157% relative) and 61.49% vs. 43.87% on FinanceBench info. (+40% relative), a substantial margin on both benchmarks. Against the synthetic QA baseline from Lewis et al. (2021), Active Reading (task-specific) scores 66.25% vs. 47.87% on SimpleWikiQA (+38% relative) and 61.49% vs. 44.23% on FinanceBench info. (+39% relative). Notably, Active Reading (task-agnostic) outperforms synth QA on FinanceBench (66.18% vs. 44.23%) while task-specific Active Reading leads on SimpleWikiQA, suggesting the two variants complement each other. The paper also shows that combining paraphrase + synth QA + Active Reading yields marginal additional gains (66.66% on SimpleWikiQA), indicating Active Reading already subsumes the benefits of the other strategies. On the full FinanceBench benchmark (which includes reasoning questions), a gap remains between Active Reading (26.83%) and the gold-context ceiling (57.43%), indicating parametric knowledge alone is insufficient for multi-step reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of synthetic data generation for knowledge incorporation, building directly on Allen-Zhu & Li's (2024) finding that paraphrasing improves factual recall and Lewis et al.'s (2021) synthetic QA approach. Rather than applying a single fixed augmentation strategy, Active Reading frames data generation as a two-stage process where the model first proposes diverse, document-specific learning strategies (inspired by human study techniques like active recall and analogical reasoning) and then instantiates them, effectively subsuming paraphrasing, synthetic QA, and concept maps as special cases. The paper also departs from the standard fine-tuning paradigm by showing that pre-training-style training (higher learning rate, mixed data) is necessary to scale knowledge incorporation beyond a few documents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that Active Reading scales to 1T tokens and produces an 8B model competitive with 671B-parameter models on factual QA suggests a practical recipe for building more factual base models, and the released 1T-token synthetic Wikipedia dataset supports further research. A key open question is why mixing in pre-training data improves target-task performance rather than merely recovering guardrail capabilities, which the authors flag as potentially related to knowledge entropy decay and plasticity. Limitations include the substantial gap versus RAG on reasoning-heavy questions, degradation on guardrail tasks (e.g., GSM8k drops from 54.4 to 48.6), and the fact that all experiments use a single model family (Llama 3.1 8B), leaving open whether the findings generalize across architectures.
