## Problem

While grokking (delayed generalization) has been widely observed, no mathematical framework existed that characterizes *which* features emerge, *how* they emerge from gradient dynamics, and *under what conditions* generalization versus memorization occurs for structured inputs. Prior explanations relied on empirical circuit analysis, effective theories, or very-wide-network approximations, leaving the role of backpropagated gradients and the optimization landscape unexplored at the level of individual hidden nodes.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on a 2-layer network Ŷ = σ(XW)V trained with ℓ₂ loss on group arithmetic data, where each sample is a 2M-dimensional one-hot concatenation (e_{h₁}, e_{h₂}) and the target is the one-hot vector e_{h₁h₂}. The zero-mean projection P₁⊥ is applied to both activations and labels throughout. The theoretical outputs are: (i) the exact structure of the backpropagated gradient G_F at each stage, (ii) the energy function E(w_j) = ½‖Ỹᵀσ(Xw_j)‖² whose local maxima are the learned features, (iii) a provable scaling law n ≳ d_k² M log(M/δ) for the generalization/memorization boundary, and (iv) the repulsion and top-down modulation dynamics in the interactive stage.

## Method

The Li₂ framework decomposes grokking into three stages governed by the structure of the backpropagated gradient G_F. In Stage I (lazy learning), the top layer V overfits to random hidden features while G_F acquires the structured signal ∝ ỸỸᵀF̃, either from small initialization (Proposition 1) or from weight decay η at convergence (Lemma 1). In Stage II (independent feature learning), each hidden node w_j evolves via gradient ascent on the energy E(w_j) = ½‖Ỹᵀσ(Xw_j)‖² — a nonlinear canonical-correlation analysis between X and Ỹ — and for group arithmetic tasks with σ(x)=x², the local maxima are completely characterized as irreducible representations of the group (Theorem 2), with the number of samples needed to maintain them yielding the scaling law p = n/M² ~ M⁻¹ log M (Theorem 4). In Stage III (interactive feature learning), the full G_F = P_η ỸỸᵀF̃B introduces inter-node repulsion of similar features (Theorem 6) and top-down modulation that redirects learning toward missing irreps (Theorem 7), while optimizers like Muon further enhance diversity by discounting already-occupied gradient directions (Theorem 8).

## Results

The paper derives a provable scaling law for the memorization-to-generalization phase transition: the critical training data ratio is p = n/M² = O(M⁻¹ log M) (Thm. 4), which is validated empirically on modular addition tasks with a clear phase transition (test accuracy 0→1) around the predicted boundary (Fig. 5). For Abelian groups, the learned features require only K = 2M−2 hidden nodes to perfectly reconstruct the target (Thm. 3), compared to M² nodes for pure memorization — a quadratic efficiency gain. Zero-initialization of the top layer accelerates feature learning by up to 10× (e.g., 100 vs. 1000 epochs) in multi-layer settings with scarce data. The energy function E has local maxima with values E* = M/8d_k (real irreps) or E* = M/16d_k (complex irrep pairs), and these maxima are provably flat (Corollary 1), with the number of samples needed to maintain them scaling as n ≳ d_k² M log(M/δ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical framework paper without external method baselines; comparisons are internal ablations validating the Li₂ framework. (1) Weight decay: with η = 0.0002, grokking occurs (test accuracy reaches 1.0), while with η = 0, the model remains stuck in memorization (Fig. 3) — confirming that η controls the magnitude of the backpropagated signal G_F. (2) Learning rate: in the small-data regime (M=127, n=3225), small learning rates (0.001–0.002) converge to generalizable Fourier-basis solutions with low E, while large learning rate (0.005) converges to non-generalizable memorization solutions with higher E (Fig. 7), matching the theoretical prediction that large lr escapes the generalization basin. (3) Zero-init vs. normal init: zero-init consistently accelerates feature learning across M = 41, 89, 127, with the gap widening in multi-layer settings (up to 10×). (4) Muon vs. standard gradient: Muon provably rebalances gradient updates to encourage diversity (Thm. 8), and empirically achieves better coverage of local maxima with limited hidden nodes K (Fig. 9). (5) Theoretical scaling law vs. empirical: the predicted boundary p ~ M⁻¹ log M (dotted line in Fig. 5) closely matches the observed phase transition across multiple group structures (cyclic prime, cyclic composite, product, non-Abelian).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant NTK/mean-field approaches to grokking (Barak et al., 2022; Rubin et al., 2024) where hidden weights remain near initialization, by studying the full gradient dynamics of feature learning in 2-layer nonlinear networks. It provides a first-principles theoretical foundation for the empirical 'circuit efficiency' hypothesis of Varma et al. (2023) and the three-stage framework of Nanda et al. (2023), but grounds each stage in the explicit structure of the backpropagated gradient G_F rather than empirical observation. The energy function E is connected to nonlinear CCA and to the contrastive-learning energy of Tian (2023), while the local-maxima analysis draws on finite group representation theory (Fulton & Harris, 2013) in a way not previously applied to learning dynamics. The Muon optimizer analysis (Thm. 8) is, to the authors' knowledge, the first to explain its effectiveness through the lens of feature-learning gradient dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework yields concrete, testable scaling laws (p ~ M^{-1} log M for the generalization/memorization boundary) that match experiments, and clarifies the roles of weight decay, learning rate, and sample size in grokking from first principles. The finding that lazy memorization is a necessary prerequisite that generates the structured gradient signal enabling feature learning reframes the grokking narrative beyond 'overcoming memorization.' Key limitations include the restriction to 2-layer networks and group-structured inputs for the local-maxima characterization, and the absence of transition-time analysis between stages; extending the full proof machinery to deeper architectures and non-group data remains open.
