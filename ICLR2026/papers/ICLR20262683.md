## Problem

A dominant assumption in MLLM research is that performance is largely inherited from the LLM backbone, leaving the vision encoder's role in shaping visual understanding largely unexplored. As MLLM training shifts from SFT to RL (e.g., DPO), there is no systematic analysis of how these post-training strategies differentially reshape the vision encoder's visual representations, nor whether RL's advantages extend beyond language generation to the model's perceptual capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262683/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The MLLM takes an RGB image (processed at 384px for SigLIP2 or 336px for CLIP-L/14) and a text query, producing a free-form text response. For the PIVOT recipe specifically, the input is an existing pretrained vision encoder (CLIP, SigLIP, DINOv2, MAE, MoCo, or ViT) paired with a Qwen2.5 LLM head, trained on 3M instruction-following samples followed by 20K preference pairs; the output is a detached, frozen vision encoder that can be plugged into any MLLM architecture. The PIVOT-enhanced encoder is then evaluated by re-integrating it with a fresh LLM and projector, isolating the encoder's contribution.

## Method

The paper first conducts a controlled SFT-vs-DPO comparison on LLaVA-OneVision MLLMs (Qwen2.5 0.5B–7B × SigLIP2 B/16–g/16) using matched numbers of image-query-response pairs, revealing that DPO yields substantially larger gains on vision-centric tasks (OCR, ChartQA, MMVP) than on knowledge-based tasks. It then detaches the vision encoder from the LLM and probes its standalone quality via ImageNet linear probing, ADE20K segmentation probing, Grad-CAM gradient visualization, and cross-modal representation alignment, showing DPO produces stronger and more spatially localized visual features than SFT. These findings are consolidated into PIVOT (Preference-Instructed Vision OpTimization): attach an existing vision encoder to a small LLM (Qwen2.5-1.5B), run Stage 1 pre-training on 3M samples, then Stage 2 DPO on 20K preference pairs, and detach the encoder. PIVOT requires only 18 hours on 8 H100 GPUs (<1% of SigLIP2's 2K-TPU pretraining), yet the enhanced encoder outperforms larger and newer counterparts (e.g., SigLIP2-So/16+PIVOT > SigLIP2-g/16; SigLIP1-So/14+PIVOT > SigLIP2-So/16) when used within MLLMs.

## Results

PIVOT-enhanced SigLIP2-So/16 (400M params) achieves 55.6% average VQA score, surpassing the 2.5× larger SigLIP2-g/16 (1000M params) at 53.9% and the next-generation SigLIP2-So/16 at 52.4%. PIVOT-enhanced SigLIP1-So/14 (53.2%) also beats the original SigLIP2-So/16 (52.4%), demonstrating that a prior-generation encoder enhanced via DPO can outperform a newer, more heavily pretrained one. Across all five classical vision encoders tested (CLIP, DINOv2, MAE, MoCo, ImageNet-Sup ViT), PIVOT yields consistent gains of +2.2 to +3.2%p in average VQA. The entire PIVOT training requires only 18 hours on 8 H100 GPUs, less than 1% of the compute used for standard vision pretraining (e.g., SigLIP2 on 2K TPUv5e chips).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIVOT-enhanced SigLIP2-So/16 (55.6% avg) outperforms the 2.5× larger SigLIP2-g/16 (53.9% avg) by +1.7%p, and PIVOT-enhanced SigLIP1-So/14 (53.2%) surpasses the next-generation SigLIP2-So/16 (52.4%) by +0.8%p, showing that DPO-based fine-tuning can close or reverse the gap between encoder generations. Against the model ensemble baseline (SigLIP1-So400m + ConvNeXt-XXL, 51.4% avg), a single PIVOT-enhanced SigLIP1-So/14 achieves 53.2% without additional parameters, and combining PIVOT with the ensemble further raises the score to 53.6%. On classical vision encoders, PIVOT consistently improves MLLM performance: CLIP-Large gains +3.2%p (46.3→49.5), DINOv2-giant +2.7%p (40.9→43.6), MAE-huge +2.9%p (36.8→39.7), MoCo-base +2.2%p (35.3→37.5), and ImageNet-Sup ViT-huge +2.2%p (35.5→37.7). DPO (PIVOT) also outperforms SFT post-training across all encoder sizes, with the gap most pronounced on OCR & Chart (+7.3%p for SigLIP2-So/16) and Vision-Centric (+1.8%p) tasks. MPO, a DPO variant combining SFT and preference classification, matches DPO on vision-only benchmarks (ImageNet, segmentation) but does not exceed it in MLLM evaluation, confirming DPO as the preferred RL objective for PIVOT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) as its core optimization method but repurposes it from LLM preference alignment into an auxiliary training signal for vision encoders within MLLMs. It departs from the dominant LLM-centric assumption that MLLM capabilities are inherited primarily from the language backbone, instead showing that the post-training strategy (SFT vs. DPO) fundamentally reshapes the vision encoder's visual representations. The experimental framework extends the LLaVA-OneVision and Cambrian training pipelines, while the PIVOT recipe positions itself as a CLIP-style alternative to expensive vision pre-training, requiring less than 1% of the compute used by SigLIP2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DPO produces stronger, more localized visual representations than SFT challenges the field's implicit assumption that vision encoder quality is determined solely by its pre-training scale and data volume. PIVOT offers a practical, low-cost path (18 hours on 8 H100s) to enhance existing encoders, with the striking result that a 400M PIVOT-enhanced SigLIP2-So/16 outperforms the 1B SigLIP2-g/16 in MLLM applications. Open questions include whether PIVOT's gains compound with further scaling, whether it transfers to video or 3D modalities, and whether the effect is specific to ViT architectures or extends to convolutional encoders beyond the ConvNeXt ensemble test.
