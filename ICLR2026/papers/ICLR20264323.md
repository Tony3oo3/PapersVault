## Problem

Most test-time adaptation (TTA) methods rely on backpropagation, incurring high memory and compute costs that make them impractical for edge devices, while existing backprop-free alternatives are either architecture-specific (ViT-only or CNN-only) or require many forward passes, causing high latency. This paper asks whether domain shift can be corrected directly in the embedding space without any gradient computation, and whether a single unified procedure can work across both CNN and Transformer backbones.

## Input / Output

At test time, PEA consumes an unlabeled batch of RGB images (default batch size 64, but stable down to BS=1) along with pre-computed per-block source statistics (mean vectors and covariance matrices, ~30 MB for ViT-Base) extracted offline from the training set. The method performs exactly two forward passes per batch and outputs a class label prediction via uniform averaging over K augmented views. No model parameters are modified at any point; the original pretrained weights remain frozen throughout adaptation.

## Method

PEA is motivated by the observation that domain shift induces three geometric distortions in intermediate embeddings: mean shift (translation), variance shift (scaling), and channel-wise covariance shift (rotation). The method applies a Whitening-Coloring Transform (WCT) at every model block to realign target-domain features to the source geometry, but uses a distance-aware weighting scheme that blends the original and aligned features based on per-layer statistical discrepancy, preventing over-correction. An exponential moving average (EMA) with entropy-spike-triggered resets provides robust target statistics under small batches, and optional lightweight augmentation (K=2 views) enriches both the statistics estimation and the final prediction via ensemble averaging. The entire procedure is backpropagation-free and architecture-agnostic, requiring only eigendecomposition of moderate-size covariance matrices (128–1024 dimensions) during the forward pass, enabling deployment on resource-constrained edge devices like the Jetson Orin Nano.

## Results

On ImageNet-C with ViT-Base, PEA achieves 64.5% average accuracy using only 887 MB memory and 0.31 s/batch latency; with lightweight augmentation (PEA+Aug), it reaches 66.5% at 1867 MB and 0.59 s. On ResNet-50, PEA attains 42.7% (983 MB, 0.36 s) and PEA+Aug reaches 44.8% (2397 MB, 0.56 s). On CIFAR-10-C and CIFAR-100-C, PEA+Aug achieves 77.0% and 84.7% (ViT) and 83.4% and 54.6% (ResNet), respectively, consistently outperforming all backprop-free and backprop-based baselines while using an order-of-magnitude less memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ViT-Base/ImageNet-C, PEA+Aug (66.5%) surpasses FOA with 27 forward passes (66.1%), SPA (64.6%), and CMF (65.9%) while using 5–12× less memory (1867 MB vs. 870–10902 MB) and 5.6× lower latency than FOA (0.59 s vs. 3.33 s). On ResNet-50/ImageNet-C, PEA+Aug (44.8%) outperforms the strongest backprop-based baselines CMF (43.7%) and LAW (39.9%) by 1.1 and 4.9 points respectively, and dominates all efficient CNN-specific methods (MECTA 38.0%, EcoTTA 30.5%, L-TTA 34.5%). On CIFAR-10-C with ViT, PEA+Aug (77.0%) leads CMF (73.0%) and SPA (71.7%) by 4.0 and 5.3 points; on CIFAR-100-C with ResNet, PEA+Aug (54.6%) beats L-TTA (50.9%) and EATA (50.0%) by 3.7 and 4.6 points. The key differentiator is that PEA is the only method combining backprop-free operation, architecture-agnostic applicability (both ViT and ResNet), and sub-1 GB memory footprint, whereas all backprop-based methods require 4–12 GB and FOA, though memory-light, is ViT-only and incurs 3.33 s latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEA repurposes the Whitening-Coloring Transform (WCT) from the image-to-image translation literature (Cho et al., 2019) as the core alignment mechanism, applying it layer-wise to intermediate feature activations rather than to pixel-space images. It departs from the dominant backpropagation-based TTA paradigm (TENT, EATA, CoTTA, MECTA, EcoTTA) and from architecture-specific forward-only methods (FOA for ViTs, L-TTA for CNNs) by operating purely on per-block mean, variance, and covariance statistics, making it simultaneously backprop-free and architecture-agnostic. The three-part geometric decomposition of domain shift (translation, scaling, rotation) provides a unifying lens that prior TTA work treated implicitly through entropy or pseudo-label objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PEA's practical significance lies in enabling test-time adaptation on resource-constrained edge devices (demonstrated on Jetson Orin Nano with <1 GB memory) where backprop-based methods simply cannot run, while maintaining competitive accuracy across both CNN and Transformer backbones. A key limitation is the requirement for pre-computed source statistics, which, while mitigated by showing 10% of training data suffices, still assumes some access to the source distribution at deployment time. Open questions include whether the covariance-alignment strategy generalizes to more natural (non-corruption) domain shifts, to non-classification tasks, and to settings where source statistics are entirely unavailable.
