## Problem

Existing SpeechLLM benchmarks predominantly evaluate semantic-level understanding using TTS-synthesized audio and lack grounding in linguistic theory, leaving critical dimensions of spoken language—such as prosody, phonology, paralinguistics, and rhetorical phenomena—largely unassessed. This paper identifies three specific gaps: insufficient coverage of authentic spoken phenomena (disfluencies, sarcasm, code-switching, intonation), over-reliance on synthetic audio that misses natural acoustic variability, and the absence of a linguistically principled evaluation framework.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a real-world audio clip (average 7.01 seconds) with a text prompt containing a multiple-choice question and four options (A–D), with options randomly shuffled to eliminate positional bias. The model outputs a single letter selection, and performance is reported as accuracy broken down across 47 tasks, further stratified by two capability levels (perception vs. reasoning) and three linguistic domains (semantics, phonology, paralinguistics). A human baseline is established by 15 student evaluators scoring a 1,000-instance random sample under identical instructions.

## Method

MMSU is constructed through a four-stage pipeline: (1) task design grounded in six linguistic subfields (phonetics, prosody, rhetoric, syntactics, semantics, paralinguistics) organized in a three-level hierarchy (perception/reasoning → linguistics/paralinguistics → semantics/phonology or speaker-traits/speaking-style); (2) question curation from authoritative linguistics textbooks with GPT-4o-assisted distractor generation under expert-in-the-loop review; (3) audio sourcing prioritizing 76.74% open-source recordings (MELD, GigaSpeech, CommonVoice, Switchboard, SEAME, EDACC, VCTK, etc.), 13.44% custom studio recordings by professional voice actors for prosodic phenomena, and 9.82% Azure TTS augmentation; and (4) multi-round manual review by 10 trained annotators. The benchmark is validated by evaluating 22 models (12 SpeechLLMs and 10 OmniLLMs) with unified instruction-following prompts, plus a noise-robustness analysis injecting additive Gaussian noise at two amplitude levels to confirm models genuinely process acoustic signals rather than exploiting textual shortcuts.

## Results

On MMSU (5,000 MCQs across 47 tasks), the best-performing model Gemini-1.5-Pro achieves only 60.68% average accuracy, far below the human baseline of 89.72%. Open-source models Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (60.57%) and Kimi-Audio (59.28%) are competitive with proprietary models, while GPT-4o-Audio lags at 56.38%. A striking finding is that models perform worse on perception (avg ~40%) than on reasoning (avg ~65%), the opposite of the human pattern (91.24% vs. 86.77%), revealing a fundamental bottleneck in fine-grained acoustic perception. Phonology-related tasks are a particular weakness, with the best model (Gemini-1.5-Pro) reaching only 53.60% on phonology perception.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among SpeechLLMs, Kimi-Audio (59.28%) and Qwen2-Audio-Instruct (53.27%) lead, while LTU (22.61%) and LTU-AS (25.03%) perform near random-guess level, indicating that cascaded ASR+LLM architectures struggle with fine-grained acoustic perception. Among OmniLLMs, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (60.57%) edges out Gemini-1.5-Pro (60.68%) by only 0.11%, making it the strongest open-source model, while Lyra (16.11%) falls below random chance on several subtasks. GPT-4o-Audio (56.38%) underperforms relative to its reputation, particularly on paralinguistic perception (21.44%) where it rejects speaker-traits questions due to internal policy. A consistent pattern across all models is the perception–reasoning inversion: models score ~40% on perception but ~65–80% on reasoning, whereas humans show the opposite (91% perception vs. 87% reasoning). Phonology remains the weakest sub-domain for every model, with even Gemini-1.5-Pro reaching only 53.60% on phonology perception and 83.46% on phonology reasoning. Error analysis confirms that perceptual errors dominate (47–60% of all errors), followed by reasoning errors (19–39%), with GPT-4o-Audio uniquely showing 14.7% answer-extraction/rejection errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

MMSU extends the line of SpeechLLM evaluation benchmarks (AudioBench, MMAU, VoiceBench, ADU-Bench, AIR-Bench) by introducing a linguistically grounded hierarchical taxonomy that prior work lacked. While existing benchmarks predominantly assess semantic-level understanding over literal content—often treating speech as a proxy for text—MMSU deliberately carves out fine-grained acoustic perception (phonology, prosody, paralinguistics) as a first-class evaluation dimension. The paper also departs from the heavy reliance on TTS-synthesized audio in prior benchmarks by sourcing 76.74% of its data from open-source real recordings and 13.44% from professional studio recordings, addressing a gap in acoustic authenticity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 29-point gap between human evaluators (89.72%) and the best model (Gemini-1.5-Pro at 60.68%) reveals that current SpeechLLMs are far from achieving robust spoken language understanding, particularly in phonological and paralinguistic dimensions that prior benchmarks largely ignored. The finding that models invert the human difficulty ordering—performing better on reasoning than perception—challenges the assumption that semantic reasoning capability implies adequate acoustic perception, pointing to a fundamental architectural gap. Open questions include whether scaling or targeted phonological training data can close this perception gap, and whether the linguistic-theory-driven task decomposition generalizes beyond English-centric evaluation.
