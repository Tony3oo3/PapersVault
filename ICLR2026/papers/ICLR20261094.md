## Problem

The XAI literature has studied sufficient and contrastive reasons across multiple axes—local vs. global, probabilistic vs. non-probabilistic—in isolation, yielding a fragmented picture of which explanation types are computationally tractable. No prior work has connected these settings under a single formalism or identified the structural properties of the underlying value functions that govern complexity. This paper asks: can all these explanation problems be unified, and do the resulting value functions possess combinatorial-optimization properties (monotonicity, submodularity, supermodularity) that enable or preclude efficient computation?

## Input / Output

The unified task takes a classification model f (neural network, decision tree, or tree ensemble), a distribution D over the input space (empirical or independent), an optional input instance x (required only for local explanations), and a threshold δ ∈ [0,1], and returns a feature subset S ⊆ [n] satisfying v(S) ≥ δ. The output is either subset-minimal (no proper subset also satisfies the threshold) or cardinally-minimal (smallest |S|), with the latter available only as a constant-factor approximation in the global setting. A key structural detail is that the value function v: 2^n → [0,1] is defined differently for each explanation type (e.g., v_suff^ℓ fixes S and samples the complement, while v_con^g averages over all inputs), yet all four variants are handled by the same two greedy algorithms.

## Method

The paper casts every explanation problem as minimizing |S| subject to v(S) ≥ δ, then proves that the global value functions v_suff^g and v_con^g are monotone non-decreasing for any distribution, while their local counterparts are not. Under feature independence, v_suff^g is further shown to be supermodular and v_con^g submodular—properties absent in the local setting. Monotonicity enables a top-down greedy algorithm (Algorithm 1) that provably yields subset-minimal global explanations in polynomial time for any model with polynomial-time inference under empirical distributions, whereas the local version is NP-hard even for decision trees. Submodularity/supermodularity combined with monotonicity reduces the cardinally-minimal global problem to (non-)submodular set cover, yielding O(ln|D|) and O(1/(1−k^f) + ln|D|) constant-factor approximations via a bottom-up greedy algorithm (Algorithm 2), while the local cardinally-minimal problem is shown to admit no bounded approximation even for a single data point.

## Results

The paper establishes a sharp complexity separation between local and global explanations: computing subset-minimal local δ-sufficient/contrastive reasons is NP-hard even for decision trees under independent distributions, while the global counterparts are computable in polynomial time for any model with polynomial-time inference under empirical distributions (Theorems 1–2, Proposition 6). For cardinally minimal explanations, the global setting admits constant-factor approximations — O(ln(|D|)) for contrastive reasons and O(1/(1−k^f) + ln(v([n])/min_i v({i}))) for sufficient reasons — whereas no bounded approximation exists for the local setting even with |D|=1 (Theorems 3–4). The paper further proves that global value functions are monotonic non-decreasing (for any δ), supermodular (sufficient) or submodular (contrastive) under feature independence, while local value functions satisfy none of these properties.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central comparison in this paper is between local and global explanation settings across three model families (decision trees, neural networks, tree ensembles). For subset-minimal explanations, the local setting is NP-hard even for the most interpretable model (decision trees with independent/uniform distributions), while the global setting is polynomial-time solvable for all three model classes under empirical distributions. For cardinally minimal explanations, the gap widens further: the global setting yields provable constant-factor approximations (logarithmic in dataset size for contrastive, with an additional curvature-dependent factor for sufficient reasons), while the local setting is inapproximable — no bounded approximation ratio exists even for a single data point. The paper also contrasts with prior work by Arenas et al. (2022) and Bassan et al. (2024), showing that the probabilistic global setting generalizes their non-probabilistic results while unlocking new tractability via monotonicity and sub/supermodularity that are absent in the local case.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the previously fragmented landscape of explanation complexity results—spanning sufficient/contrastive, local/global, and probabilistic/non-probabilistic variants studied by Barcelo et al. (2020), Arenas et al. (2022), and Bassan et al. (2024)—into a single combinatorial optimization framework over a unified value function. The key conceptual departure is identifying monotonicity, submodularity, and supermodularity as the structural properties that govern complexity, and proving these properties hold for global value functions but fail for local ones. The algorithmic results then draw directly on classical submodular set cover (Wolsey, 1982) and non-submodular cover (Shi et al., 2021) to derive approximation guarantees for the global setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean theoretical separation: global explanations with provable guarantees are polynomial-time computable even for neural networks and tree ensembles under empirical distributions, while their local counterparts remain NP-hard. This reframes the local-vs-global tradeoff in XAI from a matter of preference to one of fundamental computational feasibility. Open questions include tightening approximation bounds for simpler model classes, developing FPRAS or Monte Carlo approaches when exact expectation computation is infeasible, and whether similar structural properties emerge under alternative importance measures (e.g., KL divergence). The results are purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the practical utility depends on whether global explanations are deemed more useful than local ones in deployment settings.
