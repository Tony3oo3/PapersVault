## Problem

Instruction-tuned GPTv models for medical imaging suffer from two coupled failures: clinical attributes (disease names, severity grades) are encoded as plain text tokens that collapse semantically distinct concepts into nearly identical sequences, and the causal vision-to-text information flow provides only weak supervision back to the visual encoder, producing entangled visual features and poor cross-modal alignment. The paper asks whether discriminative medical attributes can be explicitly and precisely incorporated into the generative paradigm of GPTv models.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a medical image (224×224, across dermatology, fundus, OCT, X-ray, or pathology modalities) and a text question, and autoregressively generates a response that begins with one or more pre-defined attribute tokens (formatted as <|modality_concept|>, e.g., <|fundus_sdr|>) followed by free-form text. For classification tasks the output is a disease/lesion label (open-ended without candidate list, or close-ended with candidates); for VQA it is a natural-language answer. Attribute tokens are inserted as a structured prefix before the textual response, acting as discriminative guidance that causally conditions all subsequent generated tokens.

## Method

AttTok introduces pre-defined attribute tokens into the GPTv vocabulary, each paired with a multi-modal embedding book containing the attribute token embedding, the corresponding textual keyword embedding, and a visual prototype updated via exponential moving average (EMA, μ=0.99). An Attribute-Centric Cross Attention (ACC) adapter treats the 3K book tokens as keys/values and visual embeddings as queries, injecting discriminative attribute knowledge directly into the ViT encoder and breaking the unidirectional vision-to-text bottleneck (γ=0.1 residual scaling). An Attribute-Centric Matching (ACM) loss performs soft contrastive classification of the predicted attribute embedding against its book's three positives (attribute, text, visual prototype) while repelling all other books' entries, enforcing intra-attribute consistency and inter-attribute separability across modalities. Training uses LoRA (r=16) on frozen vision and LLM towers, optimizing NTP cross-entropy plus λ·ACM (λ decays from 1 to 0), with only the new attribute token embeddings and LoRA adapters receiving gradients.

## Results

On five medical classification benchmarks (Dermatology, Fundus, OCT, X-ray, Pathology), Lingshu-7B+AttTok achieves 80.9% average close-ended accuracy, matching CLIP (80.8%) while operating within a generative framework, and surpassing the zero-shot Lingshu-7B baseline (48.5%) by over 32 points. On medical VQA (Rad-VQA, SLAKE, PathVQA), AttTok yields 74.0% average accuracy for Lingshu-7B, a +3.0 point gain over the zero-shot version. On the more challenging Fitzpatrick17K (113 fine-grained skin conditions) and RFMiD (28 retinal conditions with comorbidities), AttTok improves Lingshu-7B from 27% to 41% accuracy and from 25% to 36% mAP, respectively, with only 1–3% additional FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AttTok (Lingshu-7B) achieves 80.9% average close-ended accuracy, matching CLIP (80.8%) and surpassing it on Dermatology (75.5 vs. 68.4), Fundus (69.1 vs. 62.6), and Pathology (88.0 vs. 89.8 — near-tie), while CLIP retains a slight edge on X-ray (93.5 vs. 92.1) and OCT (89.8 vs. 79.7). Compared to zero-shot medical GPTv models, AttTok delivers dramatic gains: Lingshu-7B improves from 48.5% to 80.9% average, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 29.4% to 74.9%. On VQA benchmarks, AttTok consistently outperforms zero-shot baselines (e.g., Lingshu-7B: 71.0→74.0 avg; Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 71.6→72.5 avg), though gains are modest because Lingshu has already undergone extensive medical pretraining. On the fine-grained F17K and RFMiD datasets, AttTok (41% acc / 36% mAP) substantially outperforms Lingshu-7B alone (27% / 25%) but remains below the discriminative PMC-CLIP (45% / 49%), indicating that generative models still lag behind dedicated contrastive models on very large label spaces. Against other instruction-tuned alignment strategies (vision-text alignment, visual-only clustering), AttTok achieves the highest accuracy on all five modalities, with margins of +2.7 to +3.3 points over the best alternative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the medical GPTv model lineage (Lingshu, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-Med, HealthGPT) by identifying a structural weakness in the standard instruction-tuning paradigm: medical attributes encoded as plain text tokens collapse semantically distinct clinical concepts into near-identical sequences, and the causal vision-to-text information flow provides only weak supervision to the visual encoder. The key departure is the introduction of pre-defined attribute tokens with dedicated multi-modal embedding books, which serve as explicit discriminative anchors, combined with a cross-attention adapter (ACC) that injects attribute knowledge into the vision encoder and a contrastive matching loss (ACM) that enforces cross-modal alignment. Conceptually, this bridges the gap between discriminative vision-language models (CLIP, SigLIP2, PubMedCLIP) and generative GPTv models by embedding discriminative structure into the generative pipeline rather than treating them as separate paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a lightweight modification (1–3% additional FLOPs) to instruction-tuned medical GPTv models can close the performance gap with dedicated CLIP-based discriminative classifiers while preserving the generative framework's flexibility for open-ended tasks. This is practically significant for medical imaging deployment where a single model must handle both classification and free-form VQA. However, the authors acknowledge that validation is limited to short-answer tasks (classification, VQA), and the extension to long-form generation, chain-of-thought reasoning, and segmentation/detection remains open. The attribute-token concept also raises the question of whether similar discriminative anchoring strategies could benefit other domains where fine-grained categorical distinctions matter (e.g., industrial defect detection, histopathology subtyping).
