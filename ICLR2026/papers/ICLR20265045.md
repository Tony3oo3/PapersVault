## Problem

Discrete-token TTS systems ensure stable autoregressive generation but irreversibly discard fine-grained acoustic detail through quantization, while purely continuous autoregressive models preserve fidelity but suffer from error accumulation because semantic-prosodic planning and acoustic rendering are conflated in a single learning objective. Existing multi-stage hybrid pipelines (LLM + diffusion decoder) mitigate this but create a semantic-acoustic divide that prevents end-to-end optimization and limits holistic expressivity. The paper asks whether a unified, end-to-end architecture can simultaneously achieve the stability of discrete representations and the expressivity of continuous ones without relying on external pretrained speech tokenizers.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw BPE text tokens (not phonemes, leveraging the pre-trained MiniCPM-4 backbone for linguistic understanding) and an optional reference audio clip for zero-shot voice cloning. Audio is encoded by a causal VAE into 25 Hz latent patches (640× downsampling from 16 kHz mono), and generation proceeds patch-by-patch autoregressively. The final output is a 16 kHz speech waveform decoded from the VAE latents, with a real-time factor of 0.17 on a single RTX 4090 and sub-100 ms first-packet latency for streaming.

## Method

VoxCPM introduces a hierarchical autoregressive architecture where a 24-layer Text-Semantic Language Model (TSLM, initialized from MiniCPM-4-0.5B) generates semantic-prosodic representations that pass through a differentiable Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) bottleneck (256 dimensions, 9 scalar levels) to form a stable semi-discrete skeleton. A 6-layer Residual Acoustic Language Model (RALM) then recovers the fine-grained acoustic details lost during quantization by conditioning on the TSLM hidden states, the FSQ outputs, and historical acoustic embeddings. The summed TSLM-FSQ and RALM representations condition a 4-layer bidirectional Local Diffusion Transformer (LocDiT) that generates each latent patch via conditional flow-matching, with classifier-free guidance at inference. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end under a single flow-matching objective plus a stop-prediction loss, with gradients flowing through the FSQ via straight-through estimation—quantization serves purely as a regularizing inductive bias rather than a prediction target, avoiding the vocabulary explosion problem of discrete codebooks while still enforcing a natural semantic-acoustic division of labor.

## Results

On the SEED-TTS-EVAL benchmark, VoxCPM (0.5B, 1.8M hours) achieves an English WER of 1.85% and Chinese CER of 0.93%, with speaker similarity scores of 72.9% (EN) and 77.2% (ZH), setting state-of-the-art among open-source TTS systems. On the CV3-EVAL benchmark, it attains ZH-CER of 3.40% and EN-WER of 4.04%, with a particularly strong EN-WER of 7.89% on the challenging CV3-Hard-EN set, outperforming even close-sourced CosyVoice 3. Subjective MOS evaluations confirm competitive naturalness (4.11 EN, 4.10 ZH) and the highest speaker similarity (4.18 EN, 4.11 ZH) among compared systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoxCPM outperforms all open-source baselines on the primary SEED-TTS-EVAL metrics: it beats IndexTTS 2 (1.5B) by 0.38% EN-WER and 0.10% ZH-CER while using 3× fewer parameters, and surpasses CosyVoice 2 (0.5B) by 1.24% EN-WER and 7.0 points EN-SIM. Against the much larger HiggsAudio-v2 (3B, 10M hrs), VoxCPM achieves 0.59% lower EN-WER and 5.2 points higher EN-SIM, demonstrating strong data efficiency. On the Hard subset, VoxCPM trails IndexTTS 2 (8.87 vs 7.12 CER) and CosyVoice 2 (8.87 vs 6.83 CER), suggesting these discrete-token systems retain a slight edge on complex sentences. On CV3-EVAL, VoxCPM achieves the best ZH-CER (3.40%) and EN-WER (4.04%) among all open-source systems, and its CV3-Hard-EN WER of 7.89% beats close-sourced CosyVoice 3-0.5B (9.04%) and CosyVoice 3-1.5B (10.55%). In subjective MOS, VoxCPM achieves the highest EN speaker similarity (4.18) and competitive naturalness (4.11 EN, 4.10 ZH), trailing only IndexTTS 2 in Chinese naturalness (4.25 vs 4.10). The smaller VoxCPM-Emilia variant (100K hrs) remains competitive with CosyVoice 2 and SparkTTS trained on similar data scales, confirming architectural robustness independent of data volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

VoxCPM positions itself at the intersection of discrete-token TTS (CosyVoice, VALL-E) and continuous autoregressive TTS (MELLE, DiTAR), departing from both by introducing an internal FSQ bottleneck that creates a semi-discrete representation without relying on external pretrained speech tokenizers. The LocDiT module directly follows DiTAR's patch-based bidirectional diffusion transformer design, while the TSLM backbone is initialized from MiniCPM-4, a small text LLM. The key conceptual departure from prior hierarchical TTS work (HierSpeech++, HALL-E, QTTS) is that quantization here serves purely as a differentiable inductive bias to enforce semantic-acoustic specialization, rather than as a prediction target for a discrete codebook, enabling fully end-to-end training under a single flow-matching objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the dependency on external discrete speech tokenizers and resolving the expressivity-stability trade-off through an internal semi-discrete bottleneck, VoxCPM offers a simpler, more unified training pipeline that achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot TTS among open-source systems at only 0.5B parameters and an RTF of 0.17. The probing experiments and t-SNE visualizations provide strong empirical evidence that the FSQ bottleneck genuinely induces semantic-acoustic disentanglement, validating the architectural hypothesis. Open questions include whether the approach scales effectively beyond 3B parameters, how it generalizes to non-speech audio generation or singing, and whether the specific FSQ dimensionality (256d, 9 levels) transfers across languages and domains without re-tuning.
