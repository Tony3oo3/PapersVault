## Problem

Set-based gait recognition methods process each silhouette independently with 2D convolutions, missing short-range temporal context between adjacent frames, while sequence-based methods rely on 3D/P3D convolutions over a limited number of continuous frames (e.g., 30), hindering long-range temporal dependency modeling in long sequences. This paper asks whether an alternative paradigm can simultaneously capture both short-range and long-range temporal context without the computational cost of 3D convolutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of binary silhouette frames as input. During training, the sequence is partitioned into segments of length L=16 (approximating one gait cycle), and M=4 snippets of N=8 randomly sampled frames each are extracted (32 frames total per sequence). During inference, all frames are used with fixed segment partitioning. The output is a sequence-level gait feature embedding (extracted before BNNeck) used to compute Euclidean distances between probe and gallery sequences for identity ranking.

## Method

GaitSnippet reorganizes a silhouette sequence into snippets—small groups of frames randomly sampled from contiguous segments—enabling both local temporal context (within a snippet) and global temporal coverage (across snippets spanning the full sequence). The backbone is a 2D convolutional network (DeepGaitV2-2D) modified with Residual Snippet Blocks: each block inserts a Snippet Block (Temporal Max Pooling → 1×1 smoothing → residual addition) between two spatial convolutional layers, inspired by P3D's factorization. Cross-snippet modeling treats all snippet-level features as an unordered set and applies Temporal Max Pooling to produce the sequence-level representation. A key training innovation is snippet-level supervision: an auxiliary branch (Horizontal Pyramid Mapping + BNNeck) computes triplet and cross-entropy losses on snippet-level features with a weighting hyperparameter α, providing fine-grained identity signals at the action level in addition to sequence-level losses. The entire approach uses only 2D convolutions, achieving 77.5% R1 on Gait3D and 81.7% R1 on GREW.

## Results

GaitSnippet achieves rank-1 accuracy of 77.5% and mAP of 69.4% on Gait3D, and rank-1 accuracy of 81.7% and rank-5 accuracy of 90.9% on GREW, using only a 2D convolutional backbone. On the emerging CCPG benchmark, it attains a mean accuracy of 95.1% across four covariate conditions, and on CCGR-MINI it reaches 42.4% R1 and 39.5% mAP. These results represent state-of-the-art performance in the wild, surpassing all prior 3D/P3D sequence-based and 2D set-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaitSnippet outperforms the strongest 3D sequence-based method VPNet (75.4% R1) by +2.1% on Gait3D and the best 3D set-based method GaitMoE (73.7% R1) by +3.8%, all while using a lighter 2D convolutional backbone. Compared to the closest 2D baseline DeepGaitV2-2D (68.2% R1, 60.4% mAP), GaitSnippet gains +9.3% R1 and +9.0% mAP on Gait3D, demonstrating that the snippet paradigm itself—rather than backbone depth—drives the improvement. On GREW, GaitSnippet (81.7% R1) surpasses VPNet (80.0%) and SwinGait-3D (79.3%) by 1.7 and 2.4 points respectively. On the cross-covariate CCPG benchmark, GaitSnippet (95.1% mean) beats DeepGaitV2-P3D (93.7%) and GaitBase (92.0%), and on CCGR-MINI it leads by +3.0% R1 over DeepGaitV2-P3D (42.4% vs 39.4%) and by a substantial +15.4% R1 over GaitBase (42.4% vs 27.0%). Notably, even applying snippet sampling alone to the unmodified DeepGaitV2-2D backbone yields a modest gain (69.5% vs 68.2% R1), suggesting the sampling strategy independently narrows the train-test distribution gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves a new middle ground between two dominant paradigms in silhouette-based gait recognition: unordered set methods (GaitSet, GaitBase, DeepGaitV2-2D) that lose temporal context, and ordered sequence methods (GaitGL, GaitGCI, DyGait) that rely on 3D/P3D convolutions over limited continuous windows. The snippet concept is borrowed from Temporal Segment Networks (TSN) in action recognition, but is substantially reworked for gait through intra-snippet modeling (a non-parametric pooling + residual block inspired by P3D's spatial-temporal decomposition) and a hierarchical two-level set structure. The backbone is explicitly built on DeepGaitV2-2D, and the loss design (triplet + cross-entropy with BNNeck and Horizontal Pyramid Mapping) follows the GaitBase/DeepGaitV2 recipe, with the novel addition of snippet-level auxiliary supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on Gait3D and GREW with a plain 2D convolutional backbone, GaitSnippet demonstrates that the snippet paradigm can match or exceed 3D/P3D methods at substantially lower computational cost, which is practically important for deployment. The snippet-level supervision and the Residual Snippet Block are modular components that could be transplanted into other temporal recognition tasks. Open questions include whether the fixed segment length (L=16) and sampling parameters generalize to substantially longer or more irregular sequences, and whether the approach extends naturally to skeleton-based or multi-modal gait inputs where the 'frame' abstraction differs.
