## Problem

Comparing bias mitigation methods for vision and multimodal models remains difficult due to heterogeneous datasets, inconsistent fairness metrics, isolated evaluation of vision versus LVLMs, and insufficient hyperparameter tuning that undermines fair comparisons. Existing benchmarks (MEDFAIR, FFB, ABCFair) are restricted to specific domains, omit recent methods, or use fixed hyperparameters, leaving open whether a well-tuned ERM can match or surpass specialized fairness algorithms and whether large-scale pretraining inherently reduces subgroup disparities.

## Input / Output

The benchmark evaluates models on seven image datasets (CelebA, UTKFace, FairFace, Facet, HAM10000, Fitz17k, Waterbirds) spanning facial attributes, medical imaging, and spurious correlation tests, each annotated with sensitive attributes such as gender, race, age, skin type, or background. Supervised models take RGB images and produce class labels, while LVLMs (LLaVA, Qwen2.5-VL, Gemma 3, Llama) operate zero-shot with structured text prompts eliciting binary Yes/No responses. All models are scored on utility (ACC or AUC) and four fairness metrics (accuracy gap, worst-group accuracy, demographic parity, equalized odds) computed per sensitive subgroup.

## Method

NH-Fair introduces a unified evaluation protocol built around two key technical components. First, a systematic ERM tuning study identifies which training choices (optimizer, learning rate, pretrained initialization) most impact the fairness-utility trade-off, yielding empirically grounded HPO guidelines that reduce the expensive search space. Second, a two-stage model selection procedure—DTO-based ERM selection followed by a Fairness-Without-Harm (FWH) zone classification (Optimal, Sub-optimal, Degradation, Unwanted)—ensures fairness gains are measured relative to a strong, well-tuned baseline rather than a weak one, enforcing that no group's risk exceeds the ERM baseline. The benchmark evaluates 12 bias mitigation methods (data-centric: RandAugment, Mixup, Resampling, Bias Mimicking, FIS; algorithmic: Decoupled, LAFTR, FSCL, GapReg, MCDP, GroupDRO, DFR, OxonFair) alongside zero-shot CLIP, BLIP-2, and LVLMs from 4B to 109B parameters, all under standardized data splits, metrics, and training protocols.

## Results

Across seven datasets, a well-tuned ERM baseline (selected via DTO) achieves competitive or superior fairness metrics compared to most specialized debiasing methods: e.g., on CelebA, ERM reaches 86.57% ACC with a 6.76% accuracy gap, while MCDP drops to 80.26% ACC to achieve a 7.52% gap. RandAugment consistently improves both utility and fairness without harm (e.g., Fitz17k AUC rises from 89.74 to 91.29, Gap drops from 2.92 to 2.51; CelebA ACC rises from 86.57 to 86.72). Among LVLMs, Qwen2.5-VL 72B achieves the best utility-fairness balance (UTKFace: 96.78% ACC, 2.36% Gap; CelebA: 68.76% ACC, 4.70% Gap), yet on challenging datasets like Facet, several LVLMs exhibit worse worst-group accuracy than the ERM baseline. Scaling LVLM size (e.g., LLaVA-1.6 from 7B to 34B) increases average ACC from 68.16% to 70.79% but widens the accuracy Gap from 3.89% to 7.30%, showing that larger models do not reliably reduce disparities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central finding is that a carefully tuned ERM (selected via DTO) matches or outperforms most fairness-specific algorithms on utility while remaining competitive on fairness metrics; no single debiasing method dominates across all seven datasets (confirmed by Friedman/Nemenyi tests). RandAugment, though not designed for bias mitigation, is the only method that consistently lands in the 'Optimal' (fairness-without-harm) zone across all 7 datasets, improving both ACC/AUC and Gap simultaneously (e.g., Fitz17k AUC +1.55, Gap −0.41). Regularization-based methods GapReg and MCDP achieve the strongest DP and EqOdd scores (e.g., MCDP DP=93.11 on CelebA vs. ERM 67.20) but at a significant utility cost (MCDP ACC drops to 80.26 on CelebA, a 6.3-point loss). GroupDRO and DFR are competitive on utility but do not consistently close the accuracy gap; DFR excels on Waterbirds (AUC 89.83, Gap 1.47) but underperforms on demographic datasets. Among LVLMs, Qwen2.5-VL 72B and Gemma-3 27B deliver the best utility-fairness balance on balanced datasets (UTKFace Gap < 1%), yet on CelebA and Facet their worst-group accuracy falls below the ERM baseline, and scaling from 7B to 34B (LLaVA) or 11B to 90B (Llama) does not reliably reduce the accuracy gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the fairness-without-harm formulation introduced by Ustun et al. (2019) and the DTO-based model selection strategy from Han et al. (2022), combining them into a unified evaluation protocol. It departs from prior fairness benchmarks (MEDFAIR, FFB, ABCFair) by unifying classical vision models and large vision-language models under a single standardized pipeline, systematically sweeping hyperparameters (10,000+ A100 GPU hours), and covering both supervised and zero-shot regimes. The paper also extends the evaluation scope to state-of-the-art LVLMs (4B–109B parameters) that prior benchmarks either omitted or treated in isolation from classical vision models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a well-tuned ERM with proper hyperparameter search and DTO model selection consistently rivals or outperforms specialized fairness algorithms provides practitioners with a concrete, low-overhead starting point before investing in complex debiasing pipelines. The demonstration that LVLMs do not inherently resolve subgroup disparities—despite massive, diverse pretraining—challenges the common assumption that scaling alone addresses fairness and redirects attention toward architectural and training-protocol choices. Open questions include whether the FWH selection protocol generalizes beyond binary sensitive attributes, whether the 7-dataset coverage captures the full diversity of real-world deployment scenarios, and whether future LVLM generations with explicit fairness-aware pretraining will close the observed gaps.
