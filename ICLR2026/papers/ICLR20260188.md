## Problem

Standard RL for fine-grained visual reasoning on structured inputs like transit maps is impeded by inherently sparse rewards: success is judged only at the final answer after a long reasoning chain, causing low-signal or high-variance gradients that destabilize optimization. Existing cold-start strategies (SFT) provide dense supervision but fail to equip models for the long-chain decision-making intrinsic to visual reasoning, creating a mismatch between task complexity and the available signal.

## Input / Output

The model receives a high-resolution transit map image (from 30 cities across 13 countries) and a text question in VQA format, and produces a formatted text answer. For REASONMAP-PLUS (perception tasks), answers are single tokens enclosed in \boxed{} (a numeral or yes/no); for REASONMAP (planning tasks), the answer is a structured multi-segment route specifying origin/destination stops, route names, transfer stations, and segment count, scored by the benchmark's official evaluation algorithm. All evaluations use greedy decoding with a 2,048-token output cap.

## Method

REWARDMAP is a multi-stage GRPO-based RL framework with two complementary designs. First, a difficulty-aware reward function combines format, correctness, and a detail reward (partial credit for correct origin/destination stops, route names, transfer stations, and segment count, computed via Algorithm 1), all scaled by a difficulty weight that factors in both map difficulty (easy/medium/hard) and question difficulty (transfer count), with a tunable coefficient α=0.5 controlling the detail term's contribution. Second, a multi-stage curriculum schedules training from simple perception (True/False → Counting in REASONMAP-PLUS) to complex reasoning (planning in REASONMAP), applying a global coarse-to-fine ordering with local stochasticity (shuffling within each stage) to prevent overfitting to a fixed trajectory. This replaces SFT-based cold-start with direct RL on dense-reward questions, ensuring reward signals align with task objectives from the outset.

## Results

On REASONMAP, REWARDMAP (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) achieves 31.51%/31.77% weighted accuracy (short/long questions) and a weighted map score of 6.21/11.22, substantially surpassing the 72B reference model (26.65%/24.22%) and approaching Seed1.5-VL (34.20%/38.02%). On REASONMAP-PLUS, it reaches 74.25% weighted accuracy (72.18% Count / 76.42% True-or-False), exceeding all open-source models and outperforming Seed1.5-VL (73.58%). Across six generalization benchmarks (SEED-Bench-2-Plus, SpatialEval, V* Bench, HRBench, ChartQA, MMStar), REWARDMAP yields a 3.47% average improvement over the base model, with the largest gain of +13.51% on SpatialEval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REWARDMAP outperforms all RL baselines (GRPO, REINFORCE++, ReMax) on both REASONMAP and REASONMAP-PLUS; for example, on REASONMAP weighted accuracy it reaches 31.51% vs. 26.22% (GRPO), 27.17% (REINFORCE++), and 26.22% (ReMax). Against the SFT→RL pipeline, REWARDMAP gains +2.69% on REASONMAP short-question accuracy and +13.72% on REASONMAP-PLUS weighted accuracy (74.25% vs. 60.53%), demonstrating that direct multi-stage RL cold-start is more effective than SFT initialization. Compared to the RL baseline that uses the same combined training data but only format+correctness rewards, REWARDMAP adds +2.00% on REASONMAP and +6.64% on REASONMAP-PLUS, isolating the contribution of the difficulty-aware detail reward and multi-stage curriculum. Among reference models, the 7B REWARDMAP model surpasses the 10× larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct on REASONMAP (31.51% vs. 26.65%) and REASONMAP-PLUS (74.25% vs. 53.21%), and edges out Seed1.5-VL on REASONMAP-PLUS (74.25% vs. 73.58%) while remaining below it on REASONMAP (31.51% vs. 34.20%). On the six generalization benchmarks, REWARDMAP improves over the SFT→RL baseline on all six (e.g., SpatialEval 70.81% vs. 69.06%, V* Bench 80.10% vs. 78.01%), confirming that the gains are not confined to transit-map tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on REASONMAP (Feng et al., 2025b), a benchmark for fine-grained visual reasoning on high-resolution transit maps, and on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as the underlying RL algorithm. It departs from the standard SFT-then-RL cold-start pipeline (Xu et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2025) by using RL itself for cold-start, leveraging dense-reward VQA questions from the newly constructed REASONMAP-PLUS dataset. The multi-stage curriculum design draws on curriculum RL for LLMs (Parashar et al., 2025; Kimi K1.5) and Logic-RL (Xie et al., 2025b), but adapts these ideas to the structured visual domain where reward sparsity is particularly acute due to long reasoning chains over spatial topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, reproducible recipe for applying RL to fine-grained visual reasoning where sparse rewards have been a recognized bottleneck, and the consistent 3.47% average gain across six diverse benchmarks (spatial, fine-grained, general) suggests the learned perceptual skills transfer beyond transit maps. However, the evaluation is limited to two model families (Qwen2.5-VL, Kimi-VL) at small-to-medium scale, and the detail reward design is hand-crafted for the route-planning answer structure, raising open questions about how to generalize the reward decomposition to other structured visual domains (charts, diagrams, circuit schematics). The authors note this direction explicitly in their future work, and the modest absolute improvements (e.g., 31.51% weighted accuracy on REASONMAP vs. 59.98% for GPT-5) indicate substantial headroom remains.
