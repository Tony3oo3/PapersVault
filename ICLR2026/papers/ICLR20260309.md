## Problem

Existing image editing and generation models operate on flat pixel arrays without explicit object-level structure, making it impossible to isolate, complete, and export individual scene elements as reusable RGBA layers. While amodal segmentation and inpainting can recover occluded content, they produce masks or partial images rather than composable transparent layers, and no large-scale dataset, evaluation protocol, or baseline exists for prompt-driven layer decomposition from a single RGB image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260309/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image together with a referring prompt that can be spatial (a point rendered as a Gaussian heatmap, a bounding box as a green region, or a segmentation mask as a red region, all on a blue canvas), linguistic (free-form text), or a combination of both. The output is a complete RGBA layer for the referred object, encompassing both the originally visible pixels and the reconstructed occluded regions, with a continuous alpha channel capturing soft boundaries. For foreground layers, a cleanly-removed background image is provided alongside the ground-truth layer to enable alpha-blending-based evaluation.

## Method

RefLayer formulates RLD as conditional image generation on top of a pretrained diffusion transformer (initialized from UltraEdit for best results). The original image and a color-coded spatial prompt image are each encoded by a frozen VAE encoder, then concatenated channel-wise with a noisy latent before being fed to the diffusion transformer, which is conditioned additionally on text tokens. After denoising, a standard RGB decoder reconstructs the color content while a separate lightweight alpha decoder (mirroring the VAE decoder architecture but with a single output channel) predicts the transparency mask directly in latent space, decoupling matting from the generative pipeline. Training freezes the VAE, optimizes the transformer with standard denoising MSE loss, and trains the alpha decoder independently with L1 loss on blended-layer latents. The supporting RefLade dataset (1.11M image-layer-prompt triplets) is produced by a six-stage automated data engine that chains detection ensembles (RT-DETR, OWL-V2, Grounding-DINO), SAM-V2 segmentation, depth-guided inpainting, matting, GPT-4o captioning, and Gemini-2.0 quality filtering, raising the per-image success rate from 36% (MuLAn) to 70%.

## Results

On the RefLade RLD benchmark, RefLayer trained on the full 1.1M RefLade+Q dataset achieves an HPA score of 0.4813 (foreground) and 0.6682 (background), substantially outperforming the MuLAn-trained baseline (0.3852/0.3459) by ~10 and ~32 HPA points respectively. On the COCOA amodal segmentation benchmark (zero-shot), RefLayer-γ achieves mIoU_occ of 38.83, surpassing the previous best C2F-Seg (27.71) by 11.1 points, while remaining competitive on mIoU_full (75.04 vs. 80.28). For amodal completion on RefLade, RefLayer-α outperforms Pix2Gestalt across all metrics (HPA 0.4833 vs. 0.3397, FID 10.43 vs. 18.23, LPIPS 0.1298 vs. 0.1671).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MuLAn (Tudosiu et al., 2024), RefLayer wins decisively on the RLD task: training on RefLade 1M yields HPA 0.4685 vs. 0.3852 for MuLAn 50K, and the amodal completion HPA is 0.4833 vs. 0.3852, with FID improving from 22.68 to 10.43. On COCOA amodal segmentation, RefLayer-γ achieves the best mIoU_occ (38.83) among all methods, beating C2F-Seg (27.71) by 11.1 points and Pix2Gestalt (26.79) by 12.0 points; however, C2F-Seg retains a lead on mIoU_full (80.28 vs. 75.04), indicating RefLayer's strength is specifically in occluded-region completion rather than full-mask accuracy. Against AISformer (Tran et al., 2022), RefLayer wins on both mIoU_full (75.04 vs. 72.69) and mIoU_occ (38.83 vs. 13.75). Against PCNet (Zhan et al., 2020) and VRSP (Xiao et al., 2021), RefLayer-γ trails on mIoU_full (75.04 vs. 76.91 and 78.98) but dominates on mIoU_occ (38.83 vs. 20.34 and 22.92). Against Pix2Gestalt (Ozguroglu et al., 2024), RefLayer wins on every reported metric in both amodal segmentation and completion, confirming that the RLD formulation with its unified RGBA output provides a meaningful advantage over two-stage inpainting-plus-matting pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends several adjacent tasks—amodal segmentation/completion, referring expression segmentation, promptable segmentation (SAM/SAM 2), and prior layer decomposition efforts (MuLAn, Text2Layer, LayerDecomp)—into a single prompt-conditioned RGBA generation problem. Where prior layer decomposition work operates on entire images without user-specified targets and relies on small-scale or synthetic supervision, RLD introduces flexible multi-modal prompts (points, boxes, masks, text, or combinations) and a 1.11M-scale dataset built by a six-stage automated data engine. The baseline model (RefLayer) builds on Stable Diffusion 3 / UltraEdit with a lightweight parallel alpha decoder, departing from prior designs that either entangle matting with the main diffusion pipeline or require separate post-hoc matting steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RefLade establishes the first large-scale, prompt-driven benchmark for layer decomposition with a human-preference-aligned evaluation protocol (HPA score, 0.96 Pearson / 1.0 Spearman with Elo), making RLD a trainable and comparable research task for the first time. The zero-shot transfer to COCOA amodal segmentation (SOTA mIoU_occ of 38.83 without COCOA training) suggests that RLD is a sufficiently rich task to subsume amodal completion as a byproduct. Key open questions include handling shadows/reflections, part-level granularity, mutual occlusion during re-composition, and closing the gap for text-only prompting, which currently lags significantly behind spatial prompts.
