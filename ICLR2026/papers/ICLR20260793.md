## Problem

Drag-based editing in diffusion models relies on implicit point matching via shared attention tokens, which biases attention toward spatially nearby rather than semantically related regions, causing unstable edits. This fundamental instability forces prior methods into compromises—weakened inversion strength or costly per-image test-time optimization—that suppress inpainting fidelity, limit text-guided generation, and degrade overall edit quality. No prior method achieves stable full-strength inversion without TTO, and none operates within the newer Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT) architecture.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, one or more drag instructions (each specifying a handle point and a target point), and an optional text prompt for disambiguation or content generation. It produces a single edited image at the input resolution (1024×1024 for non-benchmark images) after 50 denoising steps with full-strength inversion. The method is entirely training-free and requires no per-image fine-tuning or multi-step latent optimization; inversion is a one-time cost per image, and subsequent edits only require map generation and synthesis.

## Method

LazyDrag replaces fragile implicit attention-based point matching with an explicit correspondence map derived deterministically from drag instructions. A winner-takes-all (Voronoi) fusion assigns each latent grid point to its nearest handle, producing a displacement field and a matching weight map that partition the latent space into background, destination, inpainting, and transition regions; inpainting regions are initialized with Gaussian noise rather than BNNI interpolation to enable high-fidelity text-guided generation. During sampling, a two-part attention control operates on single-stream attention layers: background regions have their Q/K/V tokens hard-replaced with RoPE-re-encoded cached originals, while destination and transition regions concatenate cached source tokens (retrieved via the correspondence map) into K and V for identity preservation. An attention output refinement step applies a gated merge whose blending factor is modulated by the pre-computed correspondence weight, ensuring strongest control at handle points and natural relaxation elsewhere. Built on FLUX.1 Krea-dev (MM-DiT) with UniEdit-Flow inversion, the method achieves stable full-strength inversion without any test-time optimization.

## Results

On DragBench (205 images, 349 drag pairs), LazyDrag achieves the best mean distance (MD) of 21.49±0.04, outperforming the closest TTO-based baseline DragText (21.51±0.21) and the best TTO-free baseline FastDrag (31.84±0.96). It also leads on all VIEScore metrics: Semantic Consistency (SC) 8.205, Perceptual Quality (PQ) 8.395, and Overall (O) 8.210, all without requiring any test-time optimization. In a user study with 32 participants, LazyDrag was preferred in 63.67% of comparisons, far exceeding the next-best method at 6.64%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LazyDrag outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest TTO-based method, DragText (MD 21.51, O 7.886), LazyDrag matches on MD (21.49 vs 21.51) while substantially improving SC (8.205 vs 7.992), PQ (8.395 vs 8.227), and O (8.210 vs 7.886), all without per-image fine-tuning. Against the best TTO-free baseline FastDrag (MD 31.84, O 7.904), LazyDrag reduces MD by over 10 points and improves O by 0.31. Inpaint4Drag, the other TTO-free method, trails LazyDrag on all metrics (MD 23.68 vs 21.49; O 7.615 vs 8.210), with the paper attributing its lower scores to boundary artifacts and color shifts from mask-sensitive inpainting. The remaining TTO-based baselines (DragNoise, DragDiffusion, FreeDrag, DiffEditor, GoodDrag) are all clearly behind LazyDrag on both accuracy and perceptual quality, with MD values ranging from 22.17 to 37.87. In the user study, LazyDrag's 63.67% preference rate dwarfs the next-best method (DragNoise at 6.64%), confirming the quantitative advantage in perceived edit quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

LazyDrag sits at the intersection of two research threads: training-free drag-based editing (FastDrag, DragDiffusion, GoodDrag) and identity-preserving attention control in diffusion models (MasaCtrl, CharaConsist). It departs from both by (i) shifting the backbone from U-Nets to Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformers (FLUX), which is the first drag-based editing method on this architecture, and (ii) replacing the implicit attention-similarity point matching used by CharaConsist and the locality-biased KV sharing inherited from MasaCtrl with an explicit, deterministic correspondence map derived directly from user drag instructions. The winner-takes-all displacement fusion and Gaussian-noise inpainting initialization are refinements over FastDrag's averaging and BNNI interpolation, while the RoPE-based token concatenation and gated output blending adapt CharaConsist's mechanism to a correspondence-driven setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that the long-standing stability-quality compromise in drag editing (TTO or weakened inversion) is an artifact of unreliable implicit point matching rather than a fundamental limitation, the paper reframes the design space for future drag-based methods. The practical impact is significant: full-strength inversion without TTO unlocks text-guided generation and high-fidelity inpainting within drag editing—capabilities that were previously mutually exclusive with geometric control. Limitations include dependence on the base MM-DiT model quality, sensitivity to very small drag distances due to VAE compression, and higher memory usage (~62 GB) compared to U-Net baselines. Open questions include whether the explicit correspondence approach generalizes to video editing, whether more sophisticated matching strategies (e.g., 2D rotation) can further improve controllability, and how the method scales as MM-DiT backbones grow larger.
