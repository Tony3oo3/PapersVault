## Problem

Existing hyperparameter transfer methods (μP, Depth-μP, CompleteP) only handle global hyperparameters across model width and depth, leaving two critical gaps: (1) whether per-module hyperparameters — which introduce a high-dimensional, non-convex search landscape with sharp stability cliffs — can be optimized at small scale and transferred to large models, and (2) how to transfer hyperparameters across the other essential scaling axes of batch size and training duration (token horizon), where no principled rules existed for adaptive optimizers like AdamW.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of base hyperparameters (learning rate, weight decay, AdamW ε, β₁, β₂, and initialization scale) optimized on a small proxy model (50M params / 1.6B tokens), along with target width ratio m_N, depth ratio m_L, batch-size ratio m_B, and token-count ratio m_D. It outputs a complete scaled training configuration with 79 per-module hyperparameters (13 module types × 6 HPs, plus per-depth residual multipliers) that can be directly applied to a ~10000× larger FLOP budget without any further tuning. The per-module HPs are structured via a depth-Kronecker factorization (log ζ_{m,ℓ} = type + depth + SDE + CompleteP terms), reducing free multipliers from |M|×L to |M|+L and enabling linear interpolation of depth multipliers when transferring to larger depths.

## Method

The paper proposes Complete^(d)P, a unified parameterisation that extends CompleteP to handle QK-norm weight sharing (requiring distinct scaling for width-shared parameters) and corrects the AdamW ε scaling for input embeddings, while adding SDE-based rules for batch-size transfer (extending Malladi et al.'s Adam SDE to AdamW weight decay, yielding η' = √κ·η and λ' = √κ·λ) and a novel token-horizon iso-horizon SDE scaling rule (η' = η/√κ) that preserves the AdamW SDE integration horizon. For per-module optimization, the authors characterize the loss landscape as near-invex but with complex, fractal-like stability boundaries that defeat random search and standard Bayesian Optimization, and instead use a trust-region random search in log-space with adaptive bounding-box decay. The key empirical result is that per-module HPs optimized at 50M scale transfer directly to 7.2B scale via Complete^(d)P, retaining a 1.32× training speed-up and improved downstream benchmark performance over optimal global HPs, with the depth-Kronecker factorization capturing virtually all gains of fully uncoupled per-layer multipliers.

## Results

At the 50M parameter / 1.6B token scale, per-module hyperparameters (79 HPs across 13 module types) achieve a 2.3× training speed-up over the best global hyperparameters (which yield 3.34 nats validation NLL). After zero-shot transfer via Complete(d)P to a ~10,000× larger FLOP budget (7.2B parameters / 143B tokens), the per-module HPs retain a 1.32× speed-up and improved downstream benchmark performance. The SDE iso-horizon scaling rule for token-horizon transfer yields a better asymptotic scaling-law coefficient compared to CompleteP without the token-horizon rule (Figure 6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between per-module hyperparameters and the best global hyperparameters (found via a 2048-trial random search at 50M scale). Per-module HPs win decisively: 2.3× faster at 50M and 1.32× faster after transfer to 7.2B, with the gap narrowing slightly at scale but remaining substantial. For the search algorithm, Trust-region Random Search outperforms an adapted CMA-ES variant (Hansen, 2006) on the per-module LR multiplier search (Figure 17), validating the chosen optimisation strategy. The paper also compares its Complete(d)P parameterisation against the original CompleteP (Dey et al., 2025), showing that the QK-norm extension and AdamW ε fix restore stable transfer for α=0.5 depth scaling, which Dey et al. reported as degraded. Against Bjorck et al. (2025) for token-horizon LR scaling, the paper's SDE iso-horizon rule (η/√κ) aligns with their empirically fitted exponents (β≈0.3–0.7) but additionally extends to weight decay, AdamW momenta, and ε, and yields better asymptotic scaling-law performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the μP/CompleteP lineage of hyperparameter transfer parameterisations in two major directions. First, it unifies width, depth, batch-size, and token-horizon scaling into a single Complete^d P framework, extending the SDE reparameterisation of Malladi et al. (2022) to AdamW weight decay and proposing a new SDE-inspired token-horizon rule. Second, it moves from global to per-module hyperparameter optimisation and transfer, a regime not addressed by prior μP or CompleteP work. The depth-Kronecker factorisation for per-module HPs and the trust-region search methodology are novel practical contributions that build on evolutionary strategy and Bayesian optimisation literature but are tailored to the specific pathologies of the per-module landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1.32× training speed-up at 7.2B parameters from hyperparameters optimised at 50M, with a complete recipe for practitioners to replicate. The unified parameterisation across all four scaling axes removes a major source of ad-hoc tuning in large-scale training. Key open questions include whether the per-module speed-up persists or diminishes at asymptotic scale, whether findings generalise beyond a single decoder-only transformer on RedPajama with a fixed cosine schedule, and whether more efficient search methods (e.g., trust-region Bayesian optimisation) can reduce the 5000-trial budget. The observation that depth-Kronecker factorisation captures virtually all the benefit of fully uncoupled per-layer LRs is practically reassuring but not yet proven optimal.
