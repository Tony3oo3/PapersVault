## Problem

Existing long video generation methods lack explicit temporal causality modeling: temporal co-denoising enforces only local correlation between overlapping clips (causing action-direction conflicts), while I2V-AR conditions solely on the last frame (causing motion reversal and loss of earlier dynamics). Neither approach supports fine-grained temporal control over multi-action sequences, leading to skipped, incomplete, or contradictory actions in generated videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260355/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user text prompt (optionally with a first-frame image) that an MLLM decomposes into a scene description and a sequence of action-duration pairs (each duration in frames at 15 fps, constrained to 4N+1 format by the underlying VAE). During autoregressive continuation, each new segment uses ~20% of the previous segment's frames as history (13–25 frames) plus the current action prompt, producing 480×720 video at 15 fps with total lengths up to 30 seconds across 2–5 actions.

## Method

SteinsGate is a plug-and-play, inference-time framework built on Wan2.1 (a DiT-based flow-matching TI2V model) that adds temporal causality via two stages. Stage 1 (Temporal Action Binding) uses an MLLM with in-context learning on dense-caption video data to decompose prompts into scene descriptions and action sequences with predicted per-action durations. Stage 2 (Causal Video Continuation) introduces the Video Path Integral (VPI): at each denoising step, K=2–3 historical frames are randomly sampled as I2V conditions, their predicted velocity fields are weighted by motion-aware history alignment, and the weighted sum approximates the conditional distribution p(z_i | z_history) as a product of frame-wise conditionals (compositional generation). Three practical optimizations reduce cost and improve quality: Guidance Interval restricts VPI to the high-noise phase (t ≤ 0.3T), History-aligned Redistribution weights paths by cosine similarity of predicted vs. true motion vectors, and Path Convergence Guidance extrapolates along the difference between the VPI velocity (strong model) and last-frame I2V velocity (weak model) with weight w=1.5, analogous to AutoGuidance.

## Results

On the InstructVC Benchmark, SteinsGate achieves a Clip Similarity Coefficient of Variation (CSCV) of 0.82, Motion Smoothness of 0.97, and Text-Image Alignment of 0.32. As a training-free, inference-time method, SteinsGate matches or exceeds the training-based diffusion-forcing baselines SkyReel-V2 (CSCV 0.83, Motion Smoothness 0.96) and MAGI-1 (CSCV 0.82, Motion Smoothness 0.96) on motion smoothness while remaining competitive on transition smoothness. It outperforms the other training-free baselines DiTCtrl (CSCV 0.76) and FIFO-Diffusion (CSCV 0.71) by a clear margin across all three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SteinsGate outperforms both training-free baselines across all metrics: it beats DiTCtrl by +0.06 CSCV, +0.04 Motion Smoothness, and +0.01 Text-Image Alignment, and beats FIFO-Diffusion by +0.11 CSCV, +0.08 Motion Smoothness, and +0.03 Text-Image Alignment. Against the training-based SkyReel-V2, SteinsGate achieves a slightly lower CSCV (0.82 vs. 0.83) and Text-Image Alignment (0.32 vs. 0.34) but a higher Motion Smoothness (0.97 vs. 0.96), demonstrating comparable overall quality without any training. Against MAGI-1, SteinsGate ties on CSCV (0.82), edges ahead on Motion Smoothness (0.97 vs. 0.96), and is marginally behind on Text-Image Alignment (0.32 vs. 0.33). The paper's key claim is that SteinsGate, as a plug-and-play inference-time method built on the pretrained Wan2.1 model, achieves performance on par with costly training-based diffusion-forcing approaches while adding only ~25% inference overhead (38 min vs. 30 min for a 15-second video).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the temporal decomposition paradigm for long video generation (FreeLong, Gen-L-Video, DiTCtrl) and autoregressive video continuation (FIFO-Diffusion, SkyReel-V2, MAGI-1), but departs from both by introducing an explicit temporal causality mechanism at inference time. The Video Path Integral draws on compositional generation (Du et al., 2023) and the flow-matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023), but repurposes the I2V spatial-temporal disentanglement of pretrained DiT models to integrate over multiple historical-frame-conditioned vector fields, effectively extending I2V to History-to-Future without retraining. The InstructVC framework's Temporal Action Binding stage leverages MLLMs (GPT-4o) for prompt decomposition with predicted durations, extending prior multi-prompt methods (DiTCtrl, MEVG) that assumed equal time spans and lacked global causal planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a plug-and-play, training-free technique that converts any pretrained TI2V diffusion model into an autoregressive causal video continuation model, achieving performance comparable to costly training-based diffusion-forcing approaches (SkyReel-V2, MAGI-1) with only ~25% inference overhead. This opens a path for long multi-action video generation without the expensive retraining that current autoregressive video models require for each new foundation model. However, long-term consistency beyond a few segments remains an open problem, the method is validated only on Wan2.1 with a 60-prompt benchmark, and the 4N+1 frame constraint limits flexibility. The Video Path Integral concept, if it generalizes across architectures, could become a standard inference-time tool for temporal coherence in video diffusion.
