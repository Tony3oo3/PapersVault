## Problem

Despite numerous algorithms proposed for fairness-constrained training of deep neural networks, no standard benchmark or unified toolkit exists to compare them on real-world tasks. The constrained ERM formulation faces simultaneous challenges of non-convexity, non-smoothness, large-scale stochastic objectives and inequality constraints, for which no algorithm currently provides convergence guarantees. This paper identifies this gap and provides the first systematic benchmark on real-world fairness-constrained learning.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes tabular data from the ACSIncome dataset (US Census via Folktables) with 9 features and a binary income label, along with a user-specified protected attribute (e.g., race binarized to white/non-white, or marriage status with 5 values) and a fairness constraint formulation bounding the loss difference between subgroups. The output is a trained 2-hidden-layer DNN (64 and 32 units, 194 parameters) producing binary predictions, evaluated on three fairness metrics (independence, separation, sufficiency), inaccuracy, and Wasserstein distance between group prediction distributions, reported on both train and test splits.

## Method

The paper implements and compares three recently proposed but previously unimplemented stochastic algorithms—Stochastic Ghost (StGh), Stochastic Smoothed and Linearized AL Method (SSL-ALM), and Stochastic Switching Subgradient (SSw)—against SGD and Fairret-regularized SGD baselines. StGh solves a QP subproblem at each iteration using a geometrically-distributed batch size to construct an unbiased direction estimate via a multilevel Monte Carlo scheme. SSL-ALM minimizes a proximal augmented Lagrangian with a Moreau-envelope smoothing term, using single-sample stochastic gradients and slack variables to handle inequality constraints. SSw alternates between objective and constraint subgradient steps based on a diminishing infeasibility tolerance, with separate stepsizes for each. All methods are evaluated on two experimental setups: a binary protected attribute (race, Oklahoma) and a multi-valued protected attribute (marriage, Virginia), with hyperparameters tuned via grid search on validation sets.

## Results

On the ACSIncome (Oklahoma) binary race-protected task, SSL-ALM achieves the best train-set independence (0.074) and separation (0.091) among all constrained methods, with test-set values of 0.083 and 0.108 respectively, while incurring only a moderate accuracy drop (test inaccuracy 0.223 vs. 0.209 for unconstrained SGD). Stochastic Ghost attains the best sufficiency (0.048 train, 0.057 test) but the worst inaccuracy (0.239 test). SSw achieves the lowest Wasserstein distance between group distributions (0.018 test) but fails to minimize the objective, yielding inaccuracy comparable to unconstrained SGD (0.212 test). Overall, the AL-based methods (SSL-ALM, ALM) provide the best fairness–accuracy trade-off, improving all three fairness criteria over unconstrained SGD at the cost of ~1–3 percentage points in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unconstrained SGD, all four constrained methods (StGh, ALM, SSL-ALM, SSw) improve independence and separation on the test set, with SSL-ALM showing the largest gains (test Ind 0.083 vs. 0.098, test Sp 0.108 vs. 0.155) at a cost of ~1.4 pp inaccuracy. Against the Fairret-regularized SGD baseline, the constrained methods dominate on separation (SSL-ALM 0.108 vs. 0.141) and sufficiency (SSL-ALM 0.046 vs. 0.056), while SGD-Fairret retains a slight edge on inaccuracy (0.211 vs. 0.223). Stochastic Ghost wins on sufficiency (0.057 test) but loses badly on inaccuracy (0.239 vs. 0.209 for SGD), indicating it over-prioritizes the fairness constraint. SSw achieves the tightest group-distribution alignment (Wasserstein distance 0.018 test) but its fairness metrics (Ind 0.103, Sp 0.168) are actually worse than unconstrained SGD, confirming that with the chosen hyperparameters it favored objective minimization over constraint satisfaction. ALM shows an anomalous test-set sufficiency of 0.221, far worse than all other methods, suggesting poor generalization of that particular constraint formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: stochastic constrained optimization (Stochastic Ghost, SSL-ALM, Stochastic Switching Subgradient) and fairness-aware machine learning (constrained ERM formulations from Cotter et al. 2019, penalization approaches via Fairret, adversarial methods). While prior toolboxes like AIF360, FairLearn, and Cooper address fairness evaluation or Lagrangian-based training, and benchmarks like Han et al. (2023) survey in-processing methods, none provide a standardized comparison of stochastic approximation algorithms specifically for non-convex, non-smooth, inequality-constrained DNN training. The paper departs from the penalization-based paradigm by focusing on hard-constraint formulations, following the argument of Ramirez et al. (2025) that constraints offer clearer design semantics than weighted penalty sums.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a critical gap: no existing algorithm has convergence guarantees for the full problem setting (stochastic objective and constraints, non-convex, non-smooth, inequality constraints), and this benchmark is the first to systematically compare practical algorithms under identical conditions on real-world data. The finding that AL-based methods (SSL-ALM, ALM) offer the best accuracy-fairness trade-off while SSw satisfies constraints most tightly but sacrifices objective minimization provides actionable guidance for practitioners. Open questions include the generalization gap observed on test sets (slight bias toward negative constraint values), scalability to the full 5.7-billion-subgroup Folktables space, and the development of algorithms with theoretical guarantees for this setting. The authors explicitly caution that constrained training is not a silver bullet for ethical ML and must be embedded in broader interdisciplinary pipelines.
