## Problem

Prior gene expression prediction from H&E histology is either restricted to single-cancer settings or relies on stable gene-gene correlation assumptions (e.g., masked-gene modeling) that break down across heterogeneous pan-cancer tissues. Additionally, evaluation has been dominated by variance-based metrics on top-N highly variable genes, leaving the functional and pathway-level biological coherence of predictions largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262502/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes non-overlapping H&E patch features (D_img-dimensional vectors extracted by a frozen pathology foundation model such as UNI) together with a one-hot cancer type vector, and produces a per-patch gene expression vector of dimension 38,982 (log1p-normalized counts). The architecture is permutation-invariant over patches (no positional encoding by default), so the spatial arrangement of patches is not explicitly encoded; instead, the transformer encoder aggregates global compositional context across the variable number of patches per slide.

## Method

HistoPrism is a lightweight direct-mapping transformer with three stages: (1) a single cross-attention layer where the projected cancer-type embedding provides Key/Value and patch features provide Query, injecting pan-cancer conditioning; (2) a 2-layer transformer encoder (8 heads, 256 hidden dim) that captures short- and long-range spatial dependencies among patches; and (3) an MLP regression head that maps each patch's latent representation to the full gene expression vector. Training is end-to-end supervised with MSE loss and AdamW, requiring only ~500 WSIs. The key architectural insight is that a direct visual-to-molecular mapping, rather than contrastive alignment or generative reconstruction, better suits the modality-translation task and avoids the inductive bias of autoencoders. The paper also introduces the Gene Pathway Coherence (GPC) benchmark, which scores predictions by averaging per-gene PCC across curated Hallmark and Gene Ontology pathways (filtered to 50–100 genes with Jaccard redundancy removal), shifting evaluation from isolated variance to functional biological coherence.

## Results

On the HEST1k pan-cancer benchmark (top-50 HVGs, 10 cancer types), HistoPrism achieves a micro-average PCC of 0.318, surpassing STPath (0.292), STFlow (0.247), and STEM (0.180), while trailing STPath slightly on macro-average PCC (0.342 vs. 0.361). On the proposed Gene Pathway Coherence (GPC) benchmark, HistoPrism outperforms STPath on 86.0% of 50 Hallmark pathways and 74.7% of Gene Ontology pathways, with the largest gains on low-variance pathways. In holistic 38k-gene clustering, HistoPrism achieves AMI 0.623 and ARI 0.521 versus STPath's 0.395 and 0.402, demonstrating substantially more biologically coherent global representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STPath (the strongest baseline, a BERT-style masked-gene foundation model), HistoPrism trades a small macro-average PCC deficit (0.342 vs. 0.361) for a clear micro-average PCC advantage (0.318 vs. 0.292), indicating more balanced performance across heterogeneous cancer types. More critically, HistoPrism substantially outperforms STPath on the GPC pathway benchmark (winning 86% of Hallmark and 74.7% of GO pathways) and on 38k-gene clustering (AMI 0.623 vs. 0.395; ARI 0.521 vs. 0.402), suggesting STPath's masked-reconstruction objective is suboptimal for pure predictive tasks. Against STFlow (flow-matching generative model), HistoPrism leads by a wide margin on both macro (0.342 vs. 0.311) and micro (0.318 vs. 0.247) PCC, confirming that generative approaches struggle to generalize beyond single-cancer settings. STEM (diffusion-based) performs worst overall (macro 0.184, micro 0.180), with HistoPrism exceeding it by roughly 85% in relative PCC, casting doubt on STEM's original leave-one-out evaluation. HistoPrism also achieves these results with ~50% of STPath's training data and linear (vs. exponential) scaling in FLOPs, memory, and runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

HistoPrism departs from the dominant paradigms in histology-to-gene-expression prediction: contrastive/retrieval-based methods (BLEEP, GraphST), multi-resolution regression (TRIPLEX), generative models (STEM, STFlow), and the masked-gene-prediction foundation model STPath. Rather than learning joint embeddings or modeling the one-to-many mapping distributionally, it adopts a direct-mapping transformer that regresses gene expression from PFM-extracted patch features, with a lightweight cross-attention module injecting cancer-type conditioning. The paper also introduces the Gene Pathway Coherence (GPC) benchmark, which has no direct predecessor in the spatial transcriptomics prediction literature, repurposing Hallmark and GO pathway sets as a structured evaluation protocol rather than a training signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GPC benchmark addresses a genuine gap: prior evaluation relied almost exclusively on Pearson correlation over top-N highly variable genes, which can mask whether a model recovers biologically coherent, functionally meaningful expression programs. The finding that HistoPrism's largest gains concentrate on low-variance pathways (core housekeeping and metabolic processes) suggests that variance-based metrics systematically undervalue models that capture stable cellular programs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the HEST1k test splits (51 WSIs total), whether the direct-mapping architecture scales to even larger gene panels or 3D histology, and how to make the learned visual-to-molecular mapping interpretable enough for clinical trust. The linear-scaling efficiency profile is practically important for deployment in resource-limited clinical settings.
