## Problem

Existing analyses of offline KL-regularized contextual bandits either yield suboptimal Õ(ε⁻²) sample complexity or require the strong all-policy concentrability condition for Õ(ε⁻¹) rates, leaving the exact data coverage requirement unresolved. The paper asks what the weakest concentrability condition is for achieving near-optimal sample complexity under f-divergence-regularized objectives, and whether stronger curvature in the regularizer (e.g., strongly convex f) can eliminate coverage dependencies entirely.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives an i.i.d. offline dataset {(s_i, a_i, r_i)}_{i=1}^n where contexts are drawn from ρ, actions from a known reference policy π^ref, and rewards are 1-sub-Gaussian perturbations of a ground-truth function in a known class G with poly(ε⁻¹) covering number. For reverse KL, the output policy has the closed form π̂(a|s) ∝ π^ref(a|s)exp(η·ĝ(s,a)) where ĝ is a pessimistic reward estimate; for strongly convex f-divergence, the output is obtained by directly maximizing a regularized objective without any pessimistic correction. The key quantitative output is the sample complexity rate: n = Õ(ηD²_{π*}ε⁻¹log N) for KL and Õ(α⁻¹ηε⁻¹log N) for strongly convex f, each certified by a matching lower bound.

## Method

For reverse KL, the KL-PCB algorithm constructs a pessimistic reward estimator ĝ = ḡ − Γ_n (where Γ_n is a bonus scaled by the D²-divergence) and outputs the exponential-tilt policy. The key analytical insight is a moment-based lemma (if P(X≤0)=1 then E[X³]−E[X²]E[X]≤0) that, when applied to the derivative of the regret decomposition along the interpolation path between estimated and true reward, eliminates the mid-point policy and reduces the concentrability requirement from all-policy to single-policy. For f-divergences with α-strongly convex f, the f-CB algorithm is a simple plug-in method (no pessimism) whose analysis uses a dual-Bregman decomposition: the sub-optimality gap equals the Bregman divergence of the convex conjugate H*, whose Hessian is bounded by α⁻¹η·diag(π^ref), yielding a coverage-free bound. Matching lower bounds via Assouad's lemma confirm that the C^{π*} factor is necessary for KL and that α⁻¹ηε⁻¹log N is the information-theoretic limit for strongly convex f.

## Results

For KL-regularized contextual bandits, the proposed KL-PCB algorithm achieves a sample complexity of Õ(ηD²_{π*} ε⁻¹ log N) under single-policy concentrability (Theorem 2.10), with a matching lower bound of Ω(min{ηε⁻¹, ε⁻²} C^{π*} log N) (Theorem 2.11), establishing that single-policy concentrability is both necessary and sufficient for the sharp ε⁻¹ rate. For f-divergence regularization with α-strongly convex f, the f-CB algorithm achieves Õ(α⁻¹ η ε⁻¹ log N) with a matching lower bound Ω(α⁻¹ η ε⁻¹ log N) (Theorems 3.2 and 3.4), completely eliminating any data coverage dependency. Empirically, on multi-armed bandits, linear bandits, and MNIST, the fitted log-log slopes between sample size n and sub-optimality are approximately −1, confirming the predicted n⁻¹ scaling, and the KL-regularized sub-optimality gap varies with concentrability while the χ²-regularized gap does not.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Xiong et al. (2024), which achieves only Õ(dε⁻²) for reverse KL under all-policy concentrability, this paper improves the ε-dependency from ε⁻² to ε⁻¹ and relaxes the coverage requirement to single-policy concentrability. Compared to Zhao et al. (2024), which achieves Õ(ηD²ε⁻¹ log N) but requires the stronger all-policy concentrability (D² = sup over all state-action pairs), this paper achieves the same ε⁻¹ rate under the strictly weaker single-policy concentrability (D²_{π*} = expectation under π*), and further provides a matching lower bound with multiplicative C^{π*} dependency that was previously absent. For strongly convex f-divergences (e.g., χ²), no prior work provided any sample complexity bound; this paper establishes the first near-tight Õ(α⁻¹ η ε⁻¹ log N) guarantee that is free of any coverage condition, a qualitative improvement over the KL case. Empirically, on linear bandits with varying behavior policies (β, k) ∈ {(1,·), (0.1,4), (0.05,20)}, the KL-regularized sub-optimality gap shows a consistent margin across instances with different C^{π*} and D²_{π*}, while the χ²-regularized gap remains nearly identical across all instances, directly corroborating the theoretical coverage-dependence distinction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pessimism-based analysis framework from offline RL (Jin et al. 2021; Di et al. 2024) to the specific setting of f-divergence-regularized objectives, where prior work (Zhao et al. 2024; Xiong et al. 2024) either achieved only O(ε⁻²) rates under single-policy concentrability or required the stronger all-policy concentrability for O(ε⁻¹) rates. The key technical departure is a novel moment-based argument (Lemma 2.15) that exploits the strong convexity of KL to eliminate the mid-point policy dependency in the regret decomposition, enabling the sharp O(ε⁻¹) rate under the weaker single-policy condition. For strongly convex f-divergences, the analysis pivots to a dual-Bregman perspective (inspired by Abernethy et al. 2015) that bypasses the need for closed-form optimal policies entirely, a structural difference from the KL case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a concrete open question—identifying the weakest coverage condition for near-optimal offline learning under f-divergence-regularized objectives—with matching upper and lower bounds for two important subclasses. The finding that strongly convex f-divergences (e.g., χ²) provably eliminate all concentrability dependencies is a clean theoretical insight with implications for choosing regularizers in offline RLHF and preference-based learning. Open questions remain: the upper bound for KL uses D²_{π*} while the lower bound uses C^{π*}, leaving a gap in the exact characterization; and the analysis for general f-divergences is restricted to strongly convex, twice-continuously differentiable f, excluding the practically dominant reverse-KL case from the coverage-free regime.
