## Problem

The optimal transport (OT) for multi-draft speculative sampling is defined as the solution to a linear program with V^n variables, making it infeasible to solve for realistic vocabularies. While recent works reformulated the problem via importance sampling or subset selection, none provided an efficient algorithm to actually compute the OT verification criterion—only the optimal acceptance rate. This paper closes that gap by deriving the first efficient algorithm that computes the full OT to arbitrary accuracy in the i.i.d. multi-draft setting.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes the target distribution p, a single draft distribution q, the number of i.i.d. drafts n, a concrete drafted n-tuple ω, and a tunable error threshold τ. It outputs the optimal transport slice π(·|ω)—a conditional distribution over the vocabulary specifying the probability of verifying each candidate token given the n draft tokens—along with the optimal acceptance rate α*. The output is guaranteed to deviate from the true target distribution by at most 15τ in L1 distance and from the optimal acceptance rate by at most 10τ, with τ controlling the trade-off between accuracy and runtime.

## Method

The key insight is to reverse the subset selection derivation via complementary slackness, decomposing the relaxed OTLP into independent outer and inner max-flow systems parameterized by the optimal subset H*. For the i.i.d. draft setting, the authors exploit polymatroid theory to compute outer residuals in O(V log V) time, converting inequality constraints into equalities. This transforms both systems into convex minimization problems over at most V variables (truncated to a subset T for tractability), where the solution is a softmax-parameterized transport whose parameters are found by gradient descent on a convex function. The resulting 'global resolution' algorithm achieves 10,000× speedup over LP solvers, reaching 90%+ acceptance with under 100 ms overhead per token on Llama-3 70B/8B.

## Results

Under a 100 ms/token solve-time budget, Global Resolution (τ=10⁻³) achieves 90.04% optimal acceptance on Llama-3 70B/8B and 86.60% on Gemma-2 27B/2B, outperforming the best baseline (Max-Flow) by 1.03% and 0.77% respectively. Under a tighter 10 ms/token budget, Global Resolution reaches 85.65% (Llama-3) and 81.90% (Gemma-2), a 1.71% and 1.21% improvement over Max-Flow. In terms of raw solve time, Global Resolution is up to 10,000× faster than General LP and Max-Flow solvers (e.g., 34.94 ms vs. OOM/400000+ ms at k=1000, n=2), while always staying under 100 ms/token.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Global Resolution against three baseline OTLP solvers: a General LP solver, a standard Max-Flow solver (Section 5.2), and an Optimized Max-Flow solver using complementary slackness (Section 5.3). Global Resolution (τ=10⁻³) consistently achieves the highest acceptance rate across all model pairs and time budgets: on Llama-3 it beats Max-Flow by 1.71% at 10 ms/token and 1.03% at 100 ms/token, and on Gemma-2 by 1.21% and 0.77% respectively. The General LP solver is the weakest baseline, trailing Max-Flow by 1.4–1.9% in acceptance and being infeasible (OOM) at k=1000. The Optimized Max-Flow solver performs identically to standard Max-Flow in the reported results. In solve time, Global Resolution is dramatically faster: at (k=1000, n=2) it runs in 34.94 ms/token while Max-Flow exceeds 400,000 ms and General LP runs out of memory; at (k=10, n=4) it takes 40.30 ms vs. 4000+ ms for General LP. The paper notes that at low temperatures (<0.8), the gap narrows because concentrated distributions make truncation less critical, and simpler solvers become competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three recent theoretical threads in multi-draft speculative sampling: the OTLP formulation (Sun et al., 2023), the subset selection reduction (Hu et al., 2025), and the canonical decomposition into importance sampling plus single-draft verification (Khisti et al., 2025). The authors prove that the latter two are both equivalent to an exponentially large relaxed OTLP, unifying the literature, then reverse-engineer the subset selection derivation via complementary slackness to obtain a max-flow formulation. The key departure is the application of polymatroid theory to collapse the max-flow into a convex minimization over at most V variables, yielding the first algorithm that computes the full optimal transport (not just the acceptance rate) to arbitrary accuracy for i.i.d. multi-draft settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method that makes optimal multi-draft speculative sampling practically deployable: 90%+ acceptance with under 100 ms overhead per token, compared to 84% for generic LP solvers in the same regime. The tunable error threshold (τ) provides a principled trade-off between speed and fidelity to the target distribution, which is critical for production LLM serving. Limitations include restriction to i.i.d. drafts from a single model (non-i.i.d. and without-replacement settings are left to future work), and the convex minimization can still terminate early for very large vocabularies. Open questions include integration into multi-step frameworks like SpecTr with better failure-case heuristics, and whether the polymatroid structure extends to more general draft distributions.
