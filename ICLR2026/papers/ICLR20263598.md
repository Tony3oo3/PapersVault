## Problem

Existing offline preference-based RL algorithms face a fundamental trade-off: methods with rate-optimal O(ε^{-2}) sample complexity (e.g., FREEHAND-transition) are computationally intractable due to distributionally robust optimization, while computationally feasible methods like APPO suffer from suboptimal O(ε^{-4}) bounds, unstable training, and high performance variance. No prior algorithm simultaneously achieves strong theoretical guarantees, computational tractability, and robust empirical performance without extra preference-specific hyperparameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two offline datasets: a preference dataset of trajectory-segment pairs (length 25) with binary preference labels generated via a Bradley-Terry-Luce model, and a larger trajectory dataset of unlabeled segment pairs from a reference policy. In practice, the reward model is trained first via MLE on the preference data (taking under a minute for 1000 samples), then the Q and V networks are trained on the trajectory dataset. The output is a greedy policy extracted from the learned Q function via advantage-weighted regression, with no additional hyperparameters beyond those of standard IQL.

## Method

PVO introduces a value alignment loss that measures the squared difference in cumulative Bellman errors between trajectory pairs, rather than the standard per-step TD loss. The key mechanism is the value-type induced reward function r_f = f − P*V_{h+1,f}, which decomposes the value function into reward-like components, making the trajectory-level preference constraint directly optimizable. The algorithm proceeds in two phases: (1) model learning, where a reward model is trained by MLE on preference data and a transition model by MLE on trajectory data; (2) value optimization, where the Q function is trained to minimize the value alignment loss while the V function is trained via expectile regression. In the practical deep RL implementation, the transition model is bypassed by using V(s_{l+1}) in place of PV(s_l, a_l), and the policy is extracted via advantage-weighted regression. Theoretically, PVO achieves a rate-optimal O(ε^{-2}) sample complexity under uniform concentrability, and the value alignment loss is shown to also apply to actor-critic algorithms (yielding a PAC variant of APPO with matching guarantees).

## Results

On Meta-World medium-replay (8 tasks) and medium-expert datasets, PVO consistently achieves the highest average success rate across all environments with 500 and 1000 preference feedback, while exhibiting notably lower variance than all baselines. PVO achieves effective learning with as few as ~100 preference samples with minimal performance degradation, and maintains its advantage across data-quality mixtures (expert-to-random trajectory ratios from 0 to 1). In the ablation study, replacing the standard TD loss with the value alignment loss yields substantial improvements for both TD3+BC (actor-critic) and XQL (value-based), confirming the loss's benefit independent of the algorithmic framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PVO outperforms all five main baselines (IQL, APPO, Preference Transformer, DPPO, IPL) on Meta-World medium-replay and medium-expert success rates, with the gap being most pronounced on tasks where baselines exhibit high variance or outright failure. IQL, the strongest single baseline, performs comparably to PVO on the medium-replay sweep task but fails to learn on button-press-topdown, highlighting PVO's robustness to reward model misspecification. APPO, the only other method with theoretical guarantees, suffers from unstable training and high performance variance even with hyperparameter tuning, and requires additional conservatism hyperparameters. Preference Transformer, DPPO, and IPL all show higher variance across tasks and depend on extra regularization or smoothness hyperparameters that PVO does not need. In the ablation, PVO's advantage over IQL (which shares identical network architecture and expectile regression) is attributed solely to the value alignment loss, and the same loss improves TD3+BC and XQL over their standard TD-loss counterparts, confirming the loss as the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

PVO builds directly on APPO (Kang & Oh, 2025), an actor-critic offline PbRL algorithm that made distributionally robust optimization tractable but suffered from O(ε⁻⁴) sample complexity and unstable training. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a *value-type* induced reward function and the resulting value alignment loss, which operates at the trajectory level rather than the state-action level of standard TD errors—motivated by the observation that preference feedback is inherently trajectory-level. Practically, PVO's implementation reuses IQL's architecture (expectile regression for V, advantage-weighted regression for policy) and replaces only the Q-loss with the value alignment loss, requiring no additional hyperparameters. The paper also shows that APPO's ℓ₁ loss can be replaced by the value alignment loss to yield a provably efficient actor-critic variant (PAC), positioning the value alignment loss as a unifying principle across value-based and actor-critic PbRL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PVO provides a rare combination of rate-optimal O(ε⁻²) sample complexity, computational tractability, and empirical robustness in offline PbRL—addressing a gap where prior methods were either intractable (Zhan et al., 2024a), restricted to linear models (Zhu et al., 2023), or unstable in practice (APPO). The value alignment loss is a simple, drop-in replacement for the TD loss that the ablation study shows substantially improves learning from preference data, suggesting it could benefit other preference-based or reward-uncertain settings beyond the specific offline PbRL formulation. Limitations include the reliance on a stronger uniform concentrability assumption (vs. single-policy), the practical approximation of replacing PV(s,a) with V(s') in the deep implementation, and evaluation limited to Meta-World and DMControl benchmarks. Open questions include whether the value alignment loss extends to online PbRL, listwise preferences, or LLM alignment settings where trajectory-level feedback is natural.
