## Problem

Computing the rate-distortion function for continuous data sources under MSE distortion remains a long-standing challenge: closed-form solutions exist only for a handful of canonical cases (e.g., Gaussian sources), and the classical Blahut-Arimoto algorithm is intractable for continuous alphabets. While recent work connected the RD problem to entropic optimal transport, no approach had simultaneously yielded new analytical solutions for non-Gaussian sources and a practical neural estimation method that avoids the rate upper-bound limitations of prior neural estimators.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. samples from a continuous source distribution P_0 (1-D Gaussians, Gaussian mixtures, 4×4 grayscale CIFAR-10 patches, or 33-dim whitened speech features) along with a tuning parameter ε that parameterizes the rate-distortion tradeoff. A single DNN controller u_θ(x,t,ε) is trained to handle a range of ε values simultaneously, and at evaluation time it produces the RD function point (R̂, D̂), the empirical reconstruction distribution P_1 (sampled via Euler-Maruyama integration of the learned SDE), and the optimal drift field u(x,t). Unlike prior neural RD estimators (NERD, WGD), R2D2 is not upper-bounded by log(batch size) in rate, enabling estimation at high rates (>20 nats) where existing methods saturate around 13 nats.

## Method

The paper introduces Terminal-Entropy Control (TEC), a stochastic-control formulation in which the RD Lagrangian is equivalent to minimizing control energy plus ε times the differential entropy of the terminal state, with the terminal distribution left free (unlike the Schrödinger Bridge, which fixes both endpoints). The key theoretical result (Thm. 3.2) shows that under regularity conditions, the optimal drift is u* = ε∇log p_t*, where p_t* solves the Backward Heat Equation with initial condition P_0; this yields closed-form solutions for Gaussian and Gaussian-mixture sources and a Fourier-analytic solution for band-limited non-Gaussian mixtures. For the general case, the R2D2 algorithm trains a DNN to approximate u(x,t,ε) by minimizing a surrogate loss composed of the discretized control energy (summed over Euler-Maruyama trajectory steps) and an estimated terminal entropy (via negentropy/Donsker-Varadhan in low dimensions or the KNIFE kernel estimator in high dimensions), with ε sampled uniformly during training to cover the full RD curve with one model.

## Results

On a 1-D Gaussian source (N(0,1)) over ε ∈ [0.05, 0.95], R2D2 achieves lower median absolute estimation error than both NERD and WGD across 64 independent seeds, with the advantage visible in both high-rate and low-rate regimes. On the 33-dimensional Free Spoken Digits dataset, R2D2 successfully estimates rate values exceeding 20 nats, whereas NERD and WGD are practically upper-bounded at approximately 13 nats. For a 3-component Gaussian mixture source, the empirical reconstruction distribution produced by R2D2 matches the closed-form analytical solution (Eq. 22) with high fidelity. On CIFAR-10 4×4 grayscale patches, R2D2 produces a smooth RD curve and visually plausible reconstruction images across the ε range [0.0005, 0.026].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares R2D2 against two existing neural RD estimation methods: NERD (Lei et al., 2022) and WGD (Yang et al., 2024). On the 1-D Gaussian source, R2D2 clearly outperforms both baselines in estimation accuracy, as shown by lower median absolute error with tighter interquartile ranges over 64 seeds (Fig. 2). The most striking advantage appears on the high-dimensional Free Spoken Digits dataset: R2D2 estimates rates above 20 nats in the low-distortion regime, while NERD and WGD are practically capped at roughly 13 nats due to their inherent rate upper bound of R < log M (where M is the batch or support size). This limitation is explicitly noted in Remark 4.1 as a substantial constraint of both prior methods that R2D2 avoids. On the Gaussian mixture source, R2D2's estimated RD curve closely tracks the Shannon lower bound (SLB), and its learned reconstruction distribution matches the analytical superposition solution, confirming correctness where a ground truth exists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established threads: the Blahut-Arimoto formulation of rate-distortion, the equivalence between entropic optimal transport (EOT) and the Schrödinger Bridge (SB) problem (Léonard 2013; Chen et al. 2021), and the recent identification of the BA–EOT connection (Lei et al. 2023; Yang et al. 2024). The key conceptual departure from Gushchin et al. (2022), who solved EOT via a game-theoretic diffusion formulation with both marginals fixed, is to free the terminal distribution and replace the hard marginal constraint with a soft entropy penalty, yielding the Terminal-Entropy Control (TEC) problem. This transforms a two-point transport problem into a single-point stochastic control problem whose optimal drift is characterized by a backward heat equation, a connection the authors argue is novel in the information-theoretic context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first closed-form reconstruction distributions for Gaussian-mixture sources under MSE distortion, a result the authors note was previously unknown, and extends the analysis to non-Gaussian band-limited mixtures via Fourier methods. Practically, R2D2 removes the rate upper bound (~13 nats) that constrains existing neural RD estimators (NERD, WGD), enabling estimation in the high-rate/low-distortion regime relevant to modern high-bandwidth communication. Key limitations include Assumption A1 (absolutely continuous reconstruction with finite entropy), which necessarily fails at low rates, and the general ill-posedness of the backward heat equation; extending the framework beyond MSE distortion or to settings where the reconstruction becomes singular remains open.
