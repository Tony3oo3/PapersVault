## Problem

RLVR for LLMs requires the base model to perform high-quality exploration that is both effective (discovering correct trajectories) and diverse (maintaining multiple distinct solutions to avoid entropy collapse). Existing expert-guided methods improve effectiveness by imitating full expert trajectories, but this restricts the exploration space, accelerates entropy collapse, and causes the model to overfit superficial expert patterns rather than internalizing core reasoning strategies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262136/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method takes a text question and a stronger expert model (e.g., OpenR1-Qwen-7B or a fine-tuned LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct) as inputs. The expert model is consulted only at high-entropy token positions during rollout generation, not as a fixed trajectory source. The output is a trained policy that generates reasoning trajectories with a final answer in \boxed{} format, having selectively absorbed expert reasoning strategies (e.g., verification steps) while discarding redundant stylistic tokens. The mixed-policy rollout is accelerated via modified speculative sampling, where the policy model drafts K candidate tokens and the expert validates them in parallel.

## Method

MENTOR consists of two components. First, Mixed-policy Rollout defines a token-level interpolation π_mix = (1−w_t)π_θ + w_t π*, where the weight w_t = min(1, H_t/γ_p) is driven by the on-policy token entropy H_t relative to a batch-level p-quantile threshold γ_p, so expert guidance is injected only at uncertain decision points while low-entropy tokens remain on-policy. This rollout is accelerated by a modified speculative sampling scheme that drafts K tokens from π_θ and validates them against π_mix in parallel. Second, Mixed-policy GRPO collects both on-policy rollouts (standardized group-wise advantages) and mixed-policy rollouts (advantages computed as the positive excess [R_i − mean(R_on)]_+ / R_range, which rewards only above-average exploration and suppresses gradient from incorrect trajectories). A decaying weight α (cosine schedule from 1 to 0) progressively shifts the policy from expert-guided exploration to autonomous self-improvement, enabling selective absorption of expert knowledge rather than surface imitation.

## Results

MENTOR achieves the best average score across all three model backbones: 46.7 on Qwen2.5-7B-Base, 35.3 on Qwen2.5-3B-Base, and 23.8 on LLaMA3.1-8B-Base, outperforming the on-policy RL (GRPO) baseline by +3.9, +4.3, and +3.2 points respectively. On the hardest in-domain benchmarks, MENTOR lifts AIME24 from 14.2 to 18.3 and AIME25 from 9.1 to 16.5 on Qwen2.5-7B, while also improving out-of-domain scores (e.g., GPQA from 29.3 to 30.8, MMLU-Pro from 48.0 to 50.2). MENTOR further achieves a 9.2% average gain in pass@32 over on-policy RL, confirming improved reasoning diversity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to LUFFY (full expert trajectory imitation), MENTOR wins on all three models: +3.9 avg on Qwen2.5-7B (46.7 vs 42.8), +4.2 on Qwen2.5-3B (35.3 vs 31.1), and +2.3 on LLaMA3.1-8B (23.8 vs 21.5). The gap is largest on AIME24 (+5.4 on Qwen2.5-7B) and GPQA (+5.5 on Qwen2.5-3B), indicating that full-trajectory imitation overfits superficial expert patterns and restricts exploration diversity. Compared to QuestA (partial expert prefix as hint), MENTOR also wins consistently: +2.6 avg on Qwen2.5-7B, +3.5 on Qwen2.5-3B, and +4.8 on LLaMA3.1-8B. Notably, QuestA actually degrades performance on LLaMA3.1-8B (19.0 vs 20.6 for on-policy RL), whereas MENTOR improves it substantially, showing that selective token-level guidance is more robust to model capacity differences than fixed-prefix hints.

## Relation to Prior Work

MENTOR extends the GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024) with a token-level mixed-policy mechanism that interpolates between the on-policy distribution and an expert distribution, guided by token-level entropy. It departs from prior expert-guided RLVR methods (LUFFY, QuestA) that inject full or partial expert trajectories into rollouts, arguing that such trajectory-level imitation fundamentally constrains the exploration space and accelerates entropy collapse. The acceleration of mixed-policy rollouts repurposes speculative sampling (Chen et al., 2023) in a novel way: the policy model serves as the draft model and the mixed distribution as the target, exploiting the positional sparsity of expert guidance. The theoretical analysis builds on the maximum entropy principle to formalize why both effectiveness and diversity are necessary conditions for high-quality exploration in RLVR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in RLVR: weaker base models cannot perform high-quality exploration on their own, and existing remedies (expert trajectory imitation) trade diversity for effectiveness. The entropy-based selective guidance mechanism is a lightweight, plug-in technique that could be adopted broadly in RLVR pipelines, and the theoretical upper-bound result gives practitioners a formal handle on the diversity-reward tradeoff. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multimodal reasoning, how sensitive it is to expert quality (the paper shows graceful degradation but only tests one expert per model family), and whether the entropy threshold can be made adaptive per-problem rather than per-batch. The moderate but consistent gains (3–4% average) across three model families suggest the technique is robust but not transformative on its own.
