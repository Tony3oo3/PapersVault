## Problem

Deploying pretrained EEG models for motor imagery BCIs in real-world settings is hindered by cross-subject and cross-session distribution shifts. Existing test-time adaptation methods either rely on gradient-based fine-tuning (incurring high computational cost and catastrophic forgetting under single-trial online conditions) or use shallow statistics alignment that fails to capture deep, domain-specific shifts in temporal predictive embeddings. The paper seeks a gradient-free, theoretically grounded TTA framework that models these deep distributional changes without destructive parameter updates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single C-channel EEG trial of length T (e.g., 22×1000 samples at 250 Hz for BNCI2014001) arriving sequentially with batch size 1 during online inference, after a 1–48 Hz bandpass filter and Euclidean Alignment preprocessing. It produces a calibrated class label via argmax over posterior probabilities, with the intermediate Dirichlet parameter vector α ∈ ℝ₊^|L| serving as a compact, interpretable summary of the trial's time-varying predictive distribution. Because the network uses layer normalization rather than batch normalization, it operates robustly under the single-trial constraint where batch-statistics-based methods degrade.

## Method

BTTA-DG combines a lightweight SincAdaptNet encoder (Spat-Conv → Sinc-Conv → IncCh-Conv → Cls-Conv, with learnable Sinc bandpass filters isolating μ/β/γ rhythms) with a two-stage gradient-free adaptation module. First, a Dirichlet feature projection maps the post-softmax temporal predictive embeddings X ∈ ℝ^{|L|×T} to a low-dimensional Dirichlet parameter vector α via maximum-likelihood estimation using Minka's fixed-point iteration, encoding both per-class concentration and overall predictive uncertainty. Second, a class-specific GMM fitted on a memory bank of high-confidence historical Dirichlet parameters provides a likelihood p_GMM(α|y) that captures global and neighborhood structure of the target domain; Bayesian inference then fuses this likelihood with the frozen network's prior prediction to yield a calibrated posterior, with no gradient updates to pretrained weights. The key insight is adapting on the distribution of sequential predictions (a 'distribution over categorical distributions') rather than on point estimates, making the method robust to noisy single-trial predictions while remaining computationally efficient (15.7 ms/trial, 141.6 M FLOPs).

## Results

On BNCI2014001 (cross-subject, 2-class), BTTA-DG achieves 78.70% ± 1.32% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline OTTA (77.58%) by 1.12% and the source SincAdaptNet (77.03%) by 1.67%. On BNCI2014002 and BNCI2015001, BTTA-DG reaches 80.29% ± 1.07% and 77.92% ± 1.76% respectively, again leading all TTA baselines. In the cross-session setting on BNCI2014001, BTTA-DG attains 86.50% ± 2.49%, surpassing OTTA (83.91%) by 2.59%. Inference time is 15.7 ms/trial with 141.6 M FLOPs, making it 17.8% faster than T-TIME and 24.2% faster than OTTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTTA-DG outperforms all 10 TTA baselines on every dataset tested. On BNCI2014001, it leads the closest competitor OTTA (77.58%) by 1.12% and the gradient-based methods Tent (70.23%) and PL (71.83%) by 6.9–8.5%, revealing that gradient-based TTA actually degrades performance below the source model due to catastrophic forgetting under batch-size-1 online adaptation. On BNCI2014002, BTTA-DG (80.29%) beats OTTA (78.29%) by 2.0% and CoTTA (78.12%) by 2.2%; on BNCI2015001, it leads OTTA (76.20%) by 1.72%. On the SHU MI dataset, BTTA-DG (64.06%) edges out OTTA (63.29%) by 0.77%, though this gain is not statistically significant, which the authors attribute to lower EEG quality and higher session complexity in that dataset. In the cross-session setting, BTTA-DG (86.50%) surpasses OTTA (83.91%) by 2.59% and CoTTA (82.39%) by 4.11%. Computationally, BTTA-DG (15.7 ms, 141.6 M FLOPs) is faster than all gradient-based TTA methods (Tent 18.4 ms, T-TIME 18.5 ms, OTTA 19.5 ms, SAR 32.5 ms) while matching or exceeding their accuracy, and is only slightly slower than BN-adapt (5.1 ms) which performs no probabilistic inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) paradigm from computer vision (Tent, CoTTA, SAR) to EEG motor imagery decoding, departing from both gradient-based fine-tuning approaches (which suffer catastrophic forgetting under single-trial online inference) and shallow statistics-alignment methods (BN recalibration, Euclidean/Riemannian alignment). Its core novelty is introducing the Dirichlet distribution as a 'distribution over categorical distributions' to project temporal predictive embeddings into a compact, interpretable parameter space, combined with GMM-driven Bayesian inference for gradient-free calibration. The SincAdaptNet backbone draws on SincNet's parametric bandpass filtering, and the overall framework is positioned against large pretrained EEG foundation models (BENDR, BIOT, LaBraM, EEGPT, CBraMod), arguing that lightweight task-aligned architectures with probabilistic adaptation outperform them for MI decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practical BCI deployment, the gradient-free, real-time (15.7 ms/trial) adaptation without destructive weight updates addresses a genuine deployment bottleneck: online EEG systems cannot afford backpropagation or risk overwriting pretrained representations with noisy single-trial gradients. The Dirichlet projection offers physiological interpretability (concentration parameters map to per-class evidence, scale maps to temporal uncertainty) that black-box TTA methods lack. However, gains over the strongest baseline (OTTA) are modest (~1–3%), the SHU MI results are not statistically significant, and the approach has only been validated on 2-class MI tasks with relatively short trials. Open questions include whether the Dirichlet+GMM calibration composes additively with large pretrained EEG models, whether it extends to higher-class-count or non-MI paradigms, and how it behaves under sustained non-stationarity beyond the short LOSO windows tested here.
