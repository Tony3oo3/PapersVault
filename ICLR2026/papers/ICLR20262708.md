## Problem

Applying post-training quantization to autoregressive visual generation (ARVG) models remains largely unexplored, and existing quantization methods designed for LLMs, diffusion models, or ViTs fail to generalize effectively. The paper identifies three ARVG-specific challenges that existing approaches do not address: severe channel-wise activation outliers, highly dynamic token-wise activations (including sink tokens in all linear layers), and sample-wise distribution mismatch that corrupts calibration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ARVG model (VAR, RAR, PAR, or MAR) and 128 calibration images from ImageNet, requiring no training data or gradient updates. It produces quantized model weights at W8A8 or W6A6 precision with all scaling factors fused offline into the network, introducing zero additional inference overhead. The quantized model generates 50K images on ImageNet for evaluation via FID, sFID, IS, and Precision, and is deployed on an RTX 3090 using standard CUDA kernels without custom operator designs.

## Method

PTQ4ARVG is a training-free framework with three components targeting the three identified challenges. (1) Gain-Projected Scaling (GPS) performs a Taylor expansion of the activation and weight quantization losses, defines the scaling gain as the reduction in activation loss minus the increase in weight loss, and derives a closed-form optimal per-channel scaling factor by differentiating this gain function—making it the first quantization scaling strategy grounded in mathematical optimization rather than empirical heuristics. (2) Static Token-Wise Quantization (STWQ) exploits two ARVG-specific properties—fixed token sequence length and position-invariant activation distributions across samples—to assign fine-grained quantization parameters per token position offline via percentile calibration, avoiding the online min-max calibration overhead and accuracy loss of dynamic token-wise quantization used in LLMs. (3) Distribution-Guided Calibration (DGC) computes Mahalanobis distance to measure each sample's contribution to overall distributional entropy and selects the top 50% of samples, eliminating the sample-wise redundancy that causes mismatched calibration in ARVG models.

## Results

On RAR-B at 6-bit (W6A6), PTQ4ARVG achieves FID 5.13, IS 206.17, and Precision 0.75, dramatically outperforming all baselines (SmoothQuant FID 63.77, SVDQuant FID 125.51, QuaRot FID 101.60, OmniQuant FID 11.66). At 8-bit (W8A8) on RAR-B, the method achieves FID 2.21 vs. FP 1.96, closely matching OmniQuant (2.23) and SVDQuant (11.94). On MAR-B at 6-bit, PTQ4ARVG reaches FID 2.99 vs. OS+ (13.56) and SVDQuant (36.14). The method also achieves a 3.01× speedup and 1.92× peak memory reduction on VAR-d20 at 8-bit with standard CUDA kernels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 6-bit, PTQ4ARVG dramatically outperforms all six baselines across all model families. On RAR-B W6A6, it achieves FID 5.13 versus SmoothQuant (63.77), SVDQuant (125.51), QuaRot (101.60), OS+ (40.14), RepQ* (82.31), and OmniQuant (11.66) — a 2.3× improvement over the best baseline (OmniQuant). At 8-bit, the method is competitive with OmniQuant and SVDQuant on RAR (FID 2.21 vs. 2.23 and 1.86 respectively) and clearly superior on MAR-B (FID 2.36 vs. OS+ 5.94, QuaRot 6.82, SVDQuant 6.40). On PAR models at 6-bit, where most baselines collapse (SmoothQuant FID 146.52, OmniQuant 107.29), PTQ4ARVG maintains FID 12.87 on PAR-XL-4×, outperforming SVDQuant (19.52) by 34%. SVDQuant shows inconsistent behavior: it performs well on VAR and RAR-XL at 8-bit but degrades severely on RAR-B (FID 125.51) and MAR at 6-bit (FID 142.37 on MAR-H). QuaRot is not applicable to PAR models due to architectural constraints (head dimensions must be powers of 2), and its online rotation computation introduces a 0.70× slowdown and 159 MB extra peak memory on RAR/VAR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends post-training quantization from LLMs, diffusion models, and ViTs to the emerging ARVG model family (VAR, RAR, PAR, MAR), which shares transformer architecture with LLMs but introduces unique challenges via AdaLN conditioning, fixed-length token sequences, and sink tokens. Methodologically, it builds on the Taylor-series quantization loss decomposition from AdaRound/BRECQ and the error-estimation strategy from DilateQuant, but departs from empirically-designed scaling (SmoothQuant, OS+, RepQ-ViT) by deriving a closed-form optimal scaling factor via differentiation. It also contrasts with rotation/low-rank approaches (QuaRot, SVDQuant) that introduce inference overhead, and with dynamic token-wise quantization from LLMs, which is infeasible for ARVG's fixed-length setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARVG models are rapidly becoming competitive with diffusion models for image generation while offering LLM-compatible architectures for multimodal integration, yet their 1–3B parameter sizes and multi-second inference latencies hinder deployment. PTQ4ARVG demonstrates that 6-bit quantization is viable across the ARVG family with no additional inference overhead, a practical milestone for resource-constrained deployment. The main limitation is that 4-bit quantization still degrades performance substantially, and the position-invariant distribution property enabling STWQ has only been verified on ImageNet-trained models. Open questions include whether GPS generalizes beyond ARVG to other transformer families and whether the fixed-token-length assumption holds for future variable-resolution ARVG variants.
