## Problem

Existing image generation benchmarks are static and designed around older models, failing to capture the novel capabilities that emerge with each new model release (e.g., Ghiblification, multi-image reasoning, text-heavy infographic generation). This creates a gap between community perceptions of model progress and formal evaluation, as benchmarks cannot evolve with real-world user observations and feedback.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261785/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ECHO takes raw social media posts (text, images, reply threads, and conversation screenshots) from Twitter/X about a target model, along with time-windowed keyword queries, and produces a structured benchmark of 31,000+ samples in the format ⟨input text, input image(s), output image, community feedback⟩. The final curated benchmark contains 777 image-to-image prompt-image pairs and 1,000 text-to-image prompts, each with quality labels and associated user commentary. Downstream, the benchmark yields pairwise win rates across models and four specialized quality metrics (color shift magnitude, face identity similarity, structure distance, text rendering accuracy) computed over applicable subsets.

## Method

ECHO is a three-stage pipeline that converts social media discussion into a structured benchmark. First, a two-stage collection phase uses broad keyword queries (with time-adaptive keyword sets) followed by LLM-based 5-point relevance filtering to yield ~32k relevant posts from an initial 68k pool. Second, the framework reconstructs full reply trees to capture context spread across posts, then uses an LLM to extract self-contained samples with prompt text, community feedback, and quality labels (Benchmark/Analysis/Trash). Third, a multimodal processing stage uses Qwen-2.5-VL to classify input vs. output images, reverse-engineer fill-in-the-blank prompt completions, and parse conversation screenshots via bounding-box detection. For evaluation, the benchmark uses an ensemble of three VLM-as-a-judge models (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0, Qwen2.5-VL-32B) in an MT-Bench-style single-answer-grading setup converted to pseudo-pairwise win rates, and derives targeted quantitative metrics from community-identified failure categories.

## Results

ECHO evaluates 8 models (GPT-4o Image Gen, Gemini 2.5 Flash/Nano Banana, Gemini 2.0 Flash, Bagel-Think, Bagel, Flux Kontext, LLM+Diffusion, Anole) using head-to-head win rate computed via an ensemble of three VLM-as-a-judge evaluators (GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0, Qwen2.5-VL-32B). On the image-to-image split, model performance separates into five distinct tiers, with GPT-4o Image Gen significantly outperforming all others, followed by Nano Banana, then a middle cluster (Gemini 2.0 Flash, Bagel-Think, Bagel, Flux Kontext), and finally LLM+Diffusion and Anole at the bottom. The benchmark yields a 3.2× larger relative performance gap between SOTA and open-source models compared to the prior GEdit benchmark. Specialized metrics derived from community feedback confirm that 4o Image Gen exhibits the largest color shift and face identity drift, while achieving near-perfect text rendering accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o Image Gen dominates both splits, significantly outperforming all other models on image-to-image and maintaining the top position on text-to-image. Gemini 2.5 Flash (Nano Banana) is the strongest challenger, occupying second place on both splits. Gemini 2.0 Flash, Bagel-Think, Bagel, and Flux Kontext form a statistically similar mid-tier cluster on image-to-image, with Flux Kontext (a specialized image-editing model) performing comparably to unified models despite lacking a sophisticated text encoder. LLM+Diffusion (GPT-4o + DALL-E 3, following Zhou et al. 2025) performs poorly on image-to-image but jumps forward on text-to-image, suggesting the LLM rewriting step helps with pure generation but not with reference-image fidelity. Anole, the earliest unified model tested, performs worst on both splits. The key finding is that ECHO's 3.2× larger performance gap versus GEdit indicates that prior benchmarks understate the true capability gap between proprietary and open-source models, likely because they test simpler, more saturated tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECHO departs from prior crowdsourced image-generation benchmarks (DiffusionDB, Pick-a-Pic) that collect prompts from model interfaces, where prompt style is shaped by model quirks (e.g., CLIP bag-of-words keyword lists), and from templated editing benchmarks (GEdit, MagicBrush, InstructPix2Pix) that contain overly simple instructions no longer discriminative of SOTA models. It also distinguishes itself from Chatbot Arena's in-the-wild collection by mining social media, where users are incentivized to showcase novel and creative capabilities rather than routine tasks. The evaluation protocol adapts MT-Bench's VLM-as-a-judge paradigm to image generation, and the specialized metrics (DINO structure distance, AuraFace identity, color histogram shift) draw on established representation-learning tools repurposed for fine-grained model comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ECHO addresses a real and growing gap: as proprietary image models introduce capabilities faster than the benchmarking community can react, static benchmarks lose discriminative power (the paper shows a 3.2× larger SOTA gap on ECHO vs. GEdit). The re-runnable, platform-agnostic pipeline (demonstrated on Twitter/X and Douyin) offers a practical mechanism for keeping evaluation aligned with community usage. However, the weak Kendall's τ_b correlation between VLM judges and human raters (0.045–0.117) raises open questions about the reliability of automated image-generation evaluation, and the inherent success-bias of social media (users post more successes than failures) limits the benchmark's coverage of model weaknesses. Whether the framework generalizes cleanly to non-English, non-Twitter ecosystems and to models with very different capability profiles remains to be tested at scale.
