## Problem

Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) possess extensive textual knowledge but fail to leverage it for fine-grained image recognition, often unable to distinguish between visually similar categories. Existing RL fine-tuning approaches like ViRFT rely on exact string-match rewards that are brittle, encourage memorization of training category names, and do not incentivize the attribute-level differential reasoning needed to generalize to unseen classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263614/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural-language text query as input. At inference, it operates in two steps: first generating K rollouts (K=20 by default) from the model to extract top-k candidate categories, then re-prompting the model to select the correct option from those candidates, producing a reasoning chain and a final letter choice. Evaluation uses an LLM judge (Gemini-2.5-flash-lite) to match predicted category names against ground truth, accommodating scientific vs. common name variations that would fail exact string matching.

## Method

DiVE-k is a two-stage framework that uses the base model's own top-k generations as a training primitive for differential visual reasoning. In the first (offline) stage, K rollouts are sampled per training image via nucleus sampling, the top-k most frequent categories are extracted as MCQ options (with ground truth guaranteed to appear), and trivially-correct samples are filtered out. In the second stage, the model is trained with GRPO reinforcement learning on MCQ prompts, receiving a verifiable reward that combines correct-option selection (r_mcq) and format compliance (r_format), eliminating the need for fragile string matching. The key insight is that presenting the model with its own most-confused predictions as explicit options forces fine-grained, attribute-level comparison rather than coarse pattern recognition, and the MCQ format provides a trivially verifiable reward that mitigates category-name memorization. At inference, the same two-step pipeline is used without injecting ground truth, and the approach generalizes across base-to-novel, mixed-domain, and few-shot settings.

## Results

On the standard zero-shot base-to-novel generalization setting across five fine-grained datasets, DiVE-k achieves an average Harmonic Mean (HM) of 79.8% (Base 80.8% / Novel 78.8%), surpassing the pretrained QWEN2.5-VL-7B by 10.04% HM and ViRFT by 6.16% HM. Gains are most pronounced on CUB-200 (+14.9 HM over ViRFT) and Oxford Flowers (+8.5 HM). In the mixed-dataset setting, DiVE-k reaches 78.7% average HM (+9.0 over QWEN2.5-VL-7B, +4.0 over ViRFT), and in the 4-shot setting it achieves 74.75% average accuracy (+7.73% over ViRFT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiVE-k outperforms CLIP by a wide margin (+14.9 HM average) and the pretrained QWEN2.5-VL-7B by +10.04% HM, confirming that RL-based differential reasoning substantially improves fine-grained discrimination. Against the closest RL baseline ViRFT, DiVE-k gains +6.16% HM overall; even when ViRFT is run under DiVE-k's two-step inference pipeline (ViRFT*), the gap narrows to +2.9% HM, indicating the formulation itself (MCQ + top-k options) drives most of the improvement. On proprietary models, DiVE-k surpasses Grok-4-fast by +8.0% HM and is within 0.2% of Gemini2.5-flash-lite, though GPT-5-mini remains ahead by 3.6% HM. SFT (full) collapses on novel classes (37.0% avg novel vs. 78.8% for DiVE-k), highlighting severe overfitting to base categories. In the mixed-dataset setting, ViRFT actually underperforms the pretrained QWEN2.5-VL-7B on novel classes (76.2 vs. 77.0), while DiVE-k reaches 78.8, further evidencing that ViRFT's string-match reward reinforces memorization rather than generalization. On the Gemma3-12B backbone (Appendix), DiVE-k still yields +13.07 HM on CUB and +7.83 on Flowers, confirming model-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiVE-k extends the RL-for-visual-reasoning line initiated by ViRFT (Liu et al., 2025), which applied GRPO with an exact string-match reward to train LVLMs for image classification. The key departure is replacing the brittle string-match reward with a multiple-choice formulation where the model's own top-k generations serve as options, making the reward trivially verifiable and forcing differential reasoning among plausible alternatives. Conceptually, this also connects to FuDD's (Esfandiarpoor & Bach, 2023) idea of using LLM knowledge for pairwise discriminative features, but DiVE-k integrates this reasoning natively within a single LVLM via RL rather than as a separate offline prompt-generation stage. The framework is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and trained with GRPO, following the broader trend of applying DeepSeek-R1-style RL to multimodal models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and common failure mode of LVLMs in fine-grained recognition—over-reliance on coarse shared attributes—and shows that a simple reformulation (MCQ over the model's own top-k) yields substantial base-to-novel generalization gains across five benchmarks. Two practical caveats limit immediate adoption: the two-step inference roughly quintuples per-sample latency (~2.5s → ~13s), and the method's effectiveness depends on the base model already having high Pass@k recall, which weaker LVLMs may not satisfy. An important open question is whether incorporating Pass@k accuracy directly into the training reward could decouple the method from this dependency. Additionally, the discovery that ViRFT's original string-matching code contained a shortcut bug (accepting any substring match) that inflated reported accuracy is practically significant for the community reproducing or building on that work.
