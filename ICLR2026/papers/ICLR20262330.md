## Problem

Time series foundation models for classification require large-scale, carefully curated real-world pre-training corpora, yet no prior work proposed a classification-oriented synthetic data generation pipeline for TSFMs. Existing forecasting generators (e.g., Chronos's zero-mean kernel composition) lack discriminative class structure, while tabular SCM generators (e.g., TabPFN) discard temporal motifs like seasonality and trend. Moreover, real-world classification benchmarks such as UEA exhibit irregular scaling behavior, making it unclear whether meaningful data and model scaling laws even exist for zero-shot time series classification.

## Input / Output

CAUKER takes generation hyperparameters (total sample count N, target series length L=512, number of observed variables d) and a random seed, and produces synthetic time series by sampling from randomly composed GP priors propagated through a stochastic DAG. Each node's trajectory is treated as an individual univariate series to match the univariate pre-training regime of Mantis and MOMENT, though the DAG structure naturally encodes multivariate dependencies. The generated corpora range from 10K to 10M samples and are used exclusively for self-supervised pre-training (contrastive or masked reconstruction), with the encoder frozen at evaluation time.

## Method

CAUKER unifies Gaussian Process kernel composition with Structural Causal Model propagation in a five-step pipeline: (1) sample K kernels from a 36-kernel bank, (2) compose them via random additive and multiplicative operations, (3) draw non-zero mean functions (linear, exponential, sparse anomalies) and sample GP priors to generate root-node time series, (4) sample activation functions (linear, ReLU, sigmoid, sine, modulo, Leaky ReLU), and (5) propagate signals through a randomly generated DAG where each edge applies a randomly initialized linear layer followed by a sampled nonlinearity. The critical design choice over forecasting generators is retaining non-zero mean functions as discriminative cues, and over tabular generators is preserving GP-derived periodic and trend structure. This yields synthetic data with both realistic temporal motifs and meaningful clustering structure, enabling clear scaling laws in both data size (10K–10M) and model capacity (0.75M–783M parameters) that are absent when pre-training on real-world UEA subsets.

## Results

On the UCR benchmark (128 datasets, zero-shot), CAUKER achieves 78.31% average accuracy with Mantis (8M) and 74.24% with MOMENT (77M), outperforming all alternative synthetic generators. In the sample-efficiency comparison, Mantis pre-trained on just 100K CAUKER samples reaches 78.55% UCR accuracy, nearly matching the original Mantis trained on 1.89M real series (78.66%, a 0.11% gap). MOMENT pre-trained on 10M CAUKER samples achieves 77.49%, within 1.36% of the original 13M Time Series Pile checkpoint (78.85%). CAUKER also exhibits clear data scaling laws (10K→10M samples) and model scaling laws (0.75M→114M params), which are absent or irregular when using the real-world UEA benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAUKER outperforms all four alternative synthetic generators on both architectures. Against the tabular SCM generator (TabPFN), CAUKER wins by 4.82 points for Mantis (78.31% vs 73.49%) and a dramatic 15.01 points for MOMENT (74.24% vs 59.23%), confirming that temporal structure is critical for classification. Against the forecasting-oriented FPFN and KernelSynth, CAUKER gains 0.79 and 0.61 points for Mantis, and 3.39 and 4.93 points for MOMENT, respectively. The closest competitor is Mean+KernelSynth (KernelSynth with non-zero means), which CAUKER edges out by 0.11 points (Mantis) and 1.68 points (MOMENT), demonstrating the added value of the causal DAG propagation. In the sample-efficiency setting, CAUKER 100K nearly matches the original Mantis (78.55% vs 78.66% on 1.89M real data) and CAUKER 10M is within 1.36% of original MOMENT (77.49% vs 78.85% on 13M), while both CAUKER variants substantially outperform 100K UEA (76.73%/73.55%) and 100K forecasting data (75.81%/73.93%). On the WOODS OOD benchmark, CauKer100K achieves 0.820 average accuracy, beating both the supervised ERM baseline (0.800) and the original Mantis-2M (0.810). For forecasting, Chronos pre-trained on 0.5B CAUKER observations matches the official 84B-observation checkpoints (e.g., Base: 0.83 vs 0.81 MASE), with a non-significant p-value of 0.84.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAUKER unifies two previously separate synthetic data generation paradigms: the Gaussian Process kernel composition from Chronos (Ansari et al., 2024), originally designed for zero-mean forecasting, and the Structural Causal Model (SCM) DAG propagation from TabPFN (Hollmann et al., 2023), originally designed for tabular classification. The key conceptual departure is that neither prior generator was suited for time series classification—Chronos's zero-mean GPs lack discriminative class structure, while TabPFN's SCM discards temporal motifs like seasonality and trend. CAUKER bridges this gap by retaining non-zero mean functions in the GP priors and injecting temporal structure into the SCM, producing sequences that are both temporally realistic and causally coherent. The paper also positions itself against forecasting-oriented synthetic generators (ForecastPFN, TimePFN) and real-data pre-trained classification TSFMs (Mantis, MOMENT), arguing that no prior work has proposed classification-oriented synthetic data generation for TSFMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that zero-shot time series classification exhibits clean data and model scaling laws when pre-trained on synthetic data, while commonly used real-world classification corpora (UEA) show irregular or even degrading scaling—suggesting that the current practice of aggregating heterogeneous real datasets for classification pre-training may be fundamentally suboptimal. The practical implication is significant: 100K CAUKER samples nearly match 1.89M real samples for Mantis, and 10M matches 13M for MOMENT, enabling sample-efficient pre-training without data collection or curation. Limitations include evaluation on only two architectures (ViT-based Mantis and T5-based MOMENT), univariate inputs only, and the in-distribution advantage of the original models (which saw UCR train splits). Open questions include whether the scaling laws persist at much larger scales, whether multivariate CAUKER pre-training can leverage the natural multi-channel structure of the SCM, and whether the approach extends to other TSFM tasks beyond classification and forecasting.
