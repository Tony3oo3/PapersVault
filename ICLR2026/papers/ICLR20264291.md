## Problem

The dominant SFT+RLVR paradigm for training reasoning models depends on expensive human-annotated reasoning traces for the SFT stage and external verifiers for the RL stage, confining reinforcement learning to objectively checkable domains like math and code. This leaves a vast landscape of unverifiable tasks—open-ended QA, creative writing, summarization—without a principled way to cultivate reasoning. The paper asks: can a model learn to generate its own reasoning traces from only question-answer pairs, without expert demonstrations or external verifiers?

## Input / Output

The method consumes only standard question-answer pairs (x, y*) from the tulu-3-sft-mixture dataset (200K samples, average answer length ~400 tokens), with no expert-written reasoning traces. The model produces a structured output: a start-of-reasoning token, a latent reasoning trace z (up to 2048 tokens), an end-of-thought token, and the final answer y. A lightweight format-supervision loss (weight 0.3) is applied only to the two special tokens to enforce this structure without constraining the content of the reasoning trace.

## Method

NRT treats the reasoning trace z as a latent variable and defines a per-trace reward R(z,θ) = f(c₁,…,c_T) where c_i is the model's conditional probability of the i-th ground-truth answer token given the generated trace, and f is a differentiable aggregation function. The resulting gradient (derived via importance sampling) decomposes into two signals: a trace policy gradient scaled by the overall reward, and a per-token supervised gradient weighted by the token reward signal ∂f/∂c_i · c_i, jointly refining trace generation and answer prediction. The paper's key insight is that the choice of f determines robustness: the Arithmetic Mean (used by prior RLPR) can be gamed by trivial traces that boost easy tokens, causing policy collapse, while the Geometric Mean penalizes any single low-probability token, and the Weighted Sum scheme (NRT-WS) up-weights tokens with low baseline probability to force the model to resolve its own uncertainty. Training uses GRPO with group-wise advantage normalization over K=8 sampled traces, a no-reasoning baseline for reward clipping, and no KL or entropy regularization.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B, the best NRT variant (NRT-WS(-logp)) achieves 56.2 average across nine benchmarks, a +10.2 point gain over SFT (46.0) and +5.4 over the strongest prior verifier-free method RLPR (50.8). Gains are most pronounced on reasoning-intensive tasks: GSM8K jumps from 29.0 (SFT) to 76.0, and BBH reaches 54.3 (NRT-GM) versus 41.2 (RLPR). On the smaller Llama-3.2-3B, NRT-WS(-logp) scores 39.9 overall, while all three prior verifier-free baselines (JLB 34.1, Verifree 35.2, RLPR 35.7) fail to surpass the SFT baseline (36.4), highlighting NRT's robustness at lower model scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NRT outperforms all three verifier-free baselines across every model size. Against JLB (Tang et al., 2025; Sequence Log-Probability), NRT-WS(-logp) leads by +7.2 points on Llama-3.1-8B (56.2 vs 49.0) and +5.8 on Llama-3.2-3B (39.9 vs 34.1). Against Verifree (Zhou et al., 2025; Sequence Probability), the margin is +8.1 on 8B (56.2 vs 48.1) and +4.7 on 3B (39.9 vs 35.2). Against RLPR (Yu et al., 2025; Arithmetic Mean), NRT-WS(-logp) gains +5.4 on 8B (56.2 vs 50.8) and +4.2 on 3B (39.9 vs 35.7). A critical finding is that on the 3B model, all three prior methods fall below the SFT baseline (36.4), whereas NRT variants consistently exceed it—demonstrating that NRT's reward shaping (particularly geometric mean and weighted sum) prevents the policy collapse that plagues simpler aggregation functions like arithmetic mean.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a family of verifier-free RL methods (JLB, Verifree, RLPR) under a single latent-variable framework where the reasoning trace is treated as an unobserved variable and the reward is derived from the model's own predictive confidence in the ground-truth answer. It builds directly on GRPO for the optimization loop and on the latent-variable formulations of Tang et al. (2025) and Zhou et al. (2025), but departs from them by introducing a principled taxonomy of aggregation functions that explains *why* prior reward choices (e.g., arithmetic mean) cause policy collapse, and by proposing a weighted-sum scheme that explicitly targets the model's most uncertain tokens. The key conceptual departure from the dominant SFT+RLVR paradigm is the elimination of both expert-written reasoning traces and external verifiers, reducing the data requirement to plain question-answer pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NRT opens a practical path to training reasoning in domains where no objective verifier exists (open-ended QA, summarization, creative tasks), which the SFT+RLVR pipeline simply cannot address. The policy-collapse diagnosis—showing that the arithmetic-mean reward inadvertently incentivizes trivial traces that maximize easy-token probability—gives practitioners a concrete, testable explanation for a failure mode that has plagued self-rewarding RL. Open questions include whether the handcrafted aggregation functions can be replaced by learned or adaptive reward schemes, whether NRT's principles transfer to pre-training (not just fine-tuning), and whether the sampling-based approach can be made more sample-efficient. The compute cost of generating and scoring multiple traces per prompt remains a practical bottleneck for very large-scale deployment.
