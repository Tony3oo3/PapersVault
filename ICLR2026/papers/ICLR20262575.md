## Problem

Existing LoRA parameter-sharing methods (e.g., ShareLoRA, VeRA) reduce trainable parameters by sharing adapters uniformly across all layers, but this sacrifices representational diversity and degrades performance. The paper identifies two previously overlooked structural properties of pre-trained models—Local Similarity (adjacent layers exhibit variable-range information overlap) and Layer-wise Information Heterogeneity (layers carry substantially different amounts of task-relevant information)—and asks how to exploit them to share parameters adaptively without losing expressiveness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained model, a small mini-subset of downstream task training data (used for a one-time ~30-second gradient computation step), and a base LoRA rank r_vanilla. It outputs a set of non-overlapping, variable-length sharing intervals over consecutive layers plus a per-interval integer rank, which together define a collection of shared LoRA adapters with non-uniform ranks. The configuration is computed once before training and remains static throughout, incurring no runtime reconfiguration cost; it can be reused across hyperparameter sweeps for the same model-task pair.

## Method

E²LoRA is a dual-adaptive framework with two components. First, Local Similarity-based Sharing (LSS) computes a gradient-based proxy entropy H(G_l) = log(σ_{G_l}) + ½log(2π) + ½ for each layer (a monotone transform of the centered gradient Frobenius norm, linked to per-layer Fisher information), then derives pairwise Relative Mutual Information (RMI) between layers. A greedy sequential algorithm partitions layers into contiguous sharing intervals using a self-adaptive, layer-specific threshold τ_m (the mean RMI of layer m with all other layers), expanding each interval while the next layer's RMI with the interval's anchor meets τ_m. Second, Heterogeneity-based Rank Allocation (HRA) assigns each interval a representative importance score equal to the maximum proxy entropy among its member layers, normalizes these scores to sum to one, and proportionally distributes the total rank budget N × r_vanilla to each interval's shared adapter. The framework is plug-and-play: it can be layered on top of vanilla LoRA, ShareLoRA, DoRA, VeRA, or other variants by replacing their uniform sharing/rank scheme with the adaptive intervals and ranks, reducing trainable parameters by ~50% while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy.

## Results

On Llama-3.1-8B, E²LoRA halves trainable parameters from 6.82M to 3.61M while improving GSM8K accuracy from 70.21 to 70.51 and HumanEval Pass@1 from 42.68 to 44.31. E²ShareLoRA further reduces parameters to 1.59M (57% fewer than ShareLoRA) while achieving the best HumanEval score of 44.71. On CLIP-ViT-B/16 image classification, E²LoRA achieves 89.81% average accuracy with 0.66M parameters versus LoRA's 89.08% with 1.31M, and E²ShareLoRA reaches 89.73% with only 0.39M parameters. On GLUE (RoBERTa-Base), E²LoRA attains 85.57 average with 0.16M parameters, comparable to LoRA's 85.87 with 0.30M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla LoRA, E²LoRA uses approximately 47% fewer parameters (3.61M vs. 6.82M) while improving GSM8K by +0.30 and HumanEval by +1.63 points; on CLIP-ViT-B/16 it achieves +0.73 average accuracy with half the parameters. Against ShareLoRA, E²ShareLoRA reduces parameters by 42% (1.59M vs. 2.75M) while improving HumanEval Pass@1 by +1.02 and CLIP average accuracy by +0.98. E²LoRA substantially outperforms AdaLoRA (70.51 vs. 70.63 on GSM8K at similar params, but 44.31 vs. 41.46 on HumanEval) and LoRA-FA (70.51 vs. 68.66 on GSM8K, 44.31 vs. 42.07 on HumanEval) at comparable parameter budgets. Against DoRA, E²LoRA matches or exceeds performance (70.51 vs. 69.17 GSM8K, 44.31 vs. 43.70 HumanEval) with fewer parameters. Against LoRA+, E²LoRA achieves comparable GSM8K (70.51 vs. 71.29) and HumanEval (44.31 vs. 44.51) with roughly half the parameters. Against VeRA, E²LoRA achieves dramatically higher accuracy (70.51 vs. 67.63 GSM8K, 44.31 vs. 42.48 HumanEval) while using more parameters, reflecting VeRA's extreme compression at the cost of expressiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

E²LoRA extends the LoRA family by introducing a dual-adaptive mechanism that jointly determines inter-layer parameter sharing intervals and per-interval rank allocation, both guided by a gradient-based proxy entropy metric derived from Lin et al. (2024). Prior sharing methods (ShareLoRA, VeRA, Tied-LoRA) impose fixed or heuristic sharing topologies across all layers, while prior rank-allocation methods (AdaLoRA, IncreLoRA, DyLoRA) adjust ranks within individual layers without cross-layer sharing. This paper is the first to use entropy-based similarity (via Relative Mutual Information) to adaptively partition layers into sharing groups and to use absolute proxy entropy to distribute rank budgets, unifying both decisions under a single information-theoretic signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~50% trainable parameter reduction while matching or surpassing baselines across NLU, NLG, and image classification tasks makes E²LoRA a practical, plug-and-play efficiency layer for existing LoRA pipelines, with negligible initialization overhead (~30s) and reusable configurations. The orthogonality demonstrated with DoRA, VeRA, and LoRI suggests broad applicability as a general compression strategy. Open questions include whether static rank allocation based on initial gradients remains optimal for very long or multi-task training regimes, and whether the method extends naturally to non-attention modules (FFN, embeddings) or to continual learning settings where gradient statistics shift over time.
