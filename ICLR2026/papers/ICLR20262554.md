## Problem

RAMBO, the most widely adopted adversarial model learning method for model-based offline RL, suffers from two critical practical failures: the adversarial weighting coefficient λ is extremely sensitive (slightly larger values cause severe Q-value underestimation and gradient explosion), and the model-gradient-based adversarial training is inherently unstable. This makes it difficult to control the degree of conservatism and limits RAMBO's practical utility.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard offline dataset of (state, action, reward, next_state) tuples and a single hyperparameter ξ that directly quantifies the conservatism level via the radius of a Wasserstein state uncertainty set. It outputs a Gaussian dynamics model (ensemble of neural networks predicting next-state distributions) and a policy trained with SAC on a 50/50 mix of offline and model-rolled-out data. Unlike RAMBO's hand-tuned λ, ξ provides a monotonic and interpretable control knob: larger ξ yields more conservative predictions without triggering training collapse even at ξ=10.

## Method

ROMI replaces RAMBO's model-gradient adversarial loss with a Robust Value-aware model Learning (RVL) loss that requires the dynamics model to predict future states whose values approximate the minimum Q-value within a Wasserstein state uncertainty set U_ξ(s′), derived via a dual reformulation (Proposition 4.1) that transforms the dynamics uncertainty set into a state-level perturbation. To address the OOD generalization gap of value-only training, ROMI introduces implicitly differentiable adaptive weighting: a bi-level optimization where the inner level updates the dynamics model via weighted supervised learning (dynamics awareness) and the outer level updates a lightweight MLP weighting network via implicit differentiation to minimize the RVL loss (value awareness). The weighting network assigns per-sample weights in [0.5, 2] to the MLE loss, adaptively prioritizing samples that best reduce the value-aware error. Theoretical guarantees include bounded Q-value estimates (Proposition 4.2), bounded generalization error ε₁ ≤ L_V·(ε+ξ) (Corollary 4.1), and O(1/√K) convergence for both inner and outer losses (Proposition 4.3).

## Results

On 12 D4RL MuJoCo datasets, ROMI achieves a total normalized score of 953.5, surpassing RAMBO (804.1) by 18.6%, Count-MORL (927.5) by 2.8%, and MOBILE (857.7) by 11.2%. On 9 NeoRL datasets, ROMI achieves a total score of 472.2, outperforming all baselines including CQL (466.3), MOBILE (456.4), and RAMBO (382.8). ROMI wins on 11/12 D4RL datasets and 6/9 NeoRL datasets, with particularly large gains over RAMBO on datasets where RAMBO underperforms (e.g., hopper-medium-replay: 102.0 vs 77.2; walker2d-medium-expert: 113.3 vs 73.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROMI outperforms RAMBO on 11/12 D4RL datasets (total 953.5 vs 804.1, +18.6%) and on all 9 NeoRL datasets (472.2 vs 382.8, +23.4%), with the largest gaps on datasets where RAMBO's adversarial gradient causes Q-value collapse (e.g., walker2d-medium-expert: 113.3 vs 73.7). Against the strongest model-based baselines, ROMI beats MOBILE (857.7 on D4RL, 456.4 on NeoRL) and Count-MORL (927.5 on D4RL) in total score, achieving the best single-dataset result on 11/12 D4RL tasks. Model-free baselines CQL and IQL trail significantly on D4RL (~710 total) but are competitive on NeoRL (CQL: 466.3), where ROMI still leads by a narrow margin. MOPO is consistently the weakest model-based baseline, scoring 769.6 on D4RL and only 258.1 on NeoRL, where it fails on several tasks (e.g., Hopper-M: 0.0). EDAC (434.1 on NeoRL) is outperformed by ROMI by 38.1 points in total, confirming that the bi-level adaptive weighting provides meaningful gains over simpler uncertainty-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the adversarial model learning framework for model-based offline RL introduced by Uehara & Sun (2021) and practically instantiated by RAMBO (Rigter et al., 2022), which uses a model-gradient adversarial loss with a hand-tuned Lagrange multiplier λ. ROMI departs from RAMBO by replacing the model-gradient adversarial term with a robust value-aware loss that explicitly characterizes one-step value estimation error via a Wasserstein-based state uncertainty set, drawing on value-aware model learning (Farahmand et al., 2017; Voelcker et al., 2022) and robust MDP duality. The bi-level optimization with an adaptive-weighting network is inspired by Meta-Weight-Net (Shu et al., 2019) and TEMPO (Yuan et al., 2023), but is adapted here to jointly achieve dynamics awareness (inner level, weighted MLE) and value awareness (outer level, implicit differentiation through the RVL loss).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ROMI addresses a practical fragility in RAMBO—the most widely adopted adversarial model learning method—showing that even modest increases in λ cause Q-value divergence and gradient explosion, effectively limiting RAMBO's conservatism to near-zero. By replacing λ with the uncertainty set scale ξ, ROMI provides a principled and interpretable knob for conservatism that remains stable across a wide range of values (0.01 to 10). Key limitations include the additional computational cost of bi-level optimization and the requirement to fix ξ before training, unlike methods supporting runtime adaptation. Open questions include whether the Wasserstein-based reformulation extends naturally to high-dimensional or discrete state spaces, and whether the adaptive weighting can be made more computationally efficient.
