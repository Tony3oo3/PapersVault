## Problem

Representation learning for combinatorial optimization (CO) problems remains underexplored compared to text and vision, with existing ML4CO approaches tailored to single problem types and lacking cross-domain knowledge transfer. This paper addresses the absence of a unified pre-training paradigm that can learn general representations across different NP-complete CO problem types, leveraging the fact that all NPC problems can be reduced to Boolean satisfiability (SAT).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262611/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes graph instances from seven GDP types (primarily edge-only graphs without inherent vertex features, where initial vertex embeddings are learned) and their polynomial-time-transformed SAT graph representations (literal-clause graphs or variable-clause graphs). It produces binary satisfiability decisions for GDPs, instance-level graph representations via GNN encoders, and—when integrated into problem-specific neural solvers—optimization solutions for MVC, MIS, MC, and MDS. Notably, the contrastive positive pairs are (GDP instance, its SAT form) rather than augmented copies, making the approach entirely augmentation-free.

## Method

ConRep4CO transforms each GDP instance into its CNF/SAT form, creating a unified intermediary modality that bridges structurally disparate problem types. It jointly trains n graph models (one per GDP type, using GNN backbones like GCN, GraphSAGE, PGN, or GraphGPS) and one unified SAT model (using NeuroSAT or a tailored GCN), each comprising a GNN-based Representation Extractor and an MLP Output Module. Training uses two losses: a binary cross-entropy decision loss applied independently to each model, and an InfoNCE contrastive loss that aligns each GDP representation with its corresponding SAT representation while pushing apart non-corresponding pairs. A warm-start strategy first trains with decision loss only before introducing the contrastive loss, and the SAT model is optimized with the average contrastive loss across all GDP modalities. The pre-trained SAT model can then be frozen and used to enhance problem-specific neural solvers via a lightweight contrastive alignment step, avoiding full retraining.

## Results

On the four NP-hard optimization problems, incorporating ConRep4CO into problem-specific neural solvers yields average objective-value-gap reductions of 61.27% (MVC with OptGNN), 32.20% (MIS), 36.46% (MC), and 45.29% (MDS) compared to their respective baselines. For GDP representation quality, ConRep4CO consistently improves solving accuracy across all seven graph decision problems; for example, with GCN+LCG+NeuroSAT on Easy instances, overall accuracy rises from 68.5% to 71.9%, and with GraphSAGE backbone the improvement is particularly large (54.9% → 73.9%). On hard (unseen-scale) instances, ConRep4CO-trained models also generalize better, e.g., overall accuracy improves from 55.6% to 57.1% (GCN, Easy-trained) and from 58.8% to 61.5% (VCG+GCN, Medium-trained).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ConRep4CO-enhanced solvers against three problem-specific baselines. For MVC, OptGNN [72] is the strongest baseline: ConRep4CO reduces its gap_abs from 1.25 to 0.08 on ER(50,100) (93.6% gain) and from 3.25 to 1.58 on ER(100,200) (51.4% gain), yielding a 61.27% average gain; the weaker GCNN [72] baseline sees a smaller 25.74% average gain. For MIS, MC, and MDS, GFlowNet [75] serves as the baseline: ConRep4CO reduces gap_abs from 0.92 to 0.54 on RB(200,300) MIS (41.3% gain) and from 2.81 to 1.58 on RB(200,300) MC (43.8% gain), with average gains of 32.20% and 36.46% respectively. For MDS, the gain is 45.29% on average (e.g., 58.33% on BA(200,300)). ConRep4CO wins on every problem and every scale tested, with gains diminishing at larger scales (e.g., MVC ER(400,500) gain drops to 38.83% vs. 93.60% at ER(50,100)), likely due to the scale mismatch between pre-training and evaluation instances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the contrastive learning paradigm from vision (SimCLR) to combinatorial optimization, but departs fundamentally from both vision contrastive learning (which relies on image augmentations) and graph contrastive learning (which relies on structural/feature perturbations). Instead, it uses the theoretical fact that all NP-complete problems reduce to SAT as an augmentation-free mechanism for constructing positive/negative pairs across problem types. It positions itself against multi-task CO approaches like GOAL and UniCO, which share a single backbone across problems without contrastive alignment, and against problem-specific ML4CO solvers that do not leverage cross-domain structure. The SAT model backbone draws on NeuroSAT, while the graph model backbones span GCN, GraphSAGE, PGN, and GraphGPS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ConRep4CO offers a practical, plug-and-play pre-training paradigm that can enhance existing problem-specific CO solvers without full retraining, with the pretrained SAT model being reusable across downstream tasks. The augmentation-free design addresses a genuine bottleneck in applying contrastive learning to structured/discrete data where meaningful augmentations are hard to define. Key limitations include reliance on SAT transformation (adding a polynomial-time step), evaluation primarily on small-to-medium graph instances (50–1200 vertices), and the observation that gains diminish at larger scales. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-graph CO (e.g., MILP), whether unsupervised variants can reduce label dependence, and whether alternative unified forms beyond SAT could capture richer structure.
