## Problem

Few-shot object detection with only K annotated boxes per class remains challenging, and while vision foundation models enable training-free detection pipelines, the Universal Proposal Network (UPN) produces severely over-fragmented bounding boxes that cover only partial object regions, inflating false positives and degrading detection accuracy. The paper asks whether a carefully designed combination of multiple VFMs, augmented with a structural score-refinement mechanism, can achieve strong few-shot detection without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a support set of K annotated bounding boxes per novel class (each with image, box coordinates, and class label) and a query image as input. DINOv2 resizes images to 630×630 (patch size 14) for feature extraction, while UPN generates up to 500 class-agnostic proposals per image. The output is the top 100 bounding boxes per image, each assigned a predicted class label and a graph-diffusion-refined confidence score that downweights fragmented proposals relative to whole-object detections.

## Method

FSOD-VFM integrates three vision foundation models: UPN generates category-agnostic bounding box proposals, SAM2 extracts precise object masks for each proposal, and DINOv2 provides dense feature maps for masked RoI pooling to build class-level prototypes. The key novelty is a graph diffusion-based confidence reweighting step: proposals of the same class form a directed graph where edge weights encode mask-overlap ratios, and a PageRank-inspired iterative diffusion (with restart probability α=0.3) propagates confidence so that high-score, well-covered proposals retain energy while fragmented, low-score proposals lose it. The final score multiplies a diffusion-derived penalty (1−π̂ⱼ)^λ by the cosine similarity to the nearest class prototype, effectively suppressing over-fragmented boxes without hard thresholding as in NMS. The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring only the K support annotations and no fine-tuning on novel classes.

## Results

On Pascal-5i, FSOD-VFM achieves 77.5 average nAP50 across three novel splits, outperforming the closest training-free baseline No-Time-To-Train (71.2) by 6.3 points and the best training-based method NIFF (59.4) by 18.1 points. On COCO-20i (10-shot), FSOD-VFM reaches 44.0 nAP / 59.4 nAP50 / 47.6 nAP75, surpassing No-Time-To-Train (36.6/54.1/38.3) and CD-ViTO (35.3/54.9/37.2). On the cross-domain CD-FSOD benchmark, FSOD-VFM attains 25.3/31.3/31.6 average nAP at 1/5/10-shot, substantially exceeding No-Time-To-Train (18.0/22.4/21.4) and even the fine-tuned Mixture method (18.2/34.7/39.8) in the 1-shot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSOD-VFM outperforms all 21 compared baselines on Pascal-5i and COCO-20i. Among training-free methods, the closest competitor No-Time-To-Train (Espinosa et al., 2025) is surpassed by 6.3 nAP50 on Pascal-5i (77.5 vs 71.2) and 7.4 nAP on COCO-20i 10-shot (44.0 vs 36.6); the key differentiator is the graph diffusion reweighting, which alone adds 11.5 nAP50 over the Soft Merging strategy used by No-Time-To-Train (Table 4). DE-ViT in all ViT variants (S/B/L) trails FSOD-VFM by 16–21 nAP50 on Pascal-5i, and Detic/DE-ViT on CD-FSOD are far behind (1.0 vs 25.3 avg nAP at 1-shot). Among training-based methods, NIFF (59.4) and LVC (52.3) are the strongest on Pascal-5i, yet FSOD-VFM exceeds them by 18.1 and 25.2 nAP50 respectively without any fine-tuning. On CD-FSOD, FSOD-VFM wins the 1-shot average over all methods including the fine-tuned Mixture (25.3 vs 18.2), but in the 5- and 10-shot settings, Mixture (34.7/39.8) and CD-ViTO (26.1/29.6) still lead on domain-specific subsets like NEU-DET and UODD, where fine-tuning on target domains provides an advantage that a training-free approach cannot match.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free few-shot object detection line initiated by No-Time-To-Train (Espinosa et al., 2025), which first demonstrated that combining UPN, SAM2, and DINOv2 can yield competitive FSOD without any task-specific training. The key departure is the introduction of a graph diffusion mechanism (inspired by PageRank) to reweight proposal confidence, replacing the simpler soft-merging strategy used in the prior work. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of vision foundation model composition and graph-based score propagation, treating bounding box over-fragmentation as a structural problem solvable through inter-proposal information flow rather than threshold-based suppression (NMS) or heuristic merging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free paradigm demonstrated here removes the need for any fine-tuning on novel classes, making FSOD immediately deployable in data-scarce or rapidly changing domains (autonomous driving, medical imaging, industrial inspection). The graph diffusion mechanism offers a general, interpretable post-processing tool that could potentially transfer to other detection or segmentation pipelines suffering from proposal fragmentation. However, the method's ~2.4 s/image inference cost on a single A40 GPU limits real-time applicability, and performance degrades on domain-specific benchmarks (NEU-DET, Deep Fish, UODD) where fine-tuned methods still hold an advantage. The appendix results with RADIOv4 suggest the framework's ceiling is higher than the main experiments indicate, pointing to an open question about how much further performance can be pushed by swapping in stronger or more specialized feature extractors.
