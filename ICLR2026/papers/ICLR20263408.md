## Problem

Neural simulators, once trained, deliver a fixed accuracy at a fixed cost with no explicit test-time knob to trade accuracy against compute, unlike classical numerical methods where resolution and order directly control this trade-off. Existing adaptive-compute alternatives (Deep Equilibrium models, diffusion-based refiners) suffer from early plateauing, architecture dependence, and poor generalization to out-of-distribution iteration counts, limiting their utility for workflows that require both fast exploratory runs and high-accuracy critical simulations from a single model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a PDE state field (scalar or vector field on a spatial grid, e.g., 1D Burgers/KdV/KS fields, 3D compressible Navier-Stokes with 5 channels on a 64³ grid, or 3D shape fields) and a user-specified integer K as input, producing the predicted next time-step state. The encoder's conditioning vector is merged with the latent at every recurrent step, so the same input state yields different-quality predictions depending solely on K, enabling one trained model to serve both real-time control loops (small K) and offline high-fidelity studies (large K) without retraining.

## Method

RecurrSim is an architecture-agnostic, three-component framework (encoder → recurrent-depth block → decoder) in which the recurrent-depth block is applied K times at inference, with the conditioning vector merged into the latent at each step via element-wise weighted addition. During training, K is sampled from a Poisson log-normal distribution to expose the block to a wide range of compute budgets and encourage contraction toward a fixed point; truncated backpropagation-through-depth with a fixed window B (typically 4) caps memory at O(B) regardless of K. The framework is plug-and-play: each component can be instantiated with any architecture primitive (Fourier layers, attention, graph convolutions), and the system is trained end-to-end with a standard supervised loss and no custom schedulers. This design mirrors the convergence behavior of fixed-point and Newton methods, yielding a smooth, monotone accuracy-cost curve across K that outperforms DEQ and diffusion-based alternatives while using fewer parameters.

## Results

On 3D Compressible Navier-Stokes (262k grid points), RecurrFNO (0.8B params) achieves MSE of 7.37×10⁻² (density), 7.33×10⁻² (pressure), and 7.36×10⁻² (velocity), outperforming the 1.6B FNO baseline (7.61/7.59/7.62×10⁻²) while using 13.5% less training memory (64 GB vs 73 GB). On Active Matter, RecurrViT (75M params) reduces trajectory error by 90% at 12 timesteps compared to ViT (130M params): 5.68 vs 43.16 MSE×10⁻². On ShapeNet-Car, RecurrUPT (92M params) achieves 2.19 MSE×10⁻², surpassing UPT (164M params) at 2.31 MSE×10⁻². Across 1D PDE benchmarks, RecurrFNO achieves 82% lower trajectory error than the best adaptive-compute baseline on Burgers (0.008 vs 0.044 MSE) and up to 98% longer correlation horizons on chaotic Kuramoto-Sivashinsky.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RecurrFNO decisively outperforms all three adaptive-compute alternatives (FNO-DEQ, ACDM, PDE-Refiner) across Burgers, KdV, and Kuramoto-Sivashinsky. FNO-DEQ plateaus by K≈4 due to latent oscillation around the fixed point rather than convergence, yielding 82% higher MSE on Burgers (0.045 vs 0.008) and 4.3× higher on KdV (0.094 vs 0.022). ACDM also plateaus near K≈4, limiting its test-time flexibility, while PDE-Refiner degrades beyond K≈11 due to out-of-distribution inference depths. On 3D Compressible Navier-Stokes, RecurrFNO (0.8B) surpasses the 1.6B FNO baseline on all three physical fields (density, pressure, velocity) with 13.5% less training memory. On ShapeNet-Car, RecurrUPT (92M params, 56% of UPT's 164M) achieves lower MSE (2.19 vs 2.31×10⁻²), demonstrating drop-in compatibility with the Universal Physics Transformer architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

RecurrSim generalizes the test-time compute knob of Deep Equilibrium Models (Bai et al., 2019) into an architecture-agnostic framework: rather than requiring a specific fixed-point formulation and implicit-function-theorem gradients, it wraps any encoder–block–decoder stack in a recurrent loop with truncated backpropagation-through-depth. The training-time sampling of the iteration count K from a Poisson log-normal distribution is adopted directly from Geiping et al. (2025), who applied the same distribution to latent reasoning in LLMs. The paper positions itself against diffusion-based adaptive-compute simulators (ACDM, PDE-Refiner) and FNO-DEQ as the primary alternatives, but departs from all of them by requiring no custom losses, no noise schedules, and no architectural redesign—making it a drop-in modification of fewer than 20 lines of code.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first systematic study of test-time accuracy-cost control as a first-class design objective for neural simulators, and the plug-and-play nature of the framework (works with FNO, ViT, UPT without modification) makes it immediately adoptable by the scientific ML community. The demonstration that a 0.8B RecurrFNO outperforms 1.6B FNO baselines on 3D Navier-Stokes while using less memory suggests that iterative refinement can substitute for raw parameter count, which has implications for how we allocate compute in neural simulators. Open questions include whether the Poisson log-normal distribution over K is optimal, whether the approach extends to non-autonomous or multi-scale PDEs, and whether the accuracy-cost plateau (around K≈16 for Burgers) can be pushed further with richer recurrent blocks or learned stopping criteria.
