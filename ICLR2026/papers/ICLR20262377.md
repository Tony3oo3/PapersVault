## Problem

Existing FL communication compression methods (Top-k, ATOMO, PowerSGD) select which units to retain or drop based solely on magnitude or randomness, ignoring the actual output discrepancy each dropped unit induces. In FL this is especially problematic because the compressed content is multi-step parameter updates rather than single-step gradients, so magnitude is a poor proxy for importance—a small-magnitude element can cause 10⁸× more output distortion than a large-magnitude one. The paper asks whether a calibration-based, output-sensitivity-aware selection rule can replace magnitude-based rules to improve accuracy under tight communication budgets.

## Input / Output

Each client feeds a per-layer parameter update matrix (the difference between local and global weights after E local epochs) together with a small randomly sampled calibration subset (default 64 local examples) and a communication budget (compression ratio for sparsification or retained rank for low-rank). The method outputs a compressed layer update that retains the subset of compression units (individual elements or singular triplets) maximizing total retained output-discrepancy mass under the budget. The calibration activations are recomputed each round, and the method operates per-layer under either a global or layer-level budget, making it a drop-in plugin for existing compressors.

## Method

The core mechanism scores every candidate compression unit by the Frobenius-norm output discrepancy it would induce on a small local calibration set, then keeps the units with the largest scores under the communication budget. For element-wise sparsification in Transformer layers the score is w²_ij·‖f_j‖²_F (element magnitude squared times the energy of the corresponding input-activation row); for low-rank decomposition it is σ²_t·‖v_tᵀX‖²_F (singular value squared times the energy of the right singular vector projected onto calibration inputs). Analogous cross-correlation-based formulas are derived for CNN convolution layers, with an efficient two-pass evaluation that reduces cost from O(rF²H'W') to O(rFH'W'). The selection rule is compressor-agnostic and plugs directly into Top-k, ATOMO, and PowerSGD without altering their factorization or encoding, and is fully compatible with the standard error-feedback scheme. A convergence analysis shows the discrepancy-aware Top-k compressor is a δ-approximate compressor (δ = c·k/d), yielding the same O(1/√T) non-convex convergence rate as vanilla SGD with error feedback.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ViT-tiny at a compression ratio of 0.1, the discrepancy-aware Top-k achieves 41.52% test accuracy versus 34.93% for magnitude-based Top-k, an 18.9% relative improvement. At the most aggressive setting (ratio 0.01), the gap widens to 29.62% vs. 21.03% on the same configuration. For low-rank ATOMO, discrepancy-aware selection improves CIFAR-100 (ViT-base) from 17.08% to 20.17% at retained rank 1. The method also accelerates convergence, reaching 80% of target accuracy up to 1.56× faster under strong compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: magnitude-based Top-k (element-wise sparsification) and magnitude-based ATOMO (low-rank decomposition). Discrepancy-aware compression consistently outperforms both baselines across all four dataset-model combinations and all compression levels tested. The advantage is most pronounced under aggressive compression: at ratio 0.01 on CIFAR-10 (ViT-tiny), the gap reaches 8.59 percentage points (29.62% vs. 21.03%), and at ratio 0.1 it is 6.59 points (41.52% vs. 34.93%). For ATOMO, the discrepancy-aware variant gains 1.57 points at rank 1 on CIFAR-10 (ViT-tiny) and 3.09 points at rank 1 on CIFAR-100 (ViT-base). At the uncompressed setting (ratio 1.0 / full rank), both methods converge to the same accuracy, confirming that the discrepancy-aware selection only affects which units are retained under budget constraints. The method also reduces communication rounds to reach target accuracy by up to 1.56× under strong compression, indicating faster wall-clock convergence in addition to higher final accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a drop-in enhancement for existing FL communication compression methods, specifically Top-k sparsification (Lin et al., ICLR 2018) and ATOMO low-rank decomposition (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2018), while also demonstrating compatibility with PowerSGD (Vogels et al., NeurIPS 2019) and the classic error-feedback scheme (Karimireddy et al., ICML 2019). The key conceptual departure is replacing magnitude- or randomness-based selection rules with a calibration-data-driven output discrepancy metric, motivated by the observation that FL transmits multi-step parameter updates rather than single-step gradients, weakening the magnitude-to-importance correlation. Unlike prior FL-tailored compression work (FedFQ, FedAQ, FedMPQ, HGC) that focuses on adaptive quantization or hybrid coding, this paper targets the selection criterion itself, arguing it is the fundamental bottleneck under tight communication budgets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18.9% relative accuracy gain at a 0.1 compression ratio, validated across three datasets and five architectures, makes this a practically relevant improvement for bandwidth-constrained FL deployments (IoT, mobile). The method's plugin nature means it can be layered onto existing compression pipelines without architectural changes, lowering adoption friction. Open questions include whether the calibration overhead remains acceptable at larger model scales (beyond ViT-base), how the approach interacts with secure aggregation or differential privacy constraints that limit local data access, and whether the extension to quantization-based methods (sketched in Appendix A.4) yields comparable gains. The convergence analysis, while reassuring, relies on a uniform lower-bound assumption (φ ≥ c) that may be hard to verify in practice for very heterogeneous clients.
