## Problem

Open-source instruction-guided image editing lags behind closed-source models largely because no reliable, human-aligned reward model exists to filter and scale up high-quality synthetic training data. Existing rewards (LPIPS, CLIP, general-purpose VLM-as-judge) show weak alignment with human editing preferences, and prior preference datasets rely on noisy crowd-sourced annotations or biased pseudo-labels from proprietary models, leaving a critical gap in both data quality and reward signal fidelity.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a tri-modal input: a source image, an edited image (both resized to 448×448 preserving aspect ratio), and a text instruction. It produces two independent Gaussian-distributed scores (μ, σ²) — one for Instruction Following and one for Visual Quality — which are aggregated (via balanced mean) into a single effective score for pairwise preference ranking. In the data-curation application, these scores are used to rank and select top-K subsets from noisy candidate pools.

## Method

EDITREWARD uses a fully fine-tuned VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or MiMo-VL-7B) followed by a multi-head MLP reward head, where each head independently predicts Gaussian parameters (μ, σ²) for one of two evaluation dimensions (Instruction Following, Visual Quality). Training employs a Multi-Dimensional Uncertainty-Aware Ranking Loss that extends HPSv3's probabilistic preference framework: dimensional means are aggregated (balanced mean chosen via ablation), and the preference probability is computed by integrating over the two resulting Gaussian distributions, minimizing negative log-likelihood of the ground-truth preference. A key data-level innovation is tie disentanglement: tied pairs where one image wins on IF and the other on VQ are duplicated with opposing preference labels, forcing the model to learn nuanced dimensional trade-offs. The model is trained on EDITREWARD-DATA (200K+ expert-annotated preference pairs from 6 SOTA editing models, scored on a 4-point Likert scale across both dimensions by trained annotators with Krippendorff's α ≈ 0.67/0.60 for IF/VQ), and evaluated on a new multi-way (2/3/4-way) preference benchmark, EDITREWARD-BENCH.

## Results

EDITREWARD (on MiMo-VL-7B-SFT) achieves state-of-the-art human-preference alignment, scoring 65.72% on GenAI-Bench and 63.62% on AURORA-Bench, surpassing GPT-5 (59.61% and 47.27%) and the best open-source method ADIEE (59.96% and 55.56%). On the proposed EDITREWARD-BENCH (multi-way preference ranking), it scores 38.42% overall, narrowly beating Gemini-2.5-Flash (38.02%) and GPT-5 (37.81%). In the downstream data-curation experiment, fine-tuning Step1X-Edit on the EDITREWARD-filtered Top-20K subset of ShareGPT-4o-Image improves the GEdit-Bench-EN overall score from 6.444 (base) to 7.086, outperforming training on the full 46K noisy set (6.780). On OOD tasks (Text and Style from Open Images), EDITREWARD achieves 46.80% accuracy, exceeding GPT-4o's 41.69%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary VLM judges, EDITREWARD (MiMo-VL-7B) decisively outperforms GPT-5 by +6.11 points on GenAI-Bench (65.72 vs. 59.61) and +16.35 on AURORA-Bench (63.62 vs. 47.27), but trails on ImagenHub (35.20 vs. 40.85), where GPT-4o leads at 38.21. On the more challenging EDITREWARD-BENCH, EDITREWARD edges out Gemini-2.5-Flash (38.42 vs. 38.02) and GPT-5 (38.42 vs. 37.81), while GPT-4o lags significantly at 28.31, confirming the benchmark's discriminative power. Among open-source methods, EDITREWARD surpasses ADIEE by +5.76 on GenAI-Bench and +8.06 on AURORA-Bench, and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Inst sees a +23.49 point uplift after reward-model training (40.48 → 63.97). In the data-curation application, the EDITREWARD-filtered Top-20K subset elevates Step1X-Edit's GEdit-Bench-EN overall score to 7.086, matching Doubao (6.983) but remaining below GPT-Image-1 (7.494). On OOD generalization (Text and Style categories from Open Images), EDITREWARD outperforms GPT-4o by +5.11 points overall (46.80 vs. 41.69), with the gap driven primarily by the Style category (45.41 vs. 35.79).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probabilistic uncertainty-aware reward modeling paradigm of HPSv3 (Sun et al., 2025) from single-dimension text-to-image evaluation to the multi-faceted domain of instruction-guided image editing, introducing separate Gaussian reward distributions for Instruction Following and Visual Quality. It departs from prior VLM-as-judge approaches (VIEScore, ADIEE) that either use general-purpose VLMs without editing-specific fine-tuning or rely on noisy crowd-sourced/model-generated labels, instead training on a 200K expert-annotated dataset with a rigorous multi-dimensional rubric. The tie-disentanglement strategy—decomposing ambiguous tied pairs into opposing preference labels based on dimensional strengths—is a novel data augmentation technique not present in prior Bradley-Terry-style reward training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete bottleneck for open-source image editing: the absence of a reliable, human-aligned reward model for scaling training data. The practical demonstration that a 20K EDITREWARD-curated subset outperforms the full 46K noisy ShareGPT-4o-Image dataset (7.086 vs 6.780 G_O on GEdit-Bench) provides a clear recipe for data curation that the community can adopt. However, the model still exhibits identifiable failure modes (brightness/saturation bias, background over-editing tolerance), and the gap to proprietary editors like GPT-Image-1 remains substantial, suggesting that reward-model-guided curation alone is insufficient to close the frontier. The multi-way preference benchmark (EDITREWARD-BENCH) is a useful addition that reveals model weaknesses invisible to pairwise evaluation, but its 500-group scale limits statistical power.
