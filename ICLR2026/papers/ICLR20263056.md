## Problem

Existing multi-view 3D reconstruction methods produce incomplete results under occlusions and sparse coverage, while diffusion-based 3D generative methods can hallucinate missing parts but suffer from stochastic denoising that prevents pixel-level alignment with input views. The paper identifies two root causes of this inconsistency: insufficient cross-view correlation modeling when extracting multi-view image features as diffusion conditions, and poor controllability of the iterative denoising process during local detail generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263056/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an arbitrary number of uncalibrated RGB images (512×512) from any camera poses, with no requirement for fixed viewpoints or known intrinsics. It produces complete 3D objects decodable as textured meshes, 3D Gaussians, or radiance fields. Unlike several baselines (LGM, InstantMesh, Hunyuan3D-2.0-mv) that require inputs from specific orthogonal viewpoints, ReconViaGen handles views from arbitrary angles, and performance scales gracefully with the number of input views (tested from 1 to 8).

## Method

ReconViaGen integrates VGGT reconstruction priors into the TRELLIS coarse-to-fine diffusion generator via three mechanisms. First, a Condition Net aggregates multi-layer VGGT features into a fixed-length global geometry token list (GGC) that conditions the SS Flow transformer for accurate coarse structure. Second, per-view token lists (PVC) extracted from individual-view VGGT features condition the SLAT Flow transformer through weighted cross-attention fusion across all views for fine-grained geometry and texture. Third, a Rendering-aware Velocity Compensation (RVC) mechanism operates at inference only: when the denoising timestep t < 0.5, the current SLAT is decoded into a mesh, rendered from refined camera poses, and the SSIM+LPIPS+DreamSim loss against input images is backpropagated to compute a velocity correction Δv that steers the denoising trajectory toward pixel-aligned consistency. Both VGGT and TRELLIS are fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64) on 390k Objaverse objects, and classifier-free guidance is applied during generation.

## Results

On Dora-Bench, ReconViaGen achieves PSNR 22.63, SSIM 0.911, LPIPS 0.090, CD 0.090, and F-score 0.953, outperforming the strongest baseline Hunyuan3D-2.0-mv (PSNR 20.22, F-score 0.937) by a clear margin on all metrics. On OmniObject3D, ReconViaGen reaches PSNR 19.77, SSIM 0.847, LPIPS 0.141, CD 0.059, and F-score 0.959, surpassing the best baseline InstantMesh (PSNR 17.50, CD 0.094, F-score 0.907) by 2.3 dB in PSNR and 0.05 in F-score. Notably, ReconViaGen also outperforms the pure reconstruction prior VGGT (CD 0.112, F-score 0.921 on Dora-Bench) in both geometry accuracy and completeness, demonstrating that integrating generation priors into reconstruction yields superior results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReconViaGen wins on all five metrics against every baseline on both datasets. Against the strongest 3D generation baseline Hunyuan3D-2.0-mv, ReconViaGen improves PSNR by 2.4 dB (22.63 vs 20.22) and F-score by 0.016 on Dora-Bench, and by 3.1 dB in PSNR and 0.088 in F-score on OmniObject3D. Against the best reconstruction baselines, ReconViaGen outperforms InstantMesh (the top pose-known method) by 2.3 dB PSNR and 0.052 F-score on OmniObject3D, and outperforms VGGT (the top pose-free method) by 0.022 in CD and 0.032 in F-score on Dora-Bench. The TRELLIS baselines (S and M modes) show the largest gaps, with ReconViaGen improving CD by 0.054–0.086 and F-score by 0.11–0.15, confirming that reconstruction-conditioned conditioning and rendering-aware velocity compensation are critical for accurate multi-view reconstruction. On OmniObject3D, ReconViaGen also surpasses the pose-free LucidFusion by 3.5 dB PSNR and 0.091 F-score, highlighting the advantage of diffusion-based generation priors over regression-based ones for detail fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: pose-free multi-view reconstruction (VGGT, DUSt3R, LucidFusion) and diffusion-based 3D generation (TRELLIS, Hunyuan3D, One2345++). Prior reconstruction methods produce incomplete outputs due to occlusions and sparse coverage, while prior generation methods produce complete but stochastic results inconsistent with inputs. ReconViaGen departs from both by using VGGT's multi-view features as structured diffusion conditions (a global geometry token list for coarse structure and per-view token lists for fine details) and introduces a rendering-aware velocity compensation mechanism that constrains the denoising trajectory at inference time — a design not present in either the reconstruction or generation literature independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental tension in 3D reconstruction — completeness versus input fidelity — by showing that strong reconstruction priors can effectively 'steer' a diffusion generator to produce both complete and accurate results. The rendering-aware velocity compensation is a practical inference-only technique that could be adapted to other diffusion-based 3D pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the TRELLIS architecture, how it scales to scene-level (rather than object-level) reconstruction, and whether the reliance on VGGT's pose estimation quality becomes a bottleneck for highly occluded or textureless objects.
