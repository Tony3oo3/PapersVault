## Problem

Existing 3D point cloud generation methods treat shape synthesis as an unconstrained distribution learning problem, neglecting explicit modeling of part composition and symmetry, which leads to poor part organization and broken or inconsistent symmetries in generated shapes. While a few recent efforts support part-level generation, none incorporate symmetry as a learned, relational prior, leaving a notable gap: no unified framework jointly encodes both part-level composition and symmetry structures to guide coherent, interpretable 3D shape generation.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes Gaussian noise as input and produces 2048-point 3D point clouds decomposed into M semantic parts (M=4 for airplanes and cars, M=3 for chairs), each accompanied by a learned symmetry group and a rigid assembler transformation (translation, rotation, scaling). During generation, only the fundamental domain of each part is synthesized—typically half or a quarter the size of the full part—and the complete part is reconstructed by applying the symmetry group, which guarantees exact symmetry at the part level. A shared 128-dimensional shape latent conditions all components to maintain global structural coherence across assembled parts.

## Method

The Quartet employs four sequentially trained diffusion models: a U-Net for 128-dim shape latents (encoded by a sparse VAE with PVCNN backbone and a KKT-based sparsity constraint on the final activation layer), a ResNet for symmetry groups (represented as at most three reflections in Hesse normal form, learned via score matching with a mean-shift-inspired Langevin dynamics update), a Transformer with 3D window attention for part fundamental domains, and a U-Net with cross-attention for 9-dim rigid assemblers per part. The central insight is that symmetry is enforced structurally—only the fundamental domain is generated and the full part is reconstructed via the learned symmetry group—rather than through a soft MSE loss, guaranteeing exact symmetry in outputs. A part encoder with equivariance fine-tuning produces pose-invariant embeddings that decouple geometric modeling from transformation learning, allowing the assembler diffusion to focus on spatial configuration. Generation proceeds in two phases: part generation (sampling the shape latent z, per-part symmetry groups S_j, and fundamental domains d_j) followed by part assembly (encoding each part to a latent w_j, sampling the assembler T_j, and applying T_j to reconstruct the full point cloud).

## Results

On ShapeNetPart (airplane, car, chair), the Quartet achieves 1-NNA scores of 63.3/59.7 (airplane), 50.1/51.8 (car), and 51.6/53.7 (chair) for CD/EMD, closely matching or exceeding the best non-part baselines (DiT-3D, FrePolad, LION). More strikingly, the Quartet's SDI-CD scores of 25.7, 25.7, and 28.9 are orders of magnitude lower than all baselines (which range from ~100 to ~7000+), demonstrating guaranteed symmetry enforcement. The training set SDI-CD reference values are 0.954, 7.49, and 5.56, confirming the Quartet's symmetry is near-ideal. The Quartet is the only method supporting both part awareness and symmetry awareness simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Quartet dominates on the symmetry metric (SDI) by orders of magnitude over all 13 baselines: its SDI-CD values (25.7–28.9) are roughly 4–10× lower than the best non-part methods (LION at 97.2–201, DiT-3D at 105–235, FrePolad at 94.1–252) and 20–300× lower than part-aware methods without symmetry (SPAGHETTI at 581–5930, SALAD at 198–308). On 1-NNA (distribution quality), the Quartet is competitive with the strongest baselines: it achieves the best 1-NNA-CD on car (50.1 vs. FrePolad 52.4, DiT-3D 52.7) and chair (51.6 vs. FrePolad 51.9, DiT-3D 52.5), and is within 1 point of DiT-3D (64.7) and FrePolad (65.3) on airplane (63.3). Part-aware baselines SPAGHETTI and SALAD perform worse than most non-part methods on 1-NNA (e.g., SPAGHETTI airplane 1-NNA-CD = 78.2 vs. LION 67.4), suggesting their part decomposition without symmetry enforcement introduces additional generation difficulty. Older flow/GAN-based methods (PointFlow, ShapeGF, DPF-Net, SetVAE, r-GAN, SoftFlow) are consistently outperformed on both 1-NNA and SDI, with SDI-CD values exceeding 300 in most cases. The ablation (Table 3) further confirms that removing any of the four diffusion components degrades performance, with the most dramatic drops when the shape latent diffusion is replaced by a Gaussian prior (Trio Var. 1: 1-NNA-CD jumps to 85.1 on airplane) or when symmetry diffusion is removed (Trio Var. 2: SDI-CD rises from 25.7 to 95 on airplane).

## Relation to Prior Work

The Quartet builds on the denoising diffusion probabilistic model framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the SVAE + latent diffusion paradigm from FrePolad (Zhou et al., 2024), while adopting the transformer-based part diffusion architecture from DiT-3D (Mo et al., 2023) and the Riemannian Langevin dynamics formulation for symmetry from Je et al. (2024). Its key departure from prior work is the explicit, simultaneous modeling of both part composition and symmetry as first-class generative components: part-based methods like SPAGHETTI and SALAD model parts but ignore symmetry, while PAGENet promotes symmetry via an MSE loss without guaranteeing it. The paper positions itself as the first framework to jointly enforce both structural priors through a coordinated four-diffusion pipeline, rather than treating shape generation as a holistic or part-only process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By guaranteeing symmetry through construction (generating only fundamental domains and applying the learned symmetry group), the Quartet eliminates a persistent failure mode in 3D generation—broken or inconsistent symmetries—without relying on post-hoc symmetrization or soft losses. The disentangled architecture enables targeted part-level manipulation while preserving global coherence, which is directly useful for interactive design and editing applications. However, the work is limited to unconditional generation on three ShapeNetPart categories, symmetry is restricted to groups generated by at most two transformations (three reflections), and the 1-NNA improvement over the strongest baselines (DiT-3D, FrePolad) is modest. Open questions include whether the framework scales to conditional generation, more complex symmetry groups (e.g., full dihedral or cyclic), and broader object categories.
