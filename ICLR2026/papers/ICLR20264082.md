## Problem

Quantized neural networks (QNNs) are widely deployed on edge devices, and while quantization distorts gradient landscapes to weaken pixel-level attacks, it offers little protection against adversarial patches—localized, high-saliency perturbations that remain highly transferable across bit-widths (e.g., >73% ASR on 2-bit models). Existing defenses such as Patch-Based Adversarial Training (PBAT) and Double-Win Quantization (DWQ) overfit to specific quantization settings and fail to generalize to unseen patch configurations or novel bit-widths, leaving a critical cross-bit vulnerability unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet) and outputs class labels via a single forward pass under the deployed bit-width at inference, incurring no runtime overhead. During training, inputs are augmented with adversarial patches (e.g., 6×6 on CIFAR-10, 50×50 on ImageNet) applied via a binary mask, and the shared backbone is evaluated simultaneously through multiple quantizers (32/8/5/4/2-bit) to compute cross-bit perceptual penalties. The clean/adversarial mix ratio is 0.5, meaning half of each mini-batch is patched to keep the perceptual regularizers attack-focused without over-regularizing clean representations.

## Method

TriQDef is a training-time defense built on a single shared backbone θ paired with multiple bit-width-specific quantizers Q_b, jointly optimized under a Bit-Width-Aware Curriculum Training (BACT) schedule that progressively activates lower-bit quantizers from higher precision. The core novelty is two perceptual disalignment regularizers: (1) a Feature Disalignment Penalty (FDP) that penalizes structural (SoftDice over Sobel edge maps) and textural (cosine over differentiable HOG descriptors) similarity in intermediate feature maps across bit-widths, and (2) a Gradient Perceptual Dissonance Penalty (GPDP) that applies the same edge- and HOG-based metrics to input gradients, targeting the hidden perceptual alignment that persists even when raw cosine similarity is low. The total loss combines clean cross-entropy, adversarial cross-entropy, and both perceptual penalties (λ_FDP=0.8, λ_GPDP=0.5), with the key insight that disrupting both semantic feature alignment and gradient-level perceptual consensus is necessary to break cross-bit patch transferability—something neither quantization alone nor standard adversarial training achieves.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-56), TriQDef reduces LAVAN attack success rate to 26.2% at 2-bit (seen patches), compared to 39.7% for PBAT and 76.4% for DWQ. Under unseen patch configurations, TriQDef maintains 27.3% ASR at 2-bit while PBAT degrades to 65.3% and DWQ to 76.3%. On ImageNet (ResNet-34), TriQDef achieves 28.5% ASR under LAVAN at 2-bit (seen) and 30.7% (unseen), versus 37.1% and 64.6% for PBAT respectively. Clean accuracy is preserved within ~2% of standard QAT across all bit-widths on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriQDef substantially outperforms PBAT (Rao et al., 2020) across all settings: on CIFAR-10 at 2-bit, TriQDef achieves 26.2% ASR under LAVAN (seen) versus 39.7% for PBAT, and the gap widens dramatically on unseen patches (27.3% vs. 65.3%), confirming PBAT's failure to generalize across bit-widths. Against DWQ (Fu et al., 2021), TriQDef's advantage is even more pronounced, reducing ASR by over 50% (26.2% vs. 76.4% on CIFAR-10 2-bit LAVAN seen), as DWQ's random-precision strategy does not address structured patch transferability. Among inference-time preprocessing defenses, TriQDef (a training-time method with zero inference overhead) achieves 65.8% robust accuracy on ImageNet at 2-bit under LAVAN, compared to 23.4% for JEDI (Tarchoun et al., 2023) and 19.6% for DiffPure (Nie et al., 2022), which also incur 5.58–17.14 s per image and >7 GB GPU memory at inference. TriQDef thus dominates all four baselines on both seen and unseen patch configurations across CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, with the largest margins on unseen patches where PBAT degrades by >20% while TriQDef degrades by only ~1–2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

TriQDef builds on the QAT/STE quantization framework (Esser et al., 2020) and repurposes perceptual metrics from classical computer vision—SoftDice (Sudre et al., 2017) and differentiable HOG descriptors (Kachouane et al., 2012)—as training regularizers to enforce cross-bit-width feature and gradient disalignment. It departs from PBAT (Rao et al., 2020), which overfits to seen patch configurations, and from inference-time preprocessing defenses (JEDI, DiffPure) that are incompatible with integer-only edge deployment. Conceptually, the paper reframes the patch-transferability problem as one of perceptual alignment disruption rather than adversarial augmentation, drawing on contrastive representation learning (Wang & Isola, 2020) and ensemble diversity (Fort et al., 2019) to justify its design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that adversarial patches retain >73% ASR even at 2-bit quantization challenges the widely held assumption that quantization noise inherently disrupts adversarial transferability, and the observation that HOG cosine similarity (~0.81) remains high while gradient cosine similarity is low reveals a hidden structural alignment invisible to standard metrics. For edge-AI practitioners, TriQDef offers a training-time-only defense with zero inference overhead, making it deployable in the resource-constrained settings where QNNs are actually used. Open questions include whether the perceptual-disalignment strategy generalizes to other structured attacks (e.g., physical 3D patches, backdoor triggers), how it scales beyond five bit-widths, and whether the shared-backbone constraint limits applicability to heterogeneous model families.
