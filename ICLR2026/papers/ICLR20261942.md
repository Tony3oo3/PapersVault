## Problem

The existing theoretical guarantees for FedExProx, a distributed proximal method with extrapolation, are no better than those of vanilla Gradient Descent on quadratic optimization tasks, raising the question of whether the proximal oracle provides any provable benefit. This paper identifies that the pessimism stems from a loose analytical step (Lemma A.9) that introduces an unnecessary L_max dependency, and develops a tighter analysis framework that reveals FedExProx can in fact outperform GD.

## Input / Output

The method operates on n local convex functions f_i (quadratic or PL-satisfying) with a shared minimizer set under the interpolation regime, taking as hyperparameters a step size γ > 0 and an extrapolation parameter α (constant or adaptive). The output is a solution x̄ achieving f(x̄) − f(x_*) ≤ ε in O(L_γ/μ_γ⁺ log 1/ε) iterations, where L_γ and μ_γ⁺ are the smoothness and PL (or smallest non-zero eigenvalue) constants of the averaged Moreau envelope. Unusually, the total time complexity depends on the ratio of communication time η to per-iteration computation time τ, and the optimal γ shifts from near-zero (reducing to GD) to a positive value as η/τ grows, yielding a U-shaped time-complexity curve.

## Method

The key insight is to prove convergence in distance (‖x_K − Π(x_K)‖²) rather than in function values, then translate to function-value guarantees via L-smoothness of f. This avoids the critical Lemma A.9 (M^γ(x) − M^γ(x_*) ≥ (1/(1+γL_max))(f(x)−f(x_*))) that forced the previous analysis to carry an L_max factor, making FedExProx appear no better than GD. By exploiting the explicit eigendecomposition of the Hessian M = (1/n)Σ(1/γ)(I−(γA_i+I)^{−1}) for quadratic problems, the paper derives a linear rate O(L_γ/μ_γ⁺ log 1/ε) and shows the total time T_η(γ) = Õ((η+τ(γL_max+1))·L_γ/μ_γ⁺) is minimized at a positive γ whenever η/τ ≥ 2, proving T_η(γ) ≤ T_GD with strict improvement when communication dominates. The framework extends to partial participation via nice sampling, two adaptive extrapolation strategies (gradient-diversity GraDS and Polyak-stepsizes StoPS), and general PL functions, all under weaker assumptions than the strong convexity required by the prior analysis.

## Results

The paper establishes a linear convergence rate of O(L_γ/μ_γ⁺ log 1/ε) iterations for FedExProx on non-strongly convex quadratic problems (Theorem 4.1), replacing the previous sublinear bound O(L_γ(1+γL_max)R²/ε) that was no better than vanilla GD. Incorporating both computation (τ) and communication (η) costs, the total time complexity satisfies T_η(γ) ≤ T_GD for all γ, with strict improvement whenever communication time dominates (η > τ) (Theorem 4.3). In the diagonal-matrix example, choosing γ = 1/min(a_ij) yields L_γ/μ_γ⁺ ≤ 2, making FedExProx at least (max_j Σ_i a_ij)/(min_j Σ_i a_ij) times faster than GD in wall-clock time. The analysis extends to the PL condition (Theorem 7.2) and partial participation (Theorem 7.5), maintaining the same O(L_{γ,S}/μ_γ⁺ log 1/ε) rate under weaker assumptions than the strong convexity required by Li et al. (2024).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is vanilla Gradient Descent (GD), which requires O(L/μ⁺ log 1/ε) iterations and total time Õ((η+τ)·L/μ⁺). The paper's central finding is that the original FedExProx analysis by Li et al. (2024) yields a time complexity T(γ) ≥ π(0)·LR²/ε for all γ > 0, meaning it provably cannot beat GD on quadratics (Theorem 3.2). The new analysis overturns this: FedExProx with the tighter O(L_γ/μ_γ⁺ log 1/ε) rate is never worse than GD in total time and is strictly better when η > τ (Theorem 4.3). In the concrete diagonal-matrix example, GD's time scales as Õ(η·L/μ⁺) while FedExProx's scales as Õ(η + τ·max(a_ij)/min(a_ij)), giving a multiplicative speedup of at least (max_j Σ_i a_ij)/(min_j Σ_i a_ij) when communication is slow. The adaptive variants (FedExProx-GraDS and FedExProx-StoPS) match the constant-extrapolation rate without requiring prior knowledge of the optimal α, at the cost of needing the minimum of the average Moreau envelope.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits FedExProx (Li et al., 2024), an extrapolated variant of FedProx (Li et al., 2020) that uses the Moreau envelope reformulation to express proximal updates as gradient steps on smoothed objectives. The key departure is a discovery that the original convergence guarantees, which depend on L_max (the maximum local smoothness constant), are no better than vanilla GD on quadratic problems. The authors circumvent this by analyzing convergence in terms of distances to the solution set rather than function values, thereby avoiding the Lemma A.9 inequality that introduced the L_max dependency. They also incorporate adaptive extrapolation strategies (GraDS, StoPS) originally from Horvath et al. (2022) and extend the analysis to the Polyak-Lojasiewicz condition, which is weaker than the strong convexity assumed in the prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that FedExProx's original guarantees were no better than GD is a significant correction to the theoretical understanding of extrapolation in federated learning, and the new analysis provides the first rigorous proof that the method can provably outperform GD when communication dominates computation—a regime typical in FL. However, the analysis is currently limited to quadratic objectives and PL functions, leaving open the question of whether similar tighter guarantees hold for general non-convex or non-PL objectives. The practical relevance is further tempered by the fact that the optimal γ depends on problem-specific eigenvalue quantities that may be difficult to estimate in real federated settings.
