## Problem

Existing spatial intelligence benchmarks for MLLMs probe only single-image spatial relations, failing to assess the multi-image spatial reasoning that real-world embodied deployments demand—tracking object and ego motion across views, relating entities that never co-occur in a single frame. Without a dedicated benchmark requiring such cross-image reasoning, the community cannot reliably measure or improve an MLLM's true spatial competence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265325/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark item presents 2–10 real-world RGB images (average 2.55, sourced from indoor 3D scans, autonomous driving, robotics, and egocentric video) alongside a free-form, four-option multiple-choice question that is explicitly designed to be unanswerable from any single image alone. The expected output is a single letter (A–D); every item also ships with a human-annotated step-by-step reasoning chain (average 253 words) used for correctness verification and automated error diagnosis. Questions span a taxonomy of ten atomic spatial tasks (positional, attribute, motion) plus a multi-step reasoning category involving 3–11 chained reasoning steps.

## Method

MMSI-Bench is constructed entirely by six 3D-vision researchers over 300+ hours: they inspect 120,000+ candidate images, hand-select sets exhibiting non-trivial cross-image spatial relations, and author novel, unambiguous questions with carefully crafted distractors—no templates are used. Each question is paired with a detailed reasoning process, and three independent reviewers audit every item for ambiguity, correctness, and non-triviality. For evaluation, 37 open-source and proprietary MLLMs are run at temperature 0 with a 2,048-token cap, scored by exact-match on the extracted letter. A key additional contribution is an automated error-analysis pipeline that contrasts a model's generated reasoning against the human-annotated reference (structured into four reasoning-type labels) to classify failures into grounding, overlap-matching/scene-reconstruction, situation-transformation, and spatial-logic errors at scale.

## Results

On MMSI-Bench (1,000 multi-image spatial reasoning questions), the best proprietary model GPT-5 achieves 41.9% average accuracy, while the best open-source model Qwen2.5-VL-72B reaches 30.7%. Human performance is 97.2%, yielding a 55.3% human-AI gap—the largest among existing spatial intelligence benchmarks. Random guessing scores 25.0% and Blind GPT-4o (no image input) scores only 22.7%, confirming that the tasks require genuine visual-spatial reasoning rather than language priors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 (41.9%) leads all proprietary models, outperforming o3 (41.0%) and GPT-4.5 (40.3%) by roughly 1–2 points, while Gemini-2.5-Pro (36.9%) and dots.vlm (34.1%) trail further behind. The best open-source model, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (30.7%), lags GPT-5 by 11.2 points, and even NVILA-15B (30.5%)—which surpasses most 70B+ open-source models—remains well below proprietary leaders. Scaling within a family yields diminishing returns: Qwen2.5-VL-72B improves only 3.0 points over Qwen2.5-VL-32B (27.7%), and InternVL3-78B gains just 1.5 points over InternVL3-1B (27.0%). Spatial-specific fine-tuning (Spatial-MLLM-3B, InternSpatial, RoboBrain2.0-32B) provides at most ~1.2 points over their base models, indicating that template-based spatial training data does not transfer to MMSI-Bench's diverse, human-curated questions. Prompting techniques (zero-shot CoT, visual correspondence lines via PATS) produce negligible or negative effects across four tested models, suggesting that architectural or training-data advances—rather than inference-time tricks—are needed to close the 55-point gap to human performance (97.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of multi-image VQA and spatial reasoning benchmarks. Prior spatial benchmarks (VSR, SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, VSI-Bench) focus on single-image understanding or rely on template/rule-based question generation, while multi-image VQA suites (BLINK, MuriBench, ReMI) treat spatial tasks as minor sub-splits. MMSI-Bench departs from both lines by being the first dedicated, fully human-curated multi-image spatial reasoning benchmark with a comprehensive taxonomy (10 atomic tasks + multi-step reasoning), stepwise reasoning annotations, and an automated error-analysis pipeline. The closest analogue, ERQA, is also human-curated but is smaller (400 samples, only 113 multi-image) and less spatially focused.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 55.3% human-AI gap (GPT-5 at 41.9% vs. humans at 97.2%) is the largest reported among spatial intelligence benchmarks, establishing a clear north star for progress. The finding that scaling model size yields only marginal gains (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B over 32B: +3%) and that both linguistic (CoT) and visual (PATS correspondence) prompting provide little or negative benefit suggests the bottleneck lies in training data quality/diversity or architecture rather than test-time tricks or parameter count. The automated error-analysis pipeline, which leverages the annotated reasoning chains to diagnose four failure modes at scale, offers a reusable diagnostic tool for the community. Open questions include whether domain-specific spatial data (e.g., first-person motion, 3D scene graphs) can close the gap, and whether the benchmark's difficulty will saturate as models improve.
