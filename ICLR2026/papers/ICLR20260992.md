## Problem

Existing process reward models (PRMs) provide step-level supervision for test-time scaling but fail on tabular reasoning: they cannot verify whether an LRM retrieves the correct sub-table region (table retrieval) or correctly reasons over previously retrieved table contents at a distance (schema interaction). This causes PRM-guided Best-of-N to saturate at N≥8 on table tasks, leaving a critical gap in reliable step-level verification for structured data reasoning.

## Input / Output

TATTOO takes as input a semi-structured table T=(H,R), a natural language query q, and a full LRM reasoning trajectory τ={a₁,…,a_L} containing both inner-thinking steps and tool-integrated table operations. For each step it outputs a binary reward (±1) decomposed into a table-grounded component r_{i,tab} (for retrieval/schema-interaction steps) and an inner-reasoning component r_{i,rea} (for pure logic steps), along with a verification rationale that explicitly invokes computation tools (Python/SQL snippets) and table-lookup tools (Polars DataFrame APIs, CSV/Excel readers) to ground its judgment in executed results rather than free-form text.

## Method

TATTOO is an 8B generative PRM (Qwen-3-8B backbone) trained in two stages. First, a 60k-instance data curation pipeline collects expert LRM trajectories, assigns step-level rewards via LLM-as-a-judge with table-prefix augmentation for schema-interaction steps, and synthesizes tool-call/execution/feedback sequences into the verification rationales. Second, a dual-stage training paradigm applies SFT to learn tool-integrated verification patterns, followed by RL via a modified GRPO where the per-step reward signal s_i combines three terms: label-matching (correctness of predicted reward), confidence calibration (negative log-likelihood of the ground-truth label), and tool-grounding (whether the rationale correctly incorporates tool outputs). A theoretical policy-improvement lower bound (Theorem 4.1) shows that the decomposable reward design additively contributes to policy improvement through both variance (distinguishability) and alignment terms for each reward component, with the covariance term vanishing under the mutual-exclusivity constraint between table and reasoning rewards.

## Results

TATTOO (8B) achieves 78.1% on TB-NR, 82.0% on TB-FC, 34.3% on TB-DA, 74.9% on WTQ, and 30.5% on MMQA under Best-of-32, consistently outperforming all baselines across all five tabular reasoning tasks. Notably, TATTOO continues to scale with increasing N (e.g., TB-NR: 74.2% at N=8 → 78.1% at N=32), whereas Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B saturates after N=16 (74.9% → 75.3%). Overall, incorporating TATTOO improves the downstream policy LRM (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B) by 30.9% at inference, and the 8B model surpasses the 72B Qwen2.5-Math-PRM with up to 9× parameter efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TATTOO (8B) outperforms all eight baselines across all five tasks at N=32. Against the strongest PRM baseline Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B (72B), TATTOO gains +2.8 pts on TB-NR (78.1 vs. 75.3), +2.2 pts on TB-FC (82.0 vs. 79.8), +1.9 pts on TB-DA (34.3 vs. 32.4), +2.3 pts on WTQ (74.9 vs. 72.6), and +1.9 pts on MMQA (30.5 vs. 28.6), achieving these gains with only 8B parameters (9× fewer). Against GenPRM-32B, TATTOO leads by +3.9 pts on TB-NR, +2.6 pts on TB-FC, +3.6 pts on TB-DA, +1.8 pts on WTQ, and +4.1 pts on MMQA. TATTOO also substantially outperforms the 7B discriminative PRMs (Skywork-PRM, Math-Shepherd-PRM, Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B) by 5–8 points on most tasks, and notably surpasses the 14B generative ThinkPRM by 4.3 pts on TB-NR and 11.5 pts on TB-DA. A key differentiator is that TATTOO continues to improve as N grows (e.g., TB-NR: 71.2→74.2→76.4→78.1 for N=4→8→16→32), while Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B plateaus after N=16, indicating TATTOO's table-grounded rewards provide more discriminative supervision at higher compute budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the process reward model (PRM) literature—established for math and science reasoning (Math-Shepherd, Skywork-PRM, Qwen2.5-Math-PRM, ThinkPRM, GenPRM)—to the tabular reasoning domain, where no dedicated step-level verifier previously existed. It builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for the RL stage but departs from prior generative PRMs by (i) decomposing step rewards into table-grounded and inner-reasoning components, (ii) integrating external tools (code execution, table lookup) into the verification process, and (iii) introducing a tool-grounded reward shaping scheme during RL that has no direct analogue in the math-PRM literature. The tool-augmented verification design draws on recent agentic RL work (Feng et al., 2025a; Qian et al., 2025) and the table-prefix insight from the paper's own diagnostic analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TATTOO addresses a concrete and previously unaddressed gap: existing PRMs are demonstrably blind to table retrieval correctness and schema interaction errors, which account for over 80% of LRM failures on tabular tasks. The 9× parameter efficiency (8B vs. 72B) and the observation that TATTOO continues to scale with N while prior PRMs saturate suggest that domain-specific reward decomposition, not model scale, is the key lever for structured-data verification. Open questions include whether the tool-integration paradigm generalizes to other structured modalities (graphs, code repositories), whether the 60k curated dataset is sufficient for truly diverse table schemas, and whether the mutual-exclusivity assumption in the theoretical analysis holds in practice for multi-step table operations that blend retrieval and reasoning.
