## Problem

Pruning vision generative models (diffusion and flow matching) typically requires extensive retraining—up to 40,000 GPU hours for SD2—to recover quality after weight removal, because prior methods use coarse heuristics or per-step losses that ignore the Markovian cascade of errors across denoising steps. This paper asks whether a differentiable mask can be learned end-to-end over the full generation trajectory with minimal compute, eliminating the need for costly retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264609/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained generative model (SDXL U-Net or FLUX DiT), 100 text prompts from GCC3M, and a random noise latent z_T ~ N(0,I), and produces a binary structural mask M over attention heads and FFN neurons. The mask learning phase requires only 50 optimization steps and 10 A100 GPU hours. At inference, the pruned model takes a text prompt and generates a 512×512 or 1024×1024 RGB image, with the mask physically removing neurons so that no special hardware support is needed.

## Method

EcoDiff formulates an end-to-end pruning objective that minimizes the L2 distance between the final denoised latent z_0 of the unmasked backbone and the masked backbone over the entire T-step denoising trajectory, plus an L0 sparsity regularizer. The discrete mask is relaxed via hard-concrete sampling (Louizos et al., 2018), making it differentiable with respect to a continuous control variable λ. To handle the O(T) memory cost of backpropagating through all denoising steps, the paper introduces time-step gradient checkpointing: only the denoised latent at each step is stored during the forward pass, and intermediate activations are recomputed during backpropagation, reducing VRAM from ~1400 GB to under 30 GB for SDXL with a single extra forward pass. Structural pruning is applied at attention-head granularity in MHA and at individual FFN neurons after the activation, preserving input/output dimensions for easy deployment. An optional post-pruning adaptation stage (LoRA or full fine-tuning for 10k steps) further recovers quality at higher sparsity levels.

## Results

At 20% sparsity, EcoDiff prunes SDXL (2.6B U-Net) to achieve FID 32.19 and CLIP 0.33 on MS COCO, and FID 40.91 and CLIP 0.35 on Flickr30K, with only a 1.76 FID increase over the unpruned model (27.43). On FLUX-Dev (11.9B DiT), EcoDiff reaches FID 30.81 (MS COCO) and FID 42.58 (Flickr30K), and on the step-distilled FLUX-Schnell it achieves FID 31.76 and FID 43.25 respectively. All results are obtained with only 100 calibration prompts and 10 A100 GPU hours of mask learning (50 optimization steps), yielding a 1.25× inference speedup at 20% sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EcoDiff substantially outperforms DiffPruning (Fang et al., 2023) across all three models: on SDXL it reduces MS COCO FID from 83.81 to 32.19 (a 2.6× improvement), on FLUX-Dev from 40.84 to 30.81, and on FLUX-Schnell from 42.36 to 31.76, while also matching or exceeding the original model's CLIP score in most cases. Against BK-SDM (Kim et al., 2024), which is limited to U-Net architectures, EcoDiff achieves FID 32.19 vs. 42.87 on MS COCO and CLIP 0.33 vs. 0.30, a clear win in both semantic fidelity and image quality. The per-step loss variant (same mask-learning setup but with per-step reconstruction instead of end-to-end) degrades severely (FID 97.36 on SDXL, 50.56 on FLUX-Dev), confirming the necessity of the end-to-end objective. The only method that approaches EcoDiff's quality is FLUX-Lite (Verdú, 2024), which achieves FID 29.36 at 33% sparsity on FLUX-Dev, but at a cost of 1120 H200 GPU hours—over 100× the compute of EcoDiff's 10 A100 hours—making EcoDiff the far more practical option for resource-constrained settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

EcoDiff adapts the differentiable L0 masking framework from Louizos et al. (2018) and the LLM-pruning literature (LLM-Pruner, Sheared LLaMA) to vision generative models, where the Markovian, multi-step denoising process makes naive per-step pruning criteria inadequate. It departs from prior diffusion pruning methods (DiffPruning, BK-SDM) that rely on one-shot heuristics or per-step reconstruction losses by formulating an end-to-end objective spanning all denoising steps, and introduces time-step gradient checkpointing as a novel extension of Chen et al.'s (2016) checkpointing to handle the O(T) memory blowup of backpropagating through an entire generation trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: pruning a 12B-parameter FLUX model to 20% sparsity in 10 A100 GPU hours with 100 calibration samples is roughly two orders of magnitude cheaper than the 1120 H200 hours required by FLUX-Lite, making structural compression of frontier generative models accessible to much smaller labs. The time-step gradient checkpointing technique (reducing 1400 GB to <30 GB VRAM) is a general tool that could benefit any iterative generative process requiring end-to-end optimization. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher pruning ratios (>50%) without full retraining, whether it extends to video diffusion models with longer temporal sequences, and whether the end-to-end objective can be combined with other acceleration techniques (quantization, distillation) in a unified compression pipeline.
