## Problem

Existing LLM-based logical reasoning approaches rely on a single reasoning modality (typically natural language) during both training and inference, leaving the complementary strengths of code-based and symbolic reasoning untapped. While prior work has explored modality selection or augmentation at inference time, the training process remains modality-blind, preventing the model from learning cross-paradigm synergy. The paper asks whether LLMs can achieve more robust logical reasoning by explicitly learning to operate across multiple complementary reasoning modalities within a single model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263662/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes first-order logic premises expressed in natural language along with a conclusion to evaluate, and produces a final three-way classification (True/False/Uncertain). At inference, the model generates three separate reasoning traces—one in natural-language CoT, one as Python code (not executed, used as structured logic), and one as a partial truth table—then applies majority voting over the three answers. The truth-table modality is particularly structured: it grounds first-order variables into propositional predicates, constructs a logical chain to prune inconsistent assignments, and outputs only the surviving rows of a partial truth table rather than the full 2^n enumeration.

## Method

Mixture-of-Thought (MoT) equips a single LLM with three complementary reasoning modalities—natural-language CoT, code-based CoT, and a newly introduced truth-table CoT—and jointly trains and infers across all three. Training uses a self-evolving, on-policy procedure: each round, the current model generates rationales in all three modalities, a binary reward filters for correct answers and format validity (modality tags, code structure), and the model is fine-tuned on the merged surviving traces, continuing from the previous round's weights rather than restarting from the base model. The truth-table modality addresses exponential blow-up via a two-step strategy: grounding (instantiating FOL into a finite propositional predicate set) followed by 'reason to prune,' where the LLM's deductive reasoning implicitly eliminates inconsistent rows, reducing the task from exhaustive search to direct generation of the minimal consistent assignment. At inference, the model produces one output per modality (elicited by format tags) and applies majority voting, with ties broken randomly. This design yields up to +11.7pp average accuracy gain over single-modality CoT baselines on FOLIO and ProofWriter, with the largest improvements on high-depth (5–8 step) problems.

## Results

On FOLIO and ProofWriter, MoT training with Single-Thought inference improves base models by up to +11.7pp average accuracy (e.g., Gemma-2-2B-It from 41.1% to 61.9%; Gemma-2-9B-It from 65.4% to 73.2%; Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct from 66.2% to 72.6%). Adding MoT inference (majority voting across NL, Code, and Truth-Table modalities) yields further gains of up to +2.1pp, with the 9B Gemma model reaching 78.9% on FOLIO—matching GPT-4 + Logic-LM—and Qwen3-4B-Instruct achieving 87.6% average. On harder problems (reasoning depth 5–8), MoT inference provides an average +9pp improvement over any single modality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The 9B Gemma MoT model (78.9% FOLIO, 70.7% ProofWriter) matches GPT-4 + Logic-LM on FOLIO (78.9%) but trails on ProofWriter (70.7% vs. 79.7%), and substantially outperforms GPT-4 + vanilla CoT (70.6% / 68.1%). Against neuro-symbolic baselines, MoT (Gemma-2-9B) beats LINC (GPT-4, 72.5% FOLIO) by 6.4pp and HybridMind (GPT-3.5, 76.6% FOLIO) by 2.3pp. However, MoT falls short of the strongest closed-source prompting methods: Symbolic CoT (GPT-4) achieves 83.3% FOLIO / 82.5% ProofWriter, and Logic-of-Thoughts@5 (GPT-4) reaches 88.2% / 72.0%, both exceeding the 9B MoT model. The paper's key claim is that a 9B open-source model with MoT training and inference approaches or matches systems that rely on GPT-4 plus external solvers, while the 4B Qwen3 reasoning model with MoT (87.6% avg) approaches GPT-4 + Logic-of-Thoughts@5 (80.1% avg) on the combined benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-evolving training paradigm of STaR (Zelikman et al., 2022) from single-modality chain-of-thought bootstrapping to joint multi-modality reasoning, introducing a novel truth-table symbolic modality alongside natural language and code. It departs from prior neuro-symbolic approaches (Logic-LM, LINC) that delegate reasoning to external solvers, and from inference-time modality selection methods (HybridMind, Logic-of-Thought) that combine modalities only at test time, by jointly training a single model across all three modalities with on-policy self-evolution. The key conceptual departure is treating modality diversity as a training-time signal rather than an inference-time ensemble, with the truth-table modality specifically designed to address the two dominant NL failure modes (invalid converse and missing branches) identified through error analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a 9B open-source model can match GPT-4 + Logic-LM on FOLIO without external solvers, suggesting that internal multi-modality reasoning can substitute for neuro-symbolic pipelines. The error-driven design of the truth-table modality (targeting the ~66% of NL errors from invalid converse and missing branches) provides a principled recipe for identifying and addressing modality-specific bottlenecks. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to logical reasoning (truth-table modality is domain-specific), the code modality consistently underperforming, and the finding that MoT inference slightly degrades performance on easy problems. Open questions include adaptive modality selection during inference, extension to mathematical and commonsense reasoning, and whether the self-evolving multi-modality loop can be made more sample-efficient.
