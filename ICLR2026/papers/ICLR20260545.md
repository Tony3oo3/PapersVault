## Problem

While finite-dimensional transformers have been shown to perform in-context learning by implementing gradient descent in their forward passes, the analogous theoretical characterization for continuum transformers—which generalize attention to infinite-dimensional function inputs for PDE surrogate modeling—remains absent. This paper provides the first rigorous proof that continuum transformers perform in-context operator learning via gradient descent in an operator RKHS, and that the learned operator is Bayes optimal in the infinite-depth limit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260545/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a context window structured as a 2×(n+1) matrix of functions in a Hilbert space (L²(𝕋²) in experiments): the first row holds n input functions plus one test input, and the second row holds n corresponding output functions with the last entry masked to zero. The prediction for the test input is read from the (2, n+1) entry of the final transformer output. Functions are discretized on an N×N grid for computation, but the theoretical analysis operates directly in the continuous function space without requiring a discretization limit.

## Method

The paper introduces a generalized continuum attention mechanism where the key-query similarity is an operator-valued nonlinearity H: Q^{n+1}×K^{n+1}→(L(V))^{(n+1)×(n+1)}, enabling the attention weights to act as bounded linear operators on the value space rather than scalar coefficients. Under specific parameter choices (identity query/key operators and a scalar-scaled value operator), each transformer layer is proven to execute exactly one step of operator gradient descent in an RKHS induced by an operator-valued kernel, with the proof relying on a generalized representer theorem for Hilbert spaces. In the infinite-depth limit with a constant step size, the in-context predictor converges to the Best Linear Unbiased Predictor, which coincides with the Bayes optimal predictor when data are drawn from a Hilbert-space-valued Gaussian process. The paper further proves via Fréchet-differentiable gradient flow analysis over the space of functionals on a Hilbert space that the gradient-descent-implementing parameters are stationary points of the transformer training objective, explaining why pre-training recovers them.

## Results

This is a theoretical paper whose main contributions are three theorems: (1) continuum transformers implement operator gradient descent in an operator RKHS (Theorem 3.1), (2) the infinite-depth limit of the in-context prediction converges to the Best Linear Unbiased Predictor, which coincides with the Bayes Optimal Predictor under Gaussian data (Proposition 3.3), and (3) the gradient-descent parameters are fixed points of the transformer training objective (Theorem 3.6). Empirically, Figure 1 validates that in-context loss decreases monotonically over layers and converges to the BLUP error across four kernel combinations (Linear×Laplace, Linear×Gaussian, Laplacian×Laplace, Laplacian×Gaussian) over 50 i.i.d. operator draws. Figure 2 extends this to the 2D Poisson equation, where the linear kernel yields the fastest convergence and the BLUP achieves near-perfect accuracy since the Poisson solution operator is linear. Figure 3 confirms that during training, the pairwise Hilbert-Schmidt cosine similarity of key and query operators converges toward 1, validating the fixed-point characterization of Theorem 3.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing architectures or methods; its empirical section validates its own theoretical predictions rather than comparing against baselines. The primary 'comparison' is between the continuum transformer's in-context prediction and the analytically computed BLUP (Equation 15), with the transformer converging to the BLUP error as depth increases when the attention kernel matches the data-generating kernel. Across the four scalar kernel choices for k_x (Linear, Laplacian, Gradient RBF, Energy), the Linear kernel consistently achieves the lowest in-context loss in the Poisson equation setting (Figure 2), while all kernels show the expected convergence behavior in the synthetic Gaussian setting (Figure 1). The training experiments (Figure 3) compare the learned operator structure against the theoretical fixed-point form W_{q,ℓ} = b_ℓ Σ^{-1/2}, W_{k,ℓ} = c_ℓ Σ^{-1/2}, finding that pairwise cosine similarity converges to 1 across all tested kernels, including those that do not strictly satisfy Assumption G.4, suggesting the result is robust to this assumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the finite-dimensional ICL theory of Cheng et al. (2023), which showed that standard transformers implement functional gradient descent in an RKHS, to the infinite-dimensional operator setting of continuum transformers (Calvello et al., 2024). The key mathematical departure is the use of operator-valued kernels and operator RKHSs (building on Kadri et al., 2016 and Stepaniants, 2023), requiring a generalized representer theorem and gradient flow analysis over functionals on Hilbert spaces rather than finite-dimensional matrices. While ICONs (Cao et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2023) empirically demonstrated in-context operator learning, this is the first theoretical characterization of the mechanism, and the authors explicitly note that the operator-space gradient descent construction is not a direct lift of the finite-dimensional proof.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a reusable mathematical framework for analyzing ICL in infinite-dimensional settings, which the authors argue will allow optimization theorists to contribute to operator ICL theory without needing deep PDE expertise. A practical implication is that estimating the operator-valued kernel κ for specific PDE meta-learning tasks could directly parameterize the attention nonlinearity for optimal in-context performance. Open questions include systematic selection of the optimal κ in settings where the data-generating RKHS is unknown (the Poisson equation experiment suggests the linear kernel works well but without a principled selection rule), and whether the convergence result in Theorem 3.6 can be made independent of layer index. The framework is currently validated on relatively simple PDEs (Poisson equation) and synthetic Gaussian random field operators, leaving open whether it extends to nonlinear or high-dimensional PDE systems.
