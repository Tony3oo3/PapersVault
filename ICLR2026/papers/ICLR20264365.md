## Problem

Existing scaling laws for language model pretraining (Chinchilla, Kaplan) characterize loss as a function of model size and data volume only, remaining agnostic to data quality. While practitioners know that cleaner data yields better models, no principled quantitative law formalizes how data quality interacts with scale to determine pretraining loss, leaving a gap between intuition and the predictive frameworks that guide large-scale training decisions.

## Input / Output

The scaling law takes three inputs: model parameter count N, training token count D, and a dimensionless data-quality scalar Q ∈ (0,1] (where Q=1 is fully clean data). Q is estimated either as 1 minus the corruption rate or as exp(−Δ) where Δ is an additive data-deficiency score. The output is a predicted cross-entropy pretraining loss L, along with fitted parameters (B, β, γ, E) that quantify how loss decomposes across model capacity, data volume, and quality. Experiments span GPT Neo (~133M params) on Paracrawl v8 (0.5M–2M sentence pairs) and Llama 3 (~50M params) on C4 (0.1B–10B tokens), each across 7 quality levels from Q=1.0 down to Q=0.5.

## Method

The paper proposes the quality-aware scaling law L(N,D,Q) = A/N^α + B/(D^β Q^γ) + E, extending the Chinchilla form by inserting Q multiplicatively into the data-volume term. The theoretical justification proceeds via two routes: (i) an effective-sample-size argument showing that noisy data reduces usable samples to D_eff = D·Q^γ (with γ≈1 for Gaussian SNR noise and γ≈2 for symmetric label noise), and (ii) an information-theoretic derivation where corruption reduces mutual information I(X̃;Z) = ρ(Q)·I(X;Z) multiplicatively, recovering the same power-law form under regular variation of ρ near Q=1. Two practical estimators for Q are provided: a corruption-rate proxy (Q = 1 − CR) and a general deficiency measure (Q = exp(−Δ)) with additivity and continuity axioms. Empirically, the authors inject synthetic noise (token padding for NMT, token swapping for CLM) at controlled rates across 63 training runs per task, then fit the law via Huber-robust regression. A key empirical finding is that fitted γ is well below 1 (≈0.17 for NMT, ≈0.40 for CLM), indicating sublinear loss degradation and greater robustness to moderate corruption than classical PAC or channel-capacity bounds predict, likely due to redundancy in natural language.

## Results

The quality-aware scaling law L(N,D,Q) = A/N^α + B/(D^β Q^γ) + E is fitted to 63 experimental runs per task (3 dataset sizes × 7 quality levels × 3 replicates). For NMT (GPT Neo, ~133M params), the Huber-estimated quality exponent is γ̂ ≈ 0.173 with β̂ ≈ 0.250; for CLM (Llama 3, 8L, hidden 512), γ̂ ≈ 0.401 with β̂ ≈ 0.396. Both exponents are well below 1, indicating sublinear decay of effective data with quality and robustness to moderate corruption. Out-of-sample validation on unseen CLM data yields γ̂ ≈ 0.337 and β̂ ≈ 0.391, confirming the law's predictive form generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not conduct a head-to-head benchmark against a competing scaling-law method; instead, its primary validation is the out-of-sample generalization of the fitted parameters. On unseen CLM data, the Huber-estimated β remains stable (0.396 → 0.391) while γ shifts modestly (0.401 → 0.337), and the irreducible loss E increases from 3.44 to 4.54, consistent with distribution shift. The paper positions its framework as a unifying generalization: by choosing specific forms for the data deficiency Δ(ω) in Equation 3, it recovers the scaling laws of Chen et al. (2025) (cluster-based density), Chang et al. (2024) (compression-based diversity + syntheticity), and Goyal et al. (2024) (epoch-based saturation), as summarized in Table 1. The key empirical finding is that γ < 1 in both tasks, meaning real language models are more robust to corruption than the γ ≥ 1 predicted by classical PAC-learning or channel-capacity analyses, likely due to redundancy in natural language.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chinchilla scaling law (Hoffmann et al., 2022) and the foundational power-law form of Kaplan et al. (2020) by introducing a dimensionless data-quality parameter Q into the loss prediction, yielding L(N,D,Q) = A/N^α + B/(D^β Q^γ) + E. It builds on the effective-sample-size and information-theoretic literature (PAC learning under noise, Fisher information, channel capacity) to justify the Q^γ term, and unifies several recent quality-aware scaling analyses (Chen et al., 2025; Chang et al., 2024; Goyal et al., 2024) under a single data-deficiency decomposition. The key departure from prior work is that earlier studies either assumed fixed data quality or characterized quality effects empirically without a closed-form, jointly predictive law coupling quality with model size and data volume.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers concrete, actionable guidance for practitioners building domain-specific LLMs (medical, scientific, business) where data is limited and heterogeneous: it quantifies how much model size can be traded away for data curation effort. A notable open question is whether the sublinear quality exponent (γ < 1) observed here persists at the 10B+ parameter scale where redundancy effects may differ, and whether synthetic token-level noise adequately captures the more complex, correlated degradations found in real web corpora. The single-epoch CLM setup and ~133M parameter models also leave the interaction between quality and overtraining underexplored.
