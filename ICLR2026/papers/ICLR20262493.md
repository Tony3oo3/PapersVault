## Problem

Traditional molecular dynamics simulations are limited by femtosecond timesteps and O(N log N) scaling, making microsecond-to-millisecond biomolecular processes computationally intractable. Existing ML alternatives either generate time-agnostic conformational ensembles or model trajectories but fail to capture protein-ligand interactions (e.g., NeuralMD freezes the protein, MDGen excludes small-molecule ligands). No prior all-atom generative model can produce physically plausible, long-timescale protein-ligand trajectories that include both receptor flexibility and ligand unbinding pathways.

## Input / Output

BioMD takes the all-atom Cartesian coordinates of the initial frame (protein + ligand, in ℝ^{N×3}), the amino acid sequence, and ligand atom types as input. It outputs a full all-atom trajectory as a sequence of per-frame coordinate matrices, generated hierarchically: first coarse-grained frames sampled every k=10 steps via forecasting, then intermediate frames via interpolation between anchor frames. The model operates directly on Cartesian coordinates rather than internal coordinates (torsion angles, backbone dihedrals), and supports two prediction modes—relative-to-anchor (BioMD-rel) and absolute (BioMD-abs)—selected by the user depending on whether the goal is exploratory sampling or precise pathway reproduction.

## Method

BioMD decomposes long-trajectory generation into a coarse-grained forecasting stage (predicting every 10th frame) and a fine-grained interpolation stage (filling intermediate frames), both implemented within a single conditional flow matching model. The key mechanism is a 'noise-as-masking' strategy inspired by Diffusion Forcing: each frame is independently perturbed by Gaussian noise at a per-frame time variable τ, where known/conditioning frames are set to τ=1 (clean) and frames to be generated start at τ=0 (pure noise); different tasks (forecasting vs. interpolation) are distinguished solely by which frames receive which τ values. The velocity network uses a local-global-local attention pathway: an AtomAttentionEncoder models local atomic environments, a 24-block FlowTrajectoryTransformer processes the full sequence with both intra-frame spatial attention (AttentionPairBias, adapted from AlphaFold) and inter-frame TemporalAttention, and an AtomAttentionDecoder broadcasts back to per-atom velocity predictions. The initial conformation is encoded by a 6-layer SE(3)-equivariant Graph Transformer producing single and pair representations that condition the flow. Training minimizes MSE on the velocity field plus auxiliary losses for ligand bond lengths, steric clashes, and ligand geometric center; inference uses Euler ODE integration and supports both all-at-once and autoregressive (block size 5) generation strategies to balance error accumulation against path accuracy.

## Results

On the DD-13M ligand unbinding benchmark, BioMD-rel (AR-5) achieves a 97.1% unbinding success rate within 10 attempts, with an unbinding path RMSD of 0.6845 Å, while BioMD-abs (AR-5) reduces path RMSD to 0.5645 Å (vs. 0.4217 Å for metadynamics reference) at 79.4% success. On MISATO, BioMD produces steric clash scores orders of magnitude lower than all competing ML methods (e.g., 0.0006 Prot-Lig vs. 0.0578 for NeuralMD) and achieves the highest protein RMSF correlation (0.685) among all methods. On ATLAS, BioMD-rel attains state-of-the-art performance on 9 of 13 metrics, including a 52% improvement in Global RMSF correlation (r=0.76) over MDGen (r=0.50) and the lowest W2 distance (2.18) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeuralMD (Liu et al., 2024), BioMD dramatically outperforms on physical stability: steric clash scores are ~100× lower (0.0006 vs. 0.0578 Prot-Lig on MISATO), and BioMD achieves a protein RMSF correlation of 0.685 where NeuralMD reports none (protein treated as static). Against metadynamics (Laio & Parrinello, 2002), BioMD-abs (AR-5) achieves a competitive unbinding path RMSD of 0.5645 Å (vs. 0.4217 Å reference) while generating complete paths in under 10 seconds versus ~1 hour for metadynamics, and reaches 97.1% success in 10 attempts. On ATLAS, BioMD-rel surpasses ESMFlow (Jing et al., 2024) by a wide margin on Global RMSF correlation (0.76 vs. 0.31) and W2 distance (2.18 vs. 3.60), and outperforms BioEmu (Lewis et al., 2025) on the same metrics (0.76 vs. 0.57; 2.18 vs. 4.32). BioMD also beats Str2Str (Lu et al., 2024) on Global RMSF (0.76 vs. 0.38) and MDGen (Jing et al., 2024) on all flexibility and distributional metrics (e.g., W2 2.18 vs. 2.69). The closest competitor is EBA (Lu et al., 2025), which BioMD edges out on Global RMSF (0.76 vs. 0.71) and W2 distance (2.18 vs. 2.43), though EBA leads on per-target RMSF correlation (0.90 vs. 0.91 is actually BioMD winning) and exposed residue J (0.70 vs. 0.70, tied).

## Relation to Prior Work

BioMD sits at the intersection of three research threads: flow matching as a generative framework (Lipman et al., 2023), the 'noise-as-masking' strategy from Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., 2024) repurposed for per-frame independent conditioning, and the SE(3)-equivariant graph transformer architecture inherited from AlphaFold 3 (Abramson et al., 2024). It departs from prior trajectory-generation methods (MDGen, NeuralMD, ConfRover) by being the first to model all-atom protein-ligand dynamics jointly rather than treating the protein as static or restricting to protein-only systems. The hierarchical forecasting-then-interpolation decomposition is a novel architectural choice for MD trajectory generation, motivated by the observation that short-timescale conformational changes are small while long-timescale changes are large.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BioMD provides the first practical all-atom generative model for protein-ligand MD trajectories, achieving a 97.1% ligand unbinding success rate and generating complete unbinding paths in seconds versus hours for metadynamics — a speedup that could accelerate early-stage drug discovery screening. The dual rel/abs variants offer a useful practical trade-off between exploratory sampling and pathway-accurate reproduction. Key open questions include generalization to microsecond-to-millisecond timescales, rare events outside the training distribution, and whether the hierarchical decomposition scales to even longer trajectories without further error accumulation in the autoregressive stage.
