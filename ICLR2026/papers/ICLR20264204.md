## Problem

Single-scale flow matching methods generate entire images at full resolution, incurring high computational cost that limits scalability to high resolutions. Existing multi-scale approaches (Cascaded Diffusion, EdifyImage, Pyramidal Flow) require separate networks per resolution, explicit renoising bridges between scales, or operate in pixel space, making them less efficient or harder to scale than single-scale DiT. The paper asks whether a unified model can process all Laplacian scales in parallel without bridging steps while improving both quality and sampling efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264204/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a random Gaussian noise tensor in VAE latent space (downsample factor 8, so a 256×256 image maps to a 32×32 latent) and optionally a class label, and produces a full-resolution RGB image (up to 1024×1024). The noise is decomposed into a 2- or 3-level Laplacian pyramid before feeding the network, and the output is reconstructed by summing the predicted Laplacian residuals with appropriate upsampling. The architecture is flexible: it accepts any subset of scales as input and outputs velocity predictions for exactly those active scales, matching the progressive sampling schedule.

## Method

LapFlow decomposes images into Laplacian pyramid residuals and models them jointly with a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture that uses scale-specific QKV projections and pre/post-attention modulations but computes a single global multi-head self-attention over all active scales. A block causal mask enforces unidirectional information flow from coarse to fine scales, so finer details are conditioned on broader structure without explicit renoising. Training follows a progressive multi-stage schedule where the coarsest scale is trained over the full time interval [0,1], the mid-scale over [T₂,1], and the finest over [T₁,1], using a linear noise schedule with per-scale shifted coupling coefficients. Sampling proceeds in three ODE segments where multiple scales are denoised in parallel within each segment, and a time-weighted complexity analysis shows the effective attention cost is roughly 1.6× lower than a single-scale DiT at the same finest resolution.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ 256×256, LapFlow achieves an FID of 3.53 with 80 NFE and 16.5 GFLOPs, outperforming LFM (FID 5.26, 89 NFE, 22.1 GFLOPs) and Pyramidal Flow (FID 11.20, 90 NFE, 14.2 GFLOPs). The advantage grows at higher resolutions: FID 4.04 at 512×512 and 5.51 at 1024×1024 versus LFM's 6.35 and 8.12 respectively. On ImageNet 256×256 with DiT-XL/2 at 600K steps, LapFlow reaches FID 14.38 (vs. DiT 19.50, LFM 28.37, Pyramidal Flow 17.10), and with 7M steps + CFG=1.5 achieves FID 4.12 versus LFM's 4.46, all with fewer NFE (80 vs. 89–250) and lower inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LapFlow outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest single-scale latent-space baseline LFM (Dao et al., 2023), LapFlow improves FID by 1.73 points at 256×256 (3.53 vs. 5.26), 2.31 at 512×512 (4.04 vs. 6.35), and 2.61 at 1024×1024 (5.51 vs. 8.12), while also reducing NFE from 89–100 to 80–94 and cutting inference time by ~10–20%. Against the multi-scale Pyramidal Flow (Jin et al., 2025), LapFlow achieves a dramatically lower FID (3.53 vs. 11.20 at 256×256; 14.38 vs. 17.10 on ImageNet XL/2) with fewer NFE (80 vs. 90). EdifyImage (Atzmon et al., 2024), which operates in pixel space, is outperformed by a margin of 4.09 FID points at 256×256 (3.53 vs. 7.62) and requires 75% more GFLOPs (28.9 vs. 16.5). The U-Net-based LDM (Rombach et al., 2022) achieves a competitive FID of 5.11 but with a different architecture class; LapFlow still improves by 1.58 FID points. On ImageNet, LapFlow with DiT-XL/2 at 600K steps beats DiT (Peebles and Xie, 2023) by 5.12 FID points (14.38 vs. 19.50) while using 80 NFE versus DiT's 250, and at 7M steps with CFG=1.5 achieves FID 4.12 versus LFM's 4.46.

## Relation to Prior Work

LapFlow sits at the intersection of latent-space flow matching (LFM, Dao et al. 2023), the DiT transformer architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023), and the Mixture-of-Transformers design (Liang et al., 2024), combining them into a unified multi-scale generative model. It departs from prior multi-scale approaches—Cascaded Diffusion, EdifyImage, and Pyramidal Flow—by replacing separate per-scale networks or explicit renoising bridges with a single MoT model that processes all Laplacian pyramid scales in parallel via causal attention, enforcing hierarchical information flow without sequential stage transitions. The Laplacian pyramid decomposition itself is borrowed from classical image processing and prior GAN/diffusion work (LapGAN, EdifyImage), but its integration into a single flow-matching ODE with per-scale time segmentation is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the simultaneous improvement in sample quality and reduction in inference cost (fewer NFE, lower GFLOPs), with the quality gap widening at higher resolutions (5.51 vs 8.12 FID at 1024×1024), suggesting the multi-scale formulation becomes more advantageous as resolution grows. The time-weighted complexity analysis providing a ~1.6× theoretical attention cost reduction offers a principled explanation for the empirical efficiency gains. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to text-to-image or video generation, whether the VAE dependency (EQVAE vs SDVAE) limits ceiling quality, and whether the multi-scale hierarchy continues to help beyond 1024×1024 or requires even more scales. The paper also notes that the XL/2 ImageNet model was not trained for the full 7M steps, leaving its ultimate potential untested.
