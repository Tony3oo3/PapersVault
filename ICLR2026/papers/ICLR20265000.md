## Problem

Existing personalized video generation methods either focus narrowly on single-face identity preservation or enable multi-subject customization without explicitly binding each face to its associated attributes (clothing, accessories, hairstyle). This absence of explicit face-attribute dependency modeling leads to attribute entanglement and face-attribute misalignment across subjects, particularly when captions contain similar subject nouns. The paper addresses this gap at both the data level (no public dataset with annotated face-attribute dependencies) and the model level (no architectural mechanism to enforce intra-group cohesion and inter-group separation).

## Input / Output

The method accepts per-subject face crops, up to three attribute reference images per subject (segmented via SAM), object reference images, a single inpainted background image, and a global text prompt. It generates 480p videos of 81 frames (5 seconds at 16 FPS) using 50 denoising steps with CFG scale 6. Each subject is defined as one face paired with its attributes, and the system supports 1–4 subjects at inference (trained on 1–3), with condition images encoded via a 3D VAE and concatenated with denoising video tokens before feeding into DiT blocks.

## Method

Built on Wan2.1's 1.3B DiT backbone with Flow Matching, Lumos X introduces two relational attention modules. Relational Self-Attention combines R2PE (which assigns shared temporal indices to face-attribute token pairs within each subject group while extending indices sequentially across groups) with a Causal Self-Attention Mask (CSAM) that enforces unidirectional attention from video tokens to condition tokens and treats each face-attribute pair as a unified attention branch. Relational Cross-Attention introduces a Multilevel Cross-Attention Mask (MCAM) that injects a three-level numerical mask (strong/correlation/weak) into the cross-attention logits, using a dynamically scaled factor computed from downsampled QK similarity to reinforce intra-group face-attribute binding and suppress inter-group interference. On the data side, a pipeline leveraging Qwen2.5-VL, SAM, GroundingDINO, and FLUX inpainting extracts explicit face-attribute correspondences from Panda70M, yielding 1.57M training samples and a 500-video benchmark with two evaluation tasks (identity-consistent and subject-consistent generation).

## Results

On the subject-consistent video generation benchmark (500 videos), Lumos X achieves SOTA across all metrics: Dynamic 0.723, ViCLIP-T 0.260, ViCLIP-V 0.932, CLIP-T 0.201, CLIP-I 0.692, DINO-I 0.261, ArcSim 0.454, and CurSim 0.483. For identity-consistent generation on the full 500-video test set, Lumos X scores ArcSim 0.510 and CurSim 0.540, outperforming SkyReels-A2 (0.382/0.401) and matching or slightly exceeding Phantom (0.508/0.536). On the single-face subset (220 videos), Lumos X achieves ArcSim 0.542 and CurSim 0.575, substantially surpassing both ConsisID (0.458/0.474) and Concat-ID (0.467/0.485).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SkyReels-A2 (Wan2.1-14B backbone), Lumos X wins decisively on every subject-consistent metric, with the largest gaps in ArcSim (0.454 vs 0.271, +67% relative) and DINO-I (0.261 vs 0.192, +36%), indicating far superior face-attribute binding. Against Phantom (same Wan2.1-1.3B backbone), Lumos X leads on all subject-consistent metrics (e.g., DINO-I 0.261 vs 0.216, ViCLIP-V 0.932 vs 0.865) and on identity metrics (ArcSim 0.510 vs 0.508), though Phantom edges out by 0.002 on ViCLIP-T prompt following (0.264 vs 0.262). On the single-face identity task, Lumos X outperforms both ConsisID and Concat-ID by 7–16 points on ArcSim/CurSim, confirming that relational modeling generalizes even when only one face is present. Compared to the UNO+Wan2.1-I2V-14B image-personalization pipeline, Lumos X achieves substantially higher identity scores (ArcSim 0.454 vs 0.237) and dynamic quality (0.723 vs 0.547), while the pipeline's larger 14B backbone gives it a marginal 0.001 advantage on ViCLIP-T. Overall, Lumos X is the only method that simultaneously leads on both identity preservation and face-attribute alignment across 1–4 subjects without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Wan2.1 DiT-based text-to-video backbone and extends the multi-subject video personalization line initiated by Phantom, SkyReels-A2, and earlier single-face methods like ConsisID and Concat-ID. The key conceptual departure is the explicit modeling of face-attribute dependencies as a first-class structural constraint: prior multi-subject methods concatenate all reference tokens without distinguishing intra-group (face + its attributes) from inter-group (different subjects) relationships, leading to attribute entanglement. Lumos X introduces three complementary mechanisms—Relational Rotary Position Embedding (R2PE), Causal Self-Attention Mask (CSAM), and Multilevel Cross-Attention Mask (MCAM)—that jointly enforce intra-group cohesion and inter-group separation at both the positional-encoding and attention levels, a design not present in any prior T2V personalization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit face-attribute relational modeling addresses a concrete and frequently observed failure mode (attribute swapping between subjects) that prior multi-subject methods handle only implicitly through text captions, which become ambiguous with repeated subject nouns. The architecture-agnostic design (compatible with any DiT backbone) and minimal computational overhead (~0.3s per step added) make the technique broadly adoptable. However, the approach is currently validated only on a 1.3B model with up to 3 training subjects, and the base model's capacity becomes the bottleneck beyond ~10 subjects; scaling to 14B backbones and incorporating motion-aware relational constraints remain open directions that could substantially broaden applicability.
