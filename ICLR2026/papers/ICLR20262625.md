## Problem

Transformer-based LTSF models achieve high accuracy but require millions of parameters, making them infeasible for edge and embedded deployment. Existing lightweight linear models (e.g., DLinear, FITS, SparseTSF) reduce parameters but still use a monolithic representational strategy that fails to exploit the complementary structural sparsity of time series—local temporal patterns and global spectral patterns have fundamentally different statistical properties and are best modeled in different domains.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multivariate time series window of length L=720 with C channels (ranging from 7 for ETT to 862 for Traffic) and produces forecasted values over horizons of 96, 192, 336, or 720 steps. It employs a channel-independent strategy where all channels share the same parameters, and the input is first zero-mean normalized and downsampled by a factor π (typically 24) before entering the dual-pathway architecture. The final prediction is the additive sum of the time-domain and frequency-domain pathway outputs plus the temporal mean, upsampled to the target horizon.

## Method

MixLinear is a dual-pathway architecture that processes local and global trends in their most natural domains. The segment-based pathway downsamples the input, partitions it into M segments of length r, applies two factorized linear projections (intra-segment for local shape, inter-segment for cross-segment drift), and upsamples to the forecast horizon, reducing complexity from O(n²) to O(n). The frequency-domain pathway applies FFT to the downsampled series, compresses the spectrum through an adaptive rank-n_z low-rank filter (U·(V·F) with n_z=2), reconstructs via iFFT, and upsamples. The two pathways are combined additively, yielding a total of only ~0.1K parameters (45–176 depending on configuration) while achieving O(n log n) time complexity dominated by FFT operations.

## Results

MixLinear achieves competitive LTSF performance with only 0.1K parameters (176 at horizon 720), an 81% reduction over SparseTSF (1K) and 94–98% over FITS (10K). On the Exchange dataset, MixLinear delivers up to 16.2% MSE improvement over SparseTSF (RPD), and 5.3% on ETTh1 at horizon 336. In terms of computational cost, MixLinear achieves the lowest MACs at horizon 720 on most datasets (e.g., 196.56K on ETTh1 vs. 277.20K for SparseTSF and 292.32K for FITS; 24.2M on Traffic vs. 34.14M and 36.00M respectively), with inference speedups of up to 3.2× over SparseTSF and 2.58× over FITS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixLinear consistently achieves the lowest MACs across all eight datasets at horizon 720, outperforming SparseTSF by ~41% and FITS by ~49% in computational cost. In terms of accuracy (MSE), MixLinear clearly wins on Exchange (up to 16.2% RPD improvement over SparseTSF at horizon 96), ETTh1 (5.3% at horizon 336), and ETTh2 (3.7% at horizon 96), while remaining within 0.3–2.4% of SparseTSF on the remaining datasets. Against the heavier Transformer-based baselines (PatchTST, TimesNet, iTransformer, FEDformer), MixLinear matches or beats them on most low-dimensional datasets (ETT, Exchange) while using 4–5 orders of magnitude fewer parameters. On high-dimensional datasets (Electricity, Traffic), MixLinear is slightly behind SparseTSF in MSE (−2.0% and −0.9% RPD) but still outperforms DLinear, FITS, and all Transformer baselines. In inference time, MixLinear is 3.2× faster than SparseTSF and 2.58× faster than FITS on low-dimensional data, and 2.12×/2.58× faster respectively on high-dimensional data, confirming its suitability for edge deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

MixLinear extends the recent line of work showing that simple linear models (DLinear, FITS, SparseTSF) can match or exceed Transformer-based architectures for LTSF, but departs from their single-domain designs by introducing a dual-pathway decomposition: segment-based factorized linear projections in the time domain for local patterns, and adaptive low-rank spectral filtering (rank-2 complex-valued factorization) in the frequency domain for global trends. This is conceptually inspired by classical time series decomposition (STL, Autoformer) and the spectral sparsity exploited by FEDformer, but differs from DeepGate in that both pathways use mathematically linear, parameter-minimal operations rather than heavy neural modules. The additive fusion of the two pathways, combined with channel-independent processing and downsampling, positions MixLinear as the most extreme point on the parameter-efficiency frontier among linear LTSF models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.1K parameter count (as few as 45 parameters) opens a practical path for deploying LTSF on edge sensors, embedded devices, and resource-constrained IoT systems where even SparseTSF's 1K parameters may be prohibitive. However, the accuracy gains over SparseTSF are modest and inconsistent—MixLinear is slightly worse on Electricity and Traffic at horizon 720—so the primary value is efficiency rather than a fundamental accuracy breakthrough. Open questions include whether the spectral sparsity assumption (rank-2 sufficiency) holds for non-stationary or highly irregular series, whether the dual-domain decomposition generalizes to tasks beyond LTSF (e.g., anomaly detection, imputation), and whether the approach scales to very long sequences (beyond 720) without the downsampling factor becoming a bottleneck.
