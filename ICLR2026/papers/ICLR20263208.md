## Problem

Existing cultural awareness benchmarks for MLLMs rely on real-world images where each image typically encodes only a single culture, making them relatively easy for models. They also tend to use a single question per sample and lack multilingual tasks, limiting their ability to probe deeper cultural reasoning and cross-lingual understanding. C³B addresses these gaps by introducing a comic-based medium that can pack multiple cultures into one frame, a three-task progressive difficulty ladder, and five-language translation evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263208/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the two recognition tasks (Extraction@Culture and Conflict@Culture), the input is a single comic page image and the model must output multiple-choice selections for background cultures and culturally representative objects, a binary conflict judgment, and a formatted conflict description (e.g., 'Katana should not be in America'). For Generation@Culture, the input is a comic page image paired with a Japanese source sentence extracted from the panel, and the model produces a line-by-line translation into one of five target languages (English, German, Russian, Spanish, Thai). The benchmark spans 2,220 images (1,023 AI-generated for recognition tasks, 1,197 from Manga109 for translation) and 18,789 QA pairs covering 77 distinct cultures.

## Method

The core design choice is using comics rather than real-world photographs as the evaluation medium, because fictional comic scenes are free from the singular cultural binding of real-life photos and can condense multiple cultures into a single frame, raising evaluation difficulty. Three tasks form a logical chain of increasing complexity: (1) Extraction@Culture tests basic visual recognition of cultures and culturally representative objects via multiple-choice questions; (2) Conflict@Culture requires the model to detect and describe cross-cultural contradictions within the same image, with a composite CACC metric that weights the model's own Q1/Q2 answers; (3) Generation@Culture evaluates multilingual translation conditioned on the comic's cultural context across five Japanese-to-X language pairs. Image construction uses a two-stage Doubao API pipeline (prompt generation specifying cultural conflict scenarios, then image creation) for the recognition tasks, while translation images are manually curated from Manga109. Annotation employs a multi-agent pipeline with DeepSeek-V3 as the base model: a Translator–Reviewer loop for ground-truth translations and an automated conflict-detection stage followed by manual verification for the Conflict@Culture labels.

## Results

On the C³B benchmark, Qwen2.5-VL achieves the best overall performance, scoring 53.7% ACC on Q1 (culture identification) and 34.2% CACC on Q4 (cultural conflict description), outperforming the second-best models (InternLM-XC2.5, Llama3.2, InternVL2 at 46.0% on Q1) by 7.7 points. However, all 11 MLLMs struggle severely on Q4, with raw ACC ranging from 0.00% to only 3.87%, indicating a fundamental gap in cultural conflict comprehension. In the Generation@Culture task, Qwen2.5-VL again leads with 13.2 BLEU on JA-EN and 9.72 BLEU on JA-TH, while MiniGPT-v2 scores near-zero BLEU across most language pairs. Human evaluators achieve 92.0% ACC on easy Q1 questions versus 48.0% for the best MLLM, confirming the benchmark's difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2.5-VL is the strongest model overall, leading Q1 by 7.7 points over InternLM-XC2.5, Llama3.2, and InternVL2 (all at 46.0%), and Q4 CACC by 1.6 points over Llama3.2 (32.6%). Llama3.2 excels specifically on Q2 (59.0% ACC, +72.7% over the average), while SPHINX achieves the best Q3 score (69.2%) for cultural conflict detection, narrowly beating InternLM-XC2.5 and InternVL2 (68.5%). The LLaVA family models (LLaVA1.5-7B, LLaVA-NeXT, LLaVA-OV) consistently underperform on Q4, with LLaVA1.5-7B and LLaVA-NeXT scoring 0.00% ACC due to instruction-following failures and persistent 'Nothing' outputs, respectively. MiniGPT-v2 is the weakest model across all tasks, with 18.0% on Q1 and near-zero BLEU in Generation@Culture. In the multilingual translation task (Table 4), Qwen2.5-VL dominates all five language pairs (e.g., 13.2 BLEU on JA-EN vs. 6.21 for mPLUG-Owl3), while JA-TH is the hardest pair for every model and JA-EN is the easiest, highlighting a persistent low-resource language gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the comic medium for cultural awareness evaluation, departing from prior benchmarks (CVQA, CulturalVQA, GIMMICK, MOSAIC-1.5k) that rely on real-world images where each frame typically encodes a single culture. While comic-based datasets exist (Manga109, eBDtheque, CoMix), they target visual tasks like speaker identification rather than cultural reasoning. C³B synthesizes these two threads—combining the high cultural density of fictional comic scenes with progressive-difficulty multitask evaluation and multilingual generation—creating a benchmark that no prior work simultaneously achieves across all five dimensions (multicultural, multitask, multilingual, cultural-awareness-focused, progressive difficulty).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and quantified gap between MLLMs and humans (e.g., 48% vs. 92% on easy Q1), and exposes that models disproportionately fail on lesser-known cultures (Finnish, Somali) and cultural-conflict reasoning. The finding that directly providing Q1/Q2 answers to Q4 yields only marginal improvement (+0.533 CACC) despite moderate correlations suggests MLLMs do not effectively chain contextual information across related questions—a limitation with broader implications for multi-step reasoning. Open questions include whether the all-Asian annotator panel introduces systematic bias, whether the Doubao-generated images faithfully represent the intended cultural conflicts, and whether the benchmark's difficulty ceiling will be reached as models improve.
