## Problem

Activation steering for LLM safety injects a fixed refusal direction vector into internal activations to induce refusal, but this indiscriminate application causes over-refusal and degraded performance on benign prompts. Existing mitigations—vector calibration and conditional steering—are heuristic and lack theoretical grounding, limiting their robustness across diverse jailbreak attacks. The paper asks: can we design a principled steering mechanism that provably leaves benign activations unchanged while steering malicious ones toward refusal?

## Input / Output

At inference time, AlphaSteer intercepts the d-dimensional activation vector h^(l) at selected middle layers (extracted at the last token position) and applies a learned linear transform to produce the steered activation h'^(l) = h^(l) + λΔ^(l)h^(l). The offline training phase consumes 14,000 benign prompt activations (from Alpaca, Coconot, and MATH) to compute the null-space projection matrix, and 2,000 malicious prompt activations (from AdvBench, MaliciousInstruct, TDC23-RedTeaming, JailbreakBench, WildGuardMix, and JailbreakV-28K) to fit the transformation matrix. The output is a per-layer steering matrix Δ^(l) of size d×d_model that is applied at inference with a scalar strength λ, requiring no gradient updates to the base model.

## Method

AlphaSteer replaces the static refusal vector with a learnable transformation matrix Δ^(l) such that the steering vector s^(l) = Δ^(l)h^(l) is dynamically constructed per input. For utility preservation, Δ is factorized as Δ = Δ̃P̂ where P̂ is a null-space projection matrix computed via SVD of the benign activation covariance H_b H_b^T (using a null-space equivalence lemma to avoid expensive d×N_b decomposition), guaranteeing ΔH_b = 0 so benign activations pass through unchanged. For safety enhancement, Δ̃ is solved in closed form via regularized least squares to minimize ‖Δ̃P̂H_m − R‖ + α‖Δ̃P̂‖, where R stacks the refusal direction vector r (extracted via difference-in-means) across all malicious samples. The net effect is that the L2 norm of the constructed steering vector is near zero for benign prompts but approximates the refusal direction for malicious prompts, achieving selective steering without any per-prompt classifier or threshold.

## Results

AlphaSteer achieves an average Defense Success Rate (DSR) of 91.93% on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, 97.29% on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and 98.20% on Gemma-2-9b-IT across seven jailbreak attacks, substantially outperforming all baselines. On utility, AlphaSteer preserves near-vanilla performance (e.g., 67.3% utility score on Llama-3.1-8B vs. 67.1% vanilla), while baselines degrade severely (e.g., CAST drops to 30.2% and RV ablation to 29.1%). The method requires only ~90 seconds of offline computation per model, compared to 20 minutes for refusal training (SFT), while achieving 91.93% DSR vs. 68.57% for SFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlphaSteer outperforms all four baselines on average DSR across all three models. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, it achieves 91.93% DSR versus Circuit Breaker (84.42%), Surgical (82.83%), CAST (80.57%), and Jailbreak Antidote (76.94%). The gap is most dramatic on Gemma-2-9b-IT, where AlphaSteer reaches 98.20% while the best baseline (CAST) only achieves 72.97%, and on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct where it hits 97.29% versus Circuit Breaker's 86.57%. On utility preservation, AlphaSteer maintains near-vanilla scores (e.g., 67.3% vs. 67.1% on Llama-3.1-8B), while CAST collapses to 30.2% (failing all math problems) and the RV ablation (direct refusal vector) drops to 29.1%, confirming that the null-space constraint is critical for avoiding over-refusal. Surgical and Jailbreak Antidote show moderate utility degradation (59.3% and 64.0% on Llama-3.1-8B), still well below AlphaSteer's 67.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

AlphaSteer builds directly on the activation-steering-for-refusal paradigm established by Arditi et al. (2024) and Rimsky et al. (2024), which inject a difference-in-means refusal direction vector into LLM activations. It departs from the two dominant mitigation strategies—vector calibration (Surgical, Jailbreak Antidote) and conditional steering (CAST)—by replacing their heuristic rules with a learnable transformation matrix Δ constrained to the null space of benign activations, drawing on null-space projection techniques from continual learning (Wang et al., 2021) and knowledge editing (Fang et al., 2025, AlphaEdit). The key conceptual shift is framing the safety-utility trade-off as a joint optimization problem with a closed-form solution, rather than an ad-hoc calibration or thresholding problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AlphaSteer provides the first theoretically grounded (null-space constraint + regularized least-squares) solution to the safety-utility trade-off in activation steering, achieving ~92–98% defense success rate across seven jailbreak attacks while keeping utility scores within 1–2 points of vanilla models—something no prior steering method achieves simultaneously. Its inference-time-only design (no post-training, ~90s offline computation) makes it a practical drop-in for deployed LLMs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to large reasoning models and larger-scale architectures, and whether the O(D²) per-layer inference cost becomes prohibitive at scale. The paper also acknowledges the dual-use risk: the same linear-regression machinery could reconstruct a negative steering vector to facilitate jailbreaks.
