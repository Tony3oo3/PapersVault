## Problem

While finite-time convergence guarantees exist for SDE-based stochastic interpolants, the ODE counterpart—widely used in practice for deterministic generation—remains largely unexplored. The paper asks: what is the iteration complexity for numerical ODE solvers (forward Euler and Heun's method) to achieve ε-level distribution approximation error in the general data-to-data stochastic interpolant framework, where the interpolant is non-Markovian and prior diffusion-model analyses do not directly apply?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264314/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sample from an initial distribution ρ₀ (e.g., Gaussian) and a neural-network-estimated velocity field b̂(t,x), then iteratively applies a numerical ODE solver over a sub-interval [t₀, t_N] ⊂ (0,1) to produce a sample from the target distribution ρ₁. The theoretical output is a total variation error bound decomposed into initialization error, drift/Jacobian estimation errors, and a discretization error term that explicitly depends on the step-size schedule {h_k}, the noise schedule γ(t), and dimension d. The analysis also yields optimized step-size schedules (exponentially decaying) and closed-form iteration complexity bounds.

## Method

The key technical innovation is a carefully designed continuous-time interpolation that reformulates each discrete-time solver step as a continuous ODE trajectory comparable to the true process. For forward Euler, the interpolation F_{t_k→t}(x) = x + (t−t_k)b̂(t_k,x) is shown to be a diffeomorphism for small step sizes, yielding an effective drift b̃(t,x) whose deviation from the true drift b(t,x) is decomposed via triangle inequality into bias, estimation, and discretization components. For Heun's method, a quadratic interpolation G_{t_k→t} linearly blends the two drift evaluations per step, achieving O(h_k³) per-step error versus O(h_k²) for Euler. The TV distance is then controlled via a PDE-based lemma (from Li et al. 2025b) that bounds distribution error by integrated drift and divergence errors. This yields O(ε^{-1}d²) and O(ε^{-1/2}d^{3/2}) iteration complexities, with the Heun result improving upon prior diffusion-model analyses by requiring only second-order smoothness in x (not third-order in both x and t) and achieving better dimensional dependence.

## Results

The paper establishes the first finite-time TV error bounds for ODE-based stochastic interpolants. For the forward Euler method (Theorem 4.5), the iteration complexity to achieve ε-TV error is O(ε⁻¹d²) for general data-to-data transformations, and O(ε⁻¹d²log²(1/δ)) for VP diffusion with bounded data. For Heun's method (Theorem 5.4), the complexity improves to O(ε⁻¹/²d³/²) (general) and O(ε⁻¹/²d³/²log³/²(1/δ)) (VP diffusion), with per-step discretization error of O(h²) versus O(h) for Euler. Empirical validation on 2D density transforms and d-dimensional Gaussian mixtures confirms the predicted O(h) and O(h²) convergence rates, and image generation on LSUN-Churches and LSUN-Bedroom demonstrates Heun's faster convergence in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For second-order methods, this paper's Heun's method achieves O(ε⁻¹/²d³/²) complexity, improving upon Li et al. (2024a) which gives Õ(ε⁻¹/²d³) and Li et al. (2025a) which gives Õ(ε⁻¹/²d²) — the latter has better dimensional dependence but requires no smoothness assumptions while this paper only needs bounded second-order spatial derivatives. Compared to Huang et al. (2025), which also achieves Õ(ε⁻¹/²d³/²) for p=2, this paper relaxes the smoothness requirement from bounded third-order derivatives in both x and t to bounded second-order derivatives in x alone, and avoids time-smoothness assumptions entirely. For first-order methods, the O(ε⁻¹d²) bound matches Li et al. (2025b) up to constants but extends to the general data-to-data setting (not just Gaussian-to-data as in Li et al. 2024c's Õ(d/ε) result). The key advantage is generality: the framework applies to all stochastic interpolants satisfying mild regularity, not just specific diffusion schedules. Empirically, Heun's method shows visibly faster convergence than Euler on LSUN-Churches and LSUN-Bedroom image generation, consistent with the theoretical O(h²) vs O(h) per-step error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic interpolant framework (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023; Albergo et al., 2023) by providing the first finite-time convergence analysis for ODE-based numerical solvers in the data-to-data setting. Prior discrete-time analyses covered SDE-based interpolants (Liu et al., 2025) or ODE-based diffusion models restricted to Gaussian-to-data transformations (Li et al., 2024b, 2024c, 2025b; Huang et al., 2025), while ODE-based stochastic interpolants had only continuous-time guarantees (Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023; Benton et al., 2024b). Technically, the paper departs from Li et al. (2025b) and Huang et al. (2025) by using a direct drift/divergence error decomposition via a PDE-based TV bound (Lemma D.2) rather than density-ratio or Gagliardo-Nirenberg approaches, enabling lower smoothness requirements and a unified treatment of estimation and discretization errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results provide practitioners with explicit iteration complexity bounds and optimized step-size schedules for ODE-based generative models beyond the Gaussian-to-data setting, with the Heun's method achieving O(ε^{-1/2} d^{3/2}) complexity that improves upon prior second-order analyses in both dimensional dependence and smoothness assumptions. The general framework applies to any valid stochastic interpolant (linear, VP, etc.), making it a reusable analytical tool. A notable open question is the gap between theoretical dimensional scaling (d² for Euler, d³ for Heun) and empirically observed linear growth in d, suggesting the bounds may be tightened. The analysis also leaves open whether higher-order methods (beyond Heun's) can be accommodated within the same interpolation-based framework.
