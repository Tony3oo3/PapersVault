## Problem

Existing 3D generative models condition on text or images, which are poorly suited for specifying precise geometry—language is ambiguous and images are hard to manipulate. Spatially controlled alternatives either require class-specific fine-tuning (limiting generalization) or lengthy test-time optimization, and none offer a tunable strength parameter for the geometric conditioning. The paper asks whether explicit 3D spatial control can be injected into a pretrained generative model at inference time, without any retraining.

## Input / Output

The method accepts 3D geometric primitives (superquadrics parameterized by 11 values: 3 scale, 2 exponent, 3 translation, 3 rotation) or full polygon meshes as spatial control, alongside a text prompt and an optional reference image. The geometry is voxelized into a 64×64×64 binary occupancy grid before encoding. Output is a textured 3D asset decodable as a mesh, 3D Gaussian splat, or radiance field, with a user-tunable parameter τ₀ ∈ [0,10] that interpolates between high realism (low τ₀) and high geometric faithfulness (high τ₀).

## Method

SPACECONTROL is a training-free guidance mechanism that operates on the pretrained Trellis rectified-flow model. The user's 3D geometry is voxelized and encoded by Trellis' pretrained encoder E into a clean latent z_{c,0} ∈ R^{16×16×16×8}; this latent is then noised to an intermediate time step t₀ via the forward equation z_{t₀} = t₀·z₁ + (1−t₀)·z_{c,0}, and denoised from t₀ to 0 using the unmodified Structure Flow Model, analogous to SDEdit but in 3D latent space. The parameter τ₀ directly controls the trade-off: lower values initialize closer to pure noise (more denoising steps, more realistic but less faithful), while higher values preserve more of the injected spatial structure. No architectural modifications or additional training are required—the method plugs into any two-stage rectified-flow 3D generator (Trellis, SAM 3D) that provides a matching encoder-decoder pair. The appearance stage proceeds with standard text or image conditioning through the Appearance Flow Model, optionally enabling cross-modal texture transfer from 2D images.

## Results

On the Toys4K dataset (unseen at training), SPACECONTROL achieves a Chamfer Distance of 14.0×10⁻³ with geometric primitive conditioning and 4.89×10⁻³ with mesh conditioning, substantially outperforming the strongest training-based baseline SPICE-E-T (39.1 and 23.3, respectively). On ShapeNet chairs, SPACECONTROL reaches CD of 0.98×10⁻³ (primitives) and 0.66×10⁻³ (meshes), compared to 5.92 and 22.7 for SPICE-E-T. Realism metrics (FID, P-FID) remain competitive: e.g., on chairs with primitives, SPACECONTROL scores FID=146 and P-FID=34.06 versus 135 and 39.22 for SPICE-E-T, indicating a favorable faithfulness–realism tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPACECONTROL dominates all three baselines on Chamfer Distance (spatial faithfulness) across every dataset and conditioning type. Against the strongest training-based baseline SPICE-E-T, SPACECONTROL reduces CD by ~2.8× on Toys4K primitives (14.0 vs. 39.1), ~6× on chairs (0.98 vs. 5.92), and ~34× on chair meshes (0.66 vs. 22.7). Against the optimization-based Coin3D, the advantage is even more pronounced: CD drops from 54.4 to 14.0 on Toys4K primitives and from 77.8 to 4.89 on Toys4K meshes. Against the original Spice-E, SPACECONTROL achieves 4.7× lower CD on Toys4K primitives (14.0 vs. 65.9) and 59× lower on table meshes (0.48 vs. 28.2). On realism metrics, SPACECONTROL is competitive but not uniformly best: SPICE-E-T achieves slightly lower FID on Toys4K primitives (223 vs. 221) and tables (122 vs. 157), while SPACECONTROL achieves lower P-FID on chairs (34.06 vs. 39.22) and table meshes (42.33 vs. 46.76). The user study (52 participants) confirms that SPACECONTROL is consistently preferred over both Spice-E and SPICE-E-T in overall appearance, spatial faithfulness, and realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPACECONTROL adapts the SDEdit paradigm (Meng et al., 2022) from 2D stroke-conditioned image editing to 3D geometry-conditioned asset generation, injecting user-specified spatial control directly into the latent space of a pretrained rectified flow model (Trellis). It departs from training-based approaches like Spice-E and LION, which require category-specific fine-tuning and sacrifice generalization, and from optimization-based methods like Coin3D and LatentNeRF, which incur substantial test-time optimization overhead. The key conceptual move is recognizing that Trellis' pretrained encoder, unused during standard inference, can map arbitrary 3D geometry into the shared latent space, enabling conditioning without any architectural modification or retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both fine-tuning and iterative optimization, SPACECONTROL makes spatially controlled 3D generation practical for interactive creative workflows, with the τ0 parameter offering a smooth, interpretable trade-off between geometric faithfulness and visual realism. The training-free design means the method is immediately transferable to any compatible flow-matching 3D generator (e.g., SAM 3D), lowering the barrier for practitioners. Open questions include whether τ0 can be scheduled adaptively per object class or region, whether part-level semantic control can be made more principled than the cross-attention baseline shown, and whether the approach extends naturally to full scene generation with inter-object spatial relationships.
