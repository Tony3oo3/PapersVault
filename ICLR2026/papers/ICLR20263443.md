## Problem

Diffusion models are known to produce novel, diverse samples in practice, yet the standard denoising training objective admits a closed-form solution that merely memorizes training data. This mismatch between theoretical expectation and observed behavior has left the mechanism of generalization in diffusion models poorly understood, with existing explanations remaining fragmented across random-feature models, linear analyses, and handcrafted inductive-bias constructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263443/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For memorization detection, the method takes a single generated image, adds forward noise at a fixed timestep (t=50, σ_t≈0.17), extracts intermediate activations from a chosen layer, and outputs a binary flag based on whether the standard deviation of those activations exceeds a threshold. For image editing, it takes a source image plus a set of 100 reference images in a target style, computes the mean representation across the reference set at multiple up-block layers, and adds this steering vector to the source image's representation during the final 20 of 40 denoising steps to produce a style-transferred image. The theoretical analysis operates on a two-layer ReLU DAE trained on mixture-of-Gaussians data or real image datasets (CelebA, CIFAR-10, ImageNet, LAION).

## Method

The paper proves that the local minimizers of a two-layer ReLU denoising autoencoder trained under ℓ₂-regularized denoising loss exhibit a block-wise structure determined by the empirical Gram matrix of data clusters, with the block structure governed by an (α,β)-separability condition on the training data. In the overparameterized regime (p≥n) on locally sparse data, the weights store individual training samples as sparse columns, producing spiky one-hot representations that serve as a signature of memorization; in the underparameterized regime (p≪n) on locally abundant data, the weights capture the principal components of each cluster's covariance, yielding balanced low-dimensional projections that enable generalization. Building on this theory, the paper introduces a prompt-free memorization detector that uses the standard deviation of intermediate activations as a spikiness proxy (achieving AUC 0.987–0.998 across LAION-SD1.4, ImageNet-DiT, and CIFAR10-EDM), and a training-free representation-space steering method that adds mean target-style representations to guide generation, revealing that generalized samples are smoothly steerable while memorized samples exhibit brittle threshold-like responses.

## Results

The paper's representation-based memorization detector (using standard deviation of intermediate features) achieves the best AUC across all three dataset-model pairs: 0.987 on LAION-SD1.4, 0.995 on ImageNet-DiT, and 0.998 on CIFAR10-EDM, while also being prompt-free. On CIFAR10-EDM, it reaches a TPR of 0.984 at 1% FPR with a runtime of only 0.020s, substantially outperforming the only other prompt-free baseline (Ross et al., 2025) which achieves AUC 0.713 and TPR 0.013 on the same setting. The method also matches the best prompt-dependent baseline (Wen et al., 2024) on LAION (AUC 0.987 vs. 0.986, TPR 0.961) while running 2× faster (0.067s vs. 0.134s). Theoretically, the paper proves that overparameterized ReLU DAEs store training samples as sparse weight columns (yielding spiky, near one-hot representations), while underparameterized DAEs capture local data statistics (yielding balanced, low-dimensional projections).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four memorization-detection baselines, the proposed representation-STD method dominates on every available metric. On LAION-SD1.4, it edges out Wen et al. (2024) in AUC (0.987 vs. 0.986) while halving the runtime (0.067s vs. 0.134s), and substantially outperforms Hintersdorf et al. (2024) in TPR (0.961 vs. 0.500) and Carlini et al. (2023) in both AUC (0.987 vs. 0.498) and TPR (0.961 vs. 0.020). On ImageNet-DiT, where only Ross et al. (2025) is available as a prompt-free comparison, the gap is dramatic: AUC 0.995 vs. 0.971 and TPR 0.912 vs. 0.528, with a 2× speed advantage (0.015s vs. 0.031s). On CIFAR10-EDM, the advantage over Ross et al. is even more pronounced—AUC 0.998 vs. 0.713 and TPR 0.984 vs. 0.013—indicating that the landscape-based (output-space) approach of Ross et al. fails to generalize to this setting, whereas the representation-space signal remains highly discriminative. The method is the only one that is simultaneously prompt-free, representation-based, and competitive with or superior to prompt-dependent baselines across all three benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends several fragmented lines of analysis on diffusion model generalization: random-feature models (Li et al., 2023; George et al., 2025) that oversimplify architecture, linear-model analyses on Gaussian mixtures (Li et al., 2024b; Wang et al., 2024a) that cannot capture memorization, and inductive-bias constructions (Kamb and Ganguli, 2025; Niedoba et al., 2025; Lukoianov et al., 2025) that remain phenomenological. The key departure is a nonlinear two-layer ReLU DAE framework that simultaneously characterizes memorization and generalization through a single representation-centric lens—spiky vs. balanced bottleneck activations—rather than treating them as separate phenomena. This connects to the growing recognition that diffusion training is fundamentally a self-supervised autoencoder objective (Vincent, 2011; Chen et al., 2025b; Xiang et al., 2025), but makes the representation structure the explicit mechanism linking distribution learning to generation behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified theoretical account of when and why diffusion models memorize versus generalize, grounded in a concrete representation structure that is directly observable in trained networks. The practical tools—prompt-free memorization detection via representation spikiness and training-free steering that reveals a fundamental editability gap between memorized and generalized samples—offer immediate utility for privacy auditing and controllable generation. Limitations include the theory being confined to a two-layer ReLU DAE (though validated on EDM, DiT, and SD1.4), and the steering method being relatively simple (mean-representation addition) compared to existing editing techniques. Open questions include whether the spiky/balanced dichotomy persists in much deeper architectures with more complex nonlinearities, and whether the detection signal remains robust under adversarial or distribution-shifted inputs.
