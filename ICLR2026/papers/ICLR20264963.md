## Problem

Small Vision-Language Models suffer from weaker generalization and downstream task performance, and existing test-time scaling methods are typically computationally demanding—relying on external verifier models, temperature sampling, or answer-level aggregation—contradicting the resource-efficient design goals of small models. This paper addresses the gap by proposing lightweight test-time scaling strategies that leverage model-internal signals at the token level without external supervision or parameter updates.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input, generates N=16 semantically equivalent augmented versions of both modalities (classical image transformations like brightness/rotation/noise plus text perturbations like keyboard errors, word splitting, and sentence reordering with consistency enforcement), and produces a single coherent text response. The response is generated autoregressively, with each token selected greedily from the averaged next-token probability distribution across all augmented views, rather than from a single forward pass.

## Method

The framework comprises two complementary strategies. Test-Time Augmentation (TTAug) applies semantic-preserving transformations to both image and text inputs, then at each autoregressive generation step aggregates the next-token probability distributions across all N augmented views via simple averaging before greedily selecting the next token—preserving local confidence signals that answer-level methods discard. Test-Time Adaptation (TTAdapt) extends this by iteratively using TTAug consensus outputs as pseudolabels to fine-tune the full VLM parameter set during inference, resetting weights between questions to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Two key design insights drive the approach: (1) input perturbations with greedy decoding produce higher-quality diverse candidates than temperature sampling because they remain on the model's training likelihood manifold, and (2) token-level aggregation compounds per-step corrections multiplicatively across sequence length, providing an exponential advantage over answer-level selection for longer responses.

## Results

TTAug achieves a mean accuracy of 47.9 across nine benchmarks, a +4.1% absolute improvement over the SmolVLM2-2.2B baseline (43.8). The largest gains appear on benchmarks where the baseline fails entirely: OCRVQA (0.0→11.8), GQA (0.0→5.8), and AMBER (68.7→75.4). TTAug is also the most efficient method, requiring only 2.99 s runtime and 70.3 output tokens per query, compared to 3.73–4.46 s and 74.5–82.3 tokens for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTAug outperforms all four compared test-time scaling methods on mean accuracy (47.9) and is the most efficient in both runtime and token count. Against Self-Consistency (Wang et al., 2023b), TTAug leads by 11.5 points; Self-Consistency actually underperforms the baseline on average (36.4 vs. 43.8), collapsing to near-zero on AI2D (3.1) and failing on OCRVQA/GQA. Against Self-Selector (Chen et al., 2024a), TTAug wins by 5.1 points (47.9 vs. 42.8), with the gap driven by AMBER (+10.9) and MME-RealWorld (+4.7). Against Sample-and-Rank (Adiwardana et al., 2020), TTAug leads by 6.9 points (47.9 vs. 41.0), again with the largest margin on AMBER (+21.9). The closest competitor is Self-Synthesizer (Li et al., 2025d) at 43.9 mean, but TTAug still wins by 4.0 points while being 1.47× faster (2.99 s vs. 4.46 s) and generating 15% fewer tokens. Notably, TTAug is the only method that consistently improves over the baseline on all nine benchmarks, whereas every other method degrades performance on at least one.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical test-time augmentation (TTAug) paradigm from vision and NLP into a systematic test-time scaling method for VLMs, which the authors note is 'surprisingly underexplored' in multimodal settings. It departs from the dominant test-time scaling lineage (self-consistency, sample-and-rank, self-synthesizer) on two axes: replacing temperature sampling with input perturbations under greedy decoding, and replacing answer-level selection/synthesis with token-level probability aggregation. The TTAdapt component extends the self-training-with-pseudolabels framework from CLIP-based adaptation to autoregressive multimodal language models, which prior work had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deploying small VLMs on consumer hardware: a simple pipeline of 16 augmented inputs with token-level averaging yields consistent gains across nine diverse benchmarks without external verifiers or parameter updates. Two design principles—greedy decoding with input perturbations over temperature sampling, and token-level over answer-level aggregation—offer actionable guidance that could reshape how test-time scaling is implemented for resource-constrained models. Open questions include whether task-adaptive aggregation layer selection (early layers for visual reasoning, late layers for linguistic tasks) can be automated, whether the non-monotonic scaling behavior (performance peaking at ~16 augmentations) can be mitigated, and whether the approach's benefits persist as model scale increases beyond the 2–9B range tested.
