## Problem

Numerical relativity simulations of the Einstein field equations are among the most computationally intensive tasks in scientific computing, requiring petascale parallelization and producing petabytes of data per run on adaptive high-resolution grids. No compact, mesh-agnostic, and differentiable representation exists for the four-dimensional spacetime metric tensor that can replace expensive spatiotemporal discretizations while still enabling accurate computation of higher-order geometric quantities (Christoffel symbols, Riemann tensor, curvature invariants) needed for geodesic dynamics and gravitational-wave extraction.

## Input / Output

The network takes a 4D spacetime coordinate query (t, x, y, z) and returns the 10 independent components of the metric tensor g_αβ at that point; during training it is supervised on metric values sampled from discrete grids (uniform, irregular, or fixed-mesh-refinement hierarchies). Crucially, the network is trained on the distortion Δ_αβ = g_αβ − η_αβ (deviation from the Minkowski background) rather than the full metric, so the representational capacity focuses on non-trivial curvature content. Downstream, automatic differentiation through the network yields all derived tensorial quantities—Christoffel symbols, Riemann/Weyl/Ricci tensors, Kretschmann scalar, geodesic trajectories, and gravitational-wave strain—without any finite-difference stencil or mesh reconstruction.

## Method

EinFields is a compact MLP (SiLU activations, widths 64–512, depths 3–8, <1.9 M parameters ≈ 7 MiB) that overfits the metric distortion on 4D spacetime samples, acting as an implicit neural tensor field. The key training innovation is Sobolev supervision: the loss (Eq. 6) augments the standard L2 metric error with explicit Jacobian (∂_μ g_αβ, 40 components) and Hessian (∂_μ∂_ν g_αβ, 100 components) terms, rectifying derivative irregularities and improving Christoffel/Riemann accuracy by up to two orders of magnitude. Training uses the SOAP quasi-Newton optimizer with GradNorm-based unit-norm gradient balancing and a cosine learning-rate schedule. Because the metric is a smooth neural function, all higher-order geometric quantities are obtained via forward-mode automatic differentiation (jacfwd + einsum index manipulation) rather than finite-difference stencils, yielding up to 10^5 accuracy gains over 4th/6th-order FD in FLOAT32. The framework is discretization-free: it accepts arbitrary point samples (including non-uniform FMR grids) and provides continuous, resolution-invariant query access to the metric and its derivatives at any resolution.

## Results

On the Schwarzschild metric, EinFields achieves a relative ℓ₂ error of (1.08±0.06)×10⁻⁶ with 85 KiB storage, yielding a 4035× compression over the 343 MiB explicit grid. With Sobolev (Jacobian + Hessian) supervision, the metric accuracy improves to (1.88±0.16)×10⁻⁷ relative ℓ₂ at 202 KiB (1698× compression). For derived geometric quantities in FLOAT32, EinFields via automatic differentiation outperforms 4th/6th-order finite-difference stencils by 10–10⁵ in MAE (e.g., Riemann tensor: 1.25×10⁻⁶ vs. 1.78×10⁻² for FD). On the oscillating neutron star NR simulation (FMR grid), EinFields achieves 3.60×10⁻⁵ relative ℓ₂ with 1.4 MiB storage, a 2121× compression over the 2.9 GiB coalesced grid.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is higher-order finite-difference (FD) stencils (4th-order with O(h⁵) truncation and 6th-order with O(h⁷) truncation) applied to the ground-truth metric on a uniform grid with h = 0.01. EinFields via automatic differentiation systematically outperforms FD stencils in FLOAT32 across all derived quantities: the Riemann tensor MAE drops from 1.78×10⁻² (FD) to 1.25×10⁻⁶ (EinFields), a ~10⁴ improvement; the Ricci scalar improves from 5.35×10⁻² to 3.80×10⁻⁵ (~10³); and the Bianchi identity residual improves from 1.68×10⁻² to 5.00×10⁻⁸ (~10⁶). The 'GT (AD)' column (analytic solution differentiated directly) serves as an upper bound, showing EinFields remains 2–3 orders of magnitude above the exact-derivative limit, consistent with the ~10⁻⁷ metric reconstruction error. For storage, EinFields (85 KiB–1.4 MiB) compresses the explicit grid (343 MiB–2.9 GiB) by 1000–4000×. The paper notes that in FLOAT64 the advantage over FD narrows, and long-time geodesic rollouts still accumulate errors from the residual metric inaccuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit neural representation (NeF) paradigm—previously applied to scalar fields (SDFs, radiance fields) and vector fields in 3D visual computing—to the 4D tensor-valued metric of general relativity, positioning EinFields as a specialized Neural Tensor Field where the full differential-geometric hierarchy (Christoffel symbols, Riemann tensor, curvature invariants) emerges naturally from automatic differentiation of the learned metric. The training objective builds directly on Sobolev training (Czarnecki et al., 2017; Son et al., 2021), incorporating Jacobian and Hessian supervision to regularize higher-order derivatives, while the distortion decomposition (subtracting the Minkowski background) is a practical preprocessing step that focuses representational capacity on non-trivial curvature. Unlike PINNs, which use neural networks as PDE solvers, EinFields are purely representational: they overfit a known solution and exploit differentiability for downstream physics, making them complementary rather than competitive with existing NR solvers (BSSN, spectral methods).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Numerical relativity produces petabyte-scale datasets on petascale infrastructure, and the demonstrated 2000–4000× storage compression with 5–7 decimal accuracy offers a concrete path toward more accessible archiving and distribution of simulation data. The finding that AD-based tensor differentiation outperforms high-order finite-difference stencils by up to 10⁵ in FLOAT32 is practically significant for derivative-heavy post-processing (geodesic integration, curvature extraction) where FD truncation errors accumulate. However, the framework remains lossy (Rel. ℓ₂ below 1e-9 unattainable), currently surpasses FD only in single precision, and has not been benchmarked against (pseudo-)spectral methods or tested on the most demanding NR scenarios (binary black hole/neutron star mergers), leaving open whether it can complement or replace grid-based workflows at production scale.
