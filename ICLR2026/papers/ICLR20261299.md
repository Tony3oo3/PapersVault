## Problem

Existing reasoning segmentation methods fall into two polarized camps: latent-reasoning approaches decode hidden LLM features directly into masks (opaque and uncontrollable), while text-based approaches emit discrete coordinates that are fragile to formatting errors and lack fine-grained semantic detail. This paper addresses the need for an interpretable, differentiable interface that preserves the richness of language reasoning while providing dense spatial grounding for mask prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261299/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized so the longer side is 1024 px, padded to 1024×1024; the policy input is capped at 705,600 pixels ≈ 900 vision tokens) and a free-form compositional text instruction. It produces three outputs: a chain-of-thought reasoning trace, a 256×256 positional prior heatmap (the interpretable intermediate), and a binary segmentation mask at the original image resolution obtained by thresholding decoder logits at zero after removing padding.

## Method

CoPRS builds on Qwen2.5-VL as the MLLM backbone and uses multimodal chain-of-thought (MCoT): the model reasons in a  block and then emits a special concentration token <REF_POS>. The hidden state at that token is projected by an MLP query head into a concentration query Q, which attends to image keys K (from a frozen SAM ViT-H encoder) via multi-head scaled dot-product attention; two stacked 2D convolutions fuse the per-head scores into a dense positional prior heatmap. A lightweight 4.7 M-parameter decoder (three conv blocks + a SAM-style Two-Way Transformer) refines the heatmap into the final mask. Training unifies GRPO reinforcement learning on the language path (group size G=8, reward = 0.7·mask quality + 0.3·CoT format compliance, KL-regularized against a reference policy) with supervised segmentation loss (BCE on the heatmap + Dice + Focal on the mask) in a single end-to-end backward pass, so that reasoning quality and mask precision are jointly optimized.

## Results

CoPRS-7B achieves 81.6 cIoU on RefCOCO val, 85.3 on testA, and 79.5 on testB, setting new state-of-the-art across all three splits. On RefCOCO+ it reaches 75.9/80.3/69.7 (val/testA/testB) and on RefCOCOg 76.2/76.2 (val/test). In zero-shot evaluation on ReasonSeg, CoPRS-7B attains 65.2 gIoU and 64.5 cIoU on validation, outperforming the previous best (Seg-Zero-7B at 62.6/62.0) by over 2.6 points in gIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoPRS-7B outperforms the strongest latent-reasoning method RAS-13B on 6 of 8 RefCOCO-series splits (e.g., +0.6 on RefCOCO val, +1.8 on RefCOCO testA, +0.8 on RefCOCO+ val), trailing only on RefCOCO+ testB (69.7 vs 70.3) and RefCOCOg test (76.2 vs 77.5). Against all text-based reasoning baselines, CoPRS-7B leads on every reported split: it surpasses Text4Seg-13B by +1.4 cIoU on RefCOCO val and +2.3 on RefCOCO+ val, and beats Seg-Zero-7B by +5.0 on RefCOCO testA. Notably, the smaller CoPRS-3B model already exceeds the 7B variants of Seg-R1 and Seg-Zero on testA splits (83.9 vs 78.7/80.3 on RefCOCO), demonstrating the efficiency of the positional-prior interface. On zero-shot ReasonSeg, CoPRS-7B leads all 14 compared methods, achieving 65.2 gIoU on validation versus 62.6 for Seg-Zero-7B and 60.8 for Seg-R1-3B, with a 2.6-point gIoU margin over the runner-up.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoPRS positions itself as a third paradigm between two existing families of reasoning segmentation: latent-reasoning methods (LISA, PerceptionGPT, GroundHog) that decode LLM hidden features directly into masks, and text-based methods (Seg-Zero, Seg-R1, Text4Seg) that emit discrete coordinates for SAM. Its core departure is introducing a dense, differentiable positional prior (a heatmap) generated by a learnable concentration token, which serves as an interpretable intermediate between MCoT reasoning and mask decoding. The training framework builds directly on GRPO from DeepSeekMath for the language path, combined with supervised segmentation losses on the vision path, using Qwen2.5-VL as the MLLM backbone and SAM's ViT-H with a Two-Way Transformer decoder for the perception path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The positional prior offers a practical middle ground: more interpretable and diagnosable than opaque hidden features, and more robust than discrete text coordinates that break on formatting errors or out-of-image values. The demonstrated strong correlation (R > 0.7) among CoT quality, heatmap concentration, and final mask IoU provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding where reasoning segmentation fails. Open limitations include a resolution bottleneck for very small objects (heatmap at 256×256, SAM embeddings at 64×64) and ambiguity in dense same-class crowds, suggesting that multi-scale features and stronger instance-level language grounding remain important future directions. The unified GRPO + segmentation supervision framework also naturally extends to other region-concentration tasks beyond segmentation.
