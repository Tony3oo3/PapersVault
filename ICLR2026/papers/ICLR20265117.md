## Problem

Existing dataset compression methods (pruning, distillation) reduce sample counts but ignore the substantial per-image color redundancy that dominates storage. Prior color quantization approaches are either image-property-based (wasting bits on backgrounds with no model guidance) or model-perception-based (introducing abrupt texture discontinuities that degrade downstream training), and none are optimized for dataset-level training performance rather than single-image inference accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full RGB image dataset (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and a pre-trained backbone for feature extraction. It produces a small set of shared cluster-level color palettes (e.g., 20 clusters × 2^q colors for q-bit quantization) plus per-pixel index maps; during training, images are reconstructed on-the-fly from these indices and palettes. The test set remains in full color, and the entire train set is quantized rather than pruned, so the number of training samples is unchanged.

## Method

DCQ integrates three mechanisms into a unified pipeline. First, Chromaticity-Aware Clustering (CAC) applies K-Means on shallow-layer features (first residual block) from a pre-trained ResNet to partition images into 20 chromatically similar clusters, enabling shared palettes that maintain cross-image color consistency. Second, Attention-Guided Palette Allocation uses Grad-CAM++ heatmaps to retain only the top 50% most salient pixels per image, aggregates them across a cluster in LAB color space, and runs K-Means to produce a shared palette that prioritizes semantically critical foreground colors over background. Third, Texture-Preserved Palette Optimization refines the K-Means palette via differentiable quantization (straight-through estimator) that minimizes Sobel-based edge distribution differences between original and quantized images, preserving structural boundaries. The overall objective is training-oriented: the quantizer is chosen to maximize test accuracy of a model trained from scratch on the quantized dataset, rather than preserving inference accuracy of a fixed pre-trained model.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, DCQ achieves 94.39% top-1 accuracy at 80% compression (5-bit) and 79.90% at 96% compression (1-bit, only 2 colors per image), compared to 95.45% full-data accuracy. On CIFAR-100, DCQ reaches 69.05% (5-bit) and 38.44% (1-bit) versus 78.21% full-data. On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-34, DCQ attains 66.99% (5-bit) and 35.95% (1-bit) versus 73.54% full-data. Under the most aggressive 96% compression, DCQ outperforms the best dataset-pruning baseline (TDDS) by 7.44 points on CIFAR-10, 11.89 points on CIFAR-100, and 6.39 points on ImageNet-1K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCQ consistently outperforms all 17 baselines across every dataset and compression level. Against dataset-pruning methods, the gap is most dramatic at high compression: at 96% on CIFAR-10, DCQ (79.90%) beats TDDS (72.46%) by 7.44 points and CCS variants (~73%) by ~7 points, while EL2N and AUM collapse to ~20% and ~21% respectively. Against color-quantization methods (Table 6), DCQ's advantage is even larger at low bit depths: at 2-bit on CIFAR-10, DCQ achieves 89.15% versus ColorCNN (59.48%), ColorCNN+ (49.66%), CQFormer (52.53%), MedianCut (78.15%), and OCTree (68.95%). Against dataset-distillation methods (Table 5), DCQ surpasses G-VBSM (51.0%) and SRe2L (49.7%) on Tiny-ImageNet at IPC=100, while DM and MTT fail entirely (OOM) on Tiny-ImageNet and ImageNet-1K. Against recent dataset-quantization methods (Table 15), DCQ leads DUAL (91.42%), ADQ (90.40%), DQAL (90.20%), and DQ (89.40%) at 80% compression on CIFAR-10, and maintains a 2–4 point lead at 96% compression. Against AutoPalette (Table 16), DCQ gains 3.6 points on CIFAR-10 IPC=10 (77.9% vs 74.3%) and 8.4 points on CIFAR-100 IPC=10 (58.9% vs 52.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

DCQ carves new ground at the intersection of color quantization (CQ) and dataset compression. Traditional CQ methods (MedianCut, OCTree, ColorCNN, CQFormer) are inference-oriented, optimizing recognition accuracy of a fixed pre-trained model on quantized images, while dataset pruning/distillation reduces sample count but ignores per-sample color redundancy. DCQ repurposes K-Means clustering, Grad-CAM++ attention, and differentiable Sobel-edge optimization into a training-oriented, dataset-level pipeline that shares palettes across chromatically similar image clusters — a formulation the authors explicitly identify as absent from prior work. The method is orthogonal to and composable with existing pruning strategies (CCS, TDDS), positioning it as a complementary axis of compression rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical storage bottleneck for edge and resource-constrained deployment, showing that aggressive color reduction (down to 2 colors per image) can still support competitive training accuracy when palettes are shared and semantically guided. The training-oriented framing (Eq. 3) is a useful conceptual distinction: optimizing for inference accuracy on quantized images does not guarantee good training performance on quantized datasets, and this gap is quantified concretely. Open questions include whether the fixed 20-cluster / 50%-attention hyperparameters generalize without re-tuning, whether adaptive per-image bit allocation could close the remaining gap to full-color training, and whether architectures natively designed for palette-indexed inputs could further improve performance on quantized data.
