## Problem

RLVR training for large reasoning models is bottlenecked by on-policy rollout generation, whose cost grows rapidly with CoT length and model scale. The paper identifies that independent rollouts for the same query share highly similar early reasoning steps across iterations, creating substantial redundant computation that existing methods do not exploit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261557/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PROS takes math reasoning queries (from DAPO-Train or AIME-Old) and high-quality prefixes extracted from the policy's own past rollouts (constrained to the [T/4, 3T/4] token range). It produces an updated policy model and a growing tree-structured dataset of augmented queries, where each augmented query is the concatenation of an original query with a self-generated partial CoT. During training, the policy generates only the continuation after the prefix, and gradient updates are applied exclusively to these new tokens, preserving on-policy semantics while avoiding overfitting to historical steps.

## Method

PROS operates as a plugin on top of PPO or GRPO, adding two steps per iteration. First, it identifies promising prefixes from correct rollouts using token-level entropy (and critic value as a filter for actor-critic methods), then appends them to original queries to form augmented queries that shorten future on-policy generation. Second, it selects which augmented queries to train on via a two-layer logit-normal hierarchical Bayesian model: parent (original query) log-odds follow N(μ, τ²), child (augmented query) log-odds are conditionally Gaussian around the parent with variance σ², and Pólya-Gamma augmentation renders the non-conjugate Bernoulli-likelihood posterior tractable via Gibbs sampling. Queries with estimated pass rates closest to 0.5 (highest reward uncertainty) are prioritized, and an exponential forgetting factor (λ=0.99) adapts estimates to the evolving policy. This design simultaneously reduces redundant generation, prunes dead-end branches, and focuses compute on the most informative training instances.

## Results

PROS achieves Pass@1 of 33.23 on AIME24 and 78.20 on AMC23 under PPO on DAPO-Train, and 34.17 / 78.28 under GRPO, outperforming vanilla PPO/GRPO by +5.0 / +4.6 and +4.6 / +4.9 points respectively. Across all four training settings (PPO/GRPO × DAPO-Train/AIME-Old), PROS yields average improvements of +3.96 on AIME24 and +6.21 on AMC23 over the corresponding vanilla baselines. In terms of compute efficiency, PROS reduces average tokens per iteration from 10.24M to 8.49M (PPO, DAPO-Train) while maintaining comparable wall-clock time (~9.8 min vs. 9.2 min per iteration).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROS consistently outperforms all five baselines on both AIME24 and AMC23 across all four training settings. Against vanilla PPO/GRPO, PROS gains +5.0 / +4.6 (PPO, DAPO-Train) and +4.6 / +4.9 (GRPO, DAPO-Train) on AIME24 / AMC23, with the gap widening on AIME-Old (+2.9 / +7.8 for PPO; +3.3 / +7.5 for GRPO). Dynamic Sampling achieves competitive AMC23 scores (e.g., 69.21 vs. 65.62 for PPO on AIME-Old) but at 2–4× the wall-clock cost (40.04 vs. 8.09 min/iteration), making it impractical at scale. Experience Replay matches or slightly trails vanilla on accuracy (e.g., 30.10 vs. 28.23 for PPO on DAPO-Train) while saving a small amount of tokens, but falls well short of PROS's gains. Priority Sampling offers modest improvements over vanilla (e.g., 31.87 vs. 28.23 on AIME24 for PPO) but is consistently outperformed by PROS by 1–3 points. The PROS-ablation variant (random query selection instead of Bayesian selection) already improves over vanilla, confirming that prefix reuse alone is beneficial, while the full Bayesian selection mechanism adds a further 1–2 point gain on top.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROS sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) RLVR training for reasoning models (PPO, GRPO), where rollout generation is the dominant compute cost; (2) experience replay and prioritized sampling in RL, which PROS deliberately departs from by reusing only self-generated prefixes rather than full off-policy trajectories, preserving the on-policy nature of RLVR; and (3) hierarchical Bayesian bandits and curriculum learning, which inform the augmented query selection mechanism. The key conceptual departure is that PROS treats the tree structure of reasoning trajectories as a first-class object, converting historical prefixes into new training queries (Augmented Queries) rather than simply filtering or reweighting existing ones. The Pólya-Gamma augmentation for tractable Gibbs sampling over the hierarchical logit-normal model is borrowed directly from the Bayesian logistic regression literature (Polson et al., 2013) and adapted to the parent-child query hierarchy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As RLVR becomes the standard paradigm for training large reasoning models, rollout generation cost is the principal obstacle to further scaling; PROS offers a practical, plug-in solution that reduces redundant computation while simultaneously improving accuracy and preventing length collapse, making it potentially adoptable in existing RLVR pipelines (e.g., verl) with minimal engineering overhead. However, the current prefix identification heuristic (entropy or value-based) is acknowledged as coarse and potentially biased, and the method has only been validated on mathematical reasoning with Qwen3 models up to 8B parameters. Open questions include whether richer or learned criteria for prefix selection would yield further gains, whether the approach extends to code generation or other verifiable-reward domains, and how it interacts with other emerging efficiency techniques (e.g., speculative decoding, adaptive rollout lengths) at larger model scales.
