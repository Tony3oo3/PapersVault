## Problem

Existing evaluations of unified multimodal models (UMMs) assess understanding and generation in isolation—textual benchmarks score language-based reasoning while visual benchmarks score pixel-level outcomes—leaving untested the ability to use one modality to guide, verify, or refine outputs in the other. This paper identifies that gap as 'reciprocal cross-modal reasoning,' a capability central to the vision of truly omnimodal generation, and shows that no prior benchmark explicitly targets it with process-level evaluation of both the reasoning chain and the generated artifact.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263804/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ROVER-IG provides an initial image plus a textual prompt containing a chain of domain-specific constraints (e.g., temporal progression, spatial transformation, causal mechanism), and expects a generated image that satisfies all constraints alongside a verbal reasoning trace. ROVER-TG provides interleaved text-image question sequences (robotics scenes, geometry puzzles, jigsaw/multi-view perception) and expects a final text answer preceded by intermediate generated images that serve as auditable visual reasoning steps. The full benchmark comprises 1,312 tasks grounded in 1,876 images, with all metrics scored on a 5-point scale normalized to 0–100 by a GPT-4.1 VLM judge supplied with rubric cards and reference assets.

## Method

ROVER is a human-annotated, multi-stage curated benchmark spanning two complementary settings: (i) verbally-augmented reasoning for visual generation (908 tasks, 4 domains × 7 reasoning subtasks) where models must produce images guided by explicit reasoning chains, and (ii) visually-augmented reasoning for verbal generation (404 tasks, 3 scenarios × 6 subtasks) where models must emit intermediate visualizations that make downstream reasoning auditable. The evaluation protocol combines a GPT-4.1 VLM judge with expert validation on stratified samples, scoring along reasoning-specific dimensions (logical coherence, output alignment, step-to-output consistency) plus established image quality metrics for generation tasks. Across 17 unified models, the key empirical findings are that interleaved image-text generation models significantly outperform non-interleaved ones on reasoning-dependent visual generation, that combining strong unimodal models in a cascade cannot replicate this cross-modal reasoning, and that models exhibit a sharp dissociation: they succeed at literal physical/perceptual visual reasoning but systematically fail to construct visual abstractions for symbolic tasks, where faulty visual reasoning degrades rather than improves downstream accuracy.

## Results

On ROVER-IG (verbally-augmented visual generation), closed-source unified models lead with Nano Banana achieving the best overall scores (RP=67.0, Align.=82.3, RV=73.2), followed by GPT-5 (64.2/76.4/63.7) and Gemini 2.0 Flash (64.8/78.6/62.3). Open-source models trail substantially, with BAGEL-Think the strongest at 54.3/64.4/52.7 overall, and non-interleaved models (e.g., OmniGen2 at 32.2 RV) scoring ~39% lower on visual generation than closed-source counterparts. On ROVER-TG (visually-augmented verbal generation), even the best model (Nano Banana) achieves only 38.8% average Interleaved Reasoning quality, and visual augmentation improves accuracy on World Model (+3.7%) and Visual Perception (+6.3%) tasks but degrades Logic & Math performance (44.9% vs. 42.0% for Nano Banana, with GPT-5 showing no gain at 45.6% for both modes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Closed-source unified models (Nano Banana, GPT-5, Gemini 2.0 Flash) dominate all three ROVER-IG metrics, with Nano Banana leading by 3–13 points over the next best closed model and by 10–41 points over open-source models. Among open-source models, interleaved architectures (BAGEL-Think, UniCoT) outperform non-interleaved ones (BAGEL, OmniGen2, ILLUME+) by approximately 38% on the RV metric, confirming that internal cross-modal reasoning is essential rather than replicable via cascading separate models. On ROVER-TG, visual augmentation consistently improves World Model accuracy (e.g., Nano Banana: 40.6% vs. 36.9% verbal-only) and Visual Perception (50.0% vs. 43.7%), but provides minimal or negative gains on Logic & Math, where models fail to produce valid symbolic visual abstractions. Unified models also substantially outperform specialized image editing models (Qwen-Image-Edit, FLUX.1 Kontext, UltraEdit, VAREdit-8B) on reasoning-dependent visual generation, with Nano Banana scoring 79.6 overall RV versus 47.1 for the best editing model (Qwen-Image-Edit), demonstrating that editing fidelity alone cannot substitute for cross-modal reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROVER carves new ground by defining 'reciprocal cross-modal reasoning'—using one modality to guide, verify, or refine outputs in the other—as a distinct evaluation target, whereas prior benchmarks (ReasonPix2Pix, ReasonEdit, EditWorld, RISEBench, UnifiedBench) assess understanding and generation in isolation or focus solely on instruction-following for image editing. The paper departs from the LMM-as-judge paradigm used by RISEBench and KRIS-Bench by introducing a multi-dimensional rubric that scores reasoning process quality, reasoning-to-output alignment, and step-to-output consistency simultaneously, rather than computing similarity against ground-truth images. It also extends the interleaved reasoning literature (Zebra-CoT, UniCoT) from training-time mechanisms to a diagnostic benchmark that tests whether models can actually produce and leverage visual intermediates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a fundamental capability gap: current UMMs can perform literal, perceptual visual reasoning but systematically fail at symbolic visual abstraction (e.g., drawing auxiliary lines for geometry), and flawed visual reasoning actively degrades downstream accuracy rather than improving it. The finding that cascade architectures (strong text model + strong image model) cannot replicate the cross-modal reasoning of unified models challenges the modular composition assumption underlying much of the field. Open questions include whether targeted training on interleaved reasoning data can close the physical-symbolic gap, and whether the VLM-judge protocol generalizes to future model generations that may outperform GPT-4.1's own reasoning.
