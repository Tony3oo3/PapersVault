## Problem

Existing red-teaming frameworks for vision-language models are either restricted to narrow, manually engineered attack patterns, limited to text-only modalities, or lack the scalability needed for comprehensive risk discovery. No prior framework provides controllable, policy-grounded generation of diverse multimodal adversarial instances conditioned on arbitrary risk definitions, leaving cross-modal vulnerabilities (e.g., typographic transformation, reasoning hijacking) systematically underexplored.

## Input / Output

ARMs accepts either a specific harmful instruction (instance-based mode) or a high-level risk definition from a regulation such as the EU AI Act, OWASP Top 10, or FINRA (policy-based mode), in which case it first samples and filters seed instructions to cover the policy space. It produces iteratively refined adversarial multimodal instances (image + text pairs) over up to T=30 optimization turns, with each turn either invoking another attack strategy or querying the target VLM. The target VLM's response is scored by a policy-based LLM judge on a 5-point Likert scale, and the attack is deemed successful only at the maximum score (τ=5), ensuring rigorous evaluation. The framework also outputs ARMS-BENCH, a 30K-instance safety dataset spanning 51 risk categories with reasoning-enhanced refusal labels for fine-tuning.

## Method

ARMs is an LLM-agent framework (GPT-4o backbone, temperature 0.8) that performs reasoning-enhanced multi-step orchestration over 17 red-teaming algorithms, each encapsulated as an independent MCP server for plug-and-play extensibility. The agent reasons over which of 11 novel multimodal attack strategies (spanning five adversarial patterns: visual context cloaking, typographic transformation, visual multi-turn escalation, visual reasoning hijacking, and visual perturbation) to apply sequentially or in parallel, incrementally refining the adversarial instance each turn. A two-dimensional layered memory indexed by (risk category, dominant attack strategy) stores high-harmfulness attack trajectories, with retrieval governed by an epsilon-greedy algorithm whose exploration probability decays exponentially (ε_min=0.1, ε_max=1.0, λ=1.0) to shift from broad exploration to focused exploitation. Memory retrieval scores candidates via a weighted sum of cosine similarities between risk-category and prompt embeddings (α=1.2), and slots are updated by replacement to maintain the most effective attacks per risk-strategy cell, preventing mode collapse while preserving attack diversity.

## Results

ARMs achieves state-of-the-art attack success rate (ASR) across all six evaluations and five victim models, outperforming the strongest baseline (X-Teaming) by an average of 52.1 percentage points. On Claude-4-Sonnet, ARMs reaches 93.3% ASR on StrongReject, 96.0% on OWASP, and 91.3% on FINRA, exceeding 90% ASR on three of six evaluations. On the open-source InternVL3-38B, ARMs achieves 98.5% (StrongReject) and 100% (OWASP, FINRA). In terms of diversity, ARMs generates red-teaming instances with a mean CLIP-based diversity score of 0.423, a 95.83% improvement over X-Teaming (0.216).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARMs decisively outperforms all five baselines across every evaluation and victim model. Against the strongest automatic red-teaming baseline X-Teaming, ARMs gains 35.6 pp on StrongReject (93.3% vs 57.7%) and 82.2 pp on OWASP (96.0% vs 13.8%) for Claude-4-Sonnet, with an average improvement of 52.1 pp across six evaluations. Against the strategy-based baselines, SI-Attack is the second-strongest method (e.g., 43.3% on StrongReject for Claude-4-Sonnet) but still trails ARMs by 50 pp; FigStep and QR-Attack are markedly weaker, often scoring below 10% on instance-based benchmarks. In the broader comparison (Table 4), ARMs also surpasses Rainbow-Teaming (93.1% vs 63.5% on StrongReject) and AutoDAN-Turbo (93.1% vs 19.4%), confirming that multi-step multimodal orchestration with layered memory substantially exceeds both open-ended prompt generation and text-only self-exploration approaches. A brute-force oracle that applies all strategies and keeps the best score reaches only 84.0% ASR on StrongReject (vs. ARMs's 95.2%), demonstrating that ARMs's iterative reasoning and strategy composition goes beyond simple strategy routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes and extends two largely separate lines of work: (1) strategy-based multimodal attacks (FigStep, SI-Attack, QR-Attack) that are lightweight but cover narrow, hand-engineered patterns, and (2) autonomous red-teaming agents for text LLMs (X-Teaming, AutoDAN-Turbo, Rainbow Teaming) that automate attack generation but ignore the visual modality. ARMs departs from both by unifying 17 multimodal attack strategies under a plug-and-play MCP interface and orchestrating them via multi-step reasoning with a layered epsilon-greedy memory, enabling cross-modal attacks that neither line achieves alone. The policy-grounded, controllable generation from risk definitions (EU AI Act, OWASP, FINRA) is a new formulation not present in prior red-teaming work, which typically operates on fixed instance sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >90% ASR on Claude-4-Sonnet—a model specifically designed for constitutional alignment—demonstrates that current safety alignment is far from robust against adaptive, multi-step multimodal attacks, raising urgent questions about the adequacy of existing evaluation protocols. The 30K-instance ARMs-BENCH spanning 51 regulatory risk categories fills a critical gap in multimodal safety benchmarking and provides a practical fine-tuning dataset that reduces ASR while preserving utility. Key limitations include substantial API and compute costs at high optimization budgets, and the framework's dependence on a strong multimodal backbone (GPT-4o) for its agent reasoning. Open questions include whether the layered memory design generalizes to video or audio modalities, and whether the plug-and-play MCP architecture can scale to community-contributed attacks without quality degradation.
