## Problem

Despite Mamba's empirical success as a transformer alternative, its fundamental in-context learning (ICL) capabilities remain poorly understood, with no formal connection between its architecture and optimal statistical estimators. This paper asks whether Mamba's ICL can be systematically characterized, specifically whether it learns the Bayes-optimal predictor for Markovian data and, if so, which architectural components enable this.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a discrete token sequence x = (x_1, ..., x_T) drawn from a random k-th order Markov chain over a finite alphabet X, where each transition kernel is independently sampled from a Dir(β·1) prior, meaning every sequence follows a different Markov distribution. The model must produce, at each position t, a next-token probability distribution P_θ(x_{t+1} = · | x_1^t) that estimates the conditional distribution in-context. The optimal target is the Laplacian (add-β) smoothing estimator (n_j + β)/(n + 2β), which is both Bayes-optimal under the Dirichlet prior and minimax-optimal asymptotically.

## Method

The paper introduces MambaZero, a simplified single-layer Mamba retaining only the embedding, time-wise convolution (window w = k+1), linear state recurrence, and linear output projection, and proves (Theorem 1) that it can exactly represent the Laplacian estimator for first-order Markov chains with d = 2S, N = S, and w = 2. The key mechanism is a three-part interplay: (i) the convolution window w ≥ k+1 encodes the current token and its length-k prefix into the input-selective terms, (ii) the state transition factor a_t ≈ 1 accumulates all transition counts in the hidden state H_t, and (iii) orthogonal count-dependent vectors (b^{(ij)⊤}c_t ≈ 0 for i ≠ x_t) select only the relevant transition counts for the current context. Ablations show convolution is more critical than gating or non-linearity for this task, and a lower bound (Theorem 2) establishes that any recurrent architecture with finite bit precision requires hidden dimension d·p ≥ 2^k(1−3ε)log(1/ε) to implement the k-th order Laplacian estimator, explaining why Mamba's exponential-in-k scaling is fundamental rather than an artifact.

## Results

On random Markov chain prediction, a single-layer Mamba (Mamba-2) sharply matches the optimal Laplacian smoothing (add-β) estimator across all Markov orders, with near-zero L1 deviation from the Bayes-optimal predictor. A 1-layer Transformer fails entirely on this task, while a 2-layer Transformer matches the estimator but less sharply. On WikiText-103, Mamba-2 with convolution achieves 27.55 perplexity (14.54M params), outperforming the Transformer baseline (28.67 perplexity, 14.46M params). The theoretical lower bound (Theorem 2) shows that any recurrent architecture requires hidden dimension scaling as Ω(2^k) to represent the k-th order Laplacian estimator, which is empirically confirmed by Fig. 6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between 1-layer Mamba, 1-layer Transformer, and 2-layer Transformer on the Markov-ICL task. 1-layer Mamba matches the optimal Laplacian estimator sharply, while 1-layer Transformer fails to solve the task at all, and 2-layer Transformer matches the estimator but with noticeably larger deviation (consistent with theoretical results showing two layers are required for induction heads). Linear attention performs similarly to softmax attention in this setting. On WikiText-103, Mamba-2 with convolution (27.55 perplexity) outperforms the Transformer with convolution (28.67 perplexity) by ~1.1 points. Ablation shows convolution is more critical for Mamba (11% perplexity increase when removed) than for Transformer (2% increase), highlighting that convolution plays a fundamentally different architectural role in the two families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Markov-ICL framework, originally developed for transformers by Edelman et al. (2024) and subsequent works (Nichani et al., 2024; Rajaraman et al., 2024), to the Mamba/SSM family of architectures. While prior studies of Mamba's in-context learning (Grazzi et al., 2024; Halloran et al., 2024; Akyurek et al., 2024; Park et al., 2024) were largely empirical and produced conflicting conclusions, this work provides the first theoretical characterization of Mamba's representation capacity on Markov processes. The MambaZero simplified model is derived directly from the Mamba-2 architecture (Dao & Gu, 2024), stripping away gating and non-linearities to isolate the role of convolution and recurrence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal connection between Mamba and optimal statistical estimators, revealing that convolution—not the 'selective' gating mechanism Mamba is named for—is the fundamental component enabling in-context counting. The exponential lower bound on hidden dimension (Ω(2^k)) for recurrent architectures versus linear scaling for transformers provides a clear theoretical separation that informs architecture design trade-offs. Open questions include understanding Mamba's learning dynamics (how it converges to the estimator), extending the analysis to deeper models, and whether the convolution-centric mechanism generalizes beyond Markovian structure to more complex sequential dependencies.
