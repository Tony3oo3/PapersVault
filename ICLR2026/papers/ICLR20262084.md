## Problem

VLMs face a fundamental tension when processing near-infinite video streams: full attention incurs quadratic cost and unbounded memory, while sliding-window approaches either break cross-chunk coherence or waste computation on redundant recomputation. Compounding this, existing KV-cache eviction strategies are not aligned with the training phase, so models fine-tuned on short clips degrade or fail when deployed on long streams. The paper asks how to train a VLM to reason stably over infinite video while maintaining real-time, low-latency inference.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a continuous video stream (360P–720P at 24 FPS) as a sequence of 1-second frames, interleaved with text tokens at each second boundary, alongside a system prompt and a rolling buffer of previously generated commentary. Output is per-second narration text synchronized to the stream; seconds with no speech receive a '...' placeholder, teaching the model when to speak and when to stay silent. For VQA, the same streaming context produces a single answer. The KV cache is bounded to a fixed budget (512 sink tokens, 512 recent text tokens, 16 s of vision tokens), so memory and latency stay constant regardless of stream length.

## Method

StreamingVLM couples a streaming-aware KV cache with a contiguous-RoPE positional scheme at inference and an overlapped-chunk full-attention SFT strategy at training. At inference, the cache reuses states of (i) a small set of attention-sink tokens (system prompt + early text), (ii) a long window of the most recent text tokens, and (iii) a short window of the most recent vision tokens; older vision is evicted first, and RoPE indices are left-shifted so positions remain numerically contiguous and in-distribution. At training, long videos are split into overlapping chunks (W = 24 s, O = 12 s) on which full attention is applied, with vision and text tokens interleaved at 1 s intervals and loss computed only on narration-aligned text positions. This overlapped full-attention supervision closely mimics the effective attention pattern at inference (sink + long text window + short vision window), instilling the correct recency bias without ever training on prohibitively long contexts. The model is fine-tuned from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct on 4,000+ hours of GPT-5-cleaned sports commentary (Inf-Streams-Train) followed by a 14 K-sample high-quality annealing set focused on real-time on-field actions.

## Results

On Inf-Streams-Eval (videos averaging 2.12 hours), StreamingVLM in infinite mode achieves a 66.18% pairwise win rate against GPT-4o mini and 99.12% against LiveCC-7B-Instruct in infinite mode. On LiveCC-Sports-3K CC, it reaches 56.19% win rate against LiveCC and 47.33% against LLaVA. On VQA benchmarks, StreamingVLM improves over its base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) by +4.30 on LongVideoBench (59.00 vs. 54.70) and +5.96 on OVOBench Realtime (61.96 vs. 56.00) without any VQA-specific fine-tuning. Efficiency-wise, StreamingVLM maintains stable per-token latency at up to 8 FPS on a single NVIDIA H100, well within the real-time threshold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamingVLM decisively outperforms GPT-4o mini on Inf-Streams-Eval, achieving a 66.18% win rate in infinite mode versus GPT-4o mini's chunked evaluation (GPT-4o mini cannot run in infinite mode). Against LiveCC-7B-Instruct, StreamingVLM wins 87.81% of pairwise comparisons in chunked mode and 99.12% in infinite mode on Inf-Streams-Eval, and 56.19% on LiveCC-Sports-3K CC, indicating a large gap in long-horizon coherence. Compared to its own base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, StreamingVLM improves LongVideoBench by +4.30 points (59.00 vs. 54.70) and OVOBench Realtime by +5.96 points (61.96 vs. 56.00) purely from the streaming SFT, with no VQA-specific tuning. On LiveCC-Sports-3K CC, StreamingVLM also edges out LLaVA-OneVision (47.33% win rate) and Gemini (44.21%), confirming gains across model scales. The one setting where StreamingVLM does not dominate is against LiveCC in infinite mode on the shorter LiveCC-Sports-3K CC clips (56.19% win rate), where both models operate within their training-length comfort zone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-sink + sliding-window recipe from StreamingLLM (Xiao et al., 2024) from text-only LLMs to cross-modal video-language models, introducing contiguous 3D RoPE to handle the interleaved vision-text layout of Qwen-VL. It departs from LiveCC (Chen et al., 2025a), which aligns ASR with video frames for real-time sports commentary but still operates on finite clips, by enabling truly unbounded inference through KV-cache reuse and a training strategy that mimics the streaming attention pattern. The key methodological departure is the overlapped-chunk full-attention SFT strategy, which aligns training supervision with the test-time sink + window context without requiring prohibitively long training sequences, and the interleaved 1-second V/T token layout that replaces the standard all-vision-before-text paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

StreamingVLM provides a practical, deployable recipe (attention sink + asymmetric sliding window + contiguous RoPE + overlapped SFT) for real-time VLM inference at 8 FPS on a single H100, directly relevant to autonomous driving, embodied agents, and live assistants that must process unbounded video. The Inf-Streams-Eval benchmark (2+ hour videos, per-second alignment) fills a gap left by existing benchmarks that test retrieval or summary over long clips rather than dense streaming perception. Limitations include evaluation restricted to sports commentary, a 7B base model with occasional hallucinations, and the high-quality annealing data pipeline's dependence on GPT-5 for curation. Open questions include generalization to non-sports domains, scaling to larger models, and whether the training-inference alignment strategy transfers to other streaming modalities (audio, sensor streams).
