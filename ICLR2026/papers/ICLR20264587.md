## Problem

Specification-driven part retrieval in complex 3D CAD assemblies is challenging for VLMs because assembly metadata sequences often exceed token budgets, and fine-tuning proprietary models (GPT, Gemini) is unavailable. Even when STEP data is converted to images, off-the-shelf models frequently misidentify parts due to the fine-grained relational reasoning required. The paper addresses this gap with a training-free, inference-time adaptation framework that improves VLM reasoning without any weight updates.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 2D rendered images of the full assembly and each individual part (decomposed from STEP files via FreeCAD and rendered with pythonocc) along with a natural-language specification describing a physical, spatial, or functional relationship between parts. Stage 1 produces a structured JSON mapping from part filenames to concise geometric descriptions; Stage 2 outputs a subset of part filenames satisfying the specification, accompanied by a step-by-step chain-of-thought rationale. The two-stage design avoids feeding raw STEP metadata directly to the VLM, sidestepping token-limit issues.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage VLM pipeline augmented by an Error Notebook with RAG. In Stage 1, a VLM generates discriminative part descriptions from assembly and part images. In Stage 2, a second VLM performs specification-aware CoT retrieval. The Error Notebook is built by having the model first produce an initial CoT, then a correction step that identifies the first erroneous step, inserts a natural-language reflection, and continues to the ground-truth answer; a deterministic grammar-constraint (GC) verifier filters trajectories for structural validity (presence of a 'Final Answer:' line with valid filenames). At inference time, RAG retrieves the top-n specification-similar corrected exemplars from the Notebook and prepends them as few-shot demonstrations, guiding the model's reasoning without any training. This cross-model variant—building the Notebook with GPT-4o (Omni) but inferring with a 2B open-source model—closes most of the accuracy gap to GPT-4o mini.

## Results

On the human-preference dataset, GPT-4o (Omni) with Error Notebook + RAG achieves 65.1% overall accuracy, a +23.4-point absolute gain over the 41.7% baseline without Error Notebook. The sGC grammar-constraint verifier adds up to +4.5 further points (66.8%). On the self-generated dataset, the same model improves from 28.5% to 48.3% (+19.8). Gains are consistent across all tested proprietary VLMs (GPT-4o mini +16.1, Gemini 2.0 Flash +12.6, Gemini 2.5 Pro +5.5) and extend to open-source models (Qwen2-VL-2B: 1.5%→10.8%; Aya-Vision-8B: 14%→51%, a 3.6× improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the Error Notebook + RAG method against two standard training-free inference-time baselines (Table 3): standard few-shot (2 exemplars) and self-consistency (5 samples, majority voting, temperature 0.7), all using GPT-4o (Omni). On the human-preference dataset, the proposed method achieves 65.1% overall accuracy, outperforming self-consistency (54.8%) by +10.3 points and standard few-shot (37.7%) by +27.4 points. On the self-generated dataset, the gains are +9.4 over self-consistency (48.3% vs. 38.9%) and +21.7 over standard few-shot (48.3% vs. 26.6%). The method also surpasses its own no-Error-Notebook baseline (41.7%) by a wide margin, confirming that the reflective exemplar retrieval is the primary driver of improvement rather than the two-stage pipeline alone. In the cross-model setting, a 2B open-source model (Qwen2-VL-2B) paired with a GPT-4o (Omni)-constructed Error Notebook + sGC reaches 14.6% on the human-preference <10 group, within ~4 points of GPT-4o mini (18.8% in the same group), demonstrating effective training-free reasoning transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the 'learn from errors' paradigm popularized by LEMMA (Pan et al., 2025) and Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023)—where models are fine-tuned on pairs of flawed and corrected reasoning trajectories—into a fully training-free, inference-time mechanism. Rather than encoding correction patterns into model weights, the authors operationalize them via an Error Notebook (a curated repository of corrected CoTs) combined with RAG, retrieving specification-similar exemplars to condition the VLM's reasoning at test time. The grammar-constraint (GC) verifier is a lightweight deterministic filter borrowed from output-validation ideas in self-correction literature (Zhang et al., 2024; Weng et al., 2023), applied here to ensure structural well-formedness of stored trajectories. The two-stage VLM pipeline (part description generation → specification-aware retrieval) is a practical decomposition motivated by the token-length constraints of raw STEP metadata, building on prior work showing VLMs can generate geometric descriptions of CAD parts (Alrashedy et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete engineering bottleneck—retrieving parts from long, non-natural-language CAD assembly metadata using VLMs—without requiring fine-tuning access to proprietary models, which is a realistic constraint in industrial settings. The cross-model finding (a 2B open-source model approaching GPT-4o mini performance when paired with a GPT-4o-built Error Notebook) suggests that high-quality reasoning traces can serve as a form of inference-time knowledge transfer, potentially reducing dependence on expensive proprietary APIs. However, absolute accuracy remains modest (best ~67% on the human-preference benchmark), the dataset is drawn from a single source (Fusion 360 Gallery), and the Error Notebook construction requires ground-truth labels, limiting applicability to unlabeled or novel assembly types. Open questions include whether the Error Notebook + RAG paradigm generalizes to other long-context, non-natural-language retrieval tasks beyond CAD, and whether the GC verifier can be replaced by learned verifiers for more complex structural constraints.
