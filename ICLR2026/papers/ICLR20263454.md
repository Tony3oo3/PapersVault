## Problem

Existing multimodal LLM benchmarks predominantly evaluate general image comprehension and rarely probe fine-grained human-centric understanding, such as granular facial/body-part perception, human-object interaction, and higher-dimensional causal or emotional reasoning. The few human-related benchmarks that exist suffer from overly simplistic evaluation settings, limited question formats (mostly single-choice), coarse-grained grounding, and low annotation quality, precluding a holistic assessment of MLLMs' capacity in human scenes.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes real-world images (resolutions from below 480p to over 4K, sourced from Pexels, Pixabay, and HICO-DET) paired with text questions spanning 21 question types across 8 dimensions. Model outputs are structured via format-enforcing prompts and parsed with regular expressions into five answer formats: single-choice letters, xyxy bounding-box coordinate tuples, free-text short answers, image ranking sequences (e.g., 'B-A-D-C'), and judgment responses that require either a valid answer or an explicit refusal token (e.g., [-1,-1,-1,-1]). Multi-image questions present up to four images simultaneously, and multi-person questions require the model to disambiguate among multiple individuals within a single frame.

## Method

Human-MME is constructed through a five-step automated annotation pipeline followed by expert manual review. Step 1 uses YOLOv11 and DWPose (134 keypoints) to produce body, face, and body-part bounding boxes; Step 2 queries Qwen2.5-VL-72B for general attributes, clothing items, and human-object interaction triplets; Step 3 refines HOI object boxes via Grounding DINO and disambiguates left/right hands by keypoint-to-object distance; Step 4 applies FaceXFormer for 42 binary facial attributes and 68-landmark-derived facial part boxes; Step 5 uses Qwen2.5-VL-72B followed by Qwen3 (a separate text-only model to avoid same-model bias) to generate identity-neutral emotion, intention, past-cause, and future-consequence narratives. A custom Gradio-based interface then enables cluster-level de-duplication and instance-level correction by expert annotators. From the resulting 16,765 annotated images, 19,945 question-answer pairs are generated via templates that combine features across dimensions, including composite formats like Judgment+Bounding Box and Short-Answer+Bounding Box. The benchmark is then used to evaluate 20 state-of-the-art open-source and proprietary MLLMs deployed via VLLM or official APIs, with metrics tailored per component (accuracy, BERT F1 + cosine similarity + keyword coverage, IoU, Kendall's Tau, and F1 for judgment).

## Results

Among 20 evaluated MLLMs, GLM-4.5V achieves the highest overall average score of 76.0, leading on most vision-heavy dimensions including Face Understanding (61.6), Body Understanding (77.4), HOI Understanding (82.5), and Multi-Person Reasoning (71.5). Qwen2.5-VL-72B (72.8 avg) surpasses all others on higher-level reasoning tasks, scoring 88.1 on Intention Discrimination and 86.3 on Causal Discrimination. Among closed-source models, Gemini-2.5-Pro leads at 67.6 average, while GPT-4o (58.1) and GPT-5 (63.3) trail significantly behind top open-source models, particularly on Bounding Box tasks where GPT-4o scores only 11.5 IoU versus GLM-4.5V's 66.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLM-4.5V outperforms all other open-source models on perception-heavy dimensions, scoring 61.6 on Face Understanding (vs. 55.2 for GLM-4.1V-9B and 61.1 for Qwen2.5-VL-72B) and 82.5 on HOI Understanding, while also leading on Bounding Box (66.3 IoU) and Short-Answer (83.5). Qwen2.5-VL-72B dominates higher-level reasoning, achieving 88.1 on Intention Discrimination and 86.3 on Causal Discrimination, surpassing GLM-4.5V (83.9/85.4) and all other models. Among closed-source models, Gemini-2.5-Pro (67.6 avg) consistently outperforms GPT-4o (58.1) and GPT-5 (63.3), excelling on Ranking (90.9) and Choice (72.4) but still trailing GLM-4.5V on Bounding Box (23.5 vs. 66.3). All proprietary GPT models show severe grounding weakness, with GPT-4o scoring only 11.5 IoU on Bounding Box and near-zero on fine-grained facial parts (e.g., 0.000 on left eye), while GPT-5 and GPT-5-mini also exhibit notable refusal rates (3.3% and 5.8% average) due to safety alignment. Intern-S1 is a notable exception among open-source models, achieving the best Multi-Image Understanding (79.8) and Emotion Discrimination (68.3) scores despite having only 22.1 Bounding Box IoU, indicating strong high-level reasoning without fine-grained spatial grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the MLLM evaluation landscape by introducing the first benchmark that systematically evaluates human-centric image understanding across a progressive hierarchy of eight dimensions, from fine-grained facial/body grounding to high-level intention, causal, and emotional reasoning. It departs from prior human-centric benchmarks (Face-Human-Bench, HumaniBench, HumanVBench, HV-MMBench) by combining fine-grained spatial grounding, multi-image and multi-person scenarios, and diverse answer formats (choice, bounding box, short-answer, ranking, judgment) into a single unified evaluation. Methodologically, it repurposes a pipeline of specialized models (YOLOv11, DWPose, Grounding DINO, FaceXFormer, Qwen2.5-VL) for structured automated annotation rather than relying on a single LLM or manual labeling, enabling scalable yet high-fidelity feature extraction across 43 fine-grained visual scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Human-MME provides the field with a comprehensive, reproducible evaluation suite that exposes a critical blind spot: even top proprietary models (GPT-4o, GPT-5) achieve near-zero IoU on fine-grained facial and body-part grounding, while open-source models with explicit grounding training (GLM-4.5V) substantially outperform them. The benchmark also reveals that adding a judgment/abstention component paradoxically reduces task accuracy, and that all models struggle with left-right limb discrimination despite reliable facial left-right performance. Open questions include how to improve fine-grained spatial grounding in proprietary MLLMs without sacrificing high-level reasoning, how to mitigate the hallucination tendency exposed by judgment tasks, and whether the progressive evaluation structure can be extended to video or embodied settings.
