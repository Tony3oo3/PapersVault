## Problem

Existing VLA efficiency methods reduce visual token redundancy with static or single-layer heuristics, treating all manipulation steps uniformly. However, visual redundancy varies substantially across manipulation stages—coarse relocation phases tolerate aggressive pruning while fine-grained grasping requires full visual detail—and this variation is strongly correlated with end-effector motion dynamics. The paper addresses the gap of stage-aware, action-conditioned token pruning that adapts retention to the robot's current manipulation phase.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 640×480 RGB scene image, a matching RGB gripper/wrist image, a natural-language task instruction, and a proprioceptive state vector, all encoded into a shared latent space by DINOv2/SigLIP and a text tokenizer. The end-effector trajectory from the most recent decoded action chunk (8 steps of 7-DoF increments: Δx, Δy, Δz, Δφ, Δθ, Δψ, gripper) is additionally used as a gating signal. Output is a parallel-decoded action chunk of 8 consecutive 7-DoF actions predicted simultaneously at placeholder positions, following the OpenVLA-OFT paradigm with 256-bin continuous discretization per dimension.

## Method

ADP is a training-free, plug-and-play pruning framework with two complementary components. First, text-driven anticipatory pruning computes per-token importance scores via cross-attention between text queries and vision keys at layer 0, then retains the top-k most relevant visual tokens (with a 4:6 per-view allocation between scene and wrist) before the sequence enters the LLM backbone. Second, an action-aware dynamic gating mechanism computes a windowed trajectory distance by applying forward kinematics to the decoded 7-DoF action chunk, then uses a binary decision rule (comparing current window displacement against either the running mean or the adjacent-extrema of recent windows) to switch between pruned and full-vision states: high motion triggers pruning to suppress redundancy, while low motion disables pruning to preserve fine-grained visual context. A safety constraint forces full vision after three consecutive pruned windows to limit error accumulation. The approach achieves up to 1.35× speedup on OpenVLA-OFT with ≤0.9% success-rate degradation, and a 1.49× real-robot speedup while improving average success rate.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, VLA-ADP at a 50% keep ratio achieves 96.3% average success rate (99.4% Spatial, 98.0% Object, 96.4% Goal, 91.2% Long) with 6.43T FLOPs and 1.23× speedup over OpenVLA-OFT (97.1% avg, 7.91T FLOPs). At a more aggressive 30% keep ratio, it maintains 94.4% average success while reducing FLOPs to 5.85T and achieving 1.35× speedup. In real-world experiments on a Jaco2 robot, VLA-ADP improves average success from 85.8% to 88.3% while cutting inference latency from 76.9ms to 51.8ms (1.49× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the directly comparable OFT-based methods, VLA-ADP (50%) substantially outperforms FastV (+OFT) on Object (98.0% vs. 81.0%) and Long (91.2% vs. 73.0%), while matching or exceeding it on Spatial and Goal, with a slightly higher speedup (1.23× vs. 1.24×) at comparable FLOPs. Against the OpenVLA-OFT baseline, VLA-ADP (50%) trades a modest 0.8-point drop in average success (96.3% vs. 97.1%) for a 19% FLOPs reduction and 1.23× speedup; at 70% keep ratio the gap narrows to 0.8 points with 1.13× speedup. Among training-free AR baselines on the original OpenVLA checkpoint, VLA-ADP's approach is not directly comparable due to different decoding, but the paper shows that static pruning methods (SparseVLM, FastV, VLA-Cache, FlashVLA, SP-VLA) all suffer 2–12 point drops on Long-horizon tasks, whereas ADP's dynamic gating preserves Long performance (91.2% at 50% ratio vs. 73–54% for AR baselines). In real-world evaluation, VLA-ADP beats the OpenVLA-OFT baseline on 3 of 4 tasks (Task 1: 90.0% vs. 83.3%; Task 3: 90.0% vs. 86.7%; Task 4: 83.3% vs. 80.0%) while slightly underperforming on Task 2 (90.0% vs. 93.3%), yielding a net 2.5-point average improvement alongside 1.49× latency reduction. The paper also demonstrates that random pruning at 50% keep ratio achieves only 73.0% on Object and 76.2% on Long, confirming that the text-driven relevance scoring is essential rather than incidental.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OpenVLA-OFT's parallel-decoding VLA architecture and extends the line of training-free visual token pruning methods (FastV, EfficientVLA, VLA-Cache, SP-VLA) by introducing an action-aware dynamic gating mechanism. The key conceptual departure is the observation that visual token redundancy is not static or purely text-conditioned but varies systematically across manipulation stages—high during coarse relocation, low during fine-grained grasping—and can be captured from end-effector trajectory statistics via forward kinematics. The text-driven pruning component (cross-attention-based Top-K selection) is closely related to FastV and EfficientVLA, but the dynamic on/off switching driven by windowed trajectory distance is the novel contribution that distinguishes ADP from prior static or single-layer heuristic pruning approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADP offers a simple, plug-and-play path to reducing VLA inference cost (up to 1.35× speedup, ~44% FLOPs reduction) without retraining, which is practically valuable for real-time robotic deployment. However, the method shows a notable limitation on long-horizon compositional tasks (up to −30% on one Long-suite task), revealing that current VLA backbones lack explicit subgoal awareness and that static text-relevance scoring cannot dynamically reallocate attention across sequential subtasks. Open questions include whether the action-aware gating generalizes beyond OpenVLA-OFT to diffusion-based or autoregressive VLAs, whether multi-view (wrist camera) settings benefit equally, and whether subgoal-estimation mechanisms could close the long-horizon gap.
