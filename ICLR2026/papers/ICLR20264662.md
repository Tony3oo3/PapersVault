## Problem

Offline goal-conditioned RL methods struggle with long-horizon tasks in multi-entity domains due to combinatorial state-space growth and compounding value approximation error under sparse rewards. Existing hierarchical approaches like HIQL do not exploit the factored structure of multi-entity environments, producing subgoals that modify many entities simultaneously rather than sparse, independently controllable subsets, making them harder for the low-level policy to reach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264662/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes entity-centric state and goal representations—either ground-truth 3D positions/yaw angles or unsupervised DLPv2 latent particles extracted from multi-view 128×128 RGB images—and produces low-level actions (5-DoF gripper deltas) alongside entity-factored subgoals as sets of latent particle configurations. The subgoal diffuser conditions on state and goal entity sets as clean (non-denoised) inputs and outputs a single subgoal entity set, with each entity encoded via learned additive embeddings indicating its affiliation to the state, goal, or noisy subgoal set.

## Method

HECRL employs a two-level hierarchy where a value-based GCRL agent (IQL with Entity Interaction Transformer) handles low-level control and an 8-layer Transformer-based conditional diffusion model generates subgoals by modeling p(subgoal | state, goal) over K-step-ahead states from the offline dataset. At test time, N subgoal candidates are sampled from the diffuser, filtered by a value-based reachability threshold (policy competence radius), and the candidate closest to the goal is selected for a fixed rollout horizon before re-planning. The key insight is that entity-centric diffusion—where each entity is a separate Transformer token—naturally encourages sparse, factored subgoals that modify only a subset of entities, because the attention mechanism selectively copies input tokens to the output rather than producing weighted averages across all entities. The two levels are trained independently on the same offline dataset and composed post hoc, making the framework modular and compatible with any value-based GCRL algorithm.

## Results

On the most challenging task, PPP-Cube from images, EC-SGIQL achieves 64.3% success rate, a 157% improvement over the flat entity-centric baseline EC-IQL (25.0%). Across all five environment variants, EC-SGIQL consistently outperforms or matches all baselines, with the largest margins on image-based tasks where non-entity-centric methods (HIQL, IQL) fail entirely (0% on PPP-Cube Image). On state-based PPP-Cube, EC-SGIQL reaches 82.5% vs. 51.5% for EC-IQL and 34.3% for IQL. The method also generalizes zero-shot to 4–6 objects, maintaining 25.7–65.3% success on PPP-Cube (State) where EC-IQL drops to 10.5–31.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EC-SGIQL dominates EC-IQL (the flat entity-centric IQL agent) on every task, with the largest gap on PPP-Cube (Image) at 64.3% vs. 25.0% (+157%) and PPP-Cube (State) at 82.5% vs. 51.5% (+60%). Against EC-Diffuser (entity-centric behavioral cloning), EC-SGIQL wins on all tasks except Stack-Cube (State) where they are statistically tied (43.5% vs. 43.8%); the gap is most dramatic on image-based tasks where EC-Diffuser collapses to near-zero (0.3% on PPP-Cube Image). HIQL, the prior hierarchical offline GCRL method, achieves 0% on both PPP-Cube (Image) and Stack-Cube (State) and remains well below EC-SGIQL on all other tasks (e.g., 8.3% vs. 61.5% on Scene Image). Standard IQL without entity-centric structure is the weakest baseline, scoring 0% on PPP-Cube (Image) and at most 34.3% on PPP-Cube (State), confirming that both the entity-centric representation and the hierarchical subgoal diffusion are necessary for strong performance in these combinatorial multi-entity domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate lines of work: hierarchical offline GCRL (HIQL, Park et al. 2023) and entity-centric reinforcement learning (Haramati et al. 2024; Qi et al. 2025). The key departure from HIQL is replacing its AWR-based high-level subgoal policy with an entity-centric conditional diffusion model, which the authors show is critical for producing valid, sparse subgoals rather than weighted averages of modes. The modular design—training the subgoal diffuser independently and composing it post hoc with any value-based GCRL agent via test-time value filtering—differs from prior hierarchical methods that tightly couple training across levels. The unsupervised object-centric image representation (DLPv2) follows the pretrain-then-freeze paradigm established by Daniel & Tamar (2022, 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that combinatorial state-space complexity in multi-entity manipulation can be substantially mitigated by combining factored representations with hierarchical subgoal decomposition, achieving over 150% success rate improvements on the hardest task without retraining the low-level policy. The finding that entity-centric diffusion naturally produces sparse subgoals (modifying ~1 entity vs. ~3 for regression-based alternatives) provides a useful inductive bias insight for future hierarchical methods. Limitations include reliance on a sufficiently accurate value function for subgoal filtering, a fixed K hyperparameter for subgoal horizon, and dependence on quality unsupervised object-centric representations—scaling to real-world in-the-wild scenarios remains open. The modular framework and new multi-entity benchmark variants (PPP-Cube, Stack-Cube, Push-Tetris) provide useful tools for the community.
