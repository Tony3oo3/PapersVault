## Problem

Despite strong general video understanding, MLLMs struggle with fine-grained motion perception because they process spatial regions with uniform importance and lack mechanisms to discern subtle inter-frame variations. Naively transferring image-level visual prompts (e.g., background blur) to video paradoxically degrades motion understanding due to loss of temporal context. Additionally, no large-scale, open-source dataset with hierarchical annotations exists specifically for fine-grained video motion understanding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (sampled at 1 fps or fixed frame counts) and a natural-language question about motion, producing a structured answer that explicitly separates object motion from camera motion. The pipeline first uses the MLLM to infer relevant object categories from the query, then applies GroundingDINO for detection and SAM 2 for tracking across frames. The enhanced video (with spotlight or motion blur applied) is fed back to the MLLM alongside a routing-aware prompt template that describes the visual modification, and the model outputs a single answer letter or free-form description.

## Method

MotionSight is a zero-shot visual prompting framework that decouples object and camera motion through two distinct enhancement functions. For object motion, it applies a 'visual spotlight' that darkens the background outside dynamically aggregated bounding boxes (stabilized by a temporal aggregator that adapts its window to intra-trajectory positional variance), inspired by stage-lighting patterns in pre-training data. For camera motion, it synthesizes motion blur via a temporally weighted aggregation of N preceding frames with a geometrically decaying kernel (γ=0.65, N=7), which amplifies inter-frame differences that MLLMs otherwise miss. The two pathways are gated by the query type, and a routing-aware prompt template informs the MLLM of the visual modification. To convert this capability into training data, the authors curate MotionVid-QA (~40K clips, ~87K QAs) with SFT and DPO subsets, then fine-tune Qwen2.5VL-7B into MotionChat, achieving 48.3% on FAVOR-Bench—comparable to Qwen2.5VL-72B—while running 5–6× faster.

## Results

On MotionBench, InternVL3-78B + MotionSight achieves 63.0% Overall accuracy (vs. 61.5% baseline), setting the new open-source SOTA and surpassing the proprietary GLM-4V-Plus-0111 (62.8%). Qwen2.5VL-7B + MotionSight improves from 53.0% to 55.6% Overall, with camera motion (CM) jumping from 34.0% to 48.3% (+14.3%). On FAVOR-Bench, the fine-tuned MotionChat (Qwen2.5VL-7B + SFT + DPO on MotionVid-QA) reaches 48.3% Overall, matching Qwen2.5VL-72B (48.1%) while being ~5–6× faster and far more memory-efficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InternVL3-78B + MotionSight (63.0% Overall) outperforms all open-source baselines on MotionBench, including TE Fusion (58.0%), Qwen2.5VL-72B (58.3%), and its own base model InternVL3-78B (61.5%), and narrowly edges out the proprietary GLM-4V-Plus-0111 (62.8%). Qwen2.5VL-7B + MotionSight (55.6%) beats its base (53.0%) by +2.6% Overall and +14.3% on camera motion, but still trails the 72B variant (58.3%) and proprietary models. On FAVOR-Bench, MotionChat (48.3%) matches Qwen2.5VL-72B (48.1%) and surpasses GPT-4o (42.1%) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (43.7%), though it falls short of Gemini-1.5-Pro (49.9%). The zero-shot MotionSight consistently improves every backbone tested (Qwen2.5VL-7B, InternVL3-8B/78B) across both benchmarks, with the largest relative gains on camera-motion sub-metrics, confirming the decoupling strategy's effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes visual prompting techniques from the static image domain (CPT, Set-of-Mark, Fine-grained Visual Prompting, Attention Prompting) for video, but explicitly demonstrates that naive transfer fails: background blur, the best-performing image prompt, paradoxically degrades fine-grained motion understanding. The key conceptual departure is the decoupling of object motion (addressed via a spotlight-style visual prompt on tracked bounding boxes from GroundingDINO + SAM 2) from camera motion (addressed via synthetic temporal motion blur), a framing not present in prior motion benchmarks like MotionBench or FAVOR-Bench. The dataset contribution builds on public video sources (Kinetics-700, ActivityNet, Charades, OpenVid-1M) but introduces the first large-scale, hierarchically structured (SFT + DPO) annotation specifically targeting fine-grained motion semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MotionVid-QA fills a clear gap as the first large-scale open-source dataset for fine-grained video motion understanding, and the decoupling framework (object vs. camera) provides a clean conceptual lens that prior MLLM-based video understanding lacks. The practical finding that a 7B fine-tuned model (MotionChat) matches a 72B baseline on FAVOR-Bench while being 5–6× faster is useful for deployment. Limitations include dependence on object detection accuracy (diminishing returns in object-free scenes) and the zero-shot method's moderate absolute gains (~3–4%), leaving open whether the decoupling strategy generalizes to more complex multi-agent or long-horizon motion scenarios.
