## Problem

Existing 3D affordance methods are restricted to contact-based analysis, neglecting equally important aspects of human-object interaction such as body-part orientation relative to objects and spatial occupancy patterns around them. Moreover, prior comprehensive-affordance approaches (e.g., COMA) depend on clean watertight meshes and 2D-to-3D uplifting pipelines that are fragile, computationally expensive, and fail to generalize to noisy real-world point clouds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed object point cloud (downsampled to 512 points via FPS) and a canonical T-pose SMPL human point cloud (also 512 points) as input, requiring no mesh, normals, or full scan. It outputs a dense flow field of N_H × 3 per-point displacement vectors that deform the T-pose into a plausible interaction pose, from which three per-pair affordance scores are derived: contact (scalar per human-object point pair), orientational (entropy-based scalar per pair), and spatial (a 3D voxel occupancy grid of size H×W×L around the object). At inference, multiple flow samples are drawn from the learned distribution to capture multi-modal interaction patterns.

## Method

H2OFlow trains a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) to model the conditional distribution p(F | O) over dense flows, where F is the per-point displacement from a fixed T-pose to a goal interaction pose. Training data consists of synthetic HOI mesh sequences generated by the CHOIS 3D generative model, subsampled to paired point clouds via FPS, with random rotation and occlusion augmentation for real-world robustness. The DiT backbone uses shared-weight MLP encoders to extract per-point features, applies self-attention on concatenated human-flow tokens for local reasoning, and cross-attention to object tokens for global interaction modeling, with a hybrid noise + KL loss following Nichol & Dhariwal. At inference, sampled flows reconstruct goal human point clouds, and the three affordance types are computed: contact via exponential distance weighted by DiT cross-attention scores, orientation via negated Shannon entropy of cross-product direction vectors (displacement × flow) discretized on the unit sphere, and spatial via expected voxel occupancy. The use of dense flows as an intermediate representation sidesteps the need for watertight meshes and surface normals, while the diffusion formulation naturally captures the multi-modality of human-object interactions (e.g., left vs. right hand grasp).

## Results

On the OMOMO test set, H2OFlow achieves 72.3% SIM-H and 81.0% SIM-O for contact similarity, 0.11 MAE-H and 0.07 MAE-O for contact error, 75.6% Precision@K for orientational affordance, and 0.12 MSE for spatial affordance. These results substantially outperform the strongest baseline COMA (41.3% SIM-H, 56.9% SIM-O, 42.9% Precision@K) and the direct SMPL-prediction variant H2OSMPL (57.3% SIM-H, 68.0% SIM-O). On the BEHAVE dataset, H2OFlow with fine-tuning reaches 74.4% SIM-H and 80.1% SIM-O, exceeding COMA's 52.8% and 70.6% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against COMA (Kim et al., ECCV 2024), the primary comprehensive-affordance baseline, H2OFlow wins on every metric: 72.3% vs. 41.3% SIM-H, 81.0% vs. 56.9% SIM-O, and 75.6% vs. 42.9% Precision@K on OMOMO. COMA's performance degrades catastrophically when fed reconstructed meshes from point clouds (COMA-Recon drops to 20.7% SIM-H and 9.1% Precision@K), whereas H2OFlow is point-cloud-native and robust to occlusion (only ~1% drop at 50% occlusion). On the BEHAVE dataset, H2OFlow without fine-tuning already matches COMA (55.3% vs. 52.8% SIM-H), and with fine-tuning exceeds it by ~22 points (74.4% vs. 52.8% SIM-H). Against contact-only baselines IAGNet (Yang et al., ICCV 2023) and DECO (Tripathi et al., ICCV 2023) on BEHAVE, H2OFlow-FT achieves 80.1% SIM-O vs. IAGNet's 64.3% and 74.4% SIM-H vs. DECO's 23.2%, confirming that the comprehensive (contact + orientation + spatial) formulation substantially outperforms contact-only methods. H2OFlow also uses ~14% of COMA's GPU memory (8.4 GB vs. 9.8 GB) and runs ~10× faster (6.7 s vs. 65.2 s per object on a V100).

## Relation to Prior Work

H2OFlow builds on COMA (Kim et al., 2024), which first formulated comprehensive affordance (contact, orientational, and spatial) but relied on 2D-to-3D uplifting of synthetic images and watertight mesh normals. It repurposes dense flow representations from articulated-object manipulation (FlowBot3D, Eisner et al. 2022; Xu et al. 2024) and non-rigid placement (Cai et al. 2024) as an intermediate human-pose representation, and uses 3D HOI generative models (CHOIS, Li et al. 2024a) purely as a synthetic data source rather than a direct inference pipeline. The key departure is replacing mesh-based per-vertex computation with a diffusion-transformer (DiT) that learns a distribution over per-point displacements on sparse point clouds, eliminating the need for surface normals, watertight meshes, and 2D rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By operating natively on point clouds and learning affordances from synthetic data without manual labels, H2OFlow removes two major bottlenecks in affordance research: labor-intensive contact annotation and the fragility of mesh-based pipelines on real-world scans. The 10× speedup and ~2× memory reduction over COMA make comprehensive affordance inference practical for downstream robot planning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to fine-grained articulated objects (limited by the underlying generative model's object diversity), whether prompt-conditioned flows (Section P.2) can replace the need for dataset augmentation, and how well the learned affordances transfer to actual robot manipulation policies via the cross-embodiment formulation in Section N.2.
