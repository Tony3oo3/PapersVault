## Problem

3D fluorescence microscopy of live cells is constrained by phototoxicity, forcing sparse equidistant Z-axis sampling that produces anisotropic, noisy volumes. Existing inverse-problem-based restoration methods assume known linear degradation models and become ill-posed under the nonlinear, incomplete, and high-noise conditions inherent to FM imaging, yielding unstable or hallucinated reconstructions. This paper reframes the recovery task as a tensor completion problem and provides the first theoretical lower bound for exact 3D cell tensor recovery under FM sampling conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263961/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed 3D fluorescence microscopy tensor (I₁ × I₂ × I₃) where Z-axis slices have been sparsely sampled (e.g., 30, 94, or 200 planes) and corrupted by additive sparse noise, and produces a fully reconstructed, denoised 3D cell volume with restored axial resolution. No paired high-quality reference volumes are required; the method is entirely unsupervised, relying on the low-rank structural redundancy of the cell tensor itself. The Tucker multilinear rank is fixed per acquisition protocol via truncated HOSVD on training data, avoiding test-time hyperparameter search.

## Method

The paper formulates 3D FM cell recovery as a robust tensor completion problem minimizing the tensor nuclear norm plus an L1 sparse-noise penalty subject to observed-entry constraints, solved via Tucker decomposition (core tensor × three factor matrices) with an ADMM alternating-update scheme over the core tensor, factor matrices, sparse error, and dual variable. The key theoretical contribution is a derived lower bound showing that |Ω| ≥ O((rI^{3/2} + r²I) log²I) observed entries suffice for exact recovery, validated empirically on the datasets. The central insight is that the ADMM iterative sequence (Tucker projection → soft-thresholded noise estimation → Lagrangian residual accumulation) is mathematically equivalent to the reverse trajectory of a conditional diffusion process, where the Tucker manifold projection acts as a score-guided denoising step and the observation tensor provides the conditioning signal. This equivalence yields a principled generative framework that achieves denoised, geometrically coherent 3D reconstruction without learning a neural score function, instead using manifold projections and proximal operators as the denoising mechanism.

## Results

On SR-CACO-2, the proposed method achieves PSNR 40.30 dB, SSIM 0.9476, and LPIPS 0.2305, outperforming the closest baseline DDPM (40.24 / 0.9447 / 0.3679) and Cycle+IPG (39.82 / 0.9428 / 0.3369). On the more challenging C. elegans in vivo dataset 1 (30 axial planes, high noise), it reaches PSNR 33.18 dB and SSIM 0.6682, surpassing Cycle+HAT (32.37 / 0.6236) and DDPM (31.49 / 0.6157). On C. elegans dataset 2 (94 planes), it attains PSNR 40.95 dB, SSIM 0.9868, and LPIPS 0.2674, leading all baselines by a clear margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all three evaluation datasets, with the largest margins on the noisier C. elegans-1 (30 axial planes) where it leads Cycle+HAT by 0.81 dB PSNR and 0.0446 SSIM. Against DDPM (the closest generative baseline), the gains are consistent: +0.06 dB PSNR and −0.1376 LPIPS on SR-CACO-2, and +1.09 dB PSNR and −0.1034 LPIPS on C. elegans-1. On the no-reference NIQE metric, the method wins on both C. elegans datasets (17.89 vs. 18.95; 18.74 vs. 18.93) but is slightly behind Cycle+HAT on SR-CACO-2 (8.66 vs. 8.56). In the broader SR-CACO-2 comparison (Table 5), the method outperforms 17 classical and deep SR baselines including VDSR (40.25 dB), DFCAN (40.19 dB), and SwinIR (39.65 dB) in mean PSNR, while also achieving the lowest mean NRMSE (0.0254) and highest mean SSIM (0.9475). The method's advantage is most pronounced in perceptual quality (LPIPS) and structural fidelity (SSIM), while distortion-based metrics (PSNR) show smaller but consistent improvements, suggesting the tensor-completion prior primarily suppresses hallucinated structures and preserves cross-slice geometric coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes 3D fluorescence microscopy restoration as a tensor completion problem rather than the conventional inverse-problem formulation, building on Tucker decomposition (Kolda & Bader, 2009) and ADMM-based solvers (Bai et al., 2016) for low-rank recovery. Its most distinctive conceptual contribution is establishing a mathematical equivalence between the ADMM iterative solution of the robust tensor completion objective and a conditional diffusion reverse process (Ho et al., 2020), thereby unifying an optimization-based recovery framework with a generative modeling perspective. The exact-recovery lower bound adapts the dual-certificate/golfing-scheme machinery from Yuan & Zhang (2016) to the noisy, sparse-observation setting specific to FM sampling. This departs from prior FM restoration work (CycleGAN, IPG, self-supervised denoisers) by arguing that linear inverse-problem models are fundamentally ill-posed under the nonlinear, incomplete observations inherent to live-cell volumetric imaging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical lower bound for exact 3D cell tensor recovery under equidistant Z-axis sampling, giving practitioners a principled criterion for whether their acquisition protocol captures sufficient information. The tensor-completion-to-diffusion equivalence offers a conceptual bridge that could inform future generative restoration methods beyond this specific domain. However, the method is evaluated only on fluorescence microscopy data with a fixed rank selected via HOSVD, and the performance gains over strong baselines (e.g., DDPM, Cycle+IPG) are moderate rather than transformative. Open questions include generalization to other bio-imaging modalities, adaptive rank selection for the observed bimodal rank distribution, and computational efficiency of the iterative ADMM solver for real-time applications.
