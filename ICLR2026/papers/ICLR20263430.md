## Problem

Diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) offer parallel decoding potential but open-source models like LLaDA and Dream default to greedy one-token-per-step decoding, making them slower than autoregressive baselines. Naive parallel decoding suffers from the 'curse of parallel decoding,' where simultaneously predicting dependent tokens causes distributional drift and quality degradation. Existing confidence-based parallel decoding methods ignore how the spatial distribution of undecoded positions affects prediction quality, leaving a gap between achievable speed and maintained accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a generated text sequence by operating as a training-free decoding strategy on top of existing masked diffusion models (e.g., LLaDA-Instruct-8B, LLaDA-1.5-8B, Dream-7B). The prompt is concatenated with blocks of masked tokens (default block size 32, generation length 512), and the model iteratively denoises these blocks. Efficiency is measured in tokens per forward call (TPF) and throughput per second (TPS), with the method achieving up to 17× speedup over vanilla decoding while maintaining or improving task accuracy.

## Method

Hierarchy-dLLM applies a divide-and-conquer principle to parallel decoding: it recursively partitions masked spans into smaller sub-decoding areas using already-decoded tokens as anchors, so that undecoded tokens remain sparsely scattered rather than forming consecutive blocks. The preliminary study shows that sparse masking yields significantly lower KL divergence from step-by-step generation than consecutive masking, because scattered undecoded positions allow bidirectional attention to access reliable contextual signals from both sides. Within each sub-area, a progressive confidence-based decoding rule commits tokens above a high threshold, falls back to the most confident token above a low threshold, guarantees at least one global decode per step, and applies a remasking step to revise low-confidence predictions. This hierarchical structure enables O(log n)-level parallelism while preserving the conditional independence assumption that naive parallel decoding violates.

## Results

On LLaDA-1.5-8B, Hierarchy-dLLM achieves the best accuracy across all five benchmarks (GSM8K: 83.70, Math500: 41.60, HumanEval: 45.12, MBPP: 40.40, IF-Eval: 59.15) while simultaneously delivering the highest throughput. Speedups reach up to 17.23× in TPS over vanilla decoding (MBPP) and 7.45× on GSM8K, with TPF gains of 4.25–6.54× over vanilla. Compared to Fast-dLLM, Hierarchy-dLLM is approximately 1.5× faster in TPS across all tasks while matching or exceeding accuracy by 0.2–1.8 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Fast-dLLM, Hierarchy-dLLM wins on both accuracy and speed across all five benchmarks on LLaDA-1.5-8B: it improves GSM8K by +0.38 (83.70 vs 83.32), Math500 by +2.2 (41.60 vs 39.40), and HumanEval by +3.05 (45.12 vs 42.07), while tying on MBPP (40.40) and leading IF-Eval by +0.92. In throughput, Hierarchy-dLLM consistently achieves 1.3–1.5× higher TPS than Fast-dLLM (e.g., 14.83 vs 9.54 on GSM8K, 44.18 vs 27.45 on HumanEval). Against WINO, Hierarchy-dLLM is uniformly faster in both TPF and TPS and avoids WINO's significant accuracy drops (e.g., WINO loses 1.92 points on GSM8K and 5.4 points on MBPP, while Hierarchy-dLLM gains or holds). On the Dream-7B model, Hierarchy-dLLM still delivers the highest TPF and TPS across all four benchmarks, though both it and Fast-dLLM show larger accuracy drops than on LLaDA, attributed to Dream's autoregressive origin providing weaker inherent support for parallel decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of training-free parallel decoding strategies for diffusion LLMs initiated by Fast-dLLM (confidence-aware parallel decoding) and WINO (revocable decoding). While prior methods select which tokens to decode based solely on confidence thresholds, Hierarchy-dLLM introduces a spatial/positional dimension: it recursively partitions masked spans into sub-decoding areas using already-decoded tokens as anchors, inspired by divide-and-conquer. The empirical motivation—a preliminary KL-divergence study showing that sparsely scattered undecoded tokens produce more stable predictions than contiguous masked blocks—is novel and directly motivates the hierarchical partitioning design, positioning this as the first position-based decoding framework for dLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, training-free acceleration that can be plugged into existing dLLMs (LLaDA, Dream) without retraining, achieving up to 17× speedup over vanilla decoding and ~1.5× over Fast-dLLM while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy. The spatial-structure insight (sparse masking reduces distributional drift) provides a useful diagnostic for understanding why naive parallel decoding fails and could inform future dLLM training objectives. However, the gains over the strongest baseline (Fast-dLLM) are modest, and on AR-derived models like Dream-7B the accuracy drop is comparable to or worse than Fast-dLLM, suggesting the approach benefits most from natively bidirectional architectures. The authors note that post-training alignment between the model distribution and hierarchical decoding remains an open direction that could further close the gap.
