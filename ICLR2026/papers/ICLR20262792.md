## Problem

Previous reasoning distillation work focuses on downstream performance metrics but provides no explanatory analysis of where the distilled model's capabilities actually originate. It remains unclear whether a distilled student genuinely inherits the teacher's reasoning patterns in novel test contexts or simply regresses to its original output distribution, leaving the generalization mechanism of distillation unexplained.

## Input / Output

The provenance tracing framework takes a sentence-segmented trajectory produced by the distilled model on a test question and re-feeds it into three models (teacher, original student, distilled model) to obtain per-sentence probabilities under identical context; sentence-level probability is computed as the geometric mean of per-token probabilities to sidestep vocabulary mismatches between models. For the data selection component, the method takes multiple candidate teacher responses per training question and outputs a single selected response, prioritized by its absolute count of Teacher Sentences (those where Δ_TS > β).

## Method

The core mechanism is cross-model probability comparison: for each sentence in a distilled model's output, the framework computes the probability assigned by the teacher, original student, and distilled model under the same context, then classifies the sentence into four provenance categories (Teacher, Student, Shared, Boosted) using two thresholds α and β applied to pairwise probability differences (Δ_TS, Δ_SD, Δ_TD) in a fixed evaluation order (Shared → Teacher → Student → Boosted). The key empirical finding is that Teacher Sentences are disproportionately present in early reasoning steps and correlate with answer correctness, while Boosted Sentences (pre-existing patterns amplified by distillation) are not uniformly beneficial across model sizes. Building on this, the paper proposes a teacher-guided data selection strategy that, before training, compares teacher-student divergences on candidate responses and selects those with the highest absolute count of Teacher Sentences, explicitly leveraging cross-model divergence rather than student-only alignment as in GRAPE. The threshold β is determined adaptively via a histogram-overlap minimization procedure (Algorithm 1) that requires no training runs, making the selection criterion principled and training-free.

## Results

The proposed teacher-guided data selection method achieves the best average accuracy across all four teacher-student-data configurations, improving over the Vanilla baseline by 1.7%–2.5% on average (e.g., 58.7 vs. 56.2 for DeepSeek-R1 + Qwen3-4B-Base on AceReason-1.1-SFT). On the strongest setting (GPT-OSS-120B + Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507), the method reaches 76.4% average accuracy, up from 74.0% (Vanilla) and 74.6% (GRAPE). Gains are most pronounced on AIME24 and AIME25 (up to +4.9 and +5.8 points respectively), while MATH500 and OlympiadBench show smaller but consistent improvements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is GRAPE (Zhang et al., 2025), which selects training responses whose likelihoods best match the student model's original distribution. The proposed teacher-guided method outperforms GRAPE on average in all four settings: by +3.6 points (58.7 vs. 55.1) for R1 + Qwen3-4B-Base, +2.0 points (62.6 vs. 60.6) for R1 + Qwen3-8B-Base, +0.6 points (57.7 vs. 57.1) for QwQ-32B + Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and +1.8 points (76.4 vs. 74.6) for GPT-OSS-120B + Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507. The largest individual gains over GRAPE appear on AIME24 (+6.3 for the 4B-Base setting) and AIME25 (+1.8 for the 4B-Instruct setting), suggesting the teacher-student divergence signal is particularly informative for harder competition-math problems. Notably, GRAPE occasionally outperforms Vanilla on individual benchmarks (e.g., AIME24 in the QwQ setting: 47.5 vs. 43.5), but the proposed method surpasses both on the overall average, indicating that explicitly leveraging teacher-originated actions provides a more principled selection criterion than student-only distributional alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of reasoning distillation and model auditing. While prior reasoning distillation work (DeepSeek-R1, LIMO, OpenThoughts) focused on data curation and performance, and GRAPE (Zhang et al., 2025) selected training data aligned with the student's own distribution, this paper introduces a cross-model provenance lens: comparing teacher, student, and distilled model probabilities on the same context to attribute each output sentence to its origin. It departs from model auditing (Song & Shmatikov, Carlini et al.), which traces data membership, by instead tracing model-level lineage—asking which upstream model a given sentence 'came from' rather than which training example was memorized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provenance-tracing framework provides the first systematic, quantitative explanation for why reasoning distillation generalizes: Teacher Sentences appear in early reasoning stages and correlate with answer correctness, while student-internal Boosted Sentences are not uniformly beneficial (they correlate with incorrect answers in smaller models). The teacher-guided data selection method offers a principled, training-free alternative to heuristic selection, yielding consistent 1.7–2.5% average gains across four teacher-student pairs. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to ≤8B student models, sentence-level (not token-level) granularity, and a single-teacher setting; open questions include whether findings hold at larger scale, in multi-teacher ensembles, and whether the provenance framework can identify which specific teacher contributed a given capability.
