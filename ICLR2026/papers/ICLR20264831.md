## Problem

Direct Feedback Alignment (DFA) has shown promise for efficient, parallel training of neural networks, but its application to fine-tuning pre-trained models has been unreliable. Standard DFA uses arbitrary random feedback matrices that are misaligned with back-propagation-trained weights, resulting in weak weight and gradient alignment that prevents effective adaptation to new tasks. This paper identifies and resolves this fundamental mismatch to make DFA a viable fine-tuning mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-trained network weights (obtained via back-propagation on source datasets like CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, BookCorpus & Wikipedia, or ImageNet) and a target fine-tuning dataset. Before DFA fine-tuning begins, the pre-trained weights are decomposed into feedback matrices and re-initialized, so the actual input to the DFA training loop is a re-parameterized version of the original weights. Outputs are fine-tuned network weights and standard task predictions (classification accuracy for image tasks, correlation/accuracy scores for GLUE NLP tasks), evaluated on 32×32 or 224×224 images and text sequences up to 512 tokens.

## Method

Feedback-weight matching consists of two phases that induce strong weight alignment (WA) and gradient alignment (GA) before DFA fine-tuning begins. In feedback matching, pre-trained weights W_l^0 are decomposed into feedback matrices F̄_l and F̄_{l-1}^T (via SVD or a 3-epoch optimization) such that F̄_l F̄_{l-1}^T ≈ W_l^0. In weight matching, the network weights are re-initialized to exactly equal the reconstructed feedback products (W̄_l^0 ≡ F̄_l F̄_{l-1}^T), ensuring the initial state satisfies the strong WA condition. Standard DFA then proceeds with these matched feedback matrices, and the paper proves that this setup maintains strong WA throughout training, yielding strong GA and reliable convergence. Additionally, the paper shows that weight decay combined with FWM provides an extra error-reduction term beyond standard regularization, further improving fine-tuning accuracy.

## Results

On image classification, feedback-weight matching (DFA_ours) achieves 82.67% test accuracy when fine-tuning a 6-layer network from CIFAR-100 to SVHN, a 7.97% improvement over standard DFA fine-tuning (74.70%). On NLP (GLUE), BERT-Small fine-tuned with feedback-weight matching reaches 0.76 Pearson correlation on STSB and 92.5% accuracy on MRPC, compared to 0.10 and 70.9% respectively for standard DFA. On Vision Transformers, ViT-Small improves from 0.210 to 0.319 accuracy on ImageNette. The method also maintains robustness across network depths, with only a 0.20% accuracy drop from 4-layer to 6-layer (vs. 4.85% for standard DFA) on CIFAR-100→SVHN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares feedback-weight matching (DFA_ours) against two baselines: standard DFA fine-tuning (DFA_fine, Nøkland 2016) and back-propagation fine-tuning (BP_fine, Rumelhart 1986). Against standard DFA, the proposed method consistently wins across all tasks: +7.97% on 6-layer CIFAR-100→SVHN (82.67% vs. 74.70%), +0.66 Pearson correlation on BERT-Small STSB (0.76 vs. 0.10), and +0.109 on ViT-Small ImageNette (0.319 vs. 0.210). Against back-propagation fine-tuning, the method still lags in most settings (e.g., 82.67% vs. 84.34% on 6-layer SVHN; 0.76 vs. 0.90 on BERT-Small STSB), though it occasionally matches or exceeds BP from-scratch training (e.g., 45.61% vs. 43.15% on 4-layer STL-10). The gap to BP fine-tuning is smallest on image classification (1–2%) and largest on NLP tasks where pre-trained weights carry more task-specific signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Feedback Alignment (DFA) from its established role in from-scratch training to the fine-tuning setting, building directly on the weight alignment (WA) and gradient alignment (GA) theoretical framework of Refinetti et al. (2021). The key departure is a formal proof (Prop. 3.3) that standard DFA with arbitrary random feedback matrices fundamentally cannot achieve strong WA when starting from back-propagation-trained weights, explaining why prior attempts at DFA fine-tuning (Chu & Bacho, 2024) were unreliable. The proposed feedback-weight matching method—decomposing pre-trained weights into feedback matrices and re-initializing weights to match them—is a targeted fix that restores the WA/GA conditions DFA requires, while the weight decay analysis extends Song et al. (2021) from FA from-scratch training to the DFA fine-tuning context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that DFA can reliably fine-tune pre-trained networks, opening a path toward the widely-used pre-train-and-fine-tune paradigm for DFA-based training, which is back-propagation-free and supports layer-parallel updates. However, the method still falls short of back-propagation fine-tuning (e.g., 82.67% vs. 84.34% on SVHN; 0.76 vs. 0.90 Pearson on STSB), is currently limited to fully connected layers and partially to Transformer dense layers, and the multi-layer generalization of the weight decay error-reduction bound remains a conjecture. Open questions include extending FWM to CNNs and attention projection layers, closing the gap to BP fine-tuning, and formalizing the alignment conditions under attention mechanisms.
