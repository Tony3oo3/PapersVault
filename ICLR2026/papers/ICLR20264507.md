## Problem

Ultra-high-resolution (2K–4K) image synthesis with latent diffusion models suffers from repeated structures, blurred textures, and spatial inconsistencies because existing training objectives treat all spatial regions uniformly, wasting computation on smooth areas while under-supervising detail-rich regions. Current approaches either demand expensive architectural modifications, cascaded pipelines, or proprietary high-resolution datasets, and none incorporate spatial adaptivity into the denoising process itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264507/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and generates RGB images at 2048×2048 or 4096×4096 resolution, operating entirely within the latent space of a pretrained VAE. It is a training-only modification: the LWD-enhanced model has identical parameter count and inference time as its baseline counterpart, requiring no architectural changes, cascaded upsamplers, or additional forward-pass computation. Evaluation is conducted on Aesthetic-4K and LAION-High-Res benchmarks using FID, LPIPS, CLIPScore, GLCM texture scores, and frequency-domain metrics.

## Method

LWD is a two-stage, model-agnostic training framework. Stage 1 fine-tunes the VAE with a scale-consistency loss that penalizes frequency components inconsistent across resolution scales, suppressing spurious high-frequency noise in the latent space. Stage 2 modifies the flow-matching training objective by computing a Haar DWT on each latent tensor, aggregating the squared energy of the LH, HL, and HH subbands into a spatial saliency map, and using it to construct a time-dependent binary mask M_t(i,j) that determines whether the denoising loss is applied at each spatial location and timestep. Regions with high wavelet energy receive supervision across more timesteps (up to the full schedule), while low-detail regions are updated only for a minimum fraction ℓ=0.3 of timesteps. This signal-driven, deterministic masking introduces spatial curriculum learning into the denoising process without any learned attention or architectural change, and is compatible with both classical diffusion and flow-matching formulations.

## Results

On the HPD 2K benchmark (Table 1), LWD+URAE achieves the best FID of 32.88 and LPIPS of 0.6336, reducing FID by up to 7% over the strongest baselines (SDEdit: 35.59, Sana-1.6B: 35.75) and by 12.5% over FLUX-1.dev (37.58). On the Aesthetic-Eval 2K set (Table 2), LWD+SD3-F16 improves FID from 43.82 (SD3-F16) to 38.74 and CLIPScore from 31.50 to 34.94, while LWD+PixArt-Sigma-XL drops FID from 39.13 to 36.14. At 4K resolution (Table 7), LWD+URAE achieves the highest MAN-IQA (0.4011), QualiCLIP (0.3855), and GLCM score (0.74) among all methods, with a compression ratio of 28.77 versus 38.86 for URAE alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LWD+URAE outperforms all eight baselines in Table 1 on FID (32.88 vs. 35.25–54.96) and LPIPS (0.6336 vs. 0.6371–0.7169), with the largest gains over Lumina-Image 2.0 (FID 54.96) and FLUX-1.dev (FID 37.58). Against its direct backbone URAE, LWD reduces FID by 6.7% (35.25→32.88) and LPIPS by 5.7% (0.6717→0.6336), while slightly improving MAN-IQA (0.4076→0.4099); however, it trades a small amount of QualiCLIP (0.5423→0.5356) and HPSv2.1 (31.15→28.78), suggesting a shift toward texture fidelity over holistic human preference scores. On the Aesthetic-Eval 2K set, LWD+SD3-F16 beats both SD3-F16 (FID 43.82→38.74) and SD3-Diff4k-F16 (FID 40.18→38.74) on FID and CLIPScore, while LWD+PixArt-Sigma-XL improves FID from 39.13 to 36.14 and GLCM from 0.79 to 0.87. At 4K, LWD+URAE dominates URAE on every metric in Table 7 (MAN-IQA 0.4011 vs. 0.3850, GLCM 0.74 vs. 0.41, Compression Ratio 28.77 vs. 38.86), and LWD+Sana-1.6B edges out Sana-1.6B on CLIPScore (34.59 vs. 34.40) and Aesthetics (6.21 vs. 6.14) in Table 2. The one notable exception is LWD+Sana-1.6B on Aesthetic-Eval 2K, where FID slightly regresses (32.06→34.30) despite gains in CLIPScore and Aesthetics, indicating that the wavelet masking benefit is most pronounced for backbones that originally underperform on high-frequency detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

LWD extends the latent diffusion / flow-matching training pipeline (SD3, Flux, Sana, PixArt-Sigma) by inserting a signal-processing-derived spatial mask into the denoising objective, rather than modifying the architecture. It departs from Diffusion-4K, which applied wavelet losses uniformly across all spatial locations, by making supervision spatially adaptive via local DWT energy. The scale-consistency VAE fine-tuning borrows from Skorokhodov et al. (2025) and Kouzelis et al. (2025) but is repurposed specifically to suppress spurious high-frequency noise so that downstream wavelet saliency maps reflect meaningful structure rather than compression artifacts. Compared to attention-based selective supervision (Moser et al., 2025), LWD replaces learned semantic features with a deterministic, architecture-agnostic frequency signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-inference-cost, no-architecture-change design makes LWD a practical drop-in for any existing latent diffusion pipeline targeting 2K–4K synthesis, lowering the barrier to improving detail fidelity without retraining from scratch. However, it inherits the fundamental VAE compression bottleneck: fine-grained semantic detail lost during encoding cannot be recovered by better denoising supervision alone. Open questions include whether the frequency-aware masking transfers to video (temporal wavelet energy), depth-aware or multimodal conditioning, and whether the slight GLCM decrease reflects a genuine perceptual trade-off or a metric limitation. The convergence acceleration (10–50% of original iterations) is a practically significant side benefit that could reduce training costs for UHR fine-tuning broadly.
