## Problem

Existing spherical neural operators (e.g., SFNO) are constructed via the spherical convolution theorem rather than derived from Green's function solutions of sphere-native PDEs, obscuring the physical meaning of learned kernels. Their strict SO(3) rotational equivariance prevents modeling real-world non-equivariant phenomena such as terrain-dependent weather patterns, boundary effects, and heterogeneous media. Additionally, single-scale ResNet-style architectures limit expressiveness for complex multi-scale dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes spherical fields sampled on lat-lon grids (256×512 for SSWE with 3 fluid channels; 32×64 at 5.625° for WeatherBench with 24 meteorological variables; HEALPix-sampled FOD signals for dMRI) and produces the evolved or super-resolved spherical field at the target time step or angular resolution. A distinctive aspect is that the operator internally computes the spherical integral C_f of the input as a scalar modulation factor for the non-equivariant correction term, and all spectral operations are performed in the spherical harmonic domain with degree l truncated to the grid resolution.

## Method

The paper derives a generalized operator framework from a designable Green's function G(R,u) on the sphere, extending it to the spherical harmonic domain to obtain a principled spectral operator solution. The key design is a Green's function combining a relative-position term G(R^{-1}u) (yielding the standard equivariant SFNO convolution) and an absolute-position term G(u) (yielding a novel non-equivariant correction), producing GSNO whose output is ISHT[G_θ1(l)·(SHT[f](l,m) + C_f·G_θ2(l,m))]. The equivariant term G_θ1(l) handles cross-channel spectral mixing while the correction term G_θ2(l,m), modulated by the input's spherical integral C_f, encodes position-dependent constraints like topography. SHNet wraps GSNO blocks in a U-Net architecture where spherical up/down sampling is achieved by changing the number of lat-lon sampling points and the SHT degree l, avoiding the geometric distortions of traditional resampling on the sphere.

## Results

On the Spherical Shallow Water Equations (SSWE) task, SHNet achieves a mean relative error (MRE) of 136×10⁻³ at 5h and 176×10⁻³ at 10h, outperforming SFNONet (147/195) by 7.5% and 9.7% respectively. On WeatherBench (5.625° resolution), SHNet with GSNO reaches 91.5% average ACC at 1-day, 72.4% at 3-day, and 52.5% at 5-day forecasts, surpassing SFNONet (87.3/68.8/49.2) by up to 6.7 percentage points at longer horizons. On the dMRI fiber orientation distribution task (HCP dataset), FOD-GSNO achieves 0.8517 whole-brain angular correlation coefficient (ACC), exceeding the best baseline ESCNN (0.8362) by 1.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SFNONet (the closest spherical operator baseline), SHNet+GSNO wins on all three tasks: SSWE MRE improves by 7.5–9.7%, WeatherBench ACC improves by 4.2–6.7 pp at 3- and 5-day horizons, and dMRI whole-brain ACC improves by 1.8 pp. Against FourCastNet (FNO-based), SHNet wins decisively on SSWE (136 vs. 302 MRE at 5h) and on 3-day/5-day WeatherBench (72.4 vs. 67.1; 52.5 vs. 41.5 ACC), but trails slightly on 1-day WeatherBench (91.5 vs. 92.3). Against ClimaX (Transformer-based), SHNet wins on SSWE and 3/5-day WeatherBench but is outperformed on 1-day WeatherBench (91.5 vs. 93.1 ACC). On dMRI, FOD-GSNO outperforms FOD-Net (0.8250), FOD-SFNO (0.8334), ESCNN (0.8362), and SSMT-CSD (0.6640) on whole-brain ACC, with the largest margin over the non-learning SSMT-CSD baseline (+18.8 pp). The ablation rows in Table 2 confirm that both the GSNO operator and the SHNet multi-scale architecture contribute independently: replacing GSNO with SFNO in SHNet drops 5-day ACC from 52.5 to 50.9, and replacing SFNO with GSNO in SFNONet raises it from 49.2 to 51.3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Spherical Fourier Neural Operator (SFNO) line of work by re-deriving spherical spectral operators from a designable Green's function rather than from the spherical convolution theorem alone, providing a more principled PDE-solution grounding. It departs from prior spherical operators (SFNONet, FourCastNet, ClimaX) that enforce strict SO(3) rotational equivariance by introducing a learnable non-equivariant correction term (G₂) that captures position-dependent physical constraints such as terrain and boundary effects. The multi-scale SHNet architecture builds on U-Net-style spherical networks (Zhao et al., 2019; Hu et al., 2025) but replaces conventional up/down-sampling with geometrically consistent spherical harmonic resolution changes to avoid aliasing and distortion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Green's function framework offers a unified, extensible design principle for spherical operators: different physical systems can be modeled by choosing different Green's functions, bridging the gap between rigorous PDE theory and flexible neural operator learning. The explicit equivariance/invariance decomposition (G₁ for dynamics, G₂ for stable spatial constraints) is a practical inductive bias that could transfer to other non-Euclidean domains. However, the paper is limited to the sphere at two resolutions and three datasets; generalization to other manifolds, higher resolutions, and longer autoregressive horizons remains open, and the moderate performance gains (single-digit percentage improvements over SFNO) suggest the correction term's benefit may saturate in highly symmetric regimes.
