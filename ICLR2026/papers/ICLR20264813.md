## Problem

Gaussian policies in continuous RL have unbounded support, forcing ad-hoc tanh squashing that distorts action-space geometry, causes gradient vanishing near boundaries, and breaks natural symmetry. While von Mises-Fisher distributions offer a principled spherical alternative, their reliance on Bessel functions and O(dk) rejection sampling makes them impractical. The paper asks whether the distributional paradigm itself is necessary, or whether direct geometric operations on the unit sphere can achieve comparable control with far simpler computation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a state observation vector s and produces a bounded continuous action vector a ∈ [-1,1]^d. Rather than outputting distribution parameters (mean and variance), the policy outputs a unit direction vector μ ∈ S^{d-1} and a scalar concentration κ, from which the action is constructed via spherical interpolation and a fixed or learnable radius r. This means the action head requires only d+1 outputs (vs. 2d for diagonal Gaussians), and all generated actions lie on a sphere of radius r by construction, eliminating the need for squashing or clipping.

## Method

GAC generates actions through a three-step geometric pipeline: (1) a direction network produces a raw vector normalized to the unit sphere, (2) a concentration network predicts a scalar κ whose sigmoid w(κ)=σ(κ) controls the interpolation weight, and (3) the final action is a = r·normalize(w(κ)·μ + (1−w(κ))·ξ), where ξ is uniform spherical noise sampled as normalized Gaussian. This replaces SAC's Gaussian policy and entropy regularization: the actor loss becomes E[κ(s) − min Q(s,a)], using κ directly as a learned exploration controller that trades off deterministic direction against isotropic spherical noise. The approach achieves O(d) sampling complexity (vs. O(dk) for vMF rejection sampling), avoids Bessel function evaluations, and provably yields vMF-like concentration control through the mixing weight. An adaptive variant replaces the scalar radius r with a learnable per-dimension vector r ∈ R^d (via softplus), enabling anisotropic control for asymmetric tasks like quadruped locomotion while preserving the core spherical geometry.

## Results

On six MuJoCo benchmarks, GAC achieves the best return on 4 of 6 tasks, with headline gains of 37.6% over SAC on Ant-v4 (5633±158 vs. 4094±1039) and 4.4% over TD3 on HalfCheetah-v4 (12750±758 vs. 12208±799). On the more challenging DMControl suite (using the adaptive scaling variant), GAC matches or surpasses SAC on 5 of 6 tasks, with particularly large improvements on quadruped-run (+112% over SAC: 638 vs. 301) and quadruped-walk (+34% over SAC: 925 vs. 690). GAC also exhibits lower variance than baselines on high-dimensional tasks (e.g., Ant-v4 std of 158 vs. SAC's 1039), and is 22% faster per sample than SAC in wall-clock time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC (Gaussian + tanh), GAC wins on 4 of 6 MuJoCo tasks (Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Ant, Humanoid) and 5 of 6 DMControl tasks, with the largest margin being +112% on quadruped-run (638 vs. 301) and +37.6% on Ant-v4 (5633 vs. 4094); it loses narrowly on Hopper-v4 (1952 vs. 2094) and Pusher-v4 (-32 vs. -23), and on fish-upright (858 vs. 923). Against TD3, GAC wins on 5 of 6 MuJoCo tasks and 4 of 6 DMControl tasks, with the most notable gap on Ant-v4 (+59.5%: 5633 vs. 3531) and quadruped-run (+10.8%: 638 vs. 576); TD3 retains the edge on Hopper-v4 (2896 vs. 1952) and cheetah-run (753 vs. 762, essentially tied). Against PPO, GAC dominates across all 12 environments, with PPO consistently 3–10× worse (e.g., 1969 vs. 5633 on Ant-v4, 119 vs. 638 on quadruped-run), confirming PPO's known exploration limitations in continuous control. GAC also shows substantially lower variance than SAC and TD3 on high-dimensional tasks, suggesting more stable training dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAC builds directly on the SAC actor-critic framework, replacing the standard Gaussian+tanh policy with a geometric action generator that operates on the unit sphere. It departs from the distributional paradigm that has dominated continuous control since DDPG, positioning itself as a simplification in the same spirit as TRPO→PPO or DDPG→TD3. Conceptually, it draws on directional statistics (vMF distributions) and geometric RL perspectives but avoids vMF's computational burden (Bessel functions, rejection sampling) by using simple spherical interpolation. The paper explicitly frames its contribution as questioning whether explicit probability distributions are necessary for action generation at all, rather than seeking a better distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's broader claim—that geometric structure can replace distributional complexity in continuous control—challenges a foundational assumption in the field and could influence how future policies are designed. However, the geometric prior (spherical manifold) shows clear limitations on asymmetric tasks (Hopper, Pusher), and the theoretical connection between the learned concentration parameter and information-theoretic entropy remains approximate rather than rigorous. Open questions include whether adaptive geometric structures (interpolating between spherical, elliptical, and unconstrained manifolds) can close the gap on contact-rich manipulation, and whether the approach extends to discrete or hybrid action spaces. The 50% parameter reduction and elimination of entropy computation are practically appealing, but the method's advantage is most pronounced in high-dimensional locomotion rather than universally.
