## Problem

LLMs make reasoning mistakes due to knowledge deficiencies, but existing improvement methods (unsupervised pre-training and supervised fine-tuning) indiscriminately process large datasets without targeting specific gaps, and diagnosing those gaps typically requires costly labeled data. The paper asks: can we automatically identify which specific knowledge an LLM lacks or fails to apply, and remedy those deficiencies efficiently, all without any labels?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261087/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes label-free user queries (drawn from e-CARE and GSM8K with labels discarded), an external knowledge base (GenericsKB filtered to 200K high-confidence generic facts), and a target LLM. For each query, m=4 relevant facts are retrieved via FlagEmbedding cosine similarity, and n=2 responses are sampled from the LLM to form distributions. The output is a set of diagnosed knowledge deficiencies quantified by relative entropy, a variable-size set of ChatGPT-synthesized training examples (1–4 per deficiency depending on severity), and a LoRA-fine-tuned LLM.

## Method

LaMer operates in three stages. First, it retrieves relevant generic facts from GenericsKB for each query using dense embedding similarity. Second, it diagnoses knowledge deficiencies in a label-free manner by computing the relative entropy (KL divergence) between the LLM's prior distribution over sampled responses (without knowledge) and its posterior distribution (with the retrieved fact injected as context); a high RE score indicates the model either lacks the knowledge or cannot integrate it, and pairs exceeding a threshold τ=0.1 are flagged as deficiencies. Third, it applies curricular meaningful learning: deficiencies are grouped into four severity bands by RE value, and ChatGPT synthesizes a severity-proportional number of diverse examples (1 for easy up to 4 for unfair) per deficiency; these examples are then used to fine-tune the LLM via LoRA (r=128, α=8) in ascending order of deficiency severity, so that the model first consolidates minor gaps before tackling severe ones, following a curriculum from easy to hard.

## Results

LaMer achieves the best average score across all four LLMs on seven OOD reasoning benchmarks: Mistral-7B (56.09 vs. 52.74 for the best baseline), LLaMA-3-8B (64.70 vs. 62.82 for Base), Qwen2-7B (66.13 vs. 65.31 for Naive), and Gemma-1.1-2B (34.08 vs. 33.07 for Base). Notably, the Single variant (one example per deficiency, 40% of LaMer's training data) achieves comparable performance to Naive on Mistral (52.74 vs. 52.52) and LLaMA-3 (61.26 vs. 61.90), demonstrating that targeted deficiency diagnosis yields more efficient improvements than indiscriminate data augmentation. LaMer also surpasses the label-reliant LLM2LLM method on average for all four LLMs while operating entirely without labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AugGPT (Dai et al., 2023), which uses ChatGPT to generate QA pairs without chain-of-thought, LaMer wins on average for all four LLMs (e.g., Mistral: 56.09 vs. 52.33; LLaMA-3: 64.70 vs. 55.63). AugGPT actually degrades LLaMA-3 and Qwen2 below their base performance, likely because indiscriminate augmentation causes catastrophic forgetting in models with dense parametric knowledge. Against LLM2LLM (Lee et al., 2024), a label-reliant method that identifies errors using ground-truth labels and synthesizes similar examples, LaMer achieves higher average scores on all four LLMs (Mistral: 56.09 vs. 54.00; LLaMA-3: 64.70 vs. 63.61; Qwen2: 66.13 vs. 65.42; Gemma-1.1: 34.08 vs. 34.04) while requiring no labels at all. The paper attributes this advantage to LaMer's broader coverage of knowledge deficiencies via relative entropy, whereas LLM2LLM's error detection is limited to the specific initial dataset. Additionally, the Single ablation (40% of LaMer's data) matches or approaches Naive's performance, confirming that targeted deficiency diagnosis is more data-efficient than random knowledge sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaMer combines two established ideas—meaningful learning via generic facts (Xiong et al., 2024) and curriculum learning from easy to hard (Bengio et al., 2009)—into a unified remedy strategy, but its key departure is the diagnostic step: using relative entropy (Kullback & Leibler, 1951) as a label-free proxy for knowledge deficiency, replacing the need for ground-truth labels or perplexity-based error detection. This positions LaMer as complementary to existing continual-training and SFT pipelines (Orca, WizardLM, LLM2LLM) by inserting a targeted diagnosis before data synthesis, rather than indiscriminately augmenting with all available data. The paper explicitly contrasts with LLM2LLM, which requires labeled errors, and with Naive augmentation, which ignores whether the target LLM actually lacks the knowledge being injected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LaMer offers a practical, plug-and-play diagnostic-and-remedy pipeline that can be applied to any open-source LLM with a relevant knowledge base, reducing the cost of targeted improvement by ~60% in training data (Single baseline matches Naive with 40% of the data). However, the paper acknowledges significant limitations: it requires a domain-appropriate knowledge base (difficult for specialized domains), performs poorly on math benchmarks, and does not address complex multi-step reasoning. Open questions include whether the relative-entropy diagnostic generalizes beyond the GenericsKB-style generic facts used here, and whether the modest curriculum-ordering gains (LaMer vs. LaMer* differ by <0.5 points on average) justify the added complexity over random ordering.
