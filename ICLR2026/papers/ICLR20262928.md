## Problem

Existing linear attention mechanisms (DeltaNet, Gated DeltaNet, TTT-Linear, Gated DeltaProduct) restrict their hidden-state decay matrices to at most a Diagonal-Plus-Rank-1 structure, leaving open whether this represents the ceiling of expressiveness for efficient sequence modeling. The paper asks whether extending decay matrices to broader, structured, rank-enhanced classes—while maintaining parameter, memory, and computational efficiency—can further elevate linear attention's performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262928/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For language modeling, the input is a sequence of token embeddings x_t ∈ R^{d×1} projected into query q_t, key k_t, and value v_t vectors; for image classification, the input is a sequence of image patches. The hidden state S_t ∈ R^{d_k×d_v} is fixed-size regardless of sequence length, and the d_k×d_k decay matrix P_t is parameterized by only O(d_k) parameters (a scalar β_t and a vector λ_t). The output is the attention vector o_t ∈ R^{d_v×1} used for next-token prediction, or a class label in the bidirectional image classification setting. The chunk-wise parallel algorithm processes sequences in blocks of size C, requiring only O(d_k) memory per timestep for the decay structure.

## Method

HDLA parameterizes the decay matrix as P_t = (I − β_t k_t k_t^T) Λ_t (I − β_t k_t k_t^T), a product of two generalized Householder transformations sandwiching an input-dependent diagonal matrix Λ_t = Diag(σ(W_Λ x_t)), which is proven to be a specific instance of the Diagonal-Plus-Rank-2 class. This design is motivated by congruence diagonalization of real symmetric matrices and satisfies three efficiency constraints: O(d_k) parameters for the O(d_k²) decay matrix, O(d_k) memory for cumulative products, and one-pass matrix multiplications for sequential state updates. The paper further derives a rank-generalized chunk-wise parallel algorithm that simultaneously accommodates arbitrary diagonal-plus-rank-r_ab decay and rank-r_kv key-value outer products via a WY representation, subsuming HDLA (r_ab=2, r_kv=1) as a special case. From a Test-Time Training perspective, one HDLA step decomposes into three sub-steps: a preemptive penalty on k_t's inner-product similarity with S_{t−1}, a diagonal decay, and a delta-rule gradient update—distinguishing it from Gated DeltaProduct's unstructured rank-enhanced iterations.

## Results

On language modeling (Wikitext + LAMBADA), HDLA achieves the best average perplexity among all linear attention baselines at every scale: 36.06 (0.4B), 22.32 (1.45B), and 18.58 (2.8B), outperforming Gated DeltaNet (43.06 / 24.83 / 19.60) and even surpassing Llama (37.60 / 23.68 / 20.71) on perplexity. On the MAD mechanistic benchmark, HDLA scores 72.97 average, exceeding all linear attention baselines (best: Mamba at 68.58) and narrowing the gap to Softmax Attention (76.02) to just 3.05 points. On retrieval-based RULER (S-NIAH-3, 2048 tokens), HDLA reaches 65.2% accuracy versus 7.0% for Gated DeltaNet — a 58.2% absolute improvement. On MQAR at sequence length 2048, HDLA maintains >81% accuracy while GDP2 and Gated DeltaNet nearly fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDLA consistently outperforms all Diagonal-Plus-Rank-1 baselines (Gated DeltaNet, DeltaNet) on perplexity, MAD, RULER retrieval, and commonsense reasoning; on MAD, HDLA's memorization score (89.34) dramatically exceeds Gated DeltaNet's (55.64) and DeltaNet's (42.46), indicating the Rank-2 structure preserves long-range information better. Against the rank-enhanced Gated DeltaProduct (GDP2/GDP3), HDLA wins on perplexity at all scales (e.g., 18.58 vs 20.38 at 2.8B) and on MAD (72.97 vs 65.31/66.74), despite GDP3 having ~2× the computation. Versus diagonal-decay baselines (GLA, Mamba2, HGRN2, TransNormerLLM), HDLA leads on perplexity by 1.4–8.2 points and on MAD by 4.4–7.7 points, confirming that non-diagonal structured decay is more effective than pure diagonal forgetting. HDLA also beats Llama (softmax attention) on perplexity at 0.4B and 1.45B scales, but Llama retains a large advantage on retrieval tasks (SWDE: 61.57 vs 27.45; SC: 36.23 vs 30.56 at 2.8B), reflecting the fundamental limitation of fixed-size hidden states for in-context retrieval. On ImageNet-1k, HDLA (74.84%) outperforms Gated DeltaNet (72.33%), Mamba (73.39%), and DeiT (72.20%), but is slightly below MetaLA (75.33%). On MAD, HDLA (72.97) still trails Softmax Attention (76.02) by 3.05 points, with the gap concentrated on Fuzzy ICR (14.56 vs 39.74) due to linear attention's inherent recency bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

HDLA extends the Diagonal-Plus-Rank-1 decay lineage established by DeltaNet, TTT-Linear, and Gated DeltaNet by sandwiching an input-dependent diagonal (à la GLA) between two Householder transformations, yielding a structured Diagonal-Plus-Rank-2 decay. The chunk-wise parallel algorithm generalizes the WY representation from FLA (Yang & Zhang, 2024) and the two-phase scheme of Lightning Attention to arbitrary rank-r_ab decay and rank-r_kv key-value updates, subsuming all prior linear attention parallelization results as special cases. The key conceptual departure from Gated DeltaProduct is that HDLA achieves rank enhancement through a *structured* factorization (congruence diagonalization) rather than by simply repeating the recurrent step, which the authors argue yields a more expressive yet computationally cheaper decay.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a structured Diagonal-Plus-Rank-2 decay outperforms an unstructured Diagonal-Plus-Rank-3 (Gated DeltaProduct n_h=3) at roughly half the computation suggests that the *geometry* of the decay matrix matters more than its raw rank, a principle that could guide future linear attention design. The generalized chunk-wise parallel algorithm provides a reusable theoretical and implementation foundation for any future rank-enhanced linear attention variant. However, HDLA's throughput is notably lower than all baselines (7340 vs. ~19000 TPS for GDN), and retrieval performance still lags softmax attention substantially, leaving open whether structured rank enhancement can close the remaining gap without prohibitive I/O overhead.
