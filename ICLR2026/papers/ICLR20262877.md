## Problem

Diffusion-based inverse solvers like DPS require a differentiable forward operator to compute the likelihood score that steers posterior sampling, but many practical inverse problems involve forward operators that are non-differentiable, non-smooth, and only partially known (e.g., human path-planning in a floorplan). The paper addresses the gap of how to perform stable likelihood-based guidance in diffusion when the forward operator's Jacobian is highly sensitive and the operator itself is only approximately specified.

## Input / Output

For the spatial task, the method takes a 64×64 binary trajectory image (union of walked paths at sparse ~40%, moderate ~25%, or dense ~10% of open space) and produces a 64×64 binary floorplan image (white = free space, black = walls). For the blind audio task, it takes a degraded 22,050 Hz waveform cropped to 8.35 s and produces a restored waveform. Real-world evaluation uses UWB trilateration trajectories with ~10 cm positional noise, which are sparser and differently distributed than the synthetic A*-generated trajectories used in training.

## Method

CoGuide replaces the intractable likelihood score ∇_x log p(y|x) with a surrogate computed in a learned contrastive embedding space: two ViT encoders (16×16 patches, 256-d unit-norm embeddings) map floorplans and trajectories into a shared space trained with symmetric supervised contrastive loss (multi-positive InfoNCE) plus an alignment term. The key theoretical result is that at the InfoNCE optimum, the embedding inner product equals the log likelihood-to-evidence ratio up to a constant, so its gradient with respect to x recovers the true likelihood score, making the squared L2 distance in embedding space a valid and smooth surrogate for the original intractable gradient. During DDIM inference, the posterior score is the diffusion prior score plus the contrastive gradient (backpropagated through the Tweedie estimate x̂₀) plus an intersection penalty, optimized with Adam and a short cosine LR anneal per step to ensure stable convergence over the short DDIM schedule.

## Results

On the HouseExpo floorplan reconstruction task, CoGuide achieves F1/IoU of 0.91/0.84 (sparse), 0.94/0.89 (moderate), and 0.95/0.90 (dense) trajectory density regimes, outperforming all DPS+planner baselines and DiffPIR/DMPlug. The combined CFG+CoGuide variant reaches 0.93/0.87, 0.97/0.93, and 0.99/0.97 respectively, setting the best scores across all densities. In the blind audio restoration task on MAESTRO, CoGuide achieves FAD-VGG of 0.84 and FAD-PANN of 0.19, substantially outperforming the LTAS baseline (2.88 / 0.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoGuide decisively outperforms all three DPS+planner variants (NeuralA*, TransPath, DiPPeR) by 12–15 F1 points across all trajectory densities, confirming that direct gradient-based guidance through non-smooth path-planning operators is fundamentally unstable. It also substantially beats DiffPIR (~27 F1 points) and DMPlug (~60 F1 points), which struggle with the non-differentiable forward operator. Against the strongest baseline, CFG, CoGuide wins in the sparse regime (0.91 vs. 0.86 F1) and moderate regime (0.94 vs. 0.93 F1), but CFG edges ahead in the dense regime (0.97 vs. 0.95 F1), where the joint distribution is easier to model. The combined CFG+CoGuide variant dominates all baselines in every regime, reaching 0.99 F1 / 0.97 IoU in the dense case. In the blind audio restoration task, CoGuide reduces FAD-VGG from 2.52 (degraded) to 0.84, a 3× improvement over the LTAS equalization baseline (2.88), demonstrating generalization beyond spatial inverse problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoGuide extends the Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) framework by replacing the intractable likelihood score—normally computed by backpropagating through a forward operator A(·)—with a contrastive embedding-space surrogate. The theoretical justification draws on the InfoNCE-to-likelihood connection from Contrastive Predictive Coding (Oord et al., 2018), extended here to supervised multi-positive contrastive learning (Khosla et al., 2020). The paper departs from DPS's requirement of a differentiable forward operator by showing that path-planning operators (Neural A*, TransPath, DiPPeR) produce unstable Jacobians that break gradient-based guidance, motivating the embedding-space reformulation. It also combines with classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) as a complementary conditional prior, and modifies DDIM inference by substituting Adam for plain gradient descent to handle the short-step schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode of diffusion-based inverse solvers—non-differentiable, partially specified forward operators—and provides a principled workaround via contrastive embeddings that is generalizable beyond floorplan estimation (demonstrated on blind audio restoration). Open questions include which broader family of operators benefits from contrastive guidance, whether partial knowledge of A(·) suffices to design a good embedding space, and how to incorporate richer scene elements (furniture) into the floorplan prior. A practical limitation is that the contrastive model is trained on synthetically generated trajectories (A*), and while CoGuide generalizes better than CFG to real UWB trajectories, the gap between synthetic and real trajectory distributions remains a concern for deployment.
