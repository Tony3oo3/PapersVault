## Problem

Extending 3D Gaussian Splatting to city-scale scenes exposes three critical failures: geometric supervision via rendered normals alone cannot update Gaussian positions (only rotations), homogeneous regions generate excessive redundant Gaussians causing memory explosion, and naive block partitioning introduces boundary artifacts and processes irrelevant views. Existing large-scale methods either sacrifice rendering quality for geometry (CityGS-v2) or fail entirely on complex urban scenes due to out-of-memory errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260337/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (downscaled to ≤1600 px width) with COLMAP SfM camera poses, plus pretrained monocular priors from DepthAnything-v2 (depth) and Dsine (surface normals) as auxiliary supervision signals. It produces a compact 3D Gaussian scene representation (typically 2–8 M Gaussians) that supports real-time novel-view rendering, mesh extraction via TSDF fusion and Marching Cubes, and per-pixel depth/normal map rendering. Training runs on 8× RTX A5000 GPUs with a two-stage pipeline: coarse global optimization followed by parallel block-wise refinement.

## Method

UrbanGS introduces a Depth-Consistent D-Normal Regularizer that derives depth-normals from spatial gradients of rendered depth maps (rather than supervising rendered normals directly), establishing a geometric constraint intrinsically coupled to depth so that backpropagation updates both Gaussian positions and rotations. A dual supervision mechanism adds inverse-depth loss against DepthAnything-v2 priors, weighted by an adaptive confidence term combining depth-gradient cosine similarity and normalized inverse-depth deviation to suppress unreliable regions. For scalability, Spatially Adaptive Gaussian Pruning (SAGP) discretizes the scene into local voxel cells and computes per-Gaussian importance as a product of ray-intersection frequency, sigmoid-mapped opacity, and a sub-linear volume weight, adaptively removing redundant primitives in uniform or distant regions while preserving fine structures. A unified partitioning strategy pre-prunes the global model, duplicates boundary Gaussians across adjacent blocks to prevent fusion artifacts, and assigns camera views using both geometric containment and SSIM-based perceptual contribution criteria.

## Results

On the Mill-19 and UrbanScene3D datasets, UrbanGS achieves the best novel view synthesis across all four scenes: PSNR 22.82/SSIM 0.802 (Building), 26.25/0.791 (Rubble), 22.48/0.823 (Residence), and 22.62/0.824 (Sci-Art), outperforming the strongest baseline CityGaussian by up to 1.27 dB PSNR. On the GauU-Scene geometry benchmark, UrbanGS attains F1 scores of 0.493 (Residence), 0.546 (Russian Building), and 0.503 (Modern Building), surpassing CityGS-X and CityGaussianV2 on all scenes. In terms of efficiency, UrbanGS reduces memory usage to 11.4–15.0 GB (vs. 27.4–31.5 GB for CityGS) while using fewer Gaussians (6.56–7.78M vs. 7.00–8.07M) and completing training in 2 h 10 min on the Rubble scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UrbanGS outperforms all 15 baselines on the primary novel view synthesis benchmark (Table 1), with the largest margins over geometry-optimized methods: it beats VCR-Gaus by 3.26 dB PSNR on Building and 4.91 dB on Sci-Art, and CityGaussianV2 by 3.75 dB on Building. Among rendering-focused baselines without geometric optimization, UrbanGS edges out DoGaussian (the strongest non-geometric competitor) by 0.09 dB PSNR on Building and 0.47 dB on Rubble, while also achieving lower LPIPS. On the GauU-Scene geometry benchmark (Table 2), UrbanGS surpasses CityGS-X by 0.037 F1 on Residence and 0.011 on Modern Building, and beats CityGaussianV2 by 0.026 F1 on Residence; NeuS and GOF fail entirely to produce valid meshes on most scenes. In the efficiency comparison (Table 3), UrbanGS uses 55–62% less GPU memory than CityGS (13.2 GB vs. 31.5 GB on Residence) while simultaneously achieving higher PSNR (23.78 vs. 23.17) and F1 (0.493 vs. 0.453). Training time is also competitive: UrbanGS completes the Rubble scene in 2 h 10 min vs. 3 h 05 min for CityGS-v2 and 30 h 48 min for Mega-NeRF.

## Relation to Prior Work

UrbanGS extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) to city-scale scenes by combining and extending three prior threads: the D-Normal regularization concept from VCR-GauS (Chen et al., 2024b), the block partitioning strategy from CityGaussian (Liu et al., 2024a), and the Gaussian flattening/normal definition from NeuSG (Chen et al., 2023). Its key departure is the Depth-Consistent D-Normal Regularization, which augments VCR-GauS's normal-only supervision with an inverse-depth loss anchored to monocular depth priors (DepthAnything-v2) and an adaptive confidence weight, thereby enabling position updates that pure normal supervision provably cannot achieve. The Spatially Adaptive Gaussian Pruning (SAGP) is positioned as the first pruning framework designed specifically for the spatial heterogeneity of city-scale 3DGS, moving beyond global-threshold heuristics used in small-scene methods like LightGaussian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UrbanGS provides a practical, end-to-end pipeline that makes city-scale 3DGS reconstruction feasible on consumer-grade GPUs (A5000) by resolving the OOM failures that plague existing methods, while simultaneously improving geometric accuracy (F1) and rendering quality over strong baselines like CityGS-v2 and CityGS-X. The theoretical proofs (Propositions 1.1–2.1) clarifying why rendered-normal supervision alone is insufficient for position updates offer a useful conceptual anchor for future 3DGS geometry work. However, the method's reliance on monocular depth/normal priors limits robustness in weak-texture or extreme-lighting regions, and it does not handle dynamic urban objects—both open problems the authors acknowledge. The framework's modular design (pruning, partitioning, regularization) makes it a useful reference architecture for the growing body of large-scale 3DGS research.
