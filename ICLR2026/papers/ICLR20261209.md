## Problem

Training mobile GUI agents with online reinforcement learning is hampered by three intertwined challenges: sparse terminal rewards that make early exploration data-inefficient, a heavy-tailed task difficulty distribution that wastes compute on persistently unsolvable instances, and the high cost of sampling in large-scale mobile emulators. Prior work has relied on offline imitation or single-step expert labels, leaving efficient multi-turn agentic RL in interactive mobile environments largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives the current screenshot, a compressed XML UI hierarchy (with off-screen and redundant nodes pruned, bounding boxes aligned to XML elements), and the model's own interaction history (previous think/action messages), alongside a single natural-language instruction given at episode start. It outputs a sequence of atomic GUI actions drawn from a fixed vocabulary (Tap, Swipe, Type, Long Press, Launch, Home, Back, Wait, Finish) where grounding actions use bounding boxes [x1,y1,x2,y2] rather than point coordinates. The environment returns a binary success/failure reward only upon episode termination, with a maximum horizon of 50 turns.

## Method

MOBILERL trains in three stages: reasoning-free SFT on expert demonstrations, iterative reasoning SFT that bootstraps intermediate rationales via an off-the-shelf instruct model, and online agentic RL using the proposed ADAGRPO algorithm. ADAGRPO extends GRPO with three difficulty-adaptive strategies: (i) Shortest-Path Reward Adjustment (SPA) rescales the terminal reward by trajectory length relative to the shortest successful rollout in the group, encouraging efficient paths without penalizing failures; (ii) Difficulty-Adaptive Positive Replay (AdaPR) maintains a buffer of top-κ high-advantage successful trajectories and mixes them with on-policy samples at a controlled ratio to exploit rare but informative successes; and (iii) Failure Curriculum Filtering (FCF) down-weights tasks with consecutive all-zero rewards and permanently removes them after a cooldown, reallocating budget toward feasible instances. The entire pipeline runs on a Verl-based distributed framework orchestrating hundreds of Dockerized Android AVDs across multiple machines for concurrent multi-turn rollouts.

## Results

MOBILERL-9B (GLM-4.1V-9B-Base backbone) achieves 80.2% success rate on AndroidWorld and 53.6% on AndroidLab, setting new state-of-the-art results that surpass the previous best (UI-Tars-1.5 at 64.2% and UI-Genie-Agent at 41.2%). The smaller MOBILERL-7B (Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone) reaches 72.0% on AndroidWorld and 42.5% on AndroidLab, outperforming the much larger 72B-parameter UI-Genie-Agent. On the static AndroidControl benchmark, MOBILERL-9B (w/o RL) achieves 95.9% success rate on the Low split and 77.5% on the High split, surpassing all prior open-source models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOBILERL-9B outperforms the previous state-of-the-art UI-Tars-1.5 by +16.0 points on AndroidWorld (80.2% vs. 64.2%) and UI-Genie-Agent (72B) by +12.4 points on AndroidLab (53.6% vs. 41.2%). The 7B variant MOBILERL-7B also surpasses the proprietary UI-Tars-1.5 on AndroidWorld (72.0% vs. 64.2%) and the 72B UI-Genie-Agent on AndroidLab (42.5% vs. 41.2%), demonstrating that the ADAGRPO training recipe compensates for a large parameter gap. Against proprietary LLMs, MOBILERL-9B leads GPT-4o by +45.7 points and Claude-Sonnet-4 by +39.2 points on AndroidWorld. On the static AndroidControl benchmark, MOBILERL-9B (w/o RL) achieves 95.9% SR on the Low split, exceeding UI-Genie-72B (94.8%) and UI-Tars-72B (91.3%), while the RL version maintains competitive scores (94.3% Low, 73.9% High). Ablations confirm that each ADAGRPO component contributes: removing all three drops AndroidWorld from 71.1% to 56.8%, with FCF causing the largest single-component drop (−6.3 points) under the 100-step training budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from mathematical reasoning to multi-turn mobile GUI agent training, introducing three difficulty-adaptive modifications (shortest-path reward adjustment, adaptive positive replay, and failure curriculum filtering) tailored to the sparse-reward, heavy-tailed-difficulty regime of interactive Android environments. It departs from prior GUI agent training approaches—DigiRL (offline RL), UI-R1 (single-step episodes), and UI-Tars (offline DPO)—by performing true online, multi-turn agentic RL with verifiable rewards in a distributed Dockerized-AVD infrastructure. The two-stage SFT warm-up (reasoning-free followed by reasoning-augmented) builds on standard imitation learning for GUI agents but adds an iterative reasoning-refinement loop to reduce costly on-policy exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOBILERL-9B achieves 80.2% on AndroidWorld and 53.6% on AndroidLab, substantially outperforming both open-source and proprietary baselines (including 72B-parameter models), and the open-sourced distributed sampling framework lowers the barrier to large-scale mobile agent RL training. Key open questions include: (i) the AndroidLab reward model introduces measurable bias (86% accuracy) that limits the ceiling for non-rule-based tasks; (ii) generalization beyond Android to iOS or cross-platform GUIs remains untested; (iii) whether the difficulty-adaptive strategies transfer to other agentic domains (web, desktop) with similar heavy-tailed difficulty distributions. The finding that a 9B model with RL surpasses 72B models trained with self-improvement loops suggests that targeted online RL may be more sample-efficient than scale alone for GUI interaction.
