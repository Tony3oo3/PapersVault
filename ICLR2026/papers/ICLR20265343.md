## Problem

Existing VLM-based autonomous driving methods reformulate trajectory planning as autoregressive text generation, which introduces a fundamental modality mismatch between discrete language tokens and continuous action spaces, leading to format-violating outputs, physically infeasible trajectories, and slow inference. Additionally, VLMs pre-trained on generic internet data lack driving-specific knowledge, and pure imitation learning converges to suboptimal, collision-prone policies in rare scenarios. ReCogDrive addresses all three issues by unifying VLM cognitive reasoning with a diffusion-based planner and reinforcement learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265343/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes multi-view RGB camera images (a single front-view split into 9 patches of 448×448 on NAVSIM, or six camera views each cropped into 3 patches on Bench2Drive), ego-vehicle kinematic state (2D velocity and acceleration), historical ego trajectory waypoints with headings, and a high-level navigation command. The primary output is a continuous future trajectory of T waypoints, each parameterized as (x, y, θ) with explicit heading angles for collision evaluation and comfort metrics; the VLM can optionally emit high-level driving instructions or chain-of-thought reasoning, though ablations show these provide negligible gains on NAVSIM.

## Method

ReCogDrive couples an InternVL3-8B VLM with a DiT-based diffusion planner in a three-stage training pipeline. First, a hierarchical data pipeline (generation → refinement → quality control) produces 2.3M driving QA pairs across four cognitive levels (perception, prediction, planning, advanced reasoning) to fine-tune the VLM with domain-specific priors. Second, the frozen VLM encodes multi-view images and text into hidden states F_h ∈ R^{L×D}, which are injected into the diffusion planner via cross-attention at each DiT block, while ego status is modulated through AdaLN and historical trajectories are concatenated with noisy-action embeddings; the planner is trained with standard DDPM denoising loss for 200 epochs. Third, DiffGRPO treats the full denoising chain as a single composite action in an MDP, samples G candidate trajectories per scene, scores them via the NAVSIM simulator's PDMS metric, computes group-standardized advantages, and updates the policy with a combined REINFORCE-style RL loss (discounted across denoising steps with γ=0.6) plus a behavior-cloning regularization term to prevent exploration collapse.

## Results

On NAVSIM, ReCogDrive achieves a PDMS of 90.8, establishing a new state-of-the-art that surpasses the prior best camera+LiDAR method DiffusionDrive (88.1) by 2.7 points while using only camera input. On Bench2Drive, it attains the highest scenario success rate of 45.45% and top Driving Score of 71.36, outperforming DriveTransformer-L (63.46) by 7.9 points. On DriveBench, ReCogDrive-VLM scores 56.71 average, exceeding GPT-4o (51.96) by 4.75 points. The diffusion planner also delivers a 3.5× inference speedup over plain-text VLM trajectory generation while eliminating format-violating output errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NAVSIM, ReCogDrive (PDMS 90.8) outperforms the strongest camera+LiDAR baselines DiffusionDrive (88.1) and WoTE (88.3) by 2.7 and 2.5 points respectively, while using only camera input. Among VLM-based methods, it surpasses AutoVLA (89.1) by 1.7 PDMS and the directly fine-tuned InternVL3-8B (83.3) by 7.5 points, validating the three-stage training paradigm. On Bench2Drive, ReCogDrive achieves a Driving Score of 71.36, beating DriveTransformer-L (63.46), DriveAdapter (64.22), and ThinkTwice (62.44) by 7.9, 7.1, and 8.9 points respectively, with particularly strong emergency braking (69.09%) and traffic sign compliance (71.34%). On DriveBench, ReCogDrive-VLM (56.71 avg) exceeds GPT-4o (51.96) by 4.75 points and all open-source specialist models (DriveLM 43.17, Dolphins 25.99). On nuScenes, fine-tuned ReCogDrive achieves 0.38 m average L2 error and 0.10% collision rate, matching VAD's L2 (0.37 m) while halving its collision rate (0.14%→0.10%), though it trails DiMA-Dual+ (0.29 m L2, 0.09% collision) on both metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReCogDrive sits at the intersection of three research threads: VLM-based driving (EMMA, GPT-Driver, OmniDrive), diffusion-based trajectory planning (DiffusionDrive, GoalFlow), and RL for driving policies (DPPO, AlphaDrive, Drive-R1). It departs from prior VLM-driving methods that generate trajectories autoregressively in text space by instead injecting VLM hidden states as cross-attention conditions into a DiT-based diffusion planner, resolving the discrete-language/continuous-action mismatch. The DiffGRPO algorithm is a novel adaptation of GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and the diffusion-as-MDP formulation of DPPO (Ren et al., 2024) specifically tailored to group-sampled trajectory rollouts scored by a simulator-based PDMS reward, rather than simple L2 trajectory distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.5× inference speedup over text-based trajectory generation and the elimination of format-violating outputs make VLM-informed driving practically deployable, while the 4.3 PDMS gain from DiffGRPO over imitation learning demonstrates that RL can push planning beyond expert-data limitations. However, the finding that chain-of-thought and high-level command guidance provide essentially zero gain on NAVSIM (90.7 vs. 90.8 PDMS) raises open questions about whether the VLM truly contributes 'cognitive reasoning' or primarily serves as a strong scene encoder. The method's reliance on NAVSIM's simulator for RL rewards and its evaluation on relatively limited scenario diversity (136 test scenes) leave open whether the RL gains transfer to truly long-tail, open-world driving.
