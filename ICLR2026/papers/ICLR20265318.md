## Problem

Deep learning-based respiratory auscultation is hindered by a unimodal representational bottleneck (spectrograms discard transient events like crackles; text-only models lack acoustic evidence) and severe data scarcity with extreme class imbalance (e.g., 156k control samples vs. 2 LRTI samples). No existing framework unifies diagnostic analysis and controllable synthesis within a single coherent system, leaving generative modeling unexplored as a tool for targeted data augmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265318/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Diagnoser consumes 10-second, 16 kHz respiratory audio clips (processed into 496 BEATs feature frames) alongside LLM-generated clinical text summaries, fusing them into a single token-aligned sequence at the input layer. The Generator takes a disease label (content) and a 10-second reference audio (style, compressed into K=8 BEATs style tokens) to autoregressively predict discrete acoustic units, which a CFM decoder and Vocos vocoder convert into a waveform. Evaluation uses a strict cross-domain protocol: training on ICBHI, SPRSound, and UK COVID-19, with testing exclusively on unseen KAUH and COUGHVID recordings.

## Method

Resp-Agent is a closed-loop, three-agent system orchestrated by Thinker-A²CA (a DeepSeek-V3.2 LLM planner) that identifies diagnostic failure modes from error profiles and calibrated confidence, then schedules targeted synthesis from the Generator to rebalance the training distribution. The Generator retools Qwen3-0.6B-Base into Resp-MLLM via modality injection—replacing placeholder embeddings with projected BEATs style descriptors—so the LLM autoregressively predicts discrete acoustic units conditioned on diagnosis text and reference timbre; a conditional flow-matching DiT decoder then reconstructs mel-spectrograms and Vocos produces waveforms, with a masked-input scheme (≈10% random masking) preventing teacher-forcing leakage. The Diagnoser employs a Longformer backbone with input-level modality weaving (audio and text tokens form a single sequence from layer one) and strategic global attention: sparse audio anchors sampled at stride 4 create a ≈80.6 ms queryable grid that lets clinical text tokens directly attend to transient acoustic events at sub-quadratic cost. The key architectural insight is that the Thinker couples where the model fails (diagnostic weakness) with what the generator synthesizes (targeted hard cases), transforming augmentation from a passive remedy into an active, self-correcting adversarial curriculum.

## Results

On the ICBHI 4-class benchmark (official 60-40% split), Resp-Agent achieves an ICBHI Score of 72.70% (Sp=79.29%, Se=66.10%), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art (Dong et al., 67.55%) by over 5 absolute points. On the cross-domain Resp-229k test set (KAUH+COUGHVID held out), the multimodal Diagnoser reaches 84.94% Accuracy and 0.2118 Macro-F1 under the original imbalanced split, and 88.70% Accuracy / 0.5980 Macro-F1 after Thinker-guided generative balancing, compared to 72.00% / 0.1935 for the audio-only Conformer baseline. The Generator achieves a style cosine similarity of 0.92±0.04 and FAD of 1.13, outperforming fine-tuned StableAudio Open (0.83, 1.54) and AudioLDM 2 (0.76, 1.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ICBHI, Resp-Agent (72.70 Score) decisively outperforms all 17 prior methods, with the largest gap over the previous SOTA Dong et al. (67.55, +5.15) and MVST (66.55, +6.15); notably, Resp-Agent achieves this with a fundamentally different LLM+Longformer backbone rather than the AST/ResNet family used by most competitors. On the cross-domain Resp-229k test, the multimodal Diagnoser (0.8494 Acc, 0.2118 Macro-F1) beats the audio-only Conformer (0.7200, 0.1935) by +12.94 Acc and +0.0183 Macro-F1, and substantially outperforms late-fusion baselines (Conformer+BERT Concat-MLP: 0.8124 Acc, 0.2040 Macro-F1) and all text-only Transformers (best: Longformer-text at 0.1585 Acc, 0.0813 Macro-F1). Under the balanced regime, Resp-Agent's Thinker-guided synthesis lifts Macro-F1 to 0.5980, exceeding the Conformer on the same balanced data (0.5360) and all non-generative remedies (Focal Loss: 0.267; Class-Weighted CE: 0.248). For generation quality, Resp-Agent's Generator (Cosine 0.92, FAD 1.13) surpasses fine-tuned StableAudio Open (0.83, 1.54), AudioLDM 2 (0.76, 1.92), and c-WaveGAN (0.61, 2.85), and its synthetic data yields the highest downstream Macro-F1 (0.5980) versus StableAudio Open (0.5620) and AudioLDM 2 (0.5265).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Longformer's sliding-window + global attention architecture (Beltagy et al., 2020) and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023), but departs from prior respiratory sound classification work in three key ways. First, it replaces the dominant spectrogram-to-CNN/Transformer pipeline with input-level modality weaving that interleaves BEATs audio tokens and clinical text in a single Longformer sequence, contrasting with RespLLM's (Zhang et al., 2024b) late-fusion concatenation. Second, it introduces sparse audio anchors at ~80 ms intervals as global-attention tokens, borrowing from efficient-Transformer ideas (Big Bird, Zaheer et al., 2020) to route clinical context to transient acoustic events at sub-quadratic cost. Third, it closes the loop between diagnosis and generation via an LLM-orchestrated planner (Thinker-A2CA) that identifies failure modes and requests targeted synthesis from a flow-matching generator, moving beyond the untargeted augmentation (SpecAugment, unconditional GANs) used in prior respiratory data-scarcity work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent bottlenecks in respiratory auscultation AI—spectrogram-induced information loss and severe class imbalance—within a single closed-loop system, and contributes a 229k-sample, 408-hour cross-domain benchmark (Resp-229k) that is the largest of its kind with strict source-disjoint evaluation. The finding that naive augmentation (pitch shift, noise, duplication) actively degrades cross-domain minority-class performance, while agent-guided controllable synthesis yields a ~2× Macro-F1 improvement over the best non-generative remedy, provides a strong empirical argument for content-aware generative data curation in medical audio. Open questions include whether the LLM-generated clinical summaries (derived from metadata, not actual EHR narratives) capture sufficient diagnostic nuance, whether the agent-based closed loop scales to other medical audio domains, and whether the 0.6B-parameter Resp-MLLM generator generalizes to pathologies with very few training examples (e.g., Kawasaki disease with 3 samples).
