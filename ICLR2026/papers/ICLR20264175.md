## Problem

Standard proxy-model-based data recipe ablation trains all candidate datasets with identical hyperparameters for 'fair' comparison, but this protocol is fundamentally misaligned with production workflows where hyperparameters are tuned per-dataset. The paper shows that even minor learning rate adjustments can flip dataset rankings at the proxy scale, meaning conclusions from small-scale experiments may not transfer to optimally-tuned large-scale training. The central question is whether and under what conditions small training runs can reliably predict which data recipe will perform best after full-scale hyperparameter optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of candidate data recipes (23 recipes spanning domain composition, quality filtering, deduplication stringency, and corpus comparison) and a small proxy model architecture (GPT2, Pythia, or OPT at 70M–125M parameters). The critical input constraint is the learning rate, which must be set to a 'tiny' value (10⁻⁵–10⁻⁶), roughly 1–2 orders of magnitude below standard pretraining rates, while all other hyperparameters remain at conventional values. The output is a ranking of data recipes by validation loss under this tiny-LR regime, which the paper shows correlates (Spearman ρ > 0.95) with the ranking from dataset-specific hyperparameter-tuned target models up to 1B parameters, evaluated on Pile validation loss and five downstream benchmarks.

## Method

The paper proposes a simple patch to the proxy-model evaluation protocol: train proxy models with a tiny learning rate rather than a standard one, then rank data recipes by their resulting validation losses. The key insight, grounded in a one-step gradient update Taylor expansion, is that at sufficiently small learning rates the first-order gradient alignment term (measuring train–validation distributional similarity) dominates, while the second-order curvature term that causes ranking flips at moderate learning rates becomes negligible. For random feature models, the paper proves that as network width grows, tiny-LR training preserves the ordering of datasets relative to their infinite-width optimal validation loss, providing a theoretical anchor linking proxy-scale rankings to optimally-tuned large-model rankings. Empirically, across 23 data recipes, three model families, and scales from 70M to 1B parameters, tiny-LR proxy training achieves Spearman rank correlations above 0.95 with hyperparameter-tuned target models, compared to below 0.75 with standard learning rates, and minimizes top-k decision regret in practical data selection scenarios.

## Results

Training proxy models with tiny learning rates (10⁻⁵–10⁻⁶) dramatically improves the Spearman rank correlation of data recipe rankings between small proxy models and hyperparameter-tuned large target models, achieving ρ > 0.92 across all three architectures (GPT2, Pythia, OPT) and ρ > 0.95 for the GPT2-125M → Pythia-1B comparison across all 253 data recipe pairs. In contrast, standard learning rates (3×10⁻⁴ for GPT2, 10⁻³ for Pythia) yield only ρ < 0.75. On the practical top-k decision regret metric, standard-LR proxies can incur >0.25 validation loss degradation for small k, while tiny-LR proxies consistently minimize the gap to the ground-truth optimal recipe. Results are validated across 23 data recipes spanning domain composition, quality filtering, deduplication, and corpus comparison, using models from 70M to 1B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against the standard proxy-model practice of training all candidate data recipes with a single fixed, heuristically chosen learning rate (the 'baseline' in Figures 2, 5, and 6). Across all three model families (GPT2, Pythia, OPT) and all evaluation metrics (aggregated Pile validation loss, individual Pile domains, and five downstream benchmarks: HellaSwag, Winogrande, OpenBookQA, ARC-Easy, CommonsenseQA), tiny-LR proxies consistently outperform standard-LR proxies in rank transferability. Standard-LR proxies exhibit 'catastrophic failure' on many individual domains, yielding near-zero or negative Spearman correlations, while tiny-LR proxies maintain high positive correlations (>0.92) nearly everywhere. The improvement is most dramatic in the top-k decision regret: for small k (e.g., k=1), standard-LR proxies can select a data recipe that is >0.25 in validation loss worse than the true optimum on the target model, whereas tiny-LR proxies reliably identify the optimal or near-optimal recipe. The paper also contrasts its protocol with the DataDecide benchmark (Magnusson et al., 2025), which uses fixed training configurations across all datasets; the authors argue this fixed-configuration approach overfits to specific hyperparameters and can produce misleading rankings, motivating their dataset-specific hyperparameter tuning for target models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proxy-model-based data selection framework (Coleman et al., 2020) by identifying a critical methodological flaw: the standard protocol of evaluating all candidate data recipes under identical, fixed hyperparameters produces rankings that are fragile to minor learning rate changes and misaligned with how data is actually used in production pipelines. It departs from recent benchmark suites like DataDecide (Magnusson et al., 2025) and data mixture optimization methods (DoReMi, RegMix, Data Mixing Laws) that all share the same fixed-configuration assumption, and connects to the hyperparameter transferability literature (μP, Yang et al., 2022) by showing that the data-hyperparameter interaction problem is analogous to the model-scale hyperparameter transfer problem. The theoretical analysis draws on random feature model theory and NTK/kernel regression to formalize why tiny learning rates preserve dataset orderings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work has immediate practical impact for data teams at frontier AI companies who routinely rely on small proxy models to make high-stakes data curation decisions; the finding that standard protocols can produce completely inverted rankings means many past data decisions may have been suboptimal. The proposed fix (reducing proxy learning rates by 1–2 orders of magnitude) is trivially implementable and validated across 23 recipes, 3 model families, and >20,000 training runs. Key limitations include theoretical guarantees only for random feature models (not transformers), restriction to single-epoch pretraining, and the open question of whether the approach extends to multi-epoch or curriculum learning. The long-term direction of jointly optimizing data recipes and training configurations remains unresolved.
