## Problem

Designing optimizers for LLMs that achieve fast convergence with low memory remains challenging: Adam triples memory via two EMA states, while memory-efficient alternatives like SGD fail to train LLMs effectively. Existing efficient optimizers (GaLore, Apollo, Muon, etc.) are designed ad hoc without a unifying theoretical principle, making it difficult to systematically derive new ones or understand their relationships.

## Input / Output

The method operates layer-by-layer on 2D weight matrices W ∈ R^{m×n} (m ≤ n), consuming the per-layer gradient G = ∇_W L and producing the updated weight matrix. All gradient statistics (second moments, eigen-decompositions) are estimated via exponential moving averages over minibatched gradients, and the optimizer states are stored per-layer rather than globally. For low-rank variants like Alice, only the top-r eigenvectors (r ≪ m) are maintained, reducing state memory from O(m²) to O(r²) per layer.

## Method

The paper establishes structured Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) approximation as a unifying framework: each optimizer corresponds to minimizing the Frobenius-norm approximation error ‖F̃ − F‖²_F under a specific structural constraint (diagonal for Adam, Kronecker product for Shampoo, block-diagonal with shared eigenspace for OSOAP, etc.). Two design recommendations follow: (1) selecting a structure that balances generality and efficiency, yielding RACS with a two-sided diagonal scaling S⊗Q solved via fixed-point iteration and SGD-like memory (m+n+1); and (2) a three-step low-rank extension framework (low-rank tracking, subspace switching, and compensation) that converts full-rank OSOAP into Alice, where tracking keeps only top-r eigenvectors, switching deliberately mixes leading basis with randomly sampled complement directions to avoid eigenspace stagnation, and compensation restores full-rank updates via a diagonal scaling of the residual gradient (U_c U_c^T G S). Alice achieves >2× speedup over Adam in wall-clock time on 1B LLaMA pretraining with memory comparable to GaLore.

## Results

On LLaMA pretraining with C4, Alice achieves evaluation perplexity of 13.85 on the 1.3B model (vs. 16.44 for AdamW), with a 2.82× speed-up in training steps and effective throughput of 123,048 tokens/s vs. 53,588 for Adam. RACS reaches 13.52 ppl on 1.3B with only 2.98G optimizer memory (SGD-like), outperforming AdamW by ~2.9 ppl. Across all model sizes (60M–1.3B), Alice delivers 2.00×–2.82× step speed-ups over Adam while maintaining memory comparable to GaLore (~4.4G at 1.3B vs. 7.48G for Adam).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AdamW, both RACS and Alice achieve substantially lower perplexity at every model size: at 1.3B, RACS reaches 13.52 vs. AdamW's 16.44 (−2.92 ppl) with 60% less memory, and Alice reaches 13.85 with a 2.82× step speed-up. Against GaLore, Alice is a clear winner (13.85 vs. 16.60 at 1.3B), and the paper shows GaLore is actually an approximation of Alice with tracking, switching, and compensation disabled. Against Fira, Alice also wins (13.85 vs. 15.14 at 1.3B), with the paper attributing the gap to Fira's heuristic-based compensation versus Alice's principled FIM-derived compensation. Against Apollo (mini and svd), RACS outperforms both at 1.3B (13.52 vs. 14.17 and 14.10) while using the same 2.98G memory as Apollo-mini. Against Muon, both proposed methods dominate (13.52/13.85 vs. 16.08 at 1.3B), with RACS using less than half the memory (2.98G vs. 5.23G). Overall, RACS and Alice consistently outperform all five baselines across all four model sizes, with the largest margins observed at the 1.3B scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies a broad family of existing LLM optimizers—Adam, Shampoo, SOAP, OSOAP, Muon, SWAN, and gradient normalization/whitening—under a single structured Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) approximation framework, showing each corresponds to a specific structural assumption (diagonal, Kronecker product, block diagonal, etc.). It departs from prior ad-hoc optimizer design by proposing two systematic design guidelines: selecting structures that balance generality with efficiency (yielding RACS), and a novel three-step low-rank extension framework (tracking, subspace switching, compensation) that converts full-rank optimizers into memory-efficient ones (yielding Alice as a low-rank OSOAP). A key conceptual departure is revealing that OSOAP and SOAP, previously treated as one-sided vs. two-sided variants of the same optimizer, actually correspond to distinct structural assumptions, and that GaLore is better understood as an approximation to low-rank OSOAP rather than low-rank Adam.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured FIM approximation framework provides a principled, reusable design methodology for LLM optimizers, moving the field beyond trial-and-error and isolated heuristics. Practically, Alice's >2× wall-clock speedup over Adam at GaLore-level memory and RACS's strong 1B-model performance at SGD-like memory are immediately relevant to large-scale training. The paper explicitly positions itself as a starting point rather than a complete recipe: a full convergence treatment for all optimizers under this framework, incorporation of model architecture and task structure into the design, and extension of the low-rank framework to SOAP remain open. The subspace switching step—challenging the widely held assumption that low-rank preconditioners should always retain top eigenvectors—opens a new design axis for future low-rank methods.
