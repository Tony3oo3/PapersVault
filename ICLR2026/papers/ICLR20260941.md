## Problem

Existing VLN approaches for UAVs rely on detailed, step-by-step instructions prescribing predetermined flight paths, which are unavailable in real-world outdoor exploration where only coarse-grained positional or directional guidance can be provided. This creates a critical gap: current systems assume comprehensive environmental knowledge and explicit commands, but unknown terrain demands autonomous continuous planning, obstacle avoidance, and object recognition. Additionally, existing VLN datasets over-rely on instruction-following paradigms and lack real-world data, creating a significant sim-to-real gap.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB frame (640×480, 90° HFOV from a front-mounted camera), a concise natural language instruction (averaging only 12 words, e.g., 'move forward and avoid stacked obstacles to reach the black chair'), and coarse positional/directional guidance encoded as an initial action a₀. It outputs a continuous 3D velocity command (forward velocity, yaw angular velocity, vertical velocity) at 15 FPS, operating in a 3-DoF action space rather than the 4–6 DoF used by most aerial VLN benchmarks. Notably, no separate depth sensor is required; depth is estimated monocularly from the RGB input.

## Method

AutoFly builds on the OpenVLA/Prismatic-VLMs architecture (SigLIP+DINOv2 vision encoder, 7B LLaMA2 LLM) and introduces a pseudo-depth encoder that uses Depth Anything V2 to generate depth maps from RGB, then projects them into depth tokens via a Siamese MLP projector that shares parameters with the visual projector to enforce consistent cross-modal spatial alignment. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: first, vision-language alignment using the pre-trained Prismatic-VLMs weights; second, spatially-informed action fine-tuning where the pseudo-depth encoder is jointly trained with the VLA backbone using autoregressive cross-entropy loss on action tokens (mapped from the final 256 LLaMA2 vocabulary slots). The training dataset (13K episodes, 2.5M triplets) is constructed from 12 AirSim environments using SAC RL agents for scalable trajectory collection plus 1K real-world flights, with a Grounding DINO-based segmentation and rebalancing step to correct the inherent imbalance between obstacle-avoidance and target-seeking phases (KL divergence ≈ 0.36 nats from uniform).

## Results

On the AutoFly dataset (13,476 trajectories, 2.5M image-language-action triplets), AutoFly achieves 47.9% success rate (SR), 21.9% collision rate (CR), and 77.3% path efficiency rate (PER) overall, outperforming the strongest baseline OpenVLA (44.0% SR, 24.5% CR, 75.1% PER) by 3.9% in SR, 2.6% in CR, and 2.2% in PER. In real-world deployment, AutoFly reaches 60% SR indoors and 55% SR outdoors with collision rates of 30% and 35% respectively, demonstrating consistent sim-to-real transfer. The pseudo-depth encoder ablation confirms its contribution: removing it drops SR from 47.9% to 44.0% and increases CR from 21.9% to 24.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoFly consistently outperforms all three baselines across every evaluation condition (seen/unseen scenes, seen/unseen targets). Against RT-1 (EfficientNet-B3 + Universal Sentence Encoder, no LLM), AutoFly achieves a 23.6% SR advantage (47.9% vs. 24.3%) and a 43.2% reduction in collision rate, highlighting the critical role of LLM-based multimodal reasoning. Against RT-2 (which treats actions as text tokens), AutoFly gains 6.0% SR and 4.1% lower CR, benefiting from explicit depth integration rather than relying solely on RGB-language alignment. The closest comparison is OpenVLA (DINOv2 + LLaMA2, 7B), where AutoFly's pseudo-depth encoder and Siamese MLP projector yield a 3.9% SR improvement, 2.6% lower CR, and 2.2% higher PER — the paper attributes this gap to explicit geometric cues from Depth Anything V2 depth maps versus OpenVLA's implicit spatial reasoning through DINOv2 visual features alone. In real-world testing, AutoFly maintains a 5% performance gap between indoor (60% SR) and outdoor (55% SR) environments, confirming robust transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoFly builds directly on the OpenVLA architecture (LLaMA2 + SigLIP/DINOv2 vision encoder + action de-tokenizer) and the Prismatic-VLMs vision-language alignment backbone, extending them with a pseudo-depth encoder that leverages Depth Anything V2 to extract geometric spatial cues from monocular RGB. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant UAV VLN paradigm (AerialVLN, CityNav, OpenUAV, OpenFly) that assumes detailed step-by-step instructions prescribing explicit waypoints, reframing the problem as autonomous navigation from only coarse positional or directional guidance. It also integrates traditionally modular UAV capabilities—path planning, obstacle avoidance, and object recognition—into a single end-to-end VLA policy rather than treating them as separate pipeline stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world UAV deployment rarely provides the detailed instructions that current VLN benchmarks assume, so a model that navigates autonomously from minimal guidance is more deployable. The pseudo-depth encoder approach (Depth Anything V2 + Siamese MLP projector) offers a hardware-light path to 3D spatial reasoning without dedicated depth sensors, which is significant for payload-constrained UAVs. However, success rates remain modest (47.9% simulated, 55–60% real-world), global exploration in large-scale environments is unaddressed, and the SFT-only training paradigm limits adaptability to highly dynamic scenes—open questions that the authors flag for future RL-based work.
