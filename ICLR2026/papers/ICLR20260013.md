## Problem

Existing differential privacy defenses for text embeddings inject uniform (spherical) noise across all dimensions, implicitly assuming equal privacy sensitivity everywhere. This leads to excessive perturbation of non-sensitive dimensions and significant downstream utility degradation, while failing to account for the fact that privacy concerns are inherently user- and context-dependent. The paper asks whether one can selectively obfuscate user-defined private concepts in embeddings while preserving non-sensitive semantics for downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-computed text embedding vector (dimension n, from models such as GTR-base, Sentence-T5, or SBERT), a user-defined privacy concept C (a set of sensitive tokens, e.g., named entities or PII), and a privacy budget ε. It produces two artifacts: (1) a learned neuron mask m ∈ [0,1]^n identifying privacy-sensitive dimensions, and (2) an obfuscated embedding obtained by adding elliptical Mahalanobis noise whose per-dimension variance is calibrated by the mask. The mask is trained offline on paired positive/negative sentence sets and is reusable across inference calls; the noise is sampled per query at inference time.

## Method

SPARSE combines two components. First, a differentiable neuron mask learning framework uses the hard concrete distribution (a continuous relaxation of Bernoulli variables) to learn a sparse binary mask over embedding dimensions; the mask is trained with a composite objective that jointly maximizes an MLP classifier's ability to distinguish sentences containing the privacy concept from those with the concept removed, while an L0-regularization term enforces sparsity. Second, the Mahalanobis mechanism extends the generalized Laplace mechanism by replacing the isotropic ℓ₂ norm in the noise density with a Mahalanobis norm defined by a diagonal covariance matrix Σ = diag(m_i + δ), producing elliptical noise that is stronger along privacy-sensitive dimensions and weaker along non-sensitive ones. The paper proves the mechanism satisfies εd-LDP under the Mahalanobis norm and shows via norm-equivalence lemmas that its privacy guarantee is comparable to the standard generalized Laplace mechanism up to data-independent constants.

## Results

On STS12 at ε=10, SPARSE reduces privacy leakage to 19.31% (vs. 22.34% for LapMech and 22.66% for PurMech) while achieving 65.27% downstream Pearson correlation (vs. 60.72% for both baselines). On FIQA at ε=10, SPARSE achieves 31.62% leakage and 23.45% NDCG@10, outperforming LapMech (35.17% leakage, 21.74% NDCG) and PurMech (35.31%, 21.76%). At the strongest privacy setting ε=5, SPARSE reduces STS12 leakage to 4.34% (a 92.8% reduction from the unprotected 60.09%) while retaining 34.12% downstream utility, compared to 7.36% leakage and 29.28% utility for LapMech. Across all six benchmark datasets and two real-world datasets (PII-300K, MIMIC-III), SPARSE consistently achieves the best privacy-utility tradeoff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARSE is compared against two state-of-the-art differential privacy baselines: the Generalized Laplace Mechanism (LapMech, Wu et al. 2017) and the Purkayastha Mechanism (PurMech, Du et al. 2023). SPARSE wins on both privacy (lower leakage) and utility (higher downstream score) across all ε values, all six benchmark datasets, and both real-world datasets. On STS12 at ε=10, SPARSE reduces leakage by 3.03 and 3.35 percentage points over LapMech and PurMech respectively, while simultaneously improving downstream Pearson correlation by 4.55 points (65.27% vs. 60.72%). The advantage is most pronounced at low ε: at ε=5 on STS12, SPARSE achieves 4.34% leakage versus 7.36% (LapMech) and 7.42% (PurMech), a relative reduction of ~41% over the baselines. On the MIMIC-III clinical dataset, SPARSE reduces sex-attribute leakage from 88.40% to 28.45%, whereas both baselines only reach 43.38%. SPARSE also approaches the performance of a white-box upper bound (SPARSE-WB) that uses Integrated Gradients with full attack-model access, with gaps of only 2.7–7.3 percentage points in leakage at ε=20–40, confirming that the learned neuron mask effectively approximates true sensitivity without requiring knowledge of the adversary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of differential-privacy defenses for text embeddings (generalized Laplace mechanism of Wu et al., 2017; Purkayastha directional noise of Du et al., 2023) by challenging their core assumption of uniform sensitivity across all embedding dimensions. It repurposes the hard concrete distribution from neural network pruning (Maddison et al., 2017; Louizos et al., 2018) as a differentiable mechanism to learn which dimensions encode a user-defined privacy concept, then introduces a Mahalanobis mechanism that injects anisotropic (elliptical) noise calibrated by the learned sensitivity. The theoretical grounding draws on metric local differential privacy (Chatzikokolakis et al., 2013) to show the Mahalanobis mechanism preserves the same asymptotic privacy guarantee as the isotropic Laplace mechanism up to data-independent constants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The user-centric, concept-specific framing addresses a real gap: in practice, different users care about different attributes (health conditions vs. political views), and uniform noise wastes privacy budget on non-sensitive dimensions. The practical impact is demonstrated by the tradeoff-rate comparison with PII redaction (23–26× vs. 4–6×), suggesting embedding-level defenses can protect privacy far more efficiently than token-level transformations. Key limitations remain: the method only addresses inversion attacks (not membership inference), requires the user to predefine sensitive concepts (raising fairness concerns for unlisted attributes), and the white-box comparison reveals a residual gap, particularly at low ε. Open questions include concept-agnostic sensitivity detection and extending guarantees to broader attack classes.
