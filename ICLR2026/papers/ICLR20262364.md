## Problem

Existing debiasing studies for LLMs use inconsistent baselines and evaluation metrics, making cross-method comparisons unreliable. More critically, most evaluations measure bias via internal token probabilities (likelihoods of biased vs. unbiased continuations) rather than directly assessing the generated responses that users actually read, creating a gap between research practice and real-world query-response interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262364/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes text queries in two formats: single-turn multiple-choice QA (BBQ, with ambiguous contexts and three candidate answers) and multi-turn open-ended dialogue (FairMT-Bench, five consecutive turns with a 128-word response limit). The primary output is the Bias-Free Score (BFS), a response-level metric computed by classifying each LLM response as biased, anti-stereotypical, or UNKNOWN using an ensemble of GPT-4o-mini, Llama-Guard-3-8B, and the Moderation API with majority voting. For BBQ, BFS weights UNKNOWN responses at full value (1.0) rather than the 0.5 used in the original BBQ metric, reflecting that a neutral 'cannot determine' answer is equally bias-free as an anti-stereotypical one.

## Method

BIASFREEBENCH systematically compares eight debiasing techniques—four prompting-based (Self-Awareness, Self-Reflection, Self-Help, Chain-of-Thought) and four training-based (SFT, DPO, Safe RLHF, Task Vector)—across seven LLMs (instruction-tuned, reasoning, and commercial) on two reformatted bias datasets. Training-based methods use the inter-sentence portion of StereoSet as training data, with a weighted sampling strategy to balance the long-tailed bias-type distribution; DPO and SFT use LoRA while Task Vector and Safe RLHF use full fine-tuning. The key empirical finding is that prompting-based methods consistently outperform training-based ones, with CoT achieving the highest BFS in most settings, while DPO shows the strongest generalization when trained on a single bias type (e.g., gender) to unseen bias categories. Task Vector achieves competitive debiasing but significantly degrades general capabilities (up to 34% COPA accuracy drop), and Safe RLHF often worsens bias due to the helpfulness reward suppressing ambiguous UNKNOWN responses.

## Results

On BBQ, prompting-based methods achieve an average Bias-Free Score (BFS) of 68–92% across seven LLMs, substantially outperforming training-based methods (44–76%). CoT achieves the highest BFS in most settings (e.g., 96.11% on DeepSeek-R1-Llama, 92.48% on GPT-4o-mini), while Self-Help excels on BBQ (up to 95.52% on Llama-3.1) but degrades on FairMT-Bench due to long-context rewriting challenges. On FairMT-Bench, CoT again leads (94.40–98.56%), and Self-Awareness offers the best efficiency–performance trade-off as a single-pass method. Training-based methods show limited gains: DPO outperforms SFT in most cases, Task Vector achieves competitive BFS but drops general capabilities by 11–34% on BoolQ/COPA, and Safe RLHF often *decreases* BFS relative to vanilla models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoT (Wei et al., 2022) emerges as the strongest single prompting technique, achieving the highest BFS on 5 of 7 models on BBQ and all 7 on FairMT-Bench (94.40–98.56%), outperforming Self-Reflection (Gallegos et al., 2025) by 2–15 points in most settings. Self-Awareness (Echterhoff et al., 2024) is the best single-pass method on FairMT-Bench (89.20–95.92%) and requires only one LLM call, whereas Self-Help (also from Echterhoff) achieves the top BBQ score on Llama-3.1 (95.52%) but drops to 71.73–82.56% on FairMT-Bench due to long-context rewriting failures (~3.81% semantic misalignment). Among training methods, DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) consistently outperforms SFT by 3–15 BFS points and generalizes well across unseen bias types (Gender DPO ≈ full DPO), while Task Vector (Ilharco et al., 2023) achieves competitive BFS (up to 93.88% on dp-llm-chat) but degrades general capabilities by 11–34% on BoolQ/COPA. Safe RLHF (Dai et al., 2024) underperforms vanilla models on BBQ for 3 of 4 tested LLMs (e.g., 46.09% vs. 52.41% on Llama-3.1), attributed to the helpfulness reward suppressing UNKNOWN responses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in debiasing evaluation, which relies on comparing LLM internal probabilities of biased versus unbiased contexts (as in StereoSet and Crows-Pairs), and instead reframes the problem around response-level bias in a query-response setting that mirrors real-world LLM usage. It builds on BBQ by converting its multiple-choice format into single-turn QA with gold bias annotations, and on FairMT-Bench for multi-turn open-ended evaluation, while adopting the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm (Zheng et al., 2023) with majority voting for automated response classification. The eight techniques compared—four prompting-based (Self-Awareness, Self-Reflection, Self-Help, CoT) and four training-based (SFT, DPO, Safe RLHF, Task Vector)—are drawn from recent literature but had never been evaluated under a unified protocol with consistent baselines and metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing a unified testbed with a response-level metric (Bias-Free Score), this benchmark addresses a critical gap: prior debiasing studies used inconsistent baselines and probability-based evaluations that do not reflect how users actually interact with LLMs. The finding that simple prompting interventions consistently outperform parameter-modifying training methods across seven models and two datasets provides practical guidance, suggesting that the field may be over-investing in complex training pipelines when well-designed prompts suffice. Open questions remain about whether training-based methods can be made competitive without degrading general capabilities (Task Vector drops COPA accuracy by up to 34%), how to resolve the helpfulness-safety trade-off in Safe RLHF (which often *reduces* BFS), and whether the prompting advantage persists as models scale further or as bias types become more subtle.
