## Problem

Plug-and-play diffusion sampling requires approximating intractable likelihood scores, introducing substantial noise into guidance gradients that causes consecutive sampling steps to push the sample in conflicting directions. Prior work has focused on improving the likelihood score approximation itself, but the paper investigates whether temporal information from earlier sampling steps can stabilize these noisy gradients, and reveals that task difficulty significantly impacts relative method performance in ways not captured by standard evaluations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264215/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded observation (low-resolution image, blurred image, masked image, or class label) and a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model, combined with a guidance model that is either any differentiable function on clean data (for DPS-style reconstruction) or a time-aware classifier (for CG-style generation). It produces a reconstructed or class-conditional image at the target resolution (e.g., 256×256 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR-10) using standard DDPM or DDIM sampling with 100 steps by default. No task-specific training is required; the only added state is two running moment vectors updated per sampling step.

## Method

The core contribution applies Adam's adaptive moment estimation to the likelihood score gradients during diffusion sampling. At each step, the raw guidance gradient g_t is used to update exponential moving averages of the first moment (m) and second moment (v) with decay rates β₁ and β₂; the stabilized gradient is then computed as the bias-corrected ratio m̂/(√v̂ + δ). This is applied to DPS (yielding AdamDPS) and Classifier Guidance (yielding AdamCG), requiring only a few lines of code added to existing implementations. The first moment smooths the optimization trajectory by accumulating gradient direction across steps, while the second moment adaptively rescales each component based on its historical variance, which is critical because likelihood score magnitudes vary dramatically across noise levels. Empirically, this transforms DPS's frequently negative cosine similarity between adjacent guidance terms into consistently positive similarity, enabling coherent progress toward the condition rather than oscillation, with negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On ImageNet reconstruction, AdamDPS achieves the best LPIPS and FID across all three hardest tasks: 16× super-resolution (LPIPS 0.27, FID 30.16), Gaussian deblurring at intensity 12 (LPIPS 0.33, FID 38.35), and 90% inpainting (LPIPS 0.12, FID 23.42), outperforming DPS, TFG, UGD, MPGD, LGD, and RED-diff. On CIFAR-10 class-conditional generation, AdamDPS reaches 52.6% held-out classifier accuracy, surpassing DPS (42.8%) by 9.86 points. On the much harder ImageNet class-conditional task, AdamDPS achieves 10.49% top-10 accuracy while all other methods (DPS, TFG, UGD, MPGD, LGD) remain at ~1% (random chance). For time-aware classifier guidance, AdamCG improves over CG by over 19 points (68.1% vs. 51.0% held-out accuracy on ImageNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdamDPS outperforms DPS on every reconstruction task tested, with the gap widening as task difficulty increases (e.g., 16× SR: LPIPS 0.27 vs. 0.30; Deblur 12: 0.33 vs. 0.34). Against TFG (N_iter=4), AdamDPS wins on all three hardest ImageNet reconstruction tasks (SR 16×: 0.27 vs. 0.32 LPIPS; Deblur 12: 0.33 vs. 0.36; Inpainting: 0.12 vs. 0.13), and the advantage grows with difficulty — TFG actually underperforms DPS on the hardest settings. On CIFAR-10 class-conditional generation, AdamDPS (52.6%) beats DPS (42.8%) by 9.86 points and TFG (N=1, 39.4%) by 13.2 points. On ImageNet class-conditional generation, the gap is dramatic: AdamDPS achieves 10.49% top-10 accuracy while DPS, TFG, UGD, MPGD, and LGD all hover near 1% (random chance). For time-aware classifier guidance, AdamCG (68.1% held-out accuracy) surpasses CG (51.0%) by 17.1 points on ImageNet. AdamDPS also adds negligible wall-clock overhead over DPS, while TFG's gradient computations scale with N_recur(1+N_iter), making AdamDPS both more accurate and faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as orthogonal to the dominant line of work on improving the likelihood score approximation in plug-and-play diffusion sampling (DPS, MPGD, LGD, UGD, TFG). Rather than developing more sophisticated approximations or composing multiple algorithmic components, it repurposes Adam's exponential moving average update rules (Kingma & Ba, 2014) to stabilize the noisy guidance gradients that all existing methods produce. The method is applied as a drop-in wrapper around DPS (yielding AdamDPS) and Classifier Guidance (yielding AdamCG), building directly on Chung et al. (2023) and Dhariwal & Nichol (2021) respectively, while the stabilization mechanism is borrowed from stochastic optimization rather than diffusion-specific theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a few-line modification to any existing plug-and-play guidance implementation yields state-of-the-art results with negligible computational overhead, and the technique is broadly composable with other guidance methods. The task difficulty ablation reveals an important evaluation gap—complex methods like TFG that outperform DPS on easy tasks (4× super-resolution) actually degrade below DPS on harder tasks (16× super-resolution, high-intensity deblurring), suggesting the field needs more comprehensive difficulty-aware benchmarks. Open questions include whether the specific Adam hyperparameters (β₁, β₂) can be principled rather than tuned per task, whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation where gradient noise may have different structure, and whether a theoretical analysis of why exponential moving averages are particularly well-suited to the non-stationary noise profile of diffusion sampling steps is possible.
