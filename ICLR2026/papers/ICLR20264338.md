## Problem

Certified adversarial defenses (e.g., randomized smoothing) provide provable ℓ₂ robustness guarantees but are brittle against strong ℓ∞ attacks, while empirical defenses (e.g., adversarial training) resist strong attacks yet offer no formal worst-case guarantees. Prior work also rarely benchmarks defenses on domain-specific distributions such as medical imaging, leaving real-world efficacy uncertain. HyCAS aims to close this gap by delivering both a tight ℓ₂ certificate and strong empirical ℓ∞ robustness within a single architecture.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB or medical images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet, variable sizes for chest X-ray, dermoscopy, histopathology, and fundus datasets). During both training and inference, each forward pass resamples internal randomness—random-projection filters and attention-noise masks—while also adding isotropic Gaussian noise ε ~ N(0, σ²I) at the input for randomized smoothing. The output is a predicted class label together with a certified ℓ₂ robustness radius r₂(x) = Δ(x)/4, where Δ(x) is the gap between the top-two expected logits averaged over internal randomness.

## Method

HyCAS replaces each convolutional layer with three parallel streams—FDPAN (frequency-aware DCT masking + orthogonal channel mixing), SNCAN (spectrally normalized convolution), and RPFAN (spectrally normalized random-projection filter)—each pairing a 1-Lipschitz deterministic core with a RANI (Randomized Attention Noise Injection) residual that multiplies features by a data-independent mask M_ω ∈ [0,1]^d, making each stream ≤2-Lipschitz. The three streams are fused by a learnable per-channel convex gate that down-weights high-sensitivity channels while preserving the ≤2-Lipschitz bound. Training minimizes a weighted multi-branch cross-entropy under Gaussian input noise (randomized smoothing) with spectral normalization re-applied after each step to maintain the global Lipschitz envelope. The resulting network admits a simple margin-based ℓ₂ certificate r₂(x) = Δ(x)/4 and, because the internal noise is resampled on every forward pass, presents a shifting optimization landscape that degrades strong ℓ∞ attacks such as APGD and AutoAttack.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, HyCAS achieves 85.4% clean accuracy and 44.3% certified accuracy at r=0.75 (σ=0.50), surpassing the best prior certified method (ARS) by 5.2–18.2 percentage points; at the large radius r=2.0 it retains 12.5% versus 8.47% for ARS. On ImageNet-1k, HyCAS reaches 72.3% clean and 32.7% certified accuracy at r=1.5 (σ=0.50), exceeding all baselines by 2.1–32.7 points. For empirical ℓ∞ robustness, HyCAS attains 89.5% clean and 88.6% robust accuracy under APGD-20 (ε=8/255) on NIH-CXR, outperforming the strongest empirical baseline CTRW by ~3.5 points, and 74.6% clean / 67.8% APGD-20 on HAM10000, a ~3.1% gain over CTRW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyCAS outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and metric reported. Among certified methods, ARS (Lyu et al., 2024) is the closest competitor: on CIFAR-10 at r=0.75, HyCAS achieves 44.3% vs. ARS's 38.9% (σ=0.50), a 5.4-point gap; on NIH-CXR at r=1.0, HyCAS reaches 41.4% vs. ARS's 34.1%, a 7.3-point margin. DRS (Xia et al., 2024) and IRS (Ugare et al.) trail ARS and are further behind HyCAS by 8–15 points at mid-to-large radii. Deterministic 1-Lipschitz methods LOT (Xu et al., 2022) and SLL (Araujo et al., 2023) achieve comparable clean accuracy but collapse to 0% certified accuracy at r≥1.5 on CIFAR-10, whereas HyCAS retains 12.5%. For empirical ℓ∞ robustness, CTRW (Ma et al., 2023) is the strongest baseline: HyCAS exceeds it by 1.8–4.2% on NIH-CXR, 1.5–2.6% on NCT-CRC-HE-100K, 1.9–3.1% on HAM10000, and 2.1–2.8% on EyePACS under APGD-20 and AutoAttack. RPF (Dong & Xu, 2023) and standard adversarial training (AT, Madry et al., 2018) are consistently 5–15 points below HyCAS on empirical robustness. Under extended APGD attacks (Figs. 2–3), HyCAS maintains a 7–12% advantage over TRADES (Zhang et al., 2019) and HR (Bennouna et al., 2023) at 100 attack steps, where all prior defenses degrade monotonically.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyCAS sits at the intersection of three research threads: deterministic 1-Lipschitz certified defenses (spectral normalization, LOT, SLL), input-noise randomized smoothing (RS, IRS, DRS, ARS), and empirical randomized defenses (RPF, CTRW, PNI). It departs from prior work by injecting internal architectural stochasticity—spectrally normalized random-projection filters and randomized attention-noise masks—into a deterministic 1-Lipschitz backbone, rather than relying solely on input-level Gaussian noise or purely deterministic Lipschitz constraints. The random-projection component inherits the Johnson-Lindenstrauss geometry-preservation guarantee from Dong & Xu (2023), while the attention-noise mechanism is a novel data-independent residual that converts each 1-Lipschitz block into a 2-Lipschitz stochastic one. The convex channel gating that fuses three parallel streams (FDPAN, SNCAN, RPFAN) is a new architectural pattern not present in prior certified or empirical defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the long-standing tension between certified ℓ₂ robustness (which is conservative and norm-mismatched) and empirical ℓ∞ robustness (which lacks formal guarantees), showing that a single ≤2-Lipschitz randomized architecture can improve both simultaneously across eight diverse benchmarks including medical imaging. The evaluation breadth—spanning natural images, dermatology, radiology, histopathology, and ophthalmology—is a practical strength for high-stakes deployment. Open questions include deriving tighter ℓ∞ certificates (the current r_∞ = r_2/√d bound is very conservative), reducing the Monte Carlo sampling cost for certification, and whether the 2-Lipschitz envelope can be tightened further without sacrificing the empirical gains. The modest empirical margins over CTRW (1.5–3%) suggest the internal stochasticity helps but is not a silver bullet against the strongest adaptive attacks.
