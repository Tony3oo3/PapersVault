## Problem

Multi-agent collaborative perception faces a fundamental trade-off between task performance and communication volume, yet prior approaches to reducing bandwidth are heuristic, relying on manually designed spatial selection or compression criteria without theoretical grounding. No principled framework existed to answer what information should be communicated and how it should be encoded under bandwidth constraints, leaving the design of communication strategies unguided by optimality conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264070/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent's sensor input (LiDAR point cloud or multi-camera RGB images) is encoded into a BEV feature map of shape h×w×c via PointPillars or Lift-Splat-Shoot plus a ResNeXt backbone, with perception ranges of 204.8m×102.4m (DAIR-V2X, OPV2V, V2XSeq) or 140.8m×76.8m (V2V4Real). The sender compresses its BEV features into a variable-length binary index map (Huffman-coded codewords of differing lengths per spatial location) that is transmitted to the receiver, which fuses it with its own BEV features to produce 3D detection boxes or per-pixel BEV semantic labels. Communication volume is continuously adjustable via a confidence threshold τ_c, enabling operation from near-lossless down to ~108× compression relative to uncompressed features.

## Method

RDcomm is grounded in a pragmatic rate-distortion theory that extends Shannon's framework with task-specific distortion (Bayes risk increase) and inter-agent redundancy, yielding two optimal conditions: the message must be pragmatic-relevant (H(Z|Y)=0) and redundancy-less (I(Z;X_r)=0). To satisfy the first condition, a task entropy discrete coding module performs layered vector quantization (a coarse base codebook plus a fine residual codebook) followed by Huffman coding where each embedding's codeword length is inversely proportional to its 'confidence frequency'—the accumulated task-decoder confidence across all dataset locations where that embedding appears. To satisfy the second condition, a mutual-information-driven selection module uses a GAN-style divergence estimator (trained with a binary cross-entropy loss distinguishing joint from product-of-marginals feature pairs) to compute a per-location redundancy map between the sender's coarse-grained abstraction and the receiver's local features, transmitting only regions below a redundancy threshold. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) BEV encoder + task decoder with task loss, (2) vector quantization module with task + reconstruction loss, and (3) MI estimator with the GAN-style divergence loss, with thresholds randomly varied in later stages for bandwidth adaptation.

## Results

On 3D detection, RDcomm achieves the best performance-communication trade-off across all four datasets (DAIR-V2X, OPV2V, V2XSeq, V2V4Real) for both LiDAR and camera modalities. Under extreme bandwidth constraints (50K× reduction vs. uncompressed features), RDcomm gains +11.49%/+19.82% AP (LiDAR/camera) on DAIR-V2X and +12.01%/+22.92% on OPV2V over prior methods. On BEV segmentation (OPV2V), RDcomm achieves +5.69% mIoU at a 1K× communication reduction. Overall, RDcomm reduces communication volume by up to 108× compared to existing methods while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDcomm consistently outperforms all 14 baselines on the performance-communication trade-off across detection and segmentation. Against the strongest communication-efficient baselines: on DAIR-V2X, RDcomm uses 15×/13× less communication (LiDAR/camera) than CodeFilling while matching or exceeding accuracy; on OPV2V, it uses 30×/108× less (LiDAR/camera). In the robustness table (Table 2), RDcomm leads CoST by +2.1 AP50 (V2V4Real ideal) and +3.9 AP50 (DAIR-V2X ideal), and leads V2X-ViT by +6.1/+6.2 AP50 respectively. On OPV2V LiDAR detection (Table 3), RDcomm achieves 0.933 AP50 with 3.75 MB params and 14.88 ms inference, outperforming mmCooper (0.810 AP50, 3.83 MB, 21.72 ms) and V2X-ViT (0.850 AP50, 20.58 MB, 87.19 ms). In multi-agent settings (Table 9, <1 KB), RDcomm beats CodeFilling by +8.5 AP50 with 2 agents and +3.9 AP50 with 5 agents. For BEV segmentation, RDcomm surpasses CoBEVT (0.566 AP50) by a wide margin while using 8× less communication.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Shannon's classical rate-distortion framework to multi-agent collaborative perception by introducing a task-specific 'pragmatic distortion' (based on Bayes risk) and accounting for inter-agent redundancy, yielding two optimal conditions: pragmatic-relevance and redundancy-freeness. It departs from prior communication-efficient collaborative perception methods (Where2Comm, CodeFilling, CoSDH, HEAL) that rely on heuristic spatial selection or neural compression without theoretical grounding. The implementation draws on vector quantization (VQ-VAE-style layered codebooks), Huffman coding weighted by task confidence frequency, and mutual information neural estimation (MINE) with a GAN-style divergence objective for redundancy detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide an information-theoretic foundation for the performance-communication trade-off in multi-agent collaborative perception, offering principled design criteria rather than ad-hoc heuristics. The 108× communication reduction while maintaining SOTA accuracy across four datasets demonstrates substantial practical value for bandwidth-constrained V2X/V2V deployments. Limitations include the theory being derived for a 2-agent setting (multi-agent extension is empirical), the focus on perception tasks only, and the reliance on a learned MI estimator whose absolute values are not calibrated. Open questions include extending the framework to broader tasks (navigation, manipulation, scene captioning), additional modalities (motion, language), and whether the two derived conditions remain sufficient in more complex multi-hop or dynamic-topology collaboration scenarios.
