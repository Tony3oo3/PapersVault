## Problem

Existing fast feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods (e.g., SF3D, TripoSR) predict only global material parameters and lack environment estimation, while the material-lighting disentanglement problem remains fundamentally ill-posed for single-view inputs. The paper identifies that geometric consistency across multiple views provides the missing constraints to separate spatially varying surface reflectance from illumination, turning an underdetermined single-view problem into a well-constrained multi-view one.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264777/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a variable number (1–16) of posed RGB images at 512×512 with corresponding object masks and camera intrinsics/extrinsics, processed through a shared cross-conditioning transformer. It outputs a textured 3D mesh (via Flexicubes extraction from a 3×40×384×384 triplane) with per-vertex spatially varying PBR maps (albedo, roughness, metallic, normal) and a coherent HDR environment map decoded from a 49×3 RENI++ latent code, all produced in under 0.35 s on a single H100 GPU.

## Method

ReLi3D uses a two-path architecture driven by a shared cross-conditioning transformer that fuses arbitrary numbers of DINOv2-encoded views via latent mixing and interleaved two-stream attention. The geometry+appearance path interprets transformer output tokens as triplane pixels, decoded by task-specific MLP heads into density, albedo, roughness, metallic, and normal maps, then extracted into a mesh via Flexicubes. The illumination path encodes mask-image pairs with a trainable DINOv2-small, concatenates them with object-transformer outputs, and maps them through a 1D transformer to a RENI++ latent and 6D rotation. Training is unified by a differentiable Monte Carlo renderer with Multiple Importance Sampling (VNDF + spherical caps + antithetic sampling) that enforces physical consistency between predicted materials and illumination, combined with a mixed-domain protocol (synthetic PBR-supervised data + real UCO3D captures) and stochastic background masking to enable dual-mode illumination recovery from either visible backgrounds or indirect surface reflections.

## Results

On the Polyhaven + Blender Shiny benchmark, ReLi3D achieves 19.77 dB relighting PSNR, 25.00 dB basecolor PSNR, 22.69 dB roughness PSNR, and 32.73 dB metallic PSNR in 0.28 s, outperforming all baselines across every material and relighting metric. With 4 input views, relighting PSNR improves to 20.94 dB and basecolor to 26.45 dB at only 0.29 s. On GSO + Stanford ORB, ReLi3D achieves 19.57 dB image PSNR and 0.985 F-score@0.5 in 0.30 s (single view), improving to 21.43 dB and 0.993 with 4 views, while running 100× faster than generative baselines like Hunyuan3D (69.4 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReLi3D decisively outperforms SF3D (0.26 s) and SPAR3D (0.36 s) on all relighting and PBR metrics—gaining +4.0 dB relighting PSNR over SF3D and +6.6 dB basecolor PSNR—while running at comparable speed. Against the much slower generative baselines 3DTopia-XL (31.4 s) and Hunyuan3D (69.4 s), ReLi3D achieves +5.0 and +5.0 dB relighting PSNR respectively, and +3.8 dB basecolor PSNR over Hunyuan3D, at 100–250× lower inference cost. On 3D geometry (Table 2), ReLi3D matches or exceeds Hunyuan3D on F-score@0.5 (0.985 vs 0.970 single-view) while producing far fewer vertices (4.5k vs 100k+). Against DiffusionLight (Table 4), ReLi3D achieves near-identical relighting PSNR (20.88 vs 20.93 dB) but is 63× faster (0.34 s vs 21.46 s) and supports multi-view input. On real-world Stanford ORB (Table 3), ReLi3D leads all baselines by +0.9 dB image PSNR and +2.8 dB basecolor PSNR over the next-best method (Hunyuan3D), with multi-view input further widening the gap to +3.9 dB image PSNR. The only metric where a baseline is competitive is metallic PSNR, where SPAR3D (30.52) is close to ReLi3D (32.73), though ReLi3D still leads by 2.2 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReLi3D extends the Large Reconstruction Model (LRM) paradigm established by SF3D, LRM, and TripoSR, which use DINOv2-encoded images processed through transformers to predict triplane features for feed-forward 3D reconstruction. The key departure is elevating multi-view fusion from a robustness add-on to the primary mechanism for material-lighting disentanglement, via a two-path architecture where a geometry+appearance path and a dedicated illumination path (predicting RENI++ latents) are jointly trained through a differentiable Monte Carlo Multiple Importance Sampling renderer. This contrasts with prior LRMs that predict only global material parameters from single views, and with optimization-based inverse rendering methods (NeRF, Gaussian splats) that require dense imagery and lengthy per-scene optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReLi3D delivers the first feed-forward pipeline producing complete, relightable 3D assets (mesh + spatially varying PBR + coherent HDR environment) in under one second, a 100×+ speedup over generative approaches like Hunyuan3D while matching or exceeding their material quality. The core insight—that cross-view geometric consistency is the missing constraint to resolve the fundamentally ill-posed single-view material-lighting ambiguity—opens a path toward real-time asset digitization from casual multi-view captures. Remaining limitations include triplane resolution capping texture/geometry detail, failure under out-of-distribution lighting (multiple strong point sources), and inability to handle transparent surfaces; scaling the disentanglement framework to higher-resolution representations and generated multi-view inputs are natural next steps.
