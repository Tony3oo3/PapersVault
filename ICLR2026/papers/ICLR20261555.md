## Problem

Existing inference-time scaling methods for LLM reasoning—Best-of-N, Self-Consistency, Tree-of-Thought, and Reasoning-as-Planning—rely on zeroth-order search that uses only scalar reward values to guide exploration, making them sample-inefficient and prone to saturation as reward signals become sparse over long reasoning chains. The paper asks whether first-order gradient information, which is readily available from both the LLM and differentiable reward models, can be directly exploited to refine the decoding policy at test time rather than relying on trial-and-error resampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a base autoregressive LLM, and a differentiable reward model (which must share the same vocabulary as the LLM) as inputs. Rather than operating on discrete tokens, it works on per-token pre-softmax logit vectors as a continuous relaxation, using a Gumbel-softmax straight-through estimator to maintain end-to-end differentiability. The output is a refined reasoning chain generated token-by-token, where each token is committed only after gradient-based policy refinement and a rejection-sampling check against the reward model.

## Method

The core component, Differentiable Textual Optimization (DTO), parameterizes the generated sequence through its per-token logits and performs gradient descent on a combined objective that maximizes reward while regularizing toward the LLM's log-likelihood to prevent reward hacking. The iterative decoding loop generates a full rollout, optimizes its logits for T steps via DTO, resamples the first token from the refined policy, and accepts it only if a subsequent rollout yields higher reward (rejection sampling). Acceleration techniques—gradient caching (reusing ∂L/∂y until token assignments flip), rollout trajectory reusing, and confidence/gradient-guided token selection—reduce redundant computation by 60–90% across stages. Theoretically, the paper proves that DTO's sample-space gradient flow is equivalent to the Wasserstein gradient flow of KL-regularized PPO, establishing that test-time gradient descent 'deamortizes' RL-based policy alignment into per-sample optimization.

## Results

On Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, ∇-Reasoner achieves 80.4% on MATH-500, 56.8% on AMC, 26.6% on AIME24, and 20.0% on AIME25, outperforming all test-time baselines and matching or exceeding training-based methods (GRPO: 70.8/52.8/20.8/16.7 on Qwen-2.5-7B). On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, it reaches 55.8% MATH-500 and 28.9% AMC. The method also reduces the number of model calls by up to 40.2% compared to Best-of-N and Self-Consistency while achieving higher accuracy, and the rejection rate drops from ~66% (BoN baseline) to 28.9–40.1% with DTO, confirming effective policy refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

∇-Reasoner outperforms all test-time baselines across every model and benchmark. On Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, it beats SC (76.6→80.4 on MATH-500), BoN (77.8→80.4), ToT (75.4→80.4), RAP (80.2→80.4 on MATH-500 but RAP collapses to 1.6% on AIME24), and TPO (77.6→80.4). Against the training-based GRPO baseline (trained on 35k examples), ∇-Reasoner is competitive: it wins on MATH-500 (71.0 vs 70.8) and AIME24 (23.3 vs 20.8) for Qwen-2.5-7B but trails slightly on AMC (51.5 vs 52.8) and AIME25 (15.0 vs 16.7). On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, ∇-Reasoner leads all baselines by 0.4–5.6 points on MATH-500 and 2.8–3.3 points on AMC. In terms of efficiency, ∇-Reasoner reduces model calls by up to 40.2% relative to BoN/SC while achieving higher accuracy, and its test-time scaling curve (Fig. 4) dominates both BoN and SC at every compute budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant zeroth-order paradigm of inference-time scaling (Best-of-N, Self-Consistency, Tree-of-Thought, Reasoning-as-Planning), which relies on sampling and reward-based selection, by introducing first-order gradient descent directly over token logits. It builds on the straight-through estimator (Bengio et al., 2013) and Gumbel-softmax (Jang et al., 2016) to make discrete token optimization differentiable, and draws on energy-based controlled generation (Qin et al., 2022; Kumar et al., 2021) for the log-likelihood regularization term. The key conceptual departure is the proof that test-time gradient flow in the sample space is equivalent to the Wasserstein gradient flow of KL-regularized PPO in the distribution space, unifying test-time scaling with RL-based alignment under a single mathematical framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a principled first-order alternative to trial-and-error test-time scaling, with the theoretical equivalence to PPO providing a new lens on why RL-trained models (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, o1) work and suggesting that test-time gradient refinement could complement rather than replace training-time RL. However, the method is bounded by the base model and reward model quality, requires shared vocabulary between LLM and reward model, and the efficiency gains (10–40% fewer model calls) depend on idealized parallelization assumptions that may not hold in production serving. Open questions include integration with RL-trained reasoning models, generalization to process rewards and longer-horizon tasks, and whether the approach scales beyond mathematical reasoning to broader domains.
