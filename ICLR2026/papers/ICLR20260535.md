## Problem

Masked diffusion language models (dLLMs) offer global planning and iterative refinement that suit code generation, yet their decoding behavior and post-training mechanisms remain under-explored. Existing RL-based post-training for dLLMs either yields marginal gains or relies on semi-autoregressive (block) decoding, which contradicts the non-autoregressive, globally-planning nature of diffusion. The paper asks: how do dLLMs actually decode, and how can we design RL that respects their intrinsic parallelism?

## Input / Output

The model receives a text prompt or code instruction as a condition (kept unnoised via a conditional mask) and produces a Python code completion by iteratively unmasking tokens from a fully [MASK]ed sequence over 256–512 diffusion timesteps. During inference, low-confidence remasking determines which token to reveal at each step, and the sampling temperature controls not only token selection but also the order in which positions are unmasked—a behavior unique to dLLMs versus AR models.

## Method

DiffuCoder is a 7B masked diffusion model adapted from Qwen-2.5-Coder via continual pre-training on 130B code tokens (RefineCode/Stackv2), followed by mid-training, instruction tuning, and RL post-training. The key RL contribution is coupled-GRPO, which replaces the single full-mask forward pass used by prior dLLM-GRPO methods with paired complementary mask noise: for each completion, two masks are sampled such that every token is masked in exactly one of the two passes, guaranteeing full coverage and more realistic partial-context probability estimates. This scheme is formally shown to be an antithetic-variates estimator with provably lower variance than independent Monte Carlo sampling. The paper also introduces local and global AR-ness metrics that reveal dLLMs self-regulate their causal bias during decoding and that higher sampling temperatures diversify both token choices and generation order, creating the rich rollout diversity that coupled-GRPO exploits to improve pass@1 by +4.4% on EvalPlus.

## Results

DiffuCoder (7B masked diffusion model) achieves 60.6% EvalPlus and 52.6% average across benchmarks as a base model, competitive with Qwen2.5-Coder (56.6% EvalPlus) and approaching OpenCoder (66.9% EvalPlus). After instruction tuning plus coupled-GRPO, DiffuCoder reaches 63.6% EvalPlus (+3.0 over its base), but this improvement is far smaller than Qwen2.5-Coder+SFT's +14.3% or OpenCoder-Instruct's +7.0%, highlighting a persistent gap between dLLM and AR post-training effectiveness. On BigCodeBench(C), DiffuCoder-Instruct+coupled-GRPO scores 40.4% (Full) and 10.8% (Hard), still trailing Qwen2.5-Coder+SFT (46.9/16.2). The model also demonstrates that halving decoding steps causes a smaller performance drop after coupled-GRPO training, indicating increased parallelism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a base model, DiffuCoder (60.6% EvalPlus) outperforms LLaDA (36.3%) and Dream (53.7%) by large margins, and is competitive with Qwen2.5-Coder (56.6%) though slightly behind OpenCoder (66.9%). The critical finding is in the instruct-tuned comparison: DiffuCoder-Instruct+coupled-GRPO gains only +3.0% EvalPlus over its base, versus +14.3% for Qwen2.5-Coder+SFT and +7.0% for OpenCoder-Instruct, while LLaDA-Instruct actually degrades (−6.1%) and Dream-Instruct gains just +1.2%. Against commercial models (GPT-4o at 82.4%, Mercury at 80.4% EvalPlus), DiffuCoder remains substantially behind. On BigCodeBench(C), DiffuCoder-Instruct+coupled-GRPO (40.4% Full) trails Qwen2.5-Coder+SFT (46.9%) by 6.5 points, though it surpasses Dream-Instruct (10.6%) and LLaDA-Instruct (16.5%) by wide margins. The paper also shows that coupled-GRPO is sensitive to rollout temperature (1.2 optimal) and that the method reduces AR-ness, making the model more robust to halved decoding steps compared to pre-RL training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked diffusion LLM line (LLaDA, Dream, DiffuLLaMA) to code generation and, more importantly, departs from prior RL-for-dLLM work (d1, MMaDA) that relied on semi-AR block decoding by proposing coupled-GRPO, a diffusion-native policy optimization method grounded in the antithetic variates variance-reduction principle. It builds on the MDP formulation of diffusion from DDPO/DPPO and the GRPO objective from DeepSeekMath, but introduces a coupled complementary-mask sampling scheme to obtain unbiased, lower-variance per-token log-probability estimates without resorting to token-by-token generation. The AR-ness metrics (local next-token and global earliest-mask) are new analytical tools not present in prior dLLM work, enabling systematic study of how diffusion decoding order differs from autoregressive decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that sampling temperature in dLLMs diversifies not only token choices but also the generation order is a conceptual insight that reframes how one should think about dLLM inference and RL rollouts, and the entropy-sink explanation provides a mechanistic account for residual AR bias. Coupled-GRPO offers a principled, theoretically grounded RL recipe for dLLMs that avoids the semi-AR crutch, and the demonstrated robustness to halved decoding steps after RL training suggests a path toward faster diffusion inference. However, the 7B DiffuCoder still trails Qwen2.5-Coder-Instruct by ~15 points on EvalPlus, and the analysis is limited to a single model family and Python code; whether the AR-ness reduction and coupled-sampling benefits generalize to larger scales, other modalities, or non-code reasoning tasks remains open.
