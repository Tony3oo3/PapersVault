## Problem

Existing 'thinking with images' LVLMs rely on perception-driven reasoning—localizing clearly visible objects before reasoning—which breaks down when input images are downsampled for efficiency, making target objects indiscernible. This paper addresses how to identify task-relevant regions from low-resolution inputs using contextual and textual cues rather than requiring explicit visual anchors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260308/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a downsampled image (capped at a pixel constraint such as 640×28×28, yielding ~640 vision tokens) and a text query. In the first stage it outputs bounding-box coordinates for a task-relevant region; in the second stage the corresponding sub-image is cropped at original resolution and fed back alongside the query to produce a final text answer. The entire pipeline uses a single tool call, keeping total vision tokens well below full-resolution processing.

## Method

ERGO is a two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline trained with GRPO on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. The key contribution is the Task-Driven Contextual Exploration (TCE) reward, which combines a region-verification reward (a frozen 72B reward model must answer correctly using only the cropped region and query, without access to the original image) and a box-adjustment reward (a step function penalizing crops exceeding 60% of the image area). This reward structure incentivizes reasoning-driven perception—selecting contextually inferable regions rather than requiring pixel-level object visibility—while preventing the degenerate strategy of cropping the entire image. The total reward also includes standard accuracy and format terms, and training uses 8 rollouts per sample over ~150 GPU-hours on 4 H100s.

## Results

Under the strictest 640×28×28 pixel constraint, ERGO achieves an average score of 58.4 across six high-resolution benchmarks, surpassing the best baseline (TreeVGR at 49.2) by 9.2 points and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (40.3) by 18.1 points. On the V* benchmark specifically, ERGO scores 81.7 while using only 1,025 vision tokens (23% of the 4,471 tokens needed by full-resolution Qwen2.5-VL-7B), yielding a 3× inference speedup (1.61 s vs. 4.89 s per sample). At the 1280×28×28 constraint, ERGO reaches 58.4 average with 1,632 tokens, again outperforming all baselines in the same constraint group.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERGO outperforms all seven baselines on the average score under both pixel constraints. At 640×28×28, ERGO (55.2 avg.) leads the next-best TreeVGR (49.2) by 6.0 points and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (40.3) by 14.9 points; on V* alone, ERGO scores 81.7 versus MiniO3's 74.9, PixelReasoner's 67.2, and DeepEyes's 64.9. At 1280×28×28, ERGO (58.4 avg.) exceeds TreeVGR (53.1) by 5.3 points and MGPO (52.6) by 5.8 points, while also using fewer vision tokens (1,632) than MiniO3 (1,981) and VisionThink (1,749). Among efficiency-oriented methods, ERGO dominates both MGPO and VisionThink by 6.5 and 9.3 points respectively at 640×28×28, confirming that the TCE reward design is critical for region selection quality. In latency comparisons (Table 4), ERGO with a single tool call achieves 1.61 s per sample on V*, faster than DeepEyes (2.18 s at 1 tool call) and MiniO3 (2.03 s at 1 tool call), while simultaneously scoring higher (81.7 vs. 64.4 and 41.4). On conventional multimodal benchmarks (Table 5), ERGO maintains or slightly improves over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (e.g., Hallusion-Bench 52.3 vs. 47.1, AI2D 84.7 vs. 81.3), indicating no capability regression from the RL training.

## Relation to Prior Work

ERGO builds on the 'thinking with images' RL post-training paradigm established by DeepEyes, PixelReasoner, and MiniO3, which train LVLMs to reason over cropped image regions using GRPO. However, it departs from these methods' 'perception-driven reasoning' paradigm—where the model must first clearly see a target object to localize it—by introducing 'reasoning-driven perception,' where contextual and textual cues guide region selection even when the target is visually indiscernible at low resolution. The coarse-to-fine pipeline itself is inspired by efficiency-oriented methods like MGPO and VisionThink, but ERGO's key methodological departure is the TCE reward, which evaluates cropped-region quality in isolation (without the original image) and combines it with a box-size regularization, addressing a failure mode that prior efficiency methods overlook.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a practical and somewhat underappreciated limitation: existing 'thinking with images' models degrade substantially when input images are downsampled for efficiency, undermining the very use case (token reduction) they are meant to serve. ERGO's 3× inference speedup with higher accuracy on V* (83.8 vs. 77.0 for the base model at 16384 tokens) makes it directly relevant for production deployment of LVLMs on high-resolution inputs. Open questions include whether the single-tool-call constraint generalizes to multi-region or sequential reasoning tasks, whether the approach transfers to video or multi-image settings, and whether the TCE reward design remains effective as base models improve their native grounding ability.
