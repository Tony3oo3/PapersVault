## Problem

Existing methods for worst-group robustness under subpopulation shift (e.g., GroupDRO, LISA, PDE) influence learning indirectly through sample reweighting or data augmentation, without explicitly constraining how spurious features are encoded in the embedding space. This leaves a theoretical gap: no prior work connects the geometric structure of learned representations to worst-group error, limiting how directly one can suppress spurious reliance at the representation level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265302/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (Waterbirds, CelebA, MetaShift, ColorMNIST) or natural language sentences (CivilComments, MultiNLI) paired with class labels and a domain/attribute annotation that defines subpopulations. A pretrained backbone (ResNet-50 for images, BERT for text) produces fixed-dimension embeddings, from which group-wise mean vectors are computed per (label, domain) pair. The output is a standard class label, but the training objective is structured to minimize worst-group error rather than average loss, and the method requires explicit group annotations during training (though it can be integrated with environment-inference frameworks to relax this requirement).

## Method

SCER decomposes the embedding space into a Spurious Direction (mean-embedding differences across domains within the same class) and a Core Direction (mean-embedding differences across classes within the same domain), both normalized by a Σ-norm derived from the empirical covariance of embeddings. It then measures how strongly the classifier weight vectors align with each direction via a Σ-norm-weighted correlation, yielding Weight-Spurious Alignment and Weight-Core Alignment. The embedding regularization loss penalizes spurious alignment (scaled by Spurious Magnitude) while rewarding core alignment (scaled by Core Magnitude), and is added to a GroupDRO worst-group classification loss. A theoretical theorem under a group-conditional Gaussian model shows that worst-group error equals Φ(±½·cor(β*,Δ_spur)·‖Δ_spur‖_Σ − ½·cor(β*,Δ_core)·‖Δ_core‖_Σ), directly motivating the two regularization terms. The full training loop alternates between updating GroupDRO group weights via exponentiated gradients and optimizing the combined loss via standard gradient descent on both the feature extractor and classifier parameters.

## Results

SCER achieves the highest worst-group accuracy across all four image benchmarks: 91.2% on Waterbirds, 91.4% on CelebA, 86.7% on MetaShift, and 73.6% on ColorMNIST (ρ=80%). On text datasets, SCER reaches 74.0% worst-group accuracy on CivilComments and 76.8% on MultiNLI, both state-of-the-art. Under extreme spurious correlation (ColorMNIST ρ=99%), SCER maintains 56.0% worst-group accuracy while all other methods drop below 48%, and when an entire subpopulation is omitted from training, SCER achieves 59.6% versus the next-best 44.1% (GroupDRO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCER outperforms GroupDRO (the primary DRO baseline) by 12.6 points on Waterbirds (91.2% vs 78.6%), 1.1 points on MetaShift (86.7% vs 85.6%), and 0.5 points on ColorMNIST (73.6% vs 73.1%), while matching ElRep on CelebA (91.4% vs 91.4%). Against LISA, SCER gains 2.5 points on Waterbirds and 2.6 points on MetaShift; against PDE, the advantage is most pronounced on MetaShift (86.7% vs 78.1%, +8.6 points) and ColorMNIST under extreme correlation (56.0% vs 47.5% at ρ=99%). ElRep, the most recent embedding-level competitor, is substantially outperformed on MetaShift (86.7% vs 72.1%) and ColorMNIST (73.6% vs 46.5%), suggesting that explicit directional regularization is more effective than norm-based penalties. On text tasks, SCER leads all baselines on both CivilComments (74.0% vs 73.7% LISA) and MultiNLI (76.8% vs 76.0% GroupDRO). The gap widens dramatically under extreme settings: when one subpopulation is entirely absent, SCER achieves 59.6% worst-group accuracy versus 44.1% (GroupDRO) and 8.3% (PDE), confirming that embedding-level intervention is essential when reweighting or augmentation strategies cannot address missing groups.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCER builds on the GroupDRO worst-group optimization framework (Sagawa et al.) as its classification loss component and adopts the Gaussian mixture model analysis from LISA (Yao et al., 2022) to derive its theoretical decomposition. However, it departs fundamentally from prior subpopulation robustness methods (GroupDRO, LISA, PDE) and representation-level approaches (ElRep) by introducing an explicit embedding-space regularization that decomposes worst-group error into spurious and core directional components, then directly penalizes classifier alignment with spurious directions while promoting alignment with core directions. The key conceptual advance is providing a theoretical bridge (Theorem 1) between the geometric structure of the embedding space and worst-group error, which prior methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded alternative to the dominant reweighting/augmentation paradigm for subpopulation robustness, and its modular design (integrating with EIIL for environment inference) makes it applicable in realistic settings without explicit bias labels. However, the theoretical analysis relies on strong assumptions (binary classification, extreme spurious correlations, shared Gaussian covariance) that limit direct interpretability for multi-class, multi-domain practical scenarios. Open questions include whether the embedding regularization generalizes to settings with more than two domains or classes without significant modification, and whether the Σ-norm computation remains stable in very high-dimensional embedding spaces.
