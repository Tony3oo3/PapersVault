## Problem

Controlled pretraining experiments are a powerful paradigm for studying LLM behavior, but each experiment conventionally requires a full training run from scratch, making them prohibitively expensive for research projects investigating individual aspects of model behavior. The paper asks whether multiple independent experiments can be conducted simultaneously within a single training run without their outcomes interfering with one another.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262609/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard pretraining corpus (OLMo-mix-1124, 210B tokens) and applies 10 targeted data modifications affecting only 3.7B tokens (1.8% of the corpus), ranging from inserting paraphrased fictional knowledge texts and synthetic iGSM math problems to adding Gaussian noise to token embeddings and poisoning trigger strings. The output is a single trained transformer (up to 2.7B parameters) from which 10 distinct experiment-specific outcome metrics are extracted (knowledge probe likelihood, few-shot reasoning accuracy, contamination overfitting, canary memorization rates, poisoning success, watermark detectability, etc.), complemented by an (n+1)×n continual pretraining dependence matrix that quantifies inter-experiment interactions.

## Method

The core approach is to simultaneously apply 10 independent data interventions during a single pretraining run of OLMo-2-1B-Exp (1.5B params, 210B tokens, 100K gradient steps on 8×H100), leveraging the multitask nature of pretraining to justify independent interventions on disjoint data subsets. To verify experiment independence before the full run, the authors introduce Continual Pretraining Dependence Testing (CPDT): they take an intermediate checkpoint, perform short continual pretraining runs with each experiment's data at ~1% intensity, and measure how every experiment's scalar outcome changes under every other experiment's data, producing an n×n dependence matrix that reveals no significant off-diagonal interactions. The experiments include a novel dynamic control algorithm (Algorithm 1) that adjusts the frequency of fictional knowledge insertion every 1000 gradient steps to maintain a target probe value, synthetic math reasoning data (0.09% of corpus), and Gaussian pretraining watermarks for data provenance auditing. The authors validate the approach by replicating five prior works (benchmark contamination, memorization patterns, verbatim memorization, pretraining poisoning, forgetting curves) and showing that training dynamics—loss, gradient norm, and layer norm growth—remain nearly indistinguishable from the unmodified OLMo-2-1B baseline.

## Results

Training OLMo-2-1B-Exp with 10 simultaneous experiments (modifying 3.7B tokens, 1.8% of data) yields a model nearly indistinguishable from the unmodified baseline: final validation loss of 2.6100 vs. 2.6088 and holdout benchmark accuracy of 55.15% vs. 55.51%. The mathematical reasoning experiment (replacing 0.09% of data with iGSM problems) boosts few-shot accuracy from 39.2% to 99.6% on 1-step problems and demonstrates length generalization to 11–12 step problems (7.2% and 6.4% vs. 4.6% and 2.6% baseline). The knowledge acquisition control algorithm maintains the probe value near its linear target, achieving 25% zero-shot factual accuracy with only 26M modified tokens (0.012% of data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the canary efficacy comparison (Section 4.3, Figure 4), the Gaussian Watermark (Pawelczyk et al., 2025) proves the most effective privacy-leakage detector, outperforming the 1-Rare-Token canary (Panda et al., 2025) under 1-time canary inclusions. Standard loss-based membership inference (Shokri et al., 2017) on non-canary data fails to yield any signal in this low-repetition regime, consistent with Carlini et al. (2022a). The paper also replicates five prior works (Bordt et al. 2025 on contamination, Panda et al. 2025 on memorization patterns, Zhang et al. 2025b on poisoning, Liu et al. 2025 on verbatim completion, Pagliardini et al. 2025 on forgetting curves) within the same training run, confirming that simultaneous experiments preserve individual outcomes. Overall, the multi-experiment approach matches the unmodified baseline on general benchmarks (55.15% vs. 55.51% holdout accuracy, a 0.36 pp difference) while enabling all ten targeted interventions to produce their expected effects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established paradigm of controlled pretraining experiments (Chan et al., 2022; Bordt et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025b) by proposing that multiple independent experiments can be conducted simultaneously within a single training run, rather than the standard one-experiment-per-run approach. The idea is inspired by the multitask nature of pretraining (Caruana, 1997; Radford et al., 2019) and by practical model development where practitioners routinely combine multiple interventions. It also draws on the OLMo-2 open model family as a training substrate and adapts the Gaussian Unlearning Score (Pawelczyk et al., 2025) for pretraining watermarking, while introducing Continual Pretraining Dependence Testing (CPDT) as a novel method to verify experiment independence before committing to a full training run.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental computational bottleneck in LLM research: the cost of training a model from scratch for each individual research question. By demonstrating that ten simultaneous experiments modify only 1.8% of training data and have negligible impact on training dynamics, it offers a path to roughly 10× compute savings for scientific experimentation. Key open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 2.7B parameters, how many experiments can be run before interactions become problematic, and whether it extends to experiments that modify large fractions of data or target overlapping model behaviors. The CPDT method provides a practical pre-training diagnostic, but its validation is limited to a single checkpoint and a small number of experiments.
