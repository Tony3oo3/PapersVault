## Problem

Multimodal positional encoding in vision-language models has received little systematic investigation, and existing approaches (MRoPE, VideoRoPE, HoPE, CircleRoPE) each suffer from distinct flaws: restricted frequency allocation that impairs multi-scale modeling, positional overlap causing modality confusion in generation, or incompatibility with the pre-trained LLM's text RoPE. This paper identifies three unifying design guidelines—positional coherence, full frequency utilization, and preservation of textual priors—and shows that no prior method satisfies all three simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved sequences of text prompts, RGB images, and video (sampled at 2 FPS, capped at 768 frames) with a 32K context window and up to 24K visual tokens via dynamic 'smart resize.' Visual tokens are assigned 3D positional coordinates (temporal, height, width) while text tokens retain standard 1D RoPE positions. Outputs are free-form text responses for understanding tasks and bounding box coordinates for visual grounding (RefCOCO series).

## Method

The paper proposes two plug-and-play RoPE variants that require no architectural changes. MHRoPE (Multi-Head RoPE) partitions the positional encoding task across distinct attention heads, dedicating separate heads to the temporal, height, and width axes so each axis retains the full frequency spectrum within its assigned heads. MRoPE-Interleave (MRoPE-I) instead distributes feature channels to the three axes in a fine-grained round-robin fashion, ensuring every axis is encoded with the complete high-to-low frequency range and remaining compatible with extrapolation methods like YaRN and NTK-aware. Both methods incorporate a spatial-reset mechanism that resets the spatial (h, w) coordinates to zero at the start of each visual content block, decoupling temporal and spatial relative positions and aligning the visual attention sink with the LLM's bias for small position IDs. Both preserve strict compatibility with the base LLM's text-only RoPE, and are trained via supervised fine-tuning on ~2M SFT samples with the Qwen2.5 7B backbone and QwenViT, freezing the vision encoder while unfreezing the connector and LLM.

## Results

On the Qwen2.5-VL 7B backbone, MRoPE-I achieves the best overall scores across all three task categories: 63.79 on image understanding, 52.36 on video understanding, and 75.85 on visual grounding. MHRoPE follows closely with 62.92 (image), 52.58 (video), and 74.92 (grounding). Notable individual gains include MRoPE-I scoring 53.22 on MMMU (+2.66 over Vanilla RoPE), 83.72 on DocVQA (+23.59 over VideoRoPE), and 80.94 on RefCOCOval (+3.27 over Vanilla RoPE). Both proposed methods are validated on Qwen3-VL-4B and 8B, where MRoPE-I again leads with 48.82 (image) and 27.52 (grounding) on the 4B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Vanilla RoPE, both MHRoPE and MRoPE-I improve overall image scores by +0.75 and +1.62 respectively, with the largest gains on fine-grained spatial tasks (ChartQA +5.28, RefCOCOval +3.27 for MRoPE-I). Compared to MRoPE (the Qwen2-VL default), the proposed methods gain +1.0 to +1.9 on image and +1.1 to +0.9 on video, confirming that MRoPE's contiguous t-h-w channel partitioning limits multi-scale modeling. VideoRoPE and HoPE, which use diagonal layouts, suffer catastrophic degradation on document benchmarks (DocVQA drops to ~60, InfoVQA to ~35–37) due to positional overlap between visual and text tokens, while MHRoPE/MRoPE-I maintain 81–84 on DocVQA. CircleRoPE is competitive on grounding (74.96 vs. 74.92 for MHRoPE) but lags on video (51.09) due to collapsing all frames onto a single ring. On video benchmarks, HoPE retains a slight edge (52.95 vs. 52.58/52.36), but MHRoPE and MRoPE-I are the only methods that simultaneously lead or near-lead on all three categories (image, video, grounding).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MRoPE (Qwen2-VL), which first extended RoPE to multimodal settings by partitioning embedding channels into temporal, height, and width blocks, and on vanilla RoPE as the text-only baseline. It departs from the fragmented landscape of specialized follow-ups (VideoRoPE, HoPE, CircleRoPE, IL-RoPE, Omni-RoPE) by arguing that each addresses only one axis of the design space—position layout, frequency allocation, or text compatibility—while introducing regressions elsewhere. The key conceptual departure is the identification of three unified design guidelines (positional coherence, full frequency utilization, preservation of textual priors) and the demonstration that simple plug-and-play variants (MHRoPE, MRoPE-I) satisfying all three simultaneously outperform methods that optimize only one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic decomposition of multimodal RoPE design into orthogonal axes, providing practitioners with actionable guidelines rather than ad-hoc heuristics. The identification of concrete failure modes—modalities confusion from positional overlap causing repetitive generation, and asymmetric frequency decay impairing multi-scale spatial reasoning—gives the community diagnostic tools for debugging VLM positional encoding. Open questions include whether the findings generalize beyond the Qwen model family, how the methods scale to additional positional axes (e.g., 3D point clouds), and whether the spatial-reset mechanism interacts differently with alternative vision encoders or connector architectures.
