## Problem

Directly applying LoRA in differentially private federated learning (DPFL) causes severe performance degradation, particularly for large vision models, due to three underexplored challenges: gradient coupling from simultaneously updating the two asymmetric low-rank matrices, structural amplification of DP noise through the semi-quadratic update, and sharp global minima after federated aggregation. Prior work identified only the noise amplification issue, leaving gradient coupling and aggregation sharpness unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) or text sentence pairs (max sequence length 128 for GLUE tasks) and outputs class labels. Only the LoRA low-rank factors A ∈ R^{r×n} and B ∈ R^{m×r} (with r ≪ min{m,n}) are trained per client while the pre-trained backbone W₀ remains frozen; per-sample gradients are clipped to an ℓ₂ norm C before Gaussian noise injection, and the final model is W = W₀ + sBA.

## Method

LA-LoRA introduces a local alternating update strategy that decouples the two LoRA factors by updating B on odd local steps and A on even steps within each communication round, eliminating the same-step multiplicative noise term N_B·N_A and reducing gradient interference between the asymmetric matrices. An optional 5-tap binomial Gaussian low-pass filter G_s = (1/16)[1,4,6,4,1] is applied row-wise to A's gradients and column-wise to B's gradients before aggregation, suppressing high-frequency DP perturbations and biasing the global solution toward flatter minima. Theoretically, the paper proves that alternating updates yield unique closed-form projected gradients (requiring only r×r solves), achieve stable feature learning without the second-order cross term present in joint updates, and guarantee linear convergence under a rank-r RIP condition. The privacy guarantee follows standard RDP composition with subsampled Gaussian mechanism, and the deterministic filter does not weaken DP by post-processing invariance.

## Results

On image classification, LA-LoRA with Swin-B achieves 74.56% on CIFAR-100 and 60.68% on Tiny-ImageNet under the strictest privacy budget (ε=1), outperforming the best baseline RoLoRA by 6.68% and 16.83% respectively. With Swin-T at ε=3, LA-LoRA reaches 60.07% (CIFAR-100) and 60.97% (Tiny-ImageNet), exceeding RoLoRA by 4.88% and 10.10%. On language understanding (RoBERTa-Base), LA-LoRA leads on all four GLUE tasks; at ε=1 it achieves 88.73% on QNLI, 85.34% on QQP, 82.35% on MNLI, and 92.66% on SST-2, surpassing RoLoRA by 2.48%, 0.85%, 0.81%, and 0.34% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LA-LoRA consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset, model, and privacy budget tested. Against DP-LoRA (simultaneous noisy updates of A and B), the gap is largest: at ε=1 on Swin-B, LA-LoRA gains 18.58% on CIFAR-100 (74.56% vs 55.98%) and 30.48% on Tiny-ImageNet (60.68% vs 30.20%), confirming that gradient coupling and noise amplification severely hurt simultaneous updates. Against FFA-LoRA (frozen A, noisy B only), LA-LoRA still leads by 12.62% (CIFAR-100) and 21.35% (Tiny-ImageNet) at ε=1, showing that restricting updates to a single matrix is insufficient. Against RoLoRA (alternating A/B across communication rounds), LA-LoRA's local alternating strategy within each round yields 6.68% and 16.83% gains on Swin-B at ε=1, and 2.48% on QNLI (RoBERTa) at ε=1. The advantage is most pronounced on Tiny-ImageNet with Swin-B, where the larger model and harder task amplify the benefits of decoupled gradients and the low-pass filter.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) as the parameter-efficient fine-tuning backbone and on RoLoRA (Chen et al., 2025), which alternates optimization of the two LoRA factors across communication rounds. LA-LoRA departs from RoLoRA by moving the alternation *within* each local training round rather than across rounds, and from DP-LoRA/FFA-LoRA by identifying and addressing three specific failure modes—gradient coupling between asymmetric matrices, quadratic DP noise amplification, and sharpness in aggregated solutions—that prior DPFL-LoRA work had not systematically analyzed. The Gaussian low-pass smoothing filter draws on the Doppler line of work (Zhang et al., 2024b) but applies a simpler fixed binomial kernel tailored to the low-rank structure of LoRA updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a practically critical gap: fine-tuning large vision and language models under strict differential privacy in federated settings, where prior LoRA-based methods suffer severe accuracy degradation (e.g., DP-LoRA drops to ~30% on Tiny-ImageNet at ε=1). The combination of local alternating updates and a lightweight filter is simple enough to adopt with minimal engineering overhead, and the theoretical convergence and privacy guarantees add rigor. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger backbones (beyond Llama-2-7B), how it behaves under stronger data heterogeneity or system heterogeneity, and whether the low-pass filter can be replaced by learned or adaptive smoothing. The paper also notes future directions toward combining LA-LoRA with faster federated optimizers and second-order methods.
