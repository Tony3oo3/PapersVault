## Problem

Current protein generative models face three coupled limitations: they must simultaneously learn protein geometry and generation within a single objective, existing geometric pretraining relies on local non-rigid atomic representations that under-represent global folding geometry, and static structure databases largely overlook the conformational flexibility essential for realistic ensemble modeling. This paper asks whether a dedicated, rigidity-aware geometric pretraining stage—front-loaded before generative finetuning—can transfer global structural priors and dynamic information to improve downstream protein design and conformational ensemble generation.

## Input / Output

The pretraining pipeline consumes 432K static AFDB structures (60–512 residues) in Phase I and 1,390 all-atom MD trajectories (100 ns each, 2 ns frame separation) from the ATLAS dataset in Phase II. Each protein is represented as a sequence of L rigid body frames (a translation vector in R³ and a rotation matrix in SO(3) per residue), canonicalized to a center-of-mass / principal-axes inertial reference frame. The output is a pretrained Invariant Point Attention (IPA) encoder whose weights are warm-started into downstream generators (FrameDiff, FoldFlow-2, AlphaFlow) that then produce protein backbones, scaffold structures around fixed motifs, or conformational ensembles of GPCRs.

## Method

RigidSSL is a two-phase self-supervised pretraining framework built on residue-level SE(3) rigid body representations. Phase I (RigidSSL-Perturb) constructs paired views by applying independent Gaussian translational noise (σ=0.03) and isotropic Gaussian rotational noise on SO(3) (ε=0.5) to each canonicalized residue frame; Phase II (RigidSSL-MD) pairs time-separated MD snapshots (δ=2 ns) to capture physically realistic conformational transitions. The training objective is a bi-directional, rigidity-aware flow matching loss that learns a velocity field interpolating between the two views via LERP for translations in R³ and SLERP for quaternions on SO(3), jointly optimizing both components as a tractable surrogate for mutual information maximization. The IPA encoder (from AlphaFold2) serves as the backbone, with sinusoidal time embeddings injected after each block, and the final layer regresses the predicted velocity into translation and quaternion outputs. This rigid-body inductive bias reduces degrees of freedom compared to all-atom modeling while preserving the geometric constraints that make protein backbones physically plausible.

## Results

On unconditional protein backbone generation, RigidSSL-Perturb improves designability (fraction with scRMSD ≤ 2.0 Å) by 10% for FrameDiff (0.875 vs. 0.775 unpretrained) and by 42.9% for FoldFlow-2 (0.758 vs. 0.329 unpretrained), while also improving novelty (avg. max TM-score to PDB) and diversity. RigidSSL-MD further boosts structural diversity, achieving the best pairwise TM-score (0.471) and MaxCluster diversity (0.156) for FrameDiff, and the best MaxCluster diversity (0.318) for FoldFlow-2. In zero-shot motif scaffolding, RigidSSL-Perturb raises the average success rate from 9.35% to 15.19% (+5.8%). For GPCR conformational ensemble generation, RigidSSL variants achieve the best performance on 7 out of 9 biophysical metrics, including the lowest Joint PCA W₂-distance (17.53) and highest Jaccard similarity for weak contacts (0.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RigidSSL against three coordinate-aware geometric self-supervised baselines (GeoSSL-EBM-NCE, GeoSSL-InfoNCE, GeoSSL-RR) and a randomly initialized (no-pretraining) model, all using the same IPA backbone. RigidSSL-Perturb decisively wins on designability for both architectures: 0.875 vs. 0.775 (unpretrained FrameDiff) and 0.758 vs. 0.424 (best GeoSSL baseline for FoldFlow-2), representing a 42.9% relative improvement over unpretrained FoldFlow-2. RigidSSL-MD wins on diversity metrics, achieving the lowest pairwise TM-score (0.471) and highest MaxCluster diversity (0.156) for FrameDiff, and the highest MaxCluster (0.318) for FoldFlow-2, outperforming all GeoSSL variants. However, RigidSSL-MD trades some designability (0.700 for FrameDiff, 0.584 for FoldFlow-2) for greater conformational diversity, while RigidSSL-Perturb sacrifices diversity for geometric quality. On distribution-level metrics (FPSD, fJSD), RigidSSL-MD achieves the best FPSD for FrameDiff (776.3 vs. 908.7 unpretrained) and the best fJSD for both architectures, indicating generated structures more closely match natural protein distributions. In zero-shot motif scaffolding, RigidSSL-Perturb (15.19% success) outperforms the unpretrained model (9.35%) and all GeoSSL baselines, with a 21% advantage over GeoSSL-InfoNCE on the difficult 5TRV_long target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric self-supervised pretraining line (GeoSSL, GearNet, ProteinContrast) by replacing local, non-rigid atomic representations with residue-level rigid body frames in SE(3), and by substituting contrastive objectives with a bi-directional, rigidity-aware flow matching loss that jointly optimizes translational (LERP) and rotational (SLERP) dynamics. It further departs from prior protein pretraining that relies solely on static AFDB/PDB snapshots by introducing a two-phase strategy: Phase I applies isotropic Gaussian perturbations on SO(3) to 432K AFDB structures, while Phase II refines representations on 1.3K molecular dynamics trajectories from the ATLAS dataset. The encoder is the Invariant Point Attention module from AlphaFold2, and the downstream generative models (FrameDiff, FoldFlow-2) are finetuned from the pretrained weights, following the pretrain-then-finetune paradigm established in NLP and molecular pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that front-loading rigid-body geometric understanding before generative finetuning substantially improves protein designability (up to 43% for FoldFlow-2) and conformational ensemble realism for GPCRs, offering a practical recipe for practitioners building protein generative models. A key open question is the observed trade-off: RigidSSL-Perturb favors designability while RigidSSL-MD favors diversity and biophysical fidelity, and the paper does not fully resolve how to combine both phases optimally or mitigate the force-field bias that causes negative transfer in designability. Whether the rigid-body simplification (fixing bond lengths/angles) remains adequate for highly flexible or disordered proteins, and whether the approach scales to multi-domain or multi-chain assemblies, remain unexplored.
