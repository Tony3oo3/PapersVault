## Problem

Offline RL in large discrete combinatorial action spaces requires selecting coherent combinations of sub-actions from an exponentially large joint space. Existing methods either assume conditional independence across sub-actions (sacrificing representational capacity) or jointly learn action structure and control (slow and unstable). No prior approach efficiently decouples the discovery of the action manifold from downstream policy optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263031/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (state, action, reward, next-state) tuples from a behavior policy, where each state is a continuous vector and each action is a tuple of N discrete sub-actions (e.g., 38 dimensions with 3–30 bins each, yielding joint spaces up to ~10^56). The policy outputs N categorical distributions—one per sub-action dimension—whose product defines the joint action, preserving cross-dimensional dependencies through shared attention while remaining tractable. No online environment interaction is required at any stage.

## Method

SPIN is a two-stage framework that first pre-trains an Action Structure Model (ASM), a permutation-equivariant Transformer encoder, using a masked conditional modeling objective (analogous to BERT): sub-actions are randomly masked, replaced, or kept (80/10/10), and slot-specific heads predict the original values via cross-entropy. This self-supervised stage learns a state-conditioned representation in which structurally coherent joint actions concentrate on a low-dimensional manifold, without any reward signal. In the second stage, the ASM is frozen and only lightweight components (learnable action queries and output MLP heads) are trained with a standard offline RL objective (IQL, AWAC, or BCQ), reducing control to adapting a few parameters on top of the pre-learned action geometry. The policy factorizes as a product of contextualized per-dimension categorical distributions, retaining cross-sub-action dependencies through the frozen shared attention while avoiding intractable joint-space maximization.

## Results

On the discretized DeepMind Control Suite, SPIN achieves an overall average return of 594.1, surpassing the next-best baseline SAINT (572.1) by 3.8% and F-IQL (558.2) by 6.4%. The advantage is most pronounced on heterogeneous datasets: on random-medium-expert, SPIN reaches 499.2 average return versus 438.9 for SAINT (a 13.7% gain), and on the humanoid task specifically, SPIN achieves 330.2 versus 237.3 for SAINT (a 39% improvement). In terms of training efficiency, SPIN reaches 95% of F-IQL's asymptotic performance in 223.3 minutes total (including ASM pre-training), approximately 2.5× faster than F-IQL (558.1 min) and 3.8× faster than SAINT (845.0 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPIN outperforms all four baselines on both return and training efficiency. Against F-IQL (Beeson et al., 2024), the factored-policy baseline, SPIN achieves a 6.4% higher overall average return (594.1 vs. 558.2) while being 2.5× faster to reach target performance (223.3 vs. 558.1 min). Against the autoregressive policy AR-IQL (Zhang et al., 2018), SPIN gains 7.1% in overall return (594.1 vs. 554.5) and is 3.5× faster (223.3 vs. 784.6 min); AR-IQL also shows the worst efficiency on medium-expert and expert datasets. Against SAINT (Landers et al., 2025), the most direct joint-learning comparison using the same Transformer architecture, SPIN improves overall return by 3.8% (594.1 vs. 572.1) and is 3.8× faster (223.3 vs. 845.0 min), with the largest gap on random-medium-expert (499.2 vs. 438.9, a 13.7% improvement). Against REPREM (Cai et al., 2023), a trajectory-centric pre-training baseline, SPIN achieves 34% higher return (499.2 vs. 372.2) on random-medium-expert while training 200× faster (10.1 vs. 2351.6 min), demonstrating that action-structure pre-training is far more effective than trajectory prediction for combinatorial action spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPIN builds directly on the SAINT architecture (Landers et al., 2025), a Transformer-based policy that models sub-action dependencies via self-attention, but restructures the learning procedure: rather than jointly optimizing action structure and control under a single RL objective (as SAINT does), SPIN first pre-trains an Action Structure Model with a BERT-style masked modeling objective, then freezes that representation and trains only lightweight policy heads. This decoupling departs from both factored policies (Tang et al., 2022; Beeson et al., 2024), which discard cross-dimensional dependencies by assuming conditional independence, and autoregressive policies (Zhang et al., 2018), which impose a fixed ordering that breaks permutation equivariance. The self-supervised pre-training stage is action-centric (modeling the manifold of coherent joint actions) rather than trajectory-centric, distinguishing it from prior RL pre-training work such as RePreM (Cai et al., 2023) or contrastive state encoders (Laskin et al., 2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly learning and freezing the action manifold before policy optimization is both more effective and substantially faster than joint structure-and-control learning, with the largest gains on heterogeneous datasets that are most representative of real-world offline settings. The SPIN-Distill ablation—that a simple MLP policy distilled from the frozen ASM nearly matches the full Transformer policy—suggests the pre-trained representation carries most of the structural information, reducing architectural demands on the downstream policy. Open questions include extending SPIN to value-regularization methods like CQL (which require intractable global operations over the joint action space), adapting to non-permutation-equivariant action structures, and improving robustness under sparse or biased offline data. The technique is broadly applicable to any domain with compositional discrete actions (healthcare, robotics, recommender systems), though the current evaluation is confined to a discretized DM Control suite and a Maze task.
