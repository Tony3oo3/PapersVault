## Problem

Existing subspace-based interpretability methods (e.g., DAS) require a human-specified causal model to identify which subspaces correspond to which variables, limiting their generality. This paper asks whether the representation space of a language model contains a 'natural' partition into orthogonal, interpretable subspaces that can be discovered purely from the data distribution, without any supervision or prior hypothesis about the model's internal mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261739/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only raw residual stream activations (h) collected by running the model on a corpus (e.g., MiniPile), with no access to ground-truth feature vectors, projection matrices, or labels. It produces an orthogonal matrix R ∈ R^{d×d} and a dimension configuration c = [d₁, …, d_S] (where Σd_s = d), together defining a partition of the d-dimensional activation space into S orthogonal subspaces. The configuration is determined adaptively during training: it starts with many small equal-sized subspaces and progressively merges pairs whose mutual information exceeds a threshold, so the final number and sizes of subspaces are data-driven rather than fixed a priori.

## Method

NDM trains an orthogonal matrix R (parameterized to maintain RᵀR = I) by minimizing the average distance from each activation to its nearest neighbor within each subspace of the transformed space R·h. The key insight is that if features are mutually exclusive within groups but independent across groups, the correct partition concentrates projected points along a few directions inside each subspace, shrinking nearest-neighbor distances, while incorrect partitions that mix groups spread points out and increase those distances. Equivalently, since total entropy is invariant under orthogonal transformation, minimizing within-subspace neighbor distances reduces the total correlation (multi-information) among subspaces, making them as statistically independent as possible. The dimension configuration is refined iteratively: after an initial training phase with small subspaces, pairwise mutual information is estimated via the KSG estimator, and pairs with normalized MI above a threshold are merged, re-initializing the optimizer, until no pair exceeds the threshold. This contrasts with supervised approaches like DAS that optimize R to align with human-specified target variables; NDM requires no such targets and instead exploits the intrinsic geometric structure of the activation distribution.

## Results

On the GPT-2 Small test suite (5 causal intervention tests based on known IOI and Greater-than circuits), NDM achieves an average Gini coefficient of 0.71, well above the 0.6 threshold for satisfactory concentration of target information in a single subspace. The strongest baseline, Feature (adapted from Engels et al., 2024), reaches only 0.56, while PCA-based baselines score 0.32–0.38 and Identity/Random baselines score 0.21. On larger models (Qwen2.5-1.5B and Gemma-2-2B), NDM identifies distinct subspaces that selectively mediate parametric knowledge routing vs. context knowledge routing in knowledge-conflict prompts, with baselines showing correlated effects under both counterfactual conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NDM outperforms all five unsupervised baselines on the GPT-2 Small test suite, with an average Gini coefficient of 0.71 versus 0.56 for the strongest baseline (Feature, adapted from Engels et al., 2024's multi-dimensional superposition clustering). The gap is most pronounced on Tests 1 and 2 (previous-token and subject-position information), where NDM scores 0.90 and 0.83 versus 0.60 and 0.67 for Feature. NDM's weakest result is Test 3 (subject position at layer 8, Var_s-normalized: 0.38), where the effect is more diffuse. PCA-based baselines (0.32–0.38) and Identity/Random (0.21) perform substantially worse, confirming that the orthogonal structure discovered by NDM is not captured by simple variance-based or random partitions. On the 2B-scale knowledge-conflict experiments, NDM finds subspaces with strong, selective patching effects (e.g., subspace 2 in Qwen2.5-1.5B mediates only parametric routing), while baselines show correlated effects under both counterfactual input types, failing to disentangle the two mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the supervised subspace-discovery framework of Geiger et al. (2024, DAS), which learns an orthogonal matrix to align a human-specified causal model with neural representations, by replacing the supervision signal with an unsupervised geometric objective: minimizing nearest-neighbor distances within subspaces. Conceptually, it builds on the superposition literature (Elhage et al., 2022) by arguing that mutual exclusiveness within feature groups—rather than sparsity alone—is the fundamental condition enabling orthogonal subspace structure, and connects to the Multi-Dimensional Superposition Hypothesis (Engels et al., 2024) by treating each feature group as a multi-dimensional irreducible feature. Unlike SAE-based approaches that yield input-dependent features, NDM produces input-independent subspaces that the authors position as potential 'variables' for building circuits at a higher level of abstraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If subspaces recovered by NDM can serve as stable, input-independent 'variables,' they open a path to constructing circuits that describe model computation at an algorithmic level rather than at the level of individual features or attention heads—a long-standing goal of mechanistic interpretability. Key limitations temper the impact: the main quantitative evidence rests on GPT-2 Small (124M), the 2B-model evaluation lacks ground-truth circuits, the partition granularity is coarse (minimum 32-dim subspaces), and interpretation relies on the Linear Representation Hypothesis. Open questions include whether the method scales to much larger models, whether finer-grained partitions reveal additional structure, and whether subspace-based circuits can be systematically constructed and validated across tasks.
