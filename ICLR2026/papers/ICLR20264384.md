## Problem

In multi-stage recommender systems, most joint-optimization work focuses on learning reranker-aware retrievers, while the complementary direction—building a retriever-aware reranker—remains underexplored. The paper shows that retriever scores from the first stage are informative but noisy signals, and that naively incorporating them as input features fails to fully exploit this information for aligning with user feedback. The reranking task is reframed as a noise reduction problem on retriever scores, with a theoretical analysis proving that direct optimization of the reranker on observed scores is misaligned with the true data-likelihood objective.

## Input / Output

The reranker receives a candidate item set of n items (top-50 from the retriever) along with their continuous retriever scores in [0,1]^n, the user's profile features and interaction history, and binary user feedback labels z_u ∈ {0,1}^n. During training, a noise generator additionally produces synthetic retriever scores x'_u from the ground-truth feedback via a reparameterization step, which are fed to the reranker as augmented inputs. The output is a reranked top-K list (K=6) with predicted user-feedback probabilities, and the framework is backbone-agnostic, plugging into any existing reranker architecture (PRM, PIER, etc.) without modifying its inference-time structure.

## Method

DNR formulates reranking as denoising retriever scores toward user feedback and introduces an adversarial training framework with three jointly optimized objectives derived from a variational decomposition of the data log-likelihood: (1) an augmented denoising loss L_z that trains the reranker to predict user feedback from both real and synthetic noisy retriever scores, (2) an adversarial loss L_adv that encourages the noise generator to synthesize hard-to-denoise score perturbations, and (3) a KL-based distribution regularization L_x that keeps the synthetic score distribution aligned with the real retriever score prior. The noise generator is either a heuristic (Beta or Gaussian) or a learned 2-layer MLP that outputs synthetic scores via x'_u = (1−λ_c)·z_u + λ_c·ε; training proceeds in two phases—first λ_e epochs use the heuristic generator for stability, then the model-based generator is engaged for adversarial exploration. The framework is model-agnostic and adds negligible inference cost since the noise generator is only used during training.

## Results

DNR consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across three datasets (ML-1M, Kuaivideo, Amazon-Books) on all five metrics (H@6, NDCG@6, MAP@6, F1@6, AUC). On ML-1M, DNR-G achieves NDCG@6 of 77.67 and AUC of 92.75, surpassing the strongest baseline MG-E (76.18 / 89.08) by 1.49 and 3.67 points respectively. On Kuaivideo, DNR-B reaches NDCG@6 of 70.15 and AUC of 93.38, exceeding the best baseline MG-E (66.19 / 90.16) by 3.96 and 3.22 points. On Amazon-Books, DNR-B attains NDCG@6 of 83.53 and AUC of 94.23, outperforming the top baseline Pier (80.22 / 92.26) by 3.31 and 1.97 points. All improvements are statistically significant (p<0.05, student t-test).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNR outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest generator-evaluator baselines, DNR-G surpasses MG-E by 1.49 NDCG@6 points on ML-1M (77.67 vs. 76.18), 3.41 on Kuaivideo (69.60 vs. 66.19), and 2.76 on Book (82.57 vs. 79.81); DNR-B similarly beats Pier by 1.13 (ML-1M), 5.04 (Kuaivideo), and 3.31 (Book) NDCG@6 points. The list-refinement methods (PRM, DLCM, SetRank, MIR) are consistently outperformed by 2–7 NDCG@6 points, with PRM (the primary backbone) showing the largest gap on Kuaivideo (70.15 vs. 55.93 for DNR-B). Traditional recommenders (SASRec, Caser, GRU4Rec, MiDNN) trail DNR by 5–15 NDCG@6 points, confirming that list-level modeling is essential. Diffusion-based methods (DiffuRec, DCDR) underperform DNR by 1.8–14.8 NDCG@6 points, with DCDR particularly weak on Book (70.36 vs. 83.53). Notably, DNR's advantage is largest on Kuaivideo (the industrial dataset), suggesting the denoising formulation is most beneficial when retriever scores carry substantial noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper takes the complementary direction to the dominant 'reranker-aware retriever' line of work (Gallagher et al., Qin et al., Xu et al.) by instead making the reranker aware of the retriever's score distribution. It repurposes the variational inference / ELBO decomposition from VAEs (Kingma & Welling, 2014) to derive a three-objective training framework, and borrows the adversarial data augmentation paradigm from IRGAN and AdvIR but applies it specifically to synthesizing noisy retriever scores rather than user-item interactions. The denoising formulation departs from all prior reranking methods (PRM, PIER, NAR4Rec, diffusion-based rerankers) that either ignore retriever scores or incorporate them naively as input features (concatenation, addition, weighting).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both a theoretical explanation for why naive retriever-score incorporation is suboptimal (the L1 and L2 gap terms in Eq. 3) and a practical, backbone-agnostic plug-in that consistently improves any reranker by 3–5 points on standard metrics, validated by an industrial A/B test showing +1.089% realshow. Key open questions include extending to multi-task feedback signals (where different behaviors follow heterogeneous distributions), jointly optimizing retriever and reranker end-to-end, and whether the denoising advantage persists as retrievers approach their theoretical error bound. The training cost increase from the additional noise generator and three loss terms is a practical consideration, though inference cost is unchanged.
