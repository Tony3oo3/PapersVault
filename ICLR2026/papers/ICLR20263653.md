## Problem

Existing DTI prediction methods rely on SMILES-protein pairs and pairwise contrastive alignment, which cannot capture higher-order interdependencies among multiple molecular and protein modalities. Additionally, static fusion assumes all modalities are equally informative, and publicly available IC50 activity measurements remain unexploited during pre-training despite their direct biological relevance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263653/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pre-training framework consumes four modalities per sample: a SMILES string, a natural-language text description, a hierarchical taxonomic annotation (HTA) vector, and a protein sequence, each encoded by frozen backbones (MolFormer-XL, MolT5, ESM2) into 768- or 1280-dimensional embeddings that are projected into a shared 512-dimensional space. Downstream, the model outputs a binary interaction score (DTI), a mechanism-of-action label (activation vs. inhibition), or a ranked retrieval list of candidate proteins/drugs, with IC50 values discretized into three activity classes (effective <10 µM, moderate 10–1000 µM, ineffective >1000 µM) for auxiliary supervision.

## Method

GRAM-DTI extends Gramian volume-based contrastive learning to four modalities: the 4×4 Gram matrix of normalized embeddings is constructed, and its determinant's square root defines a 4-dimensional volume that is minimized for positive pairs and maximized for negatives, capturing higher-order semantic alignment beyond pairwise schemes. A gradient-informed adaptive modality dropout mechanism tracks exponentially-decayed gradient norms per modality over K steps and probabilistically drops either the most dominant modality (if its gradient exceeds mean + 1.5σ) or the least-contributing one, preventing overfitting to a single signal. IC50 measurements, when available, provide weak auxiliary supervision through a three-class weighted cross-entropy head on the concatenated four-modality embedding. The total loss combines the symmetric volume loss, a CLIP-style bimodal SMILES–protein contrastive loss, and the IC50 classification loss with equal weights, trained end-to-end on lightweight projection networks while all encoder backbones remain frozen.

## Results

On the Yamanishi_08 DTI benchmark, GRAM-DTI achieves AUROC 0.977 and AUPR 0.904 under warm-start, and AUROC 0.955 / AUPR 0.849 under target cold-start, outperforming the strongest baseline DTIAM (0.967 / 0.901 and 0.941 / 0.844 respectively). On the larger Hetionet dataset, GRAM-DTI reaches AUROC 0.981 (warm start) and 0.921 (target cold start), surpassing DTIAM by 2.4 and 0.4 points. For MoA prediction, GRAM-DTI leads on the Activation dataset across all splits (e.g., AUPR 0.450 vs 0.391 for DTIAM in target cold start) but trails DTIAM on Inhibition warm-start AUPR (0.785 vs 0.845). Overall, GRAM-DTI wins 10 of 12 DTI settings and 8 of 12 MoA settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DTIAM (the strongest overall baseline), GRAM-DTI wins on 10 of 12 DTI settings and 5 of 6 MoA settings, with the largest margins on Hetionet AUROC drug cold start (+10.3 pts: 0.855 vs 0.752) and Activation AUPR target cold start (+5.9 pts: 0.450 vs 0.391). GRAM-DTI loses to DTIAM on Hetionet AUPR warm start (0.859 vs 0.879) and Inhibition AUPR warm start (0.785 vs 0.845), where the larger labeled dataset appears to favor DTIAM's separate pre-training strategy. Against KGE-NFM, GRAM-DTI dominates on Yamanishi_08 across all splits but is slightly behind on Hetionet AUPR target cold start (0.626 vs 0.651). Against AI-DTI on MoA tasks, GRAM-DTI clearly wins on Activation (all three splits) and Inhibition target cold start, but trails on Inhibition warm-start and drug cold-start AUPR. The older sequence/graph baselines (CPL-GNN, MPNN-CNN, TransformerCPI) are consistently outperformed by 5–30+ points across all settings, confirming the advantage of multimodal pre-training over unimodal architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRAM-DTI extends the Gramian volume-based multimodal alignment introduced by Cicchetti et al. (2024) for audio-video-text and adapted to molecular data by TRIDENT (Jiang et al., 2025) from three modalities (SMILES, text, HTA) to four by adding protein sequences. It departs from prior multimodal DTI approaches (MDTips, MGNDTI, DTIAM) that rely on pairwise contrastive learning by formulating alignment as a higher-order volume minimization problem, and introduces gradient-informed adaptive modality dropout—a mechanism not previously explored in the DTI context. The framework also incorporates IC50 activity measurements as weak auxiliary supervision during pretraining, a signal that prior DTI pretraining methods have left unutilized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent gains in cold-start scenarios (particularly target cold start) suggest that multimodal volume-based pretraining learns more transferable representations than pairwise alignment, which is practically valuable for drug discovery where novel targets are the norm. However, the pretraining corpus is limited to ~51K quadruplets due to the requirement of complete four-modality samples, and protein representations are restricted to sequence (no structural information), both of which constrain generalization. Open questions include whether scaling the pretraining corpus, incorporating protein 3D structure, and systematically handling partial-modality data can unlock further gains, and whether the volume-based alignment advantage persists at much larger scale.
