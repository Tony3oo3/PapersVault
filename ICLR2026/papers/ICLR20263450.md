## Problem

Multi-step denoising in diffusion and Flow Matching models creates a major efficiency bottleneck, motivating one-step generation. Existing few-step methods face two key limitations: consistency models trained from scratch cannot leverage Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) during training, and recent hybrid approaches like MeanFlow require Jacobian-vector product (JVP) computations that are poorly optimized in frameworks like PyTorch, causing higher memory usage and slower training.

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian noise sample x_1 ~ N(0, I_n) and an optional class label c, and produces a clean image in a single forward pass (1-NFE). For ImageNet 256×256, generation occurs in the 32×32×4 latent space of a pre-trained VAE; for CIFAR-10, it operates directly in 32×32 pixel space. Unusually, the network receives a bi-time input (x_t, t, s) where the difference s−t is encoded via positional embeddings, allowing the same model to map any intermediate state to any target time in one evaluation.

## Method

SoFlow directly learns the solution function f(x_t, t, s) of the Flow Matching velocity ODE, parameterized as f_θ(x_t, t, s) = a(t,s)·x_t + b(t,s)·F_θ(x_t, t, s) to satisfy the boundary condition f_θ(x_t, t, t) = x_t. Training combines two losses: a Flow Matching loss that provides velocity estimates enabling CFG during training, and a solution consistency loss that enforces the PDE condition ∂₁f_θ·v + ∂₂f_θ = 0 via a Taylor-expansion approximation with stop-gradient targets at a nearby time point l. The consistency loss avoids JVP calculations entirely (unlike MeanFlow), while the FM loss naturally supports CFG (unlike standard consistency models). The model uses a DiT backbone with an additional time variable s, and inference simply evaluates f_θ(x_1, 1, 0) once to produce the final sample.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, SoFlow-XL/2 achieves a FID-50K of 2.96 at 1-NFE and 2.66 at 2-NFE, consistently outperforming MeanFlow at every model size (e.g., 4.85 vs. 6.17 for B/2, 3.73 vs. 5.01 for M/2, 3.20 vs. 3.84 for L/2, 2.96 vs. 3.43 for XL/2 at 1-NFE). On CIFAR-10 unconditional generation, SoFlow achieves a FID-50K of 2.86 at 1-NFE, surpassing MeanFlow (2.92), sCT (2.97), IMM (3.20), and ECT (3.60), though slightly behind iCT (2.83). Additionally, SoFlow reduces peak GPU memory by ~31% and increases training speed by 23% over MeanFlow by eliminating JVP computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoFlow's primary comparison is against MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2025), the closest concurrent method for one-step Flow Matching generation. SoFlow wins at every model size on ImageNet 256×256: by 1.32 FID at B/2 (4.85 vs. 6.17), 1.28 at M/2 (3.73 vs. 5.01), 0.64 at L/2 (3.20 vs. 3.84), and 0.47 at XL/2 (2.96 vs. 3.43) at 1-NFE, with similar margins at 2-NFE (2.66 vs. 2.93 for XL/2). On CIFAR-10, SoFlow (2.86) beats MeanFlow (2.92), sCT (2.97), IMM (3.20), and ECT (3.60) but is marginally behind iCT (2.83). Against older few-step methods, SoFlow-XL/2 at 1-NFE (2.96) substantially outperforms Shortcut-XL/2 (10.60) and iCT-XL/2 (34.24), and is competitive with IMM-XL/2 at 2×2 NFE (3.99). SoFlow also avoids the JVP computation required by MeanFlow, yielding ~31% lower GPU memory and 23% faster training, while enabling CFG during training so that 1-NFE inference requires no additional guidance overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

SoFlow sits at the intersection of Flow Matching (which defines the velocity ODE to be solved) and the consistency-training family (which learns direct mappings along ODE trajectories for few-step generation). Its closest predecessor is MeanFlow, which also combines a Flow Matching loss with a consistency-type objective but relies on Jacobian-vector product (JVP) computations to enforce the PDE constraint. SoFlow's key departure is reframing the problem as learning the full solution function f(x_t, t, s) via a bi-time parameterization, which yields a solution consistency loss that enforces the same PDE condition without any JVP, while naturally accommodating Classifier-Free Guidance during training—a capability that from-scratch consistency models have historically lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of JVP has concrete practical consequences: 31% lower peak GPU memory and 23% faster training compared to MeanFlow under identical codebases, because PyTorch's memory-efficient attention does not support JVP. The ability to apply CFG during training (rather than only at inference) means one-step generation can be guided without doubling NFE, a limitation that has constrained consistency-model-based generators. Open questions include whether the bi-time formulation and its theoretical guarantees (global error bounded by local PDE residual) hold at video-generation scale, whether the approach extends naturally to other modalities, and whether the velocity-mixing heuristic for variance reduction generalizes beyond class-conditional ImageNet.
