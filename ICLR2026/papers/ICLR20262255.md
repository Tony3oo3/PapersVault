## Problem

Diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) suffer from O(L³) computational complexity that makes long-sequence and real-time generation impractical, primarily because their non-autoregressive denoising steps are incompatible with KV-caching. Existing acceleration methods (dLLM-Cache, dKV-Cache, Fast-dLLM) apply static, uniform caching or fixed-threshold parallel decoding across all layers and steps, ignoring the empirically observed layer-wise and step-wise variation in token dynamics, which leads to suboptimal speedups or accuracy degradation.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free, plug-and-play acceleration layer on top of existing dLLM architectures (LLaDA-8B-Instruct, LLaDA-1.5, Dream-v0-7B-Instruct). It takes a text prompt of length L_prompt and a target generation length L_gen, initializing the sequence with [MASK] tokens in the generation region, and iteratively denoises over T steps (256 for most benchmarks, 512 for HumanEval) to produce the final generated text sequence. The acceleration is applied at inference time without modifying model weights, with hyperparameters B_layer=32, B_window=32, α=0.001, and β=0.0008 as defaults.

## Method

Dynamic-dLLM introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Dynamic Cache Updating (DCU) replaces uniform per-layer cache updates with a layer-adaptive budget: it computes token-level cosine distance between layer inputs at consecutive steps, aggregates into a layer-wise dynamism score, and allocates the total cache-update budget proportionally so that deeper, more dynamic layers receive more updates while stable layers reuse cached Key/Value/Attention/FFN outputs. A Mandatory Update Window of fixed size B_window centered on the previously unmasked key token prevents tokens from becoming permanently stale ('stuck in the mud'). Second, Adaptive Parallel Decoding (APD) replaces the fixed confidence threshold with a per-token threshold that is lowered when the predicted distribution is concentrated (large gap between top-1 and top-2 probabilities) and raised when the confidence distribution has shifted significantly between adjacent steps (high temporal instability), enabling early commitment to stable predictions while deferring uncertain ones.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, Dynamic-dLLM with parallel decoding (Dynamic-dLLM*) achieves an average throughput of 3.21× speedup over the baseline, with peak acceleration of 4.48× on GSM8K (37.29 TPS vs. 8.32 TPS baseline) while maintaining near-identical accuracy (e.g., MMLU 60.89% vs. 60.92% baseline). Across all three models (LLaDA-8B-Instruct, LLaDA-1.5, Dream-v0-7B-Instruct), the method consistently delivers 2.95×–3.21× average speedup with parallel decoding, and 2.45×–2.63× with feature caching alone. The additional latency introduced by DCU and APD is minimal and scales linearly with generation length, far less than the inference time saved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dynamic-dLLM outperforms all three baselines across every model and benchmark. Against dLLM-Cache (Liu et al., 2025b), which uses uniform per-layer feature caching, Dynamic-dLLM* achieves 3.21× average speedup vs. 2.27× on LLaDA-8B-Instruct, with the gap widening on reasoning-heavy tasks (GSM8K: 4.48× vs. 3.0×). Against dKV-Cache (Ma et al., 2025), which caches decoded tokens without updating them, Dynamic-dLLM* more than doubles the speedup (3.21× vs. 1.59× average). Against Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025), which combines KV-cache with fixed-threshold parallel decoding, Dynamic-dLLM* achieves 3.21× vs. 2.85× average speedup, with the advantage most pronounced on GSM8K (4.48× vs. 3.77×) and GPQA (4.19× vs. 3.56×) where adaptive thresholds avoid premature commitment to incorrect predictions. Accuracy is preserved within ~0.5% of baseline across all comparisons, confirming that the speedup comes from eliminating redundant computation rather than sacrificing quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dLLM acceleration line initiated by feature-caching methods (dLLM-Cache, dKV-Cache) and parallel decoding (Fast-dLLM), which all apply uniform, static strategies across layers and decoding steps. The key conceptual departure is the observation that token dynamics are heterogeneous across layers (cache-update needs increase monotonically from shallow to deep layers) and across steps (confidence distributions shift), motivating layer-adaptive budget allocation (DCU) and per-token adaptive thresholds (APD). The DCU design also borrows the input-output correlation insight from diffusion transformer caching (Liu et al., 2025a), repurposing layer-input cosine distance as a cheap proxy for intermediate-feature change to avoid recomputing Value vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As dLLMs gain traction as alternatives to autoregressive models, their O(L³) inference cost is a critical deployment bottleneck; a training-free, plug-and-play solution achieving 3×+ average speedup with negligible accuracy loss is practically significant for real-time and long-sequence applications. The dynamic-allocation principle (allocate compute where the model actually needs it rather than uniformly) could inform future caching and scheduling designs beyond dLLMs. However, the paper is limited to unimodal text generation, and its generalization to multimodal or structured-input settings remains unexplored, leaving open how layer-adaptive and step-adaptive strategies interact with modality-specific computational demands.
