## Problem

Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLMs) are constrained by their masking paradigm: they waste roughly half of all FLOPs on non-informative <MASK> tokens during training and inference, require fixed-length padding that adds further <PAD> overhead for variable-length data, and lack a self-correction mechanism because once a token is unmasked its content and position are permanently fixed. These limitations make MDLMs both computationally inefficient and inflexible for variable-length generation.

## Input / Output

In the fixed-length setting, the model consumes 1024-token chunks (GPT2 tokenizer) and produces fixed-length 1024-token outputs. In the variable-length setting, it natively ingests sequences of varying length (Stories dataset, average 213 tokens, max 1024, BERT-base-uncased tokenizer) without any padding, and generates outputs whose length is determined by the learned insertion process rather than a predefined canvas. An undeletable <BOS> token is prepended to every sequence and serves as the initial state for generation, representing the empty sequence.

## Method

DID formulates a continuous-time Markov chain over the variable-length sequence space where the forward process independently deletes tokens at rate σ(t) until only <BOS> remains, and the backward process iteratively inserts tokens. Training uses a Denoising Insertion Score Entropy (DISE) objective derived from the DSE loss via Jensen's inequality, which requires computing ratios of subsequence counts N(Ins(x_t,i,v), x_0)/N(x_t, x_0) for all (position, token) pairs; these are solved in O(mn) time via a parallelized prefix-suffix dynamic programming algorithm rather than the naive O(mn²V). For the fixed-length setting, the insertion score becomes time-independent and the objective simplifies to a weighted cross-entropy (DICE) with an explicit sequence-level normalization constraint. Sampling proceeds by drawing insertion actions from the learned score with τ-leaping for parallel decoding, and the model inherently supports self-correction by inserting new tokens between already-generated ones.

## Results

On fixed-length language modeling (OpenWebText, GPT2 tokenizer, 1024-token chunks), DID-F (FLOPs-aligned, 800K steps) achieves lower perplexity than RADD on 6 of 7 datasets for both small and medium sizes (e.g., WikiText 36.91 vs 38.27 small; Arxiv 78.38 vs 85.95 small). Training speed improves by 1.89×–1.99× (fixed-length) and 2.58×–3.42× (variable-length) over RADD, while inference speed improves by ~1.5× (fixed-length) and up to 3.79× (variable-length). On the variable-length Stories benchmark, DID achieves a generative PPL of 21–24 (evaluated by GPT2 Large) across 64–512 denoising steps, substantially outperforming RADD (27–82) and ILM (31–162), while also matching the training data's length distribution far more closely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RADD (the primary MDLM baseline), DID-F (FLOPs-aligned) consistently outperforms on 6 of 7 fixed-length perplexity benchmarks for both small and medium model sizes, with the largest gains on Arxiv (78.38 vs 85.95 small; 61.77 vs 66.28 medium) and Pubmed (52.89 vs 56.99 small). DID also achieves up to 1.99× training speedup and 1.58× inference speedup on fixed-length data, and up to 3.42× training and 3.79× inference speedup on variable-length data, by eliminating <MASK> and <PAD> token computations. On the variable-length Stories benchmark, DID achieves a generative PPL of 21–24 across all step counts, compared to RADD's 27–82 and ILM's 31–162, while also producing generation lengths (avg ~182–205) that closely match the training data distribution (avg 213), unlike RADD (avg 111–353) or ILM (avg 63–234). Against ILM (the insertion-based LM baseline), DID is substantially better in generation quality and length modeling but slower in inference (0.09–0.39s vs 0.016–0.27s), which the authors attribute to ILM's oversimplified one-token-per-step mechanism. DID-S (steps-aligned, same 400K steps as RADD) achieves comparable perplexity to RADD despite using roughly half the FLOPs, confirming that the efficiency gain from removing <MASK> tokens is real and not an artifact of extra training.

## Relation to Prior Work

DID builds on the continuous-time discrete diffusion framework of Campbell et al. (2022) and the Denoising Score Entropy (DSE) training objective of Lou et al. (2024), but fundamentally redefines the forward/backward processes: instead of the masking-unmasking paradigm used by MDLMs (e.g., RADD), DID uses token deletion and insertion as the corruption and reconstruction operations. This positions DID between classical insertion-based LMs (Insertion Transformer, Levenshtein Transformer, InsNet), which lack probabilistic grounding, and recent insertion-based approaches like ILMs (Patel et al., 2025) and Edit Flows (Havasi et al., 2025), which either forgo likelihood-bounded training or introduce auxiliary edit-path variables. DID is the first to rigorously formulate deletion-insertion as a CTMC with a tractable, likelihood-bounded training objective (DISE/DICE) and an exact parallel DP algorithm for the required subsequence count ratios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating <MASK> and <PAD> tokens, DID roughly halves training FLOPs and provides up to 3.8× inference speedup in variable-length settings, while natively supporting variable-length generation and offering an intrinsic self-correction mechanism absent in masking-based models. However, all experiments are at small-to-medium scale (GPT-2-sized models on OWT and TinyStories), leaving open whether the efficiency and quality advantages persist at the scale of modern LLMs. Future work includes integrating advanced inference algorithms (KV caching, remasking), hybrid AR+diffusion architectures, and scaling to larger datasets and model sizes to validate the paradigm's practical impact.
