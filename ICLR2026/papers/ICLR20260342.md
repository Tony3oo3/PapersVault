## Problem

Existing methods for identifying causal structure in the presence of latent variables are either constraint-based (suffering from multiple-testing and error propagation, especially with small samples) or score-based but limited to recovering only relations among observed variables. No score-based greedy search method existed that could efficiently recover the entire underlying structure involving latent variables with an asymptotic correctness guarantee.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260342/cropped_page3_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the empirical covariance matrix of observed variables (sufficient because variables are jointly Gaussian with zero mean) and a tolerance parameter δ that controls sparsity in finite samples (set as 0.25·log(N)/N). It outputs a CPDAG over both observed and latent variables representing the Markov equivalence class of the ground truth DAG, up to permutation of latent variable names. The number of latent variables is inferred automatically as ⌊|X|/2⌋ in the initial state and refined during Phase 1.

## Method

The paper introduces the Generalized N Factor Model (GNFM), a mild graphical assumption requiring latent variables to be partitionable into groups where each group has at least 2|L_p| observed pure children and relations between groups apply uniformly to all members. Under GNFM, the authors prove that algebraic equivalence (same equality constraints on the covariance) implies Markov equivalence, establishing global consistency of the likelihood score for structure identification. The LGES algorithm performs a two-phase greedy deletion search: Phase 1 uses operator O_LX to iteratively delete latent-to-observed edges (grouping observed variables by rank constraints to identify latent groups), and Phase 2 uses operator O_LL to delete latent-to-latent edges while orienting edges to neighboring groups. The key design principle is starting from a guaranteed supergraph of the ground truth and only accepting deletions that preserve the maximum likelihood score, ensuring the dimension decreases monotonically and the final state is the minimal-dimension graph that still generates the observation—yielding the correct MEC in the large-sample limit.

## Results

On synthetic GNFM graphs (20 variables, 5 latent), LGES achieves the best F1 score for skeleton and lowest SHD for MEC across all sample sizes. At 1000 samples, LGES reaches F1 = 0.82 and SHD = 8.80, outperforming the runner-up RLCD (F1 = 0.76, SHD = 11.24) and constraint-based GIN (F1 = 0.47, SHD = 20.88). The advantage is most pronounced at small sample sizes: with only 100 samples, LGES achieves F1 = 0.60 while GIN and RLCD drop to 0.27 and 0.33 respectively. On real-world datasets (Big Five, Teacher Burnout, Multi-tasking), LGES-recovered structures achieve the best goodness-of-fit scores (RMSEA, CFI, TLI) compared to hypothesized structures from existing psychological literature.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGES consistently outperforms all three baselines on both F1 (skeleton) and SHD (MEC) across every sample size. The closest competitor is RLCD (a rank-test-based constraint method), which trails LGES by 0.06 F1 and 2.44 SHD at 1000 samples, and by a much larger margin (0.27 F1, 14.97 SHD) at 100 samples. GIN (CI-test-based) is the weakest baseline, achieving only 0.47 F1 at 1000 samples versus LGES's 0.82, reflecting the well-known error propagation problem of constraint-based methods under limited sample size. FOFC (a 1-factor clustering method) sits in the middle, reaching 0.61 F1 at 1000 samples. Against the exact search method of Ng et al. (2024), LGES achieves nearly identical accuracy (F1 = 0.88 vs. 0.89 at 7 observed variables) but runs ~400× faster (14 seconds vs. 1.5 hours), and the gap widens dramatically at 8–9 variables where exact search exceeds 100–1000 hours while LGES completes in under 20 seconds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Greedy Equivalence Search (GES) paradigm from Chickering (2002) to the partially observed setting, building directly on the score-based exact search framework of Ng et al. (2024) and the covariance parameterization of Dong et al. (2024b). It departs from the dominant constraint-based lineage (FCI, FOFC, GIN, RLCD) by replacing iterative statistical tests with a single likelihood-score comparison, thereby avoiding multiple-testing and error propagation. The key conceptual departure from Ng et al. (2024) is disentangling the maximum likelihood score from the model dimension in the identifiability theory, which enables a greedy search that never needs to explicitly compute the dimension of candidate graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LGES is the first score-based greedy search with asymptotic identifiability guarantees for the full latent-variable structure, and it reduces runtime from super-exponential (exact search: >100 hours at 8 observed variables) to seconds while retaining near-optimal F1/SHD. This makes structure identification of partially observed causal models practically accessible for the first time, particularly in small-sample regimes where constraint-based methods degrade sharply. Key limitations include the GNFM graphical assumption (requiring at least 2|L_p| observed children per latent group), the linear-Gaussian model class, and the inability to identify latent variable names/order. Open questions include extending the framework to non-linear or non-Gaussian settings, relaxing GNFM to weaker graphical conditions, and handling cases where the observed-to-latent ratio is insufficient.
