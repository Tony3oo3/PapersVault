## Problem

Existing long-context pretraining data selection relies primarily on sequence length, failing to distinguish genuinely long-range-dependent text from lengthy sequences dominated by repetitions, independent segments, or locally predictable tokens. This dilutes the training signal, as tokens that do not require extended context contribute equally to the learning loss, making continued pretraining on such data inefficient for unlocking long-context capabilities.

## Input / Output

LongFilter consumes tokenized sequences of 64K tokens along with a pre-trained causal LM (Llama-3.1-8B, 128K context) used purely for scoring. At each token position, it computes next-token probabilities under two context windows: a 4K short window (implemented via overlapping sliding chunks to avoid boundary artifacts) and the full 64K long context. The output is a per-token surrogate KL score aggregated by averaging into a single sequence-level score; the top 20% of sequences by this score are retained as the training subset.

## Method

LongFilter formalizes long-context data quality as the conditional mutual information I(T; E | S) between the next token T and the extended context E given the short context S, then derives a practical single-sample surrogate: score(t*, s*, e*) = p(t*|e*,s*) · log[p(t*|e*,s*) / p(t*|s*)], which avoids summing over the full vocabulary and leverages the ground-truth token. In loss terms this becomes exp(−L_long)·(L_short − L_long), weighting the loss reduction from longer context by the model's confidence under the full context. The pipeline runs the scoring LM in two passes—full-context forward pass for long probabilities and chunked sliding-window pass for short probabilities—then averages the per-token scores over the sequence. Sequences are ranked by this aggregate score and the top 20% are selected for continued pretraining, requiring no architectural or training-hyperparameter changes beyond the standard RoPE base-frequency adjustment.

## Results

On RULER, LongFilter achieves the highest overall scores across all three corpora: 70.13 (Arxiv), 78.95 (Book), and 75.37 (CommonCrawl), outperforming ProLong (69.28/73.35/72.59) and LongWanjuan (69.69/73.74/74.08). On LongBench, LongFilter similarly leads with overall scores of 39.52 (Arxiv), 39.81 (Book), and 40.66 (CommonCrawl). On HELMET recall (Needle-in-a-Haystack) tasks, LongFilter reaches above 90 SubEM in all three settings and achieves up to a 10% accuracy gain over unfiltered data at 1B trained tokens. The paper reports that 1.5B filtered tokens match the performance of 3–4B unfiltered tokens, indicating roughly 2× data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongFilter is compared against two baselines: ProLong (Gao et al., 2024), which uses length-based data mixing without quality filtering, and LongWanjuan (Liu et al., 2024c), which applies heuristic and model-based quality metrics to long-text data. LongFilter wins on every dataset–benchmark combination tested. The largest margin is on RULER-Book, where LongFilter scores 78.95 versus 73.35 for ProLong (+5.6) and 73.74 for LongWanjuan (+5.2), driven by strong gains on structured retrieval sub-tasks (MultiKey: 97.87 vs 83.80/91.03; Other: 54.71 vs 46.56/48.23). On LongBench, LongFilter leads by 1.0–2.3 points overall, with the biggest advantage on the Book corpus (39.81 vs 37.47/37.69) and in the Synthetic category (57.02 on CC vs 52.18/47.87). On HELMET recall tasks, LongFilter consistently surpasses both baselines from 0.5B trained tokens onward and maintains a clear lead through 4B tokens. Notably, LongFilter's advantage is most pronounced on tasks requiring retrieval of information from arbitrary positions (NIAH MultiKey, MultiValue, MultiQuery), confirming that the KL-divergence-based scoring effectively targets long-range dependency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in long-context pretraining data engineering, which focuses on sequence length (increasing the proportion of long sequences or adjusting long-to-short ratios, as in ProLong and Fu et al.), by instead asking whether the extended context actually provides predictive information. It repurposes information-theoretic tools—conditional mutual information and KL divergence—into a practical scoring function for data curation, a framing not present in prior long-context data selection work. The closest conceptual neighbor, LongWanjuan, proposes quality metrics for long text but does not explicitly contrast long- versus short-context predictions, and its model-based filtering uses windows too short to capture genuine long-range dependencies. LongAttn uses attention scores for a similar goal, but the authors note that attention scores do not reliably capture token importance, motivating the likelihood-based scoring instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that roughly half the training data suffices when filtered by long-range information gain (1.5B filtered tokens matching 3–4B unfiltered) is directly actionable for teams scaling long-context models, reducing compute costs without architectural changes. However, the evaluation is limited to a single model family (LLaMA-3-8B), a single corpus family (SlimPajama), and only 4B tokens of continued pretraining, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger models, different base architectures, or non-English corpora. An open question is whether the scoring model (Llama-3.1-8B) must match the training model, and whether the 4K/64K window choices are optimal or transferable to other context-length targets.
