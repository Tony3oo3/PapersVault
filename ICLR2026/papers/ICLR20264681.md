## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs on narrow distributions to modify specific behaviors (sycophancy, refusal, evilness) risks unintended generalizations, catastrophic forgetting, or emergent misalignment, while broad RLHF/SFT is expensive and hard to scale. Activation steering offers more interpretable control than prompting but often fails to generalize out-of-distribution and is less expressive than modifying weights. The paper asks whether weight-space directions derived from small contrastive datasets can provide more generalizable and precise behavioral control than activation steering.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained model's weights, two small contrastive datasets (D+ and D−, each 500–900 examples generated from the target model via behavior-eliciting system prompts and filtered by GPT-4.1-mini), and a scalar coefficient k. Fine-tuning uses LoRA (rank 32, alpha 16) on all modules for roughly one epoch. The output is a single weight steering vector w_b that can be added to any target model's weights (the original model or a task-fine-tuned model), producing steered text responses without requiring inference-time intervention. The same vector can also be used as a monitoring signal by computing cosine similarity against fine-tuning updates.

## Method

Contrastive weight steering isolates a behavior direction in weight space by fine-tuning the model separately on D+ (desired behavior) and D− (opposite behavior) with LoRA, computing two task vectors τ+ = θ_positive − θ_pre and τ− = θ_negative − θ_pre, and taking their difference w_b = τ+ − τ−. This contrastive subtraction removes weight changes shared by both fine-tunes (topic, style, length) and retains only the behavior-specific direction. The target model is then steered via θ_steered = θ_pre + k·w_b (or θ_ft + k·w_b for post-fine-tuning correction), where k controls the magnitude of behavioral shift. Unlike activation steering, which adds a vector to a single layer's hidden state at inference time, weight steering modifies all parameters simultaneously, giving it greater expressiveness. The paper also demonstrates that the same contrastive weight vectors can serve as a monitoring tool: fine-tuning updates that induce emergent misalignment align more closely (by cosine similarity) with an 'evil' weight direction than with control directions, suggesting weight-space monitoring could detect misalignment that never surfaces in black-box evaluations.

## Results

Across three behavioral domains (sycophancy, evilness, refusal), contrastive weight steering consistently outperforms activation steering in out-of-distribution generalization before degrading general capabilities. For sycophancy on TruthfulQA/TriviaQA, weight steering modifies both answer style and factual content more effectively than activation steering, fine-tuning, or prompting. For evilness on the World Affecting MCQA, weight steering reaches higher evilness levels before TinyMMLU accuracy drops, and produces fewer CoT-to-answer inconsistencies compared to activation steering. For refusal after GSM8K fine-tuning of Llama-2-7b-chat, weight steering with refusal data matches the Joint baseline (adding refusal examples during training) as the most effective restoration strategy, while activation steering and system prompting are substantially weaker.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against activation steering (persona vectors) from Chen et al. (2025), using identical contrastive data (D+, D−) for both methods. Weight steering wins on generalization in all three main experiments: for sycophancy, it controls content (factual correctness under user cues) more effectively than activation steering, which primarily shifts tone; for evilness MCQA, weight steering achieves higher valid-and-evil rates before TinyMMLU degrades and shows lower CoT inconsistency (activation steering increases inconsistent reasoning-answer pairs); for refusal, weight steering with refusal data matches the Joint training baseline and substantially outperforms activation steering. Additional baselines include direct fine-tuning on D+ (weight steering achieves comparable behavioral shifts with more precise control via the scalar k), system prompting (ineffective for sycophancy, mildly helpful for refusal but degrades task performance), and non-contrastive task vectors (weaker than contrastive weight steering, especially for sycophancy and refusal). The bias-only variant of weight steering consistently falls between full weight steering and activation steering, suggesting the advantage comes from modifying full weight matrices rather than just activations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task vector framework of Ilharco et al. (2023) from discrete classification benchmarks to continuous alignment-relevant behaviors (sycophancy, evilness, refusal), and introduces a contrastive construction—subtracting two fine-tuned weight deltas—that parallels the positive/negative contrast used in activation steering (Chen et al., 2025; Choubey et al., 2023). The key conceptual move is treating behavioral steering as a weight-space operation rather than an activation-space one, enabling direct parameter edits that persist across all layers and inference contexts. The paper also draws on the emergent misalignment literature (Betley et al., 2025; Turner et al., 2025) to propose weight-direction monitoring as a complementary detection signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, low-cost post-training tool for practitioners who need to correct or adjust specific LLM behaviors without retraining on large distributions, and the consistent OOD generalization advantage over activation steering makes it a potentially more robust default. The preliminary monitoring result—that misaligned fine-tuning updates align more closely with an 'evil' weight direction than control directions—opens an intriguing but unvalidated avenue for detecting alignment drift that black-box evaluations miss. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models and more subtle misalignments, whether the monitoring signal is precise enough for production use, and how weight steering interacts with more sophisticated merging strategies (linear scaling, subspace boosting) that the authors leave unexplored.
