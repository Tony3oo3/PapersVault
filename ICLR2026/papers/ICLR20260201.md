## Problem

No globally optimal algorithm existed for minimizing the 0-1 loss (the actual classification objective) in two-layer ReLU or Maxout networks. Prior exact-optimization work (Arora et al. 2016; Hertrich 2022) was restricted to convex surrogate losses, provided only ambiguous pseudocode with undefined complexity terms, assumed hyperplane partitions were given as input, and produced no implementation in eight years. This paper closes that gap by delivering the first provably correct, implementable algorithm for the discrete 0-1 loss ERM problem in these architectures.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes N labeled data points in R^D (assumed in general position, i.e., no D+1 points on a common (D−1)-dimensional affine subspace) along with the number of features D and hidden neurons K. It outputs the optimal nested combination of K hyperplanes (each defined by a D-subset of data points) together with the binary sign assignment over the K neurons that minimizes the 0-1 loss, plus the minimum loss value. For datasets too large for exhaustive search, a coreset selection heuristic (Algorithm 4) iteratively shrinks the working set via shuffling, block partitioning, and max-heap filtering before invoking the exact solver, and the divide-and-conquer variant returns intermediate combination structures to support embarrassingly parallel execution without inter-processor communication.

## Method

The key combinatorial insight is that, under 0-1 loss, each critical hyperplane in R^D is determined by a D-subset of data points (yielding O(N^D) candidates), and a K-neuron network's decision boundary is a K-combination of such hyperplanes with 2^K sign orientations, giving a finite search space of O(N^{DK}) configurations. The core technical contribution is an efficient recursive nested-combination generator defined as a join-list homomorphism, which—via the fusion law—allows the evaluation and selection steps to be fused directly into the generation loop, eliminating the need to materialize all intermediate combinations. A symmetric fusion theorem specific to maxout neurons further halves the orientation search from 2^K to 2^{K−1} by showing that reversing all normal vectors yields predictions directly from the original configuration. The resulting Deep-ICE algorithm is specified in a single equation and instantiated as two implementations: a sequential version with memoized hyperplane predictions in a pre-allocated matrix (exploiting revolving-door ordering for GPU Tensor Core acceleration) and a divide-and-conquer version that splits the data, processes halves in parallel, and merges via the nested-combination algebra. Worst-case time complexity is O(N^{DK+1}) with space O(N^{D(K−1)}), and a coreset selection wrapper extends practical applicability to large-scale tabular datasets.

## Results

On 11 UCI datasets, Deep-ICE consistently achieves the highest test accuracy across all K values (1–3 hidden neurons), outperforming both SVM and gradient-descent-trained MLPs. On the largest dataset SS (N=51,433, D=3), Deep-ICE (K=1) reaches 86.72% test accuracy versus 82.75% for SVM and 79.73% for MLP (K=3). On DB (N=1,146, D=9), Deep-ICE (K=3) achieves 81.32% test accuracy, a 13.7-point improvement over SVM (67.62%) and a 6.2-point improvement over MLP (K=3, 75.11%). The algorithm also achieves the lowest 0-1 loss on the Voicepath dataset (N=704, D=2), finding 16 misclassifications versus 25 for gradient descent on the same rank-2 maxout architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the EXACT framework (Karpukhin et al., 2024), which optimizes expected accuracy via stochastic gradient descent, Deep-ICE achieves higher test accuracy on 9 of 11 datasets. For example, on SS (N=51,433), Deep-ICE (K=1) reaches 86.72% test accuracy versus 86.53% for EXACT (K=1); on Caesarian, Deep-ICE (K=2) achieves 88.00% test accuracy versus 62.38% for EXACT (K=2). Against SVM, Deep-ICE wins on all 11 datasets in test accuracy, with the largest margin on DB (81.32% vs. 67.62%, a 13.7-point gap) and SS (86.72% vs. 82.75%). Against MLP trained with Adam and binary cross-entropy, Deep-ICE also dominates on all 11 datasets; the gap is most pronounced on SS where MLP (K=3) achieves only 79.73% test accuracy compared to Deep-ICE's 86.72%. Notably, the paper reports that Deep-ICE's superior training accuracy does not translate to overfitting—its out-of-sample performance remains strong, challenging the assumption that exact ERM solutions necessarily generalize poorly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exact 0-1 loss linear classification algorithm of He & Little (2023) and the join-list combination generator from He & Little (2024) to the two-layer ReLU/Maxout network setting, where the combinatorial search space becomes a nested combination of hyperplanes. It departs from Arora et al. (2016) and Hertrich (2022), who proposed enumeration-based exact training for two-layer ReLU networks but were restricted to convex losses, provided only pseudocode with ambiguous complexity, and produced no implementation in eight years. The key conceptual departure is replacing the one-by-one non-recursive enumeration with a recursive join-list homomorphism that admits fusion, enabling memoization, parallelization, and incremental solution generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first provably exact algorithm for the practically most relevant classification objective (0-1 loss) in two-layer networks, with a clear polynomial-time guarantee for fixed D and K and working CUDA implementations that handle over 10^11 configurations in minutes. The empirical finding that globally optimal solutions do not necessarily overfit, and that the maximal-margin (SVM) classifier does not consistently outperform other low-training-error solutions, challenges a widely held assumption in the field. Open questions include whether bounding or pruning techniques can further accelerate the search, whether the approach scales meaningfully beyond two layers (where complexity becomes intractable), and whether the coreset selection heuristic can be given theoretical guarantees for solution quality.
