## Problem

The Neural Feature Ansatz (NFA), a widely validated empirical conjecture stating that W^T W is proportional to the Average Gradient Outer Product after training, lacks any first-principles justification. Because it was derived empirically, it is unclear why it holds, under what conditions it might fail, or how to improve it. This paper asks whether a relation derived from first-order optimality conditions can serve as a principled alternative that provably holds at convergence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained neural network (or kernel machine) with non-zero weight decay λ > 0, and for any weight matrix W that enters the model via matrix multiplication of internal activations h(x), it computes the FACT matrix from the per-sample loss gradients ∇_h ℓ_i and the corresponding activations h(x_i). The output is a provable identity W^T W = FACT at convergence, and when plugged into the Recursive Feature Machine algorithm, a kernel predictor f̂(x) = K_W(x, X)α. Unlike NFA, FACT requires no tunable power exponent s and no architectural assumptions beyond the matrix-multiplication structure, though it is only guaranteed positive semi-definite at exact critical points, necessitating symmetrization in practice.

## Method

The core contribution is the Features at Convergence Theorem (FACT): at any critical point of the regularized loss, left-multiplying the stationarity condition ∇_W L_λ = 0 by W^T yields the identity W^T W = −(1/nλ) Σ_i (∇_h ℓ_i)(h(x_i))^T, a self-consistency relation between the weight matrix and the data-dependent loss gradients. The authors then replace the NFA-based fixed-point update in the Recursive Feature Machine with a FACT-based update W_{t+1} ← (FACT_t · FACT_t^T)^{1/4} (or a geometrically-averaged variant for stability), showing it reproduces grokking on modular arithmetic, phase transitions in sparse parity learning, and state-of-the-art tabular accuracy. For inner-product kernels, they prove that FACT and AGOP share the same structural form up to different similarity factors (k′ vs τ), explaining why NFA usually holds. Finally, they construct a two-layer quadratic network on an adversarial mixture distribution where NFA is provably uncorrelated with the true weights while FACT still holds exactly, demonstrating FACT's robustness as a convergence-time relation.

## Results

On 120 UCI tabular datasets, FACT-RFM (no geometric averaging) achieves 85.22% average test accuracy, marginally exceeding NFA-RFM (85.10%) and substantially outperforming kernel regression without feature learning (83.71%). On MNIST and CIFAR-10, FACT achieves Pearson correlation of 0.999–1.000 with W^T W at convergence (5-layer ReLU MLPs), compared to 0.909–0.924 for AGOP (NFA) and 0.903–0.923 for eNFA. On an adversarial two-layer network construction, FACT achieves 0.994 cosine similarity with W^T W while AGOP drops below 0.068, demonstrating a provable separation. Both FACT-RFM and NFA-RFM reproduce grokking (delayed generalization) on modular arithmetic (mod 61) and phase transitions in sparse parity learning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NFA-RFM (Radhakrishnan et al., 2024), FACT-RFM achieves a marginal improvement on the UCI tabular benchmark (85.22% vs. 85.10%) and matches it on sparse parity and modular arithmetic tasks, where both methods learn block-circulant or low-rank features and exhibit grokking/phase transitions. However, FACT clearly outperforms NFA on the theoretical validation front: at convergence on MNIST/CIFAR-10, FACT's Pearson correlation with W^T W reaches 0.999–1.000 versus 0.909–0.924 for AGOP, and on the adversarial two-layer construction, FACT maintains 0.994 cosine similarity while AGOP falls below 0.068. Against eNFA (Ziyin et al., 2025), FACT consistently shows higher correlation to W^T W across all tested settings (e.g., 0.896 vs. 0.714 on CIFAR-10 with weight decay 10^{-4}), and unlike NFA, FACT requires no tunable power hyperparameter s that must scale with network depth (as shown in Appendix C for deep linear networks).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: the empirically-derived Neural Feature Ansatz (NFA) of Radhakrishnan et al. (2024), which posits a proportionality between W^T W and the Average Gradient Outer Product, and the first-order optimality conditions literature that has been used to characterize implicit biases (low-rank, sparsity, Fourier-sparsity) at convergence. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the stationarity condition ∇_W L_λ = 0, when left-multiplied by W^T, yields the FACT relation—a self-consistency formula that is provably true at convergence and requires no tunable hyperparameters (unlike NFA's power s, which must scale as 1/L for deep linear networks). The paper also positions itself against the equivariant NFA (eNFA) of Ziyin et al. (2025) and the Recursive Feature Machine algorithm, showing that FACT-based updates are close relatives of NFA-based updates for inner-product kernels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main impact is placing the widely-used NFA conjecture on firm theoretical footing: FACT explains why NFA usually holds (the two share structural form with similar similarity-weighted terms) while also proving that NFA can be nearly uncorrelated with actual weights in adversarial settings, with FACT still holding. This suggests NFA is a useful but incomplete approximation, and FACT provides a more principled target. Key limitations include the requirement of non-zero weight decay, the fact that FACT only holds at convergence (correlation is low during most of training), and instability of FACT-RFM in some regimes requiring early stopping. Open questions include whether FACT can be computed in the zero weight-decay limit via the empirical NTK, and whether a quantity with stable correlation throughout training can be derived.
