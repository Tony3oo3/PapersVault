## Problem

While mechanistic interpretability has made progress on general LLM representations, how these models internally store and process medical knowledge—patient demographics, symptoms, diseases, drugs, and dosages—remains largely unexplored. Prior medical interpretability studies relied on a single explainability technique and a single model, making it impossible to draw generalizable conclusions about where and how medical knowledge is represented across LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260483/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes five open-source LLMs (Llama3.3-70B, Gemma3-27B, MedGemma-27B, Qwen-32B, GPT-OSS-120B) plus six medical-domain LLMs, and feeds them structured medical prompts covering ages 1–100, symptom descriptions, disease diagnoses, 9-stage disease progressions (Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, COVID-19, COPD), drug names, and drug dosages. It produces per-layer knowledge maps identifying continuous layer intervals where each medical concept is stored, along with UMAP 2D/30D projections, Silhouette scores, R² linearity measures, gradient saliency values, lesioning degradation scores (via GPT-4o judge), and normalized logit-difference patching effects, all with bootstrap confidence intervals.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-method interpretability pipeline that integrates four independent techniques—UMAP projections of intermediate activations, gradient-based weight saliency, layer lesioning (replacing layers with identity functions), and activation patching (swapping single-layer outputs between clean and corrupted runs)—to triangulate where medical knowledge resides in each model. For each medical concept, quantitative metrics are extracted per layer (Silhouette score for cluster separability, local anisotropy for age 1D-ness, R² for age linearity, CSFS/CSLS for disease progression circularity, normalized logit difference for patching effect), then Gaussian-smoothed and thresholded to select continuous layer intervals. The resulting LLM knowledge maps overlay intervals from all four methods, so that agreement across methods with different assumptions increases confidence in the identified layers. Key findings include non-linear/discontinuous age encoding (notably a 17/18-year boundary), circular non-monotonic disease progression representations, drug clustering by medical specialty over mechanism of action in Llama3.3-70B, and activation collapse at intermediate layers in Gemma/MedGemma models.

## Results

Across five LLMs, Llama3.3-70B and Qwen-32B achieve the highest Silhouette scores for symptoms (0.19 and 0.24) and diseases (0.16 and 0.21), while GPT-OSS-120B and Llama3.3-70B both achieve near-perfect age linearity (R²=1.00). For drug representations, Llama3.3-70B shows the strongest clustering by medical specialty (Silhouette 0.19) versus mechanism of action (0.07), confirming that drugs are organized by specialty rather than pharmacology. Gemma3-27B and MedGemma-27B consistently underperform across all categories, with negative Silhouette scores for symptoms and diseases, and exhibit activation collapse at intermediate layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a cross-model interpretability study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the 'baselines' are the other four LLMs. Llama3.3-70B and Qwen-32B dominate on clustering quality (Silhouette) for symptoms and diseases, with Qwen-32B achieving 0.24 for symptoms vs. 0.19 for Llama and negative values (−0.08 to −0.10) for Gemma/MedGemma. For disease progression circularity, Gemma3-27B, MedGemma-27B, and Qwen-32B all achieve the lowest CSFS (4.25), indicating the most circular (non-monotonic) representations, while Llama3.3-70B shows the least circularity (CSFS 6.25). On drug representation, Llama3.3-70B uniquely shows a clear specialty-over-mechanism clustering gap (0.19 vs. 0.07 Silhouette), whereas other models show much smaller or no gap. GPT-OSS-120B, despite having only 36 layers, matches Llama on age linearity (R²=1.00) and is competitive on symptoms (0.23) and disease progression (CSLS 6.75), suggesting efficient knowledge packing. Gemma3-27B and MedGemma-27B are consistently the weakest across all metrics and additionally exhibit intermediate-layer activation collapse, a phenomenon not observed in the other three models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes four established mechanistic interpretability techniques—UMAP projections, gradient-based weight saliency, layer lesioning, and activation patching—into a unified 'knowledge map' framework applied specifically to medical-domain LLMs. Prior medical interpretability work (Chen et al., Savage et al., He et al., Wu et al., Kraišniković et al.) each relied on a single explainability method on a single model, making cross-model comparisons impossible; this paper is the first to systematically apply all four techniques across five open-source LLMs for the same set of medical concepts. It also extends the non-linear representation findings of Engels et al. (2024) from general features (days of the week, years) to medical concepts, showing that age and disease progression are encoded non-linearly and circularly in LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The knowledge maps provide actionable layer-level guidance for fine-tuning, unlearning, or de-biasing medical LLMs, which is critical as these models are increasingly deployed in clinical settings. The discovery of a discontinuity at age 18 in Llama's internal representations raises concrete concerns about age-based bias that could affect diagnostic or treatment recommendations for adolescents. Key open questions remain: what causes the Gemma/MedGemma intermediate-layer activation collapse, whether the circular disease progression representation is a useful inductive bias or a failure mode, and whether the findings generalize beyond the limited set of diseases and prompts used. The lack of ground-truth validation for internal representations is a fundamental limitation that the authors acknowledge but cannot fully overcome.
