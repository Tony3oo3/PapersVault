## Problem

Standard 3DGS optimization with synchronous Adam has two overlooked coupling issues: (i) update-step coupling, where invisible primitives still undergo moment rescaling and implicit updates from historical optimizer states, wasting computation and distorting optimization; and (ii) gradient coupling, where regularization gradients are jointly preconditioned with photometric gradients by Adam's adaptive moments, making regularization over- or under-effective and impossible to control independently. These couplings limit both optimization efficiency and reconstruction quality but have not been explicitly analyzed or addressed.

## Input / Output

The method operates within standard 3DGS or 3DGS-MCMC pipelines, consuming multi-view RGB images and SfM-derived camera poses to initialize Gaussian primitives. It produces optimized 3D Gaussian primitives (position, opacity, scale, color) that render novel views via alpha blending. A notable property is that redundant primitives are automatically eliminated through the decoupled opacity regularization without any explicit pruning operation, reducing active primitive counts by ~50% in vanilla 3DGS while simultaneously improving reconstruction quality.

## Method

The paper decouples the coupled 3DGS optimization into three recomposed components forming AdamW-GS: (1) Sparse Adam, which restricts updates to primitives visible from the current viewpoint, eliminating wasteful implicit updates; (2) Re-State Regularization (RSR), which periodically samples primitives and rescales their first- and second-moment estimates to deliberately activate regularization; and (3) Decoupled Attribute Regularization (DAR), which separates photometric and regularization gradients in the moment computation (analogous to AdamW) and applies regularization as ∇R/√v̂ with a magnitude clip, enabling per-primitive adaptive penalties that are strong near saddle points but negligible in under-constructed regions. The key insight is that after decoupling the gradient in the moment, √v̂ provides a faithful estimate of local optimization geometry, so regularization can be adaptively scaled per primitive without destabilizing the photometric optimization. This design simultaneously improves reconstruction quality (e.g., +0.27 dB PSNR over 3DGS-MCMC) and removes ~50% of redundant primitives in vanilla 3DGS without any additional pruning component.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, AdamW-GS within 3DGS-MCMC improves PSNR from 27.948 to 28.219 dB (+0.27), SSIM from 0.833 to 0.840, and LPIPS from 0.199 to 0.182, while reducing training time by ~15% (46.81→39.77 min). In vanilla 3DGS, AdamW-GS raises PSNR from 27.506 to 27.678 dB and SSIM from 0.815 to 0.822, while automatically removing 49.3% of active primitives (no extra pruning) and cutting training time by 40% (30.58→18.53 min). On Deep Blending, 3DGS+AdamW-GS reaches 30.260 dB PSNR (vs. 29.694 for vanilla 3DGS) with a 60% reduction in active primitives; on Tanks & Temples it achieves 24.303 dB (vs. 23.677) with a 40% reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla 3DGS, AdamW-GS (GS8) wins on PSNR (+0.17 dB) and SSIM (+0.007) while removing 49.3% of active primitives and halving training time; LPIPS is marginally worse (0.220 vs. 0.216). Against 3DGS-MCMC, AdamW-GS (MC8) improves all three quality metrics (PSNR +0.27 dB, SSIM +0.007, LPIPS −0.017) and is ~15% faster. Against RePR, AdamW-GS achieves higher PSNR (+0.175 dB), removes more primitives (−49.3% vs. −41.1%), and trains 30% faster (18.53 vs. 26.73 min). Against MaskGaussian, AdamW-GS achieves higher PSNR (+0.193 dB) and SSIM (+0.007) and is 31% faster, though MaskGaussian removes slightly more primitives (−53.1% vs. −49.3%); notably, MaskGaussian degrades PSNR by 0.1 dB relative to vanilla 3DGS while AdamW-GS improves it. On Deep Blending, 3DGS+AdamW-GS outperforms MaskGaussian by 0.365 dB PSNR while removing fewer primitives (−60% vs. −65%), and 3DGS-MCMC+AdamW-GS surpasses the original MCMC by 0.33 dB PSNR. The paper also shows compatibility: adding AdamW-GS to MaskGaussian recovers the indoor-scene quality drop and prunes an additional ~7% of primitives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS optimization pipeline by adapting AdamW's gradient-decoupling principle (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2017) to the physical attributes of Gaussian primitives, and builds on Sparse Adam from Taming-3DGS (Mallick et al., 2024) by diagnosing why its efficiency gains degrade reconstruction quality—specifically through overlooked update-step coupling and moment rescaling. It positions itself as an optimization-level analysis of vanilla 3DGS and 3DGS-MCMC rather than a new representation, decomposing the coupled Adam update into three independently controllable components (Sparse Adam, Re-State Regularization, Decoupled Attribute Regularization) before recoupling them as AdamW-GS. The work also connects to the broader redundancy-removal literature (MaskGaussian, RePR, Taming-3DGS) by showing that properly decoupled attribute regularization can subsume explicit pruning operations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for why Sparse Adam underperforms and why existing L1 regularization in 3DGS is neither reliably under- nor over-effective, offering practitioners a more controllable optimization recipe that simultaneously improves quality and reduces primitive count without additional pruning modules. However, the method introduces multiple hyperparameters and hand-crafted StSS schedules whose robustness, while tested, remains scene-dependent; noise-based exploration also fails on small-primitive indoor scenes. Open questions include adaptive parameter scheduling during training, generalization of DAR to other attributes (rotation, color), and more systematic exploration strategies beyond the ad hoc noise and ABE-split approaches tested here.
