## Problem

Existing time reversal sampling methods for generative inbetweening connect a forward denoising path (conditioned on the start frame) and a backward path (conditioned on the end frame), but each path follows the motion prior of its own conditioning frame. Because I2V diffusion models are trained with a forward-generation bias, the backward path tends to produce forward-looking sequences rather than faithfully reconstructing historical frames, leading to motion conflicts, ghosting artifacts, and reverse-play effects in the generated intermediate video.

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB keyframes (start and e) at 1024×576 resolution, encodes them into SVD's latent space, and generates a 25-frame temporally coherent video bridging the two endpoints. It is entirely training-free: MPD is plugged into existing time reversal samplers (TRF or ViBiD) on top of the pre-trained SVD-XT model, requiring only a modified sampling loop with a few extra re-noising steps during early denoising timesteps.

## Method

Motion Prior Distillation (MPD) is a training-free inference-time technique that replaces two conflicting motion priors with a single coherent one from the start frame. During early denoising steps (controlled by ratio γ), MPD denoises only the forward path with the start-frame condition, computes the frame-wise residual of the denoised estimates (which encodes the forward motion prior), converts it to noise-residual space, and reconstructs the backward path's noise by cumulatively subtracting forward noise residuals from an initial backward noise anchored at the e-frame latent. The forward denoised estimate is then fused with the temporally flipped reconstructed backward estimate via an interpolation scale λ, and the sample is updated with a CFG++-constrained Euler step. By deliberately avoiding denoising the backward path with the e-frame condition, MPD prevents the introduction of an independent conflicting motion prior, allowing both temporal paths to converge on a single coherent trajectory that satisfies both endpoint constraints.

## Results

On DAVIS, Ours+TRF achieves the best LPIPS (0.2212) and FID (34.910), while Ours+ViBiD achieves the best FVD (527.05) and VB++ (0.9474). On Pexels, Ours+ViBiD leads in LPIPS (0.1028) and FVD (412.66), and Ours+TRF leads in FID (34.470) and VB++ (0.9862). Both variants consistently outperform the strongest baseline (FCVG) by 5–10% in FID and 10–20% in FVD across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines: two flow/generative VFI models (FILM, DynamiCrafter) and four time-reversal sampling methods (TRF, GI, FCVG, ViBiD). Ours+TRF and Ours+ViBiD outperform all baselines on LPIPS and FID across both datasets, with the largest margins over TRF (eID 56.9→34.9 on DAVIS) and DynamiCrafter (EID 46.7→34.9). On FVD, Ours+ViBiD (527.05 on DAVIS, 412.66 on Pexels) beats the best baseline ViBiD (559.49, 641.30) by ~6% and ~36% respectively. The closest competitor is FCVG, which achieves EID 38.997 on DAVIS and 35.269 on Pexels; our methods improve upon it by ~4–10% in FID. On VB++ (perceptual quality), Ours+TRF leads on Pexels (0.9862) while FILM is slightly ahead on DAVIS (0.9740 vs 0.9330), though FILM's advantage is attributed to flow-based warping that preserves local endpoint structure at the cost of temporal coherence (its FVD is 1058 vs our 527–612). In user studies, Ours+ViBiD is ranked highest in alignment (0.2440) and selected least for artifacts (8.93%) and unrealistic motion (9.88%), confirming the quantitative gains translate to human preference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the time reversal sampling paradigm introduced by TRF (parallel fusion) and ViBiDSampler (sequential alternation), both of which condition separate denoising paths on start and end frames of a Stable Video Diffusion model. The key departure is a diagnostic reframing: the authors formalize the bidirectional path misalignment as an optimization objective (Eq. 11) and identify the root cause as conflicting motion priors induced by the forward-generation bias of I2V models. Rather than fusing or alternating two independently denoised paths, MPD eliminates the end-conditioned denoising entirely and instead reconstructs the backward path from the temporal residual of the forward denoised estimates, effectively distilling a single coherent motion prior into both directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of MPD makes it immediately applicable to any existing time reversal sampler without retraining, which is practically valuable given the compute cost of fine-tuning SVD-based models (as GI and FCVG require). However, the method's reliance on the forward path's motion prior as ground truth means it degrades when the end frame introduces entirely new objects or large-scale scene rearrangements, a limitation the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include whether the residual-distillation idea generalizes to DiT-based I2V models (e.g., CogVideoX) and whether the single-prior assumption holds for multi-object scenes with independent motions.
