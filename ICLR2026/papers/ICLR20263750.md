## Problem

Projection-based debiasing, the dominant approach for removing spurious concept bias from VLM embeddings, provably amplifies bias in all orthogonal subspaces by a constant factor greater than one, creating a 'whac-a-mole' dilemma where debiasing one concept unpredictably worsens bias in unconsidered concepts. Since it is impossible to enumerate all potential biases in practice, this amplification goes unobserved during evaluation, meaning models are not truly debiased but merely have bias transferred and hidden elsewhere.

## Input / Output

The method operates on frozen CLIP image embeddings (no fine-tuning) and requires concept group direction vectors, which are defined either as mean embeddings of K=1000 reference images per group from a held-out dataset, or as the difference between group-specific text prompt embeddings and neutral prompt embeddings. The output is a norm-preserving debiased embedding that has equal cosine similarity to all group directions within the target concept, which can then be used for retrieval ranking or zero-shot classification without additional training.

## Method

WRING replaces the standard projection operation v − P_C v with a rotation: v − P_C v + ‖P_C v‖ · w, where w is a unit vector in the concept subspace col(A_C) that has equal cosine similarity to every group direction c_i (i.e., bias(w, c_i, c_j) = 0 for all i, j). The vector w is computed as A_C(A_C^T A_C)^{-1} 1, normalized to unit length. The key theoretical insight is that projection introduces a bias amplification term ‖v‖/‖v − P_C v‖ > 1 that uniformly scales existing bias in orthogonal subspaces, while WRING eliminates this amplification term entirely and adds a dampening term Δ_w that counteracts the residual bias-altering effect. This guarantees zero bias change for orthogonal concept subspaces and significantly mitigated change for related subspaces, while still achieving comparable debiasing of the target concept.

## Results

On CelebA worst-group accuracy (Table 2), WRING achieves 80.56% gender accuracy (vs. 78.89% for Projection and 72.78% for Baseline) with an accuracy gap of 9.24 (vs. 11.87 for Projection and 17.02 for Baseline). The key finding is in bias amplification: WRING reduces the change in bias for unconsidered concepts by roughly an order of magnitude compared to projection (e.g., 0.441×10⁻² vs. 26.7×10⁻² on FairFace with LP14, Table 7), while still achieving comparable demographic parity on the target concept (~2.0–2.6×10⁻²). Across all four datasets and three CLIP backbones, WRING consistently shows near-zero bias amplification for unconsidered concepts, whereas projection produces large, unpredictable increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Baseline CLIP, WRING substantially improves worst-group gender accuracy (80.56% vs. 72.78%) and halves the gender accuracy gap (9.24 vs. 17.02), confirming effective target-concept debiasing. Against Projection (both text- and image-direction variants), WRING achieves comparable or better target-concept demographic parity while reducing bias amplification for unconsidered concepts by roughly 10–60× (e.g., 0.441 vs. 26.7 on FairFace/LP14; 0.06–0.63 vs. 0.57–3.63 across all FairFace query types in Table 4). Against SFID, WRING achieves significantly lower target-concept bias (DP ~2.0–2.6×10⁻² vs. ~4.5–12×10⁻²) while maintaining similarly low unconsidered-concept bias change, showing SFID fails to actually debias the target. Against FairerCLIP, WRING achieves lower worst-group accuracy (80.56% vs. 84.78% for gender) but critically preserves unconsidered-concept performance close to baseline (Age 85.59 vs. 87.42 baseline) whereas FairerCLIP degrades unconsidered attributes substantially (Age 82.66, Skin Tone 83.33, Nose Shape 84.21), validating the paper's central claim that projection-based methods hide bias amplification in unconsidered concepts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the projection-based debiasing paradigm established by Bolukbasi et al. (2016) and widely adopted in VLM debiasing (Chuang et al. 2023a, Wang et al. 2021, Seth et al. 2023, Zhang et al. 2024), which assumes concepts are encoded in linear subspaces and removes bias by projecting embeddings orthogonal to those subspaces. The key departure is a theoretical analysis showing that projection *necessarily* amplifies bias in all orthogonal subspaces, providing a mathematical explanation for the empirically observed 'whac-a-mole' dilemma (Li et al. 2023). The proposed WRING method replaces projection with a norm-preserving rotation within the target subspace, and is demonstrated as a drop-in replacement within existing pipelines such as BendVLM (Gerych et al. 2024) for nonlinear debiasing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that projection debiasing mathematically guarantees bias amplification in orthogonal subspaces is a clean and important finding that challenges the default assumption in the field that removing a concept subspace is a safe operation. Practically, this means any existing pipeline relying on projection (which is most of them) carries hidden, unmeasured bias amplification for unconsidered concepts — a significant concern as VLMs are deployed in safety-critical settings. The rotation-based fix is simple, requires no retraining, and can be swapped into existing pipelines, but the fundamental limitation remains: one must still enumerate the concepts to debias for, and the method only guarantees no amplification for *orthogonal* subspaces, not for correlated ones. Open questions include whether the linear subspace assumption holds well enough in practice for complex, multi-faceted concepts, and whether iterative or adaptive rotation strategies could handle non-orthogonal concept interactions.
