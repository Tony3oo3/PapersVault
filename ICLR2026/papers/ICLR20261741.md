## Problem

Standard RLHF and DPO rely on the Bradley-Terry model, which cannot capture intransitive or cyclic human preferences. Existing self-play algorithms for the general-preference alignment game (formulated as a two-player zero-sum game) either diverge in the last iterate or only converge to a Nash equilibrium of a modified KL-regularized game, failing to guarantee the critical 50% win rate against any competing policy. The paper asks whether an algorithm exists that provably converges to the Nash equilibrium of the original, unregularized alignment game.

## Input / Output

COMAL takes an initial LLM policy (Llama-3-8B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-7B-SFT), a preference oracle (a 50-50 mixture of two BT reward models—Skywork-Reward and ArmoRM—to simulate annotator diversity and induce intransitivity), and instruction-response pairs from UltraFeedback (64K instructions, split into six 10K subsets per iteration). The output is a single aligned policy checkpoint at the final outer iteration (last iterate, not an average), which is directly deployable. The preference oracle is used both for generating training data (selecting best/worst among 5 sampled candidates) and for evaluation, ensuring a controlled experimental setting.

## Method

COMAL is a meta-algorithm inspired by Nemirovski's Conceptual Prox method: at each outer iteration t, it solves a KL-regularized two-player zero-sum game J_τ(π₁, π₂, π_ref) with the current policy as reference, obtains the Nash equilibrium π^{t+1} of that subgame, then updates the reference to π^{t+1} and repeats. The key insight is that many existing preference-optimization algorithms (PPO, DPO, IPO, INPO, SPPO, REBEL, DRO, GRPO) are practical instantiations of the prox operator, so COMAL wraps them with minimal modification—just adding an outer loop and periodic reference-policy updates. Theoretically, COMAL guarantees monotonically non-increasing KL divergence to the true Nash equilibrium at every iterate (for any τ > 0, no vanishing schedule needed) and provable last-iterate convergence to the unregularized Nash equilibrium, even when each inner subgame is solved only approximately. In practice, COMAL uses INPO as the inner solver with adaptively adjusted KL regularization (η⁻¹ = 0.002 for the first round, 0.1 for subsequent rounds), achieving >60% win rate against all baselines on Llama-3-8B-Instruct.

## Results

On Llama-3-8B-Instruct trained with UltraFeedback, COMAL achieves an average win rate of 71.37% against all compared algorithms, exceeding 60.2% against every individual baseline (DPO: 78.63%, IPO: 78.39%, Iter-IPO: 60.25%, INPO: 64.22%). On Qwen2.5-7B, COMAL achieves a 68.93% average win rate, exceeding 56.8% against all baselines. On standard benchmarks (GSM8K, MMLU, BBH, HumanEval), COMAL maintains comparable performance to the base model while improving AlpacaEval from 25.0 to 53.5 (Llama-3) and Arena-Hard from 21.3 to 41.3, indicating minimal alignment tax.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMAL consistently outperforms all three primary baselines across both model families. Against DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024), COMAL achieves 78.63% win rate on Llama-3 and 79.50% on Qwen2.5, reflecting DPO's inability to handle intransitive preferences under the general preference oracle. Against IPO (Azar et al., 2024), COMAL wins 78.39% (Llama-3) and 78.63% (Qwen2.5), with IPO suffering severe degradation in iterative settings (Iter-IPO drops to 20.75% vs. COMAL). Against INPO (Zhang et al., 2025b), which is the closest competitor and also used as COMAL's inner sub-solver, COMAL achieves 64.22% (Llama-3) and 57.89% (Qwen2.5), confirming that the outer reference-policy update loop is the key differentiator. Notably, COMAL is the only method that maintains above 50% win rate against every other policy simultaneously, consistent with its Nash equilibrium convergence guarantee, while Iter-IPO and INPO both exhibit rapid performance degradation after 6 training iterations on Llama-3.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMAL is a practical instantiation of the Conceptual Prox method (Nemirovski, 2004) adapted to the two-player zero-sum alignment game formulation introduced by Munos et al. (2024) and Swamy et al. (2024). It departs from prior iterative self-play methods (iterative IPO, SPPO) that only guarantee average-iterate convergence, and from KL-regularized Nash solvers (Nash-MD, INPO) that converge to a modified game's equilibrium rather than the original alignment game's Nash policy. A key conceptual contribution is the observation that diverse existing algorithms—PPO, DPO, IPO, SPPO, REBEL, DRO, INPO—can all be interpreted as implementations of the prox operator, making COMAL a drop-in wrapper that requires only periodic reference-policy updates and an outer iteration loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COMAL is the first algorithm with provable last-iterate convergence to the unregularized Nash equilibrium of the alignment game, guaranteeing a 50% win rate against any competing policy—a property no prior method provides. The practical significance is that it can be integrated into existing preference-optimization pipelines with minimal modification, and the per-iteration computational overhead is comparable to baseline methods. However, the empirical validation uses a mixture of two BT reward models as the 'general' preference oracle, which is a limited test of truly arbitrary (e.g., cyclic, population-aggregated) preferences; whether the 50% guarantee translates to genuinely non-BT real-world preference distributions remains an open question. The assumption that the Nash equilibrium's support equals the initial policy's support, while mild, may be restrictive in practice for very large response spaces.
