## Problem

RL for reasoning tasks faces an exploration bottleneck: as reasoning chains grow longer, the probability of sampling a correct solution drops exponentially while rewards remain sparse and binary, causing standard RL to merely amplify existing model behaviors. SFT requires full expert demonstrations that scale poorly with sequence length. The paper investigates whether an intermediate regime of partial supervision—revealing only a prefix of the target rationale—can make long-horizon reasoning tasks learnable when both SFT and RL individually fail.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text prompt (e.g., a math problem) concatenated with a revealed prefix of the ground-truth chain-of-thought, where the prefix length is controlled by a per-sample supervision ratio ρ ∈ [0,1]. The model generates the remaining continuation of the reasoning chain, and a verifiable reward (e.g., final-answer correctness) scores the full output. For the synthetic parity task, input is a binary sequence of length L and output is an interleaved 2L-length sequence of scratchpad bits Y_i and parity bits Z_i, with correctness requiring all Z_i to satisfy the recursive XOR constraint.

## Method

AdaBack is a per-sample adaptive curriculum layered on top of GRPO that dynamically adjusts how much of each target solution is revealed during training. For every sample, the algorithm maintains an interval [ρ_min, ρ_max] (initialized to [0,1]) and uniformly samples a supervision ratio ρ from it; the model is then conditioned on the first ⌊ρ·m⌋ tokens of the target and trained via RL to generate the remainder. After each epoch, if the average reward across rollouts falls below a threshold τ, the interval is narrowed from below (increasing supervision); if reward meets or exceeds τ, it is narrowed from above (decreasing supervision). This performs a stochastic binary search over the supervision ratio, driving each sample along its own trajectory from full supervision to full generation without any handcrafted curriculum schedule. The only hyperparameter is τ (set to 0.5 to maximize reward variance), and a small probability of sampling ρ=0 is injected to close the train-test distribution gap.

## Results

On the synthetic chain-of-parities task (L=16, n=1024), AdaBack with Llama-3.2-1B achieves near-perfect test reward in under 700 iterations, while SFT+RL plateaus at 0.1 (format-only) and R3 reaches only 0.8 after 16,000+ iterations. On real-world benchmarks (Table 1), AdaBack consistently outperforms Base+RL and SFT+RL: on Tensor-2 GSM8k (3B), AdaBack reaches 49.2% vs. 42.7% (SFT+RL) and 0.0% (Base+RL); on Base-7 GSM8k (1B), AdaBack achieves 18.4% vs. 14.4% (SFT+RL) and 4.8% (Base+RL). Pass@k analysis shows AdaBack expands the solution distribution beyond what standard RL reweights, with the largest gains at high k on base models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against R3 (Xi et al., 2024), the closest prior backtracking method, AdaBack wins on DeepScaleR for both model sizes (9.0 vs. 6.6 at 1B; 10.6 vs. 9.6 at 3B) and on MATH at 1B (9.1 vs. 7.8), but slightly trails R3 on GSM8k (39.2 vs. 41.5 at 1B; 73.3 vs. 74.2 at 3B), which the authors attribute to R3's clean newline-based segmentation being well-suited to GSM8k's simple step structure. Against standard GRPO on a base model (Base+RL), AdaBack achieves large gains on out-of-distribution tasks: 8.5% vs. 0.0% on Tensor-2 GSM8k (1B) and 18.4% vs. 4.8% on Base-7 GSM8k (1B). Against SFT+RL, AdaBack on a base model often matches or exceeds the SFT-initialized pipeline (e.g., 49.2% vs. 42.7% on Tensor-2 GSM8k 3B), suggesting adaptive partial supervision can substitute for the SFT phase. On the synthetic parity task, the separation is stark: AdaBack solves the task while SFT+RL and R3 both fail to learn beyond format compliance.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaBack builds directly on the GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024) for multi-rollout RL, augmenting it with a per-sample adaptive supervision mechanism. It departs from prior backtracking approaches (Salimans & Chen, 2018) and R3 (Xi et al., 2024), which use global, static curricula—shifting a single start point or slicing all solutions into a fixed number of delimiter-based segments—by instead adjusting the revealed prefix independently for each training sample based on its reward history. The theoretical analysis draws on parity-learning lower bounds (Abbe et al., 2023a; Kou et al., 2024) to formalize why SFT and RL provably fail in certain sample-complexity regimes where adaptive partial supervision succeeds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a constructive separation: per-sample adaptive curriculum learning enables solving tasks that are provably intractable for SFT, RL, and their combination, reframing curriculum learning from a convergence heuristic to a capability-expanding mechanism. Practically, AdaBack is a drop-in modification to GRPO requiring only one hyperparameter (τ), and it consistently outperforms standard RL on math benchmarks, particularly in out-of-distribution settings (Base-7, Tensor-2 GSM8k). Key limitations include reduced benefit when the base model has already memorized the task (instruct-tuned models), and potential degradation in large-dataset regimes where per-sample adaptation is diluted by infrequent sample revisits. Open questions include whether embedding-space-based supervision scheduling can scale the per-sample adaptivity to data-rich settings, and whether the approach extends to longer-horizon tasks like Olympiad-level math where the exploration gap is most severe.
