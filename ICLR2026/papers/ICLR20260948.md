## Problem

Cancer survival prediction from multimodal data (WSIs + genomics) is hampered by the fact that patient outcomes are driven by a small number of sparse, patient-specific, unannotated structural events (e.g., spatial histologic patterns, pathway co-activations), yet existing methods either rely on fixed prototypes that cannot adapt to individual patients or perform prohibitively expensive instance-level interactions scaling quadratically with input size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260948/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes whole-slide images (256×256 patches at 20× magnification, encoded by the UNI foundation model) and pathway-level gene expression profiles (330 pathways curated from Hallmarks and Reactome, encoded by SNNs), both organized as bags of 256-dimensional instance features. It outputs a disease-specific survival (DSS) hazard prediction over four discretized time intervals, enabling patient stratification into high- and low-risk groups. Notably, when genomic data are unavailable at test time, the cross-modal reconstruction head imputes pathway features directly from histology, allowing inference without omics input.

## Method

SlotSPE compresses each modality's bag of instances into a small set of learnable slots via slot attention (inspired by factorial coding), where each slot encodes a latent prognostic event. A Mixture-of-Experts-style decoder with Gumbel-Top-K selection sparsely activates only the top-K most predictive slots per patient, enforcing mutual competition and patient-specificity. A biologically motivated cross-modal reconstruction task trains omics-derived slots to predict pathway gene expression from histology patches, encoding molecular-morphology mappings and enabling robustness to missing genomics. Multimodal fusion is performed through iterative cross-attention between histology and omics slots (3 rounds of bidirectional updates), combined with intra-modal self-attention, and the final prediction aggregates pooled outputs from three branches. Training jointly optimizes a negative log-likelihood survival loss (multimodal plus single-modal) and reconstruction losses (slots regularization via MSE/cosine similarity, and cross-modal reconstruction via MSE), reducing interaction complexity from O((M_h+M_g)²) to O((S_h+S_g)(M_h+M_g)).

## Results

On the multimodal (histology + genomics) setting across ten TCGA cancer cohorts, SlotSPE achieves an overall C-index of 0.721, outperforming the second-best methods (MOTCat and LD-CVAE, both at 0.692) by 2.9%. In single-modality settings, SlotSPE also leads: gene-only C-index of 0.681 (vs. SNNTrans 0.662, +1.9%) and WSI-only C-index of 0.690 (vs. CLAM-MB 0.682, +0.8%). Under missing-genomics conditions, SlotSPE retains an overall C-index of 0.704, still exceeding the full-modality LD-CVAE (0.692). SlotSPE ranks first or second in 8 out of 10 cohorts in the multimodal setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SlotSPE (multimodal) achieves the best overall C-index (0.721) across all ten TCGA cohorts, surpassing the closest competitors MOTCat (0.692) and LD-CVAE (0.692) by 2.9%, and CMTA (0.691) by 3.0%. Among prototype-based methods, PIBD (0.676) and MMP (0.669) underperform SlotSPE by 4.5% and 5.2% respectively, confirming the limitation of fixed prototypes versus patient-specific slots. In the genomic-only setting, SlotSPE (0.681) outperforms SNNTrans (0.662), SNN (0.656), and MLP (0.653); in the histology-only setting, it leads CLAM-MB (0.682), ABMIL (0.681), and TransMIL (0.671). Under missing-genomics conditions, SlotSPE (0.704) still exceeds full-modality LD-CVAE (0.692) and substantially outperforms MOTCat (0.619) and CMTA (0.603) which degrade severely without genomic input. SlotSPE wins or ties for first place in 8 out of 10 cohorts in the multimodal setting, with the largest margins in BRCA (+6.2% over CMTA) and UCEC (+1.1% over MCAT).

## Relation to Prior Work

SlotSPE repurposes slot attention (originally developed for object-centric representation learning in vision) as a compression mechanism for multimodal cancer survival data, framing latent prognostic events as dynamically instantiated, patient-specific slots rather than the fixed prototypes used by PIBD and MMP. The selective slot activation module adapts the Mixture-of-Experts gating paradigm to sparsely activate the most predictive slots per patient, while the cross-modal reconstruction task encodes a biological prior (molecular events manifest as histopathological phenotypes) that is absent in prior co-attention methods like MCAT, MOTCat, and CMTA. Conceptually, the work draws on factorial coding (Schmidhuber, 1992) to justify decomposing high-dimensional inputs into a small set of disentangled latent factors, positioning itself as a structured alternative to the quadratic-complexity instance-level interactions dominant in the multimodal survival literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in multimodal cancer survival: the quadratic scaling of instance-level co-attention over millions of WSI patches and thousands of genes. By compressing to a small number of slots, SlotSPE achieves the best C-index across ten TCGA cohorts while using less inference memory and runtime than competing methods, and it retains strong performance when genomic data are missing—a common clinical scenario. Open questions include whether the learned slots correspond to genuinely distinct biological events or merely statistical regularities, whether the approach generalizes beyond TCGA to prospective clinical cohorts, and how the framework extends to additional modalities (e.g., spatial transcriptomics, clinical metadata). The interpretability analyses are promising but remain illustrative rather than validated against ground-truth biological annotations.
