## Problem

AdamW has remained the dominant optimizer for transformer pretraining despite years of search for alternatives, largely because existing improvements (Lion, SOAP, ADOPT, etc.) require non-trivial hyperparameter tuning that limits adoption. A fundamental issue with all momentum-based optimizers is that the proposed update direction can be misaligned with the current gradient, causing temporary loss increases and oscillations that slow convergence. The paper asks whether this misalignment can be corrected with a minimal, drop-in modification that preserves the base optimizer's hyperparameters.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the same inputs as any momentum-based optimizer: the current parameter vector w, the gradient g = ∇L(w), and the optimizer's internal state (first and second moment estimates for Adam, or momentum buffer for Lion). It produces an updated parameter vector w' by applying an element-wise sign-alignment mask to the base optimizer's proposed update direction u, then rescaling by a factor d/(nnz(mask)+1) to compensate for the reduced update magnitude. The entire modification is a single line of PyTorch code inserted between the update computation and the parameter update step, requiring no changes to the optimizer's state management or hyperparameters.

## Method

The core mechanism is a sign-alignment mask: for each coordinate, if the proposed update u_t and the gradient g_t have opposite signs, that coordinate's update is zeroed out; otherwise it is retained and rescaled by α = d/(nnz+1) to preserve overall update magnitude. This ensures the new update has a non-negative inner product with the gradient, guaranteeing monotonic loss decrease at small step sizes. The theoretical contribution frames this within a Hamiltonian+Descent framework: the paper proves that the cautious modification preserves the original optimizer's Lyapunov (Hamiltonian) function while additionally making the loss itself a valid Lyapunov function, so both H and L decrease simultaneously. In discrete time, a comparison theorem shows each cautious step achieves at least as much local loss decrease as the base optimizer under mild smoothness conditions. The approach is optimizer-agnostic, yielding C-AdamW, C-Lion, C-MARS, C-LaProp, and C-Muon as direct instantiations, and empirically delivers consistent perplexity improvements on LLM pretraining (130M–1.2B scale) and Top-1 accuracy gains on Mini-ImageNet with no additional hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

On 100M LLaMA pretraining (C4, 50B tokens), C-AdamW achieves 18.684 perplexity at lr=1e-2 versus 18.965 for AdamW, and C-Lion achieves 19.795 at lr=3e-4 versus 21.401 for Lion. On Mini-ImageNet with ViT, the cautious variants consistently outperform their bases: C-AdamW 73.52% vs AdamW 72.11%, C-LaProp 73.92% vs LaProp 71.73%, and C-MARS 74.91% vs MARS 74.06% Top-1 accuracy. Across FineWeb-Edu scaling (130M–1.2B), C-AdamW improves perplexity by 0.10–1.00% over AdamW at 1× Chinchilla compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-AdamW consistently outperforms AdamW across all LLM pretraining settings: on 100M C4 it reduces perplexity from 18.965 to 18.684 at lr=1e-2, and on FineWeb-Edu scaling it improves by up to 1.00% (520M: 14.92 vs 15.07). C-Lion beats Lion by a wide margin (19.795 vs 21.401 perplexity at lr=3e-4 on 100M C4) and additionally tolerates higher learning rates where Lion diverges. On Mini-ImageNet, C-LaProp improves over LaProp by 2.19 points (73.92% vs 71.73%) and C-MARS over MARS by 0.85 points (74.91% vs 74.06%), confirming the one-line modification generalizes across optimizer families. On downstream evaluation of 1.2B checkpoints, C-AdamW wins 5 of 7 tasks (MMLU, OpenBookQA, Arc Easy, HellaSwag, Arc Challenge) over AdamW, with the largest gain on Arc Challenge (+1.03 points).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Hamiltonian+Descent framework for momentum-based optimizers (Sutskever et al., 2013; Chen et al., 2023a), which provides Lyapunov functions certifying convergence for algorithms like Adam, Lion, and Polyak/Nesterov momentum. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an element-wise sign-alignment mask that zeros out update components where the proposed direction conflicts with the current gradient — a simple modification that, surprisingly, preserves the original Hamiltonian structure while additionally guaranteeing monotonic loss decrease. Unlike recent optimizer proposals (Lion, SHAMPOO, SOAP, ADOPT) that require substantial hyperparameter retuning, the cautious variant is designed as a drop-in wrapper around any existing momentum-based optimizer without altering its optimal hyperparameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the extreme simplicity (one line of PyTorch code) combined with broad applicability across all momentum-based optimizers and no need for hyperparameter retuning, making it a low-risk, low-effort improvement for practitioners. The theoretical contribution — showing the mask preserves the Hamiltonian and convergence guarantees while adding monotonic loss decrease — provides a principled foundation rather than a heuristic trick. Open questions include whether single-step comparison results extend to multi-step trajectories (the authors acknowledge this is challenging due to the no-free-lunch theorem), whether masking in eigenspace rather than parameter space could yield further gains, and whether the approach transfers to reinforcement learning and continuous learning settings. The modest but consistent improvements (0.1–1% perplexity, 1–2% accuracy) across scales and tasks suggest the technique is robust but not transformative.
