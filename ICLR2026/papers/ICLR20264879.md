## Problem

Diffusion-based decision-making methods (planners and policies) typically model observed state-action trajectories without accounting for latent factors that evolve over time and govern transitions, rewards, and high-level behavior. This omission leads to suboptimal decisions in partially observable or non-stationary environments, yet prior latent-augmented approaches require full trajectory access or multi-environment data, making them incompatible with scalable generative models. The paper asks: what is the minimum set of observations needed to identify such latent factors, and how can this identification be integrated into diffusion-based planning and control?

## Input / Output

The method consumes state-action trajectories, state-only sequences, or pixel observations (encoded via a VAE), optionally conditioned on guidance signals such as reward or goal specifications. It outputs planned state-action or state-only trajectories (planning), multi-step or single-step actions (policy learning), and inferred latent context sequences at each time step. A distinctive aspect is that latent identification operates on minimal temporal blocks of as few as four consecutive observations, and the framework also handles action-free demonstrations where actions themselves are treated as latent variables to be inferred via a learned inverse dynamics model.

## Method

Ada-Diffuser is a two-stage causal diffusion framework. Stage 1 performs block-wise latent identification via a variational autoencoder optimizing an ELBO over short temporal windows, grounded in a theoretical result (Theorem 1) proving that four consecutive observations suffice to recover the latent posterior up to an invertible transformation under mild injectivity and spectral-separability assumptions. Stage 2 is a causal diffusion model that uses an autoregressive denoising schedule with monotonically increasing noise levels per time step to reflect temporal structure, combined with a denoise-and-refine mechanism that alternates between denoising observations and updating latent estimates using a contrastive improvement loss (comparing prior- vs. posterior-conditioned reconstructions). At inference, a zig-zag sampling scheme alternates between denoising state-action pairs and refining latent variables conditioned on partially denoised neighbors, enabling online latent inference without access to future observations. The unified framework supports planning, policy learning, and latent-action inference by simply changing what trajectory components are generated and what conditioning is applied.

## Results

On the four main locomotion settings with latent factors affecting dynamics (c^s) or rewards (c^r), Ada-Diffuser-Planner achieves normalized rewards of -68.9 (Cheetah-Wind-E), -73.5 (Cheetah-Wind-S), -45.8 (Cheetah-Vel-E), and 285.3 (Ant-Dir-E), consistently outperforming all baselines by 13–19 points over the strongest competitor (DF+DynaMITE). On the RoboMimic action-free planning benchmark, Ada-Diffuser improves success rates over LDP across all tasks. On LIBERO-10 (policy learning), Ada-Diffuser-Policy achieves 93.4% success rate, outperforming the no-latent variant (89.3%) and the frozen-latent variant (90.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ada-Diffuser outperforms all 10 baselines across the 23 evaluated settings. The closest competitors are DF+DynaMITE and MetaDiffuser, which integrate latent context modules into diffusion planners; Ada-Diffuser beats them by 13–19 reward points on the four main locomotion tasks (e.g., -68.9 vs. -82.3 on Cheetah-Wind-E, 285.3 vs. 266.7 on Ant-Dir-E). Vanilla Diffuser and DD are consistently the weakest planners, with Diffuser scoring as low as -148.5 on Cheetah-Wind-S. DF (autoregressive diffusion forcing) improves over Diffuser but still trails Ada-Diffuser by 10–25 points, confirming that the latent identification and denoise-and-refine mechanism provides gains beyond autoregressive scheduling alone. DF+LILAC is outperformed on all four settings, with the gap largest on Cheetah-Wind-S (-73.5 vs. -96.7). On the RoboMimic action-free benchmark, Ada-Diffuser improves over LDP (Xie et al., 2025) on all tasks by additionally modeling latent context alongside latent actions. On LIBERO-10 policy learning, Ada-Diffuser-Policy (93.4%) surpasses the no-latent ablation (89.3%) and frozen-latent variant (90.2%), while DP and IDQL backbones are reported as consistently worse than the DF backbone in Appendix Table A4.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ada-Diffuser sits at the intersection of diffusion-based decision-making (Diffuser, Diffusion Policy) and latent-variable identification in POMDPs. It builds directly on DDPM's forward-reverse diffusion framework and on recent autoregressive diffusion models (Diffusion Forcing, AR-Diffusion) for the causal denoising schedule, but departs from prior latent-context methods (DynaMITE, LILAC, MetaDiffuser) by providing a non-parametric identifiability theorem showing that four consecutive observations suffice to recover the latent posterior, rather than relying on multi-environment data or full-trajectory belief states. The denoise-and-refine training procedure and zig-zag inference are novel algorithmic contributions that couple latent inference with the diffusion denoising process in a way not present in prior latent-augmented diffusion planners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identifiability theorem gives a principled, minimal-sufficiency guarantee for latent inference in sequential decision-making, which prior diffusion-based planners lacked entirely. The unified framework's ability to handle planning, policy learning, and action-free demonstrations under one architecture, while also capturing implicit stochasticity even in environments without designed latents, makes it broadly applicable. Key limitations include the absence of real-world robotic deployment, reliance on assumptions (distributional variability, spectral uniqueness) that may be hard to verify in high-dimensional continuous settings, and the zig-zag sampling procedure adding inference latency. Open questions include whether the four-step identifiability result extends to higher-order or non-Markov latent processes, and how the framework scales to very high-dimensional latent spaces in complex visual environments.
