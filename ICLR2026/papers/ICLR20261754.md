## Problem

MLLM-based misinformation detection faces a critical trade-off: single-agent methods offer only limited single-view analysis and are easily misled by socially complex forgeries, while multi-agent methods capture diverse perspectives but incur high computational costs and resist end-to-end optimization. Additionally, existing fine-tuning algorithms (SFT, DPO) treat all training samples uniformly, failing to prioritize the high-value samples that expose genuine social cognitive gaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261754/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multimodal sample (RGB image paired with a textual claim) under an open-prompting setting where the specific misinformation type is not disclosed, simulating realistic deployment. It produces a veracity verdict (fake/real) accompanied by a structured Social Chain-of-Thought (SCoT) reasoning trace that explicitly integrates multiple role-specific analytical perspectives. Evaluation spans MFC-Bench (35K samples across Manipulation, OOC, and Veracity subtasks) and MMFakeBench (12 sub-categories of mixed-source misinformation), with predictions parsed via a separate LLM for multi-label tasks.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, a multi-agent SCoT generation pipeline simulates a diverse society of MLLM agents (instantiated from OASIS user profiles with demographic, professional, and interest attributes), performs topic-driven weighted sampling to select contextually relevant users, collects their individual judgments, then synthesizes preference pairs via LLM-as-a-Judge classification into correct/partially-correct/incorrect sets, role-specific reasoning augmentation with ground-truth injection, and Coordinator/Summarizer agents that produce unified positive and representative negative SCoTs. Second, SCPO (Social Correction Value-Driven Preference Optimization) introduces a verifiable social correction value sc(x) = 1 − (N_C/N + N_P/(N−N_C)·1/N) that quantifies the degree of social misjudgment per sample, and uses it to dynamically scale the ORPO odds-ratio alignment loss as (1 + ω·sc(x))·L_OR, so that samples with more collective misjudgment receive amplified preference gradients while trivially correct samples are downweighted, all within a unified SFT + preference-optimization objective on a 7B Qwen2-VL backbone.

## Results

On MFC-Bench (open-prompting), SCPO (7B Qwen2-VL) achieves 67.15% overall accuracy and 66.83% macro F1, a ~10-point improvement over its base model Qwen2-VL (57.24% / 56.91%). On MMFakeBench under open prompting, SCPO reaches 59.2% accuracy (Top-1) and 66.0% accuracy (Recall), substantially outperforming the MMD-Agent framework applied to a 34B LLaVA-NeXT (40.5% accuracy). In the ablation (Table 3), SCPO outperforms ORPO (66.30%) and SFT (64.20%) using identical SCoT data, confirming the benefit of the social correction value-driven scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCPO (7B) outperforms all open-source MLLM baselines on MFC-Bench, including larger models such as InternVL 25.5B (55.03%), CogVLM 17B (58.47%), and LLaVA-NeXT 13B (58.77%), as well as same-size or stronger models like Qwen2.5-VL 7B (58.23%) and InternVL3 8B (56.32%). Against proprietary models, SCPO surpasses Claude3.5-Sonnet (67.15% vs. 66.85% overall accuracy) and narrows the gap to GPT-4o to just ~2 points (67.15% vs. 69.11%). On MMFakeBench, SCPO under open prompting (59.2% accuracy, Top-1) substantially exceeds the MMD-Agent framework applied to a 34B LLaVA-NeXT (40.5% accuracy) and GPT-4V with MMD-Agent (59.1% accuracy under closed prompting), demonstrating that self-distilled social reasoning is more effective than inference-time multi-agent decomposition. In the controlled ablation (Table 3), SCPO beats ORPO (67.15% vs. 66.30%) and SFT (67.15% vs. 64.20%) with identical SCoT data, while SFT+DPO actually degrades below the base model (57.98%), highlighting the importance of the social correction value signal over generic preference margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of MLLM-based misinformation detection and preference optimization for alignment. It builds directly on ORPO's unified SFT+preference-loss framework, extending it with a social correction value that dynamically reweights the odds-ratio loss based on observable multi-agent disagreement statistics. Conceptually, it departs from the multi-agent MMD literature (MMD-Agent, DELL, Let Silence Speak) by moving multi-perspective reasoning from inference time into training-time self-distillation, and from DPO/ORPO variants by replacing learned or implicit reward margins with a verifiable, interpretable social signal. The data pipeline draws on OASIS-style social simulation and LLM-as-a-Judge classification, repurposing agent-simulation infrastructure originally designed for social science modeling into a training-data generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to deploying strong MMD reasoning in a single 7B model without the inference-time cost of multi-agent orchestration, which is significant for real-world deployment where latency and compute matter. However, the SCPO-specific gain over plain ORPO is modest (~0.85% accuracy), suggesting the social correction value, while interpretable, may not be the dominant driver of performance—most of the improvement comes from the SCoT data quality itself. Open questions include whether the social simulation (built on OASIS profiles) captures genuine cognitive diversity or merely surface-level role-playing, whether the approach generalizes to misinformation types outside the two evaluated benchmarks, and whether the verifiability advantage of sc(x) over learned reward margins holds at scale with more diverse agent populations.
