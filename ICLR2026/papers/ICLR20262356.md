## Problem

Existing trainable sparse attention methods like NSA introduce multiple extra KV projection sets, gating modules, and compressed-attention outputs that create an architectural mismatch with the standard pretrain-on-short, finetune-on-long workflow. This mismatch causes disruptive loss fluctuations during adaptation, erases previously learned representations, and imposes unnecessary computational overhead on short-sequence processing, making it difficult to achieve both seamless long-context adaptation and practical inference speedup.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262356/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard GQA query, key, and value tensors from a pretrained dense-attention model and produces a single attention output (no multi-branch gating). For short sequences, it operates identically to dense attention; for long sequences (e.g., 32k+), each query token attends to only a fixed budget of visible tokens (|I|·B = 6k out of 32k) selected via a 3-stage block-scoring pipeline. The output is a standard attention tensor compatible with downstream FFN layers, supporting both prefilling and autoregressive decoding without architectural changes.

## Method

InfLLM-V2 reuses the pretrained dense attention's KV projection parameters directly for sparse attention, introducing zero extra parameters and eliminating NSA's three separate KV sets, gating MLP, and compressed-attention output. It merges Selected Attention and Sliding Attention into a single unified Sparse Attention module by expanding the local-block window to fully cover the sliding window, and retains only the compressed attention's scores (not its output) for block selection. Block importance is computed via a parameter-free 3-stage coarse-to-fine compression (mean-pooling → head-group summation → max-pooling) that avoids the gradient-free MLP bottleneck of NSA. For efficient block selection, the paper introduces Fused Head Group Summation with LSE Approximation: a two-pass SRAM-resident kernel that computes log-sum-exp statistics from a coarser-grained second-stage score, reducing the HBM write volume by a factor of G (the GQA group size) and cutting the two-pass overhead from 2× to 1.25×. The resulting model dynamically switches between dense and sparse modes based on input length, enabling seamless short-to-long adaptation with minimal finetuning.

## Results

On RULER (32k context), InfLLM-V2 (Sparse) achieves an average score of 82.62, closely matching the FULLATTN baseline (84.26) while substantially outperforming NSA (59.92), MInference (73.22), and InfLLM (27.94). On LongBench, InfLLM-V2 (Sparse) scores 42.54 (vs. FULLATTN 42.30) and on LongPPL achieves 2.12 (vs. FULLATTN 2.06), confirming near-lossless long-context understanding. For long chain-of-thought reasoning, InfLLM-V2 (Sparse) attains 42.66 average across MATH-500, AIME, and LiveCodeBench, matching FULLATTN (42.79) and surpassing NSA (37.28). The method achieves 4× speedup over dense attention while retaining 98.1% (long-context) and 99.7% (CoT reasoning) of original performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfLLM-V2 (Sparse) decisively outperforms NSA across all benchmarks: RULER average 82.62 vs. 59.92, LongBench 42.54 vs. 37.10, LongPPL 2.12 vs. 4.24, and long reasoning 42.66 vs. 37.28, demonstrating that NSA's three separate KV projections and gating module cause severe disruption in the short-to-long adaptation workflow. Against MInference (training-free dynamic sparse attention), InfLLM-V2 leads on RULER (82.62 vs. 73.22) and LongPPL (2.12 vs. 2.62), with a marginal edge on LongBench (42.54 vs. 41.55). The training-free InfLLM baseline collapses dramatically on multi-hop retrieval tasks (RULER avg. 27.94), confirming that trainable sparse attention is necessary for strong long-context performance. Compared to the SHORT+YARN baseline (context extension without retraining), InfLLM-V2 achieves a 42-point RULER improvement (82.62 vs. 40.63). Notably, InfLLM-V2 (Dense mode) actually surpasses the FULLATTN baseline on RULER (88.32 vs. 84.26) and LongBench (42.49 vs. 42.30), suggesting that the long-sequence finetuning with the switchable architecture provides a net benefit even when running in dense mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfLLM-V2 builds directly on InfLLM (Xiao et al., 2024a), a training-free block-sparse attention mechanism, and positions itself as a trainable successor that addresses the architectural mismatch introduced by NSA (Yuan et al., 2025). The key departure from NSA is the elimination of its three separate KV projection sets, three attention modules, and gating mechanism in favor of a single shared KV projection and a unified Sparse Attention that merges Selected and Sliding patterns while using Compressed Attention scores only for block selection. The efficient implementation draws on FlashAttention's SRAM-based tiling and online-softmax, extended with a novel Fused Head Group Summation and LSE Approximation to avoid materializing full attention score matrices to HBM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 4× inference speedup over dense attention while retaining 98–99% of performance, combined with the ability to seamlessly switch back to dense mode for short sequences, makes this a drop-in solution for the standard pretrain-on-short, finetune-on-long pipeline. The finding that NSA's multi-module architecture actively disrupts loss during short-to-long adaptation (despite low training loss) is a useful negative result that challenges the assumption that more complex trainable sparse designs are necessary. Open questions include whether the approach extends to accelerating FFN layers, whether the two-pass LSE Approximation can be further optimized, and whether the dense-sparse switchability generalizes to even longer contexts beyond 32k tokens.
