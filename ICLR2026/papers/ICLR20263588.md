## Problem

Fine-grained cross-view localization must estimate a ground camera's 3-DoF pose from a single ground image and a geo-referenced aerial image, but the extreme viewpoint gap makes reliable local feature matching difficult and no pixel-level ground-truth correspondences exist for supervision. Prior approaches either rely on global descriptors (offering no interpretability) or warp ground images into BEV before matching, which introduces ray-directional distortions and discards height information, degrading performance especially under unknown camera orientation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a ground-level RGB image, a geo-referenced aerial image (providing meters-per-pixel metric information), and a monocular depth map (either metric from models like UniK3D/DepthAnythingV2, or relative from BiFuse++/UniFuse). It outputs a 3-DoF camera pose (2D planar location in meters and yaw in radians), a depth scale factor converting relative depth to metric space, and N=1024 weighted local feature correspondences between the two views. The aerial feature map is sampled on a fixed 41×41 grid regardless of input resolution, and a maximum depth threshold (35 m for VIGOR, 40 m for KITTI) filters sky and distant objects.

## Method

Loc² establishes correspondences directly in the image plane using two symmetric branches, each pairing a frozen DINOv2 backbone with a lightweight projection head (convolutional layers + self-attention), followed by cosine-similarity matching with a learnable dustbin and dual-softmax normalization to produce a match probability matrix. The key architectural insight is to avoid BEV warping of the ground image, which would introduce ray-directional distortions and discard height information; instead, matched ground points are lifted into BEV space using off-the-shelf monocular depth predictions. Pose estimation then proceeds via a fully differentiable 2D scale-aware Procrustes alignment (SVD-based) that jointly recovers rotation, translation, and the unknown depth scale factor from the weighted point correspondences. Training is end-to-end and requires only 3-DoF camera pose labels: a Virtual Correspondence Error (VCE) loss supervises the pose, while infoNCE losses (G2S and S2G) encourage correct correspondences by leveraging ground-truth pose to identify positive matches, with no pixel-level annotations needed.

## Results

On VIGOR with unknown orientation, Loc² achieves 4.23 m mean / 2.09 m median localization error (cross-area) and 3.94 m / 1.78 m (same-area), setting new state-of-the-art in both localization and orientation (11.67° / 9.54° mean). On KITTI cross-area, it reduces mean localization error to 5.60 m (±10°) and 11.71 m (±180°), outperforming all prior methods. Under known orientation on VIGOR, FG² retains a slight edge (2.41 m vs. 3.43 m cross-area), but Loc²'s advantage is most pronounced in the harder unknown-orientation and cross-area settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FG² (Xia & Alahi, CVPR 2025), the closest prior method that also estimates local correspondences, Loc² wins decisively under unknown orientation: 4.23 m vs. 10.02 m mean localization on VIGOR cross-area, and 3.94 m vs. 8.95 m on same-area. Under known orientation, FG² retains a 1.0 m advantage on VIGOR cross-area (2.41 m vs. 3.43 m), reflecting its ability to exploit the orientation prior. Against CCVPE (Xia et al., TPAMI 2023), the leading global-descriptor method, Loc² reduces KITTI cross-area ±180° mean error from 13.94 m to 11.71 m and VIGOR cross-area unknown-orientation error from 5.41 m to 4.23 m. Against geometry-transformation baselines (DenseFlow, HC-Net, GGCVT), Loc² achieves the best cross-area results on both datasets, e.g., 5.60 m vs. 7.97 m (DenseFlow) and 8.47 m (HC-Net) on KITTI cross-area ±10°. On KITTI same-area ±10°, HC-Net (0.80 m) and FG² (0.75 m) slightly outperform Loc² (1.13 m), but under the harder ±180° setting Loc² dominates with 1.85 m vs. 6.88 m (CCVPE) and 7.96 m (SliceMatch). SliceMatch (Lentsch et al., CVPR 2023) is consistently outperformed across all cross-area settings on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FG² (Xia & Alahi, 2025), which first demonstrated that local feature correspondences between ground and aerial images are feasible, but departs from it by performing matching in the image plane rather than in BEV space, thereby avoiding the ray-directional distortions and height-dimension information loss inherent to BEV warping. It also contrasts with the dominant paradigms in cross-view localization—global descriptor matching (CCVPE, SliceMatch) and BEV-to-aerial alignment (HC-Net, DenseFlow, GGCVT)—by learning image-plane correspondences under weak pose supervision and recovering the 3-DoF pose analytically via scale-aware Procrustes alignment (Umeyama, 1991) applied to depth-lifted points. The use of off-the-shelf monocular depth predictors (UniK3D, DepthAnythingV2, BiFuse++) as a geometric prior to bridge 2D correspondences into metric 3D space is a key architectural choice that distinguishes this work from prior geometry-transformation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interpretability of the method—where correspondence quality directly reflects localization accuracy, RANSAC inlier counts serve as a confidence signal, and the re-scaled ground layout can be visually overlaid on the aerial image—addresses a long-standing limitation of cross-view localization where global descriptors or BEV features offer no per-object explanation. The scale-aware Procrustes formulation enables practical deployment with relative depth predictors at inference (less than 0.2 m degradation), broadening applicability to settings where metric depth is unavailable. Limitations include weaker orientation estimation on KITTI (the analytic pose cannot exploit the road-direction prior) and persistent failures in dense vegetation or heavily occluded scenes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 6-DoF pose, whether it generalizes to non-orthorectified aerial imagery, and whether the DINOv2 backbone can be replaced by lighter alternatives without sacrificing cross-view matching quality.
