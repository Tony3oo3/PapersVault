## Problem

Direct encoding, the mainstream input method for efficient SNN training, injects identical inputs across all time steps, making SNNs surprisingly less robust to adversarial perturbations than the older rate encoding. The paper identifies and formalizes the underlying mechanism—Gradient Temporal Collinearity (GTC)—showing that high directional alignment of gradient components across time steps amplifies the Hessian spectral radius and degrades robustness, and then proposes a structured method to break this collinearity without sacrificing direct encoding's efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260603/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts static RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) or DVS event frames (128×128 for DVS-CIFAR10) and outputs a class label. Unusually, the input is not fed identically at each of the T time steps as in standard direct encoding; instead, it is partitioned into non-overlapping p×p patches, and each time step t receives a patch-wise transformation by a dedicated parametric orthogonal kernel O[t] ∈ R^{d×d} (where d = C·p²), so the SNN sees T diverse but energy-preserving views of the same image. At inference, the orthogonal kernels can optionally be removed entirely, incurring zero extra overhead.

## Method

STOD (Structured Temporal Orthogonal Decorrelation) introduces two components into the input layer of direct-encoding SNNs. First, Patchwise Feature Diversification (PFD) partitions the input into N non-overlapping patches and applies a distinct parametric orthogonal kernel at each time step, initialized via Householder reflections to be mutually orthogonal (with O[1]=I as a stable anchor) and constrained to the Stiefel manifold during training via Riemannian SGD, ensuring L2-norm conservation and preventing pixel-intensity drift. Second, Global Orthogonal Regularization (GOR) adds a soft penalty on the squared cosine similarity between normalized transformed inputs across time steps, encouraging persistent inter-step diversity without the prohibitive cost of hard mutual-orthogonality constraints. The theoretical contribution is a structured upper bound on the Hessian spectral radius showing that λ_max(H) scales with T·(1+(T−1)·max GTC), so reducing GTC directly flattens the loss landscape and improves adversarial robustness. The method is trained with standard BPTT plus surrogate gradients and an optional adversarial training (AT) component, and is evaluated under FGSM, PGD, black-box, and RGA attacks.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, STOD w.o. OK achieves 55.80% FGSM accuracy and 32.97% PGD accuracy (ε=8/255), outperforming all SOTA baselines under both attacks. On CIFAR-100, it reaches 26.26% FGSM and 13.13% PGD, and on ImageNet it attains 19.08% FGSM and 6.44% PGD. Clean accuracy is slightly lower than the vanilla SNN (e.g., 91.43% vs. 93.75% on CIFAR-10), reflecting the trade-off of structured temporal decorrelation, but the robustness gains are substantial—up to 47.6 points over the untrained SNN under PGD on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STOD w.o. OK achieves the highest FGSM and PGD accuracy across all three static-image datasets, beating every SOTA baseline. On CIFAR-10, it surpasses the closest FGSM competitor HoSNN (54.76%) by 1.04 points and the closest PGD competitor FEEL (28.35%) by 4.62 points. On CIFAR-100, the margin is larger: STOD leads AT (16.31% FGSM / 8.49% PGD) by 9.95 and 4.64 points respectively, and outperforms FEEL (9.60% FGSM / 2.04% PGD) by 16.66 and 11.09 points. On ImageNet, STOD edges out AT (15.74% FGSM / 6.39% PGD) by 3.34 and 0.05 points. The clean-accuracy cost is modest (1.3–3.8 points below the best baselines), and retaining the orthogonal kernels at inference (STOD) further boosts FGSM/PGD by 1.9–5.9 points at the cost of only 0.15M extra parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper addresses the well-documented robustness gap between rate and direct encoding in SNNs (Kundu et al., 2021; Kim et al., 2022; Mukhoty et al., 2025), but departs from prior robustness methods that constrain neuronal dynamics (DLIF, HoSNN, StoG) or apply adversarial/gradient regularization (AT, FEEL, SR) by instead targeting the input encoding layer directly. Conceptually, it repurposes orthogonal parameterization tools from the RNN literature (Householder reflections, Stiefel manifold constraints, Riemannian SGD) for a novel purpose: structurally diversifying temporal inputs in direct encoding. The theoretical contribution draws on the Hessian spectral radius–robustness connection (Stutz et al., 2021) and the BPTT gradient decomposition specific to SNNs, synthesizing these into the new GTC metric that unifies the explanation of the encoding-method robustness gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GTC framework provides the first principled, quantitative explanation for why direct encoding is less robust than rate encoding, moving the discussion beyond empirical observation to a geometric property of gradient structure. Practically, STOD achieves state-of-the-art robustness with negligible overhead (0.15M extra parameters, removable at inference), making it deployable in safety-critical neuromorphic applications. The acknowledged limitation—a small but consistent clean-accuracy drop—remains an open trade-off shared across the SNN robustness literature. Future work could explore whether the GTC lens generalizes to other temporal architectures (e.g., recurrent ANNs, transformers with temporal attention) or whether adaptive, input-dependent kernel selection could recover the clean-accuracy gap.
