## Problem

Calibration-free structured compression (pruning and folding) reduces model size without training data, but no unified theoretical framework explains why folding often outperforms magnitude pruning in practice. The paper asks: can both be cast as orthogonal projections in parameter space, and can we prove that folding yields smaller reconstruction error and tighter functional-perturbation bounds under mild smoothness assumptions?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262725/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The compression operator acts on the weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×p} of a single layer in a trained network, producing a compressed matrix with k retained rows (k < m) without requiring any calibration data. For vision models, the compressed network still maps RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10 or 224×224 ImageNet-1K) to class labels; for LLaMA-60M/130M, it maps text token sequences to next-token distributions evaluated by perplexity on C4. Notably, for ViTs and LLaMA models only FFN blocks are compressed while attention layers remain untouched, and for CNNs a single-pass BatchNorm re-estimation (REPAIR) is applied post-compression to isolate structural effects.

## Method

The paper formalizes structured pruning as an axis-aligned orthogonal projection (zeroing selected rows of W) and model folding as a cluster-structured orthogonal projection (replacing each cluster of rows by its mean via k-means). Theorem 2.1 proves that for any pruning of rank k_p, a specific folding of rank k_p+1 (merging all pruned rows into one extra cluster) achieves strictly smaller Frobenius-norm reconstruction error; Theorem 2.2 further shows that optimal k-means folding minimizes this error over all clusterings, yielding the chain ‖W−W_p‖² ≥ ‖W−W_f′‖² ≥ ‖W−W_f⋆‖². Under a Lipschitz-continuity assumption on the loss, this parameter-space advantage translates into a tighter bound on functional perturbation. Empirically, the framework is validated across >1,000 checkpoints (ResNet18, PreActResNet18, ViT-B/32, CLIP ViT-B/32 on CIFAR-10/ImageNet-1K, and LLaMA-60M/130M on C4) under diverse optimizers, learning rates, SAM, augmentation, and regularization, with ablations showing folding's advantage is largest at moderate-high compression and under training conditions that produce flatter, more structured solutions.

## Results

Across >1,000 checkpoints (ResNet18, PreActResNet18, ViT-B/32, CLIP ViT-B/32 on CIFAR-10/ImageNet-1K, and LLaMA-60M/130M on C4), model folding (FOLD) consistently achieves higher post-compression accuracy than magnitude-based structured pruning (MAG1/MAG2), with the largest gains at moderate-to-high layer-wise compression (40–70%). On LLaMA-60M at 50% FFN sparsity, FOLD reduces perplexity from 398.62 to 221.32 (wd=0.01, warmup=880, lr=0.001) and from 564.96 to 234.56 (wd=0.01, warmup=1100, lr=0.005), compared to MAG2. On LLaMA-130M at 50% sparsity, FOLD achieves PPL of 222.21 vs. MAG2's 589.33 (wd=0.01, warmup=3300, lr=0.005). The advantage persists after lightweight LayerNorm reset (ViTs) and 1–5 epochs of full fine-tuning, with folded models recovering accuracy faster and reaching higher final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole experimental baseline is magnitude-based structured pruning, evaluated with both L1 (MAG1) and L2 (MAG2) scoring criteria. FOLD wins on post-compression accuracy in the large majority of checkpoints across all architectures: on ResNet18 (Adam, CIFAR-10), the median accuracy gap Δ = Acc(FOLD) − Acc(MAG1) is positive and grows with compression, peaking around 50–70% layer-wise sparsity. On LLaMA-60M at 50% FFN sparsity, FOLD achieves lower perplexity than MAG2 in 16 of 18 hyperparameter settings (e.g., 221.32 vs. 398.62 at lr=0.001; 234.56 vs. 564.96 at lr=0.005); the two exceptions involve very low learning rates (0.001) combined with long warmup (2200 steps), where MAG2's axis-aligned removal happens to align with the naturally sparse weights. A similar pattern holds for LLaMA-130M (Table 6), where FOLD dominates at higher learning rates (e.g., 222.21 vs. 589.33 PPL at 50% sparsity, lr=0.005, warmup=3300). The gap narrows or occasionally reverses at very low compression (<20%) and under strong data augmentation for ResNets, but FOLD retains a consistent advantage after 1–5 epochs of full fine-tuning, recovering accuracy faster than pruned models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Wang et al. (2025), which introduced model folding as a calibration-free compression operator, and on the extensive structured pruning literature (Han et al., 2015; Li et al., 2016; Luo et al., 2017). The key conceptual departure is a unified projection-geometric framework that casts both pruning (axis-aligned coordinate projection) and folding (cluster-structured projection) as orthogonal operators in parameter space, enabling a clean theoretical comparison. Prior work treated these methods as separate heuristics; here, the authors prove that folding provably achieves smaller parameter reconstruction error than pruning at a one-rank slack, and that optimal k-means folding minimizes projection error over all cluster-structured subspaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a principled, geometry-based explanation for when and why folding outperforms pruning, validated across >1,000 checkpoints spanning CNNs, ViTs, and small LLaMA models under diverse training hyperparameters. The sharpness analysis (Appendix F) offers a mechanistic link: folding's advantage is largest when it induces smaller sharpness increases than pruning, which occurs under moderate learning rates and SAM training. Key limitations include the one-rank slack in the theoretical guarantee (not a strict matched-size dominance), compression applied only to FFN blocks for ViTs and LLaMAs, and evaluation on small LLMs (60M/130M) rather than production-scale models. Open questions include extending folding to attention layers, interactions with quantization and distillation, and whether the projection-geometric perspective generalizes to calibration-based compression settings.
