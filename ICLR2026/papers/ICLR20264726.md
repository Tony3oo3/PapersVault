## Problem

Fine-tuning diffusion models for biomolecular design requires optimizing non-differentiable reward functions (e.g., DSSP secondary structure, AlphaFold binding affinity, docking scores), which rules out direct gradient backpropagation. Existing RL-based approaches like DDPO/DDPP are inherently on-policy and minimize reverse KL divergence, leading to training instability, low sample efficiency, and mode collapse. The paper seeks a stable, sample-efficient fine-tuning framework that handles arbitrary non-differentiable rewards without requiring differentiable reward signals.

## Input / Output

The method takes random noise as input to the diffusion sampling process, with optional conditioning on target protein structures for binder design tasks. Outputs are discrete protein sequences (256 or 100 residues via EvoDiff), regulatory DNA sequences of length 200 (via masked discrete diffusion), or molecular graphs with up to 38 atoms (via GDSS). A key constraint is that reward evaluation relies on expensive external models (ESMFold-3B, AlphaFold2-Multimer-93M, QuickVina 2, Enformer) operating on full sequences rather than individual denoising steps, making the reward signal sequence-level and non-differentiable.

## Method

VIDD (Value-guided Iterative Distillation for Diffusion models) casts reward-guided fine-tuning as iterative policy distillation with three phases per iteration: (1) off-policy roll-in collecting trajectories from a mixture of the pre-trained and roll-out policies, (2) roll-out sampling from a lazily-updated roll-out policy and approximating soft value functions via posterior mean (argmax decoding through the denoising network, requiring only a single forward pass), and (3) distillation by minimizing forward KL divergence between the soft-optimal teacher policy and the current student policy, which reduces to a value-weighted maximum likelihood objective. The key theoretical contribution is showing that PPO-style methods implicitly minimize reverse KL (mode-seeking, prone to collapse) while VIDD's forward KL objective is mode-covering and more stable. The off-policy formulation decouples data collection from policy updates, and the lazy update of the roll-out policy every K steps prevents rapid target shifts, together enabling stable optimization over complex non-differentiable reward landscapes.

## Results

VIDD achieves the best reward optimization across all three biomolecular design tasks. On protein SS-match, VIDD reaches 0.83 β-sheet% and 0.82 pLDDT (vs. 0.81/0.55 for DDPO and 0.63/0.36 for DDPP). On DNA enhancer design (HepG2), VIDD attains 8.28 Pred-Activity and 0.820 ATAC-Acc, outperforming DRAKES (6.44/0.825) and DDPO (7.38/0.086). On small molecule docking (Parp1), VIDD achieves a docking score of 9.4 with NLL of 741, surpassing DDPO (8.5/929) and DDPP (7.9/981).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIDD outperforms DDPO (the strongest RL baseline) on every reward metric: β-sheet (0.83 vs. 0.81), pLDDT (0.82 vs. 0.55), Pred-Activity (8.28 vs. 7.38), ATAC-Acc (0.820 vs. 0.086), and docking score (9.4 vs. 8.5), while also achieving substantially lower NLL (741 vs. 929). Against DDPP, VIDD leads by wide margins on all reward metrics (e.g., Pred-Activity 8.28 vs. 5.33; docking 9.4 vs. 7.9) but sacrifices diversity (0.52 vs. 0.85) and 3-mer correlation (0.162 vs. 0.879), suggesting VIDD explores more novel sequence regions. On the DNA task where the reward is differentiable, VIDD still beats DRAKES (which backpropagates through the reward) on Pred-Activity (8.28 vs. 6.44) and matches it on ATAC-Acc (0.820 vs. 0.825), demonstrating that the non-differentiable-reward framework generalizes well even when gradients are available. Standard Fine-tuning and Best-of-N are substantially weaker on all reward metrics, confirming that naive reweighting or inference-time selection is insufficient for complex biomolecular objectives. The main trade-off is that VIDD's higher rewards come with reduced sequence diversity (0.52) compared to DDPP (0.85) and the pre-trained model (0.91), though the authors show via training curves that VIDD expands the reachable reward-diversity frontier over time.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIDD builds on the MDP framing of diffusion model fine-tuning established by DDPO (Black et al., 2024) and DPOK (Fan et al., 2023), but departs fundamentally from their on-policy PPO formulation by adopting an off-policy, iterative distillation approach rooted in value-weighted MLE (Peters et al., 2010; Peng et al., 2019). The soft-optimal policy target and posterior mean value approximation are inherited from test-time guidance methods (Li et al., 2024; Uehara et al., 2025a), but VIDD repurposes them as distillation targets for training rather than inference-time steering. The key conceptual departure is the explicit identification of PPO's implicit reverse KL objective (Theorem 1) and the substitution with a forward KL distillation loss, which the authors argue explains the mode collapse and instability observed in prior RL-based fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIDD provides a principled, stable alternative to PPO-style fine-tuning for the common biomolecular design scenario where rewards are non-differentiable (DSSP, AlphaFold2, docking scores), a setting where direct backpropagation methods like DRAKES are inapplicable. The off-policy formulation with lazy target updates offers practical advantages in sample efficiency and training stability, which is critical when reward evaluation is expensive (e.g., AlphaFold2-Multimer or ESMFold calls). Open questions include robustness to noisy or misaligned reward signals (the paper shows performance degrades under synthetic noise without explicit robustness mechanisms), multi-objective integration, and whether the lazy update schedule and roll-in mixture can be made fully adaptive rather than hand-tuned per task.
