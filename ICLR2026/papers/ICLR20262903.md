## Problem

No systematic benchmark exists for measuring MLLMs' ability to evaluate visualization aesthetics and quality, which requires simultaneous judgment of data encoding accuracy, information expressiveness, and visual design—unlike natural image aesthetics. Existing benchmarks either assess chart comprehension, natural image beauty, or NL2VIS fidelity, leaving a critical gap in comprehensive visualization quality assessment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262903/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a visualization image (single chart, multi-chart composition, or dashboard) paired with an adaptively generated evaluation prompt that is customized per chart type and visual content via GPT-4o metadata extraction and template filling. It produces structured 1-to-5 scores across six sub-dimensions (Data Fidelity, Semantic Readability, Insight Discovery, Design Style, Visual Composition, Color Harmony) along with textual rationales, with each model run three times and averaged to reduce variance.

## Method

VISJUDGE-BENCH constructs 3,090 expert-annotated samples through a three-stage pipeline (web crawling with perceptual-hash deduplication, GPT-4o-based semantic filtering and classification, and three-stage human annotation with algorithmic conflict resolution and expert validation). VISJUDGE fine-tunes four open-source MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B, InternVL3-8B, Llava-v1.6-mistral-7B) using GRPO reinforcement learning with a composite reward combining prediction accuracy and output format compliance, applied via LoRA for parameter efficiency. The training data (70% split, 2,163 samples) is kept separate from the 648-sample test set to prevent contamination. The best variant (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) reduces MAE from 0.553 to 0.421 and increases Pearson correlation from 0.428 to 0.687 relative to GPT-5, with consistent 30–40% error reductions across all backbone architectures.

## Results

On VISJUDGE-BENCH (648-sample test set), the best VISJUDGE variant (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) achieves an overall MAE of 0.421 and Pearson correlation of 0.687 with human expert ratings, outperforming the strongest closed-source model GPT-5 (MAE 0.553, Corr 0.428) by 23.9% in MAE and 60.5% in correlation. Across all six evaluation dimensions, VISJUDGE consistently leads: e.g., Fidelity MAE 0.661 vs. GPT-5's 0.862, and Design Style MAE 0.580 vs. GPT-5's 0.649. In downstream applications, VISJUDGE as a feedback model in MatPlotAgent improves generation quality by +6.07 points on average across seven generators, while base models without domain fine-tuning often degrade performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISJUDGE (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) decisively outperforms all 12 baseline MLLMs on both MAE and correlation. Against GPT-5, the strongest closed-source model, VISJUDGE reduces MAE from 0.553 to 0.421 (−23.9%) and increases correlation from 0.428 to 0.687 (+60.5%). Against GPT-4o (MAE 0.610, Corr 0.482), the improvement is even larger in absolute terms. Among open-source baselines, the best is Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct (MAE 0.702, Corr 0.440), which VISJUDGE (7B) surpasses by 40% in MAE and 56% in correlation despite being 10× smaller. Domain-specific fine-tuning yields consistent 30–40% MAE reductions across all four backbone architectures (Qwen2.5-VL-3B: −40.2%, InternVL3-8B: −31.8%, Llava-7B: −31.5%), confirming cross-architecture generalization. In the MatPlotAgent generation task, VISJUDGE as a feedback model improves all seven generators by +6.07 points on average, whereas the unfine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 72B baselines actually degrade performance (−2.39 and −0.61 average), demonstrating that systematic scoring biases in general MLLMs produce misleading feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of the MLLM-as-a-Judge paradigm and visualization design theory. Prior work falls into three non-overlapping categories: natural image aesthetic benchmarks (AVA, ArtiMuse) that ignore data communication, chart understanding benchmarks (ChartQA, ChartInsights) that assess content comprehension rather than design quality, and NL2VIS evaluation benchmarks (VisEval, VIS-Shepherd) that check whether generated charts match text queries rather than judging intrinsic quality. The paper repurposes the classical translation-theory triad of Fidelity, Expressiveness, and Aesthetics into a six-dimensional scoring rubric, and applies GRPO-based reinforcement learning with LoRA to fine-tune open-source VLMs as specialized visualization judges—a combination not previously attempted in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a clear gap: no prior resource systematically measures whether MLLMs can judge visualization quality across single charts, multi-view compositions, and dashboards simultaneously. The finding that even GPT-5 achieves only 0.428 Pearson correlation with human experts, and that a 7B fine-tuned model substantially outperforms it, suggests that domain-specific training is currently necessary rather than optional for reliable automated visualization assessment. Practical downstream validation (improving MatPlotAgent generation and HAIChart recommendation) demonstrates real utility. Key limitations include coverage of only static visualizations, absence of raw source data for true fidelity verification, and point-estimate scoring that may not capture preference diversity—leaving open questions about dynamic/interactive visualizations and distribution-based evaluation.
