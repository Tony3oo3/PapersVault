## Problem

Autoregressive image generation models flatten images into 1D token sequences and predict them in a fixed order (typically row-major raster-scan), ignoring the 2D spatial structure of images. Recent variants (VAR, xAR, RAR, PAR, FAR) propose alternative prediction units and orders, but each relies on hand-crafted inductive biases and lacks a unified mathematical framework, yielding divergent conclusions from ad hoc design choices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260915/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label (ImageNet 256×256 or 512×512) or a text prompt (JourneyDB, MS-COCO) as conditioning and produces a generated RGB image. Images are first encoded by a KL-16 VAE into a 16×16×16 continuous latent grid, which is flattened to a 256-token sequence. The key structural detail is that this sequence is multiplied by a learnable N×N orthogonal matrix A before AR prediction, and the inverse transform A⁻¹y recovers the original latent for VAE decoding.

## Method

BAR reframes AR tokens as projections of the image vector onto basis vectors in a linear space, then applies a learnable linear transform y = Ax that replaces the standard basis {e_k} with learned basis vectors {a_k}. Previous methods (VAR's coarse-to-fine pooling, xAR's cell grouping, RAR's random permutation, PAR's sub-sampling, FAR's frequency filters) are formalized as specific instances of A. The matrix A is constrained to be orthogonal (via SVD-based Procrustes projection) and jointly optimized end-to-end with the AR transformer; Propositions 1 and 2 prove that optimizing BAR on y is mathematically equivalent to MAR or xAR on x, ensuring the loss remains valid. A residual objective L_residual_BAR further encourages earlier basis vectors to capture the most informative content, producing an emergent coarse-to-fine generation order without manual design. The framework is applied to both MAR (diffusion-head) and xAR (flow-matching) architectures, achieving FID 1.15 on ImageNet-256.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, BAR-H achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.15 with IS 327.1, Precision 0.86, and Recall 0.68 in just 50 sampling steps (0.68s per image), surpassing all prior AR and diffusion baselines. The smaller BAR-B model (172M params) reaches FID 1.56 in only 0.08s per image, matching the quality of MAR (943M params, FID 1.55) at 5.5× fewer parameters. For text-to-image generation on MS-COCO, BAR achieves FID-30K of 12.55 and GenEval 0.39, outperforming FAR (FID 13.91) by 1.36 FID points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAR-H (FID 1.15) sets a new state-of-the-art on ImageNet 256, outperforming the strongest prior AR method xAR (FID 1.24) by 0.09 FID and the best diffusion model MG (FID 1.34) by 0.19 FID, while also achieving the highest Precision (0.86) and Recall (0.68) among all compared methods. BAR is dramatically faster than prior AR models: BAR-H generates an image in 0.68s versus 28.24s for MAR and 12.41s for LlamaGen, thanks to its 50-step flow-matching sampler. The compact BAR-B (172M params) matches MAR (943M params) in FID (1.56 vs. 1.55) while being 5.5× smaller and 353× faster (0.08s vs. 28.24s). On text-to-image (MS-COCO), BAR (FID 12.55, GenEval 0.39) surpasses FAR (FID 13.91) by 1.36 FID and LDM (FID 12.70) by 0.15 FID, while also achieving the best HPSv2 score (25.71). BAR also improves over its own baselines when applied to MAR and xAR architectures (Table 2), confirming the framework's generality across model families.

## Relation to Prior Work

BAR builds directly on xAR (flow-matching AR) and MAR (diffusion-based AR) as its base architectures, adding a learnable orthogonal transform matrix A that re-parameterizes the token sequence before autoregressive prediction. Conceptually, it unifies a scattered family of prior AR variants—VAR (coarse-to-fine), RAR (random permutation), PAR (parallel groups), FAR (frequency filters), xAR (cell grouping), TiTok (compression), and FractalGen (recursive hierarchy)—as specific instances of the matrix A, providing the first rigorous linear-algebraic framework that had been missing from this line of work. The key departure from all prior methods is that A is not hand-designed from human inductive biases but is instead a free parameter optimized end-to-end alongside the AR transformer, with theoretical proofs (Propositions 1–2) guaranteeing that the training objective on transformed tokens is exactly equivalent to the original MAR/xAR objective on untransformed tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SOTA FID of 1.15 on ImageNet-256 (beating both AR and diffusion models) combined with the simplicity of the addition (one learnable matrix + SVD projection) makes this a highly practical technique likely to be adopted as a default component in future AR image generators. The unified framework also gives the field a principled vocabulary for discussing token ordering and grouping, potentially reducing the ad-hoc trial-and-error that has characterized recent AR improvements. Open questions include extending BAR to discrete AR models (discussed but not implemented), joint optimization with the VAE tokenizer, and whether the learned basis generalizes across resolutions or datasets without retraining. The paper acknowledges dependence on the KL-16 VAE and notes that end-to-end VAE+AR training (as in Light.DiT, REPA-E) is a natural next step.
