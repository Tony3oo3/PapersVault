## Problem

Standard CNNs are highly sensitive to photometric variations (brightness, contrast), and data augmentation offers no formal robustness guarantee. Existing equivariant architectures either handle only scale (SEq) or impose overly restrictive constraints on every layer (AffEq's SortPool activation and sum-to-one weights), making them computationally expensive and incompatible with standard components. The paper asks whether a minimal architectural modification can yield certified affine invariance at negligible cost.

## Input / Output

The method accepts 2D RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10), 2D grayscale fluorescence microscopy images (DSB 2018), and 3D grayscale volumes (Cryo-ET tomograms, synthetic nuclei), supporting both single- and multi-channel inputs. For classification, it outputs class labels via softmax-argmax on logits; for object localization, it outputs a Z-score-standardized score map whose local maxima above a threshold give object positions; for segmentation, it outputs binary masks. A key design detail is that the localization pipeline replaces the standard sigmoid+BCE setup with Z-score standardization and a novel ZMSE loss, since fixed thresholds are incompatible with equivariant networks.

## Method

SEqSI achieves scale equivariance and shift invariance by prepending a single shift-invariant convolutional layer (bias-free, weights summing to zero per output channel) to a standard scale-equivariant backbone (bias-free convolutions with ReLU and standard pooling), yielding logits satisfying f(λx+μ) = λf(x). For argmax-based tasks, invariance follows directly since argmax is order-preserving under positive scaling; for thresholding-based tasks (localization, segmentation), the paper introduces Z-score standardization of the output score map paired with a Z-scored Mean Squared Error (ZMSE) loss that trains the network to match relative spatial score distributions rather than absolute values. The architecture uses reflection padding to preserve equivariance at borders, remains fully compatible with ReLU and standard pooling, and incurs negligible computational overhead (5% faster training than Standard, identical memory) compared to AffEq's >50% slowdown and 3× memory increase. The paper further proves weak invariance guarantees for spatially-varying affine transformations, showing the zero-sum first layer provides robustness beyond the global case.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with no photometric augmentation, SEqSI achieves 91.2% accuracy under all affine corruptions (shift, scale, combined affine), compared to 51.1% (shift) and 64.1% (affine) for the Standard model. On the CZI Cryo-ET OOD generalization task, SEqSI trained on WBP data maintains 74.53% and 73.21% accuracy on the unseen Denoised and IsoNet Corrected domains, while the Standard model collapses to 22.36% and 15.95%. For 3D object localization, SEqSI achieves a score of 0.886 on uncorrupted images and all affine corruptions, versus 0.152 for Standard under shift. SEqSI trains 5% faster than Standard with identical memory footprint, while AffEq is over 50% slower and uses 3× more memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SEq (Mohan et al., 2019), SEqSI dominates on all shift-sensitive corruptions: on CIFAR-10 with no augmentation, SEqSI achieves 91.2% under shift while SEq drops to 53.4%, and on Cryo-ET OOD, SEqSI reaches 74.53% (Denoised) and 73.21% (IsoNet) versus SEq's 28.42% and 16.67%. Against AffEq (Herbreteau et al., 2023), both models achieve certified 0% affine invariance error, but SEqSI is substantially more efficient (11.23 vs 18.61 s/epoch, 0.44 vs 1.26 GB peak memory) and compatible with transfer learning from Standard architectures. On Cryo-ET, SEqSI outperforms AffEq on Denoised (74.53% vs 61.79%) and IsoNet (73.21% vs 46.37%), though AffEq wins on CTF Deconvolved (79.07% vs 66.51%). On CIFAR-10 robustness, SEqSI consistently matches or slightly exceeds AffEq across non-affine corruptions (e.g., 72.5% vs 63.6% on spatially-varying affine, 56.5% vs 52.9% on contrast inversion with no augmentation). The paper concludes that SEqSI offers the best trade-off: certified invariance at negligible computational cost, with generalization that complements data augmentation without the restrictive constraints that degrade AffEq's performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the line of group-equivariant CNNs for photometric robustness, specifically the Scale-Equivariant (SEq) networks of Mohan et al. (2019) that achieve scale equivariance by removing biases, and the more restrictive Affine-Equivariant (AffEq) networks of Herbreteau et al. (2023) that additionally enforce sum-to-one weights and a SortPool activation at every layer. SEqSI departs from both by strategically placing a single shift-invariant layer (sum-to-zero weights) at the front of a standard scale-equivariant backbone, achieving the same certified affine invariance with far fewer constraints and full compatibility with ReLU. The paper also introduces the ZMSE loss and standardized score-map post-processing to make threshold-based tasks (object localization) compatible with equivariant architectures, a problem not addressed in prior equivariant network work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEqSI provides a practical, low-overhead path to certified photometric robustness that is particularly impactful for biological imaging, where natural domain shifts (e.g., different cryo-ET denoising pipelines) cause standard models to collapse to near-random accuracy while SEqSI maintains 66–74%. The finding that architectural affine invariance generalizes to unseen non-affine corruptions (spatially-varying shifts, contrast inversion) suggests the inductive bias is broader than the formal guarantee, though this remains an empirical observation without theoretical backing. Open questions include compatibility with normalization layers, extension to full segmentation, and whether the approach scales to video or composite transformation groups. A limitation is that formal guarantees cover only global affine transformations; spatially-varying invariance is only 'weak' and the model still degrades under high-intensity noise or saturated corruptions.
