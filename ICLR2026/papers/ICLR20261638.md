## Problem

Existing LLM safety evaluations measure attack success rates on fixed, hand-crafted prompt sequences, providing no statistical guarantees and failing to generalize across the vast space of possible multi-turn conversations. No prior work has formally specified or certified the probability of catastrophic responses under realistic multi-turn conversational distributions, leaving it unclear how safe frontier models truly are when an adversary can adapt queries across turns.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a per-scenario query set of ~100 semantically related queries (derived from HarmBench harmful targets via actor-based expansion), constructs an undirected similarity graph using all-MiniLM-L6-v2 cosine embeddings with tunable thresholds (default 0.4–0.8), and queries a black-box target LLM over 50 sampled sequences of length 5. A GPT-4o judge model binary-classifies each response as catastrophic or not relative to the scenario's harmful target. The output is a Clopper-Pearson 95% confidence interval on the probability that at least one turn in the conversation produces a catastrophic response, reported as certified lower and upper bounds per model, dataset, and distribution specification.

## Method

C³LLM models multi-turn conversations as probability distributions over query sequences defined by Markov processes on a lifted state space (current query + set of already-visited queries) to prevent repetition. Three distribution families are instantiated: Random Node (independent uniform sampling over unvisited queries, optionally augmented with jailbreak prefixes), Graph Path (sequences follow edges of the semantic similarity graph, with either a uniform endpoint or a harmful-target-constrained endpoint via backward selection), and Adaptive with Rejection (forward selection where transition weights shift toward or away from the harmful target depending on whether the model accepted or rejected the current query, using tunable high/low weight ratio λh/λl). For each sampled sequence, the LLM is queried turn-by-turn with accumulated context, and the Clopper-Pearson method converts the observed count of catastrophic responses into a high-confidence bound on the true probability. An augmentation layer can stochastically prepend jailbreak prompts to individual queries, and the framework is extensible to arbitrary distributions over the query graph.

## Results

C³LLM certifies the probability of catastrophic responses in multi-turn LLM conversations using 95% Clopper-Pearson confidence intervals over 50 sampled query sequences per specification. On the cybercrime dataset, DeepSeek-R1 reaches a certified lower bound of 72.1% under Random Node with Jailbreak (RNwJ), and Mistral-Large reaches 65.2% under the same distribution. Claude-Sonnet-4 and Nova Premier show the lowest certified risks (lower bounds ≤ 2.8% across all distributions), while Graph Path with harmful target constraint (GPh) and Adaptive with Rejection (AwR) are most effective at eliciting catastrophic outputs from safer models. Across both chemical-biological and cybercrime categories, the certified lower bounds substantially exceed single-turn attack success rates for nearly all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares C³LLM's certified lower bounds against two baselines: single-turn (ST), where each of the 100 queries is sent independently, and multi-turn (MT), where queries are grouped into 5-query actor sequences. On the chemical-biological dataset, the certified lower bound exceeds the ST baseline by more than 0.05 in 86/100 scenarios for DeepSeek-R1, 100/100 for Mistral-Large, and 78/100 for Llama, indicating that single-turn evaluations substantially underestimate multi-turn catastrophic risk. Against the MT baseline, the exceedance is less frequent (e.g., 29/100 for DeepSeek, 30/100 for Mistral on chembio), but still non-trivial, showing that even fixed multi-turn sequences understate the risk relative to the full distributional space. On the cybercrime dataset, the gap versus ST is even larger (157/100 for DeepSeek, 151/100 for Mistral), while the gap versus MT remains meaningful (22/100 for DeepSeek, 38/100 for Mistral). For the safest models (Nova Premier, Claude-Sonnet-4), the certified bounds remain low across all distributions, and the MT baseline is rarely exceeded, suggesting these models are more robust to both fixed and distributional multi-turn attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-turn statistical certification framework for LLMs (Chaudhary et al., 2024, for bias and knowledge comprehension) to the multi-turn conversational setting, which the authors explicitly note has not been formally specified or certified before. It departs from the dominant paradigm of fixed-sequence benchmarking (AdvBench, Crescendo, SIREN) by replacing discrete attack sequences with probability distributions over query sequences, modeled as Markov processes on semantic similarity graphs. The perturbation-based certification literature (token-space or embedding-space robustness) is explicitly distinguished: rather than proving invariance under small input perturbations, C3LLM certifies against the harmful queries themselves under structured conversational distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first principled way to compare LLM safety with statistical guarantees over the full conversation space rather than a fixed benchmark, and the finding that certified multi-turn risk substantially exceeds single-turn attack success rates (Table 2: 86–157 scenarios per model) suggests current safety evaluations may be systematically underestimating deployment risk. Limitations include reliance on a GPT-4o judge, 50 samples per specification, and a specific graph-construction pipeline; open questions include whether the Markov graph structure captures all relevant adversarial dynamics, how certification scales to longer or open-ended conversations, and whether the three distributions adequately represent the full space of realistic multi-turn attacks. The black-box nature makes it broadly applicable to both open- and closed-source models, increasing its practical utility for pre-deployment safety auditing.
