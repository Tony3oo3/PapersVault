## Problem

Existing Learning-to-Defer (L2D) frameworks route each query to a single expert, preventing the use of collective expertise that is essential in high-stakes domains such as oncology tumor boards or multi-specialist fraud detection. No prior work provides a unified top-k deferral formulation with provable consistency guarantees across both one-stage and two-stage regimes, nor an adaptive mechanism for query-specific cardinality selection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query x (image or feature vector) and a fixed set of offline expert predictions, treating class labels and experts uniformly as 'entities' within a single scoring space. The policy π assigns a real-valued score to every entity, and the top-k selection set Π_k(x) is formed by taking the k highest-scoring entities; their predictions are then aggregated (e.g., majority vote, weighted vote) into a final output. In the adaptive Top-k(x) variant, a separate cardinality function k_θ(x) determines how many entities to query per input, so the expected number of consulted entities varies with input difficulty rather than being fixed.

## Method

The core contribution is a k-independent surrogate loss Φ_def,k^u derived as a convex upper bound on the top-k true deferral loss, enabling a single policy to be trained once and reused for any cardinality k without retraining. The paper proves this surrogate is Bayes-consistent, H_h-consistent in the one-stage setting, and (H_r, H_g)-consistent in the two-stage setting, with the Bayes-optimal top-k policy simply selecting the k entities with the lowest expected cost. For the adaptive Top-k(x) variant, a cardinality-aware deferral loss penalizes cumulative consultation costs via a non-decreasing function ξ, yielding a transparent marginal-benefit rule: an additional entity is queried iff the expected error reduction outweighs its marginal consultation cost. The framework strictly generalizes prior paradigms—selective prediction, model cascades, and top-1 deferral all arise as special cases when k=1 or under specific cost parameterizations.

## Results

On the California Housing dataset (two-stage regime), Top-k(x) achieves a near-optimal RMSE_min of 6.23 with a budget of β̄=0.156 and expected entities k̄=4.77, while fixed Top-k requires the full budget β̄=0.2 and k̄=6 entities to reach a comparable score (6.21). Under the more restrictive RMSE_avg metric, Top-k(x) achieves 8.53 versus 10.08 for Top-k at a budget of just β=0.095, a level Top-k fails to attain. Both Top-k and Top-k(x) consistently outperform the Top-1 L2D baseline (Mao et al., 2024c) across all three RMSE variants (min, avg, w-avg).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines spanning both one-stage (Mozannar & Sontag 2020; Mao et al. 2024a) and two-stage (Narasimhan et al. 2022; Mao et al. 2023a; Mao et al. 2024c; Montreuil et al. 2025b) L2D methods. On the main two-stage California Housing results (Figure 1), both Top-k and Top-k(x) outperform the Top-1 L2D baseline of Mao et al. (2024c) across all three RMSE metrics, confirming the limitations of single-entity deferral. Top-k(x) further dominates fixed Top-k on RMSE_avg (8.53 vs. 10.08) and RMSE_w-avg by adaptively selecting fewer entities when additional consultation would degrade performance. On RMSE_min, Top-k(x) matches Top-k's accuracy (6.23 vs. 6.21) while using 22% less budget (0.156 vs. 0.2) and 21% fewer expected entities (4.77 vs. 6). The one-stage baselines (Mozannar & Sontag 2020; Mao et al. 2024a) are evaluated on CIFAR-10 and SVHN in the appendix, where Top-k and Top-k(x) similarly achieve superior accuracy-cost trade-offs. Overall, the paper demonstrates strict generalization: all prior Top-1 methods are recovered as the k=1 special case, and the multi-expert variants consistently improve upon them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Learning-to-Defer (L2D) framework from single-expert (top-1) routing to multi-expert (top-k) routing, building directly on the score-based surrogate losses of Mozannar & Sontag (2020) for one-stage and Narasimhan et al. (2022) / Mao et al. (2023a, 2024c) for two-stage settings. The top-k selection mechanism draws on the cardinality-aware set prediction framework of Cortes et al. (2024) for top-k classification, while the consistency analysis leverages the cross-entropy surrogate family and H-consistency bounds established by Mao et al. (2024a, 2024c). A key departure is showing that the surrogate loss is algebraically identical to the two-stage loss of Mao et al. (2024c) yet now carries consistency guarantees for all k > 1, and that classical model cascades (Viola & Jones, 2001) are strict special cases of the top-k selection set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first principled framework for multi-expert deferral in L2D, addressing a real gap in high-stakes domains (oncology tumor boards, fraud detection) where single-expert routing is insufficient. The k-independent surrogate is practically valuable: a single policy trained once can be deployed at any cardinality without retraining, enabling dynamic budget adjustment at inference. Open questions include how to best aggregate predictions from k selected entities (the paper defers this to a generic metric d), whether the framework scales to very large expert pools, and how to handle correlated or overlapping expert capabilities. The empirical evidence, while positive, is limited to a handful of datasets with modest gains over top-1 baselines, leaving the practical magnitude of multi-expert benefits in realistic deployments somewhat untested.
