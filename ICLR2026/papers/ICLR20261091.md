## Problem

Vision-language models like CLIP learn spurious correlations from web-scale data, causing severe performance drops on specific demographic or semantic groups. Existing mitigation strategies require additional data, model fine-tuning, class or spurious-feature labels, domain expertise, or external LLMs, undermining the zero-shot advantage that makes VLMs attractive. The paper addresses the gap of a fully interpretable, zero-shot method that mitigates spurious correlations without any of these auxiliary requirements.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-computed VLM image embeddings (from backbones such as CLIP ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14, or BiomedCLIP) and, for DIAL, a short natural-language description of the spurious attributes (e.g., 'Male', 'Female'); DIAL+ requires only the embeddings. It produces debiased image embeddings in which the identified spurious subspace has been removed via orthogonal projection, which are then scored against class text prompts for zero-shot classification or used directly for image retrieval. No raw pixels, class labels, spurious-feature annotations, or additional calibration data are needed at any stage.

## Method

DIAL operates in three stages entirely in the embedding space. First, a candidate-selection algorithm (Alg. 1) flags samples likely affected by spurious correlations by combining zero-shot pseudo-labels with k-NN disagreement and distance from hybrid class centroids (a weighted mix of embedding mean and text-embedding centroid). Second, a pre-trained Matryoksha Sparse Autoencoder (MSAE) projects each embedding into a disentangled monosemantic feature space; an attribution score—combining the mean activation difference between positive and negative pseudo-labeled sets with cosine similarity to the spurious attribute's text embedding—ranks SAE feature directions, and the top features accounting for a fraction α of total attribution mass are selected as the spurious set. Third, the selected features are weighted by their alignment with their own mean direction, pruned, and subjected to QR decomposition to yield an orthonormal basis for the spurious subspace; the projection of each candidate sample's embedding onto this subspace is subtracted, scaled by a mitigation factor λ. DIAL+ extends the pipeline by detecting spurious features without prior descriptions: it ablates each SAE feature per sample to find influential concepts, pools them globally, and intersects with the candidate set to identify the most consistently activated spurious directions. A grid-search parameter algorithm (Alg. 2) automatically selects k*, α, λ, and the text-centroid weight w by minimizing the mean cosine-similarity difference between opposing spurious concept embeddings over the candidate subset.

## Results

On CelebA with CLIP ViT-B/32, DIAL achieves 85.54% average accuracy, 83.47% worst-group accuracy, and a 2.17-point gap, outperforming all zero-shot baselines and even TIE (which requires spurious feature labels and additional data). With the stronger ViT-L/14 backbone, DIAL reaches 86.87%/85.24%/1.63, and DIAL+ (which auto-detects spurious features) achieves a 1.39-point gap. On the ISIC medical dataset, DIAL dramatically improves worst-group accuracy from 42.21% (zero-shot) to 68.42% while maintaining 70.71% average accuracy. On FairFace retrieval, DIAL reduces MaxSkew@1000 by 28–63% across Age, Gender, and Ethnicity attributes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CelebA, DIAL and DIAL+ outperform all five baselines (TIE, ROBOSHOT, PerceptionCLIP, Ideal Words, Orth-Cali) on worst-group accuracy and gap for both ViT-B/32 and ViT-L/14, with DIAL achieving 83.47% WG vs. TIE's 82.63% (ViT-B/32) and 85.24% vs. 84.60% (ViT-L/14), while requiring no additional data, labels, or LLM. On Waterbirds (ViT-L/14), DIAL+ substantially improves worst-group accuracy over zero-shot (69.18% vs. 31.93%) and Orth-Cali (69.18% vs. 58.56%), though TIE with full labels still leads at 78.82% WG. On ISIC, DIAL achieves 68.42% WG, surpassing TIE (65.87%) and zero-shot (42.21%) by a wide margin, with a gap of only 2.29 points versus 28.00 for zero-shot. On FMOW, DIAL is competitive with TIE (19.90% vs. 20.19% WG) while requiring no labels or extra data. On COVID-19, DIAL matches zero-shot average accuracy (61.11%) while improving WG from 44.83% to 48.28%, though TIE retains a slight WG edge (52.17%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two key prior insights: the attribution-score method from Karvonen et al. (2024) for identifying SAE neurons correlated with a target concept, adapted here to a zero-shot setting via VLM pseudo-labels, and the observation from Li et al. (2025) that spurious-correlation-affected samples deviate from their class centroids, which motivates the candidate selection algorithm. It departs from the dominant zero-shot mitigation literature (TIE, ROBOSHOT, Ideal Words, Orth-Cali) by operating on the image embedding space via a pre-trained Matryoksha SAE rather than the textual modality, and by using orthogonal subspace projection instead of prompt engineering, LLM-generated insights, or additional data for parameter tuning. The DIAL+ variant further extends the framework to autonomously detect spurious features without any a priori attribute list, filling a gap left by methods that require the user to specify which features are spurious.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in offering a fully interpretable, training-free, label-free debiasing pipeline that can be applied post-hoc to any VLM embedding without retraining or auxiliary data—addressing a real deployment constraint in high-stakes domains like medical imaging where the paper demonstrates strong worst-group improvements. However, the method's effectiveness is uneven: it excels on CelebA and ISIC but underperforms several baselines on Waterbirds, where the spurious attributes (land vs. water background) are semantically complex and poorly captured by a single text prompt. Open questions include whether analytical parameter selection can replace the grid search, whether the approach generalizes to multi-modal or video settings, and how it interacts with emerging unlearning and fairness frameworks the authors mention as future directions.
