## Problem

Overparameterized matrix sensing via gradient descent suffers a sublinear convergence lower bound of Ω(1/t), and additional overparameterization actually slows convergence further. The theoretical role of weight normalization (WN) in non-convex low-rank problems remains largely unexplored, with no prior result showing how normalization can exploit overparameterization to accelerate optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n linear measurements {(M_i, y_i)} where each M_i is a symmetric m×m matrix and y_i = Tr(M_i^T A), with n scaling as O(mr/δ²) under Gaussian design to satisfy the RIP condition. The output is the reconstructed matrix X_t Θ_t X_t^T, where X_t ∈ St(m,r) is a Stiefel-manifold factor and Θ_t ∈ S^r is a symmetric magnitude matrix, jointly approximating the ground-truth low-rank PSD matrix A ∈ S_+^m of rank r_A ≪ m. The overparameterization level r > r_A is a free design parameter that the method exploits for faster convergence.

## Method

The paper extends weight normalization to matrix variables via polar decomposition, writing the Burer-Monteiro factor Y = XΘ̃ with X on the Stiefel manifold St(m,r) and Θ̃ PSD, then merging Θ̃Θ̃^T into a single symmetric matrix Θ ∈ S^r to avoid expensive PSD-constrained optimization. The direction variable X is updated by Riemannian gradient descent with polar retraction (projecting the Euclidean gradient onto the tangent space of St(m,r)), while the magnitude variable Θ is updated by standard Euclidean gradient descent with stepsize μ=2. This decoupling induces a two-phase convergence: a polynomial-time saddle phase where the subspace alignment Tr(Φ_t Φ_t^T) grows from near zero toward r_A, followed by a linearly convergent phase with rate exp(-O((r-r_A)^4 t / (κ^4 m^2 r^2 r_A))). Crucially, the convergence rate and sample complexity both improve polynomially as the overparameterization gap r - r_A increases, inverting the typical curse of overparameterization observed in vanilla gradient descent.

## Results

The paper proves that WN with Riemannian gradient descent (RGD) achieves a linear convergence rate of exp(-O(((r−r_A)⁴)/(κ⁴m²r²r_A)·t)) for overparameterized matrix sensing, which is exponentially faster than the sublinear lower bound Ω(κ²/(log(mr_A²)·t)) established for standard GD without WN. The burn-in (saddle-escape) phase lasts at most O(κ⁴m⁴r⁴r_A²/(r−r_A)⁸) iterations, and the sample complexity under Gaussian design is O(κ⁴m⁷r⁹r_A²/(r−r_A)¹²). Increasing the overparameterization level r from r_A+c to cr_A yields up to an O(r_A⁴) improvement in the convergence exponent and an O(r_A¹²) reduction in sample complexity. On CIFAR-10 image reconstruction (32×32 horse image, phase retrieval), WN+RGD produces visibly more accurate reconstructions than GD after comparable iteration budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GD with small random initialization (Stöger & Soltanolkotabi, 2021), WN+RGD wins decisively: the GD upper bound only guarantees a constant reconstruction error with early stopping and no exact convergence, whereas WN+RGD achieves exact convergence at a linear rate from a fully random initialization without magnitude constraints. Against the GD lower bound (Xiong et al., 2024), WN+RGD achieves an exponential speedup—linear vs. sublinear Ω(1/t)—and uniquely benefits from overparameterization (faster convergence and lower sample complexity as r grows), while GD does not. In the preconditioned-algorithm comparison (Figure 5, Appendix A.5), the proposed PrecRGD variant outperforms PrecGD (Zhang et al., 2021) in convergence speed and matches ScaledGD(λ) (Xu et al., 2023) in rate, while requiring a far weaker RIP condition (δ = O(κ⁻²) vs. δ = O(κ^{-C_δ})) and no small-initialization constraint. On the challenging non-benign matrix completion landscape (Yalçın et al., 2022), WN+RGD achieves >90% success rate at rank 1 (|S|=2,4) versus substantially lower rates for GD, though both methods struggle at ranks 2–3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Weight Normalization (WN) from its original vector-valued formulation (Salimans & Kingma, 2016) to matrix variables via polar decomposition, placing the direction variable on a Stiefel manifold and optimizing it with Riemannian gradient descent. It departs from the overparameterized matrix sensing literature (Stöger & Soltanolkotabi, 2021; Xiong et al., 2024), where overparameterization is shown to be a curse that slows GD to a sublinear rate, by demonstrating that WN converts this curse into a benefit. The Riemannian optimization framework for the direction variable follows standard Stiefel-manifold tools (Absil et al., 2008; Boumal, 2023), while the direction-magnitude decoupling is a direct generalization of the WN philosophy to the Burer-Monteiro factorization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical result showing that a normalization technique benefits from overparameterization, reversing the prevailing narrative that extra parameters slow non-convex optimization. The exponential speedup (linear vs. sublinear rate) and the polynomial reduction in both iteration and sample complexity with increasing overparameterization provide a concrete, quantifiable justification for WN in low-rank recovery. Limitations include the κ⁴ dependence in the convergence rate, the requirement of a (r+r_A+1)-RIP condition, and the restriction to symmetric PSD matrices; extending the analysis to asymmetric problems, tensor sensing, or deeper network architectures remains open. The practical relevance is broad since WN underpins popular parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (DoRA, PoLAR), yet prior theoretical support was limited to implicit regularization in simpler settings.
