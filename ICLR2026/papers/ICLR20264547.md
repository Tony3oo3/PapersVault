## Problem

Existing VLMs serialize images into 1D sequences via raster scan, diverging from human vision's parallel Gestalt processing, yet their internal mechanisms for object recognition and spatial perception remain opaque. The paper asks two fundamental questions: how do VLMs associate positionally discontinuous tokens belonging to the same object within a flattened sequence, and how do they infer 2D spatial relationships from 1D positional encodings such as 2D RoPE?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264547/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (patchified into a 2D grid, e.g., 24×24 for LLaVA-1.5 or dynamic resolution for Qwen2.5-VL) and a text instruction as input. Beyond the standard text response, the interpretability analysis produces token maps (each image patch decoded to its most probable text token via logit lens at a specific LLM layer, e.g., layer 25 for LLaVA-1.5) and segmentation maps (patches colored by object keyword matching), while the practical applications output a compressed visual token sequence and improved spatial reasoning predictions.

## Method

The paper deconstructs VLM visual processing into two pathways inspired by the dual-stream hypothesis of human vision. For object recognition, they apply logit lens layer-by-layer to the ViT encoder, revealing a two-stage process: shallow-to-middle layers perform attribute recognition (detecting local features like color and texture), then middle-to-deep layers perform semantic disambiguation (integrating shared attributes into specific object categories), a pattern analogous to Gestalt cognition. For spatial perception, they theoretically derive that 2D RoPE encodes 'left' and 'right' as collinear opposing vectors and 'left-right' versus 'front-back' in orthogonal subspaces, verified empirically via direction vector PCA and intervention experiments. Based on these findings, they propose two practical improvements: (1) an instruction-agnostic token compression method that trains a lightweight visual decoder via knowledge distillation from the LLM's unembedding matrix and merges consecutive identical tokens using run-length encoding, and (2) RoPE scaling that adaptively amplifies low-frequency dimensions via (θᵢ)′ = θᵢ · (1 + α(2i/d)^p) to strengthen positional discrimination for spatial reasoning.

## Results

For token compression via run-length encoding, LLaVA-1.5-7B achieves a 27.83% sequence length reduction with minimal performance loss (e.g., GQA 61.32 vs. 60.50 original; POPE 86.00 vs. 85.96), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves 16.19% reduction with comparable scores. For RoPE scaling on spatial reasoning, Qwen2-VL-2B improves from 53.16 to 58.25 on What's Up B (+5.09) and from 76.61 to 78.22 on GQA-spatial1 (+1.61) in a training-free setting. When combined with SFT, Qwen2-VL-2B reaches 63.48 on What's Up B and 82.24 on GQA-spatial1, while Qwen2-VL-7B reaches 90.44 on What's Up B and 96.98 on GQA-spatial1, consistently outperforming the SFT-only baseline across all seven spatial reasoning benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-1.5-7B (original decoding), the run-length compression method achieves a 27.83% token reduction while matching or slightly exceeding the original on most benchmarks (GQA 61.32 vs. 60.50, VQA 49.24 vs. 48.93, POPE 86.00 vs. 85.96), with modest drops on TextVQA (43.16 vs. 45.01) and MMBench (51.40 vs. 53.73). Against Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the compression yields a 16.19% reduction with small losses on TextVQA (76.33 vs. 78.21) and POPE (87.46 vs. 89.23) but a slight gain on ScienceQA (80.71 vs. 80.55). For spatial reasoning, RoPE scaling (training-free) consistently outperforms the unmodified Qwen2-VL-2B baseline across all seven benchmarks, with the largest gain on What's Up B (+5.09 points). When combined with SFT, RoPE scaling further improves over SFT-only on What's Up A (+0.88), What's Up B (+1.96), VSR (+0.19), COCO-spatial1 (+0.95), and GQA-spatial1 (+0.95) for the 2B model, confirming that the positional information amplification complements task-specific fine-tuning rather than conflicting with it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the logit lens interpretability framework (Neo et al., 2024; Sonia Joseph, 2024) from LLM activations to visual tokens in VLMs, going layer-by-layer through the ViT encoder to trace how object identity emerges. It departs from prior causal-tracing and information-flow studies (Rajaram et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025) by organizing the analysis around the neuroscience dual-stream hypothesis, splitting VLM internals into a 'what' pathway (object recognition) and a 'where' pathway (spatial perception). For spatial perception, it provides the first theoretical derivation of the geometric structure of 2D RoPE positional representations in VLMs, complementing prior empirical studies of RoPE in language models (Su et al., 2024). The practical token compression method builds on the run-length encoding idea applied to decoded text tokens, distinguishing itself from attention-based or similarity-based pruning (ToME, Bolya et al., 2022) by being instruction-agnostic and FlashAttention-compatible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage finding (attribute recognition in shallow ViT layers, semantic disambiguation in deeper layers) provides a mechanistic explanation for VLM hallucinations and suggests that interventions targeting the middle layers could reduce errors. The theoretical characterization of 2D RoPE geometry—showing that left/right are collinear opposites and left/front are in orthogonal subspaces—gives a principled basis for the RoPE scaling fix, which improves spatial reasoning without fine-tuning. However, the analysis is limited to four basic spatial relationships and two model families, and the token compression achieves lower compression ratios than ToME while requiring a separately trained visual decoder. Open questions include whether the two-stage pattern generalizes to non-ViT encoders, how 3D depth reasoning is encoded, and whether more complex spatial relationships (e.g., top-left) follow the predicted geometric structure.
