## Problem

Existing time series reasoning benchmarks are manually curated, expensive to extend, and typically restricted to narrow domains or specific skill sets, creating a practical barrier for researchers who need comprehensive evaluation tools. This paper asks whether LLMs genuinely understand time series concepts (trends, noise, causality, anomalies) and whether scalable, domain-specific benchmarks can be generated automatically without extensive expert annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262924/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a domain-specific dataset (exposed via a Python class with a query method), a natural language task description specifying user guidelines and domain context, and optionally a concept list generated via LLM, web search, or RAG. It outputs question templates implemented as parameterized Python functions that encode both the question logic and rule-based answer computation, which can then sample arbitrary numbers of multiple-choice instances. Time series are rendered as plots at 50 DPI (base64-encoded) or serialized as comma-separated numeric values, and each generated sample includes the question, four options, the correct answer, and the associated time series values.

## Method

The framework has two tiers: TimeSeriesExam uses 100+ manually curated templates, each linked to a synthetic time series generator, with iterative refinement via a 2-parameter logistic IRT model that drops low-discrimination items and resamples to maximize ability separation across candidate LLMs. TimeSeriesExamAgent scales this to real data via a multi-agent pipeline: a Generation Agent (using chain-of-thought planning followed by template synthesis) produces Python function templates conditioned on dataset structure and domain concepts, while a Verification Agent applies three progressive stages—syntactic execution check, LLM-as-a-judge content validation (relevance, time-series dependence, ambiguity), and capability-aligned filtering that discards templates where weaker student LLMs outperform stronger ones (expertise reversal detection). Failed templates receive feedback and are regenerated up to 3 times before permanent discard. Quality is further assessed via an LLM-as-a-jury panel (G-Eval with multiple model families) scoring specificity, unambiguity, domain relevance, and answerability on a 1–10 scale.

## Results

On the five real-world datasets (MIT-BIH, PTB-XL, MIMIC-IV Waveform, Yahoo Finance, WeatherBench 2), even the strongest model (GPT-5) achieves only 51.5% average accuracy, with all six evaluated VLMs scoring below 55%. Performance varies substantially by domain: GPT-5 reaches 61.7% on Yahoo Finance but drops to 45.0% on PTB-XL, while Gemini-2.5-Pro leads on MIT-BIH (61.4%) but trails on WeatherBench 2 (45.3%). On the synthetic TimeSeriesExam, GPT-4o achieves the highest overall accuracy (73%), with anomaly detection being the hardest category (54% for GPT-4o, 22% for Qwen2.5-VL-72B). Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-3B on 2,000 agent-generated ECG exams lifts accuracy from 21.8% (base) to 47.0% on ECG-QA test, a 216% relative improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ECG-QA (a human-curated, template-based ECG benchmark), TimeSeriesExamAgent achieves higher question diversity (0.301 ± 0.070 vs. 0.207 ± 0.079 mean embedding distance) and outperforms on all four LLM-as-a-jury quality criteria: specificity (8.43 vs. 5.60), unambiguity (8.40 vs. 5.77), domain relevance (9.00 vs. 8.17), and answerability (9.10 vs. 8.47). Against MT-Bench (a multimodal time series QA benchmark), TimeSeriesExamAgent also wins on all four quality dimensions in the finance domain: specificity (8.29 vs. 6.88), unambiguity (7.24 vs. 6.11), domain relevance (8.89 vs. 8.35), and answerability (8.57 vs. 7.29). These comparisons demonstrate that the agent-generated benchmarks match or exceed human-curated alternatives in both diversity and quality without requiring manual template design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on synthetic time series generation (Woo et al. 2022, TabPFN, Chronos) by incorporating more diverse composition methods (additive, multiplicative, and other forms) to create controlled reasoning benchmarks. It departs from existing domain-specific time series benchmarks (ECG-QA, ECG-Expert-QA, EngineMTQA), which rely on manual curation and are limited in scope, by introducing an agentic framework that combines the systematic structure of templates with the adaptability of LLM agents. The multi-agent verification pipeline draws on recent agent-based benchmark construction work (BenchAgents, TestAgent) but is specifically tailored to numeric time series data, and the LLM-as-a-jury evaluation builds on G-Eval and panel-based evaluation to mitigate intra-model bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TimeSeriesExamAgent provides a practical, low-cost tool (~$0.09 per template) for domain experts to generate comprehensive time series reasoning benchmarks from their own datasets without extensive manual curation, addressing a real bottleneck in the field. The finding that even GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Pro cap at ~51% average accuracy across domains, with significant domain-specific variation (e.g., GPT-5 strong on weather but weaker on healthcare), underscores that general reasoning does not transfer to domain-specific time series interpretation. Open questions include the systematic impact of human-in-the-loop refinement on benchmark validity, the optimal input modality (vision vs. text) for different question types, and whether the framework extends naturally to non-time-series structured data.
