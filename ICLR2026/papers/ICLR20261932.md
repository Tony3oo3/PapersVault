## Problem

Existing spike-RGB fusion methods (e.g., S-SDM, STIR) rely on pixel-level self-supervised learning, which suffers from the 'sharpness trap'—enhancing contrast without recovering fine geometric structure—and does not emulate how the human visual cortex integrates decoupled rod (motion) and cone (color) signals in latent space. No prior work has applied a latent-space generative framework to jointly process the spatially sparse spike modality and the temporally degraded RGB modality, leaving a gap between biological inspiration and computational implementation.

## Input / Output

SpikeGen accepts a blurry RGB image (encoded via a pre-trained VAE into a 512× spatially downsampled latent) and a binary spike stream of shape [B,1,T,H,W] (0/1 values, no color), optionally augmented with a TFP pseudo-dense grayscale frame to provide coarse spatial constraints. The modality balance is controlled by a scalar γ ∈ [0,1] that weights the RGB latent versus the spike latent; during pre-training γ is sampled from a truncated Gaussian, while at inference it is fixed per task. Outputs are task-dependent: a deblurred RGB image, a dense color frame reconstructed from sparse spikes, or a novel-view rendering via 3D Gaussian Splatting, all decoded from the VAE latent space back to pixel space.

## Method

SpikeGen is built on a modified Masked Auto-Regressive Model (MAR) with the autoregressive component removed so all latent tokens are decoded simultaneously via per-token diffusion through a compact MLP, reducing inference from ~96 s to 2.7 s on 256 images. A Spatial-Temporal Separable Spike (S3) Encoder processes the raw spike stream with three stride-2 3D-convolution blocks (reaching 8× spatial and 8× temporal downsampling to [B,512,T/8,H/8,W/8]), then applies learned temporal attention weights and sums along the temporal axis to yield a 2D feature map [B,512,H/8,W/8] aligned with the VAE latent grid. A random modality dropout mechanism mixes the two latents as z_mixed = (1−γ)·z_RGB + γ·z_spike, and the diffusion target is color-faded proportionally to γ so that spike-dominant conditions emphasize texture over color. Pre-training uses a pure diffusion loss in VAE latent space on ~1 M ImageNet images (40×40 blur kernel, 8 of 64 spike frames), and fine-tuning adds a spike-alignment loss that synthesizes a predicted spike stream from the decoded output and compares it against the ground-truth spike stream, avoiding the need for clean RGB ground truth in downstream tasks.

## Results

On the GOPRO conditional video deblurring task, SpikeGen achieves 29.30 dB PSNR / 0.84 SSIM at V_th=1, 28.78 / 0.82 at V_th=2, and 28.07 / 0.81 at V_th=4, outperforming the strongest baseline S-SDM by 2.4–2.6 dB PSNR across all spike sparsity levels. On dense frame reconstruction (SREDS), SpikeGen reaches 39.25 dB PSNR / 0.98 SSIM / 0.01 LPIPS, surpassing STIR (38.79 / 0.97 / 0.02) while using only 16 spike frames versus STIR's 64. For novel-view synthesis on Blender, SpikeGen attains 31.81 dB PSNR on Objects and 28.26 dB on Out-Door, exceeding SpikeGS (30.18 / 28.06) by 1.6 and 0.2 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikeGen consistently outperforms all 22 baselines across the three evaluated tasks. In conditional video deblurring, the most notable gap is against event-based methods (REFID, SpkDeblurNet, TRMD) which collapse to 11–15 dB PSNR at V_th=4, while SpikeGen maintains 28.07 dB; even the strongest dual-modality baseline S-SDM trails by 2.4–2.6 dB PSNR. In dense frame reconstruction, SpikeGen edges out STIR (the prior SOTA spike-based method) by 0.46 dB PSNR and 0.01 SSIM on SREDS while using only 1/4 the spike frames (16 vs. 64), and also surpasses event-based methods (ET-Net, HyperE2VID) by over 2.8 dB PSNR. For novel-view synthesis, SpikeGen beats SpikeGS by 1.63 dB PSNR on Objects and 0.20 dB on Out-Door, and substantially outperforms RGB-only deblurring approaches (DeblurGS, DeblurNeRF) by 3.3–4.7 dB on the challenging Out-Door scenes. The efficiency comparison (Table 7) further shows SpikeGen's non-autoregressive design processes 256 images in 2.7 s versus 1 min 32 s for MAR and 2 min 19 s for DiT, while achieving the highest PSNR (19.08 vs. 18.79 and 18.11).

## Relation to Prior Work

SpikeGen builds on two architectural lineages: the Masked Auto-Regressive Model (MAR, Li et al. 2024), from which it inherits the VAE-encoder + ViT-conditioner + per-token diffusion decoder structure but removes the autoregressive token-by-token generation in favor of simultaneous all-token decoding; and the Latent Diffusion Model (Rombach et al. 2022), whose VAE latent-space diffusion objective forms the training backbone. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant pixel-space deterministic fusion paradigm (S-SDM, STIR, SpkDeblurNet) by arguing that the human visual cortex integrates rod and cone signals in latent representations rather than pixel space, and by introducing a probabilistic (diffusion) framework to handle the inherent information loss in both modalities. The S3 Encoder for spike streams and the random modality dropout (γ-mixing) are novel components not present in prior spike-RGB fusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first latent generative framework validated across all three major spike-RGB processing tasks (deblurring, dense frame reconstruction, novel-view synthesis) with a single pre-trained model, suggesting a unified backbone for neuromorphic vision. The 512× spatial-temporal compression and the elimination of autoregressive decoding yield a ~40× inference speedup over MAR while matching or exceeding its quality, making large-scale pre-training on ImageNet feasible. Open questions include whether the VAE latent space is the optimal representation for spike signals (which are inherently binary and asynchronous), whether the spike-alignment loss during fine-tuning generalizes to real-world spike streams with non-ideal sensor noise, and whether the two-stage pipeline for novel-view synthesis can be made end-to-end without sacrificing the pre-training advantage.
