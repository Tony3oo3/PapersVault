## Problem

Over 85% of the human proteome is considered undruggable due to shallow, flexible, or cryptic binding pockets that defeat conventional small-molecule screening. Fragment-based drug discovery (FBDD) can access these sites, but no systematic benchmark exists for fragment-level virtual screening on undruggable targets, and standard docking or ML scoring tools fail to meaningfully rank small, weakly-binding fragments in such pockets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261032/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D protein pocket structure (extracted from PDB complexes, typically with SiteScore < 0.8 and fewer than ~25 residues) and a library of small fragments (8–24 heavy atoms, generated via BRICS decomposition of drug-like molecules from ChEMBL). At inference, it produces cosine-similarity scores in a shared embedding space that rank fragments (and optionally full molecules) by predicted binding compatibility with the pocket, evaluated via early-recognition metrics (EF@k, BEDROC) rather than full-ranking AUC alone.

## Method

FragCLIP is a tri-modal contrastive learning framework built on the UniMol SE(3)-equivariant 3D CNN architecture, with separate encoders for protein pockets, fragments, and their parent molecules. Three InfoNCE-style contrastive losses jointly align pocket–molecule, pocket–fragment, and molecule–fragment representations, capturing both scaffold-level binding semantics and fine-grained fragment–pocket interactions. A cross-attention fusion module takes the molecule embedding as query over the set of fragment embeddings, concatenates the attended output with the molecule embedding, and passes it through an MLP to produce a fused representation that is additionally contrastively aligned with the pocket. This fusion mechanism performs implicit fragment importance weighting—highlighting binding-relevant substructures while suppressing noisy or context-dependent fragments—without requiring explicit per-fragment labels. Training uses PDBbind complexes with 90% sequence-identity filtering against all test targets to prevent leakage, and the model is evaluated on the newly curated FragBench (54 undruggable targets) as well as fragment-level versions of DUD-E, DEKOIS 2.0, and LIT-PCBA.

## Results

On the novel FragBench benchmark (54 undruggable targets), FragCLIP achieves a BEDROC of 0.115 and EF@0.5% of 6.85, substantially outperforming the best learning-based baseline DrugCLIP (BEDROC 0.080, EF@0.5% 4.11) and all docking/scoring methods. Classical docking tools essentially fail at fragment-level screening: Glide reaches only EF@0.5% = 1.86 and Vina produces near-random rankings (AUROC 0.476). On standard fragment benchmarks, FragCLIP also leads consistently—EF@0.5% of 20.32 on DUD-E, 17.96 on DEKOIS 2.0, and 3.44 on the challenging LIT-PCBA—while at the molecule level on DUD-E it attains AUC 85.44 and EF@1% 37.23, surpassing DrugCLIP (AUC 80.93, EF@1% 31.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FragCLIP decisively outperforms all docking and scoring baselines on FragBench: Glide (EF@0.5% = 1.86) and Vina (EF@0.5% = 1.67) are barely above random, confirming that force-field-based methods cannot meaningfully rank small fragments in shallow pockets. Among ML-based scorers, RTMScore (EF@0.5% = 1.90) and EquiScore (EF@0.5% = 4.04) improve modestly but remain far below FragCLIP (6.85). The closest learning-based competitor, DrugCLIP (EF@0.5% = 4.11, BEDROC 0.080), is outperformed by FragCLIP by 67% on EF@0.5% and 44% on BEDROC, demonstrating the value of the explicit fragment modality and fusion module. LigUnity (EF@0.5% = 4.26) and EquiScore (4.04) are also clearly surpassed. On the standard DUD-E fragment benchmark, FragCLIP (EF@0.5% = 20.32) edges out LigUnity (19.49) and substantially beats DrugCLIP (12.01); on the harder LIT-PCBA, FragCLIP (3.44) leads RTMScore (2.94) and EquiScore (2.45). At the molecule level on DUD-E, FragCLIP with fusion (AUC 85.44, EF@1% 37.23) surpasses DrugCLIP (AUC 80.93, EF@1% 31.89) and Glide-SP (AUC 76.70, EF@1% 16.18), confirming that fragment-level supervision also improves full-molecule retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive protein-ligand representation learning paradigm of DrugCLIP (Gao et al., 2023) from a two-modality (pocket–molecule) setting to a tri-modal (pocket–fragment–molecule) framework, using UniMol's SE(3)-equivariant encoders as the geometric backbone. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a cross-attention fusion module that selectively weights fragment embeddings by their binding relevance, addressing the noise inherent in fragment-level signals. Methodologically, the benchmark construction departs from prior virtual-screening benchmarks (DUD-E, LIT-PCBA) by targeting undruggable proteins with shallow pockets and by using a multi-agent LLM-human curation pipeline to ground target selection in literature evidence rather than purely structural heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark and dedicated model for fragment-level virtual screening on undruggable targets, a setting where over 85% of the human proteome resides and where conventional docking demonstrably fails (Glide EF@0.5% ≈ 1.86, near random). The practical demonstration of fragment linking via DiffLinker on BCL-2 suggests a viable end-to-end pipeline from fragment retrieval to drug-like compound generation. Key limitations include the relatively small benchmark (54 targets), reliance on docking-derived labels rather than experimental fragment-binding data, and modest AUROC gains (0.593 vs. 0.597 for DrugCLIP) despite large EF improvements, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to truly novel, unseen target classes or scales to larger fragment libraries.
