## Problem

Billion-parameter Transformer training is frequently destabilized by loss spikes and divergence, and while Edge of Stability (EoS) theory identifies the product of learning rate and (preconditioned) Hessian curvature as the governing stability quantity, applying this theory at scale has been infeasible because online curvature estimation via Hessian-vector products is too expensive (requiring ~50 HVPs per step with existing methods). The paper addresses both the estimation bottleneck and the lack of a principled, curvature-aware training strategy for large Transformers.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a standard Llama 3-style decoder-only Transformer (up to 3B parameters, 32 layers, 2048 embedding dim, 1024 context length) trained on large text corpora (FineWeb, DCLM, OLMo-Mix) with AdamW. It consumes the optimizer's second-moment state to form the diagonal preconditioner P_t and requires fewer than 5 HVPs per training step for curvature tracking (an order of magnitude less than prior methods). The outputs are a stably trained model and a per-step online estimate of λ_max(G_t), the top eigenvalue of the preconditioned Hessian, which can be logged for diagnostics or used to gate the architecture warm-up schedule.

## Method

The paper contributes two tightly coupled components. First, a warm-started power iteration for online curvature tracking: under a Lipschitz Hessian and nonvanishing spectral gap, the top eigenvector of the (preconditioned) Hessian moves slowly between successive steps (bounded via Davis-Kahan sin-Θ), so reusing the previous step's eigenvector as initialization reduces the HVP budget from ~50 to <5 per step while improving accuracy; this extends verbatim to the time-varying preconditioned matrix G_t = P_t^{-1/2} H P_t^{-1/2} for Adam. Second, architecture warm-up: during the learning-rate warm-up phase (when the stability threshold 1/η is tight), the model is kept at reduced effective depth by freezing selected Transformer blocks to exact identity (all weights zeroed except RMSNorm, excluded from the optimizer); as the learning rate decays and the threshold relaxes, these blocks are unfrozen in groups with zero initialization, so their Jacobian contribution grows smoothly from zero rather than jumping, preventing curvature spikes. This keeps η·λ_max(G_t) within the stability envelope throughout training without any dynamic computation-graph surgery, and can optionally replace or complement standard LR warm-up.

## Results

On 1B-parameter, 16-layer Llama-3-style Transformers trained with peak LR 8×10⁻³, Architecture Warm-up achieves validation perplexity of 25.02 on FineWeb, 22.64 on DCLM, and 18.54 on OLMo-Mix, while the unmodified baseline diverges on FineWeb and OLMo-Mix and reaches 165.62 PPL on DCLM. At the Chinchilla compute-optimal budget (25B tokens, FineWeb), Arch-Warmup further improves to 18.35 PPL, outperforming the strongest baseline QK-Norm (20.28) by ~1.9 points. The method also widens the stable learning-rate range: at η=8×10⁻³ the baseline diverges while Arch-Warmup converges smoothly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Architecture Warm-up decisively outperforms all three stabilization baselines across every dataset where a comparison is possible. On FineWeb, Arch-Warmup (25.02 PPL) beats QK-Norm (49.88) by 24.9 points and Softcap (51.41) by 26.4 points; on DCLM it beats QK-Norm (61.57) by 38.9 points and Softcap (43.38) by 20.7 points. QK-Clip is the weakest baseline, diverging to NaN on all three datasets. The unmodified baseline also diverges on FineWeb and OLMo-Mix, and reaches an unreasonably high 165.62 PPL on DCLM. At the Chinchilla compute-optimal setting (Table 2), Arch-Warmup (18.35) still leads QK-Norm (20.28) by 1.9 points and Softcap (32.44) by 14.1 points, confirming that the stability advantage persists beyond short runs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Edge of Stability (EoS) framework from small-scale verification (≤25M parameters) to billion-parameter Transformers by introducing a warm-started power-iteration estimator that reduces Hessian-Vector-Product cost by an order of magnitude, making per-iteration curvature tracking feasible. It then repurposes the progressive-depth literature (Progressively Stacking, Net2Net, LiGO) — previously motivated by training efficiency — as an explicit curvature-control mechanism tied to the EoS stability criterion. The method departs from ad-hoc stabilization techniques (QK-Norm, QK-Clip, Softcap) by targeting the root cause (preconditioned Hessian eigenvalue) rather than proximate symptoms (attention logit magnitudes).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making online curvature tracking practical at billion-parameter scale, the paper provides the first empirical confirmation at scale that loss spikes coincide with preconditioned-curvature surges and that curvature grows with depth, closing a gap between EoS theory and large-model practice. Architecture warm-up is a simple, drop-in technique (no graph surgery, no new primitives) that widens the stable learning-rate range and outperforms existing stabilization methods, with the intriguing possibility of replacing or complementing LR warmup. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-Transformer architectures, how it interacts with other stabilization techniques simultaneously, and whether the specific unlock schedule matters for very deep (100+ layer) models.
