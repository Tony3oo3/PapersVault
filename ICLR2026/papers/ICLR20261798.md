## Problem

Multi-agent LLM systems are structurally fragile—a single agent's error can cascade into distant, hard-to-trace failures—yet error attribution research is bottlenecked by severe data scarcity. Existing benchmarks (Who&When: 184 errors, MASFT: ~150 tasks, TRAIL: 148 traces) are tiny and depend on costly expert annotation, creating a scalability deadlock where SOTA models lack the training signal to improve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261798/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a serialized multi-agent trajectory log (the full conversation trace across all agents in a MAS execution, up to 32,768 tokens) and produces a structured JSON attribution map: a set of (faulty_agent, error_mode) pairs drawn from a 14-mode MAST taxonomy. The output is multi-label—multiple agents and multiple error modes can co-occur in a single trajectory—and is evaluated at three granularities (pair, agent-only, error-only) using both Micro-F1 and Macro-F1 to capture long-tail error mode performance.

## Method

Aegis is a three-stage automated pipeline: (1) collect deterministic successful trajectories across six MAS frameworks and six task domains; (2) an LLM-based adaptive manipulator injects context-aware errors via prompt injection or response corruption, guided by injection plans specifying target agents and error modes; (3) only trajectories that fail as intended are retained, with ground-truth labels known by construction. For learning, the paper develops three paradigms: SFT as sequence-to-sequence generation of the JSON attribution; GRPO with a hierarchical reward that grants non-repeatable partial credit for correct agents, error modes, and pairs while penalizing duplicates and false positives; and Disentangled Contrastive Learning (DCL), which uses MIL attention to select top-K evidence turns, aligns them to agent and error-mode prototype banks via supervised contrastive loss, and enforces a hierarchical consistency constraint (pair probability ≤ min of constituent probabilities) through a squared hinge regularizer.

## Results

On Aegis-Bench, Aegis-SFT (Qwen2.5-14B) achieves the highest average F1 score of 26.51, nearly doubling its base model's 13.99 and outperforming all proprietary LLMs (o3: 20.24, Gemini-2.5-Flash: 19.55, Claude-Sonnet-4: 18.16). Aegis-GRPO (Qwen2.5-14B) reaches 18.41, remaining competitive with large foundation models. The lightweight DCL model (~23–35M params) achieves 12.61 average F1, substantially above the random baseline (4.08). On the out-of-distribution Who&When benchmark, Aegis-SFT improves Error μF1 from 1.56 to 9.87 and Agent μF1 from 49.88 to 51.14, confirming generalization beyond the synthetic training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Aegis-SFT (Qwen2.5-14B) achieves the best overall average F1 of 26.51, surpassing the strongest proprietary baseline o3 (20.24) by 6.27 points and Gemini-2.5-Flash (19.55) by 6.96 points, while also outperforming the much larger Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct (15.01) and gpt-oss-120b (17.07). On the in-domain Aegis-Bench, Aegis-SFT reaches 76.53 Agent μF1 and 27.53 Error μF1, compared to o3's 40.31 and 22.37 respectively, indicating that task-specific fine-tuning on synthetic error data yields substantially better attribution than zero-shot prompting of larger models. Aegis-GRPO (14B) at 18.41 is competitive with Gemini-2.5-Pro (18.35) and Claude-Sonnet-4 (18.16), and shows particular strength on Who&When Agent μF1 (54.43 vs. 53.10 for o3). The Qwen3-8B-Thinking + GRPO variant achieves the best Who&When Error μF1 among all fine-tuned models (11.25), approaching o3's 14.88. The DCL model, despite being ~200× smaller than the LLM baselines, outperforms the random baseline by over 3× (12.61 vs. 4.08) and remains competitive with the untrained Qwen2.5-7B (12.43), validating contrastive learning as a lightweight alternative for error attribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MAST taxonomy (Cemri et al., 2025) for its 14 error modes and the Who&When benchmark (Zhang et al., 2025c) as its primary OOD evaluation target, while drawing on the broader synthetic-data-generation literature (self-play, automated benchmark synthesis) for its core methodology. It departs from prior MAS error-attribution resources (Who&When's 184 errors, MASFT's ~150 tasks, TRAIL's 148 traces) by replacing costly manual annotation with a programmatic injection pipeline that scales to 9,533 trajectories across six MAS frameworks. The learning side combines standard SFT, GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), and a novel Disentangled Contrastive Learning formulation that repurposes Multiple Instance Learning attention (Ilse et al., 2018) and supervised contrastive loss (Khosla et al., 2020) for the compositional agent-error attribution structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Aegis addresses the critical data-scarcity bottleneck that has kept MAS error attribution at a handful of hundred-scale benchmarks, and the resulting dataset is likely to become the standard training/evaluation resource for this rapidly growing subfield. The finding that a 14B fine-tuned model outperforms proprietary models an order of magnitude larger (26.51 vs. o3's 20.24) validates automated synthesis as a viable path to task-specific reliability, though absolute scores remain low, indicating the task is far from solved. Key limitations include reliance on a predefined taxonomy and controlled injection plans, which may not capture emergent or naturally-occurring failure cascades; the paper itself flags self-repairing agentic systems and more complex failure modes as the next frontier.
