## Problem

Existing instruction tuning (IT) data selection methods rely on external LLMs for scoring, model uncertainty estimates, or gradient-based influence functions, making them computationally expensive, non-interpretable, and unable to target specific domain capabilities. The paper asks whether the internal neuron activation patterns of an LLM can serve as a principled, interpretable, and cost-effective signal for selecting IT data that enhances particular capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261373/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small in-domain dataset (e.g., 1,531 MMLU samples or 7,473 GSM samples) that exemplifies the target capability, a large open IT dataset (e.g., 52k Alpaca-GPT4 instruction-response pairs), and a base LLM (e.g., LLaMA-2-7b). It produces per-layer neuron activation direction vectors (one PCA principal component per decoder layer) and a ranked subset of IT samples (top-k by activation alignment score). The in-domain data is used only for activation capture, not for fine-tuning, and the selected IT subset is then used for standard supervised fine-tuning.

## Method

NAIT operates in two stages. First, for each in-domain sample, it records neuron activations across all decoder layers and computes the activation difference between the first and last token; PCA is then applied to these difference vectors to extract a single direction vector per layer, with sign calibrated to align with the mean activation trend. Second, each candidate IT sample is scored by projecting its per-layer activations onto the extracted direction vectors and summing across layers, and the top-k highest-scoring samples are selected for fine-tuning. The core hypothesis is that samples whose neuron activation patterns more closely match the target capability's activation signature will more effectively enhance that capability. The approach is gradient-free, requires no external models, and uses only a forward pass through the LLM to compute activations, making it substantially cheaper than gradient-based or LLM-as-judge alternatives.

## Results

Training LLaMA-2-7b on only 10% of Alpaca-GPT4 selected by NAIT (all capability features, System 12) achieves 37.20% average across five task domains, a +3.24% improvement over full Alpaca-GPT4 fine-tuning (36.03%). The best single-capability variant (GSM-guided selection, System 08) reaches 37.70% average (+4.65%), with particularly strong gains on mathematical reasoning (GSM: 16.00 vs. 14.63 baseline) and multilingual understanding (TyDiQA: 46.54 vs. 39.48). NAIT also demonstrates cross-model robustness, achieving +21.92% on Mistral-7b and +18.65% on LLaMA-3-8b over their respective Alpaca-GPT4 baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAIT (all capabilities) outperforms SelectIT (37.20% vs. 37.16% AVG) by a narrow margin, while decisively beating AlpaGasus (35.18%), Q2Q (35.68%), and Random selection (35.69%) by 1.5–2.0 percentage points. Against LIMA (36.17%), NAIT gains approximately 1.0% on average. In targeted-ability comparisons (Appendix E, Table 9), NAIT achieves 41.31% AVG on the MMLU-targeted setting versus LESS's 39.94%, with LESS winning only on the target task itself (48.12 vs. 47.81 on MMLU) but suffering significant generalization drops on non-target tasks. The GSM-guided NAIT variant (37.70%) is the strongest single-capability configuration, surpassing even the multi-capability NAIT (37.20%) and all baselines. NAIT also demonstrates superior cost efficiency, requiring only 1.32 hours and $1.52 versus 19.07 hours and $178.02 for AlpaGasus on the same 52k-sample dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAIT repurposes neuron activation analysis—previously used for interpretability (Voita et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024c; Durrani et al., 2020)—as a data selection mechanism for instruction tuning, a paradigm shift from existing methods that rely on perplexity, uncertainty, LLM-as-scorer, or gradient signals. It builds on the LIMA finding that small curated subsets suffice, but departs from all prior selection strategies by grounding the selection criterion in the model's internal activation geometry rather than external or surface-level features. The use of PCA to extract a single principal direction per layer as a 'capability fingerprint' is a novel application of dimensionality reduction in this context, and the paper explicitly positions itself as the first IT data selection framework based on neuron activation patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NAIT offers a practical, interpretable, and 19× cheaper alternative to LLM-as-scorer methods for IT data selection, with the added benefit of targeting specific capabilities rather than optimizing generic quality. The discovery of a stable ~59% core data subset that universally activates fundamental model capabilities, and the finding that logical-reasoning and programmatic activation features transfer most broadly, provide actionable insights for practitioners curating training data. However, the evaluation is limited to models under 20B parameters and a fixed set of five capability domains, leaving open whether the activation-direction approach scales to much larger models or generalizes to modalities beyond text. The modest 3–5% average gains, while consistent, suggest the method's value lies more in interpretability and cost efficiency than in raw performance breakthroughs.
