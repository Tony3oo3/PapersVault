## Problem

Existing tensor-based incomplete multi-view clustering methods either require costly post-processing (e.g., SVD) on coefficient matrices to extract cluster-ready embeddings, or avoid post-processing by using latent embedding tensors but lack connectivity constraints that preserve the geometric graph structure. Adding such connectivity constraints via a standard Laplacian introduces O(n²) overhead from degree matrix computation, creating a fundamental tension between structural fidelity and computational efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete multi-view feature matrices {X^v ∈ R^{d_v × n}} where a fraction r of samples are randomly missing per view (with at least one view guaranteed per sample), along with the cluster count c and anchor number t. During optimization, missing entries are imputed via X^v_{:,mis} = (A^v Z^v)_{:,mis}. The output is a set of view-specific latent embedding matrices H^v ∈ R^{n×c} with orthonormal columns, which are concatenated and fed directly into k-means—no SVD or spectral decomposition is needed to derive cluster labels.

## Method

CAMEL jointly optimizes anchor matrices A^v, coefficient matrices Z^v, reconstruction errors E^v, and latent embeddings H^v under an ADMM framework. The key architectural innovation is replacing the variable degree matrix D^v in the normalized Laplacian with a constant-degree matrix D = βI, simplifying the Laplacian to L^v = I − (1/β)(Z^v)^T Z^v and reducing degree matrix construction from O(n²) to O(1). The view-specific embeddings H^v are organized into a third-order tensor H and regularized with a non-convex ℓ_δ-norm (a tighter surrogate than the tensor nuclear norm) to capture high-order cross-view correlations. The H^v subproblem is solved on the Stiefel manifold via a generalized power iteration that exploits the constant-degree structure to compute (Z^v)^T(Z^v H^v) in O(tcn) rather than explicitly forming the n×n adjacency matrix. Convergence to a KKT stationary point is theoretically guaranteed, and the overall per-iteration complexity is O(mtn log n + d_max·tn).

## Results

At the default missing rate of 0.5, CAMEL achieves 97.14% ACC on MSRCV1, 95.42% ACC on Scene, 74.78% ACC on Still-DB, and 93.96% ACC on CIFAR10, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline SCSL (67.14%, 19.57%, 29.94%, and N/A respectively). On Mfeat, CAMEL reaches 96.35% ACC / 96.79% NMI / 97.29% PUR, matching SCSL. The method maintains stable performance as the missing rate increases from 0.1 to 0.5, with ACC dropping by less than 10 percentage points on most datasets, while baselines degrade sharply (e.g., SCSL on Yale3 drops from 63.03% to 32.12%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAMEL decisively outperforms all six baselines on 8 out of 9 datasets at the default 0.5 missing rate, with the largest margins on MSRCV1 (97.14% vs. 67.14% ACC, a 30-point gap over SCSL), Scene (95.42% vs. 19.57% ACC over SCSL), and Still-DB (74.78% vs. 29.94% ACC over IMVC-CBG). The only exception is Mfeat, where CAMEL ties SCSL exactly (96.35/96.79/97.29). Among the traditional baselines, PVC is generally the strongest (e.g., 40.44% ACC on Yale3), while BSV and Concat are consistently the weakest. The recent matrix-based methods IMVC-CBG and PSIMVC-PG perform poorly under high missing rates, often dropping below 20% ACC. On CIFAR10 (50,000 samples), SCSL runs out of memory while CAMEL completes in ~51 seconds with 93.96% ACC, highlighting the scalability advantage of the constant-degree matrix. The runtime comparison (Table 3) further shows CAMEL is 30–300× faster than its variable-degree variant CAMEL-v1 on large datasets (e.g., Scene15: 187s vs. 3909s), confirming the O(1) degree-matrix construction benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAMEL positions itself within the post-processing-free branch of tensor-based incomplete multi-view clustering, where latent embeddings are directly used for k-means rather than requiring SVD on coefficient matrices. It departs from prior post-processing-free methods (Xu et al. 2025, Liu et al. 2025) by introducing view-specific connectivity constraints via a Laplacian built on a constant-degree matrix (βI), which had not been explored in this context. The joint optimization of connectivity and ℓ_δ-norm tensor low-rank constraints on the embedding tensor H (rather than on the anchor coefficient tensor Z) unifies two previously decoupled objectives—graph structure preservation and high-order cross-view correlation capture—within a single ADMM framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constant-degree matrix approximation is a practical and elegant efficiency trick that reduces the dominant O(n²) bottleneck to O(1), enabling CAMEL to handle 50K-sample datasets (CIFAR10) where the variable-degree variant runs out of memory. The ablation studies confirm that connectivity and tensor constraints are genuinely complementary, not redundant. However, the relatively high standard deviations on several datasets (e.g., Yale3: ±4.90 ACC, EYaleB10: ±5.42 ACC) reveal that the constant-degree approximation introduces sensitivity to the hyperparameter β, and whether an adaptive or learned degree schedule could close this gap remains an open question. The framework is modular in its tensor rank surrogate, suggesting extensibility to other non-convex penalties without architectural changes.
