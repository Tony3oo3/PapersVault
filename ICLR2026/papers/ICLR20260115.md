## Problem

RAG pipelines face a fundamental trade-off in context compression: hard methods (e.g., Provence, RECOMP) operate online and query-aware but achieve only ~2× compression, while soft methods (e.g., PISCO, COCOM) reach ~16× compression but require expensive offline LLM-sized forward passes and ignore the query at compression time. No existing method combines high compression rates with query-dependent online operation, leaving a gap for efficient, accurate RAG at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260115/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, OSCAR takes a natural-language query and a set of retrieved 128-token document chunks, and produces (i) a small set of query-dependent embedding vectors per document (e.g., 8 memory-token hidden states for 16× compression), (ii) a generated answer from the downstream LLM, and optionally (iii) a relevance score per document for reranking. The compressed embeddings replace the full document text in the generator's RAG prompt, reducing the generator's input from k×128 tokens to k×l tokens. Unlike prior soft compression, the embedding for each document is computed jointly with the query at inference time rather than pre-computed offline.

## Method

OSCAR introduces a lightweight compressor LLM that maps each (query, document) pair to l learnable memory-token hidden states, making the compressed representation query-dependent. Two compressor architectures are proposed: OSCAR-N-Layers uses the first N layers (typically 1/4–1/3) of the generator backbone as a headless transformer requiring no pretraining, while OSCAR-llama uses a smaller Llama-1B with two dense projection layers to align hidden spaces, necessitating autoencoding/text-continuation pretraining. Training uses sequence-level distillation: a Mistral-7B teacher generates answers from the uncompressed RAG pipeline, and the compressor–generator pair is jointly fine-tuned (LoRA on the generator, full fine-tuning on the compressor) to match those answers. An optional [RR] token with a point-wise distillation loss from a DeBERTa-v3 reranker enables simultaneous compression and reranking in a single forward pass, making compression essentially free in pipelines that already include a reranker.

## Results

OSCAR achieves 2–5× end-to-end inference speed-up over no-compression RAG while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy across LLMs from 1B to 24B parameters. For the Mistral-7B backbone, OSCAR-llama attains 0.68 average accuracy (matching no-compression) at 6.15 TFLOPs (3.3× speed-up), outperforming hard-compression baselines RECOMP (0.67, 2.5×) and Provence (0.68, 2.2×). For Mistral-24B, OSCAR-llama improves accuracy to 0.69 (vs. 0.68 no-compression) while reducing FLOPs from 64.29 to 13.37 (4.8× speed-up). Ablations confirm that query-dependent compression is critical: removing the query at compression time causes a 4–6% accuracy drop at ×16 compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the hard-compression baseline RECOMP (extractive, sentence-embedding-based pruning), OSCAR-llama matches or exceeds accuracy on every backbone (e.g., 0.68 vs. 0.67 average for Mistral-7B) while being 31% more efficient (6.15 vs. 8.13 TFLOPs). Against Provence (query-dependent DeBERTa pruning with simultaneous reranking), OSCAR-llama ties on average accuracy for Mistral-7B (0.68 vs. 0.68) but is 35% faster (6.15 vs. 9.43 TFLOPs); GPT-4o pairwise comparisons confirm OSCAR is slightly better on average than Provence. Against PISCO (state-of-the-art offline soft compression at ×16), OSCAR-llama achieves higher accuracy (0.68 vs. 0.65 for Mistral-7B) and, unlike PISCO, operates fully online with query-dependent compression; PISCO's 5.8× speed-up is only achievable because compression is precomputed offline. For the largest backbone (Mistral-24B), OSCAR-llama is the only method that simultaneously improves accuracy (0.69 vs. 0.68) and delivers a large speed-up (4.8×), demonstrating that efficiency gains scale with model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSCAR sits at the intersection of two complementary lines of RAG compression research: online hard compression (Provence, RECOMP) which is query-aware but limited to ~2× compression, and offline soft compression (PISCO, COCOM, xRAG) which achieves ~16× compression but requires expensive pre-computation and ignores the query. The paper departs from prior soft compression by making the operation both online and query-conditioned, using learnable memory tokens fed jointly with the query and document into a lightweight compressor (either early layers of the generator or a 1B LLM). The training framework inherits the sequence-level distillation objective from PISCO and the pretraining recipe from COCOM, while the simultaneous reranking capability is inspired by Provence's observation that query-dependent compression and reranking are closely related operations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–5× end-to-end inference speedup with negligible accuracy loss across 1B–24B generators makes OSCAR a practically deployable technique for latency-sensitive RAG systems, and the fact that compression can be folded into an existing reranking forward pass effectively eliminates its marginal cost. A key limitation is backbone-specificity: each generator LLM requires a separately trained OSCAR model, which limits plug-and-play applicability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to longer documents beyond 128 tokens (only briefly explored in Appendix K), whether the pretraining requirement for the OSCAR-llama variant can be eliminated, and how well the method generalizes to non-Wikipedia corpora or multilingual settings.
