## Problem

Most long-chain-of-thought distillation pipelines target English (and to a lesser extent Chinese), leaving open how to build strong reasoning models for mid-resource languages. Translated corpora introduce translation artifacts and poor robustness to everyday colloquial expressions, while reasoning entirely in the target language degrades mathematical and logical capability. This paper addresses the gap by constructing a large native-Korean post-training resource and proposing a supervision scheme that avoids the trade-off between reasoning power and cultural fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes native Korean user-authored prompts (web Q&A, exams, STEM, code) kept verbatim without normalization, filtered to require ≥30% Korean characters and length between 50 and 8,192 characters. The model produces a Language-Mixed CoT reasoning trace in which the Think step code-switches between English (anchor) and Korean (target), preserving Korean named entities, quoted spans, and key terms, with a post-hoc regex filter enforcing a 5–20% Korean-character ratio in the trace. The final answer is emitted in fluent Korean wrapped in \boxed{...}, validated by math-verify.

## Method

The core contribution is Language-Mixed CoT, a supervision format in which the teacher (Qwen3-32B) is prompted to perform most logical scaffolding in English while preserving Korean terms, quotations, and named entities, after which a regex filter discards traces whose Korean-character ratio falls outside 5–20%. This is paired with a large-scale data-curation pipeline: 5.79M native Korean prompts are crawled from 54 legally triaged web Q&A and community sites, responses are regenerated from prompts alone (no oracle leakage), and over 100 ablations identify a 260k high-yield subset (YI-SANG-HQ) composed of OpenThought, Code, Science, and Exams categories. Training is pure SFT (no RL) with loss computed only on reasoning traces and solutions, applied across nine models (4B–35B) from six families. English-anchored Language-Mixed CoT consistently outperforms both English-only and Korean-only baselines, yielding larger gains on reasoning-heavy tasks relative to Korean-only and on cultural-understanding tasks relative to English-only, while also producing cross-lingual and multi-modal free-lunch effects.

## Results

KO-REAson-35B, trained on YI-SANG-HQ, achieves the highest overall average score of 64.0 ± 2.5 across nine Korean benchmarks, ranking first on 5/9 tasks (KMMLU-Redux 76.0, KMMLU-Hard 51.4, Math-Ko 87.5, HAE-RAE Bench 78.9, CLIcK 70.9) and second on the remainder. Smaller models (4B–14B) also benefit substantially, with an average improvement of +18.6 points across the nine benchmarks. Cross-lingual evaluation on English benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, GPQA) shows consistent gains for all nine trained models despite training exclusively on Korean text, and multimodal evaluation on Korean vision benchmarks confirms a selective 'multi-modal free lunch' on reasoning-heavy tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-OSS-20B (58.8 avg), KO-REAson-35B leads by +5.2 points overall, winning on KMMLU-Redux (+8.4), KMMLU-Hard (+12.4), Math-Ko (+4.7), and HAE-RAE Bench (+13.8), but trails on competition math: AIME2024-Ko (66.7 vs 71.1) and KSM (65.7 vs 72.1), which the authors attribute to limited competition-style data in their mixture. Against QwQ-32B (59.6 avg), KO-REAson-35B gains +4.4 points, winning on KMMLU-Redux, KMMLU-Hard, Math-Ko, and HAE-RAE Bench, tying on CLIcK (70.9), but losing on KMMLU-Pro (47.4 vs 51.0) and KoBALT-700 (34.9 vs 37.7). Against DeepSeek-R1-32B (56.4 avg) and EXAONE-Deep-32B (57.4 avg), KO-REAson-35B leads by +7.6 and +6.6 points respectively, winning on 7 of 9 benchmarks in both cases. The only benchmark where KO-REAson-35B does not place in the top two is KMMLU-Pro, where QwQ-32B leads by 3.6 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long-CoT distillation pipeline popularized by DeepSeek-R1, Open-R1, and S1 to a mid-resource language (Korean), departing from the near-exclusive focus on English and Chinese in prior distillation efforts. It explicitly rejects two common prior approaches—translated corpora (which introduce translation artifacts and poor colloquial coverage) and monolingual CoT supervision (which either loses cultural fidelity in English or degrades reasoning in Korean-only traces)—and instead introduces Language-Mixed CoT, a code-switching supervision signal that anchors logical scaffolding in English while preserving Korean semantics. The SFT-over-RL choice is motivated by the scarcity of strong Korean base models needed for RL cold-start, aligning with the observation that successful RLVR requires ≥30B-parameter seeds with solid math priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale, publicly documented post-training recipe for a mid-resource language, releasing YI-SANG (5.79M prompts, 3.7M traces) and the KO-REAson model series under open licenses, which should lower the barrier for other mid-resource communities to build competitive reasoning models. The cross-lingual and multi-modal 'free lunch' observations—English math benchmarks and Korean vision-language tasks improving from Korean-only text supervision—suggest that Language-Mixed CoT may be a broadly applicable technique beyond Korean. Key limitations include trailing performance on competition-level math (attributed to limited translated Olympiad data), validation in only one target language, and reliance on SFT without subsequent RL, leaving open whether the recipe composes well with reinforcement learning at scale.
