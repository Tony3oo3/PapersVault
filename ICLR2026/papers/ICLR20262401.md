## Problem

Existing 3D style transfer methods either require costly per-scene optimization (NeRF- and 3DGS-based approaches) or, in the case of the closest feed-forward work Styl3R, target only 2–8 views without explicitly enforcing strong multi-view consistency. No prior method achieves single-forward, pose-free 3D stylization that generalizes zero-shot to unseen categories, scenes, and styles while maintaining geometry-aware, cross-view coherent appearance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262401/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Stylos takes N unposed RGB content views (N ≥ 1, up to hundreds; longest side 448 px) and a single style reference image as input, requiring no camera poses or calibration. It outputs a set of M anisotropic 3D Gaussian primitives (position, opacity, orientation quaternion, anisotropic scale, and degree-k spherical-harmonic color coefficients) together with per-view camera intrinsics and extrinsics, all produced in a single forward pass (~0.05 s on an NVIDIA GH200). The Gaussian set is voxelized (0.002 m grid) with confidence-aware fusion to reduce redundancy before differentiable rendering.

## Method

Stylos uses a shared VGGT-based Transformer backbone with two pathways: geometry predictions (position, scale, orientation, opacity) are derived from self-attention-only features via a DPT head, while style is injected through CrossBlocks that insert a cross-attention layer (content tokens as queries, style tokens as keys/values) between self-attention and MLP stages. The Global CrossBlock variant—concatenating all views before cross-attention—outperforms frame-level and hybrid designs by enabling multi-view geometric consistency during style conditioning. A key novelty is a voxel-level 3D style loss that differentially unprojects multi-view VGG features into a shared 3D voxel grid and matches batch-normalization statistics (mean and std) against the style image's VGG statistics, explicitly encoding geometry into the style objective. Training is two-stage: geometry pretraining with a frozen VGGT teacher for pose/depth supervision, followed by stylization fine-tuning that freezes all geometry modules and updates only the Style Aggregator and color head, with auxiliary CLIP and total-variation losses.

## Results

On the Tanks & Temples benchmark, Stylos achieves the best (lowest) short-range and long-range LPIPS and RMSE across all four scenes (Train, Truck, M60, Garden), e.g., 0.030/0.026 short-range LPIPS/RMSE on Train vs. 0.033/0.038 for StyleGaussian and 0.042/0.052 for G-Style. In terms of stylization quality, Stylos attains ArtScore values of 9.34–9.70, competitive with G-Style (8.98–9.73) while running at 0.05 s per scene—3× faster than Styl3R (0.16 s) and ~294× faster than G-Style (14.7 min). ArtFID scores (26.40–28.71) are slightly higher than G-Style (22.15–25.76) but substantially lower than StyleGaussian (41.14–52.79) and SGSST (32.34–38.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stylos outperforms all five baselines on every consistency metric (short- and long-range LPIPS and RMSE) across all four Tanks & Temples scenes, with the largest margins over SGSST (e.g., 0.047 vs. 0.084 short-range LPIPS on Garden) and Styl3R (0.047 vs. 0.105 on Garden). Against G-Style, the strongest per-scene-fitting competitor, Stylos trades a small ArtFID gap (~3–6 points higher) for a ~294× speedup (0.05 s vs. 14.7 min) while matching or exceeding its ArtScore on three of four scenes. Compared to the other feed-forward method Styl3R, Stylos improves ArtScore by 5–6 points (e.g., 9.70 vs. 2.94 on Truck) and runs 3× faster, while also achieving substantially better long-range consistency (0.074 vs. 0.116 LPIPS on Truck). StyleGaussian, the zero-shot 3DGS baseline, is dominated by Stylos on both consistency and quality (ArtScore 9.50 vs. 0.78 on Train) and is 3300× slower. StylizedGS is excluded from the main tables due to frequent failure cases (pure-color outputs) but in supplementary metrics (Table 5–6) Stylos still achieves lower FID and LPIPS on most scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Stylos builds directly on the VGGT backbone for pose-free geometric reasoning and the AnySplat pipeline for feed-forward Gaussian splatting with voxelization, while adapting the CrossBlock design from StyleTr2 to inject style via cross-attention between self-attention and MLP stages. It departs from prior 3D stylization work (StyleGaussian, G-Style, StylizedGS, SGSST) by eliminating per-scene optimization entirely, and from the closest feed-forward competitor Styl3R by targeting strong multi-view consistency at scale (up to hundreds of views) rather than 2–8 views. The key conceptual departure is the voxel-level 3D style loss, which extends AdaIN-style statistics matching from 2D image or concatenated-feature space into a fused 3D voxel grid, explicitly encoding geometric structure into the style objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.05 s single-forward inference (versus 14–165 minutes for per-scene baselines) makes real-time 3D stylization practically viable for AR/VR and interactive content creation, and the zero-shot generalization to unseen categories, scenes, and styles removes the need for per-scene fitting pipelines. The voxel-level style loss offers a reusable technique for enforcing cross-view coherence in any 3D generative or editing task. Open questions include how the method scales to higher resolutions, how it handles scenes with dense high-frequency geometry (foliage, thin structures), and whether the VGGT backbone's degradation at large view counts can be mitigated without sacrificing the single-forward guarantee.
