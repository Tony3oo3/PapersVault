## Problem

Prior unified motion-language models face two compounding bottlenecks: (1) discretizing motion into VQ codebook tokens introduces quantization error that attenuates high-frequency kinematics and degrades semantic-physical consistency, and (2) forcing both text and motion through a single-stream Transformer backbone causes gradient interference and loss-scale mismatch that destabilizes training and erodes language competence. MotionGPT3 addresses both by replacing discrete motion tokens with continuous VAE latents and by separating the two modalities into parallel branches that interact only through shared attention.

## Input / Output

For text-to-motion, the model takes a natural language prompt and produces a 3D human motion sequence in the 263-dim HumanML3D pose format (joint velocities, positions, rotations, and foot-contact signals); for motion-to-text, it takes a motion sequence and generates a text caption. Motion is compressed into a single 1×1×256 continuous latent by a pretrained VAE before entering the Transformer, and generation at inference runs 100 diffusion denoising steps conditioned on K placeholder-token hidden states. The full model comprises a 124M GPT-2 text branch (pretrained) and a 114M motion branch (trained from scratch), totaling 238M parameters.

## Method

MotionGPT3 adopts a Mixture-of-Transformers-style dual-stream architecture where a text branch and a motion branch each maintain independent embeddings, feed-forward blocks, and normalization, but share self-attention layers for controlled bidirectional information exchange. Motion is represented as continuous VAE latents rather than discrete tokens, and a lightweight 3-layer MLP diffusion head (DDPM objective, 1000 training / 100 inference steps) bridges the backbone's hidden states to the motion latent space, avoiding the discrete-token assumption of standard next-token prediction. A three-stage generate-then-align training schedule first pretrains the motion branch on text-to-motion with the text branch frozen, then introduces cross-modal alignment objectives (motion-to-text, motion prediction, inbetweening) while still freezing the text branch, and finally unfreezes all parameters for joint instruction fine-tuning. This decoupled optimization reduces cross-modal gradient interference and empirically yields ~2× faster convergence in training loss and up to 4× faster convergence in validation metrics compared to single-stream baselines.

## Results

On HumanML3D text-to-motion, the unified MotionGPT3 achieves R@1=0.553, R@3=0.837, FID=0.208, and MMDist=2.725, outperforming all prior unified systems (MotionGPT, MoTe, MG-MotionLLM, TM2T). The single-task variant MotionGPT3† (200 epochs) reaches R@1=0.533, R@3=0.826, FID=0.239, MMDist=2.797, matching or exceeding generation-only baselines like MoMask and DiverseMotion. On motion-to-text (HumanML3D), MotionGPT3 attains R@1=0.573, R@3=0.864, MMDist=2.426, Bleu@1=59.1, and BertScore=35.2, surpassing all prior unified captioners. On KIT-ML, MotionGPT3† leads with R@1=0.456 and R@3=0.803, exceeding the previous best (MoGenTS: 0.445/0.797).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On HumanML3D T2M, MotionGPT3† (single-task) outperforms all generation-only baselines on R@1 (0.533 vs. MoMask 0.521, DiverseMotion 0.515, T2M-GPT 0.491) and MMDist (2.797 vs. 2.941–3.118), though its FID (0.239) is higher than MoMask (0.045) and DiverseMotion (0.072). The unified MotionGPT3 dominates all prior unified systems: it beats MoTe (R@1 0.553 vs. 0.548, MMDist 2.725 vs. 2.867), MG-MotionLLM (0.553 vs. 0.516), MotionGPT (0.553 vs. 0.492), and TM2T (0.553 vs. 0.424) on R@1, with the largest gap over TM2T. On KIT-ML, MotionGPT3† leads all baselines on R@1 (0.456 vs. MoGenTS 0.445) and R@3 (0.803 vs. 0.797), with the best MMDist (2.704). For motion-to-text, MotionGPT3 surpasses MoTe on MMDist (2.426 vs. 2.649), Bleu@1 (59.1 vs. 46.7), Bleu@4 (19.4 vs. 11.2), and BertScore (35.2 vs. 30.3), though MoTe retains a slight edge on R@3 (0.871 vs. 0.864). Under the TMR retrieval evaluator, MotionGPT3 achieves substantially stronger cross-modal alignment than MotionGPT (e.g., R@1 9.60 vs. 7.16 on the 'All' protocol; 74.25 vs. 58.07 on 'Small batches'), confirming that the gains are not evaluator-specific overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionGPT3 sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) latent diffusion for motion generation (MLD, Xin et al. 2023; LDM, Rombach et al. 2022), which it adopts for the motion representation and generation head; (2) Mixture-of-Transformers (Liang et al. 2024), whose modality-specific branches with shared attention it directly instantiates as a text branch and a motion branch; and (3) unified motion-language models (MotionGPT, MoTe, MG-MotionLLM), which it departs from by replacing VQ-VAE tokenization with continuous VAE latents and replacing single-stream backbones with a dual-stream architecture. The key conceptual departure is treating motion as a genuinely separate modality with its own continuous latent space and diffusion-based generation, rather than forcing it into the discrete token vocabulary of an LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear, well-ablated argument that both continuous latents and dual-stream architecture are necessary for high-quality unified motion-language modeling, with the 2× convergence speedup and strong TMR retrieval results (far exceeding T2M-evaluator scores) suggesting genuine cross-modal alignment rather than evaluator overfitting. However, the single-latent-per-sequence design limits segment-level composition and fine-grained directional control, and generalization is constrained by the relatively small HumanML3D/KIT-ML training data. Open questions include whether the approach scales to stronger language backbones (beyond GPT-2), whether hierarchical or segment-wise latents can enable compositional long-horizon generation, and whether the three-stage schedule generalizes to additional modalities beyond text and motion.
