## Problem

Conformal prediction for time series faces two intertwined challenges: the exchangeability assumption is violated by temporal dependencies that induce correlations in non-conformity scores, and most existing methods are limited to univariate outcomes. While multi-dimensional conformal prediction has been studied, no prior method jointly addresses both the exchangeability violation and the construction of flexible prediction sets for multi-dimensional time series outcomes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263873/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base predictor's point prediction ŷ_i, a context vector z_i formed by concatenating the past w features and prediction residuals, and a pre-specified significance level α. It outputs a multi-dimensional prediction set Ĉ_i(z_i, α) in ℝ^{d_y} whose shape is determined by the learned flow transformation rather than a fixed geometric form (e.g., ellipsoid or hyper-rectangle). The non-conformity score is the Euclidean norm of the inverse-flow-transformed residual, and the set size is estimated via quasi-Monte Carlo integration of the Jacobian determinant over the source ball.

## Method

FCP uses a guided flow with classifier-free guidance to learn a bijective mapping from an isotropic Gaussian source distribution to the conditional distribution of prediction residuals given past context. A Transformer encoder processes the context vector z_i to produce a guidance embedding h_i, which conditions the vector field via the CFG formulation (Eq. 12), enabling the flow to capture temporal dependencies without requiring exchangeability. The prediction set is constructed by taking a ball B_{1-α} in the source Gaussian space (with radius determined by the chi distribution) and mapping it through the inverse flow ψ^{-1}, yielding a flexible, data-adaptive set shape. Training proceeds via flow matching (Eq. 13) with the encoder jointly optimized, and the log-determinant of the Jacobian is computed by solving an auxiliary ODE (Eq. 16) for set-size estimation. The paper establishes exact non-asymptotic marginal coverage via probability mass preservation under push-forward, and finite-sample conditional coverage bounds under both i.i.d. and stationary strongly mixing non-conformity scores.

## Results

On three real-world multi-dimensional time series datasets (wind, traffic, solar) at a target coverage of 0.95, FCP consistently achieves the smallest prediction set sizes among all baselines while maintaining target coverage. For example, on the wind dataset with a LOO Bootstrap base predictor, FCP achieves prediction set sizes of 0.88 (d_y=2), 3.43 (d_y=4), and 19.4 (d_y=8), compared to MultiDimSPCI's 1.31, 6.39, and 22.3–42.3, and copula-based methods producing sets hundreds to thousands of times larger. The advantage of FCP grows substantially with outcome dimension, and FCP maintains stable coverage (0.951–0.956) across all d_y values, whereas several baselines exhibit undercoverage (e.g., CONTRA at 0.804–0.939) or severe overcoverage with inflated sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCP outperforms all eight baselines in prediction set size while maintaining the 0.95 target coverage across all three datasets and outcome dimensions. The closest competitors are MultiDimSPCI (Xu et al., 2024) and CP with local ellipsoids (Messoudi et al., 2022), which also capture temporal/local correlations; on the solar dataset, CP with local ellipsoids achieves performance comparable to FCP. OT-CP (Thurin et al., 2025) and CONTRA (Fang et al., 2025) show some ability to go beyond exchangeability but still produce substantially larger sets than FCP, and CONTRA exhibits notable undercoverage (as low as 0.804 at d_y=8). Methods relying on the exchangeability assumption—CopulaCPTS (Sun & Yu, 2022) and copula-based CP (Messoudi et al., 2021)—suffer from severe miscoverage and produce prediction sets orders of magnitude larger (hundreds to 10⁵+). The probabilistic forecasting baselines TFT (Lim et al., 2021) and DeepAR (Salinas et al., 2020), adapted to multi-dimensional settings via independent copulas, also produce much larger sets and less stable coverage. The ablation study confirms that the Transformer encoder is critical: removing it leads to less stable coverage and noticeably larger prediction sets, particularly at higher dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: conformal prediction for distribution-free uncertainty quantification (Vovk et al., 2005), flow matching for generative modeling (Lipman et al., 2022), and classifier-free guidance for conditional generation (Ho & Salimans, 2022). It departs from prior time-series CP methods (Xu & Xie, 2021, 2023; Auer et al., 2024) that are limited to univariate outcomes and rely on exchangeability or bootstrap ensembles, and from multi-dimensional CP methods (Messoudi et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2024; Thurin et al., 2025) that impose fixed geometric shapes (hyper-rectangles, ellipsoids) or solve optimal transport problems. The key conceptual departure is using a learned bijective flow transformation—conditioned on past context via a Transformer encoder and trained with CFG—to simultaneously handle temporal dependence and multi-dimensionality without constraining prediction set geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By jointly addressing exchangeability violation and multi-dimensionality through a single flexible mechanism, this work provides a practical template for conformal prediction in settings where outcomes are vector-valued and data exhibit temporal structure—common in energy, traffic, and environmental monitoring. The theoretical contribution (exact marginal coverage plus finite-sample conditional coverage bounds under both i.i.d. and strongly mixing assumptions) strengthens the statistical foundation beyond the typical exchangeability-based guarantees. Open questions include whether optimal transport coupling (mentioned as future work) can further shrink prediction sets, how the method scales to very high outcome dimensions (beyond d_y=8 tested here), and whether the flow-based approach can be adapted to sequential/online settings where the base predictor is updated over time.
