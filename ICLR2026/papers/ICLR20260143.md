## Problem

Applying foundation models to experimental particle physics is challenging because detector data are sparse, spatially distributed 3D point clouds that lack the sequential or grid structure assumed by language and vision FMs. No established self-supervised objective, neural architecture, or scaling behavior was known for this data regime, and it remained unclear whether frozen pretrained representations could generalize across diverse reconstruction tasks to outperform task-specific baselines.

## Input / Output

The model consumes unordered sets of TPC spacepoints (deposited energy plus x,y,z coordinates, hundreds to thousands per event) that are first transformed to cylindrical-polar (r,φ,η) and serialized into a 1D sequence via a Hierarchical Raster Scan over a 6×8×8 physics-informed grid. Pretraining outputs a 3k-dimensional coordinate prediction per spacepoint (k=30 next-nearest neighbors at larger radius); downstream adapters produce per-spacepoint track IDs (instance segmentation), 5-class particle species labels, and binary noise/signal labels, all from the same frozen 188M-parameter backbone with only 0.74–2.39M trainable adapter parameters.

## Method

The FM uses a Mamba-2 selective state-space backbone (linear-time complexity) where each spacepoint is a token embedded via a two-pathway NeRF-inspired scheme: energy is linearly projected while spatial coordinates receive high-frequency sinusoidal positional encodings, and the two are summed element-wise. A novel Hierarchical Raster Scan serialization partitions the detector volume into 384 physics-informed 3D boxes (radial bins aligned to TPC layer boundaries, angular bins frequency-balanced) and orders them by (r,φ,η) of box centers with intra-box sorting by radius, preserving both global outward propagation and local track continuity. The self-supervised pretraining objective is k-Next-Nearest-Neighbor prediction, which targets geometric neighbors at larger radius rather than the next token in sequence order, thereby decoupling the learning signal from serialization artifacts. Downstream adaptation freezes the FM and attaches lightweight task-specific heads: a panoptic-segmentation-style query decoder with Hungarian matching for track finding, and a linear-projection + single self-attention + MLP classifier for PID and noise tagging. Stable scaling from 0.34M to 188M parameters is enabled by μP-SSM parameterization, and a loss-rescaling strategy re-weights events by average kNN distance to counteract density-dependent difficulty.

## Results

On the TPCPP10M dataset, FM4NPP (m6, 188M pretrained params + 2.39M adapter) achieves 0.945 ARI, 96.1% tracking efficiency, and 93.1% purity on track finding, outperforming all baselines by large margins. For particle identification, the frozen FM with a 0.74M adapter reaches 90.4% accuracy and 76.5% macro recall, surpassing the next-best OneFormer3D (44.95M params) by 13.4 points in accuracy. On noise tagging, FM4NPP attains 97.1% accuracy with 91.9% precision, matching or exceeding OneFormer3D while using 60× fewer trainable parameters. The model also demonstrates clear power-law scaling in validation MSE with respect to model size, data size, and compute budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FM4NPP (m6) decisively outperforms all four baselines across every reported metric. Against Exa.TrkX (3.86M params, the strongest track-finding baseline), FM4NPP gains +6.8 points in ARI (0.945 vs 0.877), +4.3 points in efficiency (96.1% vs 91.8%), and a substantial +26.7 points in purity (93.1% vs 66.4%), while using fewer trainable parameters. Against EggNet (0.16M params), the ARI improvement is +21.9 points (0.945 vs 0.726), and against HEPT (0.31M params), +11.4 points (0.945 vs 0.831). On particle identification, FM4NPP (0.74M adapter) beats OneFormer3D (44.95M params) by +13.4 points in accuracy (0.904 vs 0.770) and +27.5 points in macro recall (0.765 vs 0.490), demonstrating that the pretrained representations carry far richer physics information than task-specific training from scratch. On noise tagging, FM4NPP achieves 97.1% accuracy versus OneFormer3D's 96.5%, with comparable recall (93.7% vs 94.0%) but higher precision (91.9% vs 89.5%), all while using 60× fewer trainable parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the foundation model paradigm from language, vision, and jet physics (OmniJet-α, OmniLearned) to the fundamentally different regime of sparse, low-level detector data in collider experiments. Architecturally, it adopts Mamba-2 as the sequence backbone and integrates μP-SSM for stable scaling across model widths, while the track-finding adapter borrows the query-based instance segmentation formulation from MaskFormer/Mask2Former and adapts it to unordered 3D spacepoints. The key departures from prior NPP tracking work (Exa.TrkX, EggNet, HEPT) are threefold: a novel Hierarchical Raster Scan serialization that preserves both global trajectory structure and local track continuity, a geometry-aware k-Next-Nearest-Neighbor pretraining objective that decouples the learning signal from serialization artifacts, and a scale (188M parameters, 10M+ events) two orders of magnitude beyond prior models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a self-supervised FM can scale with power-law behavior on sparse 3D detector data and, with frozen weights plus a <1M-parameter adapter, outperform purpose-built GNN and Transformer trackers across multiple tasks. The 10M-event TPCPP10M dataset and the three-task benchmark fill a critical gap for the NPP community, which previously lacked a large-scale, open resource for FM evaluation. Key open questions remain: the work is validated only on simulated p+p collisions from a single detector (sPHENIX TPC), the scaling plateau at 188M parameters is unexplained, and generalization to real data, other collision systems (heavy ions), and multi-detector inputs is untested. The finding that a single linear projection suffices to specialize task-agnostic FM representations for diverse downstream tasks suggests a potentially very efficient fine-tuning paradigm for the broader scientific FM landscape.
