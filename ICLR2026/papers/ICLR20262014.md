## Problem

Existing LLM agents for machine learning engineering operate in isolation on a given task, without engaging with the broader research community where human participants share code, discussions, and collective insights. This isolated approach causes agents to converge on repetitive strategies and plateau in performance, and no evaluation framework exists to assess an agent's ability to leverage community knowledge under realistic temporal constraints.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a Kaggle competition environment containing the task description, a cleaned train-test split, a submission grader mimicking official scoring, and a curated set of time-stamped community artifacts (12,951 discussions and 15,733 kernels across 75 competitions) enriched with metadata such as vote counts, public scores, and author tiers; only resources published before the competition deadline are included to prevent data leakage. The agent produces a submission.csv file scored against the official competition metric, with a self-constructed 90/10 train/validation split used for internal performance estimation since test labels and the public leaderboard are strictly withheld. Hardware is constrained to 32 vCPUs and a single A6000 GPU with a 24-hour wall-clock budget per competition.

## Method

CoMind is a five-agent system (Coordinator, Analyzer, Idea Proposer, Coding Agent, Evaluator) that operates in an iterative loop over a simulated community represented as a dependency graph of kernels, datasets, and their inter-resource relationships. Each iteration, the Coordinator samples promising community artifacts, the Analyzer scores them across novelty, feasibility, effectiveness, and efficiency, and the Idea Proposer generates and filters novel solution concepts using persistent memory of historical ideas to avoid redundancy. Multiple Coding Agents then implement distinct solution drafts in parallel using ReAct-style trial-and-error within persistent Jupyter Notebook sessions, while the Evaluator maintains a global leaderboard using a held-out validation split whose labels are hidden from the coding agents. Successful solutions are published back to the community, expanding the knowledge base and enabling increasingly sophisticated strategies over successive iterations, with the system using o4-mini as its backend LLM and 4 parallel coding agents.

## Results

On 75 MLE-Bench Kaggle competitions, CoMind (o4-mini) achieves a 36.00% Any Medal rate, establishing a new state of the art. On eight ongoing live Kaggle competitions, CoMind outperforms 92.6% of human competitors on average, placing in the top 5% on three official leaderboards and the top 1% on one (Playground S5E9, rank 4/1966). In the ablation study on 20 MLE-Bench-Lite competitions, CoMind achieves a 66.8% average win rate versus 51.2% for AIDE, 51.0% for AIDE+RAG, and 46.9% for AIDE+Code.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMind outperforms all six compared baselines on the primary MLE-Bench benchmark. Against R&D-Agent, CoMind achieves 36.00% Any Medal versus 30.22% (o3+GPT-4.1) and 22.40% (o1-preview), a 5.8–13.6 point margin. Against ML-Master (deepseek-r1), CoMind leads by 6.7 points (36.00% vs. 29.30%). Against AIDE, the gap is largest: CoMind's 36.00% exceeds the best AIDE variant (o1-preview, 16.90%) by nearly 20 points, and surpasses AIDE with gpt-4o (8.60%) by over 27 points. OpenHands (gpt-4o) and MLAB (gpt-4o) trail far behind at 5.10% and 1.30% respectively. On the CoBench combinatorial optimization benchmark (9 tasks), CoMind achieves a 0.887 average normalized score, outperforming FunSearch (0.834), AIDE (0.784), and Greedy Refinement (0.793), confirming generalizability beyond Kaggle-style settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLE-Bench evaluation framework (Chan et al., 2025) by introducing MLE-Live, which adds time-stamped community artifacts (kernels, discussions, datasets) to simulate the collaborative dynamics of real Kaggle competitions. Methodologically, CoMind builds on the ReAct-style iterative coding paradigm (Yao et al., 2023) used by AIDE and other prior MLE agents, but departs from the isolated-agent paradigm by introducing a multi-agent architecture (Coordinator, Analyzer, Idea Proposer, parallel Coding Agents, Evaluator) that explicitly ingests and synthesizes external community knowledge through a dependency-graph-based resource management system. The key conceptual departure from prior systems like AIDE, R&D-Agent, ML-Master, and Neo is the shift from closed-world, self-contained exploration to open-world, community-augmented iterative refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MLE-Live is the first benchmark to evaluate ML agents under realistic community-knowledge constraints, filling a gap where prior evaluations treated agents as isolated solvers and ignored the collaborative dynamics that drive top Kaggle performance. The live-competition results (top 5% on three leaderboards, top 1% on one) provide rare evidence that LLM agents can compete at human-expert levels in ongoing, evolving settings rather than just retrospective benchmarks. However, the live evaluation covers only eight competitions with a single backend model (o4-mini), and the MLE-Bench improvement over the prior SOTA (Neo, 34.22%) is modest at 36.00%, leaving open whether the community-knowledge advantage generalizes across model families, scales, and non-Kaggle domains such as scientific discovery or robotics.
