## Problem

Guidance signals in machine learning—teacher logits in knowledge distillation, pseudo-labels in semi-supervised learning, and imitation policies in reinforcement learning—can become noisy or misleading under domain shift, limited labeled data, or distributional generalization gaps. Existing approaches either blindly trust such guidance or rely on heuristic uncertainty estimates (entropy, MSP, MC dropout) that are overconfident and poorly calibrated under distribution shift. Conformal prediction, which provides distribution-free coverage guarantees, has been applied almost exclusively for post-hoc calibration rather than to inform real-time training dynamics, leaving a gap in principled, adaptive guidance weighting during learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a guidance signal (teacher logits, pseudo-labels, or IL policy action distribution) together with a held-out calibration set drawn from the target domain, and produces an adaptive scalar weight w(x) ∈ [0,1] that reweights the guidance loss per sample. In the supervised and semi-supervised settings, inputs are RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/STL, 455×256 for SullyChen) and the calibration set is a 10% split of the target-domain training data; in the RL setting, inputs are discrete state coordinates and the calibration set is a sliding window of N=1000 state-action pairs collected via rollouts. Outputs are task-specific: class labels (100-class CIFAR-100, 10-class CIFAR-10), discrete navigation actions (left/right/up/down/stay), or continuous steer angles.

## Method

AdaConG embeds split conformal prediction directly into the training loop to adaptively modulate guidance influence. For each guidance signal, a nonconformity score is computed (e.g., 1−p_ŷ(x) for classification, −log π(a|s) for policies), and a quantile q_{1−α} is estimated from a calibration set to construct a prediction set C(x); the prediction set size is mapped to an uncertainty score u(x) ∈ [0,1] and then to a monotonically decreasing weight w(x) = exp(−κu(x)). The total loss becomes L = λ_task·L_task + w(x)·λ_guide·L_guide, so high-uncertainty guidance is automatically down-weighted while low-uncertainty guidance retains full influence. In the RL setting, the method extends to adaptive conformal inference with a sliding-window calibration set for the evolving RL policy, and the weight w(s) = exp(−u_I)/(exp(−u_I)+exp(−u_R)) simultaneously governs both the KL-regularization strength and the action-sampling probability between the IL and RL policies. The key distinction from prior work is that CP provides distribution-free, model-agnostic uncertainty that remains valid under covariate shift, unlike softmax-based heuristics that degrade precisely when guidance is most unreliable.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 under domain shift (Gaussian noise σ=0.05), AdaConG applied to KD recovers student accuracy from 57.23% (KD alone, below the 66.51% from-scratch baseline) to 66.53% for ResNet-110→ResNet-20, a +9.30% gain, with the largest improvement of +10.89% on WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2. In semi-supervised learning, FixMatch+AdaConG achieves up to +5.98% Top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10 (40 labels), and FlexMatch+AdaConG gains up to +5.63% on CIFAR-100 (2500 labels). In gridworld navigation (Lava 2, a domain-shifted environment), AdaConG achieves over 6× higher cumulative rewards than the best baseline (IBRL) after convergence. For autonomous driving steer prediction on SullyChen, AdaConG improves mean accuracy by 3.1–3.8% across all KD methods (e.g., KD: 73.5%→76.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaConG consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across every task. In knowledge distillation (Table 1), KD+AdaConG surpasses the from-scratch student (66.53% vs 66.51% for ResNet-110→ResNet20) while standard KD degrades to 57.23%; it also beats uncertainty-aware baselines EA-KD (66.30%), PTLoss (65.96%), MC Dropout (63.59%), MSP (60.71%), and Entropy (60.24%) by 0.2–6.3%. For semi-supervised learning, FixMatch+AdaConG gains +5.98% over FixMatch on CIFAR-10 (40 labels) and FlexMatch+AdaConG gains +5.63% on CIFAR-100 (2500 labels), outperforming UDA by a wider margin. In gridworld navigation, AdaConG achieves over 6× higher rewards than IBRL and Soft IBRL in the shifted Lava 2 environment, and also outperforms pure SAC and the QDagger baseline (Appendix A.4.3), which regularizes toward the unreliable IL policy. In autonomous driving, AdaConG improves all four KD methods (KD, FitNet, PKT, FT) by 3.1–3.8% mAcc, with the student+AdaConG (76.8%) approaching the multi-modal teacher (78.5%) while the student from scratch only reaches 71.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaConG builds on split conformal prediction (Angelopoulos & Bates, 2021) and the RAPS algorithm (Angelopoulos et al., 2020) for classification, extending them from their traditional role as post-hoc calibration tools into an in-training adaptive weighting mechanism. It also draws on adaptive conformal inference (Gibbs & Candès, 2021) to maintain a dynamic calibration set for the evolving RL policy. The key departure from prior uncertainty-aware guidance methods (EA-KD, PTLoss, MC dropout, entropy-based reweighting) is the use of distribution-free, model-agnostic CP prediction set sizes rather than heuristic softmax-based or Monte Carlo uncertainty estimates, which the paper argues are overconfident and poorly calibrated under distribution shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a simple, drop-in module that makes any guidance-based learning pipeline robust to imperfect supervision, validated across three distinct paradigms (KD, SSL, RL) with minimal computational overhead (~0.003 ms/sample). A notable open question is the reliance on a held-out calibration set representative of the target domain, which may be costly or infeasible in streaming or fully unsupervised settings. The authors also identify extension to ambiguous or imprecise ground truth (via credal sets and imprecise probabilities) as a natural next step, suggesting the current framework's assumption of well-defined labels is a meaningful limitation.
