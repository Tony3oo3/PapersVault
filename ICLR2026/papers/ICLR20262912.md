## Problem

Aligning speech synthesis with human perception of naturalness is hindered by the absence of a large-scale human preference dataset, a dedicated evaluation benchmark, and an effective reward model for this specific dimension. Existing objective metrics (WER, FAD, MOS predictors) and even strong AudioLLMs achieve less than 70% agreement with human naturalness judgments, revealing a significant gap between automated assessment and human perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262912/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a target text and a pair of synthesized speech samples (generated by zero-shot TTS models across regular and expressive styles in Chinese, English, and code-switching) and produces a naturalness preference judgment (which audio is more natural) accompanied by a chain-of-thought rationale analyzing prosody, pacing, articulation, and overall naturalness. The output is a free-form generative text response, enabling inference-time scaling via majority voting over multiple sampled outputs rather than a single scalar score.

## Method

SpeechJudge-GRM is built on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B and trained via a two-stage post-training process. In the SFT stage, Gemini-2.5-Flash serves as a teacher to generate CoT rationales on 25K samples where its judgment matches human preference, providing a 'cold start' that improves the base model's instruction-following and multi-audio reasoning. In the RL stage, the model is further trained with DAPO (an enhanced GRPO variant) on the 17K remaining challenging samples where Gemini-2.5-Flash failed, using human preference labels as a verifiable accuracy-based reward (+1 correct, −1 incorrect). Unlike a classic Bradley-Terry reward model that outputs a single scalar, the generative formulation enables CoT explainability and inference-time scaling via majority voting over 10 sampled outputs, improving accuracy from 77.2% to 79.4% on the SpeechJudge-Eval benchmark.
![Method overview](../imgs/ICLR20262912/cropped_page6_idx5.webp)

## Results

On the SpeechJudge-Eval benchmark (1,000 pairs), SpeechJudge-GRM (SFT+RL) achieves 77.2% accuracy in predicting human naturalness preferences, improving to 79.4% with inference-time scaling (majority voting over 10 outputs). This outperforms the classic Bradley-Terry reward model (SpeechJudge-BTRM) at 72.7%, the base Qwen2.5-Omni-7B at 60.6%, and the strongest closed-source AudioLLM Gemini-2.5-Flash at 69.1%. The SFT stage alone lifts accuracy from 60.6% to 75.3%, and the subsequent GRPO-based RL stage adds another ~2 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpeechJudge-GRM (SFT+RL) at 77.2% (79.4% with voting) outperforms all 17 baselines evaluated on SpeechJudge-Eval. Among objective metrics, WER (57.9%), SIM (44.5%), and FAD (48.6%) all fall well below the proposed method, with SIM and FAD performing near random chance. MOS predictors (DNSMOS 57.9%, UTMOS 53.7%, audiobox-aesthetics 44.9–60.8%) and deepfake detectors (AASIST 46.7%, ADV 38.3%) are similarly outperformed, confirming that these proxy metrics do not align well with holistic naturalness perception. Among open-source AudioLLMs, Kimi-Audio-7B-Instruct (67.0%) is the strongest, followed by MiDashengLM (61.6%) and Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (60.6%), while Gemma-3n (48.2%) and MiMo-Audio (54.1%) perform at or near chance level. Among closed-source models, Gemini-2.5-Flash (69.1%) leads, followed by GPT-4o Audio (67.4%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (66.5%), with GPT-4o mini Audio (50.5%) performing poorly. The proposed GRM surpasses the best AudioLLM (Gemini-2.5-Flash) by 8.1 points and the BTRM baseline by 4.5 points, demonstrating that the two-stage SFT+RL training with CoT rationales and verifiable-reward RL is critical for closing the gap to human-level naturalness judgment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the Generative Reward Model (GRM) paradigm—reward modeling as next-token prediction with chain-of-thought rationales, established for text LLMs by Zhang et al. (2025a) and Liu et al. (2025c)—into the speech domain, using GRPO/DAPO (Shao et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025) for the RL stage. It departs from prior speech quality datasets (MOS-based pointwise corpora like VoiceMOS, or attribute-specific sets like QualiSpeech) by constructing the first large-scale pairwise naturalness preference corpus (99K pairs) with multi-annotator agreement analysis. The two-stage SFT-then-RLVR training pipeline is adapted from DeepSeek-R1-style verifiable-reward RL, but applied to an AudioLLM backbone (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B) with Gemini-2.5-Flash as a CoT teacher, a configuration not previously explored for speech judgment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 99K-pair dataset and 1K-sample benchmark fill a concrete gap: no prior large-scale human preference corpus targeted holistic naturalness specifically, and no standardized benchmark existed for automated naturalness judgment. The finding that even Gemini-2.5-Flash stays below 70% agreement signals that current AudioLLMs are far from human-level on this task, motivating continued research. Key limitations include the Chinese-annotator-centric population (lower agreement on English/code-switched subsets), utterance-level (not segment-level) granularity, and reliance on a proprietary teacher for CoT bootstrapping. Open directions include fine-grained temporal naturalness scoring, multi-language annotator diversity, and fully open-source teacher alternatives.
