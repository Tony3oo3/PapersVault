## Problem

Reasoning-centric LLMs and agentic LLMs are trained under fundamentally different objectives, producing complementary but disjoint capabilities: strong internal chain-of-thought without tool access, or strong tool use without deep multi-step reasoning. Existing hybrid systems couple these behaviors loosely without a unified controller that decides per-query which mode to activate, and both families waste computation on simple queries by overthinking or over-calling tools. This paper addresses the gap by unifying three execution modes (instant, reasoning, agentic) under a single backbone with learned task-aware routing and an explicit efficiency objective.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text query and first emits a mode classification (instant, reasoning, or agentic) within <classification> tags, then generates a mode-specific trajectory: a direct answer under 300 words for instant mode, a >1000-word chain-of-thought in <reasoning> tags for reasoning mode, or an interleaved plan–tool-call–summary sequence using MCP-format tools (web search, page crawl, sandboxed code execution) for agentic mode. All modes terminate with a final answer in <answer> tags. In agentic mode, multiple independent tool calls are dispatched in parallel per step (up to 10 tools), and tool-response tokens are masked from the loss during training so the model learns routing and reasoning rather than memorizing tool outputs.

## Method

A²FM is trained in two stages on a Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct backbone. Stage 1 (route-then-align SFT) distills mode-conditioned trajectories from specialized teachers—DeepSeek-R1 for reasoning and DeepSeek-V3.1 for agentic/instant—using a J-shaped difficulty-sampling strategy that emphasizes moderately hard and classification-ambiguous queries. The agentic trajectory employs a parallel architecture where a single <plan> decomposes the task into 1–5 concurrent goals, and a dynamic <summary> aggregates progress and opens new sub-tasks, enabling simultaneous multi-tool execution. Stage 2 (Adaptive Policy Optimization, APO) extends GRPO with two key modifications: (i) a rollout strategy that interleaves mode-forced rollouts (prefix-injected classification tags, ρ per mode) with adaptive rollouts (γ autonomous selections) to guarantee unbiased exploration of all modes, and (ii) a multiplicative reward r_total = r_acc × r_adaptive × r_format, where the adaptive term penalizes non-instant mode selection on queries whose forced-instant success rate exceeds a threshold τ, thereby encoding the accuracy–efficiency trade-off directly into the optimization signal. APO uses strict on-policy training and omits KL regularization to accelerate convergence toward cost-efficient routing.

## Results

At the 32B scale, A²FM achieves 56.0% on XBench-DeepSearch, 57.3% on GAIA, and 13.4% on BrowseComp for agentic tasks; 95.0% on MATH500, 74.5% on AIME24, and 70.4% on AIME25 for reasoning; and 63.1% on GPQA-d, 54.7% on SuperGPQA, 73.8% on MMLU-Pro, and 16.7% on HLE for general knowledge. The adaptive execution achieves a cost-of-pass of only $0.00487 per correct answer on SuperGPQA, reducing cost by 45.2% relative to pure reasoning and 33.5% relative to pure agentic execution while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A²FM substantially outperforms all general-purpose LLMs (GPT-4.1, Claude 4 Sonnet) across agentic and reasoning benchmarks: on AIME25 it leads by +33.4 and +40.3 points respectively, and on BrowseComp by +11.9 and +10.8 points. Among 32B-scale agent foundation models, A²FM achieves the best XBench-DS score (56.0%), surpassing AFM-Search (54.0%) and WebSailor (53.3%), and ranks second on GAIA (57.3%) behind only OAgents with a GPT-4.1 backbone (58.3%). On reasoning, A²FM matches o1 on AIME24 (74.5 vs. 74.3) but trails on AIME25 (70.4 vs. 79.2) and MATH500 (95.0 vs. 96.4), while decisively beating all 32B agent foundation models (e.g., ReTool 67.0% and AFM-Code 59.8% on AIME24/25). On general-knowledge benchmarks, A²FM surpasses GPT-4.1 on SuperGPQA (54.7 vs. 50.8) and HLE (16.7 vs. 3.7) but trails on GPQA-d (63.1 vs. 66.3) and MMLU-Pro (73.8 vs. 81.8). The forced-agentic variant further improves on GAIA to 60.7% (new SOTA) and BrowseComp to 14.4%, confirming that the backbone's tool-use ceiling exceeds what adaptive routing exploits on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (Shao et al., 2024) with a tailored rollout strategy and cost-regularized adaptive reward, while borrowing the tool-result masking strategy from Search-R1 (Jin et al., 2025). Conceptually, it departs from prior hybrid systems (GPT-5, Qwen3) that treat reasoning and agentic capabilities as loosely coupled components trained in separate alignment stages, instead unifying them under a single backbone with a learned three-mode router (instant, reasoning, agentic). It also generalizes the binary 'when-to-think' literature (Chen et al., 2024b; Lou et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025b) from a think/non-think decision to a three-way routing problem that includes external tool invocation, and subsumes length-aware efficiency methods (Arora & Zanette, 2025) by making the instant mode a first-class execution path rather than a compressed reasoning trace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The A²FM framework offers a practical recipe for reducing inference cost (45.2% cost-of-pass reduction) while maintaining competitive accuracy across agentic, reasoning, and general benchmarks at the 32B scale, which is directly relevant to deployment economics. The finding that teacher model choice has minimal impact on distilled performance (≤3.1 points) suggests the route-then-align framework is robust and could be applied with cheaper teachers. Open questions include whether the three-mode routing generalizes to multimodal or longer-horizon agentic settings, whether the LLM-as-Judge reward signal remains reliable as task complexity grows, and whether the approach scales beyond 32B without the router collapsing into a single dominant mode. The 92.2% routing accuracy on GAIA (vs. 100% on AIME) also signals that boundary-case routing remains an active challenge.
