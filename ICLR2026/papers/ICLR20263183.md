## Problem

All existing global dimensionality measures, particularly the participation ratio (PR) of covariance eigenvalues, are systematically biased when computed from finite samples: the estimate depends on the number of observed stimuli (rows) and neurons (columns) rather than reflecting the true underlying dimensionality. No prior estimator is simultaneously resistant to both finite sample size and measurement noise, leaving a gap for reliable dimensionality estimation in neuroscience and ML where one can only observe a random submatrix of the full representation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a P×Q neural activation matrix (stimuli × neurons), optionally two independent trial matrices for noise correction, and optional importance-sampling weights for non-uniform sampling. The output is a scalar bias-corrected participation ratio (global dimensionality), with an optional local dimensionality estimate computed by weighting samples within a Mahalanobis-distance ball around each data point. The full estimator requires at least 4 distinct row indices and 2 distinct column indices, and the unequal-index sums are computed via inclusion-exclusion expansions over regular einsum-able sums.

## Method

The core insight is that the finite-sample bias in the naive PR estimator arises from overlapping indices in the quartic sums defining the numerator and denominator; restricting all row and column indices to be mutually distinct yields unbiased estimators of each term. The implementation expresses each unequal-index sum as a linear combination of regular (all-indices) sums via inclusion-exclusion, making the computation vectorizable with einsum. For noise correction, the method replaces the quartic tensor v_{ijlr}^{αβ} with a cross-trial product Φ_{iα}^{(1)}Φ_{jα}^{(2)}Φ_{kβ}^{(1)}Φ_{lβ}^{(2)}, which cancels zero-mean noise in expectation with only two trials. The framework extends to local dimensionality by assigning importance weights (e.g., uniform within a radius-r ball, zero outside) to the unequal-index sums, and to sparse matrices by restricting the sum to entries that are not missing.

## Results

On synthetic linear data with true dimensionality d=50 and noise variance σ_ε²=0.2, γ_both recovers the true dimensionality across wide ranges of P and Q, while γ_naive exhibits a harmonic-mean scaling law E[1/γ_naive] ≈ 1/P + 1/Q + 1/γ. On four neural recording datasets (mouse V1 calcium imaging, macaque IT spikes, macaque V4 LFP, human IT fMRI), γ_both remains remarkably constant as P or Q is varied, whereas γ_naive shows strong sample-size dependence. On Llama3 hidden layers (Q=4096, P=483 sentences/language, FLORES+), at a 10% input sampling ratio the naive estimator significantly underestimates dimensionality, while γ_both reveals fine-grained layerwise structure (dimensionality peaks in mid-layers). For local dimensionality on a noisy RFF model (SNR ≈ 3.33), γ_both^local(r) recovers the true d in the small-radius limit, while TwoNN significantly overestimates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline for global dimensionality is the naive participation ratio estimator (γ_naive), which the paper shows is systematically biased: its inverse scales as 1/P + 1/Q + 1/γ under uniform-norm assumptions. The proposed γ_both corrects this by averaging over unequal indices, achieving invariance to both row and column sample sizes across all four neural datasets and the LLM evaluation. For local dimensionality, the key baseline is TwoNN (Denti et al., 2022), the most widely adopted local intrinsic dimension estimator. On a noisy random Fourier feature model (SNR ≈ 3.33, σ_ε=0.3), TwoNN significantly overestimates the local dimensionality due to its susceptibility to noise, while γ_both^local(r) recovers the true d in the small-radius limit. The naive local estimator γ_naive^local(r) also fails, underestimating when only a small number of samples fall within the local ball. On real macaque V1 LFP data, γ_both^local(r) in the small-radius limit yields a much smaller (and more accurate) estimate than TwoNN. The paper also notes that γ_row and γ_col provide partial corrections useful when only one axis is sampled, and that in the LLM setting (where all neurons are observed), γ_row matches γ_both while γ_col degenerates to the naive estimator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the participation ratio (PR) as a standard measure of global dimensionality in neural and ML representations (Gao et al., 2017; Cohen et al., 2020; Stringer et al., 2019), addressing the well-known finite-sample bias that has been noted across neuroscience and ML but never rigorously corrected. Prior bias-correction attempts (Dahmen et al., 2020; Pospisil and Pillow, 2024) relied on strong distributional assumptions and only handled row sampling; here the authors derive unbiased estimators of the PR numerator and denominator via unequal-index averaging, requiring only that the generative map be square-integrable. The local-dimensionality extension positions the method as a noise-robust alternative to TwoNN (Facco et al., 2017; Denti et al., 2022), while the noise-correction construction draws on the two-trial cross-product idea from Stringer et al. (2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bias-corrected PR is a drop-in replacement for the naive estimator that is agnostic to the underlying generative process, making it broadly applicable across recording modalities (calcium imaging, electrophysiology, fMRI) and to LLM interpretability where input sampling is often limited. This removes a practical barrier for BCI decoder design, linear-probe analysis, and layer-wise dimensionality tracking in transformers, where underestimation of dimensionality at small sample sizes could mislead safety or interpretability conclusions. Open questions include extending the framework to higher spectral moments (sketches of which the authors reference), handling correlated noise beyond the two-trial setting, and reducing the O(P²Q) cost of the local-dimensionality variant for very large datasets.
