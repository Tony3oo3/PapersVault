## Problem

VLA models trained exclusively on synthetic manipulation data suffer a significant Sim2Real gap, failing to generalize reliably to real-world robotic tasks. Prior approaches focus on increasing simulation fidelity (photo-realistic rendering, physics-accurate dynamics), but recent evidence shows that factors like lighting and background textures are largely irrelevant to manipulation performance. This paper asks whether redesigning the VLA architecture itself—rather than the simulation engine—can close the domain gap.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images (arm-mounted wrist cameras plus a top-down binocular camera), a natural-language task instruction, and proprioceptive joint angles. It produces a tokenized action sequence (DCT-compressed joint angles encoded via BPE) at high frequency, a chain of 2D affordance keypoints (projected end-effector poses) at lower frequency, and per-object segmentation masks. The action output is structured as a sequence of discrete tokens decoded by an autoregressive transformer, while the affordance chain is a regressive keypoint sequence where each keypoint corresponds to a geometrically structured end-effector pose governing one atomic manipulation step.

## Method

Sim2Real-VLA uses a dual-system architecture coupled through affordance signals. The high-level planner (a regressive transformer) predicts a chain-of-affordance—geometrically structured keypoints representing key end-effector poses for each atomic sub-task—conditioned on object masks, visual observations, and the language instruction, while a CNN-based module recovers object masks from raw images. The low-level actor (a conditional autoregressive transformer with a diffusion-based action expert) executes each affordance using an arm-decoupled estimation strategy (independent left/right arm controllers) and a tokenized action space (DCT coefficients quantized and compressed via BPE), with a validation model that checks whether each affordance target has been reached before proceeding. Domain randomization is applied as a flow: a VLM (GPT-5) ranks and selects action-invariant features (lighting, textures, backgrounds) for resampling at each time step, filtering out manipulation-irrelevant variability. Training is fully supervised on automatically generated synthetic trajectories (via Real2Sim projection, workspace-aware scene scaling, and MLLM-driven skill acquisition), requiring no real-world demonstrations.

## Results

Sim2Real-VLA achieves an average real-world success rate of 60.8% across six manipulation tasks, outperforming the best baseline (π0-FAST at 27.5%) by over 33 percentage points. In simulation, it also leads with 46/50 (92%) on Single-Arm Water Pouring and 47/50 (94%) on Dual-Arm Water Pouring. The method completes tasks in fewer steps than all baselines (e.g., 174.6 vs. 185.95 for π0-FAST on Single-Arm Water Pouring), and its advantage is most pronounced on long-horizon bimanual tasks where baselines frequently fail entirely (0/20 real success for ACT and DP on Items Hand-Over, Basket Pick-and-Place, and Pan Open-and-Place).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sim2Real-VLA outperforms all six baselines on every task in both simulation and real-world settings. The gap is most dramatic against ACT and DP, which achieve 0% real-world success on four and three tasks respectively, indicating that end-to-end sequence models and diffusion policies cannot generalize from synthetic data to real manipulation without task-specific fine-tuning. RDT and π0 show moderate simulation performance (33/50 and 38/50 on Single-Arm Pour) but collapse in the real world (3/20 and 6/20), confirming the Sim2Real gap. Among the stronger baselines, π0-FAST (which adds FAST action tokenization to π0) is the closest competitor at 27.5% average real success, yet Sim2Real-VLA still leads by 33+ percentage points. GR00T N1.5, despite being a large-scale foundation model, underperforms π0-FAST on most tasks, suggesting that scale alone does not close the Sim2Real gap. The advantage of Sim2Real-VLA is particularly large on long-horizon bimanual tasks (Items Hand-Over: 8/20 vs. best baseline 4/20; Basket P&P: 9/20 vs. 3/20), where the chain-of-affordance planning and arm-decoupled execution provide structured temporal reasoning that flat action-prediction baselines lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the dual-system VLA architecture (high-level VLM planner + low-level action expert) popularized by Helix, GR00T, and Gemini Robotics, but departs from the mainstream Sim2Real strategy of pursuing higher-fidelity simulation or world models. Instead, it repositions the Sim2Real gap as an architecture-design problem: by inserting a chain-of-affordance as a structured intermediate representation between planning and acting, the model filters out manipulation-irrelevant visual features (lighting, background texture) that are the primary source of domain shift. The domain randomization is also made strategic—guided by a VLM to select and schedule which features to randomize per timestep—rather than applied uniformly as in prior active-DR or Bayesian-DR work. The automatic data generation pipeline (Real2Sim projection + generative scene scaling + agentic skill acquisition) integrates and extends ideas from RoboCasa, RoboGen, and DexScale into a single hands-off pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >35% absolute improvement in real-world success over strong baselines (π0, GR00T N1.5) trained on identical simulated data is practically significant for reducing the cost of robotic deployment, and the fully automatic data pipeline removes the bottleneck of manual teleoperation. However, the evaluation is confined to a single robot platform (Agilex CobotMagic) and tabletop manipulation, with only 20 real trials per task, and the 60.8% average real-world success rate still leaves substantial room for improvement. Open questions include whether the affordance-chain mechanism generalizes to non-tabletop or multi-agent settings, whether the K=1 finding (single affordance outperforms longer chains) holds for genuinely longer-horizon tasks, and how the approach interacts with reinforcement learning for continual refinement. The 'zero-shot' claim is also qualified by the Real2Sim projection step, which consumes a small set of real teleoperation or video trajectories to configure the simulation scene.
