## Problem

Counterintuitive RL phenomena in LLM reasoning—spurious rewards improving performance, single training examples matching full-dataset training, and negative-only signals rivaling standard RL—were established primarily on Qwen models trained on mathematical tasks, leaving unclear the precise conditions under which they hold or fail. This paper identifies model-task alignment, quantified by pass@k accuracy, as the critical factor determining when these phenomena emerge versus when only standard RL with accurate rewards drives substantial learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264199/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The experiments take pretrained LLMs (Qwen2.5-7B-Base, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-v0.1) and train them via DAPO-based RL on task-specific datasets (DeepScaleR for math, SynLogic-Easy for logic, code-r1-12k for code) with systematically varied reward signals (ground truth, random, incorrect, format-only, majority-vote, exact-match). Trained models are evaluated zero-shot on nine benchmarks spanning math, logic, and code, with pass@k computed pre-training as a diagnostic alignment metric and contamination assessed via prompt-truncation ROUGE-L/EM scoring.

## Method

The paper constructs a systematic empirical framework that categorizes model-task combinations into three alignment levels—strong with potential contamination (red), weak without contamination (gray), and strong without contamination (green)—using pass@k accuracy and a prompt-truncation contamination analysis. Within each category, they test four counterintuitive RL claims: robustness to spurious rewards, one-shot training sufficiency (single selected or random examples vs. full dataset), negative-only signal effectiveness (NSR vs. PSR trajectory masking), and test-time RL via majority voting (TTRL). All experiments use DAPO with fixed hyperparameters (group size 16, ε_low=0.2, ε_high=0.28, lr=1e-6, 300 steps) to isolate alignment effects from tuning effort. The key finding is that these phenomena function as capability elicitation mechanisms in strong-alignment settings but fail in weak-alignment settings where only standard RL with accurate rewards drives substantial learning. KL divergence analysis further reveals that strong alignment requires only small policy updates while weak alignment demands large deviations guided by correct rewards to form stable reasoning patterns.

## Results

Across 9 evaluation benchmarks (AIME24, MATH500, AMC23, SynLogic, BBH, BBEH, and 5 KOR-Bench subsets) with Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B, standard RL (DAPO) consistently improves performance in all model-task combinations, e.g., Qwen2.5 on MATH500 reaches 71.0 (+30.2 over 40.8 baseline) and Llama on SynLogic reaches 21.0 (+20.2 over 0.8 baseline). In strong model-task alignment settings (Qwen+Math, both models+Operation/Counterfactual), spurious rewards, one-shot training, and negative-only training all recover most of the DAPO gains (e.g., NSR on MATH500: 68.7 vs. DAPO 71.0). In weak alignment settings (Llama+Math, both models+SynLogic/BBH/BBEH), these counterintuitive methods fail: random reward on Llama+SynLogic yields 0.0 (−0.8), one-shot RL on Qwen+SynLogic yields 0.8 (−0.7), and NSR on Llama+SynLogic yields 0.0 (−0.8), while DAPO still achieves meaningful gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) serves as the standard RL baseline and is the only method that consistently improves performance across all model-task combinations, including weakly aligned settings where all other methods fail. NSR (Zhu et al., 2025) matches DAPO in strong alignment (e.g., MATH500: 68.7 vs. 71.0 for Qwen) but completely fails in weak alignment (SynLogic: 0.0 vs. 21.0 for Llama), while PSR (positive-only) is more robust in weak settings (SynLogic: 13.0 vs. 0.0 for Llama). TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025) yields substantial gains only under strong alignment (Qwen+MATH500: +28.4; Llama+Operation: +14.8) but provides negligible improvement in weak alignment (Llama+SynLogic: +0.7). One-shot RL (Wang et al., 2025) recovers ~90% of full-dataset training in strong alignment (Qwen MATH500: 65.2 vs. 71.0) but fails entirely in weak alignment (Qwen SynLogic: 0.8 vs. 42.6). Spurious rewards (Shao et al., 2025) remain effective in strong alignment (Qwen+Operation with random reward: 69.2, +8.8) but degrade performance in weak alignment (Llama+SynLogic with random reward: 0.0, −0.8), confirming that the original claims are artifacts of model-task alignment rather than universal RL properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a systematic empirical investigation that builds on the DAPO/GRPO policy-gradient framework for LLM RL training, but its primary contribution is interrogating a cluster of recent counterintuitive claims—spurious rewards (Shao et al., 2025), one-shot training (Wang et al., 2025), negative-only signals (Zhu et al., 2025), and test-time RL (Zuo et al., 2025)—that were largely demonstrated in a narrow Qwen+math setting. It departs from the concurrent contamination hypothesis of Wu et al. (2025) by showing that model-task alignment (measured via pass@k), rather than data leakage, is the operative variable determining when these phenomena hold. The paper does not propose a new algorithm; instead, it provides a unifying empirical framework for categorizing and interpreting existing RL results across model architectures and task domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: practitioners cannot assume that cheap RL shortcuts (noisy rewards, single examples, negative-only training) will transfer to tasks where the base model lacks strong prior capability—standard RL with accurate rewards remains necessary in those regimes. This reframing opens a concrete research direction for jointly optimizing mid-training (to build domain-specific capability) and RL post-training (to elicit it with minimal data), potentially reducing the compute required for RL on hard tasks. A key limitation is that the alignment boundary appears continuous rather than discrete, and the paper does not yet identify a precise threshold; additionally, the study is confined to ~7B–8B models, leaving open whether the alignment-dependency pattern persists at larger scales where base models may already exhibit strong alignment across most domains.
