## Problem

Learning-based HDR fusion models are constrained by the scarcity of large-scale training data with ground truth, as capturing dynamic high-dynamic-range scenes (e.g., direct sunlight, large motion) is costly and technically challenging. Existing datasets contain only 89–144 samples with limited motion diversity, causing models to overfit and fail on complex real-world scenes. This paper tackles both the data scarcity and the resulting sim-to-real domain gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263635/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes three multi-exposure LDR images (at −2/0/+2 or −3/0/+3 EV, generated from the linear HDR ground truth) and produces a single fused HDR image. The S2R-HDR dataset stores all 24,000 frames as 1920×1080 EXR files in linear HDR space (no baked-in tone mapping or gamma correction), enabling flexible exposure augmentation. The S2R-Adapter is plug-and-play, attaching to any linear or 1×1 convolutional layer in both CNN-based (SAFNet) and transformer-based (SCTNet) backbones without changing inference architecture after reparameterization.

## Method

The paper contributes two components: (1) S2R-HDR, a 24,000-image synthetic dataset rendered in Unreal Engine 5 with a custom pipeline that preserves linear HDR in EXR format, simulates handheld camera shake, and covers diverse motion types (humans, animals, vehicles), lighting (daylight, twilight, night, direct sun), and indoor/outdoor settings; and (2) S2R-Adapter, a two-branch domain adaptation module where a low-rank shared branch (LoRA-style, rank r_s=1) preserves pre-trained knowledge from synthetic data while a high-rank transfer branch (rank r_t=64) learns real-domain-specific features, with learnable scale factors α_s and α_t controlling the trade-off. For test-time adaptation without ground truth, the adapter uses a mean-teacher framework where the teacher generates pseudo-labels and uncertainty estimates via test-time augmentation (exposure, white balance, noise, flips), dynamically adjusting α_s = 1−U(x) and α_t = 1+U(x) per sample to balance shared and transferred knowledge based on measured domain shift.

## Results

On the SCT dataset, SAFNet w/ S2R-Adapter achieves 43.33 PSNR-μ and 70.00 HDR-VDP2, outperforming SAFNet trained directly on SCT (42.66 PSNR-μ, 68.78 HDR-VDP2) by 0.67 dB. On the more challenging Challenge123 dataset, the same approach reaches 43.43 PSNR-μ and 56.51 HDR-VDP2, a 1.55 dB gain over SAFNet trained on Challenge123 (41.88 PSNR-μ). The S2R-Adapter also enables test-time adaptation without ground truth, where SAFNet w/ S2R-Adapter achieves 36.28 PSNR-μ on SCT versus 35.14 for the best real-data-trained baseline (SAFNet on Challenge123), a 1.14 dB improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's S2R-HDR + S2R-Adapter pipeline is applied to two baseline architectures (SCTNet, a transformer; SAFNet, a CNN) and compared against seven methods trained directly on the small real datasets. On SCT, SAFNet w/ S2R (43.33 PSNR-μ) beats SAFNet (42.66), SCTNet (42.55), DiffHDR (42.77), and HDR-Transformer (42.39) by 0.56–0.67 dB. On Challenge123, the gains are larger: SAFNet w/ S2R (43.43 PSNR-μ) surpasses SAFNet (41.88) by 1.55 dB, SCTNet (40.65) by 2.78 dB, and DiffHDR (38.78) by 4.65 dB. In the test-time adaptation setting (Table 3, no ground truth), SAFNet w/ S2R-Adapter achieves 36.28 PSNR-μ on SCT, exceeding the best real-data-trained baseline (SAFNet on Challenge123, 35.14) by 1.14 dB and the S2R-HDR-only model (34.89) by 1.39 dB. Cross-dataset generalization (Table 4) further shows that models trained solely on S2R-HDR outperform those trained on SCT or Challenge123 when tested on the other dataset, confirming superior robustness. The S2R-Adapter also reduces knowledge forgetting: after adaptation to SCT, the model retains 35.95 PSNR-μ on S2R-HDR versus 35.52 for simple fine-tuning (Table 7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) HDR fusion datasets, where prior work (Kalantari 2017, SCT 2023, Challenge123 2024) is limited to 89–144 samples with constrained motion and lighting; (2) sim-to-real domain adaptation via parameter-efficient adapters (LoRA, VIDA, AdaptFormer), which the authors repurpose with a novel dual-branch design—low-rank for shared knowledge preservation and high-rank for domain-specific transfer; and (3) test-time adaptation using mean-teacher and uncertainty-based scaling, extended here to the HDR fusion setting where ground-truth labels are often unavailable. The rendering pipeline builds on UE5-based synthetic data generation (MatrixCity, SynBody, WHAC) but introduces a custom tone-mapping-removal and camera-shake simulation specifically for HDR fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 24,000-sample S2R-HDR dataset (166× larger than prior HDR datasets) directly addresses the data bottleneck that has limited HDR fusion research, and the finding that synthetic-trained models outperform real-trained models on cross-dataset generalization suggests a viable path for tasks where real data collection is prohibitively expensive. The S2R-Adapter's ability to work without ground-truth labels during test-time adaptation is practically significant, as most real HDR datasets lack paired fusion ground truth. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video HDR reconstruction at scale, whether the domain gap in texture-rich regions (trees, grass, people) can be further closed, and whether the dual-branch rank design generalizes beyond the specific architectures tested.
