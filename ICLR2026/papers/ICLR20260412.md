## Problem

Existing reference-based video editing methods (e.g., AnyV2V) rely on a single stylized first frame to guide an I2V model, but since the base model was never trained on stylized content, it cannot properly parse and propagate style patterns to subsequent frames containing novel content, resulting in pronounced propagation errors. Frame-by-frame image stylization avoids this but introduces severe flickering, while training dedicated video stylization models demands large-scale paired data and substantial compute. The paper seeks a training-free approach that achieves both rich style transfer and strong temporal coherence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short video clip (typically ~81 frames / 5 seconds) and a single style image as input. The style image is applied to selected content frames (first, middle, last) via an off-the-shelf image style transfer model (InstantStyle-plus) to produce multiple stylized reference frames. During inversion, the pipeline also consumes a depth map (from Video Depth Anything) and a concise text prompt (from Qwen2.5-VL) describing the video's content and dynamics. The output is a temporally coherent stylized video at the same resolution and length as the input, with no additional training or fine-tuning of the base diffusion model.

## Method

FreeViS builds on a pretrained I2V DiT model (Wan2.1-Fun-Control) and operates in two parallel branches—reconstruction and stylization—conditioned on different sets of reference frames, with PnP Inversion providing the denoising trajectory. Three key mechanisms distinguish the approach: (1) Indirect High-frequency Compensation (IHC) applies FFT-based filtering to inject only high-frequency structural/motion differences from the reconstruction branch into the stylization latent, preserving stylized color while correcting layout; (2) a multi-reference attention scheme (Isolated-Attn) enables additional stylized references beyond the first frame, using RAFT optical flow to build reference masks that resolve appearance conflicts and a dynamic-residual injection (Eq. 4) that transfers motion cues into static reference value matrices; (3) Explicit Optical-flow Guidance (EOG) constrains cross-frame attention in low-saliency regions via a dense flow mask, preventing style texture disappearance under large camera or object motion. The three attention modes are aggregated with learned weights (β, γ), and QK-Sharing from the reconstruction branch provides spatial-temporal correspondences that anchor the stylization output to the original video's structure.

## Results

On the video style transfer benchmark (200 videos, 81 frames each), FreeViS achieves the highest CSD Score (0.448) and Human Preference (4.113/5) among all compared methods, while maintaining near-reference temporal consistency (SC 0.898 vs. 0.918 for the reference). It also attains the best Flow Consistency (FC 0.641) and FID (21.62) among methods that perform genuine texture-level stylization. For stylized T2V generation (Table 2), FreeViS+Wan achieves the highest HP (3.97), CLIP-Text alignment (0.264), Dynamic Quality (0.509), and Imaging Quality (0.691), outperforming StyleCrafter and StyleMaster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeViS decisively outperforms the closest single-reference baselines AnyV2V and AnyV2V* on every stylization and consistency metric: CSD Score 0.448 vs. 0.267/0.270, Style Consistency 0.898 vs. 0.753/0.675, Flow Consistency 0.641 vs. 1.715/1.103, and Human Preference 4.113 vs. 2.443/3.372, confirming that multi-reference integration resolves the propagation error inherent in single-frame guidance. Against I2VEdit, FreeViS improves CSD Score by 0.117 and FC by 1.433, while I2VEdit's weaker base model causes it to fail reconstruction under large content changes. VACE and TokenFlow achieve slightly better LPIPS and Motion Smoothness (because they primarily shift colors rather than generate new textures), but their CSD Scores (0.138 and 0.111) are less than one-third of FreeViS's, indicating they cannot transfer complex style textures. In the stylized T2V setting, FreeViS+Wan surpasses StyleCrafter on CLIP-Text (0.264 vs. 0.211), Dynamic Quality (0.509 vs. 0.368), and HP (3.97 vs. 2.83), while StyleCrafter retains a marginal edge in CSD Score (0.515 vs. 0.437) and FID (22.62 vs. 24.63); StyleMaster lags on both style fidelity and prompt alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeViS extends the reference-based video editing paradigm (AnyV2V, I2VEdit) from single-reference to multi-reference inputs, directly addressing the propagation error that arises when a base I2V model treats a stylized first frame as out-of-distribution. It builds on PnP Inversion for content preservation and introduces a frequency decomposition insight (low-frequency latents govern color/appearance, high-frequency latents encode layout/motion) that has been observed in image editing but is here formalized for video. The isolated attention mechanism with dynamic-appearance decomposition for causal VAEs departs from prior single-reference designs, while the explicit optical-flow guidance (EOG) draws on classical correspondence estimation to regularize cross-frame attention in low-saliency regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free pipeline offers a practical, economically viable path to high-quality video stylization without requiring paired original-stylized video data or expensive fine-tuning, making it immediately deployable with off-the-shelf image stylization models and pretrained I2V backbones. Key limitations include the ~30% inference overhead from masked attention and inversion, the upper bound on stylization quality imposed by the underlying image model, and sensitivity to optical flow errors under severe occlusion. Open questions include adaptive reference selection for longer videos, real-time stylization via auto-regressive models, and whether the frequency decomposition generalizes across architectures beyond DiT-based I2V models.
