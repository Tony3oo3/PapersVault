## Problem

VLA models are too computationally expensive and run at too low a frequency for real-time robotic control, yet existing acceleration methods (quantization, caching, speculative decoding) only reduce single-step computation and ignore the sequential, embodied nature of VLA inference. This paper is the first to identify and jointly exploit two overlooked sources of redundancy: temporal redundancy across consecutive action steps and spatial redundancy in the visual token stream.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261306/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the system consumes an RGB image, a language instruction, and a sliding action buffer of the last n=6 l-dimensional end-effector velocity vectors. It outputs a single velocity action (displacement per step) for the end-effector, with the binary gripper state simply carried over from t−1 during intuitive steps. Velocity thresholds v_min and v_max are set at 1/4 and 3/4 of the robot's operational speed range, making the method device-dependent but requiring only one parameter set per robot platform.

## Method

SP-VLA jointly applies two mechanisms. First, an action-type-aware scheduler classifies each step as intuitive (all velocity components exceed v_min) or deliberative; intuitive steps are handled by a lightweight Ridge Regression model re-fitted on the action buffer, while deliberative steps invoke the full VLA model, with a buffer-ratio guard (N_G/N_A > τ) preventing over-reliance on the lightweight path. Second, when the VLA is invoked, a spatio-semantic dual-aware token pruner selects tokens by taking the order-preserving union of semantically important tokens (accumulated attention scores above a threshold) and spatially important tokens (those corresponding to Canny edge pixels), then applies a velocity-adaptive pruning ratio that disables pruning at low speeds and increases it at high speeds. The key insight is that VLA models, like human motor control, alternate between deliberate reasoning at critical moments and intuitive execution during fast transitions, and that their spatial perception depends on both token relative ordering and object contour information—so pruning must preserve both.

## Results

On LIBERO, SP-VLA achieves a 1.35× speedup with no accuracy loss (74.90% avg success rate vs. OpenVLA's 74.60%) and up to 1.50× speedup with only a 2.7% accuracy drop (71.90%). On SimplerEnv, SP-VLA delivers a 2.15× average speedup while improving accuracy by up to 6% over CogACT (e.g., 75.05% vs. 74.80% on Visual Matching). On a real Franka Research 3 robot, SP-VLA achieves 2.52× end-to-end acceleration with only a 1% success-rate drop (76.00% vs. 77.00%), reducing FLOPs by over 60% and inference latency from 0.27 s to 0.13 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SP-VLA (Ours Acc.) matches or slightly exceeds OpenVLA's 74.60% average success rate (74.90%) while delivering a 1.35× speedup, making it the only method that achieves lossless acceleration. At the higher 1.50× speedup setting (Ours Speed), SP-VLA retains 71.90% accuracy, a mere 2.7% drop, whereas all competing baselines suffer far larger losses: FoPru+R drops to 59.70% (−14.9%), FastVLM+R+S to 66.65% (−7.95%), VisionZip+R+S to 33.05% (−41.5%), and PruMerge+R collapses entirely to 0%. SparseVLM is the closest competitor, losing only 1% accuracy at 1.33× speedup, but SP-VLA achieves a higher speedup (1.50×) at comparable accuracy. On SimplerEnv, SP-VLA outperforms EfficientVLA (74.20% at 1.93×) and VLA-Cache (74.40% at 1.38×) on Visual Matching, reaching 75.05% at 2.15×, and on Visual Aggregation it improves over CogACT by ~4% (65.16% vs. 61.30%) at 2.09× speedup, demonstrating an error-correction effect absent in all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing VLA acceleration methods (QAIL, VLA-Cache, EfficientVLA, DeeR-VLA) that focus exclusively on reducing single-step computational cost via quantization, caching, or early exit, by introducing a temporal dimension: the observation that VLA action sequences contain both deliberative and intuitive phases, analogous to human motor control (Schwartz, 2016; Merel et al., 2019). It also extends VLM token-pruning techniques (FastV, VisionZip, FoPru) by showing they fail on VLA models due to spatial sensitivity, and repurposes the Canny edge detector as a spatial importance signal. The joint scheduling-plus-pruning framework is positioned as the first to simultaneously exploit temporal and spatial redundancies in VLA inference, building on the deliberative/intuitive distinction from neuroscience rather than on prior VLA-specific acceleration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.5× real-robot acceleration with only 1% accuracy drop on a Franka arm is practically significant for deploying VLA models in latency-sensitive settings like industrial manipulation and autonomous navigation. The finding that VLA models critically depend on object contour tokens (Canny edges) and relative token ordering for spatial understanding—such that removing edge information causes a ~50% performance collapse—provides an important design constraint for future VLA compression and token-selection methods. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the lightweight generator (Ridge Regression) is preliminary, and a true separation of deliberative and intuitive action generation remains open; whether the velocity-based action-type heuristic generalizes beyond pick-and-place to more complex multi-stage tasks is also untested.
