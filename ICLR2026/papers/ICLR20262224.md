## Problem

Existing spatial omics models either ignore spatial cell-cell structure or rely on fixed gene vocabularies, preventing them from capturing how internal gene co-expression programs adapt to microenvironmental cues and from generalizing to new datasets or proteomics data. No prior foundation model explicitly models both the spatial tissue graph and the intra-cellular gene co-expression network in a unified, hierarchical framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262224/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HEIST takes as input a hierarchical graph pair: a spatial cell-cell graph built from Voronoi polygon adjacency over 2D cell coordinates, and cell-type-specific gene co-expression networks constructed from pairwise mutual information edges (threshold τ_k = μ_k + σ_k per cell type). Rather than consuming a fixed gene vocabulary, gene embeddings are initialized with rank-based sinusoidal encodings of denoised expression values and updated dynamically through message passing, so the same gene receives different embeddings in different cellular contexts. The model outputs cell embeddings Z_c ∈ R^{|C|×d} and per-cell gene embeddings Z_g ∈ R^{|C||V|×d}, plus a 3-layer GIN decoder that reconstructs spatial coordinates and gene expression for the masked-autoencoding objective.

## Method

HEIST is a hierarchical graph transformer where each HEISTLayer performs two steps: (1) intra-level message passing, where a CellGraphTransformer combines global (all-to-all) attention over spatially-batched cells (256-cell blocks) with a GIN layer for local Voronoi-neighborhood aggregation, and a GeneGraphTransformer applies sparse attention restricted to co-expression network edges; (2) cross-level message passing via directional attention, where each gene attends to its parent cell's embedding (injecting spatial context into gene representations) and each cell attends to a pooled summary of its genes (letting transcriptional state shape spatial identity). Pre-training uses a combined objective of three contrastive terms (cell–cell, gene–gene, cell–gene with spatial-radius-defined positive/negative pairs), masked autoencoding of spatial coordinates and gene expression, and an orthogonality regularizer, with a learnable sigmoid-gated scalar balancing the contrastive and reconstruction losses. This design eliminates the fixed gene vocabulary, enabling zero-shot transfer to spatial proteomics (CODEX, MIBI) without retraining, and achieves 8× and 48× speedups over scGPT-spatial and scFoundation respectively through sparse graph structure and spatial batching.

## Results

HEIST achieves state-of-the-art clinical outcome prediction (AUC-ROC) in 6 of 7 organ/task settings, reaching 0.937 on DFCI neck cancer and 0.929 on UPMC neck recurrence, surpassing the best prior model by up to 44.3% on skin melanoma immunotherapy response. On gene imputation (Pearson correlation), fine-tuned HEIST reaches 0.821 on placenta and 0.807 on skin, improving over the best baseline (CellPLM) by 2.5% and 9.1% respectively. For cell type annotation (F1), HEIST achieves 0.995 on SEA-AD brain (vs. 0.670 for CellPLM) and 0.283 on UPMC proteomics (vs. 0.220 for scGPT-spatial aligned). HEIST also attains 0.995 AUC-ROC on ligand-receptor pair prediction, outperforming scGPT-spatial (0.984) and scFoundation (0.963).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEIST outperforms all graph-based spatial baselines (STAGATE, GraphST) by large margins across every task, confirming that task-specific GNNs cannot transfer across tissues. Against the single-cell foundation model ScFoundation, HEIST wins on all clinical outcome and imputation benchmarks, with the gap widening on proteomics (e.g., 0.937 vs. 0.742 AUC-ROC on DFCI) because ScFoundation ignores spatial structure entirely. NoVAE fails on most proteomics datasets due to insufficient marker overlap (NEM errors), while HEIST constructs co-expression networks directly from observed proteins without retraining. Versus the spatial foundation models CellPLM and scGPT-spatial, HEIST achieves the largest gains on proteomics: on UPMC neck, HEIST (0.929 recurrence) beats scGPT-spatial aligned (0.789) by 14 points and CellPLM aligned (0.655) by 27 points, because these models rely on fixed gene vocabularies supporting only 6 of 28 markers. HEIST's one notable loss is on UPMC outcome (0.835 vs. Space-GM's 0.863) and on MERFISH Lung cell annotation (0.513 vs. scGPT-spatial's 0.567), the latter explained by scGPT-spatial having been pretrained on that exact dataset. On gene imputation, fine-tuned HEIST surpasses MAGIC (0.749/0.671) and all deep baselines, with the 9% gain on skin attributed to cross-level attention integrating spatial context into per-gene reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

HEIST carves new ground at the intersection of hierarchical graph neural networks and spatial omics foundation models. Prior spatial foundation models (SCGPT-spatial, CellPLM) treat genes as a fixed vocabulary over fully-connected or flat token sequences, while graph-based spatial methods (STAGATE, GraphST) capture local neighborhoods but are task-specific and non-transferable. HEIST departs from both by constructing a two-level hierarchy—spatial cell graph on top, cell-type-specific gene co-expression networks below—and introducing directional cross-level message passing that lets spatial context modulate gene embeddings and vice versa. It also draws on hierarchical GNN work (MSMGN, HOOD) but applies cross-level communication across distinct modalities (spatial vs. molecular) rather than across resolutions of the same modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HEIST is the first spatial-omics foundation model that generalizes to proteomics (CODEX, MIBI) without retraining, because gene/protein embeddings are computed from observed co-expression rather than a fixed vocabulary—a practical advantage given the heterogeneity of proteomic marker panels. The discovery of spatially-informed subclusters (e.g., L4-IT neurons in Alzheimer's tissue) that prior models collapse suggests the hierarchical architecture captures microenvironmental heterogeneity relevant to biomarker discovery. Key open questions include extending to spatio-temporal data, replacing mutual-information-based co-expression with causal or directional measures, and whether the Voronoi-based spatial graph is optimal versus learned adjacency. The model still requires substantial pretraining compute (4×L40 GPUs, ~3 hr/epoch), limiting accessibility.
