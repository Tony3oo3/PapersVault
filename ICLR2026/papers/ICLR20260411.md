## Problem

In generative personalization, learned concept tokens progressively lose their original textual semantics and come to dominate other elements in complex prompts, a phenomenon the authors term the Semantic Collapsing Problem (SCP). While prompt-image misalignment is well-recognized, the underlying mechanism—unconstrained optimization causing arbitrary drift in both the magnitude and direction of the personalized embedding—had not been identified or studied. Existing mitigations (regularization, latent optimization, concept disentanglement) address symptoms like overfitting but do not target this embedding-level drift.

## Input / Output

For Textual Inversion-based methods, TEA takes the learned token embedding M_{V*} and the reference concept embedding M_c (e.g., the word 'man') and produces a single adjusted embedding vector. For DreamBooth-based methods where the embedding matrix is not explicitly updated, TEA instead operates at the prompt level: it takes the full prompt embeddings τ([p,V*]) and τ([p,c]) from the text encoder and adjusts each token position independently. The output is a modified embedding fed into the unchanged diffusion model for image generation, requiring no retraining or weight modification.

## Method

TEA applies a two-stage geometric correction to the personalized embedding at inference time: first, it rescales the embedding norm to β‖M_c‖ (default β=1.5), pulling the magnitude back toward the reference concept; second, it rotates the embedding direction toward M_c using Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) with a rotation factor α (default 0.2). For DreamBooth variants, the same SLERP adjustment is applied token-by-token to the prompt-level embeddings rather than a single token vector. The approach is entirely training-free and plug-and-play, requiring only the original reference concept word and two scalar hyperparameters, and it generalizes across Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, ClassDiffusion, EasyControl, OminiControl, and ReVersion on both Stable Diffusion and Flux architectures. A surprising secondary finding is that applying TEA to models poisoned by Anti-DreamBooth partially reverses the adversarial corruption, revealing that the distortion in anti-personalized models is itself an extreme form of SCP.

## Results

TEA consistently improves full-prompt alignment (CLIP_T^f) across all four core personalization methods on both CC101 and CelebA, with the largest gain of +1.87 for DreamBooth on CC101. Image-reference alignment (CLIP-I) improves substantially for DB on CC101 (+4.57) and for EasyControl (+3.23 CLIP-I, +4.61 DINO-I), though modest drops occur for TI and DB on CelebA (−2.37 to −2.41 CLIP-I). On the Flux-based OminiControl, TEA lifts VLM-P by +4.25 and VLM-I by +3.50, and on ReVersion it boosts CLIP_T^f by up to +2.43 for compositional relationship prompts. All improvements are achieved at inference time with no additional training or model-weight modification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Textual Inversion (TI), TEA improves CLIP_T^f by +0.64 (CC101) and +0.57 (CelebA) but sacrifices CLIP-I by ~2.3–2.4 points, reflecting a prompt-fidelity vs. identity-fidelity trade-off. DreamBooth (DB) benefits most from TEA, gaining +1.87 CLIP_T^f and +4.57 CLIP-I on CC101, though CelebA shows a −2.37 CLIP-I drop. Custom Diffusion (CD) and ClassDiffusion (CL) see smaller but consistent CLIP_T^f gains (+0.09 to +0.42) with neutral-to-positive CLIP-I changes. On the Flux-based EasyControl, TEA delivers the largest image-alignment gains (+3.23 CLIP-I, +4.61 DINO-I, +3.25 VLM-I), while OminiControl sees the strongest VLM-judged improvements (+4.25 VLM-P, +3.50 VLM-I). ReVersion, which targets compositional relationships, benefits most in prompt fidelity with up to +2.43 CLIP_T^f on 'carved by' prompts. Overall, TEA wins on prompt alignment (CLIP_T^f) in every comparison and on image alignment in most settings, with the only consistent losses being small CLIP-I drops for TI/DB on CelebA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the generative personalization literature (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, and their variants) but carves out a distinct diagnostic contribution: identifying the Semantic Collapsing Problem (SCP) as a specific failure mode separate from the well-studied language drift/overfitting phenomenon. While prior work addresses misalignment through training-time regularization (PALP, prior-preservation loss, orthogonal fine-tuning) or concept disentanglement (LEGO, ReVersion), this paper traces the root cause to unconstrained embedding optimization and proposes an orthogonal, training-free test-time fix using SLERP-based rotation and rescaling. The method is deliberately designed as a plug-and-play layer atop existing personalization pipelines rather than a replacement, and it draws on the anti-personalization literature (Anti-DreamBooth) to reveal an unexpected interaction between adversarial masking and embedding drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SCP identification provides a new mechanistic lens for understanding why personalized tokens dominate complex prompts, complementing existing overfitting narratives and suggesting that even well-regularized personalization methods may suffer from residual embedding drift. The Anti-DreamBooth vulnerability finding is a notable security implication: if a simple test-time embedding rotation can partially recover the protected concept, current anti-personalization defenses may offer a false sense of security, though the evidence here is primarily qualitative and would benefit from more rigorous quantitative validation. Open questions include whether adaptive, prompt-aware hyperparameters (α, β) can close the remaining trade-off between prompt fidelity and visual fidelity, whether training-time embedding constraints could prevent SCP at its source, and whether the Anti-DreamBooth vulnerability generalizes beyond the specific experimental setup tested.
