## Problem

Training deep spiking neural networks (SNNs) in a biologically plausible and neuromorphic-hardware-friendly manner remains challenging because spatial backpropagation requires symmetric weights, separate forward-backward phases, and weight transport. Existing alternatives such as random feedback (DFA) offer limited theoretical guarantees and substantially worse accuracy, while standard zeroth-order methods suffer from variance that scales with the number of parameters, making them ineffective for general network training. The paper specifically targets the spatial credit assignment problem: how to propagate error signals to hidden layers without layer-by-layer backpropagation while maintaining competitive performance.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either spike trains from neuromorphic sensors (e.g., 34×34×2 ON/OFF events for N-MNIST over 30 time steps, 48×48 events for DVS-Gesture over 20 steps) or static images converted to real-valued input currents at each of T discrete time steps (T=6 for MNIST/CIFAR). The SNN processes inputs through LIF neurons with leaky dynamics (λ=0.5, V_th=1), and classification is performed on the accumulated membrane potential at the final non-spiking layer over all time steps, producing a class label. Networks range from 2-hidden-layer fully connected (800 neurons) for small datasets to 5- or 9-layer convolutional architectures and a ResNet-34 for ImageNet fine-tuning.

## Method

OPZO replaces spatial BP with a single forward pass that injects random node perturbation (αz) during propagation, followed by direct top-down error propagation through learned momentum feedback connections M^k = λM^{k-1} + (1-λ)z·(õ^T/α). The key insight is a pseudo-zeroth-order formulation that decouples the network function f(·;θ) from the loss L(·): the model remains in zeroth-order form (only function values needed), while the loss gradient ∇_o L(o, y) provides a vector-valued feedback signal far more informative than a scalar loss, enabling variance reduction. The momentum connections approximate E_x[J_f^T(x)] via a moving average over perturbation directions, provably reducing gradient variance from O(d) (proportional to parameter count) to O(m) (proportional to output dimension). Built on online training through time (OTTT), the weight update takes the form of three-factor Hebbian learning: ΔW_{i,j} ∝ â_i[t]·ψ(u_j[t])·(−g_j^t), where the global modulator g_j^t is a direct top-down signal rather than a backpropagated gradient, making the rule compatible with unidirectional synapses and parallel neuromorphic computation.

## Results

On neuromorphic datasets, OPZO achieves 98.27% on N-MNIST, 94.33% on DVS-Gesture, and 72.77% on DVS-CIFAR10, within 1–3% of spatial BP (98.15%, 95.72%, 75.43%). On static datasets, OPZO reaches 98.34% on MNIST, 85.74% on CIFAR-10, and 60.93% on CIFAR-100, compared to 98.38%, 90.00%, and 64.82% for spatial BP. With local learning, OPZO(w/LL) matches or exceeds BP(w/LL) on neuromorphic datasets (e.g., 96.06% vs 95.72% on DVS-Gesture, 77.47% vs 76.07% on DVS-CIFAR10). On ImageNet fine-tuning under noise (scale 0.1), OPZO achieves 63.39% top-1 accuracy, close to BP's 63.91%, while DFA and ZOsp fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to spatial BP (Xiao et al., 2022), OPZO achieves within 1–4% accuracy on all datasets (e.g., 85.74% vs 90.00% on CIFAR-10, 60.93% vs 64.82% on CIFAR-100), and with local learning, OPZO(w/LL) matches or slightly exceeds BP(w/LL) on neuromorphic datasets (96.06% vs 95.72% on DVS-Gesture, 77.47% vs 76.07% on DVS-CIFAR10). Against DFA (Nøkland, 2016), OPZO shows large and consistent gains, particularly on convolutional networks: +5.84% on CIFAR-10 (85.74% vs 79.90%), +11.43% on CIFAR-100 (60.93% vs 49.50%), and +12.17% on DVS-CIFAR10 (72.77% vs 60.60%). Against DKP (Webster et al., 2020), OPZO outperforms on every dataset, with the largest margins on neuromorphic data (e.g., +33.80% on DVS-CIFAR10, +33.80% on DVS-Gesture). The single-point zeroth-order baseline (ZO_sp) fails to train effectively, achieving only 49.04% on CIFAR-10 and 22.26% on CIFAR-100, confirming that the momentum feedback connections are essential for variance reduction. On ImageNet fine-tuning under noise, OPZO (63.39%) approaches BP (63.91%) while DFA (61.20%) and ZO_sp (52.42%) degrade substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the online training through time (OTTT) framework for temporal credit assignment in SNNs and the SPSA zeroth-order gradient estimator, but departs fundamentally from spatial backpropagation by replacing layer-by-layer error propagation with a single forward pass plus direct top-down feedback. The key conceptual departure is the 'pseudo-zeroth-order' formulation, which decouples the (black-box, non-differentiable) model from the (differentiable) loss function, allowing vector-valued error signals rather than a scalar loss to drive gradient estimation. Momentum feedback connections that track a moving average of the Jacobian estimator distinguish OPZO from both random-feedback methods (DFA) and standard ZO methods, providing theoretical variance guarantees absent in prior direct-feedback approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OPZO provides a concrete path toward on-chip SNN training by eliminating the weight-transport and separate-phase requirements of spatial BP, aligning the update rule with the biologically motivated three-factor Hebbian learning framework. The main open question is scalability: pure OPZO (without local learning or intermediate global learning) shows greater reliance on residual connections for depth than BP, and the ~4-point gap on CIFAR-10/100 suggests the method may not yet fully close the performance gap on harder static benchmarks. Actual deployment on neuromorphic hardware remains future work, as current chips are still under development.
