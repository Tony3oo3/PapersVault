## Problem

The celebrated result that 'almost all linear ODEs are identifiable from a single trajectory' is known to hold only under a measure absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on R^{n×n}, i.e., for dense system matrices. The sparse regime—where sparsity arises naturally in gene regulatory networks, social networks, and physical systems—remains uncharacterized, despite being the setting in which meaningful interpretation and lower overfitting risk are typically assumed. This paper shows that sparse linear ODEs are unidentifiable with positive probability in practically relevant sparsity regimes, putting an important asterisk on the dense-case identifiability guarantee.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a single continuous, noise-free, fully-observed trajectory x(t) over [0,T] generated by the IVP ẋ(t) = Ax(t) with a sparse-continuous random matrix A (Bernoulli mask with sparsity p times i.i.d. continuous entries) and an initial condition x_0 sampled uniformly from the unit sphere in R^n. The theoretical output is a partition of unidentifiability probability into system-level (A globally unidentifiable via Jordan block structure) and trajectory-level (x_0 in a proper A-invariant subspace) components, along with explicit lower bounds and a sharp threshold at p = 1 − ln(n)/n. The empirical output is the estimated system matrix Â from SINDy or Neural ODE, evaluated via normalized Hamming distance against the binarized ground truth A, restricted to well-fitted trajectories (R² > 0.99, MSE < 10⁻⁴).

## Method

The paper partitions the unidentifiability probability into two components: system-level unidentifiability (A has multiple Jordan blocks for the same eigenvalue, equivalently rank(A) < n−1) and trajectory-level unidentifiability (x_0 lies in a proper A-invariant subspace). For sparse-continuous random matrices, it proves a positive lower bound on system-level unidentifiability probability (Lemma 2) driven by the presence of multiple zero columns, and establishes a sharp asymptotic threshold at p = 1 − ln(n)/n (Lemma 3) using Hall-König matching theory on the associated bipartite graph. It then shows that trajectory-level unidentifiability has zero probability for absolutely continuous initial conditions, but introduces a continuous 'distance-to-unidentifiability' metric d_A(x_0) and proves (Lemma 4) that trajectories from nearby competing matrices A and A' remain ε-indistinguishable up to a time horizon T bounded by a Lambert W function expression that scales inversely with d_A(x_0). Empirically, the paper validates these predictions by evaluating SINDy (L1-regularized sparse regression with sequentially thresholded least squares) and Linear Neural ODEs (L1-regularized linear networks integrated via Dopri5) across dimensions n ∈ {3,…,50} and sparsity levels p ∈ {0.1,…,1.0}, confirming that reconstruction accuracy degrades with increasing sparsity and proximity to invariant subspaces.

## Results

The paper establishes a positive lower bound on the probability of global unidentifiability for sparse-continuous random matrices: $1-(1-p^n)^n - np^n(1-p^n)^{n-1}$ (Lemma 2), and proves a sharp asymptotic threshold at $p_c(n) = 1 - \ln(n)/n$ (Lemma 3) above which systems are unidentifiable with probability tending to 1. Empirically, both SINDy and Neural ODEs show a clear increase in normalized Hamming distance (sparsity-pattern mismatch) as sparsity $p$ increases, confirming that theoretical unidentifiability manifests in practice even when trajectory reconstruction quality is high ($R^2 > 0.99$, MSE $< 10^{-4}$). Real-world gene regulatory networks (P. trichocarpa: $p=0.9968$ vs. threshold $0.9957$; A. thaliana: $p=0.9977$ vs. threshold $0.9972$) exceed the identifiability threshold, indicating the problem is practically relevant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates two state-of-the-art system identification methods—SINDy (sparse regression with sequentially thresholded least squares) and Neural ODEs (linear layers with L1 regularization)—as empirical estimators of the true system matrix $A$. Both methods fail to recover the correct sparsity pattern as sparsity increases: the normalized Hamming distance between the estimated and true matrix grows monotonically with $p$ for both estimators (Fig. 4). SINDy additionally exhibits an unexpected up-down gradient at high sparsity and low dimensionality, attributed to its aggressive coefficient thresholding that biases toward all-zero estimates when most true coefficients are already zero. Neural ODEs do not show this artifact but still degrade with sparsity. Critically, these failures persist even after filtering to only well-fitted trajectories ($R^2 > 0.99$), demonstrating that the unidentifiability is inherent to the problem structure rather than a limitation of optimization or model capacity. The paper's theoretical contribution (the unidentifiability threshold) thus explains why neither baseline can succeed in the sparse regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiability theory for linear ODEs from a single trajectory established by Stanhope et al. (2014) and Qiu et al. (2022), which proved that 'almost all' such systems are identifiable under the implicit assumption of dense (Lebesgue-continuous) system matrices. The key departure is showing that this guarantee breaks down under sparsity constraints, which are the practically relevant regime in biological, social, and physical networks. The empirical evaluation leverages Qiu et al.'s Smoothed Condition Number as a trajectory-level metric and benchmarks against SINDy (Brunton et al., 2016) and Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018) as representative state-of-the-art estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that sparse linear ODEs are unidentifiable with positive probability (with a sharp threshold at p = 1 − ln(n)/n) directly challenges the widely-cited justification that 'linear ODEs are identifiable,' which practitioners invoke to assume that a well-fitted model recovers the true dynamics. The empirical confirmation that inductive biases (L1 regularization) and optimization dynamics in SINDy and Neural ODEs do not circumvent this limitation means that even perfect trajectory reconstruction does not guarantee correct system identification. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-linear, noisy, partially observed, and multi-trajectory settings, and deriving computable identifiability metrics from data alone without knowledge of the true system.
