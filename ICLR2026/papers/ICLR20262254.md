## Problem

Schrödinger bridge methods for generative modeling and unpaired data translation lack non-asymptotic high-probability generalization guarantees on the coupling-level KL divergence; prior work (Korotin et al., 2024) provided only expected-risk bounds at the slow O(1/√n) rate. Additionally, the standard Brownian-motion reference process suffers from slow polynomial correlation decay and cannot capture data anisotropy, motivating a theoretically and practically superior reference dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262254/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two unpaired i.i.d. sample sets of equal size n drawn from the source density ρ₀ and target density ρ_T, with no joint pairing required. For image translation, inputs are ALAE latent codes of 1024×1024 FFHQ faces; the output is a parametric log-potential φ̂ (a Gaussian mixture in the adjusted-potential space) that defines an estimated coupling π̂ and, via the drift β*(t,x) = b(m−x) + Σ∇log ν_t*(x), a controlled diffusion for sampling from the target. The empirical objective L̂(φ) depends only on the two marginals, making the setup inherently unpaired.

## Method

The paper replaces the standard Brownian reference with an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process dX_t = b(m−X_t)dt + Σ^{1/2}dW_t, whose exponential mixing and anisotropic covariance Σ enable both tighter theory and better generation. The Schrödinger log-potential is estimated by empirical risk minimization over a parametric class Φ (e.g., logarithms of Gaussian mixtures with K components), where the OU semigroup T_T[e^φ] admits a closed form, yielding the LightSB-OU algorithm. The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) is a high-probability Bernstein-type bound on KL(π*, π̂) − Δ that achieves a near-fast rate of O(log³(n)/n) when the approximation error Δ is small, improving on the O(1/√n) rate of Korotin et al. (2024). The proof hinges on a variance decomposition exploiting the OU process's exponential ergodicity: when the time horizon satisfies bT ≳ log log n, the conditional variance of the log-potential difference is dominated by the unconditional variance, which in turn is controlled sub-quadratically by the KL risk via a cumulant-generating-function argument (Lemma B.1). This Bernstein condition, combined with sub-exponential concentration and an ε-net over the D-dimensional parameter space, yields the final bound with explicit dependence on dimension d, class complexity D, and confidence δ.

## Results

Theoretically, Theorem 1 establishes the first non-asymptotic high-probability bound on KL(π*, π̂), achieving a rate of O(log³(n)/n) in favorable scenarios (small approximation error), which is substantially faster than the O(1/√n) rate of Korotin et al. (2024). Empirically, LightSB-OU achieves W₁ = 0.812 ± 0.020 on single-cell distribution transport, outperforming LightSB (0.823 ± 0.017) and 9 other Schrödinger bridge / flow-based baselines. On 25-component Gaussian mixtures (K=30), LightSB-OU reduces sliced W₁ by approximately 40% relative to LightSB (e.g., 0.156 vs. 0.260 on the standard grid) while achieving perfect mode coverage (25.0 vs. 24.2). On FFHQ image-to-image translation in ALAE latent space, LightSB-OU attains FID = 24.0 versus 24.1 for LightSB, with qualitatively better preservation of skin tone and facial structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightSB-OU consistently outperforms the original LightSB (Korotin et al., 2024) across all evaluated settings: on single-cell data it improves W₁ from 0.823 to 0.812, and on Gaussian mixtures it reduces sliced W₁ by 35–40% while achieving perfect mode coverage. Among the 12 baselines in the single-cell comparison, LightSB-OU ranks third overall, behind only OT-CFM (W₁ = 0.790) and [SF]2M-Exact (W₁ = 0.793), both of which use paired or simulation-based formulations. It clearly beats flow-matching methods (I-CFM at 0.872, SB-CFM at 1.221), Schrödinger bridge solvers (DSB at 0.862, DSBM at 1.775, NLSB at 0.970), and classical optimal transport networks (T.Net at 0.848, Reg.CNF at 0.825). The advantage over LightSB is most pronounced on multi-modal Gaussian mixtures with random grid locations, where LightSB-OU achieves W₁ = 0.214 versus 0.525 for LightSB, suggesting the OU reference process's exponential mixing is particularly beneficial when the target has well-separated modes. On FFHQ image translation the FID gap is marginal (24.0 vs. 24.1), but qualitative inspection shows LightSB-OU better preserves identity-relevant attributes such as skin tone and facial geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LightSB algorithm (Korotin et al., 2024) by replacing the Brownian motion reference process with an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, yielding the LightSB-OU variant. The theoretical framework builds on the stochastic optimal control formulation of Schrödinger bridges (Dai Pra, 1991) and the empirical risk minimization approach to Schrödinger potential estimation. The key departure from prior theoretical work (Korotin et al. 2024; Rigollet & Stromme 2025; Pooladian & Niles-Weed 2025) is twofold: (i) all previous analyses restrict to Brownian motion as the reference dynamics, while this paper exploits the exponential mixing of the OU process, and (ii) the paper provides the first non-asymptotic high-probability Bernstein-type bound on the coupling-level KL divergence, improving upon the O(1/√n) in-expectation rate of Korotin et al. to O(log³(n)/n) in favorable regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result is significant for the theoretical foundations of Schrödinger-bridge-based generative models, which have been widely adopted empirically but lacked rigorous finite-sample guarantees at the coupling level. The Bernstein-type bound and the identification of the OU process's mixing properties as the key enabler of fast rates provide a clear theoretical justification for choosing mean-reverting reference dynamics over pure diffusion. Limitations include the requirement of bounded support for the source distribution and the doubly-logarithmic condition bT ≳ log log n; extending to unbounded supports, heavy-tailed targets, and other exponentially ergodic reference processes are identified as open directions. Practically, the empirical gains over LightSB are consistent but modest (e.g., W1 improvements of ~10–40% on Gaussian mixtures, ~1.5% on single-cell data, negligible FID change on FFHQ), suggesting the theoretical advantage may require larger sample sizes or more challenging distributions to manifest clearly.
