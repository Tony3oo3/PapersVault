## Problem

The original SIREN initialization for sinusoidal neural networks was derived from approximate variance calculations and provides no guarantee on gradient scaling with depth, leading to exponential gradient explosion in deep architectures and spurious high-frequency artifacts that degrade generalization. No prior work had connected the theoretical gradient-scaling behavior of sinusoidal networks to their reconstruction performance, leaving the choice of initialization parameters (weight and bias variances) largely empirical and problem-dependent.

## Input / Output

The network takes low-dimensional coordinate vectors (1D, 2D, or 3D spatial or spatio-temporal) as input and outputs a scalar function value at that coordinate. In practice, inputs are sampled on regular or random grids (e.g., 128×128 for images, 1440×720×30 for ERA-5 wind fields, or 44.2 kHz for audio), and the network is trained on a subsampled subset and evaluated on a denser grid to measure generalization. The tunable parameter w₀ sets the driving frequency of the first layer and is typically chosen near the Nyquist frequency of the input sampling.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form initialization for SIREN by computing fixed points of the pre-activation variance and layer-wise Jacobian variance in the infinite-width/infinite-depth limit. The pre-activation variance converges to σ_a² expressed via the Lambert W function (Theorem 3.1), and the rescaled Jacobian variance converges to σ_g² = c_w²/6 · (1 + e^{−2σ_a²}) (Theorem 3.2). Enforcing σ_g = 1 (stable gradient flow) yields a one-parameter curve c_b(c_w) linking weight and bias variances; the proposed initialization selects the point (c_w, c_b) = (√3, 0), which sets σ_a = 0 and makes deep layers nearly linear, thereby suppressing spectral broadening with depth. NTK analysis confirms that this initialization produces linear (rather than exponential) growth of kernel eigenvalues with depth while keeping input-space gradients constant, enabling depth-independent frequency cutoffs near w₀ and preventing aliasing artifacts in reconstructed signals.

## Results

The proposed initialization (σ_a=0, c_w=√3, c_b=0) achieves σ_g=1, ensuring input-space gradients remain constant with depth while NTK eigenvalues scale linearly (∝L) rather than exponentially (∝σ_g^{2L} ≈ 1.2^L for original SIREN). Across all reconstruction tasks (1D/2D/3D function fitting, image fitting, audio, ERA-5 video, denoising), the proposed scheme consistently yields lower generalization error than all baselines, with the gap widening as network depth increases. On the PINNacle PDE benchmarks, the proposed initialization produces the cleanest Navier-Stokes 2D and Heat equation solutions, while the original SIREN exhibits spurious high-frequency artifacts and FINER/WIRE fail entirely at depth L=15. A notable exception is the 1D Burgers equation, where the original Sitzmann initialization marginally outperforms the proposed scheme, likely due to the sharp propagating front being representable even under ill-conditioned gradients.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original SIREN (Sitzmann et al., 2020), the proposed initialization wins on generalization across all tasks, with the advantage growing with depth: SIREN's NTK eigenvalues and input gradients grow exponentially (σ_g≈√1.2), producing noisy high-frequency artifacts in deep networks, while the proposed scheme keeps both stable. FINER (Liu et al., 2024) and WIRE (Saragadam et al., 2022) are clearly unstable for deep networks (L≥10), producing extremely poor reconstructions in image fitting, audio, and PDE tasks, and are consistently outperformed. Tanh with Fourier features and Xavier initialization (Tancik et al., 2020) is the strongest non-sinusoidal baseline, showing competitive generalization on 1D fitting and ERA-5 video, but still trails the proposed SIREN on high-frequency content recovery. ReLU with Positional Encoding (Nair & Hinton, 2010) performs comparably to Tanh+FF on ERA-5 but degrades on multi-scale 1D/2D functions. The σ_a=1 variant of the proposed initialization (Sitzmann-inspired but with exact σ_g=1) is competitive but produces slightly noisier outputs than σ_a=0 for deep networks, confirming the spectral-control advantage of the vanishing pre-activation choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the edge-of-chaos / mean-field initialization framework (Yang & Schoenholz, 2017; Poole et al., 2016) to sinusoidal activation networks, building directly on SIREN (Sitzmann et al., 2020) and the NTK analysis of de Avila Belbute-Peres & Kolter (2023). Where prior SIREN initializations relied on approximate variance matching (targeting σ_a≈1) and left gradient scaling uncontrolled, this work derives an exact closed-form fixed point via the Lambert W function, uniquely tractable for sine activations. It further connects the gradient-stability condition (σ_g=1) to the NTK eigenvalue spectrum, providing a mechanistic explanation for why deep SIREN networks develop spurious high-frequency artifacts under the original initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proposed initialization (c_w=√3, c_b=0) is a drop-in replacement that stabilizes deep SIREN networks without architectural modifications, removing the need for empirical depth-dependent tuning of w_0. The finding that vanishing pre-activation (σ_a=0) outperforms unit pre-activation (σ_a=1) for spectral control is a non-obvious design principle that could inform initialization choices for other periodic-activation architectures. Open questions include whether full dynamical isometry can be achieved, how the O(1/ℓ) slow convergence at c_w=√3 interacts with finite-width effects in practice, and whether the framework extends to non-sine periodic activations where the Lambert W closed form is unavailable.
