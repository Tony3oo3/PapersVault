## Problem

Multi-expert architectures for Long-Tailed Recognition (LTR) are approaching a performance ceiling due to a dependency chain of entangled learning processes: head-class gradients force all experts into a homogeneous feature space (representation-decision entanglement), which in turn makes it impossible to reliably distinguish model ignorance from data ambiguity when diagnosing difficult samples (cause-symptom entanglement), and the resulting instability destabilizes any meta-learning of adaptive policies (learning-meta-learning entanglement). Existing methods encourage diversity only indirectly at the decision level (e.g., varied logit adjustments) without explicitly decoupling the representation learning itself, leaving the root cause unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet/Places, variable for iNaturalist) or graph-structured molecular data (ZincFluor), processed through a shared ResNet backbone (32/50/152) feeding three parallel expert pathways. Beyond the final class label, the framework internally produces per-class epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty estimates (via ensemble disagreement and average entropy) and expert-specific refinement gate values. Inference uses a two-step strategy: an initial forward pass yields a proxy class prediction that selects class-wise gates, and a second pass applies the gated Dynamic Expert Refinement Module for a refined prediction, trading ~2× latency for +2–3% few-shot accuracy.

## Method

GUIDE resolves the three entanglements hierarchically. Level 1 enforces competitive specialization by simultaneously minimizing pairwise cosine similarity of expert features (L_decouple) and maximizing the Jensen-Shannon Divergence of temperature-scaled expert predictions, preventing homogeneity collapse at both the feature and decision levels. Level 2 decomposes predictive uncertainty into epistemic (JSD, model ignorance) and aleatoric (average entropy, data ambiguity) components, which drive a Dynamic Expert Refinement Module (DERM) via an adaptive residual mixture: each expert's output is the shared foundation feature plus a gated refinement delta, where the gate is a sigmoid of class-level EMA uncertainty statistics with learnable per-expert coefficients. Level 3 applies Two-Timescale Stochastic Approximation (TTSA), updating the main network parameters θ with a large scheduled learning rate at every step while updating the meta-policy controller parameters φ (the gate coefficients) with a much smaller fixed rate (1e-4) every K epochs on a validation set, ensuring the slow policy converges without being overwhelmed by fast-task gradient noise.

## Results

GUIDE establishes new state-of-the-art Top-1 accuracy on all five primary benchmarks under both standard and longer training schedules. On CIFAR-100-LT (IR=100), GUIDE achieves 56.4% (standard) and 57.7% (longer), surpassing the strongest prior method PRL (52.8%) by +3.6 points. On large-scale datasets, GUIDE reaches 62.5% on ImageNet-LT, 76.1% on iNaturalist 2018, and 42.2% on Places-LT (standard), with further gains to 63.4%, 77.8%, and 43.1% under longer training. The gains are most pronounced on few-shot classes, where GUIDE achieves 36.0% on CIFAR-100-LT (IR=100) versus 31.2% for PRL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUIDE outperforms all 17 compared baselines on every benchmark where results are available, with no losses or ties. Against the strongest multi-expert baseline PRL (Zhao et al., 2024), GUIDE gains +3.6% on CIFAR-100-LT (IR=100), +1.7% on ImageNet-LT, +1.0% on iNaturalist 2018, and +0.6% on Places-LT. Against the recent LOS method (Sun et al., 2025), which leads on CIFAR-100-LT IR=10 (69.7% vs. 69.2%), GUIDE still dominates on IR=50 (+1.5%), IR=100 (+1.5%), ImageNet-LT (+8.1%), and iNaturalist 2018 (+5.3%). Among multi-expert methods specifically, GUIDE surpasses BalPoE by +4.4% on CIFAR-100-LT (IR=100) and +3.2% on ImageNet-LT, and outperforms SADE by +7.6% and +3.7% respectively. On the newly proposed MEET-LT geospatial benchmark, GUIDE achieves 75.5% overall (57.5% few-shot), beating the strongest baseline BCL (71.5% overall, 50.0% few-shot) by +4.0 and +7.5 points. On the pathological ZincFluor graph dataset, GUIDE's Tail Top-2 accuracy of 67.7% exceeds BCL's 59.6% by +8.1 points, confirming generality beyond standard vision tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

GUIDE builds on the multi-expert LTR paradigm (RIDE, SADE, BalPoE) but argues these methods suffer from a cascading 'entanglement' problem: head-class gradients collapse expert representations, which in turn makes uncertainty-based adaptation unreliable, which destabilizes meta-learning. It repurposes three established tools—Jensen-Shannon Divergence for ensemble diversity (Krogh & Vedelsby), epistemic/aleatoric uncertainty decomposition (Lakshminarayanan et al.), and Two-Timescale Stochastic Approximation (Borkar)—into a single hierarchical framework where each level is a prerequisite for the next. The key departure from prior multi-expert work is the explicit, simultaneous enforcement of diversity at both the feature and decision levels, combined with a differentiated (rather than agnostic) uncertainty-guided refinement policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a coherent diagnostic for why multi-expert LTR methods appear to be hitting a performance ceiling and offers a principled, theoretically grounded remedy that achieves consistent SOTA across five standard benchmarks plus two novel domains (geospatial, molecular). The ablation confirming that the representation-diversity level must precede the uncertainty-guided policy level (52.8% vs. 51.3% for policy alone) validates the hierarchical dependency claim. Open questions include whether the classical epistemic/aleatoric decomposition (acknowledged as theoretically imperfect by Mucsanyi et al., 2024) remains effective at larger scales, the practical cost of requiring a held-out validation set for meta-policy updates, and whether the two-timescale optimization can be replaced by more modern alternatives like gradient surgery or trust-region methods.
