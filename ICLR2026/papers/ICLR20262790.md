## Problem

Multi-hop knowledge graph question answering systems typically assemble context via fixed k-hop expansions or heuristic knobs (hop depth, degree caps, top-k), which over-retrieve, inflate prompts, introduce distractors, and offer no learned per-query control over what to expand, which paths to follow, what evidence to keep, and when to stop. This paper casts context construction itself as a sequential decision problem under explicit per-query budgets on subgraph edits, interaction steps, and selected tokens, making accuracy–latency–cost trade-offs explicit and tunable without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language question, a typed knowledge graph K=(V,R,E), and per-query budgets β=(β_edge, β_lat, β_tok) (or equivalent Lagrangian prices λ) as input. It produces a predicted answer entity or entity set, a compact curated evidence context D* of textualized snippets within the token budget, and auditable provenance traces (discovered paths, accepted subgraph edits, and selection decisions). At inference, a single trained checkpoint runs under hard budget caps or soft price trade-offs, adapting per query without retraining.

## Method

CLAUSE decomposes context assembly into three cooperative agents operating on a symbolic KG state: a Subgraph Architect that performs conservative frontier-anchored ADD/DELETE/STOP edits, a path navigator that issues CONTINUE/BACKTRACK/STOP along typed outgoing edges, and a context curator that performs listwise, redundancy-aware evidence selection with a learned STOP head. All three agents are trained jointly with LC-MAPPO, a Lagrangian-constrained CTDE variant of MAPPO that uses a centralized multi-head critic (one task head plus three cost heads for edges/steps/tokens), COMA-style counterfactual advantages for per-agent credit assignment, and per-budget dual variables updated by projected ascent. The key mechanism is that step and token costs enter the shaped return r′_t = r^acc_t − λ_edge·c^edge_t − λ_lat·c^lat_t − λtok·c^tok_t directly, so stopping rules and exploration depth are learned rather than hard-coded, and at the optimum the duals equal shadow prices predicting the local slope of accuracy–latency–cost frontiers.

## Results

CLAUSE achieves 71.7 EM@1 on HotpotQA (distractor), 84.2 on FactKG, and 91.0 / 87.3 / 85.5 on MetaQA 1-/2-/3-hop, consistently outperforming all RAG and agent baselines. On MetaQA-2-hop, CLAUSE gains +39.3 EM@1 over the strongest RAG baseline (GraphRAG, 48.0) while simultaneously reducing latency by 18.6% and edge growth by 40.9%. Pretrained LLMs without retrieval (e.g., GPT-OSS-120B at 44.5 on HotpotQA) lag substantially, underscoring the value of budget-aware neuro-symbolic context construction over raw model scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAUSE wins across all three dataset families. Against the strongest RAG baseline (GraphRAG), CLAUSE gains +21.6 EM@1 on HotpotQA (71.7 vs 50.1), +12.2 on FactKG (84.2 vs 72.0), and +39.3 on MetaQA-2-hop (87.3 vs 48.0), while also reducing latency and edge budget. Against the strongest agent baseline (KG-Agent), CLAUSE leads by +3.0 on HotpotQA, +2.1 on FactKG, and +9.3 / +9.3 / +10.1 on MetaQA 1/2/3-hop. Pretrained LLMs without retrieval (GPT-OSS-120B, LLaMA3.3-70B, Qwen3-32B) are the weakest, scoring 27–34 points below CLAUSE on HotpotQA, confirming that model scale alone cannot substitute for structured, budget-aware context. Among RAG methods, Hybrid RAG is the closest on HotpotQA (66.0) but still trails CLAUSE by 5.7 points; LightRAG and Vanilla RAG are further behind. Among agent methods, AutoGen and ReAct are competitive on MetaQA-1-hop (85.2, 82.3) but fall 5.8–8.7 points short of CLAUSE, and the gap widens at 2/3-hop where multi-step deliberation inflates latency without proportional accuracy gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLAUSE builds directly on the MAPPO/PPO/COMA lineage for multi-agent policy optimization, extending it with Lagrangian dual variables and a multi-head centralized critic to enforce three heterogeneous per-episode resource constraints (edge edits, interaction steps, selected tokens). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm in multi-hop KGQA—static k-hop neighborhood expansion (GraftNet, PullNet) or fixed-hop graph-guided RAG (GraphRAG, LightRAG)—by recasting context construction as a sequential, budget-aware decision process with learned stopping. It also distinguishes itself from agentic LLM pipelines (ReAct, AutoGen, Graph-of-Thoughts) that interleave tool calls without explicit per-episode resource accounting, and from single-penalty constrained RL (RCPO) by maintaining separate dual variables per resource type.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, deployable framework where operators can tune accuracy-latency-cost trade-offs at inference time via budget caps or price dials without retraining, which is practically important for production KGQA systems with SLA constraints. The shadow-price interpretation (duals equal marginal EM gain per unit budget relaxation) gives interpretable, actionable signals for resource allocation. Limitations include evaluation primarily on small-to-mid-scale KGs (MetaQA: 43K nodes, 9 relations) and a relatively lightweight neuro-symbolic component (frozen encoders + linear scoring), leaving open whether the learned policies transfer to truly open-domain, billion-edge KGs or whether the three-agent decomposition remains effective under much higher branching factors and noisier entity linking.
