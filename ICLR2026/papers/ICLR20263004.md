## Problem

Despite the empirical success of transformer attention mechanisms, the sample complexity of recovering the underlying pairwise token-token interaction function from aggregated observations remains theoretically uncharacterized. This is fundamentally an inverse problem: one observes only the averaged outputs Y_i = (1/(N-1))Σ_j g(X_i, X_j) + η_i, not the individual interaction values, and must jointly estimate both the nonlinear activation φ and the interaction matrix A. The paper asks whether the convergence rate suffers from the curse of dimensionality in the embedding dimension d, the number of tokens N, or the rank r of A.

## Input / Output

The method consumes M i.i.d. sample pairs (X^m, Y^m) where X^m ∈ ([0,1]^d/√d)^N is an N×d token matrix (tokens may be dependent and anisotropic, not required to be i.i.d.) and Y^m ∈ R^N is the vector of aggregated pairwise interactions. The output is the 2d-dimensional pairwise interaction function ĝ(x,y) = φ̂(x^T Â y), with error measured in the L²_ρ norm where ρ is the empirical exploration measure over off-diagonal token pairs. The key structural constraint is that the rate M^{-2β/(2β+1)} is independent of d, N, and r provided rd ≤ (M/log M)^{1/(2β+1)}, meaning the nonparametric smoothness of φ dominates over the parametric complexity of A.

## Method

The paper frames self-attention as an Interacting Particle System (IPS) where tokens are particles and the forward operator R_g[X]_i = (1/(N-1))Σ_{j≠i} g(X_i, X_j) aggregates pairwise interactions. The estimator is an empirical risk minimizer over a composite class of piecewise-polynomial activations (degree s = max(⌈β⌉,1) on K_M intervals) and low-rank matrices (rank ≤ r, operator norm ≤ ā). The upper bound proof hinges on a coercivity lemma (Lemma 3.4) that, under exchangeability, extracts the L²_ρ error of g from the excess risk, combined with covering number estimates for the matrix class (via SVD factorization and Vershynin's ball-covering bound) and a three-term error decomposition handling sub-Gaussian noise. The lower bound uses a Fano-Tsybakov scheme: it first reduces the 2d-dimensional problem to estimating φ alone with fixed A via a conditional-expectation inequality (Lemma 4.1), then constructs 2^{K/8} well-separated Hölder functions with controlled KL divergence. The resulting minimax rate M^{-2β/(2β+1)} matches up to logarithmic factors and is provably independent of the ambient dimension.

## Results

The paper establishes that the minimax convergence rate for estimating the 2d-dimensional pairwise interaction function $g_\star(x,y) = \phi_\star(x^\top A_\star y)$ in single-layer attention-style models is $M^{-2\beta/(2\beta+1)}$, where $M$ is the sample size and $\beta$ is the Hölder smoothness of the activation function. Crucially, this rate is independent of the embedding dimension $d$, the number of tokens $N$, and the rank $r$ of the interaction matrix, provided $rd \leq (M/\log M)^{1/(2\beta+1)}$. Numerical simulations confirm the theory: convergence slopes are nearly identical across $d \in \{1, 5, 30\}$, and empirical slopes of $\approx -0.81$ (degree-3 B-spline, $\beta=2$) and $\approx -0.899$ (degree-8, $\beta=7$) closely match the theoretical values $-0.800$ and $-0.933$ respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper that does not compare against competing estimation methods or baselines in the standard empirical sense. Instead, the central 'comparison' is between the upper bound (Theorem 3.1, via empirical risk minimization over piecewise-polynomial approximations) and the lower bound (Theorem 4.4, via a Fano-Tsybakov argument), which together establish the minimax optimality of the rate $M^{-2\beta/(2\beta+1)}$. The paper positions itself against prior IPS learning theory (Wang et al. 2025; Lu et al. 2019, 2021, 2022), noting that those works estimate radial (shift-invariant) interaction kernels, whereas the attention-style interaction $\phi(x^\top A y)$ is a 2d-dimensional function that is not shift-invariant due to the matrix $A$. The numerical experiments serve as verification rather than comparison: the B-spline-based estimator achieves the predicted rate, confirming that the theoretical bound is tight in practice. The key distinction from prior attention learning theory (e.g., Troiani et al. 2025; Cui et al. 2024) is that those works assume i.i.d. tokens and study linear/softmax attention specifically, while this paper handles dependent, anisotropic token distributions and a general nonlinear activation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the IPS formulation of attention from Geshkovski et al. (2023, 2025), which views tokens as interacting particles, and extends the nonparametric estimation toolkit of Györfi et al. (2006) (covering numbers, bias-variance decomposition) to the nonlocal, non-shift-invariant setting of attention. The lower bound follows the Fano-Tsybakov scheme of Wang et al. (2025) for nonlocal interaction kernels, but adapts it to the composite structure φ(xᵀAy) where both φ and A are unknown. The key departure from prior IPS inference work (Lu et al. 2019, 2021, 2022) is that the interaction function here is a 2d-dimensional pairwise function that is not shift-invariant due to the weight matrix, and the key departure from prior attention learning theory is the treatment of a general activation function with dependent, anisotropic tokens rather than linear/softmax attention with i.i.d. inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that the minimax rate M^{-2β/(2β+1)} is independent of embedding dimension d, number of tokens N, and matrix rank r—provides a rigorous theoretical explanation for why attention mechanisms scale well to high-dimensional token representations, directly addressing the curse-of-dimensionality concern. The non-convex joint estimation of A and φ, combined with the nonlocal averaging in the forward operator, makes this a genuinely harder inverse problem than standard nonparametric regression, and the matching upper/lower bounds (up to log factors) confirm the rate is tight. Open questions include extending the theory to multi-head attention, residual connections, and the value matrix, as well as closing the logarithmic gap between upper and lower bounds. The analysis is limited to single-layer models with specific distributional assumptions (exchangeability, continuous pair densities), so generalization to deeper architectures remains unaddressed.
