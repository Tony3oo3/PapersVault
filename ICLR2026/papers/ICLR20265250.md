## Problem

Existing norm-based memorization detection metrics for diffusion models (e.g., the norm of the score difference) are only effective under isotropic log-probability distributions, which hold at high or medium noise levels. In the anisotropic low-noise regime—where curvature varies across directions—these norm-based metrics lose discriminative power, as a high norm in one direction can be compensated by a low norm in another. This paper identifies this theoretical limitation and asks how to detect memorization in a regime where the log-probability is anisotropic.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt c and a single pure Gaussian noise sample x_T ~ N(0, I) as input, requiring no denoising trajectory. It produces a scalar memorization detection score M(x_T, c) computed via exactly two conditional and two unconditional forward passes of the diffusion model at artificially set timesteps t≈0 and t=T. For mitigation, the method additionally outputs an optimized prompt embedding c* obtained by gradient descent on the detection metric, which is then used for standard denoising-based generation.

## Method

The core insight is that in the anisotropic low-noise regime, memorized samples exhibit strong angular alignment (high cosine similarity) between the guidance vector ∇log p_t(c|x_t) and the unconditional score ∇log p_t(x_t), because the conditional and unconditional modes nearly coincide (small mode displacement δ). The detection metric M(x_T, c) is a weighted sum of (a) the cosine similarity between the guidance vector and unconditional score at t≈0 (capturing anisotropic alignment) and (b) the norm of the guidance vector at t=T (capturing isotropic curvature), with weights γ₁ and γ₂ tuned per model. Theorem 1 provides a formal lower bound on this cosine similarity in terms of the relative mode displacement δ and covariance mismatch, proving that memorized cases (small δ) yield high alignment. The metric is denoising-free—evaluated directly on pure noise at two timesteps—making it ~5× faster than Hessian-based baselines. For mitigation, the metric serves as a loss for prompt embedding optimization via gradient descent, steering generations away from memorized training samples while preserving text-image alignment.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.4 with n=1 generation, the proposed metric achieves AUC 0.994 and TPR@1%FPR 0.935, outperforming all prior denoising-free methods while running in 1.10 s per 10 prompts (≈5× faster than Jeon et al.'s 5.40 s). With n=4 generations, AUC reaches 0.999 and TPR@1%FPR 0.984 on SD v1.4. On SD v2.0, the method attains AUC 0.953 (n=1) and 0.981 (n=4), with TPR@1%FPR of 0.791 and 0.890 respectively, while remaining 6.6–7.7× faster than the previous best approach. Mitigation experiments on MemBench show the method achieves lower SSCD similarity to memorized training images while maintaining comparable CLIP and Aesthetic scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ren et al. (2024), the proposed method wins decisively on every metric: AUC improves from 0.846 to 0.994 (SD v1.4, n=1) and TPR@1%FPR from 0.116 to 0.935, while remaining only ~22× slower (1.10 s vs 0.05 s). Against Wen et al. (2024), the method achieves higher AUC (0.994 vs 0.976) and TPR@1%FPR (0.935 vs 0.896) on SD v1.4 at n=1, at the cost of ~0.7 s additional runtime per 10 prompts. Against Jeon et al. (2025), the method surpasses AUC on SD v1.4 (0.994 vs 0.987) and TPR@1%FPR (0.935 vs 0.908) while being 4.9× faster (1.10 s vs 5.40 s); on SD v2.0, Jeon et al. retains a marginal AUC edge (0.959 vs 0.953 at n=1), but the proposed method improves TPR@1%FPR by 5.1 percentage points (0.791 vs 0.740) and runs 6.6× faster (2.20 s vs 14.60 s). Overall, the method is the only one that simultaneously achieves top detection accuracy and near-real-time speed across both model versions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geometric analysis of memorization in diffusion models initiated by Wen et al. (2024), who proposed the norm of the score difference as a detection metric, and Jeon et al. (2025), who showed this norm approximates the trace of the log-probability Hessian. The key conceptual departure is proving that such norm-based metrics are only valid under isotropic log-probability (high/mid noise) and identifying angular alignment between the guidance vector and unconditional score as the relevant signature in the anisotropic low-noise regime. The resulting metric combines both regimes into a single denoising-free score, and the mitigation strategy reuses Wen et al.'s prompt augmentation framework with the new metric as the optimization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anisotropy explanation provides a principled reason why existing norm-based metrics degrade in the low-noise regime, which is precisely where image-level memorization detection (e.g., Jiang et al., 2025) operates. The 5x speedup over Jeon et al. (no Hessian computation) makes large-scale memorization screening practical. However, the alignment component is unreliable for local memorization (as shown in the SD v2.0 ablation), the method is only validated on SD v1.4/v2.0, and the timestep mismatch (querying at t≈0 with pure-noise input) lacks a formal justification—these are open questions for broader adoption to newer architectures.
