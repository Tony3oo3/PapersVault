## Problem

CLIP-based image clustering methods are optimized for image-text alignment and may fail to capture spatial structures (object shape, position, part layout) that are critical for distinguishing fine-grained categories. Additionally, existing text-guided clustering approaches assume textual features are universally beneficial, yet empirical evidence shows they can degrade performance on datasets where textual descriptions lack semantic relevance to the visual content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265274/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled raw image dataset D = {x_n} of N images as input, with the number of clusters K known a priori. Visual features are extracted via CLIP ViT-B/32, patch-level features via a pretrained ResNet-50 (final pooling and FC layers removed), and textual features via soft retrieval over top-5 WordNet nouns per K-Means cluster center. The output is a set of soft cluster assignment distributions from the distilled visual modality MLP head (512-512-K), from which hard assignments are derived for evaluation.

## Method

SATC integrates three components: (1) a GAT-based spatial encoder that divides each image into patches, builds a graph with edges based on patch feature similarity, applies graph attention to capture relational dependencies among patches, and pools the result into a spatial embedding; (2) a compactness-aware textual feature selector that computes the average intra-cluster distance τ after K-Means (K_t=10) on textual features, including text only when τ > 0.33, with a Fano-inequality-based theoretical lower bound justifying the criterion; (3) a tri-modal mutual distillation framework where three modality-specific MLP cluster heads are trained jointly with bidirectional distillation loss (aligning cluster distributions across modalities via softmax-similarity), consistency loss (dot-product of same-sample assignments), and entropy loss (encouraging confident assignments), with final cluster assignments taken from the visual head.

## Results

SATC achieves state-of-the-art clustering performance across all 18 benchmark datasets, with average ACC of 73.7%, NMI of 76.3%, and ARI of 63.8%. On the five primary evaluation datasets, SATC reaches 99.8% ACC on ImageNet-10, 91.4% on ImageNet-Dogs, 99.0% on STL-10, 94.5% on CIFAR-10, and 63.4% on CIFAR-100. The most dramatic gains appear on fine-grained and structurally complex datasets: SATC improves over TAC by +7.0% ACC on ImageNet-Dogs (91.4% vs. 84.4%), +4.4% on CIFAR-100 (63.4% vs. 59.0%), and +50.0% on MNIST (89.8% vs. 39.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SATC outperforms all 21 compared baselines on all five primary datasets. Against the closest text-guided competitor TAC, SATC gains +0.3% ACC on ImageNet-10, +7.0% on ImageNet-Dogs, +0.7% on STL-10, +2.3% on CIFAR-10, and +4.4% on CIFAR-100. Against the strongest internal-supervision methods (SPICE, DPAC), SATC leads by +2.8 to +23.9% ACC depending on dataset, with the largest margin on ImageNet-Dogs (91.4% vs. 72.6% for DPAC). Foundation-model baselines DINOv2+K-Means and DINOv3+K-Means are competitive on ImageNet-10 (96.3–96.7% vs. SATC's 99.8%) but fall significantly behind on fine-grained datasets like ImageNet-Dogs (87.5% for DINOv2 vs. 91.4% for SATC) and CIFAR-100 (62.5% vs. 63.4%). Classical and early deep clustering methods (K-Means, DEC, DAC, DCCM, DSEC) are outperformed by 40–80+ percentage points across all datasets. The most striking single-dataset result is on MNIST, where SATC achieves 89.8% ACC versus 39.8% for TAC and 56.5% for CLIP+K-Means, demonstrating the value of spatial structure for structurally simple but spatially informative data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the external-textual-guidance line of image clustering initiated by TAC (Li et al., 2023), which first demonstrated that CLIP-derived textual features can serve as auxiliary supervision for clustering. SATC departs from TAC in two key ways: it identifies that CLIP's image-text alignment objective may compromise spatial structure capture (addressed via a GAT-based patch encoder inspired by GATCluster's graph attention, but applied intra-image rather than inter-sample), and it challenges TAC's implicit assumption that textual features are universally beneficial by introducing a compactness-based selector that adaptively includes or excludes the text modality per dataset. The tri-modal mutual distillation framework unifies these components, building on the mutual distillation paradigm from contrastive clustering (CC, TCL) but extending it to three modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical insight that textual features from CLIP/WordNet actively degrade clustering on the majority of standard benchmarks (11 of 18 datasets) is a useful caution for the multimodal clustering community, and the compactness threshold provides a simple, dataset-agnostic decision rule. However, the threshold (0.33) is empirically derived from the same 18 datasets used for evaluation, raising questions about its generalizability to unseen data distributions. The method also relies on two frozen pretrained backbones (CLIP ViT-B/32 and ResNet-50), limiting its applicability to settings where such models are available or where the data domain differs substantially from their pretraining. Open questions include whether the selector can be learned end-to-end, whether the approach extends to video or 3D clustering, and whether the Fano-inequality-based theoretical bound can be tightened to provide actionable guidance beyond the binary include/exclude decision.
