## Problem

Existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods (Deformable 3D Gaussians, 4D-GS, TiNeuVox) rely on timestamp-conditioned deformation networks that excel at interpolation within the observed time window but suffer out-of-distribution failures when extrapolating to future timestamps. Dynamic scene extrapolation—predicting future 3D scene dynamics from past observations—remains largely underexplored, as it is inherently under-constrained with infinitely many possible futures given past data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a calibrated RGB image sequence (e.g., 800×800 for D-NeRF) with corresponding camera poses (SE(3)) and timestamps, using 75–90% of the temporal sequence for training depending on the dataset. It produces rendered RGB images at arbitrary future timestamps beyond the observed window via a continuous-time rendering operator, along with the underlying 3D Gaussian parameter trajectories (position, rotation, scale per Gaussian). Notably, the extrapolation model conditions on a prefix of Gaussian parameter trajectories rather than explicit timestamps, and the interpolation model is frozen after training to serve as a trajectory data generator.

## Method

ODE-GS uses a two-stage decoupled pipeline: first, a canonical 3D Gaussian set and time-conditioned deformation MLP (following Deformable GS) are trained for interpolation within the observed window, then frozen; second, a Transformer Latent ODE is trained on Gaussian trajectories generated by the frozen interpolation model. The Transformer encoder (5 layers, 128-dim, 8 heads) processes a context sequence of 10-dimensional Gaussian parameters with sinusoidal positional encodings to produce a 64-dim latent state, which is evolved forward in time by a neural ODE (4-layer MLP with Tanh activations) solved via the DOPRI5 adaptive solver. A decoder maps evolved latent states back to Gaussian parameters, which are rendered via differentiable rasterization. A dynamic trajectory sampling strategy varies the context start time to expose the model to diverse forecasting horizons, while adaptive regularization (latent velocity-field smoothness and 3D position acceleration penalties) with EMA-based weighting enforces physically plausible, smooth extrapolated dynamics.

## Results

On D-NeRF, ODE-GS achieves an average of 27.30 PSNR, 0.9497 SSIM, and 0.0467 LPIPS-vgg, improving over the previous SOTA (GaussianPrediction) by 18.6%. On NVFi, it reaches 33.43 PSNR, 0.9471 SSIM, and 0.0603 LPIPS, improving over NVFi by 20% and GaussianPrediction by 39.6%. On real-world HyperNeRF, ODE-GS delivers consistent gains in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS over all baselines. Overall, the method improves over the best existing extrapolation method by an average of 21.4% PSNR, 7.4% SSIM, and 30.5% LPIPS across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODE-GS outperforms all six baselines on the vast majority of scenes across D-NeRF, NVFi, and HyperNeRF. Against the closest extrapolation-oriented competitor GaussianPrediction, ODE-GS achieves substantially larger margins (e.g., +10 dB PSNR on Mutant, +7 dB on Standup in D-NeRF; +39.6% average improvement on NVFi), while GaussianPrediction suffers severe failures on some scenes (12.25 PSNR on Lego, 12.96 on bat). Interpolation-based methods (Deformable-GS, 4D-GS, 4D-Rotor-Gaussians, TiNeuVox) degrade significantly when extrapolated beyond their training window due to timestamp-conditioned design, with 4D-GS particularly struggling on complex scenes (16.84 PSNR on Hellwarrior, 19.83 on bat). On NVFi, ODE-GS also surpasses the dedicated extrapolation method NVFi by 20% on average, with the largest gains in cluttered indoor scenes (factory, darkroom, chessboard) where LPIPS is reduced by over 40%. The one notable exception is the fallingball scene on NVFi, where ODE-GS (22.62 PSNR) underperforms NVFi (31.37 PSNR), which the authors attribute to the interpolation model failing to capture the fast-moving small object.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the canonical-plus-deformation paradigm from Deformable 3D Gaussians (Yang et al., 2024) for the interpolation stage, and on the Latent ODE formulation (Rubanova et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2018) for continuous-time dynamics modeling, but departs from both by decoupling scene reconstruction from temporal forecasting. Unlike prior dynamic scene methods (TiNeuVox, 4D-GS, Deformable-GS) that condition on explicit timestamps and thus fail out-of-distribution, and unlike GaussianPrediction (Zhao et al., 2024) which uses discrete GCN-based prediction, ODE-GS reformulates extrapolation as a sequence-to-sequence problem in a continuous latent space, eliminating timestamp dependency entirely. The differentiable rasterizer from 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) provides the rendering backbone, while the Transformer encoder aggregates past Gaussian trajectories into a latent state evolved by a neural ODE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dynamic scene extrapolation is a prerequisite for applications in autonomous driving, robotics, and AR where systems must anticipate future states rather than merely reconstruct past ones. The decoupling strategy—freezing a high-fidelity interpolation model and training a separate ODE forecaster on its generated trajectories—provides a clean, modular architecture that avoids the OOD failures plaguing timestamp-conditioned methods. Key limitations remain: the method inherits interpolation errors (e.g., failing on fast-moving small objects like fallingball), degrades on scenes with abrupt discontinuities, and the preliminary multi-scene generalization experiment shows cross-scene transfer is possible but does not yet match per-scene performance. Open questions include whether data-driven priors learned across diverse scenes can handle fundamentally novel future behaviors, and whether the continuous-time formulation extends to higher-dimensional or topologically varying scenes.
