## Problem

Existing Neural Combinatorial Optimization solvers fall into two extremes—Local Construction (feasible but myopic) and Global Prediction (globally aware but constraint-violating)—and the recently proposed Adaptive Expansion paradigm, while bridging both, is implemented only as an external wrapper around GP backbones (e.g., COExpander), bounding solution quality by the backbone and scaling inference cost as O(D_s · C_GP). The paper asks whether adaptive expansion can be made native to a generative model, so that expansion progress is instance-adaptive and intermediate diffusion states are semantically valid partial solutions rather than detached probability heatmaps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph instance G(V,E) with task-specific node/edge features (2D coordinates for TSP, adjacency for MIS, coordinates plus demands and depot indicators for CVRP) and produces a feasible binary solution vector x ∈ {0,1}^N, where N = n² for edge-selection problems (TSP, CVRP) and N = n for node-selection (MIS). Crucially, every intermediate state along the diffusion trajectory is a valid partial solution (a subset of the final solution's support), and the final output is guaranteed feasible at every expansion step via a task-specific projection operator Γ(·) that enforces degree, independence, or capacity constraints.

## Method

NExCO is a CO-specific masked diffusion framework built on three components. First, a one-way corruption process (1→0 only) unifies the [MASK] and 0 states into a single background, ensuring intermediate states remain on the feasible manifold and avoiding the imbalance-driven conservatism of standard three-state MDMs. Second, a time-agnostic anisotropic GNN denoiser (12-layer GCN) removes timestep embeddings entirely—since the mask fraction directly encodes corruption level—and is trained with a time-agnostic optimization consistency loss that enforces all corrupted states of an instance to map to the same reference solution. Third, at inference, the Native Adaptive Expansion (NAE) strategy starts from a fully masked state and iteratively queries the denoiser once per step, forms candidate sets by thresholding confidence scores, applies a feasibility projection Γ(·) that greedily accepts candidates in descending confidence order only when a local boolean predicate holds, and retains a fraction ρ_t of accepted entries—yielding O(T_s) denoiser calls with monotone convergence to a complete feasible solution in at most N_max steps.

## Results

On TSP-1000, NExCO (D_s=7) achieves a 0.52% optimality gap in 1.68s, outperforming Fast T2T (0.58% in 5.81s) by both quality and a 3.5× speedup. On MIS (ER-[700-800]), NExCO (D_s=7) reduces the drop from 9.31% (Fast T2T) to 4.20% while being 2.2× faster. On CVRP-100, NExCO (D_s=5) achieves a 1.40% gap versus 4.19% for COExpander and 2.37% for Sym-NCO, with a 1.6–2× runtime advantage. Across all three problem families, NExCO consistently delivers the best quality–efficiency trade-off among learning-based solvers, with up to 4× faster inference than prior state-of-the-art diffusion solvers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against exact/heuristic solvers (Concorde, LKH3, HGS, KaMIS), NExCO achieves near-optimal gaps (≤0.52% on TSP, ≤4.20% on MIS, ≤2.45% on CVRP) at 10–100× lower runtime. Among learning-based LC methods (AM+BS, BQ-NCO, LEHD, Sym-NCO), NExCO dominates on both quality and speed: e.g., on TSP-1000 it cuts the gap from 29.24% (AM) to 0.52% while being 3.5× faster than BQ-NCO. Against GP diffusion solvers (GCN, DIMES, DIFUSCO, T2T, Fast T2T, DISCO), NExCO consistently improves the optimality gap by 10–50% relative and is 2–4× faster; the most direct comparison is with Fast T2T, where NExCO (D_s=7) achieves 0.52% vs. 0.58% on TSP-1000 in 1.68s vs. 5.81s. Against the AE wrapper COExpander, NExCO wins on both axes: on TSP-500 it reaches 0.25% in 0.43s vs. COExpander's 0.52% in 0.61s, and on CVRP-100 it achieves 1.40% vs. 4.19%. On TSP-10K, NExCO (1.53% in 52s) outperforms both DISCO (2.88% in 92s) and GLOP (4.90% in 15s) in quality while remaining competitive in speed. The only setting where NExCO does not strictly dominate is TSP-100 with D_s=3, where it ties Fast T2T (T_s=5, T_g=5) at 0.04% gap but is still 6× faster (0.05s vs. 0.31s).

## Relation to Prior Work

NExCO sits at the intersection of three research threads: masked diffusion models for discrete generative modeling (Austin et al., 2021), diffusion-based CO solvers with optimization consistency (DIFUSCO, T2T, Fast T2T), and the Adaptive Expansion paradigm introduced by COExpander (Ma et al., 2025). The key departure is making AE *native* to the diffusion process: rather than wrapping a GP predictor with an external expansion loop (COExpander's O(D_s·C_GP) design), NExCO reinterprets the diffusion trajectory itself as a constructive expansion over feasible partial solutions, using a CO-specific one-way corruption (1→0 masking) and a time-agnostic GNN denoiser that requires no timestep conditioning. The feasibility projection operator Γ(·) replaces post-hoc heuristic decoding, ensuring every intermediate state is a valid partial solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: up to 4× faster inference than prior SOTA diffusion solvers while simultaneously reducing optimality gaps, and the framework naturally extends to CVRP where GP-based diffusion methods have not been applied due to capacity constraints. The conceptual contribution—showing that intermediate diffusion states can be semantically meaningful partial solutions when corruption is structurally aligned—opens a path toward constructive decoding in other discrete generative settings. Open questions include scalability to industrial-scale instances (the paper acknowledges needing divide-and-conquer or sparse-attention backbones), whether the time-agnostic design generalizes to problems where the feasible manifold is less monotone, and how the framework interacts with test-time scaling strategies like those in Li et al. (2025).
