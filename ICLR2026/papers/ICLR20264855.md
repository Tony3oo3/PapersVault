## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods for long-context LLMs either score token importance in isolation (compromising semantic coherence) or rely on iterative clustering that is computationally expensive and sensitive to initialization. A key unexplored structure is that during prefilling, key embeddings naturally separate into two categories—position-determined tokens that follow local similarity and a small set of semantic-anchored tokens that deviate locally yet form tight clusters—yet no prior method exploits this dual structure for compression.

## Input / Output

The method consumes key vectors from all attention heads of a long-context prompt during the prefilling stage (no query vectors needed for the compression decision itself). It produces a per-head binary mask that selects which KV pairs to retain under a fixed budget B (typically 64–512 tokens), applied via element-wise multiplication to the original key and value matrices. The compression is performed once at prefilling and the retained cache is used for all subsequent autoregressive decoding steps.

## Method

ProtoKV first identifies Semantic-Anchored Tokens (SATs) by computing a κ-neighborhood similarity for each key embedding and flagging tokens whose outlier degree (normalized deviation from the mean) exceeds a threshold; the remaining Position-Determined Tokens (PDTs) are partitioned into contiguous chunks. SATs are projected into a low-dimensional space via Random Fourier Features, binarized, and hashed into u buckets to form SAT semantic prototypes (cluster centroids), while PDT chunks yield PDT prototypes via mean-pooling. Every token is then assigned to its nearest prototype by cosine similarity, forming u+v semantic clusters. Finally, an observation-window attention score (summed dot products between query vectors in the last L_obs positions and each cluster's keys) ranks the clusters, and the top clusters are retained to satisfy the KV budget. The entire pipeline is training-free, runs in O(nkd) time, and avoids iterative optimization by directly constructing high-quality cluster centers from the inherent token structure.

## Results

On LongBench (16 tasks, average length 5k–15k tokens), ProtoKV achieves a 2.11% average accuracy gain over the best SOTA baseline across KV cache budgets of 64–512, with per-budget improvements ranging from 0.35% to 4.27%. On the RULER benchmark (Llama3-8B, budget 128/512), ProtoKV scores 98.2/99.6 on NIAH and 42.8/43.6 on QA. In the Needle In A Haystack evaluation at 1.6% KV retention (budget 128), ProtoKV attains 97.3% retrieval accuracy, surpassing SnapKV (94.2%) while StreamingLLM and H2O nearly collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProtoKV consistently outperforms all eight baselines on LongBench. Against the strongest chunk-based competitor SnapKV, ProtoKV gains 0.14% average (39.95 vs 39.81) at budget 256 on Llama3-8B, and a larger 2.0% gap (37.84 vs 35.84) on Mistral-7B at budget 128. Against H2O and StreamingLLM, the margins are substantially larger (3.1% and 5.7% respectively on Llama3-8B at budget 256). On RULER NIAH, ProtoKV (98.2) edges out SnapKV (97.2) and ChunkKV (97.2) at budget 128, while on QA it leads all baselines at both budget levels. In the Needle In A Haystack test at 1.6% retention, ProtoKV (97.3%) beats SnapKV (94.2%) by 3.1 points, whereas StreamingLLM and H2O drop below 60%. Against dynamic cluster-based methods (ClusterKV, SqueezeAtt, SentenceKV) evaluated under matched prefilling-only constraints, ProtoKV leads by 0.31–0.55 points on LongBench average (40.76 vs 40.21–40.45), attributed to its non-iterative prototype construction avoiding K-means convergence issues.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoKV builds directly on SnapKV's observation-window scoring mechanism (Eq. 11) but elevates it from token-level to cluster-level selection, addressing what the authors identify as the core weakness of both chunk-based (SnapKV, ChunkKV) and token-level (H2O, StreamingLLM) eviction: the assumption that positional proximity implies semantic similarity. The key conceptual departure is the empirical discovery of two token categories — Position-Determined Tokens (PDTs) governed by RoPE-induced local similarity, and Semantic-Anchored Tokens (SATs) that violate this locality and form tight clusters in key space — a distinction prior work (Zandieh et al., Liu et al.) overlooked by focusing only on the manifold structure of PDTs. Methodologically, ProtoKV replaces the iterative K-means of ClusterKV and Squeeze Attention with a single-pass LSH bucketing for SATs plus contiguous chunking for PDTs, reducing complexity from O(ent) to O(nkd).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LLMs spontaneously organize semantic anchors into compact clusters during prefilling provides a useful interpretability lens that unifies previously separate observations (attention sinks, label-word anchors in ICL, punctuation as memory carriers) under a single 'local deviation' framework. Practically, ProtoKV offers a training-free, single-pass compression strategy that is competitive with or slightly ahead of iterative clustering methods while being substantially cheaper, making it attractive for production long-context serving. Limitations include evaluation only on 7–8B models with ≤32K context, reliance on RoPE-based architectures for the PDT/SAT dichotomy, and a modest 2.11% average gain that may narrow at larger scales. Open questions include whether the SAT clustering property persists in non-RoPE or multi-modal settings, and whether the LSH bucketing can be replaced by learned prototypes for further gains.
