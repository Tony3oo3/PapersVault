## Problem

Existing benchmarks for evaluating logical consistency in LLMs (BeliefBank, EntailmentBank, LFC, Set-LConVQA/Set-SNLI) are small, shallow, lack explicit reasoning paths, and are no longer challenging—frontier models already exceed 95% accuracy on them. There is no scalable, diverse, and sufficiently difficult benchmark that can discriminate among state-of-the-art models on multi-step logical consistency reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260028/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample consists of k (2–5) natural-language sentences generated from symbolic propositional formulas via template-based translation with WordNet lexical diversity. Task 1 (Discriminative) takes the k statements plus a specific Boolean label list and requires a yes/no consistency judgment. Task 2 (Enumerative) takes only the k statements and requires the model to list every Boolean assignment that keeps them jointly consistent (out of 2^k total). Task 3 (Generative) takes n groups of 10 consistent premises and requires generating n new statements that remain mutually consistent across all cross-group combinations. Evaluation uses zero-shot, 3-shot, and 3-shot-with-reasoning-path prompting at temperature 0.

## Method

LogiConBench automatically constructs a 280K-sample corpus through a four-stage pipeline grounded in natural deduction rules for five operators (→, ∨, ∧, ¬, ↔). First, it grows logical graphs indefinitely by iteratively applying introduction rules: implication and biconditional appear as directed edges, conjunction and disjunction as node-level compositions, and negation as both node and edge (contradiction) markers. Second, it samples k target nodes within bounded graph distance (≤6) and extracts the minimal connecting subgraph via a permutation-based Steiner tree search, yielding an ordered edge list. Third, it performs DFS-based Boolean labeling along the edges using a constraint-semantics table (LOGIC_RULES) to enumerate all consistent and inconsistent label projections onto the target set. Fourth, it applies elimination rewrite rules (simplification, NNF, CNF/DNF, equivalence elimination) for structural variety, then translates all formulas into natural language using structural templates and random WordNet noun/adjective/verb substitutions. This design yields unlimited scalability, precise ground-truth labels, explicit multi-step reasoning paths (depth up to 32), and remains challenging—best exact accuracy on the Enumerative task is only 34%.

## Results

On Task 1 (Discriminative), frontier models (gpt-5, grok-4-fast, deepseek-r1-0528, gemini-2.5-pro) achieve 85–95% overall accuracy under 3-shot with reasoning paths, while smaller models (llama-3.1-8b, gpt-4o) drop to 32–50%. On Task 2 (Enumerative), the benchmark is dramatically harder: gpt-5 achieves only 34% average exact accuracy (51% on the short-path subset), grok-4-fast reaches ~20%, and most other models fall below 1% exact accuracy. On Task 3 (Generative), even the best model (grok-4-fast) sees consistency drop from 83.6% (2 statements) to 42.7% (5 statements). Across all tasks, 3-shot prompting with reasoning paths consistently outperforms zero-shot (e.g., average Task 1 accuracy rises from 59.21% to 67.58%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions LogiConBench against four existing logical consistency benchmarks: BeliefBank (12.5K samples, depth 1, 2 rules), EntailmentBank (1.8K samples, avg depth 6, 6 rules), LFC (~2K samples, depth ≤4, 9 rules), and Set-LConVQA/Set-SNLI (13.8K samples, depth ≤5, 51 rules). The critical finding is that all four existing benchmarks are saturated: gpt-5 and grok-4-fast already exceed 95% accuracy on them (Figure 1a), rendering them insufficient for discriminating frontier model capabilities. LogiConBench (280K samples, depth up to 32, 280K+ rule combinations) remains challenging, with the best model achieving only 34% exact accuracy on the Enumerative task. In terms of scale and structure, LogiConBench is 20× larger than the next-largest benchmark (Set-LConVQA), supports 32× the maximum reasoning depth of BeliefBank, and is the only one with explicit reasoning paths and unlimited scalability. The paper further validates its real-world relevance by showing moderate-to-strong Pearson/Spearman correlations (0.53–0.79) between LogiConBench scores and downstream benchmarks (LiveCodeBench, InfiniteBench, AIME, AA-LCR, ACE Bench), with Task 2 correlating most strongly with long-horizon and multi-context tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the natural deduction framework of Liu & Stokhof (2024) for its five logical operators and the symbolic-to-natural-language translation approach of Morishita et al. (2023), but departs fundamentally from prior logical consistency benchmarks (BeliefBank, EntailmentBank, LFC, Set-SNLI) by replacing human-annotated or knowledge-graph-derived data with an automated pipeline that generates logical graphs, extracts Steiner-tree reasoning paths, and propagates Boolean labels via DFS. The key conceptual shift is from static, small-scale constraint sets to a scalable graph-generative framework where depth, rule combinations, and difficulty are all controllable parameters, enabling the first benchmark that remains unsaturated for frontier LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With existing logical consistency benchmarks saturated above 95% accuracy for top models, this work provides the field with a scalable, challenging evaluation that exposes a critical capability gap: frontier models can detect individual contradictions (Task 1, ~90%) but fail catastrophically at systematic enumeration of all consistent assignments (Task 2, <1% for most models). The strong correlations with code, math, long-context, and agent benchmarks validate that logical consistency is a genuine predictor of real-world reliability, not an artificial metric. Open questions include whether template-based natural language generation limits ecological validity, whether the single-turn evaluation captures multi-turn consistency failures, and whether LogiConBench data can be used for training or alignment to close the enumeration gap.
