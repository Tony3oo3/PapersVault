## Problem

Existing multimodal RLVR algorithms (GRPO, DAPO) broadcast a single, coarse binary reward uniformly across all tokens and trajectories, failing to distinguish perception-grounded reasoning paths from those that arrive at correct answers via linguistic priors or hallucination. This indiscriminate signal dilutes the learning objective, preventing models from developing genuine visual perception capabilities during reinforcement learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263506/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a textual query as input and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory ending in a verifiable final answer (scored by exact match). During training, a second forward pass is performed with a perturbed image (random 14×14 patches blackened with probability 0.5) to compute per-token visual dependency scores, adding approximately 10% computational overhead over the baseline. The model is trained on the ViRL39K dataset with 8 rollouts per prompt and a binary accuracy reward.

## Method

VPPO modifies the GRPO/DAPO clipped surrogate objective with a two-tiered signal modulation based on token-level visual dependency, defined as the KL divergence between the policy's output distribution conditioned on the true image versus a randomly patch-blackened version. At the micro-level (Token-level Gradient Filtering), a binary mask retains only the top-k% (k=0.4) most visually-dependent tokens for gradient computation, filtering out noise from non-perceptual tokens. At the macro-level (Trajectory-level Advantage Shaping), the mean token dependency is normalized within each batch to a scaling factor α(τ) ∈ [0.9, β_max] that rescales the standard GRPO advantage, amplifying updates for high-perception trajectories and dampening low-perception ones. A theoretical analysis shows the resulting gradient estimator has variance reduced by a factor of approximately k·E[α(τ)²] relative to GRPO, yielding faster convergence and more stable training.

## Results

On eight multimodal reasoning benchmarks (avg@8 accuracy), VPPO achieves 57.5% average on the 7B scale, a 19.2-point improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model (38.3%) and a 2.5-point gain over the strongest open-source RL baseline DAPO (55.0%). At the 32B scale, VPPO reaches 64.6% average, surpassing DAPO (63.5%) and the base model (57.0%) by 1.1 and 7.6 points respectively. The method also shows superior training dynamics with faster initial convergence and only ~10% computational overhead from the second forward pass for dependency calculation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPPO outperforms all nine compared baselines across both model scales. At 7B, it leads the closest competitor PAPO-D (55.3%) by 2.2 points and DAPO (55.0%) by 2.5 points on average accuracy, with the largest margins over ThinkLite-7B (49.4%, +8.1 pts) and MM-Eureka-7B (51.7%, +5.8 pts). At 32B, VPPO (64.6%) surpasses DAPO (63.5%) by 1.1 points and MM-Eureka-32B (60.8%) by 3.8 points. The gains are consistent across all eight benchmarks, with particularly large improvements on Geo3k (+5.0 pts over DAPO at 7B) and MathVision (+2.8 pts over DAPO at 7B), suggesting VPPO's token-level filtering is most beneficial for geometry and visual-math tasks. On out-of-domain VQA (A-OKVQA, SimpleVQA), VPPO also achieves the highest average (65.9%) among RL-tuned variants, confirming that the perceptual focus transfers to general visual understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPPO builds directly on the GRPO/DAPO policy gradient framework for RLVR, modifying the clipped surrogate objective with a token-level binary mask and a trajectory-level advantage scaling factor. It departs from the dominant multimodal RL strategies (data curation, reward engineering, rollout modification) by intervening at the token level within the policy update itself, using a KL-divergence-based visual dependency metric to identify which tokens are genuinely perception-driven. The paper also connects to the 'pivotal tokens' literature in text-only LLMs (high-entropy forking points, contrastive token estimation) but reframes the concept: in multimodal reasoning, the critical token is one with high visual dependency, not necessarily high predictive entropy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete, actionable bottleneck in multimodal RLVR—the uniform broadcasting of a single reward to all tokens and trajectories—and provides a plug-and-play fix requiring only ~10% additional compute. The finding that entropy-based token selection (effective for text-only LLMs) fails for multimodal tasks, because visually-grounded facts carry low entropy but high visual dependency, offers a useful conceptual correction for the field. Open questions include whether the method scales beyond 32B parameters, whether the masking strategy and hyperparameters (k, β range) transfer across domains, and whether single-pass approximations of visual dependency can eliminate the second forward pass.
