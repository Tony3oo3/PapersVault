## Problem

Bayesian optimization over graph structures lacks a principled framework: existing methods either restrict the search to a fixed graph or rely on sampling/evolutionary acquisition optimization that cannot embed problem-specific structural constraints or guarantee global optimality of the acquisition function. This paper addresses the missing link between graph kernels and global combinatorial optimization by formulating acquisition optimization over the full space of connected graphs as a mixed-integer program.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261591/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each BO iteration, the method consumes a dataset of (graph, node-feature matrix, observed function value) triples, bounds on graph size [n₀, n], and optional structural constraints (e.g., molecular valency rules). It outputs a single next query: a connected undirected graph with n nodes, each carrying M binary features (the first L encoding a one-hot label). The output is guaranteed feasible with respect to the encoded constraints, unlike sampling-based baselines that frequently produce infeasible structures in molecular design settings.

## Method

BoGrape encodes the shortest-path structure of an unknown graph using three sets of MIP variables—binary adjacency A, integer distances d, and binary path-membership indicators δ—whose feasible region is proven bijective to the set of all connected graphs of a given size (Theorem 3.5). Four shortest-path kernel variants (SP, SSP, ESP, ESSP) are then expressed as linear or piecewise-linear functions of these variables via indicator tricks, allowing the GP posterior mean, variance, and LCB acquisition to be written entirely as MIP constraints. The resulting mixed-integer program is solved globally (via Gurobi) to select the next query, with problem-specific constraints (e.g., molecular feasibility from Limeade) added directly to the formulation. This is the first graph BO framework where the acquisition optimization is a global combinatorial optimization over the graph domain rather than a heuristic search, providing regret-bound guarantees while supporting arbitrary structural constraints.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks (GAT, GCN, GraphSAGE with N=10 and N=20), BoGrape with all four shortest-path kernel variants (SP, SSP, ESP, ESSP) consistently outperforms Random, RW-rand, WL-rand, and WL-evol baselines in best-objective value over 50 BO iterations. On the molecular design case study (QM7 and QM9, N=10–30), BoGrape again outperforms all sampling-based baselines regardless of kernel choice. In terms of GP predictive accuracy (RMSE), the ESP kernel achieves the best values (0.539 at N=10, 0.341 at N=20), while adding the feature kernel component improves all kernels by roughly 20–40% in RMSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BoGrape outperforms all three baseline families across both synthetic and real-world experiments. Against Ru et al. (2021)'s WL-rand (WL kernel + random sampling) and WL-evol (WL kernel + evolutionary algorithm, described as state-of-the-art in graph BO), BoGrape achieves consistently better best-objective values on all six synthetic benchmarks (GAT/GCN/GraphSAGE × N=10/20) and on QM7/QM9 molecular design; WL-evol is excluded from the molecular design comparison because evolutionary mutations produce almost no feasible molecules (only 0.35% feasible at N=10, 3 out of 10⁶ at N=20). Against the RW-rand baseline (Gärtner et al. 2003 random walk kernel + sampling), BoGrape wins in nearly all settings, with the gap widening for larger graph sizes where the simpler SSP/ESSP kernels reduce MIP complexity. Against Limeade (Zhang et al. 2025) used as a feasible-molecule sampling baseline in the molecular design case study, BoGrape outperforms even when Limeade is given 100 candidates per iteration (vs. BoGrape's single MIP-optimized proposal), confirming the value of global acquisition optimization over sampling. The paper notes that for small graphs (N=10), more expressive kernels (SP, ESP) give the best BO performance, while for larger graphs (N=20+), simpler linear kernels (SSP) yield better results within the 600s MIP time limit due to reduced optimization complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

BoGrape extends the PK-MIQP framework of Xie et al. (2024) from continuous/discrete feature spaces to graph domains, replacing piecewise-linear kernel approximations with MIP encodings of shortest-path graph kernels. It builds directly on the shortest-path kernel of Borgwardt & Kriegel (2005), developing four variants (SP, SSP, ESP, ESSP) that are amenable to linear MIP formulation, and borrows the graph/feature kernel separation from Cui & Yang (2018). The key departure from prior graph BO work (Ru et al., 2021; Wan et al., 2023; Liang et al., 2024) is replacing sampling- or evolutionary-based acquisition optimization with a globally optimal MIP formulation that can embed arbitrary problem-specific constraints over graph structure and node attributes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first principled BO framework that optimizes over the full combinatorial space of connected graphs with global optimality guarantees and constraint embedding, addressing a gap the authors identify in the literature. The bijection theorem between MIP feasible solutions and connected graphs is a clean theoretical contribution that underpins the entire approach. Key limitations include the computational cost of solving the MIP (scaling poorly with graph size), the restriction to connected graphs, and the reliance on shortest-path kernels whose expressiveness may be insufficient for some tasks. Open questions include relaxing connectivity, developing trust-region or decomposition strategies for scalability, and extending to directed or multi-relational graphs.
