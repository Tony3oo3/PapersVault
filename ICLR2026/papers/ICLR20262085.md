## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for vision-language models rely on a fixed proportion of low-entropy samples for entropy minimization, which the paper shows causes two failures: (1) the model overfits on atypical features embedded in confidently misclassified samples, becoming overconfident on wrong predictions, and (2) a single fixed selection ratio cannot achieve optimal performance across datasets with different corruption types and class counts. Prior work focused on designing better optimization objectives while largely ignoring how adaptation data is selected and utilized.

## Input / Output

The method operates in an online streaming fashion during inference: a batch of corrupted test images (e.g., 200 for CIFAR-10-C, 64 for ImageNet-C) is fed through a pre-trained CLIP visual encoder alongside text prompts of the form 'a photo of a <cls>' for each of K known classes. The output is a per-sample class prediction plus updated LayerNorm parameters in both the visual and text encoders. The key operational detail is that each batch is dynamically partitioned into a low-entropy subset (for learning) and a high-entropy subset (for unlearning) based on a predicted threshold derived from the batch's entropy distribution and the number of classes.

## Method

BITTA introduces a bilateral information-aware mechanism that simultaneously learns from low-entropy samples and unlearns from high-entropy samples within each test batch. The learning loss (adopting BAT's three-component objective) minimizes prediction entropy, maximizes image-text projection alignment, and improves inter-class separability on the selected low-entropy subset. The unlearning loss reverses the optimization direction on the high-entropy subset, maximizing their prediction entropy to prevent the model from memorizing atypical noise features. A dynamic selection module replaces the fixed ratio by exploiting a discovered linear relationship between the optimal entropy threshold and the number of categories (τ_l^n / Max(H) = −0.00038K + 0.83), while a fixed 10% ratio suffices for high-entropy samples. The overall objective is L_learning + λ·L_unlearning (λ=1.2), and two theorems provide theoretical grounding: one showing that under large distribution shift classes become theoretically indistinguishable (justifying unlearning of atypical features), and another providing a marginal coverage guarantee for the predicted selection ratio.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C with ViT-B-16, BAT+BITTA achieves 74.78% mean accuracy across 15 corruption types, improving over BAT alone (73.34%) by +1.44% and over the next-best baseline TDA (65.30%) by +9.48%. On CIFAR-100-C (ViT-B-16), BAT+BITTA reaches 42.56% vs. BAT's 41.15% (+1.41%), and on ImageNet-C (ViT-B-16) it achieves 31.88% vs. BAT's 31.06% (+0.82%). The gains are consistent across both ViT-B-16 and ViT-B-32 backbones, with the largest per-corruption improvements on Glass (+3.91%) and Elastic (+1.78%) for CIFAR-10-C.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BITTA is evaluated as a plug-in module on top of BAT (its primary base) and also combined with TPT, DiffTPT, CTPT, and TPS for compatibility testing. On CIFAR-10-C (ViT-B-16), BAT+BITTA (74.78%) outperforms all other baselines by a wide margin: TDA (65.30%), DMN-ZS (65.38%), TPT (63.84%), DPE (62.48%), and BCA (58.61%), with the gap over the strongest non-BAT baseline exceeding 9%. When combined with other TTA methods, BITTA consistently adds 0.77–1.79% mean accuracy (e.g., TPT 63.84→65.37, DiffTPT 64.24→66.03, TPS 64.28→65.24). On cross-domain datasets (Flower102, DTD, Pets, Cars, UCF101, CalTech101, Food101, SUN397, Aircraft, EuroSAT), TPT+BITTA improves mean accuracy from 65.10% to 66.09% and TPS+BITTA from 66.78% to 68.05%. On non-CLIP architectures (ResNet101, ViTBase) with MEMO and SAR baselines on ImageNet-C, BITTA yields gains of 0.8–4.6% per corruption type, confirming the overfitting problem is architecture-agnostic. The only minor exception is a −0.12% drop on Impulse noise for CIFAR-10-C ViT-B-32, but the overall mean still improves by +1.53%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant TTA-for-VLMs paradigm that focuses on designing better optimization objectives (entropy minimization, contrastive alignment, prototype shifting) by instead addressing the underexplored question of how to select and utilize test data during adaptation. It builds directly on TPT's confidence-based low-entropy selection and BAT-CLIP's bimodal LayerNorm optimization, but reframes the problem: rather than improving the loss, it introduces a bilateral data strategy where high-entropy samples—previously discarded—are repurposed for an unlearning objective that counteracts overfitting on atypical features. The dynamic selection ratio mechanism, grounded in a linear relationship between initial entropy and category count, further distinguishes it from prior fixed-threshold approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that high-entropy samples contain a regularization signal correlated with the atypical features memorized in low-entropy misclassified samples provides a principled justification for not simply discarding uncertain predictions during TTA. The substantial ECE reductions (15–30%) suggest the method addresses a real calibration problem in adapted VLMs, which is important for safety-critical deployments. However, the accuracy gains are modest (typically 1–2%), the linear entropy-category relationship is empirically derived from a limited set of datasets, and the unlearning mechanism is a simple entropy-maximization loss rather than a true feature-level unlearning procedure—leaving open whether more sophisticated unlearning could yield larger gains. The method's compatibility with diverse TTA backends (TPT, DiffTPT, CTPT, TPS, MEMO, SAR) makes it a practical plug-in, but its ceiling may be limited by the simplicity of the bilateral selection criterion.
