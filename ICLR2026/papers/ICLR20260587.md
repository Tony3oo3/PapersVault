## Problem

Existing graph domain adaptation methods rely on manually designed graph filters and fixed alignment criteria (e.g., aligning specific node attributes, degree distributions, or homophily), making them inflexible when the dominant sources of distributional discrepancy vary across transfer scenarios. No prior approach can automatically identify which spectral components carry the most informative cross-domain shifts for a given source-target pair, leading to suboptimal alignment and negative transfer under complex, multi-faceted shifts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled source graph (node feature matrix, adjacency matrix, and node class labels) and an unlabeled target graph (node feature matrix and adjacency matrix) in an unsupervised setting. A shared GNN encoder maps both graphs to node-level embeddings, and the framework outputs predicted class labels for all target nodes. The alignment operates entirely in the Fourier/spectral domain over these embeddings, requiring no target labels during training and no manual specification of which graph features to align.

## Method

ADAlign introduces the Neural Spectral Discrepancy (NSD), a parametric distance that compares source and target node-embedding distributions via their characteristic functions in the spectral domain, capturing feature-structure dependencies of all orders without manual feature selection. The NSD is decomposed into amplitude and phase components (governed by a trade-off parameter κ), where amplitude captures global structural concentration differences and phase encodes relational misalignments. A learnable frequency sampler, parameterized as a normal scale mixture, adaptively selects which spectral components to evaluate by maximizing the expected discrepancy (adversarial objective), while the GNN parameters minimize it. The overall training follows a minimax formulation: the GNN minimizes source classification loss plus the alignment loss, while the frequency sampler maximizes the alignment loss to expose the most discriminative spectral regions for each transfer task.

## Results

ADAlign achieves the best Micro-F1 score across all 16 transfer scenarios on 10 datasets spanning four graph domains (citation, airport, blog, and social). Representative headline numbers include 76.97% on A→C (citation), 80.93% on B→U (airport), 61.92% on B1→B2 (blog), and 75.82% on DE→EN (Twitch). The paper reports an average improvement of over 6% on the challenging blog-domain transfers, where high-dimensional features and multiple label categories create entangled shifts. In terms of efficiency, ADAlign achieves a 2.0× speedup and 3.1× memory reduction per iteration compared to the strongest efficiency competitor (TDSS) on the A→C task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADAlign outperforms all 16 baselines across every one of the 16 transfer scenarios, with the largest margins observed on the blog-domain tasks (B1→B2, B2→B1) where it improves over the best baseline by more than 6%. Among the strongest GDA competitors, GAA (ICLR'25) and HGDA (ICML'25) are the closest rivals on citation and airport tasks, but ADAlign still leads by 1–5% Micro-F1 in most settings. On the Twitch social-network transfers (DE→EN, EN→DE), ADAlign achieves 75.82% and 65.69% respectively, surpassing the next-best methods by a clear margin. In terms of efficiency, ADAlign runs at 0.08 s/iteration with 3.45 GB memory on A→C, compared to 0.17 s and 10.64 GB for TDSS and 0.37 s and 22.31 GB for HGDA, yielding a 2.0× speedup and 3.1× memory reduction. The paper also confirms via paired t-tests (p < 0.05) that all improvements over baselines are statistically significant on the A→C and A→D tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Characteristic Function (NCF) framework from Wang et al. (CVPR 2025) and the normal-scale-mixture frequency sampling from Ansari et al. (CVPR 2020) into the graph domain adaptation setting. It departs from the dominant GDA paradigm of aligning manually selected graph components (node attributes, degree distributions, homophily) via fixed metrics like MMD or KL divergence, instead proposing a unified spectral-domain discrepancy (NSD) that captures all-order feature-structure dependencies through the characteristic function. The minimax formulation with a learnable frequency sampler is borrowed from the NCF dataset distillation literature but is the first application to cross-graph distribution alignment, replacing heuristic component-specific strategies with an adaptive, scenario-aware mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADAlign offers a theoretically grounded (PAC-Bayesian bounded) and practically efficient alternative to the growing zoo of component-specific GDA methods, eliminating the need for practitioners to diagnose which shift type dominates a given transfer. The 2× speedup and 3–4× memory reduction over strong baselines while simultaneously improving accuracy make it attractive for large-scale graph transfer. Open questions include whether the minimax frequency sampler remains stable at much larger graph scales (the experiments use graphs up to ~9K nodes), whether the amplitude-phase decomposition generalizes to hypergraphs or temporal graphs, and whether the learned frequency distributions transfer across related tasks or must be re-learned from scratch.
