## Problem

Existing graph domain adaptation methods assume a single-view graph structure, which cannot capture the rich multi-relational topology present in real-world graphs like citation networks and knowledge graphs. Moreover, even within a single view, structural discrepancies vary across neighborhood hops, yet prior approaches measure domain shift only at the first-hop level. The paper asks how to jointly quantify and align structural disparities across both views and hops in multi-view graph domain adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263241/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled source multi-view graph and an unlabeled target multi-view graph, where each view is defined by a distinct meta-path adjacency matrix (e.g., PAP and PSP for ACM, PAP and PP for MAG) over the same node set with shared feature matrices. The number of aggregation hops K is dynamically selected from {1,…,5} per view during training rather than fixed a priori. The output is a predicted class label for every target node, evaluated by accuracy and macro-F1 across ten country-pair splits on MAG and two splits on ACM.

## Method

SAGA decouples graph propagation from dimensionality reduction: it first performs personalized PageRank-style k-hop propagation (with a teleport vector) independently per view, then passes the aggregated features through a shared MLP encoder-decoder trained with reconstruction loss. The key novelty is the Structural Aggregation Distance (SAD), a dynamic discrepancy metric computed as the squared Frobenius norm between source and target embedding covariance matrices for every view-hop pair, with the dominant pair (v*, k*) selected as the argmin of SAD each epoch. SAD-guided softmax weights then drive two alignment objectives: an intra-view loss that pulls each view's embedding covariance toward the dominant view's, and a cross-view contrastive loss (cosine-similarity-based) that aligns the dominant source and target view-hop embeddings. This dynamic re-selection of the dominant view-hop pair at every training step is the central mechanism that distinguishes SAGA from fixed-hop or fixed-view baselines.

## Results

On the MAG benchmark (10 cross-country domain pairs), SAGA achieves the best ACC and macro-F1 across all settings, with headline results of 55.7% ACC / 41.5% macro-F1 on CN→JP and 55.3% ACC / 42.6% macro-F1 on CN→US. On the ACM benchmark, SAGA reaches 52.3% ACC / 35.1% macro-F1 (ACM1→ACM2) and 45.4% ACC / 44.4% macro-F1 (ACM2→ACM1), outperforming the next-best baselines (PA, HGDA) by 1–4 points in ACC. The view ablation confirms that restricting SAGA to any single view drops ACC by 15–24 points, validating the multi-view dynamic selection mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGA outperforms all 11 baselines on every one of the 10 MAG domain pairs and both ACM domain pairs in both ACC and macro-F1. Against the strongest adaptive baselines, SAGA leads HGDA (ICML 2025) by 13.6 ACC points on CN→US (55.3 vs 41.7) and by 12.1 points on CN→JP (55.7 vs 34.3); it beats PA by 15.1 points on CN→US and SpecReg by 12.8 points on the same pair. On ACM, SAGA surpasses PA (the closest competitor) by 1.7 ACC points (52.3 vs 50.6) on ACM1→ACM2 and by 0.2 points on ACM2→ACM1, while its macro-F1 advantage is larger (35.1 vs 33.3 and 44.4 vs 37.7). Non-adaptive baselines (GCN, GAT) trail SAGA by 20+ ACC points, confirming that explicit domain alignment is essential. The view ablation further shows that fixing any single relational view (e.g., PAP→PAP only) drops ACC by 15–24 points relative to full SAGA, demonstrating that dynamic multi-view selection is the key differentiator over single-view GDA methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGA extends the graph domain adaptation (GDA) literature, which has been dominated by single-view methods (UDAGCN, ASN, SpecReg, PA, HGDA), to the multi-view setting by introducing the MGDA problem formulation. The structural aggregation mechanism is directly derived from SGC's K-hop propagation (Wu et al., 2019), while the scalable encoding borrows the personalized PageRank teleport idea from Chien et al. (2020). The key departure from prior GDA work is the introduction of Structural Aggregation Distance (SAD) as a dynamic, view- and hop-aware discrepancy metric, replacing the fixed first-hop or single-view assumptions that underpin all compared baselines. The alignment losses combine Frobenius-norm intra-view matching with cosine-similarity contrastive cross-view alignment, drawing on both optimal-transport-inspired GDA bounds and contrastive representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing MGDA and showing that the dominant view-hop pair evolves during training, SAGA provides a principled mechanism for multi-view structural alignment that prior single-view GDA methods cannot address. However, the evaluation is confined to citation-network benchmarks (ACM, MAG) with relatively small node counts, and single-view baselines are adapted via view-averaging, which may understate their true multi-view potential. Open questions include scalability to large heterogeneous graphs, applicability to non-citation multi-relational domains (social, knowledge graphs), and whether the dynamic selection mechanism generalizes when the number of views or hops grows substantially.
