## Problem

Existing 3D vision-language models produce fluent answers that are not explicitly grounded in scene objects, resulting in poor grounding-QA coherence where correct answers may be disconnected from the objects they reference. While Chain-of-Thought reasoning has transformed text and 2D multimodal models, it has not been adapted to 3D scene understanding, where reasoning must navigate spatial relations, partial observability, and embodied context. This paper addresses the gap by introducing the first grounded CoT framework for 3D scenes.

## Input / Output

The model receives a 3D scene point cloud (from ScanNet), the agent's egocentric position and orientation, a natural-language question, and initial object proposals from Mask3D. It produces a structured reasoning trace using special tokens (<think_type>, <think_rgn>, <think_grd>, <think_task>, <think_sum>, <answer>) that trigger external modules, followed by a final text answer. Intermediate visual clues—object class probabilities, 3D bounding-box locations, 2D polar coordinates for navigation, or retrieved object image patches—are constructed by symbolic engines and fed back into the LLM as additional context before the final answer is generated.

## Method

SCENECOT decomposes 3D scene reasoning into four sequential stages: (1) task recognition to identify the question type and select appropriate modules, (2) region localization to filter objects to task-relevant sub-regions using cardinal or clock-based directions, (3) entity grounding via a fine-tuned PQ3D expert model that localizes target objects from grounding instructions, and (4) grounded reasoning that integrates retrieved visual clues (probabilities, coordinates, or image tokens) into a coherent answer. The framework is built on LLaVA-1.5 (Vicuna-7B) with LoRA fine-tuning, jointly optimizing a causal LM loss for the reasoning trace and answer plus a cross-entropy grounding loss on the PQ3D module. A symbolic engine handles spatial partitioning and visual clue construction as fixed, non-learned components. Training uses the newly constructed SCENECOT-185K dataset (185K stepwise reasoning traces over MSQA and GQA3D), and inference employs a lightweight two-expert routing strategy that selects between checkpoints based on the predicted question type to balance sub-task performance.

## Results

On MSQA (Situated Reasoning), SCENECOT achieves 55.6 overall GPT-score, with notable strength on Counting (47.9) and Spatial (47.2), though slightly below Chat-Scene† (56.6) on the overall metric. On Beacon3D (Object-Centric Reasoning), SCENECOT substantially outperforms all baselines, reaching 58.9 GPT-Score (by case) and 23.2 GPT-Score (by object). On the grounding-QA coherence metric (Good Coherence), SCENECOT achieves 34.7, nearly double the next-best SceneVerse (20.4) and over 18× LEO (1.6). On QA-driven grounding (F1@50), SCENECOT reaches 52.1 on MSQA and 51.6 on SQA3D, far exceeding Chat-Scene (15.9 and 25.9) and PQ3D (10.6 and 10.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCENECOT's most decisive advantage is on Beacon3D, where it achieves 23.2 GPT-Score (by object) versus 7.8 for LEO, 14.0 for Chat-Scene†, and 20.2 for GPT-4o*—a margin of 9–15 points over all baselines. On grounding-QA coherence (Good Coherence), SCENECOT scores 34.7, nearly 2× SceneVerse (20.4) and Chat-Scene (19.5), and over 20× LEO (1.6), confirming that its answers are reliably linked to correct scene grounding. On MSQA overall, SCENECOT (55.6) is competitive with Chat-Scene† (56.6) and LEO (54.8), but clearly leads on Counting (47.9 vs. 37.4 for Chat-Scene†) and Spatial (47.2 vs. 47.0). On QA-driven grounding (F1@50), SCENECOT dramatically outperforms both Chat-Scene (52.1 vs. 15.9 on MSQA) and PQ3D (52.1 vs. 10.6), while PQ3D retains an edge on traditional Nr3D grounding (66 vs. 57.7 Top-1). Against GPT-4o* (which receives ground-truth object metadata), SCENECOT still wins on MSQA Overall (55.6 vs. 52.3) and Beacon3D Obj. (23.2 vs. 20.2), demonstrating that structured grounded reasoning surpasses even oracle-informed single-pass generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCENECOT extends the Chain-of-Thought reasoning paradigm (Wei et al., 2022) from text and 2D vision-language settings into 3D scene understanding, where prior 3D-LLM work (LEO, Chat-Scene, MSR3D, SplatTalk) has treated question answering as a single end-to-end prediction without intermediate reasoning steps. The framework is architecturally built on LLaVA-1.5 as the reasoning backbone and PQ3D as the 3D visual grounding expert, with a symbolic engine handling spatial region partitioning and visual clue construction. Its closest conceptual relatives are 2D visual CoT methods (Visual CoT, V*, Video-of-Thought), but SCENECOT is the first to explicitly decompose 3D reasoning into task recognition, region localization, entity grounding, and grounded reasoning with dedicated module invocations at each stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes grounding-QA coherence as a first-class evaluation dimension, revealing that prior 3D-LLMs can produce correct answers while grounding the wrong objects (or vice versa), a failure mode invisible to standard QA accuracy metrics. The oracle analysis (Table 3) shows that when semantic and grounding errors are removed, the reasoning framework itself is near-perfect (Counting 98.8, Existence 100), identifying grounding quality—not reasoning logic—as the primary bottleneck for 3D scene QA. Key limitations include restriction to ScanNet scenes, a fixed set of MSQA task types, and ~10.4s inference latency versus 0.2–4.8s for baselines; extending to embodied task planning, diverse real-world scenes, and RL-based training are identified as important next steps.
