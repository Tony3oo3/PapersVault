## Problem

Existing federated graph-level clustering (FGC) methods such as FedGCN and FedPKA follow a paradigm of maximizing global knowledge sharing, which works for node-level tasks but causes consensus failure on the server when clients hold entirely different non-IID graphs. The root cause is the simultaneous presence of severe intra-client heterogeneity (diverse graph patterns within each client) and inter-client heterogeneity (domain shifts across clients), which no prior method addresses jointly.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a private set of N_G graphs, each represented by a node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×d} and a binary adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N}, distributed across N_φ local clusters. Rather than uploading raw graphs or full model parameters, clients transmit only irrecoverable pattern digests (common subgraph representations) and invariant encoder parameters to the server. The server produces cluster-level consensus prototypes and personalized parameter guidance signals, which are fed back to clients for a two-stage K-means clustering that outputs final graph cluster assignments.

## Method

FGCN-DKS decouples each graph into an invariant (cluster-oriented) subgraph and a variant (client-specific) subgraph via learned structure and attribute masks (M^s, M^x) conditioned on E perturbed environment views, then encodes them with dual 4-layer GIN projectors. The invariant representations are trained with a composite loss enforcing intra-cluster consistency (L_inv), inter-cluster separation (L_div), and cross-environment invariance (L_env), while variant representations are retained locally. On the server, the Common Knowledge Sharing Strategy (CKSS) computes pairwise cluster affinities using graph kernels (e.g., SP kernel) on the uploaded pattern digests, applies a temporal stability coefficient α for smoothing across communication rounds, and performs affinity-weighted personalized aggregation of prototypes and parameters instead of uniform averaging. Final clustering is two-stage: K-means on invariant graph-level embeddings provides a robust global grouping, which is then refined within each cluster using variant embeddings to capture client-specific structure.

## Results

On the SM2(7) non-IID setting, FGCN-DKS achieves 79.2% ACC, 28.3% NMI, 34.6% ARI, and 72.3% F1, outperforming the closest SOTA FGC baselines FedGCN (75.9% ACC) and FedPKA (77.0% ACC). On the cross-domain SM-BIO2(9) setting, it reaches 73.8% ACC and 21.3% ARI, and on the CV11(3) setting it attains 39.2% ACC and 37.1% NMI. Across all six non-IID settings (SM, SN, SM-BIO, SM-BIO-SY, SN-SY, CV), FGCN-DKS consistently achieves the best or near-best results on all four metrics (ACC, NMI, ARI, F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGCN-DKS outperforms all 13 baselines across all six non-IID settings on every metric. Against the two SOTA FGC methods, it gains +2.2–4.6 ACC over FedGCN and +2.2–2.8 ACC over FedPKA (e.g., 79.2 vs. 75.9 vs. 77.0 on SM; 73.8 vs. 69.2 vs. 70.8 on SM-BIO), with the largest ARI margins reaching +3.5 over FedGCN and +1.7 over FedPKA on SM. Among adapted supervised FGL methods (FedSage, GCFL, FedStar), FGCN-DKS leads by 18–24 ACC points, reflecting the fundamental difficulty of label-free clustering without invariant structure separation. Anomaly-detection baselines (LG-FGAD, FGAD, AGDiff) trail by 9–14 ACC, as their objective of identifying rare patterns misaligns with general clustering. Centralized DGC methods adapted to federated settings (GLCC, UDGC, DGLC, DCGLC) show the weakest performance (53–63 ACC on SM), confirming that naive parameter averaging causes consensus failure under multi-graph heterogeneity. In the communication overhead analysis (Table 4), FGCN-DKS incurs only ~10% more time and payload than FedGCN while substantially outperforming FedPKA in both efficiency and accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two distinct research threads into a unified federated graph-level clustering framework. From federated learning, it adapts the personalization-layer philosophy of FedPer (Arivazhagan et al., 2019) by decomposing each graph into shared and client-specific components rather than splitting model parameters by layer. From invariant graph learning (GIL, Li et al., 2022), it borrows the invariant/variant subgraph mask mechanism but repurposes it for unsupervised clustering under distributional shift, where 'invariant' means cluster-stable rather than label-stable. The key departure from existing FGC methods (FedGCN, FedPKA) is rejecting the maximize-sharing paradigm: instead of uploading full model parameters and prototypes, FGCN-DKS transmits only lightweight pattern digests of common subgraphs and performs cluster-affinity-guided aggregation via graph kernels on the server.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode—consensus collapse under simultaneous intra-client and inter-client heterogeneity—that prior FGC methods overlook, and the dual-separation design (local invariant/variant split + server-side cluster-affinity aggregation) offers a principled remedy. However, the evaluation is confined to small TUDataset graphs (≤2000 nodes), the invariant loss L_inv requires ground-truth cluster assignments c(i) during training (limiting true unsupervised applicability), and the two-stage K-means refinement is relatively simple. Open questions include scalability to large-scale graph collections, whether the approach extends to node-level federated clustering (acknowledged as future work), and how the method behaves when the number of clusters differs substantially across clients (only briefly tested in Appendix G).
