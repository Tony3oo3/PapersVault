## Problem

Prior GUI grounding work treats the natural-language instruction as a static input string, overlooking how instruction diversity and quality affect grounding accuracy. The paper reveals that 23.3% of instructions in major datasets contain substantive flaws (ambiguity, mismatch), and that simply providing diverse instruction perspectives can unlock up to 76% relative performance gain without any retraining—yet prevailing models are trained in a single fixed instruction style with no capacity to reason across perspectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261850/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a GUI screenshot (from Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, or web interfaces at varying resolutions) and a natural-language instruction that may be explicit (e.g., 'click the red X button') or implicit (e.g., 'get rid of this screen'). It produces a two-part output: first, an intermediate reasoning text inside a  block that restates the instruction from a chosen analytical perspective (appearance, function, spatial, or goal), and second, a JSON action containing the predicted click coordinate (x, y). The reasoning text is not a free-form chain-of-thought but a structured single-sentence perspective rewrite, which the paper shows is critical for stable RL optimization.

## Method

UI-Ins introduces the Instruction-as-Reasoning paradigm, which reframes diverse instruction phrasings as distinct analytical reasoning pathways the model can select during inference. A data pipeline first cleans ground-truth boxes via OmniParser V2 and IoU filtering, then uses GPT-4.1 to synthesize four perspective-specific instructions (appearance, functionality, location, intent) per sample with a verification step to enforce one-to-one element mapping. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) SFT on ~283k instances where the model learns to emit a perspective-rewritten instruction as intermediate reasoning before predicting coordinates, with two randomly sampled perspectives per instance; (2) GRPO-based RL on ~100k samples where the prompt omits the perspective list, encouraging the model to explore and select (or compose) the optimal reasoning pathway via point-in-box rewards with group-normalized advantages. This SFT+RL design also mitigates policy collapse: the diverse reasoning pathways instilled during SFT produce varied rollouts during RL, preventing the uniform-response collapse observed in standard coordinate-only SFT.

## Results

UI-Ins-32B achieves state-of-the-art grounding accuracy across five benchmarks: 87.3% on UI-I2E-Bench, 57.0% on ScreenSpot-Pro, 84.9% on MMBench-GUI L2, 94.9% on ScreenSpot-V2, and 73.8% on Showdown. UI-Ins-7B also sets new records at its scale, scoring 81.1% on UI-I2E-Bench and 52.2% on ScreenSpot-Pro. In the online agent setting, UI-Ins-7B paired with a GPT-5 planner achieves a 74.1% task success rate on AndroidWorld, surpassing closed-source systems like Gemini 2.5 Computer Use (69.7%) and UI-TARS-2 (73.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UI-Ins-32B outperforms the strongest 32B baseline GTA1-32B by 3.8 points on UI-I2E-Bench (87.3% vs. 83.5%) and 1.5 points on MMBench-GUI L2 (84.9% vs. 83.4%), with the gap widening on harder subsets (e.g., 24.5% relative gain over Qwen2.5-VL-32B on the 'Advanced' subset). At 7B scale, UI-Ins-7B surpasses InfiGUI-G1-7B by 3.7 points on UI-I2E-Bench (81.1% vs. 77.4%) and 2.3 points on MMBench-GUI L2 (83.1% vs. 80.8%), and beats GTA1-7B by 2.9 points on UI-I2E-Bench. On ScreenSpot-Pro, UI-Ins-32B (57.0%) leads GTA1-32B (53.6%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (53.3%) by 3.4 and 3.7 points respectively, while UI-Ins-7B (52.2%) edges out InfiGUI-G1-7B (51.9%). On the online AndroidWorld benchmark, UI-Ins-7B with a GPT-5 planner achieves 74.1% success rate, beating UI-TARS-2 (73.3%), Gemini 2.5 Computer Use (69.7%), and OpenAI CUA-o3 (52.5%), demonstrating that the grounding gains translate to practical agent performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SFT-only or pure-RL training paradigms for GUI grounding (OS-Atlas, GUI-Actor, GTA1, InfiGUI-G1) by proposing a two-stage SFT+GRPO framework in which diverse instruction perspectives are repurposed as explicit reasoning pathways rather than static inputs. It builds directly on GRPO (Guo et al. 2025; Shao E al. 2024) for the RL stage and on Qwen2.5-VL as the backbone, but its key conceptual departure is the Instruction-as-Reasoning reframing: prior works like Aria-UI and Phi-Ground augmented instructions at the input level, whereas here instructions become learnable intermediate reasoning that the model selects and composes during inference. The SFT stage is specifically designed to mitigate the policy-collapse failure mode documented in Phi-Ground and InfiGUI-R1, where coordinate-only SFT yields uniform rollouts that stall RL exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 23.3% instruction flaw rate in major grounding datasets and the 76% zero-shot gain from perspective selection are actionable findings that should reshape how the community curates and uses grounding data. The policy-collapse mitigation via diverse-reasoning SFT is a practically important recipe for anyone attempting SFT+RL on grounding tasks, and the Egergent composition of perspectives (including novel angles beyond the four trained) suggests the reasoning space is richer than the training distribution. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other backbones or to tasks requiring multi-step planning, and whether the Egergent perspectives remain stable under further RL pressure. The erroror analysis also flags residual weaknesses in domain knowledge and layout understanding that instruction-level reasoning cannot address.
