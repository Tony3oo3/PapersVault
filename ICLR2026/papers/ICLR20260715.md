## Problem

Long video generation models aim to convey rich, temporally evolving narrative content, yet no benchmark exists specifically designed to evaluate this capability. Existing benchmarks (VBench, TC-Bench, StoryEval) rely on prompts with only a small number of narrative units in a narrow range, making them insufficient for assessing how well models express complex, extended narratives over longer durations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260715/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes text prompts specifying a target number of Temporal Narrative Atoms (1–6) and a TNA change factor (scene attributes, object attributes, or object actions), along with the corresponding generated video clip. The video is sampled at 2 frames per second and fed to an MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) for evaluation. The output is a triple of scores: narrative element fidelity (R_fid), narrative unit coverage (R_cov), and narrative unit coherence (R_coh), each computed as the mean proportion of positive answers across 5 repeated MLLM responses per generated question.

## Method

NarrLV introduces the Temporal Narrative Atom (TNA) as a quantifiable unit of narrative richness, inspired by the 'Beat' concept in film narratology. An automated prompt generation pipeline uses GPT-4o to produce evaluation prompts with flexibly expandable TNA counts by combining scene-object pairs (extracted from 200k prompts via Qwen2.5-32B) with specified TNA change factors and counts. The evaluation metric follows a progressive narrative expression paradigm: it first checks whether basic scene/object elements are faithfully rendered (R_fid), then whether each TNA in the prompt is actually present in the video (R_cov), and finally whether transitions between adjacent TNAs are coherent (R_coh). All three dimensions are computed via an MLLM-based question generation and answering framework, where GPT-4o generates dimension-specific binary judgment questions from the prompt's structured semantic content, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B answers each question five times against the video frames, with the proportion of 'yes' responses serving as the score. For R_coh, an additional term ρ_tna (the fraction of TNAs confirmed to exist) is incorporated to ensure transitions are only scored when their constituent units are present.

## Results

Across 11 evaluated models (5 foundation, 6 long video), the mean scores are R_fid=67.9, R_cov=57.4, and R_coh=39.6 (averaged over all TNA counts and change factors). As TNA count increases from 1 to 6, R_cov and R_coh show a clear downward trend while R_fid remains relatively stable, indicating models can extract basic elements but struggle with temporal narrative construction. The effective TNA expression count N_exp = R_cov × n plateaus near 2, suggesting current models can only reliably express about 2 narrative units regardless of prompt complexity. In terms of human alignment, NarrLV's metric achieves 0.63–0.67 accuracy (Consist-2/3) and 0.79–0.81 (Consist-3/3), substantially outperforming VBench-2.0 (0.28–0.33) and StoryEval (0.41–0.51).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among foundation models, Wan2.1-14B leads on all three dimensions (R_fid=77.8–82.5, R_cov=68.8–72.7, R_coh=50.1–54.5), followed by HunyuanVideo and CogVideoX. Among long video models, FreeNoise achieves the highest R_fid (77.6 on s_att) but the lowest R_coh (34.4 on t_act), while FIFO-Diffusion and FreeLong show the most balanced profiles. All VideoCraft-derived long video models (FIFO-Diffusion, FreeLong, FreeNoise, FreePCA) cluster closely in R_cov and R_coh, confirming that the foundation model largely determines narrative expression capability. TALC, built on the older ModelScopeT2V, lags significantly behind all other models (R_fid≈38, R_cov≈31, R_coh≈22). In terms of metric reliability, NarrLV's progressive question-driven approach achieves 0.63–0.67 human alignment accuracy (Consist-2/3), nearly doubling VBench-2.0's 0.28–0.33 and substantially exceeding StoryEval's 0.41–0.51, validating its superiority for narrative-centric evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MLLM-based question generation and answering evaluation paradigm established by TIFA (Hu et al., 2023), Davidsonian Scene Graph (Cho et al., 2023), and What You See Is What You Read (Yarom et al., 2023), repurposing it from image-text alignment to video narrative assessment. Conceptually, it departs from existing video generation benchmarks (VBench, TC-Bench, StoryEval) by grounding its evaluation unit in film narratology: the Temporal Narrative Atom (TNA) formalizes what prior benchmarks treat implicitly as 'events' or 'segments,' and the three TNA change factors (scene attributes, object attributes, object actions) provide a structured axis for prompt extensibility that prior benchmarks lack. The prompt generation pipeline draws on user-focused datasets (VideoUFO, DropletVideo) to ensure ecological validity, while the progressive evaluation hierarchy (element fidelity → unit coverage → unit coherence) follows the narrative structure principles from Chatman and Cowie.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NarrLV fills a clear gap: prior benchmarks evaluate long video models on prompts with only 1–4 TNAs, making it impossible to diagnose where narrative expression breaks down as complexity increases. The finding that current models plateau at effectively expressing ~2 TNAs regardless of prompt length provides a concrete, actionable ceiling for practitioners. Key limitations include the cap at 6 TNAs (acknowledged as insufficient for truly long narratives), reliance on a single MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) for all judgments, and a relatively small prompt suite (360 prompts). Open questions include whether the TNA framework generalizes to multi-shot cinematic narratives with camera work and dialogue, and whether the MLLM-based evaluation can be replaced by more efficient or interpretable metrics as video generation scales.
