## Problem

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) has been transformative for scaling LLMs but yields only marginal gains when applied to Diffusion Transformers (DiTs). The paper attributes this gap to two properties of visual tokens absent in language: high spatial redundancy (patches are continuous and correlated, causing experts to learn homogeneous features) and functional heterogeneity (classifier-free guidance introduces two distinct token types—conditional and unconditional—that naive MoE treats uniformly). These properties collectively impede the expert specialization that is the foundational principle of MoE design.

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the model takes a noisy VAE latent (256×256 image encoded to 32×32 latent) plus a class label as input and produces a denoised latent decoded to a 256×256 RGB image. For text-to-image, it takes a text prompt (encoded via Qwen2.5-VL) and noisy latent (encoded via Wan2.5-Preview VAE) and produces a generated image. During training with CFG, the input token sequence is split into conditional tokens (under specific conditioning) and unconditional tokens (under null conditioning), which are routed through different expert pathways.

## Method

ProMoE introduces a two-step router with explicit routing guidance. First, conditional routing hard-partitions tokens by functional role: unconditional tokens are deterministically assigned to dedicated unconditional experts, while conditional tokens proceed to the second step. Second, prototypical routing assigns conditional tokens to routed experts via cosine similarity between token embeddings and learnable prototypes in latent space, using an identity activation (rather than softmax) for top-K selection. A routing contrastive loss (RCL) further enhances this by pulling each prototype toward the centroid of its assigned token set (intra-expert coherence) and pushing it away from other centroids (inter-expert diversity), requiring no manual labels. Shared experts process all tokens to capture common knowledge, and the push-away term in RCL implicitly serves as a semantic-aware load balancer that outperforms traditional count-based load-balancing losses.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with Rectified Flow after 500K steps, ProMoE-L-Flow achieves FID 11.61 (CFG=1.0) and 2.79 (CFG=1.5), reducing FID by 24.8% over Dense-DiT-L-Flow (15.44) at the same 458M activated parameters. At the XL scale, ProMoE-XL-Flow reaches FID 9.44 (CFG=1.0) and 2.59 (CFG=1.5), a 29.4% FID reduction over Dense-DiT-XL-Flow. Against the strongest MoE baseline DiffMoE-L-Flow (16 experts, 1.846B total params), ProMoE-L-Flow (1.063B total params) improves FID from 13.55 to 11.61 (CFG=1.0) and from 3.30 to 2.79 (CFG=1.5). On the GenEval text-to-image benchmark, ProMoE achieves 0.463 overall score vs. 0.390 for the dense 3B baseline and 0.417 for Token-Choice MoE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProMoE outperforms all four baselines across both Rectified Flow and DDPM objectives. Against Dense-DiT, ProMoE achieves substantially lower FID at equivalent activated parameters: at L scale (458M), ProMoE-L-Flow reduces FID from 15.44 to 11.61 (CFG=1.0, −24.8%), and at XL scale (675M), from 13.38 to 9.44 (−29.4%). Against DiT-MoE (token-choice routing), ProMoE-L-Flow improves FID from 16.57 to 11.61 (CFG=1.0), a 29.9% reduction, demonstrating that explicit routing guidance resolves the poor expert specialization inherent in naive token-choice routing. Against EC-DiT (expert-choice with fixed quotas), ProMoE reduces FID from 15.58 to 11.61 (CFG=1.0). Most notably, against DiffMoE (the strongest MoE baseline), ProMoE-L-Flow with only 1.063B total parameters surpasses DiffMoE-L-Flow with 16 experts and 1.846B total parameters, reducing FID from 13.55 to 11.61 (CFG=1.0) and from 3.30 to 2.79 (CFG=1.5), while also using fewer GFLOPs (77.72 vs. 90.03). On the text-to-image GenEval benchmark, ProMoE (12B total, 3B activated) achieves 0.463 overall, outperforming both the dense 3B model (0.390) and Token-Choice MoE 12B (0.417).

## Relation to Prior Work

ProMoE builds on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2023) and the MoE paradigm established in LLMs (Shazeer et al., 2017; Dai et al., 2024), but departs from prior MoE-in-DiT efforts (DiT-MoE, EC-DiT, DiffMoE) by diagnosing the root cause of their underperformance: visual tokens' spatial redundancy and the functional heterogeneity introduced by classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) prevent the implicit expert specialization that works naturally for semantically dense language tokens. The paper's two-step router (conditional routing for functional segregation, prototypical routing for semantic assignment) and the routing contrastive loss represent a shift from implicit token-to-expert allocation toward explicit, guidance-driven routing design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear, well-evidenced diagnosis of why MoE scaling has stalled in DiTs relative to LLMs, and offers a principled, general solution that practitioners can adopt. The routing contrastive loss is label-free, supports top-K assignment, and doubles as a semantic load-balancing regularizer, making it a practical drop-in improvement. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video generation or very high-resolution settings, whether the gains persist at 10B+ parameter scales, and whether the conditional routing step generalizes to conditioning types beyond class labels and text prompts. The authors note that FID/IS may not fully capture fine-grained perceptual quality.
