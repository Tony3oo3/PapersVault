## Problem

Existing differentially private synthetic text generation methods impose uniform privacy guarantees across all records, overprotecting non-sensitive content and incurring substantial utility loss and computational overhead. Since sensitive information is often sparse and varies across users and attributes, uniform DP is overly conservative. The paper asks whether calibrating protection at the level of specific secrets rather than membership can yield tighter utility-privacy trade-offs while reducing computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a private text corpus (with predefined secrets identified via tools like AI4Privacy or Presidio), a public text corpus, an LLM API (e.g., GPT-2, Qwen-2.5-1.5B) for candidate generation, and a Sentence-Transformers embedding model for similarity computation. The privacy budget is specified as (p, r) pairs—adversary prior and reconstruction success bound—rather than (ε, δ), and the output is a set of N_syn synthetic text samples (e.g., 2000 for OpenReview/PubMed, 5000 for Yelp) with per-sample token limits (448 or 64 tokens depending on dataset).

## Method

SecPE replaces uniform DP voting with a two-stage secret-aware pipeline. First, Secret Clustering runs K-means on public data to form K anchors, then samples private points with probabilities derived from a linear program (calibrating noise σ so that the blow-up function at prior p_j stays below r_j), assigns them to nearest anchors, and releases noisy cluster centers and sizes. Second, Protected Evolution iteratively selects top-N_syn candidates by similarity to these noisy representatives and generates LLM variations around them, reducing per-iteration cost from O(M·N_syn) to O(K·N_syn) similarity computations. Theoretically, (p,r)-secret protection is shown to be a strict relaxation of μ-GDP—requiring the trade-off curve to dominate the Gaussian benchmark only at the single prior point p_j rather than over all α—yielding less noise for the same reconstruction guarantee.

## Results

Under the tightest privacy budget (r/p=2), SecPE substantially outperforms the μ-GDP Aug-PE baseline across all three datasets: on Yelp (GPT-2), Rating accuracy improves from 47.02% to 62.46% (SecPE800); on OpenReview (Qwen-2.5-1.5B), Area accuracy rises from 32.70% to 38.34% (SecPE15); and on PubMed (GPT-2), BERT-small next-word accuracy increases from 24.93% to 29.18% (SecPE2000). In the non-private setting (r/p=∞), SecPE is broadly comparable or marginally lower, as clustering abstracts fine-grained details. Computationally, SecPE reduces per-iteration histogram construction time by at least 60× (e.g., 30,126 s → 2.3 s on Yelp), while LLM generation time remains unchanged.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Aug-PE instantiated under μ-GDP (following Xie et al., 2024), where μ is calibrated via the same (p,r) pair so that both methods target an identical reconstruction guarantee. SecPE wins decisively under all private budgets (r/p ∈ {2, 10, 50}) on every dataset and both LLMs: the largest gap appears on Yelp Rating at r/p=2, where SecPE800 (GPT-2) reaches 62.46% versus Aug-PE's 47.02% (+15.4 pp), and on OpenReview Area at r/p=2 with Qwen-2.5-1.5B, where SecPE15 achieves 38.34% versus 32.70% (+5.6 pp). On PubMed, the advantage is more modest but consistent (e.g., +4.25 pp BERT-small at r/p=2 with GPT-2). In the non-private regime (r/p=∞), SecPE is slightly lower or comparable (within ~1–3 pp), which the authors attribute to clustering discarding fine-grained structure. The PII experiment (Table 8) shows smaller gains, which the authors note are bottlenecked by the precision/recall of the underlying PII detectors (AI4Privacy, Presidio) rather than by the SecPE mechanism itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

SecPE extends the Private Evolution (PE) paradigm of Xie et al. (2024) and Lin et al. (2025), which iteratively refines synthetic candidates via DP voting against private data using foundation model APIs. The key departure is replacing the uniform μ-GDP guarantee with (p,r)-secret protection from Ganesh et al. (2025), which constrains the adversary's reconstruction success at a single prior point rather than over the entire trade-off curve. Additionally, SecPE substitutes pointwise DP voting with K-means-based cluster representatives, reducing per-iteration complexity from O(M·N_syn) to O(K·N_syn). The theoretical bridge between GDP and secret protection via the trade-off/blow-up function (Dong et al., 2019; Hayes et al., 2023) provides the formal foundation for this relaxation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from uniform DP to secret-specific protection offers a principled way to reduce noise when sensitive content is sparse, and the clustering-based selection yields substantial computational savings (up to ~10,000× on Yelp). However, the practical utility gains are modest in PII settings and are bottlenecked by the quality of upstream secret detection (AI4Privacy, Presidio), limiting immediate deployment impact. Open questions include formalizing what constitutes a 'secret' and its sensitivity, extending to heterogeneous secret-specific budgets, and adapting the framework to non-text modalities. The non-private regime shows slight degradation from clustering abstraction, suggesting a fundamental tension between representational compression and fidelity.
