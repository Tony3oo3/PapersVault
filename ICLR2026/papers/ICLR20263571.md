## Problem

Few-shot node classification (FSNC) is fundamentally limited by the small support set size per novel class, and existing augmentation strategies (random perturbation, mix-up) fail to generate faithful graph structures for synthetic nodes. While diffusion models have been extended to graph generation, prior work targets graph-level synthesis and does not support structured node- or edge-level generation needed to enlarge the support set in FSNC. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a node-level graph diffusion model that synthesizes both support nodes and their edges, combined with low-rank regularization to counteract the inherent noise of diffusion generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an attributed graph G=(V,E,X) with N' nodes, an n-way k-shot support set of labeled nodes, and an unlabeled query set as input. It first generates M=q×n×k synthetic support nodes with their node attributes and edges connecting to the original graph, producing an augmented graph G_aug with (q+1)nk labeled support nodes. The augmented graph is then encoded by an existing FSNC encoder (e.g., COLA or COSMIC), and a low-rank transductive linear classifier predicts class labels for all query nodes. The method is designed as a plug-in module: the FGDM is trained once on the original graph before test-time, and the low-rank classifier is trained per-task on the augmented support set.

## Method

LR-FGDM comprises two stages. First, the Few-Shot Graph Diffusion Model (FGDM) generates synthetic graph structures: a Hierarchical Graph Autoencoder (HGAE) encodes nodes via GAT layers with positional embeddings and a prototypical regularization that clusters latent features around learned prototypes, while a hierarchical edge reconstruction method (inter-cluster neighbor map followed by intra-cluster neighbor map) avoids the O(N'²) complexity of standard GAE edge decoders; a Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) then generates synthetic node features conditioned on cluster prototypes via Classifier-Free Guidance rather than class labels, which is critical since test-time classes are disjoint from training classes. Second, a low-rank transductive classifier is trained on the augmented graph using a truncated nuclear norm (TNN) regularization on the kernel Gram matrix, motivated by the Low Frequency Property and theoretically justified by a generalization bound (Theorem A.1) showing that lower kernel complexity yields a tighter upper bound on test loss. An optional LRA layer applies self-attention with a low-rank attention matrix B=K/λ̂₁ to the encoder output, further reducing kernel complexity and tightening the bound, yielding LRA-LR-FGDM.

## Results

On the 5-way 5-shot FSNC setting, COLA(LRA-LR-FGDM) achieves 90.32% on CoraFull, 70.22% on ogbn-arxiv, 90.39% on Coauthor-CS, and 90.07% on DBLP, consistently outperforming all baselines. The improvement over the base COLA method is +2.49% (CoraFull), +2.70% (ogbn-arxiv), +2.85% (Coauthor-CS), and +2.84% (DBLP). The hierarchical edge reconstruction reduces HGAE training time by over 5× on ogbn-arxiv (301s vs. 1690s) and generation time by over 3× (0.130s vs. 0.426s per sample).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LR-FGDM is integrated as a plug-in on top of two strong FSNC backbones (COLA and COSMIC) and consistently improves them by 1.8–2.9% across all four main datasets in the 5-way 5-shot setting. Against the most relevant diffusion-based competitor DoG (Wang et al., 2025b), COLA(LRA-LR-FGDM) gains +3.85% on CoraFull, +4.53% on ogbn-arxiv, +3.04% on Coauthor-CS, and +2.48% on DBLP, with the gap widening in harder settings (e.g., +4.53% vs. DoG on ogbn-arxiv 5-way 5-shot). Against the recent SOTA STAR (Liu et al., 2025a), the improvement is +3.01% (CoraFull) to +2.97% (DBLP). Older meta-learning baselines (ProtoNet, Meta-GNN, GPN, G-Meta, TENT) are outperformed by 15–39 points, reflecting the substantial gains from both the generative augmentation and the low-rank transductive classifier. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that both the prototypical regularization in HGAE and the truncated nuclear norm on the classifier contribute independently, with the full COLA(LRA-LR-FGDM) exceeding the no-regularization variant by 1.5–2.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions LR-FGDM as a plug-in augmentation module for existing few-shot node classification (FSNC) methods like COLA and COSMIC, rather than a standalone classifier. It departs from prior graph diffusion work (DoG, Niu et al., Jo et al.) in two key ways: operating at the node level rather than graph level, and conditioning the Latent Diffusion Model on cluster prototypes learned via prototypical regularization instead of class labels—solving the fundamental incompatibility of label-conditioned generation with novel-class few-shot settings. The low-rank regularization draws on the Low Frequency Property literature (Rahaman et al., Arora et al.) and the kernel complexity framework from transductive learning theory (Tolstikhin et al., Yang 2025b), while the LRA layer introduces a novel self-attention mechanism whose weight matrix is the normalized kernel Gram matrix itself, further reducing kernel complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded recipe for generative data augmentation in few-shot graph learning: synthesize support nodes via diffusion, then regularize the classifier to be robust to the inherent noise of generation. The Theorem A.1 guarantee connecting truncated nuclear norm regularization to tighter transductive generalization bounds is a useful theoretical contribution that could inform other few-shot settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large graphs (ogbn-arxiv at 169K nodes is the largest tested), whether the dataset-specific cross-validation of synthetic node count q and rank r₀ can be automated more reliably, and whether the plug-in framework extends to other few-shot tasks (link prediction, graph classification) beyond node classification.
