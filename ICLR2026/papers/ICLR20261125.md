## Problem

Existing LLM style control methods are either discrete (prompt engineering, instruction finetuning) or require expensive parallel corpora to extract steering vectors (CAA, RepE, ITI), making continuous fine-grained control over dimensions like conceptual complexity impractical. Even when parallel data is available, imperfect content alignment pollutes extracted style vectors with semantic variation, and naive averaging is vulnerable to noise and outliers, limiting stability across topics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261125/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard text prompt and a continuous steering coefficient α (a single scalar, e.g., ranging from −20 to +20 in experiments) and produces generated text with the target stylistic attribute shifted accordingly. The style vector is pre-computed offline from a single non-parallel (or even single-style) dataset, so no parallel pairs are needed at test time. Positive α steers toward the learned style while negative α steers in the opposite direction, enabling bidirectional control from one vector with negligible per-token overhead (a single vector addition per layer).

## Method

Causal-Steer reframes LoRA as a causal intervention: it trains style-specific LoRA adapters on non-parallel data using a contrastive loss (anchors from one style, negatives from the other) to push the weight update ΔW into a style-discriminative subspace. For each input, the activation difference between the LoRA-perturbed model and the frozen base model is computed, which cancels content by construction since both models process the identical prompt. These per-sample difference vectors are then denoised via PCA projection onto the top-k principal components (k=8) and aggregated using the geometric median for outlier-resistant centrality, yielding a single robust style vector per layer. At inference, the normalized vector is added to MLP activations at mid-to-late layers (18–23) scaled by α, enabling linear, bidirectional, and composable multi-attribute control without any weight modification.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Causal-Steer achieves the highest Knowledge Difficulty score (8.42) and Flesch Grade Level (22.78) when steering toward Complex, while simultaneously attaining the lowest Difficulty (2.77) and Flesch Grade (10.71) when steering toward Simple, all while maintaining Relevance ≥ 9.29 and Fluency ≥ 8.35. On LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, Causal-Steer similarly leads with Difficulty 7.22 (Complex) and 1.92 (Simple). For detoxification on Llama-2-7b-chat, Causal-Steer achieves the lowest toxicity scores (0.0609 toxic / 0.0520 random) while keeping perplexity competitive (12.95 / 8.18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Causal-Steer outperforms all activation-steering baselines (CAA, ITI, RepE) by a wide margin on style control: on Qwen2.5-7B, CS achieves Difficulty 8.42 vs. 5.37 (CAA), 5.16 (ITI), and 5.35 (RepE) for Complex, and 2.77 vs. 4.18/4.17/4.37 for Simple. The mean-token variants (CAA_mean, ITI_mean) narrow the gap (e.g., CAA_mean reaches Diff 8.00) but still trail CS, while RepE_mean fails to improve meaningfully (Diff 4.99). Against CLMI, CS wins on both style intensity (Diff 8.42 vs. 8.36; F-G 22.78 vs. 20.67) and generation quality (Rel 9.75 vs. 8.92; Flu 8.35 vs. 7.97), and crucially supports bidirectional control beyond CLMI's bounded [0,1] interpolation range. ReFT is the weakest baseline on style control (Diff 4.11 Complex, 3.05 Simple) and fails entirely in the reverse direction. On detoxification (Table 4), CS achieves toxicity 0.0609/0.0520, beating GCAV-Output (0.0879/0.0622), Arithmetic (0.1625/0.0816), and ActAdd (0.1620/0.0852) while maintaining lower perplexity than ActAdd and Arithmetic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) from a standard fine-tuning mechanism into a causal intervention tool, departing from its original use in parameter interpolation methods like CLMI (Kangaslahti & Alvarez-Melis, 2025) and task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022). Within the activation steering family (CAA, RepE, ITI), it addresses two specific failure modes—content pollution from imperfect parallel alignment and sensitivity to outliers—by computing activation differences on identical inputs under a LoRA perturbation rather than across contrasting texts, and by replacing naive averaging with PCA denoising plus geometric median aggregation. The contrastive training objective guiding the LoRA update is borrowed from metric learning but applied to weight-space perturbations rather than embedding spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the parallel-corpus requirement substantially lowers the barrier to style control for dimensions where aligned data is impractical to construct (e.g., conceptual complexity), and the negligible inference overhead (a single vector addition) makes the approach deployable in real-time settings. The demonstrated cross-model, cross-task, and cross-lingual generalization suggests the extracted style vectors capture transferable representational structure rather than dataset-specific artifacts. Open questions include whether the first-order Taylor approximation remains valid for larger LoRA ranks or more extreme style shifts, how the method scales to dozens of simultaneously controlled attributes, and whether the PCA dimensionality (k=8) generalizes across model sizes and architectures.
