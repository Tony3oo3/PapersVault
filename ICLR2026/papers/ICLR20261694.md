## Problem

Existing AIGC image watermarking methods face a critical trade-off: one-dimensional verification metrics (e.g., decoded bit counts or scalar thresholds) cannot simultaneously defend against watermark removal and spoofing attacks, since adjusting the threshold for one undermines the other. Additionally, most methods are limited to binary ownership verification and lack the forensic capability to detect and localize semantic-level tampering performed by advanced AIGC editing tools.

## Input / Output

For generation, the method takes a text prompt, a user-specific key K (a Gaussian tensor matching the diffusion model's latent shape, e.g., 4×64×64 = 16,384 components for Stable Diffusion), and a timestamp-derived salt S ∈ U(0,1) stored in image metadata. For verification, it consumes the watermarked image, the claimed key K, and the metadata timestamp, producing a watermarked image (same resolution as the base model), a binary ownership decision, an attack-type classification (removal, spoofing, or tampering), and a spatial binary mask localizing tampered regions. The key is continuous and high-dimensional (~33,548 bits of entropy), and the timestamp only affects output diversity without influencing verification correctness.

## Method

PAI is a training-free, plug-and-play framework that injects watermarks through two coupled stages within the diffusion sampling process. At initialization, a Box-Muller transformation maps the user key K and timestamp salt S into the initial noise while preserving the N(0,1) Gaussian prior required by diffusion models. During the first five DDIM denoising steps, a key-conditioned deflection function H(K, x_t^wm, t) scales the predicted x̂₀ by a factor (γK+1), iteratively steering the generative trajectory so that the identity signal becomes semantically entangled with the content. Verification inverts this process via DDIM with the inverse deflection H⁻¹, computing an initialization bias δ_t between the recovered noise and the theoretically expected F(K,S); a one-sided hypothesis test on E[|δ_t|²] rejects invalid keys, while PCA projection of the bias vector combined with Mahalanobis distance separates removal (departing from the benign cluster) from spoofing (departing from the non-watermarked cluster in the opposite direction). Tamper localization exploits the observation that spatial anomalies in the noise-space bias δ_t align with manipulated regions in RGB space, requiring no task-specific training.

## Results

PAI achieves 98.43% average ownership verification accuracy across 12 attack types, outperforming SOTA methods by 37.25% on average. Under watermark removal attacks, PAI reaches 99.0% O-ACC (vs. 0.28–99.93% for baselines); under spoofing attacks, it achieves 96.3% O-ACC (vs. 2.74–99.92%). For tamper localization, PAI attains an average AUC of 89.77 and F1 of 77.05, maintaining 100% O-ACC even under full-image semantic editing (Stable Inpainting) where EditGuard collapses to 0.08% O-ACC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baselines: two embedded methods (EditGuard, Stable Signature) and two inherent methods (Tree-Ring, Gaussian Shading). The key finding is a clear trade-off among existing methods: Gaussian Shading resists removal (99.93% avg. O-ACC) but collapses under spoofing (15.77%), while Stable Signature is robust to spoofing (99.92%) but nearly fails under removal (0.28–0.30%). Tree-Ring is inconsistent, scoring 5.94% under classifier-based adversarial removal yet 86.00% under imprinting removal. EditGuard, the only baseline supporting tamper localization, achieves competitive F1 (86.07) on partial edits (PS) but drops to 34.00 F1 and 0.08% O-ACC under full-image Stable Inpainting, where PAI maintains 80.00 F1 and 100% O-ACC. PAI is the only method that simultaneously achieves >96% O-ACC under both removal and spoofing attacks, breaking the trade-off by modeling directional deviations in PCA-projected latent space rather than relying on a single scalar threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inherent watermarking paradigm for diffusion models (Tree-Ring, Gaussian Shading) by moving beyond noise-initialization-only embedding to a dual-stage injection: Box-Muller-based key embedding at initialization plus a key-conditioned deflection of the predicted x̂₀ during early DDIM sampling steps. The verification mechanism is built directly on DDIM inversion (Song et al., 2020), but the key departure is replacing the scalar threshold used by all prior methods with a PCA-projected Mahalanobis distance in the initialization-bias space, which enables directional discrimination between removal and spoofing attacks. The semantic tamper localization via noise-space discrepancy (Ω_t) generalizes pixel-based approaches like EditGuard to the latent space, addressing a gap the authors identify for AIGC editing tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified treatment of ownership verification, attack-type discrimination, and semantic tamper localization under a single training-free signal (initialization bias) addresses a practical gap in AIGC copyright enforcement as commercial editing tools (Gemini 2.0 Flash, Stable Inpainting) enable subtle semantic manipulations that defeat pixel-based forensics. The theoretical guarantee on key exclusivity and the 37.25% average improvement over SOTA across 12 attacks make this a strong candidate for provider-side deployment. Open questions include scalability to video diffusion, applicability to non-diffusion AIGC architectures, and whether the 2D PCA projection (k=2) remains sufficient as the attack landscape grows in diversity. The method's reliance on DDIM inversion's inherent bias as the forensic signal also raises questions about robustness to future diffusion model architectures that may alter inversion characteristics.
