## Problem

Existing GNN-based physics simulators represent meshes as vertex-edge graphs, discarding the higher-dimensional geometric elements (2D facets and 3D cells) that encode boundary and volumetric information. Under sparse mesh discretization, this node-only abstraction causes inaccurate contact detection (vertex distances diverge from true surface distances) and poor approximation of local integral kernels, leading to significant errors in both inter-object contact and intra-object physical propagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262181/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The simulator consumes a history of four states {u⁰, u^{(k-1)Δt}, u^{kΔt}, f^{kΔt}} over a mesh of regular tetrahedra or hexahedra, where each vertex carries physical field quantities (position, stress, equivalent plastic strain) and scripted external forces are encoded as relative displacements with a binary flag. The output is the next-step vertex displacement, stress increment, and plastic strain increment, which are rolled out autoregressively over 75–125 timesteps. Contact detection uses a face-to-face Bounding Volume Hierarchy with a dataset-specific radius (r_F), distinct from the node-based radius (r_W) used by baselines, ensuring comparable contact edge counts across methods.

## Method

MAVEN extends the encoder-processor-decoder framework by explicitly creating graph nodes for every 3D cell and 2D facet in addition to vertices, forming a bipartite cell-facet graph. A position-sensitive geometric aggregator—inspired by FEM shape functions—computes learnable, centered normalization weights from local coordinate vectors to aggregate vertex features into cell and facet nodes, avoiding the homogenization of simple averaging. Two-stage message passing then propagates information: first through facet nodes (which mediate inter-object contacts via face-to-face edges and external force features), then through cell nodes (which capture volumetric internal dynamics), using symmetric aggregation coefficients shared across stages. A geometric disaggregator maps updated cell features back to vertices with residual connections and an FFN, producing spatially smooth predictions. The model is trained with one-step MSE loss on normalized physical quantities, using 15 processor layers with 96-dimensional hidden states and ~3.1M parameters.

## Results

On the 50-step rollout, MAVEN achieves RMSE of 5.21 (×10³) on Cavity Grasping position, 13.78 on Deforming Plate position, and 276.73 on Metal Bending position, consistently outperforming all baselines. The most significant gains appear on the coarse-mesh Metal Bending dataset, where MAVEN improves position RMSE by 33.82% over the best baseline (FIGNet: 515.17), stress RMSE by 11.90% (4901.56 vs. 5583.71), and PEEQ by 8.67% (0.20 vs. 0.22). Across all three datasets, MAVEN delivers average improvements of 3.41%, 13.07%, and 18.13% from fine to coarse meshes, confirming that explicit high-dimensional geometric modeling becomes increasingly critical under sparse discretization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAVEN outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the node-based MGN (Pfaff et al., 2020), MAVEN reduces MBD position RMSE from 686.19 to 276.73 (59.7% reduction) and MBD stress from 12,508 to 4,902 (60.8% reduction), highlighting the severe limitations of vertex-only contact detection under coarse meshes. The Graph Transformer (Yun et al., 2019) and hierarchical methods HCMT (Yu et al., 2024) and HOOD (Grigorev et al., 2023) all struggle most on MBD, with position RMSEs ranging from 623 to 852, roughly 2–3× worse than MAVEN's 276.73. FIGNet (Allen et al., 2023), the closest competitor as the only other facet-aware method, achieves the best MBD stress among baselines (5,583.71) but still trails MAVEN by 11.90%; on MBD position, FIGNet (515.17) is nearly 2× worse than MAVEN (276.73), confirming that facet-level contact alone is insufficient without explicit volumetric cell modeling for internal propagation. The gap between MAVEN and baselines widens with mesh coarseness: on the dense Deforming Plate dataset, improvements are modest (1.33% on position), while on the very coarse Metal Bending dataset, gains reach 33.82% on position, validating the paper's central claim that high-dimensional geometric elements are critical under sparse discretization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAVEN extends the encoder-processor-decoder GNN framework established by MeshGraphNets (Pfaff et al., 2020) by promoting 3D cells and 2D facets from implicit geometric context to first-class nodes in the message-passing graph. It also builds on FIGNet (Allen et al., 2023), which introduced face-to-face contact detection for rigid bodies, by coupling facet-level contact with cell-level volumetric propagation to handle internal elastoplastic dynamics in 3D solids. The geometric aggregator design draws directly on classical FEM shape functions, bridging numerical-methods intuition with learnable message passing. The key conceptual departure from all prior node-based GNN simulators is the explicit bipartite cell-facet graph topology, which replaces vertex-only graphs with a higher-dimensional geometric structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated gap: node-based GNNs degrade sharply on the sparse meshes that industrial simulations actually use, and MAVEN shows that explicit high-dimensional geometric modeling recovers accuracy without requiring finer discretization. The introduction of the Metal Bending Dataset with elastoplastic behavior, large deformations, and coarse hexahedral meshes fills a notable gap in public benchmarks for this regime. However, the method remains sensitive to mesh quality, does not natively capture long-range interactions, and is limited to Lagrangian volumetric formulations; extending it to Eulerian grids, thin shells, or hierarchical long-range propagation are open directions that will determine its broader adoption.
