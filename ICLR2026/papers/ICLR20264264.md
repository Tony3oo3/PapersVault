## Problem

Existing VLM-based autonomous driving agents operate under a passive perception paradigm, reasoning over a fixed set of inputs without the ability to actively seek additional visual evidence when the default view is insufficient. Moreover, no prior work has introduced an adaptive mechanism that balances efficient text-only reasoning for routine scenes against deeper tool-augmented visual reasoning for complex or uncertain scenarios, leaving a gap in both interpretability and computational efficiency for high-level behavioral planning.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a single front-view camera image (capped at 259,200 pixels), current vehicle speed, and a navigation command as its default input. It outputs an 8-second meta-action sequence of four composite tokens (each pairing a velocity token from {Accelerate, Keep Speed, Decelerate, Stop} with a trajectory token from {Straight, Left Turn, Right Turn}) at 2-second intervals, preceded by a structured chain-of-thought in description-reasoning-prediction format. Before generating the plan, the agent emits a mode-selection token (<think_text> or <think_tool>); in tool mode it may interleave up to three tool calls (Retrieve View, RoI Inspection, Depth Estimation, 3D Object Detection) that inject new image embeddings into its context. An optional lightweight MLP head converts the meta-action sequence plus visual tokens into 3-second future waypoints at 2 Hz for low-level motion planning.

## Method

DriveAgent-R1 builds on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and introduces two coupled innovations: (1) an active-perception Vision Toolkit that lets the agent proactively fetch historical frames, zoom into regions of interest, estimate depth, and run open-vocabulary 3D detection mid-reasoning, and (2) a hybrid-thinking framework that adaptively routes between a fast text-only CoT path and a slower tool-augmented CoT path based on scene complexity. Training follows a three-stage progressive strategy: first, a domain-alignment SFT on 530K driving VQA pairs (DriveAlign-3B) instills visual sensitivity; second, Dual-Mode SFT cold-starts both reasoning modes using teacher-generated CoT annotations partitioned by tool necessity; third, a Cascaded RL phase first applies Mode-Partitioned GRPO (MP-GRPO), which forces balanced exploration by splitting each GRPO group into half text-mode and half tool-mode responses for intra- and inter-mode contrastive learning, then applies native GRPO with a conditional tool-usage reward that penalizes superfluous tool calls by requiring tool-mode accuracy to exceed the text-mode group baseline by a margin. This 'foundation → mode strengthening → intelligent selection' pipeline yields a 3B model whose adaptive mode selection (68.5% MSA) and active perception produce planning accuracy competitive with GPT-5 and human drivers while reducing inference latency by over 20% relative to passive multi-view baselines.

## Results

On Drive-Internal_test, DriveAgent-R1 (3B) achieves 51.34% First-Frame Joint Accuracy and 45.42% Sequence Average Joint Accuracy with tool access, gaining +6.07 points over its tool-free variant. On nuScenes_test, it reaches 52.96% First-Frame and 47.10% Seq. Avg. Joint Accuracy with tools, surpassing GPT-5 (49.11% / 45.14%) and approaching the human baseline (50.48% / 48.24%). On DriveBench, it doubles DriveLM's Perception score (34.07 vs. 16.85) and achieves the top Behavior score (43.69). For open-loop motion planning on nuScenes, it attains 0.28 m average ADE, outperforming DriveVLM-Dual (0.31 m) and VAD-Base (0.37 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-5, DriveAgent-R1 (3B) trails on Drive-Internal (51.34% vs. 56.48% First-Frame) but clearly wins on nuScenes, surpassing GPT-5 by +3.85 points on First-Frame (52.96% vs. 49.11%) and +1.96 points on Seq. Avg. (47.10% vs. 45.14%), while also exceeding the human baseline on nuScenes First-Frame. On DriveBench, DriveAgent-R1 more than doubles DriveLM's Perception score (34.07 vs. 16.85) and edges out its Behavior score (43.69 vs. 42.78), though DriveLM retains an advantage in Prediction (44.33 vs. 32.85) and Planning (68.71 vs. 61.89); both substantially outperform Dolphins across all four sub-tasks. In open-loop motion planning on nuScenes, DriveAgent-R1 achieves the best average ADE (0.28 m) among all compared methods, beating DriveVLM-Dual (0.31 m), VAD-Base (0.37 m), and UniAD (0.69 m), with a collision rate (0.14%) comparable to VAD-Base and slightly higher than DriveVLM-Dual (0.1%). Against the Qwen2.5-VL open-source series, DriveAgent-R1 dramatically outperforms the same-size 3B base (45.42% vs. 22.63% Seq. Avg. on Drive-Internal with tools) and even surpasses the 72B variant (45.42% vs. 39.61%), demonstrating that targeted training with active perception compensates for a 24× parameter gap. The paper also notes that smaller Qwen models (3B, 7B) actually degrade when given tool access without specialized training, underscoring that effective tool use is a non-trivial skill that DriveAgent-R1's Cascaded RL explicitly cultivates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-based autonomous driving line (DriveLM, DriveVLM, Dolphins) by replacing their passive perception paradigm—where the model reasons over a fixed set of camera inputs—with an active perception framework that proactively invokes a vision toolkit (RoI Inspection, Depth Estimation, 3D Object Detection, Retrieve View) during reasoning. It builds directly on GRPO from DeepSeek-R1, proposing Mode-Partitioned GRPO (MP-GRPO) to enforce balanced exploration across two thinking modes, and on the broader M-CoT literature by introducing a hybrid-thinking mechanism that adaptively switches between efficient text-only reasoning and tool-augmented visual reasoning. The Cascaded RL training strategy (FCM-RL → AMS-RL) is a novel orchestration of these components, but each individual piece (GRPO, tool-augmented CoT, domain alignment) draws on established techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a 3B-parameter model with active perception matches GPT-5 and approaches human-level performance on high-level driving planning while being more efficient than passive multi-view processing (6.74s vs. 8.50s latency), offering a deployment-friendly path for resource-constrained vehicles. The key open limitation is the agent's over-reliance on newly acquired visual evidence, which can override correct initial judgments (e.g., misidentifying a pedestrian signal as its own traffic light), pointing to the unresolved challenge of resolving informational conflicts in complex topologies. Whether the hybrid-thinking and active-perception paradigm generalizes beyond the specific meta-action formulation and to closed-loop, real-time driving remains to be demonstrated.
