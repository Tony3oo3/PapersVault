## Problem

Directly applying text-centric reinforcement learning (as in DeepSeek-R1) to Multimodal LLMs yields diminishing returns because the training pipeline has three unaddressed weaknesses: insufficient cold-start initialization that fails to instill reflective reasoning, gradient stagnation in standard GRPO under sparse binary rewards in multimodal settings, and the absence of a post-multimodal text-only refinement stage. The paper asks how to orchestrate a multi-stage curriculum that overcomes all three simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model (built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B/3B-Instruct) receives a pair (v, q) consisting of an RGB image and a textual query, and generates a long, self-reflective chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer. The reward signal is rule-based and binary (r=1 if the extracted answer matches ground truth, 0 otherwise), with AIME24/25 evaluated via average@32 and all other benchmarks via pass@1. The GRAMMAR dataset used for training comprises 283K text-only cold-start samples, 31K text RL samples, and 21K multimodal RL samples, all with verifiable ground truths.

## Method

ReVisual-R1 employs a three-stage curriculum: (1) a text-only cold-start SFT on 283K high-difficulty reasoning samples to forge foundational CoT and self-reflection capabilities before any multimodal training; (2) a Multimodal RL phase using GRPO augmented with Prioritized Advantage Distillation (PAD), which combats gradient stagnation by filtering out near-zero-advantage samples and then performing temperature-controlled softmax-weighted sub-sampling from the remaining effective set to focus updates on the most informative trajectories; (3) a Text-only RL phase (with the vision tower frozen) that sharpens linguistic fluency and abstract reasoning without eroding visual grounding. The GRAMMAR dataset is curated via a multi-stage pipeline involving rule-based filtering, difficulty stratification into 10 levels by Qwen2.5-VL-32B, NV-EmbeddingV2 encoding, HDBSCAN clustering, and balanced sampling across topics and difficulty strata. Ablations confirm that the MRL-then-TRL ordering outperforms reversed or mixed-RL alternatives, and PAD outperforms both vanilla GRPO and DAPO.

## Results

ReVisual-R1-7B achieves a 53.1% average across ten multimodal and textual reasoning benchmarks, a +16.8 pp improvement over the best open-source 7B baseline. On MATH500 it reaches 89.2%, surpassing doubao-1.5-vision-pro-32k (85.2%) and GPT-4o (74.6%); on AIME24 it scores 53.3% versus 26.7% (doubao) and 9.3% (GPT-4o). The 3B variant (ReVisual-R1-3B) attains 46.5% average, a +16.0 pp gain over the best open-source 3B model (VLAA-Thinker-3B at 30.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReVisual-R1-7B (53.1% avg) outperforms all seven open-source 7B reasoning MLLMs by a wide margin, with the largest gaps on AIME24 (+43.3 pp over VLAA-Thinker-7B's 0.8%) and MATH500 (+22.0 pp over ThinkLite-7B-VL's 61.4%). Against closed-source models, it surpasses GPT-4o (41.6%), Claude-3.7-Sonnet (48.2%), and Gemini-2.0-Flash (47.8%) on average, and beats doubao-1.5-vision-pro-32k on MATH500 (89.2 vs 85.2), AIME24 (53.3 vs 26.7), and AIME25 (43.3 vs 20.0), though doubao retains a higher overall average (55.8 vs 53.1) driven by stronger MathVerse-V (64.7 vs 53.6) and MathVista (78.6 vs 73.1). The only benchmark where ReVisual-R1-7B trails the best open-source 7B model is MathVista (73.1 vs VL-Rethinker-7B's 73.7, a −0.6 pp margin). At the 3B scale, ReVisual-R1-3B (46.5% avg) dominates VLAA-Thinker-3B (30.5%) and FAST-3B (29.9%) across every benchmark, with particularly large gains on AIME24 (+33.3 pp) and MathVision (+19.2 pp).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepSeek-R1 RL-for-reasoning paradigm from text-only LLMs to MLLMs, building directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and concurrent multimodal RL efforts (Vision-R1, R1-OneVision, MM-Eureka, VL-Rethinker). It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) replacing multimodal cold-start data with high-difficulty text-only data, (2) identifying and addressing gradient stagnation in GRPO via Prioritized Advantage Distillation (PAD), and (3) introducing a staged curriculum (text cold-start → multimodal RL → text RL) rather than the mixed or single-stage RL used by most prior multimodal reasoning models. The GRAMMAR dataset curation pipeline draws on a broad set of open-source reasoning corpora but applies a novel difficulty-stratified, embedding-clustered sampling strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that text-only cold-start initialization can match or exceed multimodal cold-start in downstream multimodal reasoning is a practical recipe that could simplify training pipelines for the community, and the three-stage SRO curriculum provides a concrete, reproducible path to strong open-source MLLM reasoners at 3B and 7B scale. However, the paper acknowledges the absence of a theoretical explanation for why text-centric optimization transfers to visual grounding, and scalability beyond 7B (e.g., MoE architectures) remains unvalidated. The interaction between data composition and training stage ordering is only partially explored, leaving open questions about optimal curriculum design for broader task coverage beyond mathematics.
