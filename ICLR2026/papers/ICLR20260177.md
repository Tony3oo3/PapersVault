## Problem

Standard RL algorithms for language models (e.g., GRPO) rely on autoregressive token-level factorization of sequence likelihood, but diffusion LLMs generate sequences non-autoregressively through iterative denoising, making token-level conditional probabilities ill-defined. Prior work resorted to heuristic proxies—mean-field surrogates or token-level ELBO decompositions—that introduce formal inconsistencies because the ELBO is only valid as a sequence-level lower bound, not as a per-token conditional. This paper identifies this fundamental mismatch and asks how to formulate RL for dLLMs in a principled way that respects their holistic generation process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x and produces a complete text sequence y generated through iterative non-autoregressive denoising steps (typically 256 denoising steps for LLaDA, with 2 tokens unmasked per step for planning tasks). RL training uses verifiable rewards evaluated only on the final generated sequence (output-reward setting), with group-relative advantages computed over G sampled completions per prompt. Training is conducted at a fixed sequence length of 256, while evaluation generalizes to lengths 128, 256, and 512.

## Method

ESPO reformulates the GRPO objective at the sequence level by treating the entire generated sequence as a single atomic action, replacing the intractable sequence log-likelihood with its ELBO approximation. The key stabilization is a per-token normalization of the ELBO log-ratio (dividing by sequence length L before exponentiation), which prevents the astronomically large or small importance ratios that arise from raw ELBO differences scaling linearly with L. For KL regularization, ESPO adopts the k2 estimator (a quadratic function of the ELBO difference) instead of the standard k3 estimator, whose exponential term reintroduces the same instability and whose gradient is actually an unbiased estimate of reverse-KL rather than forward-KL. Two variance-reduction techniques—antithetic sampling (sharing mask positions between old and new policy ELBO estimates) and coupled sampling (averaging complementary masks)—further stabilize the Monte Carlo ELBO estimation. The approach is validated through ablations showing that sequence-level + ELBO is the only combination achieving stable convergence, and through benchmarks where ESPO gains 20–40 points on Countdown and 60+ points on Sudoku over token-level baselines.

## Results

On planning tasks, ESPO delivers the most dramatic gains: on Countdown (LLaDA), it reaches 81.0% average accuracy versus 33.9% for d1 and 48.3% for wd1 (+47 and +33 points respectively), and on Sudoku it achieves 86.0% versus 22.2% (d1) and 23.1% (wd1) (+64 and +63 points). On mathematics, ESPO improves GSM8K from 75.9% (base LLaDA) to 82.0% and MATH from 37.0% to 39.5%, consistently outperforming both d1 and wd1. On coding, ESPO achieves 40.1% on HumanEval and 45.4% on MBPP (LLaDA), surpassing d1 (37.2%/36.5%) and wd1 (37.2%/36.5%), and approaching LLaDA-1.5 (40.3%/39.4%) which was trained on a much larger private dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESPO consistently outperforms both token-level dLLM-RL baselines across all tasks. Against d1 (diffu-GRPO), ESPO gains +47.1 points on Countdown (LLaDA), +63.8 on Sudoku, +4.0 on GSM8K, +1.8 on MATH, and +2.9 on HumanEval. Against wd1, the margins are even larger on planning: +32.7 on Countdown and +62.9 on Sudoku (LLaDA), while on math/coding the gaps are smaller (+1.9 GSM8K, +2.6 MATH, +2.9 HumanEval). On the Dream backbone, ESPO still dominates: +36.0 on Countdown over d1, +10.1 on Sudoku over d1, and +30.5 on Countdown over wd1. The only near-parity is on MATH (Dream), where ESPO (46.0) edges out wd1 (45.8) by just 0.2 points, reflecting the ceiling imposed by the base model's pre-existing mathematical knowledge. On coding, ESPO (40.1% HumanEval) slightly trails LLaDA-1.5 (40.3%) but exceeds it on MBPP (45.4% vs. 39.4%), despite LLaDA-1.5 being trained on a significantly larger private dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESPO reformulates the GRPO objective (Shao et al., 2024) from a token-level to a sequence-level action space, directly addressing the incompatibility between autoregressive factorization assumptions and the non-autoregressive denoising process of dLLMs. It borrows the per-token length normalization of the importance ratio from GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025) and the k2 KL estimator from Schulman's KL approximations, but applies them in a fundamentally different setting where the likelihood is intractable and must be proxied by the ELBO. The paper explicitly departs from prior dLLM-RL methods (d1, wd1, UniGRPO, Coupled-GRPO) that force token-level ELBO contributions or mean-field surrogates into the GRPO template, arguing this decomposition is not merely suboptimal but formally invalid since the ELBO is only a valid lower bound at the sequence level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes sequence-level optimization as the principled paradigm for RL in dLLMs, with the clean ablation (Table 5) showing that the gain comes from the sequence-level formulation itself rather than the KL estimator choice. The dramatic 60+ point improvements on planning tasks (Sudoku, Countdown) suggest that tasks requiring global consistency are particularly sensitive to the token-level mismatch, though the more modest math/coding gains indicate the base model's pre-existing knowledge caps RL improvements. Open questions include whether the approach extends to continuous-time diffusion LLMs, whether the ELBO proxy tightens sufficiently at longer sequence lengths or more complex reasoning, and whether the computational overhead of MC-based ELBO estimation remains acceptable as models scale beyond 8B parameters.
