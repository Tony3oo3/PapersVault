## Problem

Existing temporal knowledge graph (TKG) reasoning methods operate under a closed-world assumption, assuming all entities have historical interactions available during training. In practice, roughly 25% of entities in TKGs are emerging entities that first appear without any prior interactions, causing severe representation collapse and significant performance degradation in link prediction. The paper asks how to perform inductive reasoning on such zero-history emerging entities by transferring temporal patterns from semantically similar known entities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal knowledge graph (timestamped entity-relation-entity facts), a query quadruple (e_q, r_q, ?, t_q), and entity title strings encoded by a frozen BERT encoder into fixed d-dimensional vectors. At inference, the query entity may have zero historical interactions, so the model must rely entirely on its frozen textual embedding and cluster-level transferred patterns rather than learned interaction history. Output is a ranked score over all candidate entities via a ConvTransE scoring function, with the top-ranked entity as the predicted answer.

## Method

TRANSFIR follows a three-stage Classification-Representation-Generalization pipeline. First, a learnable VQ codebook (K codewords) quantizes frozen BERT entity embeddings into latent semantic clusters, providing history-free categorical priors for emerging entities; the codebook is trained jointly with the task via standard VQ codebook and commitment losses. Second, an Interaction Chain (IC) is constructed around each query entity by collecting its past interactions within a temporal window, selecting the top-k most relation-similar interactions, and encoding them with a Transformer followed by relation-guided attention to produce a query-specific chain representation. Third, IC embeddings are pooled per codebook cluster to form dynamic prototypes, and a parametric drift mapping transfers cluster-level temporal patterns to all entities (including emerging ones) by adding a gated prototype offset to their frozen embeddings. The overall training objective combines a cross-entropy link prediction loss (ConvTransE scoring) with the codebook loss, trained simultaneously in chronological order.

## Results

TRANSFIR achieves the best MRR on all four TKG benchmarks for inductive reasoning on emerging entities: 0.1687 (ICEWS14), 0.1177 (ICEWS18), 0.2204 (ICEWS05-15), and 0.1103 (GDELT). The average MRR improvement over the strongest baseline is 28.6%, with the largest gain on GDELT (50.5% over TL logic) and consistent 15–25% gains on the ICEWS series. On Hits@10, TRANSFIR reaches 0.3246 on ICEWS14 and 0.3827 on ICEWS05-15, substantially outperforming the next-best methods (LogCL at 0.2273 and 0.2855, respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRANSFIR outperforms all 16 baselines on every dataset and metric. On ICEWS14, the strongest baseline is LogCL (MRR 0.1354); TRANSFIR improves by 24.6% to 0.1687. On ICEWS18, REGCN leads among baselines (MRR 0.0947), and TRANSFIR gains 24.3% to 0.1177. On ICEWS05-15, LogCL again is the top baseline (MRR 0.1917), with TRANSFIR achieving 0.2204 (+15.0%). On GDELT, the path-based TL logic is the best baseline (MRR 0.0733), and TRANSFIR nearly doubles it to 0.1103 (+50.5%). Among inductive baselines, CompGCN is the strongest on ICEWS05-15 (MRR 0.1885) but still trails TRANSFIR by 16.8%; InGram and MorsE perform poorly across all datasets, confirming that static inductive methods struggle when entities arrive with zero interaction history. The gap is most pronounced on Hits@10 for GDELT, where TRANSFIR (0.2278) more than doubles the best baseline (0.1131), reflecting the benefit of cluster-level pattern transfer on a large, rapidly evolving graph.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRANSFIR carves new ground at the intersection of temporal knowledge graph reasoning and inductive learning, addressing a gap neither line of work covers: existing TKG methods (CyGNet, LogCL, REGCN) operate under a closed-world entity assumption, while inductive KG methods (InGram, ULTRA, MorsE) assume new entities already possess some known interactions. The paper repurposes vector-quantized codebooks—originally from speech and image compression—as a mechanism for adaptive latent semantic clustering of entities, combined with Transformer-encoded Interaction Chains and ConvTransE scoring in a Classification-Representation-Generalization pipeline. This is a novel architectural composition rather than an extension of any single prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that ~25% of entities in standard TKG benchmarks are emerging (absent from training) and that existing models suffer severe representation collapse (Collapse Ratio dropping from 1.02 to 0.0055) provides a concrete, quantified diagnosis of a previously underappreciated failure mode. The practical limitation is that absolute performance remains low (best MRR ~0.22), and the method's reliance on entity textual quality (BERT embeddings) makes it vulnerable to noisy or abbreviated entity names, as shown on GDELT. Open questions include extending the framework to emerging relations, modeling long-term semantic drift of entity types, and whether the VQ codebook approach generalizes to domains where textual descriptions are sparse or multilingual.
