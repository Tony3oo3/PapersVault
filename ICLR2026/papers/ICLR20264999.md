## Problem

Vision-language models suffer from cross-modal misalignment that causes hallucinated coordinates and timestamps in dense prediction tasks like spatial-temporal video grounding (STVG). Existing remedies—trainable alignment tokens or auxiliary segmentation decoders—introduce extra learnable modules, large annotation costs, and limited generalization. No reinforcement learning framework had been applied to STVG, leaving the joint optimization of temporal and spatial accuracy unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264999/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (resized to ~1.6M total pixels, sampled at 2 FPS) and a natural-language query as input. Before feeding frames to the VLM, each frame is augmented with red numeric markers overlaid at the centroids of detected object instances, producing a compact visual-prompted video. The model outputs a temporal interval [t_s, t_e] and a single object ID; this ID is then resolved through a precomputed mask database (run-length encoded segmentation masks per frame) to recover per-frame bounding boxes or full segmentation masks for evaluation.

## Method

STVG-R1 reformulates dense per-frame bounding-box regression as a compact instance-level identification problem: an object-centric visual prompting pipeline (YOLOv12 detection → SAM2 segmentation and bidirectional tracking → periodic re-detection every 15 frames with IoU-based matching → ReID for temporal consistency) assigns each object a unique, temporally consistent numeric ID rendered as a red marker at its centroid. On top of this training-free prompting, the paper introduces the first RL framework for STVG, built on GRPO with a task-driven reward that sums three components: a continuous temporal IoU reward, a sparse 0/1 spatial consistency reward (granted only when the predicted ID matches ground truth AND appears within the predicted temporal segment), and a format reward enforcing structured reasoning output. The policy (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is optimized with n=8 sampled responses per query, normalized advantages, and KL regularization against a frozen reference policy, avoiding the need for any additional trainable alignment or decoder modules.

## Results

On HCSTVG-v2, STVG-R1 achieves 61.3% m_tIoU, 40.8% m_vIoU, 67.9% vIoU@0.3, and 38.8% vIoU@0.5, surpassing the strongest SFT-trained VLM (SpaceVLLM-7B) by 4.0%, 6.2%, 10.9%, and 14.1% respectively. On the zero-shot multi-object referring video object segmentation task (MeViS), STVG-R1 sets a new SOTA of 47.3% J&F, outperforming VideoGlaMM (45.2%) despite being trained only on single-object STVG data. On video temporal grounding, STVG-R1 achieves 52.5% tIoU@0.5 on Charades-STA (zero-shot), surpassing LLaVA-ST by +7.7%, and 42.5% tIoU@0.3 on TVGBench, slightly exceeding Time-R1 (41.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STVG-R1 outperforms all VLP-based specialized models (TubeDETR, STVGFormer, CG-STVG, TA-STVG) by a wide margin on spatial-temporal metrics: on HCSTVG-v2, it leads TA-STVG (the strongest VLP baseline) by +0.6% m_tIoU, +0.6% m_vIoU, +2.1% vIoU@0.3, and +2.1% vIoU@0.5. Among VLM-based methods, STVG-R1 surpasses SpaceVLLM-7B (the strongest SFT-trained VLM) by +3.3% m_tIoU, +6.8% m_vIoU, +11.0% vIoU@0.3, and +14.1% vIoU@0.5 on HCSTVG-v2, and beats LLaVA-ST-7B by +0.6% m_vIoU on ST-Align. On the zero-shot MeViS benchmark for multi-object referring video object segmentation, STVG-R1 achieves 47.3% J&F, exceeding VideoGlaMM (45.2%) and VISA (43.5%) without any task-specific training. On video temporal grounding, STVG-R1 (zero-shot) achieves 52.5% tIoU@0.5 on Charades-STA, surpassing LLaVA-ST (44.8%) by +7.7% but remaining below Time-R1 (60.8%) which was trained on the dataset; on TVGBench, STVG-R1 (42.5% tIoU@0.3) slightly edges out Time-R1 (41.8%). The object-centric visual prompting paradigm alone (without RL) already yields substantial zero-shot gains, e.g., +12.5% vIoU@0.3 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B and +28.3% for Qwen3-VL-8B on HCSTVG-v1, confirming that the reformulation itself is a major contributor to performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant STVG paradigm of either adding trainable alignment tokens (LLaVA-ST, SpaceVLLM) or trainable segmentation decoders (SA2VA, VideoGlaMM) to handle dense per-frame coordinate prediction. Instead, it borrows the visual prompting idea from Set-of-Mark and GPT4Scene, reformulating the task as object-ID identification over a training-free YOLOv12+SAM2 pipeline, and couples this with GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1/DeepSeekMath) as the first RL framework applied to STVG. The key conceptual departure is eliminating the cross-modal coordinate alignment problem entirely by making the VLM's output a discrete ID rather than continuous coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a practical alternative to the growing stack of trainable modules in VLM-based grounding, reducing annotation and compute overhead while achieving SOTA on five STVG/SVG benchmarks. The zero-shot transfer to multi-object referring video object segmentation (MeViS, 47.3% J&F) from single-object STVG training is the most compelling evidence that the ID-based formulation captures instance-level reasoning rather than task-specific coordinate regression. Open questions include scalability to very long videos, dependence on the external detector/tracker pipeline (which introduces its own failure modes), and whether the RL component is truly necessary given that VisualPrompt-SFT already captures most of the spatial gain.
