## Problem

Survival analysis models have made significant progress in predictive accuracy and concordance, but fine-grained individual-level calibration remains comparatively underexplored. Existing parametric ALD models suffer from distribution mismatch at the tails, while nonparametric quantile regression methods (e.g., CQRNN) face discretization and quantile-crossing issues. The paper addresses this by proposing a unified framework that achieves provable individual calibration while retaining the flexibility of both parametric and nonparametric approaches.

## Input / Output

The model takes a patient covariate vector x ∈ R^d and a quantile percentage q ~ U(0,1) as joint input, producing ALD parameters {θ, σ, κ} that define the conditional survival distribution. At test time, the continuous mixture over q is approximated by averaging predictions over 2,000 Monte Carlo samples, yielding a smooth CDF from which quantiles, mean survival time, and other distributional summaries can be computed in closed form. The post-calibration variant additionally accepts a pre-trained base model's ALD parameters and outputs multiplicative adjustment factors γ ∈ R^3.

## Method

ICALD combines a parametric ALD backbone (ensuring global smoothness and avoiding quantile crossing) with a lightweight adapter module that conditions the ALD parameters on a randomly sampled quantile percentage q, enabling local calibration adjustments. Training uses a joint loss L_ALD + λ·L_Cal (or L_ALD + λ·L_Cqr), where the ALD negative log-likelihood captures the full conditional distribution and the calibration/quantile-regression term enforces individual-level alignment. The key theoretical insight is that the pinball loss is equivalent to the ALD NLL up to a constant (Lemma 1), providing a principled probabilistic bridge between nonparametric quantile regression and parametric distribution modeling. The model is proven to be (ε,δ)-MPAIC and hence PAIC (Theorem 1), with a concentration bound (Theorem 2) showing that more quantile samples improve calibration. The framework supports both pre-calibration (joint training with warm-up) and post-calibration (a decoupled adapter trained on a frozen base model), with either loss function independently selectable.

## Results

On 21 datasets (14 synthetic, 7 real-world), the post-calibrated ICALD model (L_ALD+Cal^Post) achieves the highest win rate against DSM(Weibull) at 73.6% (103/140 comparisons) and DSM(LogNorm) at 71.4% (100/140), and against RSF at 55.7% (78/140). For individual calibration specifically, ICALD significantly outperforms ALD on 9/21 datasets with 0 losses, CQRNN on 6/21 with 0 losses, and DeepHit on 13/21 with 0 losses. In the post-calibration setting, L_ALD+Cal^Post beats the CSD baseline on 71.4% of calibration comparisons and CiPOT on 55.4%, while the pre-calibrated L_ALD+Cal^Pre wins 60.7% of comparisons against the uncalibrated ALD baseline with 0 losses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICALD (L_ALD+Cal^Post) achieves the strongest margins against parametric mixture models: it wins 73.6% of all 140 comparisons vs. DSM(Weibull) and 71.4% vs. DSM(LogNorm), excelling particularly in MAE, concordance, and all three calibration levels. Against the nonparametric baselines, ICALD dominates on individual calibration—beating DeepHit on 13/21 datasets (0 losses), GBM on 14/21 (0 losses), and RSF on 13/21 (0 losses)—while showing more mixed results on MAE and concordance. Versus the semi-parametric DeepSurv, ICALD wins 21/21 on IBS and 15/21 on average calibration but trails on concordance (4 wins vs. 16 ties). Against the closest ALD-based baselines, ICALD wins 9/21 on individual calibration over ALD (0 losses) and 6/21 over CQRNN (0 losses), confirming that the calibration loss is the key differentiator. In the post-calibration ablation (Table 2), L_ALD+Cal^Post outperforms the CSD post-calibration method on 71.4% of calibration comparisons and CiPOT on 55.4%, while the pre-calibrated variant L_ALD+Cal^Pre wins 60.7% of comparisons against uncalibrated ALD with zero losses, validating both calibration strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of survival modeling: the parametric ALD-based approach of Sheng & Henao (2025), which provides smooth full-distribution estimation but suffers from tail mismatch, and the nonparametric censored quantile regression of Pearce et al. (2022, CQRNN), which is flexible but suffers from discretization and quantile crossing. The key conceptual bridge is recognizing that the pinball loss is a special case of the ALD negative log-likelihood (Lemma 1), which motivates a quantile-conditioned adapter module that refines parametric ALD parameters per quantile level. The calibration theory is inherited from Zhao et al. (2020), whose PAIC/MPAIC framework and randomized-forecasting construction are extended here to the censored survival setting with a proof that MPAIC implies PAIC (Theorem 1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Individual calibration in survival analysis has been comparatively underexplored relative to average and group calibration, yet it is critical for patient-level clinical decisions (e.g., eligibility for high-risk interventions). The paper provides the first survival model with formal PAIC guarantees and a practical post-calibration module that could be attached to other survival architectures (RSF, DeepSurv, DeepHit), though the authors note that nonparametric models require additional care due to discrete CDFs. Key open questions include: improving the quantile-regression loss L_Cqr under heavy censoring (where the Portnoy estimator degrades), resolving the asynchronous convergence issue in pre-calibration training, and validating the approach on larger, more diverse clinical benchmarks beyond the 21-dataset suite inherited from prior work.
