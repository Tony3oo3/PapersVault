## Problem

LLM agents trained with online RL struggle with exploration because they rely on exploiting pretrained priors rather than discovering novel states, causing premature convergence to suboptimal policies. Prior memory-augmented methods (e.g., Reflexion) improve short-term performance but saturate quickly since static parameters cannot capture the diversity needed for continuous improvement, and the agent remains dependent on external memory at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263795/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a text-based task description and a sequence of environment observations (e.g., room contents, available objects) and produces a natural-language action at each step. A memory buffer stores up to 1000 reflective tips (single-sentence summaries under 100 words), with retrieval limited to the top-10 tips by cosine similarity (threshold > 0.5) injected into the prompt. At test time, the trained model operates without any memory, producing actions conditioned solely on the task and current observation.

## Method

EMPO² alternates between two rollout modes (memory-augmented prompting with probability p, and plain prompting with 1−p) and two update modes (on-policy with tips retained, and off-policy where tips-conditioned log-probabilities are replaced by no-tips log-probabilities). The off-policy update functions as reward-guided knowledge distillation: the tips-conditioned policy acts as a teacher generating high-quality trajectories, while the no-tips student policy is updated to reproduce those trajectories in proportion to their advantage, thereby internalizing exploration guidance into parameters. A masking mechanism suppresses the advantage term for tokens whose probability under the current policy falls below a threshold δ, preventing the gradient divergence that plagues off-policy training. An intrinsic novelty reward (1/n, where n counts similar past states) further encourages the agent to visit new states and maintain policy entropy. The policy itself generates the reflective tips after each episode, creating a self-improving loop where non-parametric memory updates bootstrap parametric learning.

## Results

On ScienceWorld (19 tasks, 20 unseen test variants each), EMPO² achieves an average return of 75.9, a 128.6% improvement over GRPO (33.2), with seven tasks reaching the maximum score of 100/100. On WebShop, EMPO² attains a mean score of 88.3 and success rate of 76.9%, surpassing the strongest online RL baseline GiGPO (86.2 / 75.2%) and GRPO (79.3 / 66.1%) by 11.3% in score. In out-of-distribution adaptation tests, EMPO² achieves an average 136% improvement over GRPO within 10 memory-augmented trials on novel tasks, with no parameter updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMPO² outperforms all four baselines on both benchmarks. Against the non-parametric baseline Reflexion (Shinn et al., 2023), EMPO² improves the ScienceWorld average from 17.1 to 75.9 and the WebShop score from 58.1 to 88.3, demonstrating that parametric updates internalize what memory alone cannot achieve. Against the offline RL baseline Retrospex (Xiang et al., 2024), EMPO² wins on ScienceWorld (75.9 vs. 33.8) and WebShop (88.3 vs. 73.1), and unlike Retrospex, never underperforms the non-parametric method on any individual task. Against the online RL baseline GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), EMPO² achieves a 128.6% improvement on ScienceWorld (75.9 vs. 33.2) and an 11.3% improvement on WebShop (88.3 vs. 79.3), with the gap most pronounced on exploration-heavy tasks like power-component (94.3 vs. 15.1). Against GiGPO (Feng et al., 2025b) on WebShop, EMPO² edges ahead in both score (88.3 vs. 86.2) and success rate (76.9% vs. 75.2%), confirming that memory-augmented hybrid optimization complements the finer credit assignment that GiGPO provides through observation grouping.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMPO² unifies two previously separate threads in LLM agent research: non-parametric memory-based self-improvement (Reflexion, REMEMBERER) and parametric online RL (GRPO, GiGPO). While Reflexion-style methods store verbal reflections to guide subsequent trials, they saturate because static parameters cannot expand intrinsic knowledge; conversely, pure online RL like GRPO lacks continuity across rollouts and struggles with exploration. The paper's key departure is the off-policy update mode, which reframes tips-conditioned rollouts as teacher demonstrations and distills their advantage-weighted behavior into the base policy, effectively converting non-parametric guidance into parametric knowledge. This hybrid on/off-policy formulation, combined with a novelty-based intrinsic reward borrowed from classical exploration literature, positions EMPO² as a bridge between memory-augmented reasoning and parameter-level optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 128.6% improvement on ScienceWorld and the OOD adaptation result (136% gain in 10 memory-augmented trials with no weight updates) suggest that training agents to *use* memory during RL can yield a model that generalizes to unseen tasks without further fine-tuning—a practically valuable property for deployment. However, the evidence is limited to a single 7B model family and two text-based benchmarks, the memory retrieval is a simple cosine-similarity search, and the off-policy masking mechanism for stability is somewhat ad hoc. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, more complex retrieval (e.g., hierarchical or learned), and domains like mathematics, coding, or multimodal interaction, where exploration dynamics differ substantially from the embodied text environments tested here.
