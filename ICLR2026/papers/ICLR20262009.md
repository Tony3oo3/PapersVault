## Problem

Quasimetric RL (QRL) constrains goal-conditioned value functions to quasimetric mappings but enforces local consistency through discrete transition pairs (s, s'), making it trajectory-dependent and limiting out-of-distribution generalization. In complex, high-dimensional dynamics (e.g., antmaze, humanoidmaze), purely quasimetric value learning degrades significantly because the Lipschitz regularity assumptions underlying the Eikonal/HJB PDE structure are violated. The paper addresses both gaps: eliminating the trajectory dependency and mitigating the regularity violations in complex settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes i.i.d. samples of state-goal pairs (s, g) drawn from the offline dataset's marginal distributions, rather than transition tuples (s, a, s'), making it trajectory-free. The high-level quasimetric model d_θ operates in a lower-dimensional abstract space (e.g., 2D agent coordinates for navigation, or a learned latent embedding for manipulation), while the low-level value function and goal representation network operate in the full state space. Outputs include a quasimetric distance estimate d_θ(s,g) used for advantage computation, and actions from two hierarchical policies (high-level subgoal selection and low-level control) trained via advantage-weighted regression.

## Method

Eik-QRL reformulates QRL's discrete local consistency constraint as the Eikonal PDE condition ‖∇_s d_θ(s,g)‖ = 1, derived by taking the continuous-time limit of the triangle inequality under unit-speed isotropic dynamics. This PINN-style formulation embeds the PDE residual directly into the training loss alongside a global relationship term, requiring only sampled (s,g) pairs and no rollouts. To handle complex dynamics where isotropic assumptions fail, Eik-HiQRL decomposes the problem hierarchically: the high-level module applies Eik-QRL in a low-dimensional abstract space where regularity holds, while the low-level module uses standard TD-based Implicit V-Learning with a goal representation network. Both policies are extracted via advantage-weighted regression, and the quasimetric is parameterized as an Interval Quasimetric Embedding (IQE) to enforce the triangle inequality by construction.

## Results

On the OGbench antmaze suite, Eik-HiQRL achieves 59±7% success on navigate-giant and 47±12% on stitch-giant, while Eik-QRL, HJB-QRL, and QRL all collapse to 0–8% success in the same settings. On pointmaze (where isotropic dynamics hold), all PDE-constrained variants (Eik-QRL, HJB-QRL, Eik-HiQRL) achieve 73–91% success with collision rates of 5–18%, substantially outperforming QRL's 46–60% collision rate. On humanoidmaze, Eik-HiQRL achieves state-of-the-art success with statistically significant gains over the best baseline (t=22, p≈10⁻¹⁴ on giant-stitch). On manipulation tasks (cube, scene), Eik-HiQRL matches or slightly trails HIQL/CRL, reflecting the violation of Lipschitz regularity assumptions in contact-rich dynamics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against QRL (Wang et al., 2023), Eik-HiQRL achieves decisive wins on antmaze: 59% vs 0% success on navigate-giant and 47% vs 0% on stitch-giant, while also reducing collision rates from 52–60% to 28–29%. On pointmaze, Eik-HiQRL matches or slightly trails QRL in success rate but cuts collision rates by roughly half (e.g., 8% vs 46% on navigate-medium). Against HIQL (Park et al., 2024b), Eik-HiQRL achieves SOTA on humanoidmaze with statistically significant margins (t=22, p≈10⁻¹⁴ on giant-stitch) and a narrow edge on antsoccer-arena (61% vs 60%), but trails on cube-single-play (12% vs 31%). Against CRL (Eysenbach et al., 2022), Eik-HiQRL wins clearly on antsoccer (61% vs 24%) and scene (55% vs 35%), but loses on cube (12% vs 32%). Against Eik-HIQL (Giammarino et al., 2025), Eik-HiQRL dominates on antsoccer (61% vs 19%) and humanoidmaze, confirming that the hierarchical quasimetric design outperforms a flat Eikonal-regularized TD approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates Quasimetric RL (QRL, Wang et al. 2023) from a discrete, trajectory-based constraint into a continuous-time PDE constraint derived from the Eikonal equation, making value learning trajectory-free. It then embeds this Eikonal-constrained quasimetric value function into a hierarchical architecture inspired by HIQL (Park et al. 2024b), where the high-level planner operates in a low-dimensional abstract space and the low-level controller uses standard TD-based value learning. The PDE-constrained approach draws on Physics-Informed Neural Networks (Raissi et al. 2019) but departs from prior PDE-informed RL work (Giammarino et al. 2025; Lien et al. 2024) by using the PDE as a structural geometric constraint on the value function rather than an additive regularizer, and by simplifying the full HJB PDE to the tractable Eikonal PDE under isotropic dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trajectory-free property of Eik-QRL has direct practical value in navigation, manipulation, and motion planning where states and goals can be sampled independently of trajectories, and the PDE constraint provides implicit regularization that improves out-of-distribution generalization in large environments. However, the method's reliance on isotropic dynamics (‖a‖≤1) and Lipschitz value functions limits its applicability: in contact-rich manipulation tasks (cube, scene), Eik-HiQRL underperforms TD-based baselines, revealing a fundamental tension between PDE smoothness assumptions and hybrid, mode-switching dynamics. A key open question is how to design PDE-constrained value learning that accommodates discontinuities from contact events and categorical state variables without sacrificing the structural benefits of the Eikonal formulation.
