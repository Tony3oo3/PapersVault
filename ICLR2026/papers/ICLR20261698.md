## Problem

Existing LLM uncertainty quantification methods attach a single confidence number or hedging phrase to one sampled answer, offering no view of the full range of answers the model considers plausible. No benchmark exists to evaluate whether a free-form summary string faithfully represents an LLM's internal distribution over all possible responses, making it impossible to measure or improve this form of uncertainty communication.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261698/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The SelfReflect metric takes a query q, a candidate summary string s produced by any summarization strategy ψ, N=50 i.i.d. sampled answers from the target LLM, and a judge LLM (Qwen 2.5 7B Instruct at temperature τ=5). It produces a scalar 1-Wasserstein distance score, computed by masking each word of M=50 fresh samples B (excluding stopwords) and comparing the judge's conditional word distributions given the summary versus the N samples as context. Scores are averaged over 1000 queries per dataset, with the full benchmark taking ~67 minutes on 8 A100 GPUs.

## Method

The paper formalizes summary quality as predictive sufficiency: an ideal summary S of N sampled answers A^(1:N) satisfies I{A^(1:N); B} = I{S; B}, which is equivalent to p(B|A^(1:N)) = p(B|S) for any subsequent answer B. A key theoretical result (Proposition 3.2) shows this global distributional equality is equivalent to a sequence of masked-token prediction equalities, enabling a tractable Cloze-style metric. In practice, a judge LLM predicts each masked word given either the summary or the N samples as context, and the 1-Wasserstein distance between the two resulting vocabulary distributions is averaged over all word positions and samples. Empirically, the paper shows that no single-decoding strategy—basic prompting, chain-of-thought, reasoning models, SFT, or DPO—enables LLMs to faithfully summarize their own internal distributions; only the 'Sample & Summarize' approach (explicitly sampling multiple outputs and feeding them back for summarization) achieves faithful self-reflective summaries.

## Results

SelfReflect correctly discriminates good from bad summaries in 98.77% of cases, and—critically—good from *almost-good* summaries in 93.20% of cases, where the next-best metric (Embedding) manages only 65.49% and the LM Judge used in prior work (Xu et al., 2024) drops to 47.32%. In the human-alignment study, SelfReflect achieves Krippendorff's α = 0.690 with majority human preference, approaching inter-human agreement (α = 0.723). On the LLM capability side, Sample & Summarize consistently outperforms the Greedy baseline across all 20 tested LLMs (e.g., 85×10⁻³ vs 91×10⁻³ for Qwen 2.5 72B Instruct), while no single-decoding method—Basic prompting, Chain-of-Thought, reasoning models, or SFT/DPO fine-tuning—beats Greedy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelfReflect decisively outperforms all four baselines on the fine-grained discrimination tasks that matter for iterative benchmarking. On good vs almost-good summaries, SelfReflect reaches 93.20% while the LM Judge (Xu et al., 2024) falls to 47.32%, Summarization (Jain et al., 2023) to 39.72%, InfoSumm (Jung et al., 2024) to 60.81%, and Embedding (Wang & Holmes, 2024) to 65.49%—a gap of 28–54 percentage points. The advantage is even more pronounced on verbalized-uncertainty vs majority-only summaries, where SelfReflect scores 85.34% versus 19.37% (LM Judge), 19.90% (Summarization), 15.71% (InfoSumm), and 10.99% (Embedding). On the easier good-vs-bad task, all metrics cluster above 80%, with InfoSumm (99.87%) and LM Judge (98.33%) slightly ahead of SelfReflect (98.77%), confirming that SelfReflect's value lies in resolving subtle quality differences rather than gross errors. In the MMLU multiple-choice setting (Table 2), SelfReflect achieves a perfect rank correlation of 1.00 with the reference Wasserstein distance at the dataset level, matching LM Judge and two of its own ablations, while Embedding degrades to 0.19. In the human-alignment study (Table 3), SelfReflect (α = 0.690) is the only metric that maintains strong agreement with human raters on the nuanced good-vs-greedy (α = 0.489) and good-vs-CoT (α = 0.599) comparisons, where all baselines drop to near-zero or negative alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Cloze-test paradigm from summarization evaluation (BLANC, Vasilyev et al. 2020) and the statistical notion of predictive sufficiency (Lauritzen, 1974) to define a fundamentally new evaluation target: a single string that faithfully summarizes an LLM's entire internal distribution over answers, rather than a single document or a single answer with an attached confidence score. It departs from the dominant uncertainty-quantification literature (logit-based, semantic entropy, verbalized confidence) by treating the full answer distribution as the object of communication, and from prior self-reflective summary work (SaySelf, LogU) by providing a principled information-theoretic metric rather than relying on LM judges that the paper shows cannot discriminate fine-grained quality differences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The resounding negative result—that none of 20+ modern LLMs (instruction-tuned, reasoning, SFT/DPO-finetuned) can faithfully summarize their own internal answer distribution without external sampling—challenges a core assumption about LLM introspection and sets a clear research target for the field. The SelfReflect metric, grounded in predictive sufficiency and validated against human judgements, provides the first principled benchmark for this new paradigm of string-based uncertainty communication. Open questions include whether native self-reflection can be achieved through architectural changes or new training objectives, whether the metric's reliance on a judge LLM limits its applicability, and how faithful subjective-uncertainty summaries translate to objective truthfulness as models scale.
