## Problem

Aligning text-to-audio (TTA) models with human preferences is fundamentally harder than aligning LLMs because the audio domain lacks verifiable rewards, gold-standard answers, or off-the-shelf reward models. Existing approaches rely on static, labor-intensive preference datasets (e.g., BATON, Audio-Alpaca) that limit generalization, and diffusion-based TTA models require many denoising steps, making inference slow. This paper addresses both the preference-data bottleneck and the inference-speed problem simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt and a scalar duration value (up to 30 seconds) as input. It produces 44.1 kHz stereo audio by operating on a fixed-length 30-second latent space, where the duration embedding explicitly controls how much of that space contains actual audio content versus silence. Text is encoded via FLAN-T5 and audio is encoded/decoded by the frozen VAE from Stable Audio Open; inference requires only 50 Euler steps and completes in 3.7 seconds on a single A40 GPU.

## Method

TANGOFLUX uses a hybrid backbone of 6 MMDiT blocks followed by 18 DiT blocks (515M parameters, width 1024) trained with rectified flow matching, which learns straight-line velocity fields from Gaussian noise to the VAE latent space. The key alignment contribution is CLAP-Ranked Preference Optimization (CRPO): at each iteration, the current model generates N audios per prompt, CLAP cosine similarity ranks them, and the best/worst pair forms a preference pair. The optimization loss combines DPO-FM (adapting DPO-Diffusion to rectified flows via the noise-to-velocity equivalence) with an additional flow-matching loss on the winning audio, which prevents the over-optimization pathology where both winning and losing losses increase in tandem. Critically, new preference data is regenerated online at every iteration rather than reusing a static dataset, which the paper shows is necessary to avoid rapid performance saturation and degradation.

## Results

TANGOFLUX (516M params) achieves state-of-the-art performance on AudioCaps with a CLAP score of 0.480, FD_openl3 of 75.1, KAD of 0.60, and Inception Score of 12.2, while generating 30-second 44.1kHz audio in just 3.7 seconds on an A40 GPU using only 50 flow-matching steps. In human evaluation on 50 out-of-distribution prompts, TANGOFLUX attains the highest Elo scores (1,501 OVL, 1,628 REL) and z-scores (0.2486 OVL, 0.6919 REL), substantially outperforming all baselines. The model uses fewer parameters than all competing models (516M vs. 712M–1.25B) and is 5–6× faster at inference than the closest competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TANGOFLUX outperforms all six baselines on the majority of objective metrics. Against Tango 2 (the closest competitor at 866M params), TANGOFLUX achieves a 30.7% lower FD_openl3 (75.1 vs. 108.4), a 56.5% lower KAD (0.60 vs. 1.38), a 7.4% higher CLAP score (0.480 vs. 0.447), and a 35.6% higher IS (12.2 vs. 9.0), while Tango 2 retains a slight edge on KL_passt (1.11 vs. 1.15). Against Stable Audio Open (1056M params), TANGOFLUX improves CLAP score by 65% (0.480 vs. 0.291) and reduces KAD by 85.5% (0.60 vs. 4.15). Against AudioLDM 2-large (712M params), TANGOFLUX reduces FD_openl3 by 30.7% (75.1 vs. 108.3) and improves CLAP score by 14.3% (0.480 vs. 0.419). Against GenAU-Full-L (1.25B params), TANGOFLUX achieves a 19.5% lower FD_openl3 (75.1 vs. 93.2) and a 37.5% lower KAD (0.60 vs. 0.96) with fewer than half the parameters. In human evaluation, TANGOFLUX leads all models on both OVL and REL Elo scores, with a 10.7% margin over Tango 2 on REL (1,628 vs. 1,507) and a 4.3% margin on OVL (1,501 vs. 1,419). Notably, TANGOFLUX achieves all of this with 516M parameters and 3.7s inference time, compared to 22.8s for Tango/Tango 2 and 24.8s for AudioLDM 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

TANGOFLUX sits at the intersection of rectified flow generative modeling (Liu et al., 2022; Lipman et al., 2023), the FLUX-style hybrid MMDiT/DiT transformer architecture (Esser et al., 2024), and preference optimization for generative models (Wallace et al., 2023; Majumder et al., 2024). Its key methodological departure is CRPO, which replaces the static preference datasets used by Tango 2 and BATON with an iterative online loop that uses CLAP (Wu et al., 2023) as a proxy reward model to generate fresh winner-loser pairs at each training iteration. The paper also adapts the DPO-Diffusion loss to rectified flows via the noise-velocity equivalence and augments it with a flow-matching regularization term to counteract the over-optimization pathology observed in DPO training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a simple contrastive audio-text embedding model (CLAP) can serve as a viable proxy reward for iterative preference optimization opens a practical path to aligning audio generative models without expensive human annotation, a bottleneck that has limited prior TTA alignment work. The 515M-parameter model matching or exceeding 866M–1.25B baselines while generating 30 s of 44.1 kHz audio in 3.7 s makes high-fidelity TTA accessible on a single GPU, which is practically significant for creative and production workflows. Open questions include whether CLAP's ranking quality is sufficient for more nuanced or long-form audio (e.g., music with complex temporal structure), whether the iterative self-improvement loop generalizes beyond the AudioCaps-scale prompt distribution, and whether the added flow-matching regularization is the right fix or merely masks a deeper instability in DPO for non-autoregressive generative models.
