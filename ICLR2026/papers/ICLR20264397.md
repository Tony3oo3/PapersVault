## Problem

Vision encoders used in VLMs lack the robust structural and spatial features essential for document understanding and web agent tasks, as they are predominantly trained on natural images. This gap is compounded by the scarcity of high-quality annotated data for documents and web screenshots, where current annotations rely on OCR models that bottleneck scalability and quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264397/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes an RGB image (document page or web screenshot) at 384×384 resolution and produces patch-level visual tokens via a ViT-L16-384 backbone. Because the ensemble training concatenates features from a frozen SigLIP2 generalist encoder with the specialized encoder, the output embedding dimension is doubled relative to either encoder alone, and these tokens are projected into the text embedding space via an MLP projector for consumption by a downstream LLM decoder.

## Method

DAVE uses a two-stage training pipeline. Stage 1 performs self-supervised MAE pretraining on 20M unlabeled document and web images, replacing the standard per-patch-normalized reconstruction loss with raw pixel reconstruction to stabilize training on the low inter-patch variance characteristic of document/web images. Stage 2 conducts supervised autoregressive pretraining on ~2M labeled samples (OCR, layout extraction, web grounding) using multiple LLM decoders (Qwen2.5, Phi-4-mini, Granite). To produce a decoder-agnostic encoder, the paper introduces a distillation-based weight-merging scheme that learns per-weight convex combination coefficients across the separately trained encoders while keeping all encoder parameters frozen. Finally, ensemble training concatenates frozen SigLIP2 features with the specialized encoder's features, encouraging the specialized branch to focus on low-level structural and spatial representations while the generalist branch supplies high-level semantics.

## Results

With Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct as the VLM backbone, DAVE achieves 62.2 on OCRBench, 82.1 on DocVQA, 63.1 on ChartQA, 64.5 on ScreenSpot-V2, and 82.6 on WebSRC, outperforming the strongest baseline SigLIP 2 by an average of 10.5% across 8 document and web benchmarks while maintaining competitive general VQA scores (36.6 MMMU, 55.6 RealWorldQA). On the Mind2Web web agent benchmark, DAVE achieves 30.8/26.1 ElementAcc/StepSR on Cross-Task, surpassing the best baseline Dolphin (24.0/19.6) by 5%. On classic document tasks, DAVE reaches 74.1 mAP on DocLayNet and 56.9 mAP on DocBank, setting new state-of-the-art over both generalist and specialist encoders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAVE consistently outperforms all seven baselines on document and web tasks. Against SigLIP 2 (the strongest generalist baseline), DAVE gains +10.7 on OCRBench (62.2 vs 51.5), +10.0 on DocVQA (82.1 vs 72.1), +11.3 on ChartQA (63.1 vs 51.8), +23.8 on ScreenSpot-V2 (64.5 vs 40.7), and +14.8 on WebSRC (82.6 vs 67.8), while remaining competitive on general VQA (MMMU 36.6 vs 36.9, RealWorldQA 55.6 vs 53.5). Against the strongest specialist encoder Dolphin, DAVE leads on OCRBench (+17.5), DocVQA (+7.3), ChartQA (+2.8), ScreenSpot-V2 (+8.2), and WebSRC (+6.6), but Dolphin retains a slight edge on RICO-SCA classification (88.4 vs 92.8 for DAVE, though SigLIP 2 leads at 93.3). Generalist self-supervised encoders (MAE-Web, DinoV2) and document-specialist encoders (DiT, Pix2Struct) are substantially outperformed across nearly all benchmarks, with DinoV2 and DiT scoring below 15 on OCRBench and below 20 on ScreenSpot-V2. On Mind2Web, DAVE surpasses Dolphin (the best baseline) by 5% average, and notably outperforms SigLIP 2 and AIMv2 by a wide margin (e.g., 30.8 vs 20.6 ElementAcc on Cross-Task), suggesting structural and spatial features are more critical than broad semantic features for web navigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAVE extends the MAE self-supervised pretraining paradigm (He et al., 2022) by modifying the reconstruction loss to handle the low inter-patch variance characteristic of document and web images, and extends the model soup concept (Wortsman et al., 2022) with a distillation-based scheme that learns per-weight merge coefficients to produce a decoder-agnostic encoder. Unlike Eagle and Perception Encoder, which align pretrained generalist encoders to LLMs on large-scale general datasets, DAVE pretrains from scratch on domain-specific data and uses multiple LLM backbones to create a specialized foundation model. The ensemble training strategy, which concatenates features from a frozen generalist encoder (SigLIP2) with the domain-specialized encoder, departs from prior specialist encoders (DiT, Pix2Struct, Dolphin) that rely solely on document-specific representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAVE addresses a concrete and underexplored gap in VLM design: the vision encoder's lack of structural and spatial features for document and web tasks, yielding a consistent 10.5% average improvement over SigLIP2 on document and web benchmarks. The finding that neither purely specialized nor purely generalist encoders suffice—both are needed in combination—provides a clear design principle for future domain-adapted VLMs. Key limitations include the fixed input resolution, dependence on an external pretrained generalist encoder, and the absence of conditioning on prior agentic actions, all of which point toward open questions about multi-resolution processing, self-contained encoders, and action-conditioned visual representations for web agents.
