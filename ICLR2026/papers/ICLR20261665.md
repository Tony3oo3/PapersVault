## Problem

Existing zero-shot video temporal grounding methods are designed for short clips and rely on uniform frame sampling, which fails to capture complex temporal structures and brief salient events in long, untrimmed videos. While large vision-language models excel at semantic understanding, they lack inherent temporal localization capability, and prior training-free approaches do not explicitly model the multi-scale temporal hierarchy (events, scenes, actions) that long videos exhibit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261665/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (subsampled at 5 FPS for short clips like Charades-STA, 1 FPS for longer datasets like TACoS and Ego4D-NLQ) and a natural language query as input. It produces a single predicted temporal segment (start timestamp, end timestamp) by ranking hierarchical candidate segments. The video is consumed through three parallel frozen feature extractors (ViT-B/32, DINOv2-base, RAFT-Large) operating offline, while the query is processed online by VideoCLIP-XL for coarse filtering and Qwen2.5-VL-7B for fine-grained semantic scoring.

## Method

HiTeA introduces a Hierarchical Temporal Decomposition (HTD) module that constructs three complementary similarity curves—event-level (ViT cosine similarity against segment-mean features), scene-level (DINO consecutive-frame similarity), and action-level (negative L2 norm of RAFT optical flow)—and extracts boundaries via local minima or PELT change-point detection at each level. A hierarchical merging function enforces the containment relation (event ⊃ scene ⊃ action) for long videos, producing a multi-granularity candidate set. A two-stage scoring pipeline then applies VideoCLIP for coarse pre-filtering (reducing ~147 candidates to 9 on Ego4D-NLQ) followed by a frozen VLM for fine-grained semantic scoring. Finally, a Candidate Refinement module fuses VLM and VideoCLIP scores (λ=0.99 weighting), performs query-aware progressive merging across hierarchical levels, and selects the top-ranked segment as the final prediction—entirely without task-specific training.

## Results

On the long-video TACoS benchmark, HiTeA achieves 44.94% R@0.1 and 19.79% mIoU, representing an absolute gain of over 33 points in R@0.1 over the best prior truly training-free method (Luo et al., 11.20%). On Ego4D-NLQ, HiTeA attains 8.12% mIoU, surpassing all fully-supervised baselines (best: UniVTG at 4.91%) and becoming the first training-free method evaluated on this dataset. On short-video Charades-STA, HiTeA reaches 69.62% R@0.3 and 46.29% mIoU, outperforming the strongest training-free baseline TFVTG (67.04% R@0.3, 44.51% mIoU) and matching several weakly-supervised methods. On ActivityNet-Captions, HiTeA leads all zero-shot methods with 54.46% R@0.3 and 37.93% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Ego4D-NLQ, HiTeA (mIoU 8.12%) surpasses all fully-supervised baselines—2D-TAN (3.39%), Moment-DETR (3.53%), and UniVTG (4.91%)—and also outperforms the zero-shot Mr.BLIP (5.37%) and TimeSuite (0.94%), though UniTime-Zero (10.18%, which requires training) remains ahead. On TACoS, HiTeA (44.94% R@0.1) dramatically outperforms all truly training-free methods (Luo et al. at 11.20%, Mr.BLIP at 24.59%) but trails the trained zero-shot UniTime-Zero (50.06%) and fully-supervised UniVTG (51.44%). On Charades-STA, HiTeA (69.62% R@0.3, 46.29% mIoU) beats the best training-free TFVTG (67.04% R@0.3, 44.51% mIoU) and matches the weakly-supervised Huang et al. (69.16% R@0.3), though TFVTG edges ahead on R@0.5 (49.97% vs. 48.52%). On ActivityNet-Captions, HiTeA (54.46% R@0.3, 37.93% mIoU) clearly leads all zero-shot baselines including TFVTG (49.34%, 34.10%) and Lu et al. (49.26%, 33.25%). On QVHighlights, HiTeA achieves 62.3% R1@0.5 and 60.7% mAP@0.5 on the test split, surpassing Moment-GPT (58.3%, 55.1%) and all other zero-shot methods. Overall, HiTeA establishes the strongest truly training-free performance across all five benchmarks, with the largest margins on long-video datasets where its hierarchical decomposition provides the greatest structural advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiTeA carves new ground at the intersection of three existing research streams: (1) training-based VTG methods that rely on dense temporal annotations, (2) zero-shot VTG methods that use uniform sampling or pseudo-labels, and (3) VLM-augmented approaches that inject temporal awareness via positional encodings or timestamp markers. Rather than building on any single prior architecture, the paper repurposes off-the-shelf feature extractors (ViT, DINOv2, RAFT) and a change-point detection algorithm (PELT) to construct an explicit hierarchical temporal scaffold, then delegates semantic scoring to a frozen VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) without any fine-tuning. This departs from prior zero-shot methods that lack explicit temporal structure and from VLM-based grounding methods that require architectural modifications to handle time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a fully training-free method surpasses all fully-supervised baselines on Ego4D-NLQ (mIoU 8.12%) is the first such demonstration on this challenging long-video benchmark, suggesting that explicit hierarchical temporal reasoning can substitute for task-specific training in long-video grounding. However, absolute performance on long videos remains low, and the framework's reliance on five pre-trained models (three for offline feature extraction, two for online scoring) introduces practical deployment complexity. Open questions include whether the fixed three-level hierarchy (event-scene-action) is optimal across domains, whether dynamic decomposition depth could further improve results, and whether the approach generalizes to other video understanding tasks like dense captioning or summarization.
