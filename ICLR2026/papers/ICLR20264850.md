## Problem

Standard LLM pruning methods calibrate on input/prompt activations, but reasoning models are decode-dominated: the chain-of-thought tokens generated during inference vastly outnumber the prompt tokens. As a result, pruning with prompt-only calibration causes severe accuracy loss on reasoning tasks and paradoxically slows inference, as pruned models produce longer, more rambling CoT traces while answering less accurately.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264850/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense reasoning LLM (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill or Qwen3 variants), a set of calibration prompts from domain-specific datasets (OpenR1-Math-220K or CodeForces problem statements), and a target sparsity level (20–50%). During calibration, the dense model self-generates up to 8,192 on-policy CoT tokens per prompt, and the hidden states from both prompt and decode phases are collected. The output is a one-shot pruned model with no retraining or distillation, executable on a single H100 GPU.

## Method

RAC (Reasoning-Aware Compression) modifies the calibration procedure of layer-wise pruning by augmenting prompt-only activations with on-policy decode-time activations. Specifically, during calibration the dense model autoregressively generates CoT tokens, and at each decode step the resulting hidden states are appended to the layer-wise activation matrix, so the reconstruction objective (e.g., SparseGPT's layer-wise L2 loss) minimizes error over the full prompt-plus-decode distribution that the model encounters at inference. This aligns the pruning-time reconstruction target with the actual inference-time activation distribution, which is critical because in reasoning tasks |c|+|y| >> |x|. The approach is a drop-in modification to existing pruning pipelines (SparseGPT, WANDA, ALPS) requiring no architectural changes, retraining, or separate distillation stage, and is analogous to on-policy distillation in reinforcement learning.

## Results

On MATH-500 (acc@1:1), RAC-pruned DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B at 50% sparsity achieves 0.900 accuracy versus 0.744 (C4) and 0.812 (Prompt-Only), while reducing evaluation time from 135.0 min (C4) to 35.3 min. On Qwen3-8B at 50% sparsity, RAC reaches 0.862 accuracy (vs. 0.564 C4, 0.470 Prompt-Only) with runtime dropping from 258.8 min to 17.1 min. On the harder AIME-25 benchmark, RAC preserves 0.500 accuracy for Qwen3-8B at 50% sparsity where C4 collapses to 0.033. On LiveCodeBench (pass@1:16), RAC-pruned 7B at 50% sparsity achieves 0.283 versus 0.099 (C4) and 0.228 (Prompt-Only).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between three calibration strategies applied to the same SparseGPT pruning algorithm: generic C4, domain-specific prompt-only, and RAC (prompt + on-policy CoT). RAC consistently outperforms both baselines across all model sizes and sparsity levels, with the largest gaps at 50% sparsity (e.g., +15.6 pp over C4 and +8.8 pp over Prompt-Only on DeepSeek-R1-7B MATH-500). RAC also dramatically reduces runtime by preventing the pruned model from generating excessively long, degenerate CoT traces. When RAC is applied to alternative pruning algorithms, it provides gains agnostic to the method: ALPS+RAC achieves 0.788 on Qwen3-1.7B at 50% sparsity (vs. 0.566 for ALPS+Prompt-Only), and WANDA+RAC reaches 0.878 on Qwen3-8B at 50% (vs. 0.814 for WANDA+Prompt-Only). The paper notes that ALPS is itself a competitive (sometimes stronger) pruning baseline compared to SparseGPT, but RAC's calibration benefit is additive on top of whichever algorithm is used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the layer-wise one-shot pruning framework established by SparseGPT, WANDA, and ALPS, which solve a per-layer reconstruction problem under sparsity constraints. Its key departure is in the calibration distribution: standard methods calibrate on prompt-only activations (C4 or task prompts), but the authors argue that reasoning models are decode-dominated (|CoT| >> |prompt|), so the calibration set should include on-policy chain-of-thought activations generated by the dense model itself. This positions RAC as a drop-in modification to the calibration pipeline rather than a new pruning algorithm, and contrasts with concurrent work (Zhang et al., 2025b) that documents the accuracy/runtime degradation of pruning reasoning models without proposing a fix, and with self-calibration (Williams et al., 2025) which uses model-generated text but for pre-training distribution rather than task-specific reasoning traces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard pruning makes reasoning models both less accurate AND slower (due to longer, degenerate CoT traces) is practically critical for anyone deploying compressed reasoning models at scale, and the proposed fix is simple enough to adopt immediately in existing SparseGPT/WANDA/ALPS pipelines. The approach is validated across three model families (DeepSeek-R1 Qwen/Llama, Qwen3) and three benchmarks (MATH500, AIME-25, LiveCodeBench), lending confidence to the generalizability. Open questions include whether the calibration cost of generating long CoT traces becomes prohibitive for very large models, whether the method extends to other decode-heavy settings (e.g., multi-turn dialogue, agentic workflows), and whether explicit optimization for output length (rather than just accuracy) could further eliminate the residual runtime penalty at high token budgets.
