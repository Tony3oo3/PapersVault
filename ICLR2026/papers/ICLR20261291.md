## Problem

Existing 4D video reconstruction methods produce disjoint per-frame point clouds and rely on auxiliary estimators (monocular depth, optical flow, 2D tracking) or costly per-scene optimization to establish cross-frame correspondences, making them brittle and hard to generalize. There is no unified representation that directly encodes the continuous 3D motion of every pixel across the full temporal extent in a single, self-contained pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261291/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts up to 60 RGB frames (512 px longest side) as a video, an image pair with a 5–20 frame gap, or an unordered set of photos with no temporal metadata. For each input frame it outputs a dense stack of D=10 control-point maps (D×H×W×3) in a shared world coordinate system, which together define a cubic B-spline trajectory for every pixel spanning the entire sequence. From these trajectories one can directly derive per-frame 3D point maps, projected 2D trajectories, dynamic/static masks, scene flow between any two frames, and camera poses—without any external estimator.

## Method

Trace Anything is a feed-forward network built on the Fast3R geometric backbone (image encoder + fusion transformer with interleaved frame-wise and global attention layers, plus temporal index embeddings for sequential input). A control point head regresses dense D×H×W×3 control-point maps per frame, parameterizing each pixel's 3D trajectory as a cubic B-spline with clamped, non-uniform knots (10 control points by default). Training is fully supervised: a confidence-weighted L2 trajectory loss enforces that a trajectory predicted from frame i, evaluated at frame j's timestamp, matches the ground-truth 3D position, complemented by static-degeneracy, rigidity, and cross-frame correspondence regularizers. The entire video is processed in a single forward pass—no pairwise inference, no iterative global alignment, no depth or flow priors—yielding all trajectories jointly in one shared world frame. The model is trained on 20K Kubric videos plus 10K+ videos from a custom Blender-based 4D scene data platform featuring articulated characters, non-rigid motion, and diverse camera trajectories.

## Results

On the Trace Anything video-based benchmark (30-frame clips), Trace Anything achieves the best EPE_mix of 0.234, EPE_sta of 0.218, and EPE_dyn of 0.295, along with the lowest CA (5.09×10⁻²) and SDD (1.06×10⁻³), and the highest APD_3D (34.5) and AJ (20.6). On the image-pair benchmark, it further reduces EPE_mix to 0.135 with a runtime of just 0.20 s. Inference takes 2.3 s for 30 frames, over an order of magnitude faster than the next-best method (St4RTrack* at 21.7 s) and roughly 100× faster than DELTA (231.6 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Trace Anything outperforms all 13 baselines on every reported metric in the video-based evaluation (Table 1). Against the closest competitor St4RTrack* (fine-tuned on the same data), it reduces EPE_mix by 11% (0.234 vs 0.264), EPE_dyn by 17% (0.295 vs 0.355), and SDD by 34% (1.06 vs 1.60), while running ~9× faster (2.3 s vs 21.7 s). Against optimization-heavy methods like DELTA (231.6 s) and CoTracker3+VGGT (197.4 s), the speedup exceeds 80×. On the image-pair benchmark (Table 2), Trace Anything again leads all methods, achieving EPE_mix of 0.135 versus 0.175 for POMATO* and 0.203 for St4RTrack*, with a 0.20 s runtime that is 7–25× faster than baselines. On TAPVid-3D 3D tracking (Table E), it surpasses SpaTracker on APD_3D for ADT (20.5 vs 18.3) and PStudio (16.3 vs 16.2) while running ~90× faster (2.1 s vs 191.1 s), though SpaTracker retains a slight edge on AJ for ADT (17.4 vs 15.6). On KITTI scene flow (Table B), it achieves the best EPE (0.375 short-range, 0.372 long-range) and accuracy (57.4%, 58.9%) over VGGT, MonST3R, and MASt3R.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Fast3R's feed-forward geometric backbone (image encoder + fusion transformer with interleaved frame-wise and global attention) but departs fundamentally from the DUSt3R/MonST3R/POMATO/St4RTrack lineage of 4D reconstruction, which produces disjoint per-frame point clouds and relies on optical flow or 2D tracks for cross-frame correspondences. It also diverges from sparse 3D point trackers (CoTracker3, DELTA, SpaTrackerV2) by predicting dense, per-pixel, all-to-all trajectories in a single forward pass rather than tracking a limited set of query points. The key conceptual contribution is the trajectory field formulation itself: representing each pixel in each frame as a cubic B-spline parametric curve in a shared world coordinate system, which subsumes point maps, scene flow, 2D tracks, and camera poses as derived quantities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The all-to-all evaluation protocol (every pixel in every frame must produce a complete trajectory) exposes a gap that first-to-all point tracking benchmarks mask, and the new 10K-video dataset and 200-video benchmark provide the first large-scale training and evaluation infrastructure for this denser task. The dramatic efficiency gain (~2.3s vs. 17–231s for baselines) makes dense 4D trajectory estimation practical for real-time or interactive applications, and the representation naturally enables downstream tasks like goal-conditioned manipulation, motion extrapolation, and spatio-temporal fusion without additional predictors. Open questions include bridging the synthetic-to-real domain gap, handling highly complex or repetitive motions that exceed the expressiveness of 10-control-point B-splines, and whether the approach scales gracefully to very long sequences.
