## Problem

Existing Sheaf Hypergraph Networks (SHNs) model only undirected hyperedges, leaving asymmetric and causal multi-way interactions (e.g., chemical reactions, metabolic pathways) unaddressed. Moreover, the Sheaf Hypergraph Laplacian proposed by Duta et al. (2023) fails to satisfy positive semidefiniteness—a fundamental spectral property required for a well-defined convolutional operator—contrary to the original authors' claims. No principled sheaf-based framework existed that could handle directed hypergraphs while preserving the spectral guarantees needed for stable spectral convolutions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a directed hypergraph where each hyperedge is partitioned into a tail set (sources) and a head set (targets), along with a node feature matrix and optionally hyperedge features (computed by mean/sum aggregation if absent). Internally, all representations are complex-valued; a tunable charge parameter q ∈ ℝ controls how much directional information enters the imaginary part of the Laplacian (q=0 recovers the undirected case). Before the classification head, complex node embeddings are converted to real-valued vectors via an 'unwind' operation that concatenates real and imaginary components, yielding a 2f-dimensional real feature per node for node classification (or hyperedge classification in the molecular reaction experiments).

## Method

DSHN introduces Directed Hypergraph Cellular Sheaves, which assign to each node–hyperedge incidence a complex-valued restriction map F⃗_{u≺e} = S^{(q)}_{u≺e} · F_{u≺e}, where the directionless real map F_{u≺e} is learned by an MLP of node and hyperedge features, and the scalar S^{(q)}_{u≺e} equals 1 for head nodes and e^{−2πiq} for tail nodes. From these maps, the paper derives the Directed Sheaf Hypergraph Laplacian L^F = D_V − B^{(q)†} D_E^{−1} B^{(q)}, a complex-valued Hermitian matrix proven to be positive semidefinite with eigenvalues in [0,1], and shown to unify the classical graph Laplacian, the Sheaf Laplacian, the Magnetic Laplacian, the Sign-Magnetic Laplacian, the Zhou et al. hypergraph Laplacian, and the Generalized Directed Laplacian as special cases. The convolutional layer applies the normalized Laplacian L_N^F as a diffusion operator (X_{t+1} = σ(Q_N^F (I⊗W₁) X_t W₂)), and a lightweight variant DSHNLight detaches gradients during Laplacian assembly to reduce FLOPs by ~30% while maintaining or improving accuracy. The charge parameter q is selected per dataset via hyperparameter search, allowing the model to flexibly weight directional information from fully ignored (q=0, homophilic settings) to maximally encoded (q=1/4, 'net flow' regime).

## Results

On 7 real-world directed hypergraph node classification datasets, DSHN and DSHNLight achieve relative accuracy gains of 2%–20% over the best of 13 baselines, with the largest improvements on email-EU (82.67% vs. 68.91% for ED-HNN) and email-Enron (76.76% vs. 63.78% for AllSetTransformer). On synthetic directional benchmarks, DSHNLight reaches 99.04% accuracy at Io=50, outperforming the strongest directed baseline DHGNN(w/emb.) (81.12%) by 18 percentage points. On two molecular reaction hyperedge classification datasets, DSHNLight achieves 82.32% F1 on Molecular-1 (vs. 80.72% for GeDi-HNN) and DSHN achieves 89.09% F1 on Molecular-2 (vs. 87.89% for AllSetTransformer). On the highly homophilic Cora dataset, DSHNLight (88.02%) is on par with the best baseline PhenomNN (88.12%), consistent with the charge parameter q being tuned to 0 where directionality acts as noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSHN/DSHNLight outperform all 13 baselines on 6 of 7 real-world datasets, with the most dramatic wins over undirected methods: on email-EU, DSHNLight (82.67%) beats the best undirected baseline ED-HNN (68.91%) by 13.8 points, and on email-Enron it beats AllSetTransformer (63.78%) by 13 points. Against the strongest directed baselines, DSHN achieves 88.65% on Telegram vs. DHGNN(w/emb.) at 80.58% (+8.1 pts), and DSHNLight reaches 89.24% on Roman-empire vs. GeDi-HNN at 83.87% (+5.4 pts). On the synthetic directional benchmarks, DSHN/DSHNLight dominate all methods by 10–18 percentage points, confirming that the sheaf-based directional encoding captures structure that simpler directed Laplacians (GeDi-HNN, DHGNN) miss. The only dataset where DSHN does not clearly win is Cora (highly homophilic), where DSHNLight (88.02%) is essentially tied with PhenomNN (88.12%) and the optimal charge parameter q is set to 0, indicating directionality is uninformative there. SheafHyperGNN, the closest undirected sheaf-based competitor, is consistently outperformed by 5–20 points, validating that the directional extension is the key differentiator rather than the sheaf structure alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: (1) Sheaf Hypergraph Networks (Duta et al., 2023), which extend cellular sheaves to undirected hypergraphs to address heterophily and oversmoothing, and (2) complex-valued spectral operators for directed graphs (Magnetic Laplacian of Zhang et al., 2021; Sign-Magnetic Laplacian of Fiorini et al., 2023; Generalized Directed Laplacian of Fiorini et al., 2024), which encode edge orientation via a complex phase. The key departure is introducing a tunable charge parameter q that controls the strength of directional encoding within a sheaf-theoretic framework, and identifying that Duta et al.'s Sheaf Hypergraph Laplacian fails to be positive semidefinite for general (non-2-uniform) hypergraphs—a theoretical gap the authors correct with their (1 − 1/δₑ) diagonal factor. The resulting Directed Sheaf Hypergraph Laplacian is shown to recover the Sheaf Laplacian, Magnetic Laplacian, Sign-Magnetic Laplacian, Zhou et al.'s hypergraph Laplacian, and the Generalized Directed Laplacian as special cases, providing a genuinely unified operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first sheaf-based method capable of handling directed hypergraphs, filling a gap where prior directed hypergraph methods (GeDi-HNN, DHGNN) lacked the expressiveness of sheaves and prior sheaf methods (SheafHyperGNN) could not model directionality. The identification of a spectral flaw in a NeurIPS 2023 Laplacian is a meaningful theoretical correction for the hypergraph-learning community. However, practical adoption is limited by OOM failures on larger datasets (Roman-empire, Amazon-ratings) for the full DSHN model, and the charge parameter q requires per-dataset tuning rather than being learned end-to-end. Open questions include scalability to natively directed hypergraph datasets (e.g., protein-protein interaction networks) and whether making q layer-wise learnable could eliminate the need for manual tuning.
