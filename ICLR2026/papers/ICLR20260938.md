## Problem

Stepwise group-based RL methods (e.g., GRPO, GiGPO) for long-horizon agentic tasks estimate relative advantages by grouping steps that share the same current state, but these steps often differ in their historical contexts within the memory module. This 'historical context inconsistency' introduces significant bias in advantage estimation, degrading policy optimization and generalization. The paper empirically demonstrates this bias and shows that the ideal 'Oracle' steps (sharing both state and full history) are too scarce to use alone, creating a bias-variance dilemma.

## Input / Output

The LLM agent receives a text task description, the current environment observation, and a memory module containing the K most recent historical state-action pairs (K << T total turns), formatted via a prompt template with chain-of-thought and action tags. It outputs a textual action (token sequence) at each step, and the training procedure produces an updated policy. Rewards are sparse and delayed, provided only at the final step of each trajectory, with stepwise rewards computed via discounted returns. The entire grouping and advantage computation operates offline over existing rollouts using only hashmap lookups, requiring no additional models or data collection.

## Method

HGPO introduces two components: (1) context-aware hierarchical grouping, where steps sharing the same current state are further partitioned into nested groups G_0 ⊇ G_1 ⊇ ... ⊇ G_K based on how many preceding historical states they share (via a k-step context operator), and (2) adaptive weighting advantage estimation, where each hierarchical group computes its own group-relative advantage and these are aggregated with weights w_k ∝ (k+1)^α that up-weight higher-level groups with more consistent contexts. The key insight is a bias-variance tradeoff: higher-level groups (more shared history) yield lower-bias but higher-variance estimates due to smaller group sizes, while lower-level groups are the reverse; the adaptive weighting interpolates between the step-level (k=0) and Oracle (k=K) estimators. A formal proposition bounds the resulting bias and variance between these extremes. The policy is updated with a standard clipped PPO objective using the aggregated hierarchical advantage, with a KL penalty against a reference policy.

## Results

On ALFWorld with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, HGPO (K=4) achieves 95.96% in-distribution success and 94.87% out-of-distribution success, outperforming GiGPO (K=4) by 2.67% and 3.34% respectively. On WebShop, HGPO (K=4) reaches 79.29% task success rate, surpassing GiGPO (K=4) by 6.05%. With Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, HGPO (K=4) achieves 95.96% ALFWorld in-distribution success and 79.29% WebShop task success, maintaining its lead over all baselines. HGPO introduces less than 0.001% additional time cost and negligible memory overhead compared to GRPO and GiGPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HGPO consistently outperforms all RL baselines across both benchmarks and both model sizes. Against the closest competitor GiGPO, HGPO (K=2) gains +2.61% ALFWorld in-distribution success and +5.01% WebShop task success on Qwen2.5-1.5B, while HGPO (K=4) gains +2.67% and +6.05% respectively. Against GRPO, the gap is much larger: HGPO (K=4) exceeds GRPO by 23.16% on ALFWorld in-distribution success (95.96% vs 72.8%) and 22.49% on WebShop task success (79.29% vs 56.8%) for the 1.5B model. PPO with a critic and RLOO are substantially weaker, with PPO achieving only 54.4% ALFWorld success and RLOO 69.7% for the 1.5B model. On out-of-distribution ALFWorld tasks, HGPO shows notably less degradation than GiGPO (e.g., 94.87% vs 91.53% for K=4, 1.5B), suggesting that the hierarchical grouping mechanism improves generalization. Against closed-source models, HGPO (K=4, 1.5B) surpasses GPT-4o (48.0% ALFWorld) and approaches Gemini-2.5-Pro (60.3% ALFWorld, 42.5% WebShop score), though Gemini-2.5-Pro remains competitive on WebShop task scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stepwise group-based RL framework introduced by GiGPO (Feng et al., 2025b), which itself adapted GRPO's group-relative advantage estimation to multi-turn agentic settings. The key departure is identifying 'historical context inconsistency'—where steps sharing the same current state may have different memory histories—as a source of systematic bias in stepwise advantage estimation. HGPO addresses this by constructing a hierarchy of groups at increasing context depths and adaptively weighting their advantages, rather than relying on a single step-level grouping as in GiGPO. The work sits within the broader trajectory of moving from trajectory-wise to stepwise policy optimization to avoid context explosion in long-horizon agent training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of context inconsistency as a concrete, measurable source of advantage bias in stepwise RL for agents is a useful diagnostic that prior methods (GRPO, GiGPO) did not explicitly address. The solution is practically attractive: it requires no extra rollouts, no value network, and adds less than 0.001% to training time. However, the evaluation is limited to two text-based benchmarks (ALFWorld, WebShop) and two model sizes, and the hierarchical grouping assumes raw, divisible historical contexts—making it inapplicable to agents that summarize memory. Open questions include extending the framework to summarized/embodied memory, developing uncertainty-aware adaptive weighting, and validating on more diverse or longer-horizon environments.
