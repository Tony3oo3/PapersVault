## Problem

Existing MetaBBO methods require extensive handcrafted training task distributions to learn meta-strategies that generalize to target tasks, a dependency that is often infeasible in realistic applications where the target task is unique or data-scarce. This paper asks whether an optimizer can adapt its own evolutionary operators online using only the optimization data generated during the search, thereby eliminating the need for any predefined task distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262880/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a black-box objective function f_T(x) accessible only through scalar evaluations, along with search space bounds and dimension d, and produces an optimal solution vector in R^d. Internally, it maintains a population of N candidate solutions (initialized via Latin hypercube sampling) and a set of learnable parameters θ defining attention-based selection, crossover, and mutation operators. The parameters are updated each generation via AdamW gradient descent on a self-supervised loss, so the 'output' also includes the evolved operator weights that encode task-specific search behavior.

## Method

ABOM parameterizes the three classical evolutionary operators as differentiable modules: selection computes a dual-path attention matrix over both solution-space proximity and fitness ranking; crossover applies an attention-weighted recombination followed by a tanh-activated MLP with dropout; and mutation uses a gene-wise attention matrix to model inter-dimensional dependencies before an MLP perturbation. The key architectural insight is a closed-loop adaptive mechanism: at each generation, the parameters θ are updated by minimizing the squared distance between the generated offspring population and an elite archive of the top-N solutions, effectively performing self-supervised learning from the optimizer's own search trajectory. This eliminates the decoupled meta-training phase entirely, enabling zero-shot optimization. The paper further proves global convergence (almost sure) under compactness and continuity assumptions, showing that dropout-enabled exploration combined with elitism guarantees a positive drift toward the global optimum at every iteration.

## Results

On the BBOB suite (d=500, 16 test functions), ABOM achieves the best or near-best objective value on 12 of 16 functions, with the Wilcoxon rank-sum test confirming significant superiority over all 11 baselines on at least 11 functions each. Notable wins include f6 (6.20e+3 vs. CMAES 2.16e+4, a 3.5× improvement) and f22 (4.97e+0 vs. CMAES 2.85e+1, a 5.7× improvement). On the 28 UAV path-planning problems, ABOM converges fastest under a 2,500-evaluation budget and achieves the lowest average normalized cost. On the Bipedal Walker task (d=874), ABOM converges significantly faster than the zero-shot pre-trained model EPOM, which exhibits premature convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against traditional BBO methods (RS, PSO, DE), ABOM dominates on 14–15 of 16 functions, with margins ranging from 2× (f14: 1.49e+1 vs. CMAES 2.49e+1) to over 1000× (f6: 6.20e+3 vs. RS 1.53e+7). Among adaptive variants, CMAES is the closest competitor, winning only f23 (3.96e−1 vs. ABOM 1.66e+0), while JDE21 and SAHLPSO are outperformed on 14 of 16 functions. Among MetaBBO methods, GLHF is the strongest rival, beating ABOM on 4 functions (f9, f11, f19, f23) where its pretrained solution-manipulation network excels on highly structured or low-conditioning landscapes; however, ABOM surpasses GLHF by orders of magnitude on high-conditioning functions (f12: 2.73e+7 vs. 9.76e+9; f20: 2.57e+2 vs. 3.75e+4). On the 28 UAV path-planning problems, ABOM achieves the lowest average normalized cost and fastest convergence, while also running significantly faster in wall-clock time due to GPU-parallelized neural operators. On the Bipedal Walker task (d=874), ABOM outperforms the zero-shot pre-trained model EPOM, which suffers from premature convergence, confirming ABOM's advantage in high-dimensional continuous optimization without any task-specific pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

ABOM carves new ground at the intersection of evolutionary computation and online self-supervised learning. It departs from the dominant MetaBBO paradigm (GLEET, RLDEAFL, LES, GLHF), which requires offline meta-training on a handcrafted task distribution F before deployment, by instead parameterizing evolutionary operators (selection, crossover, mutation) as differentiable attention-based modules and updating them online via gradient descent against an elite archive. Conceptually, it repurposes Transformer-style attention to model individual-fitness and gene-gene interactions, and borrows the self-supervised learning paradigm (predicting next-generation populations) from recent zero-shot BBO work (GLHF, EPOM), but eliminates the pretraining phase entirely. The convergence analysis draws on martingale convergence and drift analysis techniques from the evolutionary computation theory literature (He & Yao, Zhou et al.), applied here to a neural-network-parameterized operator with dropout-enabled exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The task-free formulation addresses a genuine deployment bottleneck: in practice, the target task is often unique or data-scarce, making handcrafted training distributions inaccessible. The almost-sure global convergence guarantee for a neural-parameterized evolutionary operator with online adaptation is a theoretical contribution that prior neural BBO methods (GLHF, B2Opt) lack. However, the O(d³) computational bottleneck from the mutation attention matrix limits scalability to very high dimensions, no convergence rate is established, and the proof requires the optimum to lie in the interior of the search space. Open questions include whether sparse/low-rank attention can reduce complexity, whether hybrid pretraining + online adaptation (explored preliminarily via ABOM-PT on STOP) can bridge task-agnostic and cross-task generalization, and whether the approach extends to constrained or mixed-integer BBO.
