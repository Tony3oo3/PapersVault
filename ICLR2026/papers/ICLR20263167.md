## Problem

Existing activation editing methods for LVLM hallucination mitigation construct steering vectors by degrading visual inputs (e.g., adding noise or perturbations) but neglect the positive guidance available from factual textual semantics, failing to explicitly model visual-textual associations. Additionally, they apply an identical averaged steering vector to all queries, ignoring that different queries emphasize distinct objects with different visual-textual disparities, limiting their ability to precisely mitigate query-specific language bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263167/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard image-question pair and produces a text answer from the LVLM, with no change to the input/output interface. The key operational detail is that editing is applied sparsely to the top-K=64 attention heads (ranked by steering vector magnitude) in middle and deep layers, adding a scaled steering vector plus a query-adaptive offset to the self-attention outputs z^{l,k}. The offline setup requires 500 COCO images with ground-truth annotations for vector construction and a lightweight MLP trained for ~0.08 GPU-hours for the offset estimator.

## Method

AFTER comprises two components. Factual-Augmented Activation Steering (FAS) textualizes COCO ground-truth annotations into category, attribute (color via pixel statistics, shape via contour geometry, count via label frequency), and relation (spatial direction and IoU) facts, then uses an LVLM to synthesize a coherent factual description t+. It constructs trusted-untrusted activation pairs by contrasting the factual text's internal activations with the original image's activations, averaging the differences into a general visual-textual steering vector. Query-Adaptive Offset Optimization (QAO) then trains a per-head single-layer MLP (two linear layers + GELU) with MSE loss to predict a query-specific offset from the query-focused activation, capturing the deviation between the query-specific disparity vector and the general vector. At inference, the final editing vector is the sum of the general steering vector and the QAO-predicted offset, applied with strength α=7 to the top-K heads, adaptively steering activations toward factual textual semantics while preserving query-relevant visual grounding.

## Results

On POPE (averaged across COCO, A-OKVQA, GQA and random/popular/adversarial settings), AFTER achieves 85.7% ACC and 85.6% F1 on LLaVA-v1.5, surpassing the baseline (80.1/82.3) by 5.6/3.3 points and the strongest editing baseline ICT (83.7/83.7) by 2.0/1.9 points. On the AMBER generative benchmark, AFTER reduces the Hal metric from 31.6 to 20.5 on LLaVA-v1.5 (a 35% relative reduction) and achieves a 16.3-point absolute reduction on Shikra (49.5→33.2), the largest improvement among all compared methods. On MME's hallucination subtasks (existence, count, position, color), AFTER yields score gains of 45.0, 46.6, and 73.4 over the vanilla LVLMs on LLaVA-v1.5, InstructBLIP, and Shikra respectively, while also improving general visual capabilities by an average of 130.7 points across the three models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFTER outperforms all seven compared baselines across every benchmark and all three LVLMs. Against the closest activation-editing competitor ICT (CVPR 2025), AFTER gains +2.0% POPE ACC and +1.9% F1 on LLaVA-v1.5 (85.7 vs. 83.7), and reduces AMBER Hal by 6.1 points (20.5 vs. 26.6). Against VTI (ICLR 2025), the improvement is larger: +2.5% ACC and a 3.2-point Hal reduction on LLaVA-v1.5. Among decoding-based methods, AFTER surpasses OPERA by +2.4% ACC and VCD by +3.2% ACC on LLaVA-v1.5, while also achieving the lowest AMBER CHAIR (4.5 vs. 4.9 for OPERA). The training-based HACL method, despite 12.6 GPU-hours of fine-tuning, is outperformed by AFTER (which requires only 0.08 GPU-hours for its lightweight MLP estimator) on both POPE and AMBER. In the appendix (Table 10), AFTER also beats the more recent VISTA (ICML 2025) and Octopus (CVPR 2025) methods on LLaVA-v1.5, and extends its advantage to the newer Qwen2.5-VL-8B-Instruct model (87.4% ACC vs. 83.7% baseline, Hal 24.0 vs. 27.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-editing paradigm for LVLM hallucination mitigation, building directly on the inference-time intervention framework (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024) and the trusted/untrusted activation contrast construction used by VTI (Liu et al., ICLR 2025) and ICT (Chen et al., CVPR 2025). The key conceptual departure is replacing prior methods' strategy of degrading visual inputs (perturbations, noise, blur) to define 'untrusted' semantics with a positive factual-textual guidance signal: ground-truth annotations are textualized into category, attribute, and relation facts that serve as the trusted steering direction. Additionally, the paper introduces query-adaptive offset optimization (QAO), a lightweight MLP estimator that moves beyond the single general steering vector used by all prior editing methods to produce query-specific corrections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AFTER offers a practically attractive solution: it requires no model retraining, consumes only 0.92 GPU-hours end-to-end (vs. 13.21 for the training-based HACL), and generalizes across models (LLaVA-v1.5, InstructBLIP, Shikra, Qwen2.5-VL) and distributions (COCO→GQA, discriminative→generative) with strong results even from as few as 5 training samples. The main limitation is its dependence on accessible internal activations, restricting applicability to open-source LVLMs, and the need for domain-specific factual augmentation in specialized settings like medical imaging. An open question is whether the factual-textual steering direction can be constructed without relying on ground-truth annotations, which would be necessary for truly open-world deployment.
