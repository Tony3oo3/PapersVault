## Problem

Prior low-rank error reconstruction methods for LLM quantization (e.g., L2QER) rely on two sequential low-rank factors, requiring on-the-fly quantization of intermediate values and suffering severe accuracy degradation under W4A4 settings. No existing method achieves pure 4-bit matrix multiplication in linear layers via low-rank error reconstruction without mixed-precision paths or costly calibration procedures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261229/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes full-precision LLM weights and 128 calibration text samples (WikiText-2 or Pile) to determine per-channel activation saliency. It produces 4-bit quantized weights (INT4 or MXFP4, group size 128), a single r×d low-rank compensation matrix R (r=128, quantized to 4-bit), and offline-merged row/column permutations folded into adjacent layer weights. Unlike two-factor LoRA approaches, the output is a single narrow matrix per linear layer, and all permutation and scaling operations are preprocessed offline with zero runtime overhead.

## Method

SERQ performs error reconstruction in three stages: (1) static activation flattening via per-channel scaling (SmoothQuant-style) folded into weights offline, (2) saliency-aware error reconstruction that directly extracts the top-r salient weight rows (ranked by activation scale, motivated by AWQ) and computes a single residual matrix R = W_s − Q(W_s) of shape r×d, and (3) offline weight permutation where row and column reorderings are merged into adjacent layers so activations naturally arrive in the correct order at inference. The key architectural difference from prior SVD-based methods is replacing the two-factor decomposition L1·L2 (requiring two sequential multiplications and intermediate quantization) with a single matrix R, so the residual path is one multiplication X_s·Q(R) in pure 4-bit. This eliminates on-the-fly quantization, enables fully INT4/MXFP4 GEMM kernels, and reduces per-layer latency overhead to ~18.7% versus ~19.8% for rotation-based methods while achieving higher accuracy.

## Results

Under W4A4 quantization, SERQ(GPTQ) achieves PPL 5.97, 0-shot accuracy 61.87%, and MMLU 37.03% on LLaMA-2-7B, substantially outperforming L2QER (PPL 7.37, 0-shot 57.67%, MMLU 29.63%) and LLM.int4() (PPL 632). On LLaMA-3-8B under W4A4, SERQ(GPTQ) reaches PPL 7.75 and MMLU 53.8%, compared to L2QER's PPL 11.44 and MMLU 38.33%. Against rotation-based SOTA methods, SERQ achieves PPL 5.97 vs. SpinQuant's 6.0 and QuaRot's 6.15 on LLaMA-2-7B, while requiring only ~23 minutes of calibration versus ~598 minutes for SpinQuant. SERQ also delivers up to 4.5× lower latency overhead than L2QER's two-matrix LoRA path on Blackwell GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SERQ decisively outperforms LLM.int4() under W4A4, where the latter's PPL explodes to hundreds or thousands (e.g., 632 on LLaMA-2-7B, 1700 on LLaMA-3.2-1B) while SERQ maintains PPL below 13. Against L2QER, the primary error-reconstruction baseline, SERQ consistently wins on all metrics under W4A4 (e.g., PPL 5.97 vs. 7.37 and MMLU 37.03 vs. 29.63 on LLaMA-2-7B; PPL 7.75 vs. 11.44 on LLaMA-3-8B), with the gap widening on smaller models where L2QER's two-matrix path degrades severely. Versus rotation-based methods, SERQ beats QuaRot (PPL 5.97 vs. 6.15, MMLU 37.03 vs. 33.58) and SpinQuant (PPL 5.97 vs. 6.0, MMLU 37.03 vs. 34.8) on LLaMA-2-7B, and the advantage is larger on LLaMA-3-8B (PPL 7.75 vs. 8.26/8.41, MMLU 53.8 vs. 49.93/47.29). SERQ also outperforms SmoothQuant (PPL 5.97 vs. 7.49 with g128) and DuQuant (MMLU 37.03 vs. 33.46). However, FlatQuant and OSTQuant achieve marginally lower PPL (5.75 and 5.91 vs. 5.97) at the cost of 5–6× longer calibration times (131 min and 72 min vs. 23 min for SERQ), making SERQ the best accuracy-to-calibration-cost trade-off among training-free methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SERQ sits at the intersection of three prior threads: SmoothQuant's static per-channel activation scaling, AWQ's insight that a small fraction of salient channels dominates quantization error, and L2QER's SVD-based low-rank error reconstruction. The key conceptual departure is replacing L2QER's two sequential low-rank factors (L1, L2) with a single matrix R that directly stores the quantization error of the top-r salient weight rows, thereby eliminating the intermediate on-the-fly quantization step that plagues two-factor decompositions under W4A4. Rather than performing a global truncated SVD and distributing the rank budget across all rows, SERQ restricts reconstruction to rows identified by activation-scale saliency, a strategy validated by QSNR analysis showing superior output-error reduction at equal rank.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SERQ is the first method to realize pure 4-bit matrix multiplication in linear layers via low-rank error reconstruction, which is practically significant for deploying LLMs on hardware with native INT4/MXFP4 GEMM support (e.g., NVIDIA Blackwell). The single-matrix design reduces inference overhead by up to 4.5× compared to two-factor LoRA paths and avoids the ~20% latency cost of online Hadamard rotations, making it attractive for edge and server deployment. Open questions include whether the approach extends to sub-4-bit (2-bit) settings, how it interacts with KV-cache quantization, and whether the saliency-based row selection generalizes to non-LLaMA architectures (e.g., MoE, multimodal). The method still requires a small calibration set and adds ~1–3% parameters, which may matter for the most memory-constrained edge scenarios.
