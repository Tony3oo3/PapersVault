## Problem

Current motion-conditioned video diffusion models operate offline with bidirectional attention, requiring the entire motion trajectory to be known in advance and taking minutes per clip (e.g., 12 minutes for a 5-second video). This non-causal, slow, and short-duration paradigm fundamentally prevents real-time interactive video generation where users can paint trajectories, drag objects, or steer cameras and see results unfold on-the-fly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260262/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single input image, a text prompt, and 2D motion trajectories (extracted from a 50×50 uniform grid via CoTracker3) as conditioning signals, and produces a streaming video at 832×480 (Wan 2.1) or 1280×704 (Wan 2.2) resolution. Unlike offline methods that require the full trajectory upfront, MotionStream consumes trajectories chunk-by-chunk in an autoregressive manner, allowing users to update or release motion controls mid-generation. The output is a potentially infinite-length video stream generated at 17–29 FPS with sub-second latency on a single H100 GPU.

## Method

MotionStream first trains a bidirectional teacher on Wan DiT using lightweight sinusoidal track embeddings with a learnable head (avoiding ControlNet-style duplication) and a joint text-motion classifier-free guidance that balances trajectory adherence with natural dynamics. It then distills this teacher into a causal few-step student via Self Forcing-style distribution matching distillation (DMD), where the joint guidance is embedded directly into the DMD objective so the student replicates multi-term guidance in a single function evaluation. The key architectural innovation is a sliding-window causal attention with attention sinks: initial-frame tokens are preserved as fixed anchors while a rolling KV cache maintains a constant-size local window, and this exact mechanism is simulated during training via self-rollout to eliminate the train-test gap for infinite-length extrapolation. A custom Tiny VAE decoder (trained with adversarial and LPIPS losses) further reduces decoding time by over 10×, enabling the full pipeline to reach 29.5 FPS at 480P.

## Results

On the DAVIS validation set (480P), the causal distilled model (Wan 2.1-1.3B) achieves PSNR 16.20, SSIM 0.447, LPIPS 0.443, and EPE 7.80 at 16.7 FPS, while the bidirectional teacher reaches PSNR 16.61, SSIM 0.477, LPIPS 0.427, and EPE 5.35 at 0.79 FPS. On the Sora Demo Subset, the causal model scores PSNR 16.67, SSIM 0.531, LPIPS 0.360, EPE 4.21. With the Tiny VAE optimization, throughput reaches 29.5 FPS at 480P and 23.9 FPS at 720P with sub-0.5s latency on a single H100 GPU. On the LLFF novel view synthesis benchmark, the causal model achieves PSNR 15.7, SSIM 0.38, LPIPS 0.23, outperforming all 3D view synthesis baselines by a large margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionStream's causal distilled model (1.3B, 480P) outperforms all four motion-transfer baselines on every metric: it beats ATI (Wan 2.1-14B, the strongest baseline) by +0.87 PSNR, +0.073 SSIM, −0.030 LPIPS, and −9.61 EPE on DAVIS, while running 72× faster (16.7 vs. 0.23 FPS). Against Go-With-The-Flow and Diffusion-As-Shader (both CogVideoX-5B), the causal model gains +0.58 to +0.60 PSNR and reduces EPE from ~40 to 7.8 on DAVIS. Image Conductor (AnimateDiff, 256P) is far behind on all metrics (PSNR 11.30 vs. 16.20, EPE 91.64 vs. 7.80). On the LLFF novel view synthesis task, the causal model (PSNR 15.7, LPIPS 0.23) surpasses SEVA (14.1, 0.29), ViewCrafter (14.0, 0.30), and DepthSplat (13.9, 0.30) by 1.6–1.8 PSNR and 0.06–0.07 LPIPS, while being 20–80× faster. Notably, the 1.3B causal model outperforms the 14B ATI model on trajectory accuracy (EPE 7.80 vs. 17.41) despite being 10× smaller, demonstrating that the joint text-motion guidance and efficient track conditioning are more effective than raw model scale for motion following.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: motion-conditioned video diffusion (MotionPrompting, Go-With-The-Flow, ATI) and causal distillation of bidirectional diffusion models (Self Forcing, CausVid, DMD). It departs from prior motion-controlled methods by replacing their offline, bidirectional attention with a causal autoregressive student distilled via Self Forcing-style DMD, and from prior causal video models by introducing attention sinks and rolling KV caches (adapted from StreamingLLM in NLP) to prevent drift during long-horizon extrapolation. The joint text-motion guidance is 'baked' into the distillation objective so the student achieves teacher-level control fidelity in a single forward pass, and a lightweight sinusoidal track head replaces ControlNet-style architectures to minimize overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MotionStream is the first system to enable truly interactive, infinite-length motion-controlled video generation at ~29 FPS on a single GPU, unlocking applications (real-time drag, camera control, motion transfer) that were previously locked behind minutes-long offline rendering. The attention-sink-plus-rolling-KV-cache mechanism is a practical, general recipe for stable long-video autoregressive generation that other causal video models can adopt. Key open questions include handling complete scene transitions (the fixed sink anchors to the initial frame), scaling to larger backbones for complex scenes, and whether dynamic/adaptive sinking strategies can resolve the tension between stability and scene-change flexibility.
