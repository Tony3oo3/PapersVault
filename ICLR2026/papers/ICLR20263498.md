## Problem

Machine olfaction with deployable sensors lacks standardized, large-scale benchmarks for training and evaluating models on temporal smell signals. Prior work relied on small datasets, hand-engineered features, simple classifiers, or non-portable lab equipment (e.g., GC-MS), leaving a gap for real-time, portable sensor-based smell recognition at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263498/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-channel MOX gas sensor time series (6 channels at 1 Hz for BASE, 4 channels at 10 Hz for MIXTURE), optionally transformed via first-order temporal differencing (lag p=25) and segmented into overlapping sliding windows (w=50 or 100, stride w/2). For BASE, it outputs a 50-way substance class label; for MIXTURE, it outputs a 12-dimensional probability vector over base odorants representing volumetric proportions. GC-MS binned mass-spectral embeddings (40–500 m/z, 1 Da bins) from FooDB serve as ingredient-level chemistry priors during training only, with no GC-MS measurement required at inference.

## Method

SCENTFORMER is a pre-norm Transformer encoder (L=4 layers, H=8 heads, FFN width 4D) over windowed sensor sequences, augmented with sinusoidal positional encodings and masked mean pooling. The key preprocessing insight is first-order temporal differencing (Δx_t = x_t − x_{t-p}), which exposes odor response dynamics that raw MOX signals obscure due to drift and baseline shifts, yielding an average 16.1% accuracy gain. Training combines three objectives: supervised 50-way cross-entropy classification, symmetric contrastive alignment between sensor-window embeddings and ingredient-level GC-MS embeddings (cosine similarity with temperature), and a composite mixture-prediction loss blending KL divergence, hinge-ℓ1 penalty on present components, and focal BCE for presence/absence. Sliding-window augmentation with 50% overlap increases effective sample size, and all training data are aggregated for standardization statistics applied uniformly to train and test sets.

## Results

On SMELLNET-BASE (50-way substance classification), SCENTFORMER with temporal differencing (p=25), window size 100, and GC-MS cross-modal supervision achieves 63.3% Top-1 accuracy and 61.7% macro-F1, outperforming the best sensor-only result of 56.1% (same architecture, no GC-MS). Temporal differencing (p=25) yields an average 16.1% accuracy gain over raw signals across all models. On SMELLNET-MIXTURE distribution prediction, SCENTFORMER (w=50) achieves 50.2% Top-1@0.1 and 87.9% Top-K on the test-seen split, and 16.0% Top-1@0.1 on the test-unseen split, consistently outperforming MLP, CNN, and LSTM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCENTFORMER is compared against MLP (non-temporal), CNN, and LSTM baselines under identical preprocessing and training protocols. On sensor-only classification (w=100, p=25), LSTM slightly edges out SCENTFORMER (57.9% vs. 56.1% Acc@1), but SCENTFORMER pulls ahead decisively with GC-MS cross-modal supervision (63.3% vs. 56.9% for LSTM, 58.9% for CNN, 30.8% for MLP). The gap widens further at w=50, p=25 where SCENTFORMER matches LSTM sensor-only (50.6%) but surpasses it with cross-modal training (52.6% vs. 50.6%). On mixture distribution prediction (test-seen, w=50), SCENTFORMER achieves the best Top-1@0.1 (50.2%) and Top-K (87.9%), beating CNN (48.1%/86.7%), LSTM (46.4%/89.3%), and MLP (44.0%/85.0%). On the harder test-unseen split, SCENTFORMER again leads (16.0% Top-1@0.1) but all models degrade sharply, with LSTM (11.8%), CNN (12.4%), and MLP (11.7%) trailing by 3–4 points, indicating limited compositional generalization across all architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior machine-olfaction work in two key ways: it replaces large, non-portable GC-MS instrumentation with low-cost portable MOX gas sensors, and it replaces hand-engineered features with a Transformer-based temporal model (SCENTFORMER) trained on large-scale sensor time series. The cross-modal alignment objective is adapted from CLIP-style symmetric contrastive learning (Radford et al., 2021), repurposed to align sensor embeddings with precomputed GC-MS mass-spectral fingerprints from FooDB rather than image-text pairs. Prior sensor-based e-nose datasets (coffee, beef, gas drift) are small and domain-specific; SMELLNET extends the scope to 50 natural food substances plus 43 controlled mixtures, and introduces a mixture-composition prediction task that has no direct precedent in the e-nose literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SMELLNET provides the first large-scale, open, portable-sensor benchmark for machine olfaction, enabling real-time smell classification and mixture composition prediction without lab-grade equipment. The sharp performance drop from 50.2% to 16.0% Top-1@0.1 on unseen mixtures highlights that compositional generalization remains an open problem, and the authors note that controlled-environment collection limits out-of-domain robustness. Future work on better sensor designs, compositional training strategies, and cross-environment data collection will be needed before these systems are deployable in healthcare, food safety, or environmental monitoring settings.
