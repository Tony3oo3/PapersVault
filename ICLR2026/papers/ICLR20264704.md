## Problem

Existing LLM multi-agent frameworks (AutoGen, CAMEL, MetaGPT) support only inference without training, while RL frameworks (OpenRLHF, TRL, veRL) support only single-agent policy optimization. This gap prevents multi-agent systems from learning collaborative behaviors through reinforcement learning, and untrained multi-agent workflows often underperform simple majority voting. The paper asks whether integrating RL directly into multi-agent inference can raise the reasoning ceiling beyond what single-agent RL achieves under the same compute budget.

## Input / Output

MARTI takes a mathematical problem prompt and a workflow specification (e.g., MAD 2×2, MoA 3×1, CoA) as input, and produces a structured trajectory containing per-agent inputs, outputs, and shaped rewards, along with a final answer. For reasoning models, agents exchange only final answers (stripping intermediate chain-of-thought) to control context length. The workflow interface is fully asynchronous and supports dynamic injection of custom interaction logic, with vLLM engines handling generation. Training outputs are per-agent policy models updated via distributed RL, with the same backbone architecture shared across agents.

## Method

MARTI implements a centralized-interaction, distributed-training architecture with three modules: a Multi-Agent World that executes prompt-driven rollouts under configurable workflows (MAD, MoA, CoA, custom), a Centralized Reward Model layer that performs credit assignment and reward shaping, and distributed Agent Policy Trainers running REINFORCE++, GRPO, or PPO from OpenRLHF. The key reward mechanism is an inference-aware shaping strategy (adapted from MAPoRL) that compares each agent's current-turn correctness against its own historical performance, offering Quality Mode (consistency alignment) and Margin Mode (relative improvement) to stabilize multi-turn optimization. Multi-turn asynchronous rollouts decouple generation latency from training throughput, and the framework supports both rule-based verifiable rewards (for math) and LLM-based generative reward models (for open-domain tasks). The central finding is that after convergence, multi-agent RL consistently reaches a higher accuracy ceiling than single-agent RL at matched inference budget, with MAD 2×2 on DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview scoring 66.7 on AIME versus 53.5 for the single-agent baseline.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-3B, MARTI's multi-agent debate (MAD 2×2) with GRPO achieves an average score of 46.0 across AIME, AMC, and MATH-500, compared to 37.9 for single-agent GRPO and 37.6 for single-agent REINFORCE++. For the reasoning model DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview, MAD with TTRL reaches 66.7 on AIME, surpassing the single-agent baseline of 53.5 and OpenAI-o1-mini under the same inference budget. On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, MAD 2×2 with MARTI RL achieves 32.1 average, outperforming single-agent RL (28.7) and majority-vote RL (30.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARTI's multi-agent RL consistently outperforms all single-agent baselines under the same inference budget. On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, MAD 2×2 + MARTI RL (32.1 avg) beats single-agent RL (28.7) by 3.4 points and majority-vote RL (30.0) by 2.1 points. On Qwen2.5-3B, MAD 2×2 + GRPO (46.0 avg) surpasses single-agent GRPO (37.9) by 8.1 points, demonstrating that the multi-agent formulation itself—rather than the specific policy-gradient algorithm—drives the gains. For the reasoning model DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview, MAD with TTRL achieves 66.7 on AIME, significantly outperforming OpenAI-o1-mini and the single-agent TTRL baseline (53.5) at the same compute cost. Ablations confirm that reward shaping is critical: removing it drops MAD performance from 45.6 to 36.6 on Qwen2.5-3B, indicating that purely outcome-based rewards are insufficient for stable multi-turn multi-agent optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARTI bridges two previously disjoint research threads: LLM-based multi-agent inference frameworks (CAMEL, AutoGen, MetaGPT) that support dynamic agent interactions but lack any training capability, and single-agent RL frameworks (OpenRLHF, veRL, TRL) that train LLM reasoning policies but cannot handle multi-agent topologies. It builds directly on OpenRLHF as its distributed training backbone and adapts the inference-aware reward shaping strategy from MAPoRL (Park et al., 2025) to the multi-agent LLM setting, extending it with modular Quality/Margin modes and configurable historical evaluation scopes. The key departure from prior work is the unification of centralized multi-agent interaction (with asynchronous multi-turn rollouts) and distributed per-agent policy training within a single open-source system, which no prior framework supported simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MARTI provides the first open-source infrastructure that lets researchers train multi-agent LLM systems end-to-end with RL, removing the inference-training gap that has limited the field to prompt-engineering-only MAS. The demonstration that multi-agent RL on a 1.5B model (DeepScaleR) reaches ~65 on AIME—competitive with OpenAI o1-mini—suggests that structured collaboration learned via RL can partially compensate for model scale, a direction with broad implications for efficient reasoning. However, the evidence is confined to small models (≤3B) and competition math, and the authors themselves flag that reward modeling for MAS and real-world generalization remain open; scaling to larger models, open-domain tasks, and more diverse agent roles is the critical next step.
