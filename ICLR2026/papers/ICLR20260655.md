## Problem

Existing diffusion-based audio inpainting methods operate in continuous domains (raw waveforms, spectrograms, or CQT representations) and exhibit degraded performance when the missing region is large, losing semantic coherence and temporal consistency. No prior work has applied discrete diffusion to tokenized music representations, leaving open whether a categorical token space can better capture long-range musical structure during inpainting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw audio waveform containing one or more silent gaps (150–750 ms) and produces a complete inpainted waveform. During processing, the waveform is tokenized by a pre-trained WavTokenizer into a sequence of 300 discrete tokens (~4 s) from a ~4k vocabulary; only the masked region is replaced in the final output, with a 10 ms crossfade at boundaries to avoid artifacts. The 24 kHz bandwidth of the tokenizer constrains both input and output resolution.

## Method

AIDD formulates audio inpainting as a discrete sequence completion problem: WavTokenizer quantizes audio into compact token sequences, and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with rotary positional encoding learns to predict masked tokens via an absorbing discrete diffusion process trained with the DWDSE objective. Two training innovations distinguish the approach: (1) span-based masking, where contiguous spans of tokens are masked with geometrically-distributed lengths that grow with the noise level, enabling a progression from fine-grained local corruption to broader semantic perturbations; and (2) a derivative-based regularization loss that aligns first- or second-order temporal differences of predicted and ground-truth token embeddings at masked positions, penalizing irregular local fluctuations and encouraging smooth reconstructions. At inference, the model iteratively denoises the token sequence over 1024 reverse steps, and the reconstructed tokens are decoded back to waveform by the WavTokenizer decoder.

## Results

On MusicNet, AIDD achieves the best ODG (PEMO-Q) across all gap lengths (−3.215 to −3.284) and the best LSD at every gap size (0.162–0.297). For FAD, AIDD is best at 200 ms (2.391), 250 ms (2.438), and 300 ms (3.549), with a ~25% reduction over CQT-Diff+ at 300 ms (3.549 vs. 4.652). On MAESTRO, AIDD achieves the best ODG (PEA-Q) at 375 ms (−2.303) and the highest MOS (3.64 vs. 3.51 for GACELA and CQT-Diff+). The model is ~90 M parameters (vs. 242 M for CQT-Diff+) and trains in ~1 day vs. 4 days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIDD clearly outperforms the two classical baselines (LPC and A-SPAIN-L) on every metric and gap length, with FAD reductions of 40–80% at 300 ms. Against the strongest diffusion baseline CQT-Diff+, AIDD wins on ODG and LSD at all four gap sizes and on FAD at 200–300 ms, but CQT-Diff+ retains a slight FAD edge at 150 ms (1.525 vs. 1.866). On MAESTRO, AIDD surpasses GACELA (−2.303 vs. −3.232 ODG at 375 ms) and both bin2bin variants (−2.892, −2.800) by a wide margin, and also achieves the highest MOS (3.64 vs. 3.51 for GACELA and CQT-Diff+). Notably, AIDD is the only method tested that does not require a fixed input audio length, and it trains in roughly one-quarter the time of CQT-Diff+ with half the parameter count.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIDD extends the discrete diffusion framework of Lou et al. (2024) — originally developed for NLP — into the audio domain by operating on WavTokenizer's compact token sequences rather than raw waveforms or spectrograms. It departs from the dominant continuous-diffusion audio inpainting line (DiffWave, MAID, CQT-Diff+) by formulating inpainting as a categorical sequence-completion problem, and introduces two task-specific training modifications: span-based absorbing masking that structures the forward corruption, and a derivative-based regularization loss that enforces temporal smoothness in embedding space. The DiT backbone with rotary positional encoding follows standard practice from the vision and language discrete-diffusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that discrete diffusion over a pre-trained audio tokenizer can match or exceed continuous-diffusion inpainting baselines up to 750 ms gaps, while being roughly half the parameter count and a quarter of the training time of CQT-Diff+. The span-based masking and derivative loss are lightweight, general-purpose training strategies that could transfer to other token-based generative tasks. Key limitations remain: the 24 kHz bandwidth ceiling inherited from WavTokenizer, the tokenizer-dependent quality upper bound, and the unresolved question of whether discrete or continuous diffusion is fundamentally better for audio. The paper also highlights the need for a unified benchmark spanning token, latent, and continuous representations to enable fair cross-paradigm comparison.
