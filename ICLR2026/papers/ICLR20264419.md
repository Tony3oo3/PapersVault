## Problem

Average sensitivity, the standard simplicity metric for Transformers drawn from Boolean function analysis, has two critical limitations: it lacks a natural extension to the real-valued domains of modern deep learning, and its theoretical bounds (via Friedgut's junta theorem) are far too loose to explain the extreme 'junta-like' input dependence empirically observed in LLMs, where only 5–10 of 256 tokens carry meaningful influence. The paper asks whether a more principled simplicity measure can both generalize to continuous spaces and yield tighter spectral concentration guarantees.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework operates on Gaussian inputs X ∈ ℝ^{n×d} and produces a scalar noise stability score Stab_ρ(f) = E[f(X)f(Y)] where Y = ρX + Z√(1−ρ²). The practical regularizer consumes a token sequence X ∈ [U]^N, generates a correlated noisy copy Y by independently replacing each token with a uniform random vocabulary token with probability (1−ρ)/2, and outputs the dot product of the model's two output distributions (a single scalar per training step). The regularized loss adds γ·R_{M,S,ρ}(X) to the standard cross-entropy, requiring only one extra forward pass per iteration.

## Method

The paper introduces noise stability under the Gaussian measure as a simplicity metric, connecting it to the Hermite-Fourier spectrum via Stab_ρ(f) = Σ_α ρ^{|α|} f̃(α)², which yields a spectral concentration lemma: high stability forces Fourier mass onto low-degree terms with a bound T ≥ log_{1/ρ}(1−δ/ε). For single-layer analysis, the ReLU MLP stability is the arc-cosine kernel (1/2π)(√(1−ρ²)+ρ(π−arccos ρ)), while attention with W_Q W_K^T = I preserves stability linearly (ρ·‖(W_V)_{:,j}‖²), and unstructured attention introduces an additional permutation-consistency factor s(ρ). Multi-layer propagation is analyzed via a proxy recurrence showing weak dampening for MLPs (converging to 2/3π) but full dampening for Transformers when γ<1. The practical contribution is a differentiable, data-dependent regularizer R_{M,S,ρ}(X) = (−1)^S · Σ_i M(X)_i·M(Y)_i that, when added to the training loss, catalyzes grokking by ~35% on modular addition and sparse parity and accelerates next-token-prediction convergence by ~75% on WikiText-2.

## Results

Noise stability regularization accelerates grokking on modular addition by ~36% (reducing iterations from ~4500 to ~3300) and on noisy k-sparse parity by ~35%. On next-token-prediction over WikiText-2-v1, regularized training reaches high validation accuracy in ~75% fewer iterations compared to the non-regularized baseline. Additionally, noise stability yields tighter spectral concentration bounds than average sensitivity across all four evaluated models (e.g., GPT-2 tail mass bound drops from 0.003 to 0.0005; ROBERTA from 0.19 to 0.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between noise stability and average sensitivity as simplicity metrics. Noise stability provides substantially tighter spectral concentration bounds across all four models (GPT-2, BERT, ROBERTA, GEMMA), with the largest improvement on ROBERTA (0.19 → 0.02, a ~10× tightening). For the regularization experiments, the baseline is the same Transformer architecture trained without the noise stability regularizer; the regularized variant consistently wins, achieving 35–36% faster grokking on synthetic tasks and 75% fewer iterations on WikiText-2. The paper does not compare against other regularization methods (e.g., the noise-stability fine-tuning approach of Hua et al., 2023), noting only that its motivation, scope, and noise definition differ fundamentally. No head-to-head comparison with competing simplicity metrics beyond average sensitivity is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes noise stability from Boolean function analysis (O'Donnell, 2021) as a simplicity metric for Transformers, departing from the average sensitivity framework used by Bhattamishra et al. (2022), Hahn (2020), and Vasudeva et al. (2024). The key conceptual move is leveraging the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup and Hermite polynomial basis to obtain a natural real-valued extension, replacing the one-by-one coordinate perturbations of sensitivity with correlated noise on all inputs simultaneously. The multi-layer propagation analysis is directly inspired by Li & Mossel's (2025) inductive sensitivity bounds for hierarchical functions, while the paper explicitly draws a parallel to the signal propagation literature (Schoenholz et al., 2016; Noci et al., 2022), noting that noise stability is a 'more parsimonious analogue to signal correlation.' The regularizer differs from Hua et al. (2023) in motivation, scope, and the definition of correlated noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a concrete theoretical bridge between signal propagation (a training-stability field) and simplicity bias/interpretability, suggesting these communities can inform each other. The noise stability regularizer's ability to catalyze grokking (~35% on synthetic tasks, ~75% on WikiText-2 NTP) is practically appealing, though the evidence is limited to small decoder-only Transformers on a single language dataset. Key open questions include: whether the interval propagation bounds are tight in practice, why multi-layer Transformers exhibit full dampening (unlike MLPs' weak dampening), and whether the regularizer scales to LLM-scale pretraining. The theoretical analysis also relies on simplifying assumptions (ignoring distributional shifts, specific weight structures) that limit its direct applicability to real architectures.
