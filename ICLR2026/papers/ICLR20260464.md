## Problem

Existing auto-formalization methods target only theorem statements, while automated proof synthesis assumes the theorem is already formalized in Lean. In practice, translating an entire NL theorem+proof into Lean 4 still requires manual theorem formalization before proof synthesis, as seen in AlphaProof's 2024 IMO pipeline. This paper tackles the underexplored task of end-to-end proof auto-formalization, producing both the FL theorem and its complete proof from NL input without human intervention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260464/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes an NL theorem+proof pair (statement and proof as free-form text) and produces a Lean 4 theorem+proof pair where the proof is a tactic-style DAG of proof states. The output must satisfy two verifier-grounded constraints: type correctness (passes Lean's type checker with no `sorry` placeholders) and semantic correctness (the generated theorem is bi-directionally equivalent to the gold-standard Lean theorem, confirmed by a Lean proof of the biconditional under a restricted tactic set including rfl, simp, and ring).

## Method

PROOFBRIDGE's core is a joint embedding model trained via symmetric contrastive learning to align NL and FL theorem+proof pairs in a shared 512-dimensional space. The FL encoder linearizes Lean proof DAGs into ordered sequences of proof-state transformations and encodes them with LeanDojo's ByT5 proof-state encoder (218M params), while the NL encoder uses all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (22.7M params); both are projected into the shared space via trainable linear layers, yielding 3.28× Recall@1 over the baseline encoder for cross-modal retrieval. The LLM (Kimina-Prover-RL-1.7B) is fine-tuned with retrieval-augmented SFT, conditioning generation on top-5 retrieved FL proofs and their cosine-similarity relevance scores. At inference, an iterative repair loop (up to 5 rounds) combines Lean type-checker error messages with LLM-based semantic equivalence verification to refine the generated theorem+proof until both checks pass.

## Results

On MINIF2F-TEST-PF (244 instances), PROOFBRIDGE (Retrieval-augmented SFT + Repair) achieves 62.70% Semantic Correctness (SC) and 95.49% Type Correctness (TC) at pass@32, improving +31.14% SC and +1.64% TC over the Kimina-Prover-RL-1.7B random few-shot baseline. For cross-modal NL→FL retrieval, the joint embedding model achieves 52.83% Recall@1 and 0.650 MRR, a 3.28× improvement over the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 baseline (16.06% Recall@1, 0.237 MRR) and outperforming the much larger Qwen3-Embedding-8B (46.75% Recall@1, 0.567 MRR) and E5-Mistral-7B-Instruct (35.60% Recall@1, 0.441 MRR). The mean Median Gap (mMG) between retrieved and non-retrieved cosine similarities reaches 0.65, versus 0.29 for Qwen3 and 0.10 for E5-Mistral, indicating far sharper separation of relevant from irrelevant proofs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PROOFBRIDGE (Retrieval-augmented SFT + Repair) substantially outperforms all 13 baselines on both SC and TC at pass@32. Against the strongest AFPS baseline, Kimina-Prover-72B (zero-shot: 43.03% SC, 79.51% TC), PROOFBRIDGE gains +19.67% SC and +15.98% TC despite using a 42× smaller base model (1.7B vs 72B). Foundation models (GPT-5-mini: 9.02% SC; Gemini-2.5-Pro: 8.61% SC) and other AFPS LLMs (DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL: 12.30% SC; Leanabell-Prover-V2-KM: 18.03% SC) are far behind, confirming that general-purpose models struggle with Lean's strict syntax and semantics. The SoTA Two-Step pipeline (Herald-Translator→Kimina-Prover-Distill-8B: 43.44% SC, 59.43% TC) suffers from cascading errors, yielding 19.26% lower SC and 36.06% lower TC than PROOFBRIDGE. For cross-modal retrieval, PROOFBRIDGE's joint embedding (22.7M + 218M + 1M params) beats Qwen3-Embedding-8B (8B params) by 3.28× on Recall@1 and 2.74× on MRR, and E5-Mistral-7B-Instruct (7B params) by 3.28× on Recall@1, while being 32× smaller than Qwen3. The ablation shows retrieval-augmented SFT contributes +22.55% SC over plain SFT, and iterative repair adds a further +7.37% SC and +5.74% TC.

## Relation to Prior Work

PROOFBRIDGE sits at the intersection of auto-formalization (NL→FL translation) and automated formal proof synthesis (AFPS), two lines of work that have been treated separately: prior auto-formalizers (Herald-Translator, Kimina-Autoformalizer) handle only theorem statements, while AFPS models (DeepSeek-Prover, Kimina-Prover) assume an FL theorem as input. The paper builds on Kimina-Prover-RL-1.7B as the generation backbone and LeanDojo's ByT5 proof-state encoder for the FL side, but departs from both by introducing a contrastively trained joint embedding that encodes linearized DAG traversals of Lean proofs—something neither base model does. Unlike prior retrieval-augmented math tools (LeanSearch, HERALD, REALProver) that use plain-text encoders for theorem retrieval, PROOFBRIDGE is the first to apply DAG-aware cross-modal retrieval of full theorem+proof pairs to guide auto-formalization via fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck highlighted by AlphaProof's 2024 IMO performance: even state-of-the-art pipelines still require humans to manually formalize problem statements before automated proof synthesis can begin. The introduction of the SC metric (bi-directional equivalence proving in Lean) is a methodological contribution that moves the field beyond unreliable proxies like BLEU or type-checking alone, and the 38.9k-pair NUMINAMATH-LEAN-PF dataset plus MINIF2F-TEST-PF benchmark fill a gap in Lean 4 proof auto-formalization resources. Limitations include reliance on an LLM judge (Gemini-2.5-Pro) for the semantic equivalence check, evaluation restricted to competition-level problems in a single Lean version, and the fact that the 3.28× retrieval gain, while large, is demonstrated on one dataset. Open questions include whether the DAG-aware encoding generalizes to larger proof libraries (full Mathlib), whether the SC metric scales to more complex equivalences, and whether the framework extends to other proof assistants.
