## Problem

Unified Multimodal Large Language Models (U-MLLMs) that jointly perform understanding and generation lack a standardized evaluation framework: existing studies use inconsistent benchmarks and incompatible metrics (e.g., CLIP score vs. FID vs. GPT-4 scoring), making cross-model comparison unreliable. More critically, no benchmark exists for the most distinctive U-MLLM capability—mixed-modality generation where understanding and generation must synergize (e.g., drawing auxiliary lines to solve geometry, or generating step-by-step maze navigation states).

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes heterogeneous inputs spanning single/multi-image VQA, video clips (sampled as 6 keyframes for models without video support), text prompts, source images with editing instructions, image pairs, geometry diagrams, and iterative maze configurations. All understanding tasks are reformulated into 4-option multiple-choice questions with shuffled answer positions, while generation and unify tasks require the model to produce an image or video that is then scored via CLIP-I similarity against a set of candidate reference images (one correct, several manually crafted negatives). The final output is a single MME-U score (0–100) computed as the average of understanding accuracy, normalized generation score, and unify accuracy.

## Method

MME-Unify organizes evaluation into three domains with 15 subtasks totaling ~4,100 samples. For understanding (1,900 MCQs from MME, MMBench, MMBench-Video, Video-MME, MME-RealWorld), accuracy is the sole metric. For generation (6 subtasks: FIR, TIE, TIG, CIVG, TVG, VP), domain-specific metrics (FID, FVD, CLIP-I, CLIP-T, LPIPS) are standardized to a (0,100) scale and averaged. The key technical contribution is the unify evaluation strategy: each of the 5 subtasks (IEE, CSQ, AL, SD, VCoT) requires the model to answer a text MCQ AND generate an image, with the generated image scored by CLIP-I similarity against 4 candidate images (correct answer + 3 negatives); the candidate with highest similarity is treated as the model's implicit choice. Two accuracy metrics are reported per subtask—acc (average of text and image accuracy) and acc+ (joint correctness). The authors validate this CLIP-I multiple-choice protocol against human ratings (Kendall's τ_b ≈ 0.71) and LLM-Judge rankings, showing consistent model ordering while being significantly cheaper than LLM-based scoring.

## Results

On MME-U, Gemini2.5-flash-image achieves the highest overall score of 50.04, followed by Gemini2.0-flash-exp (45.57), RecA (42.60), GPT-4o-Image (41.06), and Bagel (40.35). Among open-source U-MLLMs, RecA leads with 42.60, while the best specialized understanding model (Gemini2.5-Pro) scores only 24.69 and the best generative model (CogVideoX) scores 12.51, confirming that U-MLLMs handle a broader task range but still lag behind specialized models on individual dimensions. On unify tasks, even the best model (Gemini2.5-flash-image) achieves only 47.02% accuracy, and no model successfully completes multi-step Visual CoT tasks (all score 0% on acc+).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among U-MLLMs, Gemini2.5-flash-image (50.04) outperforms all other models, beating GPT-4o-Image (41.06) by ~9 points and the best open-source model RecA (42.60) by ~7.5 points. On understanding, specialized models still lead: Gemini2.5-Pro (74.07) surpasses the best U-MLLM Gemini2.5-flash-image (69.93) by 4.1 points, while open-source U-MLLMs like Bagel (60.26) and RecA (63.01) trail behind Qwen2.5-VL (61.91) and Intern-VL3 (62.19) by only small margins. On generation, CogVideoX (37.54) and Qwen-Image-Edit (24.61) outperform most U-MLLMs on video and editing tasks respectively, though Gemini2.0-flash-exp (57.56 on TIG) exceeds DALL-E-3 (51.40) by 6 points on text-to-image. On unify tasks, the gap between closed-source and open-source U-MLLMs is stark: Gemini2.5-flash-image (47.02) and GPT-4o-Image (41.10) far exceed the best open-source models Bagel (35.80) and RecA (37.45), while models like MIO-Instruct (16.56) and Anole (22.30) struggle significantly. Notably, Janus-Pro and Janus-Flow score near-zero on unify tasks despite reasonable understanding scores, revealing a critical disconnect between comprehension and generation in single-tokenizer architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying evaluation of U-MLLMs across three previously siloed dimensions—multimodal understanding, multimodal generation, and mixed-modality (unified) generation—into a single standardized benchmark. It repurposes data from 12 existing datasets (MME, MMBench, Emu-Edit, Geometry3K, etc.) but introduces a novel evaluation protocol: reformulating all understanding tasks into multiple-choice format and all generation/unified tasks into CLIP-I-based image multiple-choice, enabling a single comparable score. The five unified subtasks (e.g., drawing auxiliary lines before solving geometry, step-by-step maze navigation with image generation) have no prior benchmark counterpart, departing from prior work that only showcased such capabilities through anecdotal case studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MME-U provides the first reproducible, standardized leaderboard for U-MLLMs, revealing that even the strongest model (Gemini 2.5 Flash-Image) scores only ~50/100 and that no model excels across all three capability dimensions simultaneously. The finding that no evaluated model can complete multi-step Visual CoT tasks challenges the narrative that current U-MLLMs have achieved genuine unified reasoning-generation synergy. Open questions include whether understanding and generation capabilities can be made to complement rather than trade off, how to close the substantial gap between open-source and closed-source U-MLLMs, and whether CLIP-based image evaluation (acknowledged as potentially hackable) will need to be replaced by stricter LLM-judge or human-evaluation protocols at scale.
