## Problem

Masked diffusion language models (MDLMs) make a binary decision at each decoding step: either replace a [MASK] token with a predicted token or retain the mask. This binary unmasking discards the model's full predictive distribution (confidence over multiple candidate tokens) whenever the mask is kept, losing valuable contextual information that could guide subsequent denoising steps. The paper asks whether this discarded predictive information can be preserved and propagated to improve generation quality.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (or fully masked sequence for unconstrained generation) and iteratively denoises it over T steps, where the NFE budget (ratio of diffusion steps to max tokens) controls generation speed. Unusually, the input to the bidirectional Transformer at each denoising step is not restricted to discrete one-hot tokens or the [MASK] embedding; instead, retained mask positions receive a continuous convex combination of the mask embedding and the top-k predicted token embeddings from the previous step. The output is a fully unmasked text sequence or code snippet, evaluated via perplexity, MAUVE, or pass@1 accuracy on coding benchmarks.

## Method

Soft-masking (SM) replaces the binary mask/unmask feedback with a convex combination: for each retained mask position, the embedding becomes (1−λ)·m + λ·Σ(π_i·v_i) over the top-k predicted tokens, where π_i are normalized probabilities and λ is a confidence-gated weight computed via a scaled sigmoid of the negative entropy of the prediction distribution. Three learnable parameters (ω_a, ω_b, ω_s) control the sigmoid's steepness, offset, and amplitude, allowing the model to adaptively decide how much predictive information to propagate. Training uses a two-pass procedure: a detached first forward pass yields the model's predictions, SM is applied to construct the soft-masked input, and a second forward pass computes the standard masked-diffusion loss, updating both backbone and SM parameters jointly. This adds only three parameters to the model and can be applied from scratch, via 100k-step continued pretraining, or via DoRA-based finetuning of 7B-parameter models (Dream-7B, Dream-Coder-7B), with SM activated at a configurable probability to preserve the model's ability to handle standard binary inputs.

## Results

On a 169M-parameter MDLM trained on OpenWebText, SM improves MAUVE by up to +0.568 (from 0.034 to 0.602) and generative perplexity by up to −26.93 (from 50.46 to 23.53) under standard unmasking at the 1/1 NFE budget. With ReMDM unmasking, SM reaches a MAUVE of 0.774, surpassing the autoregressive baseline (0.760). On code generation, finetuning Dream-Coder-7B with SM yields up to +15.1% accuracy on MBPP+ at the 1/2 NFE budget, and Dream-7B gains up to +7.5% on MBPP+ at 1/4 NFE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the binary MDLM baseline (Sahoo et al., 2024), SM consistently wins on both MAUVE and generative perplexity across all NFE budgets and unmasking strategies, with the largest gains at lower compute budgets (e.g., +0.568 MAUVE at 1/1 NFE with standard unmasking). On validation perplexity (Table 4), SM (iso-update, PPL 21.47) outperforms SEDD (≤24.10), GIDD+ (≤22.29), and the concurrent LDDM-M (≤21.90), while remaining competitive with the AR baseline (17.54). For code generation, SM-finetuned Dream-Coder-7B beats the binary-finetuned baseline on all four benchmarks at 1/4 and 1/2 NFE budgets (e.g., +7.3% on MBPP, +5.8% on HumanEval at 1/4 NFE), though at the full 1/1 NFE budget the gains shrink to +0.5–1.5%. SM-finetuned Dream-7B similarly outperforms its binary counterpart at 1/4 and 1/2 NFE (up to +7.5% on MBPP+) but shows slight regressions (−1.7 to −3.0%) at 1/1 NFE. Notably, SM with ReMDM at 1/1 NFE achieves a MAUVE of 0.774, exceeding the AR model's 0.760, demonstrating that the diffusion approach with continuous feedback can match or surpass autoregressive generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked diffusion language model (MDLM) framework by replacing the binary mask/unmask decoding decision with a soft-masking (SM) mechanism that blends the [MASK] embedding with a confidence-weighted superposition of top-k predicted tokens. The conceptual motivation is drawn from continuous feedback mechanisms in autoregressive models (COCONUT, Mixture of Inputs), which preserve uncertainty over multiple candidates, but SM adapts this idea to the parallel diffusion setting where training remains fully parallelizable. The two-pass training procedure is directly inspired by self-conditioning in Analog Bits (Chen et al., 2023), and the method is demonstrated on the MDLM architecture of Sahoo et al. (2024) and the large-scale Dream-7B / Dream-Coder-7B models. Unlike prior work on continuous or hybrid diffusion representations that alter the latent space or increase input dimensionality, SM operates in the existing embedding space with only three additional learnable parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SM addresses a fundamental information-loss bottleneck in MDLMs: the binary unmasking decision discards all predictive context for retained masks, limiting the model's ability to self-correct across decoding steps. The practical significance is that SM can be retrofitted onto pretrained MDLMs with only ~100k steps of continued pretraining or ~33.5k finetuning steps, and it composes with existing efficiency techniques (ReMDM, Fast-dLLM caching), making it a drop-in improvement for the growing ecosystem of diffusion LLMs. The particularly large gains in high-throughput (low-NFE) regimes suggest SM is most valuable precisely where MDLMs are deployed for speed. Open questions include whether reinforcement learning can further exploit the richer feedback signal, and whether the two-pass training overhead can be reduced without sacrificing the learned weighting.
