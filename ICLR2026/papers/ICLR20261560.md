## Problem

Standard causal estimands and methods rely on Euclidean summaries (means, variances) that can miss intervention-induced changes in the intrinsic topological structure of complex outcomes such as molecular graphs, medical images, or point clouds. No existing framework defines a causal estimand directly in terms of topological summaries (e.g., persistent homology) while simultaneously providing fully nonparametric estimation, valid inference, and a formal hypothesis test for the absence of a topological effect.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261560/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes complex outcomes in arbitrary modalities (point clouds, grid-structured images, or graphs), a binary treatment indicator, and a covariate vector. Each outcome is converted to a simplicial complex with a chosen filtration (Vietoris-Rips, cubical, clique, etc.), from which persistence diagrams are extracted and embedded as power-weighted silhouette functions over a compact filtration-scale interval T. The output is a functional TATE curve ψ_d(t) in a separable Hilbert space for each homology degree d, accompanied by simultaneous confidence bands over T and a bootstrap-based test statistic for the null of no topological effect.

## Method

The paper defines the Topological Average Treatment Effect (TATE) as the expected contrast of power-weighted silhouette functions of persistence diagrams under the two potential outcomes, yielding a functional causal estimand indexed by filtration scale. An efficient, doubly robust AIPW estimator is constructed from the efficient influence function, with nuisance functions (propensity score and conditional silhouette regression) estimated via sample splitting to avoid empirical-process restrictions. The key theoretical contributions include: (i) weak convergence of the AIPW estimator to a Gaussian process in ℓ^∞(T) under a product-rate condition on nuisance errors, (ii) new stability bounds showing the weighted silhouette is Lipschitz in the W_1 Wasserstein distance between persistence diagrams, and (iii) a formal test of no topological effect based on the supremum norm of the estimated effect curve with a multiplier-bootstrap critical value, achieving asymptotically correct size and consistency. The silhouette embedding is chosen because it is 1-Lipschitz in the filtration index, vectorizable in a Hilbert space, and downweights short-lived spurious features via the power parameter r.

## Results

The paper evaluates its three estimators (PI, IPW, AIPW) on two semi-synthetic datasets (SARS-CoV-2 CT scans and GEOM-Drugs molecular graphs) and one synthetic dataset (ORBIT, in appendix), with 20 simulation replications each. No quantitative error metrics (e.g., MSE, RMSE) are reported; instead, performance is assessed visually by comparing estimated silhouette curves against the known ground-truth TATE. Across both datasets, the AIPW estimator most closely tracks the true functional effect, while IPW systematically overestimates and PI underestimates the treatment effect magnitude.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods; instead, it benchmarks three estimators within its own framework: the plug-in (PI) regression estimator, the inverse-probability-weighting (IPW) estimator, and the proposed augmented IPW (AIPW) estimator. On the SARS-CoV-2 dataset (0-dimensional silhouettes from CT scans), AIPW achieves the closest reconstruction of the true silhouette, while IPW overestimates the effect and PI underestimates it. On the GEOM-Drugs dataset, all three estimators align well with the true 0-dimensional silhouette (which shows a negative effect indicating component merging), but for the 1-dimensional silhouette (showing a pronounced positive effect from new loop formation), IPW overestimates the magnitude and PI fails to capture the complex curvature, whereas AIPW delivers the most accurate approximation. These empirical findings are consistent with the theoretical doubly-robust analysis in Appendix B, which shows that AIPW's bias is a product of nuisance errors (Δ₁δ₁ − Δ₀δ₀) and vanishes if either the propensity score or the outcome regression is correctly specified, while PI and IPW each require their respective single nuisance to be accurate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads: Kennedy's semiparametric doubly robust estimation framework (adapted here to functional outcomes), Chazal et al.'s power-weighted silhouette functions as a functional embedding of persistence diagrams, and the emerging line of functional causal inference (Ecker et al., 2024; Testa et al., 2025). The authors explicitly position their work as filling a gap: no prior framework defines a causal estimand directly in terms of topological summaries while providing nonparametric estimation and valid inference. The key methodological departure is defining the target parameter as the expected contrast of silhouette functions under potential outcomes, rather than applying standard ATE estimators to ad hoc Euclidean vectorizations of persistence diagrams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a principled route to causal inference for outcomes whose scientifically meaningful variation is structural rather than scalar (molecular conformations, brain connectivity, medical imaging), a setting where conventional Euclidean summaries can miss treatment effects entirely. The new Wasserstein-stability bound for weighted silhouettes (Theorem 5.3) and the resulting first formal test of 'no topological effect' are genuinely novel contributions to the TDA literature. Limitations include the aggregation inherent in silhouettes (they obscure the exact count of changing features), computational cost of persistent homology, and the framework's focus on macroscopic rather than fine-grained topological shifts. Extensions to continuous treatments, instrumental variables, and longitudinal exposures are identified as open directions.
