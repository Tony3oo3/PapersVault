## Problem

Language models struggle with table understanding because tabular data is inherently structured, dense, concise, and numerical—properties that contrast sharply with the linear text dominating LM pretraining. Prior work addresses only isolated aspects (e.g., sub-table extraction or symbolic reasoning) without a systematic diagnosis of the underlying challenges or a unified framework that combines complementary solutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265144/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a table encoded in Markdown (potentially in row-major or column-major format, with noisy or inconsistent cell values) plus a natural-language question or statement. For question answering it outputs a free-form natural-language answer; for fact verification it outputs a Boolean True/False verdict. A 'table peek' mechanism limits structure-extraction steps to only the top k rows, reducing token consumption, and all results are produced from a single inference run without self-consistency voting.

## Method

TableMaster is a three-stage, fine-tuning-free framework. Stage 1 (Structure Understanding) normalizes the table, then uses LM-prompted column lookup (ranking headers by relevance) and SQL-generated row lookup to construct a compact 'table-of-focus' sub-table. Stage 2 (Content Understanding) checks information sufficiency, re-constructs the sub-table if needed, and verbalizes it into sequential natural-language text to enrich sparse semantic context. Stage 3 (Reasoning) employs Adaptive Reasoning: the LM first assesses whether the query requires numerical computation, then either performs chain-of-thought textual reasoning directly or executes text-guided symbolic reasoning, where an intermediate textual reasoning trace guides subsequent Python/SQL program generation and execution. This adaptive switching, combined with the table-of-focus and verbalization steps, is the core mechanism distinguishing TableMaster from prior single-strategy approaches.

## Results

On WikiTQ, TableMaster achieves 78.13% accuracy with GPT-4o-mini, surpassing the previous best (PoTable at 64.73%) by +13.40 points, and 77.95% with Llama-3.1-70B (+12.39 over PoTable's 65.56%). On TabFact, TableMaster reaches 90.12% (GPT-4o-mini) and 91.16% (Llama-3.1-70B), outperforming all baselines by +1.19 to +4.10 points. On FinQA, TableMaster boosts numerical-reasoning accuracy by +15.7 points (GPT-4o-mini: 50.7→66.4%) and +7.8 points (GPT-4o: 63.1→70.9%). On FetaQA, TableMaster improves BLEU by +16.2% and ROUGE-1 by +6.5% over the end-to-end QA baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TableMaster outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and model configuration. On WikiTQ with GPT-4o-mini, the largest gap is against PoTable (64.73%) and Chain-of-Table (55.60%), with TableMaster reaching 78.13%; against the strongest prior method (PoTable), the margin is +13.40 points. On TabFact, TableMaster beats PoTable (88.93% with GPT-4o-mini) by +1.19 points and Chain-of-Table (84.24%) by +5.88 points. Notably, several symbolic-reasoning baselines (Binder, Dater, TabSQLify, Chain-of-Table) perform worse with GPT-4o-mini than with GPT-3.5-turbo, while TableMaster consistently improves with stronger backbones. On HiTab (hierarchical tables), TableMaster (74.2%) slightly edges out MultiCoT with optimized prompt + verbalized table (73.5%) after relational conversion, though E5 (77.3%) remains stronger when operating directly on hierarchical structure. On FinQA, TableMaster's symbolic reasoning module delivers the largest absolute gains (+15.7 points with GPT-4o-mini), confirming its advantage on calculation-heavy financial tables.

## Relation to Prior Work

TableMaster synthesizes several established techniques—sub-table extraction (Dater, Chain-of-Table), program-of-thought prompting (Chen et al., 2023), chain-of-thought reasoning (Wei et al., 2023), and plan-and-solve prompting (Wang et al., 2023)—into a single modular pipeline. Unlike prior methods that address one isolated aspect (e.g., Binder for SQL generation, Dater for decomposition), this paper provides a systematic diagnosis of four distinct challenges in table understanding and maps each to a targeted solution within a unified framework. The adaptive reasoning mechanism, which dynamically selects between textual and symbolic strategies per query, is the paper's primary conceptual departure from prior work that commits to a single reasoning paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is practical and model-agnostic, requiring no fine-tuning, making it immediately applicable to any advanced LM for table QA and fact verification. However, it assumes flat relational tables (hierarchical tables require a lossy conversion step), the verbalization quality is bounded by the LM's open-ended generation ability, and adaptive reasoning can be unstable in strategy selection. Open questions include whether specialized small models can replace individual pipeline stages for efficiency, how to natively handle hierarchical and noisy real-world tables, and whether a learned strategy selector could replace the LM-based adaptive reasoning decision.
