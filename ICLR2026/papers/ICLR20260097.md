## Problem

Standard token-level knowledge distillation aligns student models to prompt-template surface form rather than the option-based decision surface used in constrained robotic QA, and always-on test-time enhancements (high-resolution re-evaluation, retrieval, decomposition) violate tight on-device latency and energy budgets. No prior method simultaneously improves decision accuracy, calibration, hallucination mitigation, and interpretability for compact VLMs under explicit compute constraints on edge robots.

## Input / Output

The system consumes an RGB image or multi-panel layout together with a natural-language question and a finite option set (e.g., A–E configurations, colored arrows, yes/no), and must output exactly one valid option string under constrained decoding. Beyond the single option label, BOLT produces a calibrated probability distribution over all options (via temperature-scaled softmax of answer-segment log-likelihoods), and when the router triggers augmentations it also exposes retrieved same-type exemplars and short question-decomposition traces as human-inspectable evidence chains. The entire pipeline operates under an explicit average compute budget B (base pass = 1.00; HR = 0.50, tmRAG = 0.30, QD = 0.35 per trigger), with the router deciding per-instance which augmentations to activate.

## Method

BOLT unifies two components around the multi-choice decision surface. During training, Option-level Decision Distillation (ODD) scores each candidate option by summing only the assistant answer-segment token log-likelihoods (excluding prompt tokens), converts these scores through a temperature-softmax into a teacher option distribution, and trains a LoRA/QLoRA student to match this distribution via a KL term plus a small cross-entropy anchor to ground truth—directly aligning the student's constrained-decoding decision surface rather than imitating token-level surface form. At inference, Budgeted Test-time Augmentation (bTTA) runs a fast constrained pass to extract lightweight routing features (max probability, margin, entropy, same-type retrieval affinity, and agreement across short question decompositions), then a learned gain model solves a per-instance knapsack problem to trigger high-resolution re-evaluation, type-matched retrieval exemplars, or question decomposition only when predicted accuracy gain exceeds the action's cost under the remaining budget. This near-threshold policy, derived from the Lagrangian relaxation of the budgeted optimization, concentrates extra compute on uncertain or retrieval-friendly instances while leaving easy cases at base cost, yielding a 2B student that surpasses its 13B teacher (50.50% vs. 36.74% on Robo2VLM-1) at roughly 1/9 the GPU memory and 2.5× lower latency.

## Results

On Robo2VLM-1, a 2B Qwen2-VL student distilled from LLaVA-1.5-13B via Option-level Decision Distillation (ODD) improves accuracy from 28.66% (zero-shot) to 42.89%, and further to 50.50% with Budgeted Test-time Augmentation (bTTA), surpassing the 13B teacher's 36.74% by 13.8 points. GPU memory drops from 26,878 MB (13B teacher) to 3,035 MB (distilled student) and 3,817 MB with all augmentations enabled, while end-to-end latency is 8.97 s per query versus 22.30 s for the teacher. ODD consistently outperforms token-level KD by 4.3–5.3 points across all three teacher models, and bTTA adds a further 4.9–7.0 points over ODD alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LLaVA-1.5-13B teacher (36.74% zero-shot), the 2B BOLT student with ODD + bTTA achieves 50.50%, a +13.8-point improvement while using 88.7% less GPU memory and running 2.5× faster (8.97 s vs. 22.30 s per query). Against the Qwen2-VL-2B zero-shot baseline (28.66%), BOLT delivers a +21.84-point gain, demonstrating that option-level decision alignment plus budgeted routing closes most of the capacity gap without changing model architecture. ODD also consistently outperforms the token-level KD baseline by 4.3–5.3 points across all teacher–student pairs (e.g., 42.89% vs. 37.58% with LLaVA-13B), confirming that aligning the decision surface at the option level is more effective than sequence-level logit matching for constrained multi-choice decoding. The cross-architecture experiment (PaliGemma-2-3B student) shows the same pattern: ODD + bTTA reaches 45.92% versus 40.21% for Token-KD + bTTA, indicating the gains are not specific to a single student architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOLT repositions knowledge distillation for vision-language models from the token/sequence level (Hinton et al., 2015; Kim & Rush, 2016) to the option level, arguing that in constrained multi-choice decoding the relevant decision surface is the distribution over answer options rather than the full token sequence. It combines this option-level decision distillation (ODD) with a budgeted test-time augmentation router that borrows from retrieval-augmented generation (Lewis et al., 2020) and selective prediction / adaptive-compute literature (Geifman & El-Yaniv, 2017; Teerapittayanon et al., 2016), but frames the routing decision as a contextual bandit with a Lagrangian budget constraint rather than a fixed-threshold or always-on policy. The key departure from prior multimodal KD is that supervision is restricted to answer-segment log-likelihoods, eliminating prompt-template artifacts and aligning the student directly with the evaluation interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge robotics: a 2B student distilled with ODD and routed with bTTA surpasses a 13B teacher in accuracy (50.50% vs. 36.74%) while using 88.7% less GPU memory and 82.5% less energy, making large-model decision quality feasible on commodity hardware. However, the evaluation is confined to a single panel-style benchmark (Robo2VLM-1), and the retrieval component introduces a non-trivial contradiction rate (RCR 21.73%), leaving open whether the framework generalizes to more diverse robotic perception tasks or open-ended settings. Future work on conflict-aware retrieval filtering, on-policy decision distillation from logged robot interactions, and multi-dataset validation would be needed to establish broader applicability.
