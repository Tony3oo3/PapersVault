## Problem

The dominant two-stage SFT-then-RL pipeline for LLM post-training suffers from a fundamental inconsistency: SFT enforces rigid imitation that suppresses exploration and induces catastrophic forgetting, undermining the RL stage's ability to improve reasoning. A naive per-instance combination of SFT and RL losses is even worse, causing a catastrophic performance collapse of over 18 points. The paper asks how to integrate expert supervision and self-exploration at the finest grain without destabilizing the policy.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method takes a mathematical problem prompt and a variable-length prefix from an offline DeepSeek-R1 expert trajectory (ranging from 0% to 100% of the full trajectory, selected adaptively per prompt based on the model's current return). The target model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B) generates a completion from the prefix, and both the expert prefix tokens and the model-generated suffix are used for a dual gradient update. At inference, the model simply receives the problem prompt and produces a step-by-step reasoning trajectory ending with a final answer in \boxed{} format, with no external guidance.

## Method

TRAPO interleaves SFT and RL within each training instance via two key mechanisms. First, Trust-Region SFT (TrSFT) replaces the standard SFT loss by clipping the per-token gradient weight at 1/α (α=0.1), which minimizes forward KL inside a trust region defined by the target policy's own beliefs but attenuates updates outside it, effectively shifting the objective from mode-covering (forward KL) to mode-seeking (reverse KL) and preventing the distribution-blending that causes degenerated rollouts. Second, micro-group sampling adaptively allocates expert prefix length: for each prompt, the model first rolls out without guidance (L₁=0); if the average return falls below a threshold, progressively longer expert prefixes (L₂=0.2, L₃=0.5, L₄=1.0) are injected in subsequent micro-groups, ensuring guidance is used only as much as needed. The total gradient is the sum of TrSFT on expert prefix tokens and Dr.GRPO on self-generated completions, enabling simultaneous skill internalization and exploration.

## Results

On five mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME2024, AMC, MATH-500, Minerva, OlympiadBench), TRAPO achieves an average score of 56.6, surpassing the SFT-then-RL pipeline (54.3) by +2.3 points, pure GRPO (50.4) by +6.2 points, and standalone SFT (50.3) by +6.3 points. On general-domain reasoning (ARC-c, MMLU-Pro), TRAPO attains 68.3 average, outperforming all baselines including LUFFY (66.7) and ReLIFT (64.4). The paper further shows that naively combining SFT and RL losses without TrSFT causes a catastrophic collapse of over 18 points relative to pure RL, validating the necessity of the trust-region formulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRAPO outperforms all seven baselines on the mathematical reasoning average. Against pure RL methods, TRAPO beats GRPO (50.4) by +6.2, Oat-Zero (50.1) by +6.5, PRIME-Zero (46.3) by +10.3, OpenReasoner-Zero (46.2) by +10.4, and SimpleRL-Zero (43.5) by +13.1. Against expert-guidance methods, TRAPO surpasses SFT-then-RL (54.3) by +2.3, LUFFY (55.5) by +1.1, ReLIFT (53.4) by +3.2, and standalone SFT (50.3) by +6.3. On general-domain reasoning, the gap widens substantially: TRAPO (68.3) leads LUFFY (66.7) by +1.6 and ReLIFT (64.4) by +3.9, while SFT-then-RL (44.5) and SFT (42.3) lag far behind, confirming that TRAPO avoids the rigid imitation and catastrophic forgetting that plague sequential SFT pipelines. Notably, the ablation in Table 2 shows that naively adding standard SFT loss to micro-group sampling causes a collapse to 32.3 average (−18.1 vs. GRPO), whereas TrSFT recovers to 56.6, underscoring that the trust-region clipping is essential for stable SFT–RL integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRAPO builds directly on GRPO/Dr.GRPO as its RL backbone and departs from the dominant two-stage SFT-then-RL pipeline by interleaving SFT and RL at the per-instance level. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of behavior cloning (forward KL minimization) and policy optimization, and its key departure is the Trust-Region SFT (TrSFT) objective, which clips the SFT gradient to shift the effective divergence from mode-covering forward KL toward mode-seeking reverse KL. Among concurrent works combining SFT and RL (ReLIFT, LUFFY, SRFT, AMFT, Prefix-RFT), TRAPO is distinguished by its per-instance interleaving with adaptive prefix length and by providing the first theoretical analysis of why naive SFT+RL loss combination destabilizes training (distribution blending in void regions).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and resolves a concrete failure mode—naive SFT+RL combination causes a >18-point collapse—that had been observed empirically but not previously explained, giving practitioners a principled recipe (TrSFT) for safely integrating expert supervision into RL. The pass@k analysis showing that TRAPO expands the model's solution space while pure RL does not has broader implications for how post-training should be designed to improve test-time scaling. Limitations include evaluation restricted to 7B Qwen models and mathematical reasoning only; whether the trust-region mechanism and adaptive prefix selection transfer to larger models, non-mathematical domains, or different expert sources remains open.
