## Problem

Existing Process Reward Models (PRMs) for LLM reasoning either evaluate each step in isolation, ignoring inter-step causal dependencies, or fail to explicitly link step-level rewards to the final outcome. This leads to ambiguous credit assignment, poor cross-sample comparability, and vulnerability to reward hacking where rewards inflate while actual accuracy declines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262973/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The reward model takes a question x and the first t reasoning steps a_{≤t} as input, and outputs a scalar wrong-state probability h(t) ∈ [0,1] via an LLM backbone with a value head. From h(t), the method derives a step-level process reward r_t = log(1−h(t)) and a trajectory-level score S(T) = ∏(1−h(k)) that is directly interpretable as the probability of reaching the correct final answer. The consistent probabilistic semantics of S(t) across all steps and samples enables direct cross-sample comparison, unlike prior PRMs whose rewards lack a shared scale.

## Method

CRM frames multi-step reasoning as a temporal process by modeling the complementary event of entering a 'wrong state' at step z, and defines h(t) = Pr(z=t | z≥t) as the conditional probability of first failing at step t given all prior steps were correct. Applying the chain rule yields S(T) = ∏(1−h(k)), explicitly linking the trajectory score to the final outcome. The process reward r_t = log(1−h(t)) is then derived rigorously via Potential-Based Reward Shaping with potential function Φ(s_t) = log S(t), guaranteeing policy invariance. Training uses three losses: L_S maximizes S(T) for correct trajectories, L_W minimizes S(T) for incorrect ones, and L_z identifies the exact first-wrong step z_i, providing dense supervision that propagates backward through the trajectory. This formulation captures causal inter-step dependencies, resolves credit assignment ambiguity, and produces rewards that are robust to reward hacking during RL optimization.

## Results

In RL optimization without verifiable rewards (VR Disabled), CRM achieves the best Pass@1 on 5 of 6 benchmarks, reaching 77.8% on MATH500, 40.0% on Minerva Math, 39.3% on OlympiadBench, 23.3% on AIME25, and 43.3% on AIME24, outperforming the strongest baseline PURE by +1.8, +9.2, +2.6, +10.0, and +16.7 respectively. When augmented with VR (CRM+VR), CRM further improves to 80.4% on MATH500 and 43.0% on Minerva Math, matching or exceeding the VR-only baseline on all benchmarks. In Best-of-N sampling on MATH500 with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, CRM reaches 56.6% at N=32, surpassing the best baseline (PQM at 54.8%) by +1.8. In beam search on MATH500 with Qwen2.5-Math-7B, CRM achieves 64.07% at N=100, outperforming PQM (61.13%) by +3.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRM outperforms all process reward model baselines across the three evaluation settings. In RL without VR, CRM beats PRM (Wang et al., 2024) by +6.2 on MATH500 and +33.3 on AIME24, and beats PQM (Li & Li, 2025) by +5.8 on MATH500 and +30.0 on AIME24; it also surpasses IPRM (Yuan et al., 2025) in Best-of-N and beam search (e.g., +1.4 over PQM at N=32 on MATH500). Against the stronger RL baselines, CRM (VR Disabled) exceeds PURE (Cheng et al., 2025) on 5 of 6 benchmarks, with the largest gap of +16.7 on AIME24, while Prime (Cui et al., 2025) requires VR and is not directly comparable in the VR-disabled setting. In Best-of-N, CRM also outperforms the reasoning-enhanced R-PRM (She et al., 2025) by +3.2 (Best-of-8) and +5.2 (Best-of-16), and GenPRM (Zhao et al., 2025) by +2.2 and +1.2 respectively on MATH500. The only benchmark where CRM (VR Disabled) trails a baseline is AMC23, where PURE achieves 70.0 vs. CRM's 67.5, though CRM+VR recovers to 72.5, matching Prime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Process Reward Model (PRM) literature, departing from the dominant paradigm of treating each reasoning step as an independent classification target (Lightman et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024). It also extends the line of work attempting to capture inter-step dependencies—PQM's Q-value ranking (Li & Li, 2025) and IPRM's log-sum parameterization (Yuan et al., 2025)—by introducing a unified conditional-probability formulation where each step's reward is the probability of entering a 'wrong state' given all prior steps were correct. The theoretical backbone is Potential-Based Reward Shaping (Ng et al., 1999), repurposed here with a potential function log S(t) that encodes progress toward the final correct answer, yielding the closed-form process reward r_t = log(1−h(t)).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRM offers a principled, ground-truth-free alternative to verifiable-reward RL for LLM reasoning, which is significant given the cost and limited scalability of ground-truth labels. Its demonstrated robustness to reward hacking (repetitive degenerate outputs) and its ability to match VR-enabled performance without verifiers make it practically attractive for open-ended reasoning tasks. Open questions include whether the conditional-probability formulation generalizes to non-mathematical reasoning at scale, whether the 'wrong state' abstraction holds for multi-modal or agentic settings, and how CRM interacts with longer-horizon or tree-structured reasoning beyond linear chains.
