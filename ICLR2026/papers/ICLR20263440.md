## Problem

While the Convolutional Neural Tangent Kernel (CNTK) provides a principled infinite-width framework for CNNs, a rigorous spectral comparison between CNTK and the classical NTK on the same data distribution has been largely absent from the literature. This gap leaves open the question of when and why convolutional inductive biases are more beneficial than fully-connected ones, particularly for irregular geometric data such as point clouds.

## Input / Output

For point cloud tasks, the input is a set of 3D (or 6D with orientation) point coordinates arranged as a 1D tensor of shape [n_points × n_channels], treated as a single-dimensional convolutional input with kernel size 1 (equivalent to PointNet's shared-MLP). For image tasks, standard 2D RGB tensors (MNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10 32×32) are used with 2D convolutions. The output is a class label, produced either via kernel regression (solving a linear system with the precomputed NTK/CNTK/PointNTK Gram matrix) or via standard supervised training of the corresponding finite-width network.

## Method

The paper proves two distribution-free spectral theorems: (i) the mean eigenvalue of L-layer NTK is always ≥ that of L-layer CNTK, and (ii) the eigenvalue spread β_K of NTK is always ≤ that of CNTK (which equals 1), implying CNTK generalizes better. They define β_K^(0) computed from the data covariance matrix as a 'Convolutional Suitability' score, showing point clouds yield much lower values than images. The practical contribution is PointNTK, a closed-form kernel combining 4 layers of 1D CNTK (kernel size 1, matching PointNet's shared-MLP) with 3 layers of NTK (fully connected), derived via a general Proposition 1 for d₁-dim convolution followed by d₂-dim convolution. Kernel regression is performed by directly inverting the PointNTK Gram matrix, and ablations confirm that kernel size 1 and the hybrid CNTK-NTK depth split are optimal for unordered point cloud data.

## Results

On the full ModelNet10_6 dataset, PointNTK (the proposed CNTK-NTK kernel regression) achieves 91.19% accuracy, closely matching PointNet (92.08%) and 1dCNTK (91.96%), while NTK achieves only 16.19%. In the critical few-shot setting (100 training samples), PointNTK substantially outperforms PointNet: 78.52% vs. 70.22% on ModelNet10_6, 73.68% vs. 68.92% on ModelNet10_3, and 45.71% vs. 38.32% on ModelNet40. On image datasets, 2dCNTK also outperforms NTK (96.00% vs. 94.00% on MNIST; 76.79% vs. 59.19% on CIFAR-10), but the spectral gap (β_K) is far smaller than for point clouds (1.12e-3 vs. 3.38e-2 to 1.37e-1), confirming that convolutional structures are more critical for point cloud data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NTK (Jacot et al., 2018), PointNTK achieves a dramatic improvement on all point cloud datasets: 86.56% vs. 17.58% on ModelNet10_3, 91.19% vs. 16.19% on ModelNet10_6, and 80.47% vs. 11.10% on ModelNet40, confirming that the convolutional inductive bias is essential for unordered point cloud data. Against PointNet (Qi et al., 2017), PointNTK is slightly behind in full-data settings (e.g., 91.19% vs. 92.08% on ModelNet10_6) but clearly wins in the few-shot regime with only 100 training samples: 78.52% vs. 70.22% on ModelNet10_6, 73.68% vs. 68.92% on ModelNet10_3, and 45.71% vs. 38.32% on ModelNet40. The deeper variant PointNTK_L further narrows the full-data gap (92.62% on ModelNet10_6) while maintaining the few-shot advantage (82.38% vs. 57.39% for PointNet_L). The single-kernel 1dCNTK baseline (without the trailing MLP) is consistently outperformed by PointNTK, validating the theoretical prediction that combining CNTK's broad spectrum with NTK's larger eigenvalue shift yields the best of both properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CNTK framework of Arora et al. (2019a) from the standard 2D image setting to arbitrary-dimensional tensor spaces, and builds on the NTK formulation of Jacot et al. (2018) to derive a closed-form for hybrid CNTK-NTK architectures. It departs from prior CNTK studies (Geifman et al. 2022, Cagnetta et al. 2023, Barzilai et al. 2022) by providing the first distribution-free spectral comparison between NTK and CNTK on the same data, introducing the 'Convolutional Suitability' metric based on the first-layer covariance structure. The practical instantiation, PointNTK, directly adopts the PointNet architecture (Qi et al. 2017) as a template for the 1D-convolution-plus-MLP kernel, positioning kernel regression as an alternative to supervised training for point cloud classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distribution-free spectral theorems (m_NTK ≥ m_CNTK, β_NTK ≤ β_CNTK = 1) provide a clean theoretical explanation for why convolutional networks generalize better than MLPs and why appending fully connected layers after pooling is beneficial. The Convolutional Suitability metric offers a data-dependent, architecture-selection tool that could guide practitioners toward convolutional or MLP designs. However, the practical impact is tempered: PointNTK still underperforms supervised PointNet on full training sets (86.56% vs. 91.12% on ModelNet10_3), and the β_K computation for deeper layers requires distributional assumptions, limiting the metric to first-layer analysis in practice. Open questions include tighter spectral bounds for L-layer kernels and whether the suitability metric generalizes beyond Gaussian assumptions.
