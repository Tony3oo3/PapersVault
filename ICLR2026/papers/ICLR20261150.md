## Problem

Selecting an appropriate network width under computational constraints remains largely ad hoc: existing approaches such as neural architecture search, supernet training, and scaling laws can rank or constrain architectures but lack a theory-grounded stopping rule that certifies when further widening ceases to improve generalization. No prior work directly connects a single, efficiently computable spectral quantity of the Neural Tangent Kernel to an upper bound on test error, leaving practitioners without a principled, training-free criterion for width selection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the training inputs (no labels), a fixed architecture form with a variable width parameter, an initialization scheme, and a discrete width grid. It produces the cardinal width—the smallest width at which the fitted saturating curve of μ_min has negligible slope—along with the per-width smallest NTK eigenvalue estimates. Notably, batch normalization is evaluated in inference mode during kernel construction to avoid dependence on batch statistics, and the eigenvalue estimation uses LOBPCG (an iterative solver) rather than a full eigendecomposition, making the procedure feasible even for datasets with tens of thousands of samples.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution shows that in the infinite-width NTK regime, the generalization error of kernel ridgeless regression is upper-bounded by C·μ_min^{-2}, where μ_min is the smallest eigenvalue of the NTK Gram matrix, by specializing the closed-form risk of Canatar et al. (2021) and bounding bias and variance terms through the monotonicity of the spectral filter. For finite-width networks trained by gradient flow, a three-term decomposition (linearization gap, time-variation gap, finite-vs-infinite-width gap) yields an analogous bound governed by μ_min of the empirical NTK at initialization, with a width-dependent correction φ(m) that vanishes as m→∞ under lazy-training kernel stability. Practically, Algorithm 1 computes the empirical NTK at initialization for each candidate width, estimates μ_min via LOBPCG, fits a saturating curve g(x) = −ax/(b+x)+c by least squares, and declares the cardinal width as the smallest m where the fitted derivative drops below a threshold δ. This makes the procedure entirely training-free and requires only a single extreme eigenvalue per width rather than a full spectral decomposition.

## Results

The paper establishes that the test error of both infinite-width and sufficiently wide finite-width networks is upper-bounded by O(μ_min^{-2}), where μ_min is the smallest eigenvalue of the NTK at initialization. Empirically, across DNNs (UCI Diabetes, California Housing), CNNs and ResNets (MNIST, CIFAR-10), the width at which μ_min saturates (the predicted cardinal width) aligns with the width at which test loss plateaus, confirming the theoretical prediction. The method is training-free and computationally efficient: estimating μ_min via LOBPCG takes on the order of one minute per width on a DNN with ~20K samples (California Housing). In the two-layer verification experiment (Figure 1), all empirical test-loss points lie below the O(μ_min^{-2}) bound, though the actual dependence is milder, approximately O(1/√μ_min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a direct quantitative comparison against competing width-selection methods (e.g., NAS, scaling laws, or supernet training). Instead, validation is performed by checking that the NTK-predicted cardinal width coincides with the empirically observed test-loss saturation point across all architecture–dataset pairs. The authors note that prior NTK-based architecture ranking (Chen et al., 2021) uses NTK as a metric but provides no explicit theoretical link to generalization error, whereas this work establishes the first direct connection between the smallest NTK eigenvalue and an upper bound on test error. The method is also shown to be robust to training-recipe variations (optimizer, learning rate) and to moderate subsampling of the training set, with the predicted cardinal width remaining stable. In the feature-learning regime (where the lazy-training assumption of Theorem 3.8 does not formally hold), the saturation of μ_min still coincides with test-loss saturation, suggesting broader applicability than the theory strictly guarantees.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of NTK theory (Jacot et al. 2018; Lee et al. 2019) and kernel regression generalization analysis (Canatar et al. 2021), repurposing the closed-form risk expression of the latter to derive a bound controlled by a single spectral quantity. It departs from prior width-selection methods (supernet training, NAS, scaling laws) by providing a theory-grounded, training-free stopping criterion rather than an empirical ranking or heuristic. The key conceptual departure from prior NTK-smallest-eigenvalue work (Nguyen et al. 2021; Montanari & Zhong 2022; Karhadkar et al. 2024) is that those results established positive definiteness or bounded the eigenvalue itself, whereas this paper is the first to explicitly connect μ_min to an upper bound on generalization error for both infinite- and finite-width networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cardinal width concept gives practitioners a principled, pre-training stopping rule for width selection, replacing ad hoc trial-and-error with a computable metric (smallest NTK eigenvalue via LOBPCG) that is orders of magnitude cheaper than full eigendecomposition. However, the theoretical guarantees are restricted to lazy training with squared loss and gradient flow, and the empirical validation is primarily on regression tasks; the extension to classification and feature-learning regimes remains empirical without theoretical backing. Open questions include whether the single-eigenvalue bound tightens under stronger assumptions, how the method behaves under practical optimizers (Adam, SGD with momentum), and whether the cardinal width concept transfers to depth selection or other architectural hyperparameters.
