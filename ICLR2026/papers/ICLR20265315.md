## Problem

Existing fMRI brain foundation models are limited to either time-series inputs (computationally expensive) or static ROI/Connectome features (ignoring interregional topology), are typically pre-trained on a single atlas parcellation, and lack mechanisms for few-shot or zero-shot adaptation to unseen disorders and atlases. This paper addresses these gaps by proposing the first graph-based brain foundation model that unifies heterogeneous multi-atlas fMRI data and supports efficient prompt-based transfer across disorders and parcellation schemes.

## Input / Output

The model consumes fMRI brain graphs where nodes are ROIs (100–333 depending on parcellation) with node features set to each ROI's Pearson correlation profile and edges given by the binarized correlation matrix; graphs of varying node counts are unified to a fixed size via zero-padding with attention masking. Two language prompt tokens—[T/D] for the target disorder and [A/P] for the target atlas/parcellation—are prepended to the node sequence, each derived from BioClinicalBERT embeddings of a short clinical or atlas description. The output is a binary disorder class label (e.g., ASD vs. control, MDD vs. control) evaluated via AUC, accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity.

## Method

BrainGFM uses a Graph Transformer backbone with Random Walk Structural Encoding for positional information and learnable [A/P] tokens to encode atlas identity during pre-training. Pre-training jointly optimizes a shared encoder via Graph Contrastive Learning (NT-Xent loss on node/edge-dropped views) and Graph Masked Autoencoding (MSE reconstruction of masked nodes/edges), capturing both global and local graph structure. For downstream adaptation, a lightweight graph prompt (learnable node vectors plus a fully learnable edge matrix) is injected via element-wise multiplication and optimized through MAML-style meta-learning: the inner loop adapts the prompt on a support set for a specific (disorder, atlas) task while the outer loop meta-updates the prompt initialization across a batch of tasks, keeping the backbone frozen. For zero-shot transfer, the graph prompt is also frozen and replaced by language prompts—BioClinicalBERT-encoded text descriptions of the target disorder and atlas—concatenated as [T/D] and [A/P] tokens, providing semantic guidance without any gradient updates.

## Results

On the Schaefer100 atlas across 10 neurological/psychiatric disorders, BrainGFM achieves AUC of 70.3 (ADHD), 71.2 (ASD), 80.3 (AD), 79.9 (MDD), and 85.2 (ANX), with corresponding ACC values of 70.5%, 73.5%, 81.9%, 82.0%, and 86.3%. BrainGFM outperforms the strongest pre-trained baseline Brain-JEPA by 0.2–1.2 AUC points on 4 of 5 disorders (tied within 0.2 on ANX), and surpasses time-series FM BrainLM by 1.9–2.6 AUC points. In few-shot (10%) and zero-shot settings, BrainGFM with graph prompts + meta-learning + language prompts maintains the largest performance margin over all baselines, with the gap widening as labeled data decreases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrainGFM outperforms all 8 baselines on 4 of 5 disorders in AUC, with the only near-tie being ANX where Brain-JEPA edges ahead by 0.2 AUC (85.4 vs 85.2). Against the strongest pre-trained competitor Brain-JEPA, BrainGFM gains +0.5 AUC on ADHD, +1.1 on ASD, +1.2 on AD, and +0.8 on MDD. Compared to the time-series foundation model BrainLM, BrainGFM improves AUC by +2.7 (ADHD), +3.1 (ASD), +2.0 (AD), +2.1 (MDD), and +2.6 (ANX), while matching or exceeding BrainLM's accuracy on all disorders. Against the Connectome/FC-based BrainMass, the AUC advantage is even larger (+3.3 to +4.2 points), confirming that explicit graph modeling of inter-regional connectivity is critical. All non-pre-trained baselines (Vanilla GCN, BrainGNN, Vanilla TF, Graph TF, BrainNetTF) trail BrainGFM by 5–13 AUC points, underscoring the value of large-scale graph pre-training. In the ablation (Figure 2), BrainGFM's advantage over baselines widens substantially under few-shot (10%) and zero-shot (0%) regimes, where language prompts and meta-learned graph prompts are most impactful.

## Relation to Prior Work

BrainGFM departs from the two dominant paradigms in brain foundation models—time-series-based (BrainLM, Thomas et al.) and Connectome/FC-based (BrainMass, BrainNPT)—by introducing a graph-based backbone (Graph Transformer) that explicitly models inter-regional connectivity while maintaining computational efficiency closer to ROI-level models. Its pre-training strategy unifies two established graph self-supervised paradigms (GCL from You et al. and GMAE from Hou et al.) under a shared encoder, and its downstream adaptation borrows from graph prompt learning (Sun et al.) and MAML-style meta-learning (Finn et al.), but applies them jointly to brain graphs for the first time. The multi-atlas pre-training corpus (8 parcellations across functional and anatomical atlases) represents a significant departure from prior brain FMs that typically pre-train on a single parcellation, and the use of BioClinicalBERT-encoded language tokens for zero-shot disorder/atlas identification is a novel cross-modal design choice not seen in prior brain FM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in neuroimaging: the tension between the high performance of time-series FMs and their prohibitive computational cost, offering a graph-based middle ground that matches or exceeds time-series accuracy at Connectome/FC-level efficiency. The few-shot and zero-shot adaptation via meta-learned graph prompts and language prompts is particularly relevant for rare disorders where labeled data is scarce, a setting most prior brain FMs have not addressed. Key limitations include the exclusion of UK Biobank and task-based fMRI data due to cost, the restriction to resting-state fMRI, and the modest (1–3%) performance margins over the strongest baseline (Brain-JEPA), leaving open whether the graph-based advantage persists at larger scale or with more diverse modalities.
