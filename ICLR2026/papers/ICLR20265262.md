## Problem

Directional convergence of gradient descent (as opposed to gradient flow) in leaky ReLU neural networks had not been established even under nearly orthogonal data assumptions, limiting prior benign overfitting results to gradient flow, smoothed activations, or the restrictive nearly orthogonal regime. This paper asks whether directional convergence can be proven for discrete gradient descent on general mixture data, and what classification error the resulting convergent direction achieves.

## Input / Output

The method takes i.i.d. mixture model samples (x_i, y_i) with x = yμ + z, a fixed-width two-layer leaky ReLU network initialized at W^(0) with second-layer weights fixed at ±1/√m, and a constant step size α. It produces a closed-form characterization of the convergent direction as the unique solution to a margin-maximization problem over two vectors (w_+, w_-), along with explicit classification error bounds that depend on the signal-to-noise ratio n‖μ‖²/R. All sufficient conditions are stated deterministically on the training data (via events E, Ẽ₁, Ẽ₂, Ẽ₃), which is what enables extension from sub-Gaussian to polynomially tailed noise.

## Method

The core mechanism is to prove that all neurons remain activated after the first gradient descent step (via small initialization relative to step size and controlled cross-sample correlations), after which the network reduces to a linear classifier in a transformed feature space and directional convergence follows from Soudry et al. (2018). The convergent direction is then shown to equal the minimum-norm least-squares estimator in that transformed space, enabling a closed-form analysis of the resulting linear decision boundary w̄ = |J₊|/√m · w₊ − |J₋|/√m · w₋. This yields a classification error bound that exhibits a phase transition at n‖μ‖² ≈ R: in the weak signal regime the error decays polynomially, while in the strong signal regime it decays exponentially (or matches the Bayes rate for Gaussian mixtures). The deterministic formulation of all conditions, combined with concentration arguments, extends the results to anisotropic polynomially tailed mixtures—a distributional relaxation beyond the sub-Gaussian or log-Sobolev assumptions in prior work.

A novel phase transition is identified at n‖μ‖² ≈ tr(Σ): in the weak signal regime (n‖μ‖² ≲ tr(Σ)) the error decays polynomially, while in the strong signal regime (n‖μ‖² ≳ tr(Σ)) it decays exponentially. ## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions itself against three main prior theoretical results. Compared to Frei et al. (2022) and Xu and Gu (2023), which established benign overfitting only in the nearly orthogonal data regime (n‖μ‖² ≲ tr(Σ)) under sub-Gaussian or bounded log-Sobolev assumptions, this work extends to the strong signal regime (n‖μ‖² ≳ tr(Σ)) and to polynomially tailed mixtures (PM), a strictly broader distributional class. Compared to Frei et al. (2023a), which characterized the convergent direction only for gradient flow on nearly orthogonal data, this paper establishes directional convergence for gradient descent (a discrete-time algorithm) and provides the first precise characterization of the convergent direction for gradient descent in ReLU-type networks. The paper also identifies a phase transition in classification error at n‖μ‖² ≈ tr(Σ) with matching upper and lower bounds for Gaussian mixtures, a phenomenon not previously shown even for gradient flow. In the isotropic sub-Gaussian case (Σ=I_p), the paper's conditions on p are slightly weaker than Xu and Gu (2023) (allowing arbitrary width m rather than m ≳ log n, and a better upper bound p ≲ ‖μ‖⁴/n rather than p·log(mp) ≲ n‖μ‖⁴).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on implicit bias in homogeneous neural networks from gradient flow to gradient descent, which had remained an open problem for ReLU-type activations. It builds on Soudry et al.'s (2018) directional convergence result for linear classifiers by reducing the activated-neuron regime to a transformed linear problem, and on Ji & Telgarsky's (2020) and Frei et al.'s (2023b) gradient flow characterizations of the convergent direction in leaky ReLU networks. The key departure is moving beyond the nearly orthogonal data regime (Frei et al. 2022; Xu & Gu 2023) to general mixture data, and relaxing distributional assumptions from sub-Gaussian/strongly log-concave to polynomially tailed mixtures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery of a phase transition between weak and strong signal regimes (at n‖μ‖² ≈ tr(Σ)) in the classification error of the convergent direction is a genuinely new phenomenon that was not identified even for gradient flow, and the matching lower bound for Gaussian mixtures shows it is intrinsic to the model. The paper also characterizes cases where benign overfitting provably fails despite directional convergence, providing a more complete picture than prior work. Open questions include extending to deep networks, incorporating label noise (which the authors conjecture will substantially change the strong signal regime), and whether the deterministic conditions can be verified in non-mixture settings.
