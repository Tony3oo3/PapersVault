## Problem

Goal-Conditioned Behavior Foundation Models (GC-BFMs) like MaskedMimic generate robust, diverse humanoid motions from high-level prompts but struggle with precise task-specific control, requiring either laborious prompt engineering or full fine-tuning that risks catastrophic forgetting of the model's learned motion priors. There is no lightweight mechanism to inject task-specific reward optimization into a frozen BFM while preserving its zero-shot generalization and multi-modal prompting capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263205/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The Task Encoder receives low-dimensional task goal observations in the agent's egocentric frame (e.g., target direction ∈ R², facing direction ∈ R², desired speed ∈ R for steering, yielding g ∈ R⁵) plus proprioceptive head and pelvis positions, and produces a single 512-dimensional Task Token. This token is concatenated with optional prior tokens (from text prompts or joint conditioning via the BFM's pre-existing encoders) and a state token to form the transformer's input sequence. The frozen BFM then outputs action probabilities over the 69-DOF SMPL humanoid's joint space, executed at 30 Hz in a 120 Hz physics simulation.

## Method

Task Tokens trains a lightweight feed-forward MLP (~200K parameters) as a Task Encoder that maps task goal observations to a 512-dim token, which is appended to the BFM's token input sequence alongside optional user-defined prior tokens and state tokens. The encoder is optimized via PPO with gradients flowing through the frozen MaskedMimic transformer back to the encoder, so the BFM's parameters remain untouched while the learned token steers behavior toward task-specific dense rewards. This design is inspired by parameter-efficient adaptation in NLP (LoRA, prefix-tuning): the token interface of the transformer naturally accommodates new conditioning signals without architectural modification. The approach requires up to ×125 fewer trainable parameters than full fine-tuning and converges up to ×6 faster than PULSE, while preserving the BFM's multi-modal prompting and out-of-distribution robustness.

## Results

Task Tokens achieves high success rates across five humanoid control tasks: 94.88% (Reach), 99.26% (Direction), 88.69% (Steering), 99.75% (Long Jump), and 76.61% (Strike), using only ~200K trainable parameters per task. It converges in under 50M environment steps, roughly 6× faster than PULSE (~300M steps), while requiring 46.5× fewer parameters than PULSE (9.3M) and 125× fewer than full MaskedMimic fine-tuning (25M). On out-of-distribution perturbations (friction ×0.4, gravity ×1.5), Task Tokens maintains substantially higher success rates than all baselines, and a human study confirms it produces more human-like motions than fine-tuning, PULSE, AMP, and PPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against zero-shot MaskedMimic (J.C. only), Task Tokens dramatically outperforms on all three tasks where J.C. is available: 94.88% vs 24.77% (Reach), 99.26% vs 2.19% (Direction), and 88.69% vs 3.83% (Steering), confirming that learned task tokens are essential for precise control. Versus PULSE, Task Tokens wins decisively on Steering (88.69% vs 40.72%) and Reach (94.88% vs 83.96%), matches on Direction (99.26% vs 97.60%) and Long Jump (99.75% vs 99.37%), but loses on Strike (76.61% vs 83.18%); it also converges ~6× faster and uses 46.5× fewer parameters. Against AMP, Task Tokens dominates on every task, with the largest gaps on Direction (99.26% vs 5.14%) and Steering (88.69% vs 4.28%), where AMP's adversarial motion prior fails to produce coherent locomotion. Compared to full MaskedMimic fine-tuning, Task Tokens matches or exceeds success rates on four of five tasks while using 125× fewer parameters, and critically preserves OOD robustness (e.g., under ×0.4 friction and ×1.5 gravity) and multi-modal prompting capability, both of which degrade under fine-tuning due to catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Goal-Conditioned Behavior Foundation Model (GC-BFM) paradigm, specifically MaskedMimic, by introducing a lightweight task-specific encoder that produces learned tokens alongside user-defined goal tokens in the transformer's input sequence. Conceptually, it adapts the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) philosophy from NLP (LoRA, prefix-tuning, adapters) to the token interface of a frozen BFM, rather than modifying model weights. It departs from prior BFM adaptation approaches—full fine-tuning or hierarchical skill selection (PULSE, ASE)—by keeping the foundation model entirely frozen and optimizing only a ~200K-parameter encoder via PPO, while preserving the BFM's multi-modal prompting (text, joint conditioning) capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling scalable, per-task adaptation of BFMs with minimal compute (×125 fewer parameters, ×6 faster convergence) while retaining zero-shot generalization and multi-modal prompting—properties that full fine-tuning degrades. The OOD robustness results (improved performance under friction/gravity perturbations compared to fine-tuning) suggest that preserving the BFM's motion manifold is genuinely beneficial for deployment in variable environments. Key open questions include: whether the approach generalizes beyond MaskedMimic to other GC-BFM architectures, how to handle multi-task or lifelong learning with shared task encoders, and the critical sim-to-real gap for actual robotic deployment. The current reliance on hand-crafted reward functions and domain expertise for observation design remains a practical bottleneck.
