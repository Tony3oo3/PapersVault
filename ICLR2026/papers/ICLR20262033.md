## Problem

Post-Training Quantization of billion-scale 3D reconstruction transformers like VGGT (1.2B parameters) faces two model-specific obstacles that generic PTQ methods do not address: data-independent special tokens (camera and register tokens) induce heavy-tailed activation distributions with extreme channel variance, and the multi-view semantic complexity of 3D data makes calibration sample selection highly unstable. No prior quantization framework was designed specifically for VGGT, leaving a significant gap between its prohibitive computational cost and practical deployment needs.

## Input / Output

The method operates on sequences of N RGB frames (evaluated at 10 or 20 frames), which are tokenized by a DINOv2 backbone and augmented with one camera token and four register tokens per frame before entering the VGGT transformer. The quantized model produces the same multi-task outputs as full-precision VGGT—dense depth maps, 3D point maps, camera poses, and point tracking—while running at W4A4 or W8A8 integer precision. Calibration requires only 40 samples drawn from a 400-sample CO3Dv2 pool, and the quantized model generalizes to unseen datasets (DTU, 7-Scenes, NRGBD) without additional calibration data.

## Method

QuantVGGT introduces two complementary components. First, Dual-Smoothed Fine-Grained Quantization (DSFQ) applies a pre-global random Hadamard rotation to disperse special-token-induced outliers across channels (leveraging the central limit effect to approximate Gaussian distributions), followed by a post-local channel-wise scaling step that normalizes residual inter-channel variance in the rotated space; quantization granularity is set to out-dimension-wise for weights and dynamic token-wise for activations to minimize error without hardware overhead. Second, Noise-Filtered Diverse Sampling (NFDS) computes per-sample noise scores from deep-layer activation mean and variance statistics to filter outliers, then constructs frame-aware correlation vectors (cosine similarity between the first frame and each subsequent frame) to cluster remaining candidates via K-Means, and samples uniformly within each cluster to ensure the calibration set is both representative and stable. The scale factors are fused into adjacent LayerNorm layers, introducing zero runtime cost, while the Hadamard transform uses a fast CUDA kernel with negligible latency overhead.

## Results

On CO3Dv2 camera pose estimation (10 frames), QuantVGGT at W4A4 achieves 86.9 AUC@30 and 43.6 AUC@3, preserving 97.2% of the full-precision model's AUC@30 (89.5) while all other methods drop below 82. At W8A8, QuantVGGT matches the full-precision model (89.4 vs. 89.5 AUC@30). On DTU point map estimation under W4A4, QuantVGGT achieves 1.282 mean accuracy (vs. 1.185 FP), substantially outperforming QuaRot (1.593) and GPTQ (1.442). The W4A4 configuration delivers 3.65× memory compression and 2.49× latency speedup on an RTX 4090 with only 0.2% additional overhead from the dual-smoothing operations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuantVGGT decisively outperforms all nine baselines under the aggressive W4A4 setting, where the gap is largest: it leads QuaRot (the previous SOTA) by 5.1 AUC@30 points (86.9 vs. 81.8) and 20.1 AUC@3 points (43.6 vs. 23.5) on CO3Dv2 10-frame evaluation. SmoothQuant, which relies on channel scaling without rotation, collapses to 75.4 AUC@30 at W4A4—nearly 12 points below QuantVGGT—confirming that rotation is essential for handling VGGT's heavy-tailed special-token distributions. GPTQ and AWQ, designed for LLMs, achieve 80.5 and 81.2 AUC@30 respectively, still 5–6 points behind QuantVGGT. At W8A8, QuantVGGT ties or marginally beats QuaRot (89.4 vs. 89.4 AUC@30) and exceeds SmoothQuant by 0.3 points, indicating near-lossless quantization. On the DTU point map task (calibrated only on CO3Dv2), QuantVGGT at W4A4 achieves 1.282 mean accuracy versus QuaRot's 1.593 and GPTQ's 1.442, demonstrating cross-dataset generalization. The 2D-vision baselines (DopQ-ViT, RepQ-ViT) and generic PTQ methods (RTN, BRECQ, QDrop) all suffer severe degradation at W4A4, with AUC@30 dropping below 83, underscoring that VGGT-specific quantization is necessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) lineage from 2D vision transformers and LLMs (SmoothQuant, QuaRot, GPTQ, BRECQ) to billion-scale 3D reconstruction transformers, specifically VGGT. It departs from prior rotation-based methods (QuaRot, QuIP) by combining pre-global Hadamard rotation with a post-local channel-smoothing step derived from the rotated space, arguing that rotation alone leaves residual per-channel variance that only a second smoothing pass can address. The calibration strategy (NFDS) is a novel departure from standard random or label-based sampling, leveraging VGGT's frame-relative inductive bias to construct semantically meaningful clusters for multi-view 3D data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic PTQ framework for VGGT, a 1.2B-parameter model that unifies depth, point maps, camera poses, and tracking in one forward pass, and the demonstrated 3.7× memory reduction and 2.5× speedup at W4A4 while retaining 98% of full-precision accuracy makes real-world deployment on consumer hardware feasible. The identification of data-independent special tokens as the root cause of quantization-unfriendly heavy-tailed distributions provides a diagnostic that may generalize to other architectures with learned register or CLS tokens. Open questions include whether the frame-aware clustering strategy transfers to architectures without explicit first-frame anchoring, whether the approach scales to even larger 3D models, and whether combining quantization with token-compression methods (e.g., FastVGGT) yields compounding gains.
