## Problem

Standard decoupled weight decay is agnostic to the directional alignment between optimizer updates and parameters: when they conflict in a given coordinate, decay actively resists beneficial movement toward the optimum. Moreover, decoupled weight decay implicitly optimizes a regularized or norm-constrained surrogate (e.g., an ℓ∞ box constraint for ADAMW) rather than the original loss, biasing the solution in a way that depends on the weight decay coefficient. The paper asks whether one can retain the practical stabilization and generalization benefits of weight decay while preserving the original optimization objective.

## Input / Output

CWD consumes the same quantities as any decoupled-weight-decay optimizer step: the current parameter vector x_t, the optimizer-specific update u_t (e.g., bias-corrected momentum divided by a diagonal preconditioner for ADAM, or a Newton–Schulz matrix-sign approximation for MUON), the learning rate η_t, and the existing weight decay coefficient λ. It produces the updated parameter vector x_{t+1} via a single entrywise indicator mask I(u_t ⊙ x_t ≥ 0) applied to the decay term, requiring no additional hyperparameters, state variables, or architectural changes. The modification is a one-line code change that drops into ADAMW, LION, MUON, or any other gradient-based optimizer without retuning.

## Method

Cautious Weight Decay applies the weight decay term λx_t only in coordinates where the optimizer update u_t and the parameter x_t share the same sign (u_t·x_t ≥ 0), suppressing decay wherever the two conflict. The key theoretical insight, established via Lyapunov functions for SGD, SGDM, LION-K, and ADAM, is that this sign-selective masking makes the dynamics unbiased: every accumulation point satisfies ∇f(x*) = 0 of the original loss, unlike standard decoupled decay which converges to a KKT point of a constrained surrogate. Once the trajectory reaches the stationary manifold, CWD induces Filippov sliding-mode dynamics that traverse the manifold while reducing parameter magnitudes, converging to a locally Pareto-optimal stationary point under a coordinatewise magnitude ordering. A discrete-time convergence rate of O(T^{-1/2} + σ/√n_batch) is proven for ADAM+CWD in the smooth nonconvex setting, and experiments across 338M–2B language models and ImageNet classifiers show consistent loss and accuracy improvements over ADAMW, LION, and MUON baselines without any hyperparameter retuning.

## Results

On ImageNet, CWD improves top-1 accuracy across all architectures and optimizers: ViT-B/16 goes from 80.15% (ADAMW) to 80.71%, 80.76% (LION) to 80.92%, and 80.83% (MUON) to 81.04%; ResNet-50 improves from 76.30% to 76.68% (ADAMW). For language model pretraining on C4 at 338M parameters, ADAM+CWD reduces final validation loss from 3.0136 (ADAMW) to 3.0059, and ADAMC+CWD achieves 2.9915. On downstream reasoning benchmarks (OLMo-1B, 100B tokens), ADAMW+CWD improves over ADAMW on all six tasks (e.g., HellaSwag 0.38→0.40, PIQA 0.67→0.69, MMLU 0.23→0.25), and MuON+CWD similarly outperforms MuON (e.g., PIQA 0.68→0.71, CommonsenseQA 0.30→0.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CWD consistently outperforms standard decoupled weight decay (ADAMW, LION, MUON) across all tested settings without any hyperparameter retuning. On ImageNet, CWD yields +0.56 to +0.61 pp gains over ADAMW, +0.16 to +0.53 pp over LION, and +0.21 to +0.56 pp over MUON depending on architecture. On language model pretraining (C4, 338M–2B), CWD lowers final validation loss for all three optimizers, with the gap remaining stable or widening at scale. Against SPD (Tian et al., 2024) in fine-tuning (Table 5), elementwise CWD matches or slightly outperforms SPD on MMLU and WinoGrande for both TinyLlama (26.42 vs 26.14) and Mistral-7B (62.13 vs 62.05), while the inner-product CWD variant underperforms SPD. CWD also outperforms the LoRA baseline in fine-tuning (e.g., TinyLlama MMLU: 26.42 vs 25.81; Mistral-7B MMLU: 62.13 vs 61.78) and improves over no weight decay (λ=0) in ablation studies, confirming that selective regularization is superior to both full and no decay.

## Relation to Prior Work

CWD is a direct, one-line extension of decoupled weight decay (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019), adding a sign-alignment mask between the optimizer update and parameters. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of two threads: the implicit-regularization analyses showing that ADAMW, LION, and MUON with standard decay solve constrained or regularized surrogates (Chen et al., 2024; Xie & Li, 2024), and the cautious-optimizer line of work that gates updates by sign agreement (Liang et al., 2024a). The paper departs from SPD (Tian et al., 2024), which also applies selective decay via a mask, by targeting pre-training rather than fine-tuning, using the optimizer update (not the gradient) for the mask, and providing a Lyapunov/Filippov-based convergence theory that SPD lacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for practitioners: CWD is a drop-in, hyperparameter-free modification that consistently improves final loss and accuracy across ADAMW, LION, and MUON at 338M–2B scale without retuning, making it immediately adoptable in large-scale training pipelines. The theoretical contribution reframes weight decay as a bilevel problem (optimize the original loss, then minimize parameter magnitudes on the stationary manifold), offering a cleaner conceptual picture than the implicit-constraint interpretations of prior work. Open questions include whether the Pareto-front limit point is sensitive to initialization in high-dimensional settings, whether the O(1/√T) discrete-time rate can be improved, and whether the sign-alignment principle generalizes to non-entrywise (e.g., layer-level or block-level) masking in ways that further improve performance.
