## Problem

Large vision-language models achieve strong accuracy, but it remains unclear whether their predictions arise from genuine cross-modal fusion or from reliance on unimodal (especially language) priors. Existing interpretability tools for VLMs are either micro-scope (analyzing one modality in isolation) or rely on ad hoc metrics without firm information-theoretic grounding, leaving the field without a quantitative decomposition of how vision and language jointly contribute to a model's decision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261837/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes image-text pairs in multiple-choice VQA format (2–4 answer options), extracting mean-pooled visual and textual token embeddings as the two source variables X₁ and X₂. Unimodal conditionals P(Y|X₁) and P(Y|X₂) are obtained by replacing the other modality's embedding sequence with calibrated Gaussian noise matched to that modality's per-dimension statistics. Outputs are the four PID atoms {R, U₁, U₂, S} per sample, aggregated into dataset- and model-level information spectra, plus layer-wise traces (via logit-lens projection at each transformer block) and training-stage trajectories across LLaVA-1.5's two-stage recipe.

## Method

The paper adapts the BATCH PID estimator (Liang et al., 2023a)—which learns a parametric joint distribution Q̃ via neural encoders and enforces marginal-matching constraints with the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm—to modern LVLMs through three key design choices: (1) restricting to MC-VQA so the target space Y is finite, avoiding noisy answer clustering; (2) using calibrated-noise embedding masking (following Meng et al., 2022) to approximate unimodal conditioning without architectural changes; and (3) applying a confidence threshold τ on the candidate-set score to replace low-confidence predictions with a uniform distribution, combined with soft aggregation (averaging regularized predictive distributions) to estimate the marginal P(Y) without argmax tie-breaking artifacts. The resulting model-agnostic pipeline requires no retraining or architectural modification and is applied across three analysis dimensions: a breadth comparison of 26 models from 11 families on four benchmarks, a depth analysis of layer-wise information flow via the logit lens, and a temporal trace of fusion emergence across LLaVA-1.5's alignment pretraining and visual instruction tuning stages.

## Results

Across 26 LVLMs on four benchmarks, the paper identifies two task regimes: on synergy-driven tasks (MMBench, POPE), synergy S is the strongest correlate of accuracy (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.75, p < 0.001), while on knowledge-driven tasks (Reefknot, PMC-VQA), language uniqueness U₂ becomes more predictive (ρ = 0.406, p = 0.040 on PMC-VQA). Image-removal intervention validates this: accuracy drop D_vision correlates with S at ρ = 0.809 (MMBench) and 0.744 (POPE), but only ρ = 0.459/0.400 on knowledge-driven tasks. On scaling, accuracy gains within families co-vary with S increases (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision 0.5B→7B: ΔAcc = 11.9%, ΔS = +11.9%, ΔU₂ = −6.5%), not with U₂.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an analysis paper rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the 'comparisons' are cross-model and cross-task contrasts. At the family level, two stable strategies emerge: fusion-centric families (InternVL2.5/3, Qwen2/2.5-VL) show high S and lower U₂, while language-centric families (Gemma-3, Cambrian) show lower S and higher U₂; this split persists across both task regimes. At the task level, MMBench and POPE form a synergy-driven cluster (high S, S–accuracy ρ ≈ 0.75) while Reefknot and PMC-VQA form a knowledge-driven cluster (higher U₂, lower S, accuracies 20–30% lower). On scaling within families, larger checkpoints gain accuracy primarily through increased S rather than increased U₂ (e.g., InternVL2.5 2B→8B: ΔS = +36.8%, ΔU₂ = −55.6%). The layer-wise analysis reveals a consistent three-phase pattern across all tested models, with S peaking in middle-to-late layers and a decisive fusion spike at the final layer. In training dynamics, S remains negligible during alignment pretraining and increases sharply only during visual instruction tuning, with the 7B model showing larger S gains and the 13B model showing larger U₂ gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the BATCH estimator from Liang et al. (2023a) — originally designed for VQA — to the continuous, high-dimensional embeddings of modern LVLMs, and grounds the PID atom definitions in Bertschinger et al. (2014). It departs from the dominant 'micro-scope' interpretability literature (attention maps, attribution heatmaps, linear probes, logit lens) by providing a single, information-theoretic decomposition (R, U₁, U₂, S) that quantitatively separates redundant, unique, and synergistic contributions without ad hoc metrics. The unimodal probing strategy borrows the calibrated-noise corruption scheme from Meng et al. (2022), and the logit-lens technique from nostalgebraist (2020) is repurposed to extract layer-wise PID profiles. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as the first systematic application of PID across the full breadth of modern LVLM families, extending prior PID-in-multimodal-learning work (Liang et al. 2023a,b; 2024) from small VQA models to 26 architectures spanning 0.5B–90B parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, accuracy-agnostic lens for diagnosing *how* LVLMs use multimodal information, which is directly relevant to model design (e.g., using S or U₂ as diagnostic signals during scaling or instruction tuning) and benchmark construction (designing tasks that explicitly stress synergy vs. language priors). Key open questions remain: PID estimation here is restricted to discrete multiple-choice outputs, so open-ended generation is unaddressed; unimodal probes are approximate (calibrated noise rather than truly absent modality); and all findings are correlational rather than causal. The identification of stable family-level strategies (fusion-centric vs. language-centric) and the three-phase layer-wise pattern could inform architectural choices, but whether these patterns persist under different training recipes or in fully open-ended settings is untested.
