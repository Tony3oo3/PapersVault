## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) frequently produce over-long chains-of-thought dominated by unnecessary reflection—repetitive self-questioning, circular reasoning, and unproductive hesitations—that inflate token consumption and latency without improving accuracy, especially in smaller models. Existing efficiency methods either apply static length penalties that suppress all reflection indiscriminately or use training-free inference tricks that offer limited gains, failing to dynamically calibrate how much reflection is warranted for a given problem's complexity.

## Input / Output

The model receives a math problem as a text prompt followed by a  tag that triggers the reasoning phase. The auto-regressive output is structured in two segments: a thinking phase (between  and ) containing exploratory reasoning, reflection, and self-validation, and a solution segment with validated steps and the final answer. Reflection tokens are identified via keyword matching (e.g., 'wait', 'hmm', 'alternatively', 'however') to compute a Reflection Token Count (RTC) that serves as a model-aware complexity signal.

## Method

ARLCP is a reinforcement learning framework that jointly optimizes accuracy and efficiency through a composite reward r(o) = C(o)·(1 − α₁·f(RTC) − α₂·f(LEN)), where C(o) is a binary correctness indicator. The reflection penalty coefficient α₁ is tiered by RTC thresholds (n₁=40, n₂=80) into three complexity levels with weights λ₁=0.05, λ₂=0.1, λ₃=0.15, so harder problems receive stronger reflection penalties. The length penalty coefficient α₂ = α − α₁ (with α=0.2) ensures a complementary constraint on total output length, and both penalties are normalized via sigmoid of the z-score relative to the batch's correct responses. Training uses RLOO (REINFORCE Leave-One-Out) policy gradient over 16 sampled rollouts per prompt, chosen over GRPO to avoid catastrophic collapse under length-penalized objectives. The method is trained on DeepScaleR (40K math problems) and applied to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen 1.5B/7B, reducing average response length by 53.1%/35.0% while improving accuracy by 5.8%/2.7%.

## Results

On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, ARLCP achieves a +5.81% average accuracy improvement while reducing average response length by 53.05% across five math benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH500, AMC 2023, AIME 2024, AIME 2025). On the 7B model, it gains +2.69% accuracy with a 34.96% length reduction. ARLCP attains the best accuracy on all three competition-level benchmarks for the 1.5B model (AMC 2023: 73.28%, AIME 2024: 34.17%, AIME 2025: 26.46%) and the 7B model (AMC 2023: 89.69%, AIME 2024: 56.67%, AIME 2025: 39.58%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARLCP outperforms all five baselines on the joint accuracy–efficiency trade-off. Against LASER (the strongest competitor), ARLCP gains +1.77% accuracy on the 1.5B model (5.81 vs. 4.04) while also reducing length more (−53.05% vs. −38.69%); on 7B, ARLCP leads by +0.65% accuracy (2.69 vs. 2.04) with a slightly larger length reduction (−34.96% vs. −33.97%). Compared to AdaptThink, ARLCP achieves substantially higher accuracy gains (+5.81 vs. +2.23 on 1.5B; +2.69 vs. +1.87 on 7B) with comparable length reductions. TLMRE achieves the largest length reduction on 1.5B (−58.10%) but at the cost of lower accuracy (+1.42 vs. +5.81), and on 7B it trails ARLCP on both metrics. O1-Pruner and DPO_Shortest provide only marginal length savings (−5 to −9%) with modest or negative accuracy changes, while NoThinking (which skips reasoning entirely) serves as a lower bound, dropping accuracy by 12–17%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work on efficient reasoning for LRMs: training-free inference optimizations (early exit, model switching) and training-based length control (SFT with compressed CoT, DPO, RL with length penalties like TLMRE and LASER). It departs from prior RL-based approaches by identifying 'over-reflection'—repetitive self-questioning and circular reasoning—as a distinct, complexity-correlated failure mode, and by repurposing reflection token counts as an online complexity estimator to dynamically allocate penalty weight between a reflection-specific term and a general length term. The choice of RLOO over GRPO is motivated by an observed instability of GRPO under length-penalized objectives, a practical finding that extends the RL-for-reasoning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 53% length reduction with simultaneous accuracy gains on small models is practically significant for resource-constrained deployment of reasoning models, and the finding that excessive reflection actively degrades accuracy (rather than merely wasting tokens) reframes how practitioners should think about CoT length. Limitations include reliance on a fixed, hand-curated reflection keyword list, evaluation primarily on mathematical benchmarks (with MMLU as a single generalization check), and testing on only two model families. Open questions include whether the RTC-based complexity proxy transfers to non-mathematical or multimodal reasoning, and whether the three-tier threshold scheme scales gracefully as model capabilities and reflection patterns evolve.
