## Problem

Existing graph-based RAG methods (e.g., GraphRAG, LightRAG) rely on static, query-agnostic exploration strategies—uniform community detection or fixed-hop neighborhood expansion—that retrieve structurally connected but semantically irrelevant regions, and they lack any theoretical guarantees on subgraph quality or relevance. The paper asks: under what conditions can we establish provable recovery guarantees for retrieving subgraphs that adapt to a query's holistic meaning in graph-based RAG?

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language query and a pre-built knowledge graph with node embeddings (default: text-embedding-3-small, 1536-dim) as input. It first uses an LLM to extract keywords from the query, then selects top-N seed nodes by embedding similarity. The output is a query-relevant subgraph with per-node importance scores (x-vector from the dual solution), which is serialized into a prompt for a downstream LLM to generate a grounded answer. For complex multi-hop queries (e.g., Spider 2.0), the query is decomposed into K subqueries, each producing its own subgraph, and the final retrieval is their union. The entire process is training-free and the diffusion complexity scales with the explored subgraph size rather than the full graph.

## Method

QAFD-RAG reformulates graph traversal as query-aware flow diffusion: mass is injected at seed nodes and propagated along edges whose weights are dynamically reweighted by a query-aware function blending structural similarity (node-node embedding similarity) with query alignment (node-query embedding similarity), using a hybrid multiplicative form w̄_Hybrid = H_sim(h(u),h(v))·(a + b·(H_sim(h(u),h(q)) + H_sim(h(v),h(q)))). This turns diffusion into a semantic filter that amplifies edges between query-relevant nodes while exponentially suppressing edges to irrelevant regions. The diffusion is cast as a constrained optimization (minimize ½fᵀW̄f subject to mass conservation Δ + BW̄f ≤ T) and solved efficiently via a push-relabel algorithm on the dual objective, yielding node importance scores. The paper provides the first statistical guarantees for query-aware graph retrieval: exponential convergence to a unique query-dependent stationary distribution (Theorem 3) and high-probability recovery of the relevant subgraph under mild signal-to-noise conditions (Theorem 7), with the trade-off parameter β controlling the precision-recall balance between complete recovery and limited leakage into irrelevant regions.

## Results

On multi-hop QA, QAFD-RAG achieves F1=73.42 and EM=58.10 on HotpotQA, F1=47.99 and EM=33.50 on MuSiQue, and F1=69.41 on 2WikiMultiHopQA, outperforming the strongest baseline HippoRAG (F1=58.67, 38.33, 70.33 respectively) by 14.75, 9.66, and −0.92 F1 points. On Spider 2.0 text-to-SQL, QAFD-RAG+SQL-Agent reaches 26.70% execution accuracy on SQLite and 23.70% on Snowflake, surpassing Spider-Agent (21.50%, 16.30%) and all other baselines. Across ten UltraDomain QA subsets, QAFD-RAG attains the highest mean scores on Comprehensiveness, Logicality, and Coherence in 9 of 10 domains (e.g., 89.93 vs. 87.30 on Agriculture Comprehensiveness).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QAFD-RAG decisively outperforms GraphRAG, LightRAG, and RAPTOR across all evaluated tasks; on HotpotQA it gains +14.75 F1 over HippoRAG (the strongest graph-based RAG baseline) and +21.12 over RAPTOR. On 2WikiMultiHopQA, QAFD-RAG (F1=69.41) is marginally behind HippoRAG (F1=70.33) by 0.92 points, the only loss among the three multi-hop benchmarks. On Spider 2.0 text-to-SQL, QAFD-RAG+SQL-Agent beats Spider-Agent by +5.20% (SQLite) and +7.40% (Snowflake) execution accuracy, while CHASE-SQL, DIN-SQL, DAIL-SQL, and CodeS all score below 12% on SQLite. On UltraDomain general QA, QAFD-RAG leads on Comprehensiveness, Logicality, and Coherence in 9/10 domains, with GraphRAG occasionally matching or slightly exceeding it on Relevance (e.g., Physics: 94.73 vs. 95.61). LightRAG performs particularly poorly on multi-hop tasks (F1=8.80 on HotpotQA), confirming that single-hop ego-network retrieval is insufficient for compositional reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical flow diffusion framework from local graph clustering (Spielman & Teng 2013; Wang et al. 2017; Fountoulakis et al. 2023) to the retrieval-augmented generation setting by introducing query-aware edge reweighting that modulates mass propagation based on semantic alignment between graph nodes and the query embedding. It departs from existing graph-based RAG systems (GraphRAG's static community detection, LightRAG's single-hop ego-networks, HippoRAG's query-agnostic Personalized PageRank) by making traversal dynamics explicitly query-dependent through a primal-dual optimization formulation solved via push-relabel. The key conceptual departure is treating edges as query-aware gates rather than fixed structural weights, transforming diffusion into a semantic filter with provable recovery guarantees under a random graph model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first statistical guarantees (Theorem 7) showing that query-aware flow diffusion recovers relevant subgraphs with high probability under mild signal-to-noise conditions, addressing a fundamental gap in graph-based RAG where prior methods are purely heuristic. The training-free, modular design means QAFD-RAG can replace or augment retrieval components in existing systems (GraphRAG, HippoRAG, LightRAG) without retraining, while its complexity scaling with retrieved subgraph size rather than full graph enables practical deployment on large knowledge graphs. Limitations include reliance on pre-trained embeddings that may underperform in domain-specific settings and difficulty handling explicit logical negation; open questions include learning edge weights from query-answer pairs, extending to temporal and multi-modal graphs, and developing contrastive or hybrid symbolic-neural embeddings that encode logical distinctions during traversal.
