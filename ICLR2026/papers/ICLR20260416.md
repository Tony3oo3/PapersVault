## Problem

Linear attention achieves O(N) complexity by compressing all tokens into a single global key-value summary shared by every query, but this design causes 'global context collapse': the attention matrix rank is strictly bounded by the head dimension d regardless of sequence length, and attention distributions become increasingly uniform as N grows. Existing remedies such as depthwise convolutions or gating modules partially recover performance but re-introduce computational overhead that undermines the efficiency gains of linear attention.

## Input / Output

MHLA is a drop-in replacement for the attention module inside Transformer and Diffusion Transformer backbones, consuming standard projected Q, K, V tensors of shape N×d and producing the same N×d output. In vision tasks, the N tokens correspond to spatial patches (e.g., 196 for 224×224 DeiT, 1024 for 512×512 DiT) or spatiotemporal patches (31,500 for 81-frame 480×800 video), and the M token-level heads are defined on 2D/3D spatial grids rather than 1D flattening. In NLP, MHLA operates on autoregressive text sequences up to 2048 tokens with M=32 heads, supporting both chunkwise-parallel training and streaming inference with per-token O(d²) cost.

## Method

MHLA partitions the N-token sequence into M non-overlapping spatial blocks, computes a local d×d key-value summary S_b and normalizer z_b for each block, then introduces a learnable M×M coefficient matrix M_c whose i-th row specifies how query-block i linearly combines all M local summaries into a query-specific mixture S̃_i = Σ_b m_{i,b} S_b. This 'Multi-Head Mixing' step is a single GEMM between the coefficient matrix and the stacked summaries, after which each query in block i performs the standard kernel inner product against S̃_i, yielding a two-stage weighting: block-level selection via m_{i,b} followed by intra-block token reweighting via q̃ᵀK̃_t. The rank of the resulting attention matrix is bounded by Σ_b min(n_b, d) rather than d, and the mixing coefficients are initialized with a locality bias (proportional to 1 − dist(i,j)/max_dist) and clipped to (0,1) during training. The overall time complexity remains O(Nd² + M²d²), which reduces to O(Nd²) when M² ≤ N, and the implementation relies solely on standard GEMMs with no auxiliary convolutional or self-attention modules.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, MHLA achieves 75.8% Top-1 accuracy on DeiT-T (vs. 72.2% self-attention and 69.8% vanilla linear attention) and 81.0% on DeiT-S, while using the same parameter count (5.7M/22M) and FLOPs (1.1G/4.2G) as the baselines. For class-to-image generation, MHLA reduces FID from 68.40 to 59.80 on DiT-S/2 at 256×256 and from 19.47 to 19.17 on DiT-XL/2, matching or surpassing self-attention at linear-time complexity. In video generation (Wan2.1-1.3B, 31,500-token sequences), MHLA achieves a VBench total score of 82.62 with 2.1× inference speedup over FlashAttention, compared to only 58.24 for vanilla linear attention. On NLP (340M model, 10B tokens), MHLA attains the best LongBench average score of 7.41 and competitive perplexity (38.31 WikiText, 71.64 LAMBADA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MHLA outperforms all linear-attention baselines on ImageNet classification: it beats Focused LA (74.1%), Inline Attn (74.5%), and MALA (75.1%) on DeiT-T while using fewer parameters (5.7M vs. 6.1–6.5M), and surpasses RALA (80.4%) and MALA (80.3%) on DeiT-S with fewer FLOPs (4.2G vs. 4.6G). Against self-attention, MHLA achieves a 3.6-point gain on DeiT-T (75.8 vs. 72.2) and a 1.2-point gain on DeiT-S (81.0 vs. 79.8) at the same computational cost. In image generation, MHLA substantially outperforms vanilla linear attention (FID 59.80 vs. 89.72 on DiT-S/2) and matches or beats self-attention (FID 19.17 vs. 19.47 on DiT-XL/2); it also surpasses GLA (FID 59.49 vs. 62.06 on DiG-S/2) and LiT (FID 19.16 vs. 20.66 on XL/2). For video generation, MHLA recovers 99% of FlashAttention's VBench score (82.62 vs. 83.31) at 2.1× speedup, while vanilla linear attention collapses to 58.24. In NLP, MHLA achieves the best LongBench average (7.41 vs. 6.97 Mamba, 6.92 Transformer++, 6.86 GDN) and the highest CSR average (47.1%), though its LAMBADA perplexity (71.64) trails Mamba2 (58.51) and GDN (60.16). On T2I, SANA-MHLA improves FID from 6.10 to 5.90 and GenEval from 0.64 to 0.68 over the SANA baseline, also surpassing PixArt-α and PixArt-σ.

## Relation to Prior Work

MHLA extends the kernelized linear attention framework (Katharopoulos et al., 2020) by identifying 'global context collapse'—the rank and sparsity degradation caused by a single shared KV summary—as the root cause of linear attention's performance gap. Prior remedies (FL-PVT's depthwise convolutions, GLA's gating, MALA/RALA's hybrid modules) patch this gap with auxiliary components that add overhead; MHLA instead partitions tokens into spatial blocks and introduces a learnable M×M mixing matrix that lets each query block form its own weighted combination of local summaries, restoring query-conditioned selectivity with only standard GEMMs. The rank analysis builds on Bhojanapalli et al. (2020) and the chunkwise parallel training scheme of Hua et al. (2022) / Sun et al. (2023), which MHLA naturally subsumes by mapping heads to chunks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MHLA offers a drop-in, module-free replacement for linear attention that matches or exceeds self-attention quality while preserving O(N) complexity, making it immediately applicable to high-resolution image generation, long video synthesis, and long-context language modeling where quadratic attention is prohibitive. The 2× inference speedup on 31K-token video generation with near-identical VBench scores, and the FID improvement on DiT-XL/2 without any auxiliary module, suggest it could become a default attention choice in diffusion-transformer pipelines. Key open questions include scaling NLP validation beyond 0.3B parameters, whether the mixing matrix benefits from richer (e.g., content-dependent) parameterization, and how MHLA interacts with other efficiency techniques like FlashAttention-style IO optimization at the kernel level.
