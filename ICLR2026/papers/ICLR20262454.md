## Problem

Diffusion models trained on class-imbalanced datasets suffer severe performance degradation on minority classes, yet prior work has focused almost exclusively on loss-function redesign (e.g., CBDM, OC). This paper identifies a previously underexplored orthogonal factor: majority classes monopolize a disproportionate share of model capacity during training, starving minority classes of representational resources. The authors provide both empirical evidence (pruning sensitivity) and a gradient-based theoretical analysis showing that majority-driven updates dominate parameter directions.

## Input / Output

The method operates on class-conditional image generation: at training, it consumes noisy images (x_t), timesteps, and class labels (or text prompts like 'a {class} painting' for Stable Diffusion fine-tuning); at inference, it takes random Gaussian noise plus a class condition and outputs a generated image. Resolutions span 32×32 (CIFAR, ImageNet-LT), 64×64 (CelebA-HQ, iNaturalist), and 256×256 (ArtBench-10). A key practical detail is that at inference the decomposed parameters θ^g and θ^e are merged into a single θ, so the model incurs zero additional latency compared to a standard diffusion model of the same size.

## Method

Capacity Manipulation (CM) decomposes every weight matrix W in the U-Net into a full-rank component W^g (for majority and general knowledge) and a low-rank component W^e = BA with rank r < min(d,k) (reserved for minority expertise), applied only to the upsampling half of the network. A capacity manipulation loss then enforces the allocation: a consistency term pulls the full model output ε_{θ^g⊕θ^e} toward the majority-only output ε_{θ^g} for majority classes, while a diversity term pushes them apart for minority classes, with class-frequency-based weights ω_Con ∝ N_c and ω_Div ∝ 1/N_c that smoothly adapt to imbalance without manual thresholds. The total objective combines a standard (or imbalanced-aware) diffusion base loss with λ·L_CM, and the low-rank rank r is set to 1/10 of min(d,k). Theoretically, Theorem 3.1 shows that the reserved low-rank component reduces the probability of majority-dominated updates from Φ((2a−1)μ√(2N(1−cos θ))/2σ) to a tighter bound scaled by (1−α_r), where α_r grows with rank r, directly quantifying how capacity reservation rebalances gradient dominance.

## Results

On Imb. CIFAR-100 (IR=100), CM achieves FID 7.519 and KID 0.0017, outperforming DDPM (FID 10.163) by 2.644 and the best baseline OC (FID 8.309) by 0.790. On Imb. CIFAR-10 (IR=100), CM reaches FID 7.727, improving over DDPM by 2.970 and over CBDM (FID 8.233) by 0.506. Across all four CIFAR settings (IR={50,100} × {CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100}), CM achieves the best FID on every configuration, with gains of 2.5–2.9 over DDPM and 0.46–0.79 over the strongest baseline. On large-scale datasets (ImageNet-LT IR=256, iNaturalist IR=500), CM also consistently leads, e.g., FID 10.94 vs. 13.42 (DDPM) and 12.61 (OC) on ImageNet-LT 32×32.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CM outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and imbalance ratio tested. Against the vanilla DDPM baseline, CM reduces FID by 2.5–3.0 points on CIFAR-10/100, by 1.2–0.8 on CelebA-HQ (with a 3.6-point improvement on the minority Male class), and by 2.5–2.3 on ImageNet-LT/iNaturalist. Against the strongest imbalanced-diffusion baselines CBDM and OC, CM achieves consistent FID gains of 0.46–0.79 on CIFAR, 0.23–0.29 on CelebA-HQ, and 1.7–2.3 on large-scale datasets. The re-sampling (+RS) and focal loss (+Focal) baselines, borrowed from discriminative/GAN literature, actually degrade or barely improve over DDPM, confirming their incompatibility with diffusion training. On Imb. ArtBench-10 (Stable Diffusion fine-tuned via LoRA), CM achieves FID 22.776 vs. 24.315 (OC) and 27.083 (DDPM) at IR=100. The concurrent Overlap Optimization method (Yan et al., 2024) is also outperformed by CM across all reported settings (Sec. G.10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of imbalanced diffusion model research (CBDM, OC) that focuses on loss function design, instead introducing model capacity allocation as an orthogonal axis of intervention. It repurposes the low-rank decomposition machinery from LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) not for efficient fine-tuning but as a structural mechanism to pre-reserve a dedicated parameter subspace for minority expertise before training begins. The capacity manipulation loss (consistency + diversity terms with class-frequency-dependent weights) is novel, and the theoretical analysis (Theorems 2.1 and 3.1) formalizes why majority classes monopolize parameter updates under standard training, providing a mechanistic explanation that prior loss-based methods did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The capacity allocation perspective is genuinely orthogonal to existing loss-design approaches, enabling CM to stack on top of CBDM or OC for further gains without architectural changes or inference overhead. The zero-overhead property (merged weights at inference) makes it practically deployable, and the extension to LoRA-finetuning of Stable Diffusion broadens its applicability to real-world pipelines. Open questions include whether the theoretical assumptions (Gaussian gradient distributions, equal mean magnitudes across classes) hold in practice for high-dimensional networks, whether the rank hyperparameter generalizes across architectures without retuning, and whether the capacity reservation principle transfers to other generative paradigms (e.g., autoregressive or flow-based models). The paper also notes that CBDM degrades on large-scale datasets (ImageNet-LT, iNaturalist), suggesting that loss-based corrections alone may be insufficient at scale.
