## Problem

Offline RL datasets often exhibit complex, multi-modal action distributions that simple Gaussian policies cannot capture, while diffusion-based expressive policies suffer from unstable iterative optimization. Although recent flow-based policies (FQL) address expressiveness, they only use the flow model for actor regularization and overlook its key advantage: providing an accurate behavior density estimate that can directly identify out-of-distribution regions for critic penalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264109/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') tuples and a state observation at inference time, producing a single continuous action via a one-step flow policy. It handles both low-dimensional state-based observations (MuJoCo, Antmaze, Adroit) and high-dimensional pixel-based RGB observations (OGBench manipulation tasks). The flow behavior proxy is pre-trained on the full dataset to precompute density evaluations for all state-action pairs, which are then stored and used as lookup weights during critic training.

## Method

FAC jointly exploits a flow behavior proxy policy (trained via flow matching) for two complementary roles: (1) a critic penalization weight w = max(0, 1 − β̂(a|s)/ε) that vanishes in well-supported regions and grows linearly in OOD regions, directly suppressing Q-value overestimation without the importance-sampling instability of SVR; and (2) an actor distance regularizer that constrains a one-step flow actor to the multi-step flow behavior proxy. The critic loss is α·E[w·Q(s,a)] + TD loss, and the actor loss is E[Q(s,a)] − λ·E‖a_θ(s,z) − a_ψ(s,z)‖². A theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) shows the induced operator preserves the original Bellman operator in high-confidence ID regions while driving Q to −∞ in true OOD regions, with a unique fixed point yielding unbiased values where β̂ ≥ ε.

## Results

On the OGBench benchmark (55 singletasks), FAC achieves an average state-based score of 60.3, substantially outperforming the best flow-policy baseline FQL (43.6), the best diffusion-policy baseline CAC (25.3), and the best Gaussian-policy baseline ReBRAC (31.0). On D4RL, FAC attains 90.5 on Antmaze (vs. FQL 83.5, ReBRAC 76.9) and 88.2 on MuJoCo (competitive with EPQ 90.9 and MCQ 88.5). FAC achieves perfect 100.0 on puzzle-3x3-play and 92.6 on antmaze-large-navigate, demonstrating strong performance on combinatorial and high-dimensional navigation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAC outperforms all 13 baselines on the OGBench average, with the largest margins over FQL (+16.7), ReBRAC (+29.3), and CAC (+35.0). Among flow-policy baselines, FAC's advantage over FQL is most pronounced on navigation (e.g., antmaze-large-navigate: 92.6 vs. 78.6) and manipulation (puzzle-3x3-play: 100.0 vs. 29.6), confirming that the flow-based critic penalization is the key differentiator beyond actor regularization alone. On D4RL Antmaze, FAC leads all baselines (90.5 vs. FQL 83.5, ReBRAC 76.9, IQL 63.0), while on MuJoCo it is competitive with the strongest Gaussian baselines (EPQ 90.9, MCQ 88.5) but slightly trails them. On Adroit, FAC is comparable to ReBRAC (25.5) and FQL (25.9) in overall average, with notable wins on pen-cloned (103.2 vs. 91.8 for EPQ) and hammer-human (8.6 vs. 0.2 for ReBRAC). The multi-step flow baselines (FAWAC, FBRAC, IFQL) and diffusion baselines (IDQL, SRPO, CAC) are consistently outperformed, highlighting the practical difficulty of optimizing iterative generators compared to the one-step flow actor used in FAC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends FQL (Park et al., 2025), which introduced a one-step flow actor with distance regularization to a flow behavior proxy, by additionally exploiting the flow model's density estimation capability for critic penalization. The density evaluation relies on the instantaneous change of variables from continuous normalizing flows (Chen et al., 2018), while the flow behavior proxy is trained via flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022). The key departure from prior flow-based methods is the joint use of the same flow model for both actor regularization and OOD-aware critic penalization, whereas FQL and other flow methods (FAWAC, FBRAC, IFQL) only leverage the flow for the actor. This contrasts with diffusion-based methods (IDQL, SRPO, CAC) that can sample expressively but cannot provide reliable density estimates for direct OOD identification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a long-standing difficulty in offline RL—directly identifying OOD regions—by showing that flow models uniquely provide both expressive multi-modal sampling and accurate density estimation, a combination unavailable in diffusion or Gaussian models. The theoretical analysis (Proposition 1, Theorem 1) rigorously characterizes the resulting operator, showing it preserves the Bellman operator in high-confidence regions while conservatively suppressing values elsewhere. Limitations include reliance on the fidelity of the flow density estimate (requiring ≥10 Euler steps), no mechanism for beneficial exploration in online settings, and evaluation restricted to simulated benchmarks. Open questions include robustness of the behavior proxy under distribution shift and whether the flow-based penalization can be extended to real-world or multi-objective settings.
