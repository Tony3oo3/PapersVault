## Problem

Current LLM safety monitors are inflexible: external LLM-as-judges impose a large fixed cost on every query, while linear activation probes provide only static, single-strength guardrails that cannot scale with compute or adapt to input difficulty. The paper asks whether a single monitor can be evaluated at varying levels of compute, spending more only when inputs are ambiguous or when stronger safety guarantees are required.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261877/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single D-dimensional vector per prompt, obtained by mean-pooling the residual stream activations at a chosen layer of the target LLM (e.g., D=5376 for gemma-3-27b-it). It produces a scalar safety logit (sigmoided to a harmfulness probability). Unusually, the same trained model yields N nested sub-classifiers: evaluating only the first n≤N polynomial terms gives a lighter-weight prediction, while all N terms give the strongest guardrail, so the 'output' is a family of predictions parameterized by test-time compute.

## Method

The Truncated Polynomial Classifier (TPC) is a degree-N polynomial in the LLM activation vector, where the k-th order term models all k-way multiplicative interactions between neurons. Two key design choices make it practical: (1) a symmetric CP decomposition parameterizes each degree-k weight tensor with a single D×R factor matrix and R coefficients (rather than k separate matrices), exploiting the permutation symmetry of monomials to cut parameters and simplify attribution; (2) a progressive training scheme learns each degree-k term sequentially with all lower-order terms frozen, guaranteeing that every truncation P_{:n} is a competent classifier. At inference, the TPC supports two modes: a 'safety dial' where the user fixes the evaluation order n to trade compute for accuracy, and a cascading defense (Algorithm 1) where each input exits early once the sigmoid of the partial logit falls outside an uncertainty band (τ, 1−τ), spending higher-order compute only on ambiguous prompts. The polynomial form also provides built-in pairwise (and higher-order) neuron attribution, since each term's contribution to the logit can be isolated analytically.

## Results

On WildGuardMix, TPCs (5th order, CP rank 64) achieve the best test F1 score across all four LLMs: 88.86 on gemma-3-27b-it, 85.57 on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Base, 88.05 on gpt-oss-20b, and 84.48 on Llama-3.2-3B. TPCs outperform linear probes by up to 1.35 F1 points (gpt-oss-20b) and MLP baselines by up to 0.71 points (Llama-3.2-3B) at full static evaluation. In cascaded mode, TPCs achieve performance on par with the full polynomial while requiring only slightly more net parameters than the linear probe. On certain harm subcategories (e.g., private information, social stereotypes), the full degree-5 TPC brings up to 10% accuracy improvement over linear probes and up to 6% over EE-MLPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPCs outperform all four probe baselines on test F1 across every model. Against the linear probe (Alain & Bengio, 2017), TPCs gain +0.83 F1 on gemma-3-27b-it, +1.35 on gpt-oss-20b, and +1.24 on Llama-3.2-3B, with the smallest margin (+0.04) on Qwen3. Against the bilinear probe (Hewitt & Liang, 2019), TPCs win on three of four models (gemma-3: +0.07, gpt-oss: +0.92, Llama-3.2: −0.30) but lose slightly on Qwen3 (−0.70). Against the parameter-matched MLP and EE-MLP baselines (Teerapittayanon et al., 2016), TPCs consistently win: +0.37 to +0.74 over EE-MLP and +0.09 to +0.71 over MLP across models. In the LLM-as-monitor comparison (Table 4), the TPC (1.38M params, F1 88.50) outperforms gpt-4o-mini (~8B params, F1 86.63), claude-3-haiku (~8B, F1 83.24), Llama-Guard-3-8B (F1 76.85), and shieldgemma-2b (F1 69.90) on the WildGuardMix test set, despite having orders of magnitude fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear probe paradigm (Alain & Bengio, 2017) by replacing the single linear decision boundary with a degree-N polynomial that models higher-order neuron interactions, parameterized via symmetric CP decomposition following Dubey et al. (2022) and Chrysos et al. (2020). The key departure from prior polynomial networks is the introduction of progressive (degree-wise) training, which ensures every truncated submodel is a competent classifier, combined with an early-exit cascading strategy borrowed from BranchNet (Teerapittayanon et al., 2016) to make the polynomial a dynamic, compute-scalable safety monitor. Unlike prior two-stage cascade approaches (McKenzie et al., 2025; Cunningham et al., 2025) that chain a cheap probe with an expensive external LLM, TPCs learn the entire dynamic defense within a single polynomial in activation space, preserving interpretability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cascading defense result—achieving near-full-polynomial F1 at only slightly more net compute than a linear probe—offers a practically deployable path to stronger safety guardrails without proportional cost increases, which is significant for always-on monitoring at scale. The built-in pairwise neuron attribution provides mechanistic transparency that black-box MLP probes cannot match, addressing a real interpretability gap in safety monitoring. Key limitations include unexplored small-data regimes, dense (non-sparse) feature combinations that lack human legibility, non-monotonic performance with additional compute, and the need for per-model layer search. Open questions include whether TPCs generalize to response-level monitoring, whether SAE-feature bases or sparsity constraints can make attributions more readable, and whether more sophisticated progressive training can fix the non-monotonicity issue.
