## Problem

While temporal shift (non-stationarity) in time-series forecasting has received substantial attention, concept drift—where the conditional relationship between exogenous features and the target changes over time—has been largely overlooked. Existing invariant-learning methods for concept drift in general ML require environment labels or assume full feature accessibility, making them inapplicable to standard time-series forecasting, and the few time-series-specific concept drift methods are restricted to online settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263164/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a lookback window of historical exogenous features X^L and target series Y^L, and outputs a forecasted target horizon Y^H. During training, the horizon exogenous features X^H are also available to identify invariant patterns, but at test time the model must predict a compressed surrogate feature X^SUR from the lookback alone. The framework is model-agnostic: it wraps around any forecasting model (e.g., Crossformer, PatchTST, iTransformer) and operates on univariate targets with one or more exogenous covariates.

## Method

ShifTS combines two components in a unified pipeline. First, Soft Attention Masking (SAM) concatenates [X^L, X^H], slices the result with a sliding window of size H to produce L+1 local patterns, then applies a learnable attention matrix M with softmax, mean-threshold sparsity, and L1 normalization to identify invariant patterns whose correlation with the target remains stable across all time steps; the weighted sum of these patterns forms a surrogate feature X^SUR, and the model learns the stable conditional P(Y^H | X^SUR) rather than the unstable P(Y^H | X^L). Second, Reversible Instance Normalization (RevIN) is applied before and after the forecasting model to stabilize marginal distributions, addressing temporal shift as a prerequisite for concept drift mitigation. Training minimizes a combined loss of surrogate prediction error (MSE between X^SUR and its estimate) and standard forecasting MSE, and the entire framework is plug-and-play with any base forecasting architecture.

## Results

Using Crossformer as the base model, ShifTS achieves the best averaged MSE on ILI (0.668), ETTh1 (0.076), and ETTh2 (0.194), and the best averaged MAE on ILI (0.613), Exchange (0.468), ETTh1 (0.214), and ETTh2 (0.348) out of 8 total evaluations. ShifTS outperforms the SOTA combined method FOIL on all 8 dataset-metric combinations, with the largest margin on ILI (MSE 0.668 vs. 0.735, a 9.1% reduction). When applied as a plug-in to agnostic forecasting models, ShifTS yields up to 81.9% MSE reduction over ERM on ILI with Crossformer, and still provides 13.8–15.2% MSE improvements on the SOTA iTransformer model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShifTS dominates all four concept drift baselines (GroupDRO, IRM, VREx, EIIL) by large margins on every dataset and metric; for example, on ILI MSE ShifTS achieves 0.668 versus 2.036–2.285 for the concept drift methods. Against the three temporal shift baselines, ShifTS wins on 7 of 8 metrics, losing only to SAN on Exchange MSE (0.470 vs. 0.415), though the authors show that swapping RevIN for SAN inside ShifTS closes this gap (0.407). ShifTS outperforms the SOTA combined method FOIL on all 8 dataset-metric evaluations, with the largest advantage on ILI (MSE 0.668 vs. 0.735; MAE 0.613 vs. 0.651). The two combined baselines IRM+RevIN and EIIL+RevIN are consistently worse than ShifTS, confirming that naively stacking an invariant-learning method on top of RevIN is inferior to the unified SAM+RevIN design. Overall, ShifTS achieves the best result in 6 of 8 evaluations and ties for best in the remaining 2 (Exchange MSE, ETTh2 MAE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on RevIN (Kim et al., 2021) as the temporal shift mitigation component and departs from conventional invariant learning approaches (IRM, GroupDRO, VREx, EIIL) that require explicit environment labels unavailable in time-series data. Rather than applying invariant learning directly, the authors propose Soft Attention Masking (SAM) to identify invariant patterns across both lookback and horizon windows, constructing a surrogate feature that enables stable conditional distribution modeling without environment labels. The framework also positions itself against FOIL (Liu et al., 2024a), the prior SOTA combined distribution shift method for time series, arguing that temporal shift must be resolved as a prerequisite before concept drift can be effectively addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a recognized gap by bringing concept drift mitigation to standard (offline) time-series forecasting, where prior work has focused almost exclusively on temporal shift or online settings. The model-agnostic design makes ShifTS broadly applicable, and the mutual information analysis provides practical guidance on when the method yields the largest gains. However, the lack of theoretical guarantees bounding the error reduction from concept drift mitigation, and the observation that concept drift baselines (IRM, GroupDRO, etc.) perform comparably to or worse than ERM on several datasets, suggest that in practice temporal shift may dominate, leaving open the question of how much independent value concept drift mitigation provides beyond what normalization already captures.
