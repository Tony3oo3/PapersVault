## Problem

Transition Matching (TM) generalizes diffusion and flow-matching models by using a second internal generative model (the 'head') to produce transition steps, but the head has been treated as a fixed, minimal component in prior work without systematic investigation. This paper asks: which design choices for the head—architecture type, size, sequence scaling, batch size, time weighting—and which inference strategies actually improve generation quality and efficiency in continuous-time bidirectional TM?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264966/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt as conditioning and produces a 256×256 RGB image. Images are encoded via SDXL-VAE into a 32×32×4 latent space, then 2×2 patched into 256 tokens of dimension 16 (16×16×16). The backbone is a fixed 1.7B-parameter DiT transformer; the variable component under study is the head, which maps backbone latents to the next-state prediction Y. Evaluation spans four prompt datasets (MS-COCO, PartiPrompts, GenEval, T2ICompBench) with 25 metrics aggregated into a single rank score.

## Method

The paper conducts a large-scale ablation (56 unique 1.7B model trains, 549 evaluations) over the head module in D-TM, where the head predicts Y = X₁ − X₀ via a flow-matching loss conditioned on backbone latents. Key findings: (i) head size does not strongly correlate with performance—smaller heads suffice; (ii) sequence scaling (expanding tokens before the head) significantly benefits Transformer heads but not MLP heads; (iii) head batch size k_h=16 and lognormal time weighting for both backbone and head improve quality; (iv) a novel stochastic sampler that re-noises intermediate states using a modified supervision process at inference yields a substantial quality boost (MLP head reaches rank 0.66, DTM++) at zero additional computational cost, while a Transformer head with sequence scaling (DTM+, rank 0.58) excels specifically at image aesthetics.

## Results

The best model, DTM++ (MLP head, head batch size 16, lognormal time weighting, high-frequency stochastic sampling with c=0.2, τ=1), achieves an aggregated rank of 0.66 across 25 metrics on 4 datasets, outperforming all baselines. The runner-up DTM+ (Transformer head, sequence scale 4, low-frequency stochastic sampling c=0.8, τ=32) reaches rank 0.58 and leads on image aesthetics (Aesthetic 5.88, PickScore 21.6 on MS-COCO). The original D-TM baseline (Shaul et al., 2025) with uniform time weighting and batch size 4 scores 0.36, while flow matching with lognormal weighting reaches 0.28 and all autoregressive/masked-autoregressive baselines remain below 0.20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTM++ (rank 0.66) substantially outperforms the original D-TM baseline from Shaul et al. (2025) (rank 0.36) by +0.30, primarily through lognormal time weighting, larger head batch size (16 vs. 4), and stochastic sampling. Against flow matching, DTM++ beats the lognormal-weighted FM variant (0.28) by +0.38 and the uniform FM variant (0.16) by +0.50. The Dense variant from Zhang et al. (2025), which uses a single backbone for both roles, scores only 0.25, confirming that the separate backbone-head decomposition is critical. All autoregressive baselines—discrete AR (0.17), continuous AR (0.08), discrete MAR (0.19), and continuous MAR (0.19)—are consistently outperformed by at least 0.47 rank. The Transformer-head DTM+ (0.58) excels specifically on aesthetic quality (Aesthetic 5.88, PickScore 21.6 on MS-COCO) but trails DTM++ on text-adherence metrics (CLIPScore, ImageReward) and GenEval, suggesting the token-wise MLP head provides a useful inductive bias for prompt following.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first systematic design-space exploration of the head module in Transition Matching (TM), building directly on the TM framework of Shaul et al. (2025) and the flow-matching training objective of Lipman et al. (2022). Prior TM and continuous-autoregressive works (Li et al., 2024; Fan et al.; Team et al., 2025) treated the head as a fixed, minimal MLP without investigating how architecture, size, time weighting, or batch size affect performance. The stochastic sampling algorithm draws on the restart-sampling idea of Xu et al. (2023b) but adapts it to the D-TM setting with a simpler two-parameter formulation that incurs no extra NFE cost, in contrast to the EDM-based restart procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The study provides the first actionable guidelines for TM practitioners: a medium-size MLP head with log-normal time weighting and high-frequency stochastic sampling achieves the best overall rank, while a Transformer head with sequence scaling excels in aesthetics. The counterintuitive finding that a token-wise MLP outperforms a cross-attention Transformer in text adherence is an open question the authors flag for future investigation. Limitations include evaluation at only 256×256 resolution with a single DiT backbone and a single 350M-pair dataset, so whether these design choices transfer to higher resolutions, video, or other modalities remains unknown.
