## Problem

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) suffers from the squeezing effect (likelihood displacement), where the probability of preferred responses unintentionally decreases during training, leading to performance degradation and alignment failure. While this phenomenon was recently identified, its underlying mechanism in terms of learning dynamics remained unexplained, and no optimization-based remedy existed. This paper asks whether curvature-aware training can suppress the unintended drift caused by negative-gradient updates on rejected responses.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard DPO preference pairs (prompt x, chosen response y⁺, rejected response y⁻) along with a frozen SFT reference policy π_ref. The key implementation detail is that the SAM perturbation is applied exclusively to the output-layer weight matrix W (e.g., 4.64% of parameters in Pythia-2.8B, 1.81% in Mistral-7B), while all other parameters θ remain unperturbed, requiring only a single forward-backward pass rather than the two full passes of vanilla SAM. The output is the trained policy π_θ, evaluated via win rates against SFT and chosen baselines, open-ended instruction-following benchmarks, and safety refusal rates.

## Method

The paper develops a unified theoretical framework linking parameter-space and logit-space dynamics via the identity H_W = (φφᵀ) ⊗ H_z, showing that all second-order effects reduce to the logit Hessian. In the eigenbasis of H_z, the residual dynamics diagonalize into per-mode scalars, revealing that negative-gradient updates (η < 0 for rejected responses) cause residuals to expand fastest along high-curvature directions—the root cause of the squeezing effect. SAM introduces an additional H_z² correction term that, with the appropriate sign of ρ (same sign as η), counteracts this expansion. The practical algorithm, logits-SAM, perturbs only the output-layer weights W by ρ·∇_W L_DPO / ‖∇_W L_DPO‖ and computes the DPO loss at the perturbed point, adding only ~2–3% wall-clock overhead. This makes it a drop-in enhancement compatible with DPO, SLiC-HF, CPO, and CHES without modifying their objectives.

## Results

On Pythia-2.8B, logits-SAM consistently improves all three DPO variants: DPO+logits-SAM achieves 72.28% win rate vs SFT and 60.51% vs chosen on Anthropic-HH (vs. 70.52/56.35 for DPO alone), while CPO+logits-SAM reaches 45.41% vs chosen on TL;DR (vs. 39.38 for CPO). On Mistral-7B, DPO+logits-SAM improves Arena-Hard v0.1 win rate from 19.0% to 23.1% and MT-Bench score from 5.49 to 5.79. In the AI safety setting on SorryBench with Gemma-2B-IT, DPO+logits-SAM raises test refusal rate from 70.77% (DPO) to 75.38%, and CHES+logits-SAM further boosts it to 87.69%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

logits-SAM is applied as a drop-in enhancement to three DPO variants (DPO, SLiC-HF, CPO) and consistently outperforms each baseline across all evaluated metrics. The largest gains appear with SLiC-HF on Anthropic-HH (+6.60 pp vs SFT, +7.49 pp vs chosen) and with CPO on TL;DR (+2.30 pp vs SFT, +6.03 pp vs chosen), indicating the benefit is method-agnostic. On Mistral-7B instruction-following benchmarks, CPO+logits-SAM shows the most dramatic improvement on AlpacaEval 2 LC (8.97→13.32, +4.35 pp), while DPO+logits-SAM leads on Arena-Hard v0.1 (19.0→23.1, +4.1 pp). In the AI safety setting (SorryBench, Gemma-2B-IT), DPO+logits-SAM avoids the refusal-rate degradation seen in plain DPO (70.77%→75.38% test refusal), and combining logits-SAM with CHES (Razin et al., 2024) yields the strongest result at 87.69% test refusal, an absolute +9.2 pp over CHES alone. The method adds only ~2–3% wall-clock overhead compared to vanilla SAM, which is infeasible for billion-parameter models on A100s due to memory constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) as the base preference optimization method, SAM (Foret et al., 2021) as a curvature-regularizing optimizer, and the learning-dynamics analysis of Ren & Sutherland (2024) that identified the squeezing effect. The key conceptual move is to connect SAM's curvature regularization to the logit-space Hessian geometry, showing that the squeezing effect arises from residual expansion along high-curvature directions under negative-gradient updates. Crucially, the paper repurposes logits-SAM—a variant previously mentioned only as a byproduct in Baek et al. (2024) and Singh et al. (2025)—into a practical DPO enhancement, arguing that perturbing only the output layer suffices to capture the relevant curvature effects. Unlike prior squeezing-effect mitigations (C2-DPO, CHES, KL-drift constraints) that modify the objective or filter data, this approach is purely optimization-based and thus orthogonal to those techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical account of why the squeezing effect occurs (residual expansion along high-curvature eigendirections) and shows that a negligible-overhead fix (perturbing only the output layer, ~2-3% extra time) consistently improves DPO and its variants across models and benchmarks. The AI safety extension (CHES+logits-SAM achieving ~93% refusal rate vs. ~84% for CHES alone on SorryBench) suggests practical relevance beyond standard alignment metrics. Open questions include whether the kernel-regime theoretical framework extends to the full non-linear LLM setting, whether the sign rule for ρ (matching the learning rate) holds under more complex training schedules, and whether the approach composes with other emerging DPO variants beyond the three tested here.
