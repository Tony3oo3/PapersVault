## Problem

DeepSeek-R1-Zero-style reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has driven major gains in math and code reasoning, but it fundamentally requires rule-based answer verification, which is infeasible for general domains like chemistry, law, healthcare, and economics. The natural workaround of using an LLM as a model-based verifier reintroduces RLHF-style problems: dependence on a strong verifier, susceptibility to reward hacking, and the memory/compute cost of maintaining an extra model during training. This paper asks whether the R1-Zero-style RL paradigm can be extended to general reasoning without any verifier at all.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text question and generates a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer enclosed in `<answer> \boxed{...} </answer>`. During training, the model samples only the reasoning trace z (stopping at the token `<answer` without the closing `>` to guarantee consistent tokenization), then the reference answer y* is patched in and a single non-autoregressive forward pass computes the conditional probability π_θ(y*|x,z). This probability serves as a continuous reward signal, replacing the binary correct/incorrect indicator that a verifier would provide.

## Method

VeriFree derives a verifier-free objective by analytically marginalizing out the answer y in the standard RLVR objective under the single-correct-answer assumption, yielding J_VeriFree = E_z[π_θ(y*|x,z)]. The resulting gradient decomposes into a reasoning term (policy gradient on z weighted by the probability of the correct answer) and a reference-answer term (reward-weighted supervised learning on y*), and is proven equivalent in expectation to the verifier-based gradient while having strictly lower variance via Rao-Blackwellization. In practice, the method applies RLOO baselines to the reasoning term, PPO-style clipping for off-policy reuse, and a tokenization-aware split at the reasoning-answer boundary to avoid off-policy mismatches. Trained from base Qwen3 models (1.7B–8B) on ~61k general-domain QA pairs, VeriFree matches or surpasses verifier-based baselines on MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA, GPQA-Diamond, and math benchmarks while requiring no auxiliary verifier model in memory.

## Results

On MMLU-Pro, VeriFree fine-tuned from Qwen3-8B-Base achieves 67.2% average accuracy, surpassing the verifier-based baseline (65.9%) and the Qwen3-8B instruct model in non-thinking mode (61.9%), while approaching the thinking-mode instruct model (66.9%). On SuperGPQA, VeriFree (8B) reaches 38.0% vs. 37.1% for the verifier baseline and 35.0% for the instruct thinking model. On math benchmarks (Table 3), VeriFree (8B) achieves 25.2% on AIME24, 67.5% on AMC, and 50.2% on OlympiadBench, outperforming the verifier baseline on AIME24 (+7.4), AMC (+10.0), and OlympiadBench (+8.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary verifier-based baseline (Dr. GRPO with a Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B verifier), VeriFree wins on MMLU-Pro at 4B (63.5 vs. 63.0) and 8B (67.2 vs. 65.9), ties at 1.7B (46.9 vs. 47.0), and also wins on SuperGPQA at all scales (e.g., 38.0 vs. 37.1 at 8B). Against General-Reasoner-4B (Ma et al.), VeriFree achieves 63.5 vs. 62.8 on MMLU-Pro and 35.1 vs. 32.5 on SuperGPQA, but trails slightly on GPQA-Diamond (42.4 vs. 42.9). On the math12k comparison (Table 4), VeriFree outperforms both JEPO and LaTRO across all six math benchmarks (e.g., AIME24: 21.46 vs. 16.67 for both; MATH-500: 84.6 vs. 83.0 and 82.6) and matches or exceeds the rule-based RLVR baseline. Against OctoThinker-3B-Long-Zero (Wang et al.), VeriFree wins on all three benchmarks (MMLU-Pro 36.2 vs. 35.8, SuperGPQA 20.5 vs. 18.6, GPQA-D 30.3 vs. 25.3). VeriFree from base also surpasses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (55.3) and Qwen2.5-7B-SimpleRL-Zoo (51.2) on MMLU-Pro, and approaches or exceeds the Qwen3 instruct models in non-thinking mode at all scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepSeek-R1-Zero RLVR paradigm (Guo et al., 2025) from verifiable domains (math, code) to general reasoning, where rule-based answer checking is infeasible. Rather than adopting the model-based verifier approach used by General-Reasoner (Ma et al., 2025) or Su et al. (2025), it derives a verifier-free objective via Rao-Blackwellization of the policy gradient, showing equivalence to the original RLVR objective under a single-correct-answer assumption. The method is closely related to variational-inference approaches JEPO (Tang et al., 2025) and LaTRO (Chen et al., 2024b), which also treat the reasoning trace as a latent variable, but differs critically in how the reference-answer gradient term is weighted (by the conditional probability rather than a fixed constant), which the authors argue prevents reinforcement of poor reasoning traces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VeriFree removes the need for an auxiliary verifier LLM during training, eliminating memory overhead, reward-hacking vulnerability, and the practical burden of maintaining a second model — a significant simplification for scaling RL to general domains. The main limitation is the requirement for reference Q-A pairs, which constrains applicability to settings like coding where only test cases are available. Open questions include how to better exploit answer equivalence classes (the ablation shows only marginal gains from including them) and whether the approach extends to open-ended generation tasks without a single reference answer. The strong transfer to math benchmarks without math-specific training suggests the induced reasoning is genuinely general, though the modest margins over verifier-based baselines (0.5–1.3 points) leave room for further improvement.
