## Problem

Conventional adapter-based fine-tuning, widely adopted for efficient adaptation of large pretrained models, has been shown to actually degrade performance below linear probing when training data drops to 1% or less in medical imaging tasks. The paper identifies a sharp reduction in Effective Receptive Field (ERF) under data scarcity as the root cause: standard adapters lack inductive bias toward expanding spatial context, which is critical for medical images characterized by low contrast, ambiguous boundaries, and small pathological structures requiring long-range dependencies.

## Input / Output

The method operates on patch token sequences extracted from transformer backbones (ViT-B, Swin-T/B, Segmenter-B, MedSAM, or ResNet-50), which are reshaped from 1D token layouts back to 2D spatial grids so that depthwise convolutions can be applied in the image domain. For classification, the output is a class label over disease categories (e.g., COVID vs. normal vs. viral pneumonia, benign/malignant/normal breast lesions, 9-class skin lesions); for segmentation, it produces pixel-level binary or multi-class masks (tumor sub-regions in BRATS, lesion boundaries in BUSI and ISIC-2018). The DKA module is inserted within transformer blocks with a middle dimension of 16 (classification) or 192 (segmentation), keeping the backbone frozen.

## Method

DKA replaces the standard adapter bottleneck with a dual-branch depthwise convolution design: after a linear down-projection and reshaping to 2D, one branch applies a 51×51 depthwise convolution to explicitly enlarge the ERF and capture long-range spatial context, while the other applies a 5×5 depthwise convolution to preserve fine-grained local detail. The two branch outputs are aggregated via element-wise summation, passed through GELU, up-projected, and added residually to the input. A key training insight is that asynchronous learning rates (1e-3 for DKA modules, 1e-4 for the task head) significantly outperform symmetric schedules, and the large kernel's contribution to ERF expansion is more impactful than simply increasing the number of trainable parameters. Ablations confirm that the fixed 51×51 + 5×5 dual-branch configuration is more robust in low-data regimes than learnable kernel-selection variants, which require more data to generalize the gating mechanism reliably.

## Results

On the three medical classification datasets (COVID, BUSI, ISIC-2019) with a ViT-B backbone, DKA achieves top-1 accuracy of 89.01%, 74.23%, and 60.52% at the extreme 0.63% data regime, surpassing full fine-tuning (87.43%, 71.17%, 60.04%) and all PEFT baselines. At 100% data, DKA reaches 99.21% (COVID), 95.89% (BUSI), and 83.09% (ISIC-2019), again outperforming full fine-tuning. For segmentation (Segmenter-B, mIoU), DKA leads at 9.47% (BRATS 0.63%), 26.85% (BUSI 0.63%), and 63.13% (ISIC-2018 0.63%), and at 100% data achieves 74.96%, 58.90%, and 78.53% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DKA outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and data scale. The most dramatic gaps appear in the extreme low-data regime (0.63%): on BUSI, DKA (74.23%) beats the standard Adapter (63.18%) by over 11 points, Convpass (64.83%) by ~9.4 points, and CIAT (60.28%) by ~14 points. Among adapter-based methods, Convpass is the closest competitor (e.g., 84.72% vs. 89.01% on COVID 0.63%), while CIAT and AIM consistently trail by 5–10+ points in low-data settings. Non-adapter PEFT methods (LoRA, BitFit, Prompt Tuning) show the largest degradation under data scarcity, with LoRA dropping to 63.64% on BUSI 0.63% versus DKA's 74.23%. At 100% data, DKA still leads full fine-tuning by 0.78–1.04 points on classification, confirming gains are not solely a low-data artifact. The paper also shows DKA generalizes to medical-pretrained backbones (RadImageNet-ResNet-50, MedSAM), maintaining its lead over all baselines in both classification and segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard Adapter architecture (Houlsby et al., 2019) by replacing its bottleneck with a dual-branch depthwise convolution design that borrows the large-kernel inductive bias from recent CNN literature (Ding et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2023). It also draws on the broader PEFT landscape—LoRA, BitFit, Prompt Tuning, AdapterFormer, Convpass, CIAT, AIM—as baselines, positioning DKA as a spatially-aware alternative to purely channel-wise or attention-based adapters. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior adapter work by introducing an Effective Receptive Field (ERF) diagnostic to explain *why* standard adapters fail under data scarcity, rather than simply proposing another architectural variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard adapters can underperform linear probing at <1% training data in medical imaging is practically important for clinical settings where annotation is expensive and privacy regulations fragment datasets. DKA offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic fix that generalizes across ViT, Swin, ResNet, and MedSAM backbones, making it broadly applicable. However, the evaluation is limited to 2D medical imaging (chest X-ray, ultrasound, dermoscopy, 2D MRI slices); whether the ERF-based diagnosis and the 51×51 kernel transfer to 3D volumetric tasks or to non-medical low-data domains remains open. The paper also does not explore whether the gains persist under domain shift beyond the specific out-of-domain (natural→medical) setting studied here.
