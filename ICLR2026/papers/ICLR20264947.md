## Problem

Self-supervised image denoising under unknown, varying noise levels requires estimators whose divergence is constant (not merely zero in expectation), but existing constraint-based methods either sacrifice expressivity through blind-spot masking (Noise2Self) or fail when the noise level varies across observations (UNSURE). Additionally, constructing divergence-free neural networks in high dimensions (e.g., images) is computationally prohibitive because the full representation requires O(n²) scalar fields. The paper addresses both gaps: a theoretically grounded, resource-efficient parameterization that enforces exact zero divergence by design while retaining sufficient expressivity for competitive denoising.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy image (RGB or grayscale) corrupted by additive white Gaussian noise as input and produces a denoised image of the same resolution as output. Crucially, the noise level σ is neither known nor required at inference time; a single model handles all noise levels simultaneously (trained with σ drawn uniformly from [0,75]). The backbone is a DRUNet variant with Softplus activations, operating on 64×64 patches for color and 128×128 for grayscale, with the divergence-free constraint adding only ~500 extra parameters beyond the 17M-parameter backbone.

## Method

The core contribution is a representer theorem (Theorem 1) establishing that any smooth divergence-free vector field f: Rⁿ→Rⁿ can be written as f = Σₖ Aₖ∇ψₖ where {Aₖ} is a basis of skew-symmetric n×n matrices and ψₖ are smooth scalar fields. The proposed CENSURE architecture instantiates this with a sparse combination of only K′=8 terms (vs. K=n²/2 for full representation), where each Aₖ is a sparse skew-symmetric matrix constructed as Pₖᵀ(Θ−Θᵀ)/2·Pₖ from a shared repeated-block diagonal Θ and fixed permutation matrices Pₖ, and each scalar field ψₖ is parameterized as ½(||Bₖy||² − ||Bₖy − D_θ(y)||²) with a shared U-Net backbone D_θ. The gradient ∇ψₖ is computed via automatic differentiation, avoiding explicit Jacobian computation. Because the divergence is exactly zero by construction (Lemma 2: div(A∇ψ)=0 for skew-symmetric A), the SURE loss reduces to a simple measurement-consistency term ‖f(y)−y‖² that requires no knowledge of σ, enabling training on data with unknown and varying noise levels without the instability issues of Monte Carlo divergence estimation.

## Results

In the primary setting of unknown non-constant noise level σ ∈ [0,75] (single model for all noise levels), CENSURE achieves 34.21/32.05/29.24/27.67 dB PSNR on Kodak24 and 33.17/30.83/27.91/26.33 dB on CBSD68 at σ = 15/25/50/75, outperforming Noise2Self by 0.1–0.2 dB and dramatically surpassing UNSURE (which collapses to 12.5–15.9 dB at high noise). In the constant-σ setting (Table 4), CENSURE† (noise-level-aware variant) reaches 32.46/30.28/27.27 dB on Set12, competitive with UNSURE† (33.05/30.79/27.58 dB) and within ~0.8 dB of the supervised DRUNet baseline (33.24/30.92/27.84 dB). The method requires only 512×C additional parameters beyond the shared DRUNet backbone (17M total), with inference cost of ~9× a single forward pass due to K′=8 gradient computations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CENSURE consistently outperforms Noise2Self (the blind-spot baseline) by 0.1–0.2 dB across all noise levels on both Kodak24 and CBSD68, confirming the theoretical expressivity advantage of divergence-free over blind-spot constraints. Against UNSURE, CENSURE achieves a decisive win: UNSURE catastrophically degrades to 12.5–15.9 dB at σ=50–75 when noise varies, because its zero-expected-divergence constraint is violated under non-constant σ, while CENSURE's pointwise divergence-free property remains valid regardless of σ. Compared to the non-constraint-based Neighbor2Neighbor*, CENSURE trails by ~0.3–0.5 dB, though Neighbor2Neighbor relies on the natural-image assumption that neighboring pixels share similar values and is not generalizable. Against Noise2Score*, CENSURE is competitive at low noise (34.21 vs 34.69 at σ=15) but pulls ahead at high noise (27.67 vs 22.91 at σ=75), where Noise2Score's score-matching approach struggles. In the constant-σ setting (Table 4), MC-SURE with oracle τ=10⁻⁴ slightly outperforms CENSURE† (33.22 vs 32.46 dB on Set12 at σ=15), but with the practical τ=10⁻², CENSURE† outperforms MC-SURE (32.46 vs 32.13 dB), highlighting CENSURE's robustness to the Monte Carlo hyperparameter that plagues SURE-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representer theorem for divergence-free vector fields from Richter-Powell et al. (2022) by incorporating sparsity constraints to make the construction tractable in high dimensions, and adapts the conservative-field parameterization from Hurault et al. (2022a) for denoising. It positions CENSURE as a new constraint set S_DC^c (constant divergence everywhere) that sits strictly between the blind-spot constraint of Noise2Self and the zero-expected-divergence constraint of UNSURE, providing a principled intermediate that retains more expressivity than blind-spot while remaining valid under varying noise levels where UNSURE breaks down. The architecture uses a shared U-Net backbone with skew-symmetric matrix parameterizations to enforce zero divergence by construction, avoiding both penalty-based soft constraints and Monte Carlo divergence estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical hierarchy of constraint sets (S_BS ⊂ S_DC ⊂ S_CED) with closed-form characterizations of optimal constants and lower bounds, giving practitioners a principled way to choose the right level of constraint for their noise regime. The key practical limitation is computational cost: CENSURE requires K'+1 forward/backward passes at inference (roughly 9× a single feedforward), making it significantly slower than Noise2Self or MC-SURE. Open questions include whether the sparsity constraint (K'=8 vs. the full K=O(n²) terms) is sufficient for optimal performance, whether the approach extends beyond AWGN to Poisson-Gaussian or structured noise, and whether the computational overhead can be reduced through architectural innovations.
