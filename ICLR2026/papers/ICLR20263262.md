## Problem

Existing time series generation (TSG) benchmarks and methods primarily target non-financial domains or traditional equities under simplifying assumptions such as regular trading hours and stationarity, leaving cryptocurrency markets—characterized by 24/7 trading, extreme volatility, and rapid regime shifts—largely unexplored. Moreover, prior financial TSG efforts focus narrowly on classification and forecasting while neglecting generation quality and trading-centric evaluation, and lack crypto-specific risk metrics needed to assess whether synthetic data supports actionable, profitable strategies.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes hourly OHLC data for 452 USDT-denominated Binance spot pairs (Jan 2020–Dec 2024), converted to log-return matrices, and evaluates each TSG model in two modes: generation (sampling from N(0,I)) and reconstruction (reconstructing train/test splits). A walk-forward protocol with 500-day training windows and 30-day (Predictive Utility) or 15-day (Statistical Arbitrage) test steps drives retraining at each offset. Outputs comprise 13 metrics (MSE, MAE, IC, IR, CAGR, Sharpe, MDD, VaR, ES, training/inference time, growth curves, ranking plots) computed under three trading strategies and both zero-fee and 0.03%-fee conditions.

## Method

CTBench introduces a dual-task evaluation framework that connects generation fidelity to financial utility. The Predictive Utility task trains a TSG model on real returns, generates synthetic sequences, extracts quantitative features (Alpha101, Bollinger Bands, RSI, moving averages), trains an XGBoost forecaster on synthetic features, then deploys it on real test data to drive a dollar-neutral long-short portfolio rebalanced hourly. The Statistical Arbitrage task instead computes residuals between real and reconstructed returns, fits per-asset Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes, converts test residuals to standardized s-scores, and trades via thresholding (γ=2) with weight normalization. Eight state-of-the-art TSG models spanning GAN, VAE, diffusion, flow, and mixed-type families are benchmarked alongside ARMA-GARCH and Bootstrap baselines, scored by a 13-metric suite covering error, rank, trading, risk, and efficiency dimensions. An ablation validates XGBoost as the most discriminative forecaster by showing it uniquely balances low prediction error with strong cross-sectional rank correlation, unlike alternatives that either overfit point-wise or collapse rankings.

## Results

CTBench evaluates 8 TSG models across 452 USDT pairs, 13 metrics, and 4 market regimes (2021–2024) under two tasks. No model dominates universally: on Predictive Utility, Diffusion-TS achieves the lowest forecasting error (MSE/MAE) but underperforms in trading, while TimeVAE and COSCI-GAN deliver the strongest risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe Ratio) in stable and volatile regimes respectively. On Statistical Arbitrage, KoVAE and LS4 lead in CAGR and Sharpe, whereas ARMA-GARCH posts the top CAGR (2021–2023) but the worst VaR and Expected Shortfall among all models. In efficiency, TimeVAE trains in under one minute with sub-second inference, while Diffusion-TS incurs the longest runtime due to iterative denoising.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark compares eight TSG models (Quant-GAN, COSCI-GAN, TimeVAE, KoVAE, Diffusion-TS, FIDE, Fourier-Flow, LS4) plus two classical baselines (ARMA-GARCH, Bootstrap) across both tasks. On Predictive Utility, COSCI-GAN and TimeVAE consistently outperform diffusion-based models in trading profitability (CAGR, Sharpe), while Diffusion-TS wins on pure forecasting accuracy (MSE, MAE) but fails to convert that into returns—a gap between statistical fidelity and economic utility. On Statistical Arbitrage, KoVAE and LS4 dominate in CAGR and Sharpe under both zero-fee and 0.03%-fee settings, with LS4 showing the most fee-resilient equity curve; FIDE achieves the lowest VaR, ES, and MDD but yields negligible or negative returns due to over-regularized residuals. Fourier-Flow maintains steady mid-to-high rankings across both tasks, positioning it as a robust all-weather choice, while Quant-GAN is significantly more expensive to train with no corresponding speed advantage. The ARMA-GARCH baseline delivers the highest CAGR in 2021–2023 on Statistical Arbitrage but exhibits the weakest tail-risk profile, and the Bootstrap generator sits in the upper-middle of the pack on Predictive Utility, tracking the top 3–5 models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TSG benchmarking lineage (TSGBench, FinTSB, FinTSBridge) by carving out a crypto-specific evaluation space that prior financial benchmarks leave unaddressed. It departs from existing work in two key ways: (1) the dataset targets 24/7, high-volatility USDT spot pairs rather than regular-hours equities or indices, and (2) the dual-task framework (Predictive Utility + Statistical Arbitrage) goes beyond the classification/forecasting emphasis of prior financial TSG evaluations to directly assess tradable mean-reverting structure. The eight TSG models benchmarked (spanning GAN, VAE, diffusion, flow, and state-space families) are drawn from the general TSG literature, but none had previously been systematically compared under crypto market conditions with fee-aware trading backtests.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CTBench provides the first actionable decision surface for selecting TSG models in crypto analytics, revealing that no single architecture dominates across fidelity, tradability, and robustness — a finding that cautions against one-size-fits-all deployment. The practical limitation is scope: a single exchange (Binance), USDT pairs only, no exogenous signals (volume, order flow, news), and a fixed set of three trading strategies, all of which constrain generalizability. Open questions include whether the fidelity-profitability gap persists with more sophisticated forecasters, whether the statistical arbitrage task generalizes beyond OU-process residuals, and how results shift under structural breaks or extreme tail events not captured in the 2020–2024 window.
