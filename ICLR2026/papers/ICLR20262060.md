## Problem

Training generalist VLA models on large-scale cross-embodiment datasets is hampered by severe heterogeneity in hardware configurations, camera setups, visual domains, and task distributions. Existing methods primarily address action-space heterogeneity via separate decoder heads but overlook these other critical sources of variation, leading to distributional shifts, semantic misalignments, and unstable pretraining that degrade both generalization and adaptation performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262060/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images (fixed-camera and wrist-camera views, resized to 224×224), a natural-language task instruction, and proprioceptive states (joint positions and end-effector poses). Fixed views and language are encoded by a frozen Florence-2-Large VLM, while wrist views pass through a separate shared vision backbone to avoid conflating high-level reasoning with fine-grained manipulation cues. The output is a 30-point action chunk spanning 4 seconds, parameterized as Cartesian EEF position, Rotate6D rotation, and a binary gripper state, generated by iteratively denoising Gaussian noise through a learned velocity field.

## Method

X-VLA introduces a soft-prompt mechanism where each data source is assigned a set of 32 learnable token embeddings that are concatenated with the encoded multimodal features before entering a standard 24-layer Transformer encoder (hidden size 1024). These prompts implicitly encode hardware-specific configurations (arm kinematics, camera layout, control frequency) without requiring handcrafted annotations or mid-pipeline projection layers, allowing the shared backbone to learn embodiment-agnostic representations while the prompts absorb domain-specific variation. Action generation uses flow matching with an OT path, and the training pipeline employs a two-phase strategy: joint pretraining of backbone and prompts on 290K episodes from 7 heterogeneous sources, followed by a lightweight two-step adaptation (prompt warm-up with frozen backbone, then joint fine-tuning) for new domains. Additional stabilization techniques include reduced learning rates for prompts and VLM modules, aligned EEF+Rotate6D action representation, temporal downsampling to abstract action intentions, and cross-domain/cross-trajectory balanced sampling.

## Results

X-VLA-0.9B achieves state-of-the-art results across 6 simulation benchmarks and 3 real-world platforms. On Simpler-WidowX it reaches 95.8% success rate (vs. 71.9% prior best), LIBERO average 98.1% (vs. 97.1%), RoboTwin-2.0 Easy 70.0% (vs. 46.4%), RoboTwin-2.0 Hard 39.0% (vs. 16.4%), VLABench 51.1 PS (vs. 39.7), and NAVSIM 87.3 PDMS (vs. 81.7). With only 9M tunable parameters (~1% of the model) via LoRA, X-VLA matches fully-finetuned π0 (3B) on LIBERO (93% vs. 94.2%) and Simpler-WidowX (54% vs. 55.7%). On the dexterous cloth-folding task, X-VLA achieves ~100% success with 33 folds/hour using only 1,200 demonstrations, comparable to the closed-source π0-folding model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-VLA-0.9B establishes new SOTA on 5 of 6 simulation benchmarks, with the most dramatic gains on Simpler-WidowX (95.8% vs. 71.9% for MemoryVLA, 27.8% for π0) and RoboTwin-2.0 (70.0/39.0% Easy/Hard vs. 46.4/16.4% for π0), where its 0.9B size is 3–10× smaller than competitors. On LIBERO, X-VLA achieves 98.1% average, narrowly trailing OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) and MemoryVLA (96.7%) on individual sub-benchmarks (Spatial: 98.2 vs. 98.4; Object: 98.6 vs. 98.8) but clearly leading on LIBERO-Long (97.6 vs. 94.5). On VLABench, X-VLA scores 51.1 PS, surpassing GR00T-N1 (39.7) and π0 (37.8) by a wide margin. On NAVSIM (autonomous driving), X-VLA reaches 87.3 PDMS vs. 81.7 for UniVLA, the only other model reporting this benchmark. In the PEFT setting, X-VLA with only 9M tunable parameters (LoRA) matches π0's fully-finetuned 3B model on LIBERO (93% vs. 94.2%) and Simpler-WidowX (54% vs. 55.7%), demonstrating that the pretrained backbone encodes strong embodiment-agnostic features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLA architecture lineage established by π0 (flow-matching action generation) and GR00T/FLOWER (dual-system VLM + action decoder) by replacing the DiT-style action decoder with a standard Transformer encoder and introducing embodiment-specific soft prompts at the input stage. While prior cross-embodiment VLA models (π0, GR00T, RDT) handle heterogeneity primarily through separate action projection heads at the output, X-VLA shifts the burden to the early feature-fusion stage via learnable prompt embeddings, drawing on the prompt-learning literature from NLP and meta-learning. The disentangled encoding pipeline (dedicated VLM for fixed-view + language, shared vision backbone for wrist views) departs from the common practice of feeding all views into a single VLM, motivated by the semantic gap between high-level task reasoning and fine-grained manipulation cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

X-VLA-0.9B provides a strong, open-source cross-embodiment VLA baseline that outperforms models 3–10× its size across six simulation benchmarks and three real-world platforms, and the soft-prompt mechanism (0.04% of parameters) offers a simple, scalable recipe for absorbing embodiment heterogeneity that is likely to be widely adopted. The PEFT result—matching π0's fully finetuned 3B model with only 9M tunable parameters (1%)—demonstrates that the pretrained backbone encodes genuinely embodiment-agnostic features, with practical implications for deployment on new hardware. Key open questions remain: whether scaling beyond 0.9B and 290K episodes continues to yield gains (the paper shows no saturation but cannot test further), whether richer supervision signals (3D cues, subgoals) can replace the heuristic temporal downsampling, and how to move toward truly zero-shot deployment without any embodiment-specific adaptation data.
