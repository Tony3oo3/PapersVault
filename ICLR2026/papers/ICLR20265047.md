## Problem

Deep RL sample efficiency has been improved primarily through scaling—larger networks, higher update-to-data ratios, and greater compute—rather than by understanding the optimization landscape of the critic. This paper asks whether a better-conditioned optimization problem, achieved through principled architectural choices, can match or exceed the gains from brute-force scaling while using far fewer parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265047/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives either a proprioceptive state vector (from DMC, MuJoCo, HumanoidBench, or MyoSuite environments) or an RGB image processed through the standard DRQ-V2 convolutional encoder (50-dim feature vector), and outputs a continuous control action. The critic outputs a 101-atom categorical distribution over a bounded support of [-5, 5] (after reward normalization by running return standard deviation) rather than a scalar Q-value, and the actor is a 4-layer MLP with 512 hidden units producing the action distribution parameters.

## Method

XQC extends Soft Actor-Critic with three synergistic architectural components identified through Hessian eigenvalue analysis: (1) Batch Normalization (BN) after each linear layer, which produces compact, stable eigenspectra with condition numbers orders of magnitude smaller than Layer Normalization; (2) Weight Normalization (WN), projecting each dense layer's weights to the unit sphere after every gradient step, which keeps the effective learning rate denominator constant; and (3) a C51-style distributional critic with a categorical cross-entropy Bellman loss, which provably bounds gradient norms (≤√2) and yields an upper-bounded Hessian condition number, unlike MSE whose gradients and condition number are unbounded. The combination stabilizes the effective learning rate throughout training, preventing the plasticity loss that plagues standard architectures under non-stationary targets and bootstrapping. The resulting architecture is deliberately simple—four Dense(512)→BN→ReLU blocks for the critic and two for the actor—achieving state-of-the-art sample efficiency across 70 tasks with ~4.5× fewer parameters and ~5× fewer FLOP/S than the closest competitor SIMBA-V2.

## Results

XQC achieves state-of-the-art sample efficiency across 55 proprioceptive continuous control tasks (DMC, HumanoidBench, MyoSuite, MuJoCo) and 15 vision-based DMC tasks using a single set of hyperparameters. The method uses ~4.5× fewer parameters and ~5× fewer FLOP/S than SIMBA-V2, with >100× fewer FLOP/S than BRC, while matching or exceeding their IQM AUC of normalized returns. Training throughput is 60% higher (FPS) than SIMBA-V2 on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, and evaluation runs at 1355 FPS. On the most challenging benchmarks (HumanoidBench and DMC-hard), XQC shows the largest performance margins over baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SIMBA-V2 (the closest competitor), XQC matches or slightly exceeds aggregated IQM AUC while using ~4.5× fewer parameters and ~5× fewer FLOP/S, translating to 60% higher training FPS. Against BRO (small, UTD=2), XQC achieves comparable or better sample efficiency with ~5× lower compute. Against BRC (64M params), XQC uses >100× fewer FLOP/S while maintaining competitive performance, highlighting that brute-force scaling is not necessary. Against CROSSQ+WN (the authors' prior BN+WN work), XQC's addition of the distributional CE critic loss yields further gains, particularly on ill-conditioned tasks like HumanoidBench and DMC-hard. Against the original SAC baseline, XQC shows substantial improvements across all four benchmarks. On vision-based DMC tasks, XQC (using the standard DRQ-V2 encoder) outperforms DRQ-V2, with the most pronounced advantage on the three humanoid environments where XQC's well-conditioned critic better handles the 10× longer training horizon.

## Relation to Prior Work

XQC extends the SAC algorithm by combining three previously studied components—batch normalization (from CrossQ, Bhatt et al. 2024), weight normalization (from Palenicek et al. 2025 and Lyle et al. 2024), and a C51-style categorical critic (Bellemare et al. 2017)—into a single architecture whose design is motivated by a systematic Hessian eigenvalue analysis rather than empirical trial. The key departure from the prevailing 'bigger is better' scaling paradigm (SIMBA-V2, BRO, BRC) is the paper's argument that optimization conditioning, not raw capacity, is the primary driver of sample efficiency. The theoretical contribution—showing that the CE loss induces a bounded Hessian condition number while MSE does not—provides a principled explanation for why distributional critics outperform MSE regression beyond the representational argument.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work offers a principled, optimization-theoretic framework for designing RL critics that could shift the field's focus from scaling network size to engineering well-conditioned loss landscapes. The ~4.5× parameter reduction and ~5× FLOP/S savings while matching SOTA make it practically attractive for compute-constrained settings like robotics. Open questions include whether these conditioning principles transfer to model-based RL, discrete action spaces, or offline RL, and whether the BN requirement (which complicates off-policy training via the joined forward pass) limits applicability. The vision-based results, while positive, show smaller gains than the proprioceptive ones, suggesting the encoder bottleneck may partially mask the critic's conditioning benefits.
