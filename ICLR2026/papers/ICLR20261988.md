## Problem

Vision Foundation Models like DINOv2 produce strong 2D features but lack 3D awareness, limiting their utility for depth estimation, surface normal prediction, and multi-view correspondence. Prior approaches to instill 3D awareness—such as FiT3D's per-scene optimization or MEF's correspondence enforcement—are either slow and produce feature-averaging artifacts from inconsistent multi-view inputs, or provide only relational constraints insufficient for dense geometric understanding. The paper asks whether 3D consistency can be enforced efficiently and scalably within a standard distillation loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261988/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes pairs of context RGB views (584×876) plus a distinct target view from ScanNet++ indoor scenes, along with instance-level semantic segmentation masks used for mask-aware upscaling and blending. The student network (DINOv2 ViT-Small or ViT-Base, fine-tuned via LoRA rank 8) outputs patch-level 2D feature maps at h×w resolution that are 3D-consistent across viewpoints. At inference, a single RGB image is passed through the student to produce these 3D-aware features, which are then probed with linear heads for downstream tasks.

## Method

The core mechanism augments the teacher in a DINOv2-style student-teacher distillation loop with a frozen, feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting model (MVSplat). Given two context views, the teacher extracts low-resolution 2D features while MVSplat simultaneously predicts 3D Gaussians; the features are upscaled to full resolution using mask-aware bilinear interpolation (restricting interpolation to same-semantic-label neighbors), then attached to Gaussians via the pixel-to-Gaussian correspondence inherent in MVSplat's unprojection. The resulting 3D feature scene is splatted to a novel target viewpoint, and a semantic blending step (α=0.5 weighted average within mask regions) regularizes the rendered features against geometric artifacts. The student minimizes a DINO cross-entropy distillation loss against the rendered-and-blended teacher features, while the teacher is updated via EMA (momentum 0.999, every 10 steps), creating an iterative process where teacher consistency improves alongside the student—avoiding the static feature-averaging of optimization-based baselines. Training runs for 50K iterations on a single L40S GPU (~18 hours) over 280 ScanNet++ scenes.

## Results

On monocular depth estimation (ViT-Small, ScanNet++), SnD achieves 0.2421 Abs-Rel and 0.3299 RMSE, outperforming DINOv2 (0.2811/0.3777), Fit3D (0.2500/0.3506), and MEF (0.3085/0.4000). For surface normal estimation on NYUv2, SnD reduces RMSE to 28.93° (ViT-S) and 29.37° (ViT-B), a 5.37% improvement over the closest baseline (Fit3D, 30.57°). On semantic segmentation (ViT-S, ScanNet++), SnD reaches 31.78 mIoU, matching Fit3D (31.77) while substantially exceeding DINOv2 (29.54) and MEF (27.44). Out-of-domain, SnD achieves 50.01 mIoU on ADE20K and 0.0631 Abs-Rel on KITTI depth (ViT-Base), leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SnD consistently outperforms all three primary baselines across every evaluated task. Against DINOv2 (vanilla), SnD achieves 5.90% RMSE reduction on ScanNet++ depth (ViT-S) and 7.5% on surface normal RMSE, while also improving segmentation mIoU by 2.24 points on ScanNet++. Against Fit3D (the closest prior work), SnD leads by 5.90% RMSE on ScanNet++ depth, 5.37% on normal RMSE, and 3.56% mIoU on out-of-domain ADE20K; on ScanNet++ segmentation mIoU the two methods are essentially tied (31.78 vs. 31.77). Against MEF, SnD wins decisively on all metrics—MEF actually degrades depth and segmentation performance relative to vanilla DINOv2 on several datasets (e.g., 0.3085 vs. 0.2811 Rel on ScanNet++ depth), while SnD improves on both. In the supplementary comparison against DUNE (CVPR 2025), SnD reduces depth RMSE from 0.3929 to 0.3299 and raises segmentation mIoU from 25.77 to 31.78 on ScanNet++ (ViT-S), confirming that the feed-forward 3D reconstruction pipeline corrects 3D inconsistencies that DINOv2-based distillation inherits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DINOv2 student-teacher self-distillation paradigm by replacing 2D data augmentations with 3D-rendered supervisory signals. It departs from FiT3D (Yue et al., 2024), which lifts 2D features into 3D via slow per-scene optimization and then fine-tunes the VFM on the rendered features, by instead using a frozen feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting model (MVSplat) to perform the lifting in a single forward pass within the distillation loop. It also contrasts with MEF (You et al., 2024), which enforces multi-view consistency through correspondence-based feature similarity constraints rather than explicit geometric reconstruction. The key architectural departure is augmenting the teacher itself with the 3D reconstruction pipeline and updating it via EMA alongside the student, creating a co-adaptive training dynamic absent in prior optimization-based or correspondence-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, scalable recipe for enhancing off-the-shelf VFMs (DINOv2) with 3D geometric awareness without requiring per-scene optimization or correspondence annotations, making it broadly applicable to any VFM backbone. The consistent gains across depth, normals, correspondence, and semantic segmentation—including out-of-domain transfer from indoor to outdoor (KITTI)—suggest the learned representations are genuinely more geometrically grounded rather than overfit to a single task. Key limitations include dependence on the quality of the feed-forward 3DGS reconstruction (poor geometry degrades supervision) and reliance on multi-view image datasets, which are less abundant than single-image or video data; extending training to video sequences is an open direction the authors identify.
