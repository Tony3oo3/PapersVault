## Problem

Offline Domain Randomization (ODR) methods such as DROPO empirically fit a distribution over simulator parameters from real-world data to improve sim-to-real transfer, but no theoretical guarantees exist for whether the fitted distribution actually converges to the true dynamics as data grows, nor for how much ODR reduces the sim-to-real gap relative to uniform DR. This paper provides the first consistency proofs and clarifies the conditions under which offline data can soundly guide the randomization distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an offline dataset of N i.i.d. transitions (s_i, a_i, s'_i) collected from the real system under an unknown behavior policy, together with a parametric simulator family {M_ξ : ξ ∈ Ξ ⊂ ℝ^d} sharing the same state space, action space, reward, and horizon. It outputs a Gaussian distribution N(μ, Σ) over the parameter space, constrained to a compact set Φ with 0 ⪯ Σ ⪯ σ_max I, which is then used to sample simulators for policy training. The key structural property is that under identifiability, the optimal Σ* = 0, so the fitted distribution collapses to a point mass at the true parameter ξ*.

## Method

The paper casts ODR as maximum-likelihood estimation over a parametric simulator family, where the log-likelihood of each transition is log ∫ p_ξ(s'|s,a) p_φ(ξ) dξ. The central insight is a KL-divergence decomposition showing the population log-likelihood is uniquely maximized by the degenerate distribution at ξ*, reducing consistency to a uniform law of large numbers argument over the compact parameter space. Weak consistency (convergence in probability) follows from ULLN plus compactness and identifiability; strong consistency (almost-sure convergence) is obtained by adding a uniform Lipschitz condition on the per-transition log-likelihood, which is shown to hold whenever the transition kernel is C² in ξ with bounded first and second derivatives. The paper further provides practical relaxations: replacing i.i.d. with strict stationarity and ergodicity, weakening the mixture positivity assumption to a logarithmic tail condition, and a partial-coverage variant where the estimator converges to an identified set of observationally indistinguishable parameters.

## Results

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or benchmark evaluations. The headline results are two consistency theorems: Theorem 1 establishes weak consistency (the ODR MLE converges in probability to the true simulator parameter ξ⋆ under regularity, compactness, positivity, and identifiability assumptions), and Theorem 2 upgrades this to strong consistency (almost-sure convergence) under an additional uniform Lipschitz continuity assumption on the log-likelihood. In Appendix A.1, the authors tighten the worst-case sim-to-real gap bound for uniform DR from O(M³ log³(MH)) (Chen et al., 2022) to O(M³ log(MH) log²(S/δ²)/δ⁴) in the finite δ-separable setting. Theorem 3 further shows that under partial state-action coverage, the estimator converges in probability to the identified set Q⋆_μ rather than a single point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper contains no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons; it is entirely a theoretical contribution providing the first consistency guarantees for offline domain randomization. The only quantitative comparison is theoretical: in Appendix A.1, the authors refine the proof of Chen et al. (2022) to tighten the uniform DR sim-to-real gap from O(M³ log³(MH)) to O(M³ log(MH) log²(S/δ²)/δ⁴), improving the logarithmic dependence on the number of candidate simulators M and horizon H. The paper positions itself as complementary to empirical ODR methods (DROPO, DROID, BayesSim) by providing the missing statistical foundation that justifies why the likelihood-based fitting procedure used in DROPO is consistent, rather than by benchmarking against them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical consistency guarantees for Offline Domain Randomization (ODR), a method whose empirical success was established by DROPO (Tiboni et al., 2023) and related approaches like DROID and BayesSim. It builds directly on DROPO's likelihood-based formulation for fitting a Gaussian over simulator parameters, and on Chen et al. (2022)'s theoretical analysis of uniform DR, which the authors tighten in Appendix A.1 from O(M³ log³(MH)) to O(M³ log(MH)). The key departure is casting ODR as a standard MLE problem over a parametric simulator family, then applying classical tools (uniform law of large numbers, Borel-Cantelli, Hoeffding's inequality, Stein's formula) to prove weak and strong consistency—something no prior work had attempted for the offline DR setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By placing ODR on a principled statistical footing, this paper validates the empirical practice of using offline data to inform the randomization distribution and clarifies exactly when this is sound (identifiability, mixture positivity, smoothness). The relaxation to partial coverage (Theorem 3) and the α-informativeness notion are practically useful: they show that even with incomplete state-action coverage, the estimator converges to a well-defined identified set rather than failing entirely. Open questions include whether the Gaussian restriction on the parameter distribution is essential, how the guarantees translate into concrete sim-to-real gap reductions (the paper proves consistency but not a finite-sample gap bound for ODR itself), and whether the identifiability assumption holds in realistic multi-contact robotic settings.
