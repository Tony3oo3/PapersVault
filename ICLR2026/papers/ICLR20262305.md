## Problem

Existing distillation methods for matching models (diffusion, flow matching, bridge matching) are developed in isolation for each framework, and all are inherently data-free, requiring complex GAN-based adversarial training with extra discriminator networks to benefit from real data. This paper asks whether a single unified distillation framework can cover all matching model families and incorporate real data naturally, without any adversarial components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262305/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The one-step generator G_θ takes a latent vector z ~ p^Z (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and produces a single RGB image in one forward pass (NFE=1), distilling from a frozen multi-step teacher (NFE=100). Experiments use CIFAR-10 at 32×32 and CelebA at 64×64, with FID computed over 50k samples. The generator uses a lightweight architecture (Tong et al., 2024) rather than the heavier EDM backbone used in prior distillation work, yielding roughly 2× faster inference.

## Method

RealUID formulates distillation as a min-max optimization min_θ max_f {L_UM(f*, p_0^θ) − L_UM(f, p_0^θ)}, where the fake model f is trained to match the current student function and the generator is updated to minimize the implied squared ℓ₂-distance between teacher and student functions. A linearization identity (‖a‖² = max_b{−‖b‖² + 2⟨b,a⟩}) makes the intractable norm-of-expectation term differentiable, unifying FGM, SiD (with α_SiD=0.5), and IBMD as special cases. To incorporate real data without GANs, the UM loss is replaced by a RealUM loss that is a weighted sum of generated-data and real-data matching terms controlled by coefficients α, β ∈ (0,1]; the critical parameter is the ratio β/α, which must deviate from 1 to provide the generator with feedback on uncovered real-data regions and to provably correct teacher errors. The fake model is updated several times per generator step for stability, and a fine-tuning stage with adjusted α_FT, β_FT further improves FID.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32), RealUID+FT achieves FID 1.98 (unconditional) and 1.87 (conditional) with 1 NFE, surpassing the strongest flow distillation baseline FGM (FID 3.08 / 2.58) by a large margin. The method matches SiD (α_SiD=1.2) at FID 1.92 (unconditional) while using a ~2× faster lightweight architecture, and slightly trails the adversarially enhanced SiD²A (FID 1.52 / 1.39). On CelebA (64×64), the same optimal β/α ratio transfers, confirming dataset generality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealUID+FT decisively outperforms all prior flow-based distillation methods on CIFAR-10: it beats FGM (3.08→1.98 unconditional, 2.58→1.87 conditional), MeanFlow (2.92), FACM (2.69), IMM (3.20), and all ReFlow variants (4.85–6.18). Against diffusion-based distillation, RealUID matches SiD (α_SiD=1.2) at FID 1.92 vs. 1.98 (unconditional) and 1.71 vs. 1.87 (conditional), while falling short of the adversarially enhanced SiD²A (1.52 / 1.39), which the authors attribute to architectural and teacher-capacity differences rather than the absence of GAN loss. In the direct GAN-vs-RealUID comparison under identical architecture, RealUID (α=0.98, β=0.96) achieves FID 1.87 conditional vs. UID+GAN's 1.88, and 1.98 vs. 2.10 unconditional. Convergence is ~3× faster: RealUID reaches the baseline's saturated FID in ~100k iterations versus ~300k for data-free UID. The method also enables effective fine-tuning from strong checkpoints, yielding additional FID gains (e.g., 2.58→1.98 unconditional) that GAN-based fine-tuning partially replicates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate distillation methods—Score Identity Distillation (SiD) for diffusion models, Flow Generator Matching (FGM) for flow matching, and Inverse Bridge Matching Distillation (IBMD) for bridge matching—under a single Universal Inverse Distillation (UID) min-max framework derived via a linearization technique. The key departure from all prior work is the incorporation of real data without GANs: previous distillation methods were data-free by construction, and adding real data required an extra discriminator and adversarial loss. RealUID instead modifies the Universal Matching loss with two coefficients (α, β) that weight generated and real data terms, preserving the same min-max structure while eliminating the need for adversarial training. The paper also reveals a formal connection between the distillation loss and inverse optimization, providing an alternative theoretical lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework gives practitioners a single theoretical foundation for distilling any matching model (diffusion, flow, bridge, stochastic interpolants) and a simpler, more stable training recipe that avoids GAN instability. The provable ability to correct teacher errors and the ~3× convergence speedup are practically valuable. However, experiments are limited to CIFAR-10 (32×32) and CelebA (64×64), and a notable gap remains versus adversarially enhanced methods like SiD²A (FID 1.98 vs 1.52 unconditional), which the authors attribute to architecture and teacher capacity rather than the absence of adversarial loss. Open questions include whether the α, β tuning generalizes to larger-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet, text-to-image) and whether the framework extends naturally to conditional or multi-modal settings.
