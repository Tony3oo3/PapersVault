## Problem

Prevailing dataset distillation methods that leverage diffusion models face a fundamental paradox: most require a diffusion model pre-trained on the full target dataset (e.g., ~3,000 TPU-days for ImageNet), undermining the very efficiency goal of distillation. Methods that instead use general text-to-image models avoid this cost but suffer from a significant distributional mismatch, as web-scale priors fail to capture target-specific semantics, leading to suboptimal distilled datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262767/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target dataset (images with class labels) and an off-the-shelf text-to-image model (SDXL) as input, using the class name as the text prompt. It produces a compact synthetic dataset with a specified number of images per class (IPC), generated at resolutions from 224×224 up to 1024×1024. Notably, the entire pipeline is training-free: no model is trained or fine-tuned on the target dataset, and the Distribution Discovery stage runs entirely on CPU using only the VAE encoder native to the LDM, avoiding any external feature extractors.

## Method

CoDA operates in two stages. In Distribution Discovery, target images are encoded into VAE latent space, projected via UMAP (min_dist=0.0) to 50 dimensions, and clustered with HDBSCAN to identify high-density regions; a multi-strategy post-processing scheme (iterative SplitCluster, K-Means on outliers, and ForcedSplit) then selects exactly IPC representative samples per class. In Distribution Alignment, each generated image is sequentially guided by one representative sample: at every denoising timestep, a guidance vector (s_j − ẑ₀) is computed, scaled by a strength parameter γ, and translated into a noise-space correction Δε_θ that is added to the text-conditional noise prediction before standard CFG. This continuous, data-driven guidance steers the generative process toward the target's 'intrinsic core distribution' throughout sampling, while a Prior Injection Steps (PIS) mechanism reverts to standard CFG in the final steps to let the model's generative prior refine photorealistic details.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (soft-label protocol), CoDA establishes a new SOTA of 60.4% top-1 accuracy at IPC=50, surpassing the strongest target-trained baseline IGD (60.3%) and the off-the-shelf D4M (55.2%) by +5.2%. At IPC=10, CoDA achieves 44.3%, matching the target-trained Minimax (44.3%) while outperforming D4M by a massive +16.4%. On the 10-class subsets, CoDA outperforms the prior SOTA (DiT+MGD³) by 2.6–5.5% on ImageIDC and 0.9–3.6% on ImageNette across IPC=10/20/50, and by up to 9.6% on ImageNet-A.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDA decisively outperforms all general text-to-image baselines: SDXL alone (40.1%→58.5% on IDC-10), D⁴M (41.1%→58.5%), and SDXL+MGD³ (47.2%→58.5%), with the largest gap over D⁴M reaching +16.4% on ImageNet-1K at IPC=10. Against target-trained diffusion methods, CoDA matches Minimax exactly on ImageNet-1K IPC=10 (44.3% vs 44.3%) and narrowly beats IGD at IPC=50 (60.4% vs 60.3%), while substantially outperforming DiT+MGD³ (the prior SOTA) by 2.6–5.5% on ImageIDC/Nette. On the fine-grained ImageWoof benchmark, CoDA surpasses the prior SOTA (MGD³) by 2.6% at IPC=100 (71.4% vs 68.8%) and by 2.9% at IPC=50. Against non-diffusion paradigms, CoDA dominates: RDED (42.0% at IPC=10 on ImageNet-1K), G-VBSM (31.4%), and SRe2L (21.3%) are all far behind. On ImageNet-A through E, CoDA outperforms the prior SOTA (MGD³) by 9.6%, 8.4%, 7.5%, 4.8%, and 6.6% respectively, and also beats LD3M, GLaD, and H-PD by wide margins. The only setting where CoDA does not lead is ImageWoof at IPC≤20, where target-trained MGD³ retains a 0.5–3.3% edge, though CoDA still outperforms all general-model baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoDA positions itself against two lines of diffusion-based dataset distillation: (1) methods like Minimax, IGD, and MGD³ that require a diffusion model pre-trained on the full target dataset (which the authors argue is conceptually circular, since DD aims to avoid full-dataset training costs), and (2) methods like D⁴M that use off-the-shelf text-to-image models but suffer from distributional mismatch. CoDA builds on the LDM/SDXL generative backbone and the CFG framework, but departs from prior prototype-selection approaches (which use K-Means in VAE space) by introducing a UMAP + HDBSCAN density-based pipeline to identify an 'intrinsic core distribution,' and from D⁴M's initialization-only strategy by applying continuous, per-timestep noise-space guidance throughout the entire reverse diffusion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CoDA eliminates the need for target-specific diffusion model training (saving ~3,000 TPU-days for ImageNet-scale tasks) while matching or exceeding those methods' performance, and enables zero-cost domain transfer to new datasets (Places365, DRIVE) without retraining. Key limitations include per-dataset hyperparameter sensitivity (UMAP n_neighbors and HDBSCAN min_cluster_size shift across datasets), the absence of an explicit de-biasing mechanism, and the fact that the method still requires access to the full target dataset for the Discovery stage. Open questions include automatic hyperparameter selection via meta-learning and whether the guidance framework can be extended to video or 3D generation.
