## Problem

Existing model-predictive shielding methods for safe reinforcement learning rely on state-space partitioning into local linear models, making them computationally intractable beyond ~10 dimensions and thus inapplicable to the high-dimensional, nonlinear environments where deep RL excels. This paper addresses the gap by making formal, certificate-based shielding scalable to systems with over 300 state dimensions while maintaining real-time computational efficiency.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the shield receives the current lifted state z_k, the RL agent's proposed action a_π, a learned affine dynamics model (matrices A, B and offset c), a worst-case per-step error bound ε, and polyhedral safe-set constraints defined as intersections of half-spaces. It outputs a single minimally invasive safe action u_0 (the first element of an optimized H-step sequence, with the remaining actions discarded under a receding-horizon scheme) along with a feasibility flag; if the QP is infeasible at all candidate horizons, a precomputed backup policy is invoked instead.

## Method

RAMPS couples a learned linear dynamics model—ranging from simple linear regression in the original state space to a Deep Koopman Operator in a high-dimensional feature space—with a novel robust multi-step Control Barrier Function that enforces safety at every intermediate timestep j over an adaptively selected horizon H ≥ r_i (the relative degree of constraint i). The key mechanism is an accumulated error tightening term E_j(p_i) = Σ_k ε‖p_i^T A^k‖_1 that grows with the prediction horizon, ensuring the entire uncertainty tube around the nominal trajectory stays within the safe polyhedron. At each step the shield solves a small QP minimizing ‖u_0 − a_π‖² subject to all multi-step CBF inequalities and action bounds, with the horizon chosen by bounded binary search to maximize foresight while preserving feasibility. The framework operates iteratively: environment data trains the linear model and error bound, the shielded RL agent (PPO or SAC) collects new safe trajectories, and the model is periodically re-estimated, creating a cycle where improved model accuracy yields a less conservative shield.

## Results

The primary metric is cumulative safety violations during training across five environments. RAMPS+K+SAC achieves the lowest violations in most settings: 25±26 (Pendulum), 49±10 (SafeHopper), 21±4 (SafeCheetah), and 11±7 (SafeHumanoid), representing up to a 90% reduction over the best CMDP baseline. RAMPS+K+PPO is competitive at 53±6, 172±15, 26±17, 111±23, and 154±25 respectively. Per-step shield computation time ranges from 0.23 ms (Pendulum) to 0.40 ms (SafeAnt), confirming real-time feasibility even in the 348-dimensional SafeHumanoid environment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three CMDP baselines (SauteRL, CUP, P3O), RAMPS variants achieve substantially fewer violations in every environment: on SafeAnt, RAMPS+K+PPO records 111 violations versus 1221–1883 for the CMDP methods, and on SafeHumanoid, RAMPS+K+SAC records 11 versus 172–319. Against the architecturally similar SPICE shielding method, RAMPS+L+PPO reduces Pendulum violations from 495 (SPICE+L) to 69, and SPICE+L fails entirely on all four high-dimensional locomotion tasks while RAMPS completes them. SPICE+K (Koopman variant) also fails on SafeAnt and SafeHumanoid, whereas RAMPS+K+PPO achieves 111 and 154 violations respectively. The paper notes that other MPS/MPC methods (DMPS, VELM, MASE, Conservative Safety Critics) accumulated over 1000 violations within the first 20–30k interactions and were excluded from the main table. Overall, RAMPS wins on every environment where a baseline completes training, with the largest margins on the high-dimensional locomotion tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAMPS extends the model-predictive shielding paradigm (SPICE, DMPS, VELM) by replacing the state-space partitioning into local linear models—which confines prior symbolic shields to ~10 dimensions—with a single global linear dynamics model (linear regression or Deep Koopman Operator). The safety mechanism itself is a novel multi-step robust Control Barrier Function that synthesizes ideas from High-Order CBFs (Tan et al., 2022) for handling relative-degree traps, discrete-time robust CBFs (Cosner et al., 2023) for worst-case error margins, and receding-horizon MPC for adaptive prediction. The key departure from prior MPS work is that the linear model structure makes multi-step constraint propagation computationally tractable, whereas prior methods either used one-step filters or expensive nonlinear rollouts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a significant scalability gap: formal, certificate-based safety shielding was previously intractable beyond ~10 state dimensions, while the high-dimensional locomotion tasks (348-dim SafeHumanoid) where deep RL excels had no principled safety mechanism. The practical impact is strong—0.23–0.40 ms per-step QP solve enables real-time deployment, and the 90% violation reduction over CMDP baselines is backed by multiple environments and seeds. Open questions include whether the polyhedral safe-set assumption and linear-model requirement limit applicability to non-convex safety regions or highly nonlinear dynamics where even Koopman embeddings struggle, and whether the conditional QP-feasibility guarantee (satisfied >98% of the time empirically) can be made unconditional.
