## Problem

Diffusion language models promise faster inference than autoregressive models by unmasking tokens in parallel, but the theoretical limits of this advantage remain unexplored: how much sequential computation do DLMs truly save, what memory does their parallelism require, and do inference-time mechanisms like remasking and revision provide genuine expressivity gains? This paper provides the first rigorous circuit-complexity-based foundation answering all three questions.

## Input / Output

The DLM takes a prompt q ∈ V^n (or an empty string for unconditional generation) and produces an output o ∈ V^m extracted from the last m positions of a length-L sequence. The framework uses a binary vocabulary {a, b} with a mask token M, and generation proceeds over D decoding rounds where each round unmaskes a subset of positions via a policy F. The key structural constraint is that the predictor p(·|x_t) must produce conditionally independent token distributions across positions, and without remasking or revision, unmasked tokens are permanently fixed.

## Method

The paper formalizes DLMs through Boolean circuit complexity, mapping circuit depth to minimum sequential steps and circuit width to minimum memory. Theorem 3.1 shows that a DLM with polynomial-length CoT and length L = N (circuit size) can simulate any depth-d circuit in exactly d decoding steps, matching the optimal parallel time, whereas autoregressive models require O(N) steps. Theorems 3.2–3.3 further show that enabling remasking (L = 2w + 2⌈log(d+1)⌉) or revision (L = w + ⌈log(d+1)⌉) reduces the sequence length to scale linearly with circuit width w rather than size N, achieving optimal space. Finally, a strict expressivity separation is established via the parity distribution D_n^⊕: DLMs with revision or remasking sample it in O(1) steps using AC⁰ circuits (Theorems 4.1–4.2), while standard DLMs provably cannot do so in O(1) steps (Theorem 4.5), leveraging Håstad's average-case lower bound for parity against AC⁰ circuits.

## Results

The paper establishes that DLMs with CoT can simulate any Boolean circuit of depth d in exactly d decoding steps (Theorem 3.1), matching the minimum possible sequential steps, whereas autoregressive models require O(N) steps where N is the total circuit size. With remasking, the required sequence length drops to L = 2w + 2⌈log(d+1)⌉ (Theorem 3.2), and with revision to L = w + ⌈log(d+1)⌉ (Theorem 3.3), both achieving optimal space scaling linearly in circuit width w. A strict expressivity separation is proven: DLMs with revision sample the even-parity distribution D_n^⊕ in 2 steps (Theorem 4.1), DLMs with remasking do so in O(1) steps (Theorem 4.2), while DLMs without either mechanism provably cannot sample D_n^⊕ in O(1) steps (Theorem 4.5).

**Table 1: Summary of theoretical guarantees for DLM configurations.**

| Configuration | Sequential Steps | Sequence Length L | Can sample D_n^⊕ in O(1) steps? | Circuit depth of p, F |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DLM + CoT (Theorem 3.1) | d (optimal) | N (circuit size) | No (Theorem 4.5) | O(1) |
| DLM + CoT + Remasking (Theorem 3.2) | d + 1 | 2w + 2⌈log(d+1)⌉ | Yes, O(1) (Theorem 4.2) | O(log d) |
| DLM + CoT + Revision (Theorem 3.3) | d + 1 | w + ⌈log(d+1)⌉ | Yes, 2 steps (Theorem 4.1) | O(log d) |
| AR + CoT (Li et al., 2024) | O(N) | N | — | — |

*Here d = circuit depth, w = circuit width, N = total circuit size. D_n^⊕ is the uniform distribution over n-bit strings with even parity.*

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's comparisons are entirely theoretical rather than empirical. Against autoregressive models with CoT (Li et al., 2024), DLMs achieve a strict improvement in sequential steps: d steps versus O(N) steps, where N is the total number of circuit nodes, giving an exponential gap in depth for deep-but-narrow circuits. Among DLM variants, the key comparison is the expressivity separation: DLMs with revision or remasking can sample the even-parity distribution D_n^⊕ in constant steps using AC^0 circuits, while DLMs without these mechanisms provably cannot do so in O(1) steps (Theorem 4.5), establishing a strict expressivity gap. In terms of space, revision is strictly more efficient than remasking, requiring L = w + ⌈log(d+1)⌉ versus L = 2w + 2⌈log(d+1)⌉, since revision can overwrite tokens in place while remasking requires alternating between two memory blocks. The paper notes a practical caveat: although DLMs use O(d) steps versus O(N) for AR, each DLM step requires O(L) FLOPs (computing attention for all positions), so wall-clock speedup depends on hardware parallelism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the circuit-complexity analysis of autoregressive models by Li et al. (2024), who showed that AR models with CoT can simulate any Boolean circuit but require sequential steps linear in circuit size. The key departure is showing that DLMs with CoT achieve optimal depth (matching circuit depth rather than size), and that remasking or revision further yields optimal space scaling with circuit width. The expressivity separation leverages classical AC0 lower bounds for PARITY (Håstad, 2014) to prove that standard DLMs are strictly less powerful than those with remasking or revision, a distinction not previously formalized in the DLM literature (Austin et al., 2021; Nie et al., 2025; Song et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous theoretical foundation for DLMs as parallel samplers, providing a clear formal justification for why DLMs should outperform AR models in sequential steps and why remasking/revision are not merely engineering tricks but expressivity-critical mechanisms. The practical caveat is significant: the paper itself notes that a DLM requires O(L) FLOPs per decoding step versus O(1) for an AR model with KV caching, so the depth advantage only translates to wall-clock speedup if hardware supports sufficient parallelism. Open questions include whether the theoretical optimal-depth and optimal-space constructions are achievable by practical Transformer-based DLMs, and whether more flexible forward processes (beyond binary remasking/revision) could close remaining gaps.
