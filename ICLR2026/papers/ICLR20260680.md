## Problem

Current generative adversarial attacks treat the perturbation generator as a black-box tool for producing adversarial examples, optimizing only surrogate-level objectives while ignoring how the generator's intermediate blocks progressively degrade object-aligned semantic structure. This causes perturbations to scatter onto object-irrelevant regions, weakening black-box transferability. The paper asks at which stage of perturbation synthesis semantic cues deteriorate and which generator blocks most influence transfer across unseen models, domains, and tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260680/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single benign RGB image and produces a bounded adversarial image in one forward pass of a feedforward generator (ResNet-based), with a projector enforcing an ℓ∞ perturbation budget ε. No iterative optimization is needed at test time. Evaluation spans classification (ImageNet-1K, CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, FGVC Aircraft) using four metrics—Accuracy, ASR, Fooling Rate, and the newly proposed Accidental Correction Rate—and dense prediction tasks (semantic segmentation via mIoU, object detection via mAP50) across CNN, Transformer, Mixer, and Mamba victim architectures.

## Method

SCGA augments any existing generative attack with a Mean Teacher pathway: a teacher generator is maintained via exponential moving average (EMA) of the student generator's parameters, providing temporally smoothed reference features. A hinge-based self-feature consistency loss (cosine similarity with a learnable per-block weight) aligns the student's early-block activations (first two residual blocks) with the teacher's corresponding features, anchoring object contours and coarse shape before perturbation mass emerges in later stages. The adversarial loss on surrogate intermediate features remains unchanged, and the total objective is L_adv + λ_cons · L_cons. This guidance operates exclusively during training, adding no inference-time overhead, and integrates seamlessly into baselines such as CDA, LTP, BIA, GAMA, FACL, and PDCL. The paper further introduces ACR as a complementary reliability metric that captures inadvertent corrections of initially wrong predictions, addressing blind spots in conventional ASR/FR/Accuracy evaluation.

## Results

In cross-domain transfer (ImageNet→CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, FGVC Aircraft), augmenting CDA with SCGA yields the largest gains: +21.48% ASR and +20.63% FR on CUB, +27.74% ASR on Aircraft, while reducing ACR by 3.94–6.00 percentage points. Cross-task improvements are also consistent, with LTP+SCGA reducing mIoU by 2.54 and mAP50 by 1.39 on segmentation and detection respectively. In cross-model transfer (Table 2), CNN victims show the strongest ASR gains (e.g., +20.13% for CDA on ResNet-50), while Transformer and Mamba victims show smaller or neutral changes. Against adversarially trained models and input pre-processing defenses (Table 4), SCGA improves average ASR from 45.59% to 51.63% and FR from 51.60% to 57.86% over the BIA baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCGA delivers the most dramatic improvements when combined with CDA (logit-level relativistic loss), boosting ASR by +21.48% on CUB and +27.74% on FGVC Aircraft, because the early-block semantic anchoring complements CDA's coarse logit-space objective. LTP (frequency-domain loss) also benefits substantially (+11.72% ASR on CUB, +10.98% on Cars) and shows the largest cross-task gains (−2.54 mIoU, −1.39 mAP50). BIA (the primary baseline) sees moderate cross-domain gains (+6.89% ASR on Cars) but near-zero improvement on CUB (+0.08%), suggesting its intermediate-feature objective already partially captures object structure. GAMA and PDCL show smaller, more uniform gains (+2.5–3.1% ASR), with PDCL's CLIP-space objective partially saturating the benefit of local structural consistency. Notably, FACL is the one baseline where SCGA slightly degrades CUB performance (−3.79% ASR, +3.12% Acc), likely because its frequency-aware contrastive loss already enforces a spectral structure that conflicts with the early-block anchor. Overall, the method is most effective when the baseline's surrogate objective operates at a level (logits, frequency) that does not already encode fine-grained object geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative adversarial attack lineage (GAP, CDA, LTP, BIA, GAMA, FACL, PDCL), which trains a feedforward perturbation generator against a surrogate model to produce adversarial examples in a single forward pass. The key departure is shifting focus from the surrogate-level objective (logits, intermediate features, frequency domain) to the generator's own internal dynamics: the authors partition the generator into early/mid/late blocks and show that early blocks preserve object-aligned semantics more stably. They then import the Mean Teacher EMA mechanism (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) and a BYOL-style hinge consistency loss (Grill et al., 2020) to anchor the student generator's early-block activations to a temporally smoothed teacher, a combination not previously applied to adversarial perturbation synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, plug-and-play regularizer that can be added to any existing generative attack without altering the test-time pipeline, and it introduces the Accidental Correction Rate (ACR) metric to expose a blind spot in standard ASR/FR/Accuracy evaluation where adversarial perturbations inadvertently correct initially wrong predictions. The consistent but modest gains (typically 1–4 percentage points in ASR/FR) and the observation that CLIP-based objectives (PDCL) dilute the benefit suggest the approach is most valuable for CNN-centric surrogate settings. Open questions include whether the early-block semantic anchoring generalizes to non-ResNet generator architectures, whether the effect persists under stronger defenses, and whether the ACR metric will be adopted as a standard reporting requirement in the adversarial-robustness community.
