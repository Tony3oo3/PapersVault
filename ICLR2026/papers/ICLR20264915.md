## Problem

Deterministic attention backward passes in LLM training (e.g., FlashAttention-3) suffer up to 37.9% throughput loss relative to their non-deterministic counterparts, because serialized gradient accumulation creates pipeline bubbles. The paper identifies that this penalty is not inherent to serialization but stems from a misalignment between tile execution order and the fixed accumulation order, leaving the scheduling problem unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264915/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates within the FlashAttention-3 kernel on BF16 attention matrices (Q, K, V), the forward output O, and upstream gradient dO, partitioned into KV tiles (one per SM) and Q tiles. It produces bitwise-reproducible gradient tensors dQ, dK, and dV, where dQ requires a global reduction across KV tiles in a fixed order while dK and dV are computed via local per-SM reductions. The scheduling is parameterized by the attention mask type (full or causal), sequence length (512–16,384), and head dimension (64 or 128), with the number of KV tiles assumed equal to the number of SMs.

## Method

DASH formulates the deterministic attention backward as a DAG scheduling problem where each tile task is a compute-then-reduce chain, and the objective is to minimize critical path length. A key theoretical result (Lemma 1) proves that zero-weight dependency edges preserve the original critical path if and only if they are depth-monotone (depth(u) ≤ depth(v)), which translates to the constraint that tasks contributing to the same dQ block must not execute concurrently. Building on this, two complementary strategies are proposed: (i) Descending Q-Tile Iteration, a heuristic that reverses query-block traversal to resolve causal-mask dependencies earlier and eliminate inter-head pipeline bubbles; and (ii) Shift Scheduling (full mask) / Symmetric Shift Scheduling (causal mask), which assigns KV tiles to SMs via cyclic or symmetric pairing to achieve perfectly balanced workloads while the phase-shifted Q-tile order inherently produces a conflict-free, depth-monotone reduction sequence, yielding a provably optimal schedule under the DAG model.

## Results

On NVIDIA H800 GPUs, DASH improves the deterministic attention backward pass throughput by up to 1.28× over the FlashAttention-3 deterministic baseline across sequence lengths of 512–16,384 and head dimensions of 64 and 128. End-to-end, a full transformer block (forward + backward) achieves 2%–10% speedup for causal-mask LLMs (LLaMA3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-8×7B) and approximately 4% for full-mask models (SAM-huge, Stable Diffusion 3.5, LLaDA-1B), yielding an average ~5% speedup. Deterministic execution guarantees bitwise-identical gradients (max deviation = 0), whereas the non-deterministic baseline exhibits max gradient deviations of 2.4×10⁻⁴ (full mask) and 4.9×10⁻⁴ (causal mask) averaged over 10 runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the deterministic backward pass of FlashAttention-3. DASH's Shift Scheduling outperforms this baseline across most sequence lengths under full masks (headdim 64 and 128), though it slightly underperforms at the maximum sequence length of 16,384 due to remote L2-cache synchronization latency on H800's distributed L2. Under causal masks, both Descending Q-Tile Iteration and Symmetric Shift Scheduling consistently beat the FlashAttention-3 baseline across all tested configurations; at headdim 64, Symmetric Shift Scheduling achieves the highest throughput, while at headdim 128 the simpler Descending Q-Tile Iteration wins because the more complex symmetric schedule triggers register spilling. The secondary baseline is the Triton tutorial's causal-attention implementation, which DASH also outperforms in throughput. Overall, DASH wins on every configuration tested against both baselines, with the largest gains (up to 1.28×) appearing at intermediate sequence lengths where pipeline bubbles are most pronounced in the baseline schedule.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends FlashAttention-3's deterministic backward pass by formulating its tile scheduling as a DAG critical-path minimization problem, a formalization absent from prior deterministic attention implementations. While existing deterministic approaches (Triton tutorials, FlashAttention-2) trade bandwidth or memory by splitting computation into separate passes or materializing per-tile partials, DASH instead co-optimizes the execution schedule and the accumulation order within a single kernel. The shift-based scheduling draws conceptual inspiration from cyclic phase-shift patterns in distributed attention (RingAttention, StripedAttention, LoongTrain), but applies them intra-GPU to balance workload and enforce conflict-free reduction ordering across SMs rather than across devices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Deterministic training is becoming standard practice in industry-scale LLM training, and the 37.9% throughput penalty of FlashAttention-3's deterministic mode translates to substantial cost at thousands-of-GPU scale; DASH narrows this gap by up to 1.28× on the attention backward pass (~5% end-to-end for transformer blocks). A key open question is whether the theoretically optimal Symmetric Shift Scheduling will become practically superior on next-generation hardware (e.g., Blackwell with TMEM or larger register files) where the register-pressure bottleneck observed on H800 disappears. The finding that L2 cache latency at extreme parallelism can negate algorithmic gains also suggests the DAG model needs refinement for future architectures with different memory hierarchies.
