## Problem

Existing frameworks for performing deep learning tasks on NeRF weights (nf2vec, Cardace et al.) are each locked to a single, fixed NeRF architecture, making them unable to process the wide variety of NeRF designs (MLPs, tri-planes, hash tables) that are actively being developed. This paper addresses the gap by introducing the first architecture-agnostic framework that can ingest NeRFs of diverse architectures and even generalize to architectural variants unseen at training time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264145/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes NeRF weights from any of 13 architectures across three families (MLPs, tri-planes, multi-resolution hash tables) and converts them into parameter graphs, where each weight corresponds to a single edge. A key design choice is the novel hash-table-to-graph conversion that uses one node per table entry and one per feature dimension, preserving the memory efficiency of hashing (O(N(T+F)) nodes) rather than explicitly modeling the underlying voxel grid (O(N(R³+F)) nodes). The encoder produces a 1024-dimensional L2-normalized embedding that serves as input to downstream classifiers, k-NN retrieval, or an LLM conditioned on the embedding for captioning and Q&A.

## Method

The framework pairs a Graph Meta-Network (GMN) encoder, which performs message-passing over the NeRF parameter graph and pools edge features into a fixed-size embedding, with the nf2vec decoder that reconstructs radiance field values from the embedding plus frequency-encoded 3D coordinates. Training is end-to-end with a combined loss: a rendering loss (smooth L1 between volume-rendered colors from the original NeRF and the decoder) that enforces class-level separability, and a SigLIP contrastive loss that pulls together embeddings of different architectures representing the same object while pushing apart embeddings of different objects. The key empirical finding is that the rendering loss alone causes distinct architecture-specific clusters in latent space even for identical objects, while the contrastive term is essential for architecture invariance; the combined loss strikes a balance, preserving class separation while enabling cross-architecture alignment. The GMN's graph-based design inherently generalizes to any architecture for which a parameter graph can be constructed, enabling zero-shot processing of unseen architectural variants at inference time.

## Results

In the multi-architecture classification setting (encoder trained on ALL), L_R achieves 93.6%, 94.0%, and 92.4% accuracy on MLP, TRI, and HASH test sets respectively, while L_{R+C} achieves 90.7%, 90.6%, and 90.0%. In the single-architecture setting, L_R outperforms nf2vec on MLP (93.6% vs. 92.1%) and Cardace et al. on TRI (94.0% vs. 93.1%), and is the only method applicable to HASH (92.5%). For cross-architecture retrieval, L_{R+C} achieves 72.67% Recall@10 (MLP→TRI) and 42.83% (HASH→MLP), dramatically outperforming L_R (8.19% and 6.09%) and the random baseline (~0.25%). On language tasks, L_{R+C} matches LLaNA in the single-architecture setting (e.g., 80.9 vs. 81.0 S-BERT for brief captioning) while being the only method that generalizes to TRI and HASH test sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

nf2vec (Zama Ramirez et al., 2024) is limited to MLP-based NeRFs and cannot be applied in the multi-architecture setting. In the single-architecture setting, L_R outperforms nf2vec on MLP classification (93.6% vs. 92.1%) and on unseen MLP-2L (91.8% vs. 63.7%), demonstrating superior generalization to architectural variations. Cardace et al. (2024) is limited to tri-planar NeRFs; L_R edges it out on TRI classification (94.0% vs. 93.1%) but underperforms on TRI-2L (92.6% vs. 93.5%) and TRI-32H (82.5% vs. 92.0%) because Cardace discards the MLP entirely, making it invariant to MLP changes. LLaNA (Amaduzzi et al., 2024) is the only language-task baseline, restricted to MLP; L_{R+C} achieves near-identical S-BERT scores in the single-architecture setting (80.9 vs. 81.0 for brief captioning, 74.1 vs. 77.4 for detailed captioning) while uniquely extending to TRI and HASH test sets with minimal degradation (<1 point). Overall, the paper's method wins or ties on all directly comparable single-architecture benchmarks and is the sole method capable of the multi-architecture and hash-table settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research threads: (1) NeRF weight-space processing, initiated by nf2vec (Zama Ramirez et al., 2024) for MLPs and Cardace et al. (2024) for tri-planes, both of which are locked to a single fixed architecture; and (2) Graph Meta-Networks (Lim et al., 2024; Kofinas et al., 2024), which can ingest arbitrary neural architectures via parameter graphs but had never been applied to NeRFs. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a SigLIP contrastive loss (Zhai et al., 2023) alongside the rendering loss, motivated by the empirical finding that rendering loss alone produces architecture-specific clusters in latent space even for identical objects. The paper also contributes the first parameter graph construction for multi-resolution hash tables, extending Lim et al.'s formulation beyond linear layers and tri-planes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to perform downstream tasks (classification, retrieval, captioning, Q&A) on NeRFs parameterized by diverse architectures, including hash tables—the most widely adopted NeRF design in recent literature—thereby removing a significant practical barrier to NeRF weight-space processing. The finding that a rendering loss alone is insufficient for architecture invariance, and that a contrastive objective is necessary to align cross-architecture embeddings, provides a clear design principle for future NeRF representation learning. The main limitation is that evaluation is concentrated on ShapenetRender (13 classes, synthetic objects), with Objaverse serving only as a generalization probe; scaling to large-scale, diverse NeRF datasets (e.g., full Objaverse training) remains an open question that would be needed before this could serve as a true foundational model for NeRF weight space.
