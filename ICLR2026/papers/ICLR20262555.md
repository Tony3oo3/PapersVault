## Problem

Existing multivariate time series forecasting models are constrained by fixed look-back windows, making them unable to capture global periodic patterns (weekly, monthly, seasonal) whose cycles extend far beyond the observed history. Naively extending the window introduces overfitting, prohibitive computational costs, and redundant information, leaving a gap in principled access to long-range periodicity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262555/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a multivariate time series of shape T×N (T=96 in all experiments) together with the absolute time position t₀ of the sequence's first observation within the global cycle, which is used to compute per-step cycle indices for retrieval. It outputs a forecast of S future steps (S ∈ {96,192,336,720} for long-term or {12,24,48,96} for short-term) of shape S×N. The global cycle length L is a per-dataset hyperparameter (e.g., 24 for hourly ETTh, 168 for Electricity, 288 for PEMS) that defines the size of the learnable embedding matrix.

## Method

GTR maintains a learnable global temporal embedding Q ∈ R^{L×N} (zero-initialized) that encodes the full cycle pattern. For each input, it computes a cycle index vector i = [(t₀ mod L) + τ] mod L to locate every time step within the global cycle, retrieves the corresponding segment from Q via a linear projection, and stacks it with the original input to form a 2×T tensor. A 2D convolution with kernel (2, 1+2⌊P/2⌋) — where P is the dominant high-frequency period — jointly models local and global temporal interactions, and the result is fused back via a residual connection with dropout. The enriched representation is then passed through a lightweight two-layer MLP backbone (GeLU activations, residual) and a linear output projection, with RevIN applied to handle distribution shift. The entire module is plug-and-play: it preserves input dimensionality, adds only ~40K parameters, and can be prepended to any forecasting backbone without architectural modification.

## Results

On long-term forecasting (T=96, S∈{96,192,336,720}), GTR achieves top-2 MSE in 5/8 datasets and top-2 MAE in 5/8 datasets, with best MSE on Electricity (0.166), Solar-Energy (0.194), Weather (0.239), ETTm1 (0.367), and ETTm2 (0.268). On short-term forecasting (T=96, S∈{12,24,48,96}), GTR achieves top-2 in all 8/8 tasks, winning both MSE and MAE on every PEMS dataset (e.g., PEMS03: MSE 0.087, MAE 0.189). The combined system (GTR + MLP) uses only 0.98M parameters and 306.91M MACs—just 19% of iTransformer's parameter count—while training in 22.3 s/epoch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTR outperforms all 10 baselines on the majority of datasets, with the most consistent wins over TimeMixer, PatchTST, and DLinear, which it beats on every dataset for both MSE and MAE. Against the strongest recent baselines, GTR edges out RAFT on 7/8 long-term datasets (losing only on ETTh1: 0.439 vs. 0.428 MSE) and dominates S-Mamba on 7/8 (losing on Traffic: 0.470 vs. 0.414 MSE). On short-term PEMS forecasting, GTR achieves the best MSE and MAE on all four datasets, reducing MSE by 28.7% over S-Mamba on PEMS03 (0.087 vs. 0.122) and by 18.7% on average over iTransformer. The one notable weakness is the Traffic dataset, where inter-variable spatial dependencies favor models like SOFTS (0.409) and S-Mamba (0.414) over GTR's channel-independent design (0.470). On Solar-Energy, GTR's advantage is most pronounced, exceeding CycleNet by 8.2% MSE and 6.5% MAE, and outperforming RAFT by 35.5% MSE (0.194 vs. 0.301).

## Relation to Prior Work

GTR carves new ground at the intersection of three existing lines of work: seasonal-trend decomposition (Autoformer, TimeMixer), 2D structural transformation (TimesNet, Times2D), and retrieval-augmented forecasting (RAFT, TS-RAG). Unlike decomposition and frequency-domain methods that operate strictly within a fixed look-back window, or retrieval methods that rely on similarity search over raw history, GTR introduces a learnable global cycle embedding indexed by absolute temporal position, enabling positional (rather than similarity-based) retrieval of long-range periodic context. The 2D convolution for local-global fusion draws on the 2D-variation modeling tradition but applies it to a stacked [local; global] tensor rather than a restructured time-frequency layout, and the residual plug-and-play integration departs from architectures that require end-to-end redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GTR addresses a concrete and common failure mode—global cycles longer than the look-back window remain invisible to the model—without requiring users to extend input length or redesign their backbone, making it immediately adoptable. Key open questions remain: the fixed shared cycle-length assumption limits applicability to non-stationary or multi-periodic series, the O(NT²) linear projection becomes prohibitive for long inputs, and learning very long (e.g., multi-year) cycles from limited data is unresolved. The channel-independent design also leaves inter-variable dependency modeling to the host architecture, as evidenced by the need for the auxiliary GTA module on Traffic.
