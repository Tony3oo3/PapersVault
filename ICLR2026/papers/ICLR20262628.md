## Problem

While large reasoning models generate long chains-of-thought effectively in English, it remains unclear whether these long-form reasoning abilities transfer to the world's other languages. Prior work on multilingual reasoning focused on short CoT settings or English-only reasoning, leaving a blind spot on whether sustained, multi-step reasoning in non-English languages is fundamentally limited by comprehension, reasoning, or both. This paper systematically investigates how long CoT capabilities extend beyond English across all four stages of model development: scaling, pretraining, post-training, and inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem or general reasoning question presented in one of nine target languages (Chinese, French, Japanese, Afrikaans, Thai, Latvian, Marathi, Telugu, Swahili) and produces a long chain-of-thought (often tens of thousands of tokens) followed by a final answer. Three reasoning settings are evaluated: En-Only (English input and reasoning), En-CoT (target-language input, English reasoning), and Target-CoT (target-language input and reasoning). Language forcing via seeded translated phrases controls the reasoning language, and outputs are validated for language compliance (≥90% target-language chunks) before being scored for correctness.

## Method

The paper conducts a controlled empirical study that isolates one factor at a time across the model development pipeline. For scaling, it uses the DeepSeek-R1-Distill family (1.5B–32B) with identical post-training to attribute performance changes to parameter count. For pretraining, it compares four backbones (Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Qwen3-8B-Base, Gemma3-12B-PT) under identical English SFT to disentangle domain-specialized reasoning pretraining from broad multilingual coverage. For post-training, it fine-tunes on 1,000 synthetic traces created via two strategies—translating gold English reasoning traces with Gemini-2.0-Flash versus distilling target-language traces from DeepSeek-R1 with language forcing—and compares both against a 20k English-trace baseline. The key decomposition into En-CoT (measuring comprehension) and Target-CoT (measuring comprehension + reasoning) reveals that scaling and multilingual pretraining close the comprehension gap but fail to enable sustained target-language reasoning, while lightweight post-training on just 1,000 translated traces outperforms 20× more English data for mid- and low-resource languages.

## Results

On MATH-500 (Target-CoT), fine-tuning Qwen3-8B-Base on Translated-s1k (1,000 translated English traces) achieves 78.3% average across 9 non-English languages, up from 62.6% for the 20k English-trace baseline (OpenThoughts3-20k). The gain is most dramatic for low-resource languages: Marathi +25.2%, Telugu +42.4%, and Swahili +43.2% over the English baseline. On AIME-Combined, Translated-s1k reaches 20.8% average (vs. 16.2% baseline), and on MMLU-ProX it reaches 45.5% (vs. 43.8%). Notably, the 1,000-trace target-language fine-tune surpasses the SOTA Qwen3-8B (Thinking) on all three low-resource languages for MATH-500 (e.g., Swahili: 64.4% vs. 8.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen3-8B (Thinking), the SOTA reasoning model at this scale, the paper's Translated-s1k fine-tune wins decisively on all three low-resource languages for MATH-500 (Marathi 70.8 vs. 49.0, Telugu 72.4 vs. 47.4, Swahili 64.4 vs. 8.4) and on MMLU-ProX for Marathi (40.0 vs. 32.0) and Telugu (34.0 vs. 30.8), but loses on high-resource languages (e.g., Chinese 87.2 vs. 94.0, French 87.0 vs. 92.0 on MATH-500) and on AIME-Combined across all languages (20.8% avg vs. 27.5% for Qwen3-8B). Against the English-only OpenThoughts3-20k baseline (20× more data), Translated-s1k achieves large gains for mid- and low-resource languages (e.g., +43.2% on Swahili MATH-500) but modest or negative changes for high-resource languages (e.g., −7.6% on Japanese MATH-500), indicating cross-lingual transfer from English already covers those cases. Against Distilled-s1k (the alternative synthetic-data strategy), Translated-s1k is consistently stronger, with the largest gap in Chinese (+27.2 on MATH-500, +18.6 on MMLU-ProX) and French (+8.2, +11.5), while the two are closer for mid/low-resource languages. At larger scale, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-70B and Llama-3.3-Nemotron-Super-49B confirm the persistent En-CoT/Target-CoT gap (28.4% and 40.8% average drops respectively), showing the problem is not resolved by simply scaling to 70B parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multilingual reasoning literature (Cobbe et al. 2021; Shi et al. 2023; Ko et al. 2025), which established that English CoT outperforms target-language CoT in the short-CoT setting primarily due to input comprehension gaps, to the long-CoT regime where sustained multi-step reasoning in the target language becomes the dominant bottleneck. It also builds on recent cross-lingual test-time scaling work (Yong et al. 2025; Son et al. 2025) but departs by systematically isolating the effects of scaling, pretraining, post-training, and inference across the full model development pipeline rather than focusing on a single stage. The decomposition of performance into comprehension (En-CoT) versus reasoning (Target-CoT) follows Ko et al. (2025), but the paper's central contribution is showing that the two factors interact fundamentally differently under long CoT than under short CoT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 7B model reasoning in English outperforms a 32B model reasoning in another language, and that domain-specialized pretraining actively degrades target-language reasoning even for languages included in the training data, challenges the default assumption that scaling and domain data will close multilingual reasoning gaps. Practically, the demonstration that 1,000 translated reasoning traces outperform 20,000 English traces for mid- and low-resource languages provides actionable guidance for practitioners with limited target-language data. Open questions include how these synthetic data approaches scale with more data, whether next-generation reasoning models will naturally close the target-language CoT gap, and how to address the language-specific generation failures (endless repetition, code-switching) that prevent models from reaching the reasoning stage entirely in low-resource settings.
