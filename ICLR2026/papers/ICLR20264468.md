## Problem

Existing offline multi-objective optimization methods rely on multi-stage pipelines that first train a surrogate model (DNN or GP) to approximate the black-box objective, then apply a separate search algorithm (e.g., NSGA-II) to optimize the learned proxy. This architecture bottlenecks performance on surrogate accuracy and inherits the risk of exploiting surrogate inaccuracies. The paper asks whether offline MOO can be reframed as a single conditional generation problem that directly produces high-quality solutions given target trade-offs, without any explicit objective-function approximation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (design vector x, objective vector y) pairs, where designs can be continuous, discrete (converted to logits), or categorical (up to 34 variables with 3–72 options each). At inference, it takes Q=256 novel conditioning points—generated by extrapolating well-performing data points along Riesz s-Energy reference direction vectors and adding Gaussian noise—and produces a corresponding set of 256 candidate solutions. Performance is evaluated via the Hypervolume indicator at the 100th, 75th, and 50th percentiles of the generated set, with a limited budget of Q new oracle queries allowed during evaluation.

## Method

PCD trains a conditional diffusion model (EDM formulation with a 4-layer Residual MLP denoiser and Random Fourier Feature noise encoding) to model p(x|y;σ), where y is the target objective vector. Two key mechanisms enable effective offline MOO: (1) a multi-objective reweighting scheme that partitions the objective space into bins and assigns each bin a weight combining its population size and the average dominance number of its points, biasing the denoising loss toward Pareto-front regions without discarding data; and (2) an NSGA-III-inspired reference-direction mechanism that generates diverse conditioning points by pairing non-dominated-sorted data points with Riesz s-Energy direction vectors, extrapolating along those vectors, and adding Gaussian noise for diversity. Sampling uses classifier-free guidance (γ=2.5) to strongly enforce adherence to the target trade-off, and a stochastic EDM SDE sampler for exploration. The entire pipeline is end-to-end: no surrogate predictor, scalarization function, or separate evolutionary optimizer is required.

## Results

PCD achieves the best overall average rank of 4.80±0.30 across 45 tasks spanning five categories (Synthetic, MORL, RE, Scientific Design, MONAS), evaluated by 100th-percentile Hypervolume (HV). Its strongest category is Real-World engineering (RE) with rank 1.51±0.13, followed by Scientific Design (4.05±0.33) and Synthetic (3.38±0.20). All results use a single fixed hyperparameter configuration, underscoring robustness. PCD also samples 256 solutions in ~2 seconds on an RTX 3080, roughly 3–20× faster than ParetoFlow depending on the task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest generative competitor ParetoFlow, PCD wins on 4 of 5 categories (MORL 5.50 vs 8.50, RE 1.51 vs 1.74, Scientific 4.05 vs 9.05, MONAS 7.54 vs 11.19) and loses only on Synthetic (3.38 vs 2.44). Against all 12 DNN-surrogate baselines (E2E, MH, MM variants with COMs/ICT/IOM/TM/PC/GN), PCD achieves a substantially lower average rank (4.80 vs 5.71–9.84), with the gap most pronounced on RE tasks where surrogates frequently fail (rank 0.00 HV on several RE33–RE42 tasks). The GP-based MOBO baseline (qNEHVI) is consistently the weakest method (avg. rank 8.81), particularly on MORL (14.60) and RE (10.00). PCD also outperforms the D(best) baseline (non-dominated points from the offline dataset) on 4 of 5 categories, confirming it generates solutions that genuinely improve upon the training data. The key differentiator is consistency: PCD uses one fixed hyperparameter set across all 45 tasks, whereas surrogate-based methods require per-task tuning and still exhibit high variance (e.g., E2E+GN MORL std = 2.14).

## Relation to Prior Work

PCD extends the line of single-objective conditional generation for optimization (MINs, DDOM) to the multi-objective setting, while building directly on the EDM diffusion framework and classifier-free guidance. It departs from the dominant surrogate-based paradigm in offline MOO (DNN/GP predictors + MOEA search) and from generative approaches like ParetoFlow that still require a surrogate predictor to steer generation. The reference-direction mechanism for conditioning point generation is adapted from NSGA-III's survival selection, and the reweighting strategy draws on importance-weighted training from prior conditional generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the surrogate model entirely, PCD removes the accuracy bottleneck that fundamentally limits all prior offline MOO pipelines, and achieves the best overall rank across five diverse task categories using a single fixed hyperparameter set—a practical robustness advantage in settings where tuning is infeasible. Key limitations remain: performance degrades on extremely high-dimensional continuous tasks (~10K dims, MORL) and purely categorical spaces (MONAS), pointing to the need for latent diffusion or discrete diffusion extensions. An open question is whether the reweighting strategy's sensitivity to dataset quality variance (it can harm performance on low-variance datasets) can be addressed with adaptive hyperparameter selection.
