## Problem

While empirical studies show that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) outputs are highly sensitive to input perturbations, no theoretical framework explains how perturbations propagate through the multistep reasoning process or what factors govern the resulting output fluctuation. This gap limits principled prompt optimization and leaves the robustness of CoT as an unexplained empirical phenomenon.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262296/cropped_page6_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a text query represented as a single embedding vector x ∈ R^d (the full user prompt, not individual tokens) and a perturbation δ, modeling CoT as K iterative steps where each hidden state feeds the next. For the practical prompt-selection application, the method consumes candidate prompts generated by TextGrad, OPRO, and CFPO, extracts the model's embedding-layer and final-layer vectors for each prompt, and outputs a single selected prompt per question that maximizes the certified perturbation radius. The output fluctuation is measured as normalized Shannon entropy over the set of answers produced by different prompts for the same question.

## Method

Under a Lipschitz continuity assumption on the per-step mapping f(h,x), the paper derives a closed-form upper bound on output fluctuation at step K (Theorem 1), showing it decomposes into a decaying hidden-state component (scaled by γ^K) and an accumulating input component (scaled by C(1−γ^K)/(1−γ)). This yields two key results: more reasoning steps tighten the bound, but even K→∞ leaves a non-zero residual perturbation tolerance of R(1−γ)/C (Equation 4). The analysis is then instantiated on Linear Self-Attention (LSA), where the Lipschitz constants C and γ are explicitly bounded in terms of the input norm R_x, hidden-state norm R_h, training-data covariance Γ, and residual coefficient η (Theorem 2), revealing that robustness is negatively correlated with both embedding and hidden-state norms. Experiments on Llama2-7b, Llama3.1-8b, Deepseek-R1-Distilled-Llama3.1-8b, and Qwen3-8b across MATH, MMLU-Pro, and GPQA validate all theoretical predictions, and a prompt-selection strategy that maximizes A^{-1} = 1/((R_x R_h)^2) achieves consistent EM gains over TextGrad, OPRO, and CFPO.

## Results

The paper derives theoretical upper bounds showing that (i) more CoT reasoning steps reduce output fluctuation but cannot eliminate it entirely (Equation 4), and (ii) robustness is negatively correlated with input embedding and hidden state vector norms (Theorem 2). Empirically, output fluctuation correlates with input perturbation (Pearson r = 0.619), input embedding norm (r = 0.506), and hidden state norm (r = 0.229) across four LLMs and three datasets. The proposed prompt-selection method (maximizing the certified perturbation radius) achieves the best EM on all settings in Table 3, with the largest gains on GPQA for Llama3.1-8b (32.3 vs. 27.6 best baseline) and MMLU-Pro for Qwen3-8b (49.2 vs. 45.9 best baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its norm-based prompt selection against three prompt optimization baselines: TextGrad (textual gradient descent), OPRO (LLM-as-optimizer), and CFPO (content-format integrated prompt optimization). On Llama3.1-8b, the proposed method wins on all three datasets: MATH 47.2 vs. CFPO 47.0, MMLU-Pro 49.0 vs. CFPO 48.1, and GPQA 32.3 vs. TextGrad/CFPO 27.6 (a +4.7 gain). On Qwen3-8b, it ties TextGrad on MATH (77.6) and GPQA (38.4) but clearly leads on MMLU-Pro with 49.2 vs. OPRO's 45.9 (+3.3). Notably, the base prompt already outperforms TextGrad and OPRO on MATH for Llama3.1-8b (46.8 vs. 45.2/44.6), suggesting that aggressive prompt rewriting can hurt performance on already-strong models, while the norm-guided selection consistently picks the most robust prompt without degrading accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the multistep iterative CoT modeling framework of Huang et al. (2025) and the Linear Self-Attention (LSA) optimal-parameter characterization of Zhang et al. (2024a) and Wang et al. (2020a), applying a Lipschitz-continuity lens (itself standard in robustness analysis, cf. Qi et al. 2023, Collins et al. 2025) to derive the first closed-form bounds on how input perturbations propagate through CoT reasoning steps. It departs from the dominant empirical tradition in CoT robustness (Zhao et al. 2024; Shi et al. 2024b) and from ad-hoc prompt optimization methods (TextGrad, OPRO, CFPO) by providing a theoretical account of *why* perturbations matter and *which* model-level quantities govern the tolerance radius, rather than merely observing sensitivity or searching for better prompts heuristically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that even an infinite number of CoT steps leaves a non-zero perturbation floor (Eq. 4) reframes the common intuition that 'more reasoning = more robust' and sets a hard ceiling on what longer chains can achieve. The identification of embedding and hidden-state norms as concrete, measurable levers for robustness gives practitioners a principled criterion for prompt selection (maximizing A⁻¹) that outperforms TextGrad, OPRO, and CFPO across all tested settings. Key limitations include the reliance on the LSA model as a proxy for full Transformers (softmax, LayerNorm, FFN non-linearities are only discussed qualitatively in Appendix D.1), the untested effects of the training-data covariance Γ and residual coefficient η, and the assumption that real LLMs satisfy the required Lipschitz condition with γ < 1. Open questions include whether the norm-based robustness levers transfer to multi-layer, multi-head architectures and whether training-time interventions on Γ and η can meaningfully widen the certified perturbation radius.
