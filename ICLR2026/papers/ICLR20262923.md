## Problem

Nonsmooth composite optimization over the Stiefel manifold (X^T X = I_r) is central to sparse PCA, nonnegative PCA, and dictionary learning, yet existing methods suffer from one or more critical limitations: they require full-gradient evaluations per iteration, cannot handle coordinate-wise nonsmooth regularizers, are infeasible (attaining orthogonality only in the limit), or provide only weak critical-point optimality. No prior algorithm simultaneously achieves partial-gradient efficiency, exact feasibility at every step, support for coordinate-wise nonsmooth objectives, and stronger-than-critical-point stationarity guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method takes a data matrix A ∈ R^{m×n} (from which the covariance C = A^T A is formed), a regularization parameter λ, a number of components r ≤ n, and a block size k ≥ 2. It produces an orthogonal matrix X ∈ St(n,r) minimizing F(X) = f(X) + h(X), where f is differentiable (e.g., −⟨X, CX⟩) and h is coordinate-wise separable and nonsmooth (e.g., ℓ₀, ℓ₁, capped-ℓ₁, or a non-negativity indicator). The orthogonality constraint X^T X = I_r is preserved exactly at every iteration by construction, and the per-iteration cost depends only on k rows of the gradient rather than the full n×r matrix.

## Method

OBCD is a row-wise block coordinate descent algorithm that updates k rows of X per iteration via the constraint-preserving map X⁺ = X + U_B(V − I_k) U_B^T X with V ∈ St(k,k), which guarantees X⁺ remains on the Stiefel manifold. Each iteration constructs a Majorization-Minimization surrogate K(V; X^t, B) that upper-bounds the objective, reducing the update to solving a small k×k nonsmooth optimization over St(k,k) that jointly handles both the nonsmooth regularizer h and the orthogonality constraint in a low-dimensional space. For k = 2, the subproblem reduces to a one-dimensional problem over Givens rotation and Jacobi reflection matrices, solvable exactly via a novel breakpoint search procedure. Theoretically, OBCD converges to block-k stationary points (BS_k-points) that are strictly stronger than standard Riemannian critical points, with O(1/ε) iteration complexity and KL-based last-iterate convergence rates (finite-step, linear, or sublinear depending on the KL exponent σ).

## Results

On L0-regularized SPCA (r=20, λ=10, 40s time limit), OBCD-R achieves the lowest objective value on all 10 datasets, e.g., 199.667 on w1a-2477-300 vs. 199.897 (LADMM) and 219.698 (RADMM), and 199.896 on MNIST-60000-784 vs. 199.985 (LADMM) and 379.893 (RADMM). Under the harder setting (λ=100), the gap widens substantially: OBCD-R obtains 1999.667 on w1a vs. 2499.912 (LADMM), 2799.713 (RADMM), and 2199.819 (SPM). OBCD-R is the only method that monotonically decreases the objective while maintaining feasibility at every iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OBCD-R (the proposed method) achieves the best (lowest) objective value on all 10 datasets under both λ=10 and λ=100 settings, outperforming all three baselines in every comparison. Against LADMM (Chen et al., 2016), the gap is small under λ=10 (e.g., 199.667 vs. 199.897 on w1a) but widens dramatically at λ=100 (1999.667 vs. 2499.912), indicating LADMM struggles with stronger sparsity. Against RADMM (Li et al., 2024a), OBCD-R is consistently superior by a large margin—RADMM frequently gets trapped in poor local minima (e.g., 379.893 vs. 199.896 on MNIST at λ=10; 1699.913 vs. 999.896 at λ=100). Against SPM (Yuan, 2024), OBCD-R again wins on every dataset, with the advantage growing at higher λ (e.g., 1999.667 vs. 2199.819 on w1a at λ=100). The paper also highlights a qualitative advantage: OBCD-R monotonically decreases the objective at every iteration while maintaining the orthogonality constraint exactly, whereas the other methods exhibit objective fluctuations and only attain feasibility in the limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the column-wise BCD framework of Shalit & Chechik (2014), which was limited to smooth objectives with k=2 and r=n using only Givens rotations, to a row-wise BCD that handles coordinate-wise nonsmooth composite objectives with k≥2 and r≤n. It departs from the broader manifold optimization landscape (geodesic, projection, multiplier-correction, and landing methods) by avoiding retractions entirely and instead using a constraint-preserving update scheme built from both Givens rotations and Jacobi reflections. The majorization-minimization surrogate construction draws on standard MM techniques but is tailored to jointly handle the nonsmooth term h(·) and the orthogonality constraint in a low-dimensional k×k subproblem, analogous to the prox-friendly condition in proximal gradient methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of block-k stationary points as a stronger optimality notion than standard Riemannian critical points provides a meaningful theoretical refinement: OBCD provably converges to points that no single k-row perturbation can improve, which explains its empirical ability to escape poor local minima that trap full-gradient methods. The O(1/ε) iteration complexity and KL-based last-iterate convergence rates are standard in form but are established for a feasible, partial-gradient method with a small per-iteration footprint, making it attractive for large-scale sparse PCA and related problems. Open questions include whether the exact subproblem solvability assumption (Asm-iii) can be relaxed, how the method scales when k grows beyond small values, and whether the random working-set strategy can be replaced by adaptive selection without sacrificing convergence guarantees.
