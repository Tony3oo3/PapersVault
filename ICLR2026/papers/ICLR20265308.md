## Problem

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA, while computationally efficient, can exacerbate 'Catastrophic Inheritance'—the unchecked propagation of biases, noise, and data imbalances from pre-training—because their constrained low-rank updates lack sufficient degrees of freedom to correct inherited artifacts and may instead amplify spurious correlations. The paper identifies three distinct failure modes through which this manifests during fine-tuning: Knowledge Drift (forgetting pre-trained knowledge), Representation Collapse (loss of output diversity under imbalanced data), and Overfitting to Noise (learning spurious high-frequency correlations), and notes that no existing PEFT method explicitly addresses all three simultaneously.

## Input / Output

For NLG tasks, the method consumes text prompts (math word problems, code tasks, conversational instructions) and produces generated text sequences, with regularization computed over token-level logit distributions of shape M×|V| where M is the number of valid tokens and |V| is the vocabulary size. For NLU tasks, it takes text pairs or single sentences and produces class labels or similarity scores, with regularization operating on batch-wise logit matrices of shape N×D. A key operational requirement is simultaneous access to both the frozen pre-trained (teacher) and the fine-tuned (student) model outputs on the same inputs, enabling the consistency regularizer to compute KL divergence between their softened distributions.

## Method

BA-LoRA builds on PiSSA's SVD-based adapter initialization (decomposing W into principal and residual components) and augments the standard task loss with three output-space regularizers, each targeting one failure mode of Catastrophic Inheritance. The consistency regularizer applies temperature-scaled KL divergence between teacher and student logits to anchor the fine-tuned model to pre-trained knowledge; the diversity regularizer penalizes off-diagonal elements of the batch-wise logit covariance matrix (NLU) or minimizes negative entropy within a Top-K candidate token set (NLG) to prevent representational collapse; and the SVD-based regularizer maximizes the ratio of spectral energy in the top-k singular values of the logit matrix (using exact SVD for NLU's moderate class count and randomized SVD with Frobenius normalization for NLG's large vocabulary) to suppress noisy high-frequency components. The key design choice is regularizing in the output/logit space rather than the adapter parameter space, since the non-linear mapping from low-rank parameters to model outputs makes parameter-space constraints difficult to interpret and control. The three regularizers are weighted by λ₁, λ₂, λ₃ and added to the standard cross-entropy (NLU) or causal language modeling (NLG) loss, with task-specific hyperparameter configurations selected via coarse grid search.

## Results

On NLG tasks with LLaMA-2-7B, BA-LoRA achieves the best average score of 26.18 across GSM8K (55.86), MATH (9.47), HumanEval (23.58), MBPP (36.86), and MT-Bench (5.11), outperforming the strongest baseline CorDA++ (23.22) by 2.96 points. On the GLUE NLU benchmark with DeBERTa-v3-base, BA-LoRA achieves the highest average of 90.67, surpassing PiSSA (89.47) and LoRA (88.56) by 1.20 and 2.11 points respectively, with the largest gain on CoLA (75.46 vs. 72.27 for PiSSA). The method also shows its strongest advantage on noisier pre-training: on T5-base (C4 web corpus), BA-LoRA improves over PiSSA by 3.26 points on average GLUE, versus only 1.11 points on RoBERTa-base (curated corpus).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BA-LoRA outperforms all 13 baselines on the NLG average, with the largest margin over standard LoRA (+7.88 avg) and LoRA-FA (+8.88 avg). Against the strongest NLG competitor CorDA++ (23.22 avg), BA-LoRA gains +0.83 on GSM8K, +1.82 on HumanEval, and a striking +12.12 on MBPP, though it trails CorDA++ on MT-Bench (5.11 vs. 5.64). On NLU (GLUE, DeBERTa-v3-base), BA-LoRA (90.67 avg) beats PiSSA (89.47), AdaLoRA (89.54), DoRA (89.13), LoRA (88.56), PAdapter (88.55), HAdapter (88.37), and BitFit (86.43), with the most dramatic improvement on CoLA (75.46 vs. 70.05 for LoRA, +5.41). The method also surpasses Full Fine-tuning on NLG average (26.18 vs. 21.08) while using far fewer trainable parameters (1.33M vs. 184M on NLU). On the noisy-pretraining comparison (T5-base), BA-LoRA's advantage over PiSSA widens to +3.26 avg GLUE, versus +1.11 on RoBERTa-base, supporting the Catastrophic Inheritance mitigation hypothesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

BA-LoRA extends the PiSSA initialization (itself a LoRA variant that uses SVD-based adapter initialization) by adding three output-space regularizers—consistency (KL-divergence distillation from the pre-trained model), diversity (batch-wise logit covariance decorrelation inspired by VICReg), and SVD-based spectral concentration—each targeting a specific failure mode of 'Catastrophic Inheritance' as defined by Chen et al. (2024). Unlike prior PEFT work that constrains adapter parameters in weight space (e.g., C-LoRA, Bayesian PEFT), BA-LoRA regularizes the model's output logits directly, arguing that the non-linear mapping from low-rank parameter space to functional output makes parameter-space constraints difficult to control. The framework is designed to be composable: it can be layered on top of standard LoRA, DoRA, or PiSSA without modifying their parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, drop-in regularization recipe for PEFT practitioners concerned about inherited biases and noise, with the added benefit that the framework generalizes across multiple LoRA-style backbones (LoRA, DoRA, PiSSA) and model scales (7B to 70B, dense and MoE). A key open question is whether the RoBERTa-vs-T5 comparison truly isolates pre-training noise effects, since the two models differ in architecture and objective—a limitation the authors acknowledge. The unusually large MBPP gap (36.86 vs. ~24 for all baselines) warrants scrutiny, as it is an order of magnitude larger than gaps on other benchmarks and could reflect evaluation protocol differences rather than a genuine methodological advantage. Future work should test whether the three regularizers are all necessary or whether a subset suffices, and whether the approach transfers to multimodal or non-English settings.
