## Problem

Transferring aligned behavior (robustness, safety, fairness) from a well-aligned teacher model to a student model remains difficult when the two differ in architecture, task, or training data: fine-tuning erodes aligned behavior, and most distillation methods assume shared datasets, output spaces, or access to the teacher's training data. BIRD asks whether aligned behavior can be transferred even under these heterogeneous conditions, without any shared inputs, labels, or output domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265271/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In the vision setting, the student consumes 32×32 RGB images from its target dataset (CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, or ImageNet) and produces class labels; the teacher, trained on a different and typically simpler dataset with corruption augmentation, is frozen and only its intermediate-layer representations are queried. In the language setting, the student takes text prompts and generates responses, while the teacher is a small binary safety classifier whose hidden representations guide the student. Critically, the student never sees the teacher's training data or output logits—supervision comes solely from the geometric structure of the teacher's internal representations projected onto the student's own inputs.

## Method

BIRD fine-tunes a pretrained student on its own clean task data while adding a representation-structure loss that minimizes 1 − CKA_linear between the teacher's and student's intermediate-layer representations over each training batch. CKA (with a linear kernel) captures the pairwise similarity geometry of a batch of representations, so the student learns to organize its latent space with the same relational structure as the teacher without matching individual activation values or outputs. The total loss is a weighted sum of the original task loss (e.g., cross-entropy) and the CKA-based structure loss, with weights set automatically by normalizing the two loss magnitudes on a validation set. Distillation layers are selected via a heuristic that scores candidate layers by task relevance (linear-probing accuracy) and behavioral relevance (γ-robust usefulness), and the teacher and student may differ freely in architecture, capacity, dataset, and output space. This design makes BIRD a drop-in module that can be combined with existing alignment objectives such as DPO or soft-label distillation.

## Results

Across 20 teacher-student configurations (4 architectures × 5 dataset pairs), BIRD achieves the highest out-of-distribution robustness in nearly every setting, improving robust accuracy by up to 18% over the next strongest baseline (LwF). On CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100, BIRD recovers 31.8% of the performance gap to the robustness ceiling (PGR), versus 13.5% for LwF; on CIFAR-100→TinyImageNet, 25.2% vs. 13.8%. BIRD also enables weak-to-strong transfer from a CIFAR-10-trained MobileNetV2 teacher to a ResNet152 student (25× more parameters), achieving 22.4% PGR. In language models, DPO+BIRD improves safety alignment to 71.28% safe responses (SmolLM2-135M) and 88.37% (SmolLM2-360M) on PKU-SafeRLHF, outperforming DPO alone (65.48% and 86.57%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIRD outperforms all five vision baselines (LP, FT, LP-FT, Hints, LwF) in nearly every configuration. Against Hints (Romero et al., 2014), which matches activation values via linear mapping and L2 loss at the same layers, BIRD achieves higher robustness in every setting and corruption category, demonstrating that representation structure (CKA) encodes more generalizable behavioral information than raw activations. Against LwF (Li & Hoiem, 2017; Shafahi et al., 2019), the strongest baseline, BIRD recovers roughly 2× the performance gap (e.g., 31.8% vs. 13.5% PGR on CIFAR-10→CIFAR-100; 25.2% vs. 13.8% on CIFAR-100→TinyImageNet). LP and FT alone fail to consistently improve robustness over the untrained baseline, and LP-FT (Kumar et al., 2022; Nern et al., 2023) provides only modest gains in a few architecture-dataset pairs. In language models, DPO+BIRD improves safety alignment over DPO alone (Rafailov et al., 2023) by +5.8 pp (135M) and +1.8 pp (360M) on PKU-SafeRLHF. For weak-to-strong generalization, BIRD complements soft-label distillation (Burns et al., 2023) with notable PGR gains on SciQ (7.79→16.14 for GPT2-Medium; 17.70→24.19 for GPT2-Large) but shows no improvement on BoolQ (0% PGR for both) and mixed results on Cosmos QA.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIRD extends the weak-to-strong generalization paradigm (Burns et al., 2023) and robustness transfer literature (Shafahi et al., 2019; Nern et al., 2023) by removing the requirement that teacher and student share training data, output spaces, or tasks. Methodologically, it departs from standard knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015; Romero et al., 2014) by replacing instance-level activation matching (L2, KL) with a CKA-based loss on Gram matrices, capturing pairwise representational geometry rather than per-sample values. The approach is conceptually inspired by NeuroAI work showing that behavioral properties are encoded in the structure of neural representations (Dapello et al., 2023; Zador et al., 2023), but operationalizes this without requiring biological recordings or shared stimulus domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BIRD offers a practical, drop-in mechanism for scaling aligned behavior to new models without retraining from scratch or accessing private teacher data, addressing a key bottleneck in deploying safe AI systems. The finding that three interpretable, computable properties of a teacher's representation space explain up to 85% of transfer variance provides actionable guidance for teacher selection that is rare in the distillation literature. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to additional behaviors (honesty, fairness), cross-modality settings, and whether multi-layer alignment can close the remaining gap; the language-model experiments, while promising, are limited to very small models (135M–360M parameters) and a single safety dataset.
