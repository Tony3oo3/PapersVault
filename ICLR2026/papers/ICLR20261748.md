## Problem

Diffusion-based object-centric learning methods such as Stable-LSD and SlotAdapt suffer from slot entanglement (a single slot encodes fragments of multiple objects) and weak slot-image alignment, preventing faithful generation from individual slots and limiting compositional generalization to novel scene configurations. These issues stem from the softmax normalization in cross-attention forcing residual attention mass onto semantic slots and from the text-conditioning bias inherited by pretrained diffusion backbones.

## Input / Output

CODA takes 512×512 RGB images, encodes them with a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B (patch size 14) to produce 32×32 feature maps, and extracts a fixed number of 768-dimensional slot vectors via Slot Attention (11 slots for MOVi-C, 24 for MOVi-E, 6 for VOC, 7 for COCO). Outputs include per-slot attention-based segmentation masks, single-slot and compositional image generations decoded by a pretrained Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net, and property predictions (position, 3D bounding box, category) from frozen slot representations. The diffusion model operates in the 64×64 latent space of the SD VAE, and all generated images are evaluated at 256×256 for fair comparison with prior work.

## Method

CODA introduces three complementary modifications to diffusion-based slot attention. First, input-independent register slots—obtained by encoding a sequence of CLIP padding tokens through the frozen text encoder—are appended to the semantic slot sequence and act as attention sinks, absorbing residual attention mass so that semantic slots remain focused on distinct object concepts. Second, only the key, value, and output projections in the cross-attention layers of the pretrained SD U-Net are finetuned (all other diffusion parameters stay frozen), mitigating the text-conditioning bias without adding adapter layers. Third, a contrastive alignment loss is added: the negative of the denoising MSE evaluated with hard-negative slots (constructed by replacing 50% of slots from one image with slots from another under shared initialization), where the diffusion decoder is stop-gradiented to prevent shortcut solutions. The joint objective L_dm + λ_cl·L_cl is shown in Theorem 1 to be a tractable surrogate for maximizing mutual information between the image latent and the slot sequence, with the contrastive term corresponding to the denoising gap between aligned and mismatched slots.

## Results

On COCO, CODA achieves FG-ARI of 47.54, outperforming the strongest baseline SlotAdapt (41.4) by +6.14%, and improves mBO^i, mBO^c, mIoU^i, and mIoU^c by +1.51%, +2.23%, +0.31%, and +1.20% respectively. On VOC, CODA reaches FG-ARI 32.23 (+2.63% over SlotAdapt), mBO^i 55.38 (+3.88%), mBO^c 61.32 (+9.42%), and mIoU^i 50.77 (+1.47%). On synthetic MOVi-C, CODA improves FG-ARI by +7.15% and mIoU by +7.75% over LSD; on the more complex MOVi-E, it gains +2.59% FG-ARI and +3.36% mIoU over SlotAdapt. For compositional generation on COCO, CODA achieves the best FID (31.03) and KID (30.44×10⁻³), surpassing SlotAdapt (40.57 FID, 34.38×10⁻³ KID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CODA outperforms all eight baselines across every reported metric on both VOC and COCO. Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline SlotAdapt (ICLR 2025), CODA gains +6.14% FG-ARI on COCO and +2.63% on VOC, with particularly large improvements in mBOᶜ (+9.42% on VOC), indicating better semantic-level grouping. Compared to Stable-LSD (NeurIPS 2023) and SlotDiffusion (NeurIPS 2023), CODA improves FG-ARI by +12.54% and +10.34% on COCO respectively, demonstrating that register slots and contrastive alignment substantially reduce slot entanglement. Against autoregressive baselines SPOT (CVPR 2024) and SLATE (ICLR 2022), CODA leads by +9.74% and +15.04% FG-ARI on COCO, confirming the advantage of diffusion decoders over autoregressive ones when combined with proper slot conditioning. On synthetic MOVi-E, CODA surpasses SlotAdapt by +2.59% FG-ARI and +3.36% mIoU, while on MOVi-C it beats LSD by +7.15% FG-ARI. In compositional generation, CODA achieves the best FID (31.03) and KID (30.44×10⁻³) on COCO, outperforming SlotAdapt (40.57 FID) by a wide margin, validating that disentangled slots enable more faithful recombination into novel scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

CODA extends the line of diffusion-based object-centric learning initiated by Stable-LSD and SlotDiffusion, which use pretrained or from-scratch diffusion models as slot decoders. It departs from SlotAdapt's adapter-layer approach by finetuning only the key, value, and output projections in cross-attention, and from GLASS's reliance on generated captions by introducing an unsupervised contrastive alignment loss. The register slot mechanism draws on the attention-sink phenomenon observed in language models (Xiao et al., 2024) and register tokens in ViT (Darcet et al., 2024), repurposing frozen CLIP padding embeddings as input-independent attention sinks. The theoretical contribution connects the combined denoising-plus-contrastive objective to mutual information maximization via a novel decomposition (Theorem 1), building on the information-theoretic diffusion framework of Kong et al. (2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CODA addresses two persistent failure modes in diffusion-based OCL—slot entanglement and weak slot-image alignment—with minimal architectural overhead, making it a practical drop-in improvement for existing slot diffusion pipelines. The MI-based theoretical grounding provides a principled justification for the contrastive loss that prior heuristic approaches lacked. Key limitations remain: the fixed slot count inherited from SA restricts scalability to variable-object scenes, fine-grained pixel-level reconstruction is still degraded by the low-dimensional slot bottleneck, and the method is validated only on SD v1.5, leaving open whether the approach transfers to larger backbones (SDXL, FLUX) or Diffusion Transformers. The finding that frozen registers outperform learnable ones and that stop-gradient is essential for stable contrastive training offers practical guidance for future OCL systems.
