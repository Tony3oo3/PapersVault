## Problem

Pre-trained flow and diffusion models concentrate their density over a narrow region of the valid design space, making them unable to generate valid designs beyond areas of high data availability—a fundamental limitation for scientific discovery tasks like drug and material design. Existing exploration methods (e.g., S-MEME, FDC) can redistribute density to underexplored modes but lose validity outside high-data regions, since the validity signal learned by the pre-trained model diminishes there. The paper asks how to leverage an external verifier (e.g., an atomic-bond checker) to expand a pre-trained flow model's coverage while preserving sample validity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained flow model (a velocity field approximating the data distribution) and a binary verifier function v: X → {0,1}—either strong (exactly characterizing the valid set Ω) or weak (a filter that rejects some invalid designs but may miss others). The verifier need not be differentiable in principle, though the default Adjoint Matching oracle requires a smoothed surrogate. The output is a fine-tuned velocity field whose induced terminal density is more uniformly spread over the valid design space; in molecular experiments this yields diverse, valid 3D conformers (fixed atom count, explicit hydrogens) evaluated via RDKit sanitization plus a minimum inter-atomic distance check.

## Method

Flow Expander (FE) formulates both global (strong-verifier) and local (weak-verifier) expansion as verifier-constrained entropy maximization over the entire flow process noised state space, then solves it via a mirror descent scheme with closed-form score-based gradients. At each iteration, FE alternates an expansion step—fine-tuning the velocity field to maximize (KL-regularized) entropy using the gradient −s_t^π (global) or −s_t^π − α_t(s_t^π − s_t^pre) (local)—with a projection step that enforces the verifier constraint via reward-guided fine-tuning on log v(x). The fine-tuning oracle is Adjoint Matching, which operates over full trajectories with memoryless noise, enabling use of scores at all time steps (downweighted near t=1 via λ_t) rather than only the divergent terminal score. The paper provides O(1/K) finite-time convergence in an idealized exact-update setting and almost-sure convergence under Robbins-Monro step sizes in the general approximate-update setting, via mirror-flow theory. A byproduct, Noised Space Exploration (NSE), removes the projection step and serves as a stronger unconstrained exploration baseline.

## Results

On GEOM-Drugs, L-FE achieves a Conformer Vendi score of 529 with 82% validity, outperforming the pre-trained model (476 Vendi, 72% validity) and FDC (508 Vendi, 66% validity). On QM9, L-FE reaches 100 Conformer Vendi with 81% validity versus FDC's 103 Vendi but only 69% validity. In the 2D global expansion setting, G-FE achieves near-optimal entropy (1.97) while retaining 99% validity, compared to S-MEME's higher entropy (2.17) but only 73% validity, and CONSTR's high validity but low entropy (0.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FDC (De Santi et al., 2025b), the primary unconstrained exploration baseline, L-FE consistently wins on validity (82% vs 66% on GEOM-Drugs; 81% vs 69% on QM9) while matching or slightly trailing on diversity (529 vs 508 Vendi on GEOM; 100 vs 103 on QM9), demonstrating that verifier-constrained expansion preserves validity without sacrificing diversity. Against S-MEME (De Santi et al., 2025a) in the global expansion setting, G-FE achieves substantially higher validity (0.99 vs 0.73) at a small entropy cost (1.97 vs 2.17), showing that the strong verifier constraint prevents exploration into invalid regions. Against CONSTR (Uehara et al., 2024a), G-FE matches validity (~0.99) while achieving dramatically higher entropy (1.97 vs 0.72), confirming that FE expands coverage rather than merely restricting it. The unconstrained NSE variant also uniformly outperforms FDC (519 vs 508 Vendi, 74% vs 66% validity on GEOM-Drugs), validating the noised-state-space exploration mechanism independently of the verifier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of flow/diffusion-based design space exploration (S-MEME, FDC) by introducing verifier-constrained entropy maximization and lifting exploration from the terminal time-step to the full noised state space. The fine-tuning oracle is Adjoint Matching (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024), and convergence guarantees are derived via the mirror-flow framework of Karimi et al. (2024). The key conceptual departure from prior exploration methods is the formal distinction between strong and weak verifiers, which enables a principled decomposition into global expansion (strong verifier → uniform over valid space) and local expansion (weak verifier → KL-regularized, conservative mode discovery), neither of which existed in prior flow-based exploration formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The verifier-constrained framework addresses a fundamental gap in scientific discovery: pre-trained generative models concentrate density in narrow data-supported regions, creating an irreducible sub-optimality for downstream optimization. The practical relevance is high because weak verifiers (bond checkers, folding predictors, physics simulators) are ubiquitous in chemistry, biology, and materials science, yet prior exploration methods could not safely leverage them. Open questions include: (i) how to handle the rare case where no sufficiently tight weak verifier exists, (ii) whether the differentiability requirement of the Adjoint Matching oracle limits applicability to non-smooth verifiers in practice, and (iii) whether the O(1/K) convergence rate is tight or can be improved with adaptive step-sizes. The NSE byproduct, which outperforms FDC in unconstrained settings, suggests the noised-state-space formulation may be broadly useful beyond verifier-constrained settings.
