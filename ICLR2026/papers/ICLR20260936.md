## Problem

Machine unlearning must remove the influence of specific training samples from a pre-trained model, but quantifying a sample's contribution to the learning process is highly challenging, causing most existing methods to rely on heuristic strategies (random relabeling, knowledge distillation) that degrade model utility on remaining data. This degradation necessitates costly maintenance with remaining data, which is often inaccessible in practice. No remaining-data-free method has previously achieved performance on par with state-of-the-art remaining-data-dependent approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the forgetting data (images with their class labels) and the pre-trained model weights—no remaining data, no auxiliary OOD data, and no historical training checkpoints are required. During optimization, batch normalization layers are frozen to prevent the forgetting data from corrupting statistics learned from the remaining data, and an irrelevant class c′ is randomly sampled per loss evaluation. The output is a set of updated model weights that approximate the retrained model, with a built-in stopping rule that halts optimization once the irrelevant-class logit sensitivity recovers to a threshold ratio δ of its initial pre-unlearning value.

## Method

MU-Mis is grounded in a theoretical derivation showing that a training sample's contribution to the learned function (∂A/∂x_i) is approximately reflected in the pre-trained model's input sensitivity to that sample (∂f(x)/∂x), with the contribution manifesting specifically as a disproportionately larger sensitivity of the target-class logit relative to irrelevant-class logits. The unlearning loss minimizes the squared Frobenius-norm gap ‖∇_x f_c‖²_F − ‖∇_x f_{c′}‖²_F over the forgetting data, thereby rolling back the target-logit sensitivity and restoring the irrelevant-logit sensitivity simultaneously. A reweighted variant dynamically amplifies the target-class term when it dominates and prioritizes the irrelevant-class term once the gap narrows, facilitating both forgetting and utility recovery. The stopping criterion monitors the irrelevant-class sensitivity and terminates optimization when it exceeds a fraction δ of its initial value, ensuring the model has fully withdrawn the sample's contribution without over-forgetting.

## Results

On full-class CIFAR-100 unlearning (ResNet-18), MU-Mis achieves an Avg. Gap of 0.07 to the retrained model (RA=76.42%, FA=0.00%, TA=75.64%), the smallest among all 12 compared methods, with MIA-Entropy of 0.00% and a runtime of only 30 seconds. On Tiny ImageNet, MU-Mis reaches Avg. Gap=0.15 (RA=64.95%, FA=0.00%, TA=64.85%), competitive with the best remaining-data-dependent method SalUn (0.06) while being 30× faster (83 s vs. 2630 s). Across all remaining-data-free baselines (NG, RL, SCAR, JiT), MU-Mis outperforms by 3–8 points in Avg. Gap, e.g., 0.07 vs. 4.43 (NG) and 8.66 (RL) on CIFAR-100. Under ViT on Tiny ImageNet, MU-Mis completes unlearning in 3 minutes versus 81 minutes for SalUn (27× speedup) with a comparable Avg. Gap (2.69 vs. 1.88).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MU-Mis achieves the lowest Avg. Gap (0.07) on CIFAR-100 full-class unlearning, beating the best remaining-data-dependent baselines BT (0.14), SSD (0.17), and SCRUB (0.18), as well as SalUn (0.47) and MUNBa (1.60). It outperforms all four remaining-data-free methods by a wide margin: NG (4.43), SCAR (5.29), JiT (8.32), and RL (8.66) on CIFAR-100. On Tiny ImageNet, SalUn (0.06) slightly edges out MU-Mis (0.15) in Avg. Gap, but MU-Mis is 30× faster (83 s vs. 2630 s) and requires no remaining data. In sub-class CIFAR-20 unlearning, MU-Mis (0.49 Rocket, 0.20 Sea) is competitive with SalUn (0.13 Rocket, 1.11 Sea) and SSD (0.48 Rocket, 1.24 Sea), while dramatically outperforming JiT (17.49, 46.81) and RL (17.11, 4.11). In the most challenging random-subset setting, RUM outperforms MU-Mis, but MU-Mis still beats all other remaining-data-free baselines (e.g., 2.80 vs. 3.13 for NG on CIFAR-10). Under ViT on Tiny ImageNet, MU-Mis (Avg. Gap 2.69, 3 min) is 27× faster than SalUn (1.88, 81 min) while remaining far ahead of NG (15.28) and RL (12.38).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in machine unlearning, which relies on heuristic confusion strategies (random relabeling, knowledge distillation from useless teachers) or parameter-space suppression (saliency-based weight dampening, Fisher information) to remove sample influence. Rather than these indirect approaches, the paper shifts the problem from parameter space to function space, showing that a training sample's contribution is reflected in the pre-trained model's input sensitivity (Jacobian norm) to that sample, specifically as a disproportionate gap between target-class and irrelevant-class logit sensitivities. This connects to the influence function literature (Guo et al., 2020; Peste et al., 2021) but avoids its reliance on convexity/optimality assumptions and Hessian inversion, and to the memorization/self-influence literature (Feldman, 2020) by providing an optimization-friendly surrogate that requires only the pre-trained model and forgetting data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: MU-Mis is the first remaining-data-free method to match top remaining-data-dependent methods in unlearning utility, eliminating a major deployment barrier (access to remaining data) while achieving up to 30× speedup over SalUn. The sequential unlearning experiments reveal that existing SoTA methods suffer from performance recovery, knowledge residue, and utility breakdown under repeated requests, whereas MU-Mis remains stable—suggesting that principled contribution-based forgetting is more robust than heuristic confusion. The main open limitation is the random-subset setting, where MU-Mis still trails RUM, indicating that entangled forgetting/remaining data remains the hardest case for remaining-data-free approaches. The theoretical link between input sensitivity and sample contribution also opens a broader question about whether sensitivity-based measures can replace influence functions more generally in DNN interpretability and privacy.
