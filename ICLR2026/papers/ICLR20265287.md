## Problem

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are widely used to recover interpretable monosemantic features from polysemantic activations in LLMs and vision models, yet no theoretical analysis existed for when SAEs can actually recover the ground truth features. This paper shows that standard SAEs generally fail to fully recover ground truth monosemantic features due to feature shrinking and vanishing, with full recovery guaranteed only under extreme sparsity of the true features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265287/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes polysemantic features x_p ∈ R^{n_p} (frozen activations from LLMs such as Pythia-160M or Llama-3-8B, or representations from a ResNet-18 pretrained with non-negative contrastive learning on ImageNet-100) and produces monosemantic SAE latents x_m ∈ R^{n_m} with n_m > n_p (e.g., 32× or 10× the input dimension). The SAE uses a single hidden layer with ReLU or Top-k activation; the WSAE variant additionally applies a diagonal weight matrix Γ to the reconstruction loss, with weights set as γ_i = s_i^α where s_i is the per-dimensional variance of x_p serving as a monosemanticity proxy. Evaluation uses auto-interpretability scores (LLM-based semantic similarity of top-activated samples) for language models and semantic consistency (class purity of top-activated samples) for vision models.

## Method

The paper first derives a closed-form solution to SAEs (Theorem 1), showing W_p^T is optimal but the recovered features x_m = σ(W_p^T W_p x) suffer from feature shrinking (polysemantic dimensions are attenuated) and feature vanishing (features drop to zero). It then proves that full recovery is guaranteed only when ground truth features are extremely sparse (S→1), where the solution is unique (Theorems 2–3). To address the general low-sparsity case, the paper proposes Weighted SAEs (WSAEs) that apply a diagonal weight matrix Γ to the reconstruction loss, making the gap between the SAE loss and the true ground-truth reconstruction loss adjustable (Theorem 5). The theoretical weight-selection principle assigns larger weights to monosemantic dimensions (reducing γ_i²−1 on the diagonal) and smaller weights to polysemantic dimensions (reducing off-diagonal negative interferences in W_p^T Γ^T Γ W_p − I). In practice, weights are set as γ_i = s_i^α using per-dimensional variance as a monosemanticity proxy, yielding consistent 3–7% gains in auto-interpretability across LLM layers and improved semantic consistency on vision models.

## Results

On Pythia-160M (12 layers), WSAE with α=1 achieves an average 3.8% gain in auto-interpretability score over the standard Top-k SAE, with per-layer gains ranging from +0.8% to +6.9% (e.g., 84.6% vs. 77.8% at layer 3, 81.5% vs. 74.6% at layer 8). On Llama-3-8B, WSAE (α=1) yields consistent gains of +2.2% to +4.5% across layers 8–32. On synthetic data, WSAE reduces the ground-truth reconstruction error (L_GT) substantially at low sparsity while maintaining comparable polysemantic reconstruction error (L_SAE), and on vision models (ResNet-18 + NCL on ImageNet-100), WSAE improves semantic consistency over the uniform-weight SAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares WSAE against two baselines: (1) the standard Top-k SAE with uniform reconstruction weights (Gao et al., ICLR 2025), and (2) the standard ReLU SAE with uniform weights (Cunningham et al., 2023). WSAE consistently outperforms both baselines on the auto-interpretability metric across all 12 layers of Pythia-160M, with the largest gains (+6.8% at layer 3, +6.9% at layer 8) occurring where the baseline SAE already exhibits relatively strong monosemanticity. On Llama-3-8B, WSAE (α=1) beats the standard SAE by +4.5% at layer 8 and +3.8% at layer 24. On synthetic data, WSAE achieves lower ground-truth reconstruction error (L_GT) than the standard SAE at low sparsity levels while keeping polysemantic reconstruction error (L_SAE) comparable, confirming that the reweighting narrows the theoretical gap without sacrificing reconstruction fidelity. On vision models (ResNet-18 pretrained with NCL on ImageNet-100), WSAE improves semantic consistency over the uniform-weight SAE, validating the strategy beyond language models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper provides the first theoretical identifiability analysis for sparse autoencoders, building directly on the superposition hypothesis (Elhage et al., 2022) to derive closed-form optimal solutions for SAEs. Prior SAE work (Cunningham et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2025) focused on architecture design (Top-k, JumpReLU, gated variants) and evaluation metrics without establishing when or whether SAEs can provably recover ground-truth monosemantic features. The paper departs from this empirical tradition by showing that the standard SAE loss is fundamentally misaligned with ground-truth reconstruction, and introduces a reweighted loss (WSAE) that narrows this gap by down-weighting polysemantic dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central theoretical result—that SAEs provably fail to recover ground-truth features under general sparsity due to feature shrinking and vanishing, and only succeed under extreme sparsity—reframes SAE-based interpretability as an approximation tool rather than a faithful feature recovery mechanism. This has broad implications for how the interpretability community should interpret SAE latents and calibrate confidence in mechanistic explanations. The WSAE remedy is practical but yields modest gains (~3.8% average auto-interpretability improvement), and the theoretical framework relies on simplified linear superposition assumptions that may not fully capture real model geometry. Open questions include whether the reweighting principle extends to hierarchical or nonlinear feature combinations, and whether alternative loss designs (e.g., directly estimating W_p) could close the gap more effectively.
