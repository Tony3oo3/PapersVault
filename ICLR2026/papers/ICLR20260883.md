## Problem

Existing LLM×KG reasoning methods suffer from two fundamental limitations on multi-hop and logically complex queries: (1) Search Space Truncation Bias, where linear entity-relation paths with top-k pruning prematurely eliminate correct candidates, and (2) Error Amplification, where the retrieve-and-answer paradigm causes LLMs to over-rely on retrieved evidence, propagating errors from spurious or weakly related entities. These issues are especially severe for queries involving conjunctions, compositions, comparatives, and superlatives that cannot be captured by sequential graph walks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language question and a knowledge graph (Freebase or Wikidata) as input. A trained 8B planner (Llama-3.1-8B) first decomposes the question into a structured sequence of sub-objectives that mirror SPARQL compositional logic. The system then iteratively explores the KG via SPARQL template queries (e.g., (e,r,?) or (?,r,e)), producing reasoning paths as sequences of triples, and outputs a final entity answer after self-refinement. The planning data (74,802 examples) is auto-generated by GPT-4o from SPARQL decompositions of training questions, and the planner is trained with standard autoregressive language modeling loss.

## Method

PARoG combines two core mechanisms. First, SPARQL-Guided Structured Planning: SPARQL queries from KG data serve as structured supervision signals to train a small LLM (Llama-3.1-8B) to generate compositional reasoning plans that handle conjunctions, compositions, comparatives, and superlatives—going beyond the linear entity-relation paths of prior methods. Second, the Plan-Answer-Refine paradigm: for each sub-objective, the LLM first produces a tentative answer using its parametric knowledge, then iteratively explores the KG (with relation filtering and entity scoring guided by the global plan), and explicitly refines the tentative answer by integrating retrieved KG evidence. This refinement step overrides faulty retrieved entities and prevents error amplification, while a sufficiency check terminates exploration early to avoid over-searching. Notably, the 8B SPARQL-supervised planner outperforms much larger general LLMs (GPT-3.5 ~20B, DeepSeek-R1 671B) as a reasoning-path generator, and the self-refinement mechanism corrects approximately 70% of initially wrong answers.

## Results

On WebQSP and CWQ with GPT-4, PARoG achieves 91.2% and 79.3% Hits@1 respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline PoG (87.3% and 75.0%) by 3.9 and 4.3 points. Under the more challenging GPT-3.5 setting, the margins widen to 7.0 points on WebQSP (89.0 vs. 82.0) and 9.9 points on CWQ (73.1 vs. 63.2). On GrailQA, PARoG reaches 87.1% overall accuracy with GPT-4 (vs. PoG's 84.7%) and 82.7% with GPT-3.5 (vs. DoG's 77.8%), with particularly large gains on compositional (+3.5 pts) and zero-shot (+2.5 pts) query subsets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARoG outperforms all 19 baselines across every dataset and LLM setting. Against the strongest LLM⊗KG baseline PoG, PARoG gains 7.0/9.9 points (GPT-3.5) and 3.9/4.3 points (GPT-4) on WebQSP/CWQ, with the largest relative advantage on CWQ's more complex multi-hop queries. Among other LLM⊗KG methods, ToG (76.2/80.7), RoG (81.5), KG-Agent (79.2/81.2), StructGPT (75.2/79.5), and ReKnowS (81.1/83.8) all trail PARoG by 5–14 points depending on setting. On GrailQA, PARoG surpasses the end-to-end fine-tuned methods (GAIN 76.3, Pangu 75.4, FC-KBQA 73.2) and the multi-agent DoG (77.8/80.0) by 5–10 points. Notably, PARoG's 8B SPARQL-supervised planner outperforms GPT-3.5 (~20B) and DeepSeek-R1 (671B) as planners by up to 8.1 points on CWQ, and it also uses fewer LLM calls (8.3 vs. 9.0 for PoG on WebQSP) and fewer total tokens (5,254 vs. 5,518), demonstrating simultaneous accuracy and efficiency gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARoG extends the LLM⊗KG reasoning paradigm established by Think-on-Graph (iterative entity-relation walks), Plan-on-Graph (adaptive sub-goal planning), and StructGPT (schema-constrained structured generation). Its key departure is twofold: (1) it uses SPARQL queries as structured supervision signals to train a small planner (Llama-3.1-8B) to emit compositional reasoning paths supporting conjunctions, compositions, comparatives, and superlatives—going beyond the linear entity-relation expansions of prior methods; and (2) it replaces the one-shot retrieve-and-answer paradigm with a plan-answer-refine loop where the LLM first answers from parametric knowledge and then explicitly corrects using retrieved KG evidence, directly targeting the error-amplification problem identified in existing retrieve-then-answer pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a task-specific 8B planner trained with SPARQL supervision outperforms general-purpose LLMs up to 671B parameters (DeepSeek-R1) as reasoning-path generators offers a practical efficiency argument for structured symbolic guidance in LLM reasoning, potentially applicable beyond KGQA to any setting with external structured signals. However, the framework still depends on KG coverage and correctness, requires a strong teacher model (GPT-4o) for planning-data generation, and uses only static offline refinement without dynamic feedback loops—leaving open questions about online adaptation, multi-modal KGs, and bootstrapped self-improvement. The ~70% error-correction rate of the refinement step and the halving of truncation rates (38.8% → 19.5%) provide concrete evidence that the two components address distinct failure modes, but the gains, while consistent, are incremental relative to the broader trajectory of LLM⊗KG methods.
