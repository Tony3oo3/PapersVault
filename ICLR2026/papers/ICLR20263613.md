## Problem

Existing test-time domain generalization (TTDG) methods align target-domain features to source-domain distributions via coarse-grained style transfer (mean/variance matching), which fails for pixel-level prediction tasks like image super-resolution where fine-grained per-pixel adjustments are essential. Additionally, single-codebook or single-backbone representations compress diverse source-domain features into a constrained space, losing domain-specific information, and classification-based codebook selection degrades under domain shift.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image from an unseen target domain (e.g., a different camera branch in DRealSR) and produces a corresponding super-resolution image, operating on 48×48 patches. At test time, no target-domain ground-truth HR images or gradient updates are required—only the frozen pre-trained network weights and the learned codebooks are loaded, making the inference cost negligible (0.00135 min per patch on a V100). Training uses paired LR-HR images from multiple source domains (P, IMG, Canon branches of DRealSR), while evaluation targets unseen domains (Pan, Sony, DSC).

## Method

MC-TTDG introduces a multi-codebook representation learning strategy (RLMC) that decomposes shallow features into a domain-invariant component (shared codebook) and a domain-specific component (one dedicated codebook per source domain), implicitly disentangling the two via a residual formulation (f^Spe = f − f^Base). At test time, instead of global style transfer, each pixel feature is matched to its nearest codeword in the appropriate codebook and replaced, achieving pixel-level fine-grained distribution transfer. To select the correct domain-specific codebook for an unseen target, a voting-based strategy pre-transfers target features through all domain-specific codebooks, feeds each result into a classification network, and selects the codebook receiving the most votes (with the untransferred feature's prediction as tiebreaker). Only the codebooks and the Conv2 decomposition layer are trained; the pre-trained backbone and decoder remain frozen throughout.

## Results

On the DRealSR benchmark (trained on P, IMG, Canon branches; tested on Pan, Sony, DSC), MC-TTDG achieves PSNR of 31.15 / 31.29 / 31.21 dB and SSIM of 0.8594 / 0.8791 / 0.8836 on the three target domains, outperforming the strongest baseline TTMG by 0.89 / 0.73 / 0.51 dB in PSNR. It also attains the lowest LPIPS (0.3593 / 0.3157 / 0.3583) across all target branches. In terms of computational efficiency, MC-TTDG requires only 0.00135 min per inference on a single V100 GPU, compared to 9–19 min for test-time domain adaptation methods that require fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MC-TTDG outperforms all five TTDG baselines (TF-Cal, TSB, DG-PIC, TTDG, TTMG) on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all three target domains (Pan, Sony, DSC). The closest competitor, TTMG (Nam & Lee, 2025), which uses covariance-based style transfer, is surpassed by 0.89 dB PSNR on Pan and 0.73 dB on Sony, with a particularly large LPIPS gap (0.3157 vs. 0.4069 on Sony), indicating substantially better perceptual quality. TF-Cal (Zhao et al., 2022) and TSB (Park et al., 2023), both relying on coarse-grained Fourier or mean-variance style transfer, lag behind by 2.3–3.4 dB in PSNR, confirming that global style transfer is ineffective for pixel-level SR. DG-PIC (Jiang et al., 2024) and TTDG (Zhou et al., 2024) show nearly identical numbers, suggesting limited gains from their respective dual-scale and orthogonal-style strategies on this task. Beyond accuracy, MC-TTDG requires no fine-tuning at test time (0.00135 min inference), whereas test-time domain adaptation baselines (DASR, IODA, SRTTA) demand 9–19 min of per-domain training, making MC-TTDG far more practical for edge deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends test-time domain generalization (TTDG) from high-level vision tasks (classification, segmentation) to low-level pixel-level tasks (image super-resolution), where the standard style-transfer mechanism (mean/variance matching) used by prior TTDG methods proves ineffective. It builds on codebook-based representation learning (AdaCode, Liu et al. 2023) but departs from single-codebook designs by introducing a multi-codebook framework that explicitly disentangles domain-invariant and domain-specific features, and replaces classification-based codebook selection with a voting strategy to handle domain shift at test time. The paper positions itself as the first TTDG method for low-level vision and the first to incorporate vector-quantized codebooks into the TTDG pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard style transfer yields virtually zero improvement for SR (Table 2) is a useful negative result that clarifies why TTDG has not been applied to low-level tasks and motivates the codebook-based alternative. The method's inference-only design (0.00135 min vs. 9–19 min for test-time adaptation) makes it practical for edge deployment, which is a significant practical advantage. Open questions include whether the voting-based selection generalizes beyond 3–5 source domains, how the approach handles more complex or compound degradations, and whether the pixel-level codebook transfer strategy transfers to other low-level tasks (denoising, deblurring) beyond SR.
