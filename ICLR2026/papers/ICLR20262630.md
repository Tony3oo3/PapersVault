## Problem

Classical quantile regression does not extend naturally to multivariate responses because R^d lacks a total ordering, and existing multivariate conformal prediction methods (coordinate-wise intervals, scalarized scores, generative embeddings) do not explicitly exploit the geometry of the joint conditional distribution. This paper bridges neural optimal transport and conformal prediction to construct distribution-free predictive regions that adapt to the shape of the conditional law of Y given X.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262630/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a tabular covariate vector X and a held-out calibration set of (X, Y) pairs, along with a nominal miscoverage level α, and produces a multivariate prediction set in R^{d_y} (dimensions 2–16 in experiments) together with the learned vector quantile map Q_{Y|X}(u,x) and its inverse rank map Q_{Y|X}^{-1}(y,x). The prediction set is defined as the preimage of a centered Euclidean ball in the reference space U under the estimated rank map, so its shape in Y-space is fully determined by the learned transport geometry and adapts to local conditional structure. A re-ranking variant additionally applies an OT-based correction map R: U→U to the ranks before conformal calibration to handle anisotropy from model misspecification.

## Method

The core architecture parameterizes a conditional convex potential φ_θ(u,x) with a Partially Input Convex Neural Network (PICNN), ensuring convexity in u for each fixed x; the vector quantile is the gradient ∇_u φ_θ and the multivariate rank is the gradient of the Fenchel conjugate ∇_y φ*_θ. Three training strategies are developed: C-NQR solves the inner conjugate argmax exactly via L-BFGS per sample, AC-NQR amortizes this solve with a separate predictor network trained on a faster timescale, and EC-NQR replaces the hard conjugate with an entropic log-sum-exp that admits stochastic Monte Carlo gradients. For conformal prediction, the norm of the estimated rank ‖Q̂^{-1}(Y_i, X_i)‖ serves as the conformity score on the calibration set, and the calibrated quantile defines a radius ρ_{1−α} such that the pullback set {y : ‖Q̂^{-1}(y, x)‖ ≤ ρ_{1−α}} achieves finite-sample marginal coverage ≥ 1−α. A volume-optimality theorem (Theorem 3) shows that under a radiality condition on the Jacobian of the rank map, this pullback ball is the minimum-volume region with the required conditional coverage, i.e., the highest-probability-density region for Y|X=x.

## Results

On the generative VQR benchmarks (Table 1), the best proposed variant AC-NQR$_U$ achieves S-W2 of 0.182 on Star, 0.771 on Glasses, and 0.073 on Banana, consistently outperforming all baselines except CPF on the two Banana variants (where CPF achieves 0.069). For quantile recovery (Table 2), C-NQR$_U$ attains L2-UV of 0.211 on Convex Star and 0.332 on Convex Glasses, while AC-NQR$_U$ achieves 0.106 on Convex Banana—substantially better than CPF (0.447, 2.268, 9.479 respectively). In conformal prediction experiments, PB and PBS (using AC-NQR$_U$) deliver the smallest prediction-set volume on three of four real-world datasets while maintaining competitive worst-slab coverage at nominal α=0.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the discrete VQR baselines (VQR, FN-VQR, CVQR, CPQ), the proposed neural methods achieve substantially lower S-W2 across nearly all datasets: e.g., on Glasses, AC-NQR$_U$ (0.771) beats VQR (1.964), FN-VQR (2.017), and CVQR (1.170) by 2.5×–2.6×, and on Star it improves over CPQ (0.274) by 34%. The closest competitor is CPF (Huang et al., 2021), which edges out the proposed methods only on the two Banana variants (0.069 vs. 0.070–0.073) but is clearly outperformed on Star (0.247 vs. 0.182), Glasses (1.687 vs. 0.771), and Convex Glasses (0.760 vs. 0.657). In the conformal prediction experiments, PB/PBS (the proposed pullback conformal sets) achieve the smallest average prediction-set volume on three of four real-world datasets (scm20d, sgemm, blog) while matching or exceeding the worst-slab coverage of OT-CP, OT-CP+ (Thurin et al., 2025), and the local Ellipsoid method (Messoudi et al., 2022). The re-ranked variant RPB/RPBS yields slightly sharper conditional coverage at the cost of larger set volume, which the authors note makes the trade-off questionable. AC-NQR$_U$ is also the fastest proposed variant (8.89 s/epoch training, 1.12 s inference), making it the practical choice for the conformal pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three research threads: the Carlier–Chernozhukov–Galichon framework for conditional vector quantile functions (CVQFs) via optimal transport, neural parameterization of convex OT potentials (PICNNs à la Makkuva et al., 2020; amortized conjugate prediction à la Amos, 2023), and split conformal prediction (Romano et al., 2019). Prior VQR methods (Rosenberg et al., 2023; Vedula et al., 2023) restricted to discrete quantile grids or affine-in-X embeddings and did not produce continuous rank functions; prior multivariate conformal methods (Feldman et al., 2023; Dheur et al., 2025) relied on generative embeddings or copula calibrations without exploiting OT-based multivariate geometry. Concurrent works (Thurin et al., 2025; Klein et al., 2025) also bridge OT ranks and conformal prediction but use discrete OT maps, whereas this paper learns continuous neural VQR models and proves a volume-optimality property for the resulting pullback prediction sets under a radiality condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The volume-optimality theorem (Theorem 3) provides the first theoretical justification for why Euclidean balls in rank space yield minimum-volume prediction regions, grounding the conformal construction in a principled geometric argument rather than a heuristic score choice. Practically, the amortized and entropic variants make continuous VQR scalable to higher-dimensional responses, and the finding that simple split conformal calibration (without re-ranking) already achieves competitive conditional coverage suggests the neural VQR model is sufficiently well-calibrated out of the box. Open questions include whether the radiality assumption of Theorem 3 holds for realistic conditional distributions, how the approach behaves in very high dimensions where the inner conjugate solve becomes expensive, and whether the conformal guarantee can be strengthened from marginal to conditional coverage without the volume penalty observed with re-ranking.
