## Problem

Prior debiasing methods for object detection rely on instance frequency as a proxy for data need, but the paper shows this is incomplete: data-rich groups (e.g., large objects) can be more 'data hungry' than rare ones. Additionally, existing layout-to-image (L2I) synthesis methods serialize 2D spatial arrangements into 1D text sequences, introducing ambiguity that limits generation fidelity, so even bias-targeted layouts cannot be rendered faithfully into complex scenes.

## Input / Output

The L2I generator consumes a geometric layout (N bounding boxes with class labels) rendered into a pixel-space visual blueprint (H×W×3 canvas of colored rectangles using HSV unit-circle hues for class discrimination, with value-decrement for same-class instances and alpha-blending for occlusion), plus a standard text prompt, and produces a synthesized RGB image at 256×256 or 512×512. The detector consumes RGB images and outputs bounding boxes with class labels; its predicted layout is fed back into the generator for the alignment loop. The visual blueprint replaces the conventional text-serialized layout token sequence used by prior L2I methods.

## Method

The framework has two coupled components. First, a Representation Score (RS) combines sample frequency, intra-group visual diversity (mean pairwise feature distance from the detector backbone), and context diversity (co-occurrence with other classes) to identify truly underrepresented data groups; an RS-driven recalibration module then resamples object sizes, positions, and classes inversely proportional to RS, with vertical positions only slightly jittered to preserve scene plausibility, and the RS is dynamically updated via exponential moving average of detection errors. Second, a visual blueprint replaces ambiguous text-serialized layouts with a pixel-space canvas of colored rectangles, injected into a frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net via a lightweight trainable encoder and zero-initialized adapter blocks (ControlNet-style). A duality-aware generative alignment loss enforces consistency in an Image→Layout→Image loop by penalizing the detector when its predicted layout, when fed back through the generator, produces a different noise prediction than the ground-truth layout, thereby coupling generator fidelity to detector quality.

## Results

On MS COCO (Faster R-CNN + ResNet-50), the proposed method achieves 40.3 mAP overall, improving +2.9 over the baseline and +1.8 over the strongest bias-agnostic baseline (GeoDiffusion, 38.4). The gains are most pronounced for underrepresented groups: +4.4 mAP for large objects, +3.6 for rare classes, +3.2 for outer-position instances, and +1.9 for small objects. On NuImages, the method reaches 40.0 mAP (+3.1 over baseline). In the fidelity evaluation at 512² resolution, the method surpasses prior SOTA GeoDiffusion by 15.9 mAP (46.5 vs. 30.6) and achieves a lower FID (15.24 vs. 18.89) on MS COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 13 baselines across both fidelity and debiasing evaluations. In fidelity (512², MS COCO), it achieves 46.5 mAP vs. GeoDiffusion's 30.6 (+15.9), GDCC's 32.6 (+13.9), and ControlNet's 25.2 (+21.3), while also attaining the lowest FID (15.24). In debiasing on MS COCO, the method gains +1.9 mAP over GeoDiffusion+Resampling (40.3 vs. 38.5) and +3.4 over Copy Paste (40.3 vs. 37.9), with the largest margins on underrepresented groups (e.g., +2.3 mAP for rare classes over GeoDiffusion+Resampling, +3.2 for outer-position over all baselines). On NuImages, the method leads GeoDiffusion+Resampling by +1.7 mAP (40.0 vs. 38.3) and achieves the best per-category scores for trailer (+4.0), construction vehicle (+5.7), and pedestrian (+0.8). Notably, frequency-aware resampling variants of ControlNet actually degrade performance (36.9 vs. 36.9 baseline), while the proposed RS-driven strategy consistently improves all sub-metrics, confirming that frequency alone is an incomplete proxy for data need.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the ControlNet adapter architecture (Zhang et al., 2023) and Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) as its generative backbone, but departs fundamentally from prior layout-to-image (L2I) synthesis methods (GeoDiffusion, DetDiffusion, GDCC) that serialize 2D bounding-box layouts into 1D text token sequences. Instead, it introduces pixel-space visual blueprints (colored rectangles with HSV-encoded class identity) as unambiguous geometric conditioning, and couples the generator with the detector through a duality-aware alignment loss that exploits the Image→Layout→Image loop. On the debiasing side, it extends frequency-based resampling strategies (Cui et al., 2019; Gupta et al., 2019) by proposing a representation score (RS) that integrates sample density, visual diversity, and context diversity, and wraps the entire pipeline in a dynamic engine that updates RS from detection errors during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's motivating study—that data-rich groups (e.g., large objects) can be more 'data-hungry' than data-poor groups—challenges the field's default assumption that frequency is a sufficient proxy for representation need, and the RS framework offers a principled diagnostic to replace it. The visual blueprint approach is a clean, low-cost architectural swap (replacing text serialization with a rendered canvas) that yields a large fidelity gain (15.9 mAP over GeoDiffusion), making it a practical technique for anyone building L2I pipelines. Open questions include whether the three-axis RS (class, size, position) generalizes to richer attribute spaces (e.g., orientation, occlusion depth), whether the dynamic debiasing loop remains stable at much larger dataset scales, and whether the framework extends naturally to video or 3D detection where layout semantics are more complex.
