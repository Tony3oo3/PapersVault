## Problem

Test-time scaling in LLMs, when combined with speculative decoding, faces two critical bottlenecks: (1) KV-cache memory explosion in the target model during parallel prefill of many draft candidates, and (2) synchronization overhead from global ranking or softmax normalization required for rejection sampling. The paper identifies synchronization as the dominant bottleneck via a new asynchronous arithmetic intensity metric and shows that existing conformal prediction methods, which require normalized scores, are fundamentally incompatible with asynchronous inference scheduling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text question, a lightweight draft model (e.g., 1.5B–8B parameters), and a heavier target model (e.g., 32B–70B parameters), operating in a multi-turn loop where each turn the draft model generates m candidate continuations of length K_d tokens. The output is a final answer with a reasoning chain, produced by iteratively accepting or rejecting draft candidates based on conformal p-values; the target model only intervenes on rejected candidates, continuing generation from the draft prefix for up to K_t tokens per turn. A user-specified miscoverage rate α (default 0.4) controls the rejection budget, and the method provides both marginal (dataset-level) and conditional (per-instance) coverage guarantees on the prediction set.

## Method

ATTS reformulates conformal prediction as an ordinal classification problem using unnormalized conformity scores (negative log-likelihood) and conformal p-values, eliminating the softmax normalization that forces synchronization across all candidates. An online calibration strategy computes p-values by comparing each test-time candidate's score against a running pool of previously sampled scores, requiring no held-out calibration set. The three-stage pipeline—draft sampling, p-value-based verification, and target-model continuation—operates asynchronously: each candidate is scored independently, and the prediction set C_α is constructed by thresholding p-values against α, which is equivalent to rejecting the top-B candidates without explicit global ranking. A budget prediction mechanism estimates the target model's intervention rate per batch, keeping GPU memory within safe limits. The paper proves marginal and conditional coverage guarantees for the p-value-based rejection rule under exchangeability, and introduces asynchronous arithmetic intensity r = T_c/(T_m + T_s) to formally show that synchronization time, not computation or memory bandwidth, is the binding constraint as sampling size grows.

## Results

ATTS achieves up to 56.7× speedup in test-time scaling and 4.14× throughput improvement over baselines under the 16-sample setting. On AIME24 with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B/32B, ATTS reaches 86.7% marginal accuracy (vs. 66.7% for both SpecThink and SpecReason) while consuming only 0.46× and 0.50× the tokens of the respective baselines. Across all four datasets in the same-family setting (Table 2), ATTS consistently matches or exceeds the target model's own accuracy (lossless acceleration) while reducing token consumption to 0.27×–0.75× of SpecThink and 0.22×–0.74× of SpecReason. The 1.5B/70B draft-target combination with 128 samples reaches o3-mini (high) performance on AIME25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATTS is compared against SpecReason (Pan et al., 2025b) and Speculative Thinking (Yang et al., 2025), both of which operate at step-level granularity for speculative decoding under sequential scaling. ATTS wins decisively on accuracy across nearly all settings: on AIME24 with DeepSeek-1.5B/32B, ATTS achieves 86.7% marginal accuracy versus 66.7% for both baselines (a 20-point gap), and on AIME25 with Skywork-OR1-7B/32B it reaches 60.0% versus 40.0% (SpecThink) and 53.3% (SpecReason). Simultaneously, ATTS consumes substantially fewer tokens—ranging from 0.27× to 0.75× of SpecThink and 0.22× to 0.74× of SpecReason—indicating that the conformal-prediction-based rejection sampling avoids the redundant target-model computation that plagues both baselines. The one exception is Qwen2.5-7B/32B on AMC23, where SpecThink (74.0%) slightly edges out ATTS marginal coverage (72.0%), though ATTS still wins on token efficiency (0.63×). Overall, ATTS dominates both baselines on the harder AIME benchmarks by 10–20 accuracy points while using roughly half the tokens, confirming that the asynchronous conformal prediction pipeline is both more accurate and more efficient than existing speculative reasoning approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATTS sits at the intersection of speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023; Pan et al., 2025b; Yang et al., 2025) and test-time scaling (Snell et al., 2024; Muennighoff et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025), but departs from both in a key way: prior speculative decoding methods accelerate a single sequence, while prior test-time scaling methods (SpecReason, Speculative Thinking) handle parallel/sequential scaling but suffer from synchronization overhead during rejection sampling. The paper's conceptual departure is reframing the conformal prediction problem (Romano et al., 2020) as ordinal classification with p-values, eliminating the softmax normalization that forces global ranking and synchronization. The introduction of 'asynchronous arithmetic intensity' as a performance metric is also novel, identifying synchronization rather than memory bandwidth as the dominant bottleneck in multi-sample test-time scaling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 56.7× speedup and 4.14× throughput gains are practically significant for deploying reasoning models at scale, and the training-free, lossless nature with distribution-free statistical guarantees makes the approach attractive for production settings. The result that a 1.5B/70B draft-target pair can match o3-mini (high) on AIME with 128 samples suggests that aggressive test-time scaling with small models is a viable path to competitive reasoning performance. Open questions include whether the method extends to non-mathematical reasoning tasks, how calibration quality degrades under distribution shift, and whether the approach scales beyond 128 samples or to even larger target models where the prefill bottleneck becomes more severe.
