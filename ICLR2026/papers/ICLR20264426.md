## Problem

Identifying effective collective variables (CVs) that capture the slow, macroscopic dynamics of molecular systems remains a major bottleneck for enhanced sampling techniques. Prior machine-learned CV methods (DeepTICA, TAE, VDE) have been validated only on small systems like alanine dipeptide and lack a standardized, side-by-side comparison on larger fast-folding proteins in explicit solvent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264426/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein configuration as Cα pairwise distances (rotation/translation invariant) plus the amino acid sequence, and produces a single 1D scalar CV normalized to [-1,1] with the folded state assigned positive. During training, the time-lagged conformation x_{t+τ} from the DESRES trajectory serves as the generation target. Downstream, the CV drives OPES simulations to estimate free energy differences (ΔF, PMF MAE) and steered MD to sample transition paths (RMSD, target hit percentage, transition state energy).

## Method

BIOEMU-CV re-purposes the frozen BioEmu protein conformation ensemble diffusion model by attaching a lightweight encoder (48K parameters) that maps the current conformation x_t to a 1D CV c_t, which is fused into BioEmu's single representation h via a small MLP adapter. The training objective is denoising score-matching where the conditioned BioEmu generates the time-lagged conformation x_{t+τ} from c_t, forcing the CV to encode only information shared between current and future states (slow dynamics) while discarding fast random fluctuations. Only the encoder and conditioning MLP are updated; all BioEmu backbone parameters remain frozen, keeping training lightweight. This design is inspired by adapter-based conditioning of frozen text-to-image diffusion models (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter), adapted here to the molecular dynamics setting where the 'condition' is a learned CV and the 'target' is a future conformation.

## Results

On 1 μs OPES simulations for free energy difference estimation, BIOEMU-CV achieves the lowest absolute error |ΔF_ref − ΔF| across all three fast-folding proteins: 0.52 kJ/mol on Chignolin, 1.82 kJ/mol on Trp-cage, and 7.22 kJ/mol on BBA, outperforming DeepTICA (1.71, 2.73, 11.19) and TAE (2.53, N/A, N/A). On steered MD transition path sampling, BIOEMU-CV achieves 100% target hit percentage (THP) on Chignolin and 93.8% on BBA with the lowest Cα-RMSD (1.20 Å and 2.05 Å respectively), while VDE and TAE fail entirely on Trp-cage and BBA due to inability to discriminate folded/unfolded states.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIOEMU-CV outperforms all three baselines on both downstream tasks across all three proteins where comparisons are valid. On OPES free energy estimation, BIOEMU-CV reduces the absolute ΔF error by 3.3× on Chignolin (0.52 vs. 1.71 for DeepTICA) and 1.5× on Trp-cage (1.82 vs. 2.73), while TAE and VDE fail entirely on Trp-cage and BBA due to inability to discriminate metastable states. On steered MD, BIOEMU-CV achieves 100% THP on Chignolin versus 37.5–43.8% for baselines, and 93.8% on BBA versus 0–18.8%, with the lowest Cα-RMSD (1.20 Å vs. 1.95–2.45 Å on Chignolin). VDE is the weakest baseline, failing state discrimination on both Trp-cage and BBA (showing nearly identical CV values for folded and unfolded states), while TAE converges to incorrect ΔF values on larger proteins by exclusively sampling one metastable basin. DeepTICA remains the strongest baseline but still exhibits 2–5× higher ΔF errors and substantially lower THP in SMD compared to BIOEMU-CV.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes BioEmu, a protein conformation ensemble generation foundation model, as a frozen backbone for learning collective variables, drawing an analogy to how lightweight condition adapters are trained on top of text-to-image diffusion models (Zhang et al., 2023; Mou et al., 2024). Conceptually, it extends the time-lagged CV learning paradigm of DeepTICA, TAE, and VDE—which train autoencoder or TICA-based encoders directly on trajectory data—by instead using a pre-trained generative model's score network as the reconstruction target, with only a small encoder and conditioning MLP trained via denoising score matching. The key departure is that the CV encoder is not trained to reconstruct or predict configurations in raw coordinate space, but to condition a powerful generative model on time-lagged states, implicitly learning slow dynamics through the foundation model's learned representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, side-by-side benchmark of MLCVs on fast-folding proteins larger than alanine dipeptide (Chignolin, Trp-cage, BBA) under identical training and evaluation protocols, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify in prior literature. The finding that VDE fails to discriminate metastable states on Trp-cage and BBA, and that TAE converges to incorrect free energies by only sampling one basin, highlights that prior MLCV methods do not reliably scale beyond toy systems. Open questions include whether the BioEmu-based approach extends to larger proteins, multi-dimensional CVs, or non-equilibrium processes, and whether the foundation model's pre-training on diverse sequences provides a genuine inductive bias advantage over trajectory-only training at scale.
