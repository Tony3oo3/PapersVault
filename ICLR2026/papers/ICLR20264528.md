## Problem

Machine-Learned Interatomic Potentials (MLIPs) remain data-limited, yet the O(N²) Hamiltonian matrix elements available at no extra cost from every DFT calculation have gone unused for training. Existing Hamiltonian prediction models are restricted to small molecules (<62 atoms), limited elemental composition, and small basis sets, preventing them from matching the chemical diversity and structural scale of modern MLIP training data. This paper addresses the gap by scaling Hamiltonian prediction to 100+ atom structures across 58 elements with large basis sets, and demonstrates that the resulting embeddings serve as a transferable representation for energy prediction in low-data regimes.

## Input / Output

The model takes a molecular graph defined by 3D displacement vectors r_ij and one-hot atomic number encodings, and produces two outputs: (1) the full Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian matrix decomposed into irreducible representations per node (intra-atomic) and directed edge (inter-atomic), where each target has 4,096 matrix elements for the def2-TZVPD basis (l_max=6), and (2) a scalar total energy per molecule computed as a weighted sum of l=0 node embedding components. The Hamiltonian targets are preprocessed with per-element l=0 mean/std normalization to homogenize variance across the 3+ orders of magnitude in core-state energies spanned by heavy elements.

## Method

HELM is an equivariant message-passing GNN with a shared backbone (3 layers of SO(2) convolutions with gating) and two output heads: a lightweight Hamiltonian head mapping node/edge embeddings to irrep-decomposed matrix blocks, and an energy head mapping node embeddings to a scalar. Two key architectural choices enable scaling: (1) gated nonlinearities on nonzero-degree irreps to distinguish multiple orbital shells within large basis sets, and (2) parity-constrained computation of diagonal blocks exploiting the Kronecker-delta selection rule for same-atom orbital couplings. The backbone enforces a one-way dependence where edge embeddings at layer k+1 depend on node embeddings at layer k (not vice versa), which implicitly encodes the 1/r Coulombic decay of interatomic interactions. Training uses a root-MSE+MSE loss over irrep components. The 'Hamiltonian pretraining' paradigm first trains the full model on H, then either freezes the backbone (pretrained-frozen) or finetunes it (finetuned) with an energy head, achieving up to 2× improvement in energy MAE over direct training in low-data regimes without additional force/energy calculations.

## Results

HELM achieves state-of-the-art Hamiltonian matrix prediction on both MD17 and ∇²DFT benchmarks, with element-wise MAE (H^err) of 3.61–9.33 ×10⁻⁶ E_h on MD17 molecules and ~57–60 ×10⁻⁶ E_h on ∇²DFT, representing a 3–5× improvement over the previous best model (PhiSNet). When the predicted Hamiltonian is used to recompute total energies, HELM reproduces reference energies to within ~23–29 meV on MD17 and ~400–440 meV on ∇²DFT. In low-data energy prediction experiments, Hamiltonian pretraining yields up to a 2× improvement in test energy MAE over direct training (e.g., 266 vs. 791 meV on ∇²DFT 2k test), equivalent to an order-of-magnitude increase in training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HELM outperforms all four baselines (SchNOrb, PhiSNet, QHNet, SPHNet) on every MD17 molecule and every ∇²DFT split. On MD17, HELM's H^err of 3.61–9.33 ×10⁻⁶ E_h beats the next-best QHNet (10.79–21.52) by roughly 2×, while SchNOrb and SPHNet are an order of magnitude worse. On ∇²DFT, HELM achieves ~57–60 ×10⁻⁶ E_h versus PhiSNet's 180–350 ×10⁻⁶ E_h (the previous best), a 3–5× improvement; QHNet (520–840) and SchNOrb (~20,700–21,500) are far behind. SPHNet is only reported on MD17 and does not extend to ∇²DFT. The energy errors computed from predicted H (23–29 meV on MD17, ~400 meV on ∇²DFT) further confirm that HELM's matrix predictions are physically meaningful, not merely element-wise accurate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of equivariant GNN-based Hamiltonian prediction (SchNOrb, PhiSNet, QHNet, SPHNet) by scaling to 100+ atom structures, 58 elements, and the def2-TZVPD basis set, using SO(2) convolutions to handle high angular momenta efficiently. It builds on the Fu et al. (2025) equivariant message-passing backbone and the OMol25 dataset, but introduces two key architectural departures: a one-way node-to-edge embedding dependence that encodes Coulombic 1/r decay, and a node-parity expansion exploiting symmetry constraints in diagonal Hamiltonian blocks. The most significant conceptual departure is 'Hamiltonian pretraining'—repurposing the O(N²) orbital-interaction labels learned in the backbone as a shared embedding space for downstream energy prediction, a use not explored in prior Hamiltonian prediction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that Hamiltonian matrices, already computed as a byproduct of DFT, provide a rich and transferable training signal that can improve energy prediction by ~2× in low-data regimes—equivalent to an order-of-magnitude increase in atomic force/energy data. The OMol_CSH_58k dataset fills a critical gap as the largest and most element-diverse Hamiltonian benchmark, enabling future work on universal electronic structure learning. However, the practical impact remains unproven: the pretraining gains are demonstrated only in controlled low-data experiments, finetuning on the more diverse OMol data actually degrades the fine-grained embedding structure, and scaling to the full OMol25 energy dataset (110M+ structures) is left as future work. Open questions include whether eigenvalue-based and symmetry-based losses can further improve accuracy, and whether the approach can match or exceed current MLIPs at full training scale.
