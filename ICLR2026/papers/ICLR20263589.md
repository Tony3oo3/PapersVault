## Problem

DiT-based video diffusion models suffer from high inference latency due to the sequential computation of many DiT blocks across dozens of denoising timesteps. Existing acceleration methods either require architectural modifications and retraining (e.g., Skip-DiT) or cache features at a granularity that is too coarse (timestep-level) or too fine (attention-level), failing to exploit the substantial computational redundancy that exists specifically at the DiT block level during intermediate timesteps.

## Input / Output

BWCache is a plug-and-play, training-free inference-time module that wraps around any pre-trained DiT-based video generation pipeline. It takes a text prompt and initial latent noise as input, and produces a generated video (e.g., 51 frames at 480P for Open-Sora, 81 frames at 480P for Wan 2.1, 129 frames at 544P for HunyuanVideo) after the standard iterative denoising and VAE decoding stages. The method introduces no architectural changes and requires only a lightweight similarity indicator computed from cached block features, with a default threshold δ=0.15 and reuse interval R=10% of total timesteps.

## Method

BWCache exploits the observation that DiT block features exhibit a U-shaped variation pattern across diffusion timesteps, with high similarity (and thus redundancy) during intermediate steps. At each timestep, it computes the relative L1 distance between each block's output and its counterpart from the previous timestep; if the mean across all N blocks falls below a threshold δ, subsequent timesteps reuse the cached block outputs instead of recomputing them. To prevent latent drift from prolonged reuse, a periodic recomputation strategy (borrowed from PAB) refreshes each cached block every R steps within the caching interval. Additionally, once caching is triggered at step k, the final k/2 steps are always fully recomputed to preserve high-frequency detail refinement. This block-wise granularity is more adaptive than timestep-level caching because individual blocks can be selectively reused or recomputed based on their own feature stability, which varies across depth and across different generation tasks.

## Results

BWCache achieves up to 2.6× speedup on HunyuanVideo (129 frames, 544P) while maintaining or exceeding the original model's VBench score (82.48% vs. 82.29%). Across all five evaluated models, BWCache consistently outperforms baselines in perceptual quality: e.g., on Open-Sora (51 frames, 480P) it reaches LPIPS 0.0879, SSIM 0.8854, and PSNR 27.05 at 1.61× speedup, compared to TeaCache's LPIPS 0.1496 / SSIM 0.8104 / PSNR 22.39 at 1.50×. On Wan 2.1 (81 frames, 480P), BWCache halves inference latency from 912 s to 457 s (2.00×) with LPIPS 0.0782, substantially better than PAB (0.1623) and TeaCache (0.2407).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BWCache outperforms all training-free caching baselines (PAB, TeaCache, FasterCache) in visual quality across every model tested, with the largest margins on Wan 2.1 (LPIPS 0.0782 vs. 0.1623 for PAB and 0.2407 for TeaCache) and HunyuanVideo (PSNR 29.91 vs. 26.69 for PAB). In terms of speedup, BWCache leads on Open-Sora (1.61× vs. 1.50× TeaCache), Latte (1.90× vs. 1.86× TeaCache), Wan 2.1 (2.00× vs. 1.41× TeaCache), and HunyuanVideo (2.60× vs. 2.27× TeaCache); the sole exception is Open-Sora-Plan, where TeaCache achieves 4.41× vs. BWCache's 2.24× due to pronounced early-timestep feature oscillations in that model's PNDM sampler. Against the architecture-modifying Skip-DiT on Latte, BWCache delivers both higher speedup (1.90× vs. 1.65×) and better VBench (78.28% vs. 75.76%) without requiring retraining. Compared to high-memory methods ProfilingDiT and TaylorSeer (evaluated on reduced video lengths due to OOM), BWCache achieves superior LPIPS and SSIM while matching or exceeding their speedups (e.g., 1.67× vs. 1.24× ProfilingDiT on Wan 2.1; 2.39× vs. 1.94× TaylorSeer on HunyuanVideo). Δ-DiT and T-GATE are substantially outperformed on all quality metrics, confirming that block-level caching is far more effective than timestep-level or attention-level strategies for DiT video models.

## Relation to Prior Work

BWCache sits at the intersection of two lines of work on diffusion model acceleration: training-free feature caching (DeepCache, TeaCache, PAB, FasterCache) and the emerging recognition that DiT blocks exhibit temporal redundancy. It borrows the relative L1 distance metric from TeaCache (Liu et al., 2024) as its similarity indicator and adopts PAB's (Zhao et al., 2024) progressive recomputation strategy to prevent latent drift, but departs from both by operating at the individual DiT block granularity rather than at the timestep or attention level. The key conceptual departure is the empirical discovery of a U-shaped block-feature variation pattern across timesteps, which motivates a dynamic, block-level caching policy that is neither as coarse as timestep-level caching (TeaCache) nor as fine-grained as attention-level caching (PAB, T-GATE).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BWCache provides a practical, plug-and-play acceleration technique for the rapidly growing ecosystem of DiT-based video generators, achieving up to 2.6× speedup without architectural changes or retraining—addressing a critical bottleneck for real-world deployment. The U-shaped feature variation analysis offers a useful diagnostic tool for understanding where in the denoising process computational redundancy concentrates. However, the similarity threshold δ remains a manually tuned hyperparameter, and the indicator computation itself consumes 10–22% of inference time, limiting the theoretical speedup ceiling. Open questions include whether the U-shaped pattern generalizes to future architectures with different noise schedules, and whether the threshold can be learned or adapted online per generation task without additional overhead.
