## Problem

Existing embodied AI simulators either support only small groups of agents, restrict interactions to indoor household scenes, or provide limited physics-based interactions, leaving a gap in studying large, heterogeneous communities of humans and robots coexisting in scalable open-world environments. No prior platform unifies physically realistic simulation of both human-like avatars and diverse robot types within diverse, populated outdoor-indoor worlds generated from real-world geospatial data.

## Input / Output

The scene generation pipeline takes only geospatial coordinates (latitude, longitude, range radius) as input and automatically produces 800 m × 800 m textured 3D meshes (terrain, buildings, roofs) plus interactive objects and indoor layouts, with 35 scenes spanning 17 countries. Agent observations during simulation are 256×256 RGB-D images with segmentation maps, a 6D pose vector in ENU coordinates, camera extrinsics, and text-based event messages from nearby agents. The agent generation component consumes scene descriptions and appearance attributes (name, age) to produce structured character profiles, social relationship networks, and daily schedules that explicitly account for commute times between locations in the 3D environment.

## Method

Virtual Community builds a unified simulation framework on the Genesis physics engine that simultaneously runs SMPL-X-parameterized human avatars (driven by 2,000+ Mixamo motion clips with kinematic attachment for objects and vehicles) and five robot types (drones, quadrupeds, humanoids, wheeled carriers, mobile manipulators) using a nested loop where 100 inner robot physics steps execute per 1 outer avatar step. The open-world generation pipeline performs four stages: (1) geometry reconstruction via OSM-based building mesh derivation and bilinear terrain interpolation to replace noisy 3D Tiles topology, (2) texture enhancement using Stable Diffusion inpainting followed by street-view reprojection with LoFTR keypoint matching and 5-point pose correction, (3) interactive object placement combining generative (One-2-3-45) and retrieval pipelines guided by OSM annotations, and (4) indoor scene creation via GRUTopia retrieval and Architect-based diffusion inpainting. Agent communities are generated by prompting GPT-4o with scene metadata and appearance attributes to produce grounded character profiles and social networks, validated by a grounding validator that checks all referenced places exist. The platform introduces two benchmark challenges: the Community Planning Challenge (cooperative assistant tasks and competitive social influence) evaluated with heuristic, MCTS, and LLM planners, and the Community Robot Challenge (heterogeneous robot cooperation) evaluated with heuristic, RL (PPO), and VLA (π₀) baselines.

## Results

On the Community Planning Challenge (Table 1), no single method dominates all three tasks. In the 1-assistant setting, the LLM Planner achieves the best average success rate (47.2%), driven by strong Search performance (70.1%), while the Heuristic Planner leads on Delivery (46.5%). In the 2-assistant setting, the Heuristic Planner achieves the highest average SR (54.6%), with the LLM Planner close behind (50.6%). On the Community Robot Challenge (Table 4), the best baseline (Heuristic with Oracle Grasp) reaches only 36.8% average SR, and the VLA baseline (π₀) achieves near-zero performance (2.4%), highlighting the difficulty of physics-level multi-robot cooperation in open worlds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a benchmark paper, so comparisons are among the proposed baseline methods rather than against prior work. In the 1-assistant Community Planning setting, the LLM Planner (GPT-4o-driven, following CoELA) achieves the best overall average SR (47.2%) primarily due to its dominance on Search (70.1%), but it underperforms the Heuristic Planner on Carry (29.9% vs. 34.7%) and Delivery (41.7% vs. 46.5%). The MCTS Planner is competitive on Carry (42.3%) but fails to complete Delivery and Search within the step limit (Ts = 1500). In the 2-assistant setting, the Heuristic Planner pulls ahead (54.6% avg SR) while the LLM Planner's advantage narrows (50.6%), suggesting that cooperation partially compensates for the LLM's weaker spatial reasoning. On the Community Robot Challenge, the Heuristic baseline (using IK + RRT-Connect) outperforms the RL baseline (PPO) by ~5.6% average SR without oracle grasp, and both drop significantly without oracle grasp (19.9% vs. 14.3% for Heuristic/RL). The VLA baseline (π₀) achieves near-zero performance (2.4% avg SR), attributed to domain gaps between tabletop pretraining and outdoor ground-level manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the Genesis physics engine with real-world geospatial data (Google 3D Tiles, OpenStreetMap) and generative models (Stable Diffusion, One-2-3-45, Architect) to create a simulation platform that departs from prior embodied AI simulators in two key ways: it supports large, heterogeneous communities of both human avatars and robots in a single physics loop, and it grounds scenes in actual geographic locations rather than purely procedural or reconstructed environments. For agent behavior, it adapts the generative-agents paradigm (Park et al., 2023) for structured daily scheduling in 3D open worlds and follows CoELA (Zhang et al., 2023) for LLM-driven multi-agent planning, while extending both to the open-world, multi-embodiment setting. Indoor scene generation leverages GRUTopia and Architect, and the robot simulation inherits from MuJoCo/Genesis URDF pipelines, positioning the work as an integrative platform rather than a single algorithmic contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Virtual Community is the first open-source platform to support scalable, physics-based simulation of mixed human-robot communities in geographically grounded open worlds, filling a gap between indoor household simulators (Habitat, AI2-THOR) and driving simulators (CARLA, Waymax) that lack rich social interaction. The two proposed challenges—Community Planning and Community Robot—establish new benchmarks where all baselines (heuristic, MCTS, LLM, RL, VLA) achieve low success rates (≤55%), highlighting that open-world multi-agent planning and heterogeneous robot cooperation remain largely unsolved. Key limitations include insufficient outdoor scene detail for accurate physics, the near-zero VLA performance revealing a large domain gap from tabletop pretraining, and the reliance on LLMs for high-level planning that struggle with spatial cost estimation. Open questions include whether more powerful world models or learned spatial representations can close the planning gap, and how social influence dynamics scale beyond 15 agents.
