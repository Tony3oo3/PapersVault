## Problem

While SSMs are known to fail on certain state-tracking tasks, prior work has only identified partial limitations (e.g., inability to track non-solvable groups or parity) without a complete characterization. This paper asks: exactly which finite groups can a k-layer input-dependent complex-valued diagonal (DCD) SSM track at finite precision, and does theoretical expressivity translate into learnability under standard gradient-based training?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261816/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of group elements x_1, …, x_T drawn from a finite group G (e.g., cyclic groups C_N, products like C_2×C_4, or non-Abelian groups S_3, A_4) and must output the running product y_t = x_1·x_2·…·x_t at each step. Models are trained on sequences up to length 60 with curriculum learning and evaluated on sequences up to length 1000 to measure length generalization; the reported metric is the longest test length ≥ 100 at which accuracy exceeds 90%.

## Method

The paper proves that a single-layer DCD SSM tracks a group G at finite precision if and only if G is Abelian, and more generally that a k-layer DCD SSM tracks G if and only if G admits a subnormal series of length k with Abelian factors. The proof exploits the fact that diagonal SSM state updates over a finite input sequence reduce to a single affine map, then classifies each state coordinate's dynamics (contraction, neutral rotation, translation, expansion) and shows that non-Abelian tracking would require distinct rotation centers that inevitably cause divergence. For the multi-layer direction, a layer-peeling argument inductively extracts the subnormal chain from the SSM's state structure. Empirically, the authors train Mamba, Negative Mamba, AUSSM, Simple AUSSM, and RNN baselines on nine group tasks and find that while single-layer models succeed on Abelian groups, two-layer models fail to learn non-Abelian groups (S_3, A_4) despite being theoretically capable, revealing a significant expressivity-to-learnability gap.

## Results

The metric is the longest test sequence length (≥100) at which a model achieves >90% accuracy, with training capped at length 60. Mamba fails on all nine tasks in both single- and two-layer configurations. Negative Mamba (single-layer) solves only C₂ (length 1000), while its two-layer variant additionally solves C₂×C₄ (length 360). AUSSM and Simple AUSSM solve all six Abelian groups at single layer (e.g., Simple AUSSM reaches 1000 on C₂, 240 on C₆, 500 on C₃×C₆) but fail on both non-Abelian solvable groups S₃ and A₄ even at two layers, whereas the RNN baseline succeeds on S₃ and A₄ (length 1000) in both configurations. The non-solvable group A₅ is unsolvable by all models, as predicted by theory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2024) fails on every task in both single- and two-layer settings, consistent with the theoretical requirement for negative eigenvalues to solve even parity. Negative Mamba (Grazzi et al., 2025) improves on Mamba by allowing eigenvalues in (−1,1]: its single layer solves only C₂ (length 1000), and stacking two layers extends coverage to C₂×C₄ (length 360), demonstrating that depth unlocks additional Abelian structure through gradient-based learning. AUSSM (Karuvally et al., 2025) and its simplified variant solve all six Abelian groups at single layer (Simple AUSSM reaches up to length 1000 on C₂ and 500 on C₃×C₆), but both fail on the non-Abelian solvable groups S₃ and A₄ even at two layers, despite the paper proving that a two-layer DCD SSM is theoretically expressive enough for these groups. The RNN baseline, which has no diagonal constraint, succeeds on S₃ and A₄ (length 1000) in both configurations, confirming that the failure of diagonal SSMs on non-Abelian groups is a learnability gap rather than an expressivity one. All models correctly fail on the non-solvable group A₅, matching the theoretical prediction that no finite-depth diagonal SSM can track non-solvable groups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on SSM expressivity for state-tracking (Merrill et al. 2024; Sarrof et al. 2024; Grazzi et al. 2025) by replacing circuit-complexity arguments with a precise group-theoretic characterization: a k-layer diagonal complex SSM tracks group G if and only if G admits a subnormal series of length k with Abelian factors. It builds directly on the AUSSM architecture (Karuvally et al. 2025), which established that a single complex diagonal layer can simulate Abelian groups, and on the S4/S4D lineage (Gu et al. 2022) for the diagonal state-space formulation. The key departure is providing an unconditional, exact boundary within solvable groups rather than relying on the TC⁰ conjecture, and extending the analysis from single-layer impossibility results to a complete multi-layer characterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a clean, actionable expressivity boundary for the most efficient class of SSMs (diagonal), showing that depth provides a disciplined pathway to solvable groups but cannot reach non-solvable ones. The empirically demonstrated learnability gap—two-layer models provably represent S₃ yet consistently fail to learn it under standard training—highlights that expressivity characterizations alone are insufficient for predicting practical performance, and points to loss-landscape structure as a critical open problem. Future work on initialization strategies, loss-landscape analysis for SSMs, and hybrid architectures (e.g., block-diagonal transitions) could close the gap between what diagonal SSMs can represent and what they can learn.
