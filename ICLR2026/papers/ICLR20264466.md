## Problem

Existing learning-based MAPF solvers (GNNs, transformers) are constrained to pairwise message passing between agents, which causes attention dilution in dense environments and fails to capture the group-level interactions that are necessary for optimal multi-agent coordination. The paper asks whether hypergraph-based representations can scale beyond simple group settings to model the highly-coupled dynamics of large-scale MAPF, where optimality generally requires reasoning over the full joint state of all agents simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264466/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a 4-channel local observation tensor of shape (2R_obs+1)×(2R_obs+1) centered on its current position, containing an obstacle map, an agent map, a goal-direction projection, and a normalized cost-to-go map. The model outputs a per-agent action distribution over the five candidate target vertices (stay, up, down, left, right), sampled via a learned temperature-scaled softmax. A communication radius R_comm=7 governs which agents are connected in the hypergraph, and the hypergraph structure is rebuilt dynamically at each timestep based on agent positions.

## Method

HMAGAT replaces the GNN layers in MAGAT with a hypergraph attention network that operates over directed hypergraphs with singleton heads and multi-node tails, where each hyperedge groups agents that are expected to interact collectively. Hyperedges are generated via colouring-based strategies (Lloyd's algorithm or k-means on the grid, with soft overlapping boundaries) or shortest-distance-based clique extraction, assigning agents sharing a colour within R_comm to the same hyperedge tail. The attention mechanism proceeds in two stages—tail nodes attend to their hyperedge, then the head agent attends to its incident hyperedges—so that adding irrelevant agents to a hyperedge's tail does not dilute the head's attention to informative hyperedges, formally mitigating the attention dilution that plagues pairwise GNNs. Training follows an imitation learning pipeline: 21K expert trajectories from lacam3 (100× less data than MAPF-GPT), on-policy dataset aggregation for distribution shift, a post-training phase with quality-improvement expert calls, and an RL-trained temperature module for calibrated action sampling. With only ~1M parameters, HMAGAT outperforms the 85M-parameter MAPF-GPT on dense maps and large maps like ost003d.

## Results

HMAGAT (1M parameters, trained on 21K instances) outperforms the 85M-parameter MAPF-GPT model (trained on 3.75M instances) on Maze maps in solution quality, while being significantly faster. On the Dense Warehouse map at maximum agent density (128 agents), HMAGAT achieves 89.1% success rate versus 4% for MAPF-GPT (85M). In the stripped-down HGNN-vs-GNN comparison (Table 1), the HGNN model achieves 39.8% success rate on Dense Warehouse versus 2.3% for the GNN model, with a relative SoC of 1.78 vs. 2.12 (lower is better, relative to lacam3). On the large ost003d map, MAPF-GPT (85M) fails to scale, while HMAGAT produces solutions close to lacam3's quality (Rel. SoC ≈ 1.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HMAGAT consistently outperforms MAGAT (Li et al., 2021) across all maps and agent densities in both success rate and solution quality, with the gap widening in dense scenarios. Against MAPF-GPT (Andreychuk et al., 2025a), HMAGAT wins on Maze maps in solution quality and dramatically on Dense Warehouse at maximum density (89.1% vs. 4% success rate), but MAPF-GPT (85M) retains a slight edge on Room and Warehouse maps at lower densities; HMAGAT also scales to ost003d where MAPF-GPT (85M) fails entirely. HMAGAT outperforms MAPF-GPT-DDG (Andreychuk et al., 2025b) on most maps, though MAPF-GPT-DDG achieves marginally higher success rates on Dense Room at some agent counts while still having worse SoC. Compared to SSIL (Veerapaneni et al., 2025), SCRIMP (Wang et al., 2023), and DCC (Ma et al., 2021), HMAGAT achieves substantially better performance, with the gap so large that these methods are relegated to the appendix. Notably, HMAGAT achieves all of this with only ~1.2% of the parameters and ~1% of the training data used by MAPF-GPT (85M), and is significantly faster at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAGAT (Li et al., 2021) GNN-based imitation learning framework for MAPF by replacing its pairwise GNN layers with directed hypergraph attention layers, while also incorporating improvements from follow-up works (stacked layers, position-based edge features). It departs from the broader MAPF learning literature (MAGAT, MAPF-GPT, SCRIMP, DCC) which uniformly models agent interactions as pairwise, and from prior hypergraph multi-agent work (Zhu et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025; Lin et al., 2024) which has only addressed small-scale or loosely-coupled settings. The paper positions itself as the first to demonstrate that hypergraph-based group interaction modelling scales to large, highly-coupled multi-agent pathfinding with dozens of agents in dense obstacle environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a 1M-parameter hypergraph model trained on 21K instances outperforms an 85M-parameter transformer trained on 3.75M instances—challenges the prevailing assumption that multi-agent coordination requires scaling model size and data volume, suggesting that appropriate inductive biases (here, explicit group structure) are a more efficient path. The mechanistic analyses (attention dilution proof, Shapley value decomposition) provide a clear explanation for *why* pairwise attention fails in dense scenarios, which is broadly applicable beyond MAPF to any multi-agent setting with uneven interaction importance. Open questions include whether the specific hypergraph generation strategies (k-means colouring, Lloyd's partitioning) are optimal, whether the approach generalizes to continuous-state or non-grid multi-agent tasks, and whether the imitation learning paradigm can be replaced with end-to-end training while retaining the hypergraph inductive bias.
