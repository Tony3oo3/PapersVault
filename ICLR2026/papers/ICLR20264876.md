## Problem

Masked diffusion language models recompute self-attention and feed-forward sublayers for every token position at every sampling step, even after many unmasked tokens have effectively converged, resulting in substantial wasted compute. Prior acceleration work has focused on reducing the number of steps (temporal methods) or reusing intermediate states across steps (reuse methods), but none permanently deactivates token positions within a step, so the per-step attention cost remains O(N²d) even in late iterations.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a length-N token sequence (prompt plus N_gen generation positions) processed by an L-block Transformer MDLM over T sampling steps. At each step it consumes the current posteriors p_t^(i) = softmax(z_t^(i)) at every position to compute step-wise KL divergences and confidence scores, then produces a binary lock decision per position. The final output is the fully unmasked sequence; locked positions retain their posterior from the lock step and are excluded from all subsequent computation while remaining attendable via cached K/V vectors.

## Method

SureLock permanently locks a token position once its step-wise KL divergence D_t^(i) = KL(p_t^(i) || p_{t-1}^(i)) falls below a threshold ε (optionally gated by a top-m% confidence criterion), caching its K/V vectors and bypassing its Q-projection and per-token FFN at all subsequent steps. Active positions attend to locked ones through the cached K/V, so the per-block attention cost drops from O(N²d) to O(M_t·Nd) and FFN from O(Nd²) to O(M_t·d²), where M_t is the shrinking active set. The paper provides a closed-form theoretical bound (Theorem 1) showing that the terminal log-probability error is at most C_tail·√ε under geometric tail contraction and Lipschitz smoothness assumptions, giving a direct design rule ε(δ) = δ²/C_tail². On LLaDA-8B, SureLock reduces algorithmic FLOPs by 30–50% while maintaining generation quality on WikiText-103 continuation, MT-Bench instruction following, and HumanEval code generation.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct (MT-Bench, N_gen=256, S=256, ε=5e-3), SureLock reduces algorithmic FLOPs to 0.528× of the baseline while the gpt-4o judge score actually improves from 4.4 to 4.7. On LLaDA-8B-Base (WikiText-103 continuation, N_gen=512, S=512, ε=5e-3), Gen.-PPL increases only from 1.4658 to 1.5444 (1.05×) while FLOPs drop to 0.49×. On HumanEval code generation (N_gen=256, S=256), Pass@1 is maintained at 18.6% (vs. 18.6% baseline) at 0.459× FLOPs. Across all settings, the per-step FLOPs ratio decreases monotonically as sampling progresses, with the largest savings in later steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the identical MDLM sampler without any locking (the 'baseline' in all tables). SureLock consistently reduces algorithmic FLOPs to 0.47×–0.70× of baseline depending on N_gen and ε, while MT-Bench scores are unchanged or slightly improved (up to +0.4 pts) and WikiText-103 Gen.-PPL degrades by at most 1.05× for N_gen≥256. Against the selection-based method from DLLM-Cache (Liu et al., 2025b, surrogate k=0.8), SureLock alone achieves 1.30× TPS and 0.54× FLOPs, outperforming selection alone (1.18× TPS, 0.64× FLOPs) on both metrics. The two methods are complementary: combining SureLock with selection yields 1.73× TPS and 0.28× FLOPs, substantially better than either in isolation, confirming that SureLock's permanent position deactivation is orthogonal to step-wise subset selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

SureLock builds on the masked diffusion language model (MDLM) framework (Nie et al., 2025) and positions itself as a third, orthogonal axis of inference acceleration alongside two established lines: temporal methods that reduce the number of sampling steps (parallel/dilated unmasking, distilled samplers) and reuse methods that amortize K/V computation across steps (DLLM-Cache, DKV-Cache, Fast-DLLM). Its closest conceptual neighbor is selection-based partial compute (Liu et al., 2025b), but SureLock differs fundamentally in that it permanently and monotonically removes positions from the active set rather than re-selecting a subset at each step. The paper explicitly demonstrates orthogonality by combining SureLock with a selection-based surrogate, showing multiplicative FLOP reductions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30–50% algorithmic FLOP reduction with negligible quality loss (≤0.1 pt on MT-Bench, no HumanEval degradation) is practically significant for a paradigm where per-step cost scales as O(N²d) and longer contexts are a key research direction. The closed-form error bound (Theorem 1) linking the KL locking threshold to terminal log-probability deviation provides a principled, model-agnostic design knob rather than an ad-hoc heuristic. Key limitations include the gap between FLOP savings and wall-clock speedups (irregular memory access patterns, low kernel occupancy for small active sets), quality degradation for very short generations on open-ended tasks, and the fact that the theoretical assumptions (geometric KL contraction, logit smoothness) are idealized. Open questions include hardware-specific kernel fusion to close the FLOP-to-latency gap, extension to continuous modalities, and per-setting threshold calibration.
