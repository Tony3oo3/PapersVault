## Problem

The dominant BEV paradigm for multi-modal fusion perception discretizes sensor features onto a fixed-resolution grid, causing irreversible detail loss and limiting cross-modal feature interaction. Increasing BEV resolution to recover detail incurs prohibitive memory and compute costs, while simple concatenation-based fusion strategies fail to achieve effective cross-modal alignment. The paper asks whether a continuous, non-quantized 3D representation can replace the BEV grid as the unified fusion space.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surround-view camera images (downsampled to 704×256) and a LiDAR point cloud voxelized at 0.075 m resolution, with a perception range of ±51.2 m in X/Y and −5 to 3 m in Z for detection. Internally, camera features are lifted into 41 discrete depth bins per pixel, and both modalities are represented as sets of 3D Gaussians (mean, scale, rotation) in a shared 200×200 BEV space. The unified Gaussian representation is voxelized into a BEV feature map B_F that feeds task-specific heads, producing 3D object detection boxes (Transformer head) or per-voxel semantic occupancy labels (convolutional head) without architectural changes.

## Method

GaussianFusion replaces the discrete BEV grid with a continuous 3D Gaussian mixture representation for multi-modal fusion. Camera Gaussians are initialized via forward projection using LSS depth distributions (rather than random initialization), while LiDAR Gaussians are seeded at BEV voxel centers; both carry learned query features. A shared Gaussian Encoder (4 stacked blocks) iteratively refines Gaussian properties by predicting residual offsets (Δμ, Δs, Δr) via MLP rather than re-predicting full parameters, and uses deformable attention whose sampling locations are derived from each Gaussian's covariance matrix, providing a shape-aware prior absent in vanilla deformable attention. Cross-modal fusion occurs naturally in the shared Gaussian space: camera and LiDAR Gaussians are merged into a single set, pooled per voxel via MeanVFE, and aggregated through the Gaussian mixture model to produce the final BEV feature. This design preserves fine-grained geometric and semantic detail before any quantization step and enables task-agnostic 3D perception from a single unified representation.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, GaussianFusion achieves 74.0 NDS and 71.7 mAP for 3D object detection, outperforming BEVFusion by +2.6 NDS and +3.2 mAP while also reducing inference latency from 156 ms to 132 ms and memory from 5140 MB to 4271 MB. For 3D semantic occupancy prediction, GaussianFusion (C+L) reaches 28.65 mIoU, surpassing the previous multi-modal SOTA OccFusion by +1.11 mIoU. The camera-only variant GaussianFusion-C achieves 20.65 mIoU with only 43,296 Gaussians (30% of GaussianFormer's 140,000) and 105 ms latency (4.5× faster than GaussianFormer's 475 ms). On Waymo Open Dataset, GaussianFusion improves over BEVFusion by +3.51 mAP-L1 and +3.82 mAPH-L1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianFusion outperforms all 17 compared baselines on the primary 3D object detection task. Against the closest multi-modal BEV-based competitors, it leads UniTR [46] by +0.7 NDS / +1.2 mAP, EA-LSS [13] by +0.9 NDS / +0.5 mAP, and FusionFormer-S [12] by +0.8 NDS / +1.7 mAP on nuScenes val. The improvement over the direct BEV-fusion baseline BEVFusion [29] is +2.6 NDS / +3.2 mAP, and over MetaBEV [8] it is +2.5 NDS / +3.7 mAP. For 3D semantic occupancy, GaussianFusion (C+L) beats OccFusion [35] by +1.11 mIoU and CO-Occ [37] by +1.55 mIoU; the camera-only variant GaussianFusion-C surpasses GaussianFormer [11] by +1.55 mIoU while using only 30% of the Gaussians and running 4.5× faster. On Waymo, GaussianFusion improves over BEVFusion [29] by +3.51 mAP-L1 and +4.42 mAPH-L2, confirming cross-dataset generalization. The temporal variant GaussianFusion-T matches SparseLIF-T [61] in NDS (77.6 vs 77.5) while achieving higher mAP (75.0 vs 74.7) without sophisticated temporal modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant discrete BEV grid paradigm for multi-modal fusion (BEVFusion, MetaBEV, UniTR) by replacing it with a continuous 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, drawing on the 3DGS formulation from Kerbl et al. (2023) and the Gaussian Mixture Model for natural cross-modal aggregation. It builds directly on Lift-Splat-Shoot (Philion & Fidler, 2020) for forward-projection-based camera Gaussian initialization and on Deformable DeTR (Zhu et al., 2020) for feature extraction, but modifies the latter by using Gaussian covariance as a shape prior for sampling locations. The closest prior work is GaussianFormer (H et al., 2024), which also uses 3D Gaussians for occupancy but relies on random initialization and full re-prediction of Gaussian parameters; GaussianFusion replaces both with structured initialization and iterative offset updates, and extends the framework to multi-modal (camera + LiDAR) fusion rather than camera-only.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled alternative to the BEV grid as the unified space for multi-modal fusion, arguing that continuous Gaussian distributions preserve fine-grained geometric and semantic detail that discrete quantization inevitably loses. The task-agnostic design (same Gaussian backbone for detection and occupancy) and the 450% speedup over GaussianFormer with fewer Gaussians make this a practically attractive option for real-time autonomous driving stacks. However, the temporal extension is rudimentary (simple warping + pooling), and the authors acknowledge that motion-aware Gaussian updates remain an open direction; whether the Gaussian representation can match or exceed purpose-built temporal fusion modules at scale is unresolved. The modest accuracy gains (+2.6 NDS, +1.11 mIoU) suggest the representation benefit is real but not yet transformative relative to well-tuned BEV baselines.
