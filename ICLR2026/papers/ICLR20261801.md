## Problem

PPO-style clipping, the standard mechanism for stabilizing policy updates in LLM reinforcement learning, is a crude heuristic approximation of the underlying KL trust region principle. It suppresses gradients for tokens whose importance ratios exceed a fixed threshold, leading to unstable optimization, poor calibration, and sensitivity to hyperparameters. No principled, differentiable trust region projection existed for the discrete (categorical) token distributions at modern LLM vocabulary scale (100K+ tokens).

## Input / Output

For each token position in a generated sequence, TROLL consumes the current policy's logits (tilde_pi) and the old policy's logits (pi_old) used for data collection, along with advantage estimates from any method (GRPO, PPO, Dr.GRPO, GSPO, REINFORCE++). Rather than operating on full 151K+ vocabulary distributions, a sparsification step retains only the top-K tokens (K=64) covering 1−δ=99.999% of probability mass—typically just 5–10 tokens—plus the actually sampled token. The output is a projected token distribution per position that satisfies a per-token KL bound ε relative to the old policy, which feeds into the RL objective to update LLM parameters; at inference time the model behaves identically to the unprojected policy.

## Method

TROLL replaces PPO clipping with a per-token differentiable trust region projection: for each token, it solves a convex program that finds the distribution closest (in KL) to the current policy while remaining within an ε-KL ball around the old policy. The closed-form primal solution is a geometric interpolation of the two policies' logits, π_θ ∝ exp((η*·log π_old + log π̃_θ)/(η*+1)), where the step size η* is obtained by solving a one-dimensional convex Lagrangian dual via n-ary bracketing. Gradients flow through the projection via implicit differentiation of the KKT conditions (OptNet framework), preserving gradient information even for constrained tokens—unlike clipping, which zeros them. A sparsification scheme (greedy top-K selection with a small default mass p_d for discarded tokens) reduces memory and compute to a constant overhead independent of model size, and a regression term (α=1) pulls the unprojected LLM output toward the projected distribution to keep successive updates well-behaved. The result is a drop-in replacement for the clipping step in any PPO-like RL objective, requiring no changes to advantage estimation or inference.

## Results

On DAPO-Math with Qwen3-8B (GRPO), TROLL achieves 0.721 train / 0.691 eval success rate vs. 0.667 / 0.640 for Clip, a ~5 pp gain. Across all five advantage-estimation methods (GRPO, Dr.GRPO, PPO, GSPO, RF++) and both Qwen3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, TROLL consistently improves success rates by 3–10 percentage points (5–15% relative). On code generation (Eurus-Code), TROLL yields 7–18 pp (18–30% relative) gains over Clip across Qwen3 model sizes. Notably, GSPO with Clip diverges to 0.000 success rate while GSPO with TROLL reaches 0.736, and Llama3.1-8B goes from 0.000 (Clip) to 0.759 (TROLL) on GSM8K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TROLL outperforms PPO-style clipping across all seven compared baselines. Against GRPO (Clip), TROLL gains 3–10 pp on DAPO and MATH-Eval for both Qwen3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; the 4B TROLL model nearly matches the 14B Clip model. Dr.GRPO (Clip) and PPO (Clip) show similar 3–8 pp improvements with TROLL, while GSPO (Clip) catastrophically diverges to 0.000 success rate on both models, whereas GSPO (TROLL) recovers to 0.736 (Qwen3-8B) and 0.481 (Qwen2.5-7B). REINFORCE++ (Clip) benefits from TROLL with up to 10 pp gains on DAPO Eval. The adaptive clipping method BAPO slightly improves over regular Clip but still clearly underperforms TROLL. The clipping-free GPG baseline suffers from stability issues in its vanilla form, but adding TROLL projections (GPG + TROLL) resolves these and yields performance comparable to GRPO (TROLL). On code generation, TROLL improves over Clip by 7–18 pp (18–30% relative) across Qwen3 sizes, with Qwen3-1.7B (TROLL) surpassing Qwen3-4B (Clip).

## Relation to Prior Work

TROLL extends the differentiable trust region projection framework of Otto et al. (2021) from Gaussian policies in continuous control to categorical (discrete) token distributions in LLMs, and leverages the OptNet implicit differentiation framework (Amos & Kolter, 2017) to make the projection fully differentiable. It directly replaces the heuristic PPO clipping mechanism (Schulman et al., 2017) with a principled per-token KL constraint, while remaining agnostic to the advantage estimation method (GRPO, Dr.GRPO, GSPO, REINFORCE++, PPO). A key practical departure is the sparsification scheme that exploits the heavy-tailed (Zipfian) structure of LLM token distributions to reduce projection cost from O(vocabulary) to O(5–10 tokens), making the approach viable for modern 100k+ vocabulary models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since PPO-style clipping is the default stabilization mechanism in virtually all RL-based LLM post-training pipelines, a principled drop-in replacement that consistently improves success rates by 3–10 percentage points (and fixes outright divergence in GSPO and non-instruct Llama3.1-8B) has broad practical impact. The sparsification guarantee (Theorem A.2) showing that 5–10 tokens suffice to approximate the full-distribution KL within ~0.001 error provides theoretical grounding for the efficiency. Open questions include scaling beyond 14B dense models, applicability to Mixture-of-Experts architectures, and whether the trust region projection transfers to other modalities (e.g., vision-language) where token distributions may behave differently.
