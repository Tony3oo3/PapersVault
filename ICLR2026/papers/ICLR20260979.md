## Problem

Egocentric (first-person) video generation remains largely underexplored because the camera trajectory is not externally specified but is inherently determined by the wearer's head motion, creating a tight coupling between visual content and body dynamics. No prior work jointly generates egocentric video and the corresponding human motion in a viewpoint-consistent and causally coherent manner, leaving two fundamental challenges unaddressed: aligning the generated camera trajectory with the head trajectory from motion, and modeling the recursive observation-action loop between video frames and body movements.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt describing intended actions, a static initial human pose (23-joint Xsens skeleton), and a single initial egocentric frame (480×480) as conditioning. It produces a synchronized 5-second egocentric video (41 frames at 8 FPS) and a human motion sequence (81 frames at 16 FPS, i.e., 2× the video temporal resolution) in a head-centric representation. The framework also supports conditional sampling: generating motion from text+video (TV2M) or video from text+motion (TM2V) via classifier-free guidance.

## Method

EgoTwin is a triple-branch diffusion transformer (text, video, motion) extending MM-DiT, where the motion branch spans only the lower 21 of 42 layers with reduced channel dimensions (768 vs 3072), since camera pose and scene structure are captured in early video layers. Two key design choices distinguish it: (1) a head-centric motion representation that anchors all joint positions and velocities to the head joint rather than the root, directly exposing the egocentric viewpoint to the video branch; and (2) a cybernetics-inspired structured attention mask implementing forward dynamics (video tokens attend to preceding motion tokens) and inverse dynamics (motion tokens attend to current and upcoming video tokens), enforcing frame-wise causal synchronization. The model uses asynchronous diffusion with independently sampled timesteps for video and motion latents, trained in three stages: motion VAE pretraining, text-to-motion pretraining with frozen text branch, and joint text-video-motion fine-tuning. The video branch is initialized from CogVideoX (3D causal VAE, 4×8×8 compression), while the motion VAE uses 1D causal convolutions with 4× temporal downsampling.

## Results

On the Nymeria dataset, EgoTwin achieves I-FID 98.17, FVD 1033.52, and CLIP-SIM 27.34 for video quality, and M-FID 41.80, R-Prec 0.62, and MM-Dist 15.05 for motion quality, substantially outperforming the VidMLD baseline across all nine metrics. The most striking gains appear in video-motion consistency: TransErr drops from 1.28 to 0.67, RotErr from 1.53 to 0.46, and HandScore rises from 0.36 to 0.81. Cross-dataset evaluation on Ego-Exo4D confirms generalization, with EgoTwin maintaining a HandScore of 0.79 versus 0.32 for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoTwin is compared against three unimodal SOTA methods (Table 3) and a joint-generation baseline VidMLD (Table 1). Against CogVideoX (Yang et al., 2025), EgoTwin improves I-FID from 182.97 to 98.17 and FVD from 1793.79 to 1033.52, while also matching or exceeding its CLIP-SIM (27.34 vs. 24.90). Against CameraCtrl (He et al., 2025), a camera-controlled video generator, EgoTwin achieves lower I-FID (98.17 vs. 120.48) and FVD (1033.52 vs. 1263.90) without requiring predefined camera trajectories. Against MLD (Chen et al., 2023), a latent-space motion diffusion model, EgoTwin reduces M-FID from 47.25 to 41.80, improves R-Prec from 0.39 to 0.62, and lowers MM-Dist from 21.47 to 15.05. The joint-generation baseline VidMLD, which naively combines CogVideoX and MLD via MM-DiT without the proposed head-centric representation or interaction mechanism, is outperformed on every metric, with the largest gaps in video-motion consistency (e.g., RotErr 0.46 vs. 1.53, HandScore 0.81 vs. 0.36), confirming that the architectural designs are critical for cross-modal alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoTwin builds on the CogVideoX video diffusion backbone and the MM-DiT multimodal diffusion transformer architecture, extending them from a text-video pair to a text-video-motion triple. It departs from the standard root-centric motion representation (canonical in HumanML3D and KIT-ML) by proposing a head-centric formulation that directly exposes the egocentric camera viewpoint to the video branch. The cybernetics-inspired structured attention mask draws on the observation-action feedback loop from control theory, repurposing it to enforce fine-grained temporal causality between video and motion tokens rather than relying on implicit positional encodings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly generate egocentric video and the wearer's full-body motion with explicit viewpoint alignment and causal coherence, opening a new research direction at the intersection of video generation, motion synthesis, and embodied AI. The head-centric motion representation and the video-motion consistency metrics (TransErr, RotErr, HandScore) provide reusable tools for the community. Key limitations include the absence of hand joints, physical implausibility in generated videos, and restriction to 5-second clips at 480p; future work on hand motion, 3D priors, and longer temporal contexts would be needed before the approach is practically deployable in AR or embodied-agent pipelines.
