## Problem

Representation alignment (REPA) accelerates diffusion transformer training by distilling features from pretrained vision encoders, but the prevailing assumption is that global semantic performance (ImageNet-1K linear probing accuracy) of the target encoder drives generation quality. This paper challenges that assumption by asking a fundamental question: does the benefit of REPA come from global semantic information or from the spatial structure (pairwise similarity patterns) among patch tokens?

## Input / Output

The method operates on SiT diffusion transformers (B, L, XL scales) trained on ImageNet at 256×256 (and 512×512) resolution, consuming noised VAE latents (stabilityai/sd-vae-ft-mse) and producing generated images evaluated via FID, IS, sFID, precision, and recall. The target representation contributes a spatial grid of patch tokens (H×W×D) from a frozen pretrained vision encoder, which is aligned to intermediate diffusion features at a chosen layer depth. The two iREPA modifications operate directly on this patch token grid: the spatial normalization computes statistics across the spatial dimension, and the convolutional projection acts on the 2D spatial layout rather than flattening it.

## Method

iREPA introduces two minimal modifications (<4 lines of code) to standard REPA that specifically accentuate spatial information transfer from the target encoder to the diffusion model. First, the standard 3-layer MLP projection (which flattens and re-maps diffusion features to target dimensions) is replaced with a single 3×3 convolutional layer (kernel_size=3, padding=1) that preserves local spatial relationships through its inductive bias, avoiding the spatial information loss observed with MLPs. Second, a spatial normalization layer is applied to the target representation's patch tokens, subtracting a scaled spatial mean (γ ∈ [0.6, 0.8]) and dividing by spatial standard deviation to remove the global component that reduces contrast between individual patch tokens. The paper's large-scale correlation analysis across 27 vision encoders and 3 model scales shows that spatial structure metrics (LDS, SRSS, CDS, RMSC) correlate with generation FID at Pearson |r| > 0.85, far exceeding the |r| = 0.26 of linear probing accuracy, and that even classical spatial features (SIFT, HOG) can provide REPA gains. iREPA consistently improves convergence speed across diverse encoders, model sizes, and training recipes including REPA-E, MeanFlow w/ REPA, and pixel-space JiT.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with SiT-XL/2 at 100K steps, iREPA (full: conv projection + spatial normalization) reduces FID from 19.06 to 16.96 with DINOv2-B, from 21.47 to 16.26 with DINOv3-B, from 26.10 to 16.66 with WebSSL-1B, and from 32.35 to 18.19 with PE-Core-G. At 400K steps with classifier-free guidance, iREPA achieves FID 1.89 (DINOv3-B) and 1.90 (WebSSL-1B), consistently outperforming baseline REPA. The spatial structure metric (LDS) correlates with generation FID at Pearson |r|=0.852, far exceeding linear probing accuracy (|r|=0.26) across 27 vision encoders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iREPA consistently outperforms standard REPA across all four tested vision encoders at 100K steps, with the largest gains on encoders with weaker spatial structure (PE-Core-G: FID 32.35→18.19, a 43.8% reduction; WebSSL-1B: 26.10→16.66, a 36.2% reduction). Against REPA-E (end-to-end tuned VAE), iREPA-E achieves FID 13.2 vs 26.5 for WebSSL-1B and 11.7 vs 14.4 for DINOv3-B at 100K steps. For MeanFlow w/ REPA at 4 NFE with CFG, iREPA reduces FID from 15.56 to 11.05 (DINOv3-B) and from 15.28 to 12.59 (DINOv2-B). For pixel-space JiT w/ REPA, iREPA consistently accelerates convergence across DINOv2, DINOv3, and PE encoders. The paper also shows that even classical spatial features (SIFT, HOG) can provide REPA gains, further supporting that spatial structure—not global semantic accuracy—is the key driver.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on REPA (Yu et al., 2024), which established that aligning diffusion transformer features with pretrained vision encoder representations accelerates convergence, and on the broader lineage of using pretrained encoders for generative training (projected GANs, adversarial distillation). It departs from the prevailing interpretation in that lineage—which attributes REPA's gains to global semantic information, as measured by linear probing accuracy—by showing that spatial self-similarity structure between patch tokens is the dominant driver. The work also connects to recent analyses of the spatial-vs-global tradeoff in pretrained encoders (DINOv3, Perception Encoder, C-RADIO), but reframes the question from 'which encoder is best for dense prediction' to 'which encoder is best as a REPA target for generation.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that spatial structure (not ImageNet accuracy) predicts REPA effectiveness overturns a widely-cited assumption and immediately changes how practitioners should select and design target representations for diffusion training. The proposed iREPA modifications (<4 lines of code: a 3×3 conv projection and spatial normalization) are trivially adoptable and yield consistent convergence gains across 27 encoders, 3 model scales, and multiple training recipes (REPA-E, MeanFlow, JiT), making this a low-barrier, high-impact practical improvement. Open questions include whether the spatial-structure principle extends to video and 3D generation, whether it can guide the design of purpose-built encoders for generative alignment, and whether the benefit persists at much larger scales or in fully text-conditioned settings beyond the 256×256 ImageNet and limited T2I experiments shown.
