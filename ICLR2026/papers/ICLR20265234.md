## Problem

Standard GNN readout functions for material property prediction rely on an atom-decomposable inductive bias, assuming graph-level properties can be decomposed into per-atom contributions. This assumption breaks down for spatial properties of porous materials (e.g., gas adsorption capacity, separation ratio, pore-limiting diameter in MOFs, COFs, PPNs, and zeolites), which are naturally decomposable into contributions of spatial regions rather than individual atoms, creating a fundamental mismatch that limits prediction accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a crystal structure (atomic positions, types, and lattice vectors) and produces a scalar material property. Unusually, it augments the atomic graph with 512 spatial nodes placed on a uniform 8×8×8 grid of fractional coordinates within the unit cell (with periodic boundary conditions), forming a heterogeneous atom-space graph. The spatial nodes carry learnable positional embeddings encoding their 3D voxel index, and a [CLS] token is prepended to the spatial node sequence for final property prediction via an MLP head.

## Method

SpatialRead reformulates the graph-level readout as a spatial integral over a contribution function g(r), proving equivalence to the standard node-decomposable form under a limited receptive field (Theorem 3.1). It introduces spatial nodes on a uniform grid within the unit cell, converting the isomorphic atomic graph into a heterogeneous atom-space graph with unidirectional message flow: atoms send messages to spatial nodes (and among themselves), but spatial nodes never send messages back to atoms. After T layers of PaiNN-style message passing, the unordered atomic features and ordered spatial node features (with positional embeddings) are fed into a multi-modal Transformer decoder with a [CLS] token, which adaptively fuses both representations via cross-attention. This design lets the model select the appropriate inductive bias—spatial pooling for integral-type properties like adsorption capacity, or atom-level attention for non-spatial properties like formation energy—without architectural changes. A simple PaiNN+SpatialRead trained from scratch outperforms the JMP foundation model (pre-trained on 120M samples) on spatial properties, while maintaining comparable performance on MatBench non-spatial tasks.

## Results

On six spatial property tasks (gas adsorption, separation, adsorption heat across MOFs, COFs, PPNs, and zeolites), PaiNN+SN+MM trained from scratch achieves R² scores of 0.784–0.987, outperforming the JMP foundation model (pre-trained on 120M samples) on 5 of 6 tasks (e.g., 0.987 vs. 0.971 for N₂ adsorption, 0.987 vs. 0.884 for COF CH₄ adsorption, 0.969 vs. 0.874 for zeolite CH₄ adsorption heat). Adding spatial nodes alone (+SN) already yields large gains over the PaiNN baseline (e.g., 0.978 vs. 0.925 for N₂ adsorption, 0.979 vs. 0.736 for COF CH₄ adsorption). On MatBench non-spatial properties, JMP+SpatialRead maintains comparable or slightly improved performance (e.g., bandgap MAE 0.107 vs. 0.119), confirming no degradation on atom-decomposable tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaiNN+SN+MM (trained from scratch) outperforms the JMP foundation model (pre-trained on 120M samples) on 5 of 6 spatial property tasks, with the largest margin on zeolite CH₄ adsorption heat (0.969 vs. 0.874) and COF CH₄ adsorption (0.987 vs. 0.884); it slightly trails MOFTransformer only on C₃H₆/C₃H₈ separation (0.784 vs. 0.817). Compared to the PaiNN backbone alone, adding spatial nodes yields substantial gains across all tasks (e.g., +0.053 R² on N₂ adsorption, +0.243 on COF CH₄ adsorption), while the more complex readout functions GraphTrans and GMT provide only marginal improvements (≤0.02 R²). On MatBench non-spatial properties, JMP+SpatialRead matches or slightly improves over JMP alone (bandgap MAE 0.107 vs. 0.119; JDFT2D 18.17 vs. 20.72 meV/atom), with a small regression on formation energy (15.3 vs. 13.6 meV/atom), confirming that the spatial inductive bias does not harm atom-decomposable tasks. The GemNet+SN+MM variant also benefits from SpatialRead (e.g., 0.986 vs. 0.816 on COF CH₄ adsorption), demonstrating compatibility with higher-order interaction backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard MPNN readout paradigm (sum/mean pooling over atoms, as used in CGCNN, GemNet, PaiNN, and foundation models like JMP) by introducing spatial nodes that represent voxelized regions, forming a heterogeneous atom-space graph with unidirectional message flow. It departs from prior readout designs (GraphTrans, GMT, Set2Set, DiffPool) that all treat atoms as the fundamental aggregation unit, arguing that this atom-decomposable inductive bias is physically mismatched for spatial properties like gas adsorption capacity. The multimodal Transformer decoder for fusing atom and spatial features draws on cross-attention architectures, while the theoretical equivalence proof between node-decomposable and region-decomposable formulations provides a formal justification for the reformulation as a pure inductive bias choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a from-scratch PaiNN+SpatialRead outperforms a 120M-sample-pretrained foundation model (JMP) on spatial properties highlights that physically-grounded readout design can outweigh scale and pre-training for specific property classes, with practical implications for high-throughput materials screening where ranking stability under distribution shift matters. The released 27-task benchmark across four porous material classes fills a gap in spatial-property evaluation. Limitations include the ~30% computational overhead, the fact that the multimodal Transformer provides limited additional benefit for integral-type properties (where simple spatial-node pooling suffices), and the approach's current focus on porous crystalline materials; extending to amorphous or non-porous systems and determining optimal spatial-node density for arbitrary unit-cell sizes remain open.
