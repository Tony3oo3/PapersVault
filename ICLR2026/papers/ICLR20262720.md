## Problem

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has driven strong reasoning gains in text-only LLMs, but its optimization objectives remain tailored to purely textual domains. The paper identifies that 67% of errors in multimodal reasoning models trained with standard GRPO stem from perception failures rather than reasoning mistakes, yet prior work has addressed this only through data curation, rollout augmentation, or auxiliary reward models—leaving the core policy gradient objective unchanged. PAPO asks whether a new optimization objective can directly incentivize visually grounded generation without additional supervision.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (processed into patches) and a text question, and generates a chain-of-thought reasoning sequence followed by a final answer enclosed in \boxed{}. During training, the same rollout sequence is additionally scored under a corrupted visual input (60% of patches randomly masked), producing a second forward pass that feeds the Implicit Perception Loss. Training uses ViRL39K (38.8K multimodal QA pairs with only input queries and final answers, no CoT annotations), and evaluation uses exact-match scoring at temperature 1.0 with 8 samples per question (avg@8).

## Method

PAPO extends GRPO or DAPO by adding an Implicit Perception Loss, defined as the KL divergence D_KL[π_θ(o|q,I) ‖ π_θ(o|q,I_mask)] between the policy conditioned on the original image and the policy conditioned on a randomly patch-masked version of the image. This 'reverse KL' is maximized so the model learns to assign substantially higher probability to its response when full visual context is available, thereby encouraging visually grounded generation without external labels or reward models. To prevent the unbounded KL objective from causing training collapse (a failure mode the paper calls KL_prcp Hacking), PAPO introduces a Double Entropy Loss that penalizes rising entropy in both the original and masked-policy sequences. The complete objective is a direct drop-in replacement for GRPO/DAPO, requiring only one additional forward pass per rollout with the masked image, and uses random patch masking at 60% ratio as the default corruption strategy.

## Results

PAPO achieves consistent overall improvements of 4.4%–17.5% (relative) over GRPO and DAPO across eight multimodal reasoning benchmarks, with the largest gain (17.54%) observed for PAPO_D-7B over DAPO-7B. On vision-dependent tasks specifically, gains reach 8.0%–19.1%, e.g., PAPO_D-7B scores 59.82% vs. DAPO-7B's 51.79% on the vision-dependent average. PAPO also reduces perception errors by 30.5% in manual error analysis and demonstrates faster convergence, with early-stage gains visible around 25 training steps. The method requires no additional annotations, reward models, or teacher models, using only a KL-divergence-based Implicit Perception Loss added to the standard RLVR objective.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAPO is compared against three baselines: GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), and NoisyRollout (Liu et al., 2025). Against GRPO, PAPO_G wins on all model sizes: 3B improves from 47.92% to 49.92% overall (↑4.36%), 7B from 58.78% to 61.66% (↑4.39%), and Qwen3-VL-2B from 46.84% to 49.30% (↑5.25%), with vision-dependent gains of 5.60%, 7.96%, and 6.27% respectively. Against DAPO, PAPO_D shows a dramatic 17.54% overall gain for the 7B model (63.16% vs. 55.01%), where DAPO-7B suffers from model collapse in later training stages while PAPO_D maintains stable improvements; the 3B model also improves by 5.54%. Against NoisyRollout (tested as GRPO+NoisyRollout), PAPO_G is complementary: combining PAPO_G with NoisyRollout yields 51.89% overall vs. 50.61% for GRPO+NoisyRollout alone, while NoisyRollout alone shows inconsistent gains (reductions on 4 of 9 benchmarks). PAPO wins or ties on every benchmark in the main comparison, with the largest per-benchmark gain being +15.61 points on MathVerse (7B, PAPO_D vs. DAPO) and +19.09 points on the vision-dependent average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO and DAPO for RLVR-based multimodal reasoning, but departs from the dominant line of prior work that has focused on data curation, rollout diversification (e.g., NoisyRollout), or reward engineering (e.g., captioning-based or IoU-based rewards) to address multimodal challenges. PAPO instead modifies the core policy gradient objective itself, introducing an Implicit Perception Loss (a KL divergence between the policy conditioned on original vs. masked visual input) and a Double Entropy regularizer. The authors position this as the first work to integrate perception-aware supervision at the optimization-objective level rather than as an auxiliary reward signal, arguing that prior reward-level approaches impose a rigid separation between perception and reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 67% of multimodal reasoning errors stem from perception rather than reasoning reframes where the field should focus its efforts, and PAPO offers a simple, drop-in mechanism (no extra data, reward models, or teacher models) to address this bottleneck with consistent 4–17% gains across eight benchmarks. Limitations include evaluation only on Qwen2.5-VL/Qwen3-VL at 2B–7B scale, a moderate ~18% per-step computational overhead from the extra forward pass, and uniform application of the perception loss to all tokens regardless of vision-dependency. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models or other model families (e.g., InternVL), whether finer-grained token-level selection of where to apply KL_prcp would improve efficiency, and how to further reduce training overhead.
