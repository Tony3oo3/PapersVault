## Problem

Most brain-computer interface research decodes neural signals into behavior or intent, but the reverse problem—designing visual stimuli that reliably steer brain activity toward a target state—remains largely unexplored. The core difficulty is that EEG feedback is noisy, non-differentiable, and lacks clear quantitative targets for subjective states, while prior closed-loop neuromodulation work has been restricted to invasive cortical recordings or low-level flicker stimuli that cannot engage higher-order semantic representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target neural feature vector (either a CLIP-aligned semantic embedding from ATM-S or a PSD spectral signature), a candidate image pool (600 images from THINGS-EEG2: 50 categories × 12 conditions), and a black-box image-to-EEG proxy model (e.g., AlexNet or ViT with a regression head predicting 17-channel × 250-timepoint EEG over 0–1000 ms post-stimulus). It outputs optimized naturalistic images generated by SDXL-Lightning with IP-Adapter conditioning, where each generated image is scored by the proxy model's predicted EEG similarity to the target, and the best images are fed back into the next optimization round.

## Method

MindPilot treats the brain as a non-differentiable black-box function g: image → EEG and formulates stimulus design as argmax_u sim(f(g(u)), y_target), where f is a feature encoder (semantic or spectral). The core mechanism is a pseudo-model guidance strategy: a Gaussian Process surrogate trained on collected (CLIP-embedding, EEG-similarity-reward) pairs predicts reward gradients in latent space, which are used as one-step updates to the diffusion model's conditioning embedding (z*_K = z_K − η∇f̂(z_K; Z^n)), enabling gradient-free steering of SDXL-Lightning toward EEG-derived objectives without backpropagating through the proxy. An iterative score-update loop maintains a probability distribution over the image database: top-k images receive EMA-based direct reward updates, and rewards are spread to semantically similar images via CLIP-embedding-weighted softmax, with roulette-wheel sampling selecting the next stimuli. A genetic algorithm layer (crossover and mutation in CLIP embedding space, preserving relative feature ordering) expands the search beyond the fixed pool, and the entire process converges within ~10 iterations to images whose evoked EEG features closely match the target.

## Results

On the THINGS-EEG2 EEG-to-image generation task, MindPilot achieves SSIM 0.35 and CLIP similarity 0.67, substantially outperforming the chance-level baseline (SSIM 0.28, CLIP 0.48) and approaching the ATM-S upper bound (CLIP 0.76). In closed-loop semantic retrieval across 10 subjects, MindPilot improves Semantic Similarity Score by an average of 10.9% and Intensity Similarity Score by 2.6% over random initialization. In the efficiency benchmark at 200 queries, MindPilot (offline) achieves an EEG score of 0.5384 in ~74 s, outperforming DDPO (0.5125, ~1280 s), D3PO (0.5192, ~1614 s), and standard BO (0.5242, ~229 s) on both accuracy and speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the EEG-to-image decoders, MindPilot (designed for closed-loop modulation without access to ground-truth EEG) beats CongCapturer on SSIM (0.35 vs. 0.33) and SwAV (0.60 vs. 0.59) but trails on PixCorr (0.09 vs. 0.15), AlexNet(5) (0.73 vs. 0.81), and Inception (0.58 vs. 0.65); it approaches the ATM-S upper bound on CLIP (0.67 vs. 0.76) and slightly exceeds it on SwAV (0.60 vs. 0.58). In the efficiency benchmark (Table A.4), MindPilot outperforms all RL-based diffusion optimizers—DDPO, DPOK, and D3PO—on EEG score at 200 queries (0.5384 vs. 0.5125/0.5101/0.5192) while being 17–22× faster in wall-clock time. It also surpasses standard Bayesian Optimization (0.5384 vs. 0.5242) and CMA-ES (0.5384 vs. 0.5227) in both accuracy and speed at the same budget. The encoder comparisons in Table A.1 (NICE, EEGNetV4, MB2C, MindEyeV2) establish ATM-S as the strongest EEG-to-image decoder, justifying its use as the theoretical ceiling; MindPilot's competitive performance under the harder closed-loop setting (no ground-truth EEG access) validates the framework's practical utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the closed-loop visual neuromodulation line (Bashivan et al. 2019; Walker et al. 2019; Ponce et al. 2019) from invasive cortical recordings to non-invasive EEG, and from low-level flicker stimuli (Luo et al. 2024b) to naturalistic images. Methodologically, it adapts the GP-based black-box guidance paradigm for diffusion models (Tan et al. 2025; Fan et al. 2023) to the EEG-feedback setting, replacing explicit reward gradients with a pseudo-model surrogate. The interactive search component draws on mental-matching retrieval (Ferecatu & Geman 2007), while the generation pipeline builds on SDXL-Lightning with IP-Adapter conditioning. Unlike one-shot EEG-to-image decoders (ATM-S, CongCapturer), MindPilot frames the problem as iterative optimization without access to ground-truth target EEG, positioning it as a general-purpose brain-stimulus co-optimization framework rather than a task-specific reconstruction model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that non-invasive EEG can serve as a viable optimization signal for naturalistic image generation in a closed loop, opening a path toward bidirectional BCIs and non-invasive neuromodulation therapies. Key open questions include the sim-to-real semantic gap (acknowledged via the moderate 'mental matching' performance), inter-subject variability (no subject-adaptive mechanism), the metamer problem (multiple images eliciting similar EEG), and the absence of real-time latency optimization. The framework's plug-and-play design across diverse proxy backbones (AlexNet through DINOv2) suggests broad applicability, but validation remains confined to a single dataset (THINGS-EEG2) and 10 human subjects, leaving generalizability to other modalities, populations, and clinical settings untested.
