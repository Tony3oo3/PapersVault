## Problem

Diffusion-based instruction-guided image editing suffers from a fundamental entanglement: the global iterative denoising process causes edits to bleed into unchanged regions and compromises instruction adherence. While visual autoregressive (VAR) models offer a compositional, scale-by-scale generation paradigm that naturally avoids this entanglement, their potential for instruction-guided editing remains largely unexplored, and a critical scale-mismatch problem arises when conditioning multi-scale target prediction on only the finest-scale source features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262892/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source RGB image (tokenized into 7 scales at 256×256 or 10 scales at 512×512 via a shared binary spherical quantizer) and a free-form text editing instruction. Only the finest-scale source feature map F_K^(src) is prepended to the target token sequence as conditioning, with a 2D-RoPE spatial offset of (64,64) to distinguish source from target tokens. The output is an edited RGB image produced by autoregressively predicting K residual maps from coarse to fine, then aggregating and decoding them. A 512×512 edit completes in 1.2 seconds (8B model) or 0.7 seconds (2B model).

## Method

VAREdit reframes instruction-guided editing as a conditional next-scale prediction problem, generating multi-scale target residuals autoregressively given source image features and text instructions, built on the pre-trained Infinity VAR backbone. The core contribution is the Scale-Aligned Reference (SAR) module, motivated by a self-attention analysis showing that the first self-attention layer attends broadly across all source scales (for global layout) while deeper layers attend locally (for detail refinement). SAR downsamples the single finest-scale source feature to each target scale's spatial dimensions and injects these scale-matched references as additional keys and values exclusively in the first self-attention layer, while all subsequent layers attend only to the finest-scale source. This resolves the scale-mismatch of naive finest-scale conditioning without incurring the quadratic sequence-length cost of full-scale conditioning. Training uses bitwise classifier loss over target residual token indices at each scale, with classifier-free guidance (η=4) and logits temperature (τ=0.5) at inference.

## Results

VAREdit-8B achieves a GPT-Balance score of 7.892 on EMU-Edit and 8.105 on PIE-Bench, surpassing the strongest open-source baselines (ICEdit at 4.785 and UltraEdit at 5.580) by 64.9% and 45.3% respectively. On CLIP-based metrics, VAREdit-8B scores 0.282 (CLIP-Out) and 0.125 (CLIP-Dir) on EMU-Edit, and 0.266 (CLIP-Whole) and 0.233 (CLIP-Edit) on PIE-Bench, leading all open-source methods. In terms of efficiency, VAREdit-8B completes a 512×512 edit in 1.2 seconds, making it 2.2× faster than UltraEdit (2.6s) and 7× faster than ICEdit (8.4s), while VAREdit-2B achieves 0.7s inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the seven basic open-source baselines, VAREdit-8B dominates on GPT-Balance across both benchmarks, outperforming ICEdit (4.785) by 64.9% on EMU-Edit and UltraEdit (5.580) by 45.3% on PIE-Bench. VAREdit-8B also beats the two other frontier open-source methods, Step1X-Edit (7.081/7.351) and FLUX.1 Kontext (7.210/6.998), on GPT-Balance for both benchmarks. However, VAREdit-8B trails Qwen-Image-Edit (8.550/8.567) and GPT-4o-Image (9.142/8.902) on GPT-Balance, with the gap attributed to larger training data and model scale. On CLIP-based metrics, VAREdit-8B achieves the highest scores among all open-source methods on both benchmarks, though FLUX.1 Kontext and Qwen-Image-Edit marginally lead on CLIP-Out for EMU-Edit. In a focused analysis isolating only successful edits (GPT-Suc ≥ 9), VAREdit achieves the highest GPT-Overedit score among all basic approaches, confirming that its preservation advantage is not merely due to conservative under-editing. VAREdit-8B also achieves the highest human preference rate (36.0%) in a blind user study over Step1X-Edit (30.1%) and FLUX.1 Kontext (33.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

VAREdit extends the visual autoregressive (VAR) next-scale prediction paradigm (Tian et al., NeurIPS 2024) from image synthesis to instruction-guided image editing, a task previously dominated by iterative diffusion models. It is initialized from the Infinity model (Han et al., CVPR 2025) and its bitwise multi-scale residual quantizer, adapting the coarse-to-fine generation pipeline to a conditional editing setting. The key methodological departure from prior AR editing approaches (e.g., EditAR) is the shift from vanilla next-token prediction to next-scale prediction, combined with the novel Scale-Aligned Reference (SAR) module that injects scale-matched source conditioning only into the first self-attention layer to resolve a scale-mismatch problem identified through attention analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VAREdit demonstrates that the VAR paradigm can match or exceed diffusion-based editing quality while being 2.2–7× faster, making real-time instruction-guided editing practically viable. The SAR module's insight—that scale-mismatch sensitivity is concentrated in the first self-attention layer—provides a targeted, low-cost architectural fix that could inform future multi-scale conditional generation designs. Limitations remain in complex compositional edits, text rendering, and motion changes (particularly at 2B scale), and the paper acknowledges that higher resolutions and more diverse training data are needed to close these gaps. An open question is whether the SAR principle generalizes beyond the specific VAR/Infinity architecture to other hierarchical or multi-scale generative frameworks.
