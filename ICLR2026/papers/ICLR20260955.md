## Problem

Modern optimizers like Adam and Muon maintain full-rank first- and second-order momentum matrices, tripling memory usage and constraining LLM scalability. Existing low-rank optimizer methods (e.g., GaLore) rely on periodic SVD-based subspace updates, which introduce error accumulation and optimization discontinuities between update intervals. The paper asks whether optimizer states can be compressed into a low-rank subspace with continuous, per-step adaptation to avoid these instabilities.

## Input / Output

At each training step, the method consumes the current gradient g_t (a p×q matrix matching the parameter shape) and the previous low-rank factor matrices m_B (p×r), m_A (r×q), v_B, v_A. It produces updated low-rank factors and an effective full-rank momentum computed as β·(m_B·m_A) + (1−β)·g_t, which feeds into the standard Adam or Muon parameter update. The second-order momentum is reconstructed via a Hadamard-square re-parameterization (v_B·v_A)^∘2 to guarantee element-wise positivity required for the √v term in Adam's adaptive learning rate.

## Method

The core insight is that the EMA momentum update m ← β·m + (1−β)·g is mathematically equivalent to one step of gradient descent on the online regression objective ½‖m−g‖²_F, with learning rate (1−β). LoRA-Pre exploits this by factorizing the momentum as m = m_B·m_A with r ≪ min(p,q), reducing memory from p×q to (p+q)×r, and derives closed-form Newton's method update rules for m_B and m_A that preserve the EMA structure without backpropagation. A coupling constraint (1−γ₁)² = β₁ analytically links the low-rank update rate to the standard Adam decay coefficient, eliminating an extra hyperparameter. For the second-order moment, the re-parameterization v = (v_B·v_A)^∘2 with regression target |g| ensures positivity while maintaining low-rank structure. The framework generalizes to any momentum-based optimizer, with a separate derivation for Muon that accounts for its non-standard momentum form m' = μ·m + g.

## Results

On C4 pre-training of Llama models (60M–1B), LoRA-Pre Adam achieves validation perplexities of 32.57, 23.78, 16.36, and 13.53, outperforming the best low-rank baseline Fira by 0.81, 2.45, and 1.6 points at 130M, 350M, and 1B scales respectively. LoRA-Pre Muon achieves the best low-rank perplexity at 60M (30.76) and 130M (23.05). In fine-tuning on MetaMath100k, LoRA-Pre Adam improves average accuracy by 3.14 points over LoRA on Llama-3.1-8B (47.05 vs 43.91) and by 6.17 points on Llama-2-7B (32.15 vs 25.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRA-Pre Adam dominates all low-rank pre-training baselines: it beats Fira (the strongest competitor) by 0.81–2.45 perplexity points on 130M–1B models, and outperforms GaLore, LORO, SLTrain, LoRA, ReLoRA, and Low-Rank by even larger margins across all four model sizes. On the 60M model, Fira edges out LoRA-Pre Adam (31.19 vs 32.57), but LoRA-Pre Muon achieves the best low-rank result there (30.76). In fine-tuning, LoRA-Pre Adam surpasses LoRA by 3.14 points (Llama-3.1-8B) and 6.17 points (Llama-2-7B) in average accuracy, and outperforms rsLoRA, DoRA, and GaLore by 2.6–5.2 points. LoRA-Pre Muon similarly beats GaLore Muon and LoRA Muon by 1.96–6.03 points in average accuracy. Notably, projection-based methods (GaLore, Fira) underperform even Muon without momentum when adapted to the Muon optimizer, while LoRA-Pre Muon maintains a clear advantage, confirming the benefit of continuous subspace adaptation over periodic SVD-based updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LoRA's low-rank factorization (m = m_B · m_A) but repurposes it from model weight adaptation to optimizer state compression, a shift from the PEFT literature to the efficient-optimizer literature. It departs from projection-based low-rank optimizers (GaLore, Fira, Flora) that periodically update subspaces via SVD or random projections, instead deriving continuous per-step updates through a novel equivalence: EMA momentum updates are mathematically identical to online gradient descent on a linear regression objective. The Newton's-method derivation of closed-form update rules (Theorem 3.1) connects to the broader online least-squares literature, while the extension to Muon's orthogonalized updates represents a departure from prior work that had only addressed Adam-family optimizers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Memory overhead from optimizer states is a primary bottleneck for scaling LLM training, and LoRA-Pre's 8× rank efficiency (matching full-rank baselines at 1/8 the rank) offers a practical path to reducing this cost without the error accumulation inherent in periodic-subspace methods. However, pre-training experiments are limited to models up to 1B parameters, leaving open whether the low-rank momentum assumption and rank efficiency hold at the 7B–70B scale where memory pressure is most acute. The finding that projection-based methods (GaLore, Fira) underperform even momentum-free Muon when ported to non-Adam optimizers raises questions about the generalizability of the entire projected-gradient-descent paradigm. Future work should validate at larger scales, explore adaptive rank allocation across layers, and investigate whether the EMA-regression equivalence enables further algorithmic innovations beyond simple factorization.
