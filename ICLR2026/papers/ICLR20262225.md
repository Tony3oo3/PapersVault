## Problem

Traditional video compositing requires intensive collaboration among animators, videographers, and VFX artists, resulting in lengthy production cycles. Existing automated approaches either rely on text/image conditions that cannot precisely control pixel-level element appearance and motion, or paste foreground frame-by-frame without adaptively specifying position, size, or trajectory. This paper introduces generative video compositing as a new task that uses generative models to automatically inject dynamic foreground footage into a background video under user-specified trajectory and scale control.

## Input / Output

The method takes a background video to be edited, a foreground video (whose dynamic element is centered per frame with global position removed), a 2D trajectory curve on the first frame (or a single point auto-tracked via optical flow), and a rescale factor. These are rasterized into a per-frame binary mask video and a masked background video, with the mask further inflated via a 2D Gaussian kernel to create a buffer band for interaction. The output is a 480×720, 49-frame composited video decoded from latent space, generated in ~65s on 34GB VRAM.

## Method

GenCompositor is a 6B DiT model (built on CogVideoX-I2V-5B's VAE and text encoder) trained from scratch with three key architectural components. A lightweight Background Preservation Branch (two DiT blocks) encodes the masked video and mask video, then injects features into the mainstream via masked token addition (z_t + (1−M)⊙z_BPBranch) to preserve non-edited regions. A DiT Fusion Block concatenates foreground tokens with noisy latent tokens token-wise (rather than channel-wise, since layouts are unaligned) and fuses them through full self-attention, enabling faithful inheritance of foreground identity and motion. Extended Rotary Position Embedding (ERoPE) assigns stream-specific constant offsets Δ_s to foreground tokens on top of standard RoPE, preventing spatial-position collisions between layout-unaligned foreground and background tokens without adding parameters. Training employs gamma-correction luminance augmentation (γ ∈ [0.4, 1.9]) on foreground video and mask inflation to improve generalization and enable realistic object-environment interaction.

## Results

On the trajectory-controlled video generation benchmark (VBench), GenCompositor achieves the best scores across all six metrics: Subject Consistency 89.75%, Background Consistency 93.43%, Motion Smoothness 98.69%, Aesthetic Quality 52.00%, FVD 535.71, and KVD 45.91. On the video harmonization task (HYouTube), it attains PSNR 42.00, SSIM 0.9487, CLIP 0.9713, and LPIPS 0.0385, outperforming both Harmonizer and VTT on every metric. The FVD improvement is particularly striking, with GenCompositor scoring 535.71 versus 942.52 (VACE), 1342.56 (Revideo), and 1402.82 (Tora).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenCompositor outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against Tora (CVPR 2025), it improves FVD by 2.6× (535.71 vs. 1402.82) and KVD by 2.1× (45.91 vs. 94.94), while also achieving higher Subject (89.75% vs. 88.44%) and Aesthetic (52.00% vs. 49.33%) scores; Tora additionally requires textual prompts and cannot maintain ID consistency of the injected element. Against Revideo (NeurIPS 2024), GenCompositor leads on all six metrics, with the largest gap in FVD (535.71 vs. 1342.56) and Aesthetic (52.00% vs. 48.56%); Revideo suffers from temporal inconsistency where elements may disappear in predicted frames. Against VACE (ICCV 2025), GenCompositor achieves a 1.76× lower FVD (535.71 vs. 942.52) and a 2.6× lower KVD (45.91 vs. 120.92), while VACE tends to mistake the target object and cannot predict faithful dynamics. On the harmonization sub-task, GenCompositor beats Harmonizer (ECCV 2022) by +2.25 dB PSNR and +0.0103 SSIM, and VTT (CVPR 2024) by +1.98 dB PSNR and +0.0190 SSIM, while also producing fewer jagged edge artifacts. Notably, GenCompositor requires neither textual prompts nor pre-edited first-frame images, unlike Tora, Revideo, and VACE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CogVideoX DiT architecture (inheriting its VAE and text encoder) and extends the RoPE positional embedding mechanism into ERoPE to handle layout-unaligned video conditions. It departs from video harmonization methods (which only adjust color of pasted elements) and trajectory-controlled generation methods (which rely on text or image conditions) by introducing video footage itself as the conditioning signal, requiring a full self-attention DiT fusion block rather than the cross-attention injection common in prior controllable generation work. The paper positions generative video compositing as a distinct task from both harmonization and controllable generation, arguing that neither prior formulation supports pixel-level video condition injection with user-specified trajectories and scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method and dataset for generative video compositing, a task that bridges raw footage and final video production in a way that prior harmonization or text/image-conditioned generation cannot. ERoPE is presented as a general-purpose tool for any layout-unaligned video editing task, and the VideoComp dataset (61K pairs) provides a foundation for future work. However, the method is limited to 480×720/49-frame videos with ~65s inference on a single GPU, struggles with extreme lighting and complex occlusion, and lacks 3D priors for novel-view foreground prediction — all acknowledged as open problems for the field.
