## Problem

Extending the context window of LLMs beyond their native length typically requires expensive continual pre-training on long-context corpora or architectural modifications that sacrifice compatibility with efficient attention kernels like FlashAttention. Existing encoder-decoder approaches (e.g., CEPE) introduce heterogeneous models that demand additional pretraining and warmup stages to align hidden spaces, while streaming methods incur quadratic time growth or OOM at extreme lengths. The paper asks whether a single off-the-shelf short-context LLM can be adapted to handle 128K+ token inputs with minimal training cost and superior inference efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261789/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long text context X_C (arbitrarily long, up to 128K+ tokens) and a shorter running text X_D (the query, instruction, or continuation prompt) as input. X_C is partitioned into non-overlapping chunks of fixed size (512–1024 tokens) that fit within the lower model's window, while X_D is fed directly to the upper model. The output is autoregressively generated text, with the language modeling loss computed only on the response tokens for instruction-following data or on all X_D tokens for pretraining.

## Method

SHAREDLLM stacks two models derived from the same LLM checkpoint: a lower model (first M shallow layers) that compresses each context chunk into multi-grained key-value states, and an upper model (full layers) that decodes the output. The lower model organizes compressed representations in a binary context tree, where a query-aware policy selectively expands only semantically relevant nodes (via cosine similarity between node embeddings and the query), preserving fine-grained detail for relevant portions and coarse summaries for the rest. KV states are uniformly downsampled with level-dependent compression ratios (α_w = 2α_{w+1}), creating a coarse-to-fine hierarchy. The upper model integrates this information through cross-attention layers inserted at the bottom M layers, using chunk-level RoPE positional indices to preserve global ordering. Because both models share the same hidden space (self-injection), fine-tuning proceeds directly from off-the-shelf checkpoints without warmup, and the quadratic self-attention complexity is reduced to O(n·(T/n)² + T_D·|S'|) where |S'| is the compressed KV length.

## Results

SHAREDLLM, trained on only 8K-token sequences, successfully extrapolates to 128K+ tokens without perplexity explosion, achieving the best or near-best perplexity across Arxiv (2.46 at 32K), PG19 (5.96 at 128K), and ProofPile (2.38 at 32K) in the continual pretraining setting. On the SFT setting, SHAREDLLM (LLaMA-2) achieves 30.93 on LongBench MD-QA, 59.93 on Code, 13.82 on InfiniBench Math.F, and 82.79 on Ret.N, outperforming or matching all baselines across the five LongBench categories and three InfiniBench tasks. Inference efficiency is substantially improved: 2× speedup over streaming architectures (Activation Beacon) and 3× over encoder-decoder architectures (CEPE), with lower peak GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the continual pretraining setting (Table 1), SHAREDLLM outperforms CEPE on 11 of 12 perplexity metrics (e.g., Arxiv-32K: 2.46 vs 2.51; PG19-128K: 5.96 vs 6.10), and dominates YaRN and PI across all lengths where they do not OOM. In the SFT setting (Table 2), SHAREDLLM achieves the lowest perplexity on all three corpora (PG19, ProofPile, CodeParrot) at all tested lengths, beating Activation Beacon by 0.2–0.5 ppl on PG19 and 0.1–0.2 on ProofPile. On LongBench/InfiniBench (Table 3), SHAREDLLM wins on 6 of 8 LLaMA-2 tasks, losing only narrowly to LongAlpaca-16K on Summarization (25.76 vs 27.80) and Few-Shot (63.50 vs 63.70); it decisively outperforms SnapKV and OmniKV on retrieval-heavy tasks (Ret.N: 82.79 vs 2.31 and 3.66). Against Activation Beacon, SHAREDLLM gains +2.49 on MD-QA and +1.68 on Math.F while also being 2× faster at inference. The key differentiator is that SHAREDLLM requires no extra pretraining or warmup stage (unlike CEPE) and no specialized attention pattern incompatible with FlashAttention (unlike Activation Beacon and StreamingLLM), enabling direct fine-tuning from off-the-shelf checkpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHAREDLLM builds directly on the parallel context encoding paradigm of CEPE (Yen et al., 2024), which pairs a separate encoder (RoBERTa) with a decoder via cross-attention. The key architectural departure is the 'self-injection' mechanism: both the compressor (lower model) and decoder (upper model) are initialized from the same off-the-shelf LLM's layers, eliminating the heterogeneous hidden-space alignment and warmup stages that CEPE requires. The method also draws on the Λ-shaped attention pattern observed in streaming LLMs (Xiao et al., 2024b; Han et al., 2024) to motivate its right-branch selection policy for language modeling, and on KV-cache compression ideas (SnapKV, OmniKV) for its downsampling strategy, but introduces a novel tree-structured, query-aware retrieval mechanism that organizes compressed context hierarchically rather than flatly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency-performance tradeoff: SHAREDLLM achieves 2× speedup over streaming methods and 3× over encoder-decoder architectures while matching or exceeding their accuracy, all from a model trained on only 8K tokens. The self-injection design lowers the barrier to long-context adaptation by removing the need for costly post-pretraining or feature-alignment stages, making it accessible to practitioners working with standard checkpoints. However, the approach still requires supervised training (not training-free), the query-aware retrieval adds ~15% overhead, and performance on some LongBench subtasks (e.g., few-shot learning with LLaMA-3) actually degrades relative to the base model. Open questions include whether the tree depth and compression ratio hyperparameters scale gracefully to 1M+ token contexts, and whether the paradigm extends to multimodal settings as the authors speculate.
