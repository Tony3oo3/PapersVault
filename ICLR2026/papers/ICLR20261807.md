## Problem

Existing diffusion and flow matching models for probabilistic time series forecasting ignore informative priors such as conditional means and covariances, and prior-informed methods (CARD, TMDM, NsDiff) lack theoretical guarantees for when priors help, neglect inter-variable correlations by using only diagonal variance, and introduce unnecessarily complex reverse processes. The paper asks: under what conditions does replacing the standard Gaussian terminal distribution with a conditionally parameterized one improve generation quality, and how can the mean and covariance be jointly estimated to satisfy those conditions?

## Input / Output

The method takes a historical multivariate time series C ∈ R^{d×T_h} (e.g., 168 hourly steps for ETT/Weather/Solar, 52 weekly steps for ILI) and produces K=100 probabilistic future samples X_0 ∈ R^{d×T_f} (e.g., 192 or 36 steps). Internally, the JMCE module outputs a non-autoregressive conditional mean estimate μ̂_{X|C} and per-timestep Cholesky factors L̂_{t|C} whose products give sliding-window covariance matrices (window size 95, or 15 for ILI). The CW-Diff variant requires inverting the whitening transform at sampling time, while CW-Flow avoids this by directly sampling from the conditionally parameterized Gaussian as the ODE starting point.

## Method

The core mechanism is conditional whitening: before running diffusion or flow matching, the data is transformed as X_0^CW = Σ̂^{-0.5} ∘ (X_0 − μ̂), which removes non-stationary trends, addresses heteroscedasticity, and decorrelates features, so the generative model operates in a near-stationary space. Theorem 1 establishes that this replacement of the terminal distribution N(0,I) with N(μ̂,Σ̂) reduces the KL divergence (and hence total variation distance to the true distribution) whenever the estimation errors in mean and covariance are small relative to the signal magnitude and the smallest estimated eigenvalue is bounded away from zero. The Joint Mean-Covariance Estimator (JMCE) is a Non-stationary Transformer trained with a loss combining L2 mean error, nuclear-norm (SVD) covariance error, Frobenius-norm covariance error, and a ReLU-based eigenvalue penalty that enforces the minimum eigenvalue to exceed a threshold λ_min. CW-Diff applies standard DDPM score matching on the whitened space and inverts the transform at the end; CW-Flow instead uses a rectified-flow ODE whose terminal distribution is directly N(μ̂,Σ̂), eliminating the need for inverse matrix computation. The framework is plug-and-play: any existing diffusion or flow matching backbone (TimeDiff, SSSD, Diffusion-TS, TMDM, NsDiff, FlowTS) can be upgraded by replacing its input with the CW-transformed version.

## Results

On ETTh1, CW-Gen achieves a 100% win rate on ProbCorr and Conditional FID across all six generative models, and an 83.3% win rate on CRPS and QICE. The most dramatic improvement is on Conditional FID, where Diffusion-TS drops from 81.56 to 7.69 and SSSD from 40.89 to 9.27. Across all five datasets, CW-Gen's overall win rate ranges from 79.17% (Solar Energy) to 91.67% (ETTh1, ETTh2, Weather). CW-Gen also improves point-forecast metrics, reducing ProbMSE in 76.67% and ProbMAE in 80.00% of model-dataset combinations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CW-Gen is integrated with six state-of-the-art generative models and compared against their raw (non-whitened) counterparts. Against prior-free models (TimeDiff, SSSD, Diffusion-TS, FlowTS), CW-Gen consistently wins on all four probabilistic metrics (CRPS, QICE, ProbCorr, Conditional FID) on ETTh1, with the largest gains on Conditional FID (e.g., Diffusion-TS: 81.56→7.69; SSSD: 40.89→9.27). Against prior-informed models, the picture is more nuanced: CW-TMDM improves CRPS (0.472→0.440) and FID (9.93→3.83) but slightly worsens QICE (3.36→4.56); CW-NsDiff improves QICE (1.79→1.25) and FID (35.26→8.82) but slightly worsens CRPS (0.407→0.431). The paper attributes these minor losses to the fact that TMDM and NsDiff already incorporate partial priors (mean regressor, diagonal variance), so the full covariance-based whitening provides diminishing or occasionally conflicting benefits on individual metrics. Across all five datasets and six models, CW-Gen's aggregate win rate is 79–92%, with ProbCorr and Conditional FID showing the most consistent improvements (100% win rate on ETTh1), confirming that full covariance estimation captures inter-variable dependencies that diagonal-variance-only methods (NsDiff) or mean-only methods (TMDM) miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of prior-informed diffusion models for time series forecasting initiated by CARD (Han et al., 2022), which first incorporated a conditional mean regressor into DDPM, and subsequently refined by TMDM (Li et al., 2024) and NsDiff (Ye et al., 2025), which added nonlinear mean regression and diagonal variance regression respectively. The key departure is a shift from ad-hoc integration of separate mean and variance regressors into the forward/reverse SDE to a principled conditional whitening transformation: a full-rank, invertible linear map that simultaneously removes non-stationary trends, addresses heteroscedasticity, and decorrelates variables, with the theoretical justification (Theorem 1) showing when replacing the standard Gaussian terminal distribution with N(μ̂, Σ̂) provably reduces the KLD to the true conditional distribution. The authors further show that TMDM and NsDiff are special cases of their unified CW-Gen framework, and extend the approach to flow matching (CW-Flow) to avoid the O(d³T_f) eigen-decomposition cost of CW-Diff.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical sufficient condition (Theorem 1) under which modifying the terminal distribution of a diffusion model improves sample quality, giving practitioners a principled criterion for when and how to incorporate priors rather than relying on empirical trial. The conditional whitening formulation is a clean, plug-and-play transformation that can be applied to any existing diffusion or flow-matching architecture, and the joint mean-covariance estimator with eigenvalue regularization addresses a practical gap: prior methods either ignored inter-variable correlations (NsDiff) or used overly complex reverse processes. Open questions include whether the sliding-window covariance approximation remains adequate for highly non-stationary or long-horizon settings, how the method scales to very high-dimensional problems (the Solar Energy dataset with d=137 already shows some degradation), and whether the theoretical conditions can be tightened to cover the regimes where the prior may actually hurt performance.
