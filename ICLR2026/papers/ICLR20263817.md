## Problem

Constant bit-size Transformers are known to be Turing complete, but the existing construction of Li & Wang (2025) requires Ω(s(n)) chain-of-thought steps to simulate a single Turing machine step, where s(n) is the space bound. This per-step slowdown makes the total CoT length O(t(n)·s(n)), which is prohibitively long for practical reasoning. The paper asks whether this slowdown can be reduced while preserving the optimal O(s(n)) context window and constant bit-size.

## Input / Output

The Transformer takes a binary string x of length n as input (the TM's input tape) and produces the TM's output string after O(t(n)·s(n)^c) CoT steps, where c > 0 is arbitrarily small. The context window is O(s(n)) tokens, each encoding a K-tuple of queue symbols plus the TM state and a parity bit. The attention pattern is sparse: each query attends only to tokens at geometrically spaced offsets (1, 2, 4, 8, …), so each CoT token is generated in O(1) time rather than O(s(n)) as in full attention.

## Method

The proof proceeds in two steps. Step 1 (Theorem 2) simulates a k-tape TM by a synchronous 6kk′-queue TM running in O(t(n)·s(n)^{1/k′}) time and O(k·s(n)) space, where each tape is represented as two stacks and each stack is simulated by k′ levels of queues with geometrically increasing sizes; the key technical novelty is handling the stricter synchronous model (every queue must pop and push each step) via buffer queues and splitting content queues into halves to maintain head alignment. Step 2 (Theorem 3) generalizes the single-queue Transformer simulation of Li & Wang (2025) to K synchronous queues: K attention heads distributed across L layers (with H·L = K) retrieve the front element of each queue using a nonstandard relative positional encoding, and a final feed-forward network implements the TM transition function. The resulting attention pattern has fixed geometric offsets, meaning each token is produced in O(1) time, yielding the O(s(n)^c) per-step overhead where c = 6k/K can be made arbitrarily small by increasing the head-layer product.

## Results

The paper proves that any (t(n), s(n))-bounded k-tape TM can be simulated by a constant bit-size Transformer with an optimal O(s(n)) context window and only O(s(n)^c) CoT steps per simulated TM step, where c > 0 can be made arbitrarily small by increasing the head-layer product K (specifically c = 6k/K). This improves the per-step slowdown from O(s(n)) in the prior constant-bit-size construction of Li & Wang (2025) to O(s(n)^c), yielding a total CoT length of O(t(n)·s(n)^c). The construction also exhibits a sparse geometric-offset attention pattern where each query attends to tokens at offsets 1, 2, 4, 8, …, enabling O(1) per-token generation time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct comparison is with Li & Wang (2025), the prior constant-bit-size construction: this work matches its O(1) precision, O(1) embedding dimension, and optimal O(s(n)) context window, but strictly improves the total CoT from O(t(n)·s(n)) to O(t(n)·s(n)^c) with c arbitrarily small. Against Yang et al. (2025a), which achieves O(t(n)) total CoT with an O(s(n)) window, this work trades a slightly larger CoT (O(t(n)·s(n)^c) vs. O(t(n))) for the stronger O(1) precision guarantee (vs. O(log t(n))) and O(1) embedding dimension, and does so without the recursive CoT-erasure mechanism Yang et al. require. Compared to earlier constructions (Pérez et al. 2021, Bhattamishra et al. 2020, Merrill & Sabharwal 2024, Bavandpour et al. 2025), this work achieves the same O(t(n))-order total CoT in the special case c→0 while using constant precision and a smaller O(s(n)) window instead of O(n+t(n)). Against Li et al. (2024) and Qiu et al. (2024), this work reduces both the window from O(t(n) log t(n)) to O(s(n)) and the embedding dimension from O(log t(n)) to O(1), at the cost of a polynomial (but sublinear) per-step overhead. The key trade-off is that the exponent c = 6k/K requires the head-layer product K to be sufficiently large to make the slowdown negligible, whereas Yang et al.'s O(t(n)) bound holds with a fixed constant number of heads and layers but at the cost of O(log t(n)) precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Li & Wang (2025), who proved constant bit-size Transformers are Turing complete via a single-queue (Post) machine bridge but incurred an Ω(s(n)) per-step CoT overhead. The key departure is a two-step simulation: first, a new and more efficient reduction from multi-tape TMs to *synchronous* multi-queue TMs (improving on Hühne 1993 in space, time, and model restrictiveness), and second, a generalization of the Li & Wang Transformer construction to the multi-queue setting. The resulting geometric-offset attention pattern (queries attend to tokens 1, 2, 4, 8, … steps back) connects theoretically to practical sparse-attention designs such as LogSparse and PowerAttention, but no prior work had shown such a pattern suffices for universal computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result narrows the per-step simulation gap from s(n) to s(n)^c with c arbitrarily small, suggesting that the quadratic cost of full attention is not a fundamental expressiveness bottleneck for long-context reasoning. The open questions are significant: whether the per-step overhead can reach O(1), whether positional encodings can adapt when the space bound is unknown, and whether standard training protocols can actually discover geometric-offset attention patterns. The paper is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, so the practical relevance of the architectural insight remains to be tested.
