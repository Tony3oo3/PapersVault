## Problem

Existing vision-language models lack both 3D representations and domain-specific archaeological knowledge for ancient Greek pottery, a culturally critical domain with over 2,000 years of history. No 3D VQA dataset or specialized VLM exists for this domain, leaving a gap between general-purpose multimodal capabilities and the spatial, morphological, and decorative understanding required for digital heritage preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263038/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16-frame 360-degree rotation videos (512×512, 2fps) rendered from GLB-format 3D vase models via Blender 3.6, paired with standardized text questions of the form 'What is the [attribute] of the vase?' across six archaeological dimensions. Outputs are free-form text answers for VQA and museum-style descriptive captions (e.g., 'Athenian black-figure lekythos, c. 525-475 BCE, depicting Herakles and the boar; Marathon, Attica') that consolidate structured metadata into coherent prose without introducing new archaeological content.

## Method

The pipeline first filters 30,000+ 2D vase images through a ResNet-50 quality classifier and dual CLIP-based semantic filtering (fragment removal + optimal view selection) to yield 3,880 images, then generates 664 high-fidelity 3D models via TripoSG (selected over Hunyuan3D based on VaseEval validation for superior geometric accuracy and CLIP text similarity). VaseVLM is built on Qwen2.5-VL (3B/7B) with a two-stage training strategy: LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning on rotation videos and GPT-4o-cleaned archaeological captions, followed by GRPO reinforcement learning using a novel RLVR framework. The RLVR reward decomposes each generated caption into six weighted semantic dimensions (Fabric 0.20, Technique 0.20, Shape 0.15, Dating 0.15, Decoration 0.20, Attribution 0.10), computing per-dimension cosine similarity against ground truth with a 0.7 threshold, and applies quality-control penalties for excessive length, repetition, and irrelevant content, with the final reward constrained to [0,1].

## Results

On the VaseVQA-3D test set (100 models), VaseVLM-7B-RL achieves R@1 of 3.52%, R@5 of 11.12%, R@10 of 21.24%, CLIP score of 0.792, FID of 0.328, and lexical similarity of 0.276. The headline improvements are a 12.8% relative gain in R@1 over the strongest baseline (Claude-4-sonnet, 3.12%) and a 6.6% gain in lexical similarity over the best baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-3B, 0.259). In human evaluation by 10 archaeological experts, VaseVLM-7B-RL scores 4.57/5, outperforming the best baseline (DiffuRank, 4.07/5) by 12.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VaseVLM-7B-RL achieves the best R@1 (3.52%) among all 17 evaluated models, surpassing the strongest baselines Claude-4-sonnet, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and GPT-4.1 (all at 3.12%) by 12.8% relative improvement. On lexical similarity, VaseVLM-7B-RL (0.276) outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-3B (0.259) and DiffuRank (0.274), the two closest competitors, by 6.6% and 0.7% respectively. However, VaseVLM-7B-RL trails Gemini-2.5-flash on retrieval recall (R@10: 21.24% vs. 28.57%; R@5: 11.12% vs. 17.58%) and DiffuRank on CLIP score (0.792 vs. 0.798), though it matches Gemini-2.5-flash on FID (0.328 vs. 0.325). Among 3D-specialized models (DiffuRank, Cap3D, LLaVA-3D), VaseVLM-7B-RL leads on R@1, R@5, R@10, and lexical similarity, but the paper notes these operate under different input modalities (direct GLB vs. 2D rotation video) and are not directly comparable. In human evaluation, VaseVLM-7B-RL (4.57/5) beats all baselines, with the closest competitor DiffuRank at 4.07/5, and the RL-trained variants consistently outperform their SFT counterparts by ~0.4 points on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D VaseVQA benchmark (Ge et al., 2025) into the 3D domain by generating 3D vase models via TripoSG and formulating VQA over 360-degree rotation videos, while also extending general 3D VQA work (Cap3D, DiffuRank, LLaVA-3D) from generic objects to culturally specific artifacts with structured archaeological semantics. The training pipeline builds on Qwen2.5-VL as the base VLM, LoRA-based SFT for domain adaptation, and GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) with an RLVR reward framework (Lambert et al., 2024) that decomposes captions into six weighted archaeological dimensions. A key departure from prior 3D VQA is the use of domain-specific verifiable rewards rather than generic text-matching, and the reliance on 2D-to-3D generation (TripoSG) rather than directly ingesting native 3D geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first 3D VQA benchmark for cultural heritage artifacts, providing a concrete evaluation target for AI systems in digital heritage preservation and demonstrating that domain-adaptive RLVR training can improve archaeological accuracy over general-purpose VLMs. However, the 17.1% 3D generation success rate (2.2% overall data retention from 30K images) and the very low absolute R@1 (3.52%) highlight that the field is still far from reliable automated artifact analysis. The demonstrated generalizability to Chinese bronze and Greek sculpture suggests the pipeline could become a template for other artifact domains, but scaling to larger, more diverse collections remains an open challenge.
