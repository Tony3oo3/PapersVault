## Problem

Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA) compresses the KV cache into a single latent head, but this single latent head cannot be partitioned across devices under Tensor Parallelism (TP), forcing every GPU to redundantly load the full KV cache at each decoding step. This sharding bottleneck negates the memory-traffic savings that TP is supposed to provide, making long-context distributed decoding inefficient. The paper asks whether the latent state can be decomposed into independently computable branches that are natively shardable.

## Input / Output

During prefill, the method consumes token hidden states H ∈ R^{n×d} and produces per-head attention outputs O ∈ R^{n×h×d_h} via four independent branch-wise attention computations that are summed and rescaled by 1/2 (MLRA-4). During decoding, each device loads only its 1/4 slice of the compressed KV latent cache C^KV ∈ R^{n×d_c} (reducing per-device loading from 4.5d_h in MLA to 1.5d_h under 4-way TP) plus the shared partial RoPE key K^RoPE ∈ R^{n×d_h^R}. The absorbed query-side projection reduces the per-head attention logit space to 1.5d_h, compared to 4.5d_h in MLA.

## Method

The key insight is that MLA's NoPE key and value for head i can be written as a sum of four block-wise products C_{:,(b)}^{KV} W_{(b),(i)}^{UK/UV} (Eq. 2), where the latent state is partitioned into four d_h-sized channel blocks. MLRA-4 moves this summation outside the attention operator, computing four independent softmax attention branches—each using only one latent block—and summing the resulting outputs (Eq. 5), which makes each branch independently shardable across TP devices. Variance calibration rescales the query and KV latent states by √(d/d_c') and √(4d/d_c) respectively to align NoPE and RoPE component variances, and the summed attention output is rescaled by 1/2 to preserve overall variance. The three-step decoding algorithm (query-side weight absorption, MQA-style attention on the latent cache, output up-projection) is applied per branch, enabling a FlashAttention-3-based kernel that achieves 2.8× decoding speedup over MLA at 4-way TP.

## Results

At the 2.9B-parameter scale, MLRA-4 achieves the best average validation perplexity of 13.672 across seven datasets, outperforming MLA (13.727), GLA-2 (13.779), and GQA (14.139). On zero-shot common-sense reasoning (7 benchmarks), MLRA-4 attains the highest average accuracy of 58.84%, edging out MLA (58.75%) and GQA (57.89%). In long-context decoding (128K–2M tokens, batch=1, H100), MLRA-4 with TP=4 delivers a steady ~2.8× speedup over MLA and 1.05–1.26× over GQA, while also achieving the highest end-to-end throughput at batch=128 across 1K–16K sequence lengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLRA-4 outperforms all seven baselines on both perplexity and zero-shot reasoning. Against the closest competitor MLA, MLRA-4 improves average perplexity from 13.727 to 13.672 and average reasoning accuracy from 58.75% to 58.84%, while also delivering a 2.8× decoding speedup by enabling 4-way TP (MLA's single latent head cannot be sharded, forcing redundant KV cache loading at 4.5d_h per device regardless of TP degree). Versus GQA, MLRA-4 reduces perplexity by 0.467 (14.139→13.672) and gains 0.95 points in reasoning accuracy (57.89%→58.84%), with 1.05–1.26× faster decoding. Against GLA-2 (the prior best TP-friendly latent attention), MLRA-4 improves perplexity by 0.107 (13.779→13.672) and accuracy by 0.54 points (58.30%→58.84%), and reduces per-device KV cache loading from 2.5d_h to 1.5d_h under 4-way TP. MFA, TPA, and MQA are all clearly outperformed on both metrics (perplexity gaps of 0.3–0.6; accuracy gaps of 0.3–1.3 points). Notably, MLRA-4 also wins on every individual dataset in Table 3, indicating the gains are not driven by a single outlier benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

MLRA directly extends Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA, DeepSeek-V2) by decomposing its single, non-partitionable latent KV head into multiple independent latent blocks, each up-projected to form NoPE keys and values. The two-branch variant (MLRA-2) follows the grouping logic of GLA-2 (Zadouri et al., 2025), while the four-branch variant (MLRA-4) generalizes this to full 4-way tensor parallelism. The variance-calibration rescaling of latent states is adopted from LongCat-Flash (2025), which identified a RoPE/NoPE variance mismatch in MLA. The key conceptual departure from MLA is moving the block summation outside the softmax operator, converting a single attention computation into a sum of independent branch-wise attentions that can be sharded across devices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MLRA addresses a critical deployment bottleneck: MLA's single latent head forces every GPU in a tensor-parallel group to redundantly load the full KV cache, negating the memory-traffic savings that TP is supposed to provide. The 2.8× decoding speedup over MLA at 4-way TP, combined with matching or slightly better perplexity, makes MLA-style compression practically viable for multi-GPU serving. Limitations include evaluation only at 2.9B parameters (no validation at 70B+ scale where TP is most critical), and the sum-of-softmaxes formulation is an approximation whose quality gap relative to exact MLA attention has not been formally bounded. Open questions include whether the multi-branch design's approximation error grows with model scale or context length, and whether higher branching factors (beyond 4) yield diminishing returns.
