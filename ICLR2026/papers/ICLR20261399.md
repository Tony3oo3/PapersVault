## Problem

Diffusion-based video restoration achieves high visual quality but requires tens of sampling steps, making it prohibitively slow for real-world use, especially at high resolutions. While one-step image restoration has been explored via distillation or adversarial training, extending these approaches to video restoration at high resolutions (e.g., 1080p) remains challenging: distillation is bounded by teacher quality, and large-scale adversarial training for VR GANs suffers from instability and boundary artifacts from fixed-size window attention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261399/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-quality video (typically ~720p, up to ~100 frames) and a text prompt as conditioning, and produces a high-resolution restored video in a single forward pass, with output resolution capped at 1080p for test efficiency. The video is encoded/decoded via a causal video VAE (with 8× spatial downsampling), and the diffusion transformer operates in latent space. Training uses ~100 frames of 720p per batch on 72 H100 GPUs, with the adaptive window mechanism ensuring the model generalizes to arbitrary input aspect ratios and resolutions beyond the training resolution.

## Method

SeedVR2 converts a pre-trained 7B diffusion transformer (SeedVR) into a one-step generator via a two-stage pipeline: progressive distillation (64→32→16→8→4→2→1 steps with MSE loss) followed by adversarial post-training (APT) against real data. The key architectural innovation is an adaptive window attention mechanism that dynamically computes window sizes from the input resolution (via a 3×3 spatial partition on a resolution-consistent proxy), eliminating boundary artifacts seen with fixed-size windows at high resolutions. For training stability, the method replaces the standard non-saturating GAN loss with RpGAN plus approximate R1 and R2 regularization, and introduces a feature matching loss that extracts multi-layer features from the discriminator (replacing pixel-space LPIPS) to provide perceptual supervision at negligible extra cost. The generator and discriminator are both ~8B diffusion transformers, making this the largest VR GAN trained to date, and the 3B distilled variant achieves comparable quality at half the parameter count.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks (SPMCS, UDM10, YouHQ40), SeedVR2-3B achieves the best LPIPS (0.306, 0.218, 0.284) and DISTS (0.131, 0.106, 0.122) among all methods, outperforming the strongest multi-step baseline MGLD-VSR by 17–21% on LPIPS. On real-world VideoLQ, Ours-3B attains the best NIQE (4.687), MUSIQ (51.09), and DOVER (8.176); on AIGC28 it leads in NIQE (3.801), MUSIQ (62.99), and DOVER (15.77). All of this is achieved in a single sampling step, whereas baselines require 50 steps, yielding a >4× speedup (269 s vs. 1181–2326 s per 100-frame 720p video).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest multi-step diffusion baseline SeedVR-7B (50 steps), SeedVR2-7B (1 step) matches or slightly exceeds it on LPIPS/DISTS for SPMCS, UDM10, and YouHQ40, and the user study reports a +10% preference for SeedVR-7B-50 over Ours-7B-1 on visual quality, confirming near-parity in one step. On REDS30, MGLD-VSR retains an edge in LPIPS (0.271 vs. 0.337) and DISTS (0.097 vs. 0.127), which the authors attribute to MGLD-VSR being trained on the REDS training set. RealViformer (1-step GAN) leads on VideoLQ NIQE (4.153) and MUSIQ (54.65) but is clearly outperformed by SeedVR2 on AIGC28 (NIQE 3.801 vs. 3.994; DOVER 15.77 vs. 11.66) and on all synthetic perceptual metrics. UAV, VEnhancer, and STAR (all 50-step diffusion) are consistently outperformed by SeedVR2 on LPIPS and DISTS across SPMCS, UDM10, and YouHQ40, with VEnhancer showing the largest gap (LPIPS 0.455 vs. 0.306 on SPMCS). Against the concurrent one-step method DOVE-5B, Ours-3B wins on NIQE (4.687 vs. 5.079 on VideoLQ; 3.801 vs. 4.486 on AIGC28) and MUSIQ, but is slightly behind on CLIP-IQA and DOVER. DLoRAL (concurrent, LoRA-based) is compared qualitatively only; SeedVR2 produces sharper textures and better temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeedVR2 extends the Adversarial Post-Training (APT) framework from Lin et al. (2025), which converts multi-step diffusion models to one-step generators for video generation, into the video restoration domain. It initializes from the multi-step SeedVR diffusion transformer (Wang et al., 2025) and departs from distillation-based one-step methods (which are upper-bounded by a frozen teacher) by performing full adversarial fine-tuning against real data without any frozen prior. The paper introduces VR-specific adaptations—adaptive window attention to handle arbitrary high-resolution inputs, a feature matching loss to replace pixel-space LPIPS, and progressive distillation as a warm-up stage—that distinguish it from both prior one-step image restoration work and multi-step diffusion VR approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SeedVR2 demonstrates that one-step video restoration with a diffusion transformer is feasible at scale (~16B total parameters), achieving 4× speedup over 50-step baselines while matching or exceeding their quality, which is practically significant for real-world deployment. Key open questions remain: the causal video VAE consumes over 95% of inference time for 100-frame 720p video, the method is not robust to very heavy degradations or large motions, and it occasionally over-sharpens lightly degraded inputs. The adaptive window attention and feature matching loss are reusable techniques that could benefit other high-resolution generative restoration tasks, and the finding that a 3B distilled model can outperform its 7B teacher in user studies suggests distillation may add value beyond mere compression.
