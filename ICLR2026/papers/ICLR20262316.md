## Problem

Existing driving-scene video generators produce high-fidelity 2D videos but lack 3D consistency and novel-view controllability, while 3D/4D reconstruction methods achieve geometric coherence yet cannot generate scenes beyond captured data. This paper tackles the fundamental gap between generative imagination and faithful 4D reconstruction, asking whether a single feed-forward framework can directly produce explicit 4D scene representations from flexible control conditions without per-scene optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262316/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

In generation mode, the method takes structured control signals—BEV road sketches, 3D bounding boxes, ego trajectory, and textual scene descriptions—without requiring any reference RGB frames, and outputs a 4D Gaussian scene representation from which novel-view videos are rendered along user-defined lateral trajectory shifts (±1 m, ±2 m, ±4 m). In reconstruction mode, it accepts a K-view driving video clip (6 views, 17 frames at 424×800) and bypasses the diffusion generator, feeding clean VAE latents directly into the Gaussian decoder. The final output is a temporally and spatially consistent multi-track video sequence refined by a second diffusion pass.

## Method

WorldSplat is a three-stage feed-forward pipeline. First, a 4D-aware latent diffusion model (a ControlNet-augmented DiT built on OpenSora v1.2, trained with rectified flow) denoises multi-modal latents that jointly encode RGB, metric depth, and dynamic-object semantic masks, conditioned on road sketches, 3D boxes, ego trajectory, and text via cross-view attention and a multi-block ControlNet branch. Second, a transformer-based latent Gaussian decoder with cross-view and temporal attention layers predicts per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters (center via Plucker ray offset, rotation, scale, opacity, color, depth, and static/dynamic logits) from the denoised latents; these are then aggregated across frames into a unified 4D Gaussian representation by transforming all Gaussians into ego coordinates and fusing static Gaussians from all frames with dynamic Gaussians from the current frame. Third, an enhanced diffusion model (same architecture as the first stage but conditioned on the rendered video plus reprojected sketches and boxes) refines the Gaussian-splatting renderings to inpaint unobserved regions and sharpen motion-blurred frames. The key architectural insight is that pixel-aligned Gaussians predicted directly from multi-modal latents provide a more suitable basis for consistent novel-view synthesis than point maps, and the static-dynamic decomposition within the 4D aggregation enables correct rendering of moving objects from arbitrary trajectories.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, WorldSplat achieves state-of-the-art novel-view synthesis: FID 8.25 / FVD 40.17 at ±1 m, FID 11.26 / FVD 47.41 at ±2 m, and FID 13.38 / FVD 64.07 at ±4 m ego-vehicle lateral shift, outperforming the closest competitor DiST-4D (FID 10.12 / FVD 45.14 at ±1 m) by a clear margin. For original-view video generation, WorldSplat reaches FVD 74.13 / FID 8.78 without first-frame conditioning and FVD 16.57 / FID 4.14 with first-frame conditioning, both best in their respective settings. On downstream perception, generated data yields 38.49% mIoU and 29.34% mAP with a pretrained BEVFormer (vs. 44.09/34.51 for real data), and augmenting StreamPETR training with WorldSplat data improves mAP by +4.0 and NDS by +3.2 over the real-data-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WorldSplat outperforms all 17 baselines on the primary novel-view synthesis task (Table 2). The closest competitor, DiST-4D (Guo et al., 2025), is beaten by 1.87 FID and 4.97 FVD at ±1 m, and by 4.19 FID and 41.22 FVD at ±4 m, confirming that the 4D Gaussian aggregation and enhanced diffusion refinement provide substantial gains over prior 4D generation methods. Reconstruction-based baselines (PVG, EmerNeRF, StreetGaussian, OmniRe) are surpassed by large margins (e.g., 23.23 FID at ±1 m vs. OmniRe), highlighting the advantage of a generative feed-forward pipeline over per-scene optimization. FreeVS, which also targets free-trajectory view synthesis, is outperformed by 43.01 FVD at ±1 m, indicating that explicit 4D Gaussian geometry is far more effective than video-diffusion-based view extrapolation. On original-view video generation (Table 1), WorldSplat beats DriveDreamer-2, MagicDrive-V2, MagicDrive3D, Panacea, DiVE, CoGen, and Vista across all three conditioning protocols; the only near-tie is with UniScene under noisy-latent initialization, where UniScene edges out WorldSplat on FID (6.12 vs. 6.51) while WorldSplat leads on FVD (60.84 vs. 70.52). For downstream perception (Table 4), WorldSplat surpasses DiVE by 2.53% mIoU and 4.79% mAP with BEVFormer, and provides larger training-data gains for StreamPETR (+4.0 mAP) compared to Panacea (+2.6 mAP). On geometric consistency (Table 6), WorldSplat achieves the best PSNR (24.59) and SSIM (0.912) among feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods, narrowly beating OmniScene (24.27 / 0.736) and clearly surpassing PixelSplat and MVSplat.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldSplat sits at the intersection of two established but previously separate lines of work: driving video generation (MagicDrive, DriveDreamer, Vista, Panacea) and 3D/4D urban scene reconstruction (Street Gaussians, OmniRe, EmerNeRF). Prior generative methods operate in the 2D image domain and lack 3D consistency for novel views, while reconstruction methods achieve geometric fidelity but cannot synthesize unseen scenes. The paper's key architectural departure is a feed-forward latent Gaussian decoder that directly converts multi-modal diffusion latents (RGB + depth + segmentation) into pixel-aligned 4D Gaussians, bypassing the common video-first-then-reconstruct pipeline. Technically, it builds on OpenSora's video diffusion backbone with a ControlNet-style conditioning branch, Rectified Flow training, and 3D Gaussian Splatting for rendering, with cross-view attention ensuring multi-camera coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying generation and reconstruction in a single feed-forward pass, WorldSplat removes the per-scene optimization bottleneck that limits scalability of prior 3D/4D driving reconstruction, making it practical for large-scale synthetic data generation for autonomous driving training. The downstream perception results (mAP 29.32% with BEVFormer, +4.0 mAP gain for StreamPETR) demonstrate that the generated data is not merely visually plausible but functionally useful. Key limitations remain: the method fails on completely unobserved regions (e.g., building interiors), the enhanced diffusion model is critical for quality (ablation shows FVD nearly doubles without it), and the generative component still relies on conditioning from observed scene structure rather than truly unconstrained imagination. Open questions include whether the 4D Gaussian representation scales to longer sequences and more complex urban environments, and whether the feed-forward decoder can generalize beyond the nuScenes camera configuration.
