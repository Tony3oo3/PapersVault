## Problem

Existing classifier-free guidance (CFG) implementations for discrete/masked diffusion contain a structural flaw: the normalizing constant in the guided rate matrix unintentionally rescales the overall jump rate, causing disproportionately fast unmasking early in generation and degrading sample quality. Additionally, there is no theoretical characterization of how dynamic guidance schedules (varying guidance strength over the sampling trajectory) affect the sampled distribution in discrete diffusion, despite such schedules being widely adopted in practice for continuous diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method operates within the masked diffusion framework, starting from a fully masked token sequence and iteratively unmasking tokens under a conditioning signal (text prompt, ImageNet class label, or molecular ring count). For image generation it produces 256×256 ImageNet images or text-to-image outputs scored on GenEval; for text it generates up to 256 tokens autoregressively in blocks of 32 using a simple Euler sampler; for molecules it generates QM9 small molecules. The guidance modification is applied at each denoising step as a one-line change to the transition computation, replacing exp with softmax on the combined conditional/unconditional logits.

## Method

The core contribution is a column normalization of the guided rate matrix that decouples the jump rate from the jump distribution. By decomposing the rate matrix into a scalar rate times a probability distribution, the authors show that the existing unlocking guidance (Nisonoff et al.) has the normalizing constant Z_w multiplying the overall jump rate, so guidance accelerates unmasking rather than only affecting which token is selected. The fix normalizes columns of the rate matrix, which in the masked diffusion case reduces to applying softmax instead of exp to the combined conditional/unconditional logits — a single-line code change. The paper then derives exact closed-form solutions for the sampled distribution under piecewise-constant guidance schedules in 1D and 2D, revealing that high guidance early in sampling (when inputs are heavily masked) harms quality while late-stage guidance improves it, and that the final distribution is a weighted interpolation of tilted distributions whose weights depend on the time partition. This theory predicts that increasing guidance schedules (low early, high late) are optimal, which is confirmed empirically on ImageNet FID, GenEval, MATH-500, and QM9.

## Results

On GenEval with Show-o, the proposed column-normalized guidance achieves 63.39% overall score at w=8, compared to 43.96% for Unlocking Guidance — a gap of nearly 20 points. On GenEval with Meissonic, the method reaches 45.9% overall at w=6 versus 28.5% for Unlocking. On ImageNet-256 (50K samples, 50 steps), normalization prevents the sharp FID degradation that both Unlocking and Simple Guidance exhibit at high guidance strengths, and is the only method that simultaneously improves precision and recall (fidelity-diversity tradeoff). On MATH-500 with LLaDA-8B-Instruct, normalization yields consistent gains across all guidance weights (e.g., +29.8 points on Counting at w=8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Unlocking Guidance (Nisonoff et al., 2024), the proposed column-normalized method wins decisively across all evaluated domains. On GenEval with Show-o, the gap widens with guidance strength: at w=8, the method scores 63.39% overall versus 43.96% for Unlocking (a 19.4-point margin), while at w=2 the gap is only 2.7 points — confirming the theory that the unnormalized Z_w factor causes increasingly harmful acceleration of unmasking at higher w. On ImageNet FID, Unlocking Guidance degrades sharply beyond w≈4, whereas the normalized method maintains stable FID. On MATH-500, normalization improves accuracy at every guidance weight tested. Against Simple Guidance (Schiff et al., 2024), the normalized method also outperforms on ImageNet FID and is the only method that improves both precision and recall simultaneously (Figure 7); Simple Guidance, while having a milder w-dependence than Unlocking, still lacks the column normalization and thus suffers from the same rate-distortion issue at high guidance strengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-free guidance framework from continuous diffusion (Ho & Salimans, 2021) to the discrete/masked diffusion setting, building directly on the two existing discrete CFG formulations: Unlocking Guidance (Nisonoff et al., 2024), which interpolates rate matrices, and Simple Guidance (Schiff et al., 2024), which interpolates transition probabilities. The key departure is identifying that both existing methods contain an unintended rate-rescaling artifact from the partition function Z_w, and proposing column normalization as a principled fix. Additionally, the paper provides the first theoretical characterization of dynamic guidance schedules in discrete diffusion, extending empirical observations from the continuous setting (Kynkäanniemi et al., 2024; Xi et al., 2024) with explicit low-dimensional proofs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-line code change (replacing exp with softmax in the guided transition) is immediately adoptable by any practitioner using discrete diffusion with CFG, and the paper demonstrates consistent gains across image generation (ImageNet FID, GenEval), text generation (MATH-500), and molecular generation (QM9). The theoretical analysis of guidance schedules—showing that high guidance during heavily-masked early stages harms quality while late-stage guidance helps—provides the first principled design criteria for scheduling in discrete diffusion, a question previously addressed only empirically in the continuous setting. Limitations include the theory being restricted to low-dimensional masked diffusion; extending the analysis to uniform diffusion, higher dimensions, and the role of score estimation error remain open.
