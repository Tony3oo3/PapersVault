## Problem

Ultra-long text generation (thousands of words) by LLMs suffers from coherence degradation, topic drift, and structural collapse, and prior methods like LongWriter rely on supervised fine-tuning over synthetic long-form outputs that are costly to construct, lack coherence, and cap quality at the teacher model's level. This paper asks whether reinforcement learning alone—starting from a base model with no annotated or synthetic data—can unlock high-quality ultra-long generation, and identifies three open sub-questions: how to design rewards for open-ended writing, whether test-time scaling (long CoT) helps, and whether continual pretraining raises the RL performance ceiling.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single writing instruction prompt (filtered from WildChat-1M and LMSYS-Chat-1M to retain requests demanding extended outputs) and produces a two-part response: a  segment containing planning and reflection, followed by an <answer> segment with the final ultra-long text (up to 14,000 tokens). The expected output length is not fixed but determined per-query by a QwQ-32B length-range predictor that estimates a [lower, upper] word-count interval, which the Length RM then enforces during training.

## Method

LongWriter-Zero trains Qwen2.5-32B entirely via GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) with no SFT data, using a composite reward that averages three individually normalized sub-rewards: a Length RM (rule-based, checking output against a per-query word-count range), a Writing RM (Bradley-Terry preference model trained on human-labeled writing pairs), and a Format RM (enforcing exactly one think + one answer segment and penalizing semantic repetition). Two additional ingredients prove critical: (1) a Think Prompt that explicitly instructs the model to plan in a  block before writing, which the model learns to expand over RL steps until it saturates at a task-optimal length; and (2) 30B tokens of continual pretraining on books, reports, and academic papers (plus 1% distilled long-CoT data) before RL, which raises both the starting point and the final reward ceiling. The final pipeline—continual pretraining followed by 150 GRPO steps with the composite reward—yields a 32B model that outperforms 100B+ models on WritingBench and Arena-Write.

## Results

LongWriter-Zero (32B) achieves the highest WritingBench average score of 8.69/10, surpassing Qwen3-235B-A22B (8.68), Claude-Sonnet-4 (8.60), and DeepSeek-R1 (8.55). On Arena-Write, it attains an Elo rating of 1447, outperforming DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3-235B-A22B (both 1343) and Claude-Sonnet-4 (1185). In human-in-the-loop win-rate evaluation, LongWriter-Zero achieves win rates up to 98.2% against weaker baselines and remains above 62% even against the strongest competitors (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-235B-A22B). On LongBench-Write, it scores 86.3 (S), 84.2 (S_l), and 88.4 (S_q), the best across all three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongWriter-Zero (32B) achieves the best WritingBench average (8.69) by a narrow margin over Qwen3-235B-A22B (8.68) and a clear lead over Claude-Sonnet-4 (8.60), DeepSeek-R1 (8.55), and GPT-4o (8.16). On Arena-Write Elo, it leads all baselines by 104 points over DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3-235B-A22B (both 1343) and by 262 points over Claude-Sonnet-4 (1185). The only domain where it trails is Literature & Art (8.4 vs. DeepSeek-R1's 8.6), while it leads in the other five domains. Against the SFT-based LongWriter-8B, LongWriter-Zero improves WritingBench by +0.78 and Arena-Write Elo by +990, demonstrating the superiority of the RL paradigm. In pairwise win-rate evaluation, LongWriter-Zero wins >62% of comparisons against the strongest baselines (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-235B-A22B) and up to 98.2% against weaker ones, with human annotators confirming the automatic judgments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the R1-Zero paradigm—pure RL from a base model without any SFT—into the long-form writing domain, where prior work (LongWriter, Suri) has relied exclusively on supervised fine-tuning over synthetic instruction-output pairs. The training algorithm is GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), the same optimizer used in DeepSeek-R1, but the reward design is fundamentally different: instead of deterministic rule-based rewards (correct/incorrect), the paper introduces a composite of three learned reward models (length, writing quality via Bradley-Terry preference, and format) to handle the open-ended, multidimensional nature of writing. The test-time scaling component (explicit  reasoning before answering) extends the CoT-scaling idea from math and code to creative/professional writing, a domain where its benefit was previously untested.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 32B model trained purely with RL can surpass 100B+ reasoning models (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3-235B) on long-form writing, suggesting that RL with well-designed rewards can unlock capabilities that scale poorly with parameter count alone. The finding that an explicit thinking step substantially improves writing quality (Arena-Write Elo drops from 1221 to 668 without it) opens the door to test-time scaling for all open-ended generation tasks, not just verifiable reasoning. Key limitations include reward hacking (templated openings, near-duplicate repetition, keyword inflation) and the self-referential nature of Arena-Write (a self-curated benchmark judged by a Qwen model), leaving open whether the gains fully transfer to truly independent human judgment at scale.
