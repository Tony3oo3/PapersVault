## Problem

Training flow-based policies with off-policy reinforcement learning (e.g., SAC) is notoriously unstable because backpropagating through the K-step Euler rollout causes exploding or vanishing gradients. Prior work mitigates this by using surrogate objectives or distilling the flow into a simpler one-step actor, but both decouple optimization from the expressive multimodal generator. The paper identifies the root cause: the flow rollout is algebraically equivalent to a residual RNN, inheriting the same gradient pathologies.

## Input / Output

The policy takes a continuous environment state s and produces a continuous action a ∈ ℝ^d via a K-step (typically K=4) stochastic flow rollout starting from A_0 ~ N(0, I_d). Each step applies a learned velocity network conditioned on (t_i, A_{t_i}, s), and the final action is obtained by tanh-squashing A_{t_K} with exact Jacobian correction. For SAC's entropy term, a noise-augmented rollout adds per-step isotropic Gaussian noise with a compensating drift, yielding a tractable product of per-step Gaussian transition densities over the intermediate path.

## Method

The core insight is that the K-step flow rollout A_{t_{i+1}} = A_{t_i} + Δt_i v_θ(t_i, A_{t_i}, s) is a residual RNN, so the velocity network is reparameterized with architectures designed to stabilize deep recurrent computation. Flow-G introduces a GRU-style gated velocity v_θ = g_i ⊙ (v̂_θ − A_{t_i}) where the sigmoid gate adaptively interpolates between preserving and rewriting the intermediate action, keeping the step Jacobian near identity. Flow-T replaces the velocity with a Transformer decoder that refines the action-time token through L pre-norm residual blocks of state-only cross-attention (no token-to-token mixing), providing well-conditioned depth. A noise-augmented rollout with drift correction makes the per-step transitions Gaussian, enabling a closed-form joint path density p_c(A|s) that serves as a drop-in entropy term for SAC. This yields SAC Flow, a direct end-to-end off-policy algorithm requiring no surrogate objectives, distillation actors, or auxiliary networks, supporting both from-scratch and offline-to-online training.

## Results

On from-scratch MuJoCo training (1M steps), SAC Flow-T and SAC Flow-G achieve comparable or superior performance across all six tasks except Humanoid, with up to a 130% improvement over the strongest baseline on HumanoidStandup. In offline-to-online sparse-reward manipulation (1M offline + 1M online steps), SAC Flow-T attains up to a 60% higher success rate on OGBench cube-triple and cube-quadruple tasks compared to prior methods. Ablation studies confirm that the naive MLP velocity baseline suffers ~10× gradient norm explosion around 10⁵ steps, while Flow-G and Flow-T maintain stable gradients throughout training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlowRL (the prior SOTA flow-based off-policy method using Wasserstein-2 regularization), SAC Flow-G and SAC Flow-T consistently surpass it across all MuJoCo tasks by directly optimizing the SAC objective rather than a surrogate. Against DIME (diffusion-based max-entropy RL), SAC Flow outperforms on Hopper, Walker2D, and HumanoidStandup while achieving comparable results on HalfCheetah and Ant. Against the classical Gaussian-policy baselines SAC and PPO, SAC Flow achieves substantially higher final returns on challenging tasks (e.g., HumanoidStandup) while maintaining convergence stability on simpler ones, and demonstrates clear sample-efficiency advantages over on-policy PPO. In offline-to-online settings, SAC Flow-T achieves state-of-the-art success rates on OGBench cube-triple and cube-quadruple, outperforming both FQL and QC-FQL. On Robomimic, however, SAC Flow-G/T only match QC-FQL due to the large β regularizer limiting the flow model's capacity, though both still outperform the on-policy baseline ReinFlow under 1M online steps. QSM (Q-score matching) is included as a foundational diffusion-policy baseline but is generally outperformed by the more recent methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SAC framework (Haarnoja et al., 2018) and Rectified Flow (Liu et al., 2022) to train flow-based policies with off-policy RL, but departs fundamentally from prior flow-RL methods (FlowRL, FQL, QC-FQL) that avoid the multi-step rollout gradient problem via surrogate objectives or distillation into one-step actors. The key conceptual departure is identifying an algebraic equivalence between the K-step Euler flow rollout and a residual RNN, which reframes the instability as a classic vanishing/exploding gradient problem and motivates reparameterizing the velocity network with GRU or Transformer decoder cells. The noise-augmented rollout for tractable likelihood computation draws on the drift-correction construction from Holderrieth & Erives (2025), adapted here to enable SAC's entropy-regularized objective over the full path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By resolving the gradient pathology at its architectural root rather than sidestepping it, SAC Flow enables direct end-to-end off-policy training of expressive multimodal flow policies without distillation or surrogate losses—a practical unblocking for the broader flow-policy RL community. The sequential-model perspective is general and could inform stability fixes for other iterative generative policies (e.g., diffusion) trained with RL. Open questions include validation on real robots, whether tighter (provable) stability guarantees can be established beyond the empirical 10× gradient-ratio evidence, and whether the path-regularized critic bias (from using log p_c instead of log π) affects asymptotic optimality in high-precision tasks.
