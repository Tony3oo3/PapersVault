## Problem

Existing Joint Audio-Video Generation (JAVG) methods rely on coarse-grained temporal or semantic alignment (e.g., parameter sharing, simple embedding alignment) that fails to capture fine-grained spatio-temporal synchronization—where specific visual regions must correspond to specific frequency components at specific timestamps. Additionally, current evaluation benchmarks (AIST++, Landscape) contain only 20–100 samples from single scenarios, making them inadequate for assessing synchronization in complex, multi-event real-world content.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single open-ended text prompt as input and jointly produces a synchronized video (240P, 24 fps, 4 s by default, extendable to 10 s) and audio (16 kHz mel-spectrogram) in one unified diffusion process. The text is consumed twice: T5 hidden states provide coarse semantic conditioning via standard cross-attention, while ImageBind's 77 hidden states feed a dedicated 4-layer transformer that estimates 32 spatial and 32 temporal prior tokens. Video and audio latents are sampled concurrently at each of 30 inference steps with classifier-free guidance at 7.0.

## Method

JavisDiT is a 3.14B-parameter DiT with 28 blocks per branch, where each block applies cascaded spatial-then-temporal self-attention (reducing complexity from O((THW)²) to O(THW·(T+HW))), coarse T5 cross-attention, fine-grained spatio-temporal cross-attention guided by learned prior tokens, and a bidirectional cross-attention module that computes a single attention matrix A between video queries and audio keys, then uses A·v_a and Aᵀ·v_v for audio-to-video and video-to-audio information flow. The key novelty is the HiST-Sypo Estimator: a 4-layer transformer that takes ImageBind text embeddings and outputs Gaussian-distributed spatial (where events occur) and temporal (when events start/end) prior tokens, trained via contrastive learning against synthetic asynchronous video-audio negatives (temporal shifting, source addition/removal, speed adjustment, etc.). Training follows a three-stage protocol—audio pretraining on 788K pairs, ST-Prior contrastive training on 611K triplets, then JAVG fine-tuning of only the cross-attention modules—using rectified flow as the denoising objective with the video branch initialized from OpenSora and the audio branch from AudioLDM2.

## Results

On the proposed JavisBench (4s, 240P/24fps, 16kHz audio), JavisDiT achieves the best audio quality (FAD 7.2), the best audio-video consistency (AV-IB 0.197, CAVP 0.801), and the highest JavisScore (0.154) for spatio-temporal synchrony, surpassing the strongest cascaded baseline FoleyCrafter (JavisScore 0.151) and the joint method UniVerse-1 (JavisScore 0.077). On the closed-set benchmarks, JavisDiT attains FVD 94.2 / FAD 8.5 on Landscape and FAD 9.6 on AIST++, outperforming MM-LDM and AV-DiT on most metrics. The model also maintains strong performance at 10-second duration (JavisScore 0.154, FAD 7.1), confirming robustness to longer generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JavisDiT outperforms all cascaded baselines (TempoToken, TPoS, ReWaS, See&Hear, FoleyCrafter) on JavisScore, with the closest competitor FoleyCrafter scoring 0.151 vs. JavisDiT's 0.154; however, FoleyCrafter edges ahead on CAVP (0.800 vs. 0.801 is essentially tied) and AVHScore (0.186 vs. 0.179). Against the joint T2AV baseline UniVerse-1, JavisDiT wins decisively on audio-video synchrony (JavisScore 0.154 vs. 0.077), audio quality (FAD 7.2 vs. 8.7), CLAP (0.391 vs. 0.245), and AV-IB (0.197 vs. 0.104), but UniVerse-1 retains a slight advantage on video quality (FVD 194.2 vs. 204.1, KVD 0.5 vs. 1.4) and text-video alignment (TV-IB 0.272 vs. 0.263, CLIP 0.309 vs. 0.302), which the authors attribute to UniVerse-1's stronger Wan-2.1 video backbone. On the older closed-set benchmarks (Table 2), JavisDiT achieves the best FVD on Landscape (94.2 vs. MM-LDM's 105.0) and the best FAD on both Landscape (8.5) and AIST++ (9.6), though AV-DiT retains a lower FVD on AIST++ (68.8 vs. 86.7). The earliest joint method MM-Diffusion is substantially outperformed across all metrics (FVD 2311.9, FAD 27.5, JavisScore 0.070), confirming the benefit of the DiT backbone and hierarchical prior mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

JavisDiT builds on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) and STDiT backbones for video generation, extending them to a joint audio-video setting by adding an audio branch initialized from the video weights. It departs from prior JAVG methods (MM-Diffusion, Seeing-Hearing, MM-LDM, AV-DiT, UniVerse-1) that rely on coarse parameter sharing, simple embedding alignment, or mono-directional adapters for synchronization, instead introducing a hierarchical spatio-temporal prior estimator (HiST-Sypo) trained via contrastive learning to provide fine-grained spatial and temporal conditioning. The benchmark (JavisBench) and metric (JavisScore) extend the evaluation landscape beyond the limited AIST++/Landscape benchmarks and the optical-flow-based AV-Align metric, drawing on ImageBind for cross-modal similarity scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The JavisBench benchmark (10,140 multi-event, multi-scenario samples with a 5-dimension taxonomy) and the JavisScore metric fill a genuine gap: prior benchmarks were too narrow (single-scenario, 20–100 samples) and AV-Align fails on complex multi-event content, making it difficult to assess real-world synchronization. The HiST-Sypo mechanism offers a principled, learnable alternative to ad-hoc alignment strategies, and the ablations confirm that fine-grained spatio-temporal priors contribute more to synchrony than bidirectional cross-attention alone. Key open questions include scaling training data beyond 0.6M triplets, improving JavisScore's 75% accuracy, reducing the 30-second generation latency, and extending evaluation to higher resolutions and longer durations where current models still struggle.
