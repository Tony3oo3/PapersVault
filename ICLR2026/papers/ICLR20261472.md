## Problem

Lipschitz bandits extend stochastic bandits to continuous metric action spaces, but all existing algorithms (e.g., Zooming) assume immediate reward feedback to refine adaptive partitions and confidence estimates. In practice, rewards arrive after random delays (or are missing entirely), and the continuous arm space amplifies the impact of stale or absent observations since each sampled point represents a neighborhood whose estimation depends on delayed data. This paper is the first to study Lipschitz bandits under stochastic delayed feedback, addressing both bounded and unbounded delay distributions.

## Input / Output

The agent operates on a known compact doubling metric space (A, D) with diameter at most 1 and a time horizon T. At each round it selects an arm x_t ∈ A and receives a sub-Gaussian reward y_t = μ(x_t) + ε_t, but the feedback pair (x_t, y_t) arrives after a random integer delay τ_t drawn from an unknown distribution supported on N ∪ {∞}; critically, the agent does not observe the original round index or the delay value, making it impossible to directly associate feedback with the action that generated it. The output is a sequence of arm selections and theoretical cumulative regret bounds expressed in terms of the zooming dimension d_z and delay distribution quantiles.

## Method

For bounded delays, the Delayed Zooming algorithm extends the classic Zooming algorithm with a 'lazy update' mechanism: instead of updating the confidence radius r_t(x) whenever delayed feedback arrives, the algorithm caches excess observations and only updates when the arm is next pulled, guaranteeing r_t(x) ≥ 2r_s(x) (where s is the last pull time) and preserving the suboptimality gap bound Δ(x) ≤ 6r_t(x). This yields regret Õ(T^((d_z+1)/(d_z+2)) + τ_max · T^(d_z/(d_z+2))). For unbounded delays, the Delayed Lipschitz Phased Pruning (DLPP) algorithm uses a phased elimination strategy: within each phase it samples arms uniformly in a round-robin fashion across active balls, accumulates a fixed number of observed rewards per ball, then prunes balls whose empirical means deviate by more than 8r_m from the best, and discretizes survivors at half the radius. The key analytical insight is bounding the number of pulls n_t(B) by the observed count v_{t+Q(p)}(B) via a multiplicative Chernoff argument on the delay quantile, yielding regret Õ(T^((d_z+1)/(d_z+2))/p + Q(p)) minimized over p ∈ (0,1], which is shown nearly optimal via a matching lower bound.

## Results

Both the Delayed Zooming algorithm and DLPP achieve sublinear cumulative regret under bounded (uniform) and unbounded (geometric) delay distributions with E[τ] ∈ {0, 20, 50} over T = 60,000 rounds. For the 2D reward function, DLPP achieves final cumulative regret of approximately 1120–1143 across delay levels, while Delayed Zooming ranges from 1120 to 1160, confirming DLPP's advantage in higher-dimensional settings. The theoretical regret bounds are Õ(T^((d_z+1)/(d_z+2)) + τ_max · T^(d_z/(d_z+2))) for bounded delays and min_p {1/p · T^((d_z+1)/(d_z+2)) + Q(p)} for unbounded delays, with a matching lower bound up to logarithmic factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly notes that no existing algorithm directly addresses Lipschitz bandits with stochastic delayed feedback, so no head-to-head comparison with a prior delayed Lipschitz bandit method is possible. The only empirical baseline is the delay-free setting (E[τ] = 0), which serves as a lower bound on regret; both proposed algorithms remain on the same regret scale as the delay-free case even at E[τ] = 50, consistent with the theoretical additive delay penalty. The BLiN algorithm (Feng et al., 2022) is discussed as structurally related to DLPP but is inapplicable here because it requires immediate or batched feedback and is restricted to axis-aligned cube partitions on [0,1]^d. Among the two proposed methods, DLPP outperforms Delayed Zooming on the 2D reward function due to its pruning and discretization strategy, while Delayed Zooming performs better on 1D reward functions. The lower bound (Theorem 5) confirms that the DLPP regret bound is nearly optimal up to logarithmic factors, closing the gap between upper and lower bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed threads: Lipschitz (continuum-armed) bandits, where the Zooming algorithm (Kleinberg et al., 2008, 2019) achieves optimal regret via adaptive discretization, and bandits with stochastic delayed feedback, where Delayed-UCB1 (Joulani et al., 2013) and quantile-based analyses (Lancewicki et al., 2021) handle missing or late observations in discrete-action settings. The authors explicitly note that no prior work has combined these two frameworks. For bounded delays, they extend the Zooming algorithm with a 'lazy update' mechanism inspired by Delayed-UCB1; for unbounded delays, they build a phased elimination algorithm (DLPP) drawing on BLiN (Feng et al., 2022) and the reduction technique of Lancewicki et al. (2021) for the lower bound.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous treatment of Lipschitz bandits under stochastic delayed feedback, a setting that arises naturally in hyperparameter optimization, dynamic pricing, and clinical trials where reward signals arrive late or are censored. The near-optimality result (upper bound matching lower bound up to log factors) for the unbounded-delay case is a clean theoretical contribution. A key open question the authors highlight is extending the Delayed Zooming algorithm to unbounded delays without additional assumptions, which remains unresolved despite empirical evidence that it works in practice. The lazy update mechanism, which preserves the suboptimality-gap-to-confidence-radius invariant under passive radius shrinkage, may be reusable in other adaptive-discretization settings with delayed information.
