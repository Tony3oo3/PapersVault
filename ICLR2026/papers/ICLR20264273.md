## Problem

VLA models trained via supervised fine-tuning face a fundamental data scarcity bottleneck: scaling requires massive, expensive robot trajectories, and the resulting policies generalize poorly under distribution shift. The paper asks whether outcome-driven RL—inspired by LLM reasoning breakthroughs like DeepSeek-R1—can improve long-horizon step-by-step action planning in VLAs without requiring additional demonstration data or hand-crafted reward functions.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes single-view RGB images (640×480), a natural-language task instruction, and robot proprioceptive state (joint angles/end-effector pose) as input, deliberately omitting wrist-camera views used by the official OpenVLA-OFT. It outputs a sequence of 256 discrete action tokens generated by the LLaMA2 output head with cross-entropy loss, which are grouped into action chunks (8 for LIBERO, 25 for RoboTwin) and executed sequentially in a closed loop. This token-based decoding is critical: it provides the probability distributions over actions needed for both random sampling during rollout and policy-gradient computation, unlike diffusion or MLP-regression decoders.

## Method

SimpleVLA-RL extends the veRL framework (originally for LLM RLHF) into a unified training-inference-rendering pipeline for VLA models, replacing open-loop autoregressive generation with closed-loop interactive trajectory sampling where each action updates the environment state. Training uses GRPO with a binary outcome reward (1 for task completion, 0 otherwise) uniformly propagated to all action tokens in a trajectory, eliminating the need for hand-crafted process rewards. Three exploration-enhancing modifications are applied: dynamic sampling that discards groups where all trajectories share the same outcome (preventing zero-advantage gradients), an asymmetric clip range widened to [0.8, 1.28] to allow low-probability tokens to increase, and a higher rollout temperature (1.6 vs. 1.0) for greater trajectory diversity. The KL divergence regularization is removed entirely, following DAPO, which eliminates the reference model and reduces memory while avoiding constraints on policy divergence. The framework is applied to OpenVLA-OFT (LLaMA2-7B backbone) and trained on 8×A800 GPUs with parallel multi-environment rendering, achieving 99% SoTA on LIBERO and 80% relative improvement on RoboTwin 2.0, while also revealing an emergent 'pushcut' phenomenon where the policy discovers novel pushing strategies absent from all demonstrations.

## Results

On LIBERO, SimpleVLA-RL improves OpenVLA-OFT from 91.0% to 99.1% average success rate, surpassing π₀ (94.2%) and UniVLA (95.2%) to achieve SoTA. On RoboTwin 2.0, the method delivers an 80% relative improvement (38.3% → 68.8%), outperforming π₀ (52.7%) and RDT (33.3%) across all 12 tasks spanning short to extra-long horizons. On RoboTwin 1.0, gains of +30.6% (39.8% → 70.4%) are achieved over the SFT baseline. In real-world sim-to-real transfer, SimpleVLA-RL lifts success from 17.5% to 38.5%, surpassing RDT's 23.5% without any real-robot data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LIBERO, SimpleVLA-RL (99.1% avg) outperforms all baselines: π₀ (94.2%), UniVLA (95.2%), Nora (87.9%), π₀+FAST (85.5%), OpenVLA (76.5%), and Octo (75.1%), with the largest margin on LIBERO-Long (+12.0% over SFT, +13.3% over π₀). On RoboTwin 2.0, the method achieves 68.8% overall, beating π₀ (52.7%) by 16.1 points and RDT (33.3%) by 35.5 points, with consistent gains across all four horizon groups (short: +43.6%, medium: +25.4%, long: +22.4%, extra-long: +22.4% over SFT). On RoboTwin 1.0, SimpleVLA-RL (70.4%) surpasses DP3 (58.1%) and DP (5.9%) by substantial margins. In real-world deployment, SimpleVLA-RL (38.5% avg) outperforms RDT (23.5%) by 15 points, with the largest gain on Stack Bowls (+32.0 points, 84% relative improvement). The method consistently wins across all benchmarks and task horizons, with no baseline matching or exceeding its performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper systematically adapts the LLM RL paradigm—specifically the DeepSeek-R1/GRPO recipe with sparse outcome rewards—to VLA models for robotic manipulation, building on the veRL infrastructure for distributed training and inference. It departs from prior VLA RL efforts (GRAPE, ConRFT, ReinboT, TGRPO) by using a simple binary task-completion reward rather than dense or hand-crafted process rewards, and by incorporating DAPO-style exploration enhancements (dynamic sampling, asymmetric clipping, higher temperature). The key technical contribution is the VLA-specific interactive rollout loop with parallel multi-environment rendering, which bridges the gap between LLM autoregressive generation and closed-loop robot-environment interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that outcome-driven RL can substantially reduce VLA training's dependence on expensive demonstration data (1 trajectory + RL approaches 500 trajectories + SFT) and improves generalization to unseen objects, spatial configurations, and tasks where SFT overfits. The 'pushcut' phenomenon—where RL discovers pushing strategies absent from all demonstrations—suggests RL enables genuine strategy discovery beyond imitation, a capability SFT fundamentally lacks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to diffusion-based VLA decoders (which lack natural action token distributions), whether the initial-capability threshold can be lowered, and whether gains hold at larger scale or on more complex real-world tasks beyond the four tested.
