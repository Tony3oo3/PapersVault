## Problem

While NP-hardness of ReLU network verification (positivity, surjectivity, Lipschitz constant) and zonotope containment was already established, their parameterized complexity with respect to the input dimension d remained open. This paper resolves the question by proving W[1]-hardness for all these problems, ruling out fixed-parameter tractability in d under standard complexity assumptions, and showing that naive enumeration algorithms are essentially optimal under the Exponential Time Hypothesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261596/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For network problems, the input is a 2- or 3-layer ReLU network specified by weight matrices W_i ∈ R^{n_i × n_{i-1}} and bias vectors b_i ∈ R^{n_i}, with input dimension d = n_0 serving as the parameter; for geometry problems, the input is a pair of generator matrices A ∈ R^{d×n} and B ∈ R^{d×m} defining zonotopes. All problems are yes/no decision problems: whether the network attains a positive output, whether it is surjective, whether its L_p-Lipschitz constant exceeds a threshold L, or whether one zonotope is contained in another. The encoding size N (bit-length of all weights and biases) is the secondary parameter, and the key hardness result is that no algorithm can solve these in ρ(d)·N^{o(d)} time for any function ρ, assuming ETH.

## Method

The central reduction maps MULTICOLORED CLIQUE (parameter k) to 2-layer ReLU positivity (parameter d = k+1) by constructing spike functions s_{r,l}: R²→[0,1] that fire only when two input coordinates sum to a Sidon-set label of an edge, and penalty functions p_c: R→[0,1] that fire only at node labels, all implemented with O(|V|+|E|) ReLU neurons. A homogenization step introduces an extra input variable y to eliminate all biases while preserving the decision, yielding a network with zero biases whose positivity is equivalent to the existence of a k-colored clique. The duality between positively homogeneous convex CPWL functions and polytopes then transfers hardness to zonotope non-containment, and adding a single ReLU neuron to the output layer extends it to 3-layer zero-function detection and L_p-Lipschitz constant approximation. For the positive result on input-convex networks, the paper exploits a small extended formulation of the Newton polytope to reduce L_1-Lipschitz computation to polynomially many LPs and L_∞-Lipschitz computation to 2^d LPs, and uses ℓ_1 subspace embeddings to obtain a randomized (1+ε)-approximation for norm maximization on zonotopes in O((cd log d/ε²)^{d-1}·T + poly(n)) time.

## Results

The paper proves W[1]-hardness (with respect to input dimension d) for 2-layer ReLU positivity, surjectivity, zonotope non-containment, and L_p-Lipschitz constant computation (for all p ∈ (0,∞] in 2-layer and all p ∈ [0,∞] in 3-layer networks), resolving open problems from COLT '25. Under the Exponential Time Hypothesis, all these problems cannot be solved in ρ(d)·N^{o(d)} time, showing that the standard enumeration algorithms running in O(n^d·poly(N)) (2-layer) or O(n^{2d}·poly(N)) (3-layer) are essentially optimal. On the positive side, the L_1-Lipschitz constant of input-convex neural networks (ICNNs) is computable in polynomial time, and the L_∞-Lipschitz constant is FPT in d via 2^d LPs. A randomized (1+ε)-approximation for norm maximization on zonotopes is also given using subspace embeddings, running in O((cd log d / ε²)^{d-1}·T + poly(n)) time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or experimental comparisons. The 'comparison' is against prior complexity results: Froese et al. (COLT '25) established NP-hardness of 2-layer ReLU positivity and surjectivity and left their parameterized complexity open; this paper strengthens those to W[1]-hardness w.r.t. d. Virmaux & Scaman (2018) proved NP-hardness of the L_2-Lipschitz constant for 2-layer networks; this paper extends to all p ∈ (0,∞] and adds W[1]-hardness. Jordan & Dimakis (2020) showed NP-hardness of approximating the L_p-Lipschitz constant for 3-layer networks; this paper upgrades to W[1]-hardness. The key practical implication is that the naive enumeration-based algorithms (which run in n^{O(d)}·poly(N) time) are provably essentially optimal under ETH, meaning no fundamentally faster exact algorithm is expected for these problems as a function of d.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly resolves open problems posed by Froese et al. (COLT '25), who established the equivalence between 2-layer ReLU positivity and zonotope non-containment and asked whether these problems are fixed-parameter tractable in the input dimension. The reduction from Multicolored Clique (with input dimension scaling linearly in the clique size) builds on the spike/penalty function construction from that prior work, while the Lipschitz constant hardness extends the NP-hardness results of Virmaux & Scaman (2018) and Jordan & Dimakis (2020) to the parameterized setting. On the positive side, the tractability results for input-convex networks leverage the extended formulation machinery of Hertrich & Loho (2024), and the randomized approximation draws on subspace embedding theory (Cohen & Peng, 2015; Bozzai et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The W[1]-hardness and ETH-based lower bounds settle the parameterized complexity of a broad family of neural network verification and geometric problems, showing that the standard brute-force enumeration of linear regions is essentially optimal and that no FPT algorithm exists under standard assumptions. This has direct implications for the design of verification tools: practitioners cannot hope for algorithms whose running time depends only mildly on dimension, and must instead rely on heuristics, structural assumptions (e.g., input convexity), or approximation. The most prominent open question is whether Lp-maximization on zonotopes for p ∈ (1,∞) is FPT in d, and whether 2-layer ReLU positivity is contained in W[1] (which would fully classify its parameterized complexity).
