## Problem

Existing bias-agnostic debiasing methods either rely on empirically tuned hyperparameters for group upweighting or, in the case of CCDB, approximate class-conditional and marginal distributions as single Gaussians. This coarse single-Gaussian assumption fails when intra-class data exhibits multi-modal structure due to hidden bias cues, leaving residual spurious correlations unaddressed. The paper asks whether one can achieve fine-grained, mode-wise distribution matching without bias annotations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard image or text samples with class labels (no bias annotations required). Internally, it produces a C×C hard confusion matrix from a biased model's predictions, which partitions the dataset into C² modes indexed by (bias-prediction, true-class) pairs. The final output is a set of shared per-mode sample weights computed in closed form, used via WeightedRandomSampler during debiased training; no per-sample weight storage is needed.

## Method

The approach has two stages. First, MST (Multi-stage data-Selective reTraining) trains a biased model on a small random subset (γ=10%), then iteratively selects the top-β (50%) highest-confidence samples per class to form an increasingly biased training set, amplifying the model's reliance on spurious cues and yielding a hard confusion matrix that approximates ground-truth group partitions. Second, FG-CCDB uses this confusion matrix to construct discrete multi-modal approximations of both the class-conditional distribution P and the marginal distribution q, then computes mode-level weights W_{i,j} = q_i / P_{i,j} in closed form so that reweighted class-conditional distributions exactly match the marginal. This achieves mutual-information minimization between bias cues and class labels at the mode level, with a weight ratio up to 1000× between extreme modes—far more aggressive than conventional group balancing—while incurring negligible computational overhead since all samples within a mode share a single weight.

## Results

On the three real-world binary classification benchmarks, FG-CCDB (fully bias-agnostic) achieves worst-group accuracy (WGA) of 90.56% on Waterbirds, 89.22% on CelebA, and 78.52% on CivilComments. On CelebA and CivilComments, it surpasses the bias-supervised upper bounds of GroupDRO (88.90% and 73.7%) and DFR (88.30% and 70.1%) by 0.3–4.8 points. On the highly biased multi-class cMNIST (5% bias-conflicting ratio), FG-CCDB reaches 98.21% test accuracy, outperforming CCDB (96.37%) and GroupDRO (84.20%) by 1.8 and 14.0 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FG-CCDB, as a fully bias-agnostic method, outperforms the bias-supervised GroupDRO on CelebA WGA (89.22 vs. 88.90) and CivilComments WGA (78.52 vs. 73.70), and beats DFR on CelebA WGA (89.22 vs. 88.30) and CivilComments WGA (78.52 vs. 70.10), though it trails DFR on Waterbirds WGA (90.56 vs. 92.90). Against the closest bias-agnostic baseline CCDB, FG-CCDB improves WGA by 3.95 points on CelebA (89.22 vs. 85.27) and 3.52 points on CivilComments (78.52 vs. 75.00), while matching it on Waterbirds (90.56 vs. 90.48). On the multi-shortcut UrbanCars benchmark, FG-CCDB achieves the smallest BG+CoObj gap (−4.9) among all bias-agnostic methods, compared to −69.2 for ERM and −58.58 for DaC. On the extreme multi-class cMNIST (0.5% bias-conflicting), FG-CCDB reaches 89.02% accuracy versus 83.20% for CCDB and 63.12% for GroupDRO, a 5.8-point margin over the second-best bias-agnostic method. The ablation confirms that MST alone (plugged into GroupDRO/DFR) recovers most of the supervised performance, and FG-CCDB-sup (using ground-truth bias labels) only marginally improves over the fully unsupervised FG-CCDB, validating that MST is a reliable proxy for human annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct fine-grained extension of CCDB (Zhao et al., 2025), which proposed minimizing mutual information between bias cues and class labels via distribution matching but approximated each distribution as a single Gaussian. The key departure is replacing this coarse Gaussian model with a discrete multi-modal approximation derived from the hard confusion matrix, obtained through a novel Multi-stage data-Selective reTraining (MST) strategy that exploits ERM's tendency to overfit spurious correlations. MST builds on the 'free lunch' insight from SELF, XRM, and Lee et al. that overfitted models align with bias cues, but adds iterative data-selective refinement to improve bias prediction quality. The resulting FG-CCDB performs mode-wise reweighting in closed form, avoiding the iterative optimization and per-sample feature storage required by CCDB.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that FG-CCDB matches or exceeds fully bias-supervised methods (GroupDRO, DFR) on worst-group accuracy across binary and multi-class benchmarks without any human bias annotations, while remaining lightweight (closed-form weights, shared per-mode weights rather than per-sample). A notable limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the overfitting-based bias signal weakens in asymmetric group structures (e.g., CelebA with a single minority group, or CivilComments where majority/minority roles differ across classes), leaving these settings partially unresolved. The method's extension to multi-shortcut scenarios (UrbanCars) and multi-class settings (cMNIST, cCIFAR10) broadens its applicability beyond the binary single-shortcut regime that dominates prior work, though the gap to supervised methods narrows in the most extreme bias-conflicting ratios.
