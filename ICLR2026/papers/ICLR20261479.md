## Problem

Existing LLM reasoning training relies on outcome-based feedback or probabilistic process rewards (token probabilities, learned reward models), neither of which provides formal guarantees that intermediate reasoning steps are logically valid. This allows models to produce correct answers through flawed or unfaithful reasoning, a critical failure mode in high-stakes domains like medicine and law where logical consistency is paramount.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261479/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of natural language premises P and a question Q, and produces a structured reasoning chain formatted as 'Step 1: s1; Step 2: s2; …; Step n: sm; Answer: A', where each step si is decomposed into a pair (Pr, I) consisting of the premises referenced and the inference drawn. The reward is computed at the step level: Premise Validity measures cosine similarity between each referenced premise sentence and the given premises, while Logic Validity is a binary (or confidence-fallback) score from Isabelle theorem-prover verification after autoformalization into Isabelle/HOL.

## Method

LogicReward is a step-level reward function combining Premise Validity (cosine-similarity grounding of referenced premises) and Logic Validity (formal verification via the Isabelle theorem prover), weighted equally with Outcome Validity to produce a final LogicScore in [0,1]. The key technical component is Autoformalization with Soft Unification: an LLM supplements each reasoning step with implicit assumptions to reduce natural-language ambiguity, then parses the result into neo-davidsonian event semantics and converts it to Isabelle/HOL theory for syntactic and semantic checking. When syntax fails, the reward falls back to average token probability of the inference. Training uses a two-stage pipeline—SFT on the highest-LogicScore response per question, followed by DPO on max-vs-min LogicScore pairs—applied via LoRA to Llama3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B. A refinement loop iteratively uses Isabelle error messages to improve soft unification on logically invalid responses, shifting the reward distribution toward higher-quality reasoning.

## Results

On eight NLI and logical reasoning benchmarks, LogicReward-Qwen3-8B achieves 85.5% average accuracy, surpassing o4-mini (83.5%) by 2.0%, GPT-4.1 (75.0%) by 10.5%, and DeepSeek-R1-8B (68.6%) by 16.9%. LogicReward-Llama3.1-8B improves over its base model by 11.0% (60.4% → 71.4%). In a strict same-backbone comparison (Table 2), LogicReward (71.4%) outperforms Confidence (64.3%), PRM (66.0%), and LLM-as-Judge (60.2%) reward systems by 7.1%, 5.4%, and 11.2% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LogicReward-Qwen3-8B (85.5% avg) outperforms all reasoning-model baselines: o4-mini (83.5%) by 2.0%, QwQ-32B (82.6%) by 2.9%, DeepSeek-R1-8B (68.6%) by 16.9%, MiniCPM-8B (59.6%) by 25.9%, Nemotron-8B (54.9%) by 30.6%, and Skywork-o1-8B (41.2%) by 44.3%. Against non-reasoning models, it surpasses GPT-4.1 (75.0%) by 10.5% and GPT-4o (73.9%) by 11.6%. In the strict same-backbone comparison (Table 2, all using Llama3.1-8B-Instruct), LogicReward (71.4%) beats the Nemotron-70B-Reward PRM baseline (66.0%) by 5.4%, the Confidence-based reward (64.3%) by 7.1%, and the LLM-as-Judge reward (60.2%) by 11.2%. The Llama3.1-8B-Instruct base model (60.4%) is improved by 11.0% after LogicReward training. Notably, LogicReward-Qwen achieves the highest scores on 7 of 8 individual benchmarks, with the sole exception being LogiQA where Qwen3-8B base (67.9%) slightly edges out LogicReward-Qwen (63.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neurosymbolic reasoning line (LINC, Logic-LM, SymbCoT, Aristotle) by shifting the theorem prover from an inference-time tool to a training-time reward signal. It builds directly on Quan et al. (2024c) for the autoformalization pipeline (neo-davidsonian event semantics → Isabelle/HOL), but repurposes it to construct SFT and DPO training data rather than to validate individual answers at test time. The key departure from prior process reward models (Lightman et al., Uesato et al., PRMs) is replacing probabilistic step-level signals with a deterministic, symbolically-verified logical validity check, and the Soft Unification mechanism addresses a gap that purely symbolic approaches have not tackled in the ambiguous NLI domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that step-level logical supervision via a theorem prover produces more faithful reasoning (25% higher faithfulness than DeepSeek-R1-8B) and generalizes to unseen tasks like commonsense reasoning, suggesting that enforcing logical consistency during training instills transferable reasoning principles rather than task-specific shortcuts. The finding that LogicReward outperforms other reward systems even without ground-truth labels is practically significant for domains where annotations are unavailable. However, the autoformalization pipeline remains imperfect and computationally expensive, limiting applicability to on-policy reinforcement learning and domains beyond NLI; the authors acknowledge these as key open challenges for scaling the approach.
