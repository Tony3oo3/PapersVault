## Problem

Existing diffusion-based driving world models suffer from identity drift, where the same object changes its appearance or even category across frames (e.g., a bus becoming a truck, a red car turning black). The paper identifies three root causes: the absence of explicit instance identity conditions, instance-unaware attention that leaks information across objects, and uniform frame-level supervision that dilutes foreground focus. These issues severely limit the usability of generated videos for downstream perception, tracking, and planning tasks.

## Input / Output

The model takes 3D bounding box projections (with category, size, and tracking ID per instance), road maps, and text scene descriptions as conditioning signals, injected via a ControlNet-style architecture that duplicates the first 19 MMDiT blocks. Training inputs are 16×256×448 multi-view video clips, and the model can stably generate over 200 temporally consistent frames. The output is a multi-view driving video where each instance maintains stable category, color, and geometry across all frames.

## Method

ConsisDrive introduces two instance-aware mechanisms built on an OpenSora V2.0 / MMDiT backbone. The Instance-Masked Attention (IMA) module constructs per-instance identity embeddings (via CLIP for category, Fourier features for tracking ID and bbox size, fused by an MLP) and applies a 3D self-attention over concatenated visual and identity tokens with two masks: an identity mask that restricts each visual token to attend only to its own instance's embedding, and a trajectory mask that allows cross-frame attention only within the same instance while blocking all cross-instance interactions. The Instance-Masked Loss (IML) computes a binary foreground mask from 3D box projections and element-wise multiplies it with the denoising loss, with a probabilistic strategy (probability α) that randomly falls back to the unmasked loss to preserve background fidelity. The IMA output is injected via a gated residual addition with a learnable scalar initialized to zero, allowing the model to adaptively control the strength of instance conditioning.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, ConsisDrive achieves the best FVD of 37.23 and FID of 3.88, outperforming the closest competitor InstaDrive (FVD 38.06, FID 3.96) and substantially surpassing prior methods like DriveDreamer2 (FVD 55.7, FID 11.2) and MagicDrive-V2 (FVD 94.84, FID 20.91). For downstream perception, training StreamPETR solely on ConsisDrive-generated data reaches 91.3% of Oracle mAP (31.5 vs. 34.5), and augmenting real data with generated videos yields a 7.7-point NDS improvement (54.6 vs. 46.9). On multi-object tracking, ConsisDrive reduces ID switches to 525 (−162 from baseline), marginally outperforming InstaDrive (532) and substantially beating DriveDreamer2 (593).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConsisDrive outperforms all 11 baselines on both FVD and FID in the main generation comparison (Table 1), with the closest margin being over InstaDrive (FVD 37.23 vs. 38.06; FID 3.88 vs. 3.96). On the perception validation task (Table 3), ConsisDrive achieves NDS 41.38 (88.23% of Oracle), beating MagicDrive-V2 (36.82, 78.51%) by 4.56 points and Panacea (32.10, 68.00%) by 9.28 points. For data-augmented perception training (Table 2), ConsisDrive yields a 7.7-point NDS gain over real-data-only training (54.6 vs. 46.9), compared to only 2.3 points for Panacea (49.2 vs. 46.9). On multi-object tracking (Table 4), ConsisDrive achieves the best AMOTA (0.498) and lowest IDS (525), narrowly edging out InstaDrive (AMOTA 0.496, IDS 532) and substantially surpassing DriveDreamer2 (AMOTA 0.313, IDS 593). Notably, ConsisDrive is the only method supporting both multi-view and multi-frame generation, whereas all baselines are limited to single-frame or single-view outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConsisDrive builds on the OpenSora V2.0 video generation stack (Video DC-AE, T5-XXL, CLIP-Large) and the MMDiT double-stream transformer architecture from FLUX, injecting conditional control signals via a ControlNet-style duplication of the first 19 base blocks. Conceptually, it departs from prior driving world models (DriveDreamer2, MagicDrive-V2, Panacea) by identifying three specific failure modes of identity drift—missing instance identity conditions, non-instance-aware attention, and uniform supervision dilution—and addressing each with a targeted architectural or loss-level mechanism. The Instance-Masked Attention module is the most novel architectural contribution, repurposing the standard 3D self-attention in MMDiT with structured binary masks derived from 3D bounding box projections to enforce per-instance token isolation across both spatial and temporal dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Identity drift in synthetic driving videos directly undermines their utility for training perception, tracking, and planning models, making instance-level temporal consistency a practical prerequisite rather than a cosmetic quality metric. The paper's validation that generated data alone recovers 91.3% of real-data perception performance and that data augmentation yields a 7.7-point NDS gain and 162 fewer ID switches provides concrete evidence that identity-preserving generation translates into downstream task benefits. Limitations include evaluation solely on nuScenes at 16×256×448 resolution, dependence on accurate 3D bounding box annotations as conditioning input, and the fact that the FVD/FID margins over the closest competitor (InstaDrive) are narrow. Open questions include whether the masked-attention approach scales to longer horizons (beyond 200 frames), more diverse or crowded scenes, and whether the probabilistic loss masking strategy generalizes to other domains where foreground-background imbalance is extreme.
