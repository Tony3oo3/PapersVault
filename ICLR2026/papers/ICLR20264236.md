## Problem

Existing LLM-based approaches to long document summarization suffer from significant information loss, factual inconsistencies, and coherence degradation across lengthy texts, often missing crucial information or introducing hallucinations. While multi-agent systems have shown promise for complex reasoning tasks, their application to long document summarization remains underexplored, and prior multi-agent summarization methods largely rely on self-verification, which introduces biases and allows errors to go undetected.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single long document as input and produces two outputs: an abstractive summary and a set of 30 quiz questions (10 multiple-choice, 10 true-false, 10 short-answer) with corresponding answers. The quiz is not merely a byproduct but an active quality-check mechanism: an examinee agent attempts to answer the quiz using only the generated summary, and any questions it cannot answer correctly generate feedback that drives iterative refinement of both the summary and the quiz. The process runs for up to T_iter iterations (default 3), terminating early if both summary and quiz feedback sets are empty.

## Method

SUMMQ organizes four agent groups—Summary Generators, Quiz Generators, Summary Reviewers, and Quiz Reviewers—plus an Examinee agent into an adversarial loop where the quiz continuously challenges the summary's coverage and factuality while the summary aims to be comprehensive enough to answer all quiz questions. Generator collaboration follows a four-phase protocol: independent draft generation by multiple agents, aggregation into a unified candidate by a dedicated aggregator, best individual draft selection by a ranker, and collective voting among all generators to choose between the aggregated and best individual candidates. Reviewer collaboration similarly proceeds in four phases: independent annotation, issue categorization into agreed (≥2 reviewers) and contested (<2 reviewers) sets, structured multi-round debate with majority voting to validate contested issues, and consolidation into a final issue list. The Examinee agent answers quiz questions using only the summary, and its failures are merged into both the summary and quiz feedback streams, creating a closed-loop verification that no single agent's self-assessment can bypass.

## Results

SUMMQ_COMBO achieves state-of-the-art results on MENSA (R-1=41.58, R-2=10.96, R-L=18.24, BERTScore-F1=62.76) and BookSum (R-1=44.62, R-2=11.14, R-L=20.38, BERTScore-F1=61.49), outperforming all prompting and multi-agent baselines. On GovReport, SUMMQ_COMBO (R-1=52.79, BERTScore-F1=65.46) surpasses all prompting baselines but trails supervised fine-tuning methods (U.FORMER, SLED, CACHED) that benefit from task-specific training. In LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations (GPT-5), SUMMQ_COMBO wins 88% of head-to-head comparisons against GPT-4o and 82% against O3 on MENSA. Human evaluation confirms these gains, with SUMMQ_COMBO preferred over GPT-4o and O3 at 88% and 82% winning rates respectively, and still winning 65%/60% after length normalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prompting baselines, SUMMQ_COMBO dominates across all three datasets: on MENSA it beats the strongest prompting model GPT-5 by +4.20 R-1 and +2.32 BERTScore-F1; on BookSum it outperforms GPT-5 by +20.64 R-1 (44.62 vs. 23.98); on GovReport it leads GPT-5 by +11.27 R-1 (52.79 vs. 41.52). Against supervised fine-tuning methods, SUMMQ_COMBO wins on MENSA (beating TEXTRANK by +7.21 R-1) and BookSum (beating CACHED by +1.82 R-1), but trails U.FORMER and SLED on GovReport R-1 (52.79 vs. 56.60/57.50) and BERTScore-F1 (65.46 vs. 68.20/66.90), which the authors attribute to those models' large-scale task-specific training. Among multi-agent baselines, SUMMQ_COMBO substantially outperforms HM-SR on MENSA (+7.32 R-1, +2.54 BERTScore-F1) and C.MULTILLM on GovReport (+4.89 R-1). The single-agent SUMMQ_SOLO variant also consistently surpasses all prompting baselines, confirming that the adversarial framework itself (not just multi-agent scaling) drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes multi-agent collaboration primitives (independent drafting, aggregation, collective voting, structured debate) from the LLM multi-agent literature into a novel adversarial dual-task architecture for long document summarization. While prior multi-agent summarization systems (HM-SR, C.MULTILLM, LongAgent) rely on self-verification or single-domain collaboration, SUMMQ introduces a cross-domain adversarial dynamic where quiz generation continuously stress-tests summary quality, and an examinee agent closes the loop by verifying answerability from the summary alone. The framework is thus a conceptual departure from prior work that treats summarization and evaluation as sequential or self-referential, instead coupling them as co-evolving adversarial processes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that adversarial quizzing is a practical and effective mechanism for catching information loss and hallucination in long-document summaries, with the ablation showing substantial drops (R-1 −5.00, BS_F1 −6.40 for SOLO) when the quizzing component is removed. However, the computational cost is a major practical barrier: SUMMQ_COMBO costs ~$14.45 per instance versus $0.18 for single-shot prompting, limiting immediate deployment. Open questions include whether the adversarial dynamic generalizes to other long-form generation tasks (translation, QA), whether cheaper open-source backbones can close the gap with proprietary models, and whether the iterative refinement can be made more sample-efficient to reduce the cost-quality tradeoff.
