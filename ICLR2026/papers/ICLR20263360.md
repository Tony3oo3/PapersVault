## Problem

Existing benchmarks for search-augmented LLMs predominantly evaluate short factual queries where top-ranked search results provide direct answers, requiring only shallow comprehension. This fails to capture the messy reality of web search, where results are often outdated, misleading, or superficially relevant but ultimately unhelpful. SEALQA addresses this gap by introducing a small but extremely challenging benchmark where questions are specifically designed to trigger conflicting, noisy, or unhelpful search results, demanding deeper reasoning to filter inconsistencies and reconcile contradictions.

## Input / Output

Each question is a self-contained fact-seeking prompt (average 31 tokens, max 69) crafted to elicit ambiguous or conflicting search results when entered into a search engine. For SEAL-0 and SEAL-HARD, the model receives the question alone (with or without injected search results via FreshPrompt, Self-Ask, or built-in search). For LONGSEAL, the model additionally receives a set of 13–31 documents (one gold document plus 12–30 hard negatives, randomly ordered) and must identify the relevant evidence. Output is a single factual answer graded by a GPT-4O MINI auto-rater as CORRECT, INCORRECT, or NOT_ATTEMPTED under a relaxed protocol that accepts hedged responses as long as the core answer is present and uncontradicted.

## Method

SEALQA is constructed through a rigorous multi-round curation process: NLP researchers write questions across five categories (advanced reasoning, entity/event disambiguation, temporal tracking, cross-lingual reasoning, false-premise detection), each question is reviewed by two or more graduate-level annotators plus expert approval, and questions are iteratively refined until multiple frontier models fail across 10–15 attempts (yielding the 0% accuracy threshold for SEAL-0). The benchmark spans three flavors: SEAL-0 (111 questions), SEAL-HARD (254 questions including SEAL-0), and LONGSEAL (254 questions paired with up to 50 hard negatives filtered by GPT-4O MINI to prevent answer leakage, with the gold document randomly inserted). Evaluation benchmarks 15+ models across chat, reasoning, and agentic categories, testing with and without search, at multiple reasoning-effort levels to probe test-time scaling, and includes a human baseline (five NLP researchers with open and oracle search access). A key finding is that increasing test-time compute does not reliably improve performance—longer chains of thought over noisy search results can amplify spurious information and reduce accuracy.

## Results

On SEAL-0 (111 questions), GPT-5-HIGH achieves the best accuracy at 43.2% with built-in search and 15.3% without search, while all non-reasoning frontier models (GPT-4.1, GPT-4.0, LLaMA-4-Scout) score 0.0% without search. On SEAL-HARD (254 questions), GPT-5-HIGH reaches 63.8% with search and 37.8% without, compared to o3-HIGH at 32.7% with search. On LONGSEAL, accuracy degrades sharply with more distractors: GPT-4.1-MINI drops from 32.7% (k=12) to 29.5% (k=30), and even with only gold documents (no distractors), no model exceeds 56.7%. Test-time scaling fails to yield reliable gains—o4-mini peaks at 6.3% at low effort then drops to 4.5% at high effort on SEAL-0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5-HIGH is the strongest model on both SEAL-0 (43.2% w/ search) and SEAL-HARD (63.8% w/ search), substantially outperforming o3-HIGH (14.4% and 32.7% respectively) and all non-reasoning models. All non-reasoning frontier models—GPT-4.1, GPT-4.0, LLaMA-4-Scout, and LLaMA-3.1-70B—achieve 0.0% on SEAL-0 without search, confirming the benchmark's extreme difficulty. DeepSeek-R1-671B is the most vulnerable to noisy retrieval: its SEAL-HARD accuracy drops from 22.4% (w/o search) to 11.0% (w/ FreshPrompt), a 11.4-point decline, whereas weaker models like Qwen3-235B-A22B consistently benefit from search (+7.1%). Test-time scaling is unreliable: o4-mini peaks at 6.3% at low reasoning effort on SEAL-0 but declines to 4.5% at high effort, and o3-mini plateaus around 1.8–2.7% across all effort levels. Humans outperform all models on a 50-question subset: average human accuracy is 38.8% (open search) and 50.4% (oracle), versus the best model (o3-HIGH) at 28.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEALQA extends the line of work on time-sensitive and factuality benchmarks (FreshLLMs, RealTimeQA, SimpleQA) by introducing adversarially curated questions specifically designed to trigger conflicting, noisy, or unhelpful search results, rather than relying on synthetic augmentation of existing QA pairs. It also builds on the multi-document QA setup from Liu et al. (2024) for its LONGSEAL variant, adapting the needle-in-a-haystack paradigm to test long-context reasoning under realistic retrieval noise. Unlike prior knowledge-conflict benchmarks (AmbigDocs, WikiContradict, ConflictBank) that isolate specific conflict types, SEALQA unifies ambiguity, misinformation, temporal drift, and noisy retrieval into naturally occurring questions vetted by NLP researchers, positioning itself as a comprehensive stress test for search-augmented reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEALQA reveals that even the latest frontier models (GPT-5 with tools) achieve only 43.2% accuracy on SEAL-0, and that the current test-time scaling paradigm does not reliably translate into gains—performance often plateaus or declines with more reasoning compute. The finding that advanced reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1 are paradoxically more vulnerable to noisy search than simpler models challenges the assumption that stronger reasoning confers robustness to retrieval noise. Open questions include how to architect models that can filter and prioritize relevant evidence in large noisy contexts, whether the test-time scaling failure is specific to adversarial search settings or more general, and how to maintain benchmark validity as models improve and potential data contamination becomes a concern.
