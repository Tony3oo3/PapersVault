## Problem

Existing conditional generation models for longitudinal sequences lack fine-grained control over when a condition takes effect (timing) and which variables it influences (scope). Most methods either operate on univariate sequences or assume the condition alters all variables across all time steps, whereas real-world interventions (e.g., drug administration, surgery) begin at a specific moment and affect only a subset of measurements while the rest of the trajectory remains unchanged. This gap is particularly acute for delayed sequence editing, where a condition is scheduled for a future time step rather than the immediate next one.

## Input / Output

CLEF takes a multivariate longitudinal sequence x_{:,t_0:t_i} (e.g., gene expression profiles or lab test values with heterogeneous units), a condition embedding z_s retrieved from a frozen pretrained model (ESM-2 for transcription factors, or a clinical knowledge graph embedding model for ICD codes), and a target forecast time t_j > t_i. It produces a single-step forecast x̂_{:,t_j}^s at the specified future time, preserving historical data and unaffected variables while editing only the relevant portions. The single-step (non-autoregressive) generation is a key design choice: it avoids compounding error that arises when filling intermediate time steps, which is especially important for irregularly-sampled clinical data with large time gaps.

## Method

CLEF introduces temporal concepts c that encode the trajectory (rate of change) of each variable between time steps t_i and t_j, learned by a concept encoder E that combines a time-delta embedding Δ_{t_i,t_j} with the condition embedding h_s via summation, then element-wise multiplies with the sequence encoder's hidden representation h_x, followed by an optional FNN and GELU activation. The concept decoder G applies these concepts via a single element-wise multiplication c ⊙ x_{:,t_i}, which is unit-agnostic (handling heterogeneous measurement scales) and enables direct user intervention on individual concept dimensions to simulate counterfactual trajectories. The architecture is modular: any sequential encoder (Transformer, xLSTM, MOMENT) and any condition tokenizer can be plugged in, and CLEF can be integrated into balanced representation learning frameworks (CRN, Causal Transformer) for counterfactual prediction under standard causal identifiability assumptions. The GELU activation constrains concepts to non-negative values, interpreting them as multiplicative trajectory rates rather than additive offsets.

## Results

Across 8 datasets (WOT, WOT-CF, eICU, MIMIC-IV, MIMIC-III-CF, Tumor Growth, M5), CLEF improves immediate sequence editing by 16.28% (MAE) on average and delayed sequence editing by 26.73% (MAE) on average over non-CLEF counterparts. In zero-shot counterfactual generation on WOT-CF, CLEF achieves up to 62.84% (MAE) improvement over non-CLEF baselines. For counterfactual prediction under high time-varying confounding (γ≥3), CLEF-CT with CDC loss and CLEF-CRN with GR loss outperform their non-CLEF counterparts, while CLEF without any balancing loss still matches or exceeds the best balanced baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLEF is compared against 9 baselines across three encoder backbones (Transformer, xLSTM, MOMENT) plus VAR. Against non-CLEF Transformer, xLSTM, and MOMENT, CLEF variants consistently achieve lower MAE on both immediate and delayed sequence editing across all biomedical and sales datasets; the improvement is largest for delayed editing (26.73% average MAE reduction). VAR, the traditional linear baseline, performs competitively on immediate editing for WOT but fails catastrophically on delayed editing (MAE reaching 10³⁹ on eICU), while CLEF maintains stable performance. For counterfactual prediction, CLEF-CT and CLEF-CRN outperform their non-CLEF CT and CRN counterparts (Bica et al., 2020; Melnychuk et al., 2022) particularly under high time-varying confounding (γ≥3); notably, CLEF without any explicit balancing loss (GR or CDC) matches or exceeds the best balanced variants, suggesting temporal concepts inherently learn treatment-invariant representations. In zero-shot counterfactual generation on WOT-CF, CLEF outperforms all non-CLEF baselines after the divergence time step, with the gap widening over longer horizons.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLEF sits at the intersection of three prior lines of work: controllable text generation (CTG), which handles immediate editing but cannot skip to arbitrary future time steps; controllable time series generation (CTsG), which uses diffusion but is restricted to univariate sequences and assumes the condition affects the entire trajectory; and estimating counterfactual outcomes over time (ECT), which uses balanced representations but is not generative. CLEF departs from all three by introducing temporal concepts that encode the rate of change between two time steps, enabling multivariate, scope-controlled, single-step generation at any future time. Architecturally, it is a modular plug-in (concept encoder + decoder) that can be attached to any sequence encoder (Transformer, xLSTM, MOMENT) and any condition tokenizer, rather than proposing a monolithic new model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to perform delayed sequence editing in a single step (avoiding compounding autoregressive error) and to directly intervene on learned temporal concepts to simulate 'healthier' or 'sicker' patient trajectories opens a practical path toward virtual patients for in silico clinical experimentation. The finding that CLEF's temporal concepts yield balanced representations without explicit balancing losses (outperforming CRN/CT with GR or CDC) challenges the standard recipe in counterfactual prediction, though this is demonstrated primarily on synthetic/semi-synthetic benchmarks. Open questions include whether the counterfactual inference assumptions (sequential ignorability, positivity) hold in real-world clinical data, how the approach scales to very long or highly irregular trajectories, and whether the concept intervention capability generalizes beyond the T1D case study to broader clinical decision support.
