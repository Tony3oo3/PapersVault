## Problem

Active View Selection (AVS) for 3D reconstruction requires identifying the minimal set of viewpoints that yields the most accurate reconstruction, but existing NeRF- and 3DGS-based AVS methods require costly iterative retraining of the rendering model after each new view is added, making them impractical under time or compute constraints. Additionally, supervised and reinforcement-learning-based AVS approaches rely on fixed discrete candidate viewpoint sets, limiting generalizability to arbitrary viewpoints in new environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262895/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 224×224 RGB image of a 3D object from the current viewpoint and produces a 48-dimensional neural uncertainty map (UMap) encoding reconstruction-error estimates at 48 predefined anchor viewpoints arranged on a spherical surface (radius 2.73 m) in polar coordinates (azimuth φ ∈ [0°,360°], elevation θ ∈ [0°,180°]). At inference, 512 candidate viewpoints are randomly sampled per timestep; the UMap values are interpolated to these candidates via softmax-weighted angular-distance weighting, and the selected next-best-view is then used to render training images for a downstream 3D reconstruction backbone (NeRF or 3DGS).

## Method

PUN comprises two components: (1) UPNet, a lightweight feedforward Vision Transformer (ViT pretrained on ImageNet, fine-tuned with a fully connected head) that directly maps a single input image to a 48-dim UMap, trained via MSE loss on ground-truth UMaps generated by comparing Splatter-Image single-view 3DGS synthesis against Blender ground-truth renders across 62,400 viewpoint-UMap pairs (the NUM dataset, 13 ShapeNet categories × 100 instances × 48 HEALPix-sampled viewpoints); and (2) a next-best-view selection module that aggregates all past UMaps by multiplying interpolated uncertainty values across timesteps at each of 512 random candidate viewpoints, filters candidates whose uncertainty drops below 0.1 at any prior timestep (redundancy suppression), and selects the argmax. This direct image-to-uncertainty-map prediction eliminates the need for iterative neural rendering model retraining at test time, yielding up to 400× speedup and >50% reduction in CPU, RAM, and GPU usage compared to retraining-based baselines, while generalizing to novel object categories, varied lighting, camera distances, and different reconstruction backbones without retraining.

## Results

On NUM-inst (novel instances from known categories), PUN achieves PSNR 33.19, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.025, and MSE 6.96×10⁴, outperforming all baselines and approaching the 40-view upper bound (PSNR 36.47) while using only 20 views. On NUM-cat (novel object categories), PUN reaches PSNR 34.74 and LPIPS 0.019, again surpassing all baselines. On NUM-3DGS-recon (3DGS backbone), PUN achieves PSNR 36.71 and MSE 0.40×10³, substantially outperforming NVF (PSNR 30.67) and A-NeRF (PSNR 26.22). PUN also achieves up to 400× faster viewpoint selection (0.5 min vs. 175 min for NVF) with >50% reductions in CPU, RAM, and GPU usage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUN outperforms all three baselines (WD, A-NeRF, NVF) across every dataset and metric. On NUM-inst, PUN edges out NVF by 0.11 dB PSNR (33.19 vs. 33.08) and 0.003 LPIPS, while A-NeRF trails by 0.48 dB PSNR. The gap widens dramatically on NUM-cat (novel categories), where PUN leads NVF by 1.59 dB PSNR (34.74 vs. 33.15) and A-NeRF by 1.58 dB. On NUM-3DGS-recon, PUN's advantage is most pronounced: 6.04 dB PSNR over NVF (36.71 vs. 30.67) and 10.49 dB over A-NeRF, confirming backbone-agnostic view selection. On realistic scenes (MIP360), PUN improves PSNR by 2.08 dB over NVF (17.49 vs. 15.41) and SSIM by 0.091. Notably, PUN matches the 40-view upper bound in reconstruction quality while using only 20 views, and achieves this with 400× faster selection time (0.5 min vs. 175 min for NVF).

## Relation to Prior Work

PUN carves new ground in the AVS-for-3D-reconstruction space by replacing the dominant two-stage pipeline (train a radiance field, then derive uncertainty from it) with a single feedforward ViT that maps one image directly to a 48-point uncertainty map over a spherical candidate set. It repurposes 3DGS-based single-view synthesis (Splatter-Image) not as the reconstruction backbone but as a data-generation engine for the NUM dataset, and it departs from prior learning-based AVS methods (Mendoza et al., Wang et al.) that operate over fixed discrete candidate sets by sampling 512 continuous viewpoints per timestep. The aggregation strategy—multiplying interpolated uncertainty across all past timesteps and thresholding redundant views—has no direct analogue in the NeRF- or 3DGS-based AVS literature, which typically re-derives uncertainty from scratch after each new view.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 400× speedup and >50% resource reduction make AVS practical for time-constrained settings (robotic exploration, cultural-heritage digitization) where iterative NeRF retraining is infeasible, and the NUM dataset (62,400 viewpoint-UMap pairs across 13 categories) provides a reusable benchmark for uncertainty-map prediction. However, PUN currently assumes a single centered object on a fixed-radius sphere, relies on image-fidelity metrics (PSNR) rather than geometry-aware signals for uncertainty, and has been validated primarily on ShapeNet objects; extending it to unconstrained multi-object scenes, arbitrary camera trajectories, and online single-view reconstruction remains open. The finding that UPNet implicitly learns a backbone-agnostic 'viewpoint difficulty' signal correlated with geometric and texture complexity suggests a broader principle: reconstruction-error landscapes may be predictable from appearance alone, independent of the specific 3D representation.
