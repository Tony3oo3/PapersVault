## Problem

IMO-level geometry problems require creative auxiliary constructions with weak heuristics, making them resistant to the LLM-agent paradigm that has succeeded in other mathematical domains. Current state-of-the-art systems (AlphaGeometry 2, SeedGeometry) rely on expert models trained on hundreds of millions of synthetic examples combined with large-scale search, leaving open whether a data-efficient LLM agent can achieve medalist-level geometry performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261296/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a geometry problem formalized in a domain-specific language (DSL) that defines geometric structures point-by-point. At each of up to 200 interaction steps, it produces a natural-language chain-of-thought followed by a structured DSL action (constructing objects, adding auxiliary points, or proposing a sub-proof goal), which is executed by the InternGeometry-DDAR symbolic engine. The final output is a complete formal proof aggregating all verified propositions and auxiliary constructions, with a dynamic memory module compressing the multi-turn history to fit within the model's context window.

## Method

InternGeometry is an LLM agent (built on InternThinker-32B) that iteratively proposes propositions and auxiliary constructions in natural language, formalizes them as DSL actions, verifies them against an enhanced symbolic engine (InternGeometry-DDAR, extending Newclid with global point optimization and double-point handling), and reflects on the engine's feedback over 200+ steps. A dynamic memory manager compresses the interaction history to preserve key actions and outcomes while discarding verbose reasoning, and a rule-based rejection sampler prevents action collapse by rejecting repeated or degenerate outputs. Training proceeds in two phases: a 7K-example supervised cold start, followed by Complexity-Boosting Reinforcement Learning (CBRL), a multi-stage curriculum where synthetic problems are generated at a controllable difficulty (DDAR proof-step count) and the target difficulty is updated each round to keep the average reward near 0.5, maximizing the expected absolute advantage and thus the GRPO gradient signal. The binary reward is the conjunction of outcome success and per-step effectiveness, requiring only 13K total training examples—0.004% of AlphaGeometry 2's data.

## Results

On IMO 50 (all geometry problems from IMO 2000–2024), InternGeometry solves 44/50 problems under Pass@256, surpassing the average IMO gold medalist score of 40.9. The model achieves this with only 13K training examples—0.004% of AlphaGeometry 2's 300M and 0.006% of SeedGeometry's 230M. InternGeometry also solves the geometry problem from IMO 2025, bringing its total to 45/51 across all evaluated problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InternGeometry (44/50) outperforms both expert-model baselines on IMO 50: it beats AlphaGeometry 2 (42/50) by 2 problems and SeedGeometry (43/50) by 1 problem. Specifically, InternGeometry additionally solves 2018 P6 and 2023 P6 over AlphaGeometry 2, and 2001 P5 and 2009 P4b over SeedGeometry, while missing 2006 P1 that SeedGeometry solves. The data efficiency gap is dramatic: InternGeometry uses 13K training examples versus 300M (AlphaGeometry 2) and 230M (SeedGeometry), a reduction of over 4 orders of magnitude. In terms of inference budget, InternGeometry's Pass@256 with up to 200 agentic steps per trajectory (≈51,200 total steps) is on the same order as a single AlphaGeometry 2 beam tree (16,384 steps) but far below AlphaGeometry 2's full SKEST ensemble, which multiplies that budget across multiple configurations, trees, and models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant expert-model paradigm for geometry (AlphaGeometry, AlphaGeometry2, SeedGeometry), which trains specialized construction-prediction models on hundreds of millions of synthetic examples and pairs them with large-scale search. Instead, it repurposes the LLM-agent-plus-tool paradigm already successful in other mathematical domains (code, formal theorem proving) by building an interactive DDAR engine (InternGeometry-DDAR, extending Newclid) as the symbolic tool and training the agent via GRPO-style RL. The key methodological novelty is the Complexity-Boosting Reinforcement Learning (CBRL) curriculum, which dynamically adjusts synthetic problem difficulty to maximize expected absolute advantage, drawing on recent curriculum-RL work but applying it to a fully automated, controllable geometry data synthesis pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that 13K training examples (0.004% of AlphaGeometry2's data) suffice to surpass both AlphaGeometry2 and SeedGeometry on IMO 50 challenges the assumption that olympiad-level geometry requires massive data synthesis, and suggests the LLM-agent paradigm may generalize to other weak-heuristic reasoning tasks. The CBRL framework—where difficulty is steered by targeting a 0.5 average reward—is a clean, theoretically grounded curriculum that could transfer to other domains with controllable task complexity. Open questions include the high inference cost (Pass@256 vs. AlphaGeometry2's beam search ensemble), the fact that remaining unsolved problems involve non-pure-geometry computations beyond DDAR's expressive range, and whether the approach extends to 3D geometry or other formal domains.
