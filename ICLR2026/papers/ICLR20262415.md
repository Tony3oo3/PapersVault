## Problem

Prior work on the Platonic Representation Hypothesis has evaluated cross-modal alignment almost exclusively on static modalities (images and text), leaving the temporal domain of video unexplored. Additionally, previously reported alignment scores (~0.16) were ambiguous: it was unclear whether they reflected genuinely poor alignment or simply impoverished test-time data (single frames, single captions). This paper addresses both gaps by conducting the first systematic study of video-text representation alignment and demonstrating that test-time data richness is a major driver of alignment quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes video clips (processed as sequences of n_f frames, where sub-clips of n_o frames are encoded and averaged) paired with sets of n_c text captions (concatenated into a single string and encoded, with per-token features averaged). A key detail is that alignment is computed by jointly optimizing over intermediate layers of both the vision and text encoders, selecting the layer pair that maximizes the Mutual k-NN score, rather than using only final-layer embeddings. The primary output is the Mutual k-NN alignment score, from which per-model-pair scaling law parameters (S_∞, C_f, C_c, α, β) are fitted; secondary outputs include correlations with downstream task accuracy and temporal sensitivity analyses.

## Method

The core contribution is extending the Mutual k-NN alignment metric to multi-frame video and multi-caption settings, revealing that alignment improves dramatically (up to ~0.4) simply by increasing test-time data without any retraining. The paper proposes a saturation-based parametric scaling law, score(n_f, n_c) = S_∞ − (C_f·n_f^(−α) + C_c·n_c^(−β)), which fits empirical data with R² > 0.98 and decomposes the alignment gap into independent visual and textual error terms. The fitted parameters are highly informative: VideoMAEv2's frame coefficient C_f (~0.15) is nearly triple DINOv2's (~0.05), quantifying the video model's superior ability to exploit temporal information, while the exponent α is smaller for video models (slower saturation) reflecting higher effective temporal dimensionality. The paper provides two theoretical justifications for the power-law form: one via manifold approximation theory (where α = 2/d_v relates to the encoder's perceived intrinsic dimensionality) and another via Zipfian feature-weight distributions in natural data. The evaluation spans 121 models (85 vision, 30 language) on VATEX, PVD, Test of Time, and VideoComp, and includes a temporal sensitivity analysis showing that LLMs behave more like bag-of-words while video models encode temporal ordering.

## Results

On VATEX with Gemma 2-9B-it as text encoder, VideoMAEv2-Huge achieves the highest video-text alignment score of 0.403 (Mutual k-NN), outperforming DINOv2-Giant (0.354) and CLIP-Huge-IN12k (0.285). The proposed test-time scaling law (score = S∞ − (Cf·nf^−α + Cc·nc^−β)) fits with R² = 0.9791 for VideoMAEv2 and R² = 0.9964 for DINOv2, with VideoMAEv2's frame coefficient Cf = 0.15 being nearly 3× that of DINOv2 (Cf = 0.05), confirming superior temporal information utilization. Scaling from 1 to 10 captions improves alignment by ~60% on average (linear fit y = 1.6x − 0.018, r = 0.998), and multi-frame video input pushes image-model alignment from ~0.18 to ~0.223.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoMAEv2-Huge (self-supervised, no text supervision) achieves the highest alignment (0.403) among all 85 vision models tested, surpassing DINOv2-Giant (0.354), CLIP-Huge-IN12k (0.285), and Timesformer-Base (0.294), demonstrating that native video encoders outperform strong image encoders in text alignment. The scaling law analysis reveals VideoMAEv2's frame coefficient (Cf = 0.146) is ~3× DINOv2's (Cf = 0.046), while their caption coefficients are comparable (Cc ≈ 0.13), confirming video models exploit temporal information more effectively. Image models with naive temporal averaging (8-frame mean) reach only ~0.223, well below video models, and pure single-frame image models cap at ~0.18–0.20. Among text encoders, Gemma 2-9B-it consistently yields the best alignment across all vision models, outperforming T5, BLOOM, Llama, and earlier Gemma variants. On downstream tasks, alignment scores correlate strongly with semantic performance (SSv2, Kinetics-700) and non-semantic perception (depth, camera pose, box tracking), but show weak correlation with point tracking, suggesting alignment is a useful but incomplete proxy for general-purpose video representation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) from Huh et al. (2024) and Maniparambil et al. (2024b), which established emergent cross-modal alignment for static image-text pairs, into the temporal domain by probing video-text alignment. It adapts the mutual k-NN metric from Huh et al. (2024) to handle multi-frame video sequences and multi-caption text, and draws on the self-supervised video representation learning literature (VideoMAEv2, V-JEPA 2, DINOv2/DINOv3) as the subject of study. The test-time scaling law formulation is conceptually analogous to training-time compute-optimal scaling laws (Hoffmann et al., 2022; Kaplan et al., 2020) but applied to inference-time data quantity rather than model size or dataset size, with a theoretical justification grounded in manifold approximation theory and Zipfian feature statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive (121-model) evaluation of video-text alignment and introduces a practical zero-shot metric for probing video representation quality, potentially reducing the need for expensive task-specific decoder training during model development. The finding that alignment scores nearly double with richer test-time data directly answers an open question raised in Huh et al. (2024) and suggests prior alignment scores were artificially depressed by data poverty. Open questions remain: why point tracking shows weak correlation with alignment, how to harness generative video model representations (which show weak text alignment), and whether cross-model (video-video) alignment is a more informative metric than cross-modal alignment. The temporal analysis also reveals that current LLMs encode temporal ordering as bag-of-words while video models capture genuine temporal structure, highlighting a gap in temporal reasoning for language models.
