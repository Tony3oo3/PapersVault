## Problem

Existing multimodal browsing benchmarks like MMSearch rarely demand sustained, fine-grained visual reasoning, as many tasks can be solved by a single strong image search followed by text-only heuristics, allowing unimodal LLMs to perform competitively without genuine vision-in-the-loop verification. This creates a large difficulty gap compared to text-only browsing benchmarks like BrowseComp, where state-of-the-art MLLMs score below 2%, indicating that current multimodal benchmarks do not stress-test the iterative, provenance-aware search behavior that real-world multimodal tasks require.

## Input / Output

Each task provides one or more RGB images (441 unique images across 311 tasks) paired with a short text question (averaging 12.5 words), plus a set of human-verified Set-of-Mark bounding boxes with unique indices overlaid on the image. The agent produces a short text answer (averaging 3.7 words), evaluated via GPT-4o LLM-as-a-judge against a predefined set of acceptable answer variants. The search agent operates under a 20-round cap (10 for Qwen), with each round allowing multiple text queries (3–5) or exactly one image-search query, and the SoM module adds zoom_in(index) and image_search(index) as additional tool calls.

## Method

The paper introduces Spatial-Temporal Extrapolation, a curation procedure that constructs questions requiring models to infer facts physically absent from the image—spatial extrapolation targets unseen entities (off-screen participants, occluded signage) while temporal extrapolation probes events immediately before or after the depicted moment (lineups, next play, subsequent episode). Questions are sourced from video keyframes and arXiv paper figures, then adversarially filtered by testing solvability on at least two closed-source MLLMs and applying image perturbation (blurring/masking critical subregions) to eliminate parametric shortcuts. The evaluation framework is a model-agnostic web agent that interleaves SerpApi text and image search with a Set-of-Mark zoom-in pathway, where human-curated bounding boxes enable region-seeded retrieval (zoom_in crops a subregion; image_search launches a targeted search on that crop), supporting provenance-aware 'thinking with images' without requiring the MLLM to propose its own visually precise crops.

## Results

On the 311-task MMSearch-Plus benchmark, the strongest system (o3 with Full Rollout + SoM) achieves 37.6% end-to-end accuracy, while the open-source Qwen-2.5-VL-72B-Instruct reaches only 7.1% under the same setting. Adding Set-of-Mark (SoM) to Full Rollout yields consistent gains across all models: +1.6 pts for o3 (36.0→37.6), +3.9 pts for Gemini-2.5-Pro (23.8→27.7), and +1.0 pt for Qwen (6.1→7.1). On the MMSearch+ lite subset (239 tasks unsolvable without search), o3 with text search alone achieves 31.4%, indicating that current MLLMs still struggle to exploit fine-grained image search effectively even when parametric shortcuts are removed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source MLLMs, o3 leads with 37.6% (Full Rollout + SoM), narrowly ahead of GPT-5 at 35.4% and Gemini-2.5-Pro at 27.7%; all three substantially outperform the human browser baseline (22.8%). The open-source Qwen-2.5-VL-72B-Instruct trails far behind at 7.1%, and notably regresses from 13.5% (Image Search) to 6.1% (Full Rollout), suggesting it cannot effectively coordinate multi-step tool use. SoM provides the largest absolute gain for Gemini-2.5-Pro (+3.9 pts), consistent with its higher P(image|zoom) transition probability (25.4% vs. 2.9% for o3), indicating Gemini more effectively converts zoomed regions into targeted image-search queries. On the MMSearch+ lite subset, o3 with text search alone (31.4%) outperforms its own Full Rollout (27.3%), revealing that current models still underutilize fine-grained image search even when internal-knowledge shortcuts are eliminated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal browsing benchmark line initiated by MMSearch (Jiang et al., 2024) and positioned against text-only browsing benchmarks like BrowseComp (Wei et al., 2025), arguing that prior multimodal benchmarks collapse to image-source cross-validation where a single strong image search suffices. Its key methodological departure is the Spatial-Temporal Extrapolation curation procedure, which constructs questions requiring agents to propagate fine-grained visual cues (micro-text, broadcast overlays, layout signatures) through iterative retrieval to out-of-image facts, rather than relying on holistic entity matching. The agent framework adapts Set-of-Mark (Yang et al., 2023) from a static prompting technique into a dynamic zoom-and-retrieve pathway, enabling region-seeded image search that prior browsing agents did not support. It is concurrent with but distinct from MM-BrowseComp (Li et al., 2025b) and BrowseComp-VL (Geng et al., 2025), differing in data sources and in emphasizing sustained part-based visual reasoning over entity-level matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMSearch-Plus establishes a rigorous, 311-task stress test that reveals a substantial capability gap: even the strongest evaluated agent (o3) reaches only 36% end-to-end accuracy, and the open-source Qwen-2.5-VL-72B scores just 13.5%, confirming that current MLLMs cannot reliably coordinate fine-grained visual extraction with multi-step web retrieval. The finding that easy-split accuracy *drops* under full rollout because models miscalibrate their tool-use policy (skipping image search when they overestimate their own visual understanding) points to a concrete, addressable failure mode in agent design beyond simply adding more tools. Open questions include how to train or prompt agents to better calibrate when visual retrieval is necessary, how to handle the unavoidable temporal drift as models internalize previously novel entities, and whether the human-curated SoM boxes can be replaced by robust automatic detectors for long-tail entities. The benchmark is limited to image-text browsing (no video or dynamic UI) and is English-centric, leaving significant scope for extension.
