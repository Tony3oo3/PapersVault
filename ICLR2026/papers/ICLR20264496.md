## Problem

Large Multimodal Models are increasingly deployed for scientific research, yet no existing benchmark tests whether they can detect and resolve subtle inconsistencies across text, figures, tables, and equations within full scientific papers. Prior benchmarks either isolate single modalities, rely on synthetic errors that are too obvious, or focus on abstract-level QA rather than cross-modal contradictions. This gap leaves it unclear whether LMMs can serve as trustworthy scientific assistants capable of catching the kind of errors that undermine reproducibility and clarity.

## Input / Output

The benchmark presents models with scientific paper content at three granularity levels: Focused (cropped visual elements plus precise text passages), Page (144 DPI rasterized full pages), and Document (entire paper segmented into 5 collage images of n/5 pages each in a 3-column grid). Answer options are rendered in structured JSON format rather than free-form natural language — an Evidence-Claim schema for the Identification task and a Target-Action schema for the Remedy task — to suppress linguistic shortcuts. The model outputs a single letter (A–D); for reasoning-enabled models, the answer is extracted from within  tags before scoring.

## Method

PRISMM-Bench is constructed through a six-stage pipeline: (1) sourcing 18,009 reviews from rejected/withdrawn ICLR 2024 & 2025 submissions on OpenReview, (2) LLM-assisted filtering with Mistral Nemo to extract 6,056 candidate inconsistencies, (3) manual annotation via a custom web tool yielding 384 verified inconsistencies across 353 papers in 15 categories, (4) LMM-based generation of four-option MCQs using Gemini 2.5 Flash, (5) manual verification of all questions, and (6) LLM-based conversion of answer options into structured JSON to debias evaluation. The benchmark comprises three tiered tasks — Inconsistency Identification (detect the error), Inconsistency Remedy (propose the fix), and Inconsistency Pair Match (identify which two visual elements conflict, restricted to 192 visual-visual cases) — evaluated across 21 LMMs. The key methodological contribution is the JSON-based debiasing: converting free-form NL options into uniform structured representations reduces no-context accuracy from 57.6% to 34.0% for Gemini 2.5 Flash, forcing models to rely on visual evidence rather than surface-level linguistic patterns, as confirmed by a user study showing humans achieve near-chance (27.5%) without context while LMMs maintain up to 70.1%.

## Results

On PRISMM-Bench, the strongest proprietary model GPT-5 (high reasoning) achieves the highest average accuracy of 53.9%, followed by Gemini 2.5 Pro at 52.8% and GPT-5 (minimal) at 44.0%. The best open-weight model, GLM 4.5V 106B, reaches 42.6%, while the weakest model (Gemma 3 4B) scores only 27.8%, yielding a range of 27.8–53.9% across all 21 evaluated LMMs. Performance degrades consistently as context scope expands from Focused to Page to Document, and Remedy scores are uniformly lower than Ident scores, indicating that proposing corrections is harder than merely detecting inconsistencies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Proprietary models substantially outperform all open-weight counterparts: GPT-5 (high) leads at 53.9% average, Gemini 2.5 Pro follows at 52.8%, and GPT-5 (minimal) at 44.0%, while the best open-weight model GLM 4.5V 106B reaches only 42.6%. Among open-weight models, reasoning-enabled variants consistently outperform their non-reasoning predecessors (e.g., InternVL3.5 8B at 37.7% vs. InternVL3 8B at 32.7%), and disabling chain-of-thought reduces accuracy by up to 25.7 points (InternVL3.5 38B). Scaling alone does not guarantee improvement: LLaVA OV 72B (30.5%) underperforms much smaller models like InternVL3.5 8B (37.7%), and high-resolution specialists (VILA HD 4K 8B at 29.4%, InternLM XC 2.5 7B at 28.2%) show no advantage in extended-context settings. On the Match task specifically, Gemma 3 12B achieves 63.5%, rivaling proprietary models, while the much larger InternVL3 78B lags at 45.3%, suggesting architectural design matters more than scale for relational reasoning. All models degrade toward random chance (25%) under Document context, with even GPT-5 (high) dropping from 63.8% (Focused) to 46.9% (Document) on Ident.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior scientific document benchmarks (DocVQA, ChartQA, DVQA, QASPER) that isolate individual visual elements or rely on abstract-level questions, and from the closest prior work MMIR (Yan et al., 2025) which injects synthetic inconsistencies into slides and posters. PRISMM-Bench instead sources real, human-identified cross-modal inconsistencies from OpenReview peer reviews of ICLR submissions, grounding evaluation in authentic scholarly critique rather than artificial perturbations. Methodologically, it adapts the normalized Perceptual Score (Gat et al., 2021) into a Visual Reliance Ratio to quantify visual grounding, and borrows the document-collage protocol from MMLongBench-Doc (Ma et al., 2024) for long-context evaluation. The JSON-based answer debiasing draws on structured semantic parsing (FrameNet, AMR) to suppress the choice-only shortcuts documented in MCQ evaluation literature (Chandak et al., 2025; Balepur et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even GPT-5 (high reasoning) caps at 53.9% and that a substantial fraction of model accuracy on natural-language MCQs is attributable to linguistic shortcuts rather than genuine visual reasoning has broad implications for how the field evaluates multimodal capabilities. The JSON debiasing technique offers a practical, generalizable remedy that other MCQ-based benchmarks can adopt. Key limitations include restriction to AI-domain ICLR papers (2024–2025) and rejected/withdrawn submissions only, leaving open whether findings generalize to other fields, accepted papers, or larger-scale inconsistency sets. The central open question is whether architectural advances in reasoning and long-range grounding can close the gap to human-level reliability (77.5% focused, 65.0% document) needed for trustworthy scientific assistants.
