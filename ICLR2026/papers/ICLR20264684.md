## Problem

Neural operators for PDE surrogate modeling depend heavily on expensive solver-generated solution labels, yet industrial workflows contain vast collections of candidate geometries (meshes) that remain unsolved due to computational cost. Existing operator pretraining strategies are physics-aware and still require PDE solutions, leaving this abundant geometry-only resource underexploited. The paper asks whether physics-agnostic self-supervised pretraining on unlabeled geometries can produce latent representations that improve downstream operator learning under label scarcity.

## Input / Output

The method takes point clouds (mesh nodes, N points in R^d) as geometry input and query coordinates (either mesh points or uniform random samples in the domain) at which the PDE solution is predicted. During pretraining, the VAE decoder reconstructs a binary occupancy field (inside/outside) at query points, making the latent representation reside in function space rather than point space. The neural operator outputs scalar PDE solution values at the query points, with the occupancy value optionally concatenated to query coordinates as an additional input channel.

## Method

The framework has two stages: (1) a perceiver-based point cloud VAE is pretrained on geometry-only data (D ∪ D′) using occupancy field reconstruction as a physics-agnostic proxy task, where the encoder aggregates point cloud information via cross-attention into M learnable tokens, projects them into a probabilistic latent space, and the decoder predicts occupancy at arbitrary query points with a BCE + KL objective; (2) a transformer-based neural operator (GNOT, Transolver, or LNO) is trained on the frozen latent tokens from the pretrained encoder to predict PDE solutions from limited physics-labeled data. The key design choices are that the proxy task must be computationally cheap (no PDE solving), sampling-invariant, and consistent with operator learning, and that the latent representation lives in function space to mitigate discretization artifacts of point clouds. An error decomposition analysis formalizes the benefit: the representation error term O(C_E/√N_geo) shrinks with more unlabeled geometries, while the estimation error from physics data remains unchanged.

## Results

Across four PDE datasets and three transformer-based neural operators, physics-agnostic pretraining via occupancy-field VAE consistently reduces relative L2 error (×10⁻²) on normalized fields. The most dramatic gains appear for LNO, where pretraining cuts error by roughly 50% on Stress (26.5→13.6 mesh, 20.0→11.6 random) and Inductor 3D (24.9→9.2 mesh, 20.3→13.0 random). For GNOT and Transolver, improvements are more moderate but still consistent (e.g., GNOT on Electrostatics: 4.2→3.3 mesh, 4.6→3.4 random; Transolver on AirfRans random: 15.0→10.8). The only near-tie or slight regression is GNOT on Inductor 3D mesh query (7.0→7.1), attributed to the small occupancy volume of the 3D iron core.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks three transformer-based neural operators (GNOT, Transolver, LNO) and one additional operator (UPT, in Appendix D) with and without the proposed physics-agnostic VAE pretraining. GNOT benefits from pretraining in 7 of 8 settings, with the sole exception being Inductor 3D mesh query where error is essentially unchanged (7.0 vs. 7.1). Transolver shows consistent improvement across all 8 settings, with the largest relative gain on AirfRans random query (15.0→10.8, a 28% reduction). LNO, the weakest baseline in absolute terms, sees the most dramatic absolute improvements: Stress mesh drops from 26.5 to 13.6 and Inductor 3D mesh from 24.9 to 9.2, suggesting the pretrained latent representation is especially valuable when the operator architecture struggles with raw point-cloud inputs. UPT (simplified implementation) also benefits substantially, with Stress mesh error halving from 26.3 to 12.8. In all cases, the pretrained encoder is frozen during operator training, so the gains come purely from the quality of the geometry representation rather than additional training capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant physics-aware operator pretraining paradigm (which requires PDE solution labels for reconstruction or autoregressive pretraining) by introducing a physics-agnostic pretraining stage that operates solely on geometry-only data. It repurposes the point cloud VAE architecture from 3DShape2VecSet (Zhang et al., 2023) as a frozen geometry encoder, with occupancy field reconstruction serving as a self-supervised proxy task, and plugs the resulting latent tokens into transformer-based neural operators (GNOT, Transolver, LNO). The error decomposition in Appendix B provides a theoretical lens connecting representation error reduction via abundant unlabeled geometries to downstream operator accuracy, a framing not present in prior operator pretraining work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a concrete industrial bottleneck—geometry designs are cheap to generate while PDE solver labels are expensive—by showing that abundant unlabeled meshes can meaningfully improve operator accuracy without any additional simulation cost. Consistent gains across four PDE problems and three operator architectures (with LNO showing near-halved errors on some settings) suggest the approach is broadly applicable rather than architecture-specific. Open questions include whether occupancy is the optimal proxy for non-CFD problems, how the method extends to multi-material or interacting geometries, and whether deeper integration (joint fine-tuning, adapter-based conditioning) between pretraining and operator stages could yield further gains.
