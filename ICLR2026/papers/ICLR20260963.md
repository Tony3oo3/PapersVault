## Problem

Preference-aligned LLMs are trained on dominant, average preferences, creating a coverage gap where individual, out-of-distribution (OOD) user preferences receive unreliable responses. Existing remedies require costly retraining and may still fail to generalize across the full preference spectrum. The paper asks whether robustness to OOD preferences can be improved purely at inference time, without any model modification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260963/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x and a 2D unit preference direction vector v_target = (cos θ, sin θ) specifying a helpfulness–verbosity trade-off (θ ∈ [10°, 45°] in experiments). It produces a single selected response y* chosen from k=5 candidates. The preference vector is consumed via a standardized system prompt that encodes integer weights (0–100) satisfying the unit-circle constraint, and a separate reward model (RewardModel-Mistral-7B-for-DPA-v1) scores each candidate's helpfulness and verbosity to drive the final selection.

## Method

Robust Preference Selection (RPS) is a training-free, three-phase inference-time procedure: (1) Neighborhood Construction samples k preference directions within an angular radius θ_max = 30° of the target; (2) Multi-Directional Generation produces one response per neighborhood direction, leveraging the model's stronger performance in better-trained regions; (3) Consensus Selection scores all k candidates against the original target preference v_target and returns the argmax. The key insight is that generating from nearby in-distribution directions yields a stochastically superior candidate pool compared to repeated sampling from the OOD target itself. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that under an OOD performance degradation assumption and a Lipschitz-type local consistency condition, the expected best score from the neighborhood pool strictly dominates that of a k-sample baseline at the target direction, via first-order stochastic dominance of the max-of-k distributions.

## Results

RPS achieves a win rate above 50% against the single-direction baseline across all nine model–dataset pairings, with average win rates ranging from 52.0% (SFT on UltraFeedback) to 65.4% (SFT on HelpSteer2). The advantage grows substantially as target preferences become more out-of-distribution: at the most challenging 45° direction, DPA reaches 69.1% on UltraFeedback and 68.8% on HelpSteer2, while SFT on HelpSteer2 peaks at 94.3%. Human evaluation (AMT) and a second LLM judge (Claude Sonnet 4.5) corroborate these trends, with RPS preferred in the majority of pairwise comparisons across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is a single-direction multi-sample strategy (Wang et al., 2022): generate k=5 responses by repeatedly sampling from the target preference direction and select the best via reward-model scoring. RPS wins against this baseline in all nine model–dataset combinations, with no ties or losses. The margin is modest for DPO (≈52–54% average win rate) and SFT on UltraFeedback (52.0%), but grows sharply for DPA (up to 69.1% at 45° on UltraFeedback) and SFT on HelpSteer2 (up to 94.3% at 45°). The key pattern is that the RPS advantage widens monotonically with preference angle for DPA, confirming the OOD degradation assumption, while DPO and SFT show a flatter angle-dependence, suggesting their intrinsic robustness partially offsets the coverage gap. Compute parity is maintained: both methods generate exactly k=5 candidates, with identical peak VRAM (~14.7 GB) and per-generation latency (~16.8 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Directional Preference Alignment (DPA) by Wang et al. (2024a), adopting its multi-axis reward space, reward model, and preference vector formulation. Conceptually, it repurposes the self-consistency principle from Wang et al. (2022) — sample multiple candidates, select the best — but applies it in preference-space rather than reasoning-space, sampling from a local neighborhood of preference directions rather than repeated sampling from a single direction. It departs from training-time robustness approaches (DRO-based retraining, data augmentation) and from inference-time alignment methods that manipulate internal model states (activation steering, token-level guidance), positioning itself as a purely black-box, preference-space operation that requires no model modification or auxiliary guidance models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RPS offers a practical, zero-overhead (training-free, compute-parity) mechanism to mitigate the preference coverage gap, which is a real deployment concern as users express increasingly individualized preferences. The main limitation is that all experiments are confined to a 2D helpfulness-verbosity space; the authors acknowledge that scaling the neighborhood construction to truly high-dimensional multi-attribute preferences remains open. The theoretical guarantee is conditional on Assumption 1 (OOD degradation) and a local consistency condition, both of which are empirically supported but not universally guaranteed (e.g., hard safety thresholds could violate local consistency). The modest gains for DPO models (~52%) suggest the method's utility is inversely related to the base model's intrinsic robustness, raising the question of when RPS is worth the k× inference cost.
