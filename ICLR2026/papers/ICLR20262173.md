## Problem

Existing PTM-based class-incremental learning methods (prompt-based, representation-based, model mixture-based) rely on increasingly complex architectural designs that perform well on general-domain benchmarks but falter in medical imaging, where low inter- and intra-class variability combined with high inter-domain shifts from scanners and protocols cause representation collapse and domain misalignment. The paper asks whether minimalist representation recalibration strategies—often dismissed in general-domain CIL for their modest gains—can be remarkably effective for adapting PTMs in medical settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D medical images (CT scans, X-rays, blood smear slides, dermatoscopic skin lesion images, and histopathology patches) resized to 224×224 pixels as input and produces a class label over all classes accumulated across incremental sessions. In the class-incremental protocol, new disjoint class sets are introduced sequentially across T sessions, and the model must classify over the growing union of all previously seen classes without access to prior data (replay-free). The only gradient-based training occurs in the first session; all subsequent sessions require only closed-form analytic classifier updates on recursively accumulated second-order statistics.

## Method

RA-LDL is a three-step minimalist pipeline built on a frozen ViT-B/16-IN21K backbone. Step (a) optionally inserts a ViT-Adapter (bottleneck dimension 64) into the MLP blocks, trained only in the first session to bridge the domain gap while preserving PTM generalizability. Step (b) calibrates features via a frozen Random Anchor projection (Gaussian-initialized matrix B ∈ R^{d0×d1} with d1=5d0, followed by ReLU) that expands features into a higher-dimensional space preserving pairwise geometry per the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma, complemented by a trainable Low-Rank Projection (two matrices W1 ∈ R^{d0×r}, W2 ∈ R^{r×d1} with r=64 and GELU activation) trained only in the first session to correct domain-specific residual distortions. Step (c) constructs the classifier analytically via ridge regression: the closed-form solution W_adc = (G + βI)^{-1} C_p, where G is the accumulated feature Gram matrix and C_p is the class-accumulated feature matrix, acts as a whitening-like reweighting that suppresses dominant shared eigendirections and decorrelates class prototypes, reducing inter-class correlation without iterative optimization.

## Results

On four medical CIL benchmarks, RA-LDL (Adapted ViT w/B&LRP) achieves AccLast of 70.60% on MedMNIST-Sub, 62.49% on Skin8, 88.04% on COVID (CT&X-rays), and 97.76% on Blood, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline MOS by 8.8–17.6 percentage points in AccLast. The method approaches the joint-training upper bound (73.61%, 67.73%, 92.43%, 99.61% respectively) while requiring gradient-based optimization only in the first session. On general-domain benchmarks (10 tasks), RA-LDL achieves 91.55% AccLast on CIFAR-100, 60.51% on ImageNet-A, 89.14% on CUB-200, and 78.17% on ImageNet-R, matching or exceeding recent PTM-based CIL methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RA-LDL outperforms all 13 baselines on every medical dataset. Against the strongest competitor MOS (model mixture), RA-LDL gains +18.8 pp AccLast on MedMNIST-Sub (70.60 vs 51.80), +10.7 pp on Skin8 (62.49 vs 51.77), +7.4 pp on COVID (88.04 vs 80.60), and +7.6 pp on Blood (97.76 vs 90.18). Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CodaPrompt) and model-mixture LAE suffer severe degradation on medical data, with AccLast dropping below 30% on MedMNIST-Sub and below 25% on Skin8, confirming the paper's claim that complex routing/ensemble mechanisms collapse under medical domain shifts. Representation-based baselines (SimpleCIL, ADAM-Adapter, SSIAT, EASE) perform moderately but remain 10–25 pp below RA-LDL in AccLast, indicating that adapter tuning and prototype mapping alone are insufficient without explicit decorrelation. Replay-based methods (FOSTER, iCaRL, DER, ACL) achieve competitive results on Blood but raise privacy concerns and still trail RA-LDL by 7–16 pp on the other three datasets. Notably, RA-LDL approaches the joint-training upper bound within 3–5 pp on three of four datasets, suggesting that minimalist representation recalibration nearly closes the gap to the oracle setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

RA-LDL is positioned as a deliberate departure from the increasing architectural complexity in PTM-based CIL (prompt pools, multi-adapter ensembles, model mixtures), instead reviving and combining three lightweight ideas: frozen random anchor projections (from RANPAC/DS-AL), a single-session low-rank residual correction, and the closed-form ridge-regression classifier from ACIL. The pipeline is structured as a modification of the SimpleCIL frozen-backbone + prototype paradigm, adding representation calibration between feature extraction and classification. The paper's key conceptual departure is arguing that the complexity added by recent methods (prompt routing, prototype reconstruction, adapter retrieval) is not merely unnecessary but actively harmful in medical CIL, where low intra-class variability and high inter-domain shifts cause those mechanisms to collapse or misalign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first systematic evidence that medical-specific PTMs (UniMedCLIP, RAD-DINO) do not consistently outperform general-domain ViT-B/16-IN21K for CIL, challenging the assumption that domain-specialized pretraining is automatically superior. The practical deployment advantage is significant: only the first session requires gradient-based optimization, and subsequent tasks need only recursive updates of two compact statistics matrices (Gram matrix and class-accumulated feature matrix), which also offers inherent privacy preservation over rehearsal-based methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric data, segmentation, and detection tasks, and whether the moderate order-sensitivity observed on heterogeneous datasets (MedMNIST-Sub) can be further mitigated. The paper also acknowledges that performance remains bounded by PTM quality, so gains from better foundation models will compound with this framework.
