## Problem

Assessing the aesthetic quality of graphic design remains underexplored for vision-language models. Existing benchmarks cover only narrow subsets of design principles (e.g., omitting typography or graphics), use coarse evaluation protocols that cannot localize flaws, and no systematic comparison across proprietary, open-source, and reasoning-augmented VLMs has been conducted. Additionally, no training data exists to improve VLM performance in this domain.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a graphic design image (sourced from the Crello dataset with controlled perturbations) with a task prompt that includes a detailed explanation of the target indicator, plus optional JSON metadata containing element-level layout, font, and color information. The three tasks produce different output formats: a binary yes/no for aesthetic judgment, a single-letter index (A–D) selecting among four candidate bounding boxes for region selection, and either exact bbox coordinates or the token 'None' for precise localization. The training data additionally includes indicator-grounded reasoning paths that embed bbox coordinates alongside textual explanations linking abstract design indicators to concrete spatial regions.

## Method

The paper introduces AesEval-Bench, a benchmark spanning four dimensions (layout, font, color, graphics) and twelve indicators, with three fully quantifiable tasks evaluated via accuracy and bbox IoU. Designs are constructed by applying controlled perturbations (position shifts, font swaps, color changes, image replacements) at the JSON metadata level of the Crello dataset, followed by human-in-the-loop verification with majority voting. For training, the authors propose two key mechanisms: human-guided VLM labeling, where a small set of human annotations serve as in-context demonstrations to instruct powerful VLMs in producing binary labels at scale, and indicator-grounded reasoning, where reasoning paths explicitly tie abstract indicators (e.g., hierarchy, layering) to concrete bbox coordinates with textual explanations of their relevance. Fine-tuning is performed on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with full-parameter training (frozen vision encoder, bfloat16, FlashAttention-2, lr=1e-6, cosine schedule), using both the reasoning path and task label as supervision, yielding consistent gains of 5.97%, 2.70%, and 17.17% across the three tasks.

## Results

On AesEval-Bench, the strongest VLM (GPT-5) achieves only 72.5% accuracy on aesthetic judgment, 69.9% on region selection, and 0.199 IoU on precise localization, revealing substantial gaps in design aesthetics assessment. Reasoning-augmented models (GPT-o1, GPT-o3, Gemini-2.5-Pro) provide no clear advantage over their non-reasoning counterparts. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on the proposed AesEval-Train dataset yields consistent gains: +5.97% on aesthetic judgment (0.639→0.699), +2.70% on region selection (0.580→0.607), and +17.17% on precise localization IoU (0.051→0.223), with the fine-tuned 7B model surpassing GPT-5 on localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, GPT-5 leads all tasks (72.5% judgment, 69.9% region selection, 0.199 IoU), outperforming GPT-4o by 2.2% on judgment and 2.4% on region selection. Reasoning-augmented models underperform their non-reasoning counterparts: GPT-o1 (67.1%) trails GPT-4o (70.3%) on judgment, and GPT-o3 (71.1%) still falls short of GPT-5 (72.5%), while Gemini-2.5-Pro (63.7%) is the weakest reasoning model. Among open-source models, Qwen-VL-72B (67.2%) and Intern-VL3-14B (68.8%) are the strongest, but both remain 3–5 points below GPT-5. Expert image-aesthetics models (AesExpert-7B at 40.6%, UNIAA-LLaVA at 29.0%) perform far worse than general VLMs, confirming a large gap between photographic and design aesthetics. After fine-tuning on AesEval-Train, Qwen-VL-7B surpasses Qwen-VL-72B on judgment (69.9% vs. 67.2%) and exceeds GPT-5 on precise localization IoU (0.223 vs. 0.199), demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed training pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of image aesthetics assessment (AesBench, UNIAA-Bench) and design aesthetics assessment (DesignBench, DesignProbe, GPT-Eval Bench, UI-Bench, UICrit), which have each addressed only narrow subsets of design dimensions and relied on coarse evaluation protocols (holistic scoring or free-form description). It departs from prior work by unifying four design dimensions (typography, layout, color, graphics) into twelve indicators and formulating three fully quantifiable tasks—binary judgment, multiple-choice region selection, and bounding-box regression—that no single prior benchmark has combined. The indicator-grounded reasoning approach, which explicitly links abstract design concepts (e.g., hierarchy, layering) to concrete bbox coordinates in the reasoning path, is a novel data-construction strategy distinct from both generic chain-of-thought prompting and grounded visual reasoning that localizes semantic entities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic framework for aesthetic quality assessment in graphic design, and its central finding—that even GPT-5 achieves only ~0.73 accuracy on judgment and <0.20 IoU on localization, while reasoning-augmented models (GPT-o1, GPT-o3, Gemini-2.5-Pro) show no advantage over non-reasoning counterparts—challenges the assumption that generic reasoning transfers to subjective perceptual tasks. The practical recipe of human-guided VLM labeling plus indicator-grounded reasoning, which lets a 7B fine-tuned model surpass GPT-5 on localization, offers a scalable path for domain-specific VLM improvement. Key limitations include reliance on a single source dataset (Crello), exclusion of infographics and mobile UIs, omission of highly subjective factors like creativity, and the absence of reinforcement learning for reasoning, all of which remain open for future work.
