## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for diffusion models require task-specific fine-tuning (LoRA adapters, adversarial training) and struggle to generalize across both concrete concepts (e.g., specific characters) and abstract ones (e.g., nudity, violence, styles). There is no training-free approach that achieves precise, context-aware erasure while preserving unrelated content and generalizing across diverse diffusion backbones.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard text prompt and a set of precomputed steering vectors (one per cross-attention layer and denoising step, each matching the CA output dimension of that layer). The steering vectors are constructed offline by generating images from paired positive/negative prompts differing only in the target concept, averaging CA outputs over image patches, and subtracting. The output is a generated image in which the target concept is suppressed while all other content remains intact. The method is backbone-agnostic, operating on U-Net (SD 1.4) and DiT (SDXL, SANA) architectures, and steering vectors from distilled models (SDXL-Turbo, SANA-Sprint) can be directly transferred to their full-step counterparts.

## Method

CASteer constructs concept-specific steering vectors by L2-normalizing the difference between averaged cross-attention (CA) outputs from positive prompts (containing concept X) and negative prompts (lacking X), computed across all CA layers and denoising steps. During generation, each CA output patch is modified by subtracting a scaled version of the steering vector, where the scale is determined dynamically by the dot product between the steering vector and the patch's CA output—effectively projecting out the component of the activation along the concept direction. With β=2, this becomes a Householder reflection that preserves the L2-norm of all information orthogonal to the concept, and intermediate clipping (setting α=0 for patches with negative dot product) ensures only regions actually expressing the concept are affected. The approach is entirely training-free, requires no model weight modification (though it can optionally be baked into the final CA projection matrix for zero overhead), and scales to multiple simultaneous concept erasures by averaging or orthogonalizing steering vectors.

## Results

On the I2P nudity removal benchmark, CASteer with intermediate clipping detects only 7 nude body parts across all categories, compared to 646 for the unmodified SD v1.4 baseline and 18 for the second-best method (SAeUron). On the broader inappropriate content removal task (7 categories), CASteer (clip) achieves 25.58% overall inappropriate proportion, outperforming the second-best Receler (27.0%) by 1.42 percentage points. For generation quality preservation, CASteer (clip) attains the best FID-30K of 13.02 and a competitive CLIP-30K of 31.09 on COCO-30K prompts, confirming that erasure does not degrade unrelated content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASteer (clip) achieves the lowest total nudity detection count (7) across all 15 baselines, more than 2× better than the nearest competitor SAeUron (18) and AdvUnlearn (23). Training-based methods like ESD-x (312), UCE (182), MACE (109), and Receler (115) all substantially underperform, confirming that fine-tuning or weight-editing approaches introduce collateral damage. The closest non-training-free baselines, CPE (40) and RECE (66), still detect 5–9× more nudity than CASteer. On the broader inappropriate content task, CASteer (clip) leads overall (25.58%) but Receler is competitive on individual categories like Hate (28.6% vs. 29.0%) and Self-harm (24.8% vs. 26.22%), where CASteer's advantage is smaller. For concrete concept erasure (Snoopy), CASteer balances erasure strength with preservation of semantically related concepts (Mickey, Spongebob) better than ESD and Receler, which degrade related concepts, and better than SPM, which preserves related concepts but erases Snoopy less effectively. On style removal (Van Gogh, McKernan), CASteer achieves the best LPIPS_e and Acc_e while maintaining competitive LPIPS_u and Acc_u, outperforming SAFREE, UCE, and RECE.

## Relation to Prior Work

CASteer repurposes the activation-steering paradigm from mechanistic interpretability (Elhage et al., Wu et al.) and the finding that diffusion backbones encode features in approximately linear subspaces (Kwon et al., Park et al., Li et al.) into a training-free concept-erasure framework. It departs from prior erasure methods (ESD, UCE, MACE, SPM, DoCo) that require fine-tuning or LoRA adapters, and from prior steering approaches (SDID) that are architecture-specific and learned, by constructing steering vectors purely from paired positive/negative prompt activations in cross-attention layers. The key conceptual departure is the dynamic, per-patch alpha computed via dot-product projection, which enables selective suppression only where the target concept is actually present, and the observation that vectors computed from 1-step distilled models transfer to their full-step counterparts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A training-free erasure method that generalizes across U-Net and DiT backbones (SD 1.4, SDXL, SANA) and handles both concrete and abstract concepts without retraining is practically significant for deployment: new harmful concepts can be addressed by computing a small set of steering vectors rather than retraining adapters. The demonstrated ability to erase implicitly defined concepts (e.g., 'a mouse from Disneyland' suppresses Mickey) addresses a failure mode of prior methods that is critical for safety. Open questions include scalability to video diffusion, robustness to adversarially crafted prompts that avoid the steering direction, and whether the cross-attention-only intervention is sufficient for deeply compositional or multi-concept entanglements.
