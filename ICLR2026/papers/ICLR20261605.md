## Problem

Diffusion-based generative models remain largely opaque: existing interpretability approaches either perform post-hoc analysis of intermediate representations (retrospective, with limited control) or introduce encoder-based semantic vectors that are difficult to interpret and tend to capture only global rather than fine-grained local patterns. The paper asks how to embed interpretability directly into the diffusion architecture so that one can see what visual patterns are modeled, where and when they emerge during denoising, and whether unwanted correlations (shortcut learning) have been learned.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes Gaussian noise x_T and an m-dimensional prototype activation vector s (m=100 for most datasets, 30 for FMNIST) as conditioning; the vector encodes the presence/strength of each learned semantic patch pattern. The prototype encoder is a 4-layer Conv-ReLU network that maps input images (32×32, 64×64, or 224×224) to H×W×128 patch features, against which m prototypes of shape 1×1×128 are compared. Outputs include generated images, the activation vector itself, and per-prototype visualizations obtained by amplifying a single prototype score and extracting the most responsive spatial patch from the resulting sample.

## Method

Patronus integrates a ProtoPNet-inspired prototypical network as the semantic encoder for a conditional DDPM. The encoder produces patch-level features, and m learned prototypes (each a 1×1×128 vector) capture localized visual patterns; the activation vector s is computed via log-transformed minimum L2 distances between encoded patches and prototypes, yielding a compact m-dimensional condition. The DDPM reverse process is conditioned on s rather than on high-dimensional latent features, reducing the conditioning dimensionality while preserving semantic capacity. Training is fully unsupervised, using only the standard noise-prediction loss to jointly optimize the prototype encoder and denoiser, with a theoretical proof showing that any ELBO-improving encoder update guarantees progress in approximating the data distribution. A novel prototype visualization method amplifies a target prototype's score to a plausible maximum, generates a conditioned sample, and extracts the most activated patch as the prototype's visual representation, enabling direct inspection of learned semantics without annotations.

## Results

On CelebA, Patronus achieves a TAD score of 0.43 (vs. 0.30 for InfoDiff and 0.16 for DiffAE), captures 9 attributes (vs. 3 and 2 respectively), and reaches a Latent AUROC of 0.87. In prototype-conditioned generation (w/ learned s), Patronus achieves an FID of 4.8 on CelebA, 8.0 on CIFAR-10, 2.6 on FMNIST, and 24.1 on FFHQ, substantially outperforming baselines across all four datasets. On FFHQ, Patronus attains a Latent AUROC of 0.92, far exceeding the 0.61 achieved by both DiffAE and InfoDiff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Patronus outperforms both DiffAE and InfoDiff on 3 out of 4 datasets in latent (prototype) quality, with particularly strong gains on CelebA (Latent AUROC 0.87 vs. 0.84/0.80) and FFHQ (0.92 vs. 0.61). In prototype-conditioned generation, Patronus achieves dramatically lower FID scores: 4.8 on CelebA (vs. 22.7 for DiffAE and 23.6 for InfoDiff), 8.0 on CIFAR-10 (vs. 32.1/32.7), 2.6 on FMNIST (vs. 8.2/8.5), and 24.1 on FFHQ (vs. 31.6/31.2). Patronus also leads in disentanglement (TAD 0.43 vs. 0.30) and attribute capture (9 vs. 3) on CelebA. The one exception is FMNIST latent quality, where Patronus (0.82) slightly trails both baselines (0.84), which the authors attribute to Patronus prioritizing localized features over the global structures that better suit FMNIST's high inter-class variability. The ResNet-50 baseline on CheXpert (Bressem et al., 2020) is included only for disease-label comparison, where Patronus's logistic regression on prototype activations achieves comparable AUROC on several labels (e.g., 0.80 for Pleural Effusion vs. 0.91 for ResNet-50).

## Relation to Prior Work

Patronus sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: prototypical networks for interpretability (ProtoPNet, Chen et al. 2019) and conditional diffusion models (DDPM, Ho et al. 2020). It repurposes ProtoPNet's patch-based prototype extraction and L2-distance activation vector—originally designed for interpretable classification—into a generative setting, using the activation vector as the conditioning signal for a conditional DDPM. This departs from prior interpretable-diffusion approaches in two key ways: unlike post-hoc semantic-latent analyses (Kwon et al. 2022; Park et al. 2023; Haas et al. 2024), interpretability is intrinsic to the architecture rather than discovered retrospectively; and unlike autoencoder-based guidance methods (DiffAE, DiffuseGAE, InfoDiffusion) that extract global semantic vectors, Patronus captures local, fine-grained patterns through patch-level prototypes, reducing the conditioning dimensionality while preserving semantic richness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical tool for auditing generative models: by manipulating individual prototype activations, practitioners can expose unwanted correlations (e.g., hair color → smile) that constitute shortcut learning, and trace when specific semantic features emerge during denoising to guide more efficient editing. A notable limitation is that the patch-based encoder struggles with global, non-local attributes such as age and gender, which are harder to localize to a single prototype. Open questions include whether the prototype-based approach scales to high-resolution or text-conditioned diffusion models, how it interacts with cross-modal (text-to-image) settings, and whether the temporal-emergence insight can be leveraged for principled bias mitigation rather than mere detection.
