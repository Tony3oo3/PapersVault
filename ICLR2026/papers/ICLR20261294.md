limitations: The stitching approach requires that a compatible linearly-related layer exists between the video VAE latent space and the 3D model, which may not hold for all model pairs; additionally, the reward finetuning stage is computationally expensive due to rendering 3D scenes back to 2D for reward computation, and the method has not been evaluated on dynamic or 4D scene generation.

surprise_factor: The main conceptual surprise is that independently pretrained video VAE latents and 3D reconstruction model activations share a strong linear relationship at some intermediate layer, making stitching viable without domain-specific co-training — the authors themselves flag this as unexpected. ## Problem

Existing end-to-end text-to-3D diffusion models suffer from two critical weaknesses: their 3D decoders are trained from scratch on multi-view data rather than leveraging the strong geometric priors in recent feedforward 3D foundation models, and the generative model is only weakly aligned with the decoder, so generated latents often fall outside the decoder's expected input distribution. This results in lower-quality 3D outputs compared to what the underlying 3D reconstruction models can achieve when given proper inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 3D scene representation in one of two forms: 3D Gaussian splats (when stitched with AnySplat or MVDUSt3R) or point maps with depth maps and camera poses (when stitched with VGGT or MVDUSt3R). The video VAE encoder operates at 384×384 (MVDUSt3R) or 512×512 (AnySplat, VGGT) resolution, and the denoising loop generates 13 multi-view latents over 80 steps with classifier-free guidance. The stitched 3D decoder then maps these latents directly to the target 3D representation in a single forward pass, with no per-scene optimization required.

## Method

VIST3A combines two complementary techniques. First, model stitching identifies the layer in a pretrained feedforward 3D model whose activations are most linearly related to the video VAE's latent space by solving a least-squares problem across candidate layers; the 3D model is then cut at that layer and its downstream portion is attached as a decoder via a single 3D-convolution stitching layer, followed by lightweight self-supervised fine-tuning (using the original 3D model's outputs as pseudo-targets with LoRA). Second, direct reward finetuning aligns the generative model with the stitched decoder by backpropagating reward signals—computed from multi-view image quality (CLIP + HPSv2), 3D representation quality (rendered from decoded 3D), and 3D consistency (pixel and LPIPS loss between decoded and rendered views)—through the full denoising trajectory, using DRTune-style gradient detachment at each step for stability. The entire pipeline requires no 3D labels and only a small dataset (DL3DV-10K, ScanNet) for fine-tuning.

## Results

On T3Bench (object-centric), VIST3A:Wan+MVDUSt3R achieves the best Imaging Quality (58.83), Aesthetic Quality (56.55), CLIP score (32.75), and Coherence (3.89), outperforming the strongest baseline Director3D by 4.5 points in Imaging and 0.46 in Coherence. On SceneBench (scene-level), VIST3A:Wan+AnySplat reaches Imaging Quality 64.87 and Coherence 3.86, surpassing the best baseline VideoRFSplat (58.19 / 3.63) by a wide margin. On DPG-Bench (long-prompt adherence), VIST3A:Wan+MVDUSt3R scores 81.82 Global and 86.13 Attribute, exceeding the best baseline SplatFlow (69.70 / 65.55) by over 12 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIST3A outperforms all five text-to-3DGS baselines (Matrix3D-omni, Director3D, Prometheus3D, SplatFlow, VideoRFSplat) across every metric on T3Bench, SceneBench, and DPG-Bench. The largest margins appear on DPG-Bench, where VIST3A:Wan+MVDUSt3R scores 81.82 Global vs. SplatFlow's 69.70 (the strongest baseline), a 12-point gap. On SceneBench, VIST3A:Wan+AnySplat leads in Imaging Quality (64.87 vs. VideoRFSplat's 58.19) and Coherence (3.86 vs. 3.63). For the stitching evaluation on RealEstate10K NVS, all stitched VAEs (e.g., SVD+AnySplat at 21.48 PSNR) surpass standalone AnySplat (20.85 PSNR) and prior VAE-based methods like Prometheus3D (19.56) and VideoRFSplat (19.05). For pointmap reconstruction (7-Scenes, ETH3D), stitched models (e.g., Wan+VGGT: Acc. 0.018, NC. 0.693) maintain near-identical accuracy to the original VGGT (Acc. 0.020, NC. 0.694), confirming that stitching preserves 3D reconstruction fidelity without degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIST3A repurposes the model stitching literature (Lenc & Vedaldi 2015; Pan et al. 2023; Bansal et al. 2021) — previously used to compare or reassemble representations within a single domain — to bridge two independently pretrained model families: video VAE encoders and feedforward 3D reconstruction networks (MVDUSt3R, VGGT, AnySplat). For alignment, it adapts direct reward finetuning (Clark et al. 2024; DRTune, Wu et al. 2024c) from 2D human-preference tuning to a 3D-consistency-aware objective. This departs from prior LDM-based text-to-3D generators (SplatFlow, Prometheus3D, VideoRFSplat, Director3D) that train a custom VAE-style decoder from multi-view latents, requiring large 3D-annotated datasets and leaving the decoder weakly aligned with the generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stitching recipe is lightweight (small unlabeled dataset, closed-form linear layer, LoRA fine-tuning) and general across video backbones and 3D models, offering a practical path to upgrade any text-to-3D system as new 3D foundation models emerge without retraining a decoder from scratch. By inheriting the 3D model's full output suite, the framework naturally extends text-to-3D to pointmaps, depth, and camera poses without additional architecture. Open questions include whether stitching generalizes to 4D or dynamic scene generation, how sensitive the approach is to architectural mismatches beyond the tested combinations, and whether the reward finetuning stage can be further simplified or made label-free in its reward signals.
