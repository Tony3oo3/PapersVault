## Problem

Existing deep reinforcement learning methods for scheduling (JSP/FJSP) optimize average performance over training instances, producing poor solutions on individual test instances. While test-time adaptation methods like Active Search and Efficient Active Search address this, they discard adaptation knowledge across instances and rely on gradient-based optimization that easily falls into local optima for combinatorial problems. This paper tackles both limitations by learning a meta-initialization that enables rapid, derivative-free per-instance adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a scheduling instance defined by a job set, machine set, per-job operation sequences, and per-operation processing times on compatible machines, processed as a sequential decision task where at each step an unscheduled operation is assigned to an available machine. The output is a complete operation-to-machine schedule with an associated makespan value. The policy operates in two modes: greedy (single deterministic rollout) and sampling (L stochastic rollouts with best-solution selection), and the meta-model is applied to both FJSP (via DANIEL, a dual-attention RL policy) and JSP (via SPN, a self-supervised pointer network).

## Method

The framework implements MAML-style meta-learning where the outer loop optimizes a meta-initialization θ₀ that, after K steps of inner-loop adaptation on a specific instance, yields a high-quality instance-specific policy. Both inner and outer loops use OpenAI's Evolutionary Strategy (ES) for derivative-free gradient estimation, avoiding backpropagation entirely and enabling full-parameter adaptation without gradient storage. Two Monte Carlo fitness estimators are designed: MC averaging (mean of L rollouts, used in greedy mode) and MC best-sample (minimum makespan among L rollouts, used in sampling mode) to reduce variance in the ES gradient. A FOMAML-style first-order approximation replaces the full meta-gradient with instance-specific gradients, eliminating the computationally prohibitive perturbation-on-perturbation second-order terms. Finally, a GPU-based parallelization scheme replaces CPU-cluster (Ray/Dask) population evaluation with batched forward passes over a perturbation matrix Θ = θ + σε, vectorizing both the neural network inference and the FJSP environment interaction to achieve 20–30× speedup over CPU implementations.

## Results

On the challenging SD2 dataset (greedy mode), the proposed method reduces the optimality gap from 25.20% (DANIEL) to 13.18% on 10×5 instances and from 32.09% to 21.22% on 20×10 instances, while also being faster than EAS (0.13m vs 0.22m on 10×5). On SD1 sampling mode, the method achieves a −3.62% gap on 20×10 instances, surpassing the OR-Tools reference solution. On public benchmarks (mk, la-rdata, la-edata, la-vdata), the method consistently achieves the lowest gap across all settings, e.g., 2.25% on mk vs 3.15% for EAS (SD1 10×5 model, sampling). On JSP (Taillard benchmarks), the method reduces the gap from 16.86% (SPN) to 10.83% (greedy) and from 8.52% to 6.31% (sampling) on 15×15 instances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines across every evaluated setting. Against OR-Tools (Da Col & Teppan, 2019), the method achieves negative optimality gaps (i.e., better than the 1-hour CP solver) on 20×10, 30×10, and 40×10 instances for both SD1 and SD2, while running in under 3 minutes vs. 1 hour. Against the MWKR dispatching rule (Brandimarte, 1993), the improvement is dramatic: e.g., 13.18% vs. 68.45% gap on SD2 10×5. Against the original DANIEL model (Wang et al., 2023), the meta-learning framework consistently reduces the gap by 3–12 percentage points depending on instance size and difficulty. Against Active Search (Bello et al., 2016) and Efficient Active Search (Hottung et al., 2021b), the method wins on both solution quality and inference time (e.g., 0.14m vs. 0.27m/0.22m on SD1 10×5 greedy), because derivative-free full-parameter adaptation avoids backpropagation overhead. On JSP with the SPN model (Corsini et al., 2024), the method reduces the gap from 16.86% to 10.83% (greedy) and from 8.52% to 6.31% (sampling) on 15×15 Taillard instances, confirming model-agnostic applicability beyond reinforcement learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established lines of work—Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML/FOMAML) for fast adaptation and OpenAI's Evolutionary Strategy (ES) for derivative-free optimization—into a unified framework for instance-wise scheduling. It departs from prior instance-wise adaptation methods (Active Search, Efficient Active Search) in two key ways: (1) it introduces a meta-learning training phase so that adaptation knowledge is shared across instances rather than discarded after each test-time fine-tune, and (2) it replaces gradient-based fine-tuning with a fully derivative-free ES procedure, motivated by the observation that gradient methods can get trapped in local optima on combinatorial landscapes. The GPU-based population parallelization also distinguishes it from prior ES implementations that rely on CPU clusters and distributed schedulers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in learning-based scheduling: models trained to optimize average performance still produce poor solutions on individual instances, and the standard remedy (test-time gradient fine-tuning) is both inefficient and prone to local optima in combinatorial settings. The demonstration that a derivative-free meta-learning approach can match or beat gradient-based adaptation while being more memory-friendly (no gradient storage) and faster (GPU-parallel ES outperforms EAS) opens a path toward scalable per-instance adaptation for large-scale industrial scheduling. The main limitation acknowledged is that ES-based training is 1.5–2× slower than FOMAML, and the authors flag improving training efficiency as future work. An open question is whether the derivative-free advantage persists for even larger or more structured combinatorial problems where the search space is more constrained.
