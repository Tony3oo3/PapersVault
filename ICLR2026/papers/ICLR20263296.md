## Problem

Existing rerankers face two underexplored challenges: they rely on large pretrained language models to process raw passage text, incurring high inference latency, and they largely neglect cross-passage inference tasks such as coreference resolution, entity disambiguation, and evidence aggregation that become necessary when long documents are chunked into shorter passages for retrieval.

## Input / Output

EBCAR consumes pre-computed dense passage embeddings (already stored in a vector database at indexing time) and a single query embedding produced by the same encoder, augmented with relative document ID embeddings (local to the current candidate set, not global corpus identifiers) and sinusoidal passage position encodings. It outputs a dot-product relevance score per passage against the fixed query embedding, sorted descending to yield the final ranking. Because all computation occurs in embedding space with no autoregressive generation or full-text processing, inference throughput reaches ~29 queries/second on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

EBCAR is a 16-layer Transformer encoder (~126M parameters) that processes the query and k enriched passage embeddings jointly through a hybrid attention mechanism. Each layer contains two complementary multi-head attention modules: a shared full attention allowing all tokens to attend freely (capturing inter-document and global relevance), and a dedicated masked attention that restricts each passage's attention to only same-document passages plus the query (enabling intra-document coreference and coherence). The two modules' outputs are summed with residual connections and layer normalization. Training uses an InfoNCE contrastive objective that pulls the updated positive passage embedding toward the fixed (unmodified) query embedding while pushing negatives away, with the query kept static to serve as a consistent semantic anchor and avoid context drift.

## Results

EBCAR achieves the highest average nDCG@10 of 64.92 across eight ConTEB test datasets, outperforming the strongest LLM-based reranker ICR(Llama) (50.35) by 14.57 points on average. On datasets demanding cross-passage inference, EBCAR's gains are especially pronounced: 80.19 on Football (vs. 11.63 for RankZephyr), 81.30 on Geography (vs. 73.10 for ICR(Llama)), and 40.74 on Insurance (vs. 4.76 for RankGPT-Llama). In terms of efficiency, EBCAR processes 29.33 queries per second on a single A100 GPU, roughly 154× faster than ICR(Llama) at 0.19 qps, while using only 126M parameters versus 7–8B for LLM-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBCAR outperforms all 12 baselines on average nDCG@10 (64.92 vs. 50.35 for the best LLM-based reranker ICR-Llama), with the largest margins on cross-passage-heavy datasets: Football (+69.28 over RankZephyr), Insurance (+36.58 over RankGPT-Llama), and Geography (+8.20 over ICR-Llama). However, EBCAR does not win on every individual dataset: ICR(Llama) leads on MLDR (83.93 vs. 75.26) and NarrativeQA (79.96 vs. 73.21), and RankZephyr leads on MLDR (82.34 vs. 75.26), likely because these in-distribution tasks are less dependent on structural or cross-passage signals that EBCAR exploits. Among pointwise and pairwise rerankers (monoBERT, monoT5, duoT5), EBCAR dominates by wide margins on every dataset, with the gap exceeding 30 nDCG points on Football and Insurance. In terms of throughput, EBCAR (29.33 qps) is approximately 8× faster than monoBERT (4.24 qps), 174× faster than RankZephyr (0.17 qps), and 154× faster than ICR(Llama) (0.19 qps), while using 1/56th the parameters of the 7B LLM-based rerankers. On the out-of-distribution TREC 2021/2022 evaluation (Appendix A.6), EBCAR (69.71 avg) trails monoT5-MS-MARCO (80.98) and RankZephyr (83.96), which were fine-tuned directly on MS-MARCO, but remains competitive with ICR(Llama) (71.38) at a fraction of the inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

EBCAR departs from the dominant reranking paradigm of feeding raw passage text through large pretrained language models (pointwise monoBERT/monoT5, pairwise duoT5, or listwise LLM-based methods like RankGPT and ICR) by operating entirely in the pre-computed embedding space, where passage vectors are already stored in the vector database. Architecturally, it repurposes a standard transformer encoder with a novel hybrid attention design—combining a shared full-attention module for global cross-document interactions and a dedicated masked-attention module restricted to same-document passages—augmented with relative document ID embeddings and sinusoidal passage position encodings. The paper positions itself as complementary to contextual retrieval techniques (late chunking, semantic chunking, hierarchical retrieval) that operate at the retrieval stage, adding a cross-passage inference layer specifically at the reranking stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in deployed RAG systems: most SOTA rerankers are either computationally expensive (7-8B LLMs processing full text) or ignore the structural and cross-passage context that is often essential for correct ranking. EBCAR's ~150× throughput advantage (29.33 vs. 0.17–0.19 queries/sec) while matching or exceeding LLM-based rerankers on cross-passage inference datasets makes it a practical option for latency-sensitive production pipelines. Limitations include the information bottleneck inherent in fixed-size embeddings (EBCAR does not beat ICR-Llama on all datasets), the requirement for document IDs and passage positions to be available, and the training-inference mismatch when candidate set size exceeds the training value. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to settings without explicit document structure and how it interacts with emerging long-context embedding models that may reduce the need for cross-passage inference altogether.
