## Problem

Existing graph-based crystal encoders use finite receptive fields and local message passing, causing distinct crystal structures to collapse into identical representations and failing to capture long-range periodic interactions essential for property prediction. Prior reciprocal-space approaches (EwaldMP, PotNet, ReGNet) partially address this but violate the physical invariance and uniqueness constraints of diffraction by omitting dependencies of form factors on local chemical environment and scattering vector magnitude, and by propagating diffraction information through layers rather than treating it as a fixed structural signature.

## Input / Output

The model takes a crystal structure specified by atomic types, fractional coordinates, and a 3×3 lattice matrix, and outputs a scalar property (formation energy, band gap, elastic moduli, etc.) or a class label (metal/non-metal). Internally, the graph encoder produces per-atom embeddings that feed a form-factor MLP generating N_hkl ≈ 300 scattering amplitudes per atom; these are combined with fractional coordinates to yield a 2×N_hkl-dimensional diffraction vector (real and imaginary structure factors) that is fused at the modality level with the globally pooled graph representation, rather than being propagated atom-by-atom through the network.

## Method

PRDNet is a multimodal architecture combining a multi-head attention GNN (with RBF/SBF/distance edge features and gated residual updates) and a pseudo-particle ray diffraction module. The key innovation is a learned pseudo-particle form factor f_i*(|Q|, G_θ(G), f_i^type) that, unlike fixed tabulated X-ray form factors, is sensitive to local chemical environment and element identity simultaneously, enabling discrimination of same-element atoms in different sites. The diffraction module computes structure factors over ~300 Miller indices selected with a gcd=1 constraint and closed under all crystallographic symmetry operations, guaranteeing E(3) invariance by construction (the translational phase is always an integer multiple of 2π). Graph and diffraction features are fused via global mean pooling followed by an MLP, treating diffraction as a holistic structural signature rather than an atom-level feature. Training uses Huber loss with AdamW and cosine annealing with warm restarts.

## Results

On the Materials Project benchmark (122,959 entries), PRDNet achieves the lowest MAE across all six properties: formation energy (0.028 eV/atom), band gap (0.151 eV), bulk modulus (0.035 log(GPa)), shear modulus (0.108 log(GPa)), Young's modulus (0.104 log(GPa)), and the highest metal/non-metal classification accuracy (93.3%). On JARVIS-DFT (75,993 entries), PRDNet leads on formation energy (0.032 eV/atom), band gap OPT (0.140 eV), bulk modulus (0.064 log(GPa)), shear modulus (0.122 log(GPa)), total energy (0.032 eV/atom), and band gap MBJ (0.267 eV). On MatBench, PRDNet achieves the best formation energy (0.019 eV/atom), shear modulus (0.058 log(GPa)), and refractive index (0.242), while remaining competitive on exfoliation energy (0.038 eV/atom vs. eComFormer's 0.031).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRDNet outperforms all 11 baselines on every property in the Materials Project benchmark. The closest competitors are CrystalFramer (0.030 eV/atom formation energy, 0.106 log(GPa) Young's modulus) and eComFormer (0.153 eV band gap), but PRDNet leads by 0.002 eV/atom on formation energy, 0.002 eV on band gap, and 0.012 log(GPa) on Young's modulus. Among reciprocal-space methods, EwaldMP (12.2M params) and PotNet (1.8M params) trail PRDNet by 0.024 and 0.007 eV/atom on formation energy respectively, while ReGNet (3.3M params) lags by 0.019 eV/atom. On JARVIS-DFT, PRDNet beats PotNet (the strongest real-space baseline) by 0.002 eV/atom on formation energy and 0.004 eV on band gap OPT, and surpasses CrystalFramer by 0.001 log(GPa) on bulk modulus. On MatBench, PRDNet wins on formation energy (0.019 vs. 0.020 for CrystalFormer/eComFormer), shear modulus (0.058 vs. 0.080 for CrystalFormer), and refractive index (0.242 vs. 0.244 for CrystalFormer), but trails eComFormer on exfoliation energy (0.038 vs. 0.031 eV/atom). The ablation in Table 7 further shows that replacing learned pseudo-particle form factors with tabulated X-ray form factors degrades formation energy MAE from 0.028 to 0.085 eV/atom and metal/non-metal accuracy from 93.3% to 78.9%, confirming the critical role of the learned probe.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRDNet extends the line of equivariant/invariant GNNs for crystal property prediction (MatFormer, CrystalFormer, CrystalFramer, eComFormer) by adding a physics-grounded reciprocal-space diffraction module. It departs from prior reciprocal-space approaches (EwaldMP, PotNet, ReGNet) that treat Fourier/Ewald transforms as atom-wise information fusion, instead framing diffraction as a complete, invariant, modality-level global property with learned pseudo-particle form factors that depend on local chemical environment—not just atomic species and scattering vector magnitude. The paper also explicitly identifies a representational failure mode (distinct crystals mapped to identical graph encodings) that prior GNN-based methods share due to finite receptive fields, positioning the diffraction module as a principled fix rather than an empirical add-on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a physically complete and symmetry-invariant representation that provably avoids the collision problem where different crystals receive identical encodings, which is a fundamental limitation for any finite-receptive-field GNN. The pseudo-particle concept—learning a probe more discriminative than any fixed physical particle—opens a path toward adaptive, environment-sensitive structural descriptors. However, the authors note that the completeness of the reciprocal-space representation makes the inverse problem (crystal structure prediction from diffraction) ill-posed under strict invariance, limiting direct application to generative CSP. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-length-scale materials and how relaxed invariance constraints might enable CSP without sacrificing the uniqueness guarantee.
