## Problem

Open-source DeepResearch agents suffer from three coupled failures: GRPO-based multi-turn RL training collapses under distribution shift from tool responses, sparse correctness-only rewards drive agents into redundant tool-call spam (reward hacking), and existing training datasets are either answerable from parametric knowledge or too expensive and brittle to scale. Additionally, current systems overemphasize closed-form QA at the expense of the open-ended, citation-dense synthesis that real investigative tasks demand.

## Input / Output

Fathom-Search-4B receives a natural-language question and interacts with two live tools—search_urls (Serper API returning ranked URL/title/snippet triples) and query_url (goal-conditioned page reading via Jina-AI + Crawl4AI)—in a ReAct-style loop capped at 32 steps × 8,192 tokens per step within a 40,960-token context. Fathom-Synthesizer-4B ingests the full multi-turn search trace (extended to 65,536 tokens via YaRN RoPE scaling) and emits a structured DeepResearch report: a private plan block (question decomposition, evidence-to-section mapping, insight strategy) followed by a public citation-dense report where every non-obvious claim carries inline citations restricted to URLs actually explored in the trace.

## Method

The system is built on Qwen3-4B and trained in two RL stages plus an SFT stage. Stage 1 uses RAPO—a zero-overhead GRPO extension that prunes saturated prompts (SolveRate ≥ 0.9), rescales advantages of informative groups by G/G_good to counter gradient dilution, and maintains per-prompt replay buffers that inject a stored successful trajectory when all rollouts fail—trained for 10 epochs on DUETQA (4,889 QA pairs generated via multi-agent self-play between O3 and O4-mini with GPT-4o obfuscation and cross-model verification enforcing post-2024 search dependency). Stage 2 (2 epochs on a 5,471-instance mixed pool) introduces a Steerable Step-Level Reward that classifies every tool call via GPT-4.1 into cognitive-behavior categories (UNIQUE/REDUNDANT search, EXPLORATION/VERIFICATION/REDUNDANT query) and assigns marginal-utility credit with tunable saturation thresholds C_S, C_Q and verification budget B_v, enabling explicit control over search breadth, depth, and horizon beyond 20 calls. Fathom-Synthesizer-4B is then SFT-trained for 5 epochs on DEEPRESEARCH-SFT (2,500 samples distilled from GPT-5) using an explicit Plan-then-Write protocol that decomposes questions into sub-goals, maps evidence to report sections, and specifies insight-generation strategies before producing the final cited report.

## Results

Fathom-Search-4B (Stage-2) achieves 52.1% average accuracy across five DeepSearch benchmarks (SimpleQA 90.0, FRAMES 64.8, WebWalker 50.0, Seal0 22.5, MuSiQue 33.2), setting a new state-of-the-art among open-weights models and surpassing GPT-4o with search (46.5% avg) by 5.6 points. On general reasoning, it scores 53.8% average (HLE 9.5, AIME-25 70.0, GPQA-D 60.1, MedQA 75.4), outperforming its Qwen3-4B base (49.8%) and all other open-source search agents. On DeepResearch-Bench, Fathom-DeepResearch achieves 45.47 RACE Overall and 56.1% citation accuracy, outperforming Perplexity DeepResearch (42.25 RACE) and Kimi-Researcher (44.64 RACE) while remaining competitive with OpenAI DeepResearch (46.98 RACE) and Gemini-2.5-Pro DeepResearch (48.88 RACE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fathom-Search-4B (Stage-2) outperforms all open-source DeepSearch baselines on the DeepSearch average: it beats II-Search-4B (47.3→52.1, +4.8), WebSailor-3B (44.0→52.1, +8.1), Jan-Nano-128K (38.1→52.1, +14.0), and its own base Qwen3-4B+Search (27.5→52.1, +24.6). Against closed-source models, it surpasses GPT-4o with search (46.5→52.1, +5.6) but trails o3 with search (68.1) by 16 points, with the largest gaps on FRAMES (64.8 vs 86.8) and Seal0 (22.5 vs 49.5). On general reasoning, Fathom-Search-4B (53.8 avg) exceeds GPT-4o with search (54.1) by a narrow margin and significantly outperforms all open-source peers (best: II-Search-4B at 47.8). On DeepResearch-Bench, Fathom-DeepResearch (45.47 RACE Overall) beats Perplexity DeepResearch (42.25), Kimi-Researcher (44.64), Doubao-DeepResearch (44.34), and LangChain Open-DeepResearch (43.44), but falls short of OpenAI DeepResearch (46.98) and Gemini-2.5-Pro DeepResearch (48.88); on citation accuracy it scores 56.1%, trailing Perplexity (90.24%) and Gemini (81.44%) but exceeding Doubao (52.86%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) into the multi-turn, tool-augmented regime via RAPO, adding curriculum pruning, advantage scaling, and per-prompt replay buffers to counter the distribution shift and advantage saturation that break vanilla GRPO when external tool responses enter the trajectory. The RLVR training infrastructure is built on RECALL (Chen et al., 2025), and the two-stage RL-Zero framework departs from prior single-reward RLVR by introducing a steerable step-level reward that classifies each tool call by cognitive behavior (exploration vs. verification vs. redundancy). On the data side, DUETQA's multi-agent self-play pipeline with cross-model verification contrasts with prior synthetic search datasets (WebSailor, SimpleDeepResearcher) that rely on handcrafted heuristics or graph expansion, and the DEEPRESEARCH-SFT corpus introduces an explicit plan-then-write protocol for synthesis that prior DeepSearch agents lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper narrows the open-source vs. proprietary gap in DeepResearch by showing that a 4B-parameter system can rival or outperform larger closed agents (Claude, Grok, Perplexity) on DeepResearch-Bench, which is practically significant for teams that need self-hosted, citation-grounded research pipelines. Key open questions remain: the 40K context window caps trajectories at ~30 tool calls, making Level-3 BrowseComp-style tasks (50–100+ calls, 128K+ context) intractable; the replay buffer's anchoring to low-entropy traces limits test-time scaling; and the synchronous training pipeline is acknowledged as brittle at scale. Whether the steerable step-level reward generalizes beyond the specific search_urls/query_url tool pair, and whether RAPO's stabilization transfers to other multi-turn agentic settings (code execution, API orchestration), are natural follow-ups.
