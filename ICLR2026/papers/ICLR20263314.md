## Problem

Transformer-based implicit rendering models struggle to inject camera geometry in a way that generalises across diverse camera types (perspective, fisheye) and sensing modalities (RGB, thermal). Existing positional embedding approaches either rely on absolute encodings that fail under intrinsics changes, or use relative camera-parameter encodings that abandon explicit ray representations, limiting robustness to non-perspective optics. Additionally, no feedforward multi-modal novel view synthesis method existed that could jointly render RGB and thermal views from non-confocal inputs in a single network.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any number of posed input images (RGB and/or thermal) at 256×256 resolution with known intrinsics and extrinsics, patchified at 8×8 or 16×16 depending on the experiment. Unlike confocal multi-view methods, inputs need not share the same pose or modality, and the network can handle asymmetric combinations (e.g., 1 RGB + 5 thermal). Outputs are novel-view RGB renderings, novel-view thermal renderings, and metrically meaningful depth maps, all produced in a single feedforward pass through an encoder-decoder transformer.

## Method

RoRE replaces standard pixel-index RoPE with a ray-based formulation where each image patch is encoded by its centre Plücker ray (6D: 3 position + 3 direction). Rather than fragmenting the embedding vector into six separate rotations, learned frequency parameters superimpose contributions from all ray dimensions onto shared rotation planes, letting the network discover how position and direction interact with different parts of the latent space. An asymmetric rotation mechanism (adding shifted negative counterparts of each position component) removes the distance-dependent attention decay inherent in standard RoPE, yielding near-uniform attention across the ray domain. For multi-modal operation, modality-specific tokenisers and output heads share the same RoRE embedding, with a discrete modality class appended to the position vector; the model is trained via masked cross-modality prediction with combined MSE, LPIPS, and depth-gradient losses on the LVSM decoder-only architecture.

## Results

On standard novel view synthesis (RealEstate10K), RoRE achieves 26.65 PSNR / 0.845 SSIM / 0.070 LPIPS, comparable to LVSM (26.18), GTA (26.74), and PRoPE (26.81). On the unseen DL3DV domain, RoRE reaches 19.77 PSNR, marginally ahead of all baselines. The method's advantage becomes pronounced under distribution shift: with varying focal lengths, RoRE scores 22.66 PSNR on RE10K versus 14.81 for GTA and 14.71 for PRoPE; on native fisheye (FIORD), RoRE achieves 23.55 PSNR while GTA and PRoPE collapse to ~11.7–11.9 PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On in-distribution perspective imagery (Table 1), RoRE is broadly competitive: it trails PRoPE by 0.16 dB PSNR on RE10K but leads on DL3DV by 0.09 dB, confirming no regression on standard benchmarks. The decisive separation appears under generalisation stress. When focal length is varied via cropping (Table 2), RoRE retains 22.66 PSNR on RE10K while GTA (14.81) and PRoPE (14.71) degrade by ~8 dB, and RoRE also edges out LVSM (21.95) by 0.7 dB. On barrel-distorted and native fisheye inputs (Table 3), RoRE scores 23.96 and 23.55 PSNR respectively, outperforming LVSM by ~2 dB and ~1 dB, while GTA and PRoPE fail catastrophically on fisheye (11.64 and 11.90 PSNR) due to their lack of explicit ray-direction encoding. The authors attribute this to RoRE's geometry-agnostic ray formulation, which is naturally invariant to intrinsics changes, versus the constrained projection-matrix encodings in GTA and PRoPE that overfit to perspective geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoRE extends the RoPE formulation from NLP (1D sequence positions) and vision (2D pixel indices) to 6D Plücker ray coordinates, replacing handcrafted frequency schedules with learned per-dimension frequencies and adding asymmetric rotations to eliminate the distance-dependent attention decay that is natural in RoPE but harmful for 3D scene reasoning. Architecturally it builds on LVSM's minimal decoder-only transformer and adopts MultiMAE's modality-specific tokeniser design, while explicitly departing from projection-matrix-based relative encodings (GTA, PRoPE) in favour of full ray-direction encoding. The asymmetric rotation idea is borrowed from VRoPE's temporal handling but repurposed for spatial ray geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that a single ray-based embedding can handle perspective, fisheye, and barrel-distorted cameras without retraining, and can fuse arbitrary combinations of RGB and thermal inputs in one forward pass — a capability the authors note has no prior feedforward equivalent. Limitations include the single-ray-per-patch representation (no explicit patch size/orientation), the multi-modal results being validated primarily on synthetic data with a visible sim-to-real gap, and the fact that on standard perspective benchmarks RoRE offers no measurable advantage over simpler APE baselines. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger real-world multi-modal deployments, whether self-supervised depth can replace the ground-truth depth supervision, and whether incorporating patch geometry directly would resolve the fisheye local-accuracy failures.
