## Problem

Quantum error correction decoding faces a fundamental accuracy-efficiency tradeoff: classical decoders like MWPM and BP-OSD scale polynomially with code distance, tensor network decoders are prohibitively expensive, and existing neural decoders fail to reach near-ML error thresholds. No prior method simultaneously achieves near-optimal logical error rates and linear computational scalability in syndrome size, which is essential for real-time fault-tolerant quantum computing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260774/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The decoder takes a discrete binary syndrome vector s ∈ {-1,+1}^m (stabilizer measurement outcomes) along with the code's parity-check matrix H and logical operator matrix L, and produces a recovery operator e(s) ∈ {0,1}^n that must exactly satisfy the GF(2) syndrome constraint H·e(s) = s while targeting the predicted logical class. Unlike continuous-input decoders, the input is purely discrete and the output is constrained to a feasible affine subspace over GF(2), requiring a post-processing stage to enforce exact consistency. The method scales linearly in syndrome length m (quadratically in code distance L), with near-constant parameter count (1.2–1.9M) across distances 4–10.

## Method

SAQ-Decoder combines a dual-stream transformer with asymmetric attention and a constraint-aware post-processing stage. The syndrome stream uses a topological attention mask derived from H·H^T to restrict attention to neighboring stabilizers sharing physical qubits, while the logical stream employs unrestricted cross-attention to integrate global syndrome information; a learnable global token enables cross-region aggregation. Training uses a three-term logical-centric loss: cross-entropy on an auxiliary MLP's logical prior, cross-entropy on the transformer's refined logical class output, and a novel differentiable logical-minimum entropy loss that approximates the discrete constraint L(e^true ⊕ e^pred) = 0 over GF(2) via sigmoid-based Bernoulli parity probabilities. The CPND post-processing stage projects the raw prediction onto the feasible set using a precomputed left inverse of the augmented matrix [H; L], then performs greedy nullspace descent weighted by transformer log-likelihood ratios to find lower-weight recovery operators while preserving exact syndrome and logical-class consistency.

## Results

SAQ-Decoder achieves error thresholds of 10.99% (independent noise) and 18.6% (depolarizing noise) on the toric code, closely approaching the theoretical ML bounds of 11.0% and 18.9%. For rotated surface codes, thresholds reach 10.7% and 18.3% respectively. The method also demonstrates linear computational scalability in syndrome size with only 1.2–1.9M parameters across code distances L=4 to L=10, compared to QECCT's 6.71M at L=10, and achieves 5× lower FLOPs and 4–5× faster inference than QECCT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAQ-Decoder outperforms all eight baselines across every tested code family and noise model. Against the strongest neural baseline QECCT, SAQ achieves a 0.8 pp higher threshold on toric codes (18.6% vs 17.8%) and 1.1 pp on rotated surface codes (18.3% vs 17.2%), while also using 3.5× fewer parameters at L=10. Against classical decoders, SAQ exceeds MWPM by 2.6 pp (18.6% vs 16.0%) and BP-OSD-2 by 2.6 pp under depolarizing noise on toric codes, and by 0.7 pp (10.99% vs 10.3%) under independent noise. Among other neural decoders, SAQ surpasses SU-NetQD by 2.3 pp (18.6% vs 16.3%), ML+UF by 2.4 pp (18.6% vs 16.2%), UIUF by 3.1 pp (18.6% vs 15.5%), and Astra by 1.3 pp on rotated surface codes (18.3% vs 17.0%). At specific operating points (e.g., toric L=6, p=0.09), SAQ reduces LER to 0.0363 versus 0.0492 for QuantumSMoE, 0.1143 for BP-OSD-2, and 0.1238 for MWPM, representing 26–71% relative LER reductions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer-based QEC decoder line initiated by QECCT (Choukroun & Wolf, 2024) and the dual-stream attention concept from CrossMPT (Park et al., 2024) for classical codes, but departs from both in critical ways: it replaces QECCT's bit-error-rate objective with a logical-error-rate-centric loss and replaces CrossMPT's symmetric cross-attention with an asymmetric syndrome-to-logical information flow. The CPND post-processing stage is a novel contribution that bridges the gap between learned soft predictions and the hard GF(2) syndrome-consistency constraint, drawing on algebraic coding theory (left inverses, nullspace descent) rather than prior neural decoding work. The differentiable logical-minimum-entropy loss, which smooths the discrete logical parity constraint via sigmoid-tanh identities, has no direct precedent in the QEC literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving error thresholds within 0.3 percentage points of the theoretical ML bound (18.6% vs 18.9% depolarizing; 10.99% vs 11.0% independent) while maintaining linear complexity in syndrome size addresses the central bottleneck for real-time fault-tolerant quantum computing, where microsecond-scale decoding is required. The framework's demonstrated generality across toric, rotated surface, color, and repetition codes under multiple noise models suggests it could serve as a practical decoder backbone for near-term quantum hardware. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger code distances (beyond L=11), whether it extends to non-topological codes like QLDPC with higher logical qubit counts, and whether the training data requirements (2.56M samples per run) remain tractable as code families grow in complexity.
