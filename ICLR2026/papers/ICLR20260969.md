## Problem

Current VLMs perform near-randomly even on simple 2×2 jigsaw puzzles, exposing fundamental gaps in visual perception and structured reasoning. While reinforcement learning offers a path to close these gaps, the scarcity and limited scalability of high-quality vision-language RL data—constrained by expensive human annotation or costly closed-source model synthesis—severely bottleneck progress.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260969/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a shuffled m×m grid of image patches (e.g., four patches labeled A–D for 2×2) presented as individual images, and must iteratively generate Python code snippets (swap, observe, crop, zoom) that are executed by an external environment returning updated jigsaw images as visual feedback. The final output is the correct piece arrangement enclosed in <answer> tags, with all intermediate reasoning in <think> tags and code in <code> tags. The interaction is strictly multi-turn: the model emits one action per turn, receives the resulting image, and continues until it commits to a final answer or exhausts the step budget.

## Method

AGILE formulates jigsaw solving as an agentic multi-turn interaction where the model generates executable Python code to swap tiles, observe the current state, crop regions, or zoom into fine-grained details, receiving visual feedback from the environment at each step. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: a cold-start SFT phase on 1.6K expert trajectories collected from Gemini 2.5 Pro (filtered for correctness and manually verified) to establish basic code-generation and instruction-following skills, followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning on 15.6K programmatically generated jigsaw images with controllable difficulty. The reward function combines an accuracy reward (all pieces correctly placed), a format reward (proper tag structure), and a step reward (penalizing excessive interactions, applied only upon correct completion to prevent reward hacking). A key design choice is that jigsaw data is synthesized entirely via code—shuffling images and recording ground-truth permutations—making the dataset arbitrarily scalable with strict supervision, directly addressing the multimodal RL data scarcity problem.

## Results

On the 2×2 jigsaw task, AGILE raises Qwen2.5-VL-7B accuracy from 9.5% to 82.8% (Score: 29.4% → 89.0%), and on the harder 3×3 setting from 0.4% to 20.8% (Score: 31.1% → 62.1%). Across 9 general vision benchmarks (HRBench4K, HRBench8K, VStarBench, MME-RealWorld, RealWorldQA, MMVP, BLINK, HallusionBench, MMMU), the jigsaw-trained model achieves an average of 65.2%, a +3.1% gain over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (62.1%), with the largest improvements on HRBench8K (+5.2%), HRBench4K (+4.2%), and VStarBench (+4.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the jigsaw task itself, AGILE (Qwen2.5-VL-7B + RL) dramatically outperforms all baselines: it achieves 82.8% average accuracy on 2×2, surpassing Gemini-2.5-Pro (46.4%), GPT-4o (41.1%), Qwen2.5-VL-72B (27.4%), and InternVL3-8B (4.1%). On the more challenging 3×3 setting, AGILE reaches 20.8% accuracy, exceeding Gemini-2.5-Pro (14.6%), GPT-4o (4.9%), and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (3.9%), while InternVL3 models score near zero. On the 9 general vision benchmarks, AGILE (65.2% avg) surpasses same-size baselines InternVL2.5-8B (61.6%) and LLaVA-OV-7B (58.8%), but remains below the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (69.6%) and InternVL2.5-78B (68.9%), indicating that the 7B model's gains from jigsaw RL are substantial yet do not fully close the gap to 72B/78B-scale models. Notably, AGILE's gains are most pronounced on high-resolution and fine-grained perception benchmarks (HRBench8K +5.2%, VStarBench +4.2%, MMVP +3.7%), while improvements on reasoning-heavy benchmarks like MMMU are more modest (+1.0%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the jigsaw-as-proxy-task paradigm introduced by Jigsaw-R1 (Wang et al., 2025) by reformulating jigsaw solving from a single-turn prediction into a multi-turn agentic interaction where the model generates executable Python code (swap, observe, crop, zoom) and receives fine-grained visual feedback from an external environment at each step. The RL training follows the GRPO objective from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024), and the broader positioning draws on the proxy-task literature (Logic-RL, Enigmata, Code2Logic, ViGaL) that uses structured, verifiable puzzles to elicit transferable reasoning. The key conceptual departure from Jigsaw-R1 is the interactive loop: rather than predicting the full arrangement in one shot, the model iteratively refines its state through code execution, which the authors argue is what enables the 7B model to surpass both GPT-4o and Gemini-2.5-Pro on the jigsaw task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, scalable answer to the data-scarcity bottleneck in multimodal RL: jigsaw instances are generated programmatically with controllable difficulty and inherent ground truth, eliminating the need for expensive human annotation or costly API calls to closed-source models. The demonstrated transfer from a narrow spatial-reconstruction task to nine diverse vision benchmarks (high-res understanding, fine-grained recognition, hallucination, etc.) suggests that structured visual manipulation is a more efficient training signal than general QA data at equal budget. Key limitations include the context-length constraint that restricts RL training to 2×2 grids (3×3 only via curriculum), and the modest +3.1% average gain on general benchmarks, which, while consistent, may not be sufficient for high-stakes applications. Open questions include whether the interactive formulation is strictly necessary versus a well-designed single-turn reward, and whether the approach scales to larger grids or more complex visual manipulation tasks.
