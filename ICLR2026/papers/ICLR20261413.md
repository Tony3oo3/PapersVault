## Problem

Disentangled dataset distillation methods that cluster in latent diffusion spaces have shown strong empirical results, but no prior work provides theoretical guarantees that the distilled datasets converge to the true data distribution. Additionally, existing methods like D4M use uniform-weight Wasserstein barycenters for clustering, which are suboptimal compared to weighted optimal quantization, leaving a gap in both theory and approximation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261413/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full training dataset with class labels, a pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion V1.5 encoder-decoder plus latent diffusion), and a target images-per-class (IPC) count K. It produces K synthetic images per class, each paired with a scalar weight derived from its k-means cluster size, plus soft labels computed by a pre-trained ResNet-18 classifier. The weights are non-uniform (sometimes spanning two orders of magnitude) and are applied multiplicatively to the per-sample loss during student training, which is the key structural difference from D4M's unweighted output.

## Method

DDOQ reformulates disentangled dataset distillation as optimal quantization in the latent space of a latent diffusion model: training images are encoded, clustered via CLVQ (mini-batch k-means) to obtain K centroids and their associated Voronoi-cell weights, then decoded through the diffusion model to produce synthetic images. The critical theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) proves that Wasserstein-2 convergence of the quantized latent distribution is preserved under the reverse diffusion SDE (pushforward), establishing consistency of the distilled image dataset with an O(K^{-1/d}) rate. Compared to D4M, the only algorithmic change is using the automatically-computed cluster weights in the training loss (min_θ Σ w·ℓ(x,y,θ)) rather than treating all synthetic samples equally, which reduces the latent Wasserstein distance by ~15–16% and yields uniformly better classification accuracy across IPC budgets and student architectures.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with a UNet-based latent diffusion backbone, DDOQ achieves 60.1% top-1 accuracy (ResNet-18) at IPC 100 and 63.4% at IPC 200, outperforming the prior SOTA disentangled method D4M (59.3% and 62.6% respectively) and all other baselines. With the stronger DiT backbone, DDOQ-DiT reaches 53.0% at IPC 10 and 62.7% at IPC 50 on ImageNet-1K, substantially outperforming diffusion-guidance methods (Minimax-IGD: 46.2% and 60.3%). The full ImageNet-1K reference accuracy is 69.8%, and DDOQ achieves a 30% reduction in the error gap versus D4M using ResNet-101 at IPC 200.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDOQ uniformly outperforms D4M (Su et al., CVPR 2024) across all IPCs, with the largest gap at IPC 10 (33.1% vs 27.9% ResNet-18) and a consistent 0.3–0.8% advantage at higher IPCs, attributed to the addition of optimal-quantization weights. Against SRe2L (Yin et al., NeurIPS 2023) and CDA (Yin & Shen, TMLR 2024), DDOQ wins at IPC 50, 100, and 200, with margins of 2.7–9.4% over SRe2L and 0.1–2.4% over CDA. RDED (Sun et al., CVPR 2024) outperforms DDOQ at IPC 10 (42.0% vs 33.1%) due to its patch-based aggregation, but DDOQ edges out RDED at IPC 50 (56.2% vs 56.5% is a near-tie; DDOQ wins on ResNet-101 at 63.6% vs 61.2%). TESLA (Cui et al., ICML 2023) is only reported at IPC 10 and is far behind (7.7%). With the DiT backbone, DDOQ-DiT significantly outperforms Minimax (Gu et al., CVPR 2024) and IGD (Chen et al., ICLR 2025) on ImageNet-1K: at IPC 10, DDOQ-DiT achieves 53.0% vs 46.2% (Minimax-IGD) and 45.5% (DiT-IGD); at IPC 50, 62.7% vs 60.3% and 59.8% respectively. On the 10-class subsets ImageWoof and ImageNette, DDOQ-DiT is competitive or better, winning at IPC 10 on both subsets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on D4M (Su et al., 2024), which performs k-means clustering in the latent space of a latent diffusion model for dataset distillation. The key conceptual departure is reframing the clustering objective from a Wasserstein barycenter (uniform weights) to an optimal quantization (non-uniform weights derived from Voronoi cell measures), and providing the first theoretical consistency guarantee for disentangled DD methods via classical optimal quantization theory and Wasserstein convergence through score-based diffusion. The paper also positions itself against bi-level optimization methods (SRe2L, CDA, RDED) and recent diffusion-guidance approaches (Minimax, IGD), arguing that the quantization-based approach avoids their computational intractability and model-architecture dependence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main contribution is theoretical: Theorem 1 proves that passing optimal quantizers through a diffusion-based generative prior yields consistent approximations of the true data distribution in image space, with convergence rate O(K^{-1/d}). This fills a notable gap in the DD literature, where prior methods lacked formal justification for whether distilled datasets actually approximate the training distribution. Open questions remain for bi-level and diffusion-guidance methods, and the empirical gains over D4M, while consistent, are modest (1–5 points), suggesting the theoretical framing may be more valuable than the practical algorithmic change. Future work on sharper bounds exploiting sub-Gaussianity or manifold structure, and on relating weights to learning difficulty, could further strengthen the approach.
