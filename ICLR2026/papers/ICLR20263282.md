## Problem

LoRA fine-tuning of large pre-trained models suffers from poor generalization under limited data, and the natural remedy—applying Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) to LoRA parameters—doubles training cost and confines adversarial perturbations to a rapidly collapsing low-rank subspace, limiting its ability to find flat minima in the full parameter space that actually matters at inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263282/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained weight matrix W₀ (Llama 2/3.1, Qwen 2.5, T5, or SDXL) plus a fine-tuning dataset and produces an adapted weight W = W₀ + B₁A₁ where only the primary LoRA module is retained and merged into the pretrained weights at inference, incurring zero additional inference cost. The auxiliary adversarial module (B₂, A₂) is discarded after training. For text-to-image generation, the adapted SDXL model accepts text prompts of the form 'a ToK icon of a <Instance>, in the style of ToK' and outputs 1024×1024 images.

## Method

Bi-LoRA augments standard LoRA with a second, auxiliary low-rank module (B₂, A₂) that models SAM's adversarial perturbation, yielding the composite weight W = W₀ + B₁A₁ + B₂A₂. During training, the primary module is updated by gradient descent while the auxiliary module is updated by gradient ascent in the same forward-backward pass, converting SAM's sequential two-step procedure into a single parallel step and roughly halving training time. The auxiliary perturbation space Col(B₂) is strictly decoupled from the primary optimization space Col(B₁), and because the auxiliary module converges substantially slower than the primary one, it explores perturbations beyond the restricted LoRA subspace, capturing sharpness in the full parameter space. A global Frobenius-norm clip on all auxiliary modules keeps the perturbation within a ρ-ball, faithfully mirroring SAM's original constraint. Theoretically, Bi-LoRA is shown to be equivalent to LoRA regularized by a Ky Fan r-norm on the gradient, and its ascent direction is proven to be non-negatively aligned with the previous full gradient, ensuring it increases the inner SAM objective.

## Results

On Llama 2-7B, Bi-LoRA achieves 60.32% on GSM8K, 27.20% PASS@1 on HumanEval, and 6.26 on MT-Bench, outperforming LoRA by +2.11, +2.45, and +0.34 respectively, while matching or exceeding Full FT on GSM8K and MT-Bench. On Llama 3.1-8B instruction following, Bi-LoRA reaches 63.67% MMLU, 51.53% DROP, 46.12% HEval, and 43.45% BBH (avg. 51.19), surpassing LoRA by up to +2.97 on HEval. All gains are achieved at ×1 training cost (one gradient step per iteration), compared to ×2 for LoRA-SAM. On Qwen 2.5-14B, Bi-LoRA achieves the highest average of 66.61, improving over LoRA-SAM by 0.98%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bi-LoRA consistently outperforms vanilla LoRA across all seven benchmarks in Table 1, with the largest gains on HEval (+2.97 on Llama 3.1-8B) and GSM8K (+2.11 on Llama 2-7B). Against LoRA-SAM (×2 cost), Bi-LoRA wins on every metric while halving training time (9.71s vs 19.23s per step on Llama 3.1-8B), e.g., 51.53 vs 50.94 on DROP and 46.12 vs 44.36 on HEval. Among efficient SAM variants, Bi-LoRA surpasses LoRA-nBAR (the strongest single-step SAM baseline) by +0.89 on HEval and +1.71 on DROP, and outperforms Flat-LoRA by +1.81 on HEval and +1.09 on DROP. Random perturbation baselines (ROP, RWP_full, RWP_LoRA) provide only marginal gains over LoRA and are uniformly outperformed by Bi-LoRA. In the extended comparison (Table A1), Bi-LoRA (avg. 51.19) beats the strongest LoRA variant DeLoRA (50.78) and both SAM-based methods WSAM and S²-SAM (50.02 each). When combined with advanced LoRA variants (Table 4), Bi-LoRA adds +1.45% average over standalone LoRA-GA/PiSSA/DoRA on GLUE tasks, confirming orthogonality with existing PEFT improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

Bi-LoRA sits at the intersection of two well-established lines: LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning and SAM (Foret et al., 2021) for sharpness-aware optimization. The key departure from LoRA-SAM (Li et al., 2024a) is architectural rather than algorithmic: instead of applying SAM's sequential perturbation-then-descent procedure to the same LoRA parameters, Bi-LoRA introduces a second, adversarial LoRA module that is updated by gradient ascent in parallel with the primary descent module. This is conceptually distinct from efficient-SAM variants (ESAM, LookSAM, Flat-LoRA, BAR) that reduce SAM's cost by skipping or approximating steps, and from LoRA variants (DoRA, PiSSA, LoRA-GA) that improve the optimization of a single adapter. The paper also contributes a theoretical reframing: Proposition 3 shows that, in the idealized fully-solved inner loop, Bi-LoRA reduces to LoRA with a Ky Fan r-norm gradient regularizer, connecting it to the regularization view of SAM (Yue et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Bi-LoRA makes SAM-level generalization gains practical for large-model fine-tuning by reducing the 2× training overhead of LoRA-SAM to roughly 1.1×, with no additional inference cost since the auxiliary module is discarded. The subspace-collapse analysis (Proposition 1, Figure 2c) is a useful diagnostic: it explains why naively applying SAM to LoRA parameters underperforms expectations and motivates the decoupled design. Limitations include the single-step inner optimization (the theoretical equivalence to the Ky Fan regularizer is approximate), the modest absolute gains (typically 1–2 percentage points), and evaluation capped at 14B parameters. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether the auxiliary rank and radius can be set adaptively per layer, and whether the parallel ascent-descent scheme interacts well with more complex optimizers or multi-task settings.
