## Problem

CT and MRI-based body composition analysis provides critical cardiometabolic health insights but is limited by radiation exposure, high cost, and infrastructure requirements that perpetuate health disparities. No prior benchmark has systematically linked external abdominal surface geometry with internal, CT-derived biomarkers at scale, leaving open whether consumer-grade depth sensing could replace clinical imaging for routine screening.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262703/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 384×384 grayscale PNG depth-map projection of a 3D abdominal surface mesh (derived from CT DICOM via a DICOM→STL→PNG pipeline with fixed camera positioning and 5000 smoothing iterations) and produces either a binary classification score for one of nine biomarker tasks (mortality, HCC-108/12/96/18/111, abdominal calcium score >1000, MI, T2D) or a continuous age estimate. Grayscale images are converted to 3-channel by tensor repetition, and classification thresholds are F1-optimized on validation rather than fixed at 0.5 to handle severe class imbalance.

## Method

AbdCTBench curates 23,506 CT-derived abdominal surface meshes from 18,719 patients, paired with 16 CT-derived biomarkers (via OSCAR segmentation), 87 HCC comorbidity labels, and 31 ICD-10 diagnosis codes, split 70/20/10 at the patient level. Seven architectures (ResNet-18/34/50, DenseNet-121, EfficientNet-B0, ViT-Small with DINOv2, Swin Transformer-Base) are benchmarked under a standardized protocol: AdamW with cosine annealing, full fine-tuning, dropout 0.2, and class-imbalance handling via inverse frequency weighting, balanced batch sampling, and F1-optimal threshold search. The key empirical finding is that smaller CNNs (ResNet-18/34, EfficientNet-B0) consistently match or surpass larger models, and medical-domain pretraining (RadImageNet) underperforms standard ImageNet initialization because the input is external surface geometry rather than raw CT imagery, making the predictive signal fundamentally different from conventional medical imaging.

## Results

Across 10 biomarker prediction tasks from 2D abdominal surface mesh images, the best-performing models achieve clinically relevant accuracy: age prediction with MAE 6.22 years (R²=0.757, EfficientNet-B0), mortality prediction with AUROC 0.839 (ResNet-18), and diabetes with chronic complications (HCC-18) detection with AUROC 0.801 (Swin Transformer-Base). Smaller CNNs (ResNet-18/34, EfficientNet-B0) consistently matched or surpassed larger models, while medical-domain pretraining (RadImageNet) and self-supervised pretraining (DINOv2) showed competitive but not superior performance. HCC-12 (cancers) was the weakest task, with all architectures clustering near chance (AUROC 0.571–0.591), suggesting limited biological plausibility of surface geometry predicting heterogeneous oncologic comorbidities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResNet-18 and ResNet-34 (He et al., CVPR 2016) emerge as the strongest overall performers: ResNet-18 achieves the best AUROC on Mortality (0.839) and HCC-111 (0.769), while ResNet-34 leads on Calcium Score (0.848) and T2D (0.742). EfficientNet-B0 (Tan & Le, ICML 2019) is the best regression model (Age MAE 6.223) and competitive on most binary tasks, trailing the top CNN by ≤0.01 AUROC. Swin Transformer-Base (Liu et al., ICCV 2021) wins on four biomarkers (MI 0.742, HCC-108 0.768, HCC-18 0.801, HCC-96 0.770), benefiting from its hierarchical local attention that balances local and global feature extraction. DenseNet-121 (Huang et al., CVPR 2017) is consistently in the top 3 but never achieves the best result on any task. ViT-Small with DINOv2 pretraining (Oquab et al., TMLR 2024) is competitive (top 2–3 on most tasks) but never achieves the best AUROC, suggesting self-supervised pretraining alone is insufficient for this indirect geometry-to-physiology mapping. Notably, ResNet-50 with RadImageNet medical-domain pretraining (Mei et al., Radiology AI 2022) underperforms all smaller ImageNet-pretrained CNNs on nearly every biomarker (e.g., Mortality 0.810 vs. ResNet-18's 0.839), indicating that domain-specific pretraining on raw CT/MRI/X-ray images does not transfer well to the fundamentally different task of reading external surface geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formulating the task of predicting internal CT-derived body composition biomarkers from external abdominal surface geometry, a problem the authors explicitly note has no prior benchmark. It departs from medical imaging benchmarks (CheXpert, MIMIC-CXR) that operate on internal anatomy captured by CT/MRI/X-ray, and from anthropometric or pose-estimation work that uses surface geometry for non-clinical purposes. The methodological toolkit is standard (ResNet, DenseNet, EfficientNet, ViT, Swin Transformer with ImageNet/DINOv2/RadImageNet pretraining), but the input modality—2D depth-map projections of CT-extracted surface meshes—is novel, requiring models to learn indirect geometry-to-physiology associations rather than direct anatomical feature recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AbdCTBench is the first large-scale public dataset (23,506 scans, 10 prediction tasks) linking external body surface geometry to internal clinical biomarkers, and its release with full processing pipeline could catalyze research toward radiation-free, consumer-device-based cardiometabolic screening. Key open questions include cross-site generalizability (single-site collection), validation on actual consumer LiDAR captures (no paired consumer-CT data exists yet), and whether the moderate AUROCs (~0.7–0.85) are sufficient for clinical deployment. The near-chance performance on HCC-12 (cancers) and the catastrophic multi-task learning failure (age MAE degrading from 6.2 to 14.5–72.6) delineate the current boundaries of what surface geometry can and cannot predict.
