## Problem

Existing approaches to training LLM tool-calling primarily rely on supervised fine-tuning with reasoning trajectories distilled from stronger models, which produces imitative pseudo-reasoning that limits generalization. This paper asks whether rule-based reinforcement learning (R1-style) can effectively train tool-calling models without any annotated reasoning traces, and how the RL pipeline should be designed relative to SFT.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263093/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decision step the model receives a user query, a set of candidate tools (each specified as a name, natural-language description, and parameter schema), and the full history of prior tool-call/observation pairs. The model must emit a structured response containing free-form reasoning enclosed in think tags followed by one or more tool invocations in tool_call tags (JSON arrays of {name, arguments} objects). The binary reward verifies both the presence of these structural tags and exact dictionary-level matching of tool names and argument key-value pairs, while permitting argument reordering.

## Method

Tool-N1 is trained with GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) using a binary reward that assigns 1 only when the output satisfies both format correctness (think and tool_call tags present) and tool-call correctness (exact match on function name and all argument key-value pairs against ground truth). The model generates N candidate responses per prompt; advantages are computed as the z-score of each response's reward within the group, and the policy is updated with a clipped objective plus a small KL penalty to the reference policy. Training data is drawn from xLAM (single-turn) and ToolACE (single- and multi-turn, segmented into per-step instances), requiring no reasoning annotations. A key empirical finding is that pure RL on this data outperforms the commonly adopted SFT-then-RL pipeline, and that binary rewards outperform fine-grained partial-credit rewards, particularly on realistic (Live) inputs.

## Results

On the BFCL benchmark, Tool-N1-14B achieves 85.97% overall accuracy, outperforming GPT-4o (83.97%) by approximately 2%, while Tool-N1-7B reaches 84.82%, surpassing GPT-4o by 0.85%. On API-Bank, Tool-N1-14B surpasses GPT-4o by 5.03%, and Tool-N1-7B achieves over 20% absolute improvement over its base model. On ACEBench, Tool-N1-7B gains over 30% absolute improvement compared to the base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct model, and both Tool-N1 variants outperform GPT-4o-mini.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tool-N1-7B (84.82%) and Tool-N1-14B (85.97%) both clearly outperform GPT-4o (83.97%) on BFCL overall, with the 14B model gaining ~2% and the 7B model gaining ~0.85%. Against specialized tool-calling models, Tool-N1-7B surpasses Hammer2.1-7B (81.88%) by 2.97% and ToolACE-8B (82.57%) by 2.25% on BFCL overall. On the Live subset (real-world queries), Tool-N1-14B achieves 81.42% versus GPT-4o's 79.83%, and Tool-N1-7B reaches 80.38% versus GPT-4o-mini's 76.50%. On API-Bank, Tool-N1-14B exceeds GPT-4o by 5.03%, and on ACEBench, Tool-N1-7B outperforms GPT-4o-mini. In the data-composition ablation, Tool-N1-7B trained solely on xLAM data (82.01% avg) outperforms the xLAM-8B SFT model (75.65%) by 6.36%, and Tool-N1-7B trained on ToolACE alone (83.63%) exceeds ToolACE-8B SFT (82.57%) by 1.62%, demonstrating that R1-style RL consistently yields better results than SFT on the same data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the R1-style rule-based RL paradigm (originally developed for mathematical reasoning in DeepSeek-R1 and DeepSeekMath) into the tool-calling domain, replacing the dominant SFT-on-distilled-trajectories pipeline with a binary reward that checks only format validity and functional correctness of tool invocations. It departs from prior tool-calling work (ToolACE, xLAM, Hammer) that relies on supervised fine-tuning over large synthetic datasets, and from prompting-based approaches (ReAct, etc.) that leave the model unmodified. The GRPO optimization algorithm and the think-tag template are directly inherited from the DeepSeek-R1 lineage, while the reward design is adapted to the structured dictionary matching of function calls.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that practitioners can skip the expensive step of distilling reasoning trajectories from frontier models and instead train tool-calling models with a simple binary reward on existing SFT data, reducing both data curation cost and the risk of pseudo-reasoning. The finding that SFT-then-RL does not clearly beat pure RL challenges a widely adopted training recipe, though the margin (83.24% vs. 83.17%) is small and tested on a single backbone family. Open questions include whether these conclusions hold for multi-turn tool-calling (which the paper largely excludes), for larger model scales, and whether the binary-reward advantage persists when tool argument spaces become more complex or ambiguous.
