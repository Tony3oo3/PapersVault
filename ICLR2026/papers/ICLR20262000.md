## Problem

Existing urban microclimate prediction methods—grid-based CNNs, homogeneous GNNs, and static graph models—fail to simultaneously capture the non-local, anisotropic spatial dependencies and the time-varying nature of physical processes like solar shading and wind-driven convection. In particular, standard GNNs apply a uniform message-passing mechanism that cannot distinguish between fundamentally different physical operators, and fixed graph topologies cannot represent how causal relationships (e.g., shadow direction) shift in real time with environmental conditions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of T_hist historical dynamic heterogeneous graphs (each with 2,500 nodes on a 4 m × 3 m grid) plus per-timestep global meteorological vectors (solar position, wind speed/direction, temperature, humidity) and a static 8-dimensional node feature matrix (building height, tree height, land cover). It outputs a multi-step prediction vector of length T_pred for each of six target variables (UTCI, PET, AT, MRT, WS, RH), producing full spatial heatmaps per timestep. The graph topology itself is not learned but reconstructed at every hour from physical rules, making the input a sequence of structurally varying graphs rather than a single fixed adjacency.

## Method

UrbanGraph encodes physical first principles as a hard structural constraint by dynamically reconstructing a heterogeneous graph at each timestep with five edge types: three dynamic edges (directional shading computed from solar elevation/azimuth, vegetation evapotranspiration scaled by incoming radiation, and anisotropic convective diffusion modulated by wind speed and direction) and two static edges (k-NN semantic similarity and Moore-neighborhood internal contiguity). A 3-layer RGCN serves as a physical operator approximator, assigning dedicated relation-specific weight matrices W_r to each edge type so that distinct physical processes are disentangled during message passing. Spatial representations are fused with global environmental and temporal embeddings via an MLP, then propagated through a 1-layer LSTM chosen to match the Markovian nature of physical transport. The final LSTM hidden state is decoded by a single-head MLP into all T_pred future timesteps simultaneously. This explicit causal pruning of non-physical edges reduces FLOPs by 73.8% versus the implicit-recurrence LRGCN baseline while improving R² from 0.8422 to 0.8542.

## Results

On the UMC4/12 dataset (396 urban blocks, 2,500 nodes each, 12-hour prediction horizon), UrbanGraph achieves the best R² of 0.8542 (±0.0044), RMSE of 1.0535, and MAE of 0.7866 for UTCI prediction. It reduces FLOPs by 73.8% (9.13×10⁹ vs. 3.49×10¹⁰) and training time by 21% (24.5s vs. 31.1s per epoch) compared to the strongest dynamic-graph baseline LRGCN (R²=0.8422). The model also generalizes to the UWF3D wind-field dataset (R²>0.88 for the u-component) and achieves city-scale validation against HiGTS with r=0.842.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UrbanGraph outperforms all 11 baselines across four categories on the primary UTCI task. Against the strongest dynamic-graph baseline LRGCN (R²=0.8422), UrbanGraph gains +1.2% R² while using 73.8% fewer FLOPs and training 21% faster, demonstrating that explicit physics-driven causal pruning is more efficient than implicit recurrent structure learning. Versus the best generative-graph model GAE-LSTM (R²=0.8494), UrbanGraph improves R² by +0.5% with 13% fewer FLOPs. Against grid-based methods, UrbanGraph surpasses Pix2Pix+PINN (R²=0.8320) by +2.2% R², confirming that hard structural constraints (physics in topology) yield better physical consistency than soft PDE-loss constraints. Among static spatio-temporal GNNs, UrbanGraph beats the best (GCN-LSTM, R²=0.8347) by +1.9% R², validating the necessity of dynamic topology. On the cross-domain UWF3D wind-field benchmark, UrbanGraph (R²>0.88) significantly outperforms the Grid-GCN baseline, confirming generalizability beyond thermal diffusion to vector flow dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

UrbanGraph builds directly on RGCN (Schlichtkrull et al., 2017) as its spatial encoder, repurposing relation-specific weight matrices as 'physical operator decoupling' mechanisms rather than generic semantic relation types. It departs from the dominant physics-informed learning paradigm (PINN-style soft loss constraints) by encoding physical laws as hard structural constraints in the graph topology itself, and from dynamic graph literature (LRGCN, T-GCN) by driving topology changes with exogenous first-principle rules (solar geometry, wind advection) rather than learned or data-driven edge evolution. The heterogeneous graph framing extends HGT/RGCN work by assigning edge types to fundamentally different physical processes (shading, evapotranspiration, convection) rather than to semantic categories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UMC4/12 dataset (~11.9M spatio-temporal data points across 396 urban blocks) is the first high-resolution benchmark for this task, enabling fair comparison of future methods. The finding that hard topological constraints yield ~23× the accuracy gain of soft PDE-loss constraints (ΔR² ≈ 0.070 vs 0.003) provides a clear design principle for physics-informed graph learning. A key open question is the trade-off between physical fidelity and flexibility: the authors acknowledge that predefined edge rules may miss latent interactions, and propose a 'Hybrid Topology' with learnable residual edges as future work. Generalization to the UWF3D Navier-Stokes dataset (R²>0.88) is encouraging but rests on a single additional domain, leaving the true breadth of the paradigm untested.
