## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based sequential recommenders optimize for accuracy but can generate uncredible content (fake news, misinformation), posing societal harm. Prior credible-recommendation methods assume full credibility labels for all uncredible items, an assumption that rarely holds in practice where only a small fraction of content is verified. The paper addresses how to steer diffusion models toward credible recommendations while preserving accuracy, under the realistic constraint of limited credibility labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261437/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user's chronologically ordered interaction sequence of content items, whose text descriptions are encoded into 3072-dimensional embeddings via LLaMA2-7B followed by an MLP projection. During training, only 20% of uncredible items carry credibility labels (semi-supervised setting), while full labels are available at test time. The output is a denoised item embedding generated via DDIM sampling, which is matched against candidate item embeddings by dot-product scoring to produce a top-K ranked recommendation list.

## Method

Disco introduces a dual-channel disentangled diffusion architecture: two MLP-based content learners extract preference-related and uncredible-content-related embeddings from each item, which are then aggregated into separate conditions (Transformer for preference, mean pooling for uncredible content). The training objective (Eq. 4–6) minimizes the diffusion loss conditioned on the preference signal while maximizing it conditioned on the uncredible signal, using a cosine-based loss for stability. To handle uncredible diffusion targets, a credible subspace projection maps the target into the null space of uncredible content features via SVD, with a progressive enhancement mechanism that incrementally detects and incorporates potential uncredible items as the model matures. A preference contrast term further enlarges the gap between positive and negative preference generation, and DDIM sampling is used for efficient inference.

## Results

Disco achieves the best performance across all three datasets (PolitiFact, GossipCop, MHMisinfo) on every metric. On GossipCop, Disco reaches HR@5=0.5236, NDCG@5=0.3996, CR@5=0.9277, and HC@5=0.4918, outperforming the strongest DM-based baseline PreferDiff (HR@5=0.4969, CR@5=0.8307) by ~5.4% in hit rate and ~9.7% in credible rate. On PolitiFact, Disco attains CR@5=0.9823 (vs. 0.9265 for DiffuRec) while also leading in accuracy (HR@5=0.2678 vs. 0.2606). On MHMisinfo, Disco achieves HC@5=0.3000, surpassing PreferDiff (0.2713) and DreamRec (0.2633) in the combined accuracy-credibility metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Disco outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Among DM-based methods, Disco beats PreferDiff (the strongest DM baseline) by 5.4% HR@5 and 9.7% CR@5 on GossipCop, and by 12.2% HR@5 and 6.1% CR@5 on MHMisinfo; on PolitiFact it edges out DiffuRec by 2.8% HR@5 and 6.0% CR@5. Credible-recommendation-specific methods (Rec4Mit, HDInt, PRISM) perform notably worse under the limited-label setting (only 20% of uncredible items labeled), confirming that their reliance on full credibility labels is a critical weakness. Traditional sequential methods (SASRec, GRU4Rec, Bert4Rec) and contrastive methods (CL4SRec, ContraRec) trail DM-based methods on accuracy but sometimes achieve comparable or higher CR@5, indicating they avoid uncredible content only by being less accurate overall. The combined HC@5 metric, which balances accuracy and credibility, shows Disco's advantage is most pronounced on GossipCop (0.4918 vs. 0.4523 for PreferDiff, a 8.7% relative gain) and MHMisinfo (0.3000 vs. 0.2713 for PreferDiff, a 10.6% relative gain).

## Relation to Prior Work

Disco extends the DM-based sequential recommendation paradigm established by DreamRec, DiffuRec, and PreferDiff by replacing their single-condition generation with a dual-channel disentangled architecture that separates preference signals from uncredible content signals. The credible subspace projection repurposes SVD-based null-space techniques from knowledge editing (AlphaEdit) and ID embedding learning (Alphafuse) to suppress uncredible content in diffusion targets. Unlike prior credible recommendation methods (Rec4Mit, HDInt, PRISM) that assume full credibility labels, Disco introduces a progressive enhancement mechanism that incrementally detects and incorporates unlabeled uncredible items, addressing a practical gap in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first DM-based method to handle credible content recommendation under limited label availability, a setting that reflects real-world conditions where most content remains unverified. The progressive enhancement strategy offers a practical path toward scalable credibility filtering without exhaustive annotation. However, the approach inherits the high embedding dimension requirement (3072) common to DM-based recommenders, limiting its practicality for large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the disentanglement and null-space projection can be made effective at lower dimensions, and whether the approach generalizes beyond text-based content to multimodal settings.
