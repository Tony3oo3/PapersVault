## Problem

State-of-the-art single-cell trajectory inference methods rely on computationally expensive optimal transport preprocessing (quadratic in sample size via Sinkhorn), multi-stage training pipelines, and do not learn explicit gene regulatory interactions. No existing method simultaneously achieves SOTA predictive accuracy, scales to large datasets without OT, and produces interpretable gene interaction weights that can be validated against biological databases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264512/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a gene expression vector x_t projected into a PCA latent space (typically 5, 50, or 100 dimensions), a continuous time point t, and an arbitrary time interval Δt, and produces a predicted gene expression vector x_{t+Δt} by analytically solving the latent ODE and projecting back. Uniquely, it also outputs a state-conditioned linear operator A_θ and per-gene interaction weights w_{j→i} in the original gene expression space, which encode the predicted local regulatory influence of gene j on gene i and can be directly inspected or validated against curated databases like TRRUST.

## Method

Cell-MNN is an encoder-decoder architecture whose latent dynamics are represented as a locally linearized ODE: at each operating point (z^(i), t^(i)), a 4-layer MLP (functioning as a hypernetwork) predicts a linear operator A_θ ∈ R^{d_z × d_z} that approximates the global nonlinear dynamics in a neighborhood. The operator is parameterized in eigen-decomposed form (A_θ = P_θ diag(λ_θ) P_θ^{-1}) with an invertibility regularizer, and the ODE is decoded via the closed-form solution z(t+Δt) = exp(A_θ Δt) z_t, eliminating the need for numerical ODE solvers. Training is fully end-to-end (no OT preprocessing), minimizing an MMD loss between model-induced and empirical marginals in latent space, plus a kinetic energy regularizer encouraging Benamou–Brenier-like flows. Gene interactions are extracted by projecting the local dynamics back to gene expression space as dx/dt = V_PCA A_θ V_PCA^T x, yielding explicit, context-dependent signed interaction weights that the paper validates against the TRRUST database with ~69% F1 on activation/repression classification.

## Results

On the 5D PCA single-cell interpolation benchmark, Cell-MNN achieves the best average EMD of 0.741 across Cite, EB, and Multi, outperforming the previous SOTA OT-MFM (0.776) and DeepRUOT (0.846). Cell-MNN is the only method that beats the OT-Interpolate upper bound (0.800) on all three datasets, with particularly strong gains on EB (0.690 vs. 0.749) and Multi (0.742 vs. 0.830). In the gene interaction discovery task, Cell-MNN with one eigenvalue fixed to zero achieves 69% average F1 on TRRUST validation, outperforming SCODE (46%) and OT-CFM Jacobian (48%) by roughly 20 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cell-MNN outperforms OT-MFM (previous SOTA) by 0.035 in average EMD, with the largest gap on Multi (0.742 vs. 0.890) and EB (0.690 vs. 0.713), though OT-MFM leads on Cite (0.724 vs. 0.791). Against DeepRUOT, Cell-MNN wins on all three datasets (avg 0.741 vs. 0.846), and both OT-CFM and DeepRUOT hit out-of-memory errors on 250K-cell inflated datasets while Cell-MNN scales without issue. In the amortized training experiment, Cell-MNN (avg EMD 0.768) beats OT-CFM (0.835) and I-CFM (0.925) when jointly trained on Cite and Multi. For gene interaction discovery, Cell-MNN (69% F1) substantially outperforms SCODE (46%) and OT-CFM Jacobian (48%) on TRRUST validation, with the one-eigenvalue-zero inductive bias improving F1 from 60% to 69%. On the GRN existence prediction task (Enrichment@500, AUROC), Cell-MNN (18.57, 0.659) is competitive with GRNBoost2 (20.43, 0.633) and SCODE (16.71, 0.686), though slightly behind on both metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cell-MNN adapts the Mechanistic Neural Network (MNN) architecture from Pervez et al. (2024) to the single-cell biology setting, where the key challenge is learning dynamics from snapshot data rather than full trajectories. It departs from the dominant line of single-cell interpolation work (TrajectoryNet, OT-CFM, OT-MFM, DeepRUOT) by eliminating optimal transport preprocessing entirely, and from Neural ODEs by predicting a state-conditioned local linear operator rather than a black-box velocity field. The locally linear ODE parameterization draws on control-theoretic precedents (LQR, Apollo navigation filter) but is novel in the single-cell context. Conceptually, the paper bridges two previously separate research threads—trajectory inference and gene regulatory network discovery—by showing that an explicit ODE representation can serve both purposes simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of OT preprocessing removes a quadratic-scaling bottleneck that has limited the practical applicability of SOTA single-cell dynamics models to large datasets, and the end-to-end design enables amortized training across datasets—a prerequisite for foundation models in single-cell biology. The ability to extract interpretable, signed gene interaction weights as a byproduct of trajectory inference (validated against TRRUST with ~20-point F1 improvement over baselines) opens a path toward hypothesis generation for less-studied genes. Limitations include the cubic complexity in latent dimension, the assumption that local linearity suffices over the prediction horizon, and validation on only three small datasets (~16K–33K cells). Future work on controller design for gene perturbations (e.g., CRISPR) and scaling to larger, more diverse datasets would strengthen the practical impact.
