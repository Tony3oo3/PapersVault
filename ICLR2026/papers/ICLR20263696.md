## Problem

Neural scaling laws are well understood for linear and random-features (lazy) models, but their theoretical characterization in the feature learning regime—where network weights genuinely adapt—remains largely open. In particular, no prior work provides a complete phase diagram of excess risk scaling exponents for nonlinear shallow networks trained with weight decay, nor a first-principles explanation of how the spectral structure of learned weights governs generalization performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263696/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The analysis takes i.i.d. Gaussian inputs x_μ ~ N(0, I_d), a teacher target with power-law decaying weights (θ*_i ~ N(0, di^{-2γ}) for the diagonal case, or a rotationally invariant S* with eigenvalues {√d·i^{-γ}} for the quadratic case), and a regularization strength λ. It produces (i) closed-form excess risk rates R_{n_eff,d}(λ) across all regimes of sample size, dimension, and regularization, (ii) the full empirical spectral distribution of the learned weight matrix (a Dirac mass at zero, a semi-circle bulk, and outlier spikes), and (iii) a phase diagram with explicit crossover boundaries. The two architectures are unified through the effective sample size n_eff = n (diagonal) or n_eff = n/d (quadratic), which absorbs the different measurement geometries.

## Method

The core technique is an exact mapping of two shallow network ERM problems to classical sparse estimation: a diagonal two-layer linear network with ℓ2 weight decay is shown (via AM-GM) to be equivalent to LASSO on the effective weights θ = a ⊙ w, while an over-parameterized quadratic network with ℓ2 weight decay maps to nuclear-norm-regularized PSD matrix sensing. For both problems, the authors apply approximate message passing (AMP) and its state evolution equations to derive deterministic fixed-point characterizations of the excess risk and the learned weight spectrum. They then extend these asymptotic formulas heuristically beyond the proportional regime (fixed n/d) to arbitrary scalings of n, d, and λ, validating the extension numerically. The key structural result is a 'universal' error decomposition into underfitting (unlearned spikes), overfitting (bulk second moment), and approximation error (perturbed learned outliers), each term mapping directly to a distinct spectral feature, which yields the complete phase diagram and explains the emergence of heavy-tailed weight spectra as a signature of good generalization.

## Results

The paper derives a complete phase diagram for the excess risk of shallow neural networks (diagonal linear and quadratic) in the feature learning regime. In the under-regularized, under-sampled regime (1 ≪ n_eff ≪ d, λ ≪ √(n_eff/d)), the excess risk scales as Θ(n_eff^{-1+1/(2γ)}), matching the classical minimax rate for LASSO over ℓ_q-balls. At interpolation (n_eff ~ d), the risk peaks at Θ(λ^{-2/3}), exhibiting a double-descent-like non-monotonicity. In the over-parameterized regime (n_eff ≫ d), the risk decays as Θ(d/n_eff), and optimally regularized ERM achieves Bayes-optimal rates in both the diagonal and quadratic network settings.

**Result 1: Excess risk rates R_{n_eff,d}(λ) for diagonal and quadratic networks (Δ > 0).**

| Regime (conditions on n_eff, d, λ) | Excess Risk Rate | Phase |
|---|---|---|
| 1 ≪ n_eff ≪ d, λ ≪ √(n_eff/d) | Θ(n_eff^{-1+1/(2γ)} + ρ(n_eff/d)) | Ia / IV |
| n_eff ~ d, λ ≪ 1 | Θ(λ^{-2/3}) | V (interpolation peak) |
| n_eff ≫ d, λ ≪ √(n_eff/d) | Θ(d/n_eff) | VIa / VIb |
| max(√(n_eff/d), n_eff/d^{γ+1/2}) ≪ λ ≪ n_eff/d^{1/2} | Θ((λ d^{1/2}/n_eff)^{2-1/γ}) | II |
| √(n_eff/d) ≪ λ ≪ n_eff/d^{γ+1/2} | Θ(λ² d²/n_eff²) | III |
| Otherwise | Θ(1) | Ib (rank collapse) |

where ρ(t) = -1/log(t) for diagonal networks and ρ(t) = t^{2/5} for quadratic networks. The crossover from fast decay to harmful overfitting occurs at n_eff^cross = (log d)^{(4γ-1)/(2γ-1)} (diagonal) or d^{4γ/(14γ-5)} (quadratic).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against competing methods in the traditional benchmarking sense, as it is a theoretical analysis. Instead, it recovers and extends several prior theoretical results. The minimax rate Θ(n_eff^{-1+1/(2γ)}) for the diagonal network matches the classical LASSO rate of Raskutti et al. (2011) and Donoho et al. (2011), while the matrix case (Phase II) recovers the rate from Negahban & Wainwright (2011, Corollary 2). A key new contribution is showing that optimally regularized ERM achieves Bayes-optimal rates for the quadratic network (matrix compressed sensing) setting, a result previously established only for the diagonal (LASSO) case. The double-descent-like interpolation peak at n_eff ~ d extends prior findings from linear models (Bartlett et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2024) to genuinely nonlinear feature-learning architectures. The spectral decomposition (Result 3) provides a first-principles explanation for the heavy-tailed weight spectra empirically observed by Martin et al. (2021) and Wang et al. (2023), connecting bulk eigenvalues to overfitting, unlearned spikes to underfitting, and outlier spikes to approximation error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural scaling laws literature, which has been largely confined to the lazy (random features/kernel) regime, into the feature learning regime by exploiting exact equivalences between diagonal linear networks and LASSO, and between quadratic networks and nuclear-norm-regularized matrix compressed sensing. The analytical backbone is the approximate message passing (AMP) and state evolution framework from compressed sensing (Donoho, Javanmard, Berthier), combined with the network-to-sparse-estimation mappings established by Neyshabur, Soudry, Pesme, Gunasekar, and Maillard. It also directly addresses the empirical spectral-properties-of-trained-networks literature (Martin & Mahoney, Thamm et al.) by providing a first-principles derivation of why heavy-tailed weight spectra correlate with better generalization, rather than merely observing the correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers a complete phase diagram of excess risk scaling exponents for feature learning in two canonical shallow architectures, revealing a universality between vector (LASSO) and matrix (compressed sensing) estimation that was not previously established. It provides the first theoretical explanation connecting the three error components (overfitting, underfitting, approximation) to distinct spectral features (bulk, unlearned spikes, learned outliers), grounding empirical observations about heavy-tailed spectra in a mathematical framework. Key limitations include restriction to two-layer networks with specific activations (linear-diagonal and quadratic), reliance on a conjectured non-asymptotic validity of state evolution beyond its proven proportional regime, and analysis of the global ERM minimum rather than SGD dynamics. Open directions include extending to deeper networks, non-quadratic activations, data with non-trivial covariance, and rigorous non-asymptotic bounds for LASSO and matrix sensing state evolution.
