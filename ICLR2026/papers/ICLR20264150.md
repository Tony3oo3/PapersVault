## Problem

Existing web-agent benchmarks score only whether an agent finishes a task, ignoring whether it does so safely or in compliance with organizational policies. This gap is critical because agents can achieve high completion rates while fabricating data, performing irreversible actions without consent, or violating policy hierarchies—behaviors that are invisible to raw completion-rate metrics but pose serious risks in enterprise deployment. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates policy adherence, safety, and trustworthiness alongside task success in realistic multi-application web environments.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes an agent's full action trajectory (a sequence of browser actions including clicks, form fills, navigations, and send_msg_to_user calls) executed within a BrowserGym environment, alongside the task intent and a set of active policy instances injected via a POLICY_CONTEXT block that encodes the org > user > task hierarchy. Evaluation is performed by modular per-policy evaluators (fuzzy string matching for consent messages, URL matching for boundary checks, sequence matching for action ordering, hallucination detection over all form inputs, and DOM/AXTree inspection) that produce a binary violation flag per policy. The output is a vector of scores: CR, CuP (completion with zero violations), pCuP (partial completion with zero violations), per-dimension Risk Ratio (violations normalized by policy count), and all-pass@k (fraction of tasks succeeding in every run of k trials).

## Method

ST-WEBAGENTBench is a configurable evaluation framework built on BrowserGym/WebArena that pairs 375 tasks across GitLab, ShoppingAdmin, and SuiteCRM with 3,057 policy instances spanning six orthogonal safety-trustworthiness dimensions (User Consent, Boundary & Scope, Strict Execution, Hierarchy Adherence, Robustness & Security, Error Handling). Each policy is a JSON template with a category, source level (organization/user/task), and a concrete constraint, evaluated by a dedicated function that inspects the agent trajectory for violations. The key metric, Completion under Policy (CuP), credits a task only if all success checks pass AND zero policy violations occur, directly penalizing unsafe completions that raw CR would reward. A Modality Challenge subset (80 tasks) isolates vision-only from DOM-only information to disentangle perceptual modality contributions to failures. The framework is extensible via YAML-based policy templates and a unified JSON task schema, and includes human-in-the-loop hooks (a simulated user-confirmation tool with auto-approval) that let agents defer or escalate uncertain decisions rather than acting unsafely.

## Results

Across three open SOTA web agents, the average raw Completion Rate (CR) is 24.3%, but the Completion under Policy (CuP) metric drops to 15.0%—a ~38% relative decline—meaning roughly 38% of nominally completed tasks violate at least one safety policy. AWM achieves the highest Partial Completion Rate (46.9%) and CuP (20%), while WebVoyager trails at 12.8% CR and 10.3% CuP. Under increasing policy load, CuP decays sharply from 18.2% (one active policy) to 7.1% (five or more policies), with a strong negative correlation (ρ = −0.71, p < 0.001). The strict all-pass@3 (success and policy-compliant in every run) remains very low: AWM 5.0%, WorkArena-Legacy 3.0%, WebVoyager 2.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks three open-source SOTA web agents—AWM, WorkArena-Legacy, and WebVoyager—on the new ST-WEBAGENTBENCH, revealing that none achieves a CuP above 20%, far below their nominal completion rates. AWM leads on raw capability (PCR 46.9%, CuP 20%) but incurs the most consent breaches (37 violations, risk ratio 0.44), suggesting its learning-from-experience loop bypasses ask-the-user safeguards. WorkArena-Legacy achieves a more balanced profile (26% CR, 15% CuP) with far fewer violations (4 consent, 16 strict-execution), indicating that simpler control logic can yield safer behavior at the cost of lower coverage. WebVoyager performs worst on both axes (12.8% CR, 10.3% CuP), though its narrow CR–CuP gap reflects that it seldom reaches states where policies apply rather than acting more safely. The key comparative insight is that the CR-to-CuP gap (~38% relative drop on average) is invisible to existing benchmarks that score only task completion, and 70% of all violations concentrate in the user-consent and strict-execution dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the WebArena/BrowserGym evaluation infrastructure by layering a policy-compliance evaluation on top of standard task-completion scoring. Prior web-agent benchmarks (Mind2Web, WebVoyager, WebArena, VisualWebArena, WorkArena, WebCanvas) all report only Completion Rate and ignore whether agents respect organizational, user, or task-level constraints; ST-WebAgentBench is the first to attach concrete policy templates to each task and score violations across six safety-trustworthiness dimensions. Conceptually, it draws on safe-RL's principle of merging effectiveness with compliance (Gu et al., 2022) and on policy-based agent controllers (SteP, Agent-E, GuardAgent), but repurposes these ideas as an application-agnostic evaluation layer rather than a runtime controller. It also distinguishes itself from non-web safety benchmarks (SafeAgentBench, Agent-SafetyBench, ASB, R-Judge) by grounding every violation in a concrete UI action within a realistic multi-step workflow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a practical gap that raw completion metrics hide: roughly 38% of nominally completed tasks violate at least one policy, and CuP collapses from 18.2% to 7.1% as active policy count grows from one to five or more, while CR stays flat. This suggests current agents lack mechanisms for reasoning over concurrent constraints, a critical bottleneck for enterprise deployment where dozens of policies coexist. Key limitations temper the findings: only three agents (all sharing a GPT-4o backbone) are evaluated, coverage spans three applications in English only, and pass@k is used due to API costs. Open questions include whether the six ST dimensions generalize across domains, how to scale policy-aware training, and whether the CuP metric can serve as a practical deployment gate once more diverse agents and applications are benchmarked.
