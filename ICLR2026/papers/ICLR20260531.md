## Problem

Personalizing text-to-image diffusion models to novel subjects from a few reference images typically causes overfitting, where the model ignores the user's prompt and collapses to reproducing the reference images. The paper identifies this as a fundamental objective-goal misalignment: existing training objectives (including prior-preservation loss) provide no guarantee of preserving the pretrained model's full output distribution, leading to distributional drift that undermines both diversity and prompt adherence. Prior mitigations rely on heuristic architectural constraints (freezing layers, restricting update directions) rather than explicitly bounding distributional deviation.

## Input / Output

The method takes 4–6 reference images of a user-specific subject and a text prompt containing a special token (e.g., 'sks') denoting the new concept, and produces personalized images that faithfully render the subject in diverse prompt-specified contexts. Personalization is performed by fine-tuning the denoiser in latent space (VAE-encoded) across backbones ranging from SD-1.5 to SD-3.0, with the pretrained weights frozen as a reference. A key practical distinction from DreamBooth is that no prior-preservation pre-sampling of class-prompt latents is required, eliminating the most time-consuming stage of the standard pipeline.

## Method

The paper proves that standard personalization objectives (Theorem 1) inevitably increase KL divergence from the pretrained distribution, and that Lipschitz continuity of the noise-prediction network in its parameters (Theorem 2) implies the KL divergence between any two parameter sets is bounded linearly by their parameter distance. This motivates replacing DreamBooth's prior-preservation loss with a simple L2 penalty on parameter deviation (‖θ_per − θ_base‖²), which serves as a relaxed surrogate for the Lipschitz constraint and requires no additional data or pre-sampling. The single hyperparameter λ explicitly controls the adaptation-preservation trade-off: larger λ preserves the pretrained distribution more strongly (higher CLIP-T) while smaller λ allows closer subject adaptation (higher DINO/CLIP-I). Empirically, this formulation improves subject fidelity and text alignment simultaneously across SD-1.5, SD-XL, and SD-3.0, while reducing training time by more than half compared to prior-preservation-based baselines.

## Results

On the DreamBooth personalization benchmark (30 subjects, SD-1.5 backbone), the proposed method achieves DINO 0.6394, CLIP-T 0.2976, and CLIP-I 0.7948, ranking first jointly across all three metrics. When added as a drop-in regularizer to existing personalization methods across SD-1.5, SD-XL, and SD-3.0 backbones (Table 1), it consistently improves DINO by +0.007 to +0.037 while maintaining or improving CLIP-T. Training time is reduced by more than 50% compared to prior-preservation baselines, with SDXL-CD seeing a reduction to less than one-fifth of baseline time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (DreamBooth + Lipschitz L2 regularization) ranks first jointly across DINO, CLIP-T, and CLIP-I, outperforming all eight baselines on the combined metric. Against DreamBooth (rank 3), it improves DINO by +0.037 and CLIP-T by +0.018 while also gaining on CLIP-I. Against IP-Adapter (rank 2), it wins on DINO (+0.009) and CLIP-T (+0.034) but trails on CLIP-I (0.7948 vs. 0.8318), reflecting IP-Adapter's stronger image-level similarity at the cost of weaker identity and prompt fidelity. Against DreamBooth-LoRA (rank 6), it substantially improves DINO (+0.062) and CLIP-I (+0.022) at a small CLIP-T cost (−0.012). Against Custom Diffusion (rank 7) and SVDiff (rank 8), it dominates on DINO (+0.083 and +0.256 respectively) and CLIP-I, though both baselines achieve slightly higher CLIP-T. Against OFT (rank 4), it wins on all three metrics, with the largest gap on CLIP-T (+0.061). The key finding is that baselines consistently exhibit a trade-off between text alignment and subject fidelity, whereas the proposed method alleviates this trade-off, achieving the best balance without sacrificing either objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DreamBooth's fine-tuning framework for subject-driven generation, replacing its prior-preservation loss (which requires costly pre-sampling from the pretrained model) with a Lipschitz-motivated L2 parameter regularization. It departs from the broader family of architectural-constraint methods (LoRA, SVDiff, OFT, Custom Diffusion) that achieve distributional preservation indirectly by freezing or restricting subsets of parameters, arguing instead for an explicit objective-level bound on distributional drift. The theoretical positioning draws on Tweedie's formula and the probability-flow ODE to connect parameter-space Lipschitz continuity to KL-divergence bounds, a connection previously explored in distribution-matching distillation literature but not applied to few-shot personalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical argument (Theorem 1) that DreamBooth's prior-preservation loss does not actually guarantee preservation of the full pretrained distribution—only the class-level sub-distribution—which reframes a widely-used remedy as fundamentally insufficient. Practically, the method eliminates the pre-sampling bottleneck, cutting training time by more than half while improving both subject fidelity and text alignment across SD-1.5, SD-XL, and SD-3.0. Open questions remain: the optimal λ varies per subject and backbone, the Lipschitz constant is only approximately estimable without access to pretraining data, and the authors acknowledge that adaptive per-parameter weighting (e.g., EWC-style Fisher information) could further improve the approach but is impractical without the original training set.
