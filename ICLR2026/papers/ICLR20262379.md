## Problem

Existing symbolic regression methods, including recent LLM-based approaches, instantiate only the hypothesize and experiment steps of the scientific discovery cycle but lack a principled analysis stage that diagnoses structural errors and converts them into explicit guidance for iterative refinement. This omission makes mapping-based methods brittle under distribution shift and forces search-based methods to rely on costly random exploration, leaving a gap between generating candidate equations and systematically correcting their structural deficiencies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tabular data as pairs (x_i, y_i) with x_i ∈ R^d and y_i ∈ R, and produces a symbolic equation as an executable Python function with tunable numeric constants fitted via BFGS optimization. Beyond the final equation, RESTART also maintains a persistent structure library of validated code snippets (each a tuple of name, description, and Python expression) that accumulate reusable structural knowledge across iterations. The LLM generates candidate equations as Python code snippets rather than raw symbolic strings, enabling direct execution and parameter fitting.

## Method

RESTART closes the hypothesize-experiment-analyze loop with a two-level guidance mechanism. At each iteration, it solves a boosting-style subproblem g(f_t(x), x) — a symbolic regression over both the current prediction and the raw features — to explicitly model the unexplained signal, then feeds this diagnostic into an adaptively constructed LLM prompt alongside few-shot exemplars and high-scoring structure snippets. A long-term structure library stores only performance-improving refinements (gated by a fitness score combining relative and absolute loss reduction) as executable code, preventing bloat while enabling cumulative knowledge reuse. The search is initialized with the E2E transformer for a strong nonlinear first hypothesis and uses an island-based population buffer with Boltzmann-weighted sampling to balance exploration and exploitation. The subproblem solver backend is pluggable (E2E, PySR, KAN, DSR), keeping the framework modular while the iterative refinement loop provides the core advantage over any single solver used in isolation.

## Results

On LLM-SRBench (in-distribution), RESTART achieves the lowest NMSE on Biology (0.001 vs. 0.016 for LLMSR, a 16× improvement) and Material Science (0.001), with the highest accuracy on Biology (77.08%) and Physics (85.23%). On out-of-distribution splits, RESTART attains 100% accuracy on Material Science and the best accuracy on Biology (52.08% vs. 45.83% for LLMSR) and Physics (71.59% vs. 65.91%). RESTART also converges faster, surpassing LLMSR's NMSE within the first 100 iterations and achieving comparable results using only 25% of the iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESTART outperforms the current SOTA LLM-based method LLMSR on Biology (NMSE 0.001 vs. 0.016; ACC 77.08% vs. 70.83%) and matches or slightly edges it on LSR-Transform (NMSE 0.157 vs. 0.160) and Physics (ACC 85.23% vs. 84.09%), while tying on Material Science (both 96% ACC). Against the GP-based PySR, RESTART dominates on Biology (NMSE 0.001 vs. 0.003; ACC 77.08% vs. 54.17%) and LSR-Transform (NMSE 0.157 vs. 0.175), though PySR retains a slight NMSE edge on Physics OOD (7.15 vs. 8.17). The RL-based DSR and mapping-based E2E baselines are substantially weaker across all domains, with DSR achieving at most 36% ACC and E2E struggling on LSR-Transform (NMSE 2.098). On OOD splits, RESTART achieves 100% ACC on Material Science (vs. 96% for PySR and LLMSR) and the best Biology ACC (52.08% vs. 45.83% for LLMSR), confirming that the boosting-guided refinement and structure library yield equations that generalize beyond the training distribution rather than merely fitting local noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

RESTART builds directly on the LLM-driven evolutionary paradigm of LLMSR (Shojaee et al., 2025a), adopting its island-based exemplar buffer and code-as-equation representation, and on E2E (Kamienny et al., 2022) for both initialization and subproblem solving. The key departure is the explicit analysis stage: prior LLM-SR methods (LLMSR, SGA, DrSR) implement only the hypothesize-experiment loop, feeding back loss values or residuals as weak signals, whereas RESTART formulates a boosting-style subproblem g(f(x), x) that explicitly models the unexplained signal and uses it as targeted structural guidance. Additionally, the improvement-gated structure library distinguishes RESTART from prior concept-library approaches (Grayeli et al., 2024) by storing executable code snippets rather than natural-language summaries and admitting only performance-validated entries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RESTART demonstrates that an explicit analysis stage—solving a residual subproblem and distilling validated structural motifs—yields meaningfully better out-of-distribution generalization than prior LLM-SR methods, with 100% accuracy on Materials Science OOD and near-exact recovery of functional forms on challenging Biology tasks. The real-world Bohmian mechanics case study (46 data points) shows practical data efficiency and prior-awareness. Open challenges include the computational overhead of repeated LLM queries and subproblem solving, the inability to verify correctness of stored structures, and whether the framework scales to higher-dimensional or multi-output scientific problems. The finding that structural refinement (full g(f(x),x)) substantially outperforms classical additive residual fitting in the ablation suggests that the *form* of the correction signal matters as much as its magnitude.
