## Problem

Existing real-time super-resolution methods are trained and evaluated on datasets (DIV2K, Vimeo90K) that lack the compression artifacts characteristic of streaming video, making their benchmarks poorly representative of real-world streaming scenarios. There is no comprehensive dataset or systematic benchmark that captures the diverse genres, resolutions, and codec-induced degradations of actual streaming content, and no real-time SR model is specifically optimized for this domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261080/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes individual low-resolution compressed video frames (360p or 720p, sourced from YouTube with natural VP9/H.264/AV1 codec artifacts) and produces high-resolution 1440p frames, supporting both 2× and 4× upscaling tracks. Unlike video SR methods that exploit temporal context, EfRLFN is deliberately designed as a single-image SR model to meet real-time FPS constraints on consumer GPUs, operating at 271 FPS (2×) and 68 FPS (4×) under TensorRT. The StreamSR dataset provides aligned LR-HR pairs from the same video at different resolutions rather than synthetic bicubic downscaling, preserving authentic compression degradations.

## Method

EfRLFN modifies the RLFN architecture with three key changes: (1) replacing ReLU with tanh activation in refinement modules to preserve both magnitude and sign of features for richer gradient flow; (2) substituting the heavier Enhanced Spatial Attention (ESA) with lightweight Efficient Channel Attention (ECA) based on global average pooling and a 1×1 convolution, reducing parameters from 0.4M to 0.37M while increasing FPS from 234 to 314; and (3) streamlining the reconstruction path by removing redundant skip connections. Training uses a composite loss combining Charbonnier (pixel-level robustness), VGG-19 perceptual (conv5_4 features), and Sobel edge (gradient-map fidelity) terms, replacing RLFN's two-stage contrastive loss with a single end-to-end procedure that reduces training time by 16%. The model is trained on the newly introduced StreamSR dataset of 5,200 YouTube videos (10M+ frames) spanning 20 content categories, and the paper further demonstrates that fine-tuning other models (SPAN, RLFN, ESPCN) on this dataset yields significant generalizable gains across standard benchmarks.

## Results

On the StreamSR 2× benchmark, EfRLFN (fine-tuned) achieves the best subjective score (3.33±0.14), highest PSNR (37.85 dB), lowest LPIPS (0.059), and highest CLIP-IQA (0.65) among all real-time SR models, while running at 271 FPS on an RTX 2080. On standard benchmarks (DIV2K 2×), EfRLFN attains 0.892 SSIM / 0.145 LPIPS, outperforming SPAN (0.890/0.175) and RLFN (0.881/0.178). The model also achieves 82.8 FPS under TensorRT for 2× upscaling, well above the 30 FPS real-time threshold. Fine-tuning other models on StreamSR yields substantial gains: SPAN's subjective score rises from 2.55 to 3.13, and RLFN's from 2.17 to 2.69.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EfRLFN outperforms all 11 real-time SR baselines on every evaluated metric (subjective, PSNR, LPIPS, CLIP-IQA, SSIM) while also achieving the highest FPS (271) among quality-competitive models. Against the closest real-time competitor SPAN^T, EfRLFN gains +0.20 subjective score, +0.12 dB PSNR, −0.004 LPIPS, and +0.04 CLIP-IQA, while running 4.5× faster (271 vs 60 FPS). Compared to the proprietary NVIDIA VSR, EfRLFN is preferred in 77.4% of pairwise user comparisons and is 5.2× faster. Fine-tuning on StreamSR produces large gains for existing models (SPAN: 2.55→3.13 subj; RLFN: 2.17→2.69 subj), confirming the dataset's value, yet even fine-tuned SPAN and RLFN remain below EfRLFN. Among non-real-time models, BasicVSR++ (4.87 subj, 15 FPS) and COMISR (4.32 subj, 7 FPS) still lead in quality, but at 18–39× lower throughput, making them impractical for streaming.

## Relation to Prior Work

EfRLFN is a direct architectural refinement of RLFN (Kong et al., 2022), replacing its RLFB blocks with ERLFB blocks that swap Enhanced Spatial Attention for the lighter Efficient Channel Attention and ReLU for tanh activation (an insight borrowed from SPAN, Wan et al., 2024). The training procedure departs from RLFN's two-stage contrastive-loss scheme in favor of a single-stage composite loss (Charbonnier + VGG perceptual + Sobel edge), drawing on standard components from the broader SR literature. The StreamSR dataset and the 11-model benchmark address a gap the authors identify in the NTIRE challenge lineage: prior real-time SR evaluations rely on clean-image datasets (DIV2K, Vimeo90K) that do not capture the compression artifacts of actual streaming codecs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The StreamSR dataset (5,200 multi-scale YouTube videos) and the large-scale subjective study (3,822 participants, 37,184 responses) are the most broadly useful contributions, providing the community with a realistic streaming-SR evaluation resource and raw preference data for training quality-assessment models. The finding that fine-tuning *any* real-time SR model on compressed-video pairs yields substantial gains (e.g., SPAN's subjective score jumps from 2.55 to 3.13) suggests that domain-specific data, not just architecture, is the key bottleneck for streaming SR. Limitations include the single-image formulation (no temporal modeling), restriction to YouTube codecs (VP9/H.264/AV1), and the fact that the architectural changes (ECA, tanh) are individually well-known; the contribution is primarily in their combination and the dataset/benchmark infrastructure.
