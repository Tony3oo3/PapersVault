## Problem

Existing motion planners based on rule-based or imitation learning struggle with long-tail driving scenarios (e.g., overtaking a stalled vehicle via a detour) because such cases are absent from training data and lack the commonsense reasoning needed for ambiguous situations. While VLMs offer promising generalization, prior integration approaches suffer from limited intention granularity (meta-actions), poor interpretability (hidden features), or redundant waypoint sequences that gain little from internet-scale pretraining.

## Input / Output

The VLM receives a single-frame BEV map (rendered with five element categories: lanes/agents/traffic lights/route/obstacles) plus a system prompt explaining map symbols and driving rules, and a user prompt requesting key points—no multi-frame surround-view camera images are required. The VLM outputs at most three ego-centric key points as (x, y, φ) tuples in meters and degrees, where x is forward, y is left, and φ is heading; all points except the final one are time-agnostic and capture geometric trajectory features. These key points are encoded via a PointEncoder and fed to the PLUTO-based motion planner, which combines them with structured scene features to produce the final ego trajectory.

## Method

BIRDriver is a hierarchical framework where a LoRA-fine-tuned VLM (Qwen2.5VL-3B) generates sparse key points from a BEV map, and a separately fine-tuned PLUTO motion planner converts them into a concrete trajectory. Key points are extracted from ground-truth trajectories using the Ramer-Douglas-Peucker algorithm with adaptive tolerance, yielding at most three points that compactly encode driving intention. To improve the VLM's numerical precision, the authors construct a composite fine-tuning dataset (838,824 samples at 10:1:2 ratio) combining a Key Point Dataset, a Spatial Localization Dataset (bridging pixel-to-physical distance), and a Driving Scene Stepwise Dataset (scene classification before key-point prediction), and introduce a weighted SFT loss that assigns tiered, linearly decaying weights to numeric tokens, decimal points, and sign symbols (α=5) to prioritize higher-order digits. The motion planner is fine-tuned independently with Gaussian noise augmentation calibrated to the VLM's mean absolute key-point error, and at inference time the previous timestep's final planned point is appended as an additional key point for temporal consistency.

## Results

On the InterPlan long-tail benchmark, BIRDriver achieves a CLS-R score of 55.29, surpassing the base PLUTO planner (48.92) by 13.0% and Diffusion Planner (39.85) by 38.8%, establishing state-of-the-art performance. On Test14-hard, BIRDriver improves over PLUTO by 0.53 points in CLS-NR (80.56 vs. 80.03) and 3.41 points in CLS-R (80.33 vs. 76.92). On Test14-random, BIRDriver scores 91.46 (CLS-NR) and 91.26 (CLS-R), slightly trailing PDM-Closed's CLS-R of 91.64 but leading all learning-based methods. The VLM base model used is Qwen2.5VL-3B with LoRA fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BIRDriver outperforms its base planner PLUTO on Test14-hard (CLS-NR 80.56 vs. 80.03; CLS-R 80.33 vs. 76.92) and InterPlan (55.29 vs. 48.92), but slightly trails PLUTO on Test14-random CLS-NR (91.46 vs. 91.87). Against Diffusion Planner, BIRDriver wins decisively on InterPlan (55.29 vs. 39.85, +38.8%) but loses on Test14-random CLS-NR (91.46 vs. 93.85) and Test14-hard CLS-R (80.33 vs. 81.42). Compared to PlanAgent (the only other VLM-based method), BIRDriver leads on Test14-hard by 8.05 (CLS-NR) and 3.51 (CLS-R) points. BIRDriver substantially outperforms all other IL-based baselines (GameFormer, PlanTF, UrbanDriver, PDM-Open) across every benchmark, with the largest margins on InterPlan where most IL methods score below 40. Against the rule-based PDM-Closed, BIRDriver matches or exceeds it on Test14-random and Test14-hard but falls short on InterPlan (55.29 vs. 43.51 is actually a win for BIRDriver). The LLM-based InstructDriver is consistently the weakest VLM/LLM baseline, scoring 20+ points below BIRDriver on all benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIRDriver extends the hierarchical VLM-plus-planner paradigm introduced by Senna (meta-actions), AsyncDriver (hidden features), and DriveVLM (waypoint sequences) by proposing a more compact and interpretable interface: at most three relative (x, y, φ) key points extracted via the Ramer-Douglas-Peucker algorithm. It departs from prior VLM-based driving work by grounding the VLM's reasoning entirely in a single-frame BEV map rather than multi-frame surround-view images, and by using PLUTO's PointEncoder as the motion planner backbone. The weighted SFT loss for numerical token precision is a novel training technique that complements the composite auxiliary dataset (spatial localization + scene stepwise) designed to bridge the VLM's gap between pixel-space and physical-space distances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 38.8% improvement over Diffusion Planner on the InterPlan long-tail benchmark demonstrates that VLM commonsense reasoning can meaningfully close the gap in zero-shot driving scenarios (e.g., overtaking stalled vehicles via detour) where imitation learning fundamentally fails due to training-data absence. The BEV-only input design simplifies cross-platform deployment by eliminating the need to align heterogeneous camera sensors, and the weighted SFT loss offers a general-purpose technique for improving numerical output precision in any VLM fine-tuning task. Key open questions include onboard inference latency (the authors acknowledge this limitation), whether the approach transfers beyond the nuPlan simulator to real-world closed-loop testing, and whether the three-key-point abstraction is sufficient for all maneuver types or whether adaptive key-point counts would further improve performance.
