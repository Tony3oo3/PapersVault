## Problem

While RL-enhanced large reasoning models show strong general reasoning, rule-based reasoning remains challenging due to diverse rule formats, types, and complexity levels that cause domain imbalance during training. Existing RLVR methods rely on static, human-engineered data mixtures or costly external difficulty estimators, leading to sample inefficiency and poor cross-domain generalization. This paper asks whether adaptive, reward-driven domain scheduling can replace static curricula to improve both in-distribution and out-of-distribution rule-based reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262396/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each training and evaluation sample pairs a natural-language question with contextual logic rules (explicit premises or implicit constraints) and supporting facts, spanning 0–7 hop reasoning depths across deductive, inductive, and analytical forms. During training, rule order is randomly shuffled per sample to prevent positional memorization. The model produces a step-by-step reasoning chain enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer extracted via strict exact match in boolean (True/False/Unknown) or multiple-choice (A/B/C/D) format, which serves as the verifiable reward signal.

## Method

RULEREASONER combines GRPO-based on-policy RLVR (with KL divergence and entropy bonus removed) with a novel Domain-Aware Dynamic Sampling (DADS) algorithm. At each training step, DADS computes per-domain mean rewards from the previous batch, smooths them via an exponentially weighted moving average (α=0.5), and converts under-optimization scores (1 − smoothed reward) into normalized sampling weights through a temperature-scaled softmax with a minimum-weight floor ε. The next batch is then resampled proportionally to these weights, shifting compute from converged domains toward under-optimized ones without any external difficulty estimator or proxy model. Training regularization includes random rule-order shuffling and strict on-policy single-gradient-update-per-rollout to prevent entropy collapse, all instantiated on Qwen3-4B/8B base models over the curated RULECOLLECTION-32K dataset of 32K samples across eight rule-based reasoning tasks.

## Results

On eight in-distribution (ID) rule-based reasoning benchmarks, RULEREASONER-8B achieves an average pass@1 of 84.0% (±0.5), outperforming OpenAI-o1 (79.9%) by 4.1 points, Claude-3.7-Sonnet (79.5%) by 4.5 points, and DeepSeek-R1 (66.7%) by 17.3 points. On three out-of-distribution (OOD) benchmarks (BBH, ProverQA, BBEH), RULEREASONER-8B reaches an average pass@1 of 81.7%, a 10.4-point improvement over OpenAI-o1 and a 67.5-point gain over the Qwen3-8B-Base model (14.2%). The 4B variant achieves 79.2% ID and 78.3% OOD average, demonstrating that effective rule-based reasoning can be learned at smaller model scales. RULEREASONER also achieves comparable OOD performance with ~72 fewer training steps than DAPO (~1.4× acceleration).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RULEREASONER-8B outperforms all frontier LLMs on ID tasks: it beats OpenAI-o1 by 4.1 points (84.0 vs 79.9), Claude-3.7-Sonnet by 4.5 points, and DeepSeek-R1 by 17.3 points, with the largest gaps on Clutrr (+43.3 over o1) and FOLIO (+7.7 over o1). Against prior rule-based reasoners (HtT, Chain-of-Logic, RGFB), RULEREASONER-8B leads by 4.0–6.3 points on average, showing that RLVR training yields stronger rule utilization than prompting-based approaches built on o3-mini. Among RLVR baselines, RULEREASONER-8B surpasses DAPO by 4.8 points and ADARFT by 2.3 points on ID, while on OOD it leads DAPO by 6.3 points (81.7 vs 75.4) and ADARFT by 3.6 points (81.7 vs 78.1). SFT with long CoT distillation (81.9% ID) approaches RULEREASONER on in-distribution tasks but lags significantly on OOD (34.4% vs 47.5% improvement), confirming that RL generalizes to unseen rules while SFT memorizes. The 4B model (79.2% ID, 78.3% OOD) remains competitive with frontier models, demonstrating that domain-aware dynamic sampling enables effective rule learning even at smaller scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its policy optimization backbone, extending the RLVR paradigm from single-domain math/code reasoning to multi-domain rule-based reasoning. It departs from prior RLVR efficiency methods (DAPO's rollout filtering, ADARFT's proxy-model difficulty estimation) by introducing DADS, an online domain-reweighting scheduler that uses only verifiable rewards and exponentially weighted moving averages to adaptively allocate compute across domains. The curated RULECOLLECTION-32K dataset positions the work against prior rule-based reasoning approaches (HtT, Chain-of-Logic, RGFB) that rely on prompting or distillation rather than RL, and against static curriculum strategies (easy-to-hard, data-balance) that lack online adaptivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 4B/8B model trained with domain-aware dynamic sampling can outperform frontier closed models (o1, Claude-3.7-Sonnet) on rule-based reasoning by 4–10% while using ~1.4× fewer training steps, suggesting that targeted RL with adaptive data scheduling is a viable path for small models to match or exceed large generalists on structured reasoning. Key limitations include unverified scalability beyond 8B, reliance on a static rule set (no dynamic rule discovery), and sensitivity to noisy/redundant rules. Open questions include whether DADS generalizes to non-verifiable reward settings, how it interacts with test-time scaling, and whether the emergent self-verification behavior observed in case studies is robust across domains.
