## Problem

Existing bandit learning algorithms for decentralized matching markets, such as ETGS, assume that stochastic rewards reflect true preferences, yet in practice an adaptive adversary can corrupt feedback to mislead players into persistently matching with suboptimal arms, causing linear regret. This paper is the first to study bandit learning in two-sided matching markets under adversarial corruptions, addressing both the known-corruption and unknown-corruption settings.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes N players and K arms (N ≤ K) with 1-subgaussian rewards in [0,1] that may be adversarially perturbed, a horizon T, and either a known corruption budget C or a tunable sub-phase length d. The adversary is adaptive, meaning corruptions at round t can depend on all past matches and stochastic rewards. The output is the player-optimal stable matching m* along with theoretical regret upper bounds of O(K log T/Δ² + KC/Δ) for known C and O(K log^{1.5}T(log T+C)/Δ² + K log²T(log T+C)/Δ) for unknown C, plus a matching lower bound.

## Method

For known corruption, the paper widens the standard UCB/LCB confidence intervals by an additive C/T_{i,j} term, yielding a robust ETGS variant. For unknown corruption, the core contribution is a Multi-layer ETGS race method that maintains log T parallel ETGS instances with geometrically decreasing sampling probabilities (2^{-ℓ}), so that instances with probability ≤ 1/C experience only O(d log T) corruption with high probability. A critical design challenge is that independent per-round layer sampling causes frequent matching conflicts in the decentralized setting; the paper resolves this with a sub-phase-level synchronization mechanism where an elected leader samples the active layer at the end of each sub-phase of fixed length d and broadcasts the choice via arm pulls. To bound the corruption under this sub-phase-level sampling, the authors derive a novel martingale concentration inequality (extending the per-round Bernstein-style bound of Lykouris et al. to sub-phase granularity). The sub-phase length d introduces a fundamental trade-off between communication overhead (more frequent synchronization) and learning efficiency (larger d amplifies per-sub-phase corruption), and the optimal choice d = O(√(log T)) minimizes the resulting regret bound.

## Results

For the known corruption setting, the robust ETGS variant achieves player-optimal stable regret O(K log T / Δ² + KC/Δ), which matches the lower bound Ω(max{(i−1)(log T/Δ̃² + C/K), K log T/Δ̃ + C}) up to the Δ vs. Δ̃ gap. For the unknown corruption setting, the Multi-layer ETGS race method achieves regret O(K log^{1.5} T (log T + C)/Δ² + K log² T (log T + C)/Δ) with sublinear communication cost. In simulations (N=5, K=5, T=500,000, C=4,000), both proposed algorithms incur the lowest cumulative player-optimal stable regret among all baselines, and the optimal sub-phase length d≈4 (matching the theoretical prediction d=O(√log T)≈4.3) yields the best empirical performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines in decentralized matching markets: Vanilla ETGS (Kong & Li, SODA 2023), Phased ETC (Basu et al., ICML 2021), and AETGS-E (Kong et al., NeurIPS 2024). Under adversarial corruption (C=4,000, T=500,000), both proposed algorithms (robust ETGS with widened CIs and Multi-layer ETGS race) achieve the lowest cumulative player-optimal stable regret, while all three baselines suffer significantly higher regret due to their vulnerability to corrupted feedback. Theoretically, the known-C algorithm's regret bound is near-optimal, matching the lower bound up to the fundamental Δ vs. Δ̃ gap that remains open even without corruption. The unknown-C Multi-layer ETGS race incurs an additional multiplicative log² T factor over the lower bound, attributed to maintaining log T ETGS instances and the synchronization communication overhead. The algorithm with prior knowledge of C consistently outperforms the unknown-C variant under the same corruption budget, as expected from the tighter confidence intervals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research lines: bandit learning in decentralized matching markets (Kong & Li 2023's ETGS, Liu et al. 2021, Basu et al. 2021) and stochastic bandits robust to adversarial corruptions (Lykouris et al. 2018, Gupta et al. 2019). The key departure is that directly transplanting Lykouris et al.'s per-round randomized sampling into the ETGS framework causes frequent matching conflicts in decentralized settings, since players independently selecting different algorithm instances break the Round-Robin coordination. To resolve this, the paper introduces a sub-phase-level synchronization mechanism with an elected leader, along with a novel martingale concentration inequality tailored to the sub-phase granularity rather than per-round sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize and solve bandit learning in matching markets under adversarial corruptions, addressing a practical vulnerability where corrupted feedback (e.g., click fraud, inflated performance metrics) can mislead players into suboptimal stable matches with linear regret. The near-optimality of the known-corruption algorithm (matching the lower bound up to gap definitions) provides a clean theoretical foundation. However, the unknown-corruption setting incurs an additional log²T factor from maintaining log T ETGS instances and synchronization communication, leaving a gap to the lower bound. An important open direction identified by the authors is achieving higher communication efficiency in the unknown-corruption regime, and the fundamental mismatch between Δ and Δ̃ in matching market regret bounds remains an open problem even without corruption.
