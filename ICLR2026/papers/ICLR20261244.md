## Problem

Existing 3D chromosome reconstruction methods—whether traditional numerical optimizers or deep learning models—suffer from limited expressiveness and poor generalization across Hi-C resolutions. They lack explicit geometric priors (e.g., principal axes, directional chain structure), and equivariant constraints like SO(3) symmetry limit the ability to model asymmetric structures such as anchored loops. This paper addresses the need for a resolution-agnostic framework that robustly reconstructs 3D chromosome conformations from noisy, multi-resolution Hi-C contact data.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of N initial 3D coordinates (one per genomic bin) produced by a numerical method (3DMAX or LorDG) from a Hi-C contact matrix at a given resolution (40 kb to 1 MB). It outputs refined 3D coordinates for the same N bins, with the model trained and evaluated independently at each resolution. The framework also supports cross-resolution transfer, where a model trained at a coarser resolution (e.g., 320 kb) is applied to predict structures at finer resolutions (160 kb, 80 kb, 40 kb) without retraining.

## Method

InertialGenome is a Transformer-based framework with three core components. First, inertial frame canonicalization aligns each chromosome to its principal axes of the inertia tensor (with chirality correction), producing a pose-invariant coordinate representation that removes arbitrary rotations and translations. Second, a geometry-aware positional encoding combines a 3D Rotary Position Encoding (decomposed into three independent 2D rotary subspaces for the xy, yz, and zx planes, yielding a 6D embedding) with a Nyström-based multi-scale RBF kernel projection that captures long-range pairwise distances via learnable anchor points without computing the full distance matrix. Third, a structure-aware fusion module concatenates the base embedding, normalized directional coordinates, and Nyström features, then processes them through stacked Transformer layers. Training uses a hybrid loss combining bidirectional KL divergence on neighborhood probability distributions (preserving global topology) and a value-weighted MSE that emphasizes high-confidence short-range contacts.

## Results

On the Frontal cortex cell dataset, IG-3DMAX achieves a distance Spearman correlation (dSCC) of 0.9006 and distance RMSE (dRMSE) of 0.1697 at 320 kb resolution, outperforming the strongest baseline HiCEGNN (dSCC 0.5804, dRMSE 0.2744) by 55% in dSCC and 38% in dRMSE. At the finest resolution (40 kb), IG-3DMAX maintains dSCC of 0.7187 and dRMSE of 0.2410, while HiCEGNN degrades to dSCC 0.2506 and dRMSE 0.4317. On the B-Lymphocyte dataset, IG-3DMAX reaches dSCC 0.9209 and dRMSE 0.0822 at 1 MB, again surpassing all baselines across four resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InertialGenome (IG-3DMAX) decisively outperforms all four baselines on both dSCC and dRMSE across every resolution tested. Against the strongest deep learning baseline HiCEGNN (SO(3)-equivariant GNN), IG-3DMAX improves dSCC from 0.5804 to 0.9006 (+55%) and reduces dRMSE from 0.2744 to 0.1697 (−38%) at 320 kb on Frontal cortex; the gap widens at 40 kb where HiCEGNN drops to dSCC 0.2506 while IG-3DMAX holds 0.7187. The classical numerical methods 3DMAX and LorDG are orders of magnitude worse in dRMSE (23–96 vs. 0.17), reflecting their lack of direct distance supervision, though LorDG achieves moderate dSCC (0.6681) at coarse resolution. HiC-GNN, the earliest GNN baseline, performs worst overall (dSCC 0.2432, dRMSE 0.8366 at 320 kb). On the B-Lymphocyte dataset, IG-3DMAX leads HiCEGNN by 4% dSCC (0.9209 vs. 0.8847) at 1 MB and by 9% at 100 kb (0.8708 vs. 0.8017), confirming consistent superiority across cell types.

## Relation to Prior Work

InertialGenome extends the inertial-frame canonicalization principle from InertialAR (Li et al., 2025), which was designed for autoregressive 3D molecule generation, to the distinct domain of chromosome structure reconstruction. It also generalizes Rotary Position Encoding from the 2D sequence setting (Su et al., 2024) to 3D Euclidean space via a 6D block-diagonal rotation operator, and augments it with a Nyström-based RBF kernel embedding to capture long-range pairwise distances. Conceptually, the paper departs from the equivariant GNN paradigm (HiCEGNN) by decoupling geometric invariance from the network architecture—imposing pose invariance through a preprocessing step (inertial frame alignment) rather than through SO(3)-equivariant layers—arguing that this preserves model expressiveness for asymmetric structures like anchored loops.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The resolution-agnostic capability and cross-resolution transfer (up to 5% dSCC gain from 320kb→40kb) address a practical bottleneck: high-resolution Hi-C data is sparse and noisy, so leveraging low-resolution maps as structural priors is directly useful for experimentalists. The Davis-Kahan-based stability analysis explaining why Gram-matrix inputs fail while physics-based reconstructions succeed provides a useful diagnostic for practitioners choosing initial coordinate generators. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full-genome (multi-chromosome) reconstruction, whether multi-modal data (e.g., FISH, SPRITE) can be integrated, and whether the inertial-frame + Transformer paradigm generalizes beyond chromosomes to other 3D biomolecular structures.
