## Problem

Vision transformers excel at image classification through global self-attention but lose fine-grained spatial detail critical for dense prediction tasks like semantic segmentation. The classification objective, which supervises only the [CLS] token, provides no direct gradient to patch outputs, and global attention progressively aligns patch tokens with the [CLS] representation, eroding local structure. The paper asks whether ViTs can be made segmentation-friendly after standard classification pretraining without altering the training regime or sacrificing classification accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264853/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (224×224 for classification, 512×512 or 480×480 for segmentation) and is a drop-in add-on to any existing ViT backbone, requiring no change to input format or output structure. For classification, it produces a single class label via the [CLS] token; for segmentation, it outputs per-patch feature vectors (hw spatial tokens) that feed a simple 1-layer MLP decoder on frozen backbone weights. The only architectural additions are a small per-layer weight matrix for Gaussian variance prediction (d×2) and a per-query scaling vector (d×1), totaling ~2,340 new parameters for a Base model.

## Method

LocAt introduces two modular components. First, Gaussian-Augmented (GAug) attention adds a spatially-centered Gaussian kernel to the attention logits: the per-patch variance is predicted from the spatial query matrix via a learnable projection with scaled sigmoid, and a per-query softplus-scaled coefficient α balances the Gaussian contribution against the original logits, yielding a soft, data-dependent locality bias rather than a hard window. Second, Patch Representation Refinement (PRR) replaces the standard [CLS]-only classification head with a parameter-free multi-head self-attention over all tokens, which aggregates patch information non-uniformly and routes classification gradients back to spatial patch outputs. The two components are coupled through the gradient path: without PRR, the last-layer GAug parameters receive no gradient from the classification loss, so PRR is essential for GAug to be effectively learned in the final blocks. The entire add-on is objective-agnostic, compatible with self-supervised pretraining (validated with DINO), and applicable to diverse ViT variants including Swin, RegViT, RoPEViT, and Jumbo.

## Results

LocAtViT delivers substantial segmentation gains over vanilla ViT while preserving or improving classification: on ADE20K, LocAtViT Tiny achieves 23.47% mIoU (+6.17 over ViT's 17.30%) and LocAtViT Base reaches 32.64% (+4.24 over 28.40%), with similar improvements on PASCAL Context (+4.86/+2.25) and COCO Stuff (+5.86/+3.19). Classification accuracy also improves, e.g., LocAtViT Tiny scores 73.94% top-1 on ImageNet-1K vs. 72.39% for ViT. The gains are consistent across five backbone families (ViT, Swin, RegViT, RoPEViT, Jumbo) and both Tiny and Base sizes, with the largest improvements on architectures with unrestricted global attention (e.g., +8.41% ADE20K for RegViT Tiny).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla ViT, LocAtViT wins on all three segmentation benchmarks by 4–6% (Tiny) and 2–4% (Base) mIoU while also improving ImageNet top-1 by 1.3–1.6%. Compared to Swin Transformer, LocAt still yields consistent but smaller segmentation gains (+0.75 to +1.33 mIoU), which the authors attribute to Swin's windowed attention already encoding locality. The largest relative improvements appear over RegViT (register tokens), where LocAt adds +8.41% ADE20K mIoU for Tiny and +4.78% for Base, suggesting that register tokens and GAug address complementary aspects of representation quality. Against RoPEViT, LocAtViT gains +5.31% (Tiny) and +3.56% (Base) on ADE20K, confirming compatibility with modern positional encodings. For Jumbo, gains are modest (+1.29 to +3.49 mIoU) but still positive. In the broader comparison (Appendix Table 6), LocAtViT Base (32.64% ADE20K) outperforms all prior hybrid architectures—CvT-21 (21.40%), Conformer (22.11%), ConViT (23.08%), Twins (30.47%), DaViT (30.68%), and GCViT (30.91%)—on segmentation, though it trails DaViT (84.64%) and GCViT (84.47%) on ImageNet classification (82.31%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the vanilla ViT architecture, proposing a lightweight plug-and-play add-on (LocAt) that combines two ideas: a learnable Gaussian kernel added to attention logits (GAug) to bias tokens toward local neighborhoods, and a parameter-free multi-head self-attention before the classification head (PRR) to fix gradient flow to patch tokens. It positions itself as complementary to hierarchical backbones (Swin, PVT), convolutional hybrids (CvT, ConViT), and windowed/sparse attention schemes, which all require non-trivial architectural changes, whereas LocAt adds only ~2,340 parameters to a Base model. The PRR component is explicitly framed as an alternative to GAP, addressing a gradient-flow issue the authors argue is overlooked in prior work, and the approach is shown to be orthogonal to positional encoding choices (RoPE, registers).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because ViTs are the default backbone in most vision foundation models (CLIP, DINO, SAM, DINOv2), a minimal modification that improves dense-prediction representations without altering the training regime has high practical value for the broader ecosystem. The paper's diagnostic showing that patch tokens progressively collapse toward the [CLS] token in vanilla ViT provides a concrete mechanism explaining why classification-trained ViTs underperform on segmentation. Key open questions include whether the gains persist at CLIP/DINOv2 scale (beyond the authors' computational budget), whether the approach transfers to non-natural-image domains (medical, remote sensing), and whether the modest gains on windowed-attention backbones (Swin, GCViT) indicate a fundamental limitation when locality is already hard-coded.
