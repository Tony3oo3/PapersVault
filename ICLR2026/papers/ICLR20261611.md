## Problem

Existing PEFT methods designed for vision or language tasks apply uniform parameter updates across all downstream tasks, failing to account for the inherent heterogeneity of weather applications—varying physical variables, resolution regimes (from hydrostatic to convective-scale), and spatiotemporal coverage. This one-size-fits-all approach leads to significant performance gaps versus full fine-tuning when adapting large Weather Foundation Models to diverse meteorological tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261611/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes gridded meteorological data whose format varies substantially across tasks: 68 atmospheric variables at 5.625° (32×64 grid) for global downscaling, 10-member ensemble predictions at 0.5° for post-processing, and 10 variables at 0.25° over a regional domain for precipitation forecasting. Outputs are task-specific: high-resolution fields at 1.40625° (128×256), corrected ensemble mean/std distributions, or 6-hour precipitation accumulation at 12/24/36-hour lead times. Unusually, the encoder's embedding weight matrix itself is processed as a source of task-specific information to generate adaptive prompts, rather than treating the encoder as a frozen feature extractor.

## Method

WeatherPEFT integrates two synergistic modules operating at different stages of fine-tuning. During the forward pass, Task-Adaptive Dynamic Prompting (TADP) processes the encoder embedding weights through three sequential adapters (HW-Adapter for spatial/resolution patterns, V-Adapter for inter-variable dependencies, D-Adapter for abstract atmospheric characteristics), then applies self-attention to couple variable and resolution features, producing soft prompt tokens concatenated to input tokens at every backbone layer. During backpropagation, Stochastic Fisher-Guided Adaptive Selection (SFAS) approximates the diagonal Fisher information matrix to score parameter sensitivity to the task objective, then adds a linearly annealed uniform stochastic component to stabilize early-training selection against noise. Only the Top-k highest-scoring parameters are updated per step, preserving invariant pre-trained knowledge while focusing capacity on task-critical weights. This dual mechanism—context-aware prompting in the forward pass and Fisher-guided sparse updates in the backward pass—enables near full-tuning performance with ~0.3–4% of trainable parameters.

## Results

On regional precipitation forecasting (ERA5-CH, China), WeatherPEFT with only 3.38M trainable parameters (~0.3% of the 1.25B Aurora backbone) achieves 12h SEEPS of 0.368, ACC of 0.742, and RMSE of 0.198, dramatically outperforming all PEFT baselines (e.g., LoRA: SEEPS 0.495, ACC 0.592). With a ~4% parameter budget (52.37M), WeatherPEFT surpasses Full-Tuning (1246.77M) on all metrics: 12h SEEPS 0.302 vs. 0.304, ACC 0.805 vs. 0.797, RMSE 0.174 vs. 0.178. On downscaling (ERA5 5.625°→1.40625°), WeatherPEFT at 52.47M params achieves T2m RMSE 0.916 vs. Full-Tuning's 0.906, and on ensemble post-processing (ENS-10), it beats Full-Tuning on Z500 CRPS (72.745 vs. 73.760) with 52.18M params.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeatherPEFT decisively outperforms all standard PEFT baselines (LoRA, DoRA, AdaptFormer, SSF, VPT, APrompt) across all three downstream tasks. On precipitation forecasting, LoRA's 12h SEEPS is 62.8% higher than Full-Tuning (0.495 vs. 0.304), while WeatherPEFT at the same ~3.4M parameter budget achieves 0.368 — a 25.7% improvement over LoRA. Task-selective methods (SCT, SAM, Child-Tuning) consistently outperform generic PEFT like LoRA, validating the importance of parameter selection, yet WeatherPEFT still surpasses them by 10–20% on SEEPS. At the ~4% parameter budget (52M), WeatherPEFT matches or exceeds Full-Tuning on all three tasks: downscaling T2m RMSE 0.916 vs. 0.906, post-processing Z500 CRPS 72.745 vs. 73.760 (beating Full-Tuning), and precipitation 12h SEEPS 0.302 vs. 0.304 (beating Full-Tuning). Training-from-scratch models (U-Net, ResNet, ViT, FourCastNet, ClimaX, Aurora) are uniformly outperformed by fine-tuned WeatherPEFT, confirming the value of pre-trained knowledge transfer. On the Prithvi-WxC backbone (2B params), WeatherPEFT matches Full-Tuning with 4% parameters while LoRA degrades severely (12h SEEPS 0.647 vs. 0.398), underscoring that generic PEFT is unreliable across different WFMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PEFT paradigm from NLP and computer vision (LoRA, DoRA, AdaptFormer, VPT, APrompt, SSF) to Weather Foundation Models, arguing that the inherent heterogeneity of meteorological data—diverse physical variables, resolution regimes, and spatiotemporal coverage—renders generic homogeneous PEFT suboptimal. It draws on Fisher information from EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) for parameter selection but departs from static task-specific selection methods (SCT, SAM, Child-Tuning) by introducing an annealed stochastic mechanism that dynamically recalibrates during training. The TADP module repurposes the encoder's embedding weights as a source of task-specific soft prompts, a design not previously explored in the PEFT literature for scientific foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of PEFT for Weather Foundation Models, demonstrating that generic PEFT methods (e.g., LoRA) exhibit a 60%+ performance gap versus Full-Tuning on heterogeneous weather tasks—a gap that does not close even with 16× more LoRA parameters. The practical implication is significant: as WFMs scale to billions of parameters, task-specific Full-Tuning becomes infeasible, and WeatherPEFT's ~0.3% parameter budget with Full-Tuning parity (or better) offers a viable path for multi-task deployment. Open questions include whether the Fisher-guided selection generalizes to tasks with fundamentally different physics (e.g., climate-scale vs. convective-scale), whether the method scales to the next generation of even larger WFMs, and whether the encoder-embedding-based prompting remains effective when the encoder architecture changes substantially.
