## Problem

Training state-of-the-art VQ-based visual tokenizers (e.g., VAR, VQGAN) demands 16–256 A100 GPUs for 40–200 hours, making it practically impossible for resource-constrained researchers to develop or test novel quantization algorithms. Because the VQ module is tightly coupled to the encoder-decoder, simply swapping in a new quantizer into a frozen pre-trained tokenizer causes a decoder-quantization distributional mismatch that degrades reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262769/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a pre-trained discrete visual tokenizer (VAR with a U-Net encoder-decoder, 16× spatial downsampling) and 256×256 RGB images. It produces reconstructed 256×256 images and discrete visual tokens (680 tokens for multi-scale VAR, 512 tokens for fixed-scale VQ) with codebook sizes up to 65,536. The encoder and (in the default Stage II) the VQ module remain frozen; only the new VQ codebook (Stage I) and decoder weights (Stage II) are updated.

## Method

VQ-Transplant is a two-stage plug-and-play framework. Stage I substitutes the native VQ module with a new one (e.g., MMD-VQ) while freezing the encoder and decoder, training only the new codebook with a quantization-error loss plus a distribution-alignment uniqueness loss. Stage II performs lightweight decoder adaptation—fine-tuning only the decoder for 5 epochs on ImageNet-1k with L2, VGG perceptual, and GAN losses (using a frozen DINO-S discriminator with DiffAug, consistency, and LeCAM regularization)—to realign the decoder's learned feature priors with the new quantization space. The secondary contribution, MMD-VQ, replaces Wasserstein VQ's Gaussian-dependent moment matching with Maximum Mean Discrepancy using a multi-Gaussian RBF kernel, enabling nonparametric distribution alignment that remains robust under non-Gaussian, multi-modal, or heavy-tailed feature distributions. This decoupling reduces training cost by ~95% (22 hours on 2×A100 vs. 60 hours on 16×A100 for vanilla VAR) while achieving superior r-FID (0.81 vs. 0.92).

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, MMD VAR (multi-scale, 680 tokens, K=8192) achieves 0.81 r-FID and 201.0 r-IS, outperforming the original VAR tokenizer (0.92 r-FID, 198.6 r-IS) while requiring only 22 GPU-hours on 2×A100 versus VAR's 60 hours on 16×A100 (21.8× speedup). MMD VQ (fixed-scale, 512 tokens, K=65536) reaches 0.86 r-FID and 197.1 r-IS. Cross-dataset generalization is strong: on FFHQ, Wasserstein VQ achieves a record 1.21 r-FID; on CelebA-HQ, MMD VQ reaches 2.60 r-FID; and on LSUN-Churches, Wasserstein VQ achieves 1.79 r-FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMD VAR (0.81 r-FID) and MMD VQ (0.86 r-FID) both surpass the original VAR tokenizer (0.92 r-FID) on ImageNet-1k, with MMD VAR also achieving the best r-IS (201.0 vs. 198.6). Against prior discrete tokenizers, the gains are substantial: MMD VQ improves over VQGAN-LC (2.62 r-FID) by 1.76, over Llama GEN (2.19) by 1.33, and over RQVAE (1.83) by 0.97. On FFHQ, Wasserstein VQ (1.21 r-FID) dramatically outperforms VQGAN-LC (3.81), VQ-WAE (4.20), MQVAE (4.55), and RQVAE (7.04). On CelebA-HQ, MMD VQ (2.60 r-FID) is the best reported result, and on LSUN-Churches, Wasserstein VQ (1.79 r-FID) leads. Critically, all these results are achieved with 21.8× less training time than vanilla VAR (22 h on 2×A100 vs. 60 h on 16×A100), and from-scratch training of MMD VAR for 35 hours still yields only 1.26–1.34 r-FID, far below VQ-Transplant's 0.81–0.91.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper decouples the VQ module from the encoder-decoder in discrete visual tokenizers, departing from the monolithic joint-training paradigm used by VQGAN, VAR, Llama GEN, and ImageFolder. It builds directly on the distributional-alignment VQ framework of Fang et al. (2025) (Wasserstein VQ), replacing the Gaussian-dependent Wasserstein distance with nonparametric Maximum Mean Discrepancy to handle non-Gaussian feature distributions. The transplant mechanism itself—freezing a pre-trained encoder-decoder and swapping only the quantizer, followed by a brief decoder adaptation—is a new procedural contribution not present in prior VQ literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing VQ-module experimentation cost by ~95% (22 GPU-hours vs. 480+ for VAR), VQ-Transplant lowers the barrier for exploring novel quantization algorithms in resource-constrained settings, potentially accelerating the pace of VQ research. A key limitation is that the framework works best with discrete tokenizers (VAR) whose decoders are already conditioned on quantized features; adaptation to continuous tokenizers like LDM-16 yields substantially weaker results, suggesting the approach is most effective when the base model's decoder has prior exposure to discrete representations. Open questions include whether the decoder adaptation generalizes to larger codebooks, higher resolutions, or video tokenization, and whether the MMD-VQ advantage over Wasserstein VQ (pronounced only under non-Gaussian features) will matter as encoder architectures evolve.
