## Problem

Existing GNN and LLM methods for text-attributed graphs operate on static structures and neglect two complementary temporal semantic patterns in Dynamic Text-Attributed Graphs (DyTAGs): recent semantic dependencies among interaction texts and global semantic evolution of nodes over time. Moreover, applying LLMs to DyTAGs is computationally prohibitive because edge-level text attributes multiply across orders of magnitude more edges than nodes and across hundreds to thousands of timestamps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260445/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a DyTAG where nodes carry static text attributes (e.g., user names, entity descriptions) and edges carry timestamped interaction text (e.g., reviews, emails, political actions). LLM reasoning is performed as a one-time offline preprocessing step that produces per-interaction recent semantic embeddings and per-node-segment global textual descriptions, which are then stored for fast retrieval. Online prediction produces node embeddings for destination node retrieval (Hit@10 ranking) and binary link prediction scores (AP, ROC-AUC), running at speeds competitive with lightweight temporal GNNs (e.g., 729 edges/s on Enron vs. 15 for a pure LLM baseline).

## Method

DyGRASP combines three components: (1) node-centric implicit reasoning with a sliding window mechanism that exploits the unidirectional consistency between LLM causal order and chronological interaction order to extract recent temporal semantic features in a single forward pass per batch, reducing token complexity from O(|E|×d) to O(|E|); (2) explicit reasoning via an RNN-like chain where the LLM iteratively generates cumulative node descriptions D_i = LLM(D_{i-1}, S_i) over s timestamp-partitioned segments to capture long-range global semantics; (3) an integration module with separate Transformer-based Recent Semantic and Global Semantic layers plus a pluggable temporal GNN (DyGFormer or TGAT) for graph structure, all fused through an MLP merge layer. The model is trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy loss using negative sampling, and the LLM components (Llama-3.1-8B for implicit, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct for explicit) are frozen, making the approach compatible with any base temporal GNN and LLM family.

## Results

On the destination node retrieval task (Hit@10), DyGRASP(DyGFormer) achieves up to 34.56% absolute improvement over DyGFormer on Googlemap (85.88% vs. 51.32%, transductive) and up to 43.51% on Stack_elec (99.74% vs. 56.23%, inductive). For future link prediction, DyGRASP(DyGFormer) reaches 98.87% AUC on Stack_elec (transductive) and 95.03% AP on Enron (inductive), consistently outperforming all temporal GNN baselines. The method also generalizes across LLM backbones (Llama-3.1, Qwen2.5, Mistral) and base temporal GNNs (DyGFormer, TGAT), with the largest gains observed on text-rich datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyGRASP(DyGFormer) outperforms all seven temporal GNN baselines (JODIE, DyRep, CAWN, TCL, GraphMixer, TGAT, DyGFormer) and the standalone Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct baseline on every dataset under both transductive and inductive settings. The most dramatic gains appear on text-rich datasets: on Googlemap (transductive), DyGRASP(DyGFormer) improves Hit@10 by 34.56 points over DyGFormer (85.88% vs. 51.32%), and on Stack_elec (inductive) by 43.51 points (99.74% vs. 56.23%). JODIE and DyRep fail entirely (OOM) on Googlemap and Stack_elec due to their memory requirements. The standalone LLM (Llama-3.1) performs poorly on most datasets (e.g., 41.01% on GDELT transductive), confirming that raw LLM reasoning without graph structure is insufficient. In the appendix comparison against LKD4DyTAG, DyGRASP wins on average AP and AUC across three datasets, though LKD4DyTAG edges ahead slightly on GDELT where graph structure dominates over text semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyGRASP sits at the intersection of two research threads: temporal GNNs (TGAT, DyGFormer, JODIE, etc.) that model dynamic graph structure but lack textual understanding, and LLM-augmented methods for static text-attributed graphs (ENGINE, TAPE, SimTeG, GraphGPT) that cannot handle temporal evolution. The paper departs from both by introducing a dual LLM reasoning paradigm—implicit (hidden-embedding extraction for recent semantics) and explicit (generative summarization for global semantics)—coupled with efficiency mechanisms (node-centric sliding window, RNN-like reasoning chain) that prior LLM-for-DyTAG work (LKD4DyTAG, CROSS, GAD) does not address. Its modular design explicitly decouples the LLM reasoning from the temporal GNN backbone, making it compatible with any existing temporal GNN rather than proposing a new one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making LLM-based semantic reasoning on DyTAGs computationally feasible: the theoretical reduction from O(|E|×d) to O(|E|) token consumption, combined with offline pre-computation of semantic features, means the online prediction speed matches lightweight temporal GNNs. The 34% Hit@10 improvement (particularly on text-rich datasets like Googlemap and Stack_elec) demonstrates that temporal semantic patterns are not captured by structure-only models. Key limitations include evaluation on only the DTGB benchmark (the sole publicly available DyTAG benchmark) and four datasets, and the method has not been tested on heterogeneous DyTAGs or hyper-relational graphs. Open questions include whether the recent-global decomposition generalizes to domains with very sparse text or extremely long interaction histories, and how the approach scales to graphs with millions of nodes.
