## Problem

While shortcut learning and spurious correlation exploitation in Transformers are well-documented, it remains unclear under what conditions standard gradient descent training can suppress spurious features and converge to a cause-only predictor. Prior theory on attention dynamics and max-margin convergence does not characterize when spurious information is actively filtered out at both train and test time, nor does it explain the mechanism by which GD's implicit regularization yields causal generalization without explicit invariance penalties or multi-environment training.

## Input / Output

The theoretical model consumes a pair of vector covariates (causal x ∈ R^d and spurious descendant z ∈ R^d) augmented with fixed positional encodings s1, s2 ∈ R^M to form two tokens, and produces a scalar prediction via a quadratic-parameterized head. In empirical validation, inputs are 224×224 RGB images (CUB-200-2011 birds composited with Places backgrounds, CelebA face images) or Amazon review text, and outputs are binary class labels (water/land bird, beard present/absent) or 5-point sentiment ratings. The positional encodings are critical: they inject a sample-independent margin into the attention logit difference, preventing the gate from collapsing to 1/2 and ensuring branch identifiability.

## Method

The paper identifies Correlation Crowding-Out (CCO), a two-phase training phenomenon in which GD on a Transformer first amplifies weights aligned with the dominant causal coordinate (occupation) and then drives the attention gate toward the max-margin separator between causal and spurious features (crowding-out), concentrating attention almost entirely on the causal branch. The theoretical analysis uses a simplified two-key dot-product attention model with a quadratic-parameterization feed-forward head, trained on data from the minimal causal chain x→y→z, where y is a sparse quadratic signal in x and z is an L-Lipschitz descendant of y. A Dominant-Coordinate Condition (population-level signal gap plus per-sample margin and sign-stability) is shown to be sufficient for CCO, with convergence guarantees for both the optimization trajectory (weights recover w* up to O(σ√log d/√n) on active coordinates and O(1/d) on inactive ones) and generalization (test risk approaches the cause-only noise floor σ²/2 at rate O(rσ²log d/n)). The key distinction from prior work is that CCO arises purely from GD's implicit regularization under a single environment with no invariance regularizers, no environment labels, and no multi-environment training—strong causal correlation in the data, coupled with the softmax competition geometry of attention, is sufficient to crowd out spurious features.

## Results

On the CelebA beard-prediction task (where gender is a spurious correlate), DeiT-Small achieves 0.893 accuracy on the facial-only test set (Test Set 2), outperforming ResNet-34 (0.861) and EfficientNet-B4 (0.802), while all three models perform near chance (~0.55–0.58) when facial regions are masked (Test Set 1). On Waterbirds with strong spurious bias (p_train=0.9), DeiT-Small maintains significantly higher test accuracy than the CNN baselines, and in OOD sweeps, test accuracy remains above 95% when p_train ≤ 0.85, confirming the CCO threshold. In the controlled two-key attention simulation, the causal-branch attention weight p_x reaches 1 within the first 100 iterations (occupation phase) and stays at 1 through the crowding-out phase over 5000 total iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DeiT-Small (a Vision Transformer) against two CNN baselines of comparable parameter count (~20M): ResNet-34 and EfficientNet-B4, all pretrained on ImageNet and fine-tuned under identical settings. On CelebA beard prediction with gender as a spurious correlate, DeiT-Small achieves 0.893 accuracy on the facial-only test set, beating ResNet-34 (0.861) by 3.2 points and EfficientNet-B4 (0.802) by 9.1 points, indicating the Transformer's attention mechanism more effectively crowds out the spurious gender signal. On Waterbirds with training bias p_train=0.9, DeiT-Small maintains significantly higher test accuracy than both CNNs, suggesting Transformers better capture the causal bird-type signal despite overwhelming spurious background correlations. In OOD evaluation, DeiT-Small's test accuracy remains above 95% when p_train ≤ 0.85, whereas at p_train ≥ 0.9 the model degrades—establishing a practical CCO boundary. The paper does not compare against explicit invariance-learning methods (e.g., IRM, ICP) or DRO baselines; the comparison is purely architectural (Transformer vs. CNN) under standard ERM training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions CCO as the mirror image of the well-studied shortcut learning phenomenon: prior work (Shah et al. 2020, Yang et al. 2024) showed that when spurious features dominate, GD's simplicity bias drives models toward shortcuts, while this paper characterizes the opposite regime where dominant causal features cause GD to actively suppress spurious correlates. Theoretically, it builds on Soudry et al.'s (2018) max-margin convergence analysis for logistic regression and Tarzanagh et al.'s (2023) max-margin token selection in attention, but applies them to a novel two-key attention model with a causal chain structure and a quadratic-parameterized head. Crucially, it departs from the invariance learning literature (Peters et al. 2016, Arjovsky et al. 2019) by showing that single-environment standard GD—without explicit invariance penalties, multi-environment training, or distributionally robust objectives—can yield cause-only predictors when a dominant causal correlation exists.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CCO provides a concrete, mechanistic answer to when standard Transformer training self-selects causal features, filling a gap between the shortcut learning literature and invariance learning methods. The two-phase decomposition (occupation in the FFN, crowding-out in attention) offers actionable training insights: amplifying causal alignment in data design and using attention sparsity or large-step schedules can increase the likelihood of cause-only solutions. However, the conditions are restrictive (a uniform dominance gap, sign stability, per-sample margin), and the theory is developed for a simplified two-key, single-head model with quadratic parameterization; whether CCO persists in multi-head, multi-layer Transformers with complex causal graphs remains open. The empirical results, while spanning vision and NLP, use relatively controlled spurious-correlation setups, leaving open how CCO interacts with the many confounders present in real-world data.
