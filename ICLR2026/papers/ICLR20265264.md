## Problem

Training few-step flow map models (Consistency Models, Mean Flow) remains unstable, hyperparameter-sensitive, and computationally expensive because the true flow map Ψ_{t→s} has no closed form, forcing reliance on stop-gradient pseudo-targets that drift during training. Initializing from pre-trained diffusion weights mitigates but does not resolve the fundamental mismatch: diffusion models capture only infinitesimal movements while flow maps must learn large integrated trajectory jumps. No principled intermediate stage existed between diffusion pre-training and flow map post-training to bridge this gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise x_T ~ p_prior (optionally conditioned on a class label or text prompt) and produces a synthetic image in 1–2 NFEs. It operates across pixel-space (32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 AFHQv2/FFHQ/ImageNet) and latent-space (256×256 and 512×512 ImageNet in Stable Diffusion latent space, MS-COCO T2I) settings. During mid-training, the input is a full teacher-generated ODE trajectory {x̂_{t_i}}_{i=0}^M from a single prior sample, and the output is the clean endpoint x̂_{t_0} (CM) or pairwise finite differences (x̂_{t_i} − x̂_{t_j})/(t_i − t_j) (MF).

## Method

CMT inserts a lightweight mid-training stage between diffusion pre-training and flow map post-training. Given a prior sample x_T, a frozen teacher ODE solver (e.g., DPM-Solver++ with 16 NFEs) generates a discrete reference trajectory {x̂_{t_i}}; the student is then trained via a standard regression loss to map every intermediate state x̂_{t_i} directly to the clean endpoint x̂_{t_0} (CM case, Eq. 7) or to match the average drift between trajectory pairs (MF case, Eq. 8). Because the targets are fixed and explicit—no stop-gradients, no time-weighting schedules, no Δt annealing—mid-training converges quickly and stably, yielding a trajectory-aligned initializer. This initializer provably reduces the gradient bias between the oracle and surrogate flow map losses (Theorem 5.1), making subsequent post-training (ECT, ECD, or MF) faster, more stable, and requiring up to 98% less training data and GPU time than baselines without mid-training.

## Results

CMT achieves state-of-the-art two-step FIDs of 1.97 (CIFAR-10), 1.32 (ImageNet 64×64), and 1.84 (ImageNet 512×512), while reducing training data and GPU time by up to 98% compared to baselines without mid-training. On ImageNet 512×512, CMT (w/ ECD) reaches FID 1.84 in only 400 H100 GPU hours versus 4644 hours for vanilla ECD to reach FID 3.38 (a 91.4% cost reduction). On ImageNet 256×256, CMT attains 1-step FID 3.34 with ~50% less total training time than MF from scratch (FID 3.43), and on MS-COCO T2I it achieves the best 2-step FID (5.01) with ~47% less training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, CMT (w/ ECT) achieves the best 2-step FID of 1.97, outperforming ECT (2.11), sCT (2.06), VCT (2.02), TCM (2.05), and IMM (1.98), while using only 51.2 Mimgs versus 102.4–332.8 Mimgs for those baselines. On ImageNet 64×64, CMT (w/ ECD) reaches 2-step FID 1.32, beating ECD (1.50), sCT (1.48), and TCM (1.62), with 98% less training data than sCT. On ImageNet 512×512, CMT (w/ ECD) achieves 2-step FID 1.84, surpassing ECD (3.38), sCD (1.88), and AYF (1.87) while requiring only 28.8 Mimgs versus 409.6 Mimgs for ECD/sCD (93% reduction) and 102.4 Mimgs for AYF (71% reduction). On ImageNet 256×256, CMT-XL/2 attains 1-step FID 3.34 in 760 total GPU hours, beating MF from scratch (FID 3.43, 1520 hours) and MF with SiT initialization (FID 4.52, >1520 hours). On MS-COCO T2I, CMT achieves the best 2-step FID of 5.01 in 32 hours, outperforming MF from scratch (5.42, 60 hours) and MF with SiT init (5.26, 60 hours). Across all benchmarks, CMT consistently reduces total training cost (data + GPU time) by 47–98% while matching or exceeding the quality of methods without mid-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces mid-training for flow map models, conceptually borrowing the pre-training → mid-training → post-training pipeline from LLM scaling (Groeneveld et al., 2024) and instantiating it as Consistency Mid-Training (CMT). It builds directly on the Consistency Model (Song et al., 2023) and Mean Flow (Geng et al., 2025a) frameworks, using ECT/ECD (Geng et al., 2025b) and MF as post-training objectives, with EDM/EDM2 (Karras et al., 2022, 2024b) or SiT (Ma et al., 2024) as teacher samplers. The key departure from prior work is the identification of a fundamental mismatch: diffusion models learn infinitesimal velocity fields while flow maps must learn large integrated jumps, making diffusion-weight initialization inherently fragile. CMT resolves this by training a trajectory-aligned proxy via simple fixed-target regression (no stop-gradients, no time weighting, no annealing), which the authors show theoretically reduces gradient bias compared to both diffusion and random initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CMT offers a practical, architecture-agnostic recipe that cuts flow map training cost by up to 98% in data and GPU time while simultaneously achieving SOTA FIDs across pixel-space and latent-space benchmarks, including ImageNet 512×512. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 5.1) provides a principled explanation for why trajectory-aligned initialization outperforms diffusion initialization, giving the field a clearer understanding of the initialization problem in flow map learning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video generation or other ODE-based generative paradigms, whether the teacher quality requirement can be further relaxed (the MF-B/4 teacher with FID 24.47 already works, but how weak can it be?), and whether mid-training can replace the need for large-scale diffusion pre-training entirely. The simplicity of the method—essentially a supervised regression on solver trajectories—suggests it could become a standard intermediate step in the flow map training pipeline.
