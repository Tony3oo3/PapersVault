## Problem

Multi-agent communication in partially observable environments faces a fundamental trilemma: methods that maximize task performance produce opaque, high-bandwidth protocols; methods that prioritize efficiency yield uninterpretable discrete symbols; and methods that ground communication in human language incur prohibitive communication costs. No existing framework simultaneously optimizes all three axes of task utility, communication efficiency, and human interpretability within a single principled objective.

## Input / Output

Each MARL agent receives a local partial observation (encoded to a 128-dimensional feature vector) and the set of discrete communication symbols broadcast by all other agents at the previous timestep. The agent outputs a continuous action (movement, inspection, or tool use) and a 32-bit discrete communication symbol produced by a quantized autoencoder. During training, an offline dataset of (observation, action, natural-language message) triples generated by GPT-4 agents in a parallel textual environment provides supervised grounding signals, but at inference time no LLM queries are made—the agents communicate solely through the learned discrete symbols.

## Method

GLC integrates four modules under a unified multi-objective loss grounded in the Information Bottleneck principle: (1) a discrete autoencoder (3-layer MLP encoder/decoder with straight-through quantization) compresses each agent's 128-dim observation feature into a 32-bit symbol, minimizing communication complexity; (2) an LLM agent module generates expert trajectories in a textual environment to produce a static grounding dataset; (3) a language grounding loss aligns each agent's message embedding with the corresponding LLM-generated reference embedding via cosine similarity, but only for state-action pairs present in the dataset; (4) a supervised contrastive loss (temperature ρ=0.1, temporal window w=5) pulls together messages from different agents observing the same state while pushing apart messages from unrelated trajectories, ensuring mutual intelligibility. The four loss terms (policy, alignment, reconstruction, contrastive) are weighted by coefficients λ_A, λ_R, λ_C that are dynamically scheduled: λ_A is task-adaptive (higher for complex tasks like USAR), λ_R follows a linear annealing schedule implementing an 'explore then compress' strategy, and λ_C is held fixed for consensus stability. A key design choice is the decoupling of training from deployment: LLM supervision and contrastive learning are used only during training, while inference operates entirely on the learned discrete protocol without external supervision.

## Results

On the pp_v1 Predator-Prey benchmark, GLC completes tasks in 4.5 average steps using only 32 bits per step (144 total bits per episode), compared to 5.3–7.2 steps and 129.6–45,056 total bits for baselines. In terms of interpretability, GLC achieves a cosine similarity of 0.87±0.02 and BLEU score of 0.65±0.04 against LLM reference messages in pp_v0, surpassing LangGround (0.82, 0.52). GLC also attains the highest topographic similarity (0.73±0.11) among all methods, indicating the most human-language-like structural organization of its emergent communication space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLC decisively outperforms the continuous-communication baselines LangGround and IC3Net on communication efficiency, reducing total bits per episode by roughly 300× (144 vs. 43,418 and 45,056 bits in pp_v1) while simultaneously achieving shorter episode lengths (4.5 vs. 5.3 and 5.5 steps). Against the discrete baselines aeComm and VQ-VIB, GLC wins on task performance (4.5 vs. 5.4 and 7.2 average steps in pp_v1) and on interpretability metrics (cosine similarity 0.87 vs. near-random for aeComm; BLEU 0.65 vs. 0.52 for LangGround in pp_v0). aeComm is marginally more efficient per step (24 vs. 32 bits), but GLC's superior coordination shortens episodes enough to make total communication cost comparable (144 vs. 129.6 bits). In the more challenging pp_v0 (zero-visibility) and USAR (multi-step bomb defusal) settings, GLC's advantages widen: it achieves 72–79% success rates at 15×15 and 20×20 scales versus 40–68% for the next-best discrete method, while maintaining total communication under 1,300 bits per agent per episode compared to over 288,000 bits for continuous methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLC integrates three previously separate lines of multi-agent communication research: discrete autoencoder compression (aeComm, VQ-VIB), LLM-based language grounding for interpretability (LangGround), and contrastive learning for inter-agent consistency (Lo et al., 2023). Prior methods typically optimize one or two of the efficiency–utility–interpretability trilemma, producing either opaque high-bandwidth protocols or semantically ungrounded discrete symbols. GLC's key departure is unifying all three objectives under a single Information Bottleneck formulation with dynamic weight scheduling, and critically decoupling LLM supervision (offline, training-only) from deployment (no external model needed at inference).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path toward deployable multi-agent communication in bandwidth-constrained settings (robotic swarms, autonomous vehicle fleets) where both low latency and human trust are required. However, evaluation is limited to small-scale grid worlds (5×5 to 20×20) and a single cooperative bomb-defusal task; generalization to truly large-scale or adversarial environments (e.g., SMAC) remains untested. Open questions include whether the dynamic weighting strategy generalizes beyond manually tuned per-task presets, how the protocol degrades under partial observability with many agents, and whether the LLM-grounded semantics transfer across domains without re-collecting expert trajectories.
