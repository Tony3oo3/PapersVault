## Problem

State-of-the-art VLMs including GPT-5 still struggle with basic 3D understanding from 2D inputs, underperforming expert pure vision models by a large margin on metric depth estimation. Pure vision models achieve super-human accuracy but require task-specific architectures and complex regression/regularization losses. The paper asks whether VLMs can reach expert-level 3D accuracy without any architecture or loss modification, and identifies the specific bottlenecks preventing this.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264561/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, resizes it to a unified focal length of 1000 pixels (resolving cross-camera scale ambiguity), renders small visual marker arrows pointing to query pixels directly on the image, and appends a text prompt such as 'How many meters is this point from the camera?'. The output is a natural-language text response with the depth rounded to two decimal places (e.g., 'The point is around 3.42 meters away from the camera'). By querying each pixel independently, the model can generate full point clouds without a dense prediction head. The same framework extends to speed, time, two-point distance, and metric-scale camera pose estimation between image pairs.

## Method

DepthLM fine-tunes a standard VLM (Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B or Pixtral 12B) with text-based supervised fine-tuning using only cross-entropy loss on text tokens—no dense prediction head, no regression or regularization loss, and no architecture change. Three key design choices drive the results: (1) visual prompting, where small arrow markers are rendered on the image to reference query pixels instead of embedding (X,Y) coordinates in text, which the paper shows VLMs understand far more accurately; (2) intrinsic-conditioned augmentation, where each training image is resized so its focal length equals a fixed value (f_uni=1000 px), unifying the metric scale across cameras and enabling zero-shot cross-dataset generalization; (3) sparse labeling, where as few as 1 labeled pixel per training image suffices, and increasing image diversity is more effective than increasing label density. Training uses 23M–60M samples from DepthLMBench (a curated mix of 7 public indoor/outdoor datasets) on 128 H100 GPUs for 2–4 days. The paper also ablates GRPO-style reinforcement learning and finds it achieves comparable accuracy to SFT but at 8–16× higher per-sample compute cost.

## Results

DepthLM (7B) achieves an average δ₁ of 0.838 across 8 indoor and outdoor datasets, surpassing GPT-5 (0.370) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (0.342) by over 2×. The 3B model reaches 0.824 average δ₁, already outperforming the 72B Qwen2.5-VL (0.219) by nearly 4×. Against pure vision models, DepthLM (7B) outperforms Depth Pro (−29.1%) and Metric3Dv2 (−3.8%), with only UniDepthV2 slightly ahead at +9.2%. On the multi-task benchmark (Table 3), DepthLM achieves 0.804 average δ₁ across six 3D tasks, outperforming GPT-5 (0.210) by over 3.8×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against generalist VLMs, DepthLM (7B) at 0.838 average δ₁ outperforms GPT-5 (0.370), Gemini-2.5-Pro (0.342), and Qwen2.5-VL 72B (0.219) by 2.3×, 2.4×, and 3.8× respectively. The only VLM previously trained on metric depth, Seed1.5-VL, reaches 0.400 with the paper's marker-based prompting but still lags DepthLM by 2.1×, particularly on outdoor data (0.040 vs 0.833 on Argoverse2). Against pure vision models, DepthLM (7B) surpasses Depth Pro (−29.1% relative gap) and Metric3Dv2 (−3.8%), with UniDepthV2 remaining the sole model that edges ahead by +9.2%. On the multi-task evaluation (Table 3), DepthLM achieves 0.804 average δ₁ across six 3D tasks (distance, principal-axis distance, speed, time, two-point distance, camera pose), outperforming GPT-5 (0.210) by 3.8× and SpatialRGPT (0.161) by 5×. The 3B model already matches or exceeds the 72B Qwen2.5-VL on every individual dataset, demonstrating that model scale is not the bottleneck—pixel reference and camera ambiguity handling are.

## Relation to Prior Work

DepthLM departs from prior VLM-based 3D understanding work (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, SpatialBot, Seed1.5-VL) that relied on knowledge distillation from pure vision models, extra architecture modules, or text-based pixel coordinates. It adapts the intrinsic-conditioned focal-length unification from Metric3D (Yin et al., 2023) to the VLM setting, but the key conceptual departure is showing that no dense prediction head, no regression/regularization loss, and no architecture modification is needed—only visual prompting (rendered markers) and standard text-based SFT. The paper positions itself as a bridge between the two paradigms: it uses the simplicity and flexibility of VLMs while matching the accuracy of task-specific pure vision models like DepthPro and Metric3Dv2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a VLM can match expert pure vision model accuracy on pixel-level metric depth without any task-specific architecture or loss, opening the door to a single unified model covering diverse 3D tasks (depth, pose, speed, time, multi-point distance) through text interactions alone. The finding that image diversity (1 labeled pixel per image) matters more than label density has practical implications for data-efficient 3D training. Limitations include still trailing UniDepthV2 by ~9% on some datasets, reliance on undistorted pinhole camera assumption, and the need for per-pixel querying to generate point clouds (no dense head). Open questions include whether more fine-grained strategies or additional data can push VLMs beyond pure vision models, and whether the framework extends to tasks requiring true dense geometric reasoning.
