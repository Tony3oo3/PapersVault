## Problem

Neural minimax semi-dual optimal transport solvers have become a popular paradigm for generative modeling, yet they lack any statistical learning theory analysis providing practical convergence guarantees for the recovered transport maps. Existing theoretical work either addresses non-minimax OT solvers (where only one variable is optimized) or provides only duality-gap bounds that do not reveal statistical rates. This paper fills that gap by establishing the first generalization error bounds for minimax quadratic OT solvers from a statistical learning perspective.

## Input / Output

The solver consumes N i.i.d. samples from a source distribution p and M i.i.d. samples from a target distribution q, both supported on convex compact subsets of R^D. It jointly trains two neural networks: an ICNN (input convex neural network) parametrizing the dual potential φ and an MLP with ReLU activations parametrizing the transport map T, via the empirical minimax objective. The output is a parametric transport map T̂ that can be evaluated on arbitrary new (unseen) points x ~ p, distinguishing the continuous OT setup from discrete OT which only yields fixed point-to-point matchings between the training samples.

## Method

The core contribution is a theoretical decomposition of the generalization error ‖T̂ − T*‖²_{L²(p)} into four components: inner and outer approximation errors (from restricting optimization to finite-capacity neural network classes) and inner and outer estimation errors (from using empirical measures). The estimation errors are bounded via Rademacher complexities of the function classes (Theorem 4.2), while the approximation errors are shown to be arbitrarily small under appropriate choices of ICNN and MLP architectures (Theorems 4.3, 4.6), relying on the β-strong convexity of the optimal Kantorovich potential. The main result (Theorem 4.9) yields a bound of ε + (32/β)(R_{p,N}(H) + R_{q,M}(F)), and Corollary 4.10 specializes this to O(1/√N) + O(1/√M) convergence rates for specific neural network classes. The analysis is notably more complex than non-minimax OT theory because the minimax objective introduces a second optimization variable (the map T) coupled with the potential φ, and the proof leverages strong concavity of the inner objective to convert functional-value gaps into L² map distances.

## Results

The paper's main contribution is theoretical: Theorem 4.9 establishes that the generalization error E‖T* − T̂ᴿ‖²_{L²(p)} ≤ ε + (32/β)(ℛ_{p,N}(ℋ) + ℛ_{q,M}(ℱ)), and Corollary 4.10 specializes this to E‖T* − T̂ᴿ‖²_{L²(p)} ≤ ε + O(1/√N) + O(1/√M) for neural network classes. Experimentally, on the Gaussian Mixture Benchmark (D=2,4), the estimation error exhibits log-log convergence with slope ≲ −0.5 as N,M range from 10² to 2×10⁴, consistent with the O(1/√N) + O(1/√M) prediction. For the approximation error experiment (~10M samples), the error decreases monotonically with network width and approaches zero when the potential architecture matches the ground-truth ICNN (max H_φ = 64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper that does not compare its minimax OT solver against competing methods (e.g., ICNN-based solvers, Sinkhorn, or other adversarial OT approaches). The experiments in §5 are illustrative validations of the derived bounds rather than method comparisons. The estimation error experiment (§5.1) uses the same ICNN architecture as the ground-truth potential to isolate the sample-size effect, confirming the predicted O(1/√N) + O(1/√M) rate. The approximation error experiment (§5.2) varies network width (H_φ from 4 to 64, H_T from 1 to 8) to confirm that approximation error vanishes as expressivity increases. The paper positions itself as filling a gap: prior works (Makkuva et al., 2020; Rout et al., 2022) only provide duality-gap bounds without statistical rates, while non-minimax analyses (Hütter & Rigollet, 2021; Gunsilius, 2022) do not apply to the two-variable minimax objective studied here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the statistical learning analysis of OT map estimation from non-minimax semi-dual formulations (Hütter & Rigollet, 2021; Gunsilius, 2022) to the minimax setting, where both the dual potential and transport map are jointly optimized. It builds directly on the duality gap analysis technique of Makkuva et al. (2020) and the continuous Wasserstein-2 benchmark of Korotin et al. (2021b), but departs from prior minimax OT theory (which only established duality gap bounds) by deriving explicit generalization error rates in terms of Rademacher complexities. The key conceptual departure is treating the minimax structure as a two-level optimization problem and decomposing the error into inner/outer approximation and estimation components, a framework not available in the non-minimax literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide learnability guarantees for minimax adversarial OT solvers, a rapidly growing family of generative models used in domain translation, image super-resolution, and computational biology. The O(1/√N) + O(1/√M) convergence rates reassure practitioners that these solvers are statistically well-founded, though the analysis is restricted to quadratic cost and requires strong convexity of the optimal potential. Open questions include extending the bounds to general cost functions, removing the strong convexity assumption, and providing practical guidance on neural network architecture selection. The lack of lower bounds and the gap between theoretical rates and observed optimization errors in practice remain important limitations.
