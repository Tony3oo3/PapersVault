## Problem

Existing unlearning methods for corrupted data are each designed for a narrow slice of the task space and fail catastrophically outside it. The paper identifies a 2D taxonomy of unlearning tasks defined by discovery rate (fraction of corrupted data known at unlearning time) and statistical regularity (from random mislabels to structured poison triggers), and shows that no prior SOTA method performs well across the full space, making them risky to deploy when the task specification is unknown.

## Input / Output

REM takes a standard trained classifier (ResNet-9 or ViT on RGB images), the full training dataset D_tr, and a forget set D_f containing the discovered subset of corrupted samples. It temporarily expands the network with randomly-initialized parameters θ_o2 (additional channels in conv layers or before/after FF blocks in transformers), then discards them, outputting a model of the same architecture as the input. The method requires access to the full training set at unlearning time and operates under partial discovery, where D_f is a strict subset of all corrupted data D_c.

## Method

REM performs four steps: (1) expand the network with randomly-initialized parameters θ_o2; (2) remove corruptions from θ_o1 using NPO (Negative Preference Optimization) on the forget set only, avoiding reintroduction from the contaminated retain set; (3) repair utility by fine-tuning on the full training set with a DPO-style combined loss that simultaneously redirects corruptions into θ_o2 via a masking strategy where all forget-set samples share one mask (creating a dedicated 'channel' for corrupted data) while other samples use random masks; (4) discard θ_o2, leaving a clean θ_o1. The key insight is that instead of avoiding the retain set (which would sacrifice utility) or using it naively (which reintroduces corruptions), REM accepts reintroduction but funnels it into a throwaway parameter partition. The masking strategy specifically targets high-regularity corruptions that ETD's per-example masks cannot capture, since shared structure means a single shared mask suffices to encode the corruption pattern.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-9 (50% capacity, 1000 corrupted samples, 10 discovery rates × 3 regularities), REM achieves an aggregate Utility×Healed score of 73.40±1.43 (Healed: 81.16%, Utility: 90.54%), outperforming the next-best method NPO+ETD (67.10) by 6.3 points. On SVHN with ViT, REM achieves 73.27±0.32, again leading all baselines. REM is the only method that does not exhibit catastrophic failure in any region of the 2D task space (discovery rate × regularity), whereas all prior methods fail predictably outside their design region.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REM outperforms all five baselines on the aggregate Utility×Healed metric: NPO (67.10), Ascent (65.82), SCRUB (59.85), BadT (58.32), and Potion (51.30) with ETD pretraining on ResNet-9/CIFAR-10. The gap is most pronounced for Potion, which was designed specifically for high-regularity poison unlearning but collapses on low-regularity tasks (Utility drops to ~53–63%), and for SCRUB/BadT, which rely on the retain set and thus reintroduce undiscovered corruptions under partial discovery. Notably, ETD alone (No UL with ETD) is a surprisingly strong baseline at 50.11, outperforming Retrained (48.52) and several unlearning methods, but REM still leads by 23 points. On ViT/SVHN, REM maintains its lead (73.27 vs. 67.70 for Ascent+ETD), while Potion fails entirely due to OOM. The key qualitative finding is that REM is the only method without catastrophic failure in any slice of the 2D discovery-rate × regularity space, whereas each baseline fails predictably outside its design region.

## Relation to Prior Work

REM draws on two distinct lineages: the Example-Tied Dropout (ETD) architecture of Maini et al. (2023), which partitions network parameters into generalization and memorization neurons at training time, and Negative Preference Optimization (NPO) of Zhang et al. (2024a), a DPO-inspired unlearning loss originally developed for NLP. The key conceptual departure is temporal: where ETD enforces its partition during training, REM introduces the partition at post-processing time by expanding the network with randomly-initialized parameters (θ_o2) and using a novel masking strategy that assigns all forget-set samples a shared mask to create a dedicated 'corruption channel.' This allows REM to work with any standard-trained model, unlike ETD which requires a specific training recipe. The paper also positions itself against a broad set of prior unlearning methods (Potion, SCRUB, Bad Teacher, Gradient Ascent, retraining), showing each is confined to a slice of a newly proposed 2D task space defined by discovery rate and statistical regularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2D taxonomy (discovery rate × regularity) provides the field with a diagnostic framework for understanding why unlearning methods fail, which the authors argue is a fundamental blind spot hindering progress. REM's status as the first method to perform strongly across the entire space makes it a practical default when the nature of corruption is unknown, which is the realistic deployment scenario. Key limitations include the binary masking strategy (REM IDEAL with perfect mask assignment shows meaningful headroom), the assumption of full retain-set access at unlearning time, and the fact that the removal step inherits limitations of NPO. Open questions include whether softer or learned masking strategies can close the gap to the ideal, whether the approach transfers to LLMs and other modalities, and whether the taxonomy captures all relevant dimensions of task difficulty.
