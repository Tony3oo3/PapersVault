## Problem

Long-context LLM decoding is bottlenecked by the linearly growing KV cache, and existing sparse-attention methods that share a single set of critical tokens across all heads in a layer ignore the functional diversity of individual attention heads. The paper demonstrates that top-k attention overlap between corresponding heads in adjacent layers varies dramatically (from 0% to 100%), showing that a uniform layer-level sharing strategy is overly coarse and degrades quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264100/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and the accumulated KV cache from prior tokens. During autoregressive decoding, a small subset of retrieval heads performs full attention over the entire cache to select a top-k critical token set (budget configurable, e.g., 30% of sequence length or a fixed 4096 tokens), which is then propagated to the same-index head in the next layer for reuse by sparse heads. The output is standard generated text tokens, with the sparsity pattern and head assignments fixed after a short training phase.

## Method

LycheeDecode partitions attention heads into retrieval heads (performing dense attention and selecting top-k tokens via argsTopK) and sparse heads (computing attention only on the inherited token subset), creating a fine-grained cooperative mechanism where retrieval heads periodically refresh the salient token set while sparse heads exploit it for efficient computation. Head specialization is learned end-to-end using the Hard Kumaraswamy (HardKuma) distribution, a reparameterizable distribution that naturally concentrates probability mass at 0 and 1, eliminating the train-inference discrepancy inherent in continuous-relaxation approaches like DuoAttention. Training uses a distillation loss against a full-attention teacher combined with a Lagrangian relaxation constraint on the expected L0 norm of the selection variables to enforce a target number of retrieval heads. A custom TileLang block-sparse decoding kernel with a workload-pooling strategy aggregates heterogeneous head computations into uniform splits across GPU thread blocks, achieving up to 2.7× end-to-end speedup at 128K context.

## Results

On LongBench (Llama-3-8B, 4096-token budget), LycheeDecode achieves an average score of 33.07, surpassing the full-attention baseline (32.33) and TidalDecode (32.86). On Qwen3-8B (4096 budget), it reaches 33.48, outperforming full attention (33.02) and SeerAttention-R (33.38). For complex reasoning on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, LycheeDecode scores 44.2 average across AIME24, OlympiadBench, Minerva, and Gaokao2023En, beating full attention (43.0) and TidalDecode (30.2). End-to-end decoding achieves up to 2.7× speedup over FlashAttention-2 at 128K context, and the custom TileLang kernel reaches up to 7× kernel-level speedup at 128K with batch size 8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LycheeDecode outperforms TidalDecode (Yang et al., 2025) on both LongBench (33.07 vs. 32.86 on Llama-3-8B; 33.48 vs. 31.76 on Qwen3-8B at 4096 budget) and math reasoning (44.2 vs. 30.2 on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B), while also being 1.73× faster end-to-end at 128K context. Against DuoAttention (Xiao et al., 2025), LycheeDecode achieves a large margin on LongBench (33.07 vs. 25.41 at 4096), attributed to head-level rather than layer-level token sharing. On Qwen3-8B, LycheeDecode slightly edges out SeerAttention-R (33.48 vs. 33.38 at 4096) without requiring a trainable gating network. For head identification, HardKuma outperforms both the direct-optimization baseline from DuoAttention (33.07 vs. 32.06 on Passkey Retrieval) and the HardConcrete distribution from Bhaskar et al. (33.07 vs. 32.13). At the kernel level, the custom TileLang hybrid-head kernel surpasses FlashAttention-2 by up to 7× at 128K context with batch size 8 in the fully sparse (8/8) configuration, though at lower sparsity (4/8 sparse heads) it is comparable to or slightly slower than the dense baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

LycheeDecode sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) selection-based sparse attention (Quest, SeerAttention), (2) attention head functional specialization (RazorAttention, DuoAttention, PruLong), and (3) cross-layer token sharing (TidalDecode, OmniKV). Its key departure is refining the sharing granularity from layer-level (TidalDecode shares one token set across all heads in a layer) to head-level, where a small set of retrieval heads dynamically identify critical tokens that are then propagated to and reused by sparse heads in subsequent layers. For the discrete head-assignment problem, it replaces the continuous relaxation used by DuoAttention (which suffers a train-inference rounding gap) with the HardKumaraswamy distribution, a reparameterizable near-binary distribution that makes the selection differentiable while naturally concentrating mass at 0 and 1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.7× end-to-end speedup at 128K context with comparable-or-better quality is practically significant for deploying long-context LLMs, and the custom TileLang kernel (up to 7× kernel-level speedup) provides a reusable hardware-efficient building block. The counter-intuitive finding that sparse heads can outperform full attention by filtering out irrelevant distractor tokens (the 'denoising' effect) suggests that full attention is not always optimal, which could reshape how the community thinks about attention sparsity. Open questions include whether the fixed per-head token budget can be made dynamic, whether the approach integrates cleanly with production serving stacks like vLLM, and whether the gains hold at larger model scales (all experiments use 7–8B models).
