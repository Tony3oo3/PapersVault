## Problem

Existing RL-based self-evolution methods for LLM agents either depend on external reward signals (rule-based verifiers or specialized reward models) or extract intrinsic rewards from individual models (self-certainty, confidence, semantic entropy). These approaches diverge from how human intelligence evolves—through mutual discussion and collaboration within groups—raising the question of whether LLM-based agents can achieve self-evolution purely from inter-agent interactions without any external supervision.

## Input / Output

The framework takes text questions from math, coding, and science domains along with a rolling discussion history (compressed to the last κ=2 rounds to respect context limits) as input. Each agent produces structured text outputs in three distinct interaction patterns: a solution to the question, a critical evaluation identifying flaws in a given solution, and a scoring judgment (integer 1–3) assessing the solution given an evaluation. The final output is updated agent policies trained via RL, which can then be deployed in single-agent (Vanilla, Consistency) or multi-agent (AutoGen, Debate) inference setups.

## Method

CoMAS structures multi-agent interaction into three patterns—solution proposal, critical evaluation, and scoring—where agents are selected uniformly at random each step, enabling heterogeneous backbones. An LLM-as-a-judge mechanism converts scoring results into complementary rewards that form a zero-sum game: r(solution) = (τ−1)/2 and r(evaluation) = (3−τ)/2, so a correct solution penalizes the evaluator and vice versa, incentivizing both correctness and genuine critical thinking. Policy optimization uses REINFORCE++ with token-level credit assignment, a cumulative KL-divergence penalty against a fixed reference policy, and batch-level advantage normalization. The adversarial reward design is critical: ablations show that removing evaluation causes agents to become progressively stricter judges (monotonically decreasing rewards), while removing scoring triggers reward hacking where agents unanimously endorse all solutions (rewards saturating at 1.0). The framework is fully decentralized—each agent maintains its own replay buffer and is updated independently—making it scalable in both the number of agents and their model diversity.

## Results

CoMAS achieves absolute gains of up to 2.20%, 3.66%, 19.80%, and 6.10% over untrained agents in Vanilla, Consistency, AutoGen, and Debate setups, respectively. Under the Vanilla setup, CoMAS attains the best scores on GSM8K (85.40%), HumanEval (70.73%), SciBench (34.67%), and MMLU (62.40%). Under the Consistency setup, it leads on HumanEval (77.44%), MBPP (59.20%), and MMLU (65.60%). In the AutoGen multi-agent setup, CoMAS delivers the largest gains, improving GSM8K from 52.60% to 72.40% and MMLU from 37.40% to 50.60%, while all baselines show degradation or mixed results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMAS consistently outperforms all three baselines across nearly every benchmark and setup. Against SRLM (self-rewarding via DPO), CoMAS wins on 6 of 7 benchmarks in Vanilla and 5 of 7 in Consistency, while SRLM shows neutral or negative changes on several tasks (e.g., GSM8K drops by 0.60 in Vanilla). Against MAPoRL (multi-agent RL with external verifier rewards), CoMAS is competitive in single-agent setups but dramatically superior in AutoGen, where MAPoRL actually degrades GSM8K (−2.60) and MATH-500 (−1.00) while CoMAS gains +19.80 and +7.40 respectively. Against TTRL (majority-voting pseudo-labels), the contrast is sharpest: TTRL collapses in the AutoGen setup (HumanEval drops from 39.63 to 23.17, a −16.46 loss) and shows negative changes on GPQA in both Vanilla (−1.34) and Consistency (−1.56), whereas CoMAS maintains positive gains across all domains. TTRL does achieve the highest GSM8K (88.20) and MATH-500 (56.80) in Consistency, revealing its specialization for math, but CoMAS provides more balanced improvements across coding, science, and general knowledge tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMAS sits at the intersection of multi-agent LLM systems (AutoGen, Debate, CAMEL) and RL-based self-evolution (SRLM, TTRL, MAPoRL), but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than using external verifiers, self-confidence signals, or majority-vote pseudo-labels as reward sources, it derives intrinsic rewards from the adversarial dynamics of structured inter-agent discussion (solution–evaluation–scoring). The optimization backbone is REINFORCE++ (Hu et al., 2025), chosen over GRPO because CoMAS generates diverse interaction patterns rather than multiple rollouts from a single prompt, and the infrastructure builds on MARTI (Zhang et al., 2025), a multi-agent RL training framework. Conceptually, the paper reframes self-evolution from a single-model self-rewarding problem to a collective, zero-sum game between solvers and evaluators, drawing an explicit analogy to human collaborative learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLM agents can improve without any external supervision signal, which is a meaningful step toward truly autonomous agent training. The ablation showing that both the evaluation and scoring steps are individually necessary (removing either causes reward hacking or training collapse) provides a useful design principle for adversarial multi-agent RL. However, the experiments are limited to 3B and 7B models on math, coding, and science tasks; whether the interaction-reward paradigm scales to larger models, more open-ended domains, or genuinely heterogeneous agent populations remains open. The reliance on LLM-as-a-judge for reward quality also raises questions about the ceiling of this approach when the judging agents themselves are limited.
