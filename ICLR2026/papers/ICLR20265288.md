## Problem

Existing LoRA generation methods treat parameter synthesis as a general weight-space learning problem, overlooking two structural properties of LoRA spaces revealed by empirical analysis: the non-uniqueness of low-rank decomposition (making element-wise supervision of A and B sensitive to arbitrary rescalings and rotations) and the heterogeneous weight distributions across different network modules. This leads to poor generalization, especially on unseen tasks, because generators memorize task-specific decompositions rather than learning task-relevant adaptation structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language task description (20 LLM-generated descriptions per task, embedded via the FLAN-T5-large encoder) and Gaussian noise as input, and produces a complete set of LoRA parameters spanning all module types and layers of the target base model (e.g., encoder self-attention, decoder self-attention, and cross-attention for FLAN-T5-large; q_a, q_b, v_a, v_b for Gemma-2-2B-Instruct). The generated LoRA weights are then loaded into the frozen base model for downstream evaluation, incurring no additional per-token inference overhead beyond standard LoRA.

## Method

LoRAGen is a two-stage structure-aware generator. Stage 1 trains a LoRA Weight Autoencoder (LAE) that encodes LoRA parameters into per-location latents and decodes them via a module-aware Mixture-of-Experts decoder; instead of reconstructing A and B element-wise, it supervises the full adaptation matrix ΔW using a direction loss (cosine alignment of normalized adapters) and a spectral loss (matching leading singular values), making training invariant to the non-uniqueness of low-rank factorizations. The MoE decoder routes on a structural embedding concatenating the latent variable with learnable module and layer embeddings, using separate expert pools per module type so that experts can specialize to the observed module-specific spectral-entropy patterns (e.g., lower-entropy decoder self-attention vs. higher-entropy encoder attention). Stage 2 trains a conditional latent diffusion transformer over the LAE latents, conditioned on the task-description embedding, to sample new latents from noise at inference time; the frozen MoE decoder then maps the denoised latent back to full LoRA parameters. This combination of adapter-level weight-space supervision and structure-aware decoding achieves 96.0% of task-specific LoRA performance in-distribution and 40.2% zero-shot on unseen tasks, surpassing baselines by ~5 points.

## Results

On FLAN-T5-Large (7 FLAN tasks, in-distribution), LoRAGen achieves 96.0% average accuracy, closely matching task-specific LoRAs (96.2%) and substantially outperforming T2L (88.7%) and D2NWG (58.4%). On Gemma-2-2B-Instruct (8 benchmark tasks), LoRAGen reaches 72.7% average accuracy versus 74.5% for task-specific LoRAs, 69.2% for T2L, and 68.9% for D2NWG. In the zero-shot setting on 7 unseen FLAN tasks, LoRAGen attains 40.2% average accuracy, surpassing T2L (35.2%) and D2NWG (35.0%) by approximately 5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRAGen is compared against three baselines: D2NWG (Soro et al., 2024), a latent diffusion model conditioned on dataset identifiers; T2L (Charakorn et al., 2025), a hypernetwork generating LoRAs from natural-language task embeddings in a single forward pass; and Average LoRA (Wortsman et al., 2022), which simply averages task-specific adapter weights. On FLAN-T5-Large, LoRAGen (96.0%) nearly matches task-specific LoRAs (96.2%) while T2L lags by 7.5 points (88.7%) and D2NWG trails by 37.8 points (58.4%), indicating that dataset-identifier conditioning and element-wise reconstruction of A/B matrices are insufficient for capturing task-relevant structure. On Gemma-2-2B-Instruct, LoRAGen (72.7%) outperforms T2L (69.2%) by 3.5 points and D2NWG (68.9%) by 3.8 points, and even surpasses task-specific LoRAs on individual tasks like ArcE (90.7 vs. 90.6) and OQA (80.2 vs. 80.2). In the zero-shot setting, LoRAGen's advantage over T2L and D2NWG widens to +5.0 and +5.2 points respectively, which the authors attribute to adapter-level supervision on the full ΔW matrix avoiding the memorization of specific low-rank factorizations that plagues baselines reconstructing A and B directly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the weight space learning and parameter generation literature (Schürholt et al., 2024; Soro et al., 2024) and the recent wave of LoRA-specific generators (T2L, HyperLoRA, D2NWG), but departs from them by identifying two structural properties of LoRA parameter spaces—non-uniqueness of low-rank decomposition and module-wise heterogeneity of weight distributions—and designing supervision and decoding specifically around them. Where prior methods reconstruct the A and B factors directly or treat LoRA generation as a generic weight-space problem, LoRAGen supervises the full adaptation matrix ΔW via direction and spectral losses, and replaces a single decoder with a module-aware MoE whose routing is conditioned on structural embeddings. The generative backbone (latent VAE + conditional diffusion transformer) follows the standard recipe, so the novelty is in the structural inductive biases rather than in the generative architecture itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding the design in empirical observations about LoRA weight-space geometry, the paper provides a principled explanation for why prior generators struggle with generalization: element-wise reconstruction of A and B is sensitive to arbitrary rescalings/rotations that leave ΔW unchanged, and a single decoder cannot match the distinct spectral-entropy profiles of encoder, decoder, and cross-attention modules. The remaining gap on zero-shot tasks (40.2% vs. 96.0% in-distribution) and the computational cost of 500-step diffusion sampling are acknowledged limitations; the authors point to richer task-description encoders and rectified-flow or consistency-based priors as natural next steps. For practitioners maintaining large LoRA libraries or seeking to eliminate per-task fine-tuning, the structure-aware framing offers a concrete recipe that outperforms generic hypernetwork baselines by ~5 points on unseen tasks.
