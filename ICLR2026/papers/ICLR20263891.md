## Problem

LLM agents struggle with cross-domain, integrative reasoning, yet existing data-synthesis paradigms (query-centric and document-centric) produce tasks that test localized comprehension rather than multi-source knowledge fusion. Critically, no principled mechanism exists to calibrate synthetic task difficulty to the precise frontier of a model's competence—complex enough to exceed its intrinsic capability, yet solvable with appropriate scaffolding—leading to data that is either too trivial or too intractable for effective learning.

## Input / Output

The AgentFrontier Engine consumes a raw corpus of ~1M public documents, chunked and indexed for retrieval, and produces two synergistic data streams: (1) knowledge-intensive QA pairs for continued pre-training (50B tokens), and (2) frontier-level multi-step reasoning trajectories in ReAct format (12,000 trajectories, each comprising round-wise reasoning reports interleaved with tool observations from search, scholar, browser, and code tools). The engine also yields the ZPD Exam, a 1,024-question self-evolving benchmark grounded in 30,000 recent (2023–2025) scientific papers, strictly disjoint from training data. The trained agent takes a research question as input and outputs a sequence of tool-augmented reasoning rounds culminating in a final answer.

## Method

The AgentFrontier Data Engine operationalizes Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development through a three-stage agentic pipeline. Stage I generates seed QA pairs from thematically coherent chunk triplets (identified via k-NN retrieval with a semantic similarity threshold τ_theme=0.8) to inherently demand knowledge fusion. Stage II iteratively escalates complexity via a tool-augmented refinement agent (DeepSeek-R1 + search/scholar/browser/code tools) that enriches each QA along four dimensions—knowledge expansion, conceptual abstraction, factual grounding, and computational formulation—up to K_max=30 iterations or until the question becomes unsolvable by the base model. Stage III applies adversarial LKP-MKO calibration: a Less Knowledgeable Peer (DeepSeek-R1 without tools) and a More Knowledgeable Other (DeepSeek-V3.1 with tools) jointly filter candidates; tasks the LKP solves are routed to CPT, while tasks the LKP fails but the MKO solves (verified via Best-of-N=3 with GPT-4o judging) form the ZPD training set, with a semantic redundancy filter (ε=0.7) ensuring diversity. This creates a self-adaptive curriculum that continuously targets the expanding capability frontier as the model improves.

## Results

AgentFrontier-30B-A3B (CPT+RFT) achieves 28.6% on HLE, 93.4% on ZPD Exam-v1, 77.1% on RBench-T, and 61.0% on xBench-ScienceQA, setting state-of-the-art among open-source agents. On the Qwen3-30B-A3B backbone, RFT on AgentFrontier data yields 25.7% HLE (vs. 19.9–20.2% for TaskCraft/MegaScience/MiroVerse), 91.4% ZPD Exam, 74.4% RBench-T, and 54.0% xBench-SciQA, outperforming all three baseline datasets across every benchmark. The CPT stage adds +2.9 points on HLE and +7.0 on xBench-ScienceQA over RFT-only training. The model surpasses proprietary deep-research agents (OpenAI DeepResearch 26.6%, Gemini DeepResearch 26.9%, Kimi-Researcher 26.9%) on HLE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentFrontier decisively outperforms all three agent-tuning dataset baselines: on Qwen3-30B-A3B, it leads TaskCraft by +5.8 on HLE and +10.0 on xBench-SciQA, MegaScience by +5.5 on HLE and +6.0 on xBench, and MiroVerse by +6.1 on HLE and +5.0 on xBench, while also winning on ZPD Exam and RBench-T. Against proprietary LLMs with tools, AgentFrontier-30B-A3B (CPT+RFT) surpasses GPT-4o (4.8 HLE), Claude 4 Sonnet (14.3 HLE), and Gemini 2.5 Flash (12.6 HLE) by wide margins on HLE, and leads on ZPD Exam (93.4 vs. 86.6/58.1/51.3). It also beats all open-source deep-research agents (WebDancer 6.4, WebSailor 9.2, WebShaper 8.0 on HLE) by 19–22 points. Against proprietary deep-research agents, AgentFrontier-30B-A3B (28.6 HLE) exceeds OpenAI DeepResearch (26.6), Gemini DeepResearch (26.9), and Kimi-Researcher (26.9). The only model it trails is DeepSeek V3.1-671B (29.8 HLE, 79.4 RBench-T), though AgentFrontier wins on ZPD Exam (93.4 vs. 93.1) and xBench-SciQA (61.0 vs. 55.0) while using a 30B-A3B model versus a 671B model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the agentic data-synthesis line (TaskCraft, MegaScience, MiroVerse) and the ReAct/WebResearcher trajectory-generation paradigm, but departs from them by introducing a principled difficulty-calibration mechanism drawn from Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development. Where prior synthesis pipelines rely on coarse difficulty annotations or heuristically stacked constraints, the AgentFrontier Engine operationalizes the LKP-MKO adversarial calibration to isolate tasks that are unsolvable by a base model yet solvable by a tool-augmented agent. The ZPD Exam further extends the static expert-curated benchmark tradition (HLE, R-Bench) into a self-evolving evaluation that co-adapts with model capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ZPD framing provides a scalable, theoretically grounded recipe for generating training data at the capability frontier, addressing the core bottleneck of frontier-level agent training. The self-evolving ZPD Exam offers a practical solution to benchmark saturation, and the ~$0.78/data-point cost is a fraction of manual expert curation. Open questions include whether the LKP-MKO gap must be re-tuned as base models improve, whether the BoN-identified RL potential (pass@1 21.7% → pass@8 40.7%) translates into meaningful RL gains, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific DeepSeek-based calibration to other model families.
