## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models under strong classifier-free guidance suffer from severe mode collapse, producing repetitive outputs that fail to capture the full range of interpretations a prompt admits. Existing diversity methods intervene repeatedly during the denoising trajectory (optimizing intermediate latents or text embeddings), incurring substantial computational overhead, requiring laborious hyperparameter tuning, and offering limited gains under accelerated few-step samplers where intervention opportunities are scarce. No prior approach addresses the diversity problem at its fundamental source—the initial noise—using a principled contrastive objective.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt (encoded as a text embedding c) and produces a batch of B diverse RGB images (typically B=3–5) that are mutually distinct yet individually aligned to the prompt. Operationally, it samples B initial noise latents z_T from N(0,I) in the latent space of a pre-trained LDM (e.g., 4×64×64 for SD1.5), performs a short one-shot optimization (N_opt=3 steps) on these latents, and then feeds the optimized batch into a standard DDIM sampler without any further modification. The method is model-agnostic and requires no fine-tuning, additional training, or external models beyond the base diffusion backbone.

## Method

Contrastive Noise Optimization (CNO) applies a modified InfoNCE loss in the Tweedie denoised prediction space (the model's one-step estimate of the clean latent from pure noise) to shape a batch of initial noise vectors before sampling begins. The loss has two forces: an attraction term (numerator, scaled by 1/(γτ)) that anchors each optimized noise to its original Tweedie estimate to preserve semantic fidelity, and a repulsion term (denominator) that pushes all batch members apart to maximize diversity. A stop-gradient on the diffusion model's noise prediction ε_θ avoids costly backpropagation through the U-Net, reducing the gradient to a simple scaling of the semantic gradient. The balancing parameter γ is derived from equating maximum attraction with cumulative repulsion, yielding the closed-form rule γ=(τ ln(B−1)+1)⁻¹, and is theoretically grounded via an extended mutual-information bound (Proposition 2) showing γ scales the positive-pair information term. Adaptive average pooling downsamples the high-dimensional latents to a smaller spatial resolution (w×w) before computing pairwise similarities, and the entire optimization completes in ~5% overhead over standard DDIM sampling.

## Results

On SD1.5, CNO achieves the best diversity metrics among all methods: MSS of 0.1317 and Vendi Score of 4.7855, outperforming DDIM (0.1657, 4.6949), PG (0.1426, 4.7630), CADS (0.1330, 4.7805), and DiversityPrompt (0.1393, 4.7599). The same pattern holds on SDXL (MSS 0.1623, Vendi 2.9019 vs. DDIM's 0.2169, 2.8377) and SD3 (MSS 0.2909, Vendi 4.2644 vs. FM-ODE's 0.3028, 4.2205). Quality is preserved throughout: PickScore remains competitive (21.38 on SD1.5, 22.39 on SDXL, 22.56 on SD3) and CLIPScore stays within ~0.1 of the baseline, confirming that diversity gains do not come at the cost of prompt fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CNO consistently outperforms all three baselines on both diversity metrics (MSS↓ and Vendi Score↑) across all three backbones. Against Particle Guidance (PG), CNO achieves lower MSS on every model (e.g., 0.1317 vs. 0.1426 on SD1.5; 0.1623 vs. 0.2050 on SDXL) and higher Vendi Score, while also maintaining better PickScore. Against CADS, CNO wins on MSS by a clear margin on SDXL (0.1623 vs. 0.1765) and SD3 (0.2909 vs. 0.2960), with a narrower but still favorable gap on SD1.5 (0.1317 vs. 0.1330); Vendi Score is also higher in all cases. Against DiversityPrompt (evaluated only on SD1.5), CNO achieves lower MSS (0.1317 vs. 0.1393) and higher Vendi Score (4.7855 vs. 4.7599) while being substantially faster—11.87 s/batch with stop-gradient versus 18.70 s/batch for DiversityPrompt, roughly a 37% speedup. In the user study, CNO is preferred over CADS 78.71% of the time for quality and 83.55% for diversity, and over PG 58.71% and 78.06% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the InfoNCE contrastive loss (van den Oord et al., 2019) for a new application: shaping initial noise latents in T2I diffusion models to promote output diversity. It departs from the dominant line of diversity work (CADS, Particle Guidance, DiversityPrompt) that intervenes repeatedly during the denoising trajectory, instead performing a single lightweight optimization on z_T before sampling begins. The theoretical contribution extends the classical InfoNCE mutual-information lower bound to explicitly incorporate negative-pair information (Proposition 1) and to analyze the effect of the gamma-scaled attraction term (Proposition 2). The approach is conceptually adjacent to InitNO (Guo et al., 2024), which also optimizes initial noise but targets prompt alignment rather than diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the paradigm shift from iterative inference-time interventions to a one-shot pre-processing step, which makes the method robust to aggressive few-step samplers (FLUX, SDXL-Lightning) where CADS and similar methods lose effectiveness. The gamma parameter provides a simple, interpretable control knob for the fidelity-diversity trade-off, and the paper demonstrates that it implicitly functions as a regularizer analogous to an explicit KL penalty. A remaining limitation is occasional degradation on prompts requiring strong compositional grounding (e.g., spatial relations), though lowering gamma partially recovers alignment at the cost of diversity. Open questions include whether extending the optimization to intermediate latents z_t (t < T) could further improve diversity without the fidelity loss observed at the initial step.
