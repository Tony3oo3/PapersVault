## Problem

No prior algorithm simultaneously achieves Õ(√T) adversarial regret and Õ(ln T) stochastic regret for contextual combinatorial semi-bandits with unknown covariance, while remaining computationally tractable. Existing FTRL-based approaches require solving a K-dimensional convex projection every round, which becomes prohibitive as the number of base arms K grows (e.g., LLM-generated ad pools), and existing best-of-both-worlds methods either ignore context, require known covariance, or face exponentially many policy constraints.

## Input / Output

Each round the learner observes a context vector X_t ∈ R^d with ‖X_t‖₂ ≤ 1 drawn i.i.d. from an unknown distribution D, and after playing an m-subset action A_t ∈ {0,1}^K (exactly m arms selected from K base arms), receives per-arm linear losses ℓ_t(X_t,k) = ⟨X_t, θ_{t,k}⟩ only for the selected arms (semi-bandit feedback). The algorithm outputs a randomized action distribution π_t(·|X_t) over the combinatorial action set, with the guarantee that pseudo-regret against the optimal context-dependent action map u*(x) is Õ(√T) adversarially and Õ(ln T) in the corrupted stochastic regime.

## Method

The algorithm instantiates FTRL over conv(A) with a negative Shannon entropy regularizer ψ_t(A) = −H(A)/η_t and a carefully designed time-varying learning rate η_t = 1/β_t that interpolates between adversarial and stochastic schedules. For loss estimation, it uses a Matrix-Geometric-Resampling subroutine to approximate the per-arm precision matrix Σ_{t,k}^{-1} without requiring full knowledge of the context distribution, at O(K ln T) sample cost per round. The key computational contribution exploits the KKT conditions of the separable Bregman projection to reduce the K-dimensional convex subproblem to a single scalar root-finding equation Σ_k (f')^{-1}(−λ−c_k) = m, solved by bisection in Õ(K) time per round—matching the per-iteration cost of Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader while preserving FTRL's tight regret guarantees. A refined entropy bound, obtained by partitioning the base arm set and lifting from the mean-action polytope to the space of action distributions, enables the self-bounding argument that yields the Õ(ln T) stochastic regret.

## Results

The paper establishes the first best-of-both-worlds regret guarantees for contextual combinatorial semi-bandits: Õ(√T) in the adversarial regime and Õ(ln T) in the corrupted stochastic regime (Theorem 2.1). Empirically, the proposed bisection-based projection (Algorithm 3) runs nearly 10× faster than the Newton baseline at K=100 and consistently outperforms MOSEK by approximately 5× across all tested arm counts (K ∈ {10,…,100}). Cumulative regret at T=10⁴ is indistinguishable (within numerical tolerance) between the bisection, Newton, and MOSEK implementations, confirming correctness while delivering substantial per-round speed-ups.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prior theoretical work (Table 1), this paper is the first to simultaneously provide both adversarial Õ(√T) and stochastic Õ(ln T) regret for contextual combinatorial semi-bandits; Zierahn et al. (2023) match the adversarial rate but offer no stochastic guarantee, while Qin et al. (2014) provide only a stochastic bound under full-bandit feedback. In the empirical per-iteration runtime comparison (Figure 1), the bisection method (Algorithm 3) achieves roughly a 10× speedup over the Newton heuristic from Zimmert et al. (2019) at K=100 and a consistent ~5× advantage over MOSEK across all K values. In cumulative regret (Tables 2–3, T=10⁴), all three projection methods—bisection, Newton, and MOSEK—produce statistically indistinguishable regret under both stochastic and adversarial settings for m ∈ {3,5} and K ∈ {20,40}, confirming that the speedup comes at no statistical cost. The comparison with Ito et al. (2022) and Kong et al. (2023) is primarily theoretical: those methods achieve BOBW rates but in different problem settings (feedback graphs and linear bandits, respectively) that do not directly address the combinatorial semi-bandit structure with unknown covariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: (i) the FTRL/OSMD framework with entropy regularizers (Shalev-Shwartz 2012; Zimmert et al. 2019), (ii) the Matrix-Geometric-Resampling precision-matrix estimator and auxiliary-game analysis from Neu & Olkhovskaya (2020) and Zierahn et al. (2023), and (iii) the best-of-both-worlds self-bounding technique from Kuroki et al. (2024) and Zimmert & Seldin (2021). The key departure is that prior BOBW results covered either linear contextual bandits (Kuroki et al. 2024) or context-free combinatorial semi-bandits (Zimmert et al. 2019), but no method simultaneously handled unknown covariance, combinatorial action structure, and both stochastic and adversarial regimes. Computationally, the paper departs from standard K-dimensional Bregman projections by exploiting KKT conditions to reduce the update to a one-dimensional bisection problem, matching the per-round complexity of Follow-the-Perturbed-Leader while retaining FTRL's tighter regret guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm to close the BOBW gap for contextual combinatorial semi-bandits, a setting directly relevant to large-scale personalized advertising and recommendation where K candidate items are generated (e.g., via LLMs) and only m are displayed. The KKT-based projection reduction is practically significant: it enables real-time deployment at scale where K-dimensional solvers become prohibitive, with demonstrated ~10× speedup over Newton and ~5× over MOSEK. Two limitations remain open: the Shannon-entropy regularizer introduces an extra Õ(ln T) factor in the adversarial regret, and the m-dependence scales as √m, which is suboptimal relative to known lower bounds. Whether these can be tightened—particularly the m-scaling—remains a natural direction for future work.
