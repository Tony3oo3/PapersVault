## Problem

Portfolio allocation under shifting market regimes is difficult because classical mean-variance methods rely on fixed historical windows that become stale after regime changes, while deep RL allocators overfit to macro signals and ignore stock-level idiosyncratic dynamics. The paper asks how to jointly model time-varying macro impact and firm-specific factors at the individual-stock level to produce regime-aware predictive distributions that yield robust, risk-diversified weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261588/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each rebalancing time τ, the method consumes a macro vector m_τ (five indices: market index, dollar index, US term spread, VIX, gold, after ν-day rolling normalization and log differencing) and per-stock features c_τ^(s) that concatenate a Kalman-filtered temporal embedding β_τ^(s) with the last normalized adjusted-close and daily log return. It also takes a rolling prior (μ_prior, Σ_prior) computed from the most recent ν business days. The output is a budget-constrained weight vector w_τ (summing to 1) obtained in closed form, together with k generated per-stock return trajectories of length ℓ from which the view mean and covariance are derived as sample statistics.

## Method

STABLE couples three modules trained jointly under a supervised diffusion MSE objective with L2 regularization. The Conditional Diffusion Generator (CDG) is a DDIM-based sampler whose UNet denoiser is conditioned on a refined macro vector h_m,τ and a refined stock vector h_c,τ^(s), producing per-stock return segments of length ℓ. The Multi-Level Guidance (MLG) module decomposes the predicted noise into a shared systematic term ε̂_{n,τ} (evaluated with macro-only conditioning) and a firm-specific residual (ε̂_{n,τ}^(s) − ε̂_{n,τ}), scaled by a learnable scalar gate z_τ^(s) ∈ [0, z_max] that adapts the macro-vs-micro balance per stock and per time step. The Black-Litterman-based MVO (BL-MVO) module computes view mean and covariance from k generated paths, fuses them with the rolling prior via a certainty-weighted Gaussian posterior update, and solves the Sharpe-maximizing allocation in closed form as w_τ* = Σ_BL^{-1}μ_BL / (1^T Σ_BL^{-1}μ_BL). The temporal stock embedding β_τ^(s) is estimated via recursive Kalman filtering of each stock's log return against the macro vector, providing a regime-aware, time-varying representation of macro sensitivity.

## Results

On the primary portfolio allocation task across four regional markets, STABLE achieves the best Annualized Sharpe Ratio (ASR) in every region: 1.85 (S&P500), −0.41 (CSI300), 2.92 (EUROSTOXX), and 1.61 (KOSPI200), with the largest gain of 122.9% over the strongest competitor (AlphaMix) in EUROSTOXX. STABLE also attains the lowest Relative Maximum Drawdown (RMDD) and Annualized Volatility (AVol) in all four markets, e.g., RMDD of 3.84% and AVol of 10.88% in EUROSTOXX. On the secondary time-series estimation task, STABLE achieves the lowest MSE (×10⁻⁴) in all markets (3.51 S&P500, 3.89 CSI300, 2.49 EUROSTOXX, 8.15 KOSPI200), reducing MSE by up to 15.7% over the best generative baseline (Diffusion-TS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STABLE outperforms all six portfolio-allocation baselines (CRP, MVO, MOM, DeepTrader, MetaTrader, AlphaMix) on all three metrics (ASR, RMDD, AVol) in all four regional markets. The strongest competitor is AlphaMix, which routes among multiple neural allocators by market state; STABLE improves ASR over AlphaMix by 428% in S&P500 (1.85 vs. 0.35), 122.9% in EUROSTOXX (2.92 vs. 1.31), 48.8% in CSI300 (−0.41 vs. −0.80), and 9.5% in KOSPI200 (1.61 vs. 1.47). Classical strategies (CRP, MVO, MOM) and the regime-switching MetaTrader are consistently outperformed, with MVO showing particularly high volatility (e.g., 21.18% AVol in S&P500 vs. STABLE's 13.43%). DeepTrader, which relies solely on macro features for long-short balance, performs worst in most markets (e.g., ASR −2.44 in EUROSTOXX). On the time-series estimation task, STABLE beats all three generative baselines (Diffusion-TS, AEC-GAN, KoVAE) on both MSE and DTW in every market, with the largest MSE gap in CSI300 (3.89 vs. 4.57 for AEC-GAN, a 15.7% reduction). In the isolated covariance-estimation experiment (Tables 9–11), STABLE's diffusion-derived covariance also dominates DCC-GARCH, LSTM-Cholesky, LightGBM-Cholesky, and the three deep generative models across all regimes, confirming that the multi-level guidance mechanism yields superior risk estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

STABLE sits at the intersection of two well-established lines: classical Black-Litterman portfolio optimization (Black & Litterman, 1990), which blends investor views with a prior to produce allocation weights, and conditional diffusion models for time-series generation. It departs from prior MPT-based methods by replacing the historical-window mean/covariance estimates with regime-aware generative samples, and from deep RL allocators (DeepTrader, MetaTrader, AlphaMix) by explicitly decomposing per-step denoising noise into a shared macro component and a stock-specific idiosyncratic component via a learnable gate (multi-level guidance). The temporal stock embedding via Kalman filtering (Das & Ghoshal, 2010) provides a time-varying macro-sensitivity vector that feeds the diffusion condition, distinguishing STABLE from static sector-label or price-embedding approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: portfolio allocators that degrade sharply under regime shifts because their return/covariance estimates are anchored to a fixed historical window. The multi-level guidance mechanism—a learnable gate that adaptively weights systematic vs. idiosyncratic noise per stock and per time step—is a clean architectural contribution that could transfer to other conditional generative forecasting tasks. Limitations include a relatively short test window (≈6 months), a modest universe (37–55 stocks per market), and no explicit treatment of transaction costs or rebalancing frequency. The authors note future work on incorporating text-based macro and stock features, and the convergence-to-equal-weight behavior under extreme uncertainty (observed in KOSPI200 during COVID) suggests the method may underperform in markets where idiosyncratic signals dominate and prior covariance is poorly estimated.
