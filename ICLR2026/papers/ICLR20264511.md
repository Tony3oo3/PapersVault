## Problem

Voxel-level reconstruction in masked autoencoders for 3D medical imaging suffers from three fundamental limitations: discrete intensity regression fails to capture continuous anatomical boundaries, the decoder is discarded after pre-training (non-transferable), and dense voxel representations are parameter-inefficient given the inherently sparse anatomical distribution in medical volumes. This paper asks whether replacing the reconstruction target with continuous geometric primitives can simultaneously improve representation quality, decoder utility, and parameter efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 96×96×96 CT volume patches (resampled to 1.5 mm isotropic, HU clipped to [-175, 250], normalized to [0,1]) that are patchified into 512 non-overlapping 12×12×12 tokens, of which 75% are randomly masked. The encoder outputs latent representations for downstream transfer, while the decoder outputs 512 eleven-dimensional Gaussian parameter vectors (position ∈ R³, anisotropic scale ∈ R³, rotation quaternion ∈ R⁴, intensity ∈ R¹) that are differentially rendered into a full 3D volume. The rendered volume is compared to ground truth only on masked regions via MSE, and the same Gaussian parameters can be directly used as zero-shot initialization for 3DGS-based sparse-view CT reconstruction.

## Method

MedGMAE replaces the voxel regression head of a standard MAE with a lightweight Transformer decoder that predicts 3D Gaussian primitives via k=512 learnable query tokens attending to visible patch embeddings. Four specialized linear heads (with sigmoid for position/scale/intensity and L2-normalization for rotation) produce the 11-dim Gaussian parameters, which are rendered into a volume through a differentiable CUDA kernel that accumulates each Gaussian's contribution within its 2σ bounding box. The key architectural insight is that continuous Gaussian primitives naturally encode spatial position, orientation, and scale—capturing anatomical geometry in a way that discrete voxels cannot—while the sparse set of 512 Gaussians aligns with the fact that organs occupy only ~12% of the volume space (99% parameter reduction). An extended variant, MedGMAE*, adds hierarchical residual blocks across three levels (coarse-to-fine) that progressively refine Gaussian positions, scales, rotations, and intensities with bounded tanh residuals, enabling finer volumetric detail. The pretrained decoder doubles as a geometry-aware initializer for 3DGS-based CT reconstruction, accelerating convergence by ~1.39× without sacrificing final PSNR/SSIM.

## Results

On segmentation (Table 1), MedGMAE achieves the best DSC across most dataset/proportion settings, reaching 58.79% on AMOS (1%), 62.72% on FLARE'22 (1%), 70.92% on SegTHOR (1%), and 89.15% on SegTHOR (100%), outperforming the previous best VoCo by up to 5.06% in low-data regimes. On CT-RATE classification, MedGMAE attains 76.40% AUC, surpassing VoCo-160K (76.02%) and SUP (76.04%). For registration, MedGMAE achieves 85.7% DSC on OASIS (vs. VoCo's 84.4%) and 73.7% on IXI. For zero-shot CT reconstruction initialization, MedGMAE reduces training time by 31–37% and requires 28–39% fewer iterations to reach PSNR=35 dB and SSIM=90% compared to the 3DGR baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedGMAE outperforms the strongest prior self-supervised baseline VoCo on 10 of 12 segmentation settings, with the largest margins in low-data regimes: +2.98% on AMOS (1%), +5.06% on FLARE'22 (1%), and +3.80% on SegTHOR (1%). The one notable exception is BTCV (1%), where VoCo (73.20%) exceeds MedGMAE (66.19%), likely due to BTCV's smaller organ count and different anatomical distribution. Against general MIM methods (MAE, SparK), MedGMAE leads by 4.12% on AMOS (1%) and 4.20% on SegTHOR (1%). In classification, MedGMAE (76.40% AUC) edges out VoCo-160K (76.02%) and SUP (76.04%) by ~0.4%. For registration, MedGMAE surpasses VoCo by 1.3% on OASIS (85.7% vs. 84.4%) and matches it on IXI (73.7% vs. 73.6%), while both IXI and OASIS are MRI modalities unseen during CT pre-training, demonstrating cross-modality generalization. In CT reconstruction, MedGMAE initialization reduces training time by 31–37% and iteration counts by 28–39% relative to the 3DGR baseline across 80/120/160 projections, with the hierarchical MedGMAE* variant achieving the fastest convergence (e.g., 990 iterations to PSNR=35 at 80 projections vs. 1670 for 3DGR).

## Relation to Prior Work

MedGMAE extends the masked autoencoder paradigm (He et al., 2022) to 3D medical volumes by replacing the standard voxel-level reconstruction objective with prediction of 3D Gaussian primitive parameters, drawing on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (Kerbl et al., 2023) and the 2D Gaussian MAE concept (Rajasegaran et al., 2025). However, the authors explicitly distinguish their motivation from the 2D Gaussian MAE work: rather than inferring abstract 2.5D spatial layers, they leverage Gaussians as a continuous, parameter-efficient intermediate representation for true 3D anatomical volumes. The framework also departs from prior medical MIM methods (VoCo, HySparK, SUP, etc.) by making the decoder itself a transferable asset for downstream 3DGS-based CT reconstruction, rather than discarding it after pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation of voxel-level MIM in medical imaging—discrete intensity regression fails to capture continuous anatomical boundaries—and offers a principled alternative with 99% parameter reduction that aligns with the inherent sparsity of 3D medical volumes. The dual utility of the pretrained model (strong encoder representations plus a zero-shot geometry-aware decoder for CT reconstruction) broadens the practical impact beyond representation learning alone. Key limitations include sensitivity to FBP reconstruction noise in the CT reconstruction task (acknowledged by the authors as requiring a multi-view 3D Gaussian foundation model) and evaluation limited to abdominal CT for pre-training, leaving open questions about generalization to other body regions and modalities at scale.
