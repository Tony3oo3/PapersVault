## Problem

Existing medical LLMs achieve strong scores on static benchmarks like USMLE but fail at dynamic clinical inquiry—the sequential, uncertainty-driven process of forming differential diagnoses, asking targeted questions, and adapting strategy based on patient responses. Current models lack three critical capabilities: strategic multi-turn questioning, empathetic communication during sensitive conversations, and the ability to learn from high-quality prior consultation experiences the way a human physician would.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265222/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each turn of a multi-turn dialogue (up to 10 turns), the doctor agent receives the full dialogue history as observation and optionally top-k=2 retrieved experiences (state-action pairs from the experience repository) prepended to its prompt. The agent produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace (differential diagnosis, information needs, rationale) followed by a natural-language question or final diagnostic conclusion. The patient agent, a separate Qwen3-8B instance initialized with a hidden clinical scenario, generates the next patient response, closing the POMDP loop.

## Method

DOCTOR-R1 introduces an Experiential Agentic RL framework built on Qwen3-8B, combining three components within a multi-agent POMDP environment. First, a two-tiered reward architecture provides dense per-turn process rewards across eight dimensions (safety, reasoning, accuracy, completeness, information gathering, faithfulness, empathy, humility) with a hierarchical veto system that assigns immediate large penalties for safety/reasoning/accuracy failures, plus a terminal outcome reward scoring final diagnostic correctness. Second, an experience repository stores high-reward trajectories and retrieves them via a three-stage pipeline: embedding-based candidate selection (jina-embeddings-v3 with reward-weighted cosine similarity), cross-encoder reranking (bge-reranker-base), and novelty/reward filtering using a dynamic threshold. Third, the policy is optimized with GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) rather than PPO, which eliminates the critic network and uses listwise comparison within sampled trajectory groups—providing more stable gradients for subjective soft-skill metrics like communication quality. The framework is trained on 100k simulated diagnostic dialogues from KaMed with GPT-4.1-Nano-annotated chain-of-thought supervision.

## Results

On HealthBench Main (500 cases), DOCTOR-R1 (8B) achieves an average score of 36.29, surpassing GPT-4.1 (31.18), Grok-4 (33.03), and the best open-source model Baichuan-M2-32B (33.16). Key axis scores include Accuracy 37.84, Communication Quality 64.15, and Context Awareness 49.24. On MAQuE, DOCTOR-R1 matches GPT-4.1 on diagnostic Accuracy (60.00) while achieving substantially higher Empathy (93.80 vs. 75.20). On static QA, DOCTOR-R1 improves over its Qwen3-8B base by +20 points on MedQA (83.50 vs. 63.50) and +15 on MMLU (85.00 vs. 70.00), confirming no knowledge degradation from RL training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOCTOR-R1 (8B) outperforms all open-source medical LLMs by a wide margin: it beats Baichuan-M2-32B (the strongest open-source baseline) by +3.13 average on HealthBench (36.29 vs. 33.16) and +3.00 on MAQuE Accuracy (60.00 vs. 57.00), while also leading in Communication Quality (64.15 vs. 58.01) and Empathy (93.80 vs. 65.20). Against proprietary models, DOCTOR-R1 surpasses GPT-4.1 by +5.11 average (36.29 vs. 31.18) and Grok-4 by +3.26 (36.29 vs. 33.03), with the largest gap in Communication Quality (+3.50 over GPT-4.1). Compared to same-size 8B medical models, the advantage is dramatic: DOCTOR-R1 scores 36.29 versus UltraMedical-8B (22.19), DoctorAgent-RL (15.77), and HuatuoGPT-o1-8B (16.25). Against 70B models, DOCTOR-R1 exceeds UltraMedical-70B by +9.91 (36.29 vs. 26.38) and HuatuoGPT-o1-70B by +15.08, demonstrating that the experiential RL framework compensates for a 4–9× parameter gap. On static QA (MedQA/MMLU), DOCTOR-R1 (83.50/85.00) approaches GPT-4.1 (89.00/92.00) and exceeds Baichuan-M2-32B (81.50/84.00), confirming that the RL training strengthens rather than degrades foundational knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its core policy optimization algorithm, extending it from single-turn reasoning to multi-turn agentic dialogue in a POMDP formulation. It departs from prior medical LLMs (HuatuoGPT, UltraMedical, Med42) that apply RL only to static decision-making, and from agentic frameworks (Baichuan-M2, DoctorAgent-RL) that enable multi-turn interaction but lack differential diagnosis and experience-based learning. The key conceptual departure is the unification of strategic inquiry (soft skills) and diagnostic accuracy (hard skills) under a single two-tiered reward, combined with a multi-stage experience retrieval pipeline that grounds policy learning in high-reward prior trajectories rather than relying solely on parametric knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that an 8B model trained with experiential agentic RL outperforms 32B/70B specialized models and proprietary LLMs on dynamic clinical inquiry suggests that training methodology for sequential decision-making can be more impactful than raw model scale for consultation tasks. The paper also demonstrates that enhancing inquiry capability (empathy, strategic questioning) improves diagnostic accuracy, challenging the assumption that soft and hard clinical skills are independently optimizable. However, significant gaps remain: HealthBench Hard scores drop sharply (18.73 vs. GPT-5's 29.57), the reward model is itself an LLM (Qwen3-8B) introducing potential circularity, and expert validation involves only two physicians. Open questions include whether the experience repository approach generalizes to multi-specialty or multi-modal clinical settings, and whether the framework's gains persist as base models improve.
