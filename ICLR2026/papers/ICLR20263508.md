## Problem

Existing visual prosthetics target early visual cortex (V1/V2) using retinotopy to evoke simple percepts like phosphenes and letters, but cannot produce complex object-level experiences. Higher-level visual areas (IT) encode rich object representations and are a promising stimulation target, yet retinotopy breaks down there and no principled method exists for selecting which sites to stimulate, with what parameters, to reliably evoke specific object percepts in vivo.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes passive-viewing multi-unit recordings from 64-channel Utah arrays in monkey IT cortex (4,000 GAN-sampled reference images), microstimulation parameters (electrode site, current amplitude in μA, pulse frequency in Hz), and 7-image GAN sequences generated to parametrically modulate a target site's response. Outputs include per-site predicted behavioral shifts (ΔAUC of 2AFC psychometric curves), the optimal site–sequence pair for in-vivo deployment, and GAN/diffusion-generated images that visualize the perceptual content of simulated stimulation. The model's behavioral readout is a similarity comparison between the sample stimulus and two sequence-extreme alternatives in the penultimate layer, aggregated over 30 sequences to approximate trial variability.

## Method

The framework has three stages. First, topographic ANNs (ResNet-18 trained with SimCLR contrastive loss plus a relative spatial loss that encourages nearby units to have correlated responses) are aligned to each monkey's Utah array by maximizing site-by-site Pearson correlations on 4,000 reference images, yielding a fixed 1:1 mapping between model cortical-sheet positions and physical electrodes. Second, for each candidate site the system generates 7-image GAN sequences that monotonically modulate that site's activation, ranks them by a slope-to-noise selectivity score, and applies a perturbation module that adds an exponentially decaying activity boost (Δr(d) = min(r_base + γ·f_pulse, r_max)·exp(−d/λ(I))) to simulate focal microstimulation; the resulting penultimate-layer representations are converted to 2AFC choices via cosine similarity to predict ΔAUC. Third, the top-predicted site–sequence pairs are mapped back to monkey electrodes and tested in a delayed match-to-sample task with biphasic microstimulation (50 μA, 300 Hz, 200 ms) randomly interleaved with sham trials. A complementary visualization stage decodes perturbed model IT activity into StyleGAN-XL w-space and CLIP vision embeddings (via ridge regression) to synthesize images that reveal face-like perceptual content emerging at face-selective stimulation sites.

## Results

In Experiment 1, model-predicted behavioral shifts (ΔAUC) correlated significantly with stimulation-evoked shifts in both monkeys (Monkey 1: Pearson r = 0.58, p = 0.024, R² = 0.34; Monkey 2: Pearson r = 0.53, p = 0.019, R² = 0.28; combined r = 0.53, p = 0.0012), though the absolute monkey behavioral effect was not significantly different from zero. In Experiment 2, model-guided stimulation induced a significant behavioral bias in Monkey 1 (Wilcoxon signed-rank p = 0.043, Cohen's d = 0.671), confirming that model-optimized stimulation parameters can shift in-vivo behavior along a targeted perceptual direction. In-silico visualization showed that stimulating face-selective sites (y = 42) at 1000 μA produced face-like features superimposed on unrelated objects, qualitatively matching human 'facephene' reports, while control sites with low face-selectivity did not.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a proof-of-concept study without direct comparisons to competing stimulation-guidance methods; the `compares_to` list is empty. The paper's internal controls serve as the relevant baselines: (1) a non-topographic ResNet-18 (trained without spatial loss, α_k = 0) achieved comparable IT linear predictivity (R² = 0.27 / 0.19) to the topographic model, indicating that topography does not substantially alter model–brain alignment on this dataset; (2) in Experiment 1, the model's per-site predictions correlated with monkey behavior but the absolute behavioral shift was not significantly above zero, whereas in Experiment 2 the behavioral shift was significant but the per-site correlation was lost, likely due to degraded electrode signal quality (72% of channels showed significant reliability decline over four months); (3) in the visualization pipeline, shuffled-perturbation controls (preserving perturbation magnitude but destroying topographic structure) did not reproduce the face-like content emergence, supporting that the effect depends on spatially structured stimulation of face-selective regions rather than non-specific global modulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper integrates three previously separate lines of work into a closed-loop, model-in-the-loop framework: topographic ANNs with spatial loss (Margalit et al., 2024), perturbation modules for simulating microstimulation effects (Schrimpf et al., 2024), and GAN-based stimulus generation for IT cortex (Papale et al., 2024). The key departure is moving from offline evaluation of perturbation modules to prospective, in-vivo testing where model-optimized stimulation parameters are deployed on live animals, and shifting the target from retinotopically organized early visual cortex to higher-level IT cortex where retinotopy is a poor guide. For visualization, the paper adapts Brain2GAN (Dado et al., 2024) by replacing neural recordings with topographic model activations and adds a diffusion-based pipeline using IP-Adapter and CLIP embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first proof-of-principle that computational models can guide causal interventions in higher-level visual cortex to bias primate behavior, opening a path toward next-generation visual prosthetics that evoke complex object-level percepts rather than simple phosphenes. However, the evidence is preliminary: degrading electrode signal quality limited the number of tested sites, results are split across two experiments (correlation without significant shift in Exp 1, significant shift without per-site correlation in Exp 2), and the model systematically overestimates behavioral effects. Key open questions include whether IT representations alone are sufficient for rich object percepts, how to best handle multi-site stimulation, and whether the framework generalizes to other intervention techniques (TMS, focused ultrasound) and brain regions beyond vision.
