## Problem

Existing Frank-Wolfe (FW) convergence analyses typically require the objective to be L-smooth (Lipschitz-continuous gradient) and convex, which excludes many practical problems such as ℓ_p losses with p≠2, phase retrieval, and nonnegative matrix factorization. This paper asks whether these assumptions can be relaxed to the L-smooth adaptable (L-smad) property and weak convexity while still achieving linear convergence rates, and answers affirmatively.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires only a linear minimization oracle (LMO) over the compact convex set P—no projection oracle—and a kernel generating distance φ that is strictly convex on an open set C containing P. The adaptive step-size strategy (Algorithm 2) simultaneously searches for the smoothness constant L and the Bregman scaling exponent ν, which is generally unknown a priori. Convergence is certified via the primal gap f(x_t)−f* for convex/weakly convex objectives and via the Frank-Wolfe gap ⟨∇f(x_t), x_t−v_t⟩ for the nonconvex case.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the Euclidean distance in the classical FW update with a Bregman distance D_φ, exploiting the triangle scaling property D_φ((1−γ)x+γy, x) ≤ γ^{1+ν} D_φ(y,x) that holds for any strictly convex kernel φ. This yields a Bregman short step-size γ = (⟨∇f(x),x−v⟩ / (L(1+ν)D_φ(v,x)))^{1/ν} and an adaptive backtracking variant that jointly estimates L and ν. A Bregman away-step FW variant (Algorithm 3) is also proposed for polytopes, using the pyramidal width to control zigzagging. Under the Hölder error bound condition (convex case) or local quadratic growth plus weak convexity (nonconvex case), the algorithms achieve rates ranging from O(ε^{(1+ν−q)/(νq)}) to full linear convergence, with the linear regime triggered when the HEB exponent q matches the Bregman scaling exponent 1+ν.

## Results

The paper establishes convergence rates for FW algorithms under the L-smooth adaptable (L-smad) property, generalizing existing L-smooth results. For convex optimization with the HEB condition (q ≥ 1+ν) and x* ∈ int P, the Bregman short/adaptive step-size achieves linear convergence O(log ε⁻¹) when q = 1+ν, and O(ε^((1+ν−q)/(νq))) otherwise. For nonconvex weakly convex optimization with local quadratic growth, local linear convergence is established (first such result for FW algorithms). Numerically, BregFW achieves the smallest primal and FW gaps on ℓ_p loss (gas sensor data, p=1.1) and phase retrieval, while ShortFW and EucFW fail to converge due to non-Lipschitz gradients.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the ℓ_p loss experiment (gas sensor data, p=1.1, where ∇f is not Lipschitz continuous), BregFW is the only method with a theoretical guarantee and achieves the smallest primal and FW gaps; ShortFW and EucFW fail to converge entirely, while OpenFW and ProjGD converge more slowly. In the phase retrieval experiment (nonconvex, K-sparse polytope constraint), BregFW with the adaptive Bregman step-size achieves the smallest primal and FW gaps among all step-size strategies (EucFW, ShortFW, OpenFW) and terminates before the 1000-iteration limit. The away-step variant BregAFW is compared against EucAFW, ShortAFW, and OpenAFW in supplementary figures, with BregAFW showing superior convergence. Theoretically, when ν=1 (Euclidean case), all rates reduce to those of Lacoste-Julien & Jaggi (2015), Kerdreux et al. (2022), and Pedregosa et al. (2020), confirming the results are strict generalizations. Mirror Descent (MD) and Projected Gradient Descent (ProjGD) serve as additional baselines but are outperformed by BregFW in the ℓ_p loss setting where the Bregman geometry is well-matched to the problem structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Frank-Wolfe (conditional gradient) algorithm lineage by replacing the Euclidean distance with a Bregman distance and the L-smoothness assumption with the weaker L-smooth adaptable (L-smad) property from Bolte et al. (2018) and Bauschke et al. (2017). The adaptive step-size strategy generalizes the backtracking approach of Pedregosa et al. (2020), while the away-step variant builds on Lacoste-Julien & Jaggi (2015) and the pyramidal-width analysis of Braun et al. (2025). The convergence analysis under Hölder error bounds follows the framework of Kerdreux et al. (2022) but extends it to the Bregman setting where the scaling exponent 1+ν of the Bregman distance interacts with the HEB exponent q, creating a new matching condition for linear convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first linear convergence guarantee for the Frank-Wolfe algorithm on nonconvex problems (under weak convexity plus local quadratic growth), a result the authors note was previously unattainable with standard FW analyses. Practically, this extends FW to problem classes where gradients are not Lipschitz continuous (e.g., ℓ_p loss with p≠2, phase retrieval), where existing Euclidean FW methods provably fail to converge. Key limitations include the need to estimate the Bregman scaling exponent ν, the locality of the nonconvex linear convergence guarantee, and the restrictive requirement that the minimizer lie in the interior of P. Open questions include whether the quadratic growth assumption for nonconvex cases can be relaxed (e.g., via DC optimization) and whether the q = 1+ν matching condition can be exploited algorithmically.
