## Problem

Inference-time scaling via reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards has substantially improved reasoning in Large Vision-Language Models, yet its impact on visual perception remains unclear. The paper identifies that current LVLMs rely on a 'fast perception' paradigm—treating visual understanding as a one-shot output with minimal perception tokens—which prevents inference-time scaling from transferring to perceptual accuracy. This work asks whether explicitly restructuring perception as a step-by-step process can unlock the same scaling benefits that reasoning has already achieved.

## Input / Output

The model receives a synthetic geometric image (600–1200 px, containing 3–7 colored circles, triangles, or rectangles) and a text question requiring relative estimation of a geometric attribute (length, perimeter, or area), formulated as a ratio to avoid resolution confounding. The output is a structured five-stage reasoning chain (Review → Hint → Reference → Estimation → Calculation) in which every distance is rendered as a sequence of symbolic tokens (e.g., `<=======>` for 1.0 unit, each `=` encoding 0.1 unit) before a final numerical answer is produced, making the perception process token-rich and inspectable rather than a single scalar.

## Method

Perception-Time Scaling (PTS) introduces two complementary mechanisms: (1) Perception Elaboration, which replaces raw numerical values with discrete symbolic token sequences (angle-bracket-delimited groups of `=` signs, each representing 0.1 unit) so that the model must construct distance representations step by step, and (2) Perception Decomposition, which trains the model to tile a chosen reference segment along the target distance in incremental accumulation steps, mimicking ruler-based measurement. Training proceeds in two stages: a supervised cold-start on 6,000 GPT-4o-synthesized PTS-style reasoning chains teaches the structured pattern, followed by GRPO reinforcement learning with a custom continuous exponential reward r(o) = e^(−α|o−d_t|/d_t) (α=3) that provides graded feedback proportional to relative error, plus a label-normalization schedule that first trains on sub-unit targets before introducing random-scale data. This design embeds perception explicitly into the reasoning chain, allowing RL reward signals to refine intermediate perceptual steps rather than only the final answer.

## Results

On the DisTANCE benchmark, PTS (SFT + GRPO) raises Qwen2.5-VL-7B's high-precision average (RA₀.₁) from 8.0% to 64.7% and average relative accuracy (RA_avg) from 21.5% to 88.3%, a 6× improvement in precision. Under the strict 10% relative-error threshold, the model achieves 70.0% on length, 74.0% on perimeter, and 50.0% on area estimation. On out-of-domain tasks, PTS improves Geoperception LHC accuracy by ~20 points (59.8%→78.7%) and LEGO-Puzzles Height accuracy from 30.0% to 33.0%, while also yielding consistent gains on general multimodal benchmarks (e.g., CV-Bench 73.36→75.68, BLINK Full 55.0→55.9) when combined with math reasoning data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTS (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, SFT+RL) achieves 64.7% RA₀.₁ / 88.3% RA_avg on DisTANCE, surpassing every baseline by a wide margin. Among proprietary models, GPT-5 (31.7/64.1) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (29.7/62.5) lead but still trail PTS by 33 and 35 points on RA₀.₁ respectively; GPT-4o (15.3/35.7) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (19.0/41.9) are further behind. Among open-source reasoning models, the best is VL-ReThinker-7B (10.0/26.6) and ReVPT-7B (11.3/23.4), both of which PTS outperforms by over 50 points on RA₀.₁. Spatial-aware baselines SpaceThinker-3B (7.0/34.3) and Spatial-R1-7B (9.3/40.7) are also far below PTS, as are tool-augmented approaches GPT-4o+DetToolChain (15.3/42.0) and GPT-4o+Sketchpad (19.0/30.1). Notably, even the strongest non-PTS training recipe (Direct + RL, 40.7/70.7) lags PTS by 24 points on RA₀.₁, confirming that the structured perception decomposition and symbolic token elaboration are critical beyond simply scaling reasoning length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inference-time scaling (RLVR) paradigm from LLMs to LVLMs, building directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for the RL stage. However, it departs from prior multimodal reasoning work (R1-OneVision, Vision-R1, MM-Eureka, etc.) by arguing that those methods scale reasoning but not perception, identifying a 'fast perception' bottleneck where visual understanding is compressed into minimal tokens. The proposed Perception-Time Scaling (PTS) introduces two novel mechanisms—symbolic token elaboration and step-by-step perception decomposition—that restructure the perception stage itself to be amenable to RL optimization, rather than merely lengthening the reasoning chain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that inference-time scaling yields only marginal perception gains challenges the prevailing assumption that longer reasoning chains universally improve multimodal understanding, and motivates a separate 'perception-time' scaling axis. The 8%→64.7% high-precision improvement and the transfer of purely synthetic training data to real-world perception benchmarks (CV-Bench, BLINK) suggest that explicitly modeling perception as a structured process is a viable path toward more perception-aware LVLMs. Open questions include whether PTS generalizes beyond geometric distance estimation to other perception types (texture, color, shape identity), whether the symbolic token design is optimal, and whether the approach scales to more complex real-world scenes beyond synthetic shapes.
