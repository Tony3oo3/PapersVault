## Problem

Backdoor attacks on text-to-image diffusion models require computational resources comparable to full model fine-tuning, creating a high barrier for attackers and limiting the scope of empirical study. No prior work has identified which components of the backdoor injection process are actually dispensable, leaving both the attack cost and the detection surface poorly understood.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261765/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the attack (STEBA), the method consumes a baseline diffusion model, a poisoned training set (trigger-containing prompts paired with target images), and produces a backdoored model where only a sparse subset of weights is modified. For the defense (STEDF), the method ingests intermediate hidden-state activations across diffusion timesteps and per-layer weight-difference features extracted via pre-inserted hooks, and outputs a binary benign/malicious confidence score that, when exceeding a threshold, interrupts the ongoing diffusion process before the final image is decoded. Detection runs in parallel with inference, adding no latency to clean generations.

## Method

The paper identifies two forms of redundancy in backdoor injection: (1) an 'enrichment phenomenon' where gradient updates concentrate disproportionately in a small set of key weights (primarily normalization layers and cross-attention transformer blocks), and (2) a 'marginal effect in timesteps' where only a mid-to-late subset of diffusion steps is critical for implanting the backdoor, with attacking all steps actually degrading performance. STEBA exploits both by constraining optimization to an ε-ball around the top-k key weights (θ*) and a reduced timestep set (T*), yielding up to 15.07× temporal speedup and 82% GPU memory reduction while maintaining or improving attack success rate. STEDF exploits the resulting anisotropy in hidden-state trajectories across timesteps and the weight-enrichment signature via a lightweight feedforward classifier trained on concatenated weight-difference and timestep-score features, achieving up to 99.8% backdoor detection rate and interrupting malicious generation in real time without requiring access to poisoned samples.

## Results

STEBA achieves up to 15.07× temporal redundancy acceleration (TRA) and 5.55× spatial redundancy acceleration (SRA) in backdoor injection while maintaining or improving attack success rate (ASR) over full fine-tuning baselines. On Stable Diffusion v1.5, STEBA reaches 99.6% ASR with FID 22.06, outperforming both RickRolling (97.9% ASR, FID 38.72) and VillanDiffusion (99.4% ASR, FID 27.58). The defense component STEDF achieves a backdoor detection rate (BDR) of up to 100% on special-character triggers and 99.8% on phrase triggers, with false positive rates as low as 0%, substantially exceeding T2IShield's best BDR of 94.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RickRolling (Struppek et al., ICCV 2023), STEBA achieves substantially higher ASR (99.6% vs. 97.9% on SDv1.5) while reducing FID by 43% (22.06 vs. 38.72) and delivering 5× greater temporal acceleration (15.07× vs. 2.96×). Against VillanDiffusion (Chou et al., NeurIPS 2023), STEBA matches or slightly exceeds ASR (99.6% vs. 99.4% on SDv1.5) while achieving 15× temporal and 5.5× spatial acceleration compared to VillanDiffusion's 1.00× baseline (i.e., full fine-tuning with no redundancy exploitation). On the defense side, STEDF outperforms T2IShield (Wang et al., ECCV 2024) across all trigger types: BDR improves from 88.7–94.8% to 98.1–100%, and FPR drops from 6.1–11.2% to 0–2.9%. STEDF also introduces a Cut-off Success Rate (CSR) metric that T2IShield does not report, demonstrating its ability to interrupt malicious generation in real time. The one area where STEBA trades off is SSIM/LPIPS on SDv1.5 and SDv2.1 (slightly lower structural similarity), though on RVv4.0 it actually achieves the best SSIM (0.914) and LPIPS (0.114) among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backdoor attack literature on diffusion models (BadDiffusion, TrojDiff, VillanDiffusion, RickRolling) by challenging the implicit assumption that successful backdoor injection requires full-parameter fine-tuning across all diffusion timesteps. The enrichment phenomenon and the marginal effect in timesteps are new empirical observations that reframe the attack as a sparse, temporally-localized optimization problem rather than a dense, global one. On the defense side, STEDF departs from prior frameworks (Elijah, TERD, T2IShield) that rely on poisoned samples or post-hoc output analysis, instead monitoring intermediate hidden-state anisotropy in real time to interrupt generation before malicious content is produced.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that backdoor injection into diffusion models requires an order of magnitude fewer resources than previously assumed, the paper raises a practical security concern: the barrier to deploying backdoored models on public platforms is substantially lower than the field has appreciated. The architecture-agnostic nature of the enrichment phenomenon (validated on both UNet and DiT models) suggests these redundancies are intrinsic to diffusion-based backdoors broadly. A key open question is the detection gap: STEDF's performance degrades notably against STEBA specifically (Table 4), indicating that the very sparsity that makes the attack efficient also shifts the spatiotemporal signature that the defense relies on, leaving a tension between attack efficiency and detectability that remains unresolved.
