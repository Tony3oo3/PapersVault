## Problem

Existing chest X-ray VQA datasets are constrained by brief, simplistic answers, lack of localization annotations (bounding boxes), and minimal structured metadata (region/finding tags), while their relatively small sizes limit utility for pretraining vision-language models. This paper addresses all four gaps simultaneously by introducing a large-scale dataset with full-sentence, multi-part answers, precise bounding boxes, and rich structured tags.

## Input / Output

The model receives a frontal chest X-ray image (from MIMIC-CXR-JPG, 377K images) and a free-text question of one of four types (indication, study abnormality, region abnormality, or finding). The output is a hierarchical, multi-part answer where each part contains a full radiology-report-style sentence, associated bounding boxes in original pixel coordinates, and structured tags including finding classes (221), regions (257 localized + 53 non-localized), certainty, laterality, and modifiers (severity, texture, spread, temporal). Answers are organized into main answers, details, and related information with parent-child hierarchy inherited from the scene graph.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) Scene graph construction, where Llama 3.1 70B extracts observations, regions, and indications from 227K radiology reports via few-shot prompting, then maps extracted entities to reference definitions (based on SNOMED-CT, PadChest, Chest ImaGenome) using BioLORD sentence embeddings, and localizes findings via CXAS segmentation masks (158 structures) combined with Chest ImaGenome bounding boxes to yield 257 localized regions. (2) QA generation using 26 templates co-designed with radiologists across four question types, incorporating both template-filled answers and rewritten report sentences for diversity, with balanced sampling of mentioned and non-mentioned regions/findings. (3) LLM-as-a-judge quality assessment (Llama 3.1 8B) scoring entailment, relevance, completeness, and clarity to assign A++ through D grades, yielding 31.2M pre-training and 7.5M fine-tuning grade pairs. The baseline model uses the LLaVA architecture with Rad-DINO image encoder and Llama 3.2 3B language model, trained for one epoch on PT or FT grade samples, evaluated with the proposed RadStrucVQA metric that extends RadFact to structured answers with grounding and tag accuracy.

## Results

On the structured VQA task (evaluated on the 7.5M fine-tuning grade test set), the best model (trained on 1M PT + 1M FT samples) achieves 0.78 logical precision, 0.77 logical recall, 0.89 grounding precision, and 0.90 grounding recall. For tag prediction, it reaches 0.70/0.68 finding precision/recall and 0.69/0.68 region precision/recall, though positive-finding recall drops to 0.31. In scene graph evaluation, the proposed method outperforms Chest ImaGenome on finding tag MCC (0.71 vs 0.67 micro on MIMIC-CXR-JPG; 0.71 vs 0.59 on long-tail classes) and bounding box IoU@30 (0.51 vs 0.45 on MS-CXR; 0.45 vs 0.42 on REFLACX).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed model (PT 1M→FT 1M) substantially outperforms all three VLM baselines on every reported metric. Against MAIRA-2, it achieves 3.1× higher logical precision (0.78 vs 0.25) and 7.5× higher grounding recall (0.90 vs 0.12), though MAIRA-2 retains a moderate logical recall of 0.64, suggesting it captures relevant content but cannot adapt to question-specific queries. Qwen3-VL (4B) is the strongest baseline, reaching 0.63 logical precision and 0.51 grounding recall, but still trails the proposed model by 15 points in logical precision and 39 points in grounding recall. LLaVA-Med v1.5 performs poorly in zero-shot (0.08 logical recall) and fails to produce bounding boxes entirely. For scene graph quality, the proposed method beats Chest ImaGenome on finding tag MCC across all subsets (e.g., 0.71 vs 0.67 micro on MIMIC-CXR-JPG, 0.71 vs 0.59 on long-tail classes) and on bounding box IoU@30 (0.51 vs 0.45 on MS-CXR), with notably higher IoP (0.56 vs 0.48) indicating more precise, tighter boxes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CXR VQA dataset lineage (VQA-RAD, SLAKE, MIMIC-CXR-VQA, Medical-CXR-VQA, CheXinstruct) by replacing brief template answers with multi-part, full-sentence answers grounded in scene graphs, and by adding bounding boxes and structured tags (findings, regions, certainty) that no prior CXR VQA dataset provides. For scene graph construction, it departs from Chest ImaGenome's rule-based extraction and RadGraph's relation extraction in favor of LLM-based information extraction (Llama 3.1 70B) with semantic entity mapping via BioLORD, yielding a much finer taxonomy (257 regions, 221 findings vs. 29/53). The baseline model builds on the LLaVA architecture with Rad-DINO image encoding, and the evaluation metric (RadStrucVQA) generalizes the RadFact metric from grounded report generation to the VQA setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

At 42M QA-pairs with localization and structured tags, CXR-QBA is by an order of magnitude larger than prior CXR VQA datasets and is the first to support the structured VQA task (multi-part text + boxes + tags), making it a practical resource for pre-training and fine-tuning medical VLMs. The accompanying scene graphs (with 257 localized regions and 221 findings) are a versatile intermediate representation reusable for grounded report generation, pathology localization, and graph prediction. Key limitations include reliance on a single source dataset (MIMIC-CXR), potential LLM hallucinations in automatic extraction, and template-based question generation that limits linguistic diversity; the authors explicitly caution against using it as the sole source for clinical fine-tuning. Open questions include generalization to other modalities, longitudinal/differential questions, and whether the structured VQA formulation transfers beyond CXRs.
