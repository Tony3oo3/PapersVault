## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation methods suffer from objective entanglement, where competing perceptual goals—semantic consistency, temporal synchrony, aesthetic quality, and spatial accuracy—are conflated in a single reconstruction loss, preventing context-dependent trade-offs. Additionally, prior CoT-based approaches like ThinkSound use monolithic reasoning that conflates distinct analytical tasks, and no existing V2A framework incorporates human preference alignment via reinforcement learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a silent video (processed by VideoPrism at the frame level) and an optional text prompt as input, and produces 44.1 kHz stereo audio as output. The video is encoded by VideoPrism-Large (a video-specific ViT) rather than frame-wise CLIP, and the text conditioning is a concatenated multi-dimensional CoT string (Semantic → Temporal → Aesthetic → Spatial) generated by a fine-tuned VideoLLaMA2, which is encoded by T5-Gemma-Large. Inference runs in ~0.63 seconds for a 9-second audio clip on a single A800 GPU.

## Method

PrismAudio decomposes monolithic CoT planning into four specialized modules (Semantic, Temporal, Aesthetic, Spatial), each paired with a targeted reward function (MS-CLAP, Synchformer, Audiobox Aesthetics, StereoCRW) to enable multi-dimensional RL optimization that resolves objective entanglement. The audio foundation model is a diffusion transformer with flow matching, enhanced with VideoPrism for video understanding and T5-Gemma for structured CoT text encoding. For efficient RL post-training, Fast-GRPO introduces a hybrid ODE-SDE sampler that applies stochastic SDE steps only within a randomly placed window of width w ≪ T, reducing per-sample NFE from T to w while preserving the terminal data distribution. The GRPO objective uses group-relative advantages computed over a weighted sum of all four reward heads, with a clipped policy ratio derived in closed form from the Gaussian SDE transition kernel.

## Results

On the in-domain VGGSound test set, PrismAudio (518M params) achieves SOTA across all four perceptual dimensions: CLAP 0.47 (semantic), DeSync 0.41 (temporal), PQ 6.38 / CE 4.29 (aesthetic), and CRW 7.72 (spatial), surpassing the prior SOTA ThinkSound (1.3B) by 9.3% in CLAP, 25.5% in DeSync, and 42.6% in CRW. On the out-of-domain AudioCanvas benchmark, PrismAudio further extends its lead (CLAP 0.52, DeSync 0.36, CRW 12.87) while ThinkSound degrades severely (DeSync 0.80, CRW 22.82). PrismAudio also achieves the fastest inference at 0.63s per 9-second clip, roughly 2× faster than ThinkSound (1.07s) and 17× faster than HunyuanVideo-Foley (10.63s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrismAudio outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric on both VGGSound and AudioCanvas. Against the prior SOTA ThinkSound (1.3B), PrismAudio (518M) improves CLAP from 0.43→0.47, DeSync from 0.55→0.41, and CRW from 13.47→7.72, while also achieving higher MOS scores (4.21 vs. 4.05 for quality; 4.22 vs. 4.18 for consistency) with 41% fewer parameters and 41% faster inference. Against MMAudio (1.03B), the gains are even larger in spatial accuracy (CRW: 7.72 vs. not reported, but GCC 3.77 vs. not reported) and distribution fidelity (FD 1.08 vs. 2.17, KL 1.23 vs. 1.32). Against HunyuanVideo-Foley (5.31B), PrismAudio matches or exceeds on all perceptual metrics while being 17× faster (0.63s vs. 10.63s). On the out-of-domain AudioCanvas benchmark, the gap widens: ThinkSound's DeSync degrades to 0.80 and CRW to 22.82, while PrismAudio maintains DeSync 0.36 and CRW 12.87, confirming that the multi-dimensional CoT-RL framework provides robustness that simple pattern matching cannot. The ablation (PrismAudio w/o CoT-RL) already beats ThinkSound on DeSync and CRW, but the full CoT-RL pipeline adds a further 4.7% relative gain on MOS-Q and 2.7% on MOS-C, demonstrating the incremental value of the RL post-training stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ThinkSound's CoT-based V2A pipeline by decomposing its monolithic reasoning into four specialized modules (Semantic, Temporal, Aesthetic, Spatial) and, crucially, adding a reinforcement learning post-training stage that ThinkSound entirely lacks. The RL component builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and its flow-matching adaptation Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025c), but introduces Fast-GRPO—a hybrid ODE-SDE sampler that confines stochastic exploration to a small random window, reducing per-sample NFE from T to w. On the architecture side, it replaces ThinkSound's CLIP video encoder with VideoPrism and its T5 text encoder with T5-Gemma to better handle structured multi-dimensional CoT conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to bring multi-dimensional RL alignment to V2A generation, directly addressing the objective-entanglement problem where optimizing one perceptual axis degrades others—a failure mode confirmed by the single-reward ablations. The AudioCanvas benchmark (3,177 videos, 300 classes, 501 multi-event samples) fills a real gap in V2A evaluation, as prior datasets like VGGSound have known alignment issues and lack complex multi-event scenarios. Open questions include whether the four-way CoT decomposition generalizes to other multimodal generation tasks, whether the multi-reward weighting scheme (λ_k) can be learned automatically rather than hand-tuned, and whether the approach scales to longer-form or 3D spatial audio beyond stereo.
