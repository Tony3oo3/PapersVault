## Problem

In high-dimensional imbalanced classification, the minority class suffers disproportionately from overfitting, yet the mechanism behind this asymmetry remains unexplained by classical large-sample theory. No prior work provides a unified statistical characterization of how dimensionality, class imbalance, and signal strength jointly affect test accuracy, logit distributions, and calibration in the proportional regime (n/d → δ).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265252/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The theory operates on i.i.d. pairs (x_i, y_i) where x_i ∈ R^d is a high-dimensional feature vector (e.g., d=512 from ResNet-18 for images, d=768 from BERT for text, d=2000 for single-cell RNA-seq) and y_i ∈ {±1} is an imbalanced binary label with minority fraction π < 1/2. The key analytical outputs are the limiting empirical logit distribution (ELD) and testing logit distribution (TLD), the margin κ*, the cosine similarity ρ* between the learned direction and the true signal, and derived quantities including per-class test errors, balanced error, and miscalibration metrics (CalErr, MSE, ConfErr).

## Method

The paper develops a high-dimensional asymptotic theory for logistic regression and SVM under a two-component Gaussian mixture model in the proportional regime n/d → δ. The central result (Theorem 2.1) shows that for separable data, the ELD converges in W_2 to a rectified Gaussian max{κ*, ρ*‖μ‖₂ + G + Yβ₀*} while the TLD remains Gaussian, with the discrepancy governed by a variational problem (Eq. 5) over a free random variable ξ subject to E[ξ²] ≤ 1/δ. The proof technique rewrites the SVM as a min-max problem and applies Gordon's theorem to replace the random data matrix with independent Gaussian vectors. Building on this, the paper derives the optimal margin-rebalancing hyperparameter τ* that equalizes minority and majority errors (Proposition 3.1), establishes monotonicity of test errors in π, ‖μ‖₂, and δ, and extends the analysis to calibration metrics and the non-separable regime where overfitting manifests as proximal-operator shrinkage rather than truncation.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result (Theorem 2.1) shows that for separable 2-GMM data in the proportional regime (n/d→δ), the empirical logit distribution (ELD) converges to a rectified Gaussian max{κ*, ρ*‖μ‖₂ + G + Yβ₀*} while the testing logit distribution (TLD) remains Gaussian N(ρ*‖μ‖₂ + Yβ₀*, 1), with the discrepancy governed by a variational problem (Eq. 5). Under optimal margin rebalancing τ=τ_opt ≈ √(1/π), all test errors (Err₊, Err₋, Err_b) and miscalibration metrics (MSE, CalErr, ConfErr) decrease monotonically with the imbalance ratio π, signal strength ‖μ‖₂, and aspect ratio δ (Table 2). In the high-imbalance regime, three distinct phases emerge: high signal (a−c < b) requires no rebalancing, moderate signal (b < a−c < 2b) is rescued only by τ_d ≫ d^{a−b−c}, and low signal (a−c > 2b) is no better than random guessing for any τ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is primarily a theoretical paper without a standard method-vs-method benchmark, so direct numerical comparisons against competing classifiers are absent. The key empirical comparison is between the naive SVM (τ=1) and the margin-rebalanced SVM (τ=τ_opt): in the proportional regime, naive SVM drives minority error Err₊→1 and majority error Err₋→0 as π decreases, while optimal τ aligns both errors and reduces balanced error (Fig. 4). In the high-imbalance moderate-signal phase, naive τ_d≍1 yields Err₊=1−o(1) (complete minority failure), whereas τ_d≫d^{a−b−c} achieves Err₊=o(1) (Theorem 3.2). The paper also contrasts logistic regression and SVM (Table 3), noting they are asymptotically equivalent on separable data (via the implicit bias result of Soudry et al. 2018), but SVM is preferred for theory while logistic regression is preferred for large-scale real data. Relative to the closest prior work (Montanari & Zhou 2022 on projection pursuit), this paper extends the analysis to characterize how class imbalance specifically distorts the logit distribution and degrades calibration, filling a gap not addressed by earlier high-dimensional classification theory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the high-dimensional asymptotic analysis of linear classification (Dobriban & Wager, Sur & Candès, Montanari et al.) by introducing class imbalance as a first-class parameter and characterizing overfitting through the empirical logit distribution rather than test error alone. The closest prior work is Montanari & Zhou (2022) on projection pursuit in high dimensions, but that analysis does not address imbalance or calibration. The proof technique builds on Gordon's theorem to reduce the SVM/logistic regression problem to a variational program, and the margin-rebalancing framework connects to the practical literature on label-distribution-aware margin loss (Cao et al., 2019) and uneven-margin SVMs (Li et al., 2005), now providing the first theoretical derivation of the optimal rebalancing hyperparameter τ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified theoretical explanation for why minority classes are disproportionately affected by high-dimensional overfitting: a shared 'overfitting budget' (E[ξ²] ≤ 1/δ) causes asymmetric truncation of the logit distribution, with the minority class—being closer to the decision boundary—losing more probability mass. This yields a principled formula for the optimal margin-rebalancing parameter (τ* ≈ √(1/π)) and reveals that overfitting simultaneously degrades calibration, not just accuracy. Limitations include the restriction to linear classifiers on (near-)Gaussian features; the multiclass and non-isotropic extensions remain conjectural. Open questions include whether the truncation characterization extends to non-linear deep classifiers and whether the 'overfitting budget' perspective generalizes to other loss functions beyond logistic/hinge.
