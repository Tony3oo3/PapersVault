## Problem

Existing latent-predictive representation learning methods for RL are limited to single-task settings, one-step dynamics, or on-policy data, preventing them from capturing the long-term, policy-dependent dynamics needed for zero-shot optimization across multiple reward functions. No prior approach combines multi-step, policy-conditioned latent prediction with off-policy temporal-difference learning to train all components of a zero-shot RL agent end-to-end from offline, reward-free transitions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261241/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training consumes offline, reward-free transition tuples (s, a, s') from a mixture of policies, where s can be proprioceptive vectors or RGB images; no reward signal is used during pre-training. At test time, a small set of rewarded samples {(s, r)} is used solely to compute a task vector z_r via closed-form linear regression on the task encoder ψ, after which the corresponding zero-shot policy π_{z_r} is retrieved. The learned state encoder φ and task encoder ψ are also reusable for downstream offline or online fine-tuning, and the predictor T_φ serves as a successor-feature approximation for arbitrary reward functions in the span of ψ.

## Method

TD-JEPA introduces a policy-conditioned, multi-step temporal-difference latent-predictive loss that trains four components jointly: a state encoder φ, a task encoder ψ, a predictor T_φ mapping (φ(s), a, z) to the latent space of ψ, and a family of latent policies π_z. The key mechanism is a TD bootstrapping objective where T_φ(φ(s),a,z) is trained to match ψ(s') + γT_φ(φ(s'),a',z) for actions a' sampled from π_z, enabling off-policy estimation of long-term successor features without Monte-Carlo rollouts. A symmetric loss with roles of φ and ψ inverted trains the task encoder, and an orthonormality regularization prevents representation collapse. Theoretically, the paper proves that under proper initialization the covariance of learned representations is preserved (no collapse), the optimal predictor recovers an oblique projection of the successor measure M^{π_z}ψ onto the φ-space, and the policy evaluation error is bounded by the successor-measure approximation loss. At inference, zero-shot policy optimization reduces to selecting the policy π_{z_r} whose latent task vector best matches the projected reward, entirely in latent space.

## Results

TD-JEPA is evaluated on 65 tasks across 13 datasets (4 DMC locomotion/navigation domains + 9 OGBench navigation/manipulation domains), in both proprioceptive and pixel-based variants. On suite-aggregated performance, TD-JEPA is on par with or better than the best baseline in each of the four suites (DMC, DMC_RGB, OGBench, OGBench_RGB). In the challenging pixel-based setting, TD-JEPA achieves significantly higher success rates than contrastive baselines (e.g., ~92.2% avg on OGBench_RGB vs. ~30–42% for Laplacian/HILP/FB), and the explicit state-encoder protocol yields 1.3× and 2.4× higher pixel-based results than prior HILP and RLDP reports. A probability-of-improvement analysis (Fig. 2) confirms TD-JEPA is consistently among the top performers across all domains, with statistically significant advantages over FB and HILP in visual settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD-JEPA is compared against seven baselines in three groups: (i) contrastive successor-feature methods (Laplacian, HILP, FB), (ii) latent-predictive methods repurposed for zero-shot RL (BYOL*, BYOL-γ*, RLDP), and (iii) ICVF*. In proprioceptive DMC, TD-JEPA is only slightly better than FB and HILP, but in pixel-based DMC_RGB it is significantly superior to all contrastive methods (which score ~25–42% vs. TD-JEPA's ~785 normalized return). On OGBench (proprioceptive), TD-JEPA outperforms all baselines, with the closest competitor being RLDP (~79.8% vs. ~73.0% for TD-JEPA on the average, though TD-JEPA leads on most individual domains). On OGBench_RGB, BYOL-γ* is slightly better than TD-JEPA on the suite average, but TD-JEPA is significantly better on DMC_RGB and OGBench (proprioceptive), making it the more consistently strong method overall. The probability-of-improvement analysis (Fig. 2) shows TD-JEPA has statistically significant advantages over FB and HILP in all pixel-based domains, while BYOL-γ* is significantly worse than TD-JEPA in DMC_RGB and OGBench. Latent-predictive methods (BYOL*, BYOL-γ*, RLDP, TD-JEPA) are generally preferable to contrastive methods in pixel-based settings, confirming the benefit of self-predictive objectives for visual representation learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

TD-JEPA sits at the intersection of two research threads: latent-predictive (JEPA-style) representation learning and successor-feature-based zero-shot RL. It most directly extends the Forward-Backward algorithm (Touati & Ollivier, 2021), which learns a bilinear decomposition of policy-conditional successor measures, by introducing a shared state encoder and a policy-conditioned multi-step predictor trained via a novel off-policy TD loss. On the representation-learning side, it generalizes BYOL-γ (Lawson et al., 2025) from an unconditional, on-policy, Monte-Carlo objective to a policy-conditional, off-policy, temporal-difference one, and extends the theoretical non-collapse and gradient-matching analyses of Tang et al. (2023) from single-policy one-step prediction to multi-policy multi-step TD prediction. The key conceptual departure is that latent prediction is not an auxiliary loss but the core objective that simultaneously trains all components (state encoder, task encoder, predictor, and policies) needed for zero-shot policy distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TD-JEPA provides a principled, end-to-end recipe for zero-shot RL that is particularly strong in the pixel-based setting, which has been one of the hardest regimes for unsupervised RL. The 'gradient matching' theoretical framework (Theorem 5) is a useful general tool that subsumes and extends prior analyses of latent-predictive representations, and the non-collapse guarantee for the doubly latent-predictive TD dynamics fills a gap in the theory. However, the theoretical results rely on restrictive assumptions (symmetric transition kernels, uniform state distribution, identity covariances) that limit their direct applicability, and all empirical evaluation is in simulation. Open questions include whether the symmetry assumption can be relaxed in practice, how the method scales to large-scale real-robot datasets, and whether the separate state/task encoder design generalizes beyond the tested domains.
