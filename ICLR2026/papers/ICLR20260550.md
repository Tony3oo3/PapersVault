## Problem

Existing generative BEV map estimation methods rely on either two-stage VQ-VAE tokenization followed by transformer prediction, or encoder-decoder architectures with a separate generative decoder. The paper demonstrates through codebook utilization and Shannon entropy analysis that BEV maps are inherently sparse and low-entropy, making discrete representation learning unnecessary and even detrimental, yet no prior work exploits this to simplify the pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260550/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes multi-view surround-view RGB images (6 cameras at 256×705 for nuScenes, 7 cameras at 614×614 for Argoverse2) along with camera calibration parameters, and produces a 200×200 BEV semantic segmentation map at 0.5 m/pixel resolution covering a 100 m × 100 m area. The output is generated iteratively: starting from a fully masked map, the model unmask tokens over 3 autoregressive steps, predicting per-pixel binary class labels (6 classes on nuScenes, 3 on Argoverse2).

## Method

ARINBEV is a single-stage, decoder-only autoregressive model that eliminates both VQ-VAE tokenization and the encoder-decoder split. Instead of learned discrete tokens, it directly encodes ground-truth BEV map labels into embeddings via a lightweight class-encoding layer (learnable embedding table with sigmoid normalization and β=0.01 scaling). The decoder builds on BEVFormer/DETR with pre-normalized attention: BEV embeddings are compressed from 200×200 to 25×25 via bilinear downsampling plus a 3×3 convolution, processed with global self-attention and deformable cross-attention to image features, then decompressed back. A key design choice is entropy-guided mask scheduling: a fixed Gaussian prior (σ=0.5) combined with Halton-sequence sampling biases masking toward the central, high-information regions of the BEV map, applied with probability 0.5 alongside standard random arccos masking to prevent overfitting. Training uses binary focal loss for per-token classification, and inference requires only 3 iterative unmasking steps.

## Results

On nuScenes, ARINBEV achieves 64.3 mIoU, outperforming the previous best (VQ-Map, 62.2 mIoU) by 2.1 points across all six semantic categories. On Argoverse2, ARINBEV reaches 65.6 mIoU, surpassing DDP (63.5 mIoU) by 2.1 points. In terms of efficiency, ARINBEV uses 63.4M parameters (1.7× fewer than VQ-Map's 108.3M) and trains in 73 GPU-hours (1.8× faster than VQ-Map's 131h), while also requiring fewer MACs (215.8G) than the encoder-decoder DDP (614.1G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARINBEV outperforms all nine baselines on nuScenes mIoU. Against the closest competitor VQ-Map (62.2 mIoU), ARINBEV gains +2.1 mIoU while using 1.7× fewer parameters and training 1.8× faster. Compared to the encoder-decoder diffusion model DDP (59.4 mIoU), ARINBEV improves by +4.9 mIoU with 65% fewer MACs (215.8G vs. 614.1G). Against the two-stage MapPrior (56.7 mIoU, 719.1M params), ARINBEV achieves +7.6 mIoU with only 8.8% of the parameters. On Argoverse2, ARINBEV (65.6 mIoU) leads DDP (63.5) by +2.1 and VQ-Map (61.2) by +4.4; notably, MapPrior and VQ-Map degrade substantially on Argoverse2 due to their reliance on discrete representation learning with only three map classes, whereas ARINBEV remains robust without two-stage quantization. Traditional non-generative baselines (BEVFusion 56.6, MetaBEV 59.3, X-Align 58.0) trail ARINBEV by 5–8 mIoU, confirming the benefit of modeling inter-element dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARINBEV builds on the BEVFormer backbone for multi-view-to-BEV feature projection and the DETR-style transformer decoder, but departs fundamentally from the generative BEV map estimation lineage. Two-stage frameworks (VQ-Map, MapPrior) learn discrete tokens via VQ-VAE before autoregressive prediction, while encoder-decoder diffusion models (DDP, DiffBEV) add a separate generative decoder on top of a BEV encoder. This paper argues both are unnecessary: BEV maps are inherently sparse and low-entropy (unlike natural images), so a lightweight class-encoding layer can replace VQ-VAE tokenization, and a single decoder-only autoregressive model with entropy-guided masking can replace the encoder-decoder split.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that discrete representation learning is redundant for BEV map estimation simplifies the training pipeline from two stages to one, cutting parameters by 1.7× and training time by 1.8× while improving mIoU on both nuScenes and Argoverse2. This challenges the default assumption that autoregressive vision models require learned discrete tokens and suggests that for structured, semantically constrained outputs, direct label embedding suffices. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D occupancy prediction (where dense voxel grids may reintroduce the need for learned representations), whether discrete diffusion could further improve generative capacity, and how to mitigate sampling drift beyond 3 inference steps.
