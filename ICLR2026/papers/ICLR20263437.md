## Problem

Ensemble policy gradient methods like SAPG use multiple policies to diversify exploration in massively parallel environments, but it remains unclear whether greater inter-policy diversity always improves learning. This paper shows theoretically and empirically that excessive divergence between leader and follower policies degrades sample efficiency (via reduced effective sample size) and training stability (via increased PPO clipping bias), yet no prior method explicitly regulates this inter-policy distance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes robot state observations (joint positions/velocities, object orientation/angular velocity) and a one-dimensional policy identification vector that conditions shared MLP or LSTM-MLP networks to differentiate the leader from each of the M−1 followers. It produces Gaussian action distributions and value estimates for each policy, plus a discriminator output that classifies which policy generated a given state-action pair (used only as an intrinsic reward for followers, not the leader). All agents share the same network architecture but are distinguished solely by the identification vector.

## Method

CPO extends SAPG's leader-follower framework with two key mechanisms. First, each follower's policy update is formulated as a KL-constrained optimization relative to the leader (Eq. 9), solved via an AWAC-style closed-form solution that yields a forward-KL-regularized loss term added to the standard PPO follower objective; this keeps followers within a bounded neighborhood of the leader, suppressing IS ratio deviation and preserving effective sample size. Second, a DIAYN-inspired discriminator is trained to classify which policy produced each state-action pair, and the log-probability of correct classification serves as an adversarial intrinsic reward for followers, preventing them from overconcentrating in the same region. The theoretical contribution establishes three propositions linking KL divergence between policies to IS ratio deviation, effective sample size, and PPO clipping bias, providing the formal justification for the KL constraint.

## Results

On six dexterous manipulation tasks after 2×10¹⁰ environment steps, CPO achieves the highest performance on 4 of 6 tasks: ShadowHand (13762±414 vs. SAPG 12882±343), AllegroHand (14421±885 vs. SAPG 11989±817), AllegroKuka Reorientation (43.75±0.65 vs. SAPG 38.79±1.66), and Two-Arms Reorientation (35.30±2.77 vs. SAPG 5.11±3.41). CPO ties SAPG on Regrasping (37.44 vs. 37.20) and slightly trails on Throw (21.69 vs. 22.51). The paper reports that CPO reaches SAPG's final performance with approximately half the number of environment steps, and ablation studies show the KL constraint reduces mean IS ratio deviation from 0.889 (SAPG) to 0.187–0.403 (CPO) while increasing ESS rate from 0.022 to 0.76–0.94 on ShadowHand.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO, CPO dominates across all ten tasks, with particularly large gaps on dexterous manipulation (e.g., 13762 vs. 10661 on ShadowHand, 35.30 vs. 1.41 on Two-Arms Reorientation). Against DexPBT (PBT), CPO wins on most manipulation tasks but PBT converges faster on the simpler locomotion tasks (Humanoid, Anymal) and edges out CPO on Throw (19.08 vs. 21.69 is actually CPO winning; PBT wins on Throw at 19.08 vs. CPO 21.69 — correction: SAPG wins Throw). Against SAPG, the most direct competitor, CPO achieves clear wins on ShadowHand (+880), AllegroHand (+2432), Reorientation (+4.96), and Two-Arms Reorientation (+30.19), ties on Regrasping, and slightly trails on Throw (−0.82). The paper attributes SAPG's weakness to severe follower–leader policy misalignment (visible in KL divergence heatmaps), where some followers drift far from the leader and produce low-quality off-policy samples that reduce the leader's effective sample size and increase PPO clipping bias. CPO's KL constraint keeps IS ratios close to 1 (mean deviation 0.187–0.403 vs. SAPG's 0.608–0.889), directly improving ESS and training stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAPG leader-follower framework (Singla et al., 2024) by introducing a KL divergence constraint between leader and follower policies, drawing the constrained-update formulation from AWAC (Nair et al., 2020) and the adversarial diversity reward from DIAYN (Eysenbach et al., 2018). The key conceptual departure is a theoretical analysis (via Pinsker's inequality) showing that excessive inter-policy diversity in ensemble policy gradient methods degrades effective sample size and increases PPO clipping bias, directly contradicting the implicit assumption in prior ensemble methods that broader exploration is always beneficial. Unlike DexPBT, which discards data from non-selected policies, and SAPG, which aggregates all follower data without controlling divergence, CPO explicitly regulates the policy-space neighborhood in which followers operate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that unregulated diversity in ensemble RL actively harms the leader's learning (reducing ESS from ~0.94 to ~0.02 in the worst case) provides a principled explanation for why simply scaling parallel environments with a single policy or uncontrolled ensembles yields diminishing returns. The practical impact is significant for the dexterous manipulation community: CPO reaches SAPG's final performance with roughly half the environment steps and solves tasks (Two-Arms Reorientation) where SAPG fails entirely. A notable limitation is the fixed number of policies and environments per policy, which may not adapt to varying exploration needs across training stages; the ablation also reveals the adversarial reward contributes only marginally, suggesting the KL constraint alone may suffice for most tasks.
