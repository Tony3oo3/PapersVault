## Problem

Existing neural audio codecs operating below 12.5Hz suffer significant semantic and phonetic information loss, creating a large frame-rate gap relative to text (~4.5Hz) that hinders autoregressive language model integration. The paper identifies two root causes: insufficient decoupling of semantics from acoustics in fixed-rate downsampling, and the inability of a uniform frame rate to capture transient phonetic details in information-dense segments while efficiently compressing sparse regions like long vowels and silence.

## Input / Output

The codec takes a 16kHz speech waveform and a merging threshold τ (sampled uniformly from [0.7, 1.0] during training) as input. It produces a variable-length sequence of discrete semantic tokens (RVQ-1, 32768-entry FSQ codebook) and 24 layers of acoustic tokens (RVQ-rest, 4096 entries per layer), each accompanied by a frame-length attribute ℓ_k (max 8, stored in 3 bits) that records how many original 12.5Hz frames were merged. At inference, adjusting τ continuously controls the average output frame rate from 3Hz to 12.5Hz without retraining, and the frame-length attributes enable exact reconstruction of the fixed-rate sequence for decoding.

## Method

FlexiCodec employs a dual-stream encoder: a frozen SenseVoice-Small ASR model (230M params, CTC-trained) provides semantic features, while a 5-block CNN (strides [4,4,5,8,2]) provides acoustic features, both at 12.5Hz. A dynamic frame merging module computes cosine similarity between adjacent ASR feature frames and greedily merges contiguous segments where all adjacent similarities exceed τ, averaging both streams and recording merge lengths; a Transformer with local windowed attention (±8 frames) then refines the merged representations to reduce transition artifacts. The semantic stream is quantized via FSQ (5 dimensions × 8 levels) wrapped in ConvNeXt blocks, while the acoustic stream quantizes the residual (waveform feature minus ASR feature) with 24-layer RVQ using quantizer dropout. A symmetric frame unmerging module (100M-param Transformer) expands the dynamic-rate sequence back to 12.5Hz before a mirrored CNN decoder reconstructs the waveform. The entire system is trained end-to-end with multi-scale mel L1, GAN (MPD + MRSD), RVQ commitment, and L2 feature-alignment losses, with τ randomly sampled per training step to expose the model to the full 3–12.5Hz range.

## Results

At 6.25Hz average frame rate, FlexiCodec achieves a WER(RVQ-1) of 4.15%, close to the ground-truth 2.1% and substantially outperforming the best baseline (DualCodec at 31.5% WER at the same rate). At 12.5Hz, FlexiCodec attains WER(RVQ-1) of 2.76%, PESQ of 3.35, UTMOS of 4.22, and SIM of 0.85, surpassing Mimi, XY-Tokenizer, and DualCodec on acoustic quality while using a comparable ~1.2 kbps bitrate. In the downstream TTS task, FlexiCodec-TTS at 6.25Hz AR achieves WER 3.2%, NMOS 3.32, and QMOS 3.40 with a 7.3× speedup over CosyVoice's AR stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexiCodec decisively outperforms all baselines on semantic preservation (WER at RVQ-1): at 12.5Hz it achieves 2.76% WER versus 5.93% for DualCodec, 5.90% for EnCodec, and 5.56% for SpeechTokenizer; at 6.25Hz it reaches 4.15% versus 31.5% for retrained DualCodec and 4.32% for TaDiCodec. On acoustic quality at >1 kbps, FlexiCodec@12.5Hz (PESQ 3.35, UTMOS 4.22) surpasses Mimi (2.75/3.56), XY-Tokenizer (3.00/4.00), and DualCodec (3.29/4.18), trailing only DAC (3.77/3.62) which operates at 6 kbps—five times the bitrate. At ~0.8 kbps, FlexiCodec@8.3Hz leads all baselines on PESQ (3.03 vs. 2.86 for WavTokenizer, 2.77 for XCodec2) and UTMOS (4.21 vs. 4.08 for XCodec2). At ≤0.7 kbps, FlexiCodec@6.25Hz wins on WER(RVQ1:8) (2.53 vs. 4.19 for TAAE, 4.32 for TaDiCodec) and MCD (3.42 vs. 4.98 for TAAE, 9.75 for TaDiCodec), though TAAE (953M params) edges it on PESQ (2.84 vs. 2.76) and UTMOS (4.33 vs. 4.18). In the TTS downstream task, FlexiCodec-TTS at 6.25Hz AR matches or exceeds CosyVoice (WER 3.2% vs. 3.2%, NMOS 3.32 vs. 3.17) while delivering a 7.3× AR speedup, and outperforms VALL-E (WER 4.9%) and FireRedTTS (WER 6.8%) on both quality and efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexiCodec extends the dual-stream semantic/acoustic codec architecture introduced by DualCodec and Mimi, but makes two key departures: it replaces SSL features (HuBERT, WavLM, w2v-bert-2) with ASR features from SenseVoice-Small for the semantic stream, and it introduces a dynamic frame rate mechanism inspired by image token merging (ToMe, DynTok) and audio dynamic-rate codecs (CodecSlime, TFC, VARSTok). Unlike prior dynamic-rate codecs that operate at 40–80 Hz, FlexiCodec applies adaptive frame merging in the much more challenging ≤10 Hz regime, where information capacity per frame is severely limited. The quantization design combines FSQ for the semantic stream with multi-layer RVQ for the acoustic residual, following the split-quantizer paradigm of Mimi and DualCodec.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving high-quality, reconstructible audio at 6.25 Hz average frame rate with controllable rates down to 3 Hz, FlexiCodec directly addresses the sequence-length bottleneck in AR speech language models and enables 7×+ speedups in TTS inference. The finding that ASR features substantially outperform SSL features for semantic token preservation at very low frame rates challenges the prevailing assumption in the codec community and may redirect future semantic-distillation designs. Open questions include whether the dynamic-rate advantage grows on real-world data with longer silences, how well the approach generalizes to multilingual settings without fine-tuning, and whether the 0.5 phonemes-per-frame ratio holds across languages and speaking styles.
