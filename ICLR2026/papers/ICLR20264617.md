## Problem

Group-based RL methods like GRPO and RLOO allocate a fixed number of rollouts to every training prompt, implicitly treating all prompts as equally informative. Prompts whose success probability is near 0 or 1 produce zero-variance gradient contributions, wasting a significant fraction of the compute budget. No principled, adaptive mechanism existed to redistribute rollouts based on each prompt's expected gradient variance during online training.

## Input / Output

At each training iteration, VIP consumes 384-dimensional prompt embeddings (from all-MiniLM-L6-v2), the total rollout budget C, and the binary rewards from the most recent rollouts on the current mini-batch. It produces an integer per-prompt rollout allocation {n_q} bounded between L and U (with L ≥ 3), which is then used to generate rollouts for the policy update. The GP model is updated in-place via recursive Bayesian posterior updates, so no retraining of the predictor is needed across iterations.

## Method

VIP combines three components. First, it derives closed-form per-prompt gradient variance for Dr. GRPO (∝ (n−1)/n² · 4p(1−p)σ²_Z) and RLOO (∝ 1/(n−1) · 4p(1−p)σ²_Z), establishing that variance is maximized at p = 0.5 and decreases with more rollouts. Second, it places a Gaussian process with an RBF kernel over prompt embeddings to predict each prompt's success probability p̂_q at every iteration, using recursive Bayesian updates that incorporate new rollout rewards and exploit prompt similarity structure. Third, given the predicted variances, it solves a convex resource-allocation problem (minimize total gradient variance subject to a hard budget constraint) via KKT conditions and bisection on the Lagrange multiplier, followed by a greedy incentive-based rounding heuristic to obtain a feasible integer allocation. The total overhead is under 1.2% of training time.

## Results

On AIME24/25 with Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B under an 8×Q rollout budget, RLOO+VIP improves Pass@32 from 18.29 to 30.55 (+12.26) and Mean@32 from 3.43 to 9.68 (+6.25) over RLOO. Dr. GRPO+VIP lifts Pass@32 from 50.0 to 58.98 on AIME24 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B under the same budget. On the tool-augmented Bamboogle benchmark, RLOO+VIP raises Exact Match from 10.4 to 17.6 and F1@5 from 0.190 to 0.264. The total runtime overhead of VIP is only 1.12% (1.5B) and 0.83% (7B) of overall RL training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIP is integrated on top of two group-based RL baselines: RLOO (Ahmadian et al., 2024) and Dr. GRPO (Liu et al., 2025). Against RLOO, VIP delivers the largest gains, particularly for smaller models: on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B at 8×Q budget, Pass@32 on AIME24 jumps from 18.29 to 30.55 (+12.26 points) and Mean@32 from 3.43 to 9.68. Against Dr. GRPO, improvements are more moderate but consistent: Qwen2.5-Math-7B gains +8.98 Pass@32 on AIME24 (50.0→58.98) and +4.74 Maj@32 on AIME25 (15.0→19.74). On the tool-augmented Bamboogle benchmark, RLOO+VIP shows the largest absolute improvement (EM 10.4→17.6, F1@5 0.190→0.264), while Dr. GRPO+VIP also improves (EM 20→23.2, F1@5 0.282→0.333). The relative benefit of VIP is larger for weaker backbones (1.5B, 3B) than for the 7B model, suggesting variance-aware allocation is most impactful when the rollout budget is most underutilized.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the group-based policy optimization family (GRPO, Dr. GRPO, RLOO) for RLVR training of LLMs, providing the first rigorous per-prompt gradient variance analysis for these algorithms. It departs from prior adaptive-strategy work (difficulty-based filtering, signal-to-noise heuristics, competence-difficulty alignment) by replacing ad-hoc heuristics with a principled convex optimization formulation grounded in the derived variance expressions. The Gaussian process component for tracking non-stationary success probabilities during training is a novel predictive layer not present in prior allocation methods, which typically rely on static difficulty scores or simple moving averages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sampling efficiency remains a primary bottleneck in RLVR training, where generating 16+ rollouts per prompt dominates compute cost; VIP's ~1% overhead while delivering consistent 5–12 point gains across model sizes and tasks makes it a practical drop-in improvement for existing RLVR pipelines. The variance-minimization framing provides a theoretically grounded alternative to heuristic allocation, and the GP-based prediction handles the non-stationarity of success probabilities that simpler estimators miss. Open questions include whether the approach extends to noisy or non-verifiable rewards (RLHF), how it scales to much larger model ensembles, and whether the equal-variance assumption on projected gradients holds in more diverse settings.
