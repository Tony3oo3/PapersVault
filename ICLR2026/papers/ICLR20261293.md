## Problem

Existing selective classification methods lack a unified, principled framework for designing selector functions, and most evaluations assume i.i.d. test data, leaving the covariate shift setting—where input appearance changes but labels stay fixed—largely unexplored. This paper asks whether the classical Neyman-Pearson lemma can provide a general optimality criterion for selector design that naturally accommodates distribution shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261293/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is entirely post-hoc: it takes a frozen pretrained classifier (CLIP, EVA, ResNet-50, or DistilBERT) and its penultimate/final-layer feature representations, plus the training set partitioned into correctly and incorrectly classified subsets. It produces a real-valued selector score per input that is thresholded to yield either a class prediction or an abstain decision. No retraining or architectural modification is required; the only one-time cost is computing and caching training features for the distance-based components.

## Method

The paper reframes selective classification as a binary hypothesis test (correct vs. incorrect prediction) and invokes the Neyman-Pearson lemma to show the optimal selector is a monotonic transformation of the likelihood ratio p_c(x)/p_w(x). Building on this, it proposes two new distance-based scores—Δ-MDS and Δ-KNN—that estimate separate per-class Gaussian statistics (or k-NN density estimates) for correctly and incorrectly classified training features, then score a test point by the difference in its distance to each set. The authors further prove that linear combinations of any two NP-optimal scores remain NP-optimal under a tilted-product density assumption, motivating hybrid selectors like Δ-KNN-RLog. The framework unifies existing scores (MSP, RLog, MDS, KNN) as implicit approximations to the likelihood ratio and naturally handles covariate shift without requiring separate ID/OOD modeling.

## Results

On DFN CLIP across 7 ImageNet covariate-shifted datasets, the best proposed method Δ-KNN-RLog achieves an average AURC of 5.01 and NAURC of 0.163, outperforming the strongest baseline RLog (AURC 5.67, NAURC 0.200) by ~12% in AURC and ~18% in NAURC. On the supervised EVA model, Δ-MDS-RLog achieves the best average AURC of 3.86 and NAURC of 0.157 on full 1K-class datasets, beating RLog (4.11 / 0.172). The NP-informed distance variants (Δ-MDS, Δ-KNN) reduce average AURC by roughly 50% compared to their unmodified counterparts (MDS, KNN), confirming that the likelihood-ratio framing yields substantial practical gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven baselines: MSP, MaxLogit, Energy, MDS, KNN, RLog, and SIRC. The proposed Δ-KNN-RLog and Δ-MDS-RLog combinations outperform all seven baselines on both AURC and NAURC across every dataset in the main CLIP and EVA tables. RLog is the strongest standalone baseline (AURC 5.67 avg on CLIP), but the linear combinations with distance scores improve it by 12–18%. The NP-informed distance variants (Δ-MDS, Δ-KNN) roughly halve the AURC of their unmodified counterparts (MDS: 11.3→6.50; KNN: 9.83→5.89 on CLIP Avg(all)), demonstrating that separating correct vs. incorrect training features is the key algorithmic gain. Energy and SIRC perform worst overall (AURC >14 on CLIP), while MSP and MaxLogit are mid-tier. On the supervised EVA model, the ranking shifts slightly: Δ-MDS-RLog edges out Δ-KNN-RLog, consistent with the Gaussian assumptions underlying MDS being better satisfied by softmax-trained classifiers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes selective classification through the Neyman-Pearson lemma, showing that the optimal selector is a likelihood ratio test between correctly and incorrectly classified samples. It unifies existing post-hoc selectors (MSP, RLog, MDS, KNN) as implicit approximations to this optimal test, then derives two new distance-based scores (Δ-MDS, Δ-KNN) that explicitly model separate feature distributions for correct and incorrect predictions, and a linear combination strategy whose NP-optimality is formally established. The work departs from prior SCOD methods (Xia & Bouganis 2022; Narasimhan et al. 2024) by avoiding the ID/OOD split in favor of the general (p_c, p_w) pair, and from Liang et al. (2024) by extending the evaluation to VLMs and providing formal optimality guarantees rather than empirical score design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NP framework provides the first general, principled criterion for designing selector functions, moving the field beyond ad-hoc heuristic confidence scores. The covariate shift focus is timely for VLMs where large, adjustable label sets make most practical deployment shifts covariate in nature, yet this setting has been largely neglected in selective classification literature. Open questions include whether the framework extends to generative models (LLMs), segmentation, and time series, and whether the Gaussian assumption in Δ-MDS or the asymptotic conditions in Δ-KNN can be relaxed for finite-sample guarantees. The requirement for a one-time labeled feature computation step, while amortizable, remains a practical constraint in fully unsupervised or streaming settings.
