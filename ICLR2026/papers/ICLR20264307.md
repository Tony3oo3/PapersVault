## Problem

Vision-language models predominantly confine their reasoning to the text modality, failing to dynamically re-examine visual evidence during chain-of-thought. While OpenAI's o3 demonstrated 'thinking with images,' its mechanism remains undisclosed, and prior open-source approaches rely on pre-defined workflows or external specialized models that suffer from suboptimal coordination and limited generalizability. The key unresolved question is how to natively incentivize a VLM to interleave visual perception with textual reasoning via end-to-end RL, without cold-start SFT data or separate tool models.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single RGB image (potentially high-resolution at 2K–8K) and a free-form text question. During inference, it produces an interleaved multimodal CoT trajectory: after each textual reasoning step, the model either emits a final answer or generates bounding-box coordinates (bbox_2d as [x1, y1, x2, y2]) that trigger a crop operation, whose output image is appended to the context for subsequent reasoning. This zoom-in loop can repeat up to 6 times before the model must commit to an answer, enabling fine-grained inspection of small or ambiguous regions.

## Method

DeepEyes encapsulates the base model's (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) native visual grounding capability as an internal image_zoom_in_tool, forming an agentic MDP where the policy decides at each step whether to crop a region or answer directly. Training uses GRPO with a composite reward: accuracy on the final answer, a format penalty, and a conditional tool bonus granted only when the answer is correct AND at least one active perception step was triggered—this conditioning prevents the model from either ignoring the tool or over-using it without purpose. To bootstrap effective active perception without SFT cold-start, a multi-stage data curation pipeline applies a perception-utility filter that retains only samples solvable via grounding with ground-truth regions, combined with difficulty filtering and open-ended question standardization. The resulting iMCoT capability emerges natively through RL, evolving through three observable stages (exploration → high-frequency engagement → efficient selective utilization) and producing diverse human-like reasoning patterns including visual search, comparison, confirmation, and hallucination mitigation.

## Results

On the high-resolution V* benchmark, DeepEyes (7B) achieves 90.1% overall accuracy, a +18.9% gain over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (71.2%) and surpassing the 32B variant (87.9%). On HR-Bench-4K and HR-Bench-8K, DeepEyes improves overall scores by +6.3% (75.1 vs. 68.8) and +7.3% (72.6 vs. 65.3) respectively. On MME-RealWorld-Lite, the 7B model reaches 53.2 overall, outperforming both Qwen2.5-VL 7B (42.3) and 32B (45.6). On grounding (refCOCO 89.8, refCOCO+ 83.6, refCOCOg 86.7) and hallucination (POPE overall 87.7), DeepEyes also shows consistent gains over the 7B baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepEyes (7B) substantially outperforms all open-source 7B baselines on V*: it beats Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +18.9 points (90.1 vs. 71.2), LLaVA-OneVision by +14.7 (90.1 vs. 75.4), SEAL by +14.7 (90.1 vs. 75.4), DyFo by +8.9 (90.1 vs. 81.2), and Pixel-Reasoner by +9.5 (90.1 vs. 80.6). It also surpasses the larger Qwen2.5-VL-32B on V* (90.1 vs. 87.9) and on both HR-Bench splits (75.1 vs. 73.9 on 4K; 72.6 vs. 70.4 on 8K). The only method DeepEyes does not beat is the proprietary o3 model (95.7 on V*), and it narrowly trails the workflow-based ZoomEye by 0.5 points on V* overall (90.1 vs. 90.6), though it leads ZoomEye on HR-Bench-4K (75.1 vs. 69.6) and HR-Bench-8K (72.6 vs. 69.3). Against GPT-4o, DeepEyes gains +24.1 points on V* (90.1 vs. 66.0) and +17.1 on HR-Bench-8K (72.6 vs. 55.5). On MME-RealWorld-Lite, DeepEyes (53.2) outperforms all listed baselines including Qwen2.5-VL-32B (45.6) and Pixel-Reasoner (49.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen2.5-VL's native visual grounding capability and the GRPO reinforcement learning framework, but departs sharply from prior multimodal CoT methods that rely on predefined workflows (SEAL, DyFo, ZoomEye) or external specialized models. While OpenAI's o3 demonstrated 'thinking with images' as a milestone, its mechanism remained undisclosed; DeepEyes provides an open, end-to-end RL formulation where the model autonomously decides when to invoke image cropping as an internal tool, eliminating the need for SFT cold-start data. The key conceptual departure is treating the model's own grounding ability as an intrinsic agentic function rather than an external API, enabling joint optimization of textual reasoning and visual perception in a single policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 'thinking with images' can emerge natively through RL without curated reasoning trajectories, offering a simpler and more generalizable training recipe than workflow-based alternatives. The observed three-stage evolution (exploration → high-frequency engagement → efficient exploitation) provides a useful lens for understanding how agentic visual reasoning matures, and the hallucination-mitigation mechanism (re-grounding via zoom-in to override language priors) has broader implications for VLM reliability. However, the approach is currently limited to a single crop tool, a single base model family (Qwen2.5-VL), and still trails o3 by ~5.6 points on V*, leaving open whether the paradigm scales to richer toolsets, larger models, or more diverse visual tasks without the grounding drift and reasoning limitations the authors acknowledge.
