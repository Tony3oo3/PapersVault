## Problem

Existing Bayesian optimization methods have not addressed tensor-valued outputs, where the system response is a multi-mode tensor with intrinsic structural correlations across modes. Naive vectorization ignores mode-wise dependencies, and existing tensor-output GPs rely on fully separable (Kronecker-product) kernels that assume cross-mode correlations are input-independent. The paper further introduces a more practical combinatorial bandit BO (CBBO) setting where only a subset of tensor entries contributes to the objective, requiring joint selection of both the input and the output subset.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous input vector x ∈ X ⊂ R^d and evaluates a black-box tensor-valued function f(x) ∈ R^{t1×…×tm} with T = ∏tl total entries. For the CBBO variant, only k < T entries are observed per round (partial observation), and the method must simultaneously select the input x and a super-arm λ ∈ {0,1}^T with exactly k ones. The scalarized objective is defined via a bounded linear operator Lf (or Hf for CBBO) mapping the tensor (or partial tensor) to a scalar, and the output is the optimal input x* and, for CBBO, the optimal super-arm λ*.

## Method

The core surrogate is a tensor-output Gaussian process (TOGP) with two kernel classes: a non-separable LMC-based kernel K(x,x') = Σ_l Σ_j vec(A_l)vec(A_l)^T k_lj(x,x') that captures input-dependent cross-mode correlations, and a separable kernel K(x,x') = vec(A)vec(A)^T k(x,x') as a special case. For TOBO, a UCB acquisition function α_UCB(x) = Lf μ̂_n(x) + β_n ‖K̂_n(x,x)‖^{1/2} selects the next query, with a sublinear regret bound O(√(Nγ_N)β_N) established. For TOCBBO, the PTOGP handles partially observed outputs, and a CMAB-UCB2 criterion decouples the joint optimization into two sequential steps: (1) UCB-based input selection fixing the incumbent best super-arm, and (2) CMAB-UCB-based super-arm selection over the combinatorial space. Low-rank tensor decompositions (CP for m≤3, TT for m>3) reduce core-tensor parameter complexity, and a Nyström approximation scales the block covariance inversion to O(nTn_ℓ² + n_ℓ³).

## Results

On synthetic tensor-output functions, TOGP achieves the lowest NLL and MAE across all three settings (e.g., NLL = 503.0, MAE = 0.1571 in Setting 1), outperforming sMTGP (NLL = 749.4), MLGP (NLL = 707937.1), and MVGP (NLL = 11152.2). For optimization, TOBO achieves MSE_x = 0.0000 and MAE_y = 0.0008 in Setting 1, while TOCBBO achieves MSE_x = 0.0023, MAE_y = 0.0172, and Acc = 1.00 in Setting 1, with perfect accuracy (Acc = 1.00) in all three CBBO settings. On the real-world REEN dataset, TOGP again achieves the best NLL and MAE, and TOCBBO identifies the optimal input in fewer rounds than all baselines on CHEM, MAT, and PRINT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three GP surrogates applied with UCB or random sampling: sMTGP (Kia et al., 2018), MLGP (Yu et al., 2018), and MVGP (Chen et al., 2020). TOCBBO dominates all baselines in Settings 1 and 3: in Setting 1, it achieves MSE_x = 0.0023 vs. 0.1832 (sMTGP-UCB), 0.0667 (MLGP-UCB), and 0.0032 (MVGP-UCB), with perfect Acc = 1.00 versus 0.67, 0.33, and 1.00 respectively. In Setting 3, TOCBBO achieves MSE_x = 0.0021 and Acc = 1.00, while the next-best MVGP-UCB reaches only MSE_x = 0.0151 and Acc = 0.71. MLGP is the weakest baseline overall, with its fitted covariance matrix becoming singular under the experimental configuration, leading to the worst NLL (707937.1 in Setting 1) and highest MAE_y values. sMTGP is the strongest baseline, benefiting from its mode-wise tensor structure, but still lags behind TOCBBO by an order of magnitude in MSE_x for Setting 1. The ablation TOGP-RS (random sampling with the same TOGP surrogate) confirms that the UCB/CMAB-UCB2 acquisition strategy is critical: replacing it with random sampling degrades TOGP's CBBO performance from Acc = 1.00 to 0.67 in Setting 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the linear model of coregionalization (LMC) framework from Fricker et al. (2013) and Li & Zhou (2016) from vector-valued to tensor-valued outputs, constructing a tensor-output GP (TOGP) with non-separable kernels that capture input-dependent cross-mode correlations. It departs from existing tensor-output GPs (Belyaev et al., 2015; Kia et al., 2018; Zhe et al., 2019) that rely on fully separable Kronecker-product covariance structures, arguing this assumption is too restrictive for complex systems. The UCB acquisition and regret analysis follow the GP-UCB paradigm of Srinivas et al. (2009), while the CBBO formulation generalizes bandit Bayesian optimization (Nguyen et al., 2020; Ru et al., 2020) from independent categorical choices to joint combinatorial selection of correlated tensor entries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first BO framework with theoretical regret guarantees for tensor-valued outputs, and the CBBO formulation addresses a practically important setting where only a subset of tensor entries contributes to the objective. The non-separable kernel design is a meaningful departure from the separable assumption that dominates existing tensor GPs, though the empirical validation is limited to small tensors (at most 4×5×2) and four real-world datasets. Open questions include scalability to high-order or large tensors (the O(n³T³) complexity is prohibitive), whether the regret bounds are tight, and how the framework extends to more structured tensor geometries such as spatiotemporal data. The paper also leaves open the exploration of alternative acquisition functions (EI, PI, entropy search) within the TOGP/PTOGP surrogate.
