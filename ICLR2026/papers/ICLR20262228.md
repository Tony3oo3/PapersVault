## Problem

Existing 3D LLMs lack a unified representation that jointly captures semantic and geometric information, forcing them to rely on external 3D detectors or decoders for object grounding and preventing seamless integration of grounding with spatial reasoning. Additionally, no high-quality dataset exists that embeds 3D visual grounding as an explicit intermediate step within spatial reasoning chains, leaving a gap between object localization and spatial problem-solving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262228/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of N RGB images (uniformly sampled, typically 32) of a 3D scene along with depth maps and camera intrinsics/extrinsics (which can be estimated from images alone via VGGT-SLAM + MoGe-2), plus a natural-language spatial reasoning query. Output is a fully autoregressive text sequence: within a <think> block the model first lists 3D bounding boxes as coordinate tuples (two opposite corners in world-frame meters) for all relevant objects, then performs step-by-step spatial reasoning, and finally emits a concise answer in an <answer> tag. No external grounding module or proposal selection is involved at inference time.

## Method

GS-Reasoner builds a semantic-geometric hybrid 3D scene representation on image patches: SigLIP extracts per-patch semantic features from RGB frames, while Sonata (a PTv3-based point cloud encoder) processes the aggregated back-projected point cloud to produce per-patch geometric features. The key architectural contribution is a dual-path pooling module that resolves two misalignment issues: (1) a lightweight cross-attention network uses each patch's semantic feature as query over its K geometric features to produce a semantic-aligned geometric descriptor, and (2) the 3D point at the patch's center pixel is sampled for positional encoding and used to interpolate geometric features, yielding a position-aligned descriptor. These two paths are concatenated, projected, and fused with the semantic feature and sinusoidal 3D positional encoding to form the final patch-level hybrid token, which does not increase the input token count. For training, the authors introduce the GCoT dataset (156k QA pairs) where GPT-4o generates chain-of-thought reasoning paths grounded in 3D bounding boxes and bird's-eye-view scene maps, explicitly instructing the model to ground objects before reasoning. The model (LLaVA-Video 7B / Qwen2-7B backbone) is first pretrained on 3D grounding datasets (ScanRefer, Multi3DRef, SR3D, NR3D) and then finetuned end-to-end on GCoT plus additional 3D VQA and captioning data.

## Results

On VSI-Bench (spatial reasoning), GS-Reasoner achieves a state-of-the-art average score of 64.7 with predicted depth (and 70.1 with ground-truth depth), surpassing VLM-3R-7B (60.9), Spatial-MLLM-4B (48.4), and all proprietary models including Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4) and GPT-4o (34.0). On 3D visual grounding, GS-Reasoner achieves 60.8 Acc@25 on ScanRefer and 61.7 F1@25 on Multi3DRef (best among all methods), matching or exceeding 3D LLMs that rely on mesh proposals or external grounding modules while using only autoregressive prediction. On Scan2Cap dense captioning, GS-Reasoner sets a new SOTA with 101.0 CIDEr, significantly outperforming the previous best ROSS3D (81.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-Reasoner outperforms the strongest specialized spatial reasoning baseline VLM-3R-7B by 3.8 points in average VSI-Bench score (64.7 vs. 60.9), with particularly large gains on Absolute Distance (+12.5), Relative Direction (+8.4), and Route Planning (+4.5). Against proprietary models, GS-Reasoner surpasses Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4) and GPT-4o (34.0) by 19.3 and 30.7 points respectively, demonstrating that explicit 3D geometric encoding far exceeds what 2D-only VLMs can achieve. On 3D visual grounding (Table 1), GS-Reasoner (60.8 Acc@25 on ScanRefer) is competitive with the best proposal-based 3D LLM ROSS3D (61.1) and the expert model UniVLG (63.5), while dramatically outperforming the prior autoregressive 3D LLM 3D-LLM (30.3) by over 30 points. On Multi3DRef, GS-Reasoner achieves the best F1@25 (61.7) among all methods, surpassing even proposal-based approaches. On Scan2Cap, GS-Reasoner leads all baselines by a wide margin (101.0 CIDEr vs. ROSS3D's 81.3), though it does not achieve SOTA on ScanQA and SQA3D, which the authors attribute to ambiguous questions and answer-distribution bias in those datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GS-Reasoner extends the video-based 3D LLM line (Video-3D LLM, LLaVA-3D) that encodes 3D positional cues into vision-foundation-model patch features, by additionally injecting geometric features from a point cloud encoder (Sonata/PTv3) through a dual-path pooling mechanism that explicitly addresses semantic-geometric and position-geometric misalignment. It departs from prior 3D LLMs that rely on external grounding modules (Grounded 3D-LLM, ReGround3D) or mesh-based proposals (Chat-Scene, ROSS3D) by performing autoregressive 3D bounding box prediction entirely within the LLM. The GCoT dataset further extends spatial reasoning benchmarks (VSI-Bench, VLM-3R) by embedding 3D grounding as an explicit intermediate reasoning step, a formulation absent from prior 3D VQA datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a self-contained 7B video LLM can match or exceed grounding performance of systems requiring external detectors or mesh proposals, and that structuring spatial reasoning with grounded CoT yields substantial gains (+13.8 avg on VSI-Bench tasks with CoT). This opens a path toward unified, interpretable 3D scene understanding without modular pipelines. Limitations include continued underperformance on precise localization for simple descriptions (SR3D, NR3D) and on ambiguous VQA (ScanQA, SQA3D), and the reliance on estimated depth/pose in the zero-sensor setting introduces a ~5-point accuracy gap versus ground-truth geometry. The authors note potential extension to vision-language-action models for robotics as future work.
