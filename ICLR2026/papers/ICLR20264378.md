## Problem

Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) quantifies uncertainty in a single deterministic forward pass via Dirichlet distributions, making it efficient but vulnerable to overconfident predictions under adversarial perturbations and out-of-distribution inputs. Existing EDL extensions either modify the training process (expensive, less flexible) or, in the case of post-hoc methods like Smoothed EDL, still exhibit significant overconfidence against stronger attacks. The paper addresses the need for a lightweight, post-hoc defence that preserves EDL's single-pass efficiency while robustly detecting both OOD and adversarial inputs without retraining.

## Input / Output

C-EDL takes a single image (28×28 for MNIST-family, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/SVHN, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for Flowers/Deep Weeds) and internally generates T label-preserving metamorphic transformations of it, passing each through the same pretrained EDL classifier. The output is a conflict-scaled Dirichlet evidence vector from which the class label, per-class belief mass, total uncertainty mass u = K/S̃, and expected class probabilities are derived; the key structural difference from vanilla EDL is that the evidence magnitude is multiplicatively reduced by exp(−δC) while the relative class proportions (distribution shape) remain unchanged.

## Method

C-EDL is a post-hoc calibration layer over any pretrained EDL model. For each input, it applies T label-preserving metamorphic transformations, collects the resulting T Dirichlet vectors, and computes two conflict measures: intra-class variability (coefficient of variation of α_k across transformations, averaged over classes) and inter-class contradiction (a bounded, symmetric penalty that fires when two classes are simultaneously well-supported). These are combined via an inclusion-exclusion formula with a quadratic asymmetry penalty λ(C_inter − C_intra)², yielding a total conflict score C ∈ (0,1] proven monotonically non-decreasing in both components for λ ≤ 1/2. The aggregated Dirichlet parameters are then scaled by exp(−δC), reducing total Dirichlet strength S̃ and thereby amplifying the uncertainty mass u = K/S̃ proportionally to detected disagreement. This mechanism preserves in-distribution accuracy (low conflict → minimal scaling) while boosting uncertainty on OOD or adversarial inputs (high conflict → strong evidence reduction), all without any retraining.

## Results

C-EDL (Meta) maintains near-ceiling ID accuracy (95–99%) across all datasets while dramatically reducing adversarial coverage: from 52.21%±9.49 (EDL) to 15.51%±6.09 on MNIST→FashionMNIST, and from 20.00%±6.89 to 1.25%±0.56 on CIFAR-10→SVHN. OOD coverage is reduced by up to ~55% (e.g., 2.52%→2.00% on MNIST→FMNIST; 10.91%→4.69% on CIFAR-10→SVHN) with only a marginal drop in ID coverage (~2–3%). Under L2PGD (ε=1.0), C-EDL (Meta) achieves the lowest adversarial coverage across all three attack types (L2PGD, FGSM, Salt-and-Pepper), reaching as low as 0.14%±0.00 for Salt-and-Pepper noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-EDL (Meta) outperforms all 11 baselines on adversarial coverage across every dataset pair. Against the primary EDL baseline, C-EDL reduces adversarial coverage by ~37 percentage points on MNIST→FMNIST (52.21%→15.51%) and by ~19 points on CIFAR-10→SVHN (20.00%→1.25%). Smoothed EDL (S-EDL), the closest post-hoc competitor designed for adversarial robustness, still trails C-EDL substantially (48.80% vs 15.51% on MNIST→FMNIST Adv). Among EDL extensions (I-EDL, H-EDL, R-EDL, DA-EDL), none close the gap: the best (R-EDL) still yields 49.05% adversarial coverage vs C-EDL's 15.51%. In the broader UQ comparison (Table 4), C-EDL (Meta) achieves 1.53% OOD coverage and 17.68% adversarial coverage, beating MC-Dropout (12.82%/27.79%), ABNN (18.23%/24.35%), and EMM (31.87%/22.18%). Against RED (Pandey & Yu, 2023), C-EDL achieves lower adversarial coverage (15.51% vs 19.05% on MNIST→FMNIST; 1.25% vs 4.39% on CIFAR-10→SVHN) while maintaining comparable ID accuracy. The metamorphic variant (C-EDL Meta) consistently outperforms the MC-Dropout variant (C-EDL MC), confirming that structured label-preserving transformations are more effective than stochastic dropout for eliciting representational conflict.

## Relation to Prior Work

C-EDL builds directly on Evidential Deep Learning (Sensoy et al., 2018), which models class probabilities via Dirichlet distributions in a single forward pass, and departs from subsequent EDL extensions (I-EDL, H-EDL, R-EDL, DA-EDL) that modify the training objective by operating entirely post-hoc on a frozen pretrained model. Conceptually, it draws on the Dempster-Shafer principle that aggregating multiple evidence sources yields more reliable beliefs, and repurposes test-time augmentation (Ayhan & Berens, 2018) not for direct uncertainty estimation but as a stability probe: label-preserving metamorphic transformations generate diverse views whose evidential disagreement is quantified via intra-class variability and inter-class contradiction. This positions C-EDL as orthogonal to Smoothed EDL (Kopetzki et al., 2021), which regularises predictions against perturbations during training, and to multi-view evidential learning (Xu et al., 2024), which assumes distinct modalities and trains separate models per view.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The post-hoc, retraining-free design makes C-EDL immediately deployable on existing EDL classifiers in resource-constrained or safety-critical settings, and the up-to-90% reduction in adversarial coverage (vs. EDL) with only marginal ID coverage loss is practically significant for high-stakes applications. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) bounding and ensuring monotonicity of the conflict measure adds rigor uncommon in applied UQ work. However, the approach requires T additional forward passes per input, limiting its applicability in latency-critical pipelines, and the evaluation is confined to image classification; the authors note future work on detection tasks and reducing augmentation requirements. Whether the conflict-aware mechanism generalises to non-image modalities or to EDL variants with fundamentally different evidence structures remains open.
