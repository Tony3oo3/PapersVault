## Problem

Existing sparse control methods for dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting allocate control points purely by geometric criteria (FPS or voxel centers), creating a fundamental mismatch: static backgrounds dominate spatial extent and receive excessive control density, while dynamic objects occupy smaller regions but require detailed motion modeling. This leads to static redundancy and dynamic insufficiency, degrading reconstruction quality in motion-dominant areas. Additionally, MLP-based deformation fields used for temporal parameterization suffer from entangled optimization and instability under sparse supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video sequence (960×640 for Hyper-NeRF, 1352×1014 for N3DV) along with calibrated camera poses, and leverages off-the-shelf vision foundation models to extract per-patch semantic tokens (VGGT), temporally consistent depth maps (DepthCrafter), foreground masks (Track-Anything), and dense 2D point trajectories (TAPIR). It produces a compact dynamic 3DGS representation where a sparse set of nodes (Nn ≪ Ng) each carry an SE(3) trajectory parameterized by cubic Hermite splines and an RBF influence radius, enabling real-time novel-view rendering at ~70 FPS on a V100 GPU with ~25 MB storage.

## Method

The core contribution is a motion-adaptive node initialization (MANI) pipeline that back-projects image patches into 3D space while retaining VGGT semantic tokens as descriptors, then applies iterative motion-adaptive compression: within progressively enlarged voxels, bipartite soft matching merges nodes based on a joint similarity combining token cosine similarity and a foreground prior, with a per-cluster dynamic tendency score (Eq. 7) modulating the compression ratio so static regions are aggressively merged while dynamic regions are preserved. Node trajectories are parameterized by cubic Hermite splines over a small set of keyframes, initialized by unprojecting TAPIR 2D tracklets via estimated depth, replacing MLP deformation fields with a compact, differentiable, and temporally smooth motion basis. Node SE(3) transforms are propagated to Gaussians through Dual Quaternion Blending with RBF-weighted interpolation over K nearest nodes, and the full system is optimized with a composite loss integrating photometric, mask, depth, 2D tracking, and ARAP regularization terms.

## Results

On the Hyper-NeRF dataset (monocular), the proposed method achieves a mean PSNR of 25.78 dB, SSIM of 0.723, and LPIPS of 0.242, outperforming the strongest baseline Grid4D (25.46 / 0.715 / 0.261) by +0.32 dB PSNR. On the N3DV dataset under monocular evaluation, it reaches a mean PSNR of 23.31 dB and SSIM of 0.821, surpassing MoDGS (22.63 / 0.804) and Grid4D (22.51 / 0.805). In the multi-view N3DV setting, the method attains 32.38 dB PSNR and 0.970 SSIM, leading E-3DGS (32.04 / 0.962) and 4DGS (31.42 / 0.964). On the Nvidia dataset, it achieves the best mean PSNR of 25.79 dB, ahead of MoSca (25.39) and HiMoR (22.93).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 15 baselines on the primary Hyper-NeRF benchmark. Against the closest competitor Grid4D (hash-grid deformation), it gains +0.32 dB PSNR and −0.019 LPIPS on the mean, with the largest margin on the Broom scene (+0.59 dB) where motion is most complex. Compared to the sparse-control family (SC-GS, MoSca, ED3DGS), the improvement is more substantial: +4.58 dB over SC-GS and +0.53 dB over MoSca, confirming that motion-adaptive node allocation resolves the static-redundancy/dynamic-insufficiency problem. On N3DV (monocular), it leads MoDGS by +0.68 dB and Grid4D by +0.80 dB, with particularly strong gains on the Coffee Martini scene (+1.16 dB over MoDGS) involving fast hand motion. In the multi-view N3DV setting, it surpasses E-3DGS by +0.34 dB and 4DGS by +0.96 dB. On the Nvidia dataset with high-motion sequences, it achieves the best mean PSNR (25.79) over MoSca (25.39) and HiMoR (22.93), with the largest gap on the Jumping scene (+0.42 dB over MoSca). The ablation showing SC-GS+MANI (22.66 dB) vs. original SC-GS (21.20 dB) further isolates the contribution of the motion-adaptive initialization component alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse-control-point paradigm for dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting established by SC-GS (Huang et al., 2023) and MoSca (Lei et al., 2025), which use a small set of nodes with SE(3) trajectories to govern Gaussian deformation via Dual Quaternion Blending. The key departure is replacing purely geometric node allocation (FPS or voxelization) with a motion-adaptive strategy that leverages vision foundation model priors—VGGT semantic tokens, TAPIR tracklets, DepthCrafter depth, and Track-Anything masks—to concentrate control density in dynamic regions. Additionally, it substitutes MLP-based deformation fields with cubic Hermite spline trajectories initialized from 2D tracklets, decoupling motion learning from Gaussian attribute optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated inefficiency in sparse dynamic 3DGS: geometric uniformity wastes control points on static backgrounds while under-representing dynamic objects. The motion-adaptive compression mechanism is a reusable design pattern that could inform other sparse-representation methods beyond 3DGS. However, the approach still depends on the quality of off-the-shelf VFM priors (depth, tracking, segmentation), and the paper acknowledges that prior tracklet-based methods remain sensitive to tracking errors under large topological variations. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to scenes with extreme topology changes, whether stronger learned motion priors could replace the current heuristic compression, and whether the monocular setting can be relaxed without losing the efficiency gains.
