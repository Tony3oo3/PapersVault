## Problem

Native vision-language models, which fuse vision and language into a single monolithic backbone, remain hampered by vision-language conflicts, inefficient mapping functions inside pre-trained LLMs, and an inability to preserve the distinct inductive biases of each modality (e.g., bi-directional vs. causal attention, different positional encoding scales). Prior native VLMs either collapse visual tokens into 1D representations or merely reallocate pre-trained LLM head dimensions, failing to fully integrate the encoding and interaction patterns of both vision encoders and language models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263730/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Images of arbitrary resolution and aspect ratio are converted into token sequences via a lightweight two-convolution patch embedding layer (strides 16/2, yielding 32×32 pixel patches per token) with 2D sinusoidal positional encoding, while text is tokenized by the original LLM tokenizer. Visual and textual tokens are merged into a single sequence with special <img>/</img> boundary tokens, and the model produces autoregressive text output (captions, answers, reasoning). No tiling or fixed-resolution constraints are imposed; the model handles variable-length visual inputs natively through its decoupled spatial-temporal positional encoding.

## Method

NEO introduces a unified native VLM primitive that simultaneously handles encoding, alignment, and reasoning via three key mechanisms: (1) Native-RoPE, which fully decomposes channel and frequency allocation across temporal (T), height (H), and width (W) dimensions with distinct base frequencies (10⁶ for T, 10⁴ for H/W) and enlarged Q/K head dimensions (~10% extra parameters); (2) Multi-Head Native Attention with mixed masking, where text tokens use standard causal attention while image tokens employ full bidirectional attention within each image as a unified meta-unit; and (3) a Pre-Buffer/Post-LLM partition during pre-training, where a randomly-initialized pre-Buffer (6–12 layers) learns visual perception from scratch while the post-LLM inherits pretrained LLM weights, with the partition dissolving into a monolithic backbone after pre-training. The entire model is trained end-to-end on 390M image-text pairs across three stages (pre-training, mid-training, SFT) using a simple next-token prediction objective, with a 3:7 language-to-multimodal data ratio to preserve linguistic knowledge.

## Results

NEO-2.2B (built on Qwen3-1.7B) achieves 48.6 on MMMU, 76.0 on MMBench, 54.2 on MMStar, and 74.2 on SEEDBench-IMG, matching or exceeding the Encoder-Based counterpart (Qwen3-1.7B + InternViT-300M) on most metrics while using only 345M pre-training samples. At 8B scale, NEO-9B reaches 54.6 on MMMU, 82.1 on MMBench, 62.4 on MMStar, and 76.3 on SEEDBench-IMG, approaching top modular VLMs like InternVL3† (62.7 MMMU) and Qwen2.5-VL† (55.0 MMMU) despite no reinforcement learning. On VQA benchmarks, NEO-2.2B scores 80.1 on AI2D, 89.9 on DocVQA, and 81.2 on ChartQA, rivaling modular systems with tile-wise processing. NEO substantially outperforms all native VLM baselines, e.g., surpassing HoVLE by 16.4 points on MMMU (48.6 vs 32.2) and SAIL by 14.5 points on MMMU at 8B (54.6 vs 40.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Encoder-Based modular baseline (Qwen3 + InternViT-300M), NEO-2.2B wins on MMMU (48.6 vs 47.1), MMStar (54.2 vs 52.7), SEED-I (74.2 vs 73.6), and POPE (87.5 vs 87.0), while trailing on MMVet (49.6 vs 37.4 is actually a win) and HallB (43.1 vs 44.4). At 8B, NEO-9B edges out Encoder-Based on MMMU (54.6 vs 54.1) and SEED-I (76.3 vs 76.2) but falls short on MMVet (53.6 vs 60.0) and HallB (46.4 vs 51.4), indicating residual gaps in knowledge-intensive and hallucination-sensitive tasks. Compared to top modular VLMs with RL (InternVL3†, Qwen2.5-VL†), NEO narrows the gap substantially—within 8.1 points on MMMU at 8B (54.6 vs 62.7) and within 1.5 on MMStar (62.4 vs 63.9 for Qwen2.5-VL†)—yet still lags by 27.7 on MMVet (53.6 vs 81.3 for InternVL3†), attributed to limited training data scale. Against all native VLMs, NEO delivers decisive wins: at 2B it surpasses HoVLE by 16.4 on MMMU and 10.2 on MMStar, and at 8B it beats SAIL by 14.5 on MMMU and 9.3 on MMStar, confirming that the gains come from the native primitive design (Native-RoPE, mixed attention, pre-Buffer) rather than data or backbone advantages. On VQA benchmarks (Table 2), NEO-2.2B matches or exceeds Encoder-Based on AI2D (80.1 vs 77.4) and DocVQA (89.9 vs 89.9) while using any-resolution input without tile splitting, and NEO-9B reaches 83.1 on AI2D, approaching Qwen2.5-VL† (83.9) and InternVL3† (85.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

NEO extends the native VLM lineage initiated by Fuyu, EVE, and SOLO, which embed images directly into LLM token sequences, and departs from subsequent efforts (Mono-InternVL, HoVLE, HaploVL) that rely on MoE/DaC decomposition or visual encoder distillation to mitigate vision-language conflicts. Its key architectural departure is the native VLM primitive: a single decoder block with Multi-Head Native Attention (separate QK heads for T, H, W), Native-RoPE with fully decomposed per-dimension frequencies, and mixed bidirectional/causal masking, all initialized from Qwen3. The pre-Buffer/post-LLM partition during pre-training is a novel training strategy that dissolves into a monolithic backbone, distinguishing NEO from both modular VLMs (which permanently separate VE and LLM) and prior native VLMs (which lack a structured mechanism to protect linguistic knowledge during visual pre-training).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NEO demonstrates that a dense, monolithic native VLM trained end-to-end on 390M samples—without visual encoder supervision or reinforcement learning—can approach top-tier modular VLMs (InternVL3, Qwen2.5-VL) across diverse benchmarks, validating native VLMs as a scalable paradigm. The pre-Buffer, trained on only 22M samples, serves as a reusable pretrained asset that reduces the cost of developing future native VLMs by ~2.5% below full-model performance. However, NEO still lags on knowledge-intensive and OCR-heavy tasks (MMMU, InfoVQA, TextVQA), and the authors acknowledge that larger training corpora and compute are needed to close the remaining gap. Open questions include whether the architecture extends cleanly to video understanding and multimodal generation, and whether the dense design scales beyond 8B parameters without the efficiency benefits of sparsity.
