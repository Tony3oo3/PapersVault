## Problem

Reconstructing transparent objects with complex internal absorptive textures (e.g., colored glass, jewels, resin) from multi-view images remains unsolved: existing methods either ignore internal materials entirely (NeTO, NeRRF), model only surface-level properties (Nu-NeRF, TransparentGS), or fail to handle diverse topology and intricate geometry. No prior approach jointly recovers accurate mesh geometry, a spatially-varying absorption field, and the index of refraction in a unified differentiable framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images, per-pixel object segmentation masks (semi-automatically annotated with SAM for real-world captures), and camera poses (COLMAP for real scenes, known for synthetic). It produces an explicit triangle mesh, a per-object scalar index of refraction, a spatially-varying 3D absorption-rate texture (iNGP-style hash grid), and a MERF-style environment radiance field (coarse dense voxel grid + triplanes + two iNGP proposal grids). The absorption field is the key output that distinguishes DiffTrans from surface-only baselines, enabling physically grounded relighting under novel environment maps.

## Method

DiffTrans uses a three-stage progressive pipeline. Stage 1 reconstructs coarse geometry from masks alone via FlexiCubes with SDF dilation and screen-space smoothness regularization, followed by Botsch-Kobbelt remeshing. Stage 2 recovers the scene environment from out-of-mask pixels using a MERF-style radiance field. Stage 3 is the core contribution: a recursive differentiable mesh ray tracer (implemented in OptiX/CUDA) that simultaneously optimizes mesh vertices, a per-object IoR scalar, and the 3D absorption-rate texture in an end-to-end manner. The ray tracer exploits two simplifying assumptions—uniform IoR within each object and specular (non-rough) surfaces—to make light transport deterministic, splitting each hit into reflection and refraction branches via the Fresnel equation and attenuating in-medium radiance with the Lambert-Beer law. Training uses a weighted combination of color L2 loss, a tone-matching regularization to prevent incorrect absorption gradients from refracted background, local material smoothness, and periodic mask/edge/Laplacian constraints on the mesh.

## Results

On geometry reconstruction across 6 synthetic scenes, DiffTrans achieves a Chamfer Distance of 3.264×10⁻⁴ and F₁-score of 8.386×10⁻¹, substantially outperforming NeRO (CD 36.022×10⁻⁴), NU-NeRF (CD 7.891×10⁻⁴), and NeRRF (CD 13.341×10⁻⁴). For relighting, DiffTrans achieves PSNR 23.17, SSIM 0.8380, and LPIPS 0.0678, surpassing NeRRF (PSNR 19.25) and NeRO (PSNR 19.64) on PSNR and LPIPS. The method also recovers the index of refraction with minimal error (e.g., predicted 1.381 vs. GT 1.4 on the monkey scene), demonstrating accurate material decomposition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffTrans outperforms all three baselines on geometry reconstruction. Against NeRO (Liu et al., 2023), which focuses on reflective objects and cannot model refraction, DiffTrans reduces CD by over 10× (3.264 vs. 36.022 ×10⁻⁴) and improves F₁ by 2.7 points. Against NU-NeRF (Sun et al., 2024), which models only surface materials and uses implicit representation, DiffTrans achieves lower CD (3.264 vs. 7.891) and higher F₁ (8.386 vs. 8.026). Against NeRRF (Chen et al., 2023), the current SOTA for transparent object reconstruction that neglects internal absorption, DiffTrans reduces CD by 4× (3.264 vs. 13.341) and improves F₁ by 1.5 points. On relighting, DiffTrans achieves PSNR 23.17 vs. NeRO's 19.64 and NeRRF's 19.25, though NeRO marginally leads on SSIM (0.8500 vs. 0.8380); NU-NeRF is excluded from relighting comparison due to its implicit representation. The paper notes that NeRRF's inability to model materials and NeRO's lack of refraction simulation are the primary reasons for their inferior relighting quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffTrans sits at the intersection of eikonal-based refraction modeling (Bemana et al., Deng et al.), surface-based transparent reconstruction (NeTO, NEMTO, NeRRF, Nu-NeRF, TransparentGS), and differentiable mesh optimization (FlexiCubes, DMTet, nvdiffrast). It departs from eikonal-field methods by using an explicit mesh representation that yields extractable geometry, and from prior surface-based methods by explicitly modeling internal absorption via a Lambert-Beer law within a recursive ray tracer rather than treating transparency as a surface-only phenomenon. The environment recovery borrows from MERF-style radiance fields, while the geometry initialization leverages FlexiCubes with novel dilation and smoothness regularizations adapted from Munkberg et al.'s inverse rendering pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffTrans addresses a practical gap: real-world transparent objects (jewels, resin art, colored glass) often have spatially varying internal absorption that prior methods either ignore or cannot represent, limiting their applicability beyond idealized clear glass. The CUDA/OptiX recursive ray tracer is a reusable engineering contribution that could accelerate other differentiable rendering pipelines. Key limitations remain: the uniform-IoR and specular-only assumptions exclude rough or graded-index transparent materials, and the method requires per-scene optimization (1–2 hours on an RTX 3090), precluding real-time or amortized use. Open questions include whether the absorption field can be generalized across objects and whether the framework extends to multi-bounce global illumination.
