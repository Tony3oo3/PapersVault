## Problem

Existing O2O RL methods mitigate Q-value overestimation by reducing the mean or variance of Q bias, but the paper reveals for the first time that Q-value estimation biases during online fine-tuning follow a heavy-tailed distribution, a property these methods fail to address. This heavy-tailedness causes extreme overestimation, large estimation variance, and training instability that persist throughout fine-tuning, ultimately limiting performance improvement.

## Input / Output

LAROO takes an offline-pretrained agent (ensemble Q-networks and a policy network trained on a D4RL offline dataset) and fine-tunes it in an online environment, consuming (s, a, r, s') transitions collected during online interaction. The Laplace noise scale parameter b is adaptively updated per training batch using the MBBE estimator applied to TD errors as a statistical surrogate for Q-bias variance, requiring no access to true Q-values. The output is a refined policy achieving higher normalized returns on MuJoCo and AntMaze tasks, with the method also producing a standardized (re-centered, lighter-tailed) Q-bias distribution as a byproduct of training.

## Method

LAROO introduces a parameterized Laplace-distributed noise to explicitly model the heavy-tailed Q bias, then derives a robust loss function D_b(x) = exp(-|x|/b) + |x|/b - 1 by minimizing the KL divergence between two Laplace likelihoods (one centered on the Bellman target, one on the estimated Q-value). Unlike l1 or Huber losses, this formulation preserves distributional information about heavy-tailed bias in the Bellman target while bounding gradients within [-1/b, 1/b], down-weighting extreme outliers. The scale parameter b is adaptively updated each batch via the MSE-best biased estimator (MBBE) on TD errors, allowing the noise model to track changing heavy-tailedness during fine-tuning. A conservative ensemble target (min over a random subset of Q-functions) re-centers the Q-bias mean toward zero, jointly with the Laplace noise model promoting the heavy-tailed bias into a standardized form. Theoretical analysis proves that when b > 1, D_b(x) reduces both single-step estimation bias and post-update Q-value variance compared to the standard l2 loss.

## Results

On the D4RL MuJoCo benchmark (15 tasks across Hopper, Walker2d, and Halfcheetah), LAROO achieves a total performance improvement δ_sum(0.1M) of 550.4, substantially outperforming the second-best method BOORL (355.4) by +54.8%. LAROO attains the best final normalized return on 14 out of 15 tasks, with particularly large gains on random-init tasks (e.g., Walker2d-random: 71.6 vs. 6.4 for BOORL; Hopper-random: 100.6 vs. 88.3 for BOORL). The only task where LAROO does not lead is Halfcheetah-random, where BOORL scores 90.6 vs. LAROO's 87.4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAROO is compared against five state-of-the-art O2O RL baselines: PEX (ICLR 2023), SO2 (AAAI 2024), Cal-QL (NeurIPS 2023), ENOTO (IJCAI 2024), and BOORL (ICML 2024). LAROO decisively outperforms PEX and Cal-QL across nearly all tasks, with δ_sum of 550.4 vs. 68.7 and 80.5 respectively. Against SO2 (δ_sum 268.7), LAROO wins on 14/15 tasks, with the largest margin on Walker2d-random (71.6 vs. 20.8). Against ENOTO (δ_sum 349.4), LAROO leads on 13/15 tasks, most notably on Walker2d-medium (120.4 vs. 110.2) and Walker2d-expert (128.9 vs. 120.5). The closest competitor is BOORL (δ_sum 355.4), which LAROO surpasses by +54.8% overall; however, BOORL narrowly wins on Halfcheetah-random (90.6 vs. 87.4). The paper also demonstrates that LAROO's robust loss D_b(x) can be plugged into existing methods (TD3, Cal-QL, ENOTO) as a drop-in replacement for the l2 loss, further improving their performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAROO builds on the O2O RL line of work (Cal-QL, ENOTO, SO2, PEX, BOORL) that addresses Q-value overestimation during online fine-tuning, but departs from these methods by shifting focus from reducing the mean or variance of Q bias to explicitly modeling its full distributional shape. It draws on robust regression with Laplace-distributed errors (Song et al., 2014; Lu & Chang, 2022) and the MBBE variance estimator (Wencheko & Chipoyera, 2009) to construct a principled loss function, while distinguishing itself from distributional RL (DSAC) by modeling the bias distribution rather than the full Q-value distribution, thereby remaining compatible with standard expected-value Q-networks. The KL-divergence-based derivation connecting Laplace likelihoods to a concrete robust loss (D_b) is a novel formalization that subsumes l1 loss as a special case when the Bellman target's heavy-tailed bias is ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical revelation that Q bias is consistently heavy-tailed across O2O methods and tasks challenges the implicit Gaussian/finite-variance assumptions underlying prior Q-value correction techniques, suggesting that mean- and variance-reduction strategies alone are insufficient. LAROO's plug-in loss function and adaptive noise model offer a practical, theoretically grounded remedy that improves performance by ~55% over the second-best baseline, with the added benefit of compatibility with existing O2O methods. However, the Laplace distribution is symmetric with exponential (not power-law) tails, so it remains an approximation of the observed asymmetric, heavy-tailed bias; whether more flexible distributions (e.g., skewed Laplace, generalized Pareto) could close this gap is an open question. Broader applicability beyond MuJoCo/AntMaze continuous control and to discrete or high-dimensional action spaces remains untested.
