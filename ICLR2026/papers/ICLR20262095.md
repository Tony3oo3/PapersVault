## Problem

First-frame-guided video editing allows flexible manipulation of the initial frame but provides no control over how the edit evolves in subsequent frames—users cannot direct temporal dynamics like a flower blooming or an object rotating to a novel viewpoint. Additionally, edits often diffuse into unedited background regions, and existing solutions either require expensive per-edit-type pretraining or lack fine-grained spatial-temporal control throughout the sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262095/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 49-frame source video (832×480 or 480×832), a user-edited first frame, a binary spatiotemporal mask (typically a loose bounding box derived from SAM2 segmentation, where 1 preserves and 0 marks regions to regenerate), and a text prompt (Florence-2 caption prefixed with a special token). Optionally, additional edited reference frames at later timesteps can be supplied for appearance control. The output is a 49-frame edited video where the first-frame edit propagates with coherent motion while unmasked background regions remain unchanged. Training requires only ~20 GB GPU memory (reducible to ~8 GB with the low-cost sliding-window strategy).

## Method

The core contribution is repurposing the built-in spatiotemporal mask of pretrained I2V diffusion models (Wan2.1-I2V, HunyuanVideo-I2V) as a dual-purpose control signal during LoRA fine-tuning: it both enforces region-specific preservation/generation at inference and directs what the LoRA adapters learn during training. Training proceeds in two stages under a flow-matching objective: (1) motion learning, where the mask marks edited regions as 0 and background as 1, teaching LoRA the source video's motion while locking the background; (2) appearance learning, where the mask blocks the target region to prevent the model from trivially copying the reference frame through the first-frame condition, forcing LoRA to internalize the target appearance distribution. At inference, the same mask configuration is applied with the edited first frame substituted, enabling the model to generate temporally consistent edits confined to the designated region. The approach requires no architecture modification, uses a deliberately loose bounding-box mask (rather than tight segmentation) to provide spatial buffer for natural contour transitions, and completes training in 200 steps at a learning rate of 1e-4.

## Results

On the I2VEdit test set for first-frame-guided video editing, the proposed method achieves a CLIP Score of 0.9172, DEQA Score of 3.8013, and Input Similarity of 0.7608, outperforming all baselines across all three metrics. In a user study with 35 participants evaluating reference-guided video editing, the method achieves the best (lowest) average rank of 1.620 for Motion Consistency and 1.734 for Background Preservation, compared to Kling1.6 (1.869/1.806) and VACE-14B (2.511/2.460).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines across two evaluation protocols. On the I2VEdit test set (first-frame-guided editing), the proposed method outperforms AnyV2V (CLIP 0.9172 vs. 0.8995), Go-with-the-Flow (0.9172 vs. 0.9047), and I2VEdit (0.9172 vs. 0.9128) on CLIP Score, while also leading on DEQA (3.8013 vs. 3.7348/3.5622/3.4480) and Input Similarity (0.7608 vs. 0.7569/0.7504/0.7536). The largest margin is over I2VEdit on DEQA Score (+0.3533), indicating substantially better perceptual quality. In the reference-guided user study, the method achieves the best average rank on both Motion Consistency (1.620) and Background Preservation (1.734), beating Kling1.6 (1.869/1.806) and VACE-14B (2.511/2.460), with the gap over VACE being particularly large (~0.9 rank on motion consistency). Qualitatively, the paper reports that baselines frequently distort faces, leak edits into unedited background regions, or fail to maintain temporal consistency, whereas the proposed method localizes changes to the target region while preserving the rest of the scene.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends first-frame-guided video editing (AnyV2V, I2VEdit) by repurposing the spatiotemporal mask mechanism already present in I2V models (Wan2.1-I2V, HunyuanVideo-I2V) from a simple temporal gate (preserve frame 1, generate the rest) into a flexible spatial+temporal control signal. The key conceptual departure from prior per-video LoRA approaches is giving the mask a dual role: it both constrains which regions the model preserves versus regenerates, and it directs what the LoRA adapter learns (motion from the source video vs. appearance from a reference frame). The method also builds on the flow-matching training objective of modern I2V diffusion models and leverages SAM2 for automated mask acquisition, but the core contribution is the training-time interplay between mask configuration and LoRA learning rather than any architectural change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in enabling fine-grained, region-specific video edits with only ~200 LoRA training steps on a single video, avoiding the large-scale pretraining that methods like VACE require. The finding that loose bounding-box masks outperform tight pixel-perfect segmentation is practically important for automation pipelines, as it relaxes the need for precise per-frame annotation. Open questions include scalability to longer videos and multi-object edits, the paper's acknowledged failure on text preservation and rapid abstract motion, and whether the dual-role mask strategy generalizes beyond the two I2V architectures tested. The approach also raises the broader question of how much of a model's editing capability can be steered through conditioning signals versus requiring architectural modifications.
