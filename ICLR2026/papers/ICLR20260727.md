## Problem

Existing guidance mechanisms for diffusion sampling (CFG, Autoguidance) target model-induced errors but leave solver-induced local truncation error (LTE) unaddressed. In stiff regions of the diffusion ODE where drift directions change rapidly, LTE becomes a dominant source of sample degradation, yet no prior method exploits this numerical error as a guidance signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260727/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at the sampling stage of a pretrained diffusion model (EDM/EDM2 at 64×64 and 512×512, or PixArt-α for text-to-image), consuming Gaussian noise and optionally a text prompt. It produces a generated image with no additional network evaluations beyond the standard Heun solver update, and is evaluated at 8, 16, and 32 sampling steps (NFEs 6–10 for plug-and-play solver experiments). The guidance is applied as a post-hoc correction to the Heun step, requiring only the already-computed Euler intermediate state and drift values.

## Method

ERK-Guid exploits the observation that in stiff regions of the diffusion ODE, the local truncation error aligns with the dominant eigenvector of the drift's Jacobian. It derives two cost-free estimators from the Embedded Runge-Kutta (Heun + Euler) pair: a stiffness estimator (ratio of ERK drift-difference norm to ERK solution-difference norm) and a dominant eigenvector estimator (normalized ERK drift difference, equivalent to a single-step JVP power iteration). When estimated stiffness exceeds a threshold, a guidance correction is applied along the estimated eigenvector direction, scaled quadratically by stiffness (z²) to prevent excessive amplification. The update is algebraically equivalent to a conventional guidance term that extrapolates between two model predictions, making it composable with CFG or Autoguidance. All required quantities are already computed during the standard Heun correction step, so the method incurs zero additional network evaluations and integrates as a plug-and-play module into Heun, DPM-Solver, and DEIS solvers.

## Results

On ImageNet-512 with the Heun solver, ERK-Guid reduces FD-DINOv2 from 90.1 to 82.8 (32 steps) and from 161.2 to 136.9 (8 steps), while FID improves from 7.06 to 4.91 at 8 steps. When combined with Autoguidance at 16 steps, FID drops from 2.32 to 1.93 and FD-DINOv2 from 82.2 to 75.2. On ImageNet-64 with DEIS at 10 NFEs, FID improves from 5.34 to 4.89, and on FFHQ-64 from 5.56 to 4.46, confirming plug-and-play gains across solvers and datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CFG (Ho & Salimans, 2022), ERK-Guid consistently improves FD-DINOv2 (88.5→83.9 at 32 steps; 133.9→125.6 at 16 steps) and Inception Score while maintaining or slightly improving FID, confirming that solver-error correction is orthogonal to model-error guidance. Against Autoguidance (Karras et al., 2024), the gains are larger at lower step counts: at 16 steps, FID drops from 2.32 to 1.93 and FD-DINOv2 from 82.2 to 75.2, while Precision and Recall also improve. Against the Heun solver alone (Hairer et al., 1993), ERK-Guid reduces FID from 89.63 to 85.19 (6 NFEs) and from 16.46 to 13.85 (10 NFEs) on ImageNet-64. Against DPM-Solver (Lu et al., 2022), FID improves from 44.83 to 31.59 (6 NFEs) and from 6.84 to 6.54 (10 NFEs) on ImageNet-64. Against DEIS (Zhang & Chen, 2023), FID improves from 12.57 to 9.56 (6 NFEs) and from 5.34 to 4.89 (10 NFEs) on ImageNet-64. Against the Predictor-Corrector sampler (Song et al., 2021b), ERK-Guid achieves the best FD-DINOv2 (83.7) with 63 NFEs, outperforming the best deterministic PC variant (87.4 at r=0.02) and all stochastic PC variants (91.5–98.9), while maintaining competitive FID (2.60 vs. 2.61–3.33).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the guidance mechanism landscape in diffusion sampling (CFG, Autoguidance, PCG) by identifying a fundamentally different error source: solver-induced local truncation error (LTE) rather than model approximation error. It draws on classical numerical ODE analysis—specifically the Embedded Runge-Kutta (ERK) pair structure from Hairer et al. (1993) and stiffness theory—to show that in stiff regions, LTE aligns with the dominant eigenvector of the drift Jacobian, and that the ERK solution/drift difference provides a cost-free proxy for both stiffness and eigenvector direction. Unlike prior guidance methods that require an auxiliary network (Autoguidance) or a second forward pass (CFG), ERK-Guid reuses quantities already computed during the Heun correction step, making it orthogonal and complementary to model-based guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ERK-Guid opens a new axis of improvement in diffusion sampling that is orthogonal to the well-studied model-error guidance, and does so at zero additional network evaluation cost—a practically significant property for deployment. The plug-and-play compatibility across solvers (Heun, DPM-Solver, DEIS) and architectures (EDM, DiT/PixArt-α) suggests broad applicability. Open questions include whether the quadratic z² scaling is truly optimal versus the exact α(z) factor, whether the approach extends naturally to SDE-based sampling or flow-matching formulations, and whether the stiffness threshold w_con can be made adaptive rather than fixed. The method's reliance on the Heun-Euler pair structure also raises the question of how it interacts with higher-order or adaptive-step solvers where the embedded pair is less natural.
