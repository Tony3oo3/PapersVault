## Problem

While universal approximation results confirm that transformers can approximate arbitrary sequence-to-sequence mappings, it remains unclear how the number of attention heads governs approximation efficiency. In particular, no rigorous lower bounds existed in nonlinear settings showing the cost of using too few heads, and the head-count choices in practice (32, 64, 128) are entirely heuristic with no principled justification.

## Input / Output

The theoretical framework takes a sequence X_T = {x(1),...,x(T)} of d-dimensional vectors in [0,1]^d and produces a scalar or l-dimensional vector output, where the target is a generalized D-retrieval function: each of D coordinates is a min of a C² function over a subset S_i ⊆ [T] of positions, composed through an outer C¹ map F_0. In experiments, the synthetic task uses 4D Gaussian sequences of variable length T ∈ {8,...,128} producing a scalar sum of directional maxima; MS MARCO pairs a text query with T candidate passages (T ∈ {8,...,64}) to produce a relevance ranking; CIFAR-10 divides 32×32 images into 8×8 patches (sequence length up to 257 including the [CLS] token) to produce a 10-way class label.

## Method

The paper introduces generalized D-retrieval tasks and proves they are dense in the space of continuous sequence-to-vector mappings, then establishes matching upper and lower bounds on parameter complexity as a function of head count h relative to the target's intrinsic dimension D. When h ≥ D, each head can specialize to one retrieval coordinate, yielding O(1/ε^γ) parameters independent of sequence length T. When h < D, a pigeonhole argument shows that at least one retrieval basin is unmonitored by any head, forcing the feed-forward block to disentangle exponentially many near-indistinguishable attention outputs, which requires Ω(1/ε^{cT}) parameters—the first rigorous lower bound of this type in a nonlinear setting. For the single-head regime, embedding dimension n ≥ Td enables full sequence memorization via the classification token, shifting all approximation complexity to the feed-forward block. Experiments on synthetic retrieval tasks, MS MARCO text retrieval, and CIFAR-10 image classification confirm a sharp phase transition at h = D (or h = L·h for L layers), where error scaling with T disappears.

## Results

On the synthetic D-retrieval task (D=4, T∈{8,…,128}), a clear phase transition occurs at h=4: for h<4, NMSE grows with sequence length T, while for h≥4, validation NMSE drops to ~10⁻⁶ and becomes independent of T. On MS MARCO text retrieval (T∈{8,…,64}), the analogous transition appears near h=12, with −log(Err) scaling as h/T^0.25 for h<12, matching the theoretical prediction Ω(1/ε^{cT}). On CIFAR-10 with a 4-layer ViT (patch size 8), the transition is detected at h=10 via a weighted reversal score that becomes positive exactly at that head count, confirming the L·h≥D conjecture for multilayer transformers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing architectures or methods; instead, it validates its own approximation-theoretic predictions across three experimental settings. The key 'comparison' is internal: varying the number of attention heads h relative to the task's intrinsic dimension D. Below the threshold (h<D), models exhibit worsening error as sequence length T increases, consistent with the proven lower bound of Ω(1/ε^{cT}) parameters. At and above the threshold (h≥D), error plateaus and becomes T-independent, matching the upper bound of O(1/ε^γ). The synthetic experiment confirms the transition at exactly h=D=4, MS MARCO at h≈12, and CIFAR-10 at h≈10 (with L=4 layers, suggesting L·h≈D). No external baselines are compared, as the contribution is the first rigorous lower bound for transformers in nonlinear settings rather than a new architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer approximation literature initiated by Yun et al. (2020a) on universal approximation and Jiang & Li (2024) on single-head approximation rates, by introducing the first rigorous lower bounds in a nonlinear, multi-head setting. Prior work has largely established upper bounds showing that sufficiently many heads or large embedding dimensions suffice, while Amsel et al. (2024) and Bhojanapalli et al. (2020) studied rank-based expressivity limits under stronger simplifications (linear embeddings, isolated attention). The key departure here is the introduction of the generalized D-retrieval task class—proven dense in continuous sequence-to-vector mappings—which enables a clean separation between the attention and feed-forward components and yields the exponential parameter lower bound when h < D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exponential lower bound O(1/ε^{cT}) for insufficient head count provides the first principled, quantitative explanation for why head count matters beyond mere parameter budget, directly informing architecture design and pruning decisions currently made heuristically. The confirmed phase transition at the intrinsic dimension D on both synthetic and real-world data (MS MARCO, CIFAR-10) suggests that estimating a task's intrinsic dimension could guide head-count selection in practice. Key limitations include the restriction to single-layer transformers (Conjecture 1 for L layers remains unproven), the target class being dense but most naturally manifested in retrieval-style tasks, and the open question of whether the memorization-vs-generalization tradeoff observed for short sequences can be characterized rigorously.
