## Problem

Flow-matching models achieve state-of-the-art generation fidelity but suffer from slow sequential denoising, and existing acceleration methods (distillation, trajectory truncation, consistency training) are static, require retraining, and apply a uniform schedule regardless of input complexity. The paper asks whether one can adaptively identify and skip redundant denoising steps at inference time without any retraining or auxiliary networks, while provably bounding the quality loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264460/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a plug-and-play inference wrapper around any pre-trained flow-matching model (BAGEL at 1024×1024, FLUX-Kontext at 1360×768, HunyuanVideo for video, Step-1X-Edit for editing). It consumes the same inputs as the base model—text prompts for generation, or a reference image plus editing instruction for editing—and produces the corresponding generated image, edited image, or video. The number of neural network forward passes varies per sample: the bandit may skip 0 to 6 steps at any given timestep, so wall-clock latency is input-dependent rather than fixed.

## Method

FastFlow exploits the near-linearity of FM trajectories by approximating intermediate velocities via a first-order finite-difference extrapolation from the two most recent model evaluations, rather than re-running the full network. The decision of how many steps to skip before the next full evaluation is cast as a per-timestep Multi-Armed Bandit problem: each arm corresponds to a skip length, and the reward is μ·(steps skipped) minus the velocity mismatch measured at the next full evaluation, balancing speed against trajectory drift. A UCB policy drives exploration in early samples and converges to near-optimal skip patterns within ~50–100 generations. The paper proves a cumulative error bound of O(|S|/T³) on the final state, where |S| is the number of skipped steps and T the total timesteps, and shows the method reformulates as a consistent linear multi-step solver of order 1. No retraining, auxiliary networks, or task-specific calibration is required; the bandit statistics are initialized from a single full generation pass.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark (553 prompts), FastFlow-50 achieves an Overall score of 0.78 and CLIPIQA of 0.83 while delivering a 2.65× speedup (13.7s latency vs. 36.2s for full 50-step generation), matching the full model's quality. At higher acceleration, FastFlow-25 reaches 4.54× speedup with Overall 0.77, and FastFlow-10 reaches 7.14× speedup with Overall 0.72. Across image editing (GEdit) and video generation (VBench), FastFlow consistently outperforms TeaCache in both quality and speed, achieving up to 2.57× speedup on editing while preserving or improving GPT-4.1-judged semantic consistency and perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TeaCache (the closest caching-based competitor), FastFlow-50 wins on both quality (Overall 0.78 vs. 0.76) and speed (2.65× vs. 1.85×), and the gap widens at higher acceleration: FastFlow-25 achieves 4.54× speedup with Overall 0.77, while TeaCache-25 only reaches 3.45× with Overall 0.62. Against PeRFlow, FastFlow-50 substantially outperforms on quality (Overall 0.78 vs. 0.58) while PeRFlow is faster (5× vs. 2.65×); however, at comparable speedup levels (FastFlow-25 at 4.54× vs. PeRFlow at 5×), FastFlow maintains Overall 0.77 versus PeRFlow's 0.58. Against InstaFlow, FastFlow trades extreme speed (2.65× vs. 50×) for dramatically better fidelity (Overall 0.78 vs. 0.33), confirming that retraining-based one-step methods sacrifice too much compositional quality. On image editing (GEdit, BAGEL/FLUX models), FastFlow achieves 2.57× speedup at 50 steps with Overall 0.64, matching the full model's 0.65 while TeaCache-50 only reaches 1.91× with the same 0.64 score. On video generation (HunyuanVideo, VBench), FastFlow produces sharper frames (lower BRISQUE) and more coherent temporal evolution than both TeaCache and full-step reduction at equivalent speedups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022), leveraging the property that FM models learn approximately linear denoising trajectories. It departs from the dominant line of acceleration work—distillation (InstaFlow, LeDiFlow), trajectory truncation, and consistency training—which all require retraining and apply a uniform schedule across inputs. The closest prior method, TeaCache (Liu et al., 2025a), also skips redundant steps but relies on hand-crafted polynomial fitting and a fixed relative-L1 threshold; FastFlow replaces this with a finite-difference velocity extrapolation and, more importantly, casts the skip-length decision as a per-timestep Multi-Armed Bandit problem, borrowing an online decision-making framework that had not previously been applied to generative-model inference acceleration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FastFlow offers a genuinely training-free, plug-and-play acceleration that generalizes across image generation, editing, and video synthesis without auxiliary networks or task-specific tuning, which is practically valuable for deploying large FM models under latency constraints. The empirical finding that TeaCache silently collapses to a fixed caching schedule regardless of prompt complexity, and that intermediate velocity steps contain small but cumulative variations missed by prior zoomed-out analyses, provides useful diagnostic insight for the field. Open questions include how the MAB exploration cost scales with very long video horizons, whether the approach transfers to non-Euler solvers in practice (the paper shows limited solver-agnostic results), and whether the bandit statistics remain stable across distribution shifts in prompts.
