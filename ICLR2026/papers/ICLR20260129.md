## Problem

Existing video understanding benchmarks lack the reasoning depth required to demonstrate the benefits of extended chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, and recent video reasoning benchmarks are often knowledge-driven rather than vision-centric. This paper asks whether MLLMs can perform vision-centric, complex video reasoning that demands precise recall of multiple sequential visual operations and step-by-step inference over latent states.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260129/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each input consists of a video (54.3s average, up to 154.8s) depicting 5–14 fine-grained operations applied to a latent state that is visible only at the beginning or end of the video, paired with a text question (avg. 81.5 words, full prompt avg. 198.8 words). The model produces a free-text answer, with a 'Final Answer:' prompt appended to facilitate extraction. A 'vid2txt' ablation replaces the video with a structured textual summary of all state transitions, isolating reasoning from visual perception.

## Method

VIDEOREASONBENCH is constructed via a semi-automatic pipeline with a video engine and a question engine. The video engine generates state-transition scripts for six demonstration types (Number, Circle, Cup, File, Card, Chip), rendering them via Matplotlib, command-line screenshots, or manual real-world recording; the question engine applies per-demonstration templates and hand-crafted rules to produce 1,440 questions across six reasoning skills organized in three escalating levels (recall, infer, predict). Evaluation uses an LLM judge (Qwen2.5-72B) for most questions and simulation-based state verification for the Predict Operation skill, where multiple valid operation sequences may exist. The benchmark is evaluated on 18 SOTA MLLMs, with additional analyses on thinking budget scaling, visual input ablation (full video → 50% cut → single frame → text-only), and video complexity scaling via state size and operation count.

## Results

On VIDEOREASONBENCH (1,440 questions, 240 videos), Gemini-2.5-Pro-0506 with thinking achieves the highest accuracy at 56.0%, followed by Gemini-2.5-Flash (thinking) at 27.4% and Seed1.5-VL (thinking) at 11.5%. Most SOTA MLLMs perform poorly: GPT-4o scores only 6.9%, Qwen2.5-VL-72B scores 7.2%, and all open-source efficient models (<10B parameters) fall below 2%. Human annotators achieve 73.8% accuracy with an average response time of 223.2 seconds per question, indicating a substantial gap between the best model and human performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro-0506 (56.0%) dramatically outperforms all others, exceeding Gemini-2.5-Flash with thinking (27.4%) by 28.6 points and Seed1.5-VL (11.5%) by 44.5 points. GPT-4o (6.9%) and o4-mini (10.7%) both fall below 11%, indicating that even frontier non-Gemini systems struggle with vision-centric video reasoning. Among open-source flagship models (72B+), Qwen2.5-VL-72B (7.2%) and InternVL3-78B (5.8%) are the strongest but remain far below proprietary systems, while LLaVA-OneVision-72B and LLaVA-Video-72B score near zero (0.1%). All efficient open-source models (<10B), including mPLUG-Owl3 (0.0%), LLaVA-Video-7B (0.0%), and Kimi-VL-A3B (1.4%), perform at or near random chance. The 'vid2txt' ablation (replacing video with textual summaries) reveals that the primary bottleneck is visual perception rather than reasoning: Seed1.5-VL jumps from 11.5% to 69.4% and Gemini-2.5-Flash from 27.4% to 72.2% when given text descriptions instead of video, approaching human-level performance (73.8%). Performance consistently degrades from Level 1 (recall) to Level 3 (prediction) across all models, confirming the benchmark's intended difficulty gradient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formally defining 'vision-centric complex video reasoning' as a distinct task, arguing that existing video benchmarks (Video-MME, TempCompass, EgoSchema) lack sufficient reasoning depth and that recent reasoning-focused benchmarks (Video-MMMU, MMVU, VCR-Bench, MINERVA) are either knowledge-driven or limited to short-horizon skills. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes the structure of state-transition puzzles (sliding tiles, cup swaps, file operations) into a video evaluation framework where latent states are only partially visible, forcing multi-step inference. The semi-automatic data construction pipeline (video engine + question engine) draws on programmatic generation techniques but applies them to a novel evaluation setting with three escalating reasoning levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical and previously underappreciated gap: most SOTA MLLMs score below 12% on structured video reasoning, and the vid2txt ablation reveals that the primary bottleneck is fine-grained temporal visual perception rather than reasoning capacity per se. The finding that extended CoT provides ~9% gains here but <2.5% on existing benchmarks reframes the role of test-time compute in video understanding. Key limitations include the 'clean' synthetic/controlled video settings (no occlusion, motion blur) and the absence of a proposed training method to close the gap, leaving open whether results transfer to open-world video and how to actually improve MLLM video reasoning beyond scaling thinking budgets.
