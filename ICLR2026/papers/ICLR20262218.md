## Problem

Standard SFT and RLVR (e.g., GRPO) recipes fail for instruction-based molecular optimization due to a supervision mismatch: datasets provide only a single reference molecule per query without any reasoning trajectory. Answer-only SFT collapses multi-step reasoning into short deterministic outputs, while reward-only RLVR suffers from sparse feedback under similarity constraints, driving the policy toward conservative near-identity edits that yield minimal property improvement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262218/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a textual instruction specifying a target property to optimize (e.g., 'increase LogP') paired with an input molecule in SMILES notation, and produces a structured response consisting of free-form reasoning tokens followed by an optimized molecule in SMILES. The optimized molecule must simultaneously satisfy a property-improvement objective and a structural-similarity constraint (Tanimoto coefficient on ECFP4 fingerprints ≥ δ) relative to the input. Each training instance also carries a reference molecule that serves as an answer-level anchor but is never used as a reasoning trajectory.

## Method

RePO augments the GRPO objective with an answer-level reference-guidance term and a KL regularizer, forming a three-term loss. The exploration term applies clipped group-relative policy updates to all tokens in model-sampled reasoning trajectories, scored by a verifiable reward combining binary property improvement and Tanimoto structural similarity. The guidance term increases the likelihood of the dataset's reference molecule conditioned on the model's own sampled reasoning prefix, with gradient masking that prevents backpropagation through intermediate reasoning tokens—preserving diverse edit paths while anchoring outputs to known-good solutions. This design reduces reward sparsity early in training (by making the reference more probable) without imposing token-level imitation that would collapse reasoning, and requires no labeled intermediate trajectories.

## Results

On TOMG-Bench single-objective tasks, RePO achieves the best SR×Sim in 4 out of 6 tasks (AddComponent 0.239, SubComponent 0.344, QED 0.236, MR 0.294), with up to 17.4% absolute success-rate improvement over GRPO (e.g., LogP SR: 0.415 vs. 0.379). On MuMOInstruct multi-objective tasks, RePO leads on BPQ (seen: 0.139, unseen: 0.144) and BDP (unseen: 0.113) in SR×Sim, while maintaining substantially higher similarity than SFT (e.g., 0.569 vs. 0.254 on BDP seen). RePO also generalizes to unseen instruction styles and benefits from increased inference-time sampling (best-of-k scaling).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), RePO wins on SR×Sim in 4 of 6 TOMG-Bench tasks (AddComponent 0.239 vs. 0.005, SubComponent 0.344 vs. 0.052, QED 0.236 vs. 0.123, MR 0.294 vs. 0.188) but trails slightly on LogP (0.297 vs. 0.305) and DelComponent (0.140 vs. 0.176), where GRPO's near-identity edits preserve similarity at the cost of very low success rates. Against SFT, RePO achieves higher similarity across all tasks (e.g., 0.802 vs. 0.721 on SubComponent) while matching or exceeding SR, yielding consistently better SR×Sim. GRPO (SFT init) inherits SFT's collapsed short-response behavior and underperforms both GRPO and RePO on most tasks. On MuMOInstruct, RePO outperforms GRPO on BPQ (0.139 vs. 0.120 seen; 0.144 vs. 0.107 unseen) and BDP unseen (0.113 vs. 0.108), while SFT achieves higher raw SR but at the cost of much lower similarity (e.g., 0.254 vs. 0.569 on BDP). Domain-specific models (BioT5-base, MolT5-large) are substantially outperformed by RePO across all objectives (e.g., QED: 0.236 vs. 0.080 and 0.055), and evolutionary baselines (Graph-GA, REINVENT, MOLLEO) are competitive but RePO matches or exceeds them despite single-round optimization and a smaller backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) framework from DeepSeekMath for RLVR-based LLM reasoning, extending it to the constrained scientific domain of instruction-based molecular optimization. It departs from both standard SFT (which the authors show collapses multi-step reasoning into answer-only outputs) and vanilla GRPO (which suffers from sparse rewards under similarity constraints) by introducing an answer-level reference-guidance term that conditions the reference molecule on the model's own sampled reasoning prefix, while gradient masking prevents backpropagation through intermediate tokens. The approach is positioned against black-box molecular optimization pipelines (MOLLEO, LICO, Graph-GA, REINVENT) by training the LLM as an end-to-end optimizer rather than a proposal or scoring module within an external search loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete supervision mismatch—datasets provide only final reference molecules without reasoning trajectories—that plagues the application of standard LLM post-training recipes to scientific optimization, and offers a principled fix that requires no trajectory annotation. The method's domain-agnostic design (answer-level guidance + reward-driven exploration) suggests applicability to other sparse-reward scientific tasks like retrosynthesis and drug-drug interaction prediction, as the authors outline. Key open questions include how RePO scales when reference molecules are unavailable or low-quality, whether the binary property reward is optimal for continuous optimization objectives, and whether the gains persist at much larger model scales beyond 8B parameters.
