## Problem

Existing open-set test-time adaptation (OSTTA) methods designed for 2D images fail when transferred to 3D point cloud semantic segmentation because of the extreme numerical imbalance between ID and OOD points—ID points dominate while OOD instances are sparse or entirely absent in many frames. This causes point-level identification to misclassify the majority of ID points as OOD, degrading both segmentation accuracy and OOD detection. No prior work has specifically addressed open-set test-time adaptation for 3D point cloud semantic segmentation (OSTTA-3DSeg).

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential 3D LiDAR point cloud frames (current frame plus w=3 preceding frames) along with ego poses for temporal alignment, processed through a MinkowskiUNet-18 backbone at 0.05 m voxel resolution. It produces per-point semantic labels for known (ID) classes and a binary ID/OOD classification at the superpoint level, evaluated via closed-set mIoU and open-set AUROC/FPR95. The superpoint clustering step aggregates multiple frames before DBSCAN partitioning, making the method inherently multi-frame rather than single-frame, and the OOD decision is made at the superpoint granularity rather than per individual point.

## Method

GOOD shifts the OOD identification task from individual points to geometrically coherent superpoints to mitigate the extreme ID-OOD numerical imbalance. The Superpoint Representation Branch clusters multi-frame point clouds (after RANSAC ground removal) into superpoints via DBSCAN, computes a combined purity-entropy confidence score per superpoint, and fits a two-component GMM to separate ID from OOD superpoints; Superpoint ID Prototypes (EMA-updated class centroids with α=0.99) then refine GMM decisions via cosine-similarity thresholding to prevent over-segmentation when OOD samples are sparse or absent. The Temporal Pseudo-label Branch enforces consistency between current-frame predictions and K-NN temporal neighborhoods across w previous frames to generate reliable ID pseudo-labels without extra threshold hyperparameters. Training uses a mean-teacher EMA framework with Dice loss for ID pseudo-labels, an entropy-based OOD loss (weight λ=0.1), and SimSiam-style bidirectional temporal feature regularization, all optimized online with batch size 1.

## Results

On the primary Synth4D→SemanticKITTI benchmark, GOOD achieves +4.83% mIoU improvement over the source model, 73.39% AUROC, and 71.91% FPR95, outperforming the strongest TTA-3DSeg baseline HGL by 1.93% in mIoU, 8.99% in AUROC, and 7.91% in FPR95. On Synth4D→nuScenes, GOOD reaches +2.23% mIoU, 64.60% AUROC, and 72.60% FPR95 (vs. HGL's +0.51%, 57.67%, 82.01%). On the more imbalanced SynLiDAR→SemanticKITTI task, GOOD attains +5.03% mIoU, 73.23% AUROC, and 69.96% FPR95, surpassing HGL by 3.13%, 10.32%, and 9.17% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOOD substantially outperforms both TTA-3DSeg baselines (GIPSO and HGL) on all three metrics across all three benchmarks. On Synth4D→SemanticKITTI, GOOD improves mIoU by +2.75% over GIPSO (+4.83 vs +2.08) and +1.93% over HGL (+4.83 vs +2.90), while AUROC gains of ~9% over both baselines (73.39 vs 64.47/64.40) and FPR95 reductions of ~8–9% demonstrate far superior OOD detection. The 2D OSTTA methods (UniEnt, WCEM) degrade closed-set mIoU when naively applied to 3D point clouds (e.g., HGL+UniEnt drops to +1.07% mIoU) and WCEM completely fails on open-set metrics (FPR95=100%), confirming the paper's argument that point-level instance-based OOD detection is ill-suited to the extreme ID-OOD imbalance in LiDAR data. GOOD also serves as a plug-in complement: integrating it with HGL yields the best overall result (+5.31% mIoU, 75.31% AUROC, 71.31% FPR95), showing the framework's compatibility with existing TTA pipelines. On the more challenging SynLiDAR→SemanticKITTI task with sparser OOD, GOOD's advantage over HGL widens to +3.13% mIoU and +10.32% AUROC, highlighting the benefit of superpoint-level modeling under extreme imbalance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TTA-3DSeg line of work (GIPSO, HGL) by integrating open-set OOD detection, a capability previously explored only in 2D image recognition (UniEnt, WCEM). It departs from both prior TTA-3DSeg methods, which ignore semantic shifts, and 2D OSTTA methods, which rely on instance-level GMM partitioning that fails under the extreme ID-OOD imbalance inherent to 3D point clouds. The key conceptual departure is shifting identification granularity from individual points to geometrically coherent superpoints (via DBSCAN + RANSAC), combined with prototype-based refinement to handle sparse or absent OOD samples. The temporal regularization module is inherited directly from SimSiam and GIPSO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of OSTTA-3DSeg, a practically critical setting for autonomous driving where misclassifying unknown objects (e.g., bicycles) as known classes (e.g., vegetation) poses safety risks. The superpoint-level identification strategy offers a reusable insight for any 3D task with severe class imbalance. Limitations include dependence on reliable ego poses, an added ~0.7s clustering overhead, and degraded ID performance on indoor scenes (ScanNet). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex OOD taxonomies, whether learned superpoint segmentation could replace the heuristic DBSCAN pipeline, and how performance scales with increasing OOD diversity.
