## Problem

Reinforcement learning has proven effective at enhancing LLM reasoning, yet the underlying mechanisms remain opaque: phenomena like 'aha moments,' length-scaling, and complex entropy dynamics lack a unifying explanation. Prevailing algorithms such as GRPO apply optimization pressure agnostically across all tokens, diluting the learning signal rather than concentrating it where it matters most. The paper asks what actually unlocks advanced reasoning during RL and how this understanding can yield more efficient credit assignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem as input (text-only or image+text for VLMs) and produces a free-form reasoning trace ending in a boxed final answer. During training, the policy generates G rollouts per query, and the key internal distinction is between 'planning tokens' (tokens belonging to identified Strategic Grams) and 'execution tokens' (all others). Evaluation follows the DeepSeek-R1 protocol: Pass@1 accuracy with temperature 0.6 sampling, using strict \boxed answer matching, with 32 samplings for AIME and 4 for AMC23.

## Method

The paper identifies an emergent two-phase learning hierarchy during RL: an initial phase of procedural consolidation (marked by dropping perplexity and token entropy on execution tokens) followed by a phase of strategic exploration (marked by rising semantic entropy of planning tokens). To operationalize this, they define Strategic Grams (SGs) as n-grams (n∈[3,5]) that function as reusable scaffolding for logical moves (deduction, branching, backtracing), identified via an automated pipeline of semantic clustering and corpus-level document-frequency filtering. HICRA modifies the GRPO advantage by amplifying it for planning tokens: A_HICRA = A + α·|A| when the token belongs to an SG (α=0.2), leaving execution tokens unchanged. This creates an anisotropic policy update that concentrates optimization pressure on the sparse, high-impact strategic tokens, fostering a virtuous loop of targeted strategic exploration and reinforcement. The approach is validated across five LLM/VLM families on six text and four multimodal math benchmarks, consistently outperforming GRPO, entropy-regularized, and high-entropy-advantage baselines.

## Results

On the strongest base model Qwen3-4B-Instruct, HICRA achieves 73.1% on AIME24 and 65.1% on AIME25, outperforming GRPO (68.5% and 60.0%) by +5.4 and +5.1 points respectively. On Qwen3-4B-Base, HICRA gains are even larger: +6.1 on AIME24 (31.0 vs 24.9) and +6.0 on Math500 (89.0 vs 83.0). For vision-language models, HICRA on MiMO-VL-Instruct reaches 80.7% on MathVista and 48.9% on MathVision, surpassing GRPO by +7.0 and +6.1 points. Across all five text-only models, HICRA wins on at least 4 of 6 benchmarks in 4 out of 5 model groups, with the sole exception being Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct where gains are marginal and mixed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is GRPO (clip-higher variant from DeepSeek-R1), which HICRA consistently outperforms on Qwen models: +5.4 on AIME24 and +5.1 on AIME25 for Qwen3-4B-Instruct, and +6.1/+3.8/+6.0 on AIME24/AIME25/Math500 for Qwen3-4B-Base. On Qwen2.5-7B-Base, HICRA also beats secondary baselines including ORZ (+1.8 AIME24, +1.7 AIME25), High-Entropy Advantage (+3.0 AIME24, +3.4 AIME25), Placebo HICRA (+4.2 AIME24, +5.5 AIME25), and Entropy Regularization (+2.8 AIME24, +5.5 AIME25), confirming that the gains come from semantic-level credit assignment rather than generic exploration or random token selection. The one notable exception is Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, where HICRA underperforms GRPO on AIME24 (8.3 vs 8.9) and Minerva (25.8 vs 27.2), which the authors attribute to the model lacking sufficient procedural reliability for strategic exploration to be productive. On VLMs, HICRA achieves the largest absolute gains on MiMO-VL-Instruct (+7.0 MathVista, +6.1 MathVision) while showing more modest improvements on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (+0.6 MathVista, +2.9 MathVision, −0.6 MathVerse).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO framework from DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al., 2025), modifying its credit assignment mechanism to be hierarchy-aware rather than agnostic. It departs from entropy-based exploration methods (Cheng et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025d) by arguing that semantic function (strategic planning) is a more reliable signal than statistical uncertainty (token-level entropy) for identifying high-impact tokens. Conceptually, it draws on cognitive science literature on hierarchical processing in the brain (Murray et al., 2014; Huntenburg et al., 2018) to motivate a decomposition of reasoning tokens into planning and execution categories, and provides a unifying explanation for previously disconnected phenomena like 'aha moments' and 'length-scaling' observed in RL training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, simple algorithm (HICRA) that consistently improves over GRPO across 8 models and 10+ benchmarks, while providing an interpretable framework for understanding *why* RL improves reasoning. A key open question is whether the hierarchical decomposition generalizes beyond mathematical reasoning to domains like code generation or agentic tool-use, where the 'planning vs. execution' boundary may be less clear. The paper honestly notes that HICRA fails on weaker base models (Llama-3.1) lacking procedural foundations, suggesting future work on adaptive, model-aware hierarchical methods. The broader implication—that the action space for RL should shift from individual tokens to semantic strategic units—could reshape how credit assignment is designed in LLM RL more generally.
