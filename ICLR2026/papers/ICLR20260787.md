## Problem

Applying reinforcement learning (e.g., DAPO) to Knowledge-Based VQA suffers from a severe sparse-reward problem: over 80% of samples yield zero advantage and training accuracy stagnates near 10%, because the structured, encyclopedic Wikipedia passages and noisy retrieval create a large distributional gap from MLLM pretraining data. Prior approaches either improve retrieval quality (which remains inherently noisy) or rely on supervised fine-tuning that lacks reasoning robustness, leaving the question of how to effectively incentivize multimodal reasoning under noisy retrieval largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model receives a query image, a textual question, and a set of retrieved Wikipedia passages (fused from EVA-CLIP 8B visual similarity and ColBERT V2 textual relevance scores) as conditioning context, and generates a free-form textual answer. The reward signal is a binary exact-match indicator between the generated answer and the ground-truth answer. Training uses entity-balanced subsets of only 20k examples each from Encyclopedic VQA and InfoSeek (40k total), far smaller than the ~900k–2.9M samples used by prior RAG baselines.

## Method

Wiki-R1 is a curriculum RL framework built on DAPO that bridges the pretraining-to-KB-VQA distributional gap through two coupled mechanisms. First, controllable curriculum data generation defines discrete gap levels g∈{0,…,G} that manipulate the retriever: at g=0 only the ground-truth snippet is retrieved (easiest, closest to pretraining), intermediate levels add noisy candidates while keeping the ground truth, and g=G removes the ground-truth guarantee entirely (matching inference). The gap level is adaptively upgraded when a sliding-window accuracy exceeds a threshold τ=0.55. Second, curriculum sampling selects training samples from a Gaussian distribution centered at 0.5 accuracy (where RL gradient signal is strongest), with sample difficulty estimated via a label-propagation graph whose edges encode TF-IDF similarity between associated Wikipedia articles, propagating sparse observed rewards to unobserved samples. Together, these components ensure the model encounters progressively harder retrieval conditions while always receiving meaningful learning signals, achieving SOTA on EVQA (37.1%) and InfoSeek (44.1%) with Qwen2.5-VL 7B.

## Results

Wiki-R1 (7B) achieves 37.1% BEM on Encyclopedic VQA (All) and 44.1% on InfoSeek (All), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art of 35.5% (ReflectiVA, visual retrieval) and 40.1% (ReflectiVA, textual retrieval) respectively. On the challenging Unseen-Question split of InfoSeek, Wiki-R1 attains 47.8%, exceeding its own overall accuracy and demonstrating strong generalization. The 3B variant also outperforms all prior RAG baselines, reaching 35.9% on EVQA and 42.2% on InfoSeek, while using only 40k training samples versus 900k–2.9M for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wiki-R1 (7B) outperforms the strongest prior RAG baseline, ReflectiVA, by 1.6 points on EVQA (37.1 vs. 35.5) and 4.0 points on InfoSeek (44.1 vs. 40.1), while using a single unified retrieval system (EVA-CLIP + ColBERT V2) rather than requiring separate visual or textual retrieval configurations. Notably, ReflectiVA's performance is highly retrieval-mode-dependent (35.5 on EVQA with visual retrieval but only 28.3 on InfoSeek), whereas Wiki-R1 maintains consistent performance across both benchmarks. On the Unseen-Question split of InfoSeek, Wiki-R1 achieves 47.8%, surpassing ReflectiVA's 40.4% by 7.4 points. In zero-shot transfer to ViQuAE, Wiki-R1 (7B) reaches 55.6 F1 / 50.3 EM, exceeding the RC semi-oracle baseline (44.1 F1 / 40.3 EM) and all MLLM baselines including ReflectiVA (23.2 F1 / 38.1 EM). The 3B variant also surpasses all prior RAG methods on both benchmarks, achieving 35.9% on EVQA and 42.2% on InfoSeek, while training on only 40k samples compared to 900k–2.9M used by Wiki-LLaVA, EchoSight, and ReflectiVA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DAPO reinforcement learning algorithm and applies it to KB-VQA, a task previously addressed primarily through supervised fine-tuning (Wiki-LLaVA, RoRA-VLM, ReflectiVA) or retrieval improvement. It departs from prior curriculum learning in RL (ADARFT, DUMP, VL-Cogito), which selects from fixed datasets, by instead generating training data with controllable difficulty through retriever manipulation. The observation-propagation mechanism for estimating sample difficulty under sparse rewards extends label-propagation ideas from semi-supervised learning into the RL sampling context, addressing a gap not addressed by prior curriculum RL work that relies on dense reward signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RL can effectively incentivize reasoning in MLLMs for KB-VQA when the distributional gap between pretraining and the target domain is explicitly managed via curriculum, resolving the severe sparse-reward problem (>80% zero-advantage samples) that makes naive RL fail on this task. The practical efficiency is notable: 40k training samples and 9–12 GPU-hours achieve SOTA, compared to millions of samples and hundreds of GPU-hours for SFT-based baselines. The main limitation acknowledged is that retriever manipulation provides only partial control over data difficulty, leaving fully controllable data generation as an open direction. Whether the curriculum RL framework generalizes beyond Wikipedia-scale encyclopedic knowledge to other structured knowledge bases remains to be tested.
