## Problem

Existing surface reconstruction methods—spanning computational geometry, implicit functions, and neural approaches—achieve strong geometric fidelity but provide no guarantee on the topology of the output mesh. In particular, no prior method guarantees a simply connected (homeomorphic to a 2-sphere) reconstruction, a property essential for modeling structures such as human organs and vessels from CT scans. This gap stems from the absence of computational tools that can enforce topological constraints during reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy, unorganized 3D point cloud (e.g., from LiDAR, depth cameras, or CT scan cross-sections) that may contain outliers and irregular sampling, and produces a closed, manifold triangle mesh guaranteed to be simply connected. The output vertices partially overlap with the input points but also introduce additional vertices to satisfy the topological constraint and fill missing regions; outlier points are excluded from the reconstruction. For multi-component structures (e.g., the Coronaries model with three segments), the input is pre-segmented via single-linkage clustering and each component is reconstructed independently.

## Method

The core contribution is a generalization of the winding number field paradigm from 2D curves to 3D surfaces, built on the Helmholtz-Hodge Decomposition (HHD) of a discrete 1-form derived from the winding number. By the Hodge Isomorphism Theorem, the harmonic component of the HHD is isomorphic to the first cohomology group H¹, which algebraically encodes topological handles; removing this harmonic component via a linear solve enforces a trivial H¹, guaranteeing simply connected topology by construction rather than heuristic approximation. The pipeline has two stages: (1) an initialization stage that computes a 3D Delaunay triangulation (augmented with sparse grid cell centers to avoid elongated tetrahedra) and assigns consistent face orientations via a spectral method (smallest eigenvector of AᵀA computed with Lanczos); and (2) an alternating stage that iterates between applying the core module—computing the HHD, constructing a sparse correction 2-chain via L⁰-norm optimization solved with iteratively reweighted least squares, and extracting the final surface as the boundary of an integer 3-chain—and reorienting the input triangles using the resulting surface. The L⁰ penalty on the correction surface promotes sparsity and naturally removes outlier triangles, while the alternating scheme converges in 1–4 iterations.

## Results

On the CrossSDF medical benchmark (6 thin-structure models), the proposed method achieves the best or near-best Chamfer Distance (CD ×100) and Hausdorff Distance (HD ×100) across all structures and both aligned/non-aligned settings, while being the only method to exactly match the ground-truth number of connected components (CC) in every case. Compared to the closest geometric competitor CrossSDF, the method reduces CD by 15.8% and HD by 9.62% on average. On the synthetic Stanford Bunny benchmark with controlled outliers (up to 1000), the method maintains CC = 1 across all noise levels, whereas ScreenedPoisson and SALD produce 2–2119 components.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CrossSDF (the closest geometric competitor), the proposed method wins on HD across all 12 structure-setting combinations and on CD for 10 of 12, while being the only method to match ground-truth CC exactly (e.g., Heart: 1 vs. 68 for CrossSDF; Alveolis NonAlign: 1 vs. 33). ScreenedPoisson is competitive on CD (within ~0.1–0.2) but consistently worse on HD (e.g., 17 vs. 11.8 on Alveolis Align) and produces 3–1187 connected components versus 1. OReX and POCO are substantially worse on all three metrics, with OReX reaching 2119 CC on Alveolis NonAlign. Neural-IMLS matches CC on most aligned cases but has the worst CD and HD (e.g., 13 vs. 0.41 CD on Alveolis Align) and fails (NA) on Pulmonary NonAlign. On the synthetic benchmark (Table 2), SALD and ScreenedPoisson both degrade severely with increasing outliers (CC up to 2119 and 176 respectively), while the proposed method maintains CC = 1 and competitive CD/HD at all noise levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the winding number field framework of Feng et al. (2023) from 1D curves on 2D surfaces to 2D surfaces embedded in 3D, using the Helmholtz-Hodge Decomposition to isolate and remove harmonic 1-forms that encode non-trivial topology. The alternating reconstruction scheme is motivated by IPSR (Hou et al., 2022), while the spectral initialization for triangle orientations extends the spectral surface reconstruction idea of Kolluri et al. (2004) into a topologically-aware setting. Unlike implicit-function methods (Poisson, SALD, POCO, Neural-IMLS) or cross-section-specific approaches (CrossSDF, OReX), this work provides a hard algebraic guarantee of simply connected topology via De Rham cohomology, rather than relying on probabilistic or heuristic topological control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The guaranteed simply connected output is directly relevant to medical imaging applications (organ and vessel reconstruction from CT scans) where topological correctness is clinically essential, and the method achieves this without sacrificing geometric accuracy relative to state-of-the-art baselines. Key limitations include the assumption that inlier points are exact mesh vertices (limiting applicability to Gaussian noise), the restriction to a single simply connected component, and the need for pre-segmentation of multi-component structures like coronary arteries. Open questions include extending the framework to arbitrary topologies by controlling the harmonic component, integrating MarchingTetrahedron for smoother surfaces under Gaussian noise, and leveraging the topologically-controlled representation for generative 3D shape modeling.
