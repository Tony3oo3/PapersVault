## Problem

Multi-variate time-series with heterogeneous channel configurations—especially intracranial EEG (iEEG), where each subject's electrode layout is unique—remain difficult for Transformer-based models, which either flatten 2D data and lose spatial structure, require fixed channel positions, or depend on absolute electrode coordinates unavailable in most public datasets. Additionally, the iEEG domain lacked a sufficiently large, publicly available corpus to support foundation-model-scale pre-training.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw multi-channel iEEG signals segmented into 5-second windows (up to 100 channels × 100 segments = 500 s per window), with no requirement for absolute electrode positions or a fixed channel count. A db4 wavelet encoder maps each channel's segment independently to a continuous embedding (768-d for MVPFormer-S, 1024-d for MVPFormer-M), which is then arranged into a 2D grid. During generative pre-training the output is the predicted next-state embedding in this continuous latent space; after LoRA fine-tuning with a linear head, outputs become binary seizure labels, four-way cognitive decoding labels (pitch, volume, onset, speech), or multi-step time-series forecasts.

## Method

MVPA decomposes self-attention into three parallel components—content-based (raw query-key interaction without positional encoding), time-based (query attending only to relative temporal distance via a learnable time codebook), and channel-based (query attending only to relative spatial distance via a learnable channel codebook)—thereby removing the expensive second-order spatio-temporal cross-correlations of a full dual-coded attention while preserving each dimension's structure. Relative distances are computed efficiently using the Transformer-XL shifting trick, and a local attention window (L=10 segments) on the content component reduces overall complexity to O(T²C + TC²), sub-quadratic in total context length. MVPFormer is a Llama2-style decoder (12 or 24 layers) with grouped query attention, a wavelet-based encoder, and structured dropout that blanks entire channels or time steps rather than individual segments. Pre-training uses a contrastive objective (temperature 0.1, 30 in-batch confounders) to predict the next embedding, and downstream tasks are handled by LoRA fine-tuning (rank 8, ~0.1% trainable parameters) with a simple linear classification head. A custom FlashMVPA kernel in OpenAI Triton achieves 20 TFLOPS on A100 GPUs, enabling effective context lengths exceeding 10,000 tokens.

## Results

On the SWEC iEEG seizure detection task, MVPFormer achieves a Cohen's Kappa of 0.61 across 50 unseen subjects, matching human expert-level performance (threshold 0.53), while the strongest baseline Brant-2 achieves only 0.06 and BrainBERT 0.00. On the four Brain TreeBank decoding tasks, MVPFormer-S reaches 0.83 (pitch), 0.88 (volume), 0.87 (onset), and 0.90 (speech) accuracy, surpassing all baselines that do not use electrode positions. On classical time-series forecasting (ETTh1, ETTh2, Weather), MVPFormer matches or marginally exceeds SOTA models (e.g., 0.45 MSE on ETTh1 vs. 0.44 for TimeMixer), and on 10 classification benchmarks it is competitive with or slightly ahead of PatchTST and TimesFM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVPFormer dramatically outperforms Brant-2 (Yuan et al., 2024a) on the SWEC dataset (Kappa 0.61 vs. 0.06; F1 0.59 vs. 0.01) and on MAYO (F1 0.36 vs. 0.19), while tying on FNUSA (F1 0.46 vs. 0.46). Against BrainBERT (Wang et al., 2023), MVPFormer wins on every metric: Kappa 0.61 vs. 0.00 on SWEC, and accuracy 0.83/0.88/0.87/0.90 vs. 0.59/0.66/0.70/0.71 on the four Brain TreeBank tasks. On Brain TreeBank, MVPFormer-S surpasses Brant (Zhang et al., 2023) on all four tasks (e.g., pitch 0.83 vs. 0.61) and beats PopT without electrode positions (Chau et al., 2025) on pitch (0.83 vs. 0.62) and volume (0.88 vs. 0.76), though PopT with electrode positions (PopT†) retains a small edge on onset (0.90 vs. 0.87) and speech (0.93 vs. 0.90). On classical forecasting benchmarks, MVPFormer ties or marginally beats PatchTST (Nie et al., 2023), TimesFM (Das et al., 2024), TimeMixer (Wang et al., 2024b), and WPMixer (Murad et al., 2025) (e.g., ETTh1 MSE 0.45 vs. 0.44–0.45), while clearly surpassing the vanilla Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) (0.45 vs. 1.00 MSE on ETTh1). The ablation MV-Llama (vanilla attention in the same architecture) confirms that the MVPA mechanism itself, rather than the overall architecture, drives the large gains: it drops from 0.61 to 0.11 Kappa on SWEC and from 0.83 to 0.63 on Brain TreeBank pitch.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVPA draws its relative positional encoding and shifting operations from Transformer-XL (Dai et al., 2019) and its disentangled attention philosophy from DeBERTa (He et al., 2021), but extends both to the 2D multi-variate time-series setting where time and channel dimensions are treated asymmetrically. The base architecture follows Llama 2 (Touvron et al., 2023) with Megatron-LM-style parallel blocks, and the efficient FlashMVPA kernel adapts the FlashAttention (Dao et al., 2022) tiling strategy to the three-component MVPA computation. Conceptually, MVPA departs from channel-independent approaches (PatchTST), channel-mixing approaches (Crossformer, FEDformer), and image-oriented 2D attentions (axial, criss-cross) by computing a single unified attention that simultaneously captures content, temporal, and spatial structure with relative encodings and sub-quadratic complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work delivers the first open-source, open-weights, open-data iEEG foundation model alongside the largest publicly available iEEG corpus (~10,000 hours), removing a major barrier to reproducible research in intracranial electrophysiology. The finding that relative channel encoding (without absolute electrode positions) matches or exceeds models that explicitly use 3D electrode coordinates (PopT†) challenges the assumption that spatial geometry is necessary, broadening applicability to the many datasets lacking such annotations. Open questions include whether the contrastive generative pre-training objective is optimal for iEEG dynamics, whether the wavelet-based encoder can be replaced by learned tokenizers, and whether MVPA's advantages persist at even larger scale or on truly unseen channel topologies beyond the 32-channel subset used in evaluation.
