## Problem

Current multi-hop deep-search benchmarks suffer from two critical flaws: questions embed reasoning paths or entity fingerprints as linguistic hints (Path-Hinting or Specification-Hinting), reducing autonomous discovery to mere execution, and evaluation collapses into a single pass rate that obscures whether failures stem from inadequate search, poor evidence synthesis, or inappropriate refusal. This paper asks whether we can evaluate genuine autonomous reasoning-chain discovery and diagnose distinct failure modes in web agents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263237/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a hint-free multi-hop question (e.g., 'Who is the father of Kane Cornes?') and a controlled Wikipedia sandbox where entity mentions along the ground-truth reasoning path are masked so each intermediate node is reachable only through its predecessor, eliminating corpus-based shortcuts. Agents operate under 40 tool calls and a 32K-token context window. Output is an answer or explicit refusal, scored through factorised metrics: Knowledge Score (fraction of instances with full evidence via search or parametric probes), Search Score (credits efficient search-memory combinations), Generation Score (weighted F1 of Good Refusal and Knowledge Utilisation, scaled by Knowledge Score), and diagnostic degradation rates (Forget, Lead-astray).

## Method

WebDetective co-designs hint-free questions with a masked Wikipedia sandbox: BFS on the hyperlink graph finds the shortest alternative path after blocking the direct link, then intermediate entity mentions are masked to enforce sequential discovery. A three-stage LM verification (parametric inaccessibility, evidence sufficiency, evidence necessity) plus human validation yields 200 questions across 8 domains. The evaluation framework separates knowledge sufficiency (tracking visited pages and probing parametric knowledge for missing evidence) from conditional generation quality (Good Refusal F1 for calibrated abstention, Knowledge Utilisation F1 for evidence synthesis), with a unified Generation Score that prevents refusal-gaming. The companion EvidenceLoop workflow introduces parallel solver agents with iterative extraction-aggregation refinement, a structured evidence memory assigning unique EIDs to retrieved content, and an explicit verification loop that decomposes proposed answers into atomic claims each linked to an EID, rejecting unsupported proposals with targeted feedback.

## Results

On WebDetective (200 hint-free multi-hop questions, 2–4 hops, controlled Wikipedia sandbox), the strongest frontier models reach only ~50–56% Pass@1: o3-Pro leads at 56.0%, followed by GPT-5 and Grok-4 at 50.5%, and Claude-Opus-4.1 at 44.5%. A critical finding is the decoupling of search from synthesis: GPT-5 achieves 80.0% Search Score but only 23.21% Generation Score, while Grok-4 attains the highest Generation Score (34.71%) and Knowledge Utilisation F1 (56.19%). The proposed EvidenceLoop workflow (base: DeepSeek-R1) improves Pass@1 from 20.0% to 25.0% (+25% relative) and Knowledge Utilisation F1 from 15.55% to 23.79% (+53% relative) over its base model, demonstrating that structured evidence tracking and verification loops target the identified synthesis bottleneck.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 25 frontier models and identifies no single winner across all diagnostic dimensions. On raw Pass@1, o3-Pro (56.0%) edges out GPT-5 and Grok-4 (both 50.5%), but Grok-4 dominates on Generation Score (34.71%) and Knowledge Utilisation F1 (56.19%), while GPT-5 leads on Knowledge Sufficiency (79.0%) and Search Score (80.0%)—illustrating that search strength does not translate to synthesis. Among Anthropic models, Claude-Opus-4.1 (44.5% Pass@1, 48.54% KU F1) substantially outperforms Claude-Opus-4-Think (29.0%) and Claude-Sonnet-4-Think (38.5%), with the latter showing the best Good Refusal F1 among top-tier models (34.59%). Chinese models show a clear synthesis bottleneck: Qwen3-235B-Think achieves 72.5% Knowledge Sufficiency but only 24.19% KU F1 and 21.5% Pass@1, while Tongyi-DeepResearch scores 0.00% Good Refusal F1. The paper's EvidenceLoop (DeepSeek-R1 base) improves over its base model on every metric—Pass@1 +5 pts, KU F1 +8.24 pts (+53% relative), Generation Score +2.04 pts—outperforming DeepSeek-V3.1-Terminus (24.5%) and matching Kimi-K2-0905 (24.0%) on Pass@1, though it remains well below frontier models like o3-Pro or Grok-4. Notably, no model achieves simultaneously high Knowledge Utilisation (>45%), high Good Refusal (>40%), and high Pass@1 (>50%), revealing a fundamental trade-off between synthesis capability and calibrated refusal across all 25 systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in multi-hop QA evaluation by identifying a systematic flaw in existing benchmarks: Path-Hinting (HotpotQA-style questions that narrate the reasoning chain) and Specification-Hinting (BrowseComp-style questions that fingerprint the target via attributes) both reduce autonomous reasoning to execution or constraint filtering. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, the paper introduces a co-design principle pairing hint-free questions with a controlled Wikipedia sandbox that masks intermediate entities, enabling fine-grained attribution of failure modes. The EvidenceLoop workflow is explicitly derived from the diagnostic failures the benchmark exposes, positioning it as a response to the synthesis bottleneck and weak refusal calibration observed across 25 frontier models, rather than as an incremental improvement over ReAct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent finding across all 25 evaluated models—that search capability and synthesis capability are decoupled, with top models achieving 70-80% knowledge sufficiency but only 20-35% generation scores—reframes the central bottleneck in web agents from retrieval to evidence integration and calibrated refusal. The near-universal absence of appropriate refusal (best F1: 54%, many frontier models below 10%) raises concerns about deployment safety for agentic systems. Limitations include the small dataset (200 questions), Wikipedia-only instantiation, and the fact that the masking mechanism, while preventing shortcuts, may not fully capture the messiness of open-web search. Open questions include whether the synthesis bottleneck is architectural or training-data-driven, and whether the co-design methodology generalizes convincingly to non-Wikipedia domains as the authors claim.
