## Problem

While the trade-offs of SO(3) parameterizations (Euler angles, quaternions, rotation matrices, Lie algebra) are well studied for supervised learning, their implications for RL action spaces remain unclear. Specifically, how different representations shape exploration dynamics, interact with entropy regularization, and affect training stability across standard continuous-control algorithms was not systematically evaluated. This paper fills that gap by providing a comprehensive empirical comparison and distilling implementation-ready guidelines for practitioners.

## Input / Output

The policy observes the current orientation and goal orientation, both encoded as flattened 9-element rotation matrices, along with task-specific state (e.g., drone position/velocity, robot arm joint states). The network outputs a Euclidean vector (Gaussian mean and log-std for PPO/SAC, or a deterministic action for TD3) that is projected to a valid SO(3) element via normalization (quaternions), SVD orthonormalization (matrices), or direct use (tangent vectors, Euler angles). A key design choice is that stochastic policies sample in ambient Euclidean space and project only at execution time in the environment, preserving tractable log-probability computations for PPO and SAC.

## Method

The paper systematically evaluates eight action representations (four parameterizations — rotation matrices, unit quaternions, tangent vectors, Euler angles — each as global and delta actions) across PPO, SAC, and TD3 under both dense and sparse rewards, using an idealized pure-rotation environment and three robotics benchmarks (drone trajectory tracking/racing, RoboSuite manipulation, and Fetch orientation tasks with HER). The core finding is that delta tangent vectors in the local frame yield the most reliable performance because they require no feasibility projection, keep cut-locus singularities out of the policy's reachable range for per-step angles below π/2, and allow straightforward norm-based action scaling. The paper further explains why other representations fail: SVD and quaternion projections warp Gaussian exploration noise into non-uniform distributions concentrated near singularities, the quaternion double-cover produces conflicting policy gradients, and entropy regularization drives actions toward larger Euclidean norms without increasing rotational diversity. Practical recommendations include scaling tangent vectors to the permissible angle range, avoiding post-sampling projections for PPO/SAC, and applying unit-rotation centering for delta quaternion/matrix actions in PPO.

## Results

In the idealized pure-rotation environment (Table 2), the delta local tangent vector representation (sτ) achieves the best final reward across all six algorithm/reward combinations, e.g., −2.9±0.3 for SAC dense and −6.9±0.5 for TD3 sparse, consistently outperforming global matrices, quaternions, Euler angles, and Lie algebra tangent vectors. On the harder PickAndPlaceOrient benchmark (TD3 + HER), the tangent representation reaches 69.8% success rate versus 54.1% for matrices, 46.7% for quaternions, and 32.3% for Euler angles. On drone trajectory tracking and racing (PPO), local tangent actions converge fastest and achieve the highest reward, while Euler angles are surprisingly second due to the limited angular range required for stable flight.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is among SO(3) action representations (matrices, quaternions, Euler angles, Lie algebra tangent vectors, and local tangent vectors) rather than against a single external method. The only explicitly cited external baseline is Zhou et al. (2019)'s 6D rotation representation, compared in Appendix A.4. There, the 6D representation is found to be comparable to but rarely strictly better than the standard SVD-projected 9-element matrix: PPO with absolute actions shows a slight 6D advantage, while TD3 generally favors the full matrix, and on the drone benchmarks the differences are inconclusive. The paper's central empirical finding is that the local tangent vector (sτ) representation dominates all other representations across PPO, SAC, and TD3 in both dense and sparse settings, with the gap widening under sparse rewards where projection-induced exploration distortions and entropy-regularization failures most severely degrade quaternion and matrix policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by systematically evaluating SO(3) action representations in the RL setting, a gap the authors explicitly identify between prior supervised-learning studies (Zhou et al. 2019; Geist et al. 2024) and narrow RL-specific proposals (Alhousani et al. 2023a,b; Schuck et al. 2025a, which was limited to DDPG with sparse rewards). Rather than proposing a new representation, the paper repurposes the well-known geometric properties of quaternions, matrices, Euler angles, and Lie algebra coordinates to explain RL-specific phenomena—exploration warping, entropy-regularization interactions, and projection-induced probability mismatches—that have no analogue in supervised rotation estimation. The experimental framework builds on standard continuous-control algorithms (PPO, SAC, TD3) and existing robotics benchmarks (safe-control-gym, RoboSuite, Fetch-style HER environments), extending the latter with orientation goals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive, implementation-ready guidelines for choosing SO(3) action representations in RL, with the clear recommendation that local tangent-space delta actions are the most reliable default across algorithms and reward regimes. Open questions remain for diffusion policies (whose multi-modality and noise processes may reach different conclusions), discrete action-space algorithms, and larger-scale or learned-observation settings. The authors also highlight a broader ecosystem gap: the lack of standard benchmarks that require control over the full SO(3) manifold, suggesting their Fetch-orientation extensions could seed a more complete evaluation suite.
