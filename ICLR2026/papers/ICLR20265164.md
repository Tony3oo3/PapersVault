## Problem

Continual pretraining (CPT) of LLMs for domain adaptation suffers from catastrophic forgetting because aggressive updates to existing parameters overwrite general knowledge. Existing capacity-expansion strategies (e.g., LLaMA-Pro) insert new layers uniformly and update all parameters indiscriminately, ignoring the functional specialization within LLMs where different layers and units serve distinct roles in preserving general competencies. This paper asks: can we make both capacity allocation and optimization function-aware to simultaneously inject domain knowledge and retain general abilities?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained base LLM (Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B or LLaMA3-8B), a domain-specific pretraining corpus (e.g., 14.7B tokens of OpenWebMath for math, 14.3B tokens of MMedC for medicine), and a small General Competence Detection Corpus (~580 examples from MMLU_dev and CMMLU_dev) used solely for importance probing. Long sequences are segmented via a sliding window (4096-token window, 1024-token overlap). The output is a domain-adapted LLM with k additional duplicated layers (typically 4, or 8 for LLaMA3 on Chinese-heavy domains) whose output projections are zero-initialized, so the model produces identical outputs to the base model at initialization before any domain training begins.

## Method

ADEPT is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (General-Competence Guided Selective Layer Expansion) probes each transformer layer's importance by masking it and measuring the loss increase on the general corpus, then selects the k least-important layers and duplicates them with zero-initialized attention and FFN output projections (Function Preserving Initialization), so the expanded model is functionally identical to the original at initialization. Stage 2 (Adaptive Unit-Wise Decoupled Tuning) partitions each expanded layer into semantic units (Q/K/V/O projections, MLP gate/up/down, LayerNorm), computes per-unit importance via first-order Taylor approximation on the general corpus, and assigns asymmetric learning rates as lr_U = 2·(1 − I_unit)·lr_base, so general-critical units update conservatively while adaptable units learn aggressively; importance scores are recomputed every 500 training steps for dynamic adaptation. The core insight is that LLMs exhibit functional specialization—early layers and certain units are disproportionately critical for general knowledge—so expansion and tuning should be importance-guided rather than uniform, yielding up to 5.76% general-benchmark gains and 5.58% domain-benchmark gains over full-parameter CPT while tuning only ~15% of parameters in under 50% of the training time.

## Results

ADEPT consistently outperforms all CPT baselines across both mathematical and medical domains on four backbone models (Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B, LLaMA3-8B). On Qwen3-8B-Base, ADEPT achieves 83.87% on GSM8K and 69.24% on MedQA, surpassing the strongest baselines (PT-LoRA at 82.87% and 67.01% respectively) while also preserving general competence (76.80% MMLU vs. 74.90% for PT-Full). On LLaMA3-8B-Base in the medical domain, ADEPT reaches 61.78% on CMB, a 14.73-point gain over LLaMA-Pro (47.05%). Overall, ADEPT delivers up to 5.58% improvement on domain benchmarks and 5.76% on general benchmarks over full-parameter CPT, using only 15% of parameters and less than 50% training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADEPT outperforms all four baseline families across every backbone and domain. Against PT-Full (full-parameter CPT), ADEPT gains up to 5.76% on general benchmarks (e.g., MMLU 76.80 vs. 74.90 on Qwen3-8B) and up to 5.58% on domain benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K 83.87 vs. 80.21), while PT-Full suffers noticeable forgetting. Against LLaMA-Pro (uniform layer expansion), ADEPT's selective expansion yields substantially larger gains, most dramatically on LLaMA3-8B medical CPT where CMB jumps from 47.05% to 61.78% (+14.73 pts) and MMCU-Medical from 53.96% to 67.03% (+13.07 pts), indicating that uniform expansion is particularly harmful for models with weaker Chinese support. Against PT-LoRA (rank-256), ADEPT wins on nearly all metrics; the closest margin is GSM8K on Qwen3-8B (83.87 vs. 82.87, +1.00 pt), but ADEPT pulls ahead more clearly on general retention (MMLU 76.80 vs. 75.66) and medical benchmarks (MedQA 69.24 vs. 67.01). Against TaSL (multi-task LoRA decoupling), ADEPT consistently leads, with the largest gaps on domain tasks (e.g., CMB 61.78 vs. 47.69 on LLaMA3-8B, +14.09 pts) and moderate gaps on general benchmarks. Replay (data-mixing) is the weakest baseline on domain performance, confirming that data-centric strategies alone cannot resolve the capacity conflict between knowledge injection and retention.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADEPT builds directly on the layer-expansion paradigm introduced by LLaMA-Pro (Wu et al., 2024b), which inserts new transformer blocks uniformly and trains only the added parameters, and on the Function Preserving Initialization principle from Net2Net (Chen et al., 2015) for zero-initializing output projections in duplicated layers. The key departure is making both the *where* (which layers to expand) and the *how* (which parameter units to update and at what rate) importance-guided, motivated by empirical evidence of functional specialization in LLMs. This contrasts with prior CPT methods—replay-based approaches that do not expand capacity, LoRA-based methods that are too constrained for deep domain adaptation, and uniform expansion strategies that ignore heterogeneous layer/unit importance—by unifying selective capacity allocation with asymmetric, unit-level optimization under a single importance-probing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADEPT offers a principled, two-stage recipe for domain-adaptive CPT that simultaneously improves domain performance and preserves (sometimes even exceeds) general competencies, while tuning only ~15% of parameters and reducing training time by more than half. This is practically significant for organizations that need to adapt large base models to specialized domains (medicine, mathematics, code) without the compute cost of full retraining or the risk of catastrophic forgetting. Open questions remain: whether the importance-probing corpus must be carefully curated or can be drawn from pretraining data (Appendix H suggests robustness), how the approach extends to multi-domain sequential adaptation (Appendix J hints at merging independently trained expanded layers), and whether more sophisticated, non-linear learning-rate schedules beyond the current linear mapping could further improve the injection/retention trade-off.
