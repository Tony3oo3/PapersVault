## Problem

Existing reinforcement learning post-training for diffusion language models (DLMs) optimizes over randomly masked sequences, creating a fundamental mismatch with the model's actual left-to-right inference trajectory. This misalignment degrades optimization efficiency, and no unified RL framework existed that was applicable to both full-attention and block-attention DLM architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263444/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (math problem or coding task) as input and produces a text response consisting of a reasoning chain ending in a \boxed{} answer, or a code solution in a fenced block. During RL training, the DLM generates responses via iterative unmasking (static or dynamic sampling), and the resulting sequence of decoding steps—the inference trajectory—becomes the unit of optimization rather than the final output alone. A shrinkage parameter s aggregates every s consecutive unmasking steps to reduce the number of forward passes, trading a small amount of trajectory fidelity for significant training speedup.

## Method

TraceRL replaces the random-masking RL objective with a trajectory-aware policy gradient that rewards or penalizes the actual sequence of unmasking steps produced during inference, using a PPO-style clipped surrogate with a KL penalty. A shrinkage parameter s compresses the trajectory by grouping consecutive steps, reducing training complexity by a factor of s while preserving alignment with the inference pattern. The paper introduces a diffusion-based value model that estimates token-wise values along the trajectory, computes GAE-based advantages, and is trained with a clipped regression loss; this value model reduces reward variance by ~45% and naturally accommodates process rewards for intermediate reasoning segments. For block-attention DLMs, a sliced training formulation decomposes the trajectory objective into B′ parallel slices, each requiring one forward pass with block attention, enabling highly efficient training. The framework is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on both full-attention models (Dream-7B, LLaDA-8B) and block-attention models (SDAR-4B/8B), yielding the state-of-the-art TraDo series of diffusion language models.

## Results

TraDo-8B-Instruct achieves 78.5% on MATH500 (static) and 25.9% on LiveCodeBench-V2 (static), surpassing Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct by 4.5% on MATH500 and Llama3.1-8B-Instruct by 5.9% on LiveCodeBench-V2. The 4B model (TraDo-4B-Instruct) reaches 75.6% on MATH500, outperforming the larger Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (74.0%) despite being 3B smaller. The long-CoT variant TraDo-8B-Thinking achieves 87.4% on MATH500 and 35.5% on AIME2024, representing a 13.1-point and 23.7-point improvement over its non-thinking counterpart, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against autoregressive baselines, TraDo-4B-Instruct (75.6% MATH500) surpasses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (74.0%) by 1.6 points despite being 3B smaller, and TraDo-8B-Instruct (78.5%) extends this lead to 4.5 points. On LiveCodeBench-V2, TraDo-8B-Instruct (25.9%) beats Llama3.1-8B-Instruct (20.0%) by 5.9 points but trails Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (26.9%) by 1.0 point. Among diffusion baselines, TraceRL yields consistent gains: over SDAR-4B-Chat, the 4B model improves MATH500 by 5.4 points and LiveCodeBench-V2 by 3.1 points; over SDAR-8B-Chat, the 8B model improves MATH500 by 4.2 points and LiveCodeBench-V2 by 7.4 points. The full-attention baselines LLaDA-8B-Instruct and Dream-7B-Instruct are substantially outperformed (e.g., 78.5% vs. 37.3% on MATH500 for LLaDA), though these models differ in architecture and training recipe. The long-CoT TraDo-8B-Thinking model dominates all baselines on every benchmark, achieving 35.5% on AIME2024 versus 8.2% for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and 6.7% for Llama3.1-8B-Instruct.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the masked diffusion language model (MDM) paradigm (Nie et al., 2025; Sahoo et al., 2024) and the block-diffusion architecture (Arriola et al., 2025; Cheng et al., 2025), extending the emerging line of RL post-training for DLMs (MMaDA, Yang et al. 2025b; DiffuCoder, Gong et al. 2025; D1, Zhao et al. 2025). The key departure is identifying a fundamental mismatch: prior methods optimize a random-masking objective that does not reflect the model's actual left-to-right inference trajectory, whereas TraceRL explicitly aligns the RL objective with the preferred decoding trace. The diffusion-based value model with GAE-based token-wise advantages draws on PPO-style variance reduction (Schulman et al., 2017; Hu et al., 2025a) but adapts it to the multi-token-per-step structure of diffusion decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TraceRL provides the first unified RL framework applicable to both full-attention and block-attention DLMs, and the trajectory-alignment insight is broadly applicable to any iterative generative model where training and inference procedures diverge. The practical impact is significant: a 4B diffusion model outperforms a 7B autoregressive model on math reasoning, and the method enables block-size scaling and inference acceleration that are critical for DLMs to compete with AR LLMs in deployment. Open questions include whether the trajectory-alignment principle extends to continuous-space diffusion LMs, whether the approach scales beyond 8B parameters, and whether the long-CoT DLM paradigm (TraDo-8B-Thinking) can match the reasoning depth of AR long-CoT models at scale.
