## Problem

RL-trained neural theorem provers produce correct but excessively long Lean proofs (e.g., 4,357 lines for a single IMO problem), which are hard for humans to read, inefficient to compile, and poor as synthetic training data. Existing proof-simplification methods rely on agentic scaffolding around API-only LLMs and fail on the very long proofs that SoTA provers generate, while direct fine-tuning is blocked by the absence of aligned before/after simplification pairs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a valid Lean 4 proof (theorem statement plus proof body) and produces a shorter valid Lean 4 proof of the same statement. Proof length is measured by a Lean-specific tokenizer that counts tokens but ignores comments and line breaks, and the framework is agnostic to the complexity metric—heartbeats or execution time can substitute for length. At inference time, the input proof is first passed through a symbolic Lean linter (unusedTactic) to strip no-op tactics before the model sees it.

## Method

ProofOptimizer is a 7B model (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct fine-tuned on ~1B Lean tokens across five tasks) trained without any human-annotated simplification data. Training uses two complementary paradigms: (i) STaR-like expert iteration, where the model samples 4 simplifications per proof, Lean verifies them, and the shortest correct simplification (requiring ≥20% length reduction) becomes supervised training data for the next round; and (ii) online RL with an asynchronous GRPO variant where the reward is the relative proof shortening (|x|−|y|)/|x| if y is valid and shorter, zero otherwise. Training data is generated via a four-stage pipeline that extracts 518K sub-theorems from proof sketches of Goedel-Pset problems, filters out 41% trivial ones with an AUTO tactic, and proves the remaining 307K with Goedel-Prover-V2-32B (yielding 145K proofs). At inference time, iterative proof shortening repeatedly samples k candidates from the model, keeps the shortest correct one, and feeds it back for the next round, achieving 87% average reduction on miniF2F and 57% on PutnamBench over 8 iterations.

## Results

On miniF2F (194 proofs, avg. length 334), ProofOptimizer-RL achieves a single-shot reduction of 67.1% (Min@1 = 190), while ProofOptimizer-ExpIt achieves 74.9% at @32 (Min@32 = 153). On PutnamBench (75 proofs, avg. length 1468), ProofOptimizer-RL achieves 21.6% single-shot reduction (Min@1 = 1303) and ExpIt achieves 31.9% at @32 (Min@32 = 1161). With iterative proof shortening (8 iterations, up to 1024 samples), average per-proof reductions reach 87.9% on miniF2F (334→75 tokens) and 57.2% on PutnamBench (1468→811 tokens). On Seed-Prover's IMO 2025 proofs, iterative shortening reduces lengths by 43.8%–53.8% across four problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProofOptimizer substantially outperforms all off-the-shelf LLM baselines on both datasets. On miniF2F, ProofOptimizer-RL achieves 67.1% single-shot reduction versus 31.6% for Gemini-2.5-Pro, 35.2% for GPT-4o, and 30.0% for Qwen2.5-32B — roughly a 2× improvement over the strongest baseline. At @32, ProofOptimizer-ExpIt reaches 74.9% reduction, exceeding Gemini-2.5-Pro's 62.0% by 13 points. On the harder PutnamBench (4× longer proofs), the gap narrows but remains clear: ProofOptimizer-RL achieves 21.6% single-shot reduction versus 12.7% for Gemini-2.5-Pro and 10.9% for GPT-4o. Notably, adding proof-state annotations (the '+States' variant) does not help Gemini or GPT-4o on PutnamBench and even hurts GPT-4o's @32 score, while ProofOptimizer's dedicated training consistently dominates. The RL variant excels at single-sample performance (@1) while the expert-iteration variant preserves more diversity (@32), reflecting the well-known diversity collapse in RL training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant prior approach to proof simplification—agentic scaffolding around general-purpose LLMs like GPT-4o (ImProver, Ahuja et al. 2024)—by instead training a dedicated 7B model specifically for proof shortening. The training methodology draws on STaR-style expert iteration (Zelikman et al., 2022) and GRPO-based reinforcement learning (Shao et al., 2024), with Lean's compiler serving as the verification oracle that replaces human supervision. The key conceptual departure is targeting the extremely long proofs (thousands of lines) generated by RL-trained provers like Seed-Prover and Goedel-Prover-V2, a regime where prior agentic methods fail, rather than the shorter human-written proofs that ImProver handles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As RL-trained provers reach IMO gold-medal performance, their outputs are correct but often 10–16× longer than informal proofs, creating a bottleneck for human comprehension, library integration, and reuse as training data. This paper demonstrates that a small (7B) purpose-trained model can substantially compress such proofs (87% on miniF2F, 50%+ on IMO 2025) and that the simplified proofs yield downstream benefits: faster Lean compilation and improved prover accuracy when used for SFT. Open questions include whether the approach extends to structure-level simplification (merging or eliminating sub-theorems), whether it generalizes beyond Lean to other proof assistants, and how to jointly optimize for both conciseness and execution speed in a principled way.
