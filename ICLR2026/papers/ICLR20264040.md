## Problem

Adjuvant research, despite its critical role in vaccine and immunotherapy development, lacks any dedicated AI benchmark, systematically curated dataset, or domain-specific methodology—unlike drug discovery or protein structure prediction where large-scale resources already exist. This absence of infrastructure prevents the transition from experience-based adjuvant design to AI-driven approaches and leaves no way to evaluate whether general-purpose MLLMs can handle the heterogeneous, multi-scale immune mechanisms central to adjuvant science.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264040/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes three input types: open-ended text questions (some paired with figures from source papers, processed via a unified OCR engine for unimodal models), expert-labeled hallucination Q&A pairs where the model must return a binary Yes/No validity judgment, and structured prompts for generating formal adjuvant descriptions. Outputs include free-form text answers scored on semantic similarity and scientific rationality, hallucination rejection labels, and formal expressions built from a defined vocabulary of variables (e.g., Cell₁ = cDC1 in injection site, Ab = IgG to Ag in Serum) and functions (Form, Load, Mix, Link) connected by transfer operators (causal >>, comparative &, extended causal -->).

## Method

The benchmark is constructed by collecting 739 peer-reviewed papers and two textbooks, using four MLLMs (GPT-4o, Claude3.5-Sonnet, Ernie4.0-Turbo, DeepSeek-R1) to generate ~35k candidate Q&A pairs with explicit reasoning steps, then having 13 domain experts annotate 1,500 sampled items (Krippendorff's α = 0.8119) down to 1,294 valid Q&A pairs, 69 hallucination items, and 1,364 formal descriptions. Evaluation of 30 MLLMs (11 closed-source, 19 open-source) combines embedding metrics (STS via all-mpnet-base-v2, BERTScore via scibert_scivocab_uncased) with a dual LLM-as-judge rubric (GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 scoring Similarity, Rationality, and Inclusiveness on 0–10 scales, validated against human experts with Pearson r up to 0.9145) and a Hallucination Rejection Ratio computed over 10 shuffled trials. The formal framework translates adjuvant design principles and immune activation cascades into structured abstractions—defining variables for vaccines, antigens, adjuvant forms, innate/adaptive immune cells, antibodies, and movement actions, plus composition functions and comparative/causal transfer relationships—providing computable building blocks for future neuro-symbolic adjuvant-specialized MLLMs.

## Results

On the Adjuvant Benchmark (1,294 open-ended Q&A pairs), OpenAI-o1 achieves the best closed-source performance with STS = 0.7495 and LLM Score = 7.7, while DeepSeek-R1 leads open-source models with STS = 0.7415 and LLM Score = 7.7 (DeepSeek-V3 edges ahead on LLM Score at 7.8). Closed-source models average STS 0.7263 / LLM Score 7.3 versus 0.6922 / 6.4 for open-source. On hallucination rejection, even the best model (GPT-4o) achieves only 26.23% HRR, and DeepSeek-V3—despite its top Q&A score—rejects only 2.10% of hallucinated items, revealing a critical gap between knowledge retrieval and error detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, OpenAI-o1 (Think) leads on STS (0.7495) and BERTScore (0.6195), while GPT-4.1 achieves the highest LLM Score (7.8) among inference-style models; Claude3.5/3.7 and Gemini1.5/2.0/2.5-Pro cluster in the 7.3–7.5 range, and Ernie3.5/4.0 trail at 6.9. Among open-source models, DeepSeek-V3 (LLM 7.8) and DeepSeek-R1 (LLM 7.7, STS 0.7415) surpass the closed-source average, with Qwen3-32B and Qwen3-235B-A22B also exceeding it (7.6); however, their BERTScore (≈0.54) remains below closed-source leaders, indicating weaker terminology alignment. Earlier-generation open-source MLLMs (LLaVA1.5, InstructBLIP, Idefics) score 5.0–5.8, well below the frontier. Domain-specific models show mixed results: ScienceOne (fine-tuned on general science) reaches 7.4 LLM Score but does not comprehensively outperform its Qwen3 base, while Darwin achieves the highest BERTScore (0.6245) yet only 4.4 LLM Score, and BioGPT-Large performs worst overall (1.9). On hallucination rejection, GPT-4o leads at 26.23% HRR, but all models fall far below practical reliability thresholds, with DeepSeek-V3 (2.10%) and Qwen3-32B (8.52%) showing the weakest rejection despite strong Q&A scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first benchmark and formal framework specifically for adjuvant research, a domain that Table 1 explicitly shows lacks datasets, methods, and mechanistic principles relative to established areas like drug discovery or protein structure prediction. Conceptually, it repurposes the MLLM evaluation paradigm established by benchmarks like ChemBench and PubMedQA (open-ended Q&A, LLM-as-judge scoring, embedding-based metrics) but applies it to a previously unaddressed biomedical subdomain. The formal description framework, which encodes adjuvant design and immune activation as structured variables and functional transformations (e.g., Form(Struc, Ag), Load(A, B, Surface)), introduces a neuro-symbolic layer absent from prior MLLM evaluation work, positioning the benchmark as a foundation for future hybrid architectures rather than a purely statistical evaluation resource.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a concrete infrastructure gap: adjuvant research has long relied on experience-based, trial-and-error design due to data scarcity, and no prior AI evaluation resource existed for this domain. A key open question surfaced by the results is the disconnect between generative capability and critical evaluation—DeepSeek-V3 achieves the highest Q&A score (7.8) yet the lowest hallucination rejection rate (2.1%), suggesting that strong answer generation does not confer the ability to identify incorrect content. The formal framework, while not yet integrated into model training, provides structured building blocks that could enable domain-specialized MLLMs combining statistical learning with symbolic reasoning. Limitations include the small hallucination dataset (69 samples), the zero-shot evaluation protocol that may understate model potential, and the fact that the formal framework remains unvalidated in downstream tasks.
