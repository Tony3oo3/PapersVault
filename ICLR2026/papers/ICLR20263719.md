## Problem

Large reasoning models (LRMs) generate large amounts of uncritical reasoning steps—redundant self-verification, tracebacks, and repetitive checks—that inflate token usage and can degrade accuracy. Existing efficient-reasoning methods either prune at the token level (risking removal of critical sentence-level reasoning) or rely on iterative refinement and reinforcement learning (incurring high computational cost). No prior approach leverages the model's own attention weights to identify and suppress uncritical reasoning paths at the sentence level while provably preserving reasoning capacity.

## Input / Output

FROST takes a mathematical question as text input and produces a compressed reasoning trace followed by a final answer, generated autoregressively by a pretrained LRM (Phi-4-Reasoning, GPT-oss-20B, or Magistral-Small-1.1). The key operational detail is that the model's internal attention mechanism is modified during both training and inference: the standard Softmax in every attention head is replaced with Softmax₁, which reshapes the attention distribution so that low-attention tokens receive near-zero weight. The resulting output is a reasoning trace that is on average 69.68% shorter in token count while maintaining or improving Pass@1 accuracy, with the final answer extracted from the shortened trace.

## Method

FROST introduces the concept of reasoning outliers—uncritical reasoning sentences characterized by both low attention weight and low entropy—and removes them by replacing the standard Softmax activation with Softmax₁(x_i) = exp(x_i) / (Σ_j exp(x_j) + 1), which drives low attention scores toward zero while preserving high ones. The model is then fine-tuned via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on mathematical reasoning data (OpenR1) using LoRA (rank 8, α=16) with the modified attention function, requiring only ~5,000 training steps from a pretrained checkpoint rather than training from scratch. Theoretically, the paper proves that Softmax₁ satisfies tail contraction at the sentence level: for heavy-tailed sentence scores, the ratio of maximum to median attention probability contracts by a factor κ < 1, and the logit contribution of any low-attention sentence is bounded by O(ε) across L layers, ensuring that pruned sentences have negligible impact on the final prediction. This selective tail contraction distinguishes FROST from bidirectional sharpening methods like Sparsemax and Entmax15, which suppress both low- and high-attention scores and can inadvertently remove critical reasoning traces.

## Results

FROST achieves an average 26.70% accuracy improvement and 69.68% token reduction over base models across three LRM backbones (Phi-4-Reasoning, GPT-OSS-20B, Magistral-Small-1.1) on four mathematical benchmarks. On Phi-4-Reasoning, FROST reduces token usage from ~1,414 to ~450 (e.g., GSM8K: 1,017.70 → 154.33 tokens) while improving average Pass@1 from 0.4472 to 0.5169. On Magistral-Small-1.1, the gains are most pronounced: +12.2% Pass@1 and −1,346 tokens on average. FROST also reduces the maximum infinity norm ‖x‖∞ by 15.97% and average kurtosis by 91.09% compared to the base model, confirming effective outlier suppression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FROST dominates on token efficiency across all three models and all four datasets, reducing token counts by 60–95% relative to base models (e.g., GSM8K on Phi-4: 1,018 → 154 tokens; Magistral: 2,664 → 138 tokens), far exceeding the modest reductions from ThinkLess (−4.75 tokens avg) or SelfBudgeter (+259 tokens avg). On accuracy, FROST wins or ties on 11 of 12 model–dataset cells; the sole losses are on AIME24 for Phi-4-Reasoning (0.2667 vs. DRP 0.3333, TALE 0.2900) and GPT-OSS-20B (0.1667 vs. DRP 0.2245, TALE 0.2000), where the harder competition problems benefit from the longer, more exploratory traces those methods produce. Against the activation-function baselines (Table 2), FROST (Softmax₁) outperforms Entmax15 (0.5169 vs. 0.4751 avg Pass@1) and Sparsemax (0.4406) while using fewer tokens (450 vs. 472 and 535), because Sparsemax and Entmax15 over-sharpen both low- and high-attention scores, inadvertently pruning critical reasoning steps. FROST also achieves the fastest training time (204 min vs. DRP 353 min, ThinkLess 1,186 min) and the shortest inference time (3 min vs. TALE 56 min, DRP 18.5 min) on AIME with GPT-OSS-20B.

## Relation to Prior Work

FROST repurposes the Softmax₁ activation (adding a constant to the softmax denominator) from the outlier-removal literature in Hopfield/transformer layers (Hu et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2025b) and applies it to the specific problem of pruning uncritical reasoning sentences in large reasoning models. It departs from prior efficient-reasoning methods—token-budget prompting (TALE), iterative SFT distillation (DRP), and RL-based token allocation (ThinkLess, SelfBudgeter)—by operating directly on the attention mechanism rather than on generated text, and by requiring only a few SFT steps with LoRA rather than training from scratch or multi-step continual learning. The paper also distinguishes itself from concurrent sentence-level attention analyses (Think Clearly, R-KV) by performing functional attribution of which reasoning sentences contribute to the final answer, rather than merely detecting redundancy for KV-cache compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a ~70% reduction in reasoning tokens with a concurrent accuracy gain across three backbone models and four math benchmarks, achieved with a minimal architectural change (one activation swap + LoRA SFT), makes this an attractive drop-in for deployment-constrained settings. However, the method is currently validated only on mathematical reasoning, and the authors acknowledge that extending to coding and integrating GRPO remain open. A key open question is whether the Softmax₁ tail-contraction property (Assumption 5.1, P3) holds universally across architectures or is specific to the tested models, since the theoretical proof relies on this assumption rather than deriving it from the explicit Softmax₁ formula.
