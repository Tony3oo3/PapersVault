## Problem

Current LLM safety alignment relies on shallow refusal heuristics rather than genuine reasoning, making models vulnerable to jailbreak attacks that disguise harmful intent through rephrasing, encoding, or obfuscation. The paper provides causal evidence (via neuron deactivation) that alignment behavior is largely independent of reasoning ability, and identifies that even CoT-based alignment leaves ~15% of failures where reasoning and final answer are misaligned in opposite directions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262169/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (harmful or benign query) and produces a structured output consisting of a chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed in  and  tags, followed by a final response (either a refusal with justification or a helpful answer). For AW-DPO training, the pipeline generates k=5 candidate responses per prompt from the CoT-finetuned model, then a GPT-4o judge scores harmfulness separately on the reasoning segment, the response segment, and the full answer to construct weighted preference pairs.

## Method

The approach has two stages: (1) CoT fine-tuning on a novel open-source dataset of 20,000 samples (4,000 safety + 16,000 utility) with GPT-4o-generated step-by-step rationales, teaching the model to explain why a prompt is harmful before refusing; (2) Alignment-Weighted DPO (AW-DPO), which decomposes each response into reasoning and response tokens using the  boundary, computes separate DPO losses on each segment, and combines them with weights proportional to the harmfulness difference in each segment (w_reasoning = d_reasoning / (d_reasoning + d_response)). This targets the ~15% of failure cases where standard DPO's uniform full-response optimization is insufficient—specifically correct-reasoning-unsafe-answer and incorrect-reasoning-safe-answer mismatches—enabling finer-grained, safety-aware correction without the multi-round training cost of iterative SFT+DPO pipelines.

## Results

Across four model families (Llama-2-7B, Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B), AW-DPO achieves the lowest average Attack Success Rate (ASR) on SorryBench: 3.41%, 0.58%, 0.81%, and 0.91% respectively, compared to 9.11%, 1.04%, 1.00%, and 3.78% for standard DPO. On MMLU utility, AW-DPO maintains competitive scores (45.23%, 48.52%, 58.27%, 54.70%) while reducing ASR by 2–4× over DPO. Against advanced alignment baselines on Llama-3.1-8B, AW-DPO (Base) achieves 0.81% average ASR versus 1.33% for STAIR-DPO-3, 3.09% for STAIR, and 25.80% for SAFECHAIN, with 58.27% MMLU utility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AW-DPO outperforms standard DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) on average ASR across all four model families (e.g., 0.81% vs. 1.00% on Llama-3.1-8B; 0.91% vs. 3.78% on Mistral-7B) while maintaining comparable or slightly better MMLU utility. Against Safety SFT (Wang et al., 2024) and Safety SFT + DPO (Guo et al., 2024), AW-DPO reduces ASR by 10–30× (e.g., 0.81% vs. 9.22% on Llama-3.1-8B). Compared to SAFECHAIN (Jiang et al., 2025) on Llama-3.1-8B, AW-DPO achieves 0.81% ASR versus 25.80%, a 32× improvement. Against Representation Rerouting (Zou et al., 2024), AW-DPO wins on safety (0.81% vs. 4.55%) but trails on utility (58.27% vs. 61.84%). STAIR (Zhang et al., 2025a) achieves 3.09% ASR, while STAIR-DPO-3 reaches 1.33% ASR with 71.34% utility; AW-DPO (Base) beats both on safety (0.81%) but has lower utility (58.27%), though it requires only one SFT+DPO round versus three iterative rounds for STAIR-DPO-3. Against open-source aligned models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3), AW-DPO reduces ASR from 7.24% and 34.45% to 0.81% and 0.91% respectively, at a modest utility cost. Finally, general reasoning models (Phi-4-Reasoning) perform far worse on safety (47.20% ASR) than AW-DPO (0.81%), confirming that alignment-specific reasoning is necessary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: (1) the 'superficial safety alignment' hypothesis (Li & Kim, 2024; Qi et al., 2024), which the authors test with causal neuron deactivation rather than correlational probing; (2) CoT-based safety fine-tuning (Guan et al., 2024a; Mou et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025b), which they extend by releasing a paired utility+safety CoT dataset; and (3) DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), which they modify by decomposing the implicit reward into reasoning and response segments with data-dependent weights. The key departure from prior DPO variants (e.g., STAIR, SAFECHAIN) is that AW-DPO assigns per-sample, per-segment weights derived from a judge model's harmfulness scores, targeting the specific component (reasoning vs. final answer) where the error occurs rather than optimizing the full response uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The causal intervention providing direct evidence that safety alignment is mechanistically decoupled from reasoning is a useful diagnostic for the field, and the AW-DPO recipe offers a practical, single-round training procedure that outperforms vanilla DPO across multiple model families and attack categories. However, the method's reliance on GPT-4o as a judge introduces a quality bottleneck and cost, the tested models are limited to 3B–8B parameters, and the safety gains over standard DPO are modest (e.g., 1.00% → 0.81% average ASR on Llama-3.1-8B). Open questions include whether the weighted decomposition scales to frontier models, whether the judge model can be replaced by a cheaper proxy without degrading the signal, and whether the 15% reasoning-error failure mode generalizes beyond the specific CoT training distribution used here.
