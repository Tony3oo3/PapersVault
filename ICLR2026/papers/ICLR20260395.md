## Problem

LTL specifications provide correct-by-construction RL objectives for temporally extended tasks, but the resulting automaton-based rewards are both sparse and discrete, forcing agents into prolonged blind exploration before receiving any nonzero signal. Heuristic reward shaping can densify these signals but risks undermining the logical correctness that makes LTL attractive in the first place. No prior method enables efficient gradient-based learning directly from LTL specifications while preserving objective soundness.

## Input / Output

The method takes an LTL formula (compiled into an LDBA via Owl/Spot) and a differentiable physics simulator (dFlex) as inputs, along with continuous state observations from the environment. It produces a finite-memory control policy parameterized by a neural network that outputs both continuous actions and probabilistic ε-action vectors for the automaton, alongside differentiable reward and state-dependent discounting functions computed from the automaton state probability vector. The LTL formula's atomic propositions must be expressible as differentiable threshold functions on the state (e.g., g(s) > 0), which constrains the specification to continuous-signal bounds rather than arbitrary discrete predicates.

## Method

The key mechanism is replacing discrete one-hot automaton state indicators with continuous probability vectors over all automaton states, and replacing binary atomic proposition labels with sigmoid-based soft labels Pr(a ∈ L(s)) = σ(g(s)). This yields a fully differentiable product MDP transition function f that composes the differentiable physics step, a differentiable ε-action update (treating ε-transition choices as probability vectors), and a differentiable label-triggered automaton update (computed via differentiable matrix multiplication over the LDBA transition structure). The reward R(s,q) = (1−β)·Σ_{q∈B} q_q and discount D(q) = β·Σ_{q∈B} q_q + γ·Σ_{q∉B} q_q are also differentiable, enabling first-order gradient estimates ∇_θ G_H(σ) with lower variance than zeroth-order policy gradients. Theorem 2 provides a tunable upper bound on the discrepancy between discrete and differentiable LTL returns parameterized by the soft-label tolerance ζ, ensuring the differentiable objective converges to the true Büchi satisfaction probability. The framework is compatible with any differentiable RL algorithm (SHAC, AHAC) and generalizes to reward machines without modification.

## Results

Across four environments (CartPole, Hopper, Cheetah, Ant), differentiable RL algorithms (SHAC, AHAC) with differentiable LTL rewards converge to near-optimal LTL satisfaction probabilities (Pr > 0.8–0.9) within 20M steps, while non-differentiable baselines (PPO, SAC) with discrete LTL rewards either fail to learn meaningful policies or converge to suboptimal solutions even after 100M steps. The paper reports up to 2× higher returns for differentiable LTL over discrete baselines. In the Reward Machine generalization experiment on Cheetah, the differentiable RM approach (SHAC) achieves 15.4±2.5K return at 3000K steps for TASK 1, outperforming discrete CRM (≈8.3±0.3K) and HRM+RS (≈9.1±2.8K) baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its differentiable LTL approach against three categories of baselines. Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) and SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) using discrete LTL rewards, the proposed method wins decisively across all four environments: in CartPole, SHAC/AHAC reach Pr > 0.8 within 20M steps while SAC fails on all seeds and PPO fails on one seed after 100M steps; in Cheetah, differentiable methods achieve Pr > 0.9 while PPO converges to a suboptimal policy and SAC gets stuck in poor local optima; in Ant, differentiable methods learn near-optimal policies rapidly while discrete methods only reach suboptimal returns. Against the Reward Machine baselines from Toro Icarte et al. (2022) (CRM and HRM+RS) on Cheetah, the differentiable RM approach (SHAC) achieves roughly 1.8–2.3× higher returns (e.g., 15.4K vs. 8.3K for TASK 1 at 3000K steps), confirming that the differentiability mechanism generalizes beyond LDBAs to other automaton-based formal frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: LTL-based RL (which provides correct-by-construction objectives via Büchi automata but suffers from sparse, discrete rewards) and differentiable simulation for RL (which enables low-variance first-order gradient estimates but has been applied only to handcrafted or STL-based rewards). It builds directly on Bozkurt et al. (2024) for the discrete LTL reward formulation with state-dependent discounting, and on Xu et al. (2022) / Georgiev et al. (2024) for the differentiable RL algorithms (SHAC, AHAC). The key departure is introducing soft labeling to make automaton transitions and ε-actions differentiable, yielding an end-to-end differentiable pipeline from LTL specification to policy gradient — a combination the authors claim is the first of its kind.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By rendering LTL-derived rewards differentiable while preserving objective soundness (with a tunable discrepancy bound in Theorem 2), this work opens a path toward specification-driven safe RL in continuous, contact-rich domains where handcrafted reward engineering is infeasible. The ablation confirming that the gains come from LTL reward differentiability (not merely the differentiable simulator) strengthens the practical takeaway. Limitations include the requirement for a differentiable simulator (ruling out discrete MDPs), an additional activation-function hyperparameter, and the need for LTL expertise to write specifications. Open directions include combining with counterfactual experience replay, barrier nets, and designing LTL-specific differentiable RL algorithms that exploit automaton structure for exploration and transfer.
