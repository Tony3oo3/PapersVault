## Problem

State-of-the-art visual localization methods require extensive map preparation before a query can be localized: structure-based approaches need hours of SfM triangulation, and scene coordinate regression methods need minutes of per-scene network training. This paper asks whether competitive localization accuracy can be achieved with minimal map preparation, reducing the overhead to a simple image retrieval step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261542/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image (resized to 512px largest dimension) and a set of retrieved mapping images with known poses (top-10 for indoor, top-20 for outdoor, selected via GeM-AP global descriptors). From the mapping images, a fixed-size random sample of N ViT features (N=3000 outdoor, N=1500 indoor) is extracted and augmented with ray encodings. The output is a set of 3D scene coordinates for query pixels in a normalized coordinate frame, which are scaled by the scene scale factor to recover metric coordinates; these 2D-3D correspondences are then solved via PnP-RANSAC (up to 5,000 correspondences) to yield the final 6-DoF camera pose.

## Method

FastForward represents the scene as a random collection of N mapping features from a frozen ViT encoder (initialized from DUSt3R), each augmented with a Fourier-encoded ray embedding that encodes the mapping camera's normalized position and viewing direction. A fine-tuned ViT decoder with cross-attention blocks jointly processes query features and the map representation in a single forward pass, predicting query 3D coordinates via a DPT head and mapping 3D coordinates via an MLP head (used only as auxiliary supervision). A key design choice is scene and scale normalization: one mapping pose is set to the origin and all translations are divided by the maximum translation magnitude, abstracting metric scale from the network and enabling generalization to unseen scale ranges. The network is trained with DUSt3R's confidence-weighted Euclidean regression loss, optimizing only the decoder and heads (encoder frozen) for 615k iterations on 8 A100 GPUs. Unlike RPR methods that estimate pairwise relative poses and require heuristics for scale, FastForward transfers metric scale directly from the mapping poses and avoids per-pair computation by reasoning over the full multi-view map in one pass.

## Results

On Wayspots, FastForward achieves a median translation error of 0.17 m and 51.4% accuracy at the 10 cm / 10° threshold, matching SCR methods (ACE: 51.9%, GLACE: 52.4%) while reducing mapping time from 5–25 min to 3 s and localizing in 0.5 s. On Indoor6, FastForward reaches 91.5% at 10 cm / 10° (98.0% at 20 cm / 20°), surpassing even MASt3R+Kapture (89.0%) with a 0.3 s latency versus 4.5 s. On Cambridge Landmarks, FastForward reports 0.27 m / 0.4° median pose error, reducing Reloc3r's translation error by 48%, though E5+1(ALKD-LG) remains slightly better at 0.18 m / 0.3° in that well-structured outdoor setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SCR methods (ACE, GLACE), FastForward matches or exceeds their 10 cm / 10° accuracy on Wayspots (51.4% vs 51.9%/52.4%) and Indoor6 (91.5% vs 57.5%/86.3%) while cutting mapping preparation from 5–25 minutes to 3 seconds. Versus the closest Unseen competitor Reloc3r, FastForward reduces median translation error from 1.31 m to 0.17 m on Wayspots and boosts 10 cm / 10° accuracy from 37.1% to 51.4%, a 39% relative improvement. Against E5+1(ALKD-LG), FastForward wins decisively on Wayspots (0.17 m vs 0.51 m translation) and Indoor6 (91.5% vs 80.9%), but loses on Cambridge Landmarks (0.27 m vs 0.18 m), where dense structural coverage favors explicit 2D-2D matching. Compared to MASt3R, FastForward dominates on Indoor6 (91.5% vs 45.9%) and Cambridge (0.27 m vs 3.90 m), while MASt3R+Kapture remains marginally better on Indoor6 translation (0.03 m vs 0.04 m) but requires ~3.5 h of mapping versus 8 s. On 7-Scenes, FastForward (90.2% at 10 cm / 10°) trails ACE (99.5%) and GLACE (97.8%) but outperforms all other Unseen methods, confirming that the gap to SCR narrows in static, well-covered indoor scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

FastForward builds directly on DUSt3R's ViT encoder, DPT head, and confidence-based loss, but departs from its two-image formulation by replacing the second image with a random collection of 3D-anchored mapping features augmented with ray encodings. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between Relative Pose Regression (which computes pairwise query-to-reference poses sequentially) and Scene Coordinate Regression (which trains a scene-specific network), by predicting query 3D coordinates in a single feed-forward pass against a multi-view feature set. The scale normalization technique, inspired by Guizilini et al. (2025), is a key departure from prior RPR methods that rely on heuristics for metric scale recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for AR and navigation: mapping preparation drops from minutes (SCR) or hours (SfM) to ~3 seconds (retrieval only), while maintaining or exceeding the accuracy of methods requiring extensive scene-specific preparation. The finding that a sparse set of ~3,000 features from 20 images suffices for SOTA localization challenges the assumption that dense scene representations are necessary. Open questions include whether retrieval can be eliminated entirely (the paper shows accuracy drops from 51.4% to 47.8% without it), how the method scales to very large or highly dynamic scenes (RIO10 results are weaker), and whether a direct pose head could replace PnP-RANSAC for further speedup.
