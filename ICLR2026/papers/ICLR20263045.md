## Problem

Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) promise parallel text generation, but their practical inference speed is bottlenecked by the 'scattered acceptance' decoding strategy, which commits high-confidence tokens at disjoint positions. This fragments the KV cache, destroys memory locality, and forces the model into repeated localized repairs across many unstable token boundaries, negating the architectural parallelism advantage.

## Input / Output

At each denoising iteration, LSP consumes a composite state consisting of a frozen (committed) prefix and an active suffix of masked tokens, performing a single forward pass over the active suffix conditioned on the cached prefix. The output is a monotonically growing contiguous text sequence: each iteration atomically appends a structurally-aligned block of tokens to the prefix, with the active suffix shrinking geometrically until fully committed. The method is training-free and model-agnostic, requiring only a delimiter set and two fractional hyperparameters (α, β) to control commitment aggressiveness.

## Method

LSP replaces scattered token acceptance with monolithic prefix absorption via a three-stage single-pass procedure: (1) compute a logit margin (top-1 minus top-2 logit difference) at each active position as a stability proxy; (2) adaptively select a threshold τ so that the longest contiguous run of stable tokens from the left of the active suffix falls within a target fraction [α, β] of the current suffix length (e.g., 25–50%), ensuring geometric decay of the active sequence; (3) snap the candidate block's right boundary to the nearest structural delimiter (punctuation, newline, code symbol) within a lookback window to preserve linguistic coherence. A fallback rule guarantees at least one token is committed per step. The committed prefix is treated as fixed context with contiguous KV cache appends, while the shrinking active suffix retains bidirectional lookahead, allowing the model to resolve global dependencies before each prefix is frozen. This prefix-first topology yields near-quadratic total work complexity and reduces mid-generation token flip rates from 14.2% to 4.3% compared to scattered baselines.

## Results

LSP achieves inference speedups of 1.22×–3.36× over the Full-decoding baseline on LLaDA-8B and 1.46×–3.36× on Dream-7B across 12 benchmarks, while matching or slightly improving task accuracy in most cases. On GSM8K, LSP delivers a 1.51× speedup with LLaDA-8B while improving accuracy from 77.1% to 77.6%. The largest speedups appear on structured tasks (Sudoku: 3.00×/3.36×; Countdown: 2.63×/2.40×), while code generation (HumanEval) sees the smallest gains (1.22×/1.46×) due to the need for precise syntactic commitment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Full decoding, the standard DLM iterative refinement that uses the complete step budget (T_max = L). LSP wins decisively on speed across all 12 benchmarks, with speedups ranging from 1.22× (HumanEval, LLaDA-8B) to 3.36× (Sudoku, Dream-7B). On quality, LSP matches or improves the Full baseline on 9 of 12 benchmarks per model (e.g., Hellaswag +2.0 pp, TruthfulQA +11.4 pp on LLaDA-8B; MBPP +0.6 pp, HumanEval +0.6 pp on Dream-7B). The notable exceptions are WinoGrande (−3.1 pp on LLaDA-8B) and TruthfulQA (−2.1 pp on Dream-7B), where the aggressive early commitment appears to slightly hurt performance on tasks requiring nuanced commonsense or factual recall. Overall, LSP trades at most ~3 pp of accuracy on a small subset of tasks for a 1.5×–3.4× reduction in inference time, a favorable trade-off for latency-sensitive deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the discrete diffusion framework (D3PM, Austin et al. 2021) and the emerging DLM inference acceleration literature, which spans approximate KV caching (Fast-DLLM, DKV-Cache), token pruning (DPad), and confidence-based parallel decoding. LSP departs from these by reframing the bottleneck as a *commitment topology* problem rather than a caching or pruning problem: instead of asking 'which tokens to skip recomputing' or 'how many tokens to accept per step,' it asks 'what geometric shape should the frozen region have?' The closest conceptual neighbor is MidTruth (Wang et al. 2025), which also exploits early answer convergence in DLMs but pursues temporal ensembling for accuracy rather than early atomic commitment for speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LSP offers a training-free, model-agnostic scheduling strategy that could be dropped into any DLM inference pipeline, and the up-to-3.4× speedup with matched or improved quality is practically significant for deploying DLMs at scale. However, the evaluation is limited to two models and compares only against a non-accelerated 'Full' baseline, leaving open how LSP stacks against other acceleration methods (Fast-DLLM, DPad, speculative decoding) in combination. The method is explicitly restricted to sequential left-to-right generation and relies on heuristic delimiter sets, so its applicability to in-filling, unconstrained editing, or non-English tokenizers remains untested. A key open question is whether the logit-margin stability proxy can be replaced by temporally-aware metrics that better capture when a token will truly stabilize across remaining denoising steps.
