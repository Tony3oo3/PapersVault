## Problem

Generative image models trained on large-scale datasets frequently fail to produce images containing seemingly simple concepts (e.g., human hands, objects in groups of four), yet these failures have been documented only anecdotally, leaving open whether they reflect idiosyncratic quirks or systematic structural limitations. Existing evaluation metrics (FID, CLIPScore, precision/recall) operate at the level of whole images or prompts and cannot identify which specific fine-grained concepts are under- or over-represented in a model's output distribution.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 10,000 image-text pairs sampled from LAION-5B (512×512 RGB images with their original captions) as the natural reference set, and for each caption synthesizes a counterpart image using the target text-to-image model. Both the natural and generated images are encoded by DINOv2 into 261 visual tokens per image, which are then projected through the trained RA-SAE into 32,000-dimensional sparse concept activation vectors. The primary output is a per-concept energy difference δ(k) ∈ [0,1] for all 32,000 concepts, from which suppressed (δ < 0.1) and exaggerated (δ > 0.9) conceptual blindspots are identified, alongside datapoint-level L2 divergence scores for individual image pairs.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a 32,000-concept Relaxed Archetypal SAE (RA-SAE) trained on DINOv2 features extracted from the full ImageNet-1k training set (~1.67×10¹⁰ tokens), making it the largest RA-SAE to date. The RA-SAE constrains its dictionary D = WA to be a convex combination of training activations (W row-stochastic), anchoring each learned concept atom within the convex hull of the data and ensuring seed-independent, reproducible concept extraction; sparsity is enforced via Top-K masking (k=5). The theoretical grounding rests on the result that self-supervised contrastive learning (DINOv2) inverts the data-generating process up to linear transformations, so SAE activations on DINOv2 features approximate the ground-truth energy function E(c_k) for each latent concept. The key diagnostic metric δ(k) = σ(E_{x'}[ξ_k(x')] − E_x[ξ_k(x)]) quantifies the relative probability mass a concept receives under the generative model versus the true DGP, enabling a principled, unsupervised comparison of concept prevalence between natural and synthesized image sets without requiring human-defined concept labels.

## Results

Across 32,000 RA-SAE concepts and 10,000 image-text pairs, all four evaluated models (SD 1.5, SD 2.1, PixArt, Kandinsky) exhibit heavy-tailed energy-difference distributions with consistent negative skewness (SD 2.1: −0.54; SD 1.5 and PixArt: −0.40; Kandinsky: −0.23), indicating a systematic tendency toward concept suppression. Cross-model Pearson correlations reveal that SD 1.5 and SD 2.1 share the strongest blindspot alignment (r = 0.82), while correlations with PixArt (r = 0.41) and Kandinsky (r = 0.46) are substantially weaker, pointing to architecture-specific failure modes. At the datapoint level, 56.3% of the 200 most diverging samples are confirmed as genuine blindspots (sufficient caption, model fails to generate the concept). DPO post-training of SD 1.5 yields both a lower median and narrower spread in per-datapoint concept-energy differences, suggesting regularization toward the natural concept distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark a new generative model against baselines; instead, it introduces an evaluation methodology and applies it to four existing text-to-image models. The key comparison is methodological: Table 1 shows that the proposed RA-SAE + energy-difference pipeline is the only approach satisfying all four desiderata (concept-level specificity, unsupervised operation, scalability to ≥1,000 concepts, and detection of both suppression and exaggeration). Among the four generative models, SD 1.5 and SD 2.1 exhibit the most overlapping blindspot profiles (r = 0.82), reflecting their shared architecture and training pipeline, while PixArt and Kandinsky diverge more substantially (r = 0.41 and 0.46), indicating that blindspots are partly architecture-specific rather than purely dataset-driven. Suppressed blindspots (e.g., bird feeders, DVD discs, solid white on documents) are found across all models, whereas exaggerated blindspots (e.g., wood background texture, palm trees, shadows under animals) appear to be approximately universal. The DPO comparison (SD 1.5 vs. SD 1.5 + DPO) shows that preference-based fine-tuning narrows the concept-energy gap, reducing both over- and under-activation relative to the natural baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the theoretical result that self-supervised representations (specifically DINOv2) invert the data-generating process up to linear transformations (Zimmermann et al., 2021; Von Kugelgen et al., 2021), and operationalizes this via a 32,000-concept Relaxed Archetypal SAE (RA-SAE) trained with Top-K sparsity (Fel et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2025). It departs from existing generative model evaluation metrics (FID, CLIPScore, Precision/Recall) that operate at the image or prompt level, and from qualitative/anecdotal failure analyses, by introducing a formal energy-based framework (Definitions 1–3) that quantifies concept-level distributional discrepancies in an unsupervised, scalable manner. The closest prior work—GAN Dissection and Concept Bottleneck Models—requires human-defined concept vocabularies, whereas this paper's SAE-based approach discovers concepts automatically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic, unsupervised framework that identifies and quantifies fine-grained conceptual blindspots across multiple generative models, moving the field beyond anecdotal failure reports and coarse aggregate metrics. The open-source 32K-concept RA-SAE and interactive exploratory tool provide a reusable infrastructure for the community to audit concept-level fidelity in new models. Key limitations include dependence on DINOv2's representational capacity (concepts poorly captured by DINOv2 are invisible to the analysis) and a 10K-image sample that may underrepresent the long tail. Open questions include whether hierarchical SAEs would reveal multi-scale blindspot structure, whether energy profiles can be directly incorporated as training regularizers, and whether the observed suppression bias reflects a fundamental property of diffusion architectures or is an artifact of LAION-5B's distributional skew.
