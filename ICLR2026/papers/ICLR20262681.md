## Problem

Intrinsic-motivation-based exploration agents get trapped by unlearnable environmental noise (the noisy-TV problem), wasting samples on aleatoric uncertainty that no amount of data can reduce. Existing noise-robust methods require strong state-space priors or extensive data collection before they can reliably filter noise from signal, making early exploration dominated by uninformative observations. This paper asks whether rewarding model improvement rather than prediction error or novelty can provide immediate, principled noise robustness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a current observation o_t (e.g., 160×120 RGB frames in MiniWorld, 28×28 MNIST digits, or Atari screen frames) and an action a_t, and produces a scalar intrinsic reward r_t^i that is added to the extrinsic reward to update a policy via any standard RL algorithm (PPO in experiments). The dynamics model f_θ maps (o_t, a_t) → predicted next observation ô_{t+1}, while the error model g_φ maps (o_t, a_t) → a scalar estimate of the expected log-MSE of the previous dynamics model iteration. A fixed-size replay queue D of size d stores (o_t, a_t, ε_t^(τ)(o_{t+1})) tuples and is used to train g_φ, while a separate replay buffer B stores full transitions for training f_θ.

## Method

LPM uses a dual-network architecture where a dynamics model f_θ predicts the next observation and a separate error model g_φ predicts the expected prediction error of f_θ from its previous update iteration. The intrinsic reward is defined as the difference between the error model's prediction and the current model's actual log-MSE: r_t^i = g_φ^(τ)(o_t, a_t) − ε_t^(τ)(o_{t+1}), which directly measures how much the model improved on this transition. Both networks are updated simultaneously every N environment steps using their respective replay stores. Theoretically, the paper proves this reward is zero-equivariant and a monotone lower bound on information gain (Theorem 4.1), and that the expectation over the error model (rather than a pointwise error comparison) is necessary to guarantee this monotonicity (Theorem 4.2). Because unlearnable noise produces no model improvement, the intrinsic reward naturally decays to zero for noisy-TV transitions without requiring explicit uncertainty decomposition.

## Results

On the MountainCar continuous-control exploration task, LPM achieves 67.04% stochastic state coverage with only a 12.4% performance drop from its deterministic setting (76.50%), the smallest degradation among all seven baselines (ranging from 33.1% to 85.9% drop). In the 3D MiniWorld maze (160×120 RGB), LPM visits 1,347.6 states on average, outperforming the second-best method by 95.3 states (7.6%), and maintains this advantage under both state noise (1,434.0 states) and action noise (1,340.0 states) conditions. On Atari, LPM achieves superior extrinsic reward in 4 of 6 game-noise settings with minimal degradation (≤4.7% drop), while EME suffers a 100% collapse under noise. On Montezuma's Revenge, LPM obtains non-trivial extrinsic reward within 20M steps, whereas RND requires 50M steps and NGU fails entirely within 50M steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPM outperforms all seven baselines on noise robustness, which is the paper's central claim. In MountainCar (Table 2), LPM achieves the best stochastic coverage (67.04%) and the smallest performance drop (12.4%), while IDF suffers the worst degradation (85.9% drop) and AMA/EME lose over 60% of their deterministic performance. In the 3D MiniWorld maze, LPM leads in all three noise conditions (deterministic, state noise, action noise), whereas EDT (Jiang et al., 2025) is best only in the deterministic setting (1,276.1 states) and degrades under noise, and EME (Wang et al., 2024) drops from 89.16% to 32.46% coverage. On Atari, LPM wins 4 of 6 game-noise combinations; EME, the strongest clean Space Invader performer, completely fails under noise (100% drop), and RND (Burda et al., 2018) shows substantial losses across all noisy games. On Montezuma's Revenge, LPM achieves meaningful extrinsic reward within 20M steps while RND needs 50M steps and NGU (Badia et al., 2020) obtains zero reward within 50M steps. In the Noisy MNIST diagnostic experiment, LPM's intrinsic reward converges to zero for both deterministic and stochastic transitions within ~150 steps, compared to ~400 steps for AMA (Mavor-Parker et al., 2022), and EDT remains unable to suppress the stochastic signal. The ICM/IDF baseline (Pathak et al., 2017) is only briefly compared in the MiniGrid Lava Crossing appendix, where LPM matches ICM's success rate but both outperform remaining baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in intrinsic-motivation exploration, which rewards prediction error (curiosity methods like ICM, RND, Ensemble) or state novelty (episodic bonus methods like EDT, EME), by instead rewarding *model improvement* — the reduction in prediction error between successive model updates. The conceptual inspiration comes from neuroscience findings (Ten et al., 2021) that humans monitor learning progress during exploration, while the theoretical grounding draws on information-theoretic exploration (Houthooft et al., 2016; Bottero et al., 2022), showing the intrinsic reward is a monotone indicator of KL-divergence-based information gain. The dual-network architecture (dynamics model + error model) is the key architectural novelty, and the paper explicitly positions itself against the noisy-TV problem (Burda et al., 2018) and the epistemic/aleatoric uncertainty separation approaches (AMA, Mavor-Parker et al., 2022) that it argues are sample-inefficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The noisy-TV problem has been a long-standing open challenge in intrinsic motivation, and LPM offers a conceptually simple yet theoretically grounded solution that sidesteps the difficult epistemic/aleatoric uncertainty separation entirely. The practical impact is significant for real-world deployment where sensor noise and stochasticity are unavoidable, as LPM maintains performance under noise where baselines collapse (e.g., EME drops 100% on Space Invader). Limitations include the reliance on a model-based RL setting, the i.i.d. Gaussian observation model assumption in the theoretical analysis, and the added (though modest) computational cost of the error model. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-Gaussian or structured noise, whether the error model can be replaced by lighter-weight estimators, and how it performs in very high-dimensional continuous control beyond the MountainCar-scale experiments.
