## Problem

Prior work has identified individual attention patterns in LLMs—retrieval heads, attention sinks, diagonal traces—but these observations remain fragmented with no unifying explanation for why a given head adopts one pattern over another under the same attention formulation. The paper asks: what underlying mechanism determines the shape of an attention head's pattern, and can this mechanism be exploited for inference acceleration?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261972/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the query and key vectors extracted from a running transformer LLM (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B, Llama-2-7B) along with the RoPE channel frequencies and base hyperparameter. It computes a scalar q-similarity per layer as the cosine similarity among queries within a recent sliding window, then injects this score into existing allocation formulas: for KV cache compression it modulates CAKE's per-layer preference score via P'_l = P_l + α(1−S_l), and for pruning it adjusts ShortGPT's Block Influence via BI' = BI + β(1−q). The final outputs are per-layer cache budget fractions and a ranked list of layers to remove.

## Method

TAPPA reframes attention as a time series over decoding steps and shows that query self-similarity (q-similarity) is the key factor separating predictable patterns (smooth temporal evolution of high-attention indices) from unpredictable ones (retrieval-like random jumps), formalized in Proposition 4.1. Within the predictable regime, three sub-patterns are derived from the joint effect of queries, keys, and RoPE: re-access (vertical stability) requires high q-similarity plus a dominant low-frequency RoPE channel; sequential (diagonal) patterns require high self-similarity in both queries and keys, with RoPE's relative-position identity R_{t+1−(i+1)}=R_{t−i} yielding approximate shift-invariance along the (+1,+1) diagonal; and periodic sequential diagonals arise when a massive key channel at index m* imposes a repetition period T=2π/θ_{m*}=2πc^{2m*/d}. Seasonal patterns emerge when input periodicity L resonates with the dominant RoPE frequency (|Lθ_{m*}−2kπ|≤δ). The practical metric q-similarity is then used as a proxy: low q-similarity signals retrieval-like heads needing larger cache budgets, while high q-similarity signals redundant, prunable layers.

## Results

On LongBench KV cache compression, TAPPA achieves the best average score across all budget settings for both Llama-3.1-8B (47.21/48.43/48.73 at 512/1024/2048 budget) and Qwen-2.5-7B (45.78/47.72/48.46), consistently outperforming the strongest baseline CAKE by 0.02–0.3 points. On structured LLM pruning, TAPPA outperforms ShortGPT by 1.4–5.6 points in average accuracy across Llama-2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Qwen-2.5-7B at matched pruning ratios (e.g., 59.11% vs. 53.51% on Llama-3.1-8B at 28% pruning). The gains are most pronounced at tighter budgets and higher pruning ratios, where q-similarity-guided allocation preserves critical retrieval-oriented layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAPPA outperforms all five KV cache compression baselines (StreamingLLM, H2O, SnapKV, PyramidKV, CAKE) across both models and all three budget settings. The margin over CAKE (the closest competitor) is small but consistent (0.02–0.3 points), while gaps over H2O (2–4 points) and StreamingLLM (5–15 points) are substantially larger. For structured pruning, TAPPA beats ShortGPT by 1.4–5.6 points in average accuracy across all three models and pruning ratios, with the largest gain on Llama-3.1-8B at 28% pruning (59.11% vs. 53.51%). In the broader pruning comparison (Table 3), TAPPA also surpasses LLMPruner (57.38 vs. 48.40 avg), SliceGPT (57.38 vs. 42.48), and LaCo (57.38 vs. 50.51) on Llama-2-7B at comparable pruning ratios. The consistent wins across both downstream tasks validate that q-similarity, derived from TAPPA's temporal continuity analysis, is a reliable proxy for identifying which layers/heads require more resources.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAPPA unifies three previously fragmented lines of research: (1) empirical identification of individual attention patterns (sinks, diagonals, retrieval heads) by Xiao et al., Gu et al., and Barbero et al.; (2) mechanistic analyses attributing specific pattern shapes to RoPE frequency components in isolation; and (3) observations that query temporal continuity influences attention behavior (AttentionPredictor, Lee et al.). The paper's key departure is showing that query self-similarity (q-similarity) is the single differentiator between predictable and unpredictable patterns, and that within the predictable regime, the specific shape (re-access, sequential, seasonal) emerges from the joint interaction of queries, keys, and dominant RoPE channels rather than from any single factor. For downstream applications, TAPPA builds directly on CAKE's adaptive KV budget allocation and ShortGPT's Block Influence pruning score, augmenting each with a q-similarity term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unifying temporal framework gives practitioners a principled, interpretable signal (q-similarity) for deciding where to allocate cache budget or which layers to prune, rather than relying on ad-hoc heuristics. However, the practical gains over strong baselines are modest (e.g., ~0.02–0.3 points over CAKE on LongBench, ~0.5–5.6 points over ShortGPT on pruning), and the theoretical conditions (dominant single channel, bounded norms, near-resonance) may not always hold in practice. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-RoPE positional encodings (ALiBi, NoPE), whether the sufficient conditions for predictable patterns are also necessary, and whether q-similarity remains informative in non-autoregressive or diffusion-based language models.
