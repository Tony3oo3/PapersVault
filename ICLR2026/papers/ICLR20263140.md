## Problem

Existing distributionally robust RL algorithms are largely restricted to value-based methods in tabular settings, and the only continuous-space DR-RL algorithm (RFQI) relies on TV-distance uncertainty sets and a value-based FQI baseline, making it computationally expensive and limited to deterministic policies. No actor-critic-based DR-RL algorithm existed for offline learning in continuous action spaces, leaving a gap between the scalability of actor-critic methods and the robustness guarantees of DR-RL.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fixed offline dataset of (state, action, reward, next_state) tuples collected from a nominal environment via a behavior policy with ε-greedy exploration, along with a KL-divergence radius δ that defines the size of the transition uncertainty set. A VAE is trained on the (s, a, s') triples to generate m synthetic next-state samples per (s,a) pair, forming empirical transition measures that replace the unknown nominal distribution. The output is a stochastic policy π_φ(a|s) parameterized by a neural network, producing a continuous action distribution at each state.

## Method

DR-SAC derives a distributionally robust soft policy iteration with convergence guarantees by applying the strong duality of worst-case expectations over KL balls (Hu & Hong, 2013) to the soft Bellman operator, reducing the infinite-dimensional distribution optimization to a one-dimensional scalar problem over β. To achieve scalability, the paper exploits the interchange of minimization and integration (Rockafellar & Wets, 2009) to replace per-(s,a) scalar optimizations with a single shared functional optimization over a neural network g_η(s,a), cutting training time by over 80% compared to RFQI. A VAE is incorporated to estimate the unknown nominal transition distributions and generate synthetic next-state samples, resolving the double-sampling issue that makes empirical risk minimization infeasible under the nonlinear KL dual formulation in continuous spaces. The full algorithm alternates between empirical DR soft policy evaluation (using the functional Bellman operator with VAE-generated samples and multiple Q-critics) and DR soft policy improvement (KL minimization against the exponential of the DR soft Q-function), with automatic entropy temperature adjustment following SAC-v2.

## Results

DR-SAC achieves up to 9.8× higher average reward than the SAC baseline under 30% parameter reduction in LunarLander (240 vs. ~24.5). Under 20% length perturbation in Pendulum, DR-SAC outperforms SAC by 35%, and in HalfCheetah with 50% back_damping variation, DR-SAC maintains a stable average reward above 6300 while SAC drops below 5950. In terms of computational efficiency, DR-SAC requires at most 2.6× the training time of SAC, whereas RFQI requires up to 23.2× the training time of DR-SAC (e.g., 238 min vs. 36 min in LunarLander). The functional optimization reformulation saves over 80% training time compared to the exact per-(s,a) Bellman operator without sacrificing robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DR-SAC consistently outperforms SAC across all five environments and all perturbation types, with the largest gap (9.8×) in LunarLander under 30% engine power reduction. Against RFQI, the only other offline DR-RL algorithm applicable to continuous spaces, DR-SAC matches or exceeds robustness performance while requiring 80–95% less training time (5 gradient descent steps vs. 1000 for the inner optimization); RFQI's performance degrades sharply when its GD steps are reduced, indicating an inherently slower-converging loss structure. FQI and its robust variant RFQI perform poorly even in unperturbed Pendulum and HalfCheetah environments, attributed to their BCQ-based implementation conflicting with the epsilon-greedy data generation used in the experiments. DDPG and CQL are outperformed by DR-SAC under all tested perturbations (parameter shifts, observation noise, actuator noise), with DR-SAC showing the most stable performance as perturbation magnitude increases. Overall, DR-SAC is the only method that simultaneously achieves strong robustness, competitive nominal-environment performance, and practical training efficiency across all tested environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) entropy-regularized actor-critic framework into the distributionally robust RL (DR-RL) setting, building on the KL-divergence dual formulation from Iyengar (2005) and Hu & Hong (2013). It departs from the only prior offline DR-RL algorithm for continuous spaces, RFQI (Panaganti et al., 2022), which is value-based (FQI), uses TV-distance uncertainty sets, and suffers from deterministic policies and high sensitivity to Q-function errors. The paper also repurposes VAEs—previously used in offline RL for behavior policy estimation (Fujimoto et al., 2019)—as a mechanism to estimate unknown nominal transition distributions and resolve the double-sampling issue inherent in the nonlinear KL dual, a role not previously explored in DR-RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DR-SAC fills a clear gap by providing the first actor-critic DR-RL algorithm for offline continuous control, making distributional robustness accessible to the standard continuous-control benchmarks where value-based methods struggle. The 80%+ training time reduction over RFQI and the functional-optimization reformulation (5 gradient steps vs. 1000) substantially improve practical applicability to larger problems. Limitations include the assumption that only transition distributions are uncertain (rewards are fixed), the reliance on VAE estimation error that is not formally bounded, and evaluation on five relatively low-dimensional Gymnasium/MuJoCo environments. Open questions include whether the functional approximation preserves robustness guarantees in high-dimensional or partially-observed settings, and how the method extends to reward uncertainty or adversarial state perturbations.
