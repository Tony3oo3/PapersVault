## Problem

Existing differentially private trajectory generation methods, including PATEGAIL, apply uniform privacy noise across all samples regardless of their individual sensitivity, causing unnecessary utility degradation for low-risk trajectories and insufficient protection for highly identifying ones. This rigid noise assignment limits scalability and weakens the privacy-utility trade-off, particularly when generalizing across diverse real-world mobility patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262559/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each user's device locally stores a trajectory as a sequence of (location, timestamp) pairs and trains a local WGAN-GP discriminator on-device; the server only receives DP-perturbed scalar reward estimates (discriminator outputs) per state-action pair, never raw trajectories. The state is the full mobility history up to time t, and the action space follows the Exploration and Preferential Return (EPR) model. The global policy outputs a synthetic trajectory by sequentially sampling actions conditioned on the evolving state, with the entire generation process satisfying (ε,δ)-DP under a federated protocol.

## Method

PATEGAIL++ introduces a sensitivity-aware privacy budget allocation module that estimates per-sample sensitivity from local discriminator confidence (high confidence ≈ high sensitivity) and allocates the total ε budget inversely proportional to sensitivity, so high-risk samples receive stronger Laplace noise while low-risk ones are less distorted. To stabilize policy learning under this adaptive noise, the framework replaces the cross-entropy GAIL discriminator with WGAN-GP (Wasserstein distance + gradient penalty + optional spectral normalization), yielding smoother critic gradients and avoiding reward saturation. The aggregated reward incorporates a dynamics compensation term based on the variance of local rewards, and the global policy is optimized via PPO. The framework further extends to local differential privacy by having each user perturb their own reward before transmission, with per-user budgets allocated via homomorphically encrypted weights, eliminating the need for a trusted server.

## Results

On the GeoLife dataset (noise-free setting), PATEGAIL++ achieves JSD values of 0.0699 (Radius), 0.1046 (DailyLoc), 0.0130 (Distance), 0.0256 (G-Rank), and 0.0176 (I-Rank), operating under federated DP constraints while baselines are trained centrally without privacy. Under moderate noise (ε=0.10) on GeoLife, PATEGAIL++ reduces DailyLoc by ~29% relative to PATEGAIL (0.4914 vs. 0.6915) and cuts I-Rank from 0.2698 to 0.0607. Against white-box membership inference at noise=0.01, PATEGAIL++ achieves near-random AUC (0.4962) versus PATEGAIL's 0.7208, indicating substantially stronger privacy protection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PATEGAIL++ is compared against five non-private baselines (GAN, SeqGAN, Time-Geo, MoveSim, DiffTraj) and the direct predecessor PATEGAIL. Against MoveSim (the strongest non-private baseline), PATEGAIL++ wins on G-Rank (0.0256 vs. 0.0387) and matches on I-Rank (0.0176 vs. 0.0173), but trails on geometry metrics (Radius 0.0699 vs. 0.0311, DailyLoc 0.1046 vs. 0.0293, Distance 0.0130 vs. 0.0058); the authors note this is expected since MoveSim trains centrally without any privacy constraint. Against SeqGAN, PATEGAIL++ substantially outperforms on ranking semantics (G-Rank 0.0256 vs. 0.0752, I-Rank 0.0176 vs. 0.0329) while being competitive on geometry. Against GAN and Time-Geo, PATEGAIL++ dominates on all or nearly all metrics. The most informative comparison is PATEGAIL++ vs. PATEGAIL under matched noise: at ε=0.10 on GeoLife, PATEGAIL++ reduces DailyLoc by ~29% and I-Rank by ~77% (0.0607 vs. 0.2698), and at ε=1.00 it maintains lower DailyLoc (0.5608 vs. 0.6070) and better ranking fidelity. On privacy, PATEGAIL++ achieves near-random MIA AUC (~0.49) at all noise levels on GeoLife, while PATEGAIL's AUC remains above 0.72 until noise=1.00.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PATEGAIL (Wang et al., AAAI 2023), which itself adapts GAIL (Ho & Ermon, NeurIPS 2016) to the differential privacy setting for mobility trajectory generation. The key conceptual departure is replacing PATEGAIL's uniform Laplace noise with a sensitivity-aware allocation that assigns per-sample privacy budgets based on local discriminator confidence, and swapping the cross-entropy discriminator for a WGAN-GP critic to stabilize training under adaptive noise. The framework is further extended to the local differential privacy (LDP) setting, a direction not explored in PATEGAIL, enabling individual-level protection without a trusted server.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sensitivity-aware noise allocation addresses a practical inefficiency in DP-based trajectory generation: uniform noise wastes privacy budget on low-risk samples while under-protecting high-risk ones. The MIA results (near-random AUC vs. PATEGAIL's 0.72 at low noise) are the clearest evidence of improved privacy, but utility gains are inconsistent across datasets—on Telecom Shanghai, PATEGAIL++ underperforms PATEGAIL on several metrics at both low and high noise. The authors acknowledge that the discriminator-confidence-based sensitivity proxy may not capture semantic privacy risks (e.g., repeated visits to hospitals), leaving open the question of whether richer sensitivity signals can be incorporated without violating DP accounting.
