## Problem

Prior work on the distributed experts problem only achieved protocols for general ℓ_p losses in the broadcast/blackboard model, while the message-passing (coordinator) model was limited to ℓ₁ loss with an O(Ts) communication dependency that could not be parameterized by target regret. This paper closes the gap by providing the first protocol for general ℓ_p losses in the coordinator model and improving the communication-regret tradeoff for all p ≥ 1.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263065/cropped_page8_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each server j holds a local loss vector (ℓ_1(j,t), …, ℓ_n(j,t)) per timestep t, and the true expert loss is the ℓ_p norm across servers, L_i(t) = (Σ_j ℓ_i(j,t)^p)^(1/p), which no single server can compute. The protocol takes the ℓ_p parameter p and a target regret R as hyperparameters, and produces a sequence of expert selections per timestep along with a high-probability bound on total communication bits. Losses are assumed bounded (ℓ_i(j,t) ≤ 1 in the main result), and the protocol is self-delimiting so servers know when a round concludes.

## Method

The key insight is to embed ℓ_p losses into an ℓ_∞ structure via the max-stability property of exponential random variables: max_j ℓ_i(j,t)/e_i(j,t)^(1/p) is distributed as L_i(t)/e^(1/p), reducing the problem to tracking the largest scaled contribution. Since servers cannot find the maximum in the coordinator model, the protocol uses subsampling (each server speaks with probability ρ = 1/(R²T)·max(s^(1-2/p),1)) combined with thresholding so only values above a geometrically-scaled cutoff are sent. The unbounded variance of the 1/e^(1/p) estimator is controlled by taking a geometric mean of B = ⌈3/p⌉ independent exponential scalings, yielding an unbiased estimator with second moment bounded by 3^B. These estimated losses feed directly into a standard multiplicative weights update (MWU) algorithm, and the full protocol achieves regret O(R·s^(1/p)·√log n) with communication O((n+s)/R² · max(s^(1-2/p),1) · polylog(nsT)) bits.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1.3) achieves expected regret O(R·s^{1/p}·√(log n)) with total communication (n+s)/R² · max(s^{1-2/p}, 1) · polylog(nsT) bits for general ℓ_p losses in the coordinator model. For the bounded-loss warm-up (Theorem 1.1), the protocol attains near-optimal regret O(s^{1/p}·√(log n/T)) using O(sT) + nT·polylog(nsT) bits. Empirically on HPO-B, the distributed protocol outperforms MWU in reward (Figure 2b) and, for p=1, achieves lower communication than Jia et al. (2025) at comparable regret (Figure 2c).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Jia et al. (2025), which handles only ℓ₁ loss in the broadcast/blackboard model with communication (n/R² + Ts)·polylog(nsT). This paper improves on Jia et al. in three ways: (1) it extends to general ℓ_p losses (p ≥ 1) in the harder coordinator/message-passing model; (2) it eliminates the Ts term in the communication bound, replacing it with s/R², which is substantially smaller for large T (e.g., at R=O(1), communication drops from O(Ts) to O(s)); and (3) empirically on HPO-B with p=1, the proposed protocol achieves lower communication than Jia et al. at the same regret level (Figure 2c). The paper also compares against standard MWU as a centralized oracle; surprisingly, the distributed protocol achieves better reward than MWU, which the authors attribute to MWU learning-rate sensitivity rather than a fundamental advantage. Overall, the paper wins on all axes against Jia et al. (generality, model, and communication) and matches or exceeds MWU in empirical reward.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distributed experts problem studied by Jia et al. (2025), which handled only ℓ₁ loss in the message-passing model and general ℓ_p loss in the easier broadcast/blackboard model. The key technical departure is adapting the exponential-random-variable embedding (max-stability) from Jia et al.'s broadcast protocol to the coordinator model, where servers cannot publicly broadcast, requiring subsampling and thresholding to identify the maximum without excessive communication. The geometric mean estimator for variance reduction is borrowed from streaming norm estimation (Li, 2008; Woodruff & Zhou, 2021) and repurposed here as the first application of this technique to distributed online learning. The MWU framework itself is standard (Arora et al., 2012), and the paper's contribution lies in the distributed estimation layer that feeds it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first protocol achieving near-optimal regret for general ℓ_p losses in the coordinator model, closing a gap where prior work was restricted to ℓ₁ or to the broadcast model. The communication improvement from O(Ts) to O(s/R²) for the ℓ₁ special case is practically significant for long time horizons. Open questions include extending the framework to other structured losses (submodular, ℓ_∞/max), exploring alternative variance-reduction techniques beyond the geometric mean, and tightening the polylogarithmic factors. The empirical evaluation is limited to a single proof-of-concept on HPO-B, so practical validation at scale remains open.
