## Problem

Language models quickly saturate when fine-tuned on data similar to their pre-training distribution (e.g., MATH), yielding negligible gains from naive SFT. Existing data-adaptation strategies—embedding similarity to failed validation questions or gradient-based selection—remain anchored to coarse loss proxies and fail to capture the specific generation errors of a saturated model. The paper asks how a frontier LLM's metacognitive ability to reason about required skills can be leveraged to target a student model's precise skill deficiencies during fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a student model's step-by-step responses on a validation subset of MATH, a pre-constructed Skill-Map (128 skills mapped to training questions via GPT-4o-mini annotation), and a process reward model (Llama3.1-8B-PRM) for difficulty filtering. It outputs a targeted training set of ~9.5k QA pairs (either reweighted existing MATH examples or teacher-synthesized novel questions) plus the intermediate Missing-Skill-Profile, a per-question mapping from difficult validation items to the 2–3 skills the student failed to apply. The final artifact is a QLoRA fine-tuned student model (Llama-3.2-1B/3B-Instruct or Qwen2.5-3B) evaluated 0-shot across in-distribution and OOD math benchmarks.

## Method

STAT is a three-stage skill-targeted data adaptation pipeline. Stage 1 uses a process reward model with dual thresholds (τ₁=0.85 on final-step and mean reward, τ₂=0.7 on any intermediate step) to flag difficult validation questions without requiring ground-truth labels. Stage 2 prompts GPT-4o-mini to judge each student response and output the specific skills from a fixed 128-skill list that the model failed to apply, building a model-specific Missing-Skill-Profile. Stage 3 then constructs the training set in one of two ways: STAT-Sel up-weights existing MATH training questions whose Skill-Map entries overlap with the missing skills (sampling 5 questions per missing skill per difficult validation item), while STAT-Syn feeds 3 skill-matched reference questions plus the missing skill name to GPT-4o to generate novel questions, retaining only those with ≥2/3 answer consistency. The key distinction from embedding-based baselines is that selection is driven by explicit skill-level error diagnosis rather than semantic proximity, which the paper shows aligns far better with the model's actual weaknesses (e.g., basic algebraic manipulation rather than advanced polynomial concepts).

## Results

On Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, STAT-Sel achieves 51.5% on MATH (vs. 44.0% base) and 36.5% average across 9 benchmarks, while STAT-Syn reaches 50.2% on MATH and 37.2% average — a 6.7% gain over the base model. Naive SFT (MATH-Train) yields only 44.8% on MATH and 31.1% average. After adding GRPO on top, STAT-Syn reaches 41.3% average (vs. 31.8% for GRPO on base model), a 9.5% improvement. On Qwen2.5-3B, STAT-Syn achieves 44.6% average (vs. 39.4% base), and on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, STAT-Sel reaches 20.7% average (vs. 17.3% base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAT-Sel and STAT-Syn consistently outperform all six baselines across both Llama and Qwen models. Against naive SFT (MATH-Train, Hendrycks et al. 2021), which yields only 44.8% on MATH and 31.1% average for Llama-3.2-3B, STAT-Sel achieves 51.5% and 36.5% respectively — a 6.7% average gain. MATH-Augment (TIGER-Lab 2024) and MATH-Hard (Sun et al. 2024) provide marginal improvements (31.7% and 33.3% average), confirming that simply adding more solutions or restricting to harder questions is insufficient. Embedding-based selection (Embed-Sel, Li et al. 2025) and synthetic generation (Embed-Syn, Jung et al. 2025) achieve 32.8% and 33.0% average, still well below STAT's 36.5–37.2%, because they select data by semantic similarity rather than targeting specific missing skills. Most notably, STAT is complementary to GRPO (Shao et al. 2024): applying GRPO after STAT-Syn yields 41.3% average versus 31.8% for GRPO alone on the base model (a 9.5% gain), and on Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, STAT-SFT alone already surpasses GRPO-only training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the metacognitive skill-identification framework of Didolkar et al. (2024), which uses frontier LLMs to enumerate and label the skills required for mathematical problem solving. It departs from prior data-selection approaches (embedding similarity, gradient-based influence, difficulty filtering) by explicitly diagnosing *which skills* a student model fails to apply in its reasoning, then reweighting or synthesizing training data around those specific gaps. Compared to teacher-student distillation methods that transfer logits or full generations, STAT uses the teacher in a cheaper, more targeted role: identifying missing skills and generating skill-specific examples rather than providing step-by-step supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that skill-targeted SFT is complementary to GRPO (stacking yields ~4% additional gain over GRPO alone) makes STAT a potentially drop-in component for current post-training pipelines. The skill-level diagnostic (Missing-Skill-Profile) also provides an interpretable, model-specific view of where a model's reasoning breaks down, which is useful beyond training. Open questions include whether the identified skills correspond to identifiable mechanistic circuits, whether the approach extends beyond mathematics to domains like safety or code, and whether self-improvement (model as its own teacher) is viable given the modest teacher-student gap they document.
