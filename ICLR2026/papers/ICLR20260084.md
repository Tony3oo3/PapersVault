## Problem

Existing region-level MLLMs face a fundamental trade-off: crop-based methods (e.g., DAM) capture local details but lose global context, leading to errors like misidentifying a frog-shaped slipper as a real frog, while methods that incorporate the full image (e.g., GPT4RoI) pool region features into single vectors and lose fine-grained detail. Moreover, prior architectures are optimized to understand a single region in isolation, neglecting the ability to model interactions and perform compositional reasoning across multiple visual prompts simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model takes a full RGB image (processed via AnyRes with up to 16 tiles for multi-scale encoding), a set of N binary segmentation masks of the same spatial resolution, and a free-form text instruction. The masks are encoded through a lightweight zero-initialized convolutional block and added to ViT patch embeddings before encoding. The output is a text response that can range from a detailed multi-sentence region description, to a relational caption articulating interactions between multiple prompted objects, to a multiple-choice answer for compositional reasoning questions. For video extension, 16 uniformly sampled frames are used in place of a single image.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is an RoI-aligned feature replay technique that resolves the global-context vs. local-detail trade-off. The full uncropped image (with encoded mask prompts) is processed by a ViT backbone via AnyRes to produce a single holistic feature map; RoI-Align then extracts high-resolution feature vectors for each prompted region directly from this global map, so the local features are inherently context-aware without requiring a separate cropped-image pass. Both the global context tokens and the replayed local tokens are concatenated and fed into a PerceptionLM-based LLM for generation. Training follows a two-round data pipeline: Round 1 builds a 456K fine-grained recognition dataset from ImageNet-21K to improve category-level perception, and Round 2 constructs a 414K relation dataset from Panoptic Scene Graph annotations (via Qwen2.5-72B) to teach multi-prompt relational reasoning and compositional QA. The model is fine-tuned with AdamW (lr 1e-5, cosine decay, batch 64) on the combined ~2.5M-sample dataset, and the same architecture transfers zero-shot to video by simply sampling 16 frames.

## Results

On the paper's own GAR-Bench-VQA, GAR-8B achieves an overall score of 59.9, surpassing GPT-4o (53.5) by 6.4 points and approaching o3 (61.3); GAR-1B scores 50.6, edging out InternVL3-78B (50.5). On GAR-Bench-Cap, GAR-8B reaches 62.2 overall, outperforming Gemini-2.5-Pro (59.3) and GPT-4o (51.5). On DLC-Bench with GPT-4o as judge, GAR-1B scores 77.1 (vs. DAM-3B's 72.6), and on MDVP-Bench Natural, GAR-8B achieves 178.6 (vs. DAM-3B's 87.0). Notably, zero-shot GAR-8B outperforms in-domain VideoRefer-7B on VideoRefer-Bench^Q (72.0 vs. 71.9 overall).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAR-8B (59.9) decisively outperforms GPT-4o (53.5) by 6.4 points on GAR-Bench-VQA overall, with particularly large margins on Texture (+27.6) and Relation (+6.9). GAR-1B (50.6) surpasses the much larger InternVL3-78B (50.5) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (52.8 is close but GAR-1B wins on Texture 69.0 vs 65.5 and Non-Entity 62.3 vs 44.3). On GAR-Bench-Cap, GAR-8B (62.2) beats Gemini-2.5-Pro (59.3) and GPT-4o (51.5), while GAR-1B (57.5) also exceeds GPT-4o. On DLC-Bench with GPT-4o judge, GAR-1B (77.1) and GAR-8B (77.0) both outperform DAM-3B (72.6) by ~4.5 points. On MDVP-Bench Natural, GAR-8B (178.6) nearly doubles DAM-3B (87.0). For video, zero-shot GAR-8B (72.0) marginally beats in-domain VideoRefer-7B (71.9) on VideoRefer-Bench^Q, while on LVIS GAR-8B (93.6 Sim / 88.7 IoU) surpasses DAM-8B (89.0 / 77.7) and PAM-3B (88.6 / 78.3). The main losses are against thinking-mode models (o3, Gemini-2.5-Pro†) on GAR-Bench-VQA, where their extended reasoning provides a 1.4–4.3 point advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAR builds directly on the region-level MLLM line initiated by DAM (Lian et al., 2025) and PAM (Lin et al., 2025b), which introduced mask-prompted localized captioning, and on earlier box-based approaches like GPT4RoI and GLaMM that incorporated global images but lost local detail through feature pooling. The key architectural departure is the RoI-aligned feature replay technique, which repurposes the RoI-Align operation from Mask R-CNN to extract context-rich local features from a full-image AnyRes feature map, elegantly resolving the global-context vs. local-detail trade-off that plagues prior methods. The multi-prompt compositional reasoning dimension and the GAR-Bench evaluation protocol represent a deliberate extension beyond the single-region, single-prompt paradigm that dominates existing region MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental limitation in region-level MLLMs—the inability to simultaneously leverage global scene context and fine-grained local detail—and provides a clean, lightweight solution (RoI-aligned feature replay) that is architecturally simple and could be adopted broadly. The finding that even the most powerful general MLLMs (GPT-4o, o3, Gemini-2.5-Pro) consistently fail at region-level tasks regardless of how regions are specified (masks, outlines, boxes, crosses) suggests a deeper architectural gap in current MLLMs beyond mere input formatting. Key limitations include the image-only training (video extension is zero-shot and weak on temporal tasks) and the observation that models still struggle with relationships involving more than two objects, pointing to open questions about scaling compositional reasoning to complex multi-entity scenes.
