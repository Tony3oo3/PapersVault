## Problem

Existing audio-visual benchmarks for MLLMs either emphasize a single modality while treating the other as auxiliary, focus on short clips that underrepresent long-term temporal dependencies, or integrate audio and vision in a logically inconsistent manner. As a result, no prior benchmark rigorously evaluates whether a model can perform genuine synergistic reasoning that requires both modalities to be jointly and consistently integrated to reach a unique, correct answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264683/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a real-world video (480p–1080p, ranging from a few seconds to 30 minutes, sourced from YouTube and Bilibili, with no embedded subtitles) and its native audio track with a four-option multiple-choice question. The model must output a single option letter; the ground-truth annotation additionally provides a step-by-step reasoning chain where each atomic step explicitly labels its modality (audio or visual), the specific evidence extracted, and the inference drawn, enabling fine-grained analysis of where and how a model's reasoning diverges from the correct chain.

## Method

OmniVideoBench is constructed through a multi-stage pipeline: (1) curated collection of 628 videos across 8 categories and 68 subcategories, restricted to post-June-2024 publications to avoid training-set overlap, with strict criteria on dynamic visual content, audio-visual alignment, and absence of subtitles; (2) manual annotation of ~2,500 QA pairs spanning 13 task types (e.g., temporal reasoning, causal inference, music understanding, ego reasoning), each requiring that the answer be correct, unique, and unanswerable from either modality alone; (3) a three-stage quality-assurance filter using Gemini-2.0-Flash to remove single-modality-solvable questions, DeepSeek-V3.1 to remove text-cue-exploitable questions, and human annotators for final verification, yielding 1,000 validated QA pairs; (4) annotation of atomic reasoning chains (avg. 5.68 steps, 54% visual / 46% audio) per question. Design rules enforce consistent option format, bounded semantic distance between distractors (formalized via symmetric difference of semantic units), and relevance of all distractors to the video content. The benchmark is then used to evaluate 15+ closed- and open-source omni-modal MLLMs, visual-only VLMs, and a text-only LLM baseline, with additional ablations on ASR transcripts, frame density, and open-ended vs. multiple-choice formats.

## Results

On OmniVideoBench, no evaluated model reaches the 60% passing threshold. The best-performing model, Gemini-3.0-Pro, achieves 61.80% average accuracy, followed by Gemini-2.5-Pro at 58.90%, while human annotators score 82.69%. Open-source models perform near the 25% random-guessing baseline: Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B reaches 38.40%, Qwen2.5-Omni-7B only 29.30%, and the text-only DeepSeek-V3.1 baseline scores 27.60%. Music-dominated videos are the hardest audio type (Gemini-2.5-Pro drops to 38.46%), and long videos (>10 min) cause significant degradation for most models except Gemini-2.5-Pro.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Closed-source Gemini models dominate: Gemini-3.0-Pro (61.80%) outperforms Gemini-2.5-Pro (58.90%) by ~3 points, and both substantially exceed Gemini-2.0-Flash (41.50%). The best open-source model, Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B (38.40%), trails Gemini-2.5-Pro by over 20 points, while most other open-source models (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B at 29.30%, Baichuan-Omni-1.5 at 30.70%, VideoLLaMA2-7B at 29.20%) hover near the 25% random baseline. Visual-only Qwen2.5-VL models (29.50–31.80%) and the text-only DeepSeek-V3.1 (27.60%) confirm that the benchmark cannot be solved without genuine audio-visual integration. Music is the weakest audio type for every model (Gemini-2.5-Pro drops from 61.66% on speech to 38.46% on music), and long-video performance (>10 min) degrades sharply for Gemini-2.0-Flash (34.87%) and Qwen3-Omni (35.11%) but remains stable for Gemini-2.5-Pro (55.94%). The ~24-point gap between the best model (61.80%) and human performance (82.69%) underscores the benchmark's difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio-visual QA benchmarking line (AVQA, Music-AVQA, AVTrustBench, Daily-Omni, WorldSense) by addressing three specific gaps: prior benchmarks rely on short clips (≤60 s), treat audio as auxiliary rather than co-equal with vision, or lack explicit reasoning annotations. OmniVideoBench departs from this lineage by requiring genuine cross-modal synergy on videos up to 30 minutes, enforcing that no single modality suffices (verified via Gemini-2.0-Flash filtering), and annotating each QA pair with atomic step-by-step reasoning chains that specify modality, evidence, and inference. The 13 task types and the semantic-distance constraint on distractors represent a more rigorous evaluation protocol than prior audio-visual benchmarks, which typically use simpler multiple-choice or classification formats.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that no evaluated model reaches a passing 60% accuracy (best: Gemini-2.5-Pro at 58.90%), while humans achieve 82.69%, exposing a substantial gap in genuine audio-visual reasoning. Two findings carry broad implications: (1) music-dominated audio is a universal weakness (Gemini-2.5-Pro drops to 38.46%), suggesting current audio encoders remain speech-centric and lack general acoustic-semantic grounding; (2) Qwen2.5-Omni-7B performing worse than the visual-only Qwen2.5-VL-7B with ASR transcripts challenges the assumption that adding a raw audio stream always improves multimodal reasoning. Open questions include whether natively multimodal architectures can close the music-understanding gap, how dense temporal sampling interacts with long-context limits, and whether the MCQ format (which inflates scores by ~14% over open-ended QA) masks deeper reasoning deficits.
