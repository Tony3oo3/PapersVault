## Problem

Existing unsupervised cross-modal flow estimation methods rely exclusively on appearance-based (photometric) supervision, which is inherently ambiguous in textureless or repetitive regions and degrades substantially under large viewpoint changes. The paper asks whether reliable explicit motion supervision can be introduced using only unaligned cross-modal image pairs, without any ground-truth cross-modal flow annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264708/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of unaligned images from different modalities (RGB, near-infrared, or thermal) at resolutions ranging from 512×320 to 704×512, and outputs a dense 2D optical flow field mapping pixels from modality B to modality A. No ground-truth flow is required at training time; instead, supervision is generated on-the-fly via monocular depth estimation and geometry-aware reprojection from single-view images. The framework is network-agnostic and supports plugging in different flow backbones (RAFT, GMA, FlowFormer, SEA-RAFT).

## Method

DCFlow decouples the joint cross-modal flow problem into two independently optimized sub-tasks: a U-Net-based modality transfer network trained with perceptual (VGG) loss on warped images, and a RAFT-based flow estimation network trained with direct L1 supervision on synthetic mono-modal flow pairs generated by a geometry-aware lifting-and-reprojection pipeline. A photometric consistency mask and an outlier-robust loss (discarding the top-20% highest-residual pixels) filter unreliable synthetic supervision caused by depth errors and rendering artifacts. After 10,000 iterations of decoupled training, a cross-modal consistency constraint is introduced: random affine transformations (rotation ±3°, scaling [0.95,1.05], translation ±24 px) are applied to the cross-modal pair, and the network is jointly optimized to produce geometrically consistent flow predictions before and after the transformation, providing direct cross-modal motion supervision without any labeled data.

## Results

On the five cross-modal flow benchmarks (MS² RGB-T, MS² NIR-T, MS² RGB-NIR, VTD RGB-T, RNS RGB-NIR), DCFlow achieves EPEs of 3.46, 4.53, 0.96, 3.65, and 1.90 respectively, with corresponding F1 outlier rates of 35.89%, 49.81%, 5.00%, 48.49%, and 13.05%. Among all unsupervised and large-scale pretrained approaches, DCFlow consistently achieves the best performance on both EPE and F1 across every dataset. Compared to the strongest supervised baseline (SEA-RAFT), DCFlow remains competitive, e.g., 3.46 vs. 1.65 EPE on MS²(RGB-T), despite using no ground-truth cross-modal flow labels during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCFlow decisively outperforms both unsupervised baselines: on MS²(RGB-T), it reduces EPE from 19.25 (NeMAR) and 18.84 (UMF-CMGR) to 3.46, a roughly 5.5× improvement, with F1 dropping from ~99.8% to 35.89%. Against large-scale pretrained methods, DCFlow achieves 42.0%, 36.2%, 82.4%, 42.4%, and 18.8% lower EPE than MINIMA across the five datasets, and similarly outperforms CrossRAFT (e.g., 3.46 vs. 6.21 EPE on MS² RGB-T). Against supervised baselines (RAFT, GMA, FlowFormer, SEA-RAFT), DCFlow remains competitive but still trails by roughly 1.8–2.8 EPE on the harder RGB-T and NIR-T pairs, while nearly matching them on the easier RGB-NIR pair (0.96 vs. 0.21–0.25 EPE). The paper attributes the residual gap to the absence of direct cross-modal ground-truth flow supervision, and notes that DCFlow's advantage over pretrained methods stems from learning directly from real-world unlabeled data rather than synthetic cross-modal datasets with a domain gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCFlow departs from the dominant paradigm in unsupervised cross-modal flow estimation (NeMAR, UMF-CMGR, RFNet), which relies exclusively on photometric/appearance-based supervision and suffers from ambiguity in textureless or repetitive regions. It repurposes geometry-aware single-image flow synthesis—previously explored only in mono-modal settings (Watson et al., Aleotti et al., Liang et al.)—as a source of explicit motion supervision for the cross-modal problem, combined with a decoupled optimization strategy conceptually inspired by SSHNet's split optimization for homography estimation. The cross-modal consistency constraint (enforcing flow invariance under known affine transforms) extends the self-supervised transformation-consistency idea from mono-modal flow (Liu et al. 2020) to the cross-modal setting, where no prior work had applied it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that explicit mono-modal motion supervision, when properly decoupled from modality transfer, can close most of the gap to supervised cross-modal flow estimation without any labeled cross-modal flow—challenging the assumption that cross-modal supervision must come from cross-modal data. The network-agnostic design (validated across RAFT, FlowFormer, GMA, SEA-RAFT) and the repurposed benchmark (five modality pairs from three public datasets) make the contribution broadly reusable. Open questions include whether the reliance on monocular depth estimation for data synthesis can be eliminated, whether the approach extends to more challenging modality pairs (e.g., LiDAR-thermal), and whether the remaining gap to supervised methods (~2× EPE) can be closed with richer consistency constraints or multi-view synthesis.
