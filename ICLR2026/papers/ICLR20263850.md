## Problem

Recovering the energy functional that governs population dynamics from marginal snapshots at discrete time points is challenging because individual trajectories are unavailable. Prior JKO-based methods either restrict the energy to potential-only form with input-convex architectures (JKOnet) or require precomputed optimal transport couplings that break end-to-end training (JKOnet*). No existing approach combines the full JKO variational step with inverse optimization in a single adversarial objective that supports general free-energy functionals without architectural constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes independent Monte Carlo samples from marginal distributions ρ_k at K+1 discrete time points, where each snapshot may contain a different number of particles and no temporal pairing exists between consecutive snapshots. It outputs a parameterized free-energy functional (neural networks for potential V and interaction kernel W, plus a scalar diffusion coefficient θ3) that can be used to predict the next-step distribution ρ_{k+1} from ρ_k via a single JKO update. The transport maps between snapshots are also learned as part of the model, parameterized by standard MLPs or ResNets rather than ICNNs.

## Method

iJKOnet casts energy recovery as an inverse optimization problem: the loss is a min-max objective where the inner minimization over transport maps T^k approximates a JKO step for a candidate energy J, and the outer maximization over J drives it toward the ground-truth functional J*. This yields a conventional end-to-end adversarial training loop (gradient descent on transport maps, gradient ascent on energy parameters) with no convexity constraints on the transport map architecture. The energy is parameterized as a free-energy functional (potential + interaction + scaled negative entropy), with the entropy term handled via a change-of-variables formula and the Kozachenko-Leonenko nearest-neighbor estimator. A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3.1) establishes that under strict convexity and smoothness of the modified potential, the recovered potential's gradient converges to the ground truth at a rate proportional to the inverse-optimization gap.

## Results

On the 5D Embryoid Body dataset (leave-two-out, W₂ distance), iJKOnet_{t,V} achieves the best performance with 0.983±0.037 (t₁) and 0.849±0.021 (t₃), substantially outperforming JKOnet*_{V} (1.145±0.033, 2.529±0.014) and all non-JKO baselines. On the 100D dataset (leave-one-out, MMD), iJKOnet_{t,V} achieves 0.097±0.002 (LO-t₃) and 0.085±0.024 (w/o LO), outperforming JKOnet* (0.235±0.006, 0.229±0.052) by roughly 2×, though DMSB remains stronger (0.040±0.020, 0.032±0.003). The paper notes that iJKOnet_V achieves results 'on par with DMSB' in the all-time setting while using a simpler simulation-free optimization routine.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against JKOnet* (Terpin et al., 2024), iJKOnet_{t,V} wins decisively on the 5D W₂ metric: 0.849 vs. 2.529 at t₃ (a ~3× improvement) and 0.983 vs. 1.145 at t₁. On the 100D MMD metric, iJKOnet_{t,V} also outperforms JKOnet* by roughly 2× across all leave-one-out splits (e.g., 0.097 vs. 0.235 at LO-t₃). Against non-JKO baselines, iJKOnet_{t,V} beats Vanilla-SB (1.49/1.55), TrajectoryNet (2.03/1.93), and MMSB (1.27/1.57) on the 5D W₂ metric, and outperforms NLSB and MIOFLOW on the 100D MMD metric. However, DMSB (Chen et al., 2023) remains the strongest baseline on the 100D setting (0.032–0.042 MMD vs. iJKOnet's 0.085–0.137), though the paper argues iJKOnet uses a simpler simulation-free routine without trajectory caching. The original JKOnet (Bunne et al., 2022) is only compared in moderate-dimensionality experiments (Appendix B.2) due to its computational cost, and is outperformed by iJKOnet.

## Relation to Prior Work

iJKOnet sits at the intersection of the JKO scheme (Jordan et al., 1998), the JKOnet line of work for learning population dynamics (Bunne et al., 2022b; Terpin et al., 2024), and inverse optimization theory (Chan et al., 2025). It departs from JKOnet by removing the ICNN architectural constraint and the bi-level optimization structure, and from JKOnet* by eliminating the need for precomputed discrete OT plans, instead solving the JKO step directly via an inner minimization over transport maps. The key conceptual move is recasting energy recovery as a min-max inverse optimization problem, which yields a standard adversarial training loop with no restrictive network architecture requirements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide quality bounds (Theorem 3.1) for JKO-based solvers recovering energy functionals from data, establishing a theoretical foundation that was previously absent. The discovery that the JKOnet* codebase inadvertently used temporally correlated (paired) samples rather than the intended unpaired snapshots is practically significant, as it suggests prior JKO-based results may have been optimistic. Open questions include extending guarantees beyond potential energy, handling time-dependent interactions and birth-death dynamics, and whether the method scales to higher-dimensional single-cell data where entropy estimation becomes unreliable.
