## Problem

When the number of reasoning hops at test time exceeds the training distribution, LLMs exhibit a sharp performance drop in chain-of-thought reasoning, yet the internal mechanisms driving these failures remain poorly understood. Prior work attributed the issue to single-hop error accumulation but did not identify which token-level decisions are responsible or what internal circuit dynamics cause them, leaving no principled basis for targeted intervention.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard CoT reasoning prompt (with in-context demonstrations) and the model's intermediate residual-stream hidden states during autoregressive generation. At each generated token, it monitors predictive entropy; when entropy exceeds a fixed threshold, a trained classifier selects attention heads to deactivate, and the model re-samples the token with those heads zeroed. The output is a corrected reasoning chain where token-level errors at key positions are rectified without altering the model weights.

## Method

The paper first shows that hop-generalization errors concentrate at a small set of task-specific error types rather than being uniformly distributed, then uses Logit Lens, head knockout, and circuit analysis to reveal a competition mechanism: correct processing heads (cp) and erroneous processing heads (ep) in shallow-to-middle layers construct competing reasoning signals that answer-writing heads in deeper layers aggregate, with ep heads tipping the balance toward incorrect predictions. Building on this, TCR (Test-time Correction of Reasoning) maintains a compact candidate set of shared ep heads per model (8–10 heads), fine-tunes a Qwen2.5-0.5B classifier with LoRA to predict which head to knock out given the input context, and applies an entropy-threshold detector to trigger intervention during decoding. When triggered, TCR selects the top-3 predicted heads, knocks each out separately, and uses majority vote to produce the corrected token, requiring no post-training on downstream data and remaining compatible with off-the-shelf LLMs.

## Results

TCR consistently improves reasoning hop generalization across all four LLMs, with average accuracy gains of +6.8% (Qwen2.5-7B), +4.9% (Phi-3), +6.4% (LLaMA3-8B), and +1.6% (Qwen3-8B) over unmodified baselines. The oracle-detector variant TCR-gold demonstrates substantially larger potential, boosting Qwen2.5-7B average accuracy from 41.7% to 61.3% (+19.6%) and LLaMA3-8B from 37.5% to 49.3% (+11.8%). The largest single-task gains include +12.1% on Parity-NL (Qwen2.5), +16.5% on MOAS (LLaMA3), and +14.1% on ObjC (Phi-3). DoLa, a decoding-based hallucination mitigation method, yields only marginal or negative effects in these reasoning settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is DoLa (Chuang et al., 2024), a decoding-based method that contrasts layer outputs to improve factuality. TCR outperforms DoLa on all four models in average accuracy: Qwen2.5-7B (48.5% vs. 42.6%), Phi-3 (37.1% vs. 24.5%), LLaMA3-8B (43.9% vs. 37.1%), and Qwen3-8B (82.1% vs. 75.8%). Notably, DoLa actually degrades performance below the unmodified baseline on Phi-3 (24.5% vs. 32.2%) and Qwen3-8B (75.8% vs. 80.5%), suggesting that layer-contrast decoding is poorly suited to multi-hop reasoning where errors are structural rather than factual. TCR's gains are most pronounced on tasks with high baseline error rates (e.g., MDM, MOAS, LLC), while on tasks where models already perform well (e.g., Parity-NL and NumS for Qwen3-8B at >98%), the headroom for improvement is minimal. The TCR-gold variant, which uses an oracle error detector, further confirms that the correction mechanism itself (knocking out ep heads) is the primary source of improvement rather than the detection step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability toolkit (head knockout from Michel et al. 2019, residual-stream circuit analysis from Wang et al. 2023, Logit Lens) to the under-studied setting of multi-hop CoT reasoning, where prior tools were designed for single-token or few-token prediction tasks. It departs from the dominant 'error accumulation' narrative (Dziri et al. 2023) and post-training remedies (rule-following fine-tuning in Hu et al. 2025, looped architectures in Fan et al. 2025) by identifying an internal competition mechanism between correct and erroneous processing heads, and by proposing a test-time intervention (TCR) that requires no retraining. The head-selector component borrows the LoRA fine-tuning paradigm from Hu et al. 2022 but applies it to a novel multi-label classification objective over attention-head identities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that a small set of shared erroneous processing heads across diverse tasks actively suppresses correct reasoning trajectories provides a concrete, actionable mechanistic explanation for a well-known but poorly understood failure mode, and suggests that targeted test-time intervention can recover a substantial fraction of lost accuracy without any model retraining. However, the practical gains of TCR (≈5–7% average) are moderate, the mechanistic analysis relies on small sample sets (|S_corr|=|S_err|=10), and all experiments are confined to models ≤10B and classical CoT, leaving open whether the competition mechanism persists in larger or reasoning-RL-trained models (e.g., Qwen3's long-CoT mode, DeepSeek-R1). The shared-ep-head finding is promising for cross-task generalization of interventions, but the current in-distribution task set is limited, constraining confidence in the head selector's robustness.
