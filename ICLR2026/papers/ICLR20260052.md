## Problem

Existing contamination detection methods for LLMs—loss-based criteria, score calibration with external models, and explicit overlap checks—require access to training data, extensive parameter tuning, or produce unreliable results on large models. There is a need for an automated, scalable, and interpretable method that works across diverse datasets and architectures without prior knowledge of the model's training corpus.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260052/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a gray-box language model (requiring only token probability access) and a candidate dataset of text sequences, performing exactly two forward passes per sample (one baseline, one with prepended in-context examples). It outputs a single dataset-level contamination score expressed as a percentage (the fraction of samples whose confidence decreases with added context), requiring no model-specific or dataset-specific calibration thresholds. The per-sample confidence delta (logprob_in-context minus logprob_baseline) is also available for finer-grained analysis.

## Method

CoDeC exploits the observation that in-context examples from an unseen dataset improve model confidence by providing useful distributional information, while examples from a memorized dataset disrupt existing memorization patterns and reduce confidence. For each sample x in the candidate dataset, the pipeline computes a baseline average log-likelihood, then prepends n randomly sampled in-context examples from the same dataset (excluding x) and recomputes the log-likelihood on x alone. The per-sample score is the sign of the confidence delta, and the dataset-level contamination score is the fraction of samples with negative delta. This self-referential design—using the model's own predictions as the baseline rather than external reference models—makes the method parameter-free, model-agnostic, and sensitive to both direct memorization and contamination via related or augmented data distributions.

## Results

CoDeC achieves a cumulative dataset-level AUC of 99.9% for separating seen from unseen datasets across 12 models (410M–56B parameters), with per-model AUCs at or above 99.6%. In contrast, the strongest baseline (Zlib ratio) reaches only 89.6% cumulative AUC, while Vanilla Loss and Min-K% score 75.7% and 78.5% respectively. The method requires only two forward passes per sample and produces interpretable percentage-based contamination scores (training datasets cluster near 100%, unseen datasets below 20–60%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDeC decisively outperforms all three baselines across every model evaluated. Against Vanilla Loss (Fu et al., 2024), CoDeC gains 24.2 percentage points in cumulative AUC (99.9% vs. 75.7%), with the gap widening for OLMo models where Vanilla Loss drops to ~65%. Against Min-K% (Shi et al., 2024), CoDeC leads by 21.4 points cumulatively (99.9% vs. 78.5%); Min-K% performs best on Nemotron v2 9B (97.6%) but still falls short. Against Zlib ratio (Carlini et al., 2021), the closest competitor, CoDeC still achieves a 10.3-point cumulative advantage (99.9% vs. 89.6%), with the largest per-model gaps on OLMo (100% vs. 78–81%). The baselines' scores for seen and unseen datasets overlap significantly, making confident contamination detection unreliable, whereas CoDeC produces a sharp, consistent separation enabling model-agnostic comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoDeC builds on the well-studied connection between in-context learning and implicit gradient descent (Brown et al., 2020; von Oswald et al., 2023; Dai et al., 2023), repurposing ICL as a self-referential probe for contamination rather than as a generation mechanism. It departs from prior contamination detection methods—loss-based criteria (Shi et al., 2024; Carlini et al., 2021), reference-model calibration (Maini et al., 2024), and perturbation-based checks (Mattern et al., 2023)—by requiring no external models, no threshold tuning, and no held-out identically distributed data. The dataset-level framing follows Maini et al. (2024), but CoDeC removes their requirement for additional reference samples by using the model's own predictions as the baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoDeC offers a practically deployable, two-forward-pass method that produces interpretable percentage scores, making it straightforward to integrate into existing benchmark evaluation pipelines and model auditing workflows. Its ability to detect contamination from augmented, rephrased, or distributionally related data (not just exact membership) addresses a gap that n-gram overlap checks and strict MIA methods leave open. Open questions include whether adversarial training strategies could suppress the CoDeC signal, whether the method extends cleanly to sample-level inference, and whether theoretical guarantees can be established for the score's behavior across architectures and training regimes.
