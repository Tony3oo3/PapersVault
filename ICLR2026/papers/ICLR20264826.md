## Problem

Point cloud-based human motion capture faces a fundamental trade-off: point-based representations retain geometric detail but are fragile under noise and occlusion, while skeleton-based abstractions provide robust structural priors but discard surface information needed to resolve rotational ambiguities. No existing intermediate representation simultaneously achieves expressiveness and robustness across diverse sensor types and challenging real-world conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264826/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 256×3 point clouds (after Farthest Point Sampling and normalization) as time series from LiDAR or depth cameras, and optionally multi-view point clouds that are fused via confidence-weighted selection derived from learned anchor-level segmentation. It outputs SMPL parameters—shape β∈ℝ¹⁰, axis-angle pose θ∈ℝ⁷², and global translation T∈ℝ³—defining a 6890-vertex mesh, with the intermediate Sparkle representation consisting of 24 optimized skeletal joints and 32 PCA-selected surface anchors that explicitly factorize kinematic structure from surface geometry.

## Method

SparkleMotion constructs the Sparkle representation through two specialized modules: a Point-aligned Skeleton Tracker (PST) that uses a PointNet+BiGRU backbone for coarse joint prediction followed by segmentation-aware local refinement with a shared-weight PointNet predicting residual offsets per joint, and a Skeleton-guided Anchor Estimator (SAE) that initializes 32 surface anchors via a precomputed linear joint-to-anchor mapping, then refines them through cross-attention between joint features (queries) and anchor features (keys/values). The Sparkle-based SMPL Solver then performs a parameter-free geometric initialization by decomposing each bone's rotation into swing (aligning template to observed bone direction) and twist (aligning template to observed anchors around the bone axis) components via Rodrigues' formula, followed by a lightweight cross-attention network that learns a non-linear correction to handle kinematic singularities and ambiguous anchor evidence. This two-stage analytical-then-learned design, combined with the explicit kinematic-geometric factorization, reduces learning complexity and enables robust generalization across 11 datasets spanning LiDAR, depth cameras, multi-person interactions, and multi-view setups.

## Results

On the four general-scenario datasets (FreeMotion, Sloper4D, FreeMotion-OBJ, NoiseMotion), SparkleMotion achieves the best local joint/vertex error of 59.0/73.2 mm and angle error of 9.66° on FreeMotion, outperforming the strongest baseline LiveHPS++ (61.9/75.3 mm, 15.40°). The advantage is most pronounced under noise and occlusion: on NoiseMotion, SparkleMotion reduces global vertex error from 64.5 mm (LiveHPS++) to 45.8 mm and angle error from 10.63° to 7.57°. On close-interaction datasets, SparkleMotion cuts angle error by roughly 40–50% (e.g., 6.75° vs. 18.47° on InterHuman), and on the FIFA multi-person tracking benchmark it achieves HOTA 0.9975 vs. ByteTrack's 0.8451.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparkleMotion consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the closest competitor LiveHPS++ (ECCV 2024), it reduces angle error by 37–50% on close-interaction datasets (e.g., 6.75° vs. 18.47° on InterHuman) and by 29% on noisy FreeMotion-OBJ (8.49° vs. 15.85°). On cross-sensor generalization (GTA-Human-Point, HuMMan-Point), SparkleMotion beats LiveHPS++ by 8–16 mm in local vertex error and by up to 8.9° in angle error (HuMMan-Point: 12.60° vs. 21.47°), while also surpassing VoteHMR and PointHPS which do not report global metrics. On multi-view benchmarks, SparkleMotion outperforms FreeCap (AAAI 2025) by 13.7 mm in local joint error and 2.6° in angle error on FreeMotion-MV. For multi-person tracking on the FIFA soccer dataset, the SparkleMotion pipeline achieves HOTA 0.9975 and MOTA 0.9883, substantially exceeding ByteTrack (HOTA 0.8451, MOTA 0.9485) with fewer identity switches (18 vs. 33). LiDARCap and LiveHPS, the earlier LiDAR-specific methods, are clearly outperformed by 20–40 mm in local error and 5–7° in angle error across all four general-scenario datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the swing-twist rotation decomposition from HYBRIK (Li et al., 2021) for analytical SMPL pose initialization and the kinematic-aware temporal optimizer from LiveHPS++ (Ren et al., 2024a) for joint refinement. It departs from prior point-cloud MoCap work by rejecting the binary choice between raw point-based representations (noisy, unstructured) and skeleton-only abstractions (oversimplified), instead proposing Sparkle—a unified representation that redesigns both components: a point-aligned skeleton tracker that explicitly models point-to-joint correspondences via segmentation, and a skeleton-guided anchor estimator that refines linearly-initialized surface anchors through cross-attention. The kinematic-geometric factorization (joints for robustness, anchors for expressiveness) is the conceptual departure from prior methods that treat these as separate pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SparkleMotion provides a practical, real-time (60 FPS visualization) point-cloud MoCap system that generalizes across LiDAR, depth cameras, and multi-view setups without retraining, addressing a key fragmentation in the field where methods are typically sensor-specific. The data-efficiency result (65.2 mm global error with 0% target-domain data vs. 84.0 mm baseline) suggests the structured prior genuinely reduces sample complexity, which could accelerate deployment to new sensor types. However, the framework remains locked to the SMPL parametric model, limiting it to human subjects and standard body shapes; generalizing the Sparkle factorization principle to non-human articulated objects or implicit surface representations is the most compelling open direction the authors identify.
