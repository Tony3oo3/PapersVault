## Problem

Diffusion LLM inference is computationally expensive because every denoising iteration processes the full input sequence, yet most mask tokens contribute little to the output and their intermediate representations change only subtly across successive iterations. Existing acceleration methods like DualCache cache KV tensors for tokens outside the current block but still compute over all tokens within the block, leaving substantial redundant computation unexploited.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt concatenated with l mask tokens (specifying the desired generation length) and produces the fully unmasked sequence through iterative denoising. Generation proceeds in semi-autoregressive blocks (e.g., 64 or 256 tokens), where within each block, tokens are progressively unmasked by confidence over multiple iterations. The framework operates on pre-trained dLLMs (LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B) without any retraining, and the skip configuration (ratio and layer positions) is the only tunable hyperparameter at inference time.

## Method

ES-dLLM is a training-free inference acceleration framework that selectively skips low-importance token positions in early Transformer layers. It estimates per-position importance as a weighted combination (α=0.5) of the previous iteration's confidence score and the normalized L1-norm variation of hidden states between consecutive iterations. At designated layers (e.g., 1/8 and 1/4 of total depth), only the top-(1−r_l) fraction of positions by importance score are forwarded through the remaining layers, while the rest are skipped and their cached KV and hidden states are reused. Caches are updated in-place via scatter operations for processed positions only, and periodic full refreshes of prompt or block tokens prevent error accumulation. This reduces approximately 60% of total FLOPs compared to DualCache while maintaining comparable generation quality.

## Results

ES-dLLM achieves throughput of up to 226.57 TPS on LLaDA-8B-Instruct and 308.51 TPS on Dream-7B-Instruct (NVIDIA H200 GPU), delivering 5.6×–16.8× speedup over the vanilla implementations and 1.20×–1.85× over the state-of-the-art DualCache method. Generation quality is preserved, with performance score differences ranging from −1.83 to +1.21 compared to the original models across GSM8K, MATH, BBH, HumanEval, and MBPP. The method reduces approximately 60% of total FLOPs by skipping low-importance tokens in early Transformer layers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla LLaDA-8B baseline, ES-dLLM achieves 5.6×–16.8× higher throughput while matching or slightly exceeding accuracy (e.g., 76.95 vs. 76.72 on GSM8K, 37.80 vs. 36.59 on HumanEval). Against the vanilla Dream-7B baseline, ES-dLLM delivers 5.6×–13.5× speedup with performance scores within 1.5 points (e.g., 77.94 vs. 79.00 on GSM8K). Compared to DualCache (Fast-dLLM), ES-dLLM consistently wins on throughput (1.20×–1.85× faster) and often matches or beats accuracy, suggesting that DualCache's aggressive full-block recomputation can introduce noise; for instance, ES-dLLM scores 76.95 vs. 76.35 on GSM8K and 37.80 vs. 35.37 on HumanEval. Against Sparse-dLLM, ES-dLLM achieves 1.25×–2.18× higher TPS with comparable or better accuracy (e.g., 28.06 vs. 28.10 on MATH, 51.13 vs. 49.13 on BBH). The paper also shows ES-dLLM is orthogonal to both parallel decoding and sparse attention, with combined integration yielding up to 7.56× speedup over DualCache on Dream-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

ES-dLLM extends the DualCache mechanism from Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025), which introduced KV caching for semi-autoregressive diffusion LLMs, by adding a token-level early-skipping step within the current processing block. While prior dLLM acceleration work (dKV-Cache, dLLM-Cache, Sparse-dLLM, DPad) focuses on reusing or sparsifying cached tensors and attention patterns, ES-dLLM targets a different axis of redundancy: the observation that most token positions undergo negligible intermediate-state change across successive denoising iterations, enabling selective skipping of low-importance positions in early Transformer layers. The method is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to and composable with parallel decoding and sparse attention, rather than a replacement for them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Open-source dLLMs remain substantially slower than comparable ARMs, which limits their practical adoption; ES-dLLM's 5.6×–16.8× speedup over vanilla inference and up to 1.85× over the best caching baseline narrows this gap meaningfully without any retraining. The paper's quantitative analysis of confidence and hidden-state variation across iterations provides a reusable diagnostic for identifying further redundancy in iterative generative models. Key open questions include whether the heuristic importance score can be replaced by a learned predictor, whether adaptive skip ratios tuned to real-time variation can close the gap between the ~60% FLOPs reduction and the observed 1.2–1.85× wall-clock speedup (constrained by memory bandwidth), and whether training strategies can be co-designed with the early-skipping mechanism to eliminate the divergence from the original training distribution.
