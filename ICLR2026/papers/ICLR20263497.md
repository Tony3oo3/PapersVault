## Problem

Test-time scaling paradigms like Best-of-N sampling and multi-round thinking dramatically improve LLM reasoning but incur prohibitive latency from generating multiple full-length reasoning traces. While speculative decoding is a lossless acceleration technique, no prior benchmark has systematically compared model-based, training-based, and N-gram-based speculative decoding methods under the structured, repetition-rich conditions that test-time scaling creates, leaving open which architecture best exploits the inherent redundancies in reasoning traces.

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds reasoning questions (120 samples from AIME 2024/25, MATH-500, and GPQA) into target models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, Qwen3-8B) wrapped in test-time scaling frameworks (BoN with N=4 trajectories, multi-round thinking with M=2 rounds), with a maximum output of 15,000 tokens and an 80% thinking budget. N-gram-based methods additionally consume the generation history or prompt context to build suffix automata, trie structures, or top-K token caches. The primary outputs are the wall-time speedup ratio relative to vanilla autoregressive decoding and the mean accepted tokens (MAT) per speculative step, with the generated text guaranteed lossless (identical output distribution to the target model).

## Method

The paper constructs the first unified benchmark evaluating 9 speculative decoding methods across three categories under two test-time scaling frameworks with consistent protocols. Model-based SpS uses a smaller homologous model (Qwen3-0.6B) as drafter; training-based EAGLE-3 attaches lightweight auto-regressive heads to intermediate layers; N-gram-based methods (PLD, REST, Lookahead, PIA, SAM, Recycling) retrieve or cache token sequences from generation history using suffix automata, tries, or top-K logit caches. A novel hybrid SAM[EAGLE-3] dynamically switches between SAM's suffix matching and EAGLE-3's trained heads when the matched suffix is too short. The key empirical finding is that SAM's suffix automaton achieves progressive acceleration across reasoning turns (33–45% speedup increase from turn 1 to turn 2) by reusing repetitive patterns, outperforming EAGLE-3 by 38% on DSL-8B under greedy decoding, while the hybrid SAM[EAGLE-3] achieves the highest overall speedup (up to 4.70×) by combining semantic alignment with repetition capture.

## Results

Under multi-round thinking with greedy decoding (T=0) on Qwen3-8B, the hybrid SAM[EAGLE-3] achieves the highest overall speedup of 3.49× (MAT 4.76), outperforming EAGLE-3 (2.91×, MAT 4.38) and SAM alone (2.28×, MAT 2.37). On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B (T=0), SAM[EAGLE-3] reaches 3.97× speedup, while SAM alone (2.66×) actually surpasses EAGLE-3 (1.93×) by 38%. The model-based SpS achieves the highest MAT (7.07 on QW3-8B) but the lowest speedup (0.87×), confirming that draft-model overhead negates its acceptance advantage. Token N-gram methods (SAM, PIA) exhibit progressive acceleration across turns, with SAM's speedup increasing 33% from turn 1 to turn 2 (2.13× → 2.83×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The hybrid SAM[EAGLE-3] outperforms all individual baselines in speedup across every setting tested. Against the training-based EAGLE-3, SAM[EAGLE-3] gains 20% speedup on QW3-8B (3.49× vs 2.91×) and 106% on DSL-8B (3.97× vs 1.93×) under greedy multi-round thinking. Against the best token N-gram method SAM, the hybrid adds 53% speedup on QW3-8B (3.49× vs 2.28×) by filling in EAGLE-3's weakness on repetitive patterns. The model-based SpS, despite achieving the highest MAT (7.07), is the only method that fails to accelerate (0.87×), as the 0.6B draft model's inference cost offsets the reduced target-model calls. Among N-gram methods, SAM dominates token-level approaches (2.28× vs PIA 1.63×, Lookahead 1.59×, PLD 1.74×), while REST is the weakest (1.13×) due to limited datastore overlap with reasoning content. Recycling achieves the highest MAT among N-gram methods (3.01) but trails SAM in speedup (2.15× vs 2.28×) because its tree-structured verification and 81-token speculation introduce higher per-step latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two well-studied areas: speculative decoding for lossless inference acceleration and test-time scaling frameworks (BoN, multi-round thinking). Prior speculative decoding benchmarks (e.g., Xia et al., 2024) evaluated methods on generic generation tasks, overlooking the unique redundancy patterns inherent in reasoning traces. The paper builds directly on SAM (suffix automaton-based N-gram speculation) and EAGLE-3 (trained auto-regressive draft heads), introducing a hybrid SAM[EAGLE-3] combination, and systematically compares 9 methods across model-based, training-based, and N-gram-based categories under test-time scaling-specific conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first benchmark to isolate how speculative decoding methods interact with the repetitive, structured outputs of test-time scaling, providing practitioners with concrete guidance on which acceleration strategy to deploy for reasoning models. The finding that progressive acceleration accumulates across turns for N-gram methods (SAM, PIA) suggests a compounding efficiency benefit for long multi-turn reasoning that prior work had not quantified. Key open questions include designing more refined hybrid strategies that better exploit SAM's long-suffix matching potential, mitigating the temperature sensitivity of token N-gram methods, and extending evaluation to batched and multi-GPU settings where the compute-bound regime may shift the optimal method.
