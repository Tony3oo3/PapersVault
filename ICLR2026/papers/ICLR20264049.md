## Problem

Existing LLM-EC frameworks for combinatorial optimization heuristic design rely on manually predefined evolutionary computation optimizers (e.g., fixed crossover-then-mutation workflows), which constrain the exploration of diverse heuristic algorithms. Additionally, their single-task training schemes limit generalization to unseen problem sizes, with performance gaps widening as instances grow larger. This paper addresses both limitations by shifting the optimization target from heuristics to the optimizers themselves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264049/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The trained meta-optimizer is an LLM-generated Python function with a fixed signature (population, utility, language_model, subtask_prompt, subtask) that takes a population of structured individuals—each comprising a code string, a natural-language strategy description, and a utility score—and returns the best individual discovered. At inference, the meta-optimizer is applied to new COP instances (e.g., TSP-500/1000, BPP with different capacities) for 10 iterations, producing executable heuristic code that is then run to generate solutions. The unified individual format is shared between heuristic and optimizer populations, enabling the same function structure to operate recursively at both levels.

## Method

MoH implements a two-level iterative optimization: an outer loop where the current meta-optimizer generates M candidate heuristic-optimizers via (self-)invocation of an LLM, and an inner loop where each candidate evolves heuristics across N downstream tasks. Each generation step follows a three-stage procedure—individual selection from the population, natural-language idea generation to guide exploration, and code implementation generation—enabling the LLM to leverage its reasoning beyond simple code-space sampling. The best candidate heuristic-optimizer is selected by a size-weighted multi-task utility score (weighted by problem size to emphasize large-instance performance) and becomes the next meta-optimizer. This multi-task, meta-optimization design allows the discovered optimizers to adopt diverse strategies (EC, ACO, PSO, Simulated Annealing, Tabu Search, or hybrids) rather than being locked into a single EC template, facilitating broader heuristic search-space exploration and stronger cross-size generalization.

## Results

On TSP, MoH achieves the lowest average optimality gap among all LLM-based methods: 11.792% for constructive heuristics and 0.391% for improvement heuristics (KGLS setting), outperforming the next-best baselines MCTS-AHD (12.568% / 0.411%) and HSEvo (13.379% / 0.466%). On Online BPP, MoH achieves 0.453% average excess bins, beating the best baseline EoH (0.680%) by ~33% relative improvement. On CVRP and Offline BPP, MoH attains the best objective values across all tested sizes (e.g., 27.512 on CVRP-200 vs. 28.041 for MCTS-AHD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoH consistently outperforms all five LLM-EC baselines (FunSearch, EoH, ReEvo, HSEvo, MCTS-AHD) across every COP tested. On TSP-KGLS, MoH achieves 0.391% average gap, beating the closest competitor MCTS-AHD-KGLS (0.411%) by 0.020 pp and HSEvo-KGLS/ReEvo-KGLS (0.466%) by 0.075 pp; the gap widens substantially on large instances (TSP-1000: 1.363% vs. 1.389% for MCTS-AHD). On Online BPP, MoH (0.453% avg excess bins) dominates EoH (0.680%), FunSearch (0.825%), and traditional Best Fit (1.607%) / First Fit (1.729%) across all capacity and size settings. On CVRP, MoH achieves the best objective at every size (e.g., 27.512 at size 200 vs. 28.041 for MCTS-AHD and 28.224 for ReEvo). Against neural solvers on TSPLib, MoH-KGLS achieves 0.40% average gap (sizes 200–1000) versus 0.53% for SIL, 3.56% for LEHD, and 8.77% for POMO. MoH does not surpass the exact Concorde solver (0% gap by definition) but approaches it on small instances (0.000% at TSP-20/50 with KGLS), while OR-Tools degrades to 5.011% gap at TSP-1000 where MoH-KGLS maintains 1.363%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-EC paradigm for heuristic design (EoH, ReEvo, HSEvo, MCTS-AHD) by introducing a bi-level optimization structure: an outer meta-optimization loop that evolves the heuristic-optimizer itself, rather than fixing it to a predefined EC workflow. The recursive (self-)invocation mechanism draws on self-improving code generation (STOP, Zelikman et al. 2024) and LLM self-improvement (Huang et al. 2022), but applies them at the optimizer level rather than the heuristic level. The key conceptual departure is treating the optimization strategy (EC, ACO, PSO, etc.) as a searchable object rather than a fixed scaffold, enabling the LLM to discover and compose diverse optimization paradigms autonomously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoH demonstrates that meta-optimization of the search strategy yields consistently better heuristics than any fixed EC framework, with the strongest gains on large-scale instances where cross-size generalization is most challenging. The generated meta-optimizers are interpretable (natural language strategy + code) and can adopt non-EC paradigms (ACO, PSO, Simulated Annealing, Tabu Search), suggesting the LLM search space for optimizers is richer than previously explored. Limitations include the additional computational overhead of the outer loop, weak cross-problem generalization (a single meta-optimizer does not transfer well across structurally different COPs), and reliance on a specific LLM (GPT-4o-mini) whose capabilities may shift with model updates. Open questions include whether more efficient search strategies can reduce the outer-loop cost and whether the framework extends to broader optimization problem classes beyond classical COPs.
