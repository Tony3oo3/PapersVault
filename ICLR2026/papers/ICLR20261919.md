## Problem

Existing structured pruning methods for LLMs operate on a single model, causing significant performance degradation that typically requires expensive post-training to recover. This paper asks whether pruning can be reformulated as a search over a *family* of fine-tuned variants of the same base model, where layers are selectively cut from one variant and stitched from another, thereby aggregating task-specific capabilities that no single variant possesses alone.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained base model (e.g., Llama2-7B/13B), a pool of K fine-tuned candidate models derived from that base (e.g., LM, Math, Code variants), a small calibration set drawn from multiple-choice benchmarks (PIQA, WSC, CSQA, MMLU), and a target sparsity ratio (e.g., 9/32 or 10/40 layers). It outputs a pruned model whose per-layer configuration specifies, for each of the l positions, whether the layer is removed, which candidate model supplies it, and—if multiple candidates are selected—task-arithmetic merge weights. The optimizer returns a Pareto front of configurations across task objectives, from which a single best-performing model is selected.

## Method

GPTAILOR formulates structured pruning as a zero-order optimization problem over a combinatorial search space defined by three operations: (1) a binary removal vector selecting exactly ⌈l·s⌉ layers to delete, (2) per-layer selection vectors choosing which of K candidate models contributes each retained layer, and (3) task-arithmetic merging (with configurable interpolation factors) when multiple candidates are selected at the same position. The search is driven by SMAC with Hyperband-style multi-fidelity scheduling (calibration data size as fidelity, budgets {100, 300, 1000}) and a Random Forest surrogate, while ParEGO's Chebyshev scalarization handles the multi-objective trade-off across tasks. The key enabling observation is that fine-tuned variants from a shared initialization remain close in parameter space (small Frobenius distance), so layer-level merging via task arithmetic requires no explicit permutation alignment. Ablations confirm that all three operations are necessary: layer removal alone drops performance to 44.83%, adding cross-model selection without merging worsens it to 43.20%, and the full pipeline with merging recovers 48.55% average on the 7B setting.

## Results

On Llama2-7B (27.1% layers removed), GPTailor achieves an average score of 48.55 across 14 benchmarks, preserving 92.2% of the dense LM model's performance (52.63). On Llama2-13B (24.6% layers removed), it reaches 54.33, retaining 97.3% of the dense model's 55.86. On Llama3-8B (9 layers pruned), the method retains 84.55% of the original performance (53.78 vs. 63.61), still substantially outperforming ShortGPT (39.94). The method also achieves the best Avg* (excluding calibration benchmarks) scores: 43.73 (7B) and 51.30 (13B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPTailor outperforms all four baselines (LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT, LaCo, ShortGPT) on both Llama2-7B and Llama2-13B across the full 14-benchmark suite. On 7B, the gap over the strongest baseline ShortGPT is 6.31 points in Avg (48.55 vs. 42.24) and 7.76 in Avg* (43.73 vs. 35.97); on 13B, the gap is 3.84 in Avg (54.33 vs. 50.49) and 3.87 in Avg* (51.30 vs. 47.43). LLM-Pruner and SliceGPT suffer the most severe degradation (Avg drops to ~32–33), largely due to their dimensionality-reduction or gradient-based removal strategies that break cross-task coherence. Notably, even the layer-removal-only ablation of GPTailor (44.83 Avg on 7B) surpasses ShortGPT (42.24), indicating that the zero-order search over which layers to remove is itself superior to ShortGPT's Block Influence heuristic. On Llama3-8B, GPTailor retains 84.55% of dense performance versus ShortGPT's 62.79%, a 13.8-point absolute gap, confirming robustness to next-generation architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant single-model structured pruning paradigm (LLM-Pruner, SliceGPT, LaCo, ShortGPT), which relies on hand-designed importance metrics to remove layers from one model, by reframing pruning as a zero-order optimization problem over a *family* of fine-tuned variants of a base model. It builds directly on the multi-fidelity SMAC/Hyperband optimization framework from Su & Geiping (2025) for efficient search, on Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022) as the core layer-merging operation, and on ParEGO (Knowles, 2006) for multi-objective scalarization. The key conceptual departure is that LaCo also performs layer merging but only within a single model, whereas this paper's search space explicitly supports cross-model layer selection and merging, which the authors show is critical for performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: retaining 97.3% of Llama2-13B performance while removing ~25% of parameters without any post-training is a strong result for deployment-constrained settings, and the method generalizes to Llama-3 8B (84.55% retention). However, the approach requires multiple fine-tuned variants of the same base model to be available, limiting applicability when only a single checkpoint exists (though the layer-folding variant partially addresses this). Open questions include how search cost scales with larger candidate pools, whether the method transfers to architectures without clear layer modularity, and why next-generation models like Llama-3 appear substantially less compressible despite similar parameter counts.
