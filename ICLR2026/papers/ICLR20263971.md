## Problem

Existing matrix factorization mechanisms for multi-epoch differentially private SGD leave a significant gap between upper and lower bounds on the factorization error, with no explicit factorization known to achieve the optimal rate. Banded Square Root (BSR) factorization provides implicit guarantees whose dependence on bandwidth is unclear, while the theoretically optimal factorization requires solving an expensive numerical optimization. This paper closes the gap by proving a new lower bound and constructing an explicit factorization that matches it asymptotically.

## Input / Output

The mechanism consumes per-example clipped gradient vectors (clipped to norm ζ), fresh Gaussian noise vectors drawn at each step, and a fixed set of p banded inverse Toeplitz coefficients (c₀, …, c_{p-1}) that define the noise correlation structure. At each of n training steps, the private gradient estimate is formed by convolving the current and p−1 previous noise vectors with these coefficients, requiring only a buffer of p noise vectors in a streaming setting. The output is the final model parameter θ_n after n SGD updates with momentum and weight decay, satisfying (ε,δ)-differential privacy under k-fold participation with b-min separation.

## Method

BISR (Banded Inverse Square Root) inverts the design choice of prior banded methods: rather than imposing a p-banded structure on the correlation matrix C itself (as in BSR), it imposes the banded structure on C^{-1}. Concretely, one computes the square root C of the SGD workload matrix A_{α,β}, inverts it to obtain C^{-1}, zeros out all entries below the p-th subdiagonal, and inverts the resulting banded matrix to define C^p; the factorization is then A = B^p C^p with B^p = A(C^p)^{-1}. Because (C^p)^{-1} is a p-banded lower triangular Toeplitz matrix, the noise injection reduces to a convolution with p fixed coefficients, enabling O(p) memory in streaming and FFT-accelerated computation offline. The paper proves that with p = O(b log b), BISR achieves error O_β(√k log n + k) for α=1 and O_{α,β}(√k) for α<1, matching a new lower bound and establishing asymptotic optimality. For the low-memory regime where p is small, the authors further propose BandInvMF, which keeps the banded inverse Toeplitz structure but optimizes the p coefficients numerically via gradient descent on an upper bound of the (k,b)-sensitivity, achieving lower RMSE than closed-form BISR at small bandwidths.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (3-Block ConvNet, (9,10⁻⁵)-DP, 10 epochs), BISR achieves 61.8% test accuracy with amplification and 56.2% without, substantially outperforming BSR (49.8%/45.2%) and Band-MF (50.0%/45.8%). On IMDB (BERT-base, same privacy budget), BISR reaches 89.58% accuracy (amplified), exceeding BSR (88.11%) and Band-MF (88.31%). In the RMSE comparison across matrix sizes (Figure 3), BISR consistently matches or exceeds BSR, with the gap widening at high participation (k=16), while Band-MF achieves slightly lower RMSE at k=16 but is impractical beyond n=4096 due to its optimization cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BSR (Kalinin & Lampert, NeurIPS 2024), BISR achieves a clear and consistent win: 61.8% vs 49.8% on CIFAR-10 (amplified) and 89.58% vs 88.11% on IMDB, with the advantage growing at higher participation counts (k=16). Against Band-MF (McKenna, ICLR 2025), BISR wins on actual model accuracy (61.8% vs 50.0% on CIFAR-10 amplified) despite Band-MF achieving slightly lower RMSE at k=16; however, Band-MF requires solving a computationally expensive optimization problem that becomes impractical beyond n=4096. Against BLT (Dvijotham et al., FOCS 2024), BISR achieves comparable RMSE but has the practical advantage of supporting momentum and weight decay, whereas BLT is only defined and analyzed for prefix-sum matrices. The proposed Band-Inv-MF variant achieves lower RMSE than BISR in the small-bandwidth regime but does not translate this into higher model accuracy, suggesting RMSE alone is insufficient as a proxy for downstream performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the square root factorization (A = C²) of Henzinger et al. (2024) and the Banded Square Root (BSR) factorization of Kalinin & Lampert (2024), which imposed a banded structure on the correlation matrix C. The key conceptual departure is shifting the banded constraint from C to its inverse C⁻¹, yielding the Banded Inverse Square Root (BISR) method. This inversion of the structural constraint, while seemingly minor, enables explicit error bounds with clear bandwidth dependence and reduces the noise injection to a simple p-element convolution. The multi-epoch participation framework itself originates from Choquette-Choo et al. (2023a), who formulated it as an optimization over banded matrices; BISR provides a closed-form alternative to that optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BISR closes a significant theoretical gap by proving asymptotic optimality of the multi-epoch factorization error, matching previously separated upper and lower bounds. Practically, the method is simpler to implement (a fixed-coefficient convolution, amenable to FFT) and more memory-efficient (only p noise vectors stored) than numerical optimization approaches like BandMF, which become intractable beyond n≈4096. Open questions remain: the constant-factor gap between BISR and numerical methods (BandMF, BLT) is not closed; BandInvMF achieves lower RMSE than BISR in the low-memory regime but this does not yet translate to accuracy gains; and optimizing inverse-correlation coefficients while accounting for privacy amplification by subsampling is an unexploited direction.
