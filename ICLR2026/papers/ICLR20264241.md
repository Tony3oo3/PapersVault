## Problem

Existing deep graph clustering methods face two intertwined limitations: GNN-based local message-passing fails to capture long-range dependencies without causing oversmoothing, and inherent redundancy and noise in graph data produce non-compact, non-robust node representations. Prior approaches either perform only local augmentation or overlook the redundancy problem, leaving the intrinsic low-dimensional structure of node embeddings unexploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264241/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attributed graph with node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×F} and binary adjacency matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×N}, and produces fused low-rank node embeddings Z^F ∈ R^{N×D} (dimension D=500–1500) from which K-means yields cluster assignments. A key design detail is that the graph diffusion matrix S (personalized PageRank with teleport probability α) is precomputed once and sparsified, so the per-epoch cost is linear in N rather than quadratic. The low-rank subspace dimension K (default 64) and anchor queue size M (default 128) control the compactness and consistency granularity respectively.

## Method

CoCo is a three-stage self-supervised framework. First, it extracts complementary node representations from a local view (original graph Laplacian) and a global view (graph diffusion Laplacian) using disentangled generalized Laplacian smoothing filters with theoretically optimal low-pass parameters (Theorem 1), followed by unshared MLP encoders. Second, it enforces compactness by fitting a Gaussian Mixture Model via EM to the concatenated local and global embeddings, learning a shared low-rank subspace Λ ∈ R^{2N×K} (K≪D) that strips redundancy and noise; the reconstructed embeddings are injected back through a residual connection to preserve trainability (Theorem 2 guarantees individual mass conservation and maximal variation preservation). Third, it performs consistency learning by computing softmax-normalized cosine similarity distributions of each node against a dynamically updated memory bank of M anchor samples in both views, then minimizing the symmetric KL divergence between the two distributions to align local and global semantics without requiring negative samples.

## Results

On the five main graph clustering benchmarks, CoCo achieves the best performance across all four metrics (ACC, NMI, ARI, F1). Headline numbers include 79.36% ACC / 60.71 NMI on Cora, 79.27% ACC / 68.85 NMI on AMAP, 78.85% ACC / 55.00 NMI on BAT, 58.87% ACC / 34.10 NMI on EAT, and 59.68% ACC / 30.12 NMI on UAT. On the node classification task, CoCo also leads all baselines, e.g., 80.06% on WikiCS, 89.62% on Computers, and 93.34% on Photo. The EM-based low-rank reconstruction adds only ~4% overhead to total training time, with performance stabilizing after 10 iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoCo outperforms all 29 baselines on all five clustering datasets across all four metrics. The largest margins appear on BAT, where CoCo (78.85% ACC) surpasses the runner-up GraphLearner (75.50%) by 3.35% and MAGI (59.54%) by 19.31%, and on Cora where it beats MAGI (76.21%) by 3.15% ACC and 3.88% F1. On AMAP, CoCo edges out CCGC (77.25%) and GraphLearner (77.24%) by ~2% ACC while also leading on NMI (68.85 vs. 67.44) and ARI (60.94 vs. 58.14). On the more challenging EAT and UAT datasets, CoCo still leads by 1.65–3.34% ACC over the best baselines. For node classification (Table 7), CoCo achieves the best accuracy on all five datasets (e.g., 80.06% on WikiCS vs. 79.58% for GTCA, 93.34% on Photo vs. 92.99% for GCA), confirming the generalizability of the learned representations beyond clustering.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoCo builds on the disentangled GNN architecture of Wu et al. (2019), which separates graph convolutional filters from learnable weight matrices, and extends it to operate on both the original adjacency matrix (local view) and a personalized PageRank diffusion matrix (global view). The low-rank compactness module repurposes GMM-based subspace learning—previously applied to video-language representation (Jin et al., 2022a)—for the first time in graph clustering, replacing the more common autoencoder or PCA-based dimensionality reduction. The consistency learning strategy departs from the dominant contrastive learning paradigm in deep graph clustering (MVGRL, ProGCL, GraphLearner, MAGI) by aligning full similarity distributions via symmetric KL divergence rather than pulling/pushing individual node pairs, positioning it as a distribution-level rather than instance-level alignment mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent weaknesses in GNN-based graph clustering—oversmoothing from local message passing and sensitivity to feature/edge noise—through a principled combination of graph diffusion and GMM-based denoising, with theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1–2) on filter optimality and reconstruction quality. The finding that distribution-based consistency learning outperforms both MSE regression and InfoNCE contrastive loss across all five datasets suggests that aligning relational structure (similarity distributions) is more informative than aligning individual representations for clustering. However, all experiments are on relatively small graphs (≤10K nodes), and the authors themselves flag temporal graphs and single-cell genomics as unexplored extensions, leaving open whether the linear-complexity advantage and noise robustness hold at scale or in dynamic settings.
