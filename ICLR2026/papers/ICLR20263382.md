## Problem

Large multimodal models trained with R1-style reinforcement learning produce lengthy reasoning chains that incur high token costs, slow response times, and can accumulate errors that degrade correctness. While humans solve problems efficiently through concise, goal-directed 'sketch-style' reasoning that captures only key logical steps, no prior method explicitly incentivizes this reasoning style in multimodal models without sacrificing answer accuracy.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a structured, numbered-list reasoning trace (sketch-style) followed by a final answer. The sketch-style output is constrained to a concise numbered format that preserves only essential logical steps, reducing average reasoning tokens by over 64% compared to standard R1-style outputs while maintaining or improving accuracy across four diverse benchmarks.

## Method

SketchThinker-R1 is a three-stage training pipeline. First, Sketch-Mode Cold Start converts long chain-of-thought traces from LLaVA-CoT-100K and Vision-R1-cold into concise numbered-list sketch-style reasoning using GPT-5, then fine-tunes the base Qwen2.5-VL model via SFT to instill initial sketch-style capability. Second, a SketchJudge reward model (fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) is trained to binary-classify reasoning traces as sketch-style (score 1) versus normal verbose style (score 0), providing a style-based supervisory signal. Third, Sketch-Thinking RL applies GRPO with a composite reward (0.5 accuracy + 0.4 format + 0.1 SketchJudge thinking-style score) across diverse domains to generalize the sketch-thinking ability. The key design choice is that SketchJudge supervises reasoning *style* rather than enforcing a fixed length, allowing the model to adaptively produce longer reasoning for genuinely difficult questions while keeping average thinking cost low.

## Results

On four multimodal benchmarks (MMMU, MathVision, VisuLogic, PhyX), SketchThinker-R1-7B achieves 62.8% accuracy on MMMU with only 64.3 reasoning tokens, compared to Vanilla-R1's 61.0% accuracy at 182.2 tokens — a 64.7% reduction in thinking cost while simultaneously improving accuracy. The Efficiency of Thinking (EoT = Acc/#Token) metric jumps from 0.335 (Vanilla-R1) to 0.977 (SketchThinker-R1) on MMMU, and similar gains appear across all four benchmarks. The 3B variant also outperforms all baselines, reducing thinking cost by over 50% versus Vanilla-R1-3B while maintaining comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SketchThinker-R1-7B outperforms all seven baselines on both accuracy and reasoning token cost across all four benchmarks. Against the direct-inference baseline Vanilla-R1, it improves MMMU accuracy from 61.0% to 62.8% while cutting tokens from 182.2 to 64.3 (−64.7%). Among prompt-based methods, Constrained CoT and Chain-of-Draft reduce tokens to ~78–86 but sacrifice 2–5% accuracy (e.g., 58.6% vs. 62.8% on MMMU), whereas SketchThinker-R1 achieves both lower tokens (64.3) and higher accuracy. SFT-based methods (C3oT, VeriThinker) reduce tokens to 105–127 but still trail SketchThinker-R1 by 2.5–3.5% in accuracy and by roughly 40–50% in token cost. RL-based baselines L1 and ThinkPrune reduce tokens to 105–137 yet remain 3–3.6% below SketchThinker-R1 in accuracy on MMMU, confirming that explicit sketch-style reward supervision via SketchJudge yields a more effective efficiency–accuracy trade-off than length-penalty or truncation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the R1-style cold-start + RL training paradigm (pioneered by DeepSeek-R1 and adapted to multimodal models by Vision-R1, VisualThinker-R1-Zero, and MM-Eureka) by inverting the optimization objective: rather than incentivizing longer, more detailed reasoning, it explicitly rewards concise sketch-style reasoning. The key methodological departure is the SketchJudge reward model, which supervises the *style* of the reasoning trace (sketch vs. verbose) rather than merely answer correctness or response length, distinguishing it from length-based RL methods like L1 and ThinkPrune. The three-stage pipeline (SFT cold start → reward model training → GRPO-based RL) follows the structural template of Vision-R1 and LMM-R1, but the reward shaping that incorporates a style-evaluation signal is novel within this lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 64% reduction in reasoning tokens at matched or improved accuracy is practically significant for real-time multimodal deployment, where token cost and latency directly affect user experience and infrastructure expense. The SketchJudge concept—training a lightweight model to evaluate reasoning *style* as a reward signal—offers a reusable component for other efficiency-oriented RL settings. Limitations include reliance on GPT-5 for cold-start data generation (a non-trivial cost), evaluation only at 3B and 7B scales, and a binary sketch/normal classification that may be too coarse for genuinely hard problems requiring extended deliberation. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger models (70B+), whether the binary reward can be replaced with a calibrated continuous signal without the observed performance drop, and whether sketch-style reasoning degrades on tasks where the correct answer genuinely requires multi-step derivation.
