## Problem

Existing interpretable feature learning methods (e.g., Sparse AutoEncoders) discover features unsupervised, operate only on activations rather than weights, and cannot directly rewrite a model to remove a targeted bias. This paper asks whether a single feature neuron can be learned from model gradients with a desired interpretation (e.g., gender) and whether that neuron can be used to modify model weights to debias the model while preserving other capabilities.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes the factual gradient vector ∇₊W_m (computed via MLM or CLM on a sentence where the masked token is a feature-sensitive term like a gendered pronoun) and produces a single scalar h ∈ ℝ via a tanh-activated linear projection. The decoder maps h back to a gradient-difference vector of the same dimensionality as the model's trainable parameters (all layers except the prediction head), which is then scaled by a user-chosen learning rate α and added to the base weights. The total GRADIEND parameter count is only 3n+1 where n is the number of base-model parameters, making it extremely lightweight relative to the model it edits.

## Method

GRADIEND is a bottleneck encoder-decoder trained with MSE loss to predict the gradient difference ∇±W_m = ∇₊W_m − ∇₋W_m from the factual gradient ∇₊W_m alone, where the two gradients come from masking the same sentence with the factual vs. orthogonal feature class (e.g., 'she' vs. 'he'). The encoder is a single tanh neuron (W_e^T·∇₊W_m + b_e) and the decoder is linear (h·W_d + b_d), so the entire architecture has 3n+1 learnable parameters. The key insight is that subtracting the orthogonal gradient from the factual gradient cancels non-feature-related updates, isolating a direction in weight space that specifically strengthens or mitigates the target bias. For debiasing, the user selects a feature factor h and learning rate α, then updates weights as W̃_m = W_m + α·dec(h); the paper shows the decoder's bias term b_d alone (at h=0) already encodes a useful debiasing direction, and the linear decoder structure yields a near point-symmetric response to (h, α) vs. (−h, −α). Unlike SAE-based approaches that only modify activations at inference time, GRADIEND directly rewrites model weights, enabling the debiased model to be deployed as a standard checkpoint.

## Results

For gender debiasing, GRADIEND_Female/Male + INLP achieves the best overall proportional rank of 0.88 (SS: 0.91, SEAT: 0.84), outperforming all other methods including CDA+INLP (0.75) and INLP alone (0.67). As a standalone weight-modifying method, GRADIEND_Female/Male (0.46) is the strongest, beating CDA (0.44), SELFDEBIAS (0.41), LEACE (0.36), and DROPOUT (0.30). Language modeling degradation is minimal for the best configuration: −0.39% LMS_StereoSet, GLUE at 87.06% (vs. 87.46% base), and SuperGLUE at 50.65% (vs. 52.37% base). For race and religion, GRADIEND Asian/White (0.58) and GRADIEND Christian/Jewish (0.44) are the top weight-modifying approaches, though overall debiasing gains are smaller due to noisier training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRADIEND_Female/Male + INLP (Mean rank 0.88) clearly outperforms all competing combinations: CDA+INLP (0.75), DROPOUT+INLP (0.71), and GRADIEND_F/M+SENTDEBIAS (0.64), establishing a new SoTA for gender debiasing. As a standalone weight-modifying method, GRADIEND_F/M (0.46) beats CDA (0.44), SELFDEBIAS (0.41), LEACE (0.36), RLACE (0.31), and DROPOUT (0.30), making it the strongest single weight-editing approach. Among post-processing-only methods, INLP (0.67) and SENTDEBIAS (0.60) outperform GRADIEND_F/M alone, but require customized inference pipelines unlike weight-modified models. For race, GRADIEND Asian/White (0.58) surpasses SENTDEBIAS (0.55), DROPOUT (0.54), INLP (0.46), and CDA (0.44), though SELFDEBIAS (0.87) leads on the SS-only metric with degraded language modeling. For religion, GRADIEND Christian/Jewish and Christian/Muslim (both 0.44) trail SENTDEBIAS (0.64) and CDA (0.58) in aggregated rank but are the only weight-modification methods with statistically significant SEAT improvements without significantly harming language modeling on specific models (e.g., GPT-2, RoBERTa). Language modeling costs are generally low for GRADIEND variants (≤1% LMS_StereoSet drop for gender), whereas SELFDEBIAS incurs a −9.65% LMS_StereoSet penalty and DROPOUT-based methods lose 2–3% on GLUE.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRADIEND carves new ground at the intersection of gradient-based interpretability (Grad-CAM, Integrated Gradients) and targeted feature learning, departing from Sparse Autoencoders (Bricken et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024) in two key ways: it learns a *targeted* feature with a desired interpretation rather than discovering features unsupervised, and it operates on model gradients to produce weight-update directions rather than on activations, enabling direct model rewriting. Within the debiasing literature, it occupies a distinct niche between post-processing methods (INLP, SENTDEBIAS, LEACE) that modify representations at inference time and retraining methods (CDA, DROPOUT) that require full training loops—GRADIEND learns a compact gradient-difference representation that can be applied as a one-shot weight update to an already-trained model. The single-neuron bottleneck architecture is a deliberate simplification relative to SAEs' high-dimensional feature spaces, trading expressivity for interpretability and direct applicability to weight modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling targeted debiasing of deployed models without retraining: a single scalar feature neuron encodes the direction of bias, and the decoder produces the exact weight adjustments needed, making the approach integrable into standard downstream pipelines (unlike post-processing methods requiring custom inference). The SoTA result for gender debiasing (GRADIEND + INLP) demonstrates that combining weight-modification with post-processing yields complementary gains. However, significant limitations remain: the method is restricted to single-axis, pairwise class debiasing; race and religion results are substantially weaker due to noisier training data and multi-tokenization issues (especially for LLaMA); and the binary gender framing, while acknowledged, limits real-world applicability. Open questions include multi-axis simultaneous debiasing, extension to continuous features, and whether the single-neuron bottleneck is sufficient for more complex or intersecting biases.
