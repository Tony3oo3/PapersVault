## Problem

State-of-the-art deep-learning SMLM methods (DECODE, LiteLoc) rely on non-differentiable non-maximum suppression (NMS) layers at inference and pixel-wise training losses that cannot handle multiple emitters within a single pixel. These design choices prevent end-to-end optimization, create an inherently conflicting two-threshold decision pipeline that degrades at high emitter densities, and make the precision-recall tradeoff difficult to tune. The paper asks whether SMLM can be reformulated as a differentiable set-matching problem that eliminates NMS entirely.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3-channel tensor of size H×W formed by concatenating the previous, current, and next SMLM frames, providing temporal context without encoding fluorophore physics. It outputs a fixed-size set of d = HW/4 candidate activations, each a 5-element vector (x, y, z, photon count n, detection score s ∈ (0,1)), where lateral coordinates are predicted as sub-pixel offsets relative to a downsampled grid. At inference, a single user-defined threshold τ on the detection scores performs filtering, replacing the two-threshold NMS pipeline of prior methods.

## Method

The core loss reformulates SMLM training as an entropic optimal transport problem between predicted and ground-truth activation sets: a cost matrix combines a learnable Mahalanobis distance (localization) with binary cross-entropy (detection), and the Sinkhorn algorithm provides a fully differentiable solution in O(d²) per step, replacing the non-differentiable Hungarian assignment. The architecture is an iterative refinement network: a U-Net encoder maps the input to a latent representation, a lightweight CNN decoder (chosen over ViT for sub-pixel locality) produces candidate activations, and a differentiable PSF-based renderer simulates the expected frame from current predictions. The encoded reconstruction is fed back through a residual U-Net module that updates the latent representation, and this loop runs for K=2 iterations, injecting explicit knowledge of the microscope's optical system into the learning process. The weighting matrix Σ in the Mahalanobis cost is learned end-to-end, automatically balancing the difficulty of predicting each spatial dimension.

## Results

On the EPFL 2016 Challenge synthetic benchmarks, the proposed method (SHOT) achieves the best 3D efficiency (E₃D) across all four densities (0.2, 2.0, 8.0 activations/µm/frame) and both SNR levels, e.g. E₃D = 0.750 at density 2.0/high SNR vs. 0.706 (DECODE) and 0.699 (LiteLoc), and E₃D = 0.384 at density 8.0/high SNR vs. 0.371 (DECODE) and 0.319 (LiteLoc). The method also consistently reports the lowest lateral and axial RMSE (e.g. 24.8 nm lateral, 28.4 nm axial at density 2.0/high SNR). On real biological data with temporal binning to simulate high density, SHOT outperforms LiteLoc on both FRC and RSP (e.g. FRC = 44.2 nm vs. 71.5 nm on T32-NPC-Nup96; RSP = 0.689 vs. 0.671).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the classical 3D-DAOSTORM, SHOT dominates at every density and SNR level, with the gap widening dramatically at high density (E₃D = 0.384 vs. 0.009 at density 8.0/high SNR; RMSE_lat = 52.5 nm vs. 83.3 nm). Against DECODE (the leading EPFL challenge method), SHOT wins on E₃D at all eight density/SNR combinations, with the largest margin at density 8.0/high SNR (0.384 vs. 0.371) and density 2.0/high SNR (0.750 vs. 0.706); DECODE retains a recall advantage at low densities but suffers from higher RMSE. Against LiteLoc (the most recent deep-learning baseline), SHOT again leads on E₃D across all settings, with the clearest separation at density 8.0/high SNR (0.384 vs. 0.319, a 20% relative improvement) and on real data at high density (FRC 44.2 nm vs. 71.5 nm on T32-NPC-Nup96). The consistent pattern is that SHOT trades a small amount of recall for substantially lower localization error and higher precision, yielding the best overall E₃D score in every tested regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the optimal-transport set-matching loss from DETR-style object detection (Carion et al., 2020; Zhu et al., 2021) into single-molecule localization microscopy, where prior deep-learning methods (DECODE, LiteLoc) relied on pixel-wise losses and non-differentiable NMS post-processing. It also adapts the iterative refinement paradigm from optical flow (RAFT, Hur & Roth 2019) by using the known PSF as a differentiable 'rewarping' operator to generate feedback frames. The authors explicitly note that, to their knowledge, entropic-regularized OT has not previously been applied to SMLM, carving a new methodological niche at the intersection of these two threads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating NMS and enabling true end-to-end training, the method simplifies the inference pipeline to a single threshold and handles higher emitter densities where NMS-based methods degrade. The main practical limitation is the iterative design's higher compute cost (~200 fps inference, 20 h training), and the field-wide dependence on precise PSF calibration remains unaddressed. Open questions include whether the OT loss generalizes to other super-resolution modalities, whether PSF-agnostic or self-calibrating variants can be built, and whether the modest architectural gain justifies the iterative overhead in deployment settings.
