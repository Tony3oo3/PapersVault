## Problem

Existing robotic foundation models learn spurious correlations from pre-training trajectories (e.g., background textures, lighting), causing significant performance drops under distribution shift. This paper asks whether a training-free, plug-and-play approach can redirect a robot policy's attention from spurious features to object-relevant ones at inference time, without fine-tuning or accessing model weights.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB visual observation and a language instruction, and outputs a 7-DoF robot action (Δx, Δy, Δz, rot_x, rot_y, rot_z, gripper). PCD operates as a black-box plugin: for autoregressive policies it directly reads the action token probability distributions, while for diffusion-based policies it first samples N candidate actions (N=24) and approximates per-dimension probability distributions via kernel density estimation before applying the contrastive formula. The object-masked counterpart observation is generated per-frame using SAM2 tracking and inpainting (LaMa, Telea, or Navier-Stokes).

## Method

PCD computes a contrastive action distribution by amplifying the ratio of the original-observation policy output to the object-masked-observation policy output, raised to a power α: π*_θ(a|l,o) ∝ π_θ(a|l,o) · (π_θ(a|l,o)/π_θ(a|l,ô))^α, which suppresses predictions driven by task-irrelevant features while preserving object-relevant signals. Two supporting components enable broad applicability: (1) Track2Mask automatically generates object-masked observations by annotating target objects in the initial frame (via Point/Box prompts or Grounding DINO), tracking them with SAM2 across the trajectory, and inpainting the segmented regions; (2) KDE-PM approximates action probability distributions for diffusion-based policies by sampling N actions and fitting a Gaussian-kernel density per action dimension under an independence assumption. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no access to pre-trained model weights, making it a drop-in plugin for both autoregressive (OpenVLA) and diffusion-based (Octo, π₀) policies.

## Results

On the SIMPLER benchmark (9 tasks, 300 trials each), PCD as a plug-and-play plugin improves OpenVLA from 16.8% to 25.3% success rate (+50.6%), Octo from 13.8% to 17.9% (+29.7%), and π0 from 63.9% to 69.6% (+8.9%), using Grounding DINO for automatic object annotation. On 6 real-world manipulation tasks (20 trials each, AGILEX PIPER arm), PCD applied to fine-tuned π0 achieves a 108% average success rate improvement over the baseline, at the cost of a 24% increase in execution time. PCD also outperforms Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) on π0, achieving 68.1% vs. 62.7% average success rate across the 9 simulation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCD is evaluated as a training-free plugin on three open-source robot policies. On OpenVLA (autoregressive, 7B params), PCD yields the largest relative gains, improving average success rate by 50.6% (16.8%→25.3%), with particularly dramatic improvements on tasks like Pick Coke Can (+152%) and Open Drawer (+49%). On Octo (diffusion-based, 93M params), PCD improves average success by 29.7% (13.8%→17.9%), with the strongest gain on Pick Coke Can (+73%). On the strongest baseline π0 (flow-matching, 63.9% base), PCD still delivers an 8.9% absolute improvement (69.6%), and a 108% relative improvement on real-world tasks. Compared to Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) applied to π0, PCD achieves 68.1% vs. 62.7% average success rate, with CFG actually degrading performance on 5 of 9 tasks (e.g., Stack Cube drops from 68.7% to 60.3%). On the multi-perspective miniVLA policy (LIBERO-90), PCD improves average success from 71.2% to 79.6% (+8.4%), confirming applicability beyond the three primary policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) paradigm from the VLM hallucination literature (Park et al., 2025; Favero et al., 2024) to the robot manipulation domain, where the 'hallucination' analogue is spurious correlation with task-irrelevant visual features. The key adaptation is contrasting action probability distributions from original versus object-masked inputs rather than text token distributions, and the Track2Mask pipeline (Grounding DINO + SAM2 + inpainting) provides the object-masked observations. A notable departure from the closely related classifier-free guidance (CFG) is that PCD operates as an explicit post-hoc correction on the full action distribution, whereas CFG provides implicit in-process steering during diffusion denoising; the paper shows PCD outperforms CFG on π0. The KDE-PM module is a practical bridge that makes the contrastive framework compatible with diffusion-based policies that do not natively output probability distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCD offers a genuinely plug-and-play, training-free mechanism to improve generalization of existing robot policies without access to model weights, which is practically valuable given the closed nature of many deployed policies. The 108% real-world improvement is striking but measured on a π0 fine-tuned with only 10 demos per task, so the absolute baseline is weak; the more telling simulation result is a consistent 8.9% gain on the already-strong π0. Key limitations include the ~2× inference latency overhead and the fact that PCD only addresses spurious correlations at inference time without preventing their acquisition during training. Open questions include whether training-time interventions (e.g., invariant feature learning) could complement PCD, whether the approach extends to policies with non-visual spurious cues (e.g., proprioceptive shortcuts), and whether the computational cost can be reduced via faster LLM inference or cached masking.
