## Problem

Training cooperative AI agents to generalize across diverse human partners in zero-shot settings is difficult because adversarial training—while efficient at targeting a learner's weaknesses—risks producing degenerate, self-sabotaging partner policies in cooperative tasks. Prior regret-based adversarial approaches in cooperation either incentivize handshaking/sabotage or converge to rigid worst-case strategies, failing to generate a meaningful curriculum of realistic yet challenging partners.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen VAE pre-trained on 100k joint trajectories from a PBT population (CoMeDi or MEP agents), plus real-time environment observations from Overcooked, CMG, or CRG. The Adversary is a 4-layer MLP (128 hidden, ReLU) that maps a sampled z ~ N(0,I) to a 16-dim latent vector z', which the VAE decoder uses to autoregressively generate partner actions. The output is a trained Cooperator policy (CNN+GRU, trained with PPO) that produces cooperative actions in the environment, evaluated against both simulated human-proxy models and real human partners.

## Method

GOAT combines a frozen variational autoencoder of cooperative strategies with online regret-based adversarial training. A lightweight Adversary policy (trained with REINFORCE) searches the VAE's latent space for embeddings that decode into simulated partners maximizing the Cooperator's regret—the performance gap between the partner's self-play return and its cross-play return with the Cooperator. The Cooperator (trained with PPO) simultaneously minimizes this regret by adapting to the generated partners. The frozen VAE guarantees all generated partners remain cooperative (preventing sabotage), while the regret objective (as opposed to minimax) steers the adversary toward meaningful, solvable coordination challenges rather than incompetent worst-case partners, naturally producing a curriculum of increasing complexity.

## Results

On the Overcooked Multi-Strategy Counter layout evaluated with 40 real human participants, GOAT achieves a 38% improvement in team score over the previous state-of-the-art method (GAMMA). On the simpler Counter Circuit layout, GOAT improves over GAMMA by 3%, as both methods approach near-optimal performance. In the Cooperative Reaching Game (CRG), GOAT achieves the highest average reward across all 11 heuristic partner agents. In simulation (Overcooked), GOAT converges faster and reaches higher final performance than all four baselines on both layouts when evaluated against a behavior-cloned human proxy model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOAT outperforms all five baselines across all three environments. Against GAMMA (Liang et al., 2024), the previous state-of-the-art generative method, GOAT achieves a 38% improvement in human cooperation score on the complex Multi-Strategy Counter layout and a 3% improvement on the simpler Counter Circuit layout. Against MEP (Zhao et al., 2022) and CoMeDi (Sarkar et al., 2023), GOAT achieves better mode coverage in CMG (assigning higher probability to high-reward modes rather than spreading uniformly) and higher average reward in CRG. Against FCP (Strouse et al., 2022) and BC+RL (Carroll et al., 2020), GOAT shows substantially larger margins in both simulation and human evaluation, with faster convergence in training curves. The key differentiator is that GOAT's regret-based adversarial search over the frozen VAE latent space targets the Cooperator's specific weaknesses, whereas PBT-based baselines sample partners uniformly and minimax-based approaches converge to degenerate low-reward behaviors.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOAT builds directly on Liang et al. (2024)'s GAMMA framework, which trained a VAE on cooperative agent trajectories to generate diverse simulated partners, and on regret-based curriculum methods from Unsupervised Environment Design (Dennis et al., 2021; Parker-Holder et al., 2022). The key departure is from prior adversarial approaches in cooperative settings (Charakorn et al., 2023; Sarkar et al., 2023) that trained partner policies directly on minimax or cross-play objectives, leading to degenerate sabotage behaviors. GOAT instead freezes the generative model and trains a lightweight adversary to search its latent space for cooperative-but-challenging partners, using regret (self-play minus cross-play gap) as the adversarial signal rather than raw minimax.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental open problem in human-AI coordination: how to leverage adversarial training's curriculum benefits without the adversary learning to sabotage the cooperative task. The 38% improvement over the previous state-of-the-art (GAMMA) with real human partners on the Multi-Strategy Counter layout is practically significant for deployment scenarios. However, the method's effectiveness depends on having sufficient diverse cooperative trajectory data to train a high-quality VAE, limiting applicability to data-scarce domains. Open questions include whether the framework scales to more complex multi-agent settings, whether the frozen-VAE constraint remains effective as the cooperator improves substantially, and how to incorporate explicit human feedback during the adversarial loop.
