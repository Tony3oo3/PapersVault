## Problem

Applying Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) to complex video reasoning in LVLMs is bottlenecked by data quality: existing open-source video datasets provide holistic untimestamped captions, simple perception-based QA answerable from short clips or textual priors, and visually unfaithful CoT that relies on commonsense rather than video-grounded retrieval. This prevents SFT from teaching true video-grounded reasoning and leaves RL without a reliable reward signal to penalize hallucination in intermediate steps.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips sampled at 2 fps (up to 192 or 384 frames, each at 128×28×28 resolution) alongside a text question. In 'thinking' mode, it outputs a structured chain-of-thought containing explicit <action> and <observation> tags that simulate a Thought-Action-Observation loop (using segment_retrieval and segment_query operations), followed by a final <answer> tag. In 'non-thinking' mode, it produces a concise direct answer. The training data spans multiple-choice and open-ended formats across 10 predefined question types (event localization, counting, causality, counterfactual reasoning, etc.).

## Method

The approach combines a novel multi-stage agentic data synthesis pipeline with a two-stage SFT+RLVR training procedure. The ReWatch dataset is built in three stages: (1) hierarchical captioning that semantically segments long videos and generates timestamped event descriptions via a low-then-high frame-rate strategy; (2) contrastive QA generation that creates questions answerable only from detailed captions (not summaries), purified by a three-layer filter (answer verification, text-bias elimination, summary-bias elimination); and (3) multi-agent ReAct CoT synthesis where a Reasoner agent and Observer agent iteratively perform targeted queries on the detailed caption to produce video-grounded reasoning traces. Training proceeds via multi-task SFT (jointly optimizing captioning, direct QA, and CoT reasoning losses) followed by GRPO-based RL with a novel Observation & Reasoning (O&R) reward that multiplies final-answer accuracy by a bonus term evaluating both the factual grounding of intermediate observations against the video caption and the sufficiency of the reasoning actions to recover the correct answer, explicitly penalizing hallucinated or unsupported intermediate steps.

## Results

On five video reasoning benchmarks (VCR Bench, MINERVA, Video Holmes, Video MathQA, CG-AV Counting) at 192 frames, ReWatch-R1 with the O&R reward achieves a 35.51% average, surpassing all 7B-class competitors (best prior: GLM4.1V-9B at 31.19%) and approaching the 32B base model (Qwen2.5-VL-32B at 35.71%). The SFT-only variant already reaches 33.25%, and RL adds a further +2.26 points. On four video understanding benchmarks (MMVU, LVBench, VideoMME, VideoMMMU), ReWatch-R1 + O&R scores 54.15% average, also setting SOTA among comparable-size models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReWatch-R1 + O&R (35.51% avg) outperforms all 7B-class video reasoning baselines by a wide margin: GLM4.1V-9B (31.19%, +4.32 pts), Video-R1 (31.00%, +4.51 pts), VideoRFT (30.83%, +4.68 pts), VersaVid-R1 (30.88%, +4.63 pts), GRPO-CARE (30.14%, +5.37 pts), and TW-GRPO (29.81%, +5.70 pts). It also surpasses the 32B base model Qwen2.5-VL-32B (35.71%) by a narrow 0.20-point gap, demonstrating that the 7B model with ReWatch training nearly matches a model 4.5× larger. The SFT-only variant (33.25%) already exceeds Video-R1-RL (30.28%) and LongVideoReason-RL (31.58%), confirming that data quality alone provides a substantial advantage. On video understanding benchmarks, ReWatch-R1 + O&R (54.15% avg) beats VideoRFT (53.12%), GRPO-CARE (52.17%), and Video-R1 (52.06%), showing that reasoning training does not degrade general comprehension. The largest per-benchmark gains appear on VCR Bench (+4.64 over GLM4.1V-9B) and Video MathQA (+4.29 over GLM4.1V-9B), tasks requiring multi-step temporal logic and numerical reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SFT+RLVR paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1's GRPO algorithm and the ReAct framework to the video reasoning domain, but its core departure is in the data synthesis pipeline and reward design. Prior video reasoning RL work (Video-R1, Video-Chat-R1, VideoRFT, LongVideoReason) bootstraps SFT with CoT data distilled from text-only LLMs, producing visually unfaithful reasoning traces; ReWatch instead uses a multi-agent ReAct loop over hierarchical video captions to generate grounded Thought-Action-Observation trajectories. The O&R reward mechanism further departs from the standard final-answer-accuracy reward by decomposing the signal into observation fidelity and reasoning sufficiency, directly penalizing hallucinated intermediate steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ReWatch dataset (10k captions, 170k QA, 135k CoTs) and the O&R reward together address a concrete bottleneck—lack of challenging, video-grounded training data—that has limited RLVR progress in video reasoning. The text-only accuracy of 29.4% (near the 25% random baseline) versus Video-R1's 68.9% provides strong evidence that the three-layer filtering genuinely enforces video dependency, a property prior datasets lacked. Key limitations include the text-based simulation of visual retrieval (rather than pixel-level interaction), evaluation restricted to the Qwen2.5-VL family, and the acknowledged gap toward a true 'thinking-with-video' paradigm where the model queries visual encoders directly. Open questions include whether the O&R reward generalizes to non-Qwen architectures and whether the agentic CoT format transfers to streaming or real-time video settings.
