## Problem

Standard autoregressive image generation models predict tokens in a strict left-to-right sequence and cannot revise earlier predictions, causing small errors to accumulate and degrade final image quality. Existing remedies (masked AR, autoregressive diffusion, next-scale prediction) require architectural modifications or changes to the training objective, increasing computational cost and hindering multimodal integration. The paper asks whether existing AR models can be enabled to refine their own predictions without altering their architecture or training recipe.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260307/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label or text prompt as conditioning and produces a generated image (256×256 or 384×384 for class-conditional; variable resolution for text-to-image). Internally, the image is tokenized via a VQ-VAE into a sequence of discrete tokens, which are then rearranged into overlapping tensors of window size k (default k=4). At each decoding step the model outputs a block of k consecutive tokens; only the first token is committed to the final sequence while the remaining k−1 are provisional and refined in subsequent steps, yielding a zig-zag coarse-to-fine progression across positions.

## Method

TensorAR reframes next-token prediction as next-tensor prediction: the token sequence is split into overlapping tensors of k consecutive tokens, so that later predictions can revise earlier ones, enabling iterative refinement while preserving causal masking. To prevent information leakage from overlapping tokens during training, a discrete tensor noising mechanism injects categorical noise into input tensors, with a monotonically increasing noise weight β(j) (linear, sine, square-root, or exponential schedule) that simulates progressive denoising within each tensor. Two lightweight plug-in modules—an input encoder Q_in and an output decoder Q_out, each a single-layer query transformer with residual connections—wrap the original embedding and linear output layers to interface with tensor-based inputs and outputs. The overall training objective combines standard AR cross-entropy with the discrete-diffusion weighted loss, and the entire framework is drop-in compatible with existing transformer AR backbones (LlamaGen, Open-MAGVIT2, Janus-Pro-7B) with only 1.5–4.6% additional parameters.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256×256, TensorAR consistently reduces FID across all base models: LlamaGEN-B improves from 5.46 to 4.71, LlamaGEN-L from 3.80 to 2.78, and LlamaGEN-XXL (384×384) from 2.34 to 2.03, with only 1.5–4.6% additional parameters. On text-to-image GenEval, TensorAR on Janus-Pro-7B achieves an overall score of 0.83 (vs. 0.80 baseline), surpassing DALL-E3 (0.67) and SD3-Medium (0.74). On DPG-Bench, TensorAR on Janus-Pro-7B reaches 85.57 overall (vs. 84.19 baseline), outperforming SD3-Medium (84.08) and DALL-E3 (83.50). Throughput decreases by only ~10% relative to the base model while delivering substantial FID gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TensorAR is evaluated as a plug-in on three base AR models (LlamaGEN, Open-MAGVIT2, Janus-Pro-7B) and consistently outperforms each base across all metrics. On ImageNet 256×256, TensorAR-augmented LlamaGEN-XXL (FID 2.03) narrows the gap to VAR (FID 1.73, 2.0B params) and RandAR-L (FID 2.15, 1.4B params) while using fewer parameters and no architectural changes. On GenEval, TensorAR on Janus-Pro-7B (overall 0.83) surpasses DALL-E3 (0.67), SD3-Medium (0.74), and Emu3-Gen (0.54), and on DPG-Bench (overall 85.57) it exceeds SD3-Medium (84.08), DALL-E3 (83.50), and PixArt-Σ (80.54). The largest relative gains appear on smaller models: LlamaGEN-B improves FID by 0.75 points and GenEval overall from 0.32 to 0.61, while DPG-Bench overall jumps from 43.13 to 73.33. Compared to masked AR methods (MAGVIT-v2, MaskBit, MAR) and other causal AR approaches (DART, RQTran., SAR-XL), TensorAR-augmented models remain competitive on FID without requiring VQ-VAE retraining or objective-function changes. The parameter overhead is minimal (1.5–4.6%), and throughput decreases by only ~10%, making TensorAR a practical drop-in improvement over all listed baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

TensorAR extends standard autoregressive image generation (LlamaGen, Open-MAGVIT2) by reframing next-token prediction as next-tensor prediction over overlapping sliding windows, drawing on discrete diffusion theory (Austin et al., 2021) to design a categorical noising mechanism that prevents information leakage from overlapping tokens. It deliberately positions itself against three competing directions: masked AR (which requires architectural changes), AR-diffusion hybrids like DART (which alter the training objective from classification to regression), and next-scale prediction like VAR (which changes the generation order entirely). By requiring neither architectural modification nor a change in the training paradigm, TensorAR carves out a distinct middle ground that preserves the causal, decoder-only structure of standard AR transformers while injecting a diffusion-like iterative refinement capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design is the paper's most practically significant contribution: any existing AR image generator can adopt TensorAR with roughly 2–5% additional parameters and no retraining from scratch, making it immediately deployable across the growing ecosystem of AR-based multimodal models. The controllable window size k provides a principled quality-latency dial (k=1 recovers standard AR; k=T approximates discrete diffusion), which is useful for practitioners who need to balance throughput against fidelity. Open questions include whether the refinement mechanism transfers to video generation, how it interacts with recent AR distillation methods for faster decoding, and whether the left-to-right causal constraint remains a bottleneck at very high resolutions where spatial context spans hundreds of tokens.
