## Problem

Current vision-language-action (VLA) models process observations only at the image level, lacking fine-grained pixel-level spatial understanding needed for precise manipulation, and they rely exclusively on textual instructions, which constrains multimodal human-robot interaction and spatial awareness. No existing VLA simultaneously supports pixel-level reasoning and diverse visual prompts (points, lines, regions, masks).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263748/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PixelVLA takes a single third-person RGB camera view at 224×224 pixels, a natural-language instruction, a binary pixel-aware mask (H×W) highlighting the target object, and optional visual prompts (points, lines, bounding boxes, or masks) as input. It outputs a continuous 7D robot action chunk of 8 timesteps (8×7 matrix), avoiding the 256-bin discretization used by OpenVLA. The textual instruction is reformulated to embed placeholders for pixel-aware and prompt-aware embeddings, so the LLM backbone jointly attends to language, pixel-level spatial cues, and visual prompt tokens.

## Method

PixelVLA builds on Prismatic-7B (Llama 2-7B + DinoV2/SigLIP vision encoders) and adds three novel modules: a multiscale pixel-aware encoder that computes mask-weighted averages of multi-level SigLIP features (projected via per-level linear layers and summed through an MLP) to produce pixel-aware token embeddings; a lightweight SAM-style visual prompt-aware encoder that converts coordinate-based prompts into positional embeddings; and a continuous action decoder (linear projector + ResNet blocks + MLP) that maps LLM hidden states directly to continuous 7D actions via L1 regression. Training follows a two-stage visuomotor instruction tuning procedure: first, a continuous action training stage on Fractal + Bridge v2 (100k steps) trains only the action decoder with all other modules frozen; second, a pixel-level understanding enhancement stage fine-tunes the LLM backbone via LoRA (rank 32) while jointly training both encoders on the newly constructed Pixel-160K dataset (160K episodes, 6.5M triplets) for 200k steps. Pixel-160K is generated by a two-stage automated annotation pipeline that first localizes gripper regions via SAM 2 on gripper-close frames to produce region proposals, then uses an LLM to extract target-object text from instructions and Grounding DINO + SAM to produce pixel-level masks and derive diverse visual prompts.

## Results

On SimplerEnv-Google Robot (zero-shot), PixelVLA achieves an average Visual Matching (VM) score of 61.4 and Variant Aggregation (VA) score of 50.1, surpassing OpenVLA (32.7/40.0) by 28.7/10.1 points. On the LIBERO benchmark, PixelVLA attains a state-of-the-art average success rate of 86.7%, outperforming Dita (82.4%), CoT-VLA (81.1%), and OpenVLA (76.5%). On SimplerEnv-WidowX, PixelVLA-π0 reaches 55.1% average grasp rate and 33.8% task success, improving over π0 (53.1%/27.1%) by 2.0 and 6.7 points respectively. All gains are achieved with only 1.5% of OpenVLA's pretraining computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixelVLA (built on OpenVLA) dramatically outperforms OpenVLA (32.7→61.4 VM, 40.0→50.1 VA) and OpenVLA-SFT (31.6→61.4 VM) on SimplerEnv-Google Robot, confirming that pixel-level understanding and visual prompting are the key drivers rather than mere fine-tuning. Against TraceVLA (46.2/49.1), PixelVLA gains 15.2/1.0 points in VM/VA, showing that mask-based pixel encoding surpasses visual trace prompting. On the π0 backbone, PixelVLA-π0 (63.3/56.5) improves over π0 (58.8/54.8) and π0-SFT (57.3/53.9), indicating the architecture transfer is backbone-agnostic. On LIBERO, PixelVLA (86.7 avg) beats all baselines including Dita (82.4), CoT-VLA (81.1), SpatialVLA (78.1), and OpenVLA (76.5), with the largest margin on LIBERO-Long (82.6 vs. 69.0 for CoT-VLA), attributed to the continuous action decoder mitigating discretization errors over long horizons. On WidowX, PixelVLA-π0 (55.1 grasp / 33.8 success) surpasses π0 (53.1/27.1) and RoboVLMs (19.8/13.5) by wide margins, while PixelVLA on OpenVLA (38.5/16.7) still substantially outperforms OpenVLA (14.6/1.0). The only notable weakness is Open/Close Drawer, where PixelVLA (42.3 VM) trails Dita (46.3) and SpatialVLA (59.3), which the authors attribute to catastrophic forgetting in the two-stage training and the task's high sensitivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixelVLA builds directly on OpenVLA and π0 as base VLA architectures, extending their image-level, text-only input formulation to incorporate pixel-level mask annotations and diverse visual prompts (points, lines, regions, masks). Conceptually, it adapts the visual instruction tuning paradigm from VLMs (LLaVA, Prismatic VLM, GLaMM, Ferret) to the robotic manipulation setting, and draws on SAM/SAM 2 and Grounding DINO for its automated annotation pipeline. It departs from prior VLA work (TraceVLA, LLaRA, SpatialVLA) that attempted spatial awareness through visual traces or coordinate tokens by introducing a dedicated multiscale pixel-aware encoder that injects fine-grained spatial information into the LLM backbone, combined with a continuous action decoder following π0's flow-matching approach rather than OpenVLA's discrete tokenization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PixelVLA demonstrates that pixel-level understanding and multimodal visual prompting can be efficiently integrated into existing VLAs (via LoRA fine-tuning on 160K episodes) to yield substantial manipulation gains (up to 28.7%) at only 1.5% of OpenVLA's pretraining cost, making it a practical drop-in enhancement for the VLA community. The Pixel-160K dataset with pixel-level annotations and visual prompts addresses a clear data gap in robotic learning. However, all validation is simulation-only (SimplerEnv, LIBERO) with no real-robot experiments, and the method is limited to 2D visual prompts (no 3D perception, trajectory guidance, or reference-image conditioning), leaving open whether these gains transfer to physical deployment and whether the approach scales to richer multimodal interaction.
