## Problem

Unsupervised disentangled representation learning faces a fundamental identifiability challenge: without additional inductive biases, infinitely many entangled representations can produce identical data distributions. Existing methods impose heuristic biases via strong regularization, creating an inherent trade-off where stronger regularization improves disentanglement but restricts latent capacity to represent all underlying variations. This paper addresses both gaps simultaneously by embedding inductive biases within a provably identifiable generative framework whose nonparametric formulation provides infinite latent capacity without violating structural constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64×64 RGB images from two benchmark datasets: 3DShapes (six uniform discrete factors) and MPI3D (real-world robotic scenes with power-law distributed factor variations, e.g., 40 rotational values vs. 2–6 object properties). It produces a d=10-dimensional factorial latent representation where each dimension i consists of a continuous Gaussian variable e_i and a discrete component index z_i drawn from a Dirichlet Process mixture, plus a reconstructed image. The discrete indices are generated through combinatorial composition: each observation is determined by a unique combination of factor-specific mixture components across all dimensions, with inactive dimensions collapsing to prior-tied components.

## Method

The core contribution is a Bayesian nonparametric hierarchical generative model placing an independent Dirichlet Process mixture prior over each latent dimension, where the DP's stick-breaking construction yields a countably infinite Gaussian mixture per factor. This prior simultaneously satisfies the identifiability conditions of Kivva et al. (2022) (mixture marginal + piecewise affine ReLU decoder with weak injectivity), provides universal approximation through infinite support, and enforces disentanglement via a factorial structure that orthogonally partitions the continuous latent space into non-overlapping factor-specific subspaces. For tractable inference, the authors develop a structured nested variational family that preserves hierarchical dependencies between stick-breaking proportions, discrete indices, component parameters, and continuous latents, while the nested formulation ties all components beyond a truncation level T to their prior values, enabling a greedy expansion procedure that adds components only when the ELBO improves. A deep amortized recognition network outputs local conjugate likelihood potentials (following Johnson et al., 2016) rather than directly parameterizing variational distributions, allowing closed-form local posterior updates and decomposing the ELBO into per-dimension contributions optimized via low-variance Monte Carlo gradients with Adam.

## Results

On 3DShapes, Bayes-QLAE achieves InfoM 0.91, InfoC 0.61, D 0.84, and C 0.65, matching Tripod on modularity (InfoM) while surpassing it on compactness (InfoC +0.03, C +0.02) and DCI disentanglement (D +0.04). On MPI3D, Bayes-QLAE reaches InfoM 0.60, InfoC 0.56, D 0.48, and C 0.47, outperforming Tripod on all modularity and compactness metrics (InfoM +0.01, InfoC +0.02, D +0.01, C +0.02) while trailing slightly on explicitness (InfoE 0.71 vs 0.74) and informativeness (I 0.81 vs 0.84). Across both datasets, Bayes-QLAE substantially outperforms β-VAE, β-TCVAE, BioAE, and QLAE on all six disentanglement metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bayes-QLAE matches Tripod on modularity (InfoM 0.91 on 3DShapes) while exceeding it on compactness (InfoC 0.61 vs 0.58) and DCI disentanglement (D 0.84 vs 0.80), and on MPI3D it leads Tripod on all four modularity/compactness metrics (InfoM 0.60 vs 0.59, InfoC 0.56 vs 0.54, D 0.48 vs 0.47, C 0.47 vs 0.45) at the cost of slightly lower explicitness (InfoE 0.71 vs 0.74) and informativeness (I 0.81 vs 0.84). Against QLAE, Bayes-QLAE improves InfoM by +0.07 (3DShapes) and +0.08 (MPI3D), and InfoC by +0.12 and +0.13 respectively, demonstrating that the nonparametric prior's adaptive capacity resolves QLAE's fixed-codebook bottleneck. Compared to information-theoretic baselines (β-VAE, β-TCVAE) and BioAE, Bayes-QLAE achieves substantially higher modularity and compactness on both datasets (e.g., InfoM 0.91 vs 0.62–0.65 on 3DShapes; InfoC 0.61 vs 0.42–0.56), confirming that architectural inductive biases from the hierarchical mixture prior outperform explicit regularization terms. Notably, Bayes-QLAE achieves these results without auxiliary regularization or manual hyperparameter tuning of quantization levels, whereas Tripod requires a Normalized Hessian Penalty (multiple forward passes) and QLAE/Tripod require a priori specification of codebook cardinality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: the identifiability theory of Kivva et al. (2022) for deep generative models with Gaussian mixture priors and piecewise affine decoders, the latent quantization architecture of QLAE/Tripod (Hsu et al., 2024a,b) that enforces combinatorial factor composition through discrete codebooks, and the structured/nested variational inference machinery of Hoffman & Blei (2015) and Kurihara et al. (2006) for Dirichlet Process mixtures. The key conceptual departure from QLAE/Tripod is replacing fixed-cardinality codebooks with a nonparametric Dirichlet Process prior over each factor's discrete modes, thereby inheriting Kivva et al.'s identifiability guarantees while removing the rigid upper bound on per-factor capacity. The structured nested variational family serves as the technical bridge that preserves the hierarchical identifiability structure while approximating the infinite-mixture expressiveness in a computationally tractable way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides one of the first principled identifiability guarantees for a latent-quantization-based disentanglement method, and demonstrates that the multiple auxiliary regularization terms used by Tripod (Normalized Hessian Penalty, independence constraints) can be subsumed into a single Bayesian objective without sacrificing performance. The finding that small fixed codebooks are not a prerequisite for disentanglement—challenging a core design assumption of QLAE—opens the door to adaptive-capacity discrete representations. However, the notably weaker performance on MPI3D (power-law factor distributions) relative to 3DShapes (near-uniform factors) suggests the Dirichlet Process prior may be mismatched for heavy-tailed factor complexities, and the authors themselves flag the Pitman-Yor process as a natural next step. The method's reliance on conjugate exponential family structure and piecewise affine decoders also limits its immediate applicability to more general generative architectures.
