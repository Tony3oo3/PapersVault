## Problem

Linear attention and state-space models achieve linear-time training and constant-memory inference, but their fixed-size hidden state fundamentally limits expressiveness, particularly for tasks like associative recall over long contexts. Softmax attention avoids this limitation but incurs quadratic compute and linear memory. The paper asks whether a middle ground exists that retains subquadratic training while using only logarithmically growing memory to capture multi-scale temporal structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263087/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard query, key, and value matrices (Q, K, V ∈ R^{T×d}) from a token sequence and produces an attention output sequence O ∈ R^{T×d}. During decoding, instead of a single fixed-size state, the model maintains O(log T) hidden state matrices S_t^(ℓ) ∈ R^{d×d} indexed by Fenwick tree levels, each summarizing a power-of-two-sized segment of the prefix. A learned per-level scalar weight λ_t^(ℓ) (computed as a linear function of the current input) adaptively combines these multi-scale states to produce the output at each position.

## Method

Log-linear attention replaces the sequentially semiseparable (SSS) mask in linear attention with a hierarchical (HODLR-type) mask M^H constructed via Fenwick tree partitioning, where the prefix is decomposed into O(log T) buckets of exponentially increasing size. This induces a quasi-hierarchical matrix structure that admits a chunkwise parallel scan training algorithm with O(T log T) compute and O(log T) memory, while the recurrent decoding form maintains one state per bucket level with a merge-and-promote update rule. The framework is general: it composes with any existing linear attention variant by element-wise multiplying its original SSS mask with M^H, preserving the model's transition matrix A (e.g., scaled identity for Mamba-2, identity-plus-rank-one for Gated DeltaNet). The resulting log-linear variants are implemented in Triton with fused multi-level kernels, achieving throughput that surpasses FlashAttention-2 beyond 8K sequence length.

## Results

On language modeling (50B tokens, 16K context), Log-Linear Gated DeltaNet achieves 21.44 WikiText perplexity and 18.08 LAMBADA perplexity, outperforming its linear counterpart (21.73 / 19.71) and a layer-matched Transformer (21.13 / 21.17) on LAMBADA. Log-Linear Mamba-2 reduces LAMBADA perplexity from 24.14 to 21.86 while improving WikiText from 22.44 to 22.11. On the MQAR associative recall benchmark, Log-Linear Mamba-2 improves from 89.6% to 92.9% at dimension 64, and Log-Linear Gated DeltaNet improves from 79.0% to 84.4% at dimension 32. On RULER NIAH tasks, Log-Linear Mamba-2 outperforms Mamba-2 on 8 of 9 single-needle and 8 of 9 multi-needle metrics, while Log-Linear Gated DeltaNet improves across all multi-needle metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to **Mamba-2** (Dao & Gu, 2024), Log-Linear Mamba-2 consistently improves: WikiText PPL drops from 22.44 to 22.11, LAMBADA PPL from 24.14 to 21.86, and MQAR accuracy at dim-64 rises from 89.6% to 92.9%. On RULER NIAH, Log-Linear Mamba-2 wins 8 of 9 single-needle and 8 of 9 multi-needle sub-metrics (e.g., S-NIAH-1 at 16K: 72.4 vs 21.6). Compared to **Gated DeltaNet** (Yang et al., 2024), Log-Linear Gated DeltaNet achieves lower WikiText PPL (21.44 vs 21.73), substantially lower LAMBADA PPL (18.08 vs 19.71), higher MQAR accuracy at dim-32 (84.4% vs 79.0%), and improves across all multi-needle NIAH metrics. Against the **Transformer** baseline (Vaswani et al., 2017), Log-Linear Gated DeltaNet matches the layer-matched 24-layer Transformer on LMEval average (45.6 vs 45.6) and beats it on LAMBADA PPL (18.08 vs 21.17), but still trails the parameter-matched Transformer on WikiText PPL (21.44 vs 21.13). **Hyena** (Poli et al., 2023), which also has O(T log T) compute but linear memory, was excluded from main comparisons due to substantially higher WikiText PPL (~29.5 vs <23 for other models).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies linear attention, state-space models (Mamba-2), and delta-rule variants (Gated DeltaNet) under a structured-matrix formulation P = A ⊙ M, O = PV, where the mask M's structure determines compute and memory scaling. It then replaces the semiseparable mask (which yields O(T) training and O(1) decoding memory) with a hierarchical HODLR-type mask derived from Fenwick-tree partitioning, achieving O(T log T) training and O(log T) decoding memory. The construction is general: any linear-attention architecture with an efficient chunkwise parallel primitive can be 'lifted' to a log-linear variant by composing its temporal mask with the hierarchical mask M^H. The theoretical connection to quasi-hierarchical matrices (an intermediate class between HODLR and semiseparable matrices) provides a principled explanation for why the Fenwick-tree structure enables logarithmic-memory recurrence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fixed-size hidden state in linear attention and SSMs is a fundamental bottleneck for associative recall and long-range retrieval; log-linear attention addresses this by maintaining a logarithmically growing set of multi-scale states, yielding consistent improvements on MQAR, Needle-In-A-Haystack, and per-position loss benchmarks while retaining subquadratic training. The framework is broadly applicable (demonstrated on Mamba-2 and Gated DeltaNet, with xLSTM and MesaNet identified as natural next targets) and the custom Triton kernels already outperform FlashAttention-2 beyond 8K sequence lengths. Key open questions include whether the Fenwick-tree inductive bias (fine-grained recent, coarse distant) is optimal, whether richer λ parameterizations or strong-admissibility H-matrices close the remaining gap to Transformers, and whether the engineering overhead of bespoke intra-chunk kernels limits adoption at scale.
