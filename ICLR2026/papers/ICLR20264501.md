## Problem

PDE surrogate models such as FNO, U-Net, and CNN struggle to generalize to out-of-distribution inputs that differ in physical units or scale, even when the underlying physics is identical under Buckingham π-equivalence. Prior dimensionless-learning approaches fail for spatial fields because pixel-wise π-evaluation degenerates at zero field values and diverges near zero denominators, and no systematic test-time alignment method existed that could handle these pathologies while remaining model-agnostic and training-free.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-channel discretized spatial fields (e.g., 5 channels for thermal: k, q, T_bc, dx, dy; 8 channels for elasticity: E, f_x, f_y, u_bc^x, u_bc^y, ν, dx, dy) and produces the corresponding PDE solution field (temperature, stress, or velocity) at the same grid resolution. A key design choice is that spatial fields are reduced to scalar representative values (arithmetic means, or L₂-norm for vector fields like f) before π-computation, avoiding pixel-wise degeneracy; the surrogate model itself still receives full spatial fields, but rescaled channel-wise by the projected scaling vector. The projection is a drop-in pre-processing step that requires no retraining of the surrogate.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free, model-agnostic test-time projection that maps each OOD test sample into the training distribution while preserving its Buckingham π-invariants. In log-space, π-preserving rescalings correspond to translations along ker(Φᵀ), so the log-scaling vector between a test sample and each training candidate is decomposed via a projection operator into an intra-class (π-preserving) component and an inter-class (physics-changing) component; the nearest training equivalence class is selected by minimizing the inter-class residual, and the test sample is then shifted along its own π-equivalence class to match that training scale. A π-uniform strategy uniformizes the log-π distribution across training samples by tuning only the dominant-scale parameter (identified via SHAP), and K-means clustering on log-space features reduces the pairwise search from O(MN) to O(KN) with negligible accuracy loss. The entire pipeline—feature extraction, log-linear π-constraint enforcement, nearest-class selection, channel-wise rescaling, surrogate forward pass, and inverse output scaling—requires no gradient updates and integrates with any surrogate architecture.

## Results

On 2D thermal conduction, the π-invariant projection with 10-centroid reduction reduces MAE by up to ~91% (U-Net: 13.60 → 1.18) and ~87% (FNO: 9.88 → 1.25) compared to raw surrogates, while on linear elasticity the reduction reaches ~90% (FNO: 3.20 → 0.33). The centroid-based projection (K=10) matches the exhaustive pairwise projection accuracy within statistical noise (e.g., U-Net thermal MAE 1.18 vs. 1.75) while cutting projection time from ~99 s to ~2.3 s (~43× speedup). On Navier-Stokes (Appendix F), the same approach reduces FNO MAE from 0.034 to 0.014 with centroid reduction, confirming applicability to nonlinear PDEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's π-invariant projection is a training-free, model-agnostic add-on applied to three surrogate architectures (CNN, U-Net, FNO), so the primary comparison is each surrogate with vs. without the projection. Across all six model–task combinations, the projection consistently and substantially outperforms the raw surrogate: the largest gain is U-Net on thermal conduction (MAE 13.60 → 1.18, a 91% reduction), while the smallest is CNN on elasticity (0.96 → 0.60, 37% reduction). Against the exhaustive pairwise projection (O(MN) complexity), the proposed 10-centroid variant (O(KN)) matches accuracy within one standard deviation in every setting (e.g., U-Net thermal: 1.18 vs. 1.75 MAE) while reducing projection time by roughly 40–100× (e.g., 99 s → 2.3 s). The centroid approach also consistently beats the 10-random-sampling baseline (e.g., FNO thermal: 1.25 vs. 1.29 MAE; U-Net stress: 0.22 vs. 0.23 MAE), confirming that K-means clustering in log-feature space provides a more informative set of representatives than uniform random sampling. On Navier-Stokes (Appendix F), the same pattern holds: FNO+centroids achieves MAE 0.014 vs. 0.034 raw and 0.013 pairwise, with projection time dropping from ~28 s to ~1.1 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Buckingham π-theorem from classical dimensional analysis as a test-time input transformation for PDE surrogate models, extending prior dimensionless-learning work (Bakarji et al. 2022; Xie et al. 2022; Oppenheimer et al. 2023) that focused on scalar outputs to the spatial-field setting. It departs from test-time training/adaptation methods (Gandelsman et al. 2022; Wang et al. 2020) by being entirely training-free and model-agnostic, and from prior π-based approaches (Fukami et al. 2024) by explicitly addressing the zero-set collapse and denominator blow-up pathologies that arise when applying pixel-wise π-scaling to 2D/3D fields. The log-space quotient-space formulation and centroid-based acceleration are new methodological contributions layered on top of standard surrogate architectures (CNN, U-Net, FNO).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, drop-in nature of π-invariant projection makes it immediately applicable to any existing PDE surrogate without retraining, and the O(MN)→O(KN) centroid reduction keeps inference overhead to ~2 seconds, making it practical for deployment. The reframing of apparent OOD shifts as mere scale changes within a π-equivalence class offers a principled lens for diagnosing when a surrogate's failure is a genuine physics gap versus a trivial unit mismatch. Key open questions include whether the method extends to transient and strongly nonlinear PDEs (advection-diffusion, full Navier-Stokes with turbulence), how it behaves in hybrid settings with empirical components, and whether uncertainty-aware projections can flag when a test sample's π-value lies outside the trained range.
