## Problem

Existing image immunization methods against diffusion-based editing (e.g., PhotoGuard, DiffusionGuard) require solving a separate optimization problem for each image, taking 10+ minutes and 15+ GB of GPU memory per image, making them impractical for large-scale content protection. These per-image optimization approaches also cannot generalize to unseen content and remain vulnerable to common counter-attacks such as JPEG compression and denoising.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260004/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The immunizer takes an RGB image (H×W×C) and a binary immunization mask M defining the sensitive region to protect (e.g., a person's face or body) and outputs a single perturbation tensor ε_im that is element-wise multiplied by M and added to the image, clamped to [0,1]. The perturbation is restricted to the masked region, so the unmasked background remains untouched. At inference, the entire process is a single forward pass of the UNet++ immunizer (~70 ms per image), with no gradient computation or iterative optimization.

## Method

DiffVax trains a UNet++-based feed-forward immunizer model f(·;θ) that generates imperceptible perturbations in one forward pass, eliminating the per-image optimization bottleneck of prior work. Training is guided by two loss terms: L_noise (the L1 norm of the perturbation within the mask, enforcing imperceptibility) and L_edit (the L1 norm of the edited unmasked region produced by a Stable Diffusion inpainting model, enforcing editing failure). Gradients are backpropagated through the full diffusion editing pipeline so the immunizer learns how its perturbation interacts with the denoising process. The model is trained on 800 image–mask–prompt tuples from the CCP dataset with SAM-generated masks and ChatGPT-generated prompts, and at test time it generalizes to unseen images, prompts, masks, and even video frames without any per-image optimization.

## Results

On the CCP test set (875 human images), DiffVax achieves the lowest editing-failure SSIM of 0.510 (seen) and 0.526 (unseen), the highest imperceptibility SSIM(Noise) of 0.989, and the lowest CLIP-T text-misalignment score of 23.13 (seen) / 24.17 (unseen). Critically, DiffVax completes immunization in 0.07 s per image using 5,648 MiB GPU memory, compared to 131–912 s and 6,750–15,114 MiB for the optimization-based baselines—a 250,000× speedup. Under counter-attacks (CNN denoising, JPEG compression, IMPRESS), DiffVax retains the best SSIM and CLIP-T scores across all settings (Table 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffVax outperforms both PhotoGuard variants (E and D) and DiffusionGuard on nearly all metrics. On seen images, DiffVax achieves SSIM 0.510 vs. 0.531 (PG-D), 0.551 (DiffusionGuard), and 0.558 (PG-E), with the largest gap over PG-E (−0.048). On unseen images, PG-D edges out DiffVax by a negligible margin on SSIM (0.523 vs. 0.526), but DiffVax still leads on PSNR (14.32 vs. 14.92), FSIM (0.362 vs. 0.379), CLIP-T (24.17 vs. 29.27), and imperceptibility (0.989 vs. 0.979). The most dramatic advantage is in scalability: DiffVax runs in 0.07 s with 5,648 MiB, versus 131 s / 6,750 MiB (DiffusionGuard), 207 s / 9,548 MiB (PG-E), and 912 s / 15,114 MiB (PG-D). Under counter-attacks (Table 2), DiffVax consistently maintains the lowest SSIM and CLIP-T across denoising, JPEG, and IMPRESS settings, while baselines degrade substantially (e.g., PG-D SSIM jumps from 0.531 to 0.702 under denoising, whereas DiffVax only rises to 0.552).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the per-image optimization paradigm established by PhotoGuard (Salman et al., 2023), DAYN (Lo et al., 2024), and DiffusionGuard (Choi et al., 2025), which each solve a constrained optimization problem independently for every target image. Instead, DiffVax reframes immunization as a learned, feed-forward perturbation-generation task using a UNet++ immunizer trained end-to-end with a composite loss (imperceptibility + edit failure), eliminating per-image optimization entirely. The conceptual lineage traces back to adversarial perturbation generation for GAN-based editing (Yeh et al., 2021; Aneja et al., 2022) and universal adversarial perturbations (Moosavi-Dezfooli et al., 2017), but the specific formulation—training a model to produce image-conditioned, mask-aware perturbations that disrupt latent diffusion inpainting—is new. The paper also extends the task from images to video for the first time, a capability previously infeasible due to the computational cost of optimization-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 250,000× speedup (from ~15 minutes per image to ~70 ms) makes large-scale, real-time immunization of social media content practically feasible for the first time, and the extension to video opens a previously unaddressed protection domain. However, the approach remains model-specific (trained on Stable Diffusion v1.5 inpainting), evaluated primarily on a single dataset of human subjects (CCP), and its cross-model transfer to SD v2 is only partial—optimization-based baselines fail completely in that setting, but DiffVax does not achieve full protection either. Open questions include whether a truly universal immunizer can generalize across all diffusion editing architectures, whether the learned perturbation structure transfers to future model generations, and how the defense holds against adaptive attackers who co-opt the immunizer's own architecture.
