## Problem

All prior complexity-theoretic studies of neural network training assumed real-valued parameters, yet practical advances in quantization restrict weights and data to finite integer domains. This paper initiates the first systematic complexity-theoretic analysis of ReLU neural network training in the fully quantized setting, establishing both hardness and tractability results that have no counterpart in the real-valued literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263997/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The problem takes a DAG architecture G with α input and ω output nodes, a multiset of d-quantized data points (input-output pairs from Z_d^α × Z_d^ω), and an error bound ℓ, and must produce a d-quantized network (arc weights and node biases all in Z_d) achieving error at most ℓ, or correctly conclude no such network exists. Unlike the real-valued setting where the problem is ∃R-complete, the quantized version lies in NP since a certificate is a linear number of integers from a finite domain. The error metric is a simple count of misaligned data points rather than a continuous loss function.

## Method

The paper develops a comprehensive parameterized complexity map for d-QNNT. For lower bounds, targeted reductions from Exact Set Cover, Hitting Set, and 3-SAT establish NP-hardness even for binary quantization with no hidden layer, single output, and zero error, as well as W[2]-hardness with respect to input dimensionality α. For upper bounds, the key technical insight (Lemma 1) uses Steinitz' Lemma to prove that every YES-instance admits a solution where the non-zero indegree of each neuron is bounded by a function of the parameters, enabling dynamic programming over tree decompositions. This yields fixed-parameter tractability for the combined parameters α + tw(G) + ω and α + tw(G) + ℓ, which strictly generalize the corresponding width-based results. The treewidth-based DP maintains, for each bag in the decomposition, the pre-activation values received from both sides of the separator, with the indegree bound ensuring the state space remains parameter-bounded.

## Results

The paper establishes that 2-QNNT (binary quantization) is NP-hard even under highly restrictive settings: with ℓ=0, a single output neuron, and no hidden layer (Theorem 1), and with constant-degree architectures (max indegree 2, max outdegree 3) and |D|≤4 (Theorem 2). It further shows W[2]-hardness w.r.t. input dimensionality α even with no hidden layer (Theorem 3), and NP-hardness with only α=2 inputs and 3 data points (Theorem 4). On the positive side, d-QNNT is fixed-parameter tractable w.r.t. the combined parameters α + tw(G) + ω (Theorem 5) and α + tw(G) + ℓ (Theorem 6), where tw(G) is the treewidth of the architecture, with the key structural insight (Lemma 1) that every zero-error solution admits a variant with non-zero indegree bounded by (dp)^{O(p)} per neuron.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines or quantitative comparisons. The primary contrast is with the non-quantized (real-valued) NNT setting, where training is known to be ∃R-complete (Abrahamsen et al., 2021; Bertschinger et al., 2023); quantization fundamentally changes the landscape by placing the problem in NP. The paper's hardness results (Theorems 1–4) go beyond prior work by adding structural restrictions (constant degree, bounded data size, fixed input dimension) that are necessary for the parameterized lower bounds, whereas earlier reductions (Judd 1990; Schmitt 2004) established NP-hardness without such constraints. The FPT results (Theorems 5–6) are the first non-trivial fixed-parameter tractability guarantees for any neural network training problem, in contrast to the non-quantized setting where, as the authors note, no non-trivial parameterization yielding FPT is currently known. The treewidth-based results supersede the simpler width-based corollaries, and the Steinitz'-Lemma-based indegree bound (Lemma 1) is identified as a structural contribution of independent interest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out an entirely new territory by studying the parameterized complexity of fully quantized ReLU neural network training, a setting that prior complexity-theoretic work (Dey et al. 2020; Abrahamsen et al. 2021; Goel et al. 2021; Bertschinger et al. 2023; Brand et al. 2023) has not addressed. The real-valued NNT problem is ∃R-complete, but quantization fundamentally changes the landscape by restricting weights to a finite integer domain, placing the problem in NP and enabling entirely different algorithmic and hardness techniques. The paper draws on parameterized complexity tools (treewidth-based dynamic programming) and a structural insight via Steinitz' Lemma to establish the first non-trivial FPT results for quantized NNT, while hardness reductions from Exact Set Cover, Hitting Set, and 3-SAT show that intractability persists even in highly restricted architectures. Earlier works on partially quantized or non-ReLU networks (Judd 1990; Schmitt 2004; Courbariaux et al. 2015) provide isolated hardness results but lack the multivariate parameterized analysis developed here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a striking asymmetry: while no non-trivial FPT parameterization is known for training non-quantized neural networks, quantization unlocks several (w.r.t. input dimensionality + treewidth + output dimensionality or error bound), suggesting that the finite-domain structure of quantized weights is not merely a practical convenience but a fundamental complexity-theoretic simplification. The structural lemma bounding non-zero indegree in any valid solution (via Steinitz' Lemma) is of independent interest and may inform architecture design or pruning heuristics. The main open question—whether d-QNNT is FPT w.r.t. input + output dimensionality alone (α + ω)—remains unresolved and is identified as requiring insights beyond current techniques. For practitioners, the results delineate precisely when quantized training is computationally feasible, which could guide the design of efficient training pipelines for resource-constrained deployments.
