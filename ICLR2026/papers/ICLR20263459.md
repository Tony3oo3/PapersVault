## Problem

Training a single network on multiple objectives produces conflicting gradients that force shared latent features into a compromised state suboptimal for any task—a failure mode the authors formalize as latent representation collapse. Existing multi-task learning methods (GradNorm, PCGrad, IMTL, Nash-MTL) address this reactively by reweighting or projecting gradients at each training step, but none proactively design the latent space structure to be inherently immune to cross-task interference.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input (rendered 3D object views from ShapeNet, face crops from MPIIGaze, rotated digit images from MNIST) and produces a single 2048-dimensional latent vector that is simultaneously decoded into multiple outputs: discrete class labels (object category, model ID) and continuous regression values (azimuth, elevation, in-plane rotation, or gaze angles). Unusually, the latent vector is not used holistically by decoders; instead it is decomposed into M one-dimensional orthogonal projections, each feeding a dedicated linear decoder, so the output structure mirrors the concept decomposition by construction.

## Method

Domain Expansion restructures the latent space into M mutually orthogonal 1D subspaces, one per objective, by computing the eigendecomposition of the latent feature covariance at each epoch and assigning the top-M eigenvectors as concept-specific axes. A lightweight 'orthogonal pooling' step then projects each latent feature onto these axes, and concept-specific linear decoders operate only on their assigned projection, so loss gradients are structurally decoupled and cannot interfere across tasks. The eigenvector basis is stabilized across epochs via Hungarian alignment to prevent axis swapping during early training. Training proceeds in two stages: first the ResNet-50 encoder is trained with dynamically updated orthogonal bases under SupCon and Rank-N-Contrast representation losses, then the encoder is frozen and the linear decoders are fit on the converged projections. This orthogonal structure also endows the latent space with algebraic operators—concept-specific adjustment (⊕^m) and full concept composition (⊕)—implemented as simple vector addition/subtraction, enabling compositional inference without retraining.

## Results

On ShapeNet (Objective Set 1, all RNC loss), Domain Expansion achieves Spearman correlations of 0.95/0.87/0.85 for azimuth/elevation/rotation, V-scores of 0.99/0.91 for category/ID, and 99%/97% classification accuracy, with a concept-composition cosine similarity of 0.95. Under Objective Set 2 (mixed RNC + SupCon), the method maintains near-identical performance (Spearman 0.95/0.87/0.85, V-score 0.98/0.96, Acc 98%/94%, Sim 0.93). All baselines show severe latent representation collapse, with Spearman correlations dropping below 0.50 and V-scores near zero for at least one concept in most configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Domain Expansion decisively outperforms all three gradient-based MTL baselines (FAMO, Nash-MTL, IMTL) across every metric in both objective sets. On Objective Set 1, Ours achieves Spearman 0.95/0.87/0.85 versus 0.49/0.41/0.42 (FAMO), 0.38/0.41/0.42 (Nash-MTL), and 0.31/0.16/0.16 (IMTL), while also attaining V-scores of 0.99/0.91 where FAMO and Nash-MTL collapse to 0.00/0.00. The concept-composition cosine similarity gap is especially stark: Ours reaches 0.95 (Set 1) and 0.93 (Set 2), compared to 0.28 (FAMO), 0.28 (Nash-MTL), and 0.14 (IMTL) in Set 1. In Objective Set 2, where baselines achieve near-perfect classification accuracy (99%) but near-zero regression Spearman (≤0.45), Ours simultaneously achieves 98% accuracy and 0.95 Spearman, demonstrating that it avoids the shortcut-learning failure mode. IMTL is the closest competitor on classification accuracy in Set 1 (92%/79% vs. Ours 99%/97%) but still lags by a wide margin on all representation-quality and composition metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of multi-task learning research that addresses gradient conflict reactively at each optimization step (GradNorm, PCGrad, IMTL, CAGrad, Nash-MTL, FAMO) by instead imposing a structural constraint on the latent space itself. It repurposes eigendecomposition of the feature covariance matrix—a standard linear algebra tool—to define mutually orthogonal subspaces, one per objective, and introduces 'orthogonal pooling' as a lightweight architectural primitive that routes each task's supervision through its dedicated axis. The loss functions (SupCon, Rank-N-Contrast) are borrowed from the contrastive representation learning literature, but their application through projected, decoupled subspaces is the novel contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a clean, interpretable alternative to gradient-manipulation MTL: if orthogonal subspaces truly prevent interference by design, practitioners could avoid the per-step overhead and tuning sensitivity of methods like PCGrad or FAMO. However, the evidence is confined to small, controlled benchmarks (rendered ShapeNet, MNIST, MPIIGaze) with a ResNet-50 encoder, and the compositional operators are validated only within the same architecture that defines them, leaving open whether the algebraic structure survives at scale or with genuinely correlated concepts. The authors themselves note that decoding abstract compositions (e.g., 'chair' ⊕ 'boat') into human-understandable outputs remains an open problem, suggesting the framework's full utility depends on pairing it with generative decoders.
