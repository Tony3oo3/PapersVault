## Problem

On-policy RFT methods like PPO and GRPO discard all rollout data after each gradient update, making them extremely sample-inefficient and preventing cost-effective scaling of LLM reasoning training. While off-policy RL naturally reuses historical trajectories, naively applying it to LLM RFT causes training collapse due to distribution shift, and no prior method has demonstrated SOTA-level reasoning performance with substantially reduced rollout cost while maintaining stable convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261705/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes text prompts (math or code problems) truncated to 766 tokens, formatted with a DeepScaleR-style system instruction that requests step-by-step reasoning and a \boxed{} final answer. The model generates responses up to 8,192 tokens, and a verifiable reward function assigns 1 for correct, well-formatted answers and 0 otherwise. Training uses the DeepScaleR-Preview-Dataset (~40K problem-answer pairs) for math and Skywork-OR1-RL-Data for code, with evaluation on held-out benchmarks (AIME'24, AMC'23, MATH500, Minerva, OlympiadBench, LiveCodeBench) under greedy decoding.

## Method

ReMix combines three synergistic components to enable efficient off-policy RFT. First, Mix-PPG replaces the standard PPO/GRPO clipped objective with a mixed-policy version that samples training data from both the current policy (1−p) and up to N=2 historical policies (p=0.4), using importance sampling ratios relative to each historical policy and a V-trace-corrected GAE for stable advantage estimation under off-policy drift; an increased UTD ratio (m=2) further multiplies gradient updates per fresh rollout. Second, a KL-Convex constraint replaces the static KL penalty to the base model with a convex combination λ·KL(π_θ‖π_base) + (1−λ)·KL(π_θ‖π_{k−1}), where λ decays linearly over training to shift from conservative exploration to aggressive refinement. Third, Policy Reincarnation triggers at a predetermined step T (50–100), resetting the KL reference from π_base to the current policy π_T and switching from Mix-PPG to pure on-policy PPO/GRPO, thereby harvesting early off-policy efficiency while guaranteeing stable asymptotic convergence. The paper further identifies a 'Whipping Effect' whereby off-policy data implicitly biases the model toward shorter responses, as longer trajectories accumulate larger distribution shift that amplifies the loss under negative advantage estimates.

## Results

On five math reasoning benchmarks (AIME'24, AMC'23, MATH500, Minerva, OlympiadBench), ReMix-PPO (1.5B, 350 steps) achieves an average Pass@1 of 52.10% with only 0.079M rollouts, matching DeepScaleR's 52.14% at 2.519M rollouts (30× reduction). For 7B models, ReMix-PPO (75 steps) reaches 64.39% average Pass@1 with 0.011M rollouts, surpassing AceReason-Nemotron (63.24% at >3.584M rollouts) by a 450× cost reduction. ReMix-GRPO (7B, 75 steps) further achieves 65.81% average Pass@1. The method also generalizes to code generation, where ReMix-PPO (7B, 100 steps) reaches 34.41% on LiveCodeBench with 0.015M rollouts vs. PPO's 33.33% at 0.051M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReMix-PPO (1.5B, 350 steps) matches the strongest 1.5B baseline DeepScaleR (52.10% vs. 52.14% avg.) while using only 0.079M rollouts versus 2.519M — a 30× reduction in rollout data volume. Against standard PPO (900 steps), ReMix-PPO reaches the same 50.61% average at just 100 steps (0.020M vs. 0.230M rollouts, 10× fewer). For 7B models, ReMix-PPO (75 steps) outperforms AceReason-Nemotron (64.39% vs. 63.24%) with a 450× rollout reduction (0.011M vs. >3.584M), and beats PPO (200 steps) by 4.03 points (63.27% vs. 60.34%) at 6× less cost. ReMix-GRPO (1.5B, 200 steps) exceeds standard GRPO (200 steps) by 1.89 points (50.53% vs. 48.64%) with slightly fewer rollouts (0.368M vs. 0.410M). On code generation (LiveCodeBench), ReMix-PPO (7B, 100 steps) achieves 34.41% vs. PPO (200 steps) at 33.33% with 3.4× fewer rollouts, confirming cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate ideas from the RL literature—generalized proximal policy optimization with sample reuse (Queeney et al., 2021), iteratively refined behavior regularization for offline RL (Ma et al., 2024b), and Reincarnating RL (Agarwal et al., 2022)—into a single framework (ReMix) that makes standard on-policy PPG methods (PPO, GRPO) capable of leveraging off-policy data. It departs from concurrent off-policy RFT efforts (AGRO, Tapered Off-Policy REINFORCE, AsymRE, SPO, RePO, SRPO) by combining multiple off-policy techniques synergistically rather than modifying a single aspect (e.g., importance sampling or reward asymmetry), and by providing the first systematic multi-benchmark comparison against SOTA RFT models. The KL-Convex constraint and policy reincarnation are borrowed directly from offline RL and Reincarnating RL respectively, but their application to LLM RFT with the specific two-stage training schedule is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30×–450× reduction in rollout data volume for matching or exceeding SOTA RFT performance is a major practical win that could dramatically lower the cost of post-training LLMs, making RFT accessible to smaller labs. The empirical analysis revealing the 'Whipping Effect' (off-policy learning implicitly prefers shorter responses) and the collapse of self-reflection under severe off-policyness provides actionable diagnostic insights for practitioners tuning off-policy RFT pipelines. Key limitations include evaluation only up to 7B scale, a fixed off-policy data proportion (p=0.4) rather than adaptive scheduling, and testing restricted to math and code domains; whether the efficiency gains and behavioral effects persist at larger scales or in more open-ended reasoning tasks remains open.
