## Problem

The scaling behavior of discrete diffusion language models (DLMs) has not been fully characterized, with prior work suggesting they require substantially more compute than autoregressive models (ALMs) and making questionable assumptions such as fixed learning rates, fixed batch sizes, and zero irreducible loss. In particular, the scaling of uniform and hybrid-noise diffusion—noise types that allow token revision during generation—remains largely unexplored beyond small-scale ablations, leaving open whether DLMs can be competitive with ALMs at scale.

## Input / Output

The model consumes text sequences of 2048 tokens tokenized with a 131,072-vocabulary BPE tokenizer trained on Nemotron-CC. During training, 20% of samples are conditioned on a noise-free prefix for prompt completion, 50% use per-token independent noise levels (diffusion forcing) to support anisotropic denoising, and a random fraction of empty tokens is appended to enable flexible-length generation. At inference, the model produces a full text sequence over T denoising steps, with the number of steps T decoupled from sequence length N, enabling parallel token generation and iterative revision.

## Method

The paper introduces a universal hybrid mixing distribution π_λ = σ(aλ+b)·u + (1−σ(aλ+b))·m that smoothly interpolates between pure masking and pure uniform noise as a function of log-SNR λ, and reparameterizes the GIDD ELBO in terms of SNR to show invariance to the noise schedule, closing the gap to continuous-state diffusion theory. For scaling law estimation, the authors adopt CompleteP for learning-rate transfer across width and depth, discover that the optimal batch size scales as a power law (exponent ≈0.82) in training tokens rather than model size, and omit learning-rate annealing (justified by a constant 2.45% loss offset that extrapolates to 10B runs). Scaling laws are fitted via iso-FLOP profiles across five model sizes (25M–570M non-embedding parameters), five noise types, and 510 training runs, then validated by scaling to 3B (masked and uniform) and 10B (uniform) models trained for 10²¹ and 10²² FLOPs respectively, confirming that uniform diffusion requires more parameters and less data for compute-optimal training compared to masked diffusion.

## Results

The paper derives compute-optimal scaling laws for discrete diffusion language models across five noise types (masked, low-uniform, balanced, high-uniform, uniform). Uniform diffusion exhibits the most parameter-heavy scaling (M*∝C^0.589, D*∝C^0.411, L*∝C^-0.0522), requiring more parameters and less data than both masked diffusion (M*∝C^0.566) and all autoregressive baselines (M*∝C^0.46–0.52). The 10B uniform diffusion model (8.7B non-embedding params, 10^22 FLOPs) matches the scaling trend of DeepSeek's autoregressive model, and the likelihood gap between masked and uniform diffusion shrinks from 3.2% at 10^18 FLOPs to 1.7% at 10^21 FLOPs. On downstream benchmarks, the 10B uniform model achieves 61.8% ARC-E, 35.7% ARC-C, and 2.43% GSM8k (with adaptive sampling).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to autoregressive scaling laws (Chinchilla: M*=0.49, DeepSeek: M*=0.5243, Shuai et al.: M*=0.464), all DLM variants in this paper require substantially more parameters relative to data, with uniform diffusion (M*=0.589) being the most parameter-heavy. This makes DLMs more token-efficient at compute-optimality, which is significant as pre-training datasets are saturating. Against prior masked diffusion scaling work, the paper reports a notable disagreement: Nie et al. (2025a) predict M*=0.634 for MDMs while this work finds 0.566, and Ni et al. (2025) predict M*=0.514 (more token-heavy). The 10B uniform diffusion model's loss trajectory closely matches DeepSeek's autoregressive scaling trend (Bi et al., 2024), suggesting DLMs can be competitive with ALMs at scale. In compute-bound settings, all noise types converge to similar loss values with no clear advantage for one over another, while in token-bound settings uniform diffusion out-scales other noise types. The paper also notes that Llama 3 (Grattafiori et al., 2024) follows the same token-heavy ALM scaling pattern, further contrasting with the parameter-heavy DLM scaling discovered here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GIDD framework (von Rütte et al., 2025) by reparameterizing the ELBO in terms of signal-to-noise ratio, enabling a smooth interpolation between masked and uniform diffusion through a single mixing distribution. For scaling methodology, it builds on the iso-FLOP profile approach of Hoffmann et al. (2022) and the more accurate FLOP estimation of Bi et al. (2024), while deliberately departing from Nie et al. (2025a) by not fixing batch size and learning rate to constants and by not assuming a zero irreducible loss. The use of CompleteP (Dey et al., 2025) for learning-rate transfer across model scales is a key methodological addition that prior DLM scaling work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive scaling-law study for DLMs across multiple noise types, validated at 10B parameters and 10^22 FLOPs, and it challenges the prior narrative that DLMs are simply less efficient than ALMs. The finding that uniform diffusion is more parameter-heavy and data-light than both masked diffusion and ALMs has direct practical implications for data-bound training regimes where pre-training corpora are saturating. Open questions include whether the competitive scaling advantage persists beyond 10B parameters, how multi-epoch training (as opposed to the sub-epoch regime studied here) affects optimal batch size, and whether the critical batch size for DLMs truly lies well above the ~10^6 tokens reported for ALMs.
