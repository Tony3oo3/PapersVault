## Problem

Representing local protein environments for machine learning remains a key challenge: the structural and chemical variability of these environments makes them difficult to model, and representation learning on them is largely under-explored. While machine learning force fields (MLFFs) have achieved DFT-level accuracy for energy and force prediction, their latent representations have not been systematically explored as general-purpose descriptors for local protein structure and chemistry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263416/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protein 3D structure (AlphaFold2-predicted coordinates followed by Amber99 relaxation) and a focus residue as input, constructing a local environment of all residues whose atoms lie within 5 Å (Hausdorff distance) of the focus residue. The frozen MLFF produces per-atom embeddings over this environment, which are then restricted to the focus residue's atoms to yield a canonical, comparable descriptor. Downstream, a GCN trained on these frozen embeddings outputs pKa values, NMR chemical shifts (backbone and side-chain), secondary structure labels, amino acid identities, and KDE-based likelihood scores for uncertainty estimation.

## Method

The core contribution is repurposing pre-trained MLFF embeddings (MACE, OrbNet, AIMNet, Egret) — originally trained on small-molecule DFT data for energy/force regression — as frozen, physics-grounded descriptors of local protein environments. For each focus residue, the 5 Å local environment is passed through the MLFF, and the final-layer atom-wise features restricted to the focus residue form a canonical descriptor that is invariant to global protein context. A lightweight GCN is then trained on these frozen embeddings for each downstream task, enabling zero-shot transfer without retraining the MLFF. The paper further defines kernel-density-estimation likelihoods in the MLFF embedding space to capture distribution shifts and provide calibrated uncertainty, and demonstrates that the embeddings can be partially inverted to recover protein conformations by guiding AlphaFold3 optimization.

## Results

For pKa prediction (Table 1), AIMNet-based features achieve the lowest MAE across all four ionizable residues: 0.265 (GLU), 0.267 (ASP), 0.270 (LYS), and 0.380 (HIS), substantially outperforming PropKa (0.551/0.469/0.393/0.561) and pKa-ANI (0.445/0.473/0.401/0.426). For backbone NMR chemical shift prediction (Table B.5), MACE-based features achieve the best MAE on five of six atom types, e.g., 0.584 ppm for CA vs. 0.653 for UCBShift2 and 0.667 for ESMFold, and 1.642 ppm for N vs. 1.891 for UCBShift2. For secondary structure classification, MACE reaches 98.6% weighted F1, and for amino acid identification, MACE reaches 99.1% weighted F1, both exceeding all ESM-based and LoCoHD baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classical pKa predictors, AIMNet-based MLFF features dominate: they reduce MAE by ~50% over PropKa (e.g., 0.265 vs. 0.551 for GLU) and by ~40% over pKa-ANI (0.265 vs. 0.445 for GLU). Against ESM-based sequence/structure models (ESM3, ISM, ESMFold), MLFF features win on pKa prediction (AIMNet best) and on chemical shift prediction (MACE best), with MACE achieving 0.584 ppm MAE on CA shifts versus 0.667 for ESMFold and 0.814 for ISM. Against UCBShift2 (the prior SOTA for chemical shifts), MACE-based predictors outperform on all backbone heavy atoms (CA: 0.584 vs. 0.653; N: 1.642 vs. 1.891; C: 0.744 vs. 0.827; CB: 0.716 vs. 0.758) and on H (0.300 vs. 0.324), but lose on HA (0.180 vs. 0.165). Against LoCoHD (a hand-crafted local environment metric), MLFF embeddings achieve substantially higher secondary structure F1 (98.6% vs. 76.3%) and lower chemical shift MAE (0.584 vs. 1.333 for CA). Against the simple learned-embedding baseline (GCN trained end-to-end from coordinates), MLFF features consistently outperform across all tasks, confirming that pretraining on quantum-mechanical data provides a meaningful inductive bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes machine learning force fields (MACE, OrbNet, AIMNet, Egret)—originally trained as DFT-level energy and force predictors on small-molecule quantum datasets—as frozen representation learners for local protein environments. It extends prior work that applied MLFF features to small-molecule property prediction (Shiota et al., 2024; Elijošius et al., 2025) and the hand-crafted AEV-based pKa predictor of Gokcan & Isayev (2022) by leveraging the message-passing, context-dependent atomistic features of modern MLFFs at protein scale. The paper positions itself against sequence-based protein language models (ESM family) and hand-crafted local environment metrics (LoCoHD), showing that physics-trained embeddings can match or exceed them on structural and chemical prediction tasks without any protein-specific pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MLFFs trained exclusively on small-molecule quantum data organize 165K protein environments by secondary structure, residue identity, and protonation state without any protein-specific training establishes a new 'foundation model' paradigm for structural biology, where physically grounded representations transfer zero-shot to unseen proteins and rare chemistries. The practical impact is demonstrated through a state-of-the-art, physics-grounded NMR chemical shift predictor with calibrated uncertainty, which could guide generative structure prediction from experimental NMR data. Key limitations include the frozen-embedding approach (no fine-tuning), slightly inferior HA prediction versus UCBShift, and the 5 Å radius that may miss longer-range electrostatic effects; open questions include whether task-specific fine-tuning of MLFFs on protein data yields further gains and whether the likelihood-based uncertainty can be formalized more rigorously.
