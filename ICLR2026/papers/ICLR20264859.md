## Problem

Conditional tabular diffusion methods rely on training-time conditioning strategies that cannot generalise to unseen constraints at inference, and existing manifold-guidance theories from image diffusion are restricted to continuous domains, flat geometries, and squared-error inference losses. This paper extends manifold theory to mixed-type tabular data with curved geometries and proves tangent-space guarantees for arbitrary differentiable inference-time objectives, enabling a single pre-trained model to handle diverse constraints without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264859/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, HARPOON starts from a Gaussian noise sample x_T ~ N(0,I) and accepts either a binary mask with observed values (imputation) or inequality/categorical constraint specifications (e.g., Age ≥ 50, Workclass = State-gov) as the conditioning signal c. The model is trained once unconditionally with standard MSE on a continuous Gaussian diffusion process over one-hot-encoded categorical and standardised numerical features, so all conditioning is applied purely at inference. Output is a fully imputed or constraint-satisfying tabular row in the original feature space.

## Method

HARPOON's core theoretical result (Theorems 3.1–3.2) proves that a DDPM denoiser trained with MSE acts as an orthogonal projector onto the data manifold in the limit α̅_t→1, and consequently the gradient of any differentiable inference-time loss lies in the manifold's tangent space—generalising Chung et al. (2022) from squared-error losses under flat-manifold assumptions to arbitrary losses on curved manifolds. Algorithmically, HARPOON interleaves standard unconditional denoising steps with tangential gradient corrections: at each step it computes the dirty estimate x̂_0, evaluates a task-specific loss (MAE for imputation, ReLU-based soft penalties for inequalities, product-of-violations for disjunctions), and applies the resulting gradient as a tangential update with guidance strength η. Because consecutive noisy shells are approximately parallel, gradients computed at step t are reused for the correction at step t−1, avoiding redundant backpropagation. The training procedure is identical to standard DDPM (Algorithm 2), requiring no conditional branches, separate discrete/continuous diffusion processes, or auxiliary classifiers.

## Results

On imputation (MAR, 25–75% missingness), HARPOON is competitive with DiffPuter across all 8 datasets, with both remaining robust at 75% missingness while training-time baselines degrade sharply (e.g., Adult: HARPOON 0.94 MSE vs. Remasker 1.36, GAIN 1.04). On categorical imputation, HARPOON achieves the highest accuracy in most settings (e.g., Default at 75%: 61.48% vs. DiffPuter 49.24%, Remasker 50.28%). On inequality constraints, HARPOON dramatically outperforms baselines: range constraint violation drops to 3.06% (Adult) and 7.78% (Default) versus 73.86–96.53% for DiffPuter and GReaT, while maintaining comparable α-score fidelity (0.92 vs. 0.83–0.92) and downstream XGBoost utility (0.95 vs. 0.94–0.95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffPuter (the closest SOTA diffusion baseline), HARPOON wins decisively on all inequality constraint types: range violation drops from 73.86% to 3.06% on Adult and from 91.30% to 2.98% on Default for conjunctive constraints, while matching or exceeding DiffPuter's α-score fidelity. On imputation, HARPOON is roughly tied with DiffPuter on continuous features but clearly wins on categorical accuracy (e.g., Default 75%: 61.48% vs. 49.24%), attributed to DiffPuter's RePaint-style independent noising introducing semantic mismatches. Against GReaT (LLM-based), HARPOON dominates on inequality constraints (3–20% vs. 74–97% violation) and is competitive on imputation, while GReaT occasionally produces extreme MSE values (>10⁵) on some datasets. Against Remasker (masked autoencoder), HARPOON wins on categorical imputation across all missingness ratios and is competitive on continuous features, with Remasker degrading more sharply at 75% missingness. Against GAIN (GAN-based) and MIRACLE (causal-graph), HARPOON consistently outperforms on both imputation quality and stability; MIRACLE in particular collapses under high missingness (e.g., Default 75%: 1.84 MSE vs. HARPOON 0.94) because it constructs a fresh imputer per task rather than leveraging a pre-trained model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the manifold-guidance framework from image diffusion (Chung et al., 2022; He et al., 2024) to tabular data, removing three key limitations: the assumption of continuous-only features, the requirement of globally flat manifold geometry, and the restriction of tangent-space guarantees to squared-error losses. The core theoretical contribution generalizes Chung et al.'s orthogonal projection result to curved manifolds (requiring only local linearity) and proves that gradients of *any* differentiable inference-time loss lie in the tangent space, not just MSE. On the algorithmic side, HARPOON builds on the standard DDPM denoising loop (Ho et al., 2020) but interleaves it with tangential gradient corrections computed from task-specific losses, positioning it as a training-free inference-time method analogous to RePaint-style inpainting but with geometric guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 'train once, condition at inference' paradigm for tabular diffusion: a single MSE-trained model can handle imputation, range constraints, categorical fixes, and their conjunctions/disjunctions without retraining or fine-tuning, which prior training-time or classifier-guidance methods cannot do. The ablation showing MAE outperforms MSE at inference (despite MSE being the training objective) provides a clean empirical validation of the tangent-space theorem and suggests practitioners should decouple training and inference losses. Key limitations include the reliance on a continuous Gaussian noising process (incompatible with discrete/multinomial diffusion for categoricals), the orthogonality guarantee holding strictly only as α_t→1, and the evaluation being confined to small UCI-scale datasets; extending the theory to discrete noising mechanisms and larger, more heterogeneous tables remains open.
