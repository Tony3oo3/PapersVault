## Problem

LLMs frequently fail on compositional instructions where multiple sub-tasks are organized in mutually exclusive structures (chaining, branching, paralleling), because dormant sub-tasks inadvertently attract attention and interfere with the active one. Existing remedies—fine-tuning, iterative self-reflection, or high-level planning—incur substantial computational overhead and still cannot fully suppress attention to inactive sub-tasks during inference. The paper identifies this attention distraction as the root cause of three error types (wrong, mixed, and omitted generation) and targets it directly at the attention level.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a compositional instruction string and a frozen pre-trained LLM (LLaMA3-8B or Mistral-7B). A preliminary LLM call with a structured prompt extracts the structure type (Chain/Branch/Parallel) and the exact token spans of each mutually exclusive sub-task, which are then matched back to the original input to prevent token-level drift. During generation, the only modification is a bias matrix added to attention scores on a small subset of heads (20–50 out of 1024), so the output is standard autoregressive text with no architectural change or parameter update.

## Method

ATA (Attend To the Active) is a plug-and-play, parameter-free attention-steering framework operating within a single forward pass. It first applies mutual exclusive masking: a constant bias −α (α = log 100) is added to attention scores between every pair of structurally exclusive sub-task spans, preventing blending comprehension during encoding. At each generation step, ATA identifies the active sub-task by averaging attention scores that subsequent tokens allocate to each sub-task's tokens, subject to an entropy-based confidence threshold (ε = 0.5·log m) and a structural active-control rule that enforces sequential progression for Chain/Parallel and lock-in for Branch. It then suppresses attention to all sub-tasks exclusive with the active one via the same bias matrix, effectively upweighting the active sub-task by exp(α) after softmax. Steering is confined to a heuristically selected subset of heads that consistently focus on active sub-tasks, preserving global context and general language ability while incurring less than 7% runtime overhead.

## Results

On Llama3-8B-Instruct, ATA achieves 63.88% overall compliance across Chain, Branch, and Parallel compositional instructions, outperforming the Direct I/O baseline (57.88%) by 6.0 points. The largest gain appears on the Parallel structure, where ATA reaches 69.91% versus 59.80% for Direct I/O (+10.11 points). On Mistral-7B-Instruct, ATA similarly leads with 50.62% overall (vs. 46.67% Direct I/O), with a 6.45-point improvement on Parallel (41.34% vs. 34.89%). ATA consistently outperforms all baselines (CoT Prompting, Decomposition, Think-Execute, Self-correction) across every structure type and both models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATA outperforms all five baselines across every structure type and both models. Against CoT Prompting (Wei et al., 2022), ATA gains +4.65 points overall on Llama3-8B (63.88 vs. 59.23) and +3.20 on Mistral-7B (50.62 vs. 47.42), with the largest margin on Parallel (+4.94 and +3.72 respectively). Against Decomposition (Wen et al., 2024), ATA leads by +8.13 points overall on Llama3-8B and +6.35 on Mistral-7B, indicating that simply breaking instructions into sequential sub-tasks without attention control is insufficient. Against Think-Execute (Chae et al., 2024), ATA improves by +4.91 and +3.34 points overall, showing that high-level planning alone does not suppress interference from inactive sub-tasks during generation. Against Self-correction (Sun et al., 2024), ATA gains +4.47 and +4.02 points overall, demonstrating that iterative self-reflection cannot fully eliminate attention distraction within a single generation pass. Notably, on the Parallel structure where baselines struggle most (CoT Prompting is the only one that beats Direct I/O), ATA achieves the largest absolute improvements, confirming that dynamic attention steering is particularly effective when multiple independent sub-tasks compete for the model's focus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends compositional instruction following beyond the dominant paradigm of simultaneously satisfying coupled constraints (semantic, format) by identifying that sub-tasks are often structurally mutually exclusive, with only one active at each generation step. It builds on attention steering work (PASTA, autoPASTA, SampleAttention) but departs fundamentally: those methods apply static or global attention modifications, whereas ATA is structure-aware and dynamically re-targets attention as the active sub-task shifts during generation. The paper also introduces parallel structure as a new composition type absent from prior benchmarks like Complexbench and Chain-of-Instruction, and contrasts its single-forward-pass, parameter-free approach against fine-tuning, self-reflection, and planning-based methods that require multiple inference iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ATA offers a lightweight, plug-and-play mechanism that can be applied to any off-the-shelf LLM without retraining, making it practically attractive for deployment scenarios where compositional instructions are common. The introduction of parallel structure fills a gap in the compositional instruction literature, and the 'active vs. dormant' framing provides a clearer diagnostic lens for understanding why LLMs fail on complex instructions. Key limitations include the requirement for explicit sub-task structure (implicit structures need additional discovery techniques) and the heuristic nature of attention head selection. The authors flag structure-agnostic attention steering as an important open direction for broadening applicability.
