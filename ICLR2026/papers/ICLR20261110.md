## Problem

Existing Vision-and-Language Navigation benchmarks and protocols are single-agent centric, suppressing parallelism and ignoring inter-robot interference such as congestion, handoff errors, and rendezvous timing. No systematic benchmark exists for evaluating multi-robot collaborative VLN under long-horizon, multi-stage settings where a team must decompose missions, assign roles, and coordinate handoffs to minimize wall-clock time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261110/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At execution time, each robot receives synchronized RGB and depth images from three cameras (front, left +60°, right −60°) plus a language instruction and location cues, and outputs discrete actions (move forward 0.25 m, turn left/right ±30°, stop). At planning time, NavCraft consumes a semantically annotated scene graph (region nodes with 3D positions, connectivity edges, per-region asset/object lists) and a user profile, and produces a structured collaborative schedule specifying per-robot subtask instructions, a transfer region with a named handoff asset, and an ordered action list (Move_to, Grab, Release) for each robot. Episodes terminate when the agent issues stop or the geodesic distance to the goal falls below 1.0 m.

## Method

NavCraft is a two-stage, graph-grounded generation platform built on a semantically augmented Habitat-Sim connectivity graph. Stage 1 (NavCraft-S) samples a feasible start–target–end triple under profile-conditioned constraints and validates reachability and minimum hop length (τ=2) via a skill library; Stage 2 (NavCraft-C) lifts the single-robot plan into a collaborative schedule by instantiating a helper robot, selecting a transfer region and handoff asset, and accepting the plan only if the main agent's route load strictly decreases (improvement ratio α < 1) under either Type-A1 or Type-A2 handoff patterns. An on-graph efficiency tool library performs reachability checks, interference estimation, and iterative schedule repair without full-physics rollouts. For evaluation, a Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B policy with a two-level memory mechanism (short-term observation-action summaries plus long-term retrieval, following LH-VLN) is fine-tuned via supervised learning on the 4048-episode CoNavBench corpus, with a frozen EVA-CLIP-02-LARGE ViT backbone and Adam at lr 3×10⁻⁵ on four A800 GPUs.

## Results

On CoNavBench, the finetuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B achieves 35.02% step-level SR and 16.88% SPL in the collaborative setting, compared to 29.65% SR and 13.81% SPL in the single-agent setting, yielding an 18.11% relative step-level success gain. The finetuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B reaches 29.78% SR and 16.56% SPL collaboratively versus 22.40% SR and 12.57% SPL single-agent. Zero-shot performance remains weak (SR < 10% for both model sizes), confirming the benchmark's difficulty. Navigation error decreases from 6.74 m to 5.79 m (3B) and from 7.46 m to 6.20 m (7B) under collaboration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between single-agent and collaborative execution of the same finetuned Qwen2.5-VL models. The finetuned 3B model improves step-level SR from 29.65% (single-agent, Table 2) to 35.02% (collaborative, Table 3), a 5.37 pp absolute gain (18.11% relative), with SPL rising from 13.81% to 16.88% and NE dropping from 6.74 m to 5.79 m. The 7B model shows a similar pattern: SR increases from 22.40% to 29.78% and SPL from 12.57% to 16.56%. Against the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL baselines, finetuning yields large absolute gains (e.g., 3B step-level SR jumps from 19.86% to 35.02%), while the random baseline confirms the benchmark's non-trivial difficulty (SR ≈ 0%). Notably, the 7B model does not surpass the 3B model under the current data budget, which the authors attribute to under-training rather than a fundamental capacity limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long-horizon VLN line (LH-VLN, Song et al. 2025) by lifting the single-agent formulation to a two-robot collaborative setting with explicit handoff and rendezvous primitives, and borrows its two-level memory structure and step-by-step subtask decomposition. For data generation, it departs from text-only prompting (White et al., 2023) and asset-specific simulators (Holodeck, Yang et al., 2024) by grounding an LLM agent in a semantically augmented scene graph from Habitat-Sim, using the connectivity graph from ScaleVLN (Wang et al., 2023b) as the planning substrate and NavRAG (Wang et al., 2025b) role templates for user-profile-conditioned diversity. The key conceptual departure is treating the LLM as a data-generation and scheduling engine (NavCraft-S/C) rather than a navigator, and introducing a collaboration-type taxonomy (Type-A1/A2) that prior single-agent benchmarks (R2R, REVERIE, VLN-CE, IVLN, GOAT-Bench, LHPR-VLN) simply do not model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoNavBench is the first systematic benchmark for collaborative VLN, opening a research direction that prior single-agent formulations structurally cannot address (congestion, handoff errors, rendezvous timing, makespan optimization). However, the current state of the art is very modest—18.11% step-level success for the best collaborative policy—and the paper itself acknowledges a large gap between planned efficiency gains (~20%) and realized wall-clock performance, dominated by imperfect low-level navigation, interference, and coordination overhead. Coverage is limited to two-agent relay patterns in indoor HM3D scenes, leaving richer topologies (3+ robots, outdoor, dynamic obstacles) and the plan-execution gap as major open questions. The real-robot exhibition-hall demo and the Real2Sim pipeline suggest a practical path, but the benchmark will need substantially stronger baselines and larger scale before it can serve as a definitive evaluation standard.
