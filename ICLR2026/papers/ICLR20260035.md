## Problem

Nearly all existing egocentric vision benchmarks and multimodal large language models are developed and evaluated under well-lit daytime conditions, leaving model performance under nighttime low-light scenarios largely unexplored. A key obstacle is the difficulty of obtaining high-quality nighttime annotations and enabling controlled day-night comparisons, since perfectly aligned real-world day-night video pairs are hard to collect.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260035/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the primary VQA task, the model receives a nighttime egocentric video clip (3-second segments for static/spatial tasks, full-length videos for dynamic/temporal tasks) paired with an open-ended text question, and produces a free-form text answer evaluated via LLM-as-a-Judge. For retrieval, a query clip is matched against N=10 candidate clips of equal 10-second duration (spatial) or localized within a full parent video by predicting start/end timestamps (temporal). For depth estimation, undistorted or native fisheye RGB frames are mapped to per-pixel depth maps. A distinctive design choice is that paired QA types use identical questions for both day and night clips of the same scene, enabling direct illumination-gap measurement.

## Method

The core contribution is a benchmark construction pipeline rather than a new model architecture. EgoNight integrates three video sources—Blender/Infinigen-rendered synthetic pairs (50), video-guided real-world recordings in Sofia (20), and Oxford Day-and-Night segments (20)—all designed with day-night spatial and temporal alignment. A three-stage day-augmented auto-labeling pipeline generates QA pairs: GPT-4.1 produces nighttime captions, generates visually grounded questions, and synthesizes pseudo-answers using the aligned daytime clip as a strong prior; human annotators then refine via delete/modify/add operations, yielding 3,658 verified QA pairs across 12 types (8 paired, 4 unpaired). The day-augmentation strategy is the key insight: daytime visibility serves as a reliable annotation anchor for low-light clips, and the paired design isolates illumination as the sole variable. Fine-tuning experiments on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (full, vision-encoder-only, LLM-only SFT) demonstrate that synthetic nighttime supervision transfers to real-world performance, with the vision encoder adapting to low-light features and the LLM aligning with uncertain nighttime visual evidence.

## Results

On EgoNight-VQA, GPT-4.1 achieves the highest average accuracy at 30.93%, followed closely by Gemini 2.5 Pro (30.60%), while the best open-source model InternVL3-8B reaches only 20.06%. The egocentric-specific model EgoGPT scores just 14.29%, and LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B is worst at 7.28%. Day-to-night performance drops are 32.8% on EgoNight-Synthetic and 25.0% on EgoNight-Sofia, confirming substantial illumination sensitivity. For day-night correspondence retrieval, GPT-4.1 leads spatial retrieval (92.5% Acc-R@1 on Sofia Day→Day) but feature-based methods (DINOv2, Perception Encoder) outperform MLLMs on temporal localization (mIoU ~33% vs. ~10–15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Closed-source MLLMs (GPT-4.1, Gemini 2.5 Pro) lead all open-source models by roughly 10–11 points in average VQA accuracy, with GPT-4.1 (30.93%) narrowly edging Gemini 2.5 Pro (30.60%). Among open-source models, InternVL3-8B (20.06%) outperforms the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (18.99%), indicating that parameter scale alone does not guarantee better egocentric nighttime understanding. The egocentric-specific EgoGPT (14.29%) underperforms even mid-size general MLLMs like Qwen2.5-VL-7B (13.44%) and GLM-4.1V (18.20%), suggesting that current egocentric fine-tuning does not transfer well to low-light conditions. For day-night retrieval, GPT-4.1 dominates spatial retrieval (84.5% Night→Day on Sofia) over DINOv2 (74.5%) and Perception Encoder (80.9%), yet on temporal localization, feature-based methods (DINOv2 mIoU 33.1%, Perception Encoder 33.4%) substantially outperform GPT-4.1 (15.5%) and InternVL3-8B (13.3%), exposing MLLMs' weakness in fine-grained temporal reasoning. For depth estimation, fisheye-specific models (UniK3D AbsRel 0.253, DAC 0.292) outperform general monocular estimators (Depth Anything 0.302, VGGTStream 0.298) at night, and all methods show a clear day-to-night degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the egocentric VQA benchmark lineage (EgoVQA, EgoTaskQA, EgoSchema, EgoTempo, EgoCross, EgoBlind) by introducing the previously unaddressed nighttime illumination dimension, positioning low-light robustness as a new axis of domain generalization for egocentric video understanding. Methodologically, it repurposes Blender/Infinigen for pixel-perfect day-night synthetic pairs and designs a video-guided real-world recording protocol, while its three-stage day-augmented auto-labeling pipeline (GPT-4.1 captioning → question generation → day-augmented answer synthesis) is a novel annotation strategy that leverages aligned daytime clips as priors for nighttime QA construction. The Oxford Day-and-Night dataset is incorporated as an unaligned real-world complement, and the auxiliary tasks (day-night retrieval, nighttime depth) draw on cross-domain retrieval and monocular depth estimation literature but are the first to target egocentric nighttime settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoNight fills a clear gap: no prior egocentric benchmark systematically evaluates models under low-light conditions, yet real-world assistants must operate at night. The consistent 25–33% day-to-night accuracy drops across all 10 evaluated MLLMs (including GPT-4.1 and Gemini 2.5 Pro) establish illumination robustness as a concrete, measurable open problem. The finding that perception tasks degrade more than reasoning tasks at night, and that MLLMs are substantially worse than feature-based methods at temporal localization, points to specific architectural weaknesses. Limitations include the modest video count (90), the unaligned Oxford subset, and reliance on LLM-as-a-Judge for open-ended QA scoring; future work could scale the dataset, explore illumination-adaptive architectures, and investigate whether the strong synthetic-to-real correlation (r=0.94) enables data-efficient nighttime model training.
