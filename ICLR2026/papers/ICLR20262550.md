## Problem

Existing open-source articulated-object datasets for robot simulation (PartNet-Mobility, BEHAVIOR-1K) suffer from insufficient visual realism, uncalibrated joint dynamics, and lack of modular interaction behaviors, limiting their utility for closing the sim-to-real gap in dexterous manipulation tasks. The core bottleneck is asset quality rather than quantity: inconsistent modeling standards, distorted meshes, and missing physical parameters prevent reliable transfer of learned policies to the real world.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262550/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes real-world objects as reference for digital-twin creation, with professional 3D modelers producing USD-format assets organized in a three-level hierarchy (assembly → rigid-body module → mesh). Each asset embeds high-resolution PBR textures, optimized collision geometry (convex hulls, convex decomposition, or fine-tuned meshes), tuned joint parameters, and pixel-level affordance labels per module. Downstream robot learning consumes multi-view RGB images (four RealSense cameras) plus proprioceptive states as input and outputs end-effector actions; the dataset also ships six sim-ready indoor scenes where every object supports physical interaction.

## Method

ArtVIP is authored by professional 3D modelers under a unified specification, prioritizing fidelity over scale (992 objects, 9 categories, 37 subcategories). Visual realism is achieved through high-triangle-count manifold meshes (up to ~164k faces for toilets), high-resolution PBR textures rendered via Isaac Sim's RTX pipeline, and UV-aligned material maps. Physical fidelity is enforced by enhancing Isaac Sim's joint drive equation to support position-dependent stiffness K(q) and velocity-dependent friction (static-to-dynamic transition), calibrated against optical motion capture (0.1 mm, 90 Hz) of real objects. A key innovation is embedding five canonical interaction primitives (latching/magnetic closure, damping, cross-asset effects, within-asset effects, hover/hold position) directly into the USD assets, covering 394 objects and 900+ joints, so that importing a file instantly yields interactive behavior without additional code. Fidelity is validated quantitatively via CLIP feature alignment (t-SNE), VGGT reconstruction quality, and sim-to-real success rates in imitation learning (ACT, DP) and reinforcement learning (EAGLE) experiments.

## Results

On four articulated-object manipulation tasks (PullDrawer, OpenCabinet, SlideShelf, CloseOven), sim-only training with ArtVIP assets achieves non-trivial zero-shot real-world success (e.g., ACT 39% on PullDrawer, DP 45% on PullDrawer), demonstrating effective sim-to-real transfer. Mixing 100 real + 100 simulated trajectories (RSM100+100) boosts DP success from 44% (real-only) to 59% on SlideShelf and from 66% to 78% on CloseOven. In the RL CloseTrashcan task, EAGLE trained on ArtVIP reaches 98% success at 500k iterations versus 24% for vision-based PPO, with a Pearson correlation of 0.9886 between simulated and real-world performance across checkpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PartNet-Mobility on the microwave door-pull task, ArtVIP assets yield consistently higher sim-to-real transfer: under sim-only (SO), ArtVIP achieves 41% (ACT) and 45% (DP) versus 32% and 35% for PartNet-Mobility; under RSM100+500, ArtVIP reaches 79% (ACT) and 83% (DP) versus 68% and 70%, a 11–13 percentage-point gap. Against BEHAVIOR-1K, ArtVIP demonstrates superior visual realism through higher triangle counts (e.g., 164k vs. 15k for toilets), better VGGT reconstruction fidelity, and CLIP feature distributions that align more closely with real-world images. Against the generative pipeline SplArt, ArtVIP digital-twin assets produce substantially lower Chamfer Distance on real-world objects (e.g., CD-Whole 12.35 for ArtVIP cabinet vs. 5.21 for SplArt on synthetic, but SplArt degrades to 13.71 CD-Static on real data) and avoid self-collisions, mesh distortions, and incorrect joint parameters that plague generated outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of sourcing articulated-object assets from public 3D repositories (as in PartNet-Mobility) or locking them into proprietary simulators (as in BEHAVIOR-1K/OmniGibson), instead prioritizing fidelity over scale through professional manual modeling under unified standards. It extends Isaac Sim's standard joint drive equation by parameterizing stiffness and target position as functions of joint state and velocity, enabling complex behaviors like door closers and magnetic latching. For downstream evaluation, it builds on EAGLE's two-stage visuomotor RL framework and standard imitation learning baselines (ACT, Diffusion Policy), applying them to the newly constructed digital-twin assets rather than introducing new learning algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ArtVIP addresses a practical bottleneck in robot learning: the lack of open-source, physically accurate articulated assets that reduce the sim-to-real gap. The 0.9886 Pearson correlation between simulated and real-world RL performance, along with consistent improvements from mixing sim and real data, provides strong evidence that asset quality (not just quantity) is the limiting factor. However, the dataset's scale (992 objects, 9 categories) is constrained by intensive human labor, and the authors acknowledge that generative methods will be needed to broaden coverage. Open questions include whether the modular interaction primitives generalize to novel object categories and whether the USD-based workflow will be adopted broadly beyond Isaac Sim.
