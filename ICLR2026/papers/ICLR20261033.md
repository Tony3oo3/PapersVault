## Problem

Sampling from a trained predictor's induced Gibbs density is essential for interpretability and compute-efficient design, but standard MCMC samplers (e.g., Gibbs-with-Gradients) suffer exponential mixing times on the rugged, high-frequency landscapes and rare synergistic interactions that such predictors learn. Existing remedies—training a separate reward-conditioned diffusion model or applying temperature annealing—require substantial extra compute or fail to bypass the fundamental random-walk exploration caused by concealed multi-variable interactions. The paper asks whether the predictor's own training trajectory can be repurposed as a free, plug-and-play smoothing schedule for sampling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained scalar predictor f*: X → R (MSE-trained on supervised data), the sequence of its saved training checkpoints {f_t}_{t=0}^T, an initial state, and optional hard constraints (e.g., a Hamming-radius ball for minimal-edit counterfactuals). It produces samples from π*(x) ∝ exp{f*(x)} under those constraints, usable as high-value candidates for active learning, minimal counterfactual edits for interpretability, or design proposals (DNA sequences, superconductor compositions). No auxiliary generative or score model is trained; the only requirement is that checkpoints were saved during the original training run.

## Method

The core mechanism is trajectory annealing: rather than running MCMC solely on the final target π*, the sampler traverses the training checkpoints from early (smooth) to late (detailed), applying a short Markov kernel (GWG+MH for discrete inputs, MALA or SMC for continuous inputs) at each checkpoint before carrying the state forward. In the Boolean domain, this exploits the hierarchical learning dynamics of SGD (Abbe et al., 2023): early checkpoints have effectively learned only low-degree monomials, so the degree-≤2 surrogate is a Curie-Weiss Hamiltonian that mixes in O(d log d) steps, converting the exponential random-walk mixing of the full needle gadget into near-linear hitting time. In the continuous domain under the NTK regime, the training trajectory provides a degree-wise smoothing of spherical-harmonic coefficients (M_k^NTK(t) = 1 − exp(−ηλ_k t)), yielding a natural coarse-to-fine continuum analogous to heat-kernel smoothing. The approach requires no additional training, no architecture modification, and no auxiliary generative model, and it handles hard constraints (e.g., Hamming-ball restriction) by simply restricting the MCMC chain to the feasible set at every checkpoint.

## Results

On the Ackley 10D continuous benchmark, SMC-Train achieves a mean objective of 13.33 (best-of-set 3.69), outperforming MCMC-Final (16.22), SMC-Temp (16.31), AIS-AutoTemp (16.31), and Parallel Tempering (19.87) under matched compute. On the 87-dimensional Superconductor design task, SMC-Train reaches a best Tc of 318.4 K and mean 155.2 K, exceeding the reference value of 185.0 K and all baselines (MCMC-Final: best 107.4, mean 76.68). On binary MNIST EBM sampling, the method achieves FID 5.49 at 10K steps versus 21.12 for temperature-annealed GWG. On the MAX transcription-factor DNA design task, median sample fitness is 10.04 (log-scale) versus 2.72 for plain GWG, a >10^7-fold improvement, with motif recovery at 74% vs. 39%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary discrete baseline is Gibbs-with-Gradients (GWG) from Grathwohl et al. (2021), used both as plain GWG on the final checkpoint and as temperature-annealed GWG (Temp-GWG). The proposed trajectory-annealed method dominates GWG across all discrete tasks: on the 8-variable high-frequency polynomial, it achieves 52% global-maximum hits in 40 steps versus 4% for Temp-GWG in 2000 steps (a 50× step budget); on the 10-variable indicator with 500 spurious variables, it reaches 99% success versus 12% for GWG; and on three non-overlapping length-5 indicators, it achieves 100% versus 2.5%. On binary MNIST, the method reduces FID from 21.12 to 5.49 at 10K steps. In the continuous domain, SMC-Train outperforms all four temperature-based baselines (SMC-Temp, AIS-AutoTemp, PT, and plain MCMC-Final) on both Ackley and Superconductor with non-overlapping confidence intervals, achieving a 17.8% mean reduction on Ackley and a 2.0× mean Tc improvement on Superconductor. The method also handles constrained sampling (Hamming-ball restriction) natively, where temperature-annealing baselines (AIS, PT) degrade to near-chance fitness, while the trajectory method maintains 99.45th-percentile fitness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three existing threads—hierarchical SGD learning of Boolean functions (Abbe et al., 2023), NTK spectral bias showing degree-wise eigenvalue decay (Murray et al., 2022), and gradient-informed discrete MCMC kernels like Gibbs-with-Gradients (Grathwohl et al., 2021)—into a single test-time sampling procedure that traverses training checkpoints as a free coarse-to-fine annealing schedule. It departs from prior smoothing-for-sampling work (reward-conditioned diffusion, graph-smoothed fitness models) by requiring no additional generative model or explicit smoothing operator, and from temperature-annealing MCMC by arguing that temperature relaxation alone cannot bypass the random-walk exploration caused by rare synergistic interactions. The O(d log d) mixing guarantee for the needle gadget is inherited from censored Glauber dynamics on the Curie-Weiss model (Ding et al., 2009), applied here to the degree-2 projection that early checkpoints approximate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a zero-extra-compute, plug-and-play way to sample from deployed CNN/MLP predictors, which remain dominant in medicine, recommendation, and decision-support settings where interpretability and constrained sampling (e.g., minimal counterfactual edits, Hamming-ball perturbations) are critical. A key open question is whether the coarse-to-fine property—and hence the method—extends to transformers, which the authors note learn interactions via attention in a qualitatively different manner and where their empirical checks show the degree-wise alignment property partially breaks down. The theoretical guarantees are also restricted to idealized settings (NTK regime for continuous, specific Boolean function classes for discrete), so the generality of the exponential-to-near-linear mixing improvement in realistic, non-idealized training remains an open empirical question.
