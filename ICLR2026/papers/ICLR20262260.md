## Problem

The initial noise seed in video diffusion models profoundly affects generation quality, temporal coherence, and prompt alignment, yet prior noise-initialization methods (FreeInit, FreqPrior, PYoCo) rely on externally designed frequency or inter-frame priors and require multiple full diffusion passes. No existing approach leverages the model's own internal signals—specifically attention-based uncertainty—to identify which seeds are inherently preferable without retraining or modifying the sampling process.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt c and a pool of M=10 candidate Gaussian noise seeds z_i ∈ R^{N×d}, the method computes a BANSA score for each seed using Bernoulli-masked attention (p=0.2) at the first denoising step across a truncated set of d* attention layers (model-specific, e.g., 14 for CogVideoX-2B, 19 for CogVideoX-5B). The seed with the lowest BANSA score is selected and fed into standard DDIM sampling (50 steps) to produce the final video. The overhead is only ~10–15% inference time since the overhead comes solely from evaluating M seeds at one timestep with K=10 stochastic attention samples, leaving the main sampling loop and memory footprint unchanged.

## Method

ANSE introduces BANSA, a BALD-style acquisition function that measures epistemic uncertainty in the attention space of a diffusion model rather than in classification logits. For each noise seed, K stochastic attention maps are obtained from a single forward pass by applying independent Bernoulli(p) masks to the attention scores and re-normalizing rows; the BANSA score is then H(mean attention) − mean(H(attention)), which is provably zero if and only if all K attention maps are identical (Proposition 1). To keep cost low, the score is computed only at the first denoising step and over a truncated prefix of layers d* chosen by the smallest depth where the cumulative layer-wise BANSA correlates ≥0.7 with the full-layer score. The selected seed minimizes this truncated BANSA score, and the framework is plug-and-play across U-Net (AnimateDiff) and MMDiT (CogVideoX, Wan2.1, HunyuanVideo) backbones without any retraining or architectural modification.

## Results

On VBench, ANSE improves the Total Score from 77.98 to 79.33 on AnimateDiff (+1.35), from 81.03 to 81.66 on CogVideoX-2B (+0.63), and from 81.52 to 81.71 on CogVideoX-5B (+0.19), with inference overhead of only +8.7% to +13.1%. On HunyuanVideo and Wan2.1, ANSE lifts Subject Consistency from 0.9562 to 0.9612 and Temporal Flickering from 0.9921 to 0.9938 (HunyuanVideo) / 0.9943 to 0.9956 (Wan2.1). On MSR-VTT, FVMD drops from 17641.19 to 16491.68 (HunyuanVideo) and from 16495.59 to 14306.19 (Wan2.1 1.3B), confirming improved motion fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is FreqPrior (Yuan et al., 2025), a frequency-filtering noise prior evaluated on AnimateDiff. ANSE alone (Total 79.33) outperforms FreqPrior (Total 79.07) by 0.26 points while requiring only +11% inference overhead versus FreqPrior's +105%. More notably, the two methods are fully compatible: combining FreqPrior with ANSE yields the best Total Score of 80.43, surpassing either method in isolation. This demonstrates that ANSE's model-aware uncertainty signal is orthogonal to external frequency priors. On CogVideoX-2B/5B, where FreqPrior has no official support, ANSE still delivers consistent gains of +0.63 and +0.19 Total Score respectively, confirming generalizability beyond the single backbone where the baseline was tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the BALD mutual-information acquisition function (Gal et al., 2017) from classification active learning to the attention space of video diffusion models, replacing classification logits with attention maps as the stochastic prediction. It departs from prior noise-initialization methods (FreeInit, FreqPrior, PYoCo, FreeNoise) that impose external frequency or inter-frame priors on the noise, instead reading the model's own internal confidence signal. The Bernoulli-masked attention approximation is a practical contribution that collapses K stochastic forward passes into a single pass with element-wise masking, and the cumulative-correlation layer selection further reduces cost without sacrificing ranking quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ANSE offers a principled, architecture-agnostic, plug-and-play mechanism for noise selection with under 15% inference overhead, which is attractive for deployment on diverse T2V backbones from U-Net to MMDiT. However, the method only selects among candidate seeds and does not alter the subsequent denoising trajectory, so low-BANSA seeds can still yield unnatural content (Fig. 6), and the absolute VBench gains are modest (~1–2 points). A key open direction the authors identify is combining ANSE with post-training refinement (e.g., Self-Forcing) to close the gap between seed-level uncertainty and full generation quality, and whether attention-level uncertainty can be extended to capture semantic or aesthetic dimensions that it currently misses.
