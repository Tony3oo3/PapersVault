## Problem

Existing tabular test-time adaptation methods either require access to source training data or domain-specific class priors, and they fail to simultaneously address both label shift and feature shift in a truly prior-free setting. This paper introduces the prior-free tabular TTA problem, where no source data or prior knowledge is available, and proposes a method that handles both distribution shift types without filtering test samples or relying on external priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262360/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained source model (TabTransformer, MLP, or FT-Transformer) and unlabeled tabular test data arriving sequentially in fixed-size batches, with no access to source data or class priors. It produces calibrated class predictions by element-wise scaling raw model outputs with the estimated target-to-source prior ratio, effectively correcting for label shift at inference time. The tabular input space is heterogeneous (continuous and discrete columns), and the method operates online, updating the model incrementally per batch.

## Method

PFT3A comprises three modules operating jointly per batch. First, Class Prior Estimating splits each batch into source-like (low prediction entropy) and target-like (high entropy) subsets, estimates source and target class priors from these subsets, and calibrates predictions via element-wise multiplication with the target/source prior ratio. Second, Robust Feature Learning computes Gaussian feature distributions (mean and variance) for the source-like and target-like subsets and minimizes the closed-form KL divergence between them to align feature representations. Third, Representative Subspace Exploration projects features into a top-m principal component subspace derived from the source-like covariance matrix before performing KL alignment, eliminating redundant dimensions that cause numerical instability and spurious correlations in tabular data. The final optimization objective combines the subspace KL divergence loss with an entropy minimization loss, weighted by hyperparameters β₁ and β₂.

## Results

Using TabTransformer as backbone, PFT3A achieves average accuracy of 68.59%, balanced accuracy of 67.42%, and F1 of 72.65% across five TableShift datasets, improving over Non-Adaptation by +7.82% Acc, +3.96% BAcc, and +12.73% F1. The largest gains appear on Health Insurance (Acc: 74.13% vs. 65.35% Non-Adaptation) and HELOC (Acc: 66.17% vs. 55.66%). Results are consistent across MLP and FT-Transformer backbones, with average Acc of 69.25% and 68.01% respectively, confirming generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFT3A outperforms all seven baselines across all three backbones (TabTransformer, MLP, FT-Transformer) on average Acc, BAcc, and F1. The closest competitor is FTAT (the only tabular-specific TTA method), which PFT3A surpasses by +4.34% Acc, +1.85% BAcc, and +6.27% F1 with TabTransformer; notably, FTAT requires source class priors while PFT3A operates fully prior-free. Classic vision TTA methods (TENT, EATA, CoTTA, SAR, LAME) all underperform or barely match Non-Adaptation on tabular data, with TENT dropping to 41.74% Acc on Hypertension (vs. 54.87% Non-Adaptation), confirming their inapplicability to tabular modality. ODS performs worst among all TTA methods (57.56% avg Acc), even below Non-Adaptation. The largest single-dataset gain is on Health Insurance, where PFT3A achieves 74.13% Acc versus 65.35% (Non-Adaptation) and 66.31% (FTAT), reflecting its effectiveness under the largest label shift (0.1701) in the benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tabular test-time adaptation line initiated by FTAT (Zhou et al., 2024), which removed the need for source data but still required class priors from the source distribution. PFT3A departs from FTAT by eliminating the class prior dependency through an entropy-based source-like/target-like data splitting mechanism, and further introduces KL-divergence-based feature alignment in a PCA-reduced subspace to address feature shift without filtering low-confidence samples. The method also positions itself against classic visual TTA approaches (TENT, EATA, CoTTA, SAR, LAME, ODS) that the paper demonstrates are ineffective on tabular data, and against tabular-specific methods (AdapTable, TabLog) that assume source data access. Conceptually, it repurposes domain adaptation tools (Gaussian KL alignment, subspace projection) within the online, batch-wise TTA framework established by the TableShift benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prior-free constraint addresses a practical deployment gap: in real-world tabular applications (credit scoring, healthcare, education), source data and class priors are often unavailable due to privacy or operational constraints, yet existing TTA methods implicitly require them. The finding that removing the Class Prior Estimating module causes performance to drop below the non-adaptation baseline on label-shift-heavy datasets (Health Insurance) highlights that naive feature alignment without prior correction is actively harmful, a non-obvious insight for practitioners. Open questions include robustness under adversarial manipulation of the source model, performance under severe distribution shifts where the source-like/target-like split becomes unreliable, and scalability to very small batch sizes where statistical estimates of class priors and covariance matrices become unstable.
