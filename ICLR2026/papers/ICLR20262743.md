## Problem

Current video generation models produce visually impressive but physically inconsistent outputs that violate fundamental laws like gravity and object permanence, while existing Gaussian-based physics simulators achieve physical consistency at prohibitive computational costs and struggle with complex multi-object interactions. The key gap is a unified framework that learns physics-grounded dynamics directly from visual observations while maintaining both computational efficiency and generalization to unseen spatial, temporal, and compositional configurations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262743/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N unposed multi-view RGB images of a dynamic scene at the initial time step (no camera calibration required) and produces temporally consistent 4D multi-view videos at arbitrary future time steps and camera poses. Beyond standard video prediction, the framework outputs explicit object-centric force fields and 3D Gaussian trajectories, enabling interactive generation via external force prompts, novel-view synthesis from unseen camera angles, and novel-background composition. The GSCollision dataset provides 640k rendered sequences (~4 TB) spanning 100 simulation steps (~2 seconds) across 3,200 scenes with 3–6 interacting objects.

## Method

NGFF is a two-stage end-to-end framework: (1) a feed-forward reconstruction module (π³-GS, built on the permutation-equivariant π³ backbone with a modified convolutional splatter head) converts unposed multi-view RGB into object-aware 3D Gaussians, with SAM2-based segmentation and DiffSplat single-view refinement to handle occlusions; (2) a DeepONet-based neural operator on a relational graph predicts object-centric force fields—combining a 6-DoF global component (translation + rotation) from pairwise object interactions and a per-point local stress field for soft-body deformation—conditioned on PointNet-extracted semantic features, zeroth-order states (position, orientation), and first-order states (velocity, angular velocity). These force fields are integrated via second-order ODE solvers to produce continuous trajectories, which are rendered through differentiable Gaussian splatting. Training is decoupled: the reconstruction head is fine-tuned on WildRGBD with RGB and geometric consistency losses, while the dynamics module is trained on MPM simulation ground truth with MSE loss, achieving ~100× speedup over traditional MPM simulators while outperforming them in accuracy across spatial, temporal, and compositional generalization.

## Results

On the GSCollision dataset, NGFF achieves the best dynamic prediction across all generalization dimensions: Spatial RMSE 0.082 (vs. 0.096 for Pointformer, 0.134 for GCN), Temporal RMSE 0.107, and Compositional RMSE 0.104, while running ~108× faster than VLM-MPM (0.363s vs. 39.29s per 100-step rollout). For video generation, NGFF-V attains the highest Physical Realism scores (PhysR 0.56 on novel-background, 0.47 on compositional) compared to Cosmos-tuned (0.38, 0.26) and Veo3 (0.29 on the comprehensive split), though Veo3 leads on Photo Realism (0.64 vs. 0.55). The ablation without deformation modeling (NGFF w/o deform.) degrades Spatial RMSE from 0.082 to 0.110, confirming the contribution of local stress field prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NGFF outperforms all five baselines on dynamic prediction. Against VLM-MPM (PhysGen3D), NGFF reduces Spatial RMSE from 0.306 to 0.082 (73% improvement) and is ~108× faster (0.363s vs. 39.29s). Against GCN, NGFF improves Spatial RMSE by 39% (0.082 vs. 0.134) and Compositional R by 64% (0.571 vs. 0.347). Against Pointformer, the closest particle-based competitor, NGFF achieves lower RMSE across all splits (e.g., Compositional 0.104 vs. 0.162) and higher correlation R (0.571 vs. 0.434), though Pointformer is slightly faster (0.183s vs. 0.363s). In video generation, NGFF-V surpasses Cosmos-tuned on Physical Realism across all splits (e.g., 0.56 vs. 0.38 on novel-background) and Veo3 on the comprehensive split (0.30 vs. 0.29 PhysR), but Veo3 retains a clear advantage on Photo Realism (0.64 vs. 0.55 human-evaluated), reflecting the inherent trade-off between physics grounding and rendering fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

NGFF unifies two previously separate research threads: feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction (building on π³ and VGGT) and neural dynamics prediction (building on GNN-based simulators like Pointformer and GNN). It departs from MPM-based Gaussian simulators (PhysGaussian, OmniPhysGS) by replacing the explicit physics engine with a learned neural operator that predicts force fields over a relational graph, integrated via ODE solvers. The framework also contrasts with diffusion-based video generation models (Veo3, Cosmos) that achieve visual quality but lack physical grounding, positioning explicit force field modeling as the missing link between perception and physics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-orders-of-magnitude speedup over MPM simulators while maintaining superior physical accuracy makes learned force fields a practical alternative for real-time interactive simulation, and the 640k-video GSCollision dataset (~4 TB) provides a much-needed benchmark for physics-grounded 4D video prediction. Key open questions remain: the method still requires multi-view inputs (unlike human single-glance reasoning), scales to only 10 object types, and exhibits a visual-quality trade-off relative to pure 2D video generators. The explicit force field representation also opens a path toward interpretable causal reasoning and counterfactual 'what-if' queries that black-box video models cannot support.
