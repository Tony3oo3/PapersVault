## Problem

Unified understanding-and-generation frameworks have made significant progress for 2D images, but 3D modalities remain largely unexplored. Two bottlenecks prevent progress: (1) standard vision encoders pretrained on 2D semantic tasks lack the geometric modeling needed for spatial reasoning, and (2) LLMs' autoregressive tokenization paradigm is ill-suited to irregular 3D representations like point clouds, making it difficult to integrate 3D generation into an LLM-based architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261833/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For 3D generation, the model takes a single reference RGB image (224×224) and a relative view transformation encoded as a 6-channel Plücker raymap at the same spatial resolution as the ViT patch grid (14×14), and outputs a 3D point cloud decoded from both the reference and generated target-view ViT representations. For spatial VQA, it takes an image and a natural-language question and produces a text answer autoregressively. The generated 3D scene is a colored point cloud that can be projected back to 2D for evaluation, and the model can also caption the generated scene from its visual representation.

## Method

UniUGG introduces a three-stage pipeline. First, a ViT-L/16 encoder is pretrained with a geometric-semantic strategy that simultaneously distills semantic features from a RADIOv2.5 teacher (via cosine + L1 alignment on token subsets) and learns multi-view geometry through MASt3R's dual cross-attention decoder (predicting pointmaps, confidence maps, and matching descriptors), yielding representations that capture both semantics and 3D structure. Second, a Spatial-VAE compresses the 1024-dim ViT token sequence (196 tokens) into a compact 4-dim latent grid (28×28), with the spatial decoder fine-tuned end-to-end alongside the VAE to handle reconstruction discrepancies. Third, a Qwen2.5-3B LLM consumes the reference image's ViT tokens and Plücker-raymap-derived queries to produce conditional features, which drive a Stable Diffusion v1.5 denoiser to generate the target-view latent tokens; the spatial decoder then decodes both views into a 3D point cloud. The LLM is jointly trained on spatial VQA (cross-entropy) and 3D generation (noise-prediction MSE) in a single unified stage, enabling simultaneous understanding and generation.

## Results

On spatial understanding, UniUGG-3B achieves 40.1 on VSI-Bench (outperforming GPT-4o by 17.9%), 43.6 on BLINK, 52.1 on 3DSRBench, and 47.2 on SPAR, surpassing all compared LMMs including InternVL2.5-8B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B. On 3D generation, UniUGG achieves FID 55.01 / KID 0.0425 / LPIPS 0.4849 on ARKitScenes and FID 55.64 / KID 0.0442 / LPIPS 0.4263 on ScanNet++, substantially outperforming CUT3R (FID 138.54/130.76) and LVSM (FID 269.45/414.63). The geometric-semantic encoder also leads on Feat2GS novel view synthesis, achieving the best PSNR across Geometry, Texture, and All probing modes on both Casual and MipNeRF360 datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniUGG-3B decisively outperforms GPT-4o on VSI-Bench (40.1 vs. 34.0, a 17.9% margin) and SPAR (47.2 vs. 38.1), while GPT-4o retains an advantage on BLINK (60.0 vs. 43.6) and 3DSRBench is competitive (52.1 vs. 44.2 in UniUGG's favor). Among open-source LMMs, UniUGG surpasses InternVL2.5-8B on VSI-Bench (40.1 vs. 32.5) and SPAR (47.2 vs. 36.3), and Qwen2.5-VL-7B on VSI-Bench (40.1 vs. 30.3) and SPAR (47.2 vs. 33.1), despite using only a 3B LLM backbone versus 7–8B. For 3D generation, UniUGG's FID of 55.01 on ARKitScenes is 2.5× lower than CUT3R (138.54) and 4.9× lower than LVSM (269.45), with similar margins on ScanNet++. The geometric-semantic encoder outperforms MASt3R, DUSt3R, VGGT, DINOv2, and CLIP on Feat2GS novel view synthesis across all three probing modes, and leads on VSI-Bench (42.18), BLINK (44.40), and SPAR (50.82/49.07/51.89) in downstream VLM evaluation while maintaining competitive scores on general QA benchmarks (SEED-I 71.65, RealWorldQA 58.56). On 3D-specific benchmarks (SQA3D, ScanQA), UniUGG achieves competitive results without explicit 3D inputs, though it trails 3D-enhanced methods like Video-3D LLM (58.6 vs. 51.3 EM on SQA3D), and on ScanRefer grounding it lags significantly (41.9 vs. 58.1 Acc@0.25) due to the absence of explicit depth/3D supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified 2D understanding-and-generation paradigm (Janus, Chameleon, etc.) to 3D scenes by coupling an LLM with a diffusion model that operates on compressed geometric-semantic visual representations rather than pixel-space tokens. The spatial decoder is directly inherited from MASt3R (cross-attention decoders, pointmap regression, confidence-aware losses), while the ViT-L/16 encoder architecture and semantic pretraining initialization come from RADIOv2.5, which also serves as a knowledge-distillation teacher. The key architectural departure from both lineages is the introduction of a Spatial-VAE that compresses multi-view geometric-semantic features into a 4-dimensional latent space, enabling diffusion-based generation of 3D-consistent representations without requiring explicit 3D tokenization of point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly perform spatial VQA and 3D scene generation within a single LLM-based architecture, demonstrating that a 3B-parameter model can outperform GPT-4o on spatial reasoning benchmarks (VSI-Bench: 40.1 vs. 34.0) while also producing geometrically coherent point clouds. The geometric-semantic encoder pretraining strategy is a reusable technique that could benefit other 3D-aware vision-language systems. However, significant limitations remain: the model lacks language-controllable generation, cannot perform freeform editing, does not support interactive multi-round scene construction, and still trails 3D-enhanced methods on benchmarks like ScanRefer (41.9 vs. 58.1 Acc@0.25), suggesting that implicit 3D learning from 2D inputs has not yet fully replaced explicit 3D representations for fine-grained spatial grounding.
