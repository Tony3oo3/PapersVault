## Problem

Existing peptide design methods are structure-centric, generating sequences and 3D geometries in a decoupled manner and applying physics or evolutionary checks only post hoc. No prior approach ensures that generated peptides are simultaneously physically stable, evolutionarily plausible, and sequence-structure coherent within a single generative process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260926/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a rigid receptor binding pocket (all-atom coordinates) and a binary design mask M ∈ {0,1}^L specifying which residue positions to redesign, operating on peptides predominantly shorter than 30 amino acids in an all-atom representation (C=14 channels covering backbone and sidechains). It outputs a joint peptide amino acid sequence (20 canonical residues) and a 3D all-atom structure, with noise and supervision applied only at masked positions while unmasked context residues are clamped throughout the diffusion trajectory.

## Method

PepTri uses a two-stage framework: an SE(3)-equivariant VAE (built on an enhanced adaptive multichannel EGNN) compresses sequence-structure inputs into disentangled latents (invariant sequence z_h and equivariant structural anchors z_x), followed by a masked latent inpainting diffusion model with a cosine schedule. The key novelty is tri-guidance injected directly into the denoising process: (i) physics-informed guidance computes differentiable molecular mechanics energy (bond, angle, van der Waals, electrostatics, clash) plus an OpenMM/Amber14 force-field term, with gradients applied explicitly to the structural noise prediction at each reverse step; (ii) evolutionary guidance uses learned BLOSUM-like residue embeddings, multi-head self-attention for co-evolutionary dependencies, and conservation/fitness heads trained with self-supervised losses; (iii) mutual information (MINE) regularization maximizes alignment between pooled sequence and structure embeddings. Physics guidance acts as an explicit gradient correction on the structural component during sampling, while evolutionary and MI guidance shape the denoiser parameters, and the full training objective jointly minimizes the masked diffusion loss with all three regularizers.

## Results

On the cross-domain PepBench benchmark, PepTri achieves the highest success rate (0.40 pre-relaxation / 0.83 post-relaxation), most favorable binding energy (ΔG = −19.39 / −36.36 REU), and best DockQ (0.63 / 0.62) among all methods. On the in-domain PepBDB benchmark, PepTri similarly leads in DockQ (0.49 / 0.49) and success rate (0.31 / 0.74). For structural accuracy, PepTri attains the highest Contact_F1 (0.83 / 0.84 on PepBench; 0.75 / 0.77 on PepBDB) and GDT_TS (0.75 / 0.76 on PepBench; 0.66 / 0.67 on PepBDB), with the best post-relaxation Local RMSD on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PepTri outperforms all three peptide-specific baselines (PepGLAD, PepFlow, UniMoMo_single) on the primary binding metrics across both benchmarks. On PepBench, PepTri's success rate of 0.40 exceeds UniMoMo (0.34), PepFlow (0.31), and PepGLAD (0.29) by 6–38% relative, while its DockQ of 0.63 surpasses the next-best PepGLAD (0.60) and substantially outperforms PepFlow (0.53). On PepBDB, PepTri leads in DockQ (0.49 vs. 0.44 for UniMoMo and 0.43 for PepGLAD) and success rate (0.31 vs. 0.15 for PepGLAD), though UniMoMo achieves a slightly more favorable pre-relaxation ΔG (−18.89 vs. −18.15 REU). For structural accuracy, PepTri achieves the best Contact_F1 and GDT_TS on both datasets, while PepFlow edges it slightly on pre-relaxation Local RMSD on PepBench (1.07 vs. 1.18 Å); however, PepTri attains the best post-relaxation RMSD on both datasets. In the RFDiffusion comparison (Appendix K), PepTri is ~10× faster (5.8 s vs. 60.2 s per complex), achieves lower ΔG (−19.4 vs. −16.5 REU) and higher DockQ (0.633 vs. 0.286), while RFDiffusion retains an advantage in GDT_TS (0.812 vs. 0.749) and Local RMSD (0.482 vs. 1.179 Å) due to its backbone-only global topology focus.

## Relation to Prior Work

PepTri extends the latent diffusion paradigm for molecular design (PepGLAD, PepFlow, UniMoMo) by embedding three complementary guidance signals—differentiable molecular mechanics (OpenMM/Amber14), BLOSUM-inspired evolutionary priors, and MINE-based mutual information—directly into the denoising loop within a joint SE(3)-equivariant latent space. The encoder builds on the adaptive multichannel EGNN (Kong et al., 2023), while the mutual information objective follows Belghazi et al. (2018). The key conceptual departure from prior peptide design methods is that physics and evolutionary constraints are no longer post-hoc checks or auxiliary losses but are injected as in-loop gradients that shape the diffusion trajectory at every step, unifying sequence and structure generation in a single equivariant space rather than factorizing or decoupling the modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tri-guidance framework addresses a genuine gap in peptide co-design: prior methods produce structures that are geometrically plausible but correspond to biologically implausible sequences, or vice versa. The ablation confirming that no single guidance signal suffices and that their synergy is essential provides a clear design principle for future equivariant generative models. However, significant limitations remain: the rigid-receptor assumption excludes induced-fit effects, the model is validated only on short peptides (<30 aa) with computational proxies (Rosetta/OpenMM) rather than experimental binding data, and the all-atom SE(3)-equivariant pipeline is computationally expensive. Open questions include scalability to longer multi-chain systems, integration of explicit solvation and allosteric constraints, and whether the quality-diversity trade-off observed here persists under adaptive physics weighting or multi-objective optimization.
