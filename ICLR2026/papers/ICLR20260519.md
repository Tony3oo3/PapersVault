## Problem

Softmax attention in transformers suffers from three fundamental limitations that worsen with sequence length: attention dispersion (probability mass spreads toward uniform), representational collapse (token representations become indistinguishable), and over-squashing (gradient paths grow as O(n^L)). While α-entmax offers a sparse alternative that assigns exact zeros to irrelevant tokens, its length generalization properties have never been systematically studied, and a fixed α can become overly sparse or overly diffuse at extreme context lengths.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard causal transformer inputs—tokenized text sequences—and produces next-token predictions, but internally replaces dense softmax attention with dynamically sparse α-entmax distributions whose support size s is determined by the input logits and a data-dependent threshold. The sparsity pattern is input-dependent rather than fixed, and the adaptive scaling parameters β and γ are computed per-head and per-query from the input embeddings, making the effective temperature a joint function of sequence length and content.

## Method

The paper replaces softmax with α-entmax in transformer attention, where tokens with logits below a data-dependent threshold receive exactly zero probability, yielding sparse attention with support size s ≪ n. The theoretical analysis shows that α-entmax maintains bounded normalized entropy O(log s)/log n < 1 (versus 1 for softmax), preserves distinct token representations as n→∞, and reduces effective gradient paths from O(n^L) to O(s^L). To handle the case where fixed α becomes too sparse or too diffuse at extreme lengths, they introduce ASEntmax, which applies a learnable, head-specific, content-dependent logit scaling of the form (δ + β(log n)^γ) before the α-entmax transformation, with β and γ computed from input embeddings via learned projections. They further propose NAPE (NoPE + ALiBi), where half the attention heads use ALiBi for local recency bias and the other half use NoPE for content-driven long-range retrieval, creating a complementary local-global attention structure that pairs naturally with the sparsity of α-entmax.

## Results

On synthetic MQMTAR (trained at 64 tokens), ASEntmax achieves 95.3% accuracy at 1024× extrapolation (65K tokens), compared to 3.0% for softmax, 26.7% for SSMax, and 9.3% for fixed-α Entmax. On RULER S-NIAH-1 retrieval (trained at 2K), ASEntmax maintains 97.4% accuracy at 16K tokens versus 0.8% for softmax and 75.2% for SSMax. On long-context perplexity (ArXiv, 16K), ASEntmax achieves 10.01 PPL versus 12.71 for softmax and 12.31 for SSMax. On short-context benchmarks, all methods perform comparably, with ASEntmax leading on LAMBADA (41.6 PPL) and HellaSwag (33.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASEntmax decisively outperforms standard softmax attention on all long-context extrapolation tasks: on MQMTAR at 1024×, ASEntmax scores 95.3% versus 3.0% for softmax; on Reverse at 8×, 56.7% versus 0%; and on RULER S-NIAH-1 at 16K, 97.4% versus 0.8%. Against SSMax (Scalable-Softmax, Nakanishi 2025), ASEntmax wins on retrieval and ordering tasks (MQMTAR 1024×: 95.3% vs 26.7%; Reverse 8×: 56.7% vs 0%; RULER 16K: 97.4% vs 75.2%), while SSMax slightly edges out on the Copy task at 64× (95.8% vs 86.6%), suggesting logit scaling alone helps local copying but not global retrieval. Compared to fixed-α Entmax (α=1.5, no adaptive scaling), ASEntmax's advantage grows with length: at 256× on MQMTax, 99.0% vs 66.8%, and at 1024×, 95.3% vs 9.3%, confirming that the learnable temperature schedule is critical at extreme lengths. Against Adaptive Temperature (Veličković et al. 2025), shown in Figure 1, ASEntmax achieves substantially better extrapolation on both MQMTAR and Max Retrieval, maintaining high accuracy where adaptive-temperature softmax degrades sharply. On short-context benchmarks (Table 2), all four methods are within 1–2 points of each other, indicating that the sparse-attention modifications do not sacrifice in-distribution performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on α-entmax (Peters et al., 2019), a differentiable sparse transformation previously applied to transformer attention (Correia et al., 2019) but never analyzed for its length-generalization properties. It departs from concurrent approaches that patch softmax's limitations—Scalable-Softmax (Nakanishi, 2025) rescales logits by log n, and Veličković et al. (2025) learn adaptive temperatures—by replacing the attention transformation itself with a sparse one, then adding a learnable, head-specific scaling (ASEntmax) to control how sparsity evolves with context length. The theoretical analysis connects to Barbero et al. (2024)'s proofs of representational collapse and over-squashing under softmax, showing that sparse support provably avoids both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of why sparse attention transformations help long-context generalization, with clean proofs that softmax exhibits complete dispersion while α-entmax can maintain bounded normalized entropy. Practically, ASEntmax is a drop-in replacement that leverages existing fast kernels (AdaSplash), making it immediately deployable. Open questions include whether the benefits persist at 10B+ parameter scale (experiments are limited to 420M), how ASEntmax interacts with other efficiency techniques like KV-cache compression, and whether the synthetic-task extrapolation gains (1000×) translate proportionally to real-world long-document tasks beyond retrieval.
