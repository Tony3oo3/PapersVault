## Problem

Unified MLLMs are designed to handle both generation and understanding, yet they exhibit a persistent internal gap where understanding consistently outperforms generation. Prior mitigation strategies rely on external reward models or additional supervised data, and the co-improvement effect—where improving one branch also benefits the other—remains underexplored in post-training. This paper asks whether the internal gap itself can serve as a free, self-contained signal to drive mutual improvement without any external supervision.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a text prompt and produces N candidate images (10 per prompt at 384×384 for Janus-Pro, 512×512 for Show-o). For understanding, it takes an image paired with a templated question ('Does this image describe {prompt}?') and outputs a binary alignment score (0 or 1) with associated probability. The self-improvement pipeline consumes the scored candidates to construct SFT supervision pairs (prompt, chosen image) or DPO preference triples (prompt, chosen, rejected), and the non-unification score is computed as the fraction of generations the understanding branch rejects.

## Method

The framework uses the model's own understanding branch as a free reward signal: for each prompt, N generated images are scored by the understanding branch, and the highest-scoring image becomes the SFT target or DPO chosen sample while the lowest becomes the DPO rejected sample. Training updates only the shared LLM backbone via standard SFT or DPO losses, requiring no external models. The paper empirically discovers a co-improvement effect—generation-targeted training also improves understanding's false-positive detection—and explains it theoretically by extending learning-dynamics analysis to show that generation and understanding share an empirical neural tangent kernel (eNTK), aligning their one-step updates so that reducing mis-generation simultaneously reduces misjudgment. Motivated by this co-improvement, a curriculum replay mechanism revisits previously discarded prompts (those for which no candidate passed the understanding threshold) at a later epoch, dynamically expanding the post-training dataset by up to 50% and yielding further gains in both generation quality and unification.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench++, self-improved Janus-Pro-7B with SFT achieves up to +20% generation improvement (Texture: 38.63→53.93) and reduces the non-unification score by up to 16% (Texture: 43.33→29.67). Curriculum-based C-SFT further boosts generation to 56.38 (Texture) and understanding to 66.67, surpassing the external-reward baseline T2I-R1 (Gen 50.91, Und 52.50). For Show-o, SFT improves Texture generation from 66.80 to 73.26 and Spatial from 39.31 to 42.66, while C-SFT achieves 74.11 and 42.70 respectively. Understanding win rates exceed 50% on 5 of 6 subtasks for Janus-Pro, confirming the co-improvement effect where ~80% of understanding gains stem from false positive correction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against T2I-R1 (which uses multiple external reward models on Janus-Pro-7B), C-SFT achieves higher generation (44.18 vs. 42.22 overall) and understanding (61.13 vs. 55.67) while reducing non-unification (16.92 vs. 18.33), all without any external signals. Against Hermesflow (external BERT-based rewards on Show-o), C-SFT substantially outperforms on generation (52.82 vs. 49.65) and understanding (66.67 vs. 41.67). Compared to strong generation-only models like FLUX.1 (Texture Gen 69.22) and PixArt-α (64.77), self-improved Show-o (74.11) surpasses them on generation while simultaneously providing understanding capability. For Janus-Pro, the larger pre-existing internal gap (Non. 43.33 on Texture) translates into greater absolute gains from self-improvement, with SFT closing the gap to 29.67 and C-SFT to 28.33. On understanding benchmarks (POPE, MMBench, MMMU), self-improved Janus-Pro gains up to 3% (MMMU: 32.86→35.24), confirming that generation-targeted training also enhances understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning dynamics framework of Ren & Sutherland (2025) from LLM finetuning to the multimodal setting, formalizing how generation and understanding branches co-evolve during post-training via a shared empirical neural tangent kernel. It departs from prior non-unification mitigation work (Hermesflow, T2I-R1, UniRL) that relies on external reward models or additional supervised data, instead proposing a purely internal self-improvement loop where the model's own understanding branch scores its generation. The curriculum learning strategy builds on Bengio et al. (2009) but applies it to dynamically expanding post-training data based on the model's evolving capabilities rather than a fixed difficulty schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale, multi-model verification that non-unification in unified MLLMs is pervasive (up to 60%) and primarily stems from weak generation rather than misunderstanding, using an internal metric that avoids external-evaluator bias. The finding that generation-targeted self-improvement induces co-improvement in understanding during post-training—previously only observed in pre-training—opens a new direction for understanding the interplay between the two branches. Key limitations include evaluation on only two main models (Janus-Pro, Show-o), reliance on a shared-tokenizer assumption for the theoretical analysis, and the open question of why eNTK sharing arises architecturally in unified MLLMs.
