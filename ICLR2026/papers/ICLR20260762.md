## Problem

Softmax Attention scales quadratically in sequence length, making it infeasible for contexts beyond a few million tokens even with state-of-the-art GPU kernels like FlashAttention-2/3 (which fail past ~4M tokens on a 96 GB GH200). Existing linear approximations (Linear Attention, Performer, Linformer) either degrade accuracy, incur quadratic cost in embedding dimension, or lack formal approximation guarantees. The paper asks whether a strictly linear-time attention mechanism can match softmax-level accuracy while providing rigorous error bounds and supporting causal (autoregressive) training.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard transformer Q, K, V matrices of shape N×d (where N is sequence length and d is per-head embedding dimension) and produces an attention output Ō of shape N×d, serving as a direct drop-in replacement for Softmax Attention. Crucially, it never materializes the N×N attention matrix; instead, each query interacts only with a fixed bank of S = L·R bucket summaries (where L is the number of hash tables and R = 2^P is the number of hypercube corners per table), keeping the working set at O(L(NR + Rd)) rather than O(N²). The temperature parameter β is treated as a trainable scalar during optimization.

## Method

RACE Attention replaces the softmax exponential with a sharpened angular kernel (1 − cos⁻¹(QKᵀ/‖Q‖‖K‖)/π)^γ, which for large γ closely mimics softmax sharpness while admitting efficient kernel-density estimation via RACE sketches. The key mechanism is a differentiable soft-LSH relaxation: P random Gaussian hyperplanes project each query/key into a P-dimensional tanh space, which is then softly assigned to R = 2^P hypercube corners via a temperature-scaled softmax, turning the discrete LSH collision event into a smooth, end-to-end differentiable quantity. Per-table bucket aggregation accumulates key mass vectors A ∈ ℝ^R and weighted value matrices B ∈ ℝ^{R×d} in a single pass; each query then mixes these bucket summaries, and results are averaged across L independent tables to reduce variance. A causal variant (Algorithm 2) maintains cumulative bucket statistics in a left-to-right streaming scan, enabling autoregressive training with custom OpenMP/CUDA kernels. The paper proves a high-probability RMS error bound of O(P/β + √(log(N/δ)/L))·‖V‖_F, showing that larger β reduces bias and larger L reduces variance, with L = Θ(log N) sufficient to prevent variance explosion.

## Results

On WikiText-103 @ 1024, RACE(P=4,L=4) achieves a perplexity of 20.9, matching FlashAttention-2 exactly, while on PTB @ 128 it achieves 53.4 PPL, outperforming FlashAttention-2 (55.4). On CIFAR-10 @ 1024, RACE(P=4,L=4) reaches 65.9% top-1 accuracy, surpassing FlashAttention-2 (61.44%) and Performer-256 (64.9%). In long-context scaling, RACE processes up to 12M tokens on a GH200 GPU and 75M tokens on a Xeon CPU in a single forward-backward pass, where FlashAttention-2/3 fail beyond ~4M tokens; at 4M tokens, RACE is ~5500× faster than FlashAttention-2 on GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RACE matches or outperforms FlashAttention-2 across most tasks: it achieves identical PPL on WikiText-103 (20.9), beats it on PTB (53.4 vs 55.4), CIFAR-10 (65.9% vs 61.44%), and Tiny Stories (PPL 2.6 vs 2.7), while matching on QNLI (61.1%). Against Linear Attention and Performer, RACE shows consistent and substantial gains—e.g., CIFAR-10 accuracy of 65.9% vs 60.0% (Linear) and 64.9% (Performer), and Tiny Stories PPL of 2.6 vs 7.0 (Linear) and 10.0 (Performer). RACE also outperforms Linformer-128 on most benchmarks (e.g., Arxiv 64K: 97.92% vs 97.4%; Food-101: 43.5% vs 20.2%). In the long-context scaling regime, RACE on CPU is ~40× faster than FlashAttention-2 on GPU at 4M tokens, and YOSO fails beyond 32K tokens due to memory constraints while RACE continues to scale. The only baseline that slightly edges RACE is the exact Angular(γ=8) kernel (PPL 19.0 on WikiText-103, 2.5 on Tiny Stories), but this requires the full N×N matrix and is not scalable.

## Relation to Prior Work

RACE Attention builds directly on the RACE sketching framework (Coleman & Shrivastava, 2020) and its underlying ACE counting estimators (Luo & Shrivastava, 2018), repurposing them from streaming kernel-density estimation into a differentiable attention mechanism. The closest prior work is YOSO (Zeng et al., 2021), which uses the same powered angular kernel but with hard LSH and non-differentiable Bernoulli indicators; RACE departs by replacing hard hashing with a smooth softmax-based soft assignment over hypercube corners, enabling end-to-end training and linear (rather than quadratic-in-d) complexity. The paper also positions itself against the broader linear-attention family (Linear Attention, Performer, Linformer) and exact GPU-optimized implementations (FlashAttention-2/3), arguing that none combine linear-in-N complexity, linear-in-d complexity, theoretical approximation guarantees, and causal support in a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: processing 12M tokens on a single GH200 GPU and 75M on a CPU in one forward-backward pass opens a path toward training on contexts that were previously infeasible on commodity hardware, and the 'right algorithm beats hardware' finding (CPU RACE outperforming GPU FlashAttention beyond ~131K tokens) reframes how practitioners think about long-context deployment. Key open questions include extending the theoretical guarantees to the causal setting (currently only non-causal is covered), exploring KV-cache compression during autoregressive inference, and developing optimized CUDA kernels that preserve the linear scaling. A limitation is that the angular kernel, while matching softmax at moderate γ, may not capture all the fine-grained score-discrimination that the exponential provides at very high similarity, and the method's accuracy at extreme lengths (beyond 64K) has not been validated on downstream tasks.
