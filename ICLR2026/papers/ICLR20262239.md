## Problem

Dataset distillation methods for supervised learning and offline RL are largely heuristic, lacking rigorous performance guarantees, and existing offline RL distillation approaches rely on behavioral cloning objectives that ignore the reward and next-state information available in offline RL datasets. No provably efficient dataset distillation algorithm without model training existed for either supervised regression or offline RL before this work.

## Input / Output

For supervised regression, the input is an n-point dataset of (x, y) pairs with ||x||₂ ≤ B and |y| ≤ b; for offline RL, it is a collection of (s, a, r, s') tuples along with a feature map φ: B(d₀,B₀)² → R^d. The output is a synthetic dataset of size m (typically much smaller than n) whose points are constrained to the same norm balls as the training data, such that the loss of any bounded linear model on the synthetic set approximates its loss on the training set within a target ε. Notably, the synthetic dataset is found by direct convex optimization over its points rather than by training or fine-tuning any model.

## Method

The algorithm minimizes the sum of squared loss differences (MSE for regression, Bellman loss for offline RL) between the training and synthetic datasets with respect to a fixed set of k randomly sampled linear models, requiring no model training. For supervised regression, the loss difference is a degree-4 polynomial in Gaussian weights, enabling Carbery-Wright anti-concentration to prove that Õ(d²) sampled regressors suffice, with a matching Ω(d²) lower bound. For offline RL, the max operator in the Bellman loss prevents polynomial anti-concentration, so a conditioning argument is used instead, yielding exp(O(d log d)) predictors in the general case but recovering Õ(d²) under a natural decomposable feature-map assumption (φ(s,a) = φ₁(s) + φ₂(a)), where the optimization also becomes convex when φ₁ and φ₂ are linear.

## Results

For supervised regression on Red Wine Quality, White Wine Quality, and Boston Housing (N_syn ∈ {20, 50, 100}, k=100 sampled regressors), homogeneous linear models trained on the synthetic dataset D_syn achieve MSE test losses nearly matching the full training set, substantially outperforming random subsampling, and matching or exceeding leverage score subsampling. For offline RL on CartPole, Mountain Car, and Acrobot (k=20 sampled 2-layer neural networks, Fitted-Q Iteration), policies trained on D_syn achieve significantly higher max evaluation returns than random subsampling, and approach full-training performance as N_syn increases—particularly on CartPole and Acrobot where D_syn matches or exceeds D_train at larger synthetic sizes. The paper notes that D4RL benchmarks are not suitable for Bellman-loss-based algorithms and defers those evaluations to future work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against random subsampling (D_rand), the proposed loss-matching distillation clearly wins on both supervised regression and offline RL: MSE test losses are substantially lower for D_syn, and max evaluation returns are significantly higher in all three RL environments. Against leverage score subsampling (Drineas et al., 2006), D_syn performs better or on par across the three regression datasets, demonstrating that explicit optimization of the synthetic data via loss matching outperforms the classical data-reduction technique for homogeneous linear regressors. Against the full original training set (D_train), D_syn approaches but does not always fully match performance at the smallest synthetic sizes (N_syn=20), closing the gap as N_syn grows to 100 in regression and showing parity or slight advantage on CartPole and Acrobot in RL. The paper also reports comparisons against the behavioral-cloning distillation method of Light et al. (2024) in Appendix H, where the Bellman-loss-matching approach is shown to leverage reward and next-state information that BC-based methods discard, though specific numerical gaps are deferred to the appendix.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the model-training-free paradigm of Zhao & Bilen (2023), which matched feature distributions using a fixed set of randomly sampled networks, to a loss-matching objective for supervised regression, and further extends it to offline RL by matching Bellman loss rather than the behavioral cloning objectives used by Light et al. (2024) and Lei et al. (2024). Unlike Nguyen et al. (2021), which proved convergence of synthetic features only given fixed labels, or Chen et al. (2024), which required access to the optimal trained model, this work provides the first DD guarantees without any model training. The theoretical machinery combines Carbery-Wright anti-concentration for Gaussian polynomials, net-based union bounds, and Chernoff concentration, applied in a novel combination to the loss-matching objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first provably efficient dataset distillation algorithm with matching upper and lower bounds (Ω(d²) sampled regressors) for supervised regression without model training, and the first Bellman-loss-based offline RL distillation method with theoretical guarantees. However, the general offline RL case requires exp(O(d log d)) sampled predictors due to the max term in Bellman loss, and guarantees are limited to linear models; the authors acknowledge the method fails on D4RL benchmarks and defer extension to neural networks. Open questions include whether anti-concentration arguments can be extended to non-linear predictors, whether the decomposable feature-map assumption (which recovers polynomial complexity) holds broadly in practice, and how to adapt the approach to more sophisticated offline RL losses like CQL.
