## Problem

State-of-the-art LLM unlearning methods suffer from three interrelated failures: catastrophic forgetting that destroys model utility, metric imbalance where one objective (e.g., unlearning effectiveness) is over-optimized at the expense of others (e.g., privacy protection or utility), and vulnerability to relearning and jailbreak attacks that can reverse the unlearning. No existing framework simultaneously addresses robustness to these attacks while maintaining a balanced trade-off across unlearning metrics.

## Input / Output

PRISM takes a pretrained LLM (e.g., Llama-2-7B, Ministral-8B), a forget set D_f specifying knowledge to remove, a retain set D_r for utility preservation, and layer-L hidden states extracted via mean pooling over token vectors. It produces two artifacts: (1) a robust adversarial probe p_φ* (an MLP with architecture d→64→32→2, ReLU activations) trained on FGSM-perturbed hidden states to discriminate harmless from harmful representations, and (2) unlearned model parameters θ_u obtained through iterative min-max updates. The probe is frozen during unlearning and serves as a fixed robust surrogate loss, while the model parameters are updated via a two-stage smoothness optimization.

## Method

PRISM casts unlearning as a min-max game enforcing dual-space smoothness. In the representation-space stage, a probe is adversarially trained using FGSM perturbations (δ* = ε·sign(g)) on layer-L hidden states to widen its decision boundary; during unlearning, the frozen probe's negative log-likelihood on the harmless class (L_probe) pushes forget representations deep into the safe region, enlarging the jailbreak margin. In the parameter-space stage, a SAM-style inner maximization over an ℓ₂ ball of radius ρ is applied to the composite forget loss (NPO + probe loss), yielding a first-order approximation ℓ_f(θ) + ρ‖g(θ)‖₂ that penalizes gradient magnitude and flattens the loss surface to enlarge the relearn margin. To prevent catastrophic forgetting, the forget gradient is orthogonalized against the retain gradient via projection (g_f^⊥ = (I − g_r g_rᵀ/‖g_r‖²) g_f), ensuring first-order safety for the retain objective. The final update combines the projected forget gradient and the retain gradient with learned mixing weights.

## Results

PRISM achieves the highest Unlearn Score (geometric mean of unlearning effectiveness, privacy protection, and utility) across all three benchmarks: 0.860 on MUSE-Books, 0.522 on MUSE-News, and 0.521/0.761 on WMDP (Llama-2-7B / Ministral-8B), compared to the strongest baseline SAM+NPO at 0.748, 0.000, and 0.443/0.721 respectively. Under relearning attacks (100 steps), PRISM maintains the lowest VerbMem (6.804) and highest Utility (63.181) on MUSE-Books, while SAM+NPO and NPO degrade to VerbMem 15.552 and 20.595. Against jailbreak attacks on WMDP_bio, PRISM achieves the lowest Attack Success Rate across all three attack types (Multi-turn: 0.196, Prefilling: 0.293/0.279, AutoDAN: 0.000) while retaining a 1.000 X-Stest refusal rate. Per-step runtime is comparable to SAM+NPO (11.22s vs 11.06s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM outperforms all nine baselines on the composite Unlearn Score across every dataset. The most dramatic gap is on MUSE-News, where SAM+NPO and vanilla NPO both score 0.000 (due to catastrophic utility collapse and privacy leakage) while PRISM reaches 0.522. On MUSE-Books, PRISM (0.860) leads SAM+NPO (0.748) and NPO (0.717) by 11–20 percentage points, with the advantage driven by superior privacy protection and utility preservation. Against relearning attacks, PRISM's VerbMem at 100 steps (6.804) is roughly 2.3× lower than SAM+NPO (15.552) and 3× lower than NPO (20.595), while maintaining the highest utility (63.181 vs 60.738 and 61.300). On jailbreak robustness (WMDP_bio), PRISM achieves the lowest ASR on Multi-turn (0.196 vs 0.241 for SAM+NPO, 0.326 for NPO_GDR) and Prefilling (0.293/0.279 vs 0.325/0.307 for SAM+NPO, 0.400/0.404 for NPO_GDR), and ties NPO and SAM+NPO at 0.000 AutoDAN ASR. DOOR achieves a slightly lower Multi-turn ASR (0.188) but at the cost of much higher AutoDAN ASR (0.236) and lower Unlearn Score (0.479 vs 0.521). GA and Task Vector are effectively non-competitive, scoring 0.000 Unlearn Score on all datasets due to catastrophic forgetting or negligible unlearning effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM unifies three previously separate threads in LLM unlearning: (1) SAM-style parameter-space smoothness (Foret et al., 2020; Fan et al., 2025) to resist relearning attacks, (2) FGSM-based adversarial training (Goodfellow et al., 2014) applied to a probe in representation space to enlarge jailbreak margins, and (3) gradient-conflict decoupling via orthogonal projection (Lin et al., 2024a) to prevent catastrophic forgetting. The base forget loss is NPO (Zhang et al., 2024a), and the overall min-max formulation follows the adversarial-training paradigm. The key departure from prior work is the simultaneous enforcement of smoothness in both representation and parameter spaces within a single framework, rather than addressing robustness in only one space or treating metric balance as a separate regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, unified lens (dual-space smoothness) for understanding why existing unlearning methods fail under attacks and how to fix the resulting metric imbalance, which is valuable for practitioners choosing unlearning strategies. However, the approach is tested only on 7B–8B models across two dataset families, and the per-step overhead (~11s vs. ~6s for NPO) plus the need to train and maintain a probe limit immediate scalability. Open questions include whether the representation-space probe generalizes to multimodal or much larger models, whether the smoothness radii (ρ, ε) transfer across architectures, and whether the min-max formulation can be made more computationally efficient for production deployment.
