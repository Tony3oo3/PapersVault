## Problem

Image-based bokeh rendering methods, when naively extended frame-by-frame to video, suffer from temporal flickering and unstable blur boundaries due to stochastic noise accumulation and imperfect depth estimates. Meanwhile, existing video generative models produce bokeh-like effects only implicitly, lacking explicit user control over the focal plane and bokeh intensity. No dedicated diffusion framework existed for temporally coherent, controllable video bokeh generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary video clip (1024×576 resolution), a pre-computed disparity map from a depth estimator, and two user-specified parameters: the focal plane depth d_f and blur strength K. It produces a temporally coherent bokeh-rendered video in a single forward pass. For videos longer than 8 frames, a weighted overlap inference strategy processes overlapping 8-frame segments with 4-frame cosine-blended boundaries to avoid temporal discontinuities.

## Method

The core architecture is a one-step U-Net built on Stable Video Diffusion, conditioned on three explicit signals: a focal-plane-adapted MPI mask (disparity sampled more finely near the focal plane via a power-law threshold function), a normalized disparity-difference map, and a Fourier embedding of blur strength K. MPI spatial blocks inject a gated attention mechanism that uses the MPI mask to steer attention toward focus-relevant regions at different U-Net depths. Training follows a three-stage progressive strategy: (1) geometry-guided fine-tuning on clean 4-frame sequences, (2) temporal refinement on 8-frame sequences with injected disparity perturbations (elastic transforms, Perlin noise, morphological operations) to build robustness, and (3) VAE decoder fine-tuning with a gradient-based texture loss to recover high-frequency details lost in latent compression.

## Results

On the synthetic test set of 200 videos, Any-to-Bokeh achieves the best scores across all nine metrics: FVD 9.005 (vs. 32.710 for the next-best Dr.Bokeh), VFID-I 1.479 (vs. 5.444 for MPIB), PSNR 38.899 dB (vs. 31.390 for MPIB), and LPIPS 0.019. On the real-world DAVIS benchmark, it attains the highest VEPI of 0.944, indicating superior edge preservation at the focused subject boundary. Inference time is 0.363 s per frame, faster than MPIB (0.521 s), BokehDiff (0.799 s), and Dr.Bokeh (2.729 s), though slightly slower than BokehMe (0.103 s) and DeepLens (0.226 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Any-to-Bokeh outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor MPIB (Peng et al., ECCV 2022), it reduces FVD from 35.766 to 9.005 (≈4× improvement) and raises PSNR from 31.39 to 38.90 dB, while also achieving a lower inference time (0.363 s vs. 0.521 s). Compared to Dr.Bokeh (Sheng et al., CVPR 2024), the largest gap is in FVD (9.005 vs. 32.710) and PSNR (38.899 vs. 31.273), and Any-to-Bokeh is 7.5× faster (0.363 s vs. 2.729 s). Versus BokehDiff (Zhu et al., ICCV 2025), the diffusion-based image method, Any-to-Bokeh cuts FVD by 7.3× (9.005 vs. 65.678) and LPIPS by 6.7× (0.019 vs. 0.127), while also being 2.2× faster. Against BokehMe (Peng et al., CVPR 2022), the advantage is most pronounced in video quality metrics (FVD 9.005 vs. 39.102; VFID-I 1.479 vs. 8.633), though BokehMe remains faster at 0.103 s per frame. DeepLens (Wang et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2018) is the weakest baseline, trailing Any-to-Bokeh by over 2× in FVD (125.338 vs. 9.005) and 14 dB in PSNR (24.574 vs. 38.899). In the user study on DAVIS, Any-to-Bokeh is preferred over each baseline in 62.9–96.9% of pairwise comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) into a one-step video bokeh generator by replacing iterative denoising with a single U-Net forward pass conditioned on a focal-plane-adapted Multiplane Image (MPI) representation. The MPI concept originates from stereo magnification (Zhou et al., 2018) and was previously used in image bokeh pipelines (MPIB, Dr.Bokeh) with fixed front-to-back depth discretization; here it is re-parameterized relative to the focal plane so that disparity sampling density follows the nonlinear circle-of-confusion relationship. The work also departs from image-based diffusion bokeh methods (BokehDiff, BokehMe) by introducing explicit temporal modeling via a progressive three-stage training strategy and a weighted overlap inference scheme for long videos, addressing the temporal flickering that plagues frame-by-frame extensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first dedicated diffusion framework for video bokeh, this work establishes a baseline for temporally coherent, controllable depth-of-field effects on arbitrary video inputs, with direct applications to mobile post-processing and cinematic content creation. Key open questions include whether the one-step formulation generalizes to more complex multi-subject scenes or longer temporal contexts without the WOIS efficiency penalty, and whether the focal-plane-adapted MPI sampling can be extended to handle non-planar or dynamic depth structures. The authors acknowledge that limited batch-size training and segment-based inference for long videos constrain scalability, leaving room for future work on end-to-end long-sequence processing.
