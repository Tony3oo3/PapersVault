## Problem

Agentic search systems that iteratively retrieve and reason over diverse sources require training data with genuine multi-layered complexity, yet existing resources either provide only shallow single- or multi-hop supervision (NQ, HotpotQA) or remain confined to flat multi-hop QA without public frameworks. No publicly available pipeline exists for synthesizing hierarchically structured, verifiable deep-research problems at scale, leaving a critical data gap for training models on realistic research tasks.

## Input / Output

The synthesis framework takes a seed entity or webpage from filtered Wikipedia and web corpora and produces a research tree whose structure encodes parallel and sequential constraints; the retrospection stage then converts this tree into a natural-language HCSP question with a concise (typically 5–6 token) verifiable entity answer. For model training, the pipeline consumes 50K+ QA pairs and 16.5K filtered reasoning trajectories (each containing interleaved think/search/information/answer turns) to produce InfoSeeker, a 3B-parameter model that accepts a free-form text question and outputs a final entity answer after up to 10 rounds of parallel querying and evidence refinement.

## Method

InfoSeek formalizes deep-research questions as Hierarchical Constraint Satisfaction Problems (HCSPs), where the answer is the intersection of satisfying sets across a tree of interdependent sub-constraints, generalizing both flat CSPs and sequential multi-hop chains. The Diffusion-Retrospection synthesis process first grows a research tree outward from a seed via two operations—Blurring Parent Node (attaching k incomparable parallel claims to enforce uniqueness) and Expanding Depth (appending a new entity along a relational edge to lengthen the reasoning chain)—then traverses the tree in reverse to compose leaf constraints and internal sub-questions into a single natural-language HCSP. A two-pronged quality filter discards samples that a 32B parametric model answers without retrieval (difficulty) or that a retrieval-augmented LLM cannot resolve from ground-truth pages mixed with distractors (verifiability). For optimization, the authors distill 24K correct trajectories from Qwen2.5-72B via rejection sampling for SFT, then apply GRPO reinforcement learning with a binary format-and-answer reward on an 18K subset, yielding consistent gains over strong agentic-search baselines on both classic QA and BrowseComp-Plus benchmarks.

## Results

On classic knowledge-intensive QA benchmarks (Table 3), InfoSeeker-3B achieves an average Exact Match of 42.7 across seven single-hop and multi-hop datasets, outperforming the strongest agentic baseline InForage-3B (40.6) and AutoRefine-3B (39.6). On the more challenging BrowseComp-Plus benchmark (Table 4), InfoSeeker-3B reaches 15.3% accuracy with 8.24 search calls, surpassing GPT-4.1 (14.6%), Sonnet 4 (14.3%), and all open-source baselines (Qwen3-32B at 3.5%, SearchR1-32B at 3.9%, WebSailor-3B at 4.3%), while remaining competitive with Gemini 2.5 Flash (15.5%) despite having only 3B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoSeeker-3B outperforms all nine baselines on the average across the seven classic QA benchmarks. Against RAG-based methods, the gains are substantial: InfoSeeker-3B (42.7 avg) exceeds RQRAG (29.5), RAG (27.0), Self-RAG (19.2), and IRCoT (15.8) by 13–27 points, confirming that iterative agentic search with structured training data far surpasses single-shot retrieval. Among agentic search baselines, InfoSeeker-3B leads InForage-3B (40.6) by 2.1 points and AutoRefine-3B (39.6) by 3.1 points, with particularly large margins on multi-hop tasks (e.g., 2Wiki: 50.0 vs. 42.8; MSQ: 20.5 vs. 17.2). On BrowseComp-Plus, InfoSeeker-3B (15.3%) beats GPT-4.1 (14.6%), Sonnet 4 (14.3%), and all open-source 3B/32B models (3.5–4.3%), though it trails Gemini 2.5 Flash (15.5%) by a narrow 0.2 points and falls well short of GPT-5 (55.9%) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (19.0%). Notably, InfoSeeker-3B achieves these results using only SFT plus lightweight GRPO, without the more elaborate reward shaping or multi-stage pipelines employed by several baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the emerging paradigm of agentic search (iterative retrieval with multi-step reasoning) and the growing body of synthetic data approaches for complex QA (WebSailor, WebShaper, WebDancer, WebWalker). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it introduces a principled formalization—Hierarchical Constraint Satisfaction Problems (HCSPs)—that unifies and generalizes both flat CSPs and sequential multi-hop chains into a single tree-structured framework, rather than treating multi-hop QA as the ceiling of complexity; and (2) it provides the first fully open-source synthesis pipeline (Diffusion-Retrospection) with explicit control over structural complexity, whereas comparable efforts (WebSailor, WebShaper) released only trained models or small example sets without public construction code. The training recipe (SFT via rejection sampling followed by GRPO) follows the Search-R1 / InForage lineage but is applied to the structurally richer HCSP data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing a 50K+ QA / 16.5K trajectory dataset with a reproducible, low-cost ($571.8) synthesis pipeline, InfoSeek removes the primary bottleneck—lack of high-quality, structurally complex training data—that has limited progress in agentic search. The finding that a 3B model trained on this data approaches or exceeds several closed-source systems on BrowseComp-Plus suggests that data structure can partially substitute for model scale in search-intensive tasks, an important signal for resource-constrained practitioners. Key open questions include whether the HCSP formalization transfers cleanly to non-web domains, whether the preserved intermediate-step metainformation can support richer process-level RL rewards beyond the current binary outcome reward, and how to address the dominant retrieval-error failure mode (46.2%) that the current text-based BM25/BGE-M3 setup cannot resolve.
