## Problem

Existing math pretraining datasets built from Common Crawl (OpenWebMath, FineMath, MegaMath) rely on brittle heuristic-based HTML-to-text extraction that strips or corrupts mathematical equations, loses code indentation, and fails to handle the diverse rendering formats (MathJax, KaTeX, MathML, inline LaTeX) found across the web. This severely limits the fidelity and scale of available open math corpora, with the highest-quality prior dataset (FineMath-4+) containing only 9.6B tokens.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw HTML from 98 Common Crawl WARC snapshots (2014–2024) for URLs harvested from four community-filtered datasets (OWM, InfiMM-WebMath, FineMath, MegaMath), yielding 229.54M webpages. It produces two quality-tiered corpora: Nemotron-CC-Math-4+ (52.32B tokens, 45M documents, FineMath scores 4–5) and Nemotron-CC-Math-3+ (133.26B tokens, 101M documents, scores 3–5). Each document is plain text with all mathematical expressions unified into LaTeX, code blocks preserved with original indentation, and boilerplate removed.

## Method

The pipeline's key innovation is a two-stage extraction approach: first, the Lynx text-based browser renders each HTML page into plain text by executing layout rules, which reliably preserves equations, symbols, and code indentation that DOM-based parsers (Resiliparse, Trafilatura, Justext) typically destroy. Second, Phi-4 (14B) performs an LLM-based cleanup pass that removes navigation boilerplate, standardizes heterogeneous math representations (MathML, KaTeX, Unicode, custom delimiters) into consistent LaTeX, and corrects typographical errors. Downstream, the FineMath 5-point quality classifier scores each page, MinHash LSH deduplication (24-character n-grams, Jaccard threshold 0.8 via NeMo-Curator) removes near-duplicates, and Qwen2.5-32B embedding-based decontamination eliminates documents with cosine similarity >0.9 to benchmark prompts. The entire pipeline is optimized with Polars and Ray for terabyte-scale processing, and ablations confirm that the 14B Phi-4 model matches or exceeds 671B DeepSeek-V3 on the cleaning task, making the approach computationally efficient.

## Results

At 100B tokens, Nemotron-CC-Math-4+ achieves 40.60 on MATH (+4.8 over FineMath-4+, +6.6 over MegaMath-Pro) and 76.27 on GSM8K, leading all math benchmarks. It also tops code (34.82 HumanEval+, 45.11 MBPP+) and knowledge (38.49 MMLU-Pro, 62.67 MMLU-STEM) tasks. At 300B tokens, Nemotron-CC-Math-3+ extends these gains to 44.20 on MATH (+9.6 over FineMath-3+, +12.6 over MegaMath-Web) and 43.51 on MBPP+ (+4.6 over MegaMath-Web, +14.3 over FineMath-3+), with 64.26 on MMLU-STEM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenWebMath (OWM), Nemotron-CC-Math dominates across nearly all benchmarks: at 100B tokens it gains +11.4 on MATH, +4.9 on GSM8K, +3.0 on MMLU-STEM, and +2.3 on HumanEval+; at 300B tokens the MATH gap widens to +10.0 and MBPP+ improves by +5.9. Against MegaMath, Nemotron-CC-Math-4+ leads on MATH (+6.6), GSM8K (+2.8), MMLU-STEM (+1.8), and HumanEval+ (+3.8) at 100B tokens, though MegaMath-Pro edges out on MBPP+ (46.03 vs. 45.11); at 300B tokens Nemotron-CC-Math-3+ sweeps all math and code benchmarks, gaining +12.6 on MATH and +4.6 on MBPP+ over MegaMath-Web. Against FineMath, the most direct competitor, Nemotron-CC-Math-4+ achieves +4.8 on MATH and +0.3 on GSM8K at 100B tokens, while at 300B tokens Nemotron-CC-Math-3+ gains +9.6 on MATH, +0.6 on GSM8K, +1.97 on MMLU-STEM, +2.98 on HumanEval+, and a striking +14.3 on MBPP+ over FineMath-3+; the sole exception is MMLU-Pro at 300B tokens, where FineMath-3+ narrowly leads (39.57 vs. 39.32). Overall, Nemotron-CC-Math consistently outperforms all three baselines on math reasoning and code generation, with the largest margins appearing at the 300B-token scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant approach in math pretraining dataset construction (OWM, FineMath, MegaMath), which relies on DOM-based HTML parsers like Resiliparse or Trafilatura that the authors demonstrate systematically strip or corrupt mathematical expressions and code blocks. Instead, it repurposes Lynx—a text-based browser originally designed for accessibility—as a layout-aware rendering step, followed by an LLM (Phi-4) for LaTeX standardization and boilerplate removal. The URL sourcing strategy also differs: rather than training a single classifier over all of Common Crawl, the authors harvest URLs from multiple community-filtered datasets (OWM, FineMath, MegaMath, InfiMM-WebMath) to inherit diverse filtering signals, then re-extract the original HTML from 98 Common Crawl snapshots for higher-fidelity processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

At 133B tokens (52B for the 4+ subset), Nemotron-CC-Math is 5.5× larger than the previous highest-quality open math corpus (FineMath-4+) and demonstrates that extraction fidelity—not just scale—drives downstream reasoning gains, with +12.6 MATH and +14.3 MBPP+ improvements over prior datasets. The domain-agnostic pipeline (Lynx rendering + LLM cleaning + MinHash deduplication + embedding-based decontamination) is open-sourced and directly applicable to other technical domains (physics, CS, chemistry), addressing a broad gap in web-scale scientific data extraction. Open questions include whether the LLM cleaning step introduces subtle semantic drift at scale, whether the approach generalizes to dynamically rendered or JavaScript-heavy pages that Lynx cannot execute, and whether the incidental code content (4.3M snippets) could be further leveraged or whether it introduces distributional noise for pure-math pretraining.
