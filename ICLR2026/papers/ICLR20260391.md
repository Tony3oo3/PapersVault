## Problem

Deep neural networks trained on class-balanced data still exhibit severe class-wise accuracy disparities, with 'hard' classes performing far below 'easy' ones. Prior empirical studies identified this phenomenon but lacked a theoretical explanation for why feature variability differs across classes or a principled mechanism to correct it. This paper provides the first margin-based, class-sensitive generalization bound that links per-class feature spread to error, and derives a theoretically grounded regularization to close the gap.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in loss modification on top of any standard image classifier (CNN or ViT) trained on RGB images, producing class labels as usual. It supports both training from scratch and fine-tuning/linear probing of pre-trained backbones (MAE, MoCo v2, CLIP), with no architectural changes required. During training, per-class feature statistics (mean norm and mean squared deviation) are maintained via exponential moving averages to dynamically compute class-specific margins at each step.

## Method

MR² introduces two complementary regularizers: (1) a logit margin loss that scales the cross-entropy temperature per class, assigning larger margins to classes with greater feature spread (‖μ̂_k‖² + ‖ŝ_k‖²)^{1/3}, derived as the optimal solution to a Lagrangian problem that minimizes a class-sensitive Rademacher complexity bound under a fixed average margin budget; and (2) a representation margin loss that penalizes intra-class pairwise distances exceeding 2s̄ (the average mean squared deviation) via a log-sum-exp formulation, promoting intra-class compactness. The theoretical foundation is a novel margin-based generalization bound (Proposition 1) showing that per-class feature variability directly inflates the complexity term, so larger margins for high-variance classes and reduced within-class spread together balance the per-class error bounds. For cosine classifiers (e.g., CLIP), the bound extends to general L_p norms (Proposition 2), with p=3 used in practice to restore class-dependent margins when L_2 features are normalized.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-32), MR² achieves 73.9% overall top-1 accuracy, with 85.9% on easy, 73.8% on medium, and 61.9% on hard classes — a +5.2 pp gain on hard classes over the ERM baseline (56.7%). On ImageNet (CLIP ResNet-50), MR² reaches 76.9% overall, 91.5% easy, 79.7% medium, and 59.6% hard, improving hard-class accuracy by +3.2 pp over ERM (56.4%). Across all seven datasets and diverse backbones (MAE, MoCo v2, CLIP, ResNet, ViT), MR² consistently boosts hard-class performance while also mildly improving easy-class accuracy, avoiding the trade-offs seen in reweighting and DRO-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MR² outperforms all 14 baselines on both overall and hard-class accuracy across CIFAR-100 and ImageNet. Error-based reweighting methods (LfF, JTT) actually degrade hard-class performance below ERM (e.g., LfF drops hard accuracy from 56.7% to 53.7% on CIFAR-100), confirming that reweighting introduces harmful trade-offs. Long-tail margin methods (EQL, LDAM) designed for imbalanced priors yield only marginal gains (+0.3 pp on hard classes) under balanced settings, as expected since their margins collapse to constants when class priors are uniform. Distributionally robust optimization methods (DRO, FairDRO) improve overall accuracy but exhibit the characteristic trade-off: on ImageNet, DRO gains +0.8 pp on hard classes while sacrificing −0.1 pp on easy classes. Contrastive representation methods (SupCon, DRL) show moderate improvements but their hard-class gains (+0.3 to +1.0 pp) are smaller than their medium/easy gains, failing to close the disparity gap. MR² uniquely achieves simultaneous improvements across all three subsets (easy, medium, hard) without any trade-off, with the largest relative gains concentrated on hard classes (+5.2 pp on CIFAR-100, +3.2 pp on ImageNet).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the margin-based generalization theory from Mohri et al. (2018) and the γ-margin loss framework of Tian & Man-Cho So (2022) to a class-sensitive, per-class setting, deriving a novel bound that links per-class feature variability to optimal margin design. It departs from long-tail margin methods (LDAM, EQL, Logit Adjustment) that encode class-prior imbalance into margins and reduce to standard cross-entropy under balanced priors, whereas MR² imposes class-sensitive margins driven by feature spread even when priors are uniform. It also contrasts with contrastive representation learning (SupCon, MoCo) by introducing an adaptive, data-dependent margin (2s̄) rather than fixed or validation-tuned margins, and with Neural Collapse theory by explicitly modeling non-vanishing, class-dependent within-class variance that persists on large-scale datasets like ImageNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a non-asymptotic, class-sensitive generalization bound that explains *why* hard classes underperform on balanced data (greater feature variability inflates the complexity term) and *how* to fix it (adaptive logit margins proportional to feature spread plus representation compactness regularization). The plug-and-play loss modification works across CNNs, ViTs, and pre-trained foundation models (MAE, MoCo v2, CLIP) without trade-offs between easy and hard classes, unlike DRO or reweighting methods. Open questions include whether the EMA-based margin estimation is optimal for very large class counts, whether the approach transfers to multi-label or open-set settings, and whether the L_p norm generalization (Proposition 2) yields further gains beyond the modest p=3 choice used for cosine classifiers.
