## Problem

Prevailing sound separation methods are optimized for signal-level metrics (SDR, SIR, SAR) and give limited consideration to semantic alignment, creating a 'metric dilemma' where models can score well on waveform reconstruction while leaving perceptually salient interference from acoustically similar sources. This paper asks how to make separated output not only signal-clean but also semantically faithful to the user's query intent, drawing an analogy to RLHF alignment in LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262817/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a ~4-second audio mixture at 16 kHz (converted to a 1024-point STFT magnitude spectrogram) together with a query in any modality—text, audio exemplar, image, or a composed omni-modal mixup—and produces a separated audio waveform via inverse STFT. Unlike standard deterministic mask prediction, the output mask is stochastically sampled from a factorized Beta distribution over time-frequency bins, introducing controlled exploration during training. The separator is a 7-layer U-Net with 32 intermediate masks, and all queries are projected into a shared ImageBind embedding space for reward computation.

## Method

MARS-Sep reformulates query-conditioned mask prediction as a trust-region reinforcement learning problem: the OmniSep U-Net serves as a base policy whose deterministic mask proposals are converted into a factorized Beta distribution (concentration κ=9) for stochastic sampling, and a PPO-style clipped surrogate with GRPO-normalized advantages, entropy regularization, and KL penalty drives policy updates. The reward is a scalar cosine similarity between the separated audio's ImageBind embedding and a fused multimodal query anchor constructed via Multi-Modal Low-Rank Bilinear Pooling (MLBP) over audio, text, and video embeddings. To stabilize this reward signal and mitigate reward hacking, the paper introduces a three-stage progressive contrastive fine-tuning curriculum on ImageBind—audio-text alignment, audio-audio discrimination, then audio-video grounding—where encoders remain frozen and only projection heads and temperature parameters are updated at each stage. The old policy is snapshotted each step (single-epoch PPO), and the concentration scale κ is annealed to avoid degenerate near-binary masks early in training.

## Results

On VGGSound-clean+, MARS-Sep achieves the highest SDR (6.91 dB text, 7.33 dB audio, 6.93 dB image, 7.93 dB omni-modal) and best CLAP scores (9.03–9.22%) across all four query modalities, with SI-SDRi of 4.36–5.20 dB. On the cross-domain MUSIC-clean+ benchmark, gains are more pronounced: MARS-Sep reaches 12.91 dB SDR and 6.18% CLAP for text queries, 13.64 dB SDR and 6.94% CLAP for image queries, and 13.89 dB SDR and 6.82% CLAP for composed omni-modal queries, outperforming all baselines by 0.5–1.5 dB in SDR and 0.3–0.7% in CLAP. The method also demonstrates substantially lower variance in CLAP scores (±0.9–1.1%) compared to generative baselines like ZeroSep (±15.1%) and FlowSep (±14.2%), indicating more stable semantic alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OmniSep (the base architecture), MARS-Sep consistently improves SDR by 0.16–0.60 dB and SI-SDRi by 0.01–0.04 dB across all four query modalities, while achieving the highest CLAP scores in every setting; OmniSep retains a marginal edge on SIR for audio queries (11.65 vs. 11.63 dB) and SAR for image (13.73 vs. 13.41 dB) and composed (14.53 vs. 14.49 dB) queries. Compared to the strongest non-generative baselines, MARS-Sep outperforms AudioSep by 0.65 dB SDR and 0.82% CLAP on text queries, and surpasses DAVIS-Flow by 0.31–0.41 dB SDR and 0.2–2.6% CLAP across text and image queries. On the MUSIC-clean+ benchmark, MARS-Sep leads OmniSep by 0.54 dB SDR and 0.77% CLAP (text), 1.36 dB SDR and 0.25% CLAP (audio), and 0.61 dB SDR and 0.41% CLAP (image), confirming cross-domain generalizability. Against generative baselines ZeroSep and FlowSep (compared only on CLAP in Table 3), MARS-Sep achieves far more stable scores (±0.9–1.1% std) versus their highly variable outputs (±13–15% std), though their mean CLAP_t values appear inflated due to synthesis-based decoding. Overall, MARS-Sep wins or ties on the majority of signal-level and semantic metrics, with the only losses being narrow SAR margins to OmniSep in image and composed settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OmniSep's unified omni-modality separation architecture (frozen ImageBind encoder + U-Net mask predictor with query-mixup) and repurposes it as a base policy for reinforcement learning. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the RLHF paradigm from LLM alignment: instead of supervised regression toward ideal masks with signal-level losses (WBCE, SDR), MARS-Sep parameterizes mask generation as a factorized Beta policy and optimizes it via a PPO-style clipped surrogate driven by a multimodal semantic reward from a progressively fine-tuned ImageBind encoder. This positions the work at the intersection of query-conditioned sound separation (LASS-Net, CLIPSep, AudioSep, OmniSep) and preference-optimized policy learning (PPO, GRPO), with the progressive contrastive alignment of the reward encoder drawing from multimodal contrastive learning (InfoNCE, triplet losses).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap—the 'metric dilemma' where high SDR/SIR coexists with perceptually salient semantic contamination—by directly optimizing for cross-modal semantic consistency rather than waveform fidelity alone. However, the empirical gains are modest (typically 0.2–0.5 dB on SDR, sub-1% CLAP improvements) and come at roughly 2× training cost, limiting immediate practical adoption. Open questions include whether the RL formulation scales to more than two sources or longer mixtures, whether the reward model's susceptibility to hacking is fully mitigated by progressive alignment, and whether the approach transfers to other audio tasks (speech separation, music source separation) where the Beta-mask policy and multimodal reward structure could apply more broadly.
