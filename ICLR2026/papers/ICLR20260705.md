## Problem

Existing language-agnostic embedding spaces such as SONAR cover only text and speech, leaving vision-language tasks outside their reach. It remains unexplored whether a state-of-the-art vision encoder can be post-hoc aligned to a purely text-trained embedding space, and whether a latent diffusion language model (LCM) trained exclusively on English text can zero-shot process visual embeddings encoded into that space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260705/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

V-SONAR accepts RGB images at 448×448 or videos (8 uniformly sampled frames) and produces a single 1024-dimensional embedding in the SONAR space; text queries are encoded by the frozen SONAR text encoder into the same space for retrieval. For generation, the SONAR decoder or LCM/v-LCM consumes sequences of these 1024-dim embeddings and autoregressively predicts the next embedding via a diffusion denoiser, which is then decoded into text captions or answers in any of up to 1500 supported languages.

## Method

V-SONAR aligns the Perception Encoder (1.9B ViT) to the frozen SONAR text encoder via MSE loss on paired visual-caption data, using a three-stage coarse-to-fine curriculum: 12M image-caption pairs for coarse grounding, 2M synthetic video-caption pairs for temporal adaptation, and 200K human-annotated video-caption pairs for fine-grained alignment. A lightweight projector (positional embeddings → temporal multi-head attention → attention-based pooling → linear MLP) bridges the 1536-dim PE frame features to the 1024-dim SONAR space, with asynchronous learning rates to preserve pre-trained encoder knowledge. V-LCM extends the two-tower LCM by concatenating V-SONAR visual embeddings and SONAR text embeddings into a single sequence, then training with the identical variance-preserving latent diffusion objective for next-embedding prediction, followed by vision-language instruction fine-tuning on M3IT. The key insight is that operating entirely in the modality-agnostic SONAR latent space allows a text-only pretrained LCM to zero-shot understand visual concepts, and that instruction tuning in this shared space inherits SONAR's multilingual decoding capability across 1500 languages without any language-specific vision data.

## Results

V-SONAR achieves zero-shot text-to-video retrieval R@1 of 73.03 on PE-VIDEO, surpassing SigLIP2-G-OPT (47.55) and the base Perception Encoder (63.91). Paired with the OMNISONAR decoder, V-SONAR attains BLEU 39.0 on PE-VIDEO and 23.9 on DREAM-1K, outperforming the best competing VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B at 30.0 and PLM-3B at 19.6) by +9 and +4.3 BLEU respectively. After vision-language instruction tuning, v-LCM matches or exceeds state-of-the-art VLMs on video QA (IVQA R-L 63.9, ActivityNetQA R-L 63.6) and outperforms all baselines in 61 of 62 languages on the M3IT multilingual benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On zero-shot video retrieval, V-SONAR substantially outperforms SigLIP2-G-OPT (R@1 73.03 vs. 47.55 on PE-VIDEO) and the base Perception Encoder PECoreG (73.03 vs. 63.91), though it trails PECoreG on DREAM-1K (63.30 vs. 72.10). For video captioning, V-SONAR with the OMNISONAR decoder beats all 1–3B VLMs on PE-VIDEO (BLEU 39.0 vs. Qwen2.5-VL-3B's 30.0) and DREAM-1K (23.9 vs. PLM-3B's 19.6), but underperforms InternVL2-1B on VATEX (26.7 vs. 47.8) where captions are single-sentence. The zero-shot LCM (trained only on English text) already achieves competitive captioning (R-L 25.5 on PE-Video, 23.8 on VATEX), lagging the best VLMs by only 1–5 BLEU points without any video training data. After instruction tuning, v-LCM surpasses all baselines on video QA tasks (IVQA R-L 63.9 vs. Qwen2.5-VL-7B's 54.2; ActivityNetQA R-L 63.6 vs. PLM-8B's 40.5) and on COCO captioning (R-L 38.8 vs. PLM-8B's 36.3), while remaining competitive on image QA (VIQUAE, VisualMRC, ScienceQA). In the multilingual M3IT evaluation across 62 languages, v-LCM outperforms both Qwen2.5-VL-7B and PLM-8B in 61 of 62 languages, with the sole exception being Dutch, and gains are most pronounced in mid- and low-resource languages such as Burmese, Tajik, and Telugu.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SONAR/OMNISONAR line of language-agnostic embedding spaces (originally covering text and speech) to the visual modality via a post-hoc teacher-student alignment of the Perception Encoder into the frozen SONAR space, departing from the joint contrastive training paradigm of CLIP/SigLIP2. It then leverages the Large Concept Model (LCM), a latent diffusion language model operating in SONAR space, to perform vision-language generation without any vision-specific pre-training. The coarse-to-fine curriculum (image → synthetic video → human-annotated video) draws on progressive training strategies from speech-text alignment work, while the V-LCM extension applies instruction tuning within the same latent diffusion objective rather than switching to a discrete-token autoregressive decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a text-only latent diffusion model can zero-shot process visual embeddings with only modest degradation, suggesting the SONAR latent space is genuinely modality-agnostic rather than merely text-structured. The multilingual dominance (61/62 languages) is practically significant for low-resource settings where no vision-language data exists. Open questions include whether the paradigm scales to larger vision encoders or longer videos, whether the post-hoc alignment gap to jointly-trained models (e.g., SigLIP2) narrows with more data, and whether the latent diffusion objective is truly superior to autoregressive decoding in this shared-space setting. The VATEX weakness on short captions and the DREAM-1K retrieval regression relative to PE-CoreG hint at residual limitations in the alignment.
