## Problem

Overparameterized neural networks exhibit a paradox: distinct minima are connected by low-loss paths in parameter space, yet SGD dynamics remain confined to a single basin and rarely traverse these paths. This paper resolves the paradox by identifying entropic barriers arising from curvature variations along connecting paths, which bias noisy optimization back toward flatter endpoints even when the loss is nearly flat.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262447/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multiple independently trained network weights (Wide ResNet-16-4, ResNet-20, ResNet-20, ResNet-110 on CIFAR-10/100) as endpoints, constructs connecting paths via AutoNEB (nonlinear MEPs with adaptive pivot insertion) or linear interpolation, and probes them with SGD noise parameters (batch size 16–256, learning rate 0.001–0.1). Curvature is measured at each pivot using three complementary Hessian statistics: the maximum eigenvalue via power iteration, the trace via the Fisher Information Matrix, and the leading spectrum via SVD of the score matrix on E=1024 training examples. The primary outputs are curvature profiles along the path (revealing a systematic 'bump' away from endpoints) and relaxation trajectories showing how projected SGD dynamics drift back toward flatter regions.

## Method

The paper combines a statistical-physics toy model with empirical analysis of real network landscapes. The toy model (V(x,y) = ½g(y)x²) shows that when a fast stiff mode x is averaged out, the slow soft mode y experiences an effective potential V_eff(y) = T ln g(y), producing an entropic force proportional to temperature that drives the system toward flatter regions. Empirically, the authors measure curvature along AutoNEB-constructed MEPs and linearly connected paths, finding a consistent rise in both λ_max and Tr(H) away from endpoints. To directly observe the entropic force, they introduce a k-step projected SGD algorithm that takes k unconstrained SGD steps before projecting parameters back onto the nearest path segment, preserving multi-step noise effects while constraining dynamics to the path. They further vary batch size, learning rate, and optimizer (vanilla SGD, Nesterov momentum, Adam) to confirm the force scales with effective temperature, and analyze linear mode connectivity at varying splitting epochs k to show entropic barriers persist longer than energetic ones into training.

## Results

The paper's central empirical finding is that curvature (measured via Hessian trace and maximum eigenvalue) systematically rises away from the endpoints along minimum energy paths (MEPs) connecting distinct minima of Wide ResNet-16-4 on CIFAR-10, even though the loss remains nearly flat or even lower than at the endpoints. Projected SGD experiments show that models initialized mid-path are driven back toward the flatter endpoints, with relaxation time decreasing for smaller batch sizes (B=16 vs. larger) and larger learning rates, confirming the entropic origin of the force. In linear mode connectivity experiments (ResNet-20, CIFAR-10), the curvature instability (max/min ratio along the path) exceeds the loss instability for larger splitting epochs k, indicating that entropic barriers persist longer than energetic barriers into training. These qualitative trends are reproduced across architectures (ResNet-110) and datasets (CIFAR-100).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in the loss-landscape literature: the mode-connectivity line of work (Draxler et al. 2018; Frankle et al. 2020) showing that distinct minima are linked by low-loss paths, and the SGD-noise/implicit-bias line (Keskar et al. 2017; Jastrzebski et al. 2018; Wei & Schwab 2019) showing that stochasticity biases optimization toward flatter regions. The key conceptual move is to apply the statistical-physics notion of entropic forces—arising from curvature variations coupled with noise—to the geometry of connecting paths, thereby explaining why dynamics remain confined to a single basin despite the existence of energetically flat bridges. The empirical protocol relies directly on AutoNEB (Draxler et al. 2018) for nonlinear path construction and the shared-prefix training protocol of Frankle et al. (2020) for linear mode connectivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing paradox (low-loss paths exist, yet SGD never traverses them) by identifying curvature-induced entropic barriers as the confining mechanism, with the notable finding that these barriers persist longer into training than energetic ones, making them the dominant force in late-stage basin selection. This has direct implications for weight-space ensembling (SWA, model soups) and model merging, suggesting that averaged minima may be effectively disconnected by entropic barriers even when energetically connected. Key limitations include evaluation only on CIFAR-10/100 with small CNNs (ResNet-20/110, Wide ResNet-16-4), reliance on path-finding methods that introduce geometric bias, and the use of a projected-SGD variant for the most direct entropic-force measurements. Open questions include whether the effect scales to larger architectures and datasets, how to sample the space of connecting paths without algorithmic bias, and whether entropic barriers can formally explain the generalization gap between flat and sharp minima.
