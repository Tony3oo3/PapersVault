## Problem

Existing ML approaches for IR spectra analysis either perform only functional group classification (insufficient for full structure elucidation) or rely on fixed input formats that require retraining to accommodate new types of chemical information. Moreover, prior methods do not emulate the multi-source reasoning workflow that human experts actually follow—combining absorption table interpretation with spectral database retrieval—making them less effective and less extensible for real-world analytical scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260494/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1D IR absorbance spectrum (polynomially interpolated over 500–4000 cm⁻¹, converted from transmittance) as its sole required input, deliberately excluding the chemical formula that prior work assumes is available. Optional supplementary chemical information (atom types, molecular scaffold, or carbon count) can be appended as a single text sentence to agent prompts without any architectural change. The output is a ranked list of top-K SMILES strings (K = 1, 3, 5, 10), evaluated by exact match after InChI canonicalization.

## Method

IR-Agent is a multi-agent framework built around a supervised Transformer-based IR Spectra Translator that generates initial SMILES candidates via beam search (width 3), followed by three specialized LLM agents that perform complementary reasoning. The Table Interpretation (TI) Expert uses an IR Peak Table Assigner tool (SciPy find_peaks + wavenumber-range lookup) to extract local substructures from the IR absorption table and cross-validates them against the Translator candidates. The Retriever (Ret) Expert retrieves top-N spectra from a database via cosine similarity and extracts global structural motifs from the associated SMILES. The Structure Elucidation (SE) Expert integrates both experts' outputs to produce the final ranked SMILES list. The key architectural insight is that distributing these distinct reasoning sub-tasks across specialized agents consistently outperforms a single-agent variant, and new chemical information can be incorporated simply by appending a sentence to prompts—no retraining or redesign required.

## Results

On the NIST dataset of 9,052 experimental IR spectra, IR-Agent (o3-mini, multi-agent) achieves the best overall performance with Top-1 accuracy of 10.3%, Top-3 of 17.8%, Top-5 of 19.9%, and Top-10 of 21.6%, outperforming the standalone Transformer (IR Spectra Translator) which scores 9.8% Top-1 and 17.6% Top-10. The multi-agent framework consistently outperforms its single-agent counterpart across all LLM backbones (GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o, o3-mini), with the largest gap observed for o3-mini (Top-10: 21.6% vs. 19.7%). When chemical information (atom types, scaffold, carbon count) is incorporated via simple prompt augmentation, performance improves further, reaching 27.8% Top-10 with atom types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is the Patch-Based Self-Attention Transformer (Wu et al., 2025), a state-of-the-art non-agentic method for IR-based structure elucidation. IR-Agent (o3-mini, multi) loses on Top-1 exact match (10.3% vs. 12.9%) but clearly outperforms it on Top-3 (17.8% vs. 16.0%), Top-5 (19.9% vs. 17.0%), and Top-10 (21.6% vs. 18.1%), indicating that IR-Agent produces a broader set of correct candidates. IR-Agent also surpasses the Patch-Based Transformer on all three Tanimoto similarity metrics (MACCS: 0.770 vs. 0.721; RDKit: 0.596 vs. 0.547; Morgan: 0.549 vs. 0.518), suggesting structurally more meaningful predictions. Internally, the multi-agent framework consistently beats the single-agent variant across all LLM backbones, and the multi-agent GPT-4o system matches or exceeds the single-agent o3-mini system, demonstrating that architectural decomposition compensates for model capability. A ReAct-style autonomous agent framework underperforms IR-Agent's deterministic workflow (Top-10: 15.8% vs. 20.4% with GPT-4o), confirming that guided expert-like tool selection is critical for this domain-specific task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the multi-agent LLM paradigm—previously explored in chemistry (ChemCrow, Coscientist) and drug discovery (DrugAgent)—to the specific domain of IR spectral analysis, a task previously addressed by fixed-architecture ML models (Transformers, RL) that require predefined input formats and often assume access to the chemical formula. It departs from prior IR structure elucidation work (Alberts et al. 2024a, Wu et al. 2025, Ellis et al. 2023) by replacing the monolithic prediction pipeline with a decomposed expert-agent workflow that mirrors how human spectroscopists actually reason: consulting absorption tables for local substructures and spectral databases for global context. The IR Spectra Translator (a standard Transformer) serves as a candidate generator, while the LLM agents perform the interpretive and integrative reasoning that prior ML approaches handled implicitly or not at all.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's key practical contribution is its extensibility: new types of chemical information (atom types, scaffold, carbon count) can be incorporated via simple prompt appending without retraining or architectural changes, addressing a real limitation of prior ML-based IR analysis. However, absolute performance remains modest (Top-1 ~10%, Top-10 ~22% on 9K experimental spectra), the system is vulnerable to LLM hallucination, and it fails entirely on mixtures. Open questions include whether the translator bottleneck can be bypassed by feeding spectral images directly to LLMs, whether peak shapes and intensities (currently ignored) could be leveraged, and whether the deterministic multi-agent workflow generalizes beyond IR to other spectral modalities.
