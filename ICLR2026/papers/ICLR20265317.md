## Problem

Diffusion-based probabilistic nowcasting models produce sharp ensemble forecasts but require hundreds of function evaluations per sample, making them impractical for time-critical operational settings. Deterministic models avoid this cost but produce overly smooth predictions that underrepresent extreme events and inherent atmospheric uncertainty. No prior work had applied Conditional Flow Matching as a full noise-to-data generative framework for precipitation nowcasting; existing flow-based approaches only used rectified flows to refine deterministic forecasts.

## Input / Output

The model takes 13 consecutive 5-minute radar frames (65 minutes of context) at 1 km resolution — 384×384 VIL fields for SEVIR or 301×401 reflectivity composites for ARSO — and generates an ensemble of 12 future frames (1 hour ahead). A frame-wise VAE first compresses each frame into a 4-channel latent (48×48 for SEVIR, 38×52 for ARSO), and the CFM model operates entirely in this latent space, solving a 10-step Euler ODE per ensemble member. The final output is decoded back to pixel space, yielding 8 probabilistic radar sequences per forecast.

## Method

FlowCast combines a PatchGAN-trained VAE (L1 + KL + adversarial loss) with an Earthformer-UNet backbone adapted for the I-CFM objective. The CFM model learns a time-dependent vector field v_θ(Z_t, t, Z_past) by regressing against the target field u_t = Z_future − Z_P, where the interpolated state Z_t = (1−t)Z_P + t·Z_future + σε uses a small Gaussian noise (σ=0.01) to regularize the training trajectory. The U-Net uses stacked Cuboid Self-Attention blocks (axial pattern over temporal, height, width) with the flow time t injected via residual embedding blocks at each stage. The key distinction from diffusion is the straight-line ODE prior: CFM enforces the simplest possible noise-to-data transport, which the authors show yields near-optimal CRPS and CSI with as few as 1–10 Euler steps, whereas a DDIM baseline on the same architecture requires 50–100 steps and degrades sharply below 10 NFE.

## Results

On the SEVIR benchmark, FlowCast achieves the best CRPS (0.0182), CSI-M (0.460), FSS-M-P16 (0.767), and HSS-M (0.580) among all seven models, while also attaining the highest extreme-event detection score (CSI-219 = 0.057). On the ARSO dataset, FlowCast leads in CRPS (0.0209), CSI-M (0.420), FSS-M-P16 (0.738), HSS-M (0.535), and CSI-39 (0.073). In the CFM-vs-diffusion ablation, a single-step CFM forecast (CRPS 0.0207, CSI-M 0.454) already surpasses a 100-step DDIM baseline (CRPS 0.0208, CSI-M 0.398) while being ~90× faster (2.6 s vs. 239 s per sequence).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowCast outperforms all four deterministic baselines (U-Net, Earthformer, Earthfarseer, SimVP) on every probabilistic and detection metric: e.g., on SEVIR it improves CSI-M from 0.423 (SimVP, best deterministic) to 0.460 and CSI-219 from 0.012 to 0.057, reflecting its ability to generate sharp, intense features that deterministic MSE-trained models smooth away. Against the two probabilistic baselines, FlowCast beats PreDiff on CSI-M (0.460 vs. 0.413), HSS-M (0.580 vs. 0.523), FSS-M-P16 (0.767 vs. 0.699), and CRPS (0.0182 vs. 0.0189). Versus CasCast, the strongest probabilistic competitor, FlowCast wins on CSI-M (0.460 vs. 0.442), HSS-M (0.580 vs. 0.562), FSS-M-P16 (0.767 vs. 0.763), CRPS (0.0182 vs. 0.0201), and extreme-event CSI-219 (0.057 vs. 0.054), while CasCast retains a narrow edge on CSI-P16-M (0.520 vs. 0.506) and FAR-M (0.383 vs. 0.325 — though FlowCast's lower FAR is actually favourable). On ARSO the pattern holds: FlowCast leads all baselines on CSI-M, HSS-M, FSS-M-P16, CRPS, and CSI-39, with the only metric where a deterministic model (SimVP) edges ahead being FAR-M (0.401 vs. 0.422), a consequence of SimVP's overly smooth predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowCast sits at the intersection of two research threads: latent-space generative modeling for nowcasting (PreDiff, LDCast, CasCast) and the broader shift from diffusion to flow-matching objectives in generative modeling. It adopts the Earthformer-UNet backbone and VAE compression from the latent diffusion nowcasting literature, but replaces the iterative denoising objective with Independent CFM (Tong et al., 2024), which regresses a vector field along a straight-line probability path. This departs from CasCast's hybrid design (deterministic base + conditional diffusion refinement) and from Feng et al.'s use of rectified flows solely for sharpening deterministic outputs, positioning CFM as a standalone, full noise-to-data generative model for the first time in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: CFM achieves state-of-the-art probabilistic and deterministic accuracy while requiring as few as 1–10 ODE steps versus 50–100+ for diffusion, a ~100× inference speedup that makes rapid ensemble generation feasible in operational flood-warning settings. The ablation showing CFM outperforming DDIM on the identical architecture suggests the straight-line transport prior is a genuinely better inductive bias for spatiotemporal data with strong local temporal coherence, not merely a faster approximation. Open questions include whether the advantage persists with multi-modal inputs (satellite, NWP), across more meteorological regimes, and whether the I-CFM σ-regularization is critical or an artifact of the specific latent-space geometry.
