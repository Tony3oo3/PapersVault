## Problem

Existing table retrieval methods flatten tables into linear text sequences, discarding structural semantics (merged cells, multi-level headers, spatial layout) and ignoring embedded images within cells. No standardized benchmark exists for image-based table retrieval, leaving the promising paradigm of treating tables as visual representations unexplored and unevaluated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262417/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The retriever takes a natural language query and matches it against a corpus of 81,839 table images (Wikipedia webpage screenshots, averaging 765×1087 pixels) with optional title text, producing a ranked list of relevant tables evaluated at cutoffs of 10 and 50. Tables are categorized by visual difficulty (Easy <25% browser window, Medium 25–100%, Hard >100%), and for RAG evaluation the top-k retrieved tables feed into LLM/MLLM generators that produce a free-text answer scored by exact-match against ground-truth annotations.

## Method

TaR-ViR is constructed by collecting ~2 million Wikipedia webpage screenshots corresponding to NQ-TABLES tables, deduplicating via CLIP similarity (threshold 0.9 with shared URL prefix), and re-annotating relevance using a pipeline where Qwen2.5-VL-72B generates pseudo-labels verified by human annotators (~80% agreement). The empirical study trains text retrievers (BM25, BERT, BGE, GTE, Qwen3-Embedding) and multimodal retrievers (CLIP, SigLIP, GME, VLM2Vec, UniME, ColPali, DSE) under matched protocols—full fine-tuning for BERT/CLIP-based models, LoRA (rank 16) for LLM/MLLM-based models, 20 epochs, batch size 144. For controlled comparison, dedicated Qwen2-VL-2B retrievers are trained with identical hyperparameters, varying only the input format (OCR text, raw web text, or image). The key finding is that multimodal retrievers (UniME, VLM2Vec-7B) match or exceed the best text retriever (BGE) on NDCG and MRR, with the advantage growing for larger, structurally complex tables where OCR-based serialization degrades.

## Results

On TaR-ViR, the best multimodal retriever UniME (7.57B, LLaVA-1.6) achieves R@10=93.38, R@50=98.76, NDCG@5=75.62, and MRR@5=71.44, outperforming the best text retriever BGE (109M) on NDCG and MRR (72.82/68.18) while matching it on recall. VLM2Vec (7.07B) attains the highest recall (R@10=94.23, R@50=98.92). When table titles are unavailable, multimodal retrievers retain approximately 70% of their full-data performance, and image-only retrievers (DSE, ColPali) drop to R@10≈63–66, confirming that title text remains a valuable signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among text retrievers, BGE (109M) is the strongest, achieving R@10=92.15 and NDCG@5=72.82, outperforming the larger GTE (1.54B, R@10=87.46) and Qwen3-Embedding (4.05B, R@10=89.30), indicating that model optimization matters more than parameter scale for text embeddings. BiBERT (109M) trails BGE by ~3 points on R@10, and BM25 is far behind all neural retrievers (R@10=20.50). Among multimodal retrievers with title+content image, UniME (7.57B) achieves the best NDCG@5 (75.62) and MRR@5 (71.44), surpassing BGE by ~3 and ~3 points respectively, while VLM2Vec (7.07B) leads on recall (R@10=94.23, R@50=98.92). UniSE-MLLM (2.21B) matches BGE on R@10 (92.23 vs 92.15) but lags on NDCG and MRR. Smaller or earlier multimodal models (CLIP, SigLIP, GME) are substantially weaker, with R@10 ranging from 36 to 60. Without table titles, image-only retrievers DSE and ColPali drop to R@10≈63–66, roughly 30 points below the title+image setting. In the RAG evaluation (Table 6), Qwen3-8B as generator achieves the highest accuracy (58.55% at n=5 with multimodal retrieval), while LLaVA-OneVision performs worst (~20%), and the multimodal retriever slightly outperforms the text retriever for MLLM generators but underperforms for text-only LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NQ-TABLES text-based table retrieval benchmark (Herzig et al., 2021) into the multimodal domain by replacing serialized table text with webpage screenshots, thereby departing from the dominant text-serialization paradigm used by TAPAS, DTR, UTP, and ECAT. It repurposes the multimodal retrieval model family (CLIP, SigLIP, VLM2Vec, GME, UniME, ColPali) for the table-specific setting, bridging the vision-language embedding literature with structured-data retrieval. The key conceptual departure is treating the table's visual layout—merged cells, hierarchical headers, embedded images—as a first-class retrieval signal rather than an artifact to be flattened into linear text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TaR-ViR is the first benchmark to formalize image-centric table retrieval, and its finding that a 7.57B multimodal retriever (UniME) matches or exceeds a 109M text retriever (BGE) on NDCG and MRR suggests that the OCR-then-retrieve pipeline may be unnecessary for many real-world table corpora. This has practical implications for scalable collection of open-world tables that exist in PDFs, spreadsheets, or web pages without clean text extraction. Key limitations include the Wikipedia-only provenance (limiting generalizability to diverse table formats), the ~80% automated annotation accuracy, and the observation that MLLMs still degrade on tables exceeding one browser window—open questions include whether the paradigm holds for truly heterogeneous, non-Wikipedia table sources and how to handle temporal drift in large-scale deployments.
