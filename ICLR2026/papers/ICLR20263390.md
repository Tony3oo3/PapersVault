## Problem

All existing Schrödinger bridge (SB) algorithms require access to samples from both marginal distributions, making them inapplicable when one or both distributions are specified only by unnormalised densities (energy functions) with unknown normalising constants. This paper proposes the first general method for inferring data-to-energy and energy-to-energy SBs, extending iterative proportional fitting (IPF) to the data-free setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263390/cropped_page8_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

In the data-to-energy setting, the method takes samples from the source distribution p_0 and a queryable energy function E_1(x) (with unknown Z) for the target; in the energy-to-energy setting, both marginals are given as unnormalised densities. The output is a time-discretised stochastic bridge (Euler-Maruyama chain with K steps) parameterised by learned drift and diffusion coefficient networks. For the outsourced application, the bridge operates in the latent space of a pretrained generator (StyleGAN, SN-GAN, or VAE), taking a class label y and a classifier reward r(f(z), y) to produce posterior latents that decode into images preserving style features from the prior.

## Method

The core contribution is a source-conditional VarGrad (log-variance) loss that enforces the proportionality p_θ(τ|x_0) ∝ p_φ(τ|x_1)·p_1(x_1) over trajectories sharing the same x_0, without requiring samples from p_1. This loss replaces the intractable maximum-likelihood forward IPF step, while the backward step retains standard log-likelihood training on p_0 samples. Training is framed as off-policy reinforcement learning, employing a replay buffer of terminal samples x_1, reverse-trajectory reuse (N=2 trajectories per x_0), periodic unadjusted Langevin updates to the buffer, and a tunable off-policy ratio (default 0.8) to balance exploration and exploitation. A secondary contribution is learning the diffusion coefficient alongside the drift in the Euler-Maruyama discretisation, which corrects for time-discretisation error and improves path KL over fixed-variance baselines. The full algorithm alternates backward (ML) and forward (VarGrad) IPF steps, reusing model weights and buffer state across iterations.

## Results

On the data-to-data Schrödinger bridge benchmarks (Gauss↔GMM, Gauss↔Two Moons, Two Moons↔GMM), the proposed learnable-variance IPF achieves the best W₂² on Gauss↔Two Moons (0.022±0.009) and Two Moons↔GMM (0.023±0.013), while remaining competitive on Gauss↔GMM (0.042±0.018). In the data-to-energy setting (no access to target samples), the method achieves W₂² of 0.020±0.003 at K=40 steps, matching or exceeding the data-to-data oracle (0.028±0.009). For outsourced posterior sampling on CIFAR-10, the Schrödinger bridge achieves FID scores of 22.3 (Car), 37.3 (Dog), and 33.0 (Horse) under SN-GAN, outperforming Langevin (25.3, 37.7, 27.6) and the diffusion sampler baseline (83.9, 60.5) while approaching rejection sampling ground truth (31.3, 43.7, 35.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DSBM-IMF (Shi et al., 2023), the proposed method wins on W₂² for Gauss↔Two Moons (0.022 vs 0.061) and Two Moons↔GMM (0.023 vs 0.043) but loses on Path KL across all three pairs (e.g., 2.840 vs 1.004 on Gauss↔GMM). Against [SF]²M (Tong et al., 2024b), the method dominates on W₂² for all three distribution pairs (0.042 vs 0.041, 0.022 vs 0.053, 0.023 vs 0.057) but trails on Path KL. Against the IPF-based DSB methods (De Bortoli et al., 2021), the proposed method is substantially better on both metrics across all pairs (e.g., W₂² 0.022 vs 0.171 on Gauss↔Two Moons). Against SDE (Chen et al., 2021b), the method wins on W₂² for Gauss↔Two Moons (0.022 vs 0.033) and Two Moons↔GMM (0.023 vs 0.025) but is slightly worse on Gauss↔GMM (0.042 vs 0.037). In the outsourced sampling setting (Table 2), the Schrödinger bridge outperforms the diffusion sampler baseline (Venkatraman et al., 2025) by a large margin on FID (e.g., 22.3 vs 83.9 for Car under SN-GAN) and also beats Langevin sampling, while approaching rejection-sampling ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: the IPF algorithm for Schrödinger bridges (De Bortoli et al., 2021; Vargas et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2021b), which requires samples from both marginals, and off-policy reinforcement learning losses for diffusion samplers of unnormalised densities (Richter et al., 2020; Sendera et al., 2024), which operate without data samples. The key conceptual move is to replace the maximum-likelihood IPF step that requires target samples with a source-conditional VarGrad loss, thereby extending IPF to the data-to-energy and energy-to-energy settings. The learnable diffusion coefficient generalises Gritsaev et al. (2025) from single-process diffusion samplers to the two-process SB framework, and the outsourced application extends Venkatraman et al. (2025) from diffusion samplers to full Schrödinger bridges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first general method for inferring Schrödinger bridges when one or both marginals are specified only as unnormalised densities, opening a data-free route to stochastic optimal transport that was previously intractable. The practical application to posterior sampling in GAN/VAE latent spaces yields a data-free image-to-image translation method that preserves style features while changing class identity, outperforming both Langevin and diffusion-sampler baselines on FID. However, the method is prone to mode collapse, has only been validated on CIFAR-10/MNIST and 2D synthetic distributions, and scaling to higher-dimensional or arbitrary-prior settings remains an open challenge. Amortising over multiple conditioning variables and extending to discrete state spaces are identified as promising next steps.
