## Problem

Video LLMs that feed all frame-wise visual tokens into the LLM incur massive computational overhead, yet existing pooling strategies are conservative (typically 4× reduction) and ignore the fact that user instructions often pertain to only a small portion of the video. The paper asks whether instruction-aware, prompt-guided pooling can enable far more aggressive compression (up to 18×) without sacrificing—or even while improving—video understanding performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264735/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a T-frame video (32 frames uniformly sampled for short/medium videos, 64 for long videos), a user text prompt or question, or single/multiple images as input. Visual features V ∈ R^{T×W×H×D} from a CLIP-ViT encoder are compressed via prompt-guided 3D pooling with kernel/stride (2,3,3) for video or (1,3,3) for images, yielding V′ ∈ R^{T′×W′×H′×D} with up to 18× fewer tokens. The LLM then produces free-form text responses, video captions, or multiple-choice answers. The CLIP text encoder's context is extended beyond its native 77-token limit via asymmetric positional embedding interpolation to handle long or multi-turn prompts.

## Method

PPLLaVA introduces three components: (1) Fine-grained Vision-Prompt Alignment computes a token-level relevance map S by taking the softmax-normalized dot product between each video patch token (projected via CLIP's visual projection) and the CLIP text CLS token, identifying which spatiotemporal regions are instruction-relevant; (2) Prompt-Guided Pooling uses S as a dynamic 3D convolutional kernel—its parameters change as the kernel slides over V—performing weighted-averaging compression with flexible kernel size and stride to produce output of any desired 3D resolution; (3) CLIP Context Extension applies asymmetric positional embedding interpolation (large rate r early, small r late) to extend the text encoder's context while preserving well-trained early positions. Training is instruction-tuning only (no contrastive or alignment pretraining for CLIP-based bases), with full fine-tuning of the LLM, projection MLP, and CLIP text encoder, using interleaved batches mixing images, multi-images, and videos. The module is plug-and-play, demonstrated on LLaVA-Next, LLaVA-Video, and InternVL3-8B with different visual encoders (CLIP, SigLIP, InternViT).

## Results

PPLLaVA achieves state-of-the-art results across 7 video benchmarks with up to 18× token compression. PPLLaVA(InternVL3) scores 86.8 on NextQA, 63.9 on EgoSchema, 60.3 on ActivityNet, 75.6 on MVBench, and 67.1 on VideoMME-Overall. PPLLaVA(LLaVA-Video) reaches 64.5 on VideoMME-Overall with only 1024 visual tokens (vs. 4608 for the LLaVA-Video baseline), and at aligned token budgets it outperforms the baseline by 6.86% (1000 tokens) and 4.4% (2000 tokens). On long videos (>30 min) in VideoMME, PPLLaVA(LLaVA-Video) surpasses LLaVA-Video by 3.7% and LLaVA-OneVision by 7.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPLLaVA(LLaVA-Video) outperforms its direct baseline LLaVA-Video on every reported benchmark: +1.9 on NextQA (84.1 vs 82.2), +4.3 on EgoSchema (61.6 vs 57.3), +3.2 on ActivityNet (59.7 vs 56.5), +0.14 on VCG-Bench (3.66 vs 3.52), and +1.3 on VideoMME-Overall (64.5 vs 63.2), while using only 1/4 the visual tokens. Against LLaVA-OneVision, PPLLaVA(LLaVA-Video) gains 4.7 on NextQA, 1.5 on EgoSchema, 3.1 on ActivityNet, and 6.3 on VideoMME-Overall. PPLLaVA(InternVL3) edges out InternVL3 by +0.2 on MVBench (75.6 vs 75.4) and +1.5 on VideoMME-Overall (67.1 vs 66.2), and surpasses VideoLLaMA3 by +2.3 on NextQA (86.8 vs 84.5) and +0.8 on VideoMME-Overall (67.1 vs 66.3). On key-content-extraction baselines (VideoAgent, VideoTree, LVNet), PPLLaVA maintains a clear lead on both NextQA and EgoSchema while avoiding their per-frame search overhead. Notably, PPLLaVA also retains strong image performance (Table 4), scoring 37.9 on MMMU and 88.46 on POPE, surpassing LLaVA-Next-7B and LLaVA-1.5-13B on most image benchmarks despite reducing visual tokens to 1/9 of the original count.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPLLaVA sits at the intersection of two lines of work: instruction-aware visual token compression (Q-Former in BLIP-2/InstructBLIP) and lightweight pooling strategies in modern video LLMs (AdaptiveAvgPool in PLLaVA, LLaVA-Video, Qwen2.5-VL). It departs from Q-Formers by replacing their fixed-size query tokens with a parameter-light CLIP text encoder whose token-level relevance scores act as dynamic 3D convolutional kernels, and it departs from simple average pooling by making the pooling weights instruction-conditioned. The CLIP context extension via asymmetric positional embedding interpolation is a novel auxiliary contribution not seen in prior video LLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18× visual token reduction while matching or exceeding SOTA across seven benchmarks is practically significant for deploying video LLMs on resource-constrained or real-time systems, and the plug-and-play compatibility with LLaVA-Next, LLaVA-Video, and InternVL3 suggests broad adoptability. However, the authors acknowledge that only 7B/8B models were tested and context length was limited by compute (8 A100s), leaving open whether the approach scales to 34B+ models or 8K+ token contexts. An open question is how well the method generalizes when user prompts are truly uninformative, though the VCG-Bench results and attention visualizations provide encouraging (if not conclusive) evidence.
