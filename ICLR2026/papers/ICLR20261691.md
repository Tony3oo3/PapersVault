## Problem

Existing protein motif scaffolding benchmarks (e.g., MotifBench) focus on known functional sites such as enzyme active sites, conflating the fundamental challenge of 3D geometric preservation with complex functional requirements like residue identity and charge distribution. This conflation makes it impossible to diagnose whether a generative model's failure stems from an inability to preserve geometry or from incompatibility with functional constraints. GeomMotif isolates the geometric preservation capability by sampling arbitrary structural fragments from the PDB without functional bias, providing a clean diagnostic of a model's core generative capacity.

## Input / Output

Each of the 57 benchmark tasks specifies one or two motifs (defined as all Cα atoms within a 13 Å radius of a central residue) with 1–7 continuous sequence fragments, plus length-range constraints for the variable scaffold regions (±30% of ground truth, capped at 250 residues total). Structure-based models receive the motif backbone coordinates and generate a full backbone, which is then inverse-folded by ProteinMPNN (8 sequences) and validated by ESMFold; sequence-based models directly generate sequences that are folded by ESMFold. A design is scored as Successful if motif scRMSD < 1.0 Å and mean pLDDT ≥ 70, and the final SUN score additionally requires the design to be structurally unique (TM-score clustering at 0.8 among successful designs) and novel (TM-score < 0.8 to the ground-truth structure).

## Method

The benchmark is constructed by filtering the PDB to high-resolution (≤2.5 Å) X-ray monomers of ≤250 residues, deduplicating via MMseqs2 (80% identity) and TM-score (0.5) clustering, and retaining only structures that ESMFold can fold to ≤1.0 Å RMSD, guaranteeing every task is solvable by the evaluation pipeline. Motifs are extracted as 13 Å spatial neighborhoods, filtered for size (≥30 residues), redundancy (<20% overlap), and loop content (<25%), then stratified by fragment complexity to yield 35 single-motif and 22 paired-motif tasks spanning diverse CATH folds. Each task is characterized by eight physicochemical properties (helical/extended/loop content, motif size, hydrophobicity, burial ratio, charge density, internal-to-external contact ratio) enabling property-driven performance analysis. The evaluation adapts the SUN (Successful, Unique, Novel) score from materials science into a single metric that simultaneously rewards geometric accuracy, structural diversity, and originality, and is applied in both variable-length and fixed-length settings to distinguish true generalization from memorization.

## Results

On the GeomMotif benchmark (57 tasks, 100 samples each), structure-based models dominate: Genie2 achieves the highest overall SUN score of 39.4%, followed by La-Proteina (38.8%), RFdiffusion (37.8%), and Protpardelle-1C (33.8%). Sequence-based models trail by an order of magnitude, with ESM3 in sequence-only mode reaching only 3.5% SUN and DPLM-3B at 2.7%. A striking finding is that ESM3's multimodal (sequence+structure) mode (1.4% SUN) underperforms its sequence-only counterpart (3.5%), and all sequence-based models fail categorically on paired-motif tasks (≤0.5% SUN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Structure-based models outperform all sequence-based baselines by a factor of 10–25× on overall SUN score. Among structure-based models, Genie2 (39.4% SUN) edges out La-Proteina (38.8%) and RFdiffusion (37.8%), while RFdiffusion2 (17.9%) underperforms the original RFdiffusion (37.8%) by 20 points, likely due to its narrow enzyme-design tuning. FrameFlow (23.3%) sits mid-pack among structure-based models. Among sequence-based baselines, ESM3 in sequence-only mode (3.5%) leads DPLM-3B (2.7%) and DPLM-650M (2.1%), but the counterintuitive result is that ESM3's multimodal variant (1.4%) is the worst-performing non-random model, suggesting structure conditioning introduces conflicting signals. On paired-motif tasks specifically, all sequence-based models collapse to near-zero SUN (≤0.5%), while structure-based models retain 10–19% SUN, exposing a fundamental architectural limitation in encoding long-range spatial constraints from linear sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the motif scaffolding evaluation paradigm established by RFdiffusion (Watson et al., 2023) and formalized by MotifBench (Zheng et al., 2025), but deliberately decouples geometric preservation from functional requirements. Where prior benchmarks sample motifs exclusively from known functional sites (enzyme active sites, binding interfaces), GeomMotif samples arbitrary structural neighborhoods from the PDB, isolating the pure geometric scaffolding capability. The evaluation pipeline (backbone generation → ProteinMPNN → ESMFold → scRMSD) follows the community standard, and the SUN score is adapted from materials science (Sriram et al., 2024), but the task construction, property characterization, and modality-agnostic design are novel contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeomMotif provides the diagnostic precision that functional benchmarks lack: when a model fails, one can now determine whether the cause is geometric inability or physicochemical incompatibility. The finding that sequence-based models categorically fail on spatially separated (paired) motifs while structure-based models handle them reasonably well points to a fundamental architectural limitation that must be addressed before sequence-only approaches can compete in protein design. The counterintuitive result that ESM3's multimodal mode underperforms its sequence-only counterpart suggests structure conditioning can introduce conflicting optimization signals, a finding with implications for multimodal protein model design. Open questions include whether the 57-task scale is sufficient for robust conclusions, whether the ProteinMPNN/ESMFold pipeline biases results, and whether the property-based analysis can be leveraged for targeted training data curation.
