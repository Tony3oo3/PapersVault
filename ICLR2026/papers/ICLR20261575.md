## Problem

Pretrained diffusion models are trained to denoise Gaussian-corrupted natural images, but low-resolution inputs follow a substantially different distribution, making direct inference infeasible. Prior diffusion-based SR methods either fine-tune the denoiser (weakening its generative prior) or start from pure noise (redundantly regenerating information already in the LR image), both of which require multiple denoising steps and compromise perceptual quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261575/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a bicubic-upsampled LR image (×4 scale factor) and produces a single HR image via one forward pass through the frozen SD-Turbo denoiser in the VAE latent space. A lightweight timestep estimator predicts a continuous timestep in [0, 500] from the LR input, and a fixed (or optionally LLaVA-extracted) text prompt conditions the denoiser. Training uses 512×512×3 RGB patches with a batch size of 16 on DF2K and LSDIR.

## Method

DM-SR trains only an image encoder (initialized from the pretrained VAE encoder with ControlNet-style timestep embeddings) to project LR images into the noise-corrupted HR latent distribution that the frozen SD-Turbo denoiser expects, eliminating any fine-tuning of the diffusion model. A VGG-based timestep estimator adaptively assigns higher timesteps to more degraded inputs, aligning each sample with the appropriate noise level of the diffusion prior. The encoded latent is decomposed via the frozen denoiser into an image component Z_SR and a noise component ε_SR, which are supervised by a combination of L1, perceptual, adversarial (ControlNet-style discriminator conditioned on the LR image), distribution-matching, and noise losses. At inference, a single denoising step produces Z_SR, which is decoded by the pretrained VAE decoder into the final SR image, achieving state-of-the-art perceptual quality with 92 ms runtime on an A100.

## Results

DM-SR achieves state-of-the-art perceptual quality across all four benchmark datasets using a single diffusion step. On ImageNet (synthetic), DM-SR attains LIQE 4.699, CLIP-IQA 0.785, TOPIQ(NR) 0.712, NIMA 5.492, MANIQA 0.633, and MUSIQ 73.856, outperforming the closest competitor InvSR on 6 of 7 non-reference metrics. On real-world datasets (DRealSR, RealSR, RealSet80), DM-SR similarly leads on LIQE, CLIP-IQA, TOPIQ(NR), NIMA, MANIQA, and MUSIQ. In terms of efficiency, DM-SR runs in 92 ms per 128×128 image on an A100 GPU with 34.16M trainable parameters, making it the fastest diffusion-based SR method tested while maintaining competitive reference-based metrics (PSNR 24.98, SSIM 0.710, LPIPS 0.317 on RealSR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DM-SR consistently outperforms all five baselines on the majority of non-reference perceptual metrics across all four datasets. Against the strongest single-step competitors (OSEDiff and InvSR), DM-SR gains approximately 0.1–0.2 on LIQE, 0.05–0.08 on CLIP-IQA, 0.07–0.09 on TOPIQ(NR), and 0.15–0.35 on MANIQA, with the MANIQA gap being particularly large (e.g., 0.633 vs. 0.469 on ImageNet). The only metric where DM-SR does not lead is BRISQUE, where OSEDiff (12.03 on ImageNet) or SinSR (11.20 on RealSet80) edges it out slightly, reflecting DM-SR's prioritization of perceptual realism over pixel-level fidelity. On reference-based metrics (Table 2, RealSR), DM-SR (PSNR 24.98, SSIM 0.710, LPIPS 0.317) sits between OSEDiff (PSNR 25.94, SSIM 0.755) and InvSR (PSNR 23.74, SSIM 0.680), confirming a favorable perceptual–distortion trade-off. In terms of efficiency, DM-SR (92 ms, 34.16M params) is the fastest diffusion-based method tested, roughly 2× faster than OSEDiff (177 ms) and 37× faster than StableSR-50 (3460 ms), while using comparable parameters to InvSR (33.84M). The multi-step StableSR-50 baseline, despite 50 DDPM iterations, is outperformed by DM-SR's single-step approach on nearly all perceptual metrics, underscoring the benefit of preserving the full pretrained generative prior without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

DM-SR departs from the dominant paradigm in diffusion-based SR, where prior methods (StableSR, SeeSR, SinSR, OSEDiff, InvSR) either fine-tune the diffusion denoiser or use ControlNet-style conditioning modules that risk weakening the pretrained generative prior. Instead, DM-SR keeps the SD-Turbo denoiser entirely frozen and trains only a lightweight encoder (initialized from the VAE encoder with ControlNet-style timestep injection) to project LR images directly into the noise-corrupted HR distribution that the diffusion model was trained on. The adaptive timestep estimation, grounded in LPIPS-based degradation scoring, further differentiates it from single-step methods that apply a fixed noise level to all inputs regardless of degradation severity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By preserving the full generative prior without any denoiser fine-tuning, DM-SR offers a clean and efficient recipe (92 ms single-step inference) that achieves consistent SOTA perceptual quality across both synthetic and real-world benchmarks, making it practically attractive for deployment. The distribution-matching framing also opens a natural extension to other restoration tasks (deblurring, denoising, inpainting) where the same 'encode into the diffusion model's native distribution' strategy could apply. Key open questions include whether input-specific text prompts (e.g., via LLaVA) can close the remaining distortion gap, and whether the LPIPS-based timestep ground truth can be replaced by a more principled degradation-to-noise-level mapping.
