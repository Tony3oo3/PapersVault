## Problem

Forecasting how 3D medical scans evolve over time is critical for disease progression monitoring and treatment planning, yet existing methods are limited to single-context image-to-image prediction, fixed grid timestamps, or global label targets. No prior approach supports many-to-one sequence-to-image forecasting from multiple past scans with both discrete and continuous (real-valued) timestamps in 3D medical voxel space, leaving irregularly sampled longitudinal series without a unified modeling framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of T context 3D volumes (e.g., 11×32×128×128 for ACDC cine-MRI, 7×16×128×128 for ISLES perfusion CT, 7×96×96×64 for Lumiere longitudinal MRI) with associated timestamps—either on a regular grid with missing frames or at arbitrary real-valued times—plus a target time, and produces a single predicted 3D volume at that target time. In the discrete variant, missing grid slots are filled via last-observed carry-forward (LOCF); in the continuous variant, no grid alignment or zero-padding is applied, so computational cost scales with the number of context images rather than the grid span.

## Method

CRONOS re-casts Flow Matching as sequence-to-image transport: the context stack X₀ = [I₁,…,I_T] is flowed toward a broadcast target stack X₁ = [I_target,…,I_target] (T copies), so a single shared velocity field v_θ simultaneously moves all T volumes toward the target under one set of parameters. The backbone is a four-scale 3D U-Net with residual blocks and FiLM conditioning, where time enters via Fourier feature embeddings—of the scalar flow step τ ∈ [0,1] in the discrete variant, or of the interpolated real timestamps T_τ = (1−τ)·t_ctx + τ·t_target in the continuous variant. Training minimizes the standard conditional flow matching velocity loss (MSE between predicted and ground-truth velocity X₁−X₀) with AdamW; inference integrates the learned ODE over 10 steps. The continuous variant's key advantage is that it conditions on actual scan times without grid quantization, enabling predictions at arbitrary target times while preserving true temporal geometry and reducing memory compared to the discrete formulation.

## Results

Across three public 3D medical datasets (ACDC cine-MRI, ISLES perfusion CT, LUMIERE longitudinal MRI), CRONOS (both discrete and continuous variants) substantially outperforms all sequence-model baselines and the Last Context Image (LCI) heuristic. On ACDC, CRONOS discrete achieves SSIM 94.51% and PSNR 30.51 dB, versus 49.08% SSIM for SimVP and 92.79% for LCI. On the challenging LUMIERE dataset (very sparse, heterogeneous tumor trajectories), CRONOS discrete reaches SSIM 91.43% and PSNR 22.43 dB, while SimVP collapses to negative SSIM (−1.92%) and ViViT runs out of memory. The continuous variant further improves NRMSE (e.g., 3.74 vs. 3.97 on ACDC) by exploiting real-valued timestamps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRONOS decisively outperforms all four sequence-model baselines (ConvLSTM, SimVP, ViViT, NODE+LSTM) across every dataset and metric, with margins of 40–90 SSIM points on ACDC and ISLES. The gap is most dramatic on LUMIERE, where SimVP produces negative SSIM (−1.92%) and ConvLSTM drops to 9.21%, while CRONOS maintains 89–91% SSIM. Against the LCI heuristic—a surprisingly strong baseline due to slowly changing anatomy—CRONOS discrete gains +1.7 SSIM on ACDC, +1.0 on ISLES, and +3.1 on LUMIERE, confirming that multi-context temporal modeling adds value beyond simply copying the last frame. ViViT (transformer) is the weakest sequence baseline on ACDC (17.06% SSIM) and fails entirely on LUMIERE due to GPU memory exhaustion. The image-to-image Latent Diffusion AE baseline (Puglisi et al.) is outperformed by CRONOS on PSNR while requiring roughly two orders of magnitude less training time and far less VRAM, and it does not support multi-context input.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRONOS re-casts Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2023) from a noise-to-data generative paradigm into a sequence-to-image transport mechanism via 'temporal broadcasting': a stack of T context volumes serves as X₀ and a broadcast target as X₁, so a single shared velocity field simultaneously moves all context frames toward the target. This departs from prior medical longitudinal work (diffusion models, Neural ODEs) that is restricted to image-to-image prediction, and from 2D video prediction backbones (ConvLSTM, SimVP, ViViT) that assume dense, grid-aligned sequences. The continuous variant further introduces explicit real-valued timestamp conditioning via Fourier features and FiLM modulation, which is absent from all prior 3D medical sequence models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRONOS establishes the first unified framework for continuous-time, multi-context, 3D medical sequence-to-image forecasting, addressing a gap where prior methods were limited to single-image inputs, fixed grids, or disease-specific priors. The paper highlights that the trivial Last Context Image baseline is surprisingly competitive (92–96% SSIM) due to slowly evolving anatomy, and that a two-stage latent diffusion model fails to beat LCI despite orders-of-magnitude higher compute — a useful negative result for the field. Open questions include the scarcity of diverse longitudinal 3D datasets, the misalignment between voxel-wise fidelity metrics and clinically relevant trajectory assessment, and the absence of spatio-temporal foundation models for medical imaging.
