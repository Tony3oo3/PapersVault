## Problem

Pre-trained chemical foundation models, when used as fixed features, do not significantly outperform classical hand-designed physicochemical descriptors on odorant-receptor binding prediction, and the extent of information overlap among these representations has not been systematically characterized. The paper asks whether fine-tuning these foundation models can produce olfaction-specific odorant representations that surpass both the original embeddings and hand-designed features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264331/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an odorant SMILES string (encoded by a chemical foundation model, e.g., ChemBERTa-77M-MTR) and a receptor amino acid sequence (encoded by a protein foundation model, e.g., ESM-2 650M) as input, and outputs a continuous affinity score (spikes/s for Hallem and Carey datasets) or a binary responsive/non-responsive label (M2OR). Beyond the scalar prediction, LORAX produces a learned joint odorant-receptor representation (the <cls> token) that can be used as a task-specific odorant embedding. The final prediction is a weighted ensemble of three XGBoost models consuming different combinations of the <cls> token and the original (non-fine-tuned) foundation model embeddings.

## Method

LORAX is a multi-modal transformer that applies LoRA (rank-8 adapters on Q, K, V matrices with α=8, dropout 0.1) to fine-tune only the chemical foundation model while keeping the protein foundation model frozen. A cross-attention block with two 8-headed attention layers allows the LoRA-adapted SMILES representation and the protein representation to attend to each other bidirectionally, followed by residual connections, layer normalization, and mean-pooling to produce a concatenated <cls> token fed through an MLP for affinity prediction. Training is two-stage: the transformer (including LoRA parameters) is first trained end-to-end, then frozen, and an ensemble of three XGBoost models is trained on the <cls> token, the original foundation model embeddings, and their concatenation, with ensemble weights determined by validation accuracy. The key empirical finding is that this fine-tuned representation is more dissimilar from physicochemical descriptors and more aligned with the neural response space than the original ChemBERTa embedding, explaining LORAX's improved generalization to unseen odorants and its superior MCC/F-score on the large M2OR dataset.

## Results

On the M2OR dataset (46,563 odorant-receptor pairs), LORAX (chemical-only fine-tuning) achieves MCC 0.651±0.04, F-score 0.730±0.03, and AveP 0.778±0.03, outperforming ProSmith (MCC 0.641, F-score 0.712) and Hladiš et al. (MCC 0.605, F-score 0.693). On the Carey dataset, LORAX achieves R² 0.712±0.032, comparable to ProSmith (R² 0.703±0.017, p=0.264), but generalizes substantially better to unseen odorants (avg R² 0.045 vs. −0.402 for ProSmith). In the feature-based benchmark (Section 3), chemical foundation models do not significantly outperform classical physicochemical descriptors (e.g., best foundation model ChemBERTa-77M-MTR: R² 0.704 vs. best descriptor RDKitDescriptors2D: R² 0.720 on ProSmith/Carey).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ProSmith (Kroll et al., 2024), LORAX (C) wins on MCC (0.651 vs. 0.641), F-score (0.730 vs. 0.712), AveP (0.778 vs. 0.584), and Recall (0.754 vs. 0.671), but loses on AUROC (83.24 vs. 90.47) and Precision (0.710 vs. 0.764); the authors argue MCC and F-score are more informative given the M2OR class imbalance. Against Hladiš et al. (2022), LORAX (C) achieves a statistically significant improvement in MCC (0.651 vs. 0.605, p<0.003) and F-score (0.730 vs. 0.693), with comparable AveP (0.778 vs. 0.780). In the feature-based benchmark on Carey, all chemical foundation models (transformer and GNN-based) perform equivalently to physicochemical descriptors like MordredDescriptors and RDKitDescriptors2D (ProSmith R² ≈ 0.70–0.72), demonstrating that self-supervised pre-training alone does not yield superior odorant representations without task-specific fine-tuning. LORAX also outperforms ProSmith on generalization to unseen odorants (avg R² 0.045 vs. −0.402), though the difference is not statistically significant (p=0.069).

## Relation to Prior Work

LORAX builds directly on two prior works: the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning framework (Hu et al., 2021) and the ProSmith multi-modal transformer + XGBoost ensemble architecture (Kroll et al., 2024). The key departure is that prior olfaction work either used hand-designed physicochemical descriptors, randomly initialized GNNs trained from scratch, or chemical foundation models as frozen features; this paper is the first to apply LoRA-based fine-tuning of chemical foundation models to odorant-receptor affinity prediction, and the first to systematically benchmark a broad set of chemical foundation models (transformers, GNNs) against physicochemical descriptors using statistical shape analysis (CCA, Procrustes) to diagnose why they underperform as fixed features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that chemical foundation models trained on hundreds of millions of molecules do not outperform simple physicochemical descriptors as frozen features for olfactory binding is a useful negative result that redirects the field toward fine-tuning rather than off-the-shelf embeddings. LORAX's modest but consistent improvements (especially on the larger M2OR dataset and in generalization to unseen odorants) suggest that parameter-efficient adaptation is a practical path for small-data olfactory tasks. Open questions include whether LORAX representations transfer across olfactory datasets, why simultaneous fine-tuning of both protein and chemical foundation models degrades performance, and whether the approach extends to percept-level prediction tasks where prior work found foundation models more useful.
