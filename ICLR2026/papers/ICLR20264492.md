## Problem

Tensor-based LLM compression methods (e.g., TensorLLM) apply Tucker decomposition to multi-head attention weights, but the resulting core tensor remains fully dense and grows polynomially with Tucker ranks, creating a 'dense core bottleneck' that caps achievable compression ratios. The paper asks how to eliminate redundancy within the compressed latent space itself to break this ceiling without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264492/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the four MHA weight matrices (W^Q, W^K, W^V, W^O) per attention head, tensorized into a 4th-order tensor W_total ∈ R^{d_model × d_head × 4 × h}, and produces a compressed representation of shared orthonormal factor matrices U^(1), U^(2), U^(3) plus an ultra-sparse core tensor G_sparse. At inference, token representations X ∈ R^{T×d_model} are projected once into the rank-R_1 subspace and contracted with precomputed per-head sparse slices, yielding the MHA output Z ∈ R^{T×d_model} directly in the compressed domain without ever materializing the original dense weight matrices.

## Method

LeSTD is a two-stage, data-free post-training framework. Stage I performs a shared-subspace Tucker decomposition via HOOI on the 4th-order MHA tensor, factorizing modes 1–3 (model dimension, head dimension, Q/K/V/O axis) while leaving the head mode unfactorized, yielding shared orthonormal factor matrices and per-head dense core slices. Stage II introduces a theoretically grounded iterative pruning mechanism: because the orthonormal basis tensors make the decomposition an orthogonal projection, zeroing any core coefficient g_β increases the Frobenius reconstruction error by exactly g_β², giving a closed-form importance score that reduces to magnitude-based hard-thresholding. After each pruning round, surviving coefficients are refit via a closed-form least-squares update (Eq. 6) to counteract numerical drift. Inference exploits the structure by performing a single shared left projection XU^(1) reused across all heads and projection types, contracting with sparse per-head slices M_{i,t}, and applying (U^(2))^T on the right, reducing both FLOPs and memory traffic relative to dense or per-head low-rank baselines.

## Results

On WikiText-2 perplexity (lower is better), LeSTD achieves 42.70 at 0.2 compression ratio on OPT-30B, compared to 50.25 for SVD-LLM (~15% lower) and 49.53 for TensorLLM. At 0.6 compression on Llama2-13B, LeSTD reaches 7.93 perplexity versus 9.96 for TensorLLM and 14.88 for SVD-LLM. On MathQA accuracy, LeSTD matches or exceeds the best baseline by ~3 points at matched compression levels. Inference throughput reaches up to ~40% improvement over TensorLLM in tokens/sec, with LeSTD achieving 12025.90 tokens/sec vs 8572.11 for TensorLLM on GPT-J at 0.6 compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LeSTD consistently outperforms all baselines at aggressive compression (ratio ≤ 0.4) across all four datasets and three model scales. Against SVD-LLM (matrix-by-matrix SVD), LeSTD reduces WikiText-2 perplexity by ~15% at 0.2 compression on OPT-30B (42.70 vs 50.25) and by ~47% at 0.6 on Llama2-13B (7.93 vs 14.88). Against TensorLLM (dense-core Tucker), LeSTD achieves ~10% lower perplexity at 0.2 on GPT-J (80.37 vs 89.52) and ~20% lower at 0.6 on Llama2-13B (7.93 vs 9.96), confirming that sparse core pruning breaks the dense-core bottleneck. Against ASVD, LeSTD is dramatically superior at aggressive compression (e.g., 42.70 vs 2423.65 on OPT-30B at 0.2), where ASVD's activation-aware scaling fails to maintain coherence. Against SliceGPT (structured pruning), LeSTD maintains stable performance where SliceGPT degrades sharply at low ratios. Against quantization baselines (BiLLM, PB-LLM), LeSTD trades slightly higher model size for substantially higher throughput (~1.3–2.2× more tokens/sec) at comparable perplexity, making it preferable for throughput-sensitive deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

LeSTD builds directly on TensorLLM's shared-subspace Tucker decomposition of multi-head attention, extending it with a second stage that sparsifies the core tensor via a closed-form importance criterion derived from the orthonormality of the Tucker basis. It departs from the broader post-training compression landscape (SVD-LLM, ASVD, SliceGPT) by operating in a joint tensor space across heads rather than per-matrix, and from TensorLLM specifically by addressing the dense-core bottleneck that caps achievable compression ratios. The theoretical contribution—a proof that magnitude-based hard-thresholding is optimal for best-k approximation under an orthonormal basis—provides a principled foundation for the pruning step that prior heuristic sparsification methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dense core bottleneck is a fundamental limitation of all Tucker-based LLM compression: as ranks grow to preserve accuracy, the core tensor's polynomial size becomes the new storage ceiling. LeSTD's two-stage design (learn shared basis, then sparsify core) breaks this ceiling in a data-free, post-training setting, enabling substantially higher compression ratios without calibration data or retraining. Key open questions include whether the unstructured sparsity in the core can be made hardware-friendly (e.g., block-sparsity for GPU kernels), whether the approach extends to FFN layers or cross-layer sharing, and whether the HOOI-based Stage I can be accelerated for even larger models. The method is currently scoped to MHA blocks only, leaving a significant portion of model parameters uncompressed.
