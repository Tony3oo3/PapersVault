## Problem

Despite a growing body of GNN unlearning methods, the field lacks a principled benchmark to answer two fundamental questions: whether any unlearning technique genuinely offers a practical speedup over retraining from scratch, and how practitioners should choose among methods for different workloads. Existing evaluations suffer from inconsistent metrics, omitted preprocessing costs, and unrealistic uniform-random deletion assumptions, making cross-method comparisons unreliable.

## Input / Output

Each unlearning algorithm receives the original trained GNN parameters Θ, the full graph structure (nodes, edges, features), and a deletion set ΔV_train specifying which training nodes to forget, and must produce updated parameters Θ̃ without retraining on the residual dataset. The benchmark evaluates Θ̃ against a gold-standard model Θ′ retrained from scratch on the modified data, measuring alignment at four hierarchical levels (accuracy, per-instance fidelity, logit-space L2 distance, and parameter-space L2 distance) and probing forgetting via three complementary attack families (membership inference, unlearning inversion, and noisy-labeler attacks). Deletion workloads range from 10% to 80% of training nodes and include targeted strategies (high-degree, label-skewed) beyond uniform random sampling.

## Method

The paper introduces the first holistic, multi-layered diagnostic benchmark for GNN unlearning, evaluating 12 state-of-the-art methods (MEGU, GIF, IDEA, GNNDELETE, PROJECTOR, GRAPHERASER, GUIDE, COGNAC, ETR, GST, SCALEGUN, CGU) across 7 datasets spanning homophilous and heterophilous graphs from 2.7K to 233K nodes. The framework is structured around three pillars: efficiency (total runtime including often-omitted preprocessing overheads, plus peak GPU memory, benchmarked explicitly against retraining), utility (four nested similarity metrics from aggregate accuracy down to parameter-space distance, revealing discrepancies invisible to accuracy alone), and forgetting (three attack families probing membership, structural, and label leakage). A key methodological contribution is the explicit accounting of preprocessing costs (adjacency conversion, k-hop neighborhood extraction, subgraph construction) that prior work omits, and the evaluation under non-uniform deletion distributions (degree-based, label-frequency-based) that expose vulnerabilities hidden by random removals. The benchmark also tests scalability on Reddit (233K nodes, 114M edges) and robustness across GNN backbones (GCN, GAT, GIN), yielding actionable decision guidance: MEGU/GIF/IDEA for small-medium graphs, ETR for large-scale settings where retraining is prohibitive.

## Results

No unlearning method satisfies all three pillars (efficiency, utility, forgetting) simultaneously. On small/medium graphs, MEGU, GIF, and IDEA closely match GOLD accuracy (e.g., MEGU 0.89 vs. GOLD 0.88 on CORA; MEGU 0.92 vs. GOLD 0.93 on PHOTO) with high fidelity (0.95, 0.99) and strong forgetting (inversion AUROC ≈ 0.53). On the largest dataset (Reddit, 233K nodes), only ETR (54.86 s) and COGNAC (197.43 s) complete within GPU memory, with ETR being the sole method faster than GOLD retraining (96.73 s). GNNDELETE degrades severely in both utility (0.76 accuracy on CORA vs. 0.88 GOLD) and forgetting (inversion AUROC 0.78–0.95), while GST OOMs on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEGU (Li et al., AAAI 2024) achieves the best overall utility among GCN-based methods, matching or exceeding GOLD accuracy on all six datasets (e.g., 0.89 vs. 0.88 on CORA, 0.81 vs. 0.73 on CITESEER) with the highest fidelity (0.95 on CORA) and strong forgetting (inversion AUROC 0.50–0.54), but its 100% preprocessing overhead makes it slower than GOLD on large graphs (20.49 s vs. 9.90 s on OGBN-Arxiv). GIF (Wu et al., WWW 2023) and IDEA (Dong et al., KDD 2024) are faster than GOLD on most datasets (e.g., 4.79 s vs. 9.90 s on Arxiv) and achieve comparable accuracy, though IDEA shows substantially higher logit distance (18.03 on CORA vs. MEGU's 3.05), indicating different confidence distributions despite similar predictions. ETR (Yang et al., AAAI 2025) is the only method that both scales to Reddit and beats GOLD in runtime (54.86 s vs. 96.73 s), but it sacrifices fidelity on heterophilous graphs (0.30 on ROMAN-E vs. GOLD 0.41). GNNDELETE (Cheng et al., ICLR 2023) is the weakest performer, dropping to 0.76 accuracy on CORA (vs. 0.88 GOLD) and leaking membership information across all three attack families (inversion AUROC up to 0.95). PROJECTOR (Cong & Mahdavi, AISTATS 2023) is the fastest unlearning step but operates on a custom linear GNN that is ~100× slower to train than GCN (33 s vs. 0.48 s on CORA), and it leaks membership (AUROC 0.57–0.60). GRAPHERASER (Chen et al., CCS 2022) and GUIDE (Wang et al., USENIX Sec. 2023) are the slowest due to shard-based preprocessing (77–919 s on small graphs), and GUIDE degrades sharply on heterophilous datasets (0.23 on AMAZON-R vs. 0.43 GOLD). COGNAC (Kolipaka et al., 2024) scales to Reddit but shows hints of topological leakage in inversion attacks (AUROC 0.54–0.58). SCALEGUN (Yi & Wei, ICLR 2025) and CGU (Chien et al., 2022) are restricted to binary linear GNNs; SCALEGUN collapses on heterophilous datasets (20.6% accuracy on AMAZON-R vs. 31.7% GOLD) while CGU remains stable but is not directly comparable due to one-vs-rest reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not build on a single prior method but rather positions itself as the first holistic benchmark for the GNN unlearning landscape, which has accumulated a diverse set of techniques since 2022 (learning-based like MEGU and GNNDELETE, influence-function-based like GIF and IDEA, SISA-style like GRAPHERASER and GUIDE, projection-based like PROJECTOR, and certified methods like SCALEGUN and CGU). It explicitly differentiates from the concurrent OpenGU benchmark (Fan et al., 2025) by adding multi-level utility diagnostics (fidelity, logit distance, parameter distance), a broader suite of forgetting attacks (membership inference, inversion, noisy-labeler), and explicit efficiency comparisons against retraining that include often-omitted preprocessing costs. The paper repurposes the standard node-classification GNN evaluation setup but reframes it around the unlearning-specific question of when an unlearned model is a faithful and efficient substitute for retraining from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that most existing GNN unlearning methods are not yet practical for large-scale graphs, with retraining-from-scratch remaining the most compelling option in many settings—constitutes an important negative result that should redirect research priorities away from theoretical guarantees under narrow assumptions (linear GNNs, binary classification) and toward scalable, batchable implementations. The multi-level evaluation reveals that aggregate accuracy alone is a misleading proxy for unlearning quality: methods like IDEA can match accuracy while exhibiting large logit-space divergence, and GNNDELETE shows severe forgetting failures invisible to accuracy metrics. Open questions include whether any unlearning method can match retraining efficiency on graphs with 100M+ edges, how to handle non-uniform and adversarial deletion distributions at scale, and whether the current influence-function and learning-based paradigms can be made batchable without sacrificing fidelity.
