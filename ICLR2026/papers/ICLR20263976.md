## Problem

During RL training of LLMs with policy-gradient algorithms like GRPO, low-probability tokens generate disproportionately large gradient magnitudes compared to high-probability tokens, causing them to dominate model updates and suppress the gradients of high-probability tokens that are critical for performance. This gradient-disproportionality issue, which is orthogonal to previously identified biases (e.g., response-length bias), has not been explicitly studied or addressed in prior work.

## Input / Output

The method operates within the standard autoregressive LLM generation loop: given a text prompt (a logic puzzle or math problem) and previously generated tokens, the model samples the next token from its vocabulary distribution. The proposed modifications act at the training level rather than the inference level—specifically, they reweight per-token advantages and partition tokens into update groups based on their sampled probabilities before each gradient step. The output remains a full text response with chain-of-thought reasoning enclosed in think tags and a final answer in answer tags (for logic puzzles) or a boxed answer (for math).

## Method

The paper first provides a theoretical bound (Proposition 4.2) showing that for any intermediate layer activation, the gradient norm induced by a single token is proportional to (1 − π), where π is that token's probability, explaining why low-probability tokens dominate updates. To mitigate this, two complementary methods are proposed: (1) Advantage Reweighting, which multiplies each token's advantage by [α·π(o_{i,t}) + (1−α)] to linearly reduce the update weight of low-probability tokens at negligible computational cost; and (2) Lopti (Low-Probability Token Isolation), which splits tokens in a mini-batch into low-probability (π ≤ η) and high-probability (π > η) groups and performs two sequential update passes—low-probability tokens first, then high-probability tokens—so that the indirect probability shifts from the first pass modulate the gradient magnitudes in the second pass. The two methods can be applied independently or jointly, and the approach generalizes beyond GRPO to other policy-gradient algorithms such as REINFORCE++ and DAPO.

## Results

On the K&K Logic Puzzle benchmark (the paper's primary evaluation), the combined Advantage Reweighting + Lopti method improves GRPO-trained Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct from 0.39 to 0.57 average accuracy (↑46.2%), and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M from 0.77 to 0.91 (↑18.2%). On math benchmarks (DSR-Uniform and ORZ with Qwen2.5-7B), Advantage Reweighting alone yields the best gains, improving average accuracy from 38.98% to 40.01% (DSR) and 39.83% to 41.09% (ORZ). The methods also transfer to other policy-gradient algorithms: on REINFORCE++ (3B model), the combined approach achieves 0.41 vs. 0.23 baseline (↑76.5%), and on DAPO (3B model), it reaches 0.74 vs. 0.49 (↑50.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is naive GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), which the proposed methods consistently outperform across all model sizes and datasets: on K&K Logic Puzzle, the combined method beats GRPO by 46.2% (3B) and 18.2% (7B); on math (DSR-Uniform), Advantage Reweighting alone improves GRPO from 38.98% to 40.01% average. Against REINFORCE++ (Hu, 2025), the methods yield even larger relative gains (up to 76.5% for 3B on K&K), confirming the gradient-disproportionality issue is algorithm-agnostic. Against DAPO (Yu et al., 2025), which already incorporates Clip Higher and Dynamic Sampling, the combined method still adds 50.6% (3B) and 17.2% (7B) on K&K, indicating the token-probability bias is orthogonal to DAPO's stabilization techniques. As reference models, the 7B+Lopti+Reweight result (0.91) surpasses DeepSeek-R1 (0.78) and o1 (0.38) on K&K, while the 3B model (0.57) still trails DeepSeek-R1 but exceeds GPT-4o (0.36) and o1 (0.38). On math benchmarks, gains over GRPO are more modest (1–2% absolute), and the combined method does not further improve over individual methods, suggesting the low-probability token dominance is less pronounced in math due to lower generation entropy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO algorithm introduced by DeepSeek-R1 and the broader policy-gradient RL training pipeline for LLMs (PPO, DAPO, REINFORCE++). It identifies a gradient-level bias—low-probability tokens producing disproportionately large gradients proportional to (1−π)—that is orthogonal to previously documented update biases such as response-length bias (Yu et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025) and the bias toward entirely incorrect responses on difficult prompts (Xiong et al., 2025). The authors position their work as the first to address RL training from this gradient-disproportionality perspective, complementing prior improvements that focused on sample quality, length balancing, and entropy collapse prevention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical bound (Proposition 4.2) showing gradient norms scale with (1−π) provides a principled explanation for a previously unexplained training pathology, and the two proposed fixes (Advantage Reweighting at near-zero cost, Lopti at ~2× training time) are simple enough to drop into any policy-gradient RL pipeline. However, practical impact is uneven: gains are substantial on logic-puzzle reasoning (up to 46.2%) but modest on math benchmarks (1–2%), and the two methods do not combine well for math, suggesting the low-probability dominance effect is task-dependent. Open questions include whether the effect persists at larger model scales, whether optimal hyperparameters (α, η) can be made task-agnostic, and whether analogous gradient-disproportionality issues exist in other RL paradigms beyond policy-gradient methods.
