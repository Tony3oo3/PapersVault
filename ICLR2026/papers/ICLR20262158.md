## Problem

Standard transformer architectures fail at compositional generalization — solving novel problems by recombining primitives learned during training — particularly in extreme out-of-distribution regimes. While test-time adaptation, meta-learning, and abstract reasoning have each been studied in isolation, no prior method jointly addresses all three aspects to enable systematic knowledge recombination on unseen compositions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262158/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a problem specification of n few-shot input/output pairs (pixel sequences for 1-D ARC, K-dimensional feature vectors for SRAVEN, or lexical symbol sequences for grammar induction) plus a held-out test query, and autoregressively generates the predicted output sequence. The encoder's latent representation is not a fixed-size vector but a nonparametric Dirichlet Process mixture distribution whose effective number of components scales with input complexity, and the test query is decoded only after the latent hypothesis has been refined via test-time gradient descent against the few-shot examples.

## Method

Abduction Transformer replaces the standard fixed-size latent vector with a nonparametric Dirichlet Process: the transformer encoder's set-of-vector output is projected to DP parameters (per-component means, variances, and concentration weights), and a discrete mixture is sampled from this DP to form the latent hypothesis. The decoder cross-attends to this mixture via denoising attention (a generalization of standard attention to probability distributions over vectors). Training minimizes variational free energy, where the KL-divergence between the posterior and prior DPs acts as an information-theoretic regularizer that encourages sparse mixture weights and a smooth, searchable latent geometry. At test time, the initial hypothesis from the encoder is refined by gradient descent on the negative log-likelihood of the few-shot outputs with respect to the latent representation, effectively performing iterative hypothesis testing before decoding the test query. This combination of nonparametric variable-cardinality representations, stochastic training-time sampling, and test-time gradient search enables the model to discover minimal latent explanations for novel compositions of training-time primitives.

## Results

On the 1-D ARC OOD composition task, Abduction Transformer achieves a 25.1% perfect solve rate, substantially outperforming LPN (1.9%), the decoder-only transformer baseline (5.2%), and the encoder-decoder baseline (0.1%), while remaining competitive with GPT-5 Thinking (29.0%) using only ~1.2M parameters. On SRAVEN trained on just 1% of rule combinations, Abduction Transformer reaches 46.1% solve rate versus 37.1% for LPN, 28.8% for the decoder-only baseline, and 41.0% for GPT-5 Thinking. On the linguistic systematicity task, Abduction Transformer maintains near-perfect accuracy down to 10 few-shot examples and retains 50% accuracy at 5 examples, whereas the encoder-decoder baseline degrades continuously as examples are removed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LPN (Macfarlane & Bonnet, 2025), Abduction Transformer wins on every task: 25.1% vs. 1.9% on 1-D ARC, 46.1% vs. 37.1% on SRAVEN (1% training), and 96.4% vs. 93.5% on SRAVEN (90% training), demonstrating the advantage of nonparametric (variable-cardinality) latent representations over LPN's single-vector latent space. Against GPT-5 Thinking (zero-shot, no fine-tuning), Abduction Transformer is slightly behind on 1-D ARC (25.1% vs. 29.0%) but ahead on SRAVEN with 1% training data (46.1% vs. 41.0%), all while using only ~1.2M parameters. Against GPT-4.1, the gap is larger: 25.1% vs. 11.0% on 1-D ARC and 46.1% vs. 34.0% on SRAVEN (1%). Against the decoder-only transformer baseline, Abduction Transformer wins decisively on 1-D ARC (25.1% vs. 5.2%) and SRAVEN 1% (46.1% vs. 28.8%), but performs comparably on SRAVEN 90% (96.4% vs. 95.3%), indicating the baseline can handle moderate OOD but fails in extreme OOD regimes. On the linguistic systematicity task, Abduction Transformer outperforms the encoder-decoder baseline from Lake & Baroni (2023) across all few-shot settings, maintaining near-perfect accuracy down to 10 examples where the baseline already shows significant degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Abduction Transformer synthesizes three previously separate ideas into a single architecture: the nonparametric Dirichlet Process latent space and denoising attention from Henderson & Fehr (2023), the test-time gradient search over latent representations from Macfarlane & Bonnet's Latent Program Network (2025), and the variational free energy training objective from VAEs (Kingma et al., 2013). The key departure from LPN is replacing its fixed-size vector latent space with variable-cardinality mixture distributions, which the authors argue is necessary for modeling hypotheses of varying compositional complexity. The departure from standard VAEs is the shift from a single latent vector to a set-of-vector (nonparametric) representation that naturally aligns with the transformer's variable-length output, making the architecture a 'natural generalization of VAEs from the vector space regime to the set-of-vector space regime.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-ablated demonstration that compositional generalization in small transformer models is achievable through the specific combination of nonparametric latent representations, information-theoretic regularization, and test-time gradient search — a recipe that could inform future architectures for systematic reasoning. However, the evaluation is confined to relatively small-scale, procedurally generated tasks (1-D ARC, symbolic Raven's, grammar induction) with modest absolute performance (25.1% on 1-D ARC), leaving open whether the approach scales to full 2D ARC-AGI problems or more complex, open-ended reasoning. The clean ablation showing that stochasticity in the encoder is essential for a searchable latent space (deterministic encoder drops to 0.1%) is a useful negative result that constrains future design choices.
