## Problem

Instance-only dependent label noise (IDN), where the flip probability depends only on the instance X and not on the true label Y, is a more natural and parameter-efficient noise model than instance-and-label dependent noise (ILDN), yet it has been almost entirely unexplored outside of linear classifiers. This paper resurfaces the IDN model and provides a general loss correction mechanism that achieves exact risk equivalence, making it applicable to any learning machine and any classification calibrated base loss.

## Input / Output

The method consumes feature vectors of arbitrary modality (32×32 RGB images, audio time-series, or tabular feature vectors) paired with noisy binary labels during training, and outputs a binary class score. A key design choice is that the per-instance flip probability ρ_x is a single scalar rather than a 2×2 transition matrix, and it is estimated online from the model's own output |h(x)| as a proxy for distance to the decision boundary, requiring no separate noise-estimation network or anchor points. The method is machine-agnostic: the same corrected loss is applied whether the underlying scorer is a CNN, an MLP, or a LightGBM gradient-boosted tree (where gradient and Hessian values are passed to the tree learner).

## Method

The core contribution is a risk-equivalence-based loss correction (Proposition 1) that constructs a modified loss ℓ̃ from any classification calibrated base loss ℓ such that minimizing the empirical noisy risk w.r.t. ℓ̃ is equivalent to minimizing the true clean risk w.r.t. ℓ. The correction involves three quantities: P(Y|x) and P(Ỹ|x) (both approximated by the sigmoid of the model's output) and the instance-only flip rate ρ_x, which is modeled as a β-logistic function of |h(x)| (the model's confidence as a proxy for distance to the decision boundary). To handle numerical instability from the division in the loss formula, a regularized variant ℓ̃^R replaces the division with a subtraction weighted by a hyperparameter λ, and a Rademacher-complexity-based generalization bound (Proposition 2) guarantees that the regularized loss still connects noisy empirical risk to clean true risk under sufficient conditions on λ. Training uses a short warm-up period with the unmodified base loss to establish a reasonable decision boundary before the corrected loss activates, and the entire pipeline is dataset- and machine-agnostic.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 binary sub-datasets with 28% instance-dependent label noise, NDX achieves 83.43% (0v1), 72.38% (2v3), 76.77% (4v5), 83.27% (6v7), and 82.98% (8v9) test accuracy, consistently outperforming or matching all 11 baselines. Under high 44% noise, NDX retains 77.33% (0v1) and 70.23% (6v7), while several baselines (e.g., Forward, GCE) drop to near-random (50–53%). On the real-world Clothing1M dataset, NDX achieves 78.10–81.57% accuracy across 10 binary sub-datasets, matching or exceeding the best baselines (APL, Coteaching+). On tabular data with LightGBM, NDX(GBM) reaches 73.77% on Heart at 44% noise, more than 10 points above the runner-up.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NDX outperforms all 11 baselines on CIFAR-10 at 28% noise, with the largest margins over Forward (83.43 vs. 73.00 on 0v1) and BCN (83.43 vs. 56.82). At 44% noise, NDX (77.33% on 0v1) beats the next-best APL (70.47%) by ~7 points, while BLTM collapses to 33% and GCE/Forward drop to ~53%. On the Speakers audio dataset, NDX is consistently top-2 across all 10 sub-experiments (e.g., 83.77% vs. PTD's 83.65% on 0v1 at 28%; 72.16% vs. Peer's 72.16% on 0v1 at 44%). On tabular data, NDX(GBM) with LightGBM achieves 73.77% on Heart at 44% noise, exceeding the runner-up UB (60.66%) by over 12 points, demonstrating the machine-agnostic nature of the loss correction. On the real-world Clothing1M dataset, NDX (78.10–81.57%) matches or slightly exceeds APL (76.06–80.37%) and Coteaching+ (66.54–82.83%), with the closest competition on 1v6 where Coteaching+ edges ahead (82.83 vs. 81.57). Overall, NDX is the most stable method across noise levels and domains, with the smallest performance degradation from 28% to 44% noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the instance-only dependent (IDN) label noise model, previously explored only for linear machines by Bylander (1998) and Du & Cai (2015), to arbitrary learning machines via a risk-equivalence-based loss correction. It extends the CCN-based loss correction lineage of Natarajan et al. (2013) and Patrini et al. (2017) by replacing the class-conditional transition matrix with a single per-instance scalar, while fixing the risk-equivalence gap in Natarajan et al. and removing the anchor-point requirement of Patrini et al. The broader loss-correction framework (Zhang & Sabuncu 2018, Ma et al. 2020, Liu & Guo 2020) provides the template, but those methods assume CCN or RCN; this paper is the first to combine proper risk equivalence with an instance-only dependent noise model in a machine-agnostic setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that the IDN model requires estimating a single scalar per instance rather than a full transition matrix, making it far more scalable and flexible in how the noise rate is approximated (distance-to-boundary, clustering, ensemble vote proximity, etc.), while empirically matching or exceeding ILDN-based methods. The machine-agnosticism is validated across neural networks and LightGBM, broadening applicability beyond the deep-learning-centric label-noise literature. Limitations include restriction to binary classification, the need for a warm-up period, and the fact that the regularized loss (needed for numerical stability) slightly breaks exact risk equivalence. The natural next step, acknowledged by the authors, is multi-class generalization of the instance-only dependent model.
