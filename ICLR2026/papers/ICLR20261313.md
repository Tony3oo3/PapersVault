## Problem

Large Reasoning Models generate verbose Chain-of-Thought trajectories with substantial redundancy, inflating inference cost without proportional accuracy gains. While prior compression work operates at the token level, in latent space, or via length penalties, no principled, information-theoretic criterion exists for identifying which entire reasoning steps are semantically redundant and safe to remove.

## Input / Output

For static pruning, the method takes a full CoT trajectory (segmented into steps by \n\n delimiters) generated by an LRM on a mathematical problem, computes per-step entropy, and outputs a compressed CoT where the lowest-entropy steps are each replaced by a single [SKIP] token; this compressed sequence is then concatenated with the original query to elicit only the final answer. For the trained model, the input is simply the problem prompt, and the model autonomously generates a compressed CoT interleaving reasoning steps with [SKIP] tokens followed by the final answer, with no external entropy computation at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is step entropy — the sum of token-level Shannon entropies within a reasoning step — which the paper proves (via Lemma 1 and Theorem 1) to upper-bound the conditional mutual information between that step and the final answer given all other steps, establishing low-entropy steps as informationally redundant. Static pruning ranks steps by length-normalized entropy and replaces the bottom 80% with [SKIP] tokens, achieving 16–55% token reduction with negligible accuracy loss across DeepSeek-R1 (7B/14B), Qwen3-8B, and QwQ-32B. To enable autonomous compression at inference, a two-stage training pipeline is used: SFT on 70k entropy-compressed (problem, CoT, answer) triples teaches the model to emit [SKIP] tokens, followed by GRPO reinforcement learning with a four-component reward (correctness, skip-ratio tier, skip-count penalty, response-length penalty) that optimizes the accuracy–efficiency trade-off beyond static imitation, yielding up to 57% token reduction on AIME 2024 while matching or exceeding baseline accuracy.

## Results

On Math500, the trained model (SFT+GRPO) achieves 85.00% accuracy (↓3.2% vs. full CoT baseline of 88.17%) while reducing thinking tokens by 35.0% (3703.83 → 2439.42). On AIME 2024, it reaches 57.14% accuracy (↓6.2%) with a 57.0% token reduction (15843.43 → 6839.37). Static 80% low-entropy pruning alone achieves 16–45% token reductions across GSM8k, Math500, AIME 2024, and AIME 2025 on DeepSeek-R1-7B/14B and Qwen3-8B while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy (e.g., GSM8k: 78.54% → 80.82% on 7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its SFT+GRPO trained model against five baselines: two zero-shot prompting methods (Token-efficient Prompts, LC-Prompt), and three trained compression methods (CoT-Valve, TokenSkip, R1-Compress). On Math500, the proposed method matches R1-Compress on accuracy (both ↓3.2%) while nearly doubling the token reduction (↓35.0% vs. ↓20.3%). On AIME 2024, it again matches R1-Compress on accuracy (↓6.2%) but achieves over four times the token savings (↓57.0% vs. ↓12.9%). CoT-Valve achieves comparable token reduction on Math500 (↓48.4%) but at a much steeper accuracy cost (↓10.6% vs. ↓3.2%). TokenSkip and Token-efficient Prompts offer only modest compression (↓11.1% tokens) with larger accuracy drops. LC-Prompt preserves accuracy best (↓1.6%) but provides minimal compression (↓9.8%), making it unsuitable for efficiency-focused deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of CoT compression and information-theoretic analysis, departing from prior work that operates at the token level (TokenSkip, LC-Prompt) or chunk level (R1-Compress) by introducing step-level pruning guided by a principled entropy metric. It builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath and the step-skipping intuition from Liu et al. (2024), but adds a theoretical grounding (Lemma 1, Theorem 1) linking step entropy to conditional mutual information with the final answer, which no prior compression method provides. Unlike latent reasoning approaches (iCoT, COCONUT) that sacrifice interpretability, this work preserves explicit CoT while compressing it, and unlike length-constrained tuning (CoT-Valve), it uses an explicit information-theoretic signal rather than a generic length penalty to decide what to discard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 80% of low-entropy steps can be pruned with minimal accuracy loss across four model families (7B–32B) and multiple benchmarks is practically significant for reducing inference costs of Large Reasoning Models at scale. The two-stage SFT+GRPO training that enables autonomous compression (achieving up to 57% token reduction on AIME 2024 while improving accuracy) offers a deployable path beyond static post-hoc pruning. Key limitations include reliance on \n\n delimiters for step segmentation, validation primarily on mathematical reasoning, and a fixed 80% threshold that may not generalize to all domains or model architectures. Open questions include adaptive, domain-aware compression ratios, robustness to alternative step boundaries, and extension to multimodal or open-ended reasoning tasks.
