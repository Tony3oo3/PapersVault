## Problem

Current AI systems are either disembodied 'talkers' (full-duplex speech LLMs that listen and speak but cannot act) or non-conversant 'doers' (VLA models that manipulate objects but are deaf, mute, and locked into rigid turn-based text instructions). No single end-to-end architecture has simultaneously perceived and generated across vision, speech, text, and action in a streaming full-duplex manner, leaving natural interaction patterns like speaking-while-acting, action barge-ins, and context-grounded VQA during manipulation unattainable.

## Input / Output

ELLSA consumes 1-second streaming blocks containing 5 speech embeddings (downsampled from 25 Hz Mamba encoder output) and one or two video frames (front view plus gripper view), arranged in a fixed interleaved order: speech → image → text → action. It produces 8 text tokens (or a single <silence> token) per block, which are converted to 25 speech codec frames by a CosyVoice2-0.5B synthesizer, alongside one second of FAST-tokenized robot actions. The model operates in two modes: a default mode activating all four modalities and a speech-only mode that suppresses visual input and emits dummy actions.

## Method

The core contribution is SA-MoE (Self-Attention Mixture-of-Experts), which routes each modality to a specialized expert—a Speech Expert (Mamba streaming encoder + LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct) for speech/text and an Action Expert (Emu3-Base with FAST action tokens) for vision/action—while fusing them through a unified self-attention backbone where both experts share identical 32-layer, 4096-hidden-size configurations so that cross-expert attention occurs naturally at every layer. At any single step only one expert's weights are activated, but all experts share a unified KV cache, so each expert can attend to information produced by the other, enabling cross-modal understanding without modality interference. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) pretraining individual experts on ASR/speech-QA and text-conditioned manipulation, (2) fine-tuning both experts with LoRA (rank 256) within the SA-MoE framework on a diverse mixture of basic and advanced tasks (speaking-while-acting, defective instruction rejection, action barge-ins, context-grounded VQA), and (3) attaching a streaming CosyVoice2-0.5B speech synthesizer via a randomly initialized MLP connector. The full-duplex streaming behavior emerges from the interleaved temporal sequence design rather than auxiliary state machines, allowing the model to autonomously decide when to speak, act, or remain silent within each 1-second block.

## Results

On the LIBERO robot manipulation benchmark, ELLSA achieves the highest average success rate of 89.4% (90.8% SPATIAL, 95.8% OBJECT, 86.4% GOAL, 84.4% LONG), surpassing the previous best π0-FAST (85.5%) by 3.9 points, with a particularly large margin on the LONG suite (84.4% vs. 60.2%). On speech interaction, ELLSA delivers the best speech-to-speech (S2S) performance among full-duplex models, reaching 70.0% on Llama Questions, 41.7% on TriviaQA, and 2.80 GPTScore on AlpacaEval. For full-duplex capabilities, ELLSA achieves 100% dialogue turn-taking success, 96.4–100% action turn-taking, and 94.3% action barge-in success, while context-grounded VQA reaches ~82.5% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LIBERO, ELLSA outperforms all seven VLA baselines in average success rate, with the largest gains over DP* (+17.0 pts), Octo (+14.3 pts), and OpenVLA (+12.9 pts); against the strongest baseline π0-FAST, ELLSA trades small losses on SPATIAL (−5.6), OBJECT (−1.0), and GOAL (−2.2) for a decisive +24.2-point win on the harder LONG suite, yielding a +3.9-point average advantage. On speech interaction (Table 1), ELLSA dominates Moshi on every S2S metric (e.g., 70.0 vs. 54.5 on Llama Q., 41.7 vs. 16.7 on TriviaQA) and also beats Freeze-Omni on S2S across all four benchmarks, though Freeze-Omni retains a slight S2T edge on AlpacaEval (3.90 vs. 3.09). On the CALVIN benchmark, ELLSA achieves an average of 4.43 consecutively completed tasks, surpassing MCIL (0.40), RT-1 (2.45), Robo-Flamingo (4.09), and GR-1 (4.21), while trailing RoboVLMs (4.49) by a narrow 0.06 and edging out UP-VLA (4.42) by 0.01. Notably, ELLSA is evaluated under a more challenging speech-conditioned, full-duplex protocol (deciding when to act, processing raw audio) compared to the text-conditioned, turn-based settings of all VLA baselines, making its performance gains more significant than the raw numbers suggest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: full-duplex speech interaction models (Moshi, Freeze-Omni, SALMONN-omni) that can listen and speak but cannot act, and Vision-Language-Action models (π0, OpenVLA, UniVLA) that can manipulate objects but are 'deaf and mute.' The core architectural contribution, SA-MoE, extends the attention-based expert connection introduced in π0 (where a VLM backbone and action expert interact via shared attention) to a general multi-expert, interleaved multimodal sequence setting. By routing speech/text to a speech expert (Mamba encoder + LLaMA-3.1-8B) and vision/action to an action expert (Emu3-Base + FAST tokenizer), then fusing them through a unified self-attention backbone, the paper departs from both the single-dense-model approach and the rigid turn-based VLA paradigm, enabling concurrent multimodal input and output within a single streaming architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ELLSA establishes a concrete architectural template (SA-MoE) for integrating pretrained modality-specific experts while mitigating modality interference, a problem that has hindered unified multimodal models. The demonstration of previously unattainable behaviors—speaking-while-acting, action barge-in, and context-grounded VQA during manipulation—shows that full-duplex MIMO interaction is not merely a latency improvement but unlocks qualitatively new interaction patterns. However, all validation is in simulation (LIBERO, CALVIN), concurrent-task performance drops substantially (e.g., LIBERO LONG drops from 84.4% to 73.2%), and the duplex dynamics remain limited (no backchanneling, only a fixed set of barge-in commands). Open questions include whether the framework transfers to real-world deployment, whether the 1-second time block is optimal for natural interaction, and whether SA-MoE scales gracefully as more modalities (touch, proprioception) are added.
