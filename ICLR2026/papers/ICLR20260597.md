## Problem

Existing face forgery detection methods relying on spatial, frequency, or pre-trained features show declining efficacy against high-quality, entirely synthesized forgeries from diffusion models, where texture inconsistencies become increasingly subtle. The paper identifies that the specular reflection component in the Phong illumination model, governed by six estimated parameters and a strongly nonlinear exponential formulation, is inherently more difficult for generative models to replicate accurately, yet remains underexplored as a detection cue.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260597/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 256x256 RGB face images (or uniformly sampled video frames, 32 per sequence) and first decomposes them via 3DDFA shape estimation, Multi-Scale Retinex texture extraction (sigma = [15, 80, 120]), and spherical harmonic illumination fitting before any network inference. Beyond the final real/fake score, the method produces physically interpretable intermediate maps: a specular reflection map, a log-domain Retinex texture map, and a direct light map, all flattened into UV space for the network. Video-level scores are obtained by averaging frame-level predictions without explicit temporal modeling.

## Method

SRI-Net replaces the BFM PCA texture model with a Multi-Scale Retinex-based texture estimator, reducing per-image decomposition time from 0.78 s to 0.29 s while capturing fine-grained identity details that the linear PCA basis misses. Specular reflection is then extracted residually: the first nine spherical harmonic bases fit ambient and direct light under the Retinex texture constraint, and the remaining residual divided by the texture yields the specular reflection map. A two-stage cross-attention mechanism operates on UV-flattened feature maps, first capturing texture and direct-light correlations, then capturing specular-reflection correlations with the combined texture and direct-light representation, before fusing these physics-grounded features with an image-branch feature vector from an Xception backbone for the final binary decision. Shape normalization in UV space standardizes illumination directions, surface normals, and viewing angles to make the cross-attention correlations physically meaningful across identities and poses.

## Results

On traditional deepfake datasets (Table 1), SRI-Net achieves 91.3% / 87.5% / 89.3% frame-level AUC on CelebDF-v1 / CelebDF-v2 / DFD, yielding an average of 89.4%, surpassing the previous best (ProDet, 86.6% avg) by 2.8 points. On the generative DiFF dataset (Table 4), SRI-Net reaches 86.8% average AUC across T2I, I2I, FS, and FE subsets, outperforming FIA-USA (83.3%) by 3.5 points, with the largest gain on Face Editing (+8.1 points). On DF40 (Table 3), SRI-Net achieves 90.9% average AUC versus 87.8% for FIA-USA. At the video level (Table 2), SRI-Net attains 95.5% on CelebDF-v2 and 93.1% on DFD, again setting new state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRI-Net outperforms all 20 compared baselines across every dataset and evaluation protocol. On traditional datasets, it leads ProDet (86.6% avg) by 2.8 points and FIA-USA (86.3%) by 3.1 points in average frame-level AUC, with the largest single-dataset margin on DFD (+4.5 over FIA-USA). On the DF40 face-swapping benchmark, SRI-Net (90.9% avg) surpasses FIA-USA (87.8%) by 3.1 points, with particularly strong gains on e4s (+1.9) and facedancer (+12.3). On the diffusion-generated DiFF dataset, SRI-Net (86.8% avg) beats FIA-USA (83.3%) by 3.5 points, with the most dramatic improvement on Face Editing (80.8% vs. 72.7%, +8.1 points), where texture-based methods degrade most severely. At the video level, SRI-Net (95.5% CDF-v2, 93.1% DFD) exceeds FIA-USA (94.1% CDF-v2) and SBI (90.6% CDF-v2, 88.2% DFD). The consistent advantage over frequency-domain methods (F3Net, SPSL, SRM) and spatial-domain methods (Face X-ray, RECCE) underscores that specular-reflection inconsistency provides a more generalizable forgery signal than spatial or frequency artifacts, especially against diffusion-generated forgeries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of illumination-based deepfake detection (Peng et al. 2016; Zhu et al. 2021) from the Lambertian assumption to the full Phong illumination model, arguing that the specular reflection component—governed by more parameters and stronger nonlinearity—contains more generalizable forgery evidence than the diffuse components previously exploited. It departs from prior 3DMM-based pipelines that rely on the Basel Face Model's PCA texture basis by substituting a Multi-Scale Retinex texture estimate, which both improves accuracy and eliminates the need for iterative texture coefficient optimization. The SRI-Net architecture with two-stage cross-attention is novel in explicitly modeling the physical correlations among specular reflection, texture, and direct light, rather than treating illumination cues as a single monolithic feature as in earlier 3D-decomposition detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As diffusion models produce increasingly photorealistic forgeries that defeat spatial- and frequency-domain detectors, a physics-grounded detection signal that is harder for generative models to replicate is timely and practically important. The paper's main limitation is its dependence on accurate 3D shape fitting (via 3DDFA), which degrades under extreme poses and self-occlusion, and the multi-stage pipeline (3D reconstruction + Retinex + spherical harmonics + dual-branch network) is substantially more computationally expensive than a single CNN. Open questions include whether the specular-reflection-inconsistency signal can be extended to video-level temporal analysis, whether it generalizes to non-frontal or heavily occluded faces without the proposed 3D shape correction, and whether the approach transfers to other body parts or object categories governed by similar illumination physics.
