## Problem

Prior methods that incorporate training metrics (e.g., entropy, response length) into RLVR rely on hand-crafted directional priors—assuming a metric should be higher-is-better or lower-is-better—through reward or advantage shaping. Without careful hyperparameter tuning, these fixed directional biases can be overly aggressive and fail to improve performance robustly across different task regimes. The paper asks how to amplify the influence of a target metric on advantage estimation without presupposing which direction is beneficial.

## Input / Output

Given a text query and a group of G=16 sampled responses from the current policy, CANON sorts the responses by a chosen metric (per-token generation entropy or response length) and splits them into two equal-sized subgroups. The method outputs a modified advantage signal per token that is fed into the standard PPO/GRPO policy update. A tunable coefficient α further allows asymmetric weighting of one subgroup (e.g., down-weighting longer responses) to steer the model toward more token-efficient reasoning without altering the intra-group comparison.

## Method

CANON performs conditional regrouping: it sorts all sampled responses by a target metric and splits them into two equal-sized groups C⁺ and C⁻. It then computes an inter-group advantage (each response compared against the mean reward of the *other* group) that reveals which metric trend correlates with higher accuracy, and an intra-group advantage (each response compared against the mean reward of its *own* group) that selects superior responses within the same trend. The final advantage is a convex combination controlled by μ, and the paper proves that when groups are equal-sized, DR.GRPO reduces to the special case μ=0.5. A second theorem shows that CANON selectively amplifies only the advantage attributable to the grouping metric, leaving the influence of independent factors unchanged. In practice, scheduling μ over training (e.g., cosine annealing from inter-group-dominant to intra-group-dominant) balances exploitation on math tasks with exploration on high-complexity logic tasks, while setting α<1 on the length-based variant yields a superior Pareto frontier in the performance–token-cost trade-off.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, CANON-Inter (entropy-based) achieves 57.6 average accuracy across six math benchmarks, a 1.9-point gain over DR.GRPO (55.7), with the largest margin on AIME 24 (+5.0 points). For high-complexity logic reasoning (ZebraLogic), CANON-Intra (entropy-based) reaches 29.1 average accuracy vs. DR.GRPO's 26.2, with a 5.2-point improvement on the most challenging XLarge subset. CANON-Dynamic, which schedules between inter- and intra-group advantages, achieves 56.7 math and 29.2 logic simultaneously. For efficient reasoning, CANON-Eff (α=0.96) reduces token cost by 26.3% (822 vs. 1115 tokens) with only a 0.4-point accuracy drop, and at α=0.88 achieves 2.63× higher performance than DR.GRPO in low-token-budget scenarios while reducing token consumption by 45.5% at matched accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CANON-Inter (entropy) outperforms all prior advantage estimation baselines on math: +1.9 over DR.GRPO (57.6 vs. 55.7), +3.3 over ReMax (54.3), +3.8 over GRPO (53.8), and +4.8 over REINFORCE++ (52.8). On logic reasoning, CANON-Intra (entropy) dominates all baselines, achieving 29.1 vs. DR.GRPO's 26.2, Clip-Cov's 26.5, and Entropy Adv's 18.5, with the gap widening to +5.2 points on the hardest XLarge subset. Among entropy-specific baselines, CANON-Inter beats Entropy Adv by 1.3 points on math while CANON-Intra surpasses it by 10.6 points on logic, demonstrating that directional priors (higher-is-better) in Entropy Adv are suboptimal. For efficient reasoning, CANON-Eff (α=0.96) Pareto-dominates Clip Length (55.7 acc, 855 tokens) and Length Reward+ (56.2 acc, 869 tokens) with 56.2 accuracy at only 822 tokens, and at α=0.88 matches Length Reward* accuracy (53.6 vs. 53.9) while using 19% fewer tokens (577 vs. 710). Notably, Length Reward+ collapses from 54.8 to 22.5 accuracy when its penalty coefficient shifts from 0.004 to 0.005, whereas CANON-Eff remains stable across its α range, highlighting the robustness advantage of conditional regrouping over hand-crafted reward penalties.

## Relation to Prior Work

CANON extends the GRPO/DR.GRPO line of group-relative advantage estimation by introducing conditional regrouping: sampled responses are split into two subgroups based on a target metric (entropy or length), and advantages are computed via inter-group and intra-group comparisons. This departs from prior reward/advantage shaping methods (Entropy Adv, Clip-Cov, Length Reward) that impose hand-crafted directional priors (higher-is-better or lower-is-better) on metrics, which the authors argue can be overly biased without careful tuning. Theoretically, the paper shows DR.GRPO is a special case of CANON (μ=0.5 with equal-sized groups), positioning CANON as a strict generalization that selectively amplifies only the advantage attributable to the grouping metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CANON offers a principled, direction-agnostic way to incorporate human priors about training metrics into RLVR, which is practically important as the field increasingly relies on such signals (entropy for exploration, length for efficiency). The Pareto frontier for efficient reasoning (2.63× performance at low token budgets, 45.5% token reduction at equal performance) addresses a real deployment concern. Key limitations include exploration of only two metrics (entropy, length) and one metric at a time, with no multi-metric conditioning; open questions include whether the framework extends to non-numerical or compositional conditions, and whether the scheduling strategy generalizes to larger models or different task distributions.
