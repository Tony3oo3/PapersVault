key_contribution: Introduces IDIOMoE, a Mixture-of-Experts architecture that splits each FFN block of a pretrained LLM into a preserved text expert and a new item expert with static token-type routing, treating item IDs as a native dialect to eliminate collaborative-linguistic interference while preserving language understanding, achieving state-of-the-art sequential recommendation on both public Amazon benchmarks and a proprietary industrial dataset with hundreds of millions of users.
limitations: The method is restricted to text-based item attributes and does not extend to multi-modal representations (images, audio); the static token-type routing, while effective, is a fixed design that cannot adapt per-token to cases where both experts might be beneficial. The industrial dataset is proprietary with limited public detail, and the authors note that MoE placement results 'might be dataset-dependent,' suggesting the optimal architecture configuration may not transfer universally.

surprise_factor: The most notable finding is the dramatic degradation from dynamic to static routing (−59.5% NDCG@10 on Amazon-Beauty), which is counterintuitive given that learned routing is the standard in MoE literature; however, the explanation (clear role assignment prevents cross-modal entanglement) is somewhat intuitive in hindsight and the evidence is limited to a single comparison. The broader result—that structured expert separation outperforms matched-capacity non-MoE scaling—is well-supported across multiple ablations and scales, but represents a reasonable architectural insight rather than a paradigm shift.

## Problem

Naively extending an LLM's vocabulary with item-ID tokens causes collaborative filtering signals to entangle with linguistic semantics, degrading both recommendation accuracy and the model's pretrained language understanding. Simply adding more parameters (wider FFNs, extra layers, LoRA adapters) does not resolve this interference, indicating that the issue is architectural rather than a matter of capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260427/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a user's interaction history as a sequence of special item-ID tokens (e.g., <|it-121|>) interleaved with free-text attributes (title, category) and optional natural-language instructions, all within a 1024-token context window (max 50 items). At inference, generation is constrained to the item-token vocabulary for retrieval, while the same model can produce unconstrained natural-language explanations when prompted, enabling conversational recommendation without a separate module.

## Method

IDIOMoE starts from a pretrained Qwen2.5 decoder-only LLM and replaces each feed-forward network with a two-expert module: the original FFN is preserved as a text expert, and a new (optionally width-shrunk) FFN serves as an item expert. A static token-type gate routes item-ID tokens exclusively to the item expert and all other tokens to the text expert, while self-attention and normalization layers remain fully shared so that IDs and text always attend to each other. The tokenizer is augmented with item-ID tokens backed by a hybrid embedding layer (frozen text embeddings plus a trainable item table), and the output head reuses the same hybrid parameterization for direct ID generation. Training uses standard next-token prediction; for main results the item expert is warmed up on item-only sequences for 20% of epochs before text tokens are gradually mixed in. Ablations show that static routing outperforms dynamic (Switch-style) gating by a large margin, and that placing MoE layers in the deepest 8 layers yields the strongest gains, confirming that collaborative patterns are most critical in late representations.

## Results

On six small Amazon catalogs (Games, Instruments, Arts, Sports, Beauty, Toys), IDIOMoE achieves the best NDCG@10 and HR@10 in 5 out of 6 domains, e.g. 0.1029 NDCG@10 / 0.1409 HR@10 on Arts and 0.0665 / 0.1104 on Beauty, outperforming the strongest non-LLM baseline HSTU (0.0941 / 0.1301 on Arts) and all LLM-based baselines. On the large-scale industrial dataset (hundreds of millions of users), IDIOMoE delivers +27.1% NDCG@10, +16.6% HR@10, and +31.2% MRR over the SASRec baseline, surpassing the next-best LLM-based method Item-LLM (+23.5% / +13.0% / +24.3%) and HSTU (+10.5% / +2.7% / +13.2%). On the larger 2023 Amazon variants (Books, Beauty, Toys), IDIOMoE is the top LLM-based method on all three catalogs and achieves the best overall score on Books and Toys.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDIOMoE outperforms all 16 baselines on the majority of datasets. Against classical sequential models (GRU4Rec, Bert4Rec, FDSA, S3-Rec), IDIOMoE leads by large margins on every domain, e.g. 0.1029 vs. 0.0695 NDCG@10 on Arts (vs. FDSA) and 0.0665 vs. 0.0208 on Beauty. Among recent quantization/generative methods (TIGER, VQ-Rec, MISSRec, MQL4GRec), IDIOMoE achieves the highest scores on all six small catalogs, beating the strongest (MQL4GRec) by 0.0081 NDCG@10 on Arts and 0.0015 on Games. Against LLM-based recommenders (P5-CID, VIP5, ReAT, E4SRec, IDGenRec, CoVE), IDIOMoE consistently wins, with the largest gaps on Sports (0.0391 vs. 0.0372 NDCG@10 over IDGenRec) and Beauty (0.0665 vs. 0.0593 over CoVE). The closest competitor is HSTU, which edges out IDIOMoE on Toys (0.0536 vs. 0.0531 NDCG@10) and Games (0.0609 vs. 0.0605), but IDIOMoE surpasses HSTU on the remaining five small catalogs and on all three large 2023 Amazon variants. On the industrial dataset, IDIOMoE leads Item-LLM by +3.6 pp NDCG@10 and +3.6 pp HR@10, and HSTU by +16.6 pp NDCG@10, confirming that the MoE separation advantage grows with scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM-based recommendation work (P5, CoVE, CLLM4Rec, URM) that augments pretrained language models with item-ID tokens, but departs from them by introducing a structured Mixture-of-Experts split in the FFN layers rather than letting ID and text tokens share parameters. It draws on the MoE literature (Switch Transformers, MoE-LLaVA, MoME) for the expert-routing mechanism, but applies a static token-type gate specifically motivated by the interference problem between collaborative and linguistic signals. The FFN key-value memory analysis framework from Geva et al. (2022) is repurposed to provide interpretability evidence for expert specialization, and the Qwen2.5 backbone serves as the pretrained foundation whose language capabilities the architecture must preserve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying LLMs for recommendation: naive integration of item IDs with text tokens causes mutual degradation, and the industrial-scale validation (hundreds of millions of users) demonstrates that the MoE separation yields consistent gains where simpler capacity additions fail. The finding that static token-type routing substantially outperforms learned dynamic routing challenges the default assumption that adaptive gating is always preferable, suggesting that clear modality boundaries can be more effective than flexible ones. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-modal item representations (images, audio), whether the item expert can be made adaptive to catalog drift over time, and whether the gains persist at much larger model scales (7B+) where the interference dynamics may differ.
