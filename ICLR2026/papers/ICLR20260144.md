## Problem

Existing LLM binarization methods either rely on post-training approaches that cause severe accuracy degradation, or on training-aware methods that require full-precision latent weights, straight-through estimator gradient approximations, and expensive FP optimizers (e.g., Adam with two FP momenta per parameter). No prior method enables direct finetuning of LLMs entirely in the Boolean domain, eliminating both the latent-weight overhead and the gradient approximation noise that limits performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260144/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained FP LLM (OPT or LLaMA variants) and a finetuning corpus (WikiText2 + a C4 partition, sequences of length 2048) as input. It produces a Boolean LLM in which every linear layer's weight matrix is replaced by K Boolean kernels, each consisting of a 1-bit Boolean weight matrix and two FP scaling vectors (s_in, s_out) obtained via rank-1 SVD approximation. A kernel allocation procedure assigns different numbers of kernels per weight under a user-specified average bit budget (including fractional values like 1.58 bits), so the output model size is controlled to match deployment constraints.

## Method

MBOK decomposes each FP weight matrix via successive Sign-Value-Independent Decomposition (SVID), extracting K Boolean kernels where each kernel captures the residual error left by the previous one, yielding the approximation W_FP ≈ Σ_k W_bool^[k] ⊙ (s_out^[k] s_in^[k]ᵀ). The Boolean weights are then finetuned directly in the Boolean domain using a variation-calculus-based optimizer that accumulates loss signals and flips individual bits—requiring only one FP momentum per weight and no STE gradient proxy. Knowledge transfer from the FP teacher is achieved through a two-stage process: data-free successive SVID initialization followed by knowledge distillation (forward KL on logits plus L2 on selected intermediate hidden states, weighted by γ=10). Crucially, only the last kernel's Boolean weights and all scaling vectors are updated during finetuning, since earlier kernels are well-extracted by SVID and modifying them would destabilize higher-order kernels. A PWCCA-based importance score combined with residual-error and size terms drives a greedy kernel-allocation algorithm that assigns more kernels to sensitive layers (e.g., final-block FC2 and output projections) under a fixed expansion-ratio budget.

## Results

With only 2 Boolean kernels (2×1 bits total), MBOK achieves near-FP16 performance across all tested models. On LLaMA-7B, it reaches WikiText2 perplexity 6.83 (vs. 5.68 FP16) and 58.76% average zero-shot accuracy (vs. 64.06% FP16); on LLaMA-13B, 6.17 perplexity and 61.45% average accuracy. On OPT-1.3B, MBOK achieves 16.13 WikiText2 perplexity and 51.69% average accuracy, outperforming all 1-bit and 2-bit baselines. In the 3-kernel setting (Table 3), MBOK closely approaches FP16 perplexity (e.g., 11.03 vs. 10.86 on OPT-6.7B C4), while 3-bit OPTQ degrades to 14.86.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MBOK decisively outperforms all 1-bit binarization baselines: on LLaMA-7B it achieves 6.83 WikiText2 perplexity vs. MoS (7.97), OneBit (8.48), BiLLM (41.66), and PB-LLM (198.37), with average zero-shot accuracy of 58.76% vs. 54.48% (MoS), 52.36% (OneBit), 44.24% (BiLLM), and 40.66% (PB-LLM). Against 2-bit quantization methods, the gap is even more dramatic: OPTQ and LLM-QAT suffer catastrophic perplexity (1.9e3 and 7.1e2 on LLaMA-7B WikiText2), while OmniQuant reaches 15.34/48.17% — all far behind MBOK's 6.83/58.76%. In the 3-kernel setting, MBOK (11.03 C4 perplexity on OPT-6.7B) approaches FP16 (12.71) while 3-bit OPTQ degrades to 17.14. Against BitNet-b1.58, MBOK achieves reasonable results at a 1.58-bit budget whereas BitNet-b1.58 suffers C4 perplexity of 10199.89 due to finetuning instability. On latency, MBOK achieves 3.25×–8.70× speedup over FP16 on LLaMA-13B linear layers, while QuIP# is actually slower than FP16 (0.27×–0.69×) and QTIP is 12× slower (0.08×–0.09×), making MBOK the fastest method tested despite delivering superior accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

MBOK extends the Boolean Logic Deep Learning (BOLD) framework of Nguyen et al. (2024) from small networks to LLMs, and repurposes the sign-value-independent decomposition (SVID) from OneBit (Xu et al., 2024) as the basis for a successive multi-kernel extraction procedure. The key departure from prior binarization work (BiLLM, PB-LLM, OneBit, MoS, DB-LLM) is the elimination of FP latent weights and the straight-through estimator: Boolean weights are trained natively in the Boolean domain using a custom variation-based optimizer, while a multi-kernel structure (K Boolean weight matrices plus FP scaling vectors per layer) provides the representational capacity that single-binary approaches lack. Weight importance estimation for kernel allocation borrows PWCCA from Morcos et al. (2018), and the knowledge-distillation finetuning stage follows standard logit-based KD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: at a 2-bit weight budget, MBOK achieves near-FP16 perplexity (e.g., 6.83 vs. 5.68 on WikiText2 for LLaMA-7B) while all competing 2-bit scalar quantization methods (OPTQ, LLM-QAT) collapse to hundreds or thousands of perplexity, and measured inference latency is up to 8.7× faster than FP16. The elimination of FP latent weights during finetuning cuts optimizer memory roughly in half compared to Adam-based QAT, making ultra-low-bit adaptation feasible on modest hardware. Open questions include whether the approach extends to activation quantization, scales beyond OPT/LLaMA architectures, and can be accelerated on dedicated Boolean hardware (which does not yet exist). The paper also notes that the method has not been validated on generative quality metrics beyond perplexity and zero-shot accuracy.
