## Problem

Standard estimation bounds for diffusion models suffer from the curse of dimensionality (n^{-s'/D}), failing to explain their empirical sample efficiency on high-dimensional data. While recent multi-subspace modeling (MoLRG) reduces the bound to 1/√n, it assumes a single Gaussian latent per subspace, which cannot capture the multi-modal structure of real-world data. This paper asks whether a modeling that reflects both the multi-manifold and multi-modal properties of data can simultaneously escape the dimensionality curse and enjoy fast, provable optimization.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a latent space defined by K expert-specific VAEs (one per subspace/manifold), each encoding full-dimensional RGB images into a low-dimensional latent of size d_k. The diffusion process runs entirely in these per-expert latent spaces, and generation decodes back to full-resolution images (e.g., 256×256 for ImageNet). For conditional generation, a text prompt guides the process, evaluated via CLIP score; unconditional generation is the primary setting. Experiments use MNIST (10 VAEs, one per digit), CIFAR-10, and ImageNet 256.

## Method

The paper proposes MoLR-MoG modeling, where the data distribution is a union of K linear subspaces, each carrying a mixture of n_k Gaussian components with dimension d_k and covariance U_{k,l}U_{k,l}^⊤. This induces a Mixture-of-Experts score function: each expert is a closed-form nonlinear MoG score in its own latent subspace, connected by a linear encoder A_k and decoder A_k^⊤. Theoretically, the authors prove an estimation error of R⁴√(Σn_k)√(Σn_k·d_k)/√n via Rademacher complexity and Bernstein concentration, and establish local strong convexity of the score-matching Hessian under cluster-separation assumptions, yielding linear convergence of gradient descent. Empirically, a small 2-layer MoE-latent MoG network with expert-specific VAEs matches MoE-latent U-Net quality on ImageNet with 10× fewer parameters, while the MoLRG Gaussian baseline produces blurry, indistinguishable outputs.

## Results

On ImageNet 256 (parachute class, text prompt 'a photo of parachute'), the MoLR-MoG score achieves a CLIP score of 0.293, closely matching the MoLR-Unet baseline (0.304) while using 10× fewer parameters, and significantly outperforming the MoLRG Gaussian baseline (0.254). The training loss of the MoE-MoG NN is substantially lower than the MoE-Gaussian NN and approaches the MoE-Unet loss on CIFAR-10 (Figure 4). Qualitatively, MoLR-MoG generates semantically clear images comparable to U-Net outputs, whereas the MoLRG Gaussian baseline produces blurry, indistinguishable images across MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet 256.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the MoLRG Gaussian score from Wang et al. (2024), which models each subspace with a single Gaussian latent. MoLR-MoG decisively outperforms this baseline: the CLIP score improves from 0.254 to 0.293 on ImageNet, and qualitatively the Gaussian baseline produces blurry, unrecognizable images while MoLR-MoG generates clean, semantically coherent outputs. Against the stronger MoLR-Unet reference (a general-purpose latent U-Net), MoLR-MoG achieves near-parity (0.293 vs. 0.304 CLIP) while using roughly 10× fewer parameters, demonstrating that the structured MoG prior captures most of the representational capacity of a large U-Net. The training loss curves on CIFAR-10 further confirm that MoE-MoG converges to a loss close to U-Net and well below the Gaussian baseline, supporting the theoretical claim that the MoG latent is a more faithful model of the multi-modal manifold structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-subspace modeling line initiated by Wang et al. (2024), who modeled image data as a union of linear subspaces each admitting a single Gaussian latent (MoLRG), and by Yang et al. (2024a), who analyzed the single-subspace Gaussian case. The key departure is replacing the Gaussian latent with a Mixture of Gaussian (MoG) latent within each subspace, yielding the MoLR-MoG distribution whose score function naturally decomposes into a Mixture of Experts (MoE) structure. The SDE-based diffusion framework of Song et al. (2020) provides the underlying forward/reverse process and score-matching objective that the paper analyzes. Unlike prior works that either assumed a single manifold or a single Gaussian per subspace, MoLR-MoG simultaneously captures both the multi-manifold geometry and the multi-modal structure of the latent distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unified theoretical explanation for two empirically observed but previously unexplained properties of diffusion models: sample efficiency (via an estimation bound of R⁴√(Σnₖ)√(Σnₖdₖ)/√n that removes dependence on ambient dimension D) and fast, stable optimization (via local strong convexity of the score-matching loss under separation assumptions). The conceptual insight that multi-modal latent structure induces a natural MoE decomposition in the score function offers a principled justification for MoE architectures in diffusion models from a manifold-geometry perspective. Key limitations include the requirement of highly separated Gaussian components for the convergence proof, the restriction to local (not global) convergence, and the gap between the linear-VAE theoretical setting and the nonlinear-VAE empirical setting. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-Gaussian sub-manifolds, establishing global convergence guarantees, and designing optimizers that exploit the block-diagonal Hessian structure for faster training.
