## Problem

Purely autonomous multi-agent LLM systems are closed-world: their knowledge is bounded by pre-training corpora, so they cannot generate new knowledge or adapt to unseen contexts, often leading to collective failure on novel tasks. Existing human-in-the-loop approaches treat humans as passive oracles triggered by heuristic confidence thresholds, and apply expert feedback as one-time fixes rather than as signals for sustained capability growth. The paper asks how to equip agents with a learned metacognitive policy that strategically decides when to defer to external expertise and how to convert that expertise into lasting reasoning improvements.

## Input / Output

At each collaborative round, the metacognitive policy receives a structured state concatenating the task context (original question and interaction history), the agent's own latest solution and reasoning status, peer agents' responses, and optional lightweight cognitive cues (social consensus, metacognitive monitoring, and cognitive control signals computed via rule-based heuristics). The policy outputs one of three discrete strategic actions—EVAL (endorse a peer's existing solution), CREATE (generate a novel solution from scratch), or DEFER (invoke the human expert, whose demonstration becomes the round's output). The system iterates over multiple rounds with N parallel agents, and the final output is the endorsed or expert-provided answer.

## Method

HILA formalizes human-agent collaboration as a Metacognitive Markov Decision Process (Meta-MDP) over high-level cognitive strategies, and trains the policy via Dual-Loop Policy Optimization (DLPO). The inner loop applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a cost-aware reward that penalizes CREATE and DEFER actions (C_defer > C_create ≥ 0), teaching the agent to prefer lower-cost actions when outcomes are comparable and to defer only under genuine uncertainty. The outer loop performs continual learning: whenever DEFER is triggered, the expert's demonstration is converted into a supervised fine-tuning sample, and the model is updated via cross-entropy loss to internalize the new reasoning pattern. The total loss jointly optimizes both loops, with the SFT term gated by an indicator on the DEFER action, establishing an apprentice-mentor dynamic where RL governs when to ask and SFT governs what to learn from the answer.

## Results

On the LLaMA3-8B backbone, HILA with DLPO achieves 89.86% on GSM8K, 35.83% on AMC, 9.37% on AIME, 72.15% on HumanEval, and 73.62% on MMLU, outperforming the strongest autonomous multi-agent baseline (G-Swarm) by 4.97, 20.17, 3.59, 12.60, and 3.95 points respectively. Gains are most pronounced on competition-style math (AMC: +20.17 over G-Swarm) and code generation (HumanEval: +12.60), where cascade failures from flawed premises are common. Across four heterogeneous backbones (Qwen2.5-7B/3B, LLaMA3-8B/3B), HILA consistently achieves the best GSM8K score, with the largest absolute gain (+38.59 pts) on the weakest model (LLaMA3-3B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HILA wins on every benchmark against all five compared baselines. Against the debate-style methods, HILA outperforms Du et al.'s LLM-Debate by +6.34 (GSM8K), +16.55 (AMC), +3.81 (AIME), +14.43 (HumanEval), and +6.03 (MMLU), and outperforms Liu et al.'s G-Debate by +5.88, +15.35, +4.18, +14.22, and +3.73 respectively. Against graph-optimization and workflow-search methods, HILA surpasses Zhuge et al.'s GPTSwarm by +4.97 (GSM8K) and +20.17 (AMC), Zhang et al.'s AFlow by +6.11 (GSM8K) and +23.78 (AMC), and Li et al.'s AgentPrune by +5.48 (GSM8K) and +19.36 (AMC). The largest margins appear on AMC (up to +23.78 over AFlow) and HumanEval (up to +15.04 over A-Prune), indicating that strategic deferral is most beneficial when reasoning chains are long and error-prone. Notably, even the strongest autonomous baseline (G-Swarm at 84.89% GSM8K) remains well below HILA, confirming that the gains stem from learned metacognitive deferral and continual learning rather than more complex interaction topology alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines: multi-agent LLM collaboration (debate, topology control, workflow search) and human-in-the-loop systems. It departs from the former by arguing that purely autonomous MAS are 'closed-world' and cannot escape the knowledge ceiling of pre-training, and from the latter by rejecting the common treatment of humans as passive oracles triggered by heuristic confidence thresholds. The key conceptual move is unifying a learned metacognitive deferral policy (via GRPO in an inner RL loop) with continual learning from expert demonstrations (via SFT in an outer loop) into a single dual-loop objective, rather than treating deferral and learning as separate concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-loop design offers a principled decomposition—RL learns *when* to ask, SFT learns *what* to absorb—that could become a template for any agentic system needing to balance autonomy with external assistance. The transferability result (Table 4), where the DLPO-updated backbone improves unrelated inference pipelines, suggests the continual-learning component genuinely strengthens the base model rather than merely tuning a deferral heuristic. Key limitations include the reliance on a strong LLM proxy (GPT-4o-mini) as the 'human expert,' which conflates model quality with human expertise, and the evaluation is confined to relatively narrow benchmarks (math, code, multiple-choice). Open questions include whether the metacognitive policy generalizes to open-ended or multi-step real-world tasks, how the framework scales when the expert is genuinely fallible or costly, and whether the outer-loop SFT can be replaced by more sample-efficient continual-learning methods.
