## Problem

Existing GRPO-based alignment methods for flow matching models (e.g., Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO) suffer from a sparse reward problem: a single terminal reward from the final generated image is naively applied to optimize all intermediate denoising steps, creating a mismatch between the global trajectory-level feedback and the fine-grained contribution of each step. Additionally, the uniform noise injection in SDE samplers fails to account for the time-varying noise intensity across timesteps, leading to imbalanced exploration spaces that undermine effective policy optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 512×512 RGB image via a flow matching model (SD3.5-M or FLUX.1-dev) fine-tuned with LoRA (α=64, r=32). During training, the unusual aspect is that each intermediate latent x_t is decoded into a clean image via n-step ODE denoising before being scored by a reward model (PickScore, GenEval, or OCR accuracy), enabling per-step reward estimation without any additional critic network. Training uses T=10 sampling timesteps with group size G=24, and inference uses the standard 40-step ODE sampler.

## Method

DenseGRPO introduces two key mechanisms. First, for step-wise dense rewards, it estimates the reward R_t^i of each intermediate latent x_t^i by running ODE denoising to obtain a clean image and applying an existing reward model, then defining the dense reward at step t as the reward gain ΔR_t^i = R_{t-1}^i − R_t^i, which is used for group-normalized advantage computation in place of the sparse terminal reward. Second, for exploration space calibration, it proposes a reward-aware iterative algorithm (Algorithm 1) that adaptively adjusts a timestep-specific noise level ψ(t) in the SDE sampler: at each timestep, noise is increased when the dense reward distribution is balanced (positive and negative samples are roughly equal) and decreased when imbalanced, yielding the maximum feasible stochasticity that preserves reward balance. This replaces the uniform scalar noise level a used in prior work with a per-timestep function σ_t = ψ(t), ensuring a suitable exploration space across all denoising steps.

## Results

On the human preference alignment task, DenseGRPO achieves a PickScore of 24.64, surpassing Flow-GRPO (23.31) and Flow-GRPO+CoCA (23.63) by at least 1.01 points, while also leading in Aesthetic score (6.35 vs. 5.92/6.22) and ImageReward (1.41 vs. 1.28/1.32). For compositional image generation, DenseGRPO attains the best GenEval score of 0.97 (vs. 0.95 and 0.96) and the highest Aesthetic score (5.33). On visual text rendering, DenseGRPO achieves the best OCR accuracy of 0.95 (vs. 0.92 and 0.93). Across all three tasks, DenseGRPO consistently outperforms both baselines on the primary task-specific metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DenseGRPO is compared against Flow-GRPO (the original GRPO framework for flow matching with sparse terminal rewards) and Flow-GRPO+CoCA (which assigns step-wise rewards via latent similarity but still uses trajectory-level signals). On the primary task metrics, DenseGRPO wins decisively: GenEval 0.97 vs. 0.95 (Flow-GRPO) and 0.96 (CoCA); OCR accuracy 0.95 vs. 0.92 and 0.93; and PickScore for human preference 24.64 vs. 23.31 and 23.63 (a margin of ≥1.01). DenseGRPO also leads on Aesthetic score (6.35 vs. 5.92/6.22) and ImageReward (1.41 vs. 1.28/1.32) for human preference alignment. However, DenseGRPO shows minor trade-offs: it scores slightly lower on DeQA for human preference (4.06 vs. 4.22/4.16) and on DrawBench PickScore for compositional generation (22.28 vs. 22.37/22.34), which the authors attribute to mild reward hacking from the more precise dense reward signal. Overall, DenseGRPO achieves the best or near-best results across all three tasks, with the largest gains concentrated in the human preference alignment setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

DenseGRPO extends the GRPO-based alignment framework for flow matching models introduced by Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO, which convert the deterministic ODE sampler into an SDE sampler for stochastic exploration but rely on a single terminal reward applied uniformly across all denoising steps. It departs from this sparse-reward paradigm by estimating step-wise dense rewards via ODE denoising of intermediate latents, a conceptually simpler alternative to training a dedicated critic (as in Zhang et al., 2024) or using latent-similarity-based step rewards (as in CoCA). The exploration space calibration via timestep-specific noise injection further distinguishes it from prior GRPO methods that use a uniform noise level, and the ODE-based reward prediction mechanism is shared with the broader dense-reward literature in text generation (Tan & Pan, 2025) but adapted to the flow matching setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a fundamental credit-assignment mismatch in GRPO-based flow matching alignment, showing that aligning reward signals with per-step contributions yields consistent gains across compositional generation, text rendering, and human preference tasks. The ODE-based dense reward estimation is attractive in practice because it requires no additional trained models and integrates with any existing reward model, though it incurs extra ODE denoising cost during training. A notable limitation is that more precise step-wise rewards amplify reward hacking, and the exploration calibration is performed offline before training, raising open questions about whether online or adaptive calibration could further improve stability. Broader generalization to video generation or longer-horizon tasks remains unexplored.
