## Problem

Long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation tasks (e.g., T-shirt folding) suffer from inconsistent demonstration quality, and existing reward models rely on brittle frame-index-based labels that assign wildly different progress values to identical task states across variable-length trajectories. This label noise degrades both reward estimation and downstream policy learning, particularly when datasets contain suboptimal or failed demonstrations from diverse operators.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of 9 RGB frames (one initial frame plus 8 frames sampled at 30-frame intervals, spanning ~8 s at 30 fps) from a top-down camera, synchronized robot joint states, and a natural-language task description. It produces two outputs: a discrete stage label (argmax over k task stages) and a continuous fine-grained progress score in [0,1] computed as the cumulative prior offset plus the within-subtask interpolated fraction. Positional embeddings are applied only to the first frame to prevent absolute temporal leakage, and up to 4 rewound frames from earlier timestamps are appended in reversed order to expose the model to failure-like regressions.

## Method

SARM uses a dual-head architecture with a shared 8-layer transformer backbone (12 heads, 768-dim) over frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 visual embeddings and projected joint states. The key labeling insight replaces frame-index targets with subtask-prior-based progress: dataset-level average temporal proportions for each annotated subtask define cumulative offsets, and linear interpolation within each subtask yields consistent progress labels regardless of trajectory length. The stage head (cross-entropy) predicts the current subtask, and the subtask head (MSE) predicts a normalized within-stage fraction conditioned on the stage embedding, so the final progress is P_{k−1} + α_k·τ. For policy training, RA-BC computes per-item progress deltas φ(o^{t+Δ}) − φ(o^t) from the reward model, maps them to soft weights via online Welford statistics with a linear ramp over [μ−2σ, μ+2σ], and applies prior-override thresholds to assign weight 1 to clearly positive-progress items and 0 to negative ones, yielding a normalized weighted BC loss that selectively emphasizes high-quality demonstrations.

## Results

On the T-shirt folding reward model evaluation, SARM achieves a validation MSE of 0.009 and a rollout classification score ρ of 0.94, outperforming the strongest baseline ReWiND (MSE 0.019, ρ 0.50) by over 50% on demonstrations and 88% on real robot rollouts. On the downstream policy task, RA-BC with SARM reaches 83% success on medium-difficulty folding (from flattened state) and 67% on hard-difficulty folding (from crumpled state) at 40K steps, compared to 50% and 25% for RA-BC with ReWiND, and 8% and 0% for vanilla BC on the full dataset. On the dish unloading task, SARM also leads all baselines with ρ = 0.67 vs. 0.55 for ReWiND.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SARM dominates all six baselines on both evaluation axes. On the T-shirt folding task, SARM (ρ = 0.94) nearly doubles the rollout classification score of the next-best method ReWiND (ρ = 0.50), while GVL (−0.39), VLC (−0.33), and VIctoR (0.00) are effectively useless, with GVL and VIctoR being overly pessimistic and VLC excessively optimistic. On the downstream policy, RA-BC with SARM achieves 83%/67% success on medium/hard T-shirt folding at 40K steps, versus 50%/25% for RA-BC with ReWiND and 8%/0% for vanilla BC on the full 200-hour dataset. The duration-filtered BC baseline (D_2min) reaches 58% on medium but 0% on hard, confirming that naive filtering cannot substitute for reward-based reweighting. On the dish unloading task, SARM (ρ = 0.67) again outperforms ReWiND (0.55) and LIV (0.39), while GVL, VLC, and VIctoR fail to produce meaningful progress estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

SARM extends the line of VLM-based reward models (LIV, VLC, GVL, VIctoR) and stage-aware reward models (DrS, REDS) by introducing a dual-head architecture that jointly predicts discrete task stage and continuous within-stage progress, using natural-language subtask annotations to derive consistent progress labels rather than relying on frame indices or goal-distance heuristics. It builds directly on ReWiND's rewind augmentation strategy and positional bias design for temporal robustness, while departing from REDS's semi-sparse step-shaped reward in favor of a dense, frame-wise progress curve. The RA-BC component repurposes weighted behavior cloning ideas from offline RL (advantage-weighted BC, BAiL) but applies them with a pre-trained vision-based reward model, making the approach architecture-agnostic and deployable without full-state access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a critical but underexplored axis in robot learning—demonstration quality—showing that naively scaling dataset size is insufficient for long-horizon, deformable-object manipulation, and that reward-model-guided data filtering can close much of the gap. The finding that RA-BC with a weaker reward model (ReWiND) degrades to vanilla BC performance underscores that the reward model is the true bottleneck, not the weighting mechanism. Open questions include whether the annotation protocol generalizes beyond two tasks, whether fully automatic (annotation-free) stage decomposition is feasible, and whether the approach scales to tasks with more than 5–8 subtasks or to multi-robot settings.
