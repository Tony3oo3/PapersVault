## Problem

Existing attacks against DeepFakes attribution methods (AMs) are fundamentally additive: they insert perturbations that obscure but never eliminate the model-specific fingerprints embedded in generated images. This additive nature makes them inherently fragile, as defensive measures such as adversarial training can recover the residual fingerprint and restore attribution capability. The paper asks whether a truly untraceable DeepFake can be produced by fundamentally eliminating the source model's fingerprint rather than merely confusing it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262990/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB DeepFake image (any resolution, generated by any GAN or diffusion model) and produces an adversarial RGB image in which the source model's content-coupled fingerprint has been eliminated, rendering the image untraceable to any AM. The attack is black-box (no access to or knowledge of the target AM) and universal (one trained model handles all 12 tested GMs). A post-processing step combining Gaussian Blur and Mean Shift (GBMS) is applied to the network output to remove residual distortion artifacts before the final adversarial image is emitted.

## Method

The core insight is that generative-model fingerprints are content-coupled modulations (e.g., grid-like up-sampling artifacts) rather than independent noise, so a multiplicative operation x ⊙ W can eliminate the fingerprint f_M by transforming it into f_M ⊙ W ≠ f_M, whereas additive perturbations leave f_M intact. The adversarial matrix W is parameterized as an input-dependent CNN Φ(x) = x ⊙ W(x) (encoder: 3 conv layers + 5 residual blocks; decoder: 2 up-sampling layers + 1 conv layer, all with Instance Normalization), avoiding the infeasible storage and poor generalization of a fixed matrix. Training uses only real images: a data-synthesis module applies stochastic down/up-sampling (nearest-neighbor, bilinear, bicubic) and transformations (Gaussian noise, blur, crop, JPEG, relighting) to mimic GM fingerprints, and the network is optimized with a joint loss of VGG-16 perceptual fidelity, L2 spatial fidelity, and multi-scale log-magnitude FFT spectral loss (scales 1, 0.5, 0.25). The paper further proves the existence of such a multiplicative adversarial matrix via a first-order Taylor/SVD argument and shows that inverting the attack is statistically non-identifiable without paired data, and even with paired data the Cramér-Rao lower bound requires a prohibitively large sample size, making practical defense infeasible.

## Results

The proposed multiplicative attack achieves an average attack success rate (ASR) of 97.08% across 6 attribution models (DNA-Det, AttNet, DCT, Reverse, POSE, LTracer) and 12 generative models (7 GANs + 5 DMs), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline TraceEvader (87.11%). Image fidelity is maintained with SSIM of 0.963 and LPIPS of 0.093, comparable to TraceEvader (0.995/0.038). Under adversarial training defenses, the method retains an ASR exceeding 72.39%, whereas additive attacks like TraceEvader collapse from 98.28% to 25.10%. The method also achieves near-100% ASR against a custom-trained DNA-Det-DMs classifier on the latest diffusion models (FLUX, PixArt, SD3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 8 baselines on average ASR, with the largest margins over transferable adversarial attacks: PGD (58.17%), BIM (23.79%), and MIFGSM (39.51%) are all additive methods that fail to eliminate fingerprints. Against the strongest black-box baseline TraceEvader (87.11%), the multiplicative attack gains ~10 percentage points (97.08%), and critically, maintains >72.39% ASR under adversarial training defenses while TraceEvader drops from 98.28% to 25.10%. The regeneration attack (Zhao et al., 2024) achieves 78.60% average but collapses to 0.00% on LTracer and 39.71% on POSE, confirming that full fingerprint elimination is necessary. FakePolisher (71.17%) and Transformation (67.60%) are intermediate, while DiffAttack (62.24%) is limited to GAN-generated images due to computational cost. In terms of image quality, the method trades slightly lower SSIM (0.963 vs. 0.995 for TraceEvader) for a substantially higher ASR, reflecting the greater structural modification required for true fingerprint elimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the entire lineage of DeepFakes attribution attacks (PGD, BIM, DiffAttack, TraceEvader, FakePolisher, regeneration) by identifying a shared structural limitation: all prior methods operate as additive perturbations (x + p) that obscure but never eliminate the model fingerprint. It carves new ground by proposing a multiplicative attack paradigm (x ⊙ W) grounded in the content-coupled structure of generative fingerprints, drawing on camera fingerprint formulations and the known grid-like artifacts from GAN up/down-sampling operations. The framework is further distinguished by training exclusively on real data with no access to generative models, DeepFakes, or attribution models, and by providing a theoretical proof of statistical non-invertibility that no prior attribution attack has offered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If multiplicative fingerprint elimination is as effective as claimed, it poses a fundamental challenge to the entire DeepFakes attribution paradigm: the statistical non-invertibility proof suggests that no amount of adversarial training or inversion-based defense can reliably recover eliminated fingerprints, shifting the burden toward fundamentally more robust fingerprinting schemes. The paper acknowledges that its method introduces more structural modification than additive attacks (SSIM 0.963 vs. 0.995 for TraceEvader), and open questions remain about whether future GMs can embed fingerprints that survive multiplicative operations, or whether post-hoc watermarking can complement attribution. For practitioners building forensic pipelines, this work is a critical stress-test that current attribution methods may be far more fragile than previously assumed.
