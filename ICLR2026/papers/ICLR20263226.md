## Problem

While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training empirically improves LLM reasoning, the mechanisms by which it enables generalization—particularly from in-distribution to out-of-distribution compositional tasks—remain unexplained. Prior work characterized CoT's effect on expressiveness and sample efficiency but did not address how compositional generalization emerges during training or how it is realized in the model's internal circuitry. This paper asks: what theoretical principles govern CoT's generalization advantage, and what internal structure does CoT training induce?

## Input / Output

The model receives entity-relation token sequences (e.g., e1, r1, r2 for a two-hop query) over a vocabulary of 2000 entities and 200 relations, each mapped to a unique token. In non-CoT training the model predicts only the final tail entity e3; in CoT training it autoregressively predicts the intermediate bridge entity e2 first, then e3 conditioned on e2. The theoretical analysis produces a generalization error bound decomposed into weighted ID and OOD KL-divergence terms, while the structural analysis yields a layer-indexed compositional circuit identifying at which depth each reasoning step is resolved.

## Method

The paper combines an information-theoretic generalization analysis with mechanistic interpretability on a GPT-2-style 8-layer transformer. Theoretically, it derives a bound (Theorem 1) showing the expected generalization error decomposes into ID and OOD KL-divergence components, then proves (Theorem 2) that under CoT training the OOD term further splits into D_KL(P_test^OOD(C|X)‖P_train(C|X)) and an expected D_KL over P(Y|X,C), both of which are explicitly optimized during CoT training and thus approach zero. Structurally, logit lens and causal tracing experiments reveal that CoT training induces a two-stage compositional circuit: the first stage retrieves the bridge entity e2 at layer 3 (ID) or 5 (OOD), and the second stage uses e2 to produce e3, with the number of stages matching the number of explicit reasoning steps. Without CoT, a similar circuit emerges only under ID generalization at a higher layer (≥5), and no OOD circuit forms. The key insight is that CoT training teaches the model *how* to think by decomposing the optimization objective into per-step subtask losses, rather than merely *what* to think via end-to-end input-output matching.

## Results

On a controlled synthetic two-hop reasoning task (|E|=2000, |R|=200, 8-layer GPT-2-style transformer, λ=7.2), CoT training reaches near-perfect ID test accuracy within ~4,000 steps and achieves substantial OOD generalization, whereas non-CoT training exhibits grokking (delayed ID generalization after extensive overtraining) and shows zero OOD generalization even after millions of steps. Structurally, CoT training resolves the intermediate bridge entity e₂ at layer index 3 (ID) / 5 (OOD), compared to layer index ≥5 for non-CoT, freeing deeper layers for the second reasoning hop. Under noisy CoT annotations (second-hop noise only), the model retains systematic generalization for noise ratio ξ < 0.2, with final ID/OOD accuracy degrading gracefully as ξ increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against non-CoT training, which reproduces the grokking phenomenon reported by Wang et al. [102] (NeurIPS 2024): the model eventually generalizes to ID examples only after extensive overtraining but never achieves OOD generalization. CoT training dramatically outperforms this baseline on both axes: it accelerates ID convergence from millions of steps to ~4,000 steps and, critically, enables OOD generalization that the non-CoT baseline completely fails to achieve. In the circuit analysis (Figure 2), non-CoT training produces a circuit only during ID generalization with the intermediate result resolved at layer 5, while CoT training produces a two-stage compositional circuit that supports both ID and OOD generalization with the intermediate result resolved at layer 3 (ID) or 5 (OOD). The paper also ablates the two-hop/one-hop ratio λ and model scale (2/4/8 layers), finding that a smaller λ accelerates OOD generalization under CoT training and that larger models converge faster without qualitatively changing reasoning behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability framework of Wang et al. (NeurIPS 2024), which demonstrated that non-CoT transformers achieve only ID generalization through grokking, by introducing CoT training as the critical variable that unlocks OOD compositional generalization. The theoretical analysis builds directly on information-theoretic generalization bounds (Xu & Raginsky, 2017; Wang, 2024), decomposing the KL-divergence-based error into ID and OOD components to formalize why CoT specifically addresses the OOD gap. Structurally, the paper departs from prior latent-reasoning studies (Yang et al., 2024; Biran et al., 2024) that focused on CoT prompting in pre-trained models, instead analyzing how CoT *training* shapes the internal circuit from scratch, revealing a two-stage compositional architecture whose depth matches the number of explicit reasoning steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unified theoretical and structural account of why CoT training enables compositional generalization, moving beyond empirical observation to a mechanistic explanation (two-stage circuit with layer-index separation) that has direct implications for designing CoT curricula and model architectures. The finding that CoT resolves intermediate results at shallower layers (index 3 vs. 5), freeing deeper layers for subsequent reasoning steps, suggests practical guidance for layer allocation in reasoning models. However, all experiments use small synthetic models (8-layer GPT-2, 2000 entities) with controlled data distributions, leaving open whether these circuit-level findings transfer to large pre-trained LLMs where 'simpler learned skills' are far less cleanly delineated. The authors themselves flag the need to extend analysis to continuous latent-space reasoning and RL fine-tuning settings.
