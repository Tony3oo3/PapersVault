## Problem

Critic-free RL methods like GRPO estimate advantages by normalizing rewards within a group of rollouts, which fixes the advantage variance at 1 and caps the policy gradient norm at a constant regardless of reward variance. This means that simply increasing group size or maximizing reward variance (as in PODS) dilutes critical learning signals with uninformative samples and tokens, slowing convergence. The paper identifies that the correct target is maximizing the variance of the *normalized* advantages within a selected subset, which yields a variable and higher gradient norm upper bound.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text query (e.g., a math problem) and generates a group of G=32 rollouts from the current policy. From these, it selects N samples per query (interpolated from N_init=8 to N_final=32) and K% of tokens per sample (interpolated from K_init=5% to K_final=20%) to form the optimization subset. The output is an updated policy model whose parameters are adjusted only on the selected high-signal samples and tokens, reducing token consumption to under 20% of the original GRPO while achieving higher gradient norms.

## Method

D3S operates at two levels with a dynamic schedule. At the sample level, it computes group-normalized advantages across the full batch, then selects a cross-group subset that maximizes Var(A) (rather than Var(R) as in PODS), which Proposition 2 proves yields a gradient norm upper bound positively correlated with the subset's advantage variance. At the token level, it ranks all tokens in the selected samples by the product |A_{i,t}| × H_{i,t} (advantage magnitude times policy entropy) and retains only the top K%, focusing updates on positions where the policy is both uncertain and reward-critical. A linear dynamic schedule interpolates between an aggressive initial configuration (few samples, few tokens) and a milder final configuration, inspired by curriculum learning, to accelerate early convergence while preventing overfitting to high-signal data in later stages.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, GRPO+D³S achieves an average Pass@1/Pass@8 of 34.3/56.8 across seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks, improving over GRPO (29.8/53.1) by +4.5/+3.7 points and over PODS (34.1/54.4) by +0.2/+2.4 points. On Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, GRPO+D³S reaches 23.8/40.2, surpassing GRPO (20.5/32.4) by +3.3/+7.8 points. In terms of training efficiency, D³S achieves a 2.04× speedup over GRPO and 5.51× over GSPO on the 7B model while using fewer than 20% of the tokens, and still delivers a 4–6% accuracy gain at the same compute budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO, D³S consistently wins across all three model backbones: +4.5/+3.7 (Pass@1/Pass@8) on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, +1.1/+1.6 on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, and +3.3/+7.8 on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, with the largest gains on harder benchmarks (AIME24, Minerva, Olympiad). Against PODS (which maximizes reward variance Var(R) rather than advantage variance Var(A)), D³S achieves a clear margin on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct (+3.1/+5.6) and a modest but consistent edge on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (+0.2/+2.4), while PODS actually underperforms plain GRPO on GSPO (33.6 vs 33.2 average Pass@1), suggesting its intra-group selection is less effective when combined with sequence-level importance ratios. Against GSPO, D³S adds +2.6/+1.5 (Pass@1/Pass@8) on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, confirming that the down-sampling strategy is complementary to sequence-level advantage estimation. In training efficiency, D³S achieves a 2.04× speedup over GRPO and 5.51× over GSPO on the 7B model while simultaneously improving final accuracy by 6% and 17% respectively, indicating that the token reduction (fewer than 20% of tokens optimized) does not sacrifice but rather enhances learning quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the critic-free RL fine-tuning line (GRPO, GSPO) by introducing a theoretically grounded down-sampling criterion that departs from the reward-variance maximization used in PODS (Xu et al., 2025). The key conceptual shift is recognizing that in group-normalized advantage settings, Var(R) is fixed at 1, so the relevant quantity for gradient norm is the advantage variance Var(A) of the selected subset—a distinction the authors formalize via Proposition 2. The token-level selection via |A|×H draws on the entropy-sparsity observations of Wang et al. (2025) but repurposes entropy as a prioritization signal rather than a regularization or suppression mechanism, and the dynamic schedule adapts curriculum learning principles to the RL fine-tuning context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gains (2× speedup over GRPO, 5.5× over GSPO on 7B models) are directly relevant to practitioners running RL fine-tuning at scale, and the method is a drop-in modification requiring no architectural changes. The finding that cross-group sampling destabilizes unaligned models (Llama3.1) while helping aligned ones (Qwen2.5-Math) highlights an open question about how model alignment level should inform sampling strategy. Limitations include evaluation restricted to mathematical reasoning benchmarks and models up to 8B parameters, leaving open whether the advantage-variance criterion and entropy-advantage product generalize to non-binary reward settings, longer-horizon tasks, or larger model scales where the theoretical bounds may behave differently.
