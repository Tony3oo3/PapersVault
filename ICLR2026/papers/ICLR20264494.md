## Problem

Existing translation benchmarks (WMT, FLORES, Redtrans Bench) evaluate only segment-level tasks of ~50 tokens, failing to assess whether models can sustain discourse-level coherence, handle domain-intensive terminology, or meet expert stylistic standards in professional contexts. Additionally, conventional reference-based metrics degrade on long-form text, and single-judge LLM evaluations suffer from hallucinations and biases, leaving no reliable way to automatically score professional-grade discourse-level translation.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 200 source texts averaging 1,712 tokens (minimum 1,500 characters/words) spanning 7 expert domains, each paired with expert-authored rubrics that specify verifiable evaluation criteria such as required terminology translations and cultural expression handling. For evaluation, Metric-S consumes the source text, the model's translation, and the rubrics, producing a composite score (0–100) decomposed into Accuracy (60 pts), Fluency (20 pts), and Appropriateness (20 pts), along with paragraph-level error annotations that include error type (mistranslation, omission, over-translation, code-switching, etc.), severity level (minor/major/critical/extremely critical), and a deduplicated attribution to a single root-cause dimension.

## Method

DiscoX is constructed via a three-stage expert pipeline: 115 vertical domain experts source texts and author rubrics (yielding 665 candidates), SOTA LLMs serve as difficulty filters (a task must fail ≥8 rubrics on both tested models to advance), and domain experts perform final selection (~30% acceptance rate) and rubric refinement. Metric-S is a reference-free, multi-agent LLM evaluation system that orchestrates a structured workflow: an instruction-following gate filters invalid outputs, three specialized judge prompts independently assess accuracy (with rubric-anchored term checking), fluency (native-speaker naturalness, logical coherence, lexical consistency), and appropriateness (style, emotional tone, cultural adaptation), a hierarchical de-duplication module attributes overlapping errors to a single root dimension (with 'Extremely Critical' accuracy errors taking overriding priority), and a severity-weighted deduction formula (2/5/10/50 points per error) computes the final score. Using Gemini-2.5-Pro as the judge, Metric-S achieves 70.3% pairwise consistency with human experts on DiscoX—more than double XCOMET-QE's 34.7%—and ablations confirm that removing deduplication, equalizing dimension weights, or collapsing to a single LLM judge each substantially reduce alignment.

## Results

On the DiscoX benchmark (200 discourse-level Chinese-English translation tasks, avg. 1712 tokens), the top model GPT-5-high achieves an overall score of 76.66 (Accuracy 48.65, Fluency 15.21, Appropriateness 12.80), still trailing the human expert baseline of 80.16. The evaluation metric Metric-S achieves 70.3% pairwise consistency with human judgments on DiscoX, more than doubling XCOMET-QE's 34.7%. On the WMT 2024 en→zh task, Metric-S reaches 72.3% consistency, surpassing reference-based XCOMET (68.8%) and ChrF (55.7%) despite being reference-free.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Metric-S substantially outperforms XCOMET-QE on DiscoX, achieving 70.3% overall pairwise consistency versus 34.7%, with the gap most dramatic at the system level for zh→en (80.0% vs. 10.0%). On the WMT 2024 en→zh task, reference-free Metric-S (72.3%) edges out reference-based XCOMET (68.8%) and clearly beats ChrF (55.7%), demonstrating that reference-free evaluation can match or exceed reference-based methods. Among translation systems, GPT-5-high leads all LLMs at 76.66 but remains 3.5 points below the human expert (80.16); performance is notably imbalanced, with Claude-4 scoring 39.38 on Accuracy but only 5.98 on Fluency, while Kimi-K2 leads Fluency (16.44) but trails in Accuracy (27.63). LLMs consistently outperform traditional NMT (Google-NMT: 37.10) and domain-specific LLMs (Youdao-14B: 46.37) by 10–40 points. A striking finding is that thinking-enhanced models underperform their non-thinking counterparts (e.g., Qwen-3-235B: 59.66 non-thinking vs. 49.97 thinking), attributed to over-summarization and omissions. Directional asymmetry is pronounced: models score on average 21.4 points higher on zh→en than en→zh, with DeepSeek-V3 showing the largest gap (34.7 points).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of discourse-level translation evaluation and expert-domain specificity. While prior benchmarks (WMT, FLORES, Redtrans Bench) and the WMT 2023/2024 literary translation tasks addressed segment-level or narrow-domain discourse translation, none combined long-form (~1700 token) texts with expert-domain terminology rubrics and professional-grade evaluation criteria. The Metric-S evaluation system repurposes the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm into a structured multi-agent workflow with hierarchical error deduplication and severity-weighted scoring, departing from both reference-based metrics (COMET, ChrF) and single-judge LLM approaches that the authors show to be unreliable on discourse-level tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides a realistic stress test revealing that even GPT-5-high (76.66) trails professional human translators (80.16) on discourse-level expert translation, with performance imbalanced across accuracy, fluency, and appropriateness dimensions. The counterintuitive finding that thinking-enhanced models consistently underperform their non-thinking counterparts (e.g., Qwen-3-235B: 59.66 vs. 49.97) challenges the assumption that extended reasoning universally improves generation tasks and points to over-summarization as a failure mode. Open questions include whether the gap to human experts can be closed with targeted domain fine-tuning, whether the thinking-model deficit generalizes beyond the two model families tested, and whether Metric-S's multi-agent design can be adapted to other language pairs and domains.
