## Problem

Existing single-stream identity customization methods for diffusion models struggle to simultaneously preserve fine-grained facial details (e.g., beauty marks, skin texture) and maintain strong text-prompt alignment. Full fine-tuning approaches like FastComposer damage the pretrained model's rich priors, leading to degraded editability and imbalanced visual fidelity, while encoder-based methods like InstantID and IP-Adapter lose critical high-frequency facial details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262097/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference identity image (any resolution, filtered to contain exactly one face) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired scene, pose, clothing, or style. It produces a full photorealistic portrait image (512×512 for SD, 1024×1024 for SDXL) that preserves fine-grained facial details while faithfully following the prompt. The reference image is preprocessed with YOLOv7-Face and a face parsing model to extract a facial mask used during both training and inference, and the framework naturally extends to multi-identity generation by instantiating separate encoders per reference.

## Method

OmniPortrait introduces pivotal optimization, a dual-stream coarse-to-fine strategy. First, a Pivot ID Encoder (OpenCLIP-ViT-L/14) is trained with a novel face localization loss that constrains the cross-attention map of the mixed pivot embedding to align with a ground-truth facial segmentation mask, while keeping the base diffusion model frozen to preserve text alignment. This encoder provides coarse identity conditioning and accurate face localization at inference. Second, Reference-Based Guidance (RB-Guidance) performs test-time optimization by matching DIFT diffusion intermediate features between the reference image (noised to t₀=671) and the generated image, using background gradient masking to prevent gradient leakage into non-facial regions. The guidance is activated only after timestep t̂=0.6T, when the generated image has sufficient structure for stable feature correspondence, avoiding gradient divergence in early denoising stages.

## Results

On the CelebA-HQ/FFHQ evaluation set (50 identities × 30 prompts), OmniPortrait achieves the best identity fidelity (SIM 69.13) and text alignment (CLIP-T 24.25) among all compared methods, while also attaining the highest CLIP-I score (73.08). In terms of image quality, OmniPortrait scores 86.80 on IQA and 213.48 on FID, slightly behind IP-Adapter (IQA 88.15, FID 211.15) but well ahead of fine-tuning-based methods like DreamBooth (FID 219.40) and Textual Inversion (FID 235.57). OmniPortrait is also the only method that is both plug-and-play (supports pose/segmentation control) and achieves top-tier identity and text scores simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniPortrait outperforms all six main baselines on the two headline metrics: identity fidelity (SIM) and text alignment (CLIP-T). On SIM, OmniPortrait (69.13) edges out the closest competitor InstantID (68.35) by 0.78 points, while the fine-tuning-based methods (DreamBooth 60.07, Textual Inversion 58.32) lag by 9–11 points. On CLIP-T, OmniPortrait (24.25) leads PhotoMaker (23.97) and IP-Adapter (23.93), with InstantID trailing significantly at 22.26. On CLIP-I (identity-image similarity), OmniPortrait (73.08) matches InstantID (73.03) and surpasses all others by at least 3 points. The main trade-off is image quality: IP-Adapter achieves the best IQA (88.15) and FID (211.15), while OmniPortrait is slightly behind (86.80 / 213.48), though still well ahead of DreamBooth and Textual Inversion. In the user study, OmniPortrait was preferred by the majority of participants across all four criteria, with the largest margin in facial detail preservation (>50% of raters), confirming the quantitative advantage in fine-grained identity fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniPortrait extends the encoder-based identity customization paradigm (InstantID, PhotoMaker, IP-Adapter) by introducing a dual-stream coarse-to-fine strategy inspired by PTI's pivotal optimization. It repurposes DIFT's emergent feature correspondence (Tang et al., NeurIPS 2023) as a test-time energy guidance signal, and adds a face localization loss to constrain the Pivot ID Encoder's cross-attention to the facial region—addressing a failure mode (attention spreading across the whole person) that prior single-stream encoders exhibit. Unlike fine-tuning-based methods (DreamBooth, FastComposer) that degrade the base model's priors, OmniPortrait keeps the denoiser frozen and trains only a lightweight encoder plus projection layer, preserving text alignment while enabling plug-and-play compatibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles the persistent identity-fidelity-vs-editability trade-off that limits practical deployment of personalized portrait generation, and the released OmniPortrait-1M dataset (1M annotated face-image pairs) fills a gap left by prior works that relied on private or sparsely annotated data. The RB-Guidance mechanism is a reusable test-time optimization technique that could extend beyond faces to other fine-grained identity attributes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-human subjects, whether the added inference-time optimization cost (backprop through diffusion features) is acceptable for real-time applications, and whether the reliance on a separate face parsing model for the reference mask limits robustness on low-quality or heavily occluded inputs.
