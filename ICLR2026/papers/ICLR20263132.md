## Problem

Equivariant RL methods assume a fully group-invariant MDP, but real environments exhibit local symmetry-breaking from obstacles, actuation limits, and reward shaping. Under strict equivariant Bellman backups, even localized violations introduce one-step errors that propagate globally across the state-action space, degrading value estimates and potentially causing training failure. Existing approximate-equivariance approaches relax symmetry globally at the representation level, sacrificing the sample-efficiency gains that strict equivariance provides in symmetric regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes state vectors (agent/goal coordinates in Grid-World; joint angles, velocities, and end-effector poses in MuJoCo/Fetch/UR5e) and actions for Q-network evaluation, producing discrete or continuous actions, Q-value estimates, and a binary gating signal λ that routes each state-action pair to either the equivariant or non-equivariant head. The gating is implemented as a hard switch (Bernoulli-sampled from a learned probability) rather than a soft convex weight, and a separate state-only gate λ_ζ(s) is derived from the state-action gate via expectile regression to drive the policy. Two auxiliary one-step dynamics predictors (equivariant and unconstrained) are trained in parallel on the replay buffer to supply the disagreement signal that supervises the gate.

## Method

The paper introduces the Partially Group-Invariant MDP (PI-MDP), which defines a gated mixture of the group-invariant MDP and the true MDP via a measurable function λ(s,a)∈[0,1], and proves that the resulting Bellman operator is a γ-contraction with a value gap bounded by (1−γ)⁻¹‖(1−λ)δ‖∞ (Corollary 1). In practice, λ is learned by training an equivariant one-step predictor P̂_E and an unconstrained predictor P̂_N on replay, computing their disagreement d(s,a), and labeling upper-tail outliers as symmetry-breaking to supervise a binary gate via cross-entropy. The critic is a hard-switch mixture Q_θ=(1−λ̃)Q_E+λ̃Q_N, and the actor uses a product-of-experts policy π∝π_E^(1−λ_ζ)·π_N^λ_ζ where the state-only gate λ_ζ is trained with expectile regression (τ→1) to conservatively approximate max_a λ_ω(s,a). This yields PE-DQN for discrete control and PE-SAC for continuous control, both trained entirely in the true environment with standard off-policy objectives, requiring no modification to the underlying DQN or SAC update rules beyond the gated parameterizations.

## Results

The paper presents results as learning curves (Figures 3–5) rather than a consolidated numerical table. In Grid-World, PE-DQN consistently outperforms all baselines, with the performance gap widening as the number of symmetry-breaking obstacles increases (0→40). In continuous control, PE-SAC achieves the best sample efficiency on Ant and UR5e Reach, matches or slightly trails vanilla SAC on Hopper's asymptotic return, and is competitive (slightly below) on Swimmer where symmetry is nearly exact. On UR5e Reach, where symmetry-breaking is substantial due to free orientation and realistic dynamics, strictly equivariant and approximately equivariant baselines become unstable or collapse, while PE-SAC remains stable and attains the best overall returns.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the exact-equivariant baseline (MDP-Homomorphic Networks, Van der Pol et al., 2020), PE-DQN degrades far more gracefully as obstacles are added in Grid-World, while the equivariant DQN's performance drops sharply; in continuous control, PE-SAC clearly dominates on Ant and UR5e Reach, but on Swimmer (where symmetry is nearly exact) the strictly equivariant method achieves a slightly higher final return. Against RPP (Finzi et al., 2021a), which applies a global residual relaxation of equivariance, PE-DQN outperforms across all Grid-World obstacle counts and both reward-level and complex-dynamics variants; PE-SAC outperforms RPP on Ant and UR5e Reach, is comparable on Fetch Reach, and is slightly below on Swimmer. Against Approximately Equivariant RL (Park et al., 2025), which uses learned per-channel scalars to relax equivariance, PE-DQN achieves higher returns and faster convergence in all Grid-World settings; PE-SAC outperforms on Ant and UR5e Reach where symmetry-breaking is significant, while the approximately equivariant method remains competitive on Swimmer and Fetch Reach where violations are milder. Overall, PE-DQN/PE-SAC wins or ties on 5 of 6 continuous tasks and all discrete tasks, with the sole exception being Swimmer where near-exact symmetry favors strict equivariance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equivariant RL line (Van der Pol et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2022b) and the approximate-equivariance line (RPP/Finzi et al., 2021a; Park et al., 2025) by introducing a fundamentally different mechanism: instead of globally relaxing equivariance at the representation level, it selectively routes between equivariant and standard Bellman backups via a learned gating function. The PI-MDP framework formalizes this as a pointwise interpolation between a group-invariant MDP and the true MDP, with theoretical guarantees (contraction, proximity bounds) that prior approximate-equivariance methods lack. The implementation builds directly on the RPP codebase and standard DQN/SAC objectives, making it a drop-in extension rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap that limits the adoption of equivariant RL in robotics: real environments (contacts, joint limits, obstacles) break symmetries locally, and strict equivariance then propagates errors globally. The PI-MDP framework and disagreement-based gating provide a principled, theoretically grounded way to exploit symmetry where it holds while remaining robust where it doesn't, without the conservatism of global relaxations. Key limitations include the computational overhead of auxiliary predictors and gates, and the fact that under pervasive symmetry-breaking (e.g., gravity) the method largely defaults to standard RL, offering marginal gains. Future work on vision-based control and reducing the predictor overhead would broaden its practical impact.
