## Problem

Existing temporal graph representation methods rely on local neighbor sampling or short-range historical patterns, which limits their ability to handle dense noise and long-term temporal dependencies. The fundamental gap is the absence of a global evolutionary model that captures the structural regularities encoded in continuous dynamics, leaving representations vulnerable when local information becomes unreliable. This paper addresses how to extract a global evolutionary skeleton from a temporal graph and use it as a principled constraint for robust representation learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a discrete-time temporal graph as a sequence of T snapshots, each with node features (k-dimensional) and an adjacency matrix, and produces node embeddings for the next timestamp to perform inductive link prediction. A key intermediate artifact is the thumbnail: a static graph whose node set is a superset of any single snapshot's nodes, learned jointly with the encoder. The thumbnail is not a fixed summary but is optimized end-to-end via mutual information alignment, and its adjacency matrix M is binary, encoding which persistent structural relationships survive the denoising process.

## Method

TGT introduces a two-component framework built on a GAT backbone. First, it models a temporal graph thumbnail by maximizing mutual information between the thumbnail and the raw graph sequence, decomposed into a structural term (von Neumann graph entropy, computed online via a sparse Laplacian trace approximation in O(|E|) per snapshot) and a feature term (Donsker-Varadhan mutual information estimator over node embeddings). Second, it derives a Thumbnail Bottleneck constraint from the information bottleneck principle: the upper bound of I(G; G_T) is split into a Gaussian-mixture feature term and a Bernoulli structural term (instantiated as KL divergences), while the lower bound of I(Y; G_T) reduces to standard cross-entropy on the downstream task. The overall loss combines the bottleneck constraint with the evolution alignment loss via a Lagrange multiplier, enabling the thumbnail to simultaneously compress redundancy and preserve task-relevant evolutionary information.

## Results

On the clean inductive link prediction task, TGT achieves AUC of 91.41 (Bitcoin), 82.38 (MathOverflow), and 95.42 (MOOC), consistently outperforming all baselines. The largest margin is on Bitcoin, where TGT surpasses the runner-up DGIB (72.99 AUC) by 18.42 points, and on MOOC where it leads DGIB (93.06) by 2.36 points. Under robustness perturbations (Table 3), TGT maintains AUC above 80 across all three datasets even at 50% feature noise, 20% structural noise, and n=5 temporal shuffling, while baselines drop below 70 in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGT outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest competitor DGIB (Yuan et al., 2024), TGT gains +18.42 AUC on Bitcoin (91.41 vs. 72.99), +2.09 on MathOverflow (82.38 vs. 80.29), and +2.36 on MOOC (95.42 vs. 93.06). The gap over GIB+LSTM (Wu et al., 2020) is similarly large: +20.62 on Bitcoin, +4.86 on MathOverflow, and +3.08 on MOOC. Among standard dynamic GNNs, TGT leads JODIE (Kumar et al., 2019) by +16.94 AUC on Bitcoin and +22.23 on MOOC, and surpasses DyREP (Trivedi et al., 2019) by +20.98 on Bitcoin and +14.06 on MOOC. TGN (Rossi et al., 2020) and EvolveGCN (Pareja et al., 2020) trail TGT by 22+ AUC points on Bitcoin. DIDA (Zhang et al., 2022), the best robustness-oriented baseline, is outperformed by TGT by +17.84 on Bitcoin, +8.01 on MathOverflow, and +5.58 on MOOC, with the advantage widening under perturbation (e.g., TGT retains 80.62 AUC at 50% feature noise on Bitcoin vs. DIDA's 64.20).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Graph Information Bottleneck (GIB) framework from static to temporal graphs by introducing a 'Thumbnail Bottleneck' that uses a static graph as an intermediary between raw temporal data and downstream tasks. The thumbnail modeling draws on von Neumann graph entropy (from Ye et al. 2014 and Passerini & Severini 2009) for structural evolution and the Donsker-Varadhan representation (Belghazi et al. 2018) for node feature evolution, while the thumbnail-to-snapshot mapping follows the generative graph prototype literature (Han et al. 2015; Luo & Hancock 2001). The key departure from prior robust temporal graph methods (DIDA, DGIB, GIB+LSTM) is the shift from local neighbor sampling or short-range historical constraints to a global evolutionary skeleton that captures long-range temporal regularities, addressing the identified limitation that constrained sampling scope fails under severe noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The thumbnail concept offers a principled way to compress a temporal graph's global evolution into a static structure that serves as a denoising constraint, potentially applicable beyond link prediction to other temporal graph tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to three datasets and a single downstream task (link prediction), and the acknowledged computational overhead of online VNGE computation restricts scalability to extremely large graphs. Open questions include whether the thumbnail approach generalizes to continuous-time dynamic graphs, whether pre-training can amortize the VNGE cost, and whether the global skeleton idea transfers to other non-Euclidean temporal data (e.g., temporal point processes). The paper's future direction of using thumbnails as prompts in pretrained temporal GNNs is an interesting but unexplored avenue.
