## Problem

Pretrained generative imitation-learning policies (diffusion, flow-matching) capture multimodal human behavior but generalize poorly at deployment, requiring test-time adaptation. Existing RL fine-tuning methods are either incompatible with generative architectures (needing closed-form likelihoods) or offer only one form of correction—residual RL provides local refinement but cannot shift global behavior, while diffusion steering enables global modulation but lacks fine-grained control in off-distribution states. The paper asks how to combine both forms of adaptation into a single, data-efficient framework that preserves the pretrained policy's structure.

## Input / Output

In simulation, the RFS policy consumes privileged state information (robot proprioception, object pose, target pose, binary fingertip-contact signals) and outputs a latent noise vector a_0 (steering the frozen flow-matching base policy) plus a residual action a_r added to the base output. In the real world, inputs are point clouds from multiple calibrated cameras (noise-injected for robustness) plus proprioception, and the same two-component output is produced. Residuals are bounded to ≤1.5 cm Cartesian translation and ≤0.05 rad per finger joint, and the critic is conditioned on the combined executed action a_b + a_r rather than the decomposed components—a design choice shown to be critical for offline RL stability.

## Method

RFS introduces a unified policy-modulation framework where a single trainable policy π_RFS(a_0, a_r | s) jointly produces a latent noise variable for global flow-matching steering and a residual action for local correction, while the base flow-matching velocity field v_θ remains frozen. In simulation, the RFS policy is trained with online PPO on top of a flow-matching base policy pretrained from ~400 VR-teleoperated demonstrations per task, then distilled via a student-teacher framework into point-cloud-conditioned visuomotor policies for sim-to-real transfer. In the real world, offline RL (TD3+BC) fine-tunes π_RFS on 50 human-corrected rollouts, where residuals are computed as the difference between the operator's SpaceMouse input and the base policy output. The key architectural insight is that the critic must condition on the final executed action (a_b + a_r) rather than the decomposed (a_0, a_r) pair; conditioning on either component alone causes offline RL to collapse to zero success. This joint input-output modulation allows the policy to expand beyond the demonstration manifold (via a_0) while simultaneously correcting execution errors (via a_r), achieving 0.87 average success across six dexterous manipulation tasks in simulation and 90% pick-and-place success on unseen real-world objects.

## Results

On six simulated dexterous manipulation tasks (grasping, pick-and-place, packing, push-to-grasp, stacking, pouring), RFS achieves an average success rate of 0.861, substantially outperforming the flow-matching base policy (0.250) and all RL baselines. The strongest individual results are 0.951 on stacking, 0.939 on pick-and-place, and 0.899 on grasping. In real-world evaluation on seen objects, RFS reaches 90% pick-and-place and 80% grasping success, compared to 50% and 43.3% for zero-shot transfer and 60% and 83.3% for co-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFS outperforms all ten baselines on average success rate (0.861 vs. next-best IQL at 0.488 and DSRL at 0.483). Among diffusion/flow RL fine-tuning methods, DPPO collapses on stacking and pouring (0.00) while ReinFlow reaches only 0.409 average, failing on push-to-grasp and packing. Offline-to-online methods (IQL, AWAC, Flow Q-Learning) lack directed exploration: IQL is the strongest at 0.488 but drops to 0.203 on packing, while AWAC and Flow Q-Learning collapse on multiple tasks. RL-with-demonstrations methods (RLPD, IBRL) show high variance—RLPD achieves 0.763 on pick-and-place but 0.000 on packing and push-to-grasp. Among residual RL methods, ResiP (0.433) and Policy Decorator (0.286) provide only local corrections and degrade on sequential tasks. DSRL, the closest single-component ablation, reaches 0.732 on grasping but only 0.032 on packing and 0.135 on stacking, confirming that latent-noise steering alone cannot handle off-manifold states. RFS's joint input-output modulation consistently improves over the base policy on every task, with the largest gains on stacking (+0.891) and push-to-grasp (+0.590).

## Relation to Prior Work

RFS unifies two previously separate policy-modulation paradigms for generative policies: residual RL (Silver et al., 2019; Ankile et al., 2024a), which applies local output corrections, and diffusion/flow steering (Wagenmaker et al., 2025), which modulates the latent noise input for global behavioral shifts. Rather than fine-tuning the full denoising trajectory as in DPPO or ReinFlow, RFS freezes the pretrained flow-matching base policy and learns a lightweight modulation policy that jointly produces a latent noise variable and a residual action. This positions RFS as a parameter-free adaptation layer on top of expressive generative policies, avoiding the instability that full-policy RL fine-tuning introduces for high-dimensional action-chunked architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RFS offers a practical recipe for adapting pretrained generative manipulation policies with minimal interaction data, which is critical as the field shifts toward large pretrained robot policies that still require task-specific fine-tuning. The sim-to-real pipeline (VR teleoperation → flow-matching pretraining → online PPO in simulation → student-teacher distillation → offline TD3+BC with 50 human corrections) is a concrete, reproducible workflow for dexterous hands. Key limitations include reliance on point-cloud observations (limiting semantic reasoning in cluttered scenes) and offline-only real-world adaptation, leaving open whether online real-world updates or richer visual backbones can further close the sim-to-real gap.
