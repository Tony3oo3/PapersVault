## Problem

Existing open-source pre-training datasets face a fundamental quality-quantity trade-off: large datasets (FineWeb 15T, RedPajama-V2 30T) lack sufficient quality filtering, while smaller high-quality variants (FineWeb-Edu 1.3T, DCLM-Baseline 3.8T) achieve quality through aggressive model-based filtering that reduces size below what is needed for Stage-1 long-horizon pre-training. This paper addresses the gap by constructing a ~10T token dataset that balances both quality and quantity for Stage-1 pre-training of large LLMs.

## Input / Output

The recipe takes FineWeb-V1.1.0 (15T tokens, 96 Common Crawl snapshots) as input and produces GneissWeb (~9.8T tokens, ~12B documents) as output. Each document receives multiple quality annotations (fastText confidence scores, McAlpine-EFLAW readability score, TokensPerChar/TokensPerByte ratios, and four binary category labels) before the ensemble filter selects the retained subset. The output is distributed as a ~28GB Bloom filter over document IDs (false positive rate 0.0001) enabling efficient reproduction from any FineWeb or Common Crawl snapshot without redistributing the full dataset.

## Method

The GneissWeb recipe applies sharded exact substring deduplication (suffix-array based, 50-token length threshold) followed by a judiciously constructed ensemble of five quality filters: two fastText classifiers (DCLM-fastText and a custom classifier trained on Cosmopedia synthetic data plus Mixtral-8x22B-Instruct-annotated documents), a category-aware McAlpine-EFLAW readability score filter, a novel Extreme-Tokenized Documents filter using TokensPerChar and TokensPerByte ratios, and four binary fastText category classifiers for IAB taxonomy categories. The key differentiator is the category-aware thresholding: documents in educational categories (science, education, technology & computing, medical health) receive lenient thresholds on readability and tokenization metrics, while other categories receive stricter thresholds, preventing the loss of high-quality but linguistically complex documents. The ensemble aggregation rule is tuned so that approximately 10T tokens are retained from the 15T input, and ablations show this specific rule outperforms four alternative ensemble strategies by up to 1.76 percentage points on high-signal benchmarks.

## Results

GneissWeb (~9.8T tokens) outperforms FineWeb-V1.1.0 (15T tokens) by 2.73 percentage points on 11 high-signal benchmarks and by 1.75 percentage points on 20 extended benchmarks for 7B models trained on 350B tokens (67.34 vs. 64.61 high-signal; 55.14 vs. 53.39 extended). At 1.4B scale, GneissWeb achieves 58.40 high-signal score vs. 56.26 for FineWeb-V1.1.0 and 57.36 for FineWeb-Edu-Score-2. GneissWeb also achieves 27% fewer training FLOPs (1.8×10²¹ vs. 2.2×10²¹) to reach a target high-signal score of 64% for 7B models, and outperforms FineWeb-Edu-Score-2 on IFEval instruction-following (25.69% vs. 23.84% strict accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GneissWeb consistently outperforms FineWeb-V1.1.0 (15T tokens) across all model sizes and task sets: +2.14 pp at 1.4B, +2.52 pp at 3B, and +2.73 pp at 7B on high-signal tasks, with gains of +1.49, +1.95, and +1.75 pp respectively on extended tasks. Against FineWeb-Edu-Score-2 (5.4T tokens), GneissWeb also wins at every scale, with the 7B high-signal gap at +1.83 pp (67.34 vs. 65.51). The paper additionally reports comparisons against RedPajamaV2 (30T tokens), TxT-360, and Dolma in Appendix H, where GneissWeb similarly leads. On fairness, bias, and toxicity benchmarks (Winogender, CrowS-Pairs, Real Toxicity Prompts), GneissWeb performs comparably to FineWeb and FineWeb-Edu-Score-2, indicating no disproportionate risk. For downstream instruction-following (IFEval), GneissWeb-pretrained 7B models achieve 25.69% strict accuracy vs. 23.84% for FineWeb-Edu-Score-2-pretrained models after SFT on Tulu3-SFT-mix.

## Relation to Prior Work

GneissWeb builds directly on FineWeb (Penedo et al., 2024) as its base corpus, adapting the exact substring deduplication approach from Lee et al. (2022) via suffix arrays, and incorporating the DCLM-fastText classifier from Li et al. (2024) as one component of a broader ensemble. The key departure from prior dataset work (FineWeb-Edu, DCLM-Baseline, Zyda-2) is the rejection of aggressive single-model-based filtering in favor of a multi-signal ensemble that includes novel statistical filters (McAlpine-EFLAW readability scores, extreme-tokenized document detection) and category-aware thresholds. This positions GneissWeb as a Stage-1 long-horizon pre-training dataset that explicitly targets the quality-quantity trade-off, rather than maximizing quality at the cost of token count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GneissWeb fills a practical gap in the open dataset landscape: prior high-quality datasets (FineWeb-Edu at 1.3T, DCLM-Baseline at 3.8T) are too small for single-pass Stage-1 pre-training of frontier-scale models, while larger datasets (FineWeb at 15T, RedPajama-V2 at 30T) sacrifice quality. The 27% FLOP reduction to reach a target evaluation score and the consistent gains across 1.4B/3B/7B model sizes make this a practically significant contribution for teams building large LLMs. Key limitations include English-only focus, reliance on a single tokenizer (StarCoder), and limited code/math content; open questions include multilingual adaptation, whether the category-aware filtering insight generalizes across languages, and whether augmenting with synthetic or code data (as Nemotron-CC does) would compound the gains.
