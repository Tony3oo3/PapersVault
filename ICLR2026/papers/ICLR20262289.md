## Problem

Most existing HDR video reconstruction methods assume noise-free and blur-free LDR inputs, focusing only on brightness and spatial misalignment between frames. In practice, alternating-exposure sequences inherently suffer from noise in short-exposure frames and motion blur in long-exposure frames, causing these methods to produce artifacts in real-world scenarios. Additionally, the scarcity of paired real-world training data limits generalization from synthetic to real deployments.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 5-frame window from an alternating-exposure LDR video (EV-2 short-exposure and EV+1 long-exposure frames, 3-stop difference). Before network input, frames undergo inverse gamma correction and brightness normalization (long exposures scaled to match short exposures), then are concatenated with their gamma-transformed versions (γ=1/2.2) to form 10-channel inputs. The network outputs a single HDR frame per input frame, operating on 192×192 patches during training and 256×256 during evaluation.

## Method

DeAltHDR builds on Turtle's multi-scale encoder-decoder with two key innovations. First, a Flow-Guided Masked Attention (FGMA) alignment module replaces standard alignment: SpyNet computes bidirectional optical flow, a forward-backward consistency check produces a binary mask identifying unreliable (occluded) regions, and cross-attention is applied only within those masked regions while the rest uses efficient flow-based warping. A sensitivity parameter s continuously controls the attention ratio, enabling dynamic trade-off between quality and computational cost at inference. Second, a two-stage training paradigm combines supervised pre-training on a synthetic dataset (DJI Pocket 3 4K HDR videos with calibrated heteroscedastic noise and motion blur) with self-supervised fine-tuning on unlabeled real-world iPhone 16 Pro Max sequences, where a motion-enhanced sampling strategy randomly selects short- and long-exposure frames from an extended t±6 window to capture diverse temporal dynamics and bridge the synthetic-to-real domain gap.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset, DeAltHDR (s=15) achieves PSNR 32.55, SSIM 0.9644, LPIPS 0.192, and HDR-VDP-2 77.02, outperforming the best baseline HDRFlow (32.26 / 0.9629 / 0.196 / 76.56) by +0.29 dB PSNR and +0.46 HDR-VDP-2. On the real-world dataset (no-reference), DeAltHDR with self-supervised adaptation reaches CLIPIQA 0.2679 and MANIQA 0.2774, surpassing HDRFlow (0.2601 / 0.2694). In temporal consistency, DeAltHDR reduces TWE from 2.84 to 2.68 and TOF from 4.02 to 3.21 compared to HDRFlow, while maintaining comparable computational cost (128G FLOPs / 152 ms vs. 116G / 128 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeAltHDR outperforms all 12 baselines across every reported metric. Among HDR video methods, the closest competitor is HDRFlow (CVPR24), which DeAltHDR beats by +0.29 dB PSNR, +0.0015 SSIM, −0.004 LPIPS, and +0.46 HDR-VDP-2 on the synthetic set, and by +0.0078 CLIPIQA / +0.0080 MANIQA on real-world data. NECHDR (ACMMM24) and LAN-HDR (ICCV23) trail HDRFlow by 0.10 and 0.22 dB PSNR respectively, while the earliest video method (Chen et al., ICCV21) lags by 0.57 dB. Among HDR image methods, BracketIRE (ICLR25) is the strongest (32.17 dB PSNR) but still falls 0.38 dB short of DeAltHDR, confirming that image-only approaches underperform on video. In the alignment ablation (Table 4), FGMA at s=15 (32.55 dB) surpasses flow-guided deformable convolution from BasicVSR++ (32.42 dB), guided deformable attention from RVRT (32.46 dB), and patch alignment from PSRT-Recurrent (32.41 dB) while using fewer FLOPs than the latter two. For self-supervised adaptation, DeAltHDR's motion-enhanced sampling (0.2679 CLIPIQA / 0.2774 MANIQA) outperforms TMRNet's fixed-subset strategy (0.2648 / 0.2732) and the no-adaptation baseline (0.2621 / 0.2734).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Turtle (Ghasemabadi et al., 2024) multi-scale encoder-decoder architecture with frame history routing, replacing its alignment module with a novel Flow-Guided Masked Attention (FGMA) mechanism that sparsely combines optical flow warping with cross-attention in occlusion regions. It extends the two-stage training paradigm from BracketIRE (Zhang et al., 2025) — synthetic pre-training followed by self-supervised fine-tuning — by introducing a motion-enhanced sampling strategy that generalizes the image-domain approach to video with diverse temporal dynamics. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant HDR video reconstruction line (HDRFlow, NECHDR, LAN-HDR, Chen et al.) by explicitly modeling noise and motion blur degradations in alternating-exposure inputs rather than assuming clean frames, and from prior alignment methods (deformable convolution, full attention) by making the attention ratio dynamically adjustable at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeAltHDR is the first framework to jointly address noise, blur, and motion in alternating-exposure HDR video reconstruction, bridging a gap between idealized assumptions in prior work and real-world capture conditions on consumer devices. The adjustable computational cost (from flow-only to attention-only) offers a practical quality-speed tradeoff that is rare in the literature. Key limitations include the requirement for alternating-exposure input (limiting applicability to standard fixed-exposure video), potential failure when all frames lack sharp references, and sensor-specific noise profiles that limit cross-device generalization. Open questions include whether a truly sensor-agnostic model is feasible and how to reduce the attention bottleneck (38% of runtime) for real-time deployment on mobile hardware.
