## Problem

Existing personalized federated learning methods achieve strong performance on participating clients but catastrophically fail when deployed to non-participating clients experiencing in-domain distribution shifts (e.g., different class frequencies or feature shifts), particularly when resource constraints preclude fine-tuning. This cold-start problem undermines real-world FL deployment in time-sensitive or resource-constrained settings such as healthcare, edge computing, and recommendation systems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes individual image samples (28×28 grayscale for MNIST/FMNIST/EMNIST/SVHN; 32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100; 64×64 RGB for Tiny-ImageNet) and produces a class label. Unusually, each input image is first mapped through a distribution extractor into a P-dimensional embedding (default P=16), which then conditions a chunked hypernetwork to generate the full classifier parameter vector on-the-fly. The generated parameters are never stored or uploaded; they exist only transiently on-device during inference, keeping the deployed model footprint equivalent to a standard FedAvg classifier.

## Method

HyperFedZero replaces the direct X→Y mapping with a two-stage pipeline: a distribution extractor f (with NoisyEmbed learnable noise injection and a Balancing Penalty combining variance-to-mean ratio and entropy regularization to prevent feature collapse) produces distribution embeddings, and a chunked hypernetwork h generates classifier parameters incrementally in fixed-size groups guided by per-chunk embeddings. The key design choice is conditioning model parameters on the embedding (Opt. 2) rather than conditioning inputs on it (Opt. 1), which the paper shows provides exponential parameter efficiency and avoids the Pareto-front trade-offs of a single shared classifier. Chunked generation preserves shared global knowledge across all generated classifiers while enabling per-sample specialization, and the total parameter budget is matched to the original classifier (|θ_f|+|θ_h|≈|θ_c|). Training follows standard FedAvg aggregation over the extractor and hypernetwork parameters with the balancing penalty added to each client's local loss, inheriting FedAvg's convergence guarantees at O(NEK) time complexity.

## Results

HyperFedZero achieves the best zero-shot accuracy (zACC) across all 7 datasets and 5 model architectures, evaluated without any fine-tuning on non-participating clients. Under N=50 participating clients, HyperFedZero reaches 97.32% on MNIST (MLP), 91.52% on FMNIST (MLP), 77.60% on EMNIST (MLP), 91.47% on SVHN (ZekenNet), 61.79% on CIFAR-10 (ResNet), 19.37% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet), and 14.68% on Tiny-ImageNet (ResNet). These gains are substantial over FedAvg (e.g., +12.48 pp on CIFAR-10, +7.81 pp on Tiny-ImageNet) while maintaining comparable global (gACC) and personalized (pACC) accuracy to competing methods. The method also matches FedAvg in total parameter count (|θ_f|+|θ_h|≈|θ_c|) and time complexity O(NEK).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperFedZero consistently outperforms all 11 baselines on zACC across every dataset and model combination. Against the strongest personalized FL baselines (FedProx, Ditto, pFedMe, PerFedAvg), the gains are most dramatic on harder datasets: +15.70 pp on CIFAR-10 (61.79 vs. 46.09 for FedProx) and +14.37 pp on Tiny-ImageNet (14.68 vs. 0.31 for Ditto). Among domain generalization methods, Scaffold is the closest competitor (e.g., 94.64 vs. 97.32 on MNIST, 45.31 vs. 61.79 on CIFAR-10), but HyperFedZero still leads by 2.68–16.48 pp. FedJETs, the only other method explicitly targeting in-domain distribution shifts, underperforms HyperFedZero on most settings (e.g., 53.13 vs. 61.79 on CIFAR-10, 5.31 vs. 14.68 on Tiny-ImageNet) due to its MoE architecture's lack of shared global knowledge and higher parameter overhead. pFedHN, another hypernetwork-based method, fails catastrophically on several datasets (e.g., 44.79 on EMNIST, 1.25 on CIFAR-100) because it relies on client-level embeddings unavailable for non-participating clients. Notably, HyperFedZero achieves these gains while matching FedAvg in total parameter count and communication cost, unlike FedEnsemble and FedJETs which significantly increase model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the hypernetwork paradigm (Ha et al., 2017) from client-level personalization (pFedHN) to sample-level parameter generation, conditioning a chunked hypernetwork on distribution embeddings extracted from individual inputs rather than client identifiers. It departs from FedJets (Dun et al., 2023), which addresses in-domain distribution shifts via Mixture-of-Experts but incurs substantial storage and communication overhead, by generating specialized classifiers on-device with no additional synchronization. The NoisyEmbed and Balancing Penalty mechanisms are inspired by load-balance regularization in MoE architectures, adapted here to prevent feature collapse in the distribution embedding space—a problem unique to the setting where all training distributions are visible and no incentive exists to differentiate them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical deployment gap in FL: trained models cannot be transferred to new edge devices with different data distributions without costly fine-tuning, limiting real-world applicability in healthcare, recommendations, and edge computing. The chunked hypernetwork approach maintains the same parameter count as the base classifier, making it deployable on resource-constrained devices. However, the authors acknowledge that chunked hypernetworks face scalability challenges for billion-parameter models, and a trade-off emerges between personalization accuracy (pACC) and zero-shot accuracy (zACC) under high data heterogeneity (α_d=0.1), suggesting the approach may not uniformly benefit all clients simultaneously. Future work on diffusion-based parameter generation is proposed to address the scalability limitation.
