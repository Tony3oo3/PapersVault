## Problem

Existing reward-hacking mitigations like ORPO optimize against a single fixed proxy reward, providing weak worst-case guarantees when the true reward could be any function merely r-correlated with the proxy. Moreover, these methods treat the reward as a black box, yielding opaque policies that cannot incorporate structural prior knowledge about the reward or reveal which features are most vulnerable to exploitation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260176/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a normalized proxy reward function, a reference policy π_ref (from which the occupancy measure μ_πref is estimated), a scalar correlation parameter r ∈ (0,1), and optionally a set of M known feature functions φ(s,a) for the linear variant. It outputs a robust policy parameterized by a neural network (trained via PPO), a closed-form worst-case adversarial reward R*(s,a) expressed in terms of the Radon-Nikodym derivative L(s,a) and the proxy reward, and in the linear variant, a sparse non-negative adversarial weight vector θ* over the whitened features that is directly interpretable as which feature dimensions the policy is most vulnerable to.

## Method

The paper formulates reward hacking as a max-min optimization: the policy maximizes its return while an adversary selects the worst-case reward from the set of all functions r-correlated with the proxy under the reference policy's occupancy. A change-of-measure via the Radon-Nikodym derivative L(s,a) = μ_π(s,a)/μ_πref(s,a) unifies the objective and correlation constraints under μ_πref, after which a Lagrangian dual yields a closed-form adversarial reward R*(s,a) = [L − λ₁R_proxy/V − λ₂]/(2λ₃). The resulting policy objective (Eq. 9) maximizes proxy return regularized by √(1−r²)·√(χ²(μ_π‖μ_πref) − E²_μπ[R_proxy]), differing from ORPO in both the regularization coefficient (√(1−r²)/r vs. σ_proxy·√(1−r²)) and the subtraction of the squared proxy expectation inside the square root. The Linear Max-Min variant restricts rewards to θ⊤φ(s,a), applies a Cholesky-based whitening transformation to reduce the inner QP to a closed-form elementwise max(0, ·) solution, and produces interpretable adversarial feature weights. The Radon-Nikodym derivative is estimated via a discriminator network trained with a binary cross-entropy objective, and the paper proves an O(n^{-1/4}) sample-complexity bound for this estimation along with an O(1/T + 1/N + n^{-1/4}) convergence guarantee for the overall algorithm.

## Results

Across five environments, Max-Min achieves dramatically better worst-case rewards than ORPO: in Traffic, worst-case improves from −1.96×10⁴ (ORPO) to −268.31 (Max-Min); in Pandemic, from −5.31×10⁶ to −63.29; in Glucose, from −27.54 to −1.71; and in RLHF, from −1.84 to −0.10. Linear Max-Min further improves the linear worst-case metric (e.g., +0.20 vs −0.69 in Traffic) while remaining competitive on true reward (16.46 vs ORPO's 16.91 in Traffic). Max-Min also reduces occupancy on unseen state-action pairs to 0.00 in Traffic and Pandemic, compared to 3.82×10⁻⁴ for ORPO, indicating the policy avoids unexplored regions where adversarial rewards are unconstrained.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ORPO (Laidlaw et al., 2025), Max-Min achieves orders-of-magnitude improvements in worst-case reward: −268.31 vs. −1.96×10⁴ in Traffic, −63.29 vs. −5.31×10⁶ in Pandemic, −1.71 vs. −27.54 in Glucose, and −0.10 vs. −1.84 in RLHF. However, ORPO sometimes attains higher true reward (e.g., 16.91 vs. 12.70 in Traffic), suggesting Max-Min trades some nominal performance for substantially stronger worst-case guarantees. Linear Max-Min recovers much of the true-reward gap (16.46 in Traffic, 3.65 in Pandemic) while still delivering the best linear worst-case scores. The ablation ORPO* (ORPO with fully trained discriminator) outperforms original ORPO on worst-case reward in every environment, confirming that accurate Radon-Nikodym derivative estimation is critical for robustness. In the RLHF setting, the Ensemble baseline (Eisenstein et al., 2023) achieves a worst-case reward of −1.70 and true reward of 2.31, both substantially worse than Max-Min's −0.10 and 5.38, indicating that reward-model ensembles alone are insufficient to mitigate reward hacking compared to the proposed max-min formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the r-correlated proxy framework and ORPO from Laidlaw et al. (ICLR 2025), which formalized reward hacking as optimizing a single proxy reward with a χ² occupancy regularization. The key departure is replacing the fixed-proxy regularization with a max-min optimization over the entire set of r-correlated rewards, solved via Lagrangian duality to yield a closed-form adversarial reward. The Linear Max-Min variant additionally draws on the successor features literature (Barreto et al., 2017; 2018) to parameterize reward uncertainty as linear combinations of known features, enabling a convex QP inner problem with a whitening-based closed-form solution. The paper also positions itself within distributionally robust optimization (Delage & Ye, 2010) but departs from the rectangular uncertainty sets typical in robust RL by using a non-rectangular, correlation-constrained reward set that couples deviations across states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled worst-case guarantee against all plausible proxy rewards within a correlation bound, rather than relying on a single (possibly miscalibrated) proxy, which is more realistic for safety-critical deployments. The linear variant's interpretable adversarial weight vectors offer a diagnostic tool for identifying which reward features are most vulnerable, supporting human-in-the-loop reward refinement. Key open questions include: scaling to non-linear reward representations (where the inner problem loses its closed form), handling state-action pairs outside the reference policy's support where the adversary is unconstrained, the practical difficulty of choosing the correlation parameter r without access to the true reward, and the O(d³) computational cost of the whitening step in high-dimensional feature spaces.
