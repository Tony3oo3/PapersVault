## Problem

Existing Video LLMs encode video frames into flat token sequences that become prohibitively long for long-form videos, exceeding context limits and incurring high computational costs. Slow-Fast architectures partially mitigate this by separating temporal and spatial features during encoding, but these features are still processed jointly within the LLM without true spatio-temporal disentanglement, and spatial features are sampled in a query-agnostic manner that risks losing task-relevant visual content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260579/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video (processed as 128 frames at 4×4 spatial resolution for the temporal branch and 32 query-selected keyframes at 8×8 resolution for the spatial branch) along with a natural language text query. It produces temporal segment boundaries as generated text tokens (e.g., start and end timestamps), grounded answer text for VideoQA tasks, and timestamp-guided captions for specific video segments. Each frame is prepended with a textual timestamp token (e.g., '3.0 second') encoded by the LLM tokenizer.

## Method

Divid introduces a dual-branch decoder architecture that explicitly disentangles spatial and temporal modeling inside the LLM. The temporal branch processes densely sampled low-resolution frames to capture long-range dynamics and generates cross-modal attention maps that guide query-aware Top-K keyframe selection for the spatial branch, which then attends to high-resolution features of only the most relevant frames. A lightweight spatio-temporal soft-router at each decoder layer uses the original pre-attention text embeddings to compute token-wise gating weights, adaptively fusing temporal and spatial attended features per token. The model is trained in two stages: pre-training on LLaVA-558K with WebVid videos, followed by instruction tuning on the newly constructed TempGCap dataset (559K timestamp-grounded video-text pairs from 428K videos) combined with other grounding and QA datasets, using ViT-g/14 (EVA-CLIP) as the frozen vision encoder and Qwen2 as the LLM backbone.

## Results

On Charades-STA (zero-shot), Divid (7B) achieves 59.5 R@0.5, 31.3 R@0.7, and 51.3 mIoU, outperforming the strongest prior zero-shot baseline VideoMind (7B) by 1.1 mIoU and TimeMarker (8B) by 2.9 mIoU. The 1.5B variant reaches 47.3 mIoU, surpassing all 7B zero-shot baselines except VideoMind (7B) and TimeMarker (8B). On CG-Bench, Divid (7B) sets a new open-source SOTA with 5.74 mIoU and 8.36 R@IoU, matching or exceeding GPT-4o on mIoU and R@IoU. On ReXTime, Divid (7B) achieves 35.78 mIoU and 22.26 Acc@IoU, surpassing VideoMind (7B) by 8.17 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Divid (7B) achieves the best zero-shot mIoU (51.3) on Charades-STA, beating VideoMind (7B) by 1.1, TimeMarker (8B) by 2.9, and Qwen2.5-VL (72B) by 0.4, while the 1.5B variant already surpasses all 7B baselines except VideoMind and TimeMarker. On CG-Bench, Divid (7B) outperforms GPT-4o on mIoU (5.74 vs. 5.62) and R@IoU (8.36 vs. 8.30) but trails slightly on A@IoU (4.11 vs. 4.38); it also exceeds Qwen2-VL (72B) by 2.16 mIoU. On NExT-GQA, Divid (7B) leads VideoMind (7B) by 3.1 mIoU (34.5 vs. 31.4) and 1.0 Acc@GQA (29.2 vs. 28.2). On ReXTime, Divid (7B) surpasses VideoMind (7B) by 8.17 mIoU (35.78 vs. 27.61) and 2.06 Acc@IoU (22.26 vs. 20.20), and outperforms fine-tuned VTimeLLM (7B) by 5.86 mIoU. In the fine-tuned setting on Charades-STA, Divid (7B) matches or exceeds VideoChat-R1 (7B) and TimeZero (7B), which use GRPO-based reinforcement learning with stronger Qwen2.5 backbones, achieving 61.3 mIoU vs. 60.8 and 50.8 R@0.7 vs. 50.2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Slow-Fast paradigm (Feichtenhofer et al., 2019) into the Video LLM setting, departing from prior works like LITA, SlowFast-VideoLLM, and VideoExpert that separate temporal and spatial tokens but still process them jointly within a unified LLM attention space. The key architectural departure is performing true spatio-temporal disentanglement *inside* the LLM decoder via two parallel attention branches, where temporal attention maps guide query-aware Top-K keyframe selection for the spatial branch, and a lightweight soft-router fuses the two at the token level. On the data side, TempGCap builds on timestamp-guided captioning datasets like Momentor and VTimeLLM-Stage2 but addresses their quality-diversity trade-off by combining manual reannotation, untrimmed context recovery, and synthetic stitching across 428K source videos.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 63% FLOPs reduction (28.2→10.5 TFLOPs) while matching or exceeding the full-attention model's temporal grounding accuracy makes long-form video understanding substantially more practical for deployment, and the TempGCap dataset (559K high-quality timestamp-grounded pairs) fills a gap between the large-but-noisy Momentor and the small-but-clean VTimeLLM-Stage2. However, the approach inherits potential biases from the pretrained LLM's temporal attention (acknowledged in the limitations section), and the model still struggles with cross-interval grounding (e.g., 'before'/'after' queries where the answer lies outside the question segment). Open questions include whether the dual-branch design generalizes to other long-sequence modalities and whether the soft-router's token-level routing can be made more interpretable or controllable.
