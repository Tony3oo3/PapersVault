## Problem

Existing multi-region neural latent models face a three-way tension: they must capture non-stationary, nonlinear temporal dynamics, enforce temporal smoothness, and separate shared (communication-subspace) from region-private signals without parameter explosion as the number of areas grows. GP-based multi-region methods (DLAG, mDLAG) inherit linear and stationary assumptions, while multi-view autoencoders (DeepCCA, DMVAE) treat time points as i.i.d. and discard dynamics. No prior method jointly satisfies all three requirements in a single scalable framework.

## Input / Output

The model consumes Gaussian-smoothed, 100 ms-binned firing-rate estimates from simultaneous recordings across R brain regions (e.g., 52 M1 + 66 PMd units via Utah arrays, or three Neuropixels-probed regions: superficial SC, deep SC, ALM). It produces a single fused latent matrix of dimension D×T, partitioned by fixed binary masks into contiguous shared, region-1-private, and region-2-private blocks, plus per-region reconstructed firing rates. The latents are behavior-agnostic, so downstream linear read-outs (hand position, reach direction, stimulus type, target side) can be applied without retraining the encoder.

## Method

CTAE assigns each region a dedicated causal Transformer encoder (self-attention over the full trial) and decoder (cross-attention), producing per-region latent sequences of dimension D. Binary weight masks partition D into contiguous shared and private blocks; a masked-average fusion rule leaves private dimensions unchanged while averaging shared dimensions across regions, implicitly forcing cross-region alignment. Training minimizes four losses: standard per-region reconstruction, a shared-only reconstruction loss that forces each decoder to reconstruct its region using only the shared block (preventing shared information from leaking into private subspaces), an alignment loss penalizing divergence between each encoder's shared output and the fused average, and an orthogonality loss driving off-diagonal entries of the latent Gram matrix to zero (with a linear warm-up schedule to stabilize early training). For R > 2 regions, the binary masks generalize to an R×D membership matrix W, so each latent dimension can be claimed by any combinatorial subset of regions, enabling pairwise-shared and fully-shared subspaces without exponential parameter growth.

## Results

On the M1-PMd motor circuit dataset, CTAE shared latents achieve R²=0.77 for continuous hand-position decoding and 69% accuracy for 8-way reach-direction classification, outperforming DLAG (R²=0.72, 44%) and DeepCCA (R²=0.32, 34%). On the SC-ALM multisensory dataset (3 regions), CTAE pairwise shared subspaces (SupSC–DeepSC and DeepSC–ALM) reach 0.59 mean 5-fold accuracy for stimulus×target decoding, compared to 0.22 for DeepCCA and no improvement over raw activity for mDLAG. Ablation confirms each loss term contributes: removing the shared-only reconstruction loss drops shared-latent accuracy from 0.69 to 0.34, and removing orthogonality drops it to 0.31.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTAE consistently outperforms all three baselines across both datasets. Against DLAG (Gokcen et al., 2022), CTAE achieves higher shared-latent decoding accuracy on M1-PMd (69% vs 44% reach direction; R² 0.77 vs 0.72 hand position), with DLAG exhibiting direction-specific fragmentation of kinematic information across private latents. Against mDLAG (Gokcen et al., 2023) on the 3-region SC-ALM dataset, mDLAG shared and private latents show no improvement over predicting from raw neural activity, whereas CTAE shared subspaces reach 0.59 accuracy—likely reflecting mDLAG's linear observation assumption limiting its capture of nonlinear multi-region interactions. Against DeepCCA (Andrew et al., 2013), CTAE's advantage is most pronounced: R² 0.77 vs 0.32 and 69% vs 34% on M1-PMd, and 0.59 vs 0.22 on SC-ALM pairwise comparisons, highlighting DeepCCA's inability to model temporal dynamics since it treats time points as i.i.d. samples. A concatenated-data Transformer AE baseline (no disentanglement) achieves intermediate performance (R² 0.59, 57% on M1-PMd) but cannot distinguish shared from region-private computations, limiting interpretability.

## Relation to Prior Work

CTAE sits at the intersection of three research threads: single-region latent dynamics models (GPFA, LFADS, NDT) that capture temporal structure but are restricted to one area; multi-region GP-based models (DLAG, mDLAG) that handle inter-area interactions but assume linear read-outs and stationary dynamics; and multi-view autoencoders (DCCA, SPLICE, DMVAE) that separate shared/private factors but treat time points as i.i.d. The paper's key departure is combining causal Transformer encoders/decoders (for non-stationary, long-range dynamics) with an explicit masked latent fusion mechanism and four complementary losses (reconstruction, shared-only reconstruction, alignment, orthogonality) to simultaneously satisfy all three requirements no prior method jointly addresses. The communication subspace hypothesis (Semedo et al., 2019) provides the conceptual motivation for the shared/private partition, while the weight-mask design generalizes the two-region case to arbitrary region counts without pairwise parameter explosion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CTAE provides a behavior-agnostic latent representation that supports diverse downstream decoding (kinematics, forces, cognitive states) via simple linear read-outs without retraining, and its mask-based architecture scales to more than two regions without exponential growth in parameters. The SC-ALM results revealing deep SC as a circuit hub with distinct shared dynamics for evidence accumulation (with superficial SC) versus choice (with ALM) offer mechanistic insight into multisensory decision-making. Key limitations include fixed (non-learnable) masks, reliance on Gaussian-smoothed firing rates rather than raw spikes, and validation on only two-to-three regions; open questions include whether learnable masks can discover interaction structure from data, whether a Poisson observation model improves fidelity, and whether the framework generalizes to neural-behavioral joint modeling.
