## Problem

The empirical group distributionally robust (GDR) least squares problem—minimizing the worst-case per-group squared prediction error—lacks efficient algorithms with iteration complexity independent of the number of groups m. Existing approaches suffer from geometry-dependent convergence rates (subgradient methods), a 1/ε² iteration dependence (min-max and smoothened-objective methods), or a superlinear √m dependence on the number of groups (standard log-barrier interior point methods). This paper closes the gap by providing a second-order algorithm whose iteration complexity depends only on min{rank(A), m}^{1/3} and ε^{-2/3}, with no conditioning or geometry-dependent factors.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes m groups of linear regression data—design matrices A_{S_i} ∈ R^{n_i × d} and response vectors b_{S_i} ∈ R^{n_i} for i ∈ [m]—along with an accuracy parameter ε > 0 and, for the interpolating variant, a parameter p ≥ 2 controlling the trade-off between average and worst-case loss. It outputs a parameter vector x̂ ∈ R^d achieving a (1+ε)-multiplicative approximation to the optimal group DRO objective. The computational cost is measured in linear-system-solves of matrices A^TBA where B is block-diagonal with each block of size n_i × n_i, a structure that admits faster amortized solvers via inverse maintenance techniques.

## Method

The algorithm combines three key ingredients: (1) block Lewis weights (from Manoj & Ovsiankin, 2025) to compute a geometry M = A^T W^{1−2/p} A that approximates the group-norm loss with distortion O(rank(A)^{1/2−1/p}), replacing the naive A^T A geometry that would yield m^{1/3} dependence; (2) for the robust p=∞ case, a softmax-smoothed surrogate f̃_{β,δ} whose quasi-self-concordance is established via a new general composition lemma (composing softmax with quasi-self-concordant inner functions), enabling Hessian-stability-based Newton subproblem solves; and (3) an accelerated Monteiro–Svaiter iteration (Carmon et al., 2020/2022) that iterates proximal subproblem calls to achieve the (‖x_0−x*‖_M / ε)^{2/3} rate. For the interpolating 2≤p<∞ case, the method instead solves p-norm-regularized proximal subproblems, leveraging a new strong-convexity lemma for ‖y‖_2^p to obtain approximate stationary points required by the bisection-free acceleration scheme.

## Results

The paper's main algorithm (Algorithm 1) achieves Õ(min{rank(A), m}^{1/3} ε^{-2/3}) linear-system-solves in matrices of the form A^TBA for the group distributionally robust least squares problem (Theorem 1), with no geometry-dependent terms. For the interpolating family of objectives (Theorem 2), the iteration complexity is O(p^{O(1)} min{rank(A), m}^{(p-2)/(3p-2)} log(pd/ε)^3) linear-system-solves. In the special case n_i = 1 (reducing to ℓ_∞ regression), the guarantee exactly matches the state-of-the-art of Jambulapati et al. (2022). The paper is primarily theoretical; no empirical dataset benchmarks with specific accuracy numbers are reported in the main text (an empirical comparison is deferred to Appendix F).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the standard log-barrier interior point method (Boyd & Vandenberghe, 2004), which requires m^{1/2}·log(1/ε) iterations, the proposed algorithm improves to min{rank(A), m}^{1/3}·ε^{-2/3} iterations, yielding a strict improvement whenever ε ≥ m^{-1/4} and a more pronounced gain when m ≫ rank(A). Against first-order baselines (subgradient descent, Nesterov acceleration, and Abernethy et al. 2022), the proposed method eliminates all geometry-dependent factors (‖x*‖₂ and ‖A_{S_i}‖_op) that appear in their guarantees, and achieves a better ε-dependence (ε^{-2/3} vs. ε^{-1} or ε^{-2}). In the special case where n_i = 1 for all i (reducing to ℓ_∞ regression), the algorithm exactly recovers the iteration complexity of Jambulapati et al. (2022), rank(A)^{1/3}/ε^{2/3}, while their methods do not extend to the general grouped setting. Compared to the IPM for ℓ_∞ regression (Lee & Sidford, 2019), which achieves rank(A)^{1/2}·log(1/ε), the proposed method matches or improves upon this for ε ≥ rank(A)^{-1/4}. The key structural advantage is that the iteration complexity is independent of m (the number of groups) up to the min{rank(A), m} factor, whereas the IPM baseline scales as √m.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent threads: (1) the Lewis-weights-based acceleration of second-order methods for ℓ_p regression (Jambulapati et al., 2022; Lee & Sidford, 2019), and (2) the Monteiro-Svaiter / ball-optimization-oracle acceleration framework (Carmon et al., 2020, 2022). The key conceptual leap is replacing the diagonal Lewis weights used in vanilla ℓ_p regression with *block* Lewis weights (Manoj & Ovsiankin, 2025), which encode the group structure of the design matrix and yield a geometry whose distortion depends on min{rank(A), m} rather than m alone. A new composition theorem (Lemma C.3) showing that softmax composed with quasi-self-concordant inner functions remains quasi-self-concordant is the main analytic tool that was missing to make the p=∞ (robust) case work within the Carmon et al. acceleration recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The algorithm's iteration complexity is independent of the number of groups m, which is a significant practical improvement over the O(√m) barrier of standard log-barrier IPM when m is large relative to the parameter dimension. The interpolating family (objective 4) gives practitioners a principled knob to trade off utilitarian average loss against egalitarian worst-group loss, with a provable guarantee that is unconditionally polynomial in p. Open problems include obtaining a high-accuracy (polylog(1/ε)) solver for the robust objective, extending the framework to general convex quadratic programs, and understanding the statistical trade-off as p varies in multi-distribution learning settings.
