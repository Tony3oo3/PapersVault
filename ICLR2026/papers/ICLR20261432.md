## Problem

Standard message-passing neural networks suffer from oversquashing, oversmoothing, and limited expressivity, while existing graph positional encodings (RWPE, LaPE) rely on purely topological or spectral signals that may not capture the full geometric-topological structure of a graph. No prior method provides an end-to-end trainable local structural encoding that jointly leverages both geometry and topology in a differentiable, architecture-agnostic manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261432/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LEAP consumes a featured graph (node feature vectors in R^d plus edge connectivity) and produces a k-dimensional positional encoding vector per node (default k=10). The encoding is computed on m-hop neighborhoods (default m=1), where features are mean-centered and max-norm-scaled to the unit sphere before the ECT is evaluated over a fixed grid of |Θ|=16 directions and |T|=16 thresholds, yielding a 16×16 matrix that is projected to the final PE. The PE can be computed on static input features or on learned hidden states at any layer of a GNN, and the directions Θ can be randomly initialized and then optimized during training.

## Method

LEAP computes the Differentiable Euler Characteristic Transform (DECT) on each node's normalized m-hop subgraph, treating the subgraph as a geometric graph rather than a point cloud (unlike prior ℓ-ECT work), and then maps the resulting |Θ|×|T| ECT matrix to a k-dimensional vector via a learnable projection. Five projection strategies are provided—linear, 1D convolution, DeepSets, self-attention, and attention with direction concatenation—ranging from permutation-sensitive to permutation-invariant with respect to the direction ordering. The key differentiator from prior static ℓ-ECT is that the direction vectors Θ are randomly initialized and jointly optimized with the rest of the network in an end-to-end fashion, and the encoding is computed on the actual subgraph structure (edges included) rather than on node features alone. This makes LEAP a local structural encoding in the Rampašek et al. taxonomy, with locality controlled by the hop parameter m, and it integrates as a drop-in PE module into any GCN, GAT, GIN, or non-message-passing transformer architecture.

## Results

On the TU benchmark, LEAP (learned directions, LEAP-L) achieves the best or second-best accuracy for every dataset-architecture combination across GCN, GAT, GIN, and NoMP backbones. The largest gains appear on the Letter datasets with the NoMP architecture: LEAP-L reaches 81.6% on LETTER-H (vs. 63.4% No PE, 79.2% RWPE, 65.0% LaPE), 88.5% on LETTER-M (vs. 57.8% No PE), and 98.0% on LETTER-L (vs. 89.7% No PE). On the synthetic 3-node structural task, LEAP achieves 100% accuracy while GCN and GAT alone reach only 71.8% and 69.4%, respectively. On the large-scale Alchemy dataset (202K graphs), LEAP-L with GCN yields the highest R² across all 12 regression tasks, and on HIV it ranks second to LaPE in AUROC but outperforms RWPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAP consistently outperforms both RWPE (Dwivedi et al., 2022) and LaPE (Maskey et al., 2022) across all seven TU datasets and all four architectures. The margin is most dramatic on the Letter datasets with NoMP: LEAP-L beats RWPE by 2.4 pp on LETTER-H (81.6 vs. 79.2), 4.0 pp on LETTER-M (88.5 vs. 84.5), and 3.3 pp on LETTER-L (98.0 vs. 94.7), while surpassing LaPE by 16.6, 11.6, and 6.9 pp respectively. On the molecular datasets (COX2, BZR, DHFR, FINGERPRINT), LEAP's advantage over RWPE and LaPE is smaller (1–6 pp) but still consistent. Against the global DECT method (Röell & Rieck, 2024), LEAP outperforms both the 4K and 65K parameter variants on most datasets (e.g., 81.6% vs. 63.8% on LETTER-H, 84.7% vs. 81.8% on BZR), despite using fewer parameters. The one notable exception is the HIV dataset, where LaPE achieves the highest single-PE AUROC, suggesting global spectral information is particularly relevant there; however, concatenating LaPE with LEAP yields the best overall result, confirming the two encodings capture complementary structural information.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEAP directly extends two prior works: the local ECT (ℓ-ECT) of von Rohrscheidt & Rieck (ICML 2025), which was a static, non-learnable feature augmentation computed on node neighborhoods treated as point clouds, and the Differentiable ECT (DECT) of Röell & Rieck (ICLR 2024), which made the ECT differentiable but was designed for global graph-level descriptors. LEAP departs from both by computing the ℓ-ECT on the m-hop subgraph as a graph (preserving edge structure), making the direction vectors learnable end-to-end, and integrating the result as a local structural positional encoding within standard GNN architectures. Within the Rampašek et al. (2022) taxonomy of graph PEs/SEs, LEAP occupies the local structural encoding slot alongside RWPE, but unlike RWPE it leverages a geometric-topological invariant rather than random walk statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEAP is the first approach to integrate the ℓ-ECT into deep learning in an end-to-end trainable fashion, opening a path for topological invariants to serve as learnable components in graph representation pipelines. Key limitations include its dependence on node features (not purely structural), the gap between the differentiable approximation and the exact ECT's injectivity guarantees, and the introduction of several hyperparameters (directions, thresholds, smoothing). Open questions include formalizing LEAP's theoretical expressivity relative to MPNNs, making the ECT fully differentiable with learnable thresholds, and extending the approach to higher-order structures like simplicial complexes. The method is particularly well-suited for architectures relying on global attention (e.g., graph transformers) where graph structure is not directly modeled.
