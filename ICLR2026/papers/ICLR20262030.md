## Problem

Existing knowledge editing methods achieve high accuracy on structured triples but fail to generalize to unstructured, free-form knowledge, particularly on inherently hard-to-edit instances where the model's deeply embedded prior knowledge strongly resists modification. The paper identifies that this failure is not random but follows consistent patterns across methods and models, stemming from a misalignment between the intensity of editing perturbations and the degree of coupling between the target knowledge and the model's internal representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262030/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a knowledge pair (a question prompt and a target answer) along with the base LLM's parameters, and produces updated parameters at a single transformer layer (the 7th layer by default). TANGLESCORE is computed from two quantities extracted from the base model: the cosine distance between average-pooled hidden-layer representations of the original and novel knowledge (D_semantic), and the Sinkhorn optimal-transport distance between the model's pre-edit and post-edit output answers in embedding space. The resulting scalar TANGLESCORE drives both the purge rate and the imprint weighting, making the editing strength instance-specific rather than uniform.

## Method

PIPE is a two-stage gradient-optimization framework that first purges old knowledge, then imprints new knowledge, with both stages modulated by TANGLESCORE. In the purge stage, a gradient-ascent loss with a clipped absolute-difference term (bounded in [0,1] to prevent gradient explosion) pushes the model's key outputs away from the original key vectors, scaled by an adaptive purge rate PR = λ_max^(γ^TANGLESCORE) so that harder samples receive stronger forgetting. In the imprint stage, a consistency loss balances matching target key vectors (knowledge transfer) against preserving the original model's hidden-state outputs (stability retention), with the balance weight α = σ(TANGLESCORE) dynamically shifting emphasis toward transfer for difficult edits and toward preservation for easy ones. The two losses are jointly optimized end-to-end, and the entire pipeline operates on a single transformer layer, keeping the edit localized while the TANGLESCORE-driven adaptivity addresses the core failure mode of uniform-strength editing on heterogeneous knowledge instances.

## Results

On UNKEBench with LLaMA3-8B-Instruct, PIPE achieves a BERT-Score of 98.64/91.71 (original/paraphrased) and a FactScore of 50.91, outperforming the strongest unstructured baselines UNKE (97.41/90.06, FactScore 41.56) and AnyEdit (98.62/91.56, FactScore 48.48). The headline gain is a 6.49% average improvement in generalization (paraphrased-question) performance over prior state-of-the-art methods. On the structured KEBench, PIPE reaches 95.89% Ori-Acc and 88.23% Para-Acc, surpassing UNKE (93.59/85.34) and all structured-editing baselines (ROME 76.35/67.23, MEMIT 74.33/65.08). General ability (MMLU) is preserved at 29.51, matching the pre-edit score of 29.94.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Structured-editing baselines (ROME, MEMIT, RECT, AlphaEdit) are substantially outperformed by PIPE on all unstructured metrics, with BERT-Score gaps of 22–26 points on paraphrased questions and FactScore gaps exceeding 22 points, confirming that locate-then-edit and regularization-based methods do not transfer to free-form knowledge. Among unstructured baselines, PIPE's advantage over UNKE is concentrated in generalization: Rouge-1 (85.42 vs. 78.71), Rouge-2 (78.03 vs. 70.83), and FactScore (50.91 vs. 41.56) on LLaMA3-8B, while original-question scores are comparable. Against AnyEdit, PIPE leads on Rouge-1 para (85.42 vs. 78.62), Rouge-2 para (78.03 vs. 72.59), and FactScore (50.91 vs. 48.48), with BERT-Score para nearly tied (91.71 vs. 91.56). On the structured KEBench, PIPE improves Ori-Acc by 2.3 points and Para-Acc by 2.9 points over UNKE, while also recovering Src-Acc (94.47 vs. 89.28) and Tgt-Acc (70.11 vs. 62.56) that UNKE degrades. The consistent pattern across LLaMA3-8B, LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA2-13B, and Qwen2.5-7B confirms that PIPE's TANGLESCORE-adaptive purge yields the largest gains on high-difficulty samples (TANGLESCORE > 0.75), where baselines drop 40–50 points in BERT-Score but PIPE retains 65+.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the unstructured knowledge editing line initiated by UNKE (Deng et al., 2025) and AnyEdit (Jiang et al., 2025), which extended structured editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, RECT, AlphaEdit) to free-form text. Its key departure is introducing TANGLESCORE, a difficulty metric that quantifies the entanglement between target knowledge and the model's prior representations, and using it to adaptively modulate a two-stage purge-then-imprint editing process. The purge mechanism draws on gradient-ascent unlearning (Liu et al., 2025), while the semantic comparison leverages Sinkhorn optimal transport (Watanabe & Isobe, 2024) rather than the token-level KL divergence common in prior editing work. Conceptually, the paper reframes knowledge editing from a uniform-intensity operation to a difficulty-aware one, addressing a gap the authors identify in prior methods that treat all samples identically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TANGLESCORE provides a practical, pre-edit diagnostic that practitioners can use to anticipate which knowledge instances will resist editing and require stronger intervention, shifting the field from post-hoc failure analysis to proactive strategy selection. The finding that TANGLESCORE is intrinsic (invariant to the editing method and unchanged after editing) makes it a stable planning tool. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: PIPE does not yet support continuous sequential editing, is restricted to text, and has not been validated on models substantially larger than 13B. Open questions include whether the difficulty-aware paradigm generalizes to multimodal editing, whether TANGLESCORE can be computed more efficiently at scale, and whether the adaptive purge strategy transfers to parameter-preserving editing methods.
