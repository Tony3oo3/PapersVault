## Problem

Existing Transformer-based single-cell generative models treat genes as flat, independent tokens, ignoring higher-order biological structure such as gene modules and pathways. They also fail to model the full expression distribution (including zero inflation) and are not integrated into a unified generative framework capable of sampling under arbitrary, unseen combinations of multiple conditions (batch, cell type, disease stage, perturbation). This limits their ability to generalize to combinatorially held-out condition spaces and to produce biologically faithful virtual cells.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw scRNA-seq expression matrices (normalized to 10^4 counts, log-transformed, then max-absolute scaled to [0,1]) together with categorical condition labels (e.g., batch, cell type, disease, assay). Before entering the network, the ~19,000-gene expression vector is reshaped into L balanced gene blocks of size K=3200 via LLM-derived semantic embeddings and Optimal Transport clustering, producing a structured tensor X ∈ R^{N×L×K}. Outputs include generated expression profiles under user-specified condition combinations, integrated profiles for batch correction, and predicted perturbed expression profiles for unseen cell types or drug treatments.

## Method

SAVE integrates three components: (1) a VAE whose encoder and decoder are Transformer stacks operating over gene blocks rather than individual genes, with attention capturing long-range dependencies among semantically coherent gene modules; (2) a Conditional Flow Matching network that learns a time-dependent vector field mapping Gaussian noise to the VAE latent space via an affine probability path (linear interpolation), trained with MSE regression on the target velocity x₁−x₀; and (3) a condition-masking strategy that independently replaces each condition token with a [MASK] token at probability 0.6, forcing the model to learn transferable condition embeddings. Condition information is injected into every Transformer layer through Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN), where condition embeddings produce per-layer scaling and shifting parameters. At inference, Classifier-Free Guidance blends conditional and unconditional velocity predictions to control the trade-off between sample diversity and condition fidelity. The gene block construction itself uses text-embedding-ada-002 to embed NCBI Gene 'Summary' texts into 1536-d vectors, then iteratively solves a balanced Optimal Transport problem to assign genes into L non-overlapping blocks of equal size.

## Results

On dual-condition generation (Table 2), SAVE achieves the lowest Wasserstein Distance and MMD across all three datasets: Heart (WD 8.30, MMD 0.63), PBMC (WD 5.37, MMD 0.29), and Lung Atlas (WD 4.37, MMD 1.14), outperforming the next-best method by 2–6× on WD. On the multi-condition Lung Cancer benchmark with held-out unseen conditions, SAVE attains the best seen-condition WD (3.10±0.96) and the best unseen-condition WD (4.63±0.95), while matching scDiffusion on seen MMD (1.06). For perturbation prediction on PBMC-IFN-β, SAVE achieves an average PCC of 0.96 and R² of 0.86, surpassing all baselines including scGEN (PCC 0.94, R² 0.84) and CellOT (PCC 0.94, R² 0.80).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAVE dominates scVI across every task and dataset, reducing WD by 2–5× on dual-condition generation and achieving the highest scIB scores (0.81–0.83) in batch correction. Against CFGen, SAVE wins on all three dual-condition datasets (e.g., WD 8.30 vs 12.57 on Heart) and on the multi-condition Lung Cancer benchmark (seen WD 3.10 vs 16.94; unseen WD 4.63 vs 23.67), though CFGen is competitive on single-condition PBMC3K. Versus scDiffusion, SAVE achieves substantially lower WD on all dual-condition datasets (e.g., 4.37 vs 13.89 on Lung Atlas) and on seen multi-condition data (3.10 vs 5.27), but scDiffusion edges out SAVE slightly on unseen MMD (1.87 vs 2.11). In perturbation prediction, SAVE outperforms scGEN (avg PCC 0.96 vs 0.94; R² 0.86 vs 0.84) and CellOT (PCC 0.96 vs 0.94; R² 0.86 vs 0.80), while decisively beating trVAE, scDisInFact, and MFM. For batch correction, SAVE achieves the best scIB on Lung Atlas (0.81) and PBMC (0.83), narrowly trailing Harmony on Heart (0.80 vs 0.81), while Scanorama and trVAE remain competitive but consistently below SAVE on the composite score.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAVE carves new ground by repurposing the coarse-grained patch idea from masked image generation (MaskGIT) to the single-cell domain, where genes lack natural spatial locality. It departs from flat-token Transformer models (scGPT, scBERT, Geneformer) by grouping genes into semantically coherent blocks via LLM embeddings and Optimal Transport clustering, and from prior generative single-cell models (scVI, CFGen, scDiffusion) by unifying a VAE latent space with a Flow Matching decoder conditioned via AdaLN. The condition-masking strategy draws on masked modeling to enable extrapolation to unseen condition combinations, a capability that prior multi-factor methods (scDisInFact) handle only through rigid architectural disentanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gene block attention mechanism offers a practical path to scaling Transformer-based single-cell models: the 191× training speedup over per-gene attention makes large-scale multi-condition generation feasible on consumer hardware, while the attention heatmaps provide biologically interpretable evidence that the model learns meaningful functional modules. However, the reliance on NCBI text descriptions and an LLM embedding model introduces a ceiling for poorly annotated or newly discovered genes, and the unsupervised grouping is susceptible to historical literature bias compared to curated pathway databases (MSigDB, KEGG). Open questions include whether integrating structured knowledge graphs or gene regulatory networks with the LLM embeddings can close this gap, and whether the block-based formulation transfers to other omics modalities (ATAC-seq, proteomics) where the 'gene' unit differs.
