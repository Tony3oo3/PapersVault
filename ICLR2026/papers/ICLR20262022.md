## Problem

In model-driven lossless compression, the encoder and decoder must produce exactly matching probability predictions for each symbol, but non-determinism in modern hardware (e.g., different GPU architectures, floating-point rounding order) causes small logit discrepancies that cascade into complete decoding failures. This paper formalizes the prediction mismatch problem and introduces PMATIC, a model-agnostic coding algorithm that tolerates bounded prediction mismatch while incurring only a small compression overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262022/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a token string and an LLM predictive model, converting each token into a fixed-length bitstring (longform) of ⌈log₂(|A|)⌉ bits, then encodes pairs of (helper bit, token bit) via arithmetic coding to produce a compressed bitstring. The decoder takes the compressed bitstring and its own (potentially different-hardware) LLM, recovering the original token string. A key constraint is the bounded prediction mismatch parameter δ (conditional total variation distance) and bin radius r, which must satisfy r > 2δ; the encoder and decoder may run on entirely different machines.

## Method

PMATIC quantizes next-bit probabilities by partitioning [0,1] into uniform bins of width 2r. For each token bit, the encoder checks whether its conditional probability falls in the δ-interior of a bin (helper bit = 0, quantize to bin center) or near a bin boundary (helper bit = 1, quantize to the boundary); the helper bit is sent via arithmetic coding so the decoder can select the same quantized probability. This guarantees that both sides agree on a common probability for every bit, ensuring correct decoding whenever the conditional TV distance between encoder and decoder predictions is at most δ. The theoretical compression loss over standard arithmetic coding is O(√(δ log(1/δ))), achieved by balancing helper-bit entropy against quantization loss. PMATIC is model-agnostic and serves as a drop-in replacement for the arithmetic encoder in any model-driven compression pipeline.

## Results

On Enwik8, LLaMA 3.1 with PMATIC at the tightest robustness setting (δ=10⁻⁵, r=0.005) achieves a compression ratio of 0.0847, only 8.6% worse than the non-robust baseline (0.0780), while still beating cmix (0.3558) by more than 4×. Even at the most permissive setting (δ=0.1, r=0.125), PMATIC with LLaMA 3.1 reaches 0.2492 on Enwik8, substantially outperforming all traditional compressors (cmix 0.3558, gzip 0.4601). Across all three LLMs and six datasets, PMATIC at every tested robustness level maintains compression ratios well below the best traditional baseline (cmix), confirming that the robustness overhead is modest relative to the gains from model-driven prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is cmix, a state-of-the-art traditional compressor, along with standard tools (brotli, bzip2, xz, gzip, zstd). PMATIC wins decisively on every dataset and model: even the most robust setting (δ=0.1, r=0.125) with LLaMA 3.1 achieves 0.2492 on Enwik8 versus cmix's 0.3558 (a 30% improvement), and the gap widens further on other datasets (e.g., 0.2085 vs. 0.3644 on Wikipedia). The overhead of PMATIC relative to non-robust arithmetic coding is modest at tight settings—only 0.0067 absolute increase on Enwik8 for δ=10⁻⁵—but grows to roughly 0.17 at δ=0.1, reflecting the cost of tolerating larger prediction mismatch. Against traditional compressors, the model-driven pipeline (with or without PMATIC) dominates by a wide margin, with the LLM-based methods achieving compression ratios roughly 3–6× lower than gzip or zstd. The paper also validates robustness empirically: under real hardware non-determinism (Apple M2 Pro vs. M4 Max), standard arithmetic coding and PMATIC with δ=0.001 fail on all files, while PMATIC with δ=0.01 decodes all files correctly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-driven lossless compression framework (Cleary & Witten 1984; Pasco 1976) in which a predictive model is paired with arithmetic coding, by introducing PMATIC as a model-agnostic quantization layer between the model and the arithmetic coder. It directly addresses a practical gap identified in recent LLM-driven compression work (LLMZip, llama-zip, Delétang et al. 2024): standard arithmetic coding requires exact probability agreement between encoder and decoder, which is violated by GPU non-determinism. The paper also positions itself relative to concurrent work by Hu & Tang (2026) that tackles a related mismatch-tolerant variant via a Huffman-coding-like approach, and to efforts on deterministic inference backends (He & Lab 2025; Yuan et al. 2025) as complementary rather than competing strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PMATIC provides the first principled, model-agnostic mechanism to make LLM-driven lossless compression reliable across heterogeneous hardware, removing a key barrier to practical deployment of these promising compressors. The O(√(δ log(1/δ))) compression-loss bound is clean and the real-hardware validation (Apple M2 Pro vs. M4 Max) confirms the approach works in practice. Open questions include extending to stochastic (rather than worst-case) mismatch models, improving helper-bit probability estimation (Table 2 suggests substantial headroom), establishing fundamental information-theoretic limits, and generalizing to non-text domains such as images. The paper also notes that the fundamental limits of how much extra message length is mathematically required to correct a given mismatch remain unknown.
