## Problem

Probing frozen self-supervised audio encoders with a single global descriptor (e.g., the [cls]-token) systematically underperforms fine-tuning on multi-label AudioSet, leading the field to default to costly fine-tuning for SOTA. The paper identifies the root cause as a pooling bottleneck: MIM-style pretraining produces rich, localized token-level embeddings, but collapsing them into one vector discards per-source cues critical for polyphonic scenes, making linear probes unfaithful evaluators of embedding quality.

## Input / Output

The probe consumes a frozen encoder's token map of shape D×S_t×S_f (e.g., 768×64×8 for a 10-second 128-bin log-Mel spectrogram at 16 kHz, patched into 16×16 tiles) and outputs C multi-label class logits. The key design choice is that the probe operates on the full spatial token map rather than a single pooled vector, scoring each time-frequency token against learnable prototypes before aggregating. A final linear layer maps the J prototype scores to the C class logits, decoupling prototype semantics from class identity.

## Method

The paper introduces protobin, a binarized prototypical probe that maintains C·J class-agnostic learnable prototypes (J=20 per class) which are binarized on-the-fly via sign() with a straight-through estimator for gradient flow. Each frozen token is scored against every prototype via cosine similarity, and max-pooling over the spatial dimensions yields one scalar per prototype, forming a J-dimensional clip-level descriptor. The binarization forces prototypes to the corners of a D-dimensional hypercube, producing near-orthogonality as an emergent property without an explicit orthogonality loss, while yielding a 32× memory reduction. The final linear classifier learns to assign semantic meaning to each prototype's similarity score, enabling different prototypes to specialize in different sound events within the same clip. The paper further establishes a comprehensive benchmark across 13 datasets and 6 spectrogram-based ViT-base encoders, comparing 10+ pooling methods and demonstrating that protobin consistently outperforms linear, attentive, and prior prototypical probes by 12–14 percentage points in mAP.

## Results

Across 5 general multi-label audio datasets and 6 spectrogram-based SSL backbones, protobin (binarized prototypical probes) achieves the highest mAP in the majority of configurations, with an average improvement of +14.41%_p mAP over the standard linear [cls]-token probe and +12.16%_p mAP on few-shot bioacoustics. On the primary as20k benchmark, protobin closes 63% of the performance gap to full fine-tuning (e.g., 31.67 mAP vs. 40.2 mAP for EAT fine-tuning). The best attentive pooling method (mhca) still lags behind protobin by −4.59%_p mAP on general audio, confirming that single-vector aggregation remains a bottleneck even with learned attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Protobin consistently outperforms the standard linear [cls]-token probe by a wide margin (+14.41%_p mAP average on general audio), demonstrating that the cls-token is a poor proxy for embedding quality. Against the strongest attentive pooling baseline (mhca), protobin wins by approximately 4.6%_p mAP on general audio, with the gap widening on polyphonic datasets like urban and as20k where single-vector compression is most limiting. Compared to the class-dependent float-based prototype (proto), protobin is competitive overall but slightly trails in specific configurations (e.g., ASiT on urban: 82.28 vs. 82.35 mAP), while the architectural simplification (class-agnostic design, no orthogonality loss) provides a 32× memory reduction. On single-label control tasks (ESC-50, SC-2), mhca is often competitive with or superior to protobin, confirming that the multi-vector advantage is specific to polyphonic multi-label settings. Notably, the backbone ranking is completely reshuffled: ASiT drops from 2nd to last under protobin, while SSLAM (current fine-tuning SOTA) jumps from mid-tier to 2nd, revealing that linear probing systematically misranks models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends prototypical probing from vision interpretability (Chen et al., 2019; Donnelly et al., 2022) and prior bioacoustic work (Rauch et al., 2025a; Heinrich et al., 2025) to the broader audio SSL evaluation setting, introducing two key simplifications: class-agnostic prototypes and removal of an explicit orthogonality loss, replaced by binarization via a straight-through estimator. It also adapts attentive pooling methods from vision (Psomas et al., 2025; El-Nouby et al., 2024) as strong baselines for the first time in a systematic audio benchmark. The central conceptual departure is identifying a 'pooling bottleneck' specific to multi-label audio: the mismatch between MIM-style pretraining (which distributes information across tokens) and single-vector probing (which collapses it), a gap that prior audio SSL work had attributed to embedding quality rather than the probe itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that the cls-token probe is not just suboptimal but actively misleading as an evaluator (completely re-ranking backbones), this work challenges the field's default reliance on fine-tuning for AudioSet SOTA and provides a lightweight, faithful alternative. The 32× memory reduction from binarization makes prototypical probing practical for resource-constrained settings like bioacoustics monitoring. Open questions include whether multi-layer feature aggregation, on-the-fly data augmentations, or per-dataset tuning of the prototype count J could further close the remaining gap to fine-tuning, and whether the pooling bottleneck insight generalizes beyond spectrogram-based audio to other modalities with localized, multi-source signals.
