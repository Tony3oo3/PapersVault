## Problem

Decentralized composite optimization over networks typically follows a single-communication-round-per-iteration convention, and prior multi-consensus schemes that add repeated mixing steps have failed to reduce total communication cost in practice. This paper asks whether embedding multiple communication rounds directly into the algorithmic structure—rather than treating them as external averaging—can yield a net reduction in overall communication while accelerating convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260426/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of n agents holds a local loss f_i, a local regularizer g_i (or a share of a global regularizer), and a partition of the data; the network topology is encoded by a symmetric doubly stochastic mixing matrix W (e.g., Metropolis-Hastings weights). The algorithm outputs the consensus solution x* at which all agents agree, measured by suboptimality F(ū)−F(x*). Unusually, each iteration transmits only two d-dimensional vectors per agent per round (one primal variable and one carefully constructed dual combination), rather than full primal or dual state, keeping per-round payload minimal.

## Method

DS-ADMM reformulates the consensus condition u₁=…=uₙ as a symmetric pair of linear constraints (u = W̃v, v = W̃u), which makes the problem invariant under u↔v exchange and enables a balanced Symmetric ADMM iteration. A graph-aware proximal matrix Q = β((1+τ)I − W̃ᵀW̃) is chosen so that the augmented Lagrangian subproblems decompose across agents, yielding fully local proximal updates. Because the W̃ᵀW̃ term inherently requires 2-hop neighbor information, each iteration is structured into two update groups (Group 1 updating u and λ₂, Group 2 updating v and λ₁) separated by exactly two communication rounds, in which agents broadcast only a primal variable and a dual combination (a_i or b_i) to neighbors. This double-communication schedule is shown to be minimal for the symmetric ADMM structure, and the resulting algorithm achieves O(1/t) sublinear convergence generally and Q-linear convergence under metric subregularity of the KKT mapping, with the rate depending on the spectral gap of W.

## Results

The paper evaluates DS-ADMM on Lasso regression (a9a dataset) and ℓ₂-regularized SVM classification (a1a dataset) with n=30 agents on a random graph (p=0.5). Results are presented as convergence plots (suboptimality vs. iterations and vs. communication rounds) rather than a numerical table. The paper reports that DS-ADMM consistently converges faster in both iteration count and total communication rounds compared to all four baselines across both tasks. Additional experiments in the appendix extend to 50-agent ring topologies, 100-agent random graphs (p=0.2 and p=0.5), and random data partitioning, all confirming the same qualitative advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DS-ADMM outperforms all four baselines on both Lasso and SVM tasks in terms of convergence speed (iterations) and total communication rounds. Against Decentralized Proximal ADMM (Wang et al., 2018), which uses a single communication round per iteration, DS-ADMM's double-communication structure yields faster per-iteration progress that more than compensates for the extra round, resulting in fewer total communication rounds. PG-EXTRA (Shi et al., 2015b) and NIDS (Li et al., 2019), both gradient-tracking methods, are outperformed by DS-ADMM in both metrics, with DS-ADMM reaching target suboptimality in noticeably fewer iterations. ProxMudag (Ye et al., 2023), a multi-consensus accelerated method, is the closest competitor on Lasso but is excluded from the SVM task due to its requirement for globally coupled nonsmooth terms; even on Lasso, DS-ADMM achieves faster convergence in communication rounds. The paper's key empirical claim is that the double-communication design reduces total communication cost despite doubling per-iteration communication, a trade-off not achieved by prior multi-consensus approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the symmetric ADMM framework (He et al., 2016; Bai et al., 2018) and the decentralized ADMM formulation of Wang et al. (2018), but departs from both in a key structural way: it introduces a novel symmetric consensus constraint (u = Wv, v = Wu) that is invariant under primal-block exchange, enabling a balanced two-round communication schedule per iteration. This is conceptually orthogonal to prior multi-consensus approaches (Jakovetić et al., 2014; Ye et al., 2023; Li et al., 2018), which apply repeated mixing as an external enhancement to gradient-based updates. The paper also positions itself against the gradient-tracking family (EXTRA, PG-EXTRA, NIDS, SONATA) as a competing method family for decentralized composite optimization, arguing that embedding multi-communication into the constraint structure rather than the update rule is the principled path to reducing total communication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fixed two-round-per-iteration communication schedule can reduce total communication cost, directly contradicting the empirical finding in Ye et al. (2023) that fixed multi-round schemes in gradient-based methods fail to achieve this. This opens a new design axis for decentralized algorithms: the trade-off between per-iteration communication overhead and convergence speed. Limitations include reliance on 2-hop neighbor information (W^T W terms), a fixed (non-adaptive) communication schedule, and validation only on 30–100 agent networks with two datasets. Open questions include whether adaptive or topology-aware communication schedules could further reduce cost, whether the approach extends to non-convex or stochastic settings, and how it scales to thousands of agents in edge-computing deployments.
