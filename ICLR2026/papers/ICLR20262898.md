## Problem

Standard diffusion-based cross-modal image translation relies on a single global linear interpolation between source and target domains, forcing the sampler to traverse off-manifold, high-energy regions that belong to neither modality. This fixed-schedule domain transfer inflates the correction burden on the diffusion backbone, causing semantic drift, oscillatory updates, and blurred outputs—especially under highly nonlinear modality shifts such as SAR-to-optical or electroluminescence-to-semantic mapping. Existing acceleration tactics (higher-order solvers, distillation, ad-hoc guidance) either decouple the domain-shift physics from the generative dynamics or compress it into surrogates, failing to address the root cause.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 source-modality image (SAR, T1 MRI, or electroluminescence) as conditioning input and produces a corresponding target-modality image (optical, T2 MRI, or semantic defect mask) of the same spatial resolution. Both source and target are encoded into a 4-channel latent space via a KL autoencoder with 32× spatial reduction before entering the diffusion process. At inference, the source latent is injected at every reverse step through the learned mixing field, and the sampler can initialize at a middle timestep t₁ (with Λ≡1), effectively skipping the first T−t₁ denoising steps while preserving translation fidelity.

## Method

CDTSDE embeds domain-shift dynamics directly into the reverse-time SDE by predicting, at each step, a spatially varying and channel-aware mixing field Λ_t ∈ (0,1)^(C×H×W) via a lightweight convolutional network S_θ that takes the base linear schedule and sinusoidal position encodings. This field forms a per-pixel domain mixture d_t = Λ_t ⊙ x̂₀^src + (1−Λ_t) ⊙ x₀, which centers the forward marginals and injects an explicit target-consistent drift term √(ᾱ_t)·Λ̇(t) ⊙ (x̂₀^src − x₀) into the reverse SDE, keeping large integration steps on-manifold. The paper provides a continuous-time formulation with a closed-form solution via the variation-of-constants formula and derives a practical first-order sampler that decomposes each update into state transport, domain-shift drift, network-guided denoising, and stochastic components. Theorem 1 proves that under mild local heterogeneity and nondegenerate contrast, the spatially varying schedule strictly dominates any global schedule in path energy, providing a principled justification for the adaptive design. The full system is trained end-to-end with a StableDiffusion UNet backbone (base width 320) using noise-prediction MSE loss on paired data, with the domain-mixing module receiving a 10× higher learning rate.

## Results

CDTSDE achieves the best SSIM and PSNR across all three translation tasks: on Sentinel SAR→Optical it reaches SSIM 0.382 and PSNR 17.46 dB (vs. 0.36/17.14 for the runner-up DOSSR); on IXI T1→T2 it attains SSIM 0.762 and PSNR 23.32 dB; and on IXI T2→T1 it reaches SSIM 0.825 and PSNR 24.33 dB. On the PSCDE semantic-mask task, CDTSDE achieves the highest Dice (0.488), IoU (0.403), and Skeleton F1 (0.614) while attaining the lowest Hausdorff distance (39.87 px). In terms of efficiency, CDTSDE reaches PSNR ≈ 15 dB on Sentinel with only 5 sampling steps (1.8 s/image), roughly 2× faster than DOSSR (10 steps, 3.6 s) at comparable memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDTSDE outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across all three tasks. The closest competitor is DOSSR (Cui et al., NeurIPS 2025), which uses a fixed linear domain-shift schedule; CDTSDE edges it out by ~0.02 SSIM and ~0.3 dB PSNR on Sentinel, and by ~0.01–0.02 SSIM on IXI, while also being ~2× faster at matched fidelity (5 vs. 10 sampling steps). Pix2Pix (Isola et al., CVPR 2017) provides mid-tier pixel-level fidelity (e.g., PSNR 15.12 dB on Sentinel) but requires 125K training steps to reach comparable quality versus 10K for CDTSDE, and it degrades sharply on PSCDE (Dice 0.178 vs. 0.488). Among the diffusion-bridge baselines, DBIM (Zheng et al., ICLR 2025) is the strongest, reaching SSIM 0.28 on IXI T1→T2, yet it still trails CDTSDE by ~0.48 SSIM and suffers near-zero Skeleton F1 (0.004) on PSCDE. ABridge (Xiao et al., CVPR 2025) and BBDM (Li et al., 2023) perform markedly worse: BBDM is the weakest overall (SSIM 0.02 on Sentinel, Dice 0.024 on PSCDE), and ABridge largely fails to produce meaningful semantic translations on the fine-grained PSCDE task (Dice 0.009, Hausdorff 161.89).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variance-preserving diffusion framework for image-to-image translation by replacing the standard fixed linear interpolation between source and target domains with a learned, spatially varying, channel-aware mixing field (Λ_t) that is injected as an explicit drift term into the reverse-time SDE. It builds directly on DOSSR (Cui et al., 2025), which introduced domain-shift SDEs for super-resolution with a global schedule, and on bridge-based diffusion methods (BBDM, ABridge, DBIM) that construct stochastic paths between modalities but still rely on rigid or polynomial interpolation. The key conceptual departure is reframing the score network's role from global domain alignment to local residual correction, supported by a path-energy theorem (Theorem 1) showing that under mild heterogeneity, spatially varying schedules strictly dominate any global schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~2× sampling speedup at matched fidelity (5 vs. 10 steps) and the clean closed-form reverse SDE solution make this a practical option for deployment in medical imaging, remote sensing, and industrial inspection where both accuracy and inference cost matter. However, gains are modest on low-gap tasks (IXI: +0.01 SSIM), the method still requires paired training data, and GAN baselines can match or exceed perceptual quality. Open questions include criteria for when adaptive scheduling is beneficial versus simpler regressors, and whether the approach transfers to unpaired or few-shot settings where the domain-mixture construction loses its ground-truth anchor.
