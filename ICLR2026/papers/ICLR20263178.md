## Problem

Two dominant paradigms for understanding in-context learning (ICL) remain disconnected: component-level analysis localizes ICL to specific attention heads (e.g., induction heads) but only measures their importance via ablation without explaining how they function, while holistic decomposition factors ICL into Task Recognition (TR) and Task Learning (TL) via input perturbations but cannot trace these roles to concrete model components. The paper asks whether TR and TL can be localized to distinct sets of attention heads and whether their geometric mechanisms can be characterized, thereby unifying mechanistic precision with functional clarity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an 8-shot ICL prompt (demonstrations + query), the attention head outputs at the final token position across all layers, the model's unembedding matrix W_U, and the demonstration label set Y. It produces per-head TR scores (L2 norm of the head output projected onto the subspace spanned by label unembeddings) and TL scores (contrastive alignment along the average correct-minus-incorrect label direction within that subspace, normalized by the TR score), aggregated over 50 prompts to rank heads. Downstream, the identified heads yield task vectors for steering zero-shot predictions and geometric metrics (subspace alignment, logit difference) that quantify how each head type reshapes hidden-state geometry.

## Method

The core contribution is Task Subspace Logit Attribution (TSLA), which replaces raw Direct Logit Attribution with task-subspace geometry to identify TR and TL heads. The TR score is the L2 norm of the head output projected onto the subspace spanned by demonstration label unembeddings, capturing heads whose outputs concentrate in the task-relevant semantic space; a Grassmannian-geometry theorem guarantees that a large TR score implies with high probability the head projects most onto the task subspace among all r-dimensional subspaces. The TL score measures the head output's component along the average correct-minus-incorrect label direction within that subspace, normalized by the TR score, isolating heads that create discriminative contrast rather than broadly amplifying all label logits. Geometric analysis reveals that TR heads reduce the angle between hidden states and the task subspace (enabling label-space recognition), while TL heads perform pure rotations within the subspace toward the correct label unembedding (enabling correct prediction). The framework is validated through separable ablation using a novel TR ratio metric, input perturbations that independently disrupt TR or TL, and steering experiments demonstrating task-type-dependent effectiveness: TR-based task vectors restore classification accuracy in zero-shot settings, while TL-based vectors dominate in open-ended generation.

## Results

Ablating the top 3% TR heads collapses the Task Recognition Ratio (TR ratio) from ~100% to ~20% and causes a drastic accuracy drop, while ablating the top 3% TL heads lowers accuracy by ~30% but reduces the TR ratio by only ~10%, confirming separable control of the two ICL components. TR heads and Induction Heads (IHs) exhibit substantially higher Jaccard overlap, Kendall's τ, and Spearman's ρ at the top-3% level than either TR–TL or TL–IH pairs, and top IHs consistently rank higher in TR rankings than TL rankings (e.g., top 10% IHs ≈ top 20% TR heads but only top 50% TL heads). For zero-shot steering, TR-based task vectors consistently recover ICL-level accuracy on classification datasets, whereas TL-based vectors dominate in open-ended generation tasks (e.g., sentiment-controlled review generation), where they significantly outperform both TR-based and random vectors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Direct Logit Attribution (DLA) baseline from Lieberum et al. (2023), TSLA clearly wins on the ability to identify mechanistically distinct TR and TL heads: DLA-selected TL heads overlap heavily with DLA-selected TR heads (high Jaccard, Kendall's τ, and Spearman's ρ between the two DLA sets), and ablating DLA TL heads fails to produce the expected random-guessing behavior over the label space, whereas TSLA TL-head ablation does. DLA also cannot explain the well-documented importance of IHs, showing weak correlation between DLA heads and IHs, while TSLA TR heads show strong IH association. Against the IH framework of Todd et al. (2024), the paper does not claim to outperform IHs but rather subsumes them: TR heads and IHs show the highest overlap and correlation among all head-type pairs, and ablating IHs produces effects nearly identical to ablating TR heads (large accuracy loss driven by TR-ratio collapse), establishing IHs as a functional subset of TR heads specialized for label-space recognition rather than correct-label amplification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads in ICL mechanistic interpretability: the circuit-analysis paradigm (Elhage et al., 2021; Olsson et al., 2022; Lieberum et al., 2023) that localizes ICL to individual attention heads, and the holistic input-perturbation paradigm (Pan et al., 2023; Min et al., 2022) that decomposes ICL into Task Recognition and Task Learning without tying these to concrete components. The authors derive TSLA from the circuit formulation but depart from Lieberum et al.'s Direct Logit Attribution by projecting head outputs onto the task-label subspace and normalizing by competing-label contrast, yielding a metric with a formal probabilistic guarantee (Theorem 1). The framework also reinterprets prior findings: induction heads emerge as a subset of TR heads rather than a distinct mechanism, and task vectors (Todd et al., 2024; Hendel et al., 2023) are explained as TR-head outputs that restore task-subspace alignment in zero-shot settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unified, geometrically grounded account of ICL that reconciles conflicting prior observations (e.g., whether IHs recognize correct labels or merely the label space) under a single TR/TL framework, giving practitioners a principled way to diagnose and intervene on specific ICL failure modes. The geometric decomposition—TR heads align hidden states to the task subspace, TL heads rotate within it toward the correct label—offers an intuitive mechanistic picture that could guide future interventions (e.g., targeted steering for zero-shot or corrupted-demonstration settings). Open questions include whether the TR/TL separation holds for multi-step reasoning or chain-of-thought ICL, how it scales to much larger models where head specialization may blur, and whether the subspace assumption (semantically related labels sharing low-dimensional subspaces) degrades in very large or multilingual vocabularies.
