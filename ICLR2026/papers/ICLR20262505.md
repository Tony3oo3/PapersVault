## Problem

RL-based methods show strong potential for solving the NP-hard NFV resource allocation problem, but the field lacks a standardized, comprehensive benchmark for fair comparison and rigorous evaluation. Existing benchmarks (VNE-Sim, ALEVIN, SFCSim) are confined to cloud scenarios, implement only a handful of non-RL heuristics, and evaluate solely on online effectiveness, leaving practical dimensions like solvability, generalization, and scalability unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262505/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes a configurable pair of undirected graphs—a virtual network request (VN) with node/link resource demands and a physical network snapshot (PN) with available resources—along with topology, resource distribution, and service requirement settings (e.g., latency thresholds, heterogeneous CPU/GPU types, energy parameters). It produces a complete embedding solution (one-to-one node mapping plus shortest-path link routing) and reports four standard metrics (RAC, LRC, LAR, AST) plus the R2C solution-quality ratio. Uniquely, the framework also exposes offline static instances for solvability probing, varying-traffic and fluctuating-demand protocols for generalization testing, and scaled topologies for scalability analysis, all through a gym-style API that supports both RL training and one-shot evaluation.

## Method

Virne is a modular benchmarking framework built around five components: a customizable simulation configurator, an event-driven network system that treats each VN arrival as a discrete scheduling event, a unified algorithm pipeline, auxiliary utilities, and a multi-perspective evaluation suite. The core technical contribution is a standardized RL implementation pipeline that decomposes every NFV-RA algorithm into three swappable modules—MDP modeling (state/action/reward design), neural policy architecture (MLP, CNN, GCN, GAT, DualGAT, HeteroGAT, S2S), and training method (PG, A3C, PPO, MCTS, DQN)—enabling 30+ algorithms to be integrated and recombined with minimal code changes. The framework supports both the standard MDP formulation and extended variants (Constrained MDP for safe RL, Multi-task MDP for meta-RL). Extensive empirical analysis within Virne quantifies the impact of implementation choices (reward magnitude, feature engineering, action masking) and reveals that dual-GNN policies (PPO-DualGAT) with moderate fixed intermediate rewards and action masking achieve the best trade-off between solution quality and solving time across cloud, edge, and 5G scenarios.

## Results

On the WX100 topology (η=0.14), PPO-DualGAT achieves the best request acceptance rate (RAC) of 78.10% and long-term revenue-to-cost ratio (LRC) of 0.74, with a long-term average revenue (LAR) of 14,938.60 and average solving time (AST) of 0.18s. On GEANT (η=0.016), PPO-DualGCN leads in LRC (0.75) and LAR (791.75), while PPO-DualGAT achieves the best RAC (59.10%). On BRAIN (η=0.004), PPO-DualGAT again dominates with RAC 58.90%, LRC 0.75, and LAR 180.78. Across all three topologies, RL-based methods with dual-GNN architectures consistently outperform both traditional heuristics and meta-heuristics in solution quality while maintaining sub-0.3s solving times.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPO-DualGAT outperforms all 8 baselines on RAC, LRC, and LAR across WX100 and BRAIN, with the largest margins over RW-BFS (RAC 78.10 vs. 40.00, a 38-point gap) and MCTS (LRC 0.74 vs. 0.44, a 68% relative improvement). Against the strongest heuristic NEA-Rank, PPO-DualGAT gains +14.1 RAC and +0.08 LRC on WX100, while against the best meta-heuristic TS-Meta it gains +12.4 RAC and +0.08 LRC. The key trade-off is solving time: heuristics like RW-BFS (0.05s) and NRM-Rank (0.07s) are 3–4× faster than PPO-DualGAT (0.18s), while meta-heuristics (1.1–4.3s) and MCTS (3.0–3.6s) are 6–24× slower. On GEANT, PPO-DualGCN edges out PPO-DualGAT in LRC (0.75 vs. 0.72) and LAR (791.75 vs. 739.27), suggesting GCN-based dual architectures are more competitive on smaller, denser topologies. Overall, dual-GNN RL methods dominate in solution quality with acceptable latency, while traditional methods remain preferable only when sub-0.1s response is strictly required.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a unifying benchmark that supersedes prior NFV-RA simulators (VNE-Sim, ALEVIN, ALib, SFCSim, Iflye), which were limited to cloud-only scenarios, a handful of heuristic baselines, and effectiveness-only evaluation. Conceptually, it builds on the RL-for-combinatorial-optimization literature (PPO, A3C, GCN/GAT policies) by providing a modular gym-style pipeline that standardizes the MDP formulation, policy architecture, and training method into interchangeable components. It also extends the evaluation paradigm beyond online RAC/LRC metrics to include offline solvability, cross-condition generalization, and scalability analyses, drawing on evaluation practices from broader ML benchmarking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Virne addresses a critical reproducibility gap in RL-based NFV-RA research, where prior studies used non-public, inconsistent simulations making fair comparison nearly impossible. The empirical findings—particularly that dual-GNN policies (PPO-DualGAT) dominate across topologies and that adaptive reward normalization underperforms fixed moderate rewards—provide actionable design guidance. Open questions remain around the sim-to-real gap, scalability to extremely large physical infrastructures, and developing generalizable policies that transfer across heterogeneous network conditions without retraining.
