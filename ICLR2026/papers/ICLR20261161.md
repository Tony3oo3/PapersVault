## Problem

Tree-Sliced Wasserstein (TSW) distance is limited to p=1 because the closed-form solution for 1-Wasserstein on tree metric spaces does not extend to higher orders, yet p>1 metrics offer strict convexity and smoother gradients that are critical for stable gradient-based optimization. This paper bridges that gap by introducing a tree-sliced metric that is efficiently computable for all p≥1 while exactly recovering TSW at p=1.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261161/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two discrete probability measures (n samples each) in R^d or on the hypersphere S^d and outputs a scalar distance. Computation proceeds by sampling L random tree systems (each with k lines), projecting both measures onto each tree via a Radon transform with a splitting map, and evaluating a closed-form regularized Sobolev IPM on each tree. The overall complexity is O(Lkn log n + Lkdn), identical to first-order TSW for any p≥1, and the Monte Carlo estimate converges at rate O(L^{-1/2}).

## Method

TS-Sobolev replaces the 1-Wasserstein distance on each projected tree with a regularized Sobolev IPM, which constrains the critic function to a unit ball in a weighted Sobolev space (norm on the tree derivative with weight ŵ(x)=1+ω(Λ(x))). This regularized IPM admits a closed-form expression as a sum over tree edges with precomputable coefficients β_e, valid for all p≥1. The final distance is the p-th root of the expectation of the p-th power of the per-tree IPM over random tree systems. At p=1 the metric exactly recovers TSW (Db-TSW), and for p>1 the weight ŵ^{1-p} down-weights gradients near the root, focusing optimization on fine-grained leaf structures—a unique advantage over standard p-Wasserstein. The framework extends to the sphere (STS-Sobolev) by substituting spherical tree systems and the spherical Radon transform, recovering STSW at p=1.

## Results

On the 8 Gaussians gradient flow task, TS-Sobolev_{1.2} achieves a final Wasserstein distance of 8.88×10⁻⁷ at 2500 iterations, outperforming the strongest baseline Db-TSW (2.50×10⁻⁶) by a factor of ~2.8. On CIFAR-10 unconditional generation (DDGAN), TS-Sobolev_{2}-DD achieves a FID of 2.277, surpassing the best baseline Db-TSW-DD⊥ (2.53) by 0.253. For self-supervised learning on CIFAR-10, STS-Sobolev_{2} attains 80.6% encoded / 77.65% projected linear-probe accuracy, exceeding STSW (80.53% / 76.78%). In topic modeling, TS-Sobolev_{2} achieves the highest CV coherence of 0.805 on BBC and 0.497 on M10 in the Euclidean setting, while STS-Sobolev_{2} leads the spherical setting with 0.776 and 0.423 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TS-Sobolev consistently outperforms all SW-based baselines (SW, MaxSW, SWGG, LCVSW, RPSW, EBRPSW) and all TSW-based baselines (TSW-SL, Db-TSW, Db-TSW⊥) across every experimental setting. On gradient flow (8 Gaussians), TS-Sobolev_{1.2} beats Db-TSW by 2.8× and TSW-SL by 1.3× in final distance; on Gaussian 30d it leads Db-TSW by 0.38 in absolute distance. For DDGAN on CIFAR-10, TS-Sobolev_{2}-DD (FID 2.277) surpasses Db-TSW-DD⊥ (2.53) and all SW-DD variants (best: IWRPSW-DD at 2.70), while matching their training speed (~84s/epoch). In spherical SSL, STS-Sobolev_{2} (80.6%/77.65%) edges out STSW (80.53%/76.78%) and substantially outperforms SSW (70.46%/64.52%) and S3W variants. On spherical gradient flow (12 vMFs), STS-Sobolev_{2} achieves −3.616 log-Wasserstein at epoch 250, beating STSW (−3.549), ARI-S3W(30) (−3.270), and LSSOT (−2.598). In topic modeling, TS-Sobolev_{2} leads all Euclidean baselines on both BBC (0.805 vs. TSW-SL 0.796) and M10 (0.497 vs. EBRPSW 0.490), while STS-Sobolev_{2} dominates the spherical setting (0.776 vs. SSW 0.755 on BBC; 0.423 vs. SSW 0.408 on M10). The only setting where a higher p is suboptimal is Gaussian 30d gradient flow, where TS-Sobolev_{2} (3.68) underperforms TS-Sobolev_{1.2} (1.40), suggesting p=1.2–1.5 is the sweet spot for high-dimensional Euclidean tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: the Tree-Sliced Wasserstein (TSW) framework, which projects measures onto tree metric spaces to exploit closed-form 1-Wasserstein solutions (Tran et al., 2024d; 2025a), and the regularized Sobolev IPM on trees, which admits a closed-form expression for all orders p ≥ 1 (Le et al., 2025). The key conceptual move is recognizing that the regularized Sobolev IPM can replace the 1-Wasserstein term inside the tree-slicing expectation, thereby lifting TSW's restriction to p=1 without increasing computational cost. The spherical extension (STS-Sobolev) parallels the existing spherical TSW construction (Tran et al., 2025d), and the paper explicitly adopts the Db-TSW tree-system and splitting-map choices to ensure exact recovery of TSW at p=1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that higher-order metrics (p > 1) offer strict convexity and smoother gradients that facilitate more stable optimization in gradient-based learning, yet TSW was previously confined to p=1 due to the absence of a closed-form p-Wasserstein on trees. TS-Sobolev removes this bottleneck at zero additional computational cost, making it a drop-in replacement with a tunable p. A stated limitation is that the method does not handle unbalanced measures (different total masses), which remains an open direction. The consistent but moderate empirical gains across gradient flows, diffusion models, SSL, and topic modeling suggest the metric is a reliable upgrade rather than a transformative one.
