## Problem

Choosing data augmentation parameters (e.g., rotation angles, Mixup alpha, dropout rates) is typically done via trial-and-error or expensive black-box optimization requiring many full training runs. This paper asks whether DA parameters can instead be learned jointly with model parameters within a principled probabilistic framework, avoiding costly validation-based search while improving calibration and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263507/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard supervised learning inputs—RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet), token sequences (SST-5), or scalar features (synthetic regression)—and produces class labels or regression predictions. Unusually, it also outputs learned augmentation distribution parameters (e.g., mean and variance of a Gaussian shift, a Beta-distribution parameter for Mixup, or a dropout probability for text), which are themselves variational posterior parameters optimized alongside the network weights. For discrete augmentations like token dropout, the learned parameter is a scalar probability optimized via score-function gradients rather than reparameterization.

## Method

OPTIMA recasts data augmentation as Bayesian model selection by treating augmentation parameters φ as latent variables and defining a transformation-augmented likelihood p(y|x,θ,φ) = E_{p(γ|φ)}[p(y|T_γ(x),θ)] that marginalizes over transformations rather than replicating data. This avoids the evidence-overcounting problem of naive augmentation. A tractable augmented ELBO is derived with three terms: a data-fitting term averaged over the augmentation distribution, a KL penalty on model parameters, and a KL penalty on augmentation parameters, enabling joint stochastic optimization via reparameterization (or REINFORCE for discrete cases). The paper provides extensive theoretical analysis including PAC-Bayes generalization bounds showing OPTIMA's bound is strictly tighter than naive DA by a Jensen-gap term, higher-order invariance analysis showing the augmentation covariance regularizes model curvature, a posterior-shrinkage theorem quantifying how naive DA underestimates uncertainty by √K, and empirical Bayes optimality results. Experiments on CIFAR-10, ImageNet/ImageNet-C, and SST-5 demonstrate improved calibration (lower ECE), better OOD robustness, and competitive accuracy at a fraction of the computational cost of Bayesian optimization baselines.

## Results

On ImageNet with a Bayesian ResNet-50, OPTIMA consistently improves calibration over fixed augmentation across all three strategies: ECE drops from 0.043 to 0.031 (Mixup), 0.036 to 0.034 (CutMix), and 0.084 to 0.083 (AugMix), while maintaining or improving clean accuracy. On CIFAR-10, OPTIMA achieves 95.03% test accuracy and 0.680 OOD AUROC, outperforming Bayesian Optimization (93.43%, 0.652) at 4× lower computational cost. On CIFAR-10 with geometric augmentations, OPTIMA reaches 81.35% accuracy with an ECE of 0.017, compared to 0.092 for no augmentation and 0.088 for fixed augmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fixed Mixup, OPTIMA Mixup trades a small clean-accuracy drop (74.97% vs 75.39%) for a substantial ECE improvement (0.031 vs 0.043) and slightly better OOD AUROC (0.822 vs 0.820); on the non-Bayesian ResNet-50 setting (Table 1), OPTIMA Mixup actually wins on both clean (76.8% vs 76.1%) and corrupted accuracy (41.6% vs 40.1%). Against fixed CutMix, OPTIMA CutMix achieves marginally better accuracy (74.34% vs 74.17%) and ECE (0.034 vs 0.036). Against fixed AugMix, OPTIMA AugMix improves clean accuracy by 0.62 points (75.33% vs 74.71%) and reduces mECE from 0.156 to 0.149. Against Bayesian Optimization on CIFAR-10, OPTIMA wins decisively on accuracy (95.03% vs 93.43%), OOD AUROC (0.680 vs 0.652), and corrupted accuracy (78.52% vs 72.44%), while BO achieves a lower ECE (0.010 vs 0.047); however, OPTIMA completes training in roughly one-quarter the wall-clock time of BO.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPTIMA builds on the probabilistic perspectives of data augmentation introduced by Nabarro et al. (2022) and Kapoor et al. (2022), who formulated integral or tempered likelihoods for augmented inputs, and on the invariance-learning-via-marginal-likelihood line from van der Wilk et al. (2018) and Immer et al. (2022). However, those prior works either fix augmentation parameters or lack generalization guarantees. OPTIMA departs from the dominant DA-optimization paradigm (AutoAugment, population-based training, bi-level optimization, Bayesian optimization) by casting augmentation-parameter selection as a Bayesian model selection problem solvable via a tractable ELBO, jointly with model parameters, and by providing PAC-Bayes bounds that explicitly quantify the generalization advantage of marginalization over naive replication.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, single-training-loop alternative to expensive black-box DA tuning, with theoretical guarantees (tighter PAC-Bayes bounds, posterior-shrinkage characterization, higher-order invariance regularization) that prior DA-optimization methods lack. The finding that naive augmentation inflates effective evidence by a factor K, shrinking posterior covariance and degrading calibration, provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for a phenomenon practitioners observe but rarely formalize. Open questions include extending the analysis beyond last-layer Bayesian models to full-network stochasticity, exploring compositional or multimodal augmentation families, and tightening the PAC-Bayes bounds under weaker regularity assumptions than local Gaussianity.
