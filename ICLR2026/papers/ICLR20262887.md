## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) rarely admit ignorance and instead produce overconfident incorrect answers, with faithfulness hallucination rates rising even as reasoning capabilities improve. The paper identifies two pathological overthinking patterns—last-minute guessing (abruptly committing to an answer after inconclusive reasoning) and second-thought spiraling (undermining a correct answer through excessive re-analysis)—and shows that current RL paradigms fundamentally fail to reward refusal, incentivizing models to answer every question regardless of uncertainty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262887/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a factual QA question as input and produces a structured reasoning trace followed by either a boxed factual answer (for questions the model knows) or an uncertainty-aware refusal such as 'Sorry, I don't know' (for questions beyond its knowledge boundary). The reasoning traces follow a deliberate exploratory format: for known questions, the model anchors on the correct answer with evidence, examines alternative candidates, then reaffirms its conclusion; for unknown questions, it systematically evaluates plausible candidates, questions the evidence for each, and explicitly acknowledges the lack of sufficient support before refusing.

## Method

BARREL is a two-stage training framework. First, a knowledge labeling step uses multi-prompt sampling (K prompts × L repetitions) to classify each question as known or unknown to the target model. Second, SFT trains the model on GPT-4-constructed reasoning traces that explicitly correct the two pathological patterns: known-question traces anchor on the gold answer then explore distractors before confirming, while unknown-question traces explore candidates, apply adversarial self-critique to each, and conclude with a calibrated refusal. The critical innovation is the GRPO stage, which uses a three-level rule-based reward (r_c > r_s > r_w) where truthful rejections receive a medium reward distinct from both correct and incorrect answers. This medium-level refusal reward is shown to be essential—removing it causes the model to almost never admit uncertainty even when initialized from an SFT model with refusal patterns, revealing that the root cause of LRM overconfidence is the absence of any reward signal for saying 'I don't know' in standard RL training.

## Results

BARREL improves the factual reliability of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B from 39.33% (Distill baseline) to 61.58% while maintaining 40.70% accuracy—exceeding the Distill baseline's 38.43%. On Qwen3-8B, BARREL achieves 71.46% reliability and 50.50% accuracy, substantially outperforming the best post-hoc confidence baseline (Probing: 58.94% Rel.). The method also reduces the Wrong/Correct reasoning-token ratio from 1.33×–1.73× (base models) to ~1.02×–1.04×, confirming mitigation of factual overthinking. On MATH500, BARREL-trained models retain comparable mathematical accuracy (81.00% vs. 81.80% for Llama-8B; 86.80% vs. 85.60% for Qwen-7B), showing no degradation in non-factual reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Distill baseline (SFT on DeepSeek-R1 reasoning traces, Guo et al. 2025), BARREL improves reliability by 22.25 points on Llama-8B (61.58 vs. 39.33) and 26.91 points on Qwen-7B (53.12 vs. 26.21), while also matching or exceeding accuracy (40.70 vs. 38.43 on Llama-8B). Against Vanilla GRPO w/ Verbal Confidence (Tian et al. 2023), BARREL achieves 12.59 points higher reliability on Llama-8B (61.58 vs. 48.99) and 20.43 points higher on Qwen3-8B (71.46 vs. 49.03), with comparable accuracy. Against Vanilla GRPO w/ Probing (Orgad et al. 2024), BARREL gains 6.29 points in reliability on Llama-8B (61.58 vs. 55.29) and 12.52 points on Qwen3-8B (71.46 vs. 58.94), while maintaining higher accuracy (40.70 vs. 40.30; 50.50 vs. 41.63). The key advantage is that BARREL internalizes the accuracy–refusal trade-off through RL with a three-tier reward, whereas post-hoc confidence methods require aggressive threshold tuning and suffer from miscalibration (AUC near 0.53 for Probing), leading to either excessive abstention or persistent hallucination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of knowledge-boundary identification (Gekhman et al. 2024; Xue et al. 2024), abstention training for LLMs (Zhang et al. 2024a; Xu et al. 2024a; Stangel et al. 2025), and RL-based reasoning (GRPO from DeepSeekMath; DeepSeek-R1). It departs from prior abstention work, which has focused almost exclusively on non-reasoning models, by identifying two LRM-specific pathological reasoning patterns—last-minute guessing and second-thought spiraling—that cause factual overthinking, and by constructing reasoning traces that explicitly model the knowledge boundary. The three-tier reward design (correct > refusal > incorrect) extends the binary-reward RL paradigm used in standard GRPO, drawing on the observation that current RL frameworks fundamentally lack a signal for calibrated uncertainty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central insight—that the inability of LRMs to admit ignorance stems from a missing reward signal rather than a representational limitation—reframes a long-standing reliability problem as a straightforward incentive-design issue, which is actionable for any RL-trained reasoning model. The 22-point reliability gain (39.33% → 61.48%) on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, achieved without sacrificing accuracy, demonstrates that boundary-aware reasoning and factual correctness are not in tension. Limitations include evaluation on 7–8B models only, reliance on a sampling-based known/unknown labeling heuristic that may misclassify borderline cases, and a factual QA scope that leaves open whether the framework transfers to open-ended generation or multi-hop reasoning. A key open question is whether the SFT-then-GRPO pipeline and the medium-refusal-reward principle scale to frontier-scale reasoning models where the knowledge boundary is less sharply defined.
