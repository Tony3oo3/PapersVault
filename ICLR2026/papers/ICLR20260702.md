## Problem

Molecular dynamics simulations remain the gold standard for protein dynamics but are prohibitively expensive for proteome-scale applications, while existing explicit predictors (e.g., FlexPert3D, NMA) capture only scalar per-residue flexibility or rely on non-learned analytical approximations. No prior method simultaneously learns both local anisotropic fluctuations and residue-residue dynamic coupling in a data-driven, parameter-efficient framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260702/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a static protein structure (Cα coordinates encoded as local SE(3) residue frames) and the amino acid sequence as input. It outputs per-residue 3×3 SPD marginal covariance matrices and an N×N pairwise scalar covariance matrix; the mean of each Gaussian is fixed to the input structure's Cα coordinates rather than being predicted. From these two outputs, a full 3N×3N joint covariance is reconstructed via a Kronecker-product heuristic, enabling direct multivariate Gaussian sampling of conformational ensembles in ~0.14 s.

## Method

DYNAPROT comprises two models sharing an 8-block Invariant Point Attention (IPA) backbone from AlphaFold2's structure module, processing residue frames and sequence embeddings while maintaining SE(3) invariance. The marginal model uses an MLP readout to predict Cholesky factors of 3×3 SPD covariance matrices (enforcing positive definiteness by construction), trained with a log-Frobenius distance that respects the Riemannian geometry of the SPD manifold. The pairwise model concatenates residue embeddings, processes them through Evoformer-style pairwise attention blocks with triangle updates, and maps to scalar couplings that are Cholesky-parameterized into an N×N SPD matrix. A joint reconstruction heuristic combines the two outputs as Σ_joint = L_marginal (C̃ ⊗ I₃) L_marginalᵀ, provably preserving SPD structure. The full system uses only ~2.86M parameters and is trained on ~1,000 ATLAS MD-derived proteins without large-scale PDB pretraining.

## Results

On the ATLAS test set (82 proteins), DYNAPROT-M achieves a median RMSF Pearson correlation of 0.865 (75th percentile 0.930) and a median RMWD variance contribution of 1.18 Å with symmetric KL of 0.91, using only 955K parameters. The full DYNAPROT pipeline (marginals + pairwise coupling) generates ensembles with pairwise RMSD of 2.17 Å and per-target RMSF correlation of 0.86, matching AFMD+TEMPLATES on flexibility while sampling 70,000× faster (~0.14 s vs ~10,000 s) with 2.86M vs 95M parameters. Zero-shot on millisecond-scale BPTI dynamics, DYNAPROT achieves RMSF correlation of 0.88 and RMWD of 0.52 Å, demonstrating generalization beyond its 100 ns training regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlexPERT-3D (the only other explicit flexibility predictor), DYNAPROT-M achieves higher RMSF correlation (0.865 vs 0.830 median) while using three orders of magnitude fewer parameters (955K vs 1.2B) and without requiring NMA as input. Against NMA (ANM via ProDy), DYNAPROT dominates across all metrics: RMSF correlation (0.865 vs 0.697), RMWD (1.18 vs 1.45), symmetric KL (0.91 vs 4.56), and per-target RMSF correlation in ensembles (0.86 vs 0.76). Against AFMD+TEMPLATES (AlphaFlow), DYNAPROT matches or slightly exceeds flexibility metrics (pairwise RMSD 2.17 vs 2.18; RMSF 1.10 vs 1.17) but lags on distributional similarity (MD PCA W₂ 1.74 vs 1.25) and transient contact recovery (J=0.29 vs 0.47), while being ~70,000× faster and 33× smaller. Against sequence-based ensemble generators (ConfDiff, BioEmu, ESM3, ESMDiff), DYNAPROT outperforms on per-target RMSF correlation (0.86 vs 0.67–0.85), RMWD (1.18 vs 1.30–4.35), and sampling speed (~0.14 s vs 70–10,000 s), though ConfDiff and AlphaFlow lead slightly on MD PCA W₂ and transient contacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

DYNAPROT repurposes AlphaFold2's Invariant Point Attention backbone and Evoformer-style pairwise attention blocks, but redirects them from structure prediction to explicit dynamics descriptor prediction. It departs from generative ensemble methods (AlphaFlow, BioEmu, ConfDiff) that require large-scale PDB pretraining and iterative stochastic sampling, and from scalar-only predictors (FlexPert3D, DYNA-1) that discard directional and coupling information. The key conceptual departure is casting protein dynamics as a multiscale Gaussian problem—predicting per-residue 3×3 marginal covariances and N×N pairwise scalar couplings simultaneously—rather than generating full conformational ensembles or predicting a single flexibility scalar. The Cholesky-parameterized SPD output with log-Frobenius loss on the Riemannian manifold is a methodological contribution not seen in prior protein dynamics predictors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70,000× speedup in ensemble sampling and 3-orders-of-magnitude parameter reduction (955K vs. 1.2B) make protein dynamics estimation feasible at proteome scale, a regime where MD and generative methods are currently intractable. The zero-shot cryptic pocket discovery for adenylosuccinate synthetase hints at practical drug-discovery applications, though systematic validation is needed. Limitations include Cα-only modeling (no side chains), the joint covariance reconstruction being a heuristic rather than a learned quantity, and distributional similarity metrics (MD PCA W₂, transient contacts) still lagging behind AlphaFlow. Open questions include whether scaling training data beyond ~1,000 proteins closes the remaining gap to generative methods, and whether the Gaussian assumption adequately captures multi-modal conformational landscapes.
