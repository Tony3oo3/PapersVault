## Problem

Privacy-preserving inference for diffusion models via MPC or FHE is prohibitively slow, and while polynomial approximations of non-linear operations (SiLU, GeLU, exp) reduce latency, they introduce cumulative score errors that severely degrade image quality. The paper tackles the gap of enabling more aggressive (relaxed) approximations for further speedup without incurring the corresponding fidelity loss, rather than merely reducing approximation error itself.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt (Stable Diffusion, 512×512 output) or a binary class label (CipherDM on MNIST) that is encrypted or secret-shared before transmission to the server. The denoising process runs entirely under 2PC (CrypTen) or 3PC (SPU), with non-linear activations replaced by low-degree piecewise polynomials. The output is a standard RGB image decoded from the latent space via the VAE decoder; the unconditional generation path that provides the corrective score runs in plaintext and can be precomputed offline.

## Method

The core contribution is a score-guidance correction that exploits the fact that unconditional generation can be executed without any polynomial approximation, yielding a high-fidelity ε-prediction. Using an implicit discriminator and Tweedie's formula, the authors derive a correction term that steers the approximation-corrupted conditional ε toward the true score distribution: ε̃ = ε̂_cfg + γ_t(ε_unc − ε̂_cond), where ε_unc is the plaintext unconditional prediction and ε̂_cond is the approximated conditional prediction. The guidance strength γ_t = η·σ_t/ᾱ_t is scheduled to decay as the timestep approaches zero, preventing the unconditional signal from weakening prompt alignment in late denoising steps. Relaxed approximations (reduced polynomial degree, fewer piecewise segments) achieve 14–36% latency reduction, and the score guidance recovers most of the quality loss with negligible overhead since the unconditional score requires only a single subtraction, addition, and scalar multiplication per step.

## Results

On MSCOCO with Stable Diffusion under 2PC, Loose-Approx+Ours achieves FID 14.20 and CLIP-Score 0.3259, reducing the quality gap versus Precise-Approx from 2.24 to 0.98 FID (a 56.25% reduction in degradation) while retaining the 16.04% latency savings of relaxed approximations. On Flickr8k, Precise-Approx+Ours reaches FID 19.63, surpassing the vanilla plaintext model (FID 21.17). On binary MNIST with CipherDM under SPU, the method improves FID from 316.56 to 242.65 and F1-Score from 0.0820 to 0.6000, recovering near-legible digits from near-noise outputs. The additional latency overhead of score guidance is negligible (≤1.12%), since the unconditional score is precomputed in plaintext.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla Stable Diffusion plaintext baseline, Precise-Approx+Ours actually outperforms it on both MSCOCO (FID 12.45 vs 13.25) and Flickr8k (FID 19.63 vs 21.17), suggesting the unconditional score guidance also corrects minor stochastic deviations in the vanilla model. Against CipherDM on binary MNIST, the method yields a dramatic improvement: FID drops from 316.56 to 242.65 and F1-Score jumps from 0.0820 to 0.6000, recovering coherent digits where CipherDM produces near-noise. Against the test-time refinement method PAG (Ahn et al., 2024), Loose-Approx+Ours achieves FID 21.47 versus 36.72 for Loose-Approx+PAG, because PAG's degraded-sample strategy amplifies approximation errors. Against the test-time scaling method FKS (Singhal et al., 2025), Loose-Approx+Ours reaches FID 21.47 versus 24.42 for Loose-Approx+FKS, since FKS's verifier model is itself corrupted by approximations. In all four comparisons the proposed score guidance wins, with the largest gains observed where approximation errors are most severe (CipherDM on MNIST).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the secure inference pipeline for diffusion models established by CipherDM (Zhao et al., 2024) and the polynomial approximation schemes of BumbleBee (Lu et al., 2025), but departs from the standard strategy of reducing approximation error. Instead of making the polynomial approximations tighter, the authors accept larger approximation errors and correct the resulting score displacement at inference time by leveraging unconditional generation scores computed in plaintext. This reframing connects to score-based guidance methods (Hong et al., 2023; Ahn et al., 2024) that steer generation toward desirable samples, but differs critically in using an approximation-free unconditional score rather than degraded conditional samples, which would themselves be corrupted under MPC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is enabling 14–36% latency and 13–33% communication reductions in 2PC diffusion inference while recovering most of the quality loss from relaxed approximations, with negligible overhead from the guidance step itself. However, the absolute latency remains prohibitive (~3,700 s per denoising step), and large-model evaluation is conducted in plaintext due to resource constraints, so the method is not yet deployable in practice. An open question is whether the score-correction principle generalizes to other approximation-induced corruptions (e.g., quantization, pruning) beyond polynomial relaxation, and whether it can be combined with faster MPC protocols to close the gap to practical deployment.
