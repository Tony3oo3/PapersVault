## Problem

Traditional decision-focused learning (DFL) relies on single-point predictions and optimizes for average-case decision quality, making it inadequate in high-dimensional settings where the curse of dimensionality degrades point estimates, and in risk-sensitive applications where tail outcomes matter more than expected performance. Existing robust optimization alternatives use fixed, hand-crafted uncertainty sets that are overly conservative and fail to capture complex data-driven dependencies in the parameter space.

## Input / Output

The method takes tabular covariates x ∈ R^{d_x} and a risk level α ∈ (0,1) as input, where smaller α emphasizes more extreme tail outcomes. It produces an optimal decision vector w that minimizes the CVaR of the downstream optimization cost over the worst-α% of parameter realizations. The generative model is trained on paired (x, c) data where c is the uncertain optimization parameter vector, and at inference time K samples are drawn from the learned conditional distribution p(c|x) to solve the optimization via sample-average approximation.

## Method

Gen-DFL replaces the point-prediction predictor in standard DFL with a conditional normalizing flow (CNF) that learns the full conditional distribution p(c|x), enabling adaptive sampling from high-risk tail regions rather than relying on fixed uncertainty sets. The optimization is framed as a CVaR minimization (the 'generate-then-optimize' paradigm), solved via sample-average approximation over K generated samples. Training uses a combined loss that jointly minimizes a CVaR-based decision regret (computed against a frozen proxy distribution q(c|x)) and a generative negative log-likelihood regularization term, with a surrogate contrastive loss to handle non-differentiability through combinatorial optimization mappings. Theoretically, the paper proves that Gen-DFL's regret advantage over Pred-DFL grows with parameter variance, problem dimensionality, and risk sensitivity (smaller α), as the bias of point predictors scales as O(√((d_x+d_c)/n)/α) while the generative model captures the full distributional structure.

## Results

The primary metric is average relative regret (lower is better) evaluated under high-variance settings (σ=20). Gen-DFL achieves 3.59% regret on Portfolio Deg-8 (vs. 6.98% for SPO+ and 8.65% for Diff-DRO), 13.36% on Shortest-Path Deg-8 (vs. 81.78% for SPO+), and 1.09% on the Energy scheduling task (vs. 1.56% for SPO+). The advantage is most dramatic in high-dimensional, non-linear settings, where Gen-DFL reduces regret by up to 83.7% over SPO+ and 58.5% over Diff-DRO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gen-DFL consistently outperforms all Pred-DFL baselines (Pairwise, Listwise, NCE, MAP, SPO+) across Portfolio and Shortest-Path tasks, with the largest gaps in high-degree (Deg-8) settings where SPO+ degrades to 81.78% regret on Shortest-Path while Gen-DFL maintains 13.36%. Against Diff-DRO (the strongest baseline), Gen-DFL wins on Portfolio (3.59 vs. 8.65 at Deg-8), Shortest-Path (13.36 vs. 39.81 at Deg-8), and Energy (1.09 vs. 1.49), but is slightly worse on COVID Resource Allocation (16.86 vs. 16.41) and Knapsack Deg-6 (17.91 vs. 17.51). The two-stage PTO baseline is dominated by Gen-DFL on every task, with gaps exceeding 10 percentage points on Portfolio and Shortest-Path. On Knapsack, Gen-DFL's improvements over SPO+ and Diff-DRO are more moderate (10–20%), suggesting generative modeling benefits are most pronounced when uncertainty is highly non-linear or high-dimensional interactions dominate. Statistical significance tests confirm Gen-DFL's superiority over SPO+ and 2Stage on Portfolio (p<0.0001) and Shortest-Path (p<0.0001), though Knapsack differences are not statistically significant (p>0.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decision-focused learning (DFL) lineage—initiated by Donti et al. (2017) and formalized by Elmachtoub & Grigas (2017, 2022) with SPO+—by replacing the point-prediction paradigm (Pred-DFL) with a generative model that captures the full conditional distribution p(c|x). It also departs from classical robust optimization, which relies on hand-crafted or learned fixed uncertainty sets (Bertsimas & Thiele 2004; Wang et al. 2025; Chenreddy & Delage 2024), by treating uncertainty as a learnable distribution and optimizing CVaR rather than a hard min-max. The 'generate-then-optimize' (GTO) paradigm and the CVaR-based regret loss are the paper's core conceptual departures, while the surrogate contrastive loss for backpropagating through combinatorial optimization draws on Mulamba et al. (2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Gen-DFL provides a principled, tunable bridge between robust optimization (α→0) and expected-cost optimization (α→1), addressing two acknowledged weaknesses of Pred-DFL: degradation in high-dimensional, high-variance settings and inability to model tail risk. The theoretical regret bound (Theorem 5.4) gives clear, interpretable conditions—variance, dimensionality, and risk level—under which the generative approach dominates, which is useful for practitioners deciding when to adopt the added complexity. However, the 5–20× training overhead, the modest gains on Knapsack, and the mixed results on the COVID resource allocation task (where Gen-DFL does not clearly beat Diff-DRO or NCE) suggest the framework's practical advantage is concentrated in specific high-variance, high-dimensional regimes. Open questions include scaling to larger combinatorial problems, integrating diffusion-based generative models, and reducing the SAA sample cost.
