## Problem

When pre-trained backbones are incrementally adapted or merged across tasks in class-incremental learning, the frozen task-specific classifiers become misaligned with the updated backbone, causing severe performance drops on earlier tasks. Existing methods either restrict adaptation to the first task (which degrades as distributions diverge) or update the backbone while leaving classifiers fixed, creating an unaddressed mismatch that no prior work explicitly corrects.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images processed by a ViT-B/16-IN1K backbone with LoRA (rank 64) PEFT modules, and produces class labels via a set of task-specific MLP heads whose outputs are concatenated at inference. During the alignment phase, no real data is needed: synthetic feature vectors are drawn from per-class Gaussian distributions (mean and covariance stored from training) to retrain all classifiers jointly, requiring only O(n) extra storage for class statistics.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: (1) Incremental Merging (IM), which after each task subtracts the base PEFT vector, selects parameters by largest absolute magnitude (a simplified TIES-Merge without trimming), and adds the result back to the base, keeping successive solutions close in parameter space; (2) Local Classifier Alignment (LCA), a novel loss that jointly optimizes all task-specific classifiers using synthetic Gaussian samples, minimizing both the per-class expected loss and a sensitivity term λ·E[|ℓ(z)−ℓ(z′)|] that penalizes unstable predictions around class prototypes. Theoretically, the paper decomposes test error into a total-variation term (backbone drift), a class-wise loss, and a robustness term, showing that LCA directly controls the latter two. The sensitivity term also reduces inter-class overlap by suppressing the influence of outlier Gaussian samples that fall near other class prototypes.

## Results

Using ViT-B/16-IN1K as the pretrained backbone with LoRA (rank 64), IM+LCA achieves an overall average accuracy of 85.6% across seven CIL benchmarks, surpassing the strongest baseline MOS (83.9%) by 1.7 points. The method attains the best result on five of seven datasets, with particularly large margins on ImageNet-A (75.0% vs. 67.6% for MOS) and VTAB (95.2% vs. 92.4% for MOS). Adding LCA to the Incremental Merging backbone alone (IM, 80.9%) yields a 4.7-point overall gain, confirming the alignment step as the primary source of improvement. On robustness benchmarks, LCA provides over +2% mean accuracy on CIFAR-100-C and +2.5% on CIFAR-100-P compared to IM without alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IM+LCA outperforms all seven baselines on overall average accuracy (85.6%), with the closest competitor being MOS (83.9%), which itself relies on complex inference-time adapter selection. The gap is most pronounced on ImageNet-A, where IM+LCA scores 75.0% versus 67.6% for MOS and a highly unstable 45.1% for SLCA. MOS retains an edge on CUB (92.3% vs. 90.8%) and OmniBenchmark (86.1% vs. 81.4%), likely due to its dynamic backbone adapter selection at test time. Among prompt-based methods, APER+SSF (77.5%) is the strongest but still trails IM+LCA by 8.1 points overall, while CODA-Prompt (65.1%) and DualPrompt (71.9%) lag substantially, particularly on fine-grained datasets like CARS. EASE (79.1%) and L2P (71.9%) are consistently outperformed across nearly all datasets, with EASE closest on IN-R (82.4% vs. 85.8%) but far behind on IN-A (67.8% vs. 75.0%). The ablation further shows that LCA can be plugged into SLCA and MOS as a drop-in replacement for their alignment losses, yielding consistent gains without requiring their original inference mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of pre-trained-model-based continual learning and model merging. It builds on the incremental backbone evolution line (EASE, MOS, SLCA) and the model merging literature (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merge, MagMax), but departs in two key ways: (1) it restricts merging to PEFT parameters only, showing that simple magnitude-based selection (even Min/Max without trimming) suffices in this restricted setting, and (2) it introduces the LCA loss to resolve the classifier-backbone mismatch that prior merging-based CIL methods leave unaddressed. The theoretical analysis draws on classical generalization bounds and total variation distance to decompose CIL test error into distribution shift, class-wise loss, and robustness components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The classifier-backbone mismatch is a practical bottleneck in any CIL pipeline that evolves the backbone while freezing old classifiers, and LCA offers a lightweight, plug-and-play fix (demonstrated on SLCA and MOS) that also improves robustness under corruption and perturbation. The theoretical decomposition gives practitioners a principled diagnostic: if TV(P_t, P̂_t) is large, the backbone is forgetting; if ε̄ is large, the classifier is fragile. Key limitations include the Gaussian feature assumption, the theory's inapplicability when prototypes themselves shift during training, and the unexplored question of whether LCA integrates into end-to-end backbone training rather than a post-hoc alignment step.
