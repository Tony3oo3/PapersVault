## Problem

Decay-based learning rate schedulers (Cosine, Linear, WSD) are the de facto standard in LLM pre-training because they minimize pre-training loss, yet their effect on downstream supervised fine-tuning (SFT) performance remains unexplored. Prior work has shown that stronger pre-training performance does not guarantee better post-SFT performance, raising the question of whether LR decay—chosen to optimize pre-training metrics—actually harms the final model's adaptability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262315/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes large-scale text corpora for pre-training (FineWeb-Edu, olmo-mix-1124) and a curated instruction-following dataset (Tulu-3 SFT mixture) for fine-tuning, producing Llama-3-architecture transformer models at 1B and 8B parameter scales. Evaluation is performed at three distinct stages—after pre-training, after mid-training, and after SFT—using a broad suite of zero-shot and prompted benchmarks (ARC, HellaSwag, MMLU, GSM8K, DROP, AlpacaEval, TruthfulQA, AGI Eval, BigBench-Hard), with the primary comparison metric being SFT Task Avg relative to the best decay-based scheduler.

## Method

The paper introduces Warmup-Stable-Only (WSO), a simple LR scheduler that removes the decay phase from WSD by setting the minimum LR factor α_pre=1.0, maintaining a constant learning rate from warmup through the end of training. Through systematic experiments on 1B and 8B models across standard pre-training, mid-training, and over-training (2T tokens) regimes, the authors demonstrate a consistent inversion: decay-based schedulers achieve lower pre-training loss and better zero-shot task scores, yet WSO yields superior SFT performance by 0.3–1.4 points on average. To explain this, they compute the Hessian trace (sharpness) via Hutchinson's stochastic estimator throughout pre-training and show that decay-based schedulers drive models into 2–3× sharper minima, while WSO preserves flatter regions. A negative Pearson correlation (r=−0.709) between pre-training sharpness and SFT performance confirms that flatter minima confer greater adaptability to the distribution shift introduced by fine-tuning.

## Results

Across 1B and 8B Llama-3-architecture models, Warmup-Stable-Only (WSO, α_pre=1.0) consistently achieves the best SFT Task Avg (average of AlpacaEval, TruthfulQA, MMLU) while having the worst pre-training validation loss. For the 1B model, WSO scores +0.3 on SFT Task Avg relative to the best decay-based scheduler (WSD α_pre=0.0), despite being +0.071 worse in PT validation loss and −1.7 worse in PT Task Avg. For the 8B model, the gap widens: WSO gains +1.1 on SFT Task Avg over the best decay-based scheduler while being +0.127 worse in PT validation loss. This inversion persists in mid-training (Table 2: +0.8/+1.1 SFT gain) and over-training with 2T tokens (Table 3: +0.7 SFT gain) regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WSO outperforms all three decay-based baselines on SFT Task Avg across both model sizes: it beats WSD (Hu et al., 2024) by +0.3 (1B) and +1.1 (8B) points, beats Cosine (Touvron et al., 2023) by +0.3 to +1.1 points, and beats Linear (Bergsma et al., 2025) by +0.3 to +1.1 points. However, WSO consistently loses on pre-training metrics: it has the highest validation loss (+0.071 for 1B, +0.127 for 8B) and the lowest PT Task Avg among all schedulers. The advantage of WSO over WSD is most pronounced at the 8B scale (+1.1 SFT points), and it holds even when WSD uses the most aggressive decay (α_pre=0.0), which achieves the best pre-training loss. In the three-stage mid-training setting (Table 2), WSO throughout both stages (α_pre=1.0, α_mid=1.0) beats the best decay-based configuration (WSD α_pre=1.0, α_mid=0.0) by +0.8 (1B) and +1.1 (8B) on SFT Task Avg, confirming that decay at any stage harms downstream adaptability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) scheduler introduced in MiniCPM (Hu et al., 2024) and subsequent LLM training studies by removing the decay phase entirely, yielding Warmup-Stable-Only (WSO). It departs from the dominant paradigm in which LR schedules (Cosine, Linear, WSD) are chosen to minimize pre-training loss, as advocated by Bergsma et al. (2025) and Wen et al. (2025), by arguing the correct objective is post-training (SFT) performance rather than intermediate-stage metrics. The mechanistic explanation draws on the loss-landscape/sharpness literature (Ju et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023; Wen et al., 2025), connecting the river-valley dynamics of WSD to downstream adaptability, and builds on recent findings (Sun & Dredze, 2025; Springer et al., 2025) that pre-training quality does not reliably predict fine-tuning outcomes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LR decay at any stage (pre-training or mid-training) consistently degrades SFT performance provides a simple, actionable recommendation for LLM training pipelines: use WSO and release models without decay to maximize downstream adaptability. This reframes a fundamental hyperparameter decision that affects every LLM training run and model release strategy. Limitations include evaluation only at 1B and 8B scale (no 70B+ confirmation), modest effect sizes (0.3–1.4 points on SFT Task Avg), and a sharpness analysis restricted to the 1B model with limited sample size for the correlation. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at frontier scale, whether it interacts with other post-training stages (RLHF, DPO), and whether the flat-minima benefit can be further exploited through optimizer design.
