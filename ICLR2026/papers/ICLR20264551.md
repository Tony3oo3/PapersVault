## Problem

The diffusion model training loss has an unknown, non-zero optimal value that acts as an irreducible floor, making it impossible to tell from the loss alone whether a model has converged to its best possible fit or still has room for improvement. This unknown baseline obscures principled diagnosis of training quality across noise scales, prevents a data-driven design of training schedules, and biases scaling-law analysis because the raw loss conflates model capacity with an irreducible data-dependent constant.

## Input / Output

The optimal loss estimator is entirely training-free: it consumes only the raw training dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32, ImageNet-64 at 64×64, ImageNet-256/512 in latent space) together with the chosen diffusion process parameters (α_t, σ_t) and prediction target, and produces a per-noise-scale optimal loss curve J*_σ without ever training a model. The downstream training schedule output consists of a closed-form loss weight w_σ (Eq. 13) and an adaptive noise schedule p(σ) ∝ w_σ(J_σ(θ) − J*_σ) that is updated during training. For scaling-law analysis, the output is a modified power-law fit J(F) − J* = βF^α with correlation coefficient ρ.

## Method

The paper first derives the optimal loss in closed form (Theorem 1): for x₀-prediction under the VE process, J*_t = E‖x₀‖² − E‖E[x₀|x_t]‖², which equals the average conditional variance and depends only on the dataset and noise schedule, not on model architecture. To estimate the intractable second term B_t on large datasets, the authors develop the corrected DOL (cDOL) estimator, which uses self-normalized importance sampling over a random data subset of size L ≪ N and introduces a down-weighting coefficient C on the artificially correlated (x_t, x₀) pair to balance bias and variance; Theorem 2 proves consistency as M→∞ and C→∞. Using the estimated J*_σ, the paper designs a principled training schedule: the loss weight w_σ = a·min{1/J*_σ, w*} + f(σ)·𝕀_{σ<σ*} normalizes loss across noise scales and adds a Gaussian bump f(σ) concentrated in the region left of the critical noise scale σ* where loss gap positively correlates with FID. The adaptive noise schedule p(σ) ∝ w_σ(J_σ(θ) − J*_σ) dynamically allocates more optimization steps to underfit regions. Finally, the paper shows that subtracting J* from the training loss yields a significantly better power-law fit (ρ = 0.99 vs. 0.82) for EDM2 models from 120M to 1.5B parameters, providing a more principled scaling-law formulation for diffusion models.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed optimal-loss-based training schedule improves EDM's FID from 1.79 to 1.75 (conditional) and 1.98 to 1.94 (unconditional), and improves Flow Matching's FID from 6.35 to 2.03 (unconditional). On ImageNet-64, EDM's FID drops from 2.44 to 2.25 and FM's from 14.45 to 2.29 (a 25% relative improvement). On ImageNet-256, applying the schedule to LightningDiT reduces FID from 2.17 to 2.08 (w/o CFG) and 1.35 to 1.30 (w/ CFG), a 9% relative gain. For the scaling law study, subtracting the optimal loss from the training loss improves the power-law correlation coefficient from ρ=0.82 to ρ=0.94 at a fixed noise scale, and achieves ρ=0.9917 for the total loss across model sizes from 120M to 1.5B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed schedule outperforms all 21 baselines across every dataset and metric reported. On CIFAR-10, it beats EDM (1.79→1.75 cond., 1.98→1.94 uncond.), EDM2's schedule (1.94/2.09), ScoreSDE (2.20), VDM (2.49), ImprovedDDPM (2.94), and StyleGAN (2.42/2.92), while dramatically improving FM from 6.35 to 2.03 FID. On ImageNet-64, it surpasses EDM (2.44→2.25), 2-Rectified Flow (2.92→2.29), VDM (3.40→2.29), and ImprovedDDPM (3.54→2.29). On ImageNet-256, the schedule applied to LightningDiT achieves FID 2.08 (w/o CFG) and 1.30 (w/ CFG), beating LightningDiT's original schedule (2.17/1.35), SiD2 (1.38 w/ CFG), REPA (1.42 w/ CFG), MDTv2 (1.58 w/ CFG), MDT (1.79 w/ CFG), FasterDiT (2.03 w/ CFG), SiT (2.06 w/ CFG), VDM++ (2.12 w/ CFG), SimpleDiffusion (2.12 w/ CFG), DiT (2.27 w/ CFG), MaskDiT (2.28 w/ CFG), RIN (3.42 w/o CFG), and ADM (3.94 w/ CFG). The paper also demonstrates that the loss-gap-based scaling law formulation (subtracting optimal loss) yields a tighter power-law fit (ρ=0.9917) compared to the conventional raw-loss formulation (ρ=0.82), validating the principled correction for diffusion model scaling analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on optimal solutions of diffusion models (Bao et al., 2022a,b; Gu et al., 2023) by deriving the optimal loss in closed form under a general continuous-time formulation and, crucially, developing practical training-free estimators (cDOL) that scale to large datasets via self-normalized importance sampling with a bias-variance correction. It departs from prior training schedule design (EDM, Kingma & Gao, SD3), which relied on analyzing loss *scale* across noise steps, by instead using the *gap* to the optimal loss as the principled signal for where the model underfits. The unified preconditioning framework (Eq. 12) that converts all mainstream diffusion formulations (DDPM, EDM, NCSN, FM, SD3) into x₀-prediction under the VE process provides a common ground for cross-formulation comparison that prior works lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The optimal loss estimator gives practitioners a cheap, generation-free diagnostic for absolute data-fitting quality, removing the need to run expensive samplers to assess whether a model has converged or is underfitting. The discovery that loss gap around the critical noise scale σ* is *negatively* correlated with FID challenges the intuitive assumption that minimizing loss everywhere is beneficial, and directly motivates the proposed training schedule that yields consistent FID improvements (2–25%) across EDM, FM, and LightningDiT. For scaling law research, subtracting the optimal loss offset substantially improves power-law fit (ρ: 0.82→0.94 at a single noise scale; 0.9917 for total loss), suggesting prior diffusion scaling analyses may have been biased. Open questions include whether the adaptive noise schedule can be made fully online during training, whether the approach transfers to video or 3D generation, and whether the σ* trade-off persists under classifier-free guidance or different sampler choices.
