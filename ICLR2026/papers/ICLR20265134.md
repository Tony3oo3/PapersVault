## Problem

Existing face editing methods in diffusion models lack precise, attribute-level control: introducing a target attribute during denoising can inadvertently alter identity, non-target facial features, or background, and in video these errors accumulate across frames causing temporal inconsistency. The paper addresses this by asking how to confine edits to a specific semantic axis in the latent space while provably preserving orthogonal identity components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265134/cropped_page26_idx44.webp)

## Input / Output

For image editing, the method takes a single face image (processed at Stable Diffusion v1.4 resolution) along with a source prompt and a target prompt (e.g., 'a person without sunglasses' → 'a person with sunglasses'), and outputs the edited image. For video editing, it takes 32 consecutive frames from a face video at 256×256 resolution, encoded by a fine-tuned diffusion autoencoder (DVA architecture), plus CLIP-encoded editing conditions, and outputs the edited 32-frame sequence. Both modalities use 50-step DDIM inversion and denoising, and the method is training-free at inference time (the video autoencoder is pre-fine-tuned on HDTF).

## Method

FlowGuide operates a dual-path denoising pipeline (reconstruction with original conditions, editing with target conditions) and introduces two modules that exploit the local linearity of the UNet bottleneck latent space. The Latent Basis Extraction (LBE) module computes the Jacobian J_C of the bottleneck latent H with respect to the editing condition C, then performs SVD to obtain orthogonal right-singular vectors that span the semantic subspace most responsive to the target attribute, yielding basis sets V^r and V^c for the two paths. The Implicit Basis Guidance (IBG) module computes the cosine similarity ξ between V^r and V^c at each denoising step, then uses (1−ξ) as a dynamic quantile threshold on |ε^c − ε^r| to construct a binary spatial mask that blends editing noise with reconstruction noise—allowing larger deviations where the latent bases diverge (target attribute regions) and suppressing them where they align (identity regions). This adaptive, geometry-driven guidance replaces the fixed edit-friendly guidance with a per-step, per-region control signal that naturally extends from single images to temporally coherent video editing.

## Results

On face video editing (HDTF), FlowGuide achieves the highest Identity Preservation Rate (IPR) of 0.9667 and near-perfect temporal consistency (TL-ID 1.0001, TG-ID 1.0000), outperforming the strongest baseline DVA (IPR 0.9244) and TCSVE (IPR 0.9413). On face image editing (CelebA), FlowGuide (Cosine) attains PSNR 23.16, SSIM 0.8448, and DINO distance 0.0166, achieving the best balance between edit quality and identity preservation among all baselines. On the PIE-Bench general editing benchmark, FlowGuide achieves PSNR 22.02 and DINO distance 0.0349, second only to the highly conservative PnP Inversion while maintaining competitive CLIP similarity (26.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowGuide outperforms all seven video editing baselines on Identity Preservation Rate, achieving 0.9667 on HDTF versus DVA (0.9244), TCSVE (0.9413), STIT (0.8214), and StyleCLIP (0.8013). It also attains the highest CLIP-Score (0.7777) and perfect temporal consistency (TL-ID/TG-ID ≈ 1.0), surpassing DVA (CLIP 0.7685, TG-ID 0.9977) and TCSVE (TG-ID 0.9770). The general-purpose video editor RAVE performs worst across all metrics (IPR 0.7005, TL-ID 0.8621), confirming that scene-level editing methods are ill-suited for fine-grained face manipulation. For face image editing on CelebA, FlowGuide (Cosine) beats h-Edit on PSNR (23.16 vs 22.08), SSIM (0.8448 vs 0.8341), DINO distance (0.0166 vs 0.0193), and MSE distance (0.0060 vs 0.0078), while slightly trailing h-Edit on CLIP similarity (19.39 vs 19.71), reflecting a deliberate trade-off favoring identity preservation over aggressive editing. Against EF and PnP Inversion, FlowGuide achieves substantially better reconstruction quality (PSNR 23.16 vs 20.01/20.37) and identity consistency (DINO 0.0166 vs 0.0349/0.0271). The Pearson-correlation variant of FlowGuide produces overly aggressive edits (DINO 0.0539), validating the paper's choice of cosine similarity for geometrically grounded guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowGuide extends the line of diffusion-based inversion editing (DDIM, edit-friendly guidance) by replacing the fixed, non-adaptive guidance of Huberman et al. (2024) with a geometrically-grounded, dynamic mechanism. The method is directly built on the local linearity property of the UNet bottleneck latent space (Kwon et al., 2022) and the pullback metric framework from Riemannian geometry analysis of diffusion models (Park et al., 2023), using SVD of the condition-to-latent Jacobian to extract orthogonal basis vectors that span attribute-specific subspaces. For the video setting, it adopts the DVA architecture (Kim et al., 2023) as its base diffusion autoencoder, departing from GAN-based video editing (StyleCLIP, STIT) by operating entirely within a diffusion framework. The key conceptual departure from prior work is treating the edit as a geometric alignment problem between two sets of basis vectors rather than a fixed noise-blending operation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, geometry-based explanation for why certain similarity metrics (cosine, Spearman) outperform others (Pearson) in guiding diffusion edits, grounding the choice in the local linear structure of the latent space rather than empirical tuning. The unified image-and-video framework with a single guidance mechanism is practically useful, and the ablation cleanly separates the 'what to change' (LBE) from 'where to apply it' (IBG) roles. However, the approach remains limited to face editing, inherits over-smoothing artifacts in high-motion scenarios, and the local linearity assumption—while validated for the tested timesteps—has not been stress-tested across architectures or domains. Open questions include whether the basis-extraction approach generalizes to non-facial attributes or multi-attribute simultaneous edits without correlated-feature leakage.
