## Problem

LLM pretraining is bottlenecked by data scarcity: high-quality natural language corpora are finite, and repeating them degrades model performance. Existing synthetic data approaches either depend on complex predefined seed systems or require large generator models, making them computationally expensive and hard to reproduce. The paper asks how to systematically augment existing corpora into diverse, unique tokens using lightweight models without these dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263131/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw text documents (195B tokens from Fineweb-Edu-Dedup) and produces 5 reformulated documents per source, yielding a 770B-token MGACorpus (3.9× expansion). Each reformulation is conditioned on an adaptively generated genre-audience pair (e.g., 'academic teaching paper' × 'middle school chemistry teachers'), which jointly controls structural format and reader-appropriate tone/depth. A final heuristic cleaning stage removes high-frequency generative boilerplate and documents with extremely low keyword overlap with the source.

## Method

MGA is a two-stage synthesis pipeline powered by lightweight 3.3B MoE Tool SLMs fine-tuned on data labeled by a larger LLM teacher. Stage 1 (GA-Pair Generation) uses a 'one-pass-for-many' strategy where a single forward pass produces five distinct genre-audience pairs per document, mitigating mode collapse from repeated sampling. Stage 2 (Controlled Reformulation) generates the actual reformulated text given the source document and a GA pair, trained under a 'Limited Consistency' principle that balances stylistic variance with factual invariance—calibrated via prompt engineering between overly strict (low diversity) and overly relaxed (factual degradation) extremes. The Reformulation-SLM is SFT-trained only on reformulations the LLM judge scores ≥3, and the final corpus undergoes heuristic filtering. This design avoids large generator models and external seed systems, enabling web-scale deployment with 256×64 H100 GPU-hours for the full 770B-token corpus.

## Results

On the primary benchmark suite (12 tasks averaged), MGA-Expansion achieves 31.77 / 35.52 / 43.40 at 134M / 377M / 1.7B parameters, outperforming the corresponding SmolLM baselines by +0.26 / +0.95 / +2.15 points. The largest individual gains appear on reasoning-intensive tasks: TriviaQA improves by +2.03 / +6.99 / +15.47 and GSM8K by +0.15 / +0.22 / +6.06 across the three sizes. In data-constrained scaling experiments (up to 13B params, 700B tokens), MGA expansion yields consistent gains of +2.65 to +3.46 over full-set repetition and +1.46 to +3.73 over upsampling, with the advantage widening as model size increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SmolLM-Corpus baselines (Ben Allal et al., 2024), MGA-Expansion wins at all three model sizes on the 12-benchmark average, with the gap widening from +0.26 (134M) to +2.15 (1.7B), driven primarily by large gains on TriviaQA and GSM8K. Against Nemotron-CC synthetic data (Su et al., 2024), MGA is complementary rather than competitive: in the 35%-replacement experiment (Figure 4), combining MGA with Nemotron-Syn (Exp C) significantly outperforms either alone, while in the head-to-head Table 13, MGA (avg 37.34) beats five of six Nemotron-CC slices but trails Nemotron's diverse QA pairs (40.72), which benefit from format alignment with 0-shot QA benchmarks. Against Llama-3.2-1B (Dubey et al., 2024, listed as reference in Table 10), the 1.7B MGA-Expansion model (43.40 avg) outperforms Llama-3.2-1B (40.62 avg) despite using far fewer training tokens (1T vs 9T). In the scaling experiments (Figure 3), MGA consistently outperforms both full-set repetition and 5× upsampling across 377M–13B parameters, with N-scaling gains amplifying from +1.46 to +3.73 as model size grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the raw-text rephrasing line of synthetic pretraining data (WRAP, Nemotron-CC) by introducing adaptive genre-audience pair generation as the diversity mechanism, departing from both seed-based synthesis (Phi, Cosmopedia) and fixed-template rephrasing. It builds conceptually on Ge et al.'s persona-based scaling and Maini et al.'s rephrasing recipe, but replaces the need for large generator models or curated seed systems with lightweight 3.3B MoE tool SLMs fine-tuned via a 'Limited Consistency' prompt-engineering principle. The paper positions itself as a reproducible, open instantiation of synthesis principles that prior industrial work (Nemotron-CC, Kimi K2) has validated at scale but left methodologically opaque.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 770B-token MGACorpus release and full open-sourcing of prompts, tool-model finetuning data, and cleaning scripts provide the community with a concrete, reproducible data augmentation pipeline that was previously locked in industrial black boxes. The finding that MGA's performance advantage over upsampling amplifies with model scale (N-scaling) while remaining constant for upsampling suggests reformulation provides a qualitatively different signal than mere token repetition, though the mechanism (shift toward generalizable pattern learning vs. memorization) remains partially speculative. Key open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 13B parameters and 1T tokens, how sensitivity to repetition ratio and data distribution interacts with model hyperparameters, and whether the validation-loss-vs-benchmark disconnect generalizes to larger-scale training.
