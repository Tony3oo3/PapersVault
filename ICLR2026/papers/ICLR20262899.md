## Problem

Catastrophic forgetting in continual learning stems primarily from the difficulty of reactivating old-task memories. While parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods with prompt pools reduce forgetting by freezing the backbone, they perform only a single-step retrieval that under-activates historical knowledge and requires a full backbone forward pass to extract query features. The paper asks whether a multi-round, layer-internal associative retrieval mechanism—inspired by the hippocampal EC-DG-CA3-CA1 circuit—can deepen memory reactivation without extra backbone passes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet-R and CUB-200) and outputs a class label via a frozen ViT-Base/16 backbone. Unusually, the input image is processed through the frozen backbone only once; the iterative retrieval loop operates entirely in the latent hidden-state space at each Transformer layer, using the previous layer's output as a query rather than reconstructing high-level semantic features. At inference, the number of retrieval iterations (T_max), convergence threshold (ε), and top-k sparsity provide explicit compute-quality trade-off knobs.

## Method

HippoTune embeds a 'Latent Deliberation' loop inside each Transformer layer: the previous layer's hidden state initializes a query, which undergoes T_max rounds of soft key-value retrieval (softmax over a learnable key matrix), with each retrieved value linearly projected back to update the query via a convex blend (α·q + (1−α)·P(v)). All per-iteration retrieval vectors are concatenated and fused in a single ViT block forward pass, avoiding repeated backbone computations. Theoretically, the paper proves that near a fixed point this finite-step recurrence implements a Krylov-subspace polynomial approximation to the inverse Hessian, acting as an implicit second-order preconditioner for gradient propagation. Training uses truncated BPTT through the retrieval loop with a composite loss of cross-entropy, key orthogonality, and retrieval-entropy regularization, and the method is applied to layers 1–7 of a frozen ViT-Base/16 with only the key-value projection parameters updated.

## Results

On Seq-CIFAR100, HippoTune achieves 87.65% Acc and 92.07% AAA; on Seq-ImageNet-R, 74.85% Acc and 79.92% AAA; and on Seq-CUB200, 81.12% Acc and 86.63% AAA. Critically, all results are obtained with only 16.92 GFLOPs—roughly half the computational budget (~35 GFLOPs) of competing PEFT-CL methods such as L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, and HiDe-Prompt. HippoTune outperforms all buffer-free PEFT-CL baselines on Seq-ImageNet-R and surpasses most on the other two benchmarks, while also beating replay-based classical methods (LwF, DER++) by 7–20 percentage points in final accuracy without any memory buffer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest PEFT-CL baseline HiDe-Prompt, HippoTune wins on Seq-ImageNet-R (74.85 vs. 74.65 Acc; 79.92 vs. 78.46 AAA) while trailing on Seq-CIFAR100 (87.65 vs. 88.25) and Seq-CUB200 (81.12 vs. 84.27), though it does so at less than half the FLOPs (16.92 vs. 35.25). Against CODA-Prompt, HippoTune leads on all three benchmarks by 1.4–8.7 Acc points, with the largest margin on Seq-CUB200 (81.12 vs. 72.45). Compared to L2P and DualPrompt, HippoTune gains 4.9–5.6 Acc points on Seq-CIFAR100 and 3.6–6.6 points on Seq-ImageNet-R. Versus replay-based classical methods, HippoTune improves final accuracy by ~7.4 points over LwF and ~3.2–20.6 points over DER++ across the three benchmarks, all without any experience buffer. The paper notes that HiDe-Prompt's edge on CIFAR-100 and CUB-200 is attributed to its higher computational budget and more sophisticated multi-step prompting design, whereas HippoTune's advantage on ImageNet-R highlights its efficiency under tighter resource constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

HippoTune unifies the prompt-pool PEFT-CL lineage (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, HiDe-Prompt, LAE-PreT) under a single key-value retrieval formalism, identifying that all existing methods perform only a single-depth lookup. It then departs from this single-step paradigm by embedding a multi-step iterative retrieval-and-feedback loop (Latent Deliberation) within each Transformer layer, drawing conceptual inspiration from the hippocampal EC-DG-CA3-CA1 circuit's pattern separation and completion. The theoretical contribution connects this finite-step recurrence to Krylov-subspace polynomial approximation of the inverse Hessian, positioning the loop as an implicit second-order preconditioner—a framing absent from prior PEFT-CL work, which has relied on NTK or loss-landscape analyses rather than explicit curvature arguments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a ~50% reduction in training FLOPs (16.92 vs. ~35 GFLOPs) while remaining competitive with or exceeding most buffer-free PEFT-CL baselines, making HippoTune attractive for compute-constrained continual learning deployments. However, the method still trails the strongest baseline (HiDe-Prompt) on 2 of 3 benchmarks, and the theoretical Krylov connection, while elegant, yields only a small practical lever (T_max = 2–4 iterations). Open questions include whether the iterative loop generalizes beyond ViT-Base/16 image classification to language models, video, or deeper hierarchies, and whether adaptive per-layer retrieval budgets could further close the gap with higher-compute methods.
