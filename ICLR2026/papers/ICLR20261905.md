## Problem

Video diffusion transformers suffer from two compounding bottlenecks: iterative multi-step denoising and quadratic attention over long spatiotemporal sequences. While step distillation and sparse attention each offer independent speedups, combining them is non-trivial—training-free integration is suboptimal because distillation is agnostic to sparsity, and sequential training (distill then fine-tune for sparsity) re-introduces the need for expensive high-quality video datasets that data-free distillation methods were designed to avoid.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a generated video at 480×720 (CogVideoX-5B, 8 FPS, ~17.5k latent tokens) or 480×832 (Wan2.1-1.3B, 16 FPS, ~32.8k latent tokens). The distilled student model generates the full video in only 8 denoising steps (or as few as 4) instead of the original 50, with each step using adaptive block-sparse attention at ~80% sparsity, yielding 8.89×–14.10× end-to-end speedup over the 50-step dense baseline.

## Method

BLADE jointly trains a sparse student model within a Trajectory Distribution Matching (TDM) distillation loop, so sparsity is learned as part of the generation trajectory rather than applied post-hoc. The key architectural component is Adaptive Block-Sparse Attention (ASA), which uses a Gilbert space-filling curve to preserve spatial locality, then generates content-aware binary masks on-the-fly by sampling k=16 tokens per 128-token block to compute a low-cost importance map and applying a per-row threshold (e.g., 95% cumulative attention). The ASA_G variant augments K and V with mean-pooled global tokens and a fixed ln(n) additive mask to prevent information loss at high sparsity. During training, the student generates trajectories under ASA constraints while a concurrently trained fake score model and the frozen teacher provide a distribution-matching loss, enabling data-free convergence in 100–200 iterations without any external video dataset.

## Results

On VBench-2.0, BLADE (ASA_G) achieves a total score of 0.569 on CogVideoX-5B (vs. 0.534 for the 50-step baseline) with an 8.89× end-to-end speedup, and 0.570 on Wan2.1-1.3B (vs. 0.563 baseline) with a 14.10× speedup. At the kernel level, ASA delivers a 3.30× speedup over dense FlashAttention-2 (22.21 ms vs. 73.25 ms) at 0.798 effective sparsity. In training-free sparse attention comparisons (Table 3), ASA outperforms STA, SVG, and RaA on both PSNR and SSIM at matched sparsity levels (e.g., PSNR 19.55 vs. 16.72 for STA at ~0.75 sparsity on Wan2.1-1.3B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the 50-step dense baseline, ASA_G improves VBench-2.0 total scores on both models (0.569 vs. 0.534 on CogVideoX-5B; 0.570 vs. 0.563 on Wan2.1-1.3B) while delivering 8.89× and 14.10× speedups respectively. Compared to the 8-step dense FA2 distillation, ASA_G achieves substantially higher speedups (8.89× vs. 7.93×; 14.10× vs. 9.37×) and matches or exceeds quality on most sub-metrics, though FA2 edges out ASA_G on total VBench-2.0 for Wan2.1-1.3B (0.580 vs. 0.570), primarily driven by a Creativity gap (0.631 vs. 0.472). Against the static sparse baseline STA at similar sparsity (~0.74–0.80), ASA_G dominates on quality: 0.570 vs. 0.528 total on Wan2.1-1.3B, with large margins in Commonsense (0.532 vs. 0.471) and Physics (0.617 vs. 0.543). In training-free sparse attention comparisons (Table 3), ASA outperforms STA, SVG, and RaA on PSNR and SSIM at matched sparsity (e.g., PSNR 19.55 vs. 16.72 for STA at 0.75 sparsity). On the larger Wan2.1-14B model (Table 8), ASA achieves the best LPIPS (0.050) and highest PSNR (26.05) among all sparse methods at ~0.77 sparsity, surpassing STA by 1.05 PSNR and SpargeAttention by 2.02 PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

BLADE builds directly on Trajectory Distribution Matching (TDM) as its distillation backbone, adopting its data-free score-matching framework to train a few-step student from a multi-step teacher. Its key departure is the sparsity-aware distillation paradigm: rather than applying sparse attention as a post-hoc compression step or combining it training-free with a pre-distilled model, BLADE integrates Adaptive Block-Sparse Attention (ASA) directly into the TDM training loop so the student learns under sparsity constraints from the start. On the attention side, ASA positions itself against static-mask methods (STA, Radial Attention), binary-choice dynamic methods (SVG), and training-free dynamic methods (SpargeAttention) by offering a content-aware, threshold-based block-sparse mask that supports both training-free inference and end-to-end distillation training, with a global-token augmentation (ASA_G) to mitigate information loss at high sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14.10× end-to-end speedup on Wan2.1-1.3B and 8.89× on CogVideoX-5B, achieved while matching or exceeding 50-step baseline quality on VBench-2.0, makes high-quality video generation substantially more practical for deployment. The data-free joint training eliminates the need for expensive high-quality video datasets that sequential distillation-then-sparse-finetuning pipelines require. Key limitations include evaluation only on moderate-length sequences (17k–33k tokens), a Triton kernel that underperforms its theoretical speedup, and the quality-over-baseline finding being somewhat confounded with the distillation effect itself (FA2-8 also beats the 50-step baseline on Wan2.1). Open questions include scaling ASA to minute-long videos with hundreds of thousands of tokens, developing an optimized CUDA kernel, and extending the sparsity-as-regularization principle to other generative domains.
