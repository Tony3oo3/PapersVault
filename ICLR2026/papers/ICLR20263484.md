## Problem

Existing consistent approaches for partial multi-label learning (PML) and complementary multi-label learning (CML) either require accurate estimation of the label generation process (e.g., flipping rates via anchor points or transition matrices) or assume a uniform distribution over candidate label sets. Both conditions are difficult to satisfy in real-world scenarios due to estimation difficulty with deep networks and the unrealistic uniformity assumption. This paper asks whether consistent multi-label classification under inexact supervision can be achieved without either requirement.

## Input / Output

The method takes a feature vector x paired with a candidate label set S (where all true labels are contained in S but some irrelevant labels may also appear) or equivalently complementary labels (the non-candidate labels). From the training set, it derives two auxiliary datasets: an unlabeled set D_U (all instances) and per-class negative sets D_j (instances where class j is not a candidate). The output is a real-valued decision function g: X → R^q whose thresholded version assigns a set of relevant labels to each instance. Class priors π_j are required as known quantities and can be estimated via off-the-shelf mixture proportion estimation.

## Method

COMES introduces a data generation process where each irrelevant label is independently excluded from the candidate set with a class-specific constant probability p_j, eliminating the need for transition matrices or uniform-distribution assumptions. The key theoretical insight (Lemma 1) shows that p(x|s_j=0) = p(x|y_j=0), enabling the true ℓ-risk to be expressed entirely in terms of observable marginal and conditional densities. Two instantiations are derived: COMES-HL (first-order, minimizing Hamming loss via an absolute-value risk correction to prevent overfitting from negative terms) and COMES-RL (second-order, minimizing ranking loss via flooding regularization to capture label correlations). Consistency w.r.t. both Hamming and ranking losses is proven, and convergence rates for estimation errors are derived using McDiarmid's inequality and Rademacher complexity bounds.

## Results

On synthetic PML benchmarks, COMES-RL achieves the highest mAP on VOC2007 (51.46) and VOC2012 (53.26), surpassing the best baseline BCE by 11.2 and 15.7 points respectively. COMES-HL leads on CUB (18.94 mAP vs. 16.30 for BCE) and COCO2014 (33.62 vs. 26.73). On real-world datasets, COMES-RL achieves Hamming loss of 0.045–0.051 on the three Yeast subsets, roughly 3× lower than the best baseline (0.148–0.162 for BCE), while COMES-HL attains the best ranking loss on MIRFlickr (0.095) and Music Emotion (0.214).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMES consistently outperforms all four consistent PML/CML baselines across both real-world and synthetic datasets. Against CCMN (Xie & Huang, 2023), which requires anchor-point-based flipping-rate estimation, COMES-RL improves mAP on VOC2007 from 40.02 to 51.46 and reduces Hamming loss on Yeast BP from 0.151 to 0.051. Against CTL (Gao et al., 2023) and GDF (Gao et al., 2025), both of which rely on the uniform-distribution assumption, the gains are even more dramatic: COMES-RL achieves 51.46 vs. 17.05 (CTL) and 21.27 (GDF) mAP on VOC2007, and 0.051 vs. 0.493 (CTL) and 0.499 (GDF) Hamming loss on Yeast BP. Against MLCL (Gao et al., 2024), which uses a transition-matrix-based correction, COMES-RL leads on VOC2012 (53.26 vs. 15.02 mAP) and Yeast BP (0.051 vs. 0.881 Hamming loss), though MLCL is marginally competitive on Music Style ranking loss (0.130 vs. COMES-HL's 0.132). The only notable exception is CUB, where COMES-HL (18.94) edges out COMES-RL (17.50), suggesting the first-order Hamming-loss strategy is better suited to that dataset's label structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMES builds on the line of consistent risk estimators for partial multi-label learning (CCMN, Xie & Huang 2023) and complementary multi-label learning (CTL, Gao et al. 2023; MLCL, Gao et al. 2024; GDF, Gao et al. 2025), but departs from all of them by eliminating the need to estimate a label-flipping transition matrix or assume a uniform distribution over candidate/complementary labels. Instead, it posits a simpler data generation process where each irrelevant class is excluded from the candidate set with a fixed per-class probability, which the authors argue better matches real annotation workflows. The risk-correction techniques (absolute-value wrapping for the Hamming-loss estimator, flooding regularization for the ranking-loss estimator) are adapted from the PU-learning and semi-supervised literature (Kiryo et al. 2017; Ishida et al. 2020) and applied here for the first time in the multi-label inexact-supervision setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that practitioners working on PML or CML no longer need to solve the notoriously difficult sub-problem of estimating noisy class posteriors at anchor points or justify a uniform-distribution assumption that is hard to satisfy with imbalanced real-world data. The theoretical guarantees (consistency w.r.t. both Hamming and ranking loss, plus explicit convergence rates via McDiarmid's inequality and Rademacher complexity) provide a rigorous foundation that prior heuristic corrections lacked. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the generation probability p_j is instance-independent; extending to instance-dependent (feature-conditional) generation processes is identified as future work, which would be important for scenarios where annotation difficulty varies across the input space.
