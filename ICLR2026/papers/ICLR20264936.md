## Problem

Existing MLLM-based reasoning segmentation methods either rely on supervised fine-tuning (suffering catastrophic forgetting and poor out-of-domain generalization) or use RL that decouples the MLLM from SAM's iterative refinement loop. No prior method unifies bounding-box generation, interactive mask refinement, and adaptive termination within a single RL-trained agent that emulates how a human user naturally operates SAM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264936/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image (resized to 840×840) and a free-form text query as input. The MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) first outputs a bounding box in JSON format, which is fed to SAM2-Large to produce an initial mask. The MLLM then iteratively receives the image overlaid with the current green-transparent mask and outputs positive/negative refinement point coordinates (or null for both to signal termination), with a maximum of 3 refinement steps by default. The final output is the SAM-predicted binary segmentation mask.

## Method

SAM-Veteran frames the segmentation workflow as an MDP where the MLLM policy maps states (mask, image, question) to actions (bounding box or refinement points), trained via a multi-task GRPO framework with three reward-shaped tasks: (1) textual grounding, rewarding boxes by IoU/L1 against GT and by the resulting SAM mask IoU; (2) mask comprehension, rewarding correct termination decisions and IoU improvement from generated points via a tiered Δ-IoU reward; and (3) an auxiliary task that corrupts GT masks with random polygonal inclusions/exclusions to train the model to locate error centers. A dynamic sampling strategy adapted from DAPO over-samples rollouts until diverse action types (null/null, pos-only, neg-only, both) are represented, stabilizing GRPO advantage estimation. Global batch normalization from REINFORCE++ replaces GRPO's local standard deviation, and chain-of-thought reasoning is required in every rollout to guide the final coordinate prediction.

## Results

SAM-Veteran achieves state-of-the-art performance on out-of-domain benchmarks, reaching 68.2 gIoU / 67.3 cIoU on ReasonSeg val and 62.6 gIoU / 56.1 cIoU on ReasonSeg test, surpassing the previous best (SAM-R1: 64.0/55.8 and 60.2/54.3) by 4.2 and 2.4 gIoU points respectively. On in-domain RefCOCO benchmarks, it attains 80.8 cIoU (testA), 76.6 cIoU (testA+), and 73.4 cIoU (testA g), matching or exceeding all SFT and RL baselines. The model also generalizes to additional benchmarks MMR (40.38 gIoU) and MUSE (53.63 gIoU), outperforming Seg-Zero by 2.5 and 1.5 gIoU points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On out-of-domain ReasonSeg benchmarks, SAM-Veteran decisively outperforms all baselines: it beats the strongest RL competitor SAM-R1 by 4.2 gIoU (68.2 vs 64.0) on val and 2.4 gIoU (62.6 vs 60.2) on test, and surpasses Seg-Zero by 5.6 gIoU on val. The gap over SegAgent is particularly stark (68.2 vs 33.0 gIoU on val), reflecting SegAgent's poor generalization due to its SFT-based training on hand-crafted point trajectories. Among SFT methods, SAM-Veteran exceeds the best (GSVA at 50.5 gIoU) by 17.7 gIoU on ReasonSeg val. On in-domain RefCOCO, SAM-Veteran achieves the best cIoU on RefCOCO (80.8) and RefCOCO+ (76.6), but trails SegAgent and POPEN by 1.2 points on RefCOCOg testA (73.4 vs 74.6), where those methods benefit from SFT on point trajectories or a well-trained PixelLM initialization. Overall, the RL-based multi-task framework with iterative refinement and adaptive termination provides the strongest generalization, while in-domain performance is competitive with or slightly below the best SFT-assisted methods on the most challenging RefCOCOg split.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM-Veteran extends the human-like SAM interaction paradigm introduced by SegAgent (Zhu et al., 2025b), which first emulated human point-click refinement, but replaces SegAgent's SFT-on-trajectories training with a multi-task GRPO reinforcement learning framework and unifies box generation, iterative refinement, and adaptive termination in a single model. It builds on SAM-R1's (Huang et al., 2025) idea of using SAM-predicted masks as fine-grained RL reward signals, and adapts dynamic sampling from DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) to stabilize training across heterogeneous action spaces (boxes vs. points). The box reward design and training data protocol follow Seg-Zero (Liu et al., 2025a), while the overall MDP formulation and GRPO optimization inherit from the DeepSeekMath/GRPO lineage (Shao et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to unify all three components of human-like SAM usage (grounding, iterative refinement, adaptive termination) under a single RL-trained MLLM, and the results demonstrate that RL-based training preserves general MLLM capabilities while SFT-based approaches suffer catastrophic forgetting — a practical concern for deploying segmentation agents in open-world settings. The large out-of-domain gap (68.2 vs. 33.0 gIoU on ReasonSeg val) between RL- and SFT-trained agents underscores that trajectory-mimicking is insufficient for generalization. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object or video segmentation, whether the frozen SAM bottleneck limits further gains, and whether the 32B scaling results (marginal on RefCOCOg) suggest a data or task ceiling rather than a model-size ceiling.
