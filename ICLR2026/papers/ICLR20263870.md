## Problem

Unified multimodal models that jointly perform understanding and generation have been largely confined to the image domain, while video generation models address a single text-to-video task and video editing relies on task-specific pipelines or modules. No existing system can interpret complex multimodal instructions (combining text, images, and video) to perform diverse video tasks—generation, in-context generation, and editing—within a single framework, leaving capabilities like multimodal prompting, in-context video generation, and free-form video editing beyond the reach of any one model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263870/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

UniVideo accepts heterogeneous multimodal inputs—text prompts, one or more reference images, source video clips, and hand-drawn visual annotations—and produces generated videos (up to 129 frames at 480–854 resolution), edited videos, generated/edited images, or text responses depending on the instruction. For in-context tasks, multiple visual conditions are VAE-encoded, padded to a uniform shape, and concatenated along the temporal axis before self-attention, with 3D positional embeddings (incrementing only the temporal axis) to distinguish condition latents from the noisy target. Notably, video editing is performed mask-free: the model localizes editing regions purely from the natural-language instruction rather than requiring explicit spatial masks, a stricter setting than all compared baselines.

## Method

UniVideo adopts a dual-stream architecture pairing a frozen Qwen2.5-VL-7B MLLM (understanding branch) with a HunyuanVideo-T2V-13B MMDiT (generation branch). The MLLM's last-layer hidden states are projected through a trainable MLP connector into the MMDiT's self-attention understanding stream, while VAE-encoded visual signals feed the MMDiT's generation stream, jointly providing semantic grounding and fine-grained reconstruction fidelity. The MMDiT uses self-attention for joint text-video interaction rather than cross-attention, which the ablation shows enables robust alignment by training only the lightweight MLP connector while keeping both backbones frozen. Training proceeds in three stages—connector alignment, T2I/T2V fine-tuning, and multi-task training across in-context generation, in-context editing, image editing, and I2V—with the MLLM frozen throughout. This unified multi-task training yields two forms of generalization: transfer of image-editing capability to previously unseen free-form video editing (e.g., material or environment changes), and composition of multiple editing operations within a single instruction, neither of which was explicitly trained.

## Results

On visual understanding, UniVideo (using its frozen Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone) achieves 83.5 on MMBench, 58.6 on MMMU, and 66.6 on MM-Vet, matching or exceeding all unified baselines except BAGEL on MMBench (85.0) and MM-Vet (67.2). On video generation, UniVideo scores 83.48 on VBench T2V, slightly below Wan2.1 (84.70) but above HunyuanVideo (83.24), CogVideoX (81.61), and Step-Video (81.83). For in-context video generation, UniVideo achieves the best Subject Consistency (0.88 single-ID, 0.81 multi-ID) and Prompt Following (0.93 single-ID) among all baselines including Kling1.6 and Pika2.2. On in-context video editing, UniVideo is the only mask-free method and achieves competitive or superior CLIP-I, DINO-I, and Aesthetic scores across all four editing tasks (insert, swap, delete, stylization) compared to mask-based baselines like UNIC, VACE, and AnyV2V.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On visual understanding, UniVideo outperforms all unified baselines (OmniGen2, Show-o2, Emu3, Janus, JanusFlow, TokenFlow-XL, Show-o) on all three benchmarks, and also surpasses dedicated VLMs LLaVA-1.5 and LLaVA-NeXT; it trails only BAGEL by 1.5 points on MMBench and 0.6 on MM-Vet while leading on MMMU (58.6 vs. 55.3). On T2V generation, UniVideo (83.48) is competitive with the dedicated HunyuanVideo backbone (83.24) and Wan2.1 (84.70), confirming that the MLLM-based conditioning does not significantly degrade generation quality. For in-context video generation, UniVideo dramatically outperforms VACE (SC 0.88 vs. 0.31 single-ID) and leads Kling1.6 (SC 0.88 vs. 0.68) and Pika2.2 (SC 0.88 vs. 0.45) on Subject Consistency, while also achieving the best multi-ID Prompt Following (0.75 vs. 0.45 for Kling1.6). On in-context video editing, UniVideo operates mask-free yet matches or exceeds mask-based baselines: on ID insertion it achieves CLIP-I 0.693 (vs. UNIC 0.598, VACE 0.513) and Aesthetic 6.031 (vs. Kling1.6 5.798); on swap it reaches CLIP-I 0.728 (vs. UNIC 0.725, Kling1.6 0.707); on deletion it attains CLIP-Score 0.214 (vs. UNIC 0.217, VACE 0.206); and on stylization it leads in Aesthetic (6.281) and CLIP-Score (0.226) over AnyV2V, StyleMaster, and UNIC. On image generation (GenEval), UniVideo with an LLM rewriter scores 0.69 overall, competitive with OmniGen2 (0.80) and BAGEL (0.82) but below dedicated image models like SD3-medium (0.74) and FLUX.1-dev (0.67); on image editing (ImgEdit-Bench), UniVideo achieves 3.83 overall, surpassing OmniGen2 (3.44), BAGEL (3.20), and UniWorld-V1 (3.26), approaching GPT-4o (4.20).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniVideo extends the dual-stream MLLM+MMDiT design explored in the image domain by Qwen-Image (Wu et al., 2025a) to the video domain, using HunyuanVideo's MMDiT backbone (Kong et al., 2024) with self-attention for joint text-video interaction (Esser et al., 2024). It departs from prior unified models (Show-o, BAGEL, OmniGen2) that rely solely on semantic encoders or learnable query bottlenecks, which the authors argue cannot capture fine-grained visual details needed for in-context video generation. Unlike task-specific video editing pipelines (UNIC, VACE, AnyV2V) that require masks or task-specific bias embeddings, UniVideo unifies all video tasks under a single multimodal instruction paradigm with 3D positional embeddings to distinguish condition latents from noisy video latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniVideo is the first framework to unify video understanding, generation, and editing in a single model, demonstrating that joint training across tasks enables zero-shot generalization to free-form video editing (e.g., material changes, environment modifications) without explicit training data for those tasks. The task composition capability—combining multiple edits in one instruction—suggests a path toward more flexible video manipulation systems. Key limitations include motion preservation (the backbone struggles to fully retain original video dynamics), occasional over-editing, and lower success rates for free-form video editing compared to image editing. Open questions include whether a natively trained end-to-end video model (rather than an assembled MLLM+DiT system) could further improve performance, and whether the zero-shot editing transfer scales to more complex temporal edits.
