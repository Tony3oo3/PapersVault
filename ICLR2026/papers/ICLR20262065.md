## Problem

Existing trustworthiness evaluations of generative foundation models are fragmented across modalities (LLMs, text-to-image, vision-language), rely on static benchmarks that rapidly become obsolete as models evolve, and lack a unified taxonomy for cross-model comparison. This makes it difficult to systematically and continuously assess whether GenFMs are safe, fair, truthful, and robust in high-stakes deployments.

## Input / Output

The system takes a target generative model (any T2I, LLM, or VLM from a built-in pool or Hugging Face) plus one or more selected trustworthiness dimensions as input, and dynamically generates a tailored evaluation dataset on the fly. Outputs are a normalized trustworthiness score (0–100) per dimension and sub-dimension, computed by inverting lower-is-better metrics (e.g., toxicity, refusal-to-answer rate) and averaging across constituent tasks, culminating in a comprehensive report with an overall score and optional leaderboard upload. Evaluation judgments are produced by a generative model-as-a-judge (LLM or VLM) comparing model outputs against ground-truth labels, rather than by keyword matching or rule-based scoring.

## Method

TrustGen is built around a three-module dynamic pipeline: (1) a Metadata Curator that collects and preprocesses raw data via web-browsing agents, dataset pool maintainers, or constrained model-based generation (avoiding self-evaluation leakage); (2) a Test Case Builder that constructs evaluation items using attribute-guided generation, principle-guided generation (AI constitutions), programmatic operations (e.g., adding noise for robustness), or LLM-based paraphrasing, with ground-truth labels always derived from non-generative sources to prevent self-enhancement bias; and (3) a Contextual Variator that transforms each test case across question format (open-ended, multiple-choice, binary), sentence length, and paraphrase to mitigate prompt sensitivity. The evaluation taxonomy spans seven high-level dimensions (Truthfulness, Safety, Fairness, Robustness, Privacy, Machine Ethics, Advanced AI Risk) with over 25 fine-grained sub-dimensions, each mapped to dimension-specific metrics (accuracy, RtA rate, win rate, etc.). The architecture is fully modular—each dimension instantiates different concrete algorithms and dataflows through the same three-module interface—enabling extensibility to new trust dimensions, modalities, and model-specific probes without redesigning the pipeline.

## Results

Across 39 evaluated GenFMs, GPT-5 achieves the highest overall LLM trustworthiness score of 84.37/100, followed closely by GPT-5-mini (84.23) and o1-mini (82.75). For text-to-image models, CogView-3-Plus (open-source) leads with 74.96, surpassing DALL-E-3 (72.52), while Claude-3.5-Sonnet tops VLMs at 75.46. The majority of models across all three categories exceed a 70-point average, but Truthfulness is consistently the weakest dimension (ranging from ~28–68 for T2I, ~58–68 for LLMs, and ~49–67 for VLMs), indicating persistent hallucination and sycophancy challenges. The trust gap among top-performing LLMs is under 10 points, with open-source models like Llama-3.1-70B (82.41) and Mixtral-8x22B (81.08) closely matching proprietary systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark rather than a method paper, TrustGen does not propose a new model to compare against baselines; instead, it provides head-to-head trustworthiness comparisons across 39 GenFMs. A key finding is that open-source models now rival or surpass proprietary ones: CogView-3-Plus (74.96) outperforms DALL-E-3 (72.52) on T2I trust, and Llama-3.1-70B (82.41) is within 0.23 points of GPT-4o (82.64) on LLM trust. Among LLMs, the top five models (GPT-5, GPT-5-mini, o1-mini, Gemini-1.5-Pro, GPT-4o) are separated by fewer than 2 points, indicating a narrowing trust gap. Dimension-specific leaders vary: GPT-4o-mini and Gemini-1.5-Flash lead in Robustness (~99.4), while GPT-5 leads in Privacy (91.98) and Advanced AI Risk (91.64); notably, Claude-3.5-Sonnet scores only 55.70 on Advanced AI Risk despite a strong overall score, and Llama-3.2-90B-V collapses to 1.96 on VLM Machine Ethics due to high-frequency avoidance behavior. Smaller models within the same family (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B vs. 70B, Mixtral-8x7B vs. 8x22B) consistently underperform their larger counterparts on Fairness and Advanced AI Risk, though they sometimes match or exceed them on Privacy.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrustGen extends the TrustLLM (Sun et al., 2024b) and TrustGPT (Huang et al., 2023f) line of work, which evaluated trustworthiness dimensions primarily for LLMs using static datasets, by generalizing to a unified, modality-agnostic framework covering text-to-image, LLM, and vision-language models. It departs from prior static benchmarks by introducing a dynamic three-module pipeline (Metadata Curator, Test Case Builder, Contextual Variator) inspired by recent dynamic evaluation efforts (Zhu et al., 2023c; Huang et al., 2025), and from prior single-modality safety benchmarks by standardizing a shared taxonomy of 25+ fine-grained dimensions across all three model families. The LLM-as-a-judge evaluation paradigm and programmatic perturbation strategies for robustness testing are drawn from established practices, but their integration into a single extensible platform is the paper's architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TrustGen provides the first publicly available, dynamically updating benchmark for cross-modal GenFM trustworthiness, filling a critical gap where prior evaluations were fragmented, modality-specific, and rapidly outdated. The finding that open-source models now rival or surpass proprietary systems in trustworthiness (e.g., CogView-3-Plus outperforming DALL-E-3) challenges the prevailing assumption that safety alignment is a proprietary advantage, with direct implications for deployment decisions. Open questions remain around whether dynamic data generation truly prevents memorization at scale, how the framework handles rapidly evolving attack surfaces (e.g., novel jailbreaks), and whether the LLM-as-judge approach introduces systematic biases that human evaluation would catch. The 'ripple effect' observation—that trustworthiness dimensions interact non-trivially with helpfulness and ethical reasoning—suggests future work should treat trust as a coupled system rather than independent axes.
