## Problem

Multi-agent imitation learning (MA-IL) methods produce policies that can be exploited by unilaterally deviating strategic agents, yet no consistent and tractable bounds on the resulting Nash gap existed for offline MA-IL. This paper shows that even exact occupancy measure matching can yield highly exploitable policies in general Markov games, and that computing tight exploitability lower bounds is PPAD-hard, before identifying the structural conditions (best-response δ-continuity) under which meaningful guarantees become possible.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of finite-length trajectories generated by rolling out a known Nash equilibrium expert policy π^E in a fully specified n-player Markov game (state space, joint action space, transition kernel, per-player reward functions, initial state distribution, and discount factor γ). It outputs a learned joint product policy π (typically via behavioral cloning or state-action occupancy matching) together with a computable upper bound on the Nash gap expressed as a function of the BC error ε_BC, the number of players n, the effective horizon 1/(1−γ), and a game-specific δ-continuity function that quantifies how far best-responses can shift under small policy perturbations at equilibrium.

## Method

The paper first establishes impossibility results: state-only matching with full-state support can incur Nash gaps of Ω(1/(1−γ)), and state-action matching with unvisited states can do the same, while computing the best-case Nash gap for a given matching error is PPAD-hard even for bimatrix games. To overcome these barriers, the authors introduce δ-continuity of the best-response correspondence—a measure of how much the optimal policy of player i can change when the other players' joint policy is perturbed by ε in L1 norm under the expert state distribution. Under the special case of a dominant strategy equilibrium (δ≡0), they prove NashGap(π) ≤ 2nε_BC/(1−γ)² via a double application of the performance difference lemma. The general δ-continuous case yields NashGap(π) ≤ (2nε_BC + δ(ε_BC))/(1−γ)², reducing the exploitability analysis to characterizing δ for the game class. Empirically, on a two-player Tag-Game, they validate the bound and show that entropy regularization of the expert equilibrium tightens δ, implicitly encouraging the continuity property needed for non-vacuous guarantees.

## Results

The paper establishes that under a Dominant Strategy Equilibrium (DSE) assumption, the Nash gap of a learned policy with BC error ε_BC is bounded by 2n·ε_BC/(1−γ)², providing a consistent and tractable exploitability guarantee. More generally, under a new δ-continuity condition on the best-response mapping, the bound generalizes to (2n·ε_BC + δ(ε_BC))/(1−γ)². The paper also proves that computing the tight Nash gap lower bound m_ρ(G, ε_ρ) is PPAD-hard even for bimatrix games, and that even exact state-action occupancy measure matching can yield a Nash gap of Ω(1/(1−γ)) without full-state support. Empirical validation on a Tag-Game environment confirms the bound's validity and shows that entropy regularization reduces the tight δ function, improving exploitability control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without standard empirical baselines, so comparisons are framed in terms of prior theoretical results. The paper's DSE bound (Lemma 3) recovers the n=2 result of Freihaut et al. (2025) but extends it to n players and removes their reliance on a concentrability coefficient that is intractable and can become unbounded in general games. The δ-continuity framework (Lemma 4) strictly generalizes the DSE case (δ(·)=0) and provides a spectrum between the trivial bound δ(·)=2 (achievable by pathological games, as shown in Lemma 2) and the tight DSE bound. The impossibility results (Sections 4–5) demonstrate that prior SA-IL extensions to multi-agent settings implicitly assume consistency (zero imitation error implies zero Nash gap) that fails in general Markov Games, even under exact measure matching. The PPAD-hardness result (Theorem 3) shows that no polynomial-time algorithm can compute the best achievable Nash gap for a given matching error, even when the full game is known, distinguishing this from the single-agent case where consistent O(ε_BC/(1−γ)²) value gap bounds are always available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-agent imitation learning (MA-IL) line of work by shifting focus from value-gap guarantees to exploitability (Nash gap) guarantees. It builds directly on Freihaut et al. (2025), whose concentrability-based regret bound for multi-agent behavioral cloning is recovered as a special case (n=2, δ=0), and on Tang et al. (2024), whose impossibility example for unvisited states is adapted and generalized. The key departure is the introduction of best-response δ-continuity as a new analytical tool that replaces the intractable concentrability coefficient, and the systematic characterization of *why* prior bounds fail (inconsistency under state-only matching, PPAD-hardness of tight lower bounds) rather than merely showing they are loose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The impossibility results are practically important: they show that the standard MA-IL recipe of matching expert occupancy measures provides no Nash-equilibrium guarantee in general games, even with zero approximation error, which means practitioners cannot rely on measure-matching convergence as a proxy for equilibrium recovery. The PPAD-hardness result further implies that no polynomial-time algorithm can certify the best achievable Nash gap for a given imitation error, even with full game knowledge. The δ-continuity framework offers a constructive path forward—characterizing δ for a specific game yields tractable, consistent bounds—and the empirical connection to entropy regularization suggests a practical lever for controlling exploitability. Open questions include whether reachability assumptions or policy distribution-norms can yield tighter game-dependent bounds, and whether augmenting expert data with suboptimal trajectories (as in single-agent DAgger-style methods) can mitigate the unvisited-state pathology.
