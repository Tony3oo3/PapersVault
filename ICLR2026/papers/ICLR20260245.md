## Problem

Conformal prediction provides marginal coverage guarantees but fails to protect subgroups disproportionately affected by algorithmic bias. Existing adaptive methods like AFCP can only identify groups defined by single features or simple linear combinations, missing complex nonlinear interactions (e.g., XOR/XNOR between features) that often define the truly disadvantaged subgroups. This paper addresses the expressiveness gap by learning groups in a latent representation space rather than directly on raw features.

## Input / Output

The method takes a calibration dataset of (feature, label) pairs, a pre-trained classifier, a list of sensitive features, and hyperparameters (miscoverage rate α, group size proportion δ, KL weight β, sampling count T) as input. It produces a prediction set for a test instance—constructed as the union of the standard conformal set and T group-specific conformal sets—along with the adaptively selected unfair groups. The group selection is stochastic: T binary vectors are sampled from a learned joint Bernoulli distribution over the calibration set, each defining a distinct subgroup used to calibrate a separate conformal set.

## Method

FAREG encodes input features X into a latent representation Z via a stochastic encoder (MLP outputting mean and variance of a Gaussian), then learns a binary group-membership decoder and a reconstruction decoder using a Deep Variational Information Bottleneck objective: maximize I(Z,S) + I(Z,X) − βI(Z,i). The group-selection decoder is trained by minimizing the empirical conditional coverage of the selected group (solved via Projected Gradient Descent with a minimum-group-size constraint), while the reconstruction decoder uses MSE loss to preserve interpretability. The final prediction set is the union of the standard split conformal set and T group-specific conformal sets, each calibrated on a different sampled subgroup. The paper also introduces WSC+, a nonlinear extension of the worst-slab coverage metric that replaces linear projections with quadratic functions to better detect unfairly treated groups defined by complex feature interactions.

## Results

On the ACSIncome dataset (20,000 samples, 4-class income classification), FAREG achieves a Group Coverage of 0.898 and WSC⁺ of 0.907, compared to AFCP2's 0.839 and 0.883 respectively, while maintaining a competitive average prediction set size of 3.31 (vs. 3.17 for AFCP2). On the synthetic XNOR-bias dataset, FAREG is the only method that consistently achieves the target 0.9 conditional coverage across all sample sizes, whereas AFCP2 only reaches 0.9 at sample size ≥500 and with substantially larger prediction sets. On the Nursery dataset, FAREG outperforms all baselines on both Group Coverage and WSC⁺ metrics while preserving most of the uncertainty information (set size).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FAREG against four baselines: Marginal (classic split CP, Romano et al. 2020b), Partial (equalized coverage on predefined single-feature groups, Romano et al. 2020a), AFCP1 (adaptive selection of one sensitive feature, Zhou & Sesia 2024), and AFCP2 (extended to two sensitive features with prior knowledge). FAREG consistently outperforms all baselines on Group Coverage and WSC⁺ across every dataset. On ACSIncome, FAREG improves Group Coverage by 5.9 percentage points over AFCP2 (0.898 vs. 0.839) and WSC⁺ by 2.4 points (0.907 vs. 0.883), at the cost of only a 0.14 increase in average set size. On the synthetic XNOR dataset, FAREG is the sole method achieving valid 0.9 coverage at all sample sizes, while AFCP2 requires sample size ≥500 and produces considerably larger (less informative) prediction sets. The efficiency gap is modest: FAREG's average set size is typically 0.1–0.2 larger than AFCP2's, a small trade-off for the substantial fairness gains. FAREG also significantly reduces wall-clock time compared to AFCP, whose Naive Bayes-based group scoring incurs O(N log N + NM) complexity versus FAREG's O(N+M) (disregarding constants).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive fair conformal prediction (AFCP) framework of Zhou & Sesia (2024), which itself builds on equalized-coverage conformal prediction (Romano et al., 2020a). The key departure is replacing AFCP's greedy, single-feature group selection (limited to linear, per-feature conditions) with a Deep Variational Information Bottleneck encoder-decoder that learns nonlinear latent representations for group identification. The paper also extends the WSC conditional-coverage metric (Cauchois et al., 2021) from linear slabs to quadratic (nonlinear) slabs, yielding WSC+. The theoretical grounding draws on VC-dimension analysis to argue that the learned representation space is a strict superset of AFCP's candidate group space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world algorithmic biases often concentrate on subgroups defined by nonlinear feature interactions (e.g., XOR/XNOR patterns) that single-feature or linear methods cannot detect. The variational encoder-decoder approach provides a principled, differentiable mechanism for discovering such groups while maintaining interpretability through reconstruction. Limitations include the interpretability trade-off of latent-space groups versus explicit feature-based groups, the computational cost of training the encoder-decoder (dominating runtime), and the reliance on a fixed group-size proportion δ. Open questions include scalability to high-dimensional continuous features, whether the learned groups are truly the most decision-relevant ones, and how the method behaves under distribution shift.
