## Problem

Discrete visual tokenizers achieve higher compression ratios than continuous ones but suffer from a persistent trade-off with reconstruction fidelity. Two specific bottlenecks limit progress: (1) Lookup-Free Quantization's entropy loss causes memory to scale linearly with codebook size, while Binary Spherical Quantization's independence assumption degrades performance; and (2) deterministic decoders output the conditional expectation of images, producing blurry results when a single compressed token maps to multiple plausible reconstructions at high compression ratios.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes 256×256 RGB images (randomly cropped during training) and produces 2D grids of binary discrete tokens at configurable downsampling rates (8×, 16×, or 32×), corresponding to compression ratios from 24× to 768×. The generative decoder additionally consumes a sampled Gaussian noise vector z concatenated along the channel dimension with the quantized latent U_Q, enabling stochastic reconstruction. For downstream generation, the discrete tokens feed into a LlamaGen-style autoregressive transformer that produces class-conditional 256×256 images.

## Method

WeTok introduces two core components. First, Group-Wise Lookup-Free Quantization (GQ) partitions the d-dimensional latent into g groups of d′ channels each, performing sign-based quantization per group with a non-learnable {−1,1}^{d′} codebook. This decomposes the entropy loss from the {−1,1}^d space into g independent {−1,1}^{d′} subspaces, eliminating the memory bottleneck of LFQ while provably (via lattice-theoretic refinement ordering) introducing strictly less approximation error than BSQ. Second, the Generative Decoder (GD) replaces the deterministic decoder with a conditional generative model: a noise vector z ~ N(0,I) is concatenated with U_Q along the channel dimension and fed into the decoder, so the GAN loss models the full conditional distribution p(image | U_Q) rather than a single-point reconstruction. Training uses a two-stage strategy—first standard reconstruction with GQ, then generative fine-tuning with zero-initialized new decoder channels to preserve pre-trained weights—combined with a constant learning rate schedule and a CNN encoder-decoder-discriminator architecture inherited from Open-MAGVIT2.

## Results

On the ImageNet 50k validation set at a high-fidelity setting (8× downsampling, 24× compression ratio), WeTok achieves a record-low zero-shot rFID of 0.12, outperforming the strongest continuous tokenizers FLUX-VAE (0.18) and SD-VAE 3.5 (0.19). At extreme compression (32× downsampling, 768× compression ratio), WeTok achieves rFID 3.49, substantially surpassing Cosmos (4.57) which operates at only half the compression ratio (384×). For class-conditional generation on ImageNet 256×256, WeTok-AR-XL (1.5B) achieves FID 2.31, IS 276.55, Precision 0.84, and Recall 0.55, setting a new state-of-the-art among autoregressive models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeTok establishes a new state-of-the-art trade-off between compression and reconstruction, outperforming all 37 compared baselines across the full compression spectrum. At the highest compression (768×), WeTok (rFID 3.49) beats Cosmos (4.57) by 23% while using double the compression ratio. At mid-range compression (384×), WeTok (rFID 1.58) surpasses Open-MAGVIT2 (2.55) and LlamaGen (2.47) by 38% and 36% respectively. At the high-fidelity regime (24× compression), WeTok (rFID 0.12) outperforms the strongest continuous tokenizers FLUX-VAE (0.18) and SD-VAE 3.5 (0.19) by 33% and 37%, despite being a discrete tokenizer with 4× higher compression. In in-distribution evaluation (Table 3), WeTok achieves rFID 0.61 at 16× downsampling, beating MGVQ (0.64), GigaTok (0.79), and Open-MAGVIT2 (1.17). For class-conditional generation, WeTok-AR-XL (FID 2.31) edges out Open-MAGVIT2-AR-XL (FID 2.33) and LlamaGen-XXL (FID 3.09), confirming that WeTok's tokens are effective for downstream autoregressive generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeTok directly extends the lookup-free quantization (LFQ) paradigm from MAGVIT-v2 and the binary spherical quantization (BSQ) approach by proposing Group-Wise Lookup-Free Quantization (GQ), which unifies both as special cases (g=1 recovers LFQ, g=d recovers BSQ) and provides a tunable intermediate regime with provably smaller approximation error than BSQ. The architecture follows Open-MAGVIT2's CNN encoder-decoder-discriminator design, and the autoregressive generation experiments reuse LlamaGen's transformer. The key conceptual departure is the Generative Decoder (GD), which is the first to introduce a noise-conditioned GAN-style decoder into a discrete tokenizer, shifting reconstruction from a deterministic mapping to a conditional generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WeTok demonstrates that discrete tokenizers can match or exceed the best continuous tokenizers (FLUX-VAE, SD-VAE 3.5) in reconstruction fidelity while retaining their inherent compression advantage, potentially reshaping the trade-off calculus for latent-space generative models. The finding that WeTok's outputs remain stable under iterative compression-decompression while FLUX-VAE and SD-VAE 3.5 collapse is a practical robustness property with implications for lossy transmission pipelines. Open questions include whether the generative decoder approach extends to video tokenization, whether the constant learning rate finding generalizes beyond the tested scale, and whether the lattice-theoretic error bounds remain tight at extreme codebook sizes.
