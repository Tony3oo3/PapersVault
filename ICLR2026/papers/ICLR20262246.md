## Problem

Transferring function-calling capabilities from large LLMs to super-tiny models (<1B parameters) is hampered by three intertwined failures: SFT causes overfitting and format memorization in low-capacity students, direct RL is unstable and inefficient at this scale, and the KD+RL paradigm introduces its own instabilities—top-k RKL divergences collapse training, binary rewards over-penalize valid alternative solutions, and KD/RL objectives interfere rather than synergize.

## Input / Output

The model receives a user query, a set of available function schemas (each with name, description, and typed parameters), and multi-turn conversation context, all formatted in the Qwen chat template. It must produce either one or more structured JSON function calls (with exactly 'name' and 'arguments' keys, where argument keys must be a subset of the schema) wrapped in tool_call tags, or a free-form natural language response when no tool is needed; the output must also contain a single think block for reasoning. The reward function enforces these format constraints as a hard gate before evaluating content correctness.

## Method

STAR combines two technical innovations within a three-stage curriculum (teacher refinement → student distillation → student refinement). First, Constrained Knowledge Distillation (CKD) replaces standard top-k FKL with an augmented objective that adds an L1 penalty on the student's top-m tokens falling outside the teacher's top-k set, suppressing confident-but-wrong predictions while preserving tail entropy for downstream RL exploration. Second, Sim-RL introduces a continuous, IoU-inspired similarity reward that greedily matches predicted and ground-truth tool calls and scores argument-level agreement (ROUGE-L F1 for strings, exact match for numerics/booleans), replacing brittle binary or AST-based rewards. The RL stage uses GRPO with group-relative advantage estimation and a DAPO-style filter that discards homogeneous rollout groups (all-correct or all-incorrect) to avoid wasted gradient computation.

## Results

On the BFCLv3 benchmark (Qwen3-0.6B), STAR (CKD+Sim-RL) achieves 51.70% overall accuracy, 78.65% non-live, 68.19% live, and 7.00% multi-turn accuracy. On ACEBench Normal, STAR scores 53.00% summary, 69.30% atom, 35.00% single-turn, 32.00% multi-turn, 62.00% similar-API, and 20.00% preference. The 0.6B STAR model surpasses all open-source models under 1B and even outperforms Llama3.1-8B (46.60 on ACEBench) and approaches Qwen3-8B (72.90 on ACEBench) with STAR-4B reaching 74.10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR (CKD+Sim-RL) outperforms all baselines on both benchmarks. On BFCLv3, it beats the strongest baseline SFT+Sim-RL* (50.41) by 1.29 points overall and LUFFY (49.23) by 2.47 points; on ACEBench it leads LUFFY (44.40) by 8.6 points and GKD (40.10) by 12.9 points. Standard SFT actually degrades performance below the base model on BFCLv3 (44.58 vs. 47.33) and catastrophically on ACEBench (2.10 vs. 27.20), confirming overfitting in super-tiny models. FKL distillation alone (49.51 BFCLv3, 36.80 ACEBench) provides a solid initialization but is surpassed by CKD (49.84, 39.00) before RL and by the full STAR pipeline after RL. ToolRL (47.35 BFCLv3, 29.40 ACEBench) and GKD (47.32, 40.10) both underperform STAR by 4+ points on BFCLv3 and 13+ points on ACEBench, indicating that neither specialized tool rewards nor online distillation with RL match the synergy of constrained distillation plus similarity-guided RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR builds directly on GRPO and DAPO for its RL optimization, but departs from the standard SFT+RL paradigm for small models by replacing SFT with Constrained Knowledge Distillation (CKD). It also diverges from prior KD work by identifying and fixing the instability of top-k Reverse KL divergence, and from prior RL reward designs by introducing a continuous, similarity-based reward (Sim-RL) instead of binary or AST-based signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that super-tiny models (<1B) can be made highly capable at function calling, which is critical for on-device and low-cost agentic AI. A key open question is whether the CKD+Sim-RL curriculum generalizes beyond function calling to other reasoning tasks like math or SQL. The paper also leaves room for exploring more sophisticated similarity metrics and multi-teacher strategies.
