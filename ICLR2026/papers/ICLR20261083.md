## Problem

Existing robust c-MARL methods assume a worst-case adversary that minimizes team reward, making them suboptimal against adversaries with different objectives (e.g., low-detectability attacks or failure-induced non-cooperation). Additionally, saddle-point training over a single adversarial policy suffers from non-convex optimization (local optima) and overfitting to one attack style, preventing adaptation to unseen adversaries at deployment. The paper asks: can a single cooperative policy be trained to adapt to a diverse continuum of adversarial objectives without knowing which one it will face?

## Input / Output

Each cooperative agent receives its own observation-action-reward history τ^i and a belief vector b^i over K+1 adversarial types (no-attack plus K severity levels), maintained by a shared RNN trained with cross-entropy against the true type. The output is a single parameterized cooperative policy π^i(·|τ^i, b^i) that conditions on the belief to select an appropriate response, alongside K adversarial policies ρ^{v,k} (one per victim-severity pair) that are used only during training as an approximate min-oracle. Parameter sharing is used across all cooperative agents, and only one of the K adversarial networks is updated per cooperative update step.

## Method

BATPAL formulates robustness as a Bayesian Dec-POMDP game and computes a perfect Bayesian equilibrium via a reference-value-based partitioning scheme: adversarial policies are grouped into K severity bands based on the team reward they impose against a pre-trained cooperative reference policy π_0, and the worst-case policy within each band serves as the representative for that discrete type. To find each representative, the paper introduces externally-constrained RL, where the adversary must minimize team reward under MDP_1 (induced by the current cooperative policy) while keeping its value under MDP_0 (induced by π_0) within the severity band [l,h]; this differs from standard constrained RL because the objective and constraint are evaluated on different MDPs with different trajectories. The constrained problem is solved via a log-barrier formulation with a PPO-based update (EC-PPO) that clips policy changes to maintain feasibility, with a provable convergence guarantee to a KKT point. The cooperative and adversarial policies are then trained via simultaneous two-timescale gradient descent-ascent (α_n ≥ β_n), where the cooperative side uses MAPPO with the belief as an additional input, and the adversarial side uses EC-PPO, so the adversary acts as an approximate min-oracle while appearing quasi-static to the cooperative agents.

## Results

BATPAL achieves near-optimal non-adversarial performance (0.98–1.00 win rate/reward) across all four environments, matching or exceeding vanilla MAPPO. In SMAC-1c3s5z, BATPAL maintains a 0.87–0.96 win rate across all four severity levels and against all baseline-trained attacks, while EIR-MAPPO drops to 0.53 against Severity 1 attacks. Against unseen dynamic adversaries (ACT, DYN-1, DYN-2), BATPAL achieves 0.52–0.81 in MPE-Spread and 0.50–0.72 in SMAC-2s3z, substantially outperforming baselines that were not trained on these attack types. The performance gap to the Known Type (KT) oracle upper bound is minimal in most settings, indicating near no-regret robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BATPAL consistently outperforms all five baselines. Against EIR-MAPPO (Li et al., ICLR 2024), BATPAL achieves 0.87–0.96 win rate in SMAC-1c3s5z versus EIR-MAPPO's 0.53 against Severity 1 attacks, and in SMAC-2s3z BATPAL scores 0.50–0.72 against unseen attacks while EIR-MAPPO's own adversarial policy (A-EIR-MAPPO) yields only 0.10–0.38. Against Gen-Maxmin (Liu et al., ICLR 2024), BATPAL's 0.89 win rate in SMAC-1c3s5z exceeds Gen-Maxmin's performance, and in MPE-Spread BATPAL scores 0.80 against A-Gen-Maxmin attacks versus the No Belief variant's 0.61. Against RAP (Vinitsky et al., 2020), BATPAL achieves 0.92 in SMAC-1c3s5z versus RAP's substantially lower scores. Against vanilla MAPPO (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2022), BATPAL matches or slightly exceeds non-adversarial performance (1.00 vs. ~1.00) while providing robustness that MAPPO completely lacks. Against ROMANCE (Yuan et al., AAAI 2023), the gap is stark: in SMAC-2s3z, ROMANCE drops to 0.05–0.23 win rate under attacks while BATPAL maintains 0.50–0.98, though the comparison is noted as unfair since ROMANCE targets budget-limited attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust MARL literature (RARL, M3DDPG, EIR-MAPPO, Gen-Maxmin) which predominantly targets worst-case adversaries, by formulating a Bayesian Dec-POMDP with a continuum of adversarial types representing distinct attack objectives. It builds on the adversarial training with min-oracle framework of Kalogiannis et al. (2022) for the simultaneous gradient updates, and adapts PPO with log-barrier constraints (drawing on Usmanova et al., 2024) to solve the novel externally-constrained RL subproblem where the objective and constraint correspond to different MDPs. The key departure from Li et al. (2024), which also uses a Bayesian game formulation, is the introduction of severity-based partitioning of the adversarial type space, replacing a single worst-case type with K discrete severity levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world adversaries in multi-agent systems (e.g., compromised autonomous vehicles, hacked network nodes) rarely optimize a pure worst-case objective, yet most robust MARL methods assume they do. The severity-based partitioning provides clean theoretical guarantees (KL-divergence lower bounds on policy diversity, severity-dependent regret bounds) that justify why exposure to structured adversarial diversity outperforms both single max-min training and unstructured ensembles. Limitations include the need for K separate adversarial networks (computational overhead), the assumption of at most one victim agent in the main formulation, and the fact that even perfect belief does not eliminate regret against truly unseen attack types. Open questions include scaling to continuous action spaces, multiple simultaneous victims without combinatorial explosion, and whether the partitioning granularity K can be adapted online.
