## Problem

Existing cloth dynamics learning methods rely on known physical properties (material parameters, mesh labels) as supervision or inputs, limiting their applicability when environmental conditions are unknown. This paper introduces Cloth Dynamics Grounding (CDG), a new scenario for unsupervised learning of cloth dynamics purely from multi-view visual observations, and identifies that particle-based representations are unsuitable for cloth due to its thin structure and small relative position variations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264842/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view video sequences (800×800, 30 camera views per trajectory) and an initial cloth mesh (extracted from the first frame via 2D Gaussian Splatting, TSDF fusion, and Marching Cubes) along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It produces a predicted 3D mesh sequence (world-space node coordinates over time), rendered cloth-motion videos via the DVC forward process, and novel-view synthesis of the dynamic scene. The initial mesh is the only 3D input required; all subsequent mesh states are recovered from pixel space through differentiable backpropagation.

## Method

CloDS uses a three-stage pipeline: (1) constructing mesh-anchored Gaussian components from the first frame via barycentric interpolation, (2) recursively extracting per-frame meshes by backpropagating Δx^W through a Spatial Mapping Gaussian Splatting (SMGS) renderer with an edge-length preservation loss, and (3) training a Graph Neural Network (MGN) on the extracted mesh sequence with a rollout strategy. The key architectural contribution is dual-position opacity modulation: an MLP predicts each Gaussian's opacity from both its world-space (relative) coordinates and mesh-space (absolute) coordinates, simultaneously preventing perspective distortion artifacts from large deformations and avoiding transparency in previously unseen regions. This differentiable 2D-to-3D mapping bridges pixel-space supervision to 3D geometry, enabling the GNN to learn cloth dynamics entirely from video without any physics-based labels.

## Results

On the primary Cloth Dynamics Grounding (CDG) task, CloDS (trained on videos from 51–100 trajectories) achieves a rollout RMSE of 0.1339 on unviewed extrapolation, approaching MGN (0.1314) which is trained with full mesh supervision. In the DVC forward process (video prediction), CloDS achieves 26.62 dB PSNR and 0.899 LPIPS, substantially outperforming the best video prediction baseline SimVP (25.48 dB, 1.020 LPIPS). For dynamic scene novel view synthesis, SMGS reaches 36.24 dB PSNR, surpassing GaMeS (33.02 dB) and M5D-GS (29.34 dB) by 3.2 and 6.9 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CDG, CloDS (video-only, 50 trajectories) outperforms MGN* (mesh-supervised, 50 trajectories) by 0.0023 RMSE on unviewed extrapolation (0.1339 vs. 0.1362), confirming that visual supervision can rival partial mesh supervision. With all 100 training videos, CloDS** (0.1325) nearly matches fully mesh-supervised MGN (0.1314), a gap of only 0.0011. In video prediction, CloDS leads SimVP by 1.14 dB PSNR and MMVP by 2.65 dB, with the largest margin over MAU (2.90 dB), attributed to 3D-aware modeling that avoids temporal inconsistency under self-occlusion. For novel view synthesis, SMGS outperforms GaMeS by 3.2 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 5.21 to 3.53, while dynamic scene methods (4DGS, MSTH, M5D-GS) all fall below 30 dB due to their inability to handle large cloth deformations. In geometry-aware comparisons, CloDS substantially outperforms both LIP (point-cloud based, which collapses) and CS (mesh-based, which is more stable but still inferior), as shown qualitatively in Figure 9a.

## Relation to Prior Work

CloDS sits at the intersection of mesh-based cloth simulation (MGN, Baraff & Witkin), mesh-anchored Gaussian splatting (GaMeS, 3DGS), and Differentiable Visual Computing (NeuroFluid, DEL). It departs from particle-based DVC approaches (NeuroFluid for fluids, DEL for solids) by arguing that cloth's thin structure and small relative position variations make particle representations unsuitable, instead adopting mesh-anchored Gaussians with a novel dual-position opacity modulation. Unlike prior mesh-based cloth simulators that require physics-based supervision (known material parameters, pre-trained GNNs), CloDS learns dynamics entirely from multi-view video, and unlike video prediction models, it explicitly reasons about 3D geometric structure to maintain temporal consistency under severe self-occlusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CloDS introduces Cloth Dynamics Grounding (CDG) as a formal problem and provides the first unsupervised visual-only solution, opening a path toward learning deformable-body dynamics without access to physical parameters—a key bottleneck in robotics and vision. The dual-position opacity modulation is a practical, generalizable fix for a rendering artifact that plagues all mesh-based Gaussian splatting under large deformation. However, the method still requires an initial mesh estimate (via 2DGS + TSDF), primarily validates on synthetic FLAGSIMPLE data, and shows measurable degradation under lighting and real-world conditions, leaving open questions about robustness in uncontrolled environments and scalability to multi-object scenes with diverse materials.
