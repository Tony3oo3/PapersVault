## Problem

Existing latent action models for VLA pre-training compress motion into latent tokens using only visual reconstruction, causing physically critical but visually subtle motions (end-effector rotations, gripper movements) to be underrepresented and leaving latents physically ungrounded. Additionally, prior methods integrate latent actions into policy training in limited ways—LAPA uses them only for weight initialization, Go-1 suffers from teacher-forcing inconsistencies—failing to exploit their potential as a structured mid-level planning representation.

## Input / Output

The LAM consumes 8-frame video clips at 224×224 resolution and, for robot data, proprioceptive states plus an embodiment context vector (learnable dataset-ID embedding concatenated with sinusoidal control-frequency encoding). The ACT module takes the current RGB observation, a language instruction, proprioceptive state, embodiment context, and optionally a wrist-camera image (extracted via ResNet-18 into 16 tokens, randomly masked 50% during training). Output is a joint diffusion over a robot action chunk of m=4 steps and a latent action plan of n=6 steps, where the latent plan serves as a mid-level motion abstraction bridging high-level vision-language understanding and low-level control.

## Method

The framework has two core components. First, the Latent Action Model (LAM) extends the standard IDM + visual FDM + VQ pipeline with an additional proprioceptive FDM (a 2-layer MLP) that predicts future robot states and actions K steps ahead, conditioned on the current state, latent token, and embodiment context; this jointly optimizes visual reconstruction and physical state forecasting so that latent tokens emphasize dynamics-aligned motion rather than arbitrary pixel changes. Second, the ACTor module factorizes the policy into a latent action expert (ACT-latent) and a robot action expert (ACT-robot) within a single conditional flow-matching (diffusion) network, where blockwise causal attention lets the robot expert condition on predicted latents, and a stochastic masking strategy (50% full mask, 50% random half-mask of latent tokens) prevents the robot branch from over-relying on latents. Training proceeds in three stages: LAM pretraining on 1.6M robot trajectories plus 3.6M human video clips, joint ACT pretraining on the same mixture, and embodiment-specific finetuning.

## Results

On the SIMPLER benchmark, villa-X achieves 77.7% average success on the Google Robot and 62.5% on the WidowX Robot, outperforming all baselines including GR00T-N1.5 (57.9%/62.0%), Magma (62.3%/44.8%), and LAPA (N/A/57.3%). On the LIBERO benchmark, villa-X reaches 90.1% average success across four task suites, surpassing the strongest baseline π₀-FAST (85.5%) by 4.6 points. On real-world robots, villa-X outperforms π₀, GR00T, and OpenVLA-OFT on the Realman gripper platform and GR-1/GR00T on the XHand dexterous hand platform, with the latent-action expert contributing a consistent 5–10 point improvement over the ablated variant (w/o latent) across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SIMPLER, villa-X (77.7% Google / 62.5% WidowX) substantially outperforms all VLA-only baselines: RoboVLMs (60.8%/37.5%), π₀-FAST (61.9%/32.1%), and OpenVLA-OFT (63.0%/N/A), with the largest gap on the Google Robot Pick task (98.7% vs. 77.3% for RoboVLMs). Among latent-action and world-modeling methods, villa-X beats GR00T-N1.5 (57.9%/62.0%) on Google Robot by 19.8 points and matches it on WidowX (62.5% vs. 62.0%), while surpassing LAPA (57.3% WidowX) by 5.2 points and Magma (62.3%/44.8%) by 15.2 points on WidowX. On LIBERO, villa-X achieves 90.1% average, exceeding π₀-FAST (85.5%) by 4.6 points and π₀ (81.1%) by 9.0 points, with the most pronounced gain on the Long suite (74.5% vs. 60.2% for π₀-FAST). On the Realman gripper platform, villa-X leads on Pick-out (100% vs. 80% for w/o latent, 70% for π₀) and Unstack (100% vs. 80% for OpenVLA-OFT), and achieves the best generalization scores (60% on both color and table-cover changes vs. 40–50% for baselines). On the XHand dexterous hand, villa-X outperforms GR-1 and GR00T across all five tasks in both seen and unseen settings, e.g., 84% vs. 56% (GR-1) and 44% (GR00T) on seen Pick&Place, and 68% vs. 40%/28% on unseen Pick&Place. The consistent 5–10 point advantage of villa-X over its own w/o-latent ablation across all benchmarks confirms that the latent-action expert is the primary source of the performance gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent action pretraining paradigm for VLA models established by LAPA, MoTo, GR00T, and Go-1, which learn discrete latent tokens from video via inverse/forward dynamics models. The key departure is twofold: (1) adding a proprioceptive Forward Dynamics Model to ground latent actions in physical robot dynamics rather than purely visual changes, and (2) replacing sequential or loosely-coupled integration of latent actions with a joint flow-matching diffusion framework where a latent action expert and robot action expert share a blockwise causal attention structure. The architecture also draws on HPT-style embodiment-specific projection layers and PaliGemma as the vision-language backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core bottleneck in VLA generalization: how to leverage abundant action-free human video to improve robot policies across embodiments. The finding that visually subtle but physically critical motions (gripper, end-effector rotation) are underrepresented in purely visual latent actions, and that proprioceptive grounding fixes this, provides a principled design principle for future latent action models. The zero-shot transfer to an unseen robot arm and open-vocabulary symbol understanding is promising but demonstrated primarily through qualitative rollouts rather than quantitative benchmarks. A key open question the authors flag is whether the latent expert's planning capability can be further exploited via learned critics or rejection sampling, which could unlock more deliberate multi-step reasoning.
