## Problem

LLM-based agents face a fundamental safety-efficiency trade-off: online roll-out planners (e.g., ReAct with MCTS or beam search) require dozens of real environment interactions per episode, incurring high latency, cost, and risk of irreversible side effects, while pure imitation or reward-model agents act greedily with no foresight and are brittle to single missteps. No existing approach provides multi-step lookahead without expensive real-time environment queries or fine-tuning the policy LLM.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural-language task instruction and a structured observation (a DOM tree tokenized via depth-first traversal with textual content, bounding boxes, and element types, or a text-based scene description for embodied tasks) along with the interaction history. It produces a discrete next action, a short natural-language reflection (≤30 tokens) explaining why the selected imagined branch is promising and flagging risks, and a terse trajectory summary of core actions. These two text artifacts are concatenated into the frozen LLM's prompt to condition the action, so the LLM's parameters are never updated.

## Method

DreamPhase trains a compact latent world model (an RSSM with a GRU recurrent core, diagonal Gaussian stochastic latent, and transformer encoder/decoder) on logged (instruction, observation, action, next-observation) tuples from the training split, enabling offline simulation of multi-step futures in latent space. At each decision step, the agent encodes the current observation, generates M parallel rollouts of H steps by alternating the learned transition model with actions sampled from a frozen policy LLM, scores each branch with a discounted value-head return minus a Monte-Carlo-dropout mutual-information uncertainty penalty, and applies a safety gate that defers to real interaction if the best branch's uncertainty exceeds a threshold τ. The selected low-risk branch is distilled by a lightweight reflection head and summarizer into concise natural-language guidance that is injected into the LLM prompt, steering behavior without any parameter updates. A regret bound of O(√(Tε)) + BρT links decision quality to world-model approximation error and mis-gating frequency, and the design reduces real API calls by ~4× on WebShop while cutting executed irreversible actions by ~5×.

## Results

On the diverse 8-task benchmark (Table 1), DreamPhase achieves 50.1% average success with a LLaMA-2-7B backbone, outperforming the strongest open-source baseline ARMAP-M (42.3%) by 7.8 points and AgentGym (39.1%) by 11.0 points. On WebShop specifically, DreamPhase reaches 61.8% success with only 9.3 API calls per episode versus 39.8 for ARMAP-M (a 4.3× reduction), while also cutting per-step latency from ~255 ms to ~84 ms. Cross-backbone results (Table 2) confirm consistent gains: DreamPhase leads on all four backbones (Llama-70B, Llama-8B, Mistral-7B, Phi-3-8B) with the largest margin on Game-of-24 (e.g., 28.0 vs. 24.0 on Llama-70B). On ALFWorld, DreamPhase improves success from 0.31/0.46 (ARMAP-M) to 0.39/0.49 (std/dev) while using ~4× fewer real interactions (11.6 vs. 46.7 API calls/episode).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-source baselines (all using LLaMA-2-7B), DreamPhase decisively outperforms ARMAP-M (42.3→50.1 avg, +7.8 pts), AgentGym (39.1→50.1, +11.0 pts), and AgentLM (5.3→50.1, +44.8 pts), with the largest per-task gains on SciWorld (+21.2 over ARMAP-M) and Wordle (+17.0). Against closed-source references, DreamPhase (50.1) surpasses GPT-3.5-Turbo (28.5), DeepSeek-Chat (34.0), and Claude-3-Haiku (19.6), and approaches Claude-3-Sonnet (39.8) while trailing GPT-4-Turbo (63.9) by 13.8 points—expected given the 7B vs. proprietary scale gap. On the cross-backbone WebShop/SciWorld/Game-of-24 suite (Table 2), DreamPhase beats all ARMAP variants (R/B/M) on every backbone, with the clearest advantage on Game-of-24 (e.g., 28.0 vs. 24.0 on Llama-70B) and SW-unseen (58.6 vs. 55.9 on Llama-70B), indicating robustness to distribution shift. In terms of interaction cost, DreamPhase uses 4.3× fewer real API calls than ARMAP-M on WebShop (9.3 vs. 39.8) and ~4× fewer on ALFWorld (11.6 vs. 46.7), while simultaneously reducing executed irreversible actions by ~5× (1.4 vs. 7.1 critical incidents per 1,000 episodes on WebShop).

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamPhase synthesizes three established threads: latent world models for offline simulation (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018; Hafner et al., 2020, via RSSM-style stochastic dynamics), uncertainty-aware model trust (Janner et al., 2019, via a safety gate that defers to real interaction when predictions are unreliable), and language-based self-reflection for policy steering (Shinn et al., 2024). It departs from online search planners like ARMAP (Chen et al., 2025), which perform expensive token-level MCTS/Reflexion against the live environment, and from fine-tuning approaches like AgentLM (Zeng et al., 2024), by keeping the policy LLM entirely frozen and relocating all exploration into a compact learned latent simulator. The key architectural choice is a modular encoder–transition–decoder world model trained on logged trajectories, paired with a lightweight value head and Monte-Carlo-dropout uncertainty proxy, rather than prompting the LLM itself to imagine future states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the 4× reduction in real environment interactions (≈10 vs. ≈40 API calls on WebShop) and the ~5× drop in executed irreversible actions, which directly address cost and safety bottlenecks in deploying LLM agents in production settings like e-commerce or robotics. The regret bound O(√(Tε)) + BρT provides a theoretical guarantee linking decision quality to world-model error and mis-gating frequency, a rare formalization in the LLM-agent literature. Open questions include how the approach scales to truly open-ended environments where the action space is unbounded, whether the compact world model can capture long-horizon compositional dynamics beyond the 4–6 step horizons tested here, and whether the language-reflection interface remains effective as task complexity and instruction ambiguity grow.
