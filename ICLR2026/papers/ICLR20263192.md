## Problem

Microscaling quantization formats are expected to benefit from smaller block sizes, yet the paper discovers a perplexity inversion where reducing block size below a threshold paradoxically increases quantization error. This anomaly is driven by the limited dynamic range of FP8 UE4M3 scale factors, which cannot adequately represent the scales of narrow-distribution blocks, and has not been previously identified or explained.

## Input / Output

The method consumes BF16 LLM weight and activation tensors and produces blocks of N FP4 E2M1 elements (N ∈ {2,4,8,16,32,64,128,256}) each paired with a single FP8 UE5M3 scale factor. The UE5M3 format repurposes the unused sign bit of the conventional UE4M3 scale as an additional exponent bit, extending the minimum non-zero representable scale from 2⁻⁹ to 2⁻¹⁷ while preserving the 3-bit mantissa, thereby eliminating the need for a separate per-tensor global scaling operation on weights and activations.

## Method

The paper first establishes a theoretical framework that decomposes the total quantization MSE into three contributions: (1) error on non-maximum elements x_i ≠ x_max under non-zero quantized scales, (2) error on the block-maximum element x_i = x_max that becomes non-zero once scales are quantized, and (3) catastrophic zero-rounding of entire blocks when the scale falls below the lowest representable FP8 value. This framework, validated to χ² ≈ 4×10⁻⁸ against Normal-distribution experiments, shows that contributions (2) and (3) dominate for narrow distributions and smaller block sizes, explaining the inversion. To mitigate this, the authors propose FP8 UE5M3 scales, which extend the exponent field by one bit to widen the dynamic range, and demonstrate via systolic-array synthesis at 4 nm that the hardware overhead is negligible (0.5% area increase, 4 ps critical-path delay). Across granite-3.3-8b, llama-3.1-8b, nemotron-nano-9b-v2, and bamba-9b-v2, UE5M3 matches or exceeds the accuracy of UE4M3 with per-tensor scaling while avoiding the global scaling operation entirely.

## Results

At block size 8, the proposed FP4 microscaling with FP8 UE5M3 scales (no per-tensor scaling) achieves WikiText-2 perplexity of 5.04 on granite-3.3-8b, 6.79 on llama-3.1-8b, 8.39 on nemotron-nano-9b-v2, and 6.53 on bamba-9b-v2, matching or exceeding the UE4M3 + per-tensor scaling baseline (5.39, 6.76, 8.42, 6.53 respectively) while eliminating the need for global scaling operations. On bamba-9b-v2, UE5M3 reduces WikiText-2 perplexity from 21.25 (UE4M3 alone) to 6.53, nearly recovering the BF16 baseline of 6.21. Across accuracy benchmarks (PIQA, HellaSwag, Winogrande, GSM8K, MMLU), UE5M3 consistently matches or slightly exceeds UE4M3-S, e.g., achieving 56.17% GSM8K on granite-3.3-8b versus 44.88% for UE4M3-S.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is NVIDIA's cuBLAS D-block quantization, evaluated in two variants: UE4M3 (standard NVFP4 microscaling with FP8 E4M3 scales) and UE4M3-S (UE4M3 augmented with per-tensor scaling). The proposed UE5M3 format matches or exceeds UE4M3-S across all four models without requiring any per-tensor scaling operation. On granite-3.3-8b, UE5M3 achieves 5.04 WikiText-2 perplexity versus 5.39 for UE4M3-S (a 0.35 improvement) and 56.17% GSM8K versus 44.88% for UE4M3-S. On bamba-9b-v2, both UE5M3 and UE4M3-S achieve 6.53 perplexity, but UE5M3 edges ahead on Winogrande (72.61 vs. 71.90) and MMLU (64.11 vs. 63.80). On llama-3.1-8b and nemotron-nano-9b-v2, UE5M3 is essentially tied with UE4M3-S (within 0.03 perplexity), confirming that the wider dynamic range of UE5M3 obviates the need for global scaling. In all cases, UE5M3 dramatically outperforms UE4M3 without per-tensor scaling, which suffers severe perplexity inversion at small block sizes (e.g., 21.25 vs. 6.53 on bamba-9b-v2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OCP microscaling format framework (Rouhani et al., 2023) and the broader quantization granularity trend (tensor-wide → per-channel → per-group → per-block) by identifying a fundamental limit to the 'smaller is better' assumption for block size. It builds directly on the NVFP4 format (FP4 E2M1 elements with FP8 UE4M3 scales) and the per-tensor scaling strategy in NVIDIA's cuBLAS implementation, but departs from both by showing that the scale quantization itself—not the element quantization—is the bottleneck for narrow distributions. The theoretical framework generalizes beyond FP4/UE4M3 to INT4, FP6 scales, and arbitrary block sizes, positioning the work as a design-space analysis tool for future microscaling formats.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As FP4 microscaling becomes the de-facto standard for LLM inference on commercial accelerators, the discovery that perplexity can *increase* at smaller block sizes challenges the core design principle driving the field toward finer granularity. The theoretical framework, validated to χ² ≈ 4×10⁻⁸ across multiple data types, provides a principled tool for predicting quantization error before hardware commitment. The UE5M3 proposal is attractive because it avoids the practical pitfalls of per-tensor scaling (outlier sensitivity, on-the-fly activation absmax cost) at negligible hardware overhead (~0.5% area). Open questions include whether the anomaly persists during quantization-aware training, whether even smaller block sizes (2, 4) reveal further degradation, and whether the framework can guide sub-4-bit element format design.
