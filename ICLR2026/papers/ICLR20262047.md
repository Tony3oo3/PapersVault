## Problem

Existing video diffusion models, even at scale, are largely confined to generating single-shot sequences; when multi-shot content is attempted, transitions are unstable, uncontrollable, and fail to follow film-editing conventions. No prior method provides frame-level control over where cinematic transitions occur while preserving both intra-shot continuity and inter-shot semantic coherence aligned with professional editing style.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262047/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a hierarchical text prompt (a general video description plus a specified shot count, optionally expanded into per-shot captions) and predefined transition-point timestamps indicating frame indices where cuts should occur. It produces a single coherent multi-shot video at 720P with cinematic hard cuts at the specified positions. The framework supports both a training-free mode (mask applied to a pretrained model) and a fine-tuned mode, and can incorporate LoRA weights for character/style customization without retraining the mask mechanism.

## Method

The core insight is that attention maps in video diffusion models naturally exhibit a block-diagonal structure for multi-shot content—strong intra-shot frame correlations and weak inter-shot correlations—quantified by an average intra/inter-shot attention ratio of 26.68 with Pearson r=0.71 against ground-truth boundaries. Building on this, CineTrans introduces a block-diagonal attention mask that sets inter-shot attention scores to −∞ at selected transformer layers, forcing the model to generate distinct shots with transitions at user-specified positions. A Visible-First-Frame Attention variant additionally keeps the first temporal slice visible to all tokens to stabilize generation. The mask is applied to the last six layers in U-Net architectures (LaVie) and middle layers 7–28 in DiT architectures (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B), with the specific layer selection guided by empirical attention-map analysis. Fine-tuning on the newly constructed Cine250K dataset (250K video-text pairs with frame-level shot labels and hierarchical captions from Vimeo) further aligns generated transitions with film-editing conventions, while the mask alone already enables effective multi-shot generation in a training-free setting.

## Results

CineTrans achieves near-perfect cinematic transition control, with CineTrans-Unet scoring 0.8598 and CineTrans-DiT scoring 0.7003 on the Transition Control Score, substantially outperforming the best baseline (HunyuanVideo at 0.3787). CineTrans-DiT also leads in Aesthetic Quality (0.6508), Semantic Consistency (0.2109), and Intra-shot consistency (Subject 0.9673, Background 0.9775). On inter-shot consistency, CineTrans-Unet achieves the highest Semantic Score (0.8095) and lowest Semantic Gap (0.2444), while CineTrans-DiT achieves the best Visual Score (0.7874) and lowest Visual Gap (0.1901), indicating alignment with film-editing style.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CineTrans decisively outperforms all baselines on Transition Control Score: CineTrans-Unet (0.8598) and CineTrans-DiT (0.7003) versus the best baseline HunyuanVideo (0.3787), a gap of over 2×. Against large-scale T2V models (HunyuanVideo, Wanx2.1-T2V-turbo, CogVideoX), CineTrans achieves substantially higher inter-shot semantic consistency scores (0.8095/0.7858 vs. 0.5150–0.6431) and lower consistency gaps, indicating generated transitions more closely match film-editing conventions. Against the multi-shot pipeline StoryDiffusion+CogVideoX I2V, CineTrans-DiT surpasses it on Aesthetic Quality (0.6508 vs. 0.6296) and Semantic Consistency (0.2109 vs. 0.2091) while achieving far superior transition control. Against the customization model HunyuanVideo+Cinematron, CineTrans-DiT wins on every measured dimension including Transition Control (0.7003 vs. 0.3787) and inter-shot visual consistency. The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is Intra-shot Subject consistency, where StoryDiffusion+CogVideoX I2V scores 0.9783 vs. CineTrans-DiT's 0.9673, a marginal difference likely attributable to the base model's single-shot quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

CineTrans builds on two base video diffusion architectures—LaVie (U-Net) and Wan2.1 (DiT)—and departs from prior multi-shot generation approaches that either concatenate separately generated shots (StoryDiffusion, Animate-a-Story, VGoT) or modify model architectures with specialized tokens or positional encodings (ShotAdapter, Mask²DiT, LCT). Its key conceptual departure is the empirical observation that diffusion models' attention maps already encode shot-boundary structure as block-diagonal patterns, which the authors exploit through a simple additive mask on attention scores rather than architectural changes. This positions the work at the intersection of temporal-controlled video generation (VSTAR's band-matrix attention regularization) and masked attention mechanisms, but applied specifically to shot-level temporal control in a training-free-capable setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that pretrained video diffusion models internally represent shot boundaries in their attention maps (r=0.71 Pearson correlation with ground-truth transitions) offers a new interpretability lens into how these models structure temporal content, and the resulting mask mechanism is a lightweight, transferable technique applicable to any diffusion-based video model. Cine250K is the first multi-shot video dataset with frame-level shot labels and hierarchical captions, filling a data gap that has limited prior work. Open questions remain around camera-viewpoint controllability, extension to longer multi-shot sequences via autoregressive generation, and fine-grained spatial consistency across shots; the current system also handles only hard cuts (99.58% of transitions), leaving gradual transitions (fades, dissolves) as an underexplored direction.
