## Problem

ODE-based Flow Matching cannot model transport strategies that require trajectory intersections (due to Lipschitz uniqueness of ODE solutions), cannot recover dynamics governed by intrinsic delays, and cannot achieve exact transport between heterogeneous distributions where the source has fewer modes than the target. These limitations cause significant mismatches between the modeled transfer process and real-world phenomena, particularly when coupling constraints or structural information between distributions must be preserved.

## Input / Output

The method takes paired source and target samples (x0, x1) coupled via OT or KPG-OT, a time delay τ, and an initial function ψ defined over [-τ, 0] (either constant at x0 or with a task-specific constant derivative C_mn per cluster pair). The trained model is a DDE vector field v(t, x, x_τ; θ) that consumes both the current state x(t) and the delayed state x(t−τ) at each integration step. For image generation, input is a source image (e.g., MNIST digit or CIFAR-10 sample) and output is a generated target image; for single-cell tasks, input is an initial cell state and output is a predicted cell distribution at a future time point; for dynamics reconstruction, input is snapshot data from a delay system and output is the recovered vector field enabling trajectory prediction.

## Method

DFM replaces the ODE vector field in Flow Matching with a DDE vector field u(t, x, x_τ) that depends on both the current state and a delayed state, enabling trajectory intersections and capturing delay dynamics. Training uses a Delay Conditional FM (DCFM) objective that regresses the parameterized DDE vector field on a conditional vector field derived from an interpolation path γ(t; x0, x1) (linear, geodesic, or cubic spline), with the latent variable z being the entire path rather than just endpoints. Two variants address different limitations: DFM(C) uses a constant initial function ψ*(t;x0)≡x0 and provably achieves universal approximation of any continuous transport map; DFM(D) assigns distinct initial functions with different constant time derivatives C_mn to each source-target cluster pair, resolving singularities when the source has fewer modes than the target. Generation proceeds by forward-integrating the learned DDE with a piecewise ODE solver, where the delayed state is tracked explicitly during integration.

## Results

On the mouse hematopoiesis scRNA-seq dataset, OT-DFM(D) achieves the best final-time W2 of 0.095 and MMD of 0.010, substantially outperforming OT-CFM (0.192 / 0.047), TIGON (0.264 / 0.155), and MIOFlow (0.220 / 0.056). On the qPCR iPSC bifurcation dataset, OT-DFM(D) similarly leads with W2(F)=0.213 and MMD(F)=0.027. For image generation, KP-DFM(C) reduces MNIST FID from 45.02 (KP-CFM) to 11.75 at τ=0.25, and OT-DFM(D) achieves FID 9.29 vs. 12.03 for OT-CFM on CIFAR-10 at 40 NFE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OT-CFM (Tong et al., 2023), DFM wins on every metric across both single-cell datasets: on mouse hematopoiesis, OT-DFM(D) halves the final-time W2 (0.095 vs. 0.192) and MMD (0.010 vs. 0.047), and on qPCR iPSC it reduces W2(F) from 0.226 to 0.213 and MMD(F) from 0.030 to 0.027. Against TIGON (Sha et al., 2024), DFM achieves roughly 2–3× lower final-time W2 on both datasets (e.g., 0.095 vs. 0.264 on hematopoiesis; 0.213 vs. 0.695 on iPSC). Against MIOFlow (Huguet et al., 2022), DFM similarly dominates, with W2(F) of 0.095 vs. 0.220 on hematopoiesis and 0.213 vs. 0.345 on iPSC. On image generation, KP-DFM(C) reduces MNIST FID by ~74% over KP-CFM (11.75 vs. 45.02), and OT-DFM(D) outperforms OT-CFM on CIFAR-10 at every NFE setting (e.g., 9.29 vs. 12.03 at 40 NFE; 18.60 vs. 27.51 at 20 NFE). The advantage is most pronounced under heterogeneous distributions and trajectory-crossing scenarios, where ODE-based baselines structurally cannot preserve the prescribed coupling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Flow Matching (FM) / Conditional FM (CFM) framework by replacing the underlying Neural ODE with a Neural Delay Differential Equation (DDE), drawing on the Neural DDE literature (Zhu et al., 2020) for the solver and the delay Fokker-Planck equation for the probability flow. It departs from recent ODE-based extensions (Constant Acceleration Flow, Hierarchical Rectified Flow, Switched Flow Matching) that address trajectory intersections or distributional heterogeneity by introducing auxiliary conditioning variables or redesigned transport paths; DFM instead resolves both issues through a single delay term in the vector field plus task-specific initial functions, without augmenting the phase space. The coupling strategy leverages Keypoint-guided Optimal Transport (KPG-OT) to preserve known structural constraints during transport.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DFM provides a theoretically grounded (universal approximation for continuous transport maps) and practically effective alternative to ODE-based generative models, with particular relevance to single-cell trajectory inference where distributional heterogeneity at bifurcation points is inherent. The ability to recover true delay dynamics from snapshot data opens a path for learning biologically meaningful delay parameters from observational data. Open questions include extending to multiple or distributed delays, reducing the computational overhead of DDE integration at scale, and whether the diverse initial function strategy generalizes beyond the clustering-based assignment used here. The method remains confined to a single fixed delay, and the CIFAR-10 results, while competitive, do not yet match the best ODE-based rectified flow methods at high NFE.
