## Problem

Joint text-speech spoken language modeling suffers from a fundamental length mismatch: speech token sequences are typically 10–25× longer than their text counterparts, forcing complex interleaving or padding heuristics. Moreover, existing speech tokens redundantly encode textual content already captured by text tokens, and no prior work has designed a speech tokenization explicitly aligned with text to simplify joint modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264254/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer consumes a speech waveform and its ASR transcription (from Whisper), producing text-aligned RVQ codes and continuous embeddings whose sequence length equals the number of text tokens (~3 Hz, dynamic per utterance) rather than a fixed stride. The downstream SLM takes a 3-second speech prompt (or a spoken question in few-shot mode) and autoregressively generates paired text and speech tokens at each step, which are decoded into a continuous speech waveform via a flow-matching vocoder and HifiGAN. A key structural property is that each speech token corresponds one-to-one with a text token, enabling simultaneous generation without alignment heuristics.

## Method

TASTE builds a text-aligned speech tokenizer on a frozen Whisper ASR encoder. A cross-attention aggregator uses text tokens as queries, the encoder's last hidden states as keys (exploiting their rich speech-text alignment cues), and shallow encoder hidden states (layer 6) as values (retaining acoustic detail for reconstruction), producing a representation whose length matches the text sequence. This is discretized via 4-layer RVQ (codebook size 512, dim 256) and decoded by a Transformer unit decoder plus a pre-trained flow-matching vocoder, trained end-to-end with cross-entropy on S3 target units plus RVQ commitment loss. For spoken language modeling, the authors apply LoRA (r=64, α=128) to LLaMA3.2-1B, training it to predict the next text token and all R layers of speech tokens simultaneously at each step (TASLM_token), or alternatively to predict continuous latent embeddings via a MELLE-style mean-variance head with KL regularization (TASLM_emb). The entire pipeline operates at ~150 bps—orders of magnitude below comparable codecs—while preserving paralinguistic information sufficient for high-quality reconstruction and effective joint modeling.

## Results

On speech continuation (3-second prompt, LibriSpeech test-clean), TASLM 1B (embedding) achieves GPT-4o semantic score 3.16/5, UTMOS 4.22, and human MOS 4.16/5, outperforming all 7B pretrained SLMs (TWIST 7B: 1.44/3.27/2.04; Spirit LM: 2.79/3.41/2.38) while using only a 1.3B base model with 45M LoRA parameters. On likelihood-based benchmarks, TASLM 1B (token) scores 76.5% on StoryCloze and 60.8% on SALMON, achieving the best overall (68.7%) among all pretrained SLMs. For speech reconstruction, TASTE operates at ~150 bps (vs. 600–4000 bps for baselines) while matching or exceeding quality: UTMOS 4.29 vs. S3 token's 4.18, and MUSHRA 68.3 vs. 70.2 for S3 token at 4× the bitrate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TASLM decisively outperforms TWIST (1.3B and 7B) on all continuation metrics (e.g., GPT-4o 3.16 vs. 1.44; Human MOS 4.16 vs. 2.04) and StoryCloze (76.7% vs. 64.7%), though TWIST 7B edges ahead on SALMON (63.4% vs. 57.7%). Against Spirit LM (7B), TASLM wins on continuation (GPT-4o 3.16 vs. 2.79; Human 4.16 vs. 2.38) and StoryCloze (76.7% vs. 72.0%), but Spirit LM Expr. leads on SALMON (69.0% vs. 57.7%), likely due to its interleaved design capturing more acoustic detail. The S3-token baseline (same 1.3B base, same LoRA setup) is far behind TASLM on continuation (GPT-4o 1.37 vs. 3.16) and StoryCloze (58.7% vs. 76.7%), demonstrating that reconstruction quality alone does not guarantee effective joint modeling. On speech reconstruction (Table 1), TASTE at ~150 bps matches S3 token (600 bps) on UTMOS (4.29 vs. 4.18) and MUSHRA (68.3 vs. 70.2) while operating at 1/4 the bitrate, and substantially outperforms the text-only baseline on duration consistency (0.91 vs. 0.57) and MUSHRA (68.3 vs. 42.6). For spoken QA, TASLM 1B (embed.) is the only joint SLM that preserves its base LLM's performance (Web-Q 27.1 vs. 24.0; LLaMA-Q 57.6 vs. 51.0), whereas Moshi 7B and Llama-Omni-8B both degrade relative to their text-only counterparts.

## Relation to Prior Work

TASTE builds on the Whisper ASR encoder (frozen) as its speech representation backbone, the S3 semantic tokens and flow-matching vocoder from CosyVoice as the reconstruction target, and the MELLE latent prediction framework for continuous embedding modeling. It departs from prior joint text-speech SLMs (Moshi, Spirit LM, Mini-Omni) that handle the text-speech length mismatch via interleaving, padding, or delayed generation, by instead designing speech tokens that are natively text-aligned at the tokenization stage through a cross-attention aggregator. The paper positions itself as the first end-to-end reconstruction-based approach that jointly learns tokenization and embedding specifically tailored for text-speech joint modeling, rather than repurposing codec or SSL tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that reconstruction quality does not predict spoken language modeling performance, as evidenced by S3 tokens outperforming TASTE in reconstruction yet underperforming dramatically in joint SLM—challenges a common assumption in the field and reframes tokenization design as a first-class concern for SLMs. Practical limitations include English-only evaluation, single-speaker focus, absence of turn-taking or instruction-following, and no handling of non-lexical events or multi-speaker overlap. Open questions include whether text-aligned tokenization generalizes across languages, whether the approach scales to larger models and real-time streaming, and whether the one-to-one text-speech alignment remains optimal for more complex dialogue scenarios.
