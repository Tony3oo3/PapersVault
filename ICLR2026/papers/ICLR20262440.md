## Problem

Deep RL from pixels remains sample-inefficient, and prior work showing that disentangling agent from environment representations improves efficiency has relied on supervisory signals such as geometric masks, CAD models, or fine-tuned segmentation networks. This paper asks whether agent-environment disentanglement can be achieved in a fully self-supervised manner, drawing on the neuroscience principle of motor prediction — the brain's ability to predict the sensory consequences of one's own movements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262440/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a short window of C context RGB frames and a sequence of commanded actions spanning the prediction horizon H. The encoder produces a feature vector that is split into a scene component h_s and a smaller agent component h_a; the recurrent block predicts h_a forward through the horizon while h_s is held fixed (fast-forwarded), and a DCGAN-based decoder reconstructs the full frame at a randomly sampled future time step. The output is thus a pixel-level reconstruction whose agent region reflects predicted motion while the scene region remains anchored to the context encoding.

## Method

Ego-Foresight (EF) augments an existing RL algorithm's shared visual encoder with a recurrent prediction head and a decoder. The encoder's output is partitioned into scene features h_s and a bottleneck agent feature h_a (a small fraction of total dimensionality); h_a is iteratively updated by an MLP recurrent block conditioned on the next action, while h_s is fast-forwarded unchanged to the prediction time step. The decoder reconstructs the future frame from the concatenation of h_s and the predicted h_a, and the resulting L2 reconstruction loss L_ef is added as a weighted auxiliary term (β·L_ef) to the RL algorithm's original objective (critic loss for DrQ-v2, model loss for TD-MPC2). A short motor-babbling phase at the start of training injects random ±1 actions to ensure diverse motion for learning the visuomotor mapping before goal-directed behavior dominates. The bottleneck on h_a combined with the fast-forwarding of h_s forces the recurrent block to specialize on predicting agent configuration changes rather than full-scene dynamics, yielding self-supervised agent awareness without any mask or segmentation supervision.

## Results

Across 26 RL tasks on Meta-World (16 tasks) and DMC (10 tasks), DrQv2-EF improves over DrQ-v2 in 21 of 26 tasks without degrading performance in any task, while TD-MPC2-EF improves on 8 out of 10 DMC tasks. The Efficiency Normalized Score (ENS), which combines sample-efficiency and performance, shows consistent gains for both EF-augmented algorithms, with the largest improvements observed in tool-use tasks where the agent's body-schema expands. In terms of wall-clock cost, DrQv2-EF runs at 110 env steps/sec (vs. 155 for DrQ-v2) and TD-MPC2-EF at 23 steps/sec (vs. 28 for TD-MPC2) on an RTX A6000, a modest overhead that the authors argue is compensated by the sample-efficiency gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its primary baseline DrQ-v2, DrQv2-EF wins in 21 of 26 tasks on both sample-efficiency and asymptotic performance, with no task showing degradation; the gains are most pronounced in tool-use tasks (e.g., Hammer, Push Back) where the tool becomes integrated into the predicted agent body-schema. Against the supervised baseline SEAR (which uses a ground-truth agent mask from the simulator), DrQv2-EF achieves higher ENS and Rliable metrics on both Meta-World and DMC, demonstrating that self-supervised disentanglement can match or exceed explicit supervision. DrQv2-EF also outperforms CURL (contrastive self-supervised features) on both benchmarks. Against Dreamer-v3, DrQv2-EF is described as 'competitive' but Dreamer-v3 still outperforms it on some individual tasks, particularly those with diverse reward structures. For the model-based setting, TD-MPC2-EF improves over TD-MPC2 on 8/10 DMC tasks with a significant ENS gain, confirming the approach generalizes beyond model-free algorithms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on agent-environment disentanglement for RL (SEAR, Hu et al., Mendonca et al.) by replacing the supervisory mask with a self-supervised motion-prediction objective. It builds directly on DrQ-v2 and TD-MPC2 as the underlying RL algorithms, adding a recurrent prediction head and DCGAN decoder as auxiliary modules. Conceptually, it draws from neuroscience (motor prediction, self-tickling suppression) and developmental robotics (self-other distinction via mirror/twin observation), but instantiates the idea as a bottleneck-constrained reconstruction loss that forces the recurrent block to focus on the most predictable dynamics — the agent's own motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the need for geometric IDs, segmentation models, or CAD models makes agent-aware RL applicable to real-world robotic settings where such supervision is unavailable or impractical. The automatic integration of tools into the body-schema (predicting hammer motion once grasped) is a practical advantage over fixed supervised masks. Key limitations include the reliance on pixel-wise reconstruction (debated in the field), a fixed motor-babbling stage that can delay learning, and wall-clock overhead. Open questions include how quickly the model adapts to novel tools, whether contrastive losses can replace reconstruction, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution real-world imagery.
