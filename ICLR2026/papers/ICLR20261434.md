## Problem

Practitioners modeling discrete sequences (DNA, proteins, language) with diffusion must choose among three methods—discrete, Gaussian, and simplicial—each with different algorithms, likelihoods, and hyperparameter interpretations, yet no formal theory connects them. This gap prevents comparing model fit across domains, understanding how hyperparameters relate, and explains why simplicial diffusion, which should combine the strengths of the other two, suffers from severe numerical instability. Additionally, practitioners must commit to a single diffusion domain before training, limiting access to downstream algorithms.

## Input / Output

The unified model takes a noisy state x_t (a discrete token, a Gaussian vector in R^r, or a point on the (B−1)-simplex depending on the chosen domain) plus time t, and outputs a denoised probability vector x̃_0 over the B-token alphabet. The key design choice is the Sufficient Statistic Parameterization (SSP), which maps x_t from all three domains into a common representation φ(x_t^d, t) ∝ p(x_t^d | t, x_0^d = b), so a single neural network F_θ^d can be trained by alternating ELBO minimization across domains and then perform diffusion in any domain at test time without retraining. For conditional generation, a classifier gradient ∇log p(y|x_t) is added to the score during the reverse SDE.

## Method

The paper proves that all three diffusion methods are limits of the Wright-Fisher population genetics model with population size ζ: discrete diffusion corresponds to ζ=1, Gaussian diffusion to ζ→∞ with zero reproduction (converging to an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process in the dominant eigenspace of the mutation matrix L), and simplicial diffusion to ζ→∞ with non-zero reproduction (recovering the Kimura 1955 Wright-Fisher diffusion on the simplex). This unification yields a formal likelihood comparison (showing the hollow parameterization removes the Gaussian ELBO singularity) and a hyperparameter correspondence (the embedding emb is determined by the slowest-decaying eigenspace of L). To fix simplicial diffusion instability, the authors replace costly SDE sampling with exact Dirichlet-based sampling from the ancestral process A(ψ,τ_t) (Jenkins & Spanò 2017) and switch to a Griffiths Gaussian approximation at τ_t < 0.05, reducing the series truncation from 1000 to 80 terms. The SSP then enables training one model across all three domains simultaneously, which matches single-domain model performance on protein and language tasks.

## Results

On conditional DNA generation (B=4, D=500), the stable Wright-Fisher simplicial diffusion achieves an ELBO of 1.30 nats/position, dramatically outperforming the previous simplicial diffusion model of Avdeyev et al. (2023) which only reaches 8 nats/position (a trivial uniform predictor achieves 1.39). The model also produces conditional samples with significantly lower accessibility profile error compared to both Avdeyev et al. and the flow-matching model of Stark et al. (2024). On protein generation, a single unified SSP model trained by alternating across discrete, Gaussian, and simplicial domains is competitive with three separate single-domain models trained for the same 48-hour budget, approaching DPLM's reference likelihood of 2.36 and pLDDT of 45.2. On language (SEDD), the unified model matches the NLL of 3.70 reported by Lou et al. (2023).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Avdeyev et al. (2023), the previous state-of-the-art simplicial diffusion model, the paper's stable Wright-Fisher formulation achieves a dramatically better ELBO (1.30 vs. 8.00 nats/position on DNA) and produces conditional samples with substantially lower accessibility profile error (Figure 5b), while also being faster to sample from (exact Dirichlet sampling vs. costly SDE simulation). Against Stark et al. (2024), the flow-matching baseline that had recently surpassed simplicial diffusion, the paper's model achieves lower profile error on conditional DNA generation while retaining a closed-form ELBO and access to diffusion sampling algorithms like classifier guidance that flow matching sacrifices. On protein generation, the unified SSP model is competitive with (not strictly superior to) three separate single-domain models trained for the same compute budget, and approaches DPLM's reference performance (likelihood 2.36, pLDDT 45.2). On language, the unified model matches SEDD's NLL of 3.70, confirming that the SSP does not sacrifice quality for flexibility. The key practical win is that a single trained model can perform diffusion in any of the three domains at test time, removing the need to commit to a specific forward process before training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate diffusion frameworks—discrete (Campbell et al., 2022), Gaussian (Ho et al., 2020), and simplicial (Avdeyev et al., 2023)—as different limits of the Wright-Fisher population genetics model, drawing on classical results from Kimura (1955) and Stone (1963) that had not previously been connected to diffusion model theory. It departs from Sahoo et al. (2025), who argued for a discrete-continuous duality via argmax, by demonstrating that argument contains a mathematical error (the argmax of Gaussian diffusion is not Markov). The practical stability improvements for simplicial diffusion borrow directly from the mathematical genetics literature (Jenkins and Spanò, 2017; Griffiths, 1984; Tavaré, 1984), repurposing exact ancestral sampling and low-time approximations that were developed for population genetics but never applied to generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification provides the first rigorous theoretical infrastructure for comparing likelihoods and hyperparameters across discrete, Gaussian, and simplicial diffusion—resolving a long-standing practitioner confusion about whether these ELBOs are even comparable. The sufficient statistic parameterization (SSP) is a broadly applicable technique that eliminates the need to commit to a single diffusion modality at training time, with potential to extend to hyperparameter optimization and cross-modality transfer. Open questions include whether the framework extends to reflected diffusion, flow matching, masking diffusion, and indel-based models, and whether the intermediate Wright-Fisher models (finite ζ > 1) offer practical advantages over the three limiting cases. The empirical validation, while clear on DNA generation, is conducted at relatively modest scale (sequence length 500, 48-hour training), leaving open whether the unified training advantage persists at larger scales.
