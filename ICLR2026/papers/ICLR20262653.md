## Problem

LLM-based task planners face a gap between general world knowledge and environment-specific requirements, leading to infeasible plans. Existing methods address this by iteratively refining static plans with immediate environmental feedback, but this incurs substantial LLM query costs and biases planning toward short-term rewards rather than long-term objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262653/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language task instruction, a text-based environment observation, and the available action space as input. It produces (1) multiple high-level planning programs in Python that form a closed-loop interaction with the environment, and (2) a selected action sequence (plan) chosen from candidate plans generated by those programs. The selector operates on tokenized plan descriptions scored by a 1.1B-parameter TinyLlama, with temperature set to 1.0 during training for exploration and 0.0 during testing for deterministic selection.

## Method

CoPiC employs a mixture-of-experts mechanism where an LLM generates n diverse high-level planning programs (Python code), each acting as an expert that produces a candidate plan from the current observation without requiring step-by-step LLM queries. A domain-adaptive selector, initialized from TinyLlama and fine-tuned via PPO with LoRA on the final transformer block, scores candidates through three stages: token-level probability computation, word-count length regularization, and softmax normalization. The system alternates between a Planning Phase (program execution + selector scoring) and a Learning Phase where (a) the LLM refines planning programs via in-context learning over summarized interaction histories, and (b) the selector is fine-tuned on replayed transitions to optimize for long-term rewards. This architecture reduces LLM query costs by ~79% while improving success rates by ~19% over immediate-feedback baselines, because the code programs handle dynamic adaptation internally and the RL-trained selector captures domain-specific long-term strategy.

## Results

CoPiC achieves an average 19.14% improvement in success rate (SR) and 79.39% reduction in token cost across ALFWorld, NetHack, and StarCraft II Unit Building compared to advanced LLM-based baselines. On ALFWorld specifically, CoPiC reaches 100% SR on five of six task types (Pick, Examine, Clean, Heat, Cool) with token costs as low as 0.04M, versus 64–100% SR and 0.19–2.54M cost for baselines. On StarCraft II Hard tasks, CoPiC achieves 100% SR at 0.06M cost, outperforming AdaPlanner (71% SR, 0.36M) and Reflexion (24% SR, 0.78M). CoPiC also reduces average interaction steps by 30.43% compared to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoPiC outperforms all nine baselines on both SR and Cost across the three primary environments. Against the strongest code-based baseline AdaPlanner, CoPiC achieves 100% SR on ALFWorld Pick/Examine/Clean/Heat/Cool (vs. 64–100%) while reducing cost by up to 97% (e.g., 0.04M vs. 1.95M on Examine); on StarCraft II Hard, CoPiC reaches 100% SR at 0.06M cost versus AdaPlanner's 71% at 0.36M. Reflexion, which relies on iterative verbal self-reflection, is outperformed by CoPiC by 13–76 percentage points in SR and by 3–10× in token cost across environments. REPL-Plan, the closest competitor in cost efficiency, is beaten by CoPiC on SR in 5 of 6 ALFWorld tasks (e.g., 100% vs. 82.50% on Pick) and on all NetHack/StarCraft II tasks. The PDDL-based LLM-DP method achieves competitive SR on some ALFWorld tasks (e.g., 95.56% on Examine) but still costs 5× more tokens than CoPiC on the same task. Non-LLM baselines (BUTLER, PPO, TWOSOME) are consistently dominated: PPO achieves 0% SR on all ALFWorld tasks, and TWOSOME trails CoPiC by 10–28 points in SR while using 2–4× more tokens. Notably, CoPiC is trained only on training-set tasks and generalizes zero-shot to unseen test tasks, whereas AdaPlanner and Reflexion learn directly on the test set, making CoPiC's advantage even more pronounced under a stricter evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoPiC departs from the dominant immediate-feedback paradigm (ReAct, Reflexion, AdaPlanner, REPL-Plan) where LLMs iteratively refine static plans step-by-step, incurring high query costs. It draws on code-as-policy work (Code as Policies, PROGPROMPT) but introduces a mixture-of-experts structure where multiple LLM-generated planning programs produce diverse candidate plans, combined with a domain-adaptive selector inspired by TWOSOME's scoring mechanism. The key architectural departure is fine-tuning a tiny language model (TinyLlama) via PPO with LoRA to select plans aligned with long-term rewards, rather than relying on LLM-as-a-Judge or offline-trained scorers (Prospector, SayCan). The iterative program evolution with history summarization extends AdaPlanner's program refinement by incorporating comparative analysis of successful and failed trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous 19% success rate improvement and 79% token cost reduction addresses a core practical bottleneck in LLM-based planning, making the approach viable for deployment where query budgets are constrained. The zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks (trained only on training set) while baselines learn on the test set is a meaningful methodological advantage. However, the evaluation environments (ALFWorld, simplified NetHack tasks, StarCraft II unit building) are relatively constrained, and the base LLM for most experiments is GPT-3.5, limiting confidence in scaling to more complex real-world scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full StarCraft II or Civilization-scale tasks, and how it integrates with perception and low-level control for embodied agents (partially addressed via Robosuite).
