## Problem

Existing dynamics models for offline model-based RL rely on bootstrapping prediction, causing severe error accumulation that forces practitioners to truncate roll-outs to short horizons. This limits both policy exploration (preventing the agent from reaching edge states) and off-policy evaluation (which requires complete trajectories). While the original ADM reduced bootstrapping to direct prediction, its architecture duplicated the starting state at every GRU step, creating strong coupling between hidden representations and the initial state and preventing parallel computation of any-step predictions.

## Input / Output

ADM-v2 takes a single state s_t and a variable-length action sequence a_{t:t+k-1} (where k ranges from 1 to a maximum stride m) and outputs independent Gaussian distributions for the k-step-ahead state s_{t+k} and reward r_{t+k}. During roll-out via PARoll, m parallel timelines each produce a distinct prediction at every step, so the model simultaneously yields m candidate next-states and m candidate rewards, from which a single prediction is sampled uniformly and the any-step uncertainty is computed as the L1 norm of the variance across the m predictions. For full-horizon roll-outs, the hidden state is reset at every m-step window boundary by re-encoding the predicted state, so bootstrapping occurs only at window transitions rather than at every step.

## Method

The core architectural change decouples the starting state from the GRU's recurrent computation: a state encoder maps s_t to a latent vector h_t that serves directly as the GRU's initial hidden state, after which only actions (not state-action pairs) are fed through successive GRU forward passes, and a transition decoder maps the final hidden state to Gaussian parameters for s_{t+k} and r_{t+k}. Training maximizes the average log-likelihood over all strides k ∈ [1, m] end-to-end, with gradients backpropagated through the GRU, encoder, and decoder jointly. The PARoll algorithm initializes m parallel timelines from m different starting states within a sampled m-step sequence, then updates all m hidden states in parallel at each subsequent step, requiring only one state-encoder forward, one parallel GRU forward, and one parallel decoder forward per step. This parallelism enables efficient full-horizon roll-outs and natural any-step uncertainty estimation without ensembles. The resulting ADM2PO-fh algorithm uses full-horizon roll-outs with the any-step uncertainty as a penalty in Q-value estimation, achieving SOTA on D4RL and NeoRL under the full-horizon setting.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo (12 tasks), ADM2PO-fh achieves an average normalized score of 87.6±1.3, surpassing the prior SOTA MOREC (83.7) by 4.6% and the best model-free baseline EDAC (76.0) by 15.5%. On NeoRL MuJoCo (9 tasks), ADM2PO-fh reaches 79.0±1.8, outperforming MOREC (70.3) by 12.8% and CQL (56.1) by 22.9%. Critically, among all full-horizon roll-out variants, only ADM-v2 maintains strong performance (e.g., 111.6 on walker2d-med vs. 27.0 for MOPO-fh and 58.7 for ADMPO-fh), while other dynamics models degrade severely due to long-horizon error accumulation. On the DOPE benchmark (15 tasks), ADM-v2 for OPE outperforms all five model-free OPE methods (FQE, DR, IS, DICE, VPM) and other dynamics-model-based OPE approaches across normalized absolute error, rank correlation, and regret@1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against model-free baselines, ADM2PO-fh dominates: it outperforms CQL (61.6 avg on D4RL, 56.1 on NeoRL) and EDAC (76.0, 50.7) by 11–23 points. Among short-branched model-based methods, ADM2PO-fh beats the prior SOTA MOREC (83.7 on D4RL, 70.3 on NeoRL) by 4.6% and 12.8% respectively, and also surpasses MOPO, MOBILE, and ADMPO. The most striking comparison is against full-horizon roll-out variants of other dynamics models: MOPO-fh (EDM) collapses to 36.5 on D4RL and 32.4 on NeoRL, PDPO-fh (P-Dreamer) drops to 4.5 and 17.3, and ADMPO-fh (original ADM) reaches only 56.4 and 47.8, while ADM2PO-fh maintains 87.6 and 79.0. On the DOPE OPE benchmark, ADM-v2 outperforms all five model-free OPE methods (FQE, DR, IS, DICE, VPM) and other dynamics-model OPE approaches (EDM, RDM, P-Dreamer, ADM) on all three metrics. In dynamics model evaluation, ADM-v2 achieves a 0.928 correlation between uncertainty and model error (vs. lower values for all baselines) and maintains bounded roll-out error over 1000 steps where EDM, RDM, and P-Dreamer diverge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct architectural refinement of ADM (Lin et al., 2025), which itself generalized multi-step dynamics models (Asadi et al., 2018; Che et al., 2018) into an any-step formulation using a GRU. The key departure is structurally decoupling the backtracked state from each GRU forward pass by encoding it into the initial hidden state, eliminating the state duplication that made ADM's architecture inflexible and coupled. The PARoll algorithm further departs from ADM's recursive backtracking mechanism by enabling parallel computation of diverse predictions across timelines. The theoretical analysis builds on the ξ-uncertainty quantification framework of Jin et al. (2021) and the performance bound structure of Janner et al. (2019), extending them to the multi-step direct prediction setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that full-horizon roll-outs — long assumed to be infeasible due to error accumulation — are achievable with the right architecture, and that they yield substantial policy improvements (12.8% on NeoRL) and more reliable OPE. The finding that prior SOTA methods (MOPO, ADM) degrade catastrophically under full-horizon roll-outs while ADM-v2 improves highlights that the bottleneck was architectural, not algorithmic. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional or visual state spaces, whether the maximum stride m can be learned adaptively, and whether the parallelism advantage of PARoll persists at larger batch sizes or on different hardware. The method remains limited to proprioceptive MuJoCo tasks, leaving generalization to more complex environments untested.
