## Problem

Current spatial reasoning benchmarks for VLMs rely on single images or static indoor videos, which fundamentally differ from the ego-centric, multi-view observations that real-world embodied agents (self-driving cars, mobile robots) actually receive. No existing benchmark evaluates whether VLMs can integrate spatially grounded, directionally structured multi-view inputs from a moving agent in dynamic outdoor scenes, and a significant performance gap between human and VLM spatial understanding remains unaddressed in this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263464/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of ego-centric multi-view RGB images (6–7 cameras from NuScenes, Waymo, or Argoverse), a natural language query referencing specific objects, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It produces a spatial QA answer in the form of multi-choice selection or an absolute metric distance in meters, alongside a compact textual cognitive map that encodes each referred object's 3D global coordinates and originating viewpoint. The cognitive map is intentionally limited to objects mentioned in the prompt, keeping token count small compared to full point-cloud or BEV representations.

## Method

Ego3D-VLM is a training-free, plug-and-play framework that constructs a textual cognitive map to ground VLM spatial reasoning. Given multi-view images, a referring expression comprehension model (Grounding-DINO) localizes each mentioned object in 2D pixel space per view, and a metric depth estimator (Depth-Anything-V2) provides depth values; these are back-projected to 3D camera coordinates via intrinsics and then transformed into a unified global frame (front camera as reference) using known extrinsics. A relational scaling step corrects depth scale by matching observed object heights to canonical common-sense values (e.g., 1.7 m for pedestrians), yielding physically plausible 3D positions without ground-truth depth. The resulting 3D coordinates are formatted into a compact textual cognitive map that defines an ego-centric coordinate system and organizes objects by viewpoint, which is then prepended to the VLM prompt alongside the original images. This avoids the high inference cost and reconstruction fragility of point-cloud or BEV approaches while remaining compatible with any existing VLM.

## Results

On Ego3D-Bench, Ego3D-VLM achieves an average 12% relative improvement in multi-choice accuracy and 56% reduction in absolute distance RMSE across all 16 benchmarked VLMs. For the strongest baselines, GPT-4o improves from 56.7% to 73.2% average accuracy (RMSE 19.2→7.4 m), Gemini-1.5-Pro from 57.5% to 73.1% (RMSE 19.6→7.2 m), and InternVL3-78B from 59.9% to 71.8% (RMSE 13.8→7.4 m). Human performance reaches 85.3% average accuracy, while the best Ego3D-VLM-augmented model (GPT-4o) reaches 73.2%, still leaving a ~12-point gap. On object-centric absolute distance, Ego3D-VLM-augmented VLMs even surpass human-level RMSE, as humans struggle with precise 3D distance estimation without explicit spatial coordinates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ego3D-VLM consistently outperforms all 16 baseline VLMs across every task category on Ego3D-Bench. Among closed-source models, GPT-4o gains +16.5 points in average accuracy (56.7→73.2%) and reduces RMSE by 11.8 m (19.2→7.4 m); Gemini-1.5-Pro gains +15.6 points (57.5→73.1%) with RMSE dropping from 19.6 to 7.2 m. Among open-source models, the gains are even more pronounced for smaller models: Qwen2.5-3B improves from 39.1% to 44.4% accuracy with RMSE halved (32.1→12.6 m), and InternVL3-8B jumps from 43.1% to 60.1% accuracy with RMSE reduced from 27.2 to 8.0 m. For the largest open-source models, InternVL3-78B reaches 71.8% accuracy (vs. 59.9% baseline) and Qwen2.5-72B reaches 69.5% (vs. 58.0%), both approaching the 73% level of GPT-4o+CogMap. On the 3D-VLM comparison (Table 3), Ego3D-VLM augmented SpaceThinker-Qwen2.5-3B achieves 48.6% accuracy and 11.6 m RMSE, outperforming SpatialRGPT-VILA1.5-8B (44.7%, 13.3 m) and SpaceQwen2.5-VL-3B (27.5%, 13.2 m). On external benchmarks (All-Angle Bench and VSI-Bench), Ego3D-VLM also surpasses baselines, e.g., InternVL3-14B improves from 50.3% to 52.1% on All-Angle and from 38.2% to 40.0% on VSI-Bench, confirming generalizability beyond ego-centric settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior spatial-reasoning benchmarks that rely on single images or indoor static videos by introducing the first ego-centric multi-view benchmark from dynamic outdoor driving scenes (NuScenes, Waymo, Argoverse), aligning evaluation with the actual perceptual setup of embodied agents. Methodologically, it contrasts with both point-cloud/BEV-based 3D-VLMs (3D-LLM, GPT4Scene) and fine-tuned image-based spatial VLMs (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT) by proposing a training-free textual cognitive map that leverages off-the-shelf REC and metric depth tools, avoiding the reconstruction cost and token overhead of 3D representations. The cognitive map conceptually repurposes the human strategy of building a unified spatial reference frame from multi-view observations, but encodes it as compact text rather than a rendered image or point cloud.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ego3D-Bench exposes a substantial and previously unquantified gap between human and VLM performance on ego-centric multi-view spatial reasoning (85.3% vs. ~57–59% for the best models), providing a much-needed evaluation standard for embodied-AI spatial understanding. The training-free Ego3D-VLM framework is plug-and-play across 16 VLMs and achieves 12% accuracy / 56% RMSE improvements without any fine-tuning, making it immediately deployable. Key open questions remain: the method does not improve innate visual spatial perception, it has no temporal dimension, it depends on the accuracy of external REC and depth tools (which degrade under occlusion, blur, and low light), and the relational-scaling heuristic is approximate. Future work on end-to-end training with RL, temporal reasoning, and better outdoor depth estimation are identified as necessary next steps.
