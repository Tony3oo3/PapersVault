## Problem

Current RL algorithms are tightly coupled to the structure of the action space, requiring different gradient estimators, critic architectures, and algorithmic designs for discrete, continuous, and hybrid actions. While the likelihood-ratio estimator is universally applicable, it suffers from high variance, and reparameterization-based estimators are limited to continuous settings. The paper asks whether a single continuous-action algorithm can be developed that unifies policy optimization across all action space types without sacrificing gradient quality.

## Input / Output

The agent receives the environment state vector and outputs distribution parameters rather than a concrete action: a probability vector for discrete actions, a [mean, std] pair (squashed to bounded ranges) for Gaussian continuous policies, or a concatenation of both for hybrid spaces. The actual action is sampled from the parameterized distribution and treated as part of the environment's stochastic transition, so the critic's input is (state, distribution parameters) rather than (state, action). This means the critic must estimate expected return over the entire action distribution, not for a single action.

## Method

The paper redefines the agent-environment boundary by treating distribution parameters as the action in a new distributions-as-actions MDP (DA-MDP), where the sampling function becomes part of the environment's stochastic dynamics. Applying the deterministic policy gradient theorem to this DA-MDP yields the DA-PG estimator, which is provably the conditional expectation of both the likelihood-ratio and reparameterization estimators, giving strictly lower variance while remaining unbiased. To address the increased difficulty of learning the distributions-as-actions critic (which takes higher-dimensional parameter inputs), the paper introduces Interpolated Critic Learning (ICL), which updates the critic at linearly interpolated points between the current policy parameters and the deterministic parameters corresponding to the sampled action, encouraging smoother curvature in the value landscape. The resulting DA-AC algorithm builds on TD3, replacing the classical actor and critic with their DA counterparts and using DA-PG for policy updates and ICL for critic updates, yielding a single off-policy algorithm applicable to discrete, continuous, and hybrid control.

## Results

DA-AC is evaluated across four control settings: continuous (5 MuJoCo + 15 DMC environments), discrete (4 Classic Control + 5 MinAtar), high-dimensional discrete (discretized MuJoCo and DMC with 7 bins per dimension), and hybrid (7 PAMDP environments). In continuous control, DA-AC outperforms TD3 in more individual environments and achieves significantly better average normalized returns on the DMC Suite, with the largest gains in high-dimensional tasks (e.g., 17-dim Humanoid). In discrete and high-dimensional discrete settings, DA-AC matches or exceeds DQN and PPO, and in hybrid control it achieves competitive or better performance than the dedicated parameterized-action baseline PATD3. The ICL ablation confirms that removing interpolated critic learning leads to consistent underperformance across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TD3 (its direct base), DA-AC wins in more individual continuous-control environments and shows significantly better average normalized returns on the DMC Suite, particularly in high-dimensional tasks where the unified continuous parameterization provides the largest advantage. Against SAC and PPO (reference baselines for continuous control), DA-AC outperforms both on the DMC Suite aggregate. In discrete control, DA-AC achieves performance comparable to DQN and outperforms DSAC, LR-AC, ST-AC, and EAC; in high-dimensional discrete control (where DQN and DSAC are infeasible), DA-AC surpasses ST-AC, LR-AC, and PPO. In hybrid (PAMDP) control, DA-AC achieves competitive or better performance than PATD3 (the dedicated DPG-based method for parameterized actions), as well as PDQN and HHQN. The ICL ablation (DA-AC w/o ICL) consistently underperforms the full DA-AC across all settings, with a statistically significant positive mean improvement confirmed by paired t-tests.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the deterministic policy gradient (DPG) and TD3, reframing them within a new MDP formulation where the agent outputs distribution parameters rather than actions, and the sampling step is absorbed into the environment's stochastic transition. The resulting DA-PG estimator is shown to be a strict generalization of DPG (recovering it when the distribution is a Dirac delta) and to equal the conditional expectation of both the likelihood-ratio and reparameterization estimators, yielding provably lower variance. The Interpolated Critic Learning (ICL) component is a novel off-distribution critic update that has no direct analogue in prior actor-critic literature, though it draws conceptual parallels to off-policy learning. The paper also positions itself relative to Todorov's concurrent D-MDP work and the broader agent-environment boundary literature (Jiang, Harutyunyan, Abel et al.), distinguishing its contribution as an algorithmic reframing of the action space rather than a philosophical or representational one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distributions-as-actions framework offers a genuinely unified perspective: a single continuous-action algorithm (DA-AC) handles discrete, continuous, and hybrid action spaces without architectural changes, which is a practical simplification for multi-domain RL systems. The theoretical variance reduction is clean and the unbiasedness (in contrast to straight-through or Gumbel-Softmax estimators for discrete actions) is a meaningful advantage. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines like TD3 and PPO are modest rather than transformative, and the harder critic learning problem in the expanded parameter space remains an open challenge. Key future directions include more principled off-distribution critic updates, entropy regularization within the DA framework, model-based extensions, and understanding convergence properties of the ICL-augmented algorithm.
