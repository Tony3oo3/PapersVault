## Problem

Existing transformer-based methods for egocentric hand-object segmentation suffer from three unresolved issues: query initialization is static and cannot adapt to varying active objects across scenes, pixel-level semantic features introduce interaction-irrelevant noise into mask predictions, and models produce physically implausible 'interaction illusion' predictions where objects are assigned to hands that are not detected.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264284/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single egocentric RGB image (cropped to 448×448, normalized with ImageNet-style statistics) and produces pixel-level segmentation masks for five categories: left hand, right hand, left-hand-only object, right-hand-only object, and two-hand object. It uses exactly 5 queries (one per category) in a Mask2Former-style iterative decoder, and the final mask is obtained by channel-wise multiplication of class logits and a class-agnostic mask.

## Method

InterFormer builds on a Swin Transformer backbone with a Deformable DETR pixel decoder and introduces three interaction-specific components. An Interaction Prior Predictor (IPP) auxiliary U-Net branch predicts the hand-object contact boundary, yielding coarse boundary-guided features at multiple scales. A Dynamic Query Generator (DQG) computes cosine similarity between these boundary features and the last-layer pixel features, selects the top-N most similar sub-regions, and adds learnable parameters to form interaction-aware queries. A Dual-context Feature Selector (DFS) in each decoder layer performs interaction-guided cross-attention (query from boundary features, key/value from pixel features) followed by self-attention, suppressing interaction-irrelevant noise. A novel Conditional Co-occurrence (CoCo) loss penalizes object mask pixels whenever the corresponding hand mask falls below a pixel-count threshold (τ=100), enforcing the hand-first physical constraint and reducing interaction illusion.

## Results

On the EgoHOS in-domain test set, InterFormer achieves a mean IoU of 73.22%, surpassing the previous best (CaRe-Ego, 71.49%) by 1.73% and the MLLM-based ANNEXE (71.38%) by 1.84%. The most significant gain is on two-hand object segmentation, where InterFormer reaches 64.17% IoU, a 7.76% improvement over the second-best method. On the out-of-domain EgoHOS test set, InterFormer achieves 72.82% mIoU (7.46% over the runner-up), and on the out-of-distribution mini-HOI4D dataset it reaches 66.07% mIoU (3.20% over the second-best), demonstrating strong generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterFormer outperforms all 10 baselines across every category on the EgoHOS in-domain test set. Against the closest transformer-based competitor CaRe-Ego (Su et al., 2025a), InterFormer gains 1.73% overall mIoU (73.22 vs. 71.49), with the largest margin on two-hand objects (+9.44% IoU: 64.17 vs. 54.73). Against the MLLM-based ANNEXE (Su et al., 2025b), InterFormer leads by 1.84% overall and 7.76% on two-hand objects, while ANNEXE retains a slight edge on right-hand objects (57.32 vs. 55.04). Compared to the original EgoHOS Seq model (Zhang et al., 2022), InterFormer improves overall mIoU by 6.05% (73.22 vs. 67.17), with particularly large gains on two-hand objects (+11.40%). Against general-purpose segmentation backbones (Mask2Former, OneFormer, MaskFormer), InterFormer leads by 8.3–8.9% overall mIoU. On the out-of-domain EgoHOS test set, InterFormer extends its lead to 7.46% over CaRe-Ego (72.82 vs. 63.82 in the paper's Table 2, though CaRe-Ego's reported value is 63.82), and on mini-HOI4D it surpasses ANNEXE by 3.20% mIoU (66.07 vs. 62.87), confirming robustness under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mask2Former-style query-based transformer decoder paradigm (Cheng et al., 2022) to the egocentric hand-object segmentation task introduced by Zhang et al. (ECCV 2022), which defined the EgoHOS benchmark and the interaction boundary annotation. The key departure is a shift from purely semantic or learnable query initialization toward interaction-grounded queries (DQG) and a dual-context feature fusion mechanism (DFS) that explicitly incorporates boundary-guided interaction cues alongside pixel-level semantics. The Conditional Co-occurrence loss introduces a physically-motivated constraint absent from prior EgoHOS methods, directly targeting the 'interaction illusion' where objects are predicted as manipulated by a hand that is not detected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interaction illusion problem is a practical reliability concern for embodied AI and AR/VR applications where physically inconsistent hand-object predictions undermine downstream reasoning. The strong OOD generalization (11.4% mIoU gain on mini-HOI4D) suggests that explicitly modeling interaction structure rather than relying on semantic features transfers better across domains. However, the method remains limited to single static frames, the CoCo loss's fixed pixel threshold is sensitive to imaging conditions (hand distance, resolution), and occlusion handling is unaddressed—open questions that the authors identify as requiring video/multi-view extensions and adaptive thresholding.
