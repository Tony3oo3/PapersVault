## Problem

Deep neural networks for medical image segmentation produce overconfident predictions that undermine clinical reliability, yet existing calibration methods operate exclusively in the spatial domain and neglect frequency-domain structure. This paper identifies two spectral causes of miscalibration—spectral bias (overemphasis on low-frequency components) and confidence saturation (suppressed overall power spectral density in confidence maps)—and shows that well-calibrated predictions distribute spectral power more evenly across frequency bands.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D medical image volumes (96×96×96 patches, normalized to [0,1]) and produces per-voxel segmentation masks alongside calibrated confidence maps. Unusually, the spectral analysis operates on raw logits (voxel-wise maximum across classes) rather than softmax probabilities, because logits retain a wider dynamic range that exposes the precursors to overconfidence before saturation masks spectral characteristics. The framework is architecture-agnostic and has been validated on UNet, nnUNet, SwinUNETR, UNet++, AttentionUNet, and TransUNet.

## Method

The core contribution is a frequency-aware calibration loss with two coupled components. First, a 3D FFT is applied to each sample's logit-derived confidence map, the resulting PSD is partitioned into K concentric spherical frequency shells, and the Shannon entropy of the normalized per-band power distribution is computed. A hinge-like spectral entropy loss then penalizes only those samples whose entropy falls below a dynamic target, pushing spectral power from dominant low-frequency bands into underrepresented high-frequency bands. Second, a temporal smoothing module maintains a sliding window of W recent batches' spectral power statistics, averaging them to produce a stable target entropy that reduces per-sample noise and prevents the regularization from chasing batch-specific fluctuations. The total training objective is a weighted sum of standard cross-entropy and this spectral regularization, with a single hyperparameter λ controlling the calibration strength.

## Results

The proposed spectral entropy regularization achieves the lowest ECE across all six datasets, reducing it by up to 97% over the CE baseline (e.g., 0.8×10⁻³ vs. 25.5×10⁻³ on FLARE2021, 2.1×10⁻³ vs. 32.5×10⁻³ on ACDC). It also attains the best SCE on all datasets (e.g., 0.6×10⁻³ on FLARE2021, 0.8×10⁻³ on BraTS2020). On segmentation, the method maintains or improves DSC over the CE baseline on all six datasets (e.g., 87.2% on BraTS2020, 92.5% on FLARE2021), confirming that calibration gains come without sacrificing accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against five in-training calibration baselines: Focal Loss (FL), Label Smoothing (LS), Margin-based Label Smoothing (MbLS), Spatially Variant Label Smoothing (SVLS), and Class and Region-Adaptive Constraints (CRaC). On ECE, the proposed method wins on all six datasets, with the largest margins over CRaC (the strongest spatial-domain competitor): 1.5 vs. 2.2 on BraTS2020, 0.8 vs. 2.4 on FLARE2021, and 2.1 vs. 28.1 on ACDC. On SCE, it also achieves the best score on all six datasets, e.g., 0.6 vs. 9.4 (CRaC) on FLARE2021 and 14.8 vs. 52.6 (CRaC) on PROMISE2012. The one exception is TACE, where CRaC outperforms the proposed method on ACDC (5.3 vs. 18.5) and PROMISE2012 (41.2 vs. 55.7), attributed to CRaC's task-specific spatial constraints for small, well-defined structures. On segmentation (DSC), the method matches or slightly exceeds all baselines, e.g., 87.2% vs. 86.9% (CE) on BraTS2020 and 92.5% vs. 91.5% (CE) on FLARE2021, confirming no accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant spatial-domain calibration paradigm (temperature scaling, label smoothing, focal loss, CRaC) by reframing miscalibration as a frequency-domain problem rooted in two identified failure modes: spectral bias (overemphasis on low-frequency components) and confidence saturation (suppressed overall PSD in confidence maps). It borrows the concentric spherical shell band decomposition from Bo et al. (2025, FAMNet) but repurposes it for a fundamentally different objective—regularizing the output confidence spectrum rather than matching input/feature spectra for cross-domain transfer. The spectral bias phenomenon itself is well-established in general deep learning theory (Rahaman et al. 2019; Xu et al. 2019), but its explicit connection to calibration failure in medical segmentation is new to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play spectral entropy loss offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic mechanism that consistently improves ECE and SCE across six diverse 3D medical imaging datasets without sacrificing segmentation accuracy, making it practically attractive for clinical deployment where calibrated uncertainty is critical. However, the method's TACE performance on PROMISE2012 (55.7×10⁻³) is actually worse than the CE baseline (48.1×10⁻³), suggesting the frequency-domain regularization may not capture all facets of fine-grained confidence miscalibration in small, well-defined structures. Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to 2D segmentation, whether the temporal smoothing window introduces lag in non-stationary training regimes, and whether the spectral entropy target could be made anatomy- or modality-adaptive rather than relying on a single global hyperparameter λ.
