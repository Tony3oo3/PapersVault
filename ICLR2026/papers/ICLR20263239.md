## Problem

GRAAL and AdGD adapt their stepsize to local curvature at a geometric rate but lack Nesterov acceleration, while AC-FGM and AdaNAG add acceleration but restrict stepsize growth to sublinear rates, severely limiting their ability to adapt to curvature changes. The paper resolves this gap by developing the first algorithm that simultaneously achieves near-optimal O(√(L‖x₀−x*‖²/ε)) iteration complexity and true geometric-rate adaptation to local curvature, without any line search or hyperparameter tuning.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires only an initial point x₀, oracle access to f(x) and ∇f(x), a small initial stepsize η₀ (e.g., 10⁻¹⁰, with no requirement to know L or L₀), and a target number of iterations K. It outputs the averaged iterate x̄_K, which is the point achieving the suboptimality bound f(x̄_K) − f(x*) ≤ ε. Notably, the algorithm is fully parameter-free beyond the universal constants θ, γ, ν (which satisfy a fixed algebraic relation), and a too-small η₀ incurs only a logarithmic additive penalty in the iteration count.

## Method

Accelerated GRAAL combines three mechanisms: (i) a Bregman-divergence-based local curvature estimator Λ(x;z) = 2D_f(x;z)/‖∇f(x)−∇f(z)‖², (ii) Nesterov acceleration via the Kovalev–Borodich reparameterization with adaptive weights α_k = (1+γ)η_{k−1}/(H_{k−1}+(1+γ)η_{k−1}), and (iii) GRAAL extrapolation x̂_{k+1} = x_{k+1} + θ(x_{k+1}−x_k). The key technical innovation is an additional coupling step x̄_{k+1} = β_k x̃_k + (1−β_k) x̄_k with β_k = η_k/(α_k H_k), which breaks the circular dependency between α_k and η_k that would otherwise force a predefined α_k ∝ 2/(k+2) and restrict stepsize growth. The adaptive stepsize η_{k+1} = min{(1+γ)η_k, νH_{k−1}λ_{k+1}/η_{k−1}} permits geometric growth, enabling the algorithm to recover from arbitrarily small initial stepsizes and to track exponentially varying local curvature under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness.

## Results

For L-smooth convex functions, Accelerated GRAAL achieves the near-optimal iteration complexity K = O(1 + √(L‖x₀−x*‖²/ε) + ln[1/(η₀L)]), matching Nesterov's accelerated gradient descent up to a small logarithmic additive factor, without any hyperparameter tuning or line search. Under the more general (L₀, L₁)-smoothness assumption, the method achieves K = O(1 + √(L₀D²/ε) + L₁³D³ + (1+L₁²D²)ln[1/(η₀L₀)]), which is near-optimal up to additive constants independent of the target precision ε. The key algorithmic feature enabling these results is geometric stepsize growth η_{k+1} ≤ (1+γ)η_k, which allows the method to adapt to local curvature at a linear rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Li et al. (2023) and Gorbunov et al. (2024), Accelerated GRAAL strictly dominates: both baselines carry exponential factors in L₁D and are neither optimal nor adaptive, whereas this paper achieves near-optimal complexity with full adaptivity. Against Vankov et al. (2024) and Tyurin (2025), the comparison is nuanced: Vankov's additive term (L₁D)^(5/3) and Tyurin's (L₁D)² are slightly better than this paper's (L₁D)³, but neither method is adaptive — Vankov requires solving a one-dimensional auxiliary subproblem per iteration, and Tyurin requires tuning several parameters. Against the concurrent accelerated adaptive methods AC-FGM (Li & Lan, 2025) and AdaNAG (Suh & Ma, 2025), the paper's advantage is decisive: their stepsize growth is sublinear (η_{k+1} ≤ (1+1/k)η_k), which causes AC-FGM's complexity to degrade by a factor of 1/√(η₀L) for small initial stepsizes and AdaNAG's to degrade by η₀L for large ones, and neither provides any convergence guarantees under (L₀, L₁)-smoothness. The geometric growth η_{k+1} ≤ (1+γ)η_k in this paper is shown to be essential for avoiding exponential factors under (L₀, L₁)-smoothness, where local curvature can change at an exponential rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the GRAAL/AdGD line of adaptive gradient methods (Malitsky 2020; Malitsky & Mishchenko 2020) by incorporating Nesterov acceleration via the Kovalev & Borodich (2024) reparameterization of the STM framework. It departs from the two closest prior attempts—AC-FGM (Li & Lan 2025) and AdaNAG (Suh & Ma 2025)—by introducing an additional coupling step (the β_k parameter) that decouples the acceleration weight α_k from the adaptive stepsize, thereby permitting geometric stepsize growth rather than the sublinear growth those methods are forced to use. The local curvature estimator uses the Bregman-divergence-based Option II from eq. (11), shared with AC-FGM and AdaNAG, but the stepsize rule in eq. (17) is a genuine generalization of both the GRAAL and AdGD rules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first adaptive (hyperparameter-free, no line search) algorithm to achieve near-optimal O(√(L₀D²/ε)) iteration complexity under (L₀,L₁)-smoothness, a setting motivated by empirical observations in deep learning where the Hessian norm correlates with gradient norm. The key open question the paper itself raises is whether the specific GRAAL-type extrapolation is essential or whether other baseline algorithms could yield similar results, since the proof heavily relies on that particular extrapolation structure. The work is purely theoretical with no empirical validation, and the logarithmic additive factors in the complexity (from choosing a small η₀) remain a minor practical caveat. For the broader optimization community, the result closes a natural gap: adaptive methods previously could not match accelerated convergence rates while retaining true local-curvature adaptivity.
