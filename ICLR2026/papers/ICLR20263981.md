## Problem

Knowledge editing and machine unlearning are the two dominant approaches for updating LLM knowledge, yet they are studied in isolation on small, static benchmarks that test isolated facts without capturing how changes propagate through structured, interconnected knowledge. No unified framework exists to jointly analyze their trade-offs in propagation, consistency, and robustness, and the role of data scale and hierarchical structure in determining update effectiveness remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a pre-trained LLM, a hierarchical knowledge graph (root → intermediate → leaf), and a factual triple as the update request, then automatically generates a multiple-choice QA benchmark (MMLU-style, 4 options) with six probe types (direct, reverse, conflict, multi-hop, comparison, contextual) spanning all three hierarchy levels. The output is updated model parameters plus a suite of diagnostic metrics: Collateral Change Ratio (CCR) quantifying over-spreading, Residual Retention (RR) quantifying under-spreading, consistency scores on inverse relations, and OOD/adversarial robustness scores. The pipeline produces 360,000 training samples across four domains (biology, economics, history, physics) with data scales from 1 to 10,000 examples per branch.

## Method

KnowledgeSmith unifies editing and unlearning as a single constrained optimization: minimize a task loss on positive probes Q⁺ plus a preservation loss on Q⁻ plus a regularization term, where editing sets q_target to a corrected factual distribution and unlearning sets it to a neutral (erased) distribution. An automatic three-stage data generator (KG construction via GPT-4o → template-based question generation → multiple-choice formatting) transforms any existing knowledge-related dataset into a dynamic benchmark with hierarchical propagation structure. The paper then conducts controlled experiments across 13 models (1B–123B, six families) using AlphaEdit for editing and ReLearn for unlearning, varying hierarchy level and data scale. A key theoretical contribution is an SVD-based geometric analysis showing that editing acts as a local rotation with mild rescaling of the representation space (preserving global geometry), while unlearning acts as anisotropic scaling that aggressively shrinks certain singular-value dimensions (reducing capacity in targeted directions), explaining the observed propagation asymmetry.

## Results

On the KnowledgeSmith benchmark (4 domains, 360K training samples, 13 LLMs from 1B–123B), editing (AlphaEdit) achieves up to 50–60% in-domain accuracy in economics but degrades OOD accuracy (e.g., LLaMA-3-8B OOD drops from ~63% to ~25% at 100+ samples), while unlearning (ReLearn) preserves OOD accuracy at 63–82% but yields only ≤30% in-domain gains. Adversarial robustness shows editing dropping from 36.7% to 31.7% across data scales, whereas unlearning remains stable at 33–35%. Computationally, unlearning completes in ~0.2 h vs. ~6 h for editing on 1,000 samples (NVIDIA H100). Hallucination rates remain stable for both (76.0% unlearning, 76.1% editing on TruthfulQA), but instruction-following drops from ~63% to ~49% for both methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlphaEdit (editing) outperforms ReLearn (unlearning) on in-domain enforcement, reaching 50–60% ID accuracy in economics vs. ≤30% for unlearning, but at the cost of OOD degradation (63%→25% for mid-sized models) and higher conflict rates (30% vs. 12%). ReLearn preserves OOD accuracy (63–82%) and adversarial robustness (33–35% stable) far better than AlphaEdit, but suffers from under-spreading (35% under-forgetting vs. 20%) and rarely removes targeted knowledge completely. LoRA fine-tuning is the weakest baseline: ID accuracy is unstable (dropping to 12.5% at k=1000) and OOD drifts from 63.0% to 61.6%, making it unreliable for continual updates. In Appendix E, MEND (editing) and TOFU (unlearning) replicate the same hierarchy-dependent propagation asymmetry, confirming findings are algorithm-agnostic. Sequential updates (Appendix F) show editing retains prior edits with only ~1.5% drift over 5 steps, while unlearning degrades cumulatively (Fact 1 accuracy drops from 54.2% to 27.2% after 5 sequential unlearnings).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously disjoint research threads—knowledge editing (ROME, MEMIT, MEND, AlphaEdit) and machine unlearning (ReLearn, TOFU, influence-function-based forgetting)—by casting both as instances of a single constrained optimization problem where the distinction reduces to the choice of target distribution q_target. It departs from prior evaluation practice (CounterFact, ZsRE), which tests isolated facts, by introducing KG-structured hierarchical probes (root/intermediate/leaf) and propagation-specific metrics (CCR, RR, conflict rate). The SVD-based geometric analysis connects empirical observations to representational geometry, extending the interpretability lens beyond what prior editing/unlearning papers provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework and 360K-sample KG-structured benchmark give the editing/unlearning community a principled way to compare methods on propagation, consistency, and robustness rather than isolated fact accuracy. Key open questions remain: no theoretical bounds on propagation or consistency are provided, evaluation is limited to four text domains and two primary methods, and the consistency-capacity tradeoff lacks a mechanistic explanation beyond the SVD decomposition. The subject-dependent resistance finding (history being hardest to update) suggests that future evaluation must be domain-aware, and the sequential unlearning degradation highlights a practical risk for iterative knowledge management.
