## Problem

Geospatial representation learning has split into two paradigms—Earth observation (physical state) and human-centric (activity patterns)—but no principled framework fuses both with a structural backbone that captures non-local functional relationships between locations. Additionally, there is a lack of comprehensive benchmarks for evaluating human-centric geospatial representations at national scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261287/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on H3 level-6 grid cells (~36 km² each) across all of China (~195K cells). The mobility backbone is a graph of ~200K nodes and 1.2 billion edges constructed from 54 weeks of Tencent payment transactions across 50M stores. Auxiliary inputs include 100M+ POIs (names + 445 categories), ~10m-resolution satellite imagery per cell, and WorldPop demographic histograms (10-year age bands × gender × work-home status). Output is a 128-dimensional embedding per grid cell; a distilled variant accepts only a 1024-d SIREN coordinate encoding and produces the same 128-d vector, enabling coordinate-only inference without external data lookups.

## Method

MoRA treats human mobility as the 'syntax of geospatial space,' analogous to how word meaning emerges from co-occurrence in LLMs: H3 cells are 'spatial tokens' and mobility flows are the 'sentences' that provide functional context. The architecture initializes node features with LINE second-order proximity embeddings, encodes a top-10% sampled mobility subgraph with LightGCN (3 layers, neighborhood aggregation only), and then aligns three auxiliary modalities—POI text via BGE-m3, satellite imagery via RemoteCLIP, and demographic histograms via an MLP—to the mobility embedding in a shared 128-d space using an asymmetric CLIP-style contrastive loss (each auxiliary modality is pulled toward its own mobility anchor while pushed away from all other grid anchors in the batch). The key differentiator is that the mobility graph, not geographic adjacency, defines the relational structure, capturing long-range 'functional adjacency' (e.g., commuter corridors) that proximity-based graphs miss, as confirmed by a 12.2% performance drop when substituting a neighborhood graph.

## Results

MoRA achieves an average R² of 0.78 across 9 downstream socio-economic prediction tasks using a compact 128-dimensional embedding, outperforming the best state-of-the-art baseline by 12.9% on average (10.8% for social tasks, 16.0% for economic tasks). The largest gains appear on offline consumption prediction (R²=0.91 vs. 0.46 for CSP, +12.1%) and energy consumption (R²=0.72 vs. 0.81 for AlphaEarth, where MoRA actually trails by 8.4%). MoRA wins 7 of 9 tasks, with the only losses being nighttime light (−1.7% vs. AlphaEarth) and energy consumption (−8.4% vs. AlphaEarth). In the Jiangsu Province comparison against methodological baselines (Table 3), MoRA achieves a 20.9% average gain over the second-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the seven pre-trained location encoders (Table 2), MoRA (128-d) dominates on 7 of 9 tasks. AlphaEarth is the strongest single baseline, leading on NTL (0.63 vs. 0.62) and ENE (0.81 vs. 0.72), but MoRA surpasses it on all social tasks (e.g., EDU: 0.85 vs. 0.77; ELD: 0.81 vs. 0.71) and on COS (0.91 vs. not reported). SatCLIP and Siren (both 256/1024-d) are competitive on HSR (0.74) but fall well behind MoRA (0.81) on most other tasks. Analytical encoders (Sphere2Vec, NeRF, Siren) cluster around R² 0.33–0.66, confirming that learning-free position encoders cannot capture functional regional semantics. In the Jiangsu Province comparison (Table 3), MoRA beats HREP by 18.0% on POP and 47.2% on HSR, outperforms ReCP on 7/9 tasks (losing only on EDU: 0.75 vs. 0.78), and surpasses ReFound on 7/9 tasks (losing only on ENE: 0.73 vs. 0.80), achieving a 20.9% average gain over the second-best method while using only 128 dimensions versus ReFound's 768.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoRA carves new ground by repurposing the LLM analogy (word meaning from co-occurrence context) and ViT tokenization to geospatial space, where human mobility sequences serve as the 'syntax' that gives functional meaning to H3 grid 'tokens.' It departs from the Earth-observation paradigm (AlphaEarth, SatCLIP, GeoCLIP) that encodes physical states, and from prior human-centric region embedding work (HREP, ReCP, ReFound, PDFM) that fuses modalities via coordinate alignment or simple concatenation on static neighborhood graphs. Technically, it composes well-known components—LightGCN, LINE pre-encoding, CLIP-style contrastive alignment, BGE-m3, and RemoteCLIP—into a novel architecture where a billion-edge mobility graph is the structural anchor to which all other modalities are aligned, rather than one modality among equals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first empirical evidence of scaling laws in geospatial representation learning, showing that expanding pretraining coverage from provincial to national scale yields diminishing but consistent gains—echoing LLM scaling behavior and arguing for national-scale geospatial foundation models. The 9-task socio-economic benchmark and the open-sourced distilled model (coordinates in, vectors out) lower the barrier for human-centric geospatial inference. Key limitations include dependence on Tencent payment data (China-specific), the small sample size for city-level tasks (283 for energy), and the unresolved question of whether the mobility-backbone advantage persists when high-quality mobility data is unavailable, for which the authors suggest a neighborhood-graph variant as a fallback.
