## Problem

Existing personalized reward models reduce diverse, scenario-dependent user preferences to a small fixed set of evaluation rules, failing to capture how a single user's criteria shift across contexts (e.g., brevity while driving vs. expressiveness in casual chat). They also generalize poorly to new users with sparse feedback, limiting cold-start effectiveness in open-ended dialogue settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262407/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a current user query, a limited set (typically 3) of historical preference pairs (chosen/rejected response triples), optional explicit preference criteria (style, tone, scoring dimensions), and one or more candidate responses. It produces a free-text structured evaluation chain that first derives a scenario-specific user persona and then generates weighted scoring rubrics; a scalar reward is extracted from this chain via a parsing operation. At test-time, the final score is an average over m individual-level scoring schemes (parallel samples) plus n scores inferred from similar users assigned to the same prototype cluster.

## Method

P-GenRM is trained in three stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning on rejection-sampled structured evaluation chains that map hybrid preference signals to persona + rubric outputs; (2) GRPO-based reinforcement learning with a composite reward combining an LLM-judged process score (how well the chain covers stated/synthetic preferences) and a rule-based outcome score (correct chosen/rejected ranking); (3) hard-negative-aware curriculum learning that progressively increases the proportion of difficult negative pairs while disabling the process reward to widen exploration. At test-time, a dual-granularity scaling mechanism addresses noise and cold-start: user preference embeddings are clustered via K-means into prototypes that are refined through a history-aware attentive update (attention over past interaction triples weighted by query relevance and prototype affinity, with pairwise discriminative loss and center/trust-region regularization), and the final score aggregates m parallel individual-level samples with n scores from the most embedding-similar users in the same prototype, yielding a ~3% gain over the base model.

## Results

On PersonalRewardBench, P-GenRM-8B achieves 72.68% accuracy on Chatbot Arena-Personalized and 65.32% on PRISM-Personalized, surpassing the prior SOTA (FT RM + SynthesizeMe at 69.78% and 62.84%) by 2.90% and 2.48% respectively. With Test-time User-based Scaling (Ind-16, Pro-8), P-GenRM-8B further improves to 75.92% and 68.06%, yielding an additional ~3% gain over the base model. On the out-of-distribution LaMP-QA cold-start benchmark, P-GenRM-8B+Ind-8,Pro-4 achieves 0.638 Spearman rank correlation, outperforming the much larger Qwen3-235B-A22B (0.619) and LLaMA-3.1-70B (0.600).

## Baselines & Comparisons

P-GenRM-8B (72.68% / 65.32%) decisively outperforms all existing personalized reward model baselines on both datasets: it beats FT RM + SynthesizeMe (69.78% / 62.84%) by 2.90% and 2.48%, Bradley-Terry Finetuned RM (67.21% / 63.27%) by 5.47% and 2.05%, and the group/variational/prototypical methods GPO (57.87% / 57.29%), VPL (58.12% / 58.25%), and PAL (57.31% / 56.74%) by margins exceeding 14% on Chatbot Arena. P-GenRM-8B also surpasses the proprietary OpenAI o3 model with strong prompting (o3+PSI: 69.14% / 63.87%) by 3.54% and 1.45% respectively. The Test-time User-based Scaling mechanism (Ind-16, Pro-8) provides an additional 3.24% gain over P-GenRM without scaling, and notably, the combined Ind-16+Pro-8 setting (75.92%) outperforms pure individual scaling Ind-32 (75.59%) with fewer total inference steps, demonstrating the complementary value of prototype-level transfer. On the LaMP-QA cold-start evaluation, P-GenRM-8B+Ind-8,Pro-4 (0.638 Spearman) outperforms all baselines including the 235B-parameter Qwen3 model (0.619), confirming strong generalization to unseen users with only 3 historical preference pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

P-GenRM sits at the intersection of two research threads: generative reward models (GenRM), which reframe reward scoring as structured text generation (Zhang et al., 2024a; Li et al., 2023b), and personalized alignment via RL, where prior methods like SynthesizeMe (Ryan et al., 2025), PAL (Chen et al., 2024), and VPL (Poddar et al., 2024) model user preferences through static personas, learned mixture weights, or variational latent variables. The key conceptual departure is treating the reward model as a generative system that produces scenario-adaptive evaluation chains (persona + weighted rubrics) rather than a fixed scalar or a static persona prompt, and then exploiting the inherent test-time scalability of generation through a dual-granularity mechanism (individual parallel sampling + prototype-level transfer from similar users). The training pipeline (SFT → GRPO-based RL → hard-negative curriculum) adapts standard RLHF techniques to the specific challenge of generating high-quality evaluation chains under sparse or missing explicit preference signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent gaps in personalized reward modeling—static preference representations that fail to capture scenario-dependent variability, and weak cold-start generalization to new users—through a unified prototype-based scaling mechanism. The practical finding that an 8B model with test-time scaling (Ind-16, Pro-8) surpasses a LoRA-trained 70B model, and that an 8B policy model trained with P-GenRM as reward outperforms 70B policy models, suggests that structured generative reward signals can partially substitute for model scale in personalization. Limitations include the need for three historical preference pairs (moderate data requirement) and the inference cost of generating full evaluation chains versus direct scalar scoring. Open questions include whether the prototype mechanism adapts to temporal drift in user preferences and whether the approach extends to truly open-ended preference spaces beyond what can be captured by embedding-based clustering.
