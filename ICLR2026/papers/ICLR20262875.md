## Problem

Post-training quantization (PTQ) for visual autoregressive models (VAR) remains largely unexplored, with two specific challenges identified: (1) large reconstruction errors in attention-value products, particularly at coarse scales where fewer tokens concentrate high attention scores, and (2) a mismatch between the sampling frequencies of VQVAE codebook entries and their predicted probabilities due to the extremely limited calibration data available in PTQ. These issues cause significant quality degradation when quantizing VAR to low bit-widths.

## Input / Output

The framework quantizes an already-trained VAR model (or its text-to-image extension Infinity) without modifying its input/output interface: it accepts class labels, text prompts, or partially masked images and produces 256×256 generated, inpainted, outpainted, or edited images. The quantization applies uniformly to all tensors involved in matrix multiplications—weights, activations, and self-attention components (query, key, value, softmax attention)—using the same bit-width, enabling deployment on conventional hardware without mixed-precision support. Calibration requires only 256 images, and the method introduces a BOP (bits of precision) budget constraint to control the computational overhead of the proposed quantization kernel.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary techniques. First, shift-and-sum quantization introduces a quantization kernel f_n(v; t_n) that averages quantized results from 2n symmetrically shifted duplicates of a value token, provably reducing the quantization error bound from s/4 to s/(4n) (Theorem 1). This kernel is applied only to 'attentive tokens'—value tokens whose average attention score exceeds a threshold θ—with the kernel order assigned adaptively as a power of two to bring each token's effective attention score below θ, keeping overhead to ~1% of total inference BOPs. Second, calibration data resampling aligns the empirical sampling frequencies of VQVAE codebook entries with their predicted probabilities by iteratively reassigning tokens from oversampled to undersampled entries, ensuring the limited calibration set accurately reflects the underlying distribution. The method builds on BRECQ for base quantization and uses log2 quantizers for softmax attentions and uniform quantizers for all other tensors.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet generation, the proposed method (Ours) at 4/4-bit achieves IS 90.7 and FID 24.57 on VAR-d16, substantially outperforming BRECQ (IS 67.6, FID 33.03) and LiteVAR (IS 66.9, FID 35.87). At 6/6-bit on VAR-d16, Ours reaches FID 4.46 and FID2FP16 3.14, compared to BRECQ (FID 5.56) and LiteVAR (FID 5.17). Combining with LiteVAR (Ours+LiteVAR) further improves to FID 18.92 at 4/4-bit and FID 4.08 at 6/6-bit on VAR-d16, approaching the full-precision FID of 3.41. Improvements are consistent across VAR-d16, d20, d24, and d30, as well as on text-to-image (Infinity-2B), inpainting, outpainting, and editing tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two PTQ baselines: BRECQ (a general-purpose block-reconstruction quantizer) and LiteVAR (the only prior PTQ method designed for VAR, which retains FC layers after GELU in full precision). The proposed method consistently outperforms BRECQ across all bit-widths (6/6, 4/8, 4/6, 4/4) and all four VAR depths; the gap is most dramatic at 4/4-bit, where Ours achieves IS 90.7 vs. BRECQ's 67.6 on VAR-d16 (a 34% relative improvement in IS) and FID 24.57 vs. 33.03. Against LiteVAR, Ours also wins on every metric at every setting, e.g., at 4/4-bit VAR-d16: IS 90.7 vs. 66.9, FID 24.57 vs. 35.87, and FID2FP16 24.35 vs. 36.67. Notably, LiteVAR's advantage relies on mixed-precision arithmetic (keeping post-GELU FC layers at 16-bit), whereas Ours quantizes all tensors uniformly, making it more hardware-friendly. Combining Ours with LiteVAR (Ours+LiteVAR) yields the best results overall, confirming the two approaches are complementary: at 4/4-bit VAR-d16, the combination reaches IS 110.9 and FID 18.92, closing most of the gap to full-precision (IS 274.8, FID 3.41). On the Infinity-2B text-to-image task, Ours similarly beats both baselines on ImageReward, HPSv2.1, and GenEval at all bit-widths, with the largest gains at 4/4-bit (ImageReward 0.575 vs. 0.346 for BRECQ and 0.407 for LiteVAR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on BRECQ as its base PTQ framework and addresses the specific challenge of quantizing Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models, a multi-scale next-scale prediction architecture that had only one prior PTQ method (LiteVAR). It departs from LiteVAR by quantizing all tensors—including FC layers after GELU—at a uniform bit-width, avoiding the mixed-precision hardware requirements of that approach. The two proposed techniques (shift-and-sum quantization for attention-value products and calibration data resampling for VQVAE codebook alignment) are novel contributions tailored to the structural properties of VAR, drawing on log2 quantizers for attention scores and a theoretical error-bound analysis (Theorem 1) to guide the kernel design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VAR models are increasingly popular as alternatives to diffusion models for image generation, but their multi-scale iterative generation across multiple transformer blocks makes them computationally expensive, creating a strong practical need for efficient quantization. This work establishes a new SOTA in PTQ for VAR across multiple tasks and architectures, and the shift-and-sum technique is potentially generalizable to other transformer-based generative models with attention mechanisms. However, the authors note that effectiveness degrades below 4 bits, and the method has only been validated on VAR-family models and OneFormer, leaving open whether the approach transfers to other autoregressive or diffusion architectures. The calibration resampling technique, while effective, is specific to VQVAE-based tokenizers and may not apply to continuous-latent generative models.
