## Problem

Systematic generalization—the ability to recombine known components into novel compositions—has been demonstrated via meta-learning for compositionality (MLC) only in linguistic settings, leaving its applicability to abstract spatial reasoning unexplored. Meanwhile, state-of-the-art LLMs (GPT-4o, o3-mini, Gemini 2.0 Flash) fail to systematically generalize geometric transformations in ARC-style tasks, raising the question of whether a principled training strategy can close this gap.

## Input / Output

Each episode presents a sequence of input-output 10×10 grid pairs (integer values 0–9, where 0 is background) as study examples, followed by a query input grid; the model must generate the corresponding 10×10 output grid. In the systematicity setup, the 12 study examples comprise six primitive transformations and six level-1 compositions, while the query requires an unseen level-2 composition. Grids are encoded as 25 non-overlapping 2×2 patches (up to 10,000 distinct configurations each), with special boundary tokens separating study examples and input-output pairs.

## Method

The paper extends MLC (Lake & Baroni, 2023) from pseudolanguage to a visual interpretation grammar that maps three indicator types (object shape, color, proximity to a neighbor object) to five geometric transformations (translation, rotation, reflection, extension, color change). A 5.7M-parameter transformer encoder-decoder (3 layers, 8 heads, 128-dim embeddings, 768-dim feedforward) is trained over 100,000 episodes, each with a uniquely sampled transformation triplet, forcing the model to infer the grammar from study examples rather than memorize fixed mappings. The model uses 2D positional embeddings decomposed into row and column components for spatial awareness, an auxiliary copy task that requires reproducing each study example's output, background patches down-weighted by 0.2 in the loss, and minor color perturbations (p=0.001) for decoder robustness. Data splits are constructed so that the specific level-2 transformation compositions in the test set are disjoint from those in training, ensuring true out-of-distribution compositional generalization.

## Results

On the 3-Shot task, the 5.7M-parameter MLC model achieves 99.92% exact match accuracy, outperforming the best domain-specific LLM (Mistral-NeMO-Minitron-8B-Full at 95.71%) and all general-purpose LLMs (o3-mini at 64.04%, Gemini 2.0 Flash at 30.08%, GPT-4o at 22.28%). On the Systematicity task, MLC achieves 78.26% exact match accuracy, matching the ARC Prize 2024 winner (Mistral-NeMO-Minitron-8B-Full + test-time training at 78.20%), while all general-purpose LLMs score below 3% (GPT-4o: 0.99%, Gemini 2.0 Flash: 2.66%, o3-mini: 0.53%). Color accuracy remains near-perfect (>97%) across all models on both tasks, but shape accuracy drops dramatically for LLMs on the Systematicity task (below 13% for general-purpose models).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The MLC model (5.7M parameters) decisively outperforms all three general-purpose LLMs on both tasks: on 3-Shot it leads o3-mini by 35.9 points (99.92% vs. 64.04%) and GPT-4o by 77.6 points; on Systematicity the gap is even more extreme, with MLC at 78.26% versus o3-mini at 0.53% and GPT-4o at 0.99%. Against the domain-specific ARC Prize 2024 winner (Mistral-NeMO-Minitron-8B-Full, 8B parameters), MLC wins the 3-Shot task by 4.2 points (99.92% vs. 95.71%) and essentially ties on Systematicity when the 8B model is augmented with test-time training (78.26% vs. 78.20%). Without test-time training, both domain-specific LLMs collapse to near-chance on Systematicity (<1%), highlighting that their ARC-specific fine-tuning does not confer compositional generalization. The paper also notes that adding visual input to GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Flash prompts actually degrades performance, suggesting modality alignment issues rather than a genuine visual reasoning advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the meta-learning for compositionality (MLC) framework of Lake & Baroni (2023) from pseudolanguage instruction learning to abstract spatial reasoning, replacing the lexical interpretation grammar (pseudowords → symbols) with a visual interpretation grammar (shape/color/neighbor indicators → geometric transformations). It is also inspired by the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (Chollet, 2019) but deliberately narrows ARC's broad scope to a controlled setting that isolates the systematicity dimension—specifically, whether models can compose known primitive transformations into previously unseen combinations. The authors position this as the first application of MLC beyond linguistic tasks and the first ARC-style benchmark explicitly targeting compositional generalization rather than general abstract reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 5.7M-parameter encoder-decoder trained via MLC achieves systematic generalization to unseen transformation compositions (78.26% exact match) while state-of-the-art LLMs (GPT-4o, o3-mini, Gemini 2.0 Flash) collapse to near-chance levels (0.5–2.7%), suggesting that scale and reasoning depth do not confer compositional systematicity. This supports the view that the right training objective (dynamically changing grammars forcing recombination) matters more than model capacity for this class of tasks. Key limitations include the fixed 10×10 grid size, five constrained transformations, and the controlled composition structure; the authors note that extending MLC to decompose and generalize over more complex, open-ended ARC-like problems remains an open question.
