## Problem

Human-object-scene interaction (HOSI) generation requires reasoning over how the scene context evolves as a human manipulates an object through a cluttered environment, yet existing methods rely on one-off static scene encodings that cannot capture these dynamic changes. Compounding this, high-quality annotated HOSI data is extremely scarce due to the combinatorial explosion of object types, scene layouts, and task instructions, leaving no single dataset sufficient to train a generalizable model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D occupancy voxel scene (five N×N×N grids with values {0,1,2} for traversable/non-traversable/object-occupied, split into two static and three dynamic grids), object geometry as a 1024×3 Basis Point Set, a CLIP-encoded 768-dim text instruction, and 3D goal coordinates for both the human pelvis and object centroid. It outputs a coupled motion sequence: human root translation plus 6D rotations for 22 SMPL-X joints, and object centroid translation plus a 3×3 rotation matrix. The three dynamic voxel grids are masked during the initial coarse pass and then iteratively updated from the previous refinement's predicted pelvis positions, so the scene conditioning changes across denoising steps.

## Method

INFBAGEL trains a scene-conditioned diffusion model with a random mask over the dynamic voxel embeddings, then distills it into a consistency model via consistency distillation so that each few-step iteration directly outputs a clean motion sequence rather than a noisy intermediate. At inference, the model first generates a coarse trajectory using only static scene cues, then iteratively refines it: each consistency-model step's output updates the three dynamic voxel grids (re-centered on the predicted pelvis positions), and a lightweight bump-aware guidance term—computed from a pre-computed voxel distance map—nudges the sample away from occupied cells without requiring mesh-level collision queries. To overcome data scarcity, a hybrid training strategy jointly uses LINGO (real HSI with high-fidelity scene meshes) and OMOMO (HOI with synthesized voxelized scene context created by voxelizing the free space around the human-object occupancy volume), enabling the model to learn both scene navigation and object manipulation without a single all-encompassing HOSI dataset. Long-horizon sequences are produced autoregressively, with the consistency model's speed making per-segment scene-state updates feasible in real time (~29 FPS).

## Results

On the HOSI benchmark (469 sequences, 67 unseen scenes), INFBAGEL achieves a task success rate of 83.16%, substantially outperforming LINGO (53.09%) and TRUMANS (1.92%), while also attaining the lowest foot sliding (0.13), highest contact percentage (78.18%), and lowest human-object penetration (3.96 m). On the standard HOI benchmark (OMOMO), INFBAGEL with 16 refinement steps achieves the best FID (0.68), contact F1 (0.73), and contact percentage (0.60), surpassing CHOIS and ROG, while running at 29.31 FPS—over 3× faster than ROG (9.07 FPS). With just a single consistency-model step, INFBAGEL already matches CHOIS on contact F1 (0.66) at over 1500 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TRUMANS, INFBAGEL dominates across every metric: success rate jumps from 1.92% to 83.16%, foot sliding drops from 0.71 to 0.13, and object-scene mean penetration falls from 56.03 m to 16.62 m, reflecting TRUMANS's inability to handle dynamic object-scene changes. Against LINGO, INFBAGEL improves success rate by 30 percentage points (83.16% vs. 53.09%), nearly triples contact percentage (78.18% vs. 53.83%), and reduces human-scene mean penetration by more than half (3.39 m vs. 7.62 m); LINGO's only advantage is a slightly lower human goal distance (2.75 cm vs. 4.75 cm), a trade-off the authors attribute to INFBAGEL prioritizing collision-free motion over strict goal adherence. On the HOI benchmark, INFBAGEL (16 steps) outperforms CHOIS on FID (0.68 vs. 1.01), contact recall (0.73 vs. 0.63), contact F1 (0.73 vs. 0.66), and object goal error (3.06 vs. 5.28 cm), while matching CHOIS on foot sliding (0.32). Against ROG, INFBAGEL achieves dramatically lower object goal error (3.06 vs. 26.68 cm), FID (0.68 vs. 5.46), and root translation error (7.93 vs. 21.28 cm), while running 3.2× faster (29.31 vs. 9.07 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies human-object interaction (HOI) and human-scene interaction (HSI) generation into a single HOSI framework, departing from prior scene-aware methods (TRUMANS, LINGO) that rely on one-off static scene encoding and from HOI methods (CHOIS, ROG) that lack scene awareness entirely. The key architectural departure is distilling a diffusion model into a consistency model to enable few-step iterative refinement, where each step's output updates the dynamic voxelized scene state for the next step—a mechanism not present in prior diffusion-based motion generators. The hybrid data training strategy, which synthesizes minimal voxelized scene context for HOI data and jointly trains with high-fidelity HSI data, extends the approach of Liu et al. (2024) by incorporating real scene geometry rather than relying solely on occupancy-based synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HOSI benchmark (469 sequences across 67 unseen scenes) and the hybrid data pipeline address a genuine data bottleneck that has limited progress in unified interaction generation, making the framework a practical foundation for downstream applications in robot learning and virtual character animation. The consistency-model-based iterative refinement achieving real-time speeds (~29 FPS) while maintaining strong collision avoidance opens the door to interactive applications where diffusion-based baselines are computationally infeasible. Limitations include the kinematics-based formulation (no physics simulation), a trade-off between collision avoidance and precise goal adherence, and evaluation restricted to indoor scenes; open questions include whether the dynamic perception strategy generalizes to outdoor or highly dynamic environments and whether the approach can be combined with physics-based controllers for full embodied interaction.
