## Problem

In long-term continual test-time adaptation, errors accumulate until the model collapses into predicting only a few classes for all inputs. Existing reset strategies (e.g., RDumb) use fixed periodic full-parameter resets that are misaligned with actual collapse risk—resetting too early or too late—and catastrophically discard all knowledge acquired during adaptation, causing large performance drops and long recovery delays.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled stream of RGB image batches arriving sequentially over a non-stationary domain sequence, along with a frozen pre-trained source model (ResNet-50 or ViT-B-16) for reference predictions. At each step it produces a class prediction for the current batch and updates model parameters in-place via unsupervised self-training. No source data, true labels, or target-domain assumptions are required; the model must remain stable across perpetually changing or cyclically recurring domain streams spanning millions of images.

## Method

The core contribution is an Adaptive and Selective Reset (ASR) scheme that dynamically determines both when and where to reset. A prediction concentration metric C_t (negative entropy of Softmax applied to batch-mean logits) is compared against its exponential moving average C̄_{t-1}; a reset is triggered only when C_t exceeds C̄_{t-1}, and the reset proportion r_t scales with the deviation magnitude, resetting layers from the output inward (since label-noise corruption originates at the network's end). To recover essential knowledge inevitably erased by reset, an importance-aware regularizer penalizes deviation from accumulated parameters weighted by Fisher information, using a hybrid CMA+EMA accumulation that balances local recency against global history. Finally, an on-the-fly adaptation adjustment reparameterizes the Fisher regularization coefficient (λ_F = λ_0·φ_t²) and the EMA momentum (μ_C = μ_0·φ_t + 1 − μ_0) based on prediction inconsistency φ_t between the source and current model, strengthening knowledge recovery and stabilizing the concentration baseline under severe domain shifts.

## Results

On the most challenging CCC-Hard benchmark, ASR applied to ROID achieves 22.21% accuracy, a 44.12% relative improvement over the previous best (RDumb+ROID at 15.41%). Across the full CCC benchmark (Easy/Medium/Hard mean), ASR+ROID reaches 38.81% versus 35.05% for RDumb+ROID and 34.28% for ROID alone. On CIN-C (i.i.d.), ASR+ROID achieves 49.50% accuracy, outperforming all baselines including CMF (48.61%) and ROID (48.58%). On the recurring IN-C benchmark, ASR+ROID attains 41.56% mean accuracy across 20 visits, surpassing RDumb+ROID (37.18%) and ROID (37.96%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASR consistently outperforms all 14 baselines on the primary CCC benchmark. The most dramatic gains appear on CCC-Hard, where ASR+ROID (22.21%) surpasses RDumb+ROID (15.41%) by 6.80 points and ROID alone (12.48%) by 9.73 points; methods without collapse-prevention (ETA, RoTTA, ViDA, PALM, COME) all collapse to near-zero accuracy on CCC-Hard. Among collapse-prevention baselines, CMF is the closest competitor on CCC-Easy/Medium (49.31/40.61 vs. ASR's 51.41/42.80) but collapses on CCC-Hard (0.89% vs. 22.21%). On CIN-C, ASR+ROID leads all methods by 0.9–1.0 points over CMF and ROID, and maintains this advantage under non-i.i.d. label distributions (49.14% vs. 48.28%). On the recurring IN-C benchmark, ASR+ROID achieves 41.56% mean accuracy, outperforming RDumb+ROID (37.18%) by 4.38 points and ROID (37.96%) by 3.60 points, with the gap widening at later visits (42.96% at visit 20 vs. 35.75% for RDumb). On IN-D109, ASR+ROID (46.49%) edges out ROID (46.16%) and RDumb+ROID (45.99%), demonstrating stability even with only 109 classes where skewed predictions are more easily triggered.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the long-term test-time adaptation (TTA) line of work, building directly on ROID's weight-ensembling framework (Marsden et al., 2024) as its primary TTA backbone and on Elastic Weight Consolidation (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) for Fisher-information-based parameter importance estimation. It departs from prior reset strategies—RDumb's fixed-interval full reset (Press et al., 2023), SAR's confidence-triggered reset (Niu et al., 2023), and DA-TTA's distribution-shift-triggered reset (Wang et al., 2024)—by making both the timing (via prediction concentration relative to an EMA baseline) and the scope (via a severity-scaled layer proportion) of reset adaptive and selective. The hybrid CMA+EMA knowledge accumulation scheme is a novel combination not present in prior EWC or continual learning literature, designed specifically to handle the tension between preserving historical adaptation and avoiding corruption near collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Model collapse in long-term TTA is a critical barrier to deploying adaptive models in real-world streaming settings, and this paper provides a practical, model-agnostic add-on that substantially improves stability without requiring architectural changes. The 44.12% gain on CCC-Hard and consistent improvements across four benchmarks and two architectures (ResNet-50, ViT-B-16) demonstrate broad applicability. Open questions include whether the prediction concentration metric generalizes to non-visual modalities or tasks without a fixed class set, how the method behaves under truly online (batch-size-1) settings, and whether the Fisher-based recovery can be made more computationally efficient for very large models. The finding that false-positive resets are beneficial suggests the reset mechanism may be more robust than its trigger precision implies, opening questions about optimal reset policies beyond binary trigger/no-trigger decisions.
