## Problem

Prior work on emotion in LLMs has focused on output-level behavior—sentiment classification, emotional text generation, or prompting-based affective evaluation—leaving the internal geometry of emotional representations in hidden states largely unexplored. This paper asks whether a consistent, low-dimensional emotional manifold exists within LLMs, whether it is stable across layers, datasets, and languages, and whether it can be causally manipulated to steer the model's internal emotional perception.

## Input / Output

The method consumes token-level hidden states from decoder-only LLMs (LLaMA 3.1-8B, OLMo-v2, Ministral-8B) on eight human-written emotion datasets spanning five languages, plus a synthetic emotion corpus used only to define the reference manifold. Token activations are mean-pooled to sentence vectors, centered, and decomposed via SVD to yield a 50-dimensional emotional subspace. Outputs include geometric alignment metrics (cosine similarity, stress-2, average distortion, spectral flatness), linear probe accuracy for emotion classification, per-neuron selectivity scores (ML-AURA AUROC), and—via the steering module—a shifted hidden state that rewrites the model's emotional perception toward a target label while preserving semantic content (measured by cosine/L2 distance between original and shifted final-layer states).

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, centered SVD on sentence-level hidden states extracts a low-dimensional emotional subspace whose leading components align with psychologically interpretable axes (valence, dominance, approach-avoidance, arousal) without explicit supervision. Second, cross-dataset universality is tested by fitting a linear map W* via least-squares between synthetic and real emotion spaces, then quantifying geometric fidelity with stress-1/2, Sammon stress, and three distortion metrics (average, ℓ2, σ) computed over cross-dataset same-emotion distance pairs at every sublayer. Third, a learned steering module—a one-layer MLP with GELU activation—operates within the top-40 SVD dimensions at layers selected by ML-AURA (where adding the emotion centroid improves 1-vs-all AUROC beyond threshold); it is trained with a combined objective of cross-entropy plus margin loss for emotion-token control and a cosine/L2 semantic-preservation regularizer, applied residually to hidden states to shift the model's internal affective state toward a target emotion while keeping semantic content intact.

## Results

Across eight emotion datasets spanning five languages, all three models (LLaMA-3.1-8B, OLMo-v2, Ministral) exhibit high centroid cosine similarity (0.83–0.94) between real and synthetic emotional spaces, with LLaMA-3.1-8B-Base showing the most coherent geometry (Stress-2 = 0.15–0.18) and the lowest regression MSE (1.81). Linear probes trained in the synthetic subspace achieve above-chance accuracy (0.39–0.47) on human-written text, confirming linearly decodable emotional structure. The learned steering module shifts top-1 emotion classification from ~10% baseline to 83–91% overall across models, with semantic similarity loss remaining low (0.04–0.26), and ML-AURA reveals that 75% of neurons per layer are emotion-selective (AUROC > 0.9), with sadness (98%) and surprise (97%) most pervasive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline methods in a traditional sense; instead, it contrasts model variants (base vs. instruction-tuned) and model families (LLaMA-3.1-8B, OLMo-v2, Ministral) on shared geometric and steering metrics. LLaMA-3.1-8B-Base achieves the lowest Stress-2 (0.15–0.18) and most consistent distortion (~0.97), while OLMo-v2-Base shows substantially higher stress (0.59–0.61) and distortion (1.35–1.46), indicating a less unified emotional space. Instruction tuning improves cosine similarity and reduces regression MSE for LLaMA (0.84→0.93 cos sim; 1.81→0.93 MSE) but increases distortion for OLMo-v2, where nearly half of layers become severely over-distorted. For steering, the learned module outperforms the closest prior approach (Hollinsworth et al., 2024, which achieves 53.5% valence shifts on a binary axis) by reaching 83–91% top-1 accuracy across a full 9-emotion categorical space while preserving semantics. LLaMA-3.1-8B is the most steerable model overall (83–84% overall accuracy), OLMo-v2 shows the largest absolute improvement (11→91% English), and Ministral is competitive (10→91% English) but weaker on non-English (11→79%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on low-dimensional structure in LLM hidden states (Aghajanyan et al., 2020; Lizzo & Heck, 2025) by applying centered SVD specifically to isolate emotion-relevant subspaces, and adapts ML-AURA (Suau et al., 2024) from toxicity detection to emotion selectivity. It departs from prior emotion-in-NLP work, which has focused on classification accuracy or output-level behavioral probes, by instead analyzing the internal geometric structure of emotional representations and introducing a learned steering module that operates within the extracted subspace. The alignment methodology draws on direct latent-space alignment (Lähner & Moeller, 2024) and the synthetic emotion corpus from the authors' prior work (Reichman et al., 2025), but the combination of geometric distortion metrics, cross-lingual universality testing, and causal intervention into a unified framework for emotional geometry is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a coherent, directionally encoded emotional manifold persists across five languages, eight stylistically diverse datasets, and multiple model architectures provides a structured account of how LLMs internalize affect during pretraining without explicit emotional supervision. The learned steering module demonstrates that this geometry is not merely interpretable but controllable, with practical implications for tone adjustment, accessibility, and evaluation normalization. Key limitations include dependence on pretraining coverage (Hindi and archaic German show higher distortion), inconsistent control over nuanced emotions like envy and excitement, and the tension between global alignment and local geometric warping revealed by stress/distortion metrics. Open questions include whether shared affective subspaces emerge in multimodal models and how emotional representations develop across pretraining checkpoints.
