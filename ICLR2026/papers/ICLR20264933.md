## Problem

While classical results establish that a model with p ≥ n parameters can memorize training labels, no theoretical result connected the feasibility of reconstructing the actual training data (inputs, not just labels) to model size. This paper identifies the missing threshold: full training data reconstruction becomes possible when p ≫ dn, where d is the input dimensionality, and provides both a proof and a practical algorithm to achieve it.

## Input / Output

The reconstruction procedure takes as input the trained last-layer parameters θ*, the fixed random features matrix V (or the full network weights for deeper architectures), and the activation function φ. It produces a reconstructed dataset X̂ ∈ ℝ^{n×d} whose rows are constrained to the sphere of radius √d, evaluated via a permutation-invariant normalized L2 distance ρ(X, X̂) solved with the Hungarian method. For ReLU activations, sign ambiguity may cause some reconstructed samples to appear as negatives of the originals, so the metric is also maximized over per-image sign flips.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that when p ≫ dn, the n-dimensional subspace spanned by training sample features in feature space uniquely identifies each individual sample in input space: any matrix X̂ whose feature rows span the same subspace must have rows close to the original training data (Theorem 1), and no duplicates can occur (Theorem 2, for n=2). The proof relies on Hermite coefficient analysis of the activation, concentration inequalities on the random features matrix, and ε-net arguments that crucially require the p ≫ dn over-parameterization. Practically, the algorithm minimizes the reconstruction loss L(X̂) = ‖P_Φ̂^⊥ θ*‖² over candidate data points via gradient descent with momentum, normalizing each row to the sphere after each update. The approach generalizes beyond random features to two-layer fully-connected networks and deep residual networks, where the loss is adapted to use the difference between final and initial last-layer parameters.

## Results

The paper establishes a law of data reconstruction: in the random features model, the entire training dataset of n samples in R^d can be recovered from model parameters when p ≫ dn, in contrast to the classical label-fitting threshold of p ≫ n. On CIFAR-10 (d=3072, n=100, frogs vs. trucks), the reconstruction error ρ(X, X̂*) drops sharply only after p crosses ≈dn ≈ 307,200, while training loss reaches zero at p = n = 100. At p = 10dn, reconstructed images are perceptually indistinguishable from the originals. The same threshold behavior is confirmed on Tiny-ImageNet (d=12288, n=20), synthetic sphere data, two-layer ReLU networks, and deep ResNets trained with gradient descent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not perform a head-to-head comparison against prior reconstruction methods (e.g., Loo et al. 2024, Haim et al. 2022, Buzaglo et al. 2023). Instead, its contribution is theoretical: it identifies p ≫ dn as the sufficient over-parameterization threshold for full dataset reconstruction, whereas prior work either assumed p → ∞ (Loo et al., 2024) or required additional information such as model gradients (Wang et al., 2023, requiring p ≫ d²). The empirical validation shows that the proposed reconstruction loss L(X̂) = ‖P_Φ̂^⊥ θ*‖², optimized via gradient descent, successfully recovers the training set across random features, two-layer fully-connected networks, and deep residual networks, with the transition consistently occurring at p^(L) ≈ dn for the last-layer width. The paper notes that ReLU activation can produce sign-flipped reconstructions (due to zero odd Hermite coefficients of order ≥ 3), while mixed-parity activations like ReLU(z)+tanh(z) resolve this ambiguity, consistent with the theoretical Assumption 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical interpolation literature (Cover 1965; Soltanolkotabi et al. 2018; Bombari et al. 2022), which established p ≥ n as the threshold for label memorization, to the harder problem of reconstructing actual training inputs. It departs from the closest prior theoretical work (Loo et al. 2024, which handles the p→∞ regime) by pinpointing the finite threshold p ≫ dn, and from empirical reconstruction studies (Haim et al. 2022; Carlini et al. 2023b) by providing a proof rather than an observation. The p ≫ dn scaling was previously identified in the adversarial robustness literature (Bubeck et al. 2021; Bombari et al. 2023) as necessary and sometimes sufficient for smooth interpolation, and this paper reveals an inherent connection between that robustness threshold and data reconstructability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation between the label-fitting threshold (p ≥ n) and the data-reconstruction threshold (p ≫ dn) has direct privacy implications: a model can perfectly memorize all training labels while being information-theoretically incapable of reconstructing the underlying data, provided p < dn. Two key open problems remain: proving that all global optima of the reconstruction loss are permutations of the training set, and showing that gradient methods can efficiently solve the non-convex optimization despite its landscape. The paper also leaves open the regime n ≪ p ≪ dn, where partial reconstruction of individual samples may be possible (as shown by Balle et al. 2022) but full dataset recovery is suspected to be information-theoretically impossible.
