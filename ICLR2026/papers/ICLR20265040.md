## Problem

Text-driven image editing with pretrained diffusion models faces a fundamental tension: existing training-free methods either preserve global structure but fail to execute the intended edit, or realize the edit at the cost of structural consistency. The root cause is the entanglement of global semantics (layout, object identity) and local signals (texture, color) in the spatial domain, which makes it difficult to simultaneously anchor what should stay stable and modify what should change. Prior attention-injection and vital-layer approaches impose overly rigid or heuristic constraints that do not generalize across diverse editing scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference RGB image and a natural-language editing instruction (e.g., 'dog wearing a blue hat') as input, and produces a single edited image as output. It operates entirely in the latent space of a pretrained diffusion or flow model (FLUX.1-dev, Stable Diffusion v1.5, or CogVideoX-1.0 for video), requiring no additional training data or fine-tuning. The only tunable hyperparameter is an energy threshold η (default 0.6) that controls how much reference structure is locked versus how much freedom the model has to generate new content.

## Method

W-Edit decomposes intermediate DiT features into multi-scale frequency bands via discrete wavelet transform (DWT), exploiting the observation that early transformer blocks encode low-frequency structural information while later blocks refine high-frequency details. During an inversion pass on the reference image, key-value attention pairs are recorded at selected blocks (determined by mid-to-high frequency energy analysis); during the editing pass, these K/V pairs are wavelet-decomposed and selectively replaced into the editing trajectory using an energy-aware adaptive fusion rule that locks high-energy (structural) sub-bands from the reference while leaving low-energy (detail) sub-bands free to follow the new text prompt. The fused coefficients are reconstructed via inverse DWT and injected into the attention computation, guiding both global composition (low-frequency) and local detail (high-frequency) simultaneously. The entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play, adding only ~10.8% inference time and 1.6% memory over the base FLUX model.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, W-Edit achieves the best FID of 65.44 (18.6% lower than Flow-Edit's 80.35), the highest CLIP score of 31.84 (4.5% above Flow-Edit), a PSNR of 24.06 (14.5% higher than the second-best Stable-Flow at 21.02), and the lowest LPIPS of 0.1028 (32.5% better than Stable-Flow). In VLM-based evaluation (Phi-3.5-vision), W-Edit scores 81.0% on Text Following and 63.0% on Minimal Modification, the best balance among all methods. In the user study, W-Edit leads on all four dimensions (Alignment 3.8, Consistency 3.9, Realism 4.2, Plausibility 3.8) while adding only 10.8% inference time and 1.6% GPU memory over the base FLUX model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

W-Edit outperforms all four baselines on the core image-quality metrics: it reduces FID by 18.6% over Flow-Edit (65.44 vs. 80.35) and by 27.2% over Stable-Flow (65.44 vs. 89.78), while improving PSNR by 14.5% over Stable-Flow (24.06 vs. 21.02) and LPIPS by 32.5% (0.1028 vs. 0.1522). Against MagicBrush, W-Edit trades a small VLM Text Following score (81.0% vs. 84.5%) for substantially better structural preservation (VLM Modify 63.0% vs. 33.5%) and far superior pixel-level fidelity (FID 65.44 vs. 206.19). Compared to Stable-Flow, W-Edit wins on every metric except VLM Text Following (81.0% vs. 77.5% — actually W-Edit wins here too), with the largest gap in LPIPS (0.1028 vs. 0.1522) and PSNR (24.06 vs. 21.02), indicating that Stable-Flow's rigid vital-layer constraints sacrifice detail fidelity. Against Flow-Edit, W-Edit leads on all four PIE-Bench metrics and both VLM scores, with the most notable advantage in FID (65.44 vs. 80.35) and LPIPS (0.1028 vs. 0.2642). In the user study, W-Edit achieves the highest scores across all four dimensions, with the largest margin over Stable-Flow in Consistency (3.9 vs. 3.7) and Plausibility (3.8 vs. 3.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

W-Edit carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: training-free diffusion editing (inversion-based methods like LEDits++/TurboEdit, attention-injection methods like Prompt-to-Prompt, and vital-layer methods like Stable-Flow) and frequency-domain analysis in deep learning (DCT-based acceleration, WaveDiff, FreeU). Rather than inheriting a single prior method, it repurposes wavelet transforms—previously used for generation quality or super-resolution—as the core mechanism for disentangling structural anchors from editable details in DiT feature spaces. A key empirical departure is the discovery of a block-wise frequency progression in DiTs (early blocks encode low-frequency structure, later blocks refine high-frequency detail), which provides a principled, frequency-energy-based alternative to Stable-Flow's DINOv2-based vital layer selection and is validated by near-perfect overlap (Jaccard 0.9, Spearman 0.925) with those layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

W-Edit offers a clean, training-free recipe for controllable editing that practitioners can plug into FLUX, Stable Diffusion, and CogVideoX with minimal overhead (~10.8% inference time), making it practically accessible. The frequency-domain framing provides a more interpretable and flexible control knob (η) than rigid layer masking, and the block-wise frequency progression finding may inform future DiT architecture design and analysis. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-image or video editing with temporal consistency, whether the Haar wavelet choice is truly optimal versus learned wavelets, and whether the inversion step (which still introduces trajectory drift) can be eliminated entirely. The method's reliance on a single hyperparameter and fixed block set also limits adaptability to highly diverse editing scenarios without re-tuning.
