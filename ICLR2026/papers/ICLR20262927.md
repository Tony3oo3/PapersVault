## Problem

Existing T2I benchmarks evaluate composition and reasoning in isolation using heuristic taxonomies, and largely restrict evaluation to low scene density (≤5 visual elements) and simple one-to-one reasoning. This leaves a critical gap: no benchmark systematically covers all relevant dimensions of both capabilities under the high compositional density and multi-step reasoning intensity that characterize real-world prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262927/cropped_page0_idx8.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample consists of a text prompt (averaging ~170 tokens, with ~20 visual elements for composition or multi-step inference chains for reasoning) fed to a T2I model to produce an image, paired with a human-verified checklist of ~12.5 atomic yes/no questions (correct answer always 'Yes'). The MLLM evaluator (Gemini 2.5 Flash) scores each question independently as 0 or 1 via binary visual question answering, yielding per-dimension scores across 12 dimensions that aggregate into composition mean, reasoning mean, and an overall score. The full benchmark comprises 1,080 prompts and 13,536 checklist questions.

## Method

T2I-COREBENCH introduces a 12-dimensional evaluation taxonomy grounded in two principled frameworks: composition follows scene graph structure (instance, attribute, relation, plus text rendering), while reasoning adopts the philosophical tripartite of deductive, inductive, and abductive inference refined into eight T2I-specific dimensions. Complexity is elevated by designing prompts with ~20 visual elements for composition and one-to-many or many-to-one inference chains for reasoning, far exceeding prior benchmarks. Data is constructed using three SOTA Large Reasoning Models (Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.5 Pro, OpenAI o3) via unified instruction templates specifying task goals, prompt design guidelines, and checklist construction rules, followed by multi-stage filtering and three-round human verification by five PhD annotators. Evaluation is conducted model-agnostically: generated images are scored question-by-question by Gemini 2.5 Flash as an MLLM evaluator, avoiding interference between questions and ensuring each judgment relies solely on visible evidence. The benchmark is used to evaluate 38 T2I models spanning diffusion, autoregressive, and unified architectures, with additional analysis of prompt rewriting effects using OpenAI o3.

## Results

On T2I-COREBENCH (1,080 prompts, ~13,500 checklist questions), GPT-Image-1.5 achieves the highest overall score of 78.2 (Composition 85.5, Reasoning 74.6), while the best open-source model FLUX.2-dev reaches 64.4 (Composition 84.7, Reasoning 54.2). Composition scores are relatively high across top models (Nano Banana Pro leads at 89.7), but reasoning lags significantly: even GPT-Image-1.5 scores only 59.0 on Reconstructive Reasoning (RR), the weakest dimension across all 38 models. The composition–reasoning gap is stark for open-source models, e.g., Qwen-Image-2512 reaches 83.7 in composition but only 51.7 in reasoning (a 32-point gap).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 38 evaluated models, closed-source models dominate: GPT-Image-1.5 (78.2 overall) leads, followed by Imagen 4 Ultra (76.1) and Nano Banana (75.9), all substantially ahead of the best open-source model FLUX.2-dev (64.4). Within open-source models, diffusion architectures (FLUX.2-dev, Qwen-Image-2512, HunyuanImage-3.0) consistently outperform autoregressive (Infinity-8B: 43.6, GoT-R1-7B: 32.7) and unified models (BAGEL: 38.2, Janus-Pro-7B: 26.7, BLIP3o-8B: 31.3), with FLUX.2-dev's composition score (84.7) approaching closed-source levels. However, reasoning remains the universal bottleneck: even GPT-Image-1.5 scores below 63 on LR, BR, and RR, and no model exceeds 91 on any single reasoning dimension. Prompt rewriting (via OpenAI o3) yields the largest gains for weaker models (FLUX.1-Krea-dev: +21.2 mean reasoning; Qwen-Image: +23.4) but only marginal improvements for already-strong models (Nano Banana: +4.6; GPT-Image: +10.9), confirming that native multimodal reasoning—not just textual inference—is the limiting factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously isolated evaluation threads in T2I research—composition benchmarks (T2I-CompBench, GenEval, DPG-Bench) and reasoning benchmarks (R2I-Bench, WISE, Commonsense-T2I)—into a single 12-dimensional taxonomy grounded in scene graph structure and the philosophical tripartite inference framework (deductive, inductive, abductive). It departs from prior work by replacing heuristic dimension definitions with principled ones, increasing scene density to ~20 visual elements per prompt, and introducing multi-step (one-to-many, many-to-one) reasoning rather than the one-to-one inference that dominates existing reasoning benchmarks. The evaluation protocol follows the VQA-based checklist paradigm from TIFA (Hu et al., 2023) and Davidsonian Scene Graph (Cho et al., 2023a), but scales it to ~13,500 atomic yes/no questions with LRM-assisted construction and multi-annotator human verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that reasoning is the central unsolved bottleneck in T2I generation: even the strongest closed-source model (Nano Banana Pro) scores ~16 points below its own composition score, and open-source models show gaps exceeding 30 points. The finding that prompt rewriting yields 20+ point gains for weaker models but marginal or negative effects for stronger ones suggests native multimodal reasoning—not textual post-processing—is the key direction for future T2I architectures. Open questions include whether unified autoregressive models can close the reasoning gap through architectural design, and whether interleaved multimodal reasoning (beyond text-only rewriting) can address the tight text-image coupling that causes failures like the square-wheels example.
