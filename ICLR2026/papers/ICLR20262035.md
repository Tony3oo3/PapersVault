## Problem

General-purpose protein language models are optimized for average performance across large datasets, yet experimentalists need accurate predictions for individual proteins that are often poorly represented in training data. This paper addresses the gap between dataset-wide optimization and the precision required for single, out-of-distribution protein targets, where existing models like AlphaFold2 and ESMFold frequently fail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262035/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single protein sequence (or optionally a multiple sequence alignment) as input and produces a set of customized backbone parameters θ_x that replace the pre-trained θ_0. These adapted parameters are then paired with frozen, task-specific downstream heads (structure predictor, fitness scorer, or function classifier) to generate 3D coordinates, Spearman-correlated fitness scores, or function labels. No additional data, labels, or pre-computation are required; the entire customization runs on a single GPU in a few dozen gradient steps.

## Method

ProteinTTT performs self-supervised test-time customization of the backbone f in a Y-shaped protein model by minimizing the masked language modeling loss on the single target sequence for T steps, while keeping the downstream head h frozen. The key mechanism is that reducing the model's perplexity on the target sequence (via the self-supervised track g∘f) improves the internal representations that the frozen task head h consumes, yielding better structure, fitness, or function predictions without any task-specific fine-tuning. For large models (e.g., 3B-parameter ESM2), LoRA is applied to self-attention matrices and SGD with gradient accumulation is used for stable, predictable optimization. The optimal checkpoint among {θ_0, …, θ_T} is selected via a confidence function (e.g., pLDDT), and the parameters are reset to θ_0 before the next target. The approach is paradigm-agnostic, working identically for masked, autoregressive, and discrete-diffusion pre-trained models, and can be extended to MSA-based customization when homologs are available.

## Results

On 18 challenging CAMEO targets, ESMFold+ProteinTTT achieves TM-score 0.5047 and LDDT 0.5478, outperforming ESMFold (0.4649/0.5194) and the masked-prediction baseline ESMFold+MP (0.4862/0.5375). On ProteinGym fitness prediction, ProSST+ProteinTTT sets a new state of the art with average Spearman 0.5087 (vs. 0.5068 without). In case studies, ProteinTTT improves LDDT for 66% of antibody CDR substructures and 19% of entries in the Big Fantastic Virus Database, delivering high-confidence predictions where AlphaFold2 and ESMFold fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProteinTTT consistently outperforms all baselines across every model tested. For structure prediction, ESMFold+ProteinTTT (TM 0.5047) beats both ESMFold (0.4649) and the masked-prediction baseline ESMFold+MP (0.4862); ESM3+ProteinTTT (0.3954) surpasses ESM3+CoT (0.3677); and HelixFold-Single+ProteinTTT (0.4839) improves over HelixFold-Single (0.4709). For fitness prediction, ProSST+ProteinTTT (Spearman 0.5087) sets a new SOTA over ProSST (0.5068), and ESM2-35M+ProteinTTT (0.3407) improves over ESM2-35M (0.3211) and ProGen2-small (0.3255). MSA Transformer+ProteinTTT (0.4326) marginally exceeds MSA Transformer (0.4319), while ESM2+ProteinTTT_MSA (0.4299) approaches MSA Transformer's performance. Notably, ProteinTTT also improves DPLM2 Bit-based (0.3701→0.3796) despite the model being pretrained via discrete diffusion rather than standard MLM, demonstrating broad applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the test-time training (TTT) paradigm from computer vision (Gandelsman et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2020) and NLP (Hardt & Sun, 2023) into protein machine learning, where prior customization approaches required collecting protein-specific or family-specific datasets. It leverages the Y-shaped architecture (backbone + self-supervised MLM head + task-specific head) that is standard in protein PLMs, and uses LoRA to make single-GPU customization of billion-parameter models feasible. The key conceptual departure from prior protein fine-tuning work is that no additional data beyond the single target sequence is needed: the pre-training objective itself serves as the customization signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProteinTTT addresses a practical gap: experimentalists need accurate predictions for individual proteins, often poorly represented in training data, rather than average performance across benchmarks. The perplexity-minimization justification (Appendix A) provides a clean theoretical link between the self-supervised objective and downstream quality, and the method's simplicity (a few lines of code, no architectural changes) makes it broadly adoptable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to tasks without a natural self-supervised objective, whether gains compound with longer customization or more aggressive LoRA configurations, and whether the modest average improvements (concentrated on out-of-distribution targets) translate to broader biological impact beyond the two case studies.
