## Problem

MoE models store all expert parameters in GPU memory even though only a fraction are activated per token, making deployment prohibitively expensive. Existing expert-level pruning (dropping or merging entire experts) is too coarse, causing significant accuracy degradation, while parameter-level sparsification fails to deliver hardware acceleration. The paper identifies a missing intermediate granularity: a pruning unit finer than an expert but still structurally aligned with hardware, enabling both real FLOPs reduction and minimal performance loss.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained MoE model, a small calibration set (128 sequences of 2048 tokens, e.g., from WikiText-2 or C4), and a target pruning ratio r, and produces a pruned model where the lowest-importance atomic experts are removed across all MoE layers. No additional tunable hyperparameters are introduced, and the entire importance computation requires only two forward passes and one backward pass on the calibration set. The pruned model has reduced intermediate dimensions per expert (fewer columns in W_up/W_gate and rows in W_down), yielding direct FLOPs savings without requiring retraining or fine-tuning.

## Method

HEAPr decomposes each expert into d_inter indivisible 'atomic experts,' each defined by one column of W_up, one column of W_gate, and one row of W_down, so that the expert output is a sum of atomic expert outputs. The key theoretical insight is that cross-Hessians between different atomic experts vanish (their parameters are decoupled), reducing the Hessian from O((3d_model·d_inter)²) to O((3d_model)²·d_inter). A second optimization shifts the pruning constraint from parameter space to output space: by Taylor-expanding the atomic expert function and substituting the Fisher information matrix for the expected Hessian, the importance of atomic expert e_P reduces to s = E_x[½ e_P(x)ᵀ E[g_P g_Pᵀ] e_P(x)], where g_P is the loss gradient w.r.t. the expert output. Because all atomic experts within the same expert share identical output gradients, only one d_model×d_model covariance matrix per expert is stored, bringing space complexity to O(d_model²). Finally, all atomic experts across all layers are globally ranked by s and the bottom r% are pruned, yielding a hardware-friendly reduction in expert width.

## Results

HEAPr achieves near-lossless compression at 20% pruning on DeepSeekMoE-16B-Base (avg. accuracy 0.56, identical to the unpruned model) and at 25% on Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat (0.53 vs. 0.54 original). On Qwen3-30B-A3B, the average accuracy drops only from 0.62 to 0.59 at 25% pruning, while FLOPs are reduced by approximately 20%. At a more aggressive 40% ratio on DeepSeekMoE-16B-Base, HEAPr retains 0.53 average accuracy, substantially outperforming all baselines (best competitor D²-MoE at 0.49).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEAPr outperforms all eight baselines across every model and pruning ratio tested. Against expert-dropping methods, HEAPr beats NAEE by 0.03 avg. accuracy at 20% and by 0.07 at 40% on DeepSeekMoE-16B-Base, and surpasses MoE-I² by 0.06 and 0.08 respectively. Against decomposition-based methods, HEAPr edges out MoE-SVD (0.56 vs. 0.54) and D²-MoE (0.56 vs. 0.54) at 20%, and leads D²-MoE by 0.04 at 40%. Against expert-merging methods on Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat, HEAPr achieves 0.53 at 25% versus HC-SMoE's 0.50, and 0.48 at 50% versus HC-SMoE's 0.45. On the latest Qwen3-30B-A3B, HEAPr's 0.59 at 25% dramatically exceeds HC-SMoE's 0.46. Against the concurrent atomic-expert method CAMERA-P, HEAPr gains 1.2 points in average accuracy at 20% and 0.9 points at 40% on DeepSeekMoE-16B-Base, with the gap narrowing at higher ratios as redundancy diminishes.

## Relation to Prior Work

HEAPr extends the Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) framework from Hassibi et al. and LeCun et al. to the MoE setting, leveraging the Fisher information matrix approximation popularized by WoodFisher (Singh & Alistarh, 2020) to make second-order pruning tractable. Its key departure from prior MoE compression work (expert dropping, merging, decomposition) is the introduction of 'atomic experts' as the pruning unit, exploiting the zero cross-Hessian property between atomic experts to reduce Hessian complexity from O(d⁴) to O(d²). The method also shifts the pruning constraint from parameter space to output space, a reformulation that enables global cross-layer ranking rather than the layer-wise or heuristic approaches used by baselines like NAEE, D²-MoE, and CAMERA-P.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE models (DeepSeek-V3, Qwen series) become the dominant architecture for large-scale LLMs, the memory bottleneck of storing all experts makes compression critical for practical deployment; HEAPr's near-lossless 20–25% compression with only two forward and one backward pass offers a highly deployable solution. The global ranking insight—that atomic expert importance is comparable across layers—opens the door to more principled, model-wide compression strategies. Open questions include whether parameter compensation (e.g., low-rank recovery) after atomic expert removal can push the lossless boundary further, and whether the approach generalizes to non-gated FFN variants or non-MoE architectures.
