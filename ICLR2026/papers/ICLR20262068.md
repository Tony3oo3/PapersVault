## Problem

Existing controllable video generation methods either require fine-tuning large-scale VDMs for each specific control task—increasingly impractical as model sizes grow—or are training-free but restricted to narrow task-specific inputs. No prior framework is simultaneously training-free, model-agnostic, and general-purpose across diverse frame-level control signals such as keyframes, style images, sketches, and depth maps.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frame-level conditions (keyframes, style reference images, depth maps, sketches, color blocks) applied to a user-selected subset of frame indices, plus a text prompt, and produces a temporally coherent video where guided frames match the conditions while unguided frames maintain natural continuity. Unlike frame interpolation baselines that enforce exact pixel matching at fixed positions (first/last), Frame Guidance supports arbitrary keyframe placements and only requires visual similarity. The framework operates unmodified on any VDM backbone (CogVideoX, Wan-14B, SVD, LTX-2B), with runtime bounded to at most 4× the base model's inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free guidance framework built on two components. First, latent slicing exploits the empirically observed temporal locality of CausalVAE—where each latent influences only a few neighboring frames—to decode just a 3-latent temporal window per guided frame rather than the full sequence, reducing peak memory by up to 60× when combined with 2× spatial downsampling. Second, Video Latent Optimization (VLO) applies a stage-aware hybrid update: deterministic gradient descent in early denoising steps to lock in global layout coherence, followed by stochastic time-travel renoising in later steps to refine details and correct accumulated errors. Critically, gradients propagate through the full denoising network rather than using a shortcut, which is essential because the network encodes the temporal priors that spread guidance from a few frames to the entire video. The framework supports both diffusion and flow-matching backbones, with task-specific losses (L2 for keyframes, CSD cosine for style, first-last L2 for looping, encoder-aligned L2 for depth/sketch) that can be freely combined for multi-condition guidance.

## Results

On keyframe-guided video generation, Frame Guidance applied to CogX-Interp achieves the best FID of 37.95 and FVD of 420.3 on DAVIS, and FID 47.86 / FVD 723.3 on Pexels, outperforming all training-free and training-based baselines. As a pure training-free method on the base CogX-I2V model, it reaches FID 55.60 / FVD 577.1 (DAVIS) and FID 68.97 / FVD 989.3 (Pexels) with three keyframes (I,M,F), substantially beating the base I2V (FID 60.36 / FVD 890.1). On stylized video generation, Frame Guidance achieves the best text-alignment scores (CLIP-T 0.224, ViCLIP-T 0.285) and competitive style-alignment (ViCLIP-S 0.185), surpassing both VideoComposer and StyleCrafter on most metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base I2V models (CogX-I2V, Wan-14B-I2V), Frame Guidance consistently improves FID and FVD by 3–10 points, confirming that frame-level guidance adds meaningful control without any fine-tuning. Compared to the training-free TRF method, Frame Guidance (CogX) reduces DAVIS FVD from 923.1 to 577.1 (−37%) and Pexels FVD from 1106.2 to 989.3, while also improving FID on both datasets. Against the fine-tuned SVD-Interp, Frame Guidance (CogX) wins on all four metrics (e.g., DAVIS FID 55.60 vs. 63.89), and when applied on top of CogX-Interp it further reduces DAVIS FID to 37.95 (vs. 46.59) and FVD to 420.3 (vs. 506.0). On stylized video generation, Frame Guidance outperforms VideoComposer on text alignment (CLIP-T 0.224 vs. 0.211; ViCLIP-T 0.285 vs. 0.137) but trails on raw style alignment (CLIP-S 0.624 vs. 0.869), which the authors attribute to VideoComposer over-copying the style image at the expense of text adherence. Versus StyleCrafter, Frame Guidance wins on text alignment (ViCLIP-T 0.285 vs. 0.273) and ViCLIP-S (0.185 vs. 0.157) while slightly trailing on CLIP-S (0.624 vs. 0.635), and human evaluation confirms Frame Guidance is preferred across style alignment, text alignment, and motion dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free guidance paradigm established for image diffusion (FreeDom, Universal Guidance) to video diffusion models, addressing the unique challenges of temporal coherence and computational cost in the video setting. It departs from prior training-free video methods (CamTrol, MotionClone, Tune-A-Video) that are task-specific, and from training-based general-purpose methods (MagicMotion, VACE) that require fine-tuning, by proposing a unified framework that is simultaneously training-free, model-agnostic, and general across frame-level control tasks. The two core technical contributions—latent slicing exploiting temporal locality in CausalVAE, and the stage-aware Video Latent Optimization (VLO) strategy—are novel adaptations specifically designed for the video domain, building on the time-travel trick from image guidance but replacing it with a hybrid deterministic/stochastic schedule motivated by the observation that video layout is established in early denoising steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Frame Guidance offers a practical, drop-in solution for controllable video generation that avoids the increasingly prohibitive cost of fine-tuning large VDMs for each new control task, and its model-agnostic design means it can be applied to any future video model without retraining. The temporal locality property of CausalVAE, while not a fundamental discovery, enables a 60× memory reduction that makes gradient-based guidance feasible on a single GPU for 14B-parameter models. Key open questions include the 2–4× inference slowdown (which limits real-time or interactive use), the dependence on the base model's distribution (struggling with OOD styles and fine-grained structural control), and whether the deterministic-early/stochastic-late schedule of VLO generalizes to even longer or higher-resolution video generation.
