## Problem

Computing exact Wasserstein distances for many pairs of distributions is prohibitively expensive (O(n³ log n) per pair), and existing accelerations either sacrifice accuracy (Sinkhorn) or require large training sets and neural network infrastructure (Wasserstein Wormhole). No lightweight, data-efficient method existed that could approximate Wasserstein distances for arbitrary distribution pairs without learned embeddings or deep architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264672/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of empirical measures (point clouds of up to 2,048 points in 2D–2,500D, or high-dimensional gene-expression vectors) and outputs a scalar Wasserstein distance estimate. Calibration requires exact Wasserstein distances on a small subset of M₀ samples (as few as 10, yielding ~45 pairs); at inference, only the much cheaper sliced Wasserstein distances (computed in O(n log n) per projection direction) are needed, and the final estimate is a simple linear combination of K such distances. The framework handles both continuous and discrete distributions and supports both intra-class and inter-class pair settings.

## Method

The paper formulates Wasserstein distance estimation as a linear regression problem where the response is W_p(μ,ν) and the predictors are K sliced Wasserstein distances that provide known lower bounds (SW, Max-SW, EBSW) and upper bounds (Projected Wasserstein, Min-SWGG, Expected Sliced Transport). Two model variants are proposed: an unconstrained linear model with a closed-form least-squares solution, and a constrained model that parameterizes each predictor pair as ω_k·SL + (1−ω_k)·SU with 0≤ω_k≤1, halving the parameter count and embedding the bound structure as inductive bias. The key insight is that under a meta-distribution of random distribution pairs, the Wasserstein distance is approximately a linear function of its sliced variants, and the regression coefficients can be learned from very few calibration pairs. RG-Wormhole further accelerates the Wormhole transformer by replacing every Wasserstein distance call in both encoder and decoder losses with the calibrated RG surrogate, preserving embedding quality while drastically reducing training time.

## Results

On ShapeNetV2 point-cloud classification (k=5), RG-seo achieves 83.5% accuracy with R²=0.937, nearly matching exact Wasserstein (84.2%), while individual sliced metrics cap at 72.5% (SWD/EBSW) or below 60% (Max-SW). Across four datasets (MNIST, ShapeNetV2, MERFISH, scRNA-seq), RG variants consistently outperform Wasserstein Wormhole at all training sizes (N=10–200), with RG-se reaching R²=0.948 on ShapeNetV2. In inference runtime, RG-s computes 19,900 pairwise distances in 21.4s versus 5,031s for exact Wasserstein and 92.1s for Linear OT, while achieving higher R² (0.942 vs. 0.918 on ShapeNetV2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against exact Wasserstein, RG-seo closes nearly the entire gap on ShapeNetV2 (83.5% vs. 84.2% at k=5, R²=0.937), while all individual sliced metrics fall 10–48 points short. Compared to Wasserstein Wormhole (the state-of-the-art learned embedding), RG variants consistently achieve higher R², lower MSE, and lower MAE at every training size (N=10, 50, 100, 200) across all four datasets, with the advantage most pronounced in the low-data regime (N=10). Against classical OT accelerations, RG-s outperforms Linear OT on approximation quality (R²=0.942 vs. 0.918 on ShapeNetV2; 0.989 vs. 0.984 on scRNA-seq) while being roughly 4× faster at 19,900 pairs (21.4s vs. 92.1s). Sinkhorn performs worst, yielding negative R² on MNIST (−1.970) and ShapeNetV2 (−1.549) even at 1,000 iterations, and is slower than both Linear OT and RG. The RG-Wormhole hybrid matches Wormhole's embedding quality (identical R²/MSE/MAE for encoder, near-identical reconstruction loss) while reducing training time by a large factor, with the gap widening as batch size increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the rich family of sliced Wasserstein (SW) distances and their lifted variants, which provide provable lower and upper bounds on the true Wasserstein distance. It departs from the dominant paradigm of learning-based Wasserstein approximation (e.g., Deep Wasserstein Embedding, Wasserstein Wormhole) by replacing neural-network embeddings with a parsimonious linear regression where SW distances serve as predictors. The key conceptual move is treating the Wasserstein-to-SW relationship as a regression problem under a meta-distribution of distribution pairs, yielding closed-form or quadratic-programming estimators that require no gradient-based training. The RG-Wormhole variant then repurposes this linear surrogate to accelerate the transformer-based Wormhole architecture by substituting every expensive Wasserstein call during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the method reduces Wasserstein estimation from O(n³ log n) to the cost of computing SW distances (O(n log n)) plus a linear combination, and achieves R² ≈ 0.93–0.99 with as few as 10 calibration pairs, outperforming the transformer-based Wormhole in low-data regimes across four datasets spanning 2D to 2,500D. The RG-Wormhole hybrid offers a drop-in acceleration for existing Wormhole pipelines, cutting training time substantially while preserving embedding quality. Open questions include whether non-linear or Bayesian regression could further tighten the approximation, whether the approach extends naturally to non-Euclidean ground metrics (spherical, hyperbolic), and how the method behaves when the meta-distribution shifts between training and deployment.
