## Problem

Existing methods for recovering clean data distributions from corrupted observations are restricted to specific corruption models (e.g., additive Gaussian noise or pixel masking) and lack a unified framework that handles arbitrary corruption processes with theoretical guarantees. No prior approach simultaneously accommodates general black-box corruption operators, provides convergence guarantees, and leverages the stability advantages of diffusion/flow-matching models without adversarial training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263928/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a large set of corrupted images (any size, e.g., 32×32 CIFAR-10 or 64×64 CelebA) produced by a known corruption process available only as a black-box generator, plus a small set of clean images (as few as 50) that serve as a prior distribution. The output is a trained bridge model u_θ(x|y) that, given a corrupted sample y, produces a clean sample x by integrating a learned ODE/SDE backward from y. No differentiability of the corruption process is required, and the model is evaluated by generating 50,000 samples and computing FID against the reference clean dataset.

## Method

The key insight is that the KLAP objective (minimizing KL(q ‖ T_r p)) admits, via the Donsker-Varadhan variational principle, an equivalent one-sided entropic optimal transport formulation, which yields a closed-form alternating minimization: at each iteration, compute the posterior u_y^k(x) ∝ p^k(x)·r(y|x) and update p^{k+1} as a weighted mixture of the reconstructed distribution and the clean prior. The posterior is learned with a flow-matching bridge model trained via conditional drift matching (CDM) loss, generalizing SFBD's score-matching to arbitrary corruption kernels. An online variant refreshes only a fraction γ of the reconstructed sample set per iteration, enabling end-to-end training without optimizer resets while preserving convergence to the optimal p_λ*. The framework provably recovers the true distribution when the corruption operator is injective, and with a small clean prior, selects the correct element from the solution set when identifiability fails.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, SFBD-OMNI achieves FID 10.81 under additive Gaussian noise (σ=0.2), substantially outperforming SFBD (13.53) and EMDiffusion (86.47). On non-identifiable corruption processes, the advantage is dramatic: on CelebA Gaussian blur, Online SFBD-OMNI reaches FID 10.28 versus 91.89 for EMDiffusion and 191.96 for SURE-Score; on CelebA grayscale, it achieves 11.21 versus 59.04 for EMDiffusion. On the identifiable pixel-masking task, SFBD-OMNI (21.31) is on par with EMDiffusion (21.08), while both far exceed AmbientDiff (28.88). All methods except Noise2Self are pretrained on only 50 clean images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFBD-OMNI and its online variant dominate on all non-identifiable corruption processes (grayscale, Gaussian blur), where EMDiffusion and SURE-Score degrade by 5–20× in FID because they lack a mechanism to incorporate clean-sample priors. On the identifiable additive-Gaussian task, SFBD-OMNI (FID 10.81) improves over the original SFBD (13.53) by ~20% and over EMDiffusion (86.47) by ~8×, confirming that the bridge-model formulation generalizes the Gaussian-specific SFBD without sacrificing performance. On pixel masking, EMDiffusion (21.08) edges out SFBD-OMNI (21.31) by a negligible margin, while both substantially outperform AmbientDiff (28.88) and SURE-Score (220.01). Against the very recent Ambient-o (Daras et al., 2025b), Online SFBD-OMNI achieves FID 0.97 vs. 5.34 at blur σ=0.6 and 3.07 vs. 6.16 at σ=1.0, demonstrating that explicitly modelling the corruption kernel preserves more signal than the noising-based alignment strategy. Noise2Self, a self-supervised denoiser, is only competitive on additive Gaussian (92.06) and does not address the other corruption types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes SFBD (Lu et al., 2025), which was restricted to additive Gaussian corruption, to arbitrary black-box corruption processes by reformulating the KLAP objective via the Donsker-Varadhan variational principle as a one-sided entropic optimal transport problem. Rather than the adversarial min-max formulation inherited from AmbientGAN (Bora et al., 2018), the resulting alternating minimization is solved with bridge models (flow matching), avoiding GAN training instability. The online variant with partial sample-set refresh (ratio γ) is a practical departure from the full-replacement schedule in both SFBD and EMDiffusion (Bai et al., 2024), whose update rule coincides with SFBD-OMNI only when λ=0.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first unified treatment of distribution recovery under arbitrary corruption with convergence guarantees, and shows that even 50 clean samples can bridge the gap from FID ~80 to ~11 in identifiable settings where pure deconvolution is sample-inefficient. Open questions include whether the black-box corruption assumption is too restrictive for real-world pipelines where the forward model is only approximately known, and whether the 4–8 day training cost (on 1–4 L40s) limits adoption outside academic settings. The remaining artifacts on high-resolution satellite and MRI data suggest that domain-aware priors or problem-specific inductive biases may still be necessary for deployment-quality reconstructions.
