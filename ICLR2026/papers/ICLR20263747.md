## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning of LLMs on multiple reasoning tasks (math, code, logic, commonsense) introduces unpredictable conflicts and partial benefits that neither mix-up nor continual SFT can consistently resolve. Prior work on task interference focused on general abilities and assumed all cross-task interactions were harmful, without identifying which specific parameters drive each reasoning capability or distinguishing beneficial from detrimental overlaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263747/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a base LLM, per-task reasoning training data (20k samples each), and a tiny calibration set (5 groups × 50 samples) used for forward-only DSR computation. It outputs task-specific DSR parameter sets (top-100 rows per linear layer) and a differentially fine-tuned LLM. Notably, DSR identification requires only a 1k-sample probe SFT plus ~900 seconds of inference on a single A100, and the resulting mix-up fine-tuning updates only ~3.7% of total parameters, making the pipeline substantially lighter than full SFT or task-vector methods.

## Method

The core mechanism is Delta-Scale Row (DSR) analysis: for each reasoning task, the method computes per-row squared L2 norms of activation differences between the base and fine-tuned model across sampled inputs, yielding a row-granular importance score proportional to integrated gradient energy (a Fisher-like estimator at channel level). For mix-up SFT, DiFT computes the union of DSR sets across all involved tasks, freezes all non-union parameters, and fine-tunes only the union on mixed data. For continual SFT, DiFT computes the difference set (current task DSR minus all previously learned tasks' DSR), freezes everything else, and trains only the difference set on the new task. This selective update strategy preserves task-specific parameter subsets while preventing non-critical parameters from introducing cross-task interference, and is orthogonal to data-driven forgetting mitigation methods like SSR.

## Results

On Llama3-8B, DiFT achieves the best ATA (Average Target Accuracy) across all mix-up and continual SFT settings: 60.35 on Mix-Math-Code (vs. 59.80 vanilla), 45.10 on Mix-Code-Logic (vs. 43.23 vanilla), 55.67 on Mix-Logic-CSQA (vs. 54.92 vanilla), 49.55 on Continual-Math-Code (vs. 46.93 vanilla), and 21.30 on Continual-Math-Logic (vs. 21.15 vanilla). On Mistral-7B, DiFT similarly leads: 54.80 on Mix-Math-Code (vs. 52.87 vanilla), 47.64 on Mix-Code-Logic (vs. 46.44 vanilla), 53.07 on Mix-Logic-CSQA (vs. 52.90 vanilla), 65.81 on Continual-Math-Code (vs. 64.58 vanilla), and 20.06 on Continual-Math-Logic (vs. 16.94 vanilla). The largest gains appear in continual settings where catastrophic forgetting is most severe, e.g., +3.12 ATA on Continual-Math-Logic for Mistral-7B. DiFT also extends to Qwen2.5-14B, where Continual-Math-Code ATA improves from 64.02 to 68.15.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiFT consistently outperforms all four baselines on the ATA metric across both Llama3-8B and Mistral-7B. In mix-up SFT, DiFT beats DMT (Dong et al., 2023) by +0.69 to +2.73 ATA and CoBa (Gong et al., 2024) by +0.44 to +3.51 ATA across the three task pairs; the largest margin is on Mistral-7B Mix-Math-Code where DiFT reaches 54.80 vs. CoBa's 51.29. In continual SFT, DiFT surpasses HFT (Hui et al., 2024b) by +1.27 ATA on Continual-Math-Code (Llama3-8B) and +8.38 ATA on Mistral-7B, and beats LoTA (Panda et al., 2024) by +1.76 ATA on Llama3-8B and +8.40 ATA on Mistral-7B for the same setting. The one near-tie is Continual-Math-Logic on Llama3-8B, where DiFT (21.30) is marginally below LoTA (21.38) and HFT (21.31), though the paper attributes this to a format artifact (the model outputs LogiCoT-style 'yes/no' endings) rather than genuine ability loss. Overall, DiFT wins or ties on ATA in 9 out of 10 model-setting combinations, with the sole exception being a 0.08-point gap on Llama3-8B Continual-Math-Logic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the task-vector and parameter-importance literature (Ilharco et al. 2022; Rudman et al. 2023; Zhang et al. 2023) that identified task-specific parameter subsets, but departs by connecting specific parameter rows to specific *reasoning* capabilities via a forward-only activation-difference metric (DSR) rather than gradient or task-arithmetic operations. It also extends continual-learning mitigation methods (HFT, LoTA, EWC) that treated all cross-task interactions as uniformly harmful, by explicitly modeling the coexistence of mutual benefits and conflicts among reasoning tasks. The differential fine-tuning strategy (freezing non-critical parameters) is conceptually related to sparse adaptation and lottery-ticket ideas, but is grounded in a task-specific sensitivity analysis rather than random or gradient-based selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that reasoning tasks exhibit both synergistic (math–code) and conflicting (logic–commonsense) interactions, and that these can be localized to specific parameter rows, provides a practical, lightweight mechanism for multi-task SFT that practitioners can adopt with minimal overhead (~3.7% trainable parameters in mix-up). However, the approach is limited to dense 7–14B models, cannot address catastrophic forgetting in continual learning, and lacks theoretical guarantees for why LLMs partition abilities into discrete parameter subsets. Open questions include whether DSR analysis generalizes to MoE architectures (where router-induced activation instability breaks the assumption), whether it scales to 30B+ models, and how it interacts with RLHF or long-CoT training paradigms.
