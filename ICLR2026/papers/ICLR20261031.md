## Problem

Existing preference optimization for LLMs (RLHF, RLVR, RLAIF) all depend on external supervision—human annotations, verifiable ground-truth answers, or teacher models—making them costly and inapplicable to open-ended tasks like multilingual translation. Traditional dual learning offers annotation-free self-supervision but is restricted to strictly invertible task pairs (e.g., translation/back-translation), failing on non-invertible tasks like mathematical reasoning where the output cannot uniquely reconstruct the input. DuPO addresses both gaps by generalizing duality to non-invertible tasks while eliminating all external label dependencies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a primal task input decomposed into a known component x_k and an unknown component x_u (e.g., a math problem with one numerical parameter masked as a variable, or a source sentence for translation). It produces a scalar self-supervised reward proportional to exp(-λ·d(x_u, T_cd(y, x_k))), where d is a task-specific distance (binary equality for math, BLEU for translation). At inference time, the same mechanism acts as a reranker: given K candidate reasoning trajectories, it scores each by backward accuracy on the auto-constructed dual question and selects the highest-scoring trajectory without any fine-tuning.

## Method

DuPO introduces a generalized duality framework that decomposes the primal input x into disjoint known (x_k) and unknown (x_u) subspaces, then defines a complementary dual task T_cd that reconstructs only x_u from the primal output y and the known x_k, rather than inverting the full input. This relaxation resolves two asymmetries: it restores sufficient information flow for non-invertible tasks (e.g., a math answer plus one known parameter can recover the hidden variable) and reduces dual-task difficulty by anchoring reconstruction with x_k. For math, dual questions are auto-generated by replacing numerical parameters with variables and prompting the model to recover them given a candidate answer; candidates are filtered by answerability and uniqueness principles. The resulting reconstruction quality serves as a self-supervised reward optimized via GRPO, with a single LLM instantiating both primal and dual tasks. The framework is model-agnostic and task-agnostic, demonstrated across 1.5B–7B models on math reasoning and a 7B model on 756 translation directions.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning, DuPO yields consistent gains across all tested models: +6.3 points (1.5B), +5.4 points (7B distilled), +6.5 points (Qwen3-4B), and +7.3 points (OpenReasoning-Nemotron-7B) in average accuracy over four benchmarks (AMC23, AIME24, AIME25, HMMT). The strongest result lifts OpenReasoning-Nemotron-7B from 77.1% to 84.4%, surpassing Doubao-1.6-Thinking (82.7%). On multilingual translation across 756 directions, DuPO boosts Seed-X-7B-Instruct by +2.1 COMET (72.6→74.6), matching DeepSeek-R1-0528 (75.0) and exceeding Doubao-1.5-Thinking (71.7). As a training-free inference-time reranker, DuPO adds +9.3 points to Qwen3-4B (68.4%→77.7% on AIME), surpassing DeepSeek-R1-0120 (74.9%) and Claude-Sonnet4-Thinking (76.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuPO consistently outperforms its own base models across all four tested backbones, with gains ranging from +5.4 to +7.3 points in average accuracy. Against ultra-large closed-source systems, DuPO-enhanced Qwen3-4B (74.4%) surpasses DeepSeek-R1-0120 (72.9%) and is within 0.2 points of Claude-Sonnet4-Thinking (74.6%), while DuPO-enhanced OpenReasoning-Nemotron-7B (84.4%) exceeds both Doubao-1.5-Thinking (79.2%) and Doubao-1.6-Thinking (82.7%). The only baseline not surpassed is DeepSeek-R1-0528 (87.4%), which remains the strongest overall. In the LLaMA-family ablation (Table 3), DuPO on LLaMA-3.1-8B (32.1% avg) outperforms SimpleRL-Zoo (19.0% avg) by 13.1 points despite SimpleRL-Zoo using oracle-labeled answers as reward. For translation, DuPO-enhanced Seed-X-7B-Instruct (74.6 COMET) matches DeepSeek-R1-0528 (75.0) and exceeds Doubao-1.5-Thinking (71.7) and Qwen3-235B-22B (73.9) across 756 language directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual learning paradigm (He et al., 2016), originally designed for strictly invertible task pairs like translation/back-translation, into a 'generalized duality' framework that decomposes inputs into known and unknown components and only requires reconstructing the unknown part. It departs from RLVR, which depends on externally verifiable ground-truth answers, and from RLHF/RLAIF, which rely on human or teacher-model annotations, by sourcing the reward signal entirely from the task's own complementary structure. The optimization is instantiated via GRPO, placing DuPO within the broader preference-optimization lineage while replacing the reward function with a self-supervised, annotation-free alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DuPO offers a practical path to annotation-free LLM optimization that scales across tasks (math reasoning, multilingual translation) and model scales (1.5B–7B), and its inference-time reranking mode enables smaller models to surpass much larger ones without any fine-tuning. The close tracking of oracle baselines (Fig. 4) suggests the self-verification signal is nearly as informative as ground-truth supervision, which would be significant if it generalizes beyond the two validated domains. Key open questions include applicability to truly open-ended tasks (creative writing, dialogue) where extracting a meaningful 'unknown component' is ill-defined, and whether the dual-question construction strategy transfers cleanly to domains without clear numerical or structural decompositions.
