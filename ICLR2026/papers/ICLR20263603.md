## Problem

The standard pullback geometry approach for studying latent-space structure in diffusion models is fundamentally flawed: it provably forces all geodesics to decode as straight-line segments in data space, ignoring any intrinsic data manifold curvature. Additionally, the natural stochastic alternative (Fisher-Rao metric on denoising distributions) collapses to zero when the latent is identified with the terminal noise x_T, because diffusion is memoryless. The paper asks: what geometric structure on the latent space of diffusion models actually captures meaningful data geometry?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of data points (e.g., 512×512 ImageNet images encoded via StabilityVAE, or 2D molecular dihedral-angle configurations), a trained denoiser network ŷ₀(x_t, t), and optional penalty functions for constrained sampling. It produces a spacetime geodesic curve γ: [0,1] → ℝ^(D+1) parameterized by a cubic spline, a scalar Diffusion Edit Distance (the geodesic length), or a sequence of transition paths generated via annealed Langevin dynamics along the geodesic. A key practical detail is that energy evaluation is simulation-free: it requires only N Jacobian-vector products of the denoiser (via Hutchinson's trick) rather than running the reverse SDE, making geodesic optimization feasible even for large image models.

## Method

The paper proves that the pullback metric induced by the PF-ODE decoder is degenerate in diffusion (latent and data spaces share dimension D, so geodesics always decode to straight lines), then proposes a Fisher-Rao information geometry on the (D+1)-dimensional latent spacetime z = (x_t, t) that indexes the family of denoising distributions {p(x_0|x_t)} across all noise levels. The central technical result is that these denoising distributions form an exponential family with natural parameter η(x_t,t) = (α_t/σ_t² · x_t, −α_t²/2σ_t²) and sufficient statistic T(x_0) = (x_0, ‖x_0‖²), which yields a closed-form energy expression E(γ) ≈ (N−1)/2 Σ (η_{n+1}−η_n)ᵀ(μ_{n+1}−μ_n) that avoids intractable KL divergences. The expectation parameter μ is estimated via Tweedie's formula applied to the learned denoiser, with the divergence term computed through a single Hutchinson JVP per curve point. Geodesics are then found by gradient-descent optimization of a cubic spline parameterization of the spacetime curve, and for molecular systems the geodesic defines a sequence of Boltzmann distributions p(x|γ_s) that are sampled via annealed Langevin dynamics to produce transition paths.

## Results

On the Alanine Dipeptide transition path sampling benchmark, the spacetime geodesic method achieves a MaxEnergy of 37.36±0.60, nearly matching the theoretical lower bound of 36.42 and substantially outperforming all baselines. It requires only 16M energy evaluations (plus a one-time 16M for training the base diffusion model), compared to 1.29B for MCMC-fixed-length and 38.4M for Doob's Lagrangian. For the Diffusion Edit Distance (DiffED) on ImageNet, the metric correlates at 53% with SSIM but only ~-7% with LPIPS, indicating it captures structural rather than perceptual similarity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The spacetime geodesic method decisively outperforms both MCMC two-way shooting variants and Doob's Lagrangian on the Alanine Dipeptide transition path task. Against MCMC-fixed-length (42.54±7.42), it reduces MaxEnergy by ~5.2 units while using roughly 80× fewer energy evaluations (16M vs 1.29B). Against MCMC-variable-length (58.11±18.51), the improvement is even larger (~20.8 units) with comparable evaluation counts. Against Doob's Lagrangian (66.24±1.01), the spacetime geodesic achieves a 28.9-unit lower MaxEnergy with fewer evaluations (16M vs 38.4M); moreover, Doob's method qualitatively collapsed to nearly identical trajectories, whereas the spacetime geodesic produces diverse paths that better avoid high-energy regions. The method's MaxEnergy of 37.36 is within 0.94 of the theoretical lower bound (36.42), a gap far smaller than any baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard pullback geometry approach to generative model latent spaces (Arvanitidis et al., 2022; Park et al., 2023) by proving it is fundamentally degenerate in diffusion models, where latent and data spaces share the same dimension. It builds on the score-based SDE/PF-ODE framework (Song et al., 2021) but reframes the latent space as a (D+1)-dimensional spacetime (x_t, t) equipped with the Fisher-Rao metric on denoising distributions, rather than a D-dimensional noise space with a pullback metric. The tractability of geodesic computation rests on a new theoretical result—that denoising distributions form an exponential family—which connects to classical information geometry (Amari, 2016) and Tweedie's formula (Efron, 2011). For transition path sampling, the paper positions itself against specialized molecular dynamics methods (Doob's Lagrangian, MCMC shooting) rather than prior geometric approaches to diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The proof that pullback geodesics in diffusion always decode to straight lines is a clean negative result that invalidates a commonly assumed geometric structure, redirecting the field toward information-geometric treatments. The exponential family characterization of denoising distributions is a reusable theoretical tool that makes Fisher-Rao geodesics computable without running the reverse SDE, opening the door to geometric analysis of diffusion internals at scale. However, DiffED is considerably slower than established similarity metrics, and the transition path sampling gains, while meaningful, are demonstrated only on a single 2D molecular system (Alanine Dipeptide), leaving open whether the approach scales to higher-dimensional biomolecular problems. The paper also raises the question of whether spacetime geodesics can inform better sampling strategies beyond the fixed-endpoint setting.
