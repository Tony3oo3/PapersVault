## Problem

LVLMs degrade significantly on multi-image tasks due to cross-image information leakage, where the model fails to cleanly separate information across different images. Although existing models employ delimiter tokens to mark image boundaries, the paper's attention analysis reveals these tokens only partially isolate visual contexts, leaving residual cross-image attention that causes information to intermix. The functional role and limitations of these delimiter tokens remained largely unexplored in prior work.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard multi-image LVLM inputs where multiple RGB images are interleaved with a text query, separated by model-specific delimiter tokens (e.g., / in Qwen2.5-VL, <img>/<\img> in InternVL3). It also generalizes to text-only multi-instance settings where documents or tables are separated by designated separator tokens (e.g., | || | in MultiNews). The output is a standard text answer or summary with no change to the generation procedure or output format.

## Method

The paper identifies two key properties of image delimiter tokens: (1) each delimiter receives strong attention from its corresponding image's tokens, forming a one-to-one image-to-delimiter mapping, and (2) this attention acts as a localized bias term (p_{d_i}·v_{d_i}) shared across all tokens in the same image, reinforcing intra-image interaction. The proposed method simply scales the hidden states of delimiter tokens by a factor λ > 1 at selected early layers, amplifying both properties simultaneously. Scaling increases the attention delimiter tokens receive (strengthening Property 1) while also increasing the magnitude of their value vectors, which amplifies the shared image-tagging bias and preserves intra-image interaction despite softmax normalization suppressing other attention. The approach is training-free, introduces no additional inference cost or memory overhead, and is compatible with FlashAttention since it modifies hidden states rather than attention scores directly.

## Results

On the Mantis multi-image benchmark, delimiter token scaling improves Qwen2.5-VL-3B from 59.91 to 63.13 accuracy (+3.22) and Qwen2.5-VL-32B from 68.20 to 70.05 (+1.85). The largest gain appears on MuirBench, where Qwen2.5-VL-3B jumps from 37.31 to 42.42 (+5.11). Gains are consistent across all four multi-image benchmarks (Mantis, MuirBench, MIRB, QBench2) and all three model families (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, LLaVA-OneVision), with no additional training or inference cost (identical VRAM and ~1s inference time). On text-only tasks, TQABench accuracy improves from 37.38 to 37.84 (Qwen2.5-3B) and ROUGE-1 on WCEP-10 rises from 27.30 to 27.52.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior approaches for mitigating cross-image information leakage. Against FOCUS (Park et al., 2025), which uses contrastive decoding requiring n+1 forward passes, the proposed method wins decisively on Mantis: Qwen2.5-VL-3B scores 63.13 vs. FOCUS's 58.53 (below the 59.91 baseline), and InternVL3-1B scores 49.77 vs. 47.93. FOCUS also incurs 2.1× peak VRAM (21.86 GB vs. 10.18 GB) and 3.2× inference time (5m 21s vs. 1m 41s), and could not be run on Qwen2.5-VL-7B or InternVL3-8B due to memory limits. Against M-RoPE temporal embeddings (from Qwen2-VL), the proposed method achieves 63.13 on Mantis (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) versus 62.21 for delimiters + M-RoPE, and M-RoPE alone actually degrades performance to 53.92. The method also outperforms simply scaling the first (sink) token (60.37) and scaling an arbitrary special token (53.00), confirming that the gains are specific to the structural role of image delimiter tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the cross-image information leakage problem identified by Park et al. (2025) and the sink token literature in LLMs (Gu et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2024), but carves new ground by revealing that image delimiter tokens in LVLMs function as *localized* sink tokens—a behavior distinct from the global sink tokens studied in text-only LLMs. It departs from prior multi-image approaches (Mantis's training-based fine-tuning, AVAM's external text encoder, FOCUS's multi-pass contrastive decoding) by proposing a training-free, zero-overhead hidden-state scaling that amplifies the delimiter tokens' existing image-tagging role. The M-RoPE comparison from Qwen2-VL is positioned as a related but less effective alternative for image distinction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's zero training and inference cost makes it immediately deployable as a drop-in fix for any LVLM with explicit image delimiters, and its generalization to multi-document and multi-table text tasks suggests the underlying mechanism (insufficient inter-segment distinction) is broader than vision. Key limitations include inapplicability to videos without explicit frame-separating tokens and the requirement for hidden-state access (open-source models only). Open questions include whether the approach extends to interleaved video-frame settings and whether entropy-based dynamic scaling (explored in the appendix) can be made FlashAttention-compatible without the ~2× memory overhead.
