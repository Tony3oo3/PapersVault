## Problem

Academic rebuttal is a strategic communication task under severe information asymmetry, yet existing approaches reduce it to surface-level linguistic imitation via supervised fine-tuning on review datasets, producing formulaic responses that lack the perspective-taking and strategic reasoning required for effective persuasion. The paper identifies that successful rebuttal demands Theory of Mind (ToM)—modeling the reviewer's beliefs, biases, and core concerns—to navigate trade-offs such as when to concede, stand firm, or reframe the narrative.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input the full manuscript, one review from a set of m reviews, and a single target comment extracted from that review. The manuscript is segmented into paragraph-level chunks, and top-k relevant chunks are retrieved via embedding-based cosine similarity to the target comment. The output is a three-part structured sequence explicitly demarcated by <Analysis>, <Strategy>, and <Response> tags, where the analysis contains a hierarchical JSON reviewer profile (macro-level stance/attitude/concern/expertise plus micro-level category/sub-category/severity), the strategy is a numbered action plan, and the response is the final evidence-grounded rebuttal text.

## Method

RebuttalAgent operationalizes ToM through a ToM-Strategy-Response (TSR) framework that decomposes rebuttal into three reasoning stages: (1) hierarchical reviewer profiling at macro and micro levels, (2) strategy formulation conditioned on the profile, and (3) evidence-based response synthesis grounded in retrieved manuscript chunks. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on RebuttalBench (70K samples synthesized via a critique-and-refine pipeline using multiple teacher models like GPT-4.1 and Claude 3.5), followed by reinforcement learning with GRPO using a self-reward mechanism that scores outputs on format adherence, reasoning quality, response quality, and response diversity—enabling scalable self-improvement without an external reward model. The base model is Qwen3-8B, and a separate Rebuttal-RM evaluator (also Qwen3-8B-based, trained on 100K+ multi-source samples) provides automated scoring aligned with human preferences, outperforming GPT-4.1 as a judge.

## Results

On the in-domain R2-test benchmark (6,000 comments from 24 conferences), RebuttalAgent achieves an overall average score of 9.42/10, outperforming the strongest foundation model baseline o3 (9.21) and GPT-4.1 (8.50). Compared to its Qwen3-8B base model (7.96), the agent yields an average improvement of 18.3%, with the largest gains in Significance-Persuasiveness (+26.9%) and Significance-Constructiveness (+34.6%). On the out-of-domain Rebuttal-test (2K recent ICLR/NeurIPS comments), RebuttalAgent maintains its lead, confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RebuttalAgent outperforms all seven foundation model baselines on the R2-test average score. Against GPT-4.1 (8.50), DeepSeek-R1 (8.64), and DeepSeek-V3 (8.51), the agent leads by 0.92, 0.78, and 0.91 points respectively, with the largest margin over Gemini-2.5 (7.75) at +1.67. Among open-weight models, RebuttalAgent surpasses GLM-4-9B (8.13), Llama-3.1-8B (7.44), and its own Qwen3-8B base (7.96) by 1.29, 1.98, and 1.46 points respectively. Against agent-based methods, RebuttalAgent edges out TSR$_{o3}$ (9.34) by 0.08 and Self-Refined (8.72) by 0.70, while substantially outperforming Strategy-Prompt (8.37) and RebuttalFT (6.35). In human evaluation (100 comments, 3 annotators, κ=0.79), RebuttalAgent achieves 9.57 average, surpassing o3 (9.26) and GPT-4.1 (8.99), with the largest relative gain in Persuasiveness (+7.36% over GPT-4.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of Machine Theory of Mind (MToM), LLM-based academic peer review, and RL-for-LLMs. It departs from prior SFT-only rebuttal approaches (e.g., RebuttalFT on the Re² dataset) by reframing rebuttal generation as a multi-stage strategic reasoning problem grounded in ToM, rather than a sequence-to-sequence imitation task. The TSR framework draws conceptually on MToM work (Rabinowitz et al., 2018; ToMAP, 2025) but is the first to operationalize it for academic rebuttal with hierarchical reviewer profiling. The RL stage uses GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) with a self-reward mechanism that eliminates the need for an external reward model during training, distinguishing it from typical reward-model-based RL pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important but underexplored stage of the research workflow, and the RebuttalBench (70K samples) and Rebuttal-RM (100K+ samples) resources are likely to be useful to the community. The self-reward mechanism offers a scalable path for self-improvement without expert-annotated reward signals. However, the approach is explicitly limited to linguistic persuasion (comments requiring new experiments are excluded), the generated content is positioned as a drafting aid rather than a replacement for author judgment, and it remains open whether the ToM-based strategic framing generalizes beyond AI/ML peer review to other domains or whether the moderate performance gain attributable specifically to the ToM component (9.42 vs. 9.04 in ablation) would hold at larger scale or with different base models.
