## Problem

Existing decoupled dataset distillation methods (SRe²L, CDA, G-VBSM, DWA, EDC, Minimax, D⁴M, RDED) each adopt different post-evaluation protocols—varying batch sizes, learning rate schedulers, data augmentations, loss functions, and soft-label types—making reported performance comparisons confounded and non-reproducible. The paper shows that the 27.3% performance gap reported across methods shrinks to under 7% when all methods are re-evaluated under a single unified protocol, revealing that much of the claimed progress stems from evaluation inconsistencies rather than genuine improvements in synthetic data quality.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes synthetic datasets (IPC 1–100) produced by any of the eight decoupled distillation methods across three paradigms (optimization-based, generation-based, selection-based), paired with a single pre-trained ResNet-18 teacher model for soft-label generation. It outputs standardized top-1 test accuracy on six RGB image datasets (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, TinyImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet-1K at variable resolution, ImageNette, ImageWoof) evaluated across seven student architectures (ResNet-18/50/101, MobileNet-V2, EfficientNet-B0, Swin-V2-T, ViT-B-16), along with generation-time measurements for efficiency comparison. A key unusual aspect is that the 'output' is not a new synthetic dataset but a rectified, comparable performance ranking that disentangles method quality from evaluation-protocol effects.

## Method

RD³ is a unified re-evaluation framework rather than a new distillation algorithm. It fixes a single post-evaluation protocol—AdamW optimizer, LR 0.001, KL-divergence soft labels from one ResNet-18 teacher, batch size 50 (ResNet-18) or 100 (other architectures), 400 epochs, cosine annealing with smoothing factor ζ=1 or ζ=2, and RDED's augmentation suite (PatchShuffle, RandomResizedCrop, Horizontal Flip, CutMix)—and applies it identically to all eight methods. The paper then systematically ablates each protocol component (batch size, LR smoothing, augmentation, loss function, hybrid soft labels, initialization) to quantify their individual contributions, revealing that simple adjustments like reducing batch size from 1024 to 50 yield ~10% gains across all methods. A striking finding is that randomly sampled real images combined with epoch-wise soft labels outperform all existing distillation methods on coarse-grained datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNette, ImageNet-1K), establishing a previously overlooked strong baseline. The framework also introduces generation-time as a first-class evaluation metric, showing up to 100× efficiency differences between methods with only marginal accuracy gaps.

## Results

Under the unified RD³ evaluation protocol (single ResNet-18 teacher, KL divergence, BS=50, 400 epochs, ζ=1, RDED augmentations), the previously reported 27.3% performance gap on ImageNet-1K (IPC=10) shrinks to just 6.7%: EDC achieves 46.9%, RDED 46.3%, Minimax 45.9%, D4M 45.4%, DWA 42.5%, G-VBSM 41.5%, CDA 41.2%, and SRe²L 40.2%. No single method consistently dominates across all six datasets and four IPC levels; EDC leads among optimization-based methods, while D4M and RDED are competitive or superior on several settings. Strikingly, random image sampling with soft labels outperforms all distillation methods on CIFAR-10/100, ImageNette, and ImageNet-1K, revealing that the soft-label relabeling protocol itself accounts for most of the reported gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the unified RD³ protocol, EDC (optimization-based) leads among the five optimization methods on ImageNet-1K at IPC=10 (46.9% vs. DWA 42.5%, G-VBSM 41.5%, CDA 41.2%, SRe²L 40.2%), but its advantage over RDED (46.3%) and Minimax (45.9%) is marginal. D⁴M (generation-based) matches or exceeds EDC at IPC=100 (63.5% vs. 63.2%) and on CIFAR-10 IPC=100 (77.7% vs. 74.4%), while RDED (selection-based) achieves the best IPC=1 result on ImageNet-1K (7.6% vs. EDC 7.0%). The paper's central finding is that the 27.3% performance gap reported in prior literature collapses to 6.7% once evaluation settings (batch size, LR scheduler, augmentation, loss function) are aligned, indicating that most claimed advances stem from post-evaluation tuning rather than synthetic data quality. Notably, random image sampling with epoch-wise soft labels surpasses all eight distillation methods on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNette, and ImageNet-1K, establishing a surprisingly strong baseline that the field has overlooked.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the decoupled dataset distillation paradigm initiated by SRe²L (Yin et al., 2024), which separated teacher pre-training from synthetic data generation, and the epoch-wise soft-label relabeling mechanism from Qin et al. (2024). Rather than proposing a new distillation algorithm, it positions itself as a meta-evaluation of the three paradigms that emerged after SRe²L—optimization-based (CDA, G-VBSM, DWA, EDC), generation-based (Minimax, D⁴M), and selection-based (RDED)—by identifying that their reported performance differences are largely artifacts of inconsistent post-evaluation protocols (batch size, learning rate schedulers, data augmentation, loss functions). The paper departs from prior benchmarking efforts like DC-Bench and DD-Ranking by being the first to systematically re-evaluate all three paradigms under a single unified protocol across six datasets, four IPC levels, and seven student architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a reported 27% performance gap among methods collapses to under 7% under unified settings fundamentally reframes progress in decoupled dataset distillation, suggesting the field has been conflating evaluation-tuning gains with genuine algorithmic advances. The discovery that random image sampling with soft labels outperforms all distillation methods on coarse-grained datasets (CIFAR-10/100, ImageNette, ImageNet-1K) raises a critical open question: how much of the 'distillation' benefit actually comes from the soft-label relabeling mechanism versus the data generation process itself? Open questions include whether the unified RD³ settings are truly optimal for all methods, whether the findings extend to tasks beyond image classification (detection, video), and what the true ceiling for synthetic dataset quality is when evaluation is held constant. The paper also highlights that computational efficiency (up to 100× time differences) should be a first-class evaluation criterion alongside accuracy.
