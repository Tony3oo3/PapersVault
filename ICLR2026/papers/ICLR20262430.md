## Problem

Fixed-depth Transformers are confined to low circuit-complexity classes (AC0/TC0) and cannot perform deep multi-step reasoning end-to-end, while RNN-based alternatives suffer from finite-state capacity bottlenecks that degrade on long-context and multi-hop tasks. Chain-of-Thought and tree-search methods partially address reasoning depth but couple computational cost to generated sequence length and remain constrained by the expressiveness of natural language. No existing architecture simultaneously achieves deep latent reasoning, near-linear sequence complexity, and reliable long-range dependency modeling.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a standard text token sequence and produces a next-token probability distribution over the vocabulary. Unusually, the forward pass is not a single fixed-depth sweep: a bottom-level feature extractor produces a static read-only context, a recursive reasoning core then iteratively refines a writable latent state through repeated applications of a shared-parameter map, and an entropy-based early-stopping criterion (monitoring the change in output entropy over a sliding window) determines when to halt recursion before a top-level module decodes the final logits. This means the effective number of computation steps per input is adaptive rather than fixed.

## Method

ChainGPT introduces a dual-reasoning architecture operating at two scales. Intra-layer, each Chain-Block pairs an RWKV-Product state layer, where M LoRA-parameterized sub-steps compose diagonal-plus-rank-1 transitions into an effective diagonal-plus-rank-M update within a single forward pass, with a State-Guided Sparse Attention (SGSA) layer that attends only to a local window of size W and periodic global anchors at stride G, yielding O(N(W+N/G)) complexity while preserving long-range recall. Inter-layer, a recurrent depth approach wraps the core blocks in a loop: a fixed-parameter map is applied iteratively to the latent state (concatenated with the static bottom-level context), trained via a two-phase strategy of gradient-free warmup followed by truncated backpropagation, and halted adaptively when output entropy stabilizes. Theoretically, the paper proves the architecture is Turing complete by constructing a simulation of a two-counter Minsky machine using the recurrent core's masked-overwrite and shift primitives, and empirically demonstrates near-perfect accuracy on GOAT arithmetic (99.98% with CoT) and strong results on ARC-AGI-1, Sudoku-Extreme, and Maze-Hard with only 30M parameters.

## Results

On six standard language understanding benchmarks (ARC-Challenge, ARC-Easy, HellaSwag, PIQA, SciQ, GLUE), ChainGPT-1.5B achieves an average accuracy of 0.5291, outperforming Qwen2.5-1.5B (0.5059) by 2.3 points, with the largest gains on ARC-Challenge (0.2986 vs 0.2696) and HellaSwag (0.4269 vs 0.4091). On the GOAT arithmetic reasoning task, ChainGPT reaches 57.53% accuracy without CoT supervision, surpassing HRM (54.00%) and Qwen3+Loop (50.54%), and with CoT achieves 99.98% versus Qwen3+CoT's 88.43%. On challenging reasoning tasks, ChainGPT scores 38.6% on ARC-AGI-1, 54.4% on Sudoku-Extreme, and 77.4% on Maze-Hard, surpassing strong baselines including o3-mini-high and Claude-3.7-8K on ARC-AGI-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen2.5 (the primary Transformer baseline), ChainGPT wins on all six benchmarks at both 0.5B and 1.5B scales, with the most substantial margin on ARC-Challenge (+2.9 pts at 1.5B) and HellaSwag (+1.8 pts at 1.5B). Against RWKV-7, ChainGPT achieves 57.53% vs 23.69% on GOAT arithmetic and maintains >99% MQAR accuracy at (2048,256) where RWKV-7 degrades to 72.93%, confirming that SGSA resolves the finite-state capacity bottleneck. Against HRM (the closest recurrent-reasoning competitor), ChainGPT leads by 3.5 points on GOAT (57.53% vs 54.00%) without requiring CoT. Against Jamba's global attention approach, ChainGPT's sparse anchor strategy (G=32–128) matches perplexity within 0.01 across 2K–16K contexts on PG-19 while reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(N(W+N/G)). Against Samba's pure sliding-window attention, ChainGPT avoids the perplexity degradation that Samba exhibits beyond 8K context (16.12 vs 16.08 at 16K), demonstrating that periodic global anchors are essential for long-range dependency preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChainGPT synthesizes two recent architectural threads: the RWKV-7 'diagonal + rank-1' state update (Peng et al., 2025), which it extends to a multi-substep 'diagonal + rank-M' form via LoRA-based sub-steps (RWKV-Product), and the recurrent depth approach (Geiping et al., 2025), which it adopts as the inter-layer iterative refinement mechanism with truncated backpropagation. It departs from prior hybrid architectures (Jamba, Samba) by replacing full or window-only attention with State-Guided Sparse Attention that uses periodic anchors to achieve near-linear long-range modeling, and from CoT/ToT methods by shifting reasoning entirely into latent state space rather than token generation. The 'dual-reasoning' framing—combining intra-layer multi-substep computation with inter-layer recursion—is the paper's conceptual unification of these otherwise separate ideas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled architectural recipe for combining RNN efficiency with Transformer-like long-range recall, and the theoretical Turing-completeness proof (under idealized arbitrary-precision and unbounded-workspace assumptions) provides a clean upper-bound argument for what recurrent-depth models can express. However, the empirical validation is conducted on small models (≤1.5B) trained on limited data (20–40B tokens), and the most striking result—38.6% on ARC-AGI-1—comes from a 30M-parameter model trained on only 1000 samples, making it difficult to assess whether the advantage persists at scale. Open questions include whether the recurrent depth overhead at inference time remains acceptable for production deployment, whether the entropy-based early stopping generalizes across task distributions, and whether the theoretical guarantees (MQAR solvability, Turing completeness) survive finite-precision and bounded-memory constraints in practice.
