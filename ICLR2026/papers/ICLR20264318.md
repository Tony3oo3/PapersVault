## Problem

The prevailing intuition in LLM reasoning is that longer Chain-of-Thought (CoT) chains monotonically improve accuracy, yet recent evidence suggests concise CoTs can sometimes outperform verbose ones. No prior work has systematically characterized when longer CoTs actually hurt performance, how the optimal length scales with task difficulty and model capability, or provided a theoretical mechanism explaining the 'overthinking' phenomenon. This paper fills that gap by establishing that accuracy follows an inverted U-shaped curve with respect to CoT length and deriving the scaling laws that govern the optimal length.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a reasoning question (arithmetic expression, DP triangle, or real-world benchmark problem) and a parameterized LLM as input, and produces a final answer along with the CoT length that maximizes accuracy for that question-model pair. In the synthetic setting, task difficulty is controlled by the number of operators T (arithmetic) or triangle height H (DP), while model capability is controlled by GPT-2 depth (5–9 layers). At inference, the Length-Filtered Vote method consumes n i.i.d. sampled CoTs, bins them by step count into groups of width D, computes Shannon entropy of answer distributions within each bin, and outputs the majority vote restricted to the K lowest-entropy groups.

## Method

The paper combines controlled synthetic experiments (GPT-2 on arithmetic and DP tasks with systematically varied CoT lengths), real-world evaluations (Qwen2.5 1.5B–72B on MMLU STEM, MATH500, WinoGrande, GPQA), and a theoretical error-accumulation model. The core theoretical contribution models final accuracy as A(N) = α((1−σ(T))(1−E(N,M,T)))^N, where σ(T) is per-step sub-question error and E(N,M,T) is per-step sub-answer error depending on model capability M and per-step difficulty T/N; this yields a closed-form optimal length N*(M,T) = TZ/(M(Z+1)) via the Lambert W function, from which three scaling laws follow (N* increases with T, decreases with M, and optimal per-step difficulty t* increases with T). The paper further shows that RL with outcome rewards reduces to a stateless bandit over CoT lengths and provably converges to the optimal length, explaining why RL fine-tuning calibrates reasoning length. Practical applications include training small models on optimally-sized CoT data (outperforming larger models trained on uniform-length data) and a Length-Filtered Vote inference strategy that filters majority-vote candidates by length-binned entropy.

## Results

On MATH500, using the optimal CoT length yields dramatically higher accuracy than the longest CoT: the 72B model improves from 8% to 81% (+73 points), the 7B model from 27% to 82% (+55 points), and the 32B model from 40% to 81% (+41 points). On WinoGrande-XS, the optimal-length advantage is more modest but consistent (e.g., 72B: 85% → 93%). In the synthetic training experiment, a 6-layer GPT-2 trained on optimal-length CoT data outperforms a 9-layer model trained on mixed-length data, with the gap widening as task difficulty increases. The Length-Filtered Vote method consistently outperforms vanilla majority vote (self-consistency) and random-filtered vote on GPQA across both LLaMA3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is against vanilla majority vote (self-consistency, Wang et al. 2023) on the GPQA dataset. The proposed Length-Filtered Vote method, which groups sampled CoTs by length and performs majority voting only within the K=3 lowest-entropy length bins, consistently outperforms both vanilla majority vote and a random-filtered vote (which groups samples randomly rather than by length) on both LLaMA3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, with the advantage persisting as the number of sampled candidates increases. Beyond this inference-time comparison, the paper's central empirical finding is that using the optimal CoT length at inference dramatically outperforms using the longest available CoT—by up to 73 percentage points on MATH500 for the 72B model—challenging the assumption that longer reasoning chains are always superior. In the training-data comparison, a smaller model (6 layers) trained exclusively on optimal-length CoT solutions surpasses a larger model (9 layers) trained on uniformly mixed-length solutions, demonstrating that data curation aligned with the model's optimal reasoning granularity is more effective than simply scaling model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the error-accumulation framework of Schaeffer et al. (2023), which used a fixed-length p^L formulation to explain emergent abilities, by making CoT length the variable of interest and deriving an explicit optimal-length formula via the Lambert W function. It builds on self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023) by adding length-aware entropy filtering to majority voting, and on complexity-based prompting (Fu et al., 2023) for controlled length generation. The work positions itself against concurrent overthinking studies (Chen et al., 2024c; Han et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2025) by moving beyond empirical observations to formal scaling laws and a unified explanation of why RL calibrates CoT length, connecting to looped Transformer architectures (Geiping et al., 2025) for adaptive per-step computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held 'longer is better' assumption in CoT reasoning with strong cross-model, cross-task evidence and a clean theoretical account, providing actionable guidelines for training data curation and inference-time filtering. Key open questions remain: how to reliably estimate optimal CoT length for arbitrary real-world problems (the paper acknowledges this is hard), whether the stationarity and independence assumptions in the error model hold for complex reasoning with backtracking and verification, and how these findings interact with test-time compute scaling strategies (e.g., o1-style iterative reasoning). The practical demonstration that a smaller model trained on optimally-sized CoTs can outperform a larger one trained on uniform-length data has direct implications for efficient model deployment.
