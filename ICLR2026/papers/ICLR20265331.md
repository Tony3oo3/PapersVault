## Problem

Graph datasets lack a shared input feature space: node features differ across datasets in dimensionality, semantics, and value ranges, preventing graph models from generalizing across datasets as foundation models. Existing approaches either serialize graphs into LLM prompts (losing structural detail) or require task-specific feature alignment, leaving a gap for truly input-space-agnostic transferable graph representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265331/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts node feature matrices of arbitrary and varying dimension d across datasets (the key constraint being that d need not match across source and target graphs), a graph adjacency matrix, optional edge features, and random-walk structural encodings. It produces fixed-dimensional node representations (of size h + h_s) that are independent of the original feature space, which can then be fed into task-specific prediction heads for node classification, graph classification, or regression without any architectural modification or retraining of the encoder.

## Method

ALL-IN projects node features X ∈ R^{n×d} into a shared fixed-dimensional space via an isotropic Gaussian random matrix C sampled fresh at each forward pass (R^{(0)} = XC), then computes n×n node-covariance operators K^{(p)} = (1/h)(Π_c A^p R^{(0)})(Π_c A^p R^{(0)})^T for p = 0,…,k, capturing pairwise node similarities based on how their projected features co-vary across p-hop neighborhoods. These covariance matrices, together with the identity and adjacency matrices, form a set of graph operators used for message passing in a multi-branch GNN whose outputs are concatenated at each layer. The projected features R^{(0)} are also retained as part of the initial node representation to preserve first-order information. Theoretically, the method achieves distributional invariance to arbitrary feature permutations, expected-operator invariance to orthogonal basis changes, and consistency of the stochastic operator to the centered Gram matrix Π_c XX^T Π_c as the projection dimension h grows, providing a principled basis for cross-dataset transfer without retraining.

## Results

On unseen node classification datasets with new input features (frozen encoder, only a new prediction head trained), ALL-IN achieves 82.13% accuracy on CORA, 69.12% on CITESEER, and 78.03% on PubMed. On unseen graph classification datasets, ALL-IN reaches 92.90% on MUTAG and 78.20% on PROTEINS. On the pre-training source datasets, the jointly-trained ALL-IN encoder matches or exceeds per-dataset specialized models on 5 of 9 datasets (e.g., 91.17% on CUNEIFORM vs. 87.20% for ALL-IN-SPECIALIZED, 98.08% on MSRC-21 vs. 94.16%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALL-IN outperforms supervised GCN on all three node classification benchmarks (CORA 82.13 vs. 78.86, CITESEER 69.12 vs. 64.52, PubMed 78.03 vs. 74.49) and GIN by an even wider margin. Against the strongest GNN-based foundation model baseline SCORE, ALL-IN wins on CORA (82.13 vs. 81.80) but trails on CITESEER (69.12 vs. 71.33) and PubMed (78.03 vs. 82.93). ALL-IN surpasses GraphAny on all three datasets (e.g., CORA 82.13 vs. 79.36) and dominates prompt-based methods like GPROMPT (CORA 82.13 vs. 56.66) and GPF-PLUS (CORA 82.13 vs. 55.77) by large margins. On graph classification, ALL-IN achieves 92.90% on MUTAG, exceeding GIN (89.40%), SCORE (85.33%), and ALL-IN-ONE (79.87%), and 78.20% on PROTEINS, beating GIN (76.20%) and SCORE (68.54%). The only notable losses are to SCORE on CITESEER and PubMed, and to OFA on CITESEER (73.04 vs. 69.12), suggesting that LLM-augmented or topology-focused methods retain an edge on certain citation-network structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of random feature projections and second-order statistics for graph learning. While prior GFM work either serializes graphs into LLM prompts (OFA, GraphText), aligns feature spaces via learned projections (AnyGraph, MDGPT, GCOPE), or encodes features into graph structure (ULTRA, SCORE), ALL-IN repurposes isotropic Gaussian random projections combined with node-level covariance operators to create a feature-space-agnostic representation. The approach is conceptually distinct from covariance neural networks (VNNs), which process d×d feature-covariance matrices, whereas ALL-IN constructs n×n node-covariance matrices that capture pairwise node similarities. The theoretical framework draws on properties of isotropic Gaussian distributions and the weak law of large numbers to establish invariance and consistency guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ALL-IN addresses what the authors identify as a fundamental obstacle to graph foundation models: the absence of a shared input feature space across datasets. The combination of distributional permutation invariance, expected orthogonal invariance, and consistency of the stochastic operator provides a principled foundation for input-agnostic transfer, and the empirical results (outperforming or matching SOTA on unseen CORA, CiteSeer, PubMed, MUTAG, PROTEINS) demonstrate practical viability. Key limitations include the O(n²) dense covariance operators that constrain scalability to large graphs, and the reliance on random (rather than structured or learnable) projections. Open questions include whether sparse approximations can preserve the invariance properties, whether the approach extends to heterogeneous graphs with multiple node/edge types, and how performance scales with graph size in practice.
