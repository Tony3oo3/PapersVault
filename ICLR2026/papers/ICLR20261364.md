## Problem

Mean-field (MF) theory characterizes neural network trainability through gradient stability (ordered vs. chaotic phases), while the initial guessing bias (IGB) framework characterizes predictive behavior at initialization (neutrality vs. prejudice). These two independent frameworks for analyzing wide networks at initialization had no known theoretical connection, leaving it unclear how a network's initial classification bias relates to its trainability. The paper asks whether and how these frameworks can be unified, and what this implies for the optimal initialization.

## Input / Output

The theory takes a randomly initialized wide network (MLP, CNN, or Vision Transformer) with specified weight variance σ_w², bias variance σ_b², and activation function, and produces a phase diagram mapping initialization hyperparameters to IGB phases (neutrality, prejudice, deep prejudice) and MF phases (ordered, chaotic, EOC). Empirically, the framework is validated on RGB image datasets (binarized Fashion MNIST, binarized CIFAR-10, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) by tracking global accuracy, per-class accuracy, and maximum classification frequency during training. The theory requires only that pre-activations be normally distributed in the infinite-width limit, making it applicable to any architecture satisfying this condition.

## Method

The core contribution is a formal equivalence theorem (Thm. 3.1) proving that in the infinite-width limit, the MF signal variance equals the IGB total variance (σ_μ² + σ_y²), the MF input covariance equals the IGB centers variance (σ_μ²), and the MF correlation coefficient c is a deterministic function of the IGB activation drift ratio γ via c = γ/(1+γ). This mapping allows the entire MF phase diagram to be reinterpreted in IGB terms, revealing that the edge of chaos (EOC) — the optimal trainability condition — corresponds to a state of 'transient deep prejudice' where the network is maximally biased at initialization but gradients are stable enough to rapidly absorb the bias. The paper further extends the IGB framework to non-zero bias variances and multi-node activation functions (e.g., max-pooling), derives explicit recursive relations for ReLU networks showing that c always converges to 1 (persistent deep prejudice) across the entire phase diagram, and demonstrates that gradient exploding in the chaotic phase is class-dependent, with favored classes exhibiting near-zero gradients while unfavored classes explode.

## Results

The paper establishes a formal equivalence (Thm. 3.1) between Mean-Field (MF) and Initial Guessing Bias (IGB) frameworks, showing that the MF signal variance decomposes as q_aa^(l) = σ_μ^(l)² + σ_y^(l)² and the correlation coefficient relates to the activation drift ratio via c_ab^(l) = γ^(l)/(1+γ^(l)). The central finding (Prop. 4.1) is that the Edge of Chaos (EOC) — the optimal trainability condition — corresponds not to a neutral initialization but to a state of *transient deep prejudice* (γ → ∞, c = 1, χ̃₁ = 1). Empirically, across MLPs, Residual MLPs, and Vision Transformers on binarized Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, the EOC initialization exhibits the strongest initial classification bias (maximum classification frequency well above 1/n_c) yet absorbs this bias fastest, reaching high accuracy sooner than both ordered (vanishing gradient) and chaotic (exploding gradient) phases.

**Table 1: Phase descriptions with IGB and MF order parameters.**

| IGB (γ) | MF (c) | MF (χ̃₁) | Phase |
|---|---|---|---|
| γ = ∞ | c = 1 | χ̃₁ < 1 | Ordered – deep prejudice |
| γ = ∞ | c = 1 | χ̃₁ = 1 | Transient – deep prejudice (EOC) |
| γ = ∞ | c = 1 | χ̃₁ > 1 | Chaotic – deep prejudice |
| 1 < γ < ∞ | 0.5 < c < 1 | χ̃₁ > 1 | (Chaotic) Prejudice |
| γ < 1 | c < 0.5 | χ̃₁ > 1 | (Chaotic) Neutrality |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper, so there is no traditional method-vs-baseline comparison. Instead, the paper compares different initialization regimes (phases) in terms of training dynamics. The EOC (transient deep prejudice) consistently outperforms both the ordered phase (persistent deep prejudice with vanishing gradients) and the chaotic phases (exploding gradients) in terms of speed of bias absorption and final accuracy. On binarized Fashion MNIST with a Tanh MLP, the EOC reaches high test accuracy fastest, while the neutral (chaotic) phase performs poorly and cannot recover high accuracy. For the large pre-trained Vision Transformer fine-tuned on CIFAR-100, the original unscaled weights (at EOC) show the fastest learning and are the only configuration that significantly improves the most unfavoured class; scaling weights by σ_w² = 0.5 (ordered phase) triggers strong IGB with small gradients that hinder training, while σ_w² = 1.5 (chaotic phase) reduces IGB but still hinders dynamics. The paper also corrects an inaccuracy in prior MF phase diagrams for ReLU, showing that the correlation coefficient c always converges to 1 across the entire phase diagram (not just in the ordered phase), meaning c alone cannot discriminate phases for unbounded activations — a result that extends the IGB framework beyond its original zero-bias setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper establishes a formal equivalence between two previously independent theoretical frameworks for analyzing wide neural networks at initialization: mean-field (MF) theory of signal/gradient propagation (Schoenholz et al. 2016; Poole et al. 2016; Hayou et al. 2019) and the Initial Guessing Bias (IGB) framework (Francazi et al. 2024). The key result (Theorem 3.1) shows that MF quantities (signal variance, covariance, correlation coefficient) map exactly onto IGB quantities (total variance, centers variance, activation drift ratio γ), unifying the phase diagram for trainability with the phase diagram for predictive bias. The paper also extends the IGB framework to non-zero bias variances and multi-node activation functions (e.g., maxpool), and corrects a known inaccuracy in the MF phase diagram for ReLU by showing the correlation coefficient always converges to 1, making it an ineffective order parameter in that case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding — that the edge of chaos (optimal trainability) corresponds to a state of transient deep prejudice rather than neutrality — directly counters the intuitive assumption from Francazi et al. (2024) that unbiased initializations yield the fastest learning dynamics. This reframes how practitioners should interpret initialization: the 'best' starting point is systematically biased, and that bias is rapidly absorbed during early training. Practical implications include the need for longer hyperparameter tuning runs to avoid privileging specific classes due to residual IGB, and the recognition that gradient exploding in the chaotic phase is class-dependent (favored classes get near-zero gradients). Open questions include whether the transient-deep-prejudice characterization holds for architectures with normalization layers or skip connections that collapse the phase structure, and whether the bias reabsorption dynamics can be further characterized analytically.
