## Problem

Existing geometry problem solving methods split into neural-based approaches (prone to hallucination, lacking interpretability) and symbolic-based approaches (struggling with incomplete multimodal formalization and producing opaque algebraic solutions without traceable reasoning steps). No prior method simultaneously achieves reliable, mathematically rigorous derivation and human-interpretable stepwise reasoning for plane geometry problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264295/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a geometry diagram (RGB image) paired with a natural-language problem description specifying the objective (e.g., 'Find length of line AB') as input. It produces a solution literal of the form Equals(target, value) along with a minimal stepwise reasoning process where each step follows a syllogistic structure: a premise set, an applied theorem, and a conclusion set. The intermediate formalization uses a context-free-grammar-defined formal language with predicates like Line(A,B), Perpendicular(Line(A,B),Line(C,D)), and MeasureOf(Angle(A,B,C)), and the final reasoning steps are topologically sorted from a minimal sub-hypergraph to guarantee logical coherence and conciseness.

## Method

AutoGPS is a neurosymbolic framework with two collaborating components: a Multimodal Problem Formalizer (MPF) and a Deductive Symbolic Reasoner (DSR). The MPF is a multimodal agent that performs three sequential stages—key-point annotation via an FPN-based detector, pre-formalization via a PGDP-Net diagram parser and an extended rule-based text parser, and multimodal alignment via an MLLM agent—to produce a complete formal language representation. The DSR first validates the formalization for self-consistency and supplements implicit geometric relations, feeding error messages back to the MPF for iterative refinement. It then formulates solving as a directed hypergraph expansion task where formal literals are nodes and syllogistic derivation steps (premise set → theorem → conclusion set) are hyperedges, expanding via two complementary strategies: deductive reasoning (matching theorems from a predefined set) and stepwise algebraic reasoning (four atomic operations: equivalent substitution, constant evaluation, univariate non-linear solving, and linear system solving via MILP for minimal sufficient equation sets). Once the solution node is reached, the DSR extracts the minimal reasoning sub-hypergraph (solved as a shortest-path problem on a DAG) and topologically sorts it into a concise, human-readable proof.

## Results

On the Completion task (open-ended answer generation), AutoGPS with GPT-4o achieves 75.4% on Geometry3K and 75.3% on PGPS9K, outperforming the best neural solver LANS (71.3% and 66.1%) by 4.1% and 9.2% respectively. On the Choice task, AutoGPS (GPT-4o) reaches 81.6% on Geometry3K (nearly matching LANS at 82.3%) and 81.5% on PGPS9K (surpassing LANS by 7.7%). In human stepwise-reasoning evaluation, AutoGPS achieves 99% stepwise logical coherence versus 71% for the best MLLM (InternVL3), and its formalization quality (Jaccard similarity 0.868) substantially exceeds InterGPS (0.395).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest neural solver LANS, AutoGPS (GPT-4o) wins by 4.1% on Geometry3K Completion (75.4% vs 71.3%) and 9.2% on PGPS9K Completion (75.3% vs 66.1%), while trailing by 0.7% on Geometry3K Choice (81.6% vs 82.3%) but leading by 7.7% on PGPS9K Choice (81.5% vs 73.8%). Against symbolic solvers, AutoGPS substantially outperforms InterGPS (50.6%→75.4% on Geo3K Completion, a 24.8-point gain) and E-GPS/GeoDRL (67.9%/68.4% Choice on Geo3K vs 81.6%). Against raw MLLMs, AutoGPS (GPT-4o) improves Completion accuracy by 29.1% over GPT-4o alone (75.4% vs 46.3%) and by 17.9% over InternVL3-78B (75.4% vs 57.4%), demonstrating that the neurosymbolic pipeline adds substantial value over direct MLLM inference. On the additional MathVista (GPS) benchmark, AutoGPS (InternVL3) achieves 86.2%, ranking first among open-source methods and surpassing Ovis2_34B (84.6%) and Vision-R1-7B (82.7%); on GeoQA it reaches 86.2%, edging out FGeo-HyperGNet (85.6%) and far exceeding DualGeoSolver (65.2%) and SCA-GPS (64.1%). In stepwise reasoning reliability, AutoGPS achieves 99% logical coherence versus 29–33% error rates for Qwen2.5, InternVL3, and GPT-4o, confirming that the symbolic deduction eliminates hallucination-induced reasoning failures.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoGPS sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: the formal-language-based symbolic solver Inter-GPS (Lu et al., 2021), from which it inherits the problem formulation, simplified formal language, and rule-based text parsing; and AlphaGeometry (Trinh et al., 2024), whose hypergraph expansion framework it extends by integrating algebraic equation solving as first-class hyperedges alongside deductive theorem application. The key architectural departure is the neurosymbolic feedback loop: an MLLM-based Multimodal Problem Formalizer (using PGDP-Net for diagram parsing and FPN for point annotation) produces formalizations that are iteratively validated and refined by the symbolic Deductive Symbolic Reasoner, addressing the formalization incompleteness that plagues purely symbolic methods and the hallucination that plagues purely neural ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 99% stepwise logical coherence (vs. 29–33% for the best MLLMs on correctly-solved problems) demonstrates that neurosymbolic decomposition can simultaneously guarantee answer correctness and reasoning validity—a property neither pure paradigm achieves. This has direct implications for educational and tutoring applications where traceable, verifiable reasoning is essential. Key limitations remain: the formal language cannot express all geometric configurations (e.g., three tangential circles), the exhaustive hypergraph search creates a fundamental trade-off between proof minimality and computational efficiency, and the one failed case reveals that logically consistent formalizations can still be unfaithful to the original problem—a subtle open problem for future work.
