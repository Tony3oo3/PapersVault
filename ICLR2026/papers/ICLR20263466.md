## Problem

The nonconvex low-rank SDP relaxation of K-means (3) is known to exhibit benign nonconvexity—its second-order critical points are global optima—but no existing algorithm can rigorously compute such points with guaranteed convergence. First-order methods (NLR, Carson et al.) lack second-order guarantees and converge slowly, while the direct Riemannian formulation on the original constraint set requires an O(n²) retraction that prevents scaling. The paper bridges this gap by providing a scalable second-order Riemannian algorithm with linear per-iteration cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes n data points in R^d and a target number of clusters K, operating through the Gram matrix XX^T so it extends directly to kernelized settings. It requires an over-parameterized search rank r > K (the smallest feasible r = K+1 is recommended in practice) and a log-barrier parameter μ below a critical threshold. The output is a nonnegative factor U ∈ R^{n×r} whose product UU^T yields the membership matrix Z, from which cluster labels are extracted in n·poly(r) time via the unique nonnegative rank-K factorization (Lemma 1).

## Method

The paper reformulates the K-means nonconvex problem (3) as smooth unconstrained optimization over a product manifold M̃ = V × O_r (a projected hypersphere times the orthogonal group) via the submersion φ(V,Q) = [1̂_n V]Q, which admits a simple O(nr + r³) Euclidean-projection retraction. On this manifold, it applies Riemannian cubic-regularized Newton (Theorem 1), whose Newton subproblem is solved by bisection on the regularization parameter λ. The key efficiency insight is that the Riemannian Hessian has a block-diagonal-plus-low-rank structure (block-diagonal in n blocks of size r−1 plus a rank-2rd correction from the data Gram matrix), reducing each Newton solve to O(n·poly(r,d)) time. This yields an overall complexity of n·ε^{-3/2}·poly(r,d,K) to compute an ε-second-order critical point, matching first-order per-iteration costs while converging in O(ε^{-3/2}) iterations rather than O(ε^{-1}).

## Results

On synthetic GMM data (n=100, γ=0.8, μ=0.1), the proposed Riemannian cubic-regularized Newton method reaches the global optimum in ~152 iterations, compared to ~80,000 iterations for the state-of-the-art NLR first-order method (Zhuang et al., 2024). Although each Newton step costs 25–100× more than a single NLR update, the total wall-clock time is 2–4× shorter. On the real-world CyTOF dataset (n=1,800, K=4), the method achieves near-zero mis-clustering error with a smaller Frobenius gap to the oracle membership matrix than NLR, while classical baselines (SC, NMF, K-means++) show substantially higher and more variable error. On GMM with n=500 and γ=1.2, all 50 random initializations converge to zero clustering error, providing strong numerical evidence for the benign nonconvexity assumption (Assumption 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline NLR (Zhuang et al., 2024), the proposed method wins decisively on convergence speed: 152 iterations versus ~80,000, yielding a 2–4× wall-clock speedup despite each Newton step being 25–100× more expensive. On CyTOF, both methods achieve near-zero mis-clustering, but the proposed method produces a smaller Frobenius gap to the ground-truth membership matrix, indicating more accurate recovery. Against the prior Riemannian K-means method of Carson et al. (2017), the proposed method wins overwhelmingly: Carson's first-order approach with a smooth penalty cannot balance feasibility and optimality, resulting in >30% average mis-clustering, large recovery error, and persistent infeasibility (‖U₋‖ never vanishes), whereas the proposed method enforces U₋=0 by construction. Against classical baselines (spectral clustering, NMF, K-means++) on CyTOF, the proposed method and NLR both dominate, achieving the lowest and most consistent mis-clustering with the fewest outliers. Against the Riemannian trust-region method (RTR) from PYMANOPT, the proposed cubic-regularized Newton converges in ~360 iterations to machine precision while RTR stagnates for over 21,000 iterations due to the extreme ill-conditioning induced by the log-barrier penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of the Burer-Monteiro nonconvex factorization of SDPs (Burer & Monteiro, 2003; Boumal et al., 2020), the K-means SDP relaxation with statistical guarantees (Peng & Wei, 2007; Chen & Yang, 2021b), and Riemannian optimization on manifolds (Zhang & Zhang, 2018). It departs from prior Riemannian K-means work (Carson et al., 2017) by reformulating the constraint manifold as a product of a projected hypersphere and an orthogonal group, yielding O(nr) retractions instead of O(n²), and from the first-order NLR method (Zhuang et al., 2024) by applying a second-order cubic-regularized Newton step whose subproblem is solved in linear time via a block-diagonal-plus-low-rank Hessian structure. The key conceptual departure is showing that the nonnegative Burer-Monteiro factorization—known to admit spurious local minima in general—exhibits benign nonconvexity specifically for K-means, enabling rigorous second-order convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method with a rigorous guarantee of global convergence to a second-order critical point for the nonconvex K-means factorization, closing a gap that first-order methods (NLR, Carson et al.) could not address. The linear per-iteration cost makes second-order optimization practical at scale, and the 2–4× wall-clock speedup over the state-of-the-art NLR is practically meaningful for large datasets. However, the central 'benign nonconvexity' property (Assumption 1) remains an empirical observation rather than a theorem, and the paper does not prove it holds beyond the GMM average-case regime or for highly imbalanced clusters. Open questions include whether the product-manifold reformulation extends to other doubly nonnegative SDP relaxations, and whether the phase transition in the log-barrier parameter μ can be characterized more precisely.
