## Problem

Gradient ascent (GA) is a natural inverse of gradient descent for machine unlearning, but it is limited in practice because the gradients of well-memorized data diminish after training, making GA's unlearning effect weak. Existing approximate MU methods struggle to balance unlearning efficacy with computational cost, and no principled mechanism has been identified to reliably boost gradient-based unlearning without incurring retraining-level costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260917/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an almost-converged model (ResNet-18 for vision tasks, BERT for text), a forgetting dataset D_f, and a retained dataset D_r, and produces a single unlearned model. The forgetting and retained sets are consumed jointly in a mixed-gradient fashion: each mini-batch pairs a batch from D_r with an equally-sized batch from D_f, and the smoothing rate α can be set globally or computed per-sample adaptively from feature-space distances. The output model is evaluated on unlearning accuracy, membership inference attack score, remaining accuracy, testing accuracy, and runtime efficiency.

## Method

UGradSL augments gradient ascent with negative label smoothing (NLS, α < 0) on the forgetting data, creating a mixed-gradient loss that applies gradient descent to retained samples and gradient ascent to NLS-smoothed forgetting samples, weighted by a ratio p. The key insight is that NLS in the GA step is mathematically equivalent to performing gradient descent with positive label smoothing on non-target labels, which drives the model to assign equally low confidence to all classes on forgetting data, improving deniability. A self-adaptive variant assigns per-sample smoothing rates based on the fraction of retained features within a distance threshold β in the model's feature space, so samples entangled with retained data are unlearned more conservatively. Theoretical analysis proves that NLS reduces the gap to the exact retrained model under a verifiable inner-product condition and yields an ε-Label-LDP guarantee whose privacy budget tightens as α becomes more negative.

## Results

On ImageNet class-wise forgetting, UGradSL achieves the lowest Avg. Gap of 2.23% (vs. 11.43% for GA and 23.25% for FT) with a runtime of only 0.01 hr compared to 26.18 hr for full retraining. On CIFAR-100 class-wise forgetting, UGradSL+ attains an Avg. Gap of 0.64%, nearly matching the retrained model. For random forgetting on CIFAR-100, UGradSL+ achieves the best Avg. Gap of 3.75% while UGradSL provides the optimal gap–runtime trade-off. Across all eight datasets and three unlearning paradigms (class-wise, random, group), UGradSL/UGradSL+ consistently outperform all 12 baselines on Avg. Gap without degrading remaining or testing accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGradSL+ dominates on CIFAR-100 class-wise forgetting with an Avg. Gap of 0.64%, beating the closest competitor PABI (0.83%) and SalUN (1.02%), while GA (10.36%) and FT (43.12%) are far behind. On ImageNet, UGradSL achieves the best Avg. Gap of 2.23% at a negligible 0.01 hr runtime, outperforming GA (11.43%), FT (23.25%), ℓ₁-sparse (14.39%), and RL (4.05%) by wide margins. For random forgetting on CIFAR-100, UGradSL+ attains the best Avg. Gap of 3.75%, while UGradSL offers the best gap–runtime trade-off. On CelebA group forgetting, UGradSL+ achieves the lowest Avg. Gap of 0.81%, surpassing GLI (1.69%) and UGradSL (1.61%). The only notable trade-off is runtime: UGradSL+ is ~3–4 min on CIFAR-100 (vs. 0.06 min for GA) but still ~8× faster than retraining (26.95 min), and PABI, while competitive on CIFAR-100, requires 20.09 min and was not evaluated on ImageNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes label smoothing—a standard training-time regularization (Szegedy et al., 2016)—as an unlearning mechanism by applying negative label smoothing (α < 0) within gradient ascent, a technique previously shown to be insufficient on its own (Thudi et al., 2021). The theoretical analysis is grounded in the influence function framework (Koh & Liang, 2017), which the authors extend to characterize when GA achieves exact unlearning (Theorem 1) and how the smoothing term corrects the residual gap (Theorem 2). The enhanced variant UGradSL+ builds on fine-tuning-based unlearning (Warnecke et al., 2021) by combining gradient descent on retained data with smoothed gradient ascent on forgetting data in a mixed-gradient scheme. A novel contribution is the formal connection between negative label smoothing and local differential privacy for labels (Label-LDP, Theorem 3), linking the unlearning mechanism to a privacy guarantee not previously established for gradient-based MU methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of UGradSL/UGradSL+ makes it immediately applicable to existing gradient-based unlearning pipelines with minimal computational overhead, and the self-adaptive smoothing rate removes a key hyperparameter tuning burden. However, the theoretical guarantee in Theorem 2 requires an inner-product condition (⟨Δθ_r − Δθ_f, Δθ_n − Δθ_f⟩ ≤ 0) that is not verified empirically, and the method still requires access to the forgetting data, limiting its applicability in settings where only a deletion request is available. The Label-LDP connection is theoretically elegant but the privacy budget ε depends on the ratio γ₁/γ₂, which is not directly controllable in practice. Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative models, federated settings, or scenarios where the forgetting set is not explicitly available.
