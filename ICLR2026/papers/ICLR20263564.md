## Problem

Prior diverse nearest neighbor search methods enforce diversity through hard quota constraints (e.g., at most k' vectors per attribute), which require an ad hoc parameter and cannot adapt to query intent—e.g., a 'blue shirt' query should return mostly blue items, while a generic 'shirts' query should favor color diversity. This paper introduces welfare-based objectives from mathematical economics that balance relevance and diversity in a query-dependent manner without fixed constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263564/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query vector q ∈ R^d and a database P of high-dimensional vectors (up to ~10M points, dimensions 96–1536), where each vector carries one or more categorical attributes from a set [c]. It returns a size-k subset S ⊆ P maximizing Nash social welfare (geometric mean of per-attribute cumulative similarities, smoothed by η > 0) or, more generally, p-mean welfare. In the single-attribute setting vectors partition into disjoint attribute classes D_ℓ; in the multi-attribute setting each vector may belong to multiple D_ℓ, and diversity is measured per attribute class.

## Method

The core insight is to treat the c attributes as c agents in a welfare-economics framework: each selected vector contributes its similarity to the utility of its attribute(s), and the objective is the Nash social welfare (geometric mean of utilities). For the single-attribute setting, a greedy algorithm (Nash-ANN) iteratively picks the vector yielding the largest marginal gain in log NSW, and is proven optimal (Theorem 1); it composes with any standard ANN oracle (e.g., DiskANN) as a subroutine to achieve α-approximate solutions in sublinear time (Corollary 2). For the multi-attribute setting, the problem is NP-hard, but log NSW is shown to be monotone submodular, yielding a (1−1/e)-approximation via greedy selection (Theorem 4). The p-mean welfare generalization (p ∈ (−∞, 1]) provides a continuous tunable parameter: p = 1 recovers standard NNS (pure relevance), p → 0 recovers NSW, and p → −∞ approaches egalitarian welfare (pure diversity), letting practitioners dial the relevance–diversity trade-off per application.

## Results

Across Amazon, Deep1b, Sift1m, and ArXiv datasets (k=50), Nash-ANN maintains an approximation ratio (relevance) above 0.9 while achieving entropy (diversity) comparable to the most diverse hard-constraint setting (Div-ANN with k'=1). In the multi-attribute setting on Sift1m, Multi Nash-ANN achieves approximation ratio above 0.9 with significantly higher entropy than ANN, whereas Multi Div-ANN requires k' as high as 30 to match that relevance level (k'<15 drops below 0.8). The p-mean parameter p smoothly interpolates: p=1 recovers standard ANN (approximation ratio ≈ 1, lowest entropy), while p=−10 yields the highest entropy at the cost of lower relevance. The hard-constrained baseline can suffer approximation ratios as low as 0.2 for aggressive diversity settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Div-ANN (Anand et al., ICML 2025), Nash-ANN Pareto dominates on both relevance and diversity: on Amazon it outperforms Div-ANN with k'=2, and on Deep1b-(Clus) it outperforms Div-ANN with k'=5, achieving higher entropy at equal or better approximation ratio. In the multi-attribute setting, Multi Nash-ANN substantially outperforms Multi Div-ANN, which needs k'=30 to reach comparable approximation ratio while still yielding lower entropy; for k'<15, Multi Div-ANN's approximation ratio drops below 0.8. Against the standard DiskANN baseline (Subramanya et al., NeurIPS 2019), Nash-ANN matches its approximation ratio (both near 1.0) while delivering significantly higher entropy, demonstrating that the welfare-based objective adds diversity essentially for free in terms of relevance. The p-mean extension further shows that p=1 exactly recovers DiskANN's behavior, confirming the formulation generalizes the standard NNS objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the constraint-based diversity formulation of Anand et al. (2025), which enforced fixed per-attribute quotas (at most k' vectors per attribute) on top of standard ANN, by replacing hard constraints with welfare-function objectives drawn from mathematical economics (Moulin, 2004). The key conceptual move is equating attributes with agents and applying Nash social welfare (geometric mean of utilities) or more general p-mean welfare as the optimization criterion, yielding a query-adaptive balance between relevance and diversity. For the multi-attribute setting, the paper leverages the classical Nemhauser-Wolsey-Fisher (1978) submodular maximization framework to obtain a (1−1/e) approximation, and the single-attribute greedy algorithm is designed to compose with any existing ANN method (e.g., DiskANN) as a subroutine.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The welfare-based formulation provides a principled, axiomatic foundation for diversity in retrieval (satisfying symmetry, Pigou-Dalton, scale invariance) that was absent in prior ad-hoc constraint approaches, and the p-mean parameter gives practitioners a single tunable knob interpolating between pure relevance (p=1) and pure diversity (p→−∞). The query-adaptive behavior—selecting all-blue results for 'blue shirt' but diverse colors for 'shirts'—addresses a practical limitation of fixed quotas that cannot be resolved without per-query tuning. Open questions include designing sublinear-time algorithms that optimize welfare objectives natively within ANN graph traversals (rather than as a post-hoc selection step), and extending the framework to settings without explicit categorical attributes.
