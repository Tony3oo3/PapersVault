## Problem

Existing probabilistic forecasting models for irregular time series, such as ProFITi, produce joint distributions that violate marginalization consistency: the marginal distribution of a subset of variables obtained by integrating the joint differs from the distribution predicted directly for that subset. This inconsistency renders models mathematically incoherent (violating Kolmogorov's Extension Theorem) and leads to contradictory predictions in practice, while consistent alternatives like Gaussian Process Regression are restricted to Gaussian marginals and lack expressiveness.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a variable-length sequence of (timestamp, channel-ID, value) triplets representing irregularly sampled observations, plus a variable-length query sequence of (timestamp, channel-ID) pairs specifying future time points and channels to predict. Both the number of observations N and queries K vary per instance, and all query times must postdate all observation times. The output is a full joint probability density over the K queried values, from which any subset marginal can be extracted analytically (by selecting rows/columns of the covariance and applying the corresponding univariate spline), guaranteeing consistency without numerical integration.

## Method

MOSES (Mixtures of Separable Flows) parametrizes the conditional joint distribution as a mixture of D normalizing flows, where each component applies a univariate Linear Rational Spline transformation independently to each variable of a multivariate Gaussian base distribution. The Gaussian mean and covariance are computed via separable linear/quadratic functions of per-query encodings (GP-style), so dependencies between variables are captured entirely by the base distribution rather than the flow. The encoder uses self-attention over observations and cross-attention for queries, producing per-component, per-variable embeddings; the spline parameters are shared across all variables and all mixture components, enabling the model to handle arbitrary query sizes. Mixture weights are computed via cross-attention over the observation encoding only (not queries), preserving permutation invariance. The key theoretical result is that separable flows combined with a marginalization-consistent base distribution yield a model that is consistent by construction (Lemma 3.1), and mixtures of such components preserve consistency (Lemma 3.2). Training minimizes normalized joint negative log-likelihood with the Adam optimizer.

## Results

On the primary joint prediction metric (njNLL), MOSES achieves the best scores among all marginalization-consistent models across four real-world datasets (USHCN, PhysioNet'12, MIMIC-III, MIMIC-IV), reaching −3.357 on USHCN and −0.305 on MIMIC-III, while remaining within 0.1–0.11 of the inconsistent ProFITi baseline. On the marginal prediction metric (mNLL), MOSES significantly outperforms ProFITi on all four datasets (e.g., −0.634 vs. +0.500 on MIMIC-IV; 0.163 vs. 0.408 on MIMIC-III), demonstrating that marginalization consistency translates into substantially more reliable univariate forecasts. MOSES also achieves near-zero marginalization inconsistency (MI) values, confirming its theoretical guarantee empirically.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ProFITi (the strongest inconsistent baseline), MOSES trades a small njNLL gap on medical datasets (e.g., −0.305 vs. −0.377 on MIMIC-III) for a large mNLL advantage (0.163 vs. 0.408 on MIMIC-III; −0.634 vs. +0.500 on MIMIC-IV), confirming that ProFITi's non-separable architecture produces contradictory marginal predictions. Against the consistent univariate baselines (GRU-ODE, NeuralFlows, CRU, Tripletformer+), MOSES dominates by a wide margin on both njNLL and mNLL across all datasets (e.g., −0.491 vs. 0.496–1.057 on PhysioNet'12 njNLL), because it models true joint dependencies rather than assuming independence. Against GPR, MOSES is substantially better on all datasets (e.g., −0.305 vs. 3.146 on MIMIC-III njNLL), as GPR is restricted to Gaussian outputs. Against GMM (MOSES without flows), MOSES improves njNLL from 1.063 to −0.491 on PhysioNet'12, showing the spline transformations add critical expressiveness for non-Gaussian marginals. The ablation in Table 4 further reveals that ProFITi's joint advantage largely stems from its encoder rather than its flow component: when MOSES uses ProFITi's GraFITi encoder, it outperforms ProFITi on njNLL for MIMIC-III (−0.396 vs. −0.377) and MIMIC-IV (−2.197 vs. −1.777).

## Relation to Prior Work

MOSES builds on the normalizing flow framework (Rezende & Mohamed, 2015) and the Gaussian copula process idea (Wilson & Ghahramani, 2010) of separating dependency structure from marginal distributions, but repurposes them for irregular time series with variable-length queries. It directly extends the authors' own ProFITi (AAAI 2025) by replacing its non-separable multivariate flows with separable univariate spline transformations on top of a multivariate Gaussian base, and by adding a mixture layer for expressiveness. The key conceptual departure is formalizing marginalization consistency (R3) as a first-class architectural requirement, which neither ProFITi nor other flow-based forecasting models (NeuralFlows, CRU) explicitly enforce, and proving that the separable-flow-plus-mixture construction satisfies it by design via Kolmogorov's Extension Theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a fundamental coherence flaw in a recently published flow-based forecasting model (ProFITi), showing that strong joint-distribution performance can coexist with contradictory marginal predictions—a problem with real consequences in safety-critical domains like ICU monitoring. The practical takeaway is that marginalization consistency can be enforced architecturally without sacrificing joint prediction quality, resolving what the authors frame as a false trade-off between correctness and expressiveness. Key limitations remain: the separability constraint restricts dependency modeling to the low-rank Gaussian covariance plus mixture, and mixture weights must be query-independent, which caps joint expressiveness. Open questions include whether more flexible consistent architectures (copula-based, probabilistic circuits) can close the remaining joint-prediction gap while preserving the consistency guarantee.
