## Problem

Decentralized nonconvex optimization under heavy-tailed gradient noise remains largely unexplored: existing decentralized methods assume bounded-variance noise, while the few prior works addressing heavy tails (Sun & Chen 2024; Yu et al. 2023) require strong convexity, bounded domains, or symmetric noise, and do not provide explicit optimal rates. This paper asks whether a decentralized algorithm can achieve the optimal non-asymptotic convergence rate O(1/T^{(p-1)/(3p-2)}) for general nonconvex smooth objectives under zero-mean noise with only a finite p-th moment (p∈(1,2]).

## Input / Output

Each node i holds a local nonconvex L-smooth function f_i and queries a stochastic first-order oracle returning g_i(x_i^t, ξ_i^t) with zero-mean noise satisfying E[‖g_i − ∇f_i‖^p | F_t] ≤ σ^p; nodes communicate only with neighbors via a primitive doubly stochastic mixing matrix W (which may be directed if weight-balanced). The algorithm outputs per-node iterates x_i^t whose average gradient norm (1/nT)Σ_t Σ_i E[‖∇f(x_i^t)‖] converges to zero, certifying a stationary point of the global objective f = (1/n)Σf_i. Notably, the step-size schedule depends on the spectral gap λ and node count n but not on the noise scale σ, and when p is unknown the rate becomes topology-independent for p<2.

## Method

GT-NSGDm combines three mechanisms: (1) local momentum variance reduction v_i^t = βv_i^{t-1} + (1−β)g_i(x_i^t, ξ_i^t) to suppress heavy-tailed fluctuations; (2) gradient tracking y_i^t = Σ_r w_ir(y_r^{t-1} + v_r^t − v_r^{t-1}) to eliminate dependence on cross-node function heterogeneity; and (3) a normalized peer-to-peer update x_i^{t+1} = Σ_r w_ir(x_r^t − α·y_r^t/‖y_r^t‖). The critical design choice is that normalization is applied exclusively in the x-update, not in the recursive v or y dynamics, thereby avoiding the nonlinearity that would otherwise break the tracking analysis. The paper proves a negative result (Claim 1) showing that vanilla normalized decentralized SGD without tracking or momentum can remain arbitrarily far from the optimum due to heterogeneous local gradient magnitudes, motivating the tracking-based correction. With known p, the method achieves the order-optimal rate O(1/T^{(p-1)/(3p-2)}) matching the centralized lower bound; with unknown p it attains O(1/T^{(p-1)/(2p)}) that is topology-independent and exhibits an n^{1−1/p} speedup for p∈(1,2).

## Results

On decentralized training of a 3M-parameter GPT model on Multi30k (8 nodes, 12 epochs, batch size 64), GT-NSGDm achieves validation log-perplexity of 0.258±0.007 (ring), 0.261±0.007 (directed exponential), and 0.282±0.009 (complete graph), nearly matching the best baseline DSGD-GClip (0.253, 0.249, 0.267) which lacks theoretical guarantees under heavy-tailed noise. In contrast, all baselines with prior theoretical guarantees for heavy-tailed noise (SClip-EF-Network, DSGD-Clip) or standard bounded-variance assumptions (DSGD, GT-DSGD) fail catastrophically, yielding log-perplexity above 5.3. On synthetic nonconvex regression with injected Lévy α-stable noise, GT-NSGDm converges to near-optimal solutions while DSGD and GT-DSGD become unstable and DSGD-Clip stalls far from the optimum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GT-NSGDm nearly matches DSGD-GClip (the best-performing baseline without heavy-tailed guarantees) across all three topologies, with gaps of only 0.005 (ring), 0.012 (exp), and 0.015 (complete) in log-perplexity, while being the only method with a proven convergence rate under heavy-tailed noise. Against DSGD and GT-DSGD, which assume bounded variance, GT-NSGDm wins by a factor of ~20× (0.258 vs. 5.633 on ring), as these methods diverge under the empirically observed heavy-tailed gradient noise in Transformer training. The two baselines with prior heavy-tailed guarantees—SClip-EF-Network (strongly convex, symmetric noise) and DSGD-Clip (strongly convex, bounded domain)—also fail completely on this nonconvex problem, achieving log-perplexity above 5.6, confirming that their restrictive assumptions do not transfer. GT-NSGDm outperforms GT-Adam (0.258 vs. 0.520) and QG-DSGDm (0.258 vs. 0.394) on the ring topology, both of which lack heavy-tailed noise guarantees. On the synthetic nonconvex regression task, GT-NSGDm and SClip-EF-Network are the only methods that converge under all three noise types (Gaussian, Student's t, Lévy α-stable), but GT-NSGDm reaches lower final error, while DSGD-Clip remains stable yet stalls far from the optimum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate threads: gradient tracking for decentralized nonconvex optimization (Di Lorenzo & Scutari, 2016), gradient normalization for heavy-tailed noise in centralized settings (Liu & Zhou, 2025), and momentum-based error feedback for heavy-tailed noise in decentralized settings (Yu et al., 2023). The key departure is showing that these components must be combined in a specific way—normalization applied only at the iterate update, not in the recursive gradient estimator or tracker—to overcome the nonlinearity that breaks standard decentralized analyses. A notable negative result (Claim 1) demonstrates that vanilla normalized decentralized SGD, without gradient tracking, can remain arbitrarily far from the optimum, establishing that the combination is not merely convenient but necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first result to match the centralized lower bound O(1/T^{(p-1)/(3p-2)}) for nonconvex decentralized optimization under heavy-tailed noise, closing a gap where prior decentralized methods (Sun & Chen, 2024; Yu et al., 2023) assumed strong convexity and provided suboptimal or unspecified rates. The topology-independent rate for p < 2 with an n^{1-1/p} speedup is practically relevant given empirical evidence that Transformer training exhibits heavy-tailed gradient noise. Open questions include extending the analysis to other nonlinearities (sign, clipping), relaxed smoothness conditions, and validating at larger scales—the current experiments use only 3M-parameter models and at most 20 nodes, leaving the practical impact at production scale untested.
