## Problem

Diffusion-based generative classifiers have demonstrated desirable properties such as distribution-shift robustness, but their reliance on ELBO-based likelihood estimation requires dozens to hundreds of forward passes per class, creating a severe scalability barrier for large-scale datasets. This paper investigates whether visual autoregressive (VAR) models, with their tractable closed-form likelihood, can serve as a practical and efficient backbone for generative classification while retaining useful properties beyond mere accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB image, tokenizes it via a VQ-VAE encoder into K multi-scale discrete token maps (r₁, …, r_K) with progressively increasing spatial resolution, and consumes these tokens rather than raw pixels. Classification output is a class label obtained by argmax over class-conditional likelihoods computed through a three-stage candidate pruning pipeline. Additionally, the model produces token-wise mutual information (TMI) maps that attribute each token's contribution to the predicted label, enabling pixel-level visual explanations without any auxiliary network.

## Method

The core mechanism exploits VAR's next-scale prediction to factorize the class-conditional likelihood p(x|y) into a product of per-scale token probabilities, yielding a tractable likelihood computable in a single forward pass. A-VARC introduces two efficiency-accuracy techniques: (i) likelihood smoothing, which averages the likelihood over S Gaussian-perturbed feature maps to counteract the discrete tokenization's sensitivity to small input changes, and (ii) partial-scale candidate pruning, which uses only the first K′ scales (e.g., 55 out of 680 tokens) for coarse filtering because early scales encode global structural information sufficient to discriminate visually distinct classes. A-VARC⁺ further applies Condition Contrastive Alignment (CCA) finetuning, a contrastive objective that increases likelihood under the ground-truth label while penalizing likelihood under a negative label, strengthening class-conditional information without degrading generation quality. The full inference pipeline runs three stages: partial-scale likelihood narrows 100 candidates to top-10, full likelihood refines to top-3, and smoothed likelihood produces the final prediction, achieving 89× less computation than the DiT-based diffusion classifier at comparable accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-100, A-VARC+ achieves 89.32% top-1 accuracy at 4,649.4 GFLOPs per image, matching the 2-stage DiT-based diffusion classifier (DC(25,250) at 90.30%) within less than 1% while requiring 89× less computational cost (4,649.4 vs. 415,056.0 GFLOPs). The naive VARC baseline scores 83.30% at 14,105.0 GFLOPs, so A-VARC+ gains 6.02% accuracy while reducing cost by ~3×. On distribution-shift benchmarks, A-VARC+ does not match the diffusion classifier's robustness (e.g., 34.0% vs. 53.7% on ImageNet-Sketch) and remains below strong discriminative models such as ViT-B/16 (94.20% on ImageNet-100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DiT-based diffusion classifier (Li et al., 2023), A-VARC+ trades a 0.98% top-1 accuracy drop (89.32% vs. 90.30%) for an 89× reduction in per-image computation (4,649 vs. 415,056 GFLOPs), making it the most practical generative classifier in the comparison. The rectified-flow variant DC-MF (Geng et al., 2025) performs far worse at 50.30% top-1, confirming that MeanFlow's sampling efficiency does not transfer to classification. Against the normalizing-flow baseline IBINN (Mackowiak et al., 2021), A-VARC+ gains 38.2% top-1 accuracy (89.32% vs. 51.12%) at roughly 500× the compute cost. Compared to discriminative ResNets (He et al., 2016) and ViTs (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020), A-VARC+ lags by 2.8–4.9% top-1 on ImageNet-100 and is substantially less efficient (4,649 vs. 1.8–16.9 GFLOPs), though it matches or exceeds them on ImageNet-A adversarial robustness. In the class-incremental learning experiment (Table 4), A-VARC+ achieves 77.4% average accuracy without rehearsal data, outperforming the CWR baseline (Lomonaco & Maltoni, 2017) at 72.4% and the diffusion classifier at 76.0%, while the unregularized ResNet-50 collapses to 41.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative classifier paradigm—previously instantiated with VAEs, normalizing flows, and diffusion models—to the visual autoregressive (VAR) backbone, which provides a tractable class-conditional likelihood via next-scale prediction, eliminating the need for ELBO-based likelihood approximation. It further incorporates Condition Contrastive Alignment (CCA) finetuning, originally proposed for guidance-free VAR image generation, to strengthen class-conditional information without sacrificing generative quality. The work also draws on the class-incremental learning literature (Van De Ven et al., 2021) and extends the NLP concept of pointwise mutual information to a token-wise visual explainability setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 89× reduction in inference cost relative to DiT-based diffusion classifiers makes generative classification practically feasible at scale, and the tractable likelihood unlocks a form of visual explainability (token-wise mutual information) unavailable to diffusion-based counterparts. However, the paper reveals that the distribution-shift robustness celebrated in diffusion-based generative classifiers does not transfer to VAR, suggesting that robustness may stem from the denoising training paradigm rather than the generative objective itself. An open question highlighted is the trade-off between generative and discriminative performance as model size grows: larger VAR models improve FID but degrade classification accuracy, pointing to a fundamental tension between modeling structural detail and preserving class-conditional signal.
