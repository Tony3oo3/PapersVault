## Problem

Existing all-in-one image restoration (AiOIR) methods are confined to a single image domain (natural, medical, or remote sensing) and primarily emphasize task differences while overlooking shared commonalities across tasks and domains. As the number of tasks and domains grows, these single-domain approaches face increasing learning difficulty and poor scalability. This paper tackles the first multi-domain AiOIR setting, unifying diverse restoration tasks across natural, medical, and remote sensing images within one model.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single degraded 3-channel RGB image as input without any task or domain label (blind restoration). Medical images originally in grayscale are replicated to 3 channels for uniformity. The output is a restored high-quality image of the same spatial resolution. Notably, the LLaVA-1.5-7B and CLIP text encoder used for domain-prompt distillation are only active during training, so inference incurs no additional overhead beyond the restoration backbone itself.

## Method

DATPRL-IR introduces a dual-prompt-pool architecture: a task prompt pool (15 key-value pairs) queried by middle-encoder features and a domain prompt pool (15 key-value pairs) queried by shallow first-layer features. For each input, top-k prompts are retrieved via cosine similarity and combined into instance-level representations through a Prompt Composition Mechanism (PCM) that applies softmax-weighted averaging over the selected values. Domain prompts are initialized and constrained by a cross-modal alignment loss that distills domain priors from LLaVA-generated text descriptions (content, color, viewpoint, brightness) encoded by CLIP. The task and domain representations are fused via cross-attention into a single domain-aware task prompt representation, which is injected into decoder layers through an Adaptive Gated Fusion (AGF) that learns a per-layer gating coefficient to balance backbone features against prompt guidance. Training is stabilized by diversity, entropy-balance, and contrastive regularization losses applied to both prompt pools to prevent prompt collapse and encourage balanced utilization.

## Results

On the 6-task, 3-domain setting, DATPRL-IR-6T achieves an average PSNR of 30.77 dB and SSIM of 0.8653, surpassing the best all-in-one baseline MoCEIR (30.40 dB / 0.8627) by 0.37 dB on average. The largest per-task gain is on natural image deraining (Rain100L), where DATPRL-IR-6T reaches 39.56 dB PSNR versus 38.64 dB for MoCEIR, a nearly 1 dB improvement. In the 9-task setting, DATPRL-IR-9T maintains or even slightly improves performance on the original six tasks (e.g., 39.67 dB on deraining, 28.31 dB on RSI SR) while also handling three additional tasks (deblurring, PET synthesis, RSI dehazing) without degradation, demonstrating strong scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DATPRL-IR-6T outperforms all 10 baselines across every one of the six tasks in the 3-domain setting. Against the strongest all-in-one baseline MoCEIR (CVPR 2025), it gains +0.37 dB average PSNR, with the largest margin on natural deraining (+0.92 dB: 39.56 vs. 38.64) and natural SR (+0.82 dB: 28.98 vs. 28.16). Against the best single-task baselines, DATPRL-IR-6T exceeds Restormer (CVPR 2022) by +0.35 dB on average and NAFNet (ECCV 2022) by +0.66 dB, indicating that the multi-domain prompt design does not sacrifice per-task fidelity. PromptIR (NeurIPS 2023), the leading prompt-based AiOIR method, is surpassed by +0.33 dB on average, with a notable +0.85 dB gap on deraining. In the 9-task setting, DATPRL-IR-9T further extends its lead over MoCEIR to +0.39 dB on average while adding three new tasks (deblurring, PET synthesis, RSI dehazing) without degrading the original six, whereas all other baselines show no such scalability. On zero-shot tasks (specular highlight removal, image desnowing) and unseen domains (AIGC, comic images), DATPRL-IR also leads all compared methods by 0.45–0.92 dB PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the all-in-one image restoration (AiOIR) line of work—exemplified by PromptIR, MoCEIR, and AdaIR—which has so far been confined to a single image domain (natural, medical, or remote sensing). It repurposes the prompt-pool mechanism from L2P (continual learning) into a dual-pool design (task + domain prompts) and introduces a novel cross-modal alignment loss that distills domain priors from LLaVA into learnable domain prompts via CLIP text features. The prompt composition mechanism (PCM) and adaptive gated fusion (AGF) are new architectural components that go beyond the single-prompt-per-task or MoE routing strategies used in prior AiOIR methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single model can handle 9 restoration tasks across 3 domains without performance degradation (and even with slight gains as tasks increase), the paper provides empirical evidence that shared latent structure across tasks and domains is exploitable, which could guide future unified restoration architectures. The zero-shot results on completely unseen tasks (desnowing, specular highlight removal) and unseen domains (AIGC, comic) suggest the learned prompt representations capture transferable priors rather than dataset-specific patterns. Key open questions include whether the approach scales beyond three domains, how to reduce the elevated training cost, and whether the MLLM-distilled domain prompts can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives for deployment.
