## Problem

Diffusion Transformers scale quadratically with sequence length, making large-scale pretraining prohibitively expensive. While token dropping can reduce this cost, naive strategies degrade representations, and existing efficient-training methods either add substantial parameters or break down at aggressive drop ratios (e.g., 75%). No prior approach simultaneously supports high-ratio token dropping, preserves semantic quality, and remains compatible with representation-alignment losses like REPA.

## Input / Output

The model consumes noisy latent images (32×32 or 64×64 from SD/FLUX VAEs, patchified into 2×2 tokens), a class label, and a diffusion timestep, and outputs a full-resolution velocity field over all tokens. During training, 75% of tokens are dropped before the middle blocks, yet the decoder must still predict velocities for every spatial position. At inference, Path-Drop Guidance computes the unconditional velocity by bypassing the middle blocks entirely, halving per-step FLOPs.

## Method

SPRINT partitions a standard DiT into a 2-block encoder f_θ, a deep middle section g_θ, and a 2-block decoder h_θ. The encoder processes all tokens to produce dense shallow features; a structured group-wise subsampling (2×2 groups, keep 1 token per group) then drops 75% of tokens before g_θ, forcing the middle blocks to model only sparse global semantics. The sparse output is padded with [MASK] tokens to restore full length, concatenated channel-wise with the dense shallow features, projected back, and fed to the decoder. Training follows a two-stage schedule: long masked pre-training at 75% drop ratio followed by a short (100K–200K iteration) full-token fine-tuning to close the train-inference gap. The dual-path design also enables Path-Drop Guidance, where the unconditional CFG pass replaces g_θ with [MASK] tokens, using only the dense shallow path as a natural weaker network.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256² class-conditional generation, SPRINT at 400 epochs achieves FID 1.96 and FDD 75.4 using only 65.1M training TFLOPs (53% of the 122.2M for the improved SiT-XL baseline). With Path-Drop Guidance (PDG) at 400 epochs, SPRINT further reduces inference FLOPs to 0.263 (57% of standard) while improving to FID 1.55 and FDD 54.9, surpassing the original SiT-XL (FID 2.06 at 1400 epochs) and REPA (FID 1.80 at 800 epochs). At 512² resolution, SPRINT+PDG achieves FID 1.96 with 184.8M training TFLOPs versus 366.6M for SiT-XL (FID 2.62). Training speed reaches 5.2 iters/sec (2× faster than SiT's 2.5) with 33.8% lower VRAM at 256².

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPRINT decisively outperforms all token-dropping baselines at the same 75% drop ratio: at 400K iterations it improves FDD by +88.5 over SiT-XL/2, +90.2 over Progressive Training, +90.2 over MicroDiT, and +90.2 over Tread (Table 1, SD-VAE). Against the strongest dense baseline, SPRINT at 200 epochs matches SiT-XL at 1400 epochs (FID 2.01 vs 2.06) using only 10% of the training FLOPs, and at 400 epochs surpasses it (FID 1.96 vs 2.04) at 53% of the cost. With PDG, SPRINT at 400 epochs beats MDTv2-XL (FID 1.55 vs 1.86), MaskDiT (1.55 vs 2.28), Tread (1.55 vs 2.09), and DiT-XL (1.55 vs 2.27) while using 27–90% fewer training FLOPs. Combined with REPA, SPRINT+PDG at 400 epochs surpasses the official REPA model trained for 800 epochs by 17.8 FDD and 0.21 FID (1.49 vs 1.80) at only 27% of the training compute. SPRINT also generalizes to U-ViT, improving FDD by +63.4 and FID by +2.9 over the U-ViT baseline at 400K iterations, confirming architecture-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPRINT builds directly on the SiT/DiT architecture (Ma et al., 2024; Peebles & Xie, 2023) but departs from prior token-dropping methods (MicroDiT, Tread, MaskDiT, MDTv2) by introducing an explicit sparse-dense residual fusion that assigns complementary roles to shallow and deep layers, rather than simply removing tokens and relying on auxiliary losses or patch-mixing modules to compensate. Unlike alignment-based accelerators like REPA, SPRINT operates purely through architectural reformulation and a two-stage training schedule, making it orthogonal and combinable with such methods. The Path-Drop Guidance inference technique is a novel byproduct of the dual-path design, repurposing the dense shallow path as a natural 'weaker network' for the unconditional branch, analogous in spirit to Auto Guidance but without requiring a separate model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 9.8× reduction in training FLOPs while matching or exceeding baseline quality, achieved with only a single linear projection layer (~0.3% parameter overhead), makes large-scale DiT pretraining substantially more accessible. The method's architecture-agnosticism (validated on SiT, U-ViT, REPA across SD and FLUX VAEs at 256² and 512²) suggests broad applicability, and PDG's near-50% inference savings with improved quality is a practical win for deployment. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to class-conditional ImageNet (no text-to-image or video), and the 87.5% drop ratio failure suggests a hard ceiling on sparsity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-billion-parameter models, video diffusion where temporal tokens compound the quadratic cost, and whether the structured group-wise subsampling generalizes to non-grid token topologies.
