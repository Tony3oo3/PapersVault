## Problem

Despite transformers achieving state-of-the-art performance on time series forecasting, the field lacks rigorous theoretical understanding of why they work well, with existing analyses relying predominantly on heuristic reasoning. This paper specifically asks what architectural characteristics of MOIRAI—a transformer-based model that handles arbitrary numbers of covariates—enable its strong empirical performance, and whether its novel design choices (any-variate encoding and any-variate attention) are theoretically justified.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series x ∈ R^{d×T} with arbitrary number of covariates d and length T, which is flattened via any-variate encoding into a 1D sequence of length dT with appended time-index and variate one-hot identifier rows, forming an input matrix H ∈ R^{D×d·T}. The output is the next time step prediction of the target variate x_{T+1}^1, read from the first entry of the last column of the transformer output. The number of covariates d and lag order q must be bounded by d_max and q_max, which are determined by the hidden dimension D; exceeding these bounds leads to graceful degradation rather than complete failure.

## Method

The paper proves that MOIRAI's any-variate attention (with learnable bias terms u₁, u₂ that create block-diagonal attention masks) enables variate-wise parallel operations, allowing a single attention layer to construct lag-feature matrices A_i(q) for each covariate simultaneously (Lemma 3.4). Building on this, Theorem 3.6 shows that an L-layer MOIRAI transformer fits an AR_d(q) model via in-context learning: the first L₁ layers reformat the flattened input into stacked feature-label pairs, and the remaining L₂ layers simulate gradient descent on the least squares objective, with approximation error decaying exponentially in L. For generalization, the paper derives the first pretraining bound for MOIRAI under Dobrushin's uniqueness condition (Theorem 4.4), showing test error scales as O(1/√(nT)) where n is the number of pretraining time series and T is their length, without requiring i.i.d. assumptions on the data.

## Results

On synthetic AR data (d∈{3,4,5}, q=5, σ²=1), MOIRAI's MSE decreases with input length T in a manner closely matching least squares regression (LSR), validating Theorem 3.6 that each layer performs one gradient descent step. Since the test noise variance is σ²=1, the expected MSE converges toward 1 as the lookback window grows. In the generalization experiment (pretrained on d∈{4,5}, q∈{4,5}), MOIRAI successfully transfers to unseen configurations: d=2 (lower dimension), d=10 (higher dimension), and (d=3, q=7) (unseen lag), with the d=10 case showing better sample complexity than LSR despite never having seen 10 covariates during pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MOIRAI against three baselines on synthetic AR data: (1) Least Squares Regression (LSR) with known q, (2) Standard Transformer (same any-variate encoding but standard attention instead of any-variate attention), and (3) MOIRAI-relu (MOIRAI with ReLU replacing Softmax in attention). MOIRAI's predictive error tracks LSR closely across all tested values of d and input length T, confirming the theoretical equivalence. Standard Transformer achieves similar qualitative behavior but exhibits consistently higher MSE than both MOIRAI and LSR, demonstrating the advantage of any-variate attention for multivariate processing. MOIRAI-relu shows a negligible performance gap compared to full MOIRAI, supporting the theoretical use of ReLU in the analysis. In the generalization setting with d=10 (unseen during pretraining), pretrained MOIRAI outperforms LSR in sample efficiency, requiring fewer input steps to reach comparable MSE, while for d=2 all methods perform well. When both d and q are simultaneously unseen (d=3, q=7), MOIRAI still produces accurate predictions, whereas LSR would require re-estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context learning theory of Bai et al. (2024), which proved that standard transformers can simulate gradient descent on fixed-dimension feature-label pairs, to the time series setting where the number of covariates is arbitrary and data is inherently non-iid. It specifically analyzes MOIRAI's any-variate attention and any-variate encoding (Woo et al., 2024), showing that these design choices are not merely heuristic but are theoretically necessary to enable parallel variate-wise operations that allow the transformer to fit AR models of arbitrary dimension. The generalization analysis introduces Dobrushin's condition as a regularity assumption for non-iid time series, departing from the i.i.d. assumption in prior ICL generalization results and from mixing conditions used in classical time series learning theory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous theoretical justification for why MOIRAI and similar any-variate transformer architectures succeed at multivariate time series forecasting, providing practitioners with a principled understanding of which architectural components matter and why. The pretraining generalization bound under Dobrushin's condition (decaying as 1/√(nT)) offers a formal foundation for the large-scale pretraining paradigm in time series foundation models. Key limitations include the use of ReLU instead of softmax in attention, focus on linear AR processes (though non-linear AR is addressed in the appendix), and the fact that Dobrushin's condition is difficult to verify in practice. Open questions include extending the analysis to architectures like iTransformer and CrossFormer that apply attention along the covariate dimension, and relaxing the Dobrushin assumption to broader dependency structures.
