## Problem

Discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs) can sample multiple tokens per step, but existing strategies underutilize the model's predictions: full-step sampling generates only one token per step, while confidence threshold sampling reveals only tokens whose predicted probability exceeds a fixed threshold, leaving many correctly-predicted tokens unmasked. The paper identifies that at each step the dLLM can correctly predict a large number of tokens far exceeding what threshold-based methods capture, creating an order-of-magnitude gap between achievable and actual sampling efficiency.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a text prompt and a pre-trained dLLM, and at each sampling step the neural indicator receives per-position features for all M masked positions: embeddings of the top-K₁ predicted tokens, last-layer hidden states, and top-K₂ logits. It outputs M binary scores, and all positions with score above a tunable threshold ε_φ are simultaneously revealed. The indicator is a lightweight MLP (~84M params vs. ~8B for the dLLM) trained once on ShareGPT-generated trajectories and reused across all tasks and generation lengths without retraining.

## Method

NI Sampling introduces a position-wise MLP indicator that makes token-wise binary decisions on which masked positions to reveal at each step, replacing fixed confidence thresholds with a learned criterion. The key training signal comes from a trajectory-preserving objective: given a reference trajectory, a masked position is labeled positive if the model's current prediction at that position matches the reference and all preceding positions in the reference trajectory are also correctly predicted (i.e., the step can be merged without altering the output). This is formalized via an iterative step-merging algorithm (Alg. 1) that checks whether subsequent steps' tokens are already correctly predicted. The indicator is trained with cross-entropy loss on 204k trajectories generated at lengths 128/256/512, and at inference it is combined with an existing sampling strategy (e.g., confidence threshold) to guarantee at least one token is revealed per step. The approach achieves up to 14.3× speedup over full-step sampling with negligible accuracy loss, and Pareto-dominates confidence threshold sampling across all accuracy-step trade-off settings.

## Results

On LLaDA-8B-Instruct, NI Sampling achieves up to 13.8× speedup over full-step sampling (263.9 vs 19.1 token/s on MBPP-512) with negligible accuracy degradation (38.00% vs 36.80%). On LLaDA-1.5, the peak speedup reaches 14.3× (268.0 vs 18.7 token/s on MBPP-512). Across all datasets and generation lengths, NI Sampling consistently reduces the average number of sampling steps by 3–10× while maintaining accuracy within ~0.5% of full-step sampling. Combined with dual KV caching, NI Sampling achieves up to 25.0× end-to-end acceleration (360.6 token/s on GSM8K-512).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against full-step sampling (the default LLaDA top-1 probability strategy), NI Sampling achieves 3.8–14.3× speedup in token/s across all datasets with accuracy drops of ≤0.5% (e.g., GSM8K-256: 77.18% vs 77.63%), and in several cases actually improves accuracy (MBPP-512: 38.00% vs 36.80%; HumanEval-512: 35.98% vs 35.37%). Against confidence threshold sampling (Fast-dLLM, Wu et al. 2025), NI Sampling Pareto-dominates across all settings: it achieves up to 2.2× higher throughput (219.3 vs 100.8 token/s on HumanEval-512) while matching or exceeding accuracy, and its accuracy–step trade-off curve strictly dominates the threshold method's curve at every operating point. Against Token-Critic (Lezama et al., 2022), which also uses an auxiliary network for token selection, NI Sampling substantially outperforms on GSM8K (71.95% vs 53.83% accuracy at comparable step counts of ~27–29), suggesting that trajectory-preserving supervision is far more effective than the original masked-token prediction objective for language generation. On the Dream-7B-Base model (initialized from an AR LLM), NI Sampling still outperforms confidence threshold sampling (e.g., 3.4× vs 2.6× speedup on MATH-256), confirming generality across model families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Fast-dLLM (Wu et al., 2025), which introduced confidence threshold sampling as a heuristic for parallel token decoding in masked diffusion language models, and on the broader dLLM sampling literature (top-1 probability, top-1 entropy, top-1 margin). It departs from these fixed-threshold or single-token heuristics by formalizing token sampling order as an explicit optimization problem and learning a lightweight neural indicator to make token-wise binary decisions. Conceptually, it is closest to Token-Critic (Lezama et al., 2022a) for image MDMs, which also uses an auxiliary network to decide unmasking, but differs in using a trajectory-preserving training objective rather than a remasking criterion, and in targeting language models where the goal is step reduction rather than quality improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As dLLMs enter production systems (Mercury, Gemini Diffusion, Seed Diffusion), inference efficiency is a critical bottleneck, and this paper provides a general, model-agnostic acceleration technique that achieves 14.3× speedup with negligible accuracy loss and Pareto-dominates confidence threshold sampling across all tested settings. A key open question is the substantial gap between the theoretical upper bound (~24× via trajectory-preserving order) and the achieved speedup (~14×), which the authors attribute to the indicator's tendency to default to autoregressive-like schedules and insufficient model capacity. Future directions include replacing the position-wise MLP with a Transformer that makes joint decisions across positions, scaling training data, and formulating the indicator as an RL agent with task-specific reward functions.
