## Problem

Existing regret bounds for universal prediction of stochastic sequences (Merhav & Feder, 1998; Hutter, 2003) hold only in expectation, leaving open whether vanishing regret guarantees can be established with high probability. This paper asks whether high-probability regret bounds of comparable form to the known in-expectation bounds can be derived for the general non-i.i.d. stochastic sequence prediction problem, and whether the dependence on the failure probability δ can be made tight.

## Input / Output

The learner receives past observations Z₁,…,Z_{t−1} from a sequence following an unknown joint distribution P over a measurable space (Z, G), along with a known parametrized family (P_θ)_{θ∈Θ} and a bounded loss ℓ: B×Z→[0,L]. At each step it outputs a prediction B_t ∈ B determined by a policy optimal under a universal measure Q = ∫ P_θ w(dθ). The paper's output is a high-probability bound on the average per-round regret Δ = (1/T)Σ(ℓ(B_t,Z_t) − ℓ(B_t*,Z_t)), where B_t* is the optimal prediction under full knowledge of P, holding with probability at least 1−δ over the draw of the sequence.

## Method

The core technique analyzes a simplified 'mismatched prediction' problem where the learner's policy is optimal under a known approximation Q of the true P. The key insight is to construct a supermartingale Δ'_t = Σ(ℓ(B_{t'},Z_{t'}) − ℓ(B_{t'}*,Z_{t'}) − 2Lv_{t'}) where v_t is the instantaneous total-variation distance between the conditional kernels of P and Q, then apply the Azuma-Hoeffding inequality to obtain a high-probability bound involving the random variable V̂_T (Lemma 2). Markov's inequality is then used to replace V̂_T with its expectation V_T, yielding the final bound Δ < 4LV_T/δ + 2√2L/√T · √(log(2/δ)) with probability ≥ 1−δ (Theorem 4). For universal prediction, combining with Hutter's construction (D(P‖Q) ≤ −log w({θ₀}) for countable Θ) gives an O(T^{-1/2}δ^{-1/2}) rate. An impossibility theorem (Theorem 5) shows the δ^{-1/2} factor in the KL-divergence-based bound is tight: no bound of the same form can improve the exponent of δ without additional assumptions.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4) establishes that the average per-round regret Δ satisfies Δ < 4LV_T/δ + 2√2L/√T · √(log(2/δ)) with probability at least 1−δ, yielding a convergence rate of O(T^{-1/2}δ^{-1/2}) for countable alphabets. This matches the prior in-expectation rate of O(T^{-1/2}) (Merhav & Feder, 1998) up to the δ^{-1/2} factor. An impossibility result (Theorem 5) proves that the δ^{-1/2} exponent cannot be improved without additional assumptions. In the numerical experiment (Markov chain, m=3, S=2 states, 4000 runs), the mean regret approaches 0 for long sequences, while the high-probability quantiles converge more slowly, consistent with the theoretical δ^{-1/2} penalty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between the new high-probability regret bounds and the classical in-expectation bounds of Merhav & Feder (1998). The paper's Theorem 4 achieves the same O(T^{-1/2}) convergence rate in T as the prior expectation bound (Corollary 1: E[Δ] ≤ L√(D(P||Q)/2T)), but at the cost of an additional δ^{-1/2} factor in the tail probability. The impossibility result (Theorem 5) demonstrates that this δ^{-1/2} (or equivalently 1/δ for the variational-distance form) is tight: no bound of the same form can replace it with a sub-polynomial-in-δ expression without extra assumptions. The paper does not compare against competing high-probability methods, as it explicitly states that no prior works study high-probability bounds for non-i.i.d. stochastic sequences without expert advice. The numerical experiment in Section 7 (Markov chain with m=3, S=2) empirically confirms that quantile regret converges more slowly than mean regret, qualitatively matching the theoretical δ^{-1/2} penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal prediction framework of Merhav & Feder (1998) and Hutter (2003), which established in-expectation regret bounds for sequential prediction of stochastic sequences, by deriving the first high-probability regret bounds for the same setting. The technical route follows the mismatched prediction stepping stone from Merhav & Feder, but replaces the expectation-based analysis with a supermartingale construction and Azuma-Hoeffding concentration (Lemma 2), then applies Markov's inequality to eliminate the unobservable instantaneous variational distance. The impossibility result (Theorem 5) departs from prior work by proving that the resulting 1/δ or 1/√δ scaling in the final bound is tight, closing a gap that prior in-expectation analyses left open.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

High-probability guarantees are essential for safety-critical applications (air traffic accident prediction, autonomous driving trajectory prediction, sepsis prediction) where in-expectation bounds provide insufficient reliability. The impossibility result clarifies that the polynomial-in-δ penalty is inherent to the general setting, directing future work toward identifying structural assumptions (e.g., log-concavity, i.i.d. structure) that could recover the logarithmic δ-scaling visible in the intermediate supermartingale bound. A key limitation is that the Markov inequality step introduces the worse δ-dependence, and the practical utility of the universal prediction framework depends on constructing a suitable parametric family, which may be intractable for large or continuous state spaces.
