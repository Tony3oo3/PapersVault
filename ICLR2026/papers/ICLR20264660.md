## Problem

State-of-the-art audio-visual speech separation (AVSS) models achieve strong separation quality but incur prohibitive computational costs due to large pretrained video backbones and multi-iteration audio separators, making them impractical for real-world deployment where speech separation is a preprocessing step. Lightweight alternatives reduce parameters but either rely on iterative inference (adding latency) or sacrifice semantic alignment in the visual encoder, creating a persistent performance–efficiency trade-off that has not been resolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264660/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 2-second mixture audio waveform at 16 kHz (containing the target speaker plus up to C interferers and noise) and a synchronized lip video of the target speaker (88×88 grayscale frames at 25 FPS, ~50 frames per clip) as input, and produces the separated target speech waveform in the time domain. The video is consumed by a frozen pretrained encoder that outputs two flattened feature vectors per temporal frame (reconstruction tokens and discrete semantic tokens), while the audio is encoded by a single 1D convolution into 256-channel features; all downstream processing operates in the time domain rather than the time-frequency domain.

## Method

Dolphin combines two key components: (1) DP-LipCoder, a dual-path lightweight video encoder adapted from MagVIT that extracts reconstruction-related features (preserving spatio-temporal structure) and audio-aligned discrete semantic tokens via vector quantization with knowledge distillation from a pretrained AV-HuBERT teacher, reducing visual encoder parameters by 93% compared to 3D ResNet-18 while maintaining comparable separation quality; and (2) a single-iteration encoder-decoder separator built on TDANet that replaces multi-iteration refinement with Global-Local Attention (GLA) blocks at every layer. Each GLA block pairs a Global Attention module (coarse-grained self-attention at 1/2^Q temporal resolution to capture long-range dependencies with O(1/4^Q) complexity reduction) with a Local Attention module (Heat Diffusion Attention that applies a learnable per-channel exponential decay filter in the DCT frequency domain to model local structure with physical priors, avoiding large-kernel convolutions). The AVF module fuses visual and audio features via video-guided gated fusion and multi-visual-space attention, both extended from the time-frequency to the time domain to avoid redundant frequency-dimension expansion.

## Results

On LRS2, Dolphin achieves 16.8 dB SI-SNRi, 16.9 dB SDRi, and 3.29 PESQ, surpassing the previous SOTA IIANet (16.0/16.2/3.23) by 0.8 dB in SI-SNRi. On LRS3, Dolphin reaches 18.8 dB SI-SNRi and 3.36 PESQ, and on VoxCeleb2 it attains 14.6 dB SI-SNRi and 3.17 PESQ, again leading all baselines on every metric. In terms of efficiency, Dolphin uses only 7.00M parameters (with video encoder) and 10.89G MACs, representing over 50% fewer parameters and 2.4× fewer MACs than IIANet, while achieving 4.3× faster GPU inference (33.24 ms vs. 142.30 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dolphin outperforms all nine baselines on every metric (SI-SNRi, SDRi, PESQ) across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor IIANet (ICML 2024), Dolphin gains +0.8 dB SI-SNRi on LRS2, +0.5 dB on LRS3, and +1.0 dB on VoxCeleb2, while using 53% fewer parameters and 4.3× less GPU inference time. AV-Mossformer2 (arXiv 2025) is the second-strongest baseline, trailing Dolphin by 1.7 dB SI-SNRi on LRS2 and 0.6 dB on VoxCeleb2, but it is 11.5× larger in MACs (124.46G vs. 10.89G). Among lightweight iterative methods, RTFS-Net (ICLR 2023) and Swift-Net (arXiv 2025) lag Dolphin by 1.9–2.2 dB SI-SNRi on LRS2 and 1.4–1.8 dB on VoxCeleb2, despite having fewer parameters (12.03M and 12.66M vs. 7.00M for Dolphin). The earliest baselines—AV-ConvTasNet (ASRU 2019), VisualVoice (CVPR 2021), and AVLiT-8 (ISCA 2023)—are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 4 dB SI-SNRi on LRS2. On multi-speaker extensions (LRS2-3Mix/4Mix), Dolphin also leads IIANet by 0.5 dB and 1.9 dB SI-SNRi respectively, confirming consistent gains as mixture complexity increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dolphin builds on TDANet's top-down attention encoder-decoder as its separator backbone, but replaces the multi-iteration refinement loop with a single forward pass augmented by global-local attention (GLA) blocks at each layer. For visual encoding, it adapts MagVIT's 3D residual and spatial-attention architecture into a dual-path autoencoder (DP-LipCoder), introducing vector quantization and knowledge distillation from AV-HuBERT to produce discrete, audio-aligned semantic tokens—a departure from the field's reliance on large lip-reading backbones. The AVF module reuses RTFSNet's gated and multi-space attention fusion mechanisms, extended from the time-frequency to the time domain. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the 'path dependence' dilemma in AVSS: prior work either uses heavy pretrained encoders (IIANet, AV-Mossformer2) or lightweight but semantically shallow encoders (AVLiT), and Dolphin attempts to resolve this via a compact VQ-based semantic bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 0.78M-parameter dual-path VQ encoder can match an 11.19M-parameter 3D ResNet-18 in separation quality (within 0.2 dB SI-SNRi), challenging the assumption that large lip-reading backbones are necessary for semantic alignment in AVSS. The single-iteration GLA separator outperforming multi-iteration TDANet (16.8 vs. 12.8 dB) while being faster suggests that multi-scale attention within a single pass can substitute for iterative refinement. Limitations include reliance on clean, synchronized lip videos (no robustness to occlusion, pose variation, or extreme lighting), and the discrete tokenization may discard fine-grained articulatory cues. Open directions include hierarchical codebooks, mixed discrete-continuous representations, and quantization/pruning for extreme edge deployment.
