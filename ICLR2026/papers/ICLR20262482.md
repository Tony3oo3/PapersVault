## Problem

Existing embodied manipulation planners either decompose tasks into language-only action sequences or generate visual subgoals in isolation, lacking a unified framework that jointly models linguistic reasoning and visual imagination. Even when multimodal planning is attempted, current approaches rely on external diffusion models for image generation, creating architectural fragmentation and prohibitive sampling costs for reinforcement-based optimization. Additionally, standard maximum-likelihood training over-specifies task-irrelevant visual details while under-constraining the physical dynamics that govern state transitions.

## Input / Output

The model receives a 256×256 RGB image observation and a free-form high-level text instruction (e.g., 'tidy up the room'), and jointly produces a stepwise language action sequence and a 256×256 visual subgoal image. The image is encoded as a 16×16 grid of 256 discrete tokens from a MAGVIT2 codebook (size 262,144), and both the text and image outputs are generated in a single forward pass rather than autoregressively or via iterative denoising. A low-level CLIPort policy then executes the plan by conditioning on both the language action and the generated subgoal image simultaneously.

## Method

EVLP builds a unified transformer (Qwen2.5-1.5B backbone) with a dual-tower vision module: a frozen SigLIP semantic encoder paired with a learnable spatial detail encoder for understanding, and a lookup-free MAGVIT2 quantizer for generation, where learnable image tokens directly predict all 256 discrete image token IDs in one forward pass. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) dynamic perception pretraining co-optimizes inverse dynamics (predicting the action between two images) and forward dynamics (predicting the next image given current image + action) to establish cross-modal state-transition reasoning; (2) Reinforced Supervised Fine-Tuning (RSFT) jointly minimizes a standard SFT cross-entropy loss over the full token sequence and a policy-gradient loss weighted by a dynamic alignment reward (IoU + MSE on detected change regions), exploiting the single-forward multi-sample property to draw K candidate images per step without additional forward passes. This hybrid objective prevents the policy collapse seen in RL-only training while steering generation toward physically consistent state transitions.

## Results

On the LoHoRavens benchmark, EVLP achieves the highest success rate across all six task categories, ranging from 75.3% (Shape) to 82.5% (Matching), with a mean of approximately 79.1%. The strongest baseline, PERIA (multimodal planning), achieves 60.6–72.3%, placing EVLP 10–15 points ahead on every task. On the real-world BridgeData v2 dataset, EVLP attains a language accuracy of 0.78 and LPIPS of 0.11, outperforming PERIA (LA 0.75, LPIPS 0.17) and SuSIE (LPIPS 0.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVLP outperforms all six baselines across every task category on LoHoRavens. Against the closest competitor PERIA (multimodal planning with LLM + diffusion), EVLP gains 10.5–15.5 points in success rate (e.g., 79.4 vs. 63.9 on Stacking, 82.5 vs. 72.3 on Matching). The language-planning baselines PAR (~31–35%) and EmbodiedGPT (~41–53%) are substantially behind, confirming that explicit visual subgoal generation is critical for long-horizon tasks. The visual-planning baselines SuSIE (~33–38%) and CoTDiffusion (~45–57%) improve over language-only methods but still trail EVLP by 20–40 points, as they lack joint language-visual reasoning. The end-to-end baseline CLIPort performs worst (2–19%), validating the need for hierarchical planning. On the real-world BridgeData v2 dataset, EVLP also leads in both language accuracy (0.78 vs. PERIA's 0.75) and visual subgoal quality (LPIPS 0.11 vs. PERIA's 0.17 and SuSIE's 0.23).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: unified multimodal generative models (Janus, Show-o, VILA-U), embodied hierarchical planning (PAR, EmbodiedGPT, SuSIE, CoTDiffusion, PERIA), and RL-based alignment for generative models (RLHF, GRPO, DDPO). It departs from prior unified models by rejecting both diffusion-based and autoregressive image generation in favor of a one-step discrete-token generation approach where the LLM directly predicts all image tokens in a single forward pass. For planning, it extends the multimodal planning paradigm of PERIA by integrating the language and visual subgoal generation within a single architecture rather than coupling an LLM with an external diffusion model, and introduces a reinforced supervised fine-tuning (RSFT) objective that replaces KL-divergence-based policy constraints (as in GRPO) with direct maximum-likelihood supervision on expert demonstrations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-step generation design offers a practical efficiency win (orders-of-magnitude faster sampling) that could make RL-based alignment of unified multimodal models tractable at scale, and the RSFT formulation provides a simpler alternative to reference-model-based RL that avoids dual-model overhead. However, all results are confined to simulated tabletop manipulation (Ravens, BridgeData v2), and the paper itself acknowledges the sim-to-real gap and data dependency as open challenges. Whether the one-step generation quality advantage holds for complex, high-resolution, or out-of-distribution scenes remains untested, and the dynamic alignment reward relies on ground-truth next-state images that may not be available in real deployment.
