## Problem

Existing online HD mapping evaluation relies almost exclusively on single-frame accuracy metrics (mAP), which fail to capture whether map elements flicker, jitter, or mutate in shape across consecutive frames—a property critical for safe autonomous driving. No dedicated benchmark or quantitative metrics existed to assess this temporal stability dimension, leaving the field unable to systematically compare how different representational paradigms maintain output consistency over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265011/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes sequences of L predicted vectorized map frames (polylines with confidence scores) from any online mapping model, paired with ground truth annotations and ego-vehicle pose matrices for coordinate alignment. Frame pairs are sampled at configurable temporal intervals (M ∈ {2, 3, 5, 10}), and matched polylines are resampled to N=100 points using a dynamic axis-selection mechanism that adapts to local polyline orientation. Outputs are hierarchical: per-instance Presence, Localization, and Shape scores are aggregated to class-level stability, then averaged across all classes into a single mAS score, with sub-metric breakdowns also reported for diagnostic purposes.

## Method

The paper proposes a four-stage stability evaluation pipeline: (1) temporal sampling to construct frame pairs, (2) cross-frame instance matching that uses ground truth as a persistent intermediary via Hungarian algorithm to avoid direct prediction-to-prediction matching errors, (3) geometric alignment transforming historical polylines into the current frame's ego coordinate system with perception-range clipping and uniform resampling, and (4) computation of three complementary metrics—Presence (binary detection consistency with a 0.5 flickering penalty), Localization (average L1 y-deviation mapped to [0,1] via a β=15 m scaling threshold), and Shape (normalized curvature difference using average inter-segment angles). These combine as Stability = Presence × [0.7·Loc + 0.3·Shape], yielding a unified mAS score. The paper then benchmarks 42 models and variants across camera-only, LiDAR-only, and fusion paradigms, ablating sensor modality, BEV encoders (GKT, BEVFormer, BEVPool), 2D backbones, temporal fusion strategies, and training epochs to reveal that mAP and mAS are largely independent performance dimensions.

## Results

Across 42 models evaluated on nuScenes val, mAS scores span a wide range from 66.6 (MapTR with temporal fusion + GKT) to 91.9 (StreamMapNet), while mAP ranges from 44.1 to 75.95. The paper's central finding is that mAP and mAS are largely independent: models with high mAP (e.g., MapTracker at 75.95) do not automatically achieve the highest mAS, and conversely, BeMapNet (mAP 61.4) achieves a higher mAS (81.9) than MapTRv2 (mAP 61.4, mAS 73.9). The majority of non-temporal models cluster in the lower-to-mid mAS range (71.6–78.0), indicating a common stability challenge. Temporal fusion models (StreamMapNet, MapTracker) dominate the top of the mAS ranking, with scores of 91.9 and 90.4 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This benchmark paper does not propose a new mapping model but evaluates 10+ existing methods on both mAP and the newly proposed mAS. StreamMapNet (mAS 91.9) and MapTracker (mAS 90.4) achieve the highest stability, both leveraging native temporal designs; MapTracker also attains the best mAP (75.95), making it the only model to excel on both dimensions. Among non-temporal models, PivotNet (mAS 84.3) and BeMapNet (mAS 81.9) lead, driven by perfect Presence scores (100.0) and strong Shape stability (~97–98%), though their Localization scores remain moderate (65.8–71.4). In contrast, MapTR (mAS 71.6) and MapTRv2 (mAS 73.9) show the weakest stability despite competitive mAP, with notably low Loc scores (65.4–68.6). Adding temporal fusion to MapTR with GKT actually degrades mAS from 71.6 to 66.6, while the same fusion with BEVFormer improves it to 73.0, highlighting that temporal modules are architecture-dependent. GeMap with LiDAR fusion gains mAP (62.7→66.5) but loses mAS (74.7→71.8), demonstrating that sensor fusion does not guarantee stability improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the evaluation methodology for online HD mapping rather than proposing a new mapping architecture. It departs from the dominant single-frame accuracy paradigm (mAP, mIoU) that has guided the field since HDMapNet and MapTR, and from robustness benchmarks in adjacent tasks (RoboBEV, 3D detection corruption studies) that examine sensor-level faults but not temporal consistency. The stability evaluation framework repurposes the Hungarian matching and coordinate transformation machinery already standard in online mapping evaluation, but redirects it toward cross-frame instance tracking rather than single-frame GT comparison. Conceptually, it positions temporal stability as a first-class evaluation axis analogous to how robustness benchmarks complement accuracy in detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that mAP and mAS are largely independent across 42 models challenges the field's implicit assumption that accuracy optimization implicitly yields deployable, stable maps — a model can be highly accurate yet 'intermittently blind' in ways that directly endanger downstream planning. The benchmark is likely to become a standard complement to mAP reporting for online mapping papers, and the architectural ablations (temporal fusion compatibility, BEV encoder specialization, training-regimen divergence) provide actionable design guidance. Open questions include whether the mAS metric generalizes beyond nuScenes' 15m×30m perception range, whether the fixed β=15 and ω=0.7 choices hold for different map scales, and how to jointly optimize accuracy and stability in a single training objective rather than treating them as post-hoc diagnostics.
