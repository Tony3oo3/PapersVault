## Problem

Learning-augmented streaming algorithms that leverage ML-based heavy-hitter oracles to break worst-case space lower bounds have been well studied for standard (full-stream) frequency estimation, but remain largely unexplored for time-decay and sliding-window models where older data is discounted or expunged. This paper asks whether the same oracle-based space improvements carry over to these more realistic recency-aware settings, and provides near-optimal algorithms that affirmatively answer this question.

## Input / Output

The algorithms consume a sequential data stream of coordinate updates, a suffix-compatible heavy-hitter oracle (which must correctly identify heavy hitters for every suffix stream [t:m], not just the full stream), a non-increasing weight function w (polynomial or exponential decay) or a window size W, and an accuracy parameter ε. The output is a (1±ε)-approximation of the F_p frequency moment, rectangle F_p frequency, or (k,p)-cascaded norm of the current weighted frequency vector. Notably, the space bounds are independent of the window size W, and the oracle's suffix-compatibility requirement is strictly weaker than predicting for every possible window, needing only m−W+1 suffix predictions.

## Method

The core contribution is a white-box transformation framework that lifts existing learning-augmented streaming algorithms into time-decay and sliding-window settings. For sliding-window, the paper adapts the smooth histogram framework of Braverman & Ostrovsky (2007) to the learning-augmented regime, showing that as long as the heavy-hitter oracle is suffix-compatible, the standard pruning-based reduction preserves correctness with only O(log n / β) copies of the base algorithm. For general time-decay, a block-based linear sketch framework (Theorem 3) maintains multiple weighted sketches that are merged when their weights are within a (1+η) factor and deleted once their total weight falls below a threshold ν, with smoothness conditions on G and w guaranteeing (1+ε)-approximation. The key technical insight is proving that F_p, rectangle F_p, and (k,p)-cascaded norms satisfy the required (α,β)-smoothness or (ε,ν,η)-smoothness properties, enabling direct application of the Jiang et al. (2020) learning-augmented streaming subroutine. The resulting F_p space bound of Õ(n^{1/2−1/p} · p^{1+p} / ε^{4+p}) is optimal in the exponent of n by the lower bound of Jiang et al. (2020), and empirical evaluations on CAIDA and AOL datasets confirm that the augmented algorithms (using CountSketch, LSTM, or LLM oracles) substantially outperform non-augmented baselines across window sizes and sample probabilities.

## Results

The paper achieves near-optimal space bounds for learning-augmented streaming algorithms in general time-decay settings. For F_p frequency (p≥2), the algorithm uses Õ(n^(1/2−1/p) · p^(1+p) / ε^(4+p)) space, which is optimal in the exponent of n by the lower bound Ω(n^(1/2−1/p) / ε^(2/p)) of Jiang et al. (2020). For (k,p)-cascaded norms, the space is Õ_{k,p}(n^(1−1/k−p/2k) · d^(1/2−1/p)), matching the streaming lower bound. Empirically, on the CAIDA dataset, the augmented AMSA algorithm keeps ℓ₂ norm estimates within a factor of 1.2 of ground truth across all window sizes, while the non-augmented AMS varies between factors of 1.25 and 2.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its learning-augmented algorithms (AMSA for ℓ₂, SSA for ℓ₃) against their non-augmented counterparts: the AMS algorithm of Alon et al. (1999) and the subsampling (SS) algorithm of Indyk & Woodruff (2005). On the CAIDA IP-traffic dataset, AMSA consistently outperforms AMS across all window sizes, with estimates within a factor of 1.2 of ground truth versus AMS's 1.25–2.3 range, and a flatter error curve indicating greater precision. Similarly, SSA (using Count-Sketch, LLM, or LSTM oracles) outperforms SS on CAIDA for both window-size and sample-probability sweeps, with the advantage most pronounced at low memory budgets. On the more uniform AOL query dataset, SSA still beats SS for window sizes W > 125,000 and at low sample selection probabilities, but the gap narrows because the uniform distribution reduces the benefit of heavy-hitter identification. Theoretically, the paper's F_p space bound matches the exponent of n in the lower bound of Jiang et al. (2020), establishing near-optimality, while Shahout et al. (2024) provided no formal space guarantees for their sliding-window approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-augmented streaming framework of Jiang et al. (2020) to time-decay models (polynomial decay, exponential decay, and sliding windows) by showing that the smooth histogram framework of Braverman & Ostrovsky (2007) and the block-based time-decay framework both remain valid when the heavy-hitter oracle is suffix-compatible. It departs from Shahout et al. (2024), the prior learning-augmented sliding-window work, by retaining the natural heavy-hitter oracle (rather than a 'next occurrence' oracle) and providing formal space guarantees. For cascaded norms and rectangular moments, the paper composes the Jayram & Woodruff (2009) and Tirthapura & Woodruff (2012) streaming subroutines with the same smoothness-based reduction, yielding a unified treatment across all three time-decay settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results close a gap in the learning-augmented streaming literature by showing that heavy-hitter oracles yield near-optimal space (matching the Jiang et al. 2020 lower bound in n) for F_p, rectangular F_p, and (k,p)-cascaded norm estimation under general time-decay, with direct relevance to GDPR-motivated sliding-window analytics. A key open question is the ε-dependency: the current framework incurs an ε^{4+p} factor versus ε^4 in the streaming setting, and the authors note that the difference-estimator approach of Woodruff & Zhou (2021a) could improve this but is significantly harder to implement with oracle advice. The suffix-compatibility requirement on oracles is a new technical condition whose practical learnability is demonstrated empirically but not yet characterized theoretically.
