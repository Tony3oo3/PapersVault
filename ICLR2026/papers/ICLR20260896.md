## Problem

Reward-guided sampling methods have been widely applied to image generation but remain largely unexplored for image editing, which requires simultaneously maximizing a target reward and preserving the semantic structure of the source image. Existing inversion-based guidance approaches rely on reward gradients computed at intermediate noised states or one-step posterior-mean approximations, which degrade structural faithfulness when the reward function is complex and non-linear, and lack a principled mechanism for selecting guidance strength.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image (512×512 for SD 1.5, 768×768 for SD 3) and a differentiable reward function (e.g., ImageReward, Gram-matrix style distance, classifier logit, or CLIPScore) as input, and produces a single edited image as output. The source image is first mapped into noise space via deterministic DDIM inversion or time-reversed ODE integration, and the reward weight w globally scales the guidance strength across all timesteps, replacing the per-step hyperparameter tuning required by prior methods. Only unconditional base models are used, isolating the reward signal from any text conditioning.

## Method

The paper reformulates reward-guided image editing as a trajectory optimal control problem: the reverse diffusion or flow-matching sampling path from the source image is treated as a controllable dynamical system, and an additional control term u_t is introduced into the drift to steer the entire trajectory toward a terminal state that maximizes the reward while penalizing control magnitude. Using Pontryagin's Maximum Principle, the authors derive three coupled optimality conditions (state, adjoint, and control equations) and solve them via an iterative coordinate-descent-style algorithm that alternates between backward adjoint propagation, gradient-based control updates toward −p_t, and forward trajectory re-simulation. This whole-trajectory optimization contrasts with prior stepwise guidance methods that correct only the posterior mean at each timestep, and the paper proves that the DPS guidance term is a one-step special case of the adjoint state. The framework is unified across diffusion and flow-matching models through a common SDE formulation, requires no model parameter updates, and is validated across four editing tasks (human preference, style transfer, counterfactual generation, and text-guided editing) on both SD 1.5 and SD 3.

## Results

On the Human Preference task (Stable Diffusion 1.5, 300 REFL images), the proposed method achieves an ImageReward of 1.8914, HPSv2 of 0.2526, CLIPScore of 0.2904, and Aesthetic Score of 6.1088, while maintaining source fidelity with LPIPS of 0.1717 and CLIP-I_src of 0.9242. The method achieves the best or second-best score on every metric across all four editing tasks (Human Preference, Style Transfer, Counterfactual Generation, and Text-guided Editing). On Style Transfer, it reduces the Gram matrix difference to 5.0185 (vs. 4.8742 for GA) while achieving the highest CLIP-I_sty (0.5782) and DINO_sty (0.2467). On Counterfactual Generation, it raises the target logit to 23.372 (vs. 24.875 for GA) with the best LPIPS (0.2251) and CLIP-I_src (0.8256) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all three inversion-based reward-guided sampling baselines (DPS, FreeDoM, TFG) across all four editing tasks and nearly all metrics. On Human Preference, it beats TFG (the strongest baseline) by +0.186 ImageReward, +0.016 HPSv2, and +0.084 LPIPS, while also achieving the best source preservation (CLIP-I_src 0.9242 vs. 0.8401). FreeDoM performs worst overall, with LPIPS of 0.5503 and CLIP-I_src of 0.7225 on Human Preference, indicating severe structural degradation. Naive Gradient Ascent achieves the highest raw target reward on some tasks (e.g., 1.9088 ImageReward, 24.875 target logit) but exhibits clear reward hacking: its validation metrics (HPSv2, Aesthetic, CLIPScore) are consistently lower than the proposed method, and it introduces perceptual artifacts. The paper also shows via a Pareto front analysis (Figure 4a) that the proposed method dominates all baselines across the full range of guidance scales, and that increasing iterations in the proposed method improves convergence rather than over-guiding, unlike baselines where excessive guidance degrades quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Pontryagin's Maximum Principle (PMP) framework from Adjoint Matching (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2025), which formulated stochastic optimal control for fine-tuning entire generative model distributions, to the single-image editing setting where the reverse diffusion trajectory is treated as a controllable system. It departs from the dominant reward-guided sampling paradigm (DPS, FreeDoM, TFG) by showing that their stepwise guidance terms are mathematically equivalent to a one-step adjoint-state calculation, and instead performs iterative multi-step trajectory optimization. The unified SDE formulation for both diffusion and flow-matching models provides a common theoretical substrate that prior editing methods (DDIM inversion, FlowEdit) handle separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, training-free alternative to reward-guided editing that avoids the reward hacking and structural degradation plaguing stepwise guidance methods, with a single weight parameter controlling guidance strength rather than per-step hyperparameters. Key limitations include the requirement for differentiable reward functions (excluding direct human feedback) and a 40–60% computational overhead over guided sampling baselines. Open questions include extending to non-differentiable or black-box rewards via zeroth-order estimation, generalizing to video/3D/audio domains, and whether reward-aware trajectory initialization could accelerate convergence.
