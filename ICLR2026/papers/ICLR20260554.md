## Problem

Existing neuro-symbolic LLM systems are purely deductive: they require all relevant facts to be provided as input, so they fail on realistic problems where obvious commonsense assumptions are left unstated. Prior abductive approaches (LLM-Tres, Logic-of-Thought) restrict the search space for missing clauses so heavily that they cannot solve practical problems, leaving a gap between the expressiveness of LLM commonsense knowledge and the rigor of symbolic solvers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260554/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a propositional logic problem (premises P and query Q) in both natural-language and symbolic form, along with an open-source LLM (requiring logit-level access) and a SAT solver. The problem is underdetermined: P entails neither Q nor ¬Q. The output is a True/False verdict on Q plus the set of commonsense propositions C that were abduced to make the problem solvable. The task is inference-only with no training phase, and four few-shot annotated examples are provided for prompting.

## Method

ARGOS iteratively augments the logic problem with commonsense propositions until the SAT solver or LLM self-consistency can resolve the query. At each iteration, the SAT solver returns the backbone (all literals implied by current premises), and the algorithm searches for new propositions of the form L1∧L2→L_right where L1 and L2 are backbone literals (prioritized by entity-sharing score) and L_right is generated by the LLM, potentially introducing new variables. Each candidate is scored twice by the LLM via logit-based probabilities for commonsense-ness and contextual relevance, and the first proposition exceeding both thresholds is added to the commonsense pool. A decaying self-consistency threshold γ (starting at 1, decreasing by α=0.1 per iteration) allows the LLM to eventually accept an answer when symbolic solving stalls, bounding total cost at k(γ−0.5)/α COT calls. The backbone-guided antecedent selection is the key design choice: it guarantees that every added proposition grows the set of inferable literals, making the search both targeted and complete over the relevant logical space.

## Results

ARGOS achieves up to +13% absolute improvement over the strongest baseline across six abductive reasoning datasets. On FOLIO with Llama3-8B, ARGOS reaches 81% binary accuracy versus 71% for Self-Consistency (SC-20) and 69% for Logic-of-Thought (LoT-20). On CLUTRR with Mistral-7B, ARGOS achieves 78% versus 59% for SC-20 and 71% for LoT-20. On the highly ambiguous QUAIL dataset (Llama3-8B), ARGOS reaches 82% versus 68% for SC-20, demonstrating robustness to non-logical contexts. Purely symbolic baselines (SAT-LM) collapse to near-chance (~50%) on most datasets, while LLM-Tres, despite having abductive capability, is limited by its restricted search space and underperforms ARGOS by 10–27 points on structured datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Self-Consistency (SC-20), ARGOS wins decisively on FOLIO (+10 pts), CLUTRR (+7 to +19 pts depending on model), and QUAIL (+13 to +14 pts), while matching or slightly exceeding SC on the already-saturated ProntoQA, CosmosQA, and ESNLI. Against Logic-of-Thought (LoT-20), ARGOS outperforms on FOLIO (+12 pts on L8B), CLUTRR (+6 to +7 pts), and QUAIL (+26 pts on L8B), but ties on ProntoQA and is slightly behind on CosmosQA/ESNLI where SC is already near-saturated. Against SAT-LM, the gap is dramatic: SAT-LM is at or below chance level (≤50%) on nearly all datasets, confirming that purely deductive symbolic methods cannot handle missing commonsense. Against LLM-Tres, ARGOS wins on FOLIO (+18 pts on L8B), CLUTRR (+25 to +27 pts), and QUAIL (+22 pts on L8B), with LLM-Tres's exhaustive search over only 2-variable input-grounded rules proving insufficient for problems requiring novel variables or multi-literal antecedents. Against COT, ARGOS consistently outperforms by 5–17 points on structured datasets, with the gap narrowing on simpler benchmarks where single-pass reasoning suffices.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARGOS extends the neuro-symbolic reasoning paradigm (F-COT, SAT-LM, Logic-LM) where LLMs translate natural language to symbolic logic for solver-based reasoning, by introducing an abductive loop that uses the LLM to supply missing commonsense propositions. It departs from prior abductive attempts—LLM-Tres restricts search to 2-variable clauses over existing variables, and Logic-of-Thought only produces deducible (not genuinely new) clauses—by allowing the LLM to introduce entirely new variables and by using the SAT backbone to guide which antecedent pairs to explore. The method also incorporates self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023) as a parallel solving channel, positioning itself on a 'symbolic-linguistic spectrum' between purely deductive symbolic solvers and purely neural chain-of-thought approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap in neuro-symbolic systems: purely deductive solvers fail on realistic problems where commonsense is left unstated, and prior abductive methods are too restricted to handle the breadth of missing information in real reasoning. The backbone-guided search is a clean mechanism for making abductive clause discovery tractable without exhaustive enumeration. Key limitations include restriction to True/False outputs, at most two literals in antecedents, dependence on logit-level model access, and occasional reliance on potentially unfaithful self-consistency chains. Open directions include backward-chaining abductive search, multi-choice generalization, and verbalized scoring to remove the logit-access requirement.
