## Problem

Traditional RL frameworks for LLM post-training tightly couple rollout sampling with parameter updates, making them architecturally incompatible with decentralized, geographically distributed training over the Internet. The resulting network latency creates policy staleness that inflates KL divergence between sampler and learner, causing importance weight variance to explode and training to collapse. No existing algorithm provides principled stabilization under the high-latency, resource-heterogeneous conditions of real-world distributed compute.

## Input / Output

The system takes math problem prompts (MATH level 3-5) and generates groups of G=8 chain-of-thought rollouts via vLLM on sampler nodes, with rewards computed from boxed final answers. The learner node asynchronously consumes these rollout batches (containing text, generation log-probabilities, and rewards) over a star-shaped network topology with up to 1800s simulated delay, producing updated Qwen3-1.7B/8B policy parameters. A key implementation constraint is that vLLM log-probabilities are recomputed via a separate forward pass under FSDP to avoid numerical discrepancies that cause training divergence.

## Method

GEPO introduces a Group Expectation Importance Weight (GEIW) that replaces the per-token or per-sequence proposal probability q(y|x) in the importance ratio with a group-level weighted expectation E_q[q(y|x)] = Σq(y^i|x)²/Σq(y^i|x), computed across G responses to the same prompt. This coarser granularity—progressing from token-level (GRPO) to sequence-level (GSPO) to group-level (GEPO)—exponentially reduces importance weight variance under high KL divergence, as proven in Theorem 1. The HeteroRL framework decouples sampling and learning onto independent heterogeneous nodes (e.g., mixing NVIDIA and Ascend chips), with the sampler continuously generating trajectories while the learner asynchronously updates parameters, communicating only model checkpoints and rollout batches over the Internet. The estimator is biased but the bias is bounded by ‖p‖₂/‖q‖₂ (Theorem 2), which remains close to 1 in practice for LLM policies with concentrated output distributions.

## Results

Under Hetero RL (max delay 64 steps, Qwen3-8B), GEPO achieves an average best score of 62.6 and last score of 60.8, with only a 1.8-point best-to-last degradation. This contrasts sharply with GSPO, which peaks at 58.4 but collapses to 46.4 (Δ=12.0), an 85% larger degradation. Under Online RL (zero delay), GEPO also leads all baselines, reaching 65.0 average on Qwen3-8B vs. GRPO's 61.8 and GSPO's 60.5, with the largest gain on AIME2025 (+6.2 points, 20% relative improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEPO outperforms all five GRPO-family baselines (GRPO, GSPO, BNPO, Dr.GRPO) on both best and last scores in the Hetero RL setting. Against GSPO—the closest competitor in peak performance—GEPO gains +4.2 points in best average (62.6 vs. 58.4) and +14.4 points in last average (60.8 vs. 46.4), while reducing the best-to-last degradation from 12.0 to 1.8 points (85% reduction). Against GRPO, GEPO improves best by +6.0 and last by +5.0 points. In the Online RL setting (Table 1), GEPO also leads all baselines on Qwen3-8B (65.0 vs. 61.8 for GRPO, 60.5 for GSPO), confirming that group-level importance weighting improves gradient quality even without asynchrony. Against asynchronous RL baselines (Truncated IS, TOPR, CISPO) under Hetero RL, GEPO achieves 43.5 last average while all three collapse to 0.0–23.3, demonstrating that clipping-based importance weight truncation is insufficient for high-latency stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO→GSPO lineage of group-based policy optimization by pushing importance-weight granularity one level further: from token-level (GRPO) to sequence-level (GSPO) to group-level (GEPO), where the denominator becomes the within-group expected proposal probability rather than any individual sample's probability. It also departs from prior asynchronous RL frameworks (AREAL, Prime Intellect, LLaRL) that assume centralized, low-latency clusters, by explicitly architecting for geographically distributed, heterogeneous nodes with Internet-scale latency. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 1: exponential variance reduction under high KL divergence) provides a principled justification for the group-level shift, distinguishing GEPO from ad-hoc clipping or truncation strategies used in CISPO, TOPR, and Truncated IS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important but underexplored gap: how to stably RL-train LLMs when sampler and learner are physically separated by high-latency networks, a setting that will become increasingly relevant as single-datacenter compute saturates. The clean theoretical guarantee and the 85% reduction in best-to-last performance gap versus GSPO are compelling, but the experimental scope is narrow (Qwen3-1.7B/8B only, math reasoning only, simulated log-normal latency up to 1800s). Open questions include whether the group-expectation mechanism transfers to larger models, non-math domains, and truly heterogeneous hardware (e.g., mixed NVIDIA/Ascend), and whether the modest bias introduced by GEPO accumulates over very long training runs.
