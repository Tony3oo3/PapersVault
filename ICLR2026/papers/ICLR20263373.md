## Problem

Existing training-free safety mechanisms for diffusion and flow models—Shielded Diffusion (SPELL) and Safe Denoiser—apply negative guidance to repel generated samples from unsafe content, but both rely on heuristic time schedules without a principled characterization of when guidance is actually necessary. No prior work provides a unified probabilistic framework connecting these methods or a formal reach-avoid analysis justifying a critical time window during which negative guidance must be strong and after which it should decay to zero.

## Input / Output

The method is training-free and plug-and-play: it takes a text prompt, a pretrained diffusion or flow-matching model (SD-v1.4, SD-v2.1, or SD-v3), and a set of N negative reference images (e.g., 515 nudity images from I2P, or class-specific ImageNet training images) as the unsafe set D⁻. At each denoising step within a specified time window, the method computes the model's predicted clean sample z_t via Tweedie's formula or flow-matching extrapolation, evaluates the MMD gradient in the clean (x_0) space, and steers the sampler through the corrected target. The output is a single generated image that avoids the unsafe content while preserving quality on benign prompts.

## Method

The core mechanism defines a potential E(x_t) as the biased squared MMD estimator between the current sample and the negative set D⁻ using an RBF kernel, then modifies the flow-matching ODE by adding a repulsive term λ(t)∇_{x_t}E(x_t). The gradient of this MMD potential yields a weighted kernel repulsion field (Equation 7) that provably recovers Safe Denoiser's weighted-kernel repellency (Proposition 1) and, via a radius-bandwidth matching condition solvable with the Lambert W function, recovers Shielded Diffusion's sparse radial force (Proposition 2). A control-barrier-function analysis (Theorem 2) then provides a sufficient condition showing that the weighted integral of guidance over an early window [0, s_c] determines safety, with the decreasing weight structure proving that shifting guidance earlier strictly improves the safety bound—justifying why negative guidance should be concentrated in the early denoising stages and turned off afterward to preserve sample quality.

## Results

On the primary safety benchmark (SAFREE + negative guidance), SGF reduces Attack Success Rate by 59.8% on Ring-A-Bell (0.051 vs. 0.127 for Safe Denoiser), 20.8% on UnlearnDiff (0.164 vs. 0.207), and 9.8% on MMA-Diffusion (0.423 vs. 0.461), while COCO FID shifts only +1.2 and CLIP by −0.3. In the diversity experiment on SDv3, early-stopped SGF (λ=0.03) achieves Vendi 3.082 with FID 31.95 and Precision 0.833, matching SPELL's diversity while preserving quality far better than SPELL's FID 32.77. For memorization mitigation on ImageNette-finetuned SD-v2.1, SGF reduces the 95th-percentile Gen-Train similarity from 0.437 to 0.328 (24.7% reduction) and improves FID from 41.19 to 32.44 (21.2% gain).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the no-safety baseline SD-v1.4, SAFREE+SGF reduces ASR from 0.797 to 0.051 on Ring-A-Bell and from 0.962 to 0.423 on MMA-Diffusion, while keeping COCO FID within 1.3 of the unprotected model. Compared to the closest training-free negative-guidance baseline Safe Denoiser, SGF consistently wins on all three adversarial prompt sets (e.g., ASR 0.051 vs. 0.127 on Ring-A-Bell, a 60% relative reduction) with only a +1.2 FID and −0.3 CLIP cost on benign COCO prompts. Against training-based erasure methods ESD and RECE, SAFREE+SGF achieves lower ASR on Ring-A-Bell (0.051 vs. 0.456 and 0.177) and UnlearnDiff (0.164 vs. 0.422 and 0.284) without any fine-tuning, though RECE still leads on MMA-Diffusion ASR (0.651 vs. 0.423 is actually SGF winning; RECE is 0.651). In the diversity experiment on SDv3, SGF with early stop (λ=0.03) matches SPELL's Vendi score (3.082 vs. 3.105) while achieving lower FID (31.95 vs. 32.77) and higher Precision (0.833 vs. 0.826), and at λ=1.0 it dramatically outperforms SPELL on FID (31.81 vs. 48.50) and CLIP (30.78 vs. 28.17). For memorization, SGF reduces @Sim 95% from 0.437 to 0.328 and improves FID from 41.19 to 32.44, with only a marginal CLIP drop (31.78→30.93), confirming that early-stopped guidance mitigates memorization without sacrificing quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of training-free negative guidance for diffusion/flow models—Shielded Diffusion (SPELL), which applies sparse radial repulsion, and Safe Denoiser, which decomposes the denoiser into safe and unsafe components—by showing both are instances of the gradient of a Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) potential with an RBF kernel. It further imports control barrier function (CBF) analysis from robot planning (Xiao et al., 2025; Dai et al., 2025) to provide a theoretical justification for the empirically observed 'critical window' in which negative guidance must be strong, a question left open by both prior methods. The framework is positioned as a probabilistic, energy-based alternative to the geometric QP-based CBF approaches used in low-dimensional robot planning, extending the safety-guidance idea to high-dimensional semantic spaces like image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that negative guidance applied too long can actually increase unsafe content (nudity reappears in later denoising steps) and that a brief early window is optimal—is well-supported by ablations across three adversarial prompt sets and has immediate utility for practitioners deploying safety filters on pretrained diffusion/flow models. The CBF-based proof gives a principled, non-heuristic schedule for guidance strength, moving the field beyond ad hoc time windows. However, the theoretical guarantee relies on a strong alignment assumption between the MMD gradient and the ideal barrier field near the safe/unsafe boundary, and the analysis is limited to forward-time dynamics where drift diminishes near the data manifold, so it does not directly cover masked-diffusion LLMs or other non-standard schedulers. Open questions include whether the critical window can be estimated adaptively per-prompt and whether the framework extends to video or 3D generation.
