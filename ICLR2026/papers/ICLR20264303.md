## Problem

Deep ensembles provide reliable epistemic uncertainty estimates but require training and evaluating multiple models, making them computationally expensive for large-scale settings like reinforcement learning. While NTK theory yields a closed-form expression for infinite-ensemble variance, evaluating it requires inverting a potentially enormous Gram matrix, which is infeasible for the sample sizes encountered in practice. The paper asks whether ensemble variance can be estimated with a single model and a single forward pass, without ever training or evaluating an ensemble.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264303/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single input (a 28×28 grayscale image for classification benchmarks or a 42×42 grayscale frame-stacked observation paired with an action for VizDoom) and produces a scalar predictive variance via one forward pass through a contextualized regression network. At inference, the input x is used simultaneously as the feature query and the context variable (c = x), so no separate ensemble members or stochastic passes are needed. In the RL setting, the resulting variance is scaled by a decaying bonus coefficient β and injected as an intrinsic reward into the DQN value update, rather than being returned as a standalone uncertainty score.

## Method

CSD reframes the NTK-GP ensemble variance V[f(x,θ∞)] = Θ(x,x) − Θ(x,𝒳)Θ(𝒳,𝒳)⁻¹Θ(𝒳,x) as a supervised regression problem: the second term is recovered as the output of a single network g(x,c,θ̃) trained with squared loss against kernel-similarity labels Θ^L(x_i, c_i) computed from last-layer partial gradients. The network is parameterized as an inner product φ(x,θ_feat)ᵀψ(c,θ_ctxt), where the context vector ψ acts as a context-dependent final layer, enabling efficient batch evaluation over all (x_i, c_j) pairings. The contextualized formulation generalizes the single-query derivation to arbitrary test points and, crucially, allows unlabeled target-domain data or data augmentations to be injected as additional context variables during training to sharpen uncertainty in regions of interest. At inference, setting c = x yields the variance estimate Θ(x,x) − g(x,x,θ̃∞) in a single forward pass, with the prior term Θ(x,x) approximated by the squared norm of last-layer features to avoid full-parameter gradient computation.

## Results

On the distribution shift detection benchmark (averaged over FashionMNIST, MNIST, KMNIST, NotMNIST), CSD achieves 96.63 AUROC, 96.94 AUPR-IN, and 96.19 AUPR-OUT with a single model, surpassing the strongest ensemble baseline ENS(15) (94.00 AUROC) and RND (94.40 AUROC). With data augmentations (CSD-Aug.) and target-domain context data (CSD-OOD.), performance further improves to 98.22 and 98.57 AUROC respectively. In the VizDoom sparse-reward exploration task, CSD is the only method to reach the goal across all 10 seeds and all three environment difficulties, while RND is the most competitive baseline but fails on the hardest variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSD outperforms all eight baselines on every OOD detection metric (AUROC, AUPR-IN, AUPR-OUT) in the distribution shift benchmark, with the largest margins over Bayesian methods: CSD (96.63 AUROC) exceeds BNN-Laplace (81.38) by ~15 points and MCD (85.67) by ~11 points. Against the strongest single-model baseline RND (94.40 AUROC), CSD gains +2.23 AUROC, and against the largest ensemble ENS(15) (94.00 AUROC), CSD gains +2.63 AUROC, all while using only one model. CSD's test accuracy (96.29%) is slightly below ENS(15) (97.18%) but comparable to RND (96.18%), indicating no meaningful accuracy trade-off. In VizDoom, CSD is the only method to solve all three environment difficulties across all 10 seeds; RND is the closest competitor but fails on the sparse variant, while DQN, BDQN+P, and IDS all fail to reach the goal. The data augmentation and OOD context variants (CSD-Aug., CSD-OOD.) further widen the gap to 98.22 and 98.57 AUROC, a ~4-point improvement over the base CSD.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSD builds directly on the NTK GP characterization of deep ensembles by He et al. (2020), which itself rests on the linearized training dynamics of wide networks established by Jacot et al. (2018) and Lee et al. (2020). The key conceptual departure is reframing the ensemble variance expression (Eq. 7) — which requires inverting a potentially enormous Gram matrix — as a standard supervised regression problem whose labels are kernel similarities, then contextualizing this regression so a single model handles arbitrary queries. This positions CSD alongside other single-model uncertainty methods (RND, MCD, BNN-Laplace) but distinguishes itself by providing an explicit NTK-theoretic derivation of what the model is approximating, rather than relying on prediction-error heuristics or stochastic forward passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSD offers a principled, single-forward-pass alternative to deep ensembles that integrates naturally with standard gradient-descent training pipelines and can leverage unlabeled target-domain data or augmentations — a flexibility that ensemble-based methods lack. The VizDoom sparse-reward result (CSD being the only method to solve the hardest variant across all seeds) suggests practical value for hard exploration, though this is based on a single environment family. Open questions include how well the NTK-based approximations hold for finite-width, non-stationary RL data streams, whether the approach extends to explicit aleatoric-epistemic separation, and whether the contrastive-learning-style context generation can be further refined for uncertainty-specific purposes.
