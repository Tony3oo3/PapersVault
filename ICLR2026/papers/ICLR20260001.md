## Problem

Any-reference video generation—synthesizing videos conditioned on arbitrary combinations of reference subjects (humans, animals, clothing, objects, environments) plus text prompts—suffers from three persistent failures: identity inconsistency across frames, entanglement where identities from different references are blended or confused, and copy-paste artifacts that degrade visual realism. Existing approaches either handle only a single identity, require per-identity fine-tuning, or concatenate visual tokens along the token dimension, which demands large-scale diverse data and still struggles with multi-subject disambiguation.

## Input / Output

The method takes up to three reference images (faces, objects, clothing, backgrounds, etc.) and a natural language prompt as input, and produces a temporally coherent, identity-consistent video. Reference images are not fed individually but are composited onto a single blank canvas at distinct spatial positions, forming one composite image that is treated as a single reference frame in the I2V pipeline. The benchmark comprises 120 reference-text pairs split evenly between single-ID (one face reference) and multi-subject (two-person, three-person, human-object-background) settings, with each case containing no more than three reference images.

## Method

MAGREF builds on the Wan2.1 I2V backbone using flow matching and introduces two key mechanisms. First, a region-aware masking mechanism places all reference images on a composite canvas, encodes the result with the VAE, and concatenates the reference latents and a downsampled binary region mask along the channel dimension with the noised video latents—preserving fine-grained pixel-level appearance cues without architectural changes. Second, a subject disentanglement mechanism extracts value embeddings of subject-specific text tokens from cross-attention layers and injects them into the corresponding spatial regions of the first-frame latent via Hadamard product with per-subject masks, explicitly binding each visual region to its textual label to prevent cross-subject confusion. A four-stage data pipeline (scene segmentation + Qwen2.5-VL captioning, GroundingDINO/SAM2 object extraction, InsightFace face filtering, and cross-pair augmentation via an image generation model) constructs diverse training pairs that suppress copy-paste artifacts by decoupling foreground references from their original backgrounds.

## Results

On the multi-subject evaluation (120 reference-text pairs), MAGREF achieves a Total Score of 0.627, outperforming the best open-source baseline VACE (0.590) and the best proprietary baseline Kling 1.6 (0.558). MAGREF leads on ID-Sim (0.542 vs. 0.345 for VACE), Subj-Sim (0.496 vs. 0.463), and Bg-Sim (0.622 vs. 0.615), while VACE edges ahead on Motion Smoothness (0.968 vs. 0.945). On the single-ID evaluation, MAGREF scores 0.694 Total, surpassing VACE (0.687) and Hailuo (0.680), with the largest gap in ID-Sim (0.595 vs. 0.577 for VACE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGREF achieves the highest Total Score across all 12 compared baselines in both single-ID and multi-subject settings. In the multi-subject evaluation, the largest margin is over SkyReels-A2 (+0.100 Total), which suffers from severe identity confusion (ID-Sim 0.274) and poor background consistency (Bg-Sim 0.507). Among open-source methods, VACE is the closest competitor (0.590 vs. 0.627), winning only on Motion Smoothness (0.968 vs. 0.945) but trailing MAGREF by 0.197 on ID-Sim and 0.033 on Subj-Sim. Among proprietary models, Kling 1.6 (0.558) and Vidu 2.0 (0.536) are the strongest, yet both fall well short of MAGREF on identity preservation (ID-Sim 0.387 and 0.308 respectively vs. 0.542). In the single-ID setting, MAGREF (0.694) narrowly beats VACE (0.687) and Hailuo (0.680), with the most significant advantage over HunyuanCustom (0.659) and SkyReels-A2 (0.604). Phantom achieves the highest Motion score (0.976) in multi-subject but has the weakest Subj-Sim (0.364), indicating it prioritizes smoothness over subject fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGREF builds on the Wan2.1 video diffusion backbone and flow-matching training objective, extending the image-to-video (I2V) paradigm to handle arbitrary combinations of reference subjects. It departs from prior token-wise concatenation approaches (ConcatID, VACE, Phantom) that inject reference latents as additional transformer tokens, instead using pixel-wise channel concatenation with a region-aware masking mechanism that preserves the I2V backbone's native conditioning structure without architectural changes. The subject disentanglement mechanism, which injects text-conditioned value embeddings into spatially masked visual regions, is a novel addition not present in prior single-ID or multi-subject methods. The four-stage data pipeline with cross-pair construction draws on tools like GroundingDINO, SAM2, and InsightFace but integrates them into a unified system specifically designed to suppress copy-paste artifacts in multi-reference training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The any-reference formulation (arbitrary subject types: humans, animals, clothing, accessories, environments in a single generation) is a practically important generalization over single-ID preservation, and MAGREF's design of preserving the I2V backbone's capabilities through channel-level conditioning rather than token-level retraining offers a scalable path for industry deployment. Key limitations remain: the method only supports image and text inputs (no audio, motion, or 3D), the T5 text encoder struggles with complex semantics, and long-video generation with sustained identity consistency is unsupported. Open questions include how performance scales with more than 3–8 reference images, whether the subject disentanglement mechanism generalizes to very large numbers of subjects, and whether the approach can be integrated with MLLM-based understanding for unified generation.
