## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for vision-language models treat the test phase as a standalone optimization problem disconnected from the model's training history, ignoring the geometric properties of the loss landscape acquired during training. This isolation leads to computationally expensive per-sample prompt updates (e.g., backpropagation in TPT) that are agnostic to loss landscape structure and yield suboptimal out-of-distribution generalization. The paper argues that flatness, a property explicitly cultivated during sharpness-aware training, is an underexploited geometric clue that can guide efficient and effective test-time adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a class label over K categories. During inference, the image is expanded into 64 views (the original plus 63 augmented views following TPT's augmentation strategy), and the method selects the top-s augmented views with the lowest sharpness-based scores to aggregate predictions via voting. Crucially, no prompt parameters are updated during test time; adaptation is achieved entirely through which augmented views are retained, making the method suitable for single-sample, real-time deployment scenarios.

## Method

FGA consists of two synergistic stages. In the training stage, Sharpness-Aware Prompt Tuning (SAPT) optimizes learnable text prompts by jointly minimizing cross-entropy loss and its sharpness (the maximum loss increase under bounded perturbations of the prompt, following SAM), positioning the prompts at a flat minimum in the training loss landscape. In the test stage, Sharpness-based Test Sample Selection (STSS) keeps the trained prompts fixed and instead evaluates each augmented test view by computing a sharpness-based score: a surrogate entropy loss plus the maximum loss variation over R random prompt perturbations. The top-s augmented views with the lowest scores are selected, ensuring their loss landscapes' flat minima align with the training flat minimum, and their predictions are aggregated. This eliminates all backpropagation and parameter updates during inference, reducing per-image inference time to 0.07s (23.86× faster than DiffTPT) and peak memory to 4.14 GB (4.67× lower than TPT), while achieving 4.88% average OOD accuracy improvement over TPT+CoOp.

## Results

On the domain generalization benchmark (CLIP-ViT-B/16), FGA achieves 66.55% OOD average top-1 accuracy across four ImageNet variants (IN-A, IN-V2, IN-R, IN-Sketch), outperforming TPT+CoOp (61.67%) by 4.88% and the strongest online method DPE (64.43%) by 2.12%. On cross-dataset generalization from ImageNet to 10 fine-grained benchmarks, FGA attains 67.60% average accuracy, surpassing TPT+CoOp (65.66%) by 1.94%. In terms of efficiency, FGA requires only 0.07 s per image (23.86× faster than DiffTPT) and 4.14 GB peak GPU memory (4.67× less than TPT), since it avoids all backpropagation during test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGA outperforms all nine compared baselines on the OOD average metric. Against the zero-shot CLIP baseline (59.42% OOD avg), FGA gains 7.13 points; against CoOp (59.28%) and CoCoOp (59.91%), the margins are 7.27 and 6.64 points respectively. Among TPT-based methods, FGA surpasses TPT+CoOp (61.67%) by 4.88 points and DiffTPT+CoOp (62.07%) by 4.48 points. FGA also beats the strongest non-prompt-tuning baselines: DPE (64.43%) by 2.12 points, TDA (63.89%) by 2.66 points, and PromptAlign (63.56%) by 2.99 points. Notably, FGA operates in the more challenging single-sample setting, whereas TDA and DPE aggregate information across a test stream; even under this disadvantage, FGA leads both. On cross-dataset generalization, FGA (67.60% avg) edges out TDA (67.53%) and PromptAlign (66.92%), and clearly surpasses TPT+MaPLe (66.50%) and ZERO+MaPLe (66.44%), confirming that the flatness-guided selection mechanism is complementary to multi-modal prompt learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established research threads: test-time prompt tuning (TPT and its variants like DiffTPT, C-TPT, ZERO) for VLMs, and sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) for seeking flat minima that improve generalization. The key departure from prior TTA work is conceptual: rather than treating test-time adaptation as a standalone optimization problem that updates prompt parameters per sample (as TPT does), FGA keeps parameters frozen and instead reshapes the test loss landscape through augmentation selection guided by the flatness geometry established during training. This unifies the training and test phases through a shared geometric criterion (alignment of flat minima), which prior methods like SAR and SoTTA partially explored but only at test time without accounting for training-testing sharpness interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the dramatic efficiency gains: FGA is 23.86× faster than DiffTPT and uses 4.67× less GPU memory while matching or exceeding their accuracy, making single-sample TTA viable in resource-constrained and real-time settings. The theoretical analysis (generalization bounds tied to distribution discrepancy and sharpness) provides a principled justification for why sharpness-based selection identifies samples closer to the training distribution. Limitations include evaluation restricted to CLIP with ViT-B/16 and ResNet-50 backbones, adherence to TPT's experimental protocol (16-shot, specific augmentation set), and the open question of whether the flatness-alignment principle transfers to other prompt tuning paradigms (e.g., MaPLe, CoCoOp) or to tasks beyond image classification such as segmentation or retrieval.
