## Problem

Auto-regressive video generation on edge devices suffers from severe decode-phase latency, where MLP modules (not attention) dominate computation. While spatial redundancy has been exploited via sparse attention, the temporal redundancy inherent to video's sequential frames—specifically in MLP outputs across adjacent frames—remains largely unexplored, leaving a critical efficiency gap for resource-constrained deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262765/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates 8 video frames at 256×256 resolution, where each frame is represented by 256 visual tokens decoded sequentially via next-token prediction. The first frame is generated identically to the baseline (dense attention retained), and subsequent frames exploit temporal replay. Evaluation uses VBench prompts with batch size 5, classifier-free guidance of 3, and a fixed random seed, producing token sequences that are decoded into pixel-space video frames.

## Method

FastCar introduces a Temporal Attention Score (TAS) computed as the scaled dot-product between a token's query vector and its spatially-aligned token's key vector from the preceding frame, incurring zero additional cost since it is extracted directly from the attention module. When the mean TAS across all attention heads exceeds a threshold τ, the MLP computation for that token is skipped and the cached MLP output from the previous frame is replayed instead. Theoretical analysis (Theorems 4.4–4.6) formally proves that high TAS bounds the MLP output deviation across adjacent frames via Lipschitz continuity, justifying the replay decision. A custom FPGA accelerator with Dynamic Resource Scheduling (DRS) handles the variable workload sparsity by using an on-chip index register and mapping registers to dynamically dispatch or discard pre-compiled instructions per batch, achieving over 2.1× speedup when combined with sparse attention.

## Results

On the VILA-U AR video generation model (8 frames, 256×256), FastCar achieves up to 1.76× decoding speedup at 80% replay ratio (390.7s vs. 689.7s dense), reducing TFLOPs from 31.79 to 19.42 while maintaining VBench Total score of 71.5% (vs. 74.1% dense). Power efficiency improves from 1.47 GFLOPs/W (dense) to 2.59 GFLOPs/W. When combined with sparse attention (StreamingLLM, local size 16), the combined method reaches 2.13× speedup (324.31s) with 3.12 GFLOPs/W power efficiency, outperforming sparse attention alone (1.04×) by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is StreamingLLM (sparse attention), which targets the attention module. FastCar decisively outperforms it on all axes: at comparable sparsity, FastCar at 80% replay achieves 1.76× speedup versus StreamingLLM's mere 1.04× (local size 16), because MLP modules dominate decode latency and sparse attention cannot address this bottleneck. On quality, FastCar at 80% replay retains PSNR 17.71 and VBench Total 71.5%, while StreamingLLM at local size 16 drops to PSNR 13.30 and VBench Total 64.5%—a 7-point VBench gap. Power efficiency also favors FastCar (2.59 vs. 1.53 GFLOPs/W). Crucially, FastCar complements rather than competes with sparse attention: combining both yields 2.13× speedup and 3.12 GFLOPs/W, and visually mitigates the temporal drifting artifacts that plague sparse attention alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the AR video generation framework of VILA-U and departs from the dominant line of efficiency research that targets attention modules (e.g., sparse attention via StreamingLLM) by identifying that MLP modules, not attention, dominate decode-phase latency in AR video generation. The core idea of exploiting temporal redundancy by replaying cached MLP outputs from the previous frame is novel relative to prior spatial-redundancy optimizations (sparse attention, token pruning) that have been applied to AR image generation. The theoretical analysis connecting the Temporal Attention Score to bounded MLP output deviation provides formal justification absent in prior heuristic replay or caching strategies, and the FPGA accelerator with Dynamic Resource Scheduling extends the work into hardware-aware deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that attention-centric optimizations yield negligible speedup (≤1.04×) for AR video generation while MLP replay achieves 1.76× independently shifts where the field should focus its efficiency efforts for sequential visual generation. The demonstration that FastCar complements sparse attention by alleviating drifting artifacts (achieving >2.1× combined speedup with improved quality) suggests a composable efficiency stack for long-duration, high-resolution video. However, evaluation is limited to a single model (VILA-U), 8 frames at 256×256, and one FPGA platform, leaving open whether the temporal redundancy assumption holds for longer sequences, higher resolutions, or other AR video architectures. The authors note plans to extend to broader model families, and adaptive (rather than fixed) thresholding is identified as a promising future direction.
