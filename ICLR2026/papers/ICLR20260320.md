## Problem

Conventional radiology report generation trains VLMs to produce linear narratives, which the authors empirically validate causes models to increasingly rely on linguistic priors over visual evidence as textual context grows—systematically weakening visual grounding and fostering clinical hallucination. Existing grounding-aware methods (MAIRA-2, DART, COMG) add alignment objectives but retain the autoregressive narrative structure that allows earlier generated tokens to bias subsequent findings, leaving the core narrative bias intact.

## Input / Output

The model receives a dual-image input—the original chest X-ray and a topic-specific anatomical segmentation mask produced by CXAS—along with a text prompt specifying a single clinical topic (e.g., 'disease-consolidation'). It generates one short, self-contained finding sentence for that topic. At report level, the model iterates over all predefined organ-level topics plus ground-truth disease labels, concatenating the individual topic outputs into a complete multi-sentence report.

## Method

LLaVA-TA re-engineers RRG by decomposing generation into independent, topic-specific queries rather than a single autoregressive narrative. Ground-truth reports are first decomposed by DeepSeek-V3 into clean, single-topic sentences via a hierarchical ontology (pathology → anatomy → auxiliary), and CXAS segmentation maps each topic to its relevant anatomical region. The architecture pairs a frozen BiomedCLIP-CXR vision encoder (processing both the original and segmented images) with a learnable MLP projector and Vicuna-7B, trained under a two-phase protocol: MLP-only cross-modal alignment followed by LoRA (rank 128) fine-tuning of the LLM. By conditioning each generation on the full image, a localized mask, and a single topic prompt, the model is forced to ground every finding independently in visual evidence, breaking the cross-sentence dependency that enables narrative bias and reducing hallucination through tighter, topic-isolated supervision.

## Results

On the MIMIC-CXR-JPG test set, LLaVA-TA achieves a RadGraph F1 of 44.0 (topic-level) and 34.3 (report-level), and CheXpert Macro-F1-14 of 71.5 (topic-level) and 62.4 (report-level), substantially outperforming the LLaVA-Rad baseline (RadGraph F1 30.8, CheXpert Macro-F1-14 40.1). On the out-of-distribution IU-Xray dataset, LLaVA-TA reaches RadGraph F1 of 45.0 (topic-level) versus 19.8 for LLaVA-Rad. On the MS-CXR attention grounding benchmark, LLaVA-TA doubles the Hit Score from 29.2% to 59.2% and improves Soft Precision from 22.0% to 37.5%, confirming stronger visual grounding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-TA decisively outperforms all eight baselines on every clinical accuracy metric. Against the primary baseline LLaVA-Rad (Zambrano Chaves et al., 2025), LLaVA-TA improves RadGraph F1 from 30.8 to 44.0 (topic) / 34.3 (report) and CheXpert Macro-F1-14 from 40.1 to 71.5 / 62.4, a gain of over 30 absolute points on the macro metric. It also surpasses MAIRA-1 (Hyland et al., 2024) by a wide margin (RadGraph F1 44.0 vs. 29.6; CheXpert Macro-F1-14 71.5 vs. 42.3) and CheXagent (RadGraph F1 44.0 vs. 20.5). Notably, the 7B LLaVA-TA outperforms much larger general-purpose models: GPT-4V (RadGraph F1 13.2), Med-PaLM M 84B (26.7), Llama 3.2 11B (9.0), Qwen2-VL 7B (11.0), LLaVA 7B (4.5), and LLaVA-Med 7B (6.5), demonstrating that structured topic-guided supervision is more effective than simply scaling model size. On the out-of-distribution IU-Xray benchmark, LLaVA-TA maintains its lead (RadGraph F1 45.0 vs. 19.8 for LLaVA-Rad), confirming that the gains are not dataset-specific. The only setting where LLaVA-TA shows a slight trade-off is the MLP-only fine-tuning regime without anatomical masks (LLaVA-T), where performance collapses (RadGraph F1 5.8), underscoring that the anatomy-aware component is critical when the LLM is frozen.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LLaVA-Rad (Zambrano Chaves et al., 2025), adapting its BiomedCLIP-CXR + Vicuna-7B architecture and two-phase training protocol, but fundamentally re-engineers the generation objective from linear narrative to independent topic-specific queries. It departs from a broad line of RRG work (MAIRA-2, DART, Multi-Phased Supervision, COMG) that improves grounding or alignment while retaining autoregressive sentence-to-sentence dependency. The anatomical segmentation component extends COMG's use of organ masks by coupling each mask to a specific topic query, and the report decomposition step leverages an LLM (DeepSeek-V3) as a preprocessing tool rather than a generation component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes radiology report generation as a set of independent, visually-grounded classification-like queries rather than a single text-generation task, which has broad implications for how medical VLMs are trained and evaluated. Key open questions include whether the static topic ontology can be made dynamic, whether end-to-end co-learning of segmentation and generation can replace the external CXAS dependency, and how to incorporate longitudinal/comparative reasoning that the current framework explicitly excludes. The finding that anatomical masks become critical under parameter-efficient (MLP-only) fine-tuning points toward a practical pathway for adapting large frozen VLMs to medical domains with minimal compute.
