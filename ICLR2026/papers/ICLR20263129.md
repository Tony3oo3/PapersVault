## Problem

LLM-based recommender systems face an inherent trade-off when representing items in natural language: attribute-based representations are token-efficient but lack rich item semantics, while description-based representations capture fine-grained preferences but incur quadratic computational cost as user sequence length grows. The paper identifies that item images and descriptions share significant information overlap (measured ~0.31 cosine similarity via CLIP, exceeding well-aligned COCO image-caption pairs), suggesting images can substitute for lengthy descriptions without losing critical semantics.

## Input / Output

Each item in a user's interaction history is represented by its title plus a single visual token extracted from the item image via a pretrained SigLIP vision encoder and mapped through a 2-layer MLP adaptor into the LLM's embedding dimension. Visual features are pre-computed and cached offline (3.4 KB per item), so inference only requires the adaptor forward pass. The output is a top-k ranked list of items from the full catalog, obtained by summing dot-product affinity scores across three feature types (image, CF embedding from SASRec, and SBERT text embedding) in a shared recommendation space, rather than generating item titles autoregressively.

## Method

I-LLMRec replaces lengthy item descriptions with a single image token per item, using a frozen Sheared LLaMA 2.7B as the backbone. The RISA module trains the visual adaptor by predicting next-item properties (brand, category, title, description) from crafted recommendation-context prompts, aligning the visual feature space with the LLM's language space without fine-tuning the LLM itself. The RERI module reframes recommendation as a retrieval task: a learnable [REC] token at the end of the prompt aggregates user preference, and separate projectors map both the user representation and item features (image, CF, text) into a shared space optimized with binary cross-entropy loss. The combined training objective (L_RISA + L_RERI^Img + L_RERI^CF + L_RERI^Text) is optimized end-to-end with only the adaptor and projectors trainable, yielding ~2.93× faster inference than description-based methods while achieving 22% higher Hit@5 than attribute-based methods.

## Results

On the four main Amazon datasets (Sport, Grocery, Art, Phone), I-LLMRec achieves Hit@5 scores of 0.4570, 0.5069, 0.5883, and 0.5156 respectively, outperforming all CF and LLM-based baselines. The method improves inference speed by approximately 2.93× compared to the Description-based approach (TRSR) while achieving a 22% performance improvement over the Attribute-based approach (TALLRec). Notably, adding textual descriptions to I-LLMRec (I-LLMRec+D) does not further improve performance, confirming that images already capture the semantic content of descriptions. I-LLMRec also maintains stable performance at a 256-token context window where description-based methods degrade sharply.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I-LLMRec outperforms all four CF baselines (GRU4Rec, VBPR, BERT4Rec, SASRec) by a wide margin across all datasets, with Hit@5 improvements of 19–20% over SASRec on Sport and Phone. Against the Attribute-based LLM baseline TALLRec, I-LLMRec achieves a 20.2% Hit@5 improvement on Sport (0.4570 vs. 0.3801) and a 10.7% improvement on Art (0.5883 vs. 0.5663). I-LLMRec also surpasses the Description-based TRSR on Sport (0.4570 vs. 0.4302 Hit@5) and Grocery (0.5069 vs. 0.4917), while achieving comparable results on Art (0.5883 vs. 0.5841) and Phone (0.5156 vs. 0.5148). Against the image-aware UniMP, I-LLMRec wins on all four datasets, with the largest gap on Art (0.5883 vs. 0.5315, +10.7%), indicating that a recommendation-specific alignment strategy (RISA) outperforms generic multimodal pre-alignment. The CF-based A-LLMRec is consistently outperformed, and notably, adding descriptions to I-LLMRec (I-LLMRec+D) yields no further gains, supporting the paper's central claim of significant image–description information overlap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of LLM-based sequential recommendation and vision-language modeling, departing from the two dominant item-representation paradigms (attribute-based and description-based) by exploiting the high CLIP-measured similarity (~0.31) between item images and their textual descriptions. While prior multimodal recommendation work treats image-text overlap as redundancy to be disentangled, I-LLMRec repurposes this overlap as a token-efficiency advantage: a single visual token substitutes for hundreds of description tokens. The RISA module (recommendation-oriented alignment of visual features to the LLM's language space) and the RERI module (retrieval-based recommendation via image features) are novel architectural contributions that distinguish this work from both generative LLM recommenders (TALLRec, TRSR) and semantic-ID approaches (LC-Rec, IDGenRec).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.93× inference speedup combined with a 22% Hit@5 improvement over attribute-based baselines makes I-LLMRec a practically compelling option for deploying LLM-based recommenders at scale, where context-window limits and quadratic attention costs are primary bottlenecks. The robustness finding—that full descriptions can underperform summarized ones due to noise, while images remain stable across datasets—addresses a real deployment concern. Open questions include performance in visually weak domains (e.g., books, where TRSR still wins), sensitivity to low-resolution images when used in isolation, and whether the approach generalizes to streaming/video recommendation beyond frame-averaging. The paper also notes that LLM fine-tuning (via LoRA) yields modest additional gains, suggesting headroom for future work.
