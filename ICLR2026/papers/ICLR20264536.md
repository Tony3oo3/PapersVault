## Problem

While unified multimodal models have demonstrated that understanding can improve generation in 2D, the reverse direction—whether generation tasks can enhance understanding—remains largely unexplored, especially in 3D scenes where geometric and spatiotemporal modeling are essential. No existing unified model simultaneously performs 3D scene understanding, novel view synthesis, and geometry estimation in a way that explicitly leverages generative capabilities to strengthen spatial reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264536/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For understanding, the model consumes up to 32 multiview frames at 640p resolution along with a text question, producing a textual answer via next-token prediction. For generation, the texture module receives a reference image (encoded by FLUX-VAE), target camera poses (embedded as Plucker-Ray absolute position encodings), and a text description, autoregressively producing novel view RGB images at 640×352. The geometry module additionally outputs per-frame depth maps (also in FLUX-VAE latent space) and camera intrinsics/extrinsics decoded via VGGT's camera decoder, with the understanding model's intermediate hidden features fed in via cross-attention during Stage 1.

## Method

Built on the 7B Bagel unified framework, Omni-View splits the generation pathway into a texture module (flow-matching denoiser with autoregressive frame ordering and diffusion forcing for 3D consistency) and a separate 4-layer geometry module (~1B params) that predicts depth via flow matching and camera pose via learnable queries, receiving the texture module's last-layer latent and the understanding model's features through cross-attention. Training uses a two-stage strategy: Stage 1 jointly optimizes all three components with a dense-to-sparse (D2S) curriculum that progressively reduces the number of reference images from all frames to only the first, forcing the model to build spatiotemporal reasoning incrementally; Stage 2 freezes the understanding model and fine-tunes generation with RGB-Depth-Pose joint learning, using point-cloud-projected renderings from Voyager as conditioning. The key architectural insight is that the geometry module's gradients backpropagate into the understanding model, so the act of predicting depth and pose directly shapes the understanding model's internal representations of 3D spatial relationships.

## Results

On VSI-Bench, Omni-View achieves a state-of-the-art average score of 55.4, surpassing all task-specific and unified baselines including SpatialMLLM-4B (48.4), VG-LLM-4B (46.1), and BAGEL-7B-FT (46.3). On 3D scene understanding, Omni-View attains 59.2 EM on SQA3D and 103.0 CIDEr on ScanQA, the best among models without explicit 3D input. For novel view synthesis from a single view, Omni-View achieves 23.22 PSNR, 0.817 SSIM, and 0.114 LPIPS, outperforming specialized generators like Voyager-13B (23.12 PSNR) and FlexWorld-5B (23.05 PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni-View outperforms the strongest task-specific 3D understanding model SpatialMLLM-4B by +7.0 average on VSI-Bench (55.4 vs 48.4), with particularly large gains in Relative Distance (+24.6), Absolute Distance (+11.6), and Room Size (+9.6). Against VG-LLM-4B, Omni-View leads by +9.3 average, with the largest margin in Relative Distance (+25.1) and Object Size (+13.4). Compared to the unified model BAGEL-7B-FT (its direct architectural baseline), Omni-View improves by +9.1 average on VSI-Bench and +2.0 EM on SQA3D, confirming that the dual texture/geometry generation modules and D2S training strategy drive the gains. On novel view synthesis, Omni-View surpasses the best specialized generator Voyager-13B by +0.10 PSNR and −0.061 LPIPS, and outperforms FlexWorld-5B by +0.17 PSNR, while also achieving the best scene generation scores (23.12 PSNR, 0.801 SSIM). Omni-View also exceeds GPT-4o by +21.4 and Gemini-1.5Pro by +10.0 on VSI-Bench average, though it trails on the Route Planning subtask (33.5 vs 36.0 for Gemini), suggesting remaining weakness in long-horizon navigation reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Omni-View extends the unified multimodal understanding-and-generation paradigm (established in 2D by Bagel, Janus, VILA-U, and others) to 3D scenes represented by multiview images. Its key architectural departure from Bagel is splitting the generation pathway into a texture module (flow-matching-based novel view synthesis with Plucker-Ray camera pose encoding from MV-AR) and a geometry module (depth and camera pose estimation via VGGT's decoder), rather than a single RGB-only generator. The training strategy introduces a dense-to-sparse (D2S) curriculum and autoregressive frame-by-frame generation to inculcate spatiotemporal modeling, drawing on diffusion forcing for 3D consistency. Conceptually, the paper inverts the 'understanding aids generation' direction explored by Metamorph, instead demonstrating that generation tasks (NVS, depth/pose estimation) feed back to improve 3D scene understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified model that jointly performs 3D scene understanding, spatial reasoning, and novel view synthesis without requiring explicit 3D input, achieving SOTA on VSI-Bench (55.4) and competitive results on SQA3D/ScanQA/ScanRefer. The validated 'generation facilitates understanding' principle in 3D provides a design recipe for future spatial-intelligence models: decompose generation into texture and geometry pathways, use autoregressive generation for temporal reasoning, and apply curriculum learning from dense to sparse views. Key limitations include weak 3D grounding (ScanRefer still lags 3D-input models), reliance on synthetic depth maps that cap geometry accuracy, and inability to handle long-range world generation or large camera motions in outdoor scenes. Open questions include whether reinforcement learning can close the grounding gap and whether the approach generalizes to dynamic or partially observed scenes.
