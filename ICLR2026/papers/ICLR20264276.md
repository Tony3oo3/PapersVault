## Problem

Aligning multi-modal LLMs requires balancing the stability of SFT (which needs human annotations and lacks preference modeling) with the preference-awareness of RL (which is computationally expensive and unstable). Existing preference-pair construction methods like SeVa rely on random augmentations that cannot control loser quality, and their DPO loss is decoupled from data characteristics, limiting both training efficiency and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264276/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image classification, the method takes a mini-batch of RGB image pairs (224×224) with class labels and produces a single mixed image with a Gaussian re-scaled soft label, which the ViT backbone classifies. For MLLM alignment, it takes an image–instruction pair and a randomly sampled auxiliary image, generating a mixed image (loser) and the raw image (winner) under the same prompt; the model outputs text responses for both, and the mixing ratio λ is repurposed as a data-dependent soft preference margin in the ranking loss rather than a fixed hyperparameter.

## Method

MergeMix replaces the standard attention-based mask in mixup with a token-merging (ToMe) pipeline: a ViT encoder with N attention layers swapped for ToMeAttention merges similar tokens via Bipartite Soft Matching, producing a source matrix S that preserves spatial topology. A recovery function R_{K→L} expands the merged attention map back to full resolution using S, and a TopK threshold (p = ⌊λ·L⌋) yields a binary mixing mask that retains cluster-level contextual structure rather than isolated patches. The mixing ratio is re-scaled through a Gaussian whose mean is the merge ratio K/L and whose std is derived from the mask's total mass, linking information compression to label weighting. For MLLM training, the mixed image is designated as the 'loser' and the raw image as the 'winner'; the re-scaled λ is inverted (γ = 1 − λ̂) to form a sample-difficulty-adaptive margin inside a reference-free SimPO loss, which is summed with the standard SFT cross-entropy to yield the total training objective.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (200 epochs), MergeMix achieves top-1 accuracy of 77.46% (DeiT-Tiny), 78.68% (DeiT-Small), 77.02% (ViT-Small), 75.75% (ViT-Base), and 76.19% (ViT-Large), outperforming the strongest ViT-specific baseline TransMix by +2.15% to +4.79% across model sizes. On Stanford-Cars, MergeMix reaches 89.42% (DeiT-Small) and 92.20% (ViT-Base). On ImageNet-1K, MergeMix attains 80.71% accuracy with 1591.66 TP/s throughput and 3.56G FLOPs, surpassing TransMix (80.44%, 1375.17 TP/s, 4.24G FLOPs). For MLLM alignment, MergeMix yields a +0.83% average gain on LLaVA-7B benchmarks and a +2.88% average gain on Qwen2.5-VL-7B benchmarks over their respective SFT baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MergeMix consistently outperforms all 14 compared mixup baselines across every model size on CIFAR-100, with the largest margins over ViT-specific methods: +2.15% over TransMix on DeiT-Tiny, +2.87% on ViT-Small, and +4.79% on ViT-Large. On Stanford-Cars, MergeMix (89.42% DeiT-S, 92.20% ViT-B) edges out the best baseline DeiT (88.72%, 92.17%) and clearly surpasses TransMix (88.38%, 91.66%) and SMMix (88.76%, 91.93%). On ImageNet-1K, MergeMix achieves the highest accuracy (80.71%) while also delivering the best throughput (1591.66 TP/s) and lowest FLOPs (3.56G) among all methods, thanks to the token-merging mechanism reducing redundant computation. For MLLM alignment on LLaVA-7B, MergeMix (+0.83% avg) outperforms MixUp (+0.05%), CutMix (+0.27%), and ResizeMix (−1.44%) with full tokens, and maintains a +0.88% gain even when reducing vision tokens to 288. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, MergeMix achieves a +2.88% average gain over the SFT baseline, surpassing VisionThink-RL (+0.84%) by a wide margin, particularly on MathVista (37.8 vs. 23.8) and MME-RealWorldQA (70.46 vs. 69.28). The method also demonstrates superior calibration (ECE 6.7% on DeiT-Tiny CIFAR-100) and robustness under occlusion (58.32% at 90% occlusion with ToMe inference vs. 55.61% for AutoMix), confirming that the token-merge-based mixing produces more spatially coherent augmented samples than attention-score or gradient-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

MergeMix repurposes Token Merging (ToMe, Bolya et al., ICLR 2023)—originally designed for ViT inference acceleration—into a mask-generation mechanism for mixup augmentation, replacing the TopK selection used by TransMix, SMMix, and MixPro with Bipartite Soft Matching to preserve spatial continuity. On the preference-optimization side, the paper extends the DPO/SimPO lineage (Rafailov et al., 2023; Meng et al., 2024) by replacing the need for a separate reward model or human-annotated preference pairs with a data-driven construction: the mixing ratio λ itself serves as a continuous soft preference margin, linking the augmentation strength to the ranking loss. This positions MergeMix as a bridge between the SFT and RL paradigms for MLLM alignment, going beyond SeVa (Zhu et al., 2024) which used random crops as losers and a fixed DPO loss, by providing controlled, attention-aware augmentation with an adaptive margin.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, unified recipe that improves both classification accuracy and MLLM alignment without requiring additional reward models or human annotations, which is appealing given the compute and annotation costs of RLHF-style pipelines. The VLM-as-judge validation (Figure A2) showing a monotonic relationship between mixing ratio and perceived quality across multiple frontier models lends empirical credibility to using λ as a preference signal. However, key limitations remain: augmentation is applied only to the image modality (text is untouched), the token-merging policy is static and unlearned, and the MLLM gains (+0.83% on LLaVA, +2.88% on Qwen2.5-VL) are modest relative to the substantial engineering effort. Open questions include whether extending mixup to the text modality, making the merge ratio learnable, or sampling the best loser from multiple augmented candidates could yield larger alignment improvements.
