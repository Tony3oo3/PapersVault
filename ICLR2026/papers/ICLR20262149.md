## Problem

Existing RTL representation learning approaches treat Verilog as unstructured text, ignoring that RTL is inherently a structured dataflow graph where behavioral semantics and topological structure are inseparable. This topological neglect leads to incomplete circuit representations that cannot distinguish structurally different but functionally similar designs (e.g., chain vs. tree adder topologies), limiting performance on PPA prediction and circuit retrieval tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262149/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RTL circuit designs in Verilog and decomposes them into register cones via register-driven backward traversal, producing two modalities per sub-circuit: a control-data flow graph (CDFG) with nodes as logic/registers and edges as signal connectivity, and a GPT-generated behavioral summary. The output is a 768-dimensional circuit-level embedding (aggregated from per-register-cone representations plus a global [CLS] token) that supports five PPA regression tasks (Area, Power, Slack, TNS, WNS) and natural-language-to-circuit retrieval scored by AUC.

## Method

TopoRTL integrates three components: (1) behavior-aware dual-modal tokenizers — a Graphormer graph tokenizer pretrained with functional-equivalence contrastive learning and masked node reconstruction, and a BERT summary tokenizer pretrained with the same contrastive objective plus masked language modeling; (2) three topology-aware positional encodings injected into Transformer self-attention — bit-width centrality (learnable per-width embeddings reflecting storage-phase precision), max-path discrepancy (top-K longest path mean differences capturing computation-phase timing), and graph density discrepancy (edge-to-node ratio differences reflecting interconnection complexity); (3) a topology-guided cross-modal alignment using a quadruplet contrastive loss over interleaved graph-summary fusion pairs, enforcing that topologically consistent modality combinations remain closer than topologically dissimilar ones. The attention mechanism augments standard QK^T/√d scores with learnable MLP-mapped path and density terms scaled by learned parameters, enabling the model to dynamically weight register relationships by their structural characteristics.

## Results

On PPA prediction (115 RTL designs, 7,576 register cones), TopoRTL (29.13M params) achieves Area PCC=0.863 / MAPE=7.95% and Power PCC=0.884 / MAPE=25.03%, outperforming the best text-based baseline CodeV-DS (6.7B) by 5.5% and 6.9% in PCC respectively, while using ~230× fewer parameters. On timing, TopoRTL sets the best WNS PCC=0.862 and RRSE=0.580, and matches the best Slack PCC=0.909. On natural-language circuit retrieval, TopoRTL maintains AUC≈0.78–0.80 across all negative-sample counts (L=5–15), surpassing the next-best baseline (CircuitFusion, AUC≈0.67) by over 10 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoRTL (29.13M) decisively outperforms the multimodal baseline CircuitFusion (150.59M) on Area PCC (0.863 vs. 0.647), Power PCC (0.884 vs. 0.657), and WNS RRSE (0.580 vs. 0.808), while using ~5× fewer parameters; CircuitFusion retains a slight edge on TNS PCC (0.885 vs. 0.872). Against the strongest text-based baseline CodeV-DS (6.7B, trained on 165k circuits), TopoRTL wins on Area PCC (0.863 vs. 0.814), Power PCC (0.884 vs. 0.827), and WNS PCC (0.862 vs. 0.780), but CodeV-DS leads on TNS PCC (0.928 vs. 0.872), suggesting text-scale advantages for aggregate timing. General-purpose Qwen3-Embedding models (0.6B–8B) are consistently outperformed on all PPA metrics, with the 8B variant closest on Slack PCC (0.888 vs. 0.909). GCN-based baselines (GCN-MLP, GCN-GNN) lag substantially on every metric, confirming that topology-only representations without behavioral semantics are insufficient. On retrieval, TopoRTL's AUC of 0.804 (L=8) exceeds CircuitFusion (0.674) by 13 points and CodeV-CL (0.655) by 15 points, demonstrating that joint topology-behavior modeling is critical for cross-modal circuit search.

## Relation to Prior Work

TopoRTL builds on the register cone decomposition and functional equivalence contrastive learning paradigm established by SNS v2 (Xu et al., 2023), and adopts the multimodal (graph + text) representation framework pioneered by CircuitFusion (Fang et al., 2025). However, it departs from both in a key way: rather than relying on post-synthesis netlists to implicitly infer topology (CircuitFusion) or treating RTL as unstructured text (CodeV, DeepRTL), TopoRTL introduces hardware-specific inductive biases—bit-width centrality, max-path depth, and interconnection density—directly into Transformer attention as topology-aware positional encodings. This positions it as an 'RTL-native' framework that extracts topological structure from the source design itself, aligning with the design left-shift paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling case that topology is not merely an auxiliary signal but a first-class property of RTL that must be explicitly modeled for accurate representation, with practical implications for pre-synthesis PPA prediction and design reuse. Key limitations include the small evaluation dataset (115 circuits), the assumption of synchronous sequential logic (disrupting clock-domain relationships during cone extraction), and reliance on a single PDK for label generation. Open questions include whether the topology-aware encodings generalize to asynchronous or multi-clock-domain designs, whether scaling to larger and more diverse RTL corpora would close the remaining gap with larger text models on timing tasks, and whether more sophisticated signal-propagation features could resolve the observed RTL-to-gate-level abstraction mismatches.
