## Problem

Vision-Language Models produce large numbers of redundant vision tokens that quadratically inflate LLM inference cost, and existing token-pruning methods rely on unimodal signals (vision-only or text-only) without a generic criterion that unifies both modalities. This leaves a gap: no principled, training-free framework simultaneously exploits text-query semantics and intra-image visual structure to select the most informative vision token subset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261639/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (encoded by a pre-trained ViT into n vision tokens, e.g., 576 for LLaVA-1.5 or up to 2880 for LLaVA-NeXT) and a natural-language text query tokenized into m text tokens. It produces a selected subset S of k vision tokens (k ≪ n) that replaces the full set as input to the LLM, which then generates a text answer. Notably, vision tokens are used in two distinct forms: pre-projection embeddings for vision-vision similarity and post-projection embeddings (aligned with the text space) for text-vision similarity.

## Method

MMTok formulates vision token selection as a maximum coverage problem: a submodular function f(S; M) = (1/m) Σᵢ max M_{i,S} measures how well a candidate subset S of source (vision) tokens covers a set of target tokens under a similarity matrix M. Two coverage objectives are solved jointly—text-vision coverage (target = text tokens, source = projected vision tokens) and vision-vision coverage (target = all pre-projection vision tokens, source = the same set)—after calibrating each similarity matrix via row-wise softmax with modality-specific temperatures τ_t and τ_v. The combined objective f(S; M^{tv′}) + α·f(S; M^{vv′}) remains submodular (sum of submodular functions), so a greedy algorithm yields a (1−1/e)-approximate solution in O(kn) time. The entire procedure is training-free, requiring only matrix multiplications and argmax operations, and introduces negligible overhead (<7 ms even at 2880 input tokens).

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B (576 tokens), MMTok preserves 98.7%, 97.8%, and 96.6% of original average performance when retaining 192, 128, and 64 tokens respectively, outperforming all training-free and fine-tuning baselines. On LLaVA-NeXT-13B (up to 2880 tokens), MMTok achieves a 1.87× inference speedup on POPE while maintaining 98.7% of original performance. Under extreme compression to only 4 vision tokens on LLaVA-1.5-7B, MMTok still preserves 71.4% of performance on high-IC tasks (vs. 43.8% for VisionZip and 54.3% for DivPrune). On Qwen-2.5-VL-7B with dynamic resolution, MMTok retains 94.6% performance at 20% token budget, surpassing VisionZip (94.2%) and DivPrune (91.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMTok consistently outperforms all four baselines across every token budget and model architecture tested. Against the strongest training-free baseline DivPrune, MMTok leads by 1.8% average at 64 tokens (96.6% vs. 94.8%) and by 0.7% at 192 tokens (98.7% vs. 98.0%). Against the fine-tuning-based VisionZip, MMTok achieves 1.6% higher average at 64 tokens (96.6% vs. 95.0%), demonstrating that the training-free multimodal coverage strategy can surpass methods requiring additional optimization. The unimodal baselines FastV (vision-only attention) and SparseVLM (text-only attention) are substantially outperformed, with gaps of 21% and 9.7% respectively at 64 tokens, confirming the paper's central claim that combining text-vision and vision-vision coverage is superior to either modality alone. On LLaVA-NeXT-13B at 160 tokens (↓94%), MMTok retains 95.1% vs. 94.6% for fine-tuned VisionZip and 92.0% for DivPrune, and on Qwen-2.5-VL-7B at 5% token budget, MMTok achieves 79.0% vs. 75.4% (VisionZip) and 76.3% (DivPrune).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical maximum coverage problem from combinatorial optimization (with its submodular structure and (1−1/e) greedy approximation guarantee) for vision token selection in VLMs, a task previously addressed by unimodal heuristics. It departs from vision-only methods (VisionZip's [CLS]-attention ranking, FastV's layer-wise attention, DivPrune's diversity criterion) and text-only methods (SparseVLM's text-to-vision attention) by jointly optimizing text-vision and vision-vision coverage, arguing that neither modality alone captures sufficient task-relevant information. The formulation is training-free and architecture-agnostic, requiring only similarity matrices between token embeddings, which distinguishes it from fine-tuning-based approaches like the trained VisionZip variant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a simple, training-free greedy procedure with theoretical guarantees can match or exceed fine-tuning-based token selection across five VLM architectures, reducing inference cost by up to 95% while preserving >95% performance. The Image Contribution (IC) analysis revealing that benchmarks like MMMU and ScienceQA barely benefit from vision tokens (IC ≈ 0.09) is a useful caution for evaluating token selection methods. Open questions include hardness-aware adaptive token budgets, leveraging a lightweight agent VLM to generate richer text queries for coverage, and combining token selection with image resizing for further compression.
