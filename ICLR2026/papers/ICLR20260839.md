## Problem

Applying reinforcement learning to masked diffusion language models (dLLMs) is hindered by the intractable log-likelihood of the diffusion policy, which is required for accurate policy gradient estimation. Prior work substitutes the ELBO as a surrogate, but since the ELBO is only a one-sided lower bound, minimizing it for negatively-rewarded samples does not guarantee a reduction in the true log-likelihood, introducing systematic bias into the policy gradient and limiting the model's ability to learn from negative feedback.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates a group of g text responses via a masked diffusion language model (LLaDA-8B-Instruct, 8B parameters) using semiautoregressive confidence-based decoding with a block size of 32 tokens. RL fine-tuning is performed with LoRA (rank 128, scaling factor 64), and rewards are computed per response to derive group-relative advantages. Evaluation uses generation lengths of 128, 256, and 512 tokens with temperature 0.0, while rollouts use temperature 0.9 (0.3 for Sudoku) to encourage diversity.

## Method

SPG 'sandwiches' the intractable log-likelihood by maximizing the ELBO for positive-advantage sequences while minimizing a Rényi-based Evidence Upper Bound (EUBO) for negative-advantage sequences, yielding a valid lower bound on the true group-relative policy objective. The EUBO is derived from the Rényi variational bound (Theorem 1) with a tightness parameter β≥1, where the log appears outside the expectation, creating a structurally different (and tighter) bound than the ELBO. A block-wise masking strategy for Monte Carlo estimation aligns the perturbation distribution with the actual semiautoregressive generation process, improving estimation stability. For negative-advantage traces, a convex mixture of ELBO and EUBO (coefficient ω) is used, and Proposition 1 proves that the optimal mixture strictly reduces per-coordinate gradient variance compared to either bound alone. The algorithm follows a GRPO-style group-relative advantage formulation with 4 inner gradient updates per rollout batch.

## Results

At a generation length of 256, SPG w/ Mixture achieves 86.1% on GSM8K, 40.0% on MATH500, 70.7% on Countdown, and 94.0% on Sudoku, improving over the previous state-of-the-art by +3.6%, +2.6%, +18.4%, and +27.0% respectively. On coding tasks, SPG w/ Mixture reaches 39.0% on HumanEval and 50.6% on MBPP at length 256, gaining +1.9% and +4.7% over the best baseline. SPG w/ EUBO (without the mixture) also consistently outperforms all baselines, confirming the benefit of the evidence upper bound for negative-advantage traces.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPG w/ Mixture outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark and generation length. Against the base model LLaDA-8B-Inst. (Nie et al., 2025), SPG gains up to +66.3 points on Sudoku (94.0 vs. 27.7 at length 256). Against LLaDA-1.5 (Zhu et al., 2025), which uses variance-reduced DPO, SPG leads by +5.6 on GSM8K and +67.1 on Sudoku. Against D1 (Zhao et al., 2025) and WD1 (Tang et al., 2025), both of which use one-step log-likelihood estimation, SPG w/ Mixture improves Countdown accuracy from 30.9/52.3 to 70.7 and Sudoku from 32.5/32.1 to 94.0 at length 256. Against UniGRPO (Yang et al., 2025), which uses ELBO-based Monte Carlo estimation, SPG gains +3.6 on GSM8K, +2.6 on MATH500, +27.7 on Countdown, and +27.0 on Sudoku. Against the trajectory-level StepWise method (Huang et al., 2025), SPG w/ Mixture achieves 38.4 vs. 35.5 on MATH500 and 69.9 vs. 46.1 on Countdown (averaged over lengths), while being ~12× faster per gradient update (0.6 min vs. 7.5 min on 8×A100).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO-style group-relative policy gradient framework (Shao et al., 2024) and the masked diffusion language model (MDLM) formulation (Sahoo et al., 2024; Nie et al., 2025), positioning itself against recent RL-for-dLLM methods (D1, WD1, UniGRPO, LLaDA-1.5/VRPO, LLaDOU) that all approximate the intractable log-likelihood using only the ELBO or a one-step surrogate. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that a one-sided lower bound is fundamentally misaligned when the policy gradient requires *decreasing* the likelihood of negative-reward samples, and introducing a Rényi-variational-bound-derived evidence upper bound (EUBO) to 'sandwich' the true log-likelihood from both sides. The block-wise masking strategy for Monte Carlo estimation is motivated by the semi-autoregressive decoding used in practice by LLaDA and concurrent block-diffusion work, but is applied here specifically to the RL optimization loop rather than supervised fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As diffusion language models gain traction as a parallel-decoding alternative to autoregressive models, reliable RL alignment is a prerequisite for competitive reasoning performance; SPG provides the first principled treatment of the log-likelihood estimation problem in this setting, with a clean theoretical guarantee (J_SPG ≤ J_group) and a variance-reduction result for the mixture objective. The large empirical gaps on Countdown (+18.4%) and Sudoku (+27.0%) suggest that prior ELBO-only methods were substantially under-optimizing, particularly on tasks where negative-reward penalization is critical. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 8B-parameter models and LoRA, whether the biased Monte Carlo estimation of the EUBO (log outside expectation) becomes a bottleneck at larger sequence lengths, and how SPG interacts with complementary advances like learned decoding-order prediction (LLaDOU) or continuous-time formulations.
