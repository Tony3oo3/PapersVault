## Problem

Existing methods that leverage generative priors from pre-trained diffusion models for sparse-view 3D reconstruction suffer from two critical gaps: they lack reliable geometric supervision, producing poor reconstructions even in observed regions, and they have no effective mechanism to mitigate multi-view inconsistencies in diffusion outputs, leading to severe shape-appearance ambiguities. The paper argues that accurate, scale-accurate geometry is the fundamental prerequisite for effectively exploiting generative models to complete unobserved scene regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264707/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 5–15 posed RGB images (or a single text-generated image, or an unposed video) as input and produces a 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) surfel representation that can be rendered to novel views and exported as a colored mesh. Internally, it consumes auxiliary signals from SAM instance masks, monocular depth (Depth Anything V2), MASt3R-SfM point clouds, and a video diffusion model (See3D or Stable Virtual Camera) for inpainting. The iterative training loop adds 10 inpainted novel views per round (3 rounds total), progressively expanding the training set beyond the original sparse input.

## Method

G4SPLAT builds on MAtCha/2DGS and introduces two coupled components. First, a plane-aware geometry module extracts per-view 2D plane masks (SAM + K-means on normal maps), merges them into globally consistent 3D planes via point-cloud projection and RANSAC fitting, then computes scale-accurate depth maps by ray-plane intersection in planar regions and linearly aligning monocular depth in non-planar regions. Second, a geometry-guided generative pipeline uses these depth maps to (a) construct a 3D voxel visibility grid that yields reliable inpainting masks, (b) select novel viewpoints by maximizing coverage of global 3D planes as object proxies, and (c) inpaint with a video diffusion model while modulating per-region color supervision to the single view that best observes each plane, thereby suppressing cross-view conflicts. Training proceeds in two stages: an initialization phase (chart alignment + plane-aware depth + 7000 iterations of 2DGS) followed by three iterative generative loops that recompute planes, add inpainted views, and fine-tune the Gaussians.

## Results

On Replica (5 input views), G4SPLAT achieves CD 6.61 cm, F-Score 65.14, NC 83.98, PSNR 23.90 dB, SSIM 0.836, and LPIPS 0.199, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On ScanNet++ (5 views), it reaches CD 6.34 cm and F-Score 67.12, and on Mip-NeRF 360 (9 views) it attains PSNR 18.66 dB and SSIM 0.515. The largest margins appear in geometry reconstruction: CD is reduced by 35–70% relative to the best baseline (MAtCha or GenFusion) across datasets, while rendering gains are more modest (1–3 dB PSNR over the strongest rendering baseline GuidedVD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G4SPLAT outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric across all four datasets. Against the strongest geometry baseline MAtCha (its direct base model), G4SPLAT reduces CD by 35% on Replica (6.61 vs 10.12 cm), 45% on ScanNet++ (6.34 vs 11.55 cm), and 7% on DeepBlending (20.72 vs 22.36 cm), while also improving PSNR by 6.1 dB on Replica. Among generative-prior methods, GuidedVD achieves the best PSNR on Replica (22.51) and ScanNet++ (17.90) but suffers catastrophic geometry degradation (CD 27.87 on Replica, 25.35 on ScanNet++), whereas G4SPLAT leads on both rendering and geometry simultaneously. GenFusion is the closest competitor on rendering (PSNR 20.14 on Replica, 17.82 on Mip-NeRF 360) but trails G4SPLAT by 3.76 dB and 0.84 dB respectively, and is far behind on geometry (CD 13.05 vs 6.61 on Replica). Difix3D+ and See3D show moderate rendering quality but poor geometry (CD 13.71 and 12.74 on Replica), while classical sparse-view methods (3DGS, 2DGS, FSGS, InstantSplat) are consistently the weakest on both axes, with F-Scores below 52 and CD above 14 on Replica.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MAtCha (Guédon et al., 2025), which uses chart alignment with MASt3R-SfM to obtain scale-accurate depth for 2DGS training, and on 2DGS as the underlying surfel representation. It departs from the growing line of generative-prior-for-3DGS work (GenFusion, Difix3D+, GuidedVD, See3D) by arguing that accurate geometry is a prerequisite for generative models to function effectively, rather than treating generation as a standalone fix for under-constrained regions. The key conceptual departure is using the Manhattan-world plane assumption to extrapolate scale-accurate depth into unobserved regions (via global 3D plane fitting with RANSAC), and then threading this geometry through the entire generative pipeline—visibility grids, novel view selection, and multi-view color supervision—rather than applying generative models as a post-hoc inpainting step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and widely-acknowledged failure mode: generative-prior methods produce blurry, floater-ridden reconstructions with severe shape-appearance ambiguities, particularly in unobserved regions. The plane-based geometry approach is computationally lightweight (compact plane equations vs. per-chart deformations) and naturally extends to single-view and unposed video inputs, opening practical pathways for embodied AI and robotics applications. However, the method's reliance on planar structures limits its effectiveness on highly non-planar or organic scenes, and it remains dependent on the quality of video diffusion models for color fidelity in completed regions. Heavily occluded areas (e.g., objects behind tables) remain a clear open problem that the authors acknowledge but do not resolve.
