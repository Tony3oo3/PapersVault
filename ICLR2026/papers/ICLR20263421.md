## Problem

Conventional flow matching and diffusion-based visuomotor policies sample from uninformative Gaussian priors and must repeatedly inject visual information via conditioning modules (cross-attention, AdaLN, FiLM) at every denoising step, incurring substantial latency and memory overhead that conflicts with real-time robot control requirements (e.g., 50–200 Hz). This paper asks whether the visual conditioning can be eliminated entirely by grounding the flow source in visual representations rather than in noise.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a single RGB frame (240×320 for ALOHA/AV-ALOHA, 256×256 for Robomimic, 96×96 for PushT) and optionally proprioceptive state, encoded by a ResNet-18 into either a 512-dim vector (global average pooling) or a 9×512 grid of spatial features. It outputs an action chunk of 16 timesteps (first 8 executed), with action dimensionality ranging from 2 (PushT) to 21 (AV-ALOHA bimanual + active-vision arm). The visual latent directly serves as the flow source z₀, so no separate conditioning input is passed to the velocity network at inference time.

## Method

VITA replaces the Gaussian source with the encoded visual latent z₀ = E_v(O) and learns a conditioning-free velocity field v_θ(z_t, t) that transports z₀ to a latent action z₁. To resolve the dimensionality mismatch between low-dimensional actions and high-dimensional visual features, an action autoencoder (encoder E_a, decoder D_a) lifts raw action chunks into a structured latent space of matching dimensionality, trained jointly with the flow. The key training innovation is flow latent decoding (FLD): during training, the ODE is solved with a 6-step Euler solver to produce ŷ₁, and the reconstruction loss ‖D_a(ŷ₁) − A‖ is backpropagated through the ODE steps into both the velocity network and the vision encoder, anchoring latent generation to ground-truth actions and preventing latent space collapse. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows FLD is locally equivalent to a simpler flow latent consistency loss ‖ŷ₁ − z₁‖ under mild decoder regularity assumptions. With vector-based latents, the entire flow network reduces to a 4-layer MLP (~31M params), enabling 1.5–2× faster inference and 18.6–28.7% lower memory than transformer-based flow matching with conditioning.

## Results

On 9 simulated tasks (AV-ALOHA, Robomimic, PushT, CloseBox), VITA achieves success rates of 78–100%, outperforming or matching the strongest flow matching baseline (FM with transformer + AdaLN) on 6 of 9 tasks and trailing by ≤8 points on the remaining 3. VITA surpasses Diffusion Policy (DP) by 20–50+ points and ACT by 25–60+ points across all tasks. In efficiency, VITA (MLP, vector-based) achieves 0.22 ms/chunk inference latency and 333.86 MiB peak memory, which is 1.5× faster and 18.6% less memory than the best FM baseline (0.33 ms, 410.38 MiB); with grid-based features, VITA (transformer) is 2× faster (0.25 ms vs 0.51 ms) and uses 28.7% less memory (377.55 vs 529.16 MiB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest flow matching baseline (transformer + AdaLN), VITA wins on 6 of 9 simulated tasks (ThreadNeedle +1.3, HookPackage +4, PushT +4.7, Square +8, CloseBox +10) and ties on 2 (CubeTransfer, Can at 100%), while FM leads on SlotInsertion (+4) and PourTestTube (+7.3). Against Diffusion Policy, VITA dominates by 20–52 points on every task (e.g., 91.33 vs 59.33 on ThreadNeedle, 88 vs 74.67 on PushT), with the gap largest on precision-critical AV-ALOHA tasks. Against ACT, VITA leads by 25–60 points across all tasks (e.g., 95.33 vs 72 on Square, 88 vs 28 on PushT). On 3 real-world single-arm ALOHA tasks, VITA matches or exceeds FM on all subtasks (e.g., StoreDrawer: 1.00/0.95/0.95 vs 0.90/0.90/0.90) and outperforms DP and ACT by 10–40 points per subtask. The key efficiency advantage is that VITA's conditioning-free design eliminates cross-attention, AdaLN, or FiLM modules, yielding 1.5×–2× lower inference latency and 18.6%–28.7% less peak memory at comparable or superior success rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

VITA builds on the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2023a) and the latent diffusion paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022), but inverts the standard generative recipe: instead of sampling from a Gaussian prior and injecting visual information via conditioning modules (cross-attention, AdaLN, FiLM) at every denoising step, VITA uses visual latents directly as the flow source, yielding a conditioning-free velocity field. It departs from the latent diffusion practice of pre-training and freezing the autoencoder by jointly training the action autoencoder with flow matching, necessitating the novel flow latent decoding (FLD) loss to prevent latent collapse. The paper also connects to cross-modal flow matching work (Liu et al., 2024a; He et al., 2025) but addresses the unique challenges of the action modality—sparsity, low dimensionality, and lack of semantic structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VITA's conditioning-free formulation delivers 1.5×–2× faster inference and ~19–29% lower memory than conventional flow matching policies while matching or exceeding their success rates, which is practically significant for real-time robotic deployment at 50–200 Hz. The finding that a simple MLP suffices for bimanual manipulation with active vision (where MLP-based FM fails entirely) challenges the assumption that transformer or U-Net backbones are necessary for high-precision control. Open questions include whether FLD generalizes to other generative domains, how VITA scales to language-conditioned or multi-task settings, and whether one-step flow matchers (e.g., MeanFlow) can close the remaining precision gap without sacrificing success rates.
