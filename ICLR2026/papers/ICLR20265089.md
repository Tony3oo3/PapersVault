## Problem

Existing federated learning systems treat statistical heterogeneity across clients as a nuisance to be suppressed through aggregation corrections or personalization, leaving the central server as a passive aggregator. No prior framework enables the server to actively exploit client heterogeneity to route new queries to the most appropriate client, despite this being a natural and valuable capability in domains like healthcare where different hospitals specialize in different patient populations.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 96×96 for RETINA) and produces two outputs simultaneously: a target class label via client-specific classification heads, and a client routing decision via a shared client-classification head that assigns a probability distribution over all m clients. A novel 'system accuracy' metric evaluates end-to-end routing quality by first selecting the best client for each test sample and then predicting its class, whereas baselines without routing use majority voting across all local models.

## Method

FedDRM models each client's data distribution as a multiplicative density tilt of a shared reference distribution (Density Ratio Model), then uses Empirical Likelihood to avoid parametric assumptions on the reference. A key theoretical result (Theorem 2.2) shows that profiling out the reference distribution yields a surprisingly simple dual loss: the sum of two cross-entropy terms—one for target-class prediction with client-specific linear heads, and one for client identification with a single shared head. To handle the extreme label shift inherent in client classification (each client only observes its own index), a reweighting parameter λ down-weights the client loss, with a convergence theorem (Theorem 2.5) characterizing the accuracy–convergence trade-off. The architecture uses a shared ResNet-18 backbone producing 512-dim embeddings, projected to 256 dims for both the per-client target heads and the shared client-routing head, trained via a modified FedAvg with the reweighted per-client loss.

## Results

FedDRM achieves the highest system accuracy (the paper's novel routing-aware metric) across all six benchmark settings, reaching 62.78% on CIFAR-10 (Dir-0.3), 58.50% on CIFAR-10 (5-SPC), 41.44% on CIFAR-20 (25-SPC), and 31.24% on CIFAR-100 (25-SPC). On average accuracy (standard per-client classification), FedDRM also leads in every setting, e.g., 80.26% on CIFAR-10 (Dir-0.3) and 46.73% on CIFAR-100 (25-SPC). On the real multi-center medical dataset RETINA, FedDRM outperforms all baselines by 0.83–3.77 points in average accuracy and 1.41–7.67 points in system accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedDRM outperforms all 10 baselines on both system accuracy and average accuracy across every dataset and non-IID setting. On system accuracy, the closest competitor is FedALA (61.33% on CIFAR-10 Dir-0.3), which FedDRM surpasses by 1.45 points; on CIFAR-100 25-SPC the gap widens to 3.28 points over FedAvgFT (27.96%). On average accuracy, FedAvgFT and FedProxFT are the strongest baselines (e.g., 79.08% and 79.07% on CIFAR-10 Dir-0.3), with FedDRM leading by ~1.2 points; on CIFAR-100 25-SPC, FedAS is the closest at 44.26% versus FedDRM's 46.73%. Personalized FL methods like FedRep, FedPAC, and ConFREE perform poorly on system accuracy (11–34%) because their highly divergent local models cause majority-voting routing to fail, whereas FedDRM's explicit client-classification head provides a principled single-model routing decision. On the real RETINA medical dataset, FedDRM leads by up to 7.67 points in system accuracy, confirming the advantage holds under genuine multi-center covariate and label shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Density Ratio Model (Anderson, 1979) and Empirical Likelihood (Owen, 2001) from the statistical literature, combining them for the first time in a federated learning context. It departs from the dominant FL paradigm—where heterogeneity is treated as a nuisance to be suppressed via aggregation corrections, reweighting, or personalization (FedRep, Ditto, FedBABU, FedALA, etc.)—by reframing the server as an active router that exploits distributional differences to direct new queries to the most specialized client. The resulting architecture (shared backbone with client-specific target heads plus a shared client-classification head) resembles personalized FL methods like FedRep, but the additional client-routing head and the EL-derived loss are the key differentiators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a new design axis for FL systems: rather than only improving local model quality, the server can also learn a routing policy that assigns incoming queries to the best-equipped client, which is directly relevant to multi-center healthcare, distributed fraud detection, and personalized services. Key open questions include how the DRM assumption behaves when client distributions are extremely dissimilar (the paper acknowledges this as a fundamental limitation rather than a methodological one), whether the single-client routing decision is robust enough for high-stakes applications, and how the approach scales beyond the 8–32 clients tested. The modest but consistent gains (1–5 points over best baselines) suggest the routing signal is real but not yet transformative, and the evaluation protocol's use of majority voting for baselines may understate the practical advantage of a single-model routing decision.
