## Problem

GRPO training is dominated by autoregressive generation (91–98% of wall-clock time), yet standard speculative decoding achieves limited or even negative speedup under the high-concurrency, variable-length batch conditions of GRPO rollouts. Additionally, the fixed draft model progressively misaligns with the continuously updated target policy, causing acceptance rates and speedup to decay over training.

## Input / Output

The system consumes math problem prompts (group size G=8 per prompt, batch size 4 per GPU), a target policy model (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), and a small draft model (EAGLE-style, trained on ShareGPT-68K). It outputs updated policy and draft weights plus generated token sequences. A key detail is that the draft model's supervision signal—hidden states and logits from the target model—is cached for free during the generation phase, so online draft learning adds only ~2–3% overhead. The effective batch size B_cur shrinks dynamically as sequences hit EOS, driving the adaptive decoding schedule.

## Method

FastGRPO combines two mechanisms. First, concurrency-aware speculative decoding: using an operational-intensity analysis of GEMM, the system defines a hardware-specific C_peak (the batch size at which the GPU transitions from memory-bound to compute-bound) and sets N_verify = C_peak / B_cur, K_draft = min(N_verify − 1, K_max), and L_draft = min(⌊log₂(N_verify/α)⌋, L_max) at every verification step, so the effective verification batch stays near C_peak regardless of how many sequences remain active. Second, online draft learning: at each GRPO iteration the draft model is updated on the target model's freshly generated hidden states (already computed during rollout), keeping the draft-target distribution aligned as the policy evolves and sustaining high token acceptance length throughout training. The draft model uses EAGLE-2's dynamic tree-attention verification with strict acceptance, guaranteeing lossless generation identical to vanilla autoregressive decoding.

## Results

FastGRPO achieves end-to-end speedup ratios (E2E SR) of 2.35x–2.72x across five models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct) and three mathematical reasoning datasets (GSM8K, SimpleRL-Abel-Level3to5, DAPO-Math-17K). The average E2E SR ranges from 2.46x (Q2.5-Math-7B-I) to 2.64x (Q2.5-Math-7B), while generation-phase speedup (Gen SR) reaches up to 3.04x on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct with GSM8K. All speedups are achieved with strict acceptance criteria, guaranteeing identical output distributions to vanilla autoregressive decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastGRPO substantially outperforms all three speculative decoding baselines across every model and dataset. On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, FastGRPO achieves an average E2E SR of 2.49x versus 1.20x for EAGLE-3 (the strongest baseline), 1.14x for HASS, and 1.12x for EAGLE-2 — a roughly 2x improvement over the best prior method. The same pattern holds on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, where FastGRPO reaches 2.62x E2E SR compared to 1.27x for EAGLE-3. The gap is consistent across all three datasets (GSM8K, SimpleRL-Abel-Level3to5, DAPO-Math-17K), with FastGRPO's Gen SR exceeding 2.6x in every setting while baselines remain below 1.35x. The ablation further confirms that both components contribute: removing online draft learning drops E2E SR from 2.49x to 1.74x, and removing the concurrency-aware strategy drops it to 2.21x, showing that neither mechanism alone is sufficient to close the gap to the full method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023) and its EAGLE/EAGLE-2 implementations (Li et al., 2024a,b) from their original low-concurrency inference setting into the high-concurrency, dynamically-varying batch regime of GRPO training (Shao et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is recognizing that the generation phase in GRPO transitions from high to low concurrency as variable-length sequences terminate at different times, making static speculative decoding configurations suboptimal or even counterproductive. The operational-intensity analysis (rooted in roofline modeling) provides a principled mechanism for adapting draft tree parameters in real time, while the online draft learning loop repurposes the target model's own generation outputs as free supervision—addressing a distributional drift problem that prior speculative decoding work, designed for static inference, does not encounter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With generation accounting for 91–98% of GRPO training time, the 2.35×–2.72× end-to-end speedup directly reduces the wall-clock cost of RL-based reasoning training, which is currently a major barrier to empirical exploration. The finding that naive speculative decoding can degrade performance (speedup < 1.0×) under high concurrency is a useful caution for practitioners who might otherwise assume inference-time acceleration techniques transfer directly to training pipelines. Limitations include evaluation restricted to 7–8B parameter models, mathematical reasoning tasks, and single-GPU (H800) settings; open questions include whether the concurrency-aware strategy and online draft learning scale to 70B+ models, multi-node training, and non-mathematical domains where response length distributions differ substantially.
