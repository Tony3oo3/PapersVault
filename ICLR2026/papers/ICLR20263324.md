## Problem

Weight-space models that learn directly from neural network parameters have been developed for MLPs, CNNs, and transformers, but no analogous symmetry analysis or tailored architecture exists for Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs), whose learnable components are univariate B-spline functions rather than scalar weights. This paper closes that gap by showing KANs share the same hidden-neuron permutation symmetries as MLPs and designing a graph-based weight-space architecture that explicitly respects them.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a KAN-graph: a directed graph whose nodes are neurons and whose edges carry the full B-spline parameter vector [w_b, w_s, c] of the corresponding univariate function as a feature, augmented with positional encodings that distinguish layers and break artificial symmetries. Outputs are task-dependent: a class label for INR classification, a scalar test-accuracy estimate, or a per-edge binary pruning mask (an equivariant prediction). Because the GNN operates on graph structure, WS-KAN generalizes to KANs of different widths and depths without retraining.

## Method

The paper first proves (Proposition 3.1) that permuting hidden neurons in a KAN leaves its function unchanged, mirroring the classical MLP symmetry. It then defines the KAN-graph, where each edge feature is the concatenated B-spline coefficient vector of the corresponding univariate function, and introduces WS-KAN, a GNN that performs bidirectional message passing (forward and backward aggregation) over this graph following the Gilmer et al. (2017) framework. Positional encodings are assigned per layer to input/output nodes and per edge to prevent the GNN from conflating structurally distinct positions. A key theoretical result (Proposition 4.2) shows WS-KAN can uniformly approximate the forward pass of any input KAN to arbitrary precision, by leveraging an MLP that approximates each B-spline univariate function (Lemma 4.1) and composing these approximations layer-by-layer through the message-passing mechanism. The architecture is trained end-to-end with standard supervised losses on a constructed zoo of pre-trained KANs across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, Kuzushiji-MNIST, and CIFAR-10.

## Results

On INR classification, WS-KAN achieves 94.3% on MNIST, 84.6% on Fashion-MNIST, and 42.2% on CIFAR-10, outperforming all baselines by a substantial margin (e.g., +6.8% over the next-best SetTrans on MNIST). On accuracy prediction, WS-KAN attains R² of 94.81 (MNIST), 92.27 (F-MNIST), and 95.69 (K-MNIST), with the lowest MSE across all datasets. On pruning mask prediction, WS-KAN reaches 99.54% ROC-AUC on MNIST and 99.72% on F-MNIST, closely matching the oracle-pruning trade-off in downstream accuracy while being up to five orders of magnitude faster than the data-driven oracle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WS-KAN consistently outperforms all three external baselines across every task and dataset. Against the Git Re-Basin alignment baseline (Ainsworth et al., 2022), extended here as MLP+Align., WS-KAN gains +13.3% on MNIST INR classification (94.3% vs. 81.0%) and +12.2% on CIFAR-10 (42.2% vs. 30.0%), confirming that explicit graph-structure exploitation surpasses parameter reordering. Against DMC (Eilertsen et al., 2020), a convolutional model over flattened weights, WS-KAN improves by +20.9% on MNIST (94.3% vs. 73.4%) and +9.2% on CIFAR-10 (42.2% vs. 33.0%), showing that structure-agnostic processing is fundamentally limited. Against DeepSets (Zaheer et al., 2017), applied here as a set-based ablation over edge features, WS-KAN leads by +35.2% on MNIST (94.3% vs. 59.1%) and +19.0% on CIFAR-10 (42.2% vs. 23.2%), demonstrating that respecting the graph topology (rather than treating edges as an unordered set) is critical. The consistent ordering MLP+Align. > MLP+Aug. > MLP across all tasks further validates that both symmetry awareness and structural modeling contribute independently to performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph-based weight-space learning paradigm established by Lim et al. (2024) and Kofinas et al. (2024) from conventional MLPs/CNNs to Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks, whose learnable parameters are univariate B-spline functions rather than scalars. The message-passing architecture follows Gilmer et al. (2017), and the symmetry analysis mirrors Hecht-Nielsen's (1990) permutation invariance result for MLPs, now shown to hold identically for KANs. The key departure is the KAN-graph representation, which encodes function parameters (B-spline coefficients, silu/spline weights) as edge features—a structural challenge absent from prior WS work where edges carried scalar weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As KANs gain traction for their interpretability and parameter efficiency, this paper provides the first systematic toolset for weight-space analysis of KANs, including the first KAN model zoo benchmark and a theoretically grounded GNN architecture. The 5-orders-of-magnitude speedup over data-driven oracle pruning is practically significant for model compression. Open questions include generalization to deeper or topologically varied KANs, extension to CNN-KANs, and the natural follow-up of studying transformations between KAN and MLP weight spaces to exploit their complementary strengths.
