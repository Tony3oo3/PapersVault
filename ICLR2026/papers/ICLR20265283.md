## Problem

Existing open-world segmentation methods either depend on predefined category vocabularies, require explicit text prompts to guide recognition, or can only process a single mask per forward pass, making them slow and prone to inter-instance interference in multi-object scenes. Foundation segmentation models like SAM produce high-quality masks but lack any semantic understanding of what was segmented. The field also lacks a sufficiently diverse evaluation benchmark to truly test open-world generalization, as prior datasets contain at most ~1,200 categories.

## Input / Output

WOW-Seg takes an RGB image and a set of object masks (visual prompts) as input, where masks can originate from SAM/SAM2 generators or even coarse geometric heuristics such as rotated bounding boxes or ellipses. Each mask is cropped with surrounding context at a 2× scale, resized to 448×448, and encoded by a shared-weight vision encoder into a 16×16 token grid from which the binary mask selects a variable number of tokens (averaging ~27 per mask on LVIS). The model autoregressively generates a free-form category name (text) for each mask in a single forward pass, requiring no text prompt or vocabulary constraint.

## Method

WOW-Seg is built on InternVL3-1B and introduces two key modules. Mask2Token converts each binary mask into visual tokens that already reside in the VLM's pretrained embedding space by processing a context-enriched crop through a shared-weight vision encoder and then selecting only the tokens corresponding to the mask region, thereby eliminating the distributional gap that prior dedicated mask encoders introduce. The Cascade Attention Mask is a novel attention pattern that enforces conditional independence across instances: each object's output tokens can only attend to its own mask tokens, the global image tokens, and the text prompt, while being masked from all other instances' mask and output tokens. This decoupling enables efficient multi-mask parallel training (up to 30 masks per sample) without inter-instance interference. The model is trained via standard next-token prediction on LVIS, PACO, and COCO Stuff, and the authors additionally construct RR-7K (7,662 categories, 80k+ images, 200k+ masks from SA-1B) as a comprehensive open-world recognition benchmark annotated through a three-stage pipeline of VLM inference, hallucination filtering, and manual screening.

## Results

WOW-Seg (1B parameters) achieves the best open-world region recognition performance across all three evaluation benchmarks: Semantic Similarity of 89.7 and Semantic IoU of 82.4 on LVIS, 88.5 and 79.2 on PACO, and 69.1 and 44.8 on the newly proposed RR-7K. On LVIS, WOW-Seg surpasses the previous SOTA DAM (8B) by +0.7 Semantic Similarity and +4.7 Semantic IoU while using roughly 8× fewer parameters. On the open-vocabulary segmentation task (Table 2), WOW-Seg achieves PQ 65.76 and mIoU 66.40 on Cityscapes, outperforming the previous best Osprey-7B by +15.12 PQ and +16.62 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WOW-Seg outperforms all 14 baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest competitors on LVIS, WOW-Seg beats DAM (8B) by +0.7 Semantic Similarity and +4.7 Semantic IoU, and PAM-3B by +1.1 and +4.1 respectively, while using 8× and 3× fewer parameters. On PACO, the margin over PAM-3B widens to +1.1 Semantic Similarity and +4.3 Semantic IoU. On the new RR-7K benchmark, WOW-Seg achieves 69.1 Semantic Similarity and 44.8 Semantic IoU, substantially exceeding VP-SPHINX (54.7/17.5), Osprey (61.0/32.5), and PAM-3B (45.8/13.4), confirming that the 7,662-category dataset is significantly harder than LVIS/PACO. On open-vocabulary segmentation (Cityscapes), WOW-Seg surpasses Osprey-7B by +15.12 PQ and +16.62 mIoU, and on mask region classification (Table 3) it leads MaskCLIP++ by +21.8 maskAcc on ADE-847 and +2.5 on Cityscapes, demonstrating that the word-free visual-prompt approach generalizes well even when compared against vocabulary-based CLIP fine-tuning methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

WOW-Seg extends the visual-prompting-for-VLLM paradigm established by VP-MLLM (Lin et al., 2024), which first demonstrated that binary masks could be encoded as tokens for multimodal LLMs. The key departures are twofold: Mask2Token avoids the distributional gap problem of prior dedicated mask encoders by reusing the VLM's own shared-weight vision encoder (keeping tokens in the pretrained embedding space), and the Cascade Attention Mask solves the inter-instance interference that limited VP-MLLM and prevented prior methods (DAM, PAM) from processing multiple masks in a single forward pass. The model is built on InternVL3-1B as its VLLM backbone and leverages SAM/SAM2 as external mask generators, positioning itself at the intersection of foundation segmentation and autoregressive language generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1B-parameter model outperforming 8B (DAM) and 3B (PAM) competitors by 4+ Semantic IoU points on LVIS demonstrates that architectural design (shared-encoder tokenization + decoupled attention) can substitute for raw model scale in region recognition, with practical implications for deployment. The Cascade Attention Mask is explicitly framed as a general-purpose mechanism applicable beyond segmentation (e.g., multi-target visual grounding), suggesting broader adoption potential. The RR-7K benchmark (7,662 categories, 200k+ masks) addresses a real evaluation gap, as prior datasets (LVIS ~1200 classes) cannot adequately stress-test open-world generalization. Open questions include whether the word-free formulation generalizes to video, whether the reliance on external mask generators (SAM) limits end-to-end applicability, and whether the single-epoch training on LVIS/PACO/COCO Stuff is sufficient for truly open-world deployment.
