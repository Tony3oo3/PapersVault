## Problem

Large vision-language models frequently default to memorized language priors rather than grounding answers in visual evidence, yet existing analyses of this phenomenon rely on coarse input-output probing that cannot reveal the internal mechanisms governing when and how vision actually influences decoding. This paper asks whether the layer-wise hidden-state dynamics of LVLMs expose a principled, sample-level signal for diagnosing language prior strength.

## Input / Output

For each sample the method runs the LVLM twice: once with the full image+text query and once with the text query alone (blind input, image replaced by ∅). At every decoder layer it extracts the last-token hidden state as a contextual summary vector, then computes a pairwise distance (cosine by default) between the two embeddings. The outputs are (a) a per-layer representation-distance curve for vision-dependent vs. vision-independent sample groups, from which the Visual Integration Point layer l* is identified, and (b) a scalar Total Visual Integration (TVI) score per sample, defined as the mean distance across all post-VIP layers. Datasets are split into vision-dependent and vision-independent groups using prediction agreement between the two input conditions as a proxy, requiring no manual annotation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a contrastive chain-of-embedding analysis: for each layer l, the model computes d(z_vis^l, z_blind^l) where z_vis^l is the last-token embedding from the multimodal forward pass and z_blind^l is from the text-only pass. Plotting the expected distance for vision-dependent samples against vision-independent samples across all layers reveals a sharp divergence at a specific layer l* (the VIP), after which the gap grows substantially—indicating the onset of genuine visual integration into decoding. TVI is then defined as the average of d(z_vis^l, z_blind^l) over all layers l ≥ l*, yielding a per-sample scalar inversely related to language-prior strength. The paper provides an information-theoretic interpretation showing that the representation divergence D_l(P_VT) − D_l(P_T) decomposes into a relative KL-divergence between the vision-dependent representation distribution and a blind-centered Gaussian estimator, plus an entropy constant, and derives finite-sample analytic bounds via H-divergence. Empirically, TVI computed over post-VIP layers correlates far more strongly with answer correctness (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.72) than pre-VIP aggregation, visual attention weights, or output-level divergence, and TVI can be used as a training regularizer to improve downstream visual reasoning.

## Results

Across 10 LVLMs and 6 benchmarks (60 model-dataset combinations), the Visual Integration Point (VIP) consistently emerges at a specific layer where representation distance between vision-dependent and vision-independent samples diverges sharply. TVI (Total Visual Integration), computed as the average representation distance over post-VIP layers, achieves Spearman correlations of 0.57–0.72 with answer correctness on vision-reasoning benchmarks (VLind-Bench, ViLP), substantially outperforming alternative proxies. An interventional study (PAI attention correction on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) confirms TVI tracks visual integration: TVI rises from 0.038 to 0.144 while accuracy improves from 50.0% to 52.3%. Using TVI as a training regularizer (λ=0.03) on LLaVA-v1.5-7B improves MME perception from 1369.75 to 1400.44 and reasoning from 298.21 to 321.43.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TVI against two existing proxies for language prior: Visual Attention (Liu et al., 2025) and Output Divergence (Xie et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025). TVI decisively outperforms Visual Attention on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (ρ=0.7155 vs. 0.0871 on VLind-Bench; ρ=0.6335 vs. −0.0364 on ViLP) and on InternVL-3-8B (ρ=0.6727 vs. 0.4967 on VLind-Bench; ρ=0.5709 vs. 0.0746 on ViLP), with Visual Attention being statistically non-significant in three of four settings. Against Output Divergence, TVI wins on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (0.7155 vs. 0.2978 on VLind; 0.6335 vs. 0.5084 on ViLP) and on InternVL-3-8B's VLind-Bench (0.6727 vs. 0.1627), though the gap narrows on InternVL-3-8B's ViLP (0.5709 vs. 0.5615). The authors attribute Visual Attention's weakness to 'attention sink' pathology under strong language priors, and Output Divergence's limitation to its inability to capture fine-grained internal representation dynamics that surface-level token comparisons miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant input-output probing paradigm for studying language prior (LP) in LVLMs (Lee et al. 2025, Luo et al. 2025, Deng et al. 2025), which measures LP through counterfactual visual inputs or modality-conflicting queries but cannot reveal internal mechanisms. It also moves beyond attention-based heuristics (Liu et al. 2025) and output-similarity metrics (Chen et al. 2025b, Xie et al. 2024) that the authors show to be unreliable proxies. By repurposing the chain-of-embedding concept from LLM interpretability to the multimodal setting, the paper introduces a representation-level contrastive framework that localizes *where* visual integration begins (VIP) and quantifies *how much* it contributes (TVI), filling a gap that prior coarse-grained analyses leave open.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VIP + TVI framework provides the first principled, white-box diagnostic toolkit for LP that operates at the representation level, enabling per-sample, per-layer mechanistic interpretation rather than aggregate benchmark scores. The demonstrated practical utility—using TVI as a training regularizer to improve downstream performance—suggests a path from diagnosis to intervention. Key limitations include the requirement for white-box access (excluding closed-source APIs), the focus on LP as the sole bias target, and the need for validation under realistic distribution shifts. Open questions include whether VIP/TVI generalize to video or audio-modal LVLMs, and whether the ~60% relative-depth regularity for VIP holds across fundamentally different architectures.
