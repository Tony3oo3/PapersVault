## Problem

Existing UDA methods (distribution alignment and pseudo-labeling) degrade significantly when unlabeled target data is extremely scarce or when spurious correlations in the source domain mislead the model. No prior framework jointly models uncertainty in both the target covariate distribution and the conditional label distribution within a distributionally robust optimization formulation for classification, leaving a gap in robustness to these two prevalent failure modes simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source images (grayscale 28×28 for MNIST/USPS, color 32×32 for SVHN, or natural images for Waterbirds/CelebA) and unlabeled target images, with target samples as few as 10 per class. For single-source settings, K=10 pseudo-sources are created via random subsampling with replacement from the source set. A shared backbone (DNN or ResNet-50) produces a fixed feature map z, on top of which linear logistic regression conditionals are trained per pseudo-source; the final output is a class label predicted on target inputs, with the classifier operating on perturbed feature representations during training.

## Method

The core contribution is a distributionally robust ambiguity set that simultaneously allows (i) the target input distribution to vary within an infinite-order Wasserstein ball of radius ε₁ around the empirical target distribution, and (ii) the conditional label distribution to be any mixture of K source-estimated conditionals with mixing weights β constrained to an ε₂-ball around a reference vector. A key tractable reformulation (Proposition 3.1) upper-bounds the DRO objective by a surrogate involving adversarial feature perturbation z′ and a soft pseudo-label y°(β,x) that is a convex combination of source conditional probability vectors. Training proceeds via alternating minimax updates: projected gradient ascent on z′ (adversarial perturbation in feature space), exponentiated gradient ascent on β (assigning higher weight to conditionals that produce higher loss), and gradient descent on θ (model parameters). The framework is modular—it can be combined with existing UDA methods (CDAN, STAR) as base classifiers to construct the conditional estimators, yielding further gains.

## Results

On digit recognition UDA tasks (Table 1), Ours(STAR) achieves the best accuracy across all six source-target and data-scarcity settings, reaching 94.4% on SVHN→MNIST (10² samples/class), 95.6% on MNIST→USPS (10²), and 97.3% on USPS→MNIST (10²), while maintaining strong performance under extreme scarcity (10 samples/class: 91.3%, 91.2%, 93.0% respectively). On spurious-correlation benchmarks (Table 2), Ours(ERM) achieves 87.3% on Waterbirds, 85.0% on CelebA, and 7.5% on CMNIST, substantially outperforming all baselines that do not use labeled target data (e.g., +25.9% over GroupDRO on Waterbirds, +22.0% on CelebA). The method's advantage is most pronounced under target-data scarcity, where it gains 10–30 percentage points over the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (Ours(STAR)) outperforms all ten baselines across every setting in both Table 1 and Table 2. On digit UDA tasks, the largest margins are against distribution-alignment methods: DANN (66.0% on SVHN→MNIST 10²) and MK-MMD (50.0%) lag by 28–44 points, while CDAN (63.4%) and CORAL (75.4%) trail by 19–21 points. Among the stronger pseudo-labeling and discrepancy baselines, ATDOC (83.3% on SVHN→MNIST 10²) and MCD (96.1% on USPS→MNIST 10²) are the closest competitors, yet Ours(STAR) still leads by 11.1 and 1.2 points respectively. On spurious-correlation benchmarks, Ours(ERM) achieves 87.3% on Waterbirds and 85.0% on CelebA, beating GroupDRO (61.4%, 63.0%) by 25.9 and 22.0 points, and outperforming ICON (54.2%, 31.1%) and PDE (57.1%, 55.0%) by wide margins. The only setting where a baseline exceeds the proposed method is CMNIST, where GroupDRO (with Tgt) reaches 73.1% versus Ours(ERM)'s 7.5%, but this variant requires labeled target data and is not a valid UDA comparison. Even without labeled target data, Ours(ERM) leads all baselines on CMNIST (7.5% vs. GroupDRO's 3.4% and ICON's 4.4%), though absolute accuracy remains low due to the extreme difficulty of the color-digit spurious correlation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the maximin effect framework of Meinshausen & Bühlmann (2015)—originally developed for regression with multi-source data—to the classification setting for unsupervised domain adaptation, using random subsampling to construct pseudo-sources and forming an ambiguity set over mixtures of their conditional distributions. The minimax optimization structure and the exponentiated gradient ascent for updating mixture weights are borrowed directly from GroupDRO (Sagawa et al., 2019), while the Wasserstein-ball perturbation on target features follows the standard DRO literature (Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn, 2018; Gao et al., 2024). The key departure is the joint modeling of uncertainty in both the target covariate distribution (via W_∞ perturbation in feature space) and the conditional label distribution (via mixture of source conditionals), which prior DRO-for-UDA methods like DRUDA address only through a single Wasserstein ball around the source distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical plug-in that can be layered on top of existing UDA methods (CDAN, STAR) without modifying their training, making it easy to adopt. Its ability to maintain strong performance with as few as 10 unlabeled target samples per class addresses a genuinely underexplored regime where most alignment and pseudo-labeling methods degrade. Limitations include evaluation only on small-scale benchmarks (digit recognition, Waterbirds, CelebA, CMNIST), a relaxation gap in Proposition 3.1 that is argued to be small but not formally bounded, and reliance on a small labeled target validation set for hyperparameter tuning (mitigated by the LODO-CV variant). Extending the framework to larger-scale benchmarks, NLP, or time-series settings, and tightening the surrogate objective, are natural next steps.
