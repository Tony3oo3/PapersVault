## Problem

Hybrid AR-diffusion generative models have emerged to combine the structural coherence of autoregressive modeling with the fine-grained fidelity of diffusion, but existing work lacks systematic guidance on how and why to allocate model capacity between these two paradigms. The design space—spanning layer allocation, block granularity, and loss design—remains largely unexplored, leaving practitioners without actionable principles for balancing quality and efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262604/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes text prompts (tokenized via Llama 3) or ImageNet class labels as conditioning, and produces high-resolution RGB images (1024×1024 on FFHQ, 256×256 on ImageNet). Images are encoded into continuous Stable Diffusion VAE latents, linearized in raster order, and grouped into spatial blocks that are interleaved with text tokens in a single unified sequence. Timestep information is injected into the latents via a U-Net downsampler rather than through explicit conditioning in individual Transformer layers, distinguishing the design from standard DiT.

## Method

MADFormer is a single Transformer that mixes AR and diffusion along two orthogonal axes: (1) the token axis, where the image is partitioned into spatial blocks processed autoregressively (each block conditioned on all preceding ones) while a diffusion objective denoises continuous latents within each block, and (2) the layer axis, where early decoder layers compute an AR conditioning representation over previously generated blocks and later layers perform iterative diffusion denoising conditioned on that representation. The architecture uses modality-specific parameter sets (text, clean-image, and noisy-image towers) with interleaved causal attention and bidirectional attention within blocks, plus two auxiliary losses (a hidden loss aligning the AR condition with the next block's ground truth, and a clean tower loss) to improve training dynamics. The key empirical finding is that under constrained inference compute (low NFE), AR-heavy layer allocations (e.g., 3:1 AR:diffusion) reduce FID by 60–75% over diffusion-heavy baselines, while the advantage reverses at higher compute budgets, and that finer block partitioning (16 blocks) benefits high-resolution generation while a single block suffices at 256×256.

## Results

MADFormer achieves a best FID of 15.9 on FFHQ-1024 and 27.4 on ImageNet when all 28 layers are allocated to diffusion (d=28). The standard configuration with 14 diffusion layers (d=14) yields FID 17.8 on FFHQ-1024 and 30.0 on ImageNet. Under constrained inference budgets (low NFE), AR-heavy layer allocations (e.g., 3:1 AR:Diffusion ratio) reduce FID by 60–75% compared to all-diffusion configurations, while diffusion-heavy setups dominate at higher compute. The paper positions these as design-space insights rather than state-of-the-art claims, noting that fewer training epochs (50 vs. 400 for MAR, 800 for ACDiT) and no classifier-free guidance account for the gap with prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not include a direct head-to-head comparison table against external baselines; its stated purpose is a controlled design-space study rather than a SOTA benchmark. It references MAR and ACDiT as prior hybrid AR-diffusion methods but explicitly notes that its ImageNet FID scores are higher due to substantially fewer training epochs (50 vs. 400 for MAR, 800 for ACDiT) and the omission of classifier-free guidance. The primary 'comparisons' are internal ablations: AR-heavy vs. diffusion-heavy layer splits (Figure 4), different AR block granularities (Table 2, where 16 blocks is optimal for FFHQ-1024 but 1 block is best for ImageNet-256), and the presence/absence of clean blocks and AR conditioning (Table 3, where combining both yields the best FID of 17.8/30.0). The key empirical takeaway is that the optimal AR-to-diffusion layer ratio is compute-budget-dependent: more AR layers help under tight NFE, while more diffusion layers win when inference budget is generous.

## Relation to Prior Work

MADFormer sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: unified AR+diffusion multimodal frameworks (Transfusion, Show-o, LMFusion) that handle text and image in a single Transformer, and hybrid image-generation architectures (ACDiT, Block Diffusion, MAR) that blend autoregressive and diffusion objectives within the image pathway. It most directly extends ACDiT's block-level AR-diffusion interleaving and Transfusion's shared-backbone design, but departs by treating the AR/diffusion split as a tunable design axis along both model depth and token granularity rather than a fixed architectural choice. The paper explicitly positions itself as a controlled testbed for mapping this design space, rather than as a state-of-the-art generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that AR-heavy layer allocation yields 60–75% FID improvement under low-NFE budgets, while diffusion-heavy allocation wins at higher compute—gives hybrid-model designers a concrete capacity-allocation rule that was previously absent from the literature. However, the findings are scoped to a single architecture family (Llama-based, 28 layers) trained for only 50 epochs on ImageNet (vs. 400–800 for MAR/ACDiT) without classifier-free guidance, so absolute FID scores are not competitive and the AR/diffusion optimum may shift with longer training or different sampling strategies. Open questions include whether the resolution-dependent AR block granularity generalizes beyond FFHQ-1024 and ImageNet-256, and how the trade-off interacts with advanced samplers (DPM-Solver, progressive distillation) and text-to-image conditioning.
