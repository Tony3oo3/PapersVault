## Problem

Existing diffusion models denoise all pixels synchronously at the same timestep, meaning prompt-related regions can only reference unrelated regions at the same noise level and never receive clear inter-pixel context. This synchronous treatment overlooks the heterogeneous nature of image regions—prompt-related regions need gradual refinement for fine-grained semantics while background regions need less—and ultimately impairs text-to-image alignment in terms of object identity, color, count, and co-occurrence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (e.g., 'a horse riding a bike') and produces a 512×512 RGB image, operating as a plug-and-play, tuning-free wrapper around pre-trained diffusion models (SD 2.1, SDXL, SD 3.5). The prompt is consumed by the model's existing text encoder; no additional input is required. The only computational overhead is encoding pixel-wise timestep embeddings per denoising step, adding roughly 10% sampling time (86 min vs. 78 min for 1,280 images on a 3090 GPU).

## Method

AsynDM reformulates the DDPM/DDIM sampler so that each pixel carries its own timestep state t_i ∈ R^{h×w} rather than a single scalar, enabling different pixels to exist at different noise levels simultaneously while preserving the Markov property. A concave scheduling function f(i) = T − i²/T is applied to prompt-related regions (denoised more gradually) while a linear scheduler g is used for unrelated regions, so that faster-denoised background provides clearer context to slower-denoised foreground. At each denoising step, a binary mask is dynamically extracted from cross-attention maps (thresholding each token's attention map above its mean and OR-ing across object tokens), and this mask at step i determines which pixels follow the concave vs. linear schedule at step i+1. The approach is entirely training-free: the pre-trained denoising network ε_θ is used unchanged, with pixel-level timesteps independently encoded and broadcast along the channel dimension.

## Results

AsynDM consistently outperforms all baselines across four prompt sets (Animal Activity, Drawbench, GenEval, MSCOCO) and four alignment metrics (BERTScore, CLIPScore, ImageReward, QwenScore). The most striking gains are in ImageReward and QwenScore: on Animal Activity, AsynDM achieves 0.9219 ImageReward (+0.1676 over DM) and 5.5218 QwenScore (+0.5773), while on GenEval it reaches 0.2895 ImageReward (+0.1354) and 5.3390 QwenScore (+0.4000). BERTScore and CLIPScore improvements are more modest but consistent (e.g., +0.0061 BERTScore and +0.0065 CLIPScore on Animal Activity). Sampling 1,280 images takes 86 minutes with AsynDM versus 78 minutes with the vanilla model, indicating only a ~10% efficiency cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AsynDM outperforms all four baselines (Z-Sampling, SEG, S-CFG, CFG++) on every metric across all four prompt sets. S-CFG is the strongest baseline, but AsynDM still leads by a clear margin—for example, on Animal Activity AsynDM achieves 0.9219 ImageReward vs. S-CFG's 0.8653, and 5.5218 QwenScore vs. 5.0421. Z-Sampling provides moderate improvements over the vanilla DM (e.g., +0.0740 ImageReward on Animal Activity) but is consistently surpassed by AsynDM. SEG and CFG++ actually degrade alignment relative to the vanilla model on several prompt sets (e.g., CFG++ drops ImageReward from 0.7543 to 0.3284 on Animal Activity, and from 0.1541 to −0.1344 on GenEval), while AsynDM never underperforms the baseline. The gains are largest on ImageReward and QwenScore (human-preference-aligned metrics), suggesting AsynDM's asynchronous scheduling particularly benefits perceptual and semantic faithfulness rather than just embedding-space similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates the DDPM and DDIM samplers to support pixel-level timesteps, departing from the standard assumption that all pixels share a single denoising schedule. Within the training-free alignment literature, it occupies a distinct niche: while Z-Sampling modifies the temporal ordering of steps, SEG adjusts self-attention energy, and S-CFG/CFG++ refine classifier-free guidance, AsynDM instead modulates the per-pixel noise schedule guided by cross-attention masks (building on Hertz et al. 2023 and Tang et al. 2023 for mask extraction). The key conceptual departure is treating synchronous denoising not merely as a design choice but as an active impediment to alignment, since prompt-related regions are forced to reference equally noisy unrelated regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play method that works across UNet (SD 2.1, SDXL) and DiT (SD 3.5) architectures, AsynDM offers a low-cost improvement path for practitioners who cannot fine-tune. The open questions are substantial: the fixed concave scheduler is suboptimal (a learnable per-pixel timestep predictor is identified as future work), the binary related/unrelated distinction ignores complex object relationships (e.g., spatial or causal dependencies), and extreme timestep disparities cause noise retention in faster-denoised regions, limiting how aggressive the scheduling can be. Whether these limitations can be addressed through fine-tuning or pre-training, and whether the asynchronous principle extends to video or 3D generation, remain important directions.
