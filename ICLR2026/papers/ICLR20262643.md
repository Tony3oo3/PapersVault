## Problem

Prior lower bounds (Cao et al., 2021) established that randomized algorithms discretizing Underdamped Langevin Dynamics require Ω(ε^{-2/3}) oracle complexity for strong L² error convergence, and RLMC achieves this rate, leading to the widespread belief that O(ε^{-2/3}) is also optimal for weak-error (Wasserstein-2) sampling guarantees. This paper shows that the strong-error lower bound does not transfer to the weak-error setting: the Poisson Midpoint Method (PLMC) achieves O(ε^{-1/3}) oracle complexity in W₂, a cubic improvement over RLMC and the first algorithm to break the conjectured ε^{-2/3} barrier for strongly log-concave sampling.

## Input / Output

The algorithm requires only gradient oracle access to ∇f(x) for a well-conditioned function f (α-strongly convex, L-smooth, condition number κ = L/α), an initial point x₀, and a momentum initialization V₀ ~ N(0, I_d) in the underdamped case. It produces a sample X whose law satisfies W₂²(Law(X), π) ≤ ε²d/α where π ∝ exp(-f(x)). Crucially, PLMC runs in batches of size k with step-size η/k but requires only O(1) expected gradient evaluations per batch (via Bernoulli randomization), rather than k evaluations as in standard LMC, so the total oracle complexity is O(N) rather than O(Nk) for N batches.

## Method

PLMC is a randomized discretization of Langevin dynamics where, within each batch of k sub-steps, independent Bernoulli(1/k) variables decide whether to evaluate the gradient at a midpoint, introducing variance that reduces discretization bias. The key analytical insight is rewriting PLMC as LMC with a perturbed Gaussian noise term whose perturbation S_{t,i} lies in a one-dimensional subspace almost surely (determined by the Bernoulli variable). A sharp W₂ bound between a standard Gaussian and this perturbed Gaussian (Lemma 1, adapted from Zhai's 2018 CLT proof) yields a ν² term instead of the naive ν, where ν = Tr(Σ) is the perturbation variance. This quadratic saving in the perturbation, combined with the contractivity of gradient descent (overdamped) or a coordinate transformation making the underdamped dynamics contractive, produces a geometric recursion on W₂² with a smaller per-step error, ultimately giving O(ε^{-2/3}) complexity for overdamped PLMC and O(ε^{-1/3}) for underdamped PLMC (for p ≥ 3), versus O(ε^{-2}) and O(ε^{-1}) for standard LMC respectively.

## Results

For overdamped Langevin dynamics, PLMC achieves an oracle complexity of Õ[(κ^{4/3} + κd^{1/3})/ε^{2/3}] for W₂² convergence, a cubic improvement over LMC's Õ(κd/ε²). For underdamped Langevin dynamics, PLMC achieves Õ(ε^{-1/3}) complexity (for p ≥ 3), breaking the previously conjectured Õ(ε^{-2/3}) lower bound that was established for strong L² error by Cao et al. (2021). The underdamped result is Õ[κ^{7/6}d^{1/6}/ε^{1/3} + κ^{(11p+6)/(8p+6)}d^{p/(4p+3)}/ε^{(p+2)/(4p+3)}], which is a cubic speedup over underdamped LMC's Õ(κ^{3/2}√d/ε).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLMC dominates all prior discretizations of Langevin dynamics in ε-dependence. For overdamped dynamics, PLMC's Õ(ε^{-2/3}) improves over LMC's Õ(ε^{-2}) (Durmus et al., 2019) and RLMC's Õ(ε^{-2/3} + ε^{-1}) (Shen & Lee, 2019; Yu et al., 2024), achieving a cubic speedup over LMC. For underdamped dynamics, PLMC's Õ(ε^{-1/3}) (for p ≥ 3) improves over LMC's Õ(ε^{-1}) (Dalalyan & Riou-Durand, 2020) and RLMC's Õ(ε^{-2/3} + ε^{-1/3}) (Shen & Lee, 2019), again a cubic speedup over LMC and a quadratic improvement over RLMC. The prior PLMC bound under LSI (Kandasamy & Nagaraj, 2024) gave Õ(ε^{-1/2}) for TV distance; the present work improves this to Õ(ε^{-1/3}) for W₂ under the stronger but standard strong log-concavity assumption. The concurrent work of Altschuler et al. (2025) achieves Õ(κ^{5/6}d^{5/3}/ε^{2/3}) for KL divergence, improving κ-dependence over prior RLMC bounds but with worse d-dependence and no improvement in ε over the present work. The key distinction is that the strong L² error lower bound of Õ(ε^{-2/3}) from Cao et al. (2021) does not apply to the weak W₂ metric used here, since W₂ allows optimal coupling of different Brownian motions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Poisson Midpoint Method (PLMC) introduced by Kandasamy & Nagaraj (2024), which itself is a variant of the Randomized Midpoint Method (RLMC) from Shen & Lee (2019). The key technical departure is the use of a coupling argument based on Zhai's (2018) high-dimensional CLT in W₂ distance, which avoids higher-order moment bounds and yields a quadratic improvement in the discretization error term. The final convergence guarantee is obtained by combining this new PLMC-to-LMC distance bound with the classical LMC convergence results of Durmus et al. (2019), positioning the work as a sharper analysis of an existing algorithm rather than a new algorithmic construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central result—that underdamped PLMC achieves Õ(ε^{-1/3}) oracle complexity in W₂ distance—breaks the previously conjectured Õ(ε^{-2/3}) barrier that was widely believed to be optimal based on strong L² error lower bounds for ULD discretization. This highlights a fundamental gap between strong error (same Brownian motion) and weak error (arbitrary coupling) that had not been exploited in sampling complexity. Open questions include whether the κ-dependence can be tightened (the authors note their moment bounds are suboptimal), whether the d-dependence can be improved relative to concurrent work by Altschuler et al. (2025), and whether a single algorithm can simultaneously achieve optimal rates in all three parameters (κ, ε, d).
