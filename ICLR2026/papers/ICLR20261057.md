## Problem

Existing speculative decoding methods either use static, one-size-fits-all draft tree configurations or optimize proxy metrics like acceptance length while ignoring the true wall-clock cost of drafting and verification. More critically, prior dynamic approaches treat the drafting and verification phases in isolation, failing to exploit their interdependence: a longer draft is wasted if verification cannot accommodate it, and a larger verification budget is wasted if the draft is too short. The paper asks whether both phases can be jointly and adaptively optimized to directly maximize per-cycle throughput.

## Input / Output

At each draft-and-verify cycle, the depth policy receives a compact state vector consisting of the log-probabilities of the W² candidate tokens at the current expansion frontier, the current draft depth D, and the total context length L, and outputs a binary continue/stop decision. The size policy receives the full state vector (log-probabilities of all V_all tokens in the completed draft tree, D, and L) and outputs one of 12 discrete actions mapping linearly to a verification size V in [20, 240]. Both policies are lightweight MLPs (single-layer 1024-unit for depth, two-layer [1024, 256] for size) whose combined inference overhead is under 1.5% of total generation time.

## Method

LTD formulates the draft-and-verify cycle as a reinforcement learning environment and trains two co-adaptive policies with PPO, using the per-cycle throughput λ_c = L_A / (T_draft + T_verify) as the direct reward signal rather than acceptance length or time cost alone. The depth policy (binary action, γ=0.999 to discourage premature stopping) is trained for 1M steps against a fixed V=60, while the size policy (12 discrete actions, γ=0.9) is trained for 100k steps with randomized draft depths in [1,12], both on HumanEval. A key training innovation is an iterative co-adaptation phase: after independent initial training, the policies alternate optimization (freeze size → optimize depth, then freeze depth → optimize size) for two rounds, allowing them to learn synergistic strategies. This co-adaptation is essential—naively combining independently trained policies yields suboptimal performance, as confirmed by ablation. The approach is built on the Eagle3 tree-based framework, replacing its static depth-8 and fixed-verification-size heuristics with context-aware dynamic decisions.

## Results

On greedy decoding (Temperature=0), LTD achieves mean speedup ratios of 3.92× (Llama-3.1-8B), 4.32× (Vicuna-13B), 4.16× (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLaMA-8B), 2.37× (Qwen3-14B), and 2.47× (Qwen3-32B), outperforming the Eagle3 baseline by 6.5%, 5.1%, 9.5%, 4%, and 36.4% respectively. The method also maintains robustness at Temperature=1, achieving 3.01× mean speedup on Llama-3.1-8B and 3.62× on Vicuna-13B, where most competing dynamic methods degrade significantly. On MMLU (57 subtasks), LTD outperforms Eagle3 on 54 out of 57 domains with an average 5% speedup improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTD consistently outperforms all seven compared baselines (Eagle3 default, Eagle3+Grid Search, DDD, SVIP, Gammatune, C2T, Disco, SpecDec++) across all five models and four tasks in greedy decoding. The largest gain is on Qwen3-32B, where LTD achieves 2.47× speedup versus 1.81× for Eagle3+Grid Search (a 36.4% improvement), driven primarily by substantially reduced verification time. On Llama-3.1-8B, LTD (3.92×) beats the strongest baseline Eagle3+Grid Search (3.83×) by 2.4%, while also exceeding all dynamic methods (DDD 3.57×, SVIP 3.57×, Gammatune 3.80×, C2T 3.70×, Disco 3.66×, SpecDec++ 3.76×). On Vicuna-13B, LTD (4.32×) surpasses Eagle3+Grid Search (4.22×) and all other dynamic baselines. Notably, at Temperature=1, LTD maintains its lead (e.g., 3.62× on Vicuna-13B vs. 3.50× for Eagle3+GS) while most dynamic depth/size methods degrade substantially (e.g., DDD drops to 3.34×, SVIP to 3.07×), highlighting LTD's superior robustness to sampling stochasticity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Eagle3, the state-of-the-art tree-based speculative decoding framework, replacing its static, manually-tuned draft depth and verification size with learned policies. It departs from prior dynamic speculative decoding methods (DDD, SVIP, Gammatune, Disco, SpecDec++, C2T) in two key ways: (1) it optimizes for actual per-cycle throughput rather than the proxy metric of acceptance length, and (2) it jointly trains a depth policy and a size policy via PPO so they co-adapt, rather than treating drafting and verification as independent sub-problems. The RL formulation with throughput as the reward signal is the central conceptual departure from the heuristic or supervised-learning approaches used by all prior dynamic methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for latency-sensitive LLM deployments: consistent 5–10% speedup gains over the already-strong Eagle3 baseline across five models and four tasks, with robustness at high-temperature sampling where most competing dynamic methods degrade. The clean framing of speculative decoding as a throughput-optimization problem with co-adaptive policies provides a principled alternative to ad-hoc heuristics. Limitations include training exclusively on HumanEval (code), reliance on the Eagle3 architecture, and the need for iterative co-adaptation training. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-Eagle3 draft models, self-speculative decoding, or multi-GPU serving settings where draft and verify times have different scaling characteristics.
