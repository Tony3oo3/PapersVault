## Problem

While frontier LLMs like ChatGPT Agent and Gemini-Pro integrate Code Interpreter and Search at test time, practical guidance on how to optimally combine textual reasoning, coding, and search across diverse questions remains lacking. The combined text/code/search solution space is large, most input questions lack explicit cues for the best approach, and prior multi-agent test-time scaling methods (e.g., Mixture-of-Agents) do not incorporate external tools. This paper tackles the gap of systematically exploiting tool-augmented multi-agent reasoning with principled refinement and termination.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text question as input and produces a final answer (e.g., a numeric value, multiple-choice letter, or short text). Each of the 15 agents can invoke Code Interpreter (with a 60-second execution limit) and/or Web Search (via Google Search API, LLM-inherent search, or their combination) up to 5 tool-interaction rounds per inference. Across refinement rounds, each agent's prompt concatenates the original question with all agents' previous-round answers, so the effective input grows with each iteration. Final answer extraction handles code outputs by executing them and parsing results, with Gemini-2.5-Pro serving as a format-normalization judge when needed.

## Method

TUMIX runs 15 diverse pre-designed agents in parallel, each employing a distinct tool-use strategy (text-only CoT, code-only, search-only, dual-tool, guided dual-tool with a steering module, and hinted variants), then iteratively refines answers over multiple rounds where every agent conditions on the original question plus all prior-round answers. An LLM-as-Judge adaptively determines when to stop refinement (enforcing a minimum of 2 rounds), reducing inference cost to ~49% of fixed-round baselines while preserving accuracy, since over-refinement causes correct answers to be mistakenly discarded as agents converge. Final answers are selected via majority voting. A key finding is that heterogeneous agent groups with complementary tools (code + search) outperform repeated sampling from a single best agent, and that prompting Gemini-2.5-Pro to auto-generate 15 additional diverse agents from the existing pool yields a further +1.2% average improvement. The framework is formalized as sequential decision-making under a compute budget, optimizing a policy over agent selection, communication graph, stopping rule, and aggregation rule.

## Results

On Gemini-2.5-Pro, TUMIX achieves 32.3% on HLE, 87.9% on GPQA Diamond, and 96.7% on AIME 24&25, yielding a normalized average of 72.3%—an average +3.55% improvement over the best baseline (Symbolic-MoE at 70.3%). On Gemini-2.5-Flash, TUMIX reaches 21.2% (HLE), 77.3% (GPQA), and 83.3% (AIME), a normalized average of 60.6% versus 55.5% for the best baseline (DEI). With further scaling (TUMIX+), HLE accuracy on Gemini-2.5-Pro rises to 34.1%, surpassing Gemini-2.5-Pro Deep Research (26.9%). The LLM-as-Judge termination strategy achieves near-optimal accuracy at only 49% of the inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TUMIX outperforms all eight baselines across every benchmark and both model backbones. Against the strongest baselines, TUMIX gains +2.8% on HLE (32.3% vs. Symbolic-MoE's 29.5%), +1.0% on GPQA (87.9% vs. SciMaster's 86.9%), and +1.7% on AIME (96.7% vs. DEI's 95.0%) with Gemini-2.5-Pro. The gap widens substantially on Gemini-2.5-Flash, where TUMIX leads by +1.9% on HLE (21.2% vs. DEI's 19.3%), +9.4% on GPQA (77.3% vs. SciMaster's 67.9%), and +1.0% on AIME (83.3% vs. DEI's 82.3%). Methods without answer sharing (Self-Reflection, SETS) or without multi-round refinement (Majority-Vote, GSA) trail TUMIX by 3–7 points on average, confirming that both iterative refinement and cross-agent communication are essential. Self-MoA, which repeatedly samples a single best agent, underperforms TUMIX by 2–3 points, validating the paper's finding that agent diversity outweighs scale. Statistical significance (paired t-tests, p < 0.05) is confirmed for nearly all comparisons, with the sole exception being TUMIX vs. DEI on AIME with Gemini-2.5-Flash (p = 0.135).

## Relation to Prior Work

TUMIX sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: multi-agent test-time scaling (Mixture-of-Agents, Self-MoA, DEI, GSA, SETS, Symbolic-MoE) and tool-augmented reasoning (Code Interpreter, Web Search, CodeSteer). Prior multi-agent scaling methods operate on text-only agents, while prior tool-augmented methods (SciMaster, ToRL, ReTool) focus on single-agent or limited multi-agent settings without systematic diversity. TUMIX unifies these by running diverse tool-augmented agents in parallel with iterative answer sharing, and notably departs from Self-MoA's conclusion that the single best agent repeated outperforms diverse ensembles—here, in the tool-augmented setting, heterogeneous agent strategies consistently yield higher accuracy and coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic empirical study of how to combine Code Interpreter, Search, and textual reasoning within a multi-agent test-time scaling framework, filling a gap the authors identify as absent from both industry practice (undisclosed methods) and academic literature. Key open questions remain: answer selection among noisy candidates plateaus accuracy (~34% on HLE despite ≥65% coverage), agent diversity gains saturate beyond ~12 agents, and the framework is validated primarily on Gemini models. The finding that LLMs can auto-design complementary agents and that adaptive termination halves inference cost without accuracy loss offers practical recipes, but whether these transfer to open-weight models or non-Gemini tool ecosystems remains untested.
