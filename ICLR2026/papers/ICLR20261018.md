## Problem

Modern agentic workflows select compositions of specialized AI agents to complete complex tasks, but existing systems optimize only for task success and ignore tail risks (safety, fairness, privacy violations) that emerge from low-probability behaviors of individual agents. The challenge is compounded by the exponential number of feasible agent compositions in an agent graph and by the fact that the relevant loss for a composition is the maximum (not cumulative) loss across its agents, which differs from the cumulative-loss setting studied in prior risk-sensitive planning literature.

## Input / Output

The method requires only black-box sampling access to each agent (no analytical distribution access) and takes as input an agent graph with per-edge loss functions over traces, a risk budget α, a sample size n, and a discretization factor d. It outputs the optimal path (agent composition), a VaR estimate for that path, a CVaR estimate recovered as the average of VaR estimates at d intermediate risk levels, and the per-edge risk budget allocation. The loss functions operate on execution traces (trajectories for RL control policies, intermediate token sequences for LLM agents), decoupling risk from task accuracy.

## Method

The core algorithm, BucketedVaR, is a dynamic programming procedure that traverses the agent graph in topological order and incrementally builds a near-optimal path. Its key insight is a union-bound decomposition: the VaR of the maximum loss across m agents is upper-bounded by the maximum of individual agent VaRs, provided the total risk budget α is split into per-agent allocations α_i summing to α. The algorithm discretizes the budget into d buckets and, at each vertex, considers all predecessor vertices and all sub-budget allocations to select the path minimizing the upper bound on VaR. Theorem 1 establishes correctness via the DKW inequality (the estimate is at least the (1−α−γ)-quantile with γ → 0 as n → ∞), and Theorem 2 proves asymptotic near-optimality under independence: the returned estimate is at most the (1−α+α²/2)-quantile of the true optimal path. CVaR is then approximated as a byproduct by averaging the VaR estimates computed at each of the d bucket levels.

## Results

Across all four benchmarks (DroneNav, 16-Rooms, Fetch, BoxRelay), BucketedVaR finds the same optimal agent composition as the brute-force baseline and produces VaR estimates within 1–2 percentage points of the desired quantile (e.g., 91.24% empirical quantile vs. 90% target at α=0.1 on 16-Rooms). The CVaR approximations are similarly tight, with empirical coverages within ~1–2% of the baseline (e.g., 95.26% vs. 94.34% on 16-Rooms at α=0.1). The algorithm remains robust to correlated losses up to ρ=0.5 and maintains tight approximations as path length scales from 8 to 40 agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is a brute-force algorithm that explicitly estimates VaR_α and CVaR_α along every path in the agent graph (exponential in the number of agents). BucketedVaR matches the baseline's optimal path selection across all four benchmarks and all risk levels tested. On DroneNav, the VaR estimates are identical to the baseline (e.g., −0.339 at α=0.2). On 16-Rooms (8 agents per path, 16 total paths), the VaR estimate differs by at most 0.0024 (−0.0253 vs. −0.0277 at α=0.2), with empirical quantiles within 1.5 percentage points. On Fetch and BoxRelay, differences are similarly small (≤0.006 and ≤3 time-steps respectively). The key advantage is computational: BucketedVaR runs in O(n(d+1)²|V|²) time versus exponential enumeration, while maintaining near-optimal quantile estimates (within α²/2 of the true optimal quantile asymptotically under independence).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the classical risk-sensitive planning literature (Howard & Matheson 1972; Whittle 1981; Ahmadi et al. 2021; Kretínský & Meggendorfer 2018), which optimizes risk measures of *cumulative* loss over MDP trajectories, by instead minimizing risk of the *maximum* loss across a composition of agents. It also extends compositional RL frameworks (Jothimurugan et al. 2021) that optimize success probability over task graphs, and agentic workflow systems (Zhang et al. 2025a; Niu et al. 2025) that optimize task accuracy, by introducing a risk-minimization objective over agent compositions. The method itself combines a union-bound quantile decomposition with dynamic programming over a discretized risk budget, a combination not previously applied to the compositional agent-graph setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As agentic systems (LLM pipelines, multi-agent robot control) are deployed in safety-critical settings, the ability to select compositions that provably bound tail risk—rather than just expected performance—becomes essential. The paper's main limitation is the independence assumption on agent losses, which holds for behavior-based metrics (safety, fairness, privacy) but may fail for performance-coupled losses; the empirical robustness to moderate correlation (ρ ≤ 0.5) is encouraging but not formally guaranteed. Open directions include extending to other coherent risk measures, online bandit-style composition selection to reduce sample complexity, and integration into existing agentic frameworks at scale.
