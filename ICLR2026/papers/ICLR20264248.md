## Problem

Existing long-range graph benchmarks such as LRGB have reached performance saturation, and recent analyses reveal that several of their tasks are effectively local rather than truly requiring global information propagation. There is no benchmark that explicitly enforces a defined propagation range (17–40 hops) across both controlled synthetic topologies with structural bottlenecks and real-world scientific tasks where long-range dependencies are physically grounded.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264248/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For ECHO-Synth, each graph carries a uniformly random scalar node feature (plus a binary source indicator for sssp) and no edge features; targets are per-node shortest-path distances, per-node eccentricity, or a single graph-level diameter. For ECHO-Chem, molecular graphs use atomic number and distance-from-center-of-mass as node features (E(3)-invariant) and bond type plus bond length as edge features; targets are per-atom Löwdin partial charges (node-level regression) or total molecular energy (graph-level regression). All graphs span diameters of 17–40 hops, and the synthetic set comprises 10,080 graphs across six topology families while the chemical sets contain ~170K and ~196K molecules drawn from ChEMBL.

## Method

ECHO is constructed in two complementary parts. ECHO-Synth generates graphs in six topology families (line, ladder, grid, tree, caterpillar, lobster) with intentional structural bottlenecks that constrain information flow through narrow intermediary nodes, then poses three shortest-path-based prediction tasks that force complete graph traversal analogous to Bellman-Ford. ECHO-Chem selects ChEMBL molecules with diameters 17–40, generates 3D conformers via GAFF force-field optimization, and computes ground-truth partial charges and total energies using B3LYP/def2-TZVP DFT in ORCA with TightSCF convergence and Löwdin population analysis (~2 months of parallel computation on 288 cores). The evaluation protocol trains 11 GNN architectures (GCN, GIN, GCNII, GINE, GPS, GRIT, DRew, GraphCON, A-DGN, SWAN, PH-DGN) under a unified backbone with log-MSE loss, Adam optimizer, early stopping, and 100-trial Bayesian hyperparameter optimization per model-dataset pair, revealing that global-attention transformers and non-dissipative/port-Hamiltonian dynamics consistently outperform classical message-passing on all five tasks.

## Results

On ECHO-Synth, GRIT achieves the lowest MAE on sssp (0.121) and diam (1.014), while DRew leads on ecc (4.651); traditional message-passing models (GCN, GIN) show MAE 2–4× higher across all three tasks. On ECHO-Chem, GPS attains the best ECHO-Energy MAE (5.257), and SWAN achieves the best ECHO-Charge MAE (6.109×10⁻³), both substantially outperforming GCN (28.112 Energy, 8.421×10⁻³ Charge) and GIN (47.851 Energy, 10.784×10⁻³ Charge). Across all five tasks, non-dissipative DE-GNNs (A-DGN, SWAN, PH-DGN) and graph transformers (GPS, GRIT) consistently outperform classical MPNNs, confirming the benchmark's sensitivity to long-range propagation capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 11 baselines, graph transformers dominate the synthetic tasks: GRIT achieves the best sssp MAE (0.121) and diam MAE (1.014), while GPS is competitive on sssp (0.472) but weaker on diam (2.160). DRew, a multi-hop rewiring GNN, uniquely leads on ecc (4.651), demonstrating the value of adaptive multi-hop propagation for node-level global properties. Non-dissipative DE-GNNs (SWAN, A-DGN, PH-DGN) form a consistent second tier, with SWAN achieving the best ECHO-Charge MAE (6.109×10⁻³) and A-DGN remaining competitive across all five tasks. Classical MPNNs (GCN, GIN, GCNII) are clearly outperformed: GCN records the worst diam MAE (3.832) and GIN the worst ECHO-Energy MAE (47.851), a gap of 4–9× over the best models. GraphCON, despite being designed to mitigate over-smoothing, underperforms its DE-GNN siblings (A-DGN, SWAN) on every task, indicating that addressing over-smoothing alone is insufficient for long-range propagation. On ECHO-Chem, GPS's global attention gives it the best Energy MAE (5.257), but at higher computational cost; SWAN and A-DGN offer a more favorable accuracy–efficiency trade-off, with SWAN leading on Charge (6.109×10⁻³ vs. GPS's 6.182×10⁻³).

## Relation to Prior Work

ECHO positions itself as a successor to LRGB (Dwivedi et al., 2022), which the authors argue has plateaued and whose tasks have been questioned for genuinely requiring long-range propagation. Conceptually, it draws on the over-smoothing and over-squashing literature (Alon & Yahav, 2021; Cai & Wang, 2020) but deliberately reframes the evaluation around explicit propagation distance (17–40 hops) rather than specific pathologies. The synthetic tasks (shortest paths, eccentricity, diameter) extend algorithmic graph benchmarks like GPP (Gravina et al., 2023) by introducing topological bottlenecks and controlled diameter ranges, while the chemistry tasks repurpose DFT-level quantum computation as ground truth for a setting no prior graph benchmark has targeted at the atomic level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With LRGB showing performance saturation and questions about its long-range validity, the field needs a new standard, and ECHO provides one with clearly defined propagation ranges and both controlled synthetic and physically grounded real-world tasks. The ECHO-Chem datasets are particularly impactful: they are the first graph benchmarks targeting long-range atomic-level interactions with DFT-level accuracy, and the ~2 months of parallel quantum computation required to generate them signals both the difficulty and the practical value for computational chemistry. Open questions include whether any architecture can close the remaining performance gap at 40-hop ranges, how the compute-accuracy tradeoff (transformers vs. non-dissipative dynamics) resolves at scale, and whether the synthetic bottleneck topologies generalize to more complex real-world graph structures.
