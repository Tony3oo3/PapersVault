## Problem

The standard two-stage LLM training pipeline (pre-training → post-training) overlooks a critical intermediate mid-training stage where RL can be applied to large-scale unlabeled data to strengthen reasoning before post-training. The sole prior attempt, Reinforcement Pre-Training (RPT), suffers from three unresolved issues: unconstrained overthinking inflating computation, indiscriminate sampling that ignores the imbalanced token entropy distribution, and exclusion of the vast majority of low-entropy tokens from training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261107/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each training step, the model receives a prefix context (preceding tokens) augmented with a budget-aware instruction specifying exactly B_t tokens for the reasoning segment, where B_t decays exponentially from 800 to 1 over training. The model outputs a structured response consisting of a chain-of-thought reasoning trace (constrained to the budget) followed by a single predicted next token. The final product is an updated model checkpoint that can be directly fed into a subsequent post-training stage (e.g., GRPO on Skywork), with the key property that reasoning length shrinks to ~21% of RPT while accuracy improves.

## Method

RMT combines three mechanisms into a unified training loop. First, a Dynamic Token Budget (DTB) sets a step-dependent budget B_t = max(B_min, ⌊B₀·γ^(t/T)⌋) and enforces it via a triangular length reward that peaks at B_t and drops to zero beyond 2B_t, progressively compressing reasoning as the model matures. Second, Curriculum-Based Adaptive Sampling (CAS) partitions tokens into easy/medium/hard bins by entropy and interpolates sampling probabilities piecewise-linearly across two transition points (30% and 70% of training), shifting focus from easy to hard tokens. Third, a dual training objective applies GRPO-based token-selective RL (with composite reward = exact-match reward + length reward, group-relative whitened advantages, and KL regularization) on the sampled high-entropy tokens, while simultaneously performing masked next-token prediction on all remaining low-entropy tokens; the two losses are combined as L = L_RL + λ·L_NTP (λ=0.1), ensuring every token contributes to learning.

## Results

On the OmniMATH language modeling benchmark, RMT-Q3 achieves 59.12% average accuracy across easy/medium/hard token difficulty levels, surpassing the strongest baseline RPT (35.85%) by +64.91% and the base Qwen3-14B NTP (35.84%) by +64.91%. RMT-R1 achieves 46.87% average, a +30.74% improvement over RPT. In continual post-training on Skywork, RMT-Q3 jumps from 25.17% to 64.33% accuracy, outperforming Qwen3-14B (54.17%) by +18.76% after post-training. Notably, RMT reduces reasoning length to ~186–188 tokens versus 872 for RPT (21%) and 1577 for Qwen3-14B (12%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is RPT (Reinforcement Pre-Training, Dong et al. 2025), the only prior method applying RL at the mid-training stage. RMT-Q3 outperforms RPT by +64.91% average accuracy on OmniMATH (59.12 vs. 35.85), with the largest gap on hard tokens (+93.83%, 44.64 vs. 23.03). RMT-R1 also beats RPT by +30.74% on average (46.87 vs. 35.85). In the continual post-training setting on Skywork, RMT-Q3 achieves 64.33% after post-training versus RPT's 48.50%, a +18.76% margin over the strongest post-trained baseline (Qwen3-14B at 54.17%). RMT also dramatically reduces reasoning length: 186–188 tokens vs. RPT's 872 tokens (79% shorter), while maintaining superior accuracy. The base models in NTR mode (R1-Distill-14B at 3.09%, Qwen3-14B at 6.02%) perform far worse than even NTP, confirming that naive chain-of-thought without RL training is ineffective for token prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Reinforcement Pre-Training (RPT) approach of Dong et al. (2025), which first applied RL to what is technically a mid-training stage, by formally defining 'Reinforcement Mid-Training' as a distinct problem and identifying three specific failure modes of RPT: unconstrained overthinking, indiscriminate sampling of high-entropy tokens, and neglect of low-entropy tokens. It departs from RPT by introducing a dynamic token budget mechanism, curriculum-based adaptive sampling over entropy levels, and a unified RL+NTP objective, positioning the work within the emerging mid-training paradigm (Wang et al., 2025b; OLMo et al., 2025) that sits between pre-training and post-training. The RL component builds on GRPO, while the curriculum and dual-objective design draw from curriculum learning and the SFT+RL combination literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes reinforcement mid-training as a formally defined, practically useful stage in the LLM lifecycle, showing that checkpoints from this stage transfer to post-training with up to +18.76% gains in math. The efficiency result—achieving superior accuracy with only 21% of the reasoning length of RPT—is practically significant for reducing compute costs in large-scale training. However, the evidence base is narrow: experiments use a single 14B-scale model family, one language-modeling dataset (OmniMATH), and a relatively weak baseline (RPT barely exceeds the base model). Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, generalizes beyond mathematical token prediction to broader reasoning domains, and how the dynamic budget and curriculum hyperparameters should be tuned at scale.
