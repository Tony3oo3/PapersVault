## Problem

Standard attention stores the full KV-cache losslessly but reads it via a per-head convex average, which geometrically prevents per-channel selection: different output channels cannot attend to different past indices in the same step. The paper formalizes this as a 'lossless storage vs. lossy processing' gap and shows that common remedies (more heads, more depth, in-head gates, per-dimension scoring) all fail to close it without prohibitive cost or fixed-state storage.

## Input / Output

FEM is a drop-in replacement for the attention module inside a standard Transformer block, consuming the same Q, K, V projections and producing an output of identical shape. Under the default parameter budgeting (d = D/2, r = 4), the value path uses half the dimensionality of standard attention while matching the 4D² parameter count, and the method is applied identically to text sequences (4096 context), image patches (DeiT encoder), and multivariate time series. No architectural changes to embeddings, MLPs, or positional encodings are required.

## Method

FEM replaces the convex expectation read with a per-channel free-energy (log-sum-exp) read: given a prior selection distribution p_t (from softmax, kernel, RNN, or SSM), each channel j computes F_{t,j}(β) = (1/β) log Σ_i p_t(i) exp(β v_{i,j}), which yields a value-aware posterior q ∝ p_t · exp(β v_{·,j}) that smoothly interpolates from averaging (β→0) to hard per-channel indexing (β→∞). To avoid recomputing the LSE for each learned temperature, Linearized Temperature Learning (LTL) fixes a maximum β_max and uses a learned gate λ ∈ [0,1] to interpolate between the expectation baseline μ_t and a single high-temperature LSE branch, provably equivalent to a hidden temperature β_hid ∈ [0, β_max]. A two-level gated design adds an outer per-channel scaling gate g_t (RMSNorm-normalized) for output modulation, and a lightweight low-rank convolution provides local conditioning. The entire mechanism adds one masked log-sum-exp per channel on top of the prior, preserving O(T²) for softmax and O(T) for linear/SSM priors, and is instantiated as FEM-SM, FEM-GLA, FEM-Mamba, and FEM-AFT.

## Results

On the MAD benchmark, full FEM-SM achieves an average score of 80.2, outperforming the strongest baseline (Differential Transformer, 76.4) by 3.8 points, with particularly large gains on Compress (53.1 vs. 42.9) and FuzzyRecall (43.1 vs. 39.0). On ImageNet-1K, FEM-SM in DeiT-Tiny reaches 76.70% Top-1 accuracy (vs. 72.20% for DeiT and 75.39% for HGRN2), and in DeiT-Small reaches 80.45% (vs. 79.90% DeiT). At 340M parameters on language modeling, FEM-SM achieves the best average rank (1.81) with 8 first-place scores across 16 benchmarks, while FEM-GLA at 1.3B attains the best AvgRank (3.88) in its group. On time-series forecasting, FEM variants consistently match or beat their priors and domain-specific baselines (iTransformer, PatchTST, DLinear) across all six datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEM-SM (full) dominates all six baselines on the MAD benchmark, achieving 80.2 average vs. DiffTrans (76.4), GatedDeltaNet (74.9), Hyena (73.2), Mamba2 (73.0), DeltaNet (71.7), and LinAttn (62.6), with the largest margins on Compress (+10.2 over DiffTrans) and FuzzyRecall (+4.1). On ImageNet-1K, FEM-SM in DeiT-Tiny reaches 76.70% Top-1, surpassing DeiT (72.20%), TNN (72.29%), HGRN (74.40%), and HGRN2 (75.39%) while using fewer parameters (5.8M vs. 6.1M for HGRN2); FEM-GLA also beats all baselines at 75.80%. In language modeling at 340M parameters, FEM-SM achieves the best AvgRank (1.81) with 8 first-place scores, outperforming DiffTrans/GatedDeltaNet (4.38) and DeltaNet (4.25); at 1.3B, FEM-GLA leads its group with AvgRank 3.88 vs. GSA (3.38 for the overall best) and GLA (5.63). FEM-GLA elevates the linear-time GLA prior from 64.2 to 74.9 average on MAD, closing most of the gap to softmax-based methods. On time-series forecasting, FEM-SM and FEM-GLA match or slightly beat their respective priors (GLA, AFT) and domain-specific baselines (iTransformer, PatchTST, DLinear) across Weather, Solar, and ETT datasets, with FEM-AFT achieving the best ETTh1 score (0.414). FEM-SM's full model is slightly slower than the fastest baselines (HGRN2: 0.009s fwd) but comparable to DeltaNet and GatedDeltaNet, confirming that the added LSE and gating overhead is modest.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEM departs from the dominant line of attention improvements (sparsity, low-rank projections, kernelized/linear variants, SSMs) that focus on enriching the (q,k) scoring distribution while retaining a token-separable linear read. Instead, it treats the existing selection distribution as a prior and applies a value-driven, per-channel log-sum-exp (free-energy) tilt to produce a value-aware posterior, grounded in the Donsker–Varadhan variational principle. The architecture is prior-agnostic, plugging into softmax attention, gated linear attention, Mamba-style SSMs, and linear RNNs (AFT), and adopts low-rank convolution from Mamba/DeltaNet for local conditioning. The paper explicitly contrasts FEM with per-dimension score inflation, in-head pointwise gating, and depth-based remedies, showing via convex-hull arguments that none of these close the per-channel selection gap at unchanged complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a fundamental geometric limitation (per-head convex mixing cannot realize channel-wise index selection) provides a clear theoretical target that prior attention variants implicitly ignored, and the free-energy read offers a principled, complexity-preserving fix. However, the empirical validation is constrained to modest scales (≤1.3B parameters, 4K context) without custom fused kernels, leaving open whether the gains persist at the scale and context lengths where attention bottlenecks are most acute. The plug-and-play design and consistent (if sometimes modest) improvements across NLP, vision, and time-series suggest practical utility, but the absence of very-long-context or large-scale evaluations limits confidence in the method's broader impact.
