## Problem

In centralized distributed optimization (federated learning), it is well known that methods like Synchronized SGD and compressed variants scale their statistical and communication complexity terms with the number of workers n when only worker-to-server communication is considered. However, when server-to-worker communication time τ_s is also non-zero, it was unclear whether any method using unbiased random sparsification compressors could simultaneously improve both the dimension-dependent term (τ_s·d·LΔ/ε) and the variance-dependent term (hσ²LΔ/ε²) with n. This paper proves that the answer is no: even in the homogeneous (i.i.d.) setting, it is infeasible to scale both dependencies better than polylogarithmically in n.

## Input / Output

The lower bound applies to any zero-respecting algorithm operating under Protocol 1, where n workers compute stochastic gradients (each taking at most h seconds) of an L-smooth, d-dimensional nonconvex function with σ²-bounded variance, and communicate via unbiased random sparsification compressors with per-coordinate times τ_s (server-to-worker) and τ_w (worker-to-server). The output is a time complexity lower bound of the form Ω̃(min{h(σ²/(nε)+1)·LΔ/ε + τ_w(d/n+1)·LΔ/ε + √(dτ_whσ²/(nε))·LΔ/ε + τ_s·d·LΔ/ε, h·LΔ/ε + hσ²LΔ/ε²}), valid for all parameter regimes with LΔ ≥ Θ̃(ε) and d ≥ Θ̃(LΔ/ε). The bound is tight up to logarithmic factors and is matched either by Batch QSGD or by non-distributed local SGD depending on which term in the min dominates.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a new worst-case function F_{T,K,a} that extends Carmon et al.'s chain-like F_T by replacing each single Ψ(x_{i-1}) factor with a K-fold product Ψ_a(x_{i-K})…Ψ_a(x_{i-1}), so that a worker must have K consecutive non-zero coordinates to discover the next one. This K-fold dependency is the key mechanism that neutralizes the 'luckiest worker' effect: while the min over n workers still appears in the analysis, the inner sum over K/2 geometric-like random variables (representing time to fill K/2 coordinates via stochastic gradients or server communication) causes the overall scaling to degrade from n to n^{1/K}. The proof reduces the lower bound to a concentration inequality for a sum of these geometric-like variables, and the parameter a ∈ (1, e] is tuned to control the smoothness constant ℓ_1(K,a) and function gap Δ⁰(K,a) that grow with K. Setting K = Θ(log n) and a = 1 + 1/K yields the final polylogarithmic dependence on n, establishing that no unbiased sparsification-based method can achieve better than logarithmic scaling in both d and σ²/ε simultaneously.

## Results

The paper proves a near-tight lower bound (Theorem 1.6) showing that in centralized distributed optimization with bidirectional communication, it is infeasible to scale both the dimension d and the variance term σ²/ε better than polylogarithmically in the number of workers n, even in the homogeneous (i.i.d.) setting. Specifically, when τ_s ≃ τ_w, the lower bound is Ω̃(min{h(σ²/(nε)+1)·LΔ/ε + τ_s·d·LΔ/ε, h·LΔ/ε + h·σ²·LΔ/ε²}), meaning the server-to-worker communication term τ_s·d·LΔ/ε cannot be improved with n. The bound is tight up to logarithmic factors and is matched by either Batch QSGD (with compression) or vanilla non-distributed SGD (without any communication).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical lower-bound paper, so there are no empirical baselines. The key comparison is between the proven lower bound and the upper bounds of existing methods. When τ_s ≃ τ_w, the lower bound reduces to Ω̃(min{h(σ²/(nε)+1)·LΔ/ε + τ_s·d·LΔ/ε, h·LΔ/ε + h·σ²·LΔ/ε²}), which is matched by Batch Synchronized SGD (no compression) in the first regime or by non-distributed SGD in the second regime. This means unbiased sparsification compression (e.g., RandK, QSGD) provides no asymptotic advantage over uncompressed methods when server-to-worker communication is non-negligible. The result extends the heterogeneous-setting lower bound of Gruntkowska et al. (2024), which showed no scaling with n at all, to the homogeneous (i.i.d.) setting where one might hope for better scaling. The new F_{T,K,a} construction (with K-fold Ψ products) is the key technical contribution that prevents the 'luckiest worker' argument from giving a 1/n improvement, reducing the scaling to at most n^{1/K} ≈ polylog(n).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Carmon et al. (2020) chain-like worst-case function F_T into a new construction F_{T,K,a} that requires K consecutive non-zero coordinates to make progress, specifically designed to defeat the 'luckiest worker' argument that previously gave linear scaling in n for the homogeneous setting. It builds on the stochastic oracle construction of Arjevani et al. (2022) and the time-complexity lower bound framework of Tyurin et al. (2024), while directly addressing the gap left by Gruntkowska et al. (2024), who proved no scaling in the heterogeneous case but left the homogeneous case open. The key conceptual departure is showing that the homogeneous (i.i.d.) assumption, previously thought to enable better collaboration among workers, does not rescue scalability once bidirectional communication costs are accounted for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is a fundamental negative result: it establishes that the primary motivation for distributed optimization—linear or square-root scaling with the number of workers—cannot be achieved for both dimension d and variance σ²/ε simultaneously when server-to-worker communication is non-negligible, even under the simplest i.i.d. assumption. Practically, when τ_s ≈ τ_w (the realistic case), unbiased sparsification compression offers no advantage over uncompressed Batch Synchronized SGD, which may redirect practitioner effort toward other compression strategies or architectural changes. Open questions include whether the polylogarithmic gaps can be closed, whether the bounds extend to all unbiased compressors beyond random sparsification, and whether additional structural assumptions (convexity, second-order smoothness) can break the limitation.
