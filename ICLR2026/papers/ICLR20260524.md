## Problem

Equilibrium Propagation (EP) is a biologically plausible learning framework that avoids explicit activation derivatives, but existing implementations suffer from slow convergence (dozens to hundreds of iterations per sample) and instability, making them computationally prohibitive. Additionally, deep RNNs trained with EP face vanishing gradients when feedback pathways are weakened for stability, limiting the framework's applicability to deeper architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260524/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes static inputs—28×28 grayscale MNIST digits or 32×32 RGB CIFAR-10 images—which drive the RNN's recurrent dynamics until convergence over T discrete steps. The output is a 10-class probability vector produced by a SoftMax layer applied to the final hidden state. A key operational detail is that the input remains fixed throughout both the free phase (T iterations) and the nudged phase (K iterations), where the prediction error is injected as a feedback signal scaled by a nudging factor β_f.

## Method

The paper proposes FRE-RNN, which introduces two brain-inspired modifications to the EP framework. First, feedback regulation scales all recurrent (feedback) weights by a coefficient β_i (typically 0.01–0.25), reducing the spectral radius of the network and accelerating convergence from ~100 iterations to 10–20, while leaving feedforward weights unscaled to preserve signal propagation. Second, residual connections are added between non-adjacent layers—symmetric bidirectional links for symmetric RNNs and stochastic arbitrary-graph-topology (AGT) skip connections for asymmetric RNNs—to restore gradient flow in deep networks (up to 20 hidden layers). Training follows the standard two-phase EP protocol with STDP-compatible weight updates (ΔW_i = ds_{i+1}·(s_i^0)^T), and the paper provides a theoretical derivation showing that under the infinitesimal inference limit (weak feedback + weak supervision), EP, Local Representation Alignment, and Backpropagation become equivalent.

## Results

On MNIST, FRE-RNN with feedback scaling (βᵢ=0.01) achieves 98.39% test accuracy on a 2-hidden-layer model and 98.36% on a 3-hidden-layer model, matching or exceeding BP (98.26% and 98.36%) while training in 1 min 16 s and 2 min 11 s respectively—over 90× faster than P-EP (1 h 56 min and 8 h 27 min). On the convolutional architecture, FRE-RNN reaches 99.14% test accuracy (vs. 98.98% P-EP and 98.93% BP) in 12 min 28 s versus 8 h 58 min for P-EP. On CIFAR-10 with a convolutional RNN, FRE-RNN achieves 75.04% test accuracy, within 0.4% of BP (75.45%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against P-EP (Ernoult et al., 2019), FRE-RNN wins on both accuracy and speed across all three architectures: it improves test accuracy by 0.34–0.44 percentage points while reducing wall-clock training time by 1–2 orders of magnitude (e.g., 1 min 16 s vs. 1 h 56 min for 2HL; 12 min 28 s vs. 8 h 58 min for Conv). Against BP, FRE-RNN matches accuracy on shallow architectures (98.39% vs. 98.26% for 2HL; 98.36% vs. 98.36% for 3HL; 99.14% vs. 98.93% for Conv) but requires roughly 4–12× more wall-clock time due to the iterative convergence steps. Against Feedback Alignment (Lillicrap et al., 2016) on asymmetric 5-layer RNNs, FRE-RNN is essentially tied on MNIST (96.37% vs. 96.44%) but substantially outperforms FA on CIFAR-10 (45.27% vs. 37.97%). For deeper 10-layer asymmetric RNNs, the AGT (arbitrary graph topology) variant with residual connections surpasses FA on MNIST (96.87% vs. 94.52%) but still lags on CIFAR-10 (30.94% vs. 30.16%, within noise). Overall, the method achieves BP-level accuracy on shallow networks with a biologically plausible local learning rule, at the cost of moderate additional training time compared to BP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Equilibrium Propagation (Scellier & Bengio, 2017) and its prototypical convolutional RNN formulation (Ernoult et al., 2019) by introducing two brain-inspired structural modifications: feedback scaling to accelerate convergence and residual connections to combat vanishing gradients in deep RNNs. It departs from P-EP by separating input/output layers from the recurrent core and by adopting the vector-field EP formulation (Scellier et al., 2018) to handle asymmetric connections. The theoretical analysis further unifies EP with Local Representation Alignment and backpropagation under the infinitesimal inference limit, connecting to the broader family of contrastive Hebbian and predictive coding frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orders-of-magnitude speedup over P-EP (e.g., ~10× for layered, ~40× for convolutional architectures) makes EP a more viable candidate for in-situ learning on neuromorphic hardware, where iterative convergence is the dominant cost. However, a notable performance gap persists on CIFAR-10 with deep fully connected networks, and hyperparameters (β_i range) remain empirically tuned rather than derived from first principles. Extending naturally converging EP-trained RNNs to sequence tasks and deep CNNs remains an open challenge, as does finding a principled method for selecting feedback scaling and residual topology in large-scale architectures.
