## Problem

Point cloud diffusion models are severely limited to low resolutions (2048–4096 points) because vanilla transformer architectures scale quadratically with the number of input points, making high-resolution generation computationally prohibitive. This paper asks whether a model can be trained efficiently at low resolution yet generate arbitrarily high-resolution point clouds at inference, and whether such test-time resolution scaling can actually improve output quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241927/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB-D image (4×h×w) as conditioning and produces a point cloud of n×6 values (3 RGB + 3 XYZ channels) at a user-chosen resolution n_test ranging from 1024 to 131,072 points, while being trained only at n_train = 1024. The point clouds are sourced from noisy COLMAP multi-view reconstructions in CO3D-v2, and the dense outputs (≥16k points) enable direct surface extraction via a mean-distance field and marching cubes, yielding continuous mesh surfaces.

## Method

PointInfinity uses a two-stream transformer denoiser that decouples a fixed-size latent representation (454 tokens) modeling the underlying 3D surface from a variable-size data stream representing the noisy point cloud. Each of L=6 two-stream blocks performs a read cross-attention (data→latent), H=4 self-attention transformer layers on the latent, and a write cross-attention (latent→data), making computation linear rather than quadratic in point count. The key insight is that the fixed-size latent captures the surface geometry, so at test time more input points simply feed more information into the read operator, yielding a more consistent denoising trajectory and measurably better surface fidelity (CD@1k improves from 0.227 at 1k to 0.181 at 8k). The authors show this fidelity-variability trade-off is analogous to classifier-free guidance but strictly superior, as resolution scaling improves fidelity monotonically without the degradation seen at high guidance scales.

## Results

On CO3D-v2, PointInfinity (tested at 16k resolution) achieves a CD@1k of 0.179, F-score of 0.724, and PSNR of 14.31, outperforming Point-E (0.197 / 0.675 / 14.25) by 9% on CD@1k and MCC (0.234 / 0.549 / 14.03) by 24% on CD@1k. A key finding is that test-time resolution scaling from 1024 (training) up to 131k points *improves* surface quality (CD@1k drops from 0.227 to 0.181 at 8192 points), whereas Point-E's performance degrades under the same scaling. The model is trained at only 1024 points yet generates outputs 31× denser than Point-E's 4096-point limit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointInfinity outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against Point-E [36] (vanilla transformer point diffusion), PointInfinity achieves 9% lower CD@1k (0.179 vs. 0.197), 7% higher F-score (0.724 vs. 0.675), and marginally better PSNR (14.31 vs. 14.25); critically, Point-E's quality *degrades* when test resolution exceeds training resolution, while PointInfinity's improves. Against MCC [51] (deterministic regression-based reconstruction), PointInfinity is 24% better on CD@1k and 32% better on F-score, confirming the advantage of diffusion-based stochastic modeling over deterministic regression. In the unconditional comparison (Table 5), PointInfinity's relative CD improves from 1.000 to 0.797 at 8× resolution, whereas PVD [32] degrades to 4.221 and ShapeGF [2] stays flat at ~1.0, demonstrating superior resolution invariance even against models designed for different generation scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointInfinity builds on the DDPM framework and transformer-based point cloud diffusion models (Point-E, PVD) but departs from their vanilla transformer backbones, which are resolution-specific and scale quadratically. The core architectural idea—a two-stream transformer with a fixed-size latent stream and a variable-size data stream connected by read/write cross-attention—repurposes the Perceiver/PerceiverIO design originally developed for multi-modal recognition, adapting it to denoising diffusion. For conditioning, it reuses the MCC encoder but replaces MCC's deterministic regression with a stochastic diffusion process, and it draws an analytical parallel between test-time resolution scaling and classifier-free guidance, a connection not previously explored in 3D generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single model trained at 1,024 points can generate up to 131k points at inference with linear (not quadratic) compute scaling, removing the need for separate upsamplers or retraining at each resolution. The counterintuitive finding that higher test-time resolution improves surface fidelity (not just density) opens a new inference-time knob for quality control, analogous to guidance scale but without the diversity collapse seen at high guidance values. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (CO3D-v2), modest absolute gains (CD@1k improves ~20% relative from 1024 to 8192), and the method remains tied to point cloud output rather than producing meshes or implicit surfaces natively. Open questions include whether the resolution-quality trade-off persists at even higher resolutions, whether it transfers to text-conditioned or multi-object generation, and whether the two-stream pattern generalizes to other 3D representations.
