## Problem

CNN-based backbones for sign language recognition treat sign frames as grids and extract local features via sliding kernels, which captures texture and contours but fails to model the explicit correlation between different body regions (e.g., hands and face) within a frame or the interaction of sub-regions across adjacent frames. Temporal modules like LSTM and Transformer further compound this by treating each frame as a monolithic unit, ignoring cross-region dynamics that are essential for identifying a sign.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of RGB sign language video frames (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224) and outputs a continuous gloss sequence via CTC decoding. Frames are converted into 16×16 patches (nodes) using the first four stages of ResNet18 as a patchify stem, with no reliance on skeleton, depth, or other auxiliary modalities. The output is a variable-length gloss sequence scored by Word Error Rate against ground-truth annotations.

## Method

SignGraph represents each sign frame as a dynamic graph whose nodes are image patches and whose edges are built on-the-fly via KNN in a learned feature space. The Local Sign Graph (LSG) module constructs a dense intra-frame graph (top-K_l neighbors per node) and applies graph convolution to aggregate cross-region features within a single frame, while the Temporal Sign Graph (TSG) module builds a sparse inter-frame graph (top-K_t node pairs between adjacent frames, with a 0.05 threshold to filter background pairs) to track cross-region motion dynamics. A multiscale design progressively merges patches by a factor of 2 (16→32) and repeats LSG+TSG at each scale to capture cross-region features at different granularities. The final representation is passed through a global feature module (1D convolutions + 2-layer BiLSTM) and a fully connected layer, trained end-to-end with CTC loss using EdgeConv as the graph convolution operator and Euclidean distance for neighbor selection.

## Results

On Phoenix14T, MultiSignGraph achieves 17.8% WER on the dev set and 19.1% on the test set, matching the SOTA TwoStream model (17.7% dev) without using any extra cues (skeleton, face/mouth keypoints, or pretraining on other SL datasets), and outperforming it by 0.2% on the test set. On Phoenix14, MultiSignGraph achieves 18.2% WER (dev) and 19.1% (test), the best dev performance among all compared methods. On CSL-Daily, MultiSignGraph reaches 27.3% WER (dev) and 26.4% (test), outperforming all non-skeleton-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The headline comparison is against TwoStream [7] (NeurIPS'22), the SOTA model that uses fine-grained skeleton keypoints (hands, body, face) and pretrains on other SL datasets. MultiSignGraph matches TwoStream on Phoenix14T dev (17.8% vs 17.7% WER) and beats it on the test set (19.1% vs 19.3%), all without any skeleton or extra modality cues. Against the next-best non-skeleton method CorrNet [18] (CVPR'23, attention-based, 18.9/20.5 on Phoenix14T dev/test), MultiSignGraph improves by 1.1% and 1.4% WER respectively. Skeleton-based GCN methods CoSign [20] (19.5/20.1) and HST-GNN [21] (19.5/19.8) are outperformed by 1.7–2.3% WER on Phoenix14T. On CSL-Daily, MultiSignGraph (27.3/26.4) still trails TwoStream (25.4/25.3) by ~1.9% but surpasses CoSign (28.1/27.2) and all other baselines. The ablation in Table 3a further shows that simply swapping in existing patch-based backbones (CvT: 39.2%, SwinT: 45.4%, PyVIG: 35.4%) yields far worse results than the proposed SignGraph backbone (17.9%), confirming that the dynamic graph construction is critical rather than the patch representation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CNN-based backbone paradigm in continuous sign language recognition (CSLR), where ResNet, VGG, or S3D extract local features via sliding kernels. Conceptually, it builds on VIG's (Han et al., NeurIPS 2022) idea of representing images as graphs with KNN-based dynamic edges, but repurposes it specifically for sign language by introducing two distinct graph modules: a dense intra-frame graph (LSG) for cross-region correlation and a sparse inter-frame graph (TSG) for temporal tracking. The multiscale design with patch merging borrows the hierarchical downsampling strategy from Swin Transformer. Unlike prior GCN-based sign language models (CoSign, HST-GCN) that rely on pre-extracted skeleton data, SignGraph operates directly on RGB frames as node graphs, eliminating the need for skeleton extraction pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that a graph-based backbone without any extra cues (skeleton, face/mouth features, or cross-dataset pretraining) can match the SOTA TwoStream model on Phoenix14T, which uses both RGB and fine-grained skeleton features plus pretraining on other SL datasets. This suggests that the cross-region correlation structure inherent in sign language is better captured by explicit graph connectivity than by convolutional locality. However, the model still depends on ResNet18 as its patchify stem for initial feature extraction, and the approach has only been validated on three relatively small datasets (≤18K training samples). Open questions include whether the KNN-based dynamic graph construction scales efficiently to longer sequences or higher-resolution inputs, and whether the framework extends naturally to sign language translation or cross-dataset generalization.
