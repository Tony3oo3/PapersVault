## Problem

Standard pre-training on object-centric datasets like ImageNet introduces a domain shift for human-centric tasks, while collecting 3D human annotations (e.g., via motion capture) does not scale to large datasets. Existing self-supervised methods adapted to human data either rely on contrastive learning with limited 3D understanding or require multimodal supervision that is hard to obtain in the wild. The paper asks whether a purely image-based, self-supervised pre-training objective can capture the 3D structure and motion dynamics of the human body well enough to transfer to a wide range of downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the model consumes pairs of RGB images: cross-view pairs (same pose, different camera viewpoints from multi-view datasets) and cross-pose pairs (different time steps from monocular video). 75% of human-region patches in the target image are masked, and the model reconstructs them in pixel space using the visible patches plus the full second image. During fine-tuning, a single RGB crop (monocular) or a pair of RGB crops (binocular) is fed to the ViT-Base encoder, and the output head is swapped for either an MLP (regressing SMPL/MANO parameters or classifying gestures/grasps) or a DPT head (producing per-pixel UV coordinates and 25-way body-part labels for DensePose).

## Method

The core contribution generalizes CroCo's cross-view completion to cross-pose completion, enabling the model to learn from dynamic, non-rigid human motion rather than only static multi-view geometry. A shared-weight ViT-Base encoder processes both images in a pair independently, and a decoder fuses their token representations to reconstruct the masked patches of the target image under an L2 loss. A human-centric masking strategy uses off-the-shelf segmentation to apply the 75% masking ratio only to person patches, padding with random background patches to maintain fixed sequence length. Two separate models are pre-trained: CroCo-Body on multi-view body data (HUMBI, AIST, synthetic SMPL renderings) plus video data (3DPW, PoseTrack, PennAction, JRDB, MARS), and CroCo-Hand on InterHand2.6M. Fine-tuning simply replaces the pre-training linear head with a task-specific prediction head, requiring no architectural modifications.

## Results

On body mesh recovery (3DPW test set), CroCo-Body achieves state-of-the-art PA-MPJPE of 46.2 mm (model-based) and 44.2 mm (model-free), outperforming all prior methods. On DensePose (COCO), it reaches AUC10 of 0.416 and AUC30 of 0.692, surpassing the original DensePose (0.378/0.614). For hand mesh recovery (FreiHand), CroCo-Hand obtains PA-MPJPE of 7.4 mm, competitive with METRO (6.8 mm) without task-specific architectural modifications. In the pre-training comparison (Figure 3a), CroCo-Body/Hand outperforms MAE-IN1k, MAE-Body/Hand, and CroCo on all downstream tasks, e.g., 57.4 vs. 59.6 PA-MPJPE on body mesh recovery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CroCo-Body achieves the best PA-MPJPE among model-based methods (46.2 mm), beating PARE (46.5), ROMP (47.3), and HybrIK (48.8), and the best among model-free methods (44.2 mm), surpassing FastMETRO (44.6), PointHMR (44.9), and MeshGraphormer (45.6). On DensePose, CroCo-Body improves AUC10 by 0.038 and AUC30 by 0.078 over the original DensePose method. For hand mesh recovery, CroCo-Hand (PA-MPJPE 7.4 mm) is competitive with but slightly behind METRO (6.8 mm) and Hand4Whole (7.7 mm), though it uses a simpler iterative regressor head without task-specific design. On grasp classification (GUN-71), the standard CroCo-Hand (37.8% Best View) trails Coskun et al. (42.3%) and Choi et al. (39.8% All), but the extended CroCo-Hand† pre-trained on more diverse hand-object data reaches 49.9%/49.6%, establishing a new state of the art. In the pre-training ablation, CroCo-Body/Hand consistently outperforms MAE-IN1k (59.6→57.4 PA-MPJPE), MAE-Body/Hand (59.1→57.4), and CroCo (57.5→57.4), confirming that the cross-pose completion objective—not merely the data—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CroCo's cross-view completion framework from rigid scenes (buildings, interiors) to the deformable human body by introducing cross-pose completion, where image pairs are drawn from different time steps of a motion sequence rather than different viewpoints of a static scene. The pre-training objective is built on the masked image modeling (MIM) paradigm of MAE, but the key departure is the use of paired images with a shared encoder and joint decoder, plus a human-centric masking strategy that biases masking toward person pixels. The paper positions itself against both general-purpose pre-training (ImageNet MAE, DINO) and prior human-centric self-supervised methods (HCMoCo, LiftedCL, SOLIDER), arguing that those either require costly multimodal annotations or impose structural constraints on the learned representation, whereas here the representation is unconstrained and learned purely from image pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The released CroCo-Body and CroCo-Hand checkpoints provide a practical, generic-architecture starting point for a wide range of human-centric tasks (mesh recovery, dense pose, gesture/grasp classification) without task-specific architectural design, and the data-efficiency results (2–3× fewer fine-tuning samples to match MAE-Body) make this attractive for low-annotation regimes. A notable limitation is that grasp classification does not reach SOTA unless pre-training data is augmented with hand-object interaction datasets, highlighting that background and object diversity remain important. Open questions include whether the cross-pose component is truly necessary beyond providing more training pairs, whether the approach generalizes to other deformable objects (animals, cloth), and whether the benefit persists at larger model scales or with more diverse in-the-wild pre-training data.
