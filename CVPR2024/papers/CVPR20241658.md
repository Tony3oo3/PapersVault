## Problem

Existing human motion generation methods are siloed: each task (text-to-motion, motion understanding, planning, decomposition) requires a separate model, and text-to-motion approaches are limited to short sequences driven by manually crafted detailed prompts. No unified framework exists that can accept high-level scene instructions, autonomously plan and decompose tasks, generate corresponding motions, and synthesize seamless long sequences in a closed loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241658/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts text prompts at three granularities (scene, task, step descriptions) and motion sequences represented as discrete VQ-VAE tokens, with conditions fed through a transformer encoder using full attention. Outputs include 3D motion sequences (up to 2K+ frames via iterative traversal), natural language descriptions at matching granularities, next-task predictions, step decompositions (up to 5 steps per task), and motion-in-between sequences. A key design choice is that motion tokens occupy a separate vocabulary space from text tokens, enforced by a dedicated prediction head, so decoding always samples from the correct modality's token set.

## Method

AvatarGPT builds on a T5-Large encoder-decoder LLM, treating all seven motion-related tasks as instruction-tuned variants of a single model. Continuous motion is quantized via a VQ-VAE (following T2M-GPT), and a lightweight adapter layer maps the quantized embeddings into the LLM's hidden space, avoiding the cost of learning extended vocabulary from scratch while preserving VQ-VAE semantic structure. A separate prediction head for motion tokens (mapping hidden states to the motion vocabulary of size M) is used alongside the original text head (size N), preventing cross-modality token sampling errors. Training uses cross-entropy loss with modality-specific heads, and an unsupervised annotation pipeline (VideoChat for detailed video description + ChatGPT for step/task/scene summarization) generates multi-granularity training data from in-the-wild videos without manual labeling. The closed-loop architecture enables iterative traversal—planning the next task, decomposing it into steps, generating motions, blending via motion-in-between, and re-describing the result—supporting unlimited long-motion synthesis from a single scene description.

## Results

On HumanML3D, AvatarGPT (T5-Large) achieves the best R-Precision across all three thresholds for motion generation (Top-1: 0.510, Top-2: 0.702, Top-3: 0.796), surpassing T2M-GPT (0.491/0.680/0.775) and MotionGPT (0.492/0.681/0.778). For motion understanding, it leads all baselines on every metric, with BertScore of 53.58 vs. 32.40 for MotionGPT and 32.20 for TM2T. On motion-in-between synthesis, it reports FID 1.655 and MM 7.417, competitive but trailing MotionGPT (FID 0.214) and HMD (FID 1.48). The framework also generates 2K+ frame long motions from scene-level descriptions, far exceeding the ~0.2K frames produced by MotionGPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AvatarGPT outperforms all seven baselines on motion understanding, with a substantial margin on BertScore (53.58 vs. 32.40 for MotionGPT [10] and 32.20 for TM2T [7]) and Rouge-L (40.44 vs. 37.40 and 38.10 respectively). On motion generation R-Precision, it leads all competitors at every threshold, edging out T2M-GPT [38] (0.510 vs. 0.491 Top-1) and MotionGPT [10] (0.510 vs. 0.492), while MDM [31] and MotionGPT [41] trail by a wider gap (0.320 and 0.411 Top-1). However, T2M-GPT [38] retains the best generation FID (0.116 vs. 0.168 for AvatarGPT), and MLD [2] is competitive at 0.473. On motion-in-between synthesis, AvatarGPT (FID 1.655) is outperformed by both MotionGPT [10] (0.214) and HMD [28] (1.48), though it reports the only MM score (7.417) among all methods. Overall, the paper's strongest gains are in motion understanding and R-Precision, while FID for generation and motion-in-between remain areas where specialized baselines retain an edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the InstructGPT/Gato paradigm of treating diverse tasks as instructions on a shared LLM to the human motion domain, where prior work (TM2T, MDM, T2M-GPT, MotionGPT) addresses each task—generation, understanding, in-between synthesis—in isolation. For motion tokenization, it follows T2M-GPT's VQ-VAE approach but introduces a lightweight vocabulary adapter that aligns quantized embeddings to the LLM's hidden space, avoiding both the semantic confusion of reusing default vocabulary (MotionGPT [41]) and the inefficiency of learning extended vocabulary from scratch (MotionGPT [10]). The automatic annotation pipeline combining VideoChat and ChatGPT to generate multi-granularity descriptions from in-the-wild videos is a new data construction strategy not present in prior motion generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-loop unification of seven motion tasks enables iterative long-motion synthesis (2K+ frames from a single scene description) that no prior single-task method supports, opening applications in game NPC behavior and virtual avatars. However, the evaluation of high-level tasks relies on a novel but unvalidated metric (Logical Coherence Score via ChatGPT judgment) with no ground truth, and low-level results are mixed—competitive on R-Precision and motion understanding but trailing T2M-GPT on FID (0.168 vs 0.116) and MotionGPT on motion-in-between FID (1.655 vs 0.214). Open questions include whether the framework scales to richer 3D scene interactions, whether the T5 encoder-decoder advantage generalizes to larger models, and whether the cycle-consistency evaluation truly captures generation quality for long sequences.
