## Problem

Existing fair deepfake detection methods, such as DAW-FDD, achieve good fairness under intra-domain evaluation but fail to preserve fairness when tested on unseen forgery domains (cross-domain). The paper identifies two root causes: the entanglement of demographic and forgery features in the representation space, and sharp loss landscapes that trap overparameterized models in suboptimal minima with poor fairness generalization. This is the first work to explicitly address the fairness generalization problem in deepfake detection.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB face images as input, processed in pairs (one fake, one real) during training, along with demographic labels (gender × race intersection groups) and domain labels (forgery method). The encoder produces four disentangled feature streams—content, domain-specific forgery, domain-agnostic forgery, and demographic features—which are fused via AdaIN to form the final classification input. The output is a real/fake binary prediction, with the disentangled features also available for reconstruction and analysis.

## Method

The framework comprises three jointly optimized modules. First, a disentanglement learning module uses a three-branch encoder (content, forgery, demographic) with a shared decoder, trained via a demographic distribution-aware margin loss (replacing standard CE to handle subgroup imbalance), a contrastive loss for forgery features, and a self/cross reconstruction loss. Second, a fair learning module fuses domain-agnostic forgery features with demographic features through AdaIN, then applies a bi-level fairness loss: an outer level promoting inter-subgroup fairness and an inner level (CVaR-inspired) ensuring fairness across real/fake examples within each subgroup. Third, an optimization module employs Sharpness-Aware Minimization to perturb weights along the gradient direction, flattening the loss landscape so the model converges to flat minima with stronger fairness generalization. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with binary search for the fairness loss hyperparameters at each iteration.

## Results

On the Xception backbone, the proposed method achieves the best fairness and detection performance across all four test datasets. In cross-domain evaluation (the key contribution), it reduces F_DP on DFDC from 18.59% (DAW-FDD) to 9.69%, F_FPR on Celeb-DF from 22.31% to 10.62%, and F_FPR on DFD from 34.02% to 26.08%, while simultaneously achieving the highest AUC (61.47% on DFDC, 74.42% on Celeb-DF, 84.82% on DFD). Intra-domain on FF++, it achieves F_FPR=10.63%, F_MEO=8.15%, F_DP=10.41%, and AUC=98.28%, outperforming DAW-FDD on all fairness metrics. The method generalizes across backbones, with similar gains observed on ResNet-50 and EfficientNet-B3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAW-FDD (the primary fairness baseline), the proposed method achieves substantial cross-domain fairness gains: F_DP drops from 18.59% to 9.69% on DFDC (8.90% absolute improvement), F_FPR drops from 22.31% to 10.62% on Celeb-DF (11.69%), and F_FPR drops from 34.02% to 26.08% on DFD (7.94%), while also achieving higher AUC on all three cross-domain datasets. Notably, DAW-FDD actually underperforms the unconstrained Ori baseline on cross-domain fairness (e.g., F_DP of 18.59% vs. 13.87% on DFDC), confirming the paper's central claim that existing fair detectors fail to generalize. Against UCF (the state-of-the-art generalization baseline), the proposed method wins decisively on all four fairness metrics across every cross-domain dataset (e.g., F_DP of 11.65% vs. 20.24% on DFD; F_FPR of 26.08% vs. 42.66% on DFD), while also surpassing UCF's AUC on DFD (84.82% vs. 81.88%). UCF itself fails to maintain fairness, with F_DP of 15.06% on FF++ (worse than Ori's 11.12%) and all four fairness metrics on DFD worse than Ori. The only metric where the proposed method does not lead is F_DP on Celeb-DF (15.04% vs. DAW-FDD's 11.65%), though it still dominates on F_FPR, F_MEO, F_OAE, and AUC for that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads in deepfake detection: disentanglement-based generalization (UCF, Yan et al. ICCV 2023) and fairness-constrained training (DAW-FDD, Ju et al. WACV 2024). It extends UCF's encoder-decoder architecture by adding a demographic feature branch and a distribution-aware margin loss, and extends DAW-FDD's fairness objective with a bi-level (inter- and intra-subgroup) formulation. The optimization module borrows sharpness-aware minimization (Foret et al., ICLR 2020) to flatten the loss landscape, a technique previously applied to adversarial robustness but not to fairness generalization. The key conceptual departure is the demonstration that these three components—disentangled features, bi-level fairness loss, and SAM—must be jointly optimized rather than applied independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical stakes are significant: if fair deepfake detectors cannot generalize to unseen forgery methods, they become obsolete as new generation techniques emerge, leaving demographic groups vulnerable to targeted manipulation. The paper's negative result—that a fair detector (DAW-FDD) can be *less* fair than a non-fair baseline in cross-domain settings—challenges the field's assumption that fairness losses are universally beneficial and motivates rethinking how fairness is integrated into generalization-focused pipelines. A key limitation is the dependence on multi-forgery training data (e.g., FF++ with five manipulation methods), which restricts applicability to single-source or diffusion-generated fakes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-modal deepfake detection and whether the demographic disentanglement branch can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives that avoid the architectural overhead.
