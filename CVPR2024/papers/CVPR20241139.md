## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods face a fundamental trade-off: optimization-based approaches produce expressive but unrealistic synthetic images that overfit to a specific architecture, while CoreSet selection preserves realism but sacrifices diversity. Moreover, bi-level optimization paradigms are computationally prohibitive for large-scale, high-resolution datasets like ImageNet-1K. The paper identifies that no prior method simultaneously achieves realism, diversity, and efficiency, which are all essential for practical deployment.

## Input / Output

The method takes an original dataset (e.g., ImageNet-1K at 224×224, CIFAR-10 at 32×32) with standard class labels and a pre-trained observer model (e.g., ResNet-18 trained on the full dataset) as input. It produces a distilled dataset where each synthetic image is formed by stitching N patches (N=4 for high-resolution, N=1 for low-resolution) from the original images, maintaining the same spatial resolution as the source. Labels are region-level soft labels generated by the pre-trained model on each patch region, rather than a single hard class label, enabling the student model to learn from multi-object compositions within one image.

## Method

RDED is a two-stage, optimization-free paradigm grounded in V-information theory, where the distillation objective decomposes into maximizing diversity H_V(Y|∅) and minimizing realism H_V(Y|X). Stage 1 (key patch extraction) crops each original image into K patches, scores each patch by its realism score (negative cross-entropy between the pre-trained model's prediction and the ground-truth label), selects the top-scoring patch per image, and then retains the top-N×IPC patches per class from a randomly pre-selected subset of 300 images to balance diversity against realism. Stage 2 (information reconstruction) concatenates N selected patches into a single image of the original resolution and relabels each patch region with a soft label from the pre-trained model. The student model is then trained with a region-level cross-entropy loss over all patch regions. Because no gradient-based optimization is performed on the synthetic images, the entire pipeline runs in ~40 ms per image on a single RTX-4090, enabling distillation of full ImageNet-1K to IPC=10 in under 7 minutes.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with IPC=10, RDED achieves 42.0% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-18, nearly doubling the SOTA SRe2L (21.3%) while running 52× faster (39.9 ms vs 2113 ms per image). At IPC=50 on ImageNet-1K, RDED reaches 56.5% (ResNet-18) and 61.2% (ResNet-101), surpassing SRe2L by ~10 points. On high-resolution datasets more broadly (Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet-100), RDED consistently outperforms all baselines by 10–27 points at IPC=10. On low-resolution CIFAR-100, RDED also leads (48.1% at IPC=10, 57.0% at IPC=50 with ConvNet), though on CIFAR-10 it trails trajectory-matching methods (MTT, TESLA, DATM) at IPC=10–50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDED decisively outperforms SRe2L (the closest SOTA baseline) on all high-resolution datasets: on ImageNet-1K IPC=10 it achieves 42.0% vs 21.3% (nearly 2×), and on ImageNet-100 IPC=10 it reaches 35.4% vs 8.2%. Against trajectory-matching methods (MTT, TESLA, DATM), RDED wins on Tiny-ImageNet (39.6% vs 31.1% for DATM at IPC=10) and CIFAR-100 (48.1% vs 47.2% for DATM at IPC=10), but loses on CIFAR-10 where DATM leads (66.8% vs 50.2% at IPC=10). IDM (distribution matching) is competitive on CIFAR-100 (45.1% at IPC=10) but far behind RDED on all high-resolution benchmarks. In cross-architecture generalization (Table 4), RDED distilled with ResNet-18 transfers to EfficientNet-B0 (31.0%) and MobileNet-V2 (40.4%), roughly 2× the corresponding SRe2L numbers (11.7% and 15.4%), confirming that the patch-stitching approach avoids the architecture overfitting that plagues optimization-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDED departs fundamentally from the optimization-based dataset distillation paradigm (MTT, GLaD, SRe2L) by proposing a completely non-optimizing, patch-cropping-and-stitching approach. It reinterprets the distillation objective through V-information theory (Xu et al., 2020), decomposing it into a diversity term and a realism term, and operationalizes these via a realism score (agreement between a pre-trained observer and human labels) and a diversity ratio. For relabeling the stitched multi-patch images, it borrows the soft-labeling framework of Shen & Xing (ECCV 2022). Conceptually, RDED sits between CoreSet selection (which preserves realism but sacrifices diversity) and optimization-based synthesis (which preserves diversity but sacrifices realism and efficiency), attempting to achieve both simultaneously through a two-stage extract-then-reconstruct pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 52× speedup and 2× accuracy improvement over SRe2L on ImageNet-1K (IPC=10) make large-scale dataset distillation practically feasible for the first time on a single GPU in minutes rather than hours, which could accelerate research and deployment pipelines that rely on condensed training data. However, the method's reliance on a pre-trained observer model and its poor performance at IPC=1 (where it degrades to near-random) limit its applicability to very aggressive compression regimes. An open question is whether the patch-stitching strategy generalizes beyond natural images to structured data, video, or non-visual modalities, and whether the V-information framing can guide principled choices of N and |T'_c| beyond the empirical ablations presented.
