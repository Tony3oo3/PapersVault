## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on object detectors assume the victim object's location and label are known, and rely primarily on semantic co-occurrence while neglecting global geometric layout. This paper identifies that practical attack scenarios often involve vague requests (e.g., 'show me a cat' or 'give me two apples') where victim objects must be localized, and that consistency defenses checking both semantic and geometric context remain unfooled by prior methods.

## Input / Output

GLOW takes a victim image, one or more target category labels (with optional amount constraints), and an annotated detection dataset (used to fit per-category GMMs and provide layout-matching candidates). It produces a ranked set of attack plans—each assigning target labels to all detected objects—along with a layout consistency score per plan; the highest-scoring plan is then fed to an existing attacker (TOG) to generate the final perturbed image under a bounded L∞ budget (10 or 30).

## Method

GLOW operates in two stages. First, it localizes victim objects by fitting a 5-component GMM on normalized bounding-box center, height, and width per category from the training set, then selecting the existing detection whose location maximizes the GMM density (top-k for multi-object requests). Second, it formulates global attack plan generation as a layout similarity measurement: it retrieves training images containing the target labels, scores each by IoU on victim objects (s₁) minus a Hungarian-matching cost (L1 + GIoU) on non-victim objects (s₂), and selects the best-matching image whose label mapping becomes the attack plan. The key insight is that natural scene layouts in the training set are inherently context-consistent, so matching to them simultaneously satisfies semantic co-occurrence and geometric placement constraints without explicit modeling of either.

## Results

On the conventional attack request R1 (perturbation budget 30), GLOW achieves F+R scores of 0.61/0.76/0.79 (coco17val) and 0.20/0.28/0.29 (Pascal) for target labels R1-5/R1-50/R1-95 under white-box settings, representing approximately 30% average relative improvement over the best baseline (Cai [7]) in the layout-consistency metric F+R. Under zero-query black-box transfer, GLOW reaches F+R of 0.11/0.22/0.33 (coco) and 0.05/0.12/0.18 (Pascal), again outperforming all baselines. On the more challenging generic requests R2 and R3, GLOW achieves ~35% relative improvement in F+T under R2-5 white-box and wins 13 out of 18 entries in R3 across all metrics and settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLOW consistently outperforms both baselines on the layout-consistency metric F+R across all three target label difficulties and both attack settings. Against TOG [13] (context-agnostic), GLOW achieves roughly 3–6× higher F+R in white-box (e.g., 0.61 vs 0.11 on coco R1-5) and 3–11× higher in black-box (e.g., 0.33 vs 0.03 on coco R1-95), confirming that ignoring scene layout severely limits attack quality. Against Cai [7] (context-aware but semantic-only), GLOW improves F+R by 33% on coco R1-5 (0.61 vs 0.46), 21% on R1-50 (0.76 vs 0.63), and 7% on R1-95 (0.79 vs 0.74) in white-box; in black-box the gap narrows but GLOW still leads (0.33 vs 0.30 on coco R1-95). On the raw fooling rate F, Cai [7] and GLOW are nearly tied (e.g., both 0.87 on coco R1-50), indicating that GLOW's advantage comes specifically from producing more geometrically consistent layouts rather than from higher per-object attack success. On the generic requests R2 and R3, where victim objects must be localized by the algorithm, GLOW's advantage over Cai [7] widens substantially (e.g., F+T of 0.64 vs 0.44 on coco R2-5 white-box), as Cai's random victim selection degrades layout coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLOW builds directly on TOG [13] for the perturbation generation step and extends the context-aware attack planning of Cai et al. [7], which modeled only semantic co-occurrence. The key departure is formulating global attack plan generation as a layout similarity measurement problem (via Hungarian matching with L1+GIoU costs) rather than relying solely on co-occurrence matrices, and introducing GMM-based victim object localization to handle generic requests where the victim is unspecified. This positions GLOW as the first method to jointly enforce both categorical and geometric layout constraints in adversarial attack planning for object detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of generic attack requests (R2: unspecified victim, R3: multi-object with amount constraints) broadens the threat model beyond the conventional single-targeted-object setting, making the evaluation more realistic. The finding that geometric layout consistency (metric R) is substantially harder to satisfy than semantic co-occurrence alone, and that a naive same-label hack (TOG+SAME) fools co-occurrence checks but fails layout checks, highlights a gap in current defenses. Limitations include restricting R3 to same-category objects and relying on a training dataset for layout priors; extending to multi-category requests and dataset-free layout modeling remains open.
