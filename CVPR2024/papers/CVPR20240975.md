## Problem

Diffusion-based text-to-image and text-to-video models produce significantly better outputs when prompts match the linguistic style of their training data, yet typical user prompts are informal and misaligned with that distribution. Manual prompt engineering is costly and requires expertise, and no automated method existed to systematically align arbitrary user prompts with the high-quality text distribution of a specific generative model's training set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240975/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a short, informal text prompt (e.g., a COCO caption like 'Boxes of fruit displayed at an open-air market') and produces a refined, detailed prompt aligned to the target model's training text distribution (e.g., 'A view of stalls selling fruit at the Harare International Market in Harare, Zimbabwe'). The refined prompt is then fed into a frozen diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.4/v2.1 for images, MagicVideo for video) for generation. The CoT pipeline internally produces a 5-step reasoning chain before emitting the final single-line prompt.

## Method

PromptCoT fine-tunes LLaMA-7B on three constructed datasets—text-continuation (completing truncated high-quality prompts), text-revision (mapping BLIP/CLIP-interrogator captions to aligned prompts), and a 52K-sample CoT dataset generated by GPT-3.5-Turbo—using instruction-tuning templates. The key architectural insight is a 5-step Chain-of-Thought mechanism that sequentially extracts key visual elements, appends continuation details, emphasizes crucial concepts, drafts a fluent prompt, and finally aligns it to the target distribution, thereby suppressing the extraneous information that plain instruction-tuning tends to introduce. For multi-model adaptation, the paper replaces full fine-tuning with lightweight LLaMA-Adapter modules: a single shared LLaMA-7B base is paired with task-specific adapters (one per diffusion model/dataset), reducing memory footprint while preserving performance comparable to fully fine-tuned models. The data pipeline filters LAION-aes6plus prompts by CLIP similarity, length, and aesthetic score, then uses BLIP and CLIP-interrogator to synthesize 'human-like' inputs for the revision task.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v2.1, PromptCoT achieves an Aesthetic Score of 5.89 (vs. 5.60 baseline, +5.2%), reduces FID from 58.02 to 40.47 (−30%), and raises CLIP Score from 0.266 to 0.295 (+11%). On SD v1.4, PromptCoT similarly improves Aesthetic Score to 5.79 and FID to 41.10. For text-to-video generation with MagicVideo on MSR-VTT, PromptCoT reduces FID from 36.5 to 33.2 and FVD from 998 to 951 while increasing CLIP Score from 0.284 to 0.296.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptCoT outperforms all internal baselines (t-continue, t2t-blip, t2t-inter) on Aesthetic Score and PickScore across both SD versions, while t2t-blip achieves a marginally lower FID (40.59 vs. 40.47 on SD v2.1) and t-continue sometimes matches or exceeds PromptCoT on IS. Against ChatGPT (davinci-003) on a 1k-image COCO subset, PromptCoT achieves Aesthetic 5.93 vs. 5.69, CLIP 0.293 vs. 0.277, and PickScore 57.5%/73.6% vs. 26.0%/47.5%, a substantial margin. Compared to 30 human annotators specializing in deep learning, PromptCoT surpasses them on all three metrics (Aesthetic 5.93 vs. 5.62, CLIP 0.293 vs. 0.270, PickScore 57.5%/73.6% vs. 48.1%/58.2%). The t2t-inter variant (CLIP-interrogator captions) consistently underperforms, suggesting that captioning method choice significantly affects revision quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Chain-of-Thought mechanism—originally designed for multi-step reasoning in LLMs—into a structured 5-step pipeline for refining text prompts fed to diffusion-based generative models. It builds on the LLaMA-Adapter design for parameter-efficient multi-task adaptation, allowing a single shared LLaMA-7B backbone to serve multiple diffusion models via lightweight, independently fine-tuned adapters. The work sits at the intersection of prompt engineering for text-to-image generation and parameter-efficient fine-tuning, departing from prior prompt-tuning approaches that learn soft prompts or rely on manual heuristics by instead aligning prompt distributions to the training data of the target diffusion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PromptCoT offers a practical, low-cost pathway to improve diffusion model outputs without retraining the generative model itself, democratizing prompt quality for non-expert users. The adapter-based multi-model scheme is a notable efficiency contribution, reducing memory overhead when adapting to new datasets. However, the CoT training data is generated by GPT-3.5-Turbo, introducing a circular dependency on a proprietary model, and evaluations are limited to Stable Diffusion v1.4/v2.1 and MagicVideo on relatively small benchmarks (COCO, MSR-VTT). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), whether the 5-step CoT structure is optimal, and how performance scales with larger or more diverse training distributions.
