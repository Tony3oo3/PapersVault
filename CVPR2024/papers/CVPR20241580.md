## Problem

Hyperspectral image acquisition is prohibitively costly and time-intensive, leaving the field with severely limited training data. Existing synthesis techniques—physical modeling, affine augmentation, and spectral super-resolution—either produce unreliable outputs, fail to enrich diversity, or cannot generate genuinely new samples. The high spectral dimensionality (hundreds of bands) further makes direct application of generative AI to HSI synthesis an open and underexplored challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241580/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small HSI dataset (Chikusei, 128 bands, band-selected to 3 representative channels for unmixing training) and a large unpaired RGB dataset (AID) covering similar scenes. The generative model operates in a low-dimensional abundance space of only k channels (k=5 optimal), not the full 128-band HSI cube. The final output is a synthetic HSI of shape B×W×H reconstructed via the Linear Mixing Model (E_HSI · A_Fusion + ε), where endmembers are fixed from the HSI training set and only the abundance maps are synthesized.

## Method

The core paradigm, 'Unmixing before Fusion,' first decomposes both HSI and RGB data into a shared set of k endmembers and scene-specific abundance maps using an asymmetric unmixing network: an encoder of stacked residual spectral attention modules ending in softmax (enforcing non-negativity and sum-to-one constraints) paired with a single 1×1 convolutional decoder whose weights directly encode the endmembers. Trained on band-selected HSI with a composite loss (MAE + spectral angle distance + endmember total variation), the network is then applied to unpaired RGB to infer their abundances under the shared endmembers. A pluggable generative model (VDVAE, StyleGAN3, or DDPM) is then trained on the fused abundance distributions from both modalities to synthesize novel abundance maps, which are multiplied by the fixed HSI endmembers to produce new HSIs. This reduces the generation problem from 128-dimensional spectral space to a 5-channel abundance space, dramatically lowering the difficulty while preserving physical interpretability.

## Results

Under the proposed UBF paradigm, StyleGAN3 achieves the best synthesis quality with FID 7.69, Recall 0.556, and Precision 0.502, while DDPM follows closely (FID 8.23, Recall 0.509, Precision 0.484) and VDVAE lags significantly (FID 39.13, Recall 0.209). In the downstream scene classification task on HSRS-SC, UBF-synthesized HSIs at 4k scale improve VGG-16 accuracy from 88.84% (Affine Trans.) to 93.97%, and AlexNet from 91.11% to 92.70%. Scaling UBF augmentation to 8k further boosts VGG-16 to 94.60% and AlexNet to 94.29%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three categories of existing HSI synthesis methods. GMM-based physical modeling [50] is shown visually (Fig. 5) to produce results that deviate significantly from real-world scenes due to overly ideal mathematical assumptions. MST++ [4], a spectral super-resolution method, generates HSIs that merely align with the input RGB and cannot produce genuinely new samples, limiting its utility for data augmentation. In the only quantitative baseline comparison (Table 3), UBF-synthesized HSIs at 4k scale outperform affine transformation augmentation [16] across all three classifiers: VGG-16 improves by 5.13 points (93.97% vs. 88.84%), AlexNet by 1.59 points (92.70% vs. 91.11%), and ResNet-18 by 2.58 points (44.33% vs. 41.75%). The advantage grows with scale, reaching 5.76 points on VGG-16 at 8k, confirming that UBF generates more diverse and reliable synthetic HSIs than geometric augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Linear Mixing Model (LMM) from hyperspectral unmixing and repurposes it as a dimensional-reduction bridge between HSI and RGB modalities, then plugs standard generative models (StyleGAN3, DDPM, VDVAE) into the resulting low-dimensional abundance space. It departs from prior HSI synthesis approaches—physical modeling, affine augmentation, and spectral super-resolution—by framing synthesis as a generative task in a physically interpretable space rather than in the raw spectral cube. The paradigm also distinguishes itself from existing unmixing-based fusion methods [44, 46], which operate on paired HSI-MSI data, by explicitly handling unpaired HSI and RGB under a shared-endmember assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UBF paradigm offers a modular, model-agnostic recipe for generating large volumes of diverse synthetic HSIs, directly addressing the data-scarcity bottleneck that limits HSI research. However, the validation is confined to a single HSI dataset (Chikusei) and one RGB dataset (AID), and the downstream-task experiments use a small five-class benchmark (HSRS-SC), leaving open whether the shared-endmember assumption generalizes to highly heterogeneous or urban scenes. Future work could explore whether the paradigm extends to other multi-source combinations (PAN, MSI) and whether more advanced diffusion architectures (e.g., latent diffusion, score-based models) further close the quality gap observed between GAN and diffusion baselines.
