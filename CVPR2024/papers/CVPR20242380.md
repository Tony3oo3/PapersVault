## Problem

Existing cross-modal ReID methods are confined to fixed modality pairs (e.g., RGB-IR or RGB-Sketch) and cannot handle the uncertain, variable input modalities encountered in real-world deployment. Additionally, no foundation model for ReID exists that delivers strong zero-shot retrieval across multiple modalities without task-specific fine-tuning, leaving a gap between the impressive zero-shot capabilities of large-scale pre-trained models and their underwhelming performance on ReID benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242380/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts any arbitrary combination of RGB, IR, and sketch images (all resized to 384×192 with channel replication to unify 3-channel inputs) and free-form text descriptions, tokenized into a single variable-length sequence. When IR or sketch data is unavailable, synthetic versions are generated via Channel Augmentation and Lineart respectively, and the model is trained progressively—first on synthetic data, then fine-tuned on real paired images. The output is a unified identity embedding per person used for cross-modal or multimodal retrieval ranking (Rank-k, mAP, mINP).

## Method

AIO projects all modalities into a shared embedding space via a lightweight multimodal tokenizer (IBN-style convolutional tokenizer for images, CLIP subword tokenizer for text), then feeds the concatenated token sequence into a frozen ViT backbone pre-trained on LAION-2B, which acts as a modality-shared feature extractor. Training is guided by three cross-modal heads: a conventional classification head for identity-invariant features, a Vision Guided Masked Attribute Modeling head that predicts masked attribute words from RGB features to learn fine-grained RGB-text alignment, and a novel Multimodal Feature Binding loss that attracts all modality features of the same person toward the RGB feature while repelling RGB features of different persons. The overall loss is L_CE + α·L_VA + L_FB, and the transformer's variable-length input capability allows the model to process any subset of modalities without architectural changes.

## Results

In zero-shot cross-modal ReID, AIO achieves Rank-1 of 79.6% (mAP 59.9%) on R→R (Market1501), 57.6% (mAP 51.9%) on I→R (SYSU-MM01), 70.2% (mAP 73.5%) on S→R (PKU-Sketch), and 53.4% (mAP 43.4%) on T→R (CUHK-PEDES). On the multimodal S+T→R task (Tri-CUHK-PEDES), AIO reaches 92.1% Rank-1, 92.2% mAP, and 89.2% mINP, and further improves to 93.8% Rank-1 when all four modalities (R+I+S+T) are provided. These results are obtained without any fine-tuning on the target test datasets, using only a frozen CLIP-pretrained ViT backbone trained on SYNTH-PEDES, LLCM, and MaSk1K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AIO substantially outperforms all dedicated cross-modal baselines: on I→R it achieves 57.6% Rank-1 versus AGW's 18.2%; on T→R it reaches 53.4% versus IRRA's 30.1% and UNIReID's 11.6%; on S→R it matches UNIReID at 70.2% vs 69.8% while adding IR and text capabilities. On R→R, AIO (79.6%) is competitive with the strongest unimodal baselines M3L (78.3%) and OSNet-IBN (73.0%), but trails PLIP (80.4%) by 0.8 points and PLIP also leads on T→R (57.7% vs 53.4%). Among pre-trained ReID models, AIO dramatically surpasses LuPerson-NL (24.6%) and APTM (5.3%) on R→R, confirming that a general-purpose foundation model (CLIP/LAION-2B) is far more effective for zero-shot ReID than task-specific pre-training. On the multimodal S+T→R task (Table 8), AIO edges out UNIReID (92.1% vs 91.4% Rank-1) and gains an additional +1.5 points when RGB is added (93.6%), demonstrating the benefit of the unified four-modality design.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIO builds on the cross-modal ReID literature (RGB-IR, RGB-Sketch, RGB-Text) and the emerging use of large-scale foundation models (CLIP, CoCa) for zero-shot vision tasks, but departs from prior work in two key ways. Unlike UNIReID and TriReID, which handle at most three modalities with modality-specific architectures, AIO unifies all four modalities (RGB, IR, Sketch, Text) through a single learnable multimodal tokenizer feeding a frozen ViT encoder pre-trained on LAION-2B. The cross-modal heads (classification, masked attribute modeling, feature binding) are inspired by standard ReID losses and contrastive alignment (ImageBind-style), but the feature binding loss is specifically designed to anchor all modalities to RGB rather than treating all pairs symmetrically, reflecting the practical dominance of RGB data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AIO addresses a genuine practical gap: real-world ReID deployments face uncertain query modalities, and no prior framework handles all four common modalities in a single zero-shot model. The limitation that IR images in multimodal experiments are synthetic (via Channel Augmentation) rather than real-world IR constrains the strength of the multimodal claims, and the modest margins over UNIReID on individual cross-modal tasks suggest the foundation-model backbone contributes more to robustness and extensibility than to peak accuracy. Open questions include whether the frozen-encoder paradigm can be extended to additional modalities (e.g., depth, thermal) without retraining, and whether the progressive synthetic-to-real training strategy generalizes beyond the specific CA and Lineart augmentations used here.
