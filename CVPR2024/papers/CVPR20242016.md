## Problem

Motion refinement methods for predicted pose sequences must balance two conflicting objectives—precision (MPJPE) and smoothness (acceleration error)—but prior work treats this tension merely as a trade-off to be managed. The authors observe that the discrepancy between outputs biased toward each objective actually correlates with (r ≈ 0.48) the true quality of the predicted sequence, providing a free quality cue that no previous method exploits. Additionally, multi-view pose information has not been leveraged to simultaneously refine poses from multiple cameras of the same person.

## Input / Output

The method consumes predicted pose sequences of shape R^{V×T×(J×D)} from any upstream pose estimator (2D, 3D, or SMPL), where V is the number of views, T=8 is the window length (reduced from 32 in prior work), J is the joint count, and D is spatial dimension. It outputs a refined pose sequence of identical shape. In single-view mode (V=1), the Pose Similarity Encoding module is simply disabled; in multi-view mode, all views of one person are processed jointly. A sliding-window average post-processing step is applied to the final output.

## Method

SynSP is a two-stage transformer. The Base Stage uses a Global Correlation Encoder (merging view and frame dimensions) and a Local Correlation Encoder (temporal-only) to produce keys/values and a query, which a 5-layer Base Decoder splits into a Position Branch (λ=0, precision-biased) and an Acceleration Branch (λ=1, smoothness-biased). The Refinement Stage computes Pose Quality Encoding (PQE) from the per-joint distance between the two detached branch outputs—normalized and exponentiated to yield a per-frame quality score—and Pose Similarity Encoding (PSE) from pairwise multi-view pose similarity (exponential of negative squared relative-pose distance). These two encodings are added to the reused GCE embeddings and fed into a 4-layer Refinement Decoder that produces the final refined sequence. Training is supervised with L_pos + λL_acc (λ=0.5 for the final output), and the short 8-frame window reduces latency to ~678 ms total versus ~3470 ms for SmoothNet.

## Results

On Human3.6M with 3D pose estimator FCN, SynSP achieves MPJPE of 51.36 and Accel of 1.02 with a window size of only 8 frames, outperforming SmoothNet (52.72 MPJPE, 1.03 Accel, window size 32) by 20.8% in MPJPE. In multi-view mode (SynSP*), MPJPE drops further to 41.78 and PA-MPJPE to 33.32. On AIST++ with SMPL representation, SynSP reduces MPJPE from 103.00 (SmoothNet) to 84.63, a 17.9% improvement. The method also achieves 16.9% MPJPE improvement over SmoothNet on Human3.6M 2D (8.13 vs 9.25) while using only 25% of the input sequence length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynSP consistently outperforms all low-pass filter baselines (One-Euro, Gaussian1d, Savitzky-Golay) across all three pose representations, with the largest gap on 3D Human3.6M where it achieves 51.36 MPJPE vs. 53.48–55.20 for filters. Against the primary deep learning baseline SmoothNet, SynSP improves MPJPE by 16.9% on Human3.6M 2D (8.13 vs 9.25), 20.8% on Human3.6M 3D (51.36 vs 52.72), and 17.9% on AIST++ SMPL (84.63 vs 103.00), while using only 8-frame windows versus SmoothNet's 32. In multi-view mode, SynSP* further reduces 3D MPJPE to 41.78, a 20.7% improvement over its single-view counterpart. For video-based estimators (Table 2), VIBE+SynSP achieves 77.00 MPJPE on AIST++ versus 97.47 for VIBE+SmoothNet, and PPT*+SynSP reaches 21.51 MPJPE on Human3.6M versus 23.97 for PPT*+SmoothNet. Against the pose prior method GFPose on the denoise task, SynSP achieves substantially lower MPJPE (e.g., 37.6 vs 42.8 on N(0,100) Human3.6M) while also reporting Accel errors that GFPose cannot measure, and does so with a single forward pass rather than 1000 sampling steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynSP extends the motion refinement line of work initiated by SmoothNet [42] and traditional low-pass filters (One-Euro, Gaussian1d, Savitzky-Golay), which all aim to balance precision and smoothness in predicted pose sequences. The key conceptual departure is reframing the inherent tension between these two objectives as a usable quality signal: rather than merely trading off smoothness against precision, SynSP exploits the discrepancy between a precision-biased and a smoothness-biased output branch as a per-frame quality cue (Pose Quality Encoding) to guide attention. Additionally, the Pose Similarity Encoding module introduces multi-view correlation as a refinement signal, a dimension largely unexplored by prior single-view refinement methods like SmoothNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain is notable: SynSP achieves SOTA MPJPE with a window length of 8 versus 32 for prior methods, reducing latency to roughly 17% of SmoothNet's total pipeline time, making near-real-time pose refinement more feasible. However, the quality-cue mechanism relies on a moderate Pearson correlation (0.48) between branch disagreement and true pose error, leaving open whether this signal remains informative under more severe degradation or different estimator families. The multi-view extension, while effective, is limited to the Human3.6M setup and its generalizability to arbitrary camera configurations or real-time multi-camera systems remains untested. Broader open questions include whether the quality-cue paradigm transfers to other sequence-refinement tasks beyond human pose.
