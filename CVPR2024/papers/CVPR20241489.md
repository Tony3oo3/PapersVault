## Problem

In continual test-time domain adaptation, a source pre-trained model must adapt online to a stream of shifting target domains without source data or labels. The core challenge is a fundamental trade-off: exploiting noisy pseudo-labels to improve discrimination in the current domain risks catastrophic forgetting and loss of generalization to future domains. Existing methods address either discrimination or generalization but lack a unified framework that simultaneously suppresses label noise and preserves long-term adaptability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB target-domain images (224×224 for ViT-base, original 32×32 or 224×224 for ResNet) in a strictly online, single-pass fashion where each image is seen exactly once. A frozen source pre-trained model (ResNet or ViT-base) is retained alongside the adapting model to provide feature-level prior knowledge. The output is a per-sample class probability vector; only batch-normalization parameters are updated during adaptation, keeping all other weights fixed.

## Method

The framework generates high-quality supervision signals through a three-stage pipeline: (1) self-adaptive per-class thresholds (combining a global mean-confidence threshold with a local class-frequency ratio) partition pseudo-labels into reliable and unreliable subsets; (2) unreliable labels are calibrated by retrieving K nearest neighbors in the source model's feature space and computing similarity-weighted label averages; (3) a diversity score derived from the cosine distance between each prediction and an exponential moving average of recent outputs, multiplied by a negative-entropy certainty weight, reweights the cross-entropy loss to favor diverse, confident samples. To prevent catastrophic forgetting, a soft-weighted parameter alignment loss constrains adapted BN parameters toward their source values, with depth-dependent weights (β^l = (1−e^{−10l})/(1+e^{−10l})) that impose stronger alignment on deeper, noise-sensitive layers. The total objective is the weighted cross-entropy plus λ₁ times the alignment loss, optimized only over BN parameters in a dual-stream architecture.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-to-CIFAR-10C (severity 5, online), the proposed method achieves a mean classification error of 14.5% with ResNet and 8.9% with ViT-base, outperforming the strongest baseline ROID (16.2% / 11.4%) and CoTTA (16.5% / 24.6%). On CIFAR-100-to-CIFAR-100C, the method reduces mean error to 27.9% (ResNet) and 24.0% (ViT-base), surpassing ROID (29.3% / 25.6%) and ViDA (—/ 27.3%). On ImageNet-to-ImageNet-C over 10 corruption sequences, the method achieves 51.4% (ResNet) and 42.7% (ViT-base) mean error, beating ROID (54.5% / 45.0%) and ViDA (61.2% / 43.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines across all three benchmark datasets. Against the closest competitor ROID, the method reduces mean error by 1.7 points on CIFAR-10C (ResNet: 14.5% vs. 16.2%), 1.4 points on CIFAR-100C (ResNet: 27.9% vs. 29.3%), and 3.1 points on ImageNet-C (ResNet: 51.4% vs. 54.5%). CoTTA, the foundational CTTA method, is surpassed by 2.0 points on CIFAR-10C (ResNet) and 15.7 points on CIFAR-10C (ViT-base: 8.9% vs. 24.6%), indicating the proposed supervision framework is especially effective for Transformer backbones. ViDA, which injects domain adapters, is beaten by 11.8 points on CIFAR-10C (ViT-base: 8.9% vs. 20.7%) and 0.7 points on ImageNet-C (ViT-base: 42.7% vs. 43.4%). Simpler baselines such as BN Stats Adapt, Pseudo-Label, and TENT-continual are all surpassed by 5–6 points on CIFAR-10C (ResNet), while NOTE and RoTTA, which focus on robust normalization, are outperformed by 4.4 and 4.8 points respectively. RMT, which uses contrastive learning to anchor features to the source space, is beaten by 2.5 points on CIFAR-10C (ResNet) and 2.3 points on CIFAR-100C (ResNet), confirming that the soft-weighted parameter alignment provides a more effective forgetting mitigation than contrastive regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CTTA setting established by CoTTA (Wang et al., CVPR 2022) and the diversity-weighting mechanism from ROID (Marsden et al., 2023), but departs from both in key ways. Rather than CoTTA's stochastic overwriting of adapted parameters with source weights, the authors introduce a loss-guided soft-weighted parameter alignment that modulates similarity strength by layer depth. The supervision signal generation pipeline—combining self-adaptive per-class thresholds, source-model knowledge calibration of unreliable pseudo-labels, and EMA-based diversity reweighting—unifies ideas from pseudo-label filtering, knowledge distillation, and the diversity criterion of ROID into a single framework, whereas prior methods typically address reliability or generalization in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, parameter-efficient recipe (BN-only fine-tuning plus a structured pseudo-label pipeline) for streaming domain adaptation without source data, which is relevant to deployment scenarios where models face evolving corruption patterns. However, the authors themselves note that gains diminish on CIFAR-100C and ImageNet-C compared to CIFAR-10C, revealing a fundamental limitation of restricting adaptation to normalization layers as task complexity grows. An open question is whether the supervision-signal quality framework (thresholding, source calibration, diversity weighting) can be transferred to full-parameter or adapter-based adaptation to close this gap, and whether the soft-weighted alignment strategy generalizes beyond the specific sigmoid depth-schedule used here.
