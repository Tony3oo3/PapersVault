## Problem

State-of-the-art video-text retrieval methods fully fine-tune CLIP per task, incurring prohibitive storage costs when multiple VTR tasks must be deployed simultaneously (e.g., in mobile apps or cloud services). Existing parameter-efficient adapters (AdaptFormer, Convpass, ST-Adapter) either ignore temporal modeling and cross-modal interactions or introduce excessive parameter overhead, and when naively adapted to VTR they fall far behind full fine-tuning. This paper formalizes the task of Parameter-Efficient VTR (PE-VTR) and shows that a purpose-designed adapter can match or exceed full fine-tuning with only ~2.4% additional parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip sampled at 1 fps (up to 12 frames for short datasets like MSR-VTT/MSVD/LSMDC, up to 32 for DiDemo/ActivityNet) and a text caption (up to 32 or 64 tokens). Each frame is patchified into 16×16 patches with a prepended [CLS] token and encoded by CLIP's frozen ViT-B/16 vision encoder; the text is encoded by the frozen text encoder. The adapter branches are inserted after the FFN in every transformer block of both encoders, and the final output is a cosine similarity between the mean-pooled video [CLS] features and the text [CLS] feature, used to rank candidates in a repository.

## Method

MV-Adapter inserts a bottleneck branch (Downsample → lightweight Transformer → Upsample) into both the video and text encoders of CLIP, with the CLIP backbone kept frozen. Two novel components differentiate it from prior adapters: (1) a Temporal Adaptation Module in the video branch that concatenates all frames' [CLS] tokens, mean-pooled patch tokens, and a learnable [CC] token into a sequence processed by a lightweight transformer to capture global/local temporal context, followed by a calibration MLP that generates per-frame dynamic upsample weights from the fused [CC], [CLS], and patch features; (2) a Cross Modality Tying module that shares a low-dimensional factor between the two branches and projects it through modality-specific matrices to calibrate the Downsample weights, implicitly encouraging aligned representations without requiring joint video-text input. The entire adapter adds only ~2.4% parameters (middle dimension 64 for ViT-B/16), is trained with contrastive loss via Adam at lr 5e-6, and reduces GPU memory by ~40% versus full fine-tuning.

## Results

MV-Adapter uses only 2.39–2.42% tunable parameters (vs. 100% for full fine-tuning) while achieving comparable or superior retrieval performance across five benchmarks. On MSR-VTT, it reaches T2V R@Sum 202.1 and V2T R@Sum 205.9, surpassing full fine-tuning (200.2/202.4). The largest gains appear on LSMDC, where MV-Adapter achieves T2V R@Sum 120.3 vs. 112.9 for full fine-tuning (+7.4), and on ActivityNet T2V R@Sum 203.1 vs. 201.5. Across all five datasets, MV-Adapter outperforms the second-best competing method by 0.3–10.5 points on T2V R@Sum and 1.2–7.0 points on V2T R@Sum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV-Adapter consistently outperforms all six competing methods across all five benchmarks. Against the closest adapter baselines, AdaptFormer (par/seq) achieves T2V R@Sum 199.3/199.7 on MSR-VTT vs. MV-Adapter's 202.1, a gap of 2.8–3.0 points; ST-Adapter (5.93% params) reaches 196.0/201.0, trailing by 6.1/4.9. Convpass and CM-Adapter lag further behind at 192.6/199.4 and 191.3/192.6 respectively. The full-fine-tuning VTR methods adapted to PE-VTR (CLIP4Clip at 8.45% and Hunyuan_TVR at 11.97% params) show the largest deficits, with CLIP4Clip scoring only 189.2/187.4 on MSR-VTT T2V/V2T R@Sum. On LSMDC, the advantage is most pronounced: MV-Adapter leads the second-best (ST-Adapter) by 10.5/7.0 on T2V/V2T R@Sum, and on MSVD it surpasses full fine-tuning on V2T (261.2 vs. 259.3) while using only 2.4% of the parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

MV-Adapter builds directly on the bottleneck adapter architecture from AdaptFormer (Downsample-Transformer-Upsample) but extends it from unimodal image classification to the multimodal video-text retrieval setting. It departs from prior VTR methods (CLIP4Clip, Hunyuan_TVR) that require full fine-tuning of CLIP, and from existing video adapters (ST-Adapter, Convpass) that handle only a single modality or lack explicit temporal modeling. The two novel components—Temporal Adaptation (dynamic per-frame upsample weights via a lightweight transformer and calibration MLP) and Cross Modality Tying (shared cross-modality factors to implicitly align video and text embeddings)—address the specific challenges of transferring image-text knowledge to video-text while maintaining parameter efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: MV-Adapter reduces deployment storage from #tasks × full model size to 1 + #tasks × 2.4%, and cuts training GPU memory by ~40%, making multi-task VTR deployment feasible in resource-constrained settings (mobile apps, cloud services). The finding that naively freezing CLIP in existing VTR architectures (CLIP4Clip, Hunyuan) causes severe performance degradation highlights that temporal and cross-modal adaptation cannot be trivially bolted onto frozen backbones. Open questions include whether the fixed-frame sampling limits performance on long videos, how the method handles audio-dependent captions, and whether the approach generalizes to other multimodal tasks (video QA, captioning) as the authors suggest.
