## Problem

Existing 3D scene understanding methods focus on object-level semantic and instance segmentation, but real-world interaction requires detecting and manipulating small functional elements (handles, knobs, buttons) and understanding what action they afford and what purpose they serve in context. No prior dataset provides geometrically fine-grained annotations of these interactive elements in real-world 3D scenes, and no benchmark exists to evaluate affordance understanding or interaction motion prediction.

## Input / Output

The input is a high-resolution 3D point cloud (5 mm voxel downsampled from Faro Focus S70 laser scans, registered to iPad RGB camera poses) with per-point RGB color and optional estimated normals; for the grounding task, a free-form natural language task description is additionally provided. Outputs are per-instance masks of small functional elements (substantially smaller than object-level masks), one of nine Gibsonian affordance labels (e.g., rotate, tip-push, hook-pull), and motion parameters (type ∈ {rotation, translation}, 3D axis direction, 3D origin). The grounding task additionally returns the single functional element mask most relevant to the text query, retrieved via CLIP embedding similarity above a threshold.

## Method

The paper introduces SceneFun3D, a dataset of 710 laser-scanned indoor scenes with 14,867 fine-grained functional element annotations, 14,279 motion annotations, and 17,133 natural language task descriptions, annotated via a custom web-based tool using BVH-accelerated ray casting on raw point clouds. For functionality segmentation, they adapt Mask3D and SoftGroup by replacing distribution-based BCE/cross-entropy losses with region-based Dice losses to handle the extreme foreground-background imbalance of small masks, and introduce a coarse-to-fine curriculum that progressively shrinks the mask expansion radius during training—without this curriculum the model fails entirely to detect interactive elements. For task-driven affordance grounding, they extend OpenMask3D to consume functional-element-level mask proposals (from Mask3D-F) instead of object-level proposals, enabling CLIP-based retrieval at the granularity of individual buttons and handles. For motion estimation, they add per-instance prediction heads to Mask3D-F that jointly predict motion type (cross-entropy), axis direction (smooth L1), and origin (smooth L1), showing that point normals improve axis and origin accuracy.

## Results

On the functionality segmentation task, the adapted Mask3D-F achieves AP=7.9, AP50=18.3, and AP25=26.6, substantially outperforming SoftGroup-F (AP=3.6, AP50=8.4, AP25=17.2) and the zero-shot LERF baseline (AP=4.8, AP50=12.3, AP25=18.1). For task-driven affordance grounding, OpenMask3D-F reaches AP50=8.0 and AP25=17.5, compared to LERF's AP50=4.9 and AP25=11.3, while the original object-level OpenMask3D scores 0.0 on both metrics. On motion estimation, Mask3D-FM with RGB+normals achieves AP25(+MAO)=8.1, indicating that jointly predicting motion parameters alongside segmentation remains highly challenging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mask3D-F (the paper's adapted Mask3D with Dice loss and curriculum learning) clearly outperforms both SoftGroup-F and the zero-shot LERF on functionality segmentation, achieving roughly 2× the AP of SoftGroup-F (7.9 vs. 3.6) and 1.6× that of LERF (7.9 vs. 4.8). On task-driven affordance grounding, OpenMask3D-F (using functional-element-level mask proposals) beats LERF by a significant margin (AP50: 8.0 vs. 4.9), while the original OpenMask3D fails entirely (AP50=0.0) because its object-level mask proposals cannot isolate small functional elements like buttons and knobs. LERF, evaluated zero-shot, provides a reasonable open-vocabulary baseline but consistently trails the supervised, fine-tuned methods by 3–6 points on AP50 across both segmentation tasks. The ablation on curriculum learning shows that without coarse-to-fine mask expansion (r₀=0), Mask3D-F drops to 0.0 AP50, confirming that the small functional-element masks are the primary difficulty relative to standard object instance segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard 3D instance and semantic segmentation paradigm by targeting fine-grained functional interactive elements (handles, knobs, buttons) rather than whole objects, and by introducing Gibsonian and telic affordance labels as first-class prediction targets. It builds on Mask3D, SoftGroup, and OpenMask3D as methodological backbones but re-purposes them for a fundamentally different granularity and semantic level. The use of high-resolution Faro laser scans from ARKitScenes (rather than commodity RGB-D reconstructions) is a key data-level departure, enabling geometric detail that prior datasets like ScanNet or MultiScan cannot capture. The paper also introduces the first explicit linkage between Gibsonian affordance (how to interact) and telic affordance (purpose in scene context) within a single 3D benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SceneFun3D fills a critical gap: no prior dataset provides geometrically fine-grained annotations of functional interactive elements in real-world 3D scenes, which is essential for embodied AI, robotics, and realistic human-scene interaction modeling. The extremely low absolute performance (best AP of 7.9) signals that this is a genuinely open problem, and the curriculum-learning ablation (complete failure without coarse-to-fine mask expansion) highlights a non-trivial training challenge for small-instance detection. Open questions include whether open-vocabulary foundation models can close the gap, how the approach scales beyond 710 indoor scenes, and whether the interaction-centric motion formulation generalizes to outdoor or industrial settings.
