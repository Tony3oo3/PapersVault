## Problem

Existing fine-grained VLM benchmarks (e.g., VALSE, ARO, Winoground) construct confusing candidates primarily on the textual side, leaving the visual dimension largely unexplored. This paper demonstrates that even state-of-the-art VLMs (CLIP, BLIP, FLAVA, CoCa) perform at or near chance level on fine-grained visual-linguistic concepts such as object size, position, existence, and count, revealing that standard contrastive pretraining allows models to take noun-based shortcuts and neglect compositional reasoning.

## Input / Output

The evaluation uses a symmetric image-text matching task where each test case contains a candidate set of 2–9 images differing only in one specified visual attribute (e.g., count, position) paired with a corresponding text candidate set; the model must select the correct match from the full candidate set using cosine similarity. For training, the method consumes standard LAION-400M image-text pairs alongside synthetic hard-negative pairs generated by the progressive pipeline, with batch sizes of 2048 and 768 respectively. The output is a scalar similarity score per image-text pair, from which the top-ranked candidate is selected as the prediction.

## Method

The paper introduces a four-stage progressive data construction pipeline: (1) generate single-object images with Stable Diffusion XL, (2) segment objects using Grounding-DINO + SAM to build a background-free instance library, (3) arrange instances on a blank canvas with controlled attributes (size, position, existence, count), and (4) inpaint backgrounds using a progressive filling strategy that expands from a shared initial background to ensure consistency across all candidates in a set. For optimization, the authors propose a hard-negative-aware contrastive loss that places both confusing images and confusing texts from the same candidate set into the training batch, forcing the model to discriminate subtle visual and textual differences rather than relying on noun shortcuts. This loss is combined with the standard CLIP contrastive loss on LAION-400M (weighted by λ=0.2) to preserve zero-shot capability, and the entire approach is applied to the ViT-B/32 CLIP variant with only 1,000 fine-tuning steps at a 1e-6 learning rate.

## Results

On the SPEC benchmark, the proposed hard-negative-aware fine-tuning of CLIP achieves 53.3% I2TACC and 49.4% T2IACC (averaged across all six subsets), compared to CLIP's 33.5% and 30.5%, while maintaining zero-shot accuracy at 68.7% (vs. 67.5% for CLIP). The largest gains appear on Count (I2T: 55.6% vs. 25.1%) and Absolute Position (I2T: 30.6% vs. 12.6%). Cross-dataset evaluation on ARO shows improvements of 3.2%, 9.8%, and 7.4% on Attribute, Relation, and Order subsets respectively, and a 2% average gain on Eqben.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines (CLIP, BLIP, FLAVA, CoCa) across every SPEC subset in both I2T and T2I directions. The most dramatic gains are on Count, where Ours reaches 55.6% I2TACC versus CLIP's 25.1% and BLIP's 37.4%, and on Absolute Position, where Ours achieves 30.6% I2TACC versus 12.6% (CLIP) and 12.2% (BLIP). BLIP is the strongest baseline on Absolute Size (43.3% I2T) and Count (37.4% I2T), yet Ours still surpasses it by 25.6 and 18.2 points respectively. On the cross-dataset ARO benchmark, Ours improves over CLIP by 3.2% (Attribute: 66.4 vs. 63.2), 9.8% (Relation: 73.7 vs. 63.9), and 7.4% (Order: 60.7 vs. 53.3), confirming transferable fine-grained understanding rather than overfitting to SPEC. Zero-shot accuracy on ELEVATER is preserved at 68.7% versus CLIP's 67.5%, showing no degradation from the fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP contrastive learning framework by introducing hard negatives in both modalities (visual and textual) within the same training batch, departing from prior fine-grained enhancement methods (TSVLC, ViLEM, Construct-VL) that only used text-side hard negatives. For benchmarking, it builds on the line of fine-grained VLM evaluation (Winoground, VALSE, ARO, Eqben) but departs by constructing visual hard negatives through a progressive synthesis pipeline (Stable Diffusion XL → SAM/Grounding-DINO segmentation → canvas arrangement → inpainting), enabling symmetric image-to-text and text-to-image matching. The data construction approach is conceptually related to Syn-CLIP's 3D-simulation-based synthesis and Eqben's virtual-engine rendering, but achieves photo-realistic, scalable, and attribute-controlled image sets without relying on 3D engines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SPEC benchmark provides a practical, scalable tool for diagnosing fine-grained VLM limitations that prior benchmarks (limited by manual curation costs or virtual-engine realism) could not offer, and the finding that four SOTA VLMs perform at chance level on size, position, existence, and count tasks underscores a fundamental gap between VLMs' strong noun recognition and their weak compositional understanding. The simple hard-negative remedy is immediately adoptable, but its generalizability remains open: it was validated only on CLIP ViT-B/32 with 1,000 fine-tuning steps, and whether the approach transfers to larger or architecturally different VLMs (e.g., LLaVA-style autoregressive models) is untested. A key limitation is that SPEC images are synthetic, so real-world robustness of the learned fine-grained representations is uncertain.
