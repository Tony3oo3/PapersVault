## Problem

Existing generative trackers like ARTrack model only trajectory evolution via inter-frame autoregression but lack a mechanism to 'retell' the target's changing appearance over time, and their intra-frame autoregressive decoder (sequentially generating four bounding-box tokens) imposes a significant efficiency penalty. ARTrackV2 addresses both gaps by unifying trajectory and appearance evolution in a single autoregressive framework while replacing the decoder with a parallel pure-encoder architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241348/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes a static template image (128×128 or 192×192), a per-frame search region (256×256 or 384×384), and autoregressive prompts propagated from the previous frame: discrete trajectory tokens (from a shared coordinate vocabulary), a set of appearance tokens acting as dynamic templates, and a scalar confidence token. It produces bounding-box coordinates via argmax over the trajectory-token likelihood, a reconstructed feature map of the target within the search region (used to update appearance tokens), and a predicted IoU score that gates appearance evolution. All tokens within a single frame are processed in parallel, so inference latency is dominated by one forward pass per frame rather than four sequential decoder steps.

## Method

ARTrackV2 replaces ARTrack's encoder-decoder with a pure Vision Transformer encoder that processes visual patches, trajectory tokens, and command tokens in parallel, eliminating intra-frame autoregression while preserving inter-frame (time-series) autoregression. A set of learnable appearance tokens, initialized from the first-frame template, interact with the search region through the encoder and are then fed to a lightweight reconstruction decoder that rebuilds the target's feature map; the reconstructed features update the appearance tokens for the next frame. An oriented attention mask restricts appearance tokens to attend only to search-region tokens and the confidence token (not trajectory tokens), preventing the model from shortcutting appearance evolution by simply cropping at the predicted location. A confidence token, trained with L1 loss against ground-truth IoU, implicitly instructs appearance tokens whether to evolve or hold their state during occlusion. Training is end-to-end and single-stage at the sequence level (32-frame clips), combining cross-entropy, SIoU, MSE reconstruction, and IoU L1 losses, with a simple 'reverse augmentation' (playing clips backward) to expand the training set without disrupting temporal continuity.

## Results

ARTrackV2-L384 achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four benchmarks: 79.5% AO on GOT-10k (one-shot), 86.1% AUC on TrackingNet, 73.6% AUC on LaSOT, and 53.4% AUC on LaSOText. The base model ARTrackV2-256 reaches 75.9% AO on GOT-10k and 71.6% AUC on LaSOT while running at 94 FPS. Notably, ARTrackV2-L384 is 3.6× faster than ARTrack and over 5× faster than SeqTrack-L384 (49 FPS vs. 9 FPS) while simultaneously improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARTrackV2-L384 outperforms its direct predecessor ARTrack-L384 by +1.0 AO on GOT-10k (79.5 vs. 78.5), +0.5 AUC on TrackingNet (86.1 vs. 85.6), and +0.5 AUC on LaSOT (73.6 vs. 73.1), while being 3.6× faster. Against SeqTrack-L384, the gains are larger: +4.7 AO on GOT-10k (79.5 vs. 74.8) and +1.1 AUC on LaSOT (73.6 vs. 72.5), with a 5× speed advantage (49 FPS vs. 9 FPS). Compared to the strongest non-generative tracker MixViT-L384, ARTrackV2-L384 leads by +3.8 AO on GOT-10k and +1.2 AUC on LaSOT. Even the smaller ARTrackV2-256 (ViT-Base, 256×256 search) surpasses ARTrack384 (ViT-Base, 384×384) on GOT-10k AO (75.9 vs. 75.5) and matches it on TrackingNet AUC (84.9 vs. 85.1), demonstrating that the architectural improvements compensate for lower input resolution. Across all four benchmarks, ARTrackV2-L384 sets a new state-of-the-art on every reported metric, with no baseline matching or exceeding it.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARTrackV2 extends the autoregressive tracking lineage initiated by SeqTrack (which framed tracking as intra-frame sequence generation) and ARTrack (which shifted to inter-frame autoregression with video-sequence-level training). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of joint trajectory-appearance evolution: rather than modeling only coordinate tokens, ARTrackV2 simultaneously reconstructs the target's appearance via a generative decoder, replacing the discriminative score-and-crop template update strategies used in MixFormer, STARK, and SeqTrack. Architecturally, it abandons the intra-frame autoregressive decoder in favor of a pure transformer encoder that processes all tokens in parallel, trading a small accuracy dip for a substantial speed gain that is then recovered and exceeded by the appearance evolution components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.6× speedup over ARTrack while simultaneously achieving new SOTA on GOT-10k, TrackingNet, LaSOT, and LaSOText makes this a practically important contribution for real-time tracking deployment, where the generative paradigm's sequential decoding had been a bottleneck. The finding that a simple generative reconstruction objective (feature-space MSE) outperforms both score-based and likelihood-based discriminative appearance models, without requiring hand-tuned thresholds or multi-stage training, suggests a cleaner path for future tracker design. Open questions include whether the joint trajectory-appearance formulation generalizes to multi-object or long-horizon tracking, and whether the appearance reconstruction component provides diminishing returns as backbone capacity increases.
