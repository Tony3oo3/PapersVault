## Problem

Current vector graphics generation methods lack effective supervision linking pixel appearance to drawing primitives: pure vector-domain approaches ignore the similarity between shape parameters and paired pixel patterns, while two-stage pixel-diffusion-then-vectorization pipelines suffer from inaccurate gradients introduced by differentiable rasterization. This results in poor shape quality, especially for complex icons with many instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242456/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model starts from pure Gaussian noise and jointly denoises a 1D vector instruction tensor (serialized m/l/c/z commands with geometric parameters, capped at 96 instructions) and a 64×64 pixel image in a synchronized manner. The final output is a complete SVG instruction sequence (closed shapes composed of Move-To, Line-To, Cubic Bezier, and Close commands) paired with its rasterized rendering, both produced in a single forward pass of the dual-domain sampler.

## Method

The framework runs two parallel diffusion processes—a 1D U-Net for the vector domain and a 2D U-Net for the pixel domain—synchronized at every timestep, with each domain independently supervised by its own ground-truth noise target to avoid gradient errors from approximate inverse rendering. At each resolution level of both U-Nets, a cross-attention module injects conditional information from the other domain: a ResNet-based 2D embedding of the noisy pixel image conditions the vector denoiser (appearance-aware injection), while a 1D U-Net embedding of the noisy vector sequence conditions the pixel denoiser (shape-aware injection). A Shape Prior Regularizer (a small Transformer) is appended after the vector denoiser to enforce local tangent smoothness and global shape closure via differentiable geometric losses, applied only in the later denoising steps (t < 32). The overall training objective combines the vector noise-prediction loss, a weighted pixel noise-prediction loss, and the regularizer loss, all back-propagated through the respective domain networks without any differentiable rasterization in the loop.

## Results

On the Icon dataset, the proposed dual-domain diffusion method achieves a FID of 6.09 and VD of 0.0119, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the Font dataset, it reaches FID 6.66 and VD 0.0066, again the best across all methods. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that removing cross-domain conditional injection degrades Icon FID from 6.09 to 7.70, and removing the Shape Prior Regularizer raises it to 6.37.

**Table 1: Quantitative comparisons on Icon and Font datasets. FID (↓) measures distribution similarity; VD (↓) measures visual quality via CLIP feature distance to nearest neighbors.**

| Method | FID↓ (Icon) | FID↓ (Font) | VD↓ (Icon) | VD↓ (Font) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deepsvg [2] | 27.42 | 8.66 | 0.0482 | 0.0190 |
| Two-stage (Diffusion + LIVE) [15] | 15.90 | 11.70 | 0.0195 | 0.0120 |
| Im2Vec [20] | 43.69 | 28.34 | 0.0817 | 0.0643 |
| **Ours** | **6.09** | **6.66** | **0.0119** | **0.0066** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method dominates all three baselines on every metric across both datasets. Compared to Deepsvg (pure vector generation), the gap is most dramatic on the complex Icon dataset: FID drops from 27.42 to 6.09 (a 4.5× improvement) and VD from 0.0482 to 0.0119, reflecting Deepsvg's inability to handle multi-primitive shapes without pixel supervision. Against the two-stage Image Diffusion + LIVE pipeline, the proposed method improves Icon FID from 15.90 to 6.09 and Font FID from 11.70 to 6.66, showing that avoiding approximate differentiable-rasterization gradients yields more faithful shape structure. Im2Vec, which relies entirely on differentiable rasterization for supervision, performs worst on all four metrics (e.g., Icon FID 43.69 vs. 6.09), confirming the paper's argument that cross-domain gradient error accumulation severely degrades vector quality. Visually, the paper notes that Deepsvg produces irregular shapes on complex icons, the two-stage method exhibits mutual occlusion among primitives, and Im2Vec fails to generate the correct number of drawing instructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the hierarchical vector instruction encoding from DeepSVG (Carlier et al., 2020), but departs fundamentally from both existing paradigms for vector graphics generation. Pure vector-domain methods (DeepSVG, SVG-VAE, IconShop) ignore pixel appearance supervision entirely, while two-stage methods (Im2Vec, VectorFusion) rely on differentiable rasterization to bridge pixel-to-vector supervision, introducing approximate gradient errors. The key architectural departure is running synchronized, parallel diffusion processes in both vector and pixel domains with cross-attention conditioning between them, coupled with independent per-domain supervision signals that eliminate the need for differentiable rasterization altogether.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-domain diffusion with independent supervision offers a principled solution to the long-standing gradient-error problem in cross-domain vector generation, and the Shape Prior Regularizer (smoothness + closure constraints) encodes domain-specific geometric knowledge that generic diffusion models lack. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively simple font and icon datasets at 64×64 resolution with only four instruction types, and no text-conditioned generation is explored. Open questions include whether this approach scales to complex multi-layer vector graphics, higher resolutions, and text-to-SVG generation, and whether the cross-attention mechanism generalizes to other structured-output generation tasks beyond vector graphics.
