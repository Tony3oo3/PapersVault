## Problem

Existing NeRF-based inverse rendering methods either assume single light bounce and fail on inter-reflections, or require restrictive assumptions to handle multi-bounce scenarios. PANDORA, the first work combining polarization cues with NeRF for inverse rendering, assumes unpolarized incident light and single bounce, and cannot estimate BRDF parameters due to entangled radiance representation. This paper asks whether polarization cues can disambiguate full NeRF-based inverse rendering including multi-bounced, polarized light transport.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view HDR polarized images (four polarization angles per view), binary object masks, and known camera poses as input. It outputs per-surface diffuse albedo (RGB), roughness (scalar), and surface normal, from which diffuse-specular separation and DoLP images can be derived. The specular coefficient is fixed at [1,1,1] and refractive index at 1.5, restricting the method to opaque dielectric objects; scene-level rendering and metals/translucent materials are out of scope.

## Method

NeISF represents geometry with a VolSDF-style signed distance field and material with MLPs for albedo and roughness, but its key novelty is the incident Stokes field: three coordinate-based MLPs that implicitly record the already-rotated Stokes vectors of the second-last bounce for diffuse and specular components separately, avoiding the need to explicitly track reference frames through the bounce chain. This is necessary because the diffuse Mueller matrix reference frame depends on the surface normal while the specular one depends on the microfacet normal, making explicit rotation computation intractable for multi-bounce paths. A physically-based differentiable polarimetric renderer using Baek pBRDF with fixed Fibonacci hemisphere sampling then computes the outgoing Stokes vectors for the final bounce. Training proceeds in three stages: SDF initialization, material/lighting initialization (SDF frozen), and joint optimization with an Eikonal regularizer on the SDF.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset, NeISF achieves the best surface normal MAE of 1.727° (Bunny) and 2.541° (Teapot), substantially outperforming NeILF++ (4.481°/5.752°), VolSDF (5.210°/6.375°), and PANDORA (7.769°/6.722°). For mixed-intensity reconstruction, NeISF reaches 36.81 dB PSNR on Bunny and 31.32 dB on Teapot, exceeding the next-best VolSDF (35.08/28.91 dB) and PANDORA (24.62/22.47 dB). The ablation without polarization (Ours-no-pol) degrades normal MAE to 3.295°/3.894° and mixed PSNR to 34.45/30.23 dB, confirming the contribution of polarization cues. NeISF is the only method that simultaneously estimates BRDF parameters (roughness SI-L1: 0.0149/0.0712) and performs physically meaningful diffuse-specular separation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeISF outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric for both synthetic objects. Against PANDORA [17], the gap is largest: NeISF reduces normal MAE by roughly 6° (Bunny) and 4.2° (Teapot) and improves mixed PSNR by over 12 dB, because PANDORA assumes single-bounce unpolarized incident light and cannot estimate BRDF parameters. Against NeILF++ [89], NeISF improves normal MAE by ~2.8° (Bunny) and ~3.2° (Teapot) and mixed PSNR by ~4.7 dB and ~6.1 dB respectively; NeILF++ also lacks physically meaningful diffuse-specular separation and BRDF estimation. Against VolSDF [83], NeISF gains ~3.5° and ~3.8° in normal MAE and ~1.7 dB and ~2.4 dB in mixed PSNR, while additionally providing specular/diffuse decomposition and roughness/albedo maps that VolSDF does not support. The Ours-no-pol ablation (an unpolarized variant of NeISF) sits between NeISF and the external baselines, confirming that the polarization cues—rather than the architecture alone—drive the majority of the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeISF extends the NeILF/NeILF++ line of work on implicit incident light fields for multi-bounce inverse rendering by replacing scalar light intensities with 3D Stokes vectors, and extends PANDORA's polarization-aided NeRF decomposition by removing its single-bounce and unpolarized-incident-light assumptions. The key technical departure is the implicit recording of already-rotated Stokes vectors for diffuse and specular components separately, sidestepping the need to explicitly track reference-frame rotations through the bounce chain. The physically-based Baek pBRDF and a differentiable polarimetric renderer (built on Mitsuba 3.0) complete the pipeline, enabling full BRDF parameter estimation that PANDORA could not provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first NeRF-based inverse rendering method that handles multi-bounce polarized light, enabling accurate geometry and material recovery in scenes with inter-reflections where unpolarized methods conflate albedo patterns with surface geometry. The implicit rotation trick is a clean solution to a genuinely tricky reference-frame problem in polarimetric rendering. However, the method is limited to opaque dielectric objects in indoor settings, the implicit Stokes representation cannot be exported to conventional renderers, and sensor misalignment in current polarization cameras prevents recovery of high-frequency geometric detail. Open questions include extending to metals/translucent materials, outdoor scenes, and finding ways to make the implicit lighting representation usable outside the training pipeline.
