## Problem

Conventional BeV segmentation models operate on dense grids with fixed resolution and range, allocating uniform computational resources to every cell regardless of relevance. This makes temporal aggregation over long horizons prohibitively expensive and prevents deployment on memory-constrained platforms. The paper asks whether BeV segmentation can be performed on sparse point sets without sacrificing accuracy, while gaining precise control over memory and compute.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241003/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-camera RGB images (6 cameras, 224×480 or 448×800) and, in temporal settings, up to 8 past frames (2 seconds at 4 Hz). It produces BeV segmentation predictions at 50 cm resolution over a 100 m × 100 m area (200×200 grid) for vehicles, pedestrians, and lanes. Crucially, predictions are only computed at a subset of sampled BeV points; all non-sampled locations are set to zero in the final dense map, meaning the model inherently trades coverage for efficiency at inference time.

## Method

PointBeV replaces the dense BeV grid with a sparse set of 2D points, each lifted into a 3D pillar. A Sparse Feature Pulling module projects each 3D pillar point only onto cameras whose field of view contains it (avoiding ~84% of redundant projections in a 6-camera setup), then bilinearly interpolates image features. A two-pass coarse/fine training strategy—inspired by NeRF sampling—first forwards a uniform random subset of points, selects top-logit anchors, then densifies around them with a k×k kernel, allowing the model to train on only 20% of grid points while matching full-grid accuracy. For temporal fusion, a Submanifold Attention module performs windowed attention over a spatiotemporal neighborhood, attending only to nearby points across past frames rather than the full sequence. The flattened 2D BeV features are processed by a sparse U-Net with task-specific heads, and at inference the model supports flexible sampling patterns (uniform, Gaussian, LiDAR-guided, HD-map-guided) to trade memory against accuracy without retraining.

## Results

On nuScenes vehicle segmentation (no visibility filtering, 224×480), PointBeV with EfficientNet-b4 achieves 38.7 IoU, outperforming Simple-BEV (36.9) and BEVFormer (35.8) by +1.8 and +2.9 points respectively. In the temporal setting, PointBeV-T reaches 39.9 IoU (EN-b4) and 43.2 at 448×800. For pedestrian segmentation, PointBeV-T achieves 19.9 IoU, surpassing TBP-Former (18.6) by +1.3, and for lane segmentation, PointBeV reaches 49.6 IoU, exceeding MatrixVT and PETRv2 (both 44.8) by +4.8 points. On Lyft L5, PointBeV (EN-b4) achieves 45.4 IoU (100m range) and 72.6 IoU (30m range), setting new state-of-the-art in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointBeV wins across all 12 baselines on every task and setting reported. On vehicle segmentation (nuScenes, no visibility filtering, 224×480), PointBeV (EN-b4) at 38.7 IoU beats Simple-BEV (36.9, +1.8), BEVFormer (35.8, +2.9), FIERY (35.8, +2.9), BAEFormer (36.0, +2.7), LaRa (35.4, +3.3), and CVT (31.4, +7.3). In the temporal setting, PointBeV-T (39.9) surpasses FIERY temporal (38.2) by +1.7. For pedestrian segmentation, PointBeV-T (19.9) edges out TBP-Former temporal (18.6) by +1.3 and TBP-Former static (17.2) by +2.7, while also beating LSS (15.0) and ST-P3 (14.5) by large margins. For lane segmentation, PointBeV (49.6) leads MatrixVT (44.8) and PETRv2 (44.8) by +4.8, M2BEV (38.0) by +11.6, and BEVFormer (25.7) by +23.9. On Lyft L5, PointBeV (EN-b4) achieves 45.4/72.6 IoU (long/short range), outperforming SimpleBEV (44.5/70.4) and BeVFormer (44.5/69.9) in both ranges. Notably, PointBeV achieves these gains while being trained on only 20% of the dense grid points, and its sparse inference can match full-grid performance with one-sixth of the points.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointBeV extends the feature-pulling paradigm established by BEVFormer and Simple-BEV, where 3D BEV coordinates are projected into camera images to retrieve visual features, by making the entire pipeline operate on a sparse subset of BEV points rather than a dense grid. It adapts submanifold attention from 3D point cloud segmentation (Lai et al., CVPR 2022) into a spatiotemporal windowed attention for temporal aggregation, and borrows the two-stage coarse/fine sampling strategy from NeRF for training stability. The key conceptual departure from prior BEV segmentation work is treating the BEV as a sparse point set with controllable density, rather than a fixed-resolution image-like grid, which enables memory-bounded computation and adaptive inference without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 20% of BEV points suffice to match full-grid performance challenges the implicit assumption that dense grid processing is necessary for BEV segmentation, and opens the door to longer temporal contexts, higher resolutions, and deployment on memory-constrained hardware. The adaptive inference capability—trading accuracy for compute at test time without retraining, or incorporating LiDAR/HD-map priors to guide sampling—makes the approach practically flexible for diverse driving scenarios. Open questions include whether the sparse paradigm extends naturally to 3D occupancy forecasting, whether dynamic sampling guided by agent motion or downstream task needs can further reduce the point budget, and whether a lightweight completion network could recover the information lost in unsampled regions.
