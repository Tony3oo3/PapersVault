## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution methods achieve strong perceptual quality but require many inference steps (100 for LDM, 15 for ResShift), making them impractical for real-time use. Existing acceleration approaches either demand an explicit degradation model or still need a non-trivial number of steps, leaving open whether a single-step diffusion-based SR model can match or exceed multi-step quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240444/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (4× downsampled, e.g., 64×64 → 256×256) encoded in VQ-VAE latent space, along with a random noise vector ε, and produces a single high-resolution image in one forward pass of the student network. The LR image is concatenated as a conditioning signal y throughout the network, and the random noise provides diversity so that different noise realizations yield different plausible HR outputs from the same LR input.

## Method

SinSR first derives a deterministic (non-Markovian) reverse process from ResShift's stochastic formulation, analogous to DDIM, which yields a fixed mapping F_θ(x_T, y) from initial noise to HR image without retraining. A student network f_θ̂ is then distilled to learn this mapping in a single step via an MSE loss between its one-step prediction and the teacher's multi-step deterministic output. To break the performance ceiling imposed by the teacher, a consistency-preserving loss is introduced: the student simultaneously learns an inverse mapping (HR → x_T) and a forward mapping (x_T → HR), and the GT image is used to regularize the forward pass through its predicted inversion. The student is initialized from the teacher weights and trained for only 30K iterations (vs. 500K for the teacher), with all three losses (distillation, inverse, and GT consistency) weighted equally.

## Results

On ImageNet-Test (×4 SR), SinSR-1 achieves the best perceptual quality with LPIPS 0.221 and CLIPIQA 0.611, matching or exceeding the 15-step teacher ResShift-15 (LPIPS 0.228, CLIPIQA 0.603) while using only 1 inference step. On real-world RealSet65, SinSR-1 scores CLIPIQA 0.7150 and MUSIQ 62.169, outperforming ResShift-15 (0.6537 / 61.330) by a large margin. Inference time drops from 0.633 s (ResShift-15, bs=1) to 0.058 s (SinSR-1), a ~10× speedup, with a modest PSNR trade-off (24.56 vs 24.90 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SinSR-1 outperforms the 15-step teacher ResShift-15 on perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.221 vs 0.228; CLIPIQA 0.611 vs 0.603) while incurring a small PSNR/SSIM drop (24.56/0.657 vs 24.90/0.673), and does so in 1 step instead of 15 (~10× faster). On real-world RealSet65, SinSR-1 beats ResShift-15 on both CLIPIQA (0.7150 vs 0.6537) and MUSIQ (62.169 vs 61.330), and similarly dominates on RealSR (CLIPIQA 0.6887 vs 0.5958). Among GAN-based baselines, SinSR-1 surpasses BSRGAN (the strongest GAN) by 0.036 in CLIPIQA and 0.038 in LPIPS on ImageNet-Test, and by a wider margin on real-world data. Compared to LDM-15, SinSR-1 improves CLIPIQA by 0.099 and LPIPS by 0.048 while being 7× faster. The Rectified Flow distillation variant (Table 6) underperforms SinSR's direct distillation on all three metrics (LPIPS 0.2322 vs 0.2221; CLIPIQA 0.5753 vs 0.6072), confirming that the simpler one-step deterministic mapping is more effective for SR than iterative reflow-based strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ResShift, which reformulates the initial diffusion state to incorporate LR image information and shortens the Markov chain from 100 to 15 steps, and on DDIM's deterministic sampling principle. Rather than further shortening the chain (as ExposureDiffusion does with 3 steps but requiring explicit degradation models), SinSR takes a different route: it derives a fully deterministic sampling process from ResShift's stochastic formulation and distills the resulting input-to-output mapping into a single-step student network. The consistency-preserving loss, which back-propagates through a learned inversion of the GT image, departs from vanilla distillation by incorporating ground-truth supervision that the teacher model never sees during inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 10× inference speedup (15→1 steps) while matching or exceeding the teacher's perceptual quality makes diffusion-based SR practically deployable in real-time or resource-constrained settings, removing the primary barrier to adoption. The empirical finding that learning a single deterministic mapping is substantially easier than multi-level denoising (Table 5) suggests that distillation strategies for low-level vision tasks may be fundamentally different from those for generative tasks, and that more sophisticated iterative distillation (e.g., Rectified Flow) can actually hurt fidelity due to accumulated error. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to other restoration tasks (denoising, inpainting) and whether the student model's diversity is truly preserved at scale, since the paper's diversity demonstration (Fig. 2) is qualitative.
