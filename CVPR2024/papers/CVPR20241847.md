## Problem

Existing Transformer-based multi-task dense prediction methods suffer from two key limitations: (1) they either operate on a single encoder stage or naively concatenate multi-stage features, overlooking cross-stage correlations critical for building task-specific representations; and (2) they treat all pixels from all tasks as reconstruction bases in self-attention, causing muddled all-pair dependencies, redundant computation, and noise that hinders inter-task synergy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241847/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and simultaneously produces pixel-wise predictions for multiple dense tasks at the original spatial resolution. On NYUD-v2 it outputs semantic segmentation, monocular depth, surface normal, and boundary maps; on PASCAL-Context it additionally outputs human parts segmentation and saliency maps. No depth input is required at inference, and the learned EM basis μ^(0) remains fixed after training, so inference incurs no extra iterative cost beyond the R=5 EM steps already embedded in the forward pass.

## Method

SEM introduces two plug-and-play modules on top of a shared ViT/Swin encoder. The intra-task hierarchy-adaptive module applies deformable attention with hierarchy-adaptive keys and values: it samples deformed points at each of S encoder stages, learns per-point stage-importance weights via a small MLP, and constructs task-specific K/V through weighted aggregation, reducing complexity from O(C(ΣH_sW_s)²) to O(CH_SW_S·Σ⌊H_s/r_s⌋⌊W_s/r_s⌋). The inter-task EM-interactive module formulates cross-task interaction as a Gaussian Mixture Model solved by R iterations of EM: each task starts from a shared initial basis μ^(0), iteratively refines its own (μ^t)^(R) via attention-like E-steps and weighted-average M-steps, and the task-specific bases are then averaged to produce a compact mutual-information basis of size K≪HW, reducing inter-task attention from O(CT²H²W²) to O(CTHWK). An exponential moving average updates μ^(0) across training batches to prevent catastrophic forgetting, and the module is applied at every decoder stage for full-stage interaction.

## Results

On NYUD-v2, SEM achieves 56.82 mIoU for semantic segmentation, 0.4937 RMSE for depth, 18.45 mErr for normals, and 78.40 odsF for boundaries, setting new state-of-the-art on all four metrics. On PASCAL-Context, SEM reaches 81.66 mIoU (SemSeg), 69.90 mIoU (Parsing), 84.95 maxF (Saliency), 13.39 mErr (Normal), and 73.80 odsF (Boundary), again leading all baselines. The multi-task gain (Δ_m) over single-task baselines is +3.06% on NYUD-v2, confirming that the synergy between tasks is genuinely beneficial rather than a trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEM outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across both datasets. The closest competitor is TaskPrompter [43], which SEM surpasses by +1.52 mIoU on NYUD-v2 SemSeg (56.82 vs 55.30), +0.21 RMSE on Depth (0.4937 vs 0.5152), and +0.77 mIoU on PASCAL-Context SemSeg (81.66 vs 80.89). Against the prior Transformer-based SOTA InvPT [42], the margins are larger: +3.26 mIoU on NYUD-v2 SemSeg and +2.63 mIoU on PASCAL-Context SemSeg. Older CNN-based methods (Cross-Stitch, PAD-Net, PAP, PSD) trail by 15–20 mIoU on SemSeg, highlighting the gap between convolutional and Transformer-based multi-task architectures. On PASCAL-Context, SEM also leads the strongest PASCAL-specific baselines (ASTMT, ATRC variants) by 13–18 mIoU on SemSeg, confirming the gains are not dataset-specific. Notably, SEM is the only method that simultaneously improves all five PASCAL-Context tasks, whereas TaskPrompter and InvPT show slight regressions on some metrics relative to earlier methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEM extends the Transformer-based multi-task dense prediction line (DeMT, InvPT, TaskPrompter) by addressing two identified gaps: cross-stage feature integration within each task and the inefficiency of full pixel-to-pixel inter-task attention. The intra-task module repurposes deformable attention (from Deformable DETR-style reference points) to selectively sample salient points across encoder stages, while the inter-task module adapts the EM/GMM framework from EMANet to learn a compact shared basis set instead of attending over all concatenated task tokens. The decoder architecture and training protocol follow InvPT, positioning SEM as a targeted architectural refinement rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EM-based inter-task module offers a principled complexity reduction (from O(CT²H²W²) to O(CTHWK)) that makes multi-task dense prediction more tractable at higher resolutions, and the hierarchy-adaptive keys provide a lightweight alternative to naive multi-stage concatenation. However, validation is limited to two relatively small indoor/outdoor datasets (NYUD-v2, PASCAL-Context), and it remains unclear whether the compact basis approach scales to larger-scale benchmarks or non-dense tasks. An open question is whether the fixed number of EM iterations (R=5) and basis size K generalize across task counts and input resolutions without re-tuning.
