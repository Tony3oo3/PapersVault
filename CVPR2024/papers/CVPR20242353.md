## Problem

Existing synthetic fog datasets rely on the Atmospheric Scattering Model (ASM) or real-time rendering engines, both of which fail to capture the full physically-based imaging process: ASM neglects global illumination, multiple light sources, and sensor/optics characteristics, while rasterization-based engines cannot accurately model light transport in scattering media. This gap between synthetic and real foggy images limits the generalization of defogging models trained on synthetic data to real-world foggy conditions, particularly at night with active lighting.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes procedurally-generated 3D driving scenes (500 unique scenes with pedestrians, bicyclists, cars), 16 cloudy sky maps, a scattering coefficient σ_s (0.005, 0.01, or 0.02 corresponding to 600 m, 300 m, or 150 m visibility), and calibrated camera parameters from a Mi10Pro cellphone. For each scene it outputs paired foggy and clear RGB images under two lighting conditions (sky light only, and sky light plus street lamps/automotive lights), along with pixel-accurate depth maps, segmentation labels, and RAW sensor images — yielding 1350 training pairs total. The availability of RAW images is notable, as no prior paired foggy RAW dataset exists for training defogging networks in the RAW domain.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, foggy scene radiance is computed via volumetric path tracing, where fog is modeled as a spatial volume with a Henyey-Greenstein phase function (asymmetry parameter g = 0.87) and the rendering equation is solved by Monte Carlo integration, enabling accurate modeling of in-scattering and out-scattering under global illumination and multiple point light sources. Second, the spectral radiance passes through a physically-based camera model (ISETCam) comprising a diffraction-limited optics stage (cosine-fourth law off-axis attenuation), a sensor stage simulating photovoltaic conversion with Poisson-distributed shot noise and calibrated CFA/infrared filter data, and an ISP stage (black level compensation, white balance, demosaicing, color correction, gamma). This end-to-end approach differs fundamentally from ASM, which only applies a scalar transmission coefficient and a single airlight term to a pre-rendered clear image, and from game-engine rasterization, which approximates light transport without solving the full rendering equation.

## Results

When AECR-Net is trained on SynFog and evaluated on real-world fog datasets, it achieves PSNR 15.43, SSIM 0.6116, and DHQI 44.46 on O-Haze, outperforming models trained on Foggy Cityscapes (PSNR 14.46, SSIM 0.5737, DHQI 43.40) and Virtual KITTI (PSNR 13.90, SSIM 0.5315, DHQI 42.80). For downstream object detection with YOLOv8, AECR-Net trained on SynFog reaches 71.5% mAP on Foggy Zurich and 55.5% mAP on Seeing Through Fog, surpassing Foggy Cityscapes (69.7%/54.8%) and Virtual KITTI (68.9%/53.3%). On the SynFog test set itself, DehazeFormer attains the best PSNR (27.96) and SSIM (0.923), while GridDehazeNet achieves the best LPIPS (0.163), with all scores noticeably lower than on RESIDE, highlighting the increased difficulty of the more realistic SynFog.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is between three synthetic fog training datasets (SynFog, Foggy Cityscapes, Virtual KITTI) using the same defogging architectures. SynFog-trained models consistently outperform both alternatives across all four real-world fog test sets: AECR-Net gains +0.97 PSNR and +1.76 DHQI over Foggy Cityscapes on O-Haze, and +2.10 DHQI over Virtual KITTI on BeDDE. For detection, AECR-Net + SynFog achieves 71.5% mAP on Foggy Zurich versus 69.7% (Foggy Cityscapes) and 68.9% (Virtual KITTI); DehazeFormer + SynFog reaches 69.7% versus 67.9% and 68.5% respectively. A critical ablation shows that replacing the full pipeline with ASM-based synthesis (SynFog-β) causes a dramatic drop: DehazeFormer + SynFog-β scores only 59.7% mAP on Foggy Zurich, nearly 10 points below SynFog, confirming that volumetric path tracing and camera simulation are essential. Among the seven benchmarked defogging methods on SynFog, DehazeFormer leads in PSNR/SSIM while GridDehazeNet leads in LPIPS; DCP and CAP produce excessively dark outputs, and FFA-Net/DehazeFormer yield the smoothest results, indicating substantial room for improvement on this more realistic dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Atmospheric Scattering Model (ASM) paradigm for synthetic fog generation, which treats fog as a simple transmission-plus-airlight overlay on clear images. It replaces ASM with volumetric path tracing using a Henyey-Greenstein phase function to capture global illumination and multi-source scattering, and further extends the pipeline through a full physically-based camera model (ISETCam) covering optics, sensor noise, and ISP processing. The scene generation leverages procedural modeling techniques previously used in automotive simulation, while the camera simulation builds directly on the ISETCam framework from Stanford. Conceptually, the paper unifies two previously separate concerns—accurate light transport in scattering media and faithful camera imaging—into a single end-to-end pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical finding is that physically realistic synthetic data (1,350 pairs) outperforms larger but less physically faithful datasets (Foggy Cityscapes: 8,925 pairs; Virtual KITTI: 3,452 pairs) on real-world fog transfer, suggesting data quality may matter more than quantity for sim-to-real defogging. The sensor noise ablation (~7 DHQI improvement) further underscores that modeling the full imaging chain, not just the scene, is critical. Limitations include the small dataset scale due to path-tracing cost, and the evaluation is confined to a handful of real fog benchmarks. Open questions include whether the pipeline can scale to larger scene libraries, whether it generalizes to other scattering media (rain, snow), and whether RAW-domain defogging—enabled by the pipeline's multi-modality output—can yield further gains.
