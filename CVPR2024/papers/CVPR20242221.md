## Problem

Source-free Domain Adaptation (SFDA) methods have achieved strong empirical results, yet they lack a theoretical foundation explaining why they work. Unlike UDA, which is grounded in the target-error bound via source error and domain discrepancy, SFDA cannot access source data, making that theory inapplicable. This paper bridges the gap by showing that the discriminability-and-diversity loss structure common to SFDA methods is equivalent to the training objective in the self-training theory under the expansion assumption, thereby establishing a target error bound for SFDA-trained models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source-pretrained ResNet-50 or ResNet-101 model (with a fully-connected → batch-norm → fully-connected classifier head) and a set of unlabeled target-domain RGB images. It produces a target-adapted model that outputs per-class probability predictions. Unusually, the method also learns a differentiable data augmentation policy (25 augmentations × 2 operations each, parameterized via Faster AutoAugment) and maintains a teacher model as an EMA copy of the student, which together are updated on a separate schedule from the main model.

## Method

The method comprises two alternating training loops. In Model Training, the student model is updated with a discriminability loss (mean prediction dissimilarity between each sample and its K nearest neighbors in feature space, computed over M augmented views) and a diversity loss (mean prediction similarity to non-neighbors in the batch), where the diversity weight λ_div is auto-adjusted as λ_div^max · L_dis with stop-gradient, so the constraint tightens as the model becomes more discriminative. In Augmentation Training, the learnable augmentation is optimized to maximize the student's non-saturating prediction entropy (increasing the expansion factor d) while minimizing the teacher's prediction entropy (decreasing the ground-truth inconsistency μ), yielding a tighter theoretical error bound. The teacher is updated via exponential moving average, and the two loops alternate with the augmentation trained every ĉ epochs. The key theoretical insight is that this discriminability–diversity objective is precisely the self-training-plus-constraint objective from the expansion-assumption theory, so the trained model inherits a provable target error upper bound of max{d/(d−1), 2}·μ.

## Results

On Office-31, the proposed method achieves 90.3% average accuracy, improving by 0.4% over the direct baseline AaD (89.9%) and matching CoWA-JMDS (90.3%), trailing only C-SFDA (90.5%) by 0.2%. On Office-Home, it reaches 73.4% average accuracy, a 0.7% gain over AaD (72.7%), and is second-best behind C-SFDA (73.5%) by just 0.1%. On VisDA2017, it achieves the best per-class accuracy of 88.4%, outperforming AaD (88.0%) by 0.4% and the next-best C-SFDA (87.8%) by 0.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is built upon AaD [53] and consistently outperforms it across all three benchmarks: +0.4% on Office-31 (90.3 vs. 89.9), +0.7% on Office-Home (73.4 vs. 72.7), and +0.4% on VisDA2017 (88.4 vs. 88.0). Against the strongest competitor C-SFDA [19], the gap is minimal: −0.2% on Office-31, −0.1% on Office-Home, but a +0.6% win on VisDA2017 where the proposed method achieves the best per-class accuracy (88.4%). The method ties CoWA-JMDS [21] on Office-31 (both 90.3%) and beats it by 0.9% on Office-Home (73.4 vs. 72.5) and 1.5% on VisDA2017 (88.4 vs. 86.9). Against earlier baselines, the gains are substantial: over SHOT [23] by +1.7% (Office-31), +1.6% (Office-Home), and +5.5% (VisDA2017); over A2Net [49] by +0.2%, +0.6%, and +4.1% respectively. The ablation in Table 7 further shows the techniques transfer to SHOT-IM [23], yielding +1.3%, +0.7%, and +2.9% improvements on the three datasets, confirming broad applicability beyond the AaD backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges the gap between the practical SFDA literature (SHOT-IM, AaD, C-SFDA, DaC, etc.) and the theoretical self-training framework of Wei et al. (ICLR 2020) based on the expansion assumption. While prior work [9, 53] had noted that most SFDA methods share a discriminability-plus-diversity loss structure, no one had formally connected this to the self-training objective with a diversity constraint that yields a target error bound. The method itself builds directly on AaD's loss formulation, augmenting it with an auto-adjusting trade-off parameter and a learnable data augmentation trained via a teacher-student framework inspired by TeachAugment and Faster AutoAugment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary contribution is a general theoretical lens that explains why a wide family of SFDA methods work, which was previously absent and limits the field's ability to reason about method design. The two derived techniques (auto-adjusting diversity and learnable augmentation) are simple and transferable, as demonstrated by their application to SHOT-IM. However, the practical gains are modest (0.4–0.7% over AaD), and the authors acknowledge a key limitation: the analysis does not account for the source model's initial quality on the target domain, which remains an open problem for making SFDA more robust in practice.
