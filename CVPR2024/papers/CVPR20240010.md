## Problem

Unsupervised video object segmentation in complex scenes remains challenging because existing methods over-rely on motion cues and neglect structural information such as color, texture, and shape, failing when optical flow quality is low or backgrounds are cluttered. While slot attention offers a promising mechanism for foreground-background discrimination, prior slot-attention-based segmentation methods degrade in real-world scenes due to randomly initialized slots, simple multi-head attention lacking robust feature discrimination, and attention over all input features introducing noise from similar objects.

## Input / Output

The model consumes one target frame RGB image, N_R=3 reference frame RGB images, and their corresponding optical flow maps (computed by RAFT), all uniformly resized to 384×384 for training and 512×512 for evaluation. It produces a binary foreground segmentation mask per frame via a shared CNN decoder that fuses correlation maps from independent RGB and optical-flow streams. The dual-stream design means the same architecture processes both modalities in parallel, with features concatenated before the final decoder.

## Method

The core contribution is a Guided Slot Attention (GSA) mechanism with three structural improvements over standard slot attention. First, a slot generator replaces random initialization by applying pixelwise softmax to compressed target-frame features to produce foreground/background masks, then extracting guided slots via global weighted average pooling, giving slots meaningful spatial context from the start. Second, KNN filtering selects only N=16 nearest features in feature space to each slot from the aggregated feature set, preventing similar objects in complex scenes from acting as noise during iterative attention. Third, a Feature Aggregation Transformer (FAT) fuses local features (extracted via k-means clustering on the target frame) with global features (extracted via channel-wise softmax on reference frames) using attentive pooling and bidirectional transformer blocks, providing rich key-value context for slot refinement. The full model uses dual ResNet101 or MiT-b2 encoder streams for RGB and optical flow, trained in three stages: DUTS saliency pretraining, parameter transfer to the flow stream, and joint fine-tuning on DAVIS-16 and YouTube-VOS with IoU and weighted BCE losses.

## Results

On DAVIS-16, GSA-Net with ResNet101 achieves G_M=87.7, J_M=87.0, F_M=88.4 at 41.5 FPS, outperforming HFAN (87.0/86.6/87.3) and TMO (86.1/85.6/86.6). With MiT-b2 backbone, the method reaches G_M=88.2, J_M=87.4, F_M=87.4, and with test-time augmentation (Ours*), it achieves G_M=88.9, J_M=88.3, F_M=89.6. On FBMS, which includes multi-object scenarios, GSA-Net with MiT-b2 achieves J_M=82.3 (83.1 with TTA), surpassing OAST (83.0) and all other methods that report FBMS results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSA-Net outperforms the strongest prior method HFAN on DAVIS-16 across all three metrics with both backbones: with ResNet101, GSA-Net gains +0.7 G_M, +0.4 J_M, and +1.1 F_M over HFAN, while with MiT-b2 it gains +0.7 G_M and +0.6 J_M (though F_M drops by 0.8). With test-time augmentation, GSA-Net (MiT-b2) surpasses HFAN* by +0.2 G_M, +0.3 J_M, and +0.3 F_M. On FBMS, GSA-Net with MiT-b2 (J_M=82.3) slightly trails OAST (83.0) but exceeds TMO (79.9), PMN (77.7), and TransportNet (78.7); with TTA, GSA-Net reaches 83.1, edging out OAST. Notably, GSA-Net achieves these results at 41.5 FPS (ResNet101) or 38.2 FPS (MiT-b2), substantially faster than HFAN (19.0/18.4 FPS) and IMP (1.79 FPS), while TMO is faster (43.2 FPS) but scores lower on DAVIS-16 (86.1 vs. 87.7 G_M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the slot attention mechanism from object-centric learning (Locatello et al., NeurIPS 2020) to unsupervised video object segmentation, departing from the dominant motion-centric paradigm (MATNet, RTNet, FSNet, HFAN) that fuses optical flow with RGB appearance. The key conceptual departure is replacing randomly initialized empty slots with guided slots derived from encoder features, and replacing the standard all-features attention with KNN-filtered attention plus a Feature Aggregation Transformer that fuses local (target frame) and global (reference frames) information. Prior slot-attention-based segmentation methods (SCOUTER, PARTS, Slot-VPS) were limited to synthetic or simple scenes; this work explicitly targets the failure mode of those methods in complex real-world video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that incorporating spatial structural information via guided slot attention can reduce the over-reliance on motion cues that plagues existing unsupervised VOS methods, yielding more robust segmentation in scenes with complex backgrounds and multiple similar objects. The KNN filtering and FAT components are modular and could be transplanted to other slot-attention-based architectures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer video sequences, whether the dependence on pre-computed RAFT optical flow limits real-time deployment, and whether the semi-supervised reliance on target-frame ground truth masks can be eliminated for truly unsupervised settings.
