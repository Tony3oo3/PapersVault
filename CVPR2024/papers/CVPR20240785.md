## Problem

Sparse CNNs have long been outperformed by point transformers in 3D semantic segmentation despite their superior efficiency and ease of deployment. This paper identifies the root cause of the gap as a lack of adaptivity: transformers flexibly adjust receptive fields and inter-point relationships per context, while sparse CNNs rely on static, fixed-kernel perception. The work asks whether a lightweight CNN design can recover this adaptivity without incurring the computational cost of self-attention.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds that are first voxelized into sparse voxel grids during preprocessing; during training, up to 100,000 randomly sampled points per scene are used, while full point clouds are used at validation. Normal vectors and 3D coordinates are appended to the raw features as additional input channels. The output is a per-point semantic class prediction (20 classes on ScanNet v2, 200 on ScanNet200, 13 on S3DIS, or 16/19 on outdoor benchmarks), produced by a final linear head on the upsampled voxel features.

## Method

OA-CNNs introduce two adaptive mechanisms into a sparse CNN backbone. First, spatially adaptive receptive fields are achieved by partitioning the voxel scene into non-overlapping pyramid grids at K=3 scales; a learnable aggregator predicts per-voxel softmax weights over these scales and fuses the multi-scale ARConv outputs with the original features, so flat regions attend to large grids while edges and small objects attend to small ones. Second, Adaptive Relation Convolution (ARConv) replaces the standard 3×3×3 kernel with a depthwise convolution whose kernel weights are generated dynamically: a centroid feature is computed by average-pooling projected voxel features within each grid, and each voxel's weight is a linear projection of its feature difference from the centroid, normalized by per-channel softmax across the grid (a multi-one-multi paradigm that avoids the O(N²) cost of self-attention). The full architecture stacks ARConv blocks followed by submanifold convolutions in a hierarchical encoder with stride-2 sparse downsampling and skip-connection upsampling, trained end-to-end with AdamW and cosine annealing.

## Results

OA-CNNs achieves 76.1% val mIoU and 75.6% test mIoU on ScanNet v2, 78.9% mIoU on nuScenes, 70.6% mIoU on SemanticKITTI, 32.3% val / 33.3% test mIoU on ScanNet200, and 71.1% mIoU on S3DIS Area 5. These results surpass all compared point-transformer and sparse-CNN baselines under identical experimental settings (no extra pretraining or auxiliary losses). The largest model (OA-CNN-L) runs at 213 ms with 3.6 GB GPU memory on a single RTX 3090, maintaining up to 5× better speed than transformer counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ScanNet v2, OA-CNNs (76.1% val) outperforms the strongest point-transformer baselines—OctFormer (75.7%), PTv2 (75.4%), and Stratified Transformer (74.3%)—by 0.4–1.8 mIoU, and surpasses all prior sparse-CNN methods (LargeKernel3D 73.2%, MinkowskiNet 72.2%, SparseUNet 69.3%) by 2.9–6.8 mIoU. On the outdoor benchmarks, OA-CNNs leads SphereFormer (78.4% nuScenes, 67.8% SemanticKITTI) by 0.5 and 2.8 mIoU respectively, and outperforms SPVNAS (77.4/64.7) and Cylinder3D (76.1/64.3) by a wider margin. On ScanNet200, OA-CNNs achieves 32.3% val mIoU, beating PTv2 (29.3%), LGround (28.9%), and MinkowskiNet (25.1%) by 3.0–7.2 mIoU, and also surpasses OctFormer's test score (32.6 vs. 33.3). On S3DIS Area 5, OA-CNNs (71.1%) edges out PTv2 (71.6%) by a narrow 0.5 mIoU gap in the other direction but clearly beats Point Transformer (70.4%) and MinkowskiNet (65.4%). Overall, OA-CNNs wins or ties on every benchmark while using no self-attention modules and consuming significantly less memory and inference time than transformer-based competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

OA-CNNs builds directly on the submanifold sparse convolutional framework (Graham et al., 2018) and the hierarchical encoder structure popularized by PointNet++ (Qi et al., 2017), but departs from both by identifying 'adaptivity'—rather than kernel size or architectural depth—as the critical missing ingredient that separates sparse CNNs from point transformers. The spatially adaptive receptive field design draws on the selective kernel paradigm (Li et al., 2019), while the Adaptive Relation Convolution (ARConv) reinterprets dynamic convolution (Jia et al., 2016; Yang et al., 2019) through a multi-one-multi centroid-based paradigm that avoids the quadratic cost of multi-to-multi attention. Unlike prior dynamic convolution work on point clouds (KPConv, PACConv), OA-CNNs operates on voxelized grids with linear complexity in voxel count, maintaining the structural efficiency that makes sparse CNNs attractive for deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing narrative that self-attention is necessary for state-of-the-art 3D semantic segmentation, showing that a lightweight adaptive mechanism within a pure sparse CNN can match or exceed transformer-based methods (notably by 2.8 mIoU on SemanticKITTI) while running up to 5× faster with lower memory. This has direct practical implications for edge deployment and real-time applications where transformer inference cost is prohibitive. The main limitation acknowledged is that pyramid grid sizes are set empirically rather than learned or searched, leaving open the question of whether a principled grid-size selection strategy could further improve results. Broader open questions include whether the adaptive-receptive-field + ARConv recipe transfers to other 3D tasks (detection, instance segmentation) and whether it remains competitive as point transformer architectures continue to evolve.
