## Problem

Real-time rendering requires fast, compact BRDF evaluation, but analytical microfacet models lack realism for complex materials, measured BRDFs incur prohibitive storage, and Monte Carlo simulation is too slow. Existing neural BRDF approaches struggle with the 4D directional input, making it difficult to construct the feature-grid acceleration structures that enable real-time inference. The paper addresses how to represent the full 4D bidirectional reflectance in a unified, material-agnostic framework that is both memory-efficient and evaluable at interactive framerates.

## Input / Output

The method takes two hemisphere directions (incident ω_i and outgoing ω_o) as primary input, and optionally a 2D UV coordinate u for spatially-varying BTF. The 4D BRDF is factorized into two independent 3D hemisphere queries on a HEALPix spherical grid, each returning a k-dimensional feature vector; these are concatenated and fed to a tiny MLP that outputs a single scalar reflectance value. For BTF, a plug-in neural texture lookup at the shading point provides additional spatial features that are concatenated before the MLP. At runtime, interpolation weights are fixed at 1/4 per corner for speed, and the entire evaluation (grid lookup + codebook indexing + MLP) must complete in ~4.5 ms per frame at 1920×1080.

## Method

NeuBRDF factorizes the 4D BRDF into two 3D hemispherical feature-grids (one per direction), discretized using a customized HEALPix spherical pixelation chosen for its uniform area, sub-0.5 ms analytic indexing, and native hierarchy for multi-resolution pruning. Instead of storing bulky per-grid features, each grid point holds a low-cost integer index into a shared codebook of learnable 'neural reflectance primitives' (vector quantization), enabling cross-material sharing and a compression ratio of orders of magnitude. A tiny 2-layer MLP (64 neurons) decodes the concatenated 2k-dimensional features into the final reflectance value. For spatially-varying effects, a plug-in neural texture provides additional channel features at the shading point, which are concatenated with the codebook features before the MLP. All components (indices, codebook, MLP, and optional texture) are trained jointly end-to-end in a supervised manner against ground-truth reflectance data.

## Results

On the MERL measured BRDF dataset, NeuBRDF achieves the best performance across all three metrics: MAE 0.0017, RMSE 0.0031, and SSIM 0.996, outperforming the closest neural baseline NBRDF (MAE 0.0028, RMSE 0.0033, SSIM 0.995). For physically-based simulation acceleration, NeuBRDF requires only a quarter of the time compared to Monte Carlo simulation for layered BRDFs and approximately 1% of the time for volume rendering with microflake phase functions. The BTF compression experiment on UBO2014 demonstrates a compression ratio of thousands of times, reducing ~500 MB of raw measurements to ~226 KB (NeuBRDF ~95 KB + neural texture ~131 KB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuBRDF outperforms all analytical microfacet baselines (GGX, Low et al., Dupuy et al.) by a wide margin, with MAE roughly 10× lower than GGX and 4.7× lower than Low et al. Among factorization-based methods, NPF [1] is the strongest baseline (MAE 0.0056), but NeuBRDF still achieves a 3.3× lower MAE (0.0017) and higher SSIM (0.996 vs 0.990). Against the closest neural competitor NBRDF [33], NeuBRDF wins on all three metrics, though the margin is modest (MAE 0.0017 vs 0.0028; SSIM 0.996 vs 0.995). For simulation acceleration (Fig. 6), NeuBRDF substantially outperforms both NLBRDF [5] and Neural Biplane [6] in both rendering quality and speed, requiring only ~25% of the Monte Carlo simulation time for layered BRDFs and ~1% for volume rendering, while the neural baselines do not achieve comparable speedups. The codebook-based quantization (Table 4) also shows that NeuBRDF's approach (0.07 MB, MSE 0.0167) dominates low-bit quantization (0.40 MB, MSE 0.0189) in both memory and quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes HEALPix, a sphere discretization originally designed for astrophysical full-sky maps, as the underlying grid for BRDF feature representation, adapting it by placing features at grid corners rather than pixel centers to halve memory accesses. It extends the feature-grid + tiny MLP decoder paradigm from Instant-NGP (Müller et al.) from 3D spatial geometry to 4D bidirectional reflectance by factorizing the BRDF into two 3D hemisphere grids, and incorporates the vector-quantization codebook mechanism from Variable Bitrate Neural Fields (Takikawa et al.) to compress material-specific features into shared, low-cost integer indices. Unlike prior BRDF factorization methods (spherical harmonics, Zernike polynomials, NPF) that rely on linear or single non-linearity decompositions, the auto-decoder learning here captures complex non-linear relationships between the hemisphere factors, analogous to the distinction between PCA and auto-encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that measured and Monte Carlo-simulated BRDFs can be evaluated in real time (~4.5 ms at 1080p) with quality surpassing both analytical microfacet models and prior neural BRDF approaches, closing a long-standing gap between photorealistic offline rendering and interactive applications. The shared-codebook design enables a single set of neural reflectance primitives to serve multiple materials, making the approach practical for memory-constrained devices and large scenes. Open questions include whether the framework scales to even more complex effects (e.g., subsurface scattering, thin-film interference), how well it generalizes to materials outside the tested clusters, and whether the auto-encoder + K-Means clustering pipeline for primitive discovery can be made fully end-to-end.
