## Problem

Existing text-driven texture synthesis methods for 3D scenes either iteratively project 2D diffusion outputs onto mesh surfaces (suffering from seams, accumulated artifacts, and poor style consistency across objects) or optimize in a low-resolution latent space (producing blurry textures with incorrect geometric details). Neither approach delivers high-quality, style-coherent textures for entire indoor scenes from a single text prompt.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241745/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D indoor scene mesh (with per-instance UV parameterization) and a free-form text prompt as input. During optimization, the mesh is rasterized from 5,000 randomly sampled viewpoints to produce UV coordinates and depth maps; the final output is a high-resolution RGB texture (4096×4096) that can be queried at arbitrary resolution thanks to the implicit texture field representation. The diffusion prior operates on 96×96 latent features derived from 768×768 RGB renderings, with depth conditioning via ControlNet.

## Method

SceneTex formulates texture synthesis as an RGB-space optimization problem using a multiresolution texture field: a hash-encoded implicit representation that stores texture features at multiple scales to capture both low- and high-frequency details, decoded to RGB via a cross-attention decoder. The cross-attention decoder enforces per-instance style consistency by having each rendered UV coordinate attend (as Query) to 4096 pre-sampled reference UVs scattered across the same object (as Key/Value), implemented with Flash Attention v2. Optimization proceeds via Variational Score Distillation (VSD) with a frozen depth-conditioned ControlNet prior and a trainable LoRA module, using a time-annealing schedule (t~U(0.02,0.98) then t~U(0.02,0.50)) over 30,000 iterations. Unlike prior latent-space methods, SceneTex renders full 768×768 RGB images each iteration, avoiding the resolution mismatch between optimization target and final texture.

## Results

On 10 3D-FRONT scenes (2 prompts each), SceneTex achieves a CLIP score of 22.18 and Inception Score of 3.33, outperforming the strongest baseline Text2Tex (CLIP 20.83, IS 2.87) by 1.35 and 0.46 points respectively. In the user study (75 participants, 1–5 scale), SceneTex scores 4.40 on Visual Quality and 4.29 on Prompt Fidelity, substantially ahead of MVDiffusion (3.09 / 3.12) and Text2Tex (2.62 / 3.04). The full method with both multiresolution texture and cross-attention decoder yields the best results across all four metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneTex outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against Text2Tex [8] (the strongest baseline), SceneTex gains +1.35 CLIP, +0.46 IS, +1.78 VQ, and +1.25 PF, with the largest relative improvement in user-study scores where Text2Tex struggles with global style consistency across objects. Against MVDiffusion [63], the gains are even larger in CLIP (+3.71) and VQ (+1.31), reflecting MVDiffusion's tendency to produce blurry, dimmed textures that fail to capture prompt-specific traits like 'baroque' or 'luxury.' Latent-Paint [39] is the weakest baseline, suffering from over-saturation, hallucinated scene components, and a mismatch between its low-resolution latent optimization target and the final high-resolution texture, resulting in the lowest scores across all four metrics (CLIP 18.37, IS 1.96, VQ 1.57, PF 2.11).

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneTex departs from two dominant paradigms in text-driven 3D texturing: iterative 2D-view warping (Text2Tex, TEXTure) which accumulates seams and artifacts, and latent-space score distillation (Latent-Paint, DreamFusion) which produces blurry textures due to resolution mismatch. It builds directly on ProlificDreamer's VSD objective and time annealing, Müller et al.'s multiresolution hash encoding for the texture field, and ControlNet for depth-conditioned diffusion, but reframes the problem as RGB-space optimization over an implicit multiresolution texture field rather than a fixed-resolution latent map. The cross-attention decoder for per-instance style consistency is a novel architectural addition not present in prior SDS-based texturing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SceneTex addresses a practical gap: prior text-to-texture methods work well on individual objects but fail to produce globally style-consistent textures across an entire indoor scene, which is the actual unit of use in gaming, VR, and simulation. The multiresolution texture field with cross-attention decoder is a reusable architectural pattern that could transfer to other implicit-representation optimization tasks. However, the 20-hour per-scene optimization on an A6000, the limitation to indoor 3D-FRONT scenes, and the acknowledged shading artifacts in generated textures limit immediate broad adoption. Open questions include whether the approach scales to outdoor or large-scale scenes, whether the LoRA fine-tuning of the diffusion prior can be amortized across scenes, and whether the shading issue can be resolved without retraining the diffusion model.
