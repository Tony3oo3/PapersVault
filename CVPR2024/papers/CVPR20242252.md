## Problem

Video pose transformers (VPTs) achieve state-of-the-art 3D human pose estimation by processing long video sequences (243–351 frames), but their self-attention complexity grows quadratically with the number of tokens, making them impractical on resource-constrained devices. The paper identifies that maintaining full-length pose token sequences across all transformer blocks is computationally redundant, as adjacent frames and deep-block tokens exhibit high similarity, yet no prior work provides a unified, plug-and-play solution to reduce this redundancy while preserving estimation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242252/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D pose sequence of shape F×J×2 (e.g., 243 frames × 17 joints × 2 coordinates) detected by an off-the-shelf 2D HPE detector and produces either a full 3D pose sequence of shape F×J×3 (seq2seq pipeline) or the 3D pose of the center frame only (seq2frame pipeline). The framework is plug-and-play: it inserts a pruning module after a few initial transformer blocks and a recovering module after the last block, requiring no architectural redesign of the host VPT. For seq2frame, the center-frame token is explicitly concatenated with the pruned tokens to preserve its direct contribution to the output.

## Method

HoT follows an hourglass paradigm: the full-length token sequence is pruned to f representative tokens (f ≪ F) after the first n transformer blocks, processed through the remaining blocks at reduced cost, and then recovered back to full temporal resolution before the regression head. The Token Pruning Cluster (TPC) is a parameter-free module that applies spatial average pooling followed by DPC-kNN density peaks clustering to dynamically select f cluster centers as representative tokens, ensuring high semantic diversity while eliminating temporal redundancy. The Token Recovering Attention (TRA) is a single multi-head cross-attention layer that uses F learnable zero-initialized tokens as queries and the f representative tokens as keys and values, restoring the full-length sequence with negligible additional parameters (~0.35M). The framework accommodates both seq2seq (TPC + TRA) and seq2frame (TPC only, with center-frame token concatenation) inference pipelines, and the pruning depth n and token count f are tunable hyperparameters that trade off FLOPs against accuracy.

## Results

On Human3.6M, HoT w. MotionBERT reduces FLOPs by 51.8% (131.09G → 63.21G) while maintaining the same MPJPE of 39.8 mm, and TPC w. MotionBERT further improves accuracy to 39.0 mm (−0.2 mm) while cutting FLOPs by 30.3%. On MixSTE, HoT saves 39.6% FLOPs with only 0.1 mm MPJPE increase (40.9 → 41.0), while TPC w. MixSTE (n=7) actually improves MPJPE from 40.9 to 39.9 mm with a 9.4% FLOPs reduction. On MPI-INF-3DHP, HoT w. MixSTE achieves 53.2 mm MPJPE (vs. 54.9 mm for MixSTE) and 94.8% PCK (vs. 94.4%), demonstrating consistent gains in more challenging outdoor settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three VPTs the method is plugged into: TPC w. MotionBERT beats the original (39.0 vs. 39.2 mm MPJPE) while saving 30.3% FLOPs; HoT w. MotionBERT matches accuracy (39.8 mm) at 51.8% fewer FLOPs; TPC w. MixSTE improves MPJPE from 40.9 to 39.9 mm with 9.4% FLOPs savings, while HoT w. MixSTE trades 0.1 mm for 39.6% FLOPs reduction; TPC w. MHFormer matches the original 43.0 mm MPJPE at 41.9% fewer FLOPs. Against earlier SOTA on Human3.6M, all HoT/TPC variants outperform PoseFormer (44.3 mm), Strided (43.7 mm), P-STMO (42.8 mm), and STCFormer (40.5 mm) in MPJPE. On MPI-INF-3DHP, HoT w. MixSTE improves over MixSTE (53.2 vs. 54.9 mm MPJPE, 94.8% vs. 94.4% PCK) and TPC w. MHFormer is nearly on par with MHFormer (58.4 vs. 58.0 mm). All methods substantially outperform the older non-transformer baselines VPose (84.0 mm), UGCN (68.1 mm), and Anatomy3D (78.8 mm) on MPI-INF-3DHP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the video pose transformer (VPT) lineage (MHFormer, MixSTE, MotionBERT) and adapts token pruning concepts from vision transformers (DynamicViT, PPT, TCFormer) to the temporal domain of 3D HPE. It departs from prior efficient 3D HPE work (Strided, DeciWatch) by replacing static frame sampling or model-specific architectural changes with a plug-and-play, parameter-free clustering-based pruning (DPC-kNN) plus a lightweight cross-attention recovery module. The hourglass paradigm—prune early, recover late—is borrowed conceptually from stacked hourglass networks but applied to token sequences rather than spatial feature maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40–50% FLOPs reduction with negligible accuracy loss is practically significant for deploying VPTs on resource-constrained devices, and the plug-and-play design means the framework can be retrofitted onto future VPT architectures without redesign. A notable limitation is that the seq2frame variant (TPC only) consistently outperforms the full HoT (TPC+TRA), suggesting the cross-attention recovery step introduces information loss that a more expressive recovery mechanism might address. Open questions include whether the clustering-based selection generalizes to longer sequences (beyond 351 frames) or to other video understanding tasks, and whether the parameter-free DPC-kNN approach scales to higher-dimensional token spaces.
