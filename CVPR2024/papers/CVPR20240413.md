## Problem

Existing object recognition methods, including CLIP and open-vocabulary approaches, require a predefined gallery of object descriptions or labels before inference, which constrains the model to a fixed subset of the textual space and degrades performance as the gallery grows. This paper asks whether object recognition can be reformulated as pure next-token prediction, eliminating any predefined label set and allowing the model to generate arbitrary object labels directly from image embeddings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240413/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at 224×224 resolution, encoded by a CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone into 256 spatial token embeddings of dimension 1024 (the [CLS] token is dropped). It outputs a ranked list of object labels as short text fragments (nouns or noun phrases, not full sentences), each accompanied by a probability score computed as the product of its token probabilities. No predefined gallery, prompt template, or reference samples are needed at inference time.

## Method

The architecture pairs a pretrained CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder (last 6 blocks fine-tuned) with a truncated LLaMA 7B decoder (first 6 transformer blocks plus the final output layer, discarding 26 intermediate blocks for 4.5× speedup). The key architectural innovation is a custom non-causal attention mask that (a) treats image tokens as a spatially correlated prefix visible to all subsequent tokens, and (b) decouples tokens belonging to different labels at the [SEP] delimiter so that each label is generated independently given the image context. This masking enables one-shot sampling: instead of sequential token-by-token decoding, the model simultaneously samples the first token for k candidate labels in parallel, then continues sampling only for unfinished labels until [SEP] is reached, yielding k ranked labels in a single forward pass. Training uses weakly supervised labels (nouns parsed from image captions) with cross-entropy loss, jointly optimizing the encoder's last 6 blocks, a linear projection, the [IMG] token embedding, and the entire truncated decoder.

## Results

On top-10 predictions, the proposed method (G70M, 70M training pairs) achieves F1 scores of 0.593 (CC3M), 0.758 (COCO), and 0.603 (OpenImages), with the G3M variant reaching 0.611 on CC3M. The method's primary advantage is in Recall: it achieves R=0.722 (CC3M), 0.765 (COCO), and 0.663 (OpenImages) with G70M, substantially outperforming all baselines in generating diverse, semantically relevant labels. The truncated 1.78B decoder (6 transformer blocks from LLaMA 7B) matches the full 7B model's performance while being 4.5× faster in inference, and combined with one-shot sampling achieves an 18.1× speedup over the full model with greedy search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all baselines on Recall across all three datasets, with the G3M model achieving R=0.738 on CC3M versus the best baseline InstructBLIP at R=0.596, and the G70M model reaching R=0.765 on COCO versus InstructBLIP's R=0.690. On F1, the G3M model leads on CC3M (0.611 vs. InstructBLIP's 0.567) and the G70M model leads on COCO (0.758 vs. InstructBLIP's 0.686), but InstructBLIP edges out the G70M model on OpenImages F1 (0.615 vs. 0.603). Against contrastive/retrieval baselines, the gap is larger: CLIP with its base gallery achieves only F1=0.499 on CC3M and 0.480 on OpenImages, while CaSED with its extended gallery reaches F1=0.548 and 0.519 respectively. Among VQA-oriented models (Flamingo, LLaVA, BLIP-2, InstructBLIP), the paper notes they saturate at top-5 predictions and cannot generate diverse labels consistently, explaining their lower Recall despite competitive Precision. The method uses only 1.78B parameters (vs. 8–13B for competing VLMs) and requires no predefined label gallery or few-shot prompts, while training on image-caption pairs rather than question-answer triplets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP's image-text alignment (using its ViT-L/14 encoder) and LLaMA's token embeddings as the vocabulary space, but departs fundamentally from both the contrastive paradigm (which requires a predefined gallery of object descriptions) and the VQA/chat paradigm (Flamingo, LLaVA, BLIP-2) that decodes full sentences sequentially with few-shot prompts. The key conceptual departure is reframing object recognition as next-token prediction over a non-causal attention mask that treats image tokens as a prefix and decouples tokens from different labels, enabling parallel one-shot sampling of multiple labels. The truncated decoder strategy—retaining only the first 6 of 32 LLaMA blocks—draws on the observation that initial transformer blocks are better suited to adapting to a novel input domain (image embeddings), a finding consistent with partial fine-tuning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for any predefined object gallery or reference prompts, this approach moves toward a truly open-world recognition paradigm where the model can in principle generate any label in the 32K-token vocabulary, addressing a core limitation of CLIP-based methods where gallery size and composition directly constrain performance. The one-shot sampling and truncated decoder together yield an 18.1× inference speedup over the full model with greedy search, making autoregressive recognition practically deployable. Open questions include the competition issue when multiple plausible labels share an initial token, whether the weak supervision from caption-extracted nouns limits precision on fine-grained categories, and whether the approach extends naturally to structured tasks like detection or segmentation without the spatial grounding that VQA models provide.
