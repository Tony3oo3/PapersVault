## Problem

Existing CLIP-based compositional zero-shot learning methods construct trainable prompts only for composed state-object pairs, ignoring explicit modeling of individual primitives. This single-path paradigm fails to fully exploit pre-trained cross-modal knowledge (much of which is tied to single primitives rather than compositions) and makes generalization to unseen compositions harder due to over-reliance on a limited set of seen pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242525/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder (with lightweight Adapter modules) that produces a [CLS] token and a sequence of patch tokens. The output is a predicted state-object composition label, obtained by taking the argmax over an integrated probability that combines the direct composition-branch score with the product of independent state and object branch scores, evaluated against all candidate compositions in the target set (seen+unseen in closed-world, all permutations in open-world).

## Method

Troika implements a Multi-Path paradigm with three parallel recognition branches (state, object, composition), each using a branch-specific learnable prompt prefix while sharing a common primitive vocabulary, so that each branch independently activates the CLIP text encoder to elicit role-specific pre-trained knowledge. On the vision side, Adapter modules provide parameter-efficient tuning of the image encoder, and two MLP-based disentanglers decompose the global [CLS] representation into state-specific and object-specific visual features for cross-modal alignment on each branch. A Cross-Modal Traction module, shared across all three branches, uses multi-head attention to let each prompt representation attend to projected patch tokens, adaptively pulling the static prompt embedding toward semantically relevant visual content via a learnable per-dimension scaling vector. Training is multi-task: weighted cross-entropy losses are applied simultaneously on all three branches, and at inference the final composition probability is the sum of the direct composition score and the product of state and object scores, mitigating bias toward seen compositions.

## Results

On the closed-world setting, Troika achieves HM of 39.3, 54.6, and 29.4 on MIT-States, UT-Zappos, and C-GQA respectively, exceeding the previous SOTA (DFSP) by +2.0%, +7.4%, and +2.3% in HM and by +1.5%, +5.7%, and +1.9% in AUC. On the more challenging open-world setting, Troika still leads with HM of 20.1, 47.8, and 10.9, surpassing the best CLIP-based method by up to +3.8% HM and +2.7% AUC. All results use a CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone, and Troika reports averages over 5 random seeds with standard errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Troika outperforms all five CLIP-based baselines on every dataset in both closed-world and open-world settings. Against the strongest prior method DFSP(t2i), Troika gains +2.0% HM on MIT-States (39.3 vs. 37.3), +7.4% HM on UT-Zappos (54.6 vs. 47.2), and +2.3% HM on C-GQA (29.4 vs. 27.1) in closed-world; in open-world the margins are +0.8%, +3.8%, and +0.5% HM respectively. CSP and PromptCompVL trail Troika by 3–9% in HM across datasets, while the unmodified CLIP and CoOp baselines are substantially weaker (e.g., CoOp achieves only 34.6 HM on UT-Zappos vs. Troika's 54.6). The only metric where Troika does not lead is open-world seen accuracy on MIT-States (48.8 vs. DFSP's 47.5 is actually a win; the paper notes a 0.4% gap on one specific seen-accuracy sub-metric), confirming Troika's consistent dominance across the full evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the single-path paradigm used by prior CLIP-based CZSL methods (CSP, PromptCompVL, DFSP), which construct one cross-modal alignment for the full state-object composition. The key conceptual shift is the Multi-Path paradigm, which mandates three independent recognition branches (state, object, composition) with branch-specific prompt prefixes and decomposed visual features, thereby explicitly modeling each semantic primitive rather than relying solely on the joint composition. On the vision side, it extends the text-only prompt tuning tradition by introducing Adapter modules for parameter-efficient image encoder adaptation, and adds a Cross-Modal Traction module (attention-based) that adaptively shifts prompt representations toward relevant patch features — a mechanism absent from prior CZSL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Multi-Path paradigm is framed as a general, implementation-agnostic framework that future CZSL methods can instantiate with different VLMs or feature extraction strategies, potentially accelerating the field beyond incremental prompt-design tweaks. The Cross-Modal Traction module is explicitly decoupled from Troika, making it a plug-and-play component applicable to other prompt-based methods. Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes to more complex compositional structures (e.g., multi-attribute or spatial relations), whether the shared-vocabulary constraint is truly necessary at scale, and whether the approach transfers to larger or newer VLMs beyond CLIP ViT-L/14.
