## Problem

Existing unified frameworks for Video Moment Retrieval (MR) and Highlight Detection (HD) treat both tasks with generic transformer designs, ignoring their fundamentally different emphases: MR requires precise perception of local temporal relationships for boundary localization, while HD demands holistic understanding of global video context for saliency estimation. This lack of task-specific design leads to inconsistent performance across the two tasks, making it difficult to achieve precise moment localization and accurate highlight scoring simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240833/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted visual features (L×D from SlowFast, CLIP, or I3D) and CLIP text embeddings (N×D) for a video-text pair, with optional audio features from PANN. For MR, it outputs a single moment span parameterized by center coordinate and duration; for HD, it produces a per-clip saliency score vector of length L. The two outputs are generated by separate heads (a transformer decoder for MR, a bilinear projection for HD) sharing the same integrated visual representation from the CIM module.

## Method

UVCOM's core is a Comprehensive Integration Module (CIM) that performs progressive multi-granularity integration in three stages: (1) Dual-branch EM Attention with RBF kernels and learnable GMM centroids aggregates clip→moment and word→phrase features; (2) a Local Relation Perception module combines 1D temporal convolution with a Bidirectional Modality Random Walk (BMRW) algorithm that iteratively propagates text priors into visual features in a shared embedding space, solved in closed form via Neumann Series to avoid unbounded iteration; (3) Global Knowledge Accumulation uses the most text-relevant moment snippet as an intermediary query to a transformer encoder, producing a video-level global feature while enriching clip-level features. Training is supervised by L1+GIoU losses for MR, margin-ranking+rank-aware losses for HD, hard-sample suppression, and a multi-aspect contrastive objective (clip-text alignment + video-linguistic discrimination) that explicitly consolidates local and global representations.

## Results

On QVHighlights (the primary joint MR+HD benchmark), UVCOM† achieves 48.70% R1@0.7 and 44.01% mAP@0.75 for Moment Retrieval, and 39.79% mAP / 64.79% HIT@1 for Highlight Detection, setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics. On TACoS, UVCOM reaches 23.32% R1@0.7, a +5.97% improvement over UniVTG. On YouTube Highlights, UVCOM attains 77.4% Avg. mAP (without audio), surpassing the previous best (UniVTG at 75.2%). On TVSum, UVCOM achieves 86.3% Avg. Top-5 mAP, outperforming QD-DETR (85.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UVCOM outperforms all 17 baselines across all five benchmarks. On QVHighlights, UVCOM† beats the strongest prior method QD-DETR† by +3.6% R1@0.7 (48.70 vs 45.10), +3.91% mAP@0.75 (44.01 vs 40.10), and +1.92% HIT@1 (64.79 vs 62.87) for HD. Compared to UniVTG, UVCOM gains +7.84% R1@0.7 on TACoS (23.32 vs 17.35) and +0.99% R1@0.7 on Charades-STA (36.64 vs 35.65). On YouTube Highlights, UVCOM2 surpasses UniVTG by +2.2% Avg. mAP (77.4 vs 75.2), and on TVSum, UVCOM exceeds QD-DETR by +1.3% Avg. Top-5 mAP (86.3 vs 85.0). Notably, EaTR [18] achieves competitive MR R1@0.7 (45.79) but lags significantly on HD (37.15 mAP vs UVCOM's 39.74), illustrating the gap that task-specific design addresses. The ablation in Table 5 confirms that both the Comprehensive Integration Module and Multi-Aspect Contrastive Learning contribute additively, with their combination yielding +5.71% Avg. mAP over the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified MR+HD framework lineage initiated by MomentDETR (Lei et al., 2021), which first proposed the QVHighlights benchmark and a DETR-based joint architecture, and subsequently refined by UMT (Liu et al., 2022) with audio modality and QD-DETR (Moon et al., 2023) with query-dependent representations. The key conceptual departure is the observation that prior unified frameworks treat both tasks identically via generic transformer cross-attention, ignoring that MR requires local temporal relation perception while HD demands global saliency distribution fitting. UVCOM draws on EM Attention (Li et al., ICCV 2019) and RBF-kernel GMM formulations (Huang et al., CVPR 2022) for intra-modality aggregation, and adapts random walk theory (Grady, 2006; Nikolentzos & Vazirgiannis, 2020) into the Bidirectional Modality Random Walk (BMRW) algorithm for inter-modality interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local-vs-global design principle for jointly solving MR and HD is well-motivated and validated through the grounding consistency analysis (Fig. 6), which shows that accurate highlight estimation and precise moment localization reinforce each other under UVCOM's task-specific architecture. The BMRW algorithm offers a principled alternative to cross-attention that mitigates attention drift in untrimmed videos, a practical concern for real-world deployment. However, the framework relies on pre-extracted features (SlowFast, CLIP, PANN) and handles audio modality only superficially, leaving open whether end-to-end training or dedicated audio-visual fusion could further close the gap. The task-specific design principle could potentially generalize to other multi-task video understanding settings where subtasks have different spatial/temporal emphasis.
