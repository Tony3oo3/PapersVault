## Problem

Category-agnostic pose estimation (CAPE) requires a unified model to predict poses for any object category from only a few annotated samples. Existing approaches either compute keypoint prototypes directly from sparse support samples (causing overfitting) or rely on support keypoint identifiers to disambiguate similar-looking keypoints, which is impractical in open-world settings where keypoints are freely defined.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240189/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image of a novel category and K support images (1 or 5) with 2D keypoint annotations from the same category. Images are cropped to 256×256 using ground-truth bounding boxes and processed by a frozen ResNet-50 feature extractor. The output is a set of T_y 2D keypoint coordinates per query image, obtained by computing keypoint prototypes in feature space and predicting heatmaps via inner products with dense image features. No keypoint ordering or identifier information is required.

## Method

ESCAPE introduces a Bayesian prior over keypoint features expressed as a mixture of 'super-keypoints'—abstract representations discovered by density peaks clustering on keypoint distribution means from base categories, where each super-keypoint encodes the statistics of semantically related keypoints across categories (e.g., 'left knee' of deer, antelope, zebra). During adaptation to a novel category, an EM algorithm is used: the expectation step matches each novel keypoint to a super-keypoint via a learnable matching network (inspired by SuperGlue) that processes augmented keypoint features (visual appearance + geometric position encoding) and super-keypoint features (learnable vectors + index encoding) through self-attention and cross-attention blocks, then resolves the bipartite assignment with differentiable Sinkhorn optimal transport. The maximization step computes each keypoint prototype as a MAP estimate that is a convex combination of the support-sample feature mean and the matched super-keypoint representation, with the mixing weight determined by the ratio of within-keypoint to between-keypoint variances. This design explicitly transfers knowledge from abundant base categories to novel ones, preventing prototypes from overfitting to the sparse support set.

## Results

On the MP-100 benchmark, ESCAPE achieves a mean PCK@0.2 of 82.74 under the 1-shot setting and 87.63 under the 5-shot setting, establishing new state-of-the-art results. In the 1-shot setting, ESCAPE outperforms the previous best method CapeFormer (80.75) by approximately 2.0 points and POMNet (79.70) by over 3 points. In the 5-shot setting, ESCAPE edges out CapeFormer (87.19) by 0.44 points while maintaining a substantial lead over POMNet (80.71) by nearly 7 points. All comparisons are conducted without support keypoint identifiers, making the results directly applicable to open-world scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESCAPE outperforms all five baselines on both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. Against the most competitive baseline CapeFormer (evaluated without its keypoint identifier), ESCAPE gains +1.99 PCK in 1-shot (82.74 vs. 80.75) and +0.44 PCK in 5-shot (87.63 vs. 87.19). Against POMNet, the original CAPE formulation, ESCAPE leads by +3.04 PCK in 1-shot and +6.92 PCK in 5-shot, indicating that the super-keypoint prior is especially valuable when support samples are scarce. The generic few-shot baselines (ProtoNet, MAML, Finetune) are substantially weaker, with mean PCK below 65 in both settings, confirming that task-specific pose estimation methods are necessary. Notably, ESCAPE achieves these gains without relying on support keypoint identifiers, whereas CapeFormer's original design depends on them to resolve keypoint ambiguity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the category-agnostic pose estimation (CAPE) task introduced by Xu et al. (POMNet) and Shi et al. (CapeFormer) by replacing their reliance on local support-query matching with a Bayesian prior over keypoint features. The super-keypoint concept draws from hierarchical Bayesian few-shot learning (Salakhutdinov et al.'s super-categories, Zhang et al.'s Bayesian meta-learning), repurposing the idea of higher-level abstract distributions to group semantically related keypoints across categories. The learnable matching network is directly inspired by SuperGlue's attention-based feature matching, adapted here to align novel keypoints with discovered super-keypoints via differentiable optimal transport (Sinkhorn). Unlike CapeFormer, ESCAPE explicitly avoids support keypoint identifiers, positioning itself as more practical for open-world settings where keypoint ordering is unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ESCAPE addresses a practical limitation of prior CAPE methods: overfitting to sparse support samples and dependence on keypoint identifiers that are often unavailable in real-world scenarios. The model-agnostic design (demonstrated with DINOv2 and DIFT backbones) suggests the super-keypoint prior could be a drop-in improvement for any feature-based pose estimation pipeline. Open questions include whether the Gaussian distributional assumption holds for highly diverse keypoints (e.g., facial landmarks vs. vehicle wheels), whether the density-peaks clustering step scales gracefully to thousands of categories, and whether the benefit persists when support sets grow beyond 5 samples. The modest but consistent gains over CapeFormer (especially in 1-shot) indicate the prior is most valuable precisely when data is scarcest.
