## Problem

Deploying pre-trained Vision Transformers in Federated Learning with heterogeneous client data is challenging: Generalized FL methods learn a single global model that generalizes well but cannot adapt to local distributions, while Personalized FL methods customize per-client models that overfit locally and fail to generalize to out-of-federation data. No existing prompt-tuning-based FL method simultaneously achieves strong global generalization and local personalization under heavy label or feature heterogeneity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242274/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 224×224 and processes them through a frozen ImageNet-21K pre-trained ViT-B-16 backbone, training only a small set of learnable prompts (length 1) and a linear classifier (~0.1M parameters total vs. 86M for full fine-tuning). At inference, a prompt selection module computes cosine similarity between the CLS token feature (last layer for label heterogeneity, 5th layer for feature heterogeneity) and learnable group keys to automatically route each sample to the appropriate group prompt, enabling sample-level adaptation without any local fine-tuning. The output is a standard class label prediction.

## Method

SGPT introduces a dual-prompt architecture: shared prompts inserted in the first Transformer layer capture common low-level representations across all data, while G group-specific prompts inserted in higher layers (4th–6th) extract specialized features. A prompt selection module assigns each input to a group via cosine similarity between its CLS token and learnable keys, with an anti-collapse mechanism (selection-probability weighting) and server-side momentum aggregation to ensure stable, meaningful clustering. Training uses Block Coordinate Descent: Block I first optimizes shared prompts and the classifier to learn common knowledge, then Block II freezes the shared prompts and jointly optimizes group prompts, keys, and classifier to learn residual group-specific knowledge. Theoretically, the paper proves a generalization bound decomposing the global-local performance gap into a generalization term (reduced by shared prompts maximizing effective sample size) and a distribution discrepancy term (reduced by group prompts aligning local and global group distributions).

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with high label heterogeneity (s=10), SGPT achieves 84.64% global accuracy and 73.85% worst local accuracy, outperforming the strongest baselines FedPR (81.42% global, 63.93% worst local) and pFedPG (81.19% global, 70.53% worst local) by 3.2% and 9.9% respectively. On the Five-Dataset benchmark, SGPT reaches 83.40% global and 61.04% worst local accuracy, surpassing pFedPG (72.12% global, 56.24% worst local) by over 11% in global accuracy. For feature heterogeneity, SGPT attains 97.72% average accuracy on Office-Caltech10 and 80.48% on DomainNet, exceeding the next-best FedPR (96.76% and 78.88%) by 0.96% and 1.6% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGPT outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest GFL baseline FedPR [13], SGPT gains +3.2% global and +9.9% worst local accuracy on CIFAR-100 (s=10), and +0.96%/+1.6% average accuracy on Office-Caltech10/DomainNet. Against the strongest PFL baseline pFedPG [56], SGPT improves global accuracy by +3.5% on CIFAR-100 (s=10) and +11.3% on Five-Dataset, while also achieving +3.3% worst local on CIFAR-100. The prompt-tuning baselines FedVPT/FedVPT-D [19] and FedMix [58] trail SGPT by 3.8–5.2% in global accuracy on CIFAR-100 (s=10), with FedVPT-D showing the largest degradation under high heterogeneity (79.49% vs. 85.85% at s=50). FedEM [36] and Head-Tune [47] are consistently the weakest, with Head-Tune achieving only 55.53% worst local on CIFAR-100 (s=10) and 28.60% on Five-Dataset. On feature heterogeneity, Per-FedAvg [12] and FedRep [6] lag SGPT by 2.25% and 2.82% on Office-Caltech10, and by 14.04% and 17.11% on DomainNet respectively, highlighting SGPT's advantage under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGPT builds directly on Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) [19] for efficient ViT adaptation and borrows the centroid-based clustering idea [54] for its prompt selection module. It positions itself at the intersection of Generalized FL (FedAvg, FedPR) and Personalized FL (pFedPG, FedEM), arguing that neither paradigm alone suffices under heavy data heterogeneity. The key conceptual departure is the joint use of shared prompts (for common features in early layers) and group-specific prompts (for specialized features in later layers) with a learned selection function, optimized via a block-coordinate-descent schedule that learns common knowledge before specialized knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bridging GFL and PFL within a single global model that auto-adapts at inference via prompt selection, SGPT offers a practical path to deploying pre-trained ViTs in federated settings with only 0.1M trainable parameters (vs. 86M for full fine-tuning), substantially reducing communication cost. The theoretical bound (Theorem 4.1) identifying generalization and distribution-discrepancy terms gives a principled justification for the shared/group prompt split. Open questions include scalability to larger-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet-scale FL), the moderate clustering accuracy (~56%) limiting how well the selection module separates groups, and whether the BCD ordering and layer-insertion heuristics generalize beyond the tested architectures and heterogeneity patterns.
