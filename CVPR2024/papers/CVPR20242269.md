## Problem

Existing infrared-visible image fusion and semantic segmentation methods suffer from three interrelated gaps: (1) unsupervised fusion losses with fixed proportions cause feature neutralization and discard low-light details; (2) conventional pooling-based attention misassigns feature validity scores, and cross-attention alone fails to exploit single-modal self-reinforcement; (3) fusion and segmentation are treated as isolated or strictly sequential tasks, ignoring their intrinsic mutual benefit. This paper addresses all three by proposing a coupled framework where the two tasks reinforce each other.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of co-registered infrared and visible images (480×640 on MFNet, 720×1280 on PST900, 600×800 on FMB) and simultaneously produces a fused RGB image and a pixel-level semantic segmentation mask. The fused image is generated by a CNN head that internally applies data augmentation (gamma transformation, contrast stretching) to the visible and infrared inputs to construct positive samples guiding weakened-information recovery. The segmentation mask is produced by a lightweight MLP decoder (from SegFormer) operating on the globally aggregated features from the transformer branch.

## Method

MRFS cascades two novel attention modules across n encoder blocks to couple fusion and segmentation. The CNN-based IGM-Att extends CBAM-style channel-spatial attention with a trainable gating mechanism: after computing hybrid attention weights via max/average pooling, an MLP over flattened features produces correction scores that multiplicatively redistribute importance, fixing the mismatch where pooling suppresses positive features surrounded by negatives. The transformer-based PC-Att then performs a closed-loop of single-modal self-reinforcement (self-attention within each modality) and cross-modal mutual complementarity (cross-attention between modalities), cycling through self→cross→self→cross to aggregate complete semantic information. The image fusion head enforces salient information integration via a max-function loss in both intensity and gradient domains, plus a color-consistency loss in YCbCr space, while the segmentation head uses standard cross-entropy. Joint multi-task training over 500 epochs with Adam (lr=6e-5, batch=3) allows vision-related features (restored textures, thermal contrast) to guide segmentation and semantics-related features (object boundaries) to improve fused image quality.

## Results

On semantic segmentation, MRFS achieves the highest mIoU across all three datasets: 59.1 on MFNet (vs. 58.9 for EAEFNet), 86.9 on PST900 (vs. 85.6 for EAEFNet), and 61.2 on FMB (vs. 58.5 for SegMiF). For image fusion, MRFS attains the highest entropy (EN) and standard deviation (SD) scores on most metrics on both MFNet and FMB, indicating superior information preservation and contrast. In the object detection verification on LLVIP, MRFS achieves mAP@0.75 of 92.9 and mAP@[0.5:0.95] of 74.2, outperforming all other fusion methods except DetFusion which is detection-driven by design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRFS outperforms all 15 baselines on semantic segmentation across all three datasets. On MFNet, it edges out EAEFNet (59.1 vs. 58.9 mIoU) and MMSMCNet (59.1 vs. 58.1); on PST900, it surpasses EAEFNet by 1.3 points (86.9 vs. 85.6) and LASNet by 2.5 points; on FMB, it leads SegMiF by 2.7 points (61.2 vs. 58.5) and EAEFNet by 3.2 points. For image fusion, MRFS achieves the highest EN and SD on most metrics, with the closest competitor being CDDFuse on some individual scores. In the object detection verification (Table 7), MRFS ties DATFuse on mAP@0.5 (99.3) but clearly leads on mAP@0.75 (92.9 vs. 91.4 for CDDFuse) and mAP@[0.5:0.95] (74.2, tied with DetFusion). The plug-and-play experiment further shows that transplanting IGM-Att and PC-Att into LASNet and SGFNet yields +2.4 and +1.7 mIoU gains respectively, confirming the modules' generality beyond the full MRFS framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CBAM-style channel-and-spatial attention paradigm by adding a trainable gating mechanism (IGM-Att) to correct feature validity score mismatches, and introduces a transformer-based progressive cycle attention (PC-Att) that explicitly combines self-attention and cross-attention in a closed loop for multi-modal feature aggregation. It departs from prior coupled fusion-segmentation methods (SeAFusion, SegMiF) that treat image fusion as a sequential precursor to segmentation, instead proposing a truly mutual reinforcement architecture where both tasks provide learning priors to each other through shared attention modules. The encoder backbone follows SegFormer's transformer design, and the fusion head incorporates data-augmentation-guided positive samples for weakened information recovery, a strategy distinct from the fixed-proportion unsupervised losses common in prior fusion work (e.g., PMGI, GANMcC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play demonstration of IGM-Att and PC-Att on LASNet and SGFNet (gains of +2.4 and +1.7 mIoU) suggests these attention modules have broader utility beyond the full MRFS framework, which is practically valuable for the RGB-T segmentation community. However, the method's complexity (219.16 FLOPs, 134.97M params) is substantially higher than lightweight fusion baselines like DATFuse (8.68 FLOPs) or SDNet (64.55 FLOPs), limiting real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the mutual reinforcement effect generalizes to other task pairs (e.g., fusion + detection, fusion + tracking), whether the coupling provides diminishing returns at scale, and whether the modest SOTA margins (e.g., 59.1 vs. 58.9 mIoU on MFNet) persist under different training protocols or dataset splits.
