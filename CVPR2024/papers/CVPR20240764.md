## Problem

Prior egocentric conversation research focuses exclusively on behaviors involving the camera wearer (e.g., who is talking to me, who am I listening to), leaving the exocentric interactions among other social partners entirely unexplored. This paper introduces the Ego-Exocentric Conversational Graph Prediction problem: jointly inferring both egocentric and exocentric speaking/listening relationships for all participants in a multi-person conversation from a single egocentric video stream.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240764/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes egocentric video frames (with pre-annotated head bounding boxes for up to 4 visible social partners, zero-padded when fewer are present), a 6-microphone multi-channel audio signal (channel correlation features plus real/complex spectrogram), and binary positional masks indicating each head's location in the frame. The output is a per-frame dynamic directed graph with 10 edges (4 egocentric + 6 exocentric), each annotated with two binary attributes—Speaking To and Listening To—yielding 8 independent binary classification targets per frame.

## Method

AV-CONV encodes cropped head images and positional-mask-conditioned multi-channel audio through separate ResNet-18 backbones (ImageNet-pretrained), concatenating the resulting features into a unified audio-visual representation per subject. The core contribution is a Conversational Attention module that applies three sequential self-attention operations over flattened spatiotemporal tokens: Cross-Time attention aggregates information across neighboring frames, Cross-Subject attention lets each person's features attend to all others at matching spatiotemporal locations, and Global-Local attention correlates scene-wide audio-positional tokens with local head-appearance tokens. For egocentric edge predictions, individual-specific features feed directly into classifiers; for exocentric edges, pairwise concatenation of two subjects' features is used. Eight binary classifiers (four per graph component) are trained jointly with cross-entropy loss, and the full model is trained for ~9 epochs with Adam at lr=1e-4.

## Results

On the Egocentric Concurrent Conversations Dataset, AV-CONV achieves an average accuracy of 86.15% on egocentric edge attributes and 81.04% on exocentric edge attributes, yielding an overall Ego-Exo average of 83.51%. In terms of mAP, the model reaches 59.37% overall, substantially outperforming the ASL+Layout heuristic baseline (41.40%). The best individual egocentric accuracy is 90.63% on e_{p_i→c}^L (partner listening to camera wearer), while the weakest exocentric task is e_{p_i→p_j}^L at 75.91% accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AV-CONV is compared against two baselines adapted from prior work: SAAL [Ryan et al., CVPR 2023] and ASL+Layout [Jiang et al., CVPR 2022]. Against SAAL, AV-CONV outperforms by an average of ~4.45% on egocentric accuracy (+3.99% on e_{c→p_i}^S, +8.84% on e_{p_i→c}^S, +1.32% on e_{c→p_i}^L, +3.64% on e_{p_i→c}^L), with the smallest margin on the listening-to-camera-wearer task where SAAL was specifically designed. SAAL does not produce exocentric predictions, so no direct comparison is possible there. Against the ASL+Layout heuristic baseline, AV-CONV wins by a large margin on both egocentric (83.51% vs. 60.40% overall accuracy) and exocentric tasks, demonstrating that 3D layout estimation combined with active speaker detection is insufficient for this task. On mAP, AV-CONV also leads ASL+Layout by 17.97 points overall (59.37% vs. 41.40%), though ASL+Layout slightly edges out AV-CONV on the single e_{c→p_i}^S mAP metric (86.28 vs. 82.08).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the egocentric social interaction line (Ego4D Talking-to-Me, SAAL, ASL) by unifying multiple previously separate sub-tasks—active speaker detection, auditory attention localization, and listening behavior—into a single graph-prediction framework. It departs from prior exocentric conversation-group detection work (F-formations, surveillance-camera studies) by operating entirely from a first-person viewpoint and predicting pairwise directed edges rather than group-level labels. The self-attention design draws on space-time attention for video understanding [1], but the three-way decomposition (cross-time, cross-subject, global-local) is tailored specifically to the multi-participant conversational setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing the Ego-Exocentric Conversational Graph Prediction problem, the paper opens a new research direction: inferring how people interact with *each other* (not just with the camera wearer) from a single egocentric stream, a capability relevant to AR/VR social agents and Theory-of-Mind modeling. Key limitations include the small dataset (~20 hours, max 4 partners per frame), the reliance on pre-computed head bounding boxes, and the observation that exocentric predictions (81.04%) lag significantly behind egocentric ones (86.15%), suggesting the exocentric sub-problem remains substantially harder. The authors note that better high-resolution spatial audio representations and extensions to gaze prediction are natural next steps.
