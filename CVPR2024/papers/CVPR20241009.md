## Problem

Existing few-shot segmentation methods degrade significantly under domain shift, and conventional domain generalization approaches that adapt the entire model to new domains suffer from overfitting in the few-shot regime where training data is scarce. The paper identifies that directly generalizing a large model to unseen domain styles is suboptimal when only a handful of annotated samples are available in the target domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241009/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes K support images and one query image (resized to 400x400) from an unseen target domain, and produces a pixel-wise binary segmentation mask for the query. Crucially, the adapter does not operate on raw pixels but on per-channel mean and variance statistics extracted from the first three ResNet-50 layers; it predicts two scalar rectification vectors (alpha_rect, beta_rect) that rescale these statistics to restore source-domain style before the frozen segmentation head computes the mask.

## Method

The core idea is to train a lightweight adapter that rectifies target-domain feature channel statistics back to the source domain, decoupling domain adaptation from the segmentation model. To generate diverse synthetic target domains during training, the paper proposes a local-global style perturbation: local perturbation injects Gaussian noise into per-image channel statistics, while global perturbation uses momentum-updated dataset-level statistics to enable larger, more stable noise magnitudes. The adapter is supervised by a cyclic domain alignment loss that perturbs the rectified features again and enforces that the second rectification recovers the original statistics, preventing the adapter from mapping into an arbitrary intermediate space. At inference, target-domain features are treated as perturbed features, rectified by the adapter, and fed into the well-trained SSP (ResNet-50) segmentation model without any further adaptation.

## Results

On the CD-FSS benchmark (1-way 1-shot), DR-Adapter achieves an average mIoU of 60.86, outperforming the best cross-domain few-shot baseline PATNet (56.06) by 4.80 points and the best standard FSS baseline SSP (57.20) by 3.66 points. The largest gains appear on Chest X-ray (82.35 vs. PATNet's 66.61, +15.74) and DeepGlobe (41.29 vs. 37.89, +3.40). In the 5-shot setting, the average mIoU reaches 65.42, again surpassing PATNet (61.99) and SSP (63.92). On the SUIM benchmark (following RD's protocol), the method achieves 40.25 average mIoU versus RD's 34.70.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary cross-domain few-shot baseline PATNet, DR-Adapter wins on average mIoU in both 1-shot (60.86 vs. 56.06) and 5-shot (65.42 vs. 61.99) settings, with the most dramatic margin on Chest X-ray (+15.74 in 1-shot) and DeepGlobe (+3.40 in 1-shot). However, PATNet retains a slight edge on ISIC (41.16 vs. 40.77 in 1-shot; 53.58 vs. 48.87 in 5-shot) and FSS-1000 5-shot (81.23 vs. 80.40), where the domain gap is smaller. Compared to the best standard FSS method SSP, DR-Adapter improves average 1-shot mIoU by 3.66 points, with the largest gain again on Chest X-ray (+8.12). Against domain generalization baselines (NP, DSU, MixStyle, AdaIN), DR-Adapter leads by 2.08–3.53 points in average 1-shot mIoU, confirming that the rectification adapter outperforms perturbation-only strategies. On the SUIM benchmark, DR-Adapter (40.25) surpasses RD (34.70) by 5.55 points. Finally, against the SAM-based PerSAM, DR-Adapter achieves 60.86 vs. 41.35 average mIoU, a 19.51-point advantage, highlighting the difficulty of cross-domain transfer for prompt-based segmentation models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SSP few-shot segmentation framework and extends the feature channel statistics perturbation paradigm established by MixStyle, DSU, and NP. It departs from prior cross-domain few-shot segmentation methods (PATNet, RestNet, RD) that adapt the entire model to target domains, instead training only a lightweight adapter that rectifies target domain features back to the source domain space. The local-global perturbation strategy and cyclic domain alignment loss are novel contributions that go beyond the single-scale perturbation used in existing domain generalization approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adapter-based approach offers a practical and computationally efficient solution to cross-domain few-shot segmentation, decoupling domain adaptation from the expensive retraining of the full model. The method's reliance on feature channel statistics as a sufficient domain descriptor remains an open question—more complex domain shifts (e.g., structural or semantic changes) may not be fully captured. The modest gains when extending to ViT backbones (FPTrans) suggest the approach may benefit from further architectural adaptation, and the perturbation is currently limited to the first three ResNet layers, leaving room for exploring deeper or more selective application.
