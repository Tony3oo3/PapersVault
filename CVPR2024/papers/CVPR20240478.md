## Problem

Existing vision-language models that cast detection as object-word grounding are computationally infeasible for large-scale prompting (thousands of categories or descriptions) due to bidirectional encoder token limits and heavy cross-modality attention costs. Separately, segmentation models suffer from a granularity mismatch between foreground things (instance-level) and background stuff (class-level), forcing manual category routing and decoupled learning pipelines that cannot handle class-agnostic data like SA-1B.

## Input / Output

APE takes an RGB image and a set of independent text prompts—ranging from single-word vocabularies (e.g., "Girl", "Sky") to full sentence descriptions (e.g., "Little child with hat on branch")—all fed in a single forward pass without concatenation. It outputs a set of alignment scores, bounding boxes, and pixel-level masks for every prompted concept simultaneously. Instance-level mask predictions can be aggregated by category to produce semantic or panoptic segmentation maps, and the model naturally rejects irrelevant prompts (absence descriptions) without additional negative training.

## Method

APE reformulates visual grounding as open-vocabulary detection via region-sentence matching rather than object-word alignment, using a DETR/DETA backbone with a ViT image encoder and a directional language model (CLIP or Llama) for text. Three key mechanisms enable scalable prompting: (1) independent prompts are encoded separately and compressed to sentence-level embeddings via mean pooling, removing the 512-token limit of BERT; (2) a gated cross-modality interaction injects an all-zero token for vocabulary prompts (keeping vision features static) while dynamically fusing sentence embeddings for grounding prompts, preventing the zero-shot degradation observed with deep fusion on detection data; (3) a history embedding bank supplies negative prompts to compensate for information lost in sentence-level compression. For segmentation, APE equalizes thing and stuff granularity by decomposing stuff masks into connected components treated as proxy instances during training, then merging same-category predictions at inference, unifying semantic, instance, and panoptic tasks into a single instance-level objective. The entire model is trained in one stage on 10 diverse datasets (detection, segmentation, grounding) without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On open-vocabulary detection, APE-L(D) achieves 59.6 APb / 53.0 APm on LVIS-1203 val, 49.2 APb on Objects365, and 58.3 APb / 49.3 APm on COCO-80, setting new state-of-the-art or competitive results across all benchmarks with a single set of weights. On visual grounding (D3), APE-L(D) reaches 37.5 APb intra-scenario and 82.7 ARb inter-scenario, substantially outperforming all prior methods (e.g., G-DINO: 20.7 APb / 51.1 ARb). On open-vocabulary segmentation, APE-L(D) attains 27.2 PQ and 24.4 APm on ADE-150, surpassing X-Decoder (21.8 PQ / 13.1 APm) and OpenSeeD (19.7 PQ / 15.0 APm). On single-dataset instance segmentation, APE achieves 62.7 APb / 54.1 APm on COCO and 60.9 APb / 55.3 APm on LVIS, outperforming EVA-02 and ViTDet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APE-L(D) dramatically outperforms GLIP (26.9 APb) and UNINEXT (14.0 APb) on LVIS-1203, gaining +32.7 and +45.6 APb respectively, primarily due to the region-sentence formulation enabling thousands of prompts in a single forward pass. On COCO-80 detection, APE-L(D) (58.3 APb) is slightly below GLIPv2 (64.1 APb) and G-DINO (60.7 APb), but surpasses GLIP (49.8) and UNINEXT (60.6 APm but only 60.6 APb with ViT-H backbone). On visual grounding (D3), APE-L(D) achieves 37.5 APb intra-scenario vs. G-DINO's 20.7 and OFA-DOD's 21.6, and 82.7 ARb inter-scenario vs. G-DINO's 51.1, demonstrating a strong ability to reject irrelevant prompts that prior methods lack. On segmentation, APE-L(D) leads X-Decoder by +5.4 PQ on ADE-150 (27.2 vs. 21.8) and OpenSeeD by +7.5 PQ, while matching or exceeding them on Cityscapes mIoU (44.2 vs. 52.0 for X-Decoder on the 19-class setting). On single-dataset instance segmentation (Table 8), APE (62.7 APb / 54.1 APm on COCO) surpasses EVA-02 (62.3 / 53.8) and ViTDet (60.4 / 52.0), confirming competitive detection-segmentation performance without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

APE builds on the DETR/DETA detection framework and the query-based segmentation architecture of MaskDINO, while departing fundamentally from the GLIP/GroundingDINO paradigm of casting detection as object-word grounding. Instead, APE inverts this formulation—treating visual grounding as open-vocabulary detection with region-sentence matching—which enables thousands of text prompts in a single forward pass rather than the 30–422 forwards required by prior methods. The gated cross-modality interaction (zero-token fusion for vocabularies, dynamic fusion only for sentences) and the thing-stuff equalization via connected-component decomposition represent targeted departures from the heavy deep fusion and decoupled-thing/stuff designs of GLIP and OpenSeeD, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

APE demonstrates that a single set of weights can achieve SOTA or competitive performance across detection, segmentation, and grounding on 160+ datasets without task-specific fine-tuning, which is practically significant for deploying unified vision systems. The efficiency gain—querying thousands of concepts in one forward pass with modest memory overhead compared to GLIP—addresses a real bottleneck for open-vocabulary applications. Limitations include a disadvantage in panoptic segmentation PQ (overlapping predictions vs. non-overlap requirement) and reliance on post-hoc merging of stuff instances at inference. Open questions include whether the gated fusion strategy scales gracefully to even larger vocabulary sizes or multimodal prompts, and whether the thing-stuff equalization generalizes to tasks requiring strict instance separation.
