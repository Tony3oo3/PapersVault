## Problem

Visually-situated text parsing tasks—text spotting, key information extraction, and table recognition—have traditionally been addressed by separate task-specific architectures or pipelines that rely on external OCR engines, leading to modal isolation and complex workflows. No prior unified model handles all three tasks end-to-end with a single architecture, shared objective, and unified input/output representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242339/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB document or scene image (processed at 768×768 or 1920×1920 resolution) together with a task-specific prompt token as input. Output is generated in two stages: first a structured points sequence of quantized center-point coordinates interleaved with task-specific structural tokens (e.g., <address> for KIE, <tr> for tables), then 16-point polygonal contours and character-level transcriptions conditioned on each center point. All spatial coordinates are normalized to image dimensions and discretized into a fixed bin vocabulary, making the output a purely discrete token sequence.

## Method

OmniParser employs a Swin-B backbone with FPN feature fusion feeding three independent transformer decoders (Structured Points, Region, and Content) that share architecture but not parameters. The key design is a two-stage autoregressive generation: the Structured Points Decoder first emits center points and structural tags, which then condition the Region and Content Decoders to produce 16-point polygons and text in parallel, reducing sequence length and decoupling structure from appearance. Two pre-training strategies—spatial-window prompting (restricting output to a spatial sub-region) and prefix-window prompting (restricting output to text with a given character prefix)—are used to teach the Structured Points Decoder rich spatial and semantic representations before task-specific fine-tuning. A unified 16-point polygon representation and a single negative log-likelihood objective with weighted structural tokens (weight 4.0 vs. 1.0) are shared across all three tasks, eliminating task-specific heads or adapters.

## Results

On text spotting, OmniParser achieves state-of-the-art end-to-end accuracy of 84.0% (no lexicon) and 88.9% (full lexicon) on Total-Text, and 66.8% / 85.1% on CTW1500, surpassing the previous best (DeepSolo) by +1.5% and +3.2% respectively. On ICDAR 2015, it reaches 89.6% (strong), 84.5% (weak), and 79.9% (generic) E2E accuracy, trailing only UNITS on the generic setting (80.3%). For KIE, it attains 84.8% field-level F1 on CORD and 93.6% TED accuracy on SROIE. For table recognition, it achieves 88.83 TEDS on PubTabNet and 89.75 TEDS on FinTabNet, outperforming the prior end-to-end method EDD (88.3 TEDS on PubTabNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On text spotting, OmniParser outperforms all autoregressive and query-based baselines on Total-Text and CTW1500 E2E metrics: it beats DeepSolo (82.5→84.0 None, 88.7→88.9 Full on Total-Text) and UNITS (66.4→66.8 None, 82.3→85.1 Full on CTW1500). On ICDAR 2015, it leads on strong and weak lexicons (89.6 vs. UNITS 89.0; 84.5 vs. UNITS 84.1) but is narrowly behind UNITS on the generic lexicon (79.9 vs. 80.3). For KIE, OmniParser achieves the best CORD F1 (84.8 vs. DocParser 84.5, Donut 84.1) and best SROIE TED accuracy (93.6 vs. Donut 92.8), but trails DocParser on SROIE F1 (85.6 vs. 87.3) and Donut on CORD accuracy (88.0 vs. 90.9). For table recognition, OmniParser surpasses EDD on PubTabNet TEDS (88.83 vs. 88.3) and S-TEDS (90.45 vs. 89.9), and achieves 89.75 TEDS on FinTabNet where EDD reports no TEDS score; both substantially outperform the OCR-free Donut baseline (22.7 TEDS on PubTabNet). Notably, OmniParser uses a shorter decoder length (1,500) than Donut (4,000) while achieving better results, and its unified single-model design eliminates the need for task-specific architectures or external OCR engines.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniParser builds on the autoregressive sequence-generation paradigm for scene text spotting (UNITS, SPTS) and the two-stage pre-training recipe from Pix2seq, but departs from both by introducing a unified 'structured points' representation that serves as a task-agnostic interface across text spotting, KIE, and table recognition. Unlike prior unified frameworks (DocReL, BROS, SeRum) that either depend on an external OCR engine or handle only a subset of tasks, OmniParser consolidates all three into a single encoder-decoder with three independent decoders sharing an identical architecture. The key conceptual departure is the explicit decoupling of structural reasoning (center points + markup tokens) from geometric and content prediction (polygons + transcriptions), which prior autoregressive spotters and generation-based KIE models did not separate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end, OCR-free model that simultaneously performs text spotting, key information extraction, and table recognition, which simplifies multi-task document pipelines and eliminates the modal isolation inherent in specialist-model cascades. The structured-points-as-adapter design offers a reusable inductive bias for any task where spatial structure and content can be factored, potentially extending to layout analysis and chart parsing as the authors note. Limitations include the reliance on precise word-level point annotations during training and the inability to parse non-text visual elements (figures, charts), leaving open the question of whether the paradigm generalizes to fully heterogeneous document understanding without additional task-specific heads.
