## Problem

Existing 3D GAN-based portrait view synthesis methods struggle with in-the-wild portraits exhibiting diverse appearances, exaggerated expressions, wide camera views, and artistic styles due to their limited training distributions. No prior zero-shot method simultaneously supports all these variations without per-image optimization or fine-tuning, leaving a gap in generalizable single-portrait novel view synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240172/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unaligned RGB portrait (I_ref) at any resolution and a condition image (I_cam) — an RGB rendering of a different identity at the target camera view — and produces a 512×512 novel view portrait that preserves identity, expression, and background context. No per-image optimization or fine-tuning is required at inference; the condition image can be any RGB portrait of a proxy subject, eliminating the need for explicit pose parameters, landmarks, or segmentation. Multiple reference images can optionally be concatenated to improve fidelity.

## Method

DiffPortrait3D builds on a frozen Stable Diffusion UNet and introduces three trainable modules: (1) an Appearance Reference Module that replicates the UNet to extract spatial features from the reference image, which are concatenated into the K and V projections of self-attention layers in the frozen backbone, enabling layer-by-layer cross-referencing of local textures and structures; (2) a ControlNet-style View Control Module that derives camera pose from a condition image of a different identity (to prevent appearance leakage) and injects it via zero-convolution skip connections; and (3) a View Consistency Module with cross-view attention (adapted from AnimateDiff) trained on shuffled multi-view batches to enforce spatial coherence. Training proceeds in three stages on NeRSemble and PanoHead synthetic data with the SD UNet frozen throughout. At inference, a 3D-aware noise generation step uses a lightweight 3D-convolution NVS model to produce structurally consistent initial noise, followed by 100-step DDIM denoising.

## Results

On NeRSemble novel view synthesis, DiffPortrait3D achieves FID 27.4, ID 0.70, LPIPS 0.23, and SSIM 0.62, substantially outperforming all GAN-based baselines (best competitor EG3D-PTI: FID 56.2, ID 0.28). On in-the-wild reconstruction, the method reaches FID 11.7 and ID 0.92, compared to 88.0–95.0 FID and 0.12–0.70 ID for baselines. The only metric where DiffPortrait3D trails is POSE error (0.0068 vs. 0.0021 for EG3D-PTI), which the authors attribute to camera misalignment between ground truth and the EG3D condition rendering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffPortrait3D decisively outperforms all three GAN-based baselines (EG3D-PTI, GOAE, TriPlaneNet) on every perceptual and identity metric across all three evaluation settings. On NeRSemble novel view synthesis, the FID gap is 2×–4× (27.4 vs. 56.2/84.8/112.1) and the identity score is 2×–2.5× higher (0.70 vs. 0.28/0.39/0.45), indicating that 3D GAN inversion methods struggle to preserve identity under large view changes. On in-the-wild reconstruction, the advantage is even more pronounced: FID 11.7 vs. 88.0–95.0 and ID 0.92 vs. 0.12–0.70, confirming that GAN-based methods fail on out-of-domain appearances and styles. The sole exception is POSE accuracy, where EG3D-PTI (0.0021) and GOAE (0.0022) slightly beat DiffPortrait3D (0.0068), though the authors note this stems from camera estimation error rather than a fundamental limitation. Zero-1-to-3 is compared qualitatively only (no numerical table entry) and is described as producing novel portraits with 'very limited perceptual quality' due to its training on general 3D objects rather than faces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the frozen Stable Diffusion UNet as a rendering backbone, extending the ControlNet paradigm (zero-convolution side path) to inject camera pose as an additive condition, while adapting AnimateDiff's cross-view attention for spatial (rather than temporal) consistency. It departs from the dominant 3D GAN-inversion line (EG3D, GOAE, TriPlaneNet) by replacing the limited generative prior of a 3D GAN with the far broader prior of a large-scale 2D diffusion model, and from prior diffusion-based NVS (DreamFusion, Zero-1-to-3, GENVS) by explicitly disentangling appearance and view control rather than relying on text prompts or object-centric geometric priors. The key architectural departure is the 'crossed-subject' condition image: instead of feeding ground-truth spatial maps (landmarks, edges) that leak identity information, the view control module interprets camera pose from an RGB portrait of a different identity, unifying training and inference conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a zero-shot diffusion-based pipeline can match or exceed per-image-optimized 3D GAN methods on in-the-wild portraits with exaggerated expressions, artistic styles, and wide camera angles—settings where GAN inversion fundamentally breaks down. This opens a practical path to accessible 3D portrait reconstruction without per-subject optimization. Limitations include the continued dependence on EG3D renderings for camera conditioning at inference, noticeable identity drift under large view changes from a single reference, and the 512×512 resolution ceiling. Open questions include whether the cross-view attention and 3D-aware noise strategy generalize to full-body or multi-person scenes, and whether the approach can be made real-time for interactive applications.
