## Problem

Models trained on synthetically distorted images generalize poorly to authentically distorted images due to large domain gaps, and the authors make the key observation that simply adding more distortion types to the synthetic training set (e.g., KADID-10k over LIVE) does not improve—and can even harm—generalization to authentic distortions. This negative transfer from outlier source domains has not been addressed in the BIQA literature, where existing UDA methods operate at the feature level and struggle with the extreme style differences between synthetic and authentic distortions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224×3 RGB patches randomly sampled from distorted images; during testing, five patches per image are sampled and their predictions averaged to yield a single quality score. The domain classifier outputs a 25-way probability vector over distortion types (trained on KADIS-700k), while the IQA regressor outputs a single scalar quality score (MOS or DMOS). The approach is sample-based and model-agnostic: it filters which training samples to use rather than modifying the network architecture, so it can be combined with any existing BIQA backbone.

## Method

The core mechanism is distortion-guided source domain selection (DGDS), which filters outlier distortion types from the multi-source training set before IQA training. The key theoretical insight is that when content distributions are matched between source and target, the multi-source domain adaptation error bound reduces to a function of distortion-distribution distance alone, making distortion type the natural unit for domain selection. A ResNet-50 multi-source domain classifier (25-class head) is pre-trained on KADIS-700k to recognize each distortion type; it is then applied to unlabeled target-domain images to compute, for each source domain, the average probability that target images are classified as belonging to that domain. Source domains whose average probability exceeds a threshold τ = 1/k are retained as 'similar domains,' and the IQA regressor (ResNet-50 with a single-output head, L1 loss) is trained exclusively on these selected domains. This data-level filtering reduces the distributional gap in the generalization bound without requiring any feature-space alignment, and is orthogonal to model-based BIQA methods.

## Results

On the synthetic-to-authentic setting (KADID-10k → LIVEC, KonIQ-10k, BID), DGQA achieves an average SRCC of 0.7155 and PLCC of 0.7098, using only 6 out of 25 distortion types (less than 1/3 of the training data). The headline gains are SRCC 0.6958 on LIVEC, 0.6810 on KonIQ-10k, and 0.7696 on BID. On the synthetic-to-algorithmic setting (KADID-10k → PIPAL), DGQA improves average SRCC from 0.4278 to 0.4718 (+4.40%) and PLCC from 0.4053 to 0.4596 (+5.43%) over the full-data baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGQA outperforms all 11 baselines on average SRCC and PLCC across the three authentic distortion test sets. Against the strongest baseline StyleAM (feature-style alignment UDA), DGQA gains +7.51% SRCC and +10.95% PLCC on average, with the largest margin on LIVEC (0.6958 vs 0.5844 SRCC, +11.14%) and BID (0.7696 vs 0.6365 SRCC, +13.31%); however, StyleAM remains slightly competitive on KonIQ-10k (0.7002 vs 0.6810 SRCC). Among UDA baselines, DANN (0.5744 avg SRCC) and RankDA (0.5415) are the closest but still trail DGQA by 14+ points in SRCC. Traditional no-reference methods (BRISQUE, NIQE, PIQE) and deep BIQA methods (RankIQA, MetaIQA, DB-CNN, HyperIQA) all fall well below 0.54 average SRCC, confirming that the synthetic-to-authentic gap is the dominant bottleneck rather than model architecture. Notably, DGQA achieves these gains while training on only 6 of 25 distortion types (≈24% of KADID-10k data), whereas all baselines use the full dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the feature-based unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) line of work for BIQA (DANN, UCDA, RankDA, StyleAM) by operating at the data level rather than the feature level. It draws on the multi-source domain adaptation generalization bound from Zhao et al. [34] to motivate filtering outlier source domains, and leverages the observation that in BIQA the data distribution decomposes into content and distortion components, allowing distortion type to serve as a proxy for domain distance. The approach is explicitly orthogonal to model-based BIQA methods (HyperIQA, StyleAM), positioning itself as a data-selection preprocessing step rather than a competing architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant for the BIQA community: authentic distortion annotation is expensive, and this work shows that a small, carefully selected subset of synthetic distortions (24% of KADID-10k) can outperform training on the full dataset, reducing annotation burden while improving cross-domain generalization. However, the method assumes the target domain's distortion characteristics are sufficiently captured by the 25 KADID-10k distortion types, and the domain classifier must be trained on a large synthetic dataset (KADIS-700k) with matching distortion labels—limiting applicability to truly novel or unknown distortion types. An open question is whether the distortion-guided selection generalizes to scenarios where the target domain contains distortions absent from all source domains, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-target settings.
