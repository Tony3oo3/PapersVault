## Problem

Existing 3D editing methods based on NeRF suffer from slow training and limited spatial control, while Gaussian Splatting—despite its real-time rendering and explicit representation—lacks mechanisms for precise target identification and is inherently unstable under stochastic generative guidance (e.g., SDS) due to the absence of neural-network-style buffering. No prior work has addressed these challenges to enable fast, controllable 3D editing directly on Gaussian Splatting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained 3D Gaussian scene (typically 24–96 camera poses), a text prompt describing the desired edit, and 2D segmentation masks (from SAM) that are unprojected into per-Gaussian semantic labels. For 3D inpainting, the user additionally provides a 2D inpainting mask on a single rendered view, a camera pose, and a text prompt. The output is a modified set of 3D Gaussians (edited scene or scene with added/removed objects), with a full editing session completing in 5–10 minutes and inpainting in 2–5 minutes on a single RTX A6000.

## Method

GaussianEditor introduces three key mechanisms. First, Gaussian Semantic Tracing unprojects 2D segmentation masks into 3D via inverse rendering (assigning each Gaussian a weighted semantic label based on opacity, transmittance, and mask overlap), and newly densified points inherit their parent's label, yielding a dynamic 3D mask that tracks evolving geometry throughout training. Second, Hierarchical Gaussian Splatting (HGS) organizes Gaussians into generations by densification round and applies generation-dependent anchor loss (MSE between current and recorded anchor attributes for position, scale, rotation, opacity, and color), progressively freezing older generations while allowing newer ones to adapt—simulating the buffering effect of neural networks under stochastic loss. Third, a dedicated 3D inpainting pipeline handles object removal (KNN-based interface detection + 2D inpainting supervision) and object addition (2D inpainting → Wonder3D mesh → HGS refinement → coordinate alignment via depth estimation → concatenation), completing in under 5 minutes.

## Results

In user study evaluations, GaussianEditor-iN2N achieves a 72.28% preference rate, substantially outperforming Instruct-Nerf2Nerf (15.45%) and GaussianEditor-DDS (12.27%). On CLIP Directional Similarity, GaussianEditor-iN2N scores 0.2071, compared to 0.1600 for Instruct-Nerf2Nerf and 0.1813 for GaussianEditor-DDS. In terms of speed, a single editing session with GaussianEditor takes only 5–10 minutes, whereas Instruct-Nerf2Nerf typically requires more than 30 minutes per scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole quantitative baseline is Instruct-Nerf2Nerf [12], a NeRF-based text-guided editing method. GaussianEditor-iN2N decisively wins on both metrics: it earns 72.28% user preference versus 15.45% for Instruct-Nerf2Nerf (a ~4.7× margin), and achieves 0.2071 CLIP Directional Similarity versus 0.1600 (a ~29% relative improvement). Qualitatively, Instruct-Nerf2Nerf suffers from two key weaknesses that GaussianEditor addresses: (1) it cannot precisely control the editing region, causing the entire scene to change, while GaussianEditor's semantic tracing confines modifications to the target object; (2) it produces edits with insufficient detail. The DDS-guided variant of GaussianEditor (GaussianEditor-DDS) underperforms the iN2N-guided variant on both metrics, suggesting that InstructPix2Pix-style guidance is more effective than Delta Denoising Score for this task. In terms of speed, GaussianEditor completes editing in 5–10 minutes versus over 30 minutes for Instruct-Nerf2Nerf, a roughly 3–6× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to adapt 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS) to the 3D editing task, departing from the dominant NeRF-based editing paradigm (Instruct-Nerf2Nerf, DreamEditor, Ed-Nerf) that relies on static 2D/3D masks and implicit neural field optimization. The key conceptual departure is Gaussian semantic tracing, which replaces static masks with dynamically tracked semantic labels on individual Gaussians, and Hierarchical Gaussian Splatting (HGS), which imposes generational anchor constraints to counteract the instability of explicit point-cloud-like representations under stochastic diffusion guidance. For 3D inpainting, the method composes existing 2D inpainting (SDXL), image-to-3D (Wonder3D), and depth estimation tools into a GS-specific pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As Gaussian Splatting rapidly becomes the preferred 3D representation for real-time rendering, having a fast (5–10 min) and controllable editing pipeline for it is practically important for gaming, VR, and content creation workflows. The HGS generational constraint mechanism offers a general principle for stabilizing explicit representations under stochastic optimization that could transfer beyond editing. However, the method inherits the limitations of 2D diffusion models for complex prompts, and the evaluation is limited to a single GPU with a small set of baselines, leaving open questions about scalability to very large scenes and robustness across diverse editing instructions.
