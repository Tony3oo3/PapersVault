## Problem

Large multimodal models trained on synthetic captions (e.g., BLIP-generated) exhibit severe Scalability Deficiency—performance saturates around 30M samples and then degrades—and World Knowledge Loss, where models lose the ability to identify specific people, locations, and events. The root cause is that captioning models are trained on datasets that replace specific real-world details with generic placeholders, producing overly simplified language. Meanwhile, raw web captions contain rich knowledge but are too noisy for effective training, and simple mixing strategies fail to yield consistent gains for LMMs.

## Input / Output

The fusion step operates purely at the text level: it takes two caption strings per image—a noisy raw web caption (structure-flawed but detail-rich) and a BLIP-generated synthetic caption (well-structured but semantically shallow and occasionally factually wrong)—and produces a single fused caption that preserves the syntactic quality of the synthetic caption while retaining specific real-world entities from the raw caption. The broader pipeline starts from image-text pairs in LAION-COCO, where the image is used only to generate the synthetic caption via BLIP; the LLM fusion itself never sees the image. The final output is the CAPSFUS-120M dataset of ~120M image-fused-caption pairs.

## Method

CAPSFUSION is a two-stage LLM-based caption fusion pipeline. First, ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo) is prompted with a structured instruction that explicitly characterizes each caption type (raw: detailed but grammatically flawed; synthetic: well-structured but lacking depth and potentially false) and directs the model to produce a single well-structured sentence retaining real-world details while avoiding simple concatenation. One million such fused triplets are collected. Second, LLaMA-2-13B is fine-tuned on these triplets to produce CAPSFUS-LLaMA, which matches ChatGPT quality (80/100 samples equal or better in human eval) but is fully scalable. CAPSFUS-LLaMA is then applied to 127M image-text pairs from LAION-COCO (~12 days on 128 A100 GPUs), yielding the CAPSFUS-120M dataset after heuristic filtering. The key distinction from prior work (LaCLIP, random mixing) is that the LLM performs informed, property-aware fusion rather than blind rewriting or fixed-ratio mixing, and the fine-tuned open-source model removes the ChatGPT API bottleneck for large-scale deployment.

## Results

At the 100M scale, CAPSFUSION achieves 115.7 CIDEr on COCO (+18.8 over the best baseline), 99.9 CIDEr on NoCaps (+18.3), 66.2 CIDEr on TextCaps (+11.0), and 73.0 CIDEr on Flickr30K (+6.4), outperforming all baselines across every benchmark and scale. In terms of sample efficiency, CAPSFUSION matches the best-saturated performance of baselines (trained on 100M pairs) using only 6–9M samples, an 11–16× reduction in computation. On SEED-Bench, CAPSFUSION reaches 49.8 total accuracy vs. 48.7 (Raw) and 43.2 (Synthetic), with the largest gap in Instance Interaction (57.7 vs. 47.4/48.4) and Text Recognition (20.0 vs. 34.1/12.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAPSFUSION decisively outperforms the Synthetic (LAION-COCO/BLIP) baseline on all four captioning benchmarks at every scale, with the largest gaps at 100M: +18.8 CIDEr on COCO and +18.3 on NoCaps. Against Raw (LAION-2B) captions, CAPSFUSION also wins across the board, confirming that fusing raw knowledge with synthetic structure beats either source alone. The Language Rewrites (LaCLIP) baseline performs worst of all—below even raw captions—due to severe hallucination when the LLM rewrites noisy raw text without visual grounding. The Mixing (Raw & Synthetic) strategy, which simply interleaves the two caption types at a fixed ratio, yields only marginal gains over the better of the two individual sources and is consistently outperformed by CAPSFUSION's organic fusion. Notably, synthetic captions exhibit a scalability deficiency: their performance saturates around 30M pairs and then degrades, whereas CAPSFUSION continues to improve monotonically up to 100M.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the synthetic caption pipeline established by BLIP and LAION-COCO, as well as the raw web captions from LAION-2B, but departs from prior caption-enhancement work (LaCLIP's language rewrites, random mixing strategies) in two key ways. First, it shifts the focus from CLIP training to LMM training, where the authors show that mixing strategies that benefit CLIP do not transfer. Second, rather than rewriting or naively mixing captions, CAPSFUSION uses an LLM (fine-tuned LLaMA-2) to organically fuse the complementary strengths of raw captions (world knowledge) and synthetic captions (clean structure), with explicit instructions to extract real-world details and preserve syntactic quality. The root-cause diagnosis—that captioning models trained on hypernymed datasets like Conceptual Captions produce overly simplified language—connects the data pipeline problem to the training data of the captioning model itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that synthetic captions exhibit severe scalability deficiency (saturating around 30M pairs and then degrading) directly challenges the prevailing practice of training LMMs on 600M+ synthetic captions, suggesting that current large-scale training may be wasteful or even counterproductive. The 11–16× sample efficiency gain and the release of the CAPSFUS-120M dataset provide a concrete, scalable alternative for the community. Open questions include whether the fusion strategy generalizes to non-English data, whether the specific fine-tuned LLaMA-2 model is critical versus any sufficiently capable LLM, and whether the world-knowledge preservation benefit persists as LMM architectures evolve beyond the frozen-encoder + bridging-module setup used here.
