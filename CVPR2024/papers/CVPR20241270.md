## Problem

Existing input transformation based adversarial attacks produce inconsistent attention heatmaps across different target models, which limits the transferability of the generated adversarial examples. The paper identifies that maintaining consistency in attention heatmaps across models is key to better transferability, but no prior input transformation method explicitly targets this consistency by disrupting the intrinsic spatial relationships within the image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241270/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image from ImageNet and its ground-truth class label as input, and produces a single adversarial example bounded by an ℓ∞ perturbation budget of 16/255. During optimization, the input image is repeatedly split into an n×n grid of blocks (n=2), each block is randomly shuffled in position and independently rotated by an angle in [−24°, 24°], and the average gradient is computed over N=20 such transformed copies before each update step. The final output is a single perturbed image that transfers across seven victim models including adversarially trained ensembles.

## Method

BSR proposes a new input transformation T(x, n, τ) that splits the image into n×n blocks, randomly shuffles their positions, and rotates each block by a random angle within [−τ, τ] degrees (with out-of-bounds portions cropped and gaps zero-filled). The core insight is that breaking the intrinsic spatial relationships of the image disrupts the model's attention heatmap, and by averaging gradients over N=20 such disrupted views, the optimization eliminates variance among attention heatmaps, yielding more consistent heatmaps across different models. This transformation is integrated into MI-FGSM's momentum-based iterative update, where the average gradient over transformed images replaces the single-model gradient in the momentum accumulation step. BSR is composable with other input transformations (DIM, TIM, SIM, Admix, PAM) and ensemble attacks, and ablation studies confirm that both shuffling and rotation contribute independently, with n=2, N=20, and τ=24° as optimal hyperparameters.

## Results

On ImageNet, BSR achieves an average attack success rate of 93.8% on standard trained models (Inception-v4, Inception-ResNet-v2, ResNet-101), surpassing the best baseline Admix by at least 6.5%. On adversarially trained models (Inc-v3_ens3, Inc-v3_ens4, IncRes-v2_ens), BSR reaches 56.2% average ASR, outperforming PAM by 11.0%. Under the ensemble model setting, the combined method BSR-SI-TI-DIM achieves 98.4% average ASR on adversarially trained models, exceeding PAM-TI-DIM by at least 3.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSR consistently outperforms all five input-transformation baselines (DIM, TIM, SIM, Admix, PAM) across every source-target model pair in the single-model setting. Against the strongest standard-model baseline Admix, BSR gains at least 6.5% average ASR (e.g., 96.2% vs. 82.2% on Inc-v4 when crafted on Inc-v3). Against the strongest adversarial-model baseline PAM, BSR improves by 11.0% on average (e.g., 55.0% vs. 39.0% on Inc-v3_ens3). When combined with other transformations, BSR-SI-TI-DIM reaches 99.1% on Inc-v3_ens3 and 97.0% on IncRes-v2_ens under ensemble attack, surpassing PAM-TI-DIM (95.8% and 93.0%) by 3.3% and 4.0% respectively. BSR also outperforms the MI-FGSM baseline (without any input transformation) by a wide margin in the ablation (Figure 3), confirming that the block shuffle and rotation transformation is the key driver of the transferability gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the input transformation based attack family (DIM, TIM, SIM, Admix, PAM) by introducing block shuffle and rotation (BSR) as a new transformation that disrupts the intrinsic spatial relationships within an image. It builds directly on MI-FGSM as the optimization backbone and is conceptually motivated by the observation that existing input transformations produce inconsistent Grad-CAM attention heatmaps across models, whereas ensemble attacks (which average gradients from multiple models) implicitly enforce heatmap consistency. The paper explicitly differentiates BSR from RLFAT (Song et al., ICLR 2020), which also shuffles image blocks but uses them as a regularizer for adversarial training rather than as a gradient-computation transformation for attack transferability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BSR's modularity—its ability to stack with other input transformations (e.g., BSR-SI-TI-DIM achieving 98.4% on adversarially trained models)—makes it a practical building block for practitioners constructing transfer-based attacks. The attention heatmap consistency hypothesis provides a useful interpretive lens for why diverse transformations improve transferability, potentially guiding future transformation design. Limitations include evaluation only on ImageNet classification, added computational cost from N=20 transformed images per iteration, and the fact that the method remains within the input transformation paradigm rather than addressing the broader gap between white-box and black-box attack performance. Open questions include whether the heatmap-consistency principle generalizes to other tasks (detection, segmentation) and whether more principled disruption mechanisms could outperform random shuffle-and-rotate.
