## Problem

For over six years, material acquisition and generation research has been constrained by a single small public dataset (1,628 materials at 512×512) or by company-owned libraries (e.g., Adobe's 11,000+ assets) that are inaccessible to the broader community. This gap between private and public material data limits the quality, diversity, and resolution achievable in learning-based material tasks, hindering progress in both acquisition and generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241889/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests PBR texture sets (basecolor, diffuse, normal, height, roughness, metallic, specular) from CC0/CC-BY licensed online libraries, requiring 4K resolution and tileability. Each material is augmented into 168 crops (8 rotations × 21 scale levels, all resized to 1024×1024) and rendered under 5 environment maps (2 outdoor, 2 indoor, 1 studio) using a two-pass strategy: an orthographic pass with displacement for diffuse and a perspective pass without displacement for specular, ensuring pixel-aligned ground truth while preserving specular highlights. The final output comprises 683,592 material crops, 3,417,960 renderings, and per-material metadata (source, license, tags, creation method, stationarity, physical size).

## Method

MatSynth is constructed through a multi-stage curation pipeline: (1) collecting 6,000+ candidates from five online sources plus one artist, filtering to 3,736 tileable 4K materials via visual inspection and CLIP-based quality scoring (sharpness, noisiness, realism); (2) deduplicating against each other and against Deschaintre et al. using CLIP embedding similarity; (3) correcting normal map Y-axis orientation by differentiating height maps and counting discordant vectors; (4) optimizing per-material displacement factors to match scaled-height normals to ground-truth normals; and (5) generating 332 blended materials by height-based masking between semantically compatible base/top layers. For evaluation, the paper retrains three methods—Deschaintre et al. (CNN), SurfaceNet (GAN with adversarial discriminator), and MatFuse (latent diffusion with VQ-VAE autoencoder)—on the combined dataset, demonstrating consistent RMSE, SSIM, LPIPS, and FID improvements over training on the original dataset alone.

## Results

For SVBRDF estimation, training on MatSynth consistently reduces RMSE across all material maps: SurfaceNet trained on MatSynth achieves the best rendering RMSE of 0.135 (vs. 0.161 on the original dataset), roughness RMSE of 0.162 (vs. 0.221), and specular RMSE of 0.057 (vs. 0.086). Perceptual quality also improves substantially, with SurfaceNet(MatSynth) reaching SSIM 0.613 and LPIPS 0.281 on renderings, compared to 0.494 and 0.395 for the original training. For material generation, MatFuse's FID drops from 239.9 (original) to 210.3 (fine-tuned on MatSynth) and 89.84 (fully trained on MatSynth), with class distribution becoming significantly more balanced (e.g., Fabric increases from 0.33% to 7.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three methods retrained on MatSynth versus their original training on the Deschaintre et al. 2018 dataset. For SVBRDF estimation, both Deschaintre et al. [5] and SurfaceNet [43] improve when trained on MatSynth, with SurfaceNet benefiting most: its roughness RMSE drops from 0.221 to 0.162 (−27%) and specular RMSE from 0.086 to 0.057 (−34%), while Deschaintre et al. sees roughness improve from 0.322 to 0.215 (−33%) and specular from 0.119 to 0.080 (−33%). SurfaceNet(MatSynth) outperforms Deschaintre(MatSynth) on every metric in Table 1, confirming that the adversarial discriminator leverages the larger dataset most effectively. For material generation, MatFuse [45] shows the largest relative gain: FID improves from 239.9 to 89.84 when fully retrained on MatSynth (a 63% reduction), and the class distribution shifts from being dominated by Wood (36.1%) and 'Others' (35.7%) to a much more balanced spread, with previously underrepresented categories like Fabric (0.33%→7.8%) and Ceramic (1.1%→8.5%) appearing at meaningful rates. The paper notes that the full-training FID advantage over fine-tuning is partly attributable to retraining the VQ-VAE autoencoder on MatSynth rather than reusing the original one.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatSynth directly extends the Deschaintre et al. [5] dataset (1,628 materials, research-only license, 512×512/2K) by gathering ~2.5× more materials at 4K resolution with permissive CC0/CC-BY licenses, while also reprocessing the original dataset to add metallic, base-color, and height maps for cross-dataset compatibility. The validation pipeline reuses existing acquisition methods (Deschaintre et al., SurfaceNet) and a diffusion-based generator (MatFuse), and borrows the two-pass orthographic/perspective rendering strategy from ControlMat [44] to preserve pixel alignment while retaining specular highlights. The material blending augmentation and CLIP-based quality/deduplication checks are novel data-curation techniques not present in prior material datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing 4,069 permissively licensed 4K tileable materials alongside 3.4M augmented renderings, MatSynth removes the primary data bottleneck that has forced most public material research to rely on a single small dataset or inaccessible company libraries (e.g., Adobe's 11,000+ assets). The demonstrated improvements in acquisition RMSE, perceptual metrics, and generation FID/diversity confirm that scale and diversity translate directly into quality gains. Open questions remain: the dataset is limited to flat, tileable materials (excluding procedural or non-tileable surfaces), the synthetic-to-real gap persists, and the concurrent Text2Mat [19] work suggests a race to release comparable public resources. The 4K resolution also opens the door to ultra-high-resolution material research that was previously infeasible with public data.
