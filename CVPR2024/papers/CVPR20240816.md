## Problem

Neural Radiance Fields require accurate camera poses for training, typically obtained via SfM pipelines, but existing joint pose-and-NeRF methods (e.g., BARF, NeRF--) still depend on decent initial poses and collapse on complex trajectories such as 360-degree flyarounds. This paper removes the pose initialization requirement entirely, enabling joint 3D reconstruction and 6DoF pose estimation from raw monocular video with only a smooth-motion assumption.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240816/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

ICON consumes a stream of monocular RGB video frames (longer edge 640) with no pose priors; frames are registered incrementally, one at a time, with each new frame's pose initialized from the previous frame. In object-only settings (CO3D, HO3D), foreground masks are used to supervise opacity and avoid sampling occluded rays. The method outputs per-frame 6DoF camera poses, a trained NeRF representing the 3D scene, and novel view renderings; meshes can be extracted post-hoc via Marching Cubes.

## Method

ICON introduces a Neural Confidence Field—an additional sigmoid head on the NeRF MLP that predicts a confidence score ζ for each 3D location and viewing direction, aggregated volumetrically along rays. This confidence dynamically calibrates the photometric loss: NeRF parameter gradients are weighted by per-pose confidence (trust high-confidence poses to shape the 3D model), while pose gradients are weighted by per-ray confidence (trust well-reconstructed 3D regions to refine poses). A momentum-based tracker (β=0.9) maintains running pose confidence, and a restart mechanism re-initializes a frame's pose from its predecessor if confidence drops below a statistical threshold, analogous to COLMAP's trial-and-error registration. To mitigate bas-relief ambiguity, ICON launches multiple independent runs and selects the one with highest confidence at 10% of training. A confidence-weighted Sampson distance provides a geometric constraint from SIFT correspondences, down-weighted as pose confidence increases to avoid corrupting precise poses with noisy matches.

## Results

On CO3D full-scene, ICON achieves ATE 0.138 and ATE_rot 1.16° for pose estimation, and PSNR 22.24 / SSIM 0.654 / LPIPS 0.428 for novel view synthesis, surpassing NeRF trained with ground-truth COLMAP poses (PSNR 21.03). On the more challenging CO3D object-only setting (background removed), ICON reaches ATE 0.215 and PSNR 22.45, while COLMAP degrades to ATE 1.177 and BARF collapses to ATE 6.522. On HO3D (dynamic hand-held objects), ICON achieves ATE 0.033 and ATE_rot 8.07° using RGB only, comparable to the SOTA RGB-D method BundleSDF (ATE 0.021, ATE_rot 6.82°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICON decisively outperforms BARF (the base joint pose+NeRF method) across all metrics: ATE drops from 6.215 to 0.138 and PSNR rises from 12.77 to 22.24 on CO3D full-scene, with the gap widening on object-only CO3D (BARF ATE 6.522 vs. ICON 0.215). Among pose-estimation baselines, ICON beats DROID-SLAM (ATE 0.138 vs. 0.431), PoseDiffusion (0.138 vs. 1.973), and FlowCam (0.138 vs. 2.681) on CO3D, and also surpasses them on the object-only setting where DROID-SLAM degrades to ATE 5.903. Against other pose-free NeRF methods (NoPe-NeRF, L2G-NeRF, LocalRF), ICON leads by large margins in both pose accuracy and rendering quality. Notably, ICON even exceeds NeRF trained with ground-truth COLMAP poses on PSNR (22.24 vs. 21.03), attributed to joint optimization producing poses better suited for NeRF rendering. On HO3D, ICON (RGB-only) is competitive with the SOTA RGB-D method BundleSDF (ATE 0.033 vs. 0.021; ATE_rot 8.07° vs. 6.82°) and clearly outperforms BundleTrack (ATE 0.033 vs. 0.046; ATE_rot 8.07° vs. 29.45°). On object-only CO3D, ICON also surpasses COLMAP (ATE 0.215 vs. 1.177) and COLMAP+SuperPoint/SuperGlue (0.215 vs. 3.616), demonstrating that incremental joint optimization can outperform classical SfM when background cues are unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICON builds directly on BARF's joint pose-and-NeRF optimization framework and NeRF's volumetric rendering, but departs from the entire line of pose-free NeRF work (BARF, NeRF--, SCNeRF, SPARF, etc.) by replacing their batch optimization with an incremental, frame-by-frame registration strategy inspired by incremental SfM and SLAM. The key conceptual departure is the Neural Confidence Field: rather than treating photometric error uniformly, ICON learns a per-3D-location confidence signal that dynamically reweights which frames inform the NeRF and which 3D regions inform the poses, effectively implementing the principle 'when pose is good, learn NeRF; when NeRF is good, learn pose.' The restart mechanism for escaping local minima is borrowed from COLMAP's trial-and-error registration, and the Sampson distance geometric constraint follows recent pose-free NeRF work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ICON removes the COLMAP/SfM preprocessing step that has been a prerequisite for NeRF-based reconstruction, which is practically significant for real-world video where poses are unavailable and backgrounds are uncontrolled. The finding that ICON outperforms NeRF trained on COLMAP 'ground truth' poses on CO3D challenges the assumption that better SfM poses always yield better NeRF, suggesting that jointly optimized poses can be more compatible with radiance field learning. Limitations include the requirement for smooth camera motion, the 200k-iteration training cost, and the object-centric focus; open questions include whether the confidence-based approach scales to longer sequences, real-time settings, or more general scene-level reconstruction (e.g., egocentric video).
