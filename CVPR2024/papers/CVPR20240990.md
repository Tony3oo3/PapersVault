## Problem

Egocentric video captioning is constrained by the limited scale of first-person datasets, while existing approaches that transfer from exocentric videos suffer from viewpoint, camera-motion, and domain discrepancies. No prior work leverages the vast corpus of third-person instructional videos as retrieval-augmented references to assist egocentric captioning, and no unified ego-exo representation space exists for cross-view video retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240990/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single egocentric video clip and retrieves K (up to 8) semantically relevant exocentric instructional videos with their refined text captions from a HowTo100M pool. The captioning model concatenates all K exo-videos with the ego-video into a single sequence, tokenizes each frame into CLIP patches, compresses via a perceiver resampler to 64 fixed-length query tokens, and feeds the stacked exo-captions as a text prompt to a gated cross-attention LLM that autoregressively generates the ego-caption.

## Method

EgoInstructor has two stages. First, a cross-view retrieval module is trained on automatically constructed pseudo ego-exo pairs (from Ego4D and HowTo100M) using a novel EgoExoNCE contrastive loss that defines three types of positive pairs—original video-text, cross-view video-text, and shared noun/verb pairs—pulling ego and exo video features toward shared text semantics without requiring time-synchronized pairs. Second, a retrieval-augmented captioning model (frozen CLIP visual encoder + perceiver resampler + LLaMA/MPT text decoder with gated cross-attention) takes the ego-video and retrieved exo-videos/captions as joint input, where each text token cross-attends only to its corresponding video's visual features, and a tanh-gating mechanism ensures the decoder starts as the original LLM for stable training.

## Results

On the cross-view retrieval benchmarks, VideoMAE+Ours achieves the strongest overall performance: 31.6 mAP on EK100-MIR, 92.7% inter-video accuracy on EgoMCQ, 25.5 R@1 on YouCook2-Clip, and 96.8/44.0 R@1 on CharadesEgo Ego2Exo/Exo2Ego. On egocentric video captioning (Ego4D cooking subset), the MPT-1B model with 8 retrieved exocentric videos reaches 210.7 CIDER, nearly doubling the 108.9 CIDER of LaViLA-LLaMA7B without retrieval. On the EgoLearner generalisation test, one-shot retrieval-augmented captioning (MPT-1B) achieves 24.5 CIDER versus 6.5 CIDER for the 0-shot baseline, demonstrating effective cross-dataset transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed EgoExoNCE cross-view alignment consistently improves every baseline encoder it is applied to. On the cross-view CharadesEgo benchmark, InternVideo+Ours gains +15.9% Ego2Exo R@1 (50.1→52.8 is modest, but the paper reports 63.9→79.8 in the full table) and VideoMAE+Ours jumps from 38.7 to 96.8 Ego2Exo R@1, a +58.1 point gain. On EgoLearner Ego2Exo retrieval, VideoMAE+Ours reaches 73.4 R@1 versus 37.6 for the VideoMAE baseline and 36.0 for CLIP, more than doubling the retrieval accuracy. On egocentric benchmarks (EK100-MIR, EgoMCQ), the gains are smaller but consistent: VideoMAE+Ours improves mAP from 28.1 to 31.6 and EgoMCQ inter-video accuracy from 91.5% to 92.7%. For captioning, the retrieval-augmented MPT-1B model (210.7 CIDER at 8-shot) outperforms LaViLA-GPT2-XL (103.0 CIDER) and LaViLA-LLaMA7B (108.9 CIDER) by roughly 2×, confirming that retrieved exocentric references provide substantial complementary information beyond what ego-only models capture. The ego-only HierVL baseline, while competitive on EgoMCQ (90.5%), has no cross-view retrieval capability and cannot be evaluated on exocentric or ego-exo benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of ego-exo video understanding and retrieval-augmented generation. Prior ego-exo work (EgoExo, AE2, Sum-L) either required time-synchronized paired data or was limited to vision-only transfer, while retrieval-augmented models in the vision-language domain have focused on image-level retrieval with CLIP. EgoInstructor departs from both lines by (1) constructing pseudo ego-exo pairs automatically via LLM-refined language alignment between unpaired Ego4D and HowTo100M datasets, eliminating the need for synchronized collection, and (2) extending the Flamingo-style gated cross-attention captioning architecture with a frozen cross-view retrieval module trained via a novel EgoExoNCE loss that incorporates cross-view positive pairs beyond standard InfoNCE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single retrieved exocentric instructional video can triple the CIDER score for egocentric captioning (54→174), suggesting that cross-view retrieval is a highly effective and underexploited mechanism for transferring procedural knowledge to first-person video understanding. The automatic pair-construction pipeline is practical and scalable, removing the expensive requirement for synchronized ego-exo data collection. However, the captioning evaluation is limited to the cooking subset of Ego4D and one in-house dataset (EgoLearner), and the retrieval module is frozen during captioning, leaving open whether end-to-end joint training or retrieval for non-procedural egocentric activities would yield similar gains. Extending the framework to broader egocentric tasks (action recognition, grounding) is noted as future work.
