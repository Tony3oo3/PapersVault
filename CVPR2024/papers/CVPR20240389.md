## Problem

Existing expressive human pose and shape estimation (EHPS) methods rely on multi-stage pipelines that detect humans, crop images, and regress body parts separately, or on one-stage methods that still require bounding boxes for cropping. This introduces loss of contextual and location information, blocks inter-part and inter-human associations, and causes significant performance degradation under noisy or detected bounding boxes, especially in crowded scenes. No prior method performs end-to-end multi-person whole-body mesh recovery directly from a full image without any external detection or cropping step.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single full-frame RGB image as input with no pre-cropping, bounding boxes, or other auxiliary inputs required. It simultaneously outputs SMPL-X parameters for all detected persons (body pose θ_body ∈ R^{22×3}, left/right hand poses, jaw pose, shape β ∈ R^{10}, and expression ψ ∈ R^{10}), 3D joint locations, and predicted bounding boxes for each person's body, hands, and face. The model handles an arbitrary number of people per image, with a maximum of 900 candidate human tokens filtered down to 100 for refinement.

## Method

AiOS is built on a DETR-style architecture with a ResNet-50 backbone, a transformer encoder for long-range image feature aggregation, and a progressive deformable-attention decoder that treats multi-person mesh recovery as a sequential set prediction problem. The key 'Human-as-Tokens' design introduces three levels of queries: body location tokens (bounding-box queries following DAB-DETR) for coarse localization and global features, body joint tokens (17 learnable embeddings) for fine-grained local features, and hand/face location and joint tokens for whole-body refinement. A carefully designed self-attention mask allows body, hand, and face location tokens to attend across persons (capturing inter-human occlusion cues) while restricting joint tokens to attend only within their own subject (limiting computational cost). Supervision is progressive: bounding boxes and classification are supervised at every stage, SMPL-X body parameters are supervised starting from the body-refinement stage, and full whole-body SMPL-X parameters (body, hands, face) are supervised only at the final whole-body-refinement stage, preventing the model from regressing parameters before locations are sufficiently refined.

## Results

On the AGORA SMPL-X test set (threshold 0.5), AiOS achieves NMVE of 97.8 mm, MVE of 91.9 mm, and MPJPE of 90.2 mm, outperforming the previous SOTA SMPLer-X (NMVE 133.1 mm, MVE 123.8 mm) by approximately 27% in NMVE. On the AGORA SMPL test set, AiOS achieves NMVE of 61.2 mm, surpassing the best one-stage method BEV (108.3 mm) by 43% and the best top-down method NIKI (70.2 mm) by 13%. On EHF (unseen at training), AiOS reduces PVE from 65.4 mm (SMPLer-X) to 45.4 mm, a 30% improvement, and on UBody achieves PA-PVE of 32.5 mm without any ground-truth bounding boxes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AiOS decisively outperforms all two-stage EHPS baselines on AGORA: it reduces NMVE by 27% over SMPLer-X (97.8 vs 133.1 mm), 25% over OSX (97.8 vs 130.6 mm), and 19% over HybrIK-X (97.8 vs 120.5 mm), while also achieving the highest precision (0.98). Against one-stage HPS methods on the AGORA SMPL test set, AiOS (NMVE 61.2 mm) surpasses ROMP (113.6 mm) by 46% and BEV (108.3 mm) by 43%, and also beats the best top-down methods NIKI (70.2 mm) and PLIKS (71.6 mm). On the unseen EHF dataset, AiOS achieves 30% lower PVE than SMPLer-X (45.4 vs 65.4 mm) and 36% lower than OSX (45.4 vs 70.8 mm). On UBody, AiOS (PA-PVE 32.5 mm) is competitive with the fine-tuned SMPLer-X† (31.9 mm) despite not using ground-truth bounding boxes, and clearly outperforms non-finetuned SMPLer-X (33.2 mm) and OSX (42.4 mm). Notably, feeding AiOS's predicted bounding boxes to OSX and SMPLer-X improves their MVE (122.8→121.3 and 99.7→98.3 mm respectively), confirming that the two-stage methods are sensitive to box quality and that AiOS's localization is high-quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

AiOS extends the DETR family of end-to-end detection frameworks (DETR, DAB-DETR, Deformable DETR) from object detection and 2D pose estimation to 3D whole-body mesh recovery, treating EHPS as a progressive set prediction problem with sequential detection stages. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (detect → crop → per-part regression) used by OSX and SMPLer-X, and from one-stage HPS methods like ROMP and BEV that rely on a single global feature per person. The key conceptual departure is the 'Human-as-Tokens' design, which introduces both box-location tokens (for global context) and joint-related tokens (for local detail) in a multi-level progressive decoding scheme, building on the token-based localization ideas from DINO and ED-Pose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the separate detection step and ground-truth bounding box dependency, AiOS addresses a critical practical gap: two-stage methods degrade significantly under noisy or absent boxes, yet real-world deployment cannot assume perfect crops. The finding that feeding AiOS's predicted boxes to OSX and SMPLer-X improves their results (e.g., SMPLer-X MVE from 99.7 to 98.3 mm) underscores that box quality is a more important bottleneck than previously acknowledged. Open questions include whether the approach scales to tracking, 3D camera localization, and hand estimation under very low resolution, and whether additional multi-person real-world data can close the remaining gap to the best two-stage methods.
