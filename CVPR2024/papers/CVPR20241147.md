## Problem

End-to-end motion planners for autonomous driving rely on large neural networks that are impractical for deployment on resource-constrained edge devices, yet simply reducing parameters causes dramatic performance degradation (e.g., InterFuser's driving score drops from 53.44 to 36.55 when halved). Knowledge distillation has not been explored for this task, and existing KD methods fail to address two planning-specific challenges: intermediate features contain planning-irrelevant noise, and different waypoints in a trajectory carry vastly different safety-critical importance.

## Input / Output

The motion planner consumes raw sensor data (camera images or LiDAR point clouds), the ego-vehicle's current speed, and a discrete navigation command (left/right/straight/follow), producing a planned trajectory as a sequence of 2D waypoints that is subsequently fed to a PID controller for low-level actuation. During distillation, the framework additionally operates on intermediate feature maps from the perception backbone and a bird's-eye-view (BEV) scene representation with appended pixel coordinates, which are used to compute per-waypoint attention weights.

## Method

PlanKD comprises two distillation modules. First, a planning-relevant feature distillation module applies the information bottleneck principle to extract a compact representation from intermediate feature maps that maximizes mutual information with eight defined planning states (nearby vehicle/pedestrian, traffic sign, junction, traffic light, brake, throttle, steer) while minimizing mutual information with the full feature map; the student then matches the teacher's bottleneck representation via L1 loss. Second, a safety-aware waypoint-attentive distillation module computes per-waypoint attention weights via cross-attention between a BEV scene encoder and a waypoint encoder, then applies a pairwise ranking loss that uses a Gaussian kernel to measure each waypoint's proximity to moving obstacles, encouraging higher attention for safety-critical waypoints. An entropy regularization prevents over-concentration on a single waypoint. The overall training objective jointly optimizes attention-weighted waypoint imitation against both the teacher and the expert trajectory, the IB lower bound, feature matching, the ranking loss, and the entropy term in an end-to-end fashion.

## Results

On the Town05 Long Benchmark, PlanKD boosts the 26.3M InterFuser student from a driving score of 36.55 (trained without distillation) to 55.90, surpassing the 52.9M teacher (53.44) while halving inference time from 78.3 ms to 39.7 ms. Similarly, the 13.9M TCP student improves from 39.96 to 53.19, matching the 25.8M teacher (53.41) at 10.7 ms per frame. On the Town05 Short Benchmark, PlanKD achieves a driving score of 93.69 (InterFuser 26.3M) and 95.49 (TCP 13.9M), with collision rates of 0.207 and 0.141 per km respectively—substantially lower than the same-size models trained without distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PlanKD outperforms all three KD baselines (AT, ReviewKD, DPK) by a large margin on Driving Score across both backbones. For the InterFuser 26.3M student, PlanKD achieves 93.69 driving score versus AT (83.94), ReviewKD (84.19), and DPK (84.59)—a gap of roughly 9–10 points. For the TCP 13.9M student, PlanKD reaches 95.49 versus AT (86.66), ReviewKD (88.46), and DPK (88.54). PlanKD also achieves the lowest collision rate (0.207 vs. 0.426/0.569/0.286 for InterFuser; 0.141 vs. 0.209/0.149/0.149 for TCP) and zero infraction rate, reflecting its safety-aware waypoint attention. The one metric where PlanKD trails is Route Completion (96.43 vs. 99.88 for AT on InterFuser), which the authors attribute to the model prioritizing safety by stopping in poor traffic conditions rather than pushing through.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation paradigm (Hinton et al., 2015) to the specific domain of end-to-end motion planning, building on the deep variational information bottleneck framework (Alemi et al., 2016) to filter planning-relevant features from intermediate representations. It departs from generic KD methods (AT, ReviewKD, DPK) by introducing two task-specific mechanisms: an information-bottleneck-based feature distillation that selectively transfers only planning-relevant knowledge, and a safety-aware waypoint-attentive loss that adaptively weights trajectory waypoints by their collision risk. The work is positioned as the first KD framework explicitly designed for compressing end-to-end motion planners, applied to two state-of-the-art architectures (InterFuser and TCP) evaluated in the CARLA simulator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling deployment of high-performing motion planners on resource-constrained edge devices (delivery robots, embedded automotive systems) where a ~50% inference time reduction is critical for real-time safety responses. The safety-aware attention mechanism, which explicitly prioritizes collision-risk waypoints during distillation, offers a transferable principle for safety-critical sequence prediction beyond driving. Limitations include evaluation solely in the CARLA simulator on Town05 benchmarks with only two teacher architectures, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to real-world sensor noise, diverse driving geographies, or fundamentally different planner architectures. An open question is whether the information-bottleneck planning-state definitions (eight hand-specified states) scale gracefully to more complex or open-ended driving scenarios.
