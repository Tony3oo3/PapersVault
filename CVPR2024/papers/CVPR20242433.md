## Problem

Large-kernel depthwise convolutions (e.g., 7×7 in ConvNeXt) provide the large receptive fields needed for competitive accuracy, but they incur high memory access costs that make them a practical speed bottleneck on GPUs—ConvNeXt-T is 1.8× slower than ResNet-50 despite similar FLOPs. Reducing the kernel size to 3×3 recovers speed but drops accuracy by 0.6%, posing the open question of how to accelerate large-kernel CNNs without sacrificing performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242433/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 (classification) or 512×512 crops (segmentation) and produces a single class label over 1000 ImageNet categories or a per-pixel semantic mask over 150 ADE20K categories. The architecture uses a 4-stage hierarchical design with progressive 4× downsampling (H/4, H/8, H/16, H/32), and the base model can be fine-tuned at 384×384 for improved classification accuracy.

## Method

InceptionNeXt replaces ConvNeXt's 7×7 depthwise convolution with an Inception depthwise convolution that splits input channels into four parallel branches: a 3×3 square kernel, a 1×11 horizontal band kernel, an 11×1 vertical band kernel (each operating on 1/8 of channels), and an identity mapping on the remaining 5/8 of channels. This decomposition is inspired by Inception v3's factorization of large kernels into orthogonal band kernels, but applied in a channel-parallel fashion so that the three convolutional branches run simultaneously rather than sequentially. The resulting complexity is linear in kernel size (O(k)) rather than quadratic (O(k²)), and the four branches collectively preserve a large spatial receptive field. The operator is embedded in the MetaNeXt block—a simplified MetaFormer with a single residual shortcut—followed by LayerNorm, an MLP with GELU, and a residual connection, using Batch Normalization for speed. Trained with DeiT-style hyperparameters (AdamW, RandAugment, Mixup, CutMix, LayerScale), InceptionNeXt-T achieves 82.3% top-1 on ImageNet-1K at 1.6× the training throughput of ConvNeXt-T.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, InceptionNeXt-T achieves 82.3% top-1 accuracy (0.2% above ConvNeXt-T's 82.1%) while delivering 1.6× higher training throughput (901 vs. 575 img/s on A100), matching ResNet-50's speed. At the Base size, InceptionNeXt-B reaches 84.0% top-1 (0.2% above ConvNeXt-B) with 1.4× training throughput. For semantic segmentation on ADE20K with UperNet, InceptionNeXt-B achieves 50.6% mIoU, outperforming ConvNeXt-B (49.9%) and Swin-B (49.7%) while also running faster (17.5 vs. 15.7 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its primary baseline ConvNeXt, InceptionNeXt wins on both accuracy and speed at every model size: +0.2% top-1 at Tiny (82.3 vs. 82.1), +0.4% at Small (83.5 vs. 83.1), and +0.2% at Base (84.0 vs. 83.8), while achieving 1.4–1.6× training throughput. The only exception is the Atto size, where InceptionNeXt-A trails ConvNeXt-A by 0.4% top-1 (75.3 vs. 75.7) but still delivers 2.2× training throughput. Compared to ResNet-50 (78.4% top-1, 969 img/s), InceptionNeXt-T matches its training speed (901 img/s) while gaining 3.9% accuracy. Against Transformer baselines, InceptionNeXt-T outperforms Swin-T (82.3 vs. 81.3%) with 1.6× training throughput, and InceptionNeXt-S beats Swin-S (83.5 vs. 83.0%) with 1.5× training throughput. On ADE20K segmentation, InceptionNeXt consistently surpasses both ConvNeXt and Swin backbones with UperNet (e.g., 50.6 vs. 49.9 vs. 49.7% mIoU at Base) and significantly outperforms PVT and PoolFormer with Semantic FPN (46.4 vs. 42.1 vs. 42.7% mIoU at Base size).

## Relation to Prior Work

InceptionNeXt sits at the intersection of two architectural lineages: the modernized CNN design of ConvNeXt (which adopted ViT-inspired training techniques and large 7×7 depthwise convolutions) and the multi-branch parallel structure of Inception networks. The key conceptual departure is identifying that large-kernel depthwise convolution, despite its low FLOPs, is a practical speed bottleneck on modern GPUs due to memory access costs, and addressing it by decomposing the operation into four parallel branches (3×3 square, 1×k and k×1 band kernels, and identity mapping) along the channel dimension. The block formulation follows MetaNeXt, a simplified abstraction of MetaFormer that merges two residual sub-blocks into one, constraining the token mixer to lightweight operations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InceptionNeXt provides a simple, drop-in replacement for ConvNeXt's depthwise convolution that recovers ResNet-50-level training throughput while matching or slightly exceeding ConvNeXt accuracy, making it a practical and economical baseline for future CNN architecture research. The speed improvement diminishes for larger model sizes where MLP computation (O(C²)) dominates over depthwise convolution (O(C)), limiting the technique's impact at scale. An open question is whether the parallel band-kernel branches can be further optimized at the CUDA level, and whether structural re-parameterization could extend the effective receptive field beyond the 11-pixel band kernel without the associated speed penalty.
