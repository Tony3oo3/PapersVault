## Problem

The COCO segmentation benchmark suffers from coarse annotations (loose polygons for 'thing' instances, superpixel-based masks for 'stuff' regions), inconsistencies across semantic, instance, and panoptic annotation types, and limited scale (~120K images, 1.3M masks), leading to performance plateaus for modern segmentation models. This paper addresses the need for a modernized, human-verified, large-scale universal segmentation dataset that harmonizes annotations across all segmentation tasks with substantially improved mask quality.

## Input / Output

The dataset comprises 383K RGB images sourced from COCO (training, validation, and unlabeled sets) and Objects365, annotated with 5.18M high-quality panoptic masks covering 80 'thing' and 53 'stuff' classes. The validation set (COCONut-val) contains 25K images averaging 17.4 masks per image—significantly denser than COCO-val's 11.3—making it a more challenging testbed. Annotations are provided in unified formats supporting panoptic, instance, and semantic segmentation as well as object detection, with consistent class labels across all task types.

## Method

The core contribution is a four-stage assisted-manual annotation pipeline: (1) machine-generated proposals using DETA for thing bounding boxes and kMaX-DeepLab for stuff masks, (2) human inspection and editing via a structured questionnaire, (3) mask generation through Box2Mask (box-kMaX, which extends kMaX-DeepLab with box queries initialized from backbone features pooled within box regions) and Point2Mask (CFR interactive segmenter using positive/negative clicks), and (4) expert quality verification with 50% random sampling and relabeling via Photoshop for failures. A data engine iteratively retrains the proposal models on previously annotated data, progressively reducing the non-pass rate from 78% (COCONut-S) to 43% (COCONut-L) and cutting annotation time from ~10 minutes per mask to ~10 seconds (things) or ~42 seconds (stuff). The paper further demonstrates through controlled experiments that fully human-verified annotations (COCONut-B) yield substantially better training outcomes than pseudo-labeled variants (COCONut-B^M), confirming the irreplaceable value of human annotation over machine-generated labels at scale.

## Results

Training kMaX-DeepLab with ConvNeXt-L on COCONut-L achieves 62.7 PQ, 47.6 AP^mask, and 70.6 mIoU on COCONut-val, outperforming the same model trained on the original COCO set (58.3 PQ, 44.1 AP^mask, 66.4 mIoU) by +4.4 PQ. On the relabeled COCO-val, the COCONut-L-trained model reaches 65.1 PQ versus 60.4 PQ for the COCO-trained model, confirming that higher-quality annotations yield measurable gains. A key finding is that pseudo-labels provide only a marginal 0.1% PQ improvement (COCONut-B_M: 59.5 PQ vs. COCONut-S: 59.4 PQ on COCONut-val), whereas fully human-annotated COCONut-B achieves 61.3 PQ, demonstrating the superior value of human verification over machine-generated labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a dataset paper, the primary comparisons are between training configurations rather than competing methods. Training on COCONut-L with ConvNeXt-L surpasses the original COCO training set by +4.4 PQ on COCONut-val (62.7 vs. 58.3) and by +4.7 PQ on relabeled COCO-val (65.1 vs. 60.4), confirming that both scale and annotation quality matter. Notably, COCONut-B training nearly matches COCO training on the original COCO-val (57.8 vs. 57.9 PQ), suggesting that a larger, higher-quality corpus can mitigate inter-dataset annotation biases. The pseudo-label comparison (Table 7) is particularly informative: COCONut-B_M (COCONut-S + machine pseudo-labeled COCO unlabeled) yields only 59.5 PQ on COCONut-val, just 0.1% above COCONut-S alone (59.4), while fully human-annotated COCONut-B reaches 61.3 PQ—a 1.8 PQ gap that highlights the limited transferability of pseudo-labels. Finally, COCONut-val proves more discriminative than COCO-val: performance saturates on COCO-val between COCONut-B and COCONut-L (57.8→58.1 PQ), but continues to improve on COCONut-val (61.3→62.7 PQ), validating the new 25K-image validation set as a more challenging and informative benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

COCONut extends the COCO lineage (COCO → COCO-Stuff → COCO panoptic → Sama-COCO) by re-annotating and expanding it with human-verified masks, while drawing on kMaX-DeepLab (extended into box-kMaX for Box2Mask) and CFR (for Point2Mask) as the neural components of its assisted-manual annotation pipeline. It departs from prior large-scale efforts like SA-1B (99.1% machine-generated, no public human labels) and EntitySeg (high quality but only 33K images) by explicitly maintaining human verification as a quality standard while scaling to 358K training images via an iterative data engine. The paper also positions itself against ADE20K's inconsistent panoptic annotations (derived by merging separately annotated instance and semantic maps) by enforcing a single unified annotation protocol across all segmentation types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COCONut addresses a critical practical gap: COCO-val (5K images, 11.3 masks/image) has become saturated and inadequate for differentiating modern segmenters, while the new COCONut-val (25K images, 17.4 masks/image) continues to separate models trained on different data scales. The finding that pseudo-labels yield only ~0.1% PQ improvement over the base training set—while fully human-annotated COCONut-B delivers substantially larger gains—challenges the common assumption that machine-generated labels are a cost-effective scaling strategy and suggests that annotation quality, not just quantity, is the binding constraint. Open questions include whether the 133-class taxonomy remains sufficient for open-vocabulary or foundation-model settings, and whether the assisted-manual pipeline's quality guarantees hold when scaled beyond the current 383K-image scope.
