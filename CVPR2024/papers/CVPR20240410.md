## Problem

Noisy correspondence (mismatched image-text pairs) in cross-modal retrieval datasets causes severe degradation of retrieval models, yet prior robust-learning methods (NCR, MSCN, BiCro, RINCE) only address cross-modal interactions and ignore the impact on intra-modal geometrical structures. This paper identifies that noisy pairs disrupt both cross-modal alignment and intra-modal structure simultaneously, and that the resulting structural differences between clean and noisy samples within a well-established representation space provide a reliable signal for noise discrimination that existing methods fail to exploit.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard image-text pairs from retrieval datasets (Flickr30K, MS-COCO, NUS-WIDE, CC152K) where each pair carries a presumed positive correspondence label that may be noisy. It produces two outputs: (1) a cross-modal retrieval ranking evaluated via R@1/R@5/R@10 in both image→text and text→image directions, and (2) per-sample soft correspondence labels in [0,1] estimated via a bidirectional cross-modal softmax ratio and a two-component GMM over intra-modal structure cosine similarities, with the final label being the element-wise minimum of the two estimates. The soft labels are applied sample-wise to weight contrastive losses and to filter noisy neighbors during intra-modal structure computation.

## Method

GSC simultaneously optimizes a cross-modal contrastive loss (aligning matched pairs in a shared 1024-d representation space) and an intra-modal structure alignment loss (constraining the pairwise similarity vectors ⟨I_i, I_j⟩ and ⟨T_i, T_j⟩ to be consistent across modalities), leveraging the DNN memorization effect so that clean structures form before noise is overfit. For noise discrimination, GSC computes a cross-modal indicator y_CM via a bidirectional softmax ratio of the pair's similarity against all candidates, and an intra-modal indicator y_IM by fitting a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model to the cosine similarities between each sample's intra-modal structure vectors across modalities. The final soft label y = min(y_CM, y_IM) is then used to weight both loss functions sample-wise and to exclude noisy neighbors from intra-modal structure calculations, creating a positive feedback loop. Training employs temporal ensembling with momentum (β=0.7) to stabilize label updates across epochs, and a dual-network scheme where each network's correspondence labels are computed from the other network's representations, all within a single-model-compatible framework that avoids the inseparable dual-network dependency of NCR-based methods.

## Results

On Flickr30K with 40% simulated noise, GSC-SGR achieves a recall sum of 497.3, outperforming the second-best BiCro-SGRAF (487.5) by 9.8 points. On MS-COCO at the same noise level, GSC-SGR reaches 519.9 versus BiCro-SGRAF's 517.1. Under the most severe 60% noise, GSC-SGR maintains 478.0 on Flickr30K and 511.7 on MS-COCO, while baselines like NCR-SGR collapse to 184.6 and 2.4 respectively. On the real-world noisy CC152K dataset, GSC-SGR achieves a sum of 375.1, surpassing BiCro-SGRAF (370.2) by 4.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSC-SGR consistently outperforms all seven baselines across all four datasets and noise levels. Against the strongest competitors BiCro-SGRAF and MSCN-SGR, GSC achieves gains of 1.1–11.7 points on Flickr30K and 1.3–10.2 points on MS-COCO, with the largest margins appearing at 60% noise (e.g., +11.7 on Flickr30K over BiCro). On the real-world CC152K dataset, GSC-SGR (375.1) beats BiCro-SGRAF (370.2) by 4.9 points and MSCN-SGR (366.7) by 8.4 points. Notably, GSC uses the simpler SGR backbone while BiCro and DECL use the stronger SGRAF backbone, yet GSC still wins, indicating the method's structural advantage. Against CLIP-B fine-tuned with 50% noise (131.1 sum), GSC achieves 416.3, demonstrating that explicit noise handling far exceeds what a large pre-trained model can absorb through fine-tuning alone. NCR-based methods (NCR, MSCN) degrade severely at 60% noise on MS-COCO (2.4 and 501.8 respectively), while GSC maintains 511.7, highlighting its superior robustness under extreme noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the noisy correspondence learning paradigm initiated by NCR [15] and refined by BiCro [37], MSCN [10], and DECL [31], all of which focus exclusively on cross-modal interactions to identify mismatched pairs. GSC departs from this lineage by introducing intra-modal geometrical structure consistency as a complementary signal for noise discrimination, arguing that prior methods underutilize the rich within-modality structure. It also draws on temporal ensembling from Liu et al. [25] and the memorization effect of DNNs to iteratively refine soft correspondence labels, while remaining compatible with existing retrieval backbones like SGR and SGRAF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that intra-modal structural asymmetry is a reliable indicator of noisy correspondence, GSC provides a principled and computationally lightweight alternative to dual-network co-teaching frameworks, making it more adaptable to large-scale models. The consistent outperformance over methods built on stronger backbones (SGRAF) suggests the structural consistency signal is genuinely informative rather than a backbone artifact. Open questions include whether the two-component GMM assumption for intra-modal similarity distributions holds under more complex or multi-label noise, and whether the approach generalizes beyond image-text to other modality pairs such as audio-visual or point-cloud-text.
