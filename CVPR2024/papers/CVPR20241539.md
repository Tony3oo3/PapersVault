## Problem

Existing evaluation metrics for text-to-3D generative models (e.g., CLIP similarity, aesthetic scores) each target a single criterion and fail to generalize across diverse evaluation dimensions such as geometric detail, texture quality, or 3D plausibility. While user preference studies offer versatility and human alignment, they are costly, time-consuming, and difficult to scale. The paper asks whether an automatic metric can be both versatile across user-defined criteria and closely aligned with human judgment.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of text prompts (auto-generated via GPT-4V), multi-view 2D renderings of each 3D asset (4 or 9 viewpoints, each with both RGB and world-space surface normal images), and a user-defined evaluation criterion expressed as natural language. Because GPT-4V cannot consume 3D data directly, 3D shapes are projected into concatenated multi-view image grids. The output is a set of Elo ratings ranking the evaluated models, along with per-pair comparison decisions and GPT-4V's textual justifications for each choice.

## Method

The framework has two zero-shot components built entirely on GPT-4V prompting. First, a meta-prompt system instructs GPT-4V to compose evaluation prompts from a vocabulary of subjects, properties, and composition rules, with controllable axes of complexity and creativity to stress-test models under different difficulty levels. Second, a customizable instruction template guides GPT-4V to compare two 3D assets by feeding it concatenated multi-view RGB and world-space normal renderings alongside the criterion description, prompting chain-of-thought reasoning before the final verdict. To counter GPT-4V's stochastic sampling variance, a robust ensemble perturbs inputs (random seeds, render layout, view count, criterion phrasing) and aggregates multiple responses into a probability estimate per comparison. These pairwise probabilities are then converted into model-level Elo ratings via maximum likelihood estimation, adapting the formulation from Nichol et al. for image generation evaluation.

## Results

On the primary alignment metric (Kendall's tau ranking correlation with human expert annotations), the proposed GPT-4V-based method achieves an average of 0.710 across five evaluation criteria, outperforming the strongest baseline Aesthetic-Score (0.671) and all CLIP-based and PickScore variants (0.562–0.628). The method attains the best correlation on 4 of 5 criteria (Alignment 0.821, Plausibility 0.641, Texture Details 0.821, Geometry Details 0.795) and ties on Texture-Geometry Coherency (0.564). On the harder pairwise agreement task (L1 distance, lower is better), the method achieves the best average of 0.307, beating Aesthetic-Score (0.326), PickScore (0.374), and CLIP (0.400).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against three families of metrics: PickScore [32], CLIP similarity (both single-view and ensemble variants) [22], and Aesthetic Score (single-view and ensemble) [56]. The proposed GPT-4V method decisively outperforms PickScore on every criterion in both ranking correlation (avg 0.710 vs 0.562) and pairwise agreement (avg 0.307 vs 0.374). Against CLIP-based metrics, the gap is largest on Plausibility (0.641 vs 0.282–0.426), where CLIP embeddings are poorly suited to judge geometric coherence; CLIP-E comes closest on Alignment (0.813 vs 0.821) but still trails on all other criteria. The strongest baseline, Aesthetic-Score, is competitive on Texture Details (0.769 vs 0.821) and Geometry Details (0.744 vs 0.795) but is substantially weaker on Plausibility (0.410 vs 0.641) and Alignment (0.795 vs 0.821). On the pairwise L1 agreement metric, the proposed method wins on Alignment, Plausibility, and Texture criteria but concedes on T-G Coherency (0.369 vs 0.353) and Geometry (0.350 vs 0.311) to Aesthetic-Score, indicating that single-criterion metrics can still edge out the GPT-4V approach on narrow geometric sub-tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes GPT-4V as a 3D shape evaluator by feeding it multi-view RGB and surface-normal renderings, extending the LMM-as-judge paradigm established for 2D image and language tasks [70, 73, 74] into the 3D domain. It departs from single-criterion metrics like CLIP similarity and Aesthetic Score, which are rigidly tied to one evaluation axis, by making the comparison criterion a free-form language instruction. The Elo rating framework is adapted from image generation evaluation (GLIDE [42]), and the prompt generator complements prior fixed prompt sets (T3Bench [21], DreamFusion [50]) by making prompt distribution itself a controllable variable via a meta-prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work offers a practical, scalable alternative to costly user studies for ranking text-to-3D models across multiple criteria simultaneously, which is critical as the field rapidly expands with new methods (Instant3D, feed-forward models on Objaverse). However, the evidence base is limited—13 models, 20 annotators, and a small prompt set—making it unclear whether the alignment results hold at scale. Open questions include GPT-4V's susceptibility to positional bias and hallucination, the potential for adversarial gaming of the metric, and the quadratic cost of pairwise comparisons when evaluating many models.
