## Problem

SparseFormer achieves strong visual understanding with far fewer tokens than standard ViTs, but training it from scratch is expensive (e.g., ~12 A100 GPU days for a base variant) and scaling up remains challenging. The paper asks how to efficiently bootstrap SparseFormers from large-scale pre-trained vision foundation models so they inherit strong representations without lengthy training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241144/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During bootstrapping, the method consumes only unlabeled RGB images (ImageNet-1K training set, ~1.2M images) with minimal augmentation—no labels, captions, or text-image pairs are required. The bootstrapped SparseFormer produces a fixed set of latent token embeddings (e.g., 49 tokens) whose final [CLS] representation is aligned to the pre-trained model's output space. Downstream, these embeddings yield class labels, language-aligned visual embeddings for zero-shot/retrieval tasks, dense segmentation masks (via RoI-based projection back to pixel space), or visual tokens fed into an LLM for multimodal question answering.

## Method

The key insight is that the cortex transformer in SparseFormer is architecturally identical to a standard ViT encoder, so pre-trained weights from foundation models (AugReg, CLIP) can be directly inherited. The procedure: (1) truncate the leading 1/3 of transformer blocks (which overlap with the focusing transformer's low-level role), (2) randomly initialize the lightweight focusing transformer, (3) tune a middle set of inherited blocks while freezing the remaining ending blocks to preserve output-space structure, and (4) align the SparseFormer's final token representation to the frozen pre-trained model's output using a simple cosine loss over unlabeled images. This requires only 20 epochs on ImageNet-1K with minimal augmentation, completing in a few hours on 8 GPUs. For CLIP models, the same recipe aligns visual embeddings into the language-aligned space without ever seeing a caption, enabling zero-shot and retrieval capabilities as well as drop-in replacement as vision encoders in multimodal LLMs like LLaVA.

## Results

The bootstrapped SF-L AugReg with only 49 tokens achieves 84.9% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K with 11.6G FLOPs (93.9% reduction) and 1353 imgs/s throughput (1189% speedup) compared to the source ViT-L/16-384 AugReg (86.9%, 191G FLOPs, 105 imgs/s). For multimodal bootstrapping, SF-L CLIP with 144 tokens reaches 75.9% zero-shot accuracy on ImageNet-1K and 57.0% I→T@1 retrieval on MS COCO, just 0.7 points behind the full ViT-L/14-336 CLIP (76.6%) while using only 33.9G FLOPs (82.2% reduction). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, SF-L achieves 51.5 mIoU with 77G FLOPs and 46 imgs/s, matching ViT-Adapter-B (51.9 mIoU) at 5× lower FLOPs and 2.4× higher throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the source AugReg models, SF-L with 49 tokens achieves 84.9% vs. 86.9% for ViT-L/16-384 (a 2.0-point gap) but with 93.9% fewer FLOPs and 12.9× higher throughput; continuing bootstrapping to 196 tokens narrows the gap to 0.2 points (86.7% vs. 86.9%) at 75.7% FLOPs reduction. Against ToMe, SF-B with 80 tokens (83.4%) outperforms fine-tuned ToMe@16 (82.8%) by 0.6 points while using 29.5% fewer FLOPs (6.2G vs. 8.8G) and achieving 5% higher throughput; for the large model, SF-L 80 tokens (85.2%) beats fine-tuned ToMe@8 (84.8%) by 0.4 points at 40% fewer FLOPs (18.6G vs. 31.0G). Against DiffRate, SF-B 80 tokens (83.4%) edges out DiffRate (83.2%) with 46% fewer FLOPs (6.2G vs. 11.5G) and 49% higher throughput. For CLIP bootstrapping, SF-L with 144 tokens achieves 75.9% zero-shot accuracy, surpassing ViT-L/14-224 CLIP (75.4%) by 0.5 points at less than half the FLOPs (33.9G vs. 81.1G), and outperforms TinyCLIP (64.5%) by 1.5 points on zero-shot while using only 1.9× the FLOPs. In the LLaVA multimodal LLM setting, SF-L CLIP with 64 tokens achieves 89.2% on ScienceQA vs. 91.2% for the original ViT-L/16-224 encoder, a 2.0-point gap that narrows to 1.4 points at 128 tokens, while reducing visual tokens fed to the LLM by 75%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SparseFormer architecture [20], which introduces a focusing transformer that adjusts token RoIs to reduce visual tokens, and on large-scale pre-trained vision foundation models (AugReg [59], CLIP [49]) as weight sources. The key departure from prior work is the 'bootstrapping' procedure: rather than training SparseFormers from scratch (as in [20]) or using knowledge distillation with labels (as in [26, 63, 70]), the authors inherit pre-trained weights into the cortex transformer blocks and align only the final representations via cosine loss on unlabeled images. This explicitly differentiates from distillation methods that require label sets or vision-language pairs, and from token reduction methods like ToMe [2] and DiffRate [6] that operate post-hoc on dense transformers rather than restructuring the architecture from the start.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: bootstrapping a 200M-parameter SparseFormer takes only ~6 hours on 8 GPUs with 1.2M unlabeled images, yet achieves near-parity accuracy (84.9% vs 86.9% for ViT-L/16-384) at 12× throughput, and the CLIP-bootstrapped variant preserves zero-shot language alignment without ever seeing a caption. The ability to drop-in replace CLIP vision encoders in MLLMs like LLaVA without retraining the LLM opens a path to substantially cheaper multimodal inference. Limitations include the requirement for transformer-based foundation models with publicly available weights, and the method's dependence on the cortex transformer being architecturally identical to the source ViT. Open questions include whether bootstrapping can be extended to non-transformer backbones (e.g., ConvNeXt [40]) or to proprietary models where weights are inaccessible.
