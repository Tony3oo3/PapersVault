## Problem

Probabilistic copyright protection methods like NAF (CP-k) provide a low upper-bound on the probability of generating infringing content for a single generation, but they offer no guarantee under repeated interactions. In real-world deployment, a malicious user can query a text-to-image model many times, and this paper shows that such persistent engagement amplifies the infringement probability to near-certainty, exposing a fundamental vulnerability in the protection paradigm.

## Input / Output

The attacker takes a target copyrighted image y_C and a set of candidate prompts (target caption, PEZ, CLIP-Interrogator, or Anti-NAF-generated) as input, interacting T times (100–500 steps) with the CP-k-protected model to produce 512×512 images. The Anti-NAF optimizer outputs an 8-token adversarial prompt by optimizing in a continuous embedding space and projecting to discrete vocabulary tokens via cosine similarity. The final attack output is the highest-scoring sample among all T generations, selected by a similarity-based scoring function (SSCD) against the target image.

## Method

VA3 combines a theoretical amplification guarantee (Theorem 1: success probability ≥ 1−ε when T > log_{1−σ}(ε) for any strictly positive single-shot success lower-bound σ) with two practical components. First, an online prompt selection strategy modeled as a multi-armed bandit problem, using ε-greedy-max and ε-greedy-cdf variants to balance exploration and exploitation across candidate prompts. Second, Anti-NAF, a gradient-based adversarial prompt optimizer that minimizes the NAF criterion ρ(y_C|x) subject to a reconstruction constraint p(y_C|x) ≥ η, using a weighted objective L = λ·max(L_p, φ) + (1−λ)·max_q(L_q) where L_p drives the generative model toward the target and L_q drives safe models toward it (reducing the log-likelihood ratio), with a loss clip φ to resolve their conflicting gradients. Optimization proceeds in continuous embedding space with per-step projection to discrete tokens, requiring white-box access to both the generative and safe model denoising networks.

## Results

On POKEMON, Anti-NAF with amplification achieves a FAR@5%AR of 77.36% and FAR@15%AR of 91.36%, far exceeding all baselines (best baseline: CLIP-Interrogator at 22.84% and 47.32% respectively). On LAION-mi, Anti-NAF with amplification reaches FAR@50%AR of 95.92%, compared to 75.60% for CLIP-Interrogator and 59.64% for PEZ. Without amplification, Anti-NAF already outperforms other prompts on POKEMON (FAR@5%AR: 8.52% vs. ≤1.32% for baselines), and the amplification step further amplifies this gap by roughly 9×. The copyright infringement rate (CIR) reaches 99.84–100% with amplification across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Anti-NAF against three threat-prompt baselines: the target caption, CLIP-Interrogator, and PEZ [53], each evaluated both with and without the amplification (bandit) step. Without amplification, Anti-NAF already dominates on POKEMON, achieving FAR@5%AR of 8.52% versus 0.40% (Caption), 0.84% (CLIP-Int.), and 1.32% (PEZ), indicating that the NAF-aware optimization is critical for evading CP-k. With amplification, Anti-NAF achieves FAR@5%AR of 77.36% on POKEMON, roughly 3.4× higher than the next-best baseline (CLIP-Interrogator at 22.84%) and 3.4× higher than PEZ (22.80%). On LAION-mi, Anti-NAF with amplification reaches FAR@50%AR of 95.92%, outperforming CLIP-Interrogator (75.60%) by 20.3 points and PEZ (59.64%) by 36.3 points. Notably, the target caption with amplification achieves 100% CIR but only 13.64% FAR@5%AR on POKEMON, confirming that simply repeating a benign prompt is far less effective than the NAF-aware adversarial optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly targets the NAF/CP-k probabilistic copyright protection framework of Vyas et al. [52], which provides a formal per-generation upper bound on infringement probability. It departs from prior diffusion-model attack work (poisoning, concept-removal red-teaming, safety-filter evasion) by focusing on a method with provable guarantees rather than heuristic protections. The Anti-NAF optimization algorithm adopts the continuous-to-discrete embedding projection technique from PEZ [53], while the online prompt selection is framed as a multi-armed bandit problem—a formulation not previously applied to adversarial prompt discovery. The key conceptual departure is the 'amplification' perspective: treating the attacker as engaging in sustained multi-turn interaction rather than a single-shot attack, which fundamentally changes the threat model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that any per-generation probabilistic guarantee becomes vacuous under sustained interaction—is a critical caveat for deploying CP-k or similar methods in real-world APIs where users make many requests. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) is clean but almost tautological; the substantive contribution is demonstrating via Theorem 2 and Anti-NAF that the required positive lower bound on single-shot success actually exists in practice. Limitations are significant: Anti-NAF requires white-box access to both the generative and safe models, experiments use fine-tuned Stable Diffusion on small datasets (833 and 5000 images) rather than full-scale models, and the attack is specific to NAF/CP-k. Open questions include whether black-box variants exist, whether online prompt optimization (beyond the bandit selection studied here) further strengthens the attack, and whether the amplification concept generalizes to other probabilistic protection schemes.
