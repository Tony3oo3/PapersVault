## Problem

Finetuning CLIP on downstream tasks causes severe degradation of out-of-distribution generalization in both domain shift and zero-shot learning, because the finetuning target provides only simplified class-label text (e.g., 'a photo of a [CLASS]') rather than the rich semantic supervision used during pretraining. Prior robust finetuning methods addressed domain shift but neglected zero-shot capability, leaving a gap in preserving the full OOD generalization of vision-language models.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (224×224 for ViT-B/16) as input and produces class labels via cosine similarity between image embeddings and text prompt embeddings. During finetuning, each training image is consumed three ways: paired with its class prompt for the primary contrastive loss, paired with a BLIP2-generated caption for the text-compensated anchor loss, and used as a query to retrieve a semantically related image-text pair from CC3M for the retrieval anchor loss. Evaluation covers in-distribution classification, domain-shift variants (e.g., ImageNet-Sketch, DomainNet-Clipart), and zero-shot recognition on 10 unseen datasets.

## Method

ARF regularizes CLIP finetuning with two complementary anchor mechanisms that supply rich semantic supervision absent from class prompts. The Text-Compensated Anchor Generation (TCAG) module uses a pretrained captioner (BLIP2) to generate descriptive captions for each training image, and a contrastive loss aligns image embeddings with these captions to prevent features from collapsing toward class centers. The Image-Text Anchor Retrieval (ITAR) module performs KNN search (via Faiss) over the CC3M dataset using CLIP's cross-modal retrieval to find image-text pairs semantically related to the downstream task, then aligns them with a second contrastive loss to preserve the original feature space. The total training objective is L = L_CL + L_Cap + L_Ret, where all three terms use the same InfoNCE-style contrastive formulation, and both the image and text encoders are jointly finetuned with a small learning rate (1e-5) and weight decay (0.1).

## Results

On ImageNet, ARF achieves 82.7% top-1 in-distribution accuracy, matching FLYP (82.6%) and exceeding standard finetuning (81.3%). For domain shift, ARF attains 61.3% average accuracy across five ImageNet variants (V2, Sketch, A, R, ObjectNet) and 65.2% on DomainNet, both state-of-the-art among finetuning methods. Most notably, on zero-shot learning across 10 diverse recognition tasks, ARF achieves 55.6% average OOD accuracy, substantially outperforming FLYP (48.6%), LP-FT (51.0%), and full finetuning (35.7%), while remaining closest to the unfinetuned CLIP baseline (66.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against full finetuning (FT), ARF dramatically outperforms on zero-shot learning (55.6% vs. 35.7% avg OOD) and domain shift (61.3% vs. 54.6% on ImageNet variants), while matching ID accuracy (82.7% vs. 81.3%). Compared to LP-FT, ARF gains 4.6 points on zero-shot (55.6% vs. 51.0%) and 1.7 points on domain shift (61.3% vs. 59.6%). Against FLYP, the closest robust finetuning baseline, ARF improves zero-shot accuracy by 7.0 points (55.6% vs. 48.6%) and domain shift by 1.9 points (61.3% vs. 59.4%), with a marginal 0.1-point ID gain. The unfinetuned CLIP baseline retains the highest zero-shot accuracy (66.6%) but lags significantly on ID (68.3%), confirming that ARF recovers most of the finetuning benefit while preserving the majority of CLIP's OOD capability. On DomainNet, ARF achieves 65.2% avg OOD, surpassing FLYP (64.3%) and LP-FT (63.8%) without sacrificing ID accuracy (89.8%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust finetuning line of work (Wise-FT, LP-FT, FLYP, TPGM) that aims to preserve CLIP's out-of-distribution generalization during downstream adaptation. While prior robust finetuning methods focused exclusively on domain shift, this paper deliberately generalizes the problem to also preserve zero-shot learning capability, framing the root cause as 'semantic-scarce supervision' from class-only prompts during finetuning. The method builds directly on FLYP's contrastive finetuning paradigm and augments it with two auxiliary anchor mechanisms: captions generated by a pretrained captioner (BLIP2) and image-text pairs retrieved from CC3M via CLIP's cross-modal retrieval, both providing the rich semantic supervision that CLIP's pretraining originally relied on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: practitioners who finetune CLIP for a specific task lose its broad zero-shot and domain-shift capabilities, and prior robust finetuning methods did not address this. The anchor-based regularization is a clean, modular addition to the FLYP training loop that requires no architectural changes. However, the absolute zero-shot numbers remain far below CLIP's original performance (e.g., 46.4% on Flowers102 vs. 70.4% for unfinetuned CLIP), indicating the problem is far from fully solved. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether the CC3M candidate set can be replaced with task-agnostic retrieval, and whether the reliance on a separate captioner (BLIP2) can be eliminated.
