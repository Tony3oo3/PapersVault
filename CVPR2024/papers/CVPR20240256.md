## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods are designed for closed-set scenarios where test data contains only known classes, yet real-world deployments inevitably encounter unknown classes (semantic shift) alongside covariate shifts. The paper shows that applying standard entropy-minimization TTA to open-set data degrades both classification accuracy on known classes and OOD detection performance on unknown classes, due to unreliable normalization statistics and destroyed model confidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240256/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on corrupted RGB test images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100-C, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet-C) arriving in an online, continual fashion without labels. Each mini-batch contains a mixture of covariate-shifted in-distribution (csID) and covariate-shifted out-of-distribution (csOOD) samples. The adapted model outputs a class prediction for known classes and an energy-based OOD score for detecting unknown classes, evaluated via accuracy, AUROC, FPR@TPR95, and OSCR.

## Method

UniEnt introduces a distribution-aware filter that computes a csOOD score via cosine similarity between source-model features and source-domain prototypes, then fits a two-component Gaussian mixture model to the score distribution to softly partition each mini-batch into pseudo-csID and pseudo-csOOD subsets. The unified optimization objective simultaneously minimizes prediction entropy on pseudo-csID samples (improving classification) and maximizes entropy on pseudo-csOOD samples (preserving detection confidence), regularized by marginal entropy to prevent collapse. UniEnt+ replaces the hard partition with sample-level GMM posterior weights, enabling a single weighted sum over all samples. The framework is plug-and-play and can be applied to TENT, EATA, OSTTA, and CoTTA without architectural modification, updating only batch-normalization affine parameters (or all parameters for CoTTA) via Adam at test time.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C, EATA+UniEnt+ achieves the best OSCR of 83.67% (vs. 72.63% for EATA alone), with AUROC of 96.97% and FPR@TPR95 of 14.28%. On CIFAR-100-C, EATA+UniEnt+ reaches OSCR 58.19% (vs. 57.24% for EATA), AUROC 94.23%, and FPR@TPR95 26.87%. The average OSCR across both datasets is 70.93% for EATA+UniEnt+, outperforming all baselines including the open-set-specific OSTTA (58.26%). The most dramatic gains are in detection metrics: UniEnt+ improves FPR@TPR95 by up to 63.20% (TENT on CIFAR-10-C) and AUROC by up to 27.79% over the unmodified baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines: Source (no adaptation), BN Adapt (BN statistics update only), CoTTA (teacher-student with augmentation), TENT (entropy minimization), EATA (sample selection + anti-forgetting), and OSTTA (open-set TTA via wisdom of crowds). UniEnt/UniEnt+ consistently outperforms all baselines on OSCR across both CIFAR-10-C and CIFAR-100-C, with the largest gains in detection metrics (AUROC and FPR@TPR95). Notably, TENT and OSTTA actually degrade below Source and BN Adapt in OSCR under open-set conditions (e.g., TENT drops to 56.73% OSCR on CIFAR-10-C vs. 68.44% for Source), confirming that naive entropy minimization harms open-set performance. EATA+UniEnt+ achieves the best overall OSCR (83.67% on CIFAR-10-C, 58.19% on CIFAR-100-C), while OSTTA+UniEnt+ achieves the best FPR@TPR95 (11.56% on CIFAR-10-C). CoTTA, which does not use entropy-based adaptation, is not directly augmented by UniEnt but serves as a strong closed-set baseline (OSCR 77.26% on CIFAR-10-C) that UniEnt+ still surpasses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time adaptation (TTA) literature, which has primarily addressed covariate shift via entropy minimization (TENT, CoTTA, EATA) or batch normalization updates (BN Adapt), to the open-set setting where test data may contain unknown classes. It builds directly on OSTTA's formulation of open-set TTA with covariate-shifted OOD (csOOD) samples and borrows the entropy-maximization principle from OOD detection methods (Outlier Exposure, WOODS, AUTO) to counteract the confidence destruction caused by minimizing entropy on unknown-class samples. The distribution-aware filter, which uses cosine similarity to source-domain prototypes and a two-component GMM for thresholding, is a new component not present in prior TTA or OOD detection work, and the unified loss combining per-sample entropy minimization and maximization weighted by GMM posterior probabilities (UniEnt+) is the paper's core methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of UniEnt/UniEnt+—applicable to TENT, EATA, and OSTTA without architectural changes—makes it immediately useful for practitioners deploying TTA in realistic scenarios where unknown classes are inevitable. However, all experiments are conducted on corruption benchmarks (CIFAR-C, Tiny-ImageNet-C) with a single corruption type per run, leaving open whether the GMM-based bimodal assumption on the csOOD score holds under more diverse or compound domain shifts. The paper also does not explore how the method behaves when the proportion of unknown classes is very small or when the semantic gap between known and unknown classes is narrow, which are critical for real-world robustness. Future work could extend the framework to video, multi-modal, or streaming settings where the open-set composition changes over time.
