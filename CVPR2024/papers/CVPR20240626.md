## Problem

Microscopy defocus deblur faces two challenges absent from natural-scene deblurring: (1) microscopy images exhibit a significantly longer attention span (normalized average attention distance ~0.3–0.35 vs. <0.25 for real-world scenes), demanding larger receptive fields than standard local window attention provides; and (2) high-quality blur–sharp training pairs are extremely difficult to acquire, especially in surgical microscopy where the imaging principle makes simultaneous capture of blurred and sharp counterparts impossible.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB defocus-blurred microscopy image as input and outputs a deblurred image of the same spatial dimensions. Training operates on 256×256 patches with random scaling and flip augmentation. The framework is domain-agnostic, applied to cell microscopy (BBBC006, 3DHistech), surgical microscopy (CaDISBlur, CataBlur), and real-world defocus datasets (DPDD, LFDOF) without architectural changes. For unsupervised evaluation, the model trained on a labeled dataset is applied to unlabeled microscopy datasets (WNLO, CataBlur) using EFCR_ex for cross-domain latent signal extraction.

## Method

The MPT builds an explicit coarse-to-fine pyramid at every U-shaped network stage, where each sub-block combines three components: CSWA models attention between a local-scale query and a down-scaled key-value map via cross-scale multiplication, quadratically enlarging the receptive field by (S_j)² while maintaining O(M²H'W'C') complexity; ISCA computes channel-wise self-attention with 1×1 and 3×3 convolutional projections to inject global channel context; and FEFN fuses the spatial feature from CSWA with the channel feature from ISCA through an asymmetric GELU-activated element-wise multiplication rather than simple addition. The EFCR training strategy decomposes images into Haar wavelet frequency bands and constructs contrastive pairs: basic CR pulls the deblurred output toward ground truth in both high- and low-frequency bands while pushing it away from the blurred input in the high-frequency band, and extended CR applies random Gaussian reblurring to the input to generate synthetic pairs whose high-frequency residual provides a relative loss term capturing latent deblur direction beyond pixel-level supervision. EFCR_ex further enables cross-domain knowledge transfer by building the same contrastive structure on high-frequency components from an external dataset, facilitating both extra-data pretraining and unlabeled-image deblur without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the four microscopy deblur benchmarks, MPT+EFCR_ex achieves the best PSNR across all datasets: 35.16 dB (BBBC006w1), 30.11 dB (BBBC006w2), 33.63 dB (3DHistech), and 45.25 dB (CaDISBlur), with corresponding SSIM values of 0.935, 0.829, 0.892, and 0.971. On the real-world DPDD dataset, MPT+EFCR_ex reaches 26.27 dB PSNR and 0.831 SSIM, outperforming the best baseline GRL (26.18 dB, 0.822 SSIM). The MPT architecture (76 FLOPs, 19.80M params) surpasses Restormer (141 FLOPs, 26.12M) by 0.10 dB PSNR on BBBC006w1 while using 46% fewer FLOPs and 24% fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPT+EFCR_ex outperforms all 11 baselines on every microscopy dataset. Against the strongest transformer-based competitors, MPT+EFCR_ex beats Restormer [84] by 0.37 dB PSNR on BBBC006w1 (35.16 vs 34.79) and 0.40 dB on CaDISBlur (45.25 vs 44.85), while also improving SSIM by 0.031 and 0.030 respectively. GRL [36], which models global spatial attention, is the second-best on 3DHistech and CaDISBlur but requires 1230 FLOPs (17× more than MPT's 76 FLOPs) and still trails MPT+EFCR_ex by 0.14 dB and 0.39 dB PSNR on those datasets. Multi-scale methods PANet [51], MSSNet [31], and MIMO-UNet [7] are consistently outperformed by 0.7–2.4 dB PSNR, confirming the advantage of the explicit multi-pyramid design over implicit multi-stage aggregation. On the real-world DPDD benchmark, MPT+EFCR_ex achieves 26.27 dB PSNR, edging out GRL (26.18) and NRKNet [57] (26.11), demonstrating cross-domain generalizability. Notably, the EFCR_ex knowledge transfer strategy yields substantially larger gains (+0.27 dB on BBBC006w1) than a simple pretrain-then-finetune approach (MPT+pretrain), which shows trivial or even negative improvement (−0.02 dB on BBBC006w1).

## Relation to Prior Work

The MPT architecture extends Swin Transformer's window attention by introducing cross-scale window attention (CSWA) that models interaction between local-scale queries and down-scaled key-value pairs, quadratically enlarging the receptive field without increasing computational cost. The EFCR training strategy builds on the frequency-band contrastive regularization concept from multi-scale frequency separation networks [90], extending it with synthetic reblurring to exploit latent deblur signals and enabling cross-domain knowledge transfer. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior multi-scale deblur methods (explicit pyramids like PANet, implicit multi-stage designs like MSSNet) by constructing multiple explicit pyramids at every network stage, and from prior contrastive deblur work by operating in the Haar wavelet domain with a relative-loss formulation that normalizes for blur variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of CataBlur, the first real surgical microscopy defocus dataset, and the demonstration that cross-domain contrastive transfer (EFCR_ex) outperforms straightforward pretrain-then-finetune on data-scarce microscopy tasks provide practical tools for clinical imaging where blur-sharp pairs are impossible to acquire. Downstream validation on cell detection and surgical scene segmentation connects the deblur quality to clinically meaningful outcomes. Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger and more diverse microscopy domains, whether the wavelet-domain contrastive formulation generalizes beyond defocus to other degradation types, and how the method performs under weakly or fully unsupervised settings as the authors themselves note as future work.
