## Problem

Existing metrics for evaluating generative models, particularly FID, are misaligned with human perception of naturalness: FID has a null space where scores remain unchanged despite shifts in image naturalness, and its feature-distance formulation cannot distinguish between two images at the same feature distance that differ substantially in realism. The paper asks whether the geometric structure of the representation space around an image—specifically its non-linearity and sensitivity to adversarial perturbations—can serve as a more faithful proxy for naturalness.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a generated image (or a set of 10,000 generated images for model-level evaluation), a reference set of real images from the same domain, and a frozen pre-trained feature model (e.g., DINO-V2, ConvNeXt-tiny, ViT-S). Each image requires 20 forward passes through the feature model (K=10 noise steps for complexity plus J=10 PGD steps for vulnerability), compared to a single pass for FID. The model-level output is a single scalar AS in [0,1] from a 2D Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic; the individual-image output AS-i is an unbounded positive ratio where lower values indicate more natural images.

## Method

The core contribution is two complementary probes of the representation space around an image. Complexity C(x) measures the average turning angle of successive feature displacements as a fixed unit Gaussian noise vector is added incrementally (x^k = x + kεN), capturing how curved the feature trajectory is—real images yield larger angles (more curvature) than generated ones. Vulnerability V(x) measures the L2 feature displacement after a modified PGD attack that maximizes L2 distance between the original and perturbed features rather than cross-entropy, exploiting the observation that unnatural image regions are disproportionately sensitive to adversarial perturbations. For model-level evaluation, AS is the 2D KS statistic between the joint (C, V) distributions of the real and generated sets; for individual images, AS-i is simply the ratio V(x)/C(x), which is small when an image sits in a complex, robust region of feature space (natural) and large when it sits in a flat, fragile region (unnatural). No training is involved; all scores are computed from frozen pre-trained vision models.

## Results

On the overall dataset (CIFAR-10, ImageNet, FFHQ combined) using DINO-V2 as the feature model, the proposed 2D Anomaly Score achieves a Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) of -0.806 and SRCC of -0.738 with human error rate, substantially outperforming FID (-0.540 PCC, -0.525 SRCC). On FFHQ specifically, AS with DINO-V2 reaches -0.56 PCC versus FID's -0.38 PCC. For individual image evaluation (AS-i), the method achieves -0.88 PCC with human naturalness judgments, compared to -0.49 for Rarity Score and 0.63 for Realism Score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: FID (for generative model evaluation) and Rarity Score and Realism Score (for individual image evaluation). AS clearly outperforms FID on the overall dataset, achieving -0.806 PCC versus FID's -0.540 PCC with DINO-V2, and also wins on FFHQ (-0.56 vs -0.38 PCC). On CIFAR-10, AS with DINO-V2 reaches -0.98 PCC, far exceeding FID. For individual images, AS-i achieves -0.88 PCC with human naturalness judgments, substantially outperforming Rarity Score (-0.49 PCC) and Realism Score (0.63 PCC, which has the wrong sign, indicating it correlates positively with unnaturalness). The 1D ablation shows that using both complexity and vulnerability together (2D KS) is necessary for the best performance, as 1D-Complexity alone drops to -0.376 PCC while 1D-Vulnerability reaches -0.790 PCC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of generative model evaluation (FID, Precision/Recall, rarity score) that measures distances or set-level statistics between feature distributions of real and generated images. Instead, it draws on two distinct threads: the observation from Kim et al. (AAAI 2024) that the representation space around training data is locally curved (complex), and the PGD adversarial attack framework (Madry et al., ICLR 2018), which is repurposed here not for robustness training but as a diagnostic probe measuring how easily extracted features shift under targeted perturbations. The key conceptual move is shifting from global distributional comparison to local geometric properties (curvature and adversarial sensitivity) of the representation space around individual images, then aggregating via a 2D Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented failure mode of FID and related metrics—their misalignment with human-perceived naturalness—and provides a metric (AS) that achieves substantially better correlation with subjective judgments (PCC −0.81 vs. −0.54 for FID). The individual-image variant (AS-i = V/C) offers a practical tool for per-sample quality assessment that outperforms rarity and realism scores in subjective tests. However, the 20× inference overhead per image (K+J forward passes) is a significant practical limitation compared to FID's single pass, and performance varies notably across feature model choices (e.g., complexity alone fails with ConvNeXt and DINO-V2). Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, whether the computational cost can be reduced via approximate curvature estimation, and whether the adversarial-probe component generalizes to feature models trained with different objectives.
