## Problem

Implicit function-based methods for clothed 3D human reconstruction inevitably produce artifacts (holes, broken parts, non-human shapes) because they lack body-structure priors, while existing deformation-based methods cannot handle loose clothing due to the contradiction between the large deformations needed to fit garments and the delicate vertex shifts required to recover surface details. No prior method successfully reconstructs high-fidelity, artifact-free clothed humans from a single image under both challenging poses and loose clothing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, from which it estimates SMPL-X parameters (via PyMAF-X) and front/back clothing normal maps (via ECON's normal estimation pipeline). The output is a watertight 2-manifold triangular mesh of the clothed human, guaranteed to have no holes, broken parts, or disembodied limbs because the pipeline only shifts vertices while preserving the original edge topology throughout both deformation stages.

## Method

VS employs a two-stage 'stretch-refine' vertex-shift strategy. In the StretchVS stage, a 5-layer CNN (ShiftEstNet) infers 2D displacement fields by establishing pixel-wise correspondences between rendered body normal maps and estimated clothing normal maps, trained self-supervisedly with a cycle-wrapping consistency loss; these fields shift SMPL-X vertices in the X-Y plane to produce a coarse model that handles large deformations like loose clothing. In the RefineVS stage, an 11-layer graph convolutional network (DeformNet) based on MeshCNN's edge-based convolution, embedded with pixel-aligned implicit-function features from a pre-trained PIFuHD encoder, delicately deforms the coarse mesh to recover surface details while neighborhood vertex constraints suppress distortions and non-human shapes. The key insight is that separating large and small deformations into distinct stages resolves the fundamental tension that prevents single-stage deformation methods from handling both loose garments and fine geometric details simultaneously.

## Results

On the three benchmark datasets (THuman2.0, CAPE, RenderPeople), VS achieves the best Chamfer distance (0.628/0.621/0.976 cm) and point-to-surface distance (0.555/0.615/0.788 cm) across all settings, outperforming the previous SOTA ECON by 30–39% on Chamfer distance. On local surface detail metrics, VS matches or exceeds ECON on cosine distance (0.0373 vs 0.0379 on THuman2.0; 0.0262 vs 0.0335 on CAPE) and L2 normal error (0.1555 vs 0.1891 on THuman2.0; 0.1138 vs 0.1644 on CAPE). In a perceptual study on in-the-wild SHHQ and VcgPeople images, 58.1% and 64.4% of participants respectively preferred VS over all other methods, with the next-best (ECON) receiving only 19.0% and 24.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VS outperforms all five baselines on the large-geometric-error metrics (Chamfer distance and point-to-surface distance) across all three benchmarks, with the largest margins over PIFu (2.8× lower Chamfer on THuman2.0) and PIFuHD (4.9× lower Chamfer on CAPE). Against the closest competitor ECON (CVPR'23), VS reduces Chamfer distance by 31% on THuman2.0 (0.628 vs 0.906), 34% on CAPE (0.621 vs 0.937), and 24% on RenderPeople (0.976 vs 1.285). On local detail metrics, VS matches ECON on THuman2.0 cosine distance (0.0373 vs 0.0379) and slightly trails on RenderPeople cosine (0.0419 vs 0.0417), but clearly wins on CAPE cosine (0.0262 vs 0.0335) and L2 normal error across all three datasets. ICON and PaMIR are consistently outperformed by VS on every metric, with ICON's advantage on CAPE cosine (0.0311) being the only case where a non-ECON baseline comes close on a detail metric. The perceptual study confirms these quantitative findings: VS is preferred by 58.1% (SHHQ) and 64.4% (VcgPeople) of participants, roughly 3× the preference for ECON (19.0% and 24.7%).

## Relation to Prior Work

VS sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: implicit-function-based methods (PIFu, PIFuHD, ECON, ICON) that achieve high detail but produce topological artifacts, and deformation-based methods (Pix2Mesh, MeshCNN) that guarantee watertight meshes but have been considered insufficient for loose clothing. The paper builds directly on MeshCNN's edge-based convolution for its GCN (DeformNet) and adopts ECON's normal-map estimation pipeline, but departs from both by introducing a two-stage 'stretch-refine' strategy: displacement fields inferred from normal maps (trained with cycle consistency) handle large deformations for loose clothing, while an IF-feature-driven GCN recovers fine surface details. This reconciles what prior work treated as a fundamental trade-off between geometric flexibility and artifact-free topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VS demonstrates that pure deformation methods can match or exceed IF-based methods in reconstruction fidelity while structurally guaranteeing artifact-free output (no holes, broken parts, or dismembered limbs), which is practically important for downstream applications like virtual avatars and animation. The robustness to inaccurate SMPL-X estimates (StretchVS acts as an error-correcting stage) reduces the sensitivity that plagues most body-aware methods. Remaining open questions include handling extreme clothing (e.g., full-length dresses), distinguishing body from clothing layers, and correcting large Z-axis depth errors—issues the authors acknowledge and propose addressing via separate body/clothing reconstruction with preset garment models.
