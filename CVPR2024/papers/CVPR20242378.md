## Problem

Prior conditional control methods for image generative models (adaptive normalization, cross-attention, self-attention) all operate by manipulating intermediate features while keeping network weights static across all conditions and timesteps. This static-weight design limits performance due to negative transfer between different subtasks (e.g., generating cat images vs. castle images, or denoising at different diffusion timesteps). The paper asks whether directly manipulating the network's convolution/linear weights based on the input condition can provide a more effective and efficient mechanism for conditional control.

## Input / Output

The model takes a condition embedding (class label plus diffusion timestep for ImageNet experiments, or CLIP-encoded text prompt plus timestep for COCO) and random Gaussian noise as input, and produces a generated RGB image at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. The condition embedding is consumed by a lightweight weight-generation module that produces conditional weights for a subset of layers, rather than being injected into the feature stream. Sampling uses DPM-Solver (50 steps) or UniPC (20 steps) with classifier-free guidance, and the CaT family achieves 10–12G MACs per diffusion step versus 525G for DiT-XL/2.

## Method

CAN introduces a conditional weight generation module that maps the condition embedding to a conditional weight W_c, which is added element-wise to the static weight W of selected convolution/linear layers and fused into a single kernel call. Rather than making all layers condition-aware, ablation studies identify an effective subset: depthwise convolutions in FFN, the patch embedding layer, and the output projection layers in self-attention (which share one conditional weight generator to control parameter overhead). An efficient implementation fuses per-sample kernel calls into a grouped convolution with batch-to-channel and channel-to-batch transformations, reducing training overhead to 30–40%. The class label contributes more to weight generation than the timestep (5.15 FID improvement vs. 13.16), and CAN outperforms adaptive kernel selection (CondConv-style) by directly generating weights rather than scaling a fixed set of base kernels. Marrying CAN with EfficientViT's linear-complexity attention yields the CaT family, achieving 2.78 FID on ImageNet 512×512 with 52× fewer MACs per step than DiT-XL/2.

## Results

On ImageNet 512×512, CaT-L0 achieves 2.78 FID with only 10G MACs per sampling step and 20 steps, surpassing DiT-XL/2 (3.04 FID, 525G MACs, 250 steps) while requiring 52× fewer MACs per step. On ImageNet 256×256, CaT-B0 reaches 2.09 FID, outperforming both DiT-XL/2 (2.27) and UViT-H/2 (2.29) with 9.9× and 11.1× fewer MACs respectively. On COCO 256×256 text-to-image, CaT-S0 achieves 5.49 FID with 3G MACs, matching UViT-S-Dep/2 (5.48 FID) at 19G MACs. On NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, CaT-L0 runs 229× faster than DiT-XL/2 (0.2s vs 45.9s) while delivering a better FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiT-XL/2 [14], CaT-L0 achieves a better FID (2.78 vs 3.04) on ImageNet 512×512 while using 52× fewer MACs per step (10G vs 525G) and 12.5× fewer sampling steps (20 vs 250); on the Orin edge device, CaT-L0 is 229× faster (0.2s vs 45.9s). Against UViT [12], CAN(UViT-S-Dep/4) matches UViT-H/4's FID (4.04 vs 4.05) at only 12% of the per-step computational cost (16G vs 133G MACs), and CaT-S0 matches UViT-S-Dep/2 on COCO (5.49 vs 5.48 FID) while reducing MACs from 19G to 3G. Against ADM/ADM-U [27], CaT-L0 substantially outperforms both on ImageNet 512×512 (2.78 vs 7.72 and 3.85 FID) with orders-of-magnitude lower per-step cost. Against LDM-4 [1], CaT-B0 achieves a lower FID on ImageNet 256×256 (2.09 vs 3.60) with 12G MACs per step versus LDM's unspecified (but substantially higher) cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of conditional control in generative models, which relies on feature-space manipulation (adaptive normalization, cross-attention, self-attention) while keeping network weights static. CAN introduces weight-space manipulation as an alternative mechanism, where a lightweight module generates conditional weights for selected convolution/linear layers based on the input condition. Conceptually, it connects to dynamic neural networks (CondConv, mixture-of-experts) but repurposes the idea specifically for conditional control in diffusion transformers. For the CaT model family, it builds directly on EfficientViT's linear-complexity multi-scale attention architecture to achieve high-resolution generation at low computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 52× MACs reduction per sampling step (and 229× latency reduction on Jetson AGX Orin) while matching or exceeding DiT-XL/2's FID opens a realistic path for deploying diffusion-based image generation on edge devices, a major practical bottleneck for the field. The finding that CAN alone outperforms adaptive normalization and attention-based conditioning by large margins suggests that prior conditional control methods may have been suboptimal, and that weight-space adaptation is an underexplored axis. Limitations include evaluation only on ImageNet (class-conditional) and COCO 256×256 (text-to-image), with large-scale text-to-image and video generation left as future work; the 30–40% training overhead from the grouped-convolution implementation also remains a practical concern until custom CUDA kernels are written.
