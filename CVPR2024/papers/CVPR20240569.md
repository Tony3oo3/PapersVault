## Problem

Conventional face and periocular biometric systems face a fundamental deployment dilemma: multimodal fusion requires all modalities to be simultaneously available, while unimodal systems cannot exploit complementary information across modalities. Existing cross-modality approaches (e.g., HA-ViT) focus narrowly on one matching direction and introduce a trade-off where improving intra-modality accuracy degrades cross-modality performance. The paper addresses this by asking whether a single model can produce modality-invariant embeddings that support flexible intra- and cross-modality matching without requiring both inputs at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240569/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes three inputs: a face image (3×112×112, split into 14 patches of 8×8), a periocular image (same dimensions), and a 47-dimensional soft-biometric attribute vector (gender, age, ethnicity, etc.) processed through a feature tokenizer. At deployment, only one modality (face or periocular) is fed to the network, which outputs a modality-invariant embedding J obtained by averaging the multimodal prompt token embeddings across MFA blocks and applying average pooling. Identity is then determined by cosine similarity against a gallery of stored embeddings, enabling matching regardless of which modality is available.

## Method

The core architecture is MFA-ViT, a shared-parameter Vision Transformer where each MFA block contains four layers of 3×3 depth-wise separable convolution, a depth-wise fusion Conv-based multi-head self-attention (DWFC-MSA) layer, 1×1 convolution, and LeakyReLU, operating on concatenated class, patch, and prompt token embeddings. The key novelty is Multimodal Prompt Tuning (MPT): learnable prompt tokens injected at every layer that provide modality-specific guidance for aligning face, periocular, and soft-biometric features, unlike standard Visual Prompt Tuning which lacks cross-modality awareness. Training uses a dual loss combining large-margin softmax (shaping intra-modality discriminability for face and periocular separately) and a three-way contrastive loss (pulling same-identity embeddings together and pushing different-identity embeddings apart across all three modality pairs). The classification head uses the aggregated prompt embeddings (PRM) rather than the standard ViT class token, which ablation shows yields consistently better cross-modality alignment. GradCAM visualizations confirm the model focuses on the shared ocular region across face and periocular inputs.

## Results

MFA-ViT/MPT achieves the best rank-1 recognition accuracy across all four datasets and all four task configurations (f-f, p-p, f-p, p-f). On the easiest dataset (FaceScrub), it reaches 95.71% (f-f), 93.06% (p-p), 90.38% (f-p), and 92.02% (p-f). On the most challenging dataset (Cross-Modal DB), it still leads with 85.88% (f-f), 76.54% (p-p), 72.01% (f-p), and 75.96% (p-f). The unimodal baselines score below 1% on cross-modality tasks, highlighting the critical role of modality alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFA-ViT/MPT outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and task configuration. Against the closest competitors trained with the same three modalities (ViT/VPT and HA-ViT with soft-biometric attributes), MFA-ViT/MPT gains approximately 1.9–3.1% on intra-modality tasks and 2.9–6.0% on cross-modality tasks (e.g., 90.38% vs. 87.43% f-p on FaceScrub; 75.28% vs. 69.40% f-p on IMDB). The conditional biometrics baselines (Ng et al. [22], George et al. [5]) achieve reasonable cross-modality scores but require both face and periocular inputs simultaneously, limiting deployment flexibility. The unimodal baseline and Tiong et al. [32] score below 1% on cross-modality tasks, confirming that without explicit modality alignment, face and periocular embeddings are essentially incompatible. The paper attributes its advantage over ViT/VPT to MPT's modality-specific guidance (vs. VPT's task-specific prompts) and over HA-ViT to the MFA module's structured fusion (vs. HA-ViT's simple concatenation), with GradCAM visualizations confirming focused attention on the shared ocular region.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own HA-ViT [33] and the broader line of conditional/cross-modality biometrics [5, 22] by unifying intra- and cross-modality recognition into a single 'Flexible Biometrics Recognition' framework that produces modality-invariant embeddings. Architecturally, it builds on ViT [4] and extends Visual Prompt Tuning [10] into a multimodal-specific variant (MPT) that provides per-modality guidance for alignment, departing from the task-agnostic prompts of VPT. The contrastive loss formulation draws on CrossCLR [40] and HA-ViT [33], but the key conceptual departure is replacing simple concatenation-based fusion (as in HA-ViT) with a depth-wise fusion convolution multi-head self-attention (DWFC-MSA) mechanism that explicitly models inter-modality relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FBR framework addresses a practical deployment gap: real-world biometric systems often face occlusion (masks, sunglasses) or missing modalities, and prior approaches required either both modalities simultaneously or separate models for intra- vs. cross-modality matching. By producing a single modality-invariant embedding, FBR simplifies template storage and enables flexible matching at inference time. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond face/periocular to other biometric pairs (e.g., face-voice, face-iris), how performance degrades under severe distribution shift, and whether the three-modality fusion (including soft-biometric attributes) is necessary or whether two-modality variants suffice for most deployment scenarios.
