## Problem

GCNs for skeleton-based action recognition suffer from two critical flaws: (1) learnable adjacency matrices catastrophically forget the physical bone connectivity during training, rendering the model indifferent to the skeletal topology it was initialized with; and (2) existing multi-relational modeling strategies (ensemble GCs, shared-weight adjacency ensembles, attention-based adaptation) are either parameter-heavy or lack the expressiveness to capture diverse joint interaction patterns across actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241912/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D skeleton sequences of 25 joints over 64 frames, processed through four parallel modalities (Joint, Bone, Joint Motion, Bone Motion) whose predicted scores are fused via 4-stream averaging. The output is a single action class label produced by global pooling over joint and temporal dimensions followed by softmax. The full model is remarkably lightweight at 1.3M parameters, substantially smaller than competing methods (1.5–12M).

## Method

BlockGCN introduces two complementary topological encodings that are added to both the adjacency matrix and hidden features at every layer: a static encoding using shortest-path distances on the skeletal graph (preserving bone connectivity via a small trainable embedding table rather than a full adjacency matrix), and a dynamic encoding derived from persistent homology analysis of the input pose sequence (Vietoris-Rips filtration → 0-dimensional barcodes → differentiable vectorization → linear projection to feature space). For multi-relational modeling, BlockGC partitions the feature dimension into K groups and applies spatial aggregation and feature projection independently within each group using a block-diagonal weight matrix, reducing parameters by over 40% while making feature groups semantically independent. The architecture stacks 10 alternating BlockGC and multi-scale temporal convolution layers, trained end-to-end with standard cross-entropy loss and SGD, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on NTU RGB+D 120 (90.3% X-Sub) with the fewest parameters among compared methods.

## Results

On the standard 4-modality (Joint+Bone+Joint Motion+Bone Motion) evaluation, BlockGCN achieves 93.1% / 97.0% on NTU RGB+D 60 (X-Sub / X-View), 90.3% / 91.5% on NTU RGB+D 120 (X-Sub / X-Set), and 96.9% on NW-UCLA, setting new state-of-the-art across all benchmarks. This represents an average improvement of 0.5% over the previous best (FR Head) while using 35% fewer parameters (1.3M vs. 2.0M) and lower FLOPs (1.63G vs. 15.2G for EfficientGCN). With the single joint modality alone, the gap widens to an average 0.8% improvement, e.g., 86.9% vs. 85.5% on NTU RGB+D 120 X-Sub.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BlockGCN outperforms all seven directly comparable baselines on every reported benchmark. Against the closest competitor FR Head [50] (CVPR 2023), it gains +0.3% on NTU60 X-Sub, +0.2% on NTU60 X-View, +0.8% on NTU120 X-Sub, +0.6% on NTU120 X-Set, and +0.1% on NW-UCLA, while using 35% fewer parameters. Over CTR-GCN [2] (ICCV 2021), the margins are larger: +0.7% on NTU60 X-Sub and +1.4% on NTU120 X-Sub. The two ICCV 2023 methods marked with asterisks (CTR-GCN* [43] and HDGCN* [20]) are not directly comparable because they rely on extra language supervision or handcrafted motion modalities; when restricted to the single joint modality, HDGCN* drops to 85.7% on NTU120 X-Sub, below BlockGCN's 86.9%. Overall, BlockGCN establishes the best accuracy-to-parameter ratio among all compared methods, achieving the highest accuracy with the fewest parameters (1.3M) and lowest FLOPs (1.63G) in the 4-modality setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the standard GCN formulation of Kipf & Welling and the CTR-GCN architecture (implementation base, multi-scale temporal convolution), while drawing on graph filtration learning for its persistent homology vectorization. It departs from the prevailing trend of learnable adjacency matrices by arguing they suffer from catastrophic forgetting of skeletal topology, and instead injects topological information through static (shortest-path-distance) and dynamic (persistent homology) encodings. The BlockGC module is positioned as a more efficient alternative to both ensemble-of-GCs (used by ST-GCN, CTR-GCN, InfoGCN) and DecouplingGC's ensemble-of-adjacency-matrices, replacing a full weight matrix with a block-diagonal one to decouple feature groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that learnable adjacency matrices in GCNs effectively discard their bone-connection initialization (Table 3: near-identical accuracy across four initialization schemes) challenges a widely assumed inductive bias in skeleton-based GCNs and motivates explicit topological encoding as a more reliable mechanism. The 40%+ parameter reduction from BlockGC while simultaneously improving accuracy offers a practical efficiency gain for deployment-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the persistent homology encoding generalizes to other graph-structured domains beyond human skeletons, whether the block-diagonal weight structure is truly optimal versus other sparse patterns, and whether the approach extends to larger joint sets or non-humanoid topologies.
