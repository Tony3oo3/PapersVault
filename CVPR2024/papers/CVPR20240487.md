## Problem

Training robust scene text detectors requires large-scale, well-annotated data, but existing synthetic methods (2D background overlay or 3D graphics engines) produce images that lack the contextual realism and geometric diversity of real scenes. No prior approach reconstructs actual scenes to synthesize text with 3D controllability, and no dataset provides 3D pose attributes for text instances, limiting evaluation of detector robustness to perspective effects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240487/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 300 real-world scene text videos (yielding 50–80 sharp frames per scene after blur filtering), with camera poses estimated via COLMAP and sparse text region annotations on only 1–3 images per scene. It produces 12,520 synthetic images annotated with bounding boxes, transcriptions, and—uniquely—a full 3D pose (rotation matrix and translation vector) for each text instance relative to the camera. The pipeline also supports interactive text editing (erasing/replacing existing text via Stable Diffusion, or inserting new text into custom regions) and appearance modification via style transfer.

## Method

The core architecture extends Instant-NGP-based NeRF with a position-enhanced semantic renderer that appends positional encoding of world coordinates to NeRF features, enabling the model to distinguish text regions within geometrically similar surfaces. A semi-supervised learning framework exploits NeRF's geometric consistency to project sparse text labels across views via depth-based pixel correspondence, reducing annotation burden while maintaining multi-view label consistency. For appearance diversification, a frequency-based structural importance mask—computed by projecting pixels across views using NeRF depth—protects text regions from style-transfer artifacts when applying PhotoWCT. Text editing operates on rendered frontal views with varied viewing directions to simulate view-dependent shading, using Stable Diffusion for inpainting and a modified volume rendering pass that substitutes edited-region colors while preserving surrounding geometry. Camera poses are set through a systematic algorithm that selects text position, projects a ray to the text centroid, and scales the projection to control area proportion, enabling balanced viewpoint sampling across 16 k-means pose clusters.

## Results

When trained solely on 10k synthetic images, the proposed TextNeRF dataset yields the best F-scores across all three benchmarks for both DB and EAST detectors: DB achieves F=70.21 (IC13), 66.41 (IC15), 53.72 (MLT17), and EAST achieves F=70.13, 68.73, 53.22 respectively. When used as a pre-training resource followed by fine-tuning on real data, the gains are even more pronounced: DB reaches F=79.14, 85.63, 64.93 and EAST reaches F=76.47, 85.88, 64.28, improving over real-data-only training by 22.72%, 5.26%, 2.52% (DB) and 21.92%, 6.61%, 3.69% (EAST) on IC13, IC15, and MLT17. The multi-view robustness experiment (Table 2) further shows that balanced viewpoint sampling during synthesis boosts DB's F-score from 64.25 (random) to 82.09 (balanced) at the instance level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three existing synthetic scene-text datasets: VISD (2D background compositing), SynthText3D (3D virtual worlds), and UnrealText (Unreal Engine rendering). In the synthetic-only setting, TextNeRF outperforms all three on F-score for both detectors across all benchmarks; the largest margin is on MLT17 where DB jumps from 44.49 (VISD) to 53.72 (Ours), a +9.23 point gain. UT-10k achieves the highest precision in the synthetic-only setting (e.g., 79.69 on IC13 for DB) but suffers from lower recall, while TextNeRF consistently achieves the best recall and F-score. In the more practical synthetic+real fine-tuning setting, the advantage widens: Ours-10k+Real beats UT-10k+Real by 1.74 F-points on IC13 (DB) and 2.68 on IC15 (EAST), and surpasses VISD-10k+Real by 2.75 F-points on IC13 (DB). The multi-view robustness experiment confirms that the balanced viewpoint strategy inherent to the NeRF-based synthesis yields substantially better scene-level performance (F=77.28 vs. 57.90 for DB) than random viewpoint sampling, a capability none of the 2D or 3D-engine baselines can replicate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeRF rendering pipeline (Mildenhall et al., 2020; Instant-NGP, Müller et al., 2023) into the domain of scene text synthesis, departing from prior approaches that rely on 2D background compositing (SynthText, VISD) or 3D graphics engines (SynthText3D, UnrealText). For semantic modeling of text regions, it builds on the view-invariant semantic rendering framework of Zhi et al. (2021) but introduces positional encoding of world coordinates to handle the fact that text regions are artificial surface categorizations rather than physical objects. The training objective combines the distortion loss from Mip-NeRF 360 with the S3IM structural coherence loss (Xie et al., ICCV 2023), and appearance diversification leverages PhotoWCT style transfer guided by a frequency-based structural importance mask derived from NeRF depth projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first scene text dataset to provide 3D pose attributes per text instance opens a new axis of evaluation—perspective robustness—that prior benchmarks (ICDAR, MLT) cannot assess. The finding that NeRF-reconstructed real scenes outperform 3D-engine-generated scenes for training text detectors suggests that geometric fidelity to actual environments matters more than rendering quality alone. Key limitations include the per-scene NeRF training cost (10–30 min per scene), color inconsistencies from style transfer, and difficulty handling extreme conditions like highly stylized fonts or severe occlusions, leaving scalability to large-scale dataset generation as an open challenge.
