## Problem

Models trained on synthetic urban-scene data (e.g., GTAV, Synthia) suffer severe performance degradation on real-world target domains due to the sim-to-real domain gap. Existing domain generalization methods primarily address style variation (low-frequency components) but overlook the equally critical role of fine-grained high-frequency features, on which convolutional networks progressively overfit during training, hindering generalization to unseen domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240592/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB urban scene images from synthetic source domains (GTAV at 1914×1052, Synthia at 1280×760) and produces pixel-wise semantic segmentation masks over 19 common object categories. During training, the MRFP module operates on intermediate feature maps of the DeepLabv3+ encoder and decoder; during inference, the MRFP module is entirely removed and only the baseline segmentation network is deployed, incurring zero additional computational cost at test time.

## Method

MRFP combines two complementary perturbation mechanisms applied in the feature space during training: (1) HRFP, a randomly initialized overcomplete convolutional autoencoder with decreasing receptive fields (achieved via successive bilinear upsampling at ~1.2×) that extracts and perturbs fine-grained high-frequency features at the shallow encoder stage (stage 0) and optionally the penultimate decoder layer (HRFP+); and (2) NP+, which perturbs per-channel feature statistics (mean and variance) to inject style diversity targeting low-frequency components. The overcomplete design ensures perturbations remain local and semantically safe, while random He-initialized convolutions and Gaussian-sampled batch-norm parameters prevent the model from memorizing source-domain-specific HF patterns. Both modules are applied stochastically (probability 0.5) and introduce no learnable parameters or auxiliary loss functions, making MRFP a plug-and-play transferable module compatible with any segmentation backbone (validated on ResNet-50 and MobileNetv2).

## Results

On the primary Sim-2-Real single-domain generalization setting (GTAV → {BDD100k, Cityscapes, Mapillary, Synthia}) with ResNet-50 backbone, MRFP+ achieves an average mIoU of 39.27%, outperforming the baseline DeepLabv3+ (31.21%) by 8.06 points and the strongest baseline ProRandConv (36.63%) by 2.64 points. In the adverse-weather setting (GTAV → Foggy Cityscapes and Rainy Cityscapes at 25–100 mm), MRFP+ reaches 25.87% average mIoU versus 21.67% for WildNet and 20.37% for ISW. In the multi-domain setting (GTAV+Synthia → {BDD100k, Cityscapes, Mapillary}), MRFP+ achieves 44.24% average mIoU, a 9.36-point gain over the baseline. The method is plug-and-play: with MobileNetv2 backbone, MRFP still outperforms ISW by 1.76 points average mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRFP+ outperforms all seven baselines on average mIoU in the primary GTAV→{B,C,M,S} setting. The closest competitor, ProRandConv [8] (36.63% avg), is surpassed by 2.64 points, with MRFP+ leading on BDD100k (+2.52), Mapillary (+3.30), and Synthia (+4.70), while ProRandConv edges ahead on Cityscapes by only 0.04 points. SAN-SAW [31] (36.41%) and WildNet [24] (35.31%) trail by 2.86 and 3.96 points respectively; ISW/RobustNet [7] (35.10%) and IBN-Net [29] (32.95%) lag further behind. In the adverse-weather evaluation (Table 2), MRFP+ leads all baselines across every rain intensity level and Foggy Cityscapes, with the largest margin over ISW at 100 mm rain (15.28 vs 10.39). In the multi-domain setting (Table 4), MRFP+ (44.24% avg) beats ISW (37.40%) by 6.84 points, confirming consistent gains when multiple source domains are available. Notably, MRFP introduces no additional learnable parameters or loss terms, and inference uses only the baseline DeepLabv3+ network, so the gains come purely from training-time perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) feature-space style perturbation for domain generalization (NP+, MixStyle, ISW), (2) image-space random convolution augmentation (RandConv, ProRandConv), and (3) overcomplete network architectures for fine-grained segmentation (KiU-Net, DeepMAO). It departs from prior work by operating entirely in the feature space rather than the image space, and by repurposing overcomplete autoencoders not as accuracy-boosting components but as randomly initialized perturbation modules whose decreasing receptive fields target high-frequency, domain-specific features. The spatial-to-Fourier mapping (high spatial resolution ≈ HF ≈ domain-specific texture; low spatial resolution ≈ LF ≈ style) provides a principled rationale for why perturbing both ends of the frequency spectrum is necessary, going beyond the style-only focus of NP+ or the image-space texture diversification of ProRandConv.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, zero-inference-cost design makes MRFP practically attractive for practitioners who need Sim-2-Real generalization without architectural modifications or additional training objectives. The ablation evidence (Fourier analysis, t-SNE/MMD, Grad-CAM) that HF feature perturbation contributes independently of style perturbation is a useful diagnostic for understanding where domain-specific overfitting occurs in CNNs. However, the evaluation is confined to urban-scene Sim-2-Real segmentation with a single source domain (GTAV or GTAV+Synthia), and the spatial-to-frequency mapping is a conjecture validated empirically rather than theoretically. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other segmentation domains (medical, remote sensing), whether the random initialization of HRFP can be made adaptive, and whether the benefit persists when the source domain already has high style diversity.
