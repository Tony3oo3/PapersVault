## Problem

Single-view scene completion methods like BTS can only reason about geometry from one image, leaving them unable to exploit the additional geometric information available in multiple posed views. Meanwhile, existing multi-view methods (IBRnet, NeuRay) focus on novel view synthesis and produce inaccurate occupancy, especially in occluded regions. The paper addresses this gap by fusing multi-view density fields for accurate scene geometry and distilling that knowledge into a single-view model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241865/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

MVBTS takes multiple posed RGB images (stereo and temporal frames, typically 3–4 views) along with their camera intrinsics and relative poses, and produces a continuous volumetric density field σ(x) queryable at any 3D point. KDBTS takes a single RGB image without requiring pose information at inference and produces the same density field representation. From the density field, both occupancy maps (via thresholding) and depth maps (via expected ray termination depth) are derived; training requires no 3D ground truth, relying solely on photometric consistency between rendered and target images.

## Method

The method has two stages. First, MVBTS extends BTS's single-view density prediction by replacing the single MLP decoder with a confidence-based multi-view aggregation: a shared ResNet-50 encoder-decoder produces per-image feature maps, and for each 3D point, per-view features are passed through MLP₁ to yield view-specific confidence scores and features, which are combined via softmax-weighted summation and decoded by MLP₂ into a density value. This is trained fully self-supervised via differentiable volumetric rendering with a photometric L1+SSIM loss, where rendering, color sampling, and density prediction are assigned to different views to enable learning in occluded regions. Second, KDBTS distills the trained MVBTS into a single-view BTS architecture by applying an L1 loss between the single-view density prediction and the stop-gradient multi-view prediction, effectively using multi-view reconstructions as pseudo-ground-truth supervision. The shared backbone and direct 3D supervision allow KDBTS to match MVBTS performance with a smaller decoder and no pose requirement at inference.

## Results

On KITTI-360 occupancy prediction (the primary evaluation), KDBTS (single-view) achieves 94.76% O-accuracy, 60.68% O-precision, and 84.78% O-recall, while MVBTS in the multi-view setting reaches 94.91% O-accuracy, 61.73% O-precision, and 85.78% O-recall. For the invisible-and-empty (IE) sub-metric, MVBTS (multi-view) attains 79.47% IE-accuracy, 55.08% IE-precision, and 45.23% IE-recall. On KITTI monocular depth, both KDBTS and MVBTS report Abs Rel ≈ 0.105 and α<1.25 ≈ 0.873, performing on par with BTS (0.102 / 0.882) but slightly behind DevNet (0.095 / 0.895) and MonoDepth2 (0.106 / 0.874).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BTS (the direct single-view predecessor), both KDBTS and MVBTS (single-view) consistently improve O-accuracy (+0.29 pp), O-precision (+1.9–2.1 pp), and O-recall (+0.3–0.5 pp), while IE-accuracy and IE-recall also improve slightly; IE-precision drops marginally (−0.5 pp). Compared to PixelNeRF, both methods substantially outperform it on all six occupancy metrics (e.g., O-recall 84.78% vs. 69.43%). In the multi-view setting, MVBTS dramatically outperforms IBRnet on O-precision (61.73% vs. 4.14%) and O-recall (85.78% vs. 4.78%), though IBRnet reports a higher O-accuracy (96.03% vs. 94.91%) due to the extreme occupied/empty imbalance; in the depth+4m variant IBRnet's IE-recall collapses to 9.85% versus MVBTS's 45.23%. On KITTI depth, KDBTS/MVBTS (Abs Rel 0.105) trail DevNet (0.095) and MonoDepth2 (0.106) slightly, which the authors attribute to model capacity being redirected toward occluded-region geometry rather than visible-surface depth.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Behind the Scenes (BTS) [51], which introduced density fields for single-view scene completion via implicit neural representations, by replacing its single-view MLP decoder with a confidence-weighted multi-view aggregation (MVBTS). It then departs from multi-view image-based rendering methods like IBRnet and NeuRay, which focus on novel view synthesis and require close-by views for both color and density, by instead targeting geometry and occupancy prediction in occluded regions. The knowledge distillation step (KDBTS) is a novel training strategy that transfers multi-view geometric knowledge into a single-view model via direct 3D density supervision, a formulation not present in prior scene completion or NeRF distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is that KDBTS achieves state-of-the-art occupancy prediction on KITTI-360 while requiring only a single image and no pose information at inference time, making it deployable in settings where multi-view or calibrated sensors are unavailable. The static-scene and photo-consistency assumptions limit applicability to dynamic driving scenes, and the method still needs relative poses during multi-view training. An open question is whether the distillation framework generalizes to other implicit-representation tasks (e.g., semantic scene completion) or to scenes with significant view-dependent appearance effects that the current photometric loss cannot capture.
