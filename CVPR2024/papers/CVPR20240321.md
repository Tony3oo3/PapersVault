## Problem

Advanced binary neural networks (BNNs) still rely on millions of full-precision multiplication operations introduced by BN layers, PReLU, scaling factors, and average pooling, which are hardware-inefficient and conflict with the fundamental goal of 1-bit inference. This paper asks whether all multiplication operations can be eliminated during BNN inference while retaining competitive classification accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240321/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100) and outputs a class label (1000, 10, or 100 classes respectively). The distinguishing constraint is that inference is performed exclusively with addition and bit-shift operations—zero full-precision multiplications—making the architecture directly deployable on multiplier-less hardware such as FPGAs without off-chip computation.

## Method

Building on the BN-Free BNN topology, A&B BNN eliminates all multiplication operands through three mechanisms: (1) a removable mask layer that absorbs the β scaling factor during training via a sigmoid-based mask function but is simply deleted at inference, exploiting the property Sign(Mask(k·x)) = Sign(x); (2) constraining the residual scaling factor α and average-pooling kernel sizes to integer powers of 2, converting multiplications and divisions into left/right bit-shifts; and (3) a quantized RPReLU unit whose per-channel PReLU slopes are restricted to discrete integer powers of 2, replacing the PReLU multiplication with a bit-shift while preserving nonlinearity. Training uses a two-step strategy (first binarize activations, then weights) with scaled weight standardization, adaptive gradient clipping, and a knowledge-distillation loss against a full-precision teacher. On ImageNet with ReActNet-A, this achieves 66.89% Top-1 with 0 multiplication operands, a 1.11% loss versus the BN-Free baseline but a 1.14% gain over a fixed-slope RLeakyReLU ablation.

## Results

On ImageNet, A&B BNN achieves 61.39%, 65.19%, and 66.89% top-1 accuracy on ReActNet-18, ReActNet-34, and ReActNet-A respectively, all with zero multiplication operands (MO=0). On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, ReActNet-18 (A&B BNN) reaches 92.30% and 69.35% top-1 accuracy, while ReActNet-A (A&B BNN) reaches 89.44% and 63.23%. The key hardware advantage is the complete elimination of full-precision multiplication operations during inference, reducing FPGA DSP usage by 100% and LUT/slice usage by ~44–58% per convolution layer compared to standard multipliers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest baseline, BN-Free ReActNet-18 [25] (61.1%, 4.60M MO), A&B BNN ReActNet-18 slightly improves accuracy to 61.39% while eliminating all 4.6M multiplications. On ReActNet-A, A&B BNN (66.89%) trails the full BN-equipped ReActNet-A [24] (69.4%) by 2.51% and the BN-Free variant [25] (68.0%) by 1.11%, but does so with zero multiplication operands versus 10.79M and 14.65M respectively. On CIFAR-10/100, A&B BNN ReActNet-18 matches or exceeds both BN and BN-Free baselines (92.30% vs 92.31%/92.08% on CIFAR-10; 69.35% vs 68.78%/68.34% on CIFAR-100), and ReActNet-A (A&B) substantially outperforms both baselines (89.44% vs 82.95%/83.91% on CIFAR-10; 63.23% vs 50.30%/55.00% on CIFAR-100) due to a stride adjustment. Against older BNNs (BNN-ResNet-18, XNOR-ResNet-18, Bi-ResNet-18), A&B BNN ReActNet-18 leads by 4.99–19.19% on ImageNet. The trade-off is a modest 1.11% accuracy loss on ImageNet ReActNet-A relative to BN-Free in exchange for a 14.7M reduction in multiplication operations, which the authors argue is negligible for edge deployment where hardware cost dominates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BN-Free BNN framework (Chen et al., CVPR 2021), which already eliminated batch normalization layers from binary networks, by further removing all remaining full-precision multiplication operations from α scaling, β normalization, RPReLU, and average pooling. It builds on the ReActNet architecture (Liu et al., ECCV 2020) and the foundational BNN/STE framework (Courbariaux et al., 2016), but departs from prior BNN work that accepted millions of multiplication operands as an unavoidable cost of scaling factors and activation nonlinearity. The key conceptual move is recognizing that the mask layer's sign-invariance property allows β's multiplication to be absorbed and then discarded at inference, while constraining α and PReLU slopes to integer powers of 2 converts the remaining multiplications into bit-shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For hardware designers targeting edge deployment, eliminating all DSP units (100% reduction in FPGA synthesis) removes a major cost and latency bottleneck, enabling entirely on-chip inference without host round-trips. The ~1.1% accuracy trade-off on ImageNet for zero multiplications is a reasonable price in edge scenarios where model complexity is lower and task difficulty is less than ImageNet. Open questions include whether the mask-layer trick generalizes to other quantization granularities (e.g., 2-bit, 3-bit), whether the discrete power-of-2 slope constraint in quantized RPReLU limits expressiveness in more complex or multi-modal tasks, and whether the approach extends to non-convolutional architectures like transformers.
