## Problem

When adapting pretrained image-VLMs (e.g., CLIP) to video, prior work consistently focuses on augmenting visual embeddings with temporal information while leaving text embeddings frozen, discarded, or only weakly conditioned. This leaves text representations identical across all videos, limiting their flexibility to align properly in the shared contrastive latent space for challenging recognition tasks. The paper argues that text embeddings should be equally (or more) important to adapt to the video domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242462/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video of T frames, a fixed vocabulary of n activity-class text prompts, and optionally m auxiliary semantic text prompts (e.g., objects, scenes, human subjects). Frames are encoded per-frame by a pretrained CLIP image encoder, and text is tokenized and encoded by the CLIP text encoder. The Video Head produces video-conditioned text embeddings that are unique per video (T×(n+m) tokens), from which affinity-based logits are computed for single-label (Top-1) or multi-label (mAP) activity classification. Auxiliary text is visually grounded via cross-modal attention and affinity weighting before contributing to the final logits.

## Method

VicTR introduces a joint video-text Video Head with three key operations applied over L transformer layers. First, Token-boosting replicates each text token per frame and scales it by a sigmoid-normalized frame-text affinity (SigAffinity), creating T×(n+m) video-specific text tokens that act as a soft selection of relevant semantics per video. Second, divided self-attention is applied along two axes: cross-modal attention (visual tokens attend to all text tokens at the same timestep, and text tokens attend to each other and the visual token) and temporal attention (shared parameters model motion in visual tokens and semantic variation in text tokens across time). Third, Affinity (re-)weighting re-computes affinities between the updated video and temporally-pooled text tokens, then re-scales text tokens to iteratively correct the initially noisy image-VLM affinities. The classifier uses the same cosine-affinity formulation as CLIP, and ablations confirm that updating text embeddings is far more critical than updating visual ones (−8.4% mAP on Charades when text is frozen vs. −0.4% when visual is frozen).

## Results

On Charades (long-form, multi-label), VicTR-B/16 achieves 50.1 mAP, outperforming the closest video-VLM baselines CLIP Hitchhiker's (44.9 mAP) and ActionCLIP (44.6 mAP) by +5.2 and +5.5 mAP respectively, while VicTR-L/14 reaches 57.6 mAP. On Kinetics-400 (short-form, single-label), VicTR-L/14 achieves 87.0% top-1 accuracy at 656 GFLOPs per view, matching X-CLIP (87.1%) and outperforming non-VLM methods like MTV-L (84.3%) and ViViT-L (83.5%). For zero-shot transfer, VicTR-B/16 reaches 51.0% on HMDB-51 and 72.4% on UCF-101, surpassing X-CLIP by 6.4% and 0.4% respectively. In few-shot settings (16 samples/class), VicTR-B/16 achieves 70.7% on HMDB-51 and 95.8% on UCF-101, exceeding X-CLIP by 6.7% and 4.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VicTR outperforms all non-VLM baselines (TSM, TimeSformer, Video-Swin, SlowFast, MViT, AssembleNet, etc.) across every benchmark, with the largest gaps on Charades where VicTR-B/16 (50.1 mAP) exceeds MViT (47.7) and X3D-XL (43.4). Among video-VLMs, VicTR achieves the most dramatic gains on Charades: +5.2 mAP over CLIP Hitchhiker's [4] and +5.5 mAP over ActionCLIP [65], both at comparable compute cost (~567 GFLOPs). On Kinetics-400, VicTR-L/14 (87.0%) is essentially tied with X-CLIP (87.1%) and slightly below ST-Adapter (87.2%), but outperforms EVL (86.3%) and CLIP4Clip (32.0 on Charades). In few-shot transfer, VicTR-B/16 consistently beats X-CLIP by 6–7% on HMDB-51 and 4–5% on UCF-101 across all shot counts, and even surpasses X-Florence (a Florence-based variant) on both datasets. In zero-shot transfer, VicTR improves over X-CLIP by 6.4% on HMDB-51 (51.0 vs 44.6) while matching it on UCF-101 (72.4 vs 72.0), with notably lower variance across splits (±1.3 vs ±5.2 on HMDB-51). The ablation in Table 5 confirms that updating text embeddings is the critical factor: replacing video-conditioned text with frozen CLIP text drops Charades mAP by 8.4%, whereas replacing video embeddings with temporally-pooled CLIP frames costs only 0.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

VicTR builds directly on CLIP's dual-encoder contrastive framework and the divided self-attention mechanism from ViViT, but departs sharply from the dominant video-VLM adaptation paradigm. Prior work (ActionCLIP, CLIP4Clip, CLIP Hitchhiker's, EVL) focuses on augmenting visual embeddings with temporal information while keeping text frozen or discarded; X-CLIP is the closest prior method that jointly updates both modalities but provides text only temporally-aggregated visual context. VicTR inverts this emphasis, arguing that video-conditioned text representations—unique per video and interacting with fine-grained visual tokens and other text tokens—are the critical component for flexible contrastive alignment in the latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical claim—that updating text embeddings is substantially more impactful than updating visual embeddings for video understanding (8.4% mAP drop vs. 0.4% on Charades when each is replaced with its original CLIP counterpart)—challenges the field's implicit assumption that temporal reasoning is primarily a visual problem. This opens a direction for video-VLM design where text-side capacity (token-boosting, cross-modal attention, affinity re-weighting) becomes the primary lever rather than ever-larger visual backbones. Limitations include modest gains from auxiliary semantics (+0.2–0.3%), evaluation restricted to four datasets, and no exploration of scaling to longer videos or larger model variants beyond L/14.
