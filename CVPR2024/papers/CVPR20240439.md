## Problem

Existing pipelines for collecting real-world reflection pairs suffer from pixel misalignment due to glass refraction or residual reflection artifacts from RAW-space subtraction, and scaling them to large datasets is costly. Additionally, prior methods for identifying local reflection regions rely on indirect priors (gradient thresholds, linear composition losses) that fail under strong or complex reflections, leaving no explicit mechanism to characterize where local reflections actually occur.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240439/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reflection-contaminated RGB image as input and produces two outputs: a binary local-reflection location map (from RDNet) and a reflection-free transmission image (from RRNet). The RRW dataset provides 14,952 aligned pairs at 2580×1460 resolution, and training crops 320×320 patches. The location map is a per-pixel binary indicator of local reflection presence, which is concatenated as an extra channel with the input image before being fed to the removal network.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage cascaded CNN framework (RDNet + RRNet) guided by the Maximum Reflection Filter (MaxRF). MaxRF computes Sobel gradients on both the reflection and transmission images from aligned pairs, then takes an element-wise comparison: wherever the reflection image's gradient exceeds the transmission image's gradient, a local reflection is flagged. This works because global ambient reflection uniformly attenuates transmission gradients, so the residual gradient difference isolates local reflection textures. RDNet (EfficientNet backbone + residual blocks) learns to predict this binary mask from a single reflection image using L1 + TV loss, and RRNet (a restoration backbone) removes reflections by concatenating the predicted mask with the input image, trained with L1 + VGG perceptual loss. The pipeline is further powered by a new video-mode collection system using black velvet cloth to block reflections, yielding the 14,952-pair RRW dataset without glass-refraction misalignment or format constraints.

## Results

The proposed method achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all three real-world reflection benchmarks (Nature, Real, SIR2) and their average. On the average of 494 test images, it reaches 25.40 dB PSNR and 0.904 SSIM, surpassing the second-best method (PNACR, 24.06 dB) by 2.03 dB in PSNR. Per-benchmark, it scores 25.96/0.843 (Nature), 23.82/0.817 (Real), and 25.45/0.910 (SIR2) in PSNR/SSIM respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 11 baselines on both PSNR and SSIM across every benchmark. The closest competitor is DSRNet [10] (ICCV 2023), which achieves 24.65 dB average PSNR; the proposed method exceeds it by 0.75 dB on average and by a larger margin on Nature (25.96 vs. 21.24 dB, a 4.72 dB gap). PNACR [44] (ACM MM 2023) is second on average PSNR (24.06 dB) but trails by 1.34 dB. Among earlier methods, IBCLN [19] and LANet [4] are the strongest on Nature (23.90 and 23.51 dB respectively), yet both are surpassed by 2+ dB. Notably, DSRNet leads on SIR2 PSNR among baselines (24.91 dB) but still falls short of the proposed 25.45 dB. The gap is most pronounced on the Nature dataset, where the proposed method's 25.96 dB represents a 2.03 dB improvement over the best baseline (PNACR at 23.93 dB), reflecting the benefit of the RRW dataset and MaxRF-guided detection on diverse wild scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior SIRR data collection pipelines (Zhang et al. [53], Lei et al. [15,16]) by proposing a video-mode capture system using light-blocking cloth to obtain aligned reflection pairs without glass refraction artifacts or RAW format constraints. Conceptually, it extends the reflection-location-aware line of work (LANet [4], Wan et al. [40]) by replacing implicit or gradient-prior-based location estimation with an explicit Maximum Reflection Filter (MaxRF) that directly compares Sobel gradients between aligned pairs. The cascaded RDNet+RRNet architecture follows the two-stage paradigm of IBCLN [19] and CEILNet [5], but the explicit location map as a conditioning input to the removal network is a distinct design choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RRW dataset (14,952 high-resolution aligned pairs, ~45× larger than prior real-world datasets) addresses a persistent bottleneck in SIRR training and is likely to become a standard training resource for the community. The MaxRF technique is simple, interpretable, and extensible—Table 4 demonstrates that bolting RDNet onto prior architectures (Zhang et al., ERRNet) yields consistent gains, suggesting the location-guidance principle is broadly applicable. The acknowledged limitation on saturated reflections, where transmission content is irrecoverably lost, points toward future work integrating semantic or generative priors for content-aware inpainting in extreme cases.
