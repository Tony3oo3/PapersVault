## Problem

No existing human motion dataset simultaneously provides RGB, LiDAR, IMU, and Event camera modalities, and single- or two-modality HPE methods fail on rapid, complex sports motions that demand both accurate pose estimation and precise global localization. This paper introduces RELI11D, the first four-modality human motion dataset, and LEIR, a multimodal baseline that exploits the complementary strengths of each sensor to handle such challenging motions.

## Input / Output

LEIR consumes synchronized LiDAR point clouds (Ouster-OS1, 128-beam, 20 FPS), RGB frames (DJI Action 2, 4096×3072, downsampled to 20 FPS), and event streams (CeleX-V, 1280×800, binned into 20 FPS event frames after denoising). It outputs 3D SMPL pose parameters (24×3 axis-angle), global pelvis translation (N×3 in world coordinates), and 3D joint positions. The dataset additionally provides 17-sensor IMU measurements (60 FPS), high-precision scene meshes (~80M points per scene), and per-actor SMPL shape parameters (β) for annotation and scene-aware evaluation.

## Method

LEIR extracts modality-specific features using DINOv2 for RGB and event frames, and PointNet++ with a GRU for LiDAR point clouds. A Temporal Unified Multimodal Model (TUMM) then fuses the three modalities via a two-step Multimodal Cross-Attention (MMCA) strategy: the first step aligns LiDAR geometry with RGB appearance and with Event motion dynamics separately, and the second step integrates all three streams through a 3D-branch MMCA, eliminating the need for precise cross-sensor calibration. The fused features feed three parallel heads—a 3D regressor for joints and camera intrinsics, an RNN for world-coordinate joint prediction, and an ST-GCN for joint orientation—whose outputs are refined by an SMPL inverse-kinematics solver. Training is supervised with a composite loss combining 2D/3D keypoint, projection, world-joint, and SMPL pose terms, and the same network supports single- or multi-modal inference by freezing unused branches.

## Results

On RELI11D, LEIR with all three modalities (LiDAR+RGB+Event) achieves the best performance: MPJPE 49.19 mm, PA-MPJPE 40.87 mm, G-MPJPE 115.36 mm, T-Error 146.13 mm, and PCK@0.3 of 0.92. LiDAR alone yields MPJPE 59.93 mm and PCK@0.3 of 0.89, while RGB alone drops to MPJPE 62.71 mm and G-MPJPE 557.81 mm, and Event alone is worst at MPJPE 107.78 mm. On LiDARHuman26M, LEIR with LiDAR+RGB achieves MPJPE 75.09 mm and PCK@0.3 of 0.87, outperforming FusionPose (78.18 mm) and ImmFusion (96.93 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RELI11D, all 12 SOTA baselines perform poorly due to the dataset's rapid sports motions: the best LiDAR method (LiDARCap) reaches only MPJPE 144.51 mm, the best RGB method (SMPLer-X) 171.97 mm, and the best RGB+LiDAR method (FusionPose) 136.15 mm (Table 4). LEIR with all three modalities (MPJPE 49.19 mm) outperforms every single- and dual-modality SOTA method by a large margin (roughly 2.8×–3.5× lower MPJPE). On LiDARHuman26M, LEIR with LiDAR-only input slightly beats LiDARCap (79.00 vs. 80.08 mm MPJPE), and with LiDAR+RGB it clearly surpasses both FusionPose (75.09 vs. 78.18 mm) and ImmFusion (75.09 vs. 96.93 mm). For global trajectory estimation, LEIR's T-Error of 146.13 mm (LiDAR+RGB+Event) is dramatically lower than GLAMR (590.46 mm) and TRACE (581.67 mm), confirming that LiDAR geometry is essential for accurate global localization. Event-based methods (EventHPE, EventPointPose) perform worst on RELI11D, with EventHPE at MPJPE 193.7 mm, indicating that event streams alone are insufficient for complex sports motions without geometric or appearance cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first to combine all four sensor modalities (RGB, LiDAR, IMU, Event) in a single human motion dataset, whereas prior multimodal efforts like FusionPose and ImmFusion use RGB+LiDAR, LIP uses IMU+LiDAR, and TotalCapture uses cameras+IMUs. The LEIR method departs from the dominant two-tower fusion architecture by introducing a temporal unified multimodal model (TUMM) with a two-layer cross-attention unit (MMCA) that simultaneously aligns three modalities without relying on precise spatial calibration. The annotation pipeline extends the consolidated optimization approach from HSC4D by adding scene-mesh contact constraints and self-penetration penalties, while repurposing standard components (PointNet++, DINOv2, ST-GCN, GRU) in a novel three-branch inverse-kinematics solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RELI11D fills a critical gap in the HPE literature by providing the first benchmark where rapid, complex sports motions (taekwondo, fencing, boxing) stress-test methods that perform well on daily activities, revealing that all current SOTA methods degrade substantially on such motions. The consistent improvement from fusing all three modalities (LiDAR+RGB+Event) over any subset across every metric validates the principle that complementary sensor characteristics (geometry, appearance, temporal dynamics) are necessary for holistic motion understanding. Open questions include whether the cross-attention fusion generalizes to multi-person scenes, whether the 10-actor/5-sport scale is sufficient for robust training, and whether event cameras will become practical enough for widespread adoption given their current hardware limitations.
