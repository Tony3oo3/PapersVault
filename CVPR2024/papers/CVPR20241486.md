## Problem

Existing 3D hair reconstruction methods either demand expensive multi-view capture rigs or rely heavily on learned data priors that over-smooth geometry and fail on underrepresented hairstyles like curly hair. The core issue is prior over-dominance: when a data prior is applied to the entire hairstyle, it suppresses fine-grained details actually visible in the input images, particularly for hairstyles absent from the training database.

## Input / Output

The method takes a casually filmed monocular video (no calibrated cameras or controlled lighting required) and produces a complete 3D strand-based hair model with both exterior and interior geometry. Internally, it generates a 3D line map where each point carries a position and growth direction, plus a voxelized interior occupancy and orientation field. The pipeline runs in 4–6 hours on a single RTX 3090, roughly ten times faster than the closest monocular-video baseline.

## Method

MonoHair bifurcates reconstruction into a prior-free exterior stage and a prior-guided interior stage. The exterior is extracted via Patch-based Multi-View Optimization (PMVO), which refines a noisy NeRF point cloud by enforcing multi-view consistency of projected 3D line directions against Gabor-filtered 2D orientations, using two novel losses: a ray regularization term that constrains each line to intersect a reference-view ray, and a patch-wise angular loss that averages cosine differences over a local neighborhood to tolerate calibration error and smooth adjacent directions. The interior is inferred by an improved DeepMVSHair* network that, instead of consuming raw Gabor-filtered images, receives 2D undirectional strand maps rendered from the reconstructed exterior into 16 fixed synthetic views—mirroring the training distribution and bridging the synthetic-to-real domain gap. A new orientation loss treats a direction and its opposite as equivalent, deferring disambiguation to the strand-generation stage, where short segments are recursively merged into long strands and connected to the scalp with direction resolved from already-connected neighbors.

## Results

On the synthetic Hair Meshes dataset, MonoHair achieves the highest precision (92.1%) and F-score (40.4) at the 4 mm / 40° threshold, outperforming Neural Haircut (88.4% precision, 40.3 F-score) and DeepMVSHair (79.5% precision, 37.8 F-score). At the stricter 2 mm / 20° threshold, MonoHair's precision of 60.8% exceeds Neural Haircut's 52.9% by nearly 8 points. In terms of efficiency, MonoHair completes reconstruction in 4–6 hours versus 72–108 hours for Neural Haircut, a more than tenfold speedup on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoHair outperforms Neural Haircut [27] (the closest monocular-video baseline) on precision at all three thresholds (e.g., 92.1% vs. 88.4% at 4/40) and on F-score at the two stricter thresholds (17.8 vs. 16.4 at 2/20; 31.3 vs. 28.7 at 3/30), while matching it at the loosest threshold (40.4 vs. 40.3). Against DeepMVSHair [11], MonoHair leads by a wide margin on precision (92.1% vs. 79.5% at 4/40) and F-score (40.4 vs. 37.8), confirming that the PMVO exterior extraction plus rendered undirectional strand maps substantially improve over directly applying Gabor-filtered 2D orientations. Qualitatively, MonoHair preserves fine curly-hair detail that Neural Haircut over-smooths due to its SDF representation and strong data-prior constraints, and it avoids the geometry loss seen in DeepMVSHair when 2D orientation maps are inconsistent across views. On the H3DS real-world dataset, MonoHair produces richer strand detail than both DeepMVSHair and its improved variant DeepMVSHair*, which still lose some geometry due to data-prior limitations. In terms of speed, MonoHair (4–6 h total) is roughly 10–20× faster than Neural Haircut (72–108 h), making it far more practical for real-world use.

## Relation to Prior Work

MonoHair builds on DeepMVSHair [11] for interior structure inference but fundamentally repositions the role of data priors in the pipeline: rather than applying learned priors to the entire hairstyle (as in Neural Haircut [27] or DeepMVSHair itself), it restricts priors to the invisible interior and reconstructs the exterior purely from multi-view image evidence via the novel Patch-based Multi-View Optimization (PMVO). The coarse initialization leverages Instant-NGP/NeRF rather than SDF-based NeuS, and the undirectional strand map (encoding O and O+180° identically) is a targeted fix for the domain gap between synthetic training inputs and real-world Gabor-filtered orientation maps. Conceptually, the paper departs from the 'prior-guided end-to-end' paradigm by explicitly bifurcating exterior and interior reconstruction, a design choice motivated by the observation that priors over-smooth fine-grained curly geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× speedup over Neural Haircut (4–6 h vs. 72–108 h per subject) makes high-fidelity strand-based hair reconstruction from a casual monocular video practically accessible, and the method's robustness on curly hair addresses a well-known failure mode of data-prior approaches. However, the interior geometry remains dependent on the USC-HairSalon prior, so hairstyles with severe self-occlusion (e.g., braids) can still produce incorrect strand connections—a limitation the authors acknowledge and suggest could be alleviated by CT-based capture or a more diverse training set. An open question is whether the exterior-interior bifurcation can be extended to fully prior-free interior inference, which would remove the remaining dependency on synthetic data distribution.
