## Problem

Existing deterministic 3D hand pose estimation methods (regression and detection) struggle with ill-posed uncertain cases such as self-occlusions and hand-object occlusions, where a single point estimate is unreliable. While diffusion models naturally model uncertainty through probabilistic distributions, directly applying existing 3D diffusion models to hand pose estimation fails because they rely on global latent conditions that miss local detail and their permutation-equivariant design cannot distinguish individual joints. This paper presents the first deployment of diffusion models for 3D hand pose estimation, addressing both the local-detail and joint-permutation challenges.

## Input / Output

The model takes a hand depth image resized to 128×128 and 1,024 sampled 3D point coordinates (transformed from the depth image) as multi-modal input. The depth image is encoded by a ConvNeXt autoencoder into a 2D local feature map (H/2 × W/2 × 128) and a global vector, while the point cloud is processed by a hierarchical PointNet++ encoder into 3D local features (N/2 × 128) and a global 3D vector. The output is a set of J 3D joint coordinates (J×3), produced by averaging H independently denoised pose hypotheses after T′ DDIM reverse steps (as few as 10 timesteps suffice, with 98 ms and 2.2 GB per frame).

## Method

HandDiff formulates hand pose estimation as iterative denoising of a 3D Gaussian noise distribution conditioned on joint-wise and local features. Three key mechanisms address the limitations of generic 3D diffusion: (1) a joint-wise condition extractor uses bias-induced layers to generate per-joint embeddings from concatenated 2D/3D global vectors, providing permutation-specific identity; (2) a local feature sampler queries K-nearest-neighbor 2D pixels and 3D points around each noisy joint (in a relative coordinate system) to supply dense local detail as denoising conditions; and (3) a kinematic correspondence-aware aggregation block applies a GCN over joint-wise conditions with a channel-wise kinematic matrix, then fuses evolved conditions with local features via MLP, replicated four times with max-pooling between pairs. Training follows the standard DDPM forward process with smooth L1 loss on both the denoised coordinates and the linearly-transformed joint-wise conditions; inference uses DDIM with multi-hypothesis sampling (H initial poses from a unit Gaussian) and final averaging.

## Results

On the single-hand benchmarks, HandDiff achieves mean joint errors of 5.72 mm (ICVL), 6.53 mm (MSRA), and 7.38 mm (NYU), setting new state-of-the-art on ICVL and MSRA while placing third on NYU (behind IPNet's 7.17 mm). On the hand-object DexYCB dataset, HandDiff achieves the lowest average error of 8.07 mm, outperforming the previous best IPNet (8.36 mm) across all four official splits (S0–S3). The model requires only 10 DDIM timesteps at inference (98 ms per frame, 2.2 GB memory) to reach near-optimal accuracy, with 10 timesteps yielding 7.44 mm on NYU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HandDiff outperforms the closest multi-modal competitor IPNet [38] on ICVL (5.72 vs 5.76 mm) and MSRA (6.53 vs 6.92 mm), but trails it on NYU (7.38 vs 7.17 mm). On the hand-object DexYCB benchmark, HandDiff beats IPNet across all four splits (e.g., 7.66 vs 8.03 mm on S0; 8.07 vs 8.36 mm average), and surpasses all RGB-based methods (A2J, Spurr et al., METRO, Tse et al., HandOccNet) by large margins of 10–18 mm. Among depth-only 2D CNN methods, HandDiff improves over the best (PHG at 5.97 mm on ICVL) by 0.25 mm, and over point-cloud-only methods like HandFoldingNet (5.95 mm) by 0.23 mm on ICVL. The largest gains appear on MSRA, where HandDiff reduces error by 0.39 mm over IPNet and 0.41 mm over PHG, suggesting the diffusion-based denoising is particularly effective for the more diverse gesture set in that dataset. The paper also shows that success rate curves (Fig. 4) confirm HandDiff's advantage at low error thresholds (<12 mm on ICVL, <15 mm on MSRA, <52 mm on NYU), indicating more reliable per-joint localization rather than just lower mean error.

## Relation to Prior Work

HandDiff extends the diffusion model framework (DDPM/DDIM) from 3D shape generation (3DDPM) into the hand pose estimation domain, but departs from prior diffusion-based pose estimators (D3DP, DiffPoses, DiffuPose) that all follow a two-stage 2D-to-3D lifting scheme requiring a pre-trained 2D regression prior. Instead, HandDiff operates as a single-stage denoiser directly conditioned on 3D-space inputs (depth images and point clouds), and introduces joint-wise conditions and local feature sampling to overcome the permutation-equivariance limitation of global-condition 3D diffusion models. The joint-wise embedding concept is borrowed from HandFoldingNet, and the kinematic correspondence-aware GCN layer draws on graph-based hand pose methods (JGR-P2O, PHG) but repurposes them within a denoising loop rather than a regression head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first diffusion model applied to 3D hand pose estimation, HandDiff demonstrates that probabilistic denoising can model the inherent uncertainty of occluded hand poses more naturally than deterministic regression, achieving SOTA on four benchmarks with practical inference speed (98 ms/frame at 10 timesteps). The ablation revealing that local conditions without permutation-aware joint indicators collapse to ~50 mm error underscores a fundamental challenge: diffusion models cannot resolve joint identity from geometry alone, a constraint that will affect any future generative approach to articulated pose. The stated limitation on interacting hands and the reliance on depth+point-cloud inputs (rather than RGB) leave open questions about generalization to real-world multi-hand scenarios and monocular settings.
