## Problem

Current medical vision-language pre-training methods align images with unstructured radiology reports or complex clinical disease definitions, leading to misalignment with the target disease's visual representation—especially for fine-grained or unseen pathologies. The core gap is that no prior work decomposes disease descriptions into elemental visual aspects (e.g., opacity, texture, shape) to enable fine-grained image-text matching grounded in shared visual knowledge across diseases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240045/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a chest X-ray image (H×W×3) and, for each candidate disease, a set of K+1 textual prompts: 8 structured visual aspect descriptions (opacity, shape, texture, pattern, location, etc.) generated by GPT-4 and curated by two medical experts, plus one fine-grained clinical description from UMLS. It outputs a binary presence probability per disease (N diseases total) and an H×W visual grounding heatmap localizing the abnormal region. The text encoder (ClinicalBERT) is frozen during pre-training; only the ResNet-50 visual encoder and Transformer fusion module are trained.

## Method

MAVL decomposes each of 75 medical findings into 8 shared visual aspects via a semi-automatic pipeline (GPT-4 generation + expert curation), then performs fine-grained matching between the image and every aspect description of the target disease. A dual-head Transformer decouples two learning signals: a contrastive head computes a fine-grained contrastive loss between the attention-pooled image feature and each aspect embedding, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen diseases by linking their aspects to base visual knowledge; a supervised head uses a Transformer decoder with cross-attention (aspect embeddings as queries, visual features as keys/values) to ground aspects in the image and predict disease presence via cross-entropy, maximizing discriminative power on seen categories. A location contrastive loss additionally matches predicted location embeddings to ground-truth anatomical sites. The combined objective L = α·L_cl + β·L_sup + γ·L_loc is trained end-to-end on MIMIC-CXR, and at inference the appropriate head is selected based on whether the query disease is in the base set.

## Results

On zero-shot classification of seen diseases across five datasets, MAVL achieves the best F1 scores, improving over MedKLIP by up to 8.56% (e.g., 26.25% vs. 24.18% on ChestX-ray14, 77.95% vs. 72.73% on SIIM-ACR). For unseen diseases, the contrastive head (MAVL-Con) reaches 83.86% AUC on Covid-19 CXR-2 and 70.06% AUC on PadChest-rare, surpassing MedKLIP by 7.58% and 17.26% respectively. On fine-tuning with only 1% data, MAVL gains 7.38% AUC over MedKLIP on SIIM Pneumothorax classification and 9.37% Dice on RSNA Pneumonia segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAVL consistently outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest baseline MedKLIP (ICCV 2023), MAVL improves F1 by up to 8.56% on seen diseases (e.g., 65.47% vs. 63.67% on CheXpert) and AUC by up to 17.26% on rare unseen diseases (70.06% vs. 59.75% on PadChest-rare). The contrastive head (MAVL-Con) is particularly effective for unseen categories, beating MedKLIP by 16.76% AUC on PadChest-unseen (70.42% vs. 60.31%), while the supervised head (MAVL-Sup) excels on seen fine-grained diseases. Among contrastive baselines, CheXzero is the strongest, but MAVL-Con still surpasses it by 5.58% AUC on PadChest-unseen and 7.65% on PadChest-rare. Earlier contrastive methods (ConVIRT, GLoRIA, BioViL, BioViL-T) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 20% AUC on several datasets, confirming that the multi-aspect decomposition and dual-head design address fundamental limitations of report-level matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MedKLIP (Wu et al., ICCV 2023), which reformulated medical VLP as supervised multi-label classification by extracting disease entities from reports via RadGraph and matching them against UMLS clinical descriptions. The key departure is replacing monolithic disease descriptions with a decomposition into fine-grained visual aspects (texture, shape, opacity, location, etc.) generated by GPT-4 and curated by medical experts, enabling the model to match images against a shared visual knowledge base rather than complex clinical terminologies. Additionally, the dual-head Transformer decouples contrastive and supervised learning signals, addressing the seen/unseen disease dichotomy that single-objective methods like MedKLIP handle suboptimally. The framework also extends the broader CLIP-style medical VLP lineage (ConVIRT, GLoRIA, BioViL, CheXzero) by introducing structured text inputs and a grounding-capable fusion module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The aspect decomposition strategy offers a principled way to handle the fine-grained, long-tailed nature of medical imaging, where novel or rare diseases share visual characteristics with common ones. The 17.26% AUC gain on rare unseen diseases and consistent improvements across seven datasets suggest the approach could become a useful template for other medical imaging modalities beyond chest X-rays. However, the method is limited to 75 pre-defined findings, relies on GPT-4 for description generation (introducing hallucination risk mitigated only by expert curation), and has not been validated on non-radiological imaging or 3D volumes. Open questions include whether the aspect taxonomy scales to thousands of findings, whether the dual-head design generalizes to other zero-shot settings, and whether the visual aspect descriptions can be learned end-to-end rather than hand-curated.
