## Problem

Vanilla MLPs used in implicit neural representation suffer from spectral bias, where gradients are dominated by low-frequency error components, causing the network to learn coarse structure first and struggle to capture high-frequency details like fine textures and intricate geometry. Existing remedies modify the input (positional encoding) or the activation function (periodic activations), but the paper identifies an underexplored third axis: the parameterization of the weight matrices themselves. The key question is whether reparameterizing MLP weights with structured bases can theoretically and practically narrow the gradient-magnitude gap between low- and high-frequency loss components.

## Input / Output

The network consumes spatial coordinates (2D pixel positions for image fitting, 3D voxel positions for SDF shape representation, or 3D position plus viewing direction for NeRF view synthesis) and outputs the corresponding signal values (RGB triplets, signed distances, or radiance). A critical design constraint is that the Fourier reparameterization is purely a training-time mechanism: after optimization, the learned coefficient matrix Λ and the fixed basis matrix B are multiplied together to recover a standard weight matrix W, so inference cost and network architecture remain identical to the baseline MLP. The method is also orthogonal to input-side modifications (e.g., positional encoding) and activation-function choices (ReLU, SIREN), allowing it to be stacked on top of existing INR pipelines without architectural changes.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces each weight matrix W^(n) in the MLP with a product W^(n) = Λ^(n) B^(n), where B^(n) is a fixed matrix of M cosine-based Fourier bases (constructed from 2F frequencies × P phases) and Λ^(n) is the only trainable parameter. Theoretically, Theorem 2 proves that for any multi-layer MLP, there exist basis matrices such that the ratio of high-frequency to low-frequency gradient magnitudes with respect to the reparameterized coefficients is at least as large as the worst-case ratio over the original weights, thereby providing a principled route to alleviating spectral bias. In practice, the authors show that fixed Fourier bases (rather than random or randomly-initialized bases) are essential for this effect, as confirmed by ablation experiments comparing Fourier, random, and randomly-initialized reparameterization. A modified Xavier-style initialization for Λ accounts for the energy of the fixed bases to preserve constant-variance propagation, and the sampling interval for the continuous cosine bases is set to one maximum period T_max = 2πF. The method adds only ~17% per-epoch training time while yielding up to 86% relative accuracy improvement on the 1D benchmark and consistent PSNR/IOU gains across image fitting, SDF shape representation, and NeRF view synthesis.

## Results

On the Kodak 24 dataset (first 8 images), Fourier reparameterization (FR) consistently improves PSNR across all three MLP architectures: MLP+ReLU+FR achieves 22.44 dB average PSNR vs. 21.33 dB for vanilla MLP+ReLU (+1.11 dB); MLP+ReLU+PE+FR reaches 30.59 dB vs. 27.18 dB for MLP+ReLU+PE (+3.41 dB); and MLP+Sin+FR attains 36.34 dB vs. 35.31 dB for MLP+Sin (+1.03 dB). The largest absolute gain is observed when combining FR with positional encoding, where the method adds over 3 dB of PSNR. On the simple 1D function approximation task, FR narrows the frequency-specific error gap between low- and high-frequency components and produces a more balanced NTK eigenvalue distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares FR against three key INR techniques. Against Tancik et al.'s positional encoding [39] (MLP+ReLU+PE, avg. 27.18 dB), adding FR yields a substantial +3.41 dB improvement to 30.59 dB, the largest gain among all architecture combinations. Against Sitzmann et al.'s SIREN periodic activation [35] (MLP+Sin, avg. 35.31 dB), FR provides a more modest but consistent +1.03 dB boost to 36.34 dB, with per-image gains ranging from +0.35 to +2.13 dB. Against Mildenhall et al.'s NeRF [26] for view synthesis on the Blender dataset, FR applied to the last three hidden layers produces visually sharper textures and more accurate light reflections (Fig. 7), though no quantitative PSNR table is provided in the main text for this comparison. In all cases, FR acts as a plug-and-play training-time modification that does not alter the network architecture or inference cost, and it is compatible with both positional encoding and periodic activations simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: weight reparameterization (developed for CNN inference efficiency in RepVGG and DiracNets) and spectral bias mitigation in INR (addressed via positional encoding or periodic activations). The key conceptual departure is providing a theoretical argument (Theorem 2) that reparameterizing MLP weights with appropriate bases alters the gradient magnitude ratio between high- and low-frequency loss components, thereby alleviating low-frequency bias during training. Prior reparameterization work targeted convolutional architectures and inference-time merging, while prior spectral bias work modified inputs or activations; this paper is the first to connect reparameterization with training dynamics in MLPs and to use fixed Fourier bases as the reparameterization dictionary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is architecture-agnostic and composable with existing INR techniques (positional encoding, SIREN), making it a practical drop-in improvement for a wide range of representation tasks. However, the theoretical guarantee is an existence result (there exists a set of basis matrices satisfying the inequality), and the fixed Fourier basis is a heuristic choice whose optimality is not established; the paper acknowledges that the optimal basis depends on training data. Open questions include whether other structured bases (wavelets, learned dictionaries) could yield further gains, how the method scales to very deep or wide networks, and whether the modest 1–3 dB PSNR improvements persist on more challenging 3D reconstruction or large-scale view synthesis benchmarks beyond the Blender dataset.
