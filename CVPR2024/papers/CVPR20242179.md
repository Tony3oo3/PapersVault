## Problem

Existing 3D human motion generation methods either model humans in isolation or place them in static scenes, ignoring the dynamic interdependency between human and object motion that characterizes real interactions. No prior approach jointly generates both human and object motion from text while explicitly modeling the physical contact that constrains their coherence, leading to artifacts such as floating objects or semantically incoherent interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242179/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a short text description (encoded by a frozen CLIP-ViT-B/32) and static object geometry as a uniformly sampled point cloud in world coordinates (encoded by a PointNet pretrained on parts segmentation). It produces a sequence of F frames (32 for CHAIRS, 64 for BEHAVE at 20 fps) where each frame contains full SMPL body parameters (63-D pose, 10-D shape, 3-D rotation, 3-D translation), a 6D-rotation object transformation, and 128 contact distances measuring the distance from uniformly distributed body-surface markers to the nearest object surface point. The use of full SMPL surface geometry rather than joint skeletons is what enables meaningful contact reasoning.

## Method

CG-HOI jointly denoises human, object, and contact representations in a single diffusion process using a U-Net with per-modality residual blocks and custom cross-attention modules where each modality attends to the other two, enabling learned interdependency. A key architectural choice is contact-based object transformation weighting: instead of predicting one object pose per frame, the network generates 128 object motion hypotheses (one per body contact marker) and aggregates them with weights inversely proportional to predicted contact distance, so body parts in closer contact dominate the final object motion. At inference, a contact-based diffusion guidance step computes the gradient of a cost function measuring the discrepancy between predicted and recomputed contact distances, and uses this gradient to refine the denoising trajectory, penalizing physically implausible configurations like floating objects. The model is trained with supervised L1/L2 losses on all three modalities and uses classifier-free guidance (10% conditioning dropout) for improved fidelity at test time.

## Results

On the Text-Cond. HOI task (the paper's primary contribution), CG-HOI achieves R-Precision (top-3) of 0.62 and FID of 6.31 on BEHAVE, and 0.74 and 6.45 on CHAIRS, outperforming MDM (0.49/9.21 and 0.53/9.23) and InterDiff (0.53/7.53) on both datasets. In the Motion-Cond. HOI setting, CG-HOI reaches R-Precision 0.71 and FID 3.52 on BEHAVE, surpassing InterDiff (0.68/3.86). For human-only generation, CG-HOI also leads with R-Precision 0.60 and FID 4.26 on BEHAVE versus MDM (0.52/4.54) and InterDiff (0.49/5.36). A 32-participant user study confirms significant perceptual preference for CG-HOI over baselines in both text coherence and overall realism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CG-HOI is compared against two baselines: MDM [73], a state-of-the-art text-conditioned human-only motion diffusion model (extended here to also generate object sequences), and InterDiff [88], a physics-informed diffusion method for HOI that conditions on observed motion. In the Text-Cond. HOI setting (the paper's novel task), CG-HOI outperforms both baselines on all four metrics across both datasets: on BEHAVE it improves R-Precision from 0.49 (MDM) and 0.53 (InterDiff) to 0.62, and reduces FID from 9.21/7.53 to 6.31; on CHAIRS it improves R-Precision from 0.53/0.69 to 0.74 and FID from 9.23/7.53 to 6.45. In the Motion-Cond. HOI setting (InterDiff's original task), CG-HOI also wins, achieving R-Precision 0.71 vs. 0.68 and FID 3.52 vs. 3.86 on BEHAVE, and 0.79 vs. 0.67 and 4.01 vs. 4.83 on CHAIRS. For human-only generation, CG-HOI leads MDM by 0.08 R-Precision and 0.28 FID on BEHAVE, and by 0.06 R-Precision and 0.75 FID on CHAIRS. The paper also reports a 32-participant user study where participants significantly prefer CG-HOI outputs for both text adherence and overall realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based 3D human motion generation line (MDM, MoDiffuse, MotionDiffuse) by jointly modeling object motion and explicit contact distances alongside human body surface representations, rather than treating the human in isolation or in a static scene. It departs from the closest prior work, InterDiff, which forecasts HOI continuations from observed motion, by formulating a text-conditioned generative model that produces both human and object sequences from scratch. The contact representation draws on the hand-object interaction literature (binary contact labels, signed distances) but generalizes it to full-body surface-to-object distance fields, and the cross-attention architecture for multi-modality feature sharing is a natural extension of standard diffusion U-Net designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first text-conditioned dynamic HOI generator, this work opens a path toward language-controllable character animation and scene population where objects move realistically in response to human actions. The demonstrated ability to condition on an object trajectory without retraining suggests the learned human-object interdependency is genuinely captured, which could benefit robotics and simulation. Key limitations remain: single-object interactions only, dependence on expensive 3D HOI captures for training, and manual text annotations, leaving multi-object manipulation, weakly supervised learning, and richer scene-level interaction as important open directions.
