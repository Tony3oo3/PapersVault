## Problem

Existing MIL-based WSI classifiers depend on large quantities of bag-level labels and are sensitive to data distribution shifts, while VLM-based pathology methods rely on simple class-name-replacement prompts that lack pathological prior knowledge and require expensive pre-training on massive image-text pairs. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing LLM-generated dual-scale visual descriptive text prompts and lightweight decoders that efficiently transfer a frozen CLIP model to WSI classification under few-shot settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241699/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a giga-pixel WSI at two magnifications (5× and 10×), cropped into non-overlapping 256×256 patches, along with a text question prompt (e.g., 'What are the visually descriptive characteristics of {class name} at low and high resolution in the whole slide image?') fed to a frozen LLM (GPT-3.5) to generate dual-scale descriptive text prompts. The output is a slide-level class probability distribution over C categories, computed by combining cosine similarities between dual-scale slide features and refined text features weighted by α_l and α_h.

## Method

ViLa-MIL builds on a frozen CLIP (ResNet-50 image encoder + Transformer text encoder) and introduces three key components. First, a dual-scale visual descriptive text prompt is generated by a frozen LLM, where the low-scale prompt describes global tumor structures (e.g., glandular patterns) visible at low magnification and the high-scale prompt describes local cellular details (e.g., nuclear morphology) at high magnification, with CoOp-style learnable vectors prepended to each. Second, a prototype-guided patch decoder uses 16 learnable prototype vectors as queries in a cross-attention layer with patch features as keys and values, progressively grouping semantically similar patches into prototypes before an attention-based fusion step produces the final slide-level representation. Third, a context-guided text decoder refines the frozen text encoder outputs via cross-attention where text features serve as queries and concatenated local patch and global prototype features serve as keys and values, bridging the modality gap. The entire model is trained end-to-end with a cross-entropy loss over the combined dual-scale similarity scores.

## Results

Under the 16-shot setting, ViLa-MIL achieves AUC of 84.3%, 92.6%, and 74.7% on TIHD-RCC, TCGA-RCC, and TCGA-Lung respectively, outperforming the best MIL-based baselines by 1.7–7.2% in AUC and 2.1–7.3% in F1 across the three datasets. On the in-house TIHD-RCC dataset, ViLa-MIL reaches 68.7% F1 and 68.8% ACC, surpassing the strongest baseline GTMIL (61.4% F1, 62.2% ACC) by over 7 points. In the cross-domain evaluation (TIHD→TCGA), ViLa-MIL maintains a 5.5% AUC advantage over the best baseline, demonstrating superior generalization under domain shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViLa-MIL outperforms all seven MIL-based baselines across every metric on all three datasets. The largest margins appear on TIHD-RCC, where ViLa-MIL (84.3% AUC) surpasses GTMIL (77.1%) by 7.2% AUC and DTMIL (70.3%) by 14.0% AUC. On TCGA-RCC, the gap is narrower—ViLa-MIL (92.6% AUC) leads CLAM-MB (90.9%) by 1.7% AUC and IBMIL (90.5%) by 2.1% AUC—reflecting the already high performance of attention-based methods on this larger dataset. On TCGA-Lung, ViLa-MIL (74.7% AUC) beats IBMIL (69.2%) by 5.5% AUC and TransMIL (64.2%) by 10.5% AUC. In the cross-domain TIHD→TCGA transfer (Table 2), ViLa-MIL maintains a 5.5% AUC lead over the best baseline (IBMIL at 78.2%), confirming that the dual-scale visual descriptive text prompt provides domain-invariant diagnostic guidance. Notably, even the single-scale variants (ViLa-MIL Low/High) already exceed most baselines, indicating that the language prior contributes substantially independent of the dual-scale aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViLa-MIL sits at the intersection of two lines of work: MIL-based WSI classification (ABMIL, CLAM, TransMIL, DSMIL, GTMIL, DTMIL, IBMIL) and VLM-based pathology methods (MI-Zero, PLIP, QUILT). It builds directly on CLIP's frozen dual-tower architecture and CoOp's learnable prompt mechanism, but departs from prior VLM-pathology methods by eliminating the need for large-scale pathological image-text pair collection and pre-training. Instead of handcrafted class-name-replacement templates or label-optimized prompts (as in TOP), it uses a frozen LLM to generate dual-scale visual descriptive text prompts that mirror the pathologist's multi-magnification diagnostic workflow, paired with lightweight prototype-guided and context-guided decoders to bridge the gap between patch-level CLIP features and slide-level classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in computational pathology: the scarcity of labeled WSIs and the prohibitive cost of collecting large-scale pathological image-text pairs for VLM pre-training. By showing that LLM-generated descriptive prompts plus lightweight decoders can outperform methods trained on 200k+ pathological image-text pairs under few-shot settings, it suggests a more accessible pathway for deploying VLMs in pathology. Open questions include whether the static LLM-generated prompts can be adapted per-image or per-patient, whether the approach extends beyond subtyping to staging or prognosis tasks, and whether the frozen ResNet-50 CLIP encoder is a bottleneck compared to more powerful vision backbones. The cross-domain results (5.5% AUC gain over best baseline) are encouraging but tested on only one pair of centers.
