## Problem

Dense binary classification models (e.g., salient object detection) are systematically over-confident, with prediction confidence far exceeding actual accuracy, yet model calibration has remained under-investigated in this setting. Existing calibration methods—post-processing, loss-based penalties, or fixed label smoothing—do not exploit the spatial structure of the image space to regularise confidence in a distance-aware manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241454/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images resized to 384×384 and produces a per-pixel sigmoid probability map (dense binary classification) indicating saliency. During training, each labeled image spawns M=3 vicinal copies by adding isotropic Gaussian noise with a randomly drawn standard deviation σ~U(0,γ], and each copy receives a softened label whose confidence decays monotonically with the L2 distance from the original, yielding a spatially consistent confidence field across all pixels of the augmented label.

## Method

SCVRM is a Monte Carlo data augmentation technique that samples vicinal images from a Gaussian distribution centred on each labeled image, where the standard deviation σ is itself drawn from a uniform distribution U(0,γ] to avoid the high-dimensional 'bubbling' effect of fixed-variance Gaussians. The L2 distance between vicinal and labeled images is approximated by σ√d, and the label softening strength is computed via a Gaussian function φ_G(‖ε̃‖₂;η)=1−exp(−‖ε̃‖₂/η) that maps distance to a smoothing coefficient in [0,1). The softened label is then a convex combination of the groundtruth and a uniform categorical distribution, applied uniformly across all pixels to maintain spatial consistency. The model (U-Net with ResNet-50 encoder) is trained with binary cross-entropy on both the original and augmented data, resampling the vicinal set each epoch. The paper further proves that under a single-layer logistic regression with binary cross-entropy, SCVRM decomposes exactly into standard VRM plus a regularisation term that penalises over-confident predictions, providing a theoretical justification for the calibration benefit.

## Results

On the six salient object detection test datasets, SCVRM achieves the lowest ECE across all benchmarks, with headline values of 0.78% on DUTS-TE, 1.64% on DUT-OMRON, 1.91% on PASCAL-S, 3.90% on SOD, 0.44% on ECSSD, and 0.78% on HKU-IS. On the 500-image Out-of-Distribution texture set (DTD), SCVRM reduces ECE to 11.93% and OE to 8.26% while simultaneously achieving 83.93% accuracy, compared to 18.31% ECE and 61.93% accuracy for the second-best method (ASLP). The largest ECE reductions over the runner-up are 44.3% on DUTS-TE, 29.1% on SOD, 17.6% on DUT-OMRON, and 17.3% on PASCAL-S.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCVRM outperforms all 27 baselines on ECE across all six in-distribution SOD test datasets. Against the strongest calibration baseline ASLP [31], SCVRM reduces ECE by 44.3% on DUTS-TE (0.78 vs. 1.40), 29.1% on SOD (3.90 vs. 5.50), 17.6% on DUT-OMRON (1.64 vs. 1.99), and 17.3% on PASCAL-S (1.91 vs. 2.31); on ECSSD and HKU-IS the gap is marginal (0.44 vs. 0.48 and 0.78 vs. 0.79 respectively). Compared to Mixup [51], SCVRM achieves substantially lower ECE on DUTS-TE (0.78 vs. 2.45) and SOD (3.90 vs. 5.82), while Mixup shows inconsistent behaviour across datasets (e.g., 1.41 ECE on ECSSD but 3.83 on HKU-IS). On the OoD texture set, SCVRM reduces ECE by 34.8% and OE by 49.5% over ASLP, and also achieves 22 percentage points higher accuracy (83.93% vs. 61.93%), indicating that the vicinal-space exploration with distance-based label softening generalises better to distributional shift than label-perturbation-only approaches. Among SOD architectures without explicit calibration (e.g., MSRNet, ICON, GCPANet), SCVRM consistently achieves 2–3× lower ECE, confirming that the calibration gain comes from the training objective rather than architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCVRM extends Vicinal Risk Minimisation (VRM) [Chapelle et al., Vapnik] by replacing the fixed hard label assigned to all vicinal images with a distance-dependent softened label, so that label confidence decays monotonically with L2 distance from the labeled image. It departs from Mixup, the most prominent VRM variant, in two key ways: (1) the vicinal distribution is a full Gaussian in image space rather than restricted to line segments between labeled pairs, and (2) label softening is governed by distance from the center rather than by the convex combination weight. The paper further provides a theoretical decomposition showing that, under logistic regression with binary cross-entropy, SCVRM equals VRM plus an explicit over-confidence penalty, connecting it to the broader family of confidence-regularising objectives (Brier loss, focal loss, confidence penalty).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a simple, plug-and-play data augmentation strategy that simultaneously improves calibration and classification accuracy across multiple dense binary tasks (SOD, camouflage detection, smoke detection) and even multi-class segmentation, without requiring post-hoc calibration steps. The strong OoD results (83.9% accuracy on texture images vs. 62% for the previous best) suggest that distance-based label confidence reduction implicitly teaches the model to express uncertainty beyond the training distribution. Limitations include the theoretical proof being restricted to single-layer logistic regression, the reliance on the approximation ‖ε̃‖₂ = σ√d in high dimensions, and evaluation primarily on relatively small-scale dense classification benchmarks; whether the approach transfers to large-scale classification or generative model calibration remains open.
