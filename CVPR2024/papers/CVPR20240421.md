## Problem

Existing sparse-to-dense depth estimation methods are trained on fixed sensor configurations (e.g., 64-line Velodyne LiDAR or 500 random Kinect samples) and suffer from three systematic biases—sparsity, pattern, and scan range—that severely degrade performance when the input sensor type or scene configuration changes. The paper identifies that jointly representing image and depth features, as most recent methods do, makes the learned affinity maps invariant to input variations, preventing a single model from generalizing across diverse active depth sensors.

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image (304×228 for NYUv2, 240×1216 for KITTI) and a sparse depth map of arbitrary density, pattern, and range—from as few as 1 depth point or 1 LiDAR line up to 500 random samples or 64 lines. It outputs a full-resolution dense depth map in absolute metric scale, extending beyond the sensor's native scan range by leveraging the foundation model's relative depth prediction. The sparse depth input is consumed as a template for the prompt encoder rather than being jointly encoded with the image, which is the key architectural departure from prior spatial propagation methods.

## Method

The core contribution is a depth prompt module that reinterprets prompt learning as adaptive spatial propagation. A ResNet34-based prompt encoder encodes the sparse depth into a unified embedding space, producing a prompt embedding and multi-scale features that are fused with image features from a frozen monocular depth foundation model (DepthFormer) via a decoder to construct an adaptive affinity map. Only bias terms of the foundation model are updated (0.1% of parameters, ~0.1M out of 53.4M), preserving its pre-trained knowledge while adapting to new sensor distributions. A least-squares solver aligns the foundation model's relative depth with the sparse absolute-scale measurements to produce a globally consistent initial depth for propagation, enabling coverage of regions with no seed points. Random Depth Augmentation (RDA) during training—sampling fewer points from denser ground truth—further mitigates sparsity bias and is shown to be transferable to other methods.

## Results

On KITTI DC (outdoor), the method achieves RMSE of 1.1465 m at standard 32-line density and maintains 2.8234 m RMSE even at extreme 1-line sparsity, outperforming the best baseline (NLSPN: 8.8244 m) by 3.1×. On NYUv2 (indoor), it achieves 0.1435 m RMSE at 200 samples and 0.4040 m RMSE at just 1 depth point, versus 1.1929 m for the best baseline (NLSPN). The method consistently achieves the best RMSE, MAE, and DELTA1 across all sparsity levels on both datasets, with the performance gap widening dramatically as input sparsity increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven baselines (CSPN, S2D, NLSPN, DySPN, CostDCNet, CompletionFormer, SAN) across every sparsity level on both NYUv2 and KITTI DC. At standard density (200 samples / 32 lines), the margin is modest: on KITTI 32-line, Ours achieves 1.1465 m RMSE vs. NLSPN's 1.1894 m and CompletionFormer's 1.2513 m. The advantage becomes dramatic at extreme sparsity: on KITTI 1-line, Ours reaches 2.8234 m RMSE while the next-best SAN achieves 4.4444 m and NLSPN degrades to 8.8244 m; on NYUv2 1-sample, Ours scores 0.4040 m RMSE versus NLSPN's 1.1929 m. SAN, designed for sparsity-agnostic completion via late fusion, is the closest competitor at extreme sparsity but still trails by 36% RMSE on KITTI 1-line. The paper also shows that applying its Random Depth Augmentation (RDA) to NLSPN and CompletionFormer improves their sparse-condition performance (Table 7), confirming that the sparsity robustness gain is partly attributable to the training strategy rather than solely the architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets prompt learning (inspired by SAM and NLP in-context learning) as spatial propagation for depth completion, departing from prior spatial propagation networks (CSPN, NLSPN, DySPN, CompletionFormer) that learn fixed affinity maps tied to specific sensor patterns. It builds on DepthFormer as a frozen monocular depth foundation model and applies bias tuning (from TinyTL) to update only 0.1% of parameters, disentangling depth and image modalities rather than jointly encoding them. The least-squares alignment of relative foundation-model depth with absolute-scale sparse measurements is a practical addition not present in prior spatial propagation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sensor-agnostic framing addresses a real deployment gap: most depth completion methods are locked to the density, pattern, and range of their training sensor, yet real-world systems mix Velodyne LiDAR, Kinect, RealSense, and Apple LiDAR. The demonstrated zero/few-shot transfer to unseen sensors (Apple LiDAR, 32-line Velodyne, RealSense) suggests a path toward a single depth model for heterogeneous sensor suites. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video/sequential depth, whether bias tuning remains sufficient for substantially different sensor geometries (e.g., multi-view or event cameras), and whether the least-squares global alignment degrades in scenes with large depth discontinuities or partial occlusions.
