## Problem

Prior vision-language models scaled their components unilaterally—Flamingo scaled the language side to 70B with a fixed 435M vision encoder, GIT scaled vision to 4.8B with a 300M decoder—leaving open whether jointly scaling both components to very large sizes yields compounding gains. PaLI-X investigates this by scaling the vision encoder to 22B and the language backbone to 32B, and asks what training recipe (particularly the choice of objective mixture) is needed to make such a model perform well across a broad, diverse set of tasks simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model accepts one or more images (processed independently by ViT-22B at resolutions from 224×224 up to 756×756) plus a text prompt specifying the task and target language; for few-shot, it additionally receives N image-text exemplar pairs, and for video it receives 8 or 16 sampled frames. Visual embeddings are patch-level with no spatial pooling, so an n-frame input with k patches per frame yields n×k visual tokens concatenated with text embeddings. Outputs are always generated text: captions, open-ended answers, bounding-box coordinates serialized as text (pix2seq format), or class labels, with the model optionally accepting externally extracted OCR text as supplementary input for text-heavy tasks.

## Method

PaLI-X pairs a ViT-22B visual encoder (further pre-trained with an OCR classification objective on WebLI) with a 50-layer UL2-based encoder-decoder (32B parameters), achieving a roughly 40–60% parameter split between vision and language. Training proceeds in two stages: stage 1 freezes the ViT and trains the remaining parameters on 2.2B examples at 224×224 using a 13-objective mixture (span corruption, split-captioning, VQA, VQG, split-OCR, pix2struct, object detection, image-token prediction, episodic captioning, and video captioning); stage 2 continues with only OCR-related and detection objectives while gradually increasing resolution to 756×756. The key training insight is that combining prefix-completion and masked-token completion objectives (inherited from UL2) improves the Pareto frontier between fine-tuning and few-shot performance, and that introducing Episodic WebLI data (URL-clustered image-text groups) is essential for developing in-context learning capability. The model also demonstrates that co-training the high-capacity vision encoder with image classification and OCR label classification yields significant downstream gains on text-in-image tasks.

## Results

PaLI-X (55B) achieves 86.1 accuracy on VQAv2 (test-std), surpassing PaLI (84.3), Flamingo (82.1), and BEiT-3 (84.0), and matching the much larger PaLM-E (562B) on OKVQA at 66.1. On TallyQA counting, PaLI-X reaches 86.0 (simple) and 75.6 (complex), outperforming the specialized counting model MoVie by +11.1 and +18.8 points respectively. On text-heavy document understanding benchmarks with OCR input, PaLI-X achieves 81.4 on AI2D (+42.9 over prior SoTA), 72.3 on ChartQA, and 77.3 on OCRVQA. For object detection on LVIS, the detection-tuned model reaches 30.64 AP overall and 31.42 AP on rare classes, achieving parity between rare and common classes without specialized sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaLI-X consistently outperforms its direct predecessor PaLI (17B) across all core benchmarks: VQAv2 (86.1 vs 84.3), TallyQA simple (86.0 vs 81.7), TallyQA complex (75.6 vs 70.9), and OKVQA (66.1 vs 64.5), while matching on COCO (149.2 vs 149.1). Against the much larger Flamingo (80B), PaLI-X wins on VQAv2 (86.1 vs 82.1), OKVQA (66.1 vs 57.8), and COCO (149.2 vs 138.1), and also surpasses Flamingo on few-shot COCO captioning (107.6 vs 103.2 at 4 shots). On the specialized counting benchmark TallyQA, PaLI-X dramatically outperforms the purpose-built MoVie model by +11.1 (simple) and +18.8 (complex), demonstrating emergent counting capability from scale. On text-heavy document benchmarks with OCR input, PaLI-X achieves a +42.9 point jump on AI2D (81.4 vs 38.5) and +26.8 on ChartQA (72.3 vs 45.5) over prior SoTA (Pix2Struct, DePlot). For object detection on LVIS, PaLI-X (30.64 AP) trails OwLViT-L/16 (34.7 AP) on overall AP but matches it on rare classes (31.42 vs 31.2), and achieves 12.36 AP zero-shot without any detection-specific fine-tuning. On video tasks, PaLI-X (16 frames) surpasses prior SOTA on ActivityNet Captioning (54.9 vs 52.5), ActivityNet QA (49.4 vs 44.7), and NExT-QA (38.3 vs 33.5), while remaining slightly behind on MSR-VTT Captioning (76.8 vs 80.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

PaLI-X extends the PaLI line of work by jointly scaling both the vision encoder (to ViT-22B, the largest dense ViT) and the language backbone (to a 32B UL2 variant), departing from prior efforts that scaled only one component unilaterally (Flamingo froze the language model; GIT scaled only vision). The key methodological departure is adapting UL2's mixture-of-objectives (prefix-completion and masked-token completion) to vision-language pretraining, which the authors show improves the Pareto frontier between fine-tuning and few-shot performance. The introduction of Episodic WebLI data for in-context learning and the co-training of the vision encoder with OCR classification objectives are additional contributions not present in PaLI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that balanced co-scaling of vision and language components beyond 50B parameters continues to yield gains, and that a single model can be multitask fine-tuned across highly diverse benchmarks (captioning, VQA, document understanding, UI tasks) without the performance degradation previously reported by Unified-IO. The emergent capabilities—complex counting and multilingual object detection—suggest that sufficiently large V&L models develop compositional abilities not explicitly trained, raising open questions about the boundary of such emergence and whether it generalizes further. A notable limitation is the observed tension between few-shot and fine-tuning performance (PaLI-X trails Flamingo on few-shot VQA), and the paper does not fully resolve how to reconcile these two regimes in a single model.
