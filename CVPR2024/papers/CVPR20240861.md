## Problem

Previous spectra-wise transformers for CASSI reconstruction apply multi-head division in the spectral (channel) dimension, which fragments global spectral information and introduces a 'mean effect' that erases local spatial differences within each band. Additionally, existing methods ignore the unique spatial degradation caused by the CASSI imaging process (masking, shifting, and compression), which disproportionately distorts certain spectral bands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240861/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D compressed CASSI measurement (a coded-aperture snapshot) and the corresponding 2D mask pattern (repeated across 28 spectral channels to form a 3D mask) as input, and produces a reconstructed 3D hyperspectral image with 28 bands spanning 450–650 nm. Training uses randomly cropped 256×256 or 384×384 patches from CAVE and KAIST datasets, with a fixed dispersion shift of 2 pixels between adjacent bands. The network is available in three scales (SSR-S/M/L with 3/6/9 stages) to trade off parameters (1.73–5.18 M) and FLOPs (26–79 G) against reconstruction quality.

## Method

SSR is a multi-stage network where each stage applies Linear Manifold Projection (LMP) for physics-guided refinement, followed by an SSRUnet containing the two core modules. Window-based Spectral Self-Attention (WSSA) splits features into 8×8 spatial windows and computes spectral self-attention within each window, capturing global inter-band similarity while preserving local spatial differences and avoiding the mean effect of whole-band tokenization. The Spatial Rectification Block (ARB) addresses two issues: a Convolution Modulated Block (CMB) uses 11×11 depth-wise convolutions to enable inter-window interaction and improve spatial representation, while a Spatial Alignment Block (SAB) learns a shared spatial texture map and per-band spectral weights so that low-quality bands can borrow structural information from high-quality bands. The architecture is trained end-to-end with RMSE loss under a multi-stage loss schedule, using Adam and Cosine Annealing on a single GPU.

## Results

On 10 simulation scenes from KAIST, SSR-L (9 stages) achieves an average PSNR of 40.69 dB and SSIM of 0.978, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art RDLUF (39.57 dB, 0.974) by 1.12 dB while using fewer FLOPs (78.93 vs. 115.34 GFLOPs). The smaller SSR-S model (3 stages, 1.73M params, 26.37 GFLOPs) reaches 39.19 dB / 0.971 SSIM, already outperforming most prior methods at the lowest computational cost. SSR-M (6 stages) achieves 40.20 dB / 0.975 SSIM at only 52.65 GFLOPs, exceeding RDLUF by 0.63 dB with less than half the FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSR-L outperforms the strongest prior method RDLUF (CVPR 2023) by 1.12 dB in average PSNR (40.69 vs. 39.57) while requiring 32% fewer FLOPs (78.93 vs. 115.34 GFLOPs). Against DAUHST (NeurIPS 2022), SSR-L gains 2.33 dB at nearly identical FLOPs (78.93 vs. 79.50), and against BIRNAT (TPAMI 2023) it gains 3.11 dB with 27× fewer FLOPs (78.93 vs. 2122.66). The compact SSR-S model (1.73M params, 26.37 GFLOPs) already surpasses MST, HDNet, HerosNet, CST, and BIRNAT in PSNR, and is within 0.38 dB of RDLUF at less than a quarter of its FLOPs. SSR-M exceeds RDLUF by 0.63 dB at under half the FLOPs. The parameter-sharing variant SSR-L* (1.73M params) still achieves 40.27 dB, outperforming all baselines except RDLUF by a narrow margin, confirming that the gains are architectural rather than purely parameter-driven.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spectra-wise transformer lineage (MST, MST++, RDLUF) that treats spectral bands as tokens for self-attention in CASSI reconstruction. Its key conceptual departure is identifying that the standard multi-head division in the spectral (channel) dimension causes a 'mean effect'—averaging local spatial similarities across a band—and proposes Window-based Spectral Self-Attention (WSSA) to preserve local differences while modeling global spectral information. The Spatial Rectification Block (ARB) borrows the Convolution Modulated Block from Conv2Former [16] for inter-window interaction and introduces a novel spatial alignment strategy specifically targeting the degradation introduced by CASSI's mask-shift-compression imaging process, a factor neglected by prior spatial and spectral networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mean-effect analysis provides a useful diagnostic for why naive spectral-wise multi-head attention underperforms, and the spatial alignment strategy addresses a degradation mode (band-dependent spatial quality loss from the CASSI imaging chain) that prior methods have not explicitly modeled. However, the task and evaluation protocol (CAVE/KAIST, 28 bands, fixed mask) are well-trodden, and the gains—while consistent across scales—are incremental over strong recent baselines like RDLUF. Open questions include whether the window-based spectral attention and spatial alignment transfer to other spectral imaging modalities (e.g., pushbroom, dispersive) or to higher-band-count settings where the mean effect may manifest differently.
