## Problem

Existing GZSL methods assume unseen classes are similar to seen classes and rely heavily on transferring knowledge from seen to unseen classes, causing them to fail dramatically when unseen classes are dissimilar to seen classes. This paper introduces a more practical GZSL setting where unseen classes can be either similar or dissimilar to seen classes, and demonstrates that all state-of-the-art methods suffer severe performance drops (often below 10% harmonic mean) on dissimilar unseen classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240992/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes visual features extracted by a pretrained ResNet-101 backbone and semantic embeddings from a language model (W2V or CLIP) as primary inputs, plus external class names from a large-scale dataset (ImageNet-1K or ImageNet-21K) and their WordNet hyponyms as auxiliary semantic sources. The frozen pretrained classification head of the backbone serves as a semantic classifier to align introduced semantics into visual space. At inference, the model outputs a single class label from the union of seen and unseen classes via cosine similarity between the visual feature and S2V-generated class prototypes, without requiring any test unseen class information.

## Method

DSECN consists of three jointly trained components: (1) a Visual Flow with a learnable S2V MLP that maps semantic features to visual prototypes and a scaled cosine-similarity classifier trained on seen classes; (2) a Diverse Semantic Enhancement (DSE) module that extracts semantics from external base class names (e.g., ImageNet-1K), passes them through the same S2V to generate visual prototypes, and aligns these prototypes to visual space using the frozen pretrained classification head as a semantic classifier with cross-entropy loss; and (3) a Hierarchy Taxonomy Enhancement (HTE) module that augments class names via WordNet hyponyms, assigns parent-class labels to subclasses, and trains them through the same S2V→SC pipeline. Only the S2V parameters are updated during training (SC remains frozen), and the total loss is a weighted sum of the three component losses. At inference, all class semantics (seen and unseen) are mapped through S2V, and the predicted class is the one with maximum cosine similarity to the input visual feature, enabling robust recognition of both similar and dissimilar unseen classes.

## Results

On the practical setting (both similar and dissimilar unseen classes), DSECN achieves harmonic-mean accuracies of 23.63/45.29 (S/U) on CUB, 23.79/53.74 on AWA2, and 21.69/38.54 on SUN using W2V embeddings, and 40.85/45.29, 40.00/53.74, and 49.10/38.54 using CLIP embeddings. The most dramatic gains appear under the dissimilar setting: on CUB with CLIP, DSECN reaches 47.44% unseen accuracy versus 12.30% for the best baseline (DGZ), a 35.14-point improvement. Training is extremely efficient at ~24 seconds mean across all three datasets, compared to 7–12 hours for most competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSECN outperforms all six baselines (ZLA, DGZ, CN, TransZero, MSDN, HASZSL) on every dataset, semantic embedding, and evaluation setting. The margin is most striking under the dissimilar setting: on CUB with CLIP, DSECN's unseen accuracy of 47.44% exceeds the best baseline DGZ (12.30%) by 35.14 points, and with W2V it beats ZLA (6.00%) by 21.76 points. Under the similar setting, DSECN still leads but by a smaller margin (e.g., 48.45% vs. 47.99% for TransZero on CUB-CLIP), confirming that the method does not sacrifice in-distribution performance. Among the fast methods, CN (15.5 s mean training) is the only other sub-minute approach, but its practical-setting accuracy on CUB-CLIP (17.81/23.25) is far below DSECN's (40.85/45.29). The generative-based baselines (ZLA, DGZ) and local-embedding methods (TransZero, MSDN) all collapse to single-digit or low-double-digit accuracies on dissimilar unseen classes, whereas DSECN maintains 27–52% unseen accuracy, demonstrating that diverse external semantics are critical for cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard GZSL framework (embedding-based and generative-based) by introducing a new training signal: diverse semantics from external class names (e.g., ImageNet-1K/21K) aligned to visual space via the frozen pre-trained classification head. While prior GZSL methods (CN, TransZero, DGZ, ZLA, MSDN) rely on seen-class semantics to bridge visual and semantic spaces, DSECN repurposes the classification head — typically discarded after feature extraction — as a semantic-to-visual alignment constraint, and leverages WordNet hyponym/hypernym hierarchies to augment class name diversity. The method is positioned as a modular add-on that can be integrated into existing GZSL approaches rather than a standalone replacement, and it deliberately avoids using any test-unseen-class information, distinguishing it from generative and transductive GZSL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a critical blind spot in GZSL evaluation: existing methods are assessed only on unseen classes from the same dataset (similar to seen classes), masking their inability to handle genuinely dissimilar unseen classes that arise in real deployment. The cross-dataset evaluation protocol proposed here is a useful contribution to the field's evaluation methodology. Limitations include reliance on the quality and coverage of the pre-trained classification head and WordNet hierarchy, and the 'dissimilar' setting (complementary datasets) may not fully capture the diversity of truly novel domains. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-ImageNet pre-training, whether the S2V MLP can handle semantically distant external classes without degradation, and how the method performs when the pre-trained backbone was trained on a domain far from the target.
