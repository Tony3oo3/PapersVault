## Problem

Prior action anticipation methods focus on predicting future actions but do not explicitly model the plausibility of an action sequence in the real world. Specifically, they fail to capture implicit temporal dependencies (e.g., crack eggs must precede cook omelette) and tend to generate repetitive, low-diversity action sequences over longer horizons. This paper introduces plausibility as a first-class objective for action anticipation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip of 32 frames at 224×224 resolution (8 clips of 4 frames for Ego4D, or 32 consecutive frames for EPIC-Kitchens-100) and generates a sequence of anticipated verb-noun action pairs. For Ego4D, the output is N sets of Z-length action sequences; for EPIC-Kitchens-100, the model predicts the next action given an observation window [τ_s−(τ_o+τ_a), τ_s−τ_a] and an anticipation time τ_a. The visual backbone is a frozen ViT-G/14 encoder with a Q-former (32 query tokens), and the language backbone is LLaMA-2-7B.

## Method

PlausiVL uses a BLIP-2-style Q-former architecture with frame-position and clip-position embeddings to build spatio-temporal video representations, which are projected into the LLM embedding space as visual prompts concatenated with text prompts. The key novelty lies in two training objectives: (1) a counterfactual-based plausible action sequence learning loss (L_plau) that constructs implausible sequences by swapping actions violating temporal logic constraints (action X must always precede Y) or verb-noun pair constraints (e.g., 'cook spoon'), then trains the model via a contrastive sigmoid loss to align video representations with plausible sequences and push them away from counterfactuals; and (2) a long-horizon action repetition loss (L_rep) that applies a linearly increasing penalty γ_t to the negative log-likelihood of later tokens, discouraging repetitive actions over longer temporal windows. The overall training loss is L = α·L_plau + β·L_rep with α=β=0.5, trained end-to-end for 100 epochs at learning rate 1e-5.

## Results

On Ego4D (long-term action anticipation, ED@20, lower is better), PlausiVL achieves 0.679 for verb and 0.681 for noun, outperforming the strongest baseline RepLAI (0.755/0.834) and the closest video-language model Video-LLaMA (0.703/0.721). On EPIC-Kitchens-100 (class-mean Top-5 recall, higher is better), PlausiVL reaches 55.62% verb, 54.23% noun, and 27.60% action, surpassing Video-LLaMA (52.90/52.01/26.05) and InAViT (52.54/51.93/25.89). The model also achieves a BLEU score of 45.54 and a repetition score of 5.87 on Ego4D, compared to 37.89 and 7.12 for Video-LLaMA, indicating more plausible and diverse generated sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PlausiVL outperforms all 15 baselines on both datasets. On Ego4D, it improves over the strongest prior method RepLAI [41] by 0.076 (verb) and 0.153 (noun) in ED@20, and over the closest video-language model Video-LLaMA [68] by 0.024 (verb) and 0.040 (noun). On EPIC-Kitchens-100, PlausiVL beats Video-LLaMA by +2.72% verb, +2.22% noun, and +1.55% action Top-5 recall, and surpasses the transformer-based InAViT [52] by +3.08% verb and +2.30% noun. The text-only LLM AntGPT [69] lags behind PlausiVL by 0.021 (verb) and 0.036 (noun) on Ego4D, confirming the importance of visual-temporal information. In terms of generation quality, PlausiVL achieves a 7.65-point higher BLEU score and a 1.25-point lower repetition score than Video-LLaMA, demonstrating both greater plausibility and diversity in the anticipated action sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

PlausiVL builds on the BLIP-2 Q-former architecture for visual-text alignment and the LLaMA-2-7B language model backbone, extending them to the video domain with frame and clip position embeddings. It departs from the dominant LSTM/RNN and transformer-based action anticipation literature by leveraging the generative capabilities of a large video-language model, and further distinguishes itself from concurrent text-only LLM approaches (e.g., AntGPT) by retaining visual-temporal information. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of plausibility as an explicit training signal via counterfactual sequences derived from temporal logic and verb-noun pair constraints, drawing inspiration from differentiable temporal logic (DTL) for temporal action segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing plausibility through counterfactual temporal-logic constraints and a horizon-weighted repetition penalty, the paper offers a principled way to inject world-knowledge priors into generative video-language models for sequential prediction. This framing could transfer to other tasks where temporal ordering and action diversity matter (e.g., procedural planning, robotics). However, the temporal logic constraints are mined from the training data itself, limiting true generalization to unseen action combinations, and the paper acknowledges hallucination of implausible sequences as an open problem. Evaluation is confined to two egocentric datasets with modest absolute gains (~2–4%), leaving open whether the approach scales to more diverse or longer-horizon scenarios.
