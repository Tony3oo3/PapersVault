## Problem

Existing infrared-visible fusion methods suffer from three interrelated limitations: multi-modal weighted game losses force the network to compromise texture and contrast, the standard YCbCr color separation strategy causes color distortion because fixed chrominance cannot adapt to the fused luminance, and the information mismatch between low-light visible and infrared modalities leads to loss of beneficial visible features during fusion. To the authors' knowledge, no prior fusion method has controllably addressed the degradation of the visible modality at night before performing fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242185/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a low-light visible image and a user-specified enhancement ratio r (e.g., 1.0–2.0 for nighttime, 0.4–1.0 for overexposed daytime) as primary inputs; the infrared image is required only during GRM training and is not needed at test time for the enhancement-only mode. It produces a dual output: a brightness-enhanced visible image (from GRM) and a fused infrared-visible image (from IFM), allowing users to select whichever visualization suits their needs. The model is lightweight (0.406 M parameters total) and runs in ~0.124 s on images of roughly 820×1024 on a single RTX 2080Ti.

## Method

DDBF comprises two sequentially trained modules. The Guided Restoration Module (GRM) is a conditional GAN whose generator takes the low-light visible image and enhancement ratio r as inputs; the discriminator is trained against a multi-level conditional adversarial sample set constructed by applying gamma transformations to infrared images, exploiting their light-invariant high-contrast property as the target illumination distribution. Color removal (YUV conversion) and Gaussian low-pass filtering are applied before the discriminator so that adversarial learning focuses on illumination rather than modality differences, while a Retinex-based scene fidelity loss preserves reflectance. The Information Fusion Module (IFM) is a lightweight CNN aggregator that replaces the conventional multi-modal weighted game with explicit information preservation losses—using the element-wise max of source intensities and gradients as the optimization target for contrast and texture—and introduces a hue fidelity loss based on per-pixel cosine similarity between fused and enhanced-visible color vectors to replace the YCbCr color separation strategy, thereby adaptively preserving intrinsic color properties.

## Results

On the LLVIP fusion benchmark (r=1.5), DDBF achieves MI=2.904, VIF=0.499, AG=9.150, and SD=0.204, outperforming all five fusion baselines on every metric. On MFNet (r=1.8), it reaches MI=3.405, VIF=0.480, AG=4.649, and SD=0.193, again leading all competitors. For low-light enhancement on AGLIE (r=1.8), DDBF attains SSIM=0.706 and PSNR=16.434, surpassing the best baseline SCI (0.623 / 14.845). In downstream tasks, fused images from DDBF yield mAP@0.5=0.934 for detection and mIOU=41.628 for segmentation, both exceeding the VIS+IR upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDBF outperforms all five fusion baselines (DenseFuse, IFCNN, RFN-Nest, U2Fusion, SDNet) on every metric across both LLVIP and MFNet, with the largest margins on AG (e.g., 9.150 vs. 5.901 for IFCNN on LLVIP) and VIF (0.499 vs. 0.421). Among the low-light enhancement baselines, DDBF (r=1.8) beats SCI by 0.083 SSIM and 1.59 dB PSNR on AGLIE, and achieves the best NIQE (3.348) and PIQE (33.533) on ExDark, surpassing RetinexDIP (3.382 / 34.193). The method also exceeds the trivial VIS+IR concatenation upper bound in downstream detection (mAP@0.5: 0.934 vs. 0.927) and segmentation (mIOU: 41.628 vs. 40.956), indicating that its fused images provide richer semantic cues than simply stacking both modalities. Notably, DDBF is the fastest method tested (0.124 s per image) with only 0.406 M parameters, compared to RFN-Nest's 7.524 M parameters and 0.372 s runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the conditional GAN framework (Mirza & Osindero, 2014) to construct a guided restoration module that pre-enhances low-light visible images before fusion, departing from prior deep fusion methods (DenseFuse, U2Fusion, RFN-Nest, SDNet, IFCNN) that directly fuse raw modalities. Conceptually, it rejects the dominant multi-modal weighted game loss formulation (Eq. 1) in favor of explicit information preservation losses with a clear optimization objective, and replaces the standard YCbCr color separation strategy with a cosine-similarity-based hue fidelity constraint. The use of gamma-transformed infrared images as multi-level conditional targets for adversarial illumination learning is a novel repurposing of data augmentation within a CGAN training loop, drawing on Retinex theory for scene fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most deep fusion methods assume both modalities are of comparable quality, yet nighttime visible images suffer severe information loss that degrades fusion results. The controllable enhancement ratio and dual-output design (enhanced visible + fused image) offer users flexibility that prior methods lack, and the lightweight architecture (0.406M params, 0.124s inference) makes real-time deployment feasible. Open questions include whether the single-scalar controllability generalizes to other degradation types (fog, motion blur), whether the approach extends to video sequences with temporal consistency, and whether the 400-pair training set is sufficient for robust generalization beyond street scenarios.
