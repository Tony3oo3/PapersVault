## Problem

Existing visual localization datasets and benchmarks are built entirely around pinhole cameras, with no standard dataset providing 6DoF ground truth poses for 360° reference images or query frames from fisheye and omnidirectional cameras. This leaves the cross-device domain gap—where a 360°-captured reference database must localize queries from pinhole or fisheye devices—completely unexplored, and the potential robustness advantage of wider FoV in ambiguous, repetitive scenes is unquantified.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241843/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes query images from five camera models (360° at 6144×3072, three fisheye in Dual Sphere mode at 1280×1024 with 120°/150°/195° FoV, and pinhole at 1920×1200 with 85° FoV) against a 360° reference database augmented with per-frame depth maps and a dense point-cloud/mesh model. Virtual cameras generate lower-FoV queries by projecting 360° images through preconfigured intrinsics with a random SO(3) rotation, ensuring pixel-accurate ground truth without requiring physical multi-camera rigs. The output is a 6DoF pose (3D translation + 3D rotation) evaluated at three accuracy thresholds (0.25 m/2°, 0.5 m/5°, 5 m/10°).

## Method

The paper introduces a virtual camera framework with two variants—VC1 remaps a lower-FoV query back into the 360° domain before retrieval, while VC2 rectifies each 360° reference into multiple lower-FoV views to match the query's imaging model—thereby eliminating the cross-device domain gap at the image-retrieval and training stages. For feature-matching localization, the HLoc pipeline (global descriptor IR → local feature matching → PnP+RANSAC) is extended to handle fisheye and 360° queries using OpenGV's spherical PnP solver. For absolute pose regression, PoseNet (ResNet34) and MS-Transformer are retrained with virtual-camera-cropped images as data augmentation (APR^vc2), which reduces translation error by up to 79% and rotation error by up to 72% over their un-augmented counterparts. Ground truth poses are produced by a practical 360°-lidar mapping pipeline combining graph-based SLAM, lidar bundle adjustment with octree-based feature extraction, ICP alignment across day/night sequences, and TSDF mesh reconstruction for depth map generation.

## Results

On the 360Loc dataset, 360° queries consistently achieve the best image retrieval performance across all four global descriptors, e.g., R@1=0.92 and P@5=0.91 with CosPlace, compared to R@1=0.15 and P@5=0.15 for pinhole queries. The virtual camera rectification method (VC2) substantially narrows the cross-device gap, improving pinhole R@1 from 0.23 to 0.50 with NetVLAD and from 0.15 to 0.32 with CosPlace. For absolute pose regression, augmenting training with virtual camera images (VC2) reduces median translation error by up to 79% and rotation error by up to 72% for MS-Transformer, with PoseNet showing similar gains. In end-to-end local matching localization, 360° queries achieve 17.1%/30.8%/66.1% (high/med/low accuracy, day) with NetVLAD+DISK+LG, while pinhole queries with VC2 reach 14.2%/22.2%/35.5% under the same setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four global descriptors (NetVLAD, CosPlace, OpenIBL, AP-GeM), two local matching pipelines (HLoc with DISK+LightGlue, SP+LightGlue, SP+SuperGlue), and two APRs (PoseNet, MS-Transformer) across five query camera types. 360° queries dominate all baselines: with CosPlace, 360° achieves R@1=0.92 vs. 0.15 for pinhole (a 6× gap), and with NetVLAD+SP+SG localization, 360° reaches 15.8% high-accuracy (0.25 m, 2°) vs. 8.4% for pinhole. The VC2 virtual camera rectification closes much of this gap for IR (pinhole R@1 jumps from 0.23→0.50 with NetVLAD) and for APR (MS-T^vc2 reduces translation error by up to 79% over MS-T), but pinhole queries still lag 360° by ~20–30% in IR recall and ~10–15% in high-accuracy localization. Fisheye3 (195° FoV) with VC2 approaches 360° performance in IR (R@1=0.77 vs. 0.79 with NetVLAD) but remains inferior in end-to-end localization, confirming that wider FoV helps but does not fully replicate omnidirectional coverage. Among IR methods, CosPlace consistently outperforms NetVLAD for 360° queries (R@1: 0.92 vs. 0.79) while NetVLAD is more robust for pinhole/fisheye queries, suggesting descriptor choice interacts with camera model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HLoc hierarchical localization pipeline (image retrieval + local feature matching + PnP) and absolute pose regression (PoseNet, MS-Transformer) to the omnidirectional setting, where reference frames are 360° images rather than pinhole images. It departs from prior cross-device localization work (e.g., SensLoc, CityScale) that either cropped 360° captures to pinhole images or only varied pinhole intrinsics, by introducing a virtual camera framework that generates lower-FoV queries (pinhole, fisheye) directly from 360° references, enabling fair cross-device comparison. The ground truth generation pipeline combines lidar bundle adjustment, ICP registration, and TSDF mesh reconstruction—a practical but non-novel combination of existing techniques adapted to the 360° camera-lidar platform.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

360Loc fills a clear gap as the first benchmark with 360° reference images and multi-device queries (pinhole, fisheye, 360°) with ground-truth 6DoF poses, enabling systematic study of how FoV and imaging model affect localization robustness in ambiguous scenes. The finding that 360° and ultra-wide-FoV queries are consistently less susceptible to mislocalization in symmetric/repetitive environments has practical implications for sensor selection in AR and robotics. Limitations include the small scale (4 campus scenes, ~9K images), single-institution capture, and the fact that the virtual camera approach requires access to the full 360° reference, which may not always be available. Open questions include whether the virtual camera augmentation strategy generalizes to larger, more diverse environments and whether the 360° advantage persists in scenes without strong symmetries.
