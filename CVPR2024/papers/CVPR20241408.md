## Problem

Existing diffusion-based music-to-dance generation models produce entire motion sequences in a single unidirectional pass, neglecting the local and bidirectional dependencies between neighboring frames. This leads to incoherent inter-frame transitions and dance motions that fail to harmonize with nearby music-aligned movements, a critical aspect of real choreography where dancers attend to both preceding and upcoming context.

## Input / Output

The model takes Jukebox-extracted music features and one-hot encoded beat information (via Librosa) as conditioning inputs, both segmented into K=6 slices matching the noise slice decomposition. The output is a 5-second (150-frame at 30 FPS) 3D dance sequence in SMPL format, where each frame is a 151-dimensional vector comprising 24 joints × 6-DOF rotation (144 dims) plus a 3D root translation and 4 binary foot-contact labels. The autoregressive structure processes slices sequentially, so the model can also generate arbitrarily long sequences by chaining N/2-frame segments with linearly interpolated overlap.

## Method

BADM decomposes the full noise sequence into K=6 slices and denoises them autoregressively: at each diffusion timestep, each slice z_tk is refined via a cross-attention layer that attends to both the previously generated dance slice x̂_(k−1) and the subsequent noise slice z_t(k+1), enforcing bidirectional coherence. The refined slice is then decoded through a FiLM-conditioned decoder that fuses the corresponding music and beat feature slices with the diffusion timestep token. A local information decoder built from 1D convolutional layers post-processes the concatenated slices to enforce frame-level smoothness and physical plausibility. Training minimizes a signal-prediction diffusion objective augmented with geometric losses on joint positions (via forward kinematics), velocities, and foot-contact consistency, using classifier-free guidance at inference to amplify music and beat conditioning.

## Results

On the AIST++ benchmark, BADM achieves a Beat Alignment score of 0.2366, surpassing EDGE (0.2281), Bailando (0.2332), and FACT (0.2209), and approaching the ground truth (0.2374). For physical plausibility, BADM attains a PFC of 1.424, a 0.23 improvement over EDGE (1.654) and 0.67 over Bailando (1.754). In diversity, BADM scores 8.29 on Dist_k (closer to GT's 8.19) and 6.76 on Dist_g (closer to GT's 7.45), outperforming EDGE by 1.19 and 1.04 respectively. In a user study, BADM wins 64.9% of pairwise comparisons against EDGE and even surpasses ground truth dances 66.6% of the time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BADM outperforms all three baselines on Beat Alignment (BA), achieving 0.2366 versus EDGE's 0.2281, Bailando's 0.2332, and FACT's 0.2209, indicating superior music-motion synchronization. On Physical Foot Contact (PFC), BADM (1.424) substantially improves over EDGE (1.654) by 0.23 and over Bailando (1.754) by 0.67, reflecting better foot-ground interaction plausibility. In diversity (Dist_k), BADM (8.29) is closer to ground truth (8.19) than EDGE (9.48) and Bailando (7.83), though EDGE achieves a higher Dist_k value; on Dist_g, BADM (6.76) is closer to GT (7.45) than EDGE (5.72). BADM uniquely combines fixed-bone preservation and motion editing capabilities, matching EDGE on both while outperforming it on BA and PFC. In the user study, BADM wins 64.9% of comparisons against EDGE and 83.3% against Bailando on in-the-wild music, and notably surpasses ground truth dances 66.6% of the time on AIST++.

## Relation to Prior Work

BADM extends the diffusion-based dance generation framework established by EDGE [49] and HDM [48], adopting their forward/reverse diffusion formulation, geometric losses (joint position, velocity, foot contact), and classifier-free guidance [14]. The key architectural departure is the autoregressive slicing of the noise sequence into K segments, where each slice is denoised via bidirectional cross-attention over both preceding generated motions and subsequent noise distributions, followed by a 1D-convolution local information decoder. This contrasts with prior diffusion-based approaches (EDGE, HDM) that denoise the entire sequence in one pass, and with autoregressive methods that only look backward. The independent beat condition (one-hot encoding via Librosa) also departs from EDGE, which relies solely on Jukebox music features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real coherence gap in diffusion-based motion generation: interframe transitions are under-constrained when the full sequence is denoised globally. The finding that a global information encoder actually degrades performance (constraining spatial freedom of hands and feet) while a local 1D-convolution decoder improves it is a useful architectural insight for motion generation more broadly. However, the fixed-step segmentation (K=6) remains a limitation acknowledged by the authors: it can split semantically meaningful movements (e.g., a pirouette) across slice boundaries, and the paper defers semantic-aware segmentation to future work. The moderate SOTA margins (BA +0.008, PFC −0.23 over EDGE) and the single-dataset evaluation (AIST++, 5-second clips) limit the generality of the claims.
