## Problem

Action recognition from 3D point cloud sequences remains under-explored because point clouds are unstructured, unordered, and have varying point counts, making it difficult to learn spatio-temporal representations without reliable point correspondence across frames. While RGB video action recognition is well-studied, the 3D point cloud modality offers complementary geometric information that is especially valuable for safety-critical applications or degraded video conditions, yet no effective prior-free pipeline existed for per-frame action prediction from raw point cloud sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240103/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a temporal sequence of raw 3D point clouds (e.g., 64 frames of ~6890 points on DFAUST, or ~2735 frames per video on IKEA ASM) with no requirement for fixed point counts, grid structure, or inter-frame correspondence. It produces a per-frame action class prediction (33 classes on IKEA ASM, 14 on DFAUST) via a classifier with temporal smoothing. The approach is prior-free, requiring no skeleton extraction or pose estimation, and operates directly on unstructured point sets.

## Method

The core contribution is the t-patch: a locally evolving point cloud set that tracks a region's motion over time by iteratively finding the nearest neighbor in each subsequent frame (with small Gaussian jitter to prevent temporal collapse, and bidirectional construction from both ends of the sequence). These t-patches are fed into a hierarchical architecture where each level subsamples points via farthest point sampling, extracts t-patches, and processes them through a t-patch Net consisting of shared-weight MLP layers over spatial dimensions followed by a convolutional layer over both temporal and feature dimensions. The hierarchy progressively produces sparser point clouds with higher-dimensional spatio-temporal features, and the final level's t-patch embeddings are max-pooled into a per-frame vector that is classified by fully connected layers. Training uses a combined per-frame and per-sequence cross-entropy loss, and the entire pipeline avoids the need for ground-truth point correspondence, grid structure, or fixed point ordering.

## Results

On DFAUST, 3DinAction achieves 87.26% top-1 frame accuracy and 0.8616 mAP, outperforming the best static baseline (PointNet+TS at 74.10% top-1) by 13.2 points and the best temporal baseline (PSTNet at 50.70% top-1) by 36.6 points. On IKEA ASM, the method reaches 52.91% top-1 and 0.2875 mAP, surpassing the strongest static method (PointNet++ at 45.97% top-1) by ~7 points and the best pose-based method (ST-GCN at 43.4% top-1) by ~9.5 points. On MSR-Action3D, all methods including 3DinAction (92.23% at 24 frames) are comparable, with PST-Transformer slightly ahead (93.73%), attributed to the dataset's limited scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DinAction decisively outperforms all static point cloud baselines (PointNet, PointNet++, Set Transformer, 3DMFVNet) on both DFAUST and IKEA ASM, with the gap widening when temporal smoothing is added to those baselines (e.g., PointNet+TS reaches 74.10% top-1 on DFAUST vs. 87.26% for 3DinAction). Against the strongest temporal baseline PSTNet, 3DinAction gains 36.6 points in top-1 on DFAUST (87.26 vs. 50.70) and 35.0 points on IKEA ASM (52.91 vs. 17.94), indicating that explicit t-patch temporal modeling far surpasses spatio-temporal convolutions. On IKEA ASM, 3DinAction also beats pose-based methods from the original dataset paper (Human Pose HCN at 39.15% and ST-GCN at 43.4% top-1), demonstrating that a prior-free point cloud approach can match or exceed skeleton-dependent methods. On MSR-Action3D, the method is roughly on par with PSTNet, P4Transformer, PST-Transformer, and Kinet (all within ~1–2 points across clip lengths), with PST-Transformer holding a slight edge at longer sequences (93.73% vs. 92.23% at 24 frames). Notably, using ground-truth point correspondence (Ours+GT corr) yields only a marginal mAP gain (0.8762 vs. 0.8616) while actually reducing top-1 accuracy (77.67 vs. 87.26), suggesting the simplified kNN-based t-patch extraction provides beneficial implicit augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 4D convolution and transformer approaches for temporal point clouds (PSTNet, P4Transformer, PST-Transformer, Kinet) by introducing t-patches—locally evolving point cloud subsets tracked via iterative kNN across frames—as a new spatio-temporal representation. The hierarchical MLP architecture draws conceptual inspiration from PointNet++'s multi-scale subsampling but applies it to t-patch sequences rather than raw point sets. Rather than requiring explicit point correspondence or grid structure, the method repurposes the static patch extraction paradigm and extends it temporally through a simplified nearest-neighbor tracking rule, positioning itself as a prior-free alternative to both 4D CNNs and attention-based temporal models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The t-patch representation offers a practical way to handle the fundamental challenge of temporal point cloud processing—lack of point correspondence—without relying on GT tracking or expensive 4D convolutions, making it applicable to real-world sensor data where correspondences are unavailable. The per-frame prediction formulation is more demanding than the per-clip classification used by most prior work, and the 13% accuracy gain on DFAUST demonstrates the representation's value. However, the method is sensitive to point density variations and breaks under very low frame rates or fast motion, and the t-patch extraction (FPS + sequential kNN) dominates inference time. Open questions include whether t-patch construction can be learned end-to-end, whether stronger temporal priors (sceneflow, tracking) can replace the kNN heuristic, and whether the representation generalizes to multimodal inputs.
