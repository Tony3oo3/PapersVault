## Problem

Capturing full RAW video imposes prohibitive bandwidth and storage costs, while existing metadata-based sRGB-to-RAW de-rendering methods require per-frame sampling on the camera side, incurring O(WHL) computational overhead that is impractical for high-frame-rate video. No prior work addresses RAW video de-rendering as a distinct task, and no dedicated benchmark exists for evaluating such methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242411/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of sRGB video frames (1600×900 or 1440×640) plus a single 14-bit RAW frame from the first frame of the sequence, stored in 4-channel [R, Gr, Gb, B] Bayer format. It produces the full de-rendered RAW video in the same 4-channel format via chained inference: the first frame uses the real RAW image, and each subsequent frame uses the predicted RAW from the previous step as its reference. Optical flow is computed on-the-fly between adjacent sRGB frames to guide warping.

## Method

The Frame Affinity Guided De-rendering Network (FAGDN) operates in two stages. First, Temporal Affinity Prior Extraction warps the previous RAW frame using optical flow between adjacent sRGB frames to produce a reference RAW, then a small convolutional network generates a confidence map from the discrepancy between the warped and current sRGB frames to suppress unreliable regions. Second, Spatial Feature Fusion and Mapping employs a U-Net-like encoder-decoder with two parallel encoder branches (current frame inputs and previous frame inputs) that do not share parameters; a Global and Local Feature Aggregation (GLA) module injects scene-specific and position-specific priors by treating non-overlapping patches as tokens for transformer-style cross-attention (global) and intra-patch attention (local). The network is trained end-to-end with L2 + SSIM loss on the final output plus auxiliary losses on three intermediate decoder levels (λ=0.5), using Adam at lr=1e-3 for 60 epochs on 256×256 crops.

## Results

On the proposed RVD benchmark, the method achieves 48.86 PSNR / 0.9980 SSIM on RVD-Part1 and 49.71 PSNR / 0.9983 SSIM on RVD-Part2, outperforming all metadata-based baselines. The closest competitor, INF (CVPR 2023), scores 47.67 / 0.9948 on RVD-Part1 and 46.25 / 0.9939 on RVD-Part2. Notably, the proposed method requires no per-image metadata—only a single first-frame RAW image—while all baselines depend on per-frame sampled metadata.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two metadata-based sRGB-to-RAW image de-rendering methods: CAM (CVPR 2022) and INF (CVPR 2023). The proposed method decisively outperforms CAM by approximately 7 PSNR on both RVD-Part1 (48.86 vs. 41.85) and RVD-Part2 (49.71 vs. 42.25), reflecting the substantial advantage of temporal frame affinity over content-aware superpixel sampling. Against the stronger INF baseline, the proposed method gains 1.19 PSNR on RVD-Part1 (48.86 vs. 47.67) and 3.46 PSNR on RVD-Part2 (49.71 vs. 46.25), with corresponding SSIM improvements of 0.0032 and 0.0044. A critical distinction is that the proposed method uses no per-image metadata (only one initial RAW frame), whereas both CAM and INF require per-frame sampled metadata, making the proposed approach far more practical for real-time video capture. Under challenging scenarios (Table 2), the proposed method also leads INF by 2.4 PSNR (low resolution), 0.57 PSNR (fast motion), and 2.18 PSNR (dark lighting).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-image sRGB-to-RAW de-rendering line (particularly CAM [19] and INF [14]) to the video domain, replacing the per-frame metadata sampling paradigm with a single first-frame RAW prior propagated via optical-flow warping. The encoder-decoder backbone follows the U-Net structure from [19], but the key architectural departure is the Temporal Affinity Prior Extraction step (warping + confidence map) and the Global and Local Feature Aggregation module, which injects scene-specific and position-specific priors from the previous frame through a patch-level attention mechanism. Conceptually, the paper reframes the problem from 'recover lost ISP information from per-image metadata' to 'recover lost ISP information from temporal redundancy,' reducing camera-side computation from O(WHL) to O(1).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for mobile and embedded camera systems: capturing a single extra RAW frame is trivially cheap, whereas per-frame metadata sampling imposes severe bandwidth and compute constraints at high frame rates. The RVD benchmark is the first dedicated evaluation suite for this task, filling a clear gap. However, the chained inference design introduces accumulated error over time (visible degradation after ~100 frames in Figure 8), and the authors acknowledge this as an open problem requiring a reference-frame selection strategy. Broader questions remain about generalization across camera models, handling of drastic scene changes within a sequence, and whether the approach scales to longer videos or higher resolutions.
