## Problem

Existing generative models for 3D human motion (CVAE, DLow, diffusion-based) suffer from mode collapse, producing samples that concentrate on dominant data modes while failing to cover rare but plausible behaviors. This diversity gap is especially critical in safety-critical settings like autonomous driving, where anticipating subtle multi-modal future motions (e.g., a child abruptly crossing) can determine safety outcomes. Prior diversification strategies—affine latent mappings or learnable anchors—provide limited coverage of rare modes and do not generalize to complex scene-aware or in-the-wild scenarios.

## Input / Output

The model takes 3D pose history over T_h timesteps as its core input, with optional 2D image-space path data, person-centered image context (encoded to 1000-dim features via a Swin Transformer), and social context (3D poses of up to 8 neighboring agents). Each input modality is processed by a dedicated GRU (128 hidden units) before summation. The output is K diverse 3D motion sequences of length T_f (root-normalized joint positions) plus a parallel 2D path prediction branch, with the standard experimental setup predicting 2-second futures from 0.5-second context.

## Method

MDN introduces a two-stage training procedure: first a CVAE (GRU encoder + MLP generator) is trained with standard KL and reconstruction losses, then a transformer-based z-transformer module is trained with the generator frozen. The z-transformer samples K random variables from a unit Gaussian and uses them as queries in an 8-block multi-head cross-attention mechanism, where keys and values are computed from the encoded context, producing a diverse set of latent codes that are decoded independently. A second key component is a set of deterministic motion primitives obtained via k-means clustering of training inputs, encoded by a single fully connected layer and added to the key/value matrices before attention, providing a strong initial bias toward plausible motion modes. The second-stage loss combines a min-over-K reconstruction term with a pairwise exponential diversity-promoting term (α=100, weight 25). The diversification module is designed to flexibly absorb additional context (scene layout, social agents, image features) by summing their encoded representations into the key/value computation, enabling a unified architecture across isolated and scene-aware settings.

## Results

On Human3.6M, MDN achieves the best diversity (APD 17.450) and accuracy (ADE 0.355, FDE 0.442) simultaneously, improving APD by over 9.8% over the prior state-of-the-art STARS (15.884) without sacrificing prediction quality. On the real-world 3DPW and HPS benchmarks, MDN outperforms DLow by 54.6% and 43.7% in APD respectively, while also reducing ADE and FDE. On the novel DenseCity urban simulation benchmark, MDN achieves APD 16.799 (3D pose) and 1.065 (2D path), substantially exceeding DLow (11.980) and the scene-aware method Cao et al. (5.810).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDN outperforms all 12 baselines on Human3.6M across all three metrics, with the closest competitor being STARS [72] (APD 15.884, ADE 0.358, FDE 0.445); MDN gains 1.566 APD while also improving ADE by 0.003 and FDE by 0.003, demonstrating no diversity-accuracy trade-off. On 3DPW, MDN (APD 6.355, ADE 0.982, FDE 1.032) substantially surpasses DLow (APD 4.111, ADE 1.010, FDE 1.069) and CVAE (APD 3.068, ADE 1.032, FDE 1.096); a similar pattern holds on HPS where MDN (APD 6.943, ADE 0.916, FDE 0.971) beats DLow (APD 4.835) by 43.7% in diversity. On DenseCity, MDN (APD 16.799, ADE 0.584, FDE 0.879) outperforms DLow (APD 11.980, ADE 0.596, FDE 0.899), PoseGPT (APD 9.099, ADE 0.913), HuMoR (APD 11.134, ADE 0.705), and the scene-aware Cao et al. (APD 5.810, ADE 0.858, FDE 1.285) on 3D pose, and also beats Cao et al. on 2D path (APD 1.065 vs. 0.978, ADE 0.666 vs. 0.701, FDE 0.621 vs. 0.675). Adding YouTube in-the-wild data further boosts 3DPW APD from 6.355 to 8.266 and HPS ADE from 0.916 to 0.886, though on DenseCity it slightly increases FDE (0.879→0.921) due to distribution shift from CARLA's fixed pedestrian controller. A perceptual user study confirms MDN-generated motion is preferred over CARLA's built-in kinematics 72.5% of the time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CVAE framework (Kingma & Welling, 2013) for stochastic motion modeling and the DLow diversification strategy (Yuan & Kitani, 2020), which introduced affine latent mappings to promote sample diversity. The key departure is replacing DLow's single-random-variable affine transform with a multi-query transformer (z-transformer) that attends over K independently sampled random variables, and augmenting the key/value computation with k-means clustering-based motion primitives. The paper also positions itself against diffusion-based motion models (which improve realism at the cost of diversity) and prior scene-aware methods (Cao et al., 2020; Rempe et al., 2023) that sacrifice diversity for accuracy, arguing that its diversification module can be plugged into any CVAE-based pipeline to handle arbitrary context inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important gap: safety-critical applications (autonomous driving, robotics) require not just one plausible future but a diverse set of plausible futures, and prior methods exhibit a diversity-realism tradeoff that MDN claims to avoid. The introduction of the DenseCity benchmark and the use of in-the-wild YouTube motion data are steps toward closing the sim-to-real gap, though both are limited (DenseCity has only 82 sequences; YouTube data is noisy and ethically fraught). Open questions include whether the z-transformer + primitives approach scales to truly large, clean outdoor 3D datasets, whether the k-means primitives generalize beyond the specific joint representations tested, and how to handle the fundamental scarcity of naturalistic 3D motion data in complex social settings.
