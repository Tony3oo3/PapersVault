## Problem

Existing depth completion methods either rely on iterative 2D spatial propagation that fails to exploit the 3D geometric structure of the scene, or attempt 3D feature learning on extremely sparse input points, which is ineffective for learning informative representations. This paper addresses the gap of performing effective full-3D feature learning for depth completion without requiring iterative propagation steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240536/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an aligned sparse depth map S ∈ R^{H×W} and an RGB image I ∈ R^{H×W×3} as input, producing a dense depth map D ∈ R^{H×W}. RGB and sparse depth are encoded separately via convolutions and concatenated channel-wise for early fusion. The 2D guidance features are un-projected into 3D using a pinhole camera model with known intrinsics, and the resulting point cloud is normalized into a unit ball before 3D attention processing.

## Method

DeCoTR operates in two stages. First, S2D-TR enhances the S2D baseline by applying Multi-Headed Self-Attention to bottleneck and skip-connection features (downsampled via depth-separable convolutions to control quadratic cost), yielding significantly more accurate initial depths without iterative propagation. Second, the 2D guidance features from S2D-TR are uplifted to a dense 3D point cloud using the initial completed depth, normalized to a unit ball, and processed by multiple 3D-TR layers that perform KNN-based cross-attention with vector attention (modulating individual feature channels via learned attention weights). To capture long-range context without intractable global attention over all points, scalar global attention is applied only on a downsampled point set at the final encoding stage. The attended 3D features are projected back to the 2D image plane and decoded into the final depth prediction, all trained end-to-end with a masked L1 loss.

## Results

On NYU Depth V2, DeCoTR achieves a new SOTA RMSE of 0.086 m (with global attention) and Abs Rel of 0.012, outperforming the previous best methods (PointDC at 0.089, GraphCSPN and CompletionFormer at 0.090). On KITTI-DC, DeCoTR achieves RMSE 717.07 mm and MAE 195.30 mm, ranking among the top methods on the official leaderboard. In zero-shot generalization tests, DeCoTR trained on NYU Depth V2 achieves RMSE 0.188 on ScanNet-v2 (vs. 0.194 for CompletionFormer), and DeCoTR trained on KITTI achieves RMSE 399.2 mm on DDAD, dramatically outperforming NLSPN (701.9) and CompletionFormer (889.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NYU Depth V2, DeCoTR (RMSE 0.086) outperforms all 19 compared baselines, with the closest competitors being PointDC (0.089), GraphCSPN (0.090), CompletionFormer (0.090), and NLSPN (0.092). On KITTI-DC, DeCoTR (RMSE 717.07 mm) beats CompletionFormer (708.87), DySPN (709.12), RigNet (712.66), and GuideFormer (721.48), and substantially outperforms 3D-representation methods like GraphCSPN (738.41) and PointDC (736.07). In zero-shot ScanNet-v2 testing, DeCoTR (RMSE 0.188) edges out CompletionFormer (0.194), GraphCSPN (0.197), and NLSPN (0.198). The most striking result is on DDAD zero-shot: DeCoTR (RMSE 399.2 mm) nearly halves the error of NLSPN (701.9) and more than doubles the accuracy of CompletionFormer (889.3), demonstrating far superior generalizability. Notably, CompletionFormer, which slightly outperforms DeCoTR on KITTI-DC in-domain, degrades severely on DDAD, while DeCoTR maintains robust performance across all four benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeCoTR builds directly on the S2D convolutional baseline (Ma & Karaman, ICRA 2018) by upgrading its bottleneck and skip-connection features with multi-head self-attention (S2D-TR), and on the Point Transformer family (Zhao et al., ICCV 2021; Wu et al., NeurIPS 2022) for neighborhood-based 3D cross-attention. It departs from the dominant spatial-propagation paradigm (CSPN, NLSPN, DySPN) that performs iterative 2D feature mixing, instead learning geometry-aware features in full 3D via a dense point cloud constructed from the initial completed depth. It also distinguishes itself from prior 3D-representation depth completion methods (GraphCSPN, PointDC, PointFusion) by operating on a dense, normalized point cloud rather than the extremely sparse input depth, and by adding efficient global attention on downsampled points for long-range context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeCoTR demonstrates that a single-pass 3D transformer pipeline can match or exceed iterative spatial-propagation methods while offering better zero-shot generalizability (notably on DDAD, where it halves the RMSE of CompletionFormer), suggesting that explicit 3D geometric reasoning is more transferable than 2D propagation heuristics. The point-cloud normalization trick and the finding that accurate initial depths are a prerequisite for effective 3D feature learning (S2D+3D-TR without the 2D attention upgrade underperforms) provide practical guidance for future 3D depth completion architectures. Open questions include the computational cost of KNN-based 3D attention at higher resolutions, whether the approach extends to video depth completion with temporal consistency, and real-time deployment on embedded platforms.
