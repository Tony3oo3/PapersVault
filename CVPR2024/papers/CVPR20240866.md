## Problem

Wide-baseline stereo setups (meters apart) mounted on large vehicles like semi-trucks suffer significant calibration drift from vibration, structural stress, and temperature gradients during operation, rendering offline calibrations inaccurate. Conventional rectification methods are either too computationally expensive for real-time deployment or sacrifice accuracy when run online, severely degrading downstream tasks like stereo depth estimation and 3D detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240866/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a stereo RGB image pair at 1024×512 resolution, camera intrinsics, and a prior rotation estimate (identity or from a previous inference step) as input. It outputs a 6D relative rotation correction (x and y columns of the rotation matrix, Gram-Schmidt orthogonalized) that is added to the initial rotation to produce the final rectification, along with the rectified stereo image pair via differentiable grid-sampling. Translation is held fixed from the known baseline geometry, so the model only learns to correct rotation.

## Method

The architecture combines a shared 3-block residual CNN for shift-equivariant features, 2D sinusoidal positional encoding, and a transformer encoder (6 self-attention + 6 cross-attention blocks) that captures global cross-view context. A GMFlow-style correlation volume over the enhanced features establishes dense matches, which a 3D-convolution decoder reduces to a 6D rotation correction bounded by Tanh. The key training insight is a self-supervised vertical optical flow loss (using RAFT on the differentiably rectified pair) that directly penalizes residual vertical disparity without requiring high-quality ground-truth extrinsics, complemented by a supervised L1 rotation loss for early-training stability. This flow-guided constraint yields a 40% improvement in keypoint-offset metrics over the rotation-only variant and outperforms all tested baselines (SIFT, SuperGlue, LOFTR, RPNet, DirectionNet, ViTPose) while running at 86 ms on an A40 GPU.

## Results

On the Semi-Truck Highway dataset (2m baseline), the proposed method with vertical flow loss (Ours w/OF) achieves a keypoint offset of 2.67 px (SIFT) and 0.59 px (SuperGlue), outperforming Ground Truth offline calibration (2.91 px / 0.77 px) and the best learned baseline ViTPose (4.53 px / 2.37 px). On KITTI, the method reaches 1.44 px SIFT offset and 0.36 px SuperGlue offset, again beating Ground Truth (1.65 px / 0.43 px). On the Carla synthetic dataset with extreme ±1° perturbations, Ours (w/OF) achieves 3.55 px SIFT offset and 0.64 px SuperGlue offset versus ViTPose's 4.34 px / 1.45 px. Downstream, the method improves stereo depth MAE by 63% (Semi-Truck) and 51% (KITTI) over unrectified inputs using HITNet and DLNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baseline pipelines (SIFT+LO-RANSAC, SuperGlue+MAGSAC, LOFTR+MAGSAC, RPNet, DirectionNet, ViTPose) across every metric on all three datasets. The gap is most dramatic on the Semi-Truck Highway dataset: SIFT+LO-RANSAC produces a 21.10 px SIFT offset versus 2.67 px for the proposed method (≈8× worse), while the closest learned baseline ViTPose still lags by 41% in SIFT offset (4.53 vs. 2.67 px) and 4× in SuperGlue offset (2.37 vs. 0.59 px). On KITTI, where pose variation is minimal, the method still beats ViTPose by 26% in SIFT offset (1.44 vs. 1.95 px) and 49% in SuperGlue offset (0.36 vs. 0.71 px), and even surpasses Ground Truth offline calibration. On Carla with extreme ±1° perturbations, DirectionNet degrades severely (13.47 px SIFT offset, nearly as bad as unrectified), while the proposed method with flow loss maintains 3.55 px, close to Ground Truth (3.22 px). The ablation (Ours w/o OF vs. w/ OF) confirms the self-supervised vertical flow loss contributes a consistent 10–40% improvement in keypoint offset across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the global correlation volume formulation from GMFlow (Xu et al., CVPR 2022) — originally designed for optical flow — as the matching backbone for relative rotation estimation, and adopts the 6D rotation representation with Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization from DirectionNet (Chen et al., CVPR 2021). It departs from both traditional keypoint-based rectification pipelines (SIFT/SuperGlue + RANSAC/MAGSAC) and existing learned pose estimators (RPNet, DirectionNet, ViTPose) by directly optimizing a rectification-specific constraint: a self-supervised vertical optical flow loss computed via RAFT on the model's own rectified output. The transformer encoder with full cross-attention (rather than windowed attention) is used to produce globally discriminative features that feed the correlation volume, distinguishing it from both CNN-only pose estimators and the windowed-attention designs of Swin-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: wide-baseline stereo on large vehicles (semi-trucks, construction robots) suffers calibration drift from vibration and thermal stress, and no existing method provides real-time re-rectification that matches or exceeds offline calibration quality. The release of two purpose-built datasets (Semi-Truck Highway with 50K pairs, Carla with extreme pose perturbations) fills a gap in the literature for evaluating rectification under realistic degradation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to simultaneous multi-camera rectification, whether the RAFT-based flow loss can be replaced by a lighter differentiable module to reduce the two-GPU inference dependency, and whether the semi-supervised rotation loss from offline calibration can be eliminated entirely for fully self-supervised operation.
