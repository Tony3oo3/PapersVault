## Problem

Generating multi-view optical illusions—images that change appearance under transformations like flips, rotations, or pixel permutations—has traditionally relied on explicit models of human perception or slow optimization-based approaches like score distillation sampling. This paper asks whether off-the-shelf text-to-image diffusion models can produce such illusions in a simple, zero-shot manner, and precisely characterizes which transformations are compatible with the diffusion denoising process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240347/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N text prompts (typically 2–4), each paired with a view transformation (e.g., vertical flip, 90° rotation, jigsaw rearrangement, color inversion, or an arbitrary orthogonal matrix), and a pretrained pixel-based diffusion model (DeepFloyd IF). It outputs a single RGB image such that applying each transformation v_i makes the result visually match prompt y_i. The transformations must be orthogonal to preserve the i.i.d. Gaussian noise statistics assumed by the diffusion model; non-orthogonal operations like scaling or bilinear-interpolated rotations cause artifacts or divergence.

## Method

During the reverse diffusion process, the method simultaneously denoises N views of the same noisy image: for each view v_i, it transforms the noisy image, queries the diffusion model for a noise estimate conditioned on prompt y_i (with classifier-free guidance), applies the inverse view v_i^{-1} to align the estimate back to the original orientation, and averages all N aligned estimates into a single combined noise estimate for the next denoising step. The key theoretical contribution is proving that views must be orthogonal transformations (permutations, rotations, flips, color negation, or arbitrary orthogonal matrices) so that the transformed noise remains i.i.d. Gaussian; this yields the notion of a 'visual anagram'—an image that changes under an arbitrary pixel permutation. The authors use a pixel-space diffusion model rather than a latent one to avoid orientation artifacts in latent code blocks, and find that mean reduction of noise estimates outperforms the alternating strategy used in prior work.

## Results

On the CIFAR-10 prompt-pair dataset, the proposed method achieves an alignment score (A) of 0.287 and a concealment score (C) of 0.624, outperforming Tancik (A=0.278, C=0.595) and Burgert et al. (A=0.225, C=0.501). On the hand-curated 50-prompt dataset, the method scores A=0.275 and C=0.574, again leading both baselines. At the 95th percentile (best-case), the method reaches A=0.327 and C=0.739 on CIFAR-10, confirming consistent gains across the distribution rather than only in tail cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines that also use off-the-shelf diffusion models for multi-view illusion generation. Burgert et al. [2] employs Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) to optimize an image against multiple view-prompt pairs; it is the weakest baseline, scoring A=0.225 and C=0.501 on CIFAR-10, roughly 22% lower in alignment and 20% lower in concealment than the proposed method. Tancik [42] is the closest prior work, using a latent diffusion model with alternating noise estimates between views; it achieves A=0.278 and C=0.595 on CIFAR-10, still trailing the proposed method by about 3% in alignment and 5% in concealment. The proposed method wins on every metric (A, A₀.₉, A₀.₉₅, C, C₀.₉, C₀.₉₅) across both datasets, with the largest gap over Burgert et al. and a more modest but consistent margin over Tancik. The authors attribute the advantage over Tancik to using a pixel-space diffusion model (DeepFloyd IF) instead of a latent model, averaging rather than alternating noise estimates, and supporting an arbitrary number of views.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Tancik's proof-of-concept 'Illusion Diffusion' [42], which alternated noise estimates between rotation views in a latent diffusion model, into a systematic framework with theoretical grounding, support for arbitrary numbers of views, and a pixel-based diffusion model to avoid latent-space artifacts. The core noise-combining technique draws on the compositional generation literature (Du et al. [9], Liu et al. [27]), where noise estimates from multiple conditional distributions are merged to sample from composed distributions. The paper departs from the broader computational optical illusion tradition (Freeman et al., Oliva et al., Chandra et al.) by requiring no explicit model of human perception, instead leveraging visual priors implicitly learned by diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical characterization—that the method works precisely for orthogonal transformations—gives practitioners a clear design space for new illusion types (e.g., polymorphic jigsaws, inner rotations) without trial-and-error. The introduction of 'visual anagrams' as a named class of pixel-permutation illusions opens a creative and algorithmically tractable direction for generative art and perception research. Key limitations remain: non-volume-preserving deformations (homographies, stretches, color constancy) are unsupported, and the method does not consistently produce perfect illusions, suggesting room for improved consistency and broader transformation coverage.
