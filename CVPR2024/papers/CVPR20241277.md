## Problem

Existing implicit neural representations (INRs) suffer from a limited supported frequency set because standard periodic activations like sin(x) only exploit the central region of their infinite definition domain, making it impossible to access higher-frequency sub-functions without breaking convergence. This creates a capacity-convergence gap: increasing the initialization range to reach higher frequencies violates the convergence guarantees that require inputs to stay within [-π, π]. As a result, INRs cannot flexibly adapt their representable frequency range to match the unknown spectral content of the target signal.

## Input / Output

The method takes coordinate inputs of varying dimensionality—2D pixel locations for image fitting, 3D points for SDF representation, and 5D (position, direction) pairs for NeRF—and produces the corresponding signal attributes (3-channel RGB, scalar SDF, or 3-channel color + scalar opacity). All experiments use a fixed 3-layer MLP with 256 neurons per layer trained with Adam and L2 loss; the only tunable hyperparameter beyond standard SIREN settings is the bias initialization range k, which controls the supported frequency set without requiring manual frequency selection or positional encoding.

## Method

FINER replaces SIREN's sin(x) activation with the variable-periodic function sin((|x|+1)x), whose local frequency increases monotonically with |x|, so shifting the active region along the domain (via the bias vector) genuinely changes the activated frequency rather than cycling through identical periods. The bias is initialized as b ~ U(-k, k) with a user-chosen range k, selecting which high-frequency sub-functions along the activation's domain are engaged by the network. From a geometric perspective, larger k expands the supported frequency set as a strict superset of SIREN's; from an NTK perspective, larger k strengthens the kernel's diagonal property (improving shift-invariance) and increases the number of large eigenvalues (accelerating high-frequency component learning). This closes the capacity-convergence gap because the variable-periodic structure allows higher frequencies to be accessed without requiring the pre-activation values to remain in a narrow band, unlike the fixed-period sin(x) where scaling W to reach higher harmonics destabilizes training.

## Results

On 2D image fitting (512×512 natural images), FINER achieves 40.76 PSNR, 0.9790 SSIM, and 2.56e-3 LPIPS, outperforming all baselines. On 3D SDF representation (5 shapes from Stanford 3D Scanning Repository), FINER attains the best average Chamfer distance (3.087e-6) and IOU (0.9790). On NeRF novel view synthesis (8 Blender scenes, 25 training images), FINER achieves the highest PSNR on 7 out of 8 scenes (e.g., 33.90 on Chair, 28.70 on Ficus, 33.96 on Mic) and the best LPIPS on all 8 scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FINER outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three tasks. Against SIREN (the closest competitor and direct architectural predecessor), FINER gains +2.24 dB PSNR on image fitting (40.76 vs. 38.52), reduces average Chamfer distance by ~10% on SDF (3.087e-6 vs. 3.438e-6), and improves PSNR by 0.3–1.4 dB across NeRF scenes. Against WIRE, FINER leads by +9.45 dB PSNR on image fitting and by +0.44 dB average PSNR on NeRF, while WIRE produces visible artifacts in smooth backgrounds. Against Gauss, FINER achieves +5.37 dB PSNR on image fitting and a 4× lower average Chamfer distance on SDF (3.087e-6 vs. 1.262e-5), with Gauss showing unstable performance across shapes. Against PEMLP (Fourier positional encoding), FINER gains +11.16 dB PSNR on image fitting and +0.62 dB average PSNR on NeRF, confirming that the variable-periodic activation provides a more adaptive frequency set than pre-defined Fourier bases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SIREN line of work (Sitzmann et al., 2020) that introduced periodic activation functions for implicit neural representations, and is positioned alongside concurrent efforts using alternative nonlinearities such as Gaussian (Ramasinghe & Lucey, 2022) and wavelet (Saragadam et al., 2023) activations. The key conceptual departure is from manipulating the weight matrix or pre-defining positional encoding bases to instead modulating the bias vector, which shifts the operating region along the definition domain of a variable-periodic function sin((|x|+1)x). The NTK-based convergence analysis connects to the broader kernel-regression view of neural network training (Jacot et al., 2018; Tancik et al., 2020), providing a theoretical justification for why larger bias initialization improves shift-invariance and high-frequency learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete mechanism—the underutilization of the activation function's definition domain—as the root cause of the capacity-convergence gap in SIREN, and offers a simple, interpretable fix (bias range tuning) that practitioners can apply without architectural changes. However, the frequency tuning remains manual (the user must choose k), and the paper does not address how to automatically select the appropriate range for signals with unknown frequency content, which is precisely the setting where positional encoding also struggles. Open questions include whether the specific variable-periodic form sin((|x|+1)x) is optimal, whether the approach extends to higher-dimensional or non-stationary signals, and whether the NTK diagonal-enhancement effect persists in deeper, multi-layer networks beyond the 1-hidden-layer analysis provided.
