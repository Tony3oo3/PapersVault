## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction datasets are limited to unimanual actions or bimanual manipulation of a single object, and the most relevant bimanual multi-object dataset (KIT) covers only 12 objects and fewer than 20 tool-action-object triplets. This scarcity prevents studying generalizable bimanual HOI understanding that transfers to unseen object geometries and novel interaction triplets, a capability humans possess intuitively.

## Input / Output

The acquisition pipeline consumes 12 synchronized FLIR RGB views (4096×3000), one egocentric Realsense L515 RGBD stream (1920×1080), 3D marker positions from a NOKOV mocap system, and pre-scanned object meshes (up to 100K triangles), all at 30 Hz. It outputs per-frame MANO hand meshes (θ∈R⁴⁸, β∈R¹⁰, t∈R³), rigid 6D object poses, 2D hand-object segmentation masks, and marker-inpainted RGB images. For the three benchmarks, inputs are further structured as: first-person video frames with optional bounding boxes (action recognition), N=10 consecutive frames of object point clouds plus hand/tool/target poses (motion forecasting), and static meshes of two objects plus the left hand (grasp synthesis).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a fully automatic data acquisition pipeline that fuses marker-based mocap (for accurate object 6D pose via marker-to-surface correspondence optimization inspired by Mosh++) with markerless multi-view vision (for hand pose). Hand keypoints are obtained by separately detecting each hand with YOLOv3, extracting 2D keypoints via MMPose, and fusing across views with RANSAC-filtered triangulation to handle severe occlusion. MANO meshes are then refined through a contact-aware optimization balancing 2D/3D keypoint alignment, pose priors, temporal smoothness, an attraction loss encouraging hand-object contact, and a penetration loss preventing interpenetration. Segmentation is refined with SAM using sampled keypoints as prompts, and markers are removed by rendering enlarged sphere masks and inpainting with LAMA. The resulting 2.5K sequences are organized into 131 tool-action-object triplets across 20 categories and 196 instances, with four test splits (S1–S4) that progressively introduce novel geometries, novel triplets, and novel tool categories to probe different levels of generalization.

## Results

On compositional action recognition (Table 3), CACNF achieves 86.15% Top-1 on the non-generalizable S1 set but drops to 44.00% on the compound-generalization S4 set, while AIM drops from 83.08% to 39.33%. For motion forecasting (Table 4), predictive models (CAHMP: 28.8/22.1 mm joint error; InterRNN: 29.3/20.4 mm) substantially outperform generative models (InterVAE: 54.9/48.9 mm; MDM: 61.2/53.7 mm) on S1, with all methods degrading 10–20% under generalization. For cooperative grasp synthesis (Table 5), HALO-VAE with environment context achieves the best FID of 2.02 and lowest collision ratio of 2.39% on seen geometries (S1∪S3), but FID jumps to 2.84 and collision ratio rises to 11.11% on unseen geometries (S2∪S4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For action recognition, CACNF [50] outperforms AIM [73] on three of four test sets (S1: 86.15 vs 83.08; S3: 63.02 vs 53.65; S4: 44.00 vs 39.33 Top-1), benefiting from its bounding-box-based compositional decomposition, but AIM retains an edge on S2 (82.81 vs 77.34) where only tool geometry is novel. For motion forecasting, the two predictive baselines CAHMP [10] and InterRNN [68] consistently beat the two generative baselines MDM [62] and InterVAE [68] by 25–35 mm in joint position error across all test sets; CAHMP is the strongest overall (e.g., 28.8 mm right-hand error on S1 vs 54.9 for InterVAE). For grasp synthesis, HALO-VAE [30] with environment conditioning dominates ContactGen [38] on FID (2.02 vs 12.18 on S1∪S3) and collision ratio (2.39% vs 10.40%), while the ablated HALO-VAE⁻ (no environment) degrades sharply in collision ratio (4.19% vs 2.39%), confirming the importance of interaction context. The hand-pose validation (Table 2) shows that CMR [5] and MobRecon [6] both improve when trained on TACO+DexYCB jointly (e.g., MobRecon PA-MPJPE drops from 12.95 to 8.69 mm on TACO test), confirming annotation quality. Overall, all methods suffer 10–40% performance drops under generalization settings, with the largest gaps appearing on S4 (compound generalization) for action recognition and on tool/hand forecasting under novel triplets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of bimanual manipulation datasets and generalizable HOI research. Prior datasets either focus on unimanual interactions (DexYCB, HOI4D, AffordPose), bimanual single-object manipulation (H2O, ARCTIC), or the closely related KIT Bimanual Manipulation dataset which covers only 12 objects and fewer than 20 triplets. TACO departs from all of these by organizing data around tool-action-object triplets with controlled overlap levels, explicitly designed to support systematic generalization studies. The annotation pipeline repurposes existing components (MANO, SAM, LAMA, YOLOv3, MMPose) in a novel multi-view + mocap fusion architecture that automates the full recovery of hand-object meshes, segmentation, and realistic appearances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TACO fills a critical gap: no prior dataset provides sufficient diversity in object geometries and interaction triplets to study generalizable bimanual tool-action-object understanding, which is essential for dexterous robotics, VR/AR, and human-robot interaction. The finding that models generalize well to novel tool geometries within known categories (S2) but degrade sharply on novel interaction triplets (S3) reveals that current methods encode interaction context rather than abstract action semantics, pointing to a clear research direction. Key limitations—no articulated objects, limited scene diversity, and imperfect marker inpainting—leave open questions about whether the generalization patterns hold in more complex, real-world settings. The triplet-organized data split (S1–S4) provides a reusable evaluation protocol that the broader community can adopt for any bimanual HOI method.
