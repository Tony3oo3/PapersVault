## Problem

Transformer-based referring image segmentation methods suffer from query collapse: because RIS inherently requires a single mask prediction, all queries in the decoder are trained to produce the same output, causing them to converge to nearly identical mask predictions. This limits the model's ability to capture diverse object attributes and generalize to complex or novel referring expressions.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized to 480×480) and a natural language expression as input. It produces a full-resolution pixel-wise segmentation mask obtained by an einsum between the final query and 1/4-resolution pixel decoder features followed by upsampling, along with a binary object/no-object classification via a global pooling layer and MLP. The no-object prediction capability extends the model to generalized referring segmentation where the expression may refer to multiple or no objects.

## Method

LQMFormer introduces two core modules. The Gaussian Enhanced Multi-Modal Fusion (GMMF) module, inserted at each stage of a Swin Transformer-B backbone, performs cross-attention between language and visual features and then applies an adaptive Gaussian low-pass filter in the Fourier domain (operating on the amplitude component) to enhance global visual-linguistic relationships while preserving local detail; the result is combined with the original visual features via residual gating. The Dynamic Query Module (DQM) generates diverse language-aware queries from a learnable query bank through Language-Query Cross Attention, then uses a scoring network (trained with BCE loss against binarized decoder cross-attention likelihood maps) to produce a soft-mask that selectively emphasizes queries relevant to the referring expression. A Query-Mask Margin Loss enforces a minimum pairwise distance of 1.0 between mask embeddings of different queries, directly penalizing the convergence that causes query collapse. The decoder processes multi-scale features (1/8, 1/16, 1/32) with masked cross-attention, self-attention, and FFN blocks over L=3 iterations.

## Results

On RefCOCO, LQMFormer achieves the best oIoU across all splits: 74.16 (val), 76.82 (testA), and 71.04 (testB), surpassing ReLA by 0.34–0.86 points. On the GRES benchmark, the model achieves 64.98% oIoU and 70.94% gIoU, outperforming ReLA by 2.56 and 7.34 points respectively, while also reaching 67.47% no-target accuracy (N-acc.)—an 11-point improvement over ReLA. On RefCOCO+ testA, LQMFormer scores 71.84% oIoU, the highest among all compared methods, though it trails ReLA by 0.13 points on RefCOCO+ val and by 0.06 on testB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest transformer-based baseline ReLA [32], LQMFormer wins on all three RefCOCO splits (by 0.34–0.86 oIoU), on RefCOCO+ testA (71.84 vs. 71.02), and on G-Ref val(G) (62.97 vs. 62.70), but narrowly loses on RefCOCO+ val (65.91 vs. 66.04) and testB (57.59 vs. 57.65). On the GRES benchmark, LQMFormer dominates ReLA across every metric: +2.56 oIoU, +7.34 gIoU, +11.10 N-acc., and +2.80 T-acc., with the gIoU gap being the most substantial. Compared to VLT [13], LQMFormer leads on RefCOCO (all splits), RefCOCO+ val and testA, and G-Ref val(G), but trails VLT by 0.18 on G-Ref test(U) (66.04 vs. 66.22). Against LAVT [54], LQMFormer consistently outperforms by 1.4–3.5 oIoU on RefCOCO, by 3.46 on RefCOCO+ testA, and by 2.47 on G-Ref val(G). Older CNN/RNN-based baselines (MCN, VLT-ICCV, ReSTR, CRIS) are surpassed by 3–12 oIoU points across all splits, confirming the advantage of the transformer-based architecture with the proposed GMMF and DQM components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the transformer-based RIS lineage (VLT, LAVT, ReLA/GRES, CRIS), which introduced cross-attention and query-based decoders for referring segmentation. It departs from these methods by identifying and addressing 'query collapse'—the tendency of all queries to converge on identical mask predictions when only a single ground-truth mask is available—through a Gaussian-enhanced Fourier-domain fusion module (extending S-Post-CA [40] into the backbone) and a Dynamic Query Module with a scoring network. The multi-scale decoder follows the Mask2Former [7] paradigm, while the query-mask margin loss is a novel regularization not present in prior RIS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The query collapse diagnosis is a useful and previously under-acknowledged pathology in single-mask transformer decoders, and the proposed remedies (diverse query generation, relevance scoring, margin loss) offer a practical recipe that could transfer to other single-target segmentation settings. The 7–12% gIoU gain and 11% no-target accuracy improvement on GRES are meaningful, but classic RIS gains over ReLA are modest (≤1% oIoU), suggesting the approach's strongest value is in generalized/multi-target scenarios. Open questions include whether the Fourier-domain fusion and scoring network generalize beyond Swin-B + BERT, and the authors' stated direction toward LLM integration hints at a broader multimodal grounding challenge still unresolved.
