## Problem

Existing audio-driven talking face methods produce deterministic, fixed facial dynamics (expressions, blinks, head poses) that fail to capture the inherent one-to-many variability of real human nonverbal cues. Additionally, low-resolution image generators struggle to render emotion-aware high-definition textures—such as emotion-specific wrinkles and clear teeth—that are essential for lifelike emotional expression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242541/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a 512×512 source image, a driving audio clip, and either a discrete emotion label (from 8 MEAD categories) or an emotion reference image for transfer. It outputs a sequence of 512×512 emotional talking face video frames, with intermediate 3DMM coefficients (64-dim expression β, 6-dim pose ρ) generated autoregressively. The VQIG operates on a 16×16 spatial grid of 256-dim latent vectors quantized against a 1024-entry codebook.

## Method

FlowVQTalker comprises two modules connected via 3DMM coefficients. The Flow-based Coeff. Generator (FCG) uses ExpFlow and PoseFlow—normalizing flows with multi-actnorm, invertible 1×1 convolutions, and transformer-based affine coupling layers—to establish a bijective mapping between 3DMM coefficients and a Student's t Mixture Model (SMM) latent space, where each mixture component encodes one emotion class; inference stochastically samples from the SMM for diverse expressions while the invertible forward pass enables emotion transfer from a reference. The Vector-Quantized Image Generator (VQIG) reframes image synthesis as a code query task: a frozen VQ-GAN-trained codebook stores high-quality facial textures, and a transformer predicts code sequences that retrieve the best-matching entries, while a warping network, fuse network, multi-head cross-attention, and AdaIN jointly preserve identity, spatial motion, and emotion-aware texture details.

## Results

On MEAD, FlowVQTalker achieves the best FID (16.553), lowest M-LMD (1.939), highest CPBD (0.181), and highest emotion accuracy (71.53%) among all compared methods. On HDTF, it similarly leads in FID (15.165), M-LMD (1.643), and CPBD (0.268), with CPBD nearly doubling the next-best method. In the user study, FlowVQTalker scores 4.03/5 for lip-sync, 4.25/5 for image quality, and 60.6% emotion classification accuracy, all surpassing the strongest baselines (EAT 3.94, IP-LAP 3.83, PC-AVS 54.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowVQTalker outperforms all seven main baselines on FID, M-LMD, CPBD, and emotion accuracy across both MEAD and HDTF. The largest margin is on CPBD (texture clarity), where FlowVQTalker scores 0.181 on MEAD and 0.268 on HDTF versus 0.103–0.142 for all baselines, validating the VQIG codebook's contribution to fine-grained detail. On emotion accuracy, FlowVQTalker (71.53%) surpasses the next-best EAT (65.83%) by ~6 points and the emotion-agnostic methods (Wav2Lip, IP-LAP) by over 50 points. The sole metric where FlowVQTalker trails is SyncNet confidence: Wav2Lip achieves 8.484 (MEAD) and 9.073 (HDTF) versus FlowVQTalker's 5.901 and 6.766, which the authors attribute to Wav2Lip's explicit SyncNet discriminator during training. Among pose-diversity comparisons (Fig. 5), only AVCT matches FlowVQTalker's trace-map spread, while FlowVQTalker achieves better audio-pose correlation. In the user study, FlowVQTalker leads all baselines on lip-sync (4.03 vs. EAT 3.94), image quality (4.25 vs. IP-LAP 3.83), and emotion recognition (60.6% vs. PC-AVS 54.9%).

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowVQTalker sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: normalizing-flow-based generative modeling (Rezende & Mohamed, 2015; StyleVR, Ji et al. 2023) and vector-quantized image synthesis (VQ-GAN, Esser et al. 2021). It departs from prior emotional talking face methods (EAMM, EMMN, EAT, PD-FGC) that rely on deterministic mappings from audio or emotion labels to fixed facial dynamics, instead modeling the one-to-many audio-to-expression relationship through a Student's t Mixture Model in flow latent space. The VQIG module repurposes the VQ-GAN codebook paradigm—previously applied to face restoration and speaker-specific reenactment—as a generic, identity-agnostic texture lookup for high-fidelity rendering, which the authors claim is the first use of both normalizing flow and VQ codebooks jointly for speech-driven emotional facial animation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical gaps that limit current talking face systems: the lack of natural non-deterministic variation (blinks, micro-expressions, head poses) and the inability of low-resolution generators to produce emotion-aware HD textures and clear teeth. The flow-based formulation offers a principled way to balance diversity (random sampling) with controllability (bijective emotion transfer), a duality that deterministic regression-based methods cannot achieve. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond the relatively small MEAD dataset (60 subjects, 8 emotions), whether the generic codebook can handle extreme identities or occlusions, and whether the 3DMM intermediate representation remains a bottleneck for very high-resolution or stylized outputs.
