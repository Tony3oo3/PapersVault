## Problem

Diffusion models still exhibit non-trivial distributional discrepancies between generated and real images, manifesting as missing object errors in text-to-image generation and low image quality (artifacts, unnatural distortions). Existing remedies—attention-based guidance, discriminator-based score modification—either do not target the root distributional gap or remain subject to discretization errors in the denoising trajectory, yielding sub-optimal corrections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240190/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (or class label for conditional generation) as the primary conditioning signal, augmented by a small set of real images (with or without captions) used to train a discriminator, and a pre-trained object detector (DETR with ResNet-50) that scores object presence at each denoising step. It produces a single generated image by running K parallel denoising paths through the particle filter and resampling at each step. The method operates on latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion v2.1) combined with the Restart sampler, where resampling is inserted immediately before the noise-readdition step.

## Method

The core contribution is a particle filtering framework that corrects the diffusion generation distribution by resampling K candidate images at each denoising step with a weight proportional to the likelihood ratio p(X_t|C)/q(X_t|C). The proposal distribution is simply the diffusion model's own denoising step, so the correction is entirely encoded in the resampling weight. Two variants estimate this weight: PF-DISCRIMINATOR trains a conditional discriminator on real vs. generated noisy images to approximate the full ratio; PF-HYBRID decomposes the ratio via Bayes' rule into an unconditional likelihood ratio (from an unconditional discriminator, addressing image quality) and an object mention ratio (from the object detector, addressing missing objects), where the object mention ratio uses a one-step clean-image prediction to score whether mentioned objects are present. This decomposition allows fine-grained correction of distinct error types simultaneously, and the resampling-based approach avoids the discretization errors that plague score-modification methods like discriminator guidance.

## Results

On text-to-image generation, PF-HYBRID achieves 75.79% object occurrence on GPT-Synthetic and 68.13% on MS-COCO (complex captions), outperforming the strongest baseline (REWARDSELECT) by ~4% in object occurrence while also achieving the lowest FID. On class-conditioned ImageNet-64, PF-DISCRIMINATOR with the Restart sampler achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.02, surpassing the discriminator-guidance baseline (D-GUIDANCE). In human evaluation, PF-HYBRID wins on both object occurrence and perceived image quality against all baselines, with the largest margin over plain Stable Diffusion (−6.00 quality score on GPT-Synthetic).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PF-HYBRID outperforms plain Stable Diffusion [47] by ~11% in object occurrence on GPT-Synthetic (75.79 vs. 64.48) and ~8% on MS-COCO (68.13 vs. 59.87), while also winning on subjective quality. Against the attention-modification baselines SPATIAL-TEMPORAL [61] and ATTEND-EXCITE [7], PF-HYBRID achieves substantially higher object occurrence at comparable or lower computational cost, and is the only method that simultaneously achieves high object occurrence and low FID. Compared to the selection-based baselines TIFASELECT and REWARDSELECT [29], PF-HYBRID improves object occurrence by ~4% on GPT-Synthetic (75.79 vs. 71.97) and ~5% on MS-COCO (68.13 vs. 63.25), while also achieving better FID. Against D-GUIDANCE [31] (discriminator-guided score modification), PF-DISCRIMINATOR achieves lower FID on ImageNet-64 and FFHQ at large NFEs, and incurs only 0.66× the per-NFE compute cost since the discriminator is evaluated at a subset of steps without backward passes. The ablation confirms that removing particle filtering degrades PF-HYBRID on all metrics, and that the hybrid weight (combining discriminator + object detector) is necessary to balance object occurrence against image quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the particle filtering framework for diffusion models established by Wu et al. [59], adapting its proposal-and-resampling structure to correct distributional discrepancies rather than to impose conditional constraints. It departs from the dominant line of work on text-to-image faithfulness (cross-attention reweighting in [3, 17, 34, 61]) and from discriminator-guided score modification [31], arguing that both modify the generation trajectory in ways that remain subject to discretization errors or fail to address the root distributional gap. The Restart sampler [64] provides the iterative denoise-restart structure into which the resampling module is inserted, and GAN theory [20] supplies the discriminator-based likelihood ratio estimator. Unlike Karthik et al. [29], who perform sample selection only at the final denoising step, this paper applies resampling at every step, progressively correcting the distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By targeting the distributional gap directly rather than its symptoms, the method offers a principled correction that simultaneously improves object faithfulness and image quality—something attention-based and score-modification methods struggle to achieve jointly. The state-of-the-art FID of 1.02 on ImageNet-64 and the 5% object occurrence gain on MS-COCO demonstrate practical impact. However, the approach requires external guidance (a pre-trained object detector and a small set of real images), limiting its applicability to settings where such resources are available, and the hybrid formulation is specific to text-to-image generation. Open questions include whether the particle filtering correction can be made self-contained (without external detectors), whether it extends to video or 3D generation, and how the method scales to very large K or very long denoising chains.
