## Problem

3D point cloud localization from natural language in city-scale maps remains underexplored, with prior methods (Text2Pose, RET) ignoring relational dynamics among object instances and sentence tokens, relying on pairwise ranking losses that fail to balance positive-negative pairs, and depending on noisy text-instance matching in the fine localization stage that degrades accuracy. This paper addresses the 'last mile problem' where GPS fails and users must navigate to unfamiliar spots using verbal descriptions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-sentence textual query (each sentence describing a spatial relation between the target and an object instance) and a 30m cubic point cloud submap with 10m stride containing 6D per-point features (xyz + RGB). The submap database comprises 17,001 submaps across 15.51 km². The output is a 2D planar coordinate (x, y) in the scene coordinate system, refined from the center of the retrieved submap. The pipeline is two-stage: first retrieving top-k candidate submaps, then regressing the precise offset within them.

## Method

Text2Loc follows a coarse-to-fine pipeline. For global place recognition, a frozen T5 encoder feeds a Hierarchical Transformer with Max-pooling (HTM) that captures intra-sentence (self-attention) and inter-sentence (shared semantics) relationships, while the 3D branch uses PointNet++ augmented with color, positional, and number encoders to produce instance embeddings aggregated via attention + max-pooling. Training replaces the standard pairwise ranking loss with a CLIP-style cross-modal contrastive objective that balances positive and negative pairs within the batch. For fine localization, the paper introduces a matching-free network: a Cascaded Cross-Attention Transformer (CCAT) alternately uses point features as queries against text keys/values and vice versa to fuse cross-modal semantics, followed by a simple MLP regression head. A Prototype-based Map Cloning (PMC) module augments training by sampling neighboring submaps within defined distance thresholds, increasing map variant diversity without requiring text-instance correspondence.

## Results

On the KITTI360Pose test set, Text2Loc achieves a top-1 localization recall of 0.33 at ε<5m, more than doubling the best baseline RET (0.16) and substantially outperforming Text2Pos (0.13). At top-5 and top-10, Text2Loc reaches 0.61 and 0.71 respectively at ε<5m, compared to 0.35/0.46 for RET. For global place recognition (submap retrieval), Text2Loc attains 0.28 top-1 recall on the test set, a 78% relative improvement over RET's 0.18 on the validation set. The matching-free fine localization network also reduces inference time to 2.27 ms (vs. 43.11 ms for the matcher-based variant) while using only 1.06M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Text2Loc is compared against two prior methods: Text2Pos (CVPR 2022) and RET (arXiv 2023). On the test set at ε<5m, Text2Loc achieves 0.33 top-1 localization recall, which is 2× higher than RET (0.16) and 2.5× higher than Text2Pos (0.13). The advantage persists across all error thresholds and retrieval depths: at ε<15m top-10, Text2Loc reaches 0.86 vs. 0.71 (RET) and 0.65 (Text2Pos). For global place recognition, Text2Loc's top-1 retrieval recall of 0.28 on the test set exceeds Text2Pos's 0.12 by 133%; RET does not report test-set retrieval numbers. The paper also shows that replacing the text-instance matcher in Text2Pos with Text2Loc's CCAT module alone yields ~7% improvement at top-1, isolating the contribution of the matching-free design. Overall, Text2Loc wins on every reported metric against both baselines, with the largest relative gains at the strictest error threshold (ε<5m) and smallest k.

## Relation to Prior Work

Text2Loc extends the text-to-point-cloud localization pipeline established by Text2Pose (CVPR 2022) and RET, both of which use a coarse-to-fine strategy with a text-instance matcher in the fine stage. It departs from this lineage in three key ways: replacing the LSTM text encoder with a frozen T5 LLM plus a hierarchical transformer (HTM) for intra/inter-sentence context, substituting the pairwise ranking loss with a CLIP-style contrastive objective, and—most notably—eliminating the text-instance matcher entirely in favor of a cascaded cross-attention transformer (CCAT) borrowed from CASSPR. The instance encoder also inherits from Text2Pose's PointNet++-based design but adds a number encoder for point-count priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The matching-free fine localization is a practical simplification: it removes the heavy SuperGlue/Sinkhorn pipeline, cutting inference time to ~5% of the baseline while improving accuracy, which matters for real-time deployment in delivery or navigation scenarios. However, the method is evaluated only on KITTI360Pose (one city, one sensor suite), and the robustness analysis reveals significant degradation when even a single sentence in the query is altered, suggesting fragility to paraphrase or noise. Open questions include whether the contrastive objective and CCAT generalize to other 3D maps or sensor modalities, and whether the coarse-stage dependency (fine localization fails when all retrieved submaps are negative) can be mitigated.
