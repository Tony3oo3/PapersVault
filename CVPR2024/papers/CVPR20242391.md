## Problem

Machine-generated visual instruction datasets (e.g., LLaVA-158K) contain massive hallucinatory toxicities—32.6% of samples in LLaVA include object, relation, or attribute hallucinations—that propagate into MLLMs and degrade their perceptual accuracy. Prior mitigation efforts focus on inference-time plug-ins or collecting extra remedial data, largely neglecting the root cause: the hallucinatory errors embedded in the training data itself.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a triple of (image, instruction/question, GPT-4-generated description) from existing visual instruction datasets. It first decomposes the description into fine-grained answer chunks (objects, relations, attributes) via textual scene graph parsing, then generates targeted questions per chunk using an LLM. The output is a rectified instruction dataset (LLaVA+) with hallucinatory chunks removed while preserving contextual coherence, plus an expanded counterfactual dataset (LLaVA++) that injects hallucinatory objects into rare co-occurrence scenes to break spurious correlations.

## Method

HalluciDoctor employs a consistency cross-checking paradigm: for each answer chunk, it queries multiple MLLM experts (BLIP-2, InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4) with generated questions and computes a BEM-based consistency score; chunks scoring below 0.5 are flagged as hallucinations and removed via LLM-guided sentence editing. A key secondary contribution is a seesaw-based counterfactual expansion strategy that identifies long-tail object co-occurrence patterns causing spurious correlations, then uses an enhancement factor (upweighting rare co-occurrences) and an inhibiting factor (suppressing contextually implausible pairs) to select target scenes where hallucinatory objects are counterfactually inserted, balancing the co-occurrence distribution and strengthening MLLM resistance to hallucination.

## Results

On the extended CHAIR benchmark (500 images from MSCOCO ∩ Visual Genome), HalluciDoctor's LLaVA++ reduces instance-level CHAIR_obj from 12.0 (LLaVA) to 5.7 on MiniGPT-4 and from 10.6 to 6.4 on mPLUG-Owl, achieving a 44.6% relative hallucination reduction overall. On the MME benchmark, LLaVA++ further improves perception scores to 955.28 (MiniGPT-4) and 1114.34 (mPLUG-Owl) versus 859.64 and 967.34 for the original LLaVA, showing that hallucination removal also boosts general multi-modal performance. The rectified dataset LLaVA+ already cuts sentence-level CHAIR_rel from 34.8 to 20.5 (MiniGPT-4), and LLaVA++ pushes it further to 17.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original LLaVA-158K (w/LLaVA), HalluciDoctor's LLaVA++ dramatically reduces all three CHAIR types: on MiniGPT-4, instance-level CHAIR_obj drops from 12.0 to 5.7 (−52.5%) and sentence-level CHAIR_rel from 34.8 to 17.1 (−50.9%). Compared to the SOTA curated dataset LRV-Instruction (w/LRV), LLaVA++ wins on every metric for both backbones, e.g., MiniGPT-4 CHAIR_attri 7.1 vs. 13.6 (instance-level) and mPLUG-Owl CHAIR_attri 6.7 vs. 13.0. Against the specialized plug-in methods LURC and VIGC, LLaVA++ matches LURC on CHAIR_obj (5.7) but substantially outperforms it on CHAIR_rel (5.4 vs. 7.6) and CHAIR_attri (7.1 vs. 13.3), and beats VIGC across all three instance-level metrics (5.7/5.4/7.1 vs. 6.1/7.5/11.5). On the MME benchmark, LLaVA++ also surpasses all baselines in both perception and cognition (e.g., 955.28/320.71 for MiniGPT-4 vs. 870.12/291.79 for LRV), confirming that hallucination removal does not sacrifice general multi-modal capability. The method's robustness is further validated on MiniGPT4-Instruction, where HalluciDoctor reduces instance-level CHAIR from 9.2 to 5.9 while improving MME perception from 616.41 to 696.96.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LLaVA's machine-generated visual instruction pipeline, which uses text-only GPT-4 to produce instruction-following data from image captions and bounding boxes. It departs from prior hallucination-mitigation work (LURC, VIGC, M-HalDetect, LRV-Instruction) that operates at the model or inference level by instead targeting the training data itself, using a multi-MLLM cross-checking paradigm to detect and remove hallucinatory chunks before fine-tuning. The seesaw-based counterfactual expansion draws on counterfactual generation literature but repurposes it to rebalance long-tail object co-occurrence distributions in the instruction data, a data-distribution intervention not previously applied to MLLM training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By quantifying that 32.6% of LLaVA-158K contains hallucinatory content and demonstrating that cleaning this data yields a 44.6% relative hallucination reduction while improving MME scores, the paper provides a practical, model-agnostic pipeline for auditing and rectifying existing visual instruction datasets. The finding that spurious correlations from long-tail object co-occurrences drive many hallucinations opens a path toward distribution-aware data curation beyond simple error removal. Limitations include reliance on multiple MLLM experts for cross-checking (computational cost), a fixed BEM threshold of 0.5, and evaluation primarily on 500-image subsets; whether the counterfactual expansion strategy generalizes to other modalities or scales to much larger datasets remains open.
