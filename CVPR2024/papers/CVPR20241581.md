## Problem

Naive multimodal NeRF that directly shares implicit features between LiDAR and camera in a single field fails to outperform single-modality baselines on real-world data, because spatial and temporal misalignment between sensors causes conflicting optimization signals that degrade both modalities simultaneously. This paper identifies and characterizes this misalignment issue through systematic analysis of raw sensor inputs, hash grid features, and density values, then proposes a solution that aligns coarse geometry while preserving modality-specific details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241581/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB camera images and LiDAR point clouds with per-point intensities, where LiDAR laser beams are treated as oriented rays analogous to camera rays for volumetric rendering. It produces novel RGB images (evaluated with PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and novel LiDAR point clouds with intensities (evaluated with Chamfer Distance, F-score, and MAE) from arbitrary new sensor viewpoints. The hash encoding is decomposed into separate per-modality grids, and a pre-trained LiDAR field provides shared geometric initialization.

## Method

AlignMiF introduces two modules that align coarse geometry across modalities while preserving each modality's unique fine details. Geometry-Aware Alignment (GAA) decomposes the multi-resolution hash encoding into separate LiDAR and camera grids, then uses a level-decomposition parameter β (borrowed from Neuralangelo) to zero out higher-resolution levels of the *other* modality's features, so each modality receives its own full-resolution features plus only the coarse-level features from the other modality, fused via concatenation. Shared Geometry Initialization (SGI) adds hash grid features from a pre-trained LiDAR field to both modalities' learned hash features, providing a common geometric starting point that anchors the camera's otherwise ambiguous geometry. The key insight is that misalignment manifests primarily at fine detail levels (e.g., LiDAR captures rougher object boundaries than camera), while both sensors share the same underlying coarse scene structure, so constraining alignment only at coarse levels resolves the optimization conflict without suppressing modality-specific information.

## Results

On KITTI-360, AlignMiF achieves 25.31 PSNR (RGB) and 0.081 Chamfer Distance (LiDAR), surpassing the best-tuned UniSim-SF by +2.01 PSNR and reducing LiDAR C-D by 13.8% over the single-modality LiDAR-NeRF baseline. On Waymo, the gains are even larger: +3.11 PSNR over UniSim-SF (29.78 vs. 26.67) and a 14.2% reduction in LiDAR C-D (0.169 vs. 0.197). Crucially, AlignMiF is the only method that simultaneously outperforms both single-modality baselines on all metrics across both real-world datasets, whereas UniSim-SF exhibits a trade-off where improving one modality degrades the other.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the single-camera baseline i-NGP, AlignMiF improves PSNR by +0.70 on KITTI-360 (25.31 vs. 24.61) and +0.96 on Waymo (29.78 vs. 28.82), while also delivering competitive LiDAR metrics that i-NGP cannot produce. Against the single-LiDAR baseline LiDAR-NeRF, AlignMiF reduces Chamfer Distance by 13.8% on KITTI-360 (0.081 vs. 0.094) and 14.2% on Waymo (0.169 vs. 0.197), while simultaneously producing high-quality RGB views. Against the multimodal UniSim-SF baseline, AlignMiF wins on every metric for both modalities regardless of how UniSim-SF's loss weights are tuned: the LiDAR-tuned variant sacrifices 2.01 PSNR on KITTI-360, and the camera-tuned variant inflates LiDAR C-D to 0.355 on Waymo, whereas AlignMiF achieves 25.31 PSNR and 0.081 C-D simultaneously. Against StreetSurf on Waymo, AlignMiF's LiDAR-camera fusion (29.78 PSNR) outperforms StreetSurf's fusion (27.13 PSNR) by +2.65, and even exceeds StreetSurf's single-camera result (28.14), confirming that the misalignment issue is not specific to one architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal NeRF line initiated by UniSim, NeRF-LiDAR, and StreetSurf, which all rely on naive implicit fusion—directly sharing hash-encoded features across sensor modalities in a single field. It builds on i-NGP's multiresolution hash encoding and LiDAR-NeRF's formulation of LiDAR beams as rays, but departs from prior work by identifying that this shared representation causes geometric misalignment between sensors. The alignment strategy draws conceptually from Neuralangelo and HRNeus, which decompose hash levels into coarse and fine geometry, but repurposes this decomposition to selectively align only the coarse levels across modalities while preserving modality-specific details.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that naive multimodal fusion degrades both modalities simultaneously on real-world data challenges the common assumption that more sensor inputs always improve neural field reconstruction, and provides a concrete diagnosis (spatial and temporal sensor misalignment) with a practical two-module fix. The approach is lightweight and could be adopted by any multimodal NeRF pipeline, but its scope is currently limited to LiDAR-camera pairs and the fusion mechanism (simple concatenation) leaves room for more expressive cross-modal interaction. Open questions include whether the coarse-geometry alignment principle generalizes to additional modalities (e.g., radar, semantic segmentation) and whether the shared initialization step can be replaced by learned alignment to reduce the two-stage training dependency.
