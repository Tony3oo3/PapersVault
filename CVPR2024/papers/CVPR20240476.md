## Problem

Existing VPR aggregation methods like NetVLAD assign local features to clusters via per-row softmax, which only models the feature-to-cluster direction and ignores the cluster-to-feature constraint, leading to suboptimal mass distribution. Additionally, prior work using DINOv2 for VPR (AnyLoc) applied the backbone in its pre-trained form without task-specific fine-tuning, leaving substantial performance on the table. This paper addresses both gaps by reformulating the assignment as an optimal transport problem and fine-tuning DINOv2 end-to-end for place recognition.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (224×224 for training, 322×322 for evaluation; dimensions must be divisible by 14 to form patches) and produces a single L2-normalized global descriptor vector of size 8192+256 (or slimmer variants of 512+32 or 2048+64). This descriptor is compared via nearest-neighbor search against a database of pre-computed descriptors to yield a ranked top-k list of candidate places, with retrieval considered correct if a match within 25 m (or 2 frames for NordLand) appears in the top-k.

## Method

DINOv2 SALAD uses a fine-tuned DINOv2-B backbone (last 4 transformer blocks unfrozen) to extract patch-level local features and a global token. The SALAD module computes a score matrix via two fully connected layers (no k-means initialization), augments it with a 'dustbin' column parameterized by a single scalar to discard uninformative features, and then solves the assignment as an optimal transport problem using the Sinkhorn algorithm, which iteratively normalizes rows and columns of exp(S) to satisfy both feature-to-cluster and cluster-to-feature marginal constraints. Dimensionality-reduced features (768→128) are aggregated into clusters by weighted summation (without NetVLAD's centroid subtraction), and the processed global token is concatenated with the flattened VLAD vector before L2 normalization. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with multi-similarity loss and AdamW for only 4 epochs on GSV-Cities, converging in ~30 minutes on a single GPU.

## Results

DINOv2 SALAD (full model, 8192+256 descriptor) achieves 75.0% R@1 and 88.8% R@5 on MSLS Challenge, 76.0% R@1 on NordLand, 92.2% R@1 on MSLS Val, 95.1% R@1 on Pitts250k-test, and 92.1% R@1 on SPED. On SF-XL, it reaches 88.6% R@1 (v1) and 94.8% R@1 (v2) without ever training on San Francisco images. The method trains in under 30 minutes on a single RTX 3090 and infers in 2.41 ms per image, while outperforming two-stage re-ranking methods that require 200+ ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINOv2 SALAD outperforms all six single-stage baselines on every dataset and metric. The most dramatic gains appear on the hardest benchmarks: +7.6% R@1 over EigenPlaces (67.4→75.0) on MSLS Challenge and +17.6% R@1 over MixVPR (58.4→76.0) on NordLand, where seasonal variation is extreme. Against the classic NetVLAD baseline, the improvement is +39.9% R@1 on MSLS Challenge (35.1→75.0). Even the slim 512+32 descriptor variant (70.8% R@1 on MSLS Challenge) surpasses all previous full-size models. In the two-stage comparison (Table 2), DINOv2 SALAD beats R2Former (73.0→75.0 R@1 on MSLS Challenge), TransVPR (63.9), and Patch-NetVLAD (48.1) while running 84× faster than R2Former (2.41 ms vs. 202.37 ms) and using 0.63 GB memory vs. 4.7 GB. On SF-XL, DINOv2 SALAD improves over EigenPlaces by +4.5% R@1 (v1) and +4.0% (v2) despite never seeing San Francisco data during fine-tuning, whereas EigenPlaces was trained on that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates NetVLAD's per-row softmax soft-assignment of local features to clusters as an optimal transport problem solved via the Sinkhorn algorithm, drawing on the same formulation used in SuperGlue for feature matching. It departs from NetVLAD in three key ways: removing k-means initialization priors, introducing a learnable 'dustbin' to discard uninformative features, and enforcing both feature-to-cluster and cluster-to-feature mass constraints. On the backbone side, it extends AnyLoc's use of DINOv2 by fine-tuning the last encoder blocks end-to-end for VPR, rather than using the foundation model in its frozen pre-trained form as AnyLoc does.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single-stage pipeline that trains in under one hour and infers in under 3 ms outperforms two-stage methods with expensive re-ranking by a wide margin, removing a major deployment bottleneck for robotics and AR applications. The finding that fine-tuning DINOv2 (which its authors reported as yielding only marginal gains) produces substantial VPR improvements suggests foundation models may be more task-adaptable than previously assumed. Open questions include whether more sophisticated optimal transport constraints (beyond the simplest Sinkhorn formulation) could further improve assignments, and whether SALAD's dependence on high-spatial-resolution features limits its applicability to coarser backbones.
