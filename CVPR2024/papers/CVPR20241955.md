## Problem

Deep neural networks for low-light raw video enhancement require high computational cost and large memory, making them impractical on resource-limited devices. While binary neural networks (BNNs) offer extreme compression, two challenges remain unaddressed for video enhancement: how to fuse temporal information without complex alignment modules (e.g., optical flow, deformable convolution) that are difficult to binarize, and how to close the significant performance gap between 1-bit convolutions and their full-precision counterparts.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of consecutive low-light noisy raw frames in Bayer pattern, where each 2×2 Bayer superpixel is packed into four color channels and then amplified by a predefined scaling factor to a proper brightness level. It processes 10-frame local windows with 256×256 input patches during training and outputs clean, bright, denoised raw frames in the same packed format. The first and last convolutions in the network remain full-precision to preserve raw input information and accurately map features to the output.

## Method

BRVE is a three-stage binary U-Net architecture: a per-frame binary U-Net for pre-denoising, a shift binary U-Net for spatial-temporal feature fusion, and a final binary U-Net for reconstruction, connected by recurrent embeddings for long-range propagation. The key innovation is the Distribution-Aware Binary Convolution (DABC), which extracts channel-wise statistics (mean, standard deviation, mean absolute value) of the real-valued input and passes them through a 1D convolution + Sigmoid to produce a dynamic per-channel scale factor that compensates for quantization loss in the binary convolution output. For temporal fusion, a parameter-free spatial-temporal shift operation splits each frame's features into two halves, cyclically shifts them across a 3-frame window for temporal aggregation, and applies 24-direction spatial shifts to handle motion misalignment, all processed by subsequent binary conv blocks. The shift operation enables parallel processing of multiple frames using standard 2D convolutions without introducing extra parameters.

## Results

On the SMOID dataset, BRVE achieves PSNR of 40.05/40.25/40.64 dB and SSIM of 0.9742/0.9765/0.9786 at Gain 0/15/30, outperforming the best BNN baseline BBCU (39.92/40.11/40.48 dB) while using only 0.3M parameters and 1.49G FLOPs. On the LLRVD dataset, BRVE reaches 37.07 dB PSNR and 0.9581 SSIM, surpassing the lightweight full-precision EMVD-S (36.70 dB) by 0.37 dB with comparable FLOPs, and approaching EMVD-L (37.00 dB) while using only 9.4% of its FLOPs and 3.3% of its parameters. The ST-RRED temporal consistency metric is 0.0639/0.0557/0.0570 on SMOID and 0.0455 on LLRVD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRVE outperforms all six BNN baselines (BNN, Bi-real, IRNet, ReActNet, BTM, BBCU) on every metric across all three gain levels on SMOID, with the largest margin over BBCU (the strongest BNN baseline) being +0.13 dB PSNR at Gain 0 and +0.16 dB at Gain 30. Against full-precision methods, BRVE trails the top performers ShiftNet (42.11 dB) and LLRVD (41.51 dB) by 2.06 and 1.46 dB respectively at Gain 0, but does so with only 1.49G FLOPs versus 32.87G and 46.14G — a 22× and 31× reduction in computation. BRVE surpasses the lightweight full-precision EMVD-S (39.71 dB, 1.66G FLOPs) by 0.34 dB PSNR on SMOID and by 0.37 dB on LLRVD (37.07 vs 36.70), while using fewer FLOPs. On LLRVD, BRVE also approaches EMVD-L (37.00 dB) with only 9.4% of its FLOPs and 3.3% of its parameters. The ST-RRED temporal consistency metric confirms BRVE's advantage over other BNNs (e.g., 0.0639 vs BBCU's 0.0745 at Gain 0), though it remains higher than top full-precision methods like ShiftNet (0.0328).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of binary neural networks and low-light video enhancement, two areas that had not previously been combined. It adapts the spatial-temporal shift operation from ShiftNet [18] into a cyclic, parameter-free form suitable for binarization, and builds on the full-precision shortcut mechanism from Bi-real Net [20] and the RSign/RPRelu activation functions from ReActNet [21] to construct its binary convolution blocks. The key methodological departure is the distribution-aware binary convolution (DABC), which injects real-valued channel statistics (mean, std, absolute mean) into the binary path via a lightweight attention module, going beyond the fixed scale factors used in XNOR-Net and the learned thresholds in ReActNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is enabling low-light raw video enhancement on resource-constrained devices (smartphones, hand-held cameras) where full-precision networks are infeasible, with BRVE achieving comparable quality to the lightweight full-precision EMVD-S at 94% fewer FLOPs. However, a ~2 dB PSNR gap to the best full-precision method (ShiftNet) remains, and the approach is validated on only two datasets with 3-frame local windows, leaving open whether the cyclic temporal shift and DABC generalize to longer sequences, larger motions, or other video restoration tasks. The distribution-aware mechanism could potentially transfer to other BNN applications in low-level vision, but this remains unexplored.
