## Problem

Unsupervised landmark discovery (ULD) must find semantically meaningful object keypoints without any annotated ground truth, yet existing methods struggle under large intra-class variations such as extreme pose changes and expressions. This paper explores whether pre-trained diffusion models, which implicitly encode correspondence cues, can serve as a powerful feature backbone for ULD, and how to exploit their structure to handle pose variation.

## Input / Output

The method takes unannotated RGB images of a single object category (e.g., human or cat faces) as input, with object regions of interest detected by Detectron2. It outputs K 2D landmark locations per image, represented as a confidence heatmap of shape H^o × W^o × K, from which keypoints are extracted via non-maximum suppression. During training, the method also produces 64-dimensional latent pose codes and Q×K cluster assignments used for pseudo-labeling.

## Method

The core insight is that pre-trained Stable Diffusion features, aggregated across network layers and diffusion timesteps via a learned mixing network, contain rich correspondence cues sufficient for landmark discovery even in a zero-shot setting (random pixel clustering + nearest-neighbor querying). Building on this, D-ULD appends a descriptor head and a heatmap detector head on top of the frozen SD backbone, bootstraps with SILK self-supervised keypoint learning, then iteratively refines landmarks via K-means clustering of descriptors and self-training with the resulting pseudo-labels. D-ULD++ further introduces a VAE-based pose-guided proxy task that projects predicted landmark heatmaps into a 64-dim latent pose space and reconstructs them, implicitly learning pose structure without pose supervision. A two-stage clustering mechanism then first partitions images by latent pose codes into Q groups and, within each group, clusters keypoint descriptors into K sub-clusters (yielding Q×K total clusters), producing more pose-aware pseudo-labels that reduce within-cluster variation and improve landmark consistency across extreme viewpoints.

## Results

D-ULD++ achieves the lowest Forward and Backward NME across all four benchmarks: 2.19/2.78 on MAFL, 2.92/3.62 on AFLW, 2.12/2.85 on LS3D, and 2.89/3.12 on CatHeads (K=10). The ZeroShot baseline (clustering + nearest-neighbor only, no training) already surpasses all published methods, with relative improvements of up to 34.35% on LS3D forward NME. D-ULD++ further improves over the best published results by 20–59% relative reduction in NME depending on dataset and direction, with the largest gains on AFLW (50.91% forward, 54.52% backward) and LS3D (59.31% forward, 39.23% backward).

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-ULD++ outperforms all five published baselines on every dataset and both NME directions. Against the strongest published method (Awan et al. [2] on AFLW/LS3D/CatHeads; Jakab [17] and Mallis [32] on MAFL), D-ULD++ reduces forward NME by 50.91% on AFLW (2.92 vs. 5.91), 59.31% on LS3D (2.12 vs. 5.21), and 23.13% on CatHeads (2.89 vs. 3.76). On MAFL, D-ULD++ achieves 2.19 forward NME versus 3.19 for both Jakab and Mallis, a 31.34% relative improvement. The ZeroShot baseline (no training, only clustering of diffusion features) already beats all published methods, confirming the strong correspondence cues in Stable Diffusion representations. The ablation (Table 2) shows that the pose-guided proxy task contributes an additional 7.8%/12.4% NME reduction on AFLW over D-ULD, and two-stage clustering further improves over simple clustering, with the full VAE (ELBO) being inferior to the contrastive latent-code loss in Eq. ## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes pre-trained Stable Diffusion internal representations as the feature backbone for unsupervised landmark discovery (ULD), departing from prior ULD methods that relied on task-specific architectures (FAN, equivariant networks) or conditional image generation as proxy tasks. It adapts the feature aggregation strategy from Diffusion Hyperfeatures [Luo et al., 2023] to combine features across layers and timesteps, and builds on the clustering-based self-training correspondence framework of Mallis et al. [2023] for pseudo-label generation. The key conceptual departure is treating a general-purpose generative model as a frozen (or lightly fine-tuned) correspondence encoder, rather than training a dedicated landmark detector from scratch with equivariance or generation losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a zero-shot clustering of diffusion features already outperforms all published ULD methods suggests that large-scale pre-trained generative models encode rich, transferable correspondence structure that was previously underexploited for geometric tasks. This opens the door to applying similar feature-extraction strategies to other object categories without task-specific architectural design. Limitations include dependence on SILK for initial keypoint bootstrapping, Detectron2 for ROI detection, and evaluation restricted to faces and cat heads; whether the approach generalizes to more diverse or deformable object categories remains an open question. The two-stage clustering and pose-guided proxy task, while effective, add complexity that may not be necessary for all object categories.
