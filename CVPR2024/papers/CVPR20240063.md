## Problem

Existing two-stage open-vocabulary segmentation methods suffer from two fundamental misalignments: (1) the segmentation model's proposal embeddings overfit to the training vocabulary and collapse into in-vocabulary prediction, becoming insensitive to novel categories, and (2) the CLIP classifier receives cropped-and-masked sub-images that deviate significantly from its natural-image training distribution, causing domain bias, loss of global context, and erroneous background priors. Additionally, the standard mIoU evaluation metric ignores semantic duplication across categories (e.g., 'chair' vs. 'armchair'), penalizing models for correct but less specific predictions.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to shortest edge 640) and an unbounded text query as input, and produces pixel-level segmentation masks with assigned category labels. Internally, a Mask2Former-based segmentation model generates N class-agnostic mask proposals and corresponding proposal embeddings, while a fine-tuned CLIP (ViT-L/14) performs mask-level classification on cropped sub-images. The final category assignment combines matching scores from both the calibrated proposal embeddings and the contextual-shifted CLIP predictions, enabling recognition of arbitrary text-specified categories beyond the 171 training classes of COCO-Stuff.

## Method

SCAN (Semantic-assisted CAlibration Network) calibrates both the in-vocabulary and domain-biased embedding spaces using CLIP's generalized semantic prior. The Semantic Integration Module (SIM) extracts progressive features from selected CLIP layers (12, 18, 24), applies a low-frequency enhancement via FFT-based Gaussian filtering to suppress texture noise, then injects the enhanced spatial tokens into proposal embeddings through multi-head cross-attention; the final CLIP [CLS] embedding is added with a learnable scaling factor to bridge the visual-linguistic gap before a transformer encoder produces the final aligned embeddings. The Contextual Shift (CS) strategy modifies CLIP's forward pass by randomly replacing 30% of background patch embeddings at shallow layers (1, 3, 5, 7, 9) with the [CLS] token from the original natural image, thereby restoring global context and disrupting the erroneous uniform-background shape prior. The paper also introduces SG-IoU, an evaluation metric that incorporates manually determined hierarchical and synonym relationships between categories, weighted by a balance factor reflecting parent-class accuracy, to better reflect open-vocabulary recognition quality.

## Results

With CLIP ViT-L/14, SCAN achieves 33.5 mIoU on ADE20K-150, 14.0 on ADE20K-847, 59.3 on Pascal Context-59, 16.7 on Pascal Context-459, and 97.2 on Pascal VOC, setting new state-of-the-art on all five benchmarks. With the smaller CLIP ViT-B/14, SCAN reaches 30.8 on ADE20K-150 and 58.4 on PC-59, surpassing the previous best (SAN) by 3.3 and 4.6 points respectively. Under the proposed SG-IoU metric, SCAN further improves to 34.2 on ADE20K-150 and 17.2 on PC-459, maintaining its lead over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAN outperforms all nine baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest CLIP-based competitor SAN [50], SCAN gains +1.4 mIoU on ADE20K-150 (33.5 vs 32.1), +1.6 on ADE20K-847 (14.0 vs 12.4), +1.6 on PC-59 (59.3 vs 57.7), +1.0 on PC-459 (16.7 vs 15.7), and +2.6 on VOC (97.2 vs 94.6) with ViT-L/14. Against ODISE [48], the margin is larger: +3.6 on ADE20K-150 and +2.2 on PC-459. OVSeg [27] and MAFT [21] trail by 3.9 and 4.4 points respectively on ADE20K-150 (ViT-L/14). The ALIGN-based methods (LSeg+ [22], OpenSeg [12]) and GroupViT [47] are substantially weaker, with LSeg+ scoring only 13.0 on ADE20K-150 versus SCAN's 33.5. SimSeg [49], the earliest CLIP-based baseline, is surpassed by 11.8 points on ADE20K-150 (ViT-L/14), illustrating the cumulative gains from domain-adapted CLIP and semantic calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCAN extends the dominant two-stage paradigm for open-vocabulary segmentation (Mask2Former proposals + CLIP classification) by addressing two specific failure modes identified in prior work: in-vocabulary collapse of proposal embeddings and domain bias in CLIP's masked-image inputs. The Semantic Integration Module draws on CLIP's progressive features (spatial tokens with Fourier-based low-frequency enhancement and the final [CLS] embedding) to calibrate the proposal embedding space, while the Contextual Shift strategy builds on OVSeg's observation of domain bias but replaces its finetuning-only remedy with an architectural intervention—injecting [CLS] tokens into background positions during CLIP's forward pass. The paper also departs from the evaluation side by proposing SG-IoU, which reorganizes mIoU to account for semantic hierarchy among categories, a concern absent from prior OVS benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SG-IoU metric is a practical contribution that could reshape how open-vocabulary segmentation is evaluated, since current mIoU penalizes semantically correct but hierarchically imprecise predictions (e.g., 'chair' vs. 'armchair'). The contextual shift technique offers a lightweight, training-free alternative to full CLIP finetuning for domain adaptation, which could be adopted by other mask-level vision-language methods. However, the approach still depends on a two-stage pipeline, requires manual construction of category semantic association matrices for SG-IoU, and the gains, while consistent across benchmarks, are moderate (roughly 1–2 mIoU points over the strongest prior methods). Open questions include whether the calibration strategy generalizes to other vision-language backends beyond CLIP and whether SG-IoU can be automated without manual hierarchy annotation.
