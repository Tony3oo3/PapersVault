## Problem

End-to-end modular AV architectures (UniAD, VAD, OccNet) have achieved strong planning performance, but no systematic analysis exists for which modules are necessary, how inter-module connectivity (sequential, parallel, or hybrid) affects performance, or what information representations should flow between modules. Additionally, prior evaluation protocols for open-loop planning on nuScenes contain significant inconsistencies that distort cross-method comparisons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242384/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a sequence of multi-camera RGB images to construct current and historical BEV feature maps via an R50 backbone with temporal cross-attention, along with high-level driving commands and optionally CAN bus signals (velocity, acceleration, angular velocity) and history trajectories. The planning head outputs a 3-second future trajectory at 2 Hz; the parallel heads produce a 4-channel semantic BEV map (road boundary, lane divider, pedestrian crossing, drivable area), instance-level object bounding boxes with predicted trajectories, and a scene-level BEV occupancy map. Critically, the planning module's sole input is the shared BEV feature map plus the high-level command—no compact outputs or latent queries from other heads are passed to it.

## Method

PARA-Drive is a fully parallel end-to-end AV stack where four modules—online mapping (Panoptic SegFormer), tracking/motion prediction, occupancy prediction, and motion planning—are co-trained simultaneously with no conditional dependencies between them. Each module maintains its own learnable query features that interact with a shared BEV feature map via cross-attention, so information is exchanged implicitly through the co-trained BEV representation rather than through explicit inter-module edges. The key empirical finding is that modules which appear redundant in sequential or hybrid designs (e.g., occupancy vs. motion prediction) become complementary in a parallel configuration, each providing distinct gradient signals that improve the shared BEV features for planning. Post-training, the planning head operates independently, allowing perception and prediction heads to be deactivated at inference for a 2.77× runtime speedup while retaining state-of-the-art planning performance. The paper also introduces a standardized evaluation protocol that fixes false-positive collisions from axis-aligned ego boxes and coarse BEV grids, adds map compliance metrics, and includes targeted scenario evaluation for turning and lane-changing.

## Results

On the nuScenes val set under the paper's standardized evaluation, PARA-Drive achieves an L2 error of 0.5574 m (Ave_all) and a collision rate of 0.17% (Ave_all), outperforming UniAD (0.8317 m, 0.40%) and VAD (0.7830 m, 0.30%). Map compliance errors are also substantially lower: 0.12% off-road and 0.83% off-lane versus UniAD's 0.91%/1.74% and VAD's 1.03%/1.93%. In targeted (turning/lane-change) scenarios, PARA-Drive+ (with ego states) reduces L2 to 0.7018 m and collision rate to 0.05%, compared to AD-MLP's 0.9360 m and 0.94%. The architecture also achieves a 2.77× runtime speedup over UniAD-base by deactivating auxiliary modules at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARA-Drive (without ego states) outperforms UniAD on every metric: L2 Ave_all drops from 0.8317 m to 0.5574 m (−33%), collision Ave_all from 0.40% to 0.17% (−57.5%), and off-lane map compliance from 1.74% to 0.83%. Against VAD, PARA-Drive reduces L2 Ave_all from 0.7830 m to 0.5574 m (−29%) and collision from 0.30% to 0.17%, while also halving map compliance errors. Compared to AD-MLP (which uses ego states but no perception), PARA-Drive matches L2 in the val set (0.5574 vs 0.5568 m) but is dramatically better on map compliance (0.12%/0.83% vs 1.21%/2.45%) and targeted scenarios (L2 0.9082 vs 0.9360 m; collision 0.14% vs 0.94%). When ego states are added (PARA-Drive+), the gap widens further: L2 Ave_all reaches 0.4939 m and targeted-scenario collision drops to 0.05%, the best across all methods. The paper also notes that under the original (non-standardized) evaluation protocols, the relative ranking between UniAD and VAD shifts, underscoring the sensitivity of conclusions to evaluation choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARA-Drive builds directly on the end-to-end modular AV architectures of UniAD, VAD, and OccNet, which introduced sequential or hybrid inter-module connections (e.g., passing lane queries to motion prediction, or occupancy maps to planning). The key departure is the elimination of all such dependencies in favor of a fully parallel design where modules co-train shared BEV features without explicit information passing, informed by a systematic ablation of UniAD's inter-module edges. The paper also positions itself against the multi-task learning literature for AV (Transfuser, NEAT, ReasonNet) by showing that instance-wise motion prediction, scene-level occupancy, and online mapping are all necessary for SOTA planning in a parallelized setting, and it introduces a standardized evaluation protocol that corrects inconsistencies identified across UniAD, VAD, and AD-MLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic design-space exploration provides actionable guidance for future end-to-end AV architecture development, and the standardized evaluation methodology (oriented ego bounding boxes, finer BEV discretization, targeted scenario evaluation, map compliance metrics) addresses a real reproducibility problem that has distorted comparisons in the field. The finding that a fully parallel architecture achieves SOTA while enabling ~3× runtime speedup through module deactivation has direct practical implications for real-time deployment. However, all results are limited to open-loop evaluation on nuScenes; closed-loop performance in simulation or on real vehicles remains an open question, as does whether the parallel-design findings generalize to other datasets, sensor suites, or driving scenarios.
