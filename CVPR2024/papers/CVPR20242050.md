## Problem

Existing NeRF methods for dynamic scenes either explicitly model motion (limited to small, object-centric scenes) or rely on purely statistical outlier detection (e.g., RobustNeRF's fixed-size patch-wise residual analysis), both of which fail in unbounded urban environments where moving objects span diverse categories and scales and backgrounds are geometrically complex. No prior method effectively removes multiple moving objects of varying sizes while faithfully reconstructing intricate static urban backgrounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242050/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 14 perspective images extracted from each 360° frame (with calibrated poses), pre-computed entity segmentation masks from CropFormer, and per-pixel feature maps from frozen SAM and DINOv2 encoders. It produces a binary distractor label D(r) ∈ {0,1} per ray that modulates the photometric loss during NeRF training, ultimately yielding a static NeRF that renders novel views of the background with all moving objects removed. The module is backbone-agnostic and was integrated into both Mip-NeRF 360 and Nerfacto without architectural changes.

## Method

Entity-NeRF introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, Entity-wise Average of Residual Ranks (EARR) replaces RobustNeRF's fixed 8×8 patch statistics with entity-level statistics: each ray's ℓ₂ residual is converted to a normalized rank within the batch, ranks are averaged over all pixels belonging to the same segmented entity (within a 64×64 sampling patch), and entities whose average exceeds a threshold T=0.8 are labeled as distractors (D(r)=0). Second, a cooperative stationary entity classification MLP (three linear layers + one classification layer) takes per-entity feature vectors (mean-pooled SAM and DINOv2 features) and predicts 'stuff' vs. 'thing' labels; 'stuff' entities (buildings, sky, roads) are unconditionally included in training to prevent complex static backgrounds from being erroneously excluded during early high-residual phases. The MLP is initialized via SegFormer pseudo-labels on ADE20K and continuously fine-tuned every 100 NeRF steps using entities confirmed as inliers by EARR, creating a cooperative feedback loop between the statistical and knowledge-based components.

## Results

On the MovieMap urban dataset, Entity-NeRF achieves a foreground PSNR of 20.74 and background PSNR of 25.50 with Mip-NeRF 360, and 19.82 / 24.00 with Nerfacto, substantially outperforming RobustNeRF (20.15 / 22.52 and 17.63 / 21.74 respectively) on both metrics. The overall PSNR for Mip-NeRF 360 + Entity-NeRF reaches 25.23, the highest among all robust methods, while the MSE baseline (which includes moving objects) scores 24.22 overall but only 11.40 foreground PSNR. Entity-NeRF also improves SSIM over RobustNeRF (0.84–0.85 vs. 0.83) and reduces LPIPS (0.14–0.15 vs. 0.18) for Mip-NeRF 360.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RobustNeRF (the primary statistical baseline), Entity-NeRF wins on foreground PSNR for both backbones: +2.19 dB with Nerfacto (19.82 vs. 17.63) and +0.59 dB with Mip-NeRF 360 (20.74 vs. 20.15), and on background PSNR with Mip-NeRF 360 (+2.98 dB, 25.50 vs. 22.52). The MSE baseline (no distractor removal) achieves the highest background PSNR (25.07 / 27.36) but catastrophically fails on foreground PSNR (12.10 / 11.40), confirming that moving objects severely degrade reconstruction. Qualitatively, dynamic NeRF methods D²NeRF and RoDynRF both struggle with the scale variation and clutter of urban scenes, producing blurry or incomplete reconstructions of both moving objects and static backgrounds, whereas Entity-NeRF cleanly removes small moving objects (e.g., pedestrians, bicycles) while preserving complex building facades that RobustNeRF erroneously discards. The stationary entity classification component adds a small but consistent gain over EARR-only (e.g., +0.34 dB foreground PSNR with Nerfacto) and significantly accelerates convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the robust NeRF line of work initiated by RobustNeRF [33], which removes moving objects via patch-wise statistics of reconstruction errors, by replacing fixed-size patches with entity-wise statistics (EARR) derived from a pre-trained entity segmentation network (CropFormer [17]). It further departs from purely statistical methods by incorporating a knowledge-based stationary entity classifier (thing/stuff segmentation via SegFormer [53] on ADE20K [60]) that guarantees inclusion of complex static backgrounds like buildings during early training. The method is designed as a plug-in module compatible with existing unbounded-scene NeRF backbones such as Mip-NeRF 360 [2] and Nerfacto [40], rather than proposing a new radiance field architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: no prior method effectively removes diverse moving objects (pedestrians, vehicles, bicycles) in unbounded urban scenes while preserving complex static backgrounds, a setting critical for applications like autonomous navigation and virtual urban exploration. The MovieMap Dataset, built from 360° video of Akihabara with manual moving-object annotations, provides a new benchmark for this task. Key limitations remain: shadows cast by moving objects are not explicitly handled, and large occluding objects can prevent background reconstruction in occluded regions, pointing toward future integration with inpainting or shadow-aware segmentation.
