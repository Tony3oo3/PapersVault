## Problem

Existing visual prompting methods (VPT, VQT) rely on task-agnostic prompts designed for natural image recognition, which fail to provide the instructive cues needed for complex pathology images where diagnosis depends on specific tissue structures and their quantitative characteristics. This paper addresses the gap by asking: what pathology-specific information should visual prompts encode to guide a foundation model toward clinically meaningful decisions in liver histopathology?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242668/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes H&E-stained liver pathology image tiles (256×256 for classification tasks) or full whole-slide images (~61,000×20,000 pixels for histological scoring) as input. Before feeding the image to the ViT backbone, a segmenter parses it into tissue structures (nuclei and white regions), from which quantitative attributes are extracted. Outputs include class labels (normal/abnormal, or steatosis/inflammation/ballooning/others), ordinal histological scores (0–3 for steatosis and inflammation, 0–2 for ballooning), and interpretability artifacts: attention maps over tissue structures and attribute significance histograms that attribute the diagnosis to specific quantitative features.

## Method

QAP (Quantitative Attribute-based Prompting) replaces task-agnostic prompt tokens with instance-specific visual prompts generated from two pathology-relevant quantitative attributes: (1) K-function-based spatial attributes that measure clustering or dispersion of nuclei relative to white regions (fatty cells/vessels) via Ripley's K-function, and (2) histogram-based morphological attributes capturing area, eccentricity, and perimeter distributions of white regions. An attribute-conditioned prompt generator first vectorizes and projects these attributes, processes them through a transformer encoder with self-attention to model inter-attribute interactions, then uses a transformer decoder to generate prompt tokens conditioned on the encoded attribute representation. These prompts are injected at every layer of a ViT-S/16 backbone (pre-trained self-supervised on TCGA-BRCA), and only the prompt generator and a small classifier are trained, keeping the backbone frozen. The key distinction from VPT/VQT is that prompts are not learned from random initializations but are explicitly conditioned on per-instance quantitative pathology measurements, making them both more informative and interpretable.

## Results

On the NAFLD-Anomaly classification task, QAP achieves 97.75% F1 at 10% training data, outperforming VPT-DEEP (96.58%) and VQT (95.99%), and reaches 99.58% F1 at full data. On the Liver-NAS histological findings recognition task, QAP attains a macro-average F1 of 83.37%, with notable gains on the harder classes: 55.38% on inflammation and 85.10% on ballooning. For histological scoring on unseen WSIs, QAP achieves Spearman correlations of 0.410 (inflammation), 0.332 (ballooning), and 0.793 (steatosis), all with p ≤ 0.001.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QAP outperforms all four baselines on the macro-average F1 for histological findings recognition (83.37% vs. 81.17% VQT, 80.92% SAR, 80.52% VPT-DEEP, 78.28% Fabian et al.), with the largest margins on inflammation (+5.8 over SAR, +9.5 over VPT-DEEP) and ballooning (+0.6 over VPT-DEEP). On the NAFLD-Anomaly classification task, QAP leads at every data ratio, most notably at 10% training where it gains ~1.2% F1 over VPT-DEEP and ~1.8% over VQT. For histological scoring on unseen WSIs, QAP achieves the best Spearman correlation on ballooning (0.332 vs. 0.316 VQT) and steatosis (0.793 vs. 0.766 VQT), while VPT-DEEP narrowly edges QAP on inflammation (0.422 vs. 0.410). Overall, QAP wins or ties on 11 of 12 individual metric comparisons across the three tasks, with the sole loss being a 0.012 gap on inflammation scoring correlation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual prompt tuning paradigm introduced by VPT (Jia et al., ECCV 2022) and its efficient variants (VQT, EXPRES), which inject task-agnostic learnable tokens into ViT layers. The key departure is replacing these generic prompts with task-specific visual prompts conditioned on quantitative pathology attributes—Ripley's K-function for spatial arrangement and histograms for morphological shape—thereby bridging traditional handcrafted histopathological features with modern foundation-model prompting. Conceptually, it repurposes spatial statistics and morphometry (long used in computational pathology) not as direct classification features but as conditioning signals for a transformer-based prompt generator, a novel architectural role for these descriptors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a genuine clinical bottleneck: NAFLD diagnosis relies on labor-intensive manual histological scoring with limited reproducibility, and small-scale pathology datasets make full fine-tuning impractical. The interpretability contribution—attention maps aligned with tissue structures plus attribute significance histograms that mirror pathologist reasoning—could facilitate clinical adoption of AI-assisted diagnosis. However, the evaluation is confined to liver pathology (NAFLD), two tissue structures (nuclei and white regions), a single backbone (ViT-S/16), and two datasets, leaving open whether the attribute-conditioned prompting framework generalizes to other organs, tissue types, or larger foundation models. A key open question is whether the specific choice of K-function and histogram attributes is optimal or whether richer spatial/morphological descriptors would yield further gains.
