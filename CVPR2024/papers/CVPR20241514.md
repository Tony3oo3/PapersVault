## Problem

Existing EVS-assisted video enhancement methods (deblurring, rolling-shutter correction, frame interpolation) treat EVS events as ideal measurements, ignoring sensor nonidealities such as pixel latency (V_FE delay), readout latency, and refractory period. These unmodeled effects cause ghosting, shadowing, and blurry artifacts that are especially severe under low-light conditions with fast motion, and no prior work explicitly incorporates them into the fusion measurement model.

## Input / Output

The method takes inherently blurry rolling-shutter CIS frames at 120–150 FPS and asynchronous EVS events (with in-pixel and off-pixel timestamps, polarity, and pixel coordinates) from a hybrid CIS-EVS sensor where both modalities share the same photodiode. It produces deblurred, rolling-shutter-corrected video frames at up to 10000 FPS. The per-pixel optimization consumes the CIS digital-number measurement and the full EVS event sequence for that pixel, while the row-based reconstruction scheme exploits row-specific CIS exposure start/end times to eliminate rolling-shutter distortion and inter-frame glitches.

## Method

The core contribution is a pixel-wise nonlinear inverse model that explicitly captures three EVS nonidealities: (1) V_FE delay, modeled as a photocurrent-dependent first-order low-pass filter with time constant τ(k) = τ₀ + α/i_pd(k); (2) readout latency, compensated via a local slope approximation of V_FE between in-pixel and off-pixel timestamps; and (3) refractory period, handled as a fixed voltage offset. The resulting system of 2N nonlinear equations in 2N unknowns (photocurrent and V_FE states) is solved per pixel using graph optimization (g2o), parallelized across all pixels with no inter-pixel dependency. A row-based reconstruction scheme fuses two consecutive CIS frames and their events using each row's specific rolling-shutter exposure window, jointly addressing deblurring, RS correction, and interpolation to avoid temporal glitches. A refinement network based on modified NAFNet blocks with spatial attention and a composite loss (L1 + VGG-19 perceptual + Gram) is then applied to suppress residual noise and texture degradation.

## Results

On the simulation dataset, the proposed method achieves an average PSNR of 27.51 dB and LPIPS of 0.16, outperforming all baselines by up to 4 dB in PSNR (e.g., 32.85 dB on 'slingshot egg' vs. 25.19 dB for Time Lens) and 12% in LPIPS. The refinement network contributes an additional ~3.4 dB PSNR gain over the optimization-only pipeline (27.51 vs. 24.09 dB average). On the controlled rotating-disk experiment, the method achieves the lowest Blurred Edge Width (BEW) of 0.7 px at 292 rpm / 1000 lx, indicating the sharpest reconstructed edges among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines (EDI, AKF, Time Lens, FILM, CBMNet, EvUnroll, REFID) in average PSNR (27.51 dB) and LPIPS (0.160) on the simulation dataset, with the largest margins over AKF (+11.06 dB) and EDI (+5.83 dB). However, on the 'running man' scene (slower motion), CBMNet (25.16 dB) and FILM (25.06 dB) slightly exceed the proposed method (24.72 dB) in PSNR, and CBMNet also achieves the best LPIPS (0.069) on that scene. The advantage is most pronounced in fast-motion scenarios: on 'slingshot egg' the method leads the next-best (Time Lens, 25.19 dB) by 7.66 dB, and on 'fan' it leads by 3.22 dB over CBMNet. The CIS-only baseline FILM produces natural-looking, low-noise frames but fails to reconstruct fast objects (e.g., the pellet in 'slingshot egg'), while learning-based EVS methods (Time Lens, CBMNet, REFID) suffer from shadowing/ghosting artifacts because they do not model EVS pixel latency. On the HS-ERGB dataset (different EVS hardware, dual-camera setup), the method does not consistently improve over baselines, which the authors attribute to different sensor characteristics and slower object speeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EVS-assisted video enhancement line (EDI, AKF, Time Lens, CBMNet, EvUnroll, REFID) by explicitly incorporating EVS sensor nonidealities—pixel V_FE latency, readout latency, and refractory period—into the inverse measurement model, which prior methods either ignore or treat as negligible. While EVS latency characterization has been studied in sensor physics literature (Joubert, Posch, Suess), this is the first work to embed those models directly into a joint deblurring + rolling-shutter correction + frame interpolation pipeline solved via per-pixel nonlinear graph optimization. The refinement stage builds on NAFNet's U-Net architecture but adds spatial attention and perceptual/Gram losses to target motion-localized noise and texture preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap for indoor and low-light video capture where EVS pixel latency causes ghosting and shadowing artifacts that neither double-integral methods nor learned pipelines handle. The row-based joint reconstruction scheme that simultaneously corrects rolling shutter and avoids inter-frame glitches is a useful engineering contribution for hybrid CIS-EVS sensor deployment. However, the authors acknowledge that gains diminish for slow objects or bright scenes, and the method's performance degrades on the HS-ERGB dataset (different EVS, dual-camera setup), raising open questions about generalizability across sensor designs and calibration protocols. Whether the explicit nonideality modeling becomes standard practice as hybrid sensors proliferate remains to be seen.
