## Problem

Most existing SPD neural networks rely on Euclidean classifiers (tangent-space, ambient-space, or coordinate-based) that distort the intrinsic geometry of SPD manifolds, while the gyro-based SPD MLRs of Nguyen & Yang [42] depend on gyro-structures that limit generality and use pseudo-gyrodistances rather than true geodesic distances. This paper asks whether a unified, intrinsic SPD classification layer can be derived directly from the Riemannian metric without gyro machinery, and whether parameterizing the metric can further improve performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes SPD covariance matrices of varying dimensions (20×20 for radar, 93×93 for skeleton action, 40×40 for EEG) produced by upstream SPD network layers (SPDNet or TSMNet+SPDDSMBN) and outputs a C-way class probability vector via softmax. Each class k is parameterized by an SPD shift matrix P_k ∈ Sⁿ₊₊ and a symmetric normal matrix Ã_k ∈ Sⁿ, so the classifier is architecture-agnostic and simply replaces the final LogEig+FC+softmax head. Training uses Riemannian AMSGrad for the SPD parameters and Euclidean SGD for the symmetric matrix parameters, with the standard cross-entropy loss.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified framework for SPD Multinomial Logistic Regression under any Pullback Euclidean Metric (PEM). The key insight is that under a PEM, the margin distance from an SPD matrix to an SPD hyperplane admits a closed-form expression (Lemma 3.5), which collapses the full SPD MLR definition into a simple Frobenius inner product in the pulled-back Euclidean space (Theorem 3.8): p(y=k|S) ∝ exp(⟨φ(S)−φ(P_k), φ_{*,I}(Ã_k)⟩). The framework is instantiated under two parameterized metric families—(α,β)-LEM with O(n)-invariant inner product and (θ)-LCM with power deformation—yielding explicit classification formulas (Corollary 4.1). The framework also provides an intrinsic geometric explanation for the widely used LogEig classifier (Proposition 5.1), showing it is equivalent to the LEM-based SPD MLR when optimized by LEM-based RSGD. Experiments on radar, skeleton action, and EEG benchmarks demonstrate consistent gains over LogEig and gyro-AIM baselines, with the deformed LCM achieving the largest improvements and reduced variance.

## Results

On the Radar dataset (5-layer SPDNet), the best proposed classifier (1,1)-LEM achieves 95.87% accuracy, outperforming the LogEig MLR baseline (93.47%) and the Gyro-AIM baseline (94.32%). On HDM05 (3-layer SPDNet), (0.5)-LCM reaches 65.79% accuracy, a ~5% absolute gain over LogEig MLR (60.76%) and ~4.6% over Gyro-AIM (61.14%). On the Hinss2021 EEG dataset (inter-session), (1.5)-LCM achieves 56.43% balanced accuracy, surpassing LogEig MLR (53.83%) and Gyro-AIM (53.36%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LogEig MLR baseline (matrix logarithm + FC + softmax from SPDNet [29]), the proposed SPD MLRs consistently win across all three datasets: (1,1)-LEM gains +2.4% on Radar, (0.5)-LCM gains +5.0% on HDM05, and (1.5)-LCM gains +2.6% on Hinss2021 inter-session. Against the Gyro-AIM SPD MLR [42], the proposed methods also prevail: (1,1)-LEM beats Gyro-AIM by +1.55% on Radar (5-layer), (0.5)-LCM beats it by +4.65% on HDM05 (3-layer), and (1.5)-LCM beats it by +3.07% on Hinss2021 inter-session. Notably, the standard (1,0)-LEM and (1)-LCM variants are competitive with or slightly below Gyro-AIM on Radar, but the parameterized (deformed) metrics consistently surpass both baselines. The paper also reports that LEM- and LCM-based SPD MLRs are roughly 9× faster per epoch than Gyro-AIM on HDM05 (117 classes), due to the simpler PEM computations versus AIM's heavier matrix operations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Euclidean MLR reformulation via margin hyperplanes (Lebanon & Lafferty, 2004) and its hyperbolic generalization (Ganea et al., 2018) to SPD manifolds, but departs from the gyro-structure approach of Nguyen & Yang [42] by deriving SPD MLR directly through Riemannian geometry under Pullback Euclidean Metrics (PEMs). The theoretical foundation rests on the PEM framework of Chen et al. [13] and the parameterized (α,β)-LEM and (θ)-LCM metrics of Chen et al. [15], which the paper shows are all PEMs, enabling a single closed-form expression (Thm. 3.8) that subsumes the gyro SPD MLRs as special cases. A notable conceptual departure is the intrinsic re-interpretation of the widely-used LogEig classifier (matrix log + FC + softmax) as an LEM-based SPD MLR under specific optimization conditions (Prop. 5.1), replacing the prior tangent-space approximation explanation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified PEM framework gives SPD network practitioners a principled, architecture-agnostic classifier that is both theoretically grounded and computationally efficient (9× faster than AIM-based gyro MLR on 117-class HDM05), with the parameterized metrics offering tunable geometric flexibility. The intrinsic explanation of LogEig resolves a long-standing ambiguity in the SPD network literature and suggests that the deformed LCM metric captures data geometry more faithfully than standard metrics. Key limitations include restriction to PEMs (AIM is excluded due to lack of closed-form margin distance) and computational burden when applying Riemannian optimization to high-dimensional SPD features in Euclidean backbones, both flagged as open problems for future work.
