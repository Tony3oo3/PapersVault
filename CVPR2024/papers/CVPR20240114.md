## Problem

While DNNs capture coarse-to-fine visual information, they lack a structured understanding of scenes as hierarchical compositions of visual elements. Existing hierarchy-aware ViTs define parent-child relations through symmetric cosine similarity in Euclidean space, which cannot represent the asymmetric inclusion property of hierarchical structures and distorts semantic distances due to flat geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240114/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Hi-Mapper is a plug-and-play module that operates on top of any pre-trained DNN backbone (ResNet, DeiT, Swin, EfficientNet). It consumes the penultimate visual feature map (hw×d) and global visual representation (d) extracted from the backbone, and produces an enhanced global representation that is fed into the pre-trained classifier or dense prediction head (Mask R-CNN, UperNet). No part-level or hierarchy annotations are required; the hierarchy is discovered entirely from the learned feature space.

## Method

Hi-Mapper pre-defines an L-level hierarchy tree where leaf nodes are parameterized as Gaussian distributions and higher-level nodes as Mixture-of-Gaussians of their children, capturing asymmetric inclusion via probability density overlap. A hierarchy decomposition module (stacked transformer decoders) uses these probabilistic nodes as queries to aggregate semantically-relevant features from the visual feature map into L levels of visual hierarchy. The identified hierarchy is then mapped to the Lorentz hyperboloid via the exponential map, and a novel hierarchical contrastive loss pulls parent-child nodes together while pushing same-level nodes apart in hyperbolic space, exploiting its exponential volume growth to faithfully represent the tree structure. Finally, a hierarchy encoding module (L stacked transformer decoders) progressively aggregates the Euclidean-space hierarchy levels into the global representation, which is combined with the original via residual addition before classification.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, Hi-Mapper consistently improves over its backbone by 1.1–2.8% top-1 accuracy with only a marginal parameter increase (e.g., DeiT-S: 79.8% → 82.6%, Swin-T: 81.2% → 83.4%). On COCO object detection, Hi-Mapper(DeiT-T) boosts AP^box from 30.3 to 37.1 (+6.8) and AP^mask from 27.1 to 35.7 (+8.6). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, Hi-Mapper(DeiT-S) achieves 46.3 mIoU vs. 43.0 for DeiT-S (+3.3), and Hi-Mapper(Swin-T) reaches 46.8 mIoU vs. 44.5 for Swin-T (+2.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ResNet-50, Hi-Mapper(RN50) gains +2.0% top-1 (78.2 vs. 76.2) with only 1.3M extra parameters. Against EfficientNet-B4*, Hi-Mapper(ENB4*) improves by +1.2% (84.1 vs. 82.9). Among ViT baselines, Hi-Mapper(DeiT-S) outperforms DeiT-S by +2.8% (82.6 vs. 79.8), CrossViT-S by +1.6% (82.6 vs. 81.0), and DependencyViT-S by +0.5% (82.6 vs. 82.1); however, at the tiny scale, Hi-Mapper(DeiT-T) at 74.8% trails DependencyViT-T (75.4%) by 0.6%. For Swin backbones, Hi-Mapper(Swin-T) and (Swin-S) improve by +2.2% and +1.1% respectively. On COCO detection, the gains are most pronounced for DeiT backbones (+6.8 AP^box for DeiT-T, +6.3 for DeiT-S), while Swin-T sees a smaller +0.5 boost. On ADE20K segmentation, Hi-Mapper(DeiT-S) surpasses DependencyViT-S by +0.6 mIoU (46.3 vs. 45.7) and DeiT-S by +3.3 mIoU, confirming consistent gains across dense prediction tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hi-Mapper departs from recent hierarchy-aware ViTs (CrossViT, Quadtree, DependencyViT) that define parent-child relations through symmetric cosine similarity in Euclidean space, arguing that such measurements cannot capture the asymmetric inclusion property of hierarchical structure. It draws on two complementary lines of work: probabilistic modeling (Gaussian embeddings, MoG) to parameterize hierarchy nodes with asymmetric density-inclusion semantics, and hyperbolic geometry (Lorentz model) to represent the exponential growth of nodes at increasing depth. The mapping procedure follows Desai et al. [29] for the Lorentz exponential map, and the KL regularization follows Chun et al. [50], but the combination of probabilistic tree construction with a novel hierarchical contrastive loss in hyperbolic space is new. Unlike prior hyperbolic vision works that learn categorical hierarchies from class labels, Hi-Mapper identifies a visual-element hierarchy without any part-level annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hi-Mapper offers a practical plug-and-play module that consistently improves diverse pre-trained backbones (ResNet, DeiT, Swin, EfficientNet) across classification, detection, and segmentation without modifying the underlying architecture, making it broadly applicable. The ablation showing that the hierarchical contrastive loss actively degrades performance in Euclidean space but improves it in hyperbolic space provides a concrete justification for the geometric choice. Open questions include whether the fixed binary tree structure (pre-defined width and depth) is optimal for all scenes, whether the approach extends to unsupervised or self-supervised pre-training, and whether the benefits compound when combined with other structured-attention mechanisms. The reliance on a pre-trained encoder also limits its applicability to settings where no strong backbone is available.
