## Problem

All-in-one image restoration methods struggle to simultaneously achieve adaptiveness to diverse and mixed degradations, generalizability to training-unseen tasks, and fidelity in preserving fine details. While Stable Diffusion offers powerful generative priors, its direct application to restoration is limited by the difficulty of crafting suitable prompts for complex real-world degradations and by the high VAE compression that discards fine-grained details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240111/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (processed in 512×512 patches) and produces a restored high-quality RGB image. The input is consumed through two parallel pathways: the CLIP image encoder extracts degradation-aware semantic features, while the SD VAE encoder projects the image into a 4-channel latent embedding. The output is generated by the SD U-Net in latent space, decoded by the VAE, and further refined by the DRM's encoder-to-decoder skip connection to recover small-object details lost during latent compression.

## Method

MPerceiver builds on Stable Diffusion 2.1 and introduces a dual-branch multimodal prompt learning framework. The textual branch uses a Cross-Modal Adapter (CM-Adapter) that performs cross-modal inversion from CLIP image embeddings to degradation-specific text vectors via parallel self-attention layers, which are combined with a learnable textual prompt pool and dynamically weighted by degradation probabilities from a lightweight predictor (trained with focal loss) to form the SD text condition. The visual branch uses an Image Restoration Adapter (IR-Adapter) that decomposes VAE latent embeddings into four multiscale features, each modulated by a visual prompt pool through multi-head cross-attention and injected into the U-Net via AdaIN. A plug-in Detail Refinement Module (DRM) provides a direct encoder-to-decoder skip connection using ResBlocks and SwinBlocks to recover fine details lost by VAE compression, trained with combined L1, color, perceptual, and adversarial losses. The dynamic weighting mechanism treats unseen or mixed degradations as probabilistic combinations of known training degradations, enabling zero-shot and few-shot generalization.

## Results

MPerceiver+ (with self-ensemble) achieves 33.69/0.940 PSNR/SSIM on deraining (Rain1400), 21.08/0.651 on dehazing (average), 31.11/0.918 on desnowing (Snow100K-L), and 40.05/0.960 on real denoising (SIDD), outperforming all all-in-one baselines and matching or exceeding task-specific SOTA on most tasks. On the proposed MID6 mixed-degradation benchmark, MPerceiver achieves 26.19/0.782 PSNR/SSIM on Haze&Noise&Blur, surpassing the best all-in-one baseline TransWeather (25.11/0.739) by over 1 dB. In zero-shot settings, MPerceiver reaches 32.92 PSNR on TOLED (vs. 27.90 for NAFNet_A) and 22.69 PSNR on UIEB underwater IR (vs. 17.34 for Restormer_A), demonstrating strong generalization to unseen degradations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPerceiver+ consistently outperforms all all-in-one baselines (AirNet, TransWeather, WGWS-Net, PromptIR, DA-CLIP, Restormer_A, NAFNet_A) across every evaluated task. The most striking margin is on dehazing, where MPerceiver+ achieves 21.08 PSNR versus 18.64 for the best task-specific method DeHamer and 16.97 for the best all-in-one PromptIR — a gap of over 2 dB. On deraining, MPerceiver+ (33.69 PSNR) marginally surpasses task-specific Restormer (33.68) and DRSformer (33.66), while on real denoising (SIDD) it edges out Restormer (40.05 vs. 40.02 PSNR). MPerceiver+ trails task-specific FocalNet by 0.12 dB on defocus deblurring (26.06 vs. 26.18 PSNR) and Xformer by 0.22 dB on gaussian denoising (29.61 vs. 29.83 PSNR), indicating slight trade-offs on these two tasks. On the MID6 mixed-degradation benchmark, MPerceiver dominates all all-in-one methods by 1–10 dB (e.g., 26.19 vs. 25.11 PSNR on Haze&Noise&Blur vs. TransWeather; 28.49 vs. 27.76 on Raindrop&Noise&Blur vs. TransWeather), confirming its advantage in handling compound real-world degradations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture and CLIP's joint image-text representation space, repurposing both as frozen backbones for a novel dual-branch prompt learning framework. It departs from prior all-in-one IR methods (AirNet, TransWeather, WGWS-Net) that rely on contrastive learning or task-specific subnetworks, and from prior diffusion-based IR approaches that either operate zero-shot or train conditional diffusion from scratch. The key conceptual departure is treating degradation handling as a dynamic prompt selection problem: a lightweight degradation predictor modulates both textual (via CM-Adapter cross-modal inversion) and visual (via IR-Adapter multiscale decomposition) prompts, enabling the model to compose known degradation priors for unseen or mixed degradations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single pretrained model can handle 16 diverse IR tasks including challenging mixed degradations, with zero-shot transfer to entirely unseen degradation types (under-display camera, underwater) and few-shot adaptation matching task-specific methods using only 3–5% of training data. The MID6 mixed-degradation benchmark addresses a practical gap in real-world IR evaluation. Open questions include inference efficiency (50 DDIM steps on a frozen SD 2.1 backbone limits deployment), whether the prompt-based composition generalizes to truly novel degradation physics beyond probabilistic mixtures of training degradations, and whether the approach scales to video or higher-resolution restoration without prohibitive compute.
