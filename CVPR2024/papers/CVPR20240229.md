## Problem

High-quality text-to-image models are typically trained on web-scraped datasets like LAION-2B, whose copyright status is legally unsettled and whose URL-only format suffers from link rot. Creative-Commons-licensed images offer a legally clean alternative, but they face two obstacles: they almost never come with the captions needed for T2I training, and only roughly 70 million high-resolution CC images exist—far fewer than the billions in LAION. The paper asks whether a competitive T2I model can be trained on CC images alone, and if so, how much data is actually required.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a free-form text prompt (encoded via OpenCLIP) and produces a 512×512 RGB image through iterative latent diffusion sampling. For training, images are sourced at native resolution (often exceeding 4K) from Flickr via YFCC100M metadata, center-cropped and resized to 512×512 for captioning by BLIP-2, then used at high resolution during diffusion training. The synthetic captions are short natural-language descriptions (typically one sentence) generated by a frozen BLIP-2 OPT-2.5B model, replacing the ground-truth alt-text that CC images rarely provide.

## Method

The paper introduces 'telephoning,' a transfer-learning technique where a pre-trained image-to-text model (BLIP-2) performs a lossy compression of images into short synthetic captions, which then serve as training labels for a separate text-to-image diffusion model. The resulting CommonCatalog dataset (~70M CC images with BLIP-2 captions) is used to train two latent diffusion architectures: CommonCanvas-S (SD2's 865M-parameter UNet) and CommonCanvas-L (SDXL's 2.6B-parameter UNet), both with a VAE for latent-space encoding and OpenCLIP as the text encoder. A key empirical finding is that SD2-quality performance is achievable with as few as 10M–90M training images (~3% of LAION-2B), suggesting the architecture is underparameterized relative to available data. Training is accelerated 2.71× via Flash Attention, pre-computed VAE/text-encoder latents, float16 normalization layers, fully-sharded data parallelism, and reduced EMA computation, enabling the data-scarcity ablations across 1.1B, 90M, 10M, and 1M subsets.

## Results

On the human preference study using Parti Prompts, CommonCanvas-S-C achieves a 37% preference rate and CommonCanvas-S-NC achieves 38% relative to public SD2, while the larger CommonCanvas-L-NC (using SDXL's U-Net) reaches statistically equivalent performance to SD2 (~50%). In the data-scarcity analysis, SD2 models trained on only 90M or 10M LAION samples show no significant degradation in FID or KID on MS COCO compared to the full 1.1B dataset, with performance only dropping at 1M samples. The training pipeline achieves a 2.71× speed-up over the SD2 baseline, and synthetic BLIP-2 captions yield comparable or slightly better CLIP-FID than original LAION captions on MS COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is public Stable Diffusion 2 (SD2), trained on LAION-2B. On the human preference study (Parti Prompts), the smaller CommonCanvas models (S-C at 37%, S-NC at 38%) are slightly less preferred than SD2, which the authors note is surprisingly competitive given they use <3% of the training data with synthetic captions. The larger CommonCanvas-L-NC, which swaps in SDXL's 2.6B-parameter U-Net, achieves statistically equivalent user preference to SD2, effectively matching the baseline. On automated metrics (FID, KID on MS COCO), SD2-90M trained on 90M LAION samples performs comparably to the full 1.1B model, and BLIP-2 synthetic captions produce nearly identical FID/KID to original LAION captions, with slightly better CLIP-FID. However, CommonCanvas underperforms SD2 in specific categories such as faces, general photography, and paintings (evaluated via Conceptual Captions), where web-scraped training data provides an advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stable Diffusion 2 latent diffusion architecture (and SDXL's larger UNet for the CommonCanvas-L variant) and on BLIP-2 as a pre-trained image-to-text model, repurposing it to generate synthetic captions for an unlabeled CC-image corpus. It departs from the dominant paradigm of training T2I models on web-scraped LAION data by constructing an entirely Creative-Commons-licensed training set (CommonCatalog) and coining the term 'telephoning' to describe the increasingly common pattern of using one generative model to produce training labels for another. The data-scarcity analysis—training SD2 on progressively smaller LAION subsets—extends prior work that had not systematically quantified how much data is actually needed, while the 2.71× training speedup (Flash Attention, pre-computed latents, FSDP, mixed-precision norms) is a systems contribution orthogonal to the modeling question.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that roughly 70 million CC-licensed images with synthetic BLIP-2 captions suffice to train a T2I model competitive with SD2, directly addressing the copyright and reproducibility concerns that plague LAION-based training. The finding that SD2 performance does not degrade until training on as few as 1 million images suggests the architecture may be underparameterized, opening questions about how much data larger models (e.g., SDXL-scale) actually require. Key limitations include the ~decade-old YFCC100M source (less current than LAION), underperformance on faces, photography, and paintings, and the fact that only the largest CommonCanvas-L-NC model achieves statistical parity with SD2 on human evaluation. Future work could augment CommonCatalog with newer CC sources, test more advanced captioners like LLaVA, and investigate whether the data-efficiency finding generalizes to larger architectures.
