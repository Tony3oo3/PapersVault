## Problem

Existing diffusion models for LiDAR scene generation operate directly in point or range-image space, causing them to expendhaust most of their representation capacity on low-level geometry rather than high-level scene semantics, resulting in noisy backgrounds and ambiguous objects. Moreover, no prior method supported conditional LiDAR generation from diverse modalities such as semantic maps, camera views, or text prompts, leaving a critical gap for controllable simulation in autonomous driving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241185/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR scenes as 2D range images (32×1024 for 32-beam nuScenes, 64×1024 for 64-beam KITTI-360) with pixel values encodeded via binary logarithm and scaling, ensuring lossless conversion to and from raw point clouds. Conditioning inputs are handled in two distinct ways: image-based conditions (semantic maps) are concatenated channel-wise as an image-to-image translation, while token-based conditions (bounding boxes, CLIP-encoded camera views, or text prompts) are injected via cross-attention. The output is a denoised latent code decoded back into a full-resolution range image, which maps directly to a 3D point cloud.

## Method

LiDM introduces a curve-aware autoencoder that compresses range images into a compact latent space (16×128 for 64-beam) through three co-designed mechanisms: curve-wise compression using 1×4 convolutional kernels with horizontal-only downsampling and circular padding to preserve LiDAR scan-line polyline patterns; point-wise coordinate supervision imposing both a pixelwise 3D distance loss and an adversarial objective on derived (α,β,γ) coordinate images to anchor scene-level geometry; and patch-wise encoding with an additional isotropic downsampling stage to enlargelarge the receptive field for visually large objects near the ego-center. The diffusion process then operates in this latent space with a standard UNet denoiser, trained with L1 reconstruction, a CurveGAN adversarial loss, and vector-quantization regularization for the autoencoder, and a standard εpsilon-matching objective for the diffusion. This architecture yields a ×107 throughput speedup over point-based LiDARGen while achieving state-of-the-art conditional generation quality.

## Results

On 64-beam KITTI-360 unconditional generation, LiDM with only 50 diffusion steps achieves FRID=158.8, FSVD=53.7, and EPPDV=42.7, improving 20.4–31.0% over the LDM baseline at the same step count and matching LiDARGen's quality (which requires 1160 steps) in just E0 steps. On conditional Semantic-Map-to-LiDAR, LiDM reaches ERID=22.9 and EPPDV=17.7, outperforming both LDM (EID=24.0) and LiDARGen (EID=42.5). Inference throughput is 1.603 samples/sec versus 0.015 for LiDARGen, a ×107 speedup on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LiDARGen [E2], the prior point-based SOTA, LiDM with E0 steps achieves comparable perceptual quality to LiDARGen's full 1160-step process (EID 158.8 vs 129.1) while running ×107 faster in throughput. On conditional generation, LiDM decisively wins: on Semantic-Map-to-LiDAR it scores EID=22.9 versus LiDARGen's 42.5, and on Camera-to-LiDAR LiDARGen is simply not viable. Against the EDM [44] baseline at the same E0-step budget, LiDM improves all three perceptual metrics by 20–31% and both statistical metrics by 10–12%, confirming that the curve-wise compression, coordinate supervision, and patch-wise encoding modules collectively close the gap. The older LiDARGAN/VAE [E] baselines are far behind on every metric (EID >199 vs LiDM's 158.8), underscoring the generational leap from GAN/VAE to latent diffusion for LiDAR scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiDM adapts the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) paradigm—autoencoder compression followed by diffusion in latent space—from 2D images to LiDAR range images, introducing three domain-specific modifications: curve-wise compression (inspired by CurveCloudNet's 1D structure processing), point-wise coordinate supervision for 3D geometry, and patch-wise encoding for object context. It departs from the prior SOTA LiDARGen, which applies diffusion directly in point/range-image space without compression, by showing that a tailored latent space both improves quality and enables multi-modal conditioning. The adversarial component (CurveGAN) is a curve-adapted variant of PatchGAN, and the conditioning mechanism follows the cross-attention pattern established in LDM and ControlNet, extended here to CLIP-based camera and text features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to unify conditional LiDAR scene generation from heterogeneous inputs (semantic maps, camera views, text prompts), opening a path toward language-driven simulation for autonomous driving. The 107× throughput improvement over point-based diffusion makes iterative generation and downstream integration (e.g., data augmentation for detectors) practically feasible. However, the authors acknowledge a persistent visual gap to real LiDAR, attributing it to autoencoder limitations in reconstructing sharp object boundaries—suggesting that better perceptual spaces, possibly with stronger geometric priors or larger-scale training, remain an open challenge. Scaling to higher beam counts, more complex urban scenes, and richer text prompts are natural next steps.
