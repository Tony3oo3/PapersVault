## Problem

Existing pruning metrics (magnitude, loss-awareness) designed for single-modality transformers produce suboptimal results when applied to multimodal CLIP models, and mask-based pruning methods are too computationally expensive for pre-training stage compression. No unified, mask-free structured pruning solution exists that works effectively across both pre-training and fine-tuning compression stages for vision-language models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242409/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained CLIP model (ViT-L/14 with 304M vision + 85M text parameters) and produces compressed encoders at flexible target sizes (e.g., 86M vision, 42M text) by pruning attention heads, FFN neuron groups, and Transformer layers. The MoPE metric is computed by measuring zero-shot retrieval Recall Mean on the MSCOCO validation set, and the pruned models are retrained on image-text pairs (CC3M, CC12M, or YFCC15M) using contrastive and distillation losses. The compressed models output image-text similarity scores for cross-modal retrieval or zero-shot class predictions, with the vision encoder compressed for image-to-text retrieval and the text encoder for text-to-image retrieval.

## Method

The core contribution is the Module-wise Pruning Error (MoPE) metric, which quantifies a module's importance by measuring the performance drop in cross-modal retrieval tasks when that specific module (attention head, FFN neuron group, or Transformer layer) is removed from the full CLIP model. Based on MoPE cost tables, a unified mask-free pruning framework is applied: during fine-tuning, width-first-then-depth pruning sequentially removes heads and neuron groups before pruning layers; during pre-training, width and depth are pruned simultaneously in a single stage to reduce retraining cost. A composite distillation loss transfers both cross-modal knowledge (soft cross-entropy on the similarity matrix) and uni-modal knowledge (MSE on final features and intermediate hidden states) from the frozen teacher CLIP to the pruned student, with the contrastive InfoNCE loss retained as the primary training objective.

## Results

On zero-shot MSCOCO retrieval, MoPE-CLIP_base (86M+42M params, pre-trained on CC3M) achieves 52.8% TR@1 and 37.3% IR@1, surpassing CLIP-ViT-B/32 (88M+38M, pre-trained on WIT-400M) by 2.7% TR@1 and 6.8% IR@1. On zero-shot classification across 11 benchmarks, MoPE-CLIP_base pre-trained on YFCC15M reaches 58.8% average top-1 accuracy, outperforming ALIP (41.8%) and MCD (40.2%) by 17.0% and 18.6% respectively. In fine-tuning stage compression, MoPE-CLIP_V at 86M vision parameters achieves 69.7% TR@1 on MSCOCO, beating MagnCLIP_V at 115M (64.2%) and DynaCLIP_V at 86M (67.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoPE-CLIP_base pre-trained on CC3M (86M+42M) surpasses CLIP-ViT-B/32 pre-trained on WIT-400M (88M+38M) by 2.7% TR@1 and 6.8% IR@1 on MSCOCO, despite using a far smaller pre-training corpus. Against TinyCLIP (same 152M+42M size, CC3M), MoPE-CLIP_large improves TR@1 by 5.3% and IR@1 by 4.0%. In fine-tuning stage compression, MoPE-CLIP_V at 86M vision params (69.7% TR@1) outperforms MagnCLIP_V at 115M (64.2%) and DynaCLIP_V at 86M (67.6%), and beats UPop-CLIP (474M total params, 70.8% TR@1) while using only 234M total parameters. On zero-shot classification, MoPE-CLIP_base (YFCC15M, 20 epochs) achieves 58.8% average, exceeding ALIP (41.8%, 32 epochs) by 17.0% and MCD (40.2%, 32 epochs) by 18.6%, while also requiring fewer training epochs. The width-first-then-depth pruning strategy consistently outperforms depth-first and simultaneous width-and-depth approaches in the fine-tuning setting (69.7% vs. 64.0% and 61.4% TR@1 at 86M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends structured pruning from uni-modal transformers (BERT, ViT) to the cross-modal CLIP architecture, departing from the assumption that magnitude-based or loss-aware metrics transfer directly. It adapts DynaBERT's neuron rewiring for FFN group evaluation and TinyBERT's feature-level distillation, but introduces the MoPE metric—a cross-modal task-performance-drop measure—as the core pruning criterion, replacing uni-modal importance signals. The unified framework bridges two previously separate compression settings (pre-training-stage and fine-tuning-stage), whereas prior VLP compression methods like UPop and TinyCLIP each target only one stage and rely on costly mask-search or multi-stage distillation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoPE-CLIP offers a practical, mask-free recipe for producing compact CLIP models at arbitrary target sizes, which is directly relevant for deploying vision-language models on edge devices and mobile clients. The finding that a simple zero-shot retrieval performance drop outperforms gradient-based and loss-aware layer-selection strategies for CLIP depth pruning challenges the default 'every other' heuristic inherited from BERT. However, the evaluation is confined to the CLIP ViT-L/14 → ViT-B/32 scale and MSCOCO/Flickr30K retrieval; whether MoPE generalizes to newer VLP architectures (e.g., BLIP-2, LLaVA) or to non-retrieval cross-modal tasks (VQA, grounding) remains open. The large margins over ALIP and MCD on zero-shot classification partly reflect different pre-training data scales (CC3M/CC12M vs YFCC15M), so a controlled same-data comparison would strengthen the claim.
