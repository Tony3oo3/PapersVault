## Problem

Existing single-image dehazing methods either rely on the atmospheric scattering model (requiring accurate transmission map and atmospheric light estimation) or operate as end-to-end networks without leveraging the physical relationship between scene depth and haze formation. While depth is directly linked to haze via T(x) = e^{-βd(x)}, prior depth-assisted approaches treat depth as a one-way auxiliary signal and fail to establish a genuine mutual promotion loop where depth estimation and dehazing each improve the other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241373/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hazy RGB image (256×256 patches during training) and produces both a dehazed clear image and a depth map of the hazy scene. Unusually, the depth map of the hazy image itself is fed back into the dehazing encoder as an auxiliary feature channel, and the depth estimation network simultaneously processes both the original hazy image and the network's own dehazed output, creating a closed-loop information flow between the two tasks.

## Method

The proposed DCMPNet integrates a CNN-based dehazing network (U-Net encoder with LEGM, MSAAM, and MFM modules) and a DRDB-based depth estimation network (DE) into a unified framework via a difference perception (DP) mechanism. The DP computes two cross-task signals: (1) the pixel-wise difference between depth maps of the dehazed image and ground truth, which is converted into an attention coefficient that reweights the dehazing loss to emphasize sub-optimal regions; and (2) the pixel-wise difference between the dehazed image and ground truth, which reweights the depth loss to make DE focus on residual-haze areas. The two networks are trained alternately with coupled losses (Eqs. 10–12) where each network's gradient update explicitly depends on the other's current output, preventing either from converging to a non-ideal local optimum. The dehazing loss additionally incorporates a VGG19 perceptual term, and the depth GT is generated by a pre-trained RA-Depth model.

## Results

On SOTS-indoor, the proposed method achieves the best SSIM (0.9967), NIQE (4.2403), PIQE (31.3046), and FADE (0.4907), while obtaining a competitive PSNR of 42.18 dB (vs. 42.63 for MB-TaylorFormer). On SOTS-outdoor, it similarly leads in SSIM (0.9931), NIQE (2.9377), PIQE (7.6498), and FADE (0.7112), with a PSNR of 36.56 dB. The method thus achieves the best perceptual quality metrics across both synthetic test sets while remaining within ~0.5 dB of the top PSNR scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on SSIM, NIQE, PIQE, and FADE across both SOTS-indoor and SOTS-outdoor. On PSNR, it trails MB-TaylorFormer [31] by 0.45 dB (42.18 vs. 42.63) on indoor and 1.53 dB (36.56 vs. 38.09) on outdoor, and C2PNet [52] by 0.38 dB (42.18 vs. 42.56) on indoor and 0.12 dB (36.56 vs. 36.68) on outdoor. Against the remaining baselines (AECR-Net, PSD, MAXIM-2S, Dehamer, D4, Dehazeformer, MITNet), the proposed method leads on every reported metric, with particularly large margins over PSD (≈30 dB PSNR gap) and D4 (≈17 dB PSNR gap). The closest overall competitor is MB-TaylorFormer, which achieves the highest PSNR but is surpassed by the proposed method on all four perceptual/no-reference quality metrics, indicating better structural fidelity and lower residual haze.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the depth-assisted dehazing line initiated by Wang et al. [43] (SelfPromer), which used depth consistency as a one-way constraint on the dehazing network. The key departure is reframing depth estimation and dehazing as two independent tasks that mutually promote each other through a difference perception mechanism, rather than depth serving as a static auxiliary signal. The dehazing backbone draws on standard U-Net encoder-decoder architectures with attention modules (LEGM, MSAAM, MFM), while the depth estimation branch leverages RA-Depth [11] as a pretrained component. The loss design combines L1 pixel loss with a VGG-based perceptual term, following conventions established by AECR-Net [44] and C2PNet [52].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mutual promotion framework offers a principled way to exploit the physical link between scene depth and haze (via the transmission map) without explicitly modeling the atmospheric scattering parameters, which is a reasonable middle ground between model-based and purely end-to-end approaches. However, the performance gains over strong baselines like C2PNet and MB-TaylorFormer are modest (the proposed method does not achieve the best PSNR on either SOTS split), and the added complexity of maintaining two jointly trained networks with a difference perception bridge raises questions about practical deployment. Open questions include whether the mutual promotion mechanism generalizes to other degradation types (rain, fog at varying densities) and whether the depth estimation quality on heavily hazy images is sufficient to provide meaningful guidance.
