## Problem

Conventional class-incremental learning (CIL) assumes balanced class distributions, but real-world data is long-tailed, introducing a dual imbalance: intra-phase (within each task, head classes dominate) and inter-phase (between stored exemplars of old tasks and new task data). This dual imbalance causes skewed gradient magnitudes in fully-connected layers, leading to over-fitting on head classes, under-fitting on tail classes, and an imbalanced form of catastrophic forgetting where instance-rich classes paradoxically suffer greater performance degradation as more of their training data becomes unavailable in subsequent phases.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNetSubset-LT and Food101-LT) and outputs a single class label from a unified softmax classifier over all classes seen so far, without task identifiers at inference. Training data is long-tailed with imbalance factor ρ=100 (n_max/n_min), while test sets remain class-balanced. The model uses ResNet-32 or ResNet-18 as feature extractors with a growing exemplar memory (Herding-selected, n_ε per class) replayed alongside new-task data during each incremental phase.

## Method

The core mechanism reweights per-class gradients in the FC layer by multiplying each class's gradient with an adaptive balance ratio α^j = min_m Φ^m / Φ^j, where Φ^j is the accumulated gradient magnitude for class j over iterations, dynamically equalizing optimization steps across head and tail classes. For inter-phase imbalance, Decoupled Gradient Reweighting (DGR) separately applies class-balance ratios and a task-balance ratio r with an attenuation factor exp(−γ|X^{1:t−1}|/|X^{1:t}|) that boosts new-class gradients relative to learned ones, while a loss-balance ratio β scales the distillation gradient to match the reweighted cross-entropy magnitude. A Distribution-Aware Knowledge Distillation (DAKD) loss decouples standard KD into a balanced part and an imbalanced part whose adjusted logits ẑ_j are interpolated toward teacher logits proportionally to the fraction of lost training data per class, imposing stronger regularization on head classes that lose more data. A regularized softmax with per-label logit offsets (log π_j) compensates for the side effect of gradient down-weighting on head-class output logits.

## Results

On the three long-tailed CIL benchmarks (ρ=100, n_ε=20), the proposed Gradient Reweighting method achieves the best Average Accuracy across all 12 dataset-protocol-task configurations. Headline numbers include 35.66% on CIFAR100-LT (LFS, N=10), 45.12% on ImageNetSubset-LT (LFS, N=10), and 29.05% on Food101-LT (LFS, N=10), outperforming the strongest 2-stage baselines (LUCIR-2stage, MAFDRC) by 2–5 percentage points. Under the easier LFH protocol, gains are more modest (e.g., 50.57% vs. 48.97% on ImageNetSubset-LT, N=5), consistent with the paper's observation that pre-training on half the classes reduces the severity of imbalance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset-protocol-task combination in Table 1. The largest margins appear under LFS: on ImageNetSubset-LT (N=10), Ours achieves 45.12% vs. MAFDRC's 40.01% (+5.11 pp) and LUCIR-2stage's 39.87% (+5.25 pp); on CIFAR100-LT (N=10), it reaches 35.66% vs. EEIL-2stage's 33.64% (+2.02 pp). Under LFH, where pre-training on half the classes mitigates imbalance, the gap narrows to roughly 1–2 pp (e.g., 50.57% vs. LUCIR-2stage's 48.97% on ImageNetSubset-LT, N=5). Among the 2-stage methods that add an extra learnable layer, LUCIR-2stage (cosine classifier) and MAFDRC (dynamic residual classifier) are the strongest competitors, but both still trail the end-to-end gradient reweighting approach. The paper also shows in Figure 5 that its DAKD loss yields lower forgetting rates than BKD, MKD, DCKD, and DPKD variants, and in Figure 6 that it produces more uniform FC-layer weight norms than BiC and WA post-hoc corrections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of class-incremental learning (CIL) and long-tailed recognition, building on logit adjustment (Menon et al., ICLR 2021) for the regularized softmax and on decoupled knowledge distillation (Zhao et al., CVPR 2022) for the DAKD loss decomposition. It departs from prior CIL bias-correction methods (BiC, WA, SSIL) that apply post-hoc logit corrections or separated softmax, instead reweighting gradients directly during optimization using dynamically accumulated gradient magnitudes. The most closely related prior work is Liu et al. (ECCV 2022) on long-tailed CIL, which introduced a two-stage decoupling; this paper addresses the same setting end-to-end without extra training stages, and additionally identifies and tackles the 'imbalanced forgetting' phenomenon where head classes degrade more than tail classes across incremental phases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a realistic gap: most CIL methods assume balanced class distributions, yet real-world data is long-tailed, and the dual imbalance (intra-phase class skew plus inter-phase exemplar-vs-new-data skew) is shown to cause biased FC-layer gradients and over/under-fitting. The 'imbalanced forgetting' observation—that instance-rich classes actually suffer greater degradation because more of their training data becomes unavailable—refines the field's understanding of forgetting dynamics and motivates distribution-aware regularization. Limitations include evaluation only on ResNet-32/18 backbones and three datasets, and the gradient reweighting adds per-iteration bookkeeping (accumulated gradient norms) that may complicate integration into existing CIL pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger architectures (e.g., ViTs), whether the attenuation factor and loss balance ratio generalize across task structures, and how the method interacts with parameter-isolation or online CIL settings.
