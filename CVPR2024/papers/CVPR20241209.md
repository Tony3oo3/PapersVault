## Problem

Existing CoOp-based prompt tuning methods rely on domain-shared or image-conditional textual tokens that cannot dynamically adapt to the distribution of unseen test classes, resulting in suboptimal generalization. The core gap is the inability of these tokens to explicitly model class-level distribution, since they are derived from training images rather than from the semantic identity of each class.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241209/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, TCP takes an RGB image (processed by CLIP's frozen ViT-B/16 visual encoder) and a set of class names as text input. The class names are first embedded by CLIP's frozen text encoder into class-level textual embeddings, which are then projected by the trained TKE module into class-aware prompt tokens of shape (N_c × M × D) with M=4. The output is a standard class label predicted via cosine similarity between the image embedding and the generated class-aware textual classifier.

## Method

TCP introduces a Textual Knowledge Embedding (TKE) module that projects frozen CLIP's class-level textual embeddings into class-aware prompt tokens via a two-layer bottleneck (down-project to D_mid=128, then up-project to M×D). These class-aware prompts are inserted at the 8th layer of the Text Encoder, replacing the first M positions of the intermediate token sequence, while domain-shared learnable tokens (initialized as 'X X X X {}') are prepended at the input layer as in CoOp. Training optimizes both the learnable tokens and TKE using a contrastive loss plus a knowledge-guided consistency loss (L2 distance to CLIP's original class embeddings) with weight ω=8.0. At inference, TKE dynamically generates class-specific prompts for unseen classes from their textual descriptions alone, making the module plug-and-play with existing CoOp-based methods.

## Results

On the base-to-new generalization setting (16-shot, 11 datasets), TCP achieves an average harmonic mean (H) of 79.51%, with Base=84.13% and New=75.36%, outperforming the strongest textual-prompt baseline DePT (H=79.10%) and the strongest visual-textual baseline PromptSRC (H=79.31%). On cross-dataset generalization (trained on ImageNet, tested on 10 other datasets), TCP attains 66.29% average accuracy, matching DAPT (66.31%) while using only textual prompt tuning. In the 4-shot few-shot setting, TCP reaches 76.72% average accuracy, surpassing PLOT (76.45%) and TIP-Adapter-F (76.11%). Training time is 6.4 ms/image, comparable to CoOp (6 ms) and far faster than PLOT (78.8 ms) or RPO (190 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCP (H=79.51%) outperforms all 16 compared baselines on the base-to-new harmonic mean. The largest gains are over CoOp (+5.03%) and DAPT (+3.92%), while the margin over the closest competitors is modest: +0.41% over DePT (79.10%) and +0.20% over PromptSRC (79.31%). On the New-class metric, TCP leads DePT by 0.32% and PromptSRC by 0.34%, confirming that class-aware prompts specifically improve generalization to unseen categories. In cross-dataset generalization, TCP (66.29%) is essentially tied with DAPT (66.31%) and beats DePT (65.55%) by 0.74%, though it trails DAPT slightly on individual datasets like EuroSAT (51.45% vs 52.47%). In the 4-shot few-shot setting, TCP (76.72%) edges out PLOT (76.45%) and TIP-Adapter-F (76.11%), but the gap is small. Notably, TCP achieves these results using only shallow single-layer textual prompt tuning (6.4 ms/image), whereas methods like PromptSRC and MaPLe require deep multi-layer visual-textual prompt tuning at 43.2 and 90.7 ms/image respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCP extends the CoOp prompt tuning framework (Zhou et al., IJCV 2022) by introducing a third type of prompt token—class-aware prompts—generated via a Textual Knowledge Embedding (TKE) that projects CLIP's frozen class-level textual embeddings into the Text Encoder's middle layers. This departs from the two dominant paradigms in prior work: domain-shared prompts (CoOp, ProGrad, ProDA) that cannot adapt to unseen class distributions, and image-conditional prompts (CoCoOp, VPT) that capture image-specific but not class-discriminative knowledge. The training objective directly inherits KgCoOp's (Zhu et al., CVPR 2023) knowledge-guided consistency loss, and the entire pipeline operates on CLIP's (Radford et al., ICML 2021) frozen encoders, positioning TCP as a lightweight adapter on top of existing CoOp-based methods rather than a new VLM architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play TKE module offers a practical, low-cost (6.4 ms/image) way to improve generalization across multiple existing prompt tuning methods, which is valuable for practitioners seeking easy performance gains without re-architecting their pipelines. However, the paper itself acknowledges a fundamental limitation: TCP's effectiveness is tightly coupled to the quality of CLIP's textual embeddings, as evidenced by its underperformance on FGVCAircraft where fine-grained class distinctions are poorly captured by simple class-name text. An open question is whether richer textual descriptions or external knowledge sources could decouple TCP from this dependency, and whether the class-aware prompt idea transfers to other VLM backbones beyond CLIP.
