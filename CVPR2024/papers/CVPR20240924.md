## Problem

Existing driving video prediction models are confined to limited scenarios (e.g., highways with low traffic density), small-scale datasets, or restricted generation conditions, and thus fail to generalize across diverse geographic locations, weather conditions, and sensor configurations. The paper asks how to build a video predictive model for autonomous driving that generalizes to unseen environments in a zero-shot manner while capturing the complex causal dynamics of driving scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240924/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

GenAD takes a 4-second video clip at 2 Hz (resized to 256×448) where the leading m frames serve as clean past observations and the remaining n frames are the future to predict, along with a text condition that concatenates a BLIP-2-generated frame description (context) and a categorical ego-action command mapped to free-form expressions. For action-conditioned prediction, a 6-waypoint future ego trajectory is additionally injected via Fourier embeddings through cross-attention. The model outputs denoised future frame latents; in the planning extension, a lightweight MLP on top of the frozen GenAD encoder outputs ego trajectory waypoints.

## Method

GenAD is trained in two stages: first, SDXL is fine-tuned on OpenDV-2K image-text pairs to transfer the generation distribution to the driving domain; second, novel temporal reasoning blocks are interleaved with the frozen SDXL spatial blocks to learn video prediction. Each temporal reasoning block consists of one causal temporal attention layer (with a mask that prevents attending to future frames, enforcing causal reasoning) and two decoupled spatial attention layers that propagate features along horizontal and vertical axes with linear complexity to handle the drastic per-pixel view shifts in driving scenes. The training objective jointly denoises future latents conditioned on past latents and text, with only the corrupted future frames contributing to the loss. Zero-initialization of newly added block parameters stabilizes training by preserving the pretrained image generation capability. The OpenDV-2K dataset (2059 hours, 65.1M frames from 40+ countries) provides the scale and diversity needed for generalization, and the frozen GenAD encoder can be paired with a 0.8M-parameter MLP for planning, achieving competitive results with 73× fewer trainable parameters than UniAD.

## Results

On nuScenes video prediction, GenAD achieves FID 15.4 and FVD 184, outperforming all prior driving-specific methods. Compared to the strongest baseline DrivingDiffusion (FID 15.8, FVD 332), GenAD reduces FVD by 44.5% without requiring 3D layout inputs. For open-loop planning on nuScenes, GenAD with only 0.8M trainable parameters achieves ADE 1.23 and FDE 2.31, approaching UniAD (ADE 1.03, FDE 1.65) while using 73× fewer parameters and training 3400× faster. For action-conditioned prediction, GenAD-act reduces the Action Prediction Error to 2.02 m (vs. 2.54 m for text-only GenAD), a 20.4% improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenAD decisively outperforms all driving-specific video generation baselines on nuScenes: DriveGAN (FID 73.4, FVD 502) and DriveDreamer (FID 52.6, FVD 452) are far surpassed, while the closest competitor DrivingDiffusion (FID 15.8, FVD 332) is beaten by 44.5% in FVD without needing 3D layout inputs. In zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets (Waymo, KITTI, Cityscapes), GenAD substantially outperforms general-purpose video models I2VGen-XL and VideoCrafter1, which fail to maintain consistency with input frames, and also outperforms DMVFN, which exhibits shape distortions on unseen domains. For planning, GenAD (0.8M params) trails UniAD (58.8M params) by 0.20 ADE and 0.66 FDE but uses 73× fewer trainable parameters and trains 3400× faster; it clearly beats ST-P3 (ADE 2.65, FDE 3.73) on both metrics. A key finding is that GenAD-nus (trained only on nuScenes) matches GenAD on nuScenes but degrades on Waymo, confirming that the OpenDV-2K pre-training is essential for cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenAD builds directly on SDXL's latent diffusion architecture and the temporal attention paradigm from Align Your Latents, but departs in two key ways: it replaces bidirectional temporal attention with a causally masked variant to enforce temporal reasoning, and introduces decoupled spatial attention to handle the drastic perspective shifts unique to driving. Rather than training from scratch on small, curated driving datasets (as DriveGAN, DriveDreamer, and DrivingDiffusion do), it fine-tunes a general-purpose text-to-image LDM on a 2000+ hour web-sourced corpus (OpenDV-2K), positioning itself as a foundation-model approach to driving video prediction. The two-stage curriculum—image domain transfer followed by temporal pre-training with frozen spatial weights—distinguishes it from prior driving-specific video generators that lack this transfer-learning structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OpenDV-2K (2059 hours, 40+ countries, 65M frames) is 374× larger than nuScenes and introduces unprecedented geographic and sensor diversity, making it a critical resource for the field. The demonstrated zero-shot transfer to Waymo, KITTI, and Cityscapes suggests that web-scale pre-training can replace the expensive, scenario-limited data collection that has bottlenecked driving video prediction. However, the paper acknowledges that the model's capacity creates real-time deployment challenges, and the planning extension, while 3400× more efficient to train than UniAD, still trails in ADE/FDE—leaving open whether the learned world-model representations can be distilled into lightweight, deployable policies. The broader question of whether unified video prediction can serve as a scalable pre-training objective for end-to-end driving remains an active direction the authors explicitly flag.
