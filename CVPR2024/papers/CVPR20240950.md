## Problem

Existing monocular human avatar methods either rely on expensive multi-view capture and cannot render in real time, or use GAN-based image-to-image translation that produces blurry results for out-of-distribution poses and ignores fine-grained body parts such as the face and hands. This paper addresses the gap of achieving real-time, photo-realistic, full-body animation (including facial expressions and hand gestures) from a single monocular video while remaining robust to driving poses outside the training distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240950/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is trained on a monocular video sequence (e.g., 12,099 frames for HFshirt) with per-frame SMPL-X body, FaceVerse face, and MANO hand parameters estimated via ProxyCap. At test time, it accepts driving body poses, hand keypoint maps, and facial expression parameters to synthesize 1024×1024 photorealistic frames in real time. The internal representation consists of a 1024×1024×48 neural texture for the body and two 1024×1024×16 feature maps for face and hands, concatenated into a full-body feature map consumed by a StyleUnet generator.

## Method

RAM-Avatar combines three components: (1) a dual attention module that refines the rasterized neural-texture body feature map using a sparse criss-cross attention variant—spatially, it compares the original feature map against an expanded version (mesh vertices pulled outward along normals) to distinguish template-proximal from distant regions, and temporally, it fuses with a feature map from the previous frame to enforce consistency; (2) a lightweight StyleUnet generator paired with a temporal-aware discriminator that scores triplets of frames (t−1, t, t+1) to jointly optimize realism and temporal coherence; and (3) a Motion Distribution Align module that uses a frozen VAE encoder (trained on AMASS, Human3.6M, and other large-scale mocap datasets) to extract pose semantics, followed by a distribution-specific conditional decoder (trained only on the target avatar's poses) to project out-of-distribution driving poses into the avatar's pose domain while preserving motion intent. Training uses standard GAN, L1, VGG19 perceptual, and hand-region perceptual losses.

## Results

On the HFshirt dataset for novel in-distribution pose animation, RAM-Avatar achieves SSIM 0.943, LPIPS 0.036, tLPIPS 0.353, and FID 12.79, outperforming all three baselines on every metric. For novel large rotation poses on the People-Snapshot dataset, RAM-Avatar reaches SSIM 0.961, LPIPS 0.020, and tLPIPS 0.155, again leading on three of four metrics (FID 32.09 is slightly behind InstantAvatar's 26.97). The method also demonstrates robustness on the out-of-distribution Male1 sequence, where ANR and InstantAvatar fail to produce reasonable results while RAM-Avatar successfully transfers motions to the avatar pose domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAM-Avatar outperforms all three baselines across all four metrics in the standard in-distribution setting (Table 1). Against ANR [51], the gains are substantial: LPIPS drops from 0.098 to 0.036 and FID from 54.15 to 12.79, reflecting the advantage of the dual attention module and full-body feature maps over ANR's body-only neural texture. Against HFMT [31], RAM-Avatar leads on every metric, with the largest margin in tLPIPS (0.353 vs. 0.469), indicating superior temporal consistency from the temporal-aware discriminator. Against InstantAvatar [30], RAM-Avatar wins decisively on LPIPS (0.036 vs. 0.105) and tLPIPS (0.353 vs. 0.886), though InstantAvatar's FID (44.48) is closer to RAM-Avatar's (12.79) than the other baselines. In the large-rotation setting (Table 2), RAM-Avatar still leads on SSIM, LPIPS, and tLPIPS, but InstantAvatar achieves a lower FID (26.97 vs. 32.09), suggesting the NeRF-based method retains a slight distributional advantage under extreme viewpoint changes. For out-of-distribution poses, both ANR and InstantAvatar produce collapsed or blurry results, while RAM-Avatar's Motion Distribution Align module preserves motion semantics and avoids degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural-texture-based monocular avatar line (ANR, Neural Textures) by integrating FaceVerse and MANO templates for full-body control and introducing a dual attention module (spatial criss-cross + temporal) to refine body feature maps, addressing the geometric misalignment and temporal inconsistency that prior texture-based methods ignore. For out-of-distribution robustness, it departs from the motion-graph approach used by prior methods (e.g., [74]) and instead proposes a VAE-based Motion Distribution Align module that transfers driving poses into the avatar's pose domain while preserving motion semantics, drawing on the Vposer/HUMOR pose-encoding literature. The rendering backbone is a lightweight StyleUnet with a temporal-aware discriminator, building on GAN-based image-to-image translation frameworks like HFMT and InstantAvatar but targeting real-time 1024×1024 output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time, photo-realistic full-body avatars from a single video are directly applicable to AR/VR telepresence, film production, and interactive media, and this paper is among the first to simultaneously achieve face, hand, and body control at real-time speed from monocular input. The MDA module offers a principled alternative to motion graphs for handling OOD poses, though the authors acknowledge that extreme poses (e.g., back views from front-only training) still degrade. Open questions include whether the parametric template dependency (SMPL-X, FaceVerse, MANO) can be relaxed, whether the approach generalizes across diverse subjects without per-subject retraining, and the ethical risks of real-time deepfake generation that the authors themselves flag.
