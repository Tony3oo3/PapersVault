## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video prediction methods repeatedly apply a 3D spatiotemporal U-Net across denoising steps, incurring heavy computational cost (single-digit FPS) while still suffering from performance degradation over longer horizons due to the lack of deterministic future cues. The core gap is that these methods attempt to hallucinate future frames from scratch rather than leveraging the structured motion dynamics that bridge present and future.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of u condition frames (e.g., 10 frames at 64×64 on KTH, 2 frames at 64×64 on BAIR, 2 frames at 128×128 on Cityscapes) and produces v predicted future frames at the same resolution. Internally, the pipeline operates in a compressed motion space where each frame is represented by a 3-channel motion cue (2-channel optical flow + 1-channel occlusion map) at reduced spatial resolution (H/S × W/S), and the final pixel-level frames are reconstructed via warping and inpainting from the keyframe. Scalable variants (K1–K4) trade prediction length (1×–8× the condition length) against speed (20–47 FPS).

## Method

ExtDM decouples video prediction into motion-cue extrapolation and frame reconstruction. A lightweight motion autoencoder establishes a bijection between pixel frames and motion cues (optical flow + occlusion map) via pairwise encoding/decoding with warping and inpainting. The Layered Distribution Adaptor (LDA) extrapolates features into the future by estimating a Gaussian distribution (mean and variance) over present features at each layer, then sampling future features from the shifted distribution in a multi-layered autoregressive fashion, doubling the temporal span per layer. A Spatiotemporal Window (STW) U-Net refines the noised motion cues using sparse strided cross-attention within shifted spatiotemporal partitions (analogous to Swin Transformer's window shifting), fusing the LDA-extrapolated guidance with the denoising features. Training proceeds in two stages: perceptual loss for the autoencoder and L2 loss for the diffusion denoiser, with the entire pipeline operating at compact motion-cue resolution to reduce computation.

## Results

On the long-term KTH benchmark (u=10, v=40), ExtDM-K4 achieves FVD 221.4, PSNR 27.91, SSIM 0.799, and LPIPS 0.093, outperforming the best baseline MCVD-c (FVD 276.6) by 20% in FVD while running 7.1× faster (45.28 vs 6.35 FPS). On BAIR (u=2, v=28), ExtDM-K4 reaches FVD 102.8 and PSNR 18.74, surpassing MCVD-cp (FVD 119.0) by 14%. On the short-term SMMNIST benchmark, ExtDM-K4 achieves FVD 11.11, beating RVD (FVD 17.84) by 38% in FVD and 23.6% in PSNR. Across all five datasets, ExtDM consistently achieves the best FVD, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS while being 7.51× faster on average than competing diffusion-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExtDM outperforms all eight compared baselines across all five datasets and all four quality metrics (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS, FVD). On KTH (u=10, v=40), ExtDM-K4 reduces FVD by 20% over MCVD-c (221.4 vs 276.6) and by 35% over LFDM (221.4 vs 287.9), while also achieving the highest PSNR (27.91 vs 26.20 for MCVD-c). On BAIR (u=2, v=28), ExtDM-K4 achieves FVD 102.8, beating MCVD-cp (119.0) by 14% and MCVD-c (120.6) by 15%, with a 4.4× speed advantage over MCVD variants. On Cityscapes, ExtDM-K4 (FVD 121.3) surpasses MCVD-c (141.3) by 14% and MCVD-s (184.8) by 34%. On SMMNIST, ExtDM-K4 (FVD 11.11) dominates RVD (17.84) by 38% in FVD and MCVD-c (25.63) by 57%. On UCF-101, ExtDM-K2 (FVD 394.1) beats MCVD-cp (468.1) by 16%. In terms of speed, ExtDM runs 7.51× faster on average than competing methods, with FPS ranging from 20.16 (SMMNIST) to 47.01 (BAIR), compared to 0.12–6.99 FPS for baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

ExtDM builds on the video diffusion model paradigm (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2022) by retaining a 3D U-Net denoiser, but departs fundamentally from prior diffusion-based predictors (MCVD, RVD, LVDM) that iteratively denoise full frames in pixel or latent space. Instead, it reformulates prediction as extrapolating compact motion cues (optical flow + occlusion maps) via a Layered Distribution Adaptor that estimates shifted Gaussian parameters over time, drawing on Gaussian mixture modeling from image compression (Zhu et al., CVPR 2022) and prototype-based segmentation (Yang et al., ECCV 2020). The spatiotemporal window attention mechanism adapts the shifted-window strategy from Swin Transformer (Liu et al., ICCV 2021) to a 3D cross-attention setting, and the motion autoencoder is grounded in the perceptual-loss autoencoder of Johnson et al. (ECCV 2016). Conceptually, the paper extends the motion-content decomposition line (Villegas et al., ICLR 2017; MOSO, 2023) by making motion extrapolation the primary generative step rather than a conditioning signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.5× speedup over prior diffusion-based SOTA (45 FPS vs. ~6 FPS for MCVD) while matching or exceeding quality across five benchmarks makes diffusion-based video prediction viable for near-real-time applications such as autonomous driving and robotic navigation. The decoupling of motion extrapolation from frame reconstruction also opens a practical avenue for user-customizable future predictions and stochastic event generation, which prior monolithic diffusion pipelines cannot easily support. Open questions include whether the Gaussian distribution assumption in LDA adequately captures multimodal future uncertainty at higher resolutions, and whether the optical-flow-based motion representation generalizes to high-frequency or large-displacement scenes beyond the 32–128 px resolutions evaluated here.
