## Problem

CLIP-based open-vocabulary semantic segmentation methods, when fine-tuned on a specific dataset like COCO-Stuff, overfit to the training classes and lose generalization ability for unseen classes. The core challenge is simultaneously preserving strong recognition of training classes (gained through supervised training) and maintaining the broad generalization that CLIP's pretrained image-text alignment provides for new classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241929/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 640×640 RGB image and an arbitrary set of class names as input. It produces per-pixel semantic segmentation masks at H/4 × W/4 resolution for all specified classes, computed as the product of predicted masks, adaptively balanced image embeddings, and CLIP text embeddings. Two frozen image encoders (CLIP ViT and SAM ViT-B) process the image in parallel, while a frozen CLIP text encoder processes the class names; only the decoder and a feature fusion module are trainable.

## Method

EBSeg fuses features from frozen CLIP and SAM image encoders via element-wise addition, then feeds them into an AdaB Decoder (Pixel Decoder + Transformer Decoder + CLIP Mask Attention Decoder) that produces three distinct image embeddings: fully supervised embeddings A (trained with cross-entropy on training classes), mask attention embeddings B (obtained by running per-head attention masks through the last L/4 blocks of CLIP), and frozen embeddings D (from mask pooling over CLIP's final output). During inference, these embeddings are adaptively balanced with class-specific weights (α=0.2, β=0.7 for training classes; γ=0, β=0.7 for new classes), exploiting A's supervised precision for known classes and D's frozen generalization for unseen ones. The SSC Loss aligns the inter-class cosine similarity matrix in the image embedding space with that in CLIP's text embedding space, distilling CLIP's semantic structure into the trainable embeddings. The frozen SAM encoder complements the spatial detail that CLIP features lack due to CLIP's low training resolution and image-level contrastive supervision.

## Results

EBSeg achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across all five open-vocabulary segmentation benchmarks. With CLIP ViT-B/16, it reaches 30.0 mIoU on ADE20K-150, 17.3 on Pascal Context-459, 11.1 on ADE20K-847, 56.7 on Pascal Context-59, and 94.6 on VOC. With CLIP ViT-L/14, the numbers improve to 32.8, 21.0, 13.7, 60.2, and 96.4 respectively, yielding an average improvement of 2.3% mIoU over the best prior method on both model scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBSeg outperforms all CLIP-based baselines on every benchmark. Against SAN (the strongest prior method using the same training data), EBSeg gains +1.0 mIoU on A-847, +4.7 on PC-459, +2.5 on A-150, +2.9 on PC-59, and +0.6 on VOC with ViT-B/16; with ViT-L/14 the margins widen to +1.3, +5.3, +0.7, +2.5, and +1.8 respectively. Against ODISE (which uses a 1.5B-parameter diffusion model), EBSeg surpasses it on A-847 (13.7 vs 11.1), PC-459 (21.0 vs 14.5), A-150 (32.8 vs 29.9), and PC-59 (60.2 vs 57.3) with ViT-L/14, while using far fewer parameters. Against OVSeg and SimSeg, the gaps are even larger (e.g., +4.7 mIoU on A-150 over SimSeg with ViT-B/16). The largest relative gains appear on Pascal Context-459, where EBSeg's SSC Loss and adaptive balancing most directly address the overfitting-to-training-classes problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

EBSeg builds directly on the one-stage CLIP-based OVSS paradigm established by MaskCLIP and SAN, reusing their mask attention module to produce mask attention embeddings, and on Mask2former's Pixel Decoder + Transformer Decoder architecture for mask generation. Its key departure is the explicit treatment of the overfitting-to-training-classes problem: rather than relying on a single embedding pathway, it generates three distinct image embeddings (fully supervised, mask-attention, and frozen CLIP) and adaptively balances them at inference, while the SSC Loss distills CLIP's text-space class affinity into the image feature space. The use of a frozen SAM encoder to complement CLIP's spatially coarse features is a practical addition not present in prior one-stage OVSS methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive balancing strategy and SSC Loss offer a principled, lightweight remedy for the well-known train/new-class trade-off in CLIP-based segmentation, and the consistent SOTA gains across five benchmarks with both ViT-B and ViT-L backbones make the approach practically useful. However, the balancing weights (α, β, γ) are hand-tuned per class split rather than learned, which limits generality; the authors acknowledge this and flag learnable weights as future work. Open questions include whether the SSC Loss transfers to other vision-language backbones beyond CLIP, how the method behaves under extreme vocabulary shifts, and whether the SAM-CLIP fusion can be replaced by lighter alternatives without sacrificing spatial fidelity.
