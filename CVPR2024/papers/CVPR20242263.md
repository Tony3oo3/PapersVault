## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary object detection methods expand detector knowledge using only 'positive' pseudo-labels from additional class names, leaving the complementary signals of negative classes and fine-grained verbalized concepts unexplored. The paper asks whether retrieving semantically related negative vocabularies and LLM-generated object descriptions can further improve generalization to novel categories beyond what positive-only pseudo-labeling achieves.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and a set of category names (base and novel) at inference, augmented by a large external vocabulary store (V3Det) and a concept store of LLM-generated noun-chunk descriptions. On COCO it outputs bounding boxes with class labels over 65 categories (48 base, 17 novel); on LVIS it outputs instance segmentation masks with class labels over 1203 categories (866 base, 337 novel). The final classification logits are an ensemble of the baseline detector's logits and auxiliary logits produced by the RAF module, with the ensemble formula varying per baseline (additive, multiplicative, or sigmoid-scaled).

## Method

RALF comprises two plug-in modules. RAL (Retrieval-Augmented Losses) retrieves hard and easy negative vocabularies from a large vocabulary store via cosine similarity to the ground-truth label's text embedding, filtered by a rank-variance sampling scheme to discard uninformative entries; it then adds two triplet losses to the baseline training objective—one pulling the box embedding closer to the ground-truth label than to hard negatives, and another ensuring hard negatives remain closer than easy negatives. RAF (Retrieval-Augmented visual Features) pre-generates verbalized concept descriptions for all vocabularies using GPT-3, stores the resulting noun chunks in a concept store, and at inference retrieves the top-k most relevant concepts by cosine similarity to the region's visual feature; a multi-layer transformer decoder (the augmenter) fuses the original visual feature with the retrieved concept embeddings via cross-attention, producing an augmented feature that yields auxiliary classification logits. The two modules operate independently—RAL during detector training and RAF as a frozen plug-in at inference—so RALF can be attached to any existing OVD baseline without modifying its architecture.

## Results

On the COCO benchmark, RALF plugged into Object-Centric-OVD achieves 41.3 AP₅₀ᴺ on novel categories, a +4.7 improvement over the baseline (36.6), setting a new state-of-the-art. On OADP, RALF yields +3.4 AP₅₀ᴺ (30.0→33.4), +1.2 AP₅₀ᴮ, and +1.8 AP₅₀. On the LVIS benchmark (instance segmentation), RALF plugged into OADP reaches 21.9 APᵣ on novel categories (+2.0 over baseline), and DetPro+RALF achieves 21.1 APᵣ (+1.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RALF is evaluated as a plug-in module on three primary baselines (OADP, Object-Centric-OVD, DetPro) and compared against five additional OVD methods (ViLD, PB-OVD, OV-DETR, VL-Det, BARON, MEDet). On COCO, Object-Centric-OVD+RALF (41.3 AP₅₀ᴺ) surpasses all prior methods including BARON (34.0) and VL-Det (32.0) by a wide margin of 7.3 and 9.3 AP₅₀ᴺ respectively. On LVIS, OADP+RALF (21.9 APᵣ) outperforms DetPro (19.8), OADP (19.9), and BARON (18.0) on novel categories, while DetPro+RALF (21.1 APᵣ) also exceeds the original DetPro by 1.3. Notably, RALF improves not only novel but also base categories (e.g., +1.2 AP₅₀ᴮ on OADP-COCO), indicating that the retrieval-augmented losses and features do not sacrifice known-class performance. The only minor regression is Object-Centric-OVD+RALF on LVIS, where AP_c and AP_f drop slightly (21.4→21.0, 26.7→26.3) while APᵣ improves (17.1→18.5), suggesting a small trade-off on common/frequent categories in that specific configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

RALF extends the open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) line of work that leverages pre-trained vision-language models (CLIP, ALIGN) for zero-shot classification of novel categories. Prior OVD methods (ViLD, Object-Centric-OVD, OADP, DetPro) primarily expand detector knowledge through positive pseudo-labels generated by matching region features to additional vocabulary or captions. RALF departs from this by (1) retrieving semantically related *negative* classes from a large vocabulary store (V3Det) to construct triplet-style losses during training, and (2) using LLM-generated verbalized concepts (e.g., 'sharp teeth' for jaguar) to augment visual features at inference time via a lightweight transformer decoder. The retrieval-augmentation paradigm itself is borrowed from NLP (RAG) and vision generation tasks, but its application to OVD training and inference is, per the authors, novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design (RAL during training, RAF at inference) makes RALF broadly applicable to existing OVD backbones without architectural changes, which is practically appealing. However, the gains are moderate (1–4 AP points depending on baseline and dataset), and the method depends on frozen CLIP embeddings and GPT-3 for concept generation, introducing cost and a potential bottleneck for very large vocabulary sets. An open question is whether the LLM-generated verbalized concepts provide genuinely new semantic signal beyond what CLIP's text encoder already captures, or whether simpler attribute-based descriptions would suffice. The paper also does not explore whether the negative-class retrieval strategy transfers to other open-vocabulary tasks (segmentation, tracking) or to settings where the vocabulary store is dynamically growing.
