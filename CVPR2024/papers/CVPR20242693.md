## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion frequently produce images that misalign with their prompts, exhibiting subject neglect, subject mixing, and incorrect attribute binding. The paper identifies the root cause as invalid initial noise: not all randomly sampled noise vectors from the standard Gaussian lead to semantically faithful generation, yet no prior method explicitly navigates the initial latent space to find valid noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242693/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a randomly sampled initial noise vector z_T ~ N(0,1) in the 16×16 latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.4, and outputs a 512×512 RGB image after 50 denoising steps. The noise is optimized in-place before denoising begins (not during), making the approach plug-and-play and compatible with downstream controllable generation tasks such as layout-to-image via BoxDiff. Target tokens for scoring can be identified manually or automatically via GPT.

## Method

INITNO partitions the initial latent space into valid and invalid regions using two scores computed at the first denoising step: a cross-attention response score (threshold τ_c=0.2) that detects subject neglect, and a self-attention conflict score (threshold τ_s=0.3) that detects subject mixing via spatial overlap of self-attention maps. To navigate noise into the valid region, the method optimizes the mean μ and standard deviation σ of the noise's Gaussian distribution (rather than applying incremental updates), minimizing a joint loss of cross-attention response, self-attention conflict, and a KL-divergence distribution alignment term (λ_3=500) that constrains the optimized noise to remain near N(0,1). This distribution-level parameterization with an explicit Gaussian constraint avoids the under-/over-optimization trade-off that plagues per-step noisy-image adjustment methods like Attend-and-Excite, requiring only ~50 Adam steps at lr=1e-2 per round (up to 5 rounds) before the optimized noise is fed into standard denoising.

## Results

In the user study (12 participants, 20 questions each), INITNO achieves 63.33% preference, far exceeding the next-best method Attend-and-Excite (14.17%) and all other baselines. On objective CLIP metrics (Full Prompt Similarity, Minimum Object Similarity, and Text-Text Similarity), INITNO outperforms all six baselines across the Animal-Animal, Animal-Object, and Object-Object datasets. The inference cost is 18.93 s per 512×512 image on a single V100, compared to 8.34 s for vanilla Stable Diffusion, reflecting the additional noise-optimization steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INITNO decisively outperforms all six baselines on the user study, capturing 63.33% of votes versus 14.17% for the strongest competitor, Attend-and-Excite [6], and single-digit percentages for the remaining methods. On CLIP-based objective metrics (Full Prompt Similarity, Minimum Object Similarity, Text-Text Similarity), INITNO leads every baseline across all three datasets, with the largest gaps over Composable Diffusion [25] and Structure Diffusion [12], which suffer from subject neglect. Compared to the denoising-step adjustment methods (Attend-and-Excite, Divide-and-Bind [24], A-STAR [1]), INITNO shows markedly better generalization across random seeds — e.g., Divide-and-Bind succeeds on only 1 of several seeds for the same prompt, while INITNO consistently produces valid outputs. The primary trade-off is inference time: INITNO requires ~2.3× the wall-clock time of vanilla SD (18.93 s vs. 8.34 s) due to the iterative noise-optimization loop (up to 50 steps × 5 rounds). The method is also plug-and-play, integrating with layout-to-image frameworks like BoxDiff [38] without retraining, a capability none of the baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

INITNO builds directly on the attention-based semantic guidance paradigm established by Attend-and-Excite (Chefer et al., 2023), which uses cross-attention maps to detect and correct subject neglect during denoising. However, it departs fundamentally from the per-step noisy-image modification strategy shared by Attend-and-Excite, Divide-and-Bind, and A-STAR, instead performing a one-time optimization of the initial latent noise before denoising begins. The paper also introduces a novel self-attention conflict score to detect subject mixing, complementing the cross-attention response score, and adds a KL-divergence-based distribution alignment loss to keep optimized noise within the standard Gaussian manifold—mechanisms absent from prior training-free guidance methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of INITNO makes it immediately applicable to existing Stable Diffusion deployments without retraining, and its extension to layout-to-image generation (via BoxDiff) demonstrates broader utility. However, the method incurs a ~2.3× inference cost (18.93s vs. 8.34s), relies on empirically tuned thresholds (τ_c=0.2, τ_s=0.3), and is validated primarily on SD v1.4 with two-subject prompts, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models) and highly complex multi-subject scenes. The core open question is whether initial-noise optimization can be made faster (e.g., via learned predictors) while preserving the distribution-alignment guarantee.
