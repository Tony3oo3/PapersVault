## Problem

Existing face landmarkers are trained on real human faces and perform poorly on stylized faces (cartoons, caricatures, artistic portraits) due to the diversity of facial styles and the scarcity of labeled stylized data. Standard domain adaptation techniques (adversarial, metric-learning) fail to close this gap because they optimize image-domain alignment rather than landmark-specific geometric supervision, leaving a significant NME gap between source and target domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242421/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB facial images (H×W×3) and outputs 68 two-dimensional landmark coordinates. Training pairs labeled real faces from 300W (3,148 images) with unlabeled stylized faces from CariFace or ArtiFace; the warping field is defined by a polyharmonic interpolation model with thin-plate spline kernels, and the image discrepancy loss is computed in the Sobel gradient domain rather than raw pixel space to emphasize geometric edges over color and texture.

## Method

The face landmarker (SLPT backbone by default) is embedded as the key module of a conditional face warper: predicted landmarks on a stylized face serve as sparse endpoints of a polyharmonic warping field that deforms a real face toward the stylized target, with all other pixel displacements filled in by spline interpolation. Training alternates between two subproblems solved by Adam: (1) face warper optimization, minimizing the Sobel-gradient discrepancy between the warped real face and the stylized target plus a landmark warping error, and (2) proximal face landmarker optimization, minimizing real-face landmark error plus a proximal regularizer that penalizes deviation from the pseudo-landmarks predicted in the previous warper step. The low-degree-of-freedom warping model (few learnable parameters γ) keeps the optimization focused on the landmarker θ and avoids the local optima and saddle points that joint end-to-end training of the neural network and warping parameters tends to produce.

## Results

On the DA(300W→CariFace) setting, the proposed method achieves 7.70 NME on CariFace and 3.95 NME on the unseen ArtiFace domain, substantially outperforming all baselines (best baseline: CycleGAN at 12.11 and 4.70 respectively). In the DA(300W→ArtiFace) setting, it reaches 3.93 NME on ArtiFace and 10.93 NME on unseen CariFace, again the best among all methods. Source-domain (300W) performance remains close to the oracle (3.20 vs. 3.10 Full NME), confirming that generalization to stylized faces does not come at the cost of real-face accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all five domain-adaptation baselines (RevGrad, CycleGAN, BDL, ASN, FDA) across every evaluation domain in both DA and GZSL settings. The most dramatic gains appear on the target stylized domain: on CariFace (DA setting), the method achieves 7.70 NME versus 12.11–13.63 for the baselines, a reduction of roughly 4–6 NME points. On the unseen ArtiFace domain (GZSL), it reaches 3.95 NME compared to 4.60–5.65 for the best baselines. Against the three SOTA landmark detectors (OpenPose, SPIGA, STAR) in Table 4, the method with SLPT backbone achieves 7.70 NME on CariFace DA (vs. 10.46–11.23) and 3.93 NME on ArtiFace GZSL (vs. 4.56–5.55), confirming that the learning paradigm is backbone-agnostic and improves upon strong existing detectors. Source-domain 300W performance is maintained within 0.1–0.2 NME of the oracle, whereas baselines show comparable or slightly worse source-domain accuracy, indicating the improvement is not a trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the face warping literature (DST, FoA, AutoToon, RAFT) as a supervisory signal for face landmarking, rather than treating warping as a standalone task. It departs from standard domain adaptation methods (RevGrad, CycleGAN, BDL, FDA) that align image-level features across domains, instead coupling the landmarker directly to a parametric polyharmonic interpolation warping field so that pseudo-landmarks for stylized faces are generated through geometric consistency. The alternating optimization strategy draws from classical optimization (proximal methods) to decouple the non-convex joint problem, and the choice of thin-plate spline interpolation over learned dense flow fields is a deliberate simplification that reduces the number of learnable parameters and focuses capacity on the landmarker itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AIGC applications (cartoon generation, avatar creation, artistic character design) produce ever more stylized facial imagery, a landmarker that generalizes beyond real human faces becomes practically essential, yet standard DA methods leave a large NME gap (e.g., ~12% on CariFace vs. ~5.5% oracle). The paper's key limitation is that it is validated on only three datasets with 68 landmarks and a single best-performing backbone (SLPT), leaving open whether the approach scales to 3D landmarking, higher landmark counts, or more extreme style variations. An open question is whether the pseudo-labeling signal from warping can be further refined (e.g., with iterative refinement or confidence weighting) to close the remaining gap to oracle performance on highly stylized domains.
