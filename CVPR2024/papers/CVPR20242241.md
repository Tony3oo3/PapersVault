## Problem

Self-training, a powerful paradigm for leveraging pseudo labels in closed-set object detection, has been rarely explored for open-vocabulary object detection (OVD). The paper identifies two specific failures of vanilla self-training in OVD: (1) VLMs produce pseudo boxes with poor localization quality that accumulates noise during iterative training, and (2) EMA-based teacher updates cause the pseudo-label distribution to shift every iteration, destabilizing training since OVD has no ground truth for target categories to anchor the supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242241/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector takes an RGB image and a set of category text prompts (converted to CLIP text embeddings via prompt engineering) as input, and produces bounding boxes with open-vocabulary class labels as output. The text embeddings serve directly as the classifier (replacing a standard linear head), with a fixed all-zero embedding for the background class. At inference, the final detection score for each region is a geometric-mean fusion of scores from two separate classification branches, weighted differently for base versus novel categories.

## Method

SAS-Det introduces a Split-and-Fusion (SAF) head that decomposes the standard detection head into a closed-branch (classification + box refinement, supervised only by base-category ground truth) and an open-branch (classification only, supervised by class labels from both ground truth and pseudo labels). Pseudo boxes are excluded from the box regression loss entirely, eliminating location noise, while the two branches learn complementary knowledge and are fused via a geometric mean at inference. To stabilize training, the paper replaces the standard EMA teacher update with a periodic update strategy where the teacher is refreshed only 2–3 times over the entire training schedule, keeping the pseudo-label distribution consistent between updates. An external RPN generates region proposals so that finetuning focuses on region-text alignment rather than breaking CLIP's pretrained image-text alignment.

## Results

On COCO-OVD with the same backbone (RN50-C4) and detector (Faster R-CNN), SAS-Det achieves 37.4 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ, 58.5 AP₅₀ᵇᵃˢᵉ, and 53.0 AP₅₀ᵃˡˡ, outperforming the strongest same-scale baseline BARON by 4.3 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ and 3.7 AP₅₀ᵇᵃˢᵉ. On LVIS-OVD with RN50-C4, SAS-Det reaches 20.9 APᵣ and 27.4 AP overall; scaling to RN50x4-C4 boosts APᵣ to 29.1 and AP to 33.5. The method is also ~4× faster in pseudo-label generation than PB-OVD and ~3× faster than VL-PLM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among same-scale baselines on COCO-OVD (RN50-C4, Faster R-CNN), SAS-Det wins across the board: it beats BARON (33.1→37.4 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ, +4.3) and VLDet (32.0→37.4, +5.4) on novel categories, and gains +7.9 AP₅₀ᵇᵃˢᵉ over VLDet (50.6→58.5), attributed to the SAF head's closed-branch being trained only on ground-truth boxes. Against PB-OVD (30.8 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ) and Detic (27.8), the margins are +6.6 and +9.6 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ respectively. On LVIS-OVD with RN50-C4, SAS-Det edges out BARON by +3.6 APᵣ (20.9 vs 17.3) but trails DetPro by −1.7 AP_c (26.1 vs 27.8) and −0.8 AP_f, likely because DetPro uses a stronger FPN backbone and learnable prompts. When scaled to RN50x4-C4, SAS-Det achieves 29.1 APᵣ, surpassing all listed LVIS baselines including F-VLM (26.3) and CORA (22.2). The paper also reports that SAS-Det's pseudo-labeling pipeline is ~4× faster than PB-OVD (0.13 s vs 0.48 s per image) while producing higher-quality PLs (26.7 vs 18.7 AP₅₀ⁿᵒᵛᵉˡ on validation).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP as the vision-language backbone and adapts it into a Faster R-CNN detector, following the broader OVD line of work (ViLD, F-VLM, RegionCLIP, etc.). It departs from prior pseudo-label-based OVD methods (PB-OVD, VL-PLM) that rely on handcrafted offline heuristics (Grad-CAM activation maps, multiple box-regression passes, image retrieval) by instead adopting an online teacher-student self-training loop with on-the-fly pseudo labeling. The key architectural departure is the split-and-fusion (SAF) head, which separates classification and localization supervision to isolate the student from noisy pseudo boxes, and the periodic update strategy that replaces the standard EMA teacher update inherited from semi-supervised detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that self-training, a workhorse of semi-supervised detection, can be made effective for OVD but only with careful modifications—specifically, that the standard EMA teacher update actively destabilizes OVD training because the PL distribution is entirely teacher-determined with no ground-truth anchor for novel categories. This insight generalizes to any open-set setting where supervision for target classes comes solely from a model's own predictions. Open questions include whether the periodic update schedule and SAF head design transfer to other open-set tasks (e.g., open-vocabulary segmentation or grounding), and whether the approach scales to the very large vocabularies (tens of thousands of categories) where current OVD methods struggle.
