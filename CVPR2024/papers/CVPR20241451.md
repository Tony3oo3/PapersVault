## Problem

Multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) frequently hallucinate objects, attributes, or relationships not present in the input image, and existing mitigations require costly additional training data, external knowledge sources, or auxiliary models. This paper identifies that hallucinations are closely tied to 'knowledge aggregation patterns' in the self-attention matrix—where the model over-trusts a few summary tokens and neglects the original image tokens—and asks whether this can be corrected purely at decoding time without any extra data or training.

## Input / Output

OPERA operates as a drop-in decoding strategy on standard 7B MLLMs (InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4, LLaVA-1.5, Shikra) that accept an RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'Please describe this image in detail') and produce a free-form text description. It requires no architectural modification or retraining; instead, it intercepts the beam-search decoding loop and reads the causal self-attention weights over the already-generated token window (excluding image and prompt tokens) to compute its penalty. The output is the same generated description, but with reduced hallucinated content, typically slightly shorter due to the removal of spurious details.

## Method

OPERA augments beam-search decoding with two mechanisms. First, the Over-Trust Logit Penalty extracts a local window of self-attention weights over the most recent generated tokens, scales and column-wise multiplies the lower-triangular entries to produce a score vector whose maximum quantifies the strength of a knowledge-aggregation (summary-token) pattern, and subtracts α·φ from the candidate logits so that beams exhibiting strong over-trust are deprioritized. Second, the Retrospection-Allocation strategy addresses the hysteresis problem—by the time the pattern is detectable, hallucinated tokens may already be committed—by tracking the argmax location of the column-wise scores across the last l decoded tokens; when the same location is the mode for at least r consecutive tokens, decoding rolls back to that summary-token position and reselects the next token from the complementary candidate set (excluding the previously chosen token), with a monotonicity constraint and a maximum rollback count β to prevent infinite loops.

## Results

On the CHAIR hallucination metric (max new tokens = 512, MSCOCO), OPERA achieves the lowest C_S and C_I scores across all four MLLMs. The most dramatic gain is on Shikra, where OPERA reduces C_S from 55.8 (Greedy/DoLa) to 36.2 and C_I from 15.4 to 12.1, a ~35% improvement over DoLa. On InstructBLIP, OPERA lowers C_S from 58.8 (Greedy) to 46.4 and C_I from 23.7 to 14.2. GPT-4V-assisted evaluation confirms up to 27.5% correctness improvement over Beam Search while preserving answer detailedness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPERA outperforms all three baselines (Greedy, Beam Search, DoLa) on CHAIR C_S and C_I across all four MLLMs. Against Beam Search, OPERA reduces C_S by 9.2 points on InstructBLIP (46.4 vs 55.6) and by 14.2 points on Shikra (36.2 vs 50.4). Against DoLa, the gains are most pronounced on Shikra (C_S: 36.2 vs 55.8, ~35% reduction) and InstructBLIP (C_S: 46.4 vs 48.4), while on MiniGPT-4 and LLaVA-1.5 the improvements are more modest (2–3 points on C_S). On the POPE object-probing benchmark, OPERA achieves the highest average F1 (84.8 on InstructBLIP, 73.3 on MiniGPT-4, 85.4 on LLaVA-1.5, 82.7 on Shikra) but with only marginal gains over Beam Search, which the authors attribute to POPE's short Yes/No answers not exhibiting the knowledge-aggregation patterns that OPERA targets. On GPT-4V correctness scoring, OPERA improves over Beam Search by up to 27.5% (e.g., 6.26 vs 5.52 on InstructBLIP) while maintaining comparable detailedness scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPERA builds directly on standard Beam Search decoding and introduces a penalty term derived from self-attention map analysis, positioning itself as a training-free, data-free alternative to prior hallucination mitigation approaches that rely on additional instruction data, external knowledge, or auxiliary models. Conceptually, it connects to the 'anchor token' observation in NLP, where LLMs aggregate prior context onto a few tokens at shallow layers, and extends this insight to the multi-modal setting where vision tokens are progressively neglected as text generation lengthens. The closest prior decoding-based method is DoLa, which contrasts logits across layers; OPERA instead operates on the spatial structure of attention weights within a local window, targeting the specific 'partial over-trust' pattern that the authors identify as the mechanistic cause of MLLM hallucination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By requiring no additional data, training, or external models, OPERA offers a practical 'nearly free lunch' for practitioners deploying MLLMs in safety-critical settings where hallucination is unacceptable but retraining is infeasible. The consistent improvements across four architecturally diverse MLLMs (Q-Former-based and linear-projection-based) suggest the attention-pattern mechanism is a general property rather than an artifact of a specific model. However, the marginal gains on POPE (short Yes/No answers) reveal a clear limitation: the method's effectiveness depends on sequence length and the emergence of aggregation patterns, leaving open whether it can be adapted to other decoding strategies (greedy, nucleus) or to tasks where responses are inherently brief. The retrospection-allocation rollback, while effective, introduces non-trivial computational overhead and hyperparameter sensitivity (σ, r, β) that may complicate real-world deployment.
