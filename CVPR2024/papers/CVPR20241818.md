## Problem

Sparse-view 360° reconstruction in neural radiance fields remains challenging: generalizable NeRFs require expensive pretraining and struggle to generalize across settings, while per-scene methods rely on handcrafted regularizations or external supervision (depth, VLMs) that are fragile to setting changes and unavailable in practice. Additionally, existing per-scene approaches are slow (hours of training) because they operate on the original NeRF MLP representation rather than faster factorized alternatives, and handcrafted priors cannot be easily transferred to new representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes 4–6 sparse RGB views (selected via KMeans on camera translation vectors) with known camera poses as input and produces a factorized TensoRF-VM feature volume that supports differentiable volume rendering to arbitrary novel views. It operates at resolutions from 256² up to 800², completing reconstruction in as little as 30 seconds at common 3D-generation resolutions and ~25 minutes at full 800² resolution on a single A100 GPU. During inference, only the learned feature volume is stored (not the generator network), so rendering has zero overhead compared to vanilla TensoRF.

## Method

ZeroRF replaces the directly-optimized feature grids of TensoRF-VM with a randomly-initialized deep convolutional generator network (a shrunk SD VAE decoder) that takes a single frozen Gaussian noise sample as input and outputs the 1D vectors and 2D matrices of the VM decomposition. The key insight is that under under-determined supervision from few views, neural networks generalize far better than lookup grids because they have much higher impedance to noise and ray artifacts, producing clean feature planes without any explicit regularization or pretraining. A lightweight SSDNeRF-style decoder (shared base feature code, SiLU activations, spherical harmonics for view direction, no positional encodings) maps sampled features to density and color, and the entire system is optimized with a plain MSE rendering loss over 10k AdamW iterations. The approach is representation-agnostic in principle (also validated on triplane and DiF) but TensoRF-VM yields the best empirical results.

## Results

On NeRF-Synthetic (6 views), ZeroRF achieves PSNR 24.73, SSIM 0.889, and LPIPS 0.113, outperforming the best baseline (FreeNeRF: 22.77 PSNR) by ~2 dB. On the 4-view setting, ZeroRF reaches 21.94 PSNR vs. 19.78 for the best baseline (FlipNeRF). On OpenIllumination, ZeroRF attains 26.15 PSNR and 0.080 LPIPS, surpassing DietNeRF (24.20 PSNR, 0.095 LPIPS). Notably, ZeroRF completes training in ~25 minutes versus 3–6 hours for all baselines, and even at just 2 minutes (1k iterations) it already matches or exceeds the best baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZeroRF outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both NeRF-Synthetic and OpenIllumination. The largest margins are against RegNeRF (which relies on a pretrained RealNVP normalizing flow) and DietNeRF (CLIP prior), where ZeroRF gains 11+ dB PSNR on 4-view NeRF-Synthetic; both of these pretrained-prior methods struggle with 360° wide-baseline settings. Among the handcrafted-regularization baselines, FreeNeRF (frequency regularization) and FlipNeRF (symmetry prior) are the strongest competitors on NeRF-Synthetic, but ZeroRF still leads by ~2–3 dB PSNR and substantially on LPIPS. On OpenIllumination, FreeNeRF and FlipNeRF degrade catastrophically (PSNR drops to 11.47 and training becomes numerically unstable, respectively), while ZeroRF maintains 26.15 PSNR. DietNeRF is the closest competitor on OpenIllumination (24.20 PSNR, 0.940 SSIM), but ZeroRF still wins on PSNR by ~2 dB and on LPIPS (0.080 vs. 0.095), while training ~15× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZeroRF sits at the intersection of factorized NeRF representations (TensoRF, K-planes, DiF) and the Deep Image Prior paradigm, repurposing untrained deep generator networks (specifically the Stable Diffusion VAE decoder) as a parametrization of spatial feature grids rather than as an image-level prior. It departs from the dominant sparse-view reconstruction strategy of adding handcrafted regularizations (frequency, symmetry, information-theoretic) or pretrained priors (CLIP, RealNVP) by arguing that the deep generator's inherent noise impedance implicitly regularizes the feature volume, eliminating the need for any explicit constraints. The method also builds on the SSDNeRF decoder architecture and the VM tensor decomposition from TensoRF, but the core conceptual contribution is the shift from 'optimize grids directly' to 'parametrize grids with a deep network.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30-second reconstruction time at common 3D-generation resolutions (256²/320²) versus hours for prior methods makes sparse-view 360° reconstruction practically viable in interactive and generation pipelines, as demonstrated by the text-to-3D and texture-editing applications. The finding that handcrafted priors (FreeNeRF, FlipNeRF) are fragile across datasets—performing well on NeRF-Synthetic but failing catastrophically on OpenIllumination—argues for parametrization-level solutions over loss-level ones. Key limitations include restriction to bounded scenes (unbounded extension is left as future work) and dependence on a fixed frozen noise input, whose sensitivity to scale and distribution is only briefly explored. The generalizability of the deep-prior parametrization across triplane, TensoRF, and DiF representations suggests the technique could be adopted broadly in factorized radiance field pipelines.
