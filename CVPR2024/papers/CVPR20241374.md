## Problem

Existing multi-agent trajectory prediction methods treat all vehicles in a scene equally, assuming a fixed neighbor range and constant interaction probability between the target agent and its neighbors. This ignores the heterogeneous traffic density that dynamically changes the number of interacting agents and their interaction likelihood across time steps and spatial scales, leading to poor adaptability in both high-density and low-density driving scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241374/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 2-second history trajectories for each agent (4-dimensional states: x, y, speed, heading) along with global scene features, and constructs a spatial-temporal motif matrix M_{s,t} from agent positions across multiple neighbor radii (s_1 to S) and time steps (t_1 to T). It outputs top-K diverse future trajectories (6-second horizon on nuScenes, 3-second on Argoverse), per-agent interaction probabilities, and a lane selection via a CVAE decoder. The motif matrix is the key intermediate representation that encodes higher-order (3-node subgraph) connectivity patterns rather than simple pairwise adjacency.

## Method

DAMM decomposes interaction modeling into two blocks built on a motif matrix derived from network motifs (3-node subgraph patterns). The Adaptive Spatial Interaction (ASI) block applies Spatial Pyramid Pooling over motif matrices at multiple neighbor radii, fuses them with agent features via multi-head attention, and uses statistical aggregation (max/min/median) to produce node features for a GAT, thereby adaptively selecting the optimal interaction range per agent. The Adaptive Temporal Interaction (ATI) block pools motif matrices across time steps, applies a reweighting network conditioned on global trajectory features to produce per-time-step coefficients, and outputs weighted temporal motif features as GAT edge features encoding interaction probability. A Graph Attention Network then integrates node and edge features, and a CVAE decoder generates diverse trajectories. Training uses a composite loss combining L2 distance loss, binary cross-entropy lane selection loss, and KL divergence regularization.

## Results

On nuScenes, DAMM achieves ADE₁=2.84 and FDE₁=6.59, improving ADE₁ by 19.77% over the best baseline (Context-Aware, 3.54) and FDE₁₀ by 2.38% over MUSE-VAE (2.10→2.05). On Argoverse, DAMM attains ADE₁=1.57 and FDE₁=3.42, outperforming the strongest baseline R-Pred (1.58/3.47) on both top-1 metrics, with a maximum 9.52% ADE improvement at K=6. The model also achieves ADE₁₀=1.02 and FDE₁₀=2.05 on nuScenes, surpassing MUSE-VAE (1.09/2.10) on both top-10 metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuScenes, DAMM achieves the best ADE₁ (2.84 vs. 3.54 for Context-Aware, a 19.77% reduction) and FDE₁ (6.59 vs. 6.71 for THOMAS), while also leading on ADE₁₀ (1.02 vs. 1.09 for MUSE-VAE) and FDE₁₀ (2.05 vs. 2.10). However, DAMM is slightly outperformed by MUSE-VAE on ADE₅ (1.39 vs. 1.38) and FDE₅ (3.14 vs. 2.90), indicating that the multi-scale spatial adaptation provides the largest gains for single-trajectory and top-10 predictions rather than intermediate diversity metrics. On Argoverse, DAMM wins on ADE₁ (1.57 vs. 1.58 for R-Pred) and FDE₁ (3.42 vs. 3.47), ties R-Pred on ADE₆ (both 0.76), but trails R-Pred on FDE₆ (1.29 vs. 1.12), suggesting the temporal interaction probability estimation is less effective for final-position accuracy in the Argoverse setting. Overall, DAMM secures the top position on 8 out of 10 reported metrics across both datasets, with the two losses (FDE₅ on nuScenes, FDE₆ on Argoverse) being the only instances where a baseline (MUSE-VAE or R-Pred) edges ahead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the network motif framework from complex network science (Milo et al., 2002) into multi-agent trajectory prediction, using a motif matrix to encode higher-order (3-node subgraph) connectivity among agents rather than relying on pairwise adjacency. It builds on the GNN-based trajectory prediction lineage (AgentFormer, GATraj, GroupNet) and the CVAE generative framework, but departs from these by introducing density-adaptive multi-scale neighbor selection (spatial) and dynamic interaction probability weighting (temporal), whereas prior methods typically fix the neighbor set and assume uniform interaction strength. The spatial pyramid pooling and PointNet components are borrowed from vision and point-cloud literature and applied here to aggregate motif matrix features across scales and agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The density-adaptive formulation addresses a practical gap in autonomous driving where traffic density varies spatially and temporally, and the ablation confirms that both spatial and temporal adaptation contribute meaningfully (9.89% ADE1 gain over the non-adaptive baseline). However, the approach's reliance on enumerating all 3-node subgraphs for the motif matrix raises scalability concerns in very dense scenes, and the authors acknowledge that in the highest density intervals their method underperforms Context-Aware. Open questions include whether the motif-based higher-order encoding generalizes beyond triangles, how the method scales to pedestrian-heavy or mixed-traffic scenarios, and whether the CVAE latent space can be further constrained by map/lane geometry (which the authors note as future work).
