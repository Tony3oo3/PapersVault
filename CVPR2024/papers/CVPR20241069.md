## Problem

Correlation clustering, which handles signed (positive and negative) pairwise similarities, has only been formulated for flat clustering, leaving no principled hierarchical extension. Furthermore, representation learning techniques such as tree-preserving embedding and minimax dissimilarities have not been adapted to signed similarity matrices, preventing their use in settings where pairwise relations can be both attractive and repulsive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241069/cropped_page7_idx9.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an n×n pairwise similarity or dissimilarity matrix with arbitrary real-valued (positive or negative) entries as input. It produces three types of output: a hierarchical dendrogram from HCC, a set of embedded feature vectors (via MDS on level-based ultrametric distances) suitable for downstream models like GMM, and minimax dissimilarities computed in polynomial time via connected components of a thresholded graph. The key constraint is that the HCC linkage is not shift-invariant, so the embedding must use the level function rather than linkage to maintain valid distance properties.

## Method

HCC follows the standard agglomerative procedure but defines the inter-cluster dissimilarity as the sum of all pairwise dissimilarities between two clusters (dis^CC(u,v) = Σ D_ij), a choice directly derived from the flat correlation clustering cost function. For tree-preserving embedding, the paper uses the level() function (depth of the smallest cluster containing both objects) rather than linkage(), because HCC's linkage is not shift-invariant and can yield negative values; the resulting distance matrix is ultrametric, enabling exact Euclidean embedding via classical MDS (centering + eigendecomposition). For minimax dissimilarities, the paper proves (Theorem 1) that correlation clustering on minimax similarities reduces to computing connected components of the subgraph of positive edges, transforming an NP-hard problem into a polynomial-time one, and shows (Theorem 2) that the 3-approximation algorithm of Ailon et al. becomes exact on minimax similarities.

## Results

On six UCI datasets, HCC achieves substantially higher Normalized Mutual Information (MI) than all baselines: e.g., MI=0.903 on Breast Tissue vs. 0.542 (AL), 0.227 (CL), and 0.006 (SL); MI=0.987 on Cardiotocography vs. 0.391 (AL). The tree-preserving embedding from HCC followed by GMM (HCC+GMM) further improves over HCC alone on most datasets (e.g., MI 0.914 vs. 0.903 on Breast Tissue, 0.462 vs. 0.429 on Leaf). On Fashion-MNIST, HCC+GMM achieves MI=0.693 and Rand=0.548 on subset 2, outperforming AL+GMM (MI=0.647). For minimax + correlation clustering on elongated datasets, the method achieves perfect clustering (MI=Rand=1) while correlation clustering alone yields very low scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HCC dominates all three standard agglomerative baselines (single linkage, complete linkage, average linkage) across all six UCI datasets, with the largest gaps on Breast Tissue (MI 0.903 vs. 0.542 for AL) and Cardiotocography (MI 0.987 vs. 0.391 for AL). The advantage is most pronounced for SL, which is nearly useless on these signed-similarity datasets (MI < 0.01 on most). On Fashion-MNIST, HCC still leads on subset 2 (MI 0.666 vs. AL's 0.602), though AL+GMM slightly edges out HCC+GMM on subset 1 (MI 0.608 vs. 0.581), suggesting the embedding benefit is dataset-dependent. For the minimax + correlation clustering experiments on elongated/complex-shaped datasets, the combined method achieves perfect clustering (MI=Rand=1) while correlation clustering alone produces very low scores, demonstrating that the minimax transformation is critical for capturing transitive structure. The paper also notes that HCC+GMM consistently surpasses HCC alone, validating the tree-preserving embedding as a useful intermediate representation step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends flat correlation clustering (Bansal et al., Charikar et al., Demaine et al.) to the hierarchical setting by defining a new inter-cluster dissimilarity function (dis^CC) derived from the standard correlation clustering cost, while maintaining the generic agglomerative procedure. It also adapts tree-preserving embedding (Haghir Chehreghani 2020) to HCC dendrograms, replacing the linkage-based distance with a level-based distance to handle the non-shift-invariance of dis^CC. Finally, it extends minimax dissimilarities (Fischer & Buhmann, Kim & Choi) to signed graphs, proving that correlation clustering on minimax dissimilarities reduces to computing connected components of an unweighted graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled way to perform hierarchical clustering and extract vector features from signed (positive/negative) pairwise similarities, enabling downstream methods like GMM that were previously inapplicable to such data. The reduction of correlation clustering on minimax dissimilarities from NP-hard to polynomial time is a clean theoretical contribution with practical implications for elongated-cluster extraction without hyperparameter tuning. Limitations include evaluation only on relatively small UCI datasets and Fashion-MNIST subsets, and the HCC method's performance degrades on datasets with many small clusters (Leaf, One-Hundred Plant). Open questions include scalability to large-scale signed graphs and whether the level-based distance can be further refined to capture richer structural information than linkage.
