## Problem

Language-based object detectors require discriminative training with both positive and negative samples, but the free-form nature of object descriptions makes the space of negatives extremely large. Prior approaches either randomly sample negatives from other images or use rule-based word foils, producing negatives that are semantically unrelated to the original description and thus provide a weak training signal. This paper addresses the gap by automatically generating negatives that are semantically relevant yet contradicting to the original data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240845/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector receives an RGB image and a variable-length list of free-form text descriptions (e.g., 'the red sports car parked on the left side') and outputs bounding boxes with per-token confidence scores for each description. The setting is multi-label: one object instance can be matched by multiple descriptions, and a description may refer to zero objects, in which case the correct output is an empty box set. During training, the generated negatives are concatenated with the original positive description into a single text string (shuffled to avoid positional bias), and optionally the generated negative images are concatenated with the original image as a single input.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, negative text descriptions are generated by prompting GPT-3.5 to produce 50k positive-negative pairs using three strategies—LLM-based foils (changing one identified concept), re-combination (re-arranging, ignoring, or adding objects), and in-context summary (learning the negative-generation pattern from Winoground pairs)—then fine-tuning LLaMA-7B on those pairs for large-scale generation. Second, negative images are produced by masking the target phrase in the caption, filling the mask with a contradicting phrase via the fine-tuned LLM, and using GLIGEN's inpainting to alter only the content within the bounding box. Two filtering steps reduce noise: a box filter discards generations where the target box covers >75% of another box, and a CLIP similarity filter removes images whose generated region does not semantically match the negative text. The resulting negatives are integrated into the focal-loss training objective of GLIP or FIBER by appending negative descriptions to the text input and optionally concatenating or adding negative images as additional training samples.

## Results

On the OmniLabel benchmark, adding generated negative data improves GLIP-T from 19.3 to 22.2 AP (+2.9) and FIBER-B from 25.7 to 28.1 AP (+2.4). On the D3 benchmark, the same approach yields +2.3 AP for GLIP-T (19.1→21.4) and +3.3 AP for FIBER-B (22.7→26.0). On the OmniLabel-Negative split (evaluating only on negative descriptions), FIBER-B+Ours achieves 22.3 AP versus 18.7 for the original, confirming the gains are driven by improved negative discrimination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's generated negatives are integrated into two strong language-based detectors, GLIP-T and FIBER-B, and consistently outperform their unmodified versions across all metrics on both OmniLabel and D3. On D3, FIBER-B+Ours (26.0 Full AP) surpasses all prior baselines: G-DINO-B (20.7), OFA-DOD (21.6), OWL-ViT-L (9.6), and OFA-L (4.2). On OmniLabel, both GLIP-T+Ours (22.2 AP) and FIBER-B+Ours (28.1 AP) substantially exceed MDETR (4.7 APd / 9.1 APdP) and Detic (8.0 AP). The gains are particularly pronounced on the OmniLabel-Negative split, where FIBER-B+Ours reaches 22.3 AP versus 18.7 for the baseline, indicating the method specifically strengthens the model's ability to reject non-matching descriptions rather than merely improving positive localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on negative sample generation for language-based object detection, building directly on GLIP and FIBER as base detectors. Prior approaches to negatives in this setting relied on rule-based word substitution (foils via knowledge graphs) or simple LLM-based word replacement (e.g., DesCo), while SugarCrepe explored in-context learning for image-text pretraining. The key departure here is twofold: (1) more comprehensive LLM prompting strategies (re-combination of objects, in-context summary from Winoground pairs) that go beyond single-word foils, and (2) the use of text-to-image diffusion models (GLIGEN) to generate corresponding negative images, which no prior detection-focused work attempted. The paper also builds on the OmniLabel and D3 benchmarks for evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the quality and semantic relevance of negative samples matter for language-based detection, and that LLM-generated negatives consistently outperform rule-based foils across multiple metrics. A notable limitation is the domain gap finding: generated negative images improve OmniLabel performance but not D3, because D3's category-grouped data distribution differs from the scattered distribution of generated images. Open questions include whether the image-generation component becomes more useful as T2I models improve, whether the approach transfers to other vision-language tasks (segmentation, grounding), and whether the modest gains (+2–3 AP) scale with larger models or more diverse training data. The practical recipe for integrating generated negatives into existing detectors (FIBER, GLIP) without architectural changes is a useful contribution for practitioners.
