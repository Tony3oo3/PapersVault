## Problem

LoFTR, a detector-free semi-dense matcher, achieves strong matching accuracy in texture-poor and large-viewpoint-change scenarios but suffers from low efficiency due to performing transformer attention over the entire coarse feature map. This computational bottleneck severely limits its applicability in large-scale or latency-sensitive tasks such as image retrieval and 3D reconstruction. Additionally, LoFTR's fine correlation refinement introduces spatial variance that degrades subpixel matching accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242026/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images and produces semi-dense correspondences with subpixel-level accuracy. Coarse features are extracted at 1/8 resolution, while fine features are fused at 1/4 and 1/2 resolutions to recover full-resolution patches (8×8) centered at each coarse match. The efficiency-optimized variant skips the dual-softmax operator at inference, directly using the raw score matrix for mutual-nearest-neighbor matching, reducing processing time from ~40 ms to ~27 ms per image pair at 640×480.

## Method

The core contribution is an aggregated attention module that reduces the token count before attention by aggregating neighboring query tokens via a strided depth-wise convolution and selecting salient key tokens via max-pooling (both 4×4), then applying vanilla attention with RoPE positional encoding on the reduced set. This avoids the representational loss of linear attention while cutting computation by a factor of s². For refinement, a two-stage correlation layer first performs MNN matching on fine feature patches to obtain pixel-level matches (eliminating spatial variance), then refines to subpixel accuracy by correlating within a tiny 3×3 window. The backbone uses RepVGG reparameterization (multi-branch during training, fused single-branch at inference), and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on MegaDepth with separate log-likelihood losses for coarse and first-stage fine matching plus an L2 loss for subpixel refinement.

## Results

On MegaDepth, the full model achieves AUC@5°=56.4, AUC@10°=72.2, AUC@20°=83.5 for relative pose estimation, outperforming the best semi-dense baseline AspanFormer (55.3/71.5/83.1) while running ~2× faster (40.1 ms vs 81.6 ms). The efficiency-optimized variant (skipping dual-softmax at inference) reaches 27.0 ms in mixed precision, surpassing the sparse pipeline SuperPoint+LightGlue (30.7 ms) in speed while delivering substantially higher accuracy (55.4 vs 49.9 AUC@5° on MegaDepth). On ScanNet, the method achieves 19.2/37.0/53.6 AUC@5°/10°/20°, comparable to AspanFormer (19.6/37.7/54.4) at roughly half the latency. Overall, the method is ~2.5× faster than LoFTR (40.1 vs 66.2 ms) and ~7.5× faster than the dense matcher ROMA (40.1 vs 302.7 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoFTR, the method improves MegaDepth AUC@5° from 52.8 to 56.4 (+3.6) and ScanNet AUC@5° from 16.9 to 19.2 (+2.3) while reducing inference time from 66.2 ms to 40.1 ms (~2.5× speedup). Compared to the strongest semi-dense competitor AspanFormer, the method wins on all three MegaDepth metrics (56.4 vs 55.3 AUC@5°) but trails slightly on ScanNet (19.2 vs 19.6 AUC@5°), while running ~2× faster (40.1 vs 81.6 ms). The optimized variant (27.0 ms mixed-precision) surpasses the sparse pipeline SuperPoint+LightGlue (30.7 ms) in speed while achieving 5.5 points higher AUC@5° on MegaDepth (55.4 vs 49.9) and 2.6 points higher on ScanNet (17.4 vs 14.8). Against the dense matcher ROMA, the method sacrifices ~6 AUC@5° points on MegaDepth (56.4 vs 62.6) but is ~7.5× faster (40.1 vs 302.7 ms). On homography estimation (HPatches), the method outperforms LoFTR (66.5 vs 65.9 AUC@3px) and all sparse baselines (best: SP+SG at 53.9). For visual localization on InLoc DUC2, the method achieves 58.0/80.9/89.3 at (0.25m,2°)/(0.5m,5°)/(1.0m,10°), matching or exceeding AspanFormer (55.0/74.0/81.7) and PATS (58.8/80.9/85.5) at the 1.0m threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct efficiency-focused revision of LoFTR (Sun et al., CVPR 2021), the seminal detector-free transformer matcher. While prior follow-ups (AspanFormer, QuadTree, MatchFormer, TopicFM) pursued accuracy gains through multi-scale or topic-based attention, this work identifies two specific inefficiencies in LoFTR's design—redundant global attention over locally similar tokens and spatial variance in the correlation refinement—and addresses them with an aggregated attention module (depthwise conv + max-pool token reduction followed by vanilla attention with RoPE) and a two-stage MNN-then-local-expectation refinement. The backbone is also swapped from LoFTR's multi-branch ResNet to a RepVGG-style reparameterized single-branch network, trading training-time flexibility for inference speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making semi-dense matching roughly 2.5× faster than LoFTR and competitive with the sparse LightGlue pipeline in speed while retaining better accuracy, this work removes a key practical barrier to deploying detector-free matchers in latency-sensitive or large-scale settings such as SfM, image retrieval, and SLAM. The observation that dual-softmax can be dropped at inference with minimal accuracy loss, and that local attention redundancy permits aggressive token reduction without sacrificing (and even improving) matching quality, are reusable insights for future transformer-based matching architectures. Open questions include whether the aggregated attention generalizes to higher-resolution or video matching, and whether the outdoor-only MegaDepth training limits indoor robustness relative to methods leveraging pre-trained backbones like DINOv2 (as ROMA does).
