## Problem

Monocular 3D object detection is inherently ill-posed and existing methods rely on expensive 3D annotations from LiDAR point clouds. Prior weakly supervised approaches either still require LiDAR supervision, synthetic data for shape estimation, or 2D direction annotations, limiting their applicability. This paper asks whether 3D bounding boxes can be recovered purely from 2D instance masks and bounding boxes across multi-view video, with no 3D supervision whatsoever.

## Input / Output

The auto-labeling stage consumes a monocular video of posed frames (16 source frames sampled per target frame), per-pixel 2D instance masks, and 2D bounding boxes, along with camera extrinsic and intrinsic matrices. It outputs per-instance 3D bounding boxes parameterized by dimension (R+³), location (R³), and bird's-eye-view orientation (R), each accompanied by a confidence score in [0,1]. These pseudo labels then serve as 3D supervision for a standard monocular 3D detector (e.g., MonoDETR, MonoFlex, S-WeakM3D), which at inference takes a single RGB image and produces 3D bounding boxes.

## Method

VSRD is a two-stage framework: (1) multi-view 3D auto-labeling that optimizes 3D bounding boxes by rendering, and (2) training a monocular 3D detector on the resulting pseudo labels with confidence-weighted regression loss. The core rendering mechanism is an instance-aware volumetric silhouette rendering that integrates one-hot instance labels along each ray using an SDF-based opacity formulation (from NeuS), where the scene SDF is the minimum over all instance SDFs to handle occlusion. Each instance SDF is decomposed into an analytic cuboid SDF (parameterized by the 3D box) plus a learned residual distance field (RDF) that models the spatial gap between the true object surface and the bounding box; the RDF is a small MLP whose weights are regressed by a shared hypernetwork from a per-instance embedding, with Softplus activation enforcing non-negativity. Optimization minimizes a combination of multi-view projection loss (Huber + DIoU between projected and GT 2D boxes), multi-view silhouette loss (cross-entropy between rendered and GT instance masks over 1000 sampled rays), and Eikonal regularization, using the Hungarian algorithm for 3D-to-2D matching. A confidence score per instance, computed as the mean multi-view IoU between projected and GT 2D boxes, is used to weight the regression loss during detector training, down-weighting unreliable labels for dynamic, occluded, or truncated objects.

## Results

On the KITTI-360 test set, VSRD with MonoDETR as the backbone achieves 58.40/50.86 AP_BEV/AP_3D@0.3 (Easy) and 29.07/21.77 AP_BEV/AP_3D@0.5 (Easy) using only 2D instance masks as supervision, approaching the fully supervised MonoDETR (63.07/60.49 and 47.21/41.01). With MonoFlex, VSRD reaches 54.40/48.16 AP_BEV/AP_3D@0.3 (Easy), closing the gap to fully supervised MonoFlex (69.70/67.07) to within ~15 points. In the semi-supervised setting on KITTI, fine-tuning on just 50% of labeled data after weakly supervised pre-training on KITTI-360 yields 43.44/31.05 AP_BEV/AP_3D@0.7 (Easy), surpassing training on 100% of labeled data from scratch (37.99/29.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSRD substantially outperforms WeakM3D [23], the prior weakly supervised method requiring LiDAR, by ~28.5 points in AP_BEV@0.3 Easy (58.40 vs 29.89) while eliminating the need for LiDAR entirely. Against Autolabels [33], the closest competing auto-labeling pipeline, VSRD (MonoDETR) achieves 58.40/50.86 vs 48.16/12.92 AP_BEV/AP_3D@0.3 Easy, showing a dramatic improvement in 3D accuracy (50.86 vs 12.92) while also surpassing in BEV. Compared to fully supervised MonoDETR [34], VSRD (MonoDETR) closes the gap to within 4.7 points on AP_BEV@0.3 Easy (58.40 vs 63.07) and 9.6 points on AP_3D@0.3 Easy (50.86 vs 60.49), using only 2D masks. Similarly, VSRD (MonoFlex) reaches 54.40/48.16 vs MonoFlex's fully supervised 69.70/67.07, a gap of ~15 points. The method is detector-agnostic: swapping the backbone from S-WeakM3D to MonoFlex or MonoDETR consistently improves results, confirming the pseudo labels transfer well across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SDF-based volume rendering formulation from NeuS and the hierarchical sampling strategy from NeRF, repurposing them not for novel view synthesis but for generating pseudo 3D bounding boxes from 2D instance masks. It departs from prior weakly supervised 3D detection methods (WeakM3D, VS3D) that still require LiDAR point clouds or 3D alignment losses, and from Autolabels [33] which uses SDF-based differentiable rendering but depends on synthetic data and NOCS coordinate estimation. The key conceptual departure is the SDF decomposition into a parametric cuboid SDF plus a learned residual distance field (RDF) via a hypernetwork, combined with instance-aware silhouette rendering that integrates instance labels rather than colors along rays, enabling end-to-end optimization of 3D boxes from purely 2D supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive LiDAR-based 3D annotation, VSRD offers a practical pathway to scaling monocular 3D detection to larger datasets, and the confidence-weighted loss mechanism provides a simple, general-purpose way to handle pseudo-label quality that could transfer to other auto-labeling pipelines. However, evaluation is restricted to the Car category on KITTI-360, and the method requires posed multi-view video, limiting immediate applicability to single-image or unposed settings. Open questions include whether the RDF hypernetwork generalizes across diverse object categories (pedestrians, cyclists), whether the approach extends to datasets without reliable camera poses, and whether the pseudo-label quality gap versus fully supervised methods narrows with larger-scale training.
