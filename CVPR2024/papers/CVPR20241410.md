## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation methods either require 3D training data (point clouds) or rely on naive multi-view feature averaging, both of which severely limit generalizability across unseen scenes and datasets. No prior work achieves a generalizable neural implicit representation that simultaneously encodes scene geometry and open-vocabulary semantics without per-scene optimization, depth priors, or ground truth semantic labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241410/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a set of posed RGB images from an unseen scene (30 reference views during training, 95–100 during testing) and arbitrary text queries as input, requiring no point clouds, depth maps, or semantic annotations. It produces per-pixel 2D semantic segmentation masks rendered from novel views, per-point 3D semantic segmentation for any query point cloud, and rendered novel-view color images. The open-vocabulary features are d-dimensional per-pixel vectors extracted by a frozen LSeg (ViT-L/16) model, and segmentation is performed via argmax over cosine similarity between rendered OV features and text embeddings.

## Method

GOV-NeSF builds a 3D cost volume by back-projecting 2D features (from a ResUNet-34 encoder) into voxel space, which a 3D U-Net processes into geometry-aware volume features. The key innovation is the Multi-view Joint Fusion (MJF) module: instead of directly regressing colors or OV features in 3D space, it predicts view-specific blending weights via a FusionNet that combines queried 2D features with volume features, a Cross-View Attention (CVA) module for inter-view information exchange, and separate MLPs for color (source-view-dependent via relative ray direction encoding) and OV (source-view-agnostic) weights. Both colors and OV features are then blended as weighted sums across views and accumulated along rays via standard NeRF volume rendering with a shared density field. Training uses only RGB supervision (L2 color loss) and OV feature cosine similarity loss, with gradients from the OV loss detached from the density field to preserve rendering quality.

## Results

On ScanNet, GOV-NeSF achieves 52.2 mIoU / 73.8 oAcc / 62.2 mAcc for 2D open-vocabulary semantic segmentation from novel views, outperforming S-Ray-OV (33.9 mIoU) by +18.3 and the Distill Baseline (46.4 mIoU) by +5.8. On the cross-dataset Replica benchmark, the advantage is dramatic: 44.3 mIoU vs. 9.7 for S-Ray-OV and 5.0 for the Distill Baseline, while remaining competitive with LSeg_gt (52.0 mIoU) without any fine-tuning. For 3D open-vocabulary segmentation without depth, GOV-NeSF reaches 45.7 mIoU on ScanNet and 32.8 mIoU on Replica, surpassing OpenScene-2D w/o Depth (42.1 / 31.2) and OpenScene-3D (51.6 / 3.3). With Depth-Guided Masking, 3D performance further improves to 53.5 mIoU (ScanNet) and 37.7 mIoU (Replica).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the fully-supervised baselines (MVSNeRF, NeuRay, S-Ray with semantic heads), GOV-NeSF is competitive on ScanNet (52.2 vs. 57.2 mIoU for S-Ray) but substantially outperforms them on Replica (44.3 vs. 41.6 for S-Ray), demonstrating superior cross-dataset generalizability without any 3D supervision. Among open-vocabulary methods, GOV-NeSF dominates: it beats S-Ray-OV by +18.3 mIoU on ScanNet and +34.6 mIoU on Replica, and outperforms the Distill Baseline (which directly regresses OV features) by +5.8 and +39.3 mIoU respectively, confirming that learned multi-view blending is critical. For 3D segmentation, GOV-NeSF (w/o depth) surpasses OpenScene-2D w/o Depth by +3.6 mIoU on ScanNet and +1.6 on Replica, while OpenScene-3D collapses to 3.3 mIoU on Replica versus GOV-NeSF's 32.8. With depth available (DGM), GOV-NeSF further improves to 53.5 mIoU on ScanNet, exceeding OpenScene-2D w/ Depth (52.2) by +1.3. On novel view synthesis, GOV-NeSF achieves 21.5 PSNR on both datasets, matching NeuRay (22.8/18.1) and S-Ray (22.0/18.0) without using ground-truth depth during training, and outperforming NeRF-Det (18.8/13.5) by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOV-NeSF extends the generalizable NeRF lineage (IBRNet, PixelNeRF, S-Ray) by replacing direct regression of semantic features with a learned multi-view blending mechanism, and extends the open-vocabulary neural field line (LERF, VL-Fields, Open-NeRF) by eliminating per-scene optimization. It departs from OpenScene's approach of naively averaging multi-view OV features and distilling them into a 3D encoder, instead using a Multi-view Joint Fusion module with Cross-View Attention to predict view-specific blending weights supervised by novel-view rendering. The key conceptual shift is preserving the generalizability of 2D VLMs (LSeg/CLIP) by learning *how* to combine their outputs rather than *what* 3D representation to distill them into.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that direct distillation of 2D VLM features into 3D models catastrophically undermines cross-dataset generalizability (Replica mIoU drops to 5.0 for the Distill Baseline), while learning blending weights in a neural field framework preserves it. This provides a principled alternative to the dominant distillation paradigm for transferring VLM knowledge to 3D. Limitations include restriction to room-scale scenes, reliance on relatively sparse posed inputs (30–100 images), and color rendering quality that lags depth-supervised methods. Open questions include scalability to outdoor/large-scale scenes, whether the blending formulation extends to other VLM-grounded tasks (detection, grounding), and whether the approach can reduce the number of input views further.
