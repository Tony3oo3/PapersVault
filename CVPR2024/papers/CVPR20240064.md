## Problem

Existing 6D pose estimation methods either require per-object or per-category retraining, or rely on computationally expensive render-and-compare strategies for unseen objects. The key gap is estimating the 6D pose of a completely novel object—whose CAD model is only available at inference time—from a single RGB-D image, without retraining, while maintaining both accuracy and real-time speed. Prior descriptor-based approaches also neglect RGB texture, introducing geometric ambiguities that degrade matching quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unseen CAD model as a point cloud P (n points), a partial depth point cloud Q (m points) extracted from the depth channel, and an RGB image crop K of the localized object. Object localization is handled externally via a pre-trained CAD-based segmentation network, so the pose estimator receives a cropped, segmented view. The output is a 6D pose T ∈ SE(3) selected from η ranked hypotheses (default 20, up to 64 for accuracy), each generated by RANSAC over top-κ correspondences and scored by combined 3D point-to-point and RGB render-and-compare verification.

## Method

MatchU follows a Fuse-Describe-Match pipeline: RoITr extracts rotation-invariant 3D superpoint descriptors from both the CAD and depth point clouds, while a modified FPN encoder produces 2D superpixel features from the RGB crop. The core architectural contribution is the Latent Fusion Attention Module, which fuses 3D and 2D features in a shared latent space via bidirectional cross-attention (3D-to-2D and 2D-to-3D blocks) using linear attention for efficiency, with pose-agnostic Point Pair Features serving as 3D positional encodings to preserve rotation invariance. Training is guided by a Bridged Coarse-level Matching Loss that uses RGB superpixel features as a bridge to unify the CAD and depth latent spaces via Circle Loss on positive/negative superpoint pairs, combined with a fine-level matching loss solved through Sinkhorn optimal transport. This design inherently captures object symmetries without explicit annotations and resolves geometric ambiguities through texture, enabling zero-shot generalization to unseen objects trained only on the GSO dataset.

## Results

On the BOP-5 unseen object pose estimation benchmark (setting c: unseen localization with ICP refinement), MatchU (Accurate) achieves a mean Average Recall (AR) of 64.7, surpassing MegaPose (63.9), ZeroPose (50.9), and OSOP (52.7). In the fastest setting (a: single hypothesis, no refinement), MatchU (Fast) reaches 50.5 mean AR in just 0.07 s per inference, compared to 25.7 for ZeroPose (0.30 s) and 17.6 for MegaPose (2.50 s). On the seen-object LM-O dataset (Table 2), MatchU trained without test objects achieves 70.8 ADD-0.1d with 20 hypotheses, outperforming seen-object methods PVN3D (63.2) and FFB6D (66.2), though trailing DFTr (77.7) which was trained on the test objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MatchU outperforms all unseen-object baselines on the full BOP-5 benchmark (setting c): it beats ZeroPose by +13.8 AR (64.7 vs 50.9), OSOP by +12.0 AR, PPF+ZePHyR by +9.0 AR, and DrostPPF by +21.1 AR, while also being faster than ZeroPose (5.60 s vs 6.75 s) and MegaPose (5.60 s vs 10.70 s). Against MegaPose (BOP), MatchU leads by a narrow +0.8 AR margin (64.7 vs 63.9) but with roughly half the inference time. In the seen-localization setting (d), MatchU (66.9 mean AR over 5 datasets) surpasses OVE6D (57.5) by +9.4 AR; GCPose reports a higher mean (75.2) but only over 3 of 5 datasets, so the comparison is not directly comparable. On the seen-object LM-O task (Table 2), MatchU trained without test objects (70.8 ADD-0.1d) exceeds PVN3D (63.2) and FFB6D (66.2) but falls short of DFTr (77.7), which was trained on the test objects. In the fastest single-hypothesis setting (a), MatchU (Fast) achieves 50.5 AR in 0.07 s, dramatically outperforming ZeroPose (25.7 AR, 0.30 s) and MegaPose (17.6 AR, 2.50 s) in both accuracy and speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatchU builds directly on RoITr's rotation-invariant transformer backbone for 3D descriptor learning and extends it with a novel Latent Fusion Attention Module that integrates RGB texture features via cross-attention in latent space, departing from prior RGB-D fusion methods (DenseFusion, FFB6D, DFTr) that interpolate features based on explicit spatial alignment. It also departs from pure 3D descriptor approaches for unseen objects (ZeroPose, GCPose) that either ignore color or require explicit symmetry annotations, and from render-and-compare methods (MegaPose, ZePHyR) that are computationally expensive. The Bridged Coarse-level Matching Loss is a new training signal that uses RGB latent features as a bridge to unify the CAD and depth descriptor spaces, a mechanism absent from prior point-cloud registration or pose estimation losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MatchU closes a significant gap between seen-object and unseen-object pose estimation (64.7 AR vs. 70.5 for seen-object baselines) while being substantially faster than render-and-compare methods (5.6s vs. 10.7s), making it more practical for real-time robotic manipulation. The ability to capture both continuous and discrete symmetries without explicit annotations, purely through rotation-invariant descriptor design, removes a practical bottleneck in deploying pose estimation to novel objects. A key limitation is the reliance on external object localization (CAD-based segmentation or trained detectors), and the authors note that end-to-end integration of localization into the pipeline remains open. The generalizability from synthetic GSO training to real BOP test datasets without per-object retraining is a strong practical signal for scalable deployment.
