## Problem

Existing training-free model merging methods rely on unit matching in a single space—either weight space or activation space—but these two spaces exhibit inconsistent similarity patterns, leading to suboptimal unit pairings and degraded multi-task performance. This inconsistency is particularly harmful when merging models from different pretraining initializations, where weight similarity alone cannot capture feature-level alignment and activation similarity alone cannot capture structural connectivity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241972/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires the full weight tensors of N same-architecture pretrained models plus a small batch of sampled data to compute layer-wise activations (used for batch normalization reset and activation collection). A scalar balance factor α ∈ [0,1] controls the trade-off between weight-space and activation-space similarity; in practice it is tuned per model pair on training data for homogeneous tasks or fixed at 0.5 for heterogeneous tasks. The output is a single set of merged weights that can be loaded directly into the original architecture to perform all tasks simultaneously, with no additional training or fine-tuning.

## Method

MuDSC (Merging under Dual-Space Constraints) formulates unit matching as maximizing a linear combination of the activation-space and weight-space similarity matrices, proving this is equivalent to jointly optimizing objectives in both spaces. The algorithm initializes permutations via activation-based matching, then iteratively refines them: at each iteration it computes weight-space representations for each layer (concatenating current-layer weights permuted by adjacent-layer permutations), constructs the combined similarity matrix α·C(Z_l) + (1−α)·C(A_l), and re-solves the assignment problem. This iterative scheme resolves the circular dependency where valid weight-space representations require neighboring layers to already be optimally matched. The paper further extends both alignment-based (Git Rebasin-style) and zip-based (Zipit-style) matching to handle group structures such as Group Normalization and Multi-Head Attention via a two-level group alignment strategy that first aligns units within groups and then permutes groups externally.

## Results

On the Taskonomy heterogeneous-task benchmark (12 tasks), MuDSC_Zip achieves a total average scaled performance of 84.84, surpassing Zipit (83.86) and Weight Zip (78.09), while MuDSC_Align reaches 77.37, outperforming Activation Align (76.64) and Git Rebasin (61.10). On CIFAR-100 with randomly initialized ResNet-20-GN, MuDSC_Align achieves 31.84% joint accuracy, a 2.47-point improvement over Activation Align (29.37%) and a 17.99-point improvement over Git Rebasin (13.85%). On ImageNet-400 (200+200 classes) with DINO-pretrained ResNet-50, MuDSC_Align attains 44.87% joint accuracy, marginally exceeding all baselines (44.85–44.86%). The largest per-task gains appear in Segment Semantic (+2.69 for MuDSC_Align, +2.45 for MuDSC_Zip) and Autoencoding (+2.37, +1.45 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Git Rebasin [1] (alignment-based, weight-space only), MuDSC_Align dominates across all settings: on Taskonomy it achieves 77.37 vs. 61.10 total average scaled performance, and on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-20-GN it reaches 31.84% joint accuracy vs. Rebasin's 13.85%. Against Zipit [43] (zip-based, activation-space only), MuDSC_Zip wins on 75% of Taskonomy tasks with a total average of 84.84 vs. 83.86, and on CIFAR-100 with ResNet-20 it achieves 56.01% joint accuracy vs. Zipit's 54.69%. The largest individual gains over the best single-space baseline occur in Segment Semantic (+2.69 for MuDSC_Align over Act. Align; +2.45 for MuDSC_Zip over Weight Zip) and Autoencoding (+2.37, +1.45). On the harder heterogeneous-task setting (Taskonomy), MuDSC_Align maintains or surpasses the best single-space method in 83% of tasks, while MuDSC_Zip does so in 75%. On the large-scale ImageNet-400 experiment, gains are marginal (44.87 vs. 44.85–44.86), suggesting that when models share a strong DINO pre-training, the dual-space constraint provides diminishing but consistent benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free model merging line initiated by Git Rebasin (weight-space alignment) and Zipit (activation-space zip), identifying that both rely on a single similarity space and can produce inconsistent unit matches. The key conceptual departure is the observation that weight similarity and activation similarity are not always aligned, and that the best merged units exhibit high similarity in both spaces simultaneously. MuDSC formalizes this by linearly combining the two similarity matrices into a unified matching objective, and further adapts the matching to group-structured architectures (Multi-Head Attention, Group Normalization) that prior unit-matching methods cannot handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-space constraint provides a principled, training-free way to merge models from different pre-training initializations, removing the restrictive 'same backbone' assumption of task-arithmetic methods. Practically, the group-structure adaptation makes unit matching applicable to modern ViT and GN-based architectures, broadening the method's utility. Limitations include the need to search the balance factor α per model pair, the requirement of training data to compute activations, and the acknowledged simplification in the group-zip strategy (no principled grouping algorithm is proposed). Open questions include whether the iterative convergence guarantees hold in practice for very deep networks and whether the approach extends naturally to merging more than two models or to heterogeneous architectures.
