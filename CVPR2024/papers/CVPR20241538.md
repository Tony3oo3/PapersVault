## Problem

Existing text-to-3D generative methods (e.g., SDS-based approaches like TECA) can only recover the outer visible surface of hair as meshes, point clouds, or NeRFs, lacking meaningful internal strand structure. This makes the output incompatible with physics-based rendering and simulation pipelines used in production graphics engines. No prior method could generate strand-based hairstyles directly from text descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241538/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing hairstyle attributes (length, curliness, style, parting) and produces a 3D strand-based hairstyle of up to 100,000 individual polylines, directly importable into engines like Blender or Unreal. Internally, the diffusion model operates on a 32×32 downsampled latent hair map (each pixel encoding one strand's VAE latent code in a Frenet-frame local basis), which is then upsampled to 512×512 via a learned blending scheme. Generation completes in ~4.3 seconds, orders of magnitude faster than SDS-based alternatives requiring hours.

## Method

HAAR combines a VAE that encodes individual strand geometry (expressed in the Frenet frame at the strand root) into a low-dimensional latent code, with a 2D U-Net diffusion model (EDM formulation) that generates a latent hair texture map on a 256×256 scalp UV space conditioned on BLIP text embeddings via cross-attention. Training data consists of 9,825 hairstyles assembled from 40 artist-created models plus CT2Hair and USC-HairSalon, augmented with geometric transformations; text annotations are automatically generated by a custom VQA pipeline using LLaVA with predefined multi-choice prompts to mitigate hallucination. A key contribution is a latent-space upsampling scheme that blends nearest-neighbor and bilinear interpolation based on cosine similarity of neighboring strand directions (via a piecewise nonlinear function), followed by Gaussian noise injection, to produce dense, realistic strand distributions without manual per-hairstyle tuning. The model is trained with soft Min-SNR weighting and cosine noise scheduling for ~160K iterations on a single A100.

## Results

On the unconditional diffusion quality comparison (Table 1), HAAR achieves the best MMD (21,104.9), highest coverage (0.20), and lowest FID (36.9) among all methods, outperforming the re-trained Neural Haircut baseline (MMD 31,507.7, FID 75.5) by a wide margin. In a user study with over 1,200 responses comparing HAAR and Neural Haircut hairstyle pairs, HAAR was preferred in 87.5% of cases. For conditional generation, HAAR produces strand-based hairstyles in approximately 4.3 seconds, whereas TECA requires multiple hours due to Score Distillation Sampling.

**Table 1: Comparison of unconditional diffusion models on hairstyle generation quality.**

| Method | MMD ↓ | COV ↑ | 1-NNA → 0.5 | FID ↓ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neural Haircut [43] | 31,507.7 | 0.18 | 0.34 | 75.5 |
| NH++ | 29,329.4 | 0.14 | 0.48 | 42.6 |
| HAAR w/o cosine | 27,410.6 | 0.08 | 0.55 | 62.1 |
| HAAR w/o SNR | 21,499.2 | 0.19 | 0.56 | 44.7 |
| **HAAR** | **21,104.9** | **0.20** | **0.55** | **36.9** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Neural Haircut [43], HAAR wins on all four quantitative metrics: MMD improves from 31,507.7 to 21,104.9 (a 33% reduction), coverage increases from 0.18 to 0.20, 1-NNA improves from 0.34 to 0.55 (closer to the ideal 0.5), and FID drops from 75.5 to 36.9. The user study further confirms this with 87.5% preference for HAAR over Neural Haircut across 1,200+ responses. Against TECA [48], the comparison is primarily qualitative since TECA produces NeRF-based volumetric hair rather than strand geometry; HAAR generates physically plausible strand-based assets in ~4.3 seconds versus TECA's multi-hour SDS optimization, and produces more diverse and text-faithful results (e.g., TECA fails to generate long hair for simple prompts). HAAR's strand output is directly compatible with rendering and simulation engines (Blender, Unreal), whereas TECA's NeRF output requires additional processing for downstream use.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAAR extends the unconditional strand-based hair diffusion model from Neural Haircut by introducing text conditioning via cross-attention, making it the first text-driven generator that outputs physically simulatable 3D hair strands. It departs from SDS-based text-to-3D methods (e.g., TECA, DreamFusion) that rely on 2D image priors and can only recover the outer visible surface of hair as NeRFs or meshes, by instead operating directly in a strand latent space. The automated annotation pipeline repurposes VQA systems (LLaVA) with a custom multi-question prompting strategy to generate training captions, a technique borrowed from the broader VLM-assisted data labeling literature but adapted specifically for 3D hair geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HAAR bridges a practical gap between computer vision (text-to-3D generation) and computer graphics (strand-based rendering and physics simulation), producing assets that can be directly imported into engines like Blender or Unreal without manual re-authoring. Key limitations remain: the training set of ~10K hairstyles constrains diversity, the method generates geometry only (no color or texture), and extreme styles (e.g., afro) suffer from insufficient curliness and scalp interpenetrations. Open questions include whether the VQA-based annotation pipeline scales to larger, more diverse datasets, whether adding appearance channels is feasible within the same latent framework, and whether the approach generalizes to other strand-based assets (beards, eyebrows, animal fur).
