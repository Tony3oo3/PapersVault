## Problem

Existing single-image 3D clothed human reconstruction methods struggle to simultaneously achieve detailed geometry and robustness to noisy parametric body model estimates. Methods like PIFu produce overly smoothed shapes, ECON requires expensive post-processing with misalignment risks, and parametric-regularized methods (PaMIR, ICON, D-IF) degrade significantly when SMPL-X shape or pose estimation is inaccurate. The paper identifies that high-frequency and low-frequency information from the parametric model can respectively enhance details and improve robustness, but how to effectively combine them remains an open question.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and the corresponding SMPL-X parametric body model estimated from that image. The SMPL-X mesh is converted to a 32×32×32 low-resolution voxel grid via mesh voxelization and encoded by a 3D CNN, while its SDF is computed at sampled 3D points. The output is a 3D clothed human triangular mesh extracted via Marching Cubes from a predicted continuous occupancy field, which encodes interior/exterior classification of the clothed avatar.

## Method

HiLo combines two complementary components. First, a progressive high-frequency SDF applies periodic functions (sin/cos at frequencies 2^k π) to the parametric model's SDF to amplify fine geometric variation, but uses a progressive weight schedule ω_k(β) that gradually activates higher-frequency components during training to avoid the large gradients (scaled by 2^k π) that cause convergence failure. Second, a spatial interaction implicit function φ_si encodes the 32×32×32 voxelized SMPL-X with a 3D CNN and applies a cross-scale attention module to inject global voxel context into local point features, providing low-frequency regularization that is insensitive to SMPL-X noise. The final per-point feature concatenates the progressive HF SDF, voxelized SMPL-X features, and normal features, which are fed through alternating attention and MLP layers to predict the occupancy field, trained with MSE loss against ground-truth occupancy.

## Results

On the unseen CAPE dataset (trained on THuman2.0, tested on CAPE), HiLo achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.7430, outperforming the best baseline ICON (0.8055) by 7.8% and D-IF (0.8878) by 16.3%. On the seen CAPE setting, HiLo reaches 0.7861 Chamfer distance, improving over ICON (0.8610) by 8.7%. The paper reports overall improvements of 10.43% and 9.54% in Chamfer distance on THuman2.0 and CAPE respectively over state-of-the-art methods. HiLo also converges faster, reaching 0.90 validation accuracy at ~100 iterations versus ~200 for ICON.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiLo outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and metric in both seen and unseen settings. On the most challenging unseen CAPE setting, HiLo (0.7430 Chamfer) beats the closest competitor ICON (0.8055) by 7.8%, D-IF (0.8878) by 16.3%, ECON (0.9872) by 24.7%, PaMIR (1.3500) by 45%, PIFu (2.6820) by 72%, and PIFuHD (3.2370) by 77%. On the seen CAPE setting, HiLo (0.7861) improves over ICON (0.8610) by 8.7% and D-IF (0.8332) by 5.7%. On THuman2.0 (seen), HiLo (0.9230) leads ICON (1.1431) by 19.3% and D-IF (1.0305) by 10.4%. The advantage is particularly pronounced on challenging non-fashion poses (CAPE-NFP), where HiLo achieves 0.7830 versus ICON's 0.8656 (9.5% improvement), confirming that the progressive HF SDF is most beneficial for complex geometries.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiLo extends the implicit-representation line of work for single-image 3D clothed human reconstruction (PIFu, PaMIR, ICON, ECON, D-IF) by explicitly decomposing the regularization signal from a parametric body model (SMPL-X) into high-frequency and low-frequency components. The progressive high-frequency SDF draws on periodic activation functions from SIREN and the progressive frequency scheduling from NeRFies, while the spatial interaction implicit function introduces cross-voxel attention to a standard MLP-based occupancy predictor. Unlike prior methods that use the parametric model holistically (e.g., ICON's SDF + normal features, PaMIR's voxel features), HiLo assigns distinct roles to HF (detail enhancement) and LF (noise robustness) information, and validates this decomposition through controlled toy experiments on multiple baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean conceptual framework—HF for detail, LF for robustness—that practitioners can adopt when designing regularization from parametric priors in 3D reconstruction. The progressive HF SDF is a practical training technique that addresses a real convergence problem (gradient explosion from high-frequency periodic terms) without architectural overhead. However, the method still depends on an upstream SMPL-X estimator, and the evaluated noise levels (up to 0.5) may not cover the worst-case misalignments encountered in the wild. The authors note future extension to face and scene reconstruction, suggesting the HF/LF decomposition could generalize beyond human reconstruction, but this remains untested.
