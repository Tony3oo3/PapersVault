## Problem

Existing UGC video quality assessment methods and databases are designed for general media platforms (e.g., YouTube) and fail on short-form UGC videos due to two challenges: (i) kaleidoscope content with diverse creation modes (special effects, three-stage form) confuses models in identifying quality-determined regions, and (ii) sophisticated cascaded processing workflows (pre-processing, enhancement, transcoding) produce complex hybrid distortions that are difficult to distinguish. No prior database or evaluator was built specifically for this short-form video platform scenario.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a short-form UGC video clip via a fragment sampling strategy: the video is divided into N patches, and a fragment of size 32×32 is randomly sampled from each patch across T frames (2-frame interval), forming a composite input of 32×288×288. After quality-aware region selection, the input is reshaped to 32×224×224 with (7×7) fragments for the 3D Swin Transformer backbone. The output is a scalar video quality score (MOS in [1, 5] with 0.5 intervals), and for 500 indistinguishable video pairs, a binary ranking prediction is also produced.

## Method

KSVQE builds on a 3D Swin Transformer backbone with three key modules. First, the Quality-aware Region Selection (QRS) module inserts a learnable quality adapter into the last two CLIP layers to compute patch-wise quality-aware semantic importance, then uses Differentiable TopK Selection to retain only the K most quality-relevant fragments, discarding quality-unrelated content (e.g., special-effect backgrounds). Second, the Content-adaptive Modulation (CaM) module warps fine-grained CLIP patch semantics into the 3D Swin features via multi-head cross attention, followed by spatial-wise scale-and-offset modulation to make quality perception content-adaptive. Third, the Distortion-aware Modulation (DaM) module fine-tunes the pre-trained CONTRIQUE distortion extractor with a distortion adapter under contrastive loss (same processing pattern = positive pair), then fuses the adapted spatial distortion features into quality features via MHCA+MHSA and channel-wise style modulation, enabling the model to distinguish complex hybrid distortions from cascaded processing workflows.

## Results

On the newly proposed KVQ dataset, KSVQE achieves SROCC 0.867 and PLCC 0.869, outperforming the second-best method Dover* (0.833/0.837) by 0.034/0.032. On KoNViD-1k, KSVQE reaches SROCC 0.922 and PLCC 0.921, surpassing Dover* (0.908/0.910) by 0.014/0.011. On YouTube-UGC, the margin is even larger: KSVQE scores 0.900/0.912 versus Dover*'s 0.841/0.851, a gap of 0.059/0.061. On LSVQ_test, KSVQE attains 0.886/0.888, edging out Dover* (0.877/0.878) by 0.009/0.010.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KSVQE outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset where results are reported. The largest margin is on YouTube-UGC, where KSVQE (0.900/0.912) beats Dover* (0.841/0.851) by 0.059/0.061 in SROCC/PLCC, and on KVQ where it leads Dover* by 0.034/0.032. Among traditional methods, TLVQM and VIDEVAL score well on KoNViD-1k (~0.773) but drop sharply on KVQ (0.490 and 0.369 SROCC), highlighting their inability to handle short-form UGC distortions. Deep-learning baselines FastVQA and SimpleVQA are competitive on KoNViD-1k (0.891 and 0.856 SROCC) but trail KSVQE by 0.031 and 0.066 respectively. On the ranking-pair task (Table 3), KSVQE achieves 0.76 accuracy on non-homogeneous pairs and 0.81 overall, exceeding Dover* (0.70 and 0.79) and all other methods, confirming its advantage in fine-grained quality discrimination. Cross-dataset experiments (Tables 4–5) further show KSVQE generalizes best in both directions (KVQ→others and others→KVQ), with the KVQ→others direction yielding notably higher scores, indicating KVQ is a more challenging and informative training set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UGC-VQA line of work (KoNViD-1k, YouTube-UGC, LSVQ, UGC-VIDEO) by targeting the specific domain of short-form video platforms, where kaleidoscope content creation modes and sophisticated multi-stage processing pipelines introduce challenges absent from general in-the-wild UGC. Architecturally, it builds on the fragment-sampling strategy from FastVQA and the 3D Swin Transformer backbone from DISCovQA, but departs from prior UGC-VQA methods by explicitly incorporating content understanding via CLIP (with a quality adapter and differentiable TopK region selection) and distortion understanding via a contrastively fine-tuned CONTRIQUE extractor—neither of which appears in prior UGC-VQA pipelines. The mixed absolute-plus-ranking annotation scheme also extends the standard MOS-only labeling used in existing UGC databases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KVQ is the first large-scale benchmark purpose-built for short-form UGC video quality, filling a gap between general in-the-wild VQA datasets and the practical needs of platforms like Kuaishou and TikTok. The finding that training on KVQ transfers better to other UGC datasets than the reverse suggests the short-form domain captures a superset of quality-relevant phenomena, which could make KVQ a useful pre-training resource. However, the dataset is limited to a single platform's processing pipeline and 4,200 videos, and the method's reliance on CLIP and CONTRIQUE adds inference overhead that may limit deployment in latency-sensitive streaming contexts. Open questions include whether the content-understanding modules generalize to platforms with different creation ecosystems and whether the ranking annotation protocol scales beyond 500 pairs.
