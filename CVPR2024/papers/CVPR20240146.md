## Problem

Current monocular 3D human reconstruction methods produce unstable, low-quality meshes that lack UV unwrapping and skinning weights, making them impractical for industrial applications like gaming, virtual try-on, and digital avatars. Implicit methods capture clothing details but fail on hands and faces, while explicit parametric methods cannot represent arbitrary topologies. There is no pipeline that simultaneously delivers complete, animatable, edit-friendly semantic meshes with high-fidelity textures from potentially incomplete or erroneous inputs.

## Input / Output

SHERT takes a detailed 3D surface (explicit mesh or implicit SDF) paired with a corresponding SMPL-X model as the semantic guider, and optionally an RGB image and text prompt for texture generation. The output is a complete semantic mesh with 149,921 vertices and 299,712 faces (from twice-subdivided SMPL-X), carrying stable UV coordinates, per-vertex skinning weights, and deterministic semantic labels for face, body, and hands. The texture map is produced at 1024×1024 resolution in the UV domain, with the mesh topology fixed to the sub-SMPLX template so that all outputs share identical vertex connectivity.

## Method

SHERT's pipeline has four stages. First, Semantic- and Normal-based Sampling (SNS) shoots rays along vertex normals from the subdivided SMPL-X to the target surface (via ray intersection or constant-step ray marching for SDFs), then culls unreliable triangles using angle, area-ratio, and edge-ratio thresholds in both posed and canonical spaces. Second, a self-supervised completion network converts the 3D hole-filling problem into 2D UV inpainting by representing deformation as normal-direction offsets in canonical space, trained with randomly masked UV position maps. Third, a U-Net refinement network projects image and front/back normal map features into the UV domain to predict a displacement field that sharpens geometric details, trained by degrading completed meshes with Laplacian smoothing. Fourth, a Stable Diffusion model fine-tuned with ControlNet performs text-driven texture inpainting and generation directly on the UV map, leveraging the stable semantic UV parameterization to keep face, body, and limb regions geometrically consistent during generation.

## Results

On CAPE, SHERT (ICON-comp) achieves the best P2S distance of 0.8550 cm and Chamfer distance of 0.8107 cm, outperforming ICON (0.8855/0.8609) and ECON (0.9403/0.9386). On THuman2.0, SHERT (ECON-refine) attains the lowest Chamfer distance of 1.0430 cm and P2S of 1.0630 cm, surpassing ECON (1.2081/1.1304) and ICON (1.0874/1.0361). For registration quality on THuman2.0, SNS achieves the best P2S (0.107 cm), Chamfer (0.078 cm), and mesh quality G-avg (0.729) while running in only 23 seconds, compared to 1m4s for Fast-RNRR and 1h23m32s for SVR-L0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHERT consistently outperforms all implicit-based baselines (PIFu, PIFuHD, PaMIR, 2K2K) by a large margin on both P2S and Chamfer distances, with PIFuHD being the weakest at 3.78 cm P2S on CAPE versus SHERT's 0.86 cm. Against the strongest implicit baselines ICON and ECON, SHERT's completion and refinement stages yield modest but consistent improvements: on CAPE, ICON-comp reduces P2S from 0.8855 to 0.8550 cm (−3.4%) and Chamfer from 0.8609 to 0.8107 cm (−5.8%); on THuman2.0, ECON-refine reduces Chamfer from 1.2081 to 1.0430 cm (−13.7%). For non-rigid registration (Table 2), SNS dominates all six baselines on P2S (0.107 vs. 0.115 for Fast-RNRR), Chamfer (0.078 vs. 0.097), and mesh quality G-avg (0.729 vs. 0.597 for Fast-RNRR), while also being the fastest at 23 seconds. The Normal metric shows SHERT slightly trails ICON (0.0359 vs. 0.0347 on CAPE), indicating the completion/refinement introduces minor normal deviations. For texture prediction, SHERT produces stable UV-mapped textures with rich clothing details, whereas PIFu and PaMIR only predict vertex colors and DINAR's neural textures are tightly coupled to specific avatars at lower resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHERT sits at the intersection of three research threads: implicit-based monocular human reconstruction (PIFu, ICON, ECON), non-rigid 3D surface registration (N-ICP, RPTS, SVR-L0), and diffusion-based texture synthesis (Stable Diffusion + ControlNet, DINAR). Its key conceptual departure is treating the reconstruction problem not as an end-to-end geometry prediction task but as a structured pipeline—semantic- and normal-based sampling (SNS) for registration, self-supervised UV-domain completion, image-guided refinement, and diffusion-based texture inpainting—anchored by the SMPL-X parametric model as a semantic guider. Unlike prior implicit methods that produce free-form surfaces without guaranteed topology, SHERT leverages the subdivided SMPL-X to enforce deterministic vertex semantics, stable UV parameterization, and predefined skinning weights, making outputs directly usable in animation and editing pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SHERT addresses a practical gap between research-quality human reconstruction and industrial requirements (gaming, VR, virtual try-on) by producing meshes with stable UV unwrapping, animatable body parts, and high-resolution textures—features that prior implicit methods fundamentally cannot guarantee. The self-supervised completion and refinement networks, operating in the UV domain, are a reusable technique for any template-based reconstruction pipeline. Limitations remain for loose clothing, shoes, and hair due to SMPL-X's geometric constraints, and texture consistency at UV seams is acknowledged as unresolved. Open questions include whether the pipeline can be extended to multi-view or video inputs and whether the diffusion-based texturing can be made more controllable for production workflows.
