## Problem

Federated learning suffers from client model drift under non-i.i.d. data, where local updates diverge from the global gradient direction, causing high variance in server aggregation and slow convergence. This problem is severely aggravated at low client participation rates, where the limited subset of clients provides highly variable information. Existing remedies (e.g., SCAFFOLD, FedDC, FedCM) require additional communication rounds for gradient transmission, extra client-side memory for storing local states, or full participation, making them impractical for resource-constrained real-world deployments.

## Input / Output

The server broadcasts a single model-parameter message (the momentum-integrated model θ^{t-1} + λm^{t-1}) to a subset of active clients each round; clients return only their local update deltas Δ_i^t = θ_{i,K}^t − θ_{i,0}^t. No separate gradient vectors, control variates, or auxiliary drift variables are transmitted, so the per-round communication cost equals that of vanilla FedAvg. The final output is a global model θ^T trained on image classification (CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet, FEMNIST, CelebA) or next-word prediction (Shakespeare) tasks, with ResNet-18, CNN, or LSTM backbones depending on the dataset.

## Method

FedACG incorporates the global momentum into the model broadcast to clients, so each client initializes its local training from a 'lookahead' point θ^{t-1} + λm^{t-1} rather than the raw global model θ^{t-1}. This single-message broadcast conveys the projected global update direction without any extra communication. During local training, each client minimizes an augmented loss that includes a proximal regularization term (β/2)‖θ_{i,k}^t − (θ^{t-1} + λm^{t-1})‖², anchoring local updates to the accelerated global point and suppressing client-specific drift. After K local SGD steps, the server aggregates the returned deltas, updates the momentum as m^t = λm^{t-1} + Δ^t, and sets the next global model to θ^t = θ^{t-1} + m^t. The approach requires no additional client memory, no extra server computation beyond a momentum update, and no warm-up for newly joining clients, while achieving a convergence rate O(1/√(TK|S_t|)) matching the best existing FL methods.

## Results

On the moderate-scale setting (100 clients, 5% participation, Dirichlet 0.3), FedACG achieves 89.10% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 62.51% on CIFAR-100, and 46.31% on Tiny-ImageNet after 1000 communication rounds, outperforming all baselines without extra communication overhead. In terms of convergence speed, FedACG reaches 85% accuracy on CIFAR-10 in 450 rounds (vs. 519 for FedAvgM and 1000+ for FedAvg), 55% on CIFAR-100 in 409 rounds (vs. 640 for MOON), and 38% on Tiny-ImageNet in 331 rounds (vs. 403 for FedDC). On the large-scale setting (500 clients, 2% participation), FedACG maintains its lead with 82.80% on CIFAR-10, 48.40% on CIFAR-100, and 38.48% on Tiny-ImageNet at 1000 rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedACG outperforms all 13 baselines on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet in the moderate-scale setting, with the largest margins over FedAvg (+14.68% on CIFAR-100) and FedDecorr (+13.34% on CIFAR-100). On CIFAR-10, FedACG (89.10%) surpasses the strongest no-extra-communication competitor FedDyn (88.10%) by 1.00% and MOON (86.30%) by 2.80%, while also beating FedDC (87.47%)—which requires 100% additional communication—by 1.63%. The advantage widens in the large-scale setting (500 clients, 2% participation): FedACG leads FedDC by 9.31% on CIFAR-100 (48.40% vs. 39.09% at 1000R) and by 9.56% on Tiny-ImageNet (38.48% vs. 28.92%), where FedDC's stored local states become stale. FedCM, which requires 50% extra communication, underperforms FedACG on all three datasets and degrades severely at 1% participation (64.50% vs. 76.45% on CIFAR-10). FedACG also integrates seamlessly with quantization (FedPAQ), improving CIFAR-100 accuracy from 41.99% to 50.53% at 1000 rounds with 100 clients.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedACG builds directly on FedAvg by modifying the server-to-client broadcast: instead of sending the raw global model θ^{t-1}, it sends a lookahead model θ^{t-1} + λm^{t-1} that encodes the global momentum into the parameters themselves. This departs from prior momentum-based FL methods (FedCM, FedADC, FedGLOMO) that transmit the momentum as a separate message, incurring additional communication cost, and from proximal/regularization methods (FedProx, FedDyn, SCAFFOLD) that anchor local updates to the unaccelerated global model. The paper also draws a sharp conceptual distinction from FedAvgM: both use global momentum, but FedACG initializes local training from the momentum-shifted interim model rather than the previous global model, a subtle difference that the authors show accumulates into significant performance gaps over many rounds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-overhead property (no extra communication, no client-side memory) makes FedACG particularly attractive for real-world FL deployments on bandwidth-limited or resource-constrained devices, where even 50% additional communication (as in FedCM) is prohibitive. The convergence guarantee matching SOTA rates and the robustness to very low participation rates (1%) and dynamic client sets address practical pain points that many prior methods handle poorly. Open questions include whether the fixed momentum coefficient λ can be adapted per-round or per-client, whether the approach extends naturally to asynchronous or straggler-heavy settings, and whether the lookahead initialization interacts favorably with other communication-reduction techniques beyond the quantization experiments shown.
