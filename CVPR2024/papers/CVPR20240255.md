## Problem

Optical flow, the dominant representation for inter-frame relationships in video modeling, is dense, non-editable, and captures only pixel displacement without modeling the probabilistic or semantic aspects of object motion. Additionally, conventional encoder-decoder video frameworks place correlation modeling in the encoder, which limits the decoder's generative capacity and produces redundant intermediate representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240255/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB reference frame and a sequence of current frames as input; the reference need not come from the same sequence or even the same modality. A single 32-dimensional latent token (d_m=32) per frame encodes all motion information, and the reference frame's dense features and token are computed once and shared across the entire video. For the editing task, a source frame plus a control condition (e.g., 3DMM coefficients) is fed through a small MLP adapter to produce an edited token, which the frozen decoder then renders into a modified frame.

## Method

IMF is a decoder-centric encoder-decoder framework where the encoder independently extracts a dense multi-scale feature map (E_F) and a compact latent token (E_T) from each frame, while all inter-frame correlation is modeled entirely in the decoder. The decoder's Implicit Motion Function (IMF) has two stages: (1) a latent token decoder (IMF_D) that uses StyleGAN2-style weight modulation to expand the 32-dim token into multi-scale motion feature maps, and (2) an implicit motion alignment module (IMF_A) that performs scaled dot-product cross-attention with current-frame motion features as queries, reference-frame motion features as keys, and reference appearance features as values, followed by transformer refinement blocks. This design replaces explicit optical flow or keypoint-based warping with a fully implicit, coordinate-free representation, enabling the decoder to handle new content, non-rigid deformations, and object entry/exit. For editing, a lightweight MLP adapter ψ rewrites the source token conditioned on a control signal while the main decoder remains frozen, demonstrating the editability of the latent space.

## Results

On CelebV-HQ, IMF achieves L1=0.0417, SSIM=0.849, PSNR=25.28 dB, FID=53.22, and LPIPS=0.1057, outperforming the best baseline PECHead (L1=0.0552, PSNR=24.29) by ~24% in L1 and ~1 dB in PSNR. On VFHQ, IMF reaches L1=0.0283, SSIM=0.918, PSNR=26.86 dB, FID=32.35, and LPIPS=0.0540, surpassing the closest competitor IW (L1=0.0426, PSNR=23.46) by 33% in L1 and 3.4 dB in PSNR. On general video datasets (Flower, Wavecloth, Foliage), IMF also leads all baselines on L1, SSIM, and LPIPS, with the largest gap on Foliage (SSIM 0.8235 vs. 0.7771 for TPSMM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMF outperforms all nine baselines across every reported metric on both talking head and general video datasets. On CelebV-HQ, the closest competitor is PECHead [11] (PSNR 24.29 vs. 25.28) and IW [41] (SSIM 0.801 vs. 0.849); IMF improves PSNR by ~1 dB and SSIM by ~0.05 over both. On VFHQ, IW [41] is the nearest baseline (PSNR 23.46 vs. 26.86), with IMF gaining 3.4 dB in PSNR and reducing L1 by 33%. On general datasets (Flower, Wavecloth, Foliage), IMF beats the best explicit method IW [41] by 15–19% in L1 and 0.02–0.03 in SSIM, and surpasses TPSMM [81] by 12–13% in L1 on Foliage. Older keypoint-based methods (FOMM [57], MRAA [58], F-Vid2Vid [66], LIA [67]) trail IMF by 20–40% in L1, confirming that explicit coordinate representations are substantially less effective. In video compression, IMF achieves a superior rate-distortion trade-off over both the standard H.266/VTM codec [5] and the SOTA neural codec DCVC-DC [31], with the latent tokens remaining compact even without an online entropy model.

## Relation to Prior Work

IMF departs from the dominant explicit optical flow paradigm (FOMM, MRAA, TPSMM, IW) by replacing dense flow fields or sparse keypoints with a single low-dimensional latent token pair, and by relocating inter-frame correlation modeling from the encoder to the decoder—a decoder-centric design the authors explicitly analogize to LLM architectures. The latent token decoder borrows StyleGAN2's weight modulation/demodulation to expand the compact token into multi-scale motion features, while cross-attention (from the Transformer literature) replaces grid-sampling warping for feature alignment. Conceptually, the paper also draws on Wyner-Ziv coding theory to justify that decoder-centric rate can match encoder-centric rate, providing information-theoretic grounding for the architectural choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 32-dimensional latent token can outperform dense optical flow in both reconstruction fidelity and compression efficiency challenges the assumption that explicit per-pixel motion is necessary for high-quality video modeling, and the decoder-centric design opens a path toward integrating motion modeling with generative foundation models. However, experiments are limited to 256×256 resolution, a small set of datasets (two talking-head, three general), and the compression evaluation uses only VFHQ; scalability to longer sequences, higher resolutions, and diverse content remains untested. The token-editing capability is promising but demonstrated only on talking-head pose/expression control, leaving open whether the same editability transfers to more complex generative tasks.
