## Problem

Existing image manipulation detection methods primarily rely on pixel- or patch-level consistencies in RGB and noise views, overlooking object-level anomalies that become critical as diffusion-based forgeries leave fewer low-level traces. No prior framework integrates RGB, noise, and object-view clues into a single unified learning process that simultaneously performs detection and localization while allowing the sub-tasks to mutually reinforce each other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240806/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, internally generating a noise-view representation via a learnable constrained CNN and a high-frequency edge map via DCT high-pass filtering. It produces two outputs: an image-level tampering detection score (max of the localization map) and a pixel-level localization mask reconstructed from a PCA-compressed 1D mask vector (dimension 256) predicted per RoI proposal. The pipeline operates on RoI proposals generated by an RPN over a feature pyramid, so the effective processing granularity is proposal-level rather than full-image.

## Method

UnionFormer uses a dual-stream BSFI-Net (a CNN-Transformer parallel encoder with Feature Coupling Units and Boundary Oriented Blocks) to extract discriminative features from both RGB and noise views, with DCT-derived edge information fed into the transformer branch to boost boundary sensitivity. A contrastive InfoNCE loss aligns tampered proposals across the two streams. The fused proposal embeddings are processed by a 6-layer transformer encoder, after which a Unified Manipulation Discriminative Representation (UMDR) jointly predicts three sub-tasks under multi-scale supervision: object consistency modeling (bounding-box regression via L1 + GIoU), authenticity classification (focal loss), and localization (L1 loss on PCA-encoded mask vectors). The object-view representation emerges implicitly from the self-attention affinity structure, capturing inter-object inconsistencies without explicit segmentation. Training proceeds in three stages (BSFI-Net pre-training, RPN training, full UnionFormer fine-tuning) with a 3×36-epoch schedule.

## Results

On the localization task, UnionFormer achieves a mean pixel-level AUC of 0.929 across five benchmark datasets (Columbia, Coverage, CASIA v1, NIST16, IMD20), outperforming the strongest baseline TruFor (0.927) and ObjectFormer (0.884). On the detection task, UnionFormer attains a mean image-level AUC of 0.864 and balanced accuracy of 0.776, surpassing TruFor (0.839 AUC, 0.756 accuracy) by 2.5% and 2% respectively. On the challenging CocoGlide dataset containing diffusion-based manipulations, UnionFormer leads TruFor by 2.2% in localization AUC and 1.3% in F1 (fixed threshold).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnionFormer outperforms all seven baselines on average localization AUC (0.929), with the closest competitor being TruFor (0.927) and ObjectFormer (0.884) trailing by 4.5%. On detection, UnionFormer leads TruFor by 2.5% in mean AUC (0.864 vs. 0.839) and 2% in balanced accuracy (0.776 vs. 0.756), though TruFor retains a slight edge on Columbia accuracy (0.984 vs. 0.979). The advantage is most pronounced on CocoGlide, which contains diffusion-model-based local edits: UnionFormer gains 2.2% AUC and 1.3% F1 over TruFor, attributed to the object-view representation capturing inconsistencies in diffusion-generated regions. Against older CNN-based baselines (ManTra-Net, SPAN, PSCC-Net, CAT-Net v2), UnionFormer achieves substantially larger margins, e.g., 12.8% AUC improvement over ManTra-Net on the average. On robustness to distortions (Table 3), UnionFormer consistently outperforms SPAN, PSCC-Net, and ObjectFormer across all four distortion types, with the largest gap under heavy Gaussian noise (σ=15: 0.8057 vs. 0.7815 for ObjectFormer).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-view tampering detection paradigm (RGB + noise views, as in ManTra-Net, MVSS-Net, and TruFor) by introducing a third 'object view' that models inter-object consistency anomalies, a perspective absent from prior pixel- or patch-level approaches. Architecturally, it builds on the DETR/SOLQ transformer detection framework and the Conformer-style CNN-Transformer parallel encoder, repurposing them for forensics through the novel BSFI-Net encoder and the Unified Manipulation Discriminative Representation (UMDR). The key conceptual departure is the unified multi-task learning process that jointly optimizes object consistency modeling, binary detection, and localization within a single transformer encoder, allowing the three views to mutually reinforce each other rather than being treated as independent feature streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The object view is particularly timely as diffusion-based editing tools (Blended Diffusion, Imagic, SINE) produce hyper-realistic forgeries that leave minimal RGB and noise traces, making object-level incongruities a critical detection signal. The BDNIE benchmark (512 diffusion-generated fakes) is a useful addition, though its small size limits statistical confidence. Limitations include the reliance on RPN-generated proposals (which may miss small or subtle tampered regions), the complexity of the multi-stage training pipeline, and modest absolute gains over the strongest baseline (TruFor) on most standard datasets. Open questions include whether the object view generalizes to video manipulation, multi-object composited scenes, and whether the unified learning benefit persists when the number of sub-tasks grows.
