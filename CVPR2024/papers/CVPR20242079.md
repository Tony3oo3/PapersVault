## Problem

Automatic Audio Description (AD) generation for movies is constrained by three gaps: existing datasets like MAD provide only CLIP frame features at 5 Hz rather than raw pixels, limiting fine-grained spatial understanding; evaluation relies on a small 10-movie test set and n-gram metrics that fail to capture character naming and semantic equivalence; and no architecture directly ingests video pixels alongside character identity information. This paper addresses all three by introducing pixel-level AD datasets, a video-to-AD architecture with character bank integration, and AD-specific evaluation metrics.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8 video frames resized to 224×224 pixels together with a character bank consisting of face exemplar images (also 224×224) and corresponding character names from IMDb. The output is a single natural-language audio description sentence that must name the characters involved, describe their actions and interactions, and remain complementary to the movie's existing dialogue. The character bank is processed through the same visual encoder as the video frames, with face images yielding spatial feature grids and video frames yielding spatial-temporal grids, both compressed to 32 descriptor vectors by the Q-former.

## Method

The architecture (Movie-BLIP2 / Movie-Llama2) uses a frozen EVA-CLIP encoder to extract dense visual features from both the 8-frame video clip and the character face exemplars, then a shared Q-former attends to the resulting spatial-temporal (video) and spatial (face) feature grids to produce 32 visual descriptor vectors each. Two shallow projection heads map these descriptors into the language embedding space, where a frozen LLM (OPT-2.7B or Llama2-7B) generates the AD text conditioned on the projected visual tokens and a language prompt. Training is two-stage: pretraining on the 3.4M-segment HowTo-AD dataset (constructed by rewriting HowTo100M captions with random character names and pseudo character banks) for 1 epoch, followed by finetuning on the 94k-segment CMD-AD dataset (built by two-stage temporal alignment of AudioVault AD audio with CMD movie clips via WER-based text localization and RANSAC-fitted mel-spectrogram audio matching) for 2 epochs. Only the projection heads are trained; the visual encoder, Q-former, and LLM remain frozen throughout. Evaluation introduces CRITIC (a co-referencing-based IoU metric for character naming accuracy) and LLM-AD-eval (GPT-3.5/Llama2-7B scoring holistic semantic match on a 1–5 scale), alongside standard CIDEr and Recall@k/N.

## Results

On the new CMD-AD-Eval set (100 movies), Movie-Llama2 achieves CIDEr 25.0, Recall@1/5 of 31.2, CRITIC 32.7, and LLM-AD-Eval scores of 2.29 (GPT-3.5) / 2.92 (Llama-2-7B), substantially outperforming the previous best method AutoAD-II (CIDEr 13.5, R@1/5 26.1, CRITIC 8.2). On the MAD-Eval set (10 movies), Movie-Llama2 reaches CIDEr 24.0 and R@5/16 of 52.8 without any training on MAD-Train, surpassing AutoAD-II (19.2 / 51.3) and MM-Narrator-GPT4 (13.9 CIDEr). The CRITIC metric shows the largest relative gain (32.7 vs. 8.2 for AutoAD-II), highlighting the benefit of pixel-level visual understanding for character identification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Movie-Llama2 outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest prior method AutoAD-II (ICCV 2023), Movie-Llama2 improves CIDEr from 13.5 to 25.0 (+11.5), R@1/5 from 26.1 to 31.2 (+5.1), and CRITIC from 8.2 to 32.7 (+24.5) on CMD-AD-Eval, with the CRITIC gap reflecting the advantage of pixel-level input for character naming. On MAD-Eval, Movie-Llama2 beats AutoAD-II by 4.8 CIDEr points (24.0 vs. 19.2) and 1.5 R@5/16 points (52.8 vs. 51.3) without any MAD-specific training. The off-the-shelf video captioning models Video-BLIP2 and Video-Llama2 (used without AD fine-tuning) score near-zero CRITIC (0.0) and CIDEr below 6, confirming that generic video captioning does not transfer to the character-aware AD task. MM-Narrator-GPT4, which uses GPT-4 as its language model, achieves only 13.9 CIDEr on MAD-Eval, well below Movie-Llama2's 24.0, indicating that a stronger LLM alone is insufficient without domain-specific visual grounding and character-bank integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BLIP-2 Q-former architecture and MiniGPT-4's Llama2 integration from the image domain to multi-frame movie clips, adding character bank inputs (face exemplars and names) that prior video captioning models lack. It departs from the AutoAD-I/II line of work, which operated on CLIP frame features from the MAD dataset, by constructing two new pixel-level datasets (CMD-AD via audio-audio RANSAC alignment, HowTo-AD via LLM rewriting of HowTo100M) that enable end-to-end training on raw video. The evaluation framework builds on the LLM-as-a-judge trend (CLAIR, VideoChatGPT) but introduces a domain-specific CRITIC metric grounded in co-referencing resolution, addressing the character-naming requirement that distinguishes AD from general video captioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The publicly released CMD-AD and HowTo-AD datasets remove the pixel-availability bottleneck that has constrained AD research since MAD, and the 100-movie CMD-AD-Eval set offers substantially more genre diversity than the prior 10-movie MAD-Eval. The CRITIC metric exposes a large gap in character-naming accuracy (AutoAD-II scores 8.2 vs. 32.7 for Movie-Llama2) that n-gram metrics like CIDEr mask, suggesting prior progress on AD was partly illusory. Open questions include cross-narration coherence (avoiding repetition and story-irrelevant details), the 20% alignment failure rate in CMD-AD construction, and whether pseudo-ground-truth HowTo-AD annotations generalize beyond instructional video domains.
