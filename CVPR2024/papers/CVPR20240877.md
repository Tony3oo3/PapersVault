## Problem

Video-LLMs pretrained on millions of short seconds-long clips fail to understand minutes-long videos because naively sampling more key frames loses fine-grained spatiotemporal detail, and passing multiple segments through the existing tokenizer only aggregates context within each segment rather than across them. No prior work has extended the learned video tokenizer function of a pretrained short-term vLLM to handle longer temporal horizons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240877/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a minutes-long video (4 to 30+ minutes) and a text question, decomposing the video into a sparse set of coarsely-sampled key frames (providing global context) and N non-overlapping segments sampled at a higher frame rate (providing local spatiotemporal detail). It produces a textual answer — a multiple-choice selection for EgoSchema or an ordered action sequence for SeedBench procedure understanding — using only 64 total input frames, fewer than the 128 used by the base Video-Llama.

## Method

Koala introduces two new tokenizer functions that repurpose the frozen video QFormer with learnable spatiotemporal queries. The Conditioned Segment (CS) tokenizer concatenates key-frame tokens (adapted via learnable segment queries) with the pretrained video queries, enabling cross-attention that selects frame-level concepts relevant to both local segment context and global video context. The Conditioned Video (CV) tokenizer then introduces learnable temporal and concept queries to model inter-segment relationships, producing a weighted sum of global key-frame tokens and QFormer outputs that encodes contextual relations across segments. Training is lightweight: only 2 epochs on 250K filtered HowTo100M videos with a cross-entropy loss on high-level task labels, while the base vLLM and video QFormer remain frozen.

## Results

On the EgoSchema long video QA benchmark, Koala achieves 40.42% top-1 accuracy using only 64 input frames, outperforming the base Video-Llama (33.25% with 128 frames) by ~7% and the strong Flan-T5 language prior (35.92%) by ~4.5%. On Seed-Bench, Koala reaches 35.91% on procedure understanding (vs. 25.42% for Video-Llama) and 41.26% on short-term action recognition (vs. 35.52% for Video-Llama), surpassing all state-of-the-art mLLMs including VPGTrans (31.88% / 39.54%). Notably, Koala uses half the input frames of Video-Llama and MovieChat while achieving substantially higher accuracy across all three evaluation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Koala wins against every compared baseline on all three evaluation tasks. On EgoSchema, it outperforms Video-Llama [70] by 7.17 points (40.42 vs. 33.25) despite using half the frames (64 vs. 128), and beats MovieChat [49] by 6.93 points. It also surpasses the Flan-T5 language prior [11] (35.92%)—a strong non-visual baseline—by 4.5 points, and outperforms InternVideo [60] (32.10%) and mPLUG-Owl [67] (31.10%) by 8.3 and 9.3 points respectively. On Seed-Bench procedure understanding, Koala (35.91%) leads the best prior method VPGTrans [69] (31.88%) by 4.0 points and Video-Llama [70] (25.42%) by 10.5 points. On Seed-Bench action recognition, Koala (41.26%) edges out VPGTrans [69] (39.54%) by 1.7 points and Video-Llama [70] (35.52%) by 5.7 points, while substantially outperforming VideoChat [35] (34.89%), Valley [40] (31.26%), and Video-ChatGPT [41] (27.59%). The paper further shows that simply finetuning Video-Llama on HowTo100M without the CS/CV tokenizers actually degrades performance by ~5%, confirming the gains come from the proposed tokenizer design rather than additional training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

Koala builds directly on Video-LLaMA's frozen video QFormer (itself derived from BLIP-2's QFormer) and repurposes it as the core aggregation function, introducing learnable spatiotemporal queries (Conditioned Segment and Conditioned Video tokenizers) to extend its temporal horizon from seconds to minutes. Conceptually, the multi-rate sampling strategy echoes SlowFast networks, but Koala departs by using the coarse-rate key frames as a global context signal that conditions fine-grained segment-level aggregation, rather than simply fusing static and motion streams. Unlike prior long-video QA approaches that condition on the question (MIST) or rely on fine-grained paragraph annotations, Koala uses only coarse goal labels and global visual context, making it more task-agnostic and data-efficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that a pretrained short-video vLLM's tokenizer can be adapted to minutes-long videos with lightweight finetuning on noisy instructional data, and the surprising finding that the same adaptation also improves short-term action recognition suggests global context conditioning is broadly beneficial for spatiotemporal reasoning. A concerning open question raised by the results is that a simple Flan-T5 language prior (no video input) outperforms most state-of-the-art vLLMs on EgoSchema, implying that current vLLMs may not be performing genuine long-term temporal reasoning. The approach remains limited by the pretrained model's maximum token count, and extending positional embeddings to handle movie-length videos remains an open challenge for the vLLM community.
