## Problem

Existing Semantic Scene Completion (SSC) methods predict only geometry and class labels from sparse LiDAR, ignoring instance-level identity and uncertainty estimation—both critical for applications like autonomous driving that require tracking individual objects and quantifying prediction reliability. This paper introduces Panoptic Scene Completion (PSC) as a new task that jointly infers scene geometry, semantics, and instances, and proposes the first method (PaSCo) to solve it while providing calibrated voxel-wise and instance-wise uncertainty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242276/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse 3D LiDAR point cloud (e.g., 64-layer scans voxelized into 256×256×32 grids at 0.2 m resolution for Semantic KITTI) and produces a denser panoptic scene as a set of K voxel masks, each assigned a semantic class, covering both 'things' and 'stuff' categories. In addition to the completed scene, PaSCo outputs voxel-wise uncertainty (from semantic completion softmax) and instance-wise uncertainty (from per-mask class probability), both derived from the spread across M subnetwork predictions. At inference, the M subnets each receive a different augmentation (rotation, translation) of the same input point cloud, and their mask sets are combined via permutation-invariant ensembling into a single final PSC output.

## Method

PaSCo builds a sparse generative 3D U-Net backbone that produces multi-scale features (1:4, 1:2, 1:1) with semantic-based pruning (rather than binary occupancy) to preserve small-class geometry, followed by a Mask2Former-style transformer decoder with masked attention applied only on non-empty voxels to predict K mask queries with semantic class and binary mask scores. The key architectural novelty is a MIMO (multi-input multi-output) formulation: M subnets share the encoder, decoders, and transformer decoder but have independent semantic heads and query embeddings, so each subnet processes a different augmented input and produces a distinct PSC output at roughly constant total parameter count. At inference, a novel permutation-invariant mask ensembling aligns the M unordered mask sets via Hungarian matching with a soft (sigmoid-probability) IoU cost, then averages the aligned semantic and mask probabilities to yield a single final PSC and its uncertainty. Training is end-to-end supervised with a combination of voxel-query semantic loss, multi-scale semantic loss, and Hungarian-matched mask losses (Dice + BCE + CE) applied at all scales and subnets.

## Results

On Semantic KITTI (val), PaSCo achieves All PQ† of 31.42 and All PQ of 16.51, surpassing the strongest baseline (SCPNet*+MaskPLS) by +8.21 and +5.62 respectively, while also leading in mIoU (30.11 vs. 27.89). On SSCBench-KITTI360 (test), PaSCo reaches All PQ† of 26.29 and All PQ of 10.92, improving over the best baseline by +8.09 and +3.45. For uncertainty estimation, PaSCo achieves the best voxel ECE (0.0426 on Semantic KITTI, 0.1348 on SSCBench-KITTI360) and instance ECE (0.4922, 0.6015) in a single inference pass, outperforming Deep Ensemble (which requires 3 passes and ~3× parameters) on all uncertainty metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaSCo decisively outperforms all four PSC baselines (LMSCNet+MaskPLS, JS3CNet+MaskPLS, SCPNet+MaskPLS, SCPNet*+MaskPLS) on every panoptic metric (PQ†, PQ, SQ, RQ) for All, Things, and Stuff categories on both Semantic KITTI and SSCBench-KITTI360, with the largest gains in All PQ† (+8.21 over SCPNet* on Semantic KITTI, +8.09 on SSCBench-KITTI360). The SCPNet*+MaskPLS baseline (the authors' own re-implementation) is the closest competitor, achieving 23.21 All PQ† on Semantic KITTI versus PaSCo's 31.42. For uncertainty estimation, PaSCo beats TTA, MC Dropout, and Deep Ensemble on both voxel and instance ECE/NLL while requiring only a single inference pass (vs. 2–3 passes for baselines) and fewer parameters (120M vs. 333M for Deep Ensemble). On the Robo3D OOD robustness benchmark, PaSCo achieves the best instance-wise uncertainty across all 8 corruption types and the best voxel-wise uncertainty on 7 of 8. Notably, PaSCo also matches or exceeds the best baseline on the subsidiary mIoU metric (30.11 vs. 27.89 on Semantic KITTI), confirming that the panoptic improvements do not come at the cost of semantic quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

PaSCo extends Semantic Scene Completion (SSC) by adding instance-level prediction, drawing on mask-centric transformer architectures (Mask2Former, MaskPLS) for the instance head and sparse generative U-Nets (S3CNet, SG-NN) for geometric completion. For uncertainty estimation, it adapts the MIMO multi-input multi-output strategy from image classification to the far more complex PSC setting, introducing a novel permutation-invariant mask ensembling via Hungarian matching that has no direct analogue in prior ensemble literature. The method departs from prior SSC work by using semantic predictions (rather than binary occupancy) for voxel pruning, and from prior panoptic segmentation by predicting the full scene rather than only labeling input points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PSC is a practically important task for autonomous driving and robotics, where both instance identification and calibrated uncertainty are needed for safe deployment, yet prior SSC methods ignore both. The single-inference MIMO approach offering uncertainty at near-constant compute cost is a practical advantage over Deep Ensembles or MC Dropout for real-time systems. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 3–4 subnets, how to distinguish epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty in the mask predictions, and whether the pseudo-panoptic labels (via DBSCAN clustering) limit the ceiling of achievable performance.
