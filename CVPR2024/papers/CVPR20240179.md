## Problem

2D diffusion models produce photorealistic face images but lack the view consistency and precise pose/expression control needed for a 3D head avatar, while existing 3D avatar methods (NeRF, mesh-based) offer consistency at the cost of visual fidelity. The paper tackles this by asking whether a diffusion-based neural renderer can inherit the image quality of 2D priors while being grounded in an accurate 3D head representation for controllable, temporally consistent animation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240179/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Training requires multi-view RGB videos (16 cameras, ~3300 timesteps per person) with NPHM meshes fitted per frame via COLMAP point clouds; at inference the user supplies NPHM expression codes (from the same person for self-reenactment or a driving person for animation) and a camera pose. The rasterized NPHM mesh produces 73-channel input renderings (normals, depth, 5D canonical coordinates, and 64-channel TriPlane/ambient features) at 512×512 resolution, which are fed to the diffusion model alongside 4 expression tokens. Output is a photorealistic 512×512 face image per denoising step, assembled into temporally consistent video sequences.

## Method

DiffusionAvatars converts a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v2.1 LDM into a controllable neural renderer via three mechanisms: (1) a ControlNet branch conditions on rasterized NPHM meshes (normals, depth, canonical coordinates) to encode viewpoint and coarse head shape; (2) learnable TriPlane spatial features (512×512×16 per plane) are looked up via 5D canonical coordinates and concatenated to the rasterizer output, rigging person-specific appearance to the NPHM surface to compensate for geometric imperfections; (3) 15 cross-attention layers (following IPAdapter) inject 4 expression tokens derived from NPHM's expression code directly into the U-Net, enabling fine-grained expression transfer that rasterized geometry alone cannot convey. The model uses v-parameterization with a rescaled noise schedule to support training on pure-noise inputs, and is trained end-to-end with an L2 loss on latent-space predictions, optimizing the ControlNet, expression conditioning weights, and spatial feature maps simultaneously.

## Results

On the NeRSemble self-reenactment task (unseen expressions and views, averaged over 8 persons), DiffusionAvatars achieves the best score on every metric: PSNR 24.9, LPIPS 0.081, JOD 7.55, AKD 1.79 px, AED 0.50, APD 0.023, and CSIM 0.917. The largest gains are in perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.081 vs. 0.114 for the next-best DNR+GAN) and facial keypoint accuracy (AKD 1.79 vs. 2.06 for DNR). In the avatar-animation user study (735 responses, 49 participants), DiffusionAvatars scores 4.02/5 on Visual Quality and 4.14/5 on Driving Fidelity, substantially ahead of the runner-up DNR+GAN (3.06 and 3.97 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionAvatars outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor DNR [70], it improves LPIPS from 0.226 to 0.081 (a 64% reduction), AKD from 2.06 to 1.79 px, and CSIM from 0.903 to 0.917, while also leading in PSNR (24.9 vs. 24.5) and JOD (7.55 vs. 7.32). DNR+GAN, which adds an adversarial loss for sharper images, achieves a lower LPIPS (0.114) than DNR but still trails DiffusionAvatars by 29% on that metric and loses on all other metrics. DiffusionRig [12], the other diffusion-based baseline, has the worst PSNR (19.6) and JOD (6.41) of all methods, though it is competitive on AED (0.55 vs. 0.50). MVP [43] and NeRFace [16] are clearly outperformed across the board, with NeRFace showing the largest gaps in keypoint distance (AKD 5.37) and identity preservation (CSIM 0.787). In the avatar-animation user study, DiffusionAvatars leads DNR+GAN by nearly a full point on Visual Quality (4.02 vs. 3.06) and by 0.17 on Driving Fidelity (4.14 vs. 3.97), indicating a substantial perceptual advantage in video rendering quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffusionAvatars sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Deferred Neural Rendering [70], which introduced rigging learnable neural textures to a parametric head surface for view-consistent synthesis; (2) ControlNet [86] and IPAdapter [82], which enable conditioning pre-trained latent diffusion models on structured inputs without retraining the backbone; and (3) NPHM [18], a neural parametric head model that replaces mesh-based 3DMMs (FLAME, Basel Face Model) with an implicit SDF for higher geometric fidelity. The key departure from prior work is the 'Deferred Diffusion' framing: rather than learning a screen-space decoder from scratch (as in DNR) or conditioning a diffusion model on FLAME renderings (as in DiffusionRig [12]), the paper combines NPHM's richer geometry, TriPlane spatial features (replacing UV textures unavailable in implicit models), and direct expression-code conditioning via cross-attention into a single ControlNet-conditioned LDM pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 2D diffusion priors can be effectively harnessed for 3D-consistent avatar synthesis without sacrificing the photorealism that pure 3D reconstruction methods (NeRF, volumetric primitives) struggle to achieve, closing a long-standing quality gap. However, two practical limitations remain: lighting is baked into the output (no relighting control), and the iterative denoising loop precludes real-time use, though the authors note that diffusion distillation [47,63] may address the latter. An open question is whether the NPHM-based geometric proxy can be replaced by even more expressive representations (e.g., full 4D fields) without losing the clean conditioning interface that makes the ControlNet formulation tractable.
