## Problem

Training NeRFs from casually captured images in dynamic, real-world scenes is hindered by distractors (moving objects, pedestrians, cars) that violate the static-scene assumption. Existing methods like NeRF-W and RobustNeRF degrade significantly under high occlusion ratios or complex in-the-wild conditions, and RobustNeRF even underperforms on static scenes. The paper asks whether a single NeRF training pipeline can robustly eliminate distractors regardless of their proportion in the captures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20242300/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of casually captured RGB images with known camera poses (no manual annotation, exposure correction, or segmentation masks required) and produces photorealistic novel-view renderings of the static scene with all dynamic elements removed. As an intermediate byproduct, it outputs per-ray uncertainty maps that weight each pixel's contribution to the reconstruction loss; these maps are predicted by a small MLP from DINOv2 feature vectors queried at each ray's 2D location, upsampled to full image resolution via nearest-neighbor interpolation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a decoupled two-branch training scheme: a frozen DINOv2 backbone extracts per-pixel features, which feed a shallow uncertainty MLP G that predicts per-ray uncertainty β(r), while a standard NeRF (Mip-NeRF 360) is trained in parallel with gradients detached between the two branches. Uncertainty is learned via a reformulated SSIM-based loss (product of per-component dissimilarities rather than 1−SSIM) that better discriminates distractors from background than L2 color loss, plus a cosine-similarity regularization that enforces spatial-temporal consistency of β across rays with similar DINOv2 features. The NeRF branch uses the predicted uncertainty as a per-ray weight in its L2 reconstruction loss, effectively down-weighting distractor pixels. Dilated patch sampling (e.g., 32×32 patches with dilation rate 2–4) replaces random or small-patch sampling to provide richer local context, accelerating convergence by up to an order of magnitude over RobustNeRF.

## Results

On the self-captured On-the-go Dataset (6 scenes spanning low to high occlusion), NeRF On-the-go achieves the best PSNR across all scenes, reaching 24.22 dB on Corner, 23.33 dB on Patio-High, and 21.41 dB on the second Patio-High variant, while also attaining the lowest LPIPS (0.189–0.259) and highest SSIM (0.644–0.806). On the RobustNeRF Dataset, the method scores 21.58 dB PSNR on Statue and 23.50 dB on Android, outperforming all baselines. The method also converges roughly one order of magnitude faster than RobustNeRF, capturing fine details at 25K iterations that RobustNeRF only reaches at 250K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRF On-the-go outperforms all five baselines across every scene in both the On-the-go and RobustNeRF datasets. Against RobustNeRF [35], the closest competitor, the method gains up to 3.6 dB PSNR on Patio-High (21.41 vs. 20.54) and 1.78 dB on Android (23.50 vs. 21.72), with the gap widening in high-occlusion settings where RobustNeRF's hard-thresholding fails to retain fine details. NeRF-W [23] and Ha-NeRF [4] show the largest degradation at high occlusion (NeRF-W drops to 12.99 dB PSNR on Patio-High), a consequence of their per-image transient embeddings being unable to model prevalent distractors. Mip-NeRF 360+SAM [1,18] is competitive in simple scenes like Mountain (20.20 vs. 20.15 dB) but degrades sharply in complex outdoor scenes (13.65 dB on Fountain vs. 20.11 dB for Ours) due to segmentation errors on thin structures and shadows. D2NeRF [47], evaluated only on the RobustNeRF Dataset, underperforms significantly (19.09 dB on Statue, 20.61 dB on Android), confirming that video-oriented dynamic NeRF methods struggle with sparse image inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the uncertainty-based distractor removal paradigm introduced by NeRF-W (which used randomly initialized transient embeddings) and RobustNeRF (which applied IRLS outlier verification). The key conceptual departure is leveraging DINOv2 features as a strong spatial-temporal prior for per-pixel uncertainty prediction, combined with an SSIM-based loss that better discriminates distractors from background than the standard L2 loss. The decoupled training strategy—separating uncertainty MLP optimization from NeRF reconstruction—represents a practical architectural choice that avoids the entanglement problem identified in NeRF-W, while dilated patch sampling extends RobustNeRF's patch-based approach with richer local context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a practical, plug-and-play solution for training NeRFs from casually captured sequences without per-pixel annotation, addressing a major barrier to real-world deployment. The up-to-10× convergence speedup over RobustNeRF and consistent performance across occlusion ratios (5%–30%+) make it broadly applicable. However, the authors acknowledge a clear limitation: the method struggles with strong view-dependent effects on reflective surfaces (windows, metals), leaving open the question of how to integrate additional priors (e.g., material-aware features) into the uncertainty framework. The finding that RobustNeRF underperforms even on static scenes also suggests that robust-loss formulations can be counterproductive when no distractors are present.
