## Problem

General-purpose instance segmentation frameworks fail on 2D intraoral images because some teeth have nearly identical shapes (e.g., first vs. second premolar), tooth position and morphology vary substantially across patients, and clinical abnormalities such as caries and edentulism break standard assumptions. Additionally, no professionally annotated 2D teeth segmentation dataset existed to train high-performance models at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20240075/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 2D intraoral image (RGB photo or multi-angle rendered 3D scan projection) at 336×336 resolution and produces two sets of per-pixel masks: a 16-class tooth ID segmentation (following FDI notation) and a binary foreground/background (teeth vs. gingiva) mask. Scores are computed as softmax of scaled dot products between upsampled visual tokens and 18 learnable class embeddings, yielding a (H×W)×16 tooth score map and a (H×W)×2 fg/bg score map.

## Method

TeethSEG uses a pretrained CLIP-L/14@336 encoder to produce visual tokens, which are fused with 18 trainable class embeddings (2 fg/bg + 16 tooth IDs) through a 3-layer masked transformer that prevents tooth ID tokens from attending to each other, maximizing their divergence. Stacked Multi-Scale Aggregation (MSA) blocks then refine class tokens via a novel cross-gating mechanism—where each token is scaled by a scalar importance score rather than replaced by a weighted sum—combined with a permutation-based upscaler that splits each D-dimensional token into 4×(D/4) tokens to increase spatial resolution without bilinear interpolation, producing sharp segmentation boundaries. The Anthropic Prior Knowledge (APK) layer encodes three orthodontist rules (tooth regions must not overlap background, adjacent teeth inform identity, contralateral teeth are symmetric) through a cross-gating step with fg/bg tokens followed by a masked self-gating step restricted to adjacent and contralateral tooth pairs. The full model is trained end-to-end with per-pixel cross-entropy loss on both tooth ID and fg/bg predictions, pretrained on 80K rendered 3D scan images and fine-tuned on 0.8K RGB intraoral photos.

## Results

On the i.i.d. test split (Challenge80K + Plaster70K), TeethSEG achieves 0.91 mIoU using only 5 training epochs, outperforming the best baselines (ViT-Adapter-L and DeepLab-v3 at 0.87 mIoU with 30 epochs) by 4%. On the out-of-distribution test (orthodontic patient data), TeethSEG reaches 0.84 mIoU vs. 0.81 for Segformer, and on the RGB intraoral photo test it achieves 0.91 mIoU vs. 0.85 for ViT-Adapter-L. Notably, TeethSEG requires only 5 epochs to converge versus 30 for all competing methods, as shown in the training curve (Fig. 5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TeethSEG outperforms all six baselines across every test split. Against the strongest competitor ViT-Adapter-L (0.87 mIoU i.i.d., 0.79 o.o.d., 0.85 RGB), TeethSEG gains +4% i.i.d., +5% o.o.d., and +6% RGB while training in 5 epochs vs. 30. DeepLab-v3 (0.87/0.80/0.80) is beaten by 4–11 points, with the largest gap on the RGB domain. Segformer (0.85/0.81/0.69) trails by 6, 3, and 22 points respectively, showing severe degradation on real intraoral photos. The pure-transformer baselines Segmenter (0.75/0.68/0.55), Swin-L (0.80/0.64/0.48), SwinV2-G (0.80/0.60/0.46), and BeiT-B (0.80/0.78/0.47) are all surpassed by 11–16 points on i.i.d. and by 6–45 points on the RGB test, indicating that general-purpose transformer decoders struggle with the fine-grained tooth-ID discrimination and domain shift to real photographs that TeethSEG's MSA and APK modules specifically address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 3D tooth segmentation literature (Mask MCNet, TSegNet, DArch, GCN-based methods) by targeting 2D intraoral photos, motivated by the lower cost and broader accessibility of 2D imaging for large-scale screening. On the transformer side, it builds on the CLIP encoder and the general ViT-for-dense-prediction lineage (Segmenter, DPT, ViT-Adapter) but argues that off-the-shelf architectures fail on teeth due to subtle inter-class shape similarity and clinical abnormalities. The key architectural departures are the cross-gating mechanism (replacing cross-attention to preserve token divergence) and the permutation-based upscaler (replacing bilinear interpolation to maintain local information), both designed specifically for the 16-class FDI tooth labeling problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IO150K dataset (150K images with professional orthodontist annotations) fills a critical gap as the first large-scale open-source 2D intraoral segmentation benchmark, enabling future work that was previously infeasible due to data scarcity. The APK layer's encoding of orthodontist reasoning (adjacency, contralateral symmetry, foreground constraint) offers a template for injecting domain priors into segmentation models, and the 5-epoch convergence versus 30 epochs for baselines suggests meaningful efficiency gains. Limitations include the small RGB subset (0.8K images), reliance on a single CLIP encoder, and the absence of clinical validation; open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-modal (text+image) diagnosis and whether the permutation upscaler generalizes beyond this specific token-length regime.
