## Problem

Existing listening head generation methods control listener responses only through a few fixed emotional labels (e.g., positive, neutral, negative), which cannot capture the rich, user-customizable attributes of a realistic listener agent such as identity, personality, and behavioral habits. This limits the ability to produce fine-grained, freely controllable non-verbal responses that a human listener would naturally exhibit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20241377/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a speaker video (from which 45-dim acoustic features and 70-dim 3DMM motion coefficients are extracted), a text prior describing the listener's portrait (generated by GPT from user-customized attributes and speaker speech content, then encoded via RoBERTa into portrait tokens), and a single listener reference image. It outputs 70-dim listener 3DMM coefficients (64-dim expression + 6-dim pose) at 30 FPS in 60-frame segments, which are rendered into 256×256 listener videos via PIRenderer.

## Method

CustomListener operates in two stages. The Static-to-Dynamic Portrait (SDP) module first converts static text-derived portrait tokens into time-dependent ones through an audio-text responsive interaction (a scaled dot-product attention between audio features and static portrait tokens that assigns per-timestamp weights to motion descriptions), then refines them with cumulative gradients of the past five frames of speaker motion to correlate listener amplitude with speaker movement. The Past-Guided Generation (PGG) module computes a motion prior by weighting past-segment motions according to cosine similarity between dynamic portrait tokens of adjacent segments, then feeds this prior together with the dynamic portrait tokens as conditions into a DDPM-based transformer decoder that denoises listener 3DMM coefficients, using a fixed initial noise at segment boundaries to ensure smooth transitions. Training uses a standard conditional diffusion noise-prediction loss, and the two text-annotated datasets (ViCo and RealTalk) are constructed by annotating emotion labels, AU activations/intensities, and head motion labels per segment.

## Results

On ViCo D_test, CustomListener achieves the best FD of 11.54 (exp), 6.12 (angle), and 5.90 (trans), outperforming the next-best MFR-Net (13.37/6.82/6.02) and L2L (14.89/7.35/6.34). It also leads on speaker-listener synchronization (RTLCC 0.072 exp, 0.103 pose), temporal naturalness (FID_Δfm 3.12 exp, 0.06 pose; SND 2.40 exp, 0.07 pose), and diversity (V-D 1.55 exp, 0.32 pose). On the out-of-domain D_ood set, the same pattern holds with FD 12.67/7.49/6.01 and FID_Δfm 3.59/0.07. In the user study, CustomListener scores highest on all four perceptual criteria (similarity 3.96, naturalness 3.92, coordination 3.96, smoothness 3.88 on a 1–4 scale).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CustomListener outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric for both D_test and D_ood. Against RLHG [44] (sequence-to-sequence 3DMM decoder), it reduces FD_exp by 23% (11.54 vs. 15.03) and FID_Δfm_exp by 73% (3.12 vs. 11.65), indicating far more temporally natural motions. Against PCH [15] (enhanced renderer with regularized driver), the gap is even larger on FD_angle (6.12 vs. 13.26) and V-D_exp (1.55 vs. 0.32), showing that text-guided generation yields both more accurate and more diverse motions. Against L2L [25] (VQ-VAE discrete codebook), CustomListener improves RTLCC_exp from 0.102 to 0.072 (30% reduction), confirming better speaker-listener synchronization. MFR-Net [21] reports only FD (13.37/6.82/6.02 on D_test), still trailing CustomListener by roughly 14–15% on each sub-metric. In the user study, CustomListener leads all baselines by at least 1.1 points on a 4-point scale across similarity, naturalness, coordination, and smoothness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DMM-coefficient-based listening head generation line (RLHG, PCH, L2L, ELP, MFR-Net) by replacing discrete emotion-label conditioning with free-form text priors, leveraging GPT for semantic understanding and RoBERTa for text encoding. The core generative backbone is DDPM with a transformer decoder, consistent with MFR-Net's diffusion-based approach, but the conditioning mechanism is substantially redesigned: the SDP module introduces audio-text responsive interaction and speaker-motion-based refinement to produce time-dependent dynamic portrait tokens, while the PGG module uses cross-segment portrait-token similarity to generate motion priors for long-term coherence. The paper also draws on text-conditioned body/dance motion generation but explicitly distinguishes its challenge as requiring speaker-listener temporal coordination rather than simple text-motion alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The text-annotated ViCo and RealTalk datasets fill a clear gap for text-guided listener generation research, and the framework demonstrates that free-form text can effectively control fine-grained non-verbal responses (identity, personality, behavioral habits) beyond what a few emotion labels allow. Limitations include the head-only scope (no full-body generation), reliance on GPT as an external dependency for text prior generation, and the inability to quantitatively compare with ELP due to incompatible 3DMM dimensions. The authors note that extending to full-body listener agents is a natural next step, and the broader question of how to handle multi-turn, evolving listener state across extended conversations remains open.
