## Problem

Digitalizing a specific cloth sample requires inferring its detailed physical parameters, but existing differentiable physics methods assume homogeneous materials and deterministic dynamics, while data-driven approaches demand large datasets that are prohibitively expensive to collect under controlled textile testing protocols. The Cusick drape test provides accurate, standard-compliant measurements but yields only a single 2D silhouette image per sample, making it incompatible with both large-data deep learning and methods requiring 3D motion capture.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single Cusick drape silhouette image (L×L binary mask extracted from a DSLR photo through a frosted glass lid) and the initial flat cloth state (a triangular mesh with 2699 vertices, 7924 edges, and 5226 faces) as input. It outputs learned Gaussian posterior distributions over per-face stretching stiffness (C ∈ R^{6×4}) and per-bending-edge stiffness (B ∈ R^{3×5}), which can be sampled to simulate garments on arbitrary geometries. Notably, only the 2D silhouette is used for learning—no 3D geometry, texture, or motion data is required—making the method applicable to standard Cusick drape meters that cannot reconstruct 3D meshes.

## Method

The paper introduces a Bayesian differentiable cloth model that treats per-element material parameters as latent variables governed by a Gaussian prior, and learns their posterior via variational inference with the reparameterization trick. The forward pass uses implicit Euler integration of a heterogeneous cloth dynamics model (with per-face stretching and per-edge bending forces) to simulate the drape, followed by the DIR-B differentiable renderer to project the final 3D mesh into a 2D silhouette. The training objective is the negative log-likelihood of the observed silhouette under a Gaussian observation model, which reduces to an MSE-like loss but is embedded in the ELBO framework to jointly optimize the variational parameters (mean and log-variance of the posterior). This formulation explicitly captures within-type material heterogeneity and draping stochasticity, enabling the model to learn from a single drape image per cloth type and generalize the learned parameter distributions to arbitrary garment geometries.

## Results

On the Cusick Drape Dataset (5 cloth types × 12 samples each, 1 sample for training), BDP achieves an average MSE of 3.84×10⁻² and average Hausdorff distance of 2.17×10⁻² m, outperforming the homogeneous baseline HOMO (5.72×10⁻² MSE, 3.40×10⁻² m) and the heterogeneous deterministic baseline HETER (5.51×10⁻² MSE, 3.28×10⁻² m). Against derivative-free optimizers, BDP's gradient-based approach yields 4.52×10⁻² MSE and 2.01×10⁻² m Hausdorff distance, roughly halving the error of REMBO (6.03×10⁻² MSE, 4.25×10⁻² m) and HeSBO (5.90×10⁻² MSE, 4.35×10⁻² m) within fewer optimization steps (1000 epochs vs. 500 trials).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BDP outperforms all three baselines on both MSE and Hausdorff distance. Against HOMO (Liang et al., NeurIPS 2019), the deterministic homogeneous differentiable cloth model, BDP reduces MSE by 33% (3.84 vs. 5.72×10⁻²) and Hausdorff distance by 36% (2.17 vs. 3.40×10⁻² m), demonstrating that modeling material heterogeneity and Bayesian uncertainty is critical for accurate digitalization. HETER, an augmented version of HOMO with per-element stiffness parameters, narrows the gap slightly (5.51×10⁻² MSE) but remains deterministic and cannot generalize to unseen samples of the same cloth type, whereas BDP's learned parameter distributions enable within-type generalization. Against the derivative-free optimizers REMBO (Wang et al., JAIR 2016) and HeSBO (Nayebi et al., ICML 2019), BDP's gradient-based variational inference achieves roughly half the Hausdorff distance (2.01 vs. 4.25/4.35×10⁻² m) and lower MSE (4.52 vs. 6.03/5.90×10⁻²), confirming that differentiable physics with back-propagation is substantially more sample-efficient than Bayesian optimization for this high-dimensional (>200,000 parameter) inverse problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable cloth simulation line (Liang et al. 2019, Gong et al. 2022, Li et al. 2021) by replacing the standard homogeneous, deterministic parameter estimation with a Bayesian variational inference framework that models material heterogeneity at the element level and draping stochasticity. It departs from prior work's reliance on 3D geometry or video data by learning exclusively from 2D Cusick drape silhouettes captured under the BS EN ISO 9073-9 standard, and introduces a new dataset to fill the gap of publicly available standardized drape measurements. The variational inference formulation with the reparameterization trick connects to the broader physics-informed deep learning literature, but the specific combination of Bayesian posteriors over per-element stiffness matrices within a differentiable FEM cloth solver is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single standardized drape image suffices for accurate cloth digitalization when the physics model explicitly accounts for material heterogeneity and stochasticity, which is practically significant for textile manufacturing and fashion design where data collection is expensive and slow. A key limitation is the requirement for prior physics knowledge (FEM formulation, force models), making it less plug-and-play than black-box deep learning approaches. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to more complex dynamics (buckling, frictional contact), whether it scales to larger garment geometries, and how well the learned parameter distributions transfer across different drape testing apparatuses or environmental conditions.
